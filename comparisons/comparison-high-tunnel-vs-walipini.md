# High Tunnel vs Walipini (Underground Greenhouse)

## Overview

A **high tunnel** (also called a hoophouse) is an unheated, polyethylene-covered hoop structure
typically anchored directly into the soil.[[comparison-passive-solar-vs-earth-sheltered]] design with a south-facing glazed wall
(in the Northern Hemisphere) to capture heat while the surrounding earth insulates against
extreme cold.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | High Tunnel | Walipini |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Low–Medium ($500–$5,000 for 30×72 ft) | Medium–High ($3,000–$15,000 for similar footprint) |
| **Construction difficulty** | Low — can be built in 1–3 days with 2–4 people | High — excavation, drainage, structural engineering required |
| **Lifespan** | 5–15 years (poly replaced every 4–6 years) | 15–30+ years with proper waterproofing and maintenance |
| **[[comparison-season-extension-techniques-vs-cold-frame-gardening]] gain** | 4–8 weeks (spring/fall); limited true winter | 2–4 months; can grow through winter in zones 4–6 |
| **Ventilation** | Roll-up sides + end walls; excellent natural airflow | Limited; requires powered fans, ridge vents, and intake vents |
| **Light levels** | High (80–90% PAR transmission through poly) | Reduced (30–60% due to walls, earth reflection, and glazing angles) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate; manageable with ventilation | High; condensation and mold risk without active dehumidification |
| **Snow load capacity** | Good with gothic-style hoops; may require truss supports | Excellent — snow slides off angled roof; earth bears the load |
| **Drainage needs** | Minimal; raised beds and grading usually sufficient | Critical — must prevent all water infiltration into the pit |
| **Pest pressure** | Moderate; some exclusion from enclosure but open bottom | Low below-ground; aphids and fungus gnats thrive in humid enclosed air |
| **Space requirements** | Flat open area; no excavation; standard 30 ft width typical | Excavated pit 6–8 ft deep; needs additional land for spoil and access |
| **Best climate zones** | 3–9 (most versatile across climates) | 3–7 (greatest advantage in cold, dry climates with low water tables) |
| **Best uses** | Spring/fall extension, hardy winter greens, seedling starts | Year-round growing in cold climates, [[holzer-frost-protection-techniques]]
- **Tools required:** Post driver, socket wrenches, poly clip locks or wiggle wire, tape measure,
  ladder, circular saw

### Walipini — What You Need

- **Excavation:** Pit 6–8 ft deep, typically 20–30 ft wide × 40–60 ft long (manual labor or
  rented backhoe; expect 200+ cubic yards of soil displacement)
- **Drainage system:** 4–6 in. gravel layer at pit bottom + perforated drain tile around entire
  perimeter leading to daylight or a sump pump
- **Waterproofing:** EPDM rubber liner or heavy-duty 20-mil poly on all buried walls, lapped and
  sealed at all joints
- **Glazed wall:** South-facing wall framed with treated lumber or metal, covered with twin-wall
  polycarbonate (16 mm preferred) or double-layer inflated poly
- **Bermed walls:** North, east, and west walls constructed from rammed earth, cinderblock, or
  treated timber retaining walls, backfilled and compacted
- **Roof:** Structural beams (treated 2×8 or 2×10 lumber, or metal purlins) spanning the pit at
  35–45° angle optimized for winter solar angle at your latitude
- **Roof glazing:** Polycarbonate panels or poly film on the angled roof section; opaque/insulated
  section on the north side to reduce heat loss
- **Ventilation:** Ridge vent at roof peak, low-level intake vents, and an exhaust fan (thermostat
  or humidistat controlled) to manage moisture
- **Tools required:** Excavating equipment, concrete mixer (optional), carpentry tools, laser level,
  staple gun, caulking gun, safety equipment for deep trench work

## Suitable Crops

### High Tunnel — Cool Season
Spinach, kale, lettuce, arugula, mizuna, radishes, carrots, scallions, Asian greens (tatsoi,
bok choy, komatsuna), Swiss chard, cilantro, parsley, overwintered strawberries.

### High Tunnel — Warm Season
Tomatoes (indeterminate varieties excel), peppers, cucumbers (trellised), eggplant, melons,
early bush beans, basil, cut flowers (zinnias, sunflowers, lisianthus).

### Walipini — Year-Round
Leafy greens (spinach, lettuce, kale, Swiss chard) perform best due to lower light levels.
Root vegetables (carrots, beets, turnips, radishes) thrive in the stable root-zone temperatures.
Brassicas (broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage) grow well in cooler months. In well-designed units
with adequate light — tomatoes, peppers, and even strawberries can produce through winter.
The stable warmth also supports subtropical trials: citrus, avocado seedlings, and ginger that
would fail outright in an unheated high tunnel.

## Pros and Cons

### High Tunnel — Pros

- Low cost and fast to build; accessible to beginners with minimal skills
- Excellent light transmission for vigorous plant growth and fruiting
- Easy to ventilate — roll-up sides provide rapid, effective natural airflow
- Portable and temporary designs available; can relocate or expand incrementally
- Proven ROI for market gardeners — SARE data shows 2–3 year payback on average
- Minimal permitting required in most jurisdictions (check local codes)
- Compatible with standard farm equipment and crop rotation practices

### High Tunnel — Cons

- Limited winter production in cold zones (below zone 6) without supplemental heat
- Poly film degrades from UV exposure; recurring $300–$800 replacement cost every 4–6 years
- Vulnerable to extreme wind events if not properly anchored and tensioned
