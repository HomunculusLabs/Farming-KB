## BIM

[[ultra-low-cost-jadam-microorganism-solution-and-soil-foundation]]

JADAM indigenous microorganism solution (JMS) is a cornerstone input in the
JADAM ultra-low-cost organic farming system developed by Cho Young-sang in South
Korea. The JADAM philosophy is radical in its simplicity: take microorganisms
from local natural environments rather than purchasing commercial products. The
foundation of JMS is leaf mold soil that has accumulated from hundreds of years
of leaf decomposition, which JADAM calls "black gold."

The JADAM approach to microorganisms is based on several key principles.
[[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]] that have adapted to a local environment are the best
workers for that environment. All microorganisms are considered beneficial in
the JADAM philosophy; nature has what you need everywhere. A Japanese
microorganism product made in a hot, humid region was found unsuitable for other
Japanese regions, confirming that locally sourced organisms are essential.

JMS preparation begins with leaf mold soil collected from a forest or mountain
area where leaves have been decomposing for extended periods. This soil is mixed
with water and a small amount of boiled potato or sweet potato to provide
initial microbial food, then aerated and allowed to culture for several days.
The resulting solution contains a diverse community of bacteria, fungi,
actinomycetes, and other microorganisms adapted to the local environment. JMS
can be further customized by adding specific materials to the leaf mold:
throwing beans into leaf mold cultivates bean-decomposing microorganisms, while
adding specific crop residues cultivates organisms adapted to decompose those
materials.
