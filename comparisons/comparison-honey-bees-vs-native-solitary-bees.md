# Honey Bees vs Native/Solitary Bees

Understanding the differences between honey bees (*Apis mellifera*) and native solitary bees is essential for gardeners, farmers, and conservationists. Honey bees dominate agricultural pollination, but [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]'s ~4,000 native bee species — most of them solitary — are often more efficient pollinators, require virtually no maintenance, and face serious conservation threats. Choosing between (or integrating) these groups has real consequences for crop yields, ecosystem health, and biodiversity.

## Stinging Risk

Honey bees defend their colony aggressively. A sting releases an alarm pheromone that recruits other guards, and the barbed stinger is torn from the bee's body (she dies). Multiple stings are common near hives.

Most solitary bees are **extremely docile** — males have no stinger at all, and females reserve stinging for absolute self-defense (being physically squeezed). You can observe mason and leafcutter bees at arm's length without risk. This makes solitary bees ideal for school gardens, playgrounds, and urban spaces.

## Disease and Pesticide Sensitivity

Honey bees face well-documented threats: **Varroa destructor** mites vector *deformed wing virus* (DWV); *Nosema* fungi gut parasites; tracheal mites; and **Colony Collapse Disorder** (CCD). Beekeepers invest significant resources in monitoring and treatment.

Native bee health is less studied, but evidence is mounting. Neonicotinoid insecticides impair foraging and reproduction in solitary bees at sub-lethal doses. Habitat loss eliminates nesting sites and floral resources. Unlike honey bees, solitary bees receive no veterinary care — their survival depends entirely on healthy landscapes.

Honey bees also suffer from pesticide exposure — both acute poisoning and chronic sub-lethal effects on navigation, memory, and immune function — but their managed status means beekeepers can relocate hives away from treated fields. Solitary bees have no such protection; a contaminated nesting site means reproductive failure for an entire local population.

## Maintenance and Management

Beekeeping requires substantial commitment: regular hive inspections (every 1–2 weeks in season), disease monitoring and treatment, swarm prevention, supering, [[citizen-science-fungal-biodiversity-discovery]] programs (Bumble Bee Watch, iNaturalist) to contribute to native bee data.

Both honey bees and native solitary bees have vital roles. By understanding their differences and complementary strengths, we can build landscapes that sustain pollinators, food systems, and biodiversity together.
