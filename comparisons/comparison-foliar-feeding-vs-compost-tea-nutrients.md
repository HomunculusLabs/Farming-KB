# Foliar Feeding vs Compost Tea

## Overview

Foliar feeding and compost tea application are two distinct methods for delivering inputs directly to plant foliage, but they operate through fundamentally
different mechanisms and serve complementary roles in plant health management. Foliar feeding supplies mineral nutrients in ionic form for absorption through
stomata and the cuticle, while compost tea delivers living microbial communities that colonize the phyllosphere and provide biological protection.

Both approaches bypass the soil entirely when applied to foliage, making them valuable tools when root uptake is compromised by environmental stress, poor soil
biology, or chemical imbalances. Foliar feeding is a targeted nutritional intervention that acts quickly, whereas compost tea is a biological inoculation
strategy that builds protection over time. The best results come from understanding which tool fits the situation and using them in concert rather than in
isolation.

Growers who integrate both methods into their regular plant health routines can address both acute nutritional needs and long-term biological resilience
simultaneously, creating a more robust and adaptable growing system overall.

## When to Choose Compost Tea

Compost tea is the better choice when the goal is building long-term biological protection on leaf surfaces and throughout the soil ecosystem. It is the primary
tool for organic and ecological growers seeking to prevent and suppress foliar diseases without relying on synthetic fungicides or copper-based sprays. Regular
weekly applications throughout the growing season maintain a robust, diverse phyllosphere community that resists pathogen establishment through multiple
simultaneous mechanisms.

Compost tea is especially valuable in humid climates, in dense plantings with poor airflow, and in greenhouse environments where disease pressure is chronically
