# Terracing vs Swale

## Overview
Terraces and swales are the two primary earthwork techniques for managing
water and preventing erosion on sloped land. Terraces create level platforms
cut into the hillside, converting a slope into a series of flat growing
surfaces. Swales are shallow water-harvesting ditches dug on contour to
capture and infiltrate runoff. Both slow water movement downhill, but they
differ fundamentally in their primary function: terraces create arable land
while swales primarily harvest and infiltrate water. Understanding their
differences is essential for anyone designing earthworks on sloped property.

## Terracing

terraces, swales do not create level growing surfaces; their primary purpose
is water harvesting, groundwater recharge, and [[comparison-swale-design-vs-contour-swale-irrigation-design]] and construction requires accurate
contour mapping using an A-frame level, laser level, or water tube level.

Swales are most effective on moderate slopes (3 to 15 degrees) where
runoff is manageable and infiltration can occur within the soil profile
without causing saturation problems. They are the earthwork of choice for
tree planting systems, food forests, and pastoral grazing land where the
goal is to keep water on the landscape rather than creating flat surfaces
for intensive cultivation. Swales planted on the berm with fruit trees and
nitrogen-fixing support species create productive agroforestry systems that
improve with time as tree roots stabilize the soil.

Swales are simpler and cheaper to construct than terraces, requiring less
engineering and no structural retaining walls. However, they are unsuitable
for steep slopes where water velocities can exceed the swale's capacity
during intense storms, potentially causing breach and erosion. Swales also
require adequate soil permeability; in heavy clay soils, water may pool
rather than infiltrate, creating anaerobic conditions that damage plant
roots.

## When to Choose Swaling
Swales are ideal for establishing tree-based systems on moderate slopes
where the primary goal is water capture and soil building rather than
creating flat ground. They are the preferred earthwork for food forests,
silvopasture, and broadacre agroforestry. Swales are also excellent for
dryland restoration where every drop of rainfall needs to be captured and
infiltrated. Their lower cost and simpler construction make them accessible
to homesteaders and landowners without heavy equipment or engineering
expertise.

## Hybrid Approaches
On slopes with varying gradients, the two techniques can be combined:
swales on the upper moderate slopes to capture and infiltrate water, with
terraces on the steepest sections where level growing surfaces are needed.
