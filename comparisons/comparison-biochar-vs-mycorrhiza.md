Biochar is produced by heating biomass in a low-oxygen environment through
pyrolysis at temperatures ranging from 300 to 800 degrees Celsius. The resulting
carbon-rich material has an extraordinarily high surface area of 100 to 500
square meters per gram, with a negative charge that attracts and holds
positively charged nutrients like calcium, magnesium, potassium, and ammonium.
This cation exchange capacity (CEC) reduces nutrient leaching and keeps
fertility in the root zone where plants can access it. Over time, biochar's CEC
actually increases as weathering creates more oxidation sites on its surface.

photosynthetic carbon to the fungus. This partnership is one of the oldest and
