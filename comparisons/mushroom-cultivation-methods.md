# Mushroom Cultivation Methods Comparison

Mushroom cultivation methods range from simple outdoor beds requiring no equipment to sterile indoor techniques demanding precision and investment. The right method depends on your species of interest, budget, available space, and skill level. This comparison covers eight major approaches synthesized from [[pf-tek]], [[monotub-cultivation]], [[outdoor-mushroom-bed]], [[outdoor-wood-lover-cultivation]], [[mushroom-substrates]], [[mushroom-log-cultivation]], and [[straw-cultivation-methods]].

The fundamental tradeoff is control vs. simplicity. Indoor methods (PF TEK, monotub, sawdust blocks) offer environmental control and faster results but require sterile technique and equipment investment. Outdoor methods (beds, logs) leverage natural weather cycles and require minimal equipment but are slower and yield inconsistently. The best approach for most homestead growers is to start with an easy outdoor method while building skills for indoor cultivation.

## Method Details

### PF TEK (BRF Cakes)
The classic beginner method from [[pf-tek]]. Uses brown rice flour and vermiculite in half-pint jars with a dry vermiculite contamination barrier. Steam sterilized (no pressure cooker needed). Inoculated with spore syringe. Best for learning sterile technique and growing P. cubensis. Low yield per jar but very reliable. Core innovation: the dry vermiculite barrier replaces complex sterile procedures, making cultivation accessible without a pressure cooker or flow hood.

### Monotub (Bulk Substrate)
The most popular indoor method from [[monotub-cultivation]]. Grain spawn is mixed with pasteurized coco coir/vermiculite/gypsum (CVG) in a modified plastic tote. Self-contained colonization and fruiting chamber. Produces significantly higher yields than PF TEK. Requires pressure cooker for grain spawn preparation. Spawn ratio of 1:2 (spawn:bulk) is standard. Multiple flushes (3-5) are typical before the substrate is exhausted.

### Outdoor Wood Chip Bed
For wood-decomposing species from [[outdoor-wood-lover-cultivation]] and [[outdoor-mushroom-bed]]. Fresh hardwood chips layered with spawn in a shaded trench. No heat treatment needed for wine cap. Woodlovers (P. cyanescens, P. azurescens) require sterilized chip spawn first. Produces for years from a single inoculation. Pest protection (slugs, rodents) is the main challenge. Fresh chips are preferred over aged — saprophytic fungi colonize before competitors establish.

### Log Cultivation
Traditional method from [[mushroom-log-cultivation]]. Hardwood logs (oak, alder, maple) inoculated with plug or sawdust spawn, sealed with wax. Stacked in shade. Shiitake is the classic log species. Slow to establish (6-18 months) but produces for 3-5 years with minimal maintenance. Force-fruiting by soaking logs in cold water for 24 hours can trigger fruiting on demand. Logs 3-4 feet long and 4-6 inches in diameter are standard.

### Sawdust Blocks
Industry standard from [[mushroom-substrates]]. Supplemented hardwood sawdust (4:1 sawdust:bran) in filter-patch bags. Requires pressure cooker sterilization (90-120 min at 15 psi). Highest biological efficiency of any method. Essential for shiitake, lion's mane, and reishi cultivation. Moisture target: 55-65%. The squeeze test: a handful squeezed firmly should produce a few drops but no stream.

### Straw Basket
From [[straw-cultivation-methods]]. Wheat straw pasteurized in hot water (160F, 20-30 min), mixed with grain spawn (10-15% by weight), packed into baskets or poly tubing. Excellent for oyster mushrooms. Very low contamination risk since straw has high C:N ratio (~80-120:1). Simple and cheap. Chopped straw permeates more rapidly than whole stalks.

### Wine Cap / King Stropharia Beds
The easiest entry point from [[outdoor-mushroom-bed]]. Fresh hardwood chips or straw laid 4-8 inches deep in garden paths, inoculated with grain or sawdust spawn, covered with cardboard during colonization. Produces large, edible mushrooms from otherwise unused garden space. Improves soil health as mycelium breaks down organic matter. Wine cap is the most reliable outdoor species for beginners.

## Choosing a Method

- **First-time grower, indoors, minimal budget**: PF TEK or straw basket for oysters
- **Indoor, want higher yields**: Monotub (requires pressure cooker)
- **Outdoor, have a garden**: Wine cap or king stropharia in wood chip paths
- **Gourmet species (shiitake, lion's mane)**: Sawdust blocks (indoor) or log cultivation (outdoor)
- **Psilocybe woodlovers (cyanescens, azurescens)**: Outdoor wood chip beds (see [[outdoor-wood-lover-cultivation]])
- **Maximum yield per effort**: Sawdust blocks indoor; log cultivation for long-term outdoor

## Verdict

For homestead integration, outdoor wine cap and king stropharia beds are the best starting point — they require almost no investment, improve soil health, produce food from garden pathways, and establish a fungal presence on the property. For indoor cultivation, the progression is PF TEK (learn sterile technique) to monotub (scale up) to sawdust blocks (gourmet species). Each step builds on skills from the previous method. The most resilient approach combines outdoor beds (low effort, multi-year) with indoor monotub grows (faster results, seasonal production).
