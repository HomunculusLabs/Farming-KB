# Potato vs Sweet Potato Cultivation

Potatoes (*Solanum tuberosum*) and sweet potatoes (*Ipomoea batatas*) are both widely grown root crops that share a name but are otherwise completely unrelated botanically, ecologically, and culinarily. Understanding their differences is essential for choosing the right crop for your climate, soil, and goals.

## Botanical Classification

| Feature | Potato | Sweet Potato |
|---|---|---|
| **Family** | Solanaceae (nightshade) | Convolvulaceae ([[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]]** | 90–120 days | 90–150 days |
| **Heat tolerance** | Poor — heat reduces tuber set | Excellent — thrives in heat |

**Key difference**: Potatoes are a cool-season crop; sweet potatoes are a warm-season crop. They cannot be grown in the same season in most climates.

## Soil Requirements

| Factor | Potato | Sweet Potato |
|---|---|---|
| **Soil type** | Sandy loam preferred; tolerates heavier soils | Sandy, well-drained essential; intolerant of heavy clay |
| **Soil pH** | 5.0–6.5 (acidic) | 5.5–6.5 (slightly acidic) |
| **Drainage** | Good drainage needed; waterlogging causes rot | Excellent drainage critical |
| **Soil fertility** | Moderate to high nitrogen; high phosphorus & potassium | Low nitrogen; moderate phosphorus; high potassium |
| **Compaction** | Tolerates moderate; hilling helps | Intolerant; needs loose soil to 8–12 inches |

## Planting and Propagation

| Aspect | Potato | Sweet Potato |
|---|---|---|
| **Propagation** | Seed potatoes (tubers cut into pieces with "eyes") | Transplants ("slips") grown from root cuttings or direct sprouting |
| **Planting depth** | 4–6 inches deep | Planted on ridges/mounds, slip base 3–4 inches deep |
| **Spacing** | 10–14 inches apart in rows 30–36 inches apart | 12–18 inches apart in rows 36–48 inches apart |
| **Planting time** | 2–4 weeks before last spring frost | 2–4 weeks after last spring frost (soil must be warm) |
| **Seed source** | Certified disease-free seed potatoes | Disease-free slips from nurseries or home-grown |

### Potato Planting Steps
1. Chit (pre-sprout) seed potatoes 2–4 weeks before planting by placing in a warm, bright area.
2. Cut large tubers into pieces with 2–3 eyes each; let cuts cure for 24–48 hours.
3. Plant in trenches or individual holes 4–6 inches deep.
4. Hill soil around plants as they grow, covering lower leaves — this encourages tuber formation and prevents greening.

### Sweet Potato Planting Steps
1. Start slips 6–8 weeks before planting by suspending a sweet potato in water or burying it in moist sand.
2. When slips are 6–8 inches tall, twist them off the mother root.
3. Root slips in water or moist medium for 1–2 weeks.
4. Transplant into raised beds or ridges once soil has warmed to at least 65°F.
5. Never plant in cold soil — cold shock causes permanent root deformation.

## Water Requirements

| Factor | Potato | Sweet Potato |
|---|---|---|
| **Water needs** | Consistent moisture; 1–2 inches per week | Moderate; drought-tolerant once established |
| **Critical period** | During tuber set (6–10 weeks after planting) | During root enlargement (mid-season) |
| **Overwatering risk** | High — causes late blight, tuber rot | Moderate — causes root cracking and rot |
| **Drought response** | Reduced yields, misshapen tubers | Survives well; may reduce size |

**Key difference**: Sweet potatoes are significantly more drought-tolerant than potatoes, making them the better choice for water-limited gardens.

## Fertilization

| Aspect | Potato | Sweet Potato |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen** | Moderate (avoid excess — promotes foliage over tubers) | Low — excess nitrogen produces huge vines, small roots |
| **Phosphorus** | Important for root development | Moderate importance |
| **Potassium** | Critical for tuber size and disease resistance | Very important for root size and sweetness |
| **Calcium** | Important — deficiency causes internal brown spot | Important — deficiency causes internal cork |
| **Organic approach** | Compost + bone meal + wood ash | Compost + kelp meal + greensand |

**Critical note**: Excess nitrogen is the most common fertilization mistake for both crops. For potatoes, it delays tuberization; for sweet potatoes, it produces enormous vines with tiny roots.

## Common Pests and Diseases

| Problem | Potato | Sweet Potato |
|---|---|---|
| **Major pest** | Colorado potato beetle, wireworms, potato leafhopper | Sweet potato weevil, flea beetles, whiteflies |
| **Major disease** | Late blight (*Phytophthora infestans*), early blight | Fusarium wilt, scurf, root rot |
| **Nematode issues** | Root-knot nematode | Root-knot nematode (major pest) |
| **Virus issues** | Several potato viruses | Virus diseases from infected slips |
| **[[comparison-crop-rotation-vs-cover-cropping]], Bt, row covers | Crop rotation, resistant varieties, clean slips |
