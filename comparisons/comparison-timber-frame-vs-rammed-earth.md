# Timber Frame vs Rammed Earth

## Overview

Timber frame and rammed earth represent two of humanity's oldest and most
enduring building traditions. Timber framing uses large wooden posts and beams
connected by interlocking joinery to create a structural skeleton. Rammed earth
compacts damp soil inside temporary formwork to produce massive monolithic walls.
Both methods produce beautiful, durable structures with low environmental impact,
but they differ fundamentally in materials, structural approach, thermal behavior,
and the skills required to build them.

Rammed earth construction compresses damp, well-graded soil inside temporary
formwork in successive lifts to create dense, monolithic walls. With a history
spanning over 7,000 years — including sections of the Great Wall of China —
rammed earth produces walls of exceptional thermal mass, durability, and natural
beauty. The technique requires suitable soil (10-20% clay, balance sand and
gravel), robust formwork, and systematic compaction of each lift.

Modern rammed earth may be stabilised with 5-10% cement or lime for improved
water resistance and compressive strength, though unstabilised rammed earth
performs well in dry climates when protected by generous roof overhangs and
raised foundations. The characteristic horizontal stratification lines from
each compacted lift give rammed earth walls their distinctive visual appeal.

Rammed earth excels in regions with significant daily temperature swings. The
dense walls absorb heat during hot days and release it slowly at night, creating
an 8-12 hour thermal lag that moderates indoor temperatures passively. This
thermal mass effect rivals that of stone and far exceeds lightweight framing.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Timber Frame | Rammed Earth |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| **Structural System** | Post-and-beam skeleton | Monolithic load-bearing walls |
| **Primary Material** | Wood timbers | Compacted earth |
| **Wall Role** | Infill between frame members | Structural and enclosure |
| **Thermal Behavior** | Depends on infill choice | High thermal mass (8-12hr lag) |
| **Insulation** | Added in wall cavities | External insulation if needed |
| **Skill Focus** | Joinery cutting, frame raising | Soil testing, formwork, tamping |
| **Construction Speed** | Fast raising after prep joinery | Slow, methodical lift-by-lift |
| **Climate Suitability** | All climates with right infill | Best in dry or temperate regions |
| **Embodied Energy** | Low to moderate (wood processing) | Very low (earth + compaction) |
| **Cost** | Moderate to high (timber quality) | Low to moderate (local soil) |
| **Lifespan** | Centuries with maintenance | Centuries to millennia |
