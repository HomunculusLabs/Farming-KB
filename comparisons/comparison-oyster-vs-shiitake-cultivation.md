Shiitake mushrooms (*Lentinus edodes*) are traditionally grown on hardwood logs — oak and
alder being preferred species — using a spawn run that lasts 6–12 months as the mycelium
slowly colonizes the dense wood. Once fully colonized, fruiting is triggered by cold water
soaking combined with a temperature shock, mimicking the natural autumn conditions that
signal the fungus to reproduce. A single inoculated log can produce mushrooms for 3–6
years, making this method a long-term investment with relatively low ongoing costs after
the initial wait period. Log-grown shiitake also develops denser, meatier fruiting bodies
with deeper flavor than those from sawdust blocks.

For growers unwilling to wait a year for their first harvest, sawdust block culture offers
a faster alternative with 2–3 month production cycles. The trade-off is a much shorter
productive lifespan per block — typically 2–4 flushes — compared to the years of output
from a log. Sawdust blocks do allow more controlled indoor environments and higher yields
per square foot in the short term, bridging some of the speed gap with [[growing-gourmet-outdoor-log-inoculation-techniques]].
