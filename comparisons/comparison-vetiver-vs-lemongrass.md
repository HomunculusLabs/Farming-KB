# Vetiver Grass vs Lemongrass

## At a Glance

| Feature | Vetiver (*Chrysopogon zizanioides*) | Lemongrass (*[[herb-spiral]] and zone 1**: Compact, harvestable, multi-use culinary plant
- **Insectary plant**: Strongly repels mosquitoes and some crop pests; attracts beneficials
- **Living mulch**: Dense growth suppresses weeds in orchard alleys
- **Compost activator**: High nitrogen leaves accelerate compost
- **Tea and value-add crop**: Easy to process into marketable products

## Which to Choose?

**Choose Vetiver if**:
- You need erosion control on slopes, banks, or degraded land
- You want a low-maintenance, long-lived soil stabilizer
- You're in a tropical/subtropical climate with erosion challenges
- You want a perfumery-grade essential oil crop from roots

**Choose Lemongrass if**:
- You want a culinary herb for kitchen use
- You're making tea, soap, or insect repellent
- You have limited space in zone 1 or 2
- You want a fast-growing, harvestable crop

**Use both if**:
- Vetiver on contour lines and property boundaries for soil stabilization
- Lemongrass in kitchen gardens and herb spirals for culinary and pest-repelling benefits
- They complement each other perfectly in a permaculture design: vetiver for earthworks and infrastructure, lemongrass for food and daily use

## Summary

Vetiver and lemongrass are both warm-climate grasses with aromatic properties, but they serve fundamentally different niches. Vetiver is an engineering plant — its massive root system makes it the world's premier biological erosion control species. Lemongrass is a kitchen garden plant — its bright, citrusy flavor and essential oil make it one of the most useful culinary herbs for tropical and subtropical gardens. Both deserve a place in diversified permaculture systems.
