# Raised Beds vs In-Ground vs Containers

Three growing approaches for a homestead garden. The right choice depends on scale, soil conditions, mobility needs, and crop type. Most homesteads use all three for different purposes.

## At a Glance

| Dimension | Raised Beds | In-Ground | Containers |
|-----------|------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Soil control** | Full — you fill the bed | Partial — amend existing soil | Complete — isolated system |
| **Drainage** | Excellent (elevated) | Varies with native soil | Depends on drainage holes |
| **Weed pressure** | Low (filled with clean soil) | Moderate to high | Very low |
| **Soil-borne disease** | Reduced (fresh soil) | Can build up over years | Minimal (new soil each cycle) |
| **Root space** | Good (8-24 inches depth) | Unlimited | Limited (container size) |
| **Water retention** | Moderate (drains faster) | Best (deep soil profile) | Poorest (dries quickly) |
| **[[holzer-frost-protection-techniques]]** | Slight (soil warms faster in spring) | None | Portable — move indoors |
| **Lifespan** | 10-20+ years (wood, stone) | Permanent | Varies (plastic degrades) |
| **Cost per sq ft** | $5-25 (build + fill) | $1-5 (amendments only) | $5-15 (pot + soil) |
| **Labor to set up** | High (build, fill, amend) | Low (till or sheet mulch) | Low (fill pot, plant) |
| **Scalability** | Moderate (materials add up) | Unlimited | Limited by space and watering |
| **Mobility** | Permanent | Permanent | Fully portable |
| **Best for** | Intensive planting, poor native soil, accessibility | Large-scale crops, perennials, orchards | Patios, [[comparison-cannabis-indoor-growing-basics-vs-cannabis-outdoor-growing]], herbs, cannabis |

## Deep Comparison

### Raised Beds

Raised beds are the homestead garden backbone — [[homesteading-infrastructure]] recommends Sepp Holzer-style mounded beds (3-5 ft wide, steep sides). They excel where native soil is poor, compacted, or contaminated.

**Pros:**
- Soil warms 2-3 weeks earlier in spring — extends growing season
- No soil compaction from foot traffic (never walk in beds)
- Intensive planting (square foot gardening) maximizes yield per area
- Easy to [[no-till-gardening-method]] — just top-dress and mulch
- Clean root zone for [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] if used outdoors
- Reduces back strain (less bending)
- Easy to [[cover-cropping-guide]] and rotate crops in defined sections

**Cons:**
- Initial cost and labor (wood, stone, or metal + soil to fill)
- Needs more frequent watering than in-ground (drains faster)
- Soil settles over time — need to top-dress annually
- Frost risk is slightly higher in early spring (elevated soil freezes faster)
- Wooden beds rot eventually (cedar/redwood last longest; pine needs replacement in 5-8 years)

**Best depth:** 12-18 inches for most vegetables. Root crops need 18-24 inches.

### In-Ground

In-ground gardening works directly with native soil, amending it over time. It's the most scalable and lowest-cost approach.

**Pros:**
- Lowest startup cost — just amendments
- Unlimited root depth for deep-rooted crops (tomatoes, carrots, fruit trees)
- Best water retention (deep soil profile holds moisture)
- Most natural for perennials, [[food-forest-design]], and orchard integration
- Lowest maintenance long-term
- Best for [[no-till-gardening-method]] — large areas with mulch

**Cons:**
- Dependent on native soil quality — may take years to build good soil
- Harder to control drainage in heavy clay or waterlogged sites
- Soil compaction from foot traffic if not managed
- Weed seeds in native soil bank — more weeding initially
- Harder to modify pH quickly across large areas
- Gopher/mole damage in some regions

**Transition strategy:** [[no-till-gardening-method]] [[comparison-sheet-mulching-vs-chop-and-drop-mulching]] converts lawn or compacted ground to in-ground beds over 3-6 months without tilling.

### Containers

Containers provide complete soil isolation and full mobility. Essential for [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] and useful for patios, decks, and small spaces.

**Pros:**
- Complete control over soil mix, pH, and nutrients
- Portable — move to follow sun, bring indoors for frost
- No soil-borne diseases from previous cycles (replace soil)
- Ideal for invasive plants (mint, horseradish) that spread in-ground
- Great for herbs, peppers, dwarf tomatoes, and cannabis
- Good for renters and temporary setups

**Cons:**
- Limited root space — restricts plant size and yield
- Dries out fastest — may need daily watering in summer
- Nutrients leach quickly with frequent watering
- Requires premium potting mix (garden soil is too heavy)
- Expensive at scale (pots + soil for large gardens)
- Root-bound plants need repotting or root pruning
- Not suitable for large crops (corn, pumpkins, fruit trees)

**Container sizing rule:** 5 gallons minimum for peppers and determinate tomatoes. 15-30+ gallons for indeterminate tomatoes and [[modern-farm-cannabis-living-soil-vs-hydroponic-debate]] soil beds.

## Integration on the Homestead

| Growing Area | Method | Crops |
|-------------|--------|-------|
| Main [[fukuoka-dutch-priest-vegetable-garden-spiritual-renewal]] | Raised beds | Intensive annuals: tomatoes, peppers, brassicas, onions |
| Perennial food forest | In-ground | Fruit trees, berry bushes, asparagus, herbs |
| Herb garden near kitchen | Containers or raised beds | Basil, thyme, rosemary, parsley, cilantro |
| [[query-how-to-set-up-an-indoor-cannabis-grow-on-a-budget]] | Containers (living soil beds) | Cannabis — see [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] |
| Greenhouse | Raised beds or in-ground | Seed starting, winter greens, heat-loving crops |
| Patio/deck | Containers | Peppers, herbs, dwarf tomatoes, strawberries |

## Verdict

Start with raised beds for the main vegetable garden (best return on effort for food production), use in-ground for perennials and orchard, and containers for herbs, indoor cannabis, and mobile plants. Fill raised beds with a mix of [[composting-systems]] compost, [[vermicomposting]] castings, and mineral amendments from [[soil-fertility-management]] for a living soil approach from day one.
