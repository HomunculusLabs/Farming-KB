# Autoflowering Cannabis vs Photoperiod Flowering

## Overview
The two primary flowering strategies in cannabis are autoflowering (age-
triggered) and photoperiod-dependent (light-triggered). Autoflowering varieties
begin flowering after a set number of weeks from germination regardless of light
schedule, a trait inherited from [[cannabis-photoperiod-flowering-cycle]]

Photoperiod cannabis relies on changes in the dark period to trigger the
hormonal shift from vegetative to flowering stage. When darkness exceeds a
critical threshold (typically 12 hours), the plant produces florigen, initiating
bud development. This mechanism mirrors the natural seasonal light cycle, where
shortening days signal the approach of autumn and trigger reproductive flowering
in the wild. Virtually all heritage, landrace, and original [[cannabis-training-techniques-comparison]] is available, including
topping, FIM, LST, SCROG, super cropping, and main-lining, all of which can
significantly boost yield and canopy efficiency.

Photoperiod strains are also fully compatible with cloning, which is essential
for commercial operations maintaining mother plants. Cuttings retain their
vegetative state under 18/6 light, allowing growers to produce genetically
identical copies of proven performers. The downside is longer total grow time: a
typical photoperiod run takes 14-20+ weeks from seed to harvest, and each grow
requires active management of the light cycle transition. Missing the light
schedule or experiencing light leaks during the dark period can cause stress,
hermaphroditism, or delayed flowering.

## When to Choose Autoflowering Cannabis
Autoflowering varieties are ideal when speed and simplicity are priorities. They
excel for first-time growers who want a forgiving, hands-off experience with
minimal schedule management. The short life cycle enables multiple harvests per
year, making them attractive to commercial operations optimizing throughput. At
high latitudes with short summers, autoflowers may be the only reliable option
for outdoor cultivation. Their compact stature suits stealth grows, and
continuous harvest schedules can be maintained by staggering plantings every 2-3
weeks for perpetual production without managing separate vegetative and
flowering rooms.

## When to Choose Photoperiod Cannabis
Photoperiod strains are the right choice when maximum yield, genetic control,
and flexibility matter most. Longer vegetative periods and aggressive training
produce significantly larger plants with more bud sites. Mother plant operations
depend on photoperiod genetics for reliable cloning. Phenotype hunting requires
extended vegetation to properly evaluate candidates before committing to
flowering. Virtually all breeding programs, landrace preservation work, and
serious genetic development use photoperiod lines. Commercial growers with
adequate space and time typically prefer photoperiod strains for their higher
per-plant returns and proven consistency.

## Hybrid Approaches

cycle, eliminating male identification. Running photoperiod strains on 12/12

retaining cloning viability. Breeders continue developing autoflowering lines
