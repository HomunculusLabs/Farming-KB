## Blackberry

Blackberries (Rubus fructicosus and related species) produce large, firm
berries with a deep purple-black color when fully ripe. The flavor ranges
from sweet-tart to richly sweet depending on the variety. Blackberries are
among the highest-yielding berry crops, with a single mature plant producing
15 to 25 pounds of fruit per season in ideal conditions.

Blackberry plants come in several growth types. Erect varieties grow
self-supporting canes 4 to 6 feet tall and are the most cold-hardy, suitable
for zones 4 through 9. Trailing varieties produce long canes that scramble
along the ground and require a trellis for support, but they tend to produce
the largest and most flavorful berries. Semi-erect varieties offer a middle
ground with moderately vigorous growth and some self-support.
