## Chamomile

[[comparison-stinging-nettle-vs-horsetail]] (*Urtica dioica*) is a perennial herb in the Urticaceae
family, native to temperate regions across Europe, Asia, and North
America. Despite its painful sting -- caused by hollow trichomes that
inject histamine, acetylcholine, and serotonin -- nettle is one of the
most nutritionally dense wild plants available. Young spring shoots are
rich in protein (5.5 g per 100 g), vitamin A (~200% DV), vitamin C
(~370% DV), iron, calcium, magnesium, and a wide spectrum of trace
minerals.

Medicinally, nettle serves a very different role than chamomile. It is
a powerhouse anti-allergic herb, with clinical evidence showing it
inhibits key receptors involved in hay fever and allergic rhinitis.
Nettle is also used for benign prostatic hyperplasia, arthritis and
joint pain (traditionally applied as a topical sting), anaemia (due to
high iron content), and as a diuretic. It is a classic "tonic" herb --
building strength and nourishing the body over time rather than providing
immediate symptomatic relief.

In the garden, nettle functions as a [[comparison-biofertilizers-and-soil-inoculants-vs-compost-tea-guide]]. The plant also hosts over 40 species of
insects, including many beneficial predatory species, making it a
valuable component of biodiverse garden edges.

## When to Choose Chamomile

Chamomile is the right choice when you need gentle, safe, and
immediate-acting support for relaxation, sleep, and digestive comfort.
It is ideal for children, the elderly, and anyone sensitive to stronger
herbs. If your goal is a pleasant evening tea ritual, chamomile delivers
consistently with its sweet apple flavour and reliable calming effect.
