# Korean Natural Farming vs Biodynamic Agriculture
Korean Natural Farming (KNF) and biodynamic agriculture are two of the
most distinctive holistic farming systems in the world. Both reject
synthetic chemical inputs and aim to work with natural processes, yet
they emerge from radically different cultural, philosophical, and
practical traditions. KNF was developed by Dr. Cho Han-Kyu in South
Korea starting in the 1960s, while biodynamic agriculture was founded by
Rudolf Steiner in 1924 through his Agricultural Course lectures in
Silesia. This comparison examines their core philosophies, preparation
methods, soil management approaches, and practical considerations for
farmers choosing between them.
## Origins and Philosophy
**Biodynamic agriculture** is rooted in Rudolf Steiner's anthroposophy,
a [[soil-testing-methods-interpretation]] to empirically demonstrate these subtle
forces. The biodynamic farm is conceived as a self-contained living
organism whose health depends on cosmic and terrestrial rhythms working
in harmony.
**Korean Natural Farming** arose from Dr. Cho Han-Kyu's direct
observations of chemical agriculture's destructive effects on South
Korean farming communities. Cho's philosophy centers on the principle
that "nature is complete and self-sufficient" and that indigenous
microorganisms already possess everything needed for abundant food
production. Where biodynamics looks to cosmic forces and planetary
rhythms, KNF looks to locally adapted biology, plant growth stage
timing, and microbial ecology. Cho was jailed multiple times by South
Korean authorities at the instigation of chemical input lobbies.
## Core Inputs and Preparations
Biodynamic farming relies on **nine numbered preparations** (BD 500-508).
The two field sprays, BD 500 (horn manure, cow manure buried in a horn
over winter) and BD 501 (horn silica, ground quartz buried in a horn
through summer), form the backbone of soil and foliar applications. Six
compost preparations (BD 502-507) use specific plant materials, such as
yarrow in a deer bladder or chamomile in a cow intestine, each buried
in soil over winter to concentrate particular forces. BD 508 is a
horsetail tea used as an anti-fungal spray. All preparations require
specialized animal organs as containers and are applied through a
precise one-hour vortex stirring process called "dynamization."

KNF builds soil through IMO inoculation and a layered soil foundation

soil ecosystem. The approach is explicitly microbial and measurable
through temperature monitoring during fermentation.
## Cosmology and Timing
Biodynamics employs a detailed planting calendar based on the Moon's
position through the zodiac: root crops on Earth-sign days, leaf crops
on Water-sign days, flowering crops on Air-sign days, and fruiting
crops on Fire-sign days. This cosmic timing is considered fundamental
to the system's effectiveness.
KNF uses growth-stage timing rather than astronomical timing. Inputs
are matched to the plant's nutritive cycle: nitrogen-rich inputs like
FPJ during vegetative growth, phosphorus-rich inputs like FAA during
reproductive growth, and calcium-rich inputs during maturation. This
pragmatic, observation-based scheduling contrasts sharply with the
cosmic calendar approach.
## Cost and Accessibility
KNF is explicitly designed for zero-cost input self-sufficiency. Every
input is produced from locally forageable or farm-sourced materials:
wild plants for FPJ, fish waste for FAA, eggshells for WCA, rice bran
