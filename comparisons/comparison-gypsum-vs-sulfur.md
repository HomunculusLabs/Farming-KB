## Gypsum vs Elemental Sulfur

## Overview

[[sulfur-plant-nutrition]] are two important soil
amendments that share a chemical element—sulfur—but serve very different
purposes in soil management. Gypsum supplies both calcium and sulfur
without changing pH, while elemental sulfur is primarily used to lower
[[soil-ph]]. Understanding when to use each is essential for effective
soil amendment [[yield-theory-and-strategies-in-permaculture-design]] and [[biochar-in-regenerative-agriculture]].

## Gypsum (Calcium Sulfate)

[[sulfur-plant-nutrition]] (S) is a yellow crystalline
solid used primarily as a soil acidifier. Soil bacteria from the genus
Thiobacillus slowly oxidize elemental sulfur to sulfate (SO4²⁻),
releasing hydrogen ions that lower soil pH. This process is
temperature-dependent and can take weeks to months, making sulfur a
slow-release acidifying agent.

Sulfur is also an essential plant macronutrient—often called the
"fourth macronutrient" alongside nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium.
It is required for amino acid synthesis (cysteine and methionine),
chlorophyll formation, and the production of plant defense compounds
such as glucosinolates in brassicas and alliin in alliums.

Historically, atmospheric sulfur deposition from industrial emissions
provided 20-80 kg S/ha/year, making deficiency rare. Modern air
quality regulations have reduced these emissions dramatically,
revealing [[query-how-to-fix-sulfur-deficiency-in-cannabis]] many agricultural regions—especially
sandy soils in high-rainfall areas. Elemental sulfur addresses both
the pH and the nutrient supply aspects of this gap.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Gypsum | Elemental Sulfur |
|--------|--------|-------------------|
| **Chemical** | CaSO4·2H2O | S (elemental) |
| **pH Effect** | Neutral (no change) | Lowers pH |
| **Nutrients** | Ca + S | S only |
| **Primary Use** | Soil structure, Na displacement | pH reduction |
| **Speed** | Immediate availability | Slow (weeks-months) |
| **Best Soil** | Sodic/alkaline clays | Alkaline/sweet soils |
| **Rate** | 50-150 g/m² | Variable, by pH target |
| **Leaching** | Moderate sulfate leaching | Minimal until oxidized |
| **Microbes** | Supports via structure | Requires Thiobacillus |

## When to Choose Gypsum

Choose gypsum when dealing with sodic (sodium-rich) clay soils that
seal on the surface during light rain or become impassable when wet.
It is the primary remedy for reclaiming salinized and [[query-how-do-i-establish-a-food-forest-on-degraded-land]].
Because it does not alter pH, gypsum is safe for already-alkaline
soils where lime would be inappropriate.

Gypsum is also preferred when both calcium and sulfur are needed
simultaneously, or when improving soil structure without changing
chemistry is the goal. It works well in combination with deep
drainage, [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] incorporation, and heavy mulching as part
of a comprehensive soil rehabilitation strategy.

## When to Choose Elemental Sulfur

Choose elemental sulfur when soil pH is too high for optimal plant
growth and needs to be lowered. This is common in regions with
naturally [[plants-tolerant-of-alkaline-soils]] or where excessive lime has been applied.
Sulfur is also the right choice when addressing sulfur deficiency
in crops with high sulfur demand, such as brassicas, legumes, and
alliums.

performs best in warm, moist soils with active biological communities.
