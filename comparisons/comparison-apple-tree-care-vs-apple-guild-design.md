## Cost and Input Comparison

Apple tree care involves ongoing purchased inputs: horticultural oil, sulfur or lime-sulfur sprays, spinosad or Bt for codling moth, kaolin clay for plum curculio, and potentially supplemental fertilizers. For a home orchardist with 3-5 trees, annual spray materials typically cost $50-100. The labor calendar is fixed: dormant season oil, green tip fungicide, petal fall insecticide, and follow-up sprays at 10-14 day intervals demand regular attention from March through June.

Apple guild design has higher upfront costs (purchasing comfrey divisions, garlic bulbs, clover seed, and assorted herb plants) but minimal ongoing costs after establishment. Comfrey propagates easily from root cuttings. Clover and dill self-seed. The primary ongoing input is the gardener's time cutting comfrey for mulch and replanting garlic annually. After year two, the guild's fertility and pest control systems operate with very little financial input.

## Adaptability to Other Fruit Trees

The apple guild pattern serves as a template that can be adapted for other fruit tree species, while the core principles of apple tree care (central leader pruning, fruit thinning, pollination planning, and pest management) transfer broadly across temperate fruit trees.

For pear guilds, replace the apple-specific pest deterrents with plants suited to fire blight resistance. Stone fruit guilds benefit from additional plants that deter peach borer and plum curculio. Citrus guilds in warmer climates use species that deter citrus leafminer and scale insects. The structural roles (nitrogen fixer, accumulator, insectary, ground cover, understory) remain the same across all adaptations; only the specific species change.

The pruning system, however, differs by species. Pears are also trained to a central leader, but peaches and plums are typically trained to an open center system. This is where tree care knowledge specific to each species becomes essential and cannot be generalized from the guild alone.
