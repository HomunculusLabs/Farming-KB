## Green Manure vs Mulch

## Overview
Green manure is a crop grown specifically to be incorporated into the soil,
adding [[mulch-types-and-effects]]

Mulch is any material spread over the soil surface to conserve moisture,
suppress weeds, regulate temperature, and feed soil biology. Mulch types span
a wide range: green mulches (grass clippings, fresh cuttings) support
bacterial dominance, while brown mulches (wood chips, autumn leaves, straw)
support fungal dominance. Surface-applied mulch tends to favor fungi since
hyphae can travel from soil into the material more easily than bacteria can
access it from below.

The bacterial-to-fungal ratio can be deliberately shifted through mulch
choice. Bark chips applied as a 2-inch layer last 3-4 years with fungal
dominance throughout due to resistant lignin, cellulose, and waxes. Leaves
decompose completely in about 6 months, with fungal dominance initially
followed by increasing bacterial activity as decomposition progresses. Mulch
with a C:N ratio above 30:1 may cause temporary nitrogen immobilization at
the soil-mulch interface, though this rarely affects the rhizosphere where
[[dighton-mycorrhizal-networks-carbon-transfer]] near the surface. Mulch
should never be placed directly against stems or trunks to prevent microbial
decay of the plant itself. Seasonal timing matters: light spring mulching
allows soil warming, summer replenishment maintains coverage, heavy fall
mulching protects over winter, and winter mulching insulates soil life from
freezing temperatures.

## Key Differences
| Aspect | Green Manure | Mulch |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Status | Living crop, then incorporated | Dead material, surface-applied |
| Soil disturbance | Yes (digging in) | None (surface application) |
| Nutrient source | Grown in place (N fixation) | Imported or collected |
| Timing | Grown during fallow periods | Applied year-round |
| Decomposition | Fast (soft green material) | Variable by material type |
| Weed suppression | Good (dense ground cover) | Excellent (physical barrier) |
| Fungal/bacterial effect | Tends bacterial (green) | Adjustable by material choice |
| No-till compatible | Poor (requires incorporation) | Excellent (no disturbance) |
| Soil structure impact | Disrupts when dug in | Protects and improves |

## When to Choose Green Manure
Green manure is best when a bed will be empty for an extended period (winter
fallow, between crop rotations) and active soil building is desired. It
excels on light, nutrient-poor soils that leach easily during winter.
Leguminous [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]] is
needed for the following crop. Green manure suits conventional and reduced-
till gardens where periodic soil incorporation is acceptable.

## When to Choose Mulch
Mulch is the better choice for no-till gardens, permanent beds, and any
system where soil disturbance must be minimized. It provides immediate weed
suppression and moisture conservation without waiting for a cover crop to
grow. Mulch is more flexible: it can be applied in any season, in any
thickness, with materials chosen to shift soil biology toward bacterial or
fungal dominance as needed.

## Hybrid Approaches
The most effective approach combines both: grow a green manure cover crop
during fallow periods, then terminate it by cutting (not digging in) and
leave the cut material on the surface as mulch. This "chop-and-drop" approach
provides the soil-building benefits of green manure without the soil
disturbance of incorporation. The cut material decomposes as surface mulch,
feeding soil biology while protecting soil structure. Leguminous cover crops
killed at flowering release their fixed nitrogen as the surface mulch
decomposes over weeks and months.
