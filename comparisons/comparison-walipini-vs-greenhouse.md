## When to Choose a Walipini

A walipini is the better choice in cold climates where winter temperatures regularly
drop below freezing and heating a conventional greenhouse would be expensive or
impractical. It suits homesteaders prioritizing self-sufficiency and minimal external
energy inputs. The walipini excels at year-round growing without supplementary heat,
making it ideal for off-grid properties. It is particularly effective in continental
climates with large diurnal temperature swings, where the earth mass moderates both
extremes. Sites with well-drained soil and suitable slope for excavation are
essential requirements.

## When to Choose a Greenhouse

A conventional greenhouse is preferable when maximum light exposure is needed (the
walipini's earth walls block side light), when construction simplicity is important,
when the site has poor drainage or unsuitable soil for excavation, or when the budget
favors a kit-based approach. Greenhouses are more versatile for commercial production
where maximum growing area and easy access are priorities. They are also better suited
to warm and mild climates where the walipini's thermal advantages are less critical.
Greenhouses offer easier expansion, modification, and relocation compared to the
permanent earthwork of a walipini.

## Hybrid Approaches

The principles of both structures can be combined. Earth-bermed greenhouses use
partial earth sheltering on the north wall while maintaining glazed walls on the
remaining sides, balancing thermal performance with light access. Adding thermal mass
(water barrels, stone walls) to a conventional greenhouse approximates some walipini
thermal stability without excavation. A walipini can be built with additional glazed
wall sections on the south face to increase light penetration. [[cold-frame-gardening]]
