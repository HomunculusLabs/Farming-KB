## Arnica vs Calendula

## Overview

Arnica (*Arnica montana*) and calendula (*Calendula officinalis*) are two of
the most widely used topical [[arnica-montana]]

Arnica montana is a perennial herb native to the mountainous meadows of
central and southern Europe, growing at elevations of 600 to 2,800 meters.
The plant produces distinctive golden-yellow flower heads containing
helenalin, a potent sesquiterpene lactone responsible for most of its
pharmacological activity. Helenalin works by inhibiting NF-kB
transcription factor activation, which suppresses the inflammatory cascade
at a molecular level.

Arnica is exclusively a topical remedy. Internal ingestion of arnica
preparations causes severe gastrointestinal distress, cardiac arrhythmias,
and organ damage due to helenalin's reactivity with cellular thiols. Even
topical application on broken skin or large areas should be avoided.
Homeopathic arnica preparations (extremely dilute) are used internally, but
this falls outside standard herbal practice. The German Commission E and
EMA recognise arnica flower preparations for external use in bruises,
sprains, muscle aches, and postoperative swelling.

Cultivation of arnica is challenging. It requires acidic, nutrient-poor
soils, full sun, and cool mountain-like conditions. The species is
critically endangered in much of its native range due to overharvesting,
and most commercial supply now comes from cultivated sources. Gardeners
outside alpine climates may struggle to establish arnica, making it more
practical to purchase dried flowers or prepared extracts.

soil types and blooming prolifically from spring through fall. The vibrant

carotenoids (lutein, beta-carotene).

Calendula's primary medicinal action is promoting wound healing through
stimulation of fibroblast proliferation, collagen synthesis, and
angiogenesis. It also exhibits anti-inflammatory activity by inhibiting

Calendula salve or cream soothes diaper rash, eczema, dermatitis, and
radiation burns in cancer patients. Because it is antimicrobial, calendula
helps prevent infection in minor wounds while promoting tissue repair. Its
safety for internal use also makes it appropriate for mouth ulcers, sore
throats (as a gargle), and digestive complaints.

## Growing Both in a Home Garden

Calendula is effortless to grow from direct-sown seed in any sunny garden
bed, producing abundant flowers from 45 to 60 days after planting. Arnica,
by contrast, requires dedicated effort: acidic sandy soil (pH 5.0 to 6.0),
excellent drainage, full sun, and cool conditions. In warm climates, arnica
may need partial shade and consistent moisture. Many gardeners choose to
grow calendula and purchase arnica products rather than attempt cultivation.
For those with suitable conditions, arnica propagated from seed or division
rewarded with a renewable supply of this valuable medicinal flower.
