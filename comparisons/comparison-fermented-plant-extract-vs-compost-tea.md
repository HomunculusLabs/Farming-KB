# Fermented Plant Extract vs Compost Tea

## Overview
Fermented Plant Extract (FPE) and Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) are
both liquid biological amendments used to feed soil and foliar biology, but they
originate from entirely different traditions and work through different
mechanisms. FPE is a Korean Natural Farming input made by fermenting fresh plant
material with sugar to capture specific nutrients and growth hormones from
locally adapted plants. Compost tea is a Soil Food Web approach that extracts
and multiplies the full [[edaphic-factors-microbial-community-structure]] from finished compost. Choosing
between them depends on whether you need targeted nutrient delivery with minimal
equipment or broad microbial inoculation with proven soil restoration capacity.

## Fermented Plant Extract
[[compost-tea-guide]]

Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) is a liquid extract of finished compost
that is brewed with food resources and continuous aeration to multiply
beneficial microorganisms. Developed by Dr. Elaine Ingham, AACT delivers the
full soil food web, including bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes, directly
to soil or foliage. The brewing process actively extracts organisms from
high-quality compost and provides them with food sources like molasses, kelp
meal, and fish hydrolysate to drive population growth during a 24 to 36 hour
brew cycle. Dissolved oxygen must stay above 6 ppm throughout.

Compost tea is fundamentally a microbial inoculation tool. Its purpose is not
to deliver nutrients directly, though some nutrient content is present in the
brew, but to introduce and multiply the diverse community of organisms that
cycle nutrients, suppress pathogens, and build soil structure. The food
resources added during brewing are selected to favor either bacterial or fungal
dominance depending on the target crop: molasses and soybean meal favor bacteria
for vegetables and annuals, while kelp, humic acids, and oatmeal favor fungi for
perennials and trees.

The quality of compost tea is entirely dependent on the quality of the
starting compost. Compost that is anaerobic, contaminated, or biologically
impoverished will produce poor tea regardless of brewing technique. This makes
compost tea a more demanding input to produce correctly, requiring quality
compost, aeration equipment, dissolved oxygen monitoring, and careful attention
to brewing conditions. The reward is a living microbial amendment that can
transform soil biology rapidly when applied correctly. AACT must be used within
four hours of brewing, as the aerobic organisms begin dying once aeration stops.

## Key Differences
| Aspect | Fermented Plant Extract | Compost Tea |
|--------|------------------------|-------------|
| Origin tradition | Korean Natural Farming (Cho) | Soil Food Web (Ingham) |
| Primary purpose | Nutrient and hormone delivery | Microbial inoculation |
| Input material | Fresh plant parts + brown sugar | Finished compost + food
resources |
| Equipment needed | Glass jars only | Air pump, airstones, brewer vessel |
| Brewing time | 7-14 days fermentation | 24-36 hours active brewing |
| Cost | Very low (sugar + wild plants) | Moderate (compost, pump, foods) |
| Microbial diversity | Low (lactic acid bacteria, yeasts) | High (bacteria,
fungi, protozoa) |
| Specificity | High (plant-specific nutrients) | Broad (general soil
biology) |
| Dilution rate | 1:500 to 1:1000 | Undiluted or light dilution |
| Shelf life | 6-12 months (refrigerated) | Must use within 4 hours |
| Skill required | Low (basic fermentation) | Moderate (brewing management) |
| Scalability | Easy at any scale | Requires matching brewer size |

## When to Choose Fermented Plant Extract
FPE is ideal for growers following Korean Natural Farming or seeking
low-cost, low-tech inputs that target specific crop needs. When a crop shows
deficiency symptoms, selecting the right FPE from a specific plant can address
the shortfall precisely. FPE is also excellent for small-scale growers who
cannot invest in [[chamovitz-circadian-rhythm-internal-clock-plants]], and for those who want to leverage locally
abundant wild plants as [[natural-fertilizer-sources-and-application]]. The long shelf life and simplicity
make it practical for anyone, regardless of scale. Backpack sprayers can apply
FPJ over large areas with minimal effort. See [[knf-input-application-timing]] for scheduling guidance.

## When to Choose Compost Tea
Compost tea is the better choice when the goal is rapid soil biology
restoration or establishing a diverse microbial community in depleted soils. It
provides the broadest spectrum of organisms and is particularly effective for
foliar [[aact-disease-suppression-mechanisms-ingham]] when sprayed directly onto leaf surfaces at sunset.
Commercial growers, orchardists, and anyone with an existing high-quality
compost supply will get the most from AACT brewing. It is also the preferred
approach for remediation projects where introducing a complete soil food web is
necessary to jumpstart nutrient cycling. See [[compost-tea-application-rates]] for application
guidance.

## Hybrid Approaches
Many growers use both in complementary roles rather than choosing one
exclusively. FPE provides targeted nutrient support at specific growth stages
while compost tea establishes and maintains the underlying microbial community.
Some practitioners add a small amount of FPJ to compost tea as an additional
food source during brewing, combining the plant-derived nutrients and enzymes
with the microbial multiplication of AACT. In KNF systems, FPE is one of many
inputs that work alongside IMO ([[knf-indigenous-microorganism-culture]]) solutions that
function similarly to compost tea in introducing local microbial communities. A
complete program might use compost tea for initial soil biology restoration,
then maintain it with regular FPE applications for crop-specific nutrition.
