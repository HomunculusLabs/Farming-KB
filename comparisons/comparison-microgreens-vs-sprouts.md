# Microgreens vs. Sprouts

## At a Glance

| Feature | Microgreens | Sprouts |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Definition** | Young seedlings harvested 7–21 days after germination, after first true leaves appear | Germinated seeds eaten within 2–7 days, before true leaves develop |
| **What You Eat** | Stem and cotyledon (seed leaf) or first true leaves | Entire sprout — seed, root, shoot |
| **Growing Medium** | Soil, coco coir, hemp mats, hydroponic pads | Water only (no medium) |
| **Light Required** | Yes — needs light for photosynthesis and color | No — grown in dark or low light |
| **Days to Harvest** | 7–21 days | 2–7 days |
| **Food Safety Risk** | Low — clean medium, minimal humidity | Higher — warm, moist conditions promote bacterial growth |
| **Shelf Life** | 5–14 days refrigerated | 3–5 days refrigerated |
| **Nutrient Density** | Very high — 4–40× vitamin levels of mature plants | High in vitamins C and B, enzymes |
| **Flavor** | Intense, concentrated, varies widely by species | Mild, crisp, less variety |
| **Best Species** | Radish, sunflower, pea, basil, cilantro, kale, broccoli | Mung bean, alfalfa, lentil, chickpea, radish, clover |
| **Setup Cost** | $30–150 (trays, medium, light optional) | $10–50 (jar or sprouting bag) |

## Growing Process

### Microgreens
1. **Sow** untreated seeds densely on pre-moistened medium in shallow trays.
2. **Cover** for 2–4 days (blackout period) to encourage root establishment.
3. **Expose** to light — plants green up within 24–48 hours.
4. **Water** from below 1–2× daily; avoid overhead watering to prevent mold.
5. **Harvest** by cutting stems just above the medium at 1–3 inches tall when first true leaves appear.
6. **Refrigerate** immediately; use within 5–14 days.

### Sprouts
1. **Soak** pathogen-tested seeds 8–12 hours (2–4 hours for small seeds like alfalfa).
2. **Drain** and place in jar with mesh lid, sprouting bag, or tiered sprouter.
3. **Rinse** 2–3× daily at 18–24°C, out of direct sunlight.
4. **Harvest** at 3–7 days when sprouts reach desired length; final rinse and drain.
5. **Refrigerate** and use within 3–5 days.

## Nutritional Differences

- **Vitamins:** Microgreens generally have higher vitamins K, E, C, and beta-carotene due to photosynthesis. Sprouts peak in vitamin C and B vitamins during germination.
- **Minerals:** Microgreens absorb minerals from the growing medium (higher Ca, Fe, Mg, Zn). Sprouts contain only what was in the seed.
- **Antioxidants:** Both are excellent. Broccoli microgreens have 10–50× the sulforaphane of mature broccoli. Radish microgreens are among the highest in overall antioxidant capacity.
- **Enzymes:** Sprouts are the clear winner — germination activates amylase, protease, and lipase that aid digestion.

**Protein content:** Mung bean and lentil sprouts provide 3–4 g protein per 100 g fresh weight. Sunflower and pea microgreens provide 2–4 g protein per 100 g. Both are meaningful plant protein sources relative to their calorie content.

## Food Safety

**This is the most significant differentiator.**

**Sprouts carry documented foodborne illness risk.** The warm, moist conditions required for sprouting are ideal for E. coli and Salmonella. Major outbreaks have been linked to commercial sprouts. The FDA categorizes sprouts as high-risk food.

- Use only pathogen-tested seeds labeled for sprouting
- Sanitize equipment between batches; pre-soak seeds in 3% hydrogen peroxide
- Vulnerable populations (pregnant, elderly, immunocompromised) should avoid raw sprouts

**Microgreens are considerably safer.** Growing in well-draining medium with light and air circulation creates conditions far less hospitable to pathogens. The seed itself is not consumed, further reducing risk.

## Economic Comparison

| Factor | Microgreens | Sprouts |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| Cost per 100 g produced | $0.30–2.50 | $0.07–0.30 |
| Retail price per 100 g | $3–8 | $1–3 |
| Yield per harvest | 100–300 g | 200–500 g |

Microgreens cost more but command higher prices — viable for small business. Sprouts are the cheapest fresh food you can produce at home.

---

## Which Should You Grow?

**Choose Microgreens if:** you want maximum flavor and visual appeal; food safety is a priority; you want to sell at markets or restaurants; or you want species that don't sprout well (basil, cilantro, amaranth).

**Choose Sprouts if:** you want the fastest harvest with minimal equipment; you have very limited space; you primarily want mung beans, lentils, or alfalfa; or you value high enzyme content.
