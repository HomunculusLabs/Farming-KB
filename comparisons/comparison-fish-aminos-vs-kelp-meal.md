# Fish Aminos vs Kelp Meal

Fish aminos and kelp meal are two of the most powerful organic inputs available
to growers, but they serve fundamentally different nutritional roles. Fish aminos
deliver rapid nitrogen in amino acid form, while kelp meal provides a broad
spectrum of trace minerals and natural growth hormones. Understanding when and
how to use each input maximizes their complementary benefits.

## Overview

**Fish Aminos** are a [[korean-natural-farming]] (KNF) bionutrient produced by
fermenting fish scraps with brown sugar or molasses at a 1:3 ratio (fish to
sugar by weight). The fermentation process, which takes 2-4 weeks, preserves
enzymes and breaks down proteins into amino acids that plants can absorb
directly through foliage and roots. The resulting liquid is a high-nitrogen
biostimulant diluted at 1:20 for foliar sprays or soil drenches.

Fish aminos are applied primarily during the [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] when plants
have the highest demand for nitrogen. A key advantage over commercial fish
emulsion is that the low-pH fermentation preserves heat-sensitive enzymes that
commercial processing destroys. The production cost is essentially zero if fish
scraps are available from local sources. The primary limitation is a strong
nitrogen focus with relatively low levels of phosphorus, potassium, and trace
elements. Odor during fermentation can be significant.

**Kelp Meal** is a slow-release organic amendment made from dried and ground
seaweed, typically Ascophyllum nodosum. Its NPK is approximately 2.8-0.2-2.3,
but its real value lies in the 60-70 trace elements it provides, including
iron, zinc, manganese, boron, and cobalt. Kelp meal also contains natural plant
growth hormones including cytokinins, auxins, and gibberellins that stimulate
root development, improve stress resistance, and enhance overall plant vigor.

It can be used as a soil amendment mixed into beds, as a compost activator,
steeped as a foliar spray, or applied as a seed treatment. The alginate in kelp
improves soil structure by enhancing water retention and aggregation. Kelp meal
feeds beneficial soil microbes, supporting the [[compost-chemistry-soil-food-web]]. The main drawbacks
are higher cost compared to other [[cannabis-organic-fertilizers]] and a low NPK that means
it cannot serve as a primary nitrogen source.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Fish Aminos | Kelp Meal |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Nutrient Profile | High nitrogen in amino acid form | Low NPK with 60-70 trace elements |
| Application Method | Foliar spray or soil drench (liquid) | Soil amendment, compost, or foliar |
| Primary Function | Rapid nitrogen boost, vegetative stage | Trace minerals, hormones, stress resistance |
| Growth Stage | Vegetative stage primarily | All stages, especially transplanting |
| Production Cost | Nearly free if fish scraps available | More expensive than most amendments |
| Speed of Availability | Rapid; amino acids absorbed directly | Slow-release; minerals break down over weeks |
| Soil Building | Minimal direct soil improvement | Improves structure via alginates, feeds microbes |
| Trace Elements | Limited to what fish contain | Extremely broad spectrum from seaweed |
| Growth Hormones | Some enzymes, no known hormones | Cytokinins, auxins, gibberellins |
| Storage | Fermented liquid, months at room temp | Dry powder, stable for years |

## Fish Aminos: Production and Application

Producing fish aminos requires fresh fish scraps (heads, guts, bones, and
scales), brown sugar or molasses, and an airtight container. Layer the fish
and sugar alternately in a 1:3 ratio by weight, filling the container to
two-thirds capacity to allow for gas expansion. Store in a cool, shaded
location for 2-4 weeks, opening briefly to release gas buildup every few days
during active fermentation. The finished product separates into a dark liquid
layer on top and settled solids below. Strain the liquid and store in a cool
dark place.

For foliar application, dilute at 1:20 (one part fish aminos to twenty parts
water) and spray on leaf surfaces in early morning or late evening. For soil
drenches, the same dilution is applied to the root zone every 1-2 weeks during
active vegetative growth. The amino acid form of nitrogen is absorbed through
leaf stomata within hours, making fish aminos one of the fastest-acting organic
nitrogen sources available.

## Kelp Meal: Production and Application

Kelp meal is harvested from sustainable seaweed beds, dried at low temperatures
to preserve heat-sensitive compounds, and ground to a coarse powder. The drying
temperature is critical because temperatures above 120F begin to degrade
gibberellins and other growth hormones. Cold-processed kelp meal retains the
highest levels of active compounds.

As a soil amendment, mix 1-2 cups of kelp meal per 100 square feet into
planting beds before seeding or transplanting. For compost activation, sprinkle
a handful between each layer of compost material. For foliar application, steep
1-2 tablespoons of kelp meal per gallon of water for 24-48 hours, strain, and
spray. As a seed treatment, dust seeds lightly with kelp powder before planting
to improve germination rates and seedling vigor.

## When to Choose Fish Aminos

Fish aminos are the right choice when you need a fast-acting nitrogen boost
during the vegetative growth phase. They are ideal for heavy-feeding crops like
corn, tomatoes, leafy greens, and cannabis during early growth. The amino acid
form is more efficient than conventional nitrogen because plants absorb it
directly without expending energy on microbial conversion. Fish aminos also
provide a mild phosphorus contribution and enzymes that support plant metabolism.

## When to Choose Kelp Meal

Kelp meal is the better choice when you want comprehensive trace mineral
supplementation, natural growth hormone stimulation, and soil biology support.
It is particularly valuable at transplanting to reduce shock, during flowering
to support fruit quality, and during periods of environmental stress such as
heat waves, drought, or pest pressure.

## Hybrid Approach: Combined Use

Combining fish aminos and kelp meal is standard practice in KNF and holistic
organic growing systems. The typical protocol applies fish aminos for nitrogen
during vegetative growth and kelp for trace minerals and hormones throughout
the entire growth cycle. Many growers combine both in a single foliar spray,
using fish aminos at 1:20 dilution plus kelp tea, to deliver both rapid
nitrogen and comprehensive micronutrients in one application. This synergy
covers the full range of plant nutritional needs.

## Application Tips

Fish aminos should be applied in early morning or evening to avoid leaf burn
and maximize absorption. Kelp meal foliar sprays benefit from a small amount
of mild soap as a surfactant to improve coverage. Both inputs can be combined
with other KNF inputs like oriental herbal nutrient or fermented plant juice
for a complete biostimulant program.
