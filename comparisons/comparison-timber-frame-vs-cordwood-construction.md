# Timber Frame vs Cordwood Construction

## Overview

Timber framing and cordwood construction are two distinct natural building
methods that both rely heavily on wood as a primary material. Timber framing
uses large structural timbers connected by wooden joinery to create a load-
bearing frame, while cordwood masonry stacks short log segments in mortar to
build thick, insulating walls. Understanding their differences helps builders
choose the right approach for their climate, budget, and skill level.

## Timber Frame Construction

Timber frames excel in structural spans, allowing open interior spaces without
interior load-bearing walls. They are ideal for barns, great rooms, and spaces
where the frame itself becomes the aesthetic centerpiece. The building process
involves cutting all joinery on the ground, then raising the frame as an event
often involving community labor or a crane.

## Cordwood Construction

building that combines structural elegance with high energy performance.

## When to Choose Cordwood Construction

Choose cordwood when your priority is affordability and you have access to
abundant, low-cost wood. It is ideal for owner-builders with limited
construction experience who want to build incrementally — you can lay a few
courses of cordwood each weekend without specialized tools or training.
Cordwood excels in cold climates where its thick, insulated walls dramatically
reduce heating costs.

Cordwood is also an excellent choice when the building site produces its own
material. If you are clearing land for the building footprint, the resulting
logs can become the walls. The technique works well for small cabins, garden
walls, and outbuildings. When paired with a simple [[comparison-cob-vs-cordwood|post-and-beam structure]] for the roof,
cordwood creates a complete, comfortable shelter at minimal expense.

## Hybrid Approaches

Many successful natural buildings combine both methods: a timber frame provides
the structural skeleton, and cordwood masonry fills the walls between posts.
This hybrid leverages the spanning capability of timber framing with the
insulation and affordability of cordwood. The timber posts are embedded in
the cordwood walls, and the roof is carried entirely by the frame.

This combination is one of the most popular approaches in the natural building
community because it requires less joinery skill than a full timber frame
(the cordwood infill needs no precision joinery) while still producing a
beautiful, energy-efficient structure. Builders often use a simplified post-
and-beam frame with basic connections rather than full traditional joinery.
