## Key Differences
| Aspect | Biointensive | Permaculture |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| Nature | Specific growing method with 8 components | Design framework applicable to any system |
| Primary Focus | Maximum calories in minimal space | Whole-system design for resilience |
| Prescriptiveness | High (spacing, digging, ratios defined) | Low (principles, not recipes) |

## When to Choose Biointensive
Biointensive is ideal for small-scale growers who want maximum food production from
limited space and are willing to invest significant physical labor. Urban and suburban
gardeners with small yards, community gardeners, and those in developing countries
with scarce land and water resources benefit most. The method is highly prescriptive,
making it accessible to beginners who want clear instructions and predictable results.
Biointensive excels when the primary goal is calorie self-sufficiency from annual
crops and the practitioner can commit to all eight interdependent components. Its
closed-loop fertility model eliminates dependence on external inputs, making it
truly self-sufficient once the compost cycle is established.
