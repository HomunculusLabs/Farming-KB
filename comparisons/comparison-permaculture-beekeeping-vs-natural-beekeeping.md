## Permaculture Beekeeping vs Natural Beekeeping

## Overview

Permaculture beekeeping and natural beekeeping share the fundamental goal of working
with bees as partners rather than exploiting them as industrial commodities. Both
emerged as responses to conventional practices that prioritize [[permaculture-design]] system. Without their pollination, producing enough food would be impossible,
and they additionally produce honey, beeswax, propolis, and royal jelly. The approach
focuses on ensuring adequate forage, proper hive placement within the broader
landscape, and integration with other elements including water harvesting, windbreaks,
and food forests.

Key placement considerations include positioning hives at least 300 feet from food
sources (bees prefer this flying distance), sheltering hives from cold wind using
herb hedges of rosemary, acacia, thyme, or catmint as little as 3 feet tall, and
pointing the hive entrance toward forage areas and away from human activity.

A minimum of 30 forage species is recommended, including gooseberries, apples, white
clover, blackberries, citrus, buckwheat, mustard, and fragrant herbs. Bees need both
pollen (protein) and nectar (carbohydrates): pollen species within 100 feet and
nectar species at 300+ feet ensures both needs are met. Multiple honeybee types are
considered: Italian bees work harder, Caucasian bees sting less, and Carniolan bees
are gentlest. Equipment needs include standard hive components, smoker, hive tool,
bee clothing, and feeder. The permaculture beekeeper manages hives systematically for
pollination as the primary yield and honey as a secondary yield.
