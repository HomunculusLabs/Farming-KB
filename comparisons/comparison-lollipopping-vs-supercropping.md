# Lollipopping vs Supercropping

## Overview

Lollipopping and supercropping are two popular [[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]] techniques that serve very
different purposes in the grow cycle. Lollipopping is a defoliation and pruning strategy that
redirects plant energy toward the upper canopy by removing lower growth, while supercropping
is a high-stress training method that manipulates plant structure to increase branching and
bud site production. One focuses on energy allocation and canopy efficiency, the other on
structural modification and lateral growth. Both techniques can dramatically improve yield
and quality when applied correctly, and many growers use them together in a complementary
fashion throughout the vegetative and early flowering stages.

## Lollipopping

[[jorge-cervantes]], a respected cannabis cultivation
authority, supports this approach, recommending that growers "remove spindly, dimly lit
lower branches" to improve overall bud quality and reduce the plant's maintenance burden.

Beyond energy redirection, lollipopping offers several additional benefits. The removal of
lower foliage dramatically improves airflow through the plant's base, which reduces humidity
trapping and lowers the risk of mold and [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]] during flowering. It also makes
maintenance tasks like watering, [[cannabis-training-techniques-comparison]]

Supercropping is an advanced high-stress training (HST) technique that involves deliberate,
controlled damage to plant tissue in order to manipulate growth patterns and increase
branching. The most common form of supercropping is stem crushing, where the grower
squeezes and crushes the inner tissue of a branch between thumb and forefinger while
keeping the outer bark intact, then bends the branch to a more horizontal position. The
plant responds to this trauma by forming a thickened "knuckle" or callus at the damage
site that is actually stronger than the original stem. This knuckle redirects auxin
hormone flow, encouraging the plant to develop multiple lateral branches from the bend
point rather than continuing single upward growth.

The auxin manipulation is the key mechanism behind supercropping's effectiveness. Auxin
is a plant hormone that primarily flows downward from the apical meristem (the main growing
tip), and its concentration suppresses lateral branching. By bending the stem horizontally,
the grower disrupts this downward auxin flow and redistributes it more evenly along the
branch, which signals the plant to activate dormant bud sites and push out new lateral
growth. The result is a bushier plant with significantly more bud sites than an untrained
plant, all without the permanent tissue removal required by topping or fimming.

A more aggressive variation of supercropping involves partial branch breaking, where the
stem is bent until it partially snaps but remains connected. This method is riskier but
can produce even more dramatic branching when combined with a FIM (Fuck I Missed) cut at
the bend point. Supercropping should only be performed during the [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]], ideally
between weeks 3 and 6 when stems are flexible enough to bend without fully breaking. Growers
should allow 3 to 7 days of recovery between supercropping sessions and should never damage
more than 20 percent of the plant's foliage at once to avoid excessive stress that could
slow growth or invite disease. Properly executed, supercropping is one of the most effective
[[query-what-are-the-best-ways-to-increase-soil-organic-matter-quickly]] yield without adding more plants or extending the vegetative period.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Lollipopping | Supercropping |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Primary goal | Energy redirection to top canopy | Structural modification for more branching |
| Technique type | Defoliation and pruning | High-stress training (HST) |
| Timing | Pre-flower or early flower (weeks 1-2) | Vegetative stage only (weeks 3-6) |
| Tissue removal | Yes, lower branches removed | No, tissue is damaged but not removed |
| Plant stress level | Low to moderate | High |
| Effect on yield | Improves top cola quality | Increases total bud site count |
| Recovery time | Minimal | 3-7 days per session |
| Skill level required | Beginner to intermediate | Intermediate to advanced |
| Airflow improvement | Significant | Moderate |
| Reversibility | No, branches are permanently removed | Partially, plant adapts and heals |

## When to Choose Lollipopping

Lollipopping is the right choice when your plants have developed dense lower canopies that
block light penetration and trap moisture. If you notice popcorn buds forming in the lower
third of your plants that never receive adequate light, lollipopping will eliminate this
wasted energy expenditure and redirect those resources to the top colas where they produce
measurable results. The technique is especially valuable in indoor grows with limited vertical
space, where every inch of canopy height matters and lower growth simply cannot compete for
light. It is also an excellent preventative measure against mold and [[query-how-do-i-prevent-and-treat-bud-rot-on-cannabis]] in humid grow
environments, as the improved airflow around the plant base significantly reduces disease risk.

Lollipopping is also ideal for growers who want to simplify their harvest process. By removing
lower bud sites early, you eliminate the tedious work of trimming dozens of small, low-quality
popcorn buds at harvest time. This is particularly beneficial for commercial growers or anyone
processing large volumes, where labor efficiency directly impacts profitability. The technique
requires no special equipment beyond a clean pair of trimming scissors and can be performed in
a single session, making it one of the most time-efficient improvements a grower can make.

## When to Choose Supercropping

Supercropping is the better option when you want to increase the number of bud sites on your
plants without permanently removing any growth. If you have plenty of vertical space but need
more lateral branching to fill your canopy, supercropping creates a bushier, wider plant with
more potential flowering sites. The technique is especially effective for tall, stretch-prone
strains that tend to grow a single dominant cola; supercropping breaks that apical dominance
and forces the plant to develop multiple productive branches instead of one tall spike.

Supercropping is also invaluable for height management in spaces with low ceilings. By
bending tall branches horizontal, you can keep plants at a manageable height while
simultaneously increasing their width and bud site count. This makes it an essential tool
for growers working in small tents or cabinets where vertical space is at a premium. The
thickened knuckles formed at bend points also provide natural stress points that can support
heavier colas during flowering, reducing the need for external support structures like bamboo
stakes or netting.

## Hybrid Approaches

The most effective approach for many growers is to use both techniques in sequence. During
the vegetative stage, supercropping builds a wide, multi-branch structure with abundant bud
sites. Then, 1 to 2 weeks before flowering, lollipopping removes the lowest performing
branches from this expanded structure, concentrating all of the plant's energy on the
upper two-thirds of the supercropped canopy. This combination maximizes both the quantity
of top-tier bud sites (from supercropping) and the quality of the resulting colas (from
lollipopping), producing some of the highest-quality yields achievable with training alone.
