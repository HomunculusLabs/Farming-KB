# Greywater Systems vs Constructed Wetlands
## Overview
Greywater systems and constructed wetlands both recycle household
wastewater ecologically, but they differ in scale, treatment intensity,
and design philosophy. Greywater systems focus on distributing
household wastewater directly to landscape plants through simple
plumbing, while constructed wetlands engineer full ecosystem treatment
cycles for higher water quality before reuse or discharge. Choosing
between them depends on water volume, available space, treatment goals,
and whether blackwater is included in the waste stream.

## Key Differences
| Aspect | Greywater Systems | Constructed Wetlands |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Primary Goal | Irrigation and nutrient reuse | Water purification |
| Water Sources | Greywater only (sinks, showers, laundry) | Greywater and pre-treated blackwater |
| Treatment Level | Low (soil-based filtration) | High (BOD, N, P, pathogens, metals) |
| Space Required | Minimal (mulch basins in garden) | Moderate (5 to 10 sq ft per person, SSF) |
| Complexity | Low (simple plumbing) | Moderate (liner, gravel, outlet control) |
| Cost | $50 to $500 DIY | $500 to $5,000+ depending on scale |
| Maintenance | Annual mulch replacement | Occasional harvesting, media replacement |
| Mosquito Risk | None (subsurface delivery) | Possible with surface flow designs |
| Winter Operation | Requires diverter in cold climates | Reduced performance, possible freezing |
| Effluent Quality | Suitable for subsurface irrigation | Suitable for irrigation or discharge |
## When to Choose Greywater Systems
Greywater systems are ideal for homeowners who want a simple, low-cost
way to irrigate ornamental plants and [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]] with household
wastewater. They work best on sites with adequate slope for gravity
flow, on permeable soils, and where the primary goal is water
conservation rather than high-quality effluent treatment. The branched
drain approach requires no electricity and can be built in a weekend
with basic PVC plumbing skills.
Greywater systems excel in dry climates where every gallon of
irrigation water matters. They integrate naturally into permaculture
food forests, where fruit trees and perennial plants benefit from the
steady moisture and nutrient input. Households with 1 to 4 occupants
producing typical greywater volumes of 30 to 100 gallons per day are
well served by simple systems without surge tanks or pumps.
## When to Choose Constructed Wetlands
Constructed wetlands are the better choice when treatment performance
matters more than simplicity. They handle combined wastewater streams
including septic tank effluent, produce higher quality effluent, and
can meet discharge standards in regulated jurisdictions. Small-scale
systems of 2 to 5 square meters per person can provide secondary
treatment for an entire household.
Constructed wetlands also serve larger applications such as community
[[mollison-designers-wastewater-treatment-wetlands]], agricultural runoff management, industrial
effluent polishing, and acid [[acid-mine-drainage-treatment]] remediation. Their ability
to remove nutrients, metals, and emerging contaminants through
integrated biological and chemical processes makes them far more
versatile than simple greywater distribution. Sites with poor
infiltration soils, high water tables, or seasonal flooding benefit
from the contained, lined design of constructed wetlands.
## Hybrid Approaches
Many [[constructed-wetlands-wastewater-treatment]] systems combine both approaches in series.
Greywater from sinks and showers flows first through a mulch basin
for primary filtration and nutrient delivery to landscape plants, then
excess water overflows into a small constructed wetland for additional
treatment before subsurface irrigation of a larger area. This staged
approach maximizes water reuse while ensuring adequate purification.
Another hybrid pattern sends blackwater to a septic tank for primary
treatment, then routes the effluent through a constructed wetland for
secondary polishing. Separately, a greywater branched drain system
directly irrigates a food forest with minimal treatment overhead.
This dual-path design, advocated by permaculture designers including
Toby Hemenway, keeps the nutrient-rich greywater in the garden
ecosystem while still achieving high treatment standards for
blackwater.
