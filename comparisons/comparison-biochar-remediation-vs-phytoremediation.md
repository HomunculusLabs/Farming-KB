## Limitations

### Biochar Limitations

- Strong sorption capacity can reduce biodegradability of organic
  contaminants, paradoxically slowing natural attenuation.
- May temporarily sorb essential nutrients, requiring adjusted
  fertilization strategies.
- Production requires energy input for pyrolysis.
- Some biochars contain residual contaminants (PAHs, heavy metals)
  depending on feedstock and production conditions.
- Does not remove contaminants from the environment; only reduces
  bioavailability.

### Phytoremediation Limitations

- Treatment depth is limited to the root zone (typically 1-3 m).
- Remediation timelines span growing seasons to multiple years.
- Performance is climate-dependent (growing season length,
  temperature, precipitation).
- Plant tolerance thresholds limit applicability at highly
  contaminated sites.
- Harvested contaminated biomass requires proper disposal.

## Synergy and Combined Approaches

Biochar and phytoremediation are highly complementary. Biochar can
reduce contaminant phytotoxicity to levels that allow plant
establishment on sites previously too contaminated for vegetation.
The biochar-amended rhizosphere then supports enhanced microbial
activity, potentially improving rhizodegradation rates.

In combined applications, biochar provides immediate risk reduction
through immobilization while plants work over time to extract or
degrade remaining contaminants. This layered approach is increasingly
recognized as a best practice for medium-contamination sites.
