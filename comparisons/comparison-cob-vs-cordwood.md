## When to Choose Cob

Choose cob for mild to moderate climates where thermal mass alone provides
adequate temperature regulation. Owner-built homes using on-site materials
benefit enormously from cob's near-zero material cost when suitable clay
subsoil is available. Sculptural structures such as garden walls, outdoor
ovens, benches, and sheds are ideal cob projects that build skills for
larger buildings. Permaculture homesteads appreciate cob's integration with
the landscape and its ability to incorporate recycled materials like glass
bottles for light features.

Cob performs best with a good roof overhang, a raised stone foundation that
keeps walls dry, and breathable lime or earthen plaster finishes. Hybrid
buildings can use cob on south-facing walls for [[comparison-bamboo-construction-vs-timber-frame-basics]] or living roof structures. This compatibility makes
combining them straightforward for builders comfortable with both materials.

## Bottom Line

Cob maximizes design freedom and minimizes material cost in mild climates
where thermal mass drives comfort. Cordwood delivers both mass and
insulation for cold-climate efficiency while creating a distinctive aesthetic.
Both are owner-builder friendly, and the most resilient natural buildings
often incorporate elements of each.
