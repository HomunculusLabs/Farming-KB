## Soil Solarization vs Occultation

## Overview

Both soil solarization and occultation (tarping) are non-chemical methods for preparing garden beds by suppressing weeds, reducing soil-borne pathogens, and managing soil biology. They share the goal of creating a clean planting environment without herbicides or tillage, but they achieve it through fundamentally different mechanisms. ## Mechanism of Action

### Soil Solarization

Solarization works by trapping solar radiation beneath a clear plastic tarp, raising soil temperatures to lethal levels for weed seeds, soil-borne pathogens, nematodes, and some soil insect pests. Clear (not black) plastic is essential — it allows shortwave radiation to penetrate and heat the soil, while the plastic traps the resulting longwave thermal radiation. Properly executed solarization raises soil temperatures 10–15°F above ambient at depths of 2–6 inches, sustained over 4–8 weeks during the hottest part of the year.

Peak soil temperatures under solarization typically reach 110–125°F (43–52°C) at 2 inches depth and 100–108°F (38–42°C) at 6 inches. Most weed seeds are killed at sustained temperatures above 108°F, though some species (especially large-seeded and deeply dormant ones) can survive. Pathogens like *Fusarium*, *Pythium*, *Rhizoctonia*, and *Verticillium* are significantly reduced at these temperatures. The combination of heat and moisture creates a particularly lethal environment for soil organisms.

### Occultation (Tarping)

Occultation suppresses weeds by blocking light with an opaque tarp — typically black plastic, silage tarps, or heavy [[query-how-do-i-create-a-seed-bank-and-start-seed-saving]].

Occultation does not kill weed seeds through heat — it kills seedlings by denying them light. It is less effective against deeply dormant seeds that don't germinate during the tarping period, but it works well against the readily germinable portion of the seed bank. It also does not kill soil pathogens through heat, though the anaerobic conditions that can develop under impermeable tarps may suppress some organisms.
