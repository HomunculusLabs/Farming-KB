## Companion Planting vs Monoculture
## Overview
Companion planting and monoculture represent opposite ends of the biodiversity
spectrum in food production. Companion planting arranges different species
together to create mutual benefits including pest deterrence, nutrient sharing,
improved pollination, and efficient space utilization. Monoculture grows a
single crop across a large area, maximizing efficiency of planting, maintenance,
and harvest but creating vulnerability to pests, disease, and soil depletion.
The tension between these approaches reflects a fundamental question in
agriculture: does biological diversity increase or decrease overall productivity
and system resilience?

## When to Choose Companion Planting
Companion planting is the best approach for home gardens, permaculture food
forests, and small diversified farms where maximizing total production from
limited space matters more than harvesting efficiency. It is ideal for organic
growers who rely on biological pest management rather than chemical inputs.
Companion planting creates a more resilient system where the failure of one crop
does not devastate the entire garden, and the diversity supports pollinators,
predatory insects, and soil biology. Beginner gardeners benefit because
companion planting provides insurance against crop failure: if one species
struggles, others compensate.
## When to Choose Monoculture
Monoculture with JADAM's residue-return approach is practical when you need to
maximize efficiency for a single high-value crop, when equipment and
infrastructure are designed for one crop type, or when market demand focuses on
a specific product. JADAM's approach makes monoculture viable without the
traditional soil degradation, provided crop residues are systematically returned
to the soil and chemical inputs are minimized. This approach suits commercial
growers who need planting, maintenance, and harvest efficiency, and who can
manage soil biology through residue return and microbial diversity maintenance
rather than complex crop rotation schedules.
