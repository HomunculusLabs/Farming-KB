## Regulatory Landscape

Kava and kratom face very different regulatory environments:

**Kava** experienced temporary bans in Europe and Australia in the early 2000s following hepatotoxicity concerns. Most restrictions have since been lifted as evidence accumulated that the problems stemmed from non-standard products (aerial parts, poor-quality extracts, tudei varieties). The current trajectory is toward normalization and re-acceptance.

**Kratom** faces increasing regulatory scrutiny. The DEA attempted to emergency-schedule it in 2016 (withdrawn after public opposition). The FDA has issued multiple warnings, classifying kratom as an opioid. Six U.S. states have banned it (Alabama, Arkansas, Indiana, Rhode Island, Vermont, Wisconsin). Thailand — kratom's native country — decriminalized it in 2021 after a long history of prohibition. The regulatory future remains uncertain.

## Bottom Line

Kava and kratom are frequently compared because both are botanical psychoactives used for relaxation and well-being, but they are fundamentally different in pharmacology, cultural context, and risk profile. Kava is a GABAergic anxiolytic with deep ceremonial roots, minimal addiction potential, and a good safety record when sourced properly. Kratom is an opioid receptor modulator with stimulant-analgesic biphasic effects, moderate dependence potential, and a more uncertain regulatory future. They serve different purposes and should not be considered interchangeable.
