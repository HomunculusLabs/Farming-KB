# Citrus vs Mango Cultivation

Citrus (*Citrus* spp., Rutaceae) and mango (*Mangifera indica*, Anacardiaceae) are two of the most important subtropical and tropical fruit crops. While both thrive in warm climates, they differ significantly in growth habits, climate tolerances, management requirements, and production cycles.

## Quick Reference

| Feature | Citrus | Mango |
|---------|--------|-------|
| **Growth habit** | Medium evergreen (15–30 ft) | Large evergreen (30–100+ ft) |
| **USDA Zones** | 8b–11 (varies by species) | 9b–11 |
| **Cold tolerance** | 10°F (kumquat) to 32°F (lime) | 25–30°F (mature trees) |
| **Soil pH** | 5.5–7.0 | 5.5–7.5 |
| **Water needs** | Moderate (35–60 in/yr) | Moderate to low once established |
| **Time to fruit** | 2–4 years (grafted) | 3–5 years (grafted) |
| **Productive lifespan** | 30–50+ years | 40–100+ years |
| **Harvest season** | Year-round across varieties | Concentrated May–Oct |
| **Pollination** | Mostly self-fertile; bees assist | Mostly self-fertile; flies/bees |
| **Major pests** | Asian citrus psyllid, scale, leafminer | Mango hopper, mealybug, anthracnose |
| **Root system** | Shallow to medium (60% top 2 ft) | Deep taproot to 20 ft |
| **Container suitable** | Excellent (dwarf varieties) | Limited — large tree |

## Climate and Hardiness

Citrus offers far more cold-tolerant options. Kumquats and yuzu survive to 10°F (-12°C), Satsumas handle 15–20°F, and standard oranges tolerate brief freezes at 24–28°F. Mango has very limited cold tolerance — mature trees may survive 25–30°F with damage, but young trees die at 30°F.

For heat, mango is more genuinely tropical and thrives in sustained hot, humid conditions. Citrus produces sweeter fruit in heat but suffers more sunburn and heat-induced fruit drop.

**Cold climates → Citrus. Hot tropics → Mango.**

### Rainfall and Humidity

Citrus performs best with winter-spring rainfall and dry summers (Mediterranean), though humid subtropical production is also major. Excess humidity promotes fungal diseases.

Mango prefers a distinct dry season during flowering and fruit development. Rain during bloom promotes anthracnose, the most serious mango disease.

**Dry-season climates → Mango benefits. Humid climates → Both face disease pressure but mango is more adapted.**

## Soil and Space

Citrus tolerates wider soil textures including sandy soils. Rootstock selection allows adaptation to saline, calcareous, or clay soils. Mango requires excellent drainage and does poorly in heavy clay. Its deep taproot makes it more drought-tolerant once established.

Citrus trees are compact — standard oranges reach 15–25 ft; dwarfs stay 6–10 ft (100–200 trees/acre). Mango trees reach 30–60 ft, requiring 25–35 ft spacing (40–100 trees/acre).

**Small spaces → Citrus. Soil flexibility → Citrus. Drought-prone sites → Mango.**

## Production and Harvest

### Harvest Distribution

Citrus offers year-round harvest with proper variety selection: navels and mandarins in winter, Valencias in spring/summer, lemons year-round. Mango harvest is concentrated in a 2–3 month window, creating glut and scarcity.

**Extended harvest → Clear citrus advantage.**

### Storage

Citrus stores 1–3 months in cold storage. Mangoes are highly perishable — ripe fruit lasts 1–2 weeks at room temperature.

**Storage → Clear citrus advantage.**

## Pest and Disease

Citrus faces devastating Huanglongbing (HLB/citrus greening) — there is no cure, and management relies on psyllid control and tree removal. Mango's most serious disease is anthracnose, manageable through fungicides and variety selection.

**Organic management → Mango.** Anthracnose is controllable with copper sprays and pruning. Citrus organic production is challenged by HLB in affected regions.

## Nutritional Value (per 100g)

| Nutrient | Orange | Lemon | Mango |
|----------|--------|-------|-------|
| Calories | 47 | 29 | 60 |
| Vitamin C (mg) | 53 | 53 | 36 |
| Vitamin A (IU) | 225 | 22 | 1,082 |
| Fiber (g) | 2.4 | 2.8 | 1.6 |

Mango is significantly higher in vitamin A and sugar. Citrus provides more vitamin C per calorie.

## Economics

Mango commands higher per-unit prices, particularly for fresh market and organic fruit. Citrus is a higher-volume commodity benefiting from juice and oil processing infrastructure. Mango's concentrated harvest requires more peak labor.

## Permaculture Integration

Citrus functions well in the lower canopy layer, provides year-round pollinator forage, and suits containers. Mango dominates the upper canopy, provides deep nutrient cycling via taproots, and generates significant shade and biomass. In combined plantings, maintain 20+ ft between mango canopies and citrus to avoid excessive shading.

## Summary: Which to Grow?

| Scenario | Best Choice |
|----------|------------|
| Marginal subtropical (zone 8b–9a) | **Citrus** — wider cold hardiness |
| Small urban garden or patio | **Citrus** — dwarf varieties, containers |
| Year-round fruit production | **Citrus** — extended harvest season |
| Hot tropical climate (zone 11) | **Mango** — better heat adaptation |
| Organic production priority | **Mango** — fewer devastating diseases |
| Commercial juice production | **Citrus** — established juice industry |
| High-value fresh fruit market | **Mango** — premium pricing |
| Drought-prone site | **Mango** — more drought-tolerant |
