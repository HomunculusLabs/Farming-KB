# Mushroom Growing Methods Comparison

Choosing the right cultivation method depends on your experience level,
available space, budget, target species, and yield goals. This comparison
covers the five most common approaches used by home growers and small-scale
cultivators, from beginner-friendly indoor techniques to outdoor perennial
systems. For a broader overview, see
[[stropharia-rugosoannulata]]) and king stropharia
are the classic species, but blewitts, parasols, and almond mushrooms also
thrive. Very low maintenance once established — beds can produce for years
with annual wood chip additions. Best for gardeners who already have outdoor
space and patience for seasonal production.

### Log Cultivation

Hardwood logs (oak, maple, beech, sweetgum) are inoculated with plug or
sawdust spawn and left in shaded outdoor areas. Shiitake is the primary
target species, but [[oyster-mushrooms]] are the most forgiving species for this method.

## Progression Path for Growers

The typical learning progression moves from small, controlled indoor methods
to larger and more natural systems:

1. PF tek (learn sterile technique, fungal biology, fruiting triggers)
2. Monotub (learn bulk substrate, grain spawn, environmental control)
3. Straw bags (learn pasteurization, production-scale thinking)
4. Log cultivation (learn outdoor management, seasonal production cycles)
5. Outdoor beds (learn integrated permaculture mushroom systems)

Each step builds on skills from the previous one. Jumping straight to log
cultivation or outdoor beds without indoor experience is possible but leads to
higher failure rates due to limited understanding of contamination vectors and
[[fruiting-conditions]].

## Verdict

Beginners should start with PF tek to learn sterile technique and fungal
biology without significant investment. Those ready for higher yields should
move to monotubs — the best balance of yield, cost, and simplicity for indoor
growing. Outdoor beds and log cultivation are ideal for permaculture-oriented
growers who want low-maintenance perennial production integrated into their
landscape. Straw bags offer the highest production ceiling for those willing
to invest in equipment and develop their skills.

## Contamination Risk by Method

Understanding contamination risk is critical for choosing the right method.
Each approach has different vulnerability profiles:

- **PF Tek:** Low contamination risk due to small jar volumes and
  individual jar isolation. A single contaminated jar does not ruin the
  entire crop. The vermiculite barrier layer provides an effective
  dry filter against airborne spore ingress
- **Monotub:** Moderate risk. The large volume of bulk substrate means a
  single contamination event can destroy the entire tub. However, healthy
  mycelium is remarkably competitive, and minor contaminations are often
  overgrown and suppressed before they spread
- **Straw bags:** Higher risk due to the exposed substrate surface area
  during inoculation. Proper pasteurization technique (maintaining 60-70C
  for 60-90 minutes) is essential to reduce competitor organisms without
  killing beneficial thermophiles
- **Log cultivation:** Very low contamination risk outdoors. The dense wood
  substrate resists contamination, and the natural microbial community of
  fresh-cut logs provides competitive protection. The main risk is log
  drying or excessive sun exposure
- **Outdoor beds:** Low risk once established. The diverse soil microbial
  community provides biological buffering. The primary risks are
  insufficient moisture and competition from weeds or other fungi

## PF Tek in Detail

The PF Tek (Psilocybe Fanaticus Technique) remains the most accessible entry
point for mushroom cultivation. Developed in the early 1990s, it uses
half-pint wide-mouth mason jars filled with a substrate of brown rice flour
and vermiculite. The method is valued for its simplicity and low startup cost:

- **Equipment needed:** Pressure cooker, mason jars, brown rice flour,
  vermiculite, spore syringe, and a basic inoculation area (even a
  still-air bathroom works)
- **Total startup cost:** $50-100 depending on what equipment is already
  available
- **Time to first harvest:** 4-6 weeks from inoculation
- **Success rate for beginners:** 60-80% with careful attention to sterile
  procedure

The [[pf-tek-substrate-formula]] is 2 parts vermiculite, 1 part water, and 1 part
brown rice flour by volume. The vermiculite provides structure and water
retention while the rice flour supplies nutrition. A dry vermiculite layer at
the top of each jar acts as a contaminant filter during gas exchange.
