    10|     9|# Insectary Plants vs Trap Cropping
    11|    10|
    12|    11|## Overview
    13|    12|Insectary plants and trap cropping are two biological pest management
    14|    strategies that work through opposite mechanisms. Insectary plants attract
    15|    and sustain beneficial insects that prey on or parasitize pests. Trap
    16|    cropping attracts the pests themselves, concentrating them on sacrificial
    17|    plants away from the main crop. Both reduce pesticide dependence, but they
    18|    operate on different ecological principles and timelines, making them
    19|    complementary components of integrated pest management programs.
    20|    13|
    21|    14|## Insectary Plants
    22|    15|[[insectary-plants-guide]]
    23|    16|
    24|    17|Insectary plants are species selected to provide nectar, pollen, and
    25|    habitat for beneficial insects including pollinators, predatory insects,
    26|    and parasitic wasps. A well-designed insectary system supports year-round
    27|    populations of these beneficial organisms by maintaining a continuous
    28|    bloom sequence across hedgerows, interplantings, and dedicated insectary
    29|    beds. Key plant categories include hedgerow trees and shrubs like alder,
    30|    maple, and tagasaste for early-season forage; understorey herbs like
    31|    borage, lavender, thyme, and dill for mid-season pollinator and parasitoid
    32|    support; and pasture forages like clovers and lucerne that serve dual
    33|    purposes as livestock feed and insect habitat.
    34|    18|
    35|    19|The approach builds beneficial insect populations over time, providing
    36|    indirect pest control as predator and parasitoid numbers increase.
    37|    Companion plants within the insectary toolkit include species with
    38|    additional pest-deterrent properties, such as marigolds for nematode
    39|    suppression, nasturtiums as aphid indicators, garlic for codling moth and
    40|    Japanese beetle deterrence, and rosemary for cabbage moth repulsion.
    41|    Insectary hedgerows provide multiple co-benefits including wind
    42|    protection, livestock forage, fire retardance, and wildlife habitat,
    43|    making them a multifunctional investment in overall farm ecology.
    44|    20|
    45|    21|## Trap Cropping
    46|    22|[[trap-cropping]]
    47|    23|
    48|    24|Trap cropping uses sacrificial plants that are more attractive to
    49|    specific pests than the main crop, concentrating pest populations where
    50|    they can be monitored, managed, or destroyed. The push-pull strategy
    51|    refines this approach by combining repellent plants near the main crop
    52|    (push) with attractive trap crops at the perimeter (pull), creating a dual
    53|    force directing pests away from valuable plants. Well-researched pairings
    54|    include Blue Hubbard squash for squash bugs, nasturtiums and radishes for
    55|    aphids, Indian mustard for cabbage worms and flea beetles, and dill and
    56|    moonflowers for tomato hornworm.
    57|    25|
    58|    26|Perimeter trap cropping surrounds the main crop with the trap species,
    59|    while intercropping places trap plants within the crop rows. Timing is
    60|    critical: trap crops should be planted two to three weeks before the main
    61|    crop to establish attractiveness before pests arrive. Management includes
    62|    regular monitoring, hand-picking of concentrated pests, application of
    63|    insecticides only to trap plants, and destruction of severely infested
    64|    trap crops before pests migrate. Dead-end trap crops like Chinese giant
    65|    mustard for flea beetles or Datura for tomato hornworms prevent the pest
    66|    from completing its life cycle, providing population reduction rather than
    67|    mere diversion.
    68|    27|
    69|    28|## Key Differences
    70|    29|| Aspect | Insectary Plants | Trap Cropping |
    71|    30||--------|------------------|---------------|
    72|    31|| Target Organism | Beneficial insects (predators, parasitoids,
    73|    pollinators) | Pest insects (attracted and concentrated) |
    74|    32|| Mechanism | Indirect (builds predator populations) | Direct (diverts
    75|    pests from main crop) |
    76|    33|| Speed of Effect | Slow (builds over weeks to months) | Fast
    77|    (immediate pest concentration) |
    78|    34|| Specificity | Broad (supports entire beneficial community) | Narrow
    79|    (specific pest-crop pairings) |
    80|    35|| Maintenance | Low once established | Moderate to high (monitoring,
    81|    management) |
    82|    36|| Pest Knowledge | General ecology understanding needed | Specific
    83|    pest-crop pairing research needed |
    84|    37|| Co-Benefits | Pollination, biodiversity, windbreak, forage | Pest
    85|    population data, reduced pesticide use |
    86|    38|| Risk | Some plants can become invasive | Trap crop becomes pest
    87|    breeding ground if neglected |
    88|    39|
    89|    40|## When to Choose Insectary Plants
    90|    41|Choose insectary plants when building long-term ecological resilience
    91|    and supporting overall beneficial insect populations. Insectary plantings
    92|    are the foundation of any organic or permaculture pest management
    93|    strategy, providing pollination services alongside pest control. They are
    94|    particularly valuable for apiaries, orchards, and diverse polyculture
    95|    systems where beneficial insect habitat directly increases yields.
    96|    Insectary hedgerows and interplantings require planning for year-round
    97|    bloom but are relatively low-maintenance once established and provide
    98|    aesthetic and ecological benefits beyond pest control.
    99|    42|
   100|    43|## When to Choose Trap Cropping
   101|    44|Choose trap cropping when a specific, identified pest problem requires
   102|    targeted intervention. Trap cropping provides visible, relatively fast
   103|    results against known pests with well-researched plant pairings. It is
   104|    ideal for vegetable gardens and small farms dealing with persistent pest
   105|    pressure from species like squash bugs, flea beetles, or cabbage worms.
   106|    Trap cropping works best as a precision tool within a broader integrated
   107|    pest management program, where it addresses specific problems while
   108|    insectary plants provide background biological control.
   109|    45|
   110|    46|## Hybrid Approaches
   111|    47|The most effective biological pest management combines both strategies
   112|    in a push-pull-inoculate system. Insectary plantings throughout the garden
   113|    maintain populations of predatory insects and parasitoids. Trap crops at
   114|    the garden perimeter concentrate pest populations where they are
   115|    accessible to both manual control and the beneficial insects supported by
   116|    the insectary plants. Repellent companion plants (the push component) near
   117|    the main crop create an additional barrier. This three-layered approach
   118|    uses insectary plants to inoculate the system with biological control
   119|    agents, trap crops to concentrate pests for elimination, and repellent
   120|    plants to protect the crop directly.
