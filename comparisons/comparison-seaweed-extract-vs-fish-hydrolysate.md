## Seaweed Extract vs Fish Hydrolysate

Both seaweed extract and [[fish-hydrolysate]] are popular organic liquid fertilizers, but they serve different roles. Seaweed extract is primarily a biostimulant — supplying trace minerals and phytohormones that stimulate growth and stress resistance. Fish hydrolysate is primarily a nitrogen and amino acid source — feeding both plant and soil biology with readily available organic nitrogen. Understanding these distinctions is key to using them effectively.

## Nutrient Profiles

### NPK and Micronutrients at a Glance

| Attribute | Seaweed Extract | Fish Hydrolysate |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary role** | Biostimulant / micronutrient source | Nitrogen and amino acid source |
| **Typical NPK** | 0-0-1 to 1-0-4 (very low) | 2-5-0 to 5-1-1 (moderate N) |
| **Nitrogen** | Minimal (< 1%) | Significant (2–5% protein-derived) |
| **Phosphorus** | Low to moderate | Low |
| **Potassium** | Moderate (1–4%) | Low to moderate |
| **Amino acids** | Low | Very high (20+ types) |
| **Micronutrients** | Excellent (Fe, Zn, Mn, Cu, B, Mo) | Good (Fe, Zn, Ca, Mg) |
| **Trace minerals** | Rich in iodine, selenium, natural chelates | Rich in calcium, phospholipids |

### Key Differences
Seaweed extracts are derived mainly from brown algae (*Ascophyllum nodosum*, *Ecklonia maxima*, *[[mycorrhizal-drought-and-temperature-tolerance]] stress
- **Betaines** — osmoprotectants that improve salt and [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]] and soil structure
- **Polyphenols and fucoidans** — [[saprophytic-fungi]] that build stable soil organic matter.

Combined, they create synergy: fish hydrolysate feeds the microbial workforce, while seaweed extract provides signaling compounds directing that workforce toward beneficial activity.

## Application Methods and Rates

### Seaweed Extract

- **Foliar spray:** 1–2% solution (10–20 mL per liter), applied every 2–3 weeks during active growth. Apply early morning or late evening for best absorption.
- **Soil drench:** 0.5–1% solution at transplanting and during root development phases.
- **Seed soak:** 0.5–1% solution for 8–12 hours before planting (improves germination and seedling vigor).
- **Compatibility:** Mixes well with most foliar inputs. Avoid combining with high-calcium sprays (can cause precipitation).

### Fish Hydrolysate

- **Foliar spray:** 1–3% solution (10–30 mL per liter), every 2–4 weeks. Strain to prevent clogging.
- **Soil drench:** 1–2% solution, every 2–3 weeks during vegetative growth. Filter if using drip systems.
- **Compost tea additive:** 15–30 mL per gallon of aerated compost tea for a nitrogen and biology boost.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with most organic inputs but may foam with surfactants. Avoid mixing with high-pH solutions (> 8.5).

### General Tips

- Apply both to actively growing plants — they work as stimulants, not rescue treatments.
- Water plants before foliar sprays to reduce leaf burn risk.
- Reduce rates by 50% for seedlings; increase for established, heavy-feeding crops.
- Store in a cool, dark place. Fish hydrolysate is odor-sensitive — keep sealed.

## Cost and Availability

| Factor | Seaweed Extract | Fish Hydrolysate |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical cost (concentrate)** | $15–$40 per quart (retail) | $20–$45 per quart (retail) |
| **Bulk cost (55 gal drum)** | $150–$400 | $200–$500 |
| **Shelf life** | 2–4 years | 1–2 years (preserved); 6–12 months (unpreserved) |
| **Odor** | Mild, earthy | Strong fishy smell (persists for 1–3 days after application) |
| **Availability** | Widely available online and at garden centers | Moderate; more common through organic farm suppliers |

Seaweed extract is generally more shelf-stable and easier to handle, making it more accessible for small-scale gardeners. Fish hydrolysate is widely used in commercial organic agriculture but can be harder to source locally and requires more careful storage.

## Environmental Impact

### Seaweed Extract

- **Positive:** Sustainable harvesting is regulated in Norway and Ireland. No freshwater or arable land required. Alginates improve soil carbon sequestration.
- **Concerns:** Overharvesting wild beds can disrupt coastal ecosystems. Check whether the product uses wild harvest or cultivated seaweed. Processing energy varies by method.

### Fish Hydrolysate

- **Positive:** Uses fish processing waste (frames, heads, offal) that would otherwise be discarded. Recycles nitrogen and phosphorus back into the food system. Enzymatic processing uses less energy than fish meal production.
- **Concerns:** Source fishery sustainability matters — choose products from certified sustainable fisheries. Some products contain preservatives (e.g., phosphoric acid) that may affect soil pH with heavy use.

## Pros and Cons Summary

### Seaweed Extract
**Pros:**
- Excellent source of natural phytohormones and bioactive compounds
- Broad micronutrient spectrum with natural chelation
- Improves plant stress tolerance (drought, heat, cold, salinity)
- Low application rates needed; long shelf life
- Supports beneficial soil microbial communities

**Cons:**
- Very low NPK — cannot serve as a primary nutrient source
- Quality varies widely between brands and extraction methods
- Wild harvest sourcing raises sustainability questions for some products
- Cost can be high relative to nutrient content alone
