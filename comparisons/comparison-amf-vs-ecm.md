# Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi vs Ectomycorrhizal Fungi

## Overview

Arbuscular [[arbuscule-isolation-and-nutrient-exchange-mechanisms]]: the plant provides carbohydrates produced
through photosynthesis, while the fungus delivers phosphorus, nitrogen, zinc, copper,
and other minerals scavenged from the soil.

## Pros and Cons Summary

AMF are essential for agricultural productivity and soil health, offering broad host
compatibility and significant fertilizer reduction potential. However, their obligate
nature makes cultivation difficult, and they are highly vulnerable to common farming
practices including tillage and high-phosphorus fertilization.

ECM provide extraordinary [[mycelial-network-resource-sharing-plants]] through
extensive networks. Their limitation to woody hosts and complex ecological
requirements make them less immediately applicable to agriculture, though their
importance in agroforestry and reforestation is increasingly recognized.

## When to Choose AMF

AMF inoculants are the right choice for agricultural and horticultural applications
involving crops, vegetables, grasses, and ornamental plants. They are widely
available as commercial inoculants and can be applied during seeding, transplanting,
or as a soil drench. AMF are particularly beneficial in low-phosphorus soils and in
sustainable farming systems that aim to reduce chemical fertilizer inputs. For
[[mycorrhizal-fungi-ecological-restoration]] projects, AMF are essential for rapid plant establishment and
long-term soil stabilization.

## When to Choose ECM

ECM inoculation is appropriate when establishing tree plantations, orchards,
reforestation projects, or forest restoration sites, especially in temperate and
boreal regions. Nurseries can significantly improve seedling survival by ensuring
ECM colonization before field planting. Truffle cultivation represents a specialized
and commercially valuable application of ECM management. ECM are also valuable in
agroforestry systems where trees are integrated with crops or livestock.

## Hybrid Approaches

In mixed ecosystems, both AMF and ECM can coexist and contribute simultaneously.
Many natural plant communities contain both types: canopy trees form ECM associations
while understory plants and grasses associate with AMF. Some species including
willows, poplars, and eucalypts can form both AM and ECM associations depending on
environmental conditions. Forest garden designs can benefit from managing both
fungal types to support a diverse polyculture.
