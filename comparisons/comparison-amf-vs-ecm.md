     9|# Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi vs Ectomycorrhizal Fungi
    10|
    11|## Overview
    12|
    13|Arbuscular [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] (AMF) and ectomycorrhizal fungi (ECM) are the two
    14|dominant types of mycorrhizal symbioses, together forming associations with the vast
    15|majority of terrestrial plant species. AMF penetrate root cortical cells to form
    16|tree-like arbuscules for direct nutrient exchange, associating with 80-90% of all
    17|plant species including most agricultural crops and grasses. ECM form a dense fungal
    18|sheath around root tips and extend a Hartig net between root cells, primarily
    19|partnering with woody plants in temperate and boreal forest ecosystems.
    20|
    21|## Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF)
    22|
    23|[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]]
    24|
    25|AMF belong almost exclusively to the phylum Glomeromycota and are among the oldest
    26|symbionts on Earth, with fossil evidence of arbuscule-like structures dating back
    27|over 400 million years to the earliest land plants. They are obligate symbionts,
    28|meaning they cannot complete their life cycle or reproduce without a living host
    29|plant. Upon contacting a suitable root, AMF hyphae penetrate the root cortex and
    30|form highly branched arbuscules inside individual cortical cells. These arbuscules
    31|are the primary sites of nutrient exchange: the plant provides carbohydrates produced
    32|through photosynthesis, while the fungus delivers phosphorus, nitrogen, zinc, copper,
    33|and other minerals scavenged from the soil.
    34|
    35|The external hyphal network of AMF extends far beyond the root depletion zone,
    36|effectively increasing the root absorptive surface area by up to 1000 times. This
    37|vast network is particularly important for phosphorus acquisition, as phosphorus is
    38|relatively immobile in soil and quickly becomes depleted in the immediate root zone.
    39|AMF also produce glomalin, a glycoprotein that acts as a biological glue binding
    40|soil microaggregates into stable macroaggregates. Glomalin is remarkably persistent
    41|in soil, contributing significantly to long-term soil carbon storage and aggregate
    42|stability. Individual AMF networks can connect multiple plants simultaneously,
    43|facilitating nutrient and carbon transfer between individuals through common
    44|[[mycorrhizal-networks]].
    45|
    46|AMF associate with the majority of agricultural crops including wheat, corn, rice,
    47|legumes, and most vegetable species. This makes them critically important for
    48|sustainable agriculture, as they can reduce fertilizer requirements by 20-60% for
    49|phosphorus inputs. However, AMF are sensitive to soil disturbance from tillage,
    50|elevated phosphorus fertilization (which suppresses the symbiosis), and certain
    51|pesticide applications including some fungicides and soil fumigants. These
    52|sensitivities mean that conventional practices often inadvertently reduce AMF
    53|populations over time.
    54|
    55|[[symbiotic-fungi-ectomycorrhizal-networks-overview]]
    56|
    57|ECM are predominantly members of Basidiomycota, though some belong to Ascomycota,
    58|and include many familiar forest mushrooms such as boletes, chanterelles, amanitas,
    59|and the highly prized truffles (Tuber species). Rather than penetrating root cells,
    60|ECM form a thick fungal mantle (sheath) around the exterior of fine root tips,
    61|completely encasing the root in fungal tissue. From this sheath, hyphae grow inward
    62|between root cortical cells to form the Hartig net, a labyrinthine interface where
    63|nutrient exchange occurs without actually penetrating the plant cell membranes. The
    64|external mycelium can extend meters from the root tip into the surrounding soil.
    65|
    66|ECM partnerships are dominated by woody plants: most temperate and boreal forest
    67|trees including pines, oaks, birches, spruces, firs, beeches, willows, and eucalypts.
    68|These associations are particularly important in nutrient-poor forest soils where ECM
    69|fungi mine organic matter for nitrogen and phosphorus using extracellular enzymes
    70|including proteases, phosphatases, and oxidative enzymes. Many ECM fungi produce
    71|prominent fruiting bodies visible above ground, unlike AMF which rarely produce
    72|visible sporocarps and instead reproduce through large multinucleate spores.
    73|
    74|ECM fungi play a foundational role in forest ecosystem function by facilitating
    75|nutrient cycling between soil organic matter and plant uptake, connecting trees of
    76|different species through common mycorrhizal networks, and contributing to soil
    77|weathering through organic acid exudation. They are particularly effective at
    78|accessing organic nitrogen forms that plants cannot use directly, making them
    79|essential for tree nutrition in boreal and temperate forests. Research has shown
    80|that ECM networks can facilitate carbon transfer from canopy trees to shaded
    81|seedlings in the understory, sometimes called "mother tree" effects.
    82|
    83|## Key Differences
    84|
    85|| Aspect | AMF | ECM |
    86||--------|-----|-----|
    87|| Approach | Penetrate root cells forming arbuscules | Form external sheath and Hartig net |
    88|| Fungal Phyla | Glomeromycota (exclusively) | Basidiomycota (mostly), some Ascomycota |
    89|| Host Range | 80-90% of plants; most crops/grasses | Mostly woody plants; temperate/boreal trees |
    90|| Host Cell Entry | Yes (intracellular arbuscules) | No (intercellular Hartig net) |
    91|| Root Structure | No external sheath; subtle changes | Dense mantle, short branched roots |
    92|| Nutrient Strategy | Scavenge inorganic P and N from soil | Mine organic matter with enzymes |
    93|| Soil Building | Produce glomalin; aggregate stabilization | Organic matter decomposition and weathering |
    94|| Fruiting Bodies | Rarely produce visible mushrooms | Many produce prominent edible mushrooms |
    95|| Evolutionary Age | Over 400 million years old | Approximately 200 million years old |
    96|| Cultivation | Difficult; obligate symbionts | Some culturable; truffle cultivation established |
    97|| Network Scale | Moderate (centimeters from root) | Extensive (meters in forest soils) |
    98|| Best For | Crops, grasslands, tropical systems | Forests, timber, orchards, truffle production |
    99|| Drawbacks | Sensitive to tillage, high P, fungicides | Limited to woody hosts, complex ecology |
   100|
   101|## Pros and Cons Summary
   102|
   103|AMF are essential for agricultural productivity and soil health, offering broad host
   104|compatibility and significant fertilizer reduction potential. However, their obligate
   105|nature makes cultivation difficult, and they are highly vulnerable to common farming
   106|practices including tillage and high-phosphorus fertilization.
   107|
   108|ECM provide extraordinary ecosystem services in forest environments including organic
   109|matter decomposition, nutrient mining, and interplant resource sharing through
   110|extensive networks. Their limitation to woody hosts and complex ecological
   111|requirements make them less immediately applicable to agriculture, though their
   112|importance in agroforestry and reforestation is increasingly recognized.
   113|
   114|## When to Choose AMF
   115|
   116|AMF inoculants are the right choice for agricultural and horticultural applications
   117|involving crops, vegetables, grasses, and ornamental plants. They are widely
   118|available as commercial inoculants and can be applied during seeding, transplanting,
   119|or as a soil drench. AMF are particularly beneficial in low-phosphorus soils and in
   120|sustainable farming systems that aim to reduce chemical fertilizer inputs. For
   121|ecological restoration projects, AMF are essential for rapid plant establishment and
   122|long-term soil stabilization.
   123|
   124|## When to Choose ECM
   125|
   126|ECM inoculation is appropriate when establishing tree plantations, orchards,
   127|reforestation projects, or forest restoration sites, especially in temperate and
   128|boreal regions. Nurseries can significantly improve seedling survival by ensuring
   129|ECM colonization before field planting. Truffle cultivation represents a specialized
   130|and commercially valuable application of ECM management. ECM are also valuable in
   131|agroforestry systems where trees are integrated with crops or livestock.
   132|
   133|## Hybrid Approaches
   134|
   135|In mixed ecosystems, both AMF and ECM can coexist and contribute simultaneously.
   136|Many natural plant communities contain both types: canopy trees form ECM associations
   137|while understory plants and grasses associate with AMF. Some species including
   138|willows, poplars, and eucalypts can form both AM and ECM associations depending on
   139|environmental conditions. Forest garden designs can benefit from managing both
   140|fungal types to support a diverse polyculture.
