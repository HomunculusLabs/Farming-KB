## Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB) Serum vs. Compost Tea

Both LAB serum and compost tea are popular biological amendments for soil and [[query-spore-syringes-vs-liquid-culture]] of lactic acid bacteria produced through a three-stage fermentation process rooted in Korean Natural Farming (KNF):

1. **Rice wash**: Collect starchy water from washing raw rice. Cover loosely and leave 2–3 days at room temperature until a sweet, yeasty smell develops.
2. **Milk fermentation**: Add milk at 1:10 ratio (rice wash to milk). Lactic acid bacteria consume lactose and produce lactic acid, dropping the pH. In 5–7 days the mixture separates: a solid curd floats up and a yellowish liquid — the **LAB serum** — settles below.
3. **Harvest**: Skim the curd, strain the liquid, and optionally add raw sugar at 1:10 ratio to stabilize for longer storage.

No aeration, heating, or specialized equipment is needed. The entire process uses kitchen-scale ingredients.

## Application Methods and Rates

### LAB Serum

- **Foliar spray**: Dilute 1:500 to 1:1000 with non-chlorinated water. Apply evening or early morning to avoid UV degradation.
- **Soil drench**: Dilute 1:1000 into the root zone. Good for transplanting or recovering chemically damaged soil.
- **Compost accelerator**: Add undiluted or lightly diluted to speed decomposition and suppress odors.
- **Frequency**: Every 1–2 weeks during active growth.

### Compost Tea

- **Foliar spray**: Apply undiluted or lightly diluted (up to 1:3). Use a gentle sprayer; add a surfactant like yucca extract for coverage.
- **Soil drench**: Apply undiluted at roughly 5–20 gallons per acre.
- **Frequency**: Every 2–4 weeks during the growing season.
- **Timing**: Apply when soil temperatures are above 50°F (10°C).
