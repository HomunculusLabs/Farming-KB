## Mandala Garden Design vs Keyhole Garden Design

## Overview

The permaculture-designers-manual-keyhole-garden both draw on

fundamentally different scales and serve different design contexts. The mandala 
garden is a landscape-scale pattern incorporating multiple beds, radiating 
walkways, and repeated keyhole-shaped access paths across an entire garden 
area. The keyhole garden is a single, self-contained raised bed unit with a 
central compost basket. Understanding the distinction between a comprehensive

## Mandala Garden Design

A mandala garden is a circular garden layout that uses sacred geometry and 
repeating radial patterns to organize growing space. Walkways radiate from the 
center like spokes of a wheel, often with keyhole-shaped notches cut into the 
beds at intervals, creating a pattern of circular and petal-shaped growing 
areas. This approach increases usable growing area by 30-50 percent compared to 
rectangular bed layouts with the same perimeter, because circular geometry 
maximizes the edge-to-area ratio. Mandala gardens are typically established 
through [[holmgren-permaculture-education-training-and-pdc]] centers, and public spaces that need to 
communicate sustainable [[comparison-companion-planting-reference-vs-jadam-monoculture-critique]] guilds 
within the radiating geometry.

## When to Choose a Keyhole Garden

Choose a keyhole garden when you need a quick, simple, and effective growing 
solution for food production. It is the best option for gardeners with limited 
time, as a single unit can be completed in an afternoon. The self-fertilizing 
central basket reduces ongoing maintenance, making it ideal for busy families, 
elderly gardeners, or community garden plots that receive sporadic attention. 
In arid regions, the keyhole garden's wicking action provides superior water 
efficiency. It also works well as a starter project for beginning gardeners who 
want immediate results before committing to a larger garden layout. The modular 
nature allows you to add additional units over time as confidence and needs 
grow.

## Hybrid Approaches

The most powerful integration combines both concepts: design the overall garden 
layout using mandala geometry with keyhole-shaped access paths, then build 
individual keyhole garden units with central compost baskets at key positions 
within the mandala pattern. This gives you the aesthetic and efficiency 
benefits of the mandala layout alongside the self-fertilizing, water-efficient 
properties of keyhole beds. Some designers create a central keyhole bed at the 
heart of the mandala, with radiating paths leading to additional keyhole units 
arranged in the petal beds. Another approach uses the mandala walkway pattern 
but replaces individual petal beds with keyhole garden construction for 
improved moisture management in dry climates.
