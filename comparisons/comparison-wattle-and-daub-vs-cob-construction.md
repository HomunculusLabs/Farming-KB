---# Wattle and Daub vs. Cob Construction

Both wattle and daub and cob are ancient earthen building techniques experiencing a modern revival among natural builders and [[query-how-do-i-choose-the-right-cannabis-strain-for-my-grow]] method for their climate, site, and project goals.

## Core Definition

| Aspect | Wattle and Daub | Cob |
|--------|----------------|-----|
| **Basic method** | Earthen daub packed onto a woven wood lattice (wattle) set within a [[fukuoka-textdoc-plowing-ruins-soil-compaction-bread-dough-analogy]]). Mixed by tarp-rolling or foot-trampling. Straw is left long (10–20+ cm) for tensile reinforcement throughout the mass. No dung needed — the long straw provides all the fiber.

### Wood Requirements

- **Wattle and daub**: Significant timber needed for the structural frame (posts, beams, sills) plus wattling material (hazel, willow, bamboo). The wattle is integral — the wall cannot exist without it.
- **Cob**: Minimal timber — only needed for roof plates, door/window frames, and optional bond beams. No internal wood lattice required.

## Structural Behavior

### Load-Bearing

The single most important difference: **cob is load-bearing; wattle and daub is not**.

- **Cob walls** can support roof loads, floor joists, and upper stories directly. Properly designed cob walls have compressive strength of 0.5–1.5 MPa, sufficient for single and even two-story buildings.
- **Wattle and daub** walls are infill panels. They stiffen the frame and provide enclosure, but all structural loads must be carried by the timber frame, masonry, or other structural system.

### Flexibility and Seismic Resistance

- **Wattle and daub**: Surprisingly resilient in earthquakes. The flexible wattle acts like a shock absorber, and the daub is thin enough that cracking does not compromise the structure (the timber frame carries loads). Historic wattle-and-daub buildings have survived major earthquakes.
- **Cob**: More vulnerable to seismic forces due to its mass and rigidity. Modern cob builders in seismic zones add reinforcing (bamboo, rebar, or mesh) and keep walls low and thick.

## Thermal Performance

| Property | Wattle and Daub | Cob |
|----------|----------------|-----|
| **Wall thickness** | 12–20 cm | 30–60 cm |
| **R-value (per m²)** | R-1.0 to R-1.6 | R-2.0 to R-4.0 |
| **Thermal mass** | Moderate | Very high |
| **Thermal lag** | 4–6 hours | 8–12 hours |
| **Insulation** | Low-moderate | Low (improved by thickness) |

Cob's massive walls provide superior thermal mass — they absorb heat slowly and release it over a longer period. Wattle and daub walls are thinner and respond faster to temperature changes.
