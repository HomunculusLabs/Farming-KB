## Fava Bean vs Chickpea vs Lentil: Grain Legume Comparison

Fava beans (*[[vicia-faba]]*), chickpeas (*Cicer arietinum*), and lentils (*Lens culinaris*) are three of the world's most important cool-season grain legumes. Each plays a vital role in sustainable agriculture through nitrogen fixation, human nutrition, and [[building-soil-fertility-long-term]] nitrogen in rotations. Lentils fix less total nitrogen due to their smaller biomass, but their N contribution is still agronomically meaningful in low-input systems.

## Yields

| Metric | Fava Bean | Chickpea | Lentil |
|--------|-----------|----------|--------|
| **World avg. yield** | 1.5–2.0 t/ha | 1.0–1.5 t/ha | 1.0–1.5 t/ha |
| **Good commercial yield** | 3.0–5.0 t/ha | 2.0–3.5 t/ha | 1.5–2.5 t/ha |
| **Seed weight (1000 seeds)** | 300–1500 g | 150–500 g | 20–70 g |
| **Harvest index** | 0.40–0.55 | 0.35–0.50 | 0.40–0.55 |

Fava beans produce the highest biomass and grain yield per hectare but require more water and nutrients to achieve those yields. Lentils have the lowest yield ceiling but are the most efficient in water-limited environments.

## Nutritional Profile (per 100 g dry seeds)

| Nutrient | Fava Bean | Chickpea | Lentil |
|----------|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Protein** | 26–29 g | 19–22 g | 24–28 g |
| **Fiber** | 25 g | 17–22 g | 18–20 g |
| **Carbohydrates** | 58–60 g | 58–63 g | 58–63 g |
| **Fat** | 1.5–2.0 g | 4–6 g | 1.0–1.5 g |
| **Iron** | 6–7 mg | 4–5 mg | 6–7 mg |
| **Folate** | 423 µg | 557 µg | 479 µg |
| **Key antinutrient** | Vicine/convicine | Phytic acid | Phytic acid, trypsin inhibitors |

Fava beans and lentils are the highest in protein, making them excellent meat alternatives. Chickpeas have the highest fat content and folate levels. Fava beans uniquely contain vicine and convicine, which can cause favism — a hemolytic anemia — in genetically susceptible individuals (G6PD deficiency). This is a significant consideration in regions where favism is prevalent.

## Culinary Uses

**Fava Beans** — Consumed fresh (immature pods as a vegetable) or dried. Fresh favas are a staple in Mediterranean spring cuisine. Dried favas are used in ful medames (Egypt/Middle East), mashed into dips, or fried as falafel in Egypt. They are also important in Chinese and Latin American cooking.

**Chickpeas** — Extremely versatile: hummus, falafel (Levant), chana masala (India), roasted snacks, flour (besan) for batters and flatbreads. Both whole dried and canned forms are globally traded. Chickpea flour is a growing gluten-free market ingredient.

**Lentils** — Cook quickly without pre-soaking. Red/yellow lentils break down into soups and dahls across South Asia and the Middle East. Green and brown lentils hold their shape for salads and stews. French green (Puy) and beluga black lentils are premium varieties in Western cuisine. They are a staple in Ethiopian, Indian, and Lebanese diets.

|----------|-----------|----------|--------|
