Goats are also notably vulnerable to toxic plants. Species like rhododendron,
azalea, yew, laurel, and cherry leaves can be fatal. Sheep have their own
toxic plant sensitivities but are somewhat less curious about novel vegetation.
## Predation Vulnerability
Sheep are **extremely vulnerable** to predation from coyotes, dogs, bears,
and mountain lions. Their flocking instinct, while helpful for management,
works against them during predator encounters. Guardian dogs (LGDs) such as
Great Pyrenees, Anatolian Shepherds, or Maremmas are considered essential for
any sheep operation, even on small homesteads.
Goats are also prey animals, but their more independent and alert nature
makes them somewhat less vulnerable. They are more likely to vocalize warnings
and scatter rather than freeze. Guardian protection is still recommended for
both species in areas with predator pressure.
## Reproduction
Gestation periods are nearly identical: approximately 150 days for goats and
147 days for sheep. Both typically produce twins, though triplets and
quadruplets are common in well-managed herds. A key difference: buck odor
during breeding season is extremely strong and can taint goat milk if the
buck is housed near does.
## Suitability for Small Homesteads
- Choose **goats** if you want milk production, brush clearing, and
  multi-purpose animals on varied terrain
- Choose **sheep** if you want low-fencing, flock-friendly management on
  established pasture, especially with hair sheep for simplicity
Many homesteaders ultimately keep both, leveraging complementary forage
strategies to maximize land productivity. See also [[comparison-cattle-husbandry-vs-goat-husbandry]]
