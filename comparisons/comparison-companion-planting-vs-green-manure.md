## Companion Planting vs Green Manure

## Overview

Companion planting and green manure cover crops are two foundational
ecological gardening strategies, but they serve fundamentally different
primary purposes. Companion planting focuses on synergistic plant
relationships within an active [[cover-crops-green-manures]]

Green manures are cover crops grown specifically to improve soil
health. They are typically planted during fallow periods — between
seasonal cash crops, over winter, or in unused beds — and then
incorporated into the soil or terminated as surface mulch. Leguminous
green manures like [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] returned to the
soil feeds microbial communities, improves water-holding capacity,
and gradually builds humus.

The primary trade-off with green manures is time and space. A bed
dedicated to a cover crop produces no harvest during that period,
which requires planning around seasonal gaps. Termination timing
matters — legumes should be incorporated at peak flowering for
maximum nitrogen, while grasses are best terminated before seed set
to prevent volunteer weeds.
