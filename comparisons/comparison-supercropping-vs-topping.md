## Key Differences
| Aspect | Supercropping | Topping |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| Approach | Crush and bend stems horizontally | Remove the apical meristem entirely |
| Auxin Redistribution | Redirected through bending, not removed | Eliminated at cut point, redistributed below |
| Plant Material Removed | None (all tissue preserved and redirected) | Apical meristem removed and discarded |
| Recovery Time | 24-48 hours for knuckle callus formation | 3-7 days for new growth tips to establish |
| Stress Level | Moderate (tissue damage without removal) | Moderate to high (growth point removal) |
| Canopy Shape | Wide, horizontal, shaped profile | Symmetrically branched with vertical leaders |
| Stem Strength | Increases dramatically at knuckle points | Normal strength at new growth tips |
| Skill Required | Moderate (pressure calibration needed) | Low (simple clean cut above a node) |
| Risk of Failure | Stem can snap if over-bent | Infection possible; poor growth if cut too low |
| Number of Colas | 4-12+ depending on bending extent | Doubles each round (2, 4, 8, 16) |
| Predictability | Moderate; depends on technique and recovery | High; response pattern is very consistent |
| Best For | Experienced growers, light optimization | Beginners, manifolding, predictable branching |
| Drawbacks | Requires practice, stem breakage risk | Temporary stunting, requires recovery time |
## Pros and Cons Summary
Supercropping preserves all plant tissue while creating a horizontal canopy and reinforced
stems, making it the preferred technique for maximizing light utilization in indoor
environments. However, it requires practiced hands, carries the risk of accidentally snapping
stems, and produces less predictable canopy structures than topping.
Topping is simple, reliable, and highly predictable, making it the ideal technique for
beginners and for creating symmetrical manifold structures. The trade-off is temporary growth
stunting after each cut, the irreversible loss of the main growth tip, and less dramatic stem
reinforcement compared to the knuckle calluses formed by supercropping.
## When to Choose Supercropping
Supercropping is ideal for experienced growers who want to maximize their canopy spread and
stem strength without the growth delay associated with topping. It is particularly effective in
indoor grow environments where light intensity drops off rapidly with distance from the source
and a flat, even canopy dramatically increases overall light utilization across all bud sites.
Supercropping is also valuable for plants that are already taller than desired, as it allows
the grower to bend tall stems downward without sacrificing any growth or waiting for new
leaders to develop from scratch.
## When to Choose Topping
Topping is the better choice for beginners due to its simplicity, predictability, and minimal
skill requirement. The cut is straightforward, the results are reliable, and the plant's
