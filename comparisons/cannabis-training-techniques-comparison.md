# Cannabis Training Techniques Comparison

Plant training manipulates cannabis canopy structure to increase light exposure, improve airflow, and maximize yield per plant or per [[comparison-biointensive-vs-square-foot-gardening]]. Techniques range from gentle, continuous bending to aggressive structural cuts that fundamentally alter plant architecture. Choosing the right technique — or combination of techniques — depends on grow space constraints, experience level, strain characteristics, and time available for [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]. For [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]] management fundamentals, see [[cannabis-plant-anatomy]].

## Detailed Technique Breakdown

### Low Stress Training (LST)

The foundation technique: gently bending and tying branches away from the center of the plant to create an even, flat canopy. LST exposes lower bud sites to light that would otherwise be wasted on foliage, and creates multiple colas instead of a single dominant apical leader. The process is continuous — branches are adjusted every few days as they grow. No cutting is involved, so the plant experiences minimal stress.

**How it works:** Auxin (the growth hormone responsible for apical dominance) is redirected from the bent tip to lateral branches, stimulating them to grow upward and develop into colas. The plant's natural phototropism is harnessed to shape the canopy.

**Best for:** Every grower should learn LST. It is the safest, most versatile training technique and can be combined with any other method.

### Topping

Cutting off the apical meristem (main growing tip) of the plant, typically above the 4th-6th node. This forces the plant to split into two dominant branches (colas) instead of one. Each of these can be topped again for 4, 8, or more colas. Topping is most effective when performed during early vegetative stage when the plant has 4-6 nodes.

**How it works:** Removing the apical tip eliminates the source of apical-dominant auxin, allowing the two highest lateral branches to become co-dominant leaders.

**Best for:** Any setup where vertical space allows for bushier plants. Combine with LST for maximum canopy control.

### Fimming

Similar to topping but less precise: instead of cleanly cutting the growth tip, approximately 75-80% of the new growth is pinched or cut away, leaving some tissue intact. This typically produces 3-6 new branches from the cut site rather than the clean 2-way split of topping. Results are less predictable but potentially create more branch sites.

**How it works:** Partial removal of the apical meristem causes irregular branching — more sites but less uniform than topping.

**Best for:** Growers who want more colas than topping provides but with less stress than mainlining. A good middle-ground technique.

### SCRoG (Screen of Green)

A horizontal screen (chicken wire, net trellis, or string grid) is placed above the plants at a fixed height. As branches grow through the screen, they are woven and tucked under to create an even canopy plane. All bud sites sit at the same distance from the light source, maximizing light utilization. SCRoG is the most effective technique for maximizing yield per square foot under fixed lighting.

**How it works:** The screen physically constrains vertical growth and distributes the canopy horizontally, ensuring uniform light distribution across all bud sites. LST principles apply — branches below the screen are removed or tucked.

**Best for:** Indoor grows with horizontal lighting (LED, HPS), limited vertical space, growers who want maximum yield per light fixture. Not practical for outdoor grows.

### Mainlining (Manifold)

A structured, multi-topping technique that creates a symmetrical, symmetrical plant with all branches originating from the same node level. The process involves topping the plant down to the first or second true node, then training the two resulting branches to grow horizontally along opposite sides. Each branch is then topped at the same height to create 4 colas, then 8, and so on. The result is a plant with perfectly even canopy and multiple large colas fed by an established root mass.

**How it works:** By removing all growth above a specific node and training the remaining branches symmetrically, the plant develops a balanced structure where all colas have equal access to light and nutrients.

**Best for:** Experienced growers with time for extended vegetative growth. The most structured and highest-yielding single-plant technique, but requires 4-8 weeks of vegetative time and careful attention.

### Super Cropping

Also called "crushing" or "CaLS" (CRooping And LST). Branches are pinched and bent between thumb and forefinger until the inner tissue softens and the branch can be bent at a 90-degree angle without breaking. This creates a knuckle of scar tissue at the bend point that strengthens the branch and redirects growth energy to lateral bud sites. Useful for reducing plant height and strengthening branches that will support heavy colas.

**How it works:** Controlled damage to the vascular tissue causes the plant to repair the area with stronger tissue while redirecting growth hormones to lateral branches below the bend.

**Best for:** Tall or lanky plants that need height reduction, strengthening main branches before heavy flowering, and creating an auxin redistribution similar to LST without ties.

### Defoliation

Strategic removal of leaves — typically large fan leaves that shade bud sites, and lower growth that won't receive adequate light. Most commonly performed 1-2 weeks before the flip to flowering (major defoliation) and again at week 3-4 of flower (lollipop defoliation of lower branches). Controversial among growers: when done correctly it improves airflow, light penetration, and yield. When overdone it stresses the plant and reduces photosynthetic capacity.

**How it works:** Removing shade leaves redirects the plant's energy from leaf maintenance to bud development and improves light exposure to lower bud sites.

**Best for:** Dense canopies, indoor grows with limited airflow, strains with very large fan leaves. Not recommended for autoflowers or stressed plants.

## Technique Combination Guide

| Combination | Result | Veg Time Needed | Best Setup |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| LST alone | Even canopy, 4-8 colas | +1-2 weeks | Any |
| Topping + LST | Bushy multi-cola plant | +2-3 weeks | Any |
| Topping + SCRoG | Uniform canopy with many colas | +3-5 weeks | Indoor |
| Mainlining + SCRoG | Maximum per-plant yield | +6-10 weeks | Indoor, patient growers |
| Super cropping + LST | Short, strong plant with multiple colas | +2-3 weeks | Limited height spaces |
| Defoliation + SCRoG | Clean, airy canopy, maximum light penetration | +3-5 weeks | Indoor |

## Verdict

Every cannabis grower should master LST first — it is the safest, most versatile technique with the best effort-to-reward ratio. Topping adds significant yield improvement with minimal additional complexity and should be learned next. SCRoG is the most effective technique for indoor growers with fixed lighting who want to maximize yield per square foot. Mainlining produces the most impressive single-plant yields but demands extended vegetative time and experience. Defoliation is a useful complement to any training technique when canopies become dense. Super cropping is a niche technique most valuable for height management and branch strengthening. The most effective approach for most indoor growers combines LST with topping and optional defoliation. For outdoor growers, LST and topping are generally sufficient.
