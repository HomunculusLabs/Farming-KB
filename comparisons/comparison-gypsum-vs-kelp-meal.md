# Gypsum vs Kelp Meal

## Overview

Gypsum (calcium sulfate) and kelp meal are both widely used soil amendments,
but they serve fundamentally different purposes. Gypsum is an inorganic mineral
that improves soil structure and supplies calcium and sulfur without affecting
pH. Kelp meal is an organic product derived from seaweed that provides a broad
spectrum of trace elements, slow-release macronutrients, and biological
stimulants. Understanding when to use each is essential for effective soil
management.

## Gypsum

[[gypsum]]

Gypsum is a naturally occurring soft sulfate mineral (CaSO4·2H2O) that has been
used in agriculture for centuries. Its primary value lies in its ability to
improve the physical structure of clay and sodic soils. When applied, calcium
ions from gypsum displace sodium on clay exchange sites, allowing soil particles
to aggregate into stable crumbs. This improves water infiltration, reduces
surface crusting, and promotes deeper root penetration.

Unlike agricultural lime, gypsum does not raise soil pH, making it ideal for
situations where calcium is needed but the soil is already at an appropriate
acidity level. It also supplies sulfur, an essential secondary macronutrient
required for protein synthesis and chlorophyll formation. Application rates
typically range from 1 to 5 tonnes per hectare for structural improvement.

Gypsum is most effective on dispersive clay soils, sodic (high-sodium) soils,
and compacted paddocks where water infiltration is poor. It has limited effect
on sandy or loam soils that already have good structure. In permaculture
systems, gypsum is considered a targeted corrective amendment rather than a
routine fertilizer.

## Kelp Meal

[[kelp-meal-fertilizer]]

Kelp meal is produced by drying and grinding brown seaweed (kelp). It contains
60 to 70 different chemical elements, including the complete range of trace
elements needed for plant health. Its macronutrient profile is modest
(approximately 2.8% nitrogen, 0.2% phosphorus, 2.3% potassium) but its
nutritional breadth is unmatched by any single mineral amendment.

The nutrients in kelp meal are released slowly through microbial decomposition,
providing a gentle, long-lasting feed that will not burn plants or leach
rapidly. Beyond nutrition, kelp contains alginic acid which binds soil particles
together and stimulates microbial activity. Fresh kelp also contains natural
growth-promoting hormones that can be absorbed through plant leaves.

Kelp meal is most economically used as a compost activator, where its alginic
acid content attracts beneficial bacteria and accelerates decomposition. As a
soil amendment, it excels in situations where trace element deficiencies are
suspected or where a broad-spectrum nutritional boost is desired. It can be
applied at any time, though warm soil conditions improve microbial breakdown
and nutrient availability.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Gypsum | Kelp Meal |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| **Type** | Inorganic mineral | Organic seaweed product |
| **Primary Function** | Soil structure, Ca + S supply | Broad trace elements, bio-stimulant |
| **Calcium Content** | ~23% Ca | Trace amounts |
| **Nitrogen** | None | ~2.8% |
| **Trace Elements** | Sulfur only | 60-70 elements |
| **Effect on pH** | None | Minimal |
| **Soil Biology** | Limited direct effect | Stimulates microbial activity |
| **Cost per Area** | Low (cheap mineral) | Moderate to high |
| **Best Soil Type** | Clay and sodic soils | All soil types |
| **Speed of Action** | Moderate (weeks to months) | Slow (microbial breakdown) |

## When to Choose Gypsum

Choose gypsum when dealing with heavy clay soils that are hard, compacted, or
prone to waterlogging. It is the amendment of choice for sodic soil reclamation
where high sodium levels have destroyed soil structure. Use gypsum when you need
to add calcium without changing pH, especially in soils that are already at the
correct acidity for your crops. It is also valuable for improving water
infiltration in pasture and cropping systems where surface crusting restricts
rainfall absorption.

Gypsum is cost-effective for large-scale application, making it practical for
broadacre farming and pasture improvement. A single application can continue
improving soil structure for several years, particularly on heavy clays.

## When to Choose Kelp Meal

Choose kelp meal when addressing trace element deficiencies that are difficult
to diagnose individually. Its broad nutritional profile acts as an insurance
policy against micronutrient shortfalls. It is ideal for intensive garden beds,
greenhouse production, and container plants where soil mineral balance is
critical and repeated small applications are practical.

Kelp meal is particularly valuable in composting systems where its alginic acid
accelerates decomposition and enriches the finished compost with trace elements.
Use it as a foliar spray or soil drench during periods of plant stress such as
transplanting, flowering, or fruit set. The growth hormones in kelp provide an
additional benefit that no mineral amendment can replicate.

## Combined Use

Gypsum and kelp meal complement each other well in a comprehensive soil
management program. Gypsum addresses physical soil structure problems while
kelp meal enhances biological activity and nutritional breadth. A practical
approach is to apply gypsum as a baseline structural amendment on clay soils
(at 0.5 to 2 kg per square metre), then supplement with kelp meal at the
compost heap or as a targeted top-dress around heavy-feeding crops.

In raised beds and intensive gardens, kelp meal alone may suffice if soil
structure is already adequate. In paddock-scale operations, gypsum provides
the foundation and kelp meal can be reserved for compost enrichment or
foliar applications on high-value crops.
