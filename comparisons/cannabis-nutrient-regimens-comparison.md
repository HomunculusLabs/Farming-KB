# Cannabis Nutrient Regimens Comparison

Nutrient management is the primary lever for yield, flavor, and plant health in cannabis cultivation. The choice between organic and synthetic approaches — and the specific system within each category — has cascading effects on terpene production, soil health, long-term cost, and growing complexity. This comparison covers five major nutrient regimens used by home and commercial growers. For soil science fundamentals, see [[cannabis-soil-and-nutrient-requirements]]. The biological foundation of organic approaches is covered in [[soil-food-web-structure]] and [[nutrient-cycling-in-soil]].

## Detailed Regimen Breakdown

### Organic Living Soil

A living soil approach creates a complete ecosystem in the container or bed: compost, worm castings, aeration (perlite/pumice), and organic amendments (kelp, crustacean meal, bone char, rock dust) feed a diverse microbial community. microbes break down organic matter into plant-available nutrients at the rate plants can absorb them, preventing nutrient lockout and salt buildup. The grower feeds the soil, not the plant. Water-only during the grow cycle is the goal after initial amendment. Aeration and moisture management are critical.

**Best for:** Growers prioritizing flavor, terpene quality, and long-term soil health. Indoor living soil beds are the gold standard for craft cannabis.

### Synthetic Salt-Based (Mineral Nutrients)

Pre-formulated liquid or powdered nutrient lines (General Hydroponics Flora Series, Advanced Nutrients, Jack's 3-2-1) deliver precise NPK ratios in immediately bioavailable form. pH must be managed carefully (5.8-6.3 in soilless media). EC/PPM monitoring prevents overfeeding. The approach is simple, predictable, and maximizes yield potential. However, synthetic salts do not feed soil biology — prolonged use in living soil beds will kill off the microbial community and degrade soil structure.

**Best for:** Growers who want maximum yield with minimum complexity, hydroponic systems, and those who replace their media each cycle.

### Biobizz/Canna Organic Line

Bottled organic nutrient lines (Biobizz Bio-Grow, Bio-Bloom, Top-Max; Canna Bio Vega/Flores) offer a middle ground — organic-certified inputs in convenient liquid form. They feed soil biology while providing supplemental nutrition. Easier than building a complete living soil system but more expensive per cycle. pH buffering from the organic inputs reduces the need for precise pH management compared to synthetic lines.

**Best for:** Growers who want organic quality with the convenience of bottled nutrients, especially those transitioning from synthetic to organic approaches.

### Super Soil (Subcool Recipe)

Subcool's Super Soil recipe layers a nutrient-rich amended soil at the bottom third of the container with plain soil on top. As roots grow down, they encounter the "hot" amendment layer that provides complete nutrition through microbial breakdown. The recipe includes bat guano, worm castings, bone meal, blood meal, kelp meal, rock phosphate, dolomite lime, and other amendments. A 30-day cook period before planting allows the biology to activate and mellow the nutrient charge.

**Best for:** Growers who want a one-time soil preparation that feeds the plant throughout its life without additional nutrient inputs during flower.

### No-Till Recycled Soil

The most advanced approach: soil is never removed or replaced. After harvest, roots are left in place (or chopped and mixed), a layer of compost and amendments is top-dressed, and a new crop is planted directly into the recycled soil. Over multiple cycles, the soil biology reaches a self-sustaining equilibrium. Cover crops (clover, peas, oats) can be grown between cycles to fix nitrogen and add organic matter. Mulch layers protect soil surface biology and retain moisture.

**Best for:** Dedicated long-term growers who view soil as their primary asset. The highest reusability and the best long-term flavor development of any approach.

## Verdict

For growers who prioritize terpene quality and long-term sustainability, organic living soil and no-till recycled soil deliver the best results. No-till has the steepest learning curve but the lowest long-term cost and the most rewarding flavor development — soil that has been cycled for years produces noticeably more complex and nuanced terpene profiles. For beginners or those focused purely on yield, synthetic salt-based nutrients offer the simplest entry point with predictable results. Biobizz/Canna organic lines provide a gentle transition from synthetic to organic growing. Super soil offers an appealing one-and-done approach for those willing to invest in upfront soil preparation.
