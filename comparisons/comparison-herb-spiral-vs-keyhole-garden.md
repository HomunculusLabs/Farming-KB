## Herb Spiral

The herb spiral is a three-dimensional raised garden mound shaped in a 
descending spiral, typically 5-6 feet in diameter and 3-4 feet tall at its 
center. Built with stone or brick retaining walls that create a series of 
microclimates, the spiral exploits vertical space and solar orientation to 
provide a range of growing conditions in a single footprint. The top of the 
spiral is hot, dry, and sunny, suitable for [[holzer-kitchen-garden-herbs-and-medicinal-plants]] zones where frequent harvest access is needed.

## Keyhole Garden

The keyhole garden is a circular raised bed measuring approximately 5-6 feet in 
diameter and 2-3 feet tall, with a wedge-shaped notch cut into one side 
providing access to a central compost basket. This design was developed in 
Lesotho, Africa, through the humanitarian organization Send a Cow, as a 
response to drought conditions and nutrient-poor soils. The central basket is 
filled with compostable materials and kitchen scraps that release nutrients 
directly into the surrounding soil through wicking action. As water is poured 
into the central basket, it distributes moisture and dissolved nutrients 
outward into the bed. The keyhole design eliminates the need to step on the 
growing soil, while the raised structure improves drainage and reduces bending. 
Keyhole gardens are self-fertilizing, water-efficient, and accessible to 
elderly or mobility-impaired gardeners.
