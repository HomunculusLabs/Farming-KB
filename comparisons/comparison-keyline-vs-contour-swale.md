# Keyline Design vs Contour Swale

## Overview
Keyline design and contour swales are two landscape-scale water management
techniques developed to optimize water distribution, prevent erosion, and
improve soil fertility on sloping land. Keyline design, developed by P.A.
Yeomans in 1950s Australia, uses the natural topography of valleys and ridges
to spread water from wet areas to dry areas through pattern cultivation.
Contour swales, extensively developed in Bill Mollison's [[contour-swale-irrigation-design]]
Swales are broad, shallow trenches excavated on true contour across sloping
land to capture, slow, and infiltrate surface water runoff. They are typically
at least four feet wide and often much wider, with the excavated soil placed
on the downhill side to form a berm. Swales should not be used on steep slopes
— only on moderate to shallow gradient systems. The trench captures rainwater
and runoff, holding it long enough to soak into the soil rather than running
off the property and causing erosion.
Swale construction follows true contour lines established with an A-frame
level, bunyip level, or laser level. In clay soils, swales are deepened to
increase water holding capacity; in sandy and gravelly soils, they are widened
to increase surface area for infiltration. Small ponds are created at clay
pockets where water naturally collects, while wider sections are dug where
soil is more permeable. Swales work best when their ends are anchored by trees
or shrubs to prevent water from finding a path around the berm.
The primary benefit of swales is rapid groundwater recharge. A single well-
placed swale can transform a dry hillside into a productive zone by holding
thousands of gallons of water per rain event and allowing it to infiltrate
slowly. This recharges aquifers, springs, and seeps downslope while creating a
moist microclimate ideal for tree planting along the berm. In urban areas,
swales can absorb 100% of on-site rainfall, eliminating the need for
conventional drainage infrastructure. Swales are particularly valuable in
winter-wet climates and for establishing tree crops on marginal land.

## When to Choose Contour Swales
Swales are the better choice for smaller properties, homesteads, and
permaculture food forests where earthworks are manageable and the goal is
rapid groundwater recharge. They excel on moderate slopes where runoff is a
problem and tree establishment is a priority. Swales are ideal for sites with
compacted or degraded soils where deep water infiltration is needed to restore
soil biology and structure. In urban and suburban settings, swales provide
beautiful, productive drainage solutions that replace conventional stormwater
infrastructure with lush, food-producing landscapes. The immediate effect of
swales — visible water retention from the first significant rain — provides
satisfying and motivating results.
