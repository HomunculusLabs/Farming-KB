# Stinging Nettle vs Horsetail

## Overview

Stinging nettle (*Urtica dioica*) and common horsetail (*Equisetum arvense*)
are two of the most widely used wild plants in permaculture, herbal medicine,
and traditional foraging. Both are aggressive perennial spreaders, both serve
as dynamic mineral accumulators, and both have deep roots in ethnobotanical
traditions spanning thousands of years. Yet they occupy fundamentally different
botanical lineages, accumulate different nutrients, and serve different primary
functions in the garden and medicine cabinet.

Medicinally, horsetail is approved by the German Commission E and European

connective tissue repair. Historically, the abrasive silica-rich stems were
used for scouring metal cookware and polishing wood, earning the common name
"scouring rush." Horsetail is toxic to horses and livestock due to thiaminase
content (destroying vitamin B1), though drying or boiling inactivates this
enzyme safely for human herbal preparations.
