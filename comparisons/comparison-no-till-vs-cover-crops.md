## Key Takeaways

- No-till preserves existing soil structure and fungal networks; cover crops feed and extend those networks
- Termination method matters as much as cover crop selection: crimping preserves fungi, tilling destroys them
- The two practices are complementary, not interchangeable, and deliver the strongest results when combined
- Tailor cover crop choice to the microbial preferences of your intended cash crop
- Both practices reduce long-term labor and input costs while building measurable soil health improvements
- Transition gradually from tillage, using cover crops as a bridge to support soil biology during the change
- Document your observations each season: soil texture, water infiltration, weed pressure, and crop vigor all serve as indicators of progress that inform future management decisions
- Remember that soil health is a long-term investment; the microbial community takes time to rebuild after years of tillage, but once established it becomes increasingly self-maintaining
