# Psychedelic Therapy Models Comparison

Psychedelic-assisted therapy encompasses a diverse range of approaches, from rigorously controlled clinical protocols to indigenous ceremonial traditions that predate modern medicine by millennia. Each model operates from different assumptions about the nature of healing, the role of the guide or therapist, the appropriate dose, and the importance of preparation and integration. MDMA (80-120 mg, with optional 40-60 mg booster at 1.5-2 hours) is administered in three sessions spaced 3-5 weeks apart. Each session is preceded by three preparatory therapy sessions and followed by multiple integration sessions. Two therapists (male/female pair) maintain a supportive but non-directive presence. The protocol prioritizes safety, patient autonomy, and structured therapeutic framing.

**Key features:** FDA Phase 3 trials completed, structured protocol with manual, co-therapist model, extensive preparation and integration, inner-directed approach.
**Evidence base:** Strongest clinical evidence base of any psychedelic therapy — Phase 3 trials showed 67% of MDMA group no longer met PTSD criteria vs 32% placebo.

### Johns Hopkins Psilocybin Protocol

Researchers at Johns Hopkins University have developed a protocol for high-dose psilocybin (20-30 mg per 70 kg) administered in 1-2 sessions. Sessions are conducted in a comfortable, living-room-like setting with a male/female guide pair. Extensive preparation establishes trust and intention. During sessions, participants lie on a couch with eyeshades and a curated music playlist. The protocol draws from Grof's non-directive approach but emphasizes the mystical-type experience as the primary therapeutic mechanism. Used in studies for depression, anxiety, smoking cessation, and alcoholism.

**Key features:** High-dose, 1-2 sessions, non-directive guides, living-room setting, music program, emphasis on mystical experience.
**Evidence base:** Multiple published RCTs showing efficacy for depression, anxiety, nicotine and alcohol dependence. Griffiths et al. demonstrated dose-dependent mystical experience and lasting positive personality changes.
