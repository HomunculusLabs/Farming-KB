## Quick-Answer Summary

- **Choose Sedum** for sprawling [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]], [[query-how-do-green-roofs-and-living-walls-benefit-urban-environments]], living walls, and situations needing fast, low-maintenance spread.
- **Choose Sempervivum** for tight rosette form, [[hamilton-rock-garden-design]] focal points, container arrangements, and areas with harsh freeze-thaw cycles.
- Both are excellent, drought-tolerant succulents in the family Crassulaceae but differ significantly in growth habit, lifespan, and propagation.

## Taxonomic Relationship

Both genera belong to the stonecrop family **Crassulaceae**, sharing the Crassuloideae subfamily's thickened, water-storing leaves. However, they occupy different evolutionary branches:

| Feature | Sedum | Sempervivum |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| Genus | *Sedum* L. (~430 species) | *Sempervivum* L. (~40 species) |
| Subfamily | Sempervivoideae (tribe Sedeae) | Sempervivoideae (tribe Semperviveae) |
| Common names | Stonecrop, showy stonecrop | Houseleek, hens-and-chicks |

Despite the shared family, they are not closely inter-fertile and should not be confused with each other.

## Growth Habit Comparison

- **Sedum** — Mat-forming or upright, with fleshy stems that trail, creep, or stand erect depending on species. Low-growing types (e.g., *S. spurium*, *S. acre*) spread aggressively as ground cover; taller species (e.g., *S. spectabile*, *S. telephium*) form upright clumps.
- **Sempervivum** — Tight, symmetrical rosettes that grow as individual "hens" producing smaller offsets ("chicks") on stolons. No true stems — the rosette is a compressed leaf axis.

## Hardiness Comparison

| Metric | Sedum | Sempervivum |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| USDA Zones | 3–11 (species-dependent) | 3–9 |
| Cold tolerance | To −40 °F (hardy species) | To −40 °F; excels in freeze-thaw |
| Heat tolerance | Good to excellent | Moderate; struggles above 90 °F in humid climates |
| Winter survival | Dies back to ground; regrows | Rosettes remain evergreen |

Sempervivum is generally the tougher cold-weather genus, tolerating exposed, wind-blasted sites better than most Sedum.

## Light and Soil Requirements

| Requirement | Sedum | Sempervivum |
|-------------|-------|-------------|
| Sun | Full sun to partial shade | Full sun (6+ hours ideal) |
| Soil type | Sandy, gravelly, well-drained | Sandy, rocky, lean; tolerates limestone |
| Soil fertility | Low to moderate; avoid rich soils | Very low; poor soil preferred |
| pH range | 6.0–7.5 | 6.0–8.0 (alkaline-tolerant) |

Neither genus tolerates heavy clay or persistently wet feet. [[comparison-raised-beds-vs-hugelkultur]], in-ground, and containers all work with appropriate drainage. Gritty mixes are recommended.

## Water Needs

- **Sedum**: Drought-tolerant once established. Water deeply but infrequently; every 1–2 weeks in summer, almost none in winter. Overwatering [[query-what-causes-root-rot-in-cannabis-and-how-to-prevent-it]] rot.
- **Sempervivum**: Extremely drought-tolerant. Rosettes store water internally. Water only when soil is completely dry; avoid water pooling in rosette centers.

## Propagation

| Method | Sedum | Sempervivum |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| Stem cuttings | Primary method — cut and lay stems on soil | Less effective; rosettes don't have long stems |
| Leaf cuttings | Works for many species | Rarely successful |
| Offset division | Possible by dividing clumps | Primary method — detach "chicks" |
| Seed | Possible; hybrids may not come true | Possible; slow and variable |

## Bloom Characteristics

- **Sedum**: Flat or rounded clusters (cymes) of star-shaped flowers in white, pink, red, or yellow. Bloom time varies: late summer to fall for upright species; summer for creepers. Flowers are long-lasting and attract pollinators.
- **Sempervivum**: Star-shaped flowers in pink, red, or purple on tall, fleshy stalks. Blooms in midsummer. **The flowering rosette is monocarpic — it dies after blooming**, but offsets continue the colony.

## Maintenance Requirements

- **Sedum**: Very low. Trim spent flower heads in fall if desired. Control spread in rock gardens. Divide every 3–5 years to rejuvenate older clumps.
- **Sempervivum**: Minimal. Remove dead (post-bloom) rosettes. Clear debris from rosette centers. Replant offsets to maintain colony density.

## Pest and Disease Profiles

- **Sedum**: Susceptible to aphids, mealybugs, slugs, and snails. Fungal issues (Botrytis, rust) in humid, poorly ventilated conditions. Deer-resistant.
- **Sempervivum**: Relatively pest-free. Occasionally affected by mealybugs and vine weevil larvae at the root crown. Crown rot from waterlogged soil is the main threat.

## Lifespan and Monocarpic Behavior

- **Sedum**: Perennial; individual plants live many years. Upright species benefit from periodic division. No monocarpic behavior — plants flower repeatedly.
- **Sempervivum**: Each rosette is **monocarpic** — it flowers once after 2–4 years, sets seed, then dies. The colony persists indefinitely through offsets, so this is rarely a practical concern for gardeners.

## Summary Decision Table

| Your Situation | Best Choice | Reason |
|----------------|-------------|--------|
| Large ground-cover area | Sedum | Faster, denser spread |
| Harsh winter exposure | Sempervivum | Superior freeze-thaw tolerance |
| Formal container display | Sempervivum | Architectural rosette form |
| Green roof installation | Sedum | Proven shallow-root performance |
| Pollinator-friendly planting | Sedum | Late-season nectar source |
| Low-fertility alkaline soil | Sempervivum | Thrives in poor, limey soils |
| Minimal maintenance priority | Tie | Both are extremely low-care |
| Architectural rosette texture | Sempervivum | Tight, geometric form |

Both genera reward gardeners with years of beauty and resilience. For maximum impact, combine them — use Sempervivum as structural rosette accents and Sedum as the connecting, spreading ground cover beneath.
