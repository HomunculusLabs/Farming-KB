## Phytoremediation vs Mycoremediation

## Overview
Phytoremediation and mycoremediation are two biological approaches to cleaning
contaminated environments. Phytoremediation uses living plants and their
associated root-zone microorganisms to remove, degrade, or immobilize
contaminants, while mycoremediation deploys fungi and their enzyme systems to
break down pollutants. Both are cost-effective, environmentally friendly
alternatives to conventional excavation and chemical treatment, but they
operate through different biological mechanisms and are suited to different
types of contamination and site conditions.

## P
Mycoremediation uses fungi, specifically mushrooms and their mycelial
networks, to break down environmental pollutants. From Cotter's "Organic
Mushroom Farming and Mycoremediation," the approach has proven effective
against PCBs, aromatic hydrocarbons, oil spills, and biological contaminants
including E. coli. Fungi secrete powerful extracellular enzymes — including
lignin peroxidases, manganese peroxidases, and laccases — that cleave complex
molecules into smaller units, which are then further degraded by other
organisms in a sequential species approach.
A unique advantage of mycoremediation is that fungi are extracellular chemical
catalysts. Unlike bacteria, which must come into direct contact with
contaminants, fungal enzymes begin metabolic work ahead of the advancing
mycelium and can degrade compounds without the fungal body needing to be
present. [[natural-building-materials-guide]], reaching contaminants that are
physically inaccessible to plant roots. The mycelium "swims through its own
fluids," dissolving materials for absorption through cell walls.
Mycoremediation addresses two main pollution categories. For microbial
contaminants (fecal coliforms, pathogens), fungi disrupt replication, erode
cell membranes, enforce species exclusion by occupying territory, and alter
chemical gradients like pH and [[cotter-pet-livestock-waste-fungal-remediation-systems]]. It is ideal for large, shallow contamination
plumes where the visual monitoring of plant health provides easy progress
assessment. Phytoremediation works well for riparian zones, constructed
wetlands, and water treatment applications where aquatic plants can filter
contaminants from flowing water. The aesthetic value of living plants also
makes phytoremediation suitable for public spaces, parks, and residential
areas where the treatment process itself can be presented as landscaping.

## When to Choose Mycoremediation
Mycoremediation excels at breaking down organic contaminants including
petroleum products, PCBs, pesticides, and industrial chemicals. It is the
preferred approach for sites with deep or dense contamination where fungal
mycelium can penetrate materials inaccessible to plant roots. Mycoremediation
is particularly valuable for biological contamination from agricultural
runoff, failing septic systems, and livestock operations where pathogen
inactivation is needed. The ability to deploy mycelial mats, inoculated
substrates, and living barriers makes mycoremediation versatile for both in
situ treatment and preventive applications around potential contamination
sources.
