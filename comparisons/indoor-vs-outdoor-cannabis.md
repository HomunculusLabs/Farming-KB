# Indoor vs Outdoor Cannabis Cultivation

Indoor and outdoor cultivation represent fundamentally different approaches to growing cannabis. On a homestead with [[regenerative-agriculture]] principles, outdoor living soil beds integrate naturally with the broader system (compost, animals, cover crops). Indoor grows offer environmental control at the cost of energy and equipment. This comparison synthesizes from [[cannabis-indoor-living-soil]], [[cannabis-vegetative-stage]], [[cannabis-flowering-stage]], [[cannabis-plant-nutrition]], and [[cannabis-pest-management]].

## Head-to-Head Comparison

| Dimension | Indoor | Outdoor |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| **Environment control** | Full control (temp, humidity, light, CO2) | Dependent on weather and season |
| **Light source** | Artificial ([[cannabis-light-spectrums]], [[think-grow-model-h-plus]]) | Sun (free, full spectrum) |
| **Electricity cost** | Significant (lights, fans, AC, dehumidifier) | Minimal (pumps, fans if any) |
| **Watering** | Manual, controlled — dechlorinated water needed | Rain + irrigation; nature handles most |
| **Soil system** | [[cannabis-indoor-living-soil]] in beds/containers | Ground beds, amended native soil, or raised beds |
| **Pest pressure** | Higher risk (enclosed space, no natural predators) | Lower risk (beneficial insects, weather, airflow) |
| **Disease risk** | Higher (bud rot in humid indoor spaces) | Lower (natural airflow, UV from sun) |
| **Yield per plant** | Moderate (0.5-2 oz typical; 4+ oz with CO2/training) | Higher per plant (4-16+ oz, space-limited only) |
| **Yield per sq ft** | Higher (continuous cycles, vertical potential) | Lower (one crop per season in most climates) |
| **Quality (terpenes)** | Good with living soil, but can lack sun complexity | Often superior — full spectrum UV boosts terpenes |
| **Harvest frequency** | 3-4 crops per year (perpetual or seasonal rotation) | 1 crop per year in most climates |
| **Strain flexibility** | Any strain (control photoperiod) | Limited by climate and season length |
| **Discretion** | High (indoor, locked room) | Low (visible plants, smell travels) |
| **Legal exposure** | Generally lower (indoor = less visible) | Higher (outdoor = visible, smell) |
| **Start-up cost** | High ($1,000-5,000+ for equipment) | Low ($100-500 for soil amendments, irrigation) |
| **Ongoing cost** | Moderate-high (electricity, consumables) | Low (amendments, water) |
| **Labor intensity** | Daily monitoring, training, environmental management | Moderate (planting, training, watering, pest checks) |
| **Integration with homestead** | Separate system (electricity-dependent) | Natural integration (compost, animals, [[water-management]]) |

## Light: The Defining Difference

| Aspect | Indoor | Outdoor |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Source** | LED fixtures (e.g., [[think-grow-model-h-plus]]) | Sun — free, full spectrum including UV-A and UV-B |
| **Spectrum control** | Full control — tune blue/red ratio by growth stage | Fixed (sun); cannot adjust for growth stage |
| **Photoperiod** | Controlled by timer (18/6 veg → 12/12 flower) | Controlled by season; flowering triggered naturally |
| **PPFD** | 400-600 veg, 800-1200 flower (adjustable) | Varies by latitude, weather, time of day; peak 1500-2000+ |
| **Daily light integral (DLI)** | Can be optimized precisely | Depends entirely on location and season |
| **UV exposure** | Optional (some LEDs have UV; most don't) | Natural UV-A and UV-B — stimulates trichome production |
| **Impact on terpenes** | Good with living soil; limited UV means less trichome stress | Superior — UV stress drives resin/terpene production |

**Key insight**: The sun provides a DLI and spectrum that no artificial light can fully replicate. Outdoor cannabis often produces more complex terpene profiles. However, indoor growers can produce year-round and control every variable.

## Pest and Disease Management

| Aspect | Indoor | Outdoor |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Spider mites** | Major threat (enclosed, no predators) | Present but controlled by natural predators |
| **Bud rot (botrytis)** | Major threat in late flower (high humidity) | Risk during rain/fog in fall; better airflow usually |
| **Powdery mildew** | Risk in poor airflow | Risk especially in humid climates |
| **Fungus gnats** | Common (overwatering in containers) | Rare (soil dries naturally between rains) |
| **Aphids/whiteflies** | Moderate risk | Lower (beneficial insects in ecosystem) |
| **Animals (deer, rabbits)** | Not applicable | Significant risk — fencing required |
| **Prevention approach** | Sanitation, filters, biological controls ([[cannabis-pest-management]]) | [[companion-planting]], biodiversity, beneficial insect habitat |
| **Control options** | Limited in flower (no residue on buds) | More flexibility — foliar sprays safe outdoors |
| **IPM complexity** | High (no natural backup systems) | Lower (nature provides balance) |

## Nutrition and Soil

| Aspect | Indoor | Outdoor |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Soil type** | Manufactured [[cannabis-indoor-living-soil]] mix | Native soil amended, or raised beds with compost |
| **Compost integration** | Worm castings, compost tea ([[compost-tea-aact]]) | Full homestead compost ([[composting-systems]]) — duck/goat manure |
| [[knf-preparations]] | Adjusted dilution rates (1:300-500 drenches) | Standard dilution (1:1000); broader application |
| [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]] | Used as soil inoculant | Used at scale (IMO-4 field application) |
| **Nutrient availability** | Controlled via amendments and tea schedule | Soil food web + natural mineralization + cover crops |
| **pH management** | Minimal in living soil (biology buffers) | Managed via [[soil-fertility-management]] practices |
| **Water management** | Manual, dechlorinated, pH-checked | Rain harvesting ([[water-management]]), drip irrigation |
| **Top-dressing** | Between cycles (amendments + compost + mulch) | Seasonal — compost in spring, mulch year-round |

## Climate and Timing

| Aspect | Indoor | Outdoor |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Season constraints** | None — grow any time | Frost-free season determines window |
| **Photoperiod trigger** | Manual (flip to 12/12) | Natural (shortening days trigger flower) |
| **Ideal climate zone** | Any (environment controlled) | Zone 7+ for photoperiod strains; auto-flowers for shorter seasons |
| **Strain selection** | Any strain (space/height are the limits) | Must match flowering time to frost-free days |
| **Auto-flowering strains** | Less advantage (control photoperiod) | Major advantage — harvest in 60-90 days from seed |
| **Seasonal planning** | See [[seasonal-planning]] for year-round indoor schedule | Plant after last frost, harvest before first frost |

## Cost Comparison (Per Grow Cycle)

| Item | Indoor (4x4 tent) | Outdoor (100 sq ft bed) |
|------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Setup (one-time)** | $1,500-3,000 (light, tent, fan, filter, meter) | $50-200 (amendment, compost, mulch, fencing) |
| **Electricity/cycle** | $50-150 (light 12-18 hrs/day × 60-90 days) | $0 (sun) |
| **Soil/cycle** | $50-100 (amendments, compost tea inputs) | $20-50 (compost, amendments — many on-site) |
| **Water/cycle** | $5-15 | $0-10 (rain + irrigation) |
| **Pest control/cycle** | $20-80 (beneficial insects, sprays) | $5-20 (neem, companion plants) |
| **Total per cycle** | $125-345 | $25-80 |
| **Annual (3 indoor cycles)** | $375-1,035 | $25-80 (1 crop) |

## Verdict

| If you want... | Best approach |
|---|---|
| Maximum control over every variable | **Indoor** — light, temp, humidity, CO2, nutrition all adjustable |
| Lowest cost per ounce | **Outdoor** — sun is free, soil can be built on-site |
| Best terpene/flavor profile | **Outdoor** — full sun spectrum including UV produces complex resin |
| Year-round production | **Indoor** — 3-4 harvests per year vs 1 outdoor |
| Integration with homestead systems | **Outdoor** — compost, animal manure, [[water-management]], [[food-forest-design]] |
| Discretion / security | **Indoor** — locked room, no visible plants or smell |
| Breeding and seed production | **Outdoor** — more space, natural light, lower cost per plant |
| Strain experimentation | **Indoor** — any strain regardless of flowering time |
| [[regenerative-agriculture]] alignment | **Outdoor** — closed-loop with compost, animals, cover crops |

**For a homesteader**: Outdoor living soil beds are the natural choice — they integrate with existing compost, animal manure, and cover crop systems. Indoor growing is worth adding as a supplement for year-round production, strain preservation, and breeding. Both benefit from [[knf-preparations]] inputs and [[soil-food-web]] management principles.
