## Sourdough vs Wild Yeast Baking

## Overview

Both sourdough and wild yeast baking rely on naturally occurring microorganisms rather than commercial baker's yeast to leaven bread. However, they differ in scope, philosophy, and technique: sourdough centers on maintaining a stable, perpetuated starter culture, while wild yeast baking encompasses a broader set of approaches including spontaneous fermentation, environmental yeast capture, and the use of diverse regional grains grown on the homestead itself.

## Seasonal Considerations

Both approaches are influenced by seasonal temperature changes, but wild yeast baking is particularly responsive to environmental conditions. Warmer summer temperatures accelerate fermentation and may require adjusted feeding schedules or cooler proofing locations. Winter cold slows fermentation dramatically, which can be an advantage for developing deeper flavor during long cold retards but requires patience and potentially longer proof times.

For homestead grain growers, seasonal planning is essential. Winter wheat is planted in the fall and harvested in early summer, while spring wheat and rye are planted in spring for late summer harvest.
