# Topping vs Supercropping

## Overview

Topping and supercropping are two of the most widely used high-stress training
(HST) techniques in [[query-how-do-i-choose-the-right-cannabis-strain-for-my-grow]] tool for their specific grow environment, skill level, and
plant training goals.

## Topping

Supercropping is a more advanced form of high-stress training that involves
deliberately crushing or bending stems to create a "knuckle" of thickened tissue
at the bend point. Unlike topping, no tissue is removed. Instead, the inner
xylem and cambium are crushed while the outer bark remains intact, and the
branch is bent to a horizontal or downward position. This mechanical stress
triggers the plant's wound-healing response, redirecting auxin away from the
bent area and toward lateral branches below. The crushed area heals into a
thickened knuckle that is actually stronger than the original stem, creating a
reinforced nutrient highway to the upper canopy.

The technique requires careful timing and manual dexterity. Branches must be at
least 6-8 inches long and still flexible. The grower squeezes and rolls the stem
between thumb and forefinger until it softens, then gently bends it to the
desired angle. If done correctly, the branch bends without breaking; if it
snaps, plant tape can salvage the branch. The result is a wider, bushier plant
with multiple bud sites at a more uniform canopy height, which translates to
more even light distribution and larger overall harvest.

Supercropping excels at controlling plant height in limited-space grows and can
be performed throughout the [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-vegetative-phase-management]].

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Topping | Supercropping |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| Approach | Removes apical meristem | Bends and crushes stems |
| Stress Level | Medium (open wound) | High (tissue damage) |
| Recovery Time | 1-2 weeks per event | 3-7 days per bend |
| Canopy Effect | Discrete multiple colas | Wide, even bush shape |
| Height Control | Moderate | Excellent |
| Risk of Failure | Low if timed correctly | Medium (snapping risk) |
| Skill Required | Beginner-friendly | Intermediate to advanced |
| Repeatability | 3-4 events maximum | Many bends per plant |
| Equipment Needed | Scissors or pruners | Hands only |
| Best Stage | Early-mid vegetative | Vegetative through early flower |

## When to Choose Topping

Topping is the better choice for novice growers who want a reliable, predictable
method for increasing yield. It works best in grows with adequate vertical space
where the primary goal is simply to multiply main colas rather than manage
height. Topping pairs well with [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] to establish the main branch structure and determine the
number of primary colas, then use supercropping to fine-tune canopy height, fill
light gaps, and ensure even bud development across the entire plant. This
combination leverages the predictable cola multiplication of topping with the
spatial control and height management of supercropping, resulting in a maximally
productive canopy with minimal wasted light energy.
