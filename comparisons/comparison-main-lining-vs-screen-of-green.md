# Main-Lining vs Screen of Green
## Overview
Main-lining and Screen of Green (SCROG) are two of the most effective plant training techniques
for maximizing cannabis yield in indoor environments. Both techniques aim to create an even,
flat canopy that exposes the maximum number of bud sites to direct light. However, they differ
fundamentally in their approach, complexity, and the type of grower they best serve. Main-lining
relies on precise structural manipulation to build symmetrical manifold patterns, while SCROG
uses a physical mesh screen to guide and constrain plant growth horizontally. Understanding the
strengths and limitations of each method is essential for choosing the right approach for your
specific grow space, plant count, and experience level.
## Main-Lining
[[cannabis-training-techniques-comparison]]
Screen of Green (SCROG) uses a horizontal mesh screen, typically made of nylon netting,
poultry wire, or rigid trellis panels, to physically constrain plant growth into an even
canopy. The screen is installed 8 to 20 inches above the growing medium, depending on the
expected stretch of the strain. As branches grow upward through the screen openings, they
are woven or tucked back under the screen to encourage lateral spread rather than vertical
growth. This continuous tucking process fills the screen evenly, creating a flat sea of
bud sites that all receive direct light exposure from the grow lamps above.
SCROG works well with 1 to 4 plants per square foot, making it significantly more forgiving
for growers working under plant-count restrictions or those who prefer managing fewer plants.
The screen also serves a dual purpose by providing structural support for heavy colas during
late flowering, reducing or eliminating the need for bamboo stakes, yo-yo hangers, or other
support systems. The method is compatible with any growing medium including soil, coco coir,
and hydroponic systems, and adapts easily to various container sizes. Growers typically aim
to fill 70 to 80 percent of the screen before switching to the 12/12 flowering cycle, as
the remaining stretch during early flowering will fill the remaining gaps.
One of SCROG's biggest advantages is its accessibility for beginners. Unlike main-lining,
which requires precise timing and a deep understanding of plant architecture, SCROG is
relatively intuitive. As long as branches are consistently tucked under the screen during
vegetative growth, the plant naturally adapts to the grid pattern. The method also allows
for flexible plant counts; a single large plant can fill an entire screen over a long veg
period, or several smaller plants can share the space for a faster turnaround. SCROG can
be combined with other techniques like topping, fimming, or LST to further increase branching
and bud site density before the screen is introduced.
## Key Differences
| Aspect | Main-Lining | Screen of Green |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| Approach | Structural manifold via topping and LST | Physical screen constrains growth |
| Training type | High-precision, multi-stage process | Ongoing weaving and tucking |
| Plant count | Few plants (1-4 per light) | Flexible (1-4 per sq ft) |
| Veg time required | 6-8 weeks minimum | 4-6 weeks typical |
| Cola formation | 8-16 equal colas per plant | Many colas across screen surface |
| Structural support | Requires separate staking | Screen itself provides support |
| Beginner friendliness | Moderate to advanced | Beginner friendly |
| Strain compatibility | Photoperiod strains only | Photoperiod and some autoflowers |
| Canopy control | Built into the manifold | Managed through screen tucking |
| Equipment needed | Soft ties, scissors, patience | Mesh screen, scissors, ties |
| Height predictability | Very predictable | Adjustable via screen height |
| Defoliation needed | Minimal after manifold | May need some during flowering |
## When to Choose Main-Lining
Main-lining is the superior choice when you are limited to very few plants per grow and want
to extract maximum yield and quality from each one. Medical growers working within strict
plant-count limits often find main-lining ideal because it transforms a single plant into a
multi-cola powerhouse that fills the canopy space effectively. The technique is also excellent
for growers who value uniformity above all else; because every cola receives nearly identical
light exposure and airflow, harvest timing is simplified and overall bud quality is consistent
from top to bottom of the plant.
Additionally, main-lining produces visually impressive plants that are easier to manicure and
harvest. The open manifold structure means fewer hidden bud sites in shadowed lower zones,
which reduces popcorn bud production and improves overall yield quality. Growers who enjoy
the hands-on process of training and have the patience for a longer vegetative period will
find main-lining deeply rewarding. The technique pairs well with advanced methods like
defoliation and lollipopping for even greater canopy optimization and light penetration.
## When to Choose Screen of Green
SCROG is the better option for growers who want a reliable, high-yielding method that does
not require advanced training skills. The screen does much of the work by physically directing
branch growth, making it forgiving of timing mistakes and uneven growth rates. If you are
growing under plant-count restrictions but still want a full canopy, SCROG allows fewer plants
to fill the entire grow area effectively. It is also the preferred method for growers using
heavy-yielding strains that produce large colas, as the screen prevents branches from flopping
over under their own weight during late flowering.
SCROG excels in grow tents and small indoor spaces where maximizing every square foot of
canopy matters. The screen creates a defined boundary for plant growth, making height
management predictable and straightforward throughout the grow. For hydroponic growers, SCROG
integrates seamlessly with drip systems, deep water culture, and ebb-and-flow setups. The
method is also highly adaptable; you can start with a simple nylon net screen and upgrade
to more rigid trellising as your experience and confidence grow over successive runs.
## Hybrid Approaches
Many experienced growers combine both techniques for exceptional results. A main-lined plant
can be trained under a SCROG screen, where the symmetrical manifold structure fills the grid
evenly and the screen provides additional support during the heavy flowering phase. This
hybrid approach offers the structural precision of main-lining with the physical constraints
and support benefits of SCROG. Some growers also use main-lining during early vegetative
growth to establish the primary manifold, then introduce a screen for the final weeks of
vegetative growth to fine-tune canopy evenness before flipping to the 12/12 flowering cycle.
