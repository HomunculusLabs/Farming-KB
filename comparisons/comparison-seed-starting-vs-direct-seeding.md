# Seed Starting vs Direct Seeding
Two fundamental approaches to getting vegetables growing, each with distinct trade-offs in
vigor, [[direct-seeding-techniques]]
Sowing seeds directly in the garden where they will grow to maturity. Solomon argues
this produces healthier, hardier plants than transplants for most crops, and should
be the default method for the majority of [[query-how-to-prevent-transplant-shock-when-moving-cannabis-plants]].
- Lower cost (no pots, soil mix, lights, or heating equipment needed).
- Better variety control than buying commercial transplants from a nursery.
- Some crops fundamentally cannot be transplanted: beets, carrots, corn, radishes,
  and other taproot vegetables suffer permanent damage when their roots are disturbed.
### Disadvantages
- Not suitable for heat-loving crops in short-season areas with limited frost-free
  days.
- Requires careful attention to sowing timing and soil conditions.
- Demands adherence to a multi-week thinning schedule.
- Germination depends heavily on seed quality, soil moisture, and soil temperature;
  failure rates are higher than with controlled indoor germination.

## When to Choose Seed Starting Indoors
- Short-season climates where heat-loving crops need every possible growing day.
- Crops requiring a long season: tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, melons, winter squash.
- Maximizing total growing season length for sequential or multiple harvests.
- When a controlled early environment is needed for reliable germination.
- Producing transplants for sale or distribution to other gardeners.
## When to Choose Direct Seeding
- Most vegetable crops, especially cool-season varieties (lettuce, peas, brassicas).
- Root vegetables that cannot be transplanted: carrots, beets, radishes, parsnips.
- Regions with adequate frost-free season length for the crop in question.
- Maximizing plant vigor and root system health for the strongest possible plants.
- Low-input, low-cost approach requiring minimal equipment investment.
- Crops known to be sensitive to transplant shock (many brassicas, beans, peas).
## Hybrid Approach
The most practical strategy for most home gardeners combines both methods:
- Start heat-loving crops (tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, melons) indoors to ensure
  ripe harvests before fall frost.
- Direct-seed all cool-season crops, root vegetables, and crops that resent
  transplanting.
- Harden off all indoor starts thoroughly before transplanting to the field.
- Time indoor sowing so transplants go into the garden only after outdoor conditions
  are favorable, avoiding the temptation to plant too early.
