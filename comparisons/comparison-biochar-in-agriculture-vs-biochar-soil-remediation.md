adsorption, precipitation, and complexation reactions driven by biochar's
abundant surface functional groups (carboxyl, hydroxyl, phenolic, lactone) and
its alkaline pH, which reduces metal solubility through precipitation of metal
hydroxides and carbonates. Organic pollutants are sequestered through
hydrophobic partitioning into the non-polar carbon matrix, pore-filling in
micropores, and pi-pi interactions with aromatic carbon structures.

Remediation applications use significantly higher rates than agriculture: 1 to
10% by weight (10 to 100 tonnes per hectare) depending on contaminant
concentration and site conditions. Higher pyrolysis temperatures (600 to 900
degrees C) produce biochars with greater surface area and microporosity for
organic pollutant adsorption, while lower temperatures (300 to 500 degrees C)
retain more oxygen-containing functional groups for chemical complexation of
metals. Surface modification with acids, bases, or oxidizing agents can
enhance remediation properties, and impregnation with iron or manganese
creates biochar composites with enhanced catalytic properties.

Remediation biochar is increasingly combined with phytoremediation for
synergistic effects. By immobilizing metals and reducing their
bioavailability, biochar enables plant establishment on sites too toxic for
vegetation. The combination of biochar with hyperaccumulator plants enhances
overall remediation efficiency. Biochar also supports soil microbial
communities essential for rhizodegradation of organic contaminants, and can
act as an electron shuttle facilitating reductive dechlorination of
chlorinated solvents.
