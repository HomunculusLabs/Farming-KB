# Hot Composting vs Vermicompost vs Bokashi

Three composting approaches suited to different scales, inputs, and timelines on a homestead. Each has distinct strengths — most homesteads benefit from running all three in parallel.

## At a Glance

| Dimension | Hot Composting | Vermicompost | Bokashi |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|---------|
| **Process** | Aerobic, thermophilic (110-160°F) | Aerobic, mesophilic (worms) | Anaerobic, lactic fermentation |
| **Speed** | 2-4 weeks active + 2-4 weeks curing | Continuous; harvest every 2-3 months | 2-4 weeks ferment + 2-4 weeks burial |
| **Temperature** | Needs 70°F+ to start; self-heats | 55-77°F ideal; worms die above 85°F | Any temperature (slows in cold) |
| **Space needed** | Large (3x3x3 ft min pile) | Small (bin fits under sink or in closet) | Very small (kitchen bin) |
| **Inputs accepted** | Greens + browns (no meat/dairy) | Kitchen scraps, coffee, paper (no meat/dairy/citrus excess) | ALL food waste including meat, dairy, bones, citrus, onions |
| **Volume capacity** | Unlimited (scale with pile/bin size) | 1/2 lb food/day per sq ft of bin surface | 1-5 gallon bin handles typical kitchen |
| **Nutrient preservation** | Some N lost as off-gas during thermophilic phase | Excellent — worm gut processing concentrates nutrients | Best — no heat, no off-gassing, all nutrients preserved |
| **Pathogen kill** | Yes (131°F+ for 3 days) | Partial (worm gut selective) | No — fermented, not sterilized; soil burial finishes |
| **Weed seed kill** | Yes (thermophilic) | No | No |
| **Effort** | Medium (turning every 2-3 days) | Low (feed weekly, harvest occasionally) | Very low (daily sprinkle, drain tea) |
| **Startup cost** | Low (pile or bins) | Low ($30-60 for bin + worms) | Low ($30-50 for bin + bran) |
| **Ongoing cost** | Free | Free (self-reproducing worms) | Bokashi bran (~$10-20/year) |
| **End product** | Finished compost (humus) | Worm castings (biologically dense) | Pre-compost (needs soil burial to finish) |
| **Best for** | Bulk yard waste, manure, large volumes | Kitchen scraps, biological inoculant, indoor | Kitchen waste with meat/dairy, apartments |

## Deep Comparison

### Nutrient Quality

**Bokashi** preserves the most nutrients — the anaerobic lactic acid fermentation doesn't generate heat, so no nitrogen or carbon is lost to off-gassing. All original nutrients enter the soil when buried.

**Vermicompost** is biologically the richest. Worm castings contain 10-20x more microbial diversity than surrounding soil, plus plant growth hormones (cytokinins, auxins) produced during gut processing. NPK is roughly 1-1-1 with broad trace mineral coverage. However, castings quality depends on what the worms were fed — [[soil-fertility-management]] warns that mineral-poor inputs produce mineral-poor castings.

**Hot compost** is the most straightforward bulk amendment but loses some nitrogen during the thermophilic phase (as ammonia gas). Finished compost typically tests ~1-1.5% N, ~1% P, ~1% K — excellent as a soil conditioner but not a complete fertilizer on its own.

### Pathogen and Weed Seed Control

Only **hot composting** reliably kills pathogens (131°F+ for 3 days) and weed seeds. This makes it essential for composting [[duck-keeping]] and [[goat-keeping]] manure. [[vermicomposting]] and [[bokashi]] should not receive manure from sick animals or seeding weeds.

### Integration on the Homestead

The three systems form a complementary stack:

1. **Bokashi bin** in the kitchen handles ALL food waste daily — no sorting needed
2. **Vermicompost bin** processes overflow kitchen scraps, [[oyster-mushroom-coffee-grounds-cultivation]], paper waste; produces castings for [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]], [[comparison-cannabis-cloning-vs-seed-starting-indoors]], and [[compost-tea-aact]]
3. **Hot [[mushroom-cultivator-compost-pile-management]]** handles bulk waste: yard debris, [[fukuoka-textdoc-animal-manure-human-waste-natural-cycle-heretic]] + bedding, spent garden plants, large volumes from food preservation

Bokashi pre-compost can be buried in garden beds where [[vermicomposting]] worms further process it. Hot compost provides bulk humus for [[no-till-gardening-method]] top-dressing. All three feed the [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]].

## Verdict

Don't choose one — run all three. Bokashi for zero-waste kitchen processing, vermicompost for biologically dense castings, hot compost for bulk manure and yard waste management. Together they handle every organic waste stream on the homestead and produce complementary soil amendments.
