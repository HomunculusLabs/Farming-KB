## Deep Dive: Wood Chip Mulch

### What It Is

Chipped or shredded woody material from [[comparison-fruit-tree-pruning-vs-fruit-tree-grafting-guide]] or arborist work. Typically a mix of bark, wood, and green material. Quality varies by tree species, chip size, and green-to-wood ratio.

### Strengths

- **Long-lasting weed suppression:** 5–10 cm layer blocks light for 1–3+ years.
- **Fungal food:** Lignin and cellulose feed [[composting]] — converted from raw waste into stable, odor-free, pathogen-safe soil amendment rich in plant-available nutrients.

### Strengths

- **Highest [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]] of the three:** Provides significant plant-available NPK plus micronutrients. The fastest way to boost soil fertility.
- **Feeds bacterial [[soil-foodweb]], ideal for annual vegetables and brassicas.
- **Improves soil CEC:** [[teaming-with-microbes-humic-and-fulvic-acids]] acids increase the soil's ability to hold and exchange nutrients.
- **Rapid results:** Nutrients are available immediately. Visible improvement in plant growth within weeks.
- **Complete amendment:** Full spectrum of macro- and micronutrients plus beneficial microorganisms and humus.

### Weaknesses

- **Risk of contamination:** Improperly composted manure can contain pathogens, weed seeds, and pharmaceutical residues. Must reach 55°C+ for 15+ days.
- **Potential for over-application:** Excess manure, especially poultry, causes nitrogen burn and phosphate buildup. More is not better.
- **Not a mulch:** Does not provide weed suppression or moisture conservation. It's a soil amendment, not a mulch.
- **Variable quality:** Nutrient content varies by animal species, diet, bedding, and composting process.
- **Herbicide carryover:** Aminopyralid and other persistent herbicides on hay/pasture can survive composting. Source from untreated forage.

### Best Practices

- Use only fully composted, screened manure — dark, crumbly, earthy-smelling, no recognizable material.
- Apply 1–3 cm as top-dress or work 2–5 cm into top 15 cm of soil.
- Poultry manure is hot — use sparingly (0.5–1 cm) or mix with other amendments.
- Soil test every 2–3 years to monitor phosphorus levels.

1. **Base layer:** 1–3 cm composted manure for immediate nutrient availability.
2. **Middle layer:** 2–3 cm leaf mold for humic acids, structure, and water retention.
