## Fungal Dominant Tea

Fungal-dominant compost tea is brewed using humic acid, fish hydrolysate, and 
other foods that favor fungal growth over bacterial proliferation. A critical 
technique is oatmeal pre-activation, where oatmeal is added to compost 24 to 48 
hours before brewing to stimulate fungal hyphae growth that can then be 
extracted into the tea. The resulting brew has a significantly higher ratio of 
fungal to bacterial biomass. Fungal-dominant tea is designed for perennial 
plants, shrubs, trees, and forest gardens where fungal-dominated soils are the 
natural condition. Fungi build soil structure through glomalin production and 
hyphal networking, improve water retention, and provide sustained nutrient 
release. This tea formulation is particularly effective against fungal diseases 
including powdery mildew and botrytis, working through competitive exclusion 
rather than direct antagonism. Filtration is coarser at 300 to 400 microns to 
avoid damaging fungal hyphae, which means application equipment must 
accommodate larger particles.

## When to Choose Bacterial Dominant Tea

Choose bacterial-dominant tea for your annual vegetable beds, row crop fields, 
and pasture systems. It is the correct formulation for seed treatments, as 
bacterial coatings protect germinating seeds and support early root 
development. Apply it to recently disturbed or tilled soils where bacterial 
communities need rapid reestablishment. Lawn care programs benefit from 
bacterial tea because turf grasses evolved in bacterial-dominated grassland 
ecosystems. It is also appropriate for foliar applications on vegetable crops 
where bacterial leaf colonization provides a protective barrier against 
bacterial pathogens. Gardeners starting new annual beds from scratch should 
begin with bacterial-dominant teas to establish the microbial foundation that 
annual crops prefer, transitioning to fungal-dominant teas only if 
incorporating perennial elements into the bed design.

## When to Choose Fungal Dominant Tea

Choose fungal-dominant tea for orchards, food forests, berry patches, and any 
system dominated by woody perennials. It is essential when establishing trees 
and shrubs, because their root systems depend on mycorrhizal fungal 
associations for nutrient uptake. Apply fungal tea as a soil drench around the 
root zones of newly planted trees and around established perennials showing 
signs of decline. Vineyards, berry plantations, and nut orchards all benefit 
from the soil structure improvements that fungal communities provide. It is 
also the appropriate choice for remediation of compacted or degraded soils 
where fungal hyphae can physically break up compacted layers and begin 
rebuilding aggregation. Areas transitioning from annual agriculture to 
perennial polyculture should receive regular fungal tea applications to shift 
the soil microbiome toward its new plant community.

## Hybrid Approaches

Many experienced compost tea brewers create transitional blends that combine 
elements of both formulations. A balanced tea using moderate amounts of both 
molasses and humic acid can support mixed plantings of annuals and perennials. 
Another approach applies bacterial tea in spring when annual vegetables are 
actively growing and shifts to fungal tea in autumn when perennial root systems 
are preparing for dormancy. Successional application is particularly effective 
in food forests where the herbaceous understory contains annual vegetables 
while the canopy and shrub layers are perennial. Some brewers cycle between 
bacterial and fungal teas on alternating weeks to maintain diverse microbial 
communities. The most sophisticated approach involves microscope-based 
monitoring of your soil microbiome, adjusting tea formulations based on actual 
biological assessments rather than assumptions about what your plants need.
