## Comfrey Tea vs Compost Tea

## Overview

**Comfrey tea** and **compost tea** are both liquid organic fertilizers widely used in permaculture and organic growing, but they are produced by fundamentally different processes and deliver different benefits. Comfrey tea is a fermented extract made by steeping comfrey leaves (*[[knf-water-soluble-potassium-detailed]], moderate phosphorus, and trace minerals mined by comfrey's deep taproot from subsoil layers. Compost tea supplies living bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes that improve nutrient cycling, disease suppression, and soil structure.

## Nutrient Profiles

### Comfrey Tea

Comfrey leaves contain one of the highest potassium concentrations of any temperate plant. Typical NPK values for dried comfrey leaf are approximately 1.8-0.5-5.3, though actual values vary by soil, harvest timing, and cultivar. When fermented into tea, the primary nutrients extracted are:

## Preparation Methods

### Comfrey Tea

**Anaerobic fermentation** is the most common method:

1. Fill a container (bucket or barrel) loosely with fresh comfrey leaves.
2. Add water to cover (rainwater preferred; avoid chlorinated tap water).
3. Weigh leaves down with a stone or brick to keep submerged.
4. Cover loosely to allow gas escape but exclude debris and rain.
5. Ferment 2–6 weeks. The mixture produces a strong, foul odor (ammonia).
6. Dilute 1:10 to 1:15 with water before application.

An aerobic "quick brew" method exists (aerating leaves and water for 24–48 hours), but is less common and produces a milder, less concentrated product. The anaerobic method is preferred for nutrient extraction efficiency.

### Compost Tea

**[[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT)** is the standard method:

1. Use high-quality, finished thermophilic compost (aged 6–12 months).
2. Fill a brewer with dechlorinated water (rainwater, well water, or aerated tap water).
3. Add compost at a ratio of roughly 1:10 to 1:20 compost-to-water by volume.
4. Add microbial food sources: unsulfured molasses (1–4 tbsp per 5 gallons), kelp meal, or fish hydrolysate.
5. Aerate vigorously with an air pump and diffuser stones for 12–24 hours, maintaining dissolved oxygen above 6 mg/L.
6. Apply within 4–6 hours of brewing. Must smell earthy — a foul smell indicates anaerobic contamination and the batch should be discarded.
