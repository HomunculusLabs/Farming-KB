# Aphids vs Spider Mites

## Overview

Aphids and spider mites are the two most destructive sap-feeding pests
that attack cannabis and garden plants indoors and outdoors. Aphids are
soft-bodied insects (six legs) that reproduce via live birth and serve
as major disease vectors. Spider mites are arachnids (eight legs) that
spin protective webbing and thrive in hot, dry conditions. Understanding
the differences between these pests is essential for choosing the right
identification and control strategy before infestations cause serious
crop loss.

## Aphids
[[spider-mites-cannabis]]

Spider mites (Tetranychus urticae and related species) are microscopic
arachnids — not insects — with eight legs. To the naked eye they appear
as tiny specks; a 10-30X magnifier reveals yellow-white, two-spotted
brown or red mites and translucent eggs. Spider mites are the single
most common and destructive pest in indoor cannabis gardens.

The two-spotted spider mite has an extraordinarily rapid life cycle.
Each female lays roughly 100 eggs. At 80°F (27°C), the full cycle from
egg to egg-laying adult takes only five days. A single unchecked female
can produce millions of descendants within a month. Spider mites
quickly develop resistance to synthetic pyrethrum, making rotation of
control methods essential.

Their signature damage includes yellowish-white stippling on leaf tops,
fine webbing on stems and under leaves (visible when misted with water),
and pock-marked leaves with visible suck-hole marks. Unlike aphids,
spider mites thrive in hot, dry conditions and struggle when humidity
exceeds 50%.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Aphids | Spider Mites |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| Classification | Insect (6 legs) | Arachnid (8 legs) |
| Size | Pinhead-sized, visible | Microscopic, speck-like |
| Reproduction | Live birth, parthenogenesis | Egg-laying, mated females |
| Cycle speed | Fast (days) | Extremely fast (5 days at 80°F) |
| Signature sign | Honeydew, ant farming | Fine webbing, leaf stippling |
| Disease role | Major virus vector | Not a significant vector |
| Preferred climate | All climates | Hot, dry (low humidity) |
| Resistance risk | Moderate | High (rapid pesticide resistance) |
| Best first control | Manual crush, soap spray | Lower temp, raise humidity |

## When to Prioritize Aphid Control

Focus on aphid management when you see ants trailing up plant stems,
find sticky honeydew on leaves or surfaces below plants, or notice
dense clusters of soft-bodied insects on new growth. Because aphids
are the primary virus vector, any garden with disease pressure should
treat aphid control as urgent. They are relatively easy to crush or
wash off manually and respond well to insecticidal soap and

organic spray effective against both pests, and always quarantine new
