## Overview

**Natural swimming pools (NSPs)** and **conventional (chemical) pools** represent
two fundamentally different approaches to creating a backyard swimming
environment. Conventional pools rely on chemical disinfectants — primarily
chlorine, along with algaecides, clarifiers, and pH adjusters — to maintain
water clarity and kill pathogens. Natural swimming pools use biological
filtration zones planted with aquatic plants, along with beneficial bacteria and
substrate media, to purify water through ecological processes similar to those
found in wetlands and lakes.

Both systems can produce safe, swimmable water, but they differ dramatically in
construction cost, maintenance burden, environmental impact, aesthetic quality,
and long-term operating expenses. The right choice depends on climate, budget,
maintenance willingness, site conditions, and personal values around chemical
use and ecological integration.

## Water Treatment: How They Work

### Natural Swimming Pools

An NSP is divided into two zones: the **swimming zone** (the open water area)
and the **regeneration zone** (a shallow planted area). Water circulates
between the two zones via a low-energy pump. In the regeneration zone, water
passes through layers of gravel, sand, and aquatic plant roots. Beneficial
bacteria colonize the substrate and break down organic matter — body oils,
sunscreen, leaves, ammonia from urine — converting it into nutrients that the
plants absorb. The plants themselves take up nitrogen, phosphorus, and other
dissolved nutrients, effectively starving algae.

Key biological processes:

- **Nitrification**: Ammonia → Nitrite → Nitrate (by *Nitrosomonas* and
  *Nitrobacter* bacteria)
- **Nutrient uptake**: Aquatic plants absorb nitrate, phosphate, and potassium
- **Phosphorus binding**: Iron-rich substrates or phosphate binders sequester
  excess phosphorus
- **UV clarification**: Some systems incorporate UV-C lamps as a backup
  pathogen control measure

### Conventional Pools

Conventional pools maintain water quality through a chemical-mechanical system:

1. **Disinfection**: Free chlorine (hypochlorous acid) kills bacteria, viruses,
   and algae by oxidizing cell walls and disrupting metabolic processes.
   Salt-chlorine generators produce chlorine on-site by electrolyzing dissolved
   salt, reducing the need for chemical handling.
2. **Filtration**: Water passes through sand, cartridge, or diatomaceous earth
   (DE) filters to remove particulate matter.
3. **Circulation**: A pump moves water through the filter system continuously.
4. **Chemical balance**: pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, and cyanuric
   acid levels must be monitored and adjusted regularly.

## Health Considerations

- **Skin and eye irritation**: Chlorine reacts with organic compounds in pool
  water to form chloramines (combined chlorine), which cause the characteristic
  "pool smell," red eyes, and skin irritation. NSPs have no chloramines.
- **Asthma and respiratory health**: Some studies suggest a correlation between
  regular chlorinated pool exposure and increased asthma risk in children,
  particularly from chloramine inhalation in indoor pools.
- **Pathogen control**: Properly maintained conventional pools effectively kill
  pathogens. NSPs can also achieve safe water quality — European standards
  (DIN 19643 for NSPs) require fecal coliform levels below 100 CFU/100mL,
  comparable to conventional pool standards. However, NSP water quality is more
  variable and depends on proper biological establishment.
- **Allergies**: Some swimmers are sensitive to chlorine. NSPs eliminate this
  concern entirely.

## Environmental Impact

- **Chemical pollution**: Conventional pools discharge chlorinated water during
  backwashing and draining, which can harm [[dighton-fungal-biomass-production-aquatic-ecosystems]]. NSPs produce no
  chemical runoff.
- **Energy use**: NSPs typically use smaller, lower-energy pumps because flow
  rates are lower and the biological system provides most of the filtration.
  Conventional pools require higher pump flow rates and may run pumps 8–12
  hours per day.
- **Water consumption**: Both systems lose water to evaporation, but NSPs can
  be designed to capture and reuse rainwater. Conventional pools require regular
  backwashing of filters, wasting additional water.
- **Biodiversity**: NSPs create habitat for amphibians, insects, and birds.
  Conventional pools are ecological dead zones in the landscape.
- **Carbon footprint**: NSP construction has a slightly higher embodied carbon
  footprint due to the larger excavation and substrate materials, but lower
  ongoing carbon emissions from reduced chemical manufacturing and transport.

## Maintenance Comparison

### Natural Pool Seasonal Maintenance

**Spring**: Remove dead plant material from the regeneration zone, divide
overgrown plants, check pump and plumbing, test water parameters (pH, nitrate,
phosphate), add new beneficial bacteria starter if needed.

**Summer**: Trim plants to prevent overgrowth into the swimming zone, skim
debris from the water surface, ensure pump is running, monitor water clarity.
