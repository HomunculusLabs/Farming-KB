## Mind-Body Problem vs Information Paradigm of Consciousness

The mind-body problem and the [[information-paradigm-of-consciousness]] address the same basic territory from different directions.
The mind-body problem is a question: how can subjective experience relate to physical matter?
The information paradigm is a proposed answer: mind and matter may both be forms of organized information.

## The Mind-Body Problem

The mind-body problem begins with an apparent mismatch between two kinds of description.
On one side are physical descriptions: neurons fire, receptors bind ligands, and networks exchange signals.
On the other side are subjective descriptions: colors glow, memories return, fear rises, and selfhood dissolves.
Both descriptions seem necessary, but neither easily translates into the other.
This is why the problem has persisted across philosophy, psychology, neuroscience, and religion.

## The Information Paradigm

The information paradigm attempts to dissolve the mismatch by treating mind and matter as different organizations of information.
Matter is not denied; it is understood as information structured according to physical and chemical relations.
Mind is not denied; it is understood as information structured according to neural and psychological relations.
Instead of asking how two substances interact, the paradigm asks how patterns transform across levels.
A psychedelic molecule changes neural information, which changes conscious information, which may change behavior and belief.

## Psychedelics as Evidence

Psychedelics make the comparison unusually concrete.
A classic psychedelic such as psilocybin begins as a molecule with a physical structure.
It becomes pharmacologically active after metabolism into psilocin.
It then modifies serotonergic signaling and large-scale patterns of brain activity.
The person may experience visual imagery, emotional insight, [[mckenna-suppression-psychedelic-research-lsd-therapy]], the split can make subjective reports seem either supernatural or merely illusory.
Neither extreme is adequate for understanding clinical and spiritual outcomes.
The framework identifies the problem but does not by itself provide a working model for integration.

## Strengths of the Information Paradigm

The information paradigm offers a common vocabulary for chemistry, neuroscience, psychology, and phenomenology.
It can describe receptor activity, neural dynamics, symbolic imagery, and integration as related forms of pattern transformation.
It fits psychedelic reports in which consciousness feels expanded, reorganized, or flooded with meaning.
It also aligns with neuroscientific interest in complexity, entropy, predictive processing, and integrated information.
For wiki navigation, it functions as a bridge between philosophy-of-mind pages and psychedelic pharmacology pages.

## Weaknesses of the Information Paradigm

The paradigm risks becoming too broad if information is not carefully defined.
If everything is information, the term may explain too little.
Critics also argue that calling consciousness information does not fully explain why information should feel like anything.
The hard problem may be renamed rather than solved.
In psychedelic contexts, the model can also overinterpret visions as organized information without distinguishing insight from confabulation.

## Practical Difference

The practical difference is that the mind-body problem is diagnostic, while the information paradigm is constructive.
The first identifies the wound in modern thought: subjective experience and objective science seem divided.
The second proposes a suture: both sides may be understood as organized information.
A researcher may use the mind-body problem to frame a study of altered consciousness.
The same researcher may use the information paradigm to generate hypotheses about entropy, integration, and meaning.

## Which Is More Useful?

For philosophical clarity, the mind-body problem remains indispensable.
It names the unresolved issue that every theory of consciousness must confront.
For interdisciplinary psychedelic research, the information paradigm is often more operational.
It gives scientists, therapists, and theorists a way to discuss transformations across brain, mind, and culture.
The best approach is to use both: the problem as a caution and the paradigm as a working model.

## Summary

The mind-body problem asks how consciousness and matter are related.
The information paradigm answers that both may be expressions of information organized at different levels.
Psychedelics are important because they make the relation experimentally and experientially vivid.
They show that a physical compound can alter the structure of subjectivity while leaving the mystery of subjectivity intact.
The comparison therefore reveals a useful division of labor: philosophy preserves the question, while information theory explores a possible bridge.
