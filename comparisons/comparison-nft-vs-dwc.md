DWC is widely regarded as one of the fastest-growing hydroponic methods for
cannabis, with plants often outpacing those in soil, coco coir, and other
hydroponic systems. The large reservoir volume provides exceptional stability,
buffering against temperature swings, pH drift, and nutrient concentration
changes. DWC systems are simple to build from readily available materials,
making them popular with beginners. Individual bucket systems allow each plant
to be managed separately, isolating problems to single plants rather than
affecting the entire grow. Water changes are straightforward, and the submerged
root zone is less prone to drying out than NFT channels.

The limitations of DWC center on root health management. The warm, nutrient-
rich, submerged environment is ideal for pathogenic organisms including Pythium
and other root rot fungi if aeration is insufficient. Maintaining solution
temperature below 70 degrees Fahrenheit is critical for preventing outbreaks.
The large reservoirs can be difficult to move once established, making cleaning
between grows labor-intensive. Algae growth on exposed surfaces and around net
pots is common and must be managed. In multi-bucket RDWC (Recirculating DWC)
systems, problems can spread quickly between connected reservoirs.

## Hybrid Approaches

Some growers combine elements of both systems. The RDWC (Recirculating Deep
Water Culture) system connects multiple DWC buckets to a central reservoir and
controller bucket, combining DWC's growth speed with centralized nutrient
management similar to NFT. Current Culture and other commercial systems blur the
line by adding waterfall-style aeration and nutrient film delivery elements to
DWC reservoirs. NFT channels are sometimes used for cloning and early vegetative
