Free-range forage systems deliver their greatest value over time. Once established, diverse plantings provide year-round nutrition with dramatically lower feed costs. Birds gain access to natural behaviors including dust bathing, roosting, and varied foraging.

## Free Range Forage Species Selection

Key forage species for poultry include:

- Mulberry trees for summer fruit production
- Comfrey for protein-rich leaf production
- Soldier fly larvae from composting systems
- Amaranth and sunflower for seed production
- Diverse grasses and clovers for ground-level grazing
- Pigeon pea and leucaena in warmer climates

Designing the forage system requires understanding seasonal production gaps. In temperate climates, winter forage is the primary challenge. Planning for evergreen species, root crops like Jerusalem artichokes, and stored fodder such as pumpkins bridges the lean months.

Fencing is critical for free-range success. Electrified poultry netting provides effective predator exclusion and can be moved periodically to rotate grazing pressure.

## Hybrid Approach

Many successful homesteads combine both methods. Use chicken tractors within free-range forage zones to concentrate impact where soil conditioning is needed most. The tractor provides controlled fertilization and pest management on garden beds, while the permanent forage system provides baseline nutrition for the flock.

This hybrid strategy delivers immediate garden benefits from the tractor while building toward long-term feed self-sufficiency through the forage system. Over time, as the forage landscape matures, the tractor becomes less necessary for garden work and more useful as a brooding or protective enclosure.

## Drawbacks and Limitations

Chicken tractor drawbacks include daily labor to move the enclosure, limited bird capacity, predator vulnerability if not secured, and bare soil if rotation is too slow. In heavy rain, the tractor area can become a mud pit. Birds in tractors also lack behavioral enrichment.
