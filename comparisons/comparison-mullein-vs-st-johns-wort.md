## Mullein

[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]]. In its first year it forms a distinctive woolly
basal rosette close to the ground, and in the second year sends up a
towering flower spike that can reach 2.5 meters. The leaves and flowers
are covered in dense stellate trichomes that give them a soft, felt-like
texture — earning folk names like "flannel plant" and "cowboy toilet
paper."

The primary medicinal actions of mullein center on the respiratory
tract. Its leaves and flowers contain 2–3% polysaccharide mucilage,
which coats and soothes irritated mucous membranes in the throat, bronchi,
and lungs. Triterpenoid saponins provide expectorant activity, stimulating
the production and expulsion of mucus. Flavonoids including luteolin,
apigenin, and kaempferol contribute anti-inflammatory and antispasmodic
effects that help relax bronchial smooth muscle and reduce cough spasms.

Mullein is indicated for bronchitis, persistent coughs, asthma, tracheitis,
and general chest congestion. A traditional remedy combines mullein flower
oil with garlic oil as ear drops for otitis media, particularly in
pediatric care. The leaves also have vulnerary properties and have been
used historically as poultices for wounds and burns. In permaculture
design, mullein serves as a dynamic mineral accumulator with a deep
taproot, and as a pioneer species that colonizes disturbed ground.
