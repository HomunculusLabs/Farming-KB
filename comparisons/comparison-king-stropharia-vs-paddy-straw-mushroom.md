# King Stropharia vs Paddy Straw Mushroom

## Overview

King stropharia (Stropharia rugoso-annulata) and paddy straw mushroom
(Volvariella volvacea) are two outdoor-friendly, warm-weather cultivated
mushrooms that occupy very different niches. King stropharia, also called
the wine cap or garden giant, is a temperate-to-subtropical species grown
primarily on hardwood chip beds in gardens and permaculture systems. Paddy
straw mushroom is a strictly tropical species cultivated on rice straw and
agricultural waste across Southeast Asia. Both are prized for ease of
outdoor cultivation, but they diverge sharply in climate needs, substrate
preferences, and cultivation timelines.

## King Stropharia

[[paddy-straw-mushroom]]

Paddy straw mushroom is the third most cultivated mushroom worldwide by
production volume, grown extensively across China, Vietnam, Thailand,
Indonesia, and the Philippines. It is uniquely heat-loving, requiring
temperatures of 30 to 35 degrees C for mycelial growth and 28 to 31
degrees C for fruiting. It will not grow below 20 degrees C and dies
below 10 degrees C, making temperate cultivation impossible without
heated facilities.

Traditional outdoor cultivation uses bundled, soaked rice straw stacked
into layered beds. Spawn run takes only 5 to 7 days, and the first harvest
arrives just 10 to 14 days after spawning, making it one of the fastest-
cropping mushrooms in the world. Indoor methods add composting and steam
pasteurization steps for higher yields. The mushroom features a distinctive
volva at its base and pink spores, but wild foraging is dangerous due to
confusion with deadly Amanita species.
