# Mullein vs Calendula

## Overview

Mullein (*Verbascum thapsus*) and calendula (*Calendula officinalis*) are
two of the most widely used medicinal herbs in Western herbalism, yet they
serve distinctly different primary functions. Mullein is the go-to herb for
respiratory conditions — its soothing mucilage and expectorant saponins
make it indispensable for coughs, bronchitis, and chest congestion.
Calendula, often called pot marigold, is the premier topical wound healer,
with triterpenoid compounds that accelerate tissue repair and reduce skin
inflammation. Both are easy to grow, self-seed readily, and play valuable
roles in permaculture gardens as [[calendula-officinalis]]

Calendula is a cheerful annual or short-lived perennial in the aster
family (Asteraceae), growing 30–60 cm tall with bright orange and yellow
flower heads. Native to the Mediterranean, it has been cultivated for
medicinal use for over 1,000 years. The flowers are rich in triterpenoid
saponins (faradiol, arnidiol, calenduladiol), flavonoids (quercetin,
isorhamnetin), carotenoids (lutein, beta-carotene), and essential oils
that confer potent wound-healing and anti-inflammatory properties.
