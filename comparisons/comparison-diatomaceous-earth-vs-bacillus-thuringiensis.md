## Diatomaceous Earth vs Bacillus Thuringiensis

## Overview

Diatomaceous earth (DE) and [[bacillus-thuringiensis]] (Bt) are two of the
most widely used organic pest control agents in permaculture and sustainable
agriculture. Both avoid the ecological damage of synthetic chemical
pesticides,
yet they operate through fundamentally different mechanisms. DE is a physical
desiccant that abrades insect exoskeletons, while Bt is a biological agent
that produces targeted protein toxins lethal to specific insect larvae.

## Diatomaceous Earth

[[diatomaceous-earth]]

Diatomaceous earth is a naturally occurring sedimentary rock composed of
fossilised silica shells from microscopic diatoms. When milled to a fine
powder, the sharp microscopic fragments abrade the waxy epicuticle of
insects and arthropods, causing lethal dehydration within 24 to 48 hours.

DE is broad-spectrum, affecting any insect or arachnid with a waxy
exoskeleton. It works on contact rather than through ingestion, making it
effective against crawling pests like ants, fleas, beetles, and mites.
Because its action is purely mechanical, insects cannot develop physiological
resistance to DE. However, DE must remain dry to function; moisture causes
particles to clump and lose abrasive properties. It is commonly used in
poultry dust baths, grain storage, and as a perimeter barrier around plants.

## Bacillus Thuringiensis

[[bacillus-thuringiensis]]

Bacillus thuringiensis is a Gram-positive soil bacterium that produces
crystalline protein toxins (Cry and Cyt proteins) during sporulation. When
susceptible insect larvae ingest these crystals, the alkaline environment of
their midgut dissolves the protoxin, which then binds to specific gut
receptor cells. The cells lyse, the gut becomes paralysed, and the larva
stops feeding within hours and dies within two to three days.

Unlike DE, Bt is highly target-specific. Different subspecies and strains
affect particular insect orders: Bt kurstaki targets Lepidoptera
(caterpillars), Bt israelensis targets Diptera (mosquitoes, fungus gnats),
and Bt tenebrionis targets Coleoptera (beetles). This specificity makes Bt
safe for beneficial insects, pollinators, and vertebrates, but limits its
usefulness against non-target pest species.

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Diatomaceous Earth | Bacillus Thuringiensis |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Mode of action** | Physical desiccation | Biological toxin ingestion |
| **Spectrum** | Broad-spectrum (any arthropod) | Narrow, species-specific |
| **Resistance risk** | None (mechanical) | Possible with overuse |
| **Moisture tolerance** | Ineffective when wet | Effective when sprayed |
| **Speed of kill** | 24-48 hours | 2-3 days (feeding stops sooner) |
| **Application method** | Dust or dry sprinkle | Liquid spray on foliage |
| **UV stability** | Highly stable | Degrades in sunlight |
| **Beneficial insect safety** | Harms all insects | Safe for most beneficials |
| **Cost** | Low (cheap, reusable) | Moderate (product-specific) |
| **Shelf life** | Indefinite (if kept dry) | Limited (live spore product) |

## When to Choose Diatomaceous Earth

DE excels in dry environments and for crawling pest problems. It is ideal
for protecting stored grain from weevils and beetles, creating physical
barriers around garden beds to deter slugs and snails, and controlling
poultry ectoparasites when mixed into dust baths. In permaculture systems,
DE is valuable as a passive, always-on control that requires no rearing,
mixing, or timing of applications. It also functions as a soil amendment,
contributing trace silicon and improving soil structure.

Choose DE when you need persistent, broad-spectrum protection in dry
conditions, when cost is a primary concern, or when dealing with pests
that crawl through or over treated surfaces rather than feeding on foliage.

## When to Choose Bacillus Thuringiensis

Bt is the superior choice when you need to eliminate a specific caterpillar
or larvae problem while preserving beneficial insects, pollinators, and
natural predators. It is particularly effective against cabbage worms,
corn earworms, tomato hornworms, and fungus gnat larvae. Because Bt must
be ingested, it only affects insects actively feeding on treated foliage.

Bt is best used as a targeted intervention timed to egg hatching or early
larval stages. Apply as a foliar spray in the evening to reduce UV
degradation, and reapply after heavy rain. It is ideal for integrated pest
management (IPM) programs where preserving predator and parasitoid
populations is essential for long-term pest suppression.

## Combined Use

DE and Bt complement each other well in an integrated pest management
strategy. Use Bt as a targeted spray for specific foliar-feeding larvae
while applying DE as a ground-level barrier against crawling pests and as
a protective dust for stored harvests. This layered approach preserves
beneficial insects through Bt's specificity while providing broad physical
protection through DE. Avoid mixing the two into a single wet spray, as
DE loses efficacy when wet and may clog spray equipment.

## Decision Guide

| Your Situation | Best Choice |
|---------------|-------------|
| Caterpillars on brassicas | Bt (kurstaki) |
| Stored grain pests | DE (dry dust) |
| Fungus gnats in seedlings | Bt (israelensis) drench |
| Ants invading garden beds | DE perimeter barrier |
| Mixed pest pressure, dry climate | DE as primary, Bt targeted |
| Preserving beneficial insects | Bt (species-specific) |
| Poultry mite control | DE in dust baths |
| Caterpillars plus crawling pests | Both (Bt foliar + DE ground) |
