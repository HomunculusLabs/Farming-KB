## How to Grow Chili Peppers from Seed to Harvest

Chili peppers (*Capsicum* spp.) are warm-season crops in the nightshade family (Solanaceae) that range from sweet bell peppers to blazing-hot habaneros and superhots. They are rewarding to grow from seed, with enormous variety in shape, size, color, flavor, and heat. This guide covers the complete growing cycle for home gardeners.

## Understanding the Species

There are five domesticated *Capsicum* species, each with different characteristics:
| Species | Common Examples | Heat Range | Days to Maturity |
|---------|----------------|------------|-----------------|
| *C. annuum* | Jalapeño, poblano, Anaheim, bell, cayenne | 0–30,000 SHU | 65–90 days |
| *C. chinense* | Habanero, Scotch bonnet, ghost pepper, Carolina Reaper | 100,000–2,200,000+ SHU | 90–120 days |
| *C. frutescens* | Tabasco, Thai bird's eye, malagueta | 30,000–100,000 SHU | 90–100 days |
| *C. baccatum* | Ají amarillo, rocoto, bishop's crown | 30,000–50,000 SHU | 90–120 days |
| *C. pubescens* | Rocoto, manzano | 30,000–100,000 SHU | 120+ days |

Most home gardeners grow *C. annuum* varieties (jalapeños, bells, poblanos) because they are the easiest and fastest. *C. chinense* varieties (habaneros, superhots) require more heat and patience but offer extraordinary flavor and heat.

## Starting from Seed

### Timing

Chili peppers need a long [[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof-recipe-solomon]] (4-4-4 or 5-5-5)
- **Early growth** (first 4 weeks): Side-dress with nitrogen if growth is slow (fish emulsion, blood meal, or compost tea)
- **Flowering and fruiting**: Shift to higher-phosphorus and potassium (bone meal, kelp meal, or a 2-5-5 blend) to support fruit production
- **Avoid excess nitrogen**: Produces lush foliage at the expense of fruit; can also delay maturity
- **Calcium**: Adequate calcium prevents blossom-end rot. Add gypsum (calcium sulfate) at planting if your soil is deficient

### Pruning and Support

- **Staking or caging**: Most pepper varieties benefit from support, especially when loaded with fruit. Bamboo stakes, tomato cages, or the Florida weave method all work
- **Pruning**: Generally minimal. Pinch off the first flowers on young transplants to redirect energy to root and leaf growth. Remove any suckers that form below the first fork if the plant seems overly bushy
- **Superhots**: *C. chinense* superhots benefit from aggressive pruning to open the canopy and improve airflow, reducing disease pressure

## Common Problems

### Pests

| Pest | Identification | Control |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **Aphids** | Green/black clusters on new growth, curled leaves | Insecticidal soap, ladybugs, neem oil |
| **Spider mites** | Tiny dots on undersides of leaves, fine webbing | Increase humidity, spray with water, [[query-how-to-use-diatomaceous-earth-in-the-garden]] |
| **Pepper weevil** | Larvae in fruit; small holes with frass | Remove affected fruit, [[cervantes-bacillus-thuringiensis-bt-control]] (Bt) |

### Diseases

| Disease | Symptoms | Prevention |
|---------|----------|------------|
| **Blossom-end rot** | Black, sunken spots on blossom end of fruit | Consistent watering, adequate calcium |
| **Anthracnose** | Dark, sunken lesions on ripe fruit | Crop rotation, avoid overhead watering |
| **Phytophthora blight** | Sudden wilting, dark stem lesions, fruit rot | Excellent drainage, raised beds, resistant varieties |
| **Bacterial leaf spot** | Small, water-soaked spots that turn brown | Avoid overhead watering, copper sprays, resistant varieties |
| **Tobacco mosaic virus** | Mottled leaves, stunted growth, distorted fruit | Wash hands before handling; don't smoke near plants; resistant varieties |

### Environmental Issues

- **Blossom drop**: Caused by temperatures above 35°C or below 12°C, or inconsistent watering. Usually self-corrects when conditions improve
- **Sunscald**: Pale, papery patches on fruit exposed to direct sun after leaf loss. Provide adequate foliage cover or shade cloth
