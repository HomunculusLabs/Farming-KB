## What Is Azolla and Why Is It Called a Superplant?

## Short Answer

Azolla is a tiny free-floating aquatic fern that grows on the surface of still or slow-moving freshwater.

It forms a unique symbiotic relationship with a cyanobacterium called Nostoc (formerly Anabaena) azollae that lives inside its leaf cavities.

## Limitations and Cautions

Despite its benefits, Azolla is not a silver bullet and has several limitations.

Its nitrogen contribution is significant but usually insufficient as a sole nitrogen source for high-yielding modern rice varieties.

Azolla is sensitive to cold temperatures and cannot overwinter outdoors in temperate climates without protection.

In some regions Azolla species have become invasive, clogging irrigation canals and waterways.

Careful management and containment are essential, especially in tropical and subtropical areas outside its native range.

The nutrient profile of Azolla can vary depending on water chemistry, which complicates its use as a standardized animal feed.

Heavy metal accumulation is a concern if Azolla is grown in contaminated water, as the fern bioaccumulates toxins.

Farmers should always test water quality before using Azolla as feed.
