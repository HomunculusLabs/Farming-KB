# What Are the Different Types of Mycorrhizal Fungi?

## Short Answer

There are seven main types of mycorrhizal associations. Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) are the most common, found in roughly 80% of plant species. Ectomycorrhizal fungi (ECM) associate primarily with trees. Ericoid, orchid, arbutoid, monotropoid, and ectendomycorrhizal types specialize in specific plant families, each with distinct anatomical structures and ecological functions.

## Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF)

[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] are the most prevalent and agriculturally important type:

- Belong to the phylum Glomeromycota
- Form tree-like structures called arbuscules inside root cortical cells for nutrient exchange
- Associate with the vast majority of food crops, vegetables, grasses, and many cannabis cultivars
- Produce extensive external hyphal networks that explore soil far beyond the root zone
- Scavenge for phosphorus, nitrogen, zinc, and water that roots alone cannot reach
- The [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]] reference covers their life cycle and colonization patterns

AMF do not produce visible mushrooms and cannot be grown without a living plant host, which makes commercial cultivation dependent on root inoculation methods.

## Ectomycorrhizal Fungi (ECM)

ectomycorrhizal-networks form primarily with woody plants:

- Associate with trees in Pinaceae, Fagaceae, and Betulaceae families
- Common partners include pines, oaks, beeches, birches, and walnuts
- Unlike AMF, they do not penetrate root cells
- They form a dense sheath or mantle around root tips
- A network of hyphae between root cells called the Hartig net is the nutrient exchange site
- Many familiar edible mushroom species are ECM fungi: chanterelles, boletes, and truffles

The [[comparison-arbuscular-vs-ectomycorrhizal]] guide contrasts their biology, host ranges, and management considerations for growers working with both types.

## Ericoid Mycorrhizae

Ericoid mycorrhizae serve a specialized ecological niche:

- Associate with plants in the Ericaceae family including blueberries, cranberries, heaths, and heathers
- These plants typically grow in acidic, nutrient-poor soils where other plants struggle
- Ericoid fungi help access nitrogen and phosphorus from organic matter
- They produce enzymes that break down complex organic compounds
- [[dighton-ericoid-mycorrhizal-nutrient-acquisition]] research shows they access nutrients from decomposing organic matter

For growers of blueberries and other acid-loving plants, supporting ericoid mycorrhizae is essential for optimal nutrition.

## Orchid Mycorrhizae

Orchids have the most dependent mycorrhizal relationship:

- Their relationship with fungi is obligate, meaning orchids cannot survive without fungal partners
- Orchid seeds contain virtually no energy reserves
- They depend entirely on fungal partners for carbon and nutrients during the seedling stage
- Some orchid species remain partially dependent on fungi throughout their lives
- Non-photosynthetic orchids like Indian pipe are entirely dependent on fungi for carbon
- The fungi obtain carbon from neighboring trees through shared mycorrhizal networks

## Arbutoid Mycorrhizae

[[arbutoid-mycorrhiza]] represents a structural intermediate between types:

- Occurs in the Ericaceae subfamily Arbutoideae
- Common hosts include bearberry (Arctostaphylos) and madrone (Arbutus)
- Structurally intermediate between ecto- and arbuscular types
- They form a fungal mantle like ECM but also produce intracellular structures like AMF
- Relatively uncommon and poorly studied compared to the major types

## Monotropoid and Ectendomycorrhizae

These are the least common but ecologically interesting types:

**Monotropoid mycorrhizae:**
- Associate with non-photosynthetic plants like Indian pipe (Monotropa uniflora)
- These plants are entirely dependent on their fungal partners for carbon
- The fungi obtain carbon from neighboring trees through shared ECM networks
- Effectively, the non-photosynthetic plant parasitizes the tree-fungus connection

**Ectendomycorrhizae:**
- Show characteristics of both ecto- and arbuscular types
- Found primarily in certain tree seedlings, especially in the Pinaceae family
- The fungus forms both a mantle and intracellular colonization
- May represent an evolutionary transitional form between ECM and AMF types

## Choosing the Right Inoculant

For most gardeners and cannabis growers, AMF are the relevant type:

- AMF colonize the majority of food crops, vegetables, and cannabis varieties
- Commercial inoculants typically contain species like Rhizophagus irregularis
- Tree planters working with pines, oaks, or walnuts should seek ECM inoculants
- Blueberry growers should look for ericoid mycorrhizal products
- The [[symbiotic-fungi-mycorrhizal-types-and-classification]] reference helps identify the right type for your plants

## Key Points

- [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] colonize approximately 80% of plant species and are the most relevant for cannabis and vegetable growers
- ectomycorrhizal-networks associate primarily with trees and include many edible mushroom species
- Ericoid mycorrhizae help plants like blueberries and cranberries thrive in acidic, nutrient-poor soils
- Orchid mycorrhizae are obligate, meaning orchids cannot survive without their fungal partners
