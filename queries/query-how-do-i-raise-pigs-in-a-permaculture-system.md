# How Do I Raise Pigs in a Permaculture System?

## Short Answer

Raising pigs in a permaculture system means using the animal's natural behaviors — rooting, foraging, and manuring — as productive forces rather than problems to manage.
Instead of confinement barns, permaculture pigs live in [[paddock-system-pigs]] where they rotate through forest, pasture, and crop ground to build soil and clear land.
The approach draws heavily from [[mollison-free-range-pig-systems]] and [[holzer-pig-integration]], both of which treat pigs as landscape managers rather than mere meat producers.
Success depends on matching breed to environment, designing rotational paddocks, and integrating pigs with other livestock and cropping systems.

## Detailed Explanation

### Why Pigs Fit Permaculture

Pigs are omnivorous foragers that can convert kitchen scraps, crop residues, roots, acorns, and invasive plants into high-quality protein.
Their manure is rich in nitrogen and phosphorus, feeding the [[soil-food-web]] and accelerating compost production when managed correctly.
This biological fertility replaces expensive synthetic fertilizers and builds long-term soil health through each rotation cycle.
In [[permaculture-animal-integration]] systems, pigs serve multiple functions simultaneously: pest control, soil aeration, land clearing, and fertility cycling.
Sepp Holzer demonstrated this in [[holzer-livestock-integration-pigs-cattle]], where pigs prepared ground for planting by rooting up compacted soil and consuming root-dwelling pests.

### Breed Selection

Choosing the right breed matters because modern commercial hybrids are bred for confinement feeding, not foraging ability.
Heritage and landrace breeds excel in permaculture because they retain strong foraging instincts, hardiness, and maternal ability.
The [[mangalitza-pig-breeds]] from Hungary are prized for their lard production and ability to thrive on pasture and forest forage alone.
[[holzer-livestock-philosophy-pig-breeds]] emphasizes selecting locally adapted breeds over imported genetics, matching the animal to the climate and available forage.
Tamworth, Gloucester Old Spot, Large Black, and Berkshire are all excellent choices for outdoor systems with strong rooting and grazing ability.
Kunekune pigs from New Zealand are smaller and gentler, making them popular for small-scale [[pig-keeping-guide]] operations on suburban homesteads.

### Paddock Design and Rotation

The core infrastructure for permaculture pigs is a rotational [[paddock-system-pigs]] with moveable electric fencing and portable shelter.
Each paddock should include a mix of woodland, pasture, and bare ground so pigs can express different natural behaviors across the landscape.
Stocking density depends on forage quality, but a general rule from [[free-range-pig-management]] is 5–10 finishing pigs per acre of good mixed forage.
Rotate paddocks every 1–4 weeks to prevent over-rooting and allow vegetation to recover, following principles from [[pig-range-systems]].
Water infrastructure should include wallows (mud baths) for thermoregulation, as pigs cannot sweat and overheat easily in summer.

### The Pig Tractor

A [[pig-tractor-system]] uses pigs to prepare garden beds or clear land before planting, replacing tractor tillage with animal power.
Pigs are confined to a small area with dense root mats, invasive species, or crop residues and allowed to root thoroughly for 3–7 days.
[[mollison-designers-pig-tractors-forage-systems]] describes how Bill Mollison used pig tractors to convert gorse and blackberry thickets into productive orchard ground.
After the pigs finish, the area is forked level, covered with mulch or compost, and planted directly — the pigs have already tilled and fertilized it.
This technique works especially well for establishing new [[food-forest]] zones where deep-rooted perennials need loosened, fertile soil.

### Feeding and Forage Systems

Pigs in permaculture obtain 40–80% of their diet from forage when managed on diverse pasture and woodland systems.
[[pig-forage-systems-and-range-management]] details how to plant mixed forage paddocks with root crops like mangels, turnips, Jerusalem artichokes, and comfrey.
Tree crops — acorns, chestnuts, mulberries, persimmons, and apples — provide seasonal feed drops in [[silvopasture]] systems.
These "tree hay" systems were the traditional European method of fattening pigs before industrial grain feeding became standard.
Supplemental feeding with grain, whey, or kitchen scraps fills the nutritional gap, especially for growing piglets and lactating sows.
[[chos-global-natural-farming-livestock-applications]] and [[knf-livestock-odorless-piggery]] describe Korean Natural Farming approaches to pig feed fermentation, reducing odor and improving gut health.
[[fukuoka-mixed-grazing-pig-cow-chicken-orchard-integration]] shows how Masanobu Fukuoka combined multiple livestock species in orchards for mutual benefit and reduced feed costs.

### Housing and Shelter

[[holzer-earth-shelters-pigsties]] describes Sepp Holzer's approach to natural pig housing using earth-sheltered structures that stay cool in summer and warm in winter.
Simple three-sided shelters with deep straw bedding work well in most climates and are far cheaper than commercial hog barns.
Portable hog panels or electric netting allow shelters to move with the rotation, keeping pigs close to fresh forage.
Good drainage is essential — pigs create mud, so site shelters on slightly elevated ground with natural runoff away from waterways.
Access to clean drinking water at all times is non-negotiable; a single finishing pig drinks 2–4 gallons per day in warm weather.

### Health and Management

Permaculture pig systems generally have fewer disease problems than confinement operations because of fresh air, sunlight, diverse diet, and low stocking density.
Regular observation is the primary health tool — watch for changes in appetite, behavior, manure consistency, and body condition.
Internal parasites are managed through rotation (breaking the worm lifecycle by moving pigs before reinfection occurs) rather than routine deworming.
[[pig-husbandry-and-permaculture-integration]] covers basic health protocols including farrowing management, castration timing, and weaning strategies.
Work with a large-animal veterinarian to establish a baseline health plan even in low-input systems.

### Integration with Other Systems

Pigs integrate well with poultry, cattle, and orchard systems in multi-species grazing rotations.
Follow pigs with chickens, which scatter manure patties, eat fly larvae, and further fertilize the ground — this is a classic [[animal-tractor-systems-permaculture]] pattern.
In [[pig-forage-and-integrated-management]], pigs are followed by ruminants that graze the grass regrowth, then by poultry for sanitation.
Pig manure feeds [[compost]] systems directly when collected from deep-bedded shelters, or indirectly when deposited on paddocks during rotation.
The manured ground then supports cover crops, vegetables, or orchard trees, closing the nutrient loop described in [[permaculture-integrated-animal-systems]].

## What About Slaughter and Processing?

On-farm processing or mobile slaughter units reduce transport stress and give you control over the final product quality.
Pigs raised on diverse forage produce darker, more flavorful meat with higher omega-3 content than grain-fed confinement pork.
Lard-type breeds like the [[mangalitza-pig-breeds]] yield premium fat for cooking, soap-making, and charcuterie.
If you raise fewer than 10 pigs per year, consider partnering with neighboring homesteads to share mobile slaughter costs.
Home curing and smoking of pork (bacon, ham, salami) add value and extend storage without refrigeration, following traditional food preservation methods.
Check local regulations regarding on-farm slaughter, meat inspection, and direct sales before building your processing plans.

## What About Odor Control?

Conventional pig operations are notorious for smell, but well-managed permaculture pigs on adequate land with proper rotation produce minimal odor.
[[knf-piggery-without-smell]] demonstrates that fermented feed and deep-bedding systems with effective microorganisms eliminate odor entirely.
Adequate space, fresh forage, clean water, and regular paddock rotation are the primary odor-control strategies in pasture-based systems.

## Key Points

- Pigs are natural landscape managers whose rooting, foraging, and manuring behaviors serve multiple permaculture functions.
- Heritage breeds like [[mangalitza-pig-breeds]] outperform commercial hybrids in outdoor foraging systems.
- Rotational [[paddock-system-pigs]] prevent over-rooting and allow vegetation recovery between grazing periods.
- The [[pig-tractor-system]] replaces mechanical tillage for land clearing and bed preparation.
- Forage can supply 40–80% of pig diet when [[pig-forage-systems-and-range-management]] is designed with diverse root crops and tree fodder.
- [[holzer-pig-keeping-paddock-system]] and [[mollison-free-range-pig-systems]] provide detailed design guidance from two permaculture pioneers.
- Multi-species integration with poultry and cattle multiplies the benefits of [[pig-husbandry-and-permaculture-integration]].
- Forage-fed pork commands premium prices at farmers' markets and through direct sales to restaurants.
- Starting small with 2–3 weaner piglets on a quarter-acre paddock is the lowest-risk way to learn pig management.
