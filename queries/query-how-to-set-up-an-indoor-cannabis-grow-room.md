# How to Set Up an Indoor Cannabis Grow Room

## Short Answer

Setting up an indoor cannabis grow room requires addressing five core systems: lighting, ventilation, odor control, container and medium selection, and environmental monitoring. A basic setup for a 4 by 4 foot tent includes a 400 to 600 watt LED grow light, an inline fan with carbon filter for air exchange, oscillating fans for canopy airflow, fabric pots with quality soil, and a thermometer and hygrometer for monitoring. Budget for $500 to $1,500 depending on scale and quality of equipment.

## Choosing the Grow Space

The first decision is selecting and preparing the physical grow space.

Options range from a dedicated room to a grow tent, closet, or even a modified cabinet. The [[cannabis-lighting-guide]] provides detailed comparisons of available technologies. Modern LED grow lights have become the standard for indoor cultivation due to their energy efficiency, low heat output, and full-spectrum capabilities that support both vegetative and flowering stages without switching bulbs.

| Light Type | Efficiency | Heat | Spectrum | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LED (full spectrum) | High | Low | Full spectrum | Medium to high |
| CMH/LEC | Medium | Medium | Full spectrum | Medium |
| HPS (high pressure sodium) | Medium | High | Red-heavy | Low to medium |
| T5 fluorescent | Low | Very low | Blue-heavy | Low |

For a 4 by 4 foot tent, a quality LED drawing 300 to 400 actual watts from the wall provides sufficient intensity for flowering. The [[cannabis-indoor-ventilation-setup]] fundamentals include both air exchange (bringing in fresh air and exhausting stale air) and air circulation (moving air within the canopy to strengthen stems and prevent hot spots). For most grow tents, an inline centrifugal fan rated for the tent volume provides both functions.

Ventilation sizing guidelines:

- Calculate tent volume in cubic feet (length times width times height)
- Target at least one complete air exchange per minute during flowering
- Size carbon filters to match or exceed the inline fan capacity
- Add oscillating fans for horizontal airflow across the canopy
- Passive intake vents at the bottom of the tent work for small to medium setups

The [[cannabis-pot-selection-and-container-sizing]] guide covers the main options. Fabric pots (also called smart pots) are the most popular choice because they air-prune roots, prevent root binding, and improve drainage compared to plastic containers. Air pots provide similar benefits with more rigid construction.

Container sizing for photoperiod plants:

| Pot Size | Recommended Plant Count (4x4 tent) | Final Plant Size |
|---|---|---|
| 3 gallon | 4 to 6 plants | Small to medium |
| 5 gallon | 2 to 4 plants | Medium |
| 7 gallon | 1 to 3 plants | Medium to large |
| 10+ gallon | 1 to 2 plants | Large |

For autoflowering strains, which have a fixed life cycle, 3 to 5 gallon pots are typically sufficient regardless of the desired final size.

## Environmental Monitoring

Consistent environmental conditions are essential for healthy cannabis growth.

A quality thermometer and hygrometer combination unit provides the basic monitoring needed. More advanced growers may add data loggers that track conditions over time, or smart controllers that automate equipment based on sensor readings.

Target environmental ranges:

- Vegetative stage: 70 to 85F, 50 to 70 percent relative humidity
- Early flowering: 68 to 80F, 45 to 55 percent relative humidity
- Late flowering: 65 to 78F, 40 to 50 percent relative humidity
- Night temperature: 10 to 15F cooler than day temperature for terpene production
