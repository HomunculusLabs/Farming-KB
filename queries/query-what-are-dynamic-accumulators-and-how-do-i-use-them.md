# What Are Dynamic Accumulators and How Do I Use Them?

## Short Answer

Dynamic accumulators are plants with deep or extensive root systems that draw nutrients from deep soil layers and concentrate them in their leaves, stems, and roots.

When these plants are cut and used as mulch or compost, they release those concentrated nutrients back into the topsoil where crops can access them.

Common examples include comfrey, nettles, yarrow, dandelion, and daikon radish.

They function as a living fertilizer system, reducing or eliminating the need for imported amendments.

## What Makes a Plant a Dynamic Accumulator?

A dynamic accumulator is any plant that gathers specific minerals from the soil in concentrations higher than average.

The term was popularized by permaculture practitioners and has some scientific backing, though not every claim has been rigorously tested in controlled studies.

The key mechanism is deep taproots that reach subsoil layers untouched by most crop roots.

These deep roots mine calcium, potassium, phosphorus, magnesium, and trace minerals that have leached below the topsoil over decades.

## How Nutrient Accumulation Works

Dynamic accumulators work through two primary pathways in the soil profile.

The first pathway is deep nutrient mining via taproots that extend 3 to 10 feet below the surface.

These roots access mineral deposits, parent rock weathering products, and leached nutrients that shallow-rooted crops cannot reach.

The second pathway is biological solubilization through root exudates and mycorrhizal partnerships.

Root exudates include organic acids that dissolve mineral compounds, making them bioavailable for root uptake.

Mycorrhizal fungi extend the effective root zone dramatically and can transport phosphorus and micronutrients back to the host plant.

The [[dynamic-accumulator-chop-and-drop-mulching]] page covers the specific technique in detail.

Timing matters: cut accumulators just before flowering for peak nutrient concentration in the leaves.

Avoid letting them go to seed unless you want them to spread, as some accumulators like comfrey can become aggressive.

## Integrating Accumulators with Cover Crops

Dynamic accumulators pair naturally with cover cropping strategies in any rotation.

Many cover crop species such as daikon radish, clover, buckwheat, and phacelia also function as accumulators.

The [[dynamic-accumulator-plants-nitrogen]] page covers nitrogen-fixing accumulator species in detail.

For potassium, comfrey, borage, and wood ash from burned accumulator brush are the top sources.

For calcium and magnesium, dandelion, chicory, and plantain accumulate these structural minerals in their leaves.
