# How Do I Raise Geese in a Permaculture System?

## Short Answer

Geese are one of the most efficient and low-maintenance livestock options for a permaculture property.
They are primarily grazers, meaning they convert grass and forage directly into meat, eggs, and fertilizer without requiring expensive grain inputs.
In a well-designed system, geese provide weed control, pest management, soil fertility, and alarm protection while thriving on pasture with minimal supplemental feeding.
Their integration into [[goose-keeping-and-husbandry-guide|permaculture systems]] has been documented by practitioners like Sepp Holzer and Bill Mollison for decades.

## Why Geese Fit Permaculture

Geese are natural foragers that can obtain up to 80% of their diet from pasture when given adequate grazing area.
Unlike chickens, which scratch and destroy garden beds, geese graze delicately and can be directed to manage specific areas through rotational grazing.
They produce large amounts of nitrogen-rich manure that builds soil fertility across the property.
Geese are also excellent watchdogs, alerting the entire homestead to predators or unfamiliar visitors with loud honking.
Their lifespan of 15-25 years means a single investment in a breeding flock provides decades of service.
[[mollison-geese-grass-control-and-integrated-poultry|Bill Mollison]] specifically recommended geese for orchard understory management and weed suppression.

## Choosing Goose Breeds

Different goose breeds serve different purposes within a permaculture system.
The Toulouse is a heavy, docile breed ideal for meat production and orchard grazing.
Embden geese grow quickly and are excellent foragers that reach market weight efficiently.
Pilgrim geese are auto-sexing, making flock management easier since males and females have different plumage.
Chinese geese are lighter, more active foragers and prolific egg layers, producing 40-80 eggs per year.
The African goose is a striking, large breed known for its calm temperament and steady meat yield.
For a small permaculture property, a mixed flock of 4-6 geese provides all the benefits without overwhelming the system.
[[goose-keeping|Basic goose husbandry]] principles apply across all breeds.

## Housing and Fencing

Geese require minimal housing compared to other poultry, needing only a secure shelter to protect them from predators at night.
A simple three-sided shelter with good ventilation and dry bedding is sufficient for most climates.
Fencing should be at least 4 feet high since geese can climb surprisingly well despite their size.
Electric fencing is highly effective for containing geese and excluding predators like foxes and coyotes.
Unlike chickens, geese do not typically fly over fences once they reach adult weight.
[[poultry-housing-guide|Poultry housing]] should provide at least 6 square feet per goose inside the shelter.
Water access is critical — geese need fresh water for drinking and prefer having a pond or pool for bathing and mating.

## Grazing and Forage Systems

Geese thrive in [[integrated-poultry-forage-systems-zone-two|integrated forage systems]] where they rotate through paddocks of mixed grasses, clovers, and broadleaf plants.
A single goose consumes roughly 1-2 pounds of fresh forage per day during the growing season.
Clover, alfalfa, dandelion, plantain, and chicory are all excellent forage plants that geese relish.
They will also consume slugs, snails, and insect pests as they graze, providing additional pest control.
During winter or dry periods, supplement with hay, silage, or a modest amount of grain.
[[poultry-forage-species-compendium|Forage species selection]] should match your climate zone and the specific needs of your geese.
Rotational grazing prevents overgrazing and allows pasture recovery, typically with rest periods of 3-4 weeks per paddock.

## Integrating Geese with Other Livestock

Geese coexist well with [[chicken-keeping|chickens]] and [[duck-keeping|ducks]] in a multi-species permaculture system.
They occupy a different grazing niche than chickens, focusing on grass and broadleaf plants rather than scratching for insects and seeds.
Ducks and geese can share pond systems, though geese tend to be dominant at feeding areas.
Geese are particularly effective in orchards, where they mow the understory without damaging tree trunks.
[[holzer-poultry-keeping-ducks-geese-and-chickens|Sepp Holzer]] demonstrated that mixed poultry flocks create synergistic pest control and fertilization effects.
Avoid housing geese with very young poultry, as their large size can cause accidental injuries.

## Water Management

Geese need access to clean water for drinking, bathing, and reproductive health.
A natural pond or [[duck-keeping-and-integration-into-permaculture-systems|constructed wetland]] serves multiple permaculture functions alongside goose management.
Pond water enriched by goose manure becomes nutrient-rich irrigation water for surrounding gardens.
Geese help control aquatic weeds and algae in ponds, keeping waterways open and functional.
If a full pond is not feasible, a stock tank or children's wading pool provides adequate bathing water for a small flock.
Ensure water sources have gradual entry points so geese can exit safely without getting trapped.

## Predators and Protection

Common predators of geese include foxes, coyotes, raccoons, birds of prey, and neighborhood dogs.
Geese are relatively large and aggressive compared to other poultry, which deters many smaller predators.
Their loud honking serves as a natural alarm system that alerts both the flock and the farmer to danger.
Secure nighttime housing is essential since most predator attacks occur after dark.
[[faires-goose-management-and-integration|Faires' goose management guide]] recommends livestock guardian dogs for properties with heavy predator pressure.
Goslings are especially vulnerable and should be kept in protected enclosures until they reach 6-8 weeks of age.

## What About Geese for Weed Control?

Geese are among the most effective biological weed control agents available to permaculture farmers.
They selectively graze grasses and young weeds while leaving many established crops and woody plants alone.
Cotton, strawberry, mint, and orchard growers have used geese for decades as living cultivators.
[[mollison-designers-geese-and-duck-weed-control|Mollison's weed control strategies]] highlight geese as a primary tool for managing invasive grasses.
A flock of 4-6 geese can effectively maintain 1-2 acres of orchard understory or crop pathways.
They are particularly useful for controlling weeds in areas where mechanical cultivation would damage crop roots.

## Breeding and Egg Production

Goose eggs are large, rich, and excellent for baking, with each egg equivalent to about 3-4 chicken eggs.
Most breeds lay seasonally in spring, producing 20-60 eggs per year depending on the breed and management.
Geese typically mate in pairs or trios and form strong pair bonds that persist across breeding seasons.
Incubation takes 28-32 days, and goslings grow rapidly on pasture with minimal supplemental feed.
Allowing geese to hatch and raise their own goslings is simpler and more reliable than artificial incubation.
[[poultry-range-management|Range management]] during the breeding season should include protected nesting areas with tall grass or straw bedding.

## Key Points

- Geese are efficient grazers that convert pasture into meat, eggs, and fertilizer with minimal grain inputs.
- They provide weed control, pest management, soil fertility, and predator alarm functions in permaculture systems.
- Breeds like Toulouse, Embden, and Chinese geese each offer different advantages for specific property goals.
- Housing is minimal but secure nighttime shelter is essential for predator protection.
- Geese integrate well with [[backyard-poultry-and-integrated-animal-systems|other poultry species]] in multi-species systems.
- Fresh water access for drinking and bathing is critical for goose health and reproduction.
- Rotational grazing through [[cell-grazing|managed paddocks]] prevents overgrazing and maintains pasture health.
- Geese are among the best biological weed control options for orchards and crop pathways.
