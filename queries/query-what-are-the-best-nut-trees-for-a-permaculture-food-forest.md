## What Are the Best Nut Trees for a Permaculture Food Forest

## Short Answer

The best nut trees for a permaculture food forest include chestnuts, hazelnuts, pecans, walnuts, and almonds, chosen for their nutritional density, long-term productivity, and compatibility with companion plantings.

## Why Nut Trees Are Essential in Food Forests

Nut trees form the upper canopy layer of a mature food forest and provide some of the highest calorie yields per acre of any perennial crop. Unlike annual vegetables, nut trees produce food for decades with minimal annual maintenance once established.

## Chestnuts

Chestnuts are often called the bread tree because their nuts can be dried and ground into flour, making them a carbohydrate-rich staple food.

They thrive in well-drained, slightly acidic soils and can produce 50 to 100 pounds of nuts per tree at maturity.

Chinese chestnuts and chestnut hybrids are preferred over American chestnuts in most areas due to their blight resistance.

Chestnut trees grow 30 to 60 feet tall and can live for several centuries, making them a long-term investment in food security.

## Hazelnuts

Hazelnuts are one of the most versatile nut trees for permaculture systems because they can serve multiple functions simultaneously.

They work well as hedgerow plants, windbreaks, and understory shrubs beneath taller canopy trees.

Most hazelnut varieties reach only 10 to 15 feet in height, making them manageable for small properties.
