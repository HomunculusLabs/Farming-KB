# What Is the Strongest Psilocybin Mushroom Species?

## Short Answer
[[psilocybe-azurescens]] is the strongest known psilocybin mushroom, containing
up to approximately 1.8% total tryptamines by dry weight. It is followed by
[[psilocybe-cyanescens]] at roughly 1.2%, [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] and
[[psilocybe-bohemica]] at approximately 1% each, [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]]
at roughly 0.9%, and [[psilocybe-cubensis]] at approximately 0.6% average.
Potency varies significantly based on genetics, growing conditions, and harvest
timing for all species discussed here.

## Potency Rankings by Species

[[psilocybe-azurescens]], commonly called "Flying Saucers" or "Azzies," is
widely regarded as the most potent psilocybin mushroom in the world. Originally
discovered along the coast of Oregon by mycologist Paul Stamets, it contains
the highest naturally occurring concentrations of psilocybin and psilocin,
with total tryptamine content reaching up to 1.8% by dry weight in the most
potent specimens. Azurescens is a dune grassland species that grows in
sandy soils near coastal evergreen forests in the Pacific Northwest of North
America. It is notoriously difficult to cultivate indoors, which limits its
availability compared to other more easily grown species.

[[psilocybe-cyanescens]], known as "Wavy Caps" for the distinctive wavy margin
of its mature cap, ranks second in potency at approximately 1.2% total
tryptamines. Originally from the Pacific Northwest, it has become a widespread
species in temperate regions worldwide, often found in wood chip mulch in
urban parks and gardens. Cyanescens is somewhat easier to cultivate than
azurescens and is considered one of the most adaptable urban psilocybin
mushrooms, fruiting readily on enriched wood chip beds in many climates.

[[psilocybe-semilanceata]], the "Liberty Cap," is the most widespread naturally
occurring psilocybin mushroom in the world, found across temperate regions of
Europe, North America, and Asia. Despite its small size, it consistently
delivers approximately 1% total tryptamines, making it moderately potent.
Liberty Caps are pasture mushrooms that grow in nutrient-rich grasslands,
particularly those grazed by sheep and cattle. They are easily identified by
their conical cap shape and prominent nipple-like umbo on the cap apex.

## Additional Potent Species

[[psilocybe-bohemica]], found in Central Europe, matches Liberty Caps in
potency at approximately 1% total tryptamines. It grows in deciduous and
coniferous forests on woody debris and is particularly common in the Czech
Republic and surrounding countries. [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]], native to
Australia and New Zealand, contains approximately 0.9% total tryptamines and
is one of the most commonly foraged psilocybin mushrooms in the Southern
Hemisphere. It fruits prolifically on wood chips and decaying hardwood in
autumn, particularly in suburban gardens and parkland settings.

[[psilocybe-cubensis]] is by far the most widely cultivated and consumed
psilocybin mushroom globally, yet it has one of the lowest potencies among
psychoactive Psilocybe species at approximately 0.6% average total
tryptamines. However, certain cultivated strains of cubensis have been
selected for higher potency, with some varieties reaching 1% or slightly
above. [[psilocybe-mexicana]], the species used in the original psychedelic
research by Albert Hofmann and R. Gordon Wasson, has relatively low potency
but significant historical and anthropological importance as a traditional
sacred mushroom of Mesoamerican indigenous cultures.

## Factors Affecting Potency

Multiple variables influence the final tryptamine content of any psilocybin
mushroom specimen. Genetic strain is a primary factor, as different genetic
lineages within the same species can produce significantly different potency
levels. Growing conditions including substrate composition, temperature during
fruiting, light exposure, and humidity all affect the biosynthesis of
psilocybin and psilocin. Mushrooms grown on nutrient-rich substrates with
optimal environmental conditions tend to produce higher concentrations of
active compounds than those grown in suboptimal conditions.

Harvest timing is critical: psilocybin mushrooms are most potent just before
the veil tears and the cap begins to open. As the cap expands and spores
mature, the mushroom metabolizes some of its tryptamine content, reducing
potency. Drying method also matters significantly. Heat drying above 100
degrees Fahrenheit can degrade psilocin (the more potent but less stable
compound) into less active byproducts. Freeze-drying or air-drying at room
temperature in a dark, well-ventilated space preserves maximum potency.

## Key Points
- [[psilocybe-azurescens]] is the most potent species at up to roughly 1.8%
  total tryptamines, followed by [[psilocybe-cyanescens]] at roughly 1.2%
- [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] (Liberty Caps) and [[psilocybe-bohemica]] both
  contain approximately 1% total tryptamines by dry weight
- [[psilocybe-cubensis]] is the most widely cultivated species but has lower
  average potency at roughly 0.6%, though selected strains may reach higher
- Potency varies with genetics, substrate, growing conditions, harvest timing
  (pre-veil tear is optimal), and drying method
- [[psilocybe-mexicana]] has historical importance as the original species
  studied by Hofmann and Wasson in Mesoamerican ceremonial contexts
