# How to Make a DIY Still Air Box for Mushroom Cultivation

## Short Answer
A still air box (SAB) is a simple enclosed workspace that blocks airflow to create a
contaminant-free environment for sterile mushroom cultivation tasks like agar work, spore
germination, and grain inoculation. You can build an effective SAB from a large clear
plastic tote box with two arm holes cut into it. Despite its simplicity, a properly used
SAB can achieve contamination rates comparable to expensive laminar flow hoods.

## Detailed Explanation

The still air box is the most accessible piece of sterile equipment available to
mushroom cultivators, and for many home growers, it is the only piece of specialized
equipment needed to produce clean cultures. The principle is straightforward: by
enclosing your workspace and eliminating air currents, you prevent airborne contaminants
like mold spores and bacteria from settling on your sterile media. While a flow hood
provides positive pressure with filtered air, a SAB relies on the simple fact that still
air does not carry contaminants. When combined with proper technique, the results can be
excellent. For growers considering their full lab setup, the SAB is often the starting
point discussed in [[contaminant-identification-and-prevention]].
