### Best Nutrients for Foliar Application

Not all nutrients are equally effective when applied as foliar sprays. Nitrogen in the
form of amino acids or urea is readily absorbed through leaves, making fish hydrolysate
or diluted liquid kelp excellent choices for [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]], foliar feeding can be used every 1 to 2
weeks to support vigorous growth. Nitrogen-rich sprays are most appropriate during this
phase, as the plant is actively building leaf tissue and structural biomass. Some
growers also use foliar applications of silica during vegetative growth to strengthen
cell walls and improve the plant's resistance to pests and environmental stress. Kelp
extracts are another excellent choice during vegetative growth because they contain
natural plant hormones like cytokinins and auxins that promote lateral branching and
overall vigor.

### Flowering Stage Considerations

Foliar feeding during the flowering stage requires more caution. Many growers stop foliar
feeding entirely after the third or fourth week of flowering to prevent moisture from
promoting bud rot (botrytis). If you do continue foliar applications into early
flowering, keep the spray away from the buds themselves and focus on the larger fan
leaves. Phosphorus and potassium foliar sprays can support flower development during
the early flowering transition, but once buds are forming densely, the risk of mold
outweighs the benefits for most growers.

### Avoiding Common Pitfalls

The most common mistake with foliar feeding is over-application. More is not better;
nutrient burn on leaf tissue occurs quickly with foliar sprays because there is no soil
buffer to moderate concentration. Always start with a very dilute solution and increase
gradually. Another common error is spraying during the wrong time of day. Midday sprays
under intense light will almost always cause damage. Finally, be aware that foliar
feeding addresses symptoms but does not fix underlying root-zone problems. If a plant

farming approach. Compost tea foliar sprays deliver beneficial microbes directly to the
leaf surface, where they can colonize the phyllosphere and help prevent pathogen
establishment. Aloe vera gel contains saponins that act as natural surfactants while
also providing salicylic acid, which triggers the plant's immune response. Kelp
extracts provide a broad spectrum of micronutrients along with natural growth hormones.
Together, these inputs create a comprehensive organic foliar program that supports plant
