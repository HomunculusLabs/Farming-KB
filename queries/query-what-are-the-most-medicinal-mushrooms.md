# What Are the Most Medicinal Mushrooms and What Do They Do?

## Short Answer

The most well-researched medicinal mushrooms include Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum),
Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor), Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus), Shiitake
(Lentinula edodes), Maitake (Grifola frondosa), and Cordyceps (Cordyceps
militaris). Each species offers distinct bioactive compounds that support
immune function, cognitive health, metabolic balance, and overall vitality,
backed by varying levels of clinical and preclinical evidence.

## Detailed Explanation

Medicinal mushrooms have been used for centuries in traditional medicine systems
across Asia, Europe, and the Americas. Modern research has begun to validate
many of these traditional uses, identifying specific polysaccharides,
triterpenoids, and other bioactive molecules responsible for their therapeutic
effects. The field of [[medicinal-mushrooms]] encompasses dozens of species, but
a relatively small number have accumulated substantial scientific evidence
supporting their health benefits.

One of the most extensively studied medicinal mushrooms is the Turkey Tail
mushroom, scientifically known as [[trametes-versicolor]]. This bracket fungus
is rich in polysaccharopeptides, particularly PSK (polysaccharide-K) and PSP
(polysaccharopeptide), which have demonstrated significant immune-modulating and
anti-tumor properties. Research compiled under
[[turkey-tail-mushroom-cancer-research]] shows that PSK is approved as an
adjuvant cancer therapy in Japan, where it is prescribed alongside standard
treatments to improve survival rates in gastric and colorectal cancers. The
[[turkey-tail-research-psk]] page details how PSK works by activating natural
killer cells and enhancing dendritic cell function, providing a mechanistic
basis for its clinical effects.

A central mechanism shared by many medicinal mushrooms is their content of
[[beta-glucans]], which are polysaccharides found in fungal cell walls.
Beta-glucans are recognized by immune cell receptors, a process described on the
[[beta-glucan-receptor-binding]] page, triggering cascades that enhance innate
and adaptive immunity. These compounds activate macrophages, stimulate cytokine
production, and promote T-cell proliferation, making them among the most
important immunomodulatory agents found in nature. Different mushroom species
contain structurally distinct beta-glucans, which may explain why some mushrooms
appear more effective for certain conditions than others.

Reishi, or [[ganoderma-lucidum]], is often called the "mushroom of immortality"
in Traditional Chinese Medicine. It contains triterpenoids (ganoderic acids) and
polysaccharides that contribute to its anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, and
immune-enhancing properties. Clinical studies suggest Reishi may help modulate
immune responses in autoimmune conditions, reduce fatigue, and improve sleep
quality. Its adaptogenic qualities make it one of the most popular medicinal
mushrooms in supplement form worldwide. Reishi extracts have also shown
cardiovascular benefits, including the ability to lower blood pressure and
reduce LDL cholesterol in animal models.

Lion's Mane, or [[hericium-erinaceus]], has gained significant attention for its
neuroprotective effects. It contains hericenones and erinacines, compounds that
stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF) synthesis in the brain. Research indicates
potential benefits for cognitive function, memory, and mood, with emerging
evidence supporting its use in mild cognitive impairment and neurodegenerative
conditions. Unlike most other medicinal mushrooms, Lion's Mane's primary
reputation centers on brain health rather than immune support. Preliminary
clinical trials in Japan have reported improvements in cognitive function among
older adults taking Lion's Mane supplements for several months.

Shiitake ([[lentinula-edodes]]) is both a culinary staple and a potent medicinal
fungus. It is the source of lentinan, a beta-glucan with well-documented
immunostimulatory properties. Lentinan has been used as an adjuvant therapy in
cancer treatment, particularly in Japan. Shiitake also contains eritadenine,
which may help lower cholesterol levels, and various compounds with antimicrobial
activity. Its dual role as food and medicine makes it one of the most accessible
medicinal mushrooms for regular consumption. Regular dietary intake of Shiitake
has been associated with improved immune markers in human trials.

Maitake, or [[grifola-frondosa]], known as the "dancing mushroom," is
recognized for its immune-enhancing and metabolic benefits. The D-fraction
extracted from Maitake has shown promise in stimulating immune cell activity and
may have anti-tumor effects. Some studies also suggest Maitake can help regulate
blood sugar levels and improve insulin resistance, making it relevant for
metabolic health applications. Animal studies have demonstrated that Maitake
extracts can reduce blood glucose levels and improve glucose tolerance, pointing
to potential applications in diabetes management.

[[cordyceps-militaris]] represents a fascinating category of medicinal fungi that
parasitize insects. Cordyceps is valued for its energy-enhancing, anti-fatigue,
and performance-boosting properties. It contains cordycepin (3'-deoxyadenosine),
a compound with anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and potential anti-cancer
activities. Athletes commonly use Cordyceps supplements to improve oxygen
utilization and endurance. The militaris species has become increasingly
important as an alternative to the wild-harvested Cordyceps sinensis, which is
rare and expensive. Cultivated Cordyceps militaris can produce higher cordycepin
yields than its wild counterpart, making it a more sustainable option.

Beyond immune and cognitive benefits, many medicinal mushrooms exhibit
[[antiviral-properties-of-mushrooms]], including activity against influenza,
herpes, HIV, and hepatitis viruses. Compounds such as lentinan from Shiitake,
PSK from Turkey Tail, and cordycepin from Cordyceps have demonstrated direct
antiviral effects in laboratory studies, alongside their immune-boosting actions
that help the body resist infections more effectively. Some mushroom extracts
have shown the ability to inhibit viral replication by interfering with viral
attachment and entry into host cells.

The compound [[ahcc-active-hexose-correlated-compound]] is a proprietary extract
derived from Shiitake mycelia that has been the subject of considerable clinical
research. AHCC has been studied for its immune-enhancing effects, including
potential benefits for cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy, liver health, and
infection prevention. It represents an important bridge between traditional
mushroom use and modern nutraceutical development. AHCC has been shown in
clinical trials to increase dendritic cell counts and improve immune response
following vaccination.

When selecting a medicinal mushroom supplement, several factors should be
considered: the species and part of the mushroom used (fruiting body vs.
mycelium), the extraction method (hot water for polysaccharides, alcohol for
triterpenoids), standardization to known bioactive compounds, and third-party
testing for purity and potency. Whole mushroom extracts that include both
fruiting body and mycelium may offer the broadest spectrum of bioactive
compounds, though this remains an active area of research and debate within the
mycological community.

## Key Points

- Turkey Tail ([[turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor]]) is the most clinically
  validated medicinal mushroom for cancer support, with PSK approved as a
  prescription adjuvant in Japan.
- [[beta-glucans]] are the primary immunomodulatory compounds found across most
  medicinal mushroom species, activating immune cells through specific receptor
  interactions.
- [[hericium-erinaceus]] (Lion's Mane) is uniquely valued for cognitive and
  neurological benefits due to its nerve growth factor-stimulating compounds.
- [[ganoderma-lucidum]] (Reishi) offers broad-spectrum benefits including immune
  modulation, anti-inflammatory effects, and adaptogenic support for stress and
  sleep.
- [[cordyceps-militaris]] provides energy enhancement, anti-fatigue benefits, and
  antiviral activity through its bioactive compound cordycepin.
