# How to Make Agar Plates for Mushroom Cultivation?

## Short Answer
Making agar plates involves preparing a nutrient-rich agar solution (typically malt
extract agar or potato dextrose agar), sterilizing it via pressure cooking at 15 PSI
for 30-45 minutes, and pouring it into sterile petri dishes inside a still-air box
or laminar flow hood. Proper [[contaminant-identification-and-prevention]]
techniques throughout the process are essential for clean, usable plates that
support healthy mycelial growth.

## Detailed Explanation

Agar plates are the foundation of sterile mushroom cultivation work. They allow
growers to isolate clean mycelium from spores or tissue samples, expand cultures,
and store genetics long-term. The process begins with selecting and preparing your
agar medium. Malt Extract Agar (MEA) is the most popular choice for beginners and
experienced cultivators alike, consisting of water, agar powder (a gelatinous
substance derived from red algae), and malt extract powder as the nutrient source.
A standard MEA recipe calls for 10 grams of malt extract, 10 grams of agar powder,
and 500 milliliters of water per batch, which yields roughly 15-20 standard 90mm
petri dishes.

Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA) is another common medium, made by boiling sliced
potatoes in water, straining the liquid, then adding agar and dextrose. Some
cultivators enrich their agar with supplements like yeast extract, peptone, or
small amounts of grain water to boost mycelial growth rates. Regardless of the
recipe, the key is to dissolve the agar powder completely by bringing the mixture
to a gentle boil while stirring continuously. Agar powder will not dissolve in cold
water and must reach approximately 85-90°C to fully hydrate.

Once the agar is fully dissolved, it needs to be sterilized before pouring. This
is typically done using a pressure cooker or autoclave, following standard
[[jar-preparation-and-sterilization]] procedures. The agar solution is poured into
heat-resistant containers such as mason jars, media bottles, or Erlenmeyer flasks
fitted with breathable filters. The containers should be loosely capped or covered
with foil to allow steam penetration while preventing contamination during the
sterilization cycle. Sterilization at 15 PSI for 30-45 minutes is sufficient to
kill all viable organisms, including bacterial endospores that can survive boiling
temperatures alone.

After the sterilization cycle completes, the agar solution must cool to
approximately 45-50°C before pouring. If poured while too hot, condensation will
form heavily on the lids of the petri dishes, which can promote bacterial
contamination. If cooled too far below 40°C, the agar will begin to solidify in the
bottle and become impossible to pour evenly. The ideal pouring environment is a
[[query-how-to-set-up-a-mushroom-grow-lab-or-clean-room]] or at minimum a
still-air box that has been wiped down with 70% isopropyl alcohol. Laminar flow
hoods provide the highest success rate by directing HEPA-filtered air across the
work surface.

When pouring, lift the lid of each petri dish just enough to introduce the agar
stream, filling each plate to roughly one-third or one-half full (about 15-20
milliliters for a standard 90mm plate). Work quickly but smoothly, flaming the lip
of your pouring vessel between every few plates if possible. After pouring, gently
swirl each plate in a circular motion on the work surface to distribute the agar
evenly across the bottom. Leave the plates undisturbed on a level surface with lids
slightly cracked to allow residual steam to escape, then close the lids completely
once the agar has fully solidified, which typically takes 30-60 minutes at room
temperature.

Storage of poured agar plates is an important but often overlooked step. Freshly
poured plates should be stored upside-down (lid down) at room temperature for 24-48
hours before use. This incubation period, known as a contamination test, allows you
to identify any plates that were compromised during the pouring process before you
inoculate them with valuable genetic material. Clean plates can be stored in
zip-lock bags at room temperature for several weeks, or in the refrigerator for up
to several months. Cold-stored plates should be allowed to reach room temperature
before inoculation to prevent condensation from forming on the agar surface.

Agar plates serve as the starting point for many downstream cultivation techniques.
Once clean mycelium is established on agar, it can be used to inoculate
[[mushroom-liquid-culture-techniques]] for rapid expansion, transferred to grain
spawn via [[agar-to-grain-wedge-transfer-technique]], or used for strain isolation
and sectoring work. Mastering agar work is perhaps the single most important skill
that separates successful cultivators from those who struggle with recurring
contamination issues. Understanding
[[query-how-to-prevent-and-troubleshoot-mushroom-contamination]] at every stage of
the agar process dramatically increases your success rate over time.

Growers choosing between starting from spores or established cultures should
consider the tradeoffs outlined in [[query-spore-syringes-vs-liquid-culture]].
Spore syringes will always require agar work to isolate a single genetic individual,
while liquid culture can sometimes be inoculated directly onto agar for cleaning or
directly into grain jars if the source is trusted. Regardless of the starting
material, agar work remains the gold standard for verifying culture cleanliness and
vitality before committing to larger, more resource-intensive cultivation projects.

## Key Points
- MEA (malt extract agar) at 2% concentration is the standard beginner-friendly
  recipe for mushroom agar plates
- Sterilize agar solution at 15 PSI for 30-45 minutes in a pressure cooker for
  complete sterilization
- Pour plates at 45-50°C inside a still-air box or laminar flow hood to minimize
  contamination risk
- Store poured plates upside-down and incubate for 24-48 hours as a contamination
  test before inoculation
- Agar plates are the gateway to [[mushroom-liquid-culture-techniques]] and
  reliable grain spawn production
