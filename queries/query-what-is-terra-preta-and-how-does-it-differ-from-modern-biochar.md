## What Is Terra Preta and How Does It Differ from Modern Biochar?

## Short Answer

Terra preta (Portuguese for "black soil") is a class of remarkably fertile anthropogenic soils found across the Amazon Basin.

These dark, carbon-rich soils were created by indigenous peoples over centuries through the intentional addition of charcoal, organic waste, bone, and pottery fragments.

Hugelkultur relies on decomposing wood to retain moisture and release nutrients over many years.

Terra preta relies on stable charcoal to create a persistent carbon matrix that supports microbial life.

Both methods improve water retention and soil structure, but they operate through fundamentally different mechanisms.

In practice, the two approaches can be combined — burying wood beneath a biochar-amended layer creates a synergistic soil-building system.
