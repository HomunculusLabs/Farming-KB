# What Is Trap Cropping and How Does It Work?
Trap cropping is a targeted [[cannabis-pest-management-guide]] strategy in which specific
plants are deliberately grown to lure insect pests away from the main
cash or food crops. These sacrificial decoy plants concentrate pest
populations in a defined area where they can be monitored, managed, or
destroyed before reaching the crops you actually want to harvest.
Rather than fighting pests across the entire growing area, trap cropping
lets you control them in one concentrated zone. This makes pest
management more efficient, more affordable, and more environmentally
responsible. The technique pairs naturally with broader

- **Early detection**: Trap crops act as biological sentinels, revealing

- **Reduced pesticide reliance**: By concentrating pests, trap crops
  allow targeted spot treatments rather than broadcast applications.
- **Economic threshold data**: Monitoring trap crops provides data for
  informed spray-or-wait decisions based on established thresholds.
- **Ecosystem preservation**: Trap crops maintain habitat complexity and
  floral diversity, supporting pollinators and beneficial predatory
  insects throughout the season.

## Key Takeaways
- Trap cropping uses sacrificial plants to divert pests away from main
  crops, concentrating them in manageable zones.
- The push-pull strategy combines repellent intercrops with attractive
  perimeter trap plants for maximum directional [[comparison-pheromone-based-pest-control-vs-beneficial-insect-biocontrol]].
- Proven pairings include nasturtiums for aphids, marigolds for
  nematodes, blue Hubbard squash for squash bugs, and radishes for flea
  beetles.
- **Perimeter planting suits edge-migrating pests, while intercropping
  provides uniform interior protection.
- Combining trap crops with biological controls like predatory insects
  and entomopathogenic fungi dramatically amplifies effectiveness.
- Timely destruction of infested trap crops prevents pest spillover onto
  the main crop.
