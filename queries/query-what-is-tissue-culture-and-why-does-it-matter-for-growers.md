# What Is Tissue Culture and Why Does It Matter for Growers

## Short Answer
Tissue culture is a laboratory propagation technique that grows new plants
from tiny tissue samples in sterile nutrient medium, producing hundreds or
thousands of genetically identical copies from a single parent. For growers,
it offers disease-free plant material, rapid multiplication of elite
genetics, long-term genetic preservation, and the ability to rejuvenate old
or stressed cultivars that no longer perform well through conventional
cuttings.

## Detailed Explanation
Tissue culture, also called micropropagation, is the practice of growing
plant cells, tissues, or organs in an artificial sterile environment on a
nutrient-rich gel medium. The technique has been used commercially for
decades in the ornamental, fruit, and forestry industries but is
increasingly accessible to cannabis cultivators, rare plant collectors, and
serious home growers who want to preserve and multiply their best genetics
with absolute consistency.

Rooting and acclimation are the final and often most challenging steps.
Plantlets grown in the high-humidity, low-light environment of a culture
vessel lack the protective cuticle and functional stomata needed for
survival in ambient conditions. They must be gradually hardened off by
progressively exposing them to lower humidity and higher light over one to
two weeks. Many growers experience significant losses during this
transition, making proper acclimation protocols critical for realizing the
full benefits of tissue culture work.
