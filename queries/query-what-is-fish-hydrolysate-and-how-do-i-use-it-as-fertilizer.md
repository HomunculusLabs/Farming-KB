# What Is Fish Hydrolysate and How Do I Use It as Fertilizer?

## Short Answer
Fish hydrolysate is a liquid organic fertilizer made by enzymatically
breaking down whole fish or fish byproducts into a nutrient-rich liquid.
Unlike fish emulsion, hydrolysate is produced using cold processing that
preserves amino acids, proteins, and beneficial oils. It provides a
balanced source of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and micronutrients
while feeding the [[soil-food-web]] with biologically active compounds.

## Detailed Explanation

Fish hydrolysate represents one of the oldest and most effective organic
fertilizers available to gardeners. Made by processing whole fish —
typically bycatch or fish waste from processing facilities — through
enzymatic digestion rather than heat, hydrolysate retains the full
spectrum of nutrients and bioactive compounds found in the raw material.
This cold-process approach distinguishes it from fish emulsion, which is
made using heat and chemical extraction methods that destroy many of the
beneficial proteins and oils. The result is a product that functions as
both a direct plant fertilizer and a powerful biological stimulant for
soil organisms.

The nutrient profile of fish hydrolysate varies by product but typically
provides an NPK analysis in the range of 2-5-2 to 5-1-1, along with
significant amounts of calcium, magnesium, sulfur, and trace minerals.
More importantly than its macronutrient numbers, hydrolysate delivers a
rich supply of amino acids, fatty acids, vitamins, and enzymes that feed
beneficial soil microorganisms. These compounds are the building blocks
that the [[soil-food-web]] uses to build humus, cycle nutrients, and
suppress pathogens. In this way, fish hydrolysate works more like a
pre-digested food source for soil biology than a conventional fertilizer.

The concept of using fish-based fertilizers appears in several
traditional farming systems around the world. In Korean Natural Farming,
[[knf-fish-amino-acid]] is a preparation made by fermenting fish waste
with brown sugar, which extracts amino acids and minerals into a liquid
that can be diluted and applied as a foliar spray or soil drench.
Similarly, the Jadam farming system incorporates animal byproducts into
its [[jadam-liquid-fertilizer]] recipes. While the specific preparation methods
differ, the underlying principle is the same: fish provides a
concentrated, biologically active source of nutrients that supports both
plants and the microbial communities they depend on.

Fish hydrolysate is particularly valuable in living soil systems,
including [[living-soil-cannabis]] cultivation. In these systems, the goal
is to feed the soil biology rather than the plant directly, allowing the
microbial community to break down organic inputs and deliver nutrients to
plant roots in a steady, natural rhythm. Fish hydrolysate is an ideal
input for this approach because its amino acids and proteins are
immediately available to soil bacteria and fungi, which then multiply and
process these compounds into plant-available forms. The [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] includes fish hydrolysate as a recommended input for
maintaining active soil biology throughout the grow cycle.

From a soil science perspective, fish hydrolysate supports the
[[nitrogen-cycle-in-soil]] in several ways. The nitrogen in hydrolysate
is primarily in the form of amino acids and proteins rather than soluble
nitrates. This means it must be mineralized by soil microorganisms before
plants can take it up, which provides a slow-release effect and reduces
the risk of nitrogen leaching or burn. The microbial activity stimulated
by hydrolysate application also increases the rate of organic matter
decomposition and nutrient cycling throughout the soil profile, benefiting
all plants in the area.

The [[soil-microbiology-basics]] of how fish hydrolysate works are
fascinating. When applied to soil, the amino acids and peptides in
hydrolysate serve as an immediate food source for bacteria, which rapidly
multiply and begin processing the organic nitrogen into ammonium.
Nitrifying bacteria then convert ammonium to nitrate, which plant roots
can absorb. Fungi in the soil also benefit from the fatty acids and
complex organic compounds, leading to increased mycorrhizal colonization
and improved nutrient exchange networks. This cascading biological
response is what makes hydrolysate so much more than a simple nutrient
source.

Application rates and methods vary depending on the product concentration
and the crop being grown. As a general guideline, most commercial fish
hydrolysate products are diluted at a rate of 1 to 4 ounces per gallon of
water and applied as a soil drench every 2 to 4 weeks during the growing
season. It can also be used as a foliar spray at half strength, which
provides faster nutrient uptake through leaf tissue. For cannabis plants
in vegetative growth, fish hydrolysate provides an excellent nitrogen
boost without the salt buildup associated with synthetic fertilizers.

One consideration with fish hydrolysate is its odor, which can be quite
strong immediately after application. Products that are fully hydrolyzed
and stabilized tend to have less odor than lower-quality emulsions. Some
gardeners combine hydrolysate with [[compost-tea-aact]] applications,
which helps mask the smell while providing an additional inoculation of
beneficial microorganisms. The combination of fish hydrolysate and
actively aerated compost tea is one of the most powerful organic nutrient
strategies available.

Fish hydrolysate fits naturally into the broader framework of
[[nutrient-cycling-in-soil]] because it represents a way of returning
animal-derived nutrients to the soil in a form that supports biological
activity. In natural ecosystems, fish are a major nutrient pathway —
think of salmon runs that fertilize entire forest ecosystems. By using
fish hydrolysate in our gardens, we are mimicking this natural nutrient
cycling process and closing loops that industrial agriculture typically
breaks.

## Key Points
- Fish hydrolysate is cold-processed, preserving amino acids, proteins,
  and oils that heat-processed fish emulsion lacks
- It feeds the [[soil-food-web]] with biologically active compounds,
  functioning as both fertilizer and microbial stimulant
- The [[nitrogen-cycle-in-soil]] converts protein-bound nitrogen in
  hydrolysate into plant-available forms through microbial mineralization
- Apply as a soil drench at 1 to 4 ounces per gallon every 2 to 4 weeks,
  or as a half-strength foliar spray
- Combine with [[compost-tea-aact]] for maximum biological benefit and
  reduced odor
