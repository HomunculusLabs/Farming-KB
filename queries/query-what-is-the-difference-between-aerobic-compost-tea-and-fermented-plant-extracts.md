# What Is the Difference Between Aerobic Compost Tea and Fermented Plant Extracts

## Short Answer
[[aact-definitions-and-types-ingham|Aerobic compost tea (AACT)]] is
brewed with oxygen using an air pump to multiply beneficial bacteria and
fungi from finished compost, producing a living liquid inoculant that
must be used within hours. [[fermented-plant-extract-fpe|Fermented plant extracts (FPE)]] are made by anaerobically fermenting plant materials
with sugar, producing a stable liquid rich in plant nutrients, enzymes,
and organic acids that can be stored for months. Both improve soil and
plant health but work through fundamentally different biological and
chemical mechanisms.

## Detailed Explanation
These two liquid amendments are frequently confused because they are both
made from organic materials, applied as foliar sprays or soil drenches,
and promoted as natural alternatives to synthetic fertilizers and
pesticides. However, their production methods, biological content, shelf
life, and modes of action are fundamentally different.

[[compost-tea-aact|Aerobic compost tea]] begins with high-quality,
finished compost as the microbial source. The compost is placed in a mesh
bag and suspended in water that has been dechlorinated and aerated with
an air pump or venturi injector. During the brewing period, typically 24
to 36 hours, dissolved oxygen levels must remain above 6 mg/L to support
aerobic microbial growth. The [[aact-brewing-ingredients-ingham|AACT brewing ingredients]] may include supplemental foods like molasses (for
bacteria), kelp (for micronutrients), and humic acids (for fungi), each
feeding different segments of the microbial community. The result is a
liquid teeming with living bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes that
can number in the billions per milliliter. The
[[aact-aeration-oxygen-management-ingham|aeration and oxygen management]]
during brewing is critical because anaerobic conditions produce pathogenic
organisms and phytotoxic compounds.

The [[comparison-fermented-plant-extract-vs-compost-tea|key differences between FPE and compost tea]] become clear when examining the fermentation
process. [[fermented-plant-extract-fpe|Fermented plant extract]] is made
by packing fresh plant material (often fast-growing plants rich in
nutrients and growth compounds) into a container with brown sugar or
molasses at roughly equal weights. The mixture ferments anaerobically for
7 to 14 days, during which sugars are converted to organic acids,
enzymes, and bioactive compounds by naturally occurring microorganisms on
the plant surfaces. Unlike compost tea, the goal is not to multiply
living organisms but to extract and transform the chemical constituents of
the plant material. The resulting liquid is shelf-stable for months and
contains organic acids, amino acids, plant growth regulators, and
chelated minerals in plant-available forms.

From a [[teaming-with-microbes-compost-tea-brewing-guide|compost tea brewing perspective]], quality control is critical. If the tea goes
anaerobic during brewing, it can produce alcohol, ammonia, and hydrogen
sulfide that harm plants. The [[aact-ph-water-quality-ingham|pH and water quality guidelines]] for AACT brewing specify a target pH of 5.5 to 6.5
and the use of clean, dechlorinated water. By contrast,
[[fermented-plant-juice-fpj|fermented plant juice (FPJ)]] from Korean
Natural Farming is more forgiving because the fermentation is
intentionally anaerobic and the end product is stabilized by its low pH.

In terms of application, [[aact-applications-guide-ingham|AACT is primarily a biological inoculant]] that introduces living organisms to
leaf surfaces, soil, or compost. It works best when applied immediately
after brewing while microbial populations are at their peak. FPE, on the
other hand, functions more as a biostimulant and nutrient source. The
organic acids in FPE help solubilize soil minerals, the amino acids
provide direct plant nutrition, and the growth compounds stimulate root
development and stress resistance. FPE can be stored and used over weeks
or months.

The [[comparison-compost-tea-guide-vs-fermented-plant-extract-fpe| compost tea versus FPE comparison]] suggests that both tools are
valuable in a complete biological farming program. Compost tea is
superior for rapid inoculation of leaf surfaces or degraded soils with
living biology. FPE is superior for providing concentrated plant
nutrition and bioactive compounds in a stable, storable form. Many
growers use both: compost tea during the growing season for biological
inoculation and FPE as a year-round nutrient supplement.

## Key Points
- [[compost-tea-aact|Aerobic compost tea]] multiplies living microbes
  from compost using dissolved oxygen and must be used within hours
- [[fermented-plant-extract-fpe|Fermented plant extracts]] are
  anaerobically fermented plant materials rich in organic acids,
  enzymes, and nutrients, stable for months
- Compost tea is primarily a biological inoculant; FPE is primarily
  a nutrient source and biostimulant
- AACT requires careful monitoring of oxygen levels, pH, and brewing
  time to avoid going anaerobic
- Both tools are complementary and many growers use both in their
  fertility programs
