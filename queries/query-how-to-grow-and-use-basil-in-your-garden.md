## How to Grow and Use Basil in Your Garden

## Short Answer

Basil is an annual herb that thrives in warm, sunny conditions with well-drained soil. Regular harvesting encourages bushier growth and prevents flowering. Basil can be grown from seeds or transplants and requires consistent moisture but good drainage. The leaves can be harvested throughout the growing season for culinary use, and basil plants also serve as beneficial companion plants in vegetable gardens.

### Harvesting Techniques

Harvest basil leaves regularly to encourage continuous growth and prevent flowering. Pinch off individual leaves or cut stems just above a pair of leaves. Harvest in the morning when the essential oil content is highest for the best flavor.

For larger harvests, cut stems back by one-third, leaving at least 4-6 leaves on each stem. The plant will quickly regrow from the remaining nodes. Basil can be harvested continuously throughout the growing season until the first frost.

## Key Points

- Basil requires full sun (6-8 hours daily) and well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.5
- Start seeds indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost or use healthy transplants
- Pinch back growing tips to encourage bushier growth and prevent flowering
- Water consistently but avoid wetting foliage to prevent disease
- Harvest regularly for continuous production of tender leaves
- Basil serves as excellent companion plant for tomatoes, peppers, and eggplants
- Preserve basil through freezing, drying, or making pesto and infused oils
