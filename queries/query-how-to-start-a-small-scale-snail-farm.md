# How Do I Start a Small-Scale Snail Farm?

## Quick Answer

Starting a small snail farm (heliciculture) requires five essentials: **breeding stock** of a suitable edible species, a **humidity-controlled enclosure** with escape-proof barriers, **calcium-rich soil substrate**, a **reliable food source** (leafy greens and supplements), and a **purging and processing plan** before sale. The best beginner species is *Cornu aspersum* (petit gris) — it grows fast, tolerates varied conditions, and has strong market demand. A 10 m² starter pen with 100–200 breeding snails costs roughly $200–500 to set up and can produce 20–50 kg of meat in the first year.

## Step-by-Step Setup Guide

### 1. Choose Your Species

For beginners, the choice is straightforward:

- **Best choice**: *Cornu aspersum* (brown garden snail / petit gris). Hardy, fast-growing (6–8 months to harvest), widely legal, strong market. Start with this.
- **Premium option**: *Helix pomatia* (Burgundy snail). Higher price but slower (12–18 months), more temperature-sensitive, protected in some areas. Graduate to this later.
- **Avoid**: *Achatina fulica* (giant African land snail) — illegal in the US, EU, Australia, and many other countries due to invasive potential.

Source breeding stock from an established heliciculture farm, not from the wild. Wild snails carry parasites (especially rat lungworm) and may not be the optimal food-producing strain.

### 2. Build Your First Enclosure

A simple outdoor pen is the best starting point:

**Materials**:
- 4 panels of galvanized metal or untreated wood, 40 cm tall
- 20 cm of the panel buried underground (prevents burrowing escape)
- Outward-facing lip or overhang at the top (prevents climbing escape)
- Shade cloth or mesh lid to exclude birds and rodents
- Loam soil mixed 10% with ground limestone or oyster shell flour

**Dimensions**: 2 m × 5 m (10 m²) for a starter pen. This holds 100–200 snails comfortably.

**Location**: Under a tree, next to a north-facing wall, or under a simple shade structure. Morning sun is fine; avoid afternoon sun. Good airflow but no strong wind.

**Cost estimate**: $150–300 for materials.

### 3. Prepare the Substrate

Mix your soil thoroughly:
- 70% garden loam (sandy loam is ideal)
- 20% compost or well-rotted leaf mold
- 10% ground limestone, oyster shell flour, or crushed eggshells

Aim for 5–10 cm depth. Moisten until the soil forms a weak ball when squeezed. Test pH — target 6.5–7.5. Add more lime if below 6.0.

### 4. Add Shelter and Forage

Snails need daytime hiding spots. Place these inside the pen:
- Terracotta pots turned upside down (with a notch for entry)
- Wooden boards propped on bricks
- PVC pipe sections cut in half
- Branches and bark pieces

Plant or place forage directly in the pen:
- Chicory, dandelion, plantain (deep-rooted, calcium-accumulating)
- Clover or vetch (nitrogen-fixing, protein-rich)
- Lettuce, cabbage, or kale (tender greens for quick feeding)

### 5. Introduce Your Breeding Stock

- Start with 100–200 adult snails (10–20 per m²).
- Release them in the evening when temperatures are cooler.
- Mist the enclosure lightly before release.
- Don't feed for the first 24 hours — let them acclimate.
- Check daily for the first week to ensure they're feeding and active.

### 6. Establish a Daily Routine

**Every evening**:
- Mist the enclosure with clean water (not a flood — a fine spray).
- Scatter fresh food on clean tiles or boards (never directly on soil to prevent contamination).
- Remove yesterday's uneaten food.
- Check for escapees, dead snails, or signs of disease.

**Weekly**:
- Refresh calcium supply in a dedicated dish (cuttlebone, crushed shells).
- Check soil moisture and adjust misting frequency.
- Clean feeding surfaces thoroughly.
- Record mortality, growth observations, and breeding activity.

### 7. Manage Breeding

Breeding begins naturally in spring when temperatures rise and humidity increases. To manage population:

- **Egg collection**: Check moist soil patches for small white egg clusters (2–4 mm diameter). Carefully dig them up and transfer to a nursery tray if you want controlled hatching. Leave them in place for natural recruitment.
- **Nursery tray**: A shallow container with 3–5 cm of moist substrate, kept at 80–90% humidity. Hatchlings stay here for 3–4 weeks before joining the main pen.
- **Density control**: If the pen exceeds 100 snails per m², harvest the largest ones or set up a second pen.

### 8. Harvest and Process

Snails are ready at 8–12 g live weight (about the size of a large marble to a golf ball, depending on species). Harvest process:

1. **Purge**: Place harvested snails in a clean container with a damp cloth (no soil). Feed nothing for 5–7 days. Provide fresh water daily. This empties the digestive tract.
2. **Kill**: Drop into boiling water for 3–5 minutes, or freeze at -20°C for 48 hours (considered more humane).
3. **Shell**: Remove from shells with a small fork or pick.
4. **Clean**: Remove the dark digestive gland (small dark sac near the shell opening). Rinse the foot thoroughly.
5. **Cook or freeze**: Cook immediately in garlic butter and white wine, or vacuum-seal and freeze at -18°C for up to 12 months.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Matters | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| **Wild-caught breeding stock** | Parasites, disease, poor genetics | Buy from a reputable heliculture farm |
| **No calcium supplement** | Thin shells, slow growth, death | Always provide separate calcium source |
| **Overfeeding** | Mold, bacterial growth, ammonia buildup | Feed only what's consumed in 24 hours |
| **Inadequate humidity** | Snails desiccate, go dormant, or die | Mist daily; maintain 75–95% humidity |
| **Direct sunlight exposure** | Overheating, death within hours | Shade the enclosure; morning sun only |
| **Chemical contamination** | Snails absorb toxins through their skin | No pesticides, herbicides, or treated lumber near enclosures |
| **Harvesting too early** | Small, unprofitable snails | Wait until 8+ g live weight |
| **Skipping purging** | Gritty, unpleasant meat; food safety risk | Always purge 5–7 days before processing |
| **No escape prevention** | Snails climb walls and disappear | Bury fencing, add outward lip at top |
