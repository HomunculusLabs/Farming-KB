# How Does JADAM Natural Farming Differ from Korean Natural Farming?

## Short Answer
JADAM and Korean Natural Farming (KNF) both originated in South Korea and share the
philosophy of working with nature rather than against it, but JADAM is a streamlined,
ultra-low-cost simplification created by Youngsang Cho after he mastered KNF. While KNF
relies on a complex system of numerous fermented inputs (IMO, FPJ, FFJ, OHN, WCA, etc.),
JADAM reduces everything to a handful of core preparations centered around Jadam Microorganism
Solution (JMS) and Jadam Natural Pesticide (JNP).

## Detailed Explanation

Korean Natural Farming was developed by Cho Han Kyu (known as Master Cho) beginning in the
1960s, building on traditional Korean agricultural practices and his own decades of
experimentation. The system uses a collection of fermented biological inputs that each
target specific plant needs. The [[cho-natural-farming-inputs-overview]] catalogues the
full range of these preparations, from Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO) collected from
forest soil to Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ), Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ), Oriental Herbal
Nutrients (OHN), Water Soluble Calcium (WCA), and many more. Each input requires specific
collection methods, fermentation vessels, dilution ratios, and application timings, making
KNF a rich but demanding system to learn and practice.

JADAM, which stands for Jayonul Damun (meaning nature-like in Korean), was founded by
Youngsang Cho, who spent seven years mastering KNF under Master Cho before creating his
own simplified system in the 1990s. His core insight was that most of the KNF inputs could
be replaced with a single universal microorganism solution and a simple natural pesticide.
The [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]] captures one of JADAMs foundational ideas: that
microorganisms should be cultured at ambient outdoor temperatures rather than in the
controlled warm conditions KNF often recommends, making the system more accessible and
resilient.

The most significant practical difference is the number of inputs required. A complete
KNF system typically involves learning to prepare and apply a dozen or more distinct
fermented inputs, each with its own collection, fermentation, and application protocol.
The [[complete-knf-input-reference]] documents the full scope of what a KNF practitioner
needs to master. JADAM, by contrast, centers on Jadam Microorganism Solution (JMS), which
is made by fermenting leaf mold and soil with boiled potato water and a small amount of
soybean oil, and Jadam Natural Pesticide (JNP), which uses plant oils as its base. The
[[ultra-low-cost-jadam-microorganism-solution-and-soil-foundation]] page provides the
complete method for making JMS, which replaces almost all of the individual KNF inputs.

Their approaches to pest management also differ substantially. KNF uses a variety of
targeted inputs including OHN (which combines garlic, ginger, licorice, and Angelica
root fermented in alcohol) and specific plant-based pest deterrents. JADAM takes a
broader approach with its [[jadam-all-purpose-pesticide]], which uses canola oil as a
surfactant combined with various botanical extracts in a single versatile spray. JADAM
also developed the [[jadam-canola-oil-surfactant-method]], which uses food-grade canola
oil as a replacement for synthetic surfactants, allowing natural pesticide solutions to
spread and adhere to plant surfaces effectively.

Soil management philosophy also differs between the two systems. KNF generally works
with the soil as found and adds biological inputs to enhance it, while JADAM has a
stronger emphasis on no-till principles and minimal soil disturbance. The
[[jadam-soil-management-no-till-and-weed-control]] page outlines JADAMs approach to
maintaining living soil ecosystems. Both systems share a commitment to avoiding synthetic
fertilizers and pesticides, but JADAM takes this further with its [[jadam-compost-free-approach]], arguing that proper microbial inoculation eliminates the need for composting
entirely.

Cost is another major differentiator. JADAM was specifically designed to be accessible
to farmers with minimal resources. The [[ultra-low-cost-farming-overview]] demonstrates
how JADAM practitioners can produce all necessary inputs using locally available materials
at a fraction of the cost of either conventional agriculture or even KNF. While KNF inputs
are inexpensive compared to commercial fertilizers, the sheer number of them and the
specialized ingredients some require (like brown rice vinegar, specific herb collections,
or sea salt processing) add up.

Both systems share foundational principles: feeding the soil microbiome rather than the
plant directly, using fermented biological inputs, maintaining biodiversity, and avoiding
synthetic chemicals. The [[what-natural-farming-method-should-i-use]] page provides a
decision framework for choosing between these and other natural farming approaches based
on your specific context, scale, and goals.

## Key Points

- KNF uses a dozen or more specialized fermented inputs; JADAM simplifies to JMS and
  JNP [[cho-natural-farming-inputs-overview]]
- JADAM was created by Youngsang Cho after mastering KNF for seven years under Master
  Cho [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]]
- JADAM cultures microorganisms at ambient outdoor temperatures for simplicity and
  resilience [[ultra-low-cost-jadam-microorganism-solution-and-soil-foundation]]
- JADAM uses canola oil as a natural surfactant for pest control instead of multiple
  targeted inputs [[jadam-canola-oil-surfactant-method]]
- JADAM promotes a compost-free approach using microbial inoculation instead
  [[jadam-compost-free-approach]]

For most home gardeners, the choice comes down to complexity versus completeness. KNF
offers a more nuanced toolkit with specific inputs for specific growth stages and plant
needs, making it attractive for growers who enjoy the craft of input preparation and
want precise control over their plant nutrition. JADAM offers radical simplicity that
makes natural farming accessible to anyone regardless of time, budget, or expertise.
The [[ultra-low-cost-farming-overview]] demonstrates how JADAM's streamlined approach
can produce comparable results with a fraction of the effort.
