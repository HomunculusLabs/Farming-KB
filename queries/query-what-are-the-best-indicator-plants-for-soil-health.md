# What Are the Best Indicator Plants for Soil Health

## Short Answer
Indicator plants are wild or volunteer species that reveal specific soil
conditions through their presence or growth pattern. Dandelions indicate
compacted soil, clover suggests nitrogen deficiency, dock points to poor
drainage, nettles thrive in nitrogen-rich soil, and yarrow prefers poor, dry
soils. Learning to read these volunteer plants helps gardeners understand their
soil without lab tests.

## Detailed Explanation
Before modern soil testing became widely available, farmers and gardeners relied
on the plants growing naturally in an area to assess soil conditions. This
practice, sometimes called botanical soil assessment, is based on the principle
that different plant species have distinct preferences for soil pH, nutrient
levels, moisture, texture, and drainage. By observing which volunteer species
appear and thrive in your garden or field, you can infer a great deal about what
is happening beneath the surface. These indicator plants serve as living soil
test results that are available to anyone willing to look closely.

Nitrogen indicators are among the most useful for gardeners.

pH indicators help gardeners understand whether their soil is acidic or
alkaline. Sorrel, wild strawberries, and heather prefer acidic soils with a pH
below 6.0, while chicory, bladder campion, and yarrow favor alkaline soils above
7.0. In areas with extremely acidic soil, you may find sphagnum moss,
blueberries, and cranberries growing naturally. These pH preferences are
