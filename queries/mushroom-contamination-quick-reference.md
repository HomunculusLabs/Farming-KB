# Mushroom Contamination Quick Reference

Quick-lookup table for identifying, preventing, and responding to common mushroom cultivation contaminants. For sterile technique procedures, see [[mushroom-sterile-technique]]. For recovery methods, see [[mushroom-contamination-troubleshooting]].

---

## Contaminant Identification Table

| Contaminant | Appearance | Odor | Cause | Prevention | Treatment | When to Toss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trichoderma** (green mold) | White cottony mycelium turning vivid forest green as spores mature. 3-5 micron spores in sticky clusters | Musty | Too-wet substrate, under-sterilization, airborne spores, peat/humus casing | Proper sterilization (90+ min at 15 PSI), moisture at field capacity, clean casing materials, raise pH above 5.5 | Salt/baking soda on earliest colonies before sporulation. Steam pasteurize at 160F/1hr kills spores. Lower humidity, increase FAE | Immediately if sporulating. Do NOT open indoors. Red pigmy mites spread spores. Tolerates acid conditions |
| **Aspergillus** (green/yellow/black) | Powdery green, yellow, or black colonies. Bulbous capitate conidiophore. Spores in linear chains | Mild | Airborne contamination, poor ventilation, contaminated grain | HEPA filtration, filter-equipped lids, clean workspace, avoid stirring dust | Discard -- spores spread easily through air. Clean area thoroughly with bleach | Immediately. Some species produce aflatoxins. Respiratory hazard |
| **Cobweb mold** (Dactylium) | Fine grayish-white spiderweb-like mycelium draped over substrate or fruits. Wispy and aerial, not dense. Pinkish tinge with age | Mild | High humidity + stagnant air, contaminated casing, insects | Pasteurize casing soil, increase FAE, lower humidity, inspect casing components | Spray with 3% H2O2 -- kills Dactylium on contact. Mushroom mycelium tolerates H2O2 via peroxidase enzymes | If it overwhelms fruits before treatment. Spores killed at 115-122F/30 min |
| **Bacteria** (wet spot / sour rot) | Slimy patches on grain -- pinkish, yellowish, or milky. Matted kernels with wet glossy sheen. Rotten odor | Sour, rotten | Unsterilized grain, contaminated water, poor inoculation technique, substrate too wet | Full sterilization at 15 PSI for 90+ min, proper moisture content, clean inoculation | None viable. Grain jars with wet spot should be discarded carefully | Always discard bacterial grain. Do not open jars indoors |
| **Lipstick mold** (Geotrichum) | Whitish "frosty" mycelium forming balls, aging to pink then red, fading to dull orange. Cylindrical conidia 5-10 x 3-6 microns | Mild | Airborne, soil, old straw, spent compost | Pasteurize thoroughly, compost on concrete surface, use clean casing materials, eliminate spent compost debris | Thorough pasteurization is the primary control. Concrete compost surfaces on commercial grows | If established -- inhibits primordia formation entirely. Mesophilic thermophile, sensitive to pasteurization temps |
| **Yeast** | Creamy, pasty colonies on agar. Cloudy broth in liquid culture. Bubbling/fermentation in grain | Yeasty, fermented | Contaminated inoculant, unsterilized tools, airborne | Flame-sterilize all tools, use clean cultures, proper sterilization times | On agar: transfer clean mycelium sector to fresh plate away from yeast colonies | If grain jars are heavily colonized with yeast. LC should be discarded |
| **Penicillium** (blue-green mold) | Blue-green powdery growth. Conidiophore diverges into multiple spore chains. Appressed granular texture (contrast with Trichoderma's cottony growth) | Mild | Airborne contamination during agar pouring or transfer work | Work in SAB or flow hood, flame-sterilize tools between every transfer, pour plates in sterile conditions | Transfer clean mycelium sector to fresh plate. 2-3 transfers to ensure purity | If contamination is widespread on plate. Common on agar -- accept some loss rate |
| **Pin mold** (Mucor/Rhizopus) | Fast-growing coarse mold erupting vertically. "Pin cushion" appearance with spherical sporangia on tall stalks. White turning dark gray/black | Mild | Excessively wet substrate, contaminated agar | Field capacity moisture, proper sterilization, HEPA filtration | Discard. One of the fastest-growing contaminants -- overtakes plates quickly | Always. Grows faster than most mushroom mycelium |
| **Nematodes** | Tiny worm-like organisms in substrate. Mushrooms appear stunted, slimy, or rotting prematurely | None visible | Contaminated water source, unsterilized substrate, introduced via flies | Use clean water, proper pasteurization/sterilization, control flies and insects | Difficult to treat. Increase pasteurization temperature/time. Clean all equipment | Always. Nematodes spread rapidly and destroy entire crops |
| **Mites** | Microscopic arthropods. Red pigmy mites visible as tiny moving dots. Spores disappearing from plates, mycelium being consumed | None | Introduced on clothing, tools, contaminated substrate, other insects | Clean workspace, shower before lab work, seal cracks, sticky traps near entrances | No reliable treatment for contaminated cultures. Discard and sanitize everything | Always. Mites carry mold spores and bacteria between cultures |

## Prevention Checklist

- Sterilize grain jars 90+ min at 15 PSI; bulk substrates pasteurized 140-160F for 60-90 min
- Work in SAB or flow hood; flame-sterilize tools between every transfer
- Use proper gas-exchange filters (polyfill, micropore tape, filter discs) on all containers
- Substrate at field capacity -- holds shape when squeezed, releases at most 1-2 drops
- Start from verified clean agar; isolate clean sectors before moving to grain
- Increase cleaning in spring/summer (spore load peaks)
- Remove contaminated cultures promptly; wet-mop floors
- Never open sporulating contaminated jars indoors

## When to Salvage vs. Toss

**Toss immediately**: Entire jar/bag contaminated, spores released in confined space, unidentified contaminant spreading rapidly, any edible/medicinal grow with safety doubt.

**Attempt salvage**: Small surface spot on fully colonized substrate, mycelium significantly ahead, cobweb mold (H2O2 responsive), learning/non-food project.

**Rule of thumb**: If more than 25% of substrate surface is contaminated, discard. Create conditions so favorable to mushroom mycelium that competitors cannot establish (Stamets).

## Recovery Methods Summary

| Method | How | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Salt treatment** | Thin layer non-iodized salt on contaminated casing area | Surface casing contamination, delaying mold while mycelium advances |
| **H2O2 spray (3%)** | Mist directly onto cobweb mold | Cobweb mold (Dactylium) -- mushroom mycelium tolerates it |
| **Cut away** | Remove contaminated area + 1-2 inches margin, outside grow space | Small localized spot on fully colonized substrate |
| **Agar sector transfer** | Transfer clean sector far from contaminant to fresh plate, repeat 2-3x | Rescuing genetics from contaminated plate |
| **Baking soda paste** | Alkaline cover on acid-loving molds (Trichoderma) | Earliest stage Trichoderma on casing before sporulation |

## Five Sources of Contamination (Stamets)

1. **Immediate external environment** -- airborne spores, dust, open windows
2. **Culture medium** -- under-sterilized grain, contaminated water, expired agar
3. **Culturing equipment** -- unsterilized tools, dirty jars, contaminated filters
4. **The cultivator** -- skin, breath, clothing, hair carry spores and bacteria
5. **Spores/mycelium themselves** -- wild prints carry environmental contaminants

Understanding which source is causing failures helps you target prevention. Most beginners lose cultures to sources 1, 3, and 4.

## Contamination Rate Benchmarks

| Skill Level | Agar Contamination Rate | Grain Jar Contamination Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner (SAB) | 20-40% | 10-20% | Normal learning curve |
| Intermediate (SAB) | 5-15% | 5-10% | Good technique, clean workspace |
| Advanced (Flow hood) | 1-5% | 1-3% | Consistent sterile workflow |
| Professional | <1% | <1% | Dedicated lab space, HEPA, protocols |

If your contamination rate exceeds these benchmarks, focus on the weakest link in your workflow. For beginners, that is almost always sterile technique during inoculation -- working too slowly, not flame-sterilizing between transfers, or touching non-sterile surfaces.

## Sterilization Quick Reference

| Material | Method | Time/Temp | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grain jars (quart) | Pressure canner, 15 PSI | 90 min | Let cool naturally |
| Grain bags (sawdust) | Pressure canner, 15 PSI | 2-2.5 hours | Larger mass needs more time |
| Bulk substrate (straw) | Hot water pasteurization | 160-170F, 1-2 hours | Does NOT require full sterilization |
| Casing soil | Steam pasteurization | 160F, 1 hour | Kills most competitors, preserves beneficials |
| Agar media | Pressure cooker, 15 PSI | 30-45 min | Do not exceed 250F (sugar caramelizes) |
| Tools (scalpel, loop) | Flame sterilization | Red hot | Cool 5-10 sec before touching tissue |
| Work surfaces | 70% isopropyl alcohol | Wipe and let dry | 70% more effective than 91% |

## Environmental Controls

- **Spring/summer**: Spore counts 10-100x higher. Increase cleaning frequency. Consider fall/winter for first grows.
- **Air filtration**: HEPA room purifiers reduce ambient spore load. MERV 13+ on HVAC intakes.
- **Humidity**: Lab area should be 40-50% RH. High humidity promotes contaminant growth.
- **Antechamber**: If possible, create a two-room setup -- dirty room to dirty room to clean lab.
- **Bleach routine**: Spray 10% bleach solution and wait 15 minutes before every lab session.
- **Clothing**: Dedicated lab clothes. Shower before working. Hair tied back or covered.
