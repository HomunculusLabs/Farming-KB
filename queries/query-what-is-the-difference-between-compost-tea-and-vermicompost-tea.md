### Fungal Populations
Traditional compost, especially when made with woody materials, tends to
support more diverse [[biodiversity-of-fungi-soil-fungal-communities-agriculture]]. If fungal dominance is the goal,
compost tea brewed from fungal-rich compost may outperform vermicompost tea.
Fungi are critical for woody perennials and soils that need improved aggregate
structure. See [[compost-tea-brewing]]. Key considerations include:
- Aeration is critical for both teas to maintain [[aact-dissolved-oxygen-fine-bubble-aeration-ingham]] levels
  above 6 ppm and prevent anaerobic conditions that can produce toxins.
- Food sources such as molasses, fish hydrolysate, and kelp are added to
  feed microbial multiplication during the brew cycle.
- Standard brew times range from 24 to 36 hours depending on the recipe
  and target biology.
- Water quality matters: use dechlorinated water to avoid killing beneficial
  microbes before brewing begins.
- Compost tea recipes targeting fungi require longer brew times and specific
  food sources compared to bacterial-dominant recipes.
## Application Rates
Both teas are typically applied as soil drenches or foliar sprays. The
following guidelines apply to most situations:
- Soil drench: Apply at 10 to 20 gallons per acre, diluted if needed for
  sensitive plants or young seedlings.
- Foliar spray: Apply undiluted or slightly diluted, ensuring thorough
  coverage on leaf surfaces for direct microbial benefits.
- Frequency: Every 2 to 4 weeks during the growing season for optimal
  results, or more frequently for stressed plants.
- Apply in the early morning or late evening to minimize UV damage to
  [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]] on leaf surfaces.
## When to Use Compost Tea
- When [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungal-diversity-patterns-distribution]] is needed for woody perennials, orchards, or trees.
- When the goal is broad-spectrum microbial inoculation from a diverse
  compost source.
- When treating soil that needs a balanced [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-bacterial-and-fungal-dominant-soil]] community
  for long-term soil health.
- When working with established perennial systems that benefit from fungal
  associations with plant roots.
## When to Use Vermicompost Tea
- When higher plant-available nutrient levels are desired for fast-growing
  annuals and vegetables.
- When targeting bacterial-dominant inoculation for vegetable gardens or
  row crops.
- When humic acid content is a priority for soil structure improvement.
- When working with compacted or degraded soils that benefit from the humic
  substances found abundantly in worm castings.
- When plant growth hormones from vermicompost can give seedlings a
  germination and establishment advantage.
## Summary
While both [[vermicompost-tea]] are aerobic brewed
extracts, they serve complementary roles in a soil health management program.
Compost tea excels where fungal diversity and broad-spectrum biology are
needed, making it ideal for perennials and orchard systems. Vermicompost tea
delivers stronger bacterial populations, more readily available nutrients, and
valuable plant growth hormones. Choosing between them depends on the crop type,
current soil condition, and the specific biological targets of the grower.
Many integrated programs alternate between the two teas to provide the widest
range of benefits throughout the growing season.
