# What Is Light Deprivation Cannabis Growing and How Does It Work?

## Short Answer
Light deprivation, commonly called "light dep," is a technique where
outdoor cannabis growers artificially control the photoperiod by covering
plants to create 12 hours of uninterrupted darkness, triggering early
flowering. This allows outdoor growers to harvest months before natural
seasonal light changes would normally induce flowering, enabling multiple
harvests per season and avoiding fall weather problems like rain and mold.
The technique requires a reliable covering system and strict daily timing
discipline to be successful.

## Detailed Explanation

Cannabis is a photoperiodic plant, meaning its transition from
[[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] to
[[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]] is triggered by changes in the
daily light cycle rather than age alone. In nature, flowering begins when
nights lengthen to approximately 12 hours as summer transitions to autumn.
Light deprivation exploits this biological trigger by artificially
creating long nights during the middle of summer, tricking plants into
flowering weeks or months earlier than they would naturally. This
technique has revolutionized outdoor and greenhouse cannabis cultivation
by giving growers control over harvest timing that was previously only
possible indoors.

The mechanics of light dep growing are straightforward but require daily
commitment and precision. Plants are grown outdoors during the vegetative
stage under the natural long days of summer. When the grower decides to
trigger flowering, typically in late June through July depending on
latitude, the plants are covered with a light-proof tarp or structure
every evening to ensure they receive exactly 12 hours (or sometimes 13
hours) of uninterrupted darkness. The cover must be completely opaque and
seal tightly enough that no light leaks in, as even brief light
interruptions during the dark period can stress plants, cause
[[cannabis-hermaphrodite-prevention-and-management]], or
delay flowering. The covering and uncovering must happen at the same time
each day, ideally using an automated timer system for reliability.

The primary advantage of light dep is harvest timing. By triggering
flowering in midsummer, plants can be harvested in August or early
September rather than late October or November. This early harvest avoids
many of the problems that plague late-season outdoor grows, including fall
rains that promote [[cervantes-gray-mold-botrytis-prevention]], early frosts that can damage or kill plants before harvest, and
the declining light intensity that can reduce [[cannabis-glandular-trichome-types-resin-production-clarke]]
production in the final weeks. In favorable climates, the early harvest
opens the possibility of running a second crop in the same season by
replanting after harvest and allowing the natural shortening days to
carry the second crop through flowering to a late autumn harvest.

There are several practical approaches to building a light dep system.
The simplest method is a manual tarp system where growers pull a blackout
cover over a hoop house or frame each evening and remove it each morning.
Automated systems use motors and timers to roll tarps on and off
greenhouse structures, which is essential for larger operations where
manual covering becomes impractical. The structure must be tall enough to
accommodate plants through their full flowering stretch, which can double
the vegetative height for many cannabis-sativa|sativa varieties.
Proper ventilation during the covered dark period is also critical; plants
respire during the night and generate humidity that must be managed to
prevent mold and maintain healthy growing conditions.

Light dep growing integrates well with many other cultivation techniques
that maximize yield and quality. Growers can combine light dep with
[[low-stress-training]] or supercropping|supercropping to control
plant height and maximize light exposure during the vegetative phase.
Companion planting around the light dep structure can help with pest
management and provide visual screening for discreet outdoor grows. The
technique works with both cannabis-indica|indica and sativa dominant
strains, though sativa varieties with longer flowering times may not
finish before natural fall conditions arrive if started too late in the
season. Strain selection should account for the actual flowering time
available between the light dep trigger date and expected first frost.

The main challenges are the daily commitment to covering and uncovering,
the need for a reliable structure, and the risk of catastrophic failure
if the cover is left on too long or tears during a storm. Even a single
missed covering can disrupt the photoperiod enough to stress plants and
potentially trigger [[cannabis-hermaphrodite-prevention-and-management]]. For these reasons, automated systems with backup power and
alarm notifications are strongly recommended for any grow beyond a few
plants. Despite the additional infrastructure, light dep has become one
of the most popular techniques for commercial outdoor and greenhouse
cannabis cultivators who need to control their harvest schedule.

## Key Points
- Light dep uses artificial 12-hour darkness to trigger
  [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]] in midsummer for early harvests
- Complete light-proofing and consistent daily timing are critical to
  prevent stress and [[cannabis-hermaphrodite-prevention-and-management]]
- Early harvest avoids fall rain, mold, and frost problems that threaten
  late-season outdoor crops
- Automated tarp systems on hoop houses or greenhouses are the most
  practical approach at commercial scale
- In suitable climates, light dep enables two outdoor harvests per
  growing season for maximum annual production
