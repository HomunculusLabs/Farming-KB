# How to Prevent and Treat Cannabis Light Burn and Heat Stress?

## Short Answer

Light burn and heat stress are common problems in indoor cannabis grows caused
by excessive light intensity or high temperatures beyond the plant's comfort
range. Prevention involves maintaining proper light distance, ensuring adequate
ventilation, controlling temperature and humidity, and monitoring plants for
early symptoms like upward-curling leaves, bleaching, or fox-tailing buds.

## Detailed Explanation

Light burn occurs when cannabis plants receive more light intensity than they
can process through photosynthesis. The condition primarily affects the uppermost
leaves and bud sites closest to the light source. Unlike nutrient burn, which
typically affects the tips of lower leaves first, [[cannabis-light-burn]]
starts at the top of the canopy and works its way downward through the plant.

The most common cause of light burn is placing grow lights too close to the
plant canopy. Different light types have different safe distance ranges based
on their intensity and spectrum output. LED grow lights can still cause severe
light burn despite running cooler to the touch than traditional lighting
fixtures. LEDs emit intense photosynthetically active radiation in concentrated
areas that can overwhelm leaf tissue without any warning warmth.

High-intensity discharge lights like high-pressure sodium and metal halide
generate both intense light and significant radiant heat. This makes distance
management especially critical with these traditional lighting fixtures. A
general guideline is to follow the manufacturer's recommended hanging height
and adjust based on how your plants respond during the first week of use.

Early symptoms of light burn include leaves turning yellow or white at the very
top of the plant canopy. The affected leaves may also curl upward at the edges
in a pattern sometimes described as taco-ing or canoeing. This upward curling
is the plant's attempt to reduce the surface area exposed to excessive light
intensity. If left unaddressed, the bleaching can spread to developing bud
sites and reduce both overall yield and terpene potency.

In severe cases, affected bud sites may stop developing entirely and the tissue
becomes dry and crispy. Bud damage from light burn is irreversible, and
flowers that have been light-burned often have a harsh taste and reduced
cannabinoid content. Prevention is far more effective than attempting to treat
light burn after significant damage has already occurred to the plant.

Heat stress is closely related to light burn but has distinct causes and
symptoms. While light burn is caused by excessive photon intensity at the leaf
surface, [[heat-stress-cannabis]] results from elevated ambient air temperatures
that exceed the plant's biological comfort range. Cannabis generally prefers
daytime temperatures between 70 and 85 degrees Fahrenheit during vegetative
growth, dropping slightly to 65 to 80 degrees during flowering.

Temperatures consistently above 85 degrees can cause significant stress to
flowering plants and reduce both yield and quality. Prolonged exposure above 90
degrees may cause permanent cellular damage. Symptoms include upward-curling
leaves across the entire canopy, wilting despite adequate soil moisture, and
elongated or stretched growth with wide gaps between nodes as the plant tries
to move away from heat sources.

In severe cases, heat stress can cause fox-tailing, where buds develop
elongated, abnormal calyx growth that protrudes from the main bud structure.
Fox-tailing is the plant's attempt to increase surface area for transpiration
and evaporative cooling. You can learn more about this condition in the
[[query-what-causes-fox-tailing-on-cannabis-buds]].

The relationship between temperature and humidity is crucial for understanding
how environmental stress affects your plants. As temperatures rise, the air can
hold more moisture, which affects how efficiently plants transpire and cool
themselves. Understanding [[cervantes-vapor-pressure-deficit-transpiration]] helps growers maintain optimal balance between these two
critical environmental variables throughout the grow cycle.

Proper ventilation is the first and most important line of defense against both
light burn and heat stress in indoor grows. A well-designed [[cannabis-indoor-ventilation-setup]] includes an exhaust fan rated for your grow space volume, a
passive or active intake for fresh air exchange, and oscillating fans for
canopy air circulation. Exhaust fans should exchange the entire air volume at
least once every one to three minutes during lights-on periods.

Air circulation within the canopy is just as important as overall room air
exchange. Oscillating fans should create gentle movement across all parts of the
plant. This movement strengthens stems, prevents hot spots from forming under
the light, and reduces mold and pest risks. Proper [[ventilation-cannabis]] also
helps maintain consistent [[query-how-to-manage-humidity-cannabis-grow-room]].

During vegetative growth, relative humidity between 50 and 70 percent is ideal.
During flowering, humidity should be reduced to 40 to 50 percent to prevent
bud rot and other moisture-related problems. Temperature management strategies
vary by climate. In warm conditions, air conditioning may be necessary. In
cooler climates, supplemental heat during dark periods may prevent stressful
temperature swings between day and night cycles.

Light distance management is the most direct way to prevent light burn. Most
LED manufacturers provide recommended hanging heights based on wattage and growth
stage. Start lights higher than recommended and gradually lower them while
observing plants for stress. Using a PAR meter provides the most accurate data
for positioning. Cannabis thrives with 400 to 800 micromoles during vegetative
growth and 600 to 1000 during flowering.

Dimming capabilities on modern LED grow lights offer precise control over light
intensity without moving the fixture. This approach is covered in the
[[query-how-to-choose-right-grow-light-cannabis]].
If your plants already show signs of stress, take immediate corrective action.
Move lights higher or reduce intensity by 20 to 30 percent and increase airflow
with additional fans. Recovery typically takes one to two weeks.

## Key Points

- Light burn is caused by excessive light intensity and affects the top of the
  canopy first, unlike nutrient burn which starts at leaf tips
- Heat stress results from elevated ambient temperatures above 85 degrees
  Fahrenheit and causes wilting, curling, and stretched growth
- Proper [[cannabis-indoor-ventilation-setup]] and [[ventilation-cannabis]] are
  essential first lines of defense against both conditions
- Maintain correct light distance and use a PAR meter for accurate intensity
  measurement when positioning your grow lights
- Monitor both canopy temperature and root zone temperature for comprehensive
  environmental stress management
- The [[cervantes-humidity-control-dehumidification]]
  covers dehumidification strategies for warm growing environments
