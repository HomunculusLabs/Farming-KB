# What Is Chitosan and How Do I Use It in My Garden?

## Short Answer

Chitosan is a natural biopolymer derived from chitin, the structural component found in fungal cell walls and crustacean shells.
When applied to plants, chitosan acts as a powerful elicitor that stimulates the plant's innate immune system and enhances resistance to pathogens and pests.
It works through both direct antimicrobial activity and by triggering [[plant-defense-mechanisms]] such as the production of phytoalexins and pathogenesis-related proteins.
Gardeners can use chitosan as a foliar spray, seed treatment, or soil drench to boost plant health organically.
It fits naturally into [[integrated-pest-management]] programs and is compatible with Korean Natural Farming and JADAM approaches.

## What Is Chitosan Made From?

Chitosan is produced by deacetylating chitin, which removes acetyl groups to expose free amino groups.
The primary industrial source is crustacean shell waste from shrimp, crab, and lobster processing.
However, chitin also occurs naturally in [[chitin-chitosan-fungal-cell-wall|fungal cell walls]], making fungal biomass an alternative source suitable for vegan growers.
The degree of deacetylation determines how reactive the chitosan will be in agricultural applications.
Higher deacetylation means more free amino groups and stronger biological activity.

Both [[knf-chitosan-preparation|Korean Natural Farming chitosan]] and [[jadam-chitosan-fertilizer|JADAM chitosan]] methods produce chitosan solutions from easily accessible materials.
These preparations can be made at home using crustacean shells fermented with effective microorganisms.

## How Does Chitosan Trigger Plant Defense?

When chitosan contacts plant tissues, it is recognized as a microbe-associated molecular pattern (MAMP).
This recognition activates the plant immune system through the [[plant-immune-system-pti-eti|pattern-triggered immunity (PTI) pathway]].
The plant then produces a cascade of defense responses including callose deposition, reactive oxygen species, and phytoalexin synthesis.
Chitosan also triggers the jasmonic acid signaling pathway, which is part of [[jasmonic-acid-plant-defense-signaling|jasmonic acid-mediated defense]].
This pathway is particularly effective against necrotrophic fungi and chewing insects.

Unlike synthetic pesticides that directly kill pathogens, chitosan works primarily by priming the plant's own defenses.
This means resistance develops broadly rather than targeting a single pathogen.
The priming effect can last days to weeks after a single application.
Research documented in [[chitin-and-chitosan-in-agriculture]] shows chitosan induces resistance against fungal, bacterial, and viral pathogens.

## How Do I Apply Chitosan in the Garden?

The most common application methods are foliar sprays, soil drenches, and seed treatments.
For foliar application, dissolve chitosan in a slightly acidic solution (pH 5.0-6.0) and spray onto leaves until runoff.
Chitosan is insoluble at neutral and alkaline pH, so adding a small amount of vinegar or citric acid is essential.
Use concentrations between 0.1% and 1.0% depending on the crop and target pathogen.

For seed treatment, soak seeds in a dilute chitosan solution before planting to improve germination and seedling vigor.
Soil drenches deliver chitosan to the root zone where it stimulates [[mycorrhizal-plant-defense|mycorrhizal associations]] and protects against soil-borne pathogens.
Combine chitosan with [[foliar-feeding]] programs for synergistic plant health benefits.
Apply preventively rather than curatively, since chitosan works best as a prophylactic treatment.

## What Pests and Diseases Can Chitosan Help With?

Chitosan has demonstrated efficacy against a broad spectrum of plant pathogens.
It shows strong activity against [[powdery-mildew|powdery mildew]] and [[botrytis-cinerea|Botrytis cinerea (gray mold)]].
It also suppresses [[root-rot-cannabis|root rot pathogens]] including Pythium and Fusarium species.
The antimicrobial effect comes from chitosan's positive charge disrupting negatively charged microbial cell membranes.

Against insects, chitosan interferes with feeding and reproduction in soft-bodied pests.
It enhances the effectiveness of biological control agents like [[beauveria-bassiana|Beauveria bassiana]] and [[metarhizium-anisopliae|Metarhizium anisopliae]].
Combining chitosan with [[trichoderma-and-biocontrol-fungi|Trichoderma biocontrol fungi]] creates a powerful integrated disease management approach.
Chitosan also works well alongside [[organic-pest-and-disease-management]] strategies.

## What About Using Chitosan in Compost Tea?

Adding chitosan to compost tea boosts the fungal component and enriches the microbial diversity.
[[chitin-compost-tea|Chitin-enriched compost tea]] feeds chitinolytic microorganisms that naturally suppress fungal pathogens.
These beneficial microbes break down chitin and, in the process, produce enzymes that degrade pathogen cell walls.
This is particularly valuable in [[aact-brewing-ingredients-molasses-humic-kelp-fish-ingham|ACT brewing]] where fungal foods are a key ingredient.

The chitin in compost tea also supports populations of [[plant-growth-promoting-rhizobacteria|plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR)]].
These bacteria produce antibiotics, siderophores, and growth hormones that benefit plants.
Use chitosan at low concentrations in tea to avoid inhibiting beneficial fungi.
The [[aact-fungal-foods-chitin-rock-dust-protozoa-nematodes-ingham|ACT fungal foods]] recipe specifically includes chitin as a recommended fungal food source.

## What About Chitosan for Cannabis Growing?

Cannabis plants respond particularly well to chitosan applications throughout their growth cycle.
During vegetative growth, chitosan strengthens cell walls and builds disease resistance before flowering.
In the flowering stage, it helps protect dense buds from [[botrytis-cinerea|bud rot]] and mold.
Chitosan also enhances trichome production when applied as a foliar spray during early flower.
It is compatible with [[organic-pest-and-disease-management|organic cannabis pest management]] protocols.

Avoid applying chitosan during the final two weeks before harvest to prevent any residue on buds.
Use it as part of a rotation with other organic sprays to prevent pathogen adaptation.
Many living soil growers incorporate chitosan into their IPM schedule alongside [[trichoderma-harzianum|Trichoderma]] and [[beauveria-bassiana|Beauveria]].

## What About Commercial Chitosan Products?

Several commercial chitosan products are available for agricultural use at varying concentrations and formulations.
Look for products with a degree of deacetylation above 75% for best results.
Some products combine chitosan with other biostimulants such as humic acid or seaweed extract.
The [[organic-micronutrient-sources-kelp-chitin-biofertilizers|organic micronutrient sources]] reference covers products that blend chitin with kelp and biofertilizers.

Industrial chitosan applications extend beyond agriculture into [[fungal-chitosan-biotechnology-applications|biotechnology and water treatment]].
For home gardeners, making your own chitosan following KNF or JADAM methods is far more economical than buying commercial products.
Store chitosan solutions in a cool, dark place and use within a few weeks of preparation.
Dilute concentrates before application and always test on a small area first.

## Key Points

- Chitosan is derived from chitin found in crustacean shells and [[chitin-chitosan-fungal-cell-wall|fungal cell walls]].
- It triggers plant immune responses including [[plant-defense-mechanisms|callose deposition and phytoalexin production]].
- Applications include foliar spray, seed soak, and soil drench at 0.1-1.0% concentration.
- It is effective against [[powdery-mildew]], [[botrytis-cinerea]], and various root rot pathogens.
- Chitosan synergizes with biological controls like [[trichoderma-harzianum|Trichoderma]] and [[beauveria-bassiana|Beauveria]].
- Home preparation is possible through [[knf-chitosan-preparation|KNF]] or [[jadam-chitosan-fertilizer|JADAM]] methods.
- It boosts fungal diversity in [[chitin-compost-tea|compost tea]] by feeding chitinolytic organisms.
- Always dissolve chitosan in slightly acidic water (pH 5-6) before application.
