# What Is the Difference Between Sativa, Indica, and Hybrid Cannabis?

## Short Answer

Sativa and indica are the two most commonly recognized cannabis types, with sativa plants associated with uplifting effects and tall growth, and indica plants with relaxing effects and compact structure. Hybrids are crosses bred to combine traits from both lineages, and most modern cannabis is genetically a hybrid with varying sativa-indica ratios.

## Morphological Differences

The physical characteristics of sativa and indica plants differ significantly:

**Sativa traits:**
- Tall stature with long internodal spacing
- Narrow, finger-like leaflets
- Longer flowering time (10-14+ weeks)
- Lighter green foliage
- Evolved in equatorial regions with consistent light cycles

**Indica traits:**
- Compact, bushy structure with short internodes
- Broad, wide leaflets
- Faster flowering time (7-9 weeks)
- Darker green foliage
- Adapted to the shorter growing seasons of Central Asian mountains

The [[cannabis-hybridization]] and selective breeding, virtually all commercial cannabis is genetically a hybrid.

Key points about modern hybrids:

- Pure sativa or indica genetics are increasingly rare outside landrace populations
- Breeders cross parents to combine desirable traits like yield, flavor, and potency
- "Sativa-dominant" and "indica-dominant" refer to which lineage is more prominent
