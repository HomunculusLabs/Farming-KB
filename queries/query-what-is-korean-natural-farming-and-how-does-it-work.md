# What Is Korean Natural Farming and How Does It Work?

## Short Answer
Korean Natural Farming (KNF) is a sustainable agricultural system developed by Master Han Kyu Cho that uses locally sourced, naturally fermented inputs to feed soil microbiology rather than applying synthetic fertilizers directly to plants.
The core philosophy holds that plants evolved to thrive in partnership with microorganisms, and by culturing and applying specific beneficial microbes, farmers can achieve higher yields, better pest resistance, and healthier soil without chemical inputs.
KNF can be applied to any crop at any scale, from container gardens to large commercial operations, and relies on materials that are free or nearly free.

## Detailed Explanation
Korean Natural Farming was developed in South Korea by Master Han Kyu Cho beginning in the 1960s.
After observing that the most vigorous plants in nature grew without any human intervention, Cho reverse-engineered the biological processes at work and developed a system of inputs that replicate and amplify these natural relationships.
The result is a farming method that costs very little, relies on materials found on or near any farm, and builds soil health over time rather than depleting it.
Cho spent decades refining the system through observation and experimentation, eventually establishing the Janong Natural Farming Institute to teach his methods worldwide.

### Core Principles and Philosophy

The foundation of KNF rests on the [[four-principles-of-natural-farming]], which guide all decision-making in the system.
These principles emphasize that everything a plant needs is already available in its environment, and the farmer's job is to facilitate natural processes rather than replace them with synthetic alternatives.
This stands in contrast to conventional agriculture, which treats symptoms of poor soil biology with chemical corrections.
The first principle states that plants should be grown in their natural environment as much as possible, while the second principle asserts that soil should be managed to support microbial life rather than treated as an inert substrate.
The third and fourth principles extend this thinking to nutrient management and pest control, arguing that biological solutions always outperform chemical ones when the soil food web is properly supported.

KNF also shares philosophical ground with other Asian natural farming movements.
The [[comparison-fukuoka-vs-jadam-farming]] explores how Cho's work relates to Masanobu Fukuoka's "do-nothing" farming philosophy and Youngsang Cho's JADAM system.
While Fukuoka emphasized minimal intervention with natural seed ball techniques and permanent ground cover, KNF provides a more structured input system that gives farmers greater control over plant nutrition and pest management.
JADAM, founded by a student of Master Cho, further simplifies the KNF approach by reducing the number of inputs and emphasizing anaerobic fermentation methods.
Despite these differences, all three systems share a common thread of working with nature rather than against it.

### Indigenous Microorganisms and Input Production

At the heart of KNF practice is the use of [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]], which are locally collected beneficial bacteria and fungi cultured through a simple fermentation process.
IMO is collected from healthy, undisturbed natural areas, typically from forests or meadows where diverse microbial communities have developed over decades or centuries.
The microorganisms are attracted to a bait of steamed rice placed in a bamboo basket on the forest floor, then collected and cultured through a series of stages using rice, brown sugar, and finished compost.
When applied to soil or plant surfaces, these locally adapted microbes establish a thriving biological community that cycles nutrients, suppresses pathogens, and improves soil structure.
The multi-stage culturing process progressively builds microbial diversity, starting with fast-growing bacteria in early stages and encouraging fungal dominance in later stages.

KNF practitioners produce a suite of [[cho-natural-farming-inputs-overview]] that serve specific functions throughout the growing cycle.
These inputs include Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) made from fast-growing plant tips to supply growth hormones and amino acids, Oriental Herbal Nutrient (OHN) for immune stimulation and pest deterrence, Fish Amino Acid (FAA) for nitrogen supplementation during vegetative growth, and Calcium-Phosphate solutions for structural support during flowering.
Each input is made through controlled fermentation using brown sugar as a preservative and extraction medium.
The [[complete-knf-input-reference]] provides detailed recipes, ratios, and application timing for each input.
Additional specialized inputs include Water-Soluble Calcium (WCA) made from eggshells for cell wall strength, Water-Soluble Phosphorus (WSP) from animal bones for root and flower development, and Lactic Acid Bacteria Serum (LABS) for anaerobic digestion and odor control.

### Fermented Plant and Fruit Juices

The [[fermented-plant-extract-fpe]] is one of the most versatile and commonly used KNF inputs.
It is made by harvesting vigorously growing plant shoots at their peak nutritional moment, chopping them, mixing with equal weight of brown sugar, and allowing them to ferment for seven days.
The sugar draws out the plant's juices through osmotic pressure, extracting growth factors, enzymes, vitamins, and trace minerals.
Different plants are harvested at different growth stages to target specific nutritional needs.
For example, mugwort FPJ harvested before flowering provides micronutrients and growth stimulation, while comfrey FPJ supports flowering and fruiting with its high potassium content.
The choice of plant material matters greatly: fast-growing plants like bamboo shoots produce inputs rich in gibberellic acid for vegetative growth, while mature plant materials provide different hormone profiles suited to flowering and fruiting.

Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ) follows a similar process but uses ripe fruits instead of vegetative growth.
FFJ provides natural sugars, organic acids, and fruit-specific phytonutrients that boost plant energy and attract beneficial microbes.
It is particularly useful during flowering and fruiting stages when plants benefit from additional phosphorus and potassium found naturally in fruits.
The cannabis-specific application of FFJ for ripening is documented in the [[cannabis-knf-ffj-ripeness-progression]] guide, which shows how sequential applications of different fruit juices can enhance terpene and cannabinoid production.
Grape FFJ, for instance, is applied during early flowering for its balanced sugar profile, while blueberry FFJ is used later in flowering for its antioxidant properties.

Oriental Herbal Nutrient (OHN) is KNF's immune system booster and pest deterrent.
It is made by separately fermenting garlic, angelica, licorice, ginger, and cinnamon in alcohol or vinegar, then combining the extracts.
OHN stimulates the plant's natural defense mechanisms, making it more resistant to both pests and diseases.
It can be applied as a foliar spray or soil drench and is considered one of the most powerful inputs in the KNF toolkit.
The fermentation process for each herb takes between two weeks and several months, with garlic fermented first, followed by angelica, and finally licorice, ginger, and cinnamon combined.

### Accessibility and Practical Advantages

One of the key advantages of KNF is its accessibility.
Unlike some organic systems that require expensive certified inputs, KNF uses materials that are free or nearly free: rice, brown sugar, locally available weeds, eggshells, and fish scraps.
A single bag of brown sugar, the most expensive recurring input, can produce dozens of batches of various inputs.
This makes KNF particularly attractive for small-scale farmers and home gardeners in any economic context.
The [[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]] reveals that while permaculture focuses on landscape design and perennial systems, KNF focuses more on biological input production and can be applied to any scale.

KNF is also remarkably scalable and adaptable to different climates, crops, and growing systems.
The same fundamental inputs work whether you are growing vegetables in a backyard garden, managing a cannabis living soil system, or running a diversified farm.
Because the inputs are made from locally collected microorganisms, they naturally adapt to the specific biological conditions of each region, becoming more effective with continued use as microbial communities establish and mature in the soil.
This local adaptation is one of KNF's greatest strengths, as commercially produced biological inoculants often contain organisms that are not adapted to the specific conditions of a given farm or garden.
