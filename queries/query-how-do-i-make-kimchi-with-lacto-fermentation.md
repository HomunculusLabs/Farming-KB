# How do I make kimchi with lacto-fermentation?

## Short Answer

Kimchi is made by salting vegetables, mixing them with seasonings, packing them under their own brine, and letting lactic-acid bacteria acidify the mixture.
The practical kimchi process is covered in [[bionutrients-lab-lactic-acid-bacteria-serum]].
That acid lowers pH and makes the environment less friendly to many spoilage organisms.
The page [[food-fermentation-guide]] help translate between recipes, brines, and vegetable ferments.
Too little salt can lead to mushy textures and spoilage risk.
Too much salt can slow fermentation and make the finished kimchi unpleasant to eat.

### Why must the vegetables stay submerged?

Lacto-fermentation works best when vegetables remain below the brine and away from oxygen.
Exposed pieces can dry, discolor, grow surface yeast, or develop mold.
A fermentation weight, small jar, clean stone, or folded cabbage leaf can hold vegetables under the liquid.
A loose lid or airlock lets carbon dioxide escape without inviting too much oxygen exposure.
The page [[lactobacillus-brevis]] is another useful reference for lactic fermentation ecology.
The point is not to identify every microbe in the jar.
The point is to create a brined, acidic, low-oxygen habitat where beneficial fermenters dominate.

### How is kimchi different from sauerkraut?

Kimchi and sauerkraut are both lactic vegetable ferments, but kimchi usually includes more aromatics, chile, and mixed vegetables.
Sauerkraut often relies on finely shredded cabbage and salt with fewer seasonings.
