# What Are EC and TDS and How Do I Use Them for Cannabis?

## Short Answer
EC (electrical conductivity) and TDS (total dissolved solids) are
measurements that indicate the concentration of mineral nutrients in your
water or nutrient solution. EC measures conductivity directly in millisiemens
per centimeter (mS/cm) while TDS estimates concentration in parts per
million (ppm) using a conversion factor. Monitoring these values allows
cannabis growers to dial in precise feeding schedules, prevent
[[query-what-causes-nutrient-burn-in-cannabis-and-how-to-prevent-it]], and identify
[[cannabis-nutrient-lockout-and-ph]] problems before they
damage plants.

## Detailed Explanation

For cannabis growers using bottled nutrients, organic amendments, or any
systematic feeding approach, understanding EC and TDS is essential for
achieving consistent results across grows. These measurements remove the
guesswork from feeding by quantifying exactly how much dissolved mineral
content is in your water or nutrient solution. While soil growers can
sometimes achieve acceptable results without these tools, they are
virtually indispensable for any form of [[modern-farm-cannabis-living-soil-vs-hydroponic-debate]]
or [[comparison-coco-coir-vs-peat]] cultivation where the grower
controls the entire nutrient profile and there is no soil buffer to
moderate concentration fluctuations.

EC is the more scientifically precise and reliable measurement. It
quantifies the ability of dissolved ions in water to conduct electricity,
which is directly proportional to the concentration of mineral salts
present. Pure water has an EC near zero, while a typical cannabis nutrient
solution might range from 0.8 to 2.5 mS/cm depending on the growth stage
and feeding strategy. EC meters work by passing an electrical current
between two electrodes and measuring the resistance; more dissolved ions
means lower resistance and higher conductivity. This direct measurement
makes EC the preferred standard among professional growers and in
scientific agricultural research worldwide.

TDS is a derived measurement that converts EC readings into an estimated
concentration of dissolved solids expressed in parts per million. The
conversion uses a factor that varies between meter manufacturers, commonly
ranging from 500 to 700 ppm per mS/cm. This inconsistency between devices
is the primary criticism of TDS meters: a reading of 1000 ppm on one meter
may correspond to 1400 ppm on another depending on the conversion factor
used. For this reason, experienced growers generally prefer EC as their
primary reference and use TDS only as a rough secondary guide. The
[[query-what-is-the-difference-between-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] is important to
understand to avoid misinterpreting readings from different devices or
sharing feeding recipes with other growers.

Different cannabis growth stages require different nutrient concentrations
for optimal health and yield. Seedlings and clones need very low EC,
typically 0.4 to 0.8 mS/cm, since their developing root systems are
sensitive to salt concentrations and can be easily burned. During the
[[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]], plants can handle
gradually increasing EC levels, often reaching 1.2 to 1.8 mS/cm as they
build foliage and root mass. The [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]]
demands the highest nutrient loads, with many growers running 1.5 to 2.5
mS/cm depending on the strain and growing method. However, pushing EC too
high at any stage risks causing [[query-what-causes-nutrient-burn-in-cannabis-and-how-to-prevent-it]], which manifests as scorched leaf tips and can reduce both yield
and overall quality.

Using EC to manage your grow involves measuring both your input water and
your runoff. The input EC tells you the starting concentration of your
nutrient solution before it reaches the plant. Comparing this to the EC
of runoff collected after watering reveals what the plant is actually
taking up versus what is accumulating. If runoff EC is significantly
higher than input EC, salt is accumulating in the medium, which can lead
to [[cannabis-nutrient-lockout-and-ph]] over time. If runoff EC is
much lower than input, the plant is consuming nutrients aggressively and
may benefit from higher feeding concentrations. This input versus runoff
comparison is one of the most powerful diagnostic tools available to
cannabis growers for maintaining optimal root zone conditions.

When nutrient problems arise, EC and TDS readings help distinguish between
different types of disorders. Low EC in runoff alongside deficiency
symptoms suggests the plant simply needs more food. High EC alongside
deficiency symptoms despite adequate feeding points toward nutrient
lockout, often caused by pH being outside the optimal range of 5.8 to 6.5
for hydroponics or 6.0 to 7.0 for soil. In either case, flushing with
pH-adjusted plain water and then reapplying nutrients at the correct EC
is the standard corrective approach. Regular EC monitoring, combined with
pH testing, gives growers complete visibility into their root zone
chemistry.

## Key Points
- EC (mS/cm) directly measures electrical conductivity; TDS (ppm) is an
  estimated conversion that varies between meter manufacturers
- Seedlings need EC of 0.4 to 0.8; vegetative plants 1.2 to 1.8;
  flowering plants 1.5 to 2.5 mS/cm
- Comparing input EC to runoff EC reveals whether nutrients are
  accumulating or being consumed by the plant
- High EC with deficiency symptoms indicates [[cannabis-nutrient-lockout-and-ph]],
  often caused by pH being out of range
- EC meters are essential tools for [[modern-farm-cannabis-living-soil-vs-hydroponic-debate]]
  and [[comparison-coco-coir-vs-peat]] cultivation systems
