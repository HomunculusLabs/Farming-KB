# Mushroom Toxicity and Identification Warnings

Dangerous lookalike reference for wild mushroom foragers. Covers the most commonly confused edible-toxic pairs, distinguishing features, toxicity levels, and emergency procedures. Compiled from [[mushroom-identification]], [[mushroom-species-guide]], and [[psilocybin-mushroom-chemistry]].

## Edible vs. Toxic Lookalike Pairs

| Edible Species | Toxic Lookalike | Key Distinguishing Features | Habitat Overlap | Toxicity Level | Toxins |
|---------------|----------------|---------------------------|-----------------|---------------|--------|
| **Chanterelle** (Cantharellus cibarius) | **Jack-o-Lantern** (Omphalotus olearius) | Chanterelle: false gills (ridges), solid stem, apricot odor, golden yellow. Jack-o-Lantern: true gills, hollow stem, orange, glows in dark, grows in clusters on wood | Both in deciduous forests, fall | Moderate — nausea, vomiting, cramps, mild hallucinations | Illudin S |
| **Chanterelle** (Cantharellus cibarius) | **False Chanterelle** (Hygrophoropsis aurantiaca) | True chanterelle: forked ridges, single stem, wavy cap. False: true gills, thinner flesh, orange-brown, on conifer stumps | Coniferous forests, fall | Low — mild GI upset if raw | GI irritant |
| **Morel** (Morchella spp.) | **False Morel** (Gyromitra esculenta) | True morel: honeycomb cap, hollow when cut lengthwise, cap attached at base. False: brain-like wrinkled cap, chambered interior, cap hangs free like skirt | Spring, temperate forests near dead elms | HIGH — potentially fatal; gyromitrin (MMH) | Gyromitrin (monomethylhydrazine) |
| **Morel** (Morchella spp.) | **Verpa bohemica** | True morel: cap attached at base of stem. Verpa: cap attached at TOP only, hanging like a skirt; longer stem | Spring, temperate forests | Low-moderate — GI upset in some | Unknown GI irritants |
| **King Bolete** (Boletus edulis) | **Bitter Bolete** (Tylopilus felleus) | King bolete: white pore surface, reticulated stem. Bitter bolete: pink pore surface darkening with age, smooth stem, extremely bitter taste | Mixed forests, summer-fall | Low — not poisonous but intensely bitter | No toxins |
| **King Bolete** (Boletus edulis) | **Devil's Bolete** (Rubroboletus satanas) | King bolete: white pores, firm. Devil's: red-orange pore surface, yellow stem with red net, blue bruising, foul odor | Europe, oak-beech forests | HIGH — severe GI distress | Bolesatine |
| **Psilocybe cubensis** | **Galerina marginata** | Psilocybe: bruises blue, dark brown spore print, on dung/enriched soil. Galerina: does NOT bruise blue, persistent ring, brown rusty gills, brown spore print, on decaying WOOD | Both on decaying wood debris | DEADLY — amatoxins; liver/kidney failure 6-24hr | Amatoxins (alpha-amanitin) |
| **Psilocybe** spp. | **Inocybe** spp. | Psilocybe: blue bruising, dark purple-brown spore print. Inocybe: brown spore print, fibrous cap, some contain muscarine; no blue bruising | Forest floors, grass, wood chips | Moderate-HIGH — muscarine in some species | Muscarine (some species) |
| **Panaeolus cyanescens** | **Panaeolina foenisecii** | P. cyanescens: bruises blue, jet-black spore print, on dung. P. foenisecii: does NOT bruise blue, dark brown spore print, in lawns | Both in grass; P. cyanescens on dung (tropical) | Low — P. foenisecii is inactive, not toxic | None (inactive) |
| **Paddy Straw** (Volvariella volvacea) | **Death Cap** (Amanita phalloides) | Paddy straw: pink spore print, no ring. Death Cap: WHITE spore print, volva at base, white gills, greenish cap | Tropical/subtropical vs. under oaks | DEADLY — 6-24hr delayed onset, 50-90% mortality | Amatoxins |
| **Agaricus campestris** | **Death Cap** (Amanita phalloides) | Field mushroom: brown-pink spore print, no volva. Death Cap: white spore print, white gills, volva at base | Pastures, forest edges near oaks | DEADLY — see above | Amatoxins |
| **Amanita muscaria** (edible parboiled) | **Amanita phalloides** (Death Cap) | Fly agaric: bright red cap with white warts, white gills. Death Cap: olive-green cap, white gills, white spore print, volva | Forests, birch (muscaria); oaks (phalloides) | Muscaria: manageable; Phalloides: DEADLY | Muscaria = ibotenic acid/muscimol; Phalloides = amatoxins |

## Major Poisonous Genera

### Amanita
- A. phalloides, A. verna, A. virosa: amatoxins; delayed onset (6-24 hr) liver/kidney failure; frequently fatal; odourless, white spore print
- A. muscaria, A. pantherina: ibotenic acid/muscimol; nervous system effects; bright colors but NOT all Amanita are colorful

### Galerina
- G. marginata (Deadly Galerina): amatoxins identical to Death Cap; grows on decaying wood; brown spore print; easily confused with Psilocybe or Kuehneromyces

### Cortinarius
- Several species contain orellanine (kidney toxin); delayed onset (days to weeks)
- Rust-brown spore print; cobwebby partial veil (cortina)
- Notoriously difficult to identify; best avoided entirely by beginners

## Spore Print Quick Reference

Essential for identification — place cap gills-down on half white/half black paper, cover with bowl, wait 4-24 hours:

| Spore Print Color | Examples |
|------------------|----------|
| White | Amanita, Tricholoma, Lepiota — includes Death Cap |
| Pink/brown | Agaricus (edible), Entoloma (poisonous) |
| Dark brown/chocolate | Psilocybe, Galerina (deadly lookalike) |
| Rust/brown | Cortinarius (some toxic) |
| Cream/yellow | Chanterelles |
| Purple-brown | Some Boletus |

## Emergency Section

### If You Suspect Mushroom Poisoning

1. **Call Poison Control immediately**: 1-800-222-1222 (US) or local emergency number
2. **Do NOT wait for symptoms** — amatoxin has 6-24 hr delayed onset; organ damage begins before symptoms
3. **Save a whole specimen** including base/volva — wrap in paper, not plastic
4. **Note time of ingestion** and onset of any symptoms
5. **Do NOT induce vomiting** unless directed by Poison Control
6. **Collect any vomit** — may help identification
7. **Go to ER** if: nausea/vomiting 6+ hours after eating, abdominal pain, dark urine, jaundice

### Danger Signs Requiring Immediate ER
- Symptoms 6-24 hours after eating (amatoxin pattern)
- Persistent vomiting/diarrhea leading to dehydration
- Yellowing of skin or eyes (jaundice — liver failure)
- Dark or tea-colored urine
- Confusion, disorientation, seizures

### Foraging Safety Rules
- No single rule separates edible from poisonous (Ramsbottom, 1945)
- Learn each species individually using multiple features
- Always take a spore print; always check for volva at stem base
- Slice puffballs vertically to check for embryonic gill structure
- Never eat a mushroom unless 100% certain of identification
- When in doubt, throw it out
