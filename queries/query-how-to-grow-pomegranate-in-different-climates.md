# How Do I Grow Pomegranate in Different Climates?

Pomegranates ([[cover-crops]]. Fruit sunburn — ensure adequate leaf canopy.

**Subtropical:** Fungal diseases from humidity — improve airflow, use copper sprays. Splitting fruit from irregular rainfall — maintain consistent soil moisture.

**Cold/Temperate:** Winter dieback — protect trunk and roots. Late frost killing flowers — cover with frost cloth during bloom. Shorter growing season — choose early-ripening varieties.

**Arid/Desert:** Sunscald on trunks and fruit — white trunk paint, adequate canopy. Salt burn on leaves — flush soil periodically with deep irrigation.

**Tropical:** Poor fruit set due to lack of chill — try manual defoliation. Severe pest pressure (fruit flies, mealybugs) — bag fruit, use neem oil sprays.

## Quick-Start Checklist

1. Identify your USDA hardiness zone and local microclimate.
2. Select 1–2 varieties suited to your climate from the table above.
3. Prepare a well-drained planting site with full sun exposure.
4. Plant in early spring (or fall in frost-free zones).
5. Water deeply 2–3 times per week for the first year; reduce frequency once established.
6. Mulch to retain moisture and suppress weeds (keep mulch away from the trunk).
7. Fertilize lightly in spring with a balanced fruit tree fertilizer.
8. Prune annually in late winter to maintain shape and remove dead wood.
9. Protect from extreme cold, humidity-related disease, or desert sun as needed.
10. Harvest when fruit is fully colored and sounds hollow when tapped.
