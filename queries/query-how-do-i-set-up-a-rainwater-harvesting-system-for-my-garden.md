First-flush diversion is a critical but often overlooked component of rainwater harvesting systems.
The first 5 to 10 millimeters of rain from each storm event carries the majority of contaminants from the roof surface.
These include dust, pollen, bird droppings, and other debris that have accumulated since the last rainfall.
A first-flush diverter captures this initial dirty water and routes it away from the storage tank.
This ensures that only cleaner water from later in the storm enters your storage vessel.
Simple first-flush diverters can be constructed from PVC pipe with a float valve that automatically resets between rain events.
### Storage Tank Options
Storage tanks are the most visible and often most expensive component of a rainwater harvesting system.
Options range from simple 55-gallon food-grade plastic barrels for small systems to 10,000-gallon underground cisterns.
For most home gardeners, polyethylene plastic tanks in the 200 to 1000 gallon range offer the best balance of affordability and durability.
These tanks are available in a wide range of colors, with dark colors preferred to inhibit algae growth by blocking sunlight.
Tank placement is important for both function and aesthetics of your garden landscape.
The tank should be elevated on a sturdy, level platform at least 30 centimeters above the ground.
This elevation provides gravity-fed water pressure for irrigation hoses and drip systems.
A platform 60 to 90 centimeters high provides even better pressure for drip irrigation systems that require minimum operating pressure.
The tank should be located close to the garden area it serves to minimize the length of distribution hoses and reduce friction losses.
In cold climates, tanks must be drained before freezing temperatures arrive to prevent ice damage to the tank and plumbing connections.
