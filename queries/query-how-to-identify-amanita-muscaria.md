# How to Identify Amanita Muscaria (Fly Agaric)

## Short Answer

[[amanita-muscaria]] (the [[fly-agaric]]) is one of the most recognizable
mushrooms on Earth, with a bright scarlet to orange-red cap dotted
with white wart-like patches, white gills, a white stem with a skirt-
like ring, and a bulbous base encased in a cup-like volva.
It grows in mycorrhizal association with birch, pine, spruce, and fir
trees across the Northern Hemisphere.
Despite its iconic appearance, several lookalikes exist and proper
identification requires attention to multiple features.

## Cap Features

The cap is the most distinctive feature of Amanita muscaria.
Young specimens are nearly spherical, enclosed in a white universal
veil (volva).
As the mushroom expands, the cap flattens to 5-20 cm across and
develops a slightly striate (grooved) margin at the edges.
The surface color ranges from bright scarlet through orange-red to
pale yellow, depending on variety and age.
White or yellowish wart-like patches remnants of the universal veil
dot the cap surface, often arranged in concentric rings.
These warts can wash off in heavy rain, leaving a smooth cap that
may cause confusion with other species.
The cap flesh is white and firm just below the skin, becoming softer
toward the gills.

## Stem and Ring

The stem (stipe) is white, 8-20 cm tall, and 1-2.5 cm thick.
It is stuffed with cottony tissue when young, becoming hollow with age.
