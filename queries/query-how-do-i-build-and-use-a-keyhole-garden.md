# How Do I Build and Use a Keyhole Garden?

## Short Answer

A keyhole garden is a raised circular bed with a notch-shaped path that lets you reach every part of the growing area from one spot.
At its center sits a compost basket that slowly feeds nutrients and moisture to surrounding soil.
This design was developed in Africa for food security and has become popular worldwide for small-space and low-maintenance growing.
You can build one in a weekend using reclaimed bricks, stones, or even woven branches as the outer wall.

## Detailed Explanation

### What Is a Keyhole Garden?

A [[gaias-garden-keyhole-beds-and-mandala-gardens|Gaia's Garden]] popularized this concept for home gardeners as part of a broader mandala garden layout.
The [[comparison-keyhole-garden-vs-raised-bed|Keyhole gardens differ from standard raised beds]] in their circular shape and integrated compost system.
When choosing materials, consider local availability and whether the wall needs to be movable or permanent.
Stone and brick walls provide excellent thermal mass, absorbing heat during the day and releasing it at night for frost protection.
The wall must have drainage gaps or holes near the base to prevent waterlogging during heavy rain.

### Filling the Garden in Layers

The interior is filled in layers, much like [[square-foot-gardening|square-foot gardening]] spacing principles to maximize yield in the circular footprint.
Tall or vining plants can be trained upward on a central trellis attached to the compost basket, saving horizontal space.
Interplant fast-growing crops like radishes and lettuce between slower growers for continuous harvests throughout the season.

### What About Watering and Maintenance?

Keyhole gardens are notably drought-resistant because the central basket delivers moisture directly to the root zone.
During establishment, water the entire surface thoroughly for the first two weeks until roots reach the basket zone.
After that, most maintenance involves topping up the compost basket with kitchen scraps and occasional watering.
Mulching the soil surface with straw or leaves further reduces evaporation and suppresses weeds.
Check the basket weekly to ensure it is not compacted and water can flow through freely.
Refill the wall layers annually with fresh compost and topsoil to compensate for settling and nutrient depletion.

### Keyhole Gardens Compared to Other Methods

[[comparison-herb-spiral-vs-keyhole-garden|Compared to herb spirals]], keyhole gardens are larger and focused on vegetable production rather than culinary herbs.
[[raised-bed-gardening|raised-bed approach]] shares benefits like improved drainage, warmer soil, and reduced bending.
However, keyhole gardens uniquely combine raised-bed growing with in-situ composting and efficient water distribution.
For gardeners comparing options, the keyhole design works best when both growing and composting space are limited.

### Designing Keyhole Beds in a Larger Garden

Multiple keyhole gardens can be arranged in a pattern to form a [[biointensive-gardening-overview|Biointensive methods]] complement keyhole gardens by maximizing plant density within each bed.
Consider placing keyholes near the kitchen door for convenient composting of food scraps and daily harvesting.
