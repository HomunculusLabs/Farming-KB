## How to Apply Beauveria Bassiana

### Spray Applications

The most common method is applying a spore suspension as a foliar spray.

Commercial products like BotaniGard, Mycotrol, and Naturalis come as concentrated spore formulations.

Mix the product with water according to label directions, typically one to two teaspoons per gallon.

Add a surfactant or wetting agent to improve spore adhesion to insect bodies.

Coconut-based surfactants or yucca extract work well and are compatible with organic growing.

Spray in the evening or early morning when humidity is higher and UV light is low.

Direct sunlight degrades fungal spores rapidly, so timing is critical for effectiveness.

Apply to both the upper and lower leaf surfaces since many target pests hide underneath.

### Application Frequency

For heavy infestations, apply every three to five days for two to three weeks.

Lighter infestations may only need weekly applications for two weeks.

Monitor pest populations after treatment to determine if follow-up applications are needed.

Environmental conditions significantly affect how quickly the fungus works and how long it persists.

## Environmental Conditions for Success

Beauveria bassiana requires specific conditions to germinate and infect insects effectively.

Optimal temperature range is between 20 and 30 degrees Celsius (68 to 86 Fahrenheit).

Humidity above 70 percent relative humidity dramatically improves spore germination and infection rates.

Direct sunlight and UV radiation are the primary factors that reduce spore viability on leaf surfaces.

Spores can lose half their viability within four hours of direct midday sun exposure.

Applying in the evening extends the window of spore activity before sunlight degrades them.

Rainfall shortly after application can wash spores off plants, so check forecasts before spraying.

In greenhouse environments, the humidity and temperature control make Beauveria particularly effective.

## Safety and Precautions

Beauveria bassiana is classified as low toxicity to humans and mammals.

However, it can cause allergic reactions in sensitive individuals, so wear a mask and gloves during application.

People with compromised immune systems should consult a doctor before handling concentrated spore products.

The fungus can infect honeybees in laboratory settings, though field studies show minimal impact at normal application rates.

To protect pollinators, avoid spraying during active foraging hours and do not apply directly to flowering plants when bees are present.

The spores are virtually non-toxic to birds, fish, and most wildlife.

Store commercial products in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight to maintain spore viability.
