# How to Prevent Hermaphrodite Cannabis Plants

## Short Answer
Hermaphrodite cannabis plants develop both male and female sex organs,
usually in response to environmental stress or genetic predisposition.
Prevention focuses on maintaining stable growing conditions, choosing
genetically stable strains, avoiding light leaks during the dark period, and
inspecting plants regularly during early flowering to remove pollen sacs
before they open. Once a hermaphrodite releases pollen, it can pollinate
your entire crop and ruin sinsemilla flowers.

## Detailed Explanation
Cannabis is normally a dioecious species, meaning individual plants are
either male or female. However, cannabis has evolved the ability to develop
both male and female reproductive organs on the same plant as a survival
mechanism when environmental conditions threaten reproduction. In a grow
room, this stress response is problematic because any pollen released by a
hermaphrodite plant will fertilize nearby female flowers, causing them to
produce seeds instead of the premium seedless buds (sinsemilla) that growers
want. Understanding [[cannabis-hermaphrodite-prevention-and-management]]
page provides detailed identification guides with photos showing the
difference between normal female calyx development and early male pollen sac
formation.

Genetics play a significant role in hermaphrodite tendency. Some cannabis
strains are genetically predisposed to herming, especially certain
sativa-dominant hybrids and unstable crosses. When selecting genetics for
your grow, prioritize strains from reputable breeders with a track record of
stability. Feminized seeds, while convenient, carry a higher risk of
hermaphroditism than regular seeds because the feminization process involves
stressing a female plant to produce male flowers, and this tendency can be
passed to offspring. The [[cannabis-light-burn]]
page covers how light stress manifests and how to prevent light leaks in
your grow space.

Temperature stress is another major contributor. Temperatures above 85
degrees Fahrenheit or below 60 degrees Fahrenheit during flowering can push
plants into hermaphrodite mode. Rapid temperature fluctuations, such as
turning off exhaust fans at night and letting the room heat up, are
particularly problematic. Consistent temperature control throughout the
