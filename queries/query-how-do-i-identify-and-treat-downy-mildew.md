## How Do I Identify and Treat Downy Mildew?

## Short Answer

Downy mildew is a destructive plant disease caused by oomycete pathogens, not true fungi.
It appears as yellow or pale patches on leaf upper surfaces with gray-purple fuzzy growth underneath.
Unlike [[powdery-mildew]], which sits on the surface, downy mildew grows inside leaf tissue, making it harder to eradicate.
Early identification and prevention through air circulation, drip irrigation, and resistant varieties are your best defenses.
This guide covers identification, host plants, cultural prevention, and organic treatment strategies for common garden and farm crops.

## What Is Downy Mildew?

Downy mildew refers to a group of diseases caused by oomycete organisms in genera such as [[peronospora]], [[plasmopara]], and [[phytophthora]].
These pathogens belong to the [[biodiversity-oomycetes-straminipiles|oomycetes (water molds)]], which are structurally different from true fungi despite causing similar symptoms.
They thrive in cool, wet conditions with high humidity and spread rapidly through airborne spores and splashing water.
The [[bloomfield-oomycete-zoospore-biology-flagella-chemotaxis-galvanotaxis|zoospores]] use flagella to swim through water films on leaf surfaces before infecting tissue.

## How to Identify Downy Mildew

Look for angular yellow or pale green patches on the upper leaf surface bounded by leaf veins.
On the underside, you will see gray, purple, or brown fuzzy sporulation, especially visible in morning dew.
Affected leaves eventually curl downward, brown, and die from the margins inward.
The disease progresses from lower leaves upward, which distinguishes it from many nutrient deficiencies that affect new growth first.
On grapes, infection can spread to fruit clusters causing them to shrivel and drop.
On lettuce and basil, downy mildew causes irregular brown patches that make leaves unmarketable.

Each pathogen species is highly specialized, meaning the downy mildew on your grapes will not infect your lettuce.

## How Downy Mildew Differs from Powdery Mildew

Many growers confuse downy mildew with [[biodiversity-powdery-mildews|powdery mildew]] because both produce visible surface growth.
[[cervantes-downy-mildew-fungal-blight|Downy mildew]] grows inside the leaf and sporulates only on the underside, appearing as angular patches.
[[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment|Powdery mildew]] grows on both surfaces as white powder and prefers warm, dry conditions with cool nights.
Downy mildew thrives in cool, wet environments between 50 and 70 degrees Fahrenheit with prolonged leaf wetness.
Treatment strategies differ significantly, so correct identification is essential before applying any controls.

## Cultural Prevention Strategies

Prevention is far more effective than treatment because downy mildew grows internally in leaf tissue.
Improve air circulation by spacing plants properly and pruning dense canopies to reduce humidity around foliage.
Use [[drip-irrigation|drip irrigation]] instead of overhead watering to keep leaves dry, which is the single most impactful change you can make.
Ensure greenhouse [[ventilation-cannabis|ventilation]] systems exhaust humid air and bring in fresh, dry air continuously.
Practice [[crop-rotation-and-ley-farming|crop rotation]] with non-host plants for at least two years to break disease cycles.
Remove and destroy infected plant debris at the end of each season because spores overwinter on dead tissue.
Choose resistant cultivars whenever available, as breeding for [[clubroot-disease-management|disease resistance]] is the most sustainable long-term solution.

## Organic Treatment Options

Several organic treatments can suppress downy mildew if applied early, before widespread infection.
[[cervantes-neem-oil-organic-pest-control|Neem oil]] disrupts spore germination and can slow disease progression on lightly infected plants.
[[trichoderma|Trichoderma]] species act as [[trichoderma-and-biocontrol-fungi|biocontrol agents]] that compete with oomycetes for space and nutrients on leaf surfaces.
[[compost-tea-specific-disease-management|Compost tea]] applications introduce beneficial microbes that can outcompete pathogens on leaf surfaces.
Copper-based fungicides are traditionally effective against oomycetes but must be used carefully to avoid soil accumulation.
Potassium bicarbonate sprays alter leaf surface pH to inhibit spore germination and are less toxic than copper.
A comprehensive [[integrated-pest-management-ipm|IPM]] approach combines cultural, biological, and chemical tools for best results.

## What About Environmental Controls in Greenhouses?

Greenhouse growers have a significant advantage because they can control humidity and temperature directly.
Maintain relative humidity below 85 percent, especially during nighttime hours when temperatures drop and dew forms.
Use horizontal airflow fans to keep air moving across the canopy, preventing moisture from settling on leaves.
Heat and vent cycles in the early morning push out humid air before spores have conditions to germinate.
Monitor with a hygrometer placed at canopy level, not near the greenhouse entry where readings are less relevant.

## What About Downy Mildew on Cannabis?

Cannabis is susceptible to several oomycete pathogens that cause symptoms resembling downy mildew on other crops.
[[cervantes-root-rot-pythium-damping-off|Pythium species]] cause root rot and damping off in cannabis seedlings, thriving in the same wet conditions.
[[cannabis-mold-and-mildew-prevention|Mold prevention]] in cannabis grows requires strict humidity control during flowering when dense buds trap moisture.
Outdoor cannabis plants are vulnerable during cool, rainy stretches in late summer and early fall.
Preventive copper or bicarbonate sprays applied before wet weather periods can protect valuable outdoor crops.
The [[cervantes-chemical-fungicides-insecticides|chemical fungicide]] reference covers conventional options for severe outbreaks in commercial operations.

## What About Monitoring and Early Detection?

Catching downy mildew in its earliest stages dramatically improves treatment outcomes and limits crop losses.
Scout the lower canopy weekly, focusing on the most susceptible varieties and shaded areas with poor airflow.
Use a hand lens to examine leaf undersides for the characteristic gray-purple sporulation before symptoms become visible on top.
Weather-based disease forecasting models predict infection risk based on temperature, humidity, and leaf wetness duration.
Place sticky traps near the crop canopy to capture airborne spores and give early warning of pathogen presence.

## Key Points

- Downy mildew is caused by oomycetes (water molds), not true fungi, requiring different treatment approaches.
- Look for angular yellow patches on upper leaf surfaces and gray-purple fuzzy growth underneath.
- It thrives in cool, wet conditions between 50-70°F with prolonged leaf wetness periods.
- Prevention through drip irrigation, air circulation, and resistant varieties is more effective than cure.
- Each pathogen species is host-specific, so downy mildew on grapes will not spread to cucumbers.
- [[cervantes-damping-off-seedling-disease-prevention|Damping off]] and downy mildew share some oomycete pathogens, so seedling hygiene matters.
- Organic treatments including neem oil, trichoderma, compost tea, and copper can suppress early infections.
- The [[cannabis-mold-and-mildew-prevention|cannabis mold and mildew prevention]] guide applies similar principles to a high-value specialty crop.
- Always confirm identification before treating, as [[cervantes-downy-mildew-fungal-blight|downy mildew]] is easily confused with powdery mildew.
