FIMing is essentially an imprecise or partial topping that was discovered
accidentally, hence the colorful name. Instead of making a clean cut through the
entire stem above a node, FIMing involves cutting roughly 75% of the newest growth
tip, leaving a small portion of the apical meristem intact. Because some apical
tissue remains, the plant's response is less predictable. Rather than producing
exactly two new colas, FIMing often results in three to four (and sometimes more)
new growth tips emerging from the damaged site. The results vary depending on
exactly where and how the cut is made, the genetics of the plant, and environmental
conditions during recovery.

Comparing the two techniques directly, topping is generally considered more
reliable and consistent in its results. You know you will get two new colas from
each topped site, making it easier to plan your canopy architecture. FIMing is less
predictable but potentially more productive per cut, since it can create three or
more new branches from a single intervention. Recovery time is typically shorter
for FIMing since less tissue is removed and the plant retains some of its apical
growth momentum. However, the asymmetrical results can make canopy management and
light distribution more challenging compared to the even symmetry produced by
topping.
