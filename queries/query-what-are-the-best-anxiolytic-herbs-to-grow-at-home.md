## Tier 1: Easy-to-Grow, Gentle Anxiolytics

### Chamomile (German)

**Why grow it**: The most accessible anxiolytic herb for any garden. German chamomile produces abundant flowers rich in apigenin — a flavonoid that binds GABA_A receptors similarly to benzodiazepines but without sedation or dependence.

**Growing tips**:
- Direct sow in early spring; barely cover seeds (they need light to germinate)
- Full sun to partial shade; tolerates poor soil
- Harvest flowers when fully open; dry on screens in a well-ventilated area
- Self-seeds readily — may become a persistent "weed" (a welcome one)
- Yield: 1–2 cups dried flowers per plant per season

**Preparation**: Steep 2–3 g dried flowers in 200 mL hot water for 5–10 minutes. Drink 1–3 cups daily. Combines well with lemon balm and lavender.

### Lemon Balm

**Why grow it**: A vigorous perennial mint-family herb with significant anxiolytic activity. Studies show lemon balm reduces anxiety, improves mood, and enhances cognitive performance. The rosmarinic acid content contributes to GABA-transaminase inhibition.

**Growing tips**:
- Extremely easy — almost indestructible once established
- Partial shade preferred; tolerates full sun with adequate moisture
- Can become invasive — contain with barriers or grow in pots
- Harvest leaves any time; peak potency just before flowering
- Hard prune to maintain bushy growth

**Preparation**: Fresh or dried leaf tea. Steep 1.5–4 g dried herb in hot water for 10 minutes. Tincture (1:5, 45% ethanol) at 2–4 mL, 3× daily. Fresh leaves can be added to salads or fruit dishes.

### Holy Basil / Tulsi

**Why grow it**: An adaptogenic herb used in Ayurvedic medicine for millennia. Tulsi reduces cortisol, modulates the HPA axis, and provides anxiolytic effects without drowsiness. Three main varieties: Krishna (purple, most potent), Rama (green, most common), and Vana (wild, tall).

**Growing tips**:
- Annual in most climates; start indoors 6–8 weeks before last frost
- Full sun; warm conditions; well-drained soil
- Pinch flower buds to encourage leafy growth
- Can be grown indoors on sunny windowsills year-round
- Harvest leaves continuously; dry for tea or use fresh

**Preparation**: Fresh leaf tea (5–10 leaves per cup) or dried herb infusion. Also available as tincture. Traditional preparation involves chewing 2–4 fresh leaves daily.

## Tier 2: Moderate Effort, Stronger Effects

### Lavender

**Why grow it**: Beyond fragrance, lavender has demonstrated anxiolytic efficacy in clinical trials. Linalool and linalyl acetate are the primary actives. Even aromatherapy with lavender oil significantly reduces anxiety scores.

**Growing tips**: Requires excellent drainage — add gravel to planting holes. Full sun (6+ hours). Prune heavily in spring; avoid cutting into old wood. Harvest flower spikes when ~50% of buds are open. English lavender (*L. angustifolia*) is most cold-hardy.

**Preparation**: Tea from dried flowers (1–2 tsp per cup). Essential oil for aromatherapy. Lavender-infused honey for culinary use.

### Passionflower

**Why grow it**: One of the most potent herbal anxiolytics. Increases brain GABA by inhibiting reuptake. Clinical trials show efficacy comparable to oxazepam for generalized anxiety, with fewer side effects.

**Growing tips**: Vigorous perennial vine — provide a trellis. Full sun to partial shade. Dies back in winter in zones 6–7; evergreen in zones 9–10. Harvest aerial parts during growing season. Some varieties produce edible fruit (maypops).
