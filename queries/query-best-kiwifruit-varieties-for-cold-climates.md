# What Are the Best Kiwifruit Varieties for Cold Climates?

**Short answer:** For Zones 3–5, the top choices are **[[query-how-do-i-grow-figs-in-cold-climates]]

*Actinidia deliciosa* (the standard brown-fuzzy kiwi) requires 600–800 chilling hours and winter temperatures no colder than about –10°C (14°F). Even in Zone 7, severe winters can cause dieback, and late spring frosts frequently destroy flowers. In Zones 3–6, winter temperatures routinely drop to –20°C to –40°C, killing fuzzy kiwi vines to the ground. Do not attempt it without a heated greenhouse.

## Hardy Kiwi (*Actinidia arguta*) — Zones 4–7

**Issai**
- **Hardiness:** Zone 5 (less cold-hardy than other *arguta* varieties)
- **Fruit:** 8–15 g, green, smooth skin. Sweet, mild flavor.
- **Ripening:** September.
- **Pollinator:** Partially self-fertile; produces more fruit with a male.
- **Notes:** The only partially self-fertile hardy kiwi. Good for small gardens where space for a male is limited. Less vigorous than other varieties—more manageable but lower yielding.

**Dumbarton Oaks**
- **Hardiness:** Zone 5
- **Fruit:** 8–14 g, green, smooth skin. Sweet, excellent flavor.
- **Ripening:** Late September to October.
- **Pollinator:** Any *A. arguta* male.
- **Notes:** Performs especially well in the Pacific Northwest. Good disease resistance.

## Arctic Kiwi (*Actinidia kolomikta*) — Zones 3–5

For growers in Zone 3 or the coldest parts of Zone 4, arctic kiwi (*A. kolomikta*) is the only kiwifruit that reliably survives. Fruit is smaller than hardy kiwi (3–8 g) but has an exceptionally intense, concentrated flavor. The vines are less vigorous and more manageable. Male *A. kolomikta* plants are famous for their striking pink, white, and green variegated leaves—one of the most ornamental vines you can grow.

### Top Arctic Kiwi Varieties

**Krupnopladnaya**
- **Hardiness:** Zone 3
- **Fruit:** 4–8 g (large for the species), green-orange skin, orange flesh. Very sweet.
- **Ripening:** Late August to early September.
- **Pollinator:** Adam or Pavingnik male.
- **Notes:** Russian selection; largest-fruited arctic kiwi available. Most productive kolomikta variety.

**September Sun**
- **Hardiness:** Zone 3
- **Fruit:** 3–6 g, green skin, orange flesh. Intense sweet-tart flavor.
- **Ripening:** September (latest of the kolomikta varieties).
- **Pollinator:** Any *A. kolomikta* male.
- **Notes:** Extends the arctic kiwi season. Good for Zone 3–4 growers who want later fruit.

**Aromatnaya**
- **Hardiness:** Zone 3
- **Fruit:** 3–5 g, green, aromatic. Very fragrant.
- **Ripening:** Mid to late August.
- **Pollinator:** Any *A. kolomikta* male.
- **Notes:** Known for exceptional aroma. Earlier ripening—good for very short growing seasons.

### Male Pollinators for Arctic Kiwi

- **Adam** — Ornamental male with the best variegated foliage (pink-white-green). Vigorous and long-blooming.
- **Pavingnik** — Hardy male with good flower production. Less ornamental but reliable.

## Hardy Kiwi vs Arctic Kiwi: Which for Your Zone?

| Factor | Hardy Kiwi (*A. arguta*) | Arctic Kiwi (*A. kolomikta*) |
|--------|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| Minimum zone | Zone 4 | Zone 3 |
| Fruit size | 8–18 g | 3–8 g |
| Vine vigor | Very vigorous (3–6 m/year) | Moderate (1.5–3 m/year) |
| Yield (mature) | 15–45 kg/vine | 3–10 kg/vine |
| Flavor | Sweet, complex, tropical notes | Very intense, concentrated |
| Ripening window | Aug–Oct (variety-dependent) | Aug–Sep |
| Ornamental foliage | Green | Males are strikingly variegated |
| Trellis requirements | Heavy-duty | Moderate |
| Ease of management | Moderate (vigorous pruning needed) | Easier (less vigorous) |
| Best for | Zones 4–7, maximum production | Zones 3–4, ornamental + fruit |

## Cold Climate Growing Tips

1. **Site selection is everything.** Plant on a north-facing slope in Zones 3–4 to delay bud break and avoid late spring frosts. Avoid frost pockets (low-lying areas where cold air settles).

2. **Protect young vines.** For the first 1–2 winters, mulch heavily (15–20 cm of straw or leaves around the base) and consider wrapping the trunk with burlap or tree wrap.
