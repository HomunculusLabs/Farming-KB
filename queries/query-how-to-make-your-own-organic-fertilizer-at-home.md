# How Do I Make My Own Organic Fertilizer at Home?

## Short Answer
You can make effective organic fertilizers at home using compost, fermented plant
extracts, fish amino acids, and mineral blends from locally available materials.
These inputs feed soil biology rather than directly feeding plants, creating
long-term fertility, healthier crops, and reduced dependency on bottled nutrients.

## Detailed Explanation

Making your own organic fertilizer is one of the most empowering steps a grower
can take. Rather than purchasing bottled nutrients, home-brewed fertilizers tap
into local waste streams and biological processes to create inputs that are often
more effective than commercial products. The
[[complete-organic-fertilizer-recipes]] collection provides baseline recipes, but
understanding the principles lets you adapt them to whatever materials are
available in your specific environment.

The foundation of any homemade fertilizer program is high-quality compost. A
well-built compost pile following the [[compost-building-guide]] provides a
balanced source of macro and micronutrients, humic acids, and a massive
inoculation of beneficial microorganisms. Finished compost can be applied as a
top dressing, mixed into potting soil, or brewed into compost tea using the
methods described in [[compost-tea-brewing]]. For cannabis growers, the
[[cannabis-organic-fertilizers]] page details which organic inputs work best at
different growth stages, from seedling through flowering.

Fermented plant extracts are a cornerstone of systems like [[bionutrients-guide]]
and Korean Natural Farming. Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) is made by macerating
fast-growing plant tips in brown sugar at a 1:1 weight ratio and letting them
ferment for seven days. The resulting liquid is a source of growth hormones,
enzymes, and soluble nutrients. The
[[bionutrients-lab-fermented-plant-juice-fpj]] guide provides step-by-step
instructions. Similarly, Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ) uses ripe fruits and
captures sugars, vitamins, and phytonutrients that boost plant metabolism during
the flowering phase when energy demands are highest.

Fish Amino Acid (FAA) is a nitrogen-rich liquid fertilizer made by fermenting
fish scraps with brown sugar at a 1:1 ratio. The [[bionutrients-lab-fish-aminos]]
protocol explains how to extract amino acids and peptides that plants can absorb
directly through foliage or roots. FAA is particularly valuable during the
vegetative stage when nitrogen demand peaks. Combined with FPJ, it provides a
complete vegetative nutrient program without any synthetic inputs. The
fermentation process breaks down fish proteins into bioavailable forms that
plants can uptake immediately, unlike raw fish emulsion which requires further
microbial decomposition.

Oriental Herbal Nutrients (OHN) is a fermented extract of aromatic herbs
including garlic, ginger, licorice, cinnamon, and angelica. The
[[bionutrients-lab-oriental-herbal-nutrients-ohn]] recipe produces a broad-
spectrum antimicrobial and growth-stimulating tonic that strengthens plant immune
systems. OHN can be added to compost teas, used as a foliar spray, or mixed into
soil drenches. The antimicrobial properties come from the allicin in garlic and
the gingerols in ginger, which help plants resist bacterial and fungal pathogens
without the need for copper or sulfur-based fungicides.

For mineral supplementation, Calcium Phosphate (CaPhos) can be made by burning
animal bones and extracting the phosphorus with natural vinegar, as detailed in
[[bionutrients-lab-calcium-phosphate-calamansi]]. This provides bioavailable
calcium and phosphorus without relying on mined rock phosphate. Combined with
wood ash for potassium, crushed eggshells for calcium, and seaweed or kelp meal
for trace minerals and natural growth hormones, a home grower can assemble a
complete nutrient profile entirely from kitchen and yard waste. The
[[cervantes-organic-mineral-amendments]] chart is an excellent reference for
matching specific mineral amendments to nutrient deficiency symptoms.

The [[cannabis-living-soil-amendment-schedule]] shows how to layer these inputs
into a living soil bed so they release gradually through microbial activity rather
than requiring frequent top-dressing. In a well-established living soil system,
amendments are only needed at the start of each grow cycle, with compost tea
applications providing supplemental feeding during peak demand periods. This
approach dramatically reduces labor and input costs compared to liquid feeding
schedules that require mixing and applying nutrients every one to three days.

## Key Points
- Compost is the foundation of homemade fertilizer, providing balanced nutrients
  plus a massive microbial inoculation for your soil
- Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) and Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ) capture growth
  hormones and soluble nutrients from plant material through lacto-fermentation
- Fish Amino Acid (FAA) provides bioavailable nitrogen during vegetative growth
  through simple sugar fermentation with fish scraps
- Oriental Herbal Nutrients (OHN) acts as a plant immune booster and can
  replace commercial pesticides with garlic and ginger antimicrobial compounds
- Mineral amendments like bone char calcium phosphate and wood ash complete the
  nutrient profile using common kitchen and yard waste materials
