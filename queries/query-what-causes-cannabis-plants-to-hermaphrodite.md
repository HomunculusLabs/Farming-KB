# What Causes Cannabis Plants to Hermaphrodite?

## Short Answer
Cannabis plants become hermaphrodites, developing both male pollen sacs and
female pistils, primarily due to environmental stress during the flowering
stage. Common triggers include light leaks during the dark period, temperature
extremes, nutrient stress, physical damage, and inconsistent watering
schedules. Some genetic predisposition also exists, with certain strains being
more prone to herming under stress than others.

## Detailed Explanation

Hermaphroditism in cannabis is a survival mechanism triggered when the plant
perceives conditions that threaten its ability to reproduce. In nature, a
female cannabis plant that senses it may not be successfully pollinated by a
male will produce male pollen sacs to self-pollinate and ensure seed
production before it dies. While this is brilliant from an evolutionary
perspective, it is catastrophic for growers seeking seedless (sinsemilla)
flowers, as even a few pollen sacs can pollinate an entire garden and fill
buds with seeds, reducing potency, flavor, and overall quality.

Light stress is the single most common cause of hermaphroditism in indoor
grows. Cannabis is photoperiodic and requires absolute darkness for 12
consecutive hours to maintain proper flowering. Even brief light
interruptions during the dark period, such as a timer malfunction, a door
opened to a lit room, a green LED on a power strip, or light leaking through
ventilation ducting, can trigger herming in susceptible genetics. The
[[cannabis-photoperiodism-and-light-cycles]] response is sensitive enough
that some studies show light levels as low as 1 to 2 lux during the dark
period can disrupt flowering hormone production. Using light-proof ducting,
sealing all light leaks with tape, and covering indicator LEDs are essential
preventive measures for every indoor grow room.

Temperature extremes are the second major trigger for hermaphroditism. When
temperatures exceed 85 degrees Fahrenheit during the day or drop below 55
degrees at night, the resulting stress can push plants to produce male
flowers. The gap between daytime high and nighttime low should not exceed 15
degrees Fahrenheit, as large temperature swings can cause condensation and
increase humidity problems that compound the stress on flowering plants. Heat
stress particularly affects plants in small, poorly ventilated grow spaces
with inadequate exhaust capacity. See [[cannabis-indoor-ventilation-setup]]
for guidance on maintaining stable temperature profiles throughout the grow
cycle.

Nutrient imbalances contribute to herming in several ways. Excessive
nitrogen during late flowering keeps plants in a vegetative mindset and can
trigger male flower development at bud sites. Conversely, severe nutrient
deficiencies or [[cannabis-nutrient-lockout-and-ph]] that starve the plant
of essential minerals create survival stress that prompts herming. [[cannabis-nutrient-burn-and-toxicity]] from over-fertilization is equally stressful and
can push sensitive plants to hermaphrodite. Maintaining a balanced feeding
schedule and monitoring [[cannabis-ph-management-and-soil-flushing]] helps
prevent nutrient-related herming. In [[cannabis-living-soil-guide|living soil]] systems, the buffered nutrient availability naturally reduces the risk
of nutrient stress compared to hydroponic or salt-based nutrient programs.

Genetics play a significant role in hermaphrodite tendency among different
cannabis varieties. Some strains are genetically predisposed to herming under
far less stress than others, making them poor choices for beginners or
growers with less-than-perfect environments. [[cannabis-breeding-basics]] that prioritize stability select against herming tendencies
over multiple generations, while less stable crosses may carry latent
hermaphrodite traits. Feminized seeds produced through chemical induction
(typically with colloidal silver or silver thiosulfate) have a slightly
higher herming rate than regular seeds because the feminization process can
introduce genetic instability. Reputable breeders test their lines
extensively and discard hermaphrodite-prone phenotypes, but some mass-market
seeds may not undergo the same rigorous selection process.

Physical stress from training techniques can also induce herming if done
excessively or at the wrong time in the plant's life cycle. High-stress
training like [[cannabis-super-cropping-technique]] should be performed
during early to mid-vegetative growth, never during flowering when the plant
is most sensitive. Rough handling, broken branches, and root damage from
transplanting late in the life cycle all contribute to stress-induced
herming. Preventive strategies include selecting stable genetics, maintaining
a consistent environment, avoiding training during flowering, and conducting
regular inspections of bud sites during weeks three through six of flowering
when pollen sacs are most likely to appear. Any sacs found should be removed
immediately with tweezers before they open and release pollen.

## Key Points
- Light leaks during the 12-hour dark period are the most common cause of
  hermaphroditism in indoor grows
- Temperature swings exceeding 15 degrees Fahrenheit or extremes above 85
  or below 55 trigger herming stress responses
- Nutrient imbalances including [[cannabis-nutrient-burn-and-toxicity]] and
  lockout contribute to herming
- Some strains have genetic predisposition; choose stable
  [[cannabis-breeding-basics|bred]] lines to minimize risk
- Inspect bud sites weekly during weeks 3 to 6 of flowering and remove any
  pollen sacs immediately with tweezers
