# How to Grow and Care for a Tamarind Tree

Growing a tamarind tree (*[[foliar-feeding]] with [[comparison-seaweed-extract-vs-fish-hydrolysate]] during flowering can improve fruit set in deficient soils.

### Pruning

Tamarind needs very little pruning:

- **First 3 years:** Remove lower branches to establish a clear trunk to 1–1.5 m. Select 3–5 well-spaced scaffold branches.
- **Annual:** Remove dead, damaged, or crossing branches. Thin the canopy if it becomes too dense (every 3–5 years).
- **Never** heavily prune a fruiting tamarind — it fruits on old wood, and aggressive cutting removes productive wood.

### Mulching

Continue annual mulching for the life of the tree. Use fallen tamarind leaves, pod husks, crop residues, or any organic material. A mature tamarind produces abundant [[dighton-fungal-decomposition-leaf-litter-dynamics]] that self-mulches to some degree.

## Step 7: Harvesting

- **First harvest:** 3–5 years after planting grafted trees; 6–8 years for seedlings.
- **When to harvest:** Pods turn from green to brown. For sweet types, harvest when pods are brown but still slightly flexible. For sour types, allow pods to dry on the tree until brittle.
- **How:** Hand-pick from lower branches. Use a long-handled pole with a hook for higher fruit. Ripe pods also fall naturally and can be collected from the ground.
- **Yield:** A mature backyard tree (15–20 years old) typically produces 50–150 kg of pods per year.

## Step 8: Processing Your Harvest

- **Fresh pulp:** Crack the shell, remove the pulp, and separate the seeds and fibers. Soak pulp in warm water for 30 minutes, then strain to make tamarind water.
- **Tamarind paste:** Boil pulp with water, strain, and simmer the liquid until thick. Store in jars in the refrigerator for months.
- **Drying:** Spread deseeded pulp on trays in sun or a food dehydrator. Dried pulp keeps for over a year in airtight containers.
- **Freezing:** Raw pulp freezes well for up to 2 years. Portion into zip-lock bags for convenient use.
