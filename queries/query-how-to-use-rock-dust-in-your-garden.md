## How to Use Rock Dust in Your Garden

## Short Answer
Rock dust is a natural soil amendment made from finely ground volcanic, glacial, or
sedimentary rocks that provides slow-release minerals and trace elements to your
garden. Apply it at 10 to 20 pounds per 100 square feet for garden beds, or mix 1
to 2 cups per cubic foot into potting soil. It works best when combined with
compost and biological inputs that help solubilize the minerals for plant uptake.

## Detailed Explanation

Rock dust, also called rock flour, stone dust, or mineral fines, is created by
crushing and grinding various types of rock into a powder or fine grit. The most
common sources are basalt (a volcanic rock rich in silica, iron, calcium, and
magnesium), glacial moraine gravel (a naturally diverse mix of minerals ground by
glaciers over millennia), and azomite (a volcanic ash deposit containing over 70
trace minerals). These materials provide a slow-release source of the major
secondary nutrients and micronutrients that are often depleted in intensively managed
soils. The [[holmgren-mineral-balance-soil-fertility]] affects [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] covers how to
integrate rock dust into a complete living soil recipe with proper particle size and
application depth.

For outdoor garden beds and food forests, rock dust can be applied as a top-dress at
10 to 20 pounds per 100 square feet, worked into the top few inches of soil.
Heavier applications (up to 40 pounds per 100 square feet) are sometimes used for
severely depleted soils, but should be split across multiple applications to avoid
temporary nutrient imbalances. Combining rock dust with [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]], rock dust forms the
mineral backbone of a complete, self-sustaining soil fertility program that improves
with each season of application.
