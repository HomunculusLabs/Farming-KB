# What Is Supercropping Cannabis and How Does It Work?

## Short Answer

Supercropping is a high-stress training (HST) technique where you deliberately pinch and bend cannabis branches until the inner tissue softens and the stem folds at a 90-degree angle.

This controlled damage triggers the plant's wound-response system, producing a knuckle of reinforced callose tissue at the bend point.

The result is a wider, more even canopy with better light distribution and increased yield potential.

Unlike topping or fimming, supercropping does not remove any plant material.

The bent branch continues growing upward from the new angle, and the knuckle becomes structurally stronger than the original stem.

## How Supercropping Works Physiologically

When you pinch a cannabis stem between your thumb and forefinger, you crush the inner cambium layer while leaving the outer epidermis intact.

The plant responds by depositing callose at the injury site, forming a visible swollen "knuckle" or joint.

This callose reinforcement makes the bent section significantly stronger than it was before.

The branch below the knuckle is now angled horizontally, which redistributes auxin hormones away from the apical tip.

Auxin redistribution causes lateral bud sites below the bend to grow more vigorously.

The [[cannabis-plant-stress-types-and-recovery]] page details how plants redirect energy after physical stress events like this.

This is fundamentally different from [[low-stress-training]], which uses gentle bending over time without causing tissue damage.

## When to Supercrop During the Grow Cycle

Supercropping should only be performed during the vegetative stage, typically when branches are 3-5mm thick.

Attempting this technique on thin seedlings or early clones will snap the stem entirely.

The ideal window is 2-4 weeks before the flip to flowering, giving the plant enough recovery time.

Most growers perform supercropping in week 3-5 of vegetative growth.

Wait until the stem feels pliable between your fingers rather than brittle.

Stems that are too young will break, while stems that are too woody will not bend cleanly.

A healthy, well-watered plant is much easier to supercrop than a dehydrated one.

## Step-by-Step Supercropping Technique

Choose a branch that is taller than the rest or growing toward the light unevenly.

Gently roll the stem between your thumb and index finger for several seconds to soften the inner tissue.

Once the stem feels pliable, slowly bend it at a 90-degree angle away from the center of the plant.

You should feel the inner tissue give way with a slight crunch, but the outer skin should remain intact.

If the stem snaps completely, use tape or a plant tie to splint it back together.

Secure the bent branch in place with soft plant ties or garden wire anchored to the pot rim.

Check the knuckle over the next few days to ensure it is healing properly.

The branch tip will naturally reorient toward the light within 3-7 days.

## Benefits of Supercropping

The primary benefit is a more level canopy that captures light more efficiently across all branches.

Secondary branches receive more light energy, producing larger colas at harvest.

The reinforced knuckles create strong structural support that can hold heavy buds without trellising.

Supercropping allows you to control plant height in spaces with limited vertical room.

It works well in combination with [[cannabis-training-techniques]] like ScrOG nets or SOG layouts.

The [[green-cannabis-topping-pruning-training]] resource includes supercropping as part of an integrated pruning strategy.

Yield increases of 10-20% are commonly reported when supercropping is done correctly.

The technique costs nothing and requires no special equipment beyond your fingers and some ties.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

The most frequent error is bending too aggressively on the first attempt, which snaps the stem completely.

Always soften the tissue by rolling first rather than trying to force an immediate fold.

Another mistake is supercropping too late in the flowering cycle, which stresses the plant during bud development.

Some growers forget to secure the bent branch, allowing it to spring back to its original position overnight.

Avoid supercropping branches that are already pointing in the direction you want them to grow.

Do not supercrop more than two branches per plant per session to limit overall stress.

Plants that are already stressed from nutrient deficiencies, pests, or environmental problems should not be supercropped.

## Key Points

- Supercropping is a high-stress training method that pinches and bends branches to create a wider canopy
- It triggers callose deposition at the bend point, forming a strong structural knuckle
- Unlike [[cannabis-topping-training]], supercropping preserves the apical growth tip
- The technique works best during weeks 3-5 of the vegetative stage on branches 3-5mm thick
- Supercropping can be combined with [[low-stress-training]] and [[lollipopping]] for maximum effect
- Always soften the stem by rolling before bending to prevent snapping
