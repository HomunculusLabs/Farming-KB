## How Do I Grow and Use Chamomile?

## Short Answer

Chamomile is one of the easiest medicinal herbs to grow and one of the most widely used in [[matricaria-chamomilla]], an annual) and Roman chamomile (a perennial), each with distinct growing requirements and medicinal profiles.

You can grow chamomile from seed in almost any well-drained soil, harvest the flowers throughout the season, and use them fresh or dried for teas, tinctures, salves, and skincare.

## Growing Conditions

Chamomile thrives in full sun to partial shade and prefers well-drained, slightly acidic to neutral soil.

It tolerates poor soils remarkably well and can even grow in sandy or rocky conditions where many other herbs struggle.

Direct sow seeds in early spring after the last frost, barely covering them with soil since they need light to germinate.

Germination typically occurs within 7-14 days when soil temperatures reach around 68°F (20°C).

Space German chamomile plants 6-12 inches apart; Roman chamomile can be planted closer at 4-6 inches for ground cover use.

## Planting and Care

Chamomile requires minimal care once established, making it ideal for beginner herb gardeners.

Water seedlings regularly until they are established, then reduce to moderate watering during dry spells.

The plant is drought-tolerant once mature and actually produces more potent essential oils under slightly stressed conditions.

Avoid over-fertilizing chamomile, as rich soils produce lush foliage at the expense of flower production.

Deadheading spent flowers encourages continuous blooming throughout the growing season.

## Medicinal Uses

Chamomile is classified as a [[digestive-herbs-and-bitters|digestive aid]], particularly for bloating and mild indigestion.

Topically, chamomile-infused oil soothes skin irritation, eczema, and minor wounds.

For comparison with other calming herbs, see [[herbal-tincture-making]] for methods.

**Infused oil** is made by steeping dried flowers in a carrier oil for several weeks, then straining for use in salves or massage oils.

**Chamomile salve** combines infused oil with beeswax for a soothing topical application for skin irritation and diaper rash.

**Bath soak** using a muslin bag of dried flowers provides a relaxing, skin-soothing bath experience.

## Companion Planting

Chamomile is an excellent companion plant in vegetable and herb gardens.

It is said to improve the flavor of neighboring plants, especially cabbage, onions, and cucumbers.

The flowers attract beneficial insects including hoverflies, parasitic wasps, and lady beetles that prey on garden pests.

Chamomile also accumulates calcium, potassium, and sulfur, which can be returned to the soil when plants are cut back and left as mulch.

It pairs well with other medicinal herbs in a dedicated [[medicinal-herb-growing]] bed.
