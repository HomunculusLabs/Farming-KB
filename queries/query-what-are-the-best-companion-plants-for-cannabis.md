# What Are the Best Companion Plants for Cannabis

## Short Answer

The best companion plants for cannabis include aromatic herbs like basil, mint, and rosemary that mask cannabis scent and deter pests; flowers like marigolds, nasturtiums, and chamomile that attract beneficial insects; and nitrogen-fixing plants like clover and beans that improve soil fertility. Companion planting reduces pest pressure, improves soil health, and can even enhance terpene profiles when practiced strategically.

## How Companion Planting Works

Companion planting works through several mechanisms that benefit cannabis plants growing nearby.

The [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] reference describes four primary mechanisms:

- **Scent masking** - aromatic plants hide cannabis from pest insects that locate hosts by smell
- **Pest deterrence** - some plants release compounds that repel specific insect pests
- **Beneficial attraction** - flowers provide nectar and pollen for predatory insects
- **Soil improvement** - certain plants fix nitrogen, accumulate minerals, or improve soil structure

The [[companion-planting-reference]] provides a comprehensive database of plant pairings and their specific benefits. When applied to cannabis cultivation, these principles can significantly reduce the need for pesticide interventions while improving overall garden health and biodiversity.

## Pest-Repelling Companions

Several aromatic herbs are particularly effective at deterring common cannabis pests.

The [[companion-planting-deterrents]] approach uses strong-smelling plants to interfere with pest insects' ability to locate cannabis plants. Many pests find their host plants by following volatile chemical signals, and a diverse aromatic garden canopy disrupts this navigation effectively.

| Companion Plant | Pests Deterred | Additional Benefits |
|---|---|---|
| Basil | Aphids, spider mites, thrips | Scent masking, culinary use |
| Rosemary | Spider mites, cabbage moths | Strong aromatic masking |
| Mint | Ants, flea beetles, aphids | Invasive, best grown in pots |
| Lavender | Moths, fleas, whiteflies | Attracts pollinators |
| Garlic | Aphids, spider mites, nematodes | Fungicidal properties |
| Lemongrass | Mosquitoes | Effective scent masking |

These aromatic companions should be interplanted throughout the cannabis garden rather than grouped in a single location. Distributed planting creates a more effective scent barrier that confuses pests across the entire growing area. Intercropping cannabis with a diverse mix of at least three to four aromatic species provides the most reliable pest deterrence.

## Beneficial Insect Attractors

Flowering companion plants attract predatory insects that naturally control cannabis pests.

The [[beneficial-insects-cannabis]] strategy relies on maintaining populations of predators that keep pest insects below damaging thresholds. These predators need nectar and pollen from flowers to sustain their adult populations, even when pest prey is scarce.

| Flower | Attracts | Target Pests Controlled |
|---|---|---|
| Marigold | Ladybugs, hoverflies | Aphids, whiteflies |
| Yarrow | Lacewings, parasitic wasps | Caterpillars, aphids |
| Dill | Ladybugs, parasitic wasps | Spider mites, caterpillars |
| Chamomile | Hoverflies, predatory wasps | Aphids, thrips |
| Sunflower | Ladybugs, bees | Aphids, whiteflies |
| Alyssum | Hoverflies, lacewings | Aphids, thrips |
| Zinnia | Ladybugs, butterflies | Various pest insects |

Maintaining blooms throughout the growing season ensures continuous food for beneficial insects. Succession planting of fast-flowering annuals like alyssum and zinnia provides nectar during periods when perennial flowers are not actively blooming.

## Soil-Improving Companions

Some companion plants improve the soil conditions that cannabis needs for healthy growth.

The [[companion-planting-with-fungi]] approach extends beyond plant-to-plant relationships to include the fungal partners that support healthy root systems. However, even without considering mycorrhizal relationships, several companion plants directly improve soil fertility.

Nitrogen-fixing companions for cannabis gardens:

- **White clover** - fixes atmospheric nitrogen, acts as living mulch between cannabis plants
- **Red clover** - deep taproot breaks compacted soil, fixes nitrogen for heavy feeders
- **Beans and peas** - legumes add nitrogen to soil through root nodule bacteria
- **Alfalfa** - deep roots mine subsoil minerals and fix nitrogen simultaneously

Dynamic accumulator companions like comfrey and borage have deep taproots that mine minerals from subsoil layers. When cut and used as mulch around cannabis, they release accumulated nutrients in a bioavailable form that feeds the cannabis plants throughout the growing season.

## Trap Cropping

Trap cropping uses sacrificial plants to draw pests away from cannabis.

The [[comparison-insectary-plants-vs-trap-crops]] analysis shows that both approaches have value in an integrated pest management strategy. Trap crops like nasturtiums (attracts aphids), radishes (attracts flea beetles), and chervil (attracts slugs) can be planted at the perimeter of the cannabis garden to intercept pests before they reach the main crop.

## Key Points

- Aromatic herbs like basil and rosemary mask cannabis scent and deter pests per [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]]
- Flowering plants like marigolds and yarrow attract [[beneficial-insects-cannabis]] that control pests
- [[companion-planting-deterrents]] work best when distributed throughout the garden, not clustered
- Nitrogen-fixing clover and dynamic accumulators like comfrey improve soil fertility for cannabis
- Trap crops like nasturtiums draw pests away from cannabis as part of [[cannabis-pest-management]]
