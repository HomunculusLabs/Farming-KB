## What Is Integrated Pest Management for Organic Gardens

## Short Answer
Integrated Pest Management (IPM) is an ecosystem-based strategy that combines
biological controls, cultural practices, mechanical methods, and targeted
organic sprays to manage pests while minimizing environmental harm. Rather than
reaching for a pesticide at the first sign of trouble, IPM uses monitoring,
prevention, and action thresholds to decide when and how to intervene.

### Step 2: Prevention
Prevention is the first and most important line of defense. Healthy plants
growing in appropriate conditions with adequate nutrition, water, and spacing
are naturally more resistant to pest pressure. Cultural prevention strategies
include selecting pest-resistant varieties, rotating crops to break pest
cycles, timing plantings to avoid peak pest emergence, and maintaining proper
sanitation by removing diseased plant debris. These foundational practices
eliminate the majority of potential pest problems before they start and should
be considered the foundation of any organic garden.

### Establishing Action Thresholds
A key concept in IPM is the action threshold, the pest population level at
which control measures must be taken to prevent unacceptable damage. This
threshold is not zero pests; some pest presence is normal and even desirable
because it maintains a food source for predators. The goal is management, not
eradication. A few aphids on a tomato plant do not warrant intervention, but a
rapidly expanding colony that is curling leaves and stunting growth does. Learn
the tolerance levels of your specific crops and act proportionally.

### The Importance of Record-Keeping
Record-keeping ties the entire IPM system together. Documenting what pests
appear, when they arrive, what conditions favor them, and which control methods
worked creates a site-specific knowledge base that improves management year
over year. Over time, patterns emerge that allow you to anticipate problems and
implement preventive measures before pests reach damaging levels, progressively
reducing the need for any intervention at all. This accumulated knowledge is
one of the most valuable assets a gardener can develop.
