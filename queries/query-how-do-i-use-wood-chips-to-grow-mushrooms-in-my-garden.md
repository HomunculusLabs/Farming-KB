## Best Mushroom Species for Wood Chip Cultivation

King Stropharia (Stropharia rugosoannulata), also known as Wine Cap, is widely regarded as the easiest and most
productive mushroom for wood chip cultivation. It fruits prolifically from spring through fall, produces large
portobello-like caps up to 8 inches across, and tolerates a wide range of conditions including partial shade, variable
moisture, and diverse wood chip compositions.

Oyster mushrooms in the genus Pleurotus are another excellent choice for wood chip beds. The [[cotter-outdoor-mushroom-garden-mulch-beds]] beds.
The chipping process naturally fractures the wood fibers and increases surface area for fungal colonization. Chips that
are 1 to 3 inches in size provide the best balance of moisture retention, air circulation, and structural stability in
the bed.

Before inoculation, wood chips should be thoroughly hydrated. The simplest method is to pile the chips in a mound or
place them in a large container, soak them with water, and let them sit for 24 to 48 hours. Drain excess water before
assembling the bed. The chips should be damp throughout but not dripping, approximately 55 to 65 percent moisture
content by weight.

Some growers pasteurize their wood chips by soaking them in hot water at 160 to 170 degrees Fahrenheit for one to two
hours. Pasteurization reduces the population of competing microorganisms and can significantly improve colonization
rates, particularly in warm weather when opportunistic fungi are most active. However, many garden-scale cultivators
skip pasteurization with good results, especially when using fresh chips and high spawn rates.

Aged wood chips that have been sitting in piles for several months can also be used but may harbor established
