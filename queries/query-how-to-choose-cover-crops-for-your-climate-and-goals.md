# How to Choose Cover Crops for Your Climate and Goals

Cover crops are one of the highest-leverage tools you can add to a rotation, but picking the wrong species for your climate or goals wastes money and field time. This page walks you through a practical decision framework.

## Common Cover Crop Cocktails (Multi-Species Mixes)

Multi-species mixes outperform monocultures in most situations. A good mix includes a grass (structure, biomass), a legume (N fixation), and often a brassica (biofumigation, soil loosening).

- **General-purpose soil builder:** cereal rye + hairy vetch + daikon radish (cool-season)
- **Summer fertility builder:** sorghum-sudangrass + cowpeas + buckwheat
- **Quick fall cover:** oats + crimson clover + radish (winter-kills, no spring termination)
- **Maximum biomass:** sorghum-sudangrass + sunn hemp + cowpeas + buckwheat (warm-season)
- **Minimal-input lawn-to-garden transition:** cereal rye + crimson clover (broadcast, mow and terminate in spring)

## Termination Methods

- **Crimping / roller-crimping:** works best on cereal rye at boot stage (anthesis) or later — crimp once, plant directly into residue. Excellent for no-till systems
- **Mowing:** effective on most species at flowering; may require multiple passes for thick stands
- **Tillage:** disc or rototill at or before flowering. Fast and thorough but destroys soil structure
- **Herbicide (glyphosate or burndown):** effective but defeats many organic/regenerative goals
- **Winter-kill:** the easiest method — plant winter-tender species (oats, buckwheat, radish) in fall and let frost do the work

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Planting too late:** cover crops need time to establish before winter dormancy. Late planting = thin stand = wasted seed
- **Skipping inoculant:** legumes without proper Rhizobium inoculant fix negligible nitrogen
- **Letting covers go to seed:** any cover crop that sets seed becomes a persistent weed. Terminate before hard seed forms
- **Using a single species when a mix is better:** monocultures are more vulnerable to pest/disease pressure and provide fewer soil benefits
- **Ignoring herbicide carryover:** many residual herbicides (especially Group 2 and Group 14) can kill or stunt cover crop seedlings — check plant-back restrictions
- **Not adjusting planting depth:** small-seeded species (clover, buckwheat) should be planted shallow (¼–½ inch); large seeds (peas, radish) can go deeper (1–2 inches)
