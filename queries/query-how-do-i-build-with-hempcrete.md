# How Do I Build with Hempcrete?

A practical FAQ-style guide for builders and owner-builders working with this carbon-negative, breathable, insulating wall material.

---

## What Is Hempcrete and What Is It Made Of?

Hempcrete is a biocomposite insulation material made from the woody core (hurd/shiv) of the hemp plant mixed with a lime-based binder and water. It is **not** load-bearing — it serves as infill for timber frames or coating over masonry. It sequesters ~110–165 kg of CO₂ per cubic meter over its lifetime.

**Key components:** Hemp hurd/shiv (chopped woody core, not fibers), lime binder (hydraulic lime + pozzolans blend), and clean potable water.

## Where Do I Source Hemp Hurd/Shiv?

- **Specialty suppliers:** American Lime Technology (US), HempFlax (EU), IsoHemp (EU)
- **Farm-direct:** Check state hemp programs and agricultural cooperatives
- **Specs:** Hurd sized 5–25 mm, moisture content below 12%, free of fiber/leaf/seed
- **Minimum order:** Typically 500 kg+; share orders with other builders to cut cost

## What Lime Binder Should I Use?

- **Proprietary binders** (best for beginners): Tradical® HF, Batichanvre® B50 — formulated for fast set, strength, and workability
- **DIY blend:** 50% NHL 3.5 (natural hydraulic lime) + 30% hydrated lime + 20% pozzolan (metakaolin or brick dust)
- **Avoid:** Pure Portland cement — too brittle, kills breathability, negates carbon benefit

## What Is the Basic Mix Ratio?

Standard **volumetric** ratio: 1 part hemp hurd : 1.5–2 parts lime binder (by weight; ~1.3–1.5 by volume) : 2–3 parts water. Aim for a mix that holds shape when squeezed but releases water slowly. Casting is wetter; spray is drier.

## What Tools Do I Need?

- **Mixing:** Forced-action pan mixer or paddle mixer (concrete mixers tangle the hurd); tarp-and-foot for small batches
- **Placement:** 24–36" screed, wooden floats, hand tampers, 5-gallon buckets
- **Formwork:** Plywood/OSB sheets, timber studs, ratchet straps, pipe clamps, poly sheeting release
- **Safety:** Rubber boots/gloves (lime is caustic), P100 dust mask, safety glasses, long sleeves
- **Spray method (optional):** Rotary pump, spray gun, compressor — higher cost but faster

## How Do I Prepare the Mix?

1. Pre-wet hemp hurd for 15–30 minutes (prevents rapid moisture absorption from binder)
2. Combine lime binder with water to create a slurry
3. Gradually add pre-wetted hurd while mixing
4. Mix 3–5 minutes until every piece is evenly coated
5. Test: grab a fistful — should hold shape and release a few drops when squeezed

## How Do I Build the Formwork?

Construct temporary formwork on both sides of the timber frame, spaced to target wall thickness. Use 300–400 mm max lift height per pour. Brace firmly (less pressure than concrete but rigidity matters). Line with poly sheeting for easy stripping. Strip after 24 hours and move to next lift. Work in continuous pours — cold joints between lifts are weak points.

## Casting vs Spraying vs Block Methods

| Method | Speed | Cost | Best For |
|--------|-------|------|----------|
| **Hand-cast** | Moderate | Low | Owner-builders, small projects, thick walls |
| **Sprayed** | Fast | High | Commercial builds, large wall areas |
| **Pre-cast blocks** | Slow upfront | Medium | Retrofit, small structures, prefab |

Hand-casting is the most common DIY approach. Blocks (300×200×100 mm) are cast in molds, cured 4–6 weeks, then laid with lime mortar.

## Curing Time and Conditions

- **Initial set:** 12–24 hours (forms can be stripped)
- **Full cure:** 6–12 months (strength develops as lime carbonates)
- Protect from freezing for first 7 days; keep above 5°C (40°F)
- Walls take 2–12 months to fully dry depending on thickness and climate
- **Never force-dry** with heaters — cracking will result

## How Thick Should Walls Be?

- **Insulation retrofit:** 100–150 mm → R-2.5–R-4
- **New-build (moderate climate):** 200–250 mm → R-5–R-6.5
- **New-build (cold/hot climate):** 300–400 mm → R-7.5–R-10

## Do I Need a Structural Frame?

**Yes.** Hempcrete is not load-bearing. Use a timber frame (post-and-beam or stud — most common), masonry infill (hempcrete as insulation coating), or hybrid systems (reciprocal/roundwood frames). Design the frame to account for 200–300 mm of hempcrete around all members.

## How Do I Handle Windows and Doors?

Frame rough openings with timber bucks sized to wall thickness. Use naturally durable timber (larch, cedar, or pressure-treated pine). Pour hempcrete around bucks, tamp firmly at edges for clean lines. Install sill flashing (hempcrete wicks moisture). Allow extra curing time around openings before installing windows/doors.

## Plastering and Finishes

- **Interior:** Lime-based plaster, 2–3 coats (rough render then fine skim)
- **Exterior:** Lime render with pozzolan for weather resistance, or lime wash
- **Never use cement renders or acrylic paints** — they trap moisture and cause deterioration
- **Compatible finishes:** Lime wash, mineral paint, clay plaster, breathable silicate paint
- Apply only after walls are dry (moisture meter below 20% in core)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Using cement as binder** — kills breathability, causes cracking, negates carbon benefit
2. **Over-tamping** — reduces insulation value (air is the insulator)
3. **Pouring too thick per lift** — max 300–400 mm per pour
4. **Not protecting from rain/frost during cure** — waterlogging and freeze-thaw damage
5. **Skipping formwork bracing** — blowouts are messy and wasteful
6. **Applying impermeable finishes** — moisture gets trapped, mold follows
7. **Underestimating drying time** — walls can take months; plan interior finishes accordingly

## Building Code Considerations

- Hempcrete is **not** in the IBC as a standalone material
- **Permitting path:** Most jurisdictions approve under "alternative materials" (IBC §104.11) or engineered design
- **Testing:** Provide ASTM-compliant compressive strength and fire resistance data (0.3–1.0 MPa, Class A / 1-hour fire rating)
- **Precedent:** Permitted hempcrete buildings exist in 40+ US states, UK, EU, Australia
- **Hire an engineer** familiar with natural building materials — invaluable for permitting

## Climate-Specific Advice

**Cold climates (Zones 3–5):** 300+ mm walls. Use thermal breaks at studs. Pair with breathable exterior weather-resistant barrier.

**Temperate climates (Zones 6–7):** 200–250 mm sufficient. Focus on airtightness at frame-to-hempcrete interface.

**Hot-humid climates (Zones 8–10):** Hempcrete's hygroscopicity buffers humidity naturally. Use 200 mm walls, good overhangs, drainage planes; never use impermeable coatings.

**Arid climates:** Protect from direct sun/wind. Mist walls during first curing week.
