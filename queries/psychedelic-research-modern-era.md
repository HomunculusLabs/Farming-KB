# Psychedelic Research — Modern Era (1966–Present)

Continuation of [[psychedelic-research-timeline]]. Covers the prohibition era, underground therapy, and the modern clinical renaissance.

## Prohibition Era (1966-1990)

- **1966 — California bans LSD:**
  First state prohibition; other states follow rapidly.

- **1967 — Summer of Love:**
  Mass LSD use in San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury.
  Psychedelic culture peaks with music, art, and anti-war activism.
  Media sensationalism focuses on "bad trips" and psychosis.

- **1968 — Staggers-Dodd Bill:**
  Adds LSD, psilocybin, and other psychedelics to federal controlled substances.

- **1970 — Controlled Substances Act:**
  Nixon signs the CSA.
  LSD, psilocybin, and MDMA placed in Schedule I —
  "no currently accepted medical use" and "high potential for abuse."
  Clinical supplies destroyed. Research funding eliminated.
  Human research becomes nearly impossible.

- **1971 — UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances:**
  Internationalizes prohibition globally.

- **1973 — MK-Ultra exposed:**
  Church Committee hearings reveal CIA experiments on unwitting subjects.
  Frank Olson's death (1953) reinvestigated.

- **1970s — Underground therapy continues:**
  Despite prohibition, therapists including Leo Zeff ("the Secret Chief")
  continue psychedelic therapy underground.
  Zeff trains dozens of practitioners across North America and Europe
  until his death in 1988.

- **1976-1980 — Underground MDMA therapy expands:**
  Therapists use MDMA successfully for PTSD,
  relationship counseling, and anxiety
  before recreational use becomes widespread.

- **1985 — MDMA scheduled as Schedule I:**
  Emergency scheduling despite objections from therapists
  who had been using it successfully for nearly a decade.

- **1986 — MAPS founded:**
  Rick Doblin founds the Multidisciplinary Association
  for Psychedelic Studies (MAPS) in response to MDMA scheduling.
  See [[psychedelic-therapy-history]].

- **1988 — Pickard and Apperson lab seized:**
  DEA estimates the lab produced 90% of the world's LSD supply.

- **1990 — Strassman DMT research:**
  Rick Strassman receives first FDA approval in decades
  for psychedelic research, studying DMT at the University of New Mexico.
  See [[dmt-reference]].

- **1992 — FDA reopens door:**
  FDA approves a study of LSD for anxiety in advanced cancer patients,
  signaling the end of the research freeze.

## Modern Renaissance (2000-Present)

The modern era of psychedelic research — from the first clinical trials through FDA Breakthrough Therapy designations, state-level legalization, and international rescheduling — is covered in detail on [[psychedelic-research-renaissance]]. Key milestones include MAPS' MDMA-PTSD trials, Johns Hopkins' landmark psilocybin studies, FDA Breakthrough Therapy designations for both MDMA and psilocybin, Oregon and Colorado legalization, and Australia's 2023 rescheduling for therapeutic use.
