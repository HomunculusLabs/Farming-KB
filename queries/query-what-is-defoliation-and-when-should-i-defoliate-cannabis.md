# What Is Defoliation and When Should I Defoliate Cannabis?

## Short Answer
Defoliation is the selective removal of leaves from a cannabis
plant to improve light penetration, airflow, and energy
distribution. When done correctly during the vegetative and early
flowering stages, it can significantly increase yield by
redirecting the plant's resources toward bud development on the
upper canopy.

## Detailed Explanation

Defoliation is one of the most debated yet widely practiced
techniques among cannabis cultivators. The core principle is
simple: by removing fan leaves that shade lower bud sites, you
allow more light to reach the developing flowers throughout the
canopy. This results in more even bud development rather than a
plant that produces one large top cola and small, underdeveloped
popcorn buds below.

The science behind defoliation relates to plant energy
allocation. Cannabis plants invest significant resources into
producing and maintaining large fan leaves. While these leaves
are essential for photosynthesis during the
[[knf-reproductive-growth-stage]]. By strategically removing leaves that are
not directly contributing to bud development, you help the plant
focus its energy where it matters most.

There are two main approaches to defoliation. The first involves
removing large fan leaves during the [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] to flower
production.
