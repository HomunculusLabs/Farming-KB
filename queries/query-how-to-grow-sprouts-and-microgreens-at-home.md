# How to Grow Sprouts and Microgreens at Home

## Short Answer
Sprouts and microgreens are young, nutrient-dense plants grown from seeds in a
compact space with minimal equipment. Sprouts are germinated seeds eaten whole,
while microgreens are harvested as young seedlings with their first true leaves.
Both can be grown indoors year-round in just one to three weeks.

## Detailed Explanation
Growing sprouts and microgreens at home is one of the fastest and most rewarding
ways to produce fresh, nutrient-dense food. Both are essentially immature plants
harvested at the earliest stages of growth, but they differ in their [[mushroom-growing-methods-comparison]]
and the parts of the plant that are consumed.

Sprouts are seeds that have been germinated in water over two to five days and are
eaten whole, including the root, stem, and underdeveloped leaves. Common sprouting
seeds include alfalfa, mung bean, lentil, radish, broccoli, and chickpea.

Microgreens are grown in a thin layer of soil or on a growing mat and are harvested
with scissors just above the soil line when they have developed their first set of
true leaves, typically seven to twenty-one days after planting.

The [[seed-starting-methods-comparison]] provides useful information on growing media
that applies to microgreen production as well.

To grow microgreens, scatter seeds densely across the surface of the moistened
growing medium. Lightly press them into contact with the soil but do not bury them.
Cover the tray with another tray or a damp towel for two to three days to create a
dark, humid environment that encourages germination.

Once the seeds have sprouted and pushed against the cover, remove it and place the
tray in a location with bright, indirect light. A sunny windowsill works, but
grow lights provide more consistent results, especially during winter months.

Water microgreens from below by setting the tray in a larger container of water and
allowing the soil to wick up moisture through the drainage holes. Top watering can
dislodge seeds and promote mold on the stems.

Harvest microgreens when they have developed their first true leaves, which are the
smaller, more detailed leaves that appear after the initial seed leaves (cotyledons).
Use clean scissors to cut the stems just above the soil line.

Popular microgreen varieties include sunflower, pea shoots, radish, broccoli,
arugula, basil, cilantro, and wheatgrass. Each has a distinct flavor profile and
growing characteristics.

Sunflower microgreens are among the easiest and most popular, with a crunchy texture
and nutty flavor. Pea shoots produce tender, sweet tendrils that are excellent in
stir-fries and salads. Radish microgreens add a spicy kick and are ready to harvest
in just seven to ten days.
