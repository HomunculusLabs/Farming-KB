# How to Increase Cannabis Yield

## Short Answer
Increasing cannabis yield requires optimizing light intensity and coverage, training
plants for an even canopy, providing adequate nutrition through the flowering stage,
maintaining ideal environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, CO2), and
harvesting at the optimal time. The single biggest yield factors are light
intensity, canopy management through training, and healthy root zone conditions.

## Detailed Explanation

Cannabis yield is determined by the intersection of genetics and environment. No
amount of technique can overcome poor genetics, and even the best genetics will
underperform in suboptimal conditions. When selecting strains for yield, look for
varieties bred for production with strong branching, moderate internode spacing, and
dense bud structure. However, once a quality strain is selected, environmental
optimization and cultivation technique account for the vast majority of yield
differences between grows. The [[cannabis-lighting-guide]]
covers light selection and positioning for optimal coverage and intensity.

Plant training is arguably the most cost-effective yield-boosting technique available
to growers. Untrained cannabis plants naturally grow in a Christmas tree shape, with
a large central cola and progressively smaller buds on lower branches. This shape is
inefficient because most indoor grow lights emit light from directly above, meaning
the lower branches receive far less light than the top. Training techniques like

Environmental optimization extends beyond light and nutrients. Carbon dioxide
supplementation at 1000 to 1500 ppm can increase yields by 20 to 30 percent when
paired with adequate light intensity, because CO2 is a raw material for
photosynthesis. Temperature control is also critical; daytime temperatures of 75 to
82 degrees Fahrenheit (24 to 28 degrees Celsius) with nighttime temperatures 8 to
10 degrees cooler optimize metabolic activity and terpene production. Humidity

## Key Points
- Light intensity and even canopy coverage are the two biggest yield factors for
  indoor growers; most growers significantly underlight their canopy
- Training techniques like LST, SCROG, and topping create an even canopy that
  maximizes light utilization across all bud sites
- Potassium and phosphorus nutrition during weeks 3 through 8 of flowering directly
  limit bud size and density
