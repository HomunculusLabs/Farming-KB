# How Do I Make Fermented Plant Extracts for Natural Fertilizer?

Fermented plant extracts (FPEs) are liquid fertilizers made by fermenting fresh plant material in sugar or brine. They concentrate the [[comparison-biodynamic-preparations-vs-biofertilizers]], and many traditional agricultural systems worldwide.

## Why Use Fermented Plant Extracts?

- **Broad spectrum nutrition:** Different plants concentrate different minerals and growth factors. Comfrey pulls potassium and silica; nettle supplies iron and nitrogen; yarrow provides copper and sulfur.
- **Biostimulant effect:** Fermentation unlocks phytohormones (auxins, cytokinins, gibberellins) and amino acids that stimulate root growth, flowering, and stress resistance.
- **Soil food web support:** The lactic acid bacteria and wild yeasts in FPEs inoculate your soil with beneficial microbes.
- **Zero waste:** Every part of the plant is used, and the process requires no heat or electricity.
- **Low cost:** Brown sugar, water, and weeds are the only inputs.

## Choosing Your Source Plant

Select based on what your plants or soil need:

| Source Plant | Key Nutrients | Best Used For |
|---|---|---|
| Comfrey (Symphytum officinale) | Potassium, silica, calcium | Flowering and fruiting stage |
| Stinging nettle (Urtica dioica) | Nitrogen, iron, magnesium | Vegetative growth boost |
| Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) | Copper, sulfur, potassium | Disease resistance, fruit set |
| Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale) | Potassium, copper, iron | Soil biology activation |
| Bamboo shoots | Silica | Stem strength and pest resistance |
| Mugwort (Artemisia vulgaris) | Minerals, volatile oils | Pest deterrent, growth stimulant |
| Horsetail (Equisetum arvense) | High silica | Fungal [[query-how-do-fungal-volatile-compounds-affect-plant-growth-and-other-microorganisms]]. Use young, vigorous growth. Avoid plants from roadsides or sprayed areas. Identify species with certainty before harvesting.

## Basic FPE Recipe (Sugar Fermentation Method)

This method uses brown sugar (unrefined, molasses content preferred) to draw out plant juices through osmotic pressure and feed lactic acid bacteria during fermentation.

### Materials

- Fresh plant material (leaves and stems, chopped): 1 part by weight
- Brown sugar or palm sugar: 1 part by weight (equal weight to plant material)
- Glass jar or ceramic vessel with loose lid or breathable cover
- Cutting board and knife

### Steps

1. **Harvest and weigh** your plant material fresh. Wash lightly if dusty but do not dry.
2. **Chop coarsely** into pieces roughly 2 to 5 cm. Smaller pieces release sap faster.
3. **Mix with sugar** in a bowl. Massage the mixture by hand for several minutes until the plant material begins to release liquid and the sugar is evenly distributed.
4. **Pack into your vessel.** Press down firmly to eliminate air pockets. Leave about 20% headspace for gas expansion.
5. **Cover loosely** with a cloth, paper towel, or lid set slightly ajar. The mixture must off-gas CO2 but exclude insects.
6. **Ferment in a cool, dark place** (18 to 24 degrees Celsius is ideal) for 7 to 14 days. Stir once daily, pressing plant material back below the liquid line.
7. **Strain the liquid** through a fine mesh or cheesecloth into a clean bottle. This is your finished extract.
8. **Store** in a cool, dark place. Properly made FPE lasts 6 to 12 months. A layer of white sediment at the bottom is normal.

## Signs of Successful Fermentation

- A pleasant, tangy, slightly sweet or sour aroma
- Bubbles rising during daily stirring (active lactic acid fermentation)
- Liquid turns amber, brownish, or deep green depending on the plant
- Plant material breaks down and softens

## Signs of Spoilage (Discard If You See These)

- Foul, putrid, or sewage-like smell
- Black or pink mold on the surface (a thin white film of kahm yeast is acceptable)
- Liquid becomes slimy or viscous in an unpleasant way

## Brine Fermentation Method (Alternative)

For plants with low moisture content or when sugar is unavailable, use a salt brine:

1. Pack chopped plant material into a vessel, filling to 75% capacity.
2. Dissolve 3 tablespoons of non-iodized sea salt in 1 liter of filtered water.
3. Pour brine over plant material until fully submerged. Use a weight to keep plants below the waterline.
4. Ferment 2 to 4 weeks. Strain and store as above.
5. **Dilution rate is lower** than sugar FPE because salt content is higher. Use 1:500 to 1:1000.

Brine FPEs tend to be richer in mineral content but lack the sugar-derived growth factors. They pair well with sugar-based FPEs in a rotation.

## How to Apply Fermented Plant Extracts

FPEs are concentrates and must always be diluted before use:

| Application | Dilution Rate | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Foliar spray | 1:500 to 1:1000 (1 to 2 ml per liter) | Early morning or late evening, every 7 to 14 days |
| Soil drench | 1:1000 (1 ml per liter) | Water into root zone, every 2 to 4 weeks |
| Seed soak | 1:500 | Soak seeds for 4 to 8 hours before planting |
| Transplant dip | 1:200 | Dip bare roots for 30 seconds before planting |
