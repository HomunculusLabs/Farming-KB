# What Is the Difference Between LST and HST for Cannabis

## Short Answer
Low Stress Training (LST) involves gently bending and tying branches to reshape
the plant without causing physical damage, while High Stress Training (HST)
deliberately injures the plant through cutting, crushing, or removing plant
tissue to trigger regrowth responses. LST is safer for beginners and works well
with autoflowers, while HST produces more dramatic structural changes but
requires more recovery time.

## Detailed Explanation
Plant training techniques are methods used to manipulate the growth pattern and
structure of cannabis plants to increase yield, improve light distribution, and
control plant height. All training methods fall into two broad categories based
on how much stress they place on the plant. Understanding the distinction
between Low Stress Training and High Stress Training is fundamental to choosing
the right approach for your grow setup, experience level, and strain genetics.

Low Stress Training is any technique that bends or repositions plant tissue
without breaking, cutting, or crushing it. The most common LST method involves
gently tying down the main stem and branches using soft plant ties, pipe
cleaners, or garden wire to create a more horizontal canopy. By bending the
tallest growth tips downward, growers expose lower branch sites to more light,
encouraging them to develop into colas rather than remain as small,
underdeveloped lower buds. This technique can begin as soon as the plant has
developed three to four nodes and can continue throughout the vegetative stage.
The [[cannabis-training-techniques-comparison]]
covers the various LST approaches in detail.

LST offers several key advantages. It is non-destructive, meaning there is
essentially no risk of killing the plant if done correctly. Recovery time is
minimal because no tissue is damaged, so growth continues uninterrupted. LST can
be applied to autoflowering strains, which have a fixed life cycle and limited
time to recover from injuries. It also works well in small grow spaces because
you can bend plants to fit confined areas rather than letting them grow upward.
The primary downside is that LST requires daily attention during the active
training period, as plants naturally try to grow upward and must be continually
repositioned.

High Stress Training techniques include topping, fimming, supercropping,
mainlining, and lollipopping. Each of these methods deliberately damages or
removes plant tissue to redirect the plant's growth hormones.
topping-cannabis|Topping involves cutting off the main growing tip above a node,
which forces the plant to develop two new main colas instead of one.
fimming|Fimming is a similar technique that cuts through the tip at a
different angle, potentially producing three to four new colas.
[[cervantes-supercropping-plant-stress-techniques]] crushes the
inner tissue of a stem without breaking the outer skin, creating a knuckle that
supports a horizontal branch position. All HST methods work on the same
principle: by removing or damaging the apical meristem, the plant's auxin
dominance is broken, and lateral branches receive the growth signal.

HST produces more dramatic results than LST but comes with greater risk and
longer recovery times. A topped plant needs roughly one to two weeks to recover
and redirect growth, during which vertical growth stops temporarily. This
recovery period needs to be factored into your vegetative schedule, especially
in photoperiod strains where you control the timing of the flowering trigger.
HST is generally not recommended for autoflowering strains because their
vegetative period is too short to recover from the stress, which can reduce
overall yield. The [[comparison-topping-vs-fimming]] and [[comparison-topping-vs-supercropping]] detail the trade-offs between specific HST methods.

Mainlining is an advanced HST technique that combines topping with LST to create
a perfectly symmetrical manifold structure. The plant is topped at the third
node, and the two resulting branches are tied horizontally to form the first
level of a manifold. Each branch is then topped again at the third node,
creating four main colas, and the process can be repeated for eight or even
sixteen colas. Mainlining requires patience and a longer vegetative period but
produces an exceptionally even canopy with uniformly sized colas.

considered one of the highest-yielding training approaches for growers with the
time and space to execute it properly.

Choosing between LST and HST depends on your growing situation. Beginners should
start with LST because it is forgiving and teaches plant anatomy without the
risk of serious damage. Autoflower growers should stick to LST exclusively.
Photoperiod growers with ample vegetative time can combine both approaches: use
HST to establish the main structure early in veg, then use LST to fine-tune
canopy shape in the weeks before flowering. The

## Key Points
- LST bends and ties branches without damage; HST cuts, crushes, or removes
  plant tissue
- LST is safe for beginners and autoflowers; HST produces more dramatic canopy
  restructuring
- Topping creates two colas from one, fimming creates three to four, and
  supercropping creates horizontal branches
- Mainlining combines topping with LST for a symmetrical manifold with uniform
  colas
- HST requires one to two weeks of recovery time and should not be used on
  autoflowers
