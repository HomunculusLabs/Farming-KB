# How to Clone Cannabis Plants Step by Step

## Short Answer
Cannabis cloning involves cutting a healthy branch from a mother plant and
encouraging it to grow roots, creating a genetically identical copy. The
process requires selecting the right cutting, preparing a rooting medium,
maintaining proper humidity and temperature, and providing care during the
rooting period of 7 to 14 days before the clone is ready for transplant.

## Detailed Explanation
Cloning is the most reliable method of [[cannabis-propagation-techniques]]
for growers who want to preserve the exact genetics of a high-performing
plant. Unlike growing from seed, where each plant expresses unique
combinations of its parents' traits, a clone is an exact genetic duplicate.
This means predictable growth patterns, consistent cannabinoid and terpene
profiles, and guaranteed female plants. The [[cannabis-cloning]] concept
page covers the biological basis for why cannabis cuttings can develop into
independent plants.

The process begins with selecting a healthy mother plant. Your mother should
be in active vegetative growth, free from pests and disease, and showing
vigorous growth. The best cuttings come from the lower branches, as these
tend to root more readily than the stiff upper growth. Cuttings should be 4
to 6 inches long with at least two nodes, and the stem should be flexible
rather than woody. The [[cannabis-cloning-and-mother-plants]] guide explains
how to maintain a dedicated mother plant for consistent clone production
over time, including lighting, nutrition, and pruning strategies.

Before taking cuttings, prepare your workspace and materials. You will need
a clean razor blade or scalpel, rooting hormone (gel, powder, or liquid), a
rooting medium such as rockwool cubes, peat pellets, or a light soil mix, a
humidity dome or propagation tray, and a clean cutting board. Sterilize all
tools with isopropyl alcohol to prevent contamination. The
[[cannabis-cloning-guide]] provides detailed recommendations for rooting
media and hormone products that have been tested across many cannabis
cultivars.

To take the cutting, make a clean 45-degree cut just below a node. The
angled cut increases the surface area for water absorption and root
formation. Immediately after cutting, dip the stem into rooting hormone and
insert it into your prepared medium. Remove the lower leaves, leaving only 2
or 3 sets at the top, and cut the remaining leaves in half horizontally to
reduce transpiration. Place the cutting in a humidity dome with vents
partially open. The [[cannabis-clone-rooting-and-transplanting]] page covers
the specific environmental parameters needed for successful rooting,
including temperature ranges of 72 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit and humidity
levels of 70 to 90 percent.

During the first 3 to 5 days, clones need very high humidity to survive
since they have no roots to uptake water. Mist the inside of the dome
regularly but avoid spraying the cuttings directly, which can promote fungal
growth. After day 5, gradually open the vents on the humidity dome over
several days to acclimate the clones to lower humidity. By day 10 to 14,
most healthy clones will have visible white root tips emerging from the
bottom of their cubes. Clones that are yellowing or drooping at this stage
may still recover, but clones showing mold or rot should be removed
immediately to prevent spread.

Rooting hormones are not strictly necessary for cannabis clones, which will
root without them, but they significantly speed up the process and improve
success rates. Clonex gel is the most widely used product among cannabis
growers. Some organic alternatives include willow water, aloe vera gel, and
fermented plant extracts. The choice of rooting medium also affects success
rates. Rockwool and peat pellets are popular for their water retention
properties, while straight perlite or a perlite-vermiculite mix provides
excellent drainage and aeration for roots.

Lighting for clones should be gentle. T5 fluorescent tubes or low-intensity
LED strips positioned 18 to 24 inches above the dome provide sufficient
light without stressing the cuttings. Avoid using high-intensity HID lights
or placing clones under strong LED panels until they have rooted and been
transplanted. A light cycle of 18 hours on and 6 hours off is standard for
vegetative clones. Once roots are 1 to 2 inches long, clones can be
transplanted into their final growing medium.

Common problems with cloning include damping off caused by overwatering,
wilt from insufficient humidity, and slow rooting from poor mother plant
health. Maintaining a clean workspace, using sterile tools and media, and
monitoring environmental conditions closely will solve most cloning issues.
With practice, success rates of 90 percent or higher are achievable for
experienced cloners.

## Key Points
- Select healthy, pest-free mother plants in active vegetative growth for
  cuttings
- Take 4 to 6 inch cuttings from lower branches at a 45-degree angle below
  a node
- Use rooting hormone and a humidity dome to maintain 70 to 90 percent
  humidity
- Keep temperatures between 72 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit with gentle
  lighting
- Transplant clones once roots reach 1 to 2 inches long after a gradual
  hardening-off period
