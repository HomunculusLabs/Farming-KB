## Step 1: Site Assessment

Before touching a shovel, read the land:

**Soil**: Get a professional test (pH, [[pioneer-plants-for-land-restoration]]. Get water right and everything else accelerates.

**Earthworks first, then biology.**

**Swales**: Dig contour swales (level ditches with downhill berms) to capture and infiltrate runoff. Space every 10–30m depending on slope. Plant berms with N-fixing pioneers.

**Ponds**: Excavate at highest feasible points for gravity irrigation.

**Deep Ripping** (if compacted): Use single-shank ripper to fracture hardpan along contour. Follow immediately with [[cover-crop]] to prevent re-compaction.

## Step 3: Soil Rehabilitation Protocol

1. **Cover crop cocktail** — Broadcast diverse seed (vetch, clover, oats, daikon radish, buckwheat) at 2–3× normal rate
2. **Organic matter blanket** — Apply 5–15cm compost, aged manure, or green waste
3. **Biochar** — Incorporate 0.5–2 kg/m² of charged biochar into top 15cm
4. **[[extending-growing-season]] to jumpstart microbes

## Step 4: Pioneer Species Selection

Pioneer species are the first wave — tough, fast-growing plants that stabilize soil, fix nitrogen, create shade, and prepare conditions for the productive species that follow.

### Nitrogen-Fixing Pioneers

| Species | Climate | Size | Special Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black locust | Zones 4–9 | Large tree | Timber, bee forage, coppice mulch |
| Alder (*Alnus* spp.) | Zones 2–7 | Large tree | Wet sites, acid soils, riparian |
| Autumn olive | Zones 3–8 | Shrub | Berries, wildlife, dry sites (check invasiveness) |
| [[query-how-do-i-graft-fruit-trees-in-a-home-orchard]] best microsites.

### Phase 3: Year 3–5 — Canopy and Support
Plant main crop trees at final spacing. Establish understory (berries, perennial veg, herbs). First harvests from early-bearing species.

### Phase 4: Year 5–10 — Succession and Thinning
Thin pioneers as crop trees expand. System begins self-mulching. First significant fruit/nut harvests.

### Phase 5: Year 10+ — Maturing Food Forest
System largely self-sustaining. Full production from all layers. Introduce shade-tolerant crops (ginseng, mushrooms, pawpaw).

## Step 6: Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Planting crop trees without soil prep | Do 1–2 years soil building first |
| Skipping water management | Install swales and ponds before planting |
| Not enough nitrogen-fixers | Plant 20–40% of total as N-fixers |
| Ignoring compaction layers | Deep rip or radish-punch before planting |
| Planting too densely or sparsely | Follow spacing guides; use temporary fillers |
| Neglecting irrigation in early years | Provide supplemental water for at least 2 years |
| Using only one pioneer species | Diversify: minimum 3 pioneer species |

## Quick-Start Checklist

- [ ] Conduct soil test and dig test pits
- [ ] Map water flow and install contour swales
- [ ] Deep rip compacted areas; broadcast cover crop
- [ ] Apply compost/mulch blanket + charged biochar + mycorrhizal inoculant
- [ ] Plant pioneer N-fixers along swale berms (20–40% density)
- [ ] Install windbreaks and set up irrigation for first 2 years
- [ ] Year 2: begin [[chop-and-drop]] of established pioneers
- [ ] Year 3: plant first crop trees in best microsites
- [ ] Year 5: thin pioneers as crop trees expand
- [ ] Year 10: transition to maintenance mode

---

## Final Thoughts

Expect 5–10 years before self-sustaining, 10–20 years for full productivity. Every month spent on soil building before planting crop trees saves years of struggle. The land wants to heal — give it water, organic matter, biology, and pioneer plants, then guide succession toward abundance.
