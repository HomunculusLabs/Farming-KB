# How Do I Start a Silvopasture System on My Property?

## Quick Answer

Starting a silvopasture system means intentionally integrating trees, forage, and livestock on the same land. The process involves: (1) assessing your site and setting goals, (2) choosing compatible tree, forage, and livestock species, (3) designing the layout, (4) planting and protecting trees, (5) establishing forage, and (6) gradually introducing livestock. Expect 3–5 years before the system is fully productive. Start small — even 1–2 acres is enough to learn.

### Step 3: Choose Your Species

**For temperate climates (USDA Zones 4–8):**
| Purpose | Trees | Forages | Livestock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast shade + forage | Honey locust, mulberry, black locust | Tall fescue, orchardgrass, white clover | Cattle, sheep |
| Nut production | Chestnut, hazelnut, pecan (Zones 6–8) | Orchardgrass, Kentucky bluegrass, birdsfoot trefoil | Cattle, sheep, pigs |
| Timber | Pine (loblolly, shortleaf), oak, black walnut | Tall fescue, bahiagrass (south), white clover | Cattle |
| Fruit + grazing | Apple, persimmon, mulberry | Orchardgrass, red clover, chicory | Sheep, chickens, geese |
**For subtropical climates (USDA Zones 8–11):**
| Purpose | Trees | Forages | Livestock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shade + fodder | Leucaena, gliricidia, [[mollison-coconut-palm-polyculture-systems]] | Brachiaria, guinea grass | Cattle, carabao |

### Step 4: Design the Layout

**Tree spacing rules of thumb:**
- **Wide spacing (15–20 ft / 4.5–6 m between trees)** — best for maintaining forage production. Start here if livestock income is your priority.
- **Moderate spacing (10–15 ft / 3–4.5 m)** — balanced tree and forage production. Good for nut/fruit trees with livestock.
- **Row system** — plant trees in rows 20–40 ft (6–12 m) apart with trees 15–30 ft (4.5–9 m) within rows. This creates alleys for equipment and livestock movement.

**Layout tips:**
- Orient tree rows east–west to maximize light on forage between rows.
- Align rows with contour on sloping land to reduce erosion.
- Leave wide alleyways (20–30 ft minimum) for equipment access and livestock movement.
- Plan paddock subdivisions to align with tree rows. Each paddock should have shade access.
- Keep water sources within 400–800 ft of every point in the grazing area.

### Step 5: Plant and Protect Trees

**Timing:** Plant bare-root seedlings in early spring (before bud break) in cold climates, or fall in mild climates. Container seedlings offer more flexibility — plant spring through early fall.
**Planting:**
1. Mark tree locations with flags following your layout plan.
2. Kill or suppress vegetation in a 3–4 ft diameter circle around each planting spot (spot spray with glyphosate or smother with cardboard + mulch).
3. Dig holes 1.5–2× the root ball diameter and no deeper than the root ball. Rough up the sides of the hole.
4. Plant the seedling at the same depth it was in the nursery. Backfill with native soil. Firm gently. Water well.
5. Apply 3–4 inches of wood chip mulch in a 3 ft diameter circle. Keep mulch 2–3 inches away from the trunk.

**Protection (critical):**
- **Cattle and horses**: Use welded wire cages (5 ft tall, 4–6 ft diameter) or individual electric fence around each tree. Cattle rub destroys young trees.
- **Sheep and goats**: Use tree tubes (4–6 ft tall) or rigid mesh cages. Goats will browse any exposed foliage and strip bark.
- **Deer**: Tree tubes with mesh guards or bud caps. Deer browsing is a major cause of seedling mortality in many areas.
- **Rodents**: Plastic tree guards or hardware cloth cylinders at the base prevent girdling by mice and rabbits.

**Protection should remain in place for 3–5 years minimum.** Remove individual guards only when the tree bark is thick enough to withstand livestock contact (typically >4 inches DBH for cattle).

### Step 6: Establish Forage

**If starting from existing pasture:**
- The existing forage may be adequate. Overseed with legumes (white clover, red clover, birdsfoot trefoil) to add nitrogen and improve forage quality.
- As tree canopy develops, shade-tolerant species will naturally increase. Monitor and overseed as needed.
- Avoid heavy nitrogen fertilization — it favors grass over legumes and creates competition with trees.

**If starting from bare ground:**
- Prepare a firm seedbed in the alleys between tree rows.
- Seed a mix of cool-season grasses and legumes suited to your region.
- Use a no-till drill if possible to minimize soil disturbance near tree roots.
- Keep livestock off the area until forage is well-established (6–12 months after seeding).

**Forage management under developing trees:**
- Mow or graze to prevent tall grass from shading young tree seedlings.
- Leave a buffer zone of 3–4 ft around each tree where you mow rather than graze during the first 2 years.
- Monitor forage composition annually. As shade increases, overseed shade-tolerant species.
