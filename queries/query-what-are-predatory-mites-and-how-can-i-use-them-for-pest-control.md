## What Are Predatory Mites?

Predatory mites belong to the family **Phytoseiidae** within the order Mesostigmata, an entirely separate lineage from the plant-feeding mites they prey upon. While pest mites such as spider mites (family Tetranychidae) damage plants by piercing leaf cells and sucking out contents — causing yellow stippling and, in severe cases, defoliation — phytoseiid mites are predators that feed on other mites and small arthropods, causing zero damage to plants.

Adult predatory mites are extremely small, typically **0.3–0.5 mm in length**, roughly the same size as or slightly larger than their prey. They are noticeably faster-moving and more agile than spider mites when observed under magnification. Under a 10× to 20× hand lens, they appear translucent to pale amber, sometimes with a slight orange or reddish tint depending on species and diet.

Unlike spider mites, predatory mites produce no webbing and leave foliage completely unharmed, making them safe to introduce at any crop stage without risk of additional damage. Their presence does not affect plant yield, quality, or appearance in any negative way.

Their life cycle consists of five stages: **egg, larva (six legs), protonymph (eight legs), deutonymph (eight legs), and adult**. Under optimal greenhouse conditions of approximately 20–25 °C with adequate humidity, many species complete development from egg to reproducing adult in just 5–7 days — comparable to or slightly faster than their prey, which is a critical biological advantage.

Adult females live 2–4 weeks, consume 5–30 pest mites per day depending on species, and lay 1–4 eggs daily when food is abundant. Eggs are typically laid on leaf undersides near leaf veins or within prey webbing, and they hatch within 2–3 days under favorable conditions. Males are generally smaller and shorter-lived than females but still contribute to prey consumption.

Key genera used in commercial biological control programs include:

- **Phytoseiulus** — specialist spider mite predators, the most aggressive feeders on *Tetranychus* species
- **Neoseiulus** (formerly within *Amblyseius*) — includes *N. californicus* and *N. cucumeris*, used against spider mites, broad mites, and thrips
- **Amblyseius** (sensu stricto) — includes *A. swirskii*, a multi-pest predator favored in warm greenhouse crops
- **Galendromus** — dry-adapted predators such as *G. occidentalis*, important in arid climates and outdoor crops
- **Typhlodromus** — includes *T. pyri*, the principal predatory mite in commercial apple and pear orchards in temperate regions
