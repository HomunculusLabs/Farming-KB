## What Is Shellac and How Is It Produced from Lac Insects?

## Quick Answer

Shellac is a natural resin secreted by the lac insect (*Kerria lacca*), a tiny magic-mushrooms-southeast-asia-pacific. The insects encase themselves in layers of this resin as they grow. After 5–6 months, farmers harvest the resin-covered branches, crush and wash the material, then refine it into shellac flakes that dissolve in alcohol to create a versatile finish, food coating, or pharmaceutical glaze.

## What Is Shellac Used For?

### Wood Finishing
- **French polish** — the legendary mirror-gloss finish on fine furniture and musical instruments
- **Sanding sealer** — thin coat under other finishes to seal wood pores
- **Knot sealer** — prevents resin bleed-through in softwoods
- **Antique restoration** — reversible with ethanol, making it ideal for conservation work

### Food and Pharmaceuticals (FDA GRAS status)
- **Confectioner's glaze** — the shiny coating on jelly beans, candy, and chocolate-covered nuts
- **Fruit coating** — shine preservative on grocery store apples
- **Enteric coating** — shellac dissolves in intestinal (alkaline) conditions but not stomach acid, making it a natural enteric coating for pills

### Other Uses
- **Phonograph records** — shellac records were the standard from the 1890s to the 1950s
- **Electrical insulation** — used for coil winding before synthetic alternatives
- **Cosmetics** — hair spray, nail polish base
- **Fireworks** — binder in pyrotechnic compositions
