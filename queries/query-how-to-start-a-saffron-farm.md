## Quick-Start Checklist

- [ ] Confirm your USDA Hardiness Zone is 6–9 (or plan for indoor/greenhouse production)
- [ ] Test soil: target sandy loam, pH 6.0–8.0, excellent drainage
- [ ] Source 50,000–70,000 corms per acre from a reputable supplier
- [ ] Prepare [[comparison-drip-irrigation-vs-passive-irrigation]]
- [ ] Plant corms 2–4 inches deep, 4–6 inches apart, late August–September
- [ ] Harvest flowers daily during the 2–3 week bloom window (October–November)
- [ ] Extract and dry stigmas within hours of picking
- [ ] Store dried saffron in airtight, light-proof containers
- [ ] File for USDA Organic Certification (if desired) — start the 3-year transition
- [ ] Develop a sales plan: direct-to-chef, online, spice distributors

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is saffron farming profitable?

Yes, with caveats. Gross revenue per acre ranges from $2,500–$75,000 depending on yield and selling price. Net margins of 30–50% are achievable once the farm is established. Saffron's high per-pound value compensates for low per-acre volume. Key profit drivers: selling direct to consumers/chefs at premium prices, maintaining high flower-to-stigma yield, and controlling labor costs during harvest.

### How much land do I need?

### Where do I buy quality corms?

- **Domestic suppliers:** Search for "saffron corms" from reputable nurseries in Zones 6–9 states (e.g., New Mexico, California, Vermont suppliers)
- **International suppliers:** Iranian, Afghan, and Spanish corm exporters — ensure phytosanitary certificates for import
- **Tip:** Buy corms that are 8–10+ grams in weight; smaller corms won't flower in year one. Inspect for mold, soft spots, and disease before planting
- **Warning:** Avoid cheap bulk listings without provenance — disease-free corms are critical

### When do I plant and harvest?

- **Planting:** Late August to mid-September. Corms must be in the ground 4–6 weeks before the first hard freeze to establish roots
- **Harvest:** Flowers bloom October through mid-November (varies by region). Each flower lasts only 1–2 days, so daily picking is essential during the 2–3 week bloom window
- **Stigma extraction:** Same day as picking — stigmas must be removed from flowers promptly
- **Drying:** Immediately after extraction. Dry at 40–50°C (104–122°F) for 12–24 hours until brittle

### How many workers do I need?

For a 1-acre farm: **6–12 workers** during the 2–3 week harvest window. Each worker can pick roughly 1,000–2,000 flowers per day. Stigma extraction is slower: expect 500–1,000 flowers per worker per hour. Outside of harvest, 1–2 people (including yourself) can manage ongoing maintenance. Many farms use seasonal labor, family, or community harvest events.

### What equipment is required?

**Essential:**
- Corm planting tool or mechanical planter
- Drip irrigation system
- Harvesting containers (shallow baskets)
- Tweezers or stigma-extraction tools
- Food dehydrator or drying oven
- Airtight storage containers (amber glass jars preferred)
- Digital scale (precision to 0.01g)

**Optional but recommended:**
- Raised bed shaper
- Soil moisture sensors
- Lightproof drying cabinet
- Packaging equipment (small sachets, tins)

## Key Takeaways

Saffron farming is a high-value, low-volume specialty crop best suited to small-acreage farmers in dry, temperate climates. The main barriers are upfront corm costs and the labor-intensive harvest window. Profitability hinges on selling at premium prices and managing the bloom-season harvest efficiently. Start small (even a 500 sq ft test plot), master the harvest and drying process, then scale based on market demand.
