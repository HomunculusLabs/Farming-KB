# What Are the Best Natural Building Methods?

## Short Answer

Natural building methods use locally sourced, minimally processed materials like earth, straw, bamboo, and timber to create structures that are healthy, affordable, and environmentally sustainable.
The most popular methods include cob, straw bale, rammed earth, adobe, hempcrete, and [[comparison-bamboo-construction-vs-timber-frame-basics]] construction.
The [[cob-building]] page covers the fundamentals of cob construction including soil selection and mixing techniques.
Cob structures are built by hand-forming thick walls that are incredibly strong, thermally massive, and [[mollison-designers-fire-resistant-landscape-design]].
A cob wall is typically 18 to 24 inches thick, providing both structural integrity and excellent thermal performance.
The [[comparison-cob-building-vs-adobe-construction]] page compares cob with the related adobe technique.
Cob buildings can be sculpted into organic, curved shapes that are difficult to achieve with conventional materials.
The thermal mass of cob walls moderates indoor temperatures, keeping spaces cool in summer and warm in winter.
This passive temperature regulation significantly reduces heating and cooling costs over the lifetime of the building.

## Straw Bale Construction

Straw bale building uses baled straw as either infill insulation or structural wall elements.
The [[mollison-designers-straw-bale-building-techniques]] page shows how Bill Mollison integrated straw bale into permaculture designs.
The [[comparison-hempcrete-vs-straw-bale-construction]] page compares straw bale with the newer hempcrete technique.
Straw is an [[fungi-in-composting-and-agricultural-waste-conversion]] product, making bale construction extremely affordable and environmentally friendly.
Buildings that use straw that would otherwise be burned or landfilled effectively sequester carbon for the life of the structure.
The [[comparison-straw-bale-vs-adobe]] page compares two of the oldest earth-building methods known to humanity.
Adobe uses sun-dried earth bricks rather than monolithic wall construction, making it more modular and transportable.
The [[comparison-cob-construction-vs-straw-bale-construction]] page helps builders navigate the choice between mass and insulation.
Earth buildings require minimal energy to manufacture since the primary material is literally under your feet.
The embodied energy of a rammed earth wall is a fraction of that of a concrete or brick wall of equivalent size.
These methods work best in climates with distinct heating and cooling cycles where thermal mass provides the most benefit.
In humid tropical climates, lighter materials with better ventilation properties may be more appropriate than heavy earth walls.

## Hempcrete and Alternative Methods

Hempcrete is a newer natural [[fungal-building-material-deterioration]] made from hemp hurd mixed with a lime binder.
The [[natural-building-materials-guide]] resource covers additional materials like light clay straw, cordwood, and earthbags.
Timber frame construction combined with natural infill materials offers the best of [[mycelium-running-rhizomorphic-mycelium-structural-strength-erosion-control]] and insulation.
The [[natural-building-overview]] page provides a decision framework for selecting the right technique.
In cold climates, straw bale or hempcrete insulation combined with thermal mass is usually the best approach.
The insulation prevents heat loss while the thermal mass stores and releases heat to maintain stable indoor temperatures.
The [[mollison-designers-lime-and-cement-natural-building]] page covers natural binders and finishes for all methods.
Consider your local soil type, as cob and rammed earth require specific clay-to-sand ratios.
A simple jar test can help you determine whether your on-site soil is suitable for earth building without importing materials.
The most sustainable choice is always the method that uses the most locally available material with the least processing.
Building with what grows or exists naturally on your site eliminates transportation costs and supports regional building traditions.
