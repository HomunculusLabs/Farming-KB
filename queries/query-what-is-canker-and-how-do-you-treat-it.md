## What Is Canker and How Do You Treat It?

## Short Answer

Canker is a localized dead area on a woody plant's bark, trunk, branch, or stem caused by fungal or bacterial pathogens that invade through wounds.
It appears as sunken, discolored, or cracked lesions that can girdle branches and kill tissue beyond the infection site.
Treatment involves pruning out infected wood, improving tree vigor, and applying preventive measures such as [[jadam-canker-leaf-spot-pesticide]] or copper-based sprays.

## What Causes Canker?

Cankers form when pathogenic fungi or bacteria colonize wounded bark tissue and kill the underlying cambium layer.
Fungal pathogens in genera such as *Nectria*, *Botryosphaeria*, *Diaporthe*, and *Cryphonectria* are among the most common causal agents.
[[cryphonectria-parasitica]], the fungus responsible for chestnut blight, is one of the most devastating canker pathogens in history.
[[canker-fungi-and-plant-galls]] describes how these pathogens interact with host tissue to form both cankers and gall-like structures.
Bacterial cankers, caused by organisms like *Pseudomonas syringae* and *Xanthomonas* species, are especially problematic in stone fruits and citrus.
[[canker-and-gall-associated-fungi]] provides detailed taxonomic information about the fungal species involved in canker formation.
Environmental stress — including drought, freeze injury, sunscald, and nutrient deficiency — predisposes trees to canker infection by weakening natural defenses.
[[biodiversity-of-fungi-foliage-pathogens-cankers-galls-sampling]] covers field sampling methods for identifying canker and gall pathogens in diverse ecosystems.

## How to Identify Canker

Canker symptoms vary by pathogen and host, but most share common visual characteristics.
Look for sunken or swollen areas of bark that may be discolored, oozing sap, or splitting open.
Fungal fruiting bodies (pycnidia, perithecia, or stroma) may appear as small bumps or discolorations on the canker surface.
Bacterial cankers often produce a characteristic gummosis — amber-colored ooze that exudes from the infected area.
Cankers on twigs and small branches cause dieback, with leaves wilting and browning beyond the infection point.
Trunk cankers are more serious, as they can girdle the tree and cut off nutrient flow to the entire canopy.
[[canker-fungi-and-gall-forming-fungi]] illustrates the morphological diversity of canker types across different host-pathogen combinations.

## Common Types of Canker

| Canker Type | Primary Pathogen | Common Hosts |
|---|---|---|
| Chestnut blight | [[cryphonectria-parasitica]] | American chestnut |
| Nectria canker | *Nectria* spp. | Maple, oak, beech, hardwoods |
| Cytospora canker | *Cytospora* spp. | Spruce, peach, cherry |
| Botryosphaeria canker | *Botryosphaeria* spp. | Apple, oak, many woody plants |
| Bacterial canker | *Pseudomonas syringae* | Stone fruits, tomato |
| Citrus canker | *Xanthomonas citri* | Citrus species |

[[bionectria]] species (related to *Nectria*) are documented canker formers on hardwood trees in temperate forests.
Anthracnose cankers, caused by various fungi, attack young twigs and branches, especially during wet spring conditions.
Perennial cankers expand slowly over multiple seasons, forming concentric callus rings around the dead center.
Annual cankers are active for only one growing season and may be walled off by host defenses if the tree is vigorous enough.

## How to Treat Canker

### Pruning and Removal

The most effective treatment is removing infected branches entirely, cutting back to healthy wood well below the visible canker margin.
Sterilize pruning tools between cuts with 70% isopropyl alcohol or a 10% bleach solution to avoid spreading the pathogen.
[[fruit-tree-pruning]] covers proper techniques for removing diseased wood while maintaining tree structure.
Prune during dry weather to reduce the risk of spreading spores into fresh wounds.
Destroy or burn removed wood — do not compost cankered material, as spores can persist and reinfect.

### Chemical and Organic Treatments

Copper-based fungicides (Bordeaux mixture, copper hydroxide) applied during dormancy help protect pruning wounds and prevent new infections.
[[jadam-canker-leaf-spot-pesticide]] offers an organic preparation specifically targeting canker and leaf spot diseases.
Dormant oil sprays suffocate overwintering spores and reduce inoculum pressure at the start of the growing season.
Biological control agents, particularly [[beneficial-fungi-trichoderma-and-mycorrhizae]], can suppress canker pathogens through mycoparasitism and competition.
[[aact-trichoderma-biocontrol-ingham]] details how actively aerated compost tea can deliver Trichoderma directly to wound sites.
Lime sulfur sprays applied during the dormant season are effective against both fungal and bacterial canker pathogens.

### Cultural Practices

Maintaining tree vigor through proper watering, mulching, and fertilization is the most important long-term prevention strategy.
Avoid wounding trunks and branches with lawnmowers, string trimmers, or improper pruning cuts.
[[fruit-tree-care-and-orchard-management]] provides comprehensive guidance on orchard practices that reduce disease pressure.
[[apple-tree-care]] addresses cultivar selection and site considerations that minimize canker risk in apple production.
Good air circulation through proper spacing and pruning speeds wound healing and reduces moisture that favors infection.

## How Plant Defenses Fight Canker

Trees are not passive victims — they activate sophisticated defense responses when pathogens breach the bark.

The hypersensitive response causes rapid cell death around the infection site, depriving the pathogen of living tissue to colonize.

## Key Points

- Cankers are localized dead areas on bark caused by fungi or bacteria entering through wounds.
- [[cryphonectria-parasitica]] caused the American chestnut's near-extinction and remains a textbook example of canker devastation.
- Pruning infected wood to healthy tissue is the primary treatment for established cankers.
- [[jadam-canker-leaf-spot-pesticide]] and copper sprays provide preventive protection during dormancy.
- Beneficial organisms like [[beneficial-fungi-trichoderma-soil-health]] suppress canker pathogens through biological competition.
- [[plant-defense-mechanisms]] and systemic signaling compounds determine whether a tree walls off or succumbs to infection.
- Environmental stress is the single biggest predisposing factor — maintain tree vigor year-round.

## What About Canker in Cannabis?

While canker is primarily associated with woody trees and shrubs, cannabis plants can also develop stem cankers under certain conditions.
Fusarium species and Botrytis cinerea can cause stem lesions that resemble woody plant cankers, especially in dense, humid grow environments.
These infections typically start at wound sites or damaged tissue and can cause the stem to weaken, split, or collapse.
[[cervantes-trichoderma-beneficial-fungi]] discusses how Trichoderma species protect cannabis stems from such opportunistic infections.
Prevention in cannabis focuses on maintaining proper humidity, avoiding stem damage during training, and ensuring good air circulation around the main stem.

## What About Canker in Citrus?

Citrus canker, caused by *Xanthomonas citri*, is a serious bacterial disease that produces raised, corky lesions on leaves, fruit, and stems.
Unlike fungal cankers, citrus canker lesions are typically surrounded by a yellow halo and appear on all above-ground parts of the tree.
[[citrus-cultivation-and-variety-guide]] addresses cultivar susceptibility differences, with grapefruit and key lime being among the most vulnerable species.
The disease spreads rapidly through wind-driven rain and requires strict sanitation and copper spray programs in commercial groves.
Regulatory agencies in many countries enforce quarantine measures to prevent the introduction and spread of citrus canker.
Windbreaks and proper tree spacing reduce the spread of bacterial inoculum by limiting rain splash and wind-blown debris.
