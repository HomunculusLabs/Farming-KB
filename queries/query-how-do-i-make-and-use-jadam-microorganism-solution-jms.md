## How Do I Make and Use JADAM Microorganism Solution (JMS)?

## Short Answer

JADAM Microorganism Solution (JMS) is a low-cost, DIY microbial inoculant that captures and multiplies indigenous soil microorganisms using leaf mold, water, and a simple aerobic brewing setup.
It forms the biological backbone of the [[jadam-organic-farming]] system developed by Youngsang Cho, providing an affordable alternative to commercial microbial products.
JMS can be applied as a soil drench, foliar spray, or seed soak to boost plant health, suppress disease, and restore degraded soil biology.

## What Is JMS and Why Does It Matter?

JMS is the core microbial input in the JADAM organic farming system, designed to be produced on-farm for virtually zero cost.
Unlike commercial microbial products that isolate specific strains, JMS cultivates the full diversity of indigenous microorganisms already adapted to your local environment.
The [[jadam-microorganism-diversity-over-disinfection]] philosophy argues that diverse, locally adapted microbes outperform single-strain commercial inoculants because they are already synchronized with the soil food web.
This approach shares roots with [[korean-natural-farming]] principles, but JADAM simplifies the process and removes expensive inputs.
The [[jadam-ultra-low-cost-philosophy]] emphasizes that effective farming should not require purchasing off-farm biological inputs.

## Ingredients and Materials

The basic JMS recipe requires only a few materials that most farmers already have on hand.

| Ingredient | Amount (per 100L) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Leaf mold (forest soil) | 1–2 kg | Source of indigenous microorganisms |
| Potato or cooked rice | 500 g | Carbohydrate food source for microbes |
| Water (non-chlorinated) | 100 L | Growing medium |
| Optional: sea salt | 1–3 g | Mineral supplement |
| Optional: leaf mold from multiple sites | mixed | Increased microbial diversity |

The leaf mold is the critical ingredient because it contains the local microorganism communities that will multiply during brewing.
The [[jadam-leaf-mold-as-ideal-soil]] concept explains why forest leaf mold is the richest source of beneficial soil microbes.
You can also produce your own leaf mold using the [[jadam-leaf-mold-source-collection-and-artificial-production]] method if natural forest access is limited.

## Step-by-Step Brewing Process

The brewing process follows the [[jadam-anaerobic-fermentation-principles]] adapted for aerobic cultivation.

**1. Prepare the leaf mold starter.**
Collect leaf mold from undisturbed forest floor — the dark, crumbly layer beneath fresh leaf litter where decomposition is actively occurring.
The [[jadam-leaf-mold-soil-method]] recommends collecting from multiple forest sites to maximize microbial diversity.
Sift the leaf mold to remove large debris, stones, and woody chunks that would not break down during brewing.

**2. Prepare the nutrient broth.**
Boil potatoes (or steam rice) until very soft, then mash them into a paste.
This carbohydrate-rich food source fuels rapid microbial reproduction during the brew cycle.
Some practitioners add a small amount of [[jadam-brown-rice-vinegar-production]] vinegar to lower pH and favor beneficial bacteria.

**3. Mix and aerate.**
Combine the leaf mold, nutrient paste, and water in a large container.
Use an air pump or stir vigorously several times daily to maintain aerobic conditions.
The [[jadam-microbe-culturing-stages-and-indicators]] page describes how to recognize each growth phase by smell, foam, and color changes.

**4. Brew for 1–3 days.**
In warm conditions (20–30°C), the brew is typically ready in 24–48 hours.
The [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]] explains how temperature affects brewing speed and microbial populations.
In cooler weather, extend the brewing time to 3–5 days and monitor for the characteristic sweet, earthy smell that indicates a successful brew.

**5. Strain and use immediately.**
Filter the finished JMS through a coarse cloth to remove solids.
JMS is most effective when applied fresh because microbial populations decline rapidly once the food source is exhausted.

## How to Apply JMS

JMS is versatile and can be integrated into nearly every stage of plant production.

**Soil drench:** Dilute JMS 1:10 to 1:50 with water and apply to the root zone.
This is the most common application, especially valuable for restoring biology to degraded or chemically treated soils.

**Foliar spray:** Dilute JMS 1:10 to 1:30 and spray onto leaves in early morning or late afternoon.
Foliar applications establish beneficial colonies that compete with pathogens on leaf surfaces.
Combine with [[jadam-herb-solution-jhs]] or [[jadam-sulfur-germicide-js]] for enhanced protection.

**Seed soak:** Soak seeds in diluted JMS (1:20) for 1–6 hours before planting to inoculate the seed coat.
This gives germinating seedlings an immediate microbial partner in the rhizosphere.

## Signs of a Successful Brew

A properly brewed batch of JMS shows several reliable indicators.

| Indicator | Good Brew | Bad Brew |
|---|---|---|
| Smell | Sweet, earthy, forest-floor aroma | Putrid, rotten, sulfurous |
| Foam | Light, creamy foam on surface | Thick colored scum or none |
| Color | Light brown to amber | Dark black or greenish |
| Temperature | Slightly warm from microbial activity | Cold (no activity) or very hot |
| Clarity | Slightly cloudy | Murky with floating debris |

The [[jadam-microorganism-solution-culturing]] guide provides troubleshooting for failed brews.
If a brew smells rotten at any point, discard it and start over.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Using chlorinated water:** Chlorine kills the microorganisms you are trying to cultivate.
Let tap water sit uncovered for 24 hours to off-gas chlorine, or use rainwater directly.

**Brewing too long:** Over-brewed JMS shifts to anaerobic conditions, producing compounds harmful to plants.
Follow the [[jadam-microbe-culturing-stages-and-indicators]] timeline closely.

**Applying in full sun:** UV radiation kills beneficial microbes on contact.
Apply JMS in the early morning or late afternoon for maximum survival.

## Key Points

JMS is a zero-cost microbial inoculant made from forest leaf mold, potato, and water brewed aerobically for 1–3 days.
The [[jadam-microorganism-solution-jms]] is the foundational biological input in the JADAM system.
Use non-chlorinated water and high-quality [[jadam-leaf-mold-source-collection-and-artificial-production]] leaf mold for best results.
Apply as a soil drench, foliar spray, or seed soak to restore soil biology and boost plant health.
Combine with [[jadam-sulfur-js]] and [[jadam-herb-solution-jhs]] for integrated pest and disease management.
Monitor brew quality using smell, foam, and color indicators from the [[jadam-microbe-culturing-stages-and-indicators]] guide.
