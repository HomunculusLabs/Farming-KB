# How Do I Make Comfrey Tea Fertilizer?

## Short Answer

Comfrey tea is a nutrient-rich [[jadam-liquid-fertilizer-nutritional-perspective]] made by steeping [[potassium-role-in-plants|potassium]] and contains moderate amounts of [[phosphorus-plant-nutrition|phosphorus]].
It is one of the easiest and most cost-effective [[hamilton-organic-fertilizers-and-plant-nutrition]] a gardener can produce at home.

## Why Comfrey Makes Great Fertilizer

Comfrey has exceptionally deep taproots that can reach six to ten feet underground.
These roots mine minerals from subsoil layers that most other plants cannot access.
The leaves concentrate these minerals, making comfrey a [[nutrient-dense-gardening|nutrient-dense]] harvests.

## Two Methods: Aerobic Tea vs Anaerobic Fermentation

There are two primary approaches to making comfrey tea, and they produce quite different results.

### Aerobic Comfrey Tea (Quick Brew)

Aerobic comfrey tea is made with aeration and is ready in one to two weeks.
Fill a bucket about halfway with freshly harvested comfrey leaves, tearing or chopping them first.
Fill the bucket the rest of the way with water, preferably dechlorinated or rainwater.
Stir the mixture daily to introduce oxygen and prevent anaerobic conditions.
Some gardeners add a small air stone or aquarium pump to keep the mixture aerobic.
The tea is ready when the leaves have broken down and the liquid turns a deep brownish-green.
Strain the liquid through a mesh screen or old pillowcase before use.

Aerobic tea smells earthy and mild, making it pleasant to work with.
It has a shorter shelf life and should be used within a day or two of straining.

### Anaerobic Comfrey Tea (Fermented)

Anaerobic comfrey tea is the traditional method and produces a more concentrated product.
Pack comfrey leaves tightly into a container, weighing them down with a rock or brick.
Add just enough water to cover the leaves, or rely on the moisture already in fresh leaves.
Seal the container loosely to allow gas to escape but minimize oxygen exposure.
Let it ferment for four to six weeks in a warm spot.

This method produces a powerful, foul-smelling liquid that gardeners often call "comfrey stew."
Despite the smell, it is one of the highest-potency homemade [[solomon-liquid-fertilizers-and-foliar-feeding]] available.
The anaerobic process is similar to [[composting-science-solomon|compost]] or [[vermicomposting|vermicompost]] activator when poured into worm bins.
