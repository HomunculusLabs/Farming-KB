# Mushroom Cultivation — Modern Era (1950–Present)

Continuation of [[mushroom-cultivation-history]]. Covers species diversification, home cultivation revolution, and the mainstream mycology movement.

## Late 20th Century: Species Diversification and Home Cultivation

- **1960s-1970s — Exotic species cultivation begins:**
  Growing interest in shiitake, oyster, and other species beyond Agaricus.
  Researchers develop cultivation methods for species
  previously only collected wild.
  This diversification creates the foundation for the gourmet mushroom industry.

- **1970s — Log cultivation formalized:**
  Traditional Asian log inoculation methods refined with modern tools
  (drill, plug spawn, wax sealing).
  Shiitake and oyster log cultivation spreads to the US and Europe.
  See [[mushroom-log-cultivation]] and [[log-cultivation-methods]].

- **1976 — Oss and Oeric (McKenna brothers):**
  [[terence-mckenna]] and [[dennis-mckenna]] publish
  Magic Mushroom Grower's Guide under pseudonyms,
  the first practical manual for Psilocybe cubensis cultivation.

- **1983 — Stamets and Chilton's The Mushroom Cultivator:**
  [[paul-stamets]] publishes The Mushroom Cultivator with J.S. Chilton —
  the definitive technical manual covering agar work, spawn production,
  substrate preparation, environmental parameters, and fruiting
  for multiple species.
  See [[mushroom-cultivation-basics]].

- **1980s-1990s — Gourmet mushroom expansion:**
  Oyster mushroom cultivation grows rapidly using straw-based methods.
  Shiitake cultivation on supplemented sawdust blocks develops.
  Market for specialty mushrooms expands in restaurants
  and natural food stores.

- **1980s-1990s — Peter Oei's international work:**
  Dutch mycologist promotes mushroom cultivation in developing nations,
  demonstrating how mushrooms can convert agricultural waste
  into food and income.
  See [[mushroom-growing-room-design]].

- **1991 — PF TEK created:**
  [[psylocybe-fanaticus]] (Robert McPherson) develops
  the Psilocybe Fanaticus Technique in Seattle, Washington.
  Uses brown rice flour and vermiculite with a dry vermiculite barrier,
  eliminating the need for a pressure cooker.
  First advertised in High Times Magazine classifieds, September 1991.
  See [[pf-tek-guide]].

- **1992 — PF TEK published:**
  Complete method published, making prepared kits obsolete.
  PF also introduced mass spore inoculation, perlite humidification,
  steam sterilization of half-pint jars,
  and the Dual Chambered Terrarium design.
  See [[pf-tek-guide]].

- **1993 — Stamets' Growing Gourmet and Medicinal Mushrooms:**
  Expanded technical reference covering cultivation of over 20 species
  including lion's mane, reishi, maitake, enoki, and pioppino.
  See [[mushroom-species-guide]] and [[specialty-mushroom-species]].

- **1996 — Stamets' Psilocybin Mushrooms of the World:**
  Identification guide and cultivation reference
  for psychoactive species worldwide.
  See [[psilocybin-mushroom-distribution]].

- **1990s — Gottlieb's cultivation guides:**
  [[richard-gottlieb]] (as O.T. Oss and Oeric) publishes
  psilocybin cultivation guides.
  See [[psilocybin-liquid-culture]] and [[psilocybin-fruiting-chambers]].

## 21st Century: Mainstream Mycology

The modern era of mushroom cultivation — from internet democratization and monotub standardization through the gourmet mushroom boom, mycoremediation applications, and social media-driven mainstream adoption — is covered in detail on [[mushroom-cultivation-21st-century]]. Key milestones include the rise of online cultivation communities, Stamets' Mycelium Running, monotub cultivation replacing PF TEK, the commercial specialty mushroom boom, and integration with living soil and regenerative agriculture practices.
