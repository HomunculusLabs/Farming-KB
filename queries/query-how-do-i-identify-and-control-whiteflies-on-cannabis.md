# How Do I Identify and Control Whiteflies on Cannabis?

## Short Answer
Whiteflies are tiny, moth-like insects that congregate on the undersides
of cannabis leaves, sucking sap and excreting honeydew that promotes sooty
mold growth. They are easily detected by gently shaking the plant and
watching for a cloud of small white flying insects. Control involves
yellow sticky traps, biological predators like Encarsia formosa wasps,
organic sprays such as neem|neem oil and insecticidal soap, and
rigorous environmental hygiene as part of a comprehensive
[[gaias-garden-host-plants-beneficial-insects]]
program.

Organic spray options for whiteflies include neem|neem oil,
insecticidal soaps, horticultural oils, and spinosad. These products must
contact the insects directly to be effective, which means thorough
coverage of leaf undersides is essential. The waxy coating on nymphs
provides some protection, so repeated applications at 5 to 7 day
intervals are usually necessary to target each new generation as it
emerges from eggs. [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] with pest-repellent
species like marigolds, nasturtiums, and alliums can help deter whitefly
populations from establishing near cannabis plants.

## Key Points
- Whiteflies are detected by the distinctive white cloud that flies up
  when plants are gently shaken
- Yellow sticky traps are the best monitoring tool and attract whiteflies
  effectively for early detection
- The parasitic wasp Encarsia formosa provides excellent biological
  control of nymphs in enclosed environments
- Contact sprays like neem|neem oil and insecticidal soap require
  thorough coverage of leaf undersides and repeat applications
- Prevention through screening, quarantine, plant inspection, and weed
  management is more effective than reactive treatment
