## Detailed Explanation
Composting is the controlled decomposition of organic materials into a stable,
humus-rich soil amendment. The fundamental principle behind successful
composting is balancing carbon and nitrogen in the right proportions. Carbon
provides energy for the decomposer organisms, while nitrogen provides the
protein they need to grow and reproduce. Too much carbon and the pile breaks
down slowly and stays cool. Too much nitrogen and the pile becomes anaerobic,
producing foul odors and losing nitrogen as ammonia gas. The ideal

## Key Points
- Aim for a 30:1 carbon-to-nitrogen ratio by weight using roughly three parts
  browns to one part greens by volume
- Build piles at least three feet on each side and maintain 50 to 60 percent
  moisture content
- Turn the pile every one to two weeks to maintain oxygen and redistribute heat
- Target internal temperatures of 130 to 160 degrees Fahrenheit for pathogen
  kill and fast decomposition
- Finished compost is dark, crumbly, and earthy-smelling with no recognizable
  ingredients
