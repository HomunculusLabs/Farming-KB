# What Is Polyculture and How Does It Differ From Monoculture?

## Short Answer
Polyculture is the practice of growing multiple crop species together in the same space and time, mimicking natural ecosystems where plant diversity creates resilience. It differs from monoculture, which grows a single crop species over a large area, by reducing pest and disease pressure, improving soil health, increasing total yield per acre, and providing economic resilience through diversification. Permaculture and organic farming systems rely heavily on polyculture principles.

### The Historical Context of Monoculture and Polyculture

Before the advent of industrial agriculture in the mid-twentieth century, most farming systems worldwide incorporated elements of polyculture. Indigenous peoples across every continent developed sophisticated intercropping systems that sustained populations for millennia without synthetic inputs. The shift to monoculture was driven by the development of synthetic fertilizers, chemical pesticides, and mechanized farm equipment that made large-scale single-crop farming economically efficient in the short term. However, this efficiency came at the cost of long-term soil degradation, increased vulnerability to pests and diseases, and dependence on external inputs. ### Polyculture in Perennial Systems

Polyculture is not limited to annual vegetables. Perennial polycultures, including food forests, [[comparison-perennial-vegetables-vs-cover-crops]] and herbs, ground covers, root crops, and climbing vines, all occupying the same space simultaneously. These systems require less annual input than vegetable polycultures and produce food over a longer season, making them an attractive option for permaculture practitioners looking to reduce maintenance while increasing total yield over decades of production.
