## Overview
Shiitake ([[lentinula-edodes]]) is one of the most rewarding mushrooms to grow at home.
It can be cultivated on hardwood logs or supplemented sawdust blocks, each method
offering distinct advantages. This query page synthesizes the key information from
multiple cultivation resources into a single practical guide.
## Choosing a Cultivation Method
The two primary approaches are log-based and sawdust-based cultivation. Log methods
produce mushrooms over several years with minimal infrastructure, while sawdust
blocks yield faster harvests but require more equipment. See
[[shiitake-log-vs-sawdust-cultivation]] for a detailed comparison of yield, cost,
and effort between the two approaches. For a comprehensive walkthrough of both
methods, refer to the [[shiitake-cultivation-guide]].
## Strain Selection
Shiitake strains fall into broad categories based on fruiting temperature:
- Wide-range strains (fruit at 10 to 25 degrees C): most forgiving for beginners.
- Warm-weather strains (fruit above 18 degrees C): suited to summer fruiting.
- Cold-weather strains (fruit below 15 degrees C): ideal for autumn and winter.
Strain choice affects incubation time, yield, and shelf life. See
[[lentinula-edodes]] for species-level taxonomy and strain lineage details.
## Spawn Preparation
Quality spawn is the foundation of a successful grow. [[grain-spawn-preparation]]
covers sterilizing grain (typically rye, millet, or wheat) in jars or bags and
inoculating with agar culture or liquid culture. [[spawn-production]] expands
this topic to include commercial-scale considerations, but the same principles
apply to home cultivation. Always work in a still-air box or flow hood to avoid
contamination during transfer.
## Log-Based Cultivation
The traditional method uses freshly cut hardwood logs (oak, beech, or maple)
inoculated with plug or sawdust spawn. Full details are in
[[shiitake-cultivation-log-method]] and the broader [[log-cultivation-methods]]
page. Key steps include:
1. **Select logs** 10 to 20 cm in diameter, cut within the past six weeks.
2. **Drill holes** in a diamond pattern, roughly 15 cm apart along rows spaced
   5 to 10 cm around the log circumference.
3. **Insert spawn** (plug spawn is tapped in; sawdust spawn is packed with an
   inoculation tool).
4. **Seal holes** with melted wax to prevent drying and contamination.
5. **Incubate** logs in a shaded, humid location for 6 to 18 months until fully
   colonized.
## Sawdust-Based Cultivation
Sawdust blocks use supplemented hardwood sawdust (often oak) with additives like
wheat bran (10 to 20 percent dry weight) and gypsum (1 to 2 percent). The
substrate is bagged, sterilized, inoculated, and incubated. This method is
faster, producing harvests in 8 to 12 weeks from inoculation. See
[[shiitake-cultivation-parameters]] for specific recipes and environmental targets.
## Incubation Conditions
During the spawn-run phase, maintain temperatures between 20 and 25 degrees C with
high humidity (above 80 percent) and adequate fresh-air exchange. For log
cultivation, stacking logs in a shaded area under shade cloth or in a wooded
section works well. For sawdust bags, a dedicated grow room or closet with a
humidifier is ideal. Darkness or low light is acceptable during incubation.
## Fruiting Triggers
Shiitake require an environmental shift to initiate pinning:
- **Cold shock**: Lowering the temperature by 5 to 10 degrees C for 24 to 72 hours.
- **Soaking**: Submerging logs or blocks in cold water for 12 to 24 hours.
- **Increased fresh-air exchange and light**: Simulating natural autumn conditions.
For logs, the soak-and-shock cycle is typically repeated every 8 to 10 weeks during
the fruiting season. Sawdust blocks may only produce two to three flushes before
exhaustion.
## Harvest Timing
Pick mushrooms when the cap is 70 to 90 percent expanded but before the veil tears
or the cap margins fully flatten. Harvest by twisting gently at the base or cutting
with a sterile knife. Prompt harvesting preserves texture and flavor. Shelf life is
extended by refrigerating immediately after picking.
## Substrate Formulas
For sawdust-based cultivation, a typical substrate formula includes:
- Hardwood sawdust (oak, beech, or alder): 75 to 85 percent dry weight.
- Wheat bran: 10 to 20 percent dry weight as a nitrogen supplement.
- Gypsum: 1 to 2 percent to buffer pH and improve texture.
- Water: adjusted to 60 to 65 percent moisture content.
Sterilize at 121 degrees C for 90 to 120 minutes in an autoclave or pressure
cooker. Allow to cool fully before inoculating in a sterile environment.
## Yields and Expectations
Log cultivation typically yields 1 to 2 kg of fresh mushrooms per log over a
fruiting lifespan of 3 to 5 years. Sawdust blocks produce 0.5 to 1.5 kg per
block over two to three flushes, with the first flush being the largest.
Biological efficiency (fresh weight harvested divided by dry substrate weight)
generally ranges from 50 to 100 percent for well-managed sawdust grows.
## Common Problems
- **Contamination**: Green mold (Trichoderma) is the most frequent issue. Maintain
  clean technique and avoid over-supplementing sawdust substrates.
- **Slow colonization**: Usually caused by low temperature, old spawn, or
  insufficient moisture in the substrate.
- **No fruiting**: Ensure proper cold shock and soaking triggers are applied. Also
  verify that full colonization has been achieved before inducing fruiting.
- **Small or deformed caps**: Often linked to inadequate fresh-air exchange or
  humidity extremes during pinning.
