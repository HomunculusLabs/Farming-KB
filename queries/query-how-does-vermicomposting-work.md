## How Does Vermicomposting Work?

## Short Answer
Vermicomposting uses earthworms, primarily red wigglers (Eisenia fetida), to
break down organic waste into nutrient-rich castings. Worms consume decaying
[[worm-ecology-soil-building]] castings are chemically distinct from the
original feedstock, with nutrients transformed into plant-available forms.
Castings typically contain 5 to 11 times more nitrogen, phosphorus, and
potassium than surrounding soil, along with significant amounts of calcium,
magnesium, and micronutrients.

widely used as a foliar spray and soil drench in [[soil-food-web]] benefits enormously from vermicomposting. Worm castings
improve soil structure by increasing aggregate stability, enhancing water
retention, and creating channels for root growth and air movement. The humic
acids and plant [[vermicomposting]] system can process the
kitchen scraps of a typical household while producing a steady supply of
premium [[compost-tea-brewing-methods-aact]], the resulting solution
becomes a potent inoculant that introduces beneficial microorganisms to both
foliage and soil. Many organic growers incorporate regular applications of
properly brewed worm tea into their plant management programs for disease
suppression and nutrient delivery.

## Key Points
- Vermicomposting uses red wiggler worms to convert organic waste into
  nutrient-dense castings
- Worm castings contain 5 to 11 times more NPK than surrounding soil and are
  rich in beneficial microbes
- The process operates at moderate temperatures, making it ideal for indoor
