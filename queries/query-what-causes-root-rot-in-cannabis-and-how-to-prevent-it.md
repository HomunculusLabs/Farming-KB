## What Causes Root Rot in Cannabis and How to Prevent It

## Short Answer
Root rot in cannabis is primarily caused by overwatering and poor drainage, which create anaerobic conditions in the root zone where pathogenic fungi and bacteria thrive. The most common culprits are Pythium and Fusarium species. Prevention focuses on proper watering practices, well-draining growing media, adequate oxygenation in hydroponic systems, and maintaining healthy root zone conditions through temperature control and beneficial biology.

### The Importance of Early Detection

Root rot is often described as a silent killer because the damage occurs underground and out of sight. By the time a grower notices above-ground symptoms like wilting, yellowing leaves, or stunted growth, the root system has often already suffered significant damage. Unlike foliar diseases that are visible and can be treated with topical sprays, root rot requires intervention at the root zone level, which is more difficult and less intuitive for many growers. Developing the habit of regularly inspecting root health, especially in hydroponic systems where roots are visible, is one of the most valuable preventive practices you can adopt. Healthy roots are the foundation of a healthy plant, and protecting them should be a primary focus of any [[mushroom-growing-methods-comparison]].

Overwatering is the number one cultural cause of root rot. In container grows, saturating the soil repeatedly drives out oxygen from pore spaces, creating the anaerobic conditions where Pythium spores germinate and spread. The fix is simple in concept but requires discipline in practice: water only when the top inch of soil is dry, ensure containers have adequate drainage holes, and never let plants sit in saucers of standing water. In hydroponic systems, root rot is less about watering frequency and more about dissolved oxygen levels and water temperature. [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] at three percent concentration diluted to roughly 3 to 5 milliliters per gallon of water can provide an immediate oxygen boost and kill Pythium spores on contact, but it also kills beneficial organisms, so it should be used as a short-term emergency measure rather than a long-term solution. In all cases, remove severely affected roots with sterilized scissors before treatment to reduce the pathogen load and encourage new healthy root growth.

### Prevention Through Biological Controls

Building a robust root zone ecosystem is the most effective long-term strategy against root rot. Inoculating growing media with mycorrhizal fungi, Bacillus subtilis, Trichoderma harzianum, and other beneficial organisms creates a competitive barrier that prevents pathogens from establishing. These products are available as dry powders to mix into soil at planting time or as liquid concentrates for drenching or hydroponic reservoirs.
