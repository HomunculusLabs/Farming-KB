## Quick Answer

To grow mushrooms on logs at home, you need: fresh hardwood logs (oak or maple
work best), [[growing-gourmet-sawdust-spawn-production-use]]

  reputable supplier (Field & Forest Products, [[fungi-perfecti]], North Spore).
- **Drill with stop collar**: 12 mm (½ in) bit for [[comparison-grain-spawn-vs-sawdust-spawn]], 8 mm bit for
  plug spawn. Set drill depth to 35 mm (1.4 in).
- **Cheese wax or food-grade paraffin**: For sealing inoculation holes and log
  ends.
- **Small crock pot**: For melting wax.
- **Hammer**: For driving plug spawn into holes.
- **Paint pen or metal tags**: For labeling logs with species and date.

**Total startup cost**: $50–$150 for enough spawn to inoculate 10–20 logs,
plus the cost of tools if you don't already own them.

### Step 8: Force Fruiting

Once fully colonized, you can force a [[mushroom-flush]]:

1. **Soak the log** in cold water (10–15 °C / 50–59 °F) for 12–24 hours
2. **Stand the log upright** in a shady, humid fruiting area
3. **Mist daily** to maintain high humidity
4. **Expect mushrooms** in 7–14 days
5. **Harvest** when caps are 70–80% expanded but not fully flat

After harvesting a flush, let the log rest for 6–10 weeks before forcing
again. Logs can typically produce 2–4 flushes per year.
