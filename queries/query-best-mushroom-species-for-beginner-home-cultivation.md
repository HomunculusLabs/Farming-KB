## 4. Lion's Mane (*Hericium erinaceus*)

Striking cascading white spines, prized for seafood-like flavor and nootropic compounds.

**Pros:**
- Unique appearance; grows on same sawdust blocks as shiitake
- Increasing market demand driven by health and wellness interest
- Excellent culinary versatility — crab or lobster substitute
- Tolerates cooler fruiting temperatures (60–70°F)

**Cons:**
- Slower colonizer than oysters; fruits are fragile and bruise easily
- Requires higher humidity (85–95%) for proper spine formation
- Spawn can be harder to source than oyster or shiitake

**Equipment:** Pressure cooker (sawdust blocks), fruiting chamber with humidifier, hygrometer.
**Substrate Options:** Hardwood sawdust (oak, beech, maple) + 5–10% wheat or rice bran.

## 5. King Oyster (*Pleurotus eryngii*)

Largest oyster species, producing thick-stemmed, portobello-like caps prized in fine cooking.

**Pros:**
- Thick, meaty texture holds up to grilling, roasting, and sautéing
- Relatively easy using standard oyster methods; long shelf life (up to 2 weeks)
- Cooler fruiting range (55–65°F) suits basements and garages
- Fewer strain choices simplifies purchasing

**Cons:**
- Struggles in warm climates; needs good FAE for thick stems
