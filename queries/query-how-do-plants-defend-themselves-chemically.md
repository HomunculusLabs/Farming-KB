# How Do Plants Defend Themselves Chemically?

A practical FAQ for growers and gardeners on the invisible chemical arsenal
that keeps plants alive — and how you can work with it.

## What Are Chemical Defenses and Why Do Plants Need Them?

Plants are sessile — they can't run from herbivores, pathogens, or stress.
Instead, they evolved **[[caffeine]] (coffee), solanine (nightshade leaves).
- **Terpenes** — Volatile oils behind strong aromas. Mint, lavender, and
  rosemary produce terpenes that repel insects. Pyrethrins from chrysanthemums
  are natural insecticides used commercially.
- **Phenolics** — Compounds like tannins and lignin that make tissue tough and
  unpalatable. Salicylic acid (related to aspirin) triggers immune responses.
- **Glucosinolates** — Found in Brassicas (cabbage, broccoli, mustard). When
  tissue is damaged, enzymes convert them into pungent isothiocyanates toxic to
  many pests.
- **Saponins** — Soap-like compounds in quinoa and legumes that disrupt cell
  membranes in fungi and insects, providing antifungal protection.
- **Cyanogenic glycosides** — Release hydrogen cyanide when tissue is crushed.
  Found in cassava and apple seeds; lethal to herbivores in sufficient doses.

## Constitutive vs. Induced Defenses

- **Constitutive defenses** are always present — a standing army. Thorns, bark,
  and baseline alkaloid levels are ready at all times.
- **Induced defenses** activate after an attack. When a plant detects herbivore
  saliva or pathogen enzymes, it ramps up defensive compounds within hours.

Think of constitutive defenses as the lock on your door and induced defenses as
the alarm that activates after a break-in.

## How Does Stress Trigger Defense Production?

Plants detect attack through **elicitors** — signals from herbivore saliva,
pathogen walls, or compounds from wounded cells. This triggers hormone cascades:

- **Jasmonic acid** — primary hormone for anti-herbivore responses
- **Salicylic acid** — primary hormone for pathogen responses
- **Ethylene** — modulates both pathways

Environmental stresses like drought and UV exposure can also **prime** these
pathways, making future defenses stronger and faster — called *defense priming*.

## Practical Tips: Encouraging Natural Defenses

- **Allow mild stress** — Light drought or controlled herbivory primes defenses.
  Don't overwater or over-fertilize.
- **Use [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] form symbioses with over 80% of plant species. Beyond
[[query-how-do-plants-use-chemical-signals-to-defend-against-insects]] through the common mycorrhizal
network, prompting neighbors to pre-activate defenses — a process called "plant
eavesdropping." This is one of the strongest arguments for maintaining healthy
soil fungal populations.

## Key Takeaways

- Plants use diverse chemical compounds for defense — alkaloids, terpenes,
  phenolics, glucosinolates, saponins, and cyanogenic glycosides
- Defenses are always-on (constitutive) or attack-activated (induced)
- Domestication reduced defenses in most crop plants
- Companion planting works through real chemical mechanisms
- Support natural defenses via soil health, silicon, mycorrhizae, and smart
  stress management
