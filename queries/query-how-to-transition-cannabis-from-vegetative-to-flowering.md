# How to Transition Cannabis from Vegetative to Flowering

## Short Answer

Transitioning photoperiod cannabis plants into flowering requires switching the light cycle from 18 hours on and 6 hours off (18/6) to 12 hours on and 12 hours off (12/12). This change in day length triggers hormonal shifts that begin the flowering process, typically showing visible signs within one to two weeks. Plan for plants to double or triple in height during the flowering stretch.

## Understanding the Light Trigger

For photoperiod cannabis strains (the vast majority of regular and feminized seeds), flowering is triggered not by plant size or age, but by the duration of uninterrupted darkness. When the dark period reaches 12 hours or longer, the plant begins producing flowering hormones called florigens.

The [[cannabis-photoperiodism-and-light-cycles]] mechanism is precise and unforgiving. Even a few minutes of light interruption during the dark period can delay or disrupt flowering. Complete darkness during the 12-hour off period is non-negotiable for a successful transition.

Common sources of light leaks that can disrupt flowering:

- Equipment indicator LEDs on fans, timers, or power strips
- Gaps under doors or around window seals
- Streetlights or car headlights shining through windows
- Greenhouse coverings that do not fully block ambient light

Covering all indicator lights with black tape and ensuring complete light-tightness of the grow space prevents the most common flowering problems.

## Timing the Transition

The timing of the flip depends on the grower's goals, grow space constraints, and the strain being grown.

| Grow Style | Vegetative Time | Expected Final Height |
|---|---|---|
| Small tent (3-4ft) | 3 to 4 weeks | 2 to 3 feet |
| Medium tent (5-6ft) | 4 to 6 weeks | 3 to 4 feet |
| Large room | 6 to 8 weeks | 4 to 6 feet |
| Sea of Green | 1 to 2 weeks | 1 to 2 feet |

A common guideline is that plants will roughly double or triple in height during the flowering stretch, so the flip should occur when plants are about one-third to one-half of the desired final height. For [[cannabis-vegetative-stage-management]], this means considering grow space height, light distance from canopy, and pot size when deciding when to transition.

Outdoor growers have less control over timing, as flowering is triggered naturally by the shortening days of late summer and early autumn.

## The Stretch Phase

In the first one to two weeks after switching to 12/12, plants enter a rapid stretching phase where the [[cannabis-vegetative-stage]] energy redirects toward floral development.

During this period, internode spacing increases and the plant grows quickly upward. This stretch is most pronounced in sativa-dominant strains, which can triple in height, while indica-dominant varieties typically double.

Management during the stretch phase:

- Install trellis netting or stakes before the flip to support developing colas
- Lower grow lights gradually to maintain proper distance from the rising canopy
- Monitor for [[cannabis-pre-flowering-and-sexing]] to identify males early
- Begin reducing nitrogen while increasing phosphorus and potassium
- Avoid high-stress training during the first two weeks of 12/12

## Sexing and Hermaphrodite Prevention

The transition period is when [[cannabis-pre-flowering-and-sexing]] becomes critical for growers using regular seeds.

Female plants begin developing white pistils (hairs) at branch nodes, while male plants produce small pollen sacs. Growers must identify and remove males during this window to prevent pollination of the entire crop. Male plants can be identified by their distinct pre-flowers at nodes, which are round and lack the white hairs of female pre-flowers.

Even with feminized seeds, monitoring for [[cannabis-hermaphrodite-prevention-and-management]] is essential. Stress during transition from light leaks, temperature extremes, or nutrient problems can trigger some plants to develop both male and female organs.

Signs of hermaphroditism to watch for:

- Yellow banana-shaped growths (anthers) emerging from female flowers
- Pollen sacs appearing at branch nodes alongside pistils
- Unusual swelling or growth on calyxes that were previously normal

## Nutrient Transition

During the transition, nutrient needs shift significantly as the plant redirects energy from vegetative growth to flower production.

The [[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]] requires higher levels of phosphorus and potassium to support flower development, while nitrogen should be gradually reduced. Overfeeding nitrogen during early flowering can delay bud formation and result in excessively leafy flowers.

A typical transition feeding schedule:

- Week 1 of 12/12: Begin reducing nitrogen by 25 percent
- Week 2: Increase phosphorus and potassium by 25 to 50 percent
- Week 3 onward: Full flowering nutrient profile with minimal nitrogen

## Environmental Conditions

Environmental conditions during the transition are critical for success.

Temperature should remain between 70 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit during the day and drop 10 to 15 degrees at night. This diurnal variation encourages terpene production and helps prevent excessive stretching.

Humidity should be gradually reduced from around 60 percent during vegetative growth to 45 to 55 percent during early flowering. This reduces the risk of bud rot and mold as dense flowers begin to develop.

## Key Points

- Switch from 18/6 to 12/12 to trigger flowering via [[cannabis-photoperiodism-and-light-cycles]]
- Plan for plants to double or triple in height during the post-flip stretch phase
- Monitor for males and hermaphrodites during [[cannabis-pre-flowering-and-sexing]]
- Reduce nitrogen and increase phosphorus and potassium for [[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]]
- Maintain complete darkness during the 12-hour off period to prevent re-vegging or hermies
