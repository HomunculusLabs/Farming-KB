# What Is the Difference Between Perlite and Vermiculite?

## Short Answer
Perlite and vermiculite are both mined minerals used as soil amendments to improve aeration and moisture retention, but they behave quite differently.
Perlite improves drainage and aeration while retaining little water itself, making it ideal for plants that prefer drier conditions.

The expansion process for perlite involves heating crude ore to approximately 1600 degrees Fahrenheit, causing trapped water to vaporize and expand the material.
Vermiculite is heated to a similar temperature, causing it to exfoliate into accordion-like particles with high water-holding capacity.

In hydroponic systems, perlite is commonly used alone or mixed with other inert media to provide root support and aeration for plant growth.
Vermiculite is less commonly used in hydroponics because its high water retention can lead to waterlogging in recirculating systems.
