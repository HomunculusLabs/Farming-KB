# What Is Botrytis Cinerea (Gray Mold) and How Do I Manage It?

[[botrytis-cinerea]] is one of the most widespread and destructive fungal pathogens in agriculture, attacking hundreds of plant species including cannabis, grapes, strawberries, and tomatoes. Its signature symptom is a fuzzy gray-brown coating of spores on flowers, fruit, and dying tissue. Understanding its life cycle and environmental triggers is essential for prevention — see [[bloomfield-fungal-pathogens-and-parasites]] for broader fungal pathogen ecology.

---

## Short Answer

Botrytis cinerea causes gray mold (also called bud rot on cannabis and bunch rot on grapes). It thrives in cool, humid conditions (60–75°F, 90%+ humidity) and spreads rapidly through airborne spores. Prevention centers on air circulation, humidity control, and canopy management, while organic treatments include [[trichoderma]] biocontrol agents and [[neem-tree-entity|neem oil]] sprays. In wine grapes, a specific strain causes the prized "noble rot" ([[vitis-vinifera]]) that concentrates sugars for dessert wines.

---

## Identifying Botrytis / Gray Mold

### Visual Symptoms
- A gray-brown fuzzy layer of spores on flowers, buds, or fruit surfaces.
- On cannabis: [[bud-rot-cannabis]] begins inside dense buds, turning brown and mushy before the gray spore coating appears externally.
- On grapes: berries turn brown and split, leaking juice — see [[vitis-vinifera]] and [[grape-cultivation-and-variety-guide]] for cultivar susceptibility.
- On strawberries and soft fruit: rapid soft rot with characteristic gray fuzz.

### Environmental Triggers
- **Temperature**: 60–75°F (15–24°C) is the optimal range for spore germination and mycelial growth.
- **Humidity**: Above 90% relative humidity, especially with stagnant air and wet plant surfaces.
- **Plant stress**: Wounded tissue, overcrowded canopies, and nitrogen-excess growth are prime infection points.
- [[cervantes-humidity-control-dehumidification]] details humidity management for indoor growing environments.

### Life Cycle Overview
Spores (conidia) are produced in massive quantities on infected tissue and dispersed by wind, water splash, and human contact. They germinate on wet plant surfaces within hours, penetrating through wounds, natural openings, or directly through the cuticle. The fungus then produces enzymes that macerate plant tissue, creating the soft rot symptoms. For detailed fungal life cycle biology, see [[rust-fungi-life-cycle]] for comparison with other pathogenic fungi.

## Prevention Strategies

### Environmental Control
- **Reduce humidity** below 60% during flowering and fruiting stages using dehumidifiers and exhaust fans.
- **Increase air circulation** with oscillating fans — stagnant air pockets are infection hotspots.
- [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] covers mushroom-specific air management applicable to all indoor crops.
- **Space plants properly** to allow airflow between individuals and through the canopy.
- **Defoliate or prune** dense foliage to improve light penetration and airflow in cannabis canopies.

### Cultural Practices
- **Remove infected material immediately** — do not compost Botrytis-infected plant tissue, as spores survive composting.
- **Water at the base** of plants, avoiding wetting flowers and foliage.
- **Sanitize tools and hands** between plants to prevent mechanical spore transmission.
- **Avoid late nitrogen applications** that produce soft, succulent growth especially vulnerable to infection.
- **Harvest on time** — overripe fruit and over-mature buds are far more susceptible.

### Resistant Varieties
- Choose open-canopy grape cultivars (loose bunches resist Botrytis better than tight clusters).
- Select cannabis strains with less dense bud structure for humid climates.
- See [[vitis-labrusca]], [[vitis-riparia]], and [[vitis-rotundifolia]] for North American grape species with varying disease resistance profiles.

## Organic and Biological Controls

### Biocontrol Agents
- **[[trichoderma]]** species ([[trichoderma-harzianum]], [[trichoderma-virens]]) are the most studied biocontrol agents against Botrytis — they compete for space and produce antifungal metabolites.
- **[[bacillus-subtilis]]** produces lipopeptide antibiotics that inhibit Botrytis spore germination.
- [[singh-trichoderma-biocontrol-mechanisms-applications]] and [[beneficial-fungi-trichoderma-soil-health]] detail application protocols and mechanisms.

### Organic Sprays
- **Neem oil** ([[neem-oil-cannabis]], [[cervantes-neem-oil-organic-pest-control]]) disrupts spore germination and mycelial growth.
- **Potassium bicarbonate** (baking soda-based) creates an alkaline surface unfavorable to fungal growth.
- **Copper and sulfur fungicides** ([[cervantes-chemical-fungicides-insecticides]]) are traditional organic options with precautions.
- [[jadam-pesticide-for-fungal-diseases]] and [[jadam-fungal-disease-management]] cover JADAM approaches using fermented plant extracts.

### Compost Tea and Microbial Sprays
- [[ingram-aact-fungal-disease-suppression-fusarium-rhizoctonia-anthracnose|Ingham's AACT research]] demonstrates how actively aerated compost tea shifts leaf surface microbiology against pathogens.
- Foliar applications of diverse microbial communities outcompete Botrytis for leaf surface niche space.

## Noble Rot: When Botrytis Is Desirable

In viticulture, Botrytis cinerea can be beneficial under very specific conditions. "Noble rot" ([[vitis-vinifera]]) pierces grape skins, causing water evaporation that concentrates sugars, acids, and flavors. This produces the world's finest dessert wines — Sauternes (France), Trockenbeerenauslese (Germany), and Tokaji (Hungary). The conditions must alternate between humid mornings (for infection) and dry afternoons (for evaporation). See [[home-winemaking-and-country-brewing]] and [[mead-and-honey-wine-brewing-guide]] for fermentation context.

## Key Points

- [[botrytis-cinerea]] attacks 200+ plant species and is the most economically important post-harvest pathogen worldwide.
- Prevention through humidity control and air circulation is far more effective than treatment after infection.
- [[trichoderma]] and [[bacillus-subtilis]] are the two most effective organic biocontrol agents for gray mold.
- [[bud-rot-cannabis]] is the cannabis-specific manifestation and requires vigilance during the final weeks of flowering.
- Noble rot on [[vitis-vinifera]] demonstrates that the same pathogen can be either devastating or highly valuable depending on context and control.
- [[cervantes-gray-mold-botrytis-prevention]] provides cannabis-specific prevention protocols for indoor and outdoor grows.
- Infected plant material should be removed and destroyed, never composted, as Botrytis spores survive standard composting temperatures.
