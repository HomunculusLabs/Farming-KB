# How Do I Control Pests Without Chemicals?

## Short Answer

Non-chemical pest control relies on [[soil-building-ecological-garden]] resilience through biodiversity, habitat creation for [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]], cultural practices like crop rotation and [[comparison-companion-planting-reference-vs-jadam-monoculture-critique]], and targeted biological and botanical sprays only when pest populations exceed acceptable thresholds. The most effective approach is Integrated Pest Management (IPM), a systematic strategy that combines multiple methods and only intervenes when pest damage reaches a threshold.

## The IPM Framework

The [[insectary-plants-for-pest-control]] guide explains how specific plants attract predators.

Key beneficial insects and what they eat:

| Beneficial Insect | Prey | Attracted By |
|---|---|---|
| Ladybugs | Aphids, mealybugs, scale | Dill, fennel, yarrow |
| Lacewings | Aphids, thrips, whiteflies | Dill, cosmos, sunflowers |
| Hoverfly larvae | Aphids, mealybugs | Dill, alyssum, buckwheat |
| Parasitic wasps | Caterpillars, whiteflies | Dill, parsley, mint |
| Ground beetles | Slugs, cutworms | Clover, mulch, perennial plantings |

Plant families that attract beneficials:
- **Apiaceae** - dill, fennel, cilantro, parsley, yarrow
- **Asteraceae** - sunflowers, cosmos, marigolds, zinnias
- **Lamiaceae** - mint, basil, rosemary, lavender, sage

- A diverse planting that blooms throughout the growing season ensures continuous food and habitat for predators.

## Companion Planting for Pest Deterrence

The [[knf-pest-control-methods]] include several natural preparations:

- **[[bionutrients-lab-fermented-plant-juice-fpj]] (FPJ)** from pest-repellent plants
- **Neem-based sprays** for broad-spectrum insect deterrence
- **[[knf-indigenous-microorganism-culture]] (IMO) solutions** to boost plant immunity
- **Fish amino acid (FAA)** to strengthen plant cell walls

KNF pest control focuses on creating unfavorable surface conditions on plants that deter feeding and egg-laying, rather than directly killing insects.

## JADAM Pest Management

The [[jadam-insect-pest-management]] system emphasizes that healthy plants naturally resist pests, and that pesticide applications should be targeted and minimal. Use locally available, free materials: ferment plant material in water, add canola oil as a surfactant, dilute and spray on affected plants.

## Physical Barriers and Exclusion

Physical methods are highly effective and chemical-free:

| Method | Best For | How It Works |
|---|---|---|
| Floating row covers | Brassicas, seedlings | Physical barrier excludes insects |
| Insect netting | Fruit trees, berries | Prevents egg-laying and feeding |
| Copper tape | Slugs and snails | Delivers electric shock on contact |
| Sticky traps | Fungus gnats, whiteflies | Captures flying insects |
| Pheromone traps | Codling moths, beetles | Lures and traps specific pests |
| Diatomaceous earth | Slugs, ants, soft-bodied insects | Abrasive particles damage exoskeletons |

Hand-picking large insects like tomato hornworms is effective for small gardens.

## Biological Controls

Living organisms provide targeted pest management:

| Biocontrol | Target Pest | Application |
|---|---|---|
| [[cervantes-bacillus-thuringiensis-bt-control]] (Bt) | Caterpillars | Foliar spray, ingested by larvae |
| Predatory mites (Phytoseiulus) | Spider mites | Release on affected plants |
| Entomopathogenic nematodes | Soil-dwelling larvae (grubs) | Soil drench |
| Trichoderma spp. | Soil-borne fungi | Soil inoculant |
| Beauveria bassiana | Whiteflies, thrips, beetles | Foliar spray |

These tools target specific pests while leaving beneficial organisms unharmed.
