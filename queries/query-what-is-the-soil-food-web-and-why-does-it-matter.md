Tillage is one of the most damaging practices for the soil food web because it physically breaks apart fungal networks, disrupts earthworm burrows, and exposes soil organisms to desiccation and UV radiation. No-till and reduced-till gardening methods preserve fungal networks and soil structure, leading to increasingly productive soils over time. ### The Rhizosphere: Where Plants and Soil Biology Meet

The rhizosphere is the narrow zone of soil immediately surrounding plant roots, and it is the most biologically active zone in the entire soil profile. Root exudates, the mix of sugars, amino acids, organic acids, and other compounds that roots release into the soil, attract and feed specific microbial communities. In return, these microorganisms solubilize minerals, fix nitrogen, produce growth hormones, and protect roots from pathogens. This root-microbe partnership is the foundation of plant nutrition in natural systems.

Plants can actually modify their exudate profile to recruit specific microbial helpers when needed.
