# How to Make Fish Hydrolysate Fertilizer

## Short Answer
Fish hydrolysate is a cold-processed liquid fertilizer made by enzymatically digesting
fish waste into a nutrient-rich slurry. Unlike fish emulsion, which uses heat and removes
oils, hydrolysate retains all the beneficial proteins, amino acids, oils, and
micronutrients. It provides a fast-acting nitrogen source along with a full spectrum of
trace minerals that feed both plants and soil microbes.

## Detailed Explanation

Fish hydrolysate is one of the most potent organic fertilizers available to home gardeners
and farmers. The key distinction between hydrolysate and fish emulsion lies in the
production method. Fish emulsion is cooked at high temperatures, which breaks down proteins
but also destroys many beneficial compounds and strips out the oils. Hydrolysate, by
contrast, uses a cold enzymatic process that preserves the full range of amino acids,
enzymes, fatty acids, and vitamins naturally present in fish waste. This makes it a far
superior product for building soil biology and delivering complete plant nutrition, as
covered in the [[bionutrients-guide]].

### Sourcing Raw Materials

The raw material for fish hydrolysate is fish waste: heads, bones, guts, scales, and any
offal from processing. You can source this from local fish markets, seafood processors,
or your own catch. Fresh is best, but frozen waste works too if fully thawed before
processing. Avoid using fish that have been sitting out for days, as putrefaction
introduces harmful bacteria that compete with the beneficial fermentation process. The
goal is enzymatic digestion, not rotting, though some lactobacillus fermentation is part
of the traditional approach. Lean fish like cod, tilapia, and salmon scraps work well.
Shellfish waste can also be included for added calcium and chitin, which stimulates
beneficial microbial activity.

### Two Methods for Making Fish Hydrolysate

There are two main methods for making fish hydrolysate at home. The first is the enzyme
method: you blend fish waste with water and add a proteolytic enzyme such as bromelain
(from pineapple), papain (from papaya), or a commercial enzyme blend. Maintain the
mixture at around 100 to 120 degrees Fahrenheit for 24 to 48 hours while stirring
occasionally. The enzymes break down proteins into amino acids and small peptides,
creating a bioavailable nutrient solution.

The second method is the lactobacillus fermentation method, which is more aligned with
[[query-how-to-make-korean-natural-farming-inputs]]. In this approach, you blend fish
waste with an equal volume of water, add a lactobacillus serum (such as homemade LAB
serum from rice wash water), and optionally add brown sugar or molasses at about 5 to 10
percent of the total volume to feed the microbes. Ferment for 2 to 4 weeks in a
ventilated container, stirring daily to release gases. This method is more accessible for
most home growers since it does not require purchasing specialty enzymes.

### Straining and Storage

After processing, strain the liquid through a mesh screen to remove large bone fragments
and scales. The resulting liquid is your fish hydrolysate. It should have a strong fishy
smell but not a putrid, rotten odor. Store it in a sealed container in a cool, dark place.
With proper fermentation and the addition of sugar as a preservative, it can last 6 to 12
months. Dilute before use at a ratio of roughly 1 tablespoon per gallon of water for soil
drenches, or 1 teaspoon per gallon for foliar sprays. Fish hydrolysate is compatible with
many other organic inputs, making it a versatile addition to your fertility program as
described in [[query-how-to-make-your-own-organic-fertilizer-at-home]].

### Plant and Soil Benefits

Fish hydrolysate provides immediately available nitrogen in the form of amino acids and
peptides that plants can absorb directly through roots and leaves. This is particularly
valuable during periods of rapid vegetative growth when plants have high nitrogen demands.
The omega-3 fatty acids and fish oils in hydrolysate also serve as food for soil fungi,
supporting the fungal communities that are essential for nutrient cycling and disease
suppression. This aligns with the nutrient cycling principles discussed in
[[teaming-with-nutrients-organic-fertilizer-types-and-sources]].

### Application in the Garden

In the garden, fish hydrolysate can be applied as a soil drench every 1 to 2 weeks during
the growing season. It works especially well when combined with compost tea applications,
as the microbial activity in compost tea helps further break down and distribute the
nutrients. See [[query-how-to-make-compost-tea-for-my-plants]] for details on brewing
compost tea to pair with your hydrolysate applications. Avoid spraying in direct sunlight
or at temperatures above 85 degrees Fahrenheit, as this can burn leaf tissue. Early
morning or late evening applications are ideal for foliar feeding.

### Use in Cannabis Cultivation

For cannabis growers, fish hydrolysate is an excellent organic nitrogen source during the
vegetative stage. It provides a broad spectrum of nutrients that support vigorous growth
without the salt buildup associated with synthetic fertilizers. Many organic cannabis
cultivators rely on fish hydrolysate as a core input in their living soil programs, as
outlined in [[cannabis-organic-fertilizers]]. Taper off applications 2 to 3 weeks before
the flip to flowering to allow plants to transition their nutrient demands, as excess
nitrogen during early flowering can delay bud development.

### Practical Considerations

One important note: fish hydrolysate can attract animals if applied as a soil drench near
the surface. Water it in well after application, or incorporate it into your irrigation
system. If odor is a concern, you can add a small amount of kelp meal or humic acid to the
mixture, which helps buffer the smell while adding additional micronutrients. Some growers
also add a few drops of essential oil like peppermint or clove to mask the odor during
application. Always test on a small area first when using a new batch, as nutrient
concentrations can vary depending on the fish species and processing method used.

## Key Points
- Fish hydrolysate is cold-processed using enzymes or lactobacillus fermentation,
  preserving amino acids, oils, and trace minerals that heat-processed fish emulsion
  destroys
- Raw fish waste (heads, bones, guts) is blended with water and either commercial enzymes
  or a lactobacillus serum plus molasses, then digested over 1 to 4 weeks
- Dilute at 1 tablespoon per gallon for soil drenches and 1 teaspoon per gallon for
  foliar sprays; apply every 1 to 2 weeks during active growth
- The amino acids and fatty acids in hydrolysate feed both plants and beneficial soil
  microbes, supporting the fungal communities that are critical to
  [[query-how-to-build-a-compost-pile-that-works]] and overall soil health
- Store in a sealed container in a cool, dark place for 6 to 12 months; proper
  fermentation with sugar preserves stability and prevents putrefaction
