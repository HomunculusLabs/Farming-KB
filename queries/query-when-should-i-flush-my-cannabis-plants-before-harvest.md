# When Should I Flush My Cannabis Plants Before Harvest?

## Short Answer
Flushing cannabis involves giving plants only plain water for the final one to
two weeks before harvest. The goal is to allow the plant to use up stored
nutrients in its tissues, which many growers believe produces a smoother,
cleaner-tasting final product. The practice is most relevant in synthetic
nutrient programs and less critical in living soil or organic systems.

## Detailed Explanation

Flushing is one of the most debated topics in cannabis cultivation, yet it
remains a standard practice for most growers using bottled or mineral-based
nutrients. The basic concept is straightforward: by stopping all nutrient inputs
and providing only plain water during the final phase of flowering, the plant is
forced to consume the nutrients already stored in its leaves, stems, and buds.
This theoretically reduces the mineral content in the finished flower, leading
to a better smoking or vaping experience.

The standard recommendation for synthetic nutrient programs is to begin flushing
approximately 10 to 14 days before your intended harvest date. This window
allows enough time for the plant to metabolize most of the residual nitrogen and
other mobile nutrients that can contribute to a harsh, chemical taste or cause
the ash to burn black rather than white. Growers who use heavier feeding
schedules or higher nutrient concentrations may benefit from a longer flush
period of up to two weeks, while lighter feeders may only need seven to ten
days.

Timing the flush correctly depends on understanding your plant's flowering
timeline and being able to read [[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]]. Most indica-dominant strains finish in 8 to 9 weeks of flowering,
while sativa-dominant strains may need 10 to 14 weeks. You should begin your
flush based on the expected finish date for your specific
[[cannabis-strain-guide-reference]], adjusting if the plant is maturing
faster or slower than anticipated.

The [[cannabis-photoperiod-flowering-cycle]] plays a crucial role in
determining when to flush. During the final two weeks, trichomes begin shifting
from clear to cloudy, and some will start turning amber. This is the window
where flushing has the greatest impact. Pay close attention to the
[[cannabis-terpene-and-aroma-chemistry-clarke]] during this
period; many growers notice that the aroma becomes more complex and refined as
the flush progresses, suggesting that the plant is reallocating resources toward
resin production rather than vegetative processes.

For growers using living soil or organic methods, flushing is often unnecessary
or even counterproductive. In a [[cannabis-living-soil-amendment-schedule]], the microbial community regulates nutrient availability
naturally, and the soil itself acts as a buffer. Plants in these systems
typically have access to a steady, gentle supply of nutrients that does not lead
to the mineral buildup that flushing aims to correct. Many experienced organic
growers argue that their plants are effectively in a constant state of slow
flushing due to the balanced biology of the soil.

Synthetic nutrient programs, by contrast, deliver minerals in a form that is
immediately available to the plant but can accumulate in the growing medium over
time. This accumulation is particularly problematic in
[[query-dwc-vs-coco-coir-vs-soil-cannabis-growing]] and hydroponic
systems, where there is no soil buffer to absorb excess salts. In these setups,
flushing is especially important for removing residual mineral salts that would
otherwise end up in the final product.

The [[cannabis-nutrient-deficiency-and-toxicity-guide]] of your
plants going into the flush period matters significantly. If your plants are
already showing signs of nutrient deficiency before the flush begins, you may
want to skip or shorten the flush to avoid stressing the plant further.
Conversely, if plants show signs of
[[cannabis-nutrient-burn-and-toxicity]], a longer
flush with more frequent watering may be beneficial to help leach excess salts
from the root zone.

Water quality during the flush is important. Use the same quality water you
would normally use for feeding; if you typically adjust your water's pH,
continue doing so during the flush. The
[[cannabis-ph-management-and-soil-flushing]] page covers the
importance of maintaining proper pH even during the plain-water phase, as pH
lockout can prevent the plant from accessing the very nutrients you want it to
consume. For growers in containers, flushing with a volume of water equal to two
to three times the pot volume can help remove any residual salt buildup from the
growing medium.

Several visual indicators suggest your flush is progressing well. First, fan
leaves should gradually lighten in color from the bottom of the plant upward.
Second, the [[cannabis-terpene-and-aroma-chemistry-clarke]] should become more
pronounced and complex as the plant reallocates resources to resin production.
Third, buds should continue to swell and develop even without added nutrients,
drawing on the plant's internal reserves. Finally, the ash from a test sample
should burn lighter and more evenly compared to unflushed material.

## Key Points
- Start flushing 7 to 14 days before harvest when using synthetic nutrients
- Time the flush based on your [[cannabis-photoperiod-flowering-cycle]] and expected harvest date
- Watch [[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]] to refine your harvest timing
- Living soil and organic growers typically do not need to flush their plants
- Monitor leaf fade and adjust flush duration based on [[cannabis-nutrient-deficiency-and-toxicity-guide]]
- Maintain proper [[cannabis-ph-management-and-soil-flushing]] even during the plain-water flush phase
- Coco coir and hydroponic growers benefit most from a thorough pre-harvest flush
