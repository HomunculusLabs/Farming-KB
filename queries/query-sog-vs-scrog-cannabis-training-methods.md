# What is the Difference Between SOG and SCROG for Cannabis?

## Short Answer
SOG (Sea of Green) uses many small plants grown close together for rapid
turnover and high yields per square foot, while SCROG (Screen of Green) uses
fewer plants trained through a horizontal screen to create an even, flat
canopy that maximizes light exposure to every bud site. The best method
depends on your space, plant count limits, and experience level.

## Detailed Explanation
SOG and SCROG are two of the most popular [[cannabis-sea-of-green-sog-guide]] covers the specific
plant counts, container sizes, and timing that work best for this method.

SOG is popular with commercial growers because the fast turnover means more
harvests per year and the uniform plant size simplifies workflow. Each plant
produces one main cola, and the even canopy means no light is wasted on
lower branches that would otherwise be shaded. For growers without plant
count restrictions who want maximum productivity per square foot, SOG is
hard to beat in terms of efficiency and ease of execution.

SCROG, by contrast, uses a horizontal screen or net (typically poultry wire
or trellis netting) suspended above the plants. As plants grow through the
screen, growers weave branches back and forth to create an even, flat
canopy. This method typically uses 1 to 4 plants per square meter, making it
ideal for growers with legal plant count restrictions. The [[cannabis-scrog-and-sog-methods]] comparison shows
that yield potential is similar between the two methods when executed well,
but the resource requirements differ significantly. SOG demands more clones,
more pots, and more soil or growing medium upfront, while SCROG requires a
longer time investment before the first harvest and more hands-on training
during vegetative growth.

Lighting plays a crucial role in both methods. Because both techniques create
an even canopy, they work exceptionally well with directional light sources
like LEDs and HID fixtures. The goal is to maintain uniform distance between
the light and all bud sites, which both SOG and SCROG accomplish through
different means. SOG achieves this naturally through plant density and short
stature, while SCROG achieves it through physical training. Both methods

## Key Points
- SOG uses many small plants with short veg times for fast harvest cycles
- SCROG uses fewer plants trained through a screen for an even canopy

  harvests
