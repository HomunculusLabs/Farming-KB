# What is the Difference Between SOG and SCROG for Cannabis?

## Short Answer
SOG (Sea of Green) uses many small plants grown close together for rapid
turnover and high yields per square foot, while SCROG (Screen of Green) uses
fewer plants trained through a horizontal screen to create an even, flat
canopy that maximizes light exposure to every bud site. The best method
depends on your space, plant count limits, and experience level.

## Detailed Explanation
SOG and SCROG are two of the most popular [[water-in-permaculture]]
used by indoor growers to maximize yield and light efficiency. Though they
share similar names and goals, they approach the problem from opposite
directions: SOG relies on quantity of plants while SCROG relies on training
individual plants to fill the canopy.

In a SOG setup, growers use a large number of small plants, typically 16 to
64 per square meter, depending on pot size. Plants are usually started from
clones for uniformity and given only a short vegetative period of 1 to 2
weeks before being switched to flowering. This approach takes advantage of
the fact that cannabis naturally grows one large central cola. By crowding
many plants together, the canopy fills in quickly and each plant produces a
single main bud. The [[cannabis-sea-of-green-sog-guide]] covers the specific
plant counts, container sizes, and timing that work best for this method.

SOG is popular with commercial growers because the fast turnover means more
harvests per year and the uniform plant size simplifies workflow. Each plant
produces one main cola, and the even canopy means no light is wasted on
lower branches that would otherwise be shaded. For growers without plant
count restrictions who want maximum productivity per square foot, SOG is
hard to beat in terms of efficiency and ease of execution.

SCROG, by contrast, uses a horizontal screen or net (typically poultry wire
or trellis netting) suspended above the plants. As plants grow through the
screen, growers weave branches back and forth to create an even, flat
canopy. This method typically uses 1 to 4 plants per square meter, making it
ideal for growers with legal plant count restrictions. The [[screen-of-green]]
technique spreads out each plant's branches so that every bud site receives
direct light, which can produce larger individual colas than SOG.

The tradeoff with SCROG is that it requires a longer vegetative period,
often 4 to 8 weeks, and daily attention during the training phase to tuck
and weave branches. The [[cannabis-scrog-and-sog-methods]] comparison shows
that yield potential is similar between the two methods when executed well,
but the resource requirements differ significantly. SOG demands more clones,
more pots, and more soil or growing medium upfront, while SCROG requires a
longer time investment before the first harvest and more hands-on training
during vegetative growth.

Lighting plays a crucial role in both methods. Because both techniques create
an even canopy, they work exceptionally well with directional light sources
like LEDs and HID fixtures. The goal is to maintain uniform distance between
the light and all bud sites, which both SOG and SCROG accomplish through
different means. SOG achieves this naturally through plant density and short
stature, while SCROG achieves it through physical training. Both methods
benefit from the principles outlined in [[cannabis-yield-optimization]],
which emphasizes that light penetration and even canopy distribution are the
primary drivers of yield per watt.

From a practical standpoint, SOG is generally considered easier for
beginners because it requires less training and maintenance during the grow.
However, it does require a reliable source of clones since starting from seed
would introduce unwanted variability in plant height and structure. SCROG
has a steeper learning curve because improper training can lead to uneven
canopies, reduced airflow, and pest or disease issues. But once mastered,
SCROG offers more control over plant shape and can produce truly impressive
yields from just a handful of plants.

Both methods benefit from proper pruning. In SOG, lower branches are often
removed entirely since they will be shaded by the canopy above, redirecting
the plant's energy to the main cola. In SCROG, selective defoliation beneath
the screen helps improve airflow and reduces the risk of mold in the dense
canopy. Understanding plant training fundamentals through
[[water-in-permaculture]] will help you execute either method
successfully.

## Key Points
- SOG uses many small plants with short veg times for fast harvest cycles
- SCROG uses fewer plants trained through a screen for an even canopy
- SOG is better for growers with no plant count limits who want rapid
  harvests
- SCROG is ideal when legal plant counts are restricted or when maximizing
  per-plant yield
- Both methods aim to create an even canopy that maximizes light
  efficiency and [[cannabis-yield-optimization]]
