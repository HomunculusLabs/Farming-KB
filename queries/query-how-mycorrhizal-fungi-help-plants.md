## How Do Mycorrhizal Fungi Help Plants Grow?

## Short Answer

Mycorrhizal fungi form a symbiotic relationship with plant roots, extending a vast network of fungal hyphae into the soil that dramatically increases the root system's effective surface area. In exchange for carbohydrates from the plant, the fungi deliver water and nutrients, especially phosphorus and micronutrients, that would otherwise be inaccessible. This partnership is found in over 80% of terrestrial plant species.

## Drought Resistance

Mycorrhizal fungi provide significant drought resistance benefits:

- Hyphae access water from micropores too small for roots to penetrate
- Mycorrhizal plants maintain turgor and photosynthesis at lower soil moisture
- The fungal sheath around roots reduces water loss
- Improved soil aggregation from glomalin (a fungal protein) increases water retention

Research findings:
- Mycorrhizal plants use 20-30% less water than non-mycorrhizal plants under drought
- Yield reductions under drought stress are significantly less in mycorrhizal crops
- The effect is most pronounced in sandy, well-drained soils

## Disease Suppression

The disease suppression benefits of mycorrhizal associations are well documented.

Mechanisms include:
- Changes to root exudate profiles that deter pathogens
- Thickening of root cell walls (physical barrier)
- Systemic resistance responses triggered in the plant
- Competition with pathogens for root colonization sites
- Increased plant vigor making plants less susceptible overall
