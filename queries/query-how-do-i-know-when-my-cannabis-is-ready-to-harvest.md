# How Do I Know When My Cannabis Is Ready to Harvest?

## Short Answer
Cannabis is ready to harvest when the trichomes on the buds shift
from clear to cloudy (milky white) with some turning amber. This
typically occurs 6 to 10 weeks into the flowering stage depending
on the strain. Additional indicators include pistil color change
from white to orange or brown, leaf yellowing, and bud density
reaching its peak.

## Detailed Explanation

Determining the optimal harvest time is one of the most important
decisions a cannabis grower makes. Harvest too early and the buds
will lack potency, aroma, and full weight. Harvest too late and
THC begins degrading into CBN, producing a more sedative effect
while terpenes can start to diminish. The most reliable method
for determining harvest readiness is observing the trichomes.

Trichomes are the tiny resin glands that cover the surface of
buds and sugar leaves. A jeweler's loupe or digital microscope is
essential for this, as trichomes are too small to see clearly
with the naked eye. The
[[cervantes-harvest-timing-trichome-ripeness]] guide provides
detailed guidance on what to look for at each stage of maturity.

Trichomes progress through three distinct phases. During early
flowering they are clear and translucent, indicating the plant is
still producing cannabinoids and is not yet ready. As maturity
approaches, trichomes become cloudy or milky white, which signals
peak THC content and the most cerebral, uplifting effects.
Finally, some trichomes begin turning amber, indicating THC is
converting to CBN.

The [[cannabis-trichome-guide]] provides detailed photographs of
each stage for comparison. The
[[cannabis-trichome-types-and-development]] page explains that
the three types of trichomes on cannabis (bulbous,
capitate-sessile, and capitate-stalked) all contribute to the
overall resin profile, but the capitate-stalked trichomes are the
largest and most important to monitor when assessing harvest
readiness.

Pistil color is a secondary indicator that many growers use
alongside trichome observation. During the flowering stage,
pistils (the hair-like structures emerging from buds) are white
and reach upward. As harvest approaches, they curl inward and
change color to orange, red, or brown. A common rule of thumb is
that when 70 to 90 percent of pistils have darkened, the plant is
approaching maturity.

However, pistil color alone is less reliable than trichome
observation because some strains naturally have pistils that
darken early while others retain white pistils much longer. Use
pistils as a general guide but always confirm with trichome
examination. The [[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]] page
covers how flowering timeline varies between indica, sativa, and
hybrid varieties.

Before harvesting, many growers perform a
[[cannabis-flushing-before-harvest]] by feeding the plant only
plain water for the final one to two weeks. The goal is to allow
the plant to use up stored nutrients in its foliage, which some
growers believe produces a smoother, cleaner-smoking final
product. However, the effectiveness of flushing is debated.

Some growers skip flushing entirely, especially when growing in
living soil systems where the microbial ecology naturally buffers
nutrient availability. Once harvested, the buds must be properly
dried and cured to preserve their quality. The
[[cannabis-drying-and-curing]] process involves hanging trimmed
branches in a dark, well-ventilated space at around 60 to 70
degrees Fahrenheit and 50 to 60 percent humidity for 7 to 14
days.

After the stems snap rather than bend, buds are placed in
airtight jars for curing, which develops flavor and smoothness
over 2 to 8 weeks. Rushing either the drying or curing stage is
one of the most common mistakes and can ruin months of careful
cultivation. The [[cannabis-harvesting-and-curing-techniques]]
page provides a complete step-by-step guide for the entire
post-harvest process.

## Key Points
- Harvest when trichomes are 50 to 70 percent cloudy with 10 to 20 percent amber for balanced effects
- Use a jeweler's loupe or microscope to inspect [[cannabis-trichome-types-and-development]] on buds
- Pistil darkening (70 to 90 percent brown) is a secondary indicator, not a primary one
- Consider [[cannabis-flushing-before-harvest]] 1 to 2 weeks before harvest for smoother flavor
- Follow proper [[cannabis-drying-and-curing]] techniques to preserve potency, terpenes, and quality
- The [[cannabis-flowering-stage]] typically lasts 6 to 10 weeks before trichomes reach peak maturity
