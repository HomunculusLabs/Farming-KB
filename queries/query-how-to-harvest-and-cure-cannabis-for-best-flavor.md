# How to Harvest and Cure Cannabis for the Best Flavor and Potency

## Short Answer
Optimal harvest timing is determined by trichome color, with mostly cloudy trichomes
and some amber indicating peak potency and higher amber ratios producing a more
sedative effect. After harvest, buds should be dried slowly at 60 to 70 degrees
Fahrenheit and 50 to 60 percent relative humidity for seven to fourteen days, then
cured in airtight glass jars for a minimum of two to four weeks with daily burping.
Proper curing converts harsh chlorophyll into smoother compounds and allows terpenes
to develop their full complexity and flavor profile over time.

## Detailed Explanation

The harvest and cure process is the final and arguably most important stage of
cannabis cultivation. Months of careful growing, feeding, and training can be
undermined by rushing the harvest or cutting corners on drying and curing. The
[[cannabis-trichome-guide]] explains that trichomes are the resin glands where
cannabinoids and terpenes are produced, and their appearance is the most reliable
indicator of harvest readiness available to growers.

Trichome color progression follows a predictable and well-documented pattern. Early
in flowering, trichomes are clear and translucent, indicating that cannabinoid
production is still in its early stages. As the plant matures, trichomes turn cloudy
or milky white, which signals that THC and other cannabinoid levels are at or near
their maximum. Finally, some trichomes begin turning amber as THC degrades into CBN,
a cannabinoid with more sedative and body-heavy properties. The
[[cannabis-trichome-types-and-development]] page describes this progression in detail.
For maximum potency and a balanced effect, harvest when 70 to 80 percent of trichomes
are cloudy with 10 to 20 percent amber. For a more relaxing effect, wait until 30 to
50 percent have turned amber. A jeweler's loupe or digital microscope at 60 to 100x
magnification is essential for accurate trichome assessment.

The [[harvest-window-cannabis]] guide recommends cutting plants in the dark period
before lights come on, when terpene levels are at their daily peak. Some growers
perform a 48-hour dark period before harvest, believing it stresses the plant into
final resin production, though scientific evidence for this practice is mixed. The
[[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]] page covers additional visual indicators
beyond trichomes, including pistil color change, calyx swelling, and progressive leaf
yellowing that can help confirm the plant is approaching harvest readiness.

Drying is the first post-harvest step and must be done slowly to preserve terpenes
and prevent mold development. Trim large fan leaves immediately after cutting, then
either hang whole branches or place individual buds on drying racks. The ideal
drying environment is 60 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit with 50 to 60 percent relative
humidity and gentle air circulation from oscillating fans. Higher temperatures or
lower humidity will cause buds to dry too fast, locking in chlorophyll and producing a
harsh, green taste that no amount of curing can fully correct. Higher humidity or
lower temperatures increase the risk of mold, particularly [[bud-rot-cannabis]], which
can ruin an entire harvest in a matter of days. The [[drying-cannabis]] guide provides
detailed environmental targets and troubleshooting tips for common drying problems.

Curing begins once the stems snap rather than bend, which typically takes seven to
fourteen days of drying depending on bud density and environmental conditions. Place
buds in airtight glass jars, filling them about three-quarters full to leave room for
air circulation. Store jars in a cool, dark place at 60 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit. For
the first two weeks, open the jars once or twice daily for ten to fifteen minutes to
release built-up moisture and replenish oxygen. This process, called burping, prevents
anaerobic conditions that can lead to mold growth and off-flavors. The
[[curing-cannabis]] and [[cannabis-harvest-and-curing]] guides cover the science
behind why curing improves quality, including the gradual breakdown of chlorophyll
and residual sugars that cause harshness in uncured flower.

After two to four weeks of curing, cannabis is smokeable and significantly smoother
than uncured flower. However, many connoisseurs continue curing for two to six months
or longer, with the terpene profile continuing to evolve and mellow over extended
time. The [[cannabis-terpene-and-aroma-chemistry-clarke]] page explains how curing
affects the complex interactions between terpenes that create each cultivar's unique
flavor and aroma characteristics. Properly cured cannabis stored in airtight
containers with two-way humidity packs at 58 to 62 percent relative humidity can
maintain peak quality for a year or more without significant degradation.

The trimming method you choose also affects final quality. Wet trimming, where leaves
are removed immediately after harvest before drying, gives the buds a tighter, more
aesthetic appearance and reduces the risk of mold by removing leaf material that traps
moisture. Dry trimming, where leaves are removed after the initial drying phase, can
be easier to perform because leaves curl away from buds as they dry, and it may
preserve slightly more terpenes because the leaves provide some protection during the
early drying period. The [[cannabis-harvesting-techniques]] guide compares both
approaches in detail. Regardless of method, trim carefully to remove sugar leaves
without damaging the delicate trichome-covered bracts that hold the majority of
the plant's resin.

Long-term storage requires attention to four factors: light, temperature, humidity,
and oxygen. Light degrades THC and terpenes rapidly, so all containers should be
opaque or stored in a dark location. Temperatures above 77 degrees Fahrenheit
accelerate degradation and increase the risk of mold. Humidity should be maintained
at 58 to 62 percent relative humidity using two-way humidity packs. Oxygen exposure
causes slow oxidation of cannabinoids and terpenes, so airtight containers with
minimal headspace are ideal. Vacuum-sealed glass jars stored in a refrigerator
provide the longest possible storage life, though even a dark closet at room
temperature with proper humidity control will maintain quality for many months.

The [[cannabis-flowering-stage]] guide connects harvest timing decisions back to the
management choices made throughout the flowering period, showing how early and late
flowering care directly affects the quality of the final cured product. Flushing
nutrients during the final one to two weeks before harvest, as described in the
[[flushing-nutrients]] guide, is a common practice intended to improve smoothness
by allowing the plant to use up stored nutrients before harvest.

## Key Points
- Use a jeweler's loupe to check trichome color; harvest at mostly cloudy with some amber
- Dry slowly at 60 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit and 50 to 60 percent humidity for one to two weeks
- Cure in airtight glass jars, burping daily for a minimum of two to four weeks
- Longer curing of two to six months continues to improve flavor smoothness and complexity
- Proper post-harvest handling preserves the terpenes and cannabinoids that define quality
