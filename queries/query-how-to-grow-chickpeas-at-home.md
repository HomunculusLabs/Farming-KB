## Why Grow Chickpeas?

- **High protein**: 19g protein per 100g cooked — among the highest of all garden legumes.
- **[[permplant-carob-drought-tolerant-food-tree]]**: Once established, chickpeas require less water than most garden legumes.
- **Soil improvement**: Deep taproot breaks compaction; nitrogen fixation feeds the soil.
- **Versatile in the kitchen**: Hummus, curries, salads, roasted snacks, falafel, flour.
- **Good for crop rotation**: Breaks disease cycles for solanaceous and brassica crops.

## Climate and Growing Conditions

| Requirement | Details |
|---|---|
| **Hardiness zones** | 3–10 (as an annual; grown as a cool-season crop everywhere) |
| **Temperature** | Optimal 65–80°F (18–27°C) during growth; tolerates light frost as seedlings |
| **Season** | Spring planting; needs 90–100 days to maturity for dry beans |
| **Day length** | Neutral to long-day for most varieties; short-day types exist for lower latitudes |
| **Heat tolerance** | Poor — temperatures above 85°F (29°C) during flowering reduce pod set |
| **[[comparison-drip-irrigation-vs-passive-irrigation]] or soaker hoses are preferred.

## Fertilization

Chickpeas need minimal nitrogen fertilizer when properly inoculated — that's the whole point of growing them. Over-fertilizing with nitrogen suppresses nodulation and nitrogen fixation.

- **Pre-plant**: 1–2 inches of compost provides adequate phosphorus and potassium.
- **Phosphorus**: If a soil test indicates deficiency, add rock phosphate or bone meal at planting.
- **Potassium**: Greensand or kelp meal if deficient.
- **Nitrogen**: Do NOT add nitrogen fertilizer unless plants show clear deficiency symptoms (pale, stunted growth) after 4–6 weeks, which indicates inoculation failure.
- **Micronutrients**: Chickpeas are sensitive to [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]].
- **Fusarium wilt**: Yellowing, wilting from lower leaves. Crop rotation (4+ years); resistant varieties.
- **Root rot**: Stunting, dark roots, plant collapse. Ensure well-drained soil; avoid overwatering.
- **Botrytis gray mold**: Gray fuzzy growth on pods. Good air circulation; remove infected plants.

## Weed Management

Chickpeas are slow to establish and not competitive against weeds. Light cultivation between rows, organic mulch, and flame weeding before emergence are effective strategies.

## Harvesting

- **Green chickpeas** (fresh): Harvest at 60–70 days when pods are green and plump. Boil or steam 15–20 minutes.
- **Dry chickpeas**: At 90–110 days, when leaves yellow and pods dry to tan/brown. Test by biting a bean — it should be hard. Pull entire plants, hang to finish drying if needed. Thresh by bagging plants and beating gently. Winnow to separate beans from chaff.

## Storage

- **Dried**: Airtight container, cool and dark — lasts 2–3 years. Freeze 48 hours first to kill weevil eggs.
- **Cooked**: Freeze in portions for up to 6 months.
- **Green**: Refrigerate and use within 1 week, or blanch and freeze.

## Cover Crop Use

Chickpeas fix 30–60 lb N/acre and break subsoil compaction with a 3–4 foot taproot. Terminate at pod fill for maximum nitrogen contribution. Excellent preceding crop for tomatoes, corn, or brassicas.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Poor germination | Cold, wet soil; old seed | Wait for warmer soil; use fresh seed |
| Yellow leaves | Inoculation failure | Check for root nodules; side-dress with compost |
| Few pods | Heat during flowering; excess N | Plant earlier; don't over-fertilize |
| Pod rot | Wet conditions, poor drainage | Improve drainage; avoid overhead watering |

## Quick Reference Summary

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| **Plant** | 2–4 weeks before last frost, 1 inch deep, 4 inches apart |
| **Inoculate** | Use chickpea-specific rhizobium inoculant |
| **Water** | Moderate; drought-tolerant once established |
| **Fertilize** | Compost only — no nitrogen needed |
| **Harvest** | 90–110 days for dry; 60–70 days for green |
| **Yield** | 1–3 lb dry chickpeas per 10-foot row |
| **Rotation** | 4+ years between chickpea plantings |
