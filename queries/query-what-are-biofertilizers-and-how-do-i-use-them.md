## What Are Biofertilizers and How Do I Use Them?

## Short Answer

Biofertilizers are living preparations of beneficial microorganisms applied to seeds, roots, or soil to boost plant nutrition and growth naturally.

Unlike synthetic fertilizers, they do not supply nutrients directly.

Instead, they enhance the soil's biological capacity to fix nitrogen, solubilize phosphorus, and produce growth-promoting compounds.

Common types include rhizobia for legumes, mycorrhizal fungi for most crops, and free-living bacteria like [[effective-microorganisms-em]]-based systems.

## What Biofertilizers Actually Do

Biofertilizers work through several biological mechanisms rather than chemical nutrient delivery.

The most well-known mechanism is [[rhizobium-legume-symbiosis]], in which rhizobia colonize legume roots and form nodules that fix nitrogen.

Free-living bacteria like Azotobacter also fix nitrogen without a host plant, though at lower rates.

Phosphorus-solubilizing microorganisms dissolve bound phosphorus in soil, making it available to plant roots.

### Soil Application

Soil drenches and broadcasting work well for established plants and mycorrhizal inoculants.

Mix the biofertilizer with compost, vermicompost, or a carrier material like peat or lignite before spreading.

Apply near the root zone where the microorganisms can colonize quickly.

Irrigate lightly after application to help the organisms move into the soil profile.

## Common Mistakes and Limitations

Applying biofertilizers alongside high doses of synthetic nitrogen fertilizer is counterproductive.

Plants that have abundant nitrogen stop supporting rhizobial nodulation, effectively starving the inoculant.

Most biofertilizers require adequate soil moisture and moderate temperatures to establish successfully.

Extreme pH, salinity, or waterlogging can kill inoculant organisms before they colonize the root zone.
