# How to Manage Pests and Diseases in Mushroom Cultivation

Managing pests and diseases is one of the most critical skills in mushroom
cultivation. A single contamination event can destroy weeks of work, making
prevention far more important than cure. See [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]. Reducing
surface moisture and improving fresh air exchange are the most effective
controls. Avoid misting fruiting mushrooms directly.

## Insect Pests

### Fungus Gnats and Phorid Flies

These small flies are among the most common insect pests. Fungus gnat larvae
feed on mycelium and damage developing mushrooms, while phorid flies carry mold
spores and bacteria between grows. Yellow sticky traps placed at substrate level
catch adults before they lay eggs. Biological controls such as beneficial
nematodes (Steinernema feltiae) can target larvae in the substrate. Paul
Stamets provides detailed [[botrytis-cinerea]] for
identification details and targeted management approaches.

### Dry Bubble (Verticillium)

Dry bubble causes malformed, lumpy mushrooms with a cracked cap surface. It is
highly contagious and spread primarily by water splash and physical contact.
Infected mushrooms should be removed carefully and disposed of away from the
grow area. Sanitize tools between every block or tray you inspect.

## Environmental Controls

Temperature, humidity, and fresh air exchange must be tuned to favor your

## Treatment Options

When contamination is detected, assess whether the affected material can be
salvaged. Small surface mold patches on bulk substrate may be treated with salt
or hydrogen peroxide. Contaminated grain spawn should always be discarded.
Severely infected blocks should be removed from the grow space entirely and
composted or disposed of responsibly.
