## What Causes Nutrient Lockout in Cannabis?

## Short Answer

Nutrient lockout occurs when cannabis plants cannot absorb available nutrients from the growing medium, most commonly because the pH of the root zone has drifted outside the optimal range. In soil, the ideal pH is 6.0-7.0, while in hydroponic and soilless systems it is 5.5-6.5. When pH is too high or too low, essential nutrients become chemically unavailable, leading to deficiency symptoms that mimic actual nutrient shortages.

For soil grows:
- Test runoff pH to confirm lockout
- Flush with pH-adjusted water (6.3-6.8) at 3x pot volume
- Allow soil to drain fully, then resume feeding at reduced strength
- Adjust soil pH with dolomitic lime (raises) or elemental sulfur (lowers)

For hydroponic systems:
- Test and adjust reservoir pH immediately
- Perform a reservoir change if pH drifts rapidly
- Clean and inspect all equipment for mineral deposits
- Check water source for pH stability
