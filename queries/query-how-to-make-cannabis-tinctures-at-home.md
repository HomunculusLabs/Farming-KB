# How to Make Cannabis Tinctures at Home

## Short Answer
Cannabis tinctures are liquid extracts made by soaking decarboxylated cannabis flower in
high-proof alcohol or glycerin.
They offer a smoke-free, precisely dosable way to consume cannabinoids and terpenes,
with effects typically felt within 15 to 45 minutes when taken sublingually.

## Detailed Explanation
A tincture is one of the oldest and most versatile methods of consuming cannabis,
with a history stretching back to the 19th century when cannabis tinctures were a
standard pharmaceutical preparation in pharmacies across Europe and America.

Unlike smoking or vaping, tinctures deliver cannabinoids through the mucous membranes
under the tongue (sublingual absorption) or through the digestive system when added
to food and drinks.

This makes them an excellent option for medical users, non-smokers, and anyone seeking
discreet, controlled dosing without the respiratory risks associated with inhalation.

The foundation of any good cannabis tincture is proper
[[cannabis-decarboxylation-chemistry]].
Raw cannabis contains THCA and CBDA, which are non-intoxicating acidic precursors
that do not readily cross the blood-brain barrier.

Decarboxylation converts these acidic cannabinoids into their active forms, THC and CBD,
by applying gentle heat that removes a carboxyl group from the molecular structure.

This typically requires temperatures around 220 to 240 degrees Fahrenheit for 30 to
45 minutes in an oven or decarboxylation device.

Without this critical step, your tincture will have minimal psychoactive or therapeutic
effect, regardless of how long you extract the material.

Understanding the chemistry behind this conversion ensures you maximize the potency
of your final product and avoid common mistakes like overheating, which can degrade
THC into CBN and reduce overall potency.

Once your cannabis is properly decarboxylated, the extraction process begins.
The two most common solvents for home tincture-making are food-grade ethanol and
vegetable glycerin, and each has distinct advantages and limitations.

Ethanol is the preferred choice for most experienced users because it extracts a
wider range of cannabinoids, terpenes, flavonoids, and other beneficial compounds.

High-proof grain alcohol such as Everclear (190 proof) is ideal because it contains
minimal water, which means it extracts efficiently and preserves well over long periods.

The alcohol also acts as a natural preservative, giving ethanol-based tinctures a
shelf life of one to two years or more when stored properly in a cool, dark place.

Glycerin-based tinctures are sweeter, thicker, and completely alcohol-free, making
them suitable for children, seniors, or those in recovery who are avoiding alcohol.

However, glycerin extracts fewer cannabinoids than ethanol and produces a less potent
final product with a shorter shelf life of roughly six to twelve months.

The basic room-temperature ethanol tincture method is straightforward and requires
minimal equipment that most home kitchens already have.

After decarboxylating your flower, coarsely grind it and place it in a clean glass
mason jar. Cover the plant material completely with your chosen alcohol, ensuring
there is at least an inch of liquid above the top of the herb.

Seal the jar tightly and store it in a cool, dark place such as a cabinet or closet
for two to four weeks, shaking the jar gently once per day to agitate the mixture
and improve extraction efficiency.

This slow method preserves delicate terpenes better than heat-based approaches.

For those who prefer a faster method, a gentle warm extraction using a double boiler
or slow cooker set to low heat can complete the process in four to eight hours.

Once extraction is complete, strain the liquid through a cheesecloth, coffee filter,
or fine mesh strainer into a clean glass dropper bottle.

Dosage precision is one of the biggest advantages of tinctures over other consumption
methods like smoking, vaping, or edibles.

A standard 1-ounce dropper bottle delivers roughly 1 milliliter per full dropperful,
and you can calculate your approximate cannabinoid content based on the starting
material and extraction efficiency.

For example, if you start with 3.5 grams of flower testing at 20 percent THC, you
have roughly 700 milligrams of THC available in the plant material.

Assuming a typical home extraction efficiency of 60 to 80 percent, your 30-milliliter
batch would contain approximately 420 to 560 milligrams of THC, or about 14 to 19
milligrams per dropperful.

Start with a low dose of 2.5 to 5 milligrams (roughly one-quarter to one-half
dropperful) and wait at least 45 minutes before increasing your dose.

Sublingual absorption can vary significantly between individuals based on body weight,
metabolism, and tolerance, so patience is key when finding your ideal dose.

Tinctures can also be incorporated into a wide range of culinary applications beyond
sublingual use.
Adding a few dropperfuls to tea, coffee, juice, smoothies, soups, or salad dressing
is a simple way to consume cannabis without any combustion.

When ingested orally rather than sublingually, the effects take significantly longer
to onset, typically 60 to 120 minutes, because the cannabinoids must pass through
the digestive system and liver before entering the bloodstream.

This first-pass liver metabolism converts a larger proportion of THC into
11-hydroxy-THC, a metabolite that is significantly more potent and produces stronger,
more sedative, and longer-lasting psychoactive effects.

Proper storage is essential for maintaining the potency and freshness of your tincture
over time.
Keep your finished product in a dark amber or cobalt blue glass dropper bottle stored
away from direct light, heat sources, and temperature fluctuations.

Ethanol-based tinctures can last for one to two years or longer when stored properly.
Glycerin tinctures have a shorter shelf life and may develop off-flavors after six
to twelve months.

Always label your bottles with the strain used, approximate cannabinoid content, date
of creation, and the solvent used so you can track your experiences.
