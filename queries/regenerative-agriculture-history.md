# Regenerative Agriculture History

The evolution from organic certification to the regenerative agriculture movement.
See [[organic-farming-history]] for pre-2000 roots and regenerative agriculture history
for the conceptual framework.

## 2000s — Regenerative Agriculture Emerges

- **2000s — Regenerative agriculture gains traction:**
  Term "regenerative agriculture" gains traction as a framework
  that goes beyond organic (avoiding harm) to actively restoring
  soil health, increasing biodiversity, improving water cycles,
  and sequestering carbon.
  Robert Rodale coined "regenerative organic" in the 1980s.
  See regenerative agriculture history.

- **2007-2010s — No-till farming spreads:**
  Gabe Brown (North Dakota), Ray Archuleta (NRCS), and others
  demonstrate that no-till + cover cropping + diverse rotations
  can regenerate degraded soils.
  See [[no-till-gardening-method]].

## 2010s — Soil Health Movement Matures

- **2010s — Soil health movement matures:**
  USDA NRCS, Rodale Institute, and universities promote soil health.
  Soil health indicators (earthworm counts, aggregate stability,
  water infiltration, organic matter percentage) gain recognition.

- **2014 — 4 per 1000 initiative:**
  Launched at COP21 Paris Climate Conference,
  proposes increasing soil organic carbon by 0.4% per year
  to significantly offset global CO2 emissions.

- **2015 — Project Drawdown:**
  Paul Hawken's Project Drawdown identifies regenerative agriculture,
  silvopasture, and other farming practices as among the top solutions
  for reversing climate change.

- **2018 — US organic market exceeds $50 billion:**
  Organic food sales reach $52.5 billion in the US.
  Organic produce reaches ~15% of US produce market.

- **2019 — Kiss the Ground documentary:**
  Raises public awareness of regenerative agriculture
  and soil carbon sequestration.

## 2020s — Regenerative Certification and Living Soil

- **2020s — Regenerative Organic Certified (ROC):**
  Launched by Rodale Institute, Patagonia, and Dr. Bronner's.
  Goes beyond USDA Organic to require soil health practices,
  animal welfare, and social fairness.
  Bronze, Silver, and Gold certification levels.

- **2020s — Living soil movement:**
  Integration of KNF, JADAM, and soil food web principles
  into cannabis cultivation, market gardening, and homesteading.
  Living soil approaches emphasize building self-sustaining
  soil ecosystems rather than adding external inputs.
  See [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] and [[cannabis-growing-mediums]].

- **2020s — KNF and JADAM global spread:**
  Korean Natural Farming and JADAM spread worldwide through online communities.
  Particularly adopted among cannabis growers
  and tropical agriculture practitioners.
  See [[cho-han-kyu]] and [[jadam-methods]].

- **2020s — Conventional adoption of biological practices:**
  Even conventional agriculture increasingly adopts biological practices:
  cover cropping (up 50%+ in some regions), reduced tillage,
  biological seed treatments, and integrated pest management.
  The line between "organic" and "conventional" blurs.
