# What Is Main-Lining Cannabis and How Does It Work?

## Short Answer
Main-lining is a high-stress training technique that creates a
symmetrical, manifold-shaped canopy by repeatedly topping the
plant at each new node and tying branches outward. Developed by
Nugbuckets, the method produces multiple even-sized colas from a
single plant, maximizing light efficiency and yield per watt in
indoor grows.

## Detailed Explanation

Main-lining, also known as manifolding, is one of the most
structured and systematic approaches to cannabis training. Unlike
casual topping or low-stress training, main-lining follows a
specific protocol: top the seedling to create two main stems,
then top each of those stems to create four, and optionally
continue to eight or sixteen main colas. Each topping is done at
the node where the plant naturally splits.

The branches are tied down horizontally using soft plant ties to
create an even, flat canopy. The technique begins when the plant
is very young, typically at the third or fourth node during the
[[cannabis-vegetative-stage]]. The seedling is topped above the third node, and the lower two branches are kept as the new mains while the top growth is removed entirely.

These two branches are tied down in opposite directions, and the
plant is allowed to grow until each branch develops three to four
new nodes. At that point, each branch is topped again above the
second node, creating four total main colas. This process can be
repeated once more to create eight colas, though most growers
stop at either four or eight depending on pot size and light
coverage.

The primary advantage of [[main-lining]] is the uniformity it
creates. In an untrained plant, the apical dominance of the main
cola means the top bud receives the most light and grows the
largest, while lower buds remain small and underdeveloped. By
creating a perfectly even canopy through repeated topping and
tying, main-lining ensures that every cola is at the same height
and distance from the light.

This dramatically increases the percentage of the plant that
produces top-quality, dense flowers. For growers using small
numbers of plants under a fixed light, main-lining is one of the
most effective ways to maximize yield per plant. The technique is
more aggressive than [[low-stress-training]] and shares
similarities with
[[cannabis-topping-training]], but it is far more systematic.

Main-lining does require patience. Because the plant must recover
from multiple topping events, the vegetative period is typically
extended by two to four weeks compared to an untrained plant.
However, this extra time is more than compensated for during the
[[cannabis-flowering-stage]], when the even canopy produces significantly more high-quality bud than an untrained plant grown for the same total duration.

Proper support is essential when main-lining. As the flowering
stage progresses and buds develop weight, the horizontal branches
will need to be supported to prevent breakage. Many growers use a
scrog screen in conjunction with main-lining, weaving the
branches through the net as they grow. The combination of
main-lining and scrog creates an incredibly even canopy that is
difficult to achieve with either technique alone.

The [[cervantes-pruning-training]] guide covers the fundamentals
of plant architecture manipulation that make this possible. It is
important to note that main-lining is best suited for photoperiod
strains and is not recommended for [[autoflowering-cannabis]] due
to their fixed life cycle and limited recovery time between
topping events.

The [[cannabis-training-techniques-comparison]] page can help you
decide which training approach best fits your growing style and
strain selection. For growers interested in maximizing their
indoor yield with a small number of plants, main-lining is one of
the most rewarding techniques to learn and master over successive
grows. The visual symmetry of a well-executed manifold is striking and makes for an impressive garden display.

## Key Points
- [[main-lining]] creates 4 to 16 perfectly even colas from a single plant by systematic topping
- Start topping at the third node during early [[cannabis-vegetative-stage]] and repeat at each branch level
- Expect a 2 to 4 week longer vegetative period, but significantly higher yield per watt
- Combine with a scrog screen or stakes for support during heavy [[cannabis-flowering-stage]] buds
- Best suited for photoperiod strains; not recommended for [[autoflowering-cannabis]]
- Use clean tools and soft ties to avoid damaging the plant during repeated topping and tying
- Start with a healthy, well-rooted seedling before attempting any topping or training techniques
