# Cannabis Modern Reform Era (1970–Present)

Continuation of [[cannabis-prohibition-history]]. Covers the War on Drugs, medical marijuana movement, and modern legalization.

## Controlled Substances Act and War on Drugs (1970-1996)

- **1970 — Controlled Substances Act:**
  Nixon signs the CSA.
  Cannabis placed in Schedule I —
  "no currently accepted medical use" and "high potential for abuse."
  Classed alongside heroin, LSD, and peyote.

- **1970 — Shafer Commission:**
  Nixon appointed the National Commission on Marihuana and Drug Abuse.
  The 1972 report recommended decriminalization for personal possession.
  Nixon rejected his own commission"s findings.

- **1972-1973 — NORML founded:**
  The National Organization for the Reform of Marijuana Laws (NORML)
  begins lobbying for decriminalization.

- **1973-1977 — First wave of state decriminalization:**
  Oregon, Alaska, Colorado, Ohio, California, and other states
  reduce penalties for possession to civil fines rather than criminal charges.
  By 1978, 11 states had decriminalized.

- **1976 — Robert Randall wins federal medical access:**
  Randall, a glaucoma patient, becomes the first American
  to receive medical cannabis from the federal government
  under the Compassionate IND program.
  Eventually 15 patients receive federal cannabis.

- **1980s — Reagan escalation:**
  "Just Say No" campaign.
  Mandatory minimum sentencing expanded through
  the Anti-Drug Abuse Acts of 1986 and 1988.
  Cannabis arrests exceed 300,000 per year by the end of the decade.

- **1989 — George H.W. Bush's War on Drugs:**
  Escalation of enforcement and rhetoric.
  Asset forfeiture laws expanded.
  The gap between penalties for powder cocaine (predominantly white users)
  and crack cocaine (predominantly Black users) becomes a major civil rights issue.

- **1992 — Compassionate IND program shut down:**
  The federal government closes the program
  that had been supplying medical cannabis to a small number of patients.
  No new applicants accepted.

## Medical Marijuana Movement (1996-2012)

- **1996 — California Proposition 215:**
  First state to legalize medical cannabis through voter initiative.
  Allows patients with a doctor's recommendation
  to possess and cultivate cannabis.
  Written by Dennis Peron and passed with 55.6% support.

- **Late 1990s-2000s — Medical cannabis spreads:**
  Alaska, Oregon, Washington, Maine, Hawaii, Nevada, Colorado,
  and other states pass medical cannabis laws.
  By 2010, 15 states plus DC have medical cannabis programs.

- **2001 — Canada Medical Marihuana Access Regulations:**
  Canada establishes federal medical cannabis program,
  becoming the first country to formally regulate medical cannabis nationally.

- **2003-2009 — DEA raids despite state law:**
  Federal DEA raids on medical cannabis dispensaries continue
  under both Bush and Obama administrations,
  creating tension between state and federal law.
  Gonzales v. Raich (2005) upholds federal authority over state laws.

- **2009 — Ogden Memo:**
  Obama administration's Deputy Attorney General David Ogden
  issues memo indicating federal resources would not be prioritized
  for targeting individuals in compliance with state medical cannabis laws.

## Legalization Era (2012-Present)

The end of prohibition and the modern legalization movement — from Colorado and Washington's historic 2012 votes through the 2018 Farm Bill, hemp revival, state-by-state legalization, and federal rescheduling to Schedule III — is covered in detail on [[cannabis-legalization-era]]. Key milestones include the 2014 and 2018 Farm Bills separating hemp from marijuana, FDA approval of Epidiolex, Biden's 2022 pardon of federal convictions, and HHS's 2023 recommendation to reschedule cannabis.
