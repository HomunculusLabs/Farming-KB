# What Is Biochar and How Should I Use It in My Garden?

Biochar is a carbon-rich, porous material produced by heating organic biomass (wood, crop residues, manure) in a low-oxygen environment through a process called **pyrolysis**. Unlike regular charcoal, biochar is specifically intended as a long-term soil amendment. Its microscopic sponge-like structure holds water, nutrients, and beneficial microbes, making it one of the most enduring soil improvements you can add to a garden or farm.

## Why Biochar Matters for Soil

Biochar's value comes from its physical and chemical properties:

- **Porosity**: A single gram of biochar can have a surface area of 300 to 1,500 square meters, providing [[cation-exchange-capacity]]) attracts and holds positively charged nutrients like calcium, magnesium, potassium, and ammonium, reducing leaching

- **Manure charging**: Mix biochar with aged animal manure and compost together
- **Liquid nutrient charging**: Soak in liquid fish emulsion, kelp extract, or worm casting tea for 24 hours

Aim for a minimum of 2 weeks of charging time. Longer is better.

## Application Rates

| Application | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| New garden beds | 5 to 10% by volume | Mix into top 15 to 30 cm of soil |
| Container gardens | 5 to 10% by volume | Blend into potting mix before planting |
| Established beds | 1 to 2 kg per square meter | Top-dress and water in well |
| Farm fields | 1 to 5 tonnes per hectare | Incorporate during field preparation |
| Compost accelerator | 5 to 10% of pile volume | Reduces odor and retains nutrients |

Start conservative — more is not always better. Excessive biochar can raise soil pH excessively.

## Benefits Observed in Practice

Gardeners and researchers have documented a range of benefits from consistent biochar use:

- Improved soil structure in heavy clay soils, reducing compaction and improving drainage
- Enhanced water retention in sandy soils, reducing irrigation frequency by 10 to 30%
- Increased microbial biomass and diversity in the root zone
- Higher mycorrhizal colonization rates, which improves nutrient cycling
- Reduced nitrogen leaching, particularly in rainy climates
- Moderated soil pH fluctuations, buffering against acidity and alkalinity shifts
- Improved germination rates and seedling vigor in biochar-amended seed-starting mixes

## What to Watch Out For

- **Alkalinity**: Most biochar has pH 8 to 10. Use cautiously if your soil is already alkaline
- **Contamination risk**: Never use biochar made from treated wood, painted lumber, plastics, or garbage
- **Dust**: Fine biochar dust is a respiratory irritant — wear a mask and moisten before mixing
- **Cost**: Commercial biochar can be expensive; making your own from yard waste is more economical
- **Patience required**: Biochar's full benefits develop over months and years as soil biology colonizes it
