## Why Black Soldier Flies?

Black soldier flies are not pests and do not bite, sting, or transmit disease to humans.

Adult flies live only 5-8 days and spend that time mating and laying eggs rather than feeding.

The larvae are the workhorses, converting waste into two valuable products: protein-rich larvae for animal feed and nutrient-dense frass for soil amendment.

Unlike many composting methods, BSF tolerate a wide range of waste types including meat, dairy, and oily foods that would cause problems in traditional bins.

They also produce far less odor than conventional composting when managed correctly.

## Understanding the BSF Life Cycle

The complete life cycle from egg to adult takes roughly 2-3 months depending on temperature.

Adult females lay 500-900 eggs in corrugated cardboard or other textured surfaces near decaying organic matter.

Eggs hatch within 4-7 days into tiny larvae that immediately begin feeding.

The larval stage lasts 2-4 weeks, during which the grubs grow from barely visible to about 25mm long.

When ready to pupate, the larvae stop eating and seek dry sheltered areas to form a dark pupal shell.

After 2-3 weeks as pupae, adult flies emerge, mate within a day or two, and the cycle repeats.

Understanding this cycle is essential for [[black-soldier-fly-composting|BSF composting systems]].

Collected larvae can be fed live to chickens, ducks, fish, or reptiles.

They can also be dried, ground into meal, or frozen for later use.
