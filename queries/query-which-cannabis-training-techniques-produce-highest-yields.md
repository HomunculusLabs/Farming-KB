# Which Cannabis Training Techniques Produce the Highest Yields

## Short Answer
The most effective yield-boosting techniques combine structural training methods like
topping and low-stress training with canopy management systems like SCROG or sea of
green. Topping creates a bushier plant with multiple main colas, low-stress training
bends branches horizontally to expose more bud sites to light, and screen of green
creates an even canopy that maximizes light utilization across the entire grow space.

## Detailed Explanation

Plant training is the practice of manipulating cannabis growth patterns to increase
yield, improve light distribution, and control plant shape. Rather than letting a
plant grow into its natural Christmas tree form with a single dominant cola, trained
plants develop multiple evenly-sized bud sites that collectively produce more
harvestable flower. The [[water-in-permaculture]] overview covers the full range
of methods available to growers, but understanding which combinations work best
requires examining how each technique affects the plant's architecture and energy
distribution throughout the grow cycle.

Topping is the most fundamental structural training technique. By removing the apical
meristem, or growing tip, [[topping-cannabis]] breaks the plant's apical dominance
and forces it to develop two new main stems from the nodes just below the cut. This
can be repeated multiple times, with each top doubling the number of main colas. Most
growers top once or twice during the early vegetative stage, producing four to eight
main stems that form the scaffold for the rest of the training. Topping is a
high-stress technique that temporarily slows growth, so it should be performed early
enough for the plant to recover fully before flowering begins.

Low-stress training takes a gentler approach by bending branches rather than cutting
them. [[low-stress-training]] involves tying branches down and away from the center of
the plant using soft wire ties, garden twist ties, or specialized LST clips. This
horizontal bending exposes lower bud sites to more light and encourages them to grow
into full colas. Because no tissue is damaged, LST causes minimal growth shock and
can be performed throughout the vegetative stage and even into the first two weeks of
flowering. The combination of topping to create multiple main stems and LST to spread
them horizontally is one of the most reliable yield-boosting strategies available to
any grower regardless of experience level.

Screen of green, or [[screen-of-green]], takes horizontal training to its logical
conclusion by adding a physical grid above the canopy. As branches grow through the
screen, the grower weaves them into the grid to create a perfectly flat, even canopy.
Every bud site sits at the same distance from the light source, which dramatically
improves light efficiency. SCROG is particularly effective with [[photoperiod-cannabis]] strains that have a long vegetative period, allowing time to fill the screen
completely. It works well with both soil and hydroponic setups, though [[cannabis-pot-selection-and-container-sizing]] should account for the fact that SCROG plants
need wider, shallower containers to match their spread-out canopy structure.

Sea of green, or [[sea-of-green]], takes a completely different approach to yield
maximization. Instead of training a few large plants, SOG uses many small plants
grown in small containers with minimal vegetative time, often only two to three weeks.
Each plant produces a single main cola, and the sheer number of plants creates a dense
canopy of uniform buds. SOG is ideal for growers with limited vertical space and
strains that naturally grow a single large cola. It is less effective with
branching-heavy sativa varieties. SOG pairs well with [[cannabis-clone-rooting-and-transplanting]] because using identical clones ensures uniform growth across the
entire crop for a consistent, even canopy.

Supercropping is a more advanced technique that involves strategically crushing the
soft inner tissue of stems to create a knuckle that holds the branch at a new angle.
[[supercropping]] allows growers to aggressively reposition branches without cutting,
and the knuckle joint actually becomes stronger than the original stem over time. It
is useful for lowering tall branches that would otherwise shade shorter ones. Fimming,
covered in the [[fimming]] guide, is a variation of topping where the growing tip is
pinched rather than fully removed, often producing four to six new growth tips instead
of two. Both techniques can be combined with LST for maximum canopy control.

Defoliation during flowering removes leaves that shade bud sites, redirecting the
plant's energy toward flower production. [[defoliation-cannabis]] and [[lollipopping]]
both serve this purpose, with lollipopping being more aggressive by removing all lower
growth that will not receive adequate light. The [[cannabis-training-techniques-comparison]] guide breaks down the yield differences between these methods based on
grow style and strain type, helping growers choose the combination that best fits
their specific setup, light intensity, and container size.

Timing is critical when applying training techniques. Structural methods like topping
and fimming should only be performed during the vegetative stage when the plant has
enough time to recover before flowering begins. LST can continue into early flowering
but should stop by week two or three to avoid stressing the plant during the critical
bud development period. Defoliation is typically performed once at the start of
flowering and optionally a second time around week three to four. The [[cannabis-vegetative-stage-management]] guide covers the ideal windows for each technique based
on plant size and growth rate.

Light intensity plays a major role in determining how much training benefit you will
see. Low-light setups benefit less from training because the plant cannot support
additional bud sites without adequate photosynthetic energy. High-intensity lighting
systems like [[led-grow-lights]], [[hps-grow-lights]], or [[cmh-grow-lights]] paired
with training techniques produce the most dramatic yield improvements because they
provide enough light energy to fill additional bud sites. The [[cannabis-indoor-grow-lighting-systems]] guide helps match your light investment with appropriate
training strategies for maximum return.

The best approach for most growers combines two to three complementary techniques
rather than relying on a single method. A common high-yield strategy involves
topping twice during early vegetative growth to create four to eight main colas,
applying LST to spread them evenly, using a SCROG screen to maintain a flat canopy,
and performing one round of lollipopping in early flowering. This combination
addresses light distribution, structural support, and energy redirection for
comprehensive yield optimization.

## Key Points
- Topping creates multiple main colas by breaking apical dominance early in veg
- Low-stress training bends branches horizontally for even light distribution
- SCROG uses a screen to create a perfectly even canopy maximizing light per square foot
- SOG uses many small plants with single colas for fast turnover and efficient space use
- Combining topping with LST and either SCROG or SOG produces the most consistent yields
