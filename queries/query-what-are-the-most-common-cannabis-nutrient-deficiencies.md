# What Are the Most Common Cannabis Nutrient Deficiencies

## Short Answer

The most frequently encountered cannabis nutrient deficiencies are nitrogen (yellowing of older leaves), phosphorus (darkening or purpling with slow growth), calcium (brown spots on new leaves), magnesium (interveinal chlorosis on older leaves), and iron (bright yellow new growth with green veins). Correct identification requires observing which leaves are affected and the specific pattern of discoloration.

## The Mobility Principle

The key to diagnosing nutrient deficiencies is understanding nutrient mobility within the plant.

Mobile nutrients can be relocated from older tissues to new growth, so deficiency symptoms appear on lower, older leaves first. Immobile nutrients cannot be moved once deposited, so deficiency symptoms appear on new growth at the top of the plant.

| Mobile (old leaves first) | Immobile (new leaves first) |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | Calcium (Ca) |
| Phosphorus (P) | Iron (Fe) |
| Potassium (K) | Manganese (Mn) |
| Magnesium (Mg) | Zinc (Zn) |
| Sulfur (S) | Copper (Cu) |
| | Boron (B) |

The [[cannabis-nutrient-deficiency-guide]] provides a systematic approach using this principle to narrow down which nutrient is deficient based on where symptoms appear first.

## Nitrogen Deficiency

Nitrogen deficiency is the most widespread cannabis nutrient problem.

Symptoms include:

- Uniform yellowing of older fan leaves progressing from tips inward
- Leaves eventually becoming completely pale and dropping off
- Overall slow growth and reduced vigor
- Smaller than normal new leaves

While some nitrogen yellowing during late flowering is normal and even desirable for smoother smoke, premature nitrogen deficiency during vegetative growth indicates insufficient feeding. The [[cannabis-chemical-burn-vs-deficiency]] reference helps growers distinguish between natural senescence and true deficiency, avoiding overcorrection.

## Phosphorus Deficiency

Phosphorus deficiency is particularly common in indoor grows where root zone temperatures drop below 60 degrees Fahrenheit, because cold roots cannot absorb phosphorus efficiently even when it is present in the growing medium.

As documented in [[cannabis-phosphorus]] guides, symptoms include:

- Dark green or bluish-green leaf coloration
- Purplish stems and petioles
- Slow growth and stunted development
- Brown or bronze spotting on older leaves in severe cases

Correcting phosphorus deficiency often involves warming the root zone rather than adding more phosphorus. Overapplying phosphorus can cause nutrient lockout of zinc and iron.

## Calcium Deficiency

Calcium deficiency is frequent in coco coir and hydroponic grows because calcium is immobile and cannot be relocated from older tissues to new growth.

The [[cannabis-calcium-nutrient]] documentation describes these symptoms:

- Distorted or crinkled leaf edges on new growth
- Brown or rust-colored spots on new leaves
- Tips that appear burned or necrotic
- New leaves that fail to unfurl properly
- Root tip dieback in severe cases

In coco coir, calcium deficiency is often caused by using soil-formulated nutrients rather than coco-specific lines that include adequate cal-mag supplementation. Adding a calcium-magnesium supplement at 1 to 2 ml per gallon typically resolves the issue within a week.

## Magnesium Deficiency

Magnesium deficiency is extremely common, particularly during flowering when the plant's demand increases substantially.

The classic symptom from [[cannabis-magnesium-nutrient]] resources is interveinal chlorosis: the leaf tissue between the veins turns yellow while the veins themselves remain green. This typically starts on the lower older leaves and progresses upward.

Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate) foliar sprays or cal-mag supplements are the standard corrective treatments. For soil grows, a tablespoon of Epsom salt per gallon of water applied as a soil drench usually resolves symptoms within a few days.

## Iron Deficiency

Iron deficiency presents as bright yellow or white new leaves with green veins, because iron is immobile and cannot be translocated from older tissues.

The [[cannabis-iron-nutrient]] documentation explains that iron deficiency is often caused by high pH (above 6.5 in soil, above 5.8 in coco) rather than actual iron absence. Making pH adjustment the primary corrective action is more effective than adding iron supplements, which may not help if the pH remains too high for absorption.

## Key Points

- Mobile nutrients (N, Mg, K) show deficiency on older leaves; immobile nutrients (Ca, Fe, Mn) appear on new growth
- Nitrogen deficiency causes yellowing older leaves; common during flowering per [[cannabis-nutrient-deficiency-guide]]
- Phosphorus deficiency often relates to cold root zones rather than low levels per [[cannabis-phosphorus]]
- Calcium deficiency in coco coir requires cal-mag per [[cannabis-calcium-nutrient]] protocols
- Iron vs magnesium deficiency: iron affects new growth, [[cannabis-magnesium-nutrient]] affects old growth
