# What Are the Most Common Cannabis Nutrient Deficiencies

## Short Answer

The most frequently encountered cannabis nutrient deficiencies are nitrogen (yellowing of older leaves), phosphorus (darkening or purpling with slow growth), calcium (brown spots on new leaves), magnesium (interveinal chlorosis on older leaves), and iron (bright yellow new growth with green veins). Correct identification requires observing which leaves are affected and the specific pattern of discoloration.

## The Mobility Principle

The key to diagnosing nutrient deficiencies is understanding nutrient mobility within the plant.

Mobile nutrients can be relocated from older tissues to new growth, so deficiency symptoms appear on lower, older leaves first. Immobile nutrients cannot be moved once deposited, so deficiency symptoms appear on new growth at the top of the plant.

| Mobile (old leaves first) | Immobile (new leaves first) |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | Calcium (Ca) |
| Phosphorus (P) | Iron (Fe) |
| Potassium (K) | Manganese (Mn) |
| Magnesium (Mg) | Zinc (Zn) |
| Sulfur (S) | Copper (Cu) |
| | Boron (B) |

## Phosphorus Deficiency

Phosphorus deficiency is particularly common in indoor grows where root zone temperatures drop below 60 degrees Fahrenheit, because cold roots cannot absorb phosphorus efficiently even when it is present in the growing medium.

As documented in [[cannabis-calcium-nutrient]] documentation describes these symptoms:

- Distorted or crinkled leaf edges on new growth
- Brown or rust-colored spots on new leaves
- Tips that appear burned or necrotic
- New leaves that fail to unfurl properly
- Root tip dieback in severe cases

In coco coir, calcium deficiency is often caused by using soil-formulated nutrients rather than coco-specific lines that include adequate cal-mag supplementation. Adding a calcium-magnesium supplement at 1 to 2 ml per gallon typically resolves the issue within a week.

## Magnesium Deficiency

Magnesium deficiency is extremely common, particularly during flowering when the plant's demand increases substantially.

The classic symptom from [[cannabis-iron-nutrient]] documentation explains that iron deficiency is often caused by high pH (above 6.5 in soil, above 5.8 in coco) rather than actual iron absence. Making pH adjustment the primary corrective action is more effective than adding iron supplements, which may not help if the pH remains too high for absorption.

## Key Points

- Mobile nutrients (N, Mg, K) show deficiency on older leaves; immobile nutrients (Ca, Fe, Mn) appear on new growth
