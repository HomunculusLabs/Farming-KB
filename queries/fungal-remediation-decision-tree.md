# Fungal Remediation Decision Tree

Flowchart-style reference for matching fungal species to contaminant types. Covers heavy metals, petroleum hydrocarbons, PAHs, PCBs, dioxins, pesticides, pathogens, dyes, pharmaceuticals, and explosives. Compiled from [[mycoremediation-basics]], [[mycoremediation-techniques]], and [[mycofiltration-overview]].

## Contaminant-Species Matching Table

| Contaminant | Best Fungal Species | Mechanism | Setup | Timeline | Effectiveness | References |
|-------------|-------------------|-----------|-------|----------|--------------|------------|
| **Heavy metals** (Pb, Hg, Cd, Cu, Zn) | Aspergillus niger; mycorrhizal fungi | Biosorption via cell wall chitin/glucans; ion exchange, complexation, bioaccumulation in fruiting bodies | Contact with contaminated soil/water; periodic harvest of fruiting bodies (hazardous waste) | Weeks to months | High for Cu/Zn (91%/70%, Price 2001) | [[mycoremediation-basics]], [[mycofiltration-overview]] |
| **Petroleum hydrocarbons** (diesel, oil, BTEX) | Pleurotus ostreatus; white-rot fungi | Extracellular ligninolytic enzymes (LiP, MnP, laccase); enzymatic breakdown | Layer inoculated straw/wood chips 6-12" deep; cover with cardboard + straw | 8 weeks dramatic reduction | Very high — proven field trials | [[mycoremediation-basics]], [[mycoremediation-techniques]] |
| **PAHs** | P. chrysosporium; T. versicolor; P. ostreatus; Penicillium, Fusarium, Cunninghamella | Cytochrome P450 + ligninolytic enzymes; oxidative cleavage | Inoculated substrate; fungal-bacterial co-cultures enhance mineralization | Weeks to months | High low-MW; moderate high-MW | [[mycoremediation-basics]] |
| **PCBs** | Trametes versicolor; Phanerochaete chrysosporium; P. sordida | Lignin peroxidase oxidizes non-phenolic aromatics; oxidative cleavage | Substrate inoculation; nutrient-limited conditions; lab preconditioning recommended | Months; 95% degradation (Zeddel 1993) | Very high | [[mycoremediation-basics]] |
| **Dioxins** (TCDD) | Phanerochaete sordida; Cordyceps sinensis | Ligninolytic enzymes; novel dioxin pathway (Cordyceps) producing catechol intermediates | Lab-trained strains; lined pits/berms; monitor with GC-MS | Months; ~60% TCDD degradation | Moderate — ongoing research | [[mycoremediation-basics]] |
| **Pesticides/herbicides** | White-rot fungi; Stropharia rugoso-annulata | Extracellular nonspecific ligninolytic enzymes degrade organophosphates, organochlorines, carbamates | Mycelial mats on runoff pathways; straw bale mycofilters in drainage | Weeks to months | High — nonspecific enzymes | [[mycoremediation-basics]], [[mycofiltration-overview]] |
| **Pathogens** (E. coli, coliforms) | Stropharia rugoso-annulata; P. ostreatus | Antibacterial secretions; competitive displacement (Pseudomonas → Bacillus subtilis) | Mycofilter downslope; layered: gravel → sawdust → spawn → corncobs → straw | Immediate coliform die-off | Very high — millions → undetectable | [[mycofiltration-overview]] |
| **Dyes** (azo, anthraquinone) | Trametes versicolor; P. ostreatus; Coriolus versicolor | Laccase oxidizes phenolic dye structures; mediators (ABTS, HBT) extend range | Inoculated substrate in water treatment pathways | Days to weeks (reactors) | High — widely studied | [[mycoremediation-basics]] |
| **Pharmaceuticals** | White-rot fungi; oyster mushroom | Ligninolytic enzymes degrade via oxidative mechanisms | Substrate inoculation in water treatment | Research stage | Moderate — promising lab results | [[mycoremediation-basics]] |
| **Explosives** (TNT, RDX, nerve agents) | Pre-trained fungal strains | Enzymatic degradation; DMMP (VX surrogate) metabolized as sole nutrient at Battelle | Lab strain training on surrogates; lined containment; GC-MS monitoring | Weeks for DMMP | High for trained strains | [[mycoremediation-basics]], [[mycoremediation-techniques]] |

## Quick-Decision Guide: "If You Have X, Start With Y"

| Contamination | Start With | Key Species | Notes |
|--------------|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Oil spill / petroleum soil | Oyster mushroom mycelial mats on straw | Pleurotus ostreatus | 8 weeks for dramatic results; attracts insects → ecological recovery |
| Farm runoff with E. coli | Stropharia mycofilter downslope | Stropharia rugoso-annulata | Dramatic coliform die-off; also removes nitrates/phosphorus |
| Heavy metals in soil/water | Dead fungal biomass columns; mycorrhizal inoculation | A. niger (dead biomass); mycorrhizae (living) | Harvest fruiting bodies as hazardous waste |
| PCB-contaminated soil | Trametes versicolor on inoculated substrate | Trametes versicolor | 95% degradation; nutrient-limited conditions trigger enzymes |
| PAH-contaminated site | White-rot + bacterial co-culture | P. chrysosporium + native bacteria | Co-cultures outperform fungi alone; combine with phytoremediation |
| Pesticide runoff | Straw bale mycofilters in drainage | P. ostreatus; Stropharia | Replace bales every 1-3 years |
| Construction site erosion | Mycelial mats; bunker spawn berms | Oyster; mycorrhizal inoculum | 50% cost of conventional; stabilizes + filters |
| Coliform bacteria in water | Layered mycofilter with corncobs | Stropharia rugoso-annulata | Corncobs ideal for aeration; slow percolation |
| Industrial dye wastewater | Laccase-based treatment | Trametes versicolor | Consider mediator compounds for extended range |

## Key Enzyme Reference

| Enzyme | Produced By | Degrades | Key Requirement |
|--------|------------|----------|-----------------|
| **Lignin Peroxidase (LiP)** | P. chrysosporium, T. versicolor, B. adusta | Non-phenolic aromatics, PCBs, dioxins | Nutrient-limited (N, C) conditions |
| **Manganese Peroxidase (MnP)** | White-rot fungi broadly | PAHs, lignin | Mn(II) and H2O2 required |
| **Laccase** | T. versicolor, P. ostreatus, C. versicolor | Phenols, chlorophenols, PAHs, dyes | Uses O2 (not H2O2); mediators extend range |

## Mycofilter Design Principles

From [[mycofiltration-overview]], the layered construction for water filtration (bottom to top):

1. Gravel base (2-3 inches) — drainage
2. Sawdust/wood chips (3-4 inches) — primary substrate
3. Spawn layer (sawdust spawn at ~1/4 lb/sq ft)
4. Corncobs (4 inches) — aeration and rapid colonization
5. Second spawn layer
6. Waste cardboard — moisture retention, weed suppression
7. Straw (4-6 inches) — shade, moisture retention

Key rules: surface area should be several times the contamination source; water must percolate slowly through, not channel around; annual replenishment needed; replace straw bales every 1-3 years.

## Important Considerations

- Not all strains of a species perform equally — lab testing recommended for critical applications
- Mycelial mats must match the cubic size of the contamination source
- Fruiting bodies that bioaccumulate heavy metals must be treated as hazardous waste
- Fungi often transform pollutants into bound residues rather than fully mineralizing — track metabolites with monitoring
- Temperature and moisture are critical — temperate species may need seasonal deployment
- At very high contaminant concentrations, dilution or phytoremediation may be needed first
- Strain training (gradual exposure to increasing toxin concentrations) can dramatically improve efficacy
- Source wood from sawmills, NOT roadsides — automobile exhaust contaminates wood with lead/aluminum
