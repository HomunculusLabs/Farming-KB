## The Science Behind Biofumigation

The biofumigation process begins with glucosinolates, a diverse group of sulfur-containing compounds that accumulate in
the tissues of Brassica species. When brassica plant material is mechanically damaged by chopping or crushing, an enzyme
called myrosinase comes into contact with glucosinolates and catalyzes their hydrolysis.

This enzymatic reaction produces several breakdown products, the most biologically active of which are isothiocyanates.
These are volatile, lipophilic compounds that act as natural fumigants in the soil environment. They are toxic to a wide
range of soil organisms at sufficient concentrations, including plant-parasitic nematodes, soil-borne fungi such as
Fusarium, Pythium, Rhizoctonia, and Verticillium species, certain insect larvae, and even some weed seeds.
