## Soil Preparation Steps

1. **Test drainage** — Dig a 12" hole, fill with water, and check it drains within 1–2 hours.
2. **Amend heavy clay** — Mix in 2–3 inches of coarse sand or perlite plus 1–2 inches of compost.
3. **Add gravel** — A ½" layer of crushed gravel at the bottom of planting holes or containers improves drainage.
4. **Target pH** — Mediterranean herbs prefer slightly alkaline soil (pH 6.5–7.5). Add lime if your soil is acidic.
5. **Avoid rich soil** — Excess nitrogen produces leggy, less flavorful growth. Lean soil is ideal.

## Container vs. In-Ground Recommendations

**Containers** are ideal for beginners because you control soil quality and drainage. Use pots at least 10–12" in diameter with drainage holes. Terracotta is preferred — it wicks moisture from the sides, reducing overwatering risk.

## Final Notes

Start small — three to four herbs in containers is the perfect beginning. You can always expand next season as your confidence grows. The most important principle to remember: Mediterranean herbs are adapted to hot, dry, rocky hillsides. When in doubt, err on the side of less water, more sun, and poorer soil. Your herbs will reward you with intense flavor and vigorous growth for years to come.
