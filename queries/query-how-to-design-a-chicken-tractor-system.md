# How to Design a Chicken Tractor System
A chicken tractor is a mobile, floorless enclosure that lets chickens
forage on fresh ground while remaining protected from predators.
It is one of the most practical tools available in [[hamilton-animal-manure-types-and-use]] directly onto the soil surface.
This query page covers the core [[holmgren-permaculture-twelve-design-principles-framework]] and management
considerations for building and running an effective chicken tractor
system on a homestead or small farm.
## What Is a Chicken Tractor?
A chicken tractor is a portable coop, often built on a lightweight frame
with wheels or skids, that houses a small flock temporarily on a section
of ground. Unlike a fixed coop, it has no permanent floor, so birds
interact directly with the soil beneath them.
The concept is central to [[animal-tractoring]] used across permaculture
and regenerative agriculture settings.
Tractors range from simple A-frame hoop structures dragged by hand to
larger wheeled units towed by a garden tractor or ATV. The size and
complexity should match your flock size, available space, and how
often you are willing to move the unit.
## Sizing and Capacity
Provide 3 to 4 square feet of indoor shelter per bird, plus at least
8 to 10 square feet of enclosed outdoor run space per bird.
For a small flock of 6 layers, plan for roughly 18 to 24 square feet
of enclosed shelter with a connected scratching zone of 48 to 60
square feet.
Overcrowding leads to stress, feather picking, and rapid accumulation
of nitrogen that can burn plants and create ammonia buildup.
Taller tractors allow roosting space and better airflow, while lower
profile designs are easier to move and less susceptible to wind.
## Weight, Materials, and Mobility
The entire structure should be light enough for one person to move
without mechanical assistance. Use 2x3 or 2x4 lumber for the frame,
1/2-inch hardware cloth for predator-proof walls, and corrugated
metal or asphalt shingles for the roof.
PVC pipe frames are an ultra-light alternative but may not withstand
high winds or heavy snow loads.
Attach wheels on one edge or mount the frame on skid runners so the
tractor can be tipped and rolled to a new position. Pneumatic tires
work well on uneven terrain; solid wheels are fine on flat lawns.
If the tractor will be moved daily, aim for a total weight under
100 pounds. Removeable nest boxes and waterers reduce hauling weight.
## Predator Protection
Use 1/2-inch hardware cloth rather than chicken wire, which predators
can tear through or reach through to grab birds.
Bury or skirt the bottom edge of the wire mesh 6 to 12 inches outward
along the ground perimeter to deter digging animals like raccoons,
foxes, skunks, and weasels.
Install locking latches on all access doors and secure the roof
tightly against raccoons, which are adept at opening simple closures.
In areas with aerial predators such as hawks and owls, cover the top
of the run with the same hardware cloth used on the walls.
Electric net fencing around the tractor adds another layer of defense
for nighttime use if you cannot fully enclose the roof.

## Garden Bed Integration
[[mollison-designers-chicken-tractors-egg-production]] excel as a tool for garden [[azurescens-outdoor-bed-preparation-woodchips]] and
cleanup. After harvesting a crop, move the tractor onto the spent bed
for 1 to 2 weeks.
The birds will eat remaining plant material, scratch up weed seeds,
consume soil-dwelling pest larvae, and fertilize the bed with their
droppings. This creates a ready-to-plant surface with minimal effort.
This practice is a cornerstone of chicken-integration-permaculture-guide
frameworks, replacing fossil-fuel inputs with animal labor.
By cycling birds through food forests, annual vegetable beds, orchard
alleys, and cover-cropped fallow areas, a tractor system closes
nutrient loops on site and reduces the need for imported fertilizers.
The manure deposited by birds feeds soil microbes, which in turn
support the next crop cycle, creating a regenerative feedback loop.
## Key Takeaways
- Provide 3 to 4 sq ft per bird indoors plus 8 to 10 sq ft of run.
- Use 1/2-inch hardware cloth, not chicken wire, and secure edges.
- Keep total weight under 100 lbs for manageable daily moves.
- Rotate every 1 to 3 days in the [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]] to prevent nitrogen
  buildup and overgrazing.
- Integrate with garden beds post-harvest for [[comparison-pheromone-based-pest-control-vs-beneficial-insect-biocontrol]] and soil
  building without mechanical tilling.
- Design for seasonal extremes with shade, wind blocks, drainage,
  and freeze protection for waterers.
- Treat the tractor as a permaculture element that replaces mechanical
  and chemical inputs with biological processes.
