# What Is Hugelkultur and How Do I Build a Bed?
## Short Answer
Hugelkultur is a permaculture technique that involves burying logs, branches, and woody debris under a mound of soil to create a raised growing bed. As the wood decomposes, it acts like a sponge, absorbing and slowly releasing water and nutrients to plants. Hugelkultur beds improve drainage, reduce irrigation needs, build soil organic matter, and can remain productive for 10-20 years without additional fertilization.
## What Is Hugelkultur?
The [[hugelkultur]] concept originated in Eastern Europe and Germany.
The name literally translates to "mound culture" or "hill culture."
It is a form of [[permaculture-earthworks]] that turns waste wood into a long-term growing asset.
The technique is based on a simple observation:
- Fallen trees in a forest create the richest microsites for new growth
- Hugelkultur replicates this natural process in a designed garden bed
## How Hugelkultur Works
The [[hemenway-hugelkultur-guide]] explains the core mechanism.
When wood is buried under soil, a slow decomposition process begins:
1. Fungi colonize the wood and begin breaking down lignin and cellulose
2. Bacteria process simpler compounds and release nutrients
3. The decomposing wood acts as a massive water reservoir
4. Nutrients are released slowly over years as decomposition progresses
Benefits of the decomposing wood core:
- Absorbs rainfall like a sponge, releasing water during dry periods
- Provides slow-release nutrients without added fertilizer
- Generates heat during decomposition, extending the growing season
- Creates air pockets that improve root aeration and drainage
## Choosing Your Wood
Wood selection matters for hugelkultur success.
**Good choices:**
| Wood Type | Characteristics | Decomposition Speed |
|---|---|---|
| Oak | Dense, long-lasting, good structure | Slow (10-15+ years) |
| Maple | Moderate density, good nutrients | Medium (5-10 years) |
| Birch | Good nitrogen content, moderate density | Medium (5-8 years) |
| Pine | Readily available, resinous | Slow to medium |
| Fruit tree prunings | Already on site, good nutrients | Medium (3-7 years) |
| Alder | Nitrogen-fixing, soft, breaks down fast | Fast (2-5 years) |
**Avoid:**
- **Black walnut** - contains juglone, toxic to many plants
- **Treated lumber** - chemicals leach into soil and plants
- **Plywood/MDF** - glues and resins are toxic
- **Poison ivy/oak** - oils persist and cause skin reactions
- **Cedar (excessive)** - very slow to decompose, can be allelopathic
## Building a Hugelkultur Bed
The [[hugelkultur-bed-construction-detailed]] page provides complete instructions.
### Basic Construction Steps
**Step 1: Site preparation**
- Choose a location with adequate sunlight
- Mark out the bed dimensions (typically 4-6 feet wide, any length)
- Optionally dig a trench 6-12 inches deep for better soil contact
**Step 2: Wood core**
- Place the largest logs at the bottom of the bed
- Stack progressively smaller branches on top
- Fill gaps with twigs, wood chips, and brush
**Step 3: Nitrogen layer**
- Add a thick layer (2-4 inches) of nitrogen-rich material:
  - Grass clippings, kitchen scraps, manure, or green leaves
  - This feeds the bacteria decomposing the carbon-rich wood
  - Without this layer, decomposition can temporarily tie up soil nitrogen
**Step 4: Soil and compost layer**
- Add 4-6 inches of topsoil mixed with finished compost
- This is the primary growing layer for seeds and transplants
**Step 5: Mulch layer**
- Top with 2-4 inches of straw, leaves, or wood chip mulch
- This prevents erosion and retains surface moisture
### Bed Height Options
The [[hugelkultur-beds]] resource discusses different design approaches:
| Style | Height | Best Climate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunken | At or below ground level | Arid/dry | Maximizes water retention |
| Ground level | Flat with surrounding ground | Temperate | Low profile, easy to work |
| Low mound | 1-2 feet above ground | Temperate/humid | Good balance of drainage and moisture |
| Tall mound | 3-6 feet above ground | Humid/wet | Excellent drainage, dramatic height |
## Moisture Management
The [[chop-and-drop-mulching]] technique provides woody material to replenish older beds.
Moisture gradient in hugelkultur beds:
- **Top of mound** - driest zone, good for drought-tolerant herbs
- **Sides of mound** - moderate moisture, ideal for most vegetables
- **Base of mound** - wettest zone, perfect for moisture-loving plants
This gradient allows you to grow plants with different water needs in a single bed.
## Planting Strategies
Recommended plants by position:
**Top of mound (dry zone):**
- Rosemary, thyme, oregano
- Lavender, sage
- Garlic, onions
**Sides (moderate zone):**
- Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant
- Beans, peas
- Lettuce, spinach
- Squash, cucumbers
**Base (moist zone):**
- Berries (strawberries, blueberries)
- Mint, parsley, cilantro
- Water-loving greens
- Root vegetables (potatoes, carrots)
## Key Points
- [[hugelkultur]] turns waste wood into a self-fertilizing, water-retaining growing bed
- [[hugelkultur-bed-construction-detailed]] provides complete step-by-step instructions
- The decomposing wood core reduces or eliminates the need for irrigation
- A nitrogen layer between wood and topsoil prevents temporary nitrogen tie-up
- [[chop-and-drop-mulching]] provides woody material to maintain older beds
