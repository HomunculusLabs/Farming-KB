# How Do I Grow Sweet Potatoes from Slips to Harvest?

## Short Answer

Sweet potatoes are grown from slips, which are leafy sprouts that emerge from mature tubers.
You start slips indoors 6 to 8 weeks before your last frost date by suspending a sweet potato half in water.
Once slips develop roots, they are transplanted into warm, well-drained soil after all frost danger has passed.
Sweet potatoes need 90 to 150 frost-free days depending on the variety and your climate.
The [[ipomoea-batatas]] page covers the botanical characteristics and many cultivated varieties available to home growers.
Unlike Irish potatoes which are grown from seed tubers, sweet potatoes are propagated vegetatively from slips.
This distinction is important because it affects how you plan your season and source your planting material.
The [[solomon-sweet-potato-cultivation-guide]] details specific soil amendment recommendations for different soil types.

### Planting and Spacing

Transplant slips into the garden once soil temperatures consistently exceed 60°F (15°C) and all frost danger has passed.
Plant slips deeply, burying the stem up to the first set of leaves so that roots form along the buried stem.
Space plants 12 to 18 inches apart in rows that are 3 to 4 feet apart to give vines room to spread.
If you are planting in raised beds, a spacing of 12 inches in each direction works well in a grid pattern.
Water newly planted slips thoroughly and provide shade cloth for the first few days if conditions are very hot.
The [[root-crops-growing-guide]] provides organic pest management strategies that work well for sweet potatoes.
Floating row covers can protect young slips from flea beetles and other early-season insect pests.

### Harvesting

Sweet potatoes are typically ready to harvest 90 to 150 days after planting, depending on the variety.
Look for signs of maturity such as yellowing leaves and vines that begin to die back.
Before the first frost is critical because frost damages the tubers and reduces storage quality.
Use a digging fork carefully to lift tubers from the soil, working from the edges of the row inward to avoid stabbing them.
Handle harvested tubers gently because any cuts or bruises become entry points for decay during storage.
The [[solomon-growing-root-crops-guide]] includes specific techniques for maximizing yield at harvest time.

### Curing and Storage

Curing is a crucial step that converts starches to sugars, heals minor skin damage, and greatly improves flavor.
To cure, place harvested tubers in a warm (80 to 85°F) and humid (85 to 90 percent) environment for 10 to 14 days.
A greenhouse, small room with a space heater and humidifier, or even a covered porch in warm weather can work for curing.
After curing, store sweet potatoes in a cool (55 to 60°F), dark, and well-ventilated location.
Properly cured and stored sweet potatoes can last 6 months or more without significant quality loss.
Never refrigerate raw sweet potatoes because temperatures below 50°F cause chilling injury and off-flavors.

### What About Growing Sweet Potatoes in Cooler Climates?

Gardeners in short-season climates can still grow sweet potatoes by choosing early-maturing varieties that finish in 90 days.
Varieties like Beauregard, Centennial, and Georgia Jet are known for producing good yields in northern gardens.
Starting slips indoors earlier and using black plastic mulch to pre-warm soil can extend the effective growing season.
Low tunnels or row covers provide additional heat units during cool nights early in the season.
