# What Is the Ideal Temperature Range for Growing Cannabis?

## Short Answer
Cannabis thrives at 20-30°C (68-86°F) overall, but the ideal range shifts by growth
stage: seedlings prefer 22-25°C (72-77°F), vegetative plants do best at 22-28°C
(72-82°F) with warmer days, and flowering plants favor 20-26°C (68-79°F) with
cooler night temperatures to encourage resin production. Maintaining proper
temperature alongside [[humidity-cannabis-growing]] is critical for healthy
development throughout every phase.

## Detailed Explanation

Temperature is one of the most influential environmental variables in cannabis
cultivation. During the [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]], plants benefit from
warmer daytime temperatures in the range of 22-28°C (72-82°F). This warmth drives
metabolic activity, accelerating photosynthesis, stem elongation, and leaf
production. A temperature differential of about 5-8°C between day and night mimics
natural conditions and encourages robust structural growth. Night temperatures
dropping to around 18-22°C (64-72°F) give plants a recovery period while reducing
excessive stretching.

When plants transition to the [[cannabis-flowering-stage]], the optimal temperature
range narrows slightly. Daytime highs of 20-26°C (68-79°F) are ideal, with night
temperatures ideally falling to 16-20°C (61-68°F). This wider diurnal swing is
especially beneficial in the last two to three weeks of flowering, when cooler
nights are believed to stimulate terpene and cannabinoid production. Many
experienced growers purposefully lower nighttime temperatures during late flower to
enhance flavor profiles and resin density.

Seedlings and clones are the most temperature-sensitive stages of cannabis
development. Young plants prefer a consistent 22-25°C (72-77°F) with high humidity.
Temperatures below 18°C (64°F) slow root development and can stunt growth
permanently, while temperatures above 30°C (86°F) increase the risk of damping off
and other seedling diseases. Heating mats set to around 24°C (75°F) are commonly
used for seedling trays and cloning domes to maintain this warm root zone without
overheating the canopy air.

Excessive heat is a common problem, particularly in indoor grows using
high-intensity lighting. Temperatures consistently above 30°C (86°F) can cause a
cascade of problems: reduced photosynthetic efficiency, leaf cupping and curling,
nutrient lockout (especially calcium and magnesium), and increased transpiration
leading to drought stress. The concept of
[[query-what-is-vapor-pressure-deficit-and-why-does-it-matter-for-cannabis]] is
closely tied to temperature, as VPD describes the combined effect of temperature
and humidity on plant transpiration rates. High temperatures with low humidity
create excessive VPD, causing plants to close their stomata and essentially halt
gas exchange and growth.

[[cervantes-temperature-management]] provides detailed guidance on managing climate
in cannabis cultivation environments. Jorge Cervantes emphasizes that temperature
management is not just about ambient air temperature but also about root zone
temperature, canopy surface temperature, and the temperature differential between
the top and bottom of the plant canopy. Root zone temperatures above 25°C (77°F)
can promote pathogens like Pythium, while temperatures below 15°C (59°F) slow
nutrient uptake dramatically regardless of what is available in the growing medium.

The [[cannabis-photoperiod-flowering-cycle]] also interacts with temperature
management in important ways. As the light cycle shortens to 12 hours on and 12
hours off to induce flowering, growers should simultaneously adjust temperature
targets downward. Many modern [[cannabis-indoor-grow-lighting-systems]] generate
significant heat, and managing this thermal load is essential for maintaining the
correct temperature range. LED systems produce less radiant heat than HPS or MH
fixtures, which can make temperature management easier but may require supplemental
heating during dark periods in cooler climates.

Temperature stress can manifest in ways that overlap with other problems, making
diagnosis tricky. For example, [[cannabis-light-burn]] from overly intense lighting
can cause leaf symptoms nearly identical to heat stress, including upward-curling
leaf edges and bleached or yellowed tissue at the canopy top. Distinguishing
between pure heat stress and light stress is important for applying the correct
remedy. A simple diagnostic test is to place your hand at canopy level: if it feels
uncomfortably warm to the back of your hand after 30 seconds, the combination of
heat and light intensity is likely too high.

In soil grows, temperature affects the microbial community responsible for nutrient
cycling and organic matter decomposition. Cooler soil temperatures slow microbial
activity, reducing the availability of organic nutrients that depend on biological
breakdown. In hydroponic systems, temperature directly affects dissolved oxygen
levels in the nutrient solution: warmer water holds less oxygen, which can suffocate
roots and promote anaerobic pathogens. A water chiller is often necessary in
recirculating hydroponic systems to maintain root zone temperatures between 18-22°C
(64-72°F).

## Key Points
- Seedlings prefer 22-25°C (72-77°F) with minimal fluctuation to encourage strong
  root establishment
- Vegetative stage thrives at 22-28°C (72-82°F) daytime with a 5-8°C nighttime
  drop for structure
- Flowering stage benefits from 20-26°C (68-79°F) days and cooler nights below
  20°C for resin production
- Temperatures above 30°C (86°F) cause heat stress, nutrient lockout, and
  significantly reduced yields
