## How to Fix Phosphorus Deficiency in Cannabis

## Short Answer
Phosphorus deficiency in cannabis causes dark green or purplish leaves, stunted 
growth,
and slow flowering. It is fixed by correcting pH, applying a phosphorus-rich 
fertilizer or
amendment, and ensuring soil temperatures stay above 60 degrees Fahrenheit. The
[[cannabis-environment-control]] page covers temperature management strategies 
for the root
zone.

Overwatering can also trigger phosphorus deficiency symptoms by reducing root 
oxygen levels
and impairing [[compost-tea-guide]] 
brew.
Synthetic growers can use a bloom fertilizer with elevated P-K values or a 
standalone
phosphorus supplement like monopotassium phosphate. Always start with a lower 
dose and
increase gradually, as excess phosphorus causes its own problems including zinc 
and iron
lockout.

During flowering, phosphorus demand peaks as the plant channels energy into bud 
production.
Many growers switch to a bloom-specific nutrient formula with an NPK ratio 
weighted toward
phosphorus and potassium around weeks 3 to 4 of flower. The [[cannabis-grow-nutrient-deficiency-problem-solving]] page offers a 
comprehensive
troubleshooting framework for all nutrient issues encountered during a cannabis 
grow.

## Key Points
- Check root zone pH first; phosphorus lockout from improper pH is more common than actual deficiency
- Cold root zone temperatures (below 60 degrees Fahrenheit) severely limit phosphorus uptake rates
