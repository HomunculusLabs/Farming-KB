# What Is the Difference Between Wet Trimming and Dry Trimming Cannabis?

## Short Answer
Wet trimming involves removing sugar leaves immediately after harvest while
the plant is still fresh and turgid, resulting in a tighter appearance and
faster drying time. Dry trimming means hanging the whole plant to dry for
several days before removing leaves, which preserves [[cervantes-gray-mold-botrytis-prevention]] and other mold

humid, the extended leaf coverage increases mold risk substantially. If it
is too dry, the outer buds can become brittle while the inner buds remain
moist, leading to uneven drying. Dry trimming also requires more patience
and can be more difficult on the hands since the dried sugar leaves become
crispy and create more mess during the cutting process compared to wet
trimming.

The choice between methods often comes down to the grower's drying
environment and available infrastructure. In arid climates, dry trimming
provides beneficial leaf coverage that slows moisture loss and prevents
overdrying. In humid climates, wet trimming removes excess plant material
that could harbor mold and improves airflow around the buds. Many
experienced growers use a hybrid approach, removing large fan leaves while
wet and leaving sugar leaves to protect buds during the initial drying
phase, then performing a final precision trim after 3 to 5 days of
hanging. This combines the space efficiency of wet trimming with the
trichome protection benefits of dry trimming. Regardless of method,
