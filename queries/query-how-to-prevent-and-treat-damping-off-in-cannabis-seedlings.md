# How to Prevent and Treat Damping Off in Cannabis Seedlings

## Short Answer
Damping off is a fungal disease that kills cannabis seedlings by attacking the 
stem at the
soil line, causing them to topple over and die. It is caused primarily by 
Pythium, Fusarium,
and Rhizoctonia species that thrive in wet, poorly drained conditions. 
Prevention through
proper sterilization, well-draining soil, moderate moisture, and good air 
circulation is far
more effective than treatment after symptoms appear. The
[[cervantes-damping-off-seedling-disease-prevention]] page provides 
comprehensive prevention
protocols.

## Detailed Explanation

Damping off is one of the most devastating problems for cannabis growers because
 it strikes
at the most vulnerable stage of plant life. Seedlings that appear healthy one 
day can be
dead the next, their thin stems pinched and collapsed at the soil surface. The 
pathogens
responsible are ubiquitous in most growing environments and survive in soil, on 
tools, and
on trays. Understanding how these organisms operate and what conditions favor 
them is the
foundation of effective prevention.

Several fungal and oomycete organisms cause damping off in cannabis seedlings. 
Pythium
species are water molds that thrive in saturated, poorly drained media and are 
the most
common culprit. Fusarium species are soil-borne fungi that attack both seeds 
before
germination and seedlings after emergence. Rhizoctonia solani causes wirestem, 
where the
stem becomes constricted and darkened at the soil line before the seedling 
collapses. All
three pathogens are favored by the same conditions: excess moisture, poor air 
circulation,
cool temperatures, and high organic matter in the seed-starting medium. The
[[cervantes-root-rot-pythium-damping-off]] page covers the pathogen biology in 
detail.

Symptoms vary depending on the pathogen and the stage of attack. Pre-emergence 
damping off
occurs when seeds rot in the soil before they ever sprout, often caused by 
Pythium in
overly wet media. Post-emergence damping off is more visible: the seedling 
emerges normally
but the stem becomes water-soaked, thin, and constricted at the soil line, 
eventually
collapsing. The cotyledons may appear healthy even as the stem fails. In some 
cases, the
seedling shows poor growth, yellowing, and wilting before collapsing. The
[[cannabis-seedling-care-guide]] and [[cervantes-seedling-care-early-growth]] 
pages describe
normal seedling development for comparison with damping off symptoms.

Prevention starts with your seed-starting medium. Use a sterile, well-draining 
mix
specifically formulated for seed starting. Avoid using garden soil or compost 
for seed
starting, as these contain the very organisms that cause damping off. Commercial
seed-starting mixes based on peat moss or coco coir with perlite or vermiculite 
provide
good drainage while retaining adequate moisture for germination. The 
[[substrate-preparation]]
and [[substrate-pasteurization]] pages cover medium sterilization techniques for
 growers who
prefer to make their own mix.

Moisture management is the single most important prevention factor. Overwatering
 creates the
anaerobic conditions that damping-off pathogens love. Keep the seed-starting 
medium moist
but never saturated. Water from below by placing the seedling tray in a shallow 
reservoir
and allowing the medium to wick moisture upward. This keeps the soil surface 
drier, reducing
pathogen colonization of the critical stem-soil interface. Allow the surface to 
dry slightly
between waterings. A spray bottle for surface misting gives more control than 
pouring water
directly on delicate seedlings. The [[cannabis-germination-techniques]] and
[[cervantes-seed-germination]] pages cover proper moisture levels for cannabis 
seed starting.

Sanitation cannot be overstated. Sterilize all seedling trays, pots, and tools 
with a 10
percent bleach solution or 70 percent isopropyl alcohol before use. Wash your 
hands
thoroughly before handling seeds and seedlings. Never reuse seed-starting 
medium. If you
have experienced damping off in the past, replace or thoroughly sterilize any 
equipment that
contacted infected plants. The [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] 
page covers
sterilization methods for grow equipment and growing containers.

Environmental conditions influence damping-off risk significantly. Maintain 
seedling
temperatures between 70 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit for optimal germination and 
early growth.
Provide gentle air circulation with a small fan positioned to create a light 
breeze across
the seedlings without blowing them over. Good airflow strengthens stems and 
reduces the
humid microclimate at the soil surface where pathogens thrive. Avoid crowding 
seedlings, as
this restricts airflow and increases humidity around the stems.

Biological controls offer an organic defense against damping off. [[trichoderma-forest-green-mold-guide]]
species are beneficial fungi that colonize root zones and outcompete damping-off
 pathogens
for space and nutrients. Applying trichoderma to your seed-starting medium or as
 a root
drench provides ongoing protection throughout the seedling stage. [[ingham-field-guide-compost-tea-microscope-analysis]] containing diverse beneficial microorganisms can also suppress damping-off
 pathogens
when used as a soil drench at planting time. These biological approaches are 
more effective
and sustainable than chemical fungicides for most home growers.

## Key Points
- Damping off is caused by Pythium, Fusarium, and Rhizoctonia; prevention is far more effective than treatment
- Use sterile, well-draining seed-starting medium and avoid overwatering; keep the surface slightly dry
- Sterilize all trays, tools, and containers before use; never reuse seed-starting medium
- Maintain 70 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit with gentle air circulation around seedlings
- Apply [[trichoderma-forest-green-mold-guide]] or [[cervantes-compost-tea-organic-brewing]] as biological preventives
