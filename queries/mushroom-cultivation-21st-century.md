# 21st Century Mushroom Cultivation

The modern era of mushroom growing: internet democratization, monotub cultivation, gourmet market expansion, mycoremediation applications, and social media-driven mainstream adoption. See [[mushroom-cultivation-history]] for the complete arc from ancient Asian log culture through the 20th century.

## Internet Democratization (2000s)

- **2000s — Internet democratizes knowledge:**
  Online forums (Shroomery, Mycotopia, Topia),
  video tutorials on YouTube, and wikis democratize cultivation knowledge.
  Home cultivation community grows exponentially.

- **2005 — Mycelium Running:**
  [[paul-stamets]] publishes his magnum opus,
  linking mushroom cultivation to ecological restoration
  through mycorestoration.
  Introduces outdoor cultivation methods for garden integration.
  See [[mycorestoration-overview]] and [[garden-mushroom-growing]].

## Indoor Cultivation Standardizes (2000s-2010s)

- **2000s-2010s — Monotub cultivation standardizes:**
  The monotub (modified storage tote with polyfill filters)
  becomes the standard home cultivation method, replacing the PF TEK
  for larger yields.
  See [[monotub-cultivation]] and [[mushroom-indoor-cultivation]].

- **2010s — Indoor cultivation technology advances:**
  Automated environmental control systems (temperature, humidity, CO2, FAE),
  Martha tents, modular growing rooms, and all-in-one grow bags
  simplify cultivation.
  Liquid culture (LC) techniques enable rapid mycelium expansion.
  See [[mushroom-grow-equipment]] and [[mushroom-growing-reference]].

## Commercial Expansion and Applied Mycology (2010s)

- **2010s — Gourmet mushroom market booms:**
  Consumer demand for specialty mushrooms drives commercial expansion.
  Farmers markets, restaurants, health food stores, and online suppliers
  create new markets.
  Functional and medicinal mushroom products become mainstream.
  See [[mushroom-nutrition-and-medicinal]] and [[medicinal-mushroom-guide]].

- **2010s — Mycoremediation applications:**
  Mushrooms cultivated for environmental cleanup:
  oyster mushrooms for petroleum degradation,
  king stropharia for bacterial filtration,
  various species for heavy metal sequestration.
  See [[mycoremediation-basics]].

- **2010s — Outdoor cultivation expands:**
  Wine cap (Stropharia rugoso-annulata) and king stropharia
  cultivation in garden beds becomes popular
  among permaculture practitioners and homesteaders.
  See [[outdoor-mushroom-bed]] and [[garden-mushroom-growing]].

## Mainstream Adoption (2019-Present)

- **2019 — Fantastic Fungi documentary:**
  Louis Schwartzberg's documentary brings mushroom cultivation
  and mycology to mainstream audiences.
  Paul Stamets featured prominently.

- **2020s — Social media drives mainstream mycology:**
  Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube creators share cultivation tutorials,
  identification guides, and foraging content.
  Home cultivation supply industry matures with standardized products.

- **2020s — Living soil integration:**
  Mushroom cultivation techniques integrated with [[cannabis-indoor-living-soil]]
  and [[regenerative-agriculture]] practices on homesteads.
  Mycorrhizal inoculants become standard in organic gardening.
  See [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] and [[soil-food-web]].

- **2020s — Commercial gourmet industry matures:**
  Large-scale indoor cultivation facilities produce oyster, shiitake,
  and lion's mane year-round.
  Farm-to-table restaurants source locally grown specialty mushrooms.
  Direct-to-consumer mushroom growing kits become popular gifts.
