# How Do I Prevent and Treat Bud Rot on Cannabis?

## Short Answer
Bud rot, caused by the fungus Botrytis cinerea, is one of the most devastating
cannabis diseases. Prevention focuses on maintaining low humidity below 50
percent during late flowering, ensuring good airflow between buds, removing dead
or damaged plant material, and applying preventive organic sprays. Once bud rot
appears inside a cola, the affected bud must be removed immediately to prevent
spread.

## Detailed Explanation

Bud rot, also known as gray mold, is caused by the pathogenic fungus Botrytis
cinerea and represents one of the greatest threats to outdoor and indoor
cannabis crops alike. The query how do i prevent and treat bud rot on cannabis details the full
life cycle of this pathogen and how it infects cannabis flowers. The fungus
thrives in cool, humid conditions and typically attacks the densest, most
moisture-retentive parts of the plant first, which means large, tight colas are
at the highest risk, especially during the final weeks of flowering when buds
are at their densest.

Prevention is far more effective than treatment, and humidity management is the
cornerstone of any bud rot prevention strategy. During the
[[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]], especially the final three to four weeks, relative humidity should be kept below 50 percent, with 40 to 45 percent being ideal. The [[query-how-to-manage-humidity-cannabis-grow-room]]
covers equipment and techniques for maintaining these levels. In outdoor grows
where you cannot control humidity, choosing strains with looser bud structure
and ensuring adequate plant spacing for air circulation are your best defenses.

Airflow is equally critical. Fans should create enough movement to gently sway
all parts of the plant, including the inner canopy where humidity can become
trapped. [[cannabis-pruning-and-defoliation-guide]]
techniques like lollipopping the lower branches and removing large fan leaves
that block airflow through the canopy can dramatically reduce bud rot risk. The
[[cannabis-mold-and-mildew-prevention]] page provides
a comprehensive framework for keeping your grow space free from fungal
pathogens.

Plant genetics play a significant role in bud rot susceptibility. Strains with
extremely dense, tight bud structures, particularly heavy indica varieties, are
more prone to trapping moisture and creating the microclimate that Botrytis
needs to establish. When reviewing
[[cannabis-strain-guide-reference]] for outdoor or humid climate
grows, look for descriptions mentioning mold resistance or airy bud structure.
The [[cannabis-seed-selection-and-strain-types]] offers
guidance on choosing genetics suited to your specific environment.

Organic preventive sprays can provide an additional layer of protection.
Products containing Bacillus subtilis, Trichoderma, or potassium bicarbonate
create an unfavorable environment for fungal spores on the plant surface. The
[[cannabis-pest-management-guide]] page covers integrated approaches
that combine biological controls with cultural practices. Neem oil and
[[cervantes-neem-oil-organic-pest-control]] have some antifungal properties, though
they should be used cautiously during flowering to avoid affecting taste and
resin quality.

If you discover bud rot, immediate action is required. Remove the affected bud
and at least one inch of healthy tissue below the visible damage. Use sterilized
scissors and clean them between cuts to avoid spreading spores. Dispose of
infected material away from your grow space; do not add it to compost. After
removal, increase airflow and decrease humidity to create conditions unfavorable
for further spread. If more than 10 to 20 percent of the canopy is affected,
early harvest may be the best option to salvage what you can.

Environmental monitoring tools are a worthwhile investment for any serious
grower. A quality hygrometer placed at canopy level helps you track humidity
trends and catch dangerous spikes before they cause problems. In humid climates,
a dehumidifier is often necessary during late flowering. The
[[cannabis-environment-control]] covers the full range
of tools and techniques for maintaining optimal growing conditions throughout
all stages of the cannabis life cycle.

Post-harvest, the risk of bud rot continues during the drying and curing phases.
The [[comparison-cannabis-drying-and-curing-vs-flushing-before-harvest]] process must be done
carefully with adequate airflow and temperature control. Buds should be dried
slowly over 7 to 14 days at around 60 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit and 50 to 60
percent humidity. Rushing the dry or curing in jars that are too wet creates
ideal conditions for Botrytis to develop on your finished product, potentially
ruining months of work.
