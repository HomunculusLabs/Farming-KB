# How Do I Prune Brambles for Maximum Fruit Production

Pruning is the single most impactful practice for maximizing bramble yield and fruit quality. Neglected brambles produce tangled, shaded canopies with small, disease-prone berries. A properly pruned bramble planting can yield 2–3× more high-quality fruit than an unmanaged one. This guide covers the pruning systems for all major bramble types.

2. **After summer harvest:** Cut these spent floricanes to the ground.
3. **New primocanes** emerging from the base will produce the fall crop as usual.

## Step 4 — Summer Tip Pruning (Erect Blackberries and Black Raspberries Only)

This is a separate, mid-season operation that significantly boosts yield on floricane-fruiting types.

**Erect blackberries:**
- When primocanes reach 36–48 inches tall, pinch off the top 2–3 inches of each cane.
- This stops upward growth and forces the cane to produce 4–8 lateral branches.
- Each lateral branch becomes a fruiting surface next year, multiplying your yield per cane.
- Do this once — don't repeatedly tip-prune the resulting laterals.

**Black raspberries:**
- When primocanes reach 24–30 inches, pinch the top 2 inches.
- This forces lateral branching similar to erect blackberries.
- If you miss the summer window, you can do this during dormant pruning instead.

**Do NOT tip-prune trailing blackberries.** Their long, unbranched canes ARE their fruiting structure. Cutting the tips reduces yield.

**Do NOT tip-prune red raspberries.** They fruit along the entire length of the cane, not on laterals.
