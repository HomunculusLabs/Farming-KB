## What Is Lollipopping Cannabis and When Should You Do It?

## Short Answer
Lollipopping is a pruning technique where you remove the lower branches, foliage, and
bud sites from a cannabis plant, typically up to the bottom one-third of the canopy.
This directs the plant's energy away from small, low-quality lower buds and toward the
top colas that receive the most light. It is typically performed just before or shortly
after switching to the flowering light cycle for best results.

## Detailed Explanation

The term "lollipopping" comes from the shape the plant takes after pruning: a bare stem
at the bottom with a dense, round canopy of bud at the top, resembling a lollipop. The
underlying principle is straightforward and based on plant biology: lower branches that
receive little to no direct light produce small, airy buds with low cannabinoid and
terpene content. These underdeveloped buds consume energy and nutrients that could
otherwise be redirected to the top of the canopy where light intensity is highest and
bud quality is maximized. Lollipopping is one of several [[query-what-is-defoliation-and-when-should-i-defoliate-cannabis]].

### When to Lollipop

Timing is critical for effective lollipopping. The most common approach is to lollipop
during the transition from vegetative to flowering, either a few days before the light
flip or within the first one to two weeks of flowering (often referred to as the
"stretch" phase). During this window, the plant is still flexible in its energy
allocation, and removing lower growth early means the plant never wastes resources on
buds that would be suboptimal. Lollipopping too early in [[cannabis-flowering-stage]] management.

### How to Lollipop: Step by Step

Start by identifying the canopy line, which is the boundary between well-lit upper
foliage and shaded lower growth. Everything below this line, typically the bottom third
of the plant, should be removed. Use clean, sharp pruning shears or scissors sterilized
with rubbing alcohol. Remove entire branches at their junction with the main stem
rather than leaving stubs, as clean cuts heal faster and reduce the risk of infection.
Also remove any yellowing, dead, or damaged foliage on the lower portions of the upper
branches. Do not remove more than one-third of the plant's total foliage at once, as
this can cause excessive stress. After lollipopping, the plant should still have a full,
healthy canopy at the top with adequate leaf area for photosynthesis.

### Lollipopping vs. Other Pruning Techniques

Lollipopping is often confused with or combined with other pruning methods, but there
are important distinctions. Standard pruning involves selectively removing branches to
