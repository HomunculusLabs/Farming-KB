# Cannabis Prohibition History

From ancient use to modern legalization: the political, legal, and cultural forces
that criminalized and are now re-legalizing cannabis.
See [[cannabis-health-therapeutics]] for medical context, [[robert-clarke]]
for botanical and ethnobotanical history, and [[cannabis-genetics-and-breeding]]
for how prohibition shaped cannabis genetics.

## Ancient and Pre-Modern Use

- **~2700 BCE — Emperor Shen Nung (China):**
  First recorded therapeutic use of cannabis in the Chinese pharmacopeia Pen Ts'ao.
  Recommended for rheumatism, gout, malaria, and absent-mindedness.
  Fibers used for rope and cloth.

- **~1500 BCE — Ayurvedic medicine (India):**
  Cannabis (bhang) used in religious rituals and medicine for thousands of years.
  Served as an anesthetic, treatment for digestive ailments,
  and religious sacrament in Shiva worship.

- **~1000 BCE — Assyrian use:**
  Cannabis used as incense in religious ceremonies and medicine
  in ancient Mesopotamia.

- **~500 BCE — Scythian ritual use:**
  Greek historian Herodotus records Scythians inhaling cannabis vapor
  in steam baths during funeral rites.

- **Ancient Greece and Rome:**
  Galen and Dioscorides document cannabis for earaches,
  inflammation, and flatulence.

- **Medieval Islamic world:**
  Cannabis widely used as medicine and recreational drug (hashish).
  Ibn al-Baytar's 13th century pharmacopeia documents numerous preparations.

- **Middle Ages Europe:**
  Cannabis grown throughout Europe for fiber (hemp).
  Hemp rope, sails, and cloth essential to navies and economies.

## Colonial Era to Early 20th Century

- **1600s-1700s — Colonial hemp cultivation:**
  Hemp (low-THC Cannabis sativa) grown widely in the American colonies
  for fiber, rope, and sailcloth.
  George Washington and Thomas Jefferson grew hemp.

- **Early 1800s — Medical cannabis enters Western medicine:**
  William O'Shaughnessy, an Irish physician working in Bengal,
  introduces Indian cannabis preparations to Western medicine
  after observing their use for pain, spasms, and convulsions.
  Tinctures and extracts sold in pharmacies throughout Europe and America.

- **1850s-1900s — Patent medicine era:**
  Cannabis extracts sold in US pharmacies for pain, migraine,
  sleep disorders, and menstrual cramps.
  Over 100 cannabis-based patent medicines marketed.
  No significant restrictions on medical use.

- **1906 — Pure Food and Drug Act:**
  Required labeling of cannabis-containing medicines
  but did not restrict sale.
  First federal regulation of any kind on cannabis products.

## The Prohibition Campaign (1910s-1937)

- **1910-1920s — Mexican migration and "marijuana":**
  Mexican immigrants bring recreational cannabis use (smoked)
  to the US Southwest following the Mexican Revolution.
  The term "marijuana" deliberately chosen by prohibitionists
  to associate the drug with Mexicans
  and avoid the familiar medical term "cannabis."

- **1913 — California first state ban:**
  California becomes one of the first states to criminalize cannabis,
  targeting the growing Mexican-American population in Los Angeles.

- **1915-1930s — State-level prohibition wave:**
  29 states pass laws restricting or banning cannabis by 1931.
  Laws often framed around racial fears:
  Mexican-Americans in the Southwest, African-Americans in the South,
  Caribbean immigrants in the Northeast.

- **1930 — Federal Bureau of Narcotics established:**
  Harry J. Anslinger appointed first commissioner.
  Anslinger, who had previously opposed criminalizing cannabis,
  becomes the driving force behind federal prohibition
  after the end of alcohol prohibition.

- **1936 — Propaganda campaign:**
  Films like "Reefer Madness" and "Assassin of Youth"
  depict cannabis as causing insanity, violence, and death.

- **1937 — Marihuana Tax Act:**
  Anslinger testifies before Congress, citing anecdotal reports of violence.
  No medical testimony in favor was heard.
  The AMA opposed the Act, arguing it would prevent medical access,
  but was overruled.
  The Act imposed a $100 tax on cannabis transfers,
  effectively prohibiting cannabis without technically banning it.

## Federal Prohibition and Escalation (1937-1970)

- **1942 — Cannabis removed from US Pharmacopeia:**
  After approximately 5,000 years of recorded medical use,
  cannabis dropped from the official list of recognized medicines.

- **1944 — La Guardia Report:**
  New York Mayor Fiorello La Guardia commissions a comprehensive study.
  The report finds that cannabis does not cause violence,
  addiction, or insanity — directly contradicting Anslinger's claims.
  Anslinger suppressed and attacked the report.

- **1951 — Boggs Act:**
  Imposes mandatory minimum sentences for drug offenses,
  including cannabis.
  First time mandatory minimums applied to drug crimes at the federal level.

- **1956 — Narcotics Control Act:**
  Dramatically increases penalties for cannabis possession and sale.
  Possession with intent to distribute: 5-10 years.

- **1960s — Counterculture and cannabis:**
  Cannabis use surges among white middle-class youth
  during the counterculture era.

- **1969 — Leary v. United States:**
  Supreme Court strikes down the Marihuana Tax Act
  as unconstitutional under the Fifth Amendment.
  Congress responds by passing new legislation the same year.
