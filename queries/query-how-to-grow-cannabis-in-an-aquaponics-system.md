## Short Answer
Aquaponics combines [[query-how-does-the-nitrogen-cycle-work-in-a-garden]]. Fish
excrete ammonia through their gills and waste, which is toxic to both fish and
plants. Nitrosomonas bacteria convert ammonia into nitrite, and Nitrobacter
bacteria then convert nitrite into nitrate, which is the primary form of
nitrogen that plants can absorb. This bacterial colonization happens naturally
in the biofilter, which is typically a media bed filled with expanded clay
pebbles, lava rock, or BioStrate pads. Establishing this bacterial colony takes
four to six weeks, a process called cycling, before plants can be introduced.

Choosing the right fish species is critical. Tilapia are the most popular choice
for cannabis aquaponics because they are hardy, tolerate a wide temperature
range, grow quickly, and tolerate higher nutrient concentrations than many other
species. Goldfish and koi are lower-maintenance alternatives that produce less
waste, suitable for smaller systems or growers who want to keep fish maintenance
minimal. For growers in cooler climates, trout and perch can work but require
lower water temperatures that may slow cannabis growth. The

before adding cannabis is strongly recommended. Start with easy plants like
lettuce, basil, or tomatoes for the first few months to dial in your system
parameters. Once the biofilter is mature and you are comfortable maintaining
water quality, transition to cannabis clones rather than seeds, as established
root systems adapt more quickly to the aquaponic environment.
