## How to Use Aquaponics to Grow Herbs and Vegetables

Aquaponics combines aquaculture (raising fish) with hydroponics (growing plants in water) into a single recirculating system. Fish waste provides organic nutrients [[query-how-to-use-kelp-meal-as-fertilizer]], and the cleaned water returns to the fish tank. The three living components, fish, bacteria, and plants, depend on each other in a symbiotic loop.

There is no soil involved. Plants grow in a grow bed filled with a media such as expanded clay pebbles (hydroton), gravel, or river rock. Water from the fish tank is pumped into the grow bed, where bacteria colonize the media surface and plant roots take up nutrients.

## Choosing Your System Design

Several aquaponics configurations work well for herbs and vegetables, each with trade-offs in complexity, cost, and space.

**Media Bed (Flood and Drain):** The most beginner-friendly design. A bell siphon alternately floods and drains the grow bed. This provides excellent oxygenation for plant roots and supports a large bacterial colony. Best for mixed greens, herbs, and fruiting vegetables.

## Common Problems and Solutions

**Cloudy Water:** Usually caused by suspended bacteria or algae bloom during cycling. Reduce light exposure on the fish tank and avoid overfeeding. Cloudiness typically resolves within a week as the system balances.

**Plant Yellowing (Nitrogen Deficiency):** Common in new systems with too few fish or an immature biofilter. Increase feeding rate slightly or add a few more fish. Ensure the biofilter has adequate surface area for bacterial colonization.

**Fish Gasping at Surface:** Indicates low dissolved oxygen. Check that air stones are functioning and the water pump is circulating properly. Reduce feeding temporarily to lower biological oxygen demand.

**Root Rot or Slimy Roots:** Usually caused by insufficient oxygenation at the root zone. Ensure the bell siphon is draining properly between flood cycles. In DWC systems, check air stone output and consider adding additional aeration.

**pH Drift Upward:** Very common in aquaponics. Carbonates accumulate from fish food and tap water. Dose with a mild acid to target pH 6.8. Avoid rapid pH swings of more than 0.2 per day as this stresses fish and bacteria.

## Getting Started: A Simple Starter Setup

For under $500, you can build a functional 50-gallon media bed system. You need a 50 to 100-gallon fish tank (an IBC tote cut in half is popular), a 50-gallon grow bed with a bell siphon, a submersible water pump rated for the tank-to-bed lift height, expanded clay media, air pump with air stones, and a water test kit.

Position the grow bed above the fish tank so water drains back by gravity. The pump pushes water from the fish tank up to the grow bed. The bell siphon automatically floods and drains the bed every 15 minutes, providing the oxygen-rich environment that both plants and bacteria need.

Start with basil and lettuce. They grow fast, tolerate system fluctuations, and provide visible success within weeks. Add fish gradually and let the system teach you its rhythms before expanding to more demanding crops.
