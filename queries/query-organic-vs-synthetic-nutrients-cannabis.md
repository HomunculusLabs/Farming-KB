# What is the Difference Between Organic and Synthetic Nutrients for Cannabis?

## Short Answer
Organic nutrients are derived from natural sources like compost, bone meal,
and kelp, and they feed the soil microbiome which in turn feeds the plant.
Synthetic nutrients are manufactured mineral salts that are immediately
available to plant roots in precise ratios. Organic growing produces more
complex flavors and requires less frequent feeding, while synthetic growing
offers faster growth, precise control, and typically higher yields but can
result in a harsher final product if not flushed properly.

## Detailed Explanation
The choice between organic and synthetic nutrients is one of the most
fundamental decisions a cannabis grower makes, and it affects everything
from plant health and growth rate to the flavor, aroma, and smoothness of
the final product. Both approaches can produce excellent results, but they
work in fundamentally different ways and require different skill sets and
management practices.

Synthetic nutrients, also called mineral or salt-based nutrients, are
chemically manufactured compounds that provide nitrogen, phosphorus,
potassium, and micronutrients in forms that are immediately soluble and
available to plant roots. When you mix a synthetic [[cannabis-nutrient-management]]
guide covers how to calculate feeding schedules, monitor EC and pH levels,

The primary drawback of synthetic nutrients is the potential for salt
buildup in the growing medium. Over time, unused mineral salts accumulate
and can raise the EC of the root zone to toxic levels, causing nutrient
lockout, leaf tip burn, and reduced growth. This is why flushing with plain
water before harvest is standard practice with synthetic nutrients, allowing
the plant to use up stored salts so the final product burns cleanly and
tastes smooth. Without proper flushing, synthetically grown cannabis can
have a harsh, chemical taste.

Organic nutrients, in contrast, work by feeding the soil ecosystem rather
than the plant directly. [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] explains how organic growing creates a
self-sustaining ecosystem where the soil microbiome regulates nutrient
availability based on plant demand. In a well-established living soil, the
plant sends root exudates into the soil that attract specific microbes which
in turn release the exact nutrients the plant needs. This biological
feedback loop means that over-fertilization is much less common in organic
systems, and plants tend to develop stronger natural defenses against pests
and disease.
