# Best Elderberry Varieties for Different Climates and Uses

## Direct Answer

The **best all-around elderberry variety is Bob Gordon** — it offers the highest yields, excellent anthocyanin content, and performs well across zones 4–9. For cold climates, **Nova and York** are the most reliable picks. For European growers, **Haschberg and Samdal** lead in commercial production. Home gardeners should consider **York** (compact, heavy-bearing) or ornamental **Black Lace**. No elderberry is self-fruitful — you always need **two different varieties** for cross-pollination.

---

## Cold Climates (Zones 3–5)

### Nova
- Hardiest American cultivar; thrives in zone 3.
- Berries are medium-sized, born on large, flat cymes.
- Ripens early to mid-August. Excellent paired with York.
- Vigorous grower reaching 8–10 ft.

### York
- Reliable in zones 3–8; the most widely available variety.
- Largest berry clusters of any American elderberry.
- Ripens late August to September — extends harvest window.
- Pairs perfectly with Nova for sequential picking.

### Adams 1 & Adams 2
- Classic American selections; good cold hardiness.
- Adams 2 has slightly larger berries than Adams 1.
- Use alongside York or Nova for pollination.

### Johns
- Developed in New York; extremely cold-hardy.
- Produces very large, juicy berries.
- Less commonly available but worth sourcing from specialty nurseries.

### Marge
- Finnish-bred hybrid (S. canadensis × S. nigra).
- Exceptional cold tolerance down to zone 3.
- High anthocyanin content makes it a top medicinal pick for cold regions.

---

## Mild and Warm Climates (Zones 6–9)

### Bob Gordon
- Developed at the University of Missouri; zones 4–9.
- Yields up to 12,000 lbs/acre in commercial trials.
- Berries are extremely sweet with high anthocyanin levels.
- Upright, less-suckering habit simplifies management.

### Wyldewood
- Also from Missouri State's breeding program.
- Ripens 7–10 days before Bob Gordon.
- Very high anthocyanin concentration; preferred for supplements.
- Excellent for zones 5–9.

### Haschberg
- Austrian cultivar; the premier European variety for warm zones.
- Thrives in zones 5–8; tolerates heat better than most.
- Massive yields; the dominant European commercial choice.

---

## Best European Varieties (S. nigra)

### Haschberg
- Industry standard in Austria, Denmark, and Germany.
- Consistent, heavy yields on manageable 6–8 ft bushes.
- The benchmark for European elderflower and berry production.

### Korsør
- Danish selection; very high total anthocyanin content.
- Extremely vigorous; can reach 12 ft if unpruned.
- Popular in European nutraceutical production.

### Samdal
- Danish cultivar bred for large berries and easy harvest.
- Semi-upright habit suits mechanical harvesting.
- Ripens mid-season; excellent paired with Samyl.

### Sambu
- Swiss cultivar; very high berry count per umbel.
- Slightly earlier ripening than Haschberg.
- Less widely available outside central Europe.

## Best for Commercial Production

Commercial growers prioritize **yield, uniformity, and machine-harvest compatibility**:

| Rank | Variety | Yield Potential | Machine-Harvest Ready |
|------|---------|----------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Bob Gordon | Very high (10,000–12,000 lbs/ac) | Yes |
| 2 | Wyldewood | Very high | Yes |
| 3 | Samdal | High | Yes — bred for it |
| 4 | Haschberg | High | Yes — European standard |

Bob Gordon and Wyldewood also have the highest anthocyanin concentrations, making them dual-purpose (fresh market + nutraceutical extraction).

## Best for Home Gardens

- **York** — Compact (6–8 ft), massive flower heads, heavy reliable yields. Best all-around home variety.
- **Nova** — Slightly smaller than York; early ripening lets you harvest before birds focus in.
- **Black Beauty** (*S. nigra*) — Dark purple dissected foliage, pink flowers, usable berries at lower yield. Works as a privacy screen.
- **Black Lace** (*S. nigra*) — Finely cut foliage resembling Japanese maple. Zone 4 hardy ornamental.

## Best for Medicinal / Anthocyanin Content

Anthocyanin concentration determines therapeutic value for immune support and antioxidant applications:

- **Bob Gordon** — highest recorded anthocyanins in American trials
- **Wyldewood** — very close second; favored by supplement manufacturers
- **Haschberg** — European benchmark; consistent 400–800 mg/100g cyanidin-3-glucoside
- **Marge** — hybrid vigor gives exceptionally high polyphenol content

## Pollination Requirements

**All elderberries require cross-pollination** between two genetically distinct varieties. Plant at least two cultivars within 15 m of each other.

| Pair | Notes |
|------|-------|
| York × Nova | Classic American pair; sequential ripening |
| Bob Gordon × York | Complementary timing |
| Bob Gordon × Wyldewood | Both prolific; similar timing |
| Samdal × Samyl | European standard pair |
| Haschberg × Korsør | Good European combination |
