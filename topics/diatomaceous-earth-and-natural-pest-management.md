## Diatomaceous Earth and Natural Pest Management

## Overview

[[diatomaceous-earth]] (DE) is a naturally occurring, soft sedimentary rock composed of the fossilized remains of diatoms — microscopic aquatic organisms with cell walls made of silica. When crushed to a fine powder, DE becomes a powerful physical insecticide that works by abrading the waxy outer layer (epicuticle) of insects, causing them to dehydrate and die. Unlike [[comparison-parasitoid-wasps-vs-chemical-pesticides]], DE leaves no toxic residue, does not bioaccumulate, and can be used in organic production systems. This page covers DE in depth and situates it within the broader philosophy of natural pest management.

## Geological Origins and Composition

### What Are Diatoms?

Diatoms are single-celled algae (class Bacillariophyceae) that inhabit freshwater, marine, and brackish environments. Their frustules — the silica shells that encase each cell — are intricate, species-specific structures with nanoscale pores and ridges. When diatoms die, their silica shells sink and accumulate in deposits that can be millions of years old.

### Mining and Processing

DE deposits are surface-mined from ancient lakebeds and seabeds. Major producing regions include the western United States (Nevada, Oregon, California), Denmark, China, and Japan. After mining, the raw material is:

1. **Crushed** to break up consolidated rock
2. **Dried** in rotary kilns or flash dryers
3. **Milled** to a fine powder (typically 10–200 microns)
4. **Calcined** (heated to high temperature) for some grades to increase hardness

The result is a lightweight, highly porous powder that is 80–95% [[silicon]] dioxide (SiO₂), with small amounts of aluminum oxide, iron oxide, calcium oxide, and other trace minerals.

### Food Grade vs. Pool Grade

This distinction is critical for safe use:

- **Food-grade DE** is uncalcined or only gently processed. It is amorphous silica, generally recognized as safe (GRAS) by the FDA for human and animal consumption at levels up to 2% of total diet. Particle size and crystallinity are within safe limits.
- **Pool-grade DE** (also called filter-grade) is calcined at very high temperatures, which converts the amorphous silica into crystalline silica (cristobalite). This form is a known carcinogen when inhaled and must never be used [[insectary-plants-for-pest-control]], in animal feed, or anywhere humans or animals might breathe the dust.

Always verify the product is labeled "food grade" before applying in gardens or around animals.

## Mechanism of Action

### Physical Abrasion

DE works through a purely mechanical process. The microscopic sharp edges of diatom frustules act like tiny shards of glass that:

1. **Abrate the epicuticle** — the thin, waxy outermost layer of an insect's exoskeleton that prevents water loss
2. **Lacerate the cuticle** — deeper penetration compromises the exoskeleton's integrity
3. **Cause desiccation** — with the waterproofing layer destroyed, the insect loses moisture rapidly through transpiration
4. **Lead to death** — most insects die within 24–72 hours from dehydration

This mechanism means DE is effective against any insect with a waxy cuticle, regardless of whether the insect has developed resistance to chemical pesticides. There is no known mechanism for insects to develop resistance to physical abrasion.

### Absorptive Properties

DE also has strong absorptive capacity. It can absorb up to 1.5 times its weight in water and oils. When insects contact DE, it actively draws lipids and moisture from their bodies, accelerating the dehydration process. This dual action — mechanical abrasion plus absorption — makes DE more effective than inert dusts like talc or clay.

### Factors Affecting Efficacy

Several environmental and application factors influence how well DE performs:

- **Humidity**: DE is significantly less effective in high-humidity environments (>70% RH) because ambient moisture slows insect desiccation. Apply during dry periods for best results.
- **Particle size**: Smaller particles (10–50 microns) are more effective against small insects like aphids and mites. Larger particles (50–200 microns) may be needed for beetles and larger insects.
- **Application method**: Even, thin coverage is more effective than thick piles. Dust applications penetrate leaf axils and crevices better than wettable spray formulations.
- **UV exposure**: DE does not degrade in sunlight (unlike many biological pesticides), giving it extended field persistence.
- **Rainfall**: Heavy rain washes DE off plant surfaces, requiring reapplication.

## Target Pests

### Insects Effectively Controlled

DE shows strong efficacy against a wide range of arthropod pests:

**Garden and Crop Pests:**
- Aphids (Aphididae) — especially when dusted on colony clusters
- Flea beetles (Chrysomelidae: Alticinae)
- Cabbage worms and loopers (Pieris rapae, Trichoplusia ni)
- Colorado potato beetle (Leptinotarsa decemlineata) — larvae are more susceptible than adults
- Squash bugs (Anasa tristis)
- Tomato hornworms (Manduca quinquemaculata)
- Mexican bean beetles (Epilachna varivestis)
- [[jadam-pesticide-for-slugs-and-snails]] (Gastropoda) — DE creates a barrier they avoid; apply in bands around plants
- Spider mites (Tetranychidae) — moderate efficacy; dust undersides of leaves
- Thrips (Thripidae)
- Whiteflies (Aleyrodidae) — when applied to leaf undersides

**Stored Product Pests:**
- Grain weevils (Sitophilus spp.)
- Flour beetles (Tribolium spp.)
- Indian meal moth (Plodia interpunctella)
- Sawtoothed grain beetle (Oryzaephilus surinamensis)
- Cigarette beetle (Lasioderma serricorne)

**Animal and Household Pests:**
- Fleas (Siphonaptera) — apply to pet bedding, carpets, and animal housing
- Bed bugs (Cimex lectularius) — dust into cracks, mattress seams, and bed frames
- Ants (Formicidae) — create barrier lines across ant trails and entry points
- Cockroaches (Blattodea) — dust behind appliances, in cabinets, and along baseboards
- Ticks (Ixodida) — limited efficacy; best used as a supplemental barrier

### Insects Less Susceptible

- **Beetles with heavily sclerotized exoskeletons** (e.g., large ground beetles) may survive DE contact
- **Social insects with grooming behaviors** (e.g., honeybees) can remove DE from their bodies, but direct dusting of hives should be avoided
- **Insects in moist environments** may recover if they can rehydrate after exposure
- **Insect eggs and pupae** are generally protected by chorion or pupal case and are not affected

## Application Methods

### Dry Dusting

The most common and generally most effective method:

1. Use a **manual duster** (bellows, bulb, or squeeze duster) for small areas
2. Use a **power duster** or modified leaf blower for larger areas
3. Apply early morning when dew is present to help DE adhere to plant surfaces
4. Dust in a thin, even layer — you should barely see the coating
5. Target the **undersides of leaves**, leaf axils, stem joints, and soil surface
6. Wear a **dust mask** (N95 or better) to avoid inhalation during application

### Wettable Suspension (Slurry)

For situations where dust drift is a concern:

1. Mix DE at 1–2 tablespoons per gallon of water
2. Add a small amount of liquid soap (1/4 teaspoon) as a surfactant
3. Shake or stir vigorously — DE does not dissolve but stays in suspension
4. Spray with a pump sprayer, shaking frequently to keep DE suspended
5. The water evaporates, leaving a thin DE coating on treated surfaces
6. Agitate the sprayer frequently to prevent clogging

This method is ideal for greenhouse applications, indoor plants, and situations where dust could drift onto neighbors or into living spaces.

### Barrier Applications

Create physical barriers with DE:

- **Ring individual plants** with a 1–2 inch band of DE on the soil surface
- **Line garden bed borders** to exclude crawling insects
- **Dust around building foundations** to create an insect barrier
- **Apply to stored grain** at 1–2 lbs per 100 sq ft of grain surface
