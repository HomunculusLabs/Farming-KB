## Food Preservation and Storage Methods

**Food preservation and storage methods** encompass a broad range of
techniques developed over millennia to prevent food spoilage, extend shelf
life, and maintain [[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]] and flash-freezing, food
preservation has been fundamental to human survival, enabling communities
to build food reserves, survive seasonal scarcity, and reduce dependence
on continuous harvest cycles.

The core principles underlying all preservation methods involve controlling
one or more [[mollison-designers-appropriate-technology-small-scale-tools]]" for
many contexts, and Carol Deppe arguing that root cellaring is "the most
important and underutilized" method available to home growers.

This article provides an encyclopedic overview of the major food
preservation and storage methods, including canning, fermentation, drying
and solar drying, pickling, curing and smoking, root cellar and cold
storage, freezing, egg preservation, and energy-efficient cooking. Each
section covers the underlying science, practical techniques, safety
considerations, and relevant nutritional information.

## Canning

Canning is a food preservation method in which food is processed and sealed
in airtight containers, typically glass jars or metal cans, to achieve
shelf stability at room temperature. The method was developed in the early
19th century by Nicolas Appert and later refined through Louis Pasteur's
work establishing the microbial basis of spoilage. Two primary canning
methods are used today, each suited to different types of food based on
acidity.
