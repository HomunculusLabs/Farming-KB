## Botany and Origin

Potatoes belong to the Solanaceae, or nightshade family, alongside
tomatoes, peppers, and eggplants. The edible portion is a swollen
underground stem called a tuber, not a root.

Wild species range from the southwestern United States through
[[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]] to southern Chile. The greatest diversity occurs
around Lake Titicaca in Peru and Bolivia, where over 4,000
landraces are still cultivated by indigenous communities.

Domestication began roughly 8,000 years ago in the Andean highlands.
Indigenous farmers selected for larger tubers, reduced toxic
glycoalkaloid levels, and adaptability to diverse growing conditions
from sea level to 4,500 meters altitude.

The potato reached Europe in the 1570s following Spanish conquest.
It spread slowly at first, hampered by suspicion of its nightshade
ties, but eventually transformed [[fukuoka-european-agriculture-observations]] and demography,
fueling population growth through the 18th and 19th centuries.

Sir Walter Raleigh and Antoine-Augustin Parmentier both played roles
in popularizing the potato in Europe. Parmentier's advocacy followed
his experience as a prisoner of war subsisting on potatoes, and he
later convinced Louis XVI to promote potato cultivation across France.

## Varieties and Classification

Potatoes are classified by skin color, flesh color, and culinary use.
The three main categories are starchy, waxy, and all-purpose.

Starchy varieties like Russet Burbank have high starch and low
moisture. They bake fluffy, fry crisp, and mash creamy. These
dominate commercial production across North America.

Waxy varieties such as Red Pontiac and most fingerlings have low
starch and high moisture. They hold shape during boiling, making
them ideal for salads and soups.

All-purpose varieties like Yukon Gold balance starch and moisture.
They perform well across most cooking methods, offering versatility
for home gardeners and chefs alike.

Heirloom and specialty varieties have gained popularity. Purple
Peruvian, French Fingerling, and German Butterball offer unique
flavors and colors unavailable in supermarkets.

## Climate and Site Requirements

Potatoes grow best in [[glasshouse-growing-cool-temperate-climates]] with consistent
moisture. Ideal temperatures range from 15-20 degrees C during the
growing season. Tubers stop forming above 27 degrees C.

Full sun exposure is essential for maximum yield. Six to eight
hours of direct sunlight daily produces the best results. Partial
shade reduces tuber size and overall productivity significantly.

Potatoes tolerate a wide range of soil types but prefer loose,
well-drained loam. Heavy clay restricts tuber expansion and
promotes disease. Sandy soils drain well but need more irrigation.
