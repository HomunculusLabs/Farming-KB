## Medicinal Mushrooms in Cancer Treatment

[[medicinal-mushrooms]] represent one of the most clinically validated
frontiers in complementary oncology. Unlike the speculative hype that
surrounds many alternative cancer treatments, mushroom-derived
compounds have progressed through Phase I, II, and III [[medicinal-mushrooms-biological-response-modifiers]] modifiers, stimulating
macrophages, natural killer cells, T-cells, and a cascade of cytokines
that restore immune competence often depressed by chemotherapy and
surgery. This immunomodulatory approach has proven especially valuable
as an adjuvant therapy, where [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]] species:
beta-glucans bind to complement receptor 3 (CR3, also known as CD11b/
CD18) on macrophages and natural killer cells, initiating NF-kappaB
activation and downstream cytokine production including IL-1-beta, IL-2,
IL-6, IL-10, TNF-alpha, and IFN-gamma. This cascade activates the
cellular immune arm — cytotoxic T-cells, NK cells, and macrophages —
that can recognize and destroy tumor cells.

## Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor): PSK and PSP

Trametes versicolor, commonly known as turkey tail, is a bracket
fungus found worldwide on dead logs. Though too tough to eat, its hot
water extracts have been used in traditional Chinese medicine for
centuries. Modern research has produced two clinically important
compounds from this species: PSK (Polysaccharide-K, brand name Krestin)
and PSP (Polysaccharide-Peptide).

PSK is extracted from mycelial strain CM-101 and is approximately 62%
polysaccharide and 38% protein, with a molecular weight of 94,000-
100,000 daltons. The glucan portion consists of a beta-1,4 main chain
with beta-1,3 side chains and beta-1,6 branches bonded to a
polypeptide moiety through O-N-glycosidic bonds. Crucially, PSK is
orally bioavailable — a property that distinguishes it from many
other [[medicinal-mushroom-compounds]] that require intravenous delivery.

PSK acts through multiple mechanisms: direct cytostatic and cytotoxic
effects on tumor cells, T-cell activation, macrophage stimulation,
antiviral activity through interferon production, and restoration of
immune potential depressed by tumor burden or chemotherapy. It increases
white blood cell counts, IFN-gamma, and IL-2 production, and induces
gene expression of TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-8, and IL-6. Remarkably, PSK
induces T-cells that recognize it as an antigen and kill tumor cells
in an antigen-specific manner.

By 1987, PSK accounted for more than 25% of total national expenditure
on anti-cancer agents in Japan. Phase I, II, and III [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-schizophyllan-spg-clinical-trials]]
have demonstrated efficacy in cancers of the stomach, esophagus,
nasopharynx, colon, rectum, lung, and subsets of breast cancer. PSK
continues to be used extensively as an adjunct to standard radiotherapy
and chemotherapy throughout Asia.

## Shiitake (Lentinus edodes) and Lentinan

Shiitake is the second most [[classification-of-cultivated-mushroom-species]] worldwide, accounting
for 16.8% of total production. Beyond its culinary importance, it is
the source of two well-studied pharmacological preparations: Lentinan
(from [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]]) and LEM (Lentinus edodes mycelium extract).

Lentinan is a highly purified, high molecular weight polysaccharide
(~500,000 daltons) in a triple helix structure containing only glucose
molecules. It has mostly beta-(1->3)-Glc linkages with beta-(1->6)-Glc
side chains. It is protein-free, water-soluble, heat-stable,
acid-stable, and alkali-labile. A new biochemical [[lemon-cannabis-alchemy-high-volume-extraction-method]]
achieves 325 mg yield from 100g fresh mushrooms in 5 days — a dramatic
improvement over Chihara's traditional method yielding 4 mg in 14 days.

Lentinan does not attack cancer cells directly. It is a T-cell-oriented
immunopotentiator requiring functional T-cell compartments. Its
antitumor activity is abolished in neonatally thymectomised mice and
decreased by anti-macrophage agents. Lentinan activates a broad
spectrum of immune responses: macrophages, NK cells, lymphocytes
(both T and B cells), helper T cells, and cytotoxic T cells. It also
activates the classical and alternative complement pathways.
