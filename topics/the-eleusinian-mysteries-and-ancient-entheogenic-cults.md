## The Eleusinian Mysteries and Ancient Entheogenic Cults

The Eleusinian Mysteries were the most prestigious and enduring religious rites
of the ancient Greek world, conducted annually at the sanctuary of Demeter and
Persephone at Eleusis, a small town roughly eighteen kilometers northwest of
Athens. For nearly two thousand years, from approximately 1450 BCE until their
suppression by Christian authorities in 392 CE, these initiatory ceremonies
drew participants from across the Mediterranean. Initiates included common
citizens, slaves, and some of the most powerful figures of antiquity, from the
playwright Sophocles to Roman emperors such as Augustus, Hadrian, and Marcus
Aurelius. Despite this vast reach and enormous cultural importance, the central
experience of the Mysteries was guarded by a strict vow of secrecy that was
never meaningfully broken. What actually happened inside the Telesterion, the
great hall of initiation at Eleusis, remains one of antiquity's most
persistent puzzles.

The proposal that the Eleusinian Mysteries involved the ritual consumption of
a psychoactive sacrament, specifically an ergot-infused barley drink called
kykeon, was first articulated systematically in the 1978 book "The Road to
Eleusis: Unveiling the Secret of the Mysteries" by R. Gordon Wasson, Albert
Hofmann, and Carl A.P. Ruck. This hypothesis, now widely known as the
Wasson-Hofmann-Ruck theory, suggested that the transformative visionary
experiences reported by ancient initiates were pharmacologically induced rather
than purely psychological or theatrical. If correct, this would mean that
psychoactive substances played a foundational role in Western religious
history, shaping the spiritual experiences of the civilization that gave birth
to philosophy, democracy, and the arts.

This topic page explores the Eleusinian Mysteries in their full historical and
cultural context, examines the evidence for psychoactive use in ancient
religious practice, and traces the broader pattern of entheogenic sacraments
across cultures, from the [[weil-the-natural-mind-altered-states-normalcy]] of consciousness, and human civilization.
