## Crop Rotation and Succession Planting

Crop rotation and succession planting are two complementary strategies that
form the backbone of intelligent garden and farm planning. Though they serve
different purposes, both practices spring from the same fundamental insight:
nature never grows the same plant in the same place year after year, and the
most productive human cultivation systems mimic this diversity across time.
Together, these techniques allow growers to maintain soil fertility, suppress
pests and diseases, and harvest fresh food continuously from a limited area.

Crop rotation is the practice of growing different types of crops in
sequential seasons on the same piece of land. It is one of the oldest
agricultural practices known to humanity, with evidence of rotated grain
and legume cropping dating back to ancient Roman and Chinese farming
systems. The core principle is straightforward: by changing what grows
where, growers prevent the depletion of specific soil nutrients, break the
life cycles of pests and diseases that target particular plant families, and
create conditions where soil biology can recover between demanding crops.

Succession planting, by contrast, is the practice of staggering plantings
of the same or different crops throughout the growing season to ensure
continuous harvests and maximize garden productivity. Rather than planting
everything at once and facing a glut followed by scarcity, succession
planting spreads production across weeks and months. It is the temporal
equivalent of companion planting, filling gaps in both the garden bed and
the harvest calendar.
