# 5-MeO-DMT and Bufo alvarius

5-MeO-DMT, or 5-methoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, is a naturally
occurring psychedelic tryptamine known for a short duration, very rapid
onset, and unusually intense alterations of self-experience. In the
concept pages synthesized here, it is described as both a botanical
alkaloid and the principal psychoactive constituent of the defensive
secretion of the Colorado River toad, more recently classified as
Incilius alvarius and formerly known as Bufo alvarius.

The compound is often discussed beside N,N-DMT because the two molecules
share a tryptamine backbone and similar routes of administration. Their
subjective profiles, however, are sharply different. DMT is repeatedly
associated with elaborate imagery, landscapes, and entity encounters,
whereas 5-MeO-DMT is described as more unitary, less visual, and more
likely to produce ego dissolution, a whiteout, or a void-like state.

The toad association has made 5-MeO-DMT unusually visible outside the
usual history of synthetic tryptamines. Venom secretion from the
parotoid glands of the Sonoran Desert toad has been framed in popular
culture as a direct animal source of a powerful entheogen. The same fact
has also raised concerns about animal handling, habitat pressure, and
whether synthetic material or botanical traditions are more ethical
than extracting secretion from wild amphibians.

This page treats 5-MeO-DMT as a chemical, pharmacological, ecological,
historical, and harm-reduction topic. It draws on compound profiles,
TiHKAL-derived pharmacology notes, and D.M. Turner's experiential
accounts, while avoiding claims not present in those source concept
pages. The result is a broad reference article rather than an
instructional manual.

## Nomenclature and classification

5-MeO-DMT is the common abbreviation for
5-methoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine. The name identifies a dimethylated
tryptamine bearing a methoxy group at the 5 position of the indole
ring. The compound is also described in the source pages as
O-methylbufotenine, bufotenine methyl ether, and
N,N,O-trimethylserotonin.

The animal source is traditionally named Bufo alvarius in psychedelic
literature. More recent taxonomy places it in the genus Incilius, hence
Incilius alvarius. The common names Colorado River toad and Sonoran
Desert toad both occur in the source materials. The older binomial
remains historically important because many earlier references, reports,
and popular accounts use Bufo alvarius.

5-MeO-DMT belongs to the broader tryptamine family. It is closely
related to N,N-DMT, bufotenine, and other substituted tryptamines. The
5-methoxy substitution is central to its identity, because the sources
present it as much more potent by weight than DMT when smoked and more
likely to produce a nonvisual, all-encompassing state.

The compound should not be confused with 5-MeO-DIPT. 5-MeO-DIPT is
also a 5-methoxy tryptamine, but it has two isopropyl groups on the
amine and is described as orally active, longer lasting, more erotic or
body-focused in some reports, and far less similar to the compressed
whiteout profile associated with 5-MeO-DMT.

## Chemistry

The formula given in the reference page is C13H18N2O, and the compound
profile gives a molecular weight of 218.30 g/mol. The free base is
reported with a melting point of 69 to 70 degrees C, while the
hydrochloride salt is reported at 145 to 146 degrees C. These values
place the compound within the small, volatile tryptamine class commonly
discussed in relation to vaporized or smoked administration.

In the TiHKAL-derived profile, synthesis is summarized from
5-methoxyindole through oxalylation, dimethylamidation, and lithium
aluminum hydride reduction. The same source reports a 78 percent yield
for that route. This page includes the information only as historical
and chemical context; legal controls make unauthorized manufacture or
possession prohibited in many jurisdictions.

The 5-methoxy group distinguishes the compound from N,N-DMT. That small
structural difference corresponds, in the source pages, to large
changes in potency, somatic force, imagery, and emotional tone. The
compound is therefore an example of how minor substitutions on the
tryptamine scaffold can produce major changes in human reports.

Comparisons to 5-MeO-DIPT reinforce the same point. Both molecules
carry a 5-methoxy substitution, but their amine substitutions differ.
5-MeO-DIPT is described as active in the 6 to 12 mg oral range in
Shulgin's tests, lasting several hours and sometimes emphasizing
physical, erotic, or auditory qualities. 5-MeO-DMT instead appears in
the sources as a very short, very forceful smoked or intravenous drug.

## Natural sources

The most famous natural source is the secretion of the Sonoran Desert
toad. The 5-MeO-DMT compound profile identifies the venom glands of
Bufo alvarius as containing 5-MeO-DMT as the primary psychoactive
component and warns against confusing this species with Bufo marinus,
the marine toad. The TiHKAL-derived page describes the toad secretion as
containing 5-MeO-DMT along with 5-MeO-NMT and bufotenine.

The toad is not the only source. The source concepts also identify
several plant contexts. Virola species of the Amazon basin are
repeatedly mentioned, including Virola theiodora and Virola rufuta. The
materials describe some collections of V. theiodora and V. rufuta as
rich in 5-MeO-DMT, while also noting that related Virola material can be
chemically different.

Virola alkaloid preparations are associated with South American snuffs
whose names include yakee, yato, parica, epena, and nyakwana. These
preparations place 5-MeO-DMT within a broader ethnobotanical history
rather than solely within modern synthetic or toad-derived use. The
concept sources also mention Anadenanthera species, especially in the
context of yopo and vilca materials containing related tryptamines.

Other botanical references include Dictyoloma incanescens, described as
the first botanical identification in one source, as well as numerous
leguminous and grass species. These listings show that 5-MeO-DMT is not
unique to a single animal, even though the toad has become the most
visible source in recent public discussion.

## Bufo alvarius and the toad context

The toad context matters because it joins chemistry to a living desert
animal. Incilius alvarius is native to the Sonoran Desert region, and
its parotoid gland secretion functions as a defensive substance. In
psychedelic use, the secretion is dried and smoked or vaporized, but the
source pages emphasize the compound profile rather than detailed
collection methods.

The source concepts state that the popular idea of toad licking refers
to this species, but the important pharmacological point is not licking
as a technique. It is that a defensive secretion contains a highly
potent tryptamine whose smoked dose is measured in milligrams. The
popular image can obscure the precision and risk involved.

Older literature generally uses Bufo alvarius. Newer ecological and
taxonomic discussion often uses Incilius alvarius. A Wikipedia-style
page should preserve both terms because the older name is embedded in
psychedelic history, while the newer name better reflects current
taxonomic practice.

The toad's prominence also creates an ethical distinction between a
molecule and an animal source. A synthetic sample and a secretion taken
from a wild amphibian may contain the same principal psychoactive
compound, but their conservation implications differ. That difference
is one of the reasons modern discussion often separates 5-MeO-DMT from
"toad medicine" as cultural and ecological categories.

## Pharmacology

The reference page describes 5-MeO-DMT as a potent agonist at serotonin
receptors, especially 5-HT2A, with significant affinity for 5-HT1A and
other serotonin receptor subtypes. This receptor profile places it
within the classic psychedelic pharmacological field, while its
phenomenology differs from many highly visual psychedelics.

The compound is rapidly metabolized by monoamine oxidase. This rapid
metabolism helps explain why it is described as inactive orally without
an MAO inhibitor and why the smoked or vaporized experience is so
short. The compound profile lists oral 35 mg as inactive, while oral use
with an MAOI is given in the 10 to 25 mg range.

Routes reported in the source pages include smoking or vaporization,
intravenous administration, and oral use with MAO inhibition. Smoked
doses are given as 6 to 20 mg in the compound profile and 5 to 10 mg in
Turner's guide. Intravenous doses are reported from 0.25 to 3.1 mg,
with the TiHKAL-derived page describing full psychedelic experiences in
roughly the 1.3 to 3.1 mg range.

The onset is described as extremely rapid. The compound profile reports
8 to 15 seconds after smoking, while Turner describes the forceful
expansion of mind as unfolding over about 15 seconds. The peak is short,
commonly described in minutes, with the acute phase often under half an
hour and aftereffects sometimes lasting up to an hour.

## Phenomenology

The most consistent subjective description in the source pages is that
5-MeO-DMT is less visual and more unitary than DMT. Instead of complex
narrative scenes, entities, or colorful landscapes, common descriptions
include whiteout, void experience, cosmic consciousness, total
implosion, and ego dissolution.

The 5-MeO-DMT reference page states that common doses often lack the
visual hallucinations associated with N,N-DMT. The compound profile
calls the high-dose experience a whiteout in contrast with DMT's more
visual blackout. The TiHKAL-derived page describes a singularity of
experience in which judgment by ordinary mental categories becomes
impossible.

Turner's accounts emphasize force. He characterizes 5-MeO-DMT as
"sheer force" compared with N,N-DMT's "sheer perfection." In his
formulation, the mind seems to explode and expand outward from the
immediate surroundings to the planet and then the cosmos. This image is
not a clinical measure, but it captures the compressed scale of the
reported experience.

The absence of elaborate imagery should not be mistaken for mildness.
The same source pages describe overwhelming terror, ruthless love,
receiving grace, and a feeling of unconditional life or unity. These
accounts suggest that 5-MeO-DMT's intensity often lies in the collapse
of boundaries rather than in visual ornament.

Some visual phenomena are still reported. Turner describes rapidly
moving multidimensional geometric designs, often black and white or
checkerboard-like, with occasional color at larger doses. The
Wikipedia-style summary should therefore say that 5-MeO-DMT is usually
less visual than DMT, not that it is entirely nonvisual.

## History and documentation

The reference page reports that 5-MeO-DMT was first synthesized in 1936
by Toshio Hoshino and Kenya Shimodaira. Its psychoactive properties
were characterized later, and Alexander Shulgin documented its synthesis
and effects in TiHKAL. The TiHKAL-derived concept pages preserve much
of the comparison with DMT and with ayahuasca-like combinations.

The compound's modern cultural history includes several overlapping
streams. One is synthetic chemistry and psychonautic pharmacology,
represented by Shulgin-style compound profiles. A second is D.M.
Turner's experiential and comparative writing, which foregrounds
immediacy, cosmic expansion, and the dangers of combining the drug with
harmala alkaloids.

A third stream is ethnobotanical. Virola snuffs, Anadenanthera
preparations, and related South American plant practices show that
tryptamine-containing materials have long cultural histories. The
sources do not reduce these practices to purified 5-MeO-DMT alone, and
this distinction is important because plant preparations may include
multiple alkaloids and cultural protocols.

A fourth stream is the recent public focus on the Sonoran Desert toad.
This is the context in which the name Bufo alvarius became widely
recognized outside herpetology. It also made the ethics of sourcing
inseparable from the public identity of the molecule.

## Comparison with N,N-DMT

5-MeO-DMT and N,N-DMT are both naturally occurring tryptamines, both are
rapidly metabolized, and both are generally described as inactive orally
without MAO inhibition. Both can produce experiences of great intensity
when smoked or otherwise rapidly administered. Their differences,
however, are central to nearly every source concept.

By weight, 5-MeO-DMT is reported as more potent. The compound profile
lists smoked 5-MeO-DMT at 6 to 20 mg, compared with a DMT range of 60
to 100 mg in the same table. Turner gives about 5 to 10 mg for a full
5-MeO-DMT smoked dose and about 40 mg for N,N-DMT in his comparison.

The onset is also described as faster and more forceful. DMT may begin
within tens of seconds; 5-MeO-DMT is repeatedly described as almost
immediate. Turner states that the entire dose must be consumed quickly
and that users should already be lying down or reclining because a good
dose rapidly makes ordinary coordination and recognition unavailable.

The visual comparison is the best known. DMT is associated with rich,
colorful, kaleidoscopic, and entity-filled imagery. 5-MeO-DMT is
associated with fewer visuals, white light, black and white geometry,
void, unity, and ego dissolution. In shorthand, DMT is often portrayed
as world-forming, while 5-MeO-DMT is boundary-dissolving.

The emotional tone also differs. The TiHKAL-derived page reports
phrases such as cosmic consciousness, receiving grace, ruthless love,
and overwhelming terror. Turner's vocabulary contrasts awe and fright
with enchantment and perfection. These descriptions are subjective, but
they recur across the concept pages often enough to define the topic.

## Comparison with other 5-methoxy tryptamines

5-MeO-DIPT provides a useful contrast because it is another
5-methoxy-substituted tryptamine from the same broad family. It is
synthetic, orally active, and much longer lasting than smoked
5-MeO-DMT. The concept profile gives a common oral range of 8 to 12 mg
and a duration of 4 to 8 hours.

Its reported effects differ markedly. 5-MeO-DIPT is associated with
nausea, pupil dilation, increased heart rate, tactile enhancement,
erotic effects, altered musical perception, and variable body load. At
higher doses some reports describe confusion, paranoia, spasms, or an
unusable state. These reports do not resemble the compressed whiteout
state that defines many 5-MeO-DMT accounts.

This contrast cautions against treating the 5-MeO prefix as a single
experiential category. Substitution at the indole ring matters, but the
amine substituents and route of administration also matter. 5-MeO-DMT,
5-MeO-DIPT, and DMT are related, not interchangeable.

The comparison also shows why dose numbers cannot be transferred among
tryptamines. Milligram ranges, onset times, and risk profiles differ
greatly. A dose that is common for one compound may be inactive,
overwhelming, or dangerous for another.

## Risks and adverse effects

The most direct risk is dose precision. Active smoked doses are small,
and the source pages repeatedly stress that a sophisticated scale is
needed for milligram quantities. The difference between a threshold,
full, and overwhelming dose can be narrow, especially when material
strength is uncertain.

Common adverse effects in the concept pages include nausea, vomiting,
trembling, intense anxiety, increased pulse, increased blood pressure,
weakness in the knees, and breathing irregularities. These effects are
not merely peripheral, because the experience can render the person
unable to stand, speak clearly, or recognize surroundings.

The compound profile records a documented near-fatal reaction after an
unknown but very large smoked dose. The subject became cyanotic and
stopped breathing twice, requiring CPR, and later required antipsychotic
medication to restore normal behavior. The case is presented in the
source as a warning about high-dose danger and poor dosing control.

MAO inhibitor combinations are a separate risk category. The source
materials state that oral 5-MeO-DMT becomes active with harmaline or
other MAO inhibitors, but Turner describes the combination with harmala
alkaloids as physically and mentally overwhelming, with violent nausea,
extreme discomfort, convulsions, and a high proportion of unpleasant
experiences in his circle.

Cardiovascular stress is also emphasized. The reference page warns that
increases in heart rate and blood pressure make the compound
contraindicated for people with cardiovascular conditions. The same
logic applies to people using medications or substances that affect
serotonin, blood pressure, or monoamine oxidase, though specific drug
lists are outside the present source set.

## Ethics and conservation

The toad-derived form of 5-MeO-DMT raises ethical questions not present
in the same way for a purely synthetic compound. Handling wild toads,
collecting secretion, transporting animals, or disturbing breeding
habitat can affect individual animals and local populations. The source
concepts do not provide population data, so this page should avoid
quantitative claims about decline.

The ethical point supported by the sources is simpler: a psychoactive
molecule can be sourced from different ecological contexts. If the
material comes from an animal secretion, the animal and its habitat
become part of the moral landscape. If the material is synthetic, the
animal pressure may be avoided, though legal and safety questions remain.

The older name Bufo alvarius has become culturally powerful, but it can
also reduce the animal to a drug source. A more careful account treats
Incilius alvarius as an amphibian species first and a source of a
compound second. That framing is consistent with harm reduction and
conservation ethics.

Plant traditions require a different ethical lens. Virola and
Anadenanthera preparations are embedded in cultural histories and local
knowledge systems. A general article should not collapse these
traditions into isolated alkaloid extraction or assume that purified
5-MeO-DMT is equivalent to every traditional preparation.

## Clinical and research status

The 5-MeO-DMT reference page notes recent clinical research into
possible uses for treatment-resistant depression, anxiety, and substance
use disorders. It cites a 2019 study by Davis and colleagues reporting
sustained improvements in depression, anxiety, and stress ratings after
a single session, with mystical-type experience intensity correlated
with benefit.

The same source mentions research at Johns Hopkins documenting the
compound's potential for transformative experiences. These statements
support a cautious research-status summary: 5-MeO-DMT is of serious
interest to psychedelic science, especially because it can occasion
brief but profound mystical-type experiences.

This is not the same as established medical use. The source pages also
emphasize Schedule I status in the United States and similar controls in
many countries. Clinical research, where permitted, occurs in structured
settings with screening, dosing control, supervision, and follow-up.

The short duration may be attractive to researchers because it differs
from the many-hour sessions required by psilocybin or LSD. At the same
time, the intensity, cardiovascular effects, and breathing concerns mean
that brevity does not equal simplicity. A short crisis can still be a
serious crisis.

## Legal status

The reference page states that 5-MeO-DMT is a Schedule I controlled
substance in the United States and is similarly controlled or prohibited
in many countries. Legal status varies by jurisdiction and may differ
between the purified molecule, plant materials, animal handling, and
religious or indigenous contexts.

The compound profile likewise notes widespread control. Because law is
jurisdiction-specific and changes over time, this topic page should not
be used as legal advice. It is enough for the wiki topic to state that
5-MeO-DMT is broadly restricted and that the toad itself may be subject
to separate wildlife regulations.

The legal distinction between a molecule and an organism can be
confusing. A person may face one set of rules for controlled substances,
another for wildlife possession or collection, and another for import,
transport, or ceremony. The source concepts do not map these regimes,
so the article remains general.

## Harm reduction

The strongest harm-reduction theme in the source pages is precision.
Because doses are measured in milligrams, eyeballing material is unsafe.
Unknown concentration, adulteration, or confusion between compounds can
turn a brief experience into a medical emergency.

A second theme is posture and supervision. Turner warns that the drug
takes effect almost immediately and that users should be lying down or
reclining before inhalation. The reason is practical: a full experience
may quickly remove the ability to stand, coordinate movement, or respond
to ordinary surroundings.

A third theme is avoiding dangerous combinations. MAO inhibition can
make oral 5-MeO-DMT active and can prolong or intensify effects. The
source materials particularly caution that harmala combinations may be
physically and mentally overwhelming. Other serotonergic or
cardiovascularly active drugs may add risk, even though detailed
interaction lists are beyond these sources.

Screening matters. People with cardiovascular conditions, seizure
history, severe psychiatric instability, or current medication conflicts
would require medical assessment in any legitimate research context.
The documented near-fatal case also shows why emergency readiness is
not optional when high-potency tryptamines are involved.

Ethical sourcing is harm reduction at the ecological level. Avoiding
pressure on wild toads, respecting indigenous plant traditions, and not
commercializing animal extraction as spectacle all reduce harms beyond
the individual user. A molecule-centered account can miss these wider
consequences.
