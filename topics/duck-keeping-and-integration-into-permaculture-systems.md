## Duck Keeping and Integration into Permaculture Systems

Ducks are among the most versatile and underappreciated livestock a homesteader or permaculture practitioner can integrate into a diversified farm system. Unlike chickens, ducks thrive in wet environments, forage aggressively for slugs and insects, produce nutrient-rich manure, and can contribute to [[query-how-do-i-integrate-ducks-into-my-permaculture-system]] into permaculture designs for maximum ecological benefit.

## Why Ducks Over Chickens

Before diving into specifics, it is worth understanding why many permaculture practitioners prefer ducks to chickens:

| Factor | Ducks | Chickens |
|--------|-------|----------|
| Foraging ability | Excellent — consume slugs, snails, aquatic insects | Moderate — scratch and dust-bathe |
| Wet tolerance | Thrive in rain and mud | Prone to respiratory issues in damp conditions |
| [[mollison-designers-chicken-tractors-egg-production]] | 200–340 eggs/year (light breeds) | 200–300 eggs/year (layer breeds) |
| Egg quality | Larger, richer yolk, thicker shell | Standard |
| [[hamilton-slug-and-snail-control]] hunters | Good for ticks and grasshoppers |
| Manure output | Higher nitrogen content (~0.6% N) | Moderate (~0.5% N) |
| Noise level | Quieter (mumbles and quacks) | Louder (crowing, alarm calls) |
| Predator vulnerability | Higher (ground-based, less alert) | Lower (roost, alarm systems) |
| Space per bird | 3–5 sq ft indoor, 10–15 sq ft outdoor | 3–4 sq ft indoor, 8–10 sq ft outdoor |

The primary advantages of ducks in permaculture are their slug-hunting prowess, tolerance of diverse conditions, and ability to convert pond and wetland forage into eggs and meat without significant grain input.

### Muscovy Ducks: A Special Case

Muscovy ducks deserve special attention because they are a different species (*Cairina moschata*) from true ducks (*Anas platyrhynchos*). Key differences:

- **Roosting**: Muscovies can fly and prefer to roost in trees or on elevated perches
- **Noise**: Very quiet — males hiss, females make a soft trill (ideal for urban settings)
- **Foraging**: Exceptional fly, mosquito, and tick hunters
- **Meat**: Dark, beef-like meat; often considered the finest duck meat
- **Brooding**: Excellent mothers; frequently go broody
- **Crossbreeding**: Muscovy × Mallard hybrids are sterile ("mule ducks")
- **Climate**: More cold-hardy than many breeds; tolerate heat well with shade

## Housing and Shelter

### Basic Requirements

Ducks need protection from predators and [[windbreak-effects-on-soil-moisture-and-extreme-weather]], but their housing requirements are simpler than chickens:

- **Minimum space**: 3–4 sq ft per bird indoors, 10–15 sq ft per bird outdoors
- **Height**: 2–3 ft ceiling is sufficient (ducks don't roost high)
- **Bedding**: Straw, hemp, or wood shavings — ducks produce wetter manure than chickens, so bedding must be managed aggressively
- **Nesting**: Ground-level nest boxes (12×12×12 in), one per 3–4 hens
- **Ventilation**: Critical — ammonia buildup from wet manure can cause respiratory damage
- **Water access**: Ducks need water deep enough to submerge their heads and clean their nares (nostrils)

### Housing Types

1. **Simple hoop coop**: PVC or cattle panel hoop covered with tarps — cheap, portable, well-ventilated
2. **Mobile [[duck-tractor]]**: A-frame or flat-deck design on skids — moved daily across pasture or garden beds
3. **Stationary coop with run**: Traditional coop with attached fenced area — suitable for small flocks
4. **Night house only**: In mild climates, ducks may only need a secure lockup at night if they [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] hub.

**Minimum water depth**: 6–12 inches for small flocks; 18–24 inches is ideal for natural behavior
