# Sea Buckthorn Cultivation and Uses

## Overview

Sea buckthorn (*Hippophae rhamnoides*) is a hardy deciduous shrub native to Eurasia, renowned for its nutrient-dense orange berries, silvery foliage, and exceptional environmental adaptability. A pioneer species and nitrogen fixer through actinorhizal root symbiosis with *Frankia* bacteria, sea buckthorn has been cultivated for centuries across Central Asia, Europe, and Scandinavia — both as a food/medicine crop and as a soil-stabilizing windbreak plant. #### Insects
- **Sea buckthorn fly (*Rhagoletis batava*):** The most significant insect pest in commercial orchards, particularly in Europe. Larvae tunnel in the fruit. Yellow sticky traps for monitoring; targeted insecticide application (spinosad, pyrethrin) at petal fall.
- **Aphids:** Occasional on new growth. Usually controlled by natural predators (lady beetles, lacewings). Blast with water or apply insecticidal soap if severe.
