# Bee Products and Apitherapy

## Introduction

Apitherapy is the medicinal use of honey bee (*Apis mellifera*) products — honey, propolis, royal jelly, bee pollen, beeswax, bee venom, and bee bread. The practice spans millennia, with documented use in ancient Egypt (papyrus Ebers, ~1550 BCE), Greek medicine (Hippocrates prescribed honey for wound care), traditional Chinese medicine, and Ayurveda. Modern research has begun to validate many traditional claims, identifying specific [[bee-product-medicinal-uses]]'s composition, evidence-based therapeutic applications, safety considerations, and sourcing guidance.

## Honey

### Composition

Honey is a supersaturated solution produced by honey bees from flower nectar (or honeydew in some regions). Its composition varies by floral source, geography, and processing but generally includes:

| Component | Typical Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fructose | 38–44% | Dominant sugar in most honeys |
| Glucose | 24–36% | Crystallizes more readily than fructose |
| Water | 15–20% | Below 18% inhibits microbial growth |
| Sucrose | 0.5–5% | Low in pure honey; high levels indicate adulteration |
| Minerals | 0.1–1.2% | Potassium, calcium, magnesium, iron, zinc |
| Vitamins | Trace | B-complex (B6, thiamin, niacin, riboflavin), vitamin C |
| Enzymes | Various | Diastase, invertase, glucose oxidase, catalase |
| Polyphenols | 50–500 mg/kg | Flavonoids ([[luteolin]]) and phenolic acids |
| Amino acids | 0.1–0.5% | Proline is dominant; used as a freshness marker |

### Types of Honey

- **Raw honey**: Unfiltered, unpasteurized; retains pollen, enzymes, and [[acacia]] honey**: Light, mild, high fructose; slow to crystallize. Soothing for gastrointestinal discomfort.
- **Thyme honey** (*Thymus* spp.): High thymol content; strong antimicrobial activity studied in respiratory infections.
- **Wildflower honey**: Mixed nectar source; polyphenol profile varies by season and region.
- **Tualang honey** (*Koompassia excelsa*): Malaysian forest honey; studied for anti-diabetic and anti-cancer properties.
- **Honeydew honey**: Produced from aphid secretions rather than nectar; higher in oligosaccharides and minerals.

### Therapeutic Applications

**Wound healing**: Honey's therapeutic action in wounds is multifactorial — hyperosmolarity draws fluid from tissues, [[pseudomonas]]*Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Escherichia coli*, *[[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] + non-peroxide systems (in manuka) make resistance development unlikely.

**Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory**: Honey polyphenols (especially in darker varieties) reduce oxidative stress markers and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) in clinical and animal studies.

### Quality Indicators

- **Diastase activity**: Low diastase (DN < 8) indicates overheating or prolonged storage.
- **HMF (hydroxymethylfurfural)**: Levels above 40 mg/kg suggest overheating or adulteration with inverted syrup.
- **Pollen analysis** (melissopalynology): Identifies floral source and geographic origin; absence of pollen suggests ultrafiltration.
- **C3/C4 isotope ratio**: Detects adulteration with corn or cane sugar syrups.

## Propolis

### What Is Propolis?

Propolis (from Greek *pro* = "before" + *polis* = "city") is a resinous substance collected by honey bees from tree buds, sap flows, and other [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]), **green Brazilian type** (*Baccharis dracunculifolia*), **red Brazilian type** (*Dalbergia ecastaphyllum*), and **Pacific type** (New Zealand, Australia, Taiwan) each have distinct chemoprofiles.

### Pharmacological Activities

**Antibacterial**: Propolis exhibits broad-spectrum activity against Gram-positive bacteria (including MRSA) with minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) often below 400 μg/mL. Activity against Gram-negative bacteria is weaker but enhanced when combined with antibiotics — a synergistic effect documented against *P. aeruginosa* and *E. coli*. Mechanisms include disruption of cell membranes, inhibition of bacterial efflux pumps, and interference with quorum sensing.

**Antifungal**: Effective against *[[bloomfield-candida-albicans-yeast-hyphal-dimorphism]]*, *C. tropicalis*, *C. krusei*, and dermatophytes. Inhibits biofilm formation and germ tube development in *Candida* species.

**Antiviral**: In-vitro studies show activity against HSV-1, HSV-2, influenza A (H1N1, H3N2), HIV, and SARS-CoV-2. Mechanisms include blocking viral entry, inhibiting viral polymerases, and modulating host immune responses.

**Anti-inflammatory**: Inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX-2) and lipoxygenase (LOX) pathways, reduces NF-κB activation, and decreases production of prostaglandins and leukotrienes. CAPE is the primary anti-inflammatory compound.

**Immunomodulatory**: Stimulates macrophage activity, enhances antibody production, and modulates cytokine profiles. At low doses stimulates immune response; at high doses can be immunosuppressive — a biphasic effect observed in multiple studies.

**Antioxidant**: Scavenges free radicals (DPPH, ABTS assays) with potency comparable to vitamin E in some extracts. The flavonoid content is the primary contributor.

**Anti-tumor potential**: CAPE and chrysin have demonstrated pro-apoptotic effects in multiple cancer cell lines (breast, colon, prostate, glioma) through mechanisms including mitochondrial pathway activation, cell cycle arrest, and inhibition of NF-κB signaling. These remain preclinical — no human trials have established efficacy.

### Clinical Applications

- **Oral health**: Propolis mouthwashes and toothpastes reduce plaque accumulation, gingivitis, and oral bacterial counts. Effective against *Streptococcus mutans* and *Porphyromonas gingivalis*.
- **Wound healing**: Propolis ointments accelerate epithelialization and reduce inflammation in burns, ulcers, and surgical wounds.
- **Respiratory infections**: Propolis throat sprays and lozenges reduce duration and severity of upper respiratory tract infections. A meta-analysis (2020) found significant reduction in incidence of acute otitis media in children.
- **Dermatology**: Creams and ointments for herpes simplex, eczema, and acne show benefit in small clinical trials.

### Forms of Use

- **Tinctures** (alcohol extract): Most common; 20–50% ethanol for optimal extraction. Typical dose: 20–30 drops, 1–3× daily.
- **Water extracts**: Suitable for alcohol-sensitive individuals; lower extraction efficiency for some compounds.
- **Standardized extracts**: Capsules/tablets with guaranteed flavonoid content (e.g., 50 mg flavonoids/capsule).
- **Lozenges and sprays**: For oral and throat conditions; 100–200 mg propolis per dose.
- **Creams and ointments**: 3–10% propolis concentration for topical use.

## Royal Jelly

### Production and Biology

Royal jelly is a protein-rich secretion from the hypopharyngeal and mandibular glands of worker bees (6–12 days old). It is the sole food source for queen larvae throughout development, and the primary food of adult queens. This differential feeding triggers the queen's dramatically different morphology — 40× larger body weight, fully developed ovaries, and a lifespan of 3–5 years versus 4–6 weeks for workers.

A single queen cell contains approximately 200–300 mg of royal jelly, harvested by beekeepers using grafting methods that transfer worker larvae into artificial queen cups.

### Composition

| Component | Content | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Water | 60–70% | Variable; affects shelf stability |
| Proteins | 9–18% | Major Royal Jelly Proteins (MRJP1–9), apisimin |
| Lipids | 3–8% | 10-hydroxy-2-decenoic acid (10-H2DA), unique fatty acids |
| Sugars | 7–18% | Mainly fructose and glucose |
| Amino acids | ~1% | All essential amino acids; rich in proline and lysine |
| Vitamins | Trace | B-complex (especially B5/pantothenic acid), trace A, D, E |
| Minerals | Trace | Calcium, iron, potassium, zinc |
| Acetylcholine | ~1 mg/g | Highest concentration in any natural source |

10-H2DA (10-hydroxy-2-decenoic acid) is the signature fatty acid of royal jelly, found nowhere else in nature at significant concentrations. It contributes to antibacterial and antitumor activities.

### Health Claims and Evidence

**Antioxidant activity**: Moderate free radical scavenging, primarily from flavonoids and MRJPs. Protects against oxidative stress in liver, kidney, and brain tissues in animal models.

**Neuroprotective effects**: Royal jelly stimulates neural stem cell proliferation, improves memory in rodent models (Morris water maze), and shows preliminary evidence for protection against neurodegenerative processes. A small RCT (2016) found improved cognitive function in healthy older adults after 6 months of supplementation.
