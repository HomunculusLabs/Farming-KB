## Heritage and Landrace Crop Varieties for Small Farmers

Heritage and landrace crop varieties represent the genetic backbone of human
agriculture. These open-pollinated plants have been shaped by centuries of
farmer selection, environmental pressure, and cultural preference. Unlike modern
[[coleman-louis-savier-twentieth-century-maraicher]]. This erosion continues as industrial agriculture
consolidates around a shrinking number of patented hybrid and genetically
modified varieties. Small farmers who maintain heritage and landrace varieties
are not merely preserving nostalgia; they are safeguarding the raw genetic
material that future plant breeding efforts may urgently need.

## The Biology of Open-Pollinated Varieties

Open-pollinated varieties reproduce through natural pollination mechanisms,
whether by wind, insects, or self-fertilization within flowers. When grown in
isolation from other varieties of the same species, they produce offspring
that closely resemble the parent generation. This breeding stability is the
fundamental characteristic that distinguishes them from F1 hybrids.

Self-pollinating species such as tomatoes, peppers, beans, peas, lettuce,
and eggplant transfer pollen within their own flowers before they open. These
species can be saved from remarkably small populations without suffering
inbreeding depression. Steve Solomon notes that as few as five to ten plants
of a self-pollinating variety can maintain genetic vigor over many generations,
making them ideal entry points for beginning seed savers.

Outcrossing species, including all brassicas, corn, beets, carrots, onions,
squash, cucumbers, and spinach, require pollen exchange between different
plants. When saved from too small a population, these species suffer rapid
inbreeding depression characterized by declining vigor, reduced yield, and
eventual sterility. Solomon provides specific minimum population sizes: corn
requires at least fifty plants, most brassicas need two hundred or more, and
kale can sometimes be maintained with as few as twelve. These requirements
make outcrossing species significantly more challenging for small-scale seed
producers.

The wet seed versus dry seed distinction affects processing methods but not
the underlying genetics. Dry-seeded crops including beans, peas, lettuce, and
grains form seeds in pods or dried flower heads that can be harvested once
fully mature. Wet-seeded crops including tomatoes, peppers, melons, squash,
and cucumbers embed their seeds in fleshy fruit that must ripen fully before
seed extraction. Both types produce viable open-pollinated seed when proper
isolation and population size requirements are observed.

## Heirlooms Versus Landraces Versus Hybrids

The term heirloom lacks a strict legal definition but is generally understood
to describe open-pollinated varieties that have been cultivated for at least
fifty years and maintained through generations of seed saving rather than
commercial breeding programs. Many heirlooms carry rich cultural histories,
connected to specific regions, immigrant communities, or family traditions.
The Brandywine tomato, Moon and Stars watermelon, and Dragon Tongue bean are
classic examples with well-documented provenance.
