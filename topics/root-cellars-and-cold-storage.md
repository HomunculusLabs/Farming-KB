## Root Cellars and Cold Storage
A root cellar is an earth-tempered cold storage space used to keep food in a
fresh, living condition after harvest. Unlike canning, drying, freezing, or
fermentation, [[cover-crop-selection-guide]], harvest
timing, curing, storage architecture, rodent exclusion, and winter kitchen
practice into one system. The same room that keeps carrots crisp may ruin onions
if it is too damp; the same apple crate that seems harmless may cause potatoes
to sprout through ethylene exposure. Successful cold storage therefore depends
less on a single ideal cellar than on a set of zones matched to different crops.
Writers on self-sufficient gardening, including Steve Solomon, describe root
cellaring as a way to carry fresh food through the period when field harvests
are limited or impossible. Permaculture writers such as Bill Mollison place
cellars and [[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]]
ranges that turn this old practice into a precise post-harvest technology.
