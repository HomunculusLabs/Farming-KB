## Irrigation Design for Small Farms

Irrigation design for small farms is the art and science of delivering the
right amount of water to the right place at the right time, using systems
scaled to the resources and labor constraints of homesteads, market gardens,
and smallholder operations. Unlike large-scale agricultural irrigation, which
depends on center pivots, canal networks, and pump stations, small-farm
irrigation prioritizes flexibility, water efficiency, low capital cost, and
compatibility with diverse cropping systems that change from season to season.

The fundamental challenge of irrigation design is balancing three competing
demands: crop water needs, available water supply, and the labor or energy
required to deliver it. Every farm has a different combination of soil type,
climate, crop mix, topography, and water source, which means there is no
single correct irrigation system. The best design emerges from understanding
the principles that underlie all irrigation methods and then adapting them to
the specific conditions of a given site.

Water is the primary limiting factor in most terrestrial ecosystems. Soil
erosion, desertification, and water scarcity threaten food security for
billions of people worldwide. At the farm scale, even small improvements in
water management can dramatically increase yields, reduce labor, and build
resilience against drought. The difference between a farm that thrives in a
dry year and one that fails often comes down to how well its irrigation system
was designed and managed.
