# Companion Planting and Polycultures

Companion planting is the practice of growing different plant species in close
proximity for their mutual benefit. The concept is ancient -- indigenous peoples
worldwide developed sophisticated intercropping systems millennia before modern
agriculture -- yet it remains one of the most accessible and immediately
practical techniques available to home gardeners, homesteaders, and farmers
seeking to reduce their dependence on chemical inputs. At its best, companion
planting creates a miniature ecosystem in each garden bed, where plants
support each other through [[comparison-dynamic-accumulators-vs-cover-crops]],"
mining minerals from subsoil that shallow-rooted crops cannot reach. Comfrey
brings up potassium, calcium, and phosphorus from depths of six to ten feet.
Chicory, dandelion, yarrow, and daikon radish perform similar functions. When
these plants drop leaves or are cut for mulch, the minerals become available
to neighboring plants at the soil surface.
