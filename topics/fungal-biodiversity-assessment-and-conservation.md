# Fungal Biodiversity Assessment and Conservation

Fungal biodiversity represents one of the most expansive and least
understood domains of life on Earth. Current estimates place the total
number of fungal species at between 2.2 and 3.8 million, yet fewer than
150,000 have been formally described. This staggering gap means the vast
majority of fungal species remain undocumented, unnamed, and effectively
invisible to conservation efforts. Fungi underpin many [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] and decomposition to
plant mutualisms and the regulation of food webs, making their
conservation a matter of broad ecological significance.
