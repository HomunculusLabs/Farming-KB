# Vanilla Cultivation and Orchid Ecology

Vanilla is the world's most labor-intensive spice, derived from the cured seed pods of climbing orchids in the genus *Vanilla*. Native to the tropical forests of Mexico and [[permplant-carob-drought-tolerant-food-tree]] or [[stamets-cultivator-liquid-culture-propagation-methods]] is increasingly used for large-scale production and for producing disease-free planting material, particularly for *V. planifolia* 'Handy' and other improved selections.

## Growth Cycle

Vanilla vines follow a distinctive growth pattern:

1. **[[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]], May–July in the Northern Hemisphere). Each flower opens in the morning and lasts only one day — if not pollinated, it wilts and drops by evening.

### Flower Structure

The vanilla flower exhibits the typical orchid form but with a unique pollination barrier:

- **Sepals and petals**: Six tepals arranged in two whorls, pale green-yellow to white
- **Labellum**: A modified petal forming an elaborate lip, often with colored markings
- **Column**: The fused reproductive structure
- **Rostellum**: A flap of tissue separating the anther (pollen) from the stigma — this is the key obstacle that prevents self-pollination
- **Pollinia**: Two waxy pollen masses (pollinia) enclosed in a single anther cap

### Hand-Pollination Technique

The Bourbon method, as discovered by Edmond Albius:

1. In the early morning (before 10 AM), select a flower that opened that day
2. Using a bamboo splinter, toothpick, or specialized tool (stylette), lift the rostellum
3. Press the pollinia down onto the sticky stigma
4. The flower has been successfully pollinated when the pollinia adhere to the stigma

A skilled worker can pollinate 1,000–2,000 flowers per day. Pollination success rates of 80–90% are achievable with experience. Only 5–10% of flowers are typically left unpollinated to avoid overtaxing the vine.

## Bean Development

After successful pollination, the ovary begins to swell into a pod (technically a capsule, commonly called a "bean"). Development proceeds over 9–12 months:

- **Months 1–3**: Rapid elongation; the bean reaches near-full length (15–25 cm for *V. planifolia*)
- **Months 3–6**: Slow thickening; chlorophyll content is high; beans are deep green
