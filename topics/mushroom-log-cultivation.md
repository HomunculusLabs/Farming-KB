## Introduction

Mushroom log cultivation is one of the oldest and most sustainable methods for
producing gourmet and medicinal fungi. Unlike indoor substrate cultivation that
relies on sterilized grain or [[coleman-louis-savier-twentieth-century-maraicher]], these methods spread globally and were adapted for a wide
range of species beyond shiitake, including oyster mushrooms (*Pleurotus*
spp.), lion's mane (*Hericium erinaceus*), reishi (*Ganoderma lingzhi*), and
maitake (*[[query-difference-between-shiitake-and-oyster-mushrooms]] mushrooms. Maple decomposes
  somewhat faster than oak, shortening log life to 3 to 5 years.
- **Beech** (*Fagus* spp.): Excellent for shiitake and lion's mane. Beech has
  a dense, fine-grained structure that fungi colonize readily. Common in
  [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] and Europe.
- **Ironwood / Hop Hornbeam** (*Ostrya virginiana*): Very dense wood with a
  long productive life, though harder to cut and handle due to its hardness.
  Good for shiitake.
- **Birch** (*Betula* spp.): Particularly good for oyster mushrooms (*Pleurotus
  ostreatus*). Birch decomposes faster than oak or maple, so expect 2 to 4
  years of production. Paper birch and yellow birch both work.
- **Alder** (*Alnus* spp.): A nitrogen-fixing hardwood favored for shiitake on
  the Pacific coast. Alder decomposes relatively quickly (2 to 4 years) but
  produces excellent early yields.
- **Sweetgum** (*[[liquidambar-styraciflua]]*): Acceptable for shiitake, especially
  in the southeastern United States where it is abundant.
- **Poplar / Cottonwood** (*Populus* spp.): Softer hardwood suitable for oyster
  mushrooms. Decomposes rapidly, giving 1 to 3 years of production.

### Woods to Avoid

- **Conifers** (pine, spruce, fir, cedar, hemlock): Most cultivated mushroom
  species cannot efficiently digest the lignin-rich, resinous wood of conifers.
  Conifer resins also contain fungistatic compounds that inhibit mycelial
  growth. Exceptions are a few specialist species like *Lentinus lepideus*
  that have evolved to decompose conifer wood.
- **Black Walnut** (*Juglans nigra*): Produces juglone, a natural herbicide
  and fungicide, that suppresses fungal growth.
- **Black Locust** (*[[robinia-pseudoacacia]]*): Extremely rot-resistant heartwood
  that most fungi cannot decompose within a practical timeframe.
- **Eucalyptus**: Contains essential oils and phenolic compounds that inhibit
  fungal colonization. Not recommended unless using species-specific spawn
  strains developed for eucalyptus.
- **Osage Orange** (*[[maclura-pomifera]]*): One of the most decay-resistant North
  American hardwoods. Logs can persist for decades without meaningful fungal
  colonization.

## Mushroom Species for Log Cultivation

### Shiitake (*Lentinula edodes*)

The benchmark species for log cultivation worldwide. Shiitake is a white-rot
fungus that degrades both cellulose and lignin, producing meaty, umami-rich
caps prized in cuisine and valued for their [[shiitake-mushroom-cultivation-and-medicinal-properties|Shiitake Mushroom Cultivation and Medicinal Properties]]
