## Iboga, Ibogaine, and the Bwiti: African Psychedelic Medicine

Iboga ([[sacred-mexican-morning-glories-ololiuqui-and-tlitliltzin]], and the harmala
alkaloids of [[mckenna-iboga-tabernanthe-ibogaine-fang-bwiti-initiation-cult]] ceremony is one of the most demanding psychedelic
rituals documented anywhere in the world. Initiates consume large doses
of iboga root bark, roughly 10 grams of dried powder for a full
psychedelic effect, though traditional initiation doses can be much
larger, consumed over extended periods of 8 to 24 hours, to "break
open the head" and induce contact with ancestors through collapse and
hallucinations. The experience is one of death and rebirth: initiates
confront ancestral spirits, undergo a comprehensive life review, and
emerge with a transformed sense of identity and purpose.

Fresh or dried roots are eaten pure or added to palm wine. In some
traditions, the root is consumed in a ritual context with specific
songs, dances, and prayers that structure the visionary experience.
The ceremony involves all-night dancing, singing, and communal
consumption of large amounts of root bark. Several admixture plants may
be employed: Alchornea floribunda (known as Alan) is consumed to help
produce the collapse experienced during initiation, and Elaeophorbia
drupifera may be used when Alan is slow to take effect, its latex
applied directly to the eyes with a parrot feather to affect the optic
nerve and induce visions.
