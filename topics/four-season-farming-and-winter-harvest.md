# Four-Season Farming and Winter Harvest

Four-season farming, as developed by Eliot Coleman at Four Season Farm in
Harborside, Maine (44.33 degrees N), is a comprehensive system for
producing fresh vegetables year-round in cold climates without
supplementary heat. The approach synthesizes decades of practical
experimentation with insights from the [[coleman-parisian-maraicher-system]] tradition,
British organic pioneers, and modern soil science. The system proves that
cold-hardy vegetables, combined with simple protected-cultivation
structures and carefully calibrated planting schedules, can provide
harvestable crops through the depths of winter even in USDA Zone 5, where
outdoor temperatures regularly fall to minus 20 degrees Fahrenheit.

The system rests on three interlocking components: cold-hardy vegetable
varieties that tolerate freezing temperatures; [[query-what-is-succession-planting-and-how-do-i-plan-a-succession-planting-schedule]] from
late summer through late fall to provide continual harvest; and protected
cultivation using unheated greenhouses with inner row-cover layers to
create a "twice-tempered" microclimate. The synergy of these three
well-known components produces results greater than the sum of their
parts, enabling growers to harvest fresh greens 52 weeks per year from a
small farm in coastal Maine.

Coleman's broader philosophy, which he calls "deep organic" farming, goes
beyond organic certification to optimize every factor affecting crop
quality, from soil biology and mineral nutrition to variety selection and
[[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]]. The guiding principle comes from Lady Eve Balfour:
"Working to establish optimum soil conditions is the most constructive
approach in a dependable long-range philosophy of agriculture." This
philosophy views the farm as a human-managed microcosm of the natural
world, where fertility is maintained by biological processes of living
soil organisms feeding on [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]].

The economic case is compelling. Winter-harvested greens command premium
prices, typically three to ten times that of equivalent summer produce,
because they arrive during a period of natural scarcity. Coleman has
achieved gross returns of $80,000 per acre per year, with unheated cold
houses targeting $5 per square foot per year across three crops and
minimally heated cool houses targeting $10 per square foot across six.
The system requires minimal infrastructure beyond standard hoop houses,
row covers, and compost-making capacity, making it accessible to
small-scale growers worldwide.
