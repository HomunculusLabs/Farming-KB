## Dynamic Accumulators for Phosphorus Cycling

Several plant species are particularly effective at mining phosphorus from
unavailable soil pools and concentrating it in their tissues. Buckwheat is
perhaps the most celebrated non-leguminous phosphorus accumulator.
Research has shown that buckwheat not only dissolves and absorbs mineral
phosphorus but also excretes appreciable amounts back into the soil while
still growing, a phenomenon called subsequent excretion. This makes
buckwheat a biological phosphorus pump, drawing phosphorus from locked
mineral forms and depositing it in plant-available form in the root zone.

Mustards serve a complementary role as cool-season phosphorus cyclers.
Their deep taproots improve drainage and tilth in heavy clay soils, and
like buckwheat, they dissolve mineral phosphorus and release it through
root exudation. Because mustards grow in spring and fall when buckwheat
cannot, gardeners can maintain year-round phosphorus cycling by alternating
the two. Mustards also provide biofumigation benefits: their glucosinolate
compounds release volatile chemicals upon soil incorporation that
suppress soil-borne pathogens and nematodes.

Legumes such as alfalfa, red clover, and alsike clover function as dual-
purpose accumulators, building both nitrogen and phosphorus reserves
