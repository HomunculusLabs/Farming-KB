# Sedum and Stonecrop Cultivation

## Introduction

The genus **Sedum** (family Crassulaceae), commonly known as **stonecrop**, comprises approximately 400–500 species of succulent flowering plants distributed across the Northern Hemisphere, with centers of diversity in Mexico, [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]], and the Mediterranean basin. The genus name derives from the Latin *sedere* ("to sit"), referring to the way many species appear to sit upon rocks and stony ground. Stonecrops have been cultivated for centuries for their ornamental value, [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

## Morphology and Growth Habits

Sedum species exhibit remarkable morphological diversity, adapted to their varied native habitats:

### Growth Forms

- **Creeping and mat-forming species**: Low-growing, prostrate plants that spread via stolons or rooting stems to form dense carpets. Typically 2–10 cm tall. Examples include S. acre, S. album, and S. rupestre.
- **Upright species**: Clump-forming perennials with erect or semi-erect stems reaching 30–90 cm. These include the Hylotelephium group (S. spectabile, S. telephium) and produce prominent terminal flower clusters.
- **Shrubby/subshrubby species**: Semi-woody base with succulent herbaceous stems. Examples include S. palmeri and S. praealtum, native to Mexico.

### Leaf Characteristics

- Leaves are typically fleshy, entire (smooth-margined), and arranged alternately, oppositely, or in whorls depending on species.
- Colors range from bright green (S. acre) to glaucous blue-gray (S. reflexum), purple-tinged (S. spurium cultivars), and variegated forms.
- Leaf shape varies from cylindrical and terete (S. reflexum, S. sexangulare) to flattened and obovate (S. album) to broadly ovate (S. spectabile).
- Many species produce a waxy cuticle that enhances water retention and provides sun protection.

### Root Systems

- Fibrous, shallow root systems are typical, adapted to thin rocky substrates.
- Many creeping species root at stem nodes, enabling rapid vegetative spread.
- Roots are generally tolerant of poor, nutrient-deficient soils and can penetrate fissures in stone.

### Species Profiles

**Sedum spurium** — Native to the Caucasus and northern Iran. Vigorous creeping [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]] with rounded, toothed leaves. Widely cultivated cultivars include 'Dragon's Blood' (red-tinged foliage, deep pink flowers), 'Tricolor' (cream and pink variegated margins), and 'Fuldaglut' (cherry-red foliage). One of the most reliable [[hamilton-autumn-garden-tasks]]. Notable cultivars include 'Autumn Joy', 'Brilliant', 'Meteor', and 'Matrona'.

**[[sedum-acre]]** — Native to Europe and North Africa. One of the smallest stonecrops, forming tight golden-green mats. Extremely [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] by volume).

### Water Needs

- Once established, most sedums require **minimal supplemental irrigation** — often only during prolonged droughts exceeding 4–6 weeks.
- Newly planted sedums need regular watering (1–2 times per week) for the first 4–8 weeks until roots establish.
- Overwatering is the most common cause of sedum failure. Allow soil to dry completely between waterings.
- In containers, water thoroughly when soil is dry to the touch, then allow full drainage.

### Temperature and Hardiness

- Most temperate species are extremely cold-hardy, surviving USDA Zones 3–9.
- Species from Mediterranean and subtropical origins (S. dasyphyllum, S. adolphii, S. nussbaumerianum) are less cold-hardy (Zones 7–10).
- All temperate sedums require a period of winter dormancy. Consistent winter wetness (poorly drained soil) is more lethal than extreme cold.

## Propagation Methods

### Stem Cuttings

- The simplest and most reliable method for most species.
- Take 5–10 cm cuttings from healthy, non-flowering stems in spring or early summer.
- Remove lower leaves and allow cut ends to callus for 1–3 days before inserting into well-drained medium.
- Rooting typically occurs within 2–3 weeks without rooting hormone.
- Many creeping species will root simply from stems laid on moist soil surface.

### Leaf Cuttings

- Effective for many species, especially those with thick, fleshy leaves.
- Remove individual healthy leaves and lay them on dry, well-drained [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] as new growth begins.
- Dig clumps and separate into sections, each with roots and growing points.
- Replant immediately at the same depth.
- Essential for rejuvenating older, center-dying clumps of upright species like S. spectabile.
