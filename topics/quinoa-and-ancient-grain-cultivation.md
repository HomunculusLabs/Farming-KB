## Quinoa and Ancient Grain Cultivation

Ancient grains — quinoa, amaranth, teff, millet, sorghum, buckwheat, and their relatives — represent some of humanity's oldest domesticated crops. [[short-day-and-long-day-plant-classification]] sensitive
- **Salares types**: Salt-flat adapted, extremely salt-tolerant

### Climate Requirements

Quinoa thrives in cool, dry conditions and is remarkably frost-tolerant, especially during [[coleman-winter-day-length-and-persephone-months]]**: Most Andean varieties are photoperiod-sensitive (short-day); newer photoperiod-neutral varieties are available for temperate latitudes
- **Rainfall**: 250–500 mm annually; drought-tolerant once established
- **Frost**: Seedlings tolerate light frost; mature plants survive hard frost briefly

### Soil and Site Preparation

Quinoa tolerates poor, sandy, saline, and [[plants-tolerant-of-alkaline-soils]] better than most grains but performs best in well-drained loam with moderate fertility.

- **pH**: 6.0–8.5 (very alkaline-tolerant)
- **Soil type**: [[sandy-loam-texture-cannabis-microbiome-assembly]] to clay loam; tolerates rocky and shallow soils
- **Fertility**: Low to moderate nitrogen needed; excessive nitrogen causes lodging
- **Preparation**: [[comparison-no-till-vs-conventional-tillage]] or no-till into killed mulch; avoid waterlogged sites

Direct seed 1–2 cm deep into firm seedbed. For small plots, broadcast and rake in lightly, then thin to target spacing.

### Planting

| Factor | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Seed rate | 8–15 kg/ha (broadcast) or 4–8 kg/ha (row-planted) |
| [[wide-row-spacing-intensive-gardening-comparison]] | 30–50 cm (row-planted) or 15–25 cm (broadcast/thinned) |
| Plant spacing | Thin to 10–25 cm within rows |
| Sowing depth | 1–2 cm |
| Germination | 3–7 days at soil temps above 10°C |
| Timing | Spring after last frost; 2–3 weeks before last frost for cold-tolerant varieties |

In [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]], sow quinoa when soil temperatures reach 10°C (50°F) — roughly when peas and spinach are planted. In subtropical zones, sow as a winter crop.

### Growing and Maintenance

- **Irrigation**: Minimal after establishment; 1–2 deep waterings during flowering if drought-stressed
- **Weeding**: Critical during first 3 weeks; quinoa seedlings are slow to establish. Once 20+ cm tall, quinoa outcompetes most weeds
- **Nutrients**: Side-dress with 30–50 kg/ha nitrogen if soil is depleted; phosphorus and potassium benefit seed fill
- **Pest pressure**: Generally low; watch for aphids, flea beetles, and lygus bugs. Birds can devastate mature seed heads — netting may be needed

### Harvesting

Quinoa is ready when the seed heads turn color (red, gold, or brown depending on variety), seeds are visible and firm when rubbed between fingers, and lower leaves drop. The plant dries from the bottom up.

1. Test seed maturity by biting a grain — it should be hard and crunchy, not soft
2. Cut stalks at ground level or pull entire plants
3. Dry in windrows or hang upside-down under cover for 1–2 weeks
4. Thresh by hand-rubbing, stomping in a bag, or using a flail/combine
5. Winnow with a fan to separate chaff from seed
6. **Wash thoroughly** to remove saponin (the bitter, soapy coating). Soak in water, agitate, repeat until foam no longer forms. Low-saponin ("sweet") varieties require minimal washing.

Yields: 800–3,000 kg/ha depending on variety and conditions. Home gardeners can expect 500 g–2 kg per 10 m² bed.

### Saponin and Processing

Saponin is a glycoside coating that protects quinoa seeds from birds and pathogens. Most commercial quinoa requires washing. Breeding programs have developed low-saponin varieties (e.g., 'Titicaca', 'Cochabamba'), but some bitterness provides pest protection in the field. After washing and drying, quinoa can be cooked whole, ground into flour, popped like popcorn, or sprouted.

## Amaranth (*Amaranthus spp.*)

### Species and Varieties

Two main cultivated types:

- **Grain amaranth** (*A. hypochondriacus*, *A. cruentus*, *A. caudatus*): Grown for seed; plants 1.5–2.5 m tall with massive terminal seed heads
- **Vegetable amaranth** (*A. tricolor*, *A. dubius*): Grown for leaves; shorter, bushier, harvested repeatedly

Popular grain varieties: 'Hopi Red Dye', 'Golden Giant', 'Mocoa', 'Mercado', 'Plainsman', 'K432'

### Climate and Soil

Amaranth is a C4 plant — heat-loving, drought-tolerant, and efficient in full sun. It thrives where corn grows.

- **Temperature**: Optimal 25–30°C (77–86°F); frost kills immediately
- **Rainfall**: 400–800 mm; very drought-tolerant but yields improve with consistent moisture
- **Soil**: Adaptable; prefers fertile loam, pH 6.0–7.5
- **Day length**: Short-day sensitive in most varieties; newer varieties bred for temperate zones

### Planting and Cultivation

Sow after soil has warmed to 20°C+ (68°F), 2–3 weeks after last frost. Seed is tiny — broadcast lightly or sow in shallow furrows.

| Factor | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Seed rate | 1–3 kg/ha |
| Row spacing | 45–75 cm |
| Plant spacing | Thin to 15–25 cm within rows |
| Sowing depth | 0.5–1 cm (barely cover) |
| Germination | 3–10 days at 20–25°C |
