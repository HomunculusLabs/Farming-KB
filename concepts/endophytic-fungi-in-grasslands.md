# Endophytic Fungi in Grasslands

## Overview

Endophytic fungi live within plant tissues without causing visible disease
symptoms. In grasslands, the most important group is the Clavicipitaceous
endophytes (family Clavicipitaceae), which form systemic, asymptomatic
infections in above-ground tissues of cool-season grasses. These fungi produce
alkaloid compounds that profoundly affect herbivory, plant competition, and
ecosystem dynamics.

## Types of Grass Endophytes

### Clavicipitaceous Endophytes (Class 1)

Systemic colonization of all above-ground plant parts. Vertically transmitted
through seeds. Sexual stage: Epichloe species (choke disease); asexual stage:
Neotyphodium/Epichloe festucae. Host range is primarily cool-season grasses
(Poaceae subfamily Pooideae).

### Non-Clavicipitaceous Endophytes (Classes 2-3)

Localized to specific tissues, horizontally transmitted by spores between
plants. More diverse, including many ascomycete genera with broader host range
across warm-season grasses, forbs, and woody plants.

## The Symbiosis

The fungus gains sheltered environment, continuous nutrients (carbon, nitrogen),
reliable seed transmission, and dispersal. The host grass receives herbivore
deterrence through alkaloids, enhanced [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]], disease resistance,
and improved competitive ability in mixed plant communities.

## Alkaloid Chemistry

Four [[ascomycota-phylogeny-major-classes-lineages]] of alkaloids are produced by clavicipitaceous endophytes:

### Ergot Alkaloids

Compounds include ergovaline and [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] amide. Cause vasoconstriction,
hyperthermia, and reduced prolactin in mammals. Responsible for fescue
toxicosis — reduced weight gain, reproductive problems, and heat intolerance
in cattle grazing infected tall fescue.

### Lolines (Pyrrolizidines)

Compounds include loline and N-acetylloline. Toxic to insects but relatively
non-toxic to mammals. Widely distributed across endophyte-infected grasses.

### Peramine

A potent insect feeding deterrent with no detected mammalian toxicity. Common
in many Neotyphodium/Epichloe associations.

### Indole Diterpenes

Compounds include lolitrem B and paxilline. Tremorgenic in mammals and
insecticidal. Cause ryegrass staggers in sheep and cattle.

## Livestock Effects and Management

### Fescue Toxicosis

Endophyte-infected tall fescue dominates approximately 35 million acres in the
United States. The wild-type endophyte produces ergovaline and lolitrem B,
causing reduced weight gain, elevated body temperature, reproductive failure,
and rough hair coats. Annual economic losses exceed  billion.

### Novel Endophytes

Selected strains retaining insect-deterrent alkaloids (lolines, peramine) but
lacking ergot alkaloids and indole diterpenes. MaxQ (tall fescue) and
AR1/AR37 (perennial ryegrass) are widely adopted commercial varieties.

### Management Strategies

Replace wild-type with novel endophytes, renovate infected pastures, and
interseed legumes to dilute alkaloid concentration.

## Ecological Consequences

Endophyte-infected grasses can dominate pastures, reducing plant diversity.
Alkaloids reduce herbivorous insect populations, cascading through food webs.
Endophyte-mediated changes in root exudates and [[dighton-litter-quality-fungal-decomposition-rates]] affect
decomposer communities and [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]]. See
[[ericoid-and-orchid-mycorrhizae]] for comparison with other fungal symbioses.

## Evolutionary Considerations

The mutualism is context-dependent. Endophytes impose fitness costs offset by
herbivore protection only when herbivory pressure is high, explaining coexistence
of infected and uninfected grasses in many populations.
