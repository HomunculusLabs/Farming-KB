# Bioregional Resource Index — Framework for Regional Self-Reliance

In Chapter 14 of Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison presents a comprehensive Bioregional Resource Index developed with Maxine Cole for the Northern Rivers Bioregional Association of New South Wales, Australia. This index provides a systematic framework for cataloguing and organizing all practical resources available within a bioregion to support local self-reliance, resource conservation, and community security.

## Purpose and Criteria

The resource index serves as a practical inventory tool for bioregional associations. Its criteria define the types of resources to be catalogued:

**Practical resources** (people, skills, machinery, services, biological products) essential to the functioning of a small region, and assisting the conservation of resources, regional cash flow, the survival of settlement, employment, and community security. Security here means a cooperative neighbourhood and ample, sustainable resources for people.

The index is designed to be built incrementally—topic by topic, sold initially by the page, and eventually compiled as a looseleaf notebook. Volunteers enter local resource centres and addresses under each category. The system is best suited to computer retrieval, anticipating the digital organization of regional resource data.

## Primary Categories

The index is organized into ten primary categories:

### Category A: Food and Food Support Systems

**Criteria:** Native and economic species, organic and biocide-free, products of good nutritional value.

**Subcategories:**

- **A1 — Plant Resources:** Nurseries and propagation centres (including tissue culture, inoculants, mycorrhiza); plant collections and botanical gardens; economic plant assemblies and aquatics; research institutes and horticultural agencies; seed sources and exchanges; native species reserves and nurseries; demonstration farms, gardens, and teaching centres; government department resources and regulations; voluntary plant protection agencies; skilled botanists and horticulturists; publications and reference materials; contractors and farm design consultants; produce suppliers and growers; regional checklists of vegetables, fruits, nuts, and useful non-food species

- **A2 — Animal Resources:** Breeders and propagation centres (artificial insemination, hatcheries); species collections including invertebrates; fish breeders and aquatic species; useful native species reserves; demonstration farms (free range, bee culture); government regulations; voluntary protection societies; skilled farriers, vets, natural historians; contractors and farm designers; publications and references; species suppliers

- **A3 — Integrated Pest Management:** Insectaries and biological control suppliers; safe control chemicals and traps; IPM information sources; stored grain and food pest management; references and libraries; checklists of common pests, predators, and safe control procedures

- **A4 — Food Processing:** Processing equipment suppliers; food processing centres; information on preservation; yeasts, bacterial and algal fermentation materials; processed-product producers

- **A5 — Markets and Outlets:** Local markets; delivery services; export markets and wholesalers; urban-rural cooperative systems and direct marketing; retail outlets; market advisory skills; roadside and self-pick sales; packaging suppliers and ethical packaging designs; annual barter fairs

- **A6 — Support Functions:** Residue testing for biocides and nutrient content (food quality control); soil, water, and leaf analysis services; hydrological and water supply services; fence and trellis suppliers; natural fertilisers and soil amendments; farm machinery and tool suppliers (including appropriate technology and repair services); land planning services; glasshouse, shadehouse, and food dryer suppliers; lime quarries, stone dusts, and geological resources

### Category B: Shelter and Buildings

**Criteria:** Energy-efficient house design and non-toxic materials only.

**Subcategories:**

- **B1 — Construction Materials:** Timber growers and suppliers; stone, gravel, and earth materials; plumbing, piping, drainage, and roofing; bricks and concrete products; tiles, surfaces, and non-toxic paints; furniture and fittings; tools, fasteners, and repair services; library and research resources; current housing statistics; sources of toxins and unsafe materials in buildings

- **B2 — Energy Systems:** Energy-conserving home appliances; hot water and solar systems; space heating and regional house design; power generation systems; appropriate technology groups; low-energy building designers; information and publications; reliable contractors

- **B3 — Wastes and Recycling:** Sewage and greywater disposal; compost systems; solid waste disposal; waste recycling occupations

### Category C: Livelihoods and Support Systems

**Criteria:** Right livelihood or socially useful work; durable and well-made items.

**Subcategories:**

- **C1 — Community Finance and Recycling:** Barter and exchange; small business loans; community banking and investment; land access systems, commonworks, leases, trusts; legal and information services

- **C2 — Livelihood Support Services:** Small business service centres; skills resource bank (business, legal, financial advisory); self-employment opportunities; regional training courses

- **C3 — Crafts and Manufacturing:** Clothing and textiles; footwear and leatherwork; basketry and weaving; functional pottery; metalwork and smithing; functional woodwork; engine repair; functional glasswork; paper recycling and book trades; catering; draughting and illustration; soaps and cleaning materials

### Category D: Information, Media, Communication, and Research

**Criteria:** Essential community information, aids, and research.

**Subcategories:** Regional radio and CB/ham radio; regional news and newsletters; audio-visual services; business and research communications (fax, computer, libraries); computer services and training; libraries and data collections; maps; bioregional group contacts (local and overseas); standard documents and data sheets

### Category E: Community and Security

House and livestock security (siting, neighbourhood watch, cattle watch); flood cleanup; bush, cliff, and beach rescue; communication and report systems.

### Category F: Social Life

**Criteria:** Assistance for isolated people to meet like-minded others.

Introductory services, think tanks, expeditions, and work groups.

### Category G: Health Services

**Criteria:** Basic preventative and common ailment treatment, necessary hospitalization, accident treatment, local resources.

Medical and dental services; alternative health services (ecological and midwifery services, birth support); regional morbidity data and life expectancy; prioritized health concerns including accidents and injuries, infectious diseases, addictions, genetic and birth defects, and nutritional problems.

### Category H: Future Trends and Potential Threats

Assessment of emerging threats and opportunities facing the region.

### Category I: Transport Services

Regional transport infrastructure and options.

### Category J: Appendices

Maps and publications specific to the bioregion.

## Implementation Approach

Mollison recommends building the index incrementally:

1. **Start topic by topic** — don't attempt the full index at once
2. **Sell by the page initially** — generates revenue to fund continued compilation
3. **Compile as looseleaf notebook** — allows continuous updating and expansion
4. **Computer retrieval** — the system is designed for digital database management
5. **Include case histories** — each section should document successful strategies
6. **Volunteer entry** — community members contribute local knowledge

## Relationship to Bioregional Organization

The resource index is a key output of the bioregional office. It serves as both a practical directory and a diagnostic tool: by cataloguing what exists and what is lacking, a bioregion can identify areas where resources leak outward (water, soil, money, talent) and develop local self-reliance strategies to close those gaps.
