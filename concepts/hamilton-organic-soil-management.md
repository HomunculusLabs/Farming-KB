## Soil Types and Management

There are five main soil types: clay, sand, silt, chalk, and peat. Each
requires slightly different management techniques:

- **Clay**: Heavy, cold soil that is difficult to work when wet but becomes
  excellent moisture- and nutrient-retaining when ameliorated with organic
  matter. Dig clay soils in the fall, leaving the surface rough through
  winter to expose maximum soil surface to weather.

- **Sand**: Light, free-draining soil that warms quickly in spring. Nutrients
  are easily lost through drainage, so constant additions of organic matter
  are required. Mulch over beds between plants to reduce evaporation.

- **Silt**: Neither gritty nor sticky, smooth and silky to the touch. Dig
  during the fall to take advantage of weather that breaks soil down.

- **Chalk**: Pale, free-draining, and alkaline. Often hungry-looking with
  shallow topsoil. Dig in spring a few weeks before sowing.

- **Peat**: Largely composed of decomposed matter with low mineral content.
  Generally does not need addition of humus-making materials.
