---# Black Poplar Mushroom Cultivation (Agrocybe aegerita)

The Black Poplar Mushroom, Agrocybe aegerita, has a mellow and
attractive flavor when young. It grows prolifically on deciduous wood
debris, often forming large clusters both in nature and in controlled
environments. This species is an excellent candidate for stump
recycling, especially in the southeastern United States.

## Taxonomy and Common Names

- The Black Poplar Mushroom, Swordbelt Agrocybe
- Yanagi-matsutake (Japanese), Pioppino (Italian)
- South Poplar Mushroom (Chinese)
- A variable fungus that may be split into several distinct taxa
- Once called Pholiota aegerita; other synonyms include
  Agrocybe cylindracea and Agrocybe molesta

## Description

A substantial mushroom, often up to 12 inches in diameter. Cap
convex to hemispheric, expanding to plane at maturity, smooth,
yellowish gray to grayish brown to tan to dingy brown, darker
towards center. Gills gray at first, becoming chocolate brown with
spore maturity. Stem white, adorned with a well-developed
membranous ring usually colored brown from spore fall.

Spores are smooth, ovoid to slightly ellipsoid, brown, 9-11 x
5-6.5(7) microns, lacking a distinct germ pore. Clamp connections
present. Mycelium is longitudinally linear, becoming cottony,
usually not aerial, white at first, soon becoming spotted brown,
eventually tan brown. Primordia usually form on malt extract agar.

## Natural Habitat

Growing saprophytically, often in clusters, on stumps in the
southeastern United States and southern Europe. Preferring hardwoods,
especially cottonwoods, willows, poplars, maples, box elders, and
in China on tea-oil trees. Not known outside the southeastern
states of Mississippi, Louisiana, and Georgia in [[permaculture-designers-manual-climatic-zones-tropical]] of the
Far East.

## Mycelial Characteristics and Fragrance

Mycelium is longitudinally linear, becoming cottony, usually not
aerial. White at first, soon becoming spotted brown and eventually
tan brown. The fragrance signature is mealy and farinaceous, but
not pleasant.

## Spawn Production

Petri dish cultures blended via Eberbach stirrers create liquid
inoculum, which is injected into sterilized grain. Grain spawn can
be used for [[stamets-outdoor-wood-chip-bed-mycelium-transplantation]] chip beds also
produce well, much in the same manner as [[blesching-cannabis-strain-selection-receptor-targeting]].
