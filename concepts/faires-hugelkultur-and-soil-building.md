## Soil Assessment and Improvement

### Reading Soil Problems

| Symptom | Remedy |
|---------|--------|
| Bare soil, no calcium | Cement dust, bamboo mulch, grain husks |
| Bare soil, no nitrogen | Leguminous tree mulch or manure |
| Desert soil, can't retain water | Bentonite (volcanic clay absorbs water) |
| Desert soil, too much clay | Gypsum (allows water penetration) |
| Salty soil | Raised beds (salt leaches below plants) |
| Potash deficiency | Comfrey, wood ash |
| No trace elements | Mulch/compost (contains trace elements and bacteria) |
| Too alkaline (low pH) | Sulfur (target 6.0-7.5 pH) |
| Too acid (high pH) | Lime/calcium carbonate (target 6.0-7.5 pH) |

### Seven-Step Soil Restoration

1. Identify problems from the table above
2. Match symptoms to remedies and apply amendments
3. Improve all soil with cover crops, green manure, or composted manure
4. Encourage worms and beneficial soil creatures (best cultivators and composters)
5. Eroded land needs gentle treatment -- no grazing, plant deep-rooted species (daikon radish, chicory, leguminous trees)
6. Deep-rooted trees pull nutrients from deep layers; leaves return them as mulch
7. Cover all exposed soil with quick-growing local species to prevent erosion

## Soil Teas

Liquefied tea adds nutrients quickly -- especially useful when plants suffer bad weather or attacks (particularly tomatoes and peppers).

**Recipe**:
1. Mix 1 part manure to 3 parts water
2. Ferment 2+ weeks in loosely-lidded container
3. Dilute with 10-15 more parts water (should look like weak tea)
4. Keep adding manure and water for continuous supply
5. Extras: comfrey (potassium), seaweed, kitchen compost
