# Cannabis THC:CBD Ratio Guide for Conditions

Understanding the ratio of THC to CBD is essential for effective therapeutic cannabis use. The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] consistently shows that different conditions respond better to different cannabinoid ratios, based on the receptor systems involved (CB1 vs CB2) and the desired therapeutic effects.

## Receptor Activation Basics

### CB1 Receptors
- Concentrated in the brain and central nervous system
- Responsible for psychoactive effects (the "high")
- Mediate: euphoria, relaxation, altered perception, appetite stimulation, analgesia (central), anti-spasmodic effects
- Activated primarily by THC
- CBD acts as a partial CB1 antagonist (moderates THC's effects)

### CB2 Receptors
- Concentrated in peripheral tissues, especially immune system
- Not psychoactive (no "high" from CB2 activation)
- Mediate: anti-inflammatory effects, [[cancer-immune-modulation-mechanisms]], peripheral analgesia
- Activated by THC, CBD, CBG, CBN, and beta-caryophyllene
- Generally favored by lower THC:CBD ratios (indica strains)

### The Ratio Spectrum

The THC:CBD ratio falls on a spectrum from high-THC to high-CBD:

**High THC:CBD Ratio (Sativa-dominant)**
- Activates both CB1 and CB2
- More psychoactive effects
- Stimulating, euphoric, cerebral
- Better for: daytime use, mood elevation, appetite stimulation, certain neurological conditions

**Balanced THC:CBD Ratio (Hybrid)**
- Moderate CB1 and CB2 activation
- CBD moderates THC's psychoactive effects
- Best of both worlds for many conditions
- Better for: pain, inflammation, spasticity, general therapeutic use

**Low THC:CBD Ratio (Indica-dominant)**
- Favors CB2 activation
- Minimal psychoactive effects
- Relaxing, sedating, body-focused
- Better for: sleep, anti-inflammatory, [[cannabis-autoimmune-conditions-detailed]], anxiety reduction

## Condition-Specific Ratio Recommendations

### Pain Management
- **Acute pain**: Higher THC:CBD ratio for rapid, strong analgesia
- **[[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]]**: Balanced ratio (roughly 1:1 THC:CBD) for sustained relief
- **Inflammatory pain**: Lower THC:CBD ratio favoring CB2 anti-inflammatory effects
- **Neuropathic pain**: Higher THC:CBD ratio; THC is particularly effective for nerve pain
- **Musculoskeletal pain**: Balanced to indica-dominant ratios

### Neurological Conditions
- **Epilepsy**: High CBD, low THC ratios; CBD has demonstrated anticonvulsant properties
- **Multiple Sclerosis**: Balanced ratio (1:1); Sativex clinical formulation uses roughly equal THC:CBD
- **Parkinson's Disease**: Balanced ratio; THC reduces tremor and rigidity, CBD provides neuroprotection
- **Alzheimer's Disease**: Higher CBD ratio; CBD has greater affinity for CB2 and may reduce beta-amyloid
- **Tourette Syndrome**: Higher THC ratio; THC reduces tics more effectively than CBD

### Mental Health
- **Anxiety**: Higher CBD ratio; CBD is anxiolytic while high THC can increase anxiety
- **PTSD**: Balanced ratio; THC addresses nightmares and sleep, CBD addresses daytime anxiety
- **Depression**: Moderate to higher THC ratio for mood elevation; CBD prevents overstimulation
- **Insomnia**: Higher THC ratio; THC is sedating, CBD can be alerting at low doses

### Autoimmune and Inflammatory Conditions
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: Balanced ratio (1:1 THC:CBD), matching the Sativex formulation
- **Fibromyalgia**: Balanced to indica-dominant ratio
- **IBD/Crohn's Disease**: Indica-dominant ratio favoring CB2
- **Psoriasis**: Low THC:CBD ratio; topical application preferred
- **General inflammation**: Indica-dominant for systemic anti-inflammatory effects

### Cancer Symptom Management
- **Nausea/vomiting**: Higher THC ratio; THC is the most effective anti-emetic cannabinoid
- **Appetite stimulation**: Higher THC ratio; THC stimulates appetite through CB1 activation
- **Pain**: Balanced ratio for comprehensive pain management
- **Sleep**: Higher THC ratio from indica strains
- **Daytime symptom management**: Higher CBD ratio to maintain alertness

### Sleep Disorders
- **Insomnia**: High THC ratio from indica strains for sedation
- **Sleep maintenance**: Balanced ratio in edible form for longer duration
- **Sleep apnea**: Consult specialist; THC may worsen respiratory depression

### Cardiovascular Conditions
- **Hypertension**: Low THC:CBD ratio; CBD does not increase heart rate like THC
- **Atherosclerosis**: Higher CBD ratio; CBD inhibits plaque-forming enzymes

### Women's Health
- **Endometriosis**: Indica-dominant ratio favoring CB2 anti-inflammatory effects
- **Menstrual pain**: Balanced ratio for analgesia and anti-spasmodic effects
- **Libido enhancement**: Higher THC ratio from sativa strains for euphoric and sensation-enhancing effects

### Elderly Patients
- Generally prefer lower THC:CBD ratios to minimize psychoactive effects
- CBD-dominant for cognitive concerns
- Topical applications (any ratio) for localized pain

## Minor Cannabinoid Considerations

Beyond THC and CBD, other cannabinoids influence the effective ratio:

- **CBG**: Activates both CB1 and CB2 relatively equally; may enhance antidepressant and anti-inflammatory effects
- **CBN**: Higher CB2 affinity; contributes to sedative effects; increases with [[blesching-cannabis-age-related-macular-degeneration]]
- **CBC**: May work independently of cannabinoid receptors; contributes to pain relief and neurogenesis
- **THCV**: CB1 antagonist at low doses (reduces THC effects), CB1 agonist at high doses; may suppress appetite
- **CBDV**: Promising anticonvulsant properties

## Practical Guidelines

1. **Start with CBD-dominant or balanced ratios** and titrate up based on response
2. **Match ratio to primary symptom**: pain (balanced), anxiety (CBD-heavy), insomnia (THC-heavy), inflammation (CBD-heavy)
3. **Consider time of day**: CBD-dominant for daytime, THC-dominant for evening/night
4. **Use different ratios at different times**: Many patients use CBD during the day and THC at night
5. **The [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect-profiles]] means isolated ratios are imprecise**: Whole-plant preparations with additional cannabinoids and terpenes may outperform theoretically ideal ratios
6. **Track your response**: Journal dosing, ratio, strain, and effects to identify your personal optimal ratio
7. **Ratios can shift**: Your optimal ratio may change over time with tolerance, condition changes, or other factors
