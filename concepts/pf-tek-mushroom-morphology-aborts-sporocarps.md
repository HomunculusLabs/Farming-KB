## Normal Sporocarps

Sporocarps are the "perfect specimens" with fully differentiated caps and
stems. These are the mature, structurally complete mushrooms that most
cultivators recognize. While beautiful and ideal for [[spore]] collecting and
display, the PF TEK emphasizes that mature sporocarps are "weak in
psychedelic potency" compared to the diminutive abortive and mutant
specimens. The cap margins turn up beyond plain, resembling an inverted
umbrella, and gills darken to deep brown with purple spore deposits visible
on the stem.

## Primordia Formation and Pinning

The fruiting cycle begins with the appearance of tiny white pin-like
structures on the colonized substrate cake, a stage called pinning. Within
a week of pinning, small round fungus growths appear that begin to turn
yellow. Following this, primordia develop, described as tiny worm-like
structures with tiny reddish heads. These primordia represent the first true
mushrooms and are among the most potent specimens available. The PF TEK
recommends removing the fungus cake from the jar when primordia first appear,
taking care not to damage these delicate early structures.

## The Cap and Veil as Maturity Indicators

The PF TEK provides clear morphological markers for determining harvest
timing relative to potency. Mushrooms should be harvested while their caps
are still round and before they begin to open, specifically before the veil
under the cap breaks away. As the cap expands like an umbrella, the mushroom
increases in size but potency decreases by weight. The gills transition from
light in color to dark brown as sporulation progresses. Purple spore
deposits on the stem signal that the mushroom has entered its sporulation
cycle and is past its prime for psychoactive use.

## Color Changes During Maturation

Immature specimens are characterized by very light coloration with white
stems and light-colored caps. As mushrooms mature, darker colors develop,
with dark bluish colors appearing on both caps and stems. The bluish
discoloration is distinct from the bruising that occurs when cakes are
handled roughly during the birthing process. Mature mushrooms that have
begun sporulation show darkened caps, upturned margins, and deep brown gills.
The PF TEK draws a direct connection between light coloration and peak
potency, making visual assessment of maturity a practical field tool.
