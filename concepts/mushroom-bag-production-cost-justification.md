Sawdust alone accounts for nearly one-third of all material costs, making it the single most significant expense. PP bags, fuel, and rice bran each represent roughly 10% of the materials budget. Together, these four items account for over 60% of total material expenditure.

### Fuel Substitution Opportunity

Fuel for pasteurization (0.26 Baht/bag) can be significantly reduced or eliminated by substituting purchased corn cob and wood with freely available tree branches collected locally. For producers in rural or forested areas, this substitution can lower the total cost per bag from 3.46 Baht to approximately 3.20 Baht, directly improving the profit margin.

### Contamination Budget

A 5% contamination rate is budgeted at 0.13 Baht per bag. This allowance is critical for profitability:

- Contaminated bags are discarded entirely, losing all invested materials and labor.
- At 3.46 Baht per bag, a 5% loss on a batch of 11,216 bags represents approximately 1,942 Baht in sunk costs.
- Maintaining contamination below 5% is essential; rates above this erode the 17% profit margin.
- Strict hygiene during inoculation and proper pasteurization temperatures are the primary controls.

### Water and Electricity

Water and electricity are listed at 0.00 Baht per bag because they are freely available in the remote areas where these operations are typically located. This is a significant advantage over urban-based production facilities where utility charges would apply.

## Summary

Total production cost is 3.46 Baht per bag, sold at 3.95 Baht for a 17% profit margin. The three most impactful areas for cost optimization are sourcing low-cost sawdust, substituting free fuel, and keeping contamination at or below 5%.
