# Permaculture Earthworks and Water Harvesting

Earthworks are the largest, most permanent changes made to a landscape in [[keyline-design-water-management]] Scale of Permanence
P.A. Yeomans' Keyline concept (adapted by Mollison):
1. Climate
2. Landform
3. Water supply
4. Access/roads
5. Trees
6. Structures
7. Subdivision fencing
8. Soil

Earthworks primarily address items 2, 3, and 4 — they're permanent and expensive, so get them right first.

## Swales

### What Is a Swale
- A level ditch on contour that catches water running downhill
- Water spreads along the swale and infiltrates into the soil
- Not a drainage ditch (doesn't carry water off-site)
- The berm (mound on the downhill side) is the planting zone

### Design
- Use an A-frame or laser level to find the [[comparison-swale-vs-contour-line-water-management]]
- Swale depth: 1-3 feet (depends on rainfall and slope)
- Swale width: 3-6 feet at top
- Spacing: determined by slope (steeper = closer spacing)
- Swales should be absolutely level — any slope causes water to concentrate and erode

### Spacing Calculation
- On a 10% slope: 10-15 feet apart
- On a 5% slope: 20-30 feet apart
- On a 2% slope: 50-80 feet apart
- On gentle (<1%) slopes: 100+ feet apart

### Planting the Berm
- Trees on the berm (roots access stored water below)
- [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]] on the swale floor (prevents erosion)
