## The Philosophy of Mu

[[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]] originates in the philosophy of "Mu," or nothingness, and returns to a "do-nothing" nature. This is not laziness or abandonment but a profound trust in nature's wisdom. The objective of natural farming is non-action and a [[emcdda-return-to-nature-megatrend-mushroom-appeal]].

The "do-nothing" age of consolidation and convergence has arrived. Man must hurry to establish a new way of life and a spiritual culture founded on communion with nature rather than consumption and exploitation.

## Why Modern Farming Is Unnecessarily Laborious

The only conclusion possible when comparing Fukuoka's yields with regional averages is that there must be something drastically wrong with farming practices that require so much unnecessary labor. Modern Japanese farming involves:
- Multiple plowings and harrowings
- Chemical fertilizer applications
- Transplanting of rice seedlings
- Three to five pesticide sprayings
- Weeding
- All consuming far more energy than they produce

Ten times as much energy is expended in traditional Japanese farming as the food calories produced. The farmer must use extra hours away from fields to earn money for agricultural chemicals.
