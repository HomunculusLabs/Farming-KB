## What is Perlite

Perlite is a naturally occurring volcanic glass heated to extremely high
temperatures, causing it to expand dramatically. The expansion creates a
lightweight, porous material with exceptional water-holding capacity.

Each perlite particle contains countless microscopic cavities that absorb and
retain water. The enormous combined surface area is what makes perlite such an
effective humidity medium. Water evaporates from all surfaces simultaneously,
creating sustained and even humidity output.

Perlite is inexpensive, widely available at [[mushroom-crop-management-flush-harvesting]].

## Preparing Perlite

Proper preparation is essential for effective humidification.

### The Soak and Drain Method

1. Place dry perlite in a container
2. Add water and mix thoroughly until all perlite is saturated
3. Allow excess water to drain away completely
4. The perlite should be wet but not submerged in standing water

The goal is full saturation of each particle with excess water removed.
When properly prepared, squeezing a handful produces a few drops but no streams.

### Common Mistakes

Several errors reduce perlite effectiveness:

- **Too dry** — insufficient soaking leads to poor humidity output
- **Too wet** — standing water promotes bacterial contamination
- **Insufficient depth** — too thin a layer cannot sustain humidity
- **Contaminated** — reusing old perlite without [[sterilization]]

A layer of approximately 1-2 inches of properly prepared perlite in the
terrarium base is typically sufficient for the PF TEK.

## Humidity and Temperature Relationship

[[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]] are intimately connected in the terrarium.

### How Temperature Affects Humidity

Higher temperatures increase evaporation but can reduce humidity. As temperature
rises, air can hold more moisture. If evaporation cannot keep pace, relative
humidity drops. This means heating the terrarium often lowers humidity.

### Optimal Temperature Range

The PF TEK recommends room temperature cultivation, typically 70-75°F
(21-24°C). At this range, perlite evaporation matches the moisture capacity
of the enclosed air.
