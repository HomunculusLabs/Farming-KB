# Watering Plants — Techniques and Best Practice

Plants are entirely dependent on water for their survival. While natural
rainfall cannot be guaranteed, the gardener can supplement rainfall
during dry periods to keep plants healthy and productive. Good watering
practice is not simply about applying water — it is about applying the
right amount at the right time, in the right way, to encourage deep
rooting and efficient water use.

## When to Water

The guiding principle of organic watering is to water thoroughly but
infrequently rather than giving frequent light sprinklings. Light
watering encourages shallow surface roots that are vulnerable to drying
out, whereas a thorough soaking drives water deep into the soil,
encouraging roots to grow downward in search of moisture.

Water in the early morning or evening to minimise evaporation. Watering
during the heat of midday wastes a significant proportion of the water
through evaporation before it reaches the roots. Evening watering is
acceptable for most plants but avoid wetting the foliage of crops prone
to fungal diseases, as damp leaves overnight encourage problems like
[[hamilton-root-vegetables-growing]] like
carrots and parsnips need even moisture — fluctuating between wet and
dry causes splitting.

Leafy crops such as lettuce and spinach suffer badly from drought,
becoming tough and prone to bolting. Fruiting vegetables like tomatoes
and [[hamilton-perennial-flowers-organic]]
need careful watering in their first growing season while their root
systems establish. Once established, many ornamental plants are
remarkably drought-tolerant, especially if the soil has been improved
with [[hamilton-mulching-materials-and-techniques]].

generally need watering only in very dry weather once established,
provided the soil was well prepared at planting time.

### Lawns

A newly sown or turfed lawn is very vulnerable in dry weather. Water
using a lawn sprinkler at regular intervals, applying a fine spray.
Established lawns rarely need watering — they recover quickly from
drought and the water is better used elsewhere in the garden.
