## Implications for the Archaic Revival

The Zoroastrian episode is central to McKenna's larger argument about the loss of
ecstatic religion. The suppression of Haoma, whether partial or complete, set a
precedent for the later suppression of psychoactive sacraments in Christianity and
Islam. The pattern — reformer appears, condemns the old ecstatic practices, establishes
a new religion based on moral codes rather than direct experience, only to have the
ecstatic practices eventually return — repeats throughout history.

For McKenna, the recovery of the ecstatic dimension of religion through the
responsible use of psychoactive plants is not merely a personal preference but a
cultural imperative — a return to what he called the "archaic revival," recovering
a mode of religious experience that predates the [[partnership-and-dominator-cultures-in-human-history]] and their
ideological substitutes for direct encounter with the sacred.
