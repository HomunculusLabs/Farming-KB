## Psychedelic Drug Testing and Adulterant Screening

## Overview

Drug testing is one of the most important [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]] means that what you receive may not be what you expect. Adulterants, substitutes, and misidentified compounds can turn a manageable experience into a dangerous or fatal one. Reagent testing and fentanyl test strips provide accessible, low-cost methods to verify substance identity before consumption.

## Why Test Matters

- **Substitution**: A substance sold as one compound may actually be another. For example, NBOMe compounds sold as LSD can be fatal at doses that mimic LSD's effects.
- **Adulteration**: Substances may be cut with dangerous compounds (fentanyl, PMMA, levamisole) that increase toxicity.
- **Dosage uncertainty**: Even when the correct compound is present, concentration is unknown without lab testing.
- **New psychoactive substances (NPS)**: Novel compounds constantly enter circulation with unknown safety profiles.

## Reagent Test Kits

Reagent tests use chemical solutions that change color in the presence of specific functional groups or compound classes. They are inexpensive (typically $2-5 per test), easy to use, and provide rapid results.

### Primary Reagents

#### Marquis Reagent

- **Contains**: Formaldehyde and [[psilocybe-semilanceata-eight-indole-compounds]] -- LSD, [[psilocin]], DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, tryptamines
- **Color reactions**:
  - LSD: Purple/pink
  - Psilocybin/psilocin: Purple/pink
  - DMT: Purple/pink
  - No indole compound: Yellow (no reaction)
- **Critical importance**: Distinguishes real LSD from NBOMe/NBOH compounds (which do NOT react with Ehrlich). NBOMe has caused deaths and is a significant substitution risk for LSD.

#### Hofmann Reagent

- **Contains**: Diphenylcarbazone and ethanolic zinc chloride
- **Detects**: LSD specifically (stronger indication than Ehrlich)
- **Color reactions**:
  - LSD: Blue/purple (more vivid and specific than Ehrlich)
  - NBOMe: No reaction (colorless/yellow)
  - DOx compounds: No reaction
- **Best used alongside Ehrlich**: Both Ehrlich and Hofmann should turn purple for LSD. If Ehrlich is purple but Hofmann is not, the compound may be a non-LSD indole.

### How to Perform Reagent Tests

1. **Prepare**: Work in a well-lit area on a white or ceramic surface. Wear gloves (reagents are corrosive). Have a phone or camera ready to document results.
2. **Sample**: Take a tiny amount of the substance -- a piece no larger than a grain of sand. A single crystal or a small scraping from a pill edge is sufficient.
3. **Apply**: Place the sample on the testing surface. Add 1-2 drops of reagent.
4. **Observe**: Watch the color change over 30-60 seconds. Compare to the reference color chart for that specific reagent.
5. **Document**: Photograph the result next to the color chart for your records.
