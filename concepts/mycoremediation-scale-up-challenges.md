Suspended solids increase the surface area available for fungal colonization, and mixing ensures uniform distribution of nutrients and oxygen throughout the reactor.

The enhanced contact between fungal enzymes and pollutants in slurry systems can significantly accelerate degradation rates compared to solid-phase treatment. For recalcitrant compounds such as [[staycare-fungal-degradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]], the desorption of pollutants from soil particles into the aqueous phase is often the rate-limiting step, and slurry conditions facilitate this desorption process.

Slurry systems also provide better control over environmental parameters such as pH, temperature, dissolved oxygen, and nutrient availability compared to land-based treatment approaches. ## Reactor Design Considerations

Stirred tanks are the most common mycoreactors for submerged growth and are frequently used as slurry bioreactors. Under certain conditions, other reactor types such as packed beds, bubble columns, airlift tower loops, and fluidized beds are also employed. Clogging is usually a problem in packed-bed reactors because of the use of agricultural wastes as inexpensive substrates and nutrients with a large quantity of solids. Sedimentation of solids occurs in bubble columns.

In fluidized-bed and airlift tower loop reactors, solids can be suspended in the upflowing liquid, providing a configuration that combines features of both slurry and biofilm systems. Three-phase fluidized and airlift suspension reactors are used for aerobic and anaerobic wastewater treatment and for the cultivation of immobilized and pellet-forming microorganisms.

The selection of reactor type depends on the characteristics of the contaminated material, the fungal species being used, and the treatment objectives. For high-solids-content materials such as dredged sediments, slurry reactors with powerful agitation systems are required. For wastewater treatment applications, airlift or bubble column reactors may provide adequate mixing with lower energy input.

## Fungal Biomass in Slurry Reactors

Several types of cell immobilization can be used in slurry and related reactor systems: attachment of the microorganism to the surface of a carrier, colonization of a porous carrier by organisms, immobilization in hydrogel, and microbial pellets or granulated sludges. Pellet-forming fungi such as [[singh-cytochrome-p450-fungal-bioremediation]] are naturally suited to slurry reactor operation because the pellet morphology provides self-immobilization.

The capacity for wastewater treatment is proportional to the total biomass of a reactor. In slurry systems, maintaining an optimal fungal biomass concentration is critical. The rheology of fermentation fluids is affected by two primary factors: biomass concentration and fungal cell morphology. Pellet fungal suspensions are less viscous and thus exert less influence on the flow properties of a bulk liquid medium compared to dispersed mycelial suspensions.

## Process Monitoring and Control

Several physical, chemical, and biological parameters are important in monitoring and controlling the performance of slurry bioreactors. These include dissolved oxygen concentration, pH, temperature, nutrient levels, fungal biomass concentration, and pollutant concentrations. Mathematical process modeling involving various assumptions can be used to predict the efficiency of contaminant removal from bioreactors.
