# Mycoremediation Regulatory Framework

The regulatory landscape for [[mycoremediation]] is still developing. While fungi offer promising solutions for environmental cleanup, deploying living organisms in the environment involves regulatory considerations around safety, efficacy, and liability. Understanding this framework is essential for anyone considering field-scale mycoremediation projects.

## Current Status

### Regulatory Gap
- No specific regulations exist for mycoremediation in most jurisdictions
- Falls under general bioremediation regulations
- Often treated as "land application of biological material" — similar to [[composting]] or bioaugmentation
- Regulatory agencies are still developing guidance specific to fungal remediation

### Regulatory Bodies (US)
- **EPA**: Primary regulator for contaminated sites. Clean-up standards set under CERCLA/Superfund and RCRA
- **State environmental agencies**: Often have stricter standards than federal
- **USDA**: For transport of fungi across state lines (plant pest regulations)
- **FDA**: If fungi produce food or medicinal products on remediated sites

## Key Regulatory Considerations

### Site Assessment Requirements
- Contaminant type and concentration must be characterized before remediation
- Baseline ecological survey (existing organisms, groundwater monitoring)
- Cleanup goals must be defined (what concentration is "clean enough"?)
- Regulatory approval may be required before deploying fungal inoculum

### Strain Selection
- **Native strains preferred**: Using locally-occurring species avoids introducing non-native organisms
- **Non-pathogenic strains only**: Must not be pathogenic to humans, animals, or crops
- **Documentation**: Strain identity, source, and safety testing should be documented
- **Genetically modified fungi**: Subject to additional regulation (EPA/TSCA)

### Deployment Permits
- May require permits for: land application of biological material, groundwater injection, discharge to waterways
- State-specific requirements vary widely
- Small pilot projects may be exempt from full permitting

### Monitoring Requirements
- Regular monitoring of: contaminant concentrations, fungal biomass, ecological effects, groundwater quality
- Monitoring duration: typically 5-10 years after remediation completion
- Reporting requirements to regulatory agencies

## Liability Considerations

### If Remediation Fails
- Who is responsible if contaminant levels don't decrease?
- Performance bonds may be required for large projects
- Insurance coverage for remediation projects

### If Fungi Spread
- Potential liability if introduced fungi spread beyond the site
- Native strains minimize this risk
- Non-native strains may be considered "biological pollution"

### Off-Site Contamination
- If contaminants migrate during remediation, liability remains with the responsible party
- Mycoremediation should not increase contaminant mobility

## Standards and Verification

### How Success Is Measured
- Total petroleum hydrocarbon (TPH) reduction targets
- EPA Regional Screening Levels (RSLs) for soil and groundwater
- State-specific cleanup standards
- Ecological risk assessment (not just chemical concentration)

### Verification
- Third-party laboratory analysis of soil/water samples
- Statistical comparison of pre- and post-remediation data
- Ecological assessment (soil organism health, plant bioassays)

## Comparison with Traditional Remediation

| Factor | Traditional (Excavation/Incineration) | Mycoremediation |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | $50-500/ton | $5-50/ton |
| Time | Months | Years |
| Regulatory certainty | Well-established | Evolving |
| Disruption | High (dig, haul, burn) | Low (in situ) |
| Secondary pollution | Yes (transport, emissions) | Minimal |
| Liability transfer | Yes (waste goes to landfill) | Stays on site |

## Future Directions
- ASTM and ISO developing standards for biological remediation
- EPA exploring mycoremediation as a "green remediation" technology
- Insurance industry developing products for biological remediation projects
- Growing acceptance as pilot projects demonstrate efficacy
