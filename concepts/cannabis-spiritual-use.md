# Cannabis Spiritual Use

Cannabis has served as a sacrament in spiritual and religious practices across
many cultures throughout human history. In the context of pagan and wiccan
traditions, as described by Magus Sativus in "Marijuana Magick: A
Practitioners Guide," cannabis is understood as a gift from the earth that can
facilitate deeper connection with the divine when used responsibly and with
reverence.

## Historical and Cross-Cultural Context

The use of psychoactive plants in religious ceremony is a nearly universal human
phenomenon. Magus Sativus draws explicit parallels between cannabis in pagan
practice and other well-established sacramental traditions:

- **Christianity** uses bread and wine as sacred sacraments, with wine
  specifically serving a role of spiritual communion. Sativus argues that
  cannabis should hold an analogous sacred role in pagan practice.
- **Rastafarianism** recognizes cannabis as a holy sacrament central to
  worship, meditation, and connection with Jah.
- **[[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] traditions** employ peyote and ayahuasca as ceremonial
  tools to attain states conducive to communication with the divine.
- The **[[allegro-dead-sea-scrolls-and-early-christian-sects]] church** may have incorporated cannabis into its
  practices, as Sativus suggests, noting that the sacred role of this herb is
  "not something that is foreign or alien to the earth but rather comes out of
  it naturally."

These cross-cultural parallels underscore a fundamental point: the integration
of psychoactive substances into worship is a common and natural human behavior,
not an aberration.

## The Philosophical Framework

Sativus presents cannabis as simultaneously three things: a medicine, a fiber,
and a sacrament. This tripartite understanding grounds the spiritual use of
cannabis in its practical benefits while elevating it to a sacred plane.

The core philosophy rests on several principles:

1. **Responsible Use**: Cannabis should only be employed with proper motives
   and deference to the earth. The practitioner must maintain intentionality
   and avoid overreliance on any substance.
2. **Integration with Ritual**: Cannabis is not used in isolation but woven
   into existing ritual frameworks, enhancing both daily practices and special
   occasions.
3. **Connection to the Divine**: The herb is understood to help move the mind
   to a higher plane and increase feelings that facilitate communion with the
   Mother Goddess, Pachamama, the giver and sustainer of life.
4. **Honoring the Earth**: All use of cannabis is framed as an act of gratitude
   toward the earth that produced it.

## Cannabis as Sacrament vs. Abuse

A critical distinction in Sativus's framework is between sacramental use and
substance abuse. While acknowledging that drugs "can certainly cause declines
in quality of life," the author maintains they "can also cause great increase
in the joys of life as well as the ability of the witch or warlock to improve
their craft." The key differentiator is intentionality and integration: when
used with purpose, reverence, and as part of a coherent spiritual practice,
cannabis becomes a tool for enhancement rather than escape.
