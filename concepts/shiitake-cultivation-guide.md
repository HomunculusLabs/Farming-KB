# Shiitake Cultivation Guide

Shiitake (Lentinula edodes) is the second most cultivated mushroom worldwide and one of the best choices for small-scale and homestead growers. Native to East Asia, shiitake has been cultivated for over a thousand years and remains a staple in Asian cuisine and traditional medicine.

## Overview

Shiitake is a wood-decomposing (saprotrophic) basidiomycete that naturally fruits on dead or dying hardwood trees. Two primary cultivation methods exist: traditional log-based growing and modern indoor sawdust block production. Both are accessible to homestead-scale growers, though log cultivation requires more patience and outdoor space.

## Species and Strain Selection

Shiitake strains are broadly categorized by their fruiting temperature preferences:

- Wide-range strains: fruit at 50-80 F (10-27 C); most versatile for beginners
- Cold-weather strains: fruit at 50-65 F (10-18 C); ideal for spring and fall
- Warm-weather strains: fruit at 65-80 F (18-27 C); best for summer fruiting

Strains also vary by spawn run speed, yield, and cap color (light brown to dark brown). Commercial spawn suppliers typically label strains with temperature ranges and expected timing. Choose wide-range strains for your first grow to maximize success.

## Log Cultivation

### Wood Selection

The preferred substrate species for shiitake log cultivation are:

- Oak (Quercus spp.) — gold standard, long-lasting, high yields
- Sugar maple (Acer saccharum) — excellent alternative
- Ironwood/hophornbeam (Ostrya virginiana) — very dense, long-lasting
- Poplar (Populus spp.) — faster colonization but shorter productive life
- Sweetgum (Liquidambar styraciflua) — acceptable in some regions

Avoid conifers (cedar, pine, fir), black walnut, black locust, and eucalyptus. These either contain antifungal compounds or decompose too quickly. Oak logs 4-6 inches in diameter and 3-4 feet long are ideal.

### Timing

Cut logs during dormancy (late fall through early spring, before leaf bud). Trees should be healthy with intact bark. Inoculate within 2-6 weeks of cutting — waiting longer allows competing fungi to establish.

### Inoculation

1. Drill holes in a diamond pattern, spaced 6 inches apart along the log and 2 inches between rows
2. Holes should be approximately 5/16 inch diameter and 1 inch deep
3. Insert spawn (sawdust or plug spawn) into each hole
4. Seal with food-grade wax (cheese wax, beeswax, or soy wax)

Sawdust spawn colonizes faster and produces slightly higher yields but requires more careful handling. Plug spawn is easier for beginners and works well with a hammer.

### Spawn Run (Incubation)

Stack inoculated logs in a shaded, moist location:

- Cribs method: stack logs in a crisscross pattern, 3-4 logs per layer
- Lean-to method: lean logs against a horizontal support rail
- Keep logs off the ground (on pallets or stones) to avoid soil contact

The spawn run takes 6-18 months depending on strain, wood species, and climate. Logs need consistent moisture — aim for 35-45% moisture content. Water during dry spells or mist regularly. Maintain shade (70-80% canopy cover).

### Fruiting

Shiitake fruiting is triggered by a combination of:

1. Temperature shift (soak logs in cold water for 24 hours)
2. Mechanical shock (dropping or banging logs)
3. Rain/soaking event

To force a fruiting, submerge logs in cold water for 24 hours, then return to the fruiting position. Mushrooms will appear in 7-14 days. Allow 6-8 weeks of rest between flushes. Each log produces for 2-6 years depending on wood species and size.

### Yield Expectations

Healthy oak logs produce approximately 1/4 to 1/2 pound of fresh mushrooms per year per log, with 3-5 forced fruitings per season. Total lifetime yield per log is roughly 2-4 pounds over 3-5 years.

## Indoor Sawdust Block Cultivation

### Substrate Formula

A standard shiitake sawdust substrate:

- Hardwood sawdust (oak, beech, or mixed hardwood): 75-80%
- Wheat bran or rice bran: 18-22%
- Gypsum (calcium sulfate): 1-2%
- Water: 55-65% moisture content

Avoid softwood sawdust entirely. Sawdust from kiln-dried lumber may require supplementation. Fine sawdust (20-40 mesh) colonizes faster than coarse material.

### Preparation and Sterilization

1. Mix dry ingredients thoroughly
2. Add water to achieve 60% moisture (squeeze test: a handful should release a few drops)
3. Pack into autoclavable bags or polypropylene grow bags with filter patches
4. Sterilize at 15 PSI for 2-3 hours (pressure canner or autoclave)

### Inoculation and Spawn Run

Inoculate in a clean environment (still air box or laminar flow hood) once bags cool below 90 F. Use grain spawn at 5-10% rate by weight. Seal bags and incubate at 72-78 F in darkness. Full colonization takes 30-60 days.

### Fruiting Conditions

Once fully colonized, initiate fruiting:

- Temperature: 55-65 F for cold strains, 60-75 F for wide-range
- Humidity: 85-95% (misting or humidifier)
- Light: 500-1000 lux indirect light (12 hours on/off cycle)
- Fresh air exchange: 2-4 air changes per hour

Shiitake blocks typically produce 2-4 flushes with diminishing yields. Total biological efficiency (fresh weight per dry substrate weight) is typically 50-100%.

## Common Problems

- Green mold (Trichoderma): contamination from poor sterilization or inoculation hygiene
- Slow colonization: low temperature, old spawn, or low moisture
- No fruiting: insufficient cold shock, too dry, or inadequate fresh air exchange
- Split caps: rapid humidity fluctuations during fruiting
- Small caps, long stems: low light or high CO2

## Harvesting and Storage

Harvest when caps are 50-75% open (before the veil tears fully for premium grade). Twist or cut at the base. Fresh shiitake stores 1-2 weeks refrigerated. Dehydrate at 95-110 F for long-term storage — dried shiitake reconstitutes well and develops deeper umami flavor. See porcini and boletes for additional drying methods applicable to mushrooms.
