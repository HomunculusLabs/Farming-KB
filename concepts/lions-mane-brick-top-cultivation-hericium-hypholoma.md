## Lion's Mane and Brick Top — Cultivation and Identification

Lion's mane (*Hericium* spp.) and brick top (*Hypholoma* spp.) represent two distinct approaches to
specialty [[mushroom-cultivation]]. Lion's mane is a highly prized edible with remarkable neurological
[[amanita-muscaria-medicinal-properties-heart-anxiety-dreams]], while brick top is a prolific and relatively easy-to-grow species that requires
careful identification to distinguish from toxic look-alikes. Both offer unique opportunities for
outdoor and indoor cultivators.

## Lion's Mane (Hericium spp.)

### Species and Ecology

The genus *Hericium* includes *H. erinaceus* (lion's mane, pom-pom), *H. americanum*, *H. coralloides*,
and *H. ramosum* (bear's head). These mushrooms are primarily found on the eastern coastal plains of
the United States, fruiting from wounds and crevices on hardwood trees and snags. Their flavor has
been described as similar to crab or lobster. To enhance the lobster-like experience, pull the mushroom
apart by hand into shreds and sauté in oil or butter with garlic.

### Regenerative Fruiting

Wild specimens average a few pounds each, though specimens exceeding 15 pounds have been documented.
Lion's mane exhibits remarkable regenerative capacity: when a large specimen is partially harvested,
leaving tissue behind in the tree cavity, a new fruitbody can regenerate from the remaining tissue
within two weeks, producing another full-sized mushroom. This regenerative ability makes it possible
to harvest repeatedly from the same site.

### Mycelium Characteristics

The mycelium is white and very irregular, forming small primordia directly on agar even before
reaching plate edges. It is initially difficult to see due to its extremely fine appearance. Using
colored agar (blue dye with white mycelium) improves visibility. The mycelium exudes a deep yellow
metabolite and can smell sweet to slightly sour.

### Fruiting Body Development

The fruitbody attaches laterally and looks like a velvety ball that differentiates into elongating,
cascading teeth. These teeth are soft and white when fresh, yellowing when mature or overmature. The
mushroom prefers to grow off the sides of logs and trees (and bagged substrate) rather than from
above, allowing growth to hang downward as it develops spore-producing spines. The [[spore-print]] is
white.

### Cultivation

**Outdoor:** Use fresh hardwood logs, primarily oak, maple, and cherry. This mushroom prefers to
emerge from small cavities — drill 2-inch-deep holes with a paddle bit once colonization nears
completion, allowing delicate primordia to form inside before emerging. Inoculation to fruiting takes
six to ten months. Partially burying inoculated logs improves yields. Expect one flush per year.
Trimming a mushroom when half-developed will cause it to regenerate a new pom-pom within a week.

**Indoor:** Supplemented hardwood sawdust sterilized for two hours. Incubate two weeks, then cold
shock under refrigeration for one day. Commercial strains fruit within three weeks. High CO₂ and
humidity are necessary for [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]]. Once primordia form, mist lightly and allow mushrooms
to dry slightly between waterings to prevent rot. For bag cultivation, deflate bags after
colonization, wrap plastic tight around substrate, tape down, and poke two fruiting holes on one side.
Average yield is 1 pound of mushrooms per 5 pounds of [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]].

### Harvesting and Storage

Harvest when spines are approximately 1/2 inch long — as they approach full elongation, shelf life
decreases and spines yellow. Harvest by twisting or cutting clean. Fresh mushrooms keep two to three
weeks at 38–42°F (3–6°C). Hericium species do not rehydrate well, becoming chewy when reconstituted.
Alternative preservation includes powdering, cooking and freezing, canning, or brining.

### Medicinal Properties

Cultivated Hericium have protein content of 28–30% (dry weight), making them among the most
protein-rich [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]]. The genus is highly antioxidative and has demonstrated remarkable
neurological benefits:

- **Nerve regeneration:** Compounds in lion's mane (hericenones and erinacines) stimulate nerve
  growth factor (NGF) synthesis, promoting nerve cell growth and repair
- **Neurodegenerative disease:** Shown to reduce symptoms in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's patients
- **Cognitive function:** May improve memory, concentration, and overall cognitive performance
- **Antibacterial activity:** Active against several bacterial species, most prominently
  *Staphylococcus*
- **Industrial applications:** Mycelium used for treatment of paper bleaching and dye waste

## Brick Top (Hypholoma spp.)

### Species and Ecology

The primary cultivated species is *[[hypholoma-sublateritium]]* (brick top, chestnut mushroom), with *H.
capnoides* (conifer brick top) as a secondary species. Brick tops are relatively easy to grow on
hardwood species using the log raft method similar to nameko (*Pholiota nameko*) or in wood chip beds
using king stropharia (*Stropharia rugoso-annulata*) parameters.

### Critical Identification Warning

Brick tops must be carefully distinguished from sulfur tufts (*[[hypholoma-fasciculare]]*), which are
poisonous. The distinguishing features are clear when compared side by side:

- **Brick top:** Distinct reddish-orange cap with lighter yellow margin; smoky gray gills that
  darken with age
- **Sulfur tuft:** Greenish-yellow gills (the key distinguishing feature); also tends to have
  more intensely yellow tones

Growers should become very confident and experienced with identification before consuming or selling
brick top fruitings. The caps of brick tops have enough tissue above the gills for easy tissue
teasing and plating.

### Cultivation

**Outdoor:** Fruits best from buried logs and stumps using trenched log and raft methods.
Fruitbodies form at the ground-wood interface on shadier sides. Inoculation to fruiting takes six to
ten months, with fruiting primarily in cooler months. Yields are good — Hypholoma mushrooms are very
prolific fruiters.

**Indoor:** Supplemented hardwood sawdust sterilized for two hours. Inoculation to fruiting takes six
to eight weeks. These mushrooms love to fruit down the sides of bags, pushing against plastic before
tunneling upward. Cut bag tops low (just above substrate surface) to prevent stemmy growth and
promote cap development. Fogging the fruiting room helps maintain moisture on exposed blocks. A cold
shock is required to initiate fruiting.

### Mycelium and Spawn

The mycelium is white and linear, exuding a light yellow metabolite. Its smell can be extremely
sweet. Commercial spawn is usually available as wooden dowels or supplemented sawdust for outdoor
cultivation, rarely on cereal grains for bulk substrates. Adding trace amounts of sawdust from the
host tree to agar medium greatly benefits culturing, especially for *H. capnoides*, a conifer-degrading
species.
