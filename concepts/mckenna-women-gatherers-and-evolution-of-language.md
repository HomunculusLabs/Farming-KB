## The Cascade of Civilization

Agriculture, in McKenna's framework, set in motion a chain of consequences that
fundamentally altered the human relationship to nature and to consciousness
itself. Agriculture brings with it the potential for overproduction, which leads
to excess wealth, hoarding, and trade. Trade leads to cities; cities isolate
their inhabitants from the natural world. The efficiency of plant resource
utilization through agriculture paradoxically led to a breaking away from the
symbiotic relationship that had bound human beings to nature.
