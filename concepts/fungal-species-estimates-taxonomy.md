# Fungal Species Estimates and Taxonomy

Estimating the total number of fungal species is one of the most fundamental — and controversial — questions in mycology. Current estimates range from 1.5 to 5.1 million species, but only approximately 150,000 species have been formally described. This page synthesizes [[fungal-species-estimation-methods-total-diversity]], the current state of [[molecular-methods-fungal-taxonomy-and-diversity]], and the classification of the fungal kingdom.

## The Species Gap

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Described species | ~150,000 |
| Estimated total | 1.5M - 5.1M |
| Described fraction | 3-10% |
| Species described per year | ~1,200-1,500 |
| Time to describe all species (at current rate) | 800-3,000+ years |

This gap means the vast majority of fungal species remain unknown to science.

## Methods for Estimating Fungal Diversity

### Ratio-Based Estimates

**Hawksworth (1991) — 1.5 million species**:
- Based on ratio of fungal to plant species in well-studied regions
- UK: ~6 fungal species per plant species
- Global vascular plants: ~270,000 species
- 270,000 x 6 = 1.62 million (rounded to 1.5 million)
- Updated to ~2.2-3.8 million accounting for tropical plants and cryptic species

**Plant-fungus specificity approach**:
- Tropical plants may host more fungal species per plant than temperate plants
- Endophytes alone may number in the hundreds of thousands of undescribed species
- Some tropical trees host 100+ endophytic fungal species

### Molecular-Based Estimates

**Metabarcoding extrapolation**:
- Environmental DNA surveys reveal 10-100x more OTUs than described species in most habitats
- A single soil sample may contain 500-2000 fungal OTUs, most undescribed
- Scaling to global habitat diversity suggests 3-5+ million species

**Large-scale sequencing**:
- Global fungal diversity surveys using standardized ITS metabarcoding
- Sites worldwide reveal largely unique species compositions
- Accumulation curves rarely approach asymptote

### Habitat-Based Extrapolation

**Total habitat approach**:
- Estimate number of fungal species per habitat type (forest, grassland, soil, aquatic, extreme)
- Multiply by number of distinct habitat types globally
- Sum across habitats
- Challenges: many habitats undersampled; habitat boundaries unclear

## Current State of Fungal Taxonomy

### Traditional (Morphological) Taxonomy
- Based on macroscopic and microscopic morphology
- Fruiting body structure, spore characteristics, hyphal anatomy
- Culture characteristics for microfungi
- Has described ~150,000 species over ~250 years
- Limitations: convergent evolution, phenotypic plasticity, cryptic species

### Molecular Systematics Revolution
- [[environmental-dna-sequencing-fungi]] has transformed fungal taxonomy
- **ITS (Internal Transcribed Spacer)**: Official fungal barcode; resolves most species
- **LSU (28S rDNA)**: Useful for higher-level classification
- **SSU (18S rDNA)**: Deep phylogenetic relationships
- **Protein-coding genes**: TEF1, RPB1, RPB2, beta-tubulin for species-level resolution
- **Whole-genome sequencing**: Becoming standard for describing new species

### Cryptic Species
- Many "known" species are actually species complexes
- Examples:
  - *[[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]]*: 6+ phylogenetic species globally
  - *Pleurotus ostreatus* complex: Multiple species across continents
  - *Armillaria mellea* complex: At least 10+ species in [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] alone
  - *Fusarium graminearum* complex: 15+ phylogenetic species
- Cryptic species have different ecological roles, host ranges, and geographic distributions

### DNA-Based Species Discovery
- Environmental DNA (eDNA) from soil, water, air reveals vast undescribed diversity
- Single-spore sequencing identifies species without culturing
- Metagenomics provides functional information about uncultured taxa
- Citizen science DNA barcoding (e.g., iNaturalist) accelerating documentation

## Classification of the Fungal Kingdom

### Major Phyla

**Basidiomycota (Basidiomycetes)**
- ~32,000 described species
- Club fungi: mushrooms, brackets, puffballs, rusts, smuts
- Major ecological roles: wood decay (white/brown rot), mycorrhizae, plant pathogens
- Key classes: Agaricomycetes, Tremellomycetes, Pucciniomycetes, Ustilaginomycetes

**Ascomycota (Ascomycetes)**
- ~65,000 described species (largest phylum)
- Sac fungi: cup fungi, morels, truffles, yeasts, molds
- Major ecological roles: decomposition, endophytes, plant pathogens, lichens
- Key classes: Pezizomycetes, Eurotiomycetes, Sordariomycetes, Dothideomycetes, Saccharomycetes (yeasts)

**Zygomycota (sensu lato)**
- ~1,700 described species (historically; now split into multiple phyla)
- Pin molds, bread molds
- Key groups: Mucorales, Entomophthorales, Zoopagales
- Many coprophilous and soil-inhabiting species

**Glomeromycota**
- ~300 described species (likely severely underestimated)
- [[comparison-soil-food-web-vs-arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] (AM) fungi
- Obligate symbionts with ~80% of plant species
- See [[mycorrhizal-types]]

**Chytridiomycota**
- ~1,000 described species
- Flagellated, zoosporic fungi
- Aquatic and soil habitats; some parasites (Batrachochytrium)
- Includes anaerobic gut fungi (Neocallimastigales)

**Blastocladiomycota**
- ~200 described species
- Zoosporic; include Allomyces model organism
- Some important parasites (Coelomomyces — mosquito parasite)

**Neocallimastigomycota** (sometimes included in Chytridiomycota)
- ~20 described species
- Anaerobic fungi in herbivore guts
- See [[anaerobic-gut-fungi]]
