# DOx Family — Amphetamine Psychedelics from PIHKAL

The DOx family represents a critically important class of psychedelic
amphetamines documented extensively by [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]] in PIHKAL.
These compounds are structural analogs of mescaline with an alpha-methyl
group added to the phenethylamine backbone, creating a substituted
amphetamine. This single structural modification dramatically extends
duration and generally increases potency compared to their 2C
counterparts. The name "DOx" follows Shulgin's chemical nomenclature:
"D" for the 2,5-dimethoxy substitution pattern, "O" for the oxygen at
the 4-position, and "x" for the specific substituent.

## General Characteristics

All DOx compounds share defining properties that distinguish them from
other [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]]:

- **Extended duration:** 12-24 hours or more, with residual effects
  potentially lasting 36+ hours
- **Slow onset:** 1-3 hours before peak effects are reached, creating
  risk of accidental overdose from premature redosing
- **High potency:** Some DOx compounds are active in the sub-milligram
  or even microgram range
- **Stimulant properties:** The amphetamine backbone produces
  significant central stimulation alongside psychedelic effects
- **Serotonergic action:** Primarily agonists at 5-HT2A
  [[psilocybin-serotonin-5ht2a-receptor-pharmacology]]

## DOM (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Methylamphetamine)

DOM holds a unique place in psychedelic history. Originally developed
by Dow Chemical in 1963, it briefly appeared on the street as "STP"
(Serenity, Tranquility, and Peace) in 1967. Initial street doses were
far too high (10 mg tablets), causing extended ordeal-like experiences.

**Dosage:** 3-10 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 14-20 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** +++

Shulgin described DOM as producing a long, slow-building experience
with prominent stimulant qualities. The visual effects are moderate
compared to DOB or DOI, but the cognitive and emotional effects are
profound. He noted that DOM has a "pushy" quality that can become
anxious or overwhelming, particularly at higher doses. The extremely
long duration demands careful planning. Users should expect to be
affected well into the following day.

## DOB (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Bromoamphetamine)

**Dosage:** 1-3 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 18-30 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** ++++

DOB is one of the most potent members of the DOx family and received
Shulgin's rare ++++ rating. Active at remarkably low doses, it produces
intense visual hallucinations and deep cognitive alteration. Shulgin
described the visuals as among the most compelling in the entire
phenethylamine series. The bromine atom at the 4-position creates a
compound with exceptional affinity for serotonin receptors.

The extremely low active dose range (1-3 mg) makes accurate measurement
critical. Eyeballing doses is hazardous. The duration frequently
exceeds 24 hours, with users reporting lingering perceptual changes
for a full day or more. Sleep is typically impossible until 18+ hours
after ingestion.

## DOI (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Iodoamphetamine)

**Dosage:** 1.5-3 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 16-30 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** +++

DOI occupies a position between DOM and DOB in terms of intensity.
The iodine substitution produces a compound with strong visual effects
and a characteristically analytical headspace. Shulgin noted that DOI
tends toward intellectual and philosophical exploration rather than
emotional processing. The compound has been used in research settings
as a 5-HT2A agonist, providing valuable pharmacological data.

DOI's extended duration is consistent with the DOx family pattern.
The slow onset (2-3 hours) combined with the long duration makes
patience essential. Many adverse experiences result from users who
incorrectly assume the compound is inactive and take additional doses.

## DOC (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Chloroamphetamine)

**Dosage:** 1.5-3 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 12-24 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** ++ to +++

DOC is often described as the most "manageable" member of the DOx
family, with slightly shorter duration and a cleaner stimulant profile.
Shulgin noted its more friendly character compared to DOM's intensity.
Visual effects are present but less overwhelming than DOB. The chlorine
substitution produces a compound with moderate potency and a
well-rounded effect profile.

## Safety Warnings

Shulgin repeatedly emphasized several critical safety concerns specific
to the DOx family:

- **Never redose early:** The 1-3 hour onset means users may believe
  the compound is inactive and take more. This is the single most
  dangerous error with DOx compounds.
- **Duration planning:** Clear at least 48 hours free of obligations.
  Driving, work, or social commitments the following day are
  inadvisable.
- **Dose measurement:** Sub-milligram active doses require milligram
  scales or volumetric liquid dosing for safety.
- **Cardiovascular strain:** The amphetamine structure combined with
  serotonin receptor activity produces significant cardiovascular
  effects. Individuals with heart conditions should avoid DOx
  compounds entirely.
- **Drug substitution:** DOx compounds have been sold as LSD on blotters.
  Users expecting a 12-hour LSD trip may be distressed by a 30-hour
  DOx experience.

## DOx vs. 2C Compounds

The relationship between DOx and 2C compounds is instructive for
understanding structure-activity relationships in [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]].
Adding the alpha-methyl group consistently extends duration by a factor
of 2-4x and typically increases potency. This pattern demonstrates
the metabolic stability conferred by the methyl group, which impedes
monoamine oxidase degradation.
