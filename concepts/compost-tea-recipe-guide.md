# Compost Tea Recipe Guide

## Overview

Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) is a water extract of compost that is brewed with foods to select for beneficial organism growth. It must stay aerobic throughout the brewing process to retain beneficial fungi, protozoa, and nematodes. If the tea goes anaerobic, these organisms are killed and the liquid can no longer be considered compost tea.

## Critical Brewing Parameters

### Oxygen

The most critical factor. Dissolved oxygen must be maintained above 5.5 to 6 ppm (mg O2/L) throughout the entire brew. At sea level, 72 degrees F, water typically holds 9 to 9.5 mg O2/L. If oxygen drops below 5.5 ppm, bacteria begin attacking fungal hyphae. You have approximately 5-6 hours after turning off aeration before oxygen depletes and beneficial fungi are lost.

### Temperature

Optimal brewing temperature is around 65-72 degrees F. As temperature increases, the maximum oxygen concentration in water decreases. Near 100 degrees F, oxygen drops into the anaerobic range. Do not brew in direct sun or excessive heat.

### Brew Time

Standard brew cycle is 24 hours. Extended brewing (36-40+ hours) risks oxygen depletion as organisms multiply and consume oxygen. If oxygen dips and recovers, bacterial blooms may have consumed the fungal foods, potentially reducing fungal biomass.

### Water Quality

Water must be de-chlorinated. Chlorine kills the beneficial organisms you are trying to grow. Run water through an activated carbon filter, aerate overnight, or use a de-chlorinating product. Avoid water with high salt levels (high EC).

### Compost Quality

The compost is the inoculum source. It must contain the organisms you want to grow. Good compost should have thick white strands of beneficial fungi growing through it (not grey fuzzy spores). Both thermal compost and vermicompost can be used, but they should meet SFI (Soil Foodweb) minimum standards for bacteria and fungi.

## Basic Recipes

### Bacterial-Dominated Tea (for vegetables, annuals, lawns)

Per 5 gallons of de-chlorinated water:

- 2-3 cups high-quality compost (or worm castings) in a mesh bag
- 1-4 tablespoons unsulfured blackstrap molasses
- 1 teaspoon liquid kelp (optional but recommended)

The higher molasses amount favors bacterial growth. This is suitable for [[bacterial-vs-fungal-soil|bacterially dominated]] plantings with F:B ratio targets of 0.3:1 to 1:1.

### Fungal-Dominated Tea (for trees, shrubs, perennials)

Per 5 gallons of de-chlorinated water:

- 2-3 cups fungal compost in a mesh bag (compost with visible white fungal strands)
- 1 teaspoon molasses (minimal -- bacteria compete with fungi)
- 0.5 oz kelp meal or 1 teaspoon liquid kelp
- 0.5 oz fish hydrolysate
- 1 tablespoon humic acid

For a 500-gallon brew: half a gallon of molasses, kelp at 0.1%, fish hydrolysate at 0.1%. Fungal foods ranked by effectiveness: humic acids and fish hydrolysate > soy sauce > malt > molasses > brown sugar > white sugar (increasing structural complexity of sugars and proteins supports more fungal growth).

### Balanced Tea (for tomatoes, corn, wheat, prairie plants)

Per 5 gallons of de-chlorinated water:

- 2-3 cups quality compost in a mesh bag
- 1-2 tablespoons unsulfured molasses
- 1 teaspoon liquid kelp
- 1 teaspoon fish hydrolysate

Targets F:B ratio of approximately 0.8:1 to 1:1.

## Food Amendments

| Amendment | Effect | Amount (per 5 gal) |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| Unsulfured blackstrap molasses | Bacterial food (simple sugars) | 1-4 Tbsp |
| Liquid kelp | Micronutrients, some fungal food | 1 tsp |
| Kelp meal | Micronutrients, trace minerals | 0.5 oz |
| Fish hydrolysate | Nitrogen, fungal food, protein | 0.5 oz |
| Humic acid | Fungal food, [[chelated-nutrients|chelation]] | 1 Tbsp |
| Fish emulsion | Nitrogen, bacterial food | 1-2 oz |

## Important Notes on Amendments

- Use **unsulfured** molasses -- sulfur suppresses beneficial organisms
- Use **fish hydrolysate** (enzymatically digested) over fish emulsion when possible -- it retains more proteins and beneficial compounds
- Avoid starchy, simple carbohydrate foods (potato starch, white sugar) -- these favor pathogens
- Stick to lignin, cellulose, humic acid, and fulvic acid foods for fungal support
- Do not add mycorrhizal spores during brewing -- they are too delicate and will be damaged; add spores just before application

## Application Rates

### Soil Drench

- **Vegetables/flowers**: 15 gallons per acre, 2 weeks before planting in spring; also in fall
- **Trees/vines**: 10-15 gallons per acre in spring and fall
- **General**: Apply directly to soil, ideally onto compost/mulch layer

### Foliar Spray

- **Vegetables/flowers**: 5 gallons per acre mixed with adequate water for coverage; every 10-14 days during growing season
- **Trees/vines**: 5 gallons per acre per 6 feet of canopy height; once a month if no disease, every 7-14 days if disease present
- **Timing**: Start 2 weeks before bud break; do not spray when bees are pollinating
- **Disease response**: Apply every 3-5 days until outbreak stops

### Transplanting

- Dip bare roots in compost tea slurry before planting
- Use 30-50% compost/worm castings mixed with site soil for transplants
- Water in with compost tea

## Aerobic vs Anaerobic Teas

AACT must stay aerobic to retain beneficial fungi, protozoa, and nematodes. Anaerobic brews:

- Lose beneficial fungi, protozoa, and nematodes
- Cannot cycle nutrients (no predators to mineralize)
- Produce toxic compounds and bad odors
- Lose nitrogen as ammonia gas, sulfur as hydrogen sulfide
- Destroy soil structure rather than build it

## AACT vs Other Products

| Product | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **AACT** | Water extract of compost, brewed with foods, actively aerated |
| **Compost extract** | Water extract of compost, no brewing period (organisms not activated) |
| **Compost leachate** | Passive drainage from compost (mostly soluble nutrients, limited organisms) |
| **Plant tea** | Water extract of plant materials (no compost) |
| **Manure tea** | Made with manure (not compost unless fully composted) |

## Key Points

- Maintain dissolved oxygen above 5.5-6 ppm at all times
- Use quality compost with diverse biology as the inoculum
- Adjust food amendments to select for bacterial or fungal dominance
- Standard brew time is 24 hours
- De-chlorinate all water before brewing
- Apply as soil drench and/or foliar spray at recommended rates
- Test compost tea with SFI to verify organism levels meet standards
