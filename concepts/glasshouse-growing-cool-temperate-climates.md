# Glasshouse Growing in Cool Temperate Climates

The cool temperate climates are the regions where greenhouses are most commonly used for specialty crop or aquatic species production, for house heating, and for the winter production of vegetable and fruit crops. Modern glasshouses are becoming very sophisticated with respect to heat energy conservation and heat absorption, as 70–90% of the cost of crop can be that of artificial heating.

## Insulation Requirements

### Below-Ground Insulation

Insulation is essential not only for the ground below the greenhouse, usually as a trench 1 m or so deep provided with panel insulation, or with straw or sawdust-filled plastic bags, but also for the endwalls, the poleward walls, and the poleward slope of the roof (all can be solid insulated walls).

### Glazing and Air Insulation

The sunward roof slope and wall can be air-insulated using several methods:

- **Trapped bubble insulation**: Standard plastic sheets with trapped air bubbles
- **Pressurised air systems**: Keeping air at pressure between double plastic walls
- **Insulated shutters**: Using insulated shutters at night to cover glazed areas

## Internal Heat Sources

Inside the greenhouse, where the whole earth floor inside the walls is insulated from the cold earth outside, several additional heat masses can be provided:

- **Water thermal mass**: Plastic or metal tanks or drums of water (some can serve as fish ponds)
- **Animal heat**: Areas below plant benches can house rabbits, guinea pigs, poultry, or small domestic animals at night, providing considerable winter heat
- **[[greenhouse-growing]] accessible and profitable even for small-scale producers and home gardeners.
