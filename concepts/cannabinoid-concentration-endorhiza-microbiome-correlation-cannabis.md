## Cannabinoid Biosynthesis and Root Chemistry

Cannabinoid biosynthesis occurs primarily in glandular trichomes on floral and leaf surfaces. However, the precursor molecules originate from central metabolism active throughout the plant, including in root tissues.

The olivetolic acid pathway begins with hexanoyl-CoA and malonyl-CoA condensation, both products of primary metabolism in all plant cells. Whether cannabinoid pathway intermediates accumulate in root tissues at biologically relevant concentrations remains an open question.

If pathway intermediates or degradation products are present in roots, they could directly influence the endorhiza community through antimicrobial or signaling effects. Some cannabinoids have documented antimicrobial activity, and their precursors might share these properties.

Alternatively, the cannabinoid-microbiome correlation could be mediated entirely through other genotype-dependent factors such as root exudate composition or immune signaling. In this scenario, cannabinoids serve as a passive marker of cultivar genotype rather than an active agent in microbiome assembly.

## Possible Interaction Mechanisms

### Root Exudate Mediation

Root exudates are the primary interface for plant-microbe communication in the rhizosphere and at the root surface. Different cannabis cultivars likely produce different exudate profiles, and these differences could drive the observed cultivar-specific microbiome patterns.

If cannabinoid pathway activity in roots influences exudate composition, either directly through intermediate leakage or indirectly through metabolic resource allocation, this could create a mechanistic link between cannabinoid biosynthesis and microbiome assembly.
