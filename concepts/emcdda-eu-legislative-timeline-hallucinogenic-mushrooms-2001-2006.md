**United Kingdom (2005)** introduced the Drugs (Amendment) Act which
specifically addressed the legal status of hallucinogenic mushrooms.
Prior to this legislation, fresh mushrooms had existed in a legal gray
area in the UK — they were not explicitly covered by the Misuse of
Drugs Act 1971 when fresh, though preparation (drying, brewing, or

**Ireland (2006)** was the sixth country documented by the EMCDDA to
enact specific legislation against hallucinogenic mushrooms, bringing
the total to six EU member states within a five-year period.

## Impact on Availability and Market Dynamics

Reports from the UK suggested that legislation had a measurable impact
on the availability of mushrooms and the overall volume of internet
sales. Following the 2005 law, the number of physical shops selling
mushrooms decreased, and online retailers either ceased operations
or relocated to jurisdictions with more permissive laws.

The legislative cascade created a dynamic where retailers in countries
that had not yet banned mushrooms experienced increased demand, as users
in restricted countries sought alternative supply channels. This
demonstrated the cross-border nature of the trade and the limitations
of national legislation in controlling a market that operated
increasingly through international internet commerce.
