## Ethical Seed Companies and Mail-Order Sources

Not all seed companies are equal. An ethical vegetable-garden seed business has a genuine purpose: to help independent persons grow more of their own food more economically and nutritiously. This requires specific practices — variety trials, germination testing, regional adaptation, and honest business conduct — that distinguish quality suppliers from those merely seeking to profit from the gardening hobby market.

## What Makes an Ethical Seed Company

### The Four Essential Practices

An ethical seed company will:

1. **Conduct variety trials**: Grow out the varieties they sell under conditions similar to their customers' gardens, evaluating performance, uniformity, maturity timing, flavor, and disease resistance
2. **Test germination regularly**: Maintain in-house germination testing (even if uncertified) and test seed lots at least twice a year
3. **Serve both gardeners and commercial growers**: Selling to farmers and market gardeners creates accountability — commercial customers demand and verify quality
4. **Adapt varieties to the region**: Trial grounds should be located in roughly the same climatic zone as the customers they serve

### Why These Practices Matter

- **Trials** reveal what catalogs cannot — actual performance, flavor, storage potential, and disease resistance
- **Germination testing** ensures the seed sold will actually perform
- **Commercial accountability** prevents the "sweepings" problem — farmers won't accept inferior seed
- **Regional adaptation** ensures varieties will actually mature and produce in the customer's climate

### The Economics of Quality

There is nothing particularly difficult about conducting trials — it just takes a bit of land, a fair bit of effort, and money. A meaningful trials ground for a small seed business is about half an acre (2,000 sq m). A medium-sized mail-order company might use a few acres. Large companies may use ten acres or more.

Trials are usually laid out in widely spaced rows so each plant can be evaluated individually. Enough plants of each variety are grown to determine uniformity and maturity timing. A worthwhile trial needs at least five plants of a heading vegetable (cabbage, cauliflower) or a minimum 10-foot (3 m) row of a root vegetable (carrot, beet).

## Types of Seed Companies

### Specialty Gourmet Companies

Some companies specialize in gourmet or unusual vegetables. This is not necessarily bad — the customer's main concern may be culinary novelty rather than reliability. It could be perfectly okay to sell this sort of customer fine seed that doesn't germinate terrifically well, or varieties that are irregular with many nonproductive individuals.

### Organic and Heirloom Specialists

Some companies specialize in organically grown seed produced by networks of collaborating growers, or in heirloom varieties maintained by enthusiasts. While admirable, these companies sometimes have weaknesses:

- Varieties may not be regular, inbred, or uniform
- Amateurs doing production may lack skill at growing and storing seed
- Germination levels tend to be sub-par
- Heirloom varieties may be degraded versions of the originals

### The Misrepresentation Problem

Vegetable seeds are arguably the commodity most subject to misrepresentation. A bag of cabbage seed can cost $10 per pound wholesale or $250 per pound wholesale — the contents of both bags look identical. The only difference is germination rate, genetic purity, and vigor, which cannot be seen.

Even quality primary growers have been known to "accidentally" mislabel seed intended for the home-garden trade, sometimes enclosing suspect seed or a discontinued variety worth next to nothing in place of their finest product. This never seems to happen to commercial customers.

## Recommended Seed Suppliers by Climate Zone

### Short-Season Climates

The northern tier of US states and southern Canada within a few hundred miles of the US border:

- **Stokes Seeds** (Niagara Falls, Canada): 10-acre trials ground plus 24 acres for research. Main income from farmers, but home gardeners get the same quality. Never had a Stokes packet fail to germinate acceptably. Serves both US and Canada.
- **Johnny's Selected Seeds** (Winslow, Maine): ~40 certified organic acres for trials, breeding, and production. Breeds many of its own varieties. Offers organics, avoids fungicide-treated seed.
- **Veseys Seeds** (Prince Edward Island, Canada): All varieties tested for reliable short-season maturity. Rigorous germination monitoring.
- **William Dam Seeds** (Ontario, Canada): Five acres of trials, focuses on high-grade hybrid Dutch imports.

### Moderate Climates

The middle American states and similar climates:

- **Johnny's Selected Seeds** (also serves this zone from its Maine location)
- **Harris Seeds** (Rochester, NY): Particularly known for the incredibly delicious Sweet Meat squash. Catalog worth a look.

### Warm Climates

The southern American states and similar:

- **Park Seed Company** (Greenwood, SC): Virtually every variety is appropriate for semi-tropical climates. Bulbing onions labeled as short-day or long-day. Virus-free tissue-culture seed potatoes. Climate-controlled seed storage. Moisture-proof foil packaging. Reasonable prices.

### Maritime Climates

Regions with mild, wet winters and cool summers:

- **[[territorial-seed-company]]** (Cottage Grove, OR): Certified-organic trial grounds, in-house germination laboratory. Originally for Cascadian gardeners but serves broader market.
- **West Coast Seeds** (Delta, BC, Canada): Independent, runs large certified-organic trials ground. Ships overseas.
- **Chase** (UK): Offers certified-organic varieties from European quality seed houses. The only company known to still offer reasonably uniform and productive OP Brussels sprouts varieties.

### Additional Notable Suppliers

- **[[fedco-seeds]]** (Maine): Honest business, extensive catalog, downloadable from website
- **Southern Exposure Seed Exchange** (Virginia): Good for warm-climate gardeners
- **Green Harvest** (Australia): Broad assortment of organic gardening supplies and seeds
- **Diggers Seeds** (Australia): Quality vegetable seeds from international growers
- **Select** (Switzerland): Full-line company offering only the highest-quality varieties in foil-sealed packets at surprisingly low prices
- **Peace Seeds** (Oregon): Breeding service and organically grown gene pool, run by Dr. Alan Kapuler

## Importing Seeds

### United States

American home gardeners cannot affordably import small quantities of vegetable seeds due to security requirements. Imports now require costly permits and inspections.

### Canada

Canadians find importing much easier than Americans, though their own companies serve their needs well.

### Australia

Australians can easily import vegetable seed for home garden use without permits — except for four restricted species: corn, beans, peas, and sunflowers. For all other vegetables, the seed must be in packets plainly marked with the correct Latin species name.

### United Kingdom

The UK is blessed with many excellent suppliers — importing is rarely necessary.

## The Bottom Line

When you are growing a significant portion of your family's food, you cannot afford seed uncertainty. Buy from companies that conduct trials, test germination, and serve commercial growers as well as home gardeners. The few extra dollars per packet are the cheapest insurance you can buy for your garden.
