# Cold Climate Greenhouse

updated: 2026-04-18

From: [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-pattern-understanding-in-permaculture]]

## Overview

Cold climate greenhouse design in permaculture focuses on extending growing seasons, protecting tender crops, and integrating animal housing for heat production. These systems are particularly important in humid cool to cold climates and high mountain areas where the growing season averages only 60 days.

## Solar-Heated Greenhouses

The Windstar Foundation's "Biodome" in Old Snowmass, Colorado (7,500 feet ASL) uses solar electricity, heating ponds, and a domed greenhouse to grow tender crops year-round in high, cold, dryland conditions. Frogs and finches help with pest control inside the greenhouse, demonstrating integrated biological pest management within protected structures.

## Chicken-Heated Greenhouses

In Aspen, Colorado, a chicken-heated greenhouse combined with a solar-heated seedling cloche enables early planting in short-season high mountain areas. The chickens provide body heat at night, while solar collection supplements daytime heating. This integration follows the permaculture principle that every element serves multiple functions.

## Heat Sources and Storage

Multiple heat sources can be combined for cold climate greenhouses:
- Animal body heat from poultry, rabbits, or other livestock housed in or adjacent to the greenhouse
- Long compost boxes along the poleward wall, providing slow-release heat
- Compost boxes placed external to, but below, the level of the greenhouse
- Solar hot water or hot air collectors taking advantage of slope and thermosiphon effects
- Dark curtains within the greenhouse acting as long-wave heat absorbers and radiators
- Water bodies for thermal mass (ponds provide useful heat storage for night and winter heating)
- Reflective white walls behind ponds to maximise solar gain

## Carbon Dioxide Supplementation

Carbon dioxide can be supplied to greenhouses by composting or by housing animals in greenhouses where light is more than sufficient. This enhanced CO2 level significantly increases plant photosynthetic rates and growth.

## Design Considerations

Key factors for cold climate greenhouse design:
- Orientation toward the equator for maximum solar gain
- Insulated north (poleward) wall with thermal mass
- Double-glazing or insulated curtain systems for night heat retention
- Integration with animal housing for heat and CO2
- Composting systems for supplementary heat
- Manual or automatic venting systems for temperature regulation
- Slope utilisation for thermosiphon effects in water and air heating
- Reflective surfaces to concentrate light and heat

## Greenhouse Types for Cold Climates

- Attached lean-to greenhouse on the equator-facing side of the house
- Freestanding solar greenhouse with insulated north wall
- Dome-shaped structures (geodesic or Biodome style) for wind resistance
- Pit greenhouses (Walipini-style) using earth insulation
- Seedling cloches for early season plant starts
- Cold frames for hardening off plants

## Zone and Sector Integration

The greenhouse is a Zone 1 structure, visited frequently for planting, harvesting, and management. It should be placed:
- Attached to or near the house for easy access and heat sharing
- On the equator-facing side for maximum solar gain
- Away from cold wind sectors, protected by windbreaks
- Connected to water storage and greywater systems
- Adjacent to animal housing if integrated for heat

## Pit Greenhouses (Walipini)

Pit greenhouses, sometimes called Walipinis after the Aymara word for "place of warmth," are dug into the ground to take advantage of the earth's stable subsurface temperature.
In cold climates, soil temperature below the frost line remains relatively constant year-round, typically around 10-15°C (50-59°F).
By sinking the growing area 1.5-2 metres below grade and using the excavated earth to berm the exposed walls, the structure gains enormous thermal mass.
A transparent roof angled toward the equator captures solar energy during the day, which is absorbed and slowly released by the surrounding earth at night.
Drainage must be carefully planned to prevent waterlogging during rain or snowmelt — gravel-filled French drains along the perimeter are essential.
Condensation can be an issue in pit greenhouses; adequate ventilation and waterproofing of the below-grade walls prevent rot and fungal problems.

## Thermal Curtain Systems

Insulated curtain systems are critical for retaining heat during cold nights.
These curtains, typically made from quilted fabric or reflective Mylar, are deployed across the interior of the glazing at sunset and retracted at sunrise.
The air gap between the curtain and the glazing creates an additional insulating layer.
Mollison notes that dark curtains within the greenhouse can serve a dual purpose: during the day they absorb long-wave radiation as thermal mass, and at night they are drawn across the glazing to reduce radiative heat loss.
Automated curtain systems using temperature sensors and electric motors are available commercially, but simple manual draw-curtain systems work effectively for small-scale permaculture applications.
The cost of installing thermal curtains is typically recouped within one to two growing seasons through reduced heating fuel expenditure and earlier spring harvests.

## Integrated Pest Management in Enclosed Structures

The controlled environment of a cold climate greenhouse creates unique pest management challenges and opportunities.
Mollison cites the Windstar Foundation's use of frogs and finches for biological pest control inside their Biodome.
In enclosed greenhouse spaces, beneficial predator populations can be established more reliably than outdoors because they cannot disperse.
Ladybirds introduced to control aphids, predatory mites for thrips, and parasitic wasps for whitefly can maintain effective pest suppression within the sealed environment.
Companion planting inside the greenhouse provides additional pest deterrence: basil planted near tomatoes repels whitefly, marigolds deter nematodes in the root zone, and nasturtiums attract aphids away from crop plants.
Regular monitoring with yellow sticky traps allows early detection of pest outbreaks before they become established.

## Water Storage and Greywater Integration

Water stored inside the greenhouse serves dual purposes: irrigation supply and thermal mass.
Water has one of the highest specific heat capacities of common materials, absorbing and releasing heat slowly.
Fifty-five-gallon drums painted black and filled with water, positioned along the north wall, absorb solar radiation during the day and radiate heat at night.
These drums can be connected to [[permaculture-designers-manual-greywater-systems]] so that water from household sinks and showers is filtered and routed to the greenhouse, reducing the need for fresh irrigation water.
Mollison emphasises that greywater from composting toilets or animal pens, when properly treated through biological filtration systems, can supply both water and nutrients to greenhouse crops.
The integration of water storage, thermal mass, and nutrient recycling exemplifies the permaculture principle of each element performing multiple functions.

## Climate-Specific Crop Selection

Cold climate greenhouses extend the growing season but crop selection must still account for lower light levels and cooler temperatures during winter months.
Leafy greens such as spinach, kale, chard, and Asian greens perform well in cool greenhouse conditions with minimal supplemental heat.
Root vegetables like radishes, carrots, and turnips can be grown in deep raised beds inside the greenhouse.
Cold-tolerant herbs including parsley, cilantro, and chives provide year-round harvests.
For fruiting crops like tomatoes and peppers, the greenhouse must maintain minimum temperatures above 10°C (50°F); in very cold climates this typically requires supplementary heat from compost systems or animal housing.
Succession planting ensures continuous harvests: as one crop is harvested, seedlings started in a warm propagation area are transplanted into the vacated space.
