# Terfas of Africa and the Orient

The subterranean fungi properly known as terfas or kames, sometimes
also called false truffles, are almost wholly the product of arid
Mohammedan countries. These fungi were well known to the Greeks and
to the Romans of imperial times. In Latin they were designated by the
word *tubera*, but since the term "truffle" is now preempted by the
European group, it is preferable to employ one of the two terms of
Arabic origin, "terfas" or "kames."

## Botanical Classification
Botanically, the terfas constitute a family known as Terfeziaceae.
They are allies of the Tuberaceae (true truffles), but well-marked
differences exist. The botanist makes two genera of the various forms
of kames: *Terfezia* and *Tirmania*. There are probably about fifteen
valid species of *Terfezia* known, of which six to eight at most would
include all of the larger edible forms. The family differs from the
truffle family especially in that the internal veins have no outlet to
the surface, and all plants are provided with a root-like process or
rudimentary stipe to which the [[bloomfield-what-lies-beneath-mycelial-architecture-hyphal-biology-subterranean-fungus]] the surface. In Algeria the terfas vary from one to three
inches or more in diameter and may be obtained in surprising quantity.
