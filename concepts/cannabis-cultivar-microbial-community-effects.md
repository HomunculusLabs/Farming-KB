## Overview

The influence of plant genotype on root-associated microbial communities is
a central question in plant microbiome research. Winston et al. (2014)
provided the first comprehensive investigation of cultivar-specific effects
on the Cannabis microbiome, studying five distinct cultivars in controlled
field conditions. Their findings revealed that Cannabis genotype
significantly shapes the [[rhizosphere-bulk-soil-microbial-comparison|bulk soil]] communities.

## Cultivars Studied

The study examined five Cannabis sativa cultivars representing diverse
chemotypes and genetic backgrounds:

1. **Burmese**: A landrace-derived cultivar with Southeast Asian origins.
2. **BooKoo Kush**: An indica-dominant cultivar selected for high resin
   production and sedative effects.
3. **Sour Diesel**: A sativa-dominant cultivar with a distinctive aromatic
   profile and energetic effects.
4. **White Widow**: A balanced hybrid known for high trichome density.
5. **Maui Wowie**: A tropical sativa from Hawaii with unique terpene and
   cannabinoid composition.

Each cultivar was grown in replicate plots within the same field site,
ensuring comparable soil conditions across genotypes.

## Weighted vs Unweighted Community Differences

A critical finding was that cultivar effects were detected only in weighted
(abundance-based) analyses, not in unweighted (presence/absence) analyses.
Specifically, 71 OTUs showed statistically significant abundance differences
between cultivars using weighted UniFrac distances, while zero OTUs were
significant in unweighted analyses.

This means cultivar genotype does not determine which taxa are present or
absent from the [[two-tier-selection-model-plant-microbiome]], but each creates a
distinctive root environment favoring different proportions of shared taxa.

## Proteobacteria Differences Between Cultivars

The Proteobacteria phylum showed the most pronounced cultivar-specific
abundance variation in the endorhiza (Winston et al. 2014):

- **Pseudomonadales**: Pseudomonas varied significantly between cultivars.
  As a core endorhiza component, [[sphingomonas-wittichii-cannabis-endorhiza-strain-specificity]] was specifically associated
  with Maui Wowie. This species degrades aromatic compounds, and its
  enrichment may relate to Maui Wowie's distinctive metabolite profile.
- **Rhizobiales**: Nitrogen-fixing members varied between cultivars,
  potentially impacting nitrogen availability and assimilation.

## Bacteroidetes Contributions

Bacteroidetes also contributed to cultivar-specific community signatures:

- **Sphingobacteriales**: Present across all cultivars as core endorhiza
  members but showed abundance variation suggesting different root tissue
  chemistry between genotypes.
- **Flavobacteriales**: Associated with organic matter decomposition, these
  showed cultivar-dependent patterns suggesting differential root litter
  quality between genotypes.
