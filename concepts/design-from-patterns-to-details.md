## From Macro to Micro

The principle suggests a specific design methodology:
1. Observe the broadest patterns (climate, landscape, watershed, social
   context)
2. Identify the medium-scale patterns (landform, vegetation communities,
   economic flows)
3. Design specific elements and connections within the framework established
   by larger patterns

This approach ensures that detailed design decisions are informed by and
consistent with the larger systems within which they will function. Designing
details first and then trying to fit them into a larger context often leads to
contradictions and inefficiencies.

## [[observe-and-interact-permaculture-principle]] supports this
approach: careful observation of patterns must precede design intervention.
Without pattern understanding, even well-intentioned designs may create more
problems than they solve.

## Patterns in Time

Patterns are not only spatial but temporal. Seasonal patterns, ecological
succession, cultural cycles, and long-term climate trends all provide
essential context for design. Understanding the temporal pattern of energy
descent — the transition from fossil fuel abundance to renewable energy
constraint — is essential for designing systems that will be appropriate for
future conditions.
