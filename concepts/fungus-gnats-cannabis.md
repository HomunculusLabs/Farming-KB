# Fungus Gnats on Cannabis

Fungus gnats are small flying insects whose larvae (maggots) infest growing media and feed on cannabis roots. They are especially problematic in hydroponic systems with moist growing mediums and can cause significant root damage that invites secondary fungal infections. While adult gnats are primarily a nuisance, their larvae can cause serious harm to the root system, and adult gnats that stick to resinous buds can contaminate the harvest.

## Identification

**Larvae (maggots)**:

- Grow to 4-5 mm long
- Translucent bodies with black heads
- Live in the growing medium near the surface
- Feed on root material and organic matter in the medium
- Microscopic when young; become visible as they grow

**Adult gnats**:

- Gray to black, 2-4 mm long
- Long legs, winged
- Look for them around the base of plants in soil and soilless gardens
- Microscopic fungus gnats are difficult to see with the naked eye
- Adults stick to resinous buds like flypaper and are very difficult to clean from harvested buds
- Often confused with fruit flies but are generally smaller and darker

## Life Cycle

- Adult females lay approximately 200 eggs every week to ten days
- Eggs are deposited in moist growing medium near the surface
- Eggs hatch in 4-6 days depending on temperature
- Larvae feed on root material and organic matter in the medium for 10-14 days
- Larvae pupate in the growing medium for 3-7 days
- Adults emerge and repeat the cycle
- Populations can get out of control quickly, especially in hydroponic systems with very moist growing mediums
- The complete life cycle takes approximately 3-4 weeks at room temperature
- Multiple overlapping generations make control challenging once established
- Warmer temperatures accelerate development and increase reproductive rates

## Preferred Habitat

- Moist, dank environments in rockwool — the primary medium where fungus gnats thrive
- NFT-type hydroponic gardens with very moist growing mediums
- Soil gardens that are overwatered or have poor drainage
- Any medium where algae grows on the surface (algae is a primary food source for larvae)
- Damp organic potting soils rich in decomposing matter
- Trays and saucers that hold standing water beneath containers

## Damage Symptoms

- **Root feeding**: Infests growing medium and roots near the surface, targeting the most vulnerable root zone
- **Root hair loss**: Maggots eat fine root hairs and scar larger roots, reducing the plant's ability to absorb water and nutrients
- **Vigor loss**: Plants lose vigor and foliage pales as root function declines
- **Secondary infection**: Root wounds invite wilt fungi like [Fusarium or [Pythium, especially if plants are nutrient-stressed and growing in soggy conditions
- **Bud contamination**: Adult gnats stick to resinous buds, contaminating harvest and making finished product difficult to clean
- **Growth reduction**: Damaged root systems cannot support normal growth rates
- **Nutrient deficiency symptoms**: May appear as leaves yellow despite adequate nutrient availability

Maggots prefer to consume dead or decaying soggy plant material and green algae, but will also feed on healthy root tissue when other food sources are depleted. The combination of direct root damage and secondary pathogen infection makes fungus gnats one of the most damaging below-ground pests in cannabis cultivation.

## Cultural and Physical Control

- **Avoid overwatering**: Do not let growing medium remain soggy — this is the single most important control measure
- **Low ambient humidity**: Keep ambient humidity low to discourage adult activity and egg-laying
- **Cover growing medium**: Cover the surface to prevent algae growth (a food source for larvae); use sand, perlite, or plastic barriers
- **Yellow sticky traps**: Place horizontally 1-2 inches (3-6 cm) over growing medium to catch adults; check and replace regularly
- **Sanitation**: Keep grow room clean and free of decaying plant material that could harbor larvae
- **Proper drainage**: Ensure containers drain freely and no standing water accumulates in trays
- **Sterile medium**: Use fresh, sterile growing medium for each crop

## Organic Controls

- **Neem oil**: Apply as a soil drench to kill larvae in the growing medium; effective but may require multiple applications
- **Insecticidal soap**: Can be applied as a soil drench to the growing medium surface
- **Bacillus thuringiensis var. israelensis (Bt-i)**: The most effective organic control for fungus gnat larvae; available as Vectobac, Gnatrol, and Bactimos
- Note: Foliar sprays are ineffective against larvae living in the medium — treatment must reach the root zone

## Chemical Controls

- **Bt-i products** (Vectobac, Gnatrol, Bactimos): Available as soil drench; only available in large one-gallon (3.8 L) containers; check hydroponic stores for availability
- **Systemic insecticides**: Some systemic products can be taken up by roots and kill feeding larvae, but these are generally not recommended for consumable crops
- **Soil drenches with neem or insecticidal soap**: Effective for mild to moderate infestations when applied repeatedly

## Biological Controls

- **Bacillus thuringiensis var. israelensis (Bt-i)**: The most effective biological control for fungus gnat larvae. Applied as a soil drench to the growing medium. The bacteria produce toxins that specifically kill gnat larvae while being harmless to plants, animals, and humans. Unfortunately, it is available only in large containers.
- **Predatory soil mite**: Hypoaspis (Geolaelaps) miles — hunts larvae in the growing medium; a generalist predator that also attacks other soil-dwelling pests; effective at maintaining low populations when introduced preventatively
- **Nematode**: Steinernema feltiae — parasitizes fungus gnat larvae; applied as a soil drench; effective in moist growing conditions where nematodes can actively seek out prey

## Hydroponic-Specific Management

Fungus gnats are especially problematic in hydroponic systems due to the consistently moist environment:

- In NFT and aeroponic systems, larvae can clog delivery lines and nozzles
- Rockwool cubes provide ideal breeding conditions — cover surfaces immediately after planting
- DWC systems are vulnerable because larvae can enter the nutrient solution
- Check growing medium in hydroponic systems more frequently than soil gardens
- Algae growth on reservoir surfaces and medium surfaces must be controlled aggressively
- Clean and sterilize all hydroponic components between crops
- Ensure that any organic matter in the growing medium is fully decomposed before use

## Impact Assessment

The impact of fungus gnat infestations on cannabis crops includes:

- Direct yield reduction from root damage that limits nutrient uptake
- Indirect yield loss from secondary fungal infections entering through root wounds
- Harvest contamination from adult gnats stuck to resinous buds — very difficult to clean
- Increased crop vulnerability to other stressors when root systems are compromised
- The cost and effort of treatment programs that must be maintained throughout the crop cycle

## Prevention

- Do not overwater — soggy conditions are the primary cause of fungus gnat problems
- Use sterile growing medium for each crop to avoid introducing eggs or larvae
- Cover exposed medium surfaces with sand, perlite, or plastic to prevent algae growth
- Maintain proper drainage in all containers and systems
- Monitor with yellow sticky traps to catch infestations early
- Inspect new soil and growing media before use for signs of gnat activity
- Clean and disinfect containers, trays, and tools between crops
- Avoid leaving standing water in trays or saucers beneath containers
- Quarantine new plants and inspect growing media for signs of larvae before introducing to the garden
