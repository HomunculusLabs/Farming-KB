## Plants, Drugs, Ancient Pharmacology, and Astrological Medicine

## Plants as God"s Offspring

In [[allegro-dead-sea-scrolls]] contain a zodiac chart assigning physical and
spiritual characteristics to each birth sign, dividing human constitution
into "light" and "darkness" portions. There are nine such portions in
total, related to the months of gestation, each governing different
aspects of temperament and health. This was a practical medical
instrument, not a speculative curiosity.

The designations "Children of Light" and "Children of Darkness" must
therefore be understood not as moral categories but as constitutional and
medical ones. A person"s "light" or "darkness" quotient was determined
by planetary influences during gestation and birth, dictating physical
vulnerabilities, mental tendencies, and spiritual predispositions.

The "fruits of the spirit of Truth" — healing, peace, longevity,
fruitfulness — describe physiological and spiritual balance. The "ways
of the spirit of Falsehood" — greed, wickedness, lies, haughtiness,
pride — are not abstract moral failings but the faults of intemperance
and arrogance, excesses of a constitution tilted toward the "darkness"
portions. As Allegro concludes: "Medicine was as much a part of righting
this imbalance of "moral' character as religion; the two were, in fact,
inseparable." To heal the body was to restore spiritual equilibrium; the
physician and the priest were functionally identical.

## Demon = Drug Identity

The [[allegro-linguistic-evidence]] for this identity is striking. The Greek *daimōn*
derives from Sumerian *DA...U_NA*, "having power over fertility" —
describing both a spiritual being and a potent plant substance. Hebrew
*dawah* ("be sick") and Arabic "medicine" derive from the same root,
encoding the recognition that the agent of sickness and the agent of cure
are one. Allegro states it plainly: "The demon of health and sickness
and the drug are radically one and the same."

The Sumerian *A-ZU* / *I-ZU*, "water-oil-expert," designated
simultaneously the physician, the prophet, and the seer. The very name
"Essene" may derive from this same root, identifying the sect by its
primary vocation as healers of sacred substances. Even divine names
encode pharmacological meaning: Jesus/Joshua represents *JA_U_ShUSh* —
"semen which saves, restores, heals"; Dionysus represents
*IA-U-NU-ShUSh* — "semen, seed that saves." The god is the drug; the
drug is the god. "The arts of healing and religion were inseparable" —
to study ancient pharmacology is necessarily to study its theology, its
demonology, and its cosmology as one unified system of knowledge.
