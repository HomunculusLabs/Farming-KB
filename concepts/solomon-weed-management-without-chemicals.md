## Hoeing Techniques for Different Soil Conditions

Solomon describes two primary hoeing techniques depending on soil
conditions:

**Weeding soft ground:** Pull the hoe toward you, half an inch (1.25
centimeters) below the surface. This cuts off weeds as though with a
knife. Most weeds will die within hours of being cut. The odd one that
does not die because it was not completely separated from its root system
will almost surely die when you cut it again a week later.

**Weeding compacted soil (including paths):** Hold the hoe entirely
differently. Keep the handle slightly below horizontal, grip about
halfway down the handle, and use this hand as a pivot while the other
hand pulls the sharp blade toward you. Because it is sharp, it cuts off
small weeds efficiently. This action is essentially scraping the surface.

## The Sharp Hoe: Essential Tool

A sharp hoe is the single most important tool for chemical-free weed
management. When you bring a hoe home from the store, it is never sharp.
Most gardeners never properly sharpen their tools after buying them. In
Solomon's gardening classes, the tool-sharpening session is consistently
rated the second-most-popular activity, after the hands-on bed
preparation demonstration.

The difference between a sharp and dull hoe is dramatic. Try chopping
weeds with a new hoe straight from the store and after two minutes you
will be dripping with sweat, tired out for the rest of the day. A
properly sharpened hoe makes the same task effortless.

## Preventing Seed Set

The most important long-term strategy is preventing weeds from setting
seed. A new garden is usually full of weed seeds. When you turn the soil
over in late summer, a lot of those weeds sprout in autumn; the next
spring, a lot more of them sprout. But before they have a chance to get
large, you dig again and kill them off wholesale.
