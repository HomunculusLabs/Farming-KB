# Reversed-Phase HPLC and TLC Confirmation for Psilocybin/Psilocin

Bigwood and Beug employed a combined analytical approach using reversed-
phase high performance liquid chromatography as the primary quantification
method, with thin-layer chromatography as a qualitative confirmatory

## Calibration Standards and Traceability

Calibration of the HPLC system was performed against standards obtained
from the National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA). The use of NIDA-certified
reference standards provides analytical traceability to a nationally
recognized authority and ensures that the reported concentrations are
accurate and comparable to results from other laboratories using the same
standards. This is particularly important in forensic and regulatory
contexts where the absolute concentration values carry legal or clinical
significance.

The calibration relationship between concentration and peak area was found
to be linear across the working range of 0.2 to 3 micrograms of total
psilocybin or psilocin per injection, with approximately 10 percent
repeatability. This 10 percent repeatability represents the coefficient of
variation for replicate measurements and defines the precision limit of the
analytical method. Any observed differences in alkaloid levels between
flushes exceeding approximately 10 percent could therefore be considered
meaningful rather than methodological noise.

## Detection Limits

The detection limit of the HPLC method was approximately 0.01 micrograms
of psilocybin or psilocin per injection. This sensitivity was sufficient
to detect the low psilocin levels present in first and second flush
samples, where psilocin was often absent or present at only trace levels.
The ability to reliably detect and quantify psilocin at these low
concentrations was critical for documenting the flush-dependent
[[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]] that constitutes the central finding of the study.

## Thin-Layer Chromatography Confirmation

The HPLC results were qualitatively confirmed using thin-layer
chromatography with a butanol-acetic acid-water solvent system in the
ratio 12:3:5. TLC provides a rapid, low-cost confirmation that the peaks
identified by HPLC correspond to the expected compounds based on their
relative mobility values in a separate chromatographic system. Because TLC
and HPLC separate compounds by different mechanisms, agreement between the
two methods provides strong evidence for compound identity.

The butanol-acetic acid-water solvent system is a well-established
chromatographic system for separating indole alkaloids. The organic phase
of butanol carries the less polar compounds further up the plate, while
the aqueous acetic acid component modulates the overall solvent polarity.
