## Graves's Literary Position

Primarily a poet, Graves was also a prolific writer of essays and
historical novels, the most famous being I, Claudius (1934). Educated
at Charterhouse and Oxford, and a veteran of the First World War, he
had a lifelong passion for the ancient world. He spent most of his
adult days in self-imposed exile amongst the olive groves of Deia in
Mallorca, where he adhered to an eccentric personal mythology. For
Graves, poetic inspiration was a gift from the Muse, a once ubiquitous,
matriarchal moon goddess long abandoned by patriarchy because of its
deification of reason.
