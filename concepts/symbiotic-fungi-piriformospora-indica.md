# Piriformospora indica - A Cultivable Root Endophyte for Agriculture

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: Symbiotic Fungi (Varma and Kharkwal, eds.), Chapter 1: Symbiosis - The Art of Living

## Introduction

Piriformospora indica (Hymenomycetes, Basidiomycota, family Sebacinaceae) is the only cultivable endophyte that colonizes roots. Unlike [[dighton-fungal-biomass-production-aquatic-ecosystems]]. Due to its ease of culture, P. indica provides a model organism for the study of beneficial plant-microbe interactions and a new tool for improving plant production systems.

See also: [[fungal-roles-in-phosphorus-solubilization]]

### Bioprotector (Disease Resistance)

P. indica has been reported to induce resistance to fungal diseases in the monocotyledonous plant barley, along with tolerance to salt stress. The beneficial effect on defense status is detected in distal leaves, demonstrating a systemic induction of resistance by a root-endophytic fungus.

The systemically altered "defense readiness" is associated with:
- An elevated antioxidative capacity due to activation of the glutathione-ascorbate cycle
- An overall increase in grain yield
- Local and systemic resistance to fungal diseases and abiotic stress

Since P. indica can be easily propagated in the absence of a host plant, the fungus could be exploited to increase disease resistance as well as yield in crop plants (Waller et al. 2005).

See also: mycorrhizal-pathogen-resistance

### Stress Tolerance

Recent experiments have amply demonstrated that P. indica provides resistance against:
- **Heavy metal contamination**: The fungus provides resistance against heavy-metal contamination in the soil
- **Salt stress**: Tolerance to salinity has been demonstrated in barley
- **Chemical stress**: Protection against various chemical stresses
- **Thermal and physical stress**: General stress tolerance enhancement
