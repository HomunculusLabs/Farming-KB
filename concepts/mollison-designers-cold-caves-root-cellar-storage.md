# Cold Caves and Root Cellar Storage

Bill Mollison treats earth-sheltered storage structures -- caves, cellars, and
underground chambers -- as central to [[pf-tek-low-humidity-symptoms-and-remediation]] and stable warmth. Livestock shelters
built into earth banks across northern China and Mongolia similarly used
thermal mass to reduce winter feed requirements.

**Cold caves** store root crops, seeds, and perishables at cool, steady
temperatures. Root cellars in temperate regions maintain 1-5 degrees C year-
round with near-total [[comparison-passive-solar-vs-earth-sheltered]] gain

  from poor drainage rather than structural problems)
- Ventilation shafts with adjustable openings to control moisture and air

- Thermal mass walls (stone, earth, concrete) that absorb daytime heat and
  release it at night

## Global Examples

**China:** The yaodong (cave dwellings) of the Loess Plateau house millions of
people. Excavated into thick loess deposits, they maintain stable indoor
temperatures year-round. Some have been continuously inhabited for centuries.

**Iran:** The karez-linked underground villages of central Iran use
earth-sheltered construction combined with qanat water channels. Ice houses
(ychakal) -- domed structures above deep subterranean chambers -- stored ice
harvested in winter for summer use, a technique dating back millennia.

**Turkey:** Cappadocian cave cities carved into volcanic tuff provided
complete settlement systems underground, including storage rooms, animal
shelters, churches, and ventilation shafts spanning multiple levels.

**Australia:** Mollison documents Aboriginal use of natural caves and rock
shelters for food storage, as well as colonial-era root cellars and cool
rooms built into hillsides. He advocates reviving these techniques for
modern Australian homesteads, particularly in arid and semi-arid zones where

- Provide two ventilation pipes: one high (warm air exhaust) and one low
  (cool air intake), both with critter guards
- Maintain humidity at 85-95% for root crops by dampening the earthen floor
- Separate ethylene-producing crops (apples) from ethylene-sensitive crops

- Size the storage volume to your household's annual production -- Mollison
