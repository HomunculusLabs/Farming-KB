## Sugar, Spice, and the Roots of Mercantilism

The breakup of Christian medieval Europe occurred as a result of an epidemic
obsession with the new, the exotic, and the delightful. In short, it was driven
by consciousness-expanding substances. Drugs such as coffee, wormwood, and
opium, dyes, silks, rare woods, gems, and even human beings were brought back to
Europe and displayed almost like the plunder of a looted extraterrestrial
civilization. The pursuit of plants and the mental stimulation they induce was
propelling a portion of the human family into experimentation with new social
forms.

## The Rise of Sugar

Of the many new commodities that made their way into Europe during the breakup
of the medieval stasis, one in particular emerged as the new spice or drug of
choice. This was cane sugar. Sugar had been known for centuries as a rare
medicinal substance. The tropical conditions needed for sugarcane cultivation
ensured that sugar would be a rare and imported commodity. Sugar reached England
around 1319 and was popular in Sweden by 1390.

The Persian king Khusraw I dispatched envoys to India to investigate rumors of
exotic drugs. Among those brought to Jundi-Shapur was sukkar, our sugar, unknown
to Herodotus and Ktesias, but known to Nearchus and Onesicritus as "reed honey."
The juice of the sugar cane was purified and made into sugar in India about A.D.
300. At that time and for long afterwards sugar was used only to sweeten
otherwise bitter medicines.
