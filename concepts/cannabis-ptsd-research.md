## Cannabis and PTSD Research

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) is a mental health condition triggered by experiencing or witnessing a traumatic event. Cannabis has shown significant promise in managing PTSD symptoms, particularly nightmares, sleep disturbance, anxiety, and hyperarousal, through its effects on the [[serotonin]] reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) and other antidepressants
- Anti-anxiety medications (short-term)
- Prazosin for nightmares (off-label)

### Prevalence
- Approximately 7-8% of the population will experience PTSD at some point
- Much higher rates in military veterans (11-20%), first responders, and survivors of violence
- Women are twice as likely as men to develop PTSD

### The Endocannabinoid System and Trauma

Research has found that people with PTSD have altered endocannabinoid system function:
- Lower levels of anandamide (the body's natural THC-like compound) in cerebrospinal fluid
- CB1 receptor downregulation in areas of the brain involved in fear and memory
- This suggests that PTSD may involve an endocannabinoid deficiency, and that supplementing with plant cannabinoids could help restore normal function

## Strain-Specific Considerations

PTSD involves multiple symptom clusters, and different strains may help with different aspects:

- **For nightmares and sleep:** Indica-dominant strains with sedating effects (high [[blesching-cannabis-emotional-processing-expansive-constricting-emotions]]:** Balanced THC:CBD ratios; CBD modulates THC's psychoactive effects
- **Avoid:** High-THC sativas may increase anxiety and paranoia in some PTSD patients

### Dosing Considerations
- Low to moderate doses are generally more effective than high doses for PTSD
- Very high doses of THC may increase anxiety and trigger panic responses in trauma survivors
- CBD may be particularly valuable as a non-intoxicating option for daytime use
- Tolerance develops; regular users may need dose adjustment

## Integration with Therapy

Cannabis is not a replacement for trauma-focused therapy but may serve as an adjunct:
- Reducing symptom severity may make patients more able to engage in therapy
- Improved sleep may enhance cognitive function and emotional resilience
- Some patients report that cannabis helps them process traumatic memories during therapy sessions
- Caution: Some therapists believe cannabis may interfere with exposure therapy (if it reduces emotional engagement with traumatic memories during treatment)
