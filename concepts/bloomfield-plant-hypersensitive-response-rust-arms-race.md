## How Cell Death Starves the Fungus

The hypersensitive response is a deliberate cell death strategy
programmed into the plant's genome. By killing its own cells
around the infection site, the plant deprives the fungus of
living cytoplasm, which is its sole source of nutrients. Rust
fungi feed through haustoria, specialized absorptive organs
that penetrate plant [[fungal-mycelium-hyphae-and-the-feeding-phase]] can spread no further.

This strategy is remarkably effective because it is localized.
Only a handful of cells are sacrificed, leaving the rest of the
leaf functional and photosynthetically active. The cost to the
plant is minimal compared to the alternative of allowing the
fungus to spread throughout its tissues, which would result in
the death of the entire leaf and potentially the whole plant.
From an evolutionary perspective, the hypersensitive response
is a textbook example of how organisms can turn self-destruction
into a survival strategy.

## The Arms Race Dynamic

Evolution favors the emergence of new races of rusts that can
overcome the defense mechanisms of resistant plants. A mutation
in the fungal avirulence gene may allow the pathogen to go
undetected by the plant's surveillance system, rendering the
hypersensitive response ineffective. In turn, the plant alters
its response to the fungus through natural selection or via
artificial selection in the greenhouse, developing new
resistance genes that can detect the modified pathogen.
