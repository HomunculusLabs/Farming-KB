## Cannabis Life Cycle and Photoperiod Control

## Overview

Cannabis is an annual plant with a life cycle divided into distinct growth phases, each triggered by environmental cues — primarily photoperiod (day length). Understanding and manipulating these phases is the foundation of [[marijuana-horticulture-cervantes-cannabis-breeding-and-genetics]] provides comprehensive guidance on managing each stage, from seed germination through harvest.

## Cannabis Strains and Classification

While legally all cannabis is classified as Cannabis sativa, botanists recognize several varieties with distinct growth characteristics:

### Cannabis Sativa

Originating primarily in Asia, the Americas, and Africa, sativa strains share these general traits:

- **Growth habit**: Tall, leggy stature with long internodal spacing
- **Root system**: Large and sprawling
- **Leaves**: Large, narrow-bladed with long "fingers"
- **Flowering time**: Several weeks to months longer than indica strains
- **[[cannabis-glandular-trichome-types-resin-production-clarke]]**: Heavily resin-laden plants tend to be more pest and fungus resistant

### Cannabis Ruderalis and Afghanica

- **Ruderalis**: Small, auto-flowering varieties from Central/Eastern Europe; low THC but useful in breeding for auto-flower genetics
- **Afghanica**: Compact, resinous plants from the Hindu Kush mountains; foundation of many modern indica-dominant hybrids

## The Growth Cycle

### Germination and Seedling Stage

Seeds germinate when provided with moisture, warmth (70-80°F / 21-27°C), and darkness. The taproot emerges within 24-72 hours. Once the seedling breaks the surface, it needs immediate light. The seedling stage lasts 2-3 weeks, during which the plant develops its first true leaves and establishes initial root structure.

### Vegetative Growth

The [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-training-techniques]]**: Topping, bending, super-cropping, and screen-of-green (SCROG) to control height and shape
- **Transplanting**: Gradual upsizing of containers as root systems develop

### Pre-Flowering

Before the full [[cervantes-high-pressure-sodium-lighting]] (red/orange spectrum) promotes flower development
- **Duration**: 7-14 weeks depending on strain — indicas typically 7-9 weeks, sativas 10-14+ weeks

### Sinsemilla Production

Sinsemilla (Spanish for "without seed") refers to unpollinated female flowers. When females are kept isolated from male pollen, they continue producing resin and floral tissue rather than diverting energy to seed production. This results in:

- Significantly higher THC content in the floral material
- Denser, more potent flower clusters
- No seeds in the final product
- Extended flowering period as the plant continues seeking pollination

## Photoperiod Control

### The Science

Cannabis measures day length using phytochrome pigments (Pr and Pfr) in the leaves. Pr (inactive form) converts to Pfr (active form) under red light. In darkness, Pfr slowly reverts to Pr. When the dark period is long enough (12+ hours), sufficient Pfr reverts to trigger the flowering hormone (florigen) production.

### Critical Factors

- **Dark period integrity**: Any light interruption during the 12-hour dark period can delay or prevent flowering. Even brief light leaks from door cracks, equipment LEDs, or streetlights can cause problems.
- **Light intensity during day**: Higher intensity during the light period promotes faster, more robust flowering.
- **Strain variation**: Different strains have different critical photoperiods; some indica strains flower under 13-14 hours of darkness.
- **Temperature during dark**: Cooler night temperatures (65-75°F / 18-24°C) promote terpene production and resin development.

### Harvest Timing

Cervantes identifies several indicators of harvest readiness:

- **Pistil coloration**: 50-80% of pistils have darkened and curled inward
- **Trichome appearance**: Majority of glandular trichomes have turned from clear to cloudy/milky; some amber trichomes indicate peak THC degradation to CBN (more sedative effect)
- **Leaf yellowing**: Lower fan leaves naturally yellow and drop as the plant mobilizes stored nutrients
- **Bud density**: Flowers feel firm and heavy when gently squeezed
