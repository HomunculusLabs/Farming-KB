# The Bible as Cryptogram — Encoding Methods

Allegro's most radical claim is that the entire Bible — both Old and New Testaments — is a deliberate cryptogram, a text written in code to preserve the mushroom cult's teachings while concealing them from the uninitiated. This page describes the encoding methods he identifies and the evidence from the Dead Sea Scrolls.

## The Nature of the Cryptogram

The biblical cryptogram is not a simple substitution cipher. It is a multilayered linguistic technique that operates simultaneously in Sumerian, Aramaic, Hebrew, and Greek. A single word or phrase can encode multiple mushroom references across different languages. The surface narrative is comprehensible (and often edifying) in the target language; the hidden meaning requires knowledge of Sumerian and the cryptographic conventions.

Allegro identifies several specific encoding methods:

### 1. Transliteration with False Etymology
A Sumerian mushroom name is transliterated into Semitic syllables, then given a plausible-sounding Hebrew or Aramaic etymology. Example: the Sumerian mushroom name I-A-U becomes Hebrew Yahweh, explained as derived from the verb hayah ("to be") — "I AM THAT I AM" (Exod 3:14). The real origin (the god of fecundity) is hidden.

### 2. Atbash and Other Letter Substitution
The biblical writers sometimes used letter-substitution ciphers. Atbash (A→Z, B→Y, etc.) is attested in Jeremiah 25:26 and 51:41, where "Sheshach" is Atbash for "Babel." Allegro argues that similar techniques were used more extensively than has been recognized.

### 3. Bilingual Word-Play
The New Testament writers placed Aramaic words in a Greek text, giving Greek translations that encode mushroom names. "Talitha cumi" (Mark 5:41) appears to mean "little girl, arise" in Aramaic but encodes a Sumerian mushroom name. The Greek translation "he said to her, 'Girl, I say to you, arise'" is constructed to sound natural while preserving the cryptogram.

### 4. Paronomasia (Sound-Play)
Words that sound similar but have different meanings are linked to create narrative connections. The story of Peter's denial — the cock crowing three times — links the Greek alektor ("cock") with a Semitic mushroom name. The entire narrative is generated by the sound-play, not the other way around.

### 5. Constructed Etymologies
The biblical writers frequently provide folk etymologies for names: "She called his name Moses, for she said, 'Because I drew him out of the water'" (Exod 2:10). Allegro shows that these etymologies are almost always false — the real origins are Sumerian mushroom names — and that the false etymologies are part of the cryptographic technique.

## Dead Sea Scrolls Evidence

The Dead Sea Scrolls, discovered at Qumran between 1947 and 1956, provide crucial evidence for the cryptographic tradition. Allegro, who was a member of the original editorial team, argues that:

1. **Unusual spellings** in the Scrolls are not errors but deliberate cryptographic devices
2. **Repeated phrases** that seem redundant encode additional mushroom names
3. **Peculiar word choices** make sense only when read as Sumerian transliterations
4. **The sectarian vocabulary** of the Scrolls (terms like "the Teacher of Righteousness," "the Wicked Priest," "the Liar") are mushroom cryptograms

The Habakkuk Commentary (1QpHab), for example, interprets the prophet's words in ways that seem arbitrary until one recognizes that both the biblical text and the commentary encode mushroom names. The "Righteous Teacher" is the mushroom itself; the "Wicked Priest" is the mushroom's enemy (those who would destroy the cult).

## The Cryptographic Community

Allegro envisions a network of sectarian groups — Essenes, Therapeutae, early Christians, Gnostics — who shared the cryptographic tradition. Each group had its own "key" to the cryptogram, adapted to its particular language and cultural context. The diversity of the New Testament writings reflects the diversity of these groups, each encoding the same mushroom names in different ways.

The breaking of the tradition came when Christianity was adopted as the official religion of the Roman Empire. The literal interpretations that had always been the surface layer became the only officially sanctioned readings. The cryptographic key was lost — or rather, suppressed — and the mushroom origins of Christianity were forgotten.

## The Scope of the Cryptogram

Allegro does not claim that every word in the Bible is a mushroom reference. He argues that the cryptogram is concentrated in specific passages: creation narratives, patriarchal stories, prophetic oracles, psalms of lamentation, and particularly the New Testament passion narrative. But these are precisely the passages that have been most influential in shaping Western religious thought.
