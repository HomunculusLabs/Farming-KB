# Brassinosteroid Suppression of Incomplete Fruit Body Formation

The 1990 Gartz study reported that 22S,23S-homobrassinolide
almost completely suppressed the formation of incomplete fruit
bodies [[annulus-loss-morphological-effect-brassinosteroid-treatment]]. This suggests that
brassinosteroids help channel the fungus's reproductive energy more
efficiently into mature, harvestable fruit bodies.

The suppression effect was observed alongside other positive outcomes:
accelerated time to first flush, increased mushroom count per flush,
and higher total dry mass yield. Together, these results suggest that
brassinosteroids promote a more coordinated and efficient fruiting
program overall.

## Proposed Mechanism — Developmental Coordination

While the Gartz study did not investigate the molecular mechanism
underlying abort suppression, several hypotheses can be drawn from
known brassinosteroid biology in other organisms. Brassinosteroids
are known to promote cell elongation and cell division in plants
through receptor kinase signaling cascades that ultimately influence
gene expression patterns related to growth and development.

In the fungal context, brassinosteroids may enhance the
coordination between [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]].

The combination of faster colonization, earlier fruiting, more
mushrooms per flush, fewer aborts, and higher total dry mass
suggests a comprehensive enhancement of the fungal life cycle by
brassinosteroids, rather than an effect limited to a single
developmental stage.
