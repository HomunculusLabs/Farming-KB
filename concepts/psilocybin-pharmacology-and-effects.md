# Pharmacology and Acute Effects of [[emcdda-psilocybin-non-addictive-profile-tolerance-dynamics]] Mushrooms

## Overview

Psilocybin-containing mushrooms produce their effects primarily through
psilocybin and its active metabolite psilocin, which act on the serotonin
system. The effects include marked perceptual distortions, altered thought
processes, and changes in mood and self-awareness. Understanding the
pharmacology, dosing, and effects is essential for both [[hofmann-lsd-clinical-research-early-studies]]
and harm reduction.

## Chemical Classification

[[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] belong to the **indolealkylamine (tryptamine) class**
of hallucinogens, the same broad chemical group as LSD. They are structurally
similar to the neurotransmitter serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT):

- **Psilocybin**: 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-PO-DMT) — the
  phosphate ester, more stable, water-soluble prodrug
- **Psilocin**: 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-HO-DMT) — the active
  metabolite produced by dephosphorylation in the body
- **Baeocystin**: 4-PO-N-methyltryptamine — present but less active
- **Norbaeocystin**: 4-OH-N-methyltryptamine — present but less active

## Mechanism of Action

Psilocin acts primarily as a **5-HT2A post-synaptic receptor agonist or partial
agonist** in the cerebral cortex. Activation of these receptors disrupts default
mode network activity, alters thalamocortical processing, and modifies sensory
perception and temporal experience. This mechanism overlaps with other classical
psychedelics (LSD, DMT, mescaline), which all share 5-HT2A agonism.

## Terminology: [[hallucinogenic-mushroom-european-prevalence-polydrug-clustering-barriers-emcdda-2006]] vs. Psychedelic

- **"Hallucinogen"** — scientific term, though misleading (rarely causes true
  hallucinations; effects are better described as perceptual distortions)
- **"Psychedelic"** — "mind-manifesting," preferred by users
- **"Psychotomimetic"** — older clinical term (psychosis-mimicking)
- **"Psychodysleptic"** — used in some European regulatory contexts

These terms are used interchangeably in practice.

## Dose, Potency, and Onset

### Potency Variation

Potency varies significantly between species, varieties, growing conditions,
[[psilocybin-degradation-volatility-and-specimen-age]]. The primary species used recreationally include:

| Species | Common Name | Relative Potency |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| *[[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]* | Golden teacher, Mexican | Low–moderate |
| *[[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]]* | Liberty cap | Moderate–high |
| *[[growing-gourmet-caramel-capped-psilocybe-cyanescens-cultivation]]* | Wavy cap | High |
| *[[psilocybe-azurescens]]* | Flying saucer | Very high |
| *[[psilocybe-bohemica]]* | — | Moderate–high |

### Dose Ranges

A typical recreational dose of dried *P. cubensis* is 1–2.5 grams for a light
experience and 2.5–5 grams for a strong experience. For more potent species
like *P. semilanceata*, the equivalent dose may be 0.5–1.5 grams dried.

### Route of Administration

Mushrooms are typically:
- **Eaten fresh or dried** — chopped and swallowed, sometimes with food
- **Brewed as tea** — steeping in hot water, which may accelerate onset
- Occasionally mixed with juice or other beverages to mask the bitter taste

### Onset and Duration

| Phase | Timeframe |
|-------|-----------|
| Onset | 20–60 minutes after ingestion |
| Peak effects | 1.5–3 hours |
| Duration | 4–6 hours total |
| After-effects | Up to 12 hours (residual perceptual changes) |

The onset is faster and duration shorter when consumed as tea on an empty
stomach.
