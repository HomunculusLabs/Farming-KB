# Psilocybin Pharmacology and Dose-Response Effects

Psilocybin (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is a naturally occurring indole alkaloid that acts primarily as a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist. Its pharmacological profile, dose-dependent effects, and risk characteristics are documented across clinical research, ethnobotanical literature, and epidemiological surveillance data compiled by the EMCDDA and other agencies.

## Pharmacokinetics

### Absorption and Metabolism

Psilocybin is orally active and is rapidly absorbed after ingestion. It undergoes first-pass metabolism in the liver, where it is dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase to form the active metabolite psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine). Psilocin is the primary compound responsible for the psychoactive effects, although psilocybin itself may have some direct pharmacological activity.

Onset of effects typically occurs within 20-60 minutes of oral ingestion, depending on the individual, the presence of food in the stomach, and the preparation method. Effects peak at approximately 2-3 hours and gradually subside over 4-6 hours, with a total experience duration of roughly 6 hours for moderate doses.

### Excretion

Psilocin is metabolized primarily through oxidative deamination by monoamine oxidase (MAO). Metabolites are excreted renally. The half-life of psilocin is approximately 2-3 hours. Approximately 80-90% of the dose is excreted within 24 hours.

### Detection

Detection of psilocybin and psilocin in biological matrices is technically challenging due to their low typical doses and rapid metabolism. Urine testing is possible for up to 1-3 days after use using GC-MS or LC-MS methods, but routine drug screening panels do not typically include hallucinogens. Hair analysis has been attempted but is not widely validated.

## Mechanism of Action

### Serotonin Receptor Agonism

Psilocin's primary mechanism is agonism at the 5-HT2A receptor, a postsynaptic G-protein-coupled receptor found throughout the cerebral cortex. This receptor is thought to mediate the primary hallucinogenic effects. Additional activity at 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, and 5-HT2B receptors contributes to the overall profile of effects.

The binding of psilocin to 5-HT2A receptors on cortical pyramidal neurons alters thalamocortical gating, leading to increased sensory information flow from the thalamus to the cortex and reduced default mode network activity. This neurobiological mechanism is shared with other classical hallucinogens including LSD, DMT, and mescaline.

### Neuroimaging Findings

Modern neuroimaging studies using fMRI have demonstrated that psilocybin produces:
- Decreased blood flow and BOLD signal in the default mode network (DMN), a network associated with self-referential thought and the ego.
- Increased connectivity between brain regions that are normally functionally segregated.
- Disrupted functional connectivity in the resting-state networks.

These findings provide a neurobiological correlate for the experiences of ego dissolution, synesthesia, and altered perception of self and reality reported by users.

## Dose-Response Effects

### Low Doses (0.5-1.5 g dried P. cubensis equivalent)

At sub-threshold to low doses, effects are primarily perceptual and somatic:
- Mild visual enhancement (colors appear more vivid)
- Slight euphoria or mood elevation
- Physical lightness or tingling sensations
- Enhanced appreciation of music and art
- No significant cognitive impairment
- Nausea may occur but is usually mild

This range is sometimes described as a "museum dose" — sufficient for enhanced aesthetic appreciation without significant alteration of thought patterns.

### Moderate Doses (1.5-3.5 g dried P. cubensis equivalent)

Moderate doses produce more pronounced effects:
- Significant visual alterations (geometric patterns, color shifting, trailing)
- Altered perception of time (time dilation or compression)
- Enhanced introspection and emotional processing
- Possible synesthesia
- Altered thought patterns with potential for insight
- Mild to moderate anxiety in some users
- Nausea more common, especially in the first 30-60 minutes

This range is commonly associated with recreational and therapeutic use. Paul Stamets characterized 2g as producing "enhanced appreciation of surroundings" and 3g as producing "visual waves."

### High Doses (3.5-5 g dried P. cubensis equivalent)

High doses produce intense, potentially overwhelming effects:
- Strong visual hallucinations (fully formed imagery, not just patterns)
- Profound alterations in sense of self (ego dissolution)
- Mystical or spiritual experiences
- Intense emotional responses (catharsis, grief, joy)
- Significant cognitive disruption
- Possible temporary inability to distinguish self from environment
- Increased risk of anxiety, paranoia, or panic

Stamets described 5g as an "overwhelming experience" lasting approximately 6 hours. At this level, set (mindset) and setting (environment) become critical determinants of whether the experience is beneficial or distressing.

### Very High Doses (5-7+ g dried P. cubensis equivalent)

Very high doses are generally not recommended:
- Complete ego dissolution with potential for frightening experiences
- Inability to communicate or interact normally
- Possible "bad trips" requiring reassurance and supportive care
- Physical side effects: vomiting, diarrhea, tremor, tachycardia
- Stamets explicitly stated 7g+ is not recommended
- Risk of accidental injury due to impaired judgment

## Factors Modifying the Experience

### Set and Setting

The concepts of "set" (the user's mindset, expectations, mood, and personality) and "setting" (the physical and social environment) were articulated by Timothy Leary and remain the most important non-pharmacological determinants of the quality of the experience. A supportive, familiar, and safe environment with a trusted companion ("trip sitter") significantly reduces the risk of adverse psychological reactions.

### Species and Strain Variation

Different species and strains of psilocybin mushrooms contain varying amounts of active compounds. P. semilanceata and P. azurescens are significantly more potent than P. cubensis. Within P. cubensis, different strains show some variation but are generally in the 0.3-0.6% psilocybin/psilocin range by dry weight.

### Individual Variation

Individual sensitivity varies considerably based on body weight, metabolism, prior experience, tolerance, and genetic factors. Regular use produces rapid tolerance — the same dose will produce markedly diminished effects if taken within 3-7 days of a previous dose. Cross-tolerance with LSD is well documented.

### Preparation and Ingestion Method

Brewing mushrooms into tea accelerates onset and may reduce nausea but also shortens the duration. Lemon tek (soaking powdered mushrooms in lemon juice before ingestion) purportedly accelerates conversion of psilocybin to psilocin and speeds onset. Eating fresh vs. dried mushrooms: fresh contain ~90% water, so 10g fresh ≈ 1g dried.

## Acute Adverse Effects

### Physical

- Nausea and vomiting (most common, especially in the first hour)
- Mydriasis (dilated pupils)
- Tachycardia (increased heart rate)
- Elevated blood pressure
- Tremor
- Hyperreflexia
- Increased body temperature (mild)
- Dizziness and ataxia (poor coordination)

### Psychological

- Anxiety and panic ("bad trip")
- Paranoia
- Confusion and disorientation
- Fear of losing one's mind or dying
- Flashbacks (brief re-experiences; rare and usually not problematic)
- Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD; very rare)

### Risk of Harm

The risk of fatal overdose from psilocybin mushrooms is extremely low. The estimated lethal dose in humans is approximately 1,000 times the effective dose — making accidental fatal overdose essentially impossible from natural mushrooms. The primary risks are behavioral: accidental injury due to impaired judgment (falls, drowning, traffic incidents), psychological distress requiring intervention, and in rare cases, triggering of latent psychiatric conditions.

## Therapeutic Potential

Modern clinical research has revisited psilocybin as a therapeutic agent, particularly for:
- Treatment-resistant depression
- End-of-life anxiety in terminal illness
- Substance use disorders (alcohol, nicotine)
- Obsessive-compulsive disorder
- Post-traumatic stress disorder

These studies use carefully controlled doses (typically 10-25 mg of synthesized psilocybin, equivalent to roughly 1-2.5g dried mushrooms) in supervised clinical settings with trained therapists. Results from Phase 2 clinical trials have shown significant and durable effects, leading to Phase 3 trials and potential regulatory approval.
