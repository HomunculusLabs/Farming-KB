# Cannabis Harvesting, Drying, and Curing

Harvesting is the act of reaping the rewards of a cannabis grow and is
without doubt the most anticipated stage for cultivators. [[greg-green]]'s
The Cannabis Grow Bible provides detailed guidance on identifying harvest
readiness, proper harvesting techniques for different plant types, and
the critical post-harvest processes of drying and curing that determine
final product quality.

## Identifying Harvest Readiness

The breeder's guidelines about flowering times provide a baseline, but
experienced cultivators learn to identify mature bud through direct
observation. Several indicators signal that a plant is ready for harvest:
50 to 70 percent of the pistils have changed color, the plant stops
producing crystals and resin, fan leaves and lower leaves turn yellow and
begin dropping off, the smell has reached its peak, and bud mass has not
increased in the past few days.

These indicators do not appear on every strain, so cultivators should
observe their plants closely and learn the specific maturation patterns
of each variety they grow. The timing of harvest also affects the type of
high: harvesting earlier tends to produce a more cerebral effect, while
later harvesting produces a heavier body effect.

## Indica Harvest Procedure

Indica plants, typically 1 to 4 feet tall, should be cut at the base.
The plant is then hung upside down in a cool room with no light and fresh
air. Using a pair of clippers, the grower removes as many fan leaves as
possible, then removes secondary leaves into a separate pile. Finally,
trim is gathered from the bud. Trim refers to the small leaves covered in
resin.

This process yields four different qualities of material. Fan leaves
provide only an okay smoke, middle leaves are somewhat better, and trim
will be very good. The bud itself is the prime material and provides the
best quality high. The plant is left hanging until a branch can break
easily between the fingers, which takes approximately two to three weeks.

## Sativa Harvest Procedure

Sativa harvest is much like Indica but may be considerably more labor
intensive. Outdoor Sativa plants can grow up to 12 feet carrying over 20
ounces of bud. These large plants require a canvas spread or similar
material for transport. The plant is chopped at the base and spread on
the canvas, which is rolled up and tied for transport. Because of the
plant's size and bushiness, branches may need to be cut and hung
separately.

It must be emphasized that light degrades THC quantities. The drying room
does not need to be as light-proof as the grow room during the flowering
phase, but harvested material should never be exposed to direct light.

## Drying Fan Leaves and Trim

Fan leaves, secondary leaves, and trim are cured by leaving them dry on
a flat surface, away from direct light with plenty of fresh air. The
leaves will dry after approximately 3 weeks and become smokable at that
stage. Growers should never attempt to speed up the drying process with
ovens, microwaves, or heat. Allowing material to dry naturally produces a
much better quality smoke. Trim can alternatively be used to make hash.

## Curing with the Canning Method

Once branches are brittle enough to break between the fingers, curing
should begin. The canning method is the most common curing technique and
produces excellent results. Bud is clipped from branches using scissors
and placed into a can or jar with a removable lid. Branches and stems
are discarded as they contain only small quantities of THC.

The can is placed in a dark room or cupboard. Every day the lid is
opened for several hours, approximately 6 hours being optimal, and then
closed again. The bud should be shaken around every couple of days. This
process allows slow oxidation and breakdown of chlorophyll while
preserving cannabinoids and terpenes.

## Curing Timeline and Quality

The canning process should continue for 3 to 4 weeks before sampling the
product. Bud cured for 8 weeks can smoke extremely well. Year-old bud
may lose some potency but remains usable. Fresh bud at approximately 8
weeks of can curing represents the pinnacle point of cured bud. After
that peak, some of the THC cannabinoids change composition and become
different cannabinoid compounds.

If plants have been dried for three weeks while hanging upside down, that
time can be subtracted from the canning timeline. Although bud may be
smokable two weeks after harvest, waiting four weeks or more produces
significantly better results. Patience during the curing process is one
of the most important virtues a cannabis cultivator can develop.

## The Importance of Slow Processing

The overarching principle of post-harvest processing is patience. Rapid
drying through heat or forced air degrades both the flavor profile and
the psychoactive potency of the final product. The slow, natural drying
and curing process allows for the gradual breakdown of chlorophyll and
plant sugars that cause harsh smoke, while preserving and potentially
enhancing the terpene and cannabinoid profile.

Proper harvesting, drying, and curing represent the final critical steps
that determine whether months of careful cultivation produce premium
quality cannabis or an inferior product. The difference between rushed
and patient post-harvest processing can be the difference between a
smooth, flavorful experience and a harsh, unpleasant one.
