# Psilocin Synthesis from 4-Hydroxyindole

Osamu Shirota, Wataru Hakamata, and Yukihiro Goda at Japan's National Institute of
Health Sciences developed a concise, large-scale synthesis of psilocin (1) and
psilocybin (2)—the principal hallucinogenic constituents of "[[shirota-glyoxalylamide-formation-oxalyl-chloride-indole]] in anhydrous
[[diethyl-ether]] at 0°C. After stirring for fifteen minutes, n-hexane is added and
the reaction flask is placed in a freezer overnight. The resulting yellow crystals
of 3-glyoxalyl-4-acetylindole (5) are separated from the solution by filtration.
This intermediate is isolated as pure yellow crystalline material.

## Amidation with Dimethylamine

The glyoxalyl intermediate (5) is dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran and
treated with a 2M dimethylamine solution and pyridine over fifteen minutes in an
ice bath. After stirring for fifteen minutes at room temperature, the reaction
product—3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-acetylindole (6)—is separated by filtration and
washed successively with n-hexane, ethyl acetate, and water. This two-step
acylation-amidation conversion proceeds in over 80% yield, giving the amidated
product as an ivory white crystalline powder.

## Reduction to Psilocin

The key reduction step uses lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) in anhydrous
tetrahydrofuran under an argon atmosphere. A solution of the glyoxalylamide (6) in
THF is added dropwise over two hours to a suspension of LiAlH4 in THF, and the
mixture is then refluxed for two hours. After cooling, careful quenching with
anhydrous sodium sulfate and saturated sodium sulfate solution is performed.

The resulting crystalline material is collected by filtration and washed with
methanol to afford psilocin (1) as white crystals in over 85% yield. The total
yield from 4-hydroxyindole through this four-step sequence is excellent, and no
chromatographic purification is required at any stage.

## Spectroscopic Characterization

Psilocin (1) melts at 169-174°C with decomposition (literature: 173-176°C). UV
spectrum (MeOH) shows absorption maxima at 222.5 (log epsilon 4.55), 268.0
(3.72), 284.5 (3.62), and 294.0 (3.58) nm. IR spectrum (KBr) shows
characteristic bands at 3285, 2959, 2371, 1620, 1588, 1473, 1345, 1258, 1232,
1044, 833, and 722 cm-1.

The 1H NMR (CDCl3, 400 MHz) shows a broad singlet at delta 7.90 (H-1), a
doublet at 7.05 (H-6, J=8.0 Hz), a doublet of doublets at 6.86 (H-2, J=0.8,
8.0 Hz), a doublet of doublets at 6.56 (H-5, J=0.8, 8.0 Hz), multiplets at
2.94 (H2-1') and 2.70 (H2-2'), and a singlet at 2.38 (NMe2). The 13C NMR
(CDCl3, 100 MHz) assigns the quaternary C-4 at delta 152.1, C-7a at 139.0,
and the NMe2 at 45.3. Additional 13C assignments include C-2 at 120.8, C-3a
at 117.5, C-3 at 114.6, C-5 at 106.4, C-7 at 102.4, C-2' at 61.6, and C-1'
at 25.1.

ESI-MS shows the protonated molecular ion at m/z 205.1 [M+H]+, with the
N,N-dimethyl fragment at 160.1 [M-NMe2]+. A sodiated adduct appears at m/z
227.1 [M+Na]+, and the deacetylated fragment at m/z 134.0 [M-Ac+H]+ is the base
peak. High-resolution ESI-MS confirmed the molecular formula C12H17N2O
(calculated 205.1341, found 205.1303).
