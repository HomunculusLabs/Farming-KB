## Light microscopy foundations

## Fluorescent protein tagging

Green fluorescent protein (GFP) and its spectral variants (YFP, CFP,
RFP, mCherry) have revolutionised live-cell imaging of fungi. By fusing
GFP to proteins of interest, researchers can track protein localisation
and dynamics in real time within living hyphae. This approach has been
used to localise cell wall synthesis enzymes, observe Spitzenkorper
components, and monitor nuclear division and migration in growing
hyphae. Time-lapse confocal microscopy of fluorescently tagged proteins
provides spatio-temporal resolution impossible with fixed specimens.

## FRAP: Fluorescence recovery after photobleaching

FRAP is a powerful technique for measuring molecular dynamics within
living fungal cells. A region of interest is bleached with high-intensity
laser light, and recovery of fluorescence is monitored over time. The
rate and extent of recovery reflect molecular mobility, diffusion
coefficients, and binding interactions. In filamentous fungi, FRAP has
been used to study plasma membrane protein dynamics, cytoplasmic
streaming rates, and the mobility of organelles and macromolecular
complexes within hyphal compartments.

## Applications to Woronin body dynamics

FRAP studies of Woronin body dynamics in [[gadd-woronin-bodies-hyphal-septation]] are relatively
immobile under normal conditions but become rapidly mobilised following
septal damage. This mobility transition is essential for their function
as septal-pore plugs. Fluorescent tagging of HEX-1 allowed tracking of
Woronin body biogenesis, movement, and septal-pore engagement in real
time, providing direct evidence for the plugging mechanism.
