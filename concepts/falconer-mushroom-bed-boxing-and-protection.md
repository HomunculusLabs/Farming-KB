# Boxing and Protecting Mushroom Beds

The boxing of mushroom beds is a practice employed to
protect the [[falconer-growing-mushrooms-in-sheds]] cultivation, boxing takes different forms.
After the beds are spawned and cased with soil, a covering
of straw litter or hay prevents sudden drying out of the
surface. About the time the young mushrooms commence to
show themselves, the mulching is removed and replaced
with a covering of shutters raised another board's height
above the bed, or with strong calico or plant-protecting
cloth hung curtain-fashion over the beds.

The objection to mushroom beds on greenhouse benches is
their liability to frequent and marked changes of atmospheric
[[mushroom-growing-temperature-and-moisture]], and to drying out. Boxing or
curtain-covering mitigates these problems.

## Mulching with Straw and Hay

Covering the beds with hay or straw is another protection
method, particularly valuable for maintaining proper
[[falconer-growing-mushrooms-in-sheds]] and outdoor situations, heavier
protection is required during freezing weather:

- Box the beds tightly with well-fitting boards
- Cover the boxing with heavy straw or hay
- Bank earth or litter against the outside walls
- Use mats or carpeting as additional insulation over the
  top of the boxing

The beds should be made deeper in a cool shed than in a
cellar or warm [[mushroom-house-construction-duggar]], so that they may retain
their heat for a long time. This is essential because the
fermenting manure provides the primary heat source, and
without adequate depth and insulation, cold will penetrate
and stagnate the crop.

## Covering Against Condensation Drip

One of the chief troubles with flat-roofed [[nineteenth-century-mushroom-cellars]]
is the drip from the condensed moisture rising from the
beds, and this is more apparent in unheated than in heated
cellars. The wet gathers upon the ceiling and, having no
slope to run off, drips down again.

Oiled paper or calico strung along wise above the upper beds
protects them perfectly; whatever falls upon the
passage-ways upon the floor does no harm. This simple
protection prevents damage to the [[sustaining-mushroom-crop-flushing-harvesting]] from
cold drips that can chill and check the growth of the
mushrooms.

## The Importance of Ventilation with Boxing

While boxing confines heat and moisture, it must not
completely exclude fresh air. The cellars can be ventilated
without interfering with the welfare of the mushrooms when
proper boxing is employed. The lid arrangement allows the
grower to ventilate the general cellar space while keeping
the mushroom bed itself protected from cold draughts that
would harm the crop.
