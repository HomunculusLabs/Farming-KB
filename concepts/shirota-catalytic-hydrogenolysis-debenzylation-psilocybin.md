# Catalytic Hydrogenolysis in Psilocybin Synthesis

## Overview

The final step in the Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) synthesis of psilocybin is catalytic hydrogenolysis — the removal of benzyl protecting groups from the zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl [[psilocybin-synthesis-zwitterionic-phosphate-intermediate-shirota-2003]] into psilocybin as a crystalline product, completing the synthesis without any need for chromatographic purification such as the anion-exchange resin methods used in previous syntheses.

## The Zwitterionic Intermediate

The substrate for the hydrogenolysis step is the zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (compound 8 in the Shirota scheme).

This intermediate was produced in the previous step by phosphorylation of psilocin with tetrabenzylpyrophosphate, followed by an [[shirota-4-hydroxyindole-acetyl-protection-psilocin-synthesis-starting-material]] and its mono-debenzylated derivative and the appearance of the psilocybin product.

The reaction conditions are mild enough that no side reactions or decomposition products are typically observed.

## Monitoring the Reaction Progress

Shirota and colleagues monitored the hydrogenolysis reaction by TLC, observing the disappearance of the zwitterionic starting material (compound 8) and its mono-debenzyl derivative.

The appearance of psilocybin (compound 2) on the TLC plate indicated completion of the reaction.

This monitoring is important because over-reduction or under-reduction can both produce undesired products.

The use of TLC rather than more sophisticated analytical techniques reflects the practical, large-scale orientation of the Shirota synthesis.

TLC is rapid, inexpensive, and provides sufficient information for reaction monitoring in a production context.

## Workup and Isolation

Once the reaction was complete, the reaction solution was filtered through a Celite pad by suction to remove the palladium catalyst.

Celite is a [[comparison-neem-oil-cannabis-vs-diatomaceous-earth-cannabis]] material commonly used to filter fine catalyst particles from reaction mixtures.

The volume of the filtrate was reduced by evaporation, causing psilocybin to crystallize from solution.

The crystalline product was collected by filtration and washed with ethanol to afford psilocybin as a white needle crystalline powder.

This straightforward isolation procedure — filtration, concentration, and recrystallization — is consistent with the overall goal of avoiding chromatographic purification.

## Yield and Physical Properties

The hydrogenolysis step produced psilocybin in 87.5% yield from the zwitterionic intermediate, corresponding to 5.6 g of product.

The overall isolated yield of psilocybin from psilocin (the starting material for the phosphorylation step) was greater than 72%, even on gram-scale production.

This yield was described as "gratifying" by the authors, particularly when compared to previously reported yields of 20%, 47%, and other lower values from alternative syntheses.

Psilocybin was obtained as white needle crystals with a melting point of 190-198 degrees Celsius.

This melting point is consistent with the literature values of 185-195 and 210-212 degrees Celsius reported by Hofmann and colleagues in the original isolation papers.

## Spectroscopic Characterization

The psilocybin produced by the hydrogenolysis step was fully characterized by spectroscopic methods.

The UV spectrum in methanol showed absorption maxima at 221.0, 267.5, 278.5, and 290.0 nm, consistent with the indole chromophore.

The IR spectrum showed characteristic bands at 3266 and 3034 cm-1 (O-H and aromatic C-H stretches), 1620 cm-1 (aromatic ring vibration), and 1244 cm-1 (P=O stretch).

The proton NMR spectrum in D2O showed the characteristic indole ring protons and the N,N-dimethylamino signal as a singlet at 2.86 ppm.

The phosphorus-31 NMR spectrum confirmed the presence of the phosphate moiety with a signal at -4.48 ppm.

The high-resolution ESI mass spectrum confirmed the molecular formula C12H18N2O4P with [M + H]+ observed at m/z 285.0991 (calculated 285.1004).
