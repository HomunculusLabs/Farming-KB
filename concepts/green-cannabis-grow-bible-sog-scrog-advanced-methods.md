# SOG, ScrOG, and Advanced Indoor Grow Methods

Chapter 8 of Greg Green's Cannabis Grow Bible covers advanced indoor
growing techniques that maximize yield by concentrating on top cola
production. The three most common methods are SOG ([[comparison-sea-of-green-vs-screen-of-green]]),
ScrOG ([[cannabis-screen-of-green-scrog-guide]]), and Cabinet Growing.

## The Principle Behind Advanced Methods

The bottom branches of indoor cannabis plants do not catch much light
from HID fixtures and are mostly fan leaves that are not good to smoke.
The top cola produces the most bud. Advanced methods create setups that
concentrate on the top part of the plant, producing seriously large
top colas. These methods work with soil or hydroponics.

## SOG (Sea of Green)

SOG is a high-density growing method that produces a full canopy of bud:

1. Grow plants normally until they flower and you can select the best
   female.
2. Take cuttings from the chosen mother plant and root them.
3. Create as many clones as will fit in your grow setup.
4. SOG targets 1 plant per square foot or even 1 per 0.10 square feet.
5. Use tube-shaped pots (longer depth than width) to pack in maximum
   plants.
6. Plant all clones at the same time. No new plants are introduced
   into the SOG room after initial planting.
7. When clones reach desired height (keep small), switch to 12/12.

Because clones carry the age of the mother plant, clones taken from a
flowering mother will start flowering as soon as 12/12 begins. The
result is a full canopy of bud, hence "Sea of Green."

SOG enables large bud quantity turnover every 2 months. Using clones
means you only grow them for the remainder of their flowering time.

## ScrOG (Screen of Green)

ScrOG uses fewer plants than SOG combined with a training screen:

1. A wire mesh screen is placed between the light and the plants.
2. Use clones from a female plant (like SOG).
3. Allow at least 1 square foot per flowering plant (fewer plants than
   SOG).
4. Do not flower until plants have covered the entire mesh with green.
5. Train plants to grow horizontally under the screen by weaving
   branches through the netting.
6. Continue training for 2 weeks into the [[cannabis-sativa-flowering-cycle-management]], then let
   tops grow vertically through the screen.
7. Train main growing tops to the outside of the screen (they grow
   tallest).
8. Push large fan leaves down under the screen to allow light to reach
   developing bud sites.

The screen height varies from 8 to 24 inches depending on light type.
The light should be hung by adjustable chains. Screen material can be
2-inch chicken wire, 2-inch nylon poultry fencing, or hooks with 20 lb
fishing line.

### ScrOG Maintenance Tips

- Cut leaf fingers in half first rather than removing whole fan leaves.
  This allows light to reach bud sites while the remaining half still
  produces energy. Remove the rest a week later.
- Below the screen, remove all lateral branches and stray bud sites
  that have not made it through. They only drain energy from the buds
  above the canopy.
- ScrOG takes 1 to 3 weeks longer per grow than SOG due to the
  extended vegetative training period.

### ScrOG Variations

Several ScrOG variations exist:
- V-ScrOG (angled screen)
- Stadium ScrOG
- Flat ScrOG
- Cylinder ScrOG

All work on the same principle but have different shapes. A good ScrOG
grow averages 2 ounces of bud per square foot of screen. Pure Indica
or Indica-dominant crosses produce the best results in ScrOG.

### SOG vs ScrOG Comparison

The three main differences:
1. Number of plants grown (SOG uses many more).
2. The screen (ScrOG uses one, SOG does not).
3. Grow cycle length (ScrOG takes slightly longer).

Both can be done under the same light and in soil or hydroponics.

ScrOG was originally designed for grow areas limited in height using
fluorescent lights. Today's growers with HID lights have taken these
setups to new levels, producing top colas the size of soda bottles.

## Cabinet Growing

Cabinet growing uses a small enclosed space (hot press, closet,
refrigerator, box, or cupboard) accommodating 1 to 4 plants:

Three essentials for a cabinet grow:
- The cabinet itself
- A light (adjustable by chains or spring cord)
- An air vent with fan

Setup details:
- Walls painted flat white or covered with Mylar for reflection.
- A large hole near the top for exhaust (hot air rises) with a fan.
- An intake hole on the opposite side (intake fan moves slightly
  faster than exhaust to maintain fresh [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]).
- Plants go through their entire life cycle within the cabinet.
- Clones are kept in a small compartment (1-2 fluorescent lights) at
  the top near the exhaust fan.
- After harvest, clones move into the main grow space.

This creates a [[cannabis-perpetual-grow-cycle]] cycle. With the right strains, a cabinet
can produce a harvest every 30 days.

## Advanced Set-Ups

Expert growers scale these methods to entire rooms:
- 14 x 24 foot areas with 9-foot ceilings.
- Multiple vertically suspended HPS lights.
- Full reflective coverage (flat white paint and Mylar).
- Multiple air ducts, extraction fans, and CO2 generators.
