# Equatorial House Design and Tropical Home Garden

## Overview

In equatorial and tropical regions, [[permaculture-design]] demands a fundamentally different approach to housing and food production compared to temperate zones. Sun orientation is less critical than wind access; cooling relies on airflow rather than thermal mass; and the [[beekeeping-seasonal-calendar]] is driven by wet and dry periods rather than temperature. ### Roofing

Vented **sheet metal roofs** with generous overhangs shed torrential rain while allowing hot air to escape beneath. The air gap between the metal and any ceiling layer acts as a cooling buffer.

### Hurricane and Disaster Resistance

Tropical regions face hurricanes, tsunamis, and volcanic activity. Design responses include:

- **Strong central core** — a reinforced masonry or heavy-timber core that anchors the structure.
- **Deep ground anchors** tying the frame securely to foundations.
- **Hurricane-resistant construction** with braced frames and minimal overhang on windward sides.
- **Bamboo groves** planted as living wind barriers upwind of structures.
- **Site selection** in valley positions or behind earth banks for shelter.
- **Earth banks** and **oversize swales** that double as wind deflection and [[water-management]].

## The Tropical Home Garden

### Seasonal Calendar

The **wet season** is the tropical "hungry gap" — humidity promotes moulds, mildews, and rot, making crop storage difficult and increasing pest pressure. Key strategies:

- Time plantings so harvests mature **before the wet season** peaks.
- **Water storages** (tanks, ponds, swales) are essential to bridge dry periods.
- Select **mould- and mildew-resistant varieties** of all staple crops.

### Tree Crop Species

The tropical home garden centres on a polyculture of productive trees:

| Category | Species |
|---|---|
| Fruits | Mango, papaya, sapote, banana, limes, coconut, breadfruit, avocado, pineapple, durian |
| Nuts | Cashew, macadamia |
| Structural timbers | Bamboo, balsa, teak, palms, mahogany |
| Useful palms | Oil palms, coconut, and numerous other palm species |

### Leguminous Interplants

Large nitrogen-fixing legumes are interplanted throughout the orchard to supply fertility:

- **Inga** — shade and nitrogen fixation
- **Gliricidia** — green manure, living fence, fodder
- **Leucaena** — fast-growing mulch and fodder (use cautiously; can become weedy)
- **Cajanus** (pigeon pea) — food crop and soil builder

### Vegetable Garden

Ground-level [[coleman-french-historical-winter-vegetable-production]] includes:

- **Root crops:** Yam, sweet potato
- **Leaf crops:** Amaranth
- **Legumes:** Lima bean, velvet bean, Dolichos bean

## Domestic Animals in the Tropical System

Animals are integrated for pest control, nutrient cycling, and food production:

| Animal | Role |
|---|---|
| **Guinea-fowl** | Insect control in gardens and orchards |
| **Chickens / Bantams** | Scratching, insect control, egg production |
| **Geckoes, wolf spiders** | Indoor pest control (natural predators) |
| **Pigeons** | Meat and eggs; kept on elevated poles |
| **Bees** | Pollination, honey; hives on elevated poles for safety |
| **Guinea-pigs** | Grass and weed control |
| **Taiwanese pigs** | Orchard scavenging, waste recycling |
| **Waterfowl** (ducks, geese) | Grass and snail control, especially in wet areas |

### Pest and Termite Management

- **Guinea-fowl and chickens** patrol for insect pests.
- **Termite-resistant construction** is critical — use naturally resistant timbers, metal-capped posts, and physical barriers.
- **Marigolds, neem, and pyrethrum** are interplanted for [[cervantes-biological-pest-control-predators]].
- **Neem trees** planted to overhang ponds suppress mosquito larvae.

## Specialist Crops

Beyond food staples, tropical systems support valuable specialist crops:

- **Rubber (Hevea)** — latex production
- **Betel-nut** — stimulant crop
- **Chalmougra oil** — traditional medicinal and industrial oil
- **Chicle** — natural chewing gum base
- **Essential oils** — from tropical aromatics
- **Medicinals** — a wide range of tropical medicinal plants

## Interplant Strategies

- **Phosphate-fixing species:** Banksia, Casuarina, Gigasperma, Pultenea — these accumulate phosphorus in their tissues for later cycling.
- **Sugar-pumping plants** — species that draw sugars deep from the soil profile, exuding them through roots to feed soil microbiota.
- **Molasses and cane juice** applied to soils activate soil fauna and accelerate decomposition.
- **Neem overhanging ponds** provides shade and releases compounds that inhibit mosquito breeding.

## Garden Bed Design

- **Mounded beds** ensure drainage during the wet season; flat beds become waterlogged.
- **Mulch** is essential — heavy organic mulches protect topsoil from tropical rains and sun.
- **Mulch-tree species** (fast-growing legumes and palms) are grown specifically to produce continuous mulch material.
- **Logs** placed as garden bed edges retain soil, slow runoff, and decompose into humus over time.
- Logs also provide habitat for predatory insects and fungi that support plant health.

## Hurricane Damage Mitigation

An [[solomon-insects-and-diseases-integrated-approach]] combining site planning, vegetation, and earthworks:

1. **Earth banks** and ridges deflect wind and debris.
2. **Valley sites** gain natural shelter from surrounding terrain.
3. **Bamboo groves** act as the first line of wind defence.
4. **Tree canopy** of mixed species dissipates wind energy before it reaches structures.
5. **Oversize swales** manage extreme rainfall and reduce erosion during storm events.

## Key Principles

- Design for **airflow, drainage, and resilience** rather than solar gain and thermal mass.
- Integrate **structure, garden, and animal systems** for mutual benefit.
- Use **living systems** (bamboo, tree belts, animal allies) as primary defences against pests and weather extremes.
- Time all activities around the **wet/dry seasonal cycle**, not temperature.
- Build redundancy into water storage and food preservation systems.
