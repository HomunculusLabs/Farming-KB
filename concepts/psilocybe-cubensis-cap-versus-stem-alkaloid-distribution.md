# Psilocybe cubensis Cap vs Stem Alkaloid Distribution

## Overview

The distribution of psychoactive alkaloids between the cap (pileus) and
stem (stipe) of [[guzman-global-distribution-patterns-neurotropic-fungi]] has practical implications
for users seeking to optimize or standardize their dosage.

## Bigwood and Beug Findings

The study analyzed cap and stem tissues separately from three distinct
strains of [[psilocybe-cubensis]] cultivated on rye-grain substrate. All
samples were from first-flush fruitings, providing a consistent baseline
for comparison. The results revealed notable strain-dependent differences
in how alkaloids partition between cap and stem tissues:

### M.R. Strain

- **Caps**: 6.7 mg/g psilocybin, 0 mg/g psilocin
- **Stems**: 4.2 mg/g psilocybin, 0.35 mg/g psilocin

The M.R. strain showed approximately 1.6 times more psilocybin in caps
than in stems. Notably, all detectable psilocin was found exclusively in
the stem tissue, with none present in the caps.

### Equadorian Strain

- **Caps**: 7.6 mg/g psilocybin, 0 mg/g psilocin
- **Stems**: 4.7 mg/g psilocybin, 0.4 mg/g psilocin

The Equadorian strain exhibited a similar pattern to the M.R. strain,
with approximately 1.6 times more psilocybin in caps. Again, psilocin
was present exclusively in stem tissue.

### Amazon Strain

- **Caps**: 5.7 mg/g psilocybin, 0.1 mg/g psilocin
- **Stems**: 5.7 mg/g psilocybin, 0 mg/g psilocin

The Amazon strain showed a strikingly different pattern from the other
two strains. Psilocybin was distributed equally between caps and stems
at 5.7 mg/g each. Psilocin, present only in trace amounts, was found
in the cap rather than the stem—the reverse of the pattern observed
in the M.R. and Equadorian strains.

## General Pattern: Caps Concentrate Psilocybin

Across all three strains, caps consistently contained equal or greater
amounts of psilocybin compared to stems. In two of the three strains,
caps contained approximately 60% more psilocybin than stems. This
finding aligns with the general mycological understanding that the cap
is the primary site of [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]]
synthesis in agaric mushrooms. The reproductive structures of the cap

dependent than the psilocybin pattern. In the M.R. and Equadorian
strains, psilocin was found exclusively in the stem despite being
absent from the cap. In the Amazon strain, the reverse was true: a
trace of psilocin appeared in the cap with none in the stem. This
inconsistency suggests that the enzymatic dephosphorylation of
