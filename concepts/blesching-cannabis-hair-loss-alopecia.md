# Cannabis and Hair Loss (Alopecia)

Until relatively recently, the allopathic tradition believed that male pattern
baldness occurred in 3 out of 4 cases due to a gene passed down from the
maternal father. Environmental signals, in addition to genetic influences, are
thought to induce hormonal changes in the remainder. The role of a male sex
hormone called dihydrotestosterone (DHT) is implicated but not fully
understood. No allopathic cure is available. Some pharmaceutical and surgical
procedures have been used with limited success.

## The Endocannabinoid System and Hair Loss

A multi-institutional study (2009) with researchers from Germany, the United
States, England, and Hungary identified the [[blesching-endocannabinoid-system-overview]]
in the skin, including hair follicles. The main function of the ECS in the
skin is to control and balance growth, differentiation, and survival of skin
cells as well as to produce proper immune responses.

For hair loss, researchers identified a specific and counterintuitive
mechanism: down-regulation of CB1 stimulates hair growth. This means that
reducing CB1 receptor activity (rather than increasing it, as is therapeutic
in most other conditions) may promote hair follicle activity and hair
production.

## CB1 Receptor and Hair Follicle Science

Researchers (2007) from Debrecen, Hungary, made a key discovery: human scalp
hair follicles contain both endocannabinoid receptors and endocannabinoids,
which were determined to be key players in hair growth regulation. Thus, type
and dose-specific cannabinoids play a significant role in regulating unwanted
hair growth and unwanted hair loss.

The implications are significant:

- CB1 agonists (which activate the receptor) may reduce unwanted hair growth
  (see [[blesching-cannabis-hirsutism-unwanted-hair-growth]])
- CB1 antagonists (which block the receptor) might counteract hair loss
- THC, which activates CB1, could theoretically slow hair loss if the
  mechanism involves CB1-mediated inhibition of follicle activity
- CBD, which has a more complex receptor profile, may have different effects

## The Paradoxical Nature of CB1 Effects

The finding that CB1 down-regulation stimulates hair growth while CB1
up-regulation suppresses it creates an interesting therapeutic paradox. In
most conditions, CB1 activation is associated with therapeutic benefits. For
hair loss, the opposite appears to be true. This underscores the importance
of understanding condition-specific receptor dynamics before applying
cannabinoid therapies.

This paradox has important implications for patients using cannabis who may
also be experiencing hair concerns:

- Regular cannabis users who activate CB1 receptors might theoretically
  experience slower hair growth or increased hair loss
- The relationship is dose-dependent and may vary between individuals
- More research is needed to determine if the laboratory findings translate
  to clinical outcomes with whole-plant cannabis use
- The [[blesching-cannabis-four-prime-cannabinoids]] each have different
  receptor profiles that may produce different effects on hair follicles

No evidence-based CHI rating was applicable for this specific application, as
the research remains at the laboratory level.

## Conventional Hair Loss Treatments

Current pharmaceutical approaches to hair loss include:

- Minoxidil (Rogaine) -- a topical vasodilator that prolongs the growth phase
  of hair follicles
- Finasteride (Propecia) -- an oral medication that blocks the conversion of
  testosterone to DHT
- Hair transplant surgery -- moving hair follicles from donor to recipient
  areas
- Low-level laser therapy -- photobiomodulation of hair follicles
- Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections -- using the patient's own growth
  factors

Each of these approaches has limitations in efficacy, side effects, or cost.
The ECS-based approach, if validated clinically, would offer a novel mechanism
of action distinct from all current treatments.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Hair Loss

Mythology associates hair with power, as in the biblical story of Samson and
Delilah. Samson loses his supernatural strength after Delilah cuts off his
hair while he sleeps. Hair is also connected to the body beautiful, virility,
youthfulness, and sensuality. In the Western world, "long hair" has recently
come to be associated with rebellion from values that are perceived to be old
and outdated.

Haircuts, hair colors, and styles can communicate and project a strong
meaning, just as hair loss due to trauma, surgery, or chemotherapy may elicit
intense feelings. While baldness is not a disease, it can have psychological
consequences and can contribute to a negative self-image.

The [[blesching-cannabis-mental-disorders]] connection is relevant here, as
hair loss can contribute to [[cannabis-anxiety-research]],
[[blesching-cannabis-depression]], and diminished self-esteem.

Powerful questions for self-reflection:

- What are the symptoms keeping you from doing?
- How do you feel about that?
- Where do these feelings take you?
- What scenarios or memories are associated with these feelings?
- Can the answers direct your focus toward self-healing?

## Future Research Directions

Key areas for future investigation include:

- Clinical trials of CB1 antagonists for hair loss treatment
- Studies examining cannabis use patterns and hair loss in human populations
- Topical cannabinoid formulations specifically designed for scalp
  application
- Investigation of different cannabinoid ratios and their effects on hair
  follicle cycling
- Combination therapies pairing ECS-targeted approaches with conventional
  treatments
