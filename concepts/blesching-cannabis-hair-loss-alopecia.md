# Cannabis and Hair Loss (Alopecia)

Until relatively recently, the allopathic tradition believed that male pattern
baldness occurred in 3 out of 4 cases due to a gene passed down from the
maternal father. Environmental signals, in addition to genetic influences, are
thought to induce hormonal changes in the remainder. The role of a male sex
hormone called dihydrotestosterone (DHT) is implicated but not fully
understood. No allopathic cure is available. Some pharmaceutical and surgical
procedures have been used with limited success.

## The Endocannabinoid System and Hair Loss

- CBD, which has a more complex receptor profile, may have different effects

## The Paradoxical Nature of CB1 Effects

The finding that CB1 down-regulation stimulates hair growth while CB1
up-regulation suppresses it creates an interesting therapeutic paradox. In
most conditions, CB1 activation is associated with therapeutic benefits. For
hair loss, the opposite appears to be true. This underscores the importance
of understanding condition-specific receptor dynamics before applying
cannabinoid therapies.

This paradox has important implications for patients using cannabis who may
also be experiencing hair concerns:

- Regular cannabis users who activate CB1 receptors might theoretically
  experience slower hair growth or increased hair loss
- The relationship is dose-dependent and may vary between individuals
- More research is needed to determine if the laboratory findings translate
  to clinical outcomes with whole-plant cannabis use
- The [[blesching-cannabis-mental-disorders]] connection is relevant here, as
hair loss can contribute to [[blesching-cannabis-depression]], and diminished self-esteem.
