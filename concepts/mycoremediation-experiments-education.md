## 5. Engineering Mycoremediation Prototypes

The culmination of educational mycoremediation work is translating lab results into **functional prototypes** — physical filtration systems, erosion barriers, or bioremediation modules designed for real-world deployment.

### Interdisciplinary Collaboration

Effective prototype development requires collaboration between **engineering students** (who design flow systems, structural supports, and scalability) and **microbiology students** (who optimize fungal species, substrate composition, and loading rates). Joint projects teach both groups to communicate across disciplinary boundaries.

### From Data to Prototype

The workflow progresses through three stages:

1. **Data collection** — controlled experiments establish which fungi degrade which contaminants and under what conditions
2. **Prototype testing** — small-scale physical systems (e.g., mycofiltration buckets, erosion mats) are built and tested with real water or soil
3. **Implementation** — successful prototypes are scaled up or deployed in field conditions, with ongoing monitoring to evaluate performance

### Learning Outcomes

Students gain experience in experimental design, data analysis, biological cultivation, engineering prototyping, and environmental science — all within a single project arc. These experiments demonstrate that ecological problems can be addressed with biological solutions that are low-cost, sustainable, and accessible to communities worldwide.
