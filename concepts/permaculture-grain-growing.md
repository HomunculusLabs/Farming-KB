## Land Efficiency Comparison

To keep one human alive entirely on one food:
- 1,800 square feet of grain
- 5,400 square feet of potatoes
- 13,500 square feet of dairy
- 36,000 square feet of pork
- 90,000 square feet of beef

This is the greatest argument for a plant-based diet.

## Grain Growth Stages

Seed -> sprout -> seedling -> tiller (thicker shoots) -> booting (forming head) -> heading -> flowering (pollination) -> milk phase (squeezable milky fluid) -> dough phase (kernel dries) -> dead ripe. Harvest at late dough stage (past milk, still dentable).

## Harvesting the Old Way

Tools: scythe (large blade on long handle called a snath), sickle (small areas), or horse-drawn mower. Use a cradle attachment to collect cut grain into piles or windrows. Sheave and shock the grain: tie large handfuls with barren tillers, lean several sheaves against each other with one on top.

Threshing: use a hand flail on the barn floor. Winnow: toss grain up with a basket on a windy day, or use a winnowing tray.

## Seed Saving

Save seed from the second crop (not hybrids). Select the best, unbroken, healthy seed heads. Dry in the shock for at least a month. Store in rodent-proof containers in the house with cats, in a cool dry place. Add bay leaves. Grain keeps a year or more unground. Never eat moldy grain (ergot fungus). Once ground, use immediately.

## Processing

- Cracking: breaking kernel in pieces (usually corn)
- Crimping: flattening slightly (usually oats)
- Flaking: heat/moisture treatment then flattening
- Grinding: through rollers and screens
- Rolling: smashing between rollers

1 cup of grain makes 1.5 cups of flour.
