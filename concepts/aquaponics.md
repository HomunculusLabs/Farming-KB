## Aquaponics

Aquaponics is an integrated food production system that combines aquaculture (fish farming) with hydroponics (soilless plant growing) in a recirculating ecosystem. Fish waste provides nutrients for plants, and plants filter the water for fish. This creates a closed-loop system that uses approximately 90% less water than traditional agriculture while producing both protein and vegetables. Aquaponics fits naturally into [[permaculture-design]] as a Zone 1 intensive production system.

## System Types

### Deep Water Culture (DWC / Raft System)

Plants grow in floating rafts on the surface of a deep water channel (8-12 inches). Roots hang directly into the nutrient-rich water. Most common commercial aquaponics design.

**Advantages**: Simple design, excellent for leafy greens and herbs, easy to expand, good oxygenation, stable water chemistry.

**Disadvantages**: Limited to shallow-rooted plants, requires separate seedling starting system, large water volume needed, less suitable for heavy fruiting plants.

**Best crops**: Lettuce, basil, watercress, kale, chard, herbs.

### Media Bed (Flood and Drain)

Grow beds filled with gravel or expanded clay (hydroton) that flood with fish water and drain back on a timer (typically 15 min flood / 45 min drain cycle). The most common home/hobby system.

**Advantages**: Excellent biofiltration, supports a wide range of plants, serves as both grow bed and biofilter, good for beginners, supports earthworms (vermiaquaponics).

**Disadvantages**: Heavier than other systems, media can clog over time, requires bell siphon or timer, limited scalability compared to DWC.

**Best crops**: Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, beans, strawberries, leafy greens — the most versatile system.

### Nutrient Film Technique (NFT)

A thin film of water flows continuously through narrow channels where plant roots are exposed. Common in commercial hydroponics, less common in aquaponics.

**Advantages**: Water-efficient, good for small plants, easy to inspect roots, relatively inexpensive materials.

**Disadvantages**: Not suitable for large or heavy plants, limited biofiltration capacity, susceptible to pump failure, narrow channels can clog.

**Best crops**: Lettuce, herbs, small leafy greens.

### Vertical Aquaponics

Stacked growing columns or towers that maximize production in limited space. Water flows down through the media in each column.

**Advantages**: Maximum space efficiency, ideal for [[urban-permaculture-design]], dramatic visual appeal.

**Disadvantages**: Limited to small plants, complex plumbing, harder to maintain, reduced light penetration to lower tiers.

**Best crops**: Strawberries, lettuce, herbs, small greens.

## Fish Selection

### Tilapia (Most Popular)

The workhorse of warm-water aquaponics. Fast-growing, hardy, tolerant of poor water quality, efficient feed converters.

- **Temperature range**: 75-85F (will die below 55F)
- **Growth rate**: Harvestable in 6-9 months from fingerling
- **Stocking density**: 1 fish per 5-10 gallons (with adequate filtration)
- **Considerations**: Illegal to possess in some states without permits; require warm water year-round

### Bluegill and Sunfish

Native North American fish suitable for cold-climate aquaponics.

- **Temperature range**: 65-80F
- **Growth rate**: Slower than tilapia; 12-18 months to harvest
- **Advantages**: Legal everywhere, tolerate cold water, good table fish

### Trout

Excellent for cold-water systems. High-quality food fish.

- **Temperature range**: 55-65F (require cool water; die above 72F)
- **Growth rate**: 12-18 months to harvest
- **Considerations**: Require excellent water quality, cool water, and higher dissolved oxygen levels

### Koi and Goldfish

Ornamental fish that work well in aquaponics where fish harvest is not the primary goal.

- **Temperature range**: Wide (35-85F)
- **Advantages**: Very hardy, long-lived, beautiful, available everywhere
- **Disadvantages**: Not typically eaten; lower nutrient output per fish than food species

### Catfish

Hardy bottom-feeders suitable for warm-water systems.

- **Temperature range**: 70-85F
- **Advantages**: Tolerant of low dissolved oxygen, good table fish, can eat diverse diets

## Plant Selection

### High-Value Aquaponics Crops

- **Leafy greens**: Lettuce (best performer), kale, Swiss chard, watercress, arugula — fast turnover, high demand
- **Herbs**: Basil (exceptional in aquaponics), mint, cilantro, parsley, dill — premium pricing
- **Fruiting vegetables**: Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, eggplant — need more nutrients and stronger light
- **Other**: Strawberries, edible flowers, microgreens

### Plants That Struggle

- Root vegetables (carrots, potatoes, onions) need deeper media than most systems provide
- Heavy-feeding brassicas (cabbage, broccoli) can cause nutrient deficiencies
- Acid-loving plants (blueberries) are difficult to accommodate
- Large trees and shrubs are impractical in most systems

## System Cycling

Cycling is the process of establishing the bacterial colonies that convert fish waste into plant-available nutrients. This is the most critical phase of starting an aquaponics system.

### The Nitrogen Cycle

1. Fish excrete ammonia (NH3/NH4+) through gills and waste
2. Nitrosomonas bacteria convert ammonia to nitrite (NO2-) — highly toxic to fish
3. Nitrobacter/Nitrospira bacteria convert nitrite to nitrate (NO3-) — relatively harmless and excellent plant fertilizer
4. Plants absorb nitrate, cleaning the water for fish

### Cycling Methods

**Fishless cycling (recommended)**:
1. Add ammonia source (pure ammonium chloride or fish food) to reach 2-4 ppm
2. Test water daily for ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate
3. After 2-4 weeks: ammonia and nitrite will spike then drop to near zero
4. When nitrate is present and ammonia/nitrite are zero, the system is cycled
5. Add fish gradually (start with 25% of final stocking density)

**Fish-in cycling**:
1. Add a small number of hardy fish
2. Feed sparingly (25% of normal rate)
3. Test water daily; perform 25% water changes if ammonia or nitrite exceed safe levels
4. Takes 4-6 weeks
5. Stressful for fish; not recommended for beginners
