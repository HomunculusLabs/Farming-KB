## Cytochrome P450 Superfamily Overview

Cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes are a massive superfamily of heme-thiolate
monooxygenases found across all domains of life. The name derives from the 450
nm absorption peak when reduced heme-iron binds CO, first observed by
Klingenberg (1958) and confirmed by Omura and Sato (1962). Humans have 57
functional CYP genes in 18 families; over 300,000 named CYP sequences exist
across all species.

All P450s share a conserved fold around a protoporphyrin IX heme group
coordinated axially by a cysteine thiolate ligand. The iron cycles between Fe3+
(resting), Fe2+ (reduced), and Fe4+=O states during catalysis. Mammalian P450s
are ER membrane-anchored via an N-terminal helix and require electrons from
NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase (CPR) and sometimes cytochrome b5. Mitochondrial
P450s use adrenodoxin/adrenodoxin reductase.

## The P450 Catalytic Cycle

The catalytic cycle involves six steps, each a distinct chemical state:

1. **Substrate binding:** Substrate displaces water from Fe3+, causing a low-
spin to high-spin shift that lowers redox potential.
2. **First electron transfer:** CPR reduces Fe3+ to Fe2+.
3. **Oxygen binding:** O2 binds Fe2+, forming a ferric-superoxide (Fe3+-O2-*).
4. **Second electron transfer:** A second electron and proton produce the
ferric-hydroperoxo intermediate (Compound 0, Fe3+-OOH).
5. **Compound I formation:** O-O bond heterolysis releases water and generates
Compound I, [Fe4+=O Por*+], one of the most potent biological oxidants, capable
of H-atom abstraction from C-H bonds (~96-100 kcal/mol).
6. **Oxygen rebound:** Compound I abstracts H from substrate, then rebounds OH
(Groves' mechanism, 1976) to yield hydroxylated product. Uncoupling
(superoxide/H2O2 release) generates [[caffeine]] P1A2 metabolizes caffeine, theophylline, clozapine (~10% of drugs).

## CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 Variants

**CYP2C9:** *2 (R144C, ~8% Caucasians) and *3 (I359L, ~6%) reduce efficiency.
*2/*3 carriers need 30-80% lower warfarin doses with higher bleeding risk. FDA
warfarin labeling includes pharmacogenomic guidance (with VKORC1).

**CYP2C19:** *2 (loss-of-function, ~15% Caucasians, 25-30% East Asians); *17
(gain-of-function, ~18% Caucasians). Clopidogrel requires CYP2C19 activation:
PMs have ~30% reduced platelet inhibition and higher cardiovascular events. FDA
boxed warning (2010). PPI dosing affected: PMs have ~4x higher omeprazole AUC.

## Phase I and Phase II Metabolism

Phase I introduces polar groups (-OH, -NH2, -COOH) through oxidation. CYPs
account for ~70-80% of phase I. Phase II (UGTs, SULTs, GSTs, NATs) conjugates
hydrophilic molecules for excretion. Phase I often creates groups needed for
phase II (CYP hydroxylation enables UGT [[glutathione-conjugation]] detoxifies these. Overdose depletes glutathione, causing
hepatic necrosis.

## Drug-Drug Interactions

CYP-mediated DDIs are a leading cause of adverse drug reactions. Reversible
competitive inhibition is immediate; mechanism-based inactivation (reactive
metabolite covalently modifies heme/apoprotein) requires new enzyme synthesis
(36-72h for CYP3A4).

Clinically significant: simvastatin + itraconazole (rhabdomyolysis, AUC ~10x);
terfenadine + ketoconazole (fatal arrhythmia); OCs + rifampin (contraceptive
failure); cyclosporine + St. John's Wort (rejection); clopidogrel + omeprazole
(reduced activation); warfarin + amiodarone (bleeding). Rifampin via PXR is
among the most potent inducers, reducing substrate AUC >90%.
