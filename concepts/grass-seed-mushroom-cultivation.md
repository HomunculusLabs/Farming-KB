# Grass Seed Mushroom Cultivation

Grass seed substrate is a versatile and efficient medium for the cultivation of [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-amanita-muscaria-emerging-alternative]] species, particularly *Psilocybe mexicana* and *[[sclerotia-maturation-timeline-psilocybe-tampanensis-mexicana]]*, both of which produce sclerotia (truffle-like underground masses) as well as fruiting bodies. The technique was popularized by the late GUID, a pioneering figure in the online [[psilocybe-cubensis]]**: Produces mushrooms on grass seed, though rye grain is generally preferred for this species due to larger fruiting body size.

- Syringe needles must be flamed before each inoculation

- A simple cardboard box hood prevents drafts and reduces contamination

- Jar lids are opened only a crack, and work is performed swiftly
- After inoculation, jars are shaken to distribute inoculant and lids are loosened again for gas exchange

### Safety Warning

Alcohol and spray Lysol used for [[challenge-organisms-nasa-microwave-surface-sterilization-testing]] are highly flammable and must not be used near open flames used for needle/scalpel sterilization.

## Colonization

Jars are incubated in a clean, draft-free location at room temperature (approximately 20 degrees Celsius) or slightly higher. When mycelium begins growing in only a few spots, shaking the jar redistributes colonized kernels throughout the substrate, dramatically accelerating colonization speed.

### Colonization Timeline

Depending on temperature, species, and inoculation method, grass seed can be completely colonized in 5 to 20 days. Faster colonization is achieved through aggressive shaking to redistribute colonized grain.

### Metabolite Warning

If jars are incubated too long or at too high a temperature, mycelium will excrete yellowish metabolites (liquid droplets with a distinctive yellow color). This indicates stress and means the colonized grain should be cased or spawned as soon as possible to prevent degradation.

## Casing for Mushroom Fruiting

When the grass seed is fully colonized, it is transferred to a fruiting container for mushroom production.

### Tray Preparation

Colonized grain is shaken loose from the jar and poured into a 1-litre disposable plastic tray. Any lumps are broken up using the clean rim of the jar. The grain surface is leveled evenly.

### Casing Application

A thin layer (1.5-2.0 cm) of casing soil is applied over the grain surface using a large spoon and fork. The casing surface should be kept approximately even while maintaining a rough texture with small valleys and hills, which promotes even pinning.

### Casing Soil Recipe

- 10 parts peat
- 5 parts vermiculite
- 2 parts limestone (marl)

Ingredients are mixed dry, then water is added gradually while stirring. The goal is maximum moisture retention without the mixture becoming mud. The casing soil is filled into oven bags or autoclave bags and sterilized for one hour in the pressure cooker before use.

### Note on Casing Sterilization

While some authors advise against sterilizing casing soil to preserve beneficial microorganisms, the GUID method recommends sterilization based on direct experience — untreated or pasteurized casing soils produced poor results compared to sterilized material.

## Fruiting Conditions

The method uses simple clear plastic bins covered with polyethylene sheeting. Holes melted in the bin sides provide passive air exchange and can be covered with mesh to exclude flies. Five cased trays fit in each bin, and evaporation from the casing surface provides sufficient humidity.

### Cold Shocking

Harder-to-fruit strains benefit from cold shocking — placing trays in a refrigerator overnight before introducing to the fruiting bin. Easy-fruiting strains such as Ecuador cubensis do not require this treatment. *Psilocybe mexicana*, *Psilocybe tampanensis*, and *[[gartz-panaeolus-subbalteatus-taxonomy-and-intoxication-history]]* do not benefit from cold shocking.

### Watering and Flush Management

Casings are misted daily and must never be allowed to dry out. Watering is increased immediately after picking a flush, as maturing mushrooms extract significant moisture from the casing. The first pinheads typically appear 6-15 days after introducing casings to the fruiting bin. Mushrooms mature in 5-7 days.

Most species produce 3 flushes under proper management, though well-maintained casings can yield 5 or 6 flushes. *Psilocybe mexicana* typically produces one large flush followed by a smaller second flush, while other species produce more consistent yields across multiple flushes.

### Contamination Prevention

Dead pinheads (aborts) should be removed from the casing surface promptly, as they serve as nutrient sources for mold colonization.
