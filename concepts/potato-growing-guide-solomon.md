# Potato Growing Guide - Irish and Sweet

Updated: 2026-04-18

[[steve-solomon]] considers potatoes the most calorie-efficient [[vegetable-crop-demand-classification-fertility-matching-solomon]], making them essential for any serious food garden. He provides detailed guidance on both Irish potatoes and sweet potatoes.

## Irish Potatoes

### Why Potatoes Lead

When growing food during lean times, maximizing calories per unit area is essential, and potatoes lead all vegetables in this regard. Given only moderate fertility and the right soil type, with long summers and warm nights, each plant can produce 8 to 12 good-sized potatoes.

### Soil and Fertilization

- Potatoes are a medium-demand vegetable. Extremely high fertility is not needed.
- Avoid too much manure or [[compost]], which can cause the potatoes to be diseased.
- It's better to make the entire potato bed moderately fertile rather than concentrating amendments in rows or hills.
- Light soils are preferred; [[query-how-do-i-improve-heavy-clay-soil-in-my-garden]] can restrict tuber development.
- Good drainage after heavy rains is essential.

### Planting

- Plant in wide raised rows about four feet (120 cm) apart on center.
- In maritime climates, raise beds about 6 to 8 inches (15-20 cm) above narrow footpaths.
- Place seed pieces about 15 inches (40 cm) apart atop ridges.
- Alternatively, three seed pieces in a moderately fertile hill, hills on four-foot centers.
- Sprout in cooler soil than beans will.
- Unless in a short-season area, sow after soil has warmed sufficiently.

### Growing

- Keep the patch well and shallowly cultivated until the foliage has shaded the ground, suppressing any newly germinating weeds with deep shade.
- The root system sprawls to match the vines. At maturity, roots may extend 5 feet (150 cm) outward and penetrate an 8-foot (240 cm) depth.
- Late varieties grow longer before blooming and yield more. In long-season climates, use the latest of late varieties for the main storage crop.

### Harvest and Storage

- As soon as the earliest variety is in bloom, dig a plant for new potatoes. They grow one size every few days.
- Main harvest when vines have browned off, indicating skins have toughened.
- Dig carefully to avoid cuts. Cut potatoes won't heal well enough for long storage.
- Do not bruise potatoes. Handle them gently.
- Ideal storage: humid conditions, stable 40 degrees F (4 degrees C).
- Colder than that and starch converts to sugar (sweetening).
- Warmer than that and potatoes sprout prematurely.
- Solomon stores in wood boxes stacked in a tightly built, unheated outbuilding with wool blankets to stabilize temperature.

### Seed Potatoes

- Don't save your own seed potatoes. Growing through the season infects vines with virus diseases.
- Over 20 such diseases exist, transmitted year to year in planting stock.
- More years gardeners plant from their own seeds, the more viruses accumulate and the lower the yield.
- Always buy certified disease-free seed. The cost is small compared to the yield improvement.
- If you must save your own small spuds, do not do this for more than a year or two before starting anew with certified seed.
- Do not use supermarket potatoes for seed -- they are not certified and may carry disease.

## Sweet Potatoes

### Climate Requirements

- Sweet potatoes prefer light, well-drained soils.
- They are a spring crop where summers are long, hot, and humid.
- They don't like scorchingly hot weather, especially when also humid.
- They are usually grown as a spring crop in hot climates and dug early where summers are long.

### Planting

- Start from certified disease-free shoots or from your own sprouted roots.
- Virus-free starts increase yields by as much as a third.
- To start a few vines: suspend a sweet potato on toothpicks in a container, cover half with water.
- For larger quantities: place presprouted sweet potatoes on a bed of sand, cover with 2 inches of moist soil, keep between 70-75 degrees F.
- When sprouts are 10-12 inches (25-30 cm) long with stout stems, cut off 2 inches above the soil with a sharp knife.
- Plant sprouts almost horizontally, about 2 inches deep, making sure two leaves are above ground.
- Rows four feet (120 cm) on center.

### Growing

- Light soils dry out rapidly, so keep soil moist but never soaked.
- This encourages better root development.
- Keep well-weeded before vines run.
- Hill up soil against stems gradually as vines grow. Extension services say hilling reduces insect problems.
