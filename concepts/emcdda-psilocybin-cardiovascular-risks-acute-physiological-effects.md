# Psilocybin Cardiovascular Risks and Acute Physiological Effects

relevance for users with pre-existing conditions.

## Acute Physiological Effects Profile

The physiological effects of hallucinogenic mushrooms are generally not
severe and tend to be self-limiting. The EMCDDA report catalogues the
following acute somatic effects based on user reports and clinical
observations:

Dizziness is one of the most commonly reported physical effects, likely
related to psilocybin's serotonergic activity affecting the vestibular
system and autonomic regulation. Nausea is similarly prevalent — the
2005 Mixmag clubbing magazine survey found that over a quarter of those
who had used [[tompkins-bose-plant-nervous-system-hypothesis]]) and partly a response to the unpalatable taste and
texture of raw or dried mushrooms.

Additional reported effects include weakness, muscle aching, shivering,
abdominal pain, and dilation of pupils (mydriasis). These effects are
consistent with sympathetic nervous system activation, which is expected
given psilocybin's action as a serotonin receptor agonist — the
serotonergic system has extensive cross-talk with the sympathetic nervous
system through the raphe nuclei and hypothalamic pathways.

## Cardiovascular Effects in Detail

The EMCDDA specifically notes mild-to-moderate increases in heart rate
(tachycardia) and breathing rate (tachypnea), as well as elevation of
blood pressure. These sympathomimetic effects are consistent with the
classification of psilocybin as an indolealkylamine tryptamine — a
chemical group that typically produces some degree of sympathetic
activation alongside its primary serotonergic effects.

The report notes that body temperature generally remains normal during
psilocin intoxication, which is an important clinical differentiator.
Fever or hyperthermia should prompt consideration of contaminants or
co-ingested substances rather than psilocybin itself as the causative
agent.

The tendency for temporarily increased blood pressure is identified as
a specific risk factor for users with cardiovascular conditions,
especially untreated hypertension. This represents the most clearly
identified physiological risk group — individuals with existing heart
disease, hypertension, or cerebrovascular conditions face elevated risk
from the transient but significant blood pressure elevation that
accompanies psilocybin intoxication.

## Severe Physical Symptoms

While generally mild, pronounced physical symptoms have been recorded in
clinical case reports. These include severe stomach pain, persistent
vomiting, and diarrhoea. The EMCDDA notes that such severe symptoms may

## Clinical Management of Acute Intoxication

Diagnosis of hallucinogenic [[panaeolus-mushroom-intoxication-history]] is not always
straightforward unless there is information about recent ingestion from
the user, friends, or family. The clinical picture — mydriasis,
tachycardia, mild hypertension, perceptual disturbances — can resemble
other serotonergic or sympathomimetic toxidromes. First aid focuses on
reassurance and prevention of self-harm, followed by transfer to
appropriate medical facilities.

Benzodiazepines are identified as the safest medication of choice,
effective for most patients presenting with acute mushroom intoxication.
The EMCDDA specifically notes that there is no evidence of chronic
toxicity from psilocybin, no irreversible organ damage has been
reported, and insufficient data exist on mutagenicity and
teratogenicity to draw conclusions.

## Route of Administration and Physiological Onset

The route of administration affects both onset and physiological
profile. Users typically ingest mushrooms raw, dried, or brewed into tea.
The onset of effects is between 10 to 60 minutes depending on ingestion
modality, physical condition, food intake, and user experience. Brewing
into tea may accelerate onset but can also increase nausea. The EMCDDA
notes unverified reports of dried mushrooms being smoked (said to induce
faster but milder effects) and rare reports of intravenous injection of
mushroom extracts, which results in more severe acute physiological
effects — a practice carrying significant additional medical risk.
