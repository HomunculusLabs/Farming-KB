## Artificial Lighting for Cannabis Cultivation

## Overview

Artificial lighting is the foundation of indoor cannabis cultivation. Marijuana needs light with
the proper spectrum and intensity to ensure rapid, healthy growth. Light is comprised of separate
bands of colors, and each color in the spectrum sends the plant a separate signal that promotes
different types of growth. Understanding light science is essential for maximizing yield and
quality in indoor gardens.

## Light Spectrum and PAR

### Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR)

Plants use only certain portions of the light spectrum. The most important colors for maximum
chlorophyll production and photosynthetic response are in the blue and red range. The main portion
of light used by plants falls between 400 and 700 nanometers (nm), a region called the
Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) zone.

### PAR Watts

"PAR watts" measures the actual amount of specific photons a plant needs to grow. Photons are a
measure of light energy that is radiated and assimilated during photosynthesis. Blue photons are
worth more PAR watts than red photons, though scientists have difficulty measuring the exact
difference. Each color of light activates different plant functions, including positive tropism —
the plant's ability to orient leaves toward light.

### Spectrum and Plant Function

The light spectrum cannabis needs differs from what humans perceive. The human eye is most
sensitive to light between 525-625 nm, while plants heavily utilize the blue (400-500 nm) and red
(600-700 nm) portions. Light bulbs deliver only a part of the necessary spectrum, but they deliver
enough for healthy growth when properly selected.

## Measuring Light

### Foot-Candles and Lux

Most commercial light meters measure light in foot-candles or lux. Both scales measure light
visible to the human eye, not photosynthetic response. A foot-candle equals the intensity of one
candle at a distance of one foot; one foot-candle equals 10.76 lux.

These measurements are imperfect for horticultural purposes because the human eye sees much less of
the light spectrum than plants do. However, they still give a useful indication of light
distribution and relative intensity across a growing area.

### Kelvin Temperature and Color

Light is measured in spectrum using Kelvin temperature, which expresses the color a bulb emits:

| Light Source | Kelvin Rating |
|---|---|
| North Sky | 28,000K |
| Noon Sunlight | 5,600K |
| Daylight Metal Halide | 5,100K |
| Cool White Fluorescent | 4,200K |
| Standard Metal Halide | 4,000K |
| Warm Metal Halide | 3,200K |

Rendering Index (CRI) provides additional quality information — higher CRI values indicate better

## High Intensity Discharge (HID) Lights

HID lights are the standard for serious indoor cannabis cultivation. They produce the intensity and
spectrum needed for robust growth and heavy yields. The two main types of HID lamps are Metal Halide
(MH) and High Pressure Sodium (HPS), each with distinct spectral characteristics.

### Metal Halide (MH) Systems

Metal halide lamps produce a blue-white light that closely simulates spring and summer sunlight.
They are excellent for the [[cervantes-reflective-hoods-light-distribution]] to maximize light coverage and minimize waste
- Adjustable light height to maintain optimal distance from canopy
- Ballasts placed in separate areas to reduce heat in the grow room
- Timer systems for automated photoperiod control

## Compact Fluorescent Lamps (CFL)

Compact fluorescent lamps are useful for seedlings, clones, and small-scale grows. They produce
less heat than HID systems and can be placed closer to plants. CFLs with higher Kelvin ratings
(5000-6500K) are suitable for vegetative growth. While insufficient for flowering large plants,
CFLs serve well during early growth stages and for [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]] may start flowering under more light and
less darkness. The shift from [[cervantes-compact-fluorescent-lighting]]
diminishes rapidly with distance and cannot adequately penetrate a dense canopy.
