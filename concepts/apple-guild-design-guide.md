# Apple Guild Design

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

The apple guild is one of the most widely used and well-documented [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]]
- Leaf litter for mulch and soil building
- Shade and microclimate modification
- Habitat structure for birds and insects

## Standard Apple Guild Members

### Nitrogen Fixers
- **Clover** (white or red) -- grows beneath the tree, fixes nitrogen, provides bee forage
- **Alfalfa** -- deep-rooted nitrogen fixer, also breaks up compacted subsoil
- **Lupine** -- ornamental nitrogen fixer that attracts pollinators

### Nutrient Accumulators
- **Comfrey** (Symphytum officinale) -- deep taproot draws potassium, calcium, and other minerals from subsoil; leaves can be cut several times per season as mulch
- **Dandelion** -- accumulates minerals, provides [[comparison-living-mulch-vs-cover-crops]] beneath the tree, fixes nitrogen, suppresses weeds
- **Thyme or oregano** -- low-growing aromatic herbs that suppress weeds and attract beneficial insects

### Understory Plants
- **Currants or gooseberries** -- berry shrubs that produce in the partial shade of the apple tree
- **Comfrey** -- large leaves create a living mulch layer

## Guild Interactions

The apple guild functions through multiple interconnections:
1. Comfrey draws nutrients from deep soil, deposits them on the surface as mulch when cut
2. Clover and alfalfa fix atmospheric nitrogen for the apple tree's roots
3. Dill and fennel attract parasitic wasps that lay eggs in codling moth larvae
4. Garlic and chives deter borers from attacking the trunk
5. Ground covers suppress weeds that compete with shallow apple roots
6. Nasturtium traps aphids away from apple foliage
7. Beneficial insects attracted by insectary plants pollinate the apple blossoms
8. Bird-attracting plants bring birds that eat additional pests

## Installation and Maintenance

### Planting Layout
- Plant the apple tree first, allowing it to establish for 1-2 years
- Ring the trunk at 1-2 feet distance with garlic or chives
- Plant comfrey in a circle at the drip line (where rain drips from the canopy edge)
- Scatter dill, fennel, and yarrow between the trunk and drip line
- Plant clover or alfalfa as a living mulch beneath the entire canopy
- Place nasturtium at the outer edge where it can sprawl
- Add berry shrubs at the drip line in partial shade

### Ongoing Care
- Cut comfrey 2-4 times per season, laying cuttings as mulch around the tree
- Allow some dill and fennel to self-seed
- Replant garlic annually (it is not perennial)
- Maintain the ground cover layer, adding more if gaps appear
- Prune the apple tree to maintain good [[doc]]
- [[symphytum-officinale]]
- [[yarrow]]
