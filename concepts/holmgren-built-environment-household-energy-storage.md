# Built Environment as Energy Storage and the Household Economy

David Holmgren extends the concept of energy storage beyond biological systems to
encompass the built environment, household economies, and cultural systems. The
transformation of energy into progressively more elaborate and valuable forms
extends well beyond physical resources to the things we make with those resources
-- tools, buildings, and infrastructure.

## The Problem of Modern Built Assets

In modern society, the huge harvest of fossil and natural energies over the last
few hundred years has moved down the "industrial food chain" resulting in the
explosive development of cities, technology, and infrastructure. People in the
affluent world now live primarily in a built rather than natural environment,
constantly shedding "junk" to make way for new possessions made possible by
technology.

Most of these stores are useless without the rest of the industrial complex, and
they mostly encourage more energy consumption and waste. Energy-consuming
buildings and freeways rely on a constant flow of new fossil energy. Even
computers may be dependent on continuous redundancy and replacement to achieve
economies of scale and stave off accumulating software corruption.

## Design Criteria for Built Assets

When considering the development of tools, buildings, and infrastructure,
Holmgren suggests we should emulate the characteristics of natural energy
storages. The following design criteria are relevant:

- Modest in scale
- Well-designed for long life and/or made from easily renewable materials
- Simple to maintain (not necessarily maintenance-free)
- Multi-purpose and easy to adapt to other uses

Along with creative retrofitting of existing buildings and infrastructure,
applying these criteria in all new developments could help contain the
overdevelopment problem in the Western world, where maintenance costs are
beginning to erode economic wealth.

## The Melliodora Model

At Melliodora, maximum use has been made of earth and stoneworks to construct
terraces, dams, and paths, while resisting the tendency to build too many fences,
outbuildings, and structures requiring continuous maintenance and renewal.
Permanent buildings are no more modest in size than typical small rural
properties, but more functions and hours of use are packed into them with a
working-from-home and self-reliant lifestyle.

Each building is multi-purpose and constructed to be easily retrofitted
internally. Extensive use has been made of earth and wood as locally available
and renewable materials. Modern industrial building materials are used for
long-life functions, but the use of pumps, active devices, and technology in
performing essential building functions has been minimised.

## Household Energy Storages

One Permaculture strategy is to encourage household and local economic
self-reliance, which rebuilds traditional forms of energy storage in food, fuel,
and other resources. At Melliodora, the seedbox, the cellar full of produce, and
the stack of firewood are traditional storages of modest wealth.

Order and diversity in the seedbox reflect efforts to save, exchange, and
purchase the potential for abundance in future seasons. A pantry and cellar full
of preserves capture the essence and substance of a bountiful season and provide
insurance against a bad one. A large wood stack, with green wood added at one end
and two-year seasoned wood used from the other, represents abundance and work
slowly maturing through weather and sunshine.

## Characteristics of Household Storages

These types of household energy storages are by their nature:

- Diverse -- multiple forms of stored energy and resources
- Small -- appropriate to household scale
- Dispersed -- distributed across many households rather than centralised
- Easily used -- accessible without complex technology
- Not rich or portable enough to attract thieves or monopolists

A nation full of such wealth is much more secure and stable than one dependent on
the high-throughput, centralised systems of the supermarket and electricity grid.
These characteristics directly support [[holmgren-household-economy-and-self-reliance]]
and [[holmgren-household-energy-storages]].

## Energy Storage in Culture

The largest stores of high-quality embodied energy are in forms people do not
recognise as containing energy. Information and organisation in government,
economy, community, and culture are thought to be largely ethereal "goods"
unrelated to energy and resources. But the proliferation of these "things" has
been essential in our high-energy fossil-fuel-based society.

Howard Odum and other systems ecologists have shown that the EMERGY (embodied
energy) required to create complex forms of organisation and structure is very
large. Similarly, studies of human systems show how the creation and maintenance
of government, economy, education, and culture follow the same energy rules as
natural ecosystems.

## Financial Capital as Illusory Wealth

Financial capital is the most obvious form of non-material wealth. In recent
decades, the rapid movement of capital around the world and its focus on
destructive short-term investment has been recognised as the most dysfunctional
element of modern capitalism. Much of the value of financial capital may be
illusory because it has become so disconnected from sources of real wealth.

Ethical investment is one of the most rapidly growing areas of financial services,
constrained more by a shortage of appropriate businesses meeting social and
environmental criteria than by shortage of investors. Returns have generally been
as good as or better than market averages.

## [[appropriate-technology]]: The Passive Solar Example

A passive solar house uses the high-embodied energy in glass to capture solar
energy. If it does this while providing the other functions of glass -- natural
lighting and views -- this is good use of embodied non-renewable energy. This is
the archetype of appropriate technology: using concentrated non-renewable energy
to establish a system that will continue to function on renewable flows.

At Melliodora, connection to mains electricity supports less than 3 kilowatt
hours per day -- less than one-fifth of typical household use. Space heating,
water heating, and cooking are provided with renewable energy (passive solar and
wood). Electricity for refrigeration is more of an extra than an essential
service.

## Idealism Versus Pragmatism

In practice, it is hard and maybe unwise to completely avoid the generous energy
subsidy of fossil fuels for daily living. Nevertheless, we should design our
systems as if these resources were much more highly priced. Fossil fuels are very
useful, but they are massively overused and most uses to which they are put are
destructive or banal.
