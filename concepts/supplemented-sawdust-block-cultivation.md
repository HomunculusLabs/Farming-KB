## Supplemented Sawdust Block Cultivation
 Supplemented sawdust blocks represent the standard method for commercial oyster mushroom production and are increasingly adopted by advanced home
cultivators seeking higher yields and better quality fruiting bodies. This
approach produces significantly higher yields compared to straw-based methods,
at the cost of requiring [[query-how-do-fungal-volatile-compounds-affect-plant-growth-and-other-microorganisms]] that can inhibit
mycelial growth and reduce colonization rates.

Sawdust particle size affects colonization speed: fine sawdust packs too
densely limiting air exchange, while coarse material provides excellent
aeration but lower nutrient density. A mix of coarse chips (30-40%) and fine
sawdust (60-70%) provides optimal balance for most commercial and hobby
growing operations.

### Supplements

Nutritional supplements dramatically increase yield by providing readily
available nitrogen, vitamins, and minerals that accelerate mycelial growth and
support larger fruiting bodies. Common supplements and typical addition rates
include wheat bran at 10-20% by dry weight, rice bran at 8-15%, soybean hulls
at 10-25%, and cottonseed meal at 5-10%. Supplementation levels above 20%
require extended sterilization times and increase contamination risk
substantially.

### Moisture Adjustment

The final substrate mixture should be hydrated to 60-65% moisture content.
This typically requires adding water at approximately 1.5-2 times the dry
weight of [[pasteurization-in-mushroom-cultivation]] methods
used with straw substrates.

## Inoculation and Colonization

After cooling to below 30 degrees C, grain spawn is added at 5-10% by weight
through the filter patch opening or dedicated inoculation port. The spawn is
mixed through the substrate by shaking and massaging the bag thoroughly until
grain kernels are evenly distributed throughout the block.
