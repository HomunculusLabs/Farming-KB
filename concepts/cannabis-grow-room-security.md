Source: The Cannabis Grow Bible, Greg Green (Ch. 4)

Security is one of the most critical aspects of cannabis cultivation. According to Greg Green, loose lips cause 99.9% of all security breaches. The single most important rule for any grower is:

**NEVER TELL ANYONE THAT YOU ARE GROWING CANNABIS.**

## The Golden Rule of Security

If you can keep your operation completely secret, you have more security than any physical barrier could provide. Loose lips sink ships. Only people who live with you should know about your grow. If your partner has loose lips, consider guerrilla growing outdoors instead.

Key factors to consider before growing:

- Do you have time to care for plants?
- Will someone need to watch them while you are away?
- How secure is your grow area?
- Can you hide the smell during flowering?
- Are you prepared for higher electricity bills?
- Will the meter reader, gas man, or visitors see your grow room?
- Will people walk past your outdoor grow site?

## Indoor Security

Indoor security involves concealing the grow room from visitors, service workers, and neighbors:

- **Electricity**: HID lights draw significant power. Be aware of unusual spikes in your electric bill. A single 400W or 600W light running on a 12/12 cycle adds noticeable consumption.
- **Heat signatures**: HID lights produce substantial heat that can be detected by infrared cameras in some jurisdictions. Proper ventilation and insulation help mask thermal signatures.
- **Smell**: Flowering cannabis produces a strong odor that can travel through vents, windows, and shared walls. Use [[cannabis-odor-control-and-carbon-filters]] to manage this.
- **Noise**: Fans, pumps, and air filtration equipment create ambient noise. Choose quiet equipment or isolate it.
- **Visitors**: Never allow visitors into your grow space. Lock the room and keep it indistinguishable from other rooms.

## Outdoor Security

Outdoor grows face different challenges:

- **Visibility**: Plants can grow very tall, especially Sativa varieties. Use companion planting, fences, or natural barriers to screen them.
- **Access trails**: Avoid creating obvious paths to your grow site.
- **Harvest timing**: Plan harvests for times when foot traffic in the area is minimal.
- **Helicopter surveillance**: In some areas, law enforcement uses helicopters with thermal or visual detection. Dense canopy cover helps.
- See [[cannabis-guerrilla-growing-guide]] for remote growing techniques.

## Guerrilla Growing Security

For those who cannot safely grow at home, [[cannabis-guerrilla-growing-guide]] describes techniques for growing in remote, concealed locations. Key principles include:

- Choose sites far from trails, roads, and inhabited areas
- Visit only when necessary and vary your approach route
- Never leave any equipment or trash at the site
- Use natural camouflage and companion planting

## Pest Security

An often-overlooked security concern is biological:

- **NEVER bring an outdoor plant indoors** — this is the fastest way to introduce spider mites, which can destroy an entire indoor garden in days
- Always have a bottle of pesticide that kills spider mites on hand
- Quarantine new clones before introducing them to your grow room
- See [[cannabis-pest-management]] for comprehensive pest control

## Light Leaks

During the flowering cycle, even small light leaks during the dark period can cause stress and hermaphroditism:

- Seal all cracks around doors, windows, and vents
- Use light-proof materials for your grow room
- Check for light leaks by sitting in the dark grow room for several minutes to let your eyes adjust
- See [[cannabis-photoperiodism-and-light-cycles]] for why light discipline matters

## Digital Security

Modern growers must also consider digital footprints. Avoid posting photos of plants on social media, even in private groups. Purchasing equipment with cash avoids paper trails. Smart home devices (cameras, speakers) can inadvertently record evidence of cultivation. Encrypted messaging apps should be used for any grow-related communication. Some jurisdictions use electricity consumption data to identify suspected grow operations — energy-efficient lighting (LED systems) and solar supplementation reduce this risk significantly.
