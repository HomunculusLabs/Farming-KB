## Equatorial Photoperiod Early Flowering

Equatorial photoperiod early flowering is a genetic trait found in certain
cannabis [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]].

## The Photoperiod Mechanism

[[coleman-persephone-months-winter-day-length]] varies relatively little
throughout the year. At the equator, day length is approximately 12 hours
year-round with seasonal variation of less than 30 minutes. At 45-50 degrees
north, by contrast, day length swings from over 16 hours at summer solstice to
under 8 hours at winter solstice.

When equatorial landraces are relocated to higher latitudes, the dramatic
seasonal shift in day length causes them to perceive the "short days" of early
summer as a flowering signal. A plant that evolved to flower when days drop
below 12 hours may begin flowering in the northern hemisphere when days drop
to 14-15 hours, because this still represents significant rapid shortening from
the summer solstice peak. The rate of day-length change matters as much as the
absolute day length; near the equinoxes, day length changes most rapidly at
higher latitudes.

## Durban Poison

The most thoroughly documented equatorial photoperiod line is Durban Poison,
originating from the Durban region of South Africa at approximately 30 degrees
south latitude. In northern hemisphere outdoor cultivation, Durban Poison begins
flowering in late June and finishes in the second half of September, typically
around 8-9 weeks of flowering. This is remarkably early for a pure sativa and
places harvest well before the onset of coastal rains.

A strain that finishes in mid-September faces minimal weather risk in
Mediterranean climates, while a strain finishing in late November faces weeks of
increasing danger from rain, mold, and frost. Durban Poison is a pure landrace,
not a hybrid, carrying the full spectrum of equatorial photoperiod response
without dilution. Its early-flowering trait remains dominant in hybrid crosses;
the F1 generation reliably inherits the shortened flowering period, typically
flowering within 9-10 weeks regardless of the other parent's genetics.

## Brazilian White Sativa

The Brazilian White Sativa is another approximately 30-degree south latitude
landrace sharing the same photoperiod-driven [[soil-heritability-otu-sharing-white-widow-cross-soil-cannabis-endorhiza]]. It contributed not only early
flowering genetics but also heavy trichome production giving White Widow its
frosted appearance, while the father (reportedly a South Indian indica)
contributed density and yield.

The equatorial photoperiod trait addresses the biggest practical problem in
outdoor sativa cultivation: flowering time. Pure sativas can require 12-16
weeks or more of flowering, pushing harvest into late autumn and creating
