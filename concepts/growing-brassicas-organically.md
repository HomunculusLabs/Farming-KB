# Growing Brassicas Organically

The cabbage family (Brassica oleracea) includes cabbages, broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, kale, and kohlrabi. They are among the most important winter vegetables but are also the most vulnerable to [[growing-alliums-organically]] growers.

## Soil and Site

Brassicas need firm, well-consolidated soil. They prefer a slightly alkaline soil -- pH 6.5-7.5. If soil is acidic, lime in the fall before planting (not at the same time as manure application, as this releases ammonia). They do best in Plot C of a three-year rotation (following the legume plot, benefiting from the nitrogen released by the legume roots).

Dig the ground in fall and let the winter weather break it down. Do not add manure just before planting (too rich soil produces soft, disease-prone growth). Instead, manure the previous fall for the preceding crop, or apply a light dressing of blood, fish, and bone meal.

## Sowing and Planting

Most brassicas are raised in seed trays and transplanted. This produces strong plants better able to withstand clubroot and other soil-borne problems.

**Timing:** Sow spring cabbage in midsummer for planting in early fall. Sow summer/autumn cabbage in early spring. Sow winter cabbage in late spring. Brussels sprouts and cauliflower in early spring. Kale in late spring.

**Transplanting:** Harden off seedlings in a cold frame. Plant firmly -- press soil well around the roots. Space according to variety (closer for smaller types like kale, wider for Brussels sprouts).

## Coping with Clubroot

Clubroot is a crippling fungus disease causing distortion and swelling of roots. Once in the soil, it persists forever. There is no cure.

**Management strategies:**
- Raise seedlings in trays, pot on to 4in pots, grow until roots fill the pot (about 6 weeks), then plant out -- by this stage plants are strong enough to shrug off the disease
- Add lime to soil before planting
- Grow cabbage, kale, and Brussels sprouts (avoid cauliflower, which is most susceptible)
- Maintain good drainage
- Practice strict rotation (at least 4 years between brassica crops)

## Key Pests

**Cabbage white butterfly:** Larvae (caterpillars) strip foliage completely. Check undersides of leaves for egg clusters and remove. Pick off caterpillars regularly. Net crops with fine mesh to prevent egg-laying. Plant French marigolds between rows -- the scent masks the brassica smell.

**Cabbage root fly:** Larvae attack roots, stunting or killing plants. Protect with brassica collars (discs of felt or carpet around each plant stem at soil level).

**Aphids (cabbage aphid, mealy cabbage aphid):** Colonize growing tips and leaf undersides. Encourage hoverflies and ladybugs. Spray with insecticidal soap in severe cases.

**Pigeons:** Can strip entire crops in winter, especially when snow covers other food. Net with wire hoops and plastic netting. Secure netting at ground level.

**Caterpillars:** Hand-pick and drop in paraffin. Use a grease-coated board passed over rows to catch flea beetles that also attack brassica seedlings.

## Individual Crop Notes

**Cabbage:** Spring, summer, autumn, and winter types available. Each needs specific sowing time. Harvest when hearts are firm.

**Brussels sprouts:** Start from bottom of stem, picking when buttons are firm and about 1in diameter. Remove yellow leaves. Best after first frosts (improves flavor).

**Cauliflower:** Most demanding brassica. Needs constant growth -- any check causes "buttoning" (tiny curds). Blanch by tying outer leaves over the curd when it reaches 2in across.

**Broccoli:** Both sprouting types (purple and white, harvested over many weeks in spring) and calabrese (green heads, harvested in summer/autumn). Cut central head to encourage side shoots.

**Kale:** The hardiest brassica. Survives the toughest winters. Harvest leaves from the center outward. Very nutritious.

**Kohlrabi:** Grown for the swollen stem. Sow successionally for continuous harvest. Must be harvested when young (2-3in across) or becomes woody.

## Disease Prevention

Beyond clubroot, brassicas face several significant diseases in organic systems:
- **Downy mildew (Peronospora parasitica):** Grey-purple fungal growth on leaf undersides. Promoted by cool, damp conditions and overcrowding. Ensure adequate spacing (at least 18 inches for most cabbages) and good [[jadam-seed-treatment-and-nursery-management]] (50°C for 25 minutes) can reduce seed-borne infection.
