## Elevated CO2 Effects on Decomposition

Evidence for CO2 effects on litter quality and decomposition is mixed:

- De Angelis et al. (2000): Elevated CO2 increased C:N and lignin:N ratios
  of oak leaf litter in a Mediterranean forest, reducing decomposition
  constants by 5-8% in field experiments and 12.5% in microcosms.

- Cotrufo et al. (1998): Nitrogen content of leaves grown under elevated CO2
  decreased by 19-50% across species (50% in Castanea sativa, 19% in Acer
  platanoides). Reduced leaf quality stimulated grazing by isopods
  (Oniscus asellus and Porcellio scaber), reducing saprotrophic community
  activity.

- Cotrufo and Ineson (2000): Despite significant increases in C:N (45.6 to
  72.7) and lignin:N (16.3 to 22.4) ratios of beech twigs under elevated
  CO2, decomposition rates and N/lignin dynamics were unchanged.

- Gorisen and Cotrufo (2000): Increased C:N ratios of grassland leaf
  material did not correlate with decomposition rates, as additionally
  available carbon was translocated to roots (26% contribution vs. 6% from
  leaves during decomposition over 222 days).

Gifford et al. (2000) found mixed data on leaf chemistry changes, with a
general trend toward approximately 15% increase in C:N ratio under CO2
doubling, but no consensus for C:P ratio trends.
