## The Value of Ancient Varieties

Ancestral strains have qualities that modern varieties lack:

- They are adapted to local conditions through centuries of natural selection
- They possess genetic diversity that provides resilience against pests, diseases, and climate variation
- They are suited to [[fukuoka-critique-green-revolution]] paradigm:

- High-yielding varieties require chemical inputs that ancestral varieties do not
- Ancestral varieties maintain productivity over generations without degradation
- They support rather than undermine [[fukuoka-problems-darwin-evolution]], [[comparison-seed-balls-vs-direct-seeding]] and no-till methods are central to Fukuoka's philosophy
- Observing natural ecosystems provides the blueprint for sustainable food production
- Fukuoka challenged the fundamental assumptions of modern agricultural science

## Implications for Modern Agriculture

The principles demonstrated in Fukuoka's work have profound implications for
contemporary food systems. As soil degradation, water scarcity, and chemical
dependency threaten global food security, his methods offer a viable alternative.
Natural farming requires no external inputs, builds soil fertility over time,
and produces food with minimal environmental impact. These approaches are
increasingly relevant as [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]] practice
and helps practitioners move beyond techniques to genuine ecological literacy.
