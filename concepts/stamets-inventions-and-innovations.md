## Mycofiltration Membranes

Stamets developed practical methods for constructing mycofiltration membranes using readily available materials:

- Bark and crude wood chips inoculated with mycelium
- Burlap bags filled with myceliated substrate ("bunker spawn"), stacked like sandbags
- Sheet mulch beds 6 inches to 2 feet thick
- Spore slurries applied to contaminated sites

These membranes can filter pathogens, silt, and chemical toxins from water. The Skookum Inlet experiment demonstrated hundredfold coliform reduction using garden giant mycelial beds.

## Spored Oils for Chainsaws

Stamets proposes inoculating chainsaw bar oil, chipper lubricants, and other cutting equipment oils with mushroom spores. When trees are cut, the oil delivers spores directly to the cut surfaces, jump-starting decomposition by beneficial saprophytes and denying entry to parasitic pathogens. This is one of the [[mycorestoration-ecology]] itself -- the systematic use of fungi across four domains (filtration, forestry, remediation, pest control) to restore damaged environments. His vision of "customized mycological landscapes" populated with targeted fungal communities represents a new paradigm for ecological engineering.
