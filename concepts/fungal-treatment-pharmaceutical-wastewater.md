## Challenges and Considerations

- **Cost-effectiveness**: Under the variable conditions of pharmaceutical wastewater, it is necessary to develop stable and cost-effective treatment methods. The need for pH adjustment and potential for enzyme inhibition by pharmaceutical compounds must be considered.

- **Biodegradability assessment**: When researching and developing new drugs and synthetic methods, it is important to consider the biodegradability of discharges to wastewaters -- a principle that could be incorporated into green chemistry design frameworks.

- **Regulatory context**: Pharmaceutical waste is subject to environmental legislation regarding discharge of effluents. - **Toxicity reduction**: Beyond removing COD and BOD, fungal treatment must also reduce toxicity to acceptable levels. The multistage biofilm approach has shown better toxicity removal than single-stage systems.

## Research Recommendations

- **Treatability studies**: Batch and continuous laboratory-scale studies are recommended for specific compounds before pilot-scale testing.

- **Toxicity monitoring**: Regular toxicity testing of treated effluent is essential, as chemical analysis alone may not capture the biological activity of pharmaceutical residues.

- **Process stability**: Given the variability of pharmaceutical wastewater composition, treatment systems must be robust to changing influent characteristics.

## Antimicrobial Resistance and Pharmaceutical Wastes

A particularly concerning aspect of pharmaceutical waste is the contribution to antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in the environment. Antibiotics used as growth promoters in animal agriculture enter the environment through manure application and wastewater discharge. Sub-lethal concentrations of antibiotics in environmental waters can select for resistant bacterial populations, creating reservoirs of resistance genes that may transfer to human pathogens.

Fungal treatment systems that can degrade antibiotics before discharge offer a potential strategy for reducing this environmental AMR driver. The ligninolytic enzymes of white-rot fungi have shown activity against several antibiotic classes, though more research is needed on degradation pathways and residual biological activity.

## Green Chemistry and Pharmaceutical Design

An emerging approach to pharmaceutical waste management involves designing new drugs with inherent biodegradability from the outset. When researching and developing new drugs and synthetic methods, it is important to consider the biodegradability of discharges to wastewaters. This "benign by design" approach could reduce the need for end-of-pipe treatment by ensuring that pharmaceutical compounds degrade readily in conventional wastewater treatment systems or in the environment. Fungal biodegradation studies contribute to this effort by identifying structural features that promote or hinder biological degradation, informing the molecular design of more environmentally benign pharmaceuticals.
