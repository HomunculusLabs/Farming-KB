# 2C-T-8 — 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-cyclopropylmethylthiophenethylamine

## Overview

2C-T-8 is a member of the 2C-T family of [[alexander-shulgin]] as part of his systematic exploration of sulfur-containing
phenethylamine analogues. The compound demonstrated significant psychoactivity
at doses of 30–50 mg with a long duration of 10–15 hours, though reports were
highly variable — ranging from profoundly positive introspective experiences
to severely uncomfortable adverse reactions lasting over a week. This
unpredictability led Shulgin to conclude the cyclopropylmethyl substitution
was not compelling for the 2C-T series.

## Chemical Classification

2C-T-8 belongs to the 2C-T series of 2,5-dimethoxy-4-alkylthiophenethylamines.
The defining substitution places methoxy groups at the 2- and 5-positions of
the benzene ring, with a sulfur atom at the 4-position bearing a
cyclopropylmethyl group (-S-CH2-cyclopropyl). The cyclopropylmethyl moiety is
a strained three-membered ring attached to the sulfur via a methylene bridge.
The compound is a phenethylamine (two-carbon chain), distinguishing it from the
ALEPH series (three-carbon amphetamine homologues). Molecular formula:
C14H21NO2S. The cyclopropane ring introduces approximately 27 kcal/mol of ring
strain, influencing both conformational preferences and metabolic interactions.

## Synthesis

The synthesis follows the standard 2C-T route. 2,5-Dimethoxythiophenol is
alkylated with cyclopropylmethyl bromide in potassium hydroxide/methanol,
producing the thioether intermediate, which is extracted, washed, and
vacuum-distilled. Vilsmeier-Haack formylation (POCl3/N-methylformanilide)
introduces the aldehyde at the 4-position, yielding
2,5-dimethoxy-4-(cyclopropylmethylthio)benzaldehyde as white crystals
confirmed by proton NMR. Condensation with nitromethane and ammonium acetate
produces the beta-nitrostyrene as orange-yellow crystals. Final reduction with
LiAlH4/THF (H2SO4-moderated) gives the free base, which is distilled and
converted to the hydrochloride salt as white crystals. The ALEPH-8 amphetamine
counterpart was partially synthesized from the benzaldehyde intermediate but
not pursued.

## Dosage and Duration

Active dosage: 30–50 mg. Duration: 10–15 hours. Onset occurs within 45
minutes to 1.5 hours, with peak effects at 2–3 hours. The long duration
places it among the longer-acting 2C-T compounds, often extending into the
night and interfering with sleep.

## Qualitative Effects

At 30 mg: smooth onset, pleasant body warmth, good manual dexterity, easy
creative capacity. Described as non-overstimulating with mild sensual quality.
Subject was willing to try a higher dose.

At 40 mg (positive report): extraordinary experience beginning at 1.5 hours
with tremendous insight, [[mescaline]]'s oxygen atom, it was
"less appealing on the 2C-T-X sulfur atom." Compared to straight-chain
2C-T compounds (2C-T-2 ethylthio, 2C-T-7 heptylthio), the cyclopropylmethyl
group provides similar steric bulk but with fundamentally different
conformational freedom. The mixed results and abandoned ALEPH-8 development
suggest this substitution pattern does not reliably produce desirable profiles.

## Extensions and Commentary

Shulgin observed that "there are as many negatives as there are positives,"
and the substitution pattern is "not one to set the world on fire." The
ALEPH-8 amphetamine homologue was started (benzaldehyde + nitroethane) but not
pursued. Shulgin reflected that while cyclopropylmethyl was "quite something
on the mescaline oxygen atom," it generates "even less enthusiasm" for the
ALEPH series. He concluded: "That's the way it is, and who could have guessed!"
