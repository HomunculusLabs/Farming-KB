## Compost Tea and Soil Inoculation

Actively [[mushroom-cultivator-compost-pile-management]] takes effort
- Carting compost and mulches around and applying them is hard work for large gardens
- Compost and mulch take a while to reach the rhizosphere
- Neither mulch nor compost sticks to leaves

Compost teas are easy to apply to both soil and leaf surfaces and are put right where they are needed. They are a fast, inexpensive way to manage soil food web microbiology.

## What AACT Is Not

Do not confuse actively aerated compost tea with:
- **Compost leachate**: The liquid that oozes out of compost when pressed or when water runs through it. Leachates get color and may have some nutrient value but do little to impart microbial life.
- **Compost extracts**: Water extracts of compost.
- **Manure teas**: Made by soaking manure in water.

## Key Functions of Compost Tea

### Detoxification
Rule 15: Follow any chemical spraying or soil drenching with an application of compost tea. The microbes in the tea will immediately start to detoxify the soil by breaking down remaining chemicals and repopulating it. Repeat in a week.

### Foliar Protection
Plants generate exudates from their leaves, attracting bacteria and fungi to the phyllosphere (the area immediately around leaf surfaces). As in the rhizosphere, these microbes compete with pathogens for space and food. Compost tea can be sprayed directly on leaves to establish this protective microbiology.

### Soil Inoculation
Compost tea can introduce beneficial biology directly into the rhizosphere much faster than compost or mulch, which take time to decompose and release their organisms.

## Types of Tea

- **Bacterially dominated tea**: For vegetables, annuals, lawns, and grasses. Applied at approximately 5 gallons per acre for lawns.
- **Fungally dominated tea**: For trees, shrubs, and perennials. Applied as a soil drench.
- **Balanced tea**: For transitional plants and general use.

## Application Methods

### Lawns
- Traveling sprinkler with inline fertilizer dispenser filled with tea
- Concrete sprayers for small areas
- Gas blower on lowest setting (can mist an acre in 5-10 minutes, spray up into 30-foot trees)

### Transplants
- Soak transplants in aerated compost tea before planting
- Spray compost tea on seeds before planting
- Apply a soil drench after germination

### Diseased or Stressed Plants
Any plants showing signs of disease or stress should be immediately sprayed with compost tea followed by a soil drench.
