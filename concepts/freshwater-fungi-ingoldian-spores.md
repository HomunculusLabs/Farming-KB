### Aero-Aquatic Fungi

Aero-aquatic fungi produce conidia or spore-bearing structures that trap air bubbles, enabling them to float at the water surface and colonize surface films and foam. They are typically found in:

- Stagnant or slow-moving water
- Leaf litter in ponds and marshes
- Tree holes and water-filled cavities

### True Aquatic vs. Terrestrial Input

The distinction between truly aquatic fungi and terrestrial fungi entering aquatic systems is important:

- **Obligate freshwater fungi**: Complete life cycle in water; many cannot grow on standard mycological media
- **Facultative freshwater fungi**: Primarily terrestrial but able to grow and sporulate in water
- **Terrestrial transients**: Fungal propagules washed into streams; may germinate but not complete life cycle

## Ecological Roles

### Decomposition

Freshwater fungi are the primary decomposers of [[ascomycota-phylogeny-major-classes-lineages]]**: The majority of described freshwater species; include both sexual and asexual stages
- **[[chytridiomycota-phylogeny-diversity]]**: Important parasites of algae and other aquatic organisms
- **Oomycota**: Include significant pathogens (Saprolegnia, Aphanomyces) and saprobes

### Conidial Morphology

Ingoldian spore types include:
- Tetraradiate (four arms): e.g., Tricladium, Tetracladium, Flagellospora
- Sigmoid (curved): e.g., Anguillospora, Lemonniera, Trichocladium
- Branched: Various patterns of branching and appendage formation
- Filiform (threadlike): Long slender conidia adapted for flowing water
