# Aquatic Fungi and Zoospore Biology
Aquatic fungi represent a diverse and ecologically critical group of
microorganisms that live and reproduce in freshwater habitats. They
range from the spectacular [[ingoldian-hyphomycetes-spore-dispersal-in-water]], whose ornate spores
have been compared to jewels, to the destructive [[bloomfield-pythium-oomycete-water-molds]]
that cause devastating diseases of fish, crops, and mammals.
The study of aquatic fungi illuminates fundamental principles of fungal
biology, including the mechanisms of [[spore-dispersal]] in water, the
evolution of swimming cells, and the [[bloomfield-fungal-reproduction-and-chemical-communication]] systems
that guide these organisms to their hosts.

## Ingoldian Hyphomycetes

In 1938, Cecil [[terence-ingold-mycologist]] discovered a previously unknown group of
aquatic fungi while examining foam collected from a brook near his
home in Leicester. The foam, generated by fatty detergent-like
substances released from decaying leaves, was trapping and
concentrating remarkable spores of incomparable beauty.
These conidia (asexual spores) come in an extraordinary variety of
shapes:
- **Star-shaped spores**: thin limbs radiating from a central hub
- **Crescent-shaped spores**: curved like a crescent moon
- **Sigmoid spores**: elongated S-forms twisted into helices
- **Compound forms**: combinations of features resembling balloon
  animal sculptures
Ingold's discovery is considered comparable in significance to the
first scientific reports of African megafauna or Australian marsupials.
A wholly new type of fungus had been found, a rich mine of species
that had always, but until then invisibly, fashioned their spores in
creeks and streams on every continent.
More than 300 species of Ingoldian fungi have since been identified
across every continent, generating over 1,000 scientific publications.

### Ecological Function

In late fall and early winter, [[freshwater-fungi-ingoldian-spores]] reach concentrations
of 20,000 per liter of water. Their diverse shapes are adaptations for
dispersal by water currents: the appendages increase surface area and
drag, improving the likelihood of settling on submerged leaves.
As the spores flow downstream, they colonize submerged leaves and spawn
mycelia that soften and macerate the plant tissues by secreting
cellulose-degrading enzymes. This decomposition is a critical process
in freshwater nutrient cycling.
Henry Descals, an authority on Ingoldian fungi, has been working on a
definitive monograph of the group for more than twenty years. To isolate
spores from the surface of decaying leaves, he plucks his long
eyelashes and glues them to cocktail sticks, using the strong pointed
tips as perfect instruments for manipulating these microscopic jewels.

## Oomycete Water Molds

Oomycetes (water molds) are stramenopile organisms, only distantly
related to true fungi (Kingdom Fungi), but traditionally studied by
mycologists due to their hyphal growth form, absorptive nutrition, and
spore production.
Their cell walls contain cellulose microfibrils (staining blue with
iodine, unlike true fungi), and their membranes lack ergosterol, making
standard antifungal drugs ineffective against them.

### The Saprolegniaceae Family

The Saprolegniaceae include *Saprolegnia*, *Achlya*, *Thraustotheca*,
and *Dictyuchus*. Their hyphae are among the most beautiful in
microscopy, expanding to several tenths of a millimeter in diameter and
sparkling with parallel streams of organelles visible through their
translucent cytoplasm.
These fungi were among the first to be observed causing disease in
vertebrates, described by William Arderon of the Royal Society in 1748
as filaments radiating from an infected fish. *Saprolegnia* infections
continue to devastate farmed fish and amphibian populations worldwide.

## Zoospore Biology

Zoospores are motile, flagellated spores that represent one of the
most ancient innovations in the fungal and stramenopile lineages. All
fungi are believed to have evolved from flagellated ancestors, and
swimming spores are likely more ancient inventions than branched
mycelia.

### Sporangial Emptying Mechanisms

Oomycetes use internal pressure to discharge zoospores from swollen
hyphal tips called sporangia. The Saprolegniaceae display a probable
evolutionary transition series in their sporangial emptying mechanisms:
**Stage 1 (Saprolegnia-type)**: Primary spores (pip-shaped, with two
anterior flagella) are shot from the sporangium through an exit tube.
They swim briefly, encyst, and re-emerge as kidney-shaped secondary
zoospores (with two lateral flagella) that swim twice as fast.
**Stage 2 (Achlya-type)**: The primary swimming stage is eliminated.
Cysts are discharged directly from the sporangium through a nozzle,
then release secondary zoospores externally.
**Stage 3 (Advanced forms)**: Nozzle-mediated emptying is replaced by
wall fracture and internal cyst formation, with secondary spores
released through multiple exit tubes.
The transition between these mechanisms may require surprisingly simple
genetic changes. A single mutation preventing flagellar elongation
could convert a Saprolegnia-type sporangium into an Achlya-type.

### Osmoregulation

Zoospores lack cell walls and will burst unless they continuously expel
excess water absorbed by osmosis. A contractile vacuole accomplishes
this at a rate of once every 5 to 6 seconds.
When zoospores stop swimming and settle on a surface, they cease
pumping water, immediately puff up into spherical cysts, and assemble a
protective cell wall. Once pressurized, the cyst acts as a drilling
rig and pushes a slender hypha into the underlying surface.

### Chemotaxis and Galvanotaxis

Zoospores are equipped with sophisticated sensory systems that enable
them to locate suitable substrates for colonization:
- **Chemotaxis**: homing in on sugars and amino acids exuding from
  wounded plant or animal tissues
- **Galvanotaxis**: detecting weak electrical currents generated by
  the ion pumps of living cells
The electrical signature of healthy roots differs from that of wounded
ones, and zoospores respond to these different cues depending on their
host preferences.
