## Psilocybin Mushrooms

## Overview

Psilocybin mushrooms, known to the Aztecs as Teonanacatl ("flesh of the
gods"), represent one of the most culturally significant groups of
hallucinogenic fungi in human history. Found primarily in Mexico and

  studied by Hofmann. Found in subtropical grasslands of Mexico.

the dried mushroom. For [[maria-sabina-and-mazatec-mushroom-ceremony]] ceremonies, bringing
Teonanacatl to global attention. Subsequently, Roger Heim identified
and cultivated the species, and Albert Hofmann isolated psilocybine and
psilocine in pure crystalline form. The Aztec statue of Xochipilli, the
ecstatic Prince of Flowers, includes stylized glyphs depicting mushroom
caps, confirming their ancient ceremonial importance.

## Modern Research and Legal Status

With the active principles available in pure form, psilocybine and
psilocine became valuable aids to experimental psychiatry and
psychotherapy. The pure compounds allow pharmacological study under
reproducible conditions, which was not possible with the original
mushrooms due to their variable potency.

Modern research has renewed interest in psilocybin for treating
depression, anxiety, and end-of-life distress. Psilocybin remains
classified as a Schedule I substance in many countries, though clinical
trials and decriminalization efforts are advancing rapidly.
