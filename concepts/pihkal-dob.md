# DOB

## Overview

DOB (4-bromo-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine) is one of the most potent and
longest-acting [[psychedelic-substances-comparison]].

Its extraordinary profile has made DOB both a valuable pharmacological
research tool and a significant [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]]
assays.

## Chemical Structure and Classification

DOB's IUPAC name is 1-(4-bromo-2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)propan-2-amine. It
belongs to the DO family of substituted amphetamines, with methoxy groups at
positions 2 and 5 and a bromine atom at position 4. The bromine is directly
responsible for its exceptional potency.

The DO family follows the halogen trend observed in the 2C series: heavier
halogens increase potency and duration. DOB sits alongside DOI (iodine), DOC
(chlorine), and DOM (methyl). DOB is the alpha-methylated version of 2C-B,
with the alpha-methyl group dramatically increasing potency (milligram to
sub-milligram range) and duration (4 to 8 hours to 18 to 30 hours).

The alpha-methyl group confers resistance to MAO metabolism, the key factor
behind DOB's extraordinary duration of action.

## Dosage and Administration

Shulgin's recommended oral dosage is 1 to 3 milligrams, with 1 to 2 mg
considered standard. This exceptionally low range underscores DOB's status
as one of the most potent orally-active psychedelics. A fraction of a
milligram above the recommended range can produce dramatically more intense
effects.

Onset occurs within 1 to 3 hours, peaking at 4 to 6 hours. Total duration
ranges from 18 to 30 hours, with perceptual changes possible for up to 36
hours. Insufflation at 0.5 to 1 mg shortens onset but not overall duration
and is extremely caustic.

## Effects Profile

DOB produces intense psychedelic effects comparable to high-dose LSD.
Visual effects include complex layered geometric patterns, intense color
shifting, spatial perception alterations, and fully immersive hallucinations
at higher doses. The visual intensity is among the most pronounced of any
phenethylamine.

Cognitive effects include deep philosophical introspection, [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]],
and profound contemplation. The extended duration amplifies both positive and
difficult psychological content. Physical effects include pronounced
stimulation, elevated heart rate and blood pressure, pupil dilation, appetite
loss, and persistent insomnia. Vasoconstriction and muscle tension are common.

## Shulgin's Commentary

Shulgin regarded DOB with scientific appreciation and caution. In PIHKAL,
he documented its remarkable potency and duration while emphasizing the need
for extreme care in dosing. He noted DOB was "not for the casual
experimenter" and demanded both experience and careful preparation.

He was particularly impressed by DOB's visual richness, describing it as
among the most complex and beautiful in his collection, with a unique
quality attributed to the bromine substituent's influence on receptor
binding. He also noted its value as a pharmacological research tool for
studying 5-HT2A receptor mechanisms.
