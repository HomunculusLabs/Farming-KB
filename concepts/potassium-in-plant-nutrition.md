# Potassium in Plant Nutrition
**Updated:** 2026-05-09

## Overview
Potassium (K) is one of three primary macronutrients required by plants,
alongside nitrogen and phosphorus. Unlike N and P, potassium is not
incorporated into organic molecules. It functions as a soluble ion (K+)
that regulates water movement, enzyme activation, stomatal control, and
carbohydrate transport. K is often called the "quality nutrient" because
its adequacy directly affects fruit size, disease resistance, and shelf
life.

## Potassium's Role in Plant Physiology
Potassium activates over 60 enzymes involved in photosynthesis, protein
synthesis, and starch production. It is the most abundant cation in
plant cells and maintains cell turgor pressure -- the internal water
pressure keeping plants upright and driving cell expansion. K+ ions
regulate stomata: potassium accumulation in guard cells opens stomata
for gas exchange; its departure closes them to conserve water. This
makes K critical for both photosynthetic efficiency and drought
tolerance.

## Potassium and Drought Tolerance
Adequate K improves plant water-use efficiency through precise stomatal
regulation. K-sufficient plants close stomata earlier during stress,
reducing transpiration. K also maintains root hydraulic conductivity by
regulating aquaporin function. Deep-rooted crops with good K status
access water from lower soil horizons more effectively. In permaculture
systems, managing K through [[biochar]]
helps buffer plants against dry periods.

## Potassium and Disease Resistance
K strengthens cell walls, promotes thicker cuticles, and enhances
defensive compounds (phytoalexins, phenolics) in response to pathogens.
K-deficient plants have thinner cell walls, higher tissue sugars
attracting aphids and fungi, and slower wound-healing. Well-supplied
plants show reduced powdery mildew, rust, botrytis, and bacterial
diseases. K also improves frost tolerance by maintaining membrane
integrity at low temperatures.

## Potassium Deficiency Symptoms
Deficiency appears first on older leaves because K is mobile and
translocated to young tissue. Symptoms include marginal chlorosis
(yellowing at edges) progressing to marginal necrosis (brown scorching).
In severe cases, leaves curl downward, stems are weak and lodging-prone,
and fruit is small with poor colour. Potato tubers from K-deficient
plants have reduced specific gravity and higher bruising susceptibility.

clover), and composted manure. [[plant-available-nitrogen-ammonium-nitrate]].
