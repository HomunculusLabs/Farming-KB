## Dick's Valis and the Nag Hammadi Burial

In one of his final novels, Valis (1981), Philip K. Dick proposed that a creature of pure
information — the Logos — had been buried at Nag Hammadi alongside the Chenoboskion
Library around 370 CE. As static information preserved in papyrus codices, it existed in
a state of dormancy for over fifteen centuries. The Nag Hammadi library, discovered in
1945, contained Gnostic texts including the Gospel of Thomas, the Apocryphon of John, and
the Hypostasis of the Archons — works that presented radical alternatives to orthodox
Christian cosmology.

McKenna appropriated Dick's framework to argue that the Logos remained dormant as
physical text until 1947, when the texts were translated and read. "As soon as people
had the information in their minds, the symbiote came alive." The critical transition was
from static inscription to active cognition. The Logos, conceived as a living information
system, could not function while confined to unread manuscripts. It required human minds
to serve as its substrate.
