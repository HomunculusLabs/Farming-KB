# Mollison Arid Lands Design and Erosion Control

Mollison's Chapter 5 covers practical design for arid lands, including water systems, erosion control, sewage recycling, and brod-scale planting strategies.

## Water Systems

### Ideal Water-Garden Relationship
- Any slope advantage is ideal — settlements can dam rocky clefts by hand
- Overflow goes to larger swimming pools
- Pipes lead water over 1200 m to dwelling areas
- Showers can be sited in gardens and moved as trees establish around them

### Windmills
- Petrol-free, very effective for raising water to height
- Allows growing tropical crops above the frost line on hill slopes
- Gears lubricate with castor oil or jojoba oil (which grows well in arid areas)
- Frost protection from A. albida and Paulownia enables guava, pawpaw, and mango on foothills

### Tank and Dam Management
- Uncovered tanks produce green algae and mosquitoes — controlled by goldfish, grass carp, and freshwater mussels
- Native snails provide grit for ducks
- Small fish in standing water provide mosquito control

## Sewage and Sullage

### Safe Disposal
- Safest disposal: pipes or trenches below plants
- Lagoons are ideal sites for dates, plums, and peaches (banks and surrounding seepage areas)
- Sludge from septic tanks: pre-dug planting holes, filled over, plant dates, mulberry, or fig
- Grapes bear fruit from cuttings in 18 months in arid climates using this method

### Greywater Integration
- Shower water led to slotted pipe drains under shadehouse or garden
- Washing-up, shower, bath water containing soap, led under mulch, is "a benefit, not a nuisance"
- "It is a matter of integrating the garden with the waste water from washing processes"

## Erosion Control on Dry Slopes

### Net and Pan Pattern
Effective control in overgrazed, eroded, mined, or bulldozed sites:
- "Pans" can be made from tyres, filled with mulch
- Diversion drains led above tread level
- Logs staked cross-slope on slight downhill grade make water zig-zag across erosion face
- Even small logs and branches pegged across erosion channels build up "layer-cake of silt and leaves"
- Willow, ti-tree, acacia, or other fibrous-rooted hardy species planted alongside act as permanent silt traps

### Steep Slopes
- Plant pampas, bamboo, and root-mat pioneers
- Upslope plantings of chestnut, acacia, carob, olive cascade seed downslope over time
- Chisel ploughs with net and pan pattern effective where machinery available

### Critique of Heavy-Handed Approaches
Massive contour trenches allow little soil absorption, create dry strips, and expose subsoil. "Such heavy-handed approaches need massive machinery, and achieve little compared with planned chiselling and planting, which makes a permanent and stable change."

## Broadscale Planting

### Species for Arid Sites
Date palm, jujube, cork oak, pistachio, plum, white cedar, tamarisk, sweet chestnut, honey locust, carob, mesquite, paulownia, plus cuttings of grape, fig, mulberry, and coprosma.

### Runoff Harvesting
Clay pans, playa, domes, bare rocky ground, and Piedmont slopes can be graded to lead all runoff water to small, chisel-ploughed areas. Even road drains can be led to walled enclosures or directly planted to trees.

### Catchment Ratios
Any area of runoff should be in the ratio of 16-20:1 — a dome of 8 hectares led to a garden of 4000 m2 (1 acre).

## Termite-Proofing
- Cold-soak (butt-soak) treatment with Tanalith (copper-chrome-arsenic salts) for fence and trellis
- Essential technique — termites eat valuable posts in a few years
- Treated timber safe to handle but never burn (toxic fumes and residues)
- Three-day soak in hot weather suffices
