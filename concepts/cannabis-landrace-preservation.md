# Cannabis Landrace Preservation

## Overview

Cannabis landraces are naturally evolved, locally adapted populations of cannabis that have been growing in specific geographic regions for centuries or millennia. These populations developed their distinctive characteristics through natural selection in response to local climate, soil conditions, day-length patterns, and human selection by indigenous communities. The Cannabis Sativa series documents numerous landrace-derived strains, including Colombian Gold, Acapulco Gold, Thai, Jamaican, Swazi, Panama, and Malawi varieties, underscoring the importance of these genetics as the foundation of all modern cannabis breeding.

## What Defines a Landrace

A true cannabis landrace is distinguished from modern hybrids by several characteristics:

- **Geographic specificity**: The population is associated with a particular region, valley, or even village. Examples include the Hindu Kush mountains, the Mazar-i-Sharif region of Afghanistan, the Rif Mountains of Morocco, the hills of Kerala in South India, and the valleys of Jamaica's interior.
- **Genetic homogeneity within heterogeneity**: A landrace population is not genetically uniform like a stabilized modern cultivar. Instead, it contains genetic variation that allows the population to adapt to local conditions. Individual plants within a landrace may vary in height, flowering time, aroma, and potency, but they share a recognizable overall phenotype.
- **Local adaptation**: The population has been shaped by the specific climate and growing conditions of its home region. Tropical landraces are adapted to equatorial day-length patterns, while Central Asian landraces are adapted to continental climates with extreme temperature variation.
- **Cultural integration**: Most cannabis landraces have been cultivated by local communities for traditional purposes — medicine, spiritual practice, textile production, or food — for generations.

## Major Landrace Regions

### Southeast Asia

Southeast Asia is the source of some of the most potent and complex sativa landraces:

- **Thai (Thailand)**: Perhaps the most famous sativa landrace. Thai Stick — buds tied to bamboo sticks for transport — became legendary in the 1960s and 1970s. Thai landraces are characterized by extremely long flowering times (14–20 weeks), intense cerebral effects, and sweet, spicy aromas. The genetic contribution of Thai to modern breeding (particularly through Haze) is immeasurable.
- **Laotian and Cambodian**: Similar to Thai but with regional variations. Often taller and with slightly different [[cannabis-terpene-profiles]] emphasizing earthy, woody notes.
- **Vietnamese**: Less well-documented but historically significant. Vietnamese landraces contributed to the development of strains like Vietnamese Black and various [[natural-farming-guide-seed-saving-and-heirloom-varieties]] preserved by soldiers returning from the Vietnam War.

### South and Central America

- **Colombian**: Colombian Gold and Colombian Red were among the most important landraces in the development of modern cannabis. Colombian genetics contributed vigor, yield, and sweet, fruity aromas to countless hybrids. The Santa Marta region produced particularly renowned varieties.
- **Mexican**: Acapulco Gold and various Oaxacan and Michoacan landraces were the primary cannabis available in the United States during the 1960s. These fast-flowering, moderate-potency sativas provided the genetic foundation for early American breeding programs.
- **Panama**: Panama Red and related Panamanian landraces were prized for their red-tinged pistils, potent cerebral effects, and relatively short flowering time for a tropical sativa.
- **Jamaican**: Jamaican landraces (often called "Lamb's Bread" or "Collie Weed") are sativas adapted to the tropical Caribbean climate. They tend to be moderate in height, with a flowering time of 10–12 weeks, and produce an uplifting, creative high. [[oner-jamaican-dream-and-jamaican-lions]] related strains derive from these genetics.

### Africa

- **Malawi**: Malawi Gold is one of Africa's most legendary landraces. Grown in the Central Region plateau of Malawi, it is a tall, vigorous sativa with a 12–16 week flowering time and intensely psychoactive effects. It is traditionally cured by pressing into blocks for transport and aging.
- **Swazi**: From Swaziland (now Eswatini), this African sativa is adapted to high-altitude growing conditions. It flowers in 10–12 weeks and produces resinous, aromatic buds with a fruity, earthy profile. The Swazi strain documented in the Cannabis Sativa series represents one of the better-preserved African landraces.
- **Durban Poison**: Originally from the port city of Durban, South Africa. A pure sativa landrace that has become one of the most commercially successful landrace strains, valued for its resin production, sweet anise aroma, and focused, energetic effects.
- **Kilimanjaro**: From the slopes of Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania. A rare African landrace adapted to high altitude and intense UV exposure. Traditionally used by indigenous peoples for spiritual ceremonies.

### Central and South Asia

- **Hindu Kush**: The namesake of the entire "kush" family. An indica landrace from the mountains of Afghanistan and Pakistan, characterized by short stature, broad leaves, dense resin production, and sedative effects. The foundation of virtually all modern indica varieties.
- **Mazar-i-Sharif**: A distinct Afghan landrace from the Mazar-i-Sharif region, known for producing some of the world's finest hashish. Plants are heavily resin-coated with a distinctive earthy, hashy aroma.
- **Kerala (South India)**: A tall sativa landrace adapted to the tropical monsoon climate of southern India. Used traditionally in religious and spiritual contexts. South Indian genetics appear in several strains documented in the Cannabis Sativa series.

## Threats to Landrace Survival

### Hybridization

The most significant threat to cannabis landraces is genetic contamination from modern hybrids. As improved hybrid varieties are introduced to traditional growing regions, they cross with local landraces, diluting the unique genetic combinations that have developed over centuries. In Jamaica, Thailand, and Colombia, pure landraces are increasingly difficult to find due to decades of hybrid seed introduction.

### Eradication Programs

Government-sponsored cannabis eradication programs have destroyed landrace populations in many regions. The spraying of paraquat on Mexican cannabis in the 1970s, ongoing eradication efforts in Colombia and other producing countries, and the destruction of wild cannabis populations all contribute to genetic loss.

### Habitat Destruction

Urbanization, agricultural conversion, and deforestation destroy the natural habitats where landrace populations grow wild or semi-wild. Once these populations are gone, the unique genetic combinations they represent are lost permanently.

### Market Pressure

Commercial demand for high-THC, fast-flowering hybrids creates economic pressure to replace traditional landraces with modern varieties. Farmers in traditional growing regions often have strong economic incentives to grow hybrids rather than preserve landraces.

## Preservation Efforts

### Seed Banks and Collections

Several organizations and individuals maintain collections of landrace genetics:

- **ACE Seeds** (Spain): Maintains one of the most comprehensive landrace collections, with genetics from Thailand, Laos, Colombia, Panama, Malawi, India, and other regions. They actively breed with landraces to create stabilized varieties that preserve landrace traits.
- **World of Seeds**: Spanish seed bank specializing in landrace and landrace-based varieties from around the world.
- **Strain Hunters**: A project by Green House Seed Co. that conducted expeditions to remote cannabis-growing regions to collect and preserve landrace genetics.
- **Individual breeders**: Breeders like Tom Hill, DJ Short, and others maintain private collections of landrace genetics and work to preserve them through careful breeding.

### In-Situ Conservation

The most effective long-term preservation strategy is maintaining landraces in their native growing environments, where they can continue to adapt and evolve. This requires:

- Supporting traditional farming communities that maintain landrace cultivation
- Establishing protected areas where wild cannabis populations can persist
- Documenting traditional cultivation knowledge and practices
- Providing economic incentives for landrace preservation through premium pricing or certification programs

### Breeding with Landraces

Responsible breeders can contribute to landrace preservation by:

- Using landrace genetics as parents in breeding programs while maintaining pure landrace lines
- Creating stabilized hybrids that express landrace traits, increasing the genetic diversity available to growers
- Sharing landrace genetics with other breeders and collections to reduce the risk of loss from any single source
- Documenting the provenance and characteristics of landrace accessions thoroughly

### Ethical Considerations in Landrace Use

Breeders working with landrace genetics face ethical questions:

- **Biopiracy**: Taking landrace genetics from [[low-cost-mushroom-growing-house-construction-developing-countries]] without compensation or acknowledgment raises concerns about intellectual property and traditional knowledge. The communities that developed and maintained these genetics over centuries rarely benefit from the commercial products derived from them.
- **Appropriation vs. appreciation**: There is a fine line between respectfully working with landrace genetics and marketing them in ways that exploit or misrepresent their cultural origins. Breeders should acknowledge the geographic and cultural origins of their genetics.
- **Genetic ownership**: Cannabis genetics exist in a legal gray area. While patents have been filed on specific stabilized varieties, the broader genetic heritage of landraces belongs to no single entity and arguably to the communities that developed them.

## Landrace Genetics in Modern Strains

Many of the strains documented in Cannabis Sativa Vol. 3 carry significant landrace ancestry:

- **Colombian Gold**: Direct landrace or very close descendant
- **Acapulco Gold**: Mexican landrace with some hybridization
- **Wild Thailand**: Direct Thai landrace
- **Swazi**: Direct Swazi landrace
- **Jamaican Dream**: Jamaican landrace crossed with indica
- **King Congo**: Congolese landrace
- **Panama Black**: Panamanian landrace
- **Rio Negro Colombian Sativa**: Direct [[oner-colombian-landrace-sativa-strains]] from the Rio Negro region
- **South Indian**: Direct South Indian landrace
