# Cannabis Landrace Preservation

## Overview

Cannabis landraces are naturally evolved, locally adapted populations of cannabis that have been growing in specific geographic regions for centuries or millennia. These populations developed their distinctive characteristics through natural selection in response to local climate, soil conditions, day-length patterns, and human selection by indigenous communities. The Cannabis Sativa series documents numerous landrace-derived strains, including Colombian Gold, Acapulco Gold, Thai, Jamaican, Swazi, Panama, and Malawi varieties, underscoring the importance of these genetics as the foundation of all modern cannabis breeding.

## What Defines a Landrace

A true cannabis landrace is distinguished from modern hybrids by several characteristics:

- **Geographic specificity**: The population is associated with a particular region, valley, or even village. Examples include the Hindu Kush mountains, the Mazar-i-Sharif region of Afghanistan, the Rif Mountains of Morocco, the hills of Kerala in South India, and the valleys of Jamaica's interior.
- **Genetic homogeneity within heterogeneity**: A landrace population is not genetically uniform like a stabilized modern cultivar. Instead, it contains genetic variation that allows the population to adapt to local conditions. Individual plants within a landrace may vary in height, flowering time, aroma, and potency, but they share a recognizable overall phenotype.
- **Local adaptation**: The population has been shaped by the specific climate and growing conditions of its home region. Tropical landraces are adapted to equatorial day-length patterns, while Central Asian landraces are adapted to continental climates with extreme temperature variation.
- **Cultural integration**: Most cannabis landraces have been cultivated by local communities for traditional purposes — medicine, spiritual practice, textile production, or food — for generations.

## Major Landrace Regions

### Southeast Asia

Southeast Asia is the source of some of the most potent and complex sativa landraces:

- **Thai (Thailand)**: Perhaps the most famous sativa landrace. Thai Stick — buds tied to bamboo sticks for transport — became legendary in the 1960s and 1970s. Thai landraces are characterized by extremely long flowering times (14–20 weeks), intense cerebral effects, and sweet, spicy aromas. The genetic contribution of Thai to modern breeding (particularly through Haze) is immeasurable.
- **Laotian and Cambodian**: Similar to Thai but with regional variations. Often taller and with slightly different [[natural-farming-guide-seed-saving-and-heirloom-varieties]] preserved by soldiers returning from the Vietnam War.

### South and Central America

- **Colombian**: Colombian Gold and Colombian Red were among the most important landraces in the development of modern cannabis. Colombian genetics contributed vigor, yield, and sweet, fruity aromas to countless hybrids. The Santa Marta region produced particularly renowned varieties.
- **Mexican**: Acapulco Gold and various Oaxacan and Michoacan landraces were the primary cannabis available in the United States during the 1960s. These fast-flowering, moderate-potency sativas provided the genetic foundation for early American breeding programs.
- **Panama**: Panama Red and related Panamanian landraces were prized for their red-tinged pistils, potent cerebral effects, and relatively short flowering time for a tropical sativa.
- **Jamaican**: Jamaican landraces (often called "Lamb's Bread" or "Collie Weed") are sativas adapted to the tropical Caribbean climate. They tend to be moderate in height, with a flowering time of 10–12 weeks, and produce an uplifting, creative high. The communities that developed and maintained these genetics over centuries rarely benefit from the commercial products derived from them.
- **Appropriation vs. appreciation**: There is a fine line between respectfully working with landrace genetics and marketing them in ways that exploit or misrepresent their cultural origins. Breeders should acknowledge the geographic and cultural origins of their genetics.
- **Genetic ownership**: Cannabis genetics exist in a legal gray area. While patents have been filed on specific stabilized varieties, the broader genetic heritage of landraces belongs to no single entity and arguably to the communities that developed them.

## Landrace Genetics in Modern Strains

Many of the strains documented in Cannabis Sativa Vol. 3 carry significant landrace ancestry:

- **Colombian Gold**: Direct landrace or very close descendant
- **Acapulco Gold**: Mexican landrace with some hybridization
- **Wild Thailand**: Direct Thai landrace
- **Swazi**: Direct Swazi landrace
- **Jamaican Dream**: Jamaican landrace crossed with indica
- **King Congo**: Congolese landrace
- **Panama Black**: Panamanian landrace
- **Rio Negro Colombian Sativa**: Direct [[oner-colombian-landrace-sativa-strains]] from the Rio Negro region
- **South Indian**: Direct South Indian landrace
