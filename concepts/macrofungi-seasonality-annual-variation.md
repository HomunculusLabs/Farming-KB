## Annual Variation -- A Major Challenge

Annual variation in sporocarp presence can be enormous:

- **Olympic National Park:** Only 5-20% of ectomycorrhizal species
  fruited in 2 successive years across eight sites. About 30% were
  detected in both years combined.
- **European studies:** 8-88% of total reported species from a single
  site were detected in any given year (Winterhoff, cited in Arnolds
  1992).
- **Puerto Rico (Lodge 1996):** Several Entolomataceae species fruited
  every second or third year; a few were found only during 1 year of
  a 13-year survey.
- **Chicago region (Schmit et al. 1999):** Significant annual
  difference in species composition during a 3-year study.
- **Switzerland (Straatsma et al. 2001):** In a 21-year study, some
  species fruited only during one particularly rich year. Species
  richness estimators did not stabilize during the entire 21 years.

This extreme annual variation means that single-year inventories
severely underestimate total species richness. Multi-year studies
consistently reveal species not detected in previous years,
underscoring the need for long-term commitment in [[macrofungal-fruiting-phenology-interannual-variation]] in many temperate
regions. Autumn fruiting seasons have extended later into the year in
several European studies, and some spring-fruiting species are appearing
earlier. These phenological shifts have implications for the timing of
survey efforts and may disrupt [[ectomycorrhizal-host-tree-partnerships]]. Drought conditions can suppress
fruiting for months or years, even when temperatures are otherwise
favorable.
