# PF TEK Secondary Flush and Cake Exhaustion Lifecycle

The PF TEK describes a predictable and well defined lifecycle for the fungus cake from spore inoculation through fruiting exhaustion. Understanding this lifecycle helps cultivators set realistic yield expectations, optimize their harvest timing, and know when to retire spent cakes versus when to attempt secondary flushes.

## Complete Lifecycle Timeline

The PF TEK provides a clear timeline from the moment of spore inoculation to the end of the fruiting cycle. Each phase has a characteristic duration and visual indicators:

1. **Spore inoculation to spore germination:** Within one week at 70 degrees Fahrenheit. Germinating spores appear as small white fuzzy spots that quickly grow and spread with cottony white growth and strandy rhizomorphs.
2. **Spore germination to complete colonization:** Approximately 2 to 3 weeks. The mycelium spreads through the substrate until the entire cake surface turns white.
3. **Complete colonization to fruiting cycle start:** Within 2 weeks after surface colonization. The mycelium continues infiltrating the substrate until sufficient nutrients are accumulated to trigger fruiting. Primordia (tiny reddish headed structures) begin to appear.
4. **Fruiting cycle duration:** About 2 weeks for the main flush. This is the primary harvest window.

The entire process from spore inoculation to harvest takes approximately 4 to 6 weeks.

## Pinning and Primordia Development

After the substrate surface is fully colonized with white mycelium (typically 2 to 3 weeks post inoculation), the jars are left to sit in indirect light. The mycelium continues to infiltrate deeper into the substrate until it accumulates sufficient nutritional resources to trigger the fruiting cycle.

Within a week to a few weeks after surface colonization, distinct developmental stages appear sequentially on the cake surface:

- **White pin structures:** Tiny white pin like formations marking the onset of pinning. This is the beginning of the fruiting cycle.
- **Yellowing round growths:** Small round fungus growths that begin to turn yellow, representing intermediate developmental structures between pins and primordia.
- **Primordia:** Tiny worm like structures with tiny reddish heads. These are the first true mushroom formations and the signal that the cake is ready to be birthed into the fruiting terrarium.

The primordia stage is the optimal time to birth the cake from the jar. The PF TEK advises handling the cake carefully at this stage to avoid damaging the delicate primordia.

## The Initial Flush: Peak Production Phase

The initial fruiting flush is the most productive phase of the entire cake lifecycle. During this period, three distinct types of fungal growth emerge simultaneously:

- **Normal fruitbodies (sporocarps):** The stately mushroom specimens with well formed caps and stems. These are visually impressive and ideal for spore collecting but are weaker in potency than immature specimens.
- **Abortive mushrooms (abhorts):** Convoluted caps, gnarly stems, and stunted growth. Ironically, these abhorts are primo in magic alkaloids, even more powerful than the normal specimens. Over half of the small primordia that first form will abort.
- **Mutant growths:** Blobs of fungus with little or no cap. These are also harvested as they contain active alkaloids.

The PF TEK provides specific harvesting guidance for the initial flush:

- Pick abortive mushrooms before their heads turn black
- Harvest normal fruitbodies before the veil under the cap breaks, while caps are still roundish
- Collect small mutant blobs along with other specimens
- Use a pointed knife blade for removing primordia, which are among the most potent structures

## Secondary Fruiting Bodies

After the initial flush completes, a visible change occurs in the mycelium cake: it begins to turn blue. This bluing is a natural response of the fungus and indicates the cake is approaching nutritional exhaustion. No more mushrooms form under normal conditions.

However, the PF TEK notes that secondary fruit bodies can sometimes appear under specific circumstances:

- The cake must be thoroughly cleaned after the initial fruiting to remove any remaining abortive material or debris
- If secondary mushrooms do form, they are usually sparse and small
- They may be minimal in number or absent entirely
- The quality and quantity of the secondary flush are significantly diminished compared to the initial flush
- Any secondary specimens that do appear are still harvestable and usable

The blue discoloration is not a sign of contamination. It is the visual signal that the substrate nutrients have been largely consumed by the initial flush.

## Why Cake Exhaustion Occurs

The PF TEK uses small half pint (250ml) jars with a limited substrate volume. The standard formula contains only 1/8 cup of brown rice powder with 1/2 to 2/3 cup of vermiculite. Even the maximum fruiting formula contains just 1/4 cup of brown rice powder. This small nutrient base is fully consumed by the fungus during colonization and the initial fruiting flush.

Unlike bulk cultivation methods that provide a large substrate mass (several quarts or more) capable of supporting multiple substantial flushes, the PF TEK cake is intentionally designed for a single strong flush. The mass spore inoculation approach introduces full genetic diversity into each cake, ensuring vigorous fruiting but also rapid nutrient consumption by competitive mycelial strains.

## Post Flush Cake Assessment

Cultivators should assess cakes after the initial flush using these diagnostic indicators:

- **Blue mycelium:** Normal aging and exhaustion sign; indicates the cake is spent
- **No new primordia forming:** Confirms the fruiting cycle has concluded
- **Secondary mushrooms appearing:** Possible but sparse; harvest if they develop
- **Contaminant colors (pastels to black):** Indicates contamination; discard the cake immediately
- **Sour foul odor:** Bacterial contamination; discard with caution

## Birthing Timing and Its Effect on Total Yield

The PF TEK specifies that the best time to birth the fungus cake from the jar is when primordia appear on the cake while still in the jar. Birthing at this optimal stage ensures:

- The cake has fully colonized and accumulated sufficient nutrients for a strong flush
- The cake is transferred to the high humidity terrarium precisely when the fungus is ready to fruit
- Nutrient exhaustion in the jar is minimized, preserving maximum energy for the terrarium flush

Birthing too early (before primordia appear) may result in a weaker flush because the mycelium has not yet accumulated enough resources. Birthing too late (after the cake has begun exhausting in jar) wastes nutrients on in jar metabolic activity.

## Practical Planning for Continuous Harvests

Given the single flush nature of PF TEK cakes, cultivators should adopt a staggered approach:

- Prepare multiple jars and inoculate them on a rolling schedule (weekly or biweekly)
- Do not expect significant yields from secondary flushes; plan around the initial flush
- Discard exhausted cakes promptly to prevent contamination from spreading to healthy cakes
- Focus cultivation effort on optimizing the initial flush rather than attempting to extend cake lifespan
- Replace the entire cake batch after the initial flush cycle for the next round of cultivation

## Monitoring the Fruiting Cycle

Cultivators should observe several indicators during the fruiting cycle to assess progress and identify issues:

- **Mycelial growth on the cake surface:** Spreading white mycelium indicates the fungus is still actively colonizing. This is normal early in the fruiting phase but excessive spreading without pin formation may indicate temperatures are too high for fruiting.
- **Primordia density:** A high number of primordia suggests a strong initial flush. Over half will abort, so a large initial primordia count translates to a substantial harvest.
- **Cap development:** Round, light colored caps indicate immature specimens at peak potency. Caps that are spreading, darkening, and upturning indicate mature specimens past their potency prime.
- **Stem bluing:** Some bluing on the mycelium and stems is normal and indicates handling or age related bruising. This is not contamination.
- **Cessation of new growth:** When no new primordia or mushrooms are forming and existing growth has stopped, the flush is complete.

## Cleaning Cakes for Secondary Flush Attempts

If a cultivator wishes to attempt a secondary flush, the PF TEK specifies that the cake must be thoroughly cleaned after the initial fruiting. This means:

- Remove all harvested mushroom stems and abortive material from the cake surface
- Clean away any darkened or discolored areas
- Return the cleaned cake to the terrarium with continued humidity maintenance
- Monitor for new growth over the following weeks
- Expect sparse results at best; most cakes will not produce a meaningful secondary flush
