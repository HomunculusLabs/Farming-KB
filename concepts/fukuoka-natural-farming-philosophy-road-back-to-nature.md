## Core Philosophy: Reunification of God, Nature, and Man

Fukuoka's central thesis is that humanity has fractured the original unity of God,
nature, and man. This split, driven by the expansion of human intellect and reason,
has led to environmental destruction, spiritual impoverishment, and a civilization
fundamentally at odds with natural processes.

He writes: "The fragmented and diffusive development of knowledge which expands
outward without aim or direction has brought human thought to the extremes of
confusion, recklessly splitting apart God, nature, and man — originally one
indivisible — and leaving only a legacy of incoherent chaos."

Unlike purely scientific critiques, Fukuoka"s argument is fundamentally spiritual.
He asserts that God did not create nature — rather, God came to dwell in nature
of its own accord. The soul of nature is the will of God, not accessible through
dissection or [[fukuoka-critique-scientific-farming-energy-efficiency]] as essentially
slash-and-burn agriculture in disregard of nature. The yield ratio halves every ten
years. To harvest one calorie of food energy, farmers put two into the land.

## Natural Farming as Spiritual Practice

Fukuoka describes natural farming not as a technique but as a way of being:

- **Do-nothing farming**: Allowing nature to follow its own course without intervention
- **No plowing**: The soil maintains its own structure through natural processes
- **No chemicals**: Nature provides its own fertility and pest control
- **No weeding**: Weeds play beneficial roles in the ecosystem
- **Natural seed broadcasting**: Seeds are scattered without cultivation

His orchard in Japan, where cherry, plum, peach, and pear trees bloom alongside
semi-wild vegetables, is "an Eden, a paradise on Earth." Yet visitors hurry off
after photographing the beauty, unable to immerse themselves in raw, unrefined nature.
