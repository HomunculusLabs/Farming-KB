## Substrate Preparation

### The Basic Recipe

For each half-pint jar:
- 1/2 cup vermiculite
- 1/4 cup brown rice flour
- 1/4 cup water (distilled or spring water preferred)

This ratio produces a substrate with approximately 60% moisture content by weight, which is optimal for P. cubensis mycelium. The vermiculite provides structure and water retention while the brown rice flour provides nutrition. The relatively low nutrient density is intentional — it favors mushroom mycelium over competing contaminant organisms.

### Mixing Procedure

1. Place the measured vermiculite in the mixing bowl.
2. Add the water and mix thoroughly. The vermiculite should absorb all water — it should be damp but not dripping. Squeeze out excess if necessary.
3. Gradually add the brown rice flour while mixing. Distribute the flour evenly throughout the damp vermiculite. The mixture should be fluffy and crumbly, not muddy or compacted.
4. Fill each jar with the substrate mixture. Fill to approximately 1/2 inch (1.25 cm) below the rim. Do not pack the substrate down — it should remain loose for proper colonization.
5. Wipe the inside rim of each jar clean with a paper towel or cloth. No substrate should remain above the level where the dry vermiculite barrier will sit.

### The Dry Vermiculite Barrier

This is a critical innovation of the PF TEK:

1. Fill the top 1/2 inch of each jar with dry, clean vermiculite. This creates a contamination barrier that:
   - Allows gas exchange (oxygen in, CO2 out) through the vermiculite's porous structure.
   - Prevents airborne contaminant spores from reaching the nutritious substrate.
   - Acts as an indicator — if the dry layer becomes wet or discolored, contamination has likely breached the barrier.

### Lid Preparation

1. Using a hammer and nail (or a punch tool), make 4 holes in each canning jar lid. Space them evenly around the perimeter, about halfway between the center and the edge.
2. The holes should be approximately 1/16 inch (1.5 mm) in diameter — large enough for the syringe needle but small enough to limit contamination.
3. Place the lid (rubber side down, facing up) on the jar and screw on the metal band. Tighten firmly but do not overtighten.
4. Cover each lid with a square of aluminum foil, crimping it tightly around the jar neck. This foil protects the lid during sterilization and serves as an additional contamination barrier.

## Steam Sterilization

### Setup

1. Place a washcloth, jar rings, or a rack at the bottom of the large pot to elevate the jars above the direct heat.
2. Add water to the pot — approximately 1-2 inches deep. The water level should be below the bottom of the jars.
3. Place the jars in the pot. They should not be touching each other or the pot walls. If stacking is necessary, use a layer of foil between layers.
4. Put the lid on the pot.

### Sterilization Cycle

1. Bring the pot to a rolling boil over high heat.
2. Once boiling, reduce heat to maintain a steady steam.
3. Steam for 60-90 minutes. A full 90 minutes provides greater margin of safety, especially at higher altitudes where water boils at a lower temperature.
