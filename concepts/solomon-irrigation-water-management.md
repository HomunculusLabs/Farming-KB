# Irrigation and Water Management

Source: Steve Solomon, "Gardening When It Counts" (2005)

## The Water Problem with Intensive Gardening

Solomon's central critique of intensive raised-bed methods is their water dependence. Closely spaced plants in raised beds exhaust available moisture in days. During hot weather, intensive beds require daily watering. When water is scarce or must be hauled, this becomes unsustainable. The original "[[solomon-wide-spacing-versus-intensive-gardening-debate]]: each plant had a large root zone to mine for water, and capillary action from subsoil continuously recharged the root zone.

## How Much Water Does Soil Hold?

The water-holding capacity of soil depends on its clay content. Sandy soils hold less but make more available; clay soils hold more but plants access less of it.

Key thresholds (per 1,000 sq ft, per foot of depth):
- Sandy soil: ~0.8 inches available water
- Medium/loam: ~1.6 inches
- Clayey soil (20%+ clay): ~2.0 inches
- Heavy clay: ~2.4 inches

Water loss per day in summer varies by climate:
- Cool maritime (Scotland, western Washington): ~0.07 inches/day
- Moderate (western Oregon, northern US): ~0.12 inches/day
- Hot humid: ~0.16 inches/day
- Hot dry: ~0.22 inches/day
- Low desert: ~0.28 inches/day

## The Drought Strategy: Wider Spacing

If drought threatens, Solomon's primary response is NOT irrigation but spacing adjustment. In every row, harvest every second plant (carrot, beet, whatever is ready). This instantly reduces moisture consumption while doubling the capillary moisture available to each survivor. The result may not be losing half the crop -- by preventing moisture stress, you may harvest more total food.

In severe drought without irrigation, maximum spacing for corn is ~16 sq ft per plant (4-foot centers), imitating traditional [[solomon-foliar-feeding-fish-emulsion-and-kelp]] (1:100 dilution), or soluble [[cannabis-grow-nutrient-deficiency-problem-solving]] caused by dry surface soil when plant nutrients are located only in the plowed surface layer. Fertigation places nutrients in the subsoil where roots are foraging.

## Practical Water Management Guidelines

1. Know your soil's clay content using the jar test
2. Calculate how many days your soil can go between watering
3. Space plants widely enough for natural rainfall to suffice in normal years
4. Use fertigation for large plants during drought, not sprinkler irrigation
5. Keep soil surface loose to preserve capillary connections
6. Weed with a sharp hoe; minimize soil disturbance
7. In severe drought, thin stands to increase per-plant moisture
8. For new gardens in humid regions, spread amendments in late summer/fall so winter rains carry fertility into subsoil

## Rototiller Irrigation Drawbacks

Rototilling creates a hard "plow pan" a few inches below the surface, blocking root penetration into subsoil moisture. This is another reason Solomon prefers spade-digging or shallow cultivation with a sharp hoe for established beds.
