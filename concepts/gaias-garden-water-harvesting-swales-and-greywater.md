Greywater -- water from sinks, showers, and laundry drains -- is "nearly pure but just dirty enough to be unfit for direct human reuse." Plants and soil organisms eagerly consume both the water and its dissolved contents.

### The Greywater Cycle

Greywater closes a loop in the garden ecosystem. Instead of the linear flow (clean water in, dirty water to sewer), greywater creates a tight cycle: water, food, and biodegradable soaps enter the household, mix with dirt and skin in shower and laundry, and go into the soil or a backyard wetland. Microbes and plants process the mixture into clean water and fertilizer. The result is not waste but soil, trees, flowers, and transpired moisture that returns as rain.

### Tips for Using Greywater

- Greywater is a legal grey area -- check local codes before installing
- Avoid watering food plants directly with untreated greywater; apply to nonfood plants, fruit trees, or via subsurface irrigation
- Do not use chlorine bleach, boron-containing detergents, or household chemicals in greywater systems
- Hydrogen peroxide-based bleaches are safe
- Use greywater-compatible detergents (most common detergents make water too alkaline)
- In freezing climates, divert greywater to sewer in winter
- Do not store greywater for more than 1-2 days (bacteria multiply quickly)
- Greywater clogs drip irrigation unless filtered; use large-diameter hose or invest in filtration
