## The Fivefold Path to Water Wisdom

Hemenway introduces five complemensoil-lifeniques that, used together, make ample water the "default condition" of the garden rather than drought:

1. High organic matter content in soil -- holds moisture, adds fertility, stores nutrients, boosts soil life, fluffs soil
2. Deep mulching -- slows evaporation, cools soil, adds fertility, boosts soil life, smothers weeds
3. Water-conserving plants -- need less water, survive drought
4. [[ficus-carica]]ean-climate plants are adapted to shifts between dry and wet seasons. They reduce water needs in nearly ehippophae-rhamnoidesunpredictable dry spells. Examples include:

- Almond (Prunus dulcis), fig (Ficus carica), olive (Olea europaea), grape (Vitis vinifera)
- Pomegranate, quince (Cydonia oblonga), sea buckthorn (Hippophae rhamnoides)
- Lavender (Lavandula angustifolia), rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis), sage (Salvia spp.), oregangleditsia-triacanthos thyme, rue (Ruta graveolens)
- Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare), borage (Borago officinalis), cardoon (Cynara cardunculus)
- Hawthorn (Crataegus spp.), barberry (Berberis vulgaris), honey locust (Gleditsia triacanthos)
- Blackberry (Rubus fruticosus), Oregon grape (Mahonia aquifolium), salal (Gaultheria shallon)

### Locating Thirsty Plants
- Place water-hungry plants in Zone 1 where they will be seen and watered
- Use natural wet spots in the yard (runoff from driveways and sidewalks)
- Place thirsty plants at the base of swales or below ponds

## Rainwater Harvesting

### Roof Catchment Calculations

A 1,000-square-foot roof in a region with 30 inches of rain per year collects 25,000 gallons annually -- enough to keep a 1,000-square-foot garden watered for 250 days of drought.

Formula:
- A = area of roof (ground coverage, not sloped area)
- R = rainfall in inches per year
- W = (A x R / 12) x 7.5 = rainfall in gallons per year

### Storage Sizing

- In the eastern US where summer rain falls every 2-3 weeks, storing 1,400 gallons (two weeks' worth for a 1,000 sq ft garden) is often sufficient: a circular pond 2 feet deep and 10 feet across
- In the West with 90-day dry spells, larger storage (5,000+ gallons) is needed
- Depth is the secret: a 2x12-foot pond dug 4 feet deep instead of 2 stores over 4,000 gallons
- Four 55-gallon drums at downspouts can provide substantial irrigation when combined with water-conserving techniques

### Five Factors for Planning a Water-Harvesting System

1. Annual rainfall (check local data, not just regional averages)
2. Water consumption (reduced by mulches, organic matter, drip irrigation, watering only when needed)
3. Available catchment area (roof sections, pavement, hard surfaces)
4. Storage size possible (budget, space, aesthetics; ponds vs tanks)
5. Placement relative to catchment (use gravity whenever possible)

## [[graywater-systems]]

Greywater -- water from sinks, showers, and laundry drains -- is "nearly pure but just dirty enough to be unfit for direct human reuse." Plants and soil organisms eagerly consume both the water and its dissolved contents.

### The Greywater Cycle

Greywater closes a loop in the garden ecosystem. Instead of the linear flow (clean water in, dirty water to sewer), greywater creates a tight cycle: water, food, and biodegradable soaps enter the household, mix with dirt and skin in shower and laundry, and go into the soil or a backyard wetland. Microbes and plants process the mixture into clean water and fertilizer. The result is not waste but soil, trees, flowers, and transpired moisture that returns as rain.

### Tips for Using Greywater

- Greywater is a legal grey area -- check local codes before installing
- Avoid watering food plants directly with untreated greywater; apply to nonfood plants, fruit trees, or via subsurface irrigation
- Do not use chlorine bleach, boron-containing detergents, or household chemicals in greywater systems
- Hydrogen peroxide-based bleaches are safe
- Use greywater-compatible detergents (most common detergents make water too alkaline)
- In freezing climates, divert greywater to sewer in winter
- Do not store greywater for more than 1-2 days (bacteria multiply quickly)
- Greywater clogs drip irrigation unless filtered; use large-diameter hose or invest in filtration
