# EMCDDA "Return to Nature" Megatrend as a Driver of Mushroom Appeal

The EMCDDA's 2006 thematic paper on [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-contributing-factors-megatrends]] stated directly that "increasing
interest in natural and organic products may have enhanced the appeal of
hallucinogenic mushrooms over synthetic hallucinogens." This framing positioned
mushrooms favourably against synthetic drugs like ecstasy and LSD in the
perceptions of consumers who associated "natural" with safety, purity, and
authenticity. The contrast was not merely chemical but cultural: mushrooms were
understood as a product of nature, while ecstasy tablets were seen as
manufactured, industrial commodities of uncertain composition.

This natural-product preference aligned with broader lifestyle trends of the
period. The late 1990s saw a general shift toward organic foods, herbal remedies,
and environmental consciousness across European consumer markets. Hallucinogenic
mushrooms benefited from this cultural current by being categorisable as a "natural"
psychoactive, distinct from laboratory-synthesised substances.

## The Norwegian Woodland Party Milieu

The paper provided a specific national example of the return-to-nature trend in
action. Norway reported the existence of a music milieu "characterised by a focus
on organic lifestyles and woodland parties." This subculture was described as
being largely composed of economically disadvantaged people between 25 and 40
years old, many of whom used both cannabis and hallucinogenic mushrooms. The
woodland party setting -- outdoor, rural, nature-oriented gatherings -- embodied
the "back to nature" ethos and provided a social context where mushroom use was
normalised as part of a broader organic lifestyle. However, the paper noted this
milieu was considered "peculiar and unattractive" by younger age groups, limiting
its potential as a vector for wider diffusion.

## Smartshop Marketing and "Herbal Highs" Labelling

The natural-product framing was central to smartshop marketing strategies. The
paper documented that "hallucinogenic mushrooms have been sold by many retailers
under the general label of 'Herbal highs' following the trend in the late 1990s
towards the preference for organic and herbal products." This categorisation
placed [[golden-guide-salvia-divinorum-and-diviner-sage]]. Smartshops, which
specialised in psychoactive herbal substances alongside vitamins and health
products, deliberately positioned mushrooms within a wellness and natural-products
retail context rather than a drug-market context. Dutch smartshops concentrated
in Amsterdam were estimated at 120--150 outlets, with magic mushrooms reportedly
constituting approximately 50% of smartshop turnover.
