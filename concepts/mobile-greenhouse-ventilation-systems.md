# Mobile Greenhouse Ventilation Systems

Ventilation is one of the most critical [[mycoremediation-bioreactor-design-considerations]] for [[mobile-greenhouses]] used in [[tools-supplies-winter-harvest-farming]]. Unlike stationary structures, mobile hoop houses moved to multiple sites throughout the season require ventilation systems that are simple, reliable, and adaptable to conditions ranging from subfreezing winters with snow and ice to hot summers demanding maximum airflow.

## The Problem with Roll-Up Side Ventilation in Winter

In most standard tunnel greenhouse designs, roll-up sides are the conventional ventilation method. The plastic sidewall is attached to a pipe roller at the base, and the grower rolls the plastic upward to create an opening along the length of the house. However, this approach has serious drawbacks for winter-harvest applications [[query-how-do-i-grow-figs-in-cold-climates]]:

- **Snow and ice interference:** Snow accumulation around the base and ice formation on the plastic make it extremely difficult to attach or detach the pipe roller. A grower may need to clear significant snow before ventilation can be adjusted — labor-intensive and sometimes impractical during winter storms.

- **Cold air infiltration at soil level:** When roll-up sides are opened, cold outside air enters at the very bottom — directly at soil level where young plants are growing. This cold air drafts horizontally across the crops, causing thermal shock and potentially damaging sensitive winter greens and seedlings.

- **Difficulty of daily management:** Winter ventilation requires opening during sunny days and closing promptly in the evening. If the roll-up mechanism is frozen or blocked by snow, the grower loses control of the internal climate.

For these reasons, roll-up side ventilation was rejected as the primary ventilation strategy for winter-harvest [[mobile-greenhouses-winter-harvest-seed-varieties]].

## Elevated Roll-Up Side Design (Modified Approach)

A modified roll-up side design could potentially work in winter with key changes:

1. **Elevated opening height:** The rollable portion would stop 2 to 3 feet above ground level rather than extending all the way down.
2. **Separate lower covering:** A fixed piece of plastic would cover the lower 2–3 feet of each sidewall independently.
3. **Snow clearance:** An opening at 2–3 feet above ground would remain clear of snow in most conditions. Only very large roof-slides would pose a problem.

While more viable than a full ground-level roll-up, this approach still has limitations compared to end-wall ventilation alternatives.

## Drop-Down Side Ventilation

A drop-down side design addresses the primary weakness of roll-up systems by inverting the direction of the opening:

- The plastic is attached at the **top** of the sidewall and drops downward to create the ventilation opening.
- The opening forms at an elevated height (2–3 feet above ground), so cold air enters above the plant canopy rather than at soil level.
- Cold air that enters mixes with warmer interior air before reaching the plants, reducing thermal shock risk.
- Snow on the ground does not block the mechanism since the opening is well above typical accumulation levels.

This approach is preferred over elevated roll-up sides for winter conditions and cold-season mobile greenhouses.

## End-Wall Ventilation for Sliding Houses

For the 22-by-48-foot sliding [[greenhouse-design]], ventilation is handled primarily through the end walls. This design provides both high and low ventilation capabilities.

### Door Configuration

- Each end wall has **two 4-foot-wide by 5-foot-tall access doors** positioned side by side in the center.
- The base of the doors sits **16 inches above ground level**, keeping the opening elevated enough to avoid most snow accumulation while providing useful low ventilation.

### Structural Crossbar

A permanent **horizontal crossbar** extends across the full width of the greenhouse beneath the doors:

- It stiffens the end bows and prevents the greenhouse ends from **splaying inward or outward** during movement.
- The trade-off is that growers must step over the crossbar when entering or exiting — awkward but acceptable given the structural benefit.

### Summer Ventilation Strip

Below the crossbar, a **strip of plastic** covers the space between the crossbar and the soil surface:

- It is rolled up when the greenhouse is **moved over standing crops**, allowing clearance for plants below.
- It functions as a **roll-up side vent during summer**, providing additional low ventilation when the main doors alone are insufficient.

## Improved End-Wall Ventilation for Wheeled/Rolling Houses

The most advanced ventilation design was made possible by placing the mobile house on **wheels that run on rails** rather than skids.

### Elimination of the Crossbar

In sled-based models, the crossbar prevented skids from splaying during movement. With a rail-based wheeled system, directional stability is assured by the rails themselves, eliminating the crossbar and opening the entire end wall for a more versatile design.

### Wide Door Opening Design

The wheeled houses feature a dramatically improved end-wall ventilation layout:

- **12-foot-wide opening** at both ends — a massive improvement over the 8-foot combined width of the previous design.
- A **narrow 2-foot-wide door** for routine winter access is positioned between the large opening and the sidewall.
- The wide doorway is covered with plastic **secured at the top and sides using wiggle-wire channel** — a fast, reliable attachment system.
- The **bottom edge is held down with sandbags**, providing a simple but effective seal.
- The plastic door sheet is **attached to a pipe at both top and bottom**, functioning as both a roll-up and roll-down door depending on the season.

## Seasonal Ventilation Strategies

### Winter: Drop-Down Ventilation

The priority is protecting crops from cold air infiltration while allowing [[blesching-cannabis-fever-temperature-regulation]] on sunny days:

- The wiggle wire is **unclipped across the top and upper sides** of one or both ends.
- The plastic door is **dropped downward** to open as large an area as needed.
- Cold air entering from the dropped opening mixes with warm interior air above the plants rather than blowing directly across them.
- The opening is **closed up in the evening** to retain heat overnight.
- Routine access uses the separate 2-foot-wide door to minimize heat loss.

### Early Spring: Roll-Up with Air Barrier

Snow is no longer a concern but nighttime temperatures can still damage crops:

- The door is operated as a **roll-up from the bottom** during the day and rolled back down at night.
- During early spring cold snaps, a **2-foot-wide strip of plastic is stretched horizontally across the bottom of the door opening** as an air barrier.
- This air barrier deflects incoming cold air upward so it mixes with warm air before reaching the plant canopy.

### Summer: Fully Open

Maximum ventilation and easy access are the priorities:

- The doors at **both ends are rolled up entirely and tied in place**.
- This creates a wind-tunnel effect ensuring excellent airflow.
- Full open ends allow **easy access for harvesting** and re-preparing beds between succession crops.
- Combined with mobility, the fully open configuration effectively turns the structure into a shade house or rain shelter.

## Practical Tips for Converting Static Houses

### Adding Mobility

A static house can be converted to a mobile house at reasonable cost by adding:

- **Wheels** that run on rails for guided movement
- **Pipe rails** on both sides to allow the house to roll smoothly
- **Diagonal bracing** to maintain structural integrity during movement

### Static House Summer Strategy

Even without mobility, a static house can duplicate some benefits by managing its plastic covering seasonally:

- **Uncover in summer** by rolling the plastic up and over, starting from one side and leaving it attached at the bottom of the opposite side.
- **Protect the rolled plastic** with an opaque cover to shield it from UV degradation during storage.
- **Re-cover in fall** before freezing weather arrives.

This approach effectively doubles the lifespan of the greenhouse plastic, since it is not subjected to summer sun exposure and UV radiation when crop protection is not needed.

## Key Design Principles Summary

| Principle | Winter | Spring | Summer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ventilation type | Drop-down from top | Roll-up from bottom | Fully open, tied back |
| Cold air management | Opening above plant level | Air barrier strip at bottom | Not needed |
| Access method | Narrow 2ft door | Roll-up door | Full 12ft opening |
| Snow/ice concern | High — avoid ground openings | Low | None |
| Structural feature | Wiggle-wire sealed | Roll-up pipe mechanism | Tied in place |
