## Fungal Enzymatic Diversity in Decomposition
Fungi produce a remarkably diverse array of [[maintaining-organic-matter-in-vegetable-gardens]] terrestrial ecosystems,
including lignin, cellulose, hemicellulose, cutin, suberin, chitin, and
keratin.

## Categories of Fungal Decomposition Enzymes
Cellulolytic enzymes:
- Endoglucanases (endo-1,4-beta-glucanases) cleave internal cellulose bonds
- Exoglucanases (cellobiohydrolases) processively release cellobiose units
  from chain ends
- Beta-glucosidases hydrolyze cellobiose to glucose

Hemicellulolytic enzymes:
- Xylanases break down xylan (the major hemicellulose in hardwoods)
- Mannanases degrade mannan-rich hemicelluloses
- Acetyl xylan esterases remove acetyl groups from hemicellulose
- Ferulic acid esterases cleave cross-links between hemicellulose and lignin

Lignin-modifying enzymes:
- Lignin peroxidases (LiP) oxidize non-phenolic lignin subunits
- Manganese peroxidases (MnP) oxidize phenolic lignin components
- Laccases (phenol oxidases) oxidize [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]], generated by oxidases

Chitinases, proteases, lipases, cutinases, suberinases, phosphatases, and
ureases round out the fungal enzymatic repertoire for organic matter
decomposition.

## Enzyme Regulation and Induction
Fungal decomposition enzymes are typically produced in response to specific
substrate signals, allowing fungi to allocate metabolic resources
efficiently. The regulatory mechanisms governing enzyme production reflect
the ecological strategies of different fungal functional groups:

- **Carbon catabolite repression**: Many cellulolytic and ligninolytic
  enzymes are repressed by readily metabolizable carbon sources such as
  glucose. When preferred carbon sources are exhausted, fungi activate
  complex polymer [[trichoderma-reesei]], while
  ferulic acid can induce laccase production in several white-rot fungi.

- **Metal ion effects**: Manganese ions are essential cofactors for
  manganese peroxidase, and their availability directly influences the
  lignin degradation capacity of white-rot fungi. Copper ions induce
  laccase expression, as copper is a structural component of laccase
  active sites. Iron availability affects Fenton chemistry in brown-rot
  fungi.

- **Nitrogen limitation**: Many lignin-degrading enzymes, particularly
  lignin peroxidase, are produced preferentially under nitrogen-limited
  conditions. This regulatory pattern reflects the ecological context of
  wood decomposition, where nitrogen is typically the limiting nutrient
  for fungal growth.
