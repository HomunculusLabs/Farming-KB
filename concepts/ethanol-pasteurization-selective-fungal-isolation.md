# Ethanol Pasteurization for Selective Fungal Isolation

Ethanol pasteurization is a selective technique that preferentially isolates fungi with resistant propagules — particularly ascomycetes and basidiomycetes with thick-walled spores, sclerotia, or chlamydospores — from soil. It shifts the isolated population away from rapidly growing mitosporic fungi and Zygomycetes toward more resistant, often slower-growing species.

## Principle

Treatment of soil with 60% ethanol (or 2% phenol) kills or inactivates thin-walled, sensitive propagules (conidia of mitosporic fungi, Zygomycete sporangiospores) while allowing thick-walled resistant structures (ascospores, basidiospores, sclerotia, chlamydospores) to survive and subsequently germinate on culture media.

## What Ethanol Pasteurization Selects For

### Fungi Recovered at Higher Frequencies
- **Ascosporic Eurotiales** (e.g., Eurotium species)
- **Chaetomium species** (perithecial ascomycetes)
- **Xylaria species**
- **Coprophilous ascomycetes:** Chaetomium, Gelasinospora, Thelebolus, Sordaria, Ascobolus, Saccobolus, Sporormiella
- **Cleistothecial and perithecial ascomycetes** generally
- **Some ascosporic yeasts**

### Fungi Reduced or Eliminated
- Many yeast species with thin-walled conidia
- Mitosporic fungi with thin-walled spores
- Zygomycetes (sensitive to ethanol)
- Rapidly growing generalists (Alternaria, Trichoderma — generally reduced)

## Effectiveness

In Galapagos Islands study (Mahoney 1972), of 250 taxonomic entities recovered by all methods combined:
- **44 species** (17.6%) were isolated ONLY after ethanol pasteurization
- Ethanol pasteurization alone vs. sequential ethanol+heat: no significant difference
- Ethanol pasteurization was effective at extending species lists regardless

## Protocol

### Materials
- Air-dried soil samples (5-10 g per sample)
- 60% ethanol (or 2% phenol as alternative)
- Sterile mortar and pestle
- Sterile 125-250 mL Erlenmeyer flasks
- Shaker (platform)
- Petri dishes
- Isolation media (GAN, DRBC, CMC, or MEC)

### Steps
1. **Pulverize** 5-10 g air-dried soil with sterile mortar and pestle
2. **Transfer** to labeled sterile flask
3. **Add** 20-30 mL of 60% ethanol to completely cover soil
4. **Agitate** on shaker for 5 minutes
5. **Let stand** 5 minutes for soil to settle
6. **Decant** ethanol carefully
7. **Rinse** twice with sterile water (some soil loss is acceptable)
8. **Plate:** Pipette 0.5-1.0 mL treated soil suspension into Petri dishes; add 10-15 mL molten medium (~45C); slowly agitate to disperse evenly
9. **Incubate** at desired temperature
10. **Control plates:** Treat soil with water instead of ethanol

### Variables Affecting Results
- **Soil texture:** Fine-textured soils sterilized more effectively than coarse
- **[[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]:** Plant fibers and roots can interfere with pulverization and protect propagules
- **Soil volume:** May need trial runs to determine optimal amount
- **Ethanol concentration:** 60% is standard; adjust for soil type
- **Treatment time:** Standard 5 minutes; may need adjustment

## Phenol Pasteurization (Alternative)

### Protocol
- Substitute 2% phenol for 60% ethanol
- Follow same steps
- Effective at increasing ascomycete isolations

### Comparison
- Similar selectivity to ethanol pasteurization
- Phenol may be more effective for certain soil types
- Both are variations of the same selective principle

## Combination with Other Techniques

### Ethanol + Mild Heat
- Some investigators combine ethanol treatment with mild heat treatment
- Heat (40-50C) thought to stimulate ascospore germination
- Warcup and Baker (1963): effective combination
- However, ethanol alone is often sufficient (Galapagos study)

### After Particle Filtration
- Ethanol pasteurization can be applied to particles after initial separation
- Useful for selectively recovering basidiomycetes from washed particles

## Safety Considerations
- 60% ethanol is flammable — keep away from open flames
- Work in fume hood if using phenol (toxic)
- Autoclave all waste materials, especially foreign soils

## Applications

1. **Biodiversity surveys:** Extend species lists beyond what standard plating recovers
2. **Coprophilous fungi:** Isolate dung-inhabiting ascomycetes from soil
3. **[[biodiversity-ascomycete-diversity-taxonomy]]:** Specifically target teleomorphic (sexual) fungi
4. **Soil [[gadd-fungal-ecology-endophytes]]:** Compare communities with and without pasteurization
5. **Rare species detection:** Many species only recovered by this method
