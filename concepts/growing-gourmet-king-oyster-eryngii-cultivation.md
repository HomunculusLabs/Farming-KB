---# King Oyster Mushroom ([[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]] achieve greater stature and
overall size compared to ones collected in the wild.

## Common Names

The King Oyster, Boletus of the Steppes.

## Taxonomy

Synonymous with Pleurotus fuscus (Bail.) Bits. Varieties specific to
ecological niches have been commented upon by Bresinsky et al. (1987).
Although these varieties appear morphologically identical, the
distribution of ecotypes are quite distinct.

## Distribution and Natural Habitat

Throughout southern Europe, North Africa, central Asia and the southern
Soviet Union. Terrestrial, growing on the buried roots of hardwoods.
This mushroom is thought to be a facultative parasite on dying Eryngium
campestre, a member of the carrot family. Some strains are native to
conifers (Abies spp.), which if brought into culture could help recycle
conifer stumps worldwide.

## Microscopic Features

Spores white, ellipsoid, 10-14 x 4-5 micrometers. Clamp connections
present. Context monomitic.

## Mycelial Characteristics

Whitish, longitudinally radial at first, sometimes rhizomorphic, soon
thickening and becoming cottony in age. [[growing-gourmet-plug-spawn-production]]. Easily grown on straw outdoors using the mound method.

### Spawn Expansion
Traditional or [[oyster-mushroom-pasteurized-straw-cultivation]] cultivation is comparatively less productive unless
inoculated with equal quantities of sawdust and grain spawn. Every ton
of [[growing-gourmet-sawdust-spawn-production-use]] (wet
weight). This combination spawning method gives rise to large specimens
on wheat straw.

### Agar Media
MYPA or PDYA.

### Spawn Media
Rye, wheat, sorghum, milo, or millet.

### Fruiting Substrates
Most hardwoods, wheat straw, and cottonseed hulls support fruitings.
Not as adaptive as P. pulmonarius and P. ostreatus to a broad range of
substrates. See mushroom-primordia-formation-triggers.

### Primordia Formation
- Temperature: 50-60 degrees F (10-15 degrees C)
