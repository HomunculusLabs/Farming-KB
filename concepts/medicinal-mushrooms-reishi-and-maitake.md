# Medicinal Mushrooms: Reishi and Maitake

Among the polypore mushrooms, Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum) and Maitake
(Grifola frondosa) occupy a special position at the intersection of
culinary and medicinal traditions. Both have been used for centuries in
Traditional Chinese Medicine and have attracted significant modern research
attention for their immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and
anti-tumor properties. Stamets documents their cultivation in detail,
noting that their growing requirements differ markedly from typical gourmet
mushrooms.

## Reishi: Ganoderma lucidum

Reishi, known as Ling Chi in Chinese ("mushroom of immortality"), is one of
the most revered medicinal mushrooms in East Asian cultures. It has a
distinctive lacquered appearance with a varnished, kidney-shaped cap that
ranges from red-orange to dark reddish-brown. Reishi is too woody and bitter
for culinary use in its whole form but is consumed as teas, extracts, and
tinctures.

### Substrate and Cultivation

Reishi is a white-rot fungus that decomposes hardwood. Cultivation uses
hardwood sawdust supplemented with 15-25% wheat bran or rice bran, similar
to shiitake substrates. Oak, alder, and maple sawdust are preferred. The
substrate is sterilized and inoculated with grain spawn.

Colonization is slower than most gourmet species, typically requiring 30 to
60 days at 25 to 30 degrees C. Reishi prefers warmer temperatures throughout
its life cycle compared to most cultivated mushrooms. The [[mycelium]] is
vigorous and white, often becoming dense and felt-like.

### Fruiting and Morphology

Reishi produces antler-like fruiting bodies when CO2 levels are high and
normal bracket-shaped conks when CO2 is low. The antler form is often
preferred for certain medicinal preparations. For conk formation, blocks are
transferred to a fruiting environment with high humidity (85-95%), good
fresh air exchange, indirect light, and temperatures of 25 to 30 degrees C.

Fruiting can take 2 to 4 months to produce mature conks. The mushrooms are
harvested when the cap margin softens and they begin releasing [[spore]]s (a
visible brown dust). Reishi continuously produces spores in abundance during
maturity, which are also collected for their medicinal value.

### Medicinal Properties

Reishi contains [[beta-glucans]] and triterpenoids (ganoderic acids) that are
the primary bioactive compounds. Research has demonstrated
immunomodulatory effects, anti-inflammatory activity, hepatoprotective
properties, and potential anti-tumor activity. The triterpenoids contribute
to the bitter taste and are believed to have adaptogenic properties, helping
the body manage stress and maintain homeostasis.

## Maitake: Grifola frondosa

Maitake, meaning "dancing mushroom" in Japanese, is also known as
Hen-of-the-Woods. It forms large, overlapping clusters of grayish-brown
caps, resembling the ruffled feathers of a hen. Unlike Reishi, Maitake is
both an excellent edible mushroom with a rich, savory flavor and a potent
medicinal fungus.

### Substrate and Cultivation

Maitake is more challenging to cultivate than most gourmet mushrooms. It
naturally grows at the base of oak trees and is a primary decomposer of
hardwood. Cultivation uses supplemented hardwood sawdust (oak preferred)
with 20-30% bran supplementation. Higher supplementation levels than most
species are needed to achieve commercial yields.

Colonization occurs at 20 to 25 degrees C over 30 to 45 days. The mycelium
is white and forms a dense, cottony mat. Maitake is sensitive to
contamination during colonization, requiring rigorous sterile technique.

### Fruiting

Maitake requires a pronounced cold shock (reducing temperature by 10 to 15
degrees C) to initiate fruiting. After the cold shock, primordia form at
surface openings or wounds in the substrate block. Fruiting temperature is
15 to 20 degrees C with high humidity (90-95%) and ample fresh air exchange.

Mature Maitake clusters can weigh several pounds and form intricate,
layered structures. The fruiting period extends over 2 to 4 weeks from pin
formation to harvest. Yields are typically lower than oyster mushrooms, with
biological efficiencies around 50-100% on supplemented sawdust.

### Medicinal Properties

Maitake is rich in beta-1,6-glucans with beta-1,3-branches, particularly
a patented extract called D-fraction that has been extensively studied for
immune system activation. Research suggests Maitake extracts can enhance
natural killer cell activity, modulate cytokine production, and inhibit
tumor growth. It is also studied for blood sugar regulation and
cardiovascular health benefits.

## Comparison

Reishi and Maitake differ in their cultivation demands and end products.
Reishi tolerates higher temperatures and longer fruiting cycles, producing
woody conks used exclusively for extraction. Maitake requires cooler
conditions and produces a dual-purpose crop valued both as food and
medicine. Both species reward patient cultivators with products that command
premium prices in the growing medicinal mushroom market.
