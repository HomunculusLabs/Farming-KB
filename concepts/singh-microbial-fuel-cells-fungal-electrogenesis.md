# Microbial Fuel Cells and Fungal Electrogenesis

Microbial fuel cells (MFCs) represent an emerging bioelectrochemical technology
that harnesses the metabolic activity of microorganisms to generate electrical
current while simultaneously treating organic waste. Fungi, particularly
white-rot basidiomycetes, have attracted growing interest as biocatalysts in
these systems due to their extracellular electron transfer capabilities and
robust enzymatic machinery for degrading complex substrates.

## Principles of Fungal Electron Transfer

Fungal electrogenesis relies on the ability of certain fungi to transfer
electrons generated during metabolic oxidation of organic substrates to an
external electrode (anode). Unlike bacterial electrogenic systems that depend
on direct contact via conductive pili or soluble electron shuttles, fungi
employ multiple complementary mechanisms:

- Extracellular electron transfer via redox-active secondary metabolites
  and quinone-like compounds secreted into the surrounding medium
- Direct electron transfer through cell wall-associated cytochromes
  and membrane-bound redox proteins
- Mediated electron transfer using naturally produced fungal metabolites
  such as phenolic compounds, melanin, and other redox mediators
- Conductive mycelial networks that can physically bridge substrates
  and electrode surfaces over extended distances
