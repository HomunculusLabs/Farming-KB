# Strain-Dependent Alkaloid Tissue Distribution in Psilocybe cubensis Cap and Stem

The distribution of [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] between the cap (pileus) and stem
(stipe) of Psilocybe cubensis is not uniform and varies significantly between
strains. Bigwood and Beug's 1982 analysis of first-flush specimens from three
distinct strains revealed contrasting patterns of alkaloid localization, with
implications for understanding tryptamine alkaloid biosynthesis, transport, and
storage in basidiomycete fruiting bodies.

## The Three Strains Analyzed

Bigwood and Beug examined first-flush specimens from three cultivated strains of
Psilocybe cubensis, each obtained from different sources and presumably
representing distinct genetic lineages:

1. **M.R. strain**: Obtained from an outside cultivator, grown on rye grain
2. **Equadorian strain**: Also from an outside source, grown on rye grain
3. **Amazon strain**: The primary experimental strain originating from a spore
   print collected near Pucallpa, Peru in the Amazon basin

All three strains were cultivated on rye-grain substrate and harvested at
maturity from the first flush, providing [[psilocybe-cubensis-strain-potency-variability-controlled-conditions-bigwood-beug]] for comparison.
The first-flush restriction ensured that psilocin levels would be minimal in all
strains, simplifying the analysis of [[sandoz-psilocybin-distribution-program]].

## Psilocybin Distribution Patterns

The three strains showed different patterns of psilocybin distribution between
cap and stem tissue. In the M.R. strain, caps contained 6.7 mg/g dry weight of
psilocybin while stems contained 4.2 mg/g, a ratio of approximately 1.6:1 in
favor of caps. The Equadorian strain showed a similar pattern with caps at 7.6
mg/g and stems at 4.7 mg/g, a ratio of approximately 1.6:1.

The Amazon strain differed from the other two strains, showing nearly equal
psilocybin distribution between caps and stems at 5.7 mg/g and 5.7 mg/g
respectively. This 1:1 ratio contrasts sharply with the 1.6:1 cap-to-stem ratio
observed in the M.R. and Equadorian strains, indicating that the preferential
accumulation of psilocybin in cap tissue is not a universal feature of P. cubensis
but is instead strain-dependent.

## Psilocin Distribution: A Paradoxical Pattern

The psilocin distribution patterns revealed a striking and unexpected contrast
with psilocybin distribution. In the M.R. strain, psilocin was entirely absent
from the caps (0 mg/g) but present in the stems at 0.35 mg/g. The Equadorian
strain showed the same pattern: 0 mg/g psilocin in caps, 0.4 mg/g in stems.

The Amazon strain showed the reverse pattern: a trace of psilocin in the caps
(0.1 mg/g) but none in the stems (0 mg/g). This complete inversion of the tissue
localization pattern between strains is remarkable and suggests fundamentally
different metabolic compartmentalization strategies.

## Biosynthetic Implications of Tissue-Specific Localization

The contrasting distribution patterns between psilocybin and psilocin across
tissues and strains have several biosynthetic implications. The consistent
presence of psilocybin in cap tissue across all three strains suggests that the
cap is a primary site of [[baeocystin-and-norbaeocystin-in-psilocybin-biosynthesis]] or accumulation. The gill
tissues beneath the cap are metabolically active and may contain the enzyme
systems responsible for tryptamine hydroxylation and phosphorylation.

The strain-dependent patterns of psilocin localization are more difficult to
interpret. The exclusive presence of psilocin in stems of the M.R. and
Equadorian strains, despite its absence in caps, could indicate that
dephosphorylation of psilocybin occurs primarily in stem tissue in these
strains. Alternatively, psilocin may be synthesized in the stem independently
of cap-resident psilocybin, or psilocin produced in the cap may be rapidly
re-phosphorylated to psilocybin in those strains.

The Amazon strain's pattern, with a trace of psilocin only in the cap, might
reflect a different phosphatase expression profile or a different intracellular
compartmentalization of the relevant enzymes. The fact that all three strains
showed minimal psilocin in the first flush suggests that the tissue-specific
distribution patterns observed are superimposed on the general flush-dependent
delay in [[psilocin-accumulation-flushes]].
