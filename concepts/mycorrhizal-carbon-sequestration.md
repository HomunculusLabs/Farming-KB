## Mycorrhizal Carbon Sequestration

## Overview

[[dighton-fungi-climate-change-feedback]] intensifies. [[singh-mycorrhizal-network-carbon-transfer-plants]] from host plants to mycorrhizal fungi occurs primarily as sugars,
predominantly glucose and fructose, across the fungal interface within root cortical
cells. In arbuscular mycorrhizas, the arbuscule provides the exchange surface; in
ectomycorrhizas, the Hartig net serves this function.

Once inside the fungal symbiont, carbon is metabolized through glycolysis and the
tricarboxylic acid cycle, supporting fungal respiration, growth, and the production
of extraradical hyphae. A significant fraction of assimilated carbon is converted
to fungal storage compounds, including trehalose and glycogen, and to structural
biomass rich in chitin and glucans.

The extraradical mycelium represents the primary carbon sink in the mycorrhizal
symbiosis. Carbon allocated to external hyphae extends the fungal foraging network
into soil volumes beyond the root depletion zone, enhancing nutrient capture while
simultaneously depositing carbon into the soil matrix through turnover and exudation.
