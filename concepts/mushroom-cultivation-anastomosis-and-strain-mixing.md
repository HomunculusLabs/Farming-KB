## Deliberate Strain Crossing

Anastomosis can also be used deliberately as a breeding tool. By growing two different dikaryotic strains on the same agar plate or in the same substrate, a cultivator can attempt to create a new hybrid strain. If the strains are compatible, their mycelia will fuse and potentially produce a novel genotype.

The challenge is that compatibility between dikaryotic strains is not guaranteed. In many species, vegetative compatibility groups (VCGs) restrict anastomosis to strains that are genetically similar. Unrelated strains may simply refuse to fuse, growing alongside each other without genetic exchange.
