## Taxonomic Overview

Pleurotus belongs to the family Pleurotaceae in the order Agaricales. Recent
phylogenetic studies have clarified relationships within the genus, revealing
several species complexes that were previously lumped together. DNA barcoding,
particularly using the ITS (Internal Transcribed Spacer) region, has become the
standard for accurate species identification, as morphological characteristics
alone are often insufficient to distinguish closely related species.

## Major Cultivated Species

### Pleurotus ostreatus (Pearl Oyster)

The classic oyster mushroom and the most widely cultivated species worldwide.
Fruiting bodies are shelf-like with a broad fan or oyster-shaped cap, typically
5-15 cm in diameter. Color ranges from white to grey to dark brown depending on
strain and growing conditions. P. ostreatus is cold-tolerant and fruits readily
at 10-18°C, making it popular for winter cultivation. It is a vigorous
colonizer with a wide substrate range — see [[growing-gourmet-medicinal-polysaccharides-mushroom-compounds]] research due to its high
beta-glucan content.

## Strain Selection

Within each species, significant genetic variation exists between strains.
Commercial strain banks maintain hundreds of isolates selected for specific
traits including yield, colonization speed, temperature range, color, shelf
life, and disease resistance. Strain selection is one of the most important
decisions in commercial cultivation — the right strain can double yields
compared to a poorly adapted one.

Key strain selection criteria:
- **Optimal fruiting temperature** — must match available growing conditions
- **Colonization speed** — faster colonization means less contamination risk
- **Biological efficiency** — grams fresh mushroom per gram dry substrate
- **Fruit body morphology** — size, shape, color, and clustering tendency
- **Shelf life** — critical for market viability
- **Substrate preference** — match strain to available substrate materials
