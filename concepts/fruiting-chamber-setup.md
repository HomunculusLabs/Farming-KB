### Humidity Management

The self-regulating evaporative model works as follows:
1. Casing soil moisture evaporates into the sealed bin air.
2. Polyethylene sheeting traps most moisture, maintaining high humidity.
3. Ventilation holes allow slow moisture escape, but evaporation from
   five trays easily compensates.

This means no hygrometer monitoring or specific humidity targeting.
The system naturally reaches equilibrium based on tray count, ambient
temperature, and ventilation rate.

## Cold Shock Treatment

Cold shocking exposes cased trays to cold temperatures before fruiting,
simulating seasonal change to trigger fruiting. The source provides
clear species-specific guidance:

**Species that benefit:** Harder-to-fruit strains benefit from one night
in the refrigerator before transfer to fruiting bins.

**Species that do NOT benefit:**
- *Psilocybe mexicana* (see [[psilocybe-tampanensis-cultivation]])
- *Panaeolus subbalteatus*
- Easy-fruiting strains such as Ecuador *P. cubensis*

Cold shocking non-responsive species wastes time and refrigerator
space without improving results.

## Daily Maintenance Routine

### Misting

The casings are misted each day and never allowed to dry out. Consistent
moisture is essential for pin development and mushroom maturation. A
fine mist spray bottle is the standard tool. Mist lightly and evenly,
avoiding heavy spraying that could dislodge small pins or pool water.

### Post-Flush Watering

Directly after a flush is picked, watering is increased because the
maturing mushrooms pull a lot of moisture from the casing soil. A
completed flush leaves the casing significantly drier than before
the flush began. Compensating for this moisture loss promptly helps
ensure that the next flush cycle initiates without excessive delay.

### Developing a Watering Feel

The source material notes that it is very difficult to give explicit
directions on a watering regime. The cultivator will have to develop
a "feeling" for it through experience. Factors that influence watering
needs include the species, strain, number of trays per bin, ambient
temperature and humidity, and the stage of the flush cycle.

### Abort Removal

Keeping the surface of the casing as clean as possible is important
for long-term productivity. Dead pinheads (aborts) should be removed
promptly, as these can lead to moulds showing up on the casing surface.
Aborts are pins that have stopped growing and begun to decompose,
becoming breeding grounds for contamination if left in place.

## Fruiting Timeline

Once cased trays are placed in the fruiting chamber, the following
timeline is typical:

1. **Pin initiation (Days 6 to 15):** Depending on the strain, the
   first pinheads will appear 6 to 15 days after putting the casings
   in the bins.
2. **Mushroom maturation (Days 5 to 7):** After pins appear, the
   mushrooms will mature in 5 to 7 days, after which they can be
   picked at the desired stage of maturity.
3. **Subsequent flushes:** The source authors normally let casings
   produce 3 flushes, but with proper watering they may produce 5 or
   even 6 flushes. Flush size typically diminishes with each
   successive crop.
