# Toxic Elements in Soils and Biological Element Concentration

Soils contain a spectrum of elements ranging from essential nutrients to deadly poisons. The difference between a beneficial trace element and a lethal contaminant is often a matter of concentration, chemical form, and biological context. This section covers the toxic elements that threaten soil health, food safety, and human wellbeing, followed by the biological mechanisms that concentrate, disperse, and cycle elements through living systems.

## Zinc (Zn)

Zinc is a critical micronutrient but can become toxic at elevated levels. It is deficient on leached sands, dunes, and alkaline sands, and should be tested for in all desert gardens.

### Application

Zinc sulphate spray on plants or zinc salts in seed pellets supply zinc cheaply. It is essential for tree establishment in many deserts or dunes and critical to human health.

### Deficiency Effects

Zinc is essential to growth control hormones in plants. Deficiency causes gross metabolic imbalances and stunting. In people with malnutrition, low zinc blood levels cause poor wound healing. **Diabetes is often linked with zinc deficiency.**

### Toxicity and Human Health

Excessive zinc acts as an emetic, causing vomiting and weakness. Zinc is necessary to several enzymes; prostate (seminal) fluids are high in zinc. Diets of coarse grains, unleavened bread, and low meat intake contribute to zinc deficiency in poor people, especially in high-calcium soils. Alcoholism, diabetes, feverish sweating, and stress all lower zinc levels.

Severe zinc deficiency causes:
- Hair loss
- Moist eczema of the mouth area
- Impotence
- Apathy
- Diarrhoea

## Arsenic (As)

Arsenic sometimes seems to be needed by horses and is included in chicken pellets, but it is a **poison to animals even at slight concentrations**. Polluting sources come from gold processing areas and from chickens fed on pelleted foods containing high arsenic levels. Arsenic contamination of soil and water from mining is a serious concern in many regions.

## Selenium (Se)

Selenium deficiency in lambs causes **"white muscle"** or **"still lamb"** disease, cured by selenium and vitamin E injection. It also causes "thrift" (failure to thrive) in animals in many countries where severe dietary deficiency occurs.

### Dosage and Sources

- **Five mg/year** is sufficient for sheep; more causes toxicity
- **Seaweeds** may supply sufficient selenium to gardens
- Both deficiency and excess are dangerous — the therapeutic window is narrow

## Strontium (Sr)

Strontium is becoming a widespread danger primarily through its radioactive isotope **Sr-90**, a long-term and very poisonous element from atomic plants. It now pollutes many areas and milk supplies.

### Radioactive Strontium Hazards

- Causes cancer at absorption sites
- Causes leukaemia in children
- Described as a "hidden cost" of the atomic age
- Excreted in urine of breast-fed babies
- **Concentrates 4–8 times in cows' milk** — levels must be monitored after atomic fallout
- **There are no safe levels**

Ordinary (non-radioactive) strontium is a trace element with normal biological function, but radioactive contamination makes this element a major environmental health concern.

## Molybdenum (Mo)

Molybdenum is a trace element essential for clover establishment and needed by all plants and [[heavy-metal-toxicity-and-biological-element-transport|Heavy]] organic soils to block plant uptake
- Removal of lead-concentrating vegetation for disposal
- Monitoring of [[earthworms]], which may concentrate lead to lethal levels in polluted soils

Lead contamination is one of the most widespread urban soil problems, affecting food gardens in cities worldwide. Adding salt to cooking water can block uptake of some heavy metals on plant proteins, providing a simple kitchen-level mitigation.

## Radon (Rn)

Radon is a gas from the decay of uranium. It seeps up through most soils, especially over volcanic areas and igneous rock.

### Building Hazards

- Can pollute super-insulated buildings if air is not exchanged
- Several million homes in the UK and USA have high radon levels
- **Avoid** building poorly ventilated homes in areas of radioactive ores, granites, and basalts
- Do not use such rocks or crushed metals in building materials
- If houses are super-insulated or draught-proofed, **adequate heat exchangers must be fitted** to bring in clean air

## Biological Element Transport and Concentration

Beyond the chemical properties of individual elements, biological processes play a critical role in concentrating, dispersing, and cycling elements through soil systems.
