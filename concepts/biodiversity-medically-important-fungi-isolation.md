# Medically Important Fungi: Isolation and Ecology

An important component of [[candida-albicans]], C. tropicalis, C. glabrata, C.
parapsilosis, and C. krusei are strongly associated with mammalian and
avian gastrointestinal tracts. Truly commensal, purely filamentous
fungi have not been encountered, as [[blastomyces-dermatitidis]] occurs in boreal eastern
Canada, the upper Midwest, and rarely in southern Africa. Histoplasma
capsulatum var. capsulatum inhabits the Mississippi River valley
system, associated with soils enriched by bird or bat guano, and also
occurs in Central and South America and Africa. Paracoccidioides
brasiliensis is associated with moist forested soils from southern
Mexico to northern Argentina. Coccidioides immitis occurs primarily in
desert soils of the American southwest and northern Mexico.

## Subcutaneous and Opportunistic Pathogens

Subcutaneous pathogens cause disease in immunologically normal hosts
when traumatically introduced into dermal layers. Prominent genera
include Fonsecaea, Cladophialophora, Exophiala, Phialophora, and
certain Hypocrealean anamorphs such as Acremonium falciforme and
[[selective-agents-fungal-isolation-antibiotics-fungicides]] media supplemented with antibacterial antibiotics.

Skin prick serological testing provides a rough measure of environmental
prevalence for Histoplasma capsulatum and Coccidioides immitis. Sterile
culture filtrates (histoplasmin, spherulin) are injected
subcutaneously; induration exceeding 5 mm at 48 hours indicates prior
exposure. Epidemiologically designed population surveys accurately
delineate endemic geographic regions. Compiling diagnostic immunology
results from medical laboratories, especially with patient travel
histories, provides less accurate but still useful distribution data.
More sensitive in vitro immunological tests can detect exposure or
active disease but are beyond the scope of standard biodiversity
surveys.
