## Mycelial Mat Formation

Mycelial mats are dense, coherent networks of fungal hyphae that
develop at or near soil surfaces, within organic litter layers, or
along root channels. The Singh source describes their formation as
a multi-step process:

- Initial colonization: Fungal hyphae exploit nutrient-rich zones
  (decomposing organic matter, root exudates) and extend radially
  through soil pore spaces.

- Hyphal aggregation: Through hyphal fusion (anastomosis), branching,
  and the production of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS),
  individual hyphae coalesce into coherent mat structures.

- Mat maturation: The mat thickens as biomass accumulates and becomes
  physically integrated with soil particles through hyphal entanglement
  and adhesive EPS production.

- Functional differentiation: Mature mats develop heterogeneous
  microenvironments with distinct oxygen, moisture, and nutrient
  gradients that support diverse microbial communities.

Key fungal genera involved in mat formation include Rhizoctonia,
Thanatephorus, Marasmius, and various basidiomycetes. White-rot fungi
cultivated for bioremediation can also form extensive mycelial mats
when grown on lignocellulosic substrates placed on contaminated soil.

## Erosion Control

Mycelial mats provide effective erosion control through:

- Surface stabilization: Mats physically anchor soil particles against
  wind and water displacement.

- Infiltration enhancement: The porous structure of mycelial mats
  increases water infiltration rates, reducing surface runoff and
  associated erosion.
