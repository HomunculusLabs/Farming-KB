# Mushroom Radiotherapy Adjunct Use

## Overview

Mushroom-derived polysaccharides have shown significant potential as
adjuncts to radiotherapy in cancer treatment. Radiotherapy, while
effective at eradicating loco-regional disease, invariably damages the
patient's immunological defenses and produces significant side effects.
[[growing-gourmet-medicinal-polysaccharides-mushroom-compounds]] can help mitigate these effects while potentially
enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of radiation.

## The Problem with Radiotherapy Alone

While surgery and radiotherapy are effective means of eradicating
loco-regional disease, they are of little value against distant
metastases. Furthermore, radiotherapy invariably damages or weakens
the patient's immunological defenses, which may already have been
compromised by the cancer itself. Within the holistic approach of
clinical cancer therapy, there is now increasing emphasis on patient
quality of life following treatment. Survival should not be the sole
criterion for assessing treatment results. The oncologist should
combine all available disciplines that could contribute to patient
welfare after the main treatment has attempted to destroy the primary
cancer site.

## Mushroom Polysaccharides as Radioprotective Adjuncts

Several mushroom-derived compounds have demonstrated radioprotective
properties when used alongside radiation therapy:

### PSP and Radiotherapy Synergy

PSP has shown particular promise as a radiotherapy adjunct. Studies
demonstrated that PSP combined with radiation produced a significant
increase in the percentage of apoptotic cells at 24 hours compared
with radiation alone. The antitumour mechanism of PSP action may
involve the induction of DNA damage by apoptosis in the target cancer
cells. This synergistic effect is significant because it suggests that
PSP may sensitize cancer cells to the effects of radiation, making the
radiotherapy more effective at killing tumour cells while
simultaneously protecting healthy tissue through [[cancer-immune-modulation-mechanisms]].

### PSK and Radiotherapy

PSK has been studied in combination with radiotherapy in multiple
settings. In non-controlled, retrospective analyses of combined
radiation, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy (using PSK or OK-32),
results have shown prolonged survival, especially for patients with
gastric and colorectal carcinomas.

### Lentinan and Radiotherapy

Lentinan has demonstrated anti-tumour effects that complement
radiotherapy. Its primary mechanism -- activation of the [[fungal-virulence-factors-host-immune-evasion]]
system -- addresses one of radiotherapy's major weaknesses: the
suppression of immune function that accompanies radiation treatment.

## Hematopoietic Protection During Radiotherapy

A common adverse reaction of radiotherapy (and chemotherapy) is
haematopoietic toxicity -- damage to the bone marrow's ability to
produce blood cells. Several mushroom-derived compounds have shown
strong amelioration of these toxic effects:

- PSP has been shown to improve peripheral blood counts during
  radiation treatment
- Studies by Shiu et al. (1992) and Sun et al. (1999) documented the
  haematopoietic protective effects of PSP
- The preservation of blood cell counts during radiotherapy is
  crucial for maintaining the patient's immune function and overall
  health during treatment

## The Double-Blind Shanghai Trial

A double-blind Phase II trial in Shanghai hospitals enrolled almost 300
patients suffering from gastric, oesophageal, or [[blesching-cannabis-lung-cancer]] treated
with conventional radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy together with PSP or
shark liver oil (batyl alcohol). PSP improved overall clinical
symptoms, together with most symptoms associated with cancer therapy,
and was found to be effective for 82% of patients compared with 48%
for the control substance. This trial demonstrated that the benefits
