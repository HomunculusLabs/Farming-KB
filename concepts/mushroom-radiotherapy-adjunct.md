# Mushroom Radiotherapy Adjunct Use ## Overview Mushroom-derived polysaccharides have shown significant potential as adjuncts to radiotherapy in cancer treatment. Radiotherapy, while effective at eradicating loco-regional disease, invariably damages the patient's immunological defenses and produces significant side effects. Mushroom compounds can help mitigate these effects while potentially enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of radiation. ## The Problem with Radiotherapy Alone While surgery and radiotherapy are effective means of eradicating loco-regional disease, they are of little value against distant metastases. Furthermore, radiotherapy invariably damages or weakens the patient's immunological defenses, which may already have been compromised by the cancer itself. Within the holistic approach of clinical cancer therapy, there is now increasing emphasis on patient quality of life following treatment. Survival should not be the sole criterion for assessing treatment results. The oncologist should combine all available disciplines that could contribute to patient welfare after the main treatment has attempted to destroy the primary cancer site. ## Mushroom Polysaccharides as Radioprotective Adjuncts Several mushroom-derived compounds have demonstrated radioprotective properties when used alongside radiation therapy: ### PSP and Radiotherapy Synergy PSP has shown particular promise as a radiotherapy adjunct. Studies demonstrated that PSP combined with radiation produced a significant increase in the percentage of apoptotic cells at 24 hours compared with radiation alone. The antitumour mechanism of PSP action may involve the induction of DNA damage by apoptosis in the target cancer cells. This synergistic effect is significant because it suggests that PSP may sensitize cancer cells to the effects of radiation, making the radiotherapy more effective at killing tumour cells while simultaneously protecting healthy tissue through immune modulation. ### PSK and Radiotherapy PSK has been studied in combination with radiotherapy in multiple settings. In non-controlled, retrospective analyses of combined radiation, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy (using PSK or OK-32), results have shown prolonged survival, especially for patients with gastric and colorectal carcinomas. ### Lentinan and Radiotherapy Lentinan has demonstrated anti-tumour effects that complement radiotherapy. Its primary mechanism -- activation of the host immune system -- addresses one of radiotherapy's major weaknesses: the suppression of immune function that accompanies radiation treatment. ## Hematopoietic Protection During Radiotherapy A common adverse reaction of radiotherapy (and chemotherapy) is haematopoietic toxicity -- damage to the bone marrow's ability to produce blood cells. Several mushroom-derived compounds have shown strong amelioration of these toxic effects: - PSP has been shown to improve peripheral blood counts during radiation treatment - Studies by Shiu et al. (1992) and Sun et al. (1999) documented the haematopoietic protective effects of PSP - The preservation of blood cell counts during radiotherapy is crucial for maintaining the patient's immune function and overall health during treatment ## The Double-Blind Shanghai Trial A double-blind Phase II trial in Shanghai hospitals enrolled almost 300 patients suffering from gastric, oesophageal, or lung cancer treated with conventional radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy together with PSP or shark liver oil (batyl alcohol). PSP improved overall clinical symptoms, together with most symptoms associated with cancer therapy, and was found to be effective for 82% of patients compared with 48% for the control substance. This trial demonstrated that the benefits of mushroom adjuncts during radiotherapy extend beyond direct tumour effects to include improved quality of life, better clinical symptom management, and enhanced haematological parameters. ## Mechanisms of Action The mechanisms by which mushroom polysaccharides enhance radiotherapy include: 1. **Apoptosis sensitization**: Mushroom compounds may make cancer cells more susceptible to radiation-induced apoptosis 2. **Immune restoration**: By boosting immune function, mushroom compounds help compensate for radiation-induced immunosuppression 3. **Haematopoietic protection**: Preservation of bone marrow function during radiation treatment 4. **Anti-inflammatory effects**: Reduction of radiation-induced inflammation and tissue damage 5. **Antioxidant activity**: Some mushroom compounds may help protect healthy cells from radiation-induced oxidative damage ## Clinical Implications The combination of mushroom polysaccharides with radiotherapy represents a promising strategy for improving cancer treatment outcomes. The approach is particularly relevant because: - Radiotherapy remains a cornerstone of cancer treatment for many tumour types - The side effects of radiotherapy are a major limiting factor in treatment - Mushroom compounds are well-tolerated and can be administered orally - The cost of mushroom-derived adjuncts is relatively low compared to many pharmaceutical options ## Research Needs More systematic studies are needed to: - Establish optimal dosing schedules for mushroom compounds alongside radiotherapy - Identify which tumour types benefit most from mushroom adjuncts during radiation - Determine the molecular mechanisms underlying radiosensitization - Conduct large-scale randomized controlled trials with standardized preparations ## See Also - [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]]
