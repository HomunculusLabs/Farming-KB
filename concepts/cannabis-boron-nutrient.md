# Cannabis Boron (B)

## Overview

Boron is a [[cannabis-indoor-soil-mix-and-preparation]] classified as **immobile** in cannabis plants. Boron deficiencies seldom occur indoors. Boron must be available during the entire life of a plant. It is still somewhat of a biochemical mystery, but research has established several critical roles. Boron and [[cannabis-calcium-nutrient]] are poor candidates for [[cannabis-foliar-feeding]] because they translocate poorly.

## Role in Plant Biology

Boron remains somewhat of a biochemical mystery compared to other nutrients, but research continues to uncover its critical roles. Boron is unique among micronutrients because it is absorbed by plants as the undissociated boric acid molecule (B(OH)3) rather than as an ion. Scientists have collected evidence suggesting boron helps with:

- Synthesis and formation of nucleic acid (RNA uracil)
- Cell division, differentiation, maturation, and respiration
- Pollen germination and seed development
- Calcium uptake and numerous plant functions
- Cell wall formation and structural integrity
- Sugar transport through phloem tissue
- Membrane integrity and function

Strong evidence supports boron's role in cell division, differentiation, maturation, and respiration as well as a link to pollen germination. Boron helps with calcium uptake and is involved in multiple fundamental plant processes that are still being studied. Research continues to uncover the full extent of boron's role in plant biochemistry, but its importance for cell wall formation and membrane integrity is well-established.

## Mobility in Soil

Boron is **immobile** within the cannabis plant -- once deposited in tissue, it cannot be relocated to newer growth. In the soil, boron is moderately mobile and is subject to leaching, particularly in sandy, acidic soils with high rainfall. Boron availability is highest in acidic to neutral soils (pH 5.5-6.5) and decreases in alkaline conditions above pH 7.0. Boron also tends to accumulate in the leaf margins and tips, which is why toxicity symptoms first appear in these areas. Drought conditions can increase boron concentration in the soil solution and push levels into the toxic range.

## Interactions with Other Nutrients

Boron's most important nutrient interaction is with [[cannabis-calcium-nutrient]]. Boron helps facilitate calcium uptake and transport within the plant. When boron is deficient, calcium uptake is impaired even when calcium levels in the soil are adequate. High levels of calcium can also reduce boron availability. This synergistic relationship means that boron and calcium should be managed together. Excess cannabis-potassium|potassium can also reduce boron uptake. During flowering, boron, calcium, and iron all move slowly, and supplemental doses may help when growth slows.

## Organic Sources

[[organic-fertilizer-sources-guide]] of boron include:

- Compost (variable, depends on feedstock)
- Well-rotted manures
- Seaweed and kelp products (good natural source)
- Boric acid (inorganic, common treatment)
- Solubor (sodium tetraborate)

For prevention rather than treatment, adding kelp meal to the soil mix before planting provides a slow, steady supply of boron alongside other micronutrients.

## Deficiency

Boron deficiency is relatively rare in indoor cannabis cultivation. Usually causes no problems, but when it does occur, the symptoms can be severe and affect both above-ground and below-ground growth. Because boron is immobile, deficiency symptoms appear first in the upper, new leaves on top of the plant.

### Above-Ground Symptoms

- Stem tips and root tips grow abnormally
- Growing shoots look burned (may be confused with HID light burn)
- First leaves thicken and become brittle
- Top shoots contort and/or turn dark, followed by progressively lower-growing shoots
- When severe, growing tips die
- Leaf margins discolor and die back in places
- Necrotic spots develop between leaf veins
- Deficient leaves become thick, distorted, and wilted with chlorotic and necrotic spotting

### Below-Ground Symptoms

- Root tips often swell, discolor, and stop elongating
- Root steles (insides) often become mushy
- Mushy roots are perfect hosts for rot and disease organisms

### Deficiency Progression Timeline

1. Growing tips and root tips show abnormal growth
2. First leaves thicken and become brittle
3. Top shoots contort and/or turn dark
4. Growing tips die if severe
5. Leaf margins discolor and necrotic spots develop between veins

### Treatment

- Apply one teaspoon of boric acid per gallon of water as a soil drench
- Alternatively, apply as a [[cannabis-foliar-feeding]] for faster response
- Apply hydroponic micronutrients containing boron
- Hydroponic gardeners should keep boron dosage below 20 ppm because boron quickly becomes toxic if concentrated in the solution

During flowering, boron, calcium, and iron move slowly. A supplemental foliar dose often speeds the growth when it slows. However, calcium and boron are poor candidates for foliar feeding because they translocate poorly, so results may be limited.

## Toxicity

Boron can quickly become toxic if concentrated in the nutrient solution. This makes boron one of the micronutrients that requires careful monitoring in hydroponic systems.

### Symptoms

- Leaf tips yellow first
- As toxic conditions progress, margins become necrotic toward the center of the leaf
- After the leaves yellow, they fall off

### Prevention

Avoid using excessive amounts of boric acid-based insecticides. Keep boron levels below 20 ppm in hydroponic solutions. Monitor carefully when using micronutrient supplements. The relatively narrow range between optimal (0.5-5.0 ppm) and toxic levels means careful measurement is important.

## Nutrient Requirements

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Optimal range | 0.5-5.0 ppm |
| Average target | 1.0 ppm |
| Maximum (hydroponic) | Below 20 ppm |

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing boron deficiency tip burn with HID light burn (very similar appearance)
- Over-applying boric acid in hydroponics (narrow range between optimal and toxic)
- Using boric acid-based insecticides without accounting for cumulative boron in the nutrient solution
- Relying on foliar feeding for boron correction (poor translocation through leaves)
- Failing to consider boron when calcium deficiency symptoms persist despite adequate calcium levels
- Not distinguishing between above-ground and below-ground boron deficiency symptoms
