### Soil as a Thin Living Mantle

The mantle of soil and subsoil covering the earth is **as thin as the shine on the skin of an orange**. This mantle extends as living mud below the waters of earth as well as on land. It is composed of five elements:

1. **Minerals** — mainly silica, oxides of iron and aluminium, and complex minerals
2. **Soil waters** — fresh, saline, with differing pH, dissolved minerals and gases
3. **Gases** — some from the atmosphere, others emitted by rock breakdown and the earth's interior
4. **Life forms** — from fungal spores and bacteria to wombats and ground squirrels, from massive roots to minute motile algae
5. **Once-living remains** — the humus of the earth; decayed, compressed, and fossil organic material

Soils rarely extend much below 1-2 metres, and are more often a living system only **6-12 cm deep**. Subsoils, lacking the life components, are far less productive. Buried soils or deep washed silts are rare and confined to valley floors, deltas, and glacial mounds.

## Soil Particle Fractions

### The Jar Test Method

A simple and effective method for estimating the proportion of clay, silt, sand, and coarse particles:

1. Mix a sample of soil from a few typical sites
2. Pour one cup of soil in a tall jar, filled almost with water
3. Shake vigorously
4. Let the soil fractions settle out over a day to a week (clay can remain in suspension for up to a week)
5. Observe the distinct layers that form

This test is useful for:
- **Dam building assessment** — 40% or so clay is needed for dam walls
- **Mud brick construction** — less clay is needed for good mud bricks (without lime or cement)
- **Crop suitability** — different crops thrive in different soil textures

### Particle Size Classification

| Fraction | Size Range |
|----------|-----------|
| Coarse particles | > 2 mm |
| Sand | 0.05–2 mm |
| Silt | 0.02–0.05 mm |
| Clay | < 0.002 mm |

One gram of clay has a surface area of up to **1000 times** that of one gram of sand. This enormous surface area gives clay its distinctive properties: high water retention, plasticity when wet, and hardness when dry.

### Crumb Structure and Pore Space

Soil crumb structure, aided by lime (calcium), bonds the different fractions together and creates **20-60% pore space**. The organic materials and gels hold the structure open in rain, creating spaces where plant nutrients can become soluble for absorption by roots. This pore space is critical for:

- **Water infiltration** and retention
- **Root penetration** and growth
- **Gas exchange** — oxygen reaches roots, carbon dioxide escapes
- **Microbial habitat** — the pore spaces host billions of beneficial organisms

### USDA Classification

The USDA classifies soil types by particle size ratios (the soil texture triangle), plotting the percentages of sand, silt, and clay to determine textural classes such as sandy loam, clay loam, silty clay, and so on. This standardised system allows consistent communication about soil properties across regions and disciplines.
