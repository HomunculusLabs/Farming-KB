## Multi-Species Integrated Grazing

Fukuoka proposes moving beyond single-species grazing to integrated
multi-species systems. When pigs, chickens, and rabbits graze together
with cows in an orchard, each species contributes to the health of the
system. The pigs like to root up the ground looking for insects and
earthworms in valleys and damp areas, functioning as small tractors that
dig up the soil. Sowing clover and grain in the turned soil, combined
with cow and pig droppings, produces fine pasturage. Once this pasture
grass begins to nourish, chickens, goats, and rabbits can be raised in
the same environment.

## Animals in Forest and Woodland

Even fields covered with thick growth of wild roses and creepers that
seem worthless for grazing can be used to raise goats and sheep, which
love to feed on these intractable shrubs and vines and could clean up
the undergrowth in the densest jungle. Cows can be raised in mixed woods
or even in mountain forests planted with Japanese cypress or pine.
Grasses and underbrush have to be cut for the first seven or eight years
after planting trees on a mountain, but the labor of cutting can be
eliminated by raising cows. The planted saplings remain almost entirely
unaffected, because animals in nature do not indiscriminately ravage
anything unrelated to what they eat.
