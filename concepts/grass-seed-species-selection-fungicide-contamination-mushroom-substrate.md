## The Soak Method

As an alternative to the direct-mix method (measuring water and dry seed into the jar), seed can be **soaked overnight** in water before jarring. This method:

- Produces a more **homogenous substrate** with more uniform moisture distribution throughout the jar
- Allows excess water to be drained off before jarring, reducing the risk of over-hydration
- Gives the seed time to fully hydrate, which can improve consistency between batches
- Can be combined with a brief hot water soak (70-80°C for 30 minutes) to reduce the bacterial load on the seed surface

After soaking, the seed is drained and then loaded into jars for sterilization. No additional water is added.

## Cost Considerations

The source emphasizes finding **inexpensive seed** as an important practical consideration. Mushroom cultivation on grass seed is already a low-cost method compared to specialized mycological substrates, but buying overpriced seed from garden centers can significantly increase the per-jar cost. Animal feed stores, agricultural cooperatives, and online bulk seed suppliers typically offer the best value.

For context, a 1 kg bag of grass seed can fill approximately 9 jars (110 grams per jar). At animal feed store prices, this typically represents a cost of less than $1 USD per jar in substrate materials.

## Water-to-Seed Ratio Science

The recommended ratio of **180 ml water per 110 grams of grass seed** in a 720 ml jar is calibrated for average rye grass seed (Lolium perenne). This ratio provides approximately 60-65% moisture content by weight in the finished substrate, which is near the optimal range for fungal growth (55-70% for most cultivated species).

The water absorption capacity of grass seed depends on several factors:

- **Seed coat integrity** — intact seed coats limit water uptake; cracked or damaged seeds absorb more
- **Endosperm composition** — starch-rich seeds absorb more water than protein-rich seeds
- **Batch variation** — seeds harvested in different years or from different fields can have different physical properties
- **Storage conditions** — seeds stored in humid conditions may have partially pre-absorbed moisture, requiring less added water

Cultivators should test each new batch of seed by preparing a single jar and observing the results after sterilization. If the grain is stuck together in a clump (too wet), reduce water by 10-15 ml. If the grain appears dry and loose with no visible moisture (too dry), increase water by 10-15 ml.

## Alternative Substrates and Comparison

While grass seed is the focus of this cultivation method, other substrates are commonly used for the same species:

| Substrate | Pros | Cons |

| **Grass seed** | Cheap, small kernels, many inoculation points | Variable water absorption |

Rye grain is specifically noted as **preferred for [[psilocybe-mexicana]] and P. tampanensis, particularly for [[sclerotia-production]].

## Hot Water Soak Treatment

An advanced variation of the overnight soak method is the **hot water soak**, which serves a dual purpose of hydrating the seed and reducing the bacterial load on the seed surface:

1. Bring water to 70-80°C (well below boiling)
2. Pour over the grass seed in a container
3. Let soak for 30 minutes at this temperature
4. Allow to cool to room temperature (or continue soaking overnight)
5. Drain thoroughly and load into jars

The 70-80°C temperature is high enough to kill many surface bacteria and their spores but low enough to avoid gelatinizing the starches in the seed, which would make the substrate sticky and anaerobic. This pretreatment can reduce contamination rates significantly, especially when working without a laminar flow hood.
