## Ayahuasca Churches
Several Brazilian syncretic religions incorporate ayahuasca as a central
sacrament, each with distinct ceremonial characteristics:
- **Santo Daime**: Founded in the 1930s by Raimundo Irineu Serra in the
  Amazonian state of Acre. Ceremonies involve drinking daime (ayahuasca) while
  singing standardized hymns (hinarios) and dancing in coordinated patterns.
  The church has spread internationally, with centers in Europe, North America,
  and Japan.
- **Uniao do Vegetal (UDV)**: Founded in the 1960s by Jose Gabriel da Costa.
  UDV ceremonies are more sedentary, with participants seated in concentric
  circles drinking hoasca (ayahuasca) while engaging in structured study and
  discussion periods alternating with periods of focused concentration.
- **Barquinha**: A smaller tradition incorporating elements of African-Brazilian
  spiritual practices with ayahuasca use.
The Brazilian government legally recognized these churches, and the UDV
successfully defended its right to use ayahuasca in the U.S. Supreme Court case
Gonzalez v. O Centro Espirita Beneficente Uniao do Vegetal (2006). The
standardized ritual structure, peer support, and integration of the experience
within a moral and ethical framework characterize all three traditions.
