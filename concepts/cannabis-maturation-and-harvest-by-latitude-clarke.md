# Cannabis Maturation and Harvest by Latitude (Clarke)

## Maturation Fundamentals

The maturation of Cannabis is normally annual and influenced by:
- Age of the plant (about two months minimum for flowering)
- Changes in photoperiod (primary trigger)
- Other environmental conditions (temperature, moisture)

When nights lengthen following the summer solstice (June 21-22), flowering begins. This triggers the reproductive phase, followed by senescence and eventual death.

## Latitude and Photoperiod

Change in photoperiod is the factor that usually triggers the developmental stages of Cannabis. Photoperiod and seasonal cycles are determined by latitude.

### Near the Equator (0-15 degrees)
- Most even photoperiods and mildest seasonal variations
- Photoperiod never varies out of the range critical for THC production (10-14 hours)
- Two identical photoperiodic seasons per year (sun passes north and south of equator)
- Possible to grow two fully mature Cannabis crops per year at some locations
- Strains from these latitudes are often not as responsive to photoperiod change; flowering is strongly age-determined as well as light-determined
- Most strains begin flowering at 60 days old if photoperiod does not exceed 13 hours
- Colombia, southern India, Thailand, and Malawi all lie on the fringes (10-15 degrees latitude)

### Northern and Southern Subtropical Zones (15-30 degrees)
- Largest Cannabis-producing areas in the world (northern zone)
- Long season from February-March through October-December
- Short season may also exist from December-January through March-April (90-120 days)
- In Hawaii, a third short season is sometimes used (June-September or September-December)
- Southeast Asia, Hawaii, Mexico, Jamaica, Pakistan, Nepal, and India are all in this zone
- Some of the world's most potent Cannabis is grown in the northern subtropical zone

### Northern and Southern Temperate Zones (30-60 degrees)
- One medium to long season from March-May through September-December
- Central China, Korea, Japan, U.S., southern Europe, Morocco, Turkey, Lebanon, Iran, Afghanistan, and Pakistan
- Many nations produce both fiber and drug Cannabis
- Shorter season favors early-maturing strains

### Arctic and Antarctic Zones (60-70 degrees)
- Short, harsh growing season not favorable for Cannabis
- Season begins during long June-July days, continues until September-October freezes
- Plants often get quite large but do not mature completely
- Cultivation of drug Cannabis not practical without a greenhouse
- Rapidly maturing, acclimatized hybrid strains from temperate North America are best suited
- Russian Cannabis ruderalis could yield very short-season drug strains through breeding programs

## Strain Adaptation to Native Latitude

Strains adapted to their native latitude will tend to flower and mature under domestic cultivation in much the same pattern:
- Mexican strains (subtropical) usually completely mature by end of October in northern temperate areas
- Colombian strains (equatorial) usually will not mature until December in the same location
- Strains should be selected from latitudes similar to the area to be cultivated
- Short-season strains from Hawaii or Mexico may not do well in temperate areas meant for long-season cultivation

## Moon Cycles

Since ancient times, planting and harvest dates based on moon cycles have been observed:
- The moon's 28-29 day cycle is divided into four one-week phases
- Best time for planting: waxing moon (phases 1-2)
- Best time for harvest: waning moon (phases 3-4)
- Exact new moons, full moons, and quarter moons are avoided as times of interplanetary stress
- Planting, germinating, grafting, and layering favored during phases 1-2
- Harvesting and pruning favored during phases 3-4

## Staminate vs. Pistillate Maturation

- Staminate plants usually begin flowering first and releasing pollen
- Much pollen is released when pistillate plants show only a few primordial flowers
- Early pollination is advantageous: seeds ripen in warm summer when the plant is healthy
- After a month or more of shedding pollen, staminate plants enter senescence
- Pistillate plants continue developing up to three months longer as they mature seeds
- New pistillate flowers are continually produced and fertilized over several months

## Harvest Timing Indicators

### Visual Cues
- Falling pistils mark the end of resin secretion (at temperate latitudes due to low temperatures)
- Resin may still continue being secreted at equatorial and subtropical latitudes
- Trichomes change color as resins mature (transparent to amber/opaque)
- Calyxes swell with secreted resins

### Resin Maturity
- Peak floral stage with maximum THC and terpene production
- As resins age, they change color; transparent amber resins indicate active biosynthesis
- When biosynthesis ceases, resins turn opaque as cannabinoid levels decline
- Capitate-stalked trichomes may still be elongating and secreting during peak floral stage

### Environmental Factors
- Arid conditions may increase resin production (possibly a protective response)
- Low temperatures slow or stop resin secretion
- The usual aim of timing harvest is ensuring high THC with the desired balance of aromatic terpenes
