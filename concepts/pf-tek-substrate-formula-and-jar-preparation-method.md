# PF Tek Substrate Formula and Jar Preparation Method

## Overview

The PF TEK ([[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]* using common household materials. The core innovation was the **[[pf-substrate-formula]]** — a simple mixture of **brown rice powder**, **vermiculite**, and **water** — which eliminated the need for expensive laboratory equipment and specialized knowledge. The technique was widely copied and became the foundation for modern home [[mycelial-colonization]].
- **Distilled water**: Provides moisture for both [[mycelial-growth-and-colonization]] [[growing-gourmet-atmospheric-steam-sterilization]]. Distilled water is preferred to avoid introducing mineral contaminants or chlorine.

### The Role of Vermiculite

The original PF TEK described vermiculite as the "magic ingredient" and the "secret" of the technique. Its importance cannot be overstated:

- **Water reservoir**: Vermiculite can absorb and retain 3–4 times its weight in water, providing a sustained moisture supply throughout the colonization period
- **Aeration**: The exfoliated (expanded) structure creates air pockets within the substrate, allowing gas exchange for the mycelium
- **Structural scaffold**: Mycelial threads stretch across the vermiculite particles, creating a three-dimensional colonization network
- **Contamination resistance**: The loose, well-aerated structure is less favorable for anaerobic contaminants than dense grain substrates
- **Fruiting surface**: The exposed vermiculite layer at the top of the jar provides an ideal microclimate for pin formation

### Comparison with Traditional Grain Methods

Traditional mycological cultivation used whole grain (rye, wheat, millet) as substrate. The PF TEK offered several advantages:

| Property | PF Substrate | Whole Grain |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Preparation | Simple mixing | Requires soaking, simmering, draining |
| Sterilization | Steam (no [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]]) | [[pf-tek-pressure-canner-sterilization]] typically required |
| Aeration | Excellent (vermiculite) | Poor (grains pack tightly) |
| Water retention | High | Moderate |
| Contamination rate | Low | Higher (grain bacteria) |
| Cost | Very low | Moderate |

The key innovation was that the vermiculite-based substrate could be **steam sterilized** (boiling water bath) rather than requiring a **pressure canner** (autoclave), dramatically lowering the barrier to entry for home cultivators.

## Jar Preparation

### Container Selection

The PF TEK uses standard **1/2 pint (approximately 240 mL) canning jars**:

- **Wide-mouth** jars are preferred for easier removal of the colonized substrate cake
- Regular-mouth jars can be used but make cake extraction more difficult
- The small jar size ensures thorough steam penetration during sterilization
- Jars must have metal lids with a central hole for inoculation

### Substrate Mixing Ratios

The standard PF substrate ratio (per 1/2 pint jar):

- 1/2 cup vermiculite
- 1/4 cup brown rice powder
- Approximately 1/4 cup water (adjusted for moisture content)

The mixing procedure is:

1. **Vermiculite first**: Place vermiculite in a bowl and add water, mixing until the vermiculite is evenly moistened but not dripping
2. **Add brown rice powder**: Sprinkle the brown rice powder over the moistened vermiculite and mix thoroughly
3. **Load jars**: Fill each jar to within approximately 1/2 inch of the top with the substrate mixture
4. **Clean the rim**: Wipe the inside of the jar rim clean of any substrate material
5. **Dry vermiculite layer**: Add a 1/4 to 1/2 inch layer of dry, uncontaminated vermiculite on top of the substrate. This barrier layer serves as a **contamination filter**, preventing airborne contaminants from reaching the nutritious substrate during and after inoculation

### Lid Preparation

The canning jar lids are modified for the PF TEK:

- A small hole (approximately 1/8 inch or 3 mm diameter) is punched or drilled through the center of each lid
- This hole serves as the **inoculation port** for introducing the spore syringe needle
- Some practitioners cover the hole with micropore tape or polyfill for additional contamination protection
- The metal lid ring holds the modified lid in place during sterilization and colonization

### Moisture Content

Achieving the correct moisture content is critical:

- **Too dry**: Mycelium colonizes slowly or fails to grow; substrate cracks and pulls away from jar walls
