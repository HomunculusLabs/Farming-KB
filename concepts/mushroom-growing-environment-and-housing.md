## Mushroom Growing Environment and Housing

## Environmental Factors for Mushroom Growth

Successful mushroom cultivation depends on managing four critical
environmental factors: temperature, humidity, light, and ventilation.
Requirements shift significantly between the vegetative and reproductive
phases of the [[mushroom-life-cycle]].

### Temperature

- **Mycelial Growth Phase:** Optimal temperatures range from 20°C to 30°C,
  depending on species and strain. Pleurotus ostreatus grows well at 20-25°C,
  while tropical species like P. sajor-caju prefer 25-30°C. Growth declines
  sharply outside this range; above 35°C, mycelial growth stops entirely.

- **Fruiting Phase:** A significant temperature drop triggers fruiting. Most
  [[oyster-mushroom-species]] fruit best at 10-20°C. Cold-weather strains may
  fruit at 5-15°C, while warm-weather strains require 15-25°C.

### Humidity

- **Substrate Moisture:** Must be maintained at 60-75% by weight throughout
  the growing cycle. During fruiting, water is actively transpired through
  mushrooms, requiring replenishment through misting.

- **Relative Humidity:** Ambient RH must be 80-95% during fruiting. Below
  80%, fruiting bodies dry out, crack, and yields decline. Above 95%,
  condensation promotes bacterial blotch and other moisture-related diseases.
  During the vegetative phase, ambient humidity is less critical.

### Light

- **Vegetative Phase:** Mycelial growth is best in darkness or very dim
  light. Light is not required and can sometimes inhibit colonization.

- **Reproductive Phase:** Moderate indirect light (50-500 lux) is essential
  for normal fruiting body development. Without adequate light, stems
  elongate excessively and caps fail to develop properly. Direct sunlight
  should be avoided; diffuse natural light or fluorescent lighting on a
  12-hour cycle is generally sufficient.

### Ventilation

- **Vegetative Phase:** Minimal air exchange needed. Moderate CO2 levels
  (5,000-10,000 ppm) may even stimulate mycelial growth.

- **Reproductive Phase:** Fresh air exchange is essential. Elevated CO2 causes
  stem elongation, small caps, and reduced yield. Adequate ventilation
  (4-8 air changes per hour) maintains CO2 below 1,000 ppm. Excessive
  ventilation can reduce humidity below the critical threshold.

## Growing House Design Considerations

A [[low-cost-mushroom-growing-house-construction-developing-countries]] provides a controlled environment for production.
Key design considerations include:

- **Site Selection:** Access to clean water, electricity, drainage, and wind
  protection. Proximity to substrate and spawn sources reduces costs. The
  site should be away from contamination sources such as composting
  facilities, industrial pollution, and heavy pesticide use.

- **Construction Materials:** Must provide insulation, moisture resistance,
  and stable internal conditions. Common materials include concrete floors,
  bamboo or timber framing, thatch or corrugated metal roofing, and plastic
  sheeting for humidity retention.

- **Essential Functions:** Provide shade, maintain temperature range, sustain
  high humidity, allow controlled ventilation, and protect from pests and
  weather. The structure should facilitate cleaning and disinfection between
  crop cycles.

## Growing House Examples by Region

The MushWorld handbook documents growing house designs adapted to local
conditions, materials, and budgets:

- **Simple Shade Structures:** Open-sided structures with thatched or shaded
  roofs. They rely on natural ventilation and humidity, are inexpensive, and
  suit small-scale operations in favorable climates.

- **Mud Houses:** Common in South Asia and Africa. Mud or earth-bag
  construction provides excellent thermal mass, moderating temperature
  fluctuations. Mud walls absorb moisture, helping maintain humidity. Low-
  cost and built from locally available materials.

- **Bamboo Structures:** Widely used in Southeast Asia. Bamboo frames with
  plastic sheeting or woven bamboo walls provide lightweight, affordable
  housing with adjustable ventilation openings for environmental control.

- **Insulated Panel Houses:** Modern commercial operations use prefabricated
  insulated panels (foam insulation between metal or plastic sheets) for
  superior [[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]]. Often equipped with automatic
  systems for temperature, humidity, ventilation, and lighting. Highest
  level of control but requires significant capital investment.

## Growing Methods

- **Log Cultivation:** Freshly cut hardwood logs are inoculated with plug or
  sawdust spawn and left outdoors in shaded, humid areas. Colonization takes
  6-12 months. Low-cost but slow and weather-dependent.

- **Bag Cultivation:** The most widely used method globally. Substrate is
  packed into polyethylene or polypropylene bags, sterilized or pasteurized,
  inoculated with spawn, and incubated. Holes are cut for mushroom emergence
  during fruiting. Efficient, scalable, and allows high-density production.

- **Shelf (Tray) Cultivation:** Pasteurized substrate is spread in shallow
  layers on shelves or trays. Maximizes floor space and allows easy
  environmental management. Requires more infrastructure than bag cultivation
  but enables mechanized handling in large operations.

- **Bottle Cultivation:** Substrate packed into glass or plastic bottles,
  sterilized, and inoculated. Common in East Asia for specialty mushrooms.
  Provides excellent hygiene and uniform fruiting but involves higher labor
  and material costs per unit.
