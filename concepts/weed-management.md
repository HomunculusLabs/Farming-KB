# Weed Management

Weeds are plants growing where humans don't want them. In regenerative homesteading, the goal is management — not eradication. Many "weeds" are [[dynamic-accumulators]], edible, medicinal, or provide habitat for beneficial insects. The most effective long-term strategy is building healthy, competitive plant communities through [[no-till-farming]], [[cover-cropping]], and dense planting.

## Weed Ecology

### Why Weeds Appear

Weeds are pioneer species — nature's first responders to disturbed, bare, or compacted soil. Their presence signals specific soil conditions:

| Weed Indicator | Likely Soil Condition |
|---|---|
| Dandelion, plantain, chicory | Compacted soil, low calcium |
| Lamb's quarters, pigweed | Rich, high-nitrogen soil |
| Purslane | Moist, fertile soil |
| Thistle, bindweed | Compacted, low-fertility soil |
| Moss, sorrel | Acidic, low-calcium soil |
| Knotweed | Compacted, high-traffic areas |
| Nettles | Rich, moist, nitrogen-heavy soil |
| Mullein | Poor, sandy, low-fertility soil |
| Horsetail | Acidic, poorly drained, low calcium |
| Chickweed | Cool, moist, fertile soil |

**Key insight**: Weeds are symptoms, not the problem. Address the underlying soil condition and weed pressure diminishes. See [[soil-fertility-management]] for amending strategies.

### The Weed Seed Bank

Soil contains dormant weed seeds persisting for decades:
- **Annual weeds**: Seeds viable 3-10 years (pigweed, lamb's quarters, foxtail)
- **Perennial weeds**: Spread by seed and root/rhizome (bindweed, thistle, quackgrass)
- **Tilling brings buried seeds to the surface**, triggering germination — [[no-till-farming]] dramatically reduces weed pressure over time
- **Light disturbance** (top 1-2 inches) triggers most germination; deep burial keeps seeds dormant

### Succession

- Bare soil → annual weeds (crabgrass, pigweed) within weeks
- 1-2 years → biennials and perennials (thistle, dock, mullein)
- 3-5 years → shrubs and pioneer trees
- Manage which stage dominates through planting and [[cover-cropping]]

## Prevention Strategies

### 1. Dense Planting and Cover
The single most effective weed suppression: don't leave soil bare.
- **Living mulches**: White clover between rows, creeping thyme as ground cover
- **Dense spacing**: Plant close enough to shade soil by week 3-4
- **Cover crops**: Never leave beds fallow; sow cover crops immediately after harvest
- **Mulch**: 3-6 inches of organic mulch suppresses 90%+ of annual weeds

### 2. No-Till Management
See [[no-till-farming]] — never till (brings seeds to surface). Use sheet mulching (cardboard + mulch) for new areas. Maintain permanent mulch layer on all beds.

### 3. Stale Seedbed Technique
Prepare bed and water to trigger weed germination, then kill seedlings (flame weeder, hoe, shallow cultivation) before planting crop. Repeat 1-2 times for heavily weedy areas.

### 4. Clean Inputs
- Use certified seed to avoid introducing new weed species
- Don't put seeded weeds in cold compost — hot compost (130°F+ for 3+ days) kills most seeds

## Mechanical and Cultural Control

### Hand Pulling
- Best for small infestations and annual weeds
- Pull after rain when soil is soft; get whole root for perennials
- Pull before seed set — one pigweed going to seed = 100,000+ seeds

### Hoeing and Cultivation
- Scuffle hoe (stirrup hoe) cuts weeds just below surface
- Cultivate shallowly (1-2 inches) — deep cultivation brings up dormant seeds
- Time on sunny, dry days so weeds desiccate

### Flame Weeding
- Propane torch kills seedlings by bursting cell walls
- Best on weeds 1-2 inches tall; not near dry mulch or during drought

### Solarization
- Clear plastic over moist soil for 4-8 weeks in hot summer
- Kills seeds, pathogens, and soil organisms (120-150°F)
- Re-inoculate after with [[effective-microorganisms-em]] or [[compost-tea-aact]]

### Mulching
- **Straw** (seed-free): 4-6 inches, best general mulch
- **Wood chips**: Long-lasting, good for pathways and perennials
- **Leaves**: Free, 3-4 inches; shredded decomposes faster
- **Cardboard**: Base layer for sheet mulching, blocks light completely
- **Grass clippings**: Nitrogen-rich, thin layers only (1-2 inches)
- **Living mulch**: White clover, creeping thyme — suppresses weeds while fixing nitrogen

## Organic Herbicides

### Acetic Acid (Vinegar)
- Household vinegar (5%) burns young weed foliage; horticultural (20-30%) more effective
- See [[brown-rice-vinegar-brv]] for natural vinegar production
- Non-selective — damages any plant contacted

### Boiling Water
- Effective for weeds in pathways and pavers; kills foliage and shallow roots

### Corn Gluten Meal
- Natural pre-emergent — inhibits root development in germinating seeds
- Apply early spring (20 lbs/1000 sq ft); also adds ~10% N
- Don't use where direct-seeding crops

### Soap-Based Herbicides
- Commercial products (Scythe, Burnout) use fatty acid salts
- Non-selective foliage burn; best for spot treatment

## KNF and Natural Farming Approaches

### [[knf-preparations]] for Weed Management
- [[fermented-plant-extract-fpe]] from competitive weed species stimulates crops
- [[oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn]] boosts crop immune systems for competitive vigor
- [[lactic-acid-bacteria]] foliar spray supports crop health

### Fukuoka's Approach
See [[natural-farming-fukuoka]]: "Weeds are not the enemy." White clover living mulch suppresses unwanted weeds while fixing nitrogen. Straw mulch returns to fields. Build plant communities so dense that unwanted weeds can't establish.

## Seasonal Weed Management Calendar

| Season | Action |
|---|---|
| **Early Spring** | Pre-emergent (corn gluten) on beds not being direct-seeded; flame weed or stale seedbed |
| **Spring** | Cover crop any bare ground; mulch all beds |
| **Summer** | Pull before seed set; maintain mulch; cultivate between rows |
| **Late Summer** | Sow fall cover crops to prevent winter annuals |
| **Fall** | Sheet mulch new beds; collect leaves; solarize problem areas |
| **Winter** | Plan rotations; maintain mulch depth; monitor perennial crowns |

## Prioritization

1. **Prevent seed set** on any weed — highest priority
2. **Never leave soil bare** — mulch or cover crop immediately
3. **Address soil indicators** — fix underlying conditions weeds reveal
4. **Use the weeds** — harvest edibles, use accumulators as mulch
5. **Target perennials first** — they require multi-season strategy
6. **Accept some weeds** — completely weed-free is neither natural nor desirable
