# HMBC and NOESY NMR Analysis of Zwitterionic Phosphate Intermediate

## Overview

A key discovery in Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda's 2003 synthesis of psilocybin was the identification of an unexpected zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (compound 8) formed during the phosphorylation of [[shirota-zwitterion-discovery-intramolecular-benzyl-migration-phosphorylation]] migration from oxygen to nitrogen that had not been previously characterized. This page examines the NMR methodology used and the structural insights it provided.

## The Discovery

### Unexpected Reaction Product

During the phosphorylation of psilocin (1) using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-butyllithium, the expected O,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (7) was formed but was not stable:

> "After the usual aqueous workup for removing the excess reagents, the ¹H NMR spectrum of the remaining substance in CDCl₃ showed complicated signals. Rechecking the TLC showed an additional spot at the origin, and the whitish material no longer dissolved in CH₂Cl₂."

This observation — insolubility in dichloromethane and a spot at the origin on TLC — indicated that a chemical transformation had occurred during workup. The compound was isolated by preparative reversed-phase HPLC and analyzed by NMR.

### The Zwitterionic Hypothesis

Initial ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectra revealed unexpected features:

- Two distinct sets of benzyl group signals were present
- The methylene protons of one benzyl group were shifted to high field (δH 4.56, 2H, s) compared to the other (δH 4.98, 1H, s; 4.96, 1H, s)
- The methylene and N,N-dimethyl protons of the psilocin core were shifted to low field compared to psilocin itself
- A ³¹P NMR signal confirmed the presence of a phosphate moiety

These data suggested that one benzyl group had migrated from the phosphate oxygen to the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethylamino group, forming a quaternary ammonium ion. The resulting compound was zwitterionic — carrying both a positive charge (quaternary ammonium) and a negative charge (phosphate).

## Two-Dimensional NMR Techniques

### Overview of 2D NMR

Two-dimensional NMR spectroscopy correlates nuclear spins through chemical bonds or through space, providing connectivity information that cannot be obtained from one-dimensional spectra alone. The two techniques used by Shirota et al. — HMBC and NOESY — probe different types of correlations.

### HMBC (Heteronuclear Multiple Bond Correlation)

HMBC correlates protons with carbon nuclei that are separated by two or three chemical bonds (²JCH and ³JCH). This makes it invaluable for establishing connectivity in molecules where direct proton-carbon bonds (observed in HSQC/HMQC spectra) are insufficient to determine the complete structure.

**How HMBC works:**
1. Magnetization is transferred from ¹H to ¹³C via the large one-bond J-coupling (INEPT step)
2. A variable delay (typically 60–80 ms) allows long-range coupling to develop
3. Magnetization is transferred back to ¹H for detection
4. The result is a 2D spectrum with ¹H chemical shift on one axis and ¹³C chemical shift on the other
5. Cross-peaks indicate 2-3 bond connectivity between the correlated nuclei

**Application to compound 8:**
HMBC was critical for establishing that one benzyl group was linked to the nitrogen atom. The key correlation was between the high-field methylene protons (δH 4.56) and the quaternary nitrogen carbon, confirming the N-benzyl linkage. Additional correlations confirmed the remaining O-benzyl group was attached to the phosphate oxygen.

### NOESY (Nuclear Overhauser Effect Spectroscopy)

NOESY correlates protons that are close to each other in space (typically within 5 Å), regardless of whether they are bonded. This through-space correlation provides information about molecular conformation and stereochemistry.

**How NOESY works:**
1. Cross-relaxation between nearby protons transfers magnetization
2. The NOE (Nuclear Overhauser Effect) is proportional to 1/r⁶, where r is the internuclear distance
3. Cross-peaks in the NOESY spectrum indicate protons that are spatially proximate
4. The intensity of the cross-peak provides a rough measure of distance

**Application to compound 8:**
NOESY provided supporting evidence for the zwitterionic structure by showing spatial proximity between the N-benzyl methylene protons and the psilocin core protons, confirming that the benzyl group was indeed attached to the nitrogen atom rather than being in a different positional isomer.

## The Intramolecular Migration

### Mechanism

The conversion of the O,O-dibenzyl phosphate (7) to the zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate (8) represents an [[psilocybin-synthesis-zwitterionic-intermediate-filtration-method]] was the key to the synthesis's success. Its insolubility in dichloromethane made it possible to isolate it by simple filtration on a large scale, avoiding the need for column chromatography:

> "The zwitterionic nature of 8 made possible its large-scale isolation by filtration, in over 85% yield, since the excess remaining dibenzyl phosphate was removed by washing with CH₂Cl₂."

This is a rare example of a synthetic intermediate's zwitterionic character being exploited for practical purification rather than being a nuisance to be overcome.
