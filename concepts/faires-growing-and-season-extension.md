## Soil Building Through the Season
Soil health must be maintained and improved continuously throughout the growing
season. Green manure crops like buckwheat (summer) and crimson clover (autumn)
add organic matter and fix nitrogen between main crops. Buckwheat grows quickly
in warm weather, suppresses weeds, and can be turned under at flowering (about 40
days) to add phosphorus-rich biomass. Crimson clover sown in late summer provides
nitrogen and winter ground cover before being turned under in spring. Compost
applications of 2-5 cm spread across beds before each major planting replenish
nutrients. Avoid walking on planted beds to prevent soil compaction — use
designated paths and reaching boards. Worm composting (vermicomposting) in a bin
near the kitchen provides a steady supply of nutrient-dense castings for
top-dressing heavy feeders.
## Microclimate Creation
Creating favorable microclimates allows gardeners to push the boundaries of their
growing zone. Stone walls absorb heat during the day and release it at night,
raising nearby air temperatures by several degrees and allowing tender plants to
survive further into autumn. South-facing walls painted white reflect light onto
nearby beds, increasing photosynthesis. Windbreaks of dense evergreen hedges on
the north and west sides of the garden reduce wind chill and desiccation. Water
bodies (ponds, barrels) moderate temperature extremes through thermal mass — they
warm slowly in spring and cool slowly in autumn, buffering nearby plants. Dense
plantings create a humid microclimate at soil level that benefits seedlings and
