### Passive Systems
Rely on [[cervantes-ec-tds-nutrient-solution-strength]]. Absorbent mediums like vermiculite, sawdust, peat moss work well. Wick systems have no moving parts, low initial cost and low maintenance. Dutch growers line the floor with heavy plastic, fill pots with absorbent soilless mix, and flood the garden with 2-3 inches of nutrient solution. Roots absorb it in 2-5 days.

### Active Systems
Active systems "actively" move the nutrient solution. Cannabis is a fast-growing plant very well suited to active hydroponics.

**Active recovery systems** (ebb and flow, top feed, NFT) cycle reused nutrient solution into contact with roots. Growing mediums that drain rapidly and hold plenty of air include:
- Expanded clay
- Pea gravel
- Pumice rock
- Crushed brick
- Rockwool
- Coconut coir

### Ebb and Flow (Flood and Drain)
Most popular because of proven low-maintenance track records. Individual plants in pots or rockwool cubes are set on a table. Nutrient solution floods the table from the bottom, pushing out oxygen-poor air. When it drains, new oxygen-rich air is drawn to the roots. Ideal for Sea of Green gardens.

### Hydroponic Advantages
- Stepped-up nutrient control makes plants flower faster and be ready for harvest a few days earlier than soil-grown cannabis
- 60+ four-inch rockwool cubes can fit on a flood and drain table
- A two-600W HPS flowering room with a 40W fluorescent clone area and 175W MH vegetative area (1415W total) can yield at least a pound of hydroponic buds monthly

## Container Considerations

- Taller pots are preferred over wide, squat containers (cannabis root system penetrates deeply)
- Allow 1 to 1.5 gallons (4-7 L) of soil per month the plant will spend in the container
- 2-3 gallon pots support a plant for up to 3 months
- 3-6 gallon containers for 3-4 months of rapid growth
- Drainage holes: at least two half-inch holes per square foot of bottom
- White containers reflect light and keep soil cooler

### Coco Coir
Coco coir is made from the husks of coconuts. It has excellent water retention while still providing good aeration. Coco coir has a near-neutral pH (5.5-6.5), which makes it easy to work with. It is considered an organic medium and is biodegradable. However, salt content can be high in low-quality coir, so it should be leached before use. Coco coir is often mixed with perlite (70/30 ratio) to improve drainage.

### Perlite and Vermiculite
Perlite is expanded volcanic glass that is extremely light and provides excellent aeration. It holds very little water on its own. Vermiculite is expanded mica that holds large quantities of water and nutrients but compacts over time. They are often mixed together (50/50) to create a balanced medium, or perlite is mixed into soil or coco coir to improve drainage. Perlite can also be used alone in hydroponic systems, but requires frequent watering because of its low water-holding capacity.

### Expanded Clay (Hydroton)
Expanded clay pebbles (often sold as Hydroton) are lightweight, pH-neutral clay balls that provide excellent aeration and drainage. They are reusable after cleaning and sterilizing. Used primarily in hydroponic systems such as flood-and-drain and drip irrigation, they provide a stable, clean [[nutrient-film-technique]] (NFT) runs a thin film of nutrient solution over plant roots in a shallow channel. These systems offer the fastest growth rates but also the least margin for error — pump failures or nutrient imbalances can kill plants quickly.

## Container Selection

Container size directly affects plant size. Small plants in large pots can develop root problems from excess water, while large plants in small pots become root-bound and cannot access enough water and nutrients. As a general rule, allow one to two gallons of growing medium for each foot of expected plant height.

Containers must have drainage holes at the bottom. Elevating pots on small blocks or placing them in saucers ensures good drainage. Fabric pots (smart pots) provide superior air pruning of roots compared to plastic containers, preventing plants from becoming root-bound.

## Water Quality and pH Management

Water quality is fundamental to growing success. Chlorine in municipal water can harm beneficial soil organisms. Letting tap water sit for 24 hours or using a carbon filter removes chlorine. Reverse osmosis (RO) systems produce the purest water but also strip beneficial minerals.
