Mollison describes mangroves as a multi-species zonation system. Red mangroves
(Rhizophora) colonize the seaward edge, suspended on stilt roots above the
tidal range. Black mangroves (Avicennia) occupy the mid-intertidal zone with
pneumatophores rising from the mud. White mangroves (Laguncularia) and button
mangroves (Conocarpus) occupy the higher, less frequently flooded positions.

This zonation is not random. Each species has evolved specific tolerances for
salinity, inundation frequency, and substrate type. When designing mangrove
restoration or aquaculture integration, the designer must respect these natural
patterns and plant species in their correct elevational position.
