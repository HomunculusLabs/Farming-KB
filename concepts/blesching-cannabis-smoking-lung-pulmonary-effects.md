## Cannabis vs. Tobacco: Fundamentally Different Pulmonary Profiles

The pulmonary effects of cannabis and tobacco differ in several important ways:

- **Bronchodilation vs. bronchoconstriction**: THC is a bronchodilator that opens airways, while nicotine causes bronchoconstriction that narrows them.
- **No lung cancer association**: The LA study found no association between cannabis-only smoking and lung cancer incidence.
- **No COPD association**: Unlike tobacco, cannabis-only smoking was not associated with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
- **Different combustion chemistry**: While cannabis smoke does contain some carcinogens, the anti-tumor properties of cannabinoids (particularly THC and CBD) may counteract the carcinogenic potential of combustion byproducts.

## THC and COPD — Therapeutic Potential

THC has demonstrated therapeutic potential for patients already suffering from COPD:

- **Increased walking distance**: Patients with COPD who used cannabis showed an increase in walking distance, a key clinical measure of respiratory function and exercise tolerance.
- **Bronchodilation**: THC's airway-widening effects can partially compensate for the airway obstruction characteristic of COPD.
- **Weight gain**: COPD patients often suffer from cachexia (muscle wasting and weight loss). THC stimulates appetite and promotes weight gain, addressing a significant comorbidity.
- **Anti-inflammatory effects**: Chronic inflammation of the airways is a hallmark of COPD, and cannabinoids have well-documented anti-inflammatory properties.
