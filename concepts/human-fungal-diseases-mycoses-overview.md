## Malassezia and Dandruff

The yeast *Malassezia furfur* is a commensal organism normally found on
human skin, particularly in sebum-rich areas such as the scalp. Under
certain conditions, however, *Malassezia* populations can proliferate
excessively, triggering an inflammatory response that manifests as
dandruff or, in more severe cases, seborrheic dermatitis. The
relationship between *Malassezia* and its human host is a classic
example of an opportunistic infection — the fungus is present on most
healthy individuals but causes disease only when conditions shift in its
favor. Modern anti-dandruff shampoos typically contain antifungal agents
such as ketoconazole or zinc pyrithione that target *Malassezia*
specifically, reflecting the fungal etiology of this ubiquitous
condition.

## Subcutaneous Mycoses: Mycetoma

Mycetoma, sometimes called Madura foot, is a chronic subcutaneous
infection most commonly caused by the fungus *Madurella mycetomatis*.
This condition is endemic in tropical and subtropical regions,
particularly among agricultural workers who walk barefoot. The fungus
enters the body through minor skin punctures — thorns, splinters, or
small cuts — and gradually establishes a colony in the deeper tissue.
Over time, the infection produces characteristic swollen lesions that
drain through sinuses, releasing granules composed of compacted fungal
hyphae. Mycetoma is notoriously difficult to treat and may eventually
require surgical intervention or even amputation if not caught early.
