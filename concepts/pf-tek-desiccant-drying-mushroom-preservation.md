# PF TEK Desiccant Drying and Mushroom Preservation

## The Importance of Cool Desiccation

Cool desiccation is the PF TEK method for drying harvested mushrooms while
preserving their psychoactive alkaloid content. PF considers this the best
overall [[pf-tek-desiccant-drying-technique]] [[miniculture-rye-grain-cultivation-method-for-psilocybe-cubensis]]. The method uses chemical
desiccants to absorb moisture from the air surrounding the mushrooms in a
sealed environment, drawing water out of the fungal tissue gradually at cool
temperatures. This approach avoids the alkaloid degradation that occurs with
heat-based drying methods.

The immature specimens are the best in quality, digestibility, and potency.
They are characterized by very light color with white stems and light-colored
caps. The cap will spread out after the veil breaks, so harvesting just before
or right after the veil breaks is ideal. The gills on the underside of the cap
will be light in color, and the mushrooms will be conical-shaped with
sporulation not yet begun. PF's core guidance: grow them on brown rice,
harvest them when young, and cool dry them with desiccant.

## Why Heat Drying Must Be Avoided

PF explicitly warns against using an oven or hot air dryers. Heat leaches the
[[guzman-magic-mushrooms-chemical-constituents]] from the mushroom tissue and significantly reduces
their quality. The psychoactive alkaloids [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] are
sensitive to elevated temperatures, and heat drying causes measurable losses
in potency. Cool desiccation avoids this problem entirely by removing moisture
through ambient-temperature chemical absorption rather than thermal energy.

## Desiccant Types and Sources

### DRIERITE

DRIERITE is the universal laboratory-grade desiccant, available from chemical
and science supply retailers. It is anhydrous calcium sulfate and is widely
regarded as one of the most effective and reliable desiccants available. It
indicates its absorption state through a color change, making it easy to
assess when regeneration is needed. DRIERITE is the premium choice for
mushroom drying.

### Silica Gel Granules

Silica gel granules are another effective desiccant available from chemical
and science supply stores. Silica gel is a porous form of silicon dioxide
that absorbs moisture through surface adsorption. It is reusable and can be
regenerated by heating. Like DRIERITE, some silica gel products include
color-indicating beads that show when the desiccant has become saturated.

### DAMP GONE

DAMP GONE is an inexpensive desiccant product that looks similar to kitty
litter. It is designed for drying closets and damp areas in the home and is
available at well-stocked hardware stores. PF notes that despite its low cost,
DAMP GONE works as well as any other desiccant for mushroom drying purposes.
This makes it the most accessible option for cultivators on a budget.

## Safety Notes for Desiccant Handling

These desiccant products may carry toxicity warnings — users should avoid
breathing the dust and try not to touch the material directly, as it dries
skin. However, PF assures that desiccant placed in an airtight container and
under a wire screen will do nothing to the fungi except dry them. It is
completely safe for this application. The desiccant never directly contacts
the mushroom material.

## Materials Needed

The complete materials list for desiccant drying is minimal and inexpensive:
desiccant of choice (DRIERITE, silica gel, or DAMP GONE), a wire screen for
holding the mushrooms above the desiccant, a plastic tub or container, and a
plastic bag with a tie-off closure (a garbage bag type wire tie is sufficient).
If a clear plastic bag can be found, PF recommends using it to observe the
drying process without opening the system.

## The Desiccant Drying Process

### Step 1: Pre-Drying

Before placing mushrooms in the desiccant chamber, pre-dry them on a wire
screen in a room with low humidity. Air should be available to all sides of
the mushrooms. After a couple of days of air drying, the shriveling fungus
can be quickly and completely finished in the desiccant box. This two-stage
approach reduces the load on the desiccant and speeds the overall process.

### Step 2: Desiccant Preparation

Desiccant can be reused indefinitely. After each use, heat it in an oven as
instructed by the manufacturer to drive off absorbed moisture. Store it in an
airtight container between uses so it stays dry and ready. Importantly, new
desiccant is usually somewhat damp when purchased, so it should be pre-heated
and dried before first use — otherwise its function will be compromised.

### Step 3: Chamber Assembly

Place a 1-inch layer of desiccant on the bottom of the plastic container. Set
the wire screen on top of the desiccant layer. Arrange the mushrooms on the
wire screen so that air can circulate around all surfaces. Place the entire
container with mushrooms and desiccant into the plastic bag and seal it with
the wire tie.

### Step 4: Drying Timeline

After 24 hours, initial shriveling of the mushrooms will be visible. About 4
to 5 days later, the mushrooms will be dried rock hard. To verify complete
drying, the stem should snap cleanly when bent — if it bends without
breaking, additional drying time is needed.

## Refrigeration for Optimal Alkaloid Preservation

For the best alkaloid preservation technique, the desiccant box can be placed
into the refrigerator during the drying process. This allows the mushrooms
to dry at near-freezing temperatures, which PF considers the optimal
preservation method. The combination of cool temperatures and gradual
moisture removal ensures maximum retention of psilocybin and psilocin.

## Long-Term Storage

Mushrooms dried using the desiccant method lose hardly any chemical
constituents. Their truly desiccated state preserves them in their prime for
months. For storage, seal them in plastic bags or keep them in canning jars
with the rubber-edged canning lid tightened. The freezer is a good place for
long-term preservation, but PF cautions that the fungi must be tightly sealed
in their containers to protect them against moisture present in the freezer.

## Spore Syringe Boiling Sterilization Technique

The PF TEK also describes a simple boiling [[pf-tek-alcohol-flaming-sterilization-method]] for preparing
spore syringes. This technique is used when making spore syringes from spore
prints for inoculating new substrate jars.

Begin by boiling a pot of water. Draw boiling water into a syringe and squirt
it out several times to thoroughly rinse the interior. Then refill the
syringe with boiling water, replace the needle guard, and wrap the syringe in
tin foil. Prepare several syringes in this manner. Drop the foil-wrapped
syringes into the boiling water and boil them for one full hour. After
boiling, let them cool before using.
