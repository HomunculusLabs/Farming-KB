## Fungal Dark Taxa and Environmental DNA Sequencing

## Overview

Fungal dark taxa represent sequences recovered from
environmental DNA (eDNA) surveys that cannot be assigned
to any formally described species. These molecular
operational taxonomic units (MOTUs) vastly outnumber
described fungal species and constitute one of the
largest reservoirs of unknown biodiversity on Earth.

The term dark taxa draws an analogy to dark matter in
physics: their presence is detectable through indirect
means, yet their biological reality remains largely
uncharacterized. This concept has become central to
modern mycology as sequencing technologies reveal the
true depth of [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungal-diversity]] range from 2.2 to
3.8 million species, yet fewer than 150,000 have been
formally described. High-throughput sequencing of
environmental samples consistently reveals that 70 to 90
percent of recovered fungal sequences belong to lineages
with no cultured representative or formal description.

In soil surveys alone, hundreds of thousands of distinct
ITS (Internal Transcribed Spacer) operational taxonomic
units have been detected globally through projects such
as the Global Soil Mycobiome Initiative and UNITE
database curation efforts. The gap between detected and
described diversity grows wider with each new survey.

## Detection Methods

### Metabarcoding Approaches

The ITS2 region of ribosomal DNA serves as the primary
fungal barcode for environmental surveys. Primers such as
ITS3 and ITS4, along with fungal-specific variants like
fITS7, amplify broad swaths of [[ecological-roles-fungi-ecosystems]] of unknown fungi.

## Major Groups of Dark Taxa

### Early-Diverging Lineages

Environmental surveys have uncovered enormous sequence
diversity in basal fungal lineages such as Rozellomycota
(Cryptomycota) and Chytridiomycota. Rozellomycota,
described only in 2011 based on environmental sequences,
may represent one of the most diverse and ancient fungal
phyla, with thousands of distinct clades detected across
aquatic and soil samples worldwide.
