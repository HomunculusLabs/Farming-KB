# PF TEK Pressure Canner Sterilization

## Overview

While the PF TEK was designed to work with simple steam sterilization using regular
kitchen cookware, a pressure canner offers a faster and more reliable sterilization
option for cultivators who have access to one.

The PF TEK's standard substrate formula of brown rice flour, vermiculite, and water
can be sterilized without grain-cooking concerns because the substrate is airy and
non-grain based. The pressure canner provides significant advantages in speed and
sterility assurance, making it a worthwhile investment for serious cultivators.

## How It Compares to Standard Steam Sterilization

The standard PF TEK uses a 3-piece vegetable steamer or a stainless steel folding
steamer in a regular cooking pot, steaming jars at the lowest possible boil for one
hour in a tightly covered pot.

This works because there is no grain to cook up and the substrate is airy, allowing
steam to penetrate effectively. The jars can even sit in water during steaming, as
long as boiling water cannot slosh into the jars.

Gas stoves are the easiest to control for this gentle steaming approach. A good
tight-fitting pot lid is essential for successful steaming. Be careful to not
overheat the jars, as this dries the substrate.

A pressure canner elevates the steam temperature above the normal 212 degrees
Fahrenheit boiling point by operating under pressure. This higher temperature
achieves more thorough sterilization in significantly less time.

## Sterilization Times at 12 PSI

The PF TEK specifies precise sterilization parameters at 12 pounds per square inch
for each type of cultivation material used in the process.

### Half-Pint PF Substrate Jars: 12 PSI for 20 Minutes

At 12 PSI for 20 minutes, half-pint PF substrate jars receive thorough sterilization.
This is dramatically faster than the one-hour open steam method, representing a
three-fold reduction in active sterilization time.

The shorter exposure time also reduces the risk of substrate drying, which is one of
the most common problems with extended steam sterilization. Drying is evidenced by
OK spore germination that subsequently halts at a certain point depending on how
dry the substrate has become.

### Water Bottles: 12 PSI for 55 Minutes

Water bottles used in the cultivation process require 55 minutes at 12 PSI. The
substantially longer time is necessary because water has a high heat capacity and the
bottles are typically larger than the half-pint substrate jars.

Complete sterilization of the water supply is critical since it will be used in
substrate preparation and spore syringe manufacturing. Contaminated water bottles can
introduce bacteria that destroy entire batches of substrate jars.

### Syringes and Needles: 12 PSI for 10 Minutes

Syringes and needles require only 10 minutes at 12 PSI for sterilization. Their
small size and metal or glass construction allow rapid and uniform heat penetration.

This is far more convenient and reliable than the alternative method of drawing
boiling water through syringes multiple times and then boiling them wrapped in tin
foil for an hour. Multiple syringes can be sterilized simultaneously in the canner.

## Safety Considerations

Pressure canners are potentially dangerous pieces of equipment if used incorrectly.
The PF TEK explicitly warns that for proper and safe use, cultivators should always
refer to the manual that comes with the pressure canner.

If the canner is used and has no manual, an attempt should be made to obtain one
from the manufacturer before using the device. Pressure canners can be dangerous
if used incorrectly.

### Critical Safety Guidelines

Always verify the pressure relief valve is functioning properly before each use by
checking that the vent pipe is clear and unobstructed. Never exceed the rated
pressure of the canner, as over-pressurization can cause catastrophic failure.

Allow the canner to depressurize naturally rather than using quick-release methods,
as rapid depressurization can cause jars to crack or their contents to boil over
violently. Keep children and pets away from the pressure canner during the entire
operation cycle.

Inspect rubber gaskets and sealing surfaces regularly for wear, cracking, or damage,
and replace them at the first sign of deterioration. A worn gasket can fail under
pressure, creating a dangerous situation.

## Advantages Over Open Steam Sterilization

### Dramatic Speed Improvement

Pressure canner sterilization of PF substrate jars takes 20 minutes compared to 60
minutes for steam sterilization. This represents a significant time savings when
processing multiple batches.

Additionally, the time needed to bring the canner to pressure is typically shorter
than the time needed to achieve and maintain a gentle boil for steam sterilization.
The overall time from start to finish is substantially reduced.

### Greater Reliability and Consistency

The elevated temperature achieved under pressure provides more margin for error. While
steam sterilization requires careful attention to maintaining a gentle boil and
ensuring a tight-fitting pot lid, the pressure canner provides consistent, verifiable
conditions through its pressure gauge.

The cultivator can see at a glance whether proper sterilization conditions are being
maintained throughout the cycle, reducing uncertainty and improving consistency
between batches.

### Reduced Risk of Substrate Drying

One of the most common problems with steam sterilization is substrate drying from
excessive heat or insufficient moisture retention. The PF TEK specifically warns
against overheating, which dries the substrate and causes halted growth even after
successful spore germination begins.

The shorter sterilization cycle in a pressure canner significantly reduces this risk,
helping to preserve the carefully measured water content of the substrate formula.

### Higher Throughput

A pressure canner can process many more jars simultaneously than a standard cooking
pot, making it practical for larger cultivation projects. Most standard pressure
canners can hold 7 to 10 half-pint jars per batch, compared to 4 to 6 in a typical
steaming pot.
