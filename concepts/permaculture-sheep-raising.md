# Permaculture Sheep Raising

Sheep can be integrated into [[permaculture-zone-planning]] orchard after trees are at least 7 years old, but must be carefully controlled and removed if they start damaging trees. Trees provide food, protect from elements, benefit soil, and prevent erosion within their grazing area.

### Stocking and Feeding

- A legume-grass mix pasture feeds 5 ewes and 8 lambs per acre in northern regions

- Rotate weekly

- Last month of pregnancy: 0.5-1 pound (0.22-0.5 kg) grain per day

- After lambing: ewes with one lamb need 1 pound grain; with 2 lambs need 1.5-2 pounds (0.7-0.9 kg)

- Taper grain off over the next 2 months

- Ram needs 1 pound grain per day during breeding season

- Tree forage can offset grain supplements

- Each sheep needs 1.5 gallons (6 liters) fresh, clean water daily

- Spring: gradually introduce sheep to pasture for adjustment

- Winter: forage from harvested corn fields and garden roots; each sheep eats 75 pounds (34 kg) grain and 10 bales hay over winter

## Housing and Management

Sheep don't need much housing. A three-sided shelter in the pasture suffices, with a place in the barn for lambing or extreme weather.

Sheep are very vulnerable to stress -- moving them in a truck, abrupt feed changes, or loud storms can make them stop eating or even have a heart attack. They are easy targets for predators; stray dogs can chase a sheep to death without biting. The most effective prevention is a dog:

- Border collie for herding

- Great Pyrenees as a guard dog living with the sheep in the field

## Breeding

Breed sheep in October; lambs arrive in 5 months. Clip wool away from eyes and the vaginal area before breeding. Every interaction with a ewe is a good time to check her feet.

Options for breeding:

- Buy a yearling ram every year, breed with ewes, then butcher the ram

- Keep a ram full time

- Never use rams from the same family for breeding

Lambing is nearly identical to goat kidding. Orphan lambs can drink goat milk as a replacement. Clip wool from faces of sheep with woolly faces so they can see, and tag or clip wool away from the vagina.

## Health and Disease Prevention

Preventive health care is essential for a thriving sheep flock. Regular observation catches most problems early -- sheep are prey animals that hide symptoms until they are critically ill.

Vaccination protocols:

- CD-T vaccine (Clostridium perfringens types C and D plus tetanus) is the most important; give to ewes before lambing and to lambs at 6-8 weeks with a booster 3-4 weeks later

- Caseous lymphadenitis (CL) vaccine in areas where the disease is prevalent

- Footrot vaccination in wet climates where the bacteria thrive

Parasite management is one of the biggest challenges in sheep raising. Practice [[permaculture-integrated-animal-systems]] carefully -- guardian dogs should be raised with sheep from puppyhood and may not tolerate other dog species on the property. Proper training and socialization are essential.

## Seasonal Management Calendar

Managing sheep effectively means anticipating the needs of each season and preparing in advance.

Spring (March-May):

- Shear ewes before lambing begins

- Prepare lambing pens with clean, dry bedding

- Begin pasture rotation as grass starts growing; introduce sheep gradually to prevent bloat on lush new growth

- Vaccinate lambs at 6-8 weeks

- Check and repair fencing before turnout

Summer (June-August):

- Move sheep to fresh pasture frequently; parasite pressure peaks in warm, wet weather

- Monitor for fly strike and treat promptly

- Wean lambs at 8-12 weeks of age

- Provide shade and plenty of fresh water; sheep can suffer heat stress at temperatures above 80°F (27°C)
