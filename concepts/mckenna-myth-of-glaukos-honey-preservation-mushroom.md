### The Pithos as Symbolic Vessel

The pithos, or large storage jar, was a ubiquitous artifact in Minoan and Mycenaean civilization. McKenna notes that the "shape suggests the bucket-shaped graves of the Natufians," but the pithos also functioned symbolically as a vessel of transformation — a container in which something is preserved, transformed, and ultimately reborn. In the Glaukos myth, the pithos filled with honey is literally the womb from which the mushroom-child emerges, preserved but dormant, awaiting the shamanic knowledge (the serpent's herb) that can restore it to life.

- *Stropharia cubensis* is a coprophilic (dung-loving) species that grows on cattle dung

## The Serpent and Herbal Knowledge

The serpent that brings the resurrection herb is, for McKenna, a figure familiar from the Genesis story of Eden. In both narratives, the serpent possesses accurate and secret information concerning plants — specifically plants that confer immortality or restore life. The serpent is a consistent motif in ancient mythology as the guardian of esoteric botanical knowledge.

This serpent motif appears across multiple mythological traditions:

- **Genesis**: The serpent in the Garden of Eden offers knowledge of the tree of knowledge of good and evil
- **Gilgamesh**: The serpent steals the plant of immortality from the hero
- **Minoan Glaukos myth**: The serpent brings the herb that can restore the dead
- **Greek Asklepios**: The staff of Asklepios, god of medicine, is entwined by a serpent
- **Mesoamerican Quetzalcoatl**: The [[mckenna-food-gods-designer-states-of-consciousness]].

### Maria Sabina and "Los Ninos"

The Mazatec mushroom shamaness Maria Sabina consistently referred to the sacred mushrooms as *los ninos* — "the dear sweet little ones" or "the children." This terminology, far from being a mere poetic conceit, reflects a deep phenomenological truth about the psilocybin experience: the mushrooms present themselves to the consciousness of the user as sentient, childlike presences. McKenna argues that this is not a metaphor imposed on the experience but a quality of the experience itself — the mushroom entities encountered during intoxication genuinely present as small, intelligent, playful beings.
