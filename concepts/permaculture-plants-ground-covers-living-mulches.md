# Permaculture Ground Covers and Living Mulches

Ground covers are essential elements in tagasaste as Living Mulch
Tagasaste can be managed as a low hedge providing both [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]] and stock fodder. When trimmed to keep foliage within stock reach, it functions as a productive living fence and ground cover system.

### Tree Lupin (Lupinus arboreus)
A nitrogen-fixing shrub useful as a pioneer ground cover on sandy and poor soils. Fixes atmospheric nitrogen through rhizobial bacteria, improving soil for subsequent plantings.

### Lespedeza bicolor (Miracle Plant)
Nitrogen-fixing legume useful for soil improvement and as forage.

### Clovers (Trifolium spp)
Key nitrogen-fixing ground covers for pasture integration:
- Up to 400 kg/ha nitrogen yield estimated in Russia
- [[comparison-lupin-vs-crimson-clover-green-manure]] is particularly high-yielding
- **Trifolium fragiferum** (strawberry clover) -- native to NSW, SA, Vic, and WA; good ground cover for shade and full sun

### Lucerne (Alfalfa)
Very long flowering period (78-285 kg/ha/day nectar measured in USA). Good lock-up fodder. Functions as a deep-rooted living mulch bringing nutrients from subsoil.

## Perennial Grasses as Ground Covers

### Pampas Grass (Cortaderia selloana)
Clumping perennial to 3 m, forms a fountain of cascading leaves. Suited to cool areas yet thrives in heat; does well in acid and alkaline soils, including waterlogged areas. Fast to recover from grazing. Good windbreak and shelter plant for stock. Controls soil erosion. In New Zealand, 1 acre planted at 1.8m x 1.8m spacing is equivalent to 3000 bales of hay. Deep roots bring up trace elements which control grass tetany.

### Elephant Grass (Pennisetum purpureum)
Clumping perennial to 4 m, native to tropical Africa. Tolerant of different soil conditions and remarkably [[permplant-carob-drought-tolerant-food-tree]]. Spreads from creeping underground rhizome, seed, and cuttings. Very fast to recover from browsing. Very easy to propagate -- simply place small branches into damp soil.

## Salt-Tolerant Ground Covers

Species for saline and coastal environments:

- **Ambrosia hispida** (creeping ragweed) -- good sand binder
- **Arctostaphylos uva-ursi** (bearberry) -- trailing, frost hardy
- **Batis maritima** (saltwort) -- prostrate shrub for mangrove swamps
- **Rhagodia spp** (saltbush) -- prostrate forms for saltflats
- **Sarcocornia quinqueflora** (chicken claws, beaded glasswort) -- forms extensive colonies on tidal mudflats, fleshy stems absorb pickle flavour
- **Suaeda australis** (sea blight) -- fleshy-leaved plant of Australian coast, early settlers used as vegetable and in pickles
- **Sesuvium portulacastrum** (sea purslane) -- fleshy-leaved shrubby herb of northern Australian beaches and mudflats, leaves make excellent vegetable

## Design Notes

- **Multi-function selection**: Choose ground covers that serve multiple purposes (nitrogen fixation, forage, bee forage, [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]])
- **Layer integration**: Ground covers occupy the lowest layer -- ensure they don't compete with productive trees for water
- **Coastal systems**: Salt-tolerant ground covers are essential for coastal permaculture sites
- **Pasture integration**: Introduce clovers, lucerne, and chicory to pasture to extend flowering seasons and boost [[mollison-designers-beekeeping-honey-production-permaculture]]
- **Living mulch management**: Regular cutting of leguminous ground covers releases nitrogen to companion plants
