# Cannabis Pollen Collection

Pollen collection is a critical skill for any cannabis breeding program.
Properly collected, stored, and applied pollen enables breeders to make
targeted crosses, control genetic outcomes, and produce seeds with
desired traits. Cervantes provides detailed practical guidance drawn
from both commercial seed production and home breeding contexts.

## Understanding Pollen Biology

Cannabis pollen consists of microscopic grains produced by staminate
(male) flowers. Each grain contains 10 chromosomes (one copy of each
pair) and carries the genetic material from the pollen parent. When a
pollen grain lands on a receptive pistil, it hydrates, germinates, and
sends a pollen tube down through the stigma toward the ovule. The
species has evolved physical and biochemical recognition systems so
that only cannabis pollen can successfully germinate on the stigmatic
surface and fertilize the ovule.

Pollen from many species floats in the air, but cannabis pollen is
distinctive. A single male flower produces enough pollen to pollinate
many females. Under natural conditions, vast quantities disperse into
the wind and can travel miles on air currents.

## When to Collect Pollen

Male plants typically begin producing flowers one to four weeks before
females of the same variety. Male flowers can produce pollen as early
as the third or fourth week of flowering (12-16 hours of darkness).
Male pre-flowers are normally visible when plants have been under a
12-hour photoperiod for about three weeks. Anthers (pollen sacks)
appear at branch unions, looking like clusters of grapes or miniature
lobster claws growing upward.

Pollen sacks continue to open and produce flowers over an extended
period. Males can remain in dehiscence (pollen shedding) for two weeks
or longer. It is important to collect pollen before ambient THC
degradation progresses, as the male plant's potency decreases after it
begins to shed pollen.

## Collection Methods

### Bag Collection Method (Primary Method)

This is the standard method for controlled pollen collection:

1. Strip away all branches except those designated for pollen
   collection, to guard against accidental random pollination.
2. Isolate the male plant as soon as anthers show, since brushing
   against a plant in dehiscence releases airborne pollen.
3. Just prior to anther opening, place a clean paper or plastic bag
   over the selected branch.
4. Secure the bag at the bottom with string or a wire tie to prevent
   pollen escape.
5. Leave the bag in place for several days to collect pollen.
6. When enough pollen has collected, cut the branch and shake
   remaining pollen into the bag.
7. Carefully remove the spent branch and bag to prevent pollen escape.

### Alternative: Cut Branch in Water

Cut a branch of male flowers and place it in a plastic bag to collect
pollen. Alternatively, place the cut male branch in a glass of water
to extend its life while collecting pollen over several days.

## Processing Collected Pollen

After collection, process the pollen through a fine sieve or screen to
remove leaf matter, anther debris, and other contaminants that may
have fallen into the bag. Contaminated pollen spoils more quickly.
Place wax paper under the screen to catch the cleaned pollen, then
transfer it to a collection container using a sterile scraper.

## Pollen Storage

Pollen does not have a long shelf life under natural conditions. It is
easily destroyed by high temperatures and moisture. However, with
proper handling, pollen can remain viable for extended periods:

- **Short-term:** Keep in the collection bag in a cool, dry location.
- **Long-term:** Store cleaned pollen in a small coin envelope or
  sterile test tube in the freezer. Temperatures below freezing
  preserve viability for several months.
- **Critical rules:** Do not repeatedly freeze and thaw pollen. Ensure
  absolute cleanliness during collection and storage. Keep containers
  airtight and free of moisture.

## Applying Collected Pollen

Two primary methods are used to apply collected pollen to female
plants:

- **Bag pollination:** Cover the female branch with the pollen-filled
  bag, shake briefly, and leave for two days and nights to ensure
  thorough pollination.
- **Paintbrush pollination:** Dip a small artist's brush into the
  pollen container and gently brush onto receptive pistils. This
  technique is ideal when only a few seeds are needed.

After pollination, spray female plants thoroughly with water to destroy
any remaining viable pollen before moving them back to the main grow
area.

## Contamination Prevention

Airborne pollen is a significant concern in any grow operation. Male
plants used for breeding should be kept as far from females as
possible. Cover male plants with plastic bags before removing them from
the garden. Spray male plants with water to deactivate stray pollen.
Filters, water barriers, and separate pollination chambers all help
contain pollen and prevent unwanted seed production in sinsemilla
crops.
