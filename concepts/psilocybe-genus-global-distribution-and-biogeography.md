# Psilocybe Genus Global Distribution and Biogeography

## Overview

The genus *Psilocybe* is the most species-rich and geographically widespread of all neurotropic fungal genera. With 116 recognized species in the Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz (2000) survey, *Psilocybe* accounts for approximately 54% of all known [[neurotropic-fungi]]. The genus exhibits a striking biogeographical pattern: the majority of species are concentrated in or near the **[[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]] and the tropics. Key biodiversity hotspots include:

- **Mexico**: The single most important country for *Psilocybe* diversity, harboring 76 neurotropic species total (all genera), of which **44 belong to *Psilocybe*** — representing 39% of the world's *Psilocybe* species.
- **Southeast Asia and Oceania**: New Guinea and surrounding islands support significant diversity, particularly in montane and lowland rainforests.
- **South America**: The Amazon Basin, Andean cloud forests, and Atlantic coastal forests of Brazil are important centers of diversity.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa**: Tropical and subtropical forest regions support numerous species.

### Neotropical Dominance

The Neotropical realm (Mexico through South America) contains the highest concentration of *Psilocybe* species globally. This pattern correlates with:

- **Subtropical humidity**: Consistent moisture and moderate temperatures favor the decomposition ecology of *Psilocybe* species, which typically fruit on decaying plant material, dung, or enriched soils.
- **Forest complexity**: The structural diversity of humid [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] of the United States. *P. stuntzii* occurs in the same region. *P. ovoideocystidiata* is found in the Ohio River Valley and eastern deciduous forests.
- **Asia (temperate)**: Japan and Korea support several species in montane environments.

### Substrate-Associated Distribution

*Psilocybe* species distribution is strongly influenced by substrate preferences:

- **Dung-inhabiting (coprophilous)**: Species like *P. cubensis* follow the distribution of large herbivores (cattle, horses) in tropical and subtropical regions. This explains their pantropical distribution across Central and South America, Southeast Asia, and Africa.
- **Lignicolous (wood-decomposing)**: Species growing on decaying wood and forest debris are more restricted to forested habitats and show less human-mediated dispersal.
- **Terricolous (soil-inhabiting)**: Species growing directly in soil, particularly in grasslands, show broader distributions but are sensitive to habitat disturbance and [[mycorrhizal-agricultural-practices]].

## Ecological Correlates of High Diversity

### Climate Requirements

*Psilocybe* species generally require:

- Mean annual temperatures between 15–28°C
- Consistent humidity or distinct wet seasons
- Substrate moisture above 60% for fruiting
- Some species require a diurnal temperature fluctuation to trigger fruiting

These requirements naturally limit the genus to tropical, subtropical, and warm-temperate regions, explaining the Austral hemisphere concentration.

### Symbiosis with Large Herbivores

The coprophilous *Psilocybe* species have a distribution history intertwined with large herbivore ecology:

- *P. cubensis* fruits almost exclusively on bovine dung in tropical and subtropical pastures
- The introduction of cattle to new regions (e.g., Australia, Southeast Asia) has facilitated the spread of *P. cubensis* far beyond its presumed native range
- This human-mediated dispersal through livestock makes *P. cubensis* one of the most widely distributed psilocybin-containing mushrooms

### Elevation Gradients

In mountainous tropical regions, *Psilocybe* species often show distinct elevation zonation:

- Lowland species (0–500 m): Typically thermophilic, fruiting year-round in wet conditions
- Montane species (500–2000 m): Often with narrower temperature ranges, fruiting seasonally
- High-elevation species (2000+ m): Restricted to tropical highlands like the Mexican altiplano and Andean páramo

## Mexico as a Global Center of Diversity

Mexico's exceptional *Psilocybe* diversity (44 species) results from several converging factors:

1. **Geographic position**: Situated at the intersection of Nearctic and Neotropical biogeographic realms, creating ecotonal conditions that support both temperate and tropical species.
2. **Topographic complexity**: The Mexican landscape ranges from coastal plains to high-altitude plateaus (altiplano) above 2000 m, providing diverse microclimates.
3. **Vegetation diversity**: Mexico contains tropical rainforests, cloud forests, oak-pine woodlands, grasslands, and desert scrub, each supporting different *Psilocybe* communities.
4. **Indigenous land management**: Traditional agricultural practices (milpa, pasture maintenance) have created anthropogenic habitats favorable to many *Psilocybe* species for millennia.
