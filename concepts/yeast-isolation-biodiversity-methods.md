# Yeast Isolation and Biodiversity Methods

Source: [[biodiversity-of-fungi-biodiversity-patterns-ecosystems]] (Hyde et al., Ch. 16)

## Collection Methods

### Soil and Sediment
- Soil particles placed directly on [[agar-medium-preparation]], incubated 24+ hours
- Examined microscopically in situ under low power for yeast growth at particle/medium interface
- Confirmed colonies transferred to growth medium; streak cultures for purification

### Water
- Plated directly or passed through 0.45mm nitrocellulose filters
- Filter placed face-up on solid nutrient agar with bacterial inhibitors
- Volume filtered: few milliliters to 2-3 liters depending on population density

### Marine/Deep-Sea
- Grab samples, gravity/piston cores, or submersibles
- Deep-sea: Sterile Bag Sampler (General Oceanics)
- Temperature and salinity recorded simultaneously
- 50ml sample usually adequate; minimal water to avoid anaerobic conditions
- Cores sectioned, samples from recorded depths
- Process immediately or hold on ice; containers NOT air-tight

## Isolation Strategies

### Direct Plating
Soil particles on agar; incubate; examine for yeast colonies at particle/medium interface.

### Soil Dilution
Known soil quantity mixed with sterile water (sea water for marine) in test tube; dilution series made; suspensions spread onto isolation media.

### Enrichment
Soil/samples in flasks with tailored nutrient medium:
- Customized carbon/nitrogen compounds, vitamins, growth factors
- Salinity adjusted for marine species
- Substratum levels manipulated for osmophilic species
- Incubated 12-24+ hours with continuous shaking
- Shake culture causes [[fungal-biosorption-of-heavy-metals-by-filamentous-fungi]] to grow in pellets; yeasts remain free cells
- Highly selective — cannot enumerate natural populations

## Isolation Media

**Standard media**:
- **Yeast Isolation Medium (PYG)**: Commonly used
- **Wickerham's YM medium**: Alternative
- **Fresh water or filtered sea water**: For aquatic/oceanic
- Oceanic: sea water at 37‰ salinity; brackish/estuarine: 15‰
- Solidified with 2-3% agar

**Antibiotics**:
- Chloramphenicol: 200 mg/liter (0.02%), added before autoclaving
- Alternative: Penicillin G + streptomycin sulfate, each 150-500 mg/liter, added dry to cooled medium

**Incubation conditions**:
- Mimic ambient environmental conditions
- Polar/cold regions: ~5°C during incubation and examination
- Temperate/tropical: 12°C slows filamentous fungi growth
- Cyniclomyces guttulatus (rabbit feces): 35-40°C, elevated CO2

## Problems and Solutions

- **Filamentous fungal overgrowth**: Shake culture (partial solution); lower incubation temperature to 12°C; examine plates daily
- **Bacterial contamination**: Lower medium pH to 3.5-3.8; add broad-spectrum antibiotics
- **Patchy distribution**: Single-celled organisms aggregate; results from one sample cannot be easily extrapolated
- **Media selectivity**: All isolation media are selective and artificial; prevalent soil strains may not grow
- **Weed species**: Dominant species rapidly overgrow others on artificial media
- **Plate capacity**: ~300 cells per plate; rare species (1% or less of community) may be missed

## Characterization

Standard yeast description includes:
- Fermentation rates on 7-8 different sugars
- [[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]] on ~40 carbon sources, 1-2 nitrogen compounds (nitrate, lysine)
- Growth at various temperatures
- [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] description and sexual state
- **DBB test** (diazonium-blue-B): Discriminates Ascomycetes from Basidiomycetes

## Molecular Identification

- **26S rRNA D1/D2 region sequencing**: Most species identifiable via GenBank BLAST search
- **Nuclear DNA reassociation**: For phylogenetic relationships
