# Cannabis Seed Storage

Proper seed storage is essential for maintaining cannabis seed viability
over extended periods. Cervantes provides detailed guidance on optimal
[[vegetable-storage-conditions-by-temperature-and-humidity]], common threats to seed longevity, and practical
[[food-preservation-canning]] suitable for both home growers and commercial seed
banks.

## Why Storage Matters

A seed contains all the genetic characteristics of a plant -- its size,
disease and [[cannabis-disease-pest-resistance-breeding-clarke]], root, stem, leaf, and flower production,
cannabinoid levels, and many other traits. The genetic makeup of a seed
is the single most important factor dictating plant performance.
Seeds stored too long will germinate slowly and have a high rate of
failure. Strong, healthy parents and proper care yield strong seeds
that germinate well, but improper storage degrades this potential.

## The Three Enemies of Seed Viability

### Moisture

Moisture is the primary threat to stored seeds. Seed hormones -- ABA
([[abscisic-acid-plant-stress-hormone]]), cytokinins, and gibberellins -- are primed to respond
to moisture, which is the first signal to germinate. Preventing
moisture from triggering germination is the core challenge of seed
storage.

- Moisture levels above five percent cause germination rates to
  decrease rapidly.
- Small amounts of condensation can give seeds a false start, causing
  them to expend stored energy without actually germinating.
- Seed pests become active above ten percent moisture content.
- Fungi become active above fifteen percent moisture content.

### Temperature

Temperature directly affects the rate of internal seed activity and
metabolic processes. Higher temperatures accelerate degradation; lower
temperatures preserve viability.

- Seeds store best at 35-40 degrees F (2-5 degrees C) with humidity
  below five percent.
- Dry seeds are temperature-sensitive and can be disinfected with
  brief heat application.
- Super-cold liquid nitrogen and cryogenics can extend storage
  life significantly.

### Air (Oxygen)

Air entering the outer seed shell signals seeds to germinate. Viable
seeds are preserved longer when vacuum-packed to remove oxygen.
Seeds with thin outer shells that never fully seal are particularly
vulnerable, as moisture and air are always present within, causing
hormone levels to slowly dissipate.

## Optimal Storage Conditions

Cervantes identifies the ideal storage parameters:

- **Temperature:** 35-40 degrees F (2-5 degrees C)
- **Humidity:** Below five percent
- **Environment:** Cool, dark, dry
- **Container:** Airtight, with desiccant

Under these conditions, some seeds remain viable for five years or
longer. When 50 percent of stored seeds no longer germinate, the
average storage life is considered over. Seeds a year old or older
often take longer to sprout and have a lower germination rate even
under optimal conditions.

## Practical Storage Methods

### Short-Term Storage

Store seeds in an airtight plastic bag inside a film container or
similar sealed vessel. Place a packet of silicone gel crystals in the
container to absorb excess moisture. Label all containers clearly.
