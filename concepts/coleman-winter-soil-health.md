## Soil Health in the Winter Harvest Greenhouse

Soil management in the winter harvest greenhouse follows the same organic
principles that govern all of the growing at Four Season Farm, but the
protected environment creates both opportunities and challenges that differ
from outdoor soil management.

## The Living Soil Foundation

Coleman's approach to soil health is rooted in the understanding that
healthy soil is a living system. The greenhouse soil, like all soil on the
farm, is treated as a biological community rather than an inert substrate
for plant growth.

This living soil philosophy means that all management decisions are
evaluated for their impact on soil biology. Fertilization, tillage,
irrigation, and [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] and
make nutrients available to plants.

## Compost as the Primary Amendment

Compost is the foundation of soil fertility at Four Season Farm, and the
greenhouse beds receive the same compost-based fertility program as the
outdoor fields. Coleman applied finished compost to the greenhouse beds
during [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]].

The compost serves multiple functions beyond nutrient supply. It improves
soil structure, enhances water-holding capacity, provides habitat for
beneficial organisms, and buffers against pH changes.

Coleman's compost was produced on-farm using a carefully managed process
that ensured high quality and consistent [[holzer-mushroom-cultivation-outdoor-beds]] was used in the greenhouse, ensuring
uniformity across the farm's growing systems.

## Minimal Soil Disturbance

A key principle of Coleman's soil management is minimal disturbance. The
greenhouse beds are not rototilled or deeply cultivated between plantings.
Instead, they are lightly worked to incorporate surface amendments and
prepare a seedbed.

This minimal-disturbance approach preserves the soil's existing structure,
protects [[green-cannabis-rockwool-hydroponic-growing-media]] between crops, highlights the
sustainability advantages of Coleman's approach.

## Soil Biology Through Winter

One of the most significant aspects of greenhouse soil management is the
continuation of biological activity through the winter. In the outdoor
fields, soil organisms slow dramatically or become dormant during cold
periods. In the greenhouse, the moderated temperatures allow biological
activity to continue.

This winter biological activity has several important consequences. It
means that nutrients continue to cycle through the soil even during the
coldest months, supporting the growth of winter crops. It also means that
organic matter decomposition continues, maintaining the soil's organic
matter levels.

The continued biological activity also helps protect against soil-borne
diseases. A diverse and active soil biological community competes with
potential pathogens, reducing disease pressure on winter crops.
