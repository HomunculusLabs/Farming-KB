## Determinate vs Indeterminate

"Determinate" and "indeterminate" refer to different growth patterns. All
tomato vines produce a side shoot from each leaf notch. Indeterminate
vines grow indefinitely from their tip, producing ever-longer vines with
lanky, aggressive spread. They keep setting new fruit and covering new
ground until frost, as long as the root zone has room. The more room roots
have, the more [[comparison-cold-frame-gardening-vs-container-gardening-guide]] or small-space production.

## Spacing and Root Systems

Tomatoes develop enormous root systems when given space. Solomon documents
a case where an indeterminate plant, spaced four feet apart, developed
roots extending five feet in all directions from the stem and penetrating
an eight-foot depth. The general rule: the larger the above-ground plant
can grow, the more growing space you should provide. Crowded root zones
lead to disease susceptibility and lower yields.

## Pests and Diseases

There are many tomato diseases, but most are problems of commercial
fields. If the roots have room to expand, the weather is favorable, and
the soil is reasonably fertile, the vine usually will not become sick.
Key issues include:

- **Fruit worms**: The same larvae that eat corn. Can be controlled by
  spraying Bt (see [[solomon-choosing-seeds-and-catalogs]].

Special variety types worth knowing:

- **Cherry tomatoes** (indeterminate): Most aggressively growing of all
  types, best suited to [[wide-spacing-dry-gardening-low-irrigation-solomon]] methods.
- **Paste/sauce varieties**: Bred to contain less moisture, cooking down
  in half the time and superior for drying.
- **Longkeeper types**: Ripen extremely slowly; green-picked fruits
  develop a protective skin and last months in the pantry. Related to the
  classic Golden Jubilee, a late-maturing yellow beefsteak with
  outstanding flavor.
