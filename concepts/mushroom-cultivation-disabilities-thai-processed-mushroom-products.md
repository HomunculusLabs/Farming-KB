# Thai Processed Mushroom Products and Fermentation Recipes

Because mushrooms are highly perishable, strategies for conservation and
processing are essential for any [[fao-monitoring-evaluation-disabled-mushroom-entrepreneurs]] farmers in northeastern Thailand
documents several traditional Thai methods for processing fresh mushrooms
into shelf-stable products, representing an important income diversification
strategy for small-scale producers.

## The Perishability Challenge

Mushrooms cannot keep for long, and all harvested mushrooms must be sold
quickly. They can be maintained in a refrigerator for 12 to 24 hours.
To avoid humidity accumulating in storage bags, the manual recommends
placing mushrooms in bags, blowing air into them, and tying with a rubber
band. Where no refrigerator is available, mushrooms can be placed on
banana leaves in a cool area or on top of water under shade in a
well-ventilated place.

Fresh mushrooms can be processed into finished products for sale to shops
and restaurants. Many products can be made with mushrooms beyond their
simple addition to soups and sauces. Mushrooms can be converted into
sweets, cookies, candies, various snacks, and can also be dried. However,
facilities in rural areas may not support elaborate processing techniques.

## Fermented Mushrooms: Pla La Hed

Pla la hed is a traditional fermented [[cultivation-of-oyster-mushrooms]] or the Nang Fa variety, 200 grams of roasted
rice, 100 grams of salt, and three tablespoons of garlic cloves.

The preparation process involves cleaning, trimming, and shredding the
mushrooms, then steaming them until tender. After cooling, roasted rice is
pounded using a mortar, and garlic and salt are added and pounded
together. The rice mixture is blended with the mushrooms and placed in a
jar or bottle, pressed firmly with a Styrofoam sheet placed on top, and
the lid closed tightly.

The jar is placed under the sun for approximately three days, then moved
to a shaded area for about two weeks. The fermented mushrooms are then
ready to eat. After opening, the product should be stored in a refrigerator
for conservation. This lactic acid fermentation preserves the mushrooms
while developing complex sour and umami flavors.

## Fermented Mushrooms in Banana Leaf

A second fermentation method wraps the mushroom mixture in banana leaves.
The ingredients include two kilograms of trimmed oyster mushrooms or Nang
Fa variety, 200 grams of sticky rice, 150 grams of garlic, 30 grams of
salt, optionally 200 grams of Shanghai noodles, and fresh small chili
peppers to taste.

The mushrooms are cleaned with water and shredded, then steamed and cooled.
Water is pressed out. Garlic is pounded separately, then mixed with the
mushrooms, sticky rice, and salt. Chili is added and the mixture is
pressed into small balls. These are tied with banana leaves or plastic
and secured with rubber bands.

After sitting at room temperature for approximately three days, the
fermented balls are ready to eat, with a characteristically sour taste
developed through the fermentation process. They can be consumed directly
or fried with eggs, making a versatile addition to the Thai rural diet.

## Heavenly Mushrooms: Hed Sawan

transforms dried mushrooms into a confection. The ingredients are 300
grams of dried shredded mushrooms, 400 grams of palm sugar, white soy
sauce to taste, 100 grams of sesame seeds, one liter of vegetable cooking
