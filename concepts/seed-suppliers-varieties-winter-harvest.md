# Seed Suppliers and Recommended Varieties for Winter Harvest

Selecting the right seed varieties is fundamental to successful winter vegetable production. Winter growing conditions — low light, short days, cold temperatures, and protection under row covers — demand specific cultivar traits including cold tolerance, disease resistance, suitability for dense planting, and ability to maintain eating quality under stress. [[eliot-coleman]] provides a curated list of proven varieties and trusted seed suppliers for year-round harvest systems.

## Recommended Seed Suppliers

### North American Suppliers

**Johnny's Selected Seeds** (Winslow, ME)
- Since 1973, supplying seeds and tools to fresh market growers and home gardeners
- Excellent selection of winter harvest varieties
- Also stocks tools, row covers, and growing supplies
- A primary resource for winter harvest growers

**[[fedco-seeds]]** (Waterville, ME)
- Specializes in varieties for northern growers and short growing seasons
- Cooperative structure with competitive pricing
- Strong selection of cold-hardy varieties adapted to the Northeast

**[[territorial-seed-company]]** (Cottage Grove, OR)
- Publishes a supplementary catalog in June specifically focused on winter gardening
- Valuable for West Coast and milder climate winter growing
- Good selection of overwintering varieties

**Wood Prairie Farm** (Bridgewater, ME)
- Supplier of organic 'Rose Gold' seed potatoes
- Family-owned organic farm with focus on seed potato quality

### European Supplier

**Graines Baumaux** (Nancy, France)
- The most extensive seed listing of any catalog Coleman knows
- Especially strong in old-time French varieties
- Full catalog available online
- Valuable source for unique European salad and vegetable varieties not widely available in the U.S.

### Key Traits of Successful Winter Varieties

Winter greenhouse varieties share several important characteristics:
- **Cold tolerance:** Ability to survive repeated freezing and thawing without tissue damage
- **Low-light performance:** Continued growth during short winter days when light intensity is low
- **Dense planting suitability:** Tolerance for close spacing used in intensive bed systems
- **Flavor under stress:** Maintenance or improvement of eating quality after cold exposure (many greens become sweeter after frost)
- **Disease resistance:** Resistance to fungal diseases promoted by high humidity under cover

## Variety Selection Principles

### Taste and Nutritional Value Over Yield
Coleman consistently emphasizes choosing varieties for taste and nutritional quality rather than maximum yield. These two factors are closely related — varieties selected for flavor tend to be more nutrient-dense. This is a core deep-organic principle.

### Local Adaptation
Growers should trial varieties under their own conditions. Coleman's recommendations come from decades of selection in coastal Maine, but local adaptation matters. What performs best in one microclimate may not be optimal in another.

### Open-Pollinated vs. Hybrid
Coleman's list includes both types. For commercial production, hybrids often offer advantages in uniformity and disease resistance. For seed saving and variety preservation, open-pollinated varieties are preferred.

### Sourcing Diversity
Drawing from multiple seed suppliers ensures access to the widest possible genetic diversity and reduces dependence on any single source. The European supplier (Graines Baumaux) provides access to varieties not available through North American catalogs.
