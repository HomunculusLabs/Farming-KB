# Permaculture Village Infrastructure and Dwelling Design

Infrastructure planning in [[permaculture]] villages follows the principle that life, work, and recreation should be integrated rather than segregated. This stands in direct contrast to modern urban zoning practices, which designate separate areas for industrial, commercial, residential, and recreational use — a pattern that creates enormous transport energy costs and social alienation. Permaculture [[integrated-land-management-and-village-design]] draws lessons from older cities that evolved in energy-conservative environments, where cultural, crafts, commercial, and domestic functions were naturally integrated.

## Infrastructure Planning Sequence

The correct order for planning village infrastructure is:

1. **Water and energy systems** — the foundation of all settlement
2. **Access roads and pathways** — designed for minimum energy expenditure
3. **Dwellings and other structures** — placed within the framework of water, energy, and access
4. **Land use designation** — productive land, forests, recreation, and conservation zones

This sequence ensures that each layer of infrastructure is informed by the systems it depends on, creating a coherent and resilient settlement pattern.

## Community Tools and Equipment

A well-equipped permaculture village maintains shared tools and equipment for the site at large, reducing the need for individual ownership of expensive, infrequently used items:

**Site-level equipment:**
- Tractor with slasher (for fire control and land management)
- Posthole borer (for fencing)
- Trailer (for bulk transport)
- Chisel plough / soil conditioner (for land preparation)
- Chipper (for biomass and mulch production, fire control)
- Tow truck (for mulch and goods transport)
- Van (for goods delivery)
- Back-pack slasher (for blackberry and brush control)

**Workshop equipment:**
- Drill press
- Lathe
- Radial-arm saw
- Welder
- Planer or thicknesser
- Router

[[homestead-workshop-tools]] can be made available on lease or time-share arrangements. The trading trust (Trust B) can charge for replacement cost, wear and tear, fuel or power, and a service charge, ensuring that shared equipment remains well-maintained without imposing an unfair burden on any single user.

## Dwelling Diversity

Permaculture villages require a **mix of dwelling types** to accommodate different household sizes, ages, and needs. A village that only provides traditional family homes fails to serve singles, elderly residents, visitors, and people in transition. The recommended dwelling types include:

- **Family homes**: Individual or trust ownership, 2-5 bedrooms, suitable for couples and families with children
- **Singles quarters**: Flats with strata title, 1-2 bedrooms, with some capacity for guest accommodation
- **Elderly and hospital quarters**: Strata and community trust title, single and double bedrooms, designed for accessibility and community support
- **Terrace housing**: Where appropriate for the site, providing medium-density living options

A mix of housing types provides far more for needs and age differences than the traditional family home alone. Every village should also maintain **one empty strata title** for emergencies — family breakups, temporary housing for new arrivals, or staging people into permanent housing.

## Energy Efficiency in Dwellings

Every house built today can approach **100% space heating efficiency** through design alone, at no extra construction cost. Key [[aact-brewer-design-principles-aeration-systems-ingham]] include:

- **[[passive-solar-design]]**: Orienting buildings to capture winter sun while excluding summer heat
- **Thermal mass**: Using materials that store and slowly release heat
- **Insulation**: Proper insulation in walls, roof, and floor
- **Solar hot water**: Now a routine installation with rapid payback periods
- **Photovoltaics**: Increasingly affordable for on-site electricity generation

Some American towns have enacted ordinances requiring buildings to comply with **60% self-sufficient space heating requirements**. The permaculture standard is higher — close to 100% through design alone.

## The Integrated Settlement Model

The modern practice of zoning cities into industrial, commercial, residential, and recreational sectors is described in permaculture literature as **the very antithesis of good planning** for transport [[desert-house-water-energy-conservation]]. This zoning pattern bears no relationship to the natural zonation of function and available time around a settlement.

Historical examples demonstrate the superiority of integrated settlement:

- **Florence and Vienna**: Cultural, crafts, trades, commercial, and domestic functions were interwoven in walkable neighborhoods
- **Old Berlin**: Housing over street-level shops on the sunny side, with trades and workshops in accessible interior courtyards (Hofs), and markets close by
- **Istanbul and Indian cities**: Trades grouped in streets or market areas where materials, assembly, and sale were all facilitated, while the total neighborhood contained all trades needed for daily life
- **Davis, California**: A modern town with energy-conserving legislation encouraging non-polluting home businesses and a comprehensive bikeway network

In these integrated environments, young people grow up with a capacity to use many materials and make whatever they require, through informal everyday association with the open workshops of tradespeople. Neighbors and family naturally come and go at the workplace, creating a rich learning environment and strong social bonds.

## Land Allotment Categories

Land not attached to individual dwellings should be allotted according to the following categories:

1. **Land for public buildings and services**: Community centres, administrative facilities
2. **Land for recreation, trails, and walks**: Accessible green spaces for all residents
3. **Water storages and reserves, energy sites**: Infrastructure supporting the village
4. **Productive land for food**: Livestock, orchards, nurseries, greenhouses
5. **Forested areas**: For fuels, timber, and ecological services
6. **Reserves for wildlife and flora**: Conservation areas maintaining biodiversity
7. **Easements, wayleaves, roads, and public utility allotments**: Access and services
8. **Commercial areas**: Retail, manufacturing, offices, professional services, common markets

Individual dwelling lots of 0.2-2 hectares can accommodate miniature versions of many of these functions — some food production, roof water storage, home businesses, and recreational assets. However, a larger village site plan enables convivial shared access to productive land, commercial crop potential, significant forests and ponds, and properly designed access and utility easements.

## Shared Facilities and Resource Efficiency

Public access and service centres owned by the village provide multiple benefits:

- **Food processing, freezer, and laundrette facilities**: Provide part-time income for a resident while sharply reducing energy needs for individual households. They also serve as a wider district resource.
- **Tractor-tool-truck hire service**: The trading trust (Trust B) can lease infrequently used assets to residents as needed. A total vehicle leasing system with fleet purchase and insurance, local maintenance, and bulk fuel supply offers significant savings over individual ownership.
- **Community workshop**: A shared workshop with essential tools (drill press, lathe, welder, etc.) eliminates the need for every household to maintain a fully equipped workshop.

## Infrastructure Reserved for Future Development

Forward-thinking village plans should reserve areas for:

- **Education**: School, seminar, and workshop rooms, computers, library, artwork, craft areas
- **Food commerce**: Food processing centre, cafe, coffee shop, home-baked goods, surplus product sales
- **Industrial work**: Noisy/oily work areas for woodwork, metalwork, machine shops, repairs, bulk fuels and oils
- **Retail**: Plant nursery retail, reception for visitors, general retail shops
- **Agriculture**: Dairy and [[faires-dairy-processing-cheese-and-butter]] centre, livestock housing (chickens, goats, pigs, sheep, rabbits)
- **Energy and waste**: Methane generator, ferment tanks, sewage and sullage systems, biomass conversion, water cleanup
- **Horticulture**: Glasshouses, commercial crops, special crops
- **Accommodation**: Camp area for casual visitors (with toilets and showers), potentially a youth or student hostel

By reserving these areas from the outset, the village avoids the costly and disruptive process of retrofitting infrastructure into an already-settled landscape. The general plan of resources should be published and accessible to all participants, ensuring transparency and enabling informed decision-making about future development priorities.
