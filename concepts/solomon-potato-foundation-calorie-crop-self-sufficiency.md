# Potato as the Foundation Calorie Crop

## Overview

Steve Solomon identifies the potato (*[[solanum-tuberosum]]*) as the single most important crop for home food self-sufficiency. In a garden intended to produce a significant portion of a family's calories, no other vegetable approaches the potato's ability to convert garden space into edible food energy. Understanding why the potato dominates [[solomon-garden-planning-calorie-production]], and how to grow it effectively, is essential for anyone serious about food self-sufficiency.

## Why Potato Dominates

### Caloric Efficiency

The potato's advantage over other garden crops is dramatic:

- **Highest calorie yield per unit area**: Potatoes produce more edible calories per square foot than any other temperate-climate vegetable
- **Minimal processing**: Unlike cereal grains (wheat, oats, barley), potatoes require no threshing, grinding, or milling — simply dig, wash, and cook
- **Complete nutrition**: Potatoes provide not only calories (as starch) but also significant protein, vitamin C, potassium, and B vitamins
- **No special equipment**: Growing grain requires 5-10 times more land per person and specialized harvesting/threshing equipment; potatoes need only a shovel

### Comparison with Cereal Grains

Cereal grains (wheat, rye, oats, barley) are the traditional staple crops of civilization, but for the home gardener they present significant disadvantages:

- **Land requirement**: Producing equivalent calories from grain requires 5-10 times more garden area than potatoes
- **Processing complexity**: Grains must be harvested, threshed, winnowed, and ground before they become edible
- **Equipment needs**: Effective grain processing requires a grain mill at minimum; commercial-scale operations need combines and threshers
- **Yield variability**: Home-grown grain yields are often low and inconsistent without specialized knowledge and equipment
- **Nutritional tradeoff**: Grains provide calories as starch but are generally lower in vitamins and minerals than potatoes

Solomon concludes that the "healthful potato is really the thing for gardening" when the goal is calorie production.

## Garden Space and Calorie Production

### Solomon's Experience

Solomon's personal garden provides a real-world benchmark:

- **Growing beds**: Approximately 2,000 square feet (185 m²) of active growing beds
- **Production**: Provides about half of a two-person household's yearly food intake
- **Rotation**: Every 4-5 years, beds are rotated into grass/clover ley for soil restoration, effectively doubling the required land to ~4,000 ft² (370 m²) of growing beds
- **Total enclosed area**: ~6,500 square feet (600 m²) including paths, perennials, and rotation fallows
- **Potato dominance**: Over half of the caloric production comes from potatoes

### Scaling for Full Self-Sufficiency

For families seeking near-complete calorie self-sufficiency:

- **Half calories**: ~2,000 ft² growing beds for two adults
- **Full calories**: ~4,500 ft² (420 m²) growing beds plus root cellar for winter storage
- **With rotation**: Double the growing area for ley rotation, requiring ~9,000 ft² total
- **Add 500 ft²**: For additional calories per adult in the family
- **Without irrigation**: Add ~7,000 ft² (650 m²) in the rainier parts [[berry-growing]]

Potatoes and sweet potatoes form the caloric foundation of all these scenarios.

## Growing Potatoes Effectively

### Soil Requirements

Potatoes are classified as **high-demand vegetables** requiring:

- **Rich, deep soil**: Loose, well-drained soil allows tubers to expand freely
- **[[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof]] (COF)**: Solomon's COF provides balanced NPK plus [[dynamic-accumulator-plants-trace-minerals]]
- **High organic matter**: Compost or well-rotted manure improves soil structure and moisture retention
- **pH consideration**: While Solomon de-emphasizes pH correction in favor of balanced mineral nutrition, potatoes benefit from adequate calcium

### Planting Techniques

Key practices for successful potato production:

- **Seed potato selection**: Use certified disease-free seed potatoes; cut into pieces with 2-3 eyes each
- **Planting depth**: Plant 4-6 inches deep in prepared beds
- **Spacing**: Wider spacing (12-18 inches between plants) produces larger tubers; tighter spacing yields more but smaller potatoes
- **Hilling**: Mounding soil around growing plants protects developing tubers from greening and increases yield
- **Chitting**: Pre-sprouting seed potatoes before planting gives a head start on the season

### Varieties

Choose varieties based on local adaptation, storage quality (late-season varieties store best), disease resistance (especially late blight and scab), and intended culinary use.

### Pest and Disease Management

Common potato challenges and organic responses:

- **Colorado potato beetle**: Hand-picking, Bt ([[bacillus-thuringiensis]]) applications, row covers for early season
