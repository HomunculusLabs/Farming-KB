# Old World Solanaceous Hallucinogens and Iboga

The nightshade family (Solanaceae) produced some of the most feared and revered hallucinogenic plants in European and Asian history. Belladonna, henbane, mandrake, and datura — the ingredients of medieval witches' brews — contain [[mandrake-mandragora-officinarum-in-european-folklore]]. It entered the mythology of virtually every European people, who feared its deadly power. The attractive, shiny black berries frequently cause accidental poisoning, especially in children. Four species of *Atropa* are distributed from Europe to the Himalayas.

### Etymology

The name **belladonna** ("beautiful lady" in Italian) derives from a medieval cosmetic practice: Italian women of high society dropped the plant's sap into their eyes to dilate the pupils enormously, producing a glassy, intoxicated stare considered beautiful and sensual in that era.

### Chemistry and Uses

| Alkaloid | Concentration | Notes |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| **Hyoscyamine** | Principal | Anticholinergic; racemizes to atropine |
| **Scopolamine** | Present | More psychoactive than hyoscyamine |
| **Atropine** | Present | May form during extraction rather than exist in the living plant |

The alkaloids are concentrated in the **leaves and roots**. Belladonna is a commercial source of atropine, widely used in modern medicine as an antispasmodic, antisecretory, mydriatic (pupil-dilating), and cardiac stimulant. Commercial cultivation began in the 19th century in the United States, Europe, and India, replacing wild collection.

## Henbane (*Hyoscyamus niger*)

### Description and Uses

An annual or biennial plant native to Europe, northern Africa, and western and central Asia. Henbane was a standard ingredient in medieval witches' brews and toxic preparations, used to induce visual hallucinations and the **sensation of flight** — likely the origin of the witches'-broom folklore. It has long been valued in medicine as a sedative and anodyne for inducing sleep.

### Chemistry

The principal alkaloid is hyoscyamine, with significant amounts of the more hallucinogenic scopolamine also present, along with several other alkaloids in smaller concentrations. One of approximately 20 *Hyoscyamus* species in the Solanaceae.

## Mandrake (*Mandragora officinarum*)

### Cultural Significance

Mandrake possesses perhaps the most extraordinary folklore of any hallucinogenic plant in European history. Its complex history as a "magic hypnotic" is inextricably bound to the **Doctrine of Signatures** — the medieval theory that a plant's appearance reveals its properties. The mandrake root's resemblance to a human form (sometimes male, sometimes female) led to beliefs in its magical powers.

### Folklore

- **The shriek**: According to superstition, pulling a mandrake from the earth produced an unearthly shriek that could drive its collector mad. Dogs were sometimes tied to the root and provoked to pull it out
- **Aphrodisiac properties**: Widely believed across many European regions
- **Panacea**: Considered a cure-all in medieval European folk medicine
- **Harvesting rituals**: Elaborate magical procedures were prescribed for safe collection

### Chemistry and Distribution

Mandrake contains the tropane alkaloids hyoscyamine, scopolamine, and others. It was undoubtedly one of the most potent ingredients in medieval witches' brews. Six species of *Mandragora* belong to the Solanaceae and are native to the region between the Mediterranean and the Himalayas.

## Datura (*Datura metel* and relatives)

### Asian Use

Datura (*D. metel*, known as dhatura or dutra in India) has been used as a narcotic since prehistory. Its purple-flowered form has a long history across multiple civilizations:

- Possibly responsible for the intoxicating smoke of the **Oracle of Delphi**
- Identified in early Chinese writings as a hallucinogen
- Mentioned by the Arabian physician **Avicenna** (11th century) as "jouzmathel"
- Recorded as an aphrodisiac in the East Indies (1578)
- Held sacred in China — believed that when Buddha preached, heaven sprinkled the plant with dew

### Illicit Use

In many parts of Asia, Datura seeds are mixed with food and tobacco for illicit purposes, notably by thieves to stupefy victims who may remain seriously intoxicated for several days. *D. metel* is commonly mixed with cannabis and smoked. A white-flowered form (*D. fastuosa*, considered by some botanists as a distinct species) is also smoked with cannabis or tobacco across Africa and Asia.

## Iboga (*Tabernanthe iboga*)

### Description and Distribution

Native to Gabon and the Congo in equatorial West Africa, iboga is the only member of the dogbane family (Apocynaceae) known to be used as a hallucinogen. The plant provides the strongest single force against the spread of Christianity and Islam in the region.

### Cultural Significance

The yellowish root is employed in initiation rites of several secret societies, the most famous being the **Bwiti cult**. Entry into the cult requires having "seen" the god-plant Bwiti, accomplished through iboga ingestion. The drug was discovered by Europeans in the mid-19th century and has a reputation as a powerful stimulant and aphrodisiac. Hunters use it to stay awake all night; "sorcerers" take it to seek information from ancestors and the spirit world.

### Chemistry

**Ibogaine** is the principal indole alkaloid among a dozen others found in the plant. Its pharmacology is well characterized:

- **Hallucinogenic**: Produces unworldly visions at moderate doses
- **Stimulant**: Strong central nervous system stimulation at large doses
- **Toxic**: High doses lead to convulsions, paralysis, and respiratory arrest

Modern research has investigated ibogaine as a treatment for substance addiction, particularly opioid dependence, though its cardiotoxicity limits clinical application.

## New World Hallucinogens: Introduction

The New World (North, Central, and South America, and the Caribbean) harbors far more hallucinogenic plant species than the Old World — over 90 compared to fewer than 20. Some New World cultures were profoundly shaped by religious hallucinogen use, which acquired controlling significance in nearly every aspect of life. The accomplishments of indigenous Americans in the use of **plant mixtures** (additives to primary hallucinogens) were extraordinary.

### Puffballs (*Lycoperdon mixtecorum* and *L. marginotum*)

The Mixtec Indians of Oaxaca, Mexico use puffballs as **auditory hallucinogens**. After eating these fungi, a native hears voices and echoes. Unlike psilocybin mushrooms, puffballs do not serve as divinatory agents in Oaxacan culture:

- *L. mixtecorum*: Called **gi-i-wa** ("fungus of the first quality"); the stronger species
- *L. marginotum*: Called **gi-i-sa-wa** ("fungus of the second quality"); has a strong fecal odor
