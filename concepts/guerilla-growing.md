# Guerilla Growing

Guerilla growing, a term coined in the early 1970s, is the clandestine cultivation of
cannabis in remote outdoor locations. It requires strategy, time, and often physical
prowess. Depending on location and local laws, guerilla growing in remote areas may be
the only option for cultivators who cannot grow at home.

## Site Selection

Location and security are the primary concerns for a guerilla grower. Choose a location
with limited public access. Check regulations for hunting and recreation, and consider who
might use the area: hunters, hikers, mushroom foragers, other growers, dirt-bikers, and
scouts. Select a remote site unlikely to be visited casually.

## Vegetation Cover

Look for sites with big green stands of existing vegetation. Cannabis is a vigorous plant
with a large root system, and a flowering female will stand out if surrounding vegetation
dies back before harvest. Stands of thorny blackberry bushes, ferns, and meadow grass are
good options because they provide natural camouflage and deter casual visitors. Stinging
nettles also camouflage cannabis well and their painful sting deters curious visitors.

## Sunlight Requirements

Plants need a minimum of five to six hours of direct sunshine per day. Scout potential
sites during winter when foliage is absent to accurately assess sun exposure. The angle
of the sun changes through the seasons -- a site that receives full sun in summer may be
shaded in spring or autumn. Remember that the sun takes a higher path in spring and
summer. Rocky terrain, hillside terraces, and grasslands all receive good amounts of
sunlight.

## Water Access

Ample water is an important factor for site selection. If rainfall is unreliable, locate
the garden near a water source that does not dry up in summer. Hauling water to a remote
site is labor-intensive and increases the risk of detection. Exclusive access by boat can
reduce discovery risk, but ensure plants are not visible from the waterway. Many people
use waterways and explore land bordering rivers, so choose bank locations carefully.

## Soil Preparation

Prepare the marijuana patch up to six months before planting. Remove competing vegetation
in the fall for a spring garden. Clear patches to allow sufficient sunshine, cut back
roots of competing plants, and till planting holes two to three feet square. Allow
amended soil to sit for a month or more before planting. Proper planning and preparation
are important since remote locations are hard to visit regularly.

A complete low-maintenance setup is the goal for most guerilla growers. Loosen the soil,
amend it, and throw in a handful of polymers to retain moisture. A thick layer of mulch
early in the year will attract water, keep the soil cool, and prevent evaporation.
