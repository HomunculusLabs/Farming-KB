## Edge Effect in Aquaculture

The edge effect in water is pronounced. Stocking rates for fingerlings treat surface area and margin length as interchangeable, so a relatively narrow drain holds as many fish as a broadwater several times its surface area. The sinuous canal is a rich environment compared with the circular or square pond, and much cheaper to construct in clay soils.

Maximum edge is assured by swale, canal, or [[permaculture-designers-manual-ponds-and-dams]] can provide essential nesting and refuge places.

## Shellfish and Invertebrates

Shallow marine waters can be used for aquaculture and mariculture of shellfish. Impounded water has a great variety of products other than food, flowing on to energy production, recreation, irrigation systems, and transport.

## Polyculture Guild Design

Design for aquaculture applies the same methodologies as any designed system. Hold one idea (or species) at centre, and see how many designed features connect to and from it. The pond polyculture furnishes:
- Riparian insectivorous plants
- Bee-attracting marginal species
- Nesting sites for waterfowl
- Aquatic plant zones (shallow, mid-depth, deep)
- Fish of different feeding levels (bottom feeders, mid-water, surface feeders)
- Shrimp and crustacean species in shallow zones

## Yield Factors Affecting Production

Several factors can lift base pond productivity:
- Liming to adjust pH
- Manuring for nutrient input
- Providing high protein food
- Aeration (stirring in air)
- Maximising edge through crenellation

The highest fish production recorded was achieved in a rapid canal flow of mainly sewage. The least productive pond would be a circular, warm, clear, concreted basin in a quiet valley.
