## Why Not Compost?

Fukuoka was often asked why he didn't compost his straw before applying it.
His answer
was that composting is unnecessary labor. When straw is chopped and spread
as mulch,
it decomposes in place, feeding soil organisms directly. The soil surface
is the
perfect composting environment — it has the right moisture, temperature,
aeration,
and biological community.

Composting, in Fukuoka's view, is another example of unnecessary human intervention.
Nature compostes perfectly well on its own. The farmer who gathers straw,
piles it,
turns it, waters it, and then spreads it has done a great deal of work for
no additional
benefit.

## The "One Straw" Philosophy

The title "The One-Straw Revolution" refers to this practice — and to the
larger
principle it embodies. A single straw returned to the earth represents the
restoration
of natural cycles. Multiply this by millions of farmers, and you have a revolution
— not of technology or policy, but of the fundamental relationship between
humans
and the earth.
