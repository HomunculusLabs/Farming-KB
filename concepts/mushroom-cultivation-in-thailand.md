# Mushroom Cultivation in Thailand

Thailand has ideal environmental conditions [[grass-seed-substrate-for-mushroom-cultivation]] and a long
[[history-of-mushroom-cultivation]] growing. Thai people have long grown a variety of mushrooms,
recognizing that mushroom growing requires low-cost materials and technologies
while offering high and quick returns. Young rural people are eager to learn
cultivation using locally available materials. The Thai government actively
promotes mushroom cultivation through Royal Mushroom Projects aimed at rural
development and government loan programs for rural communities.

## Growing House Types

Two types of mushroom houses are used: temporary structures and long-term
buildings. Typical houses are made of thatch and bamboo poles with shading nets,
using locally available dried grasses and leaves. Durability ranges 1-5 years
depending on materials. Button and straw mushroom growers use brick houses with
two rows of shelves, which tolerate in situ pasteurization with steam.

## Shelf Cultivation: Straw and Button Mushrooms

### Straw Mushroom (Volvariella volvacea)
The most popularly grown mushroom in Thailand since the 1940s. Thailand's
temperature range (30-37C) is ideal. Spawn is easily available for local farmers.
Cultivated on shelves unlike most other bag-grown mushrooms. A simple method:
inoculate a straw bale in a bag and mushrooms emerge days later. Traditional
rice field cultivation uses rows of mounds from December to April (20% yield,
THB20/kg). Indoor cultivation with compost achieves 50-60% yield, sold fresh at
THB30/kg or to canneries at THB12-16/kg. Net profit averages THB5,000/month.

### Button Mushroom (Agaricus bisporus)
Seasonal production in cooler months (November-January). Requires organic compost
grown on shelves in brick houses. Methods include outdoor composting (Phase I),
in situ pasteurization (Phase II), spawning, spawn-run, and fruiting. One farm
produces 1 ton/year on 144m2 growing area. Another farm achieves 13kg/m2.
Local demand from Pizza Hut and McDonald's is met by imports from Holland and
Australia at THB52/kg. Farm sanitation practices are critical for quality.

## Bag Cultivation

Oyster, abalone, yanagi, and shiitake are commonly cultivated in bags. Large
farms use ribbon mixers, bagging machines, compacting machines, steam boilers,
and ventilation fans. They also supply ready-to-fruit bags to neighboring farms.

### Substrate Preparation
Mix rubber tree sawdust + 20% rice bran + additives (gypsum, lime, CaSO4 or
MgSO4). Adjust moisture to 60-65% (droplet barely escapes when squeezed). Fill
and compact bags. Pasteurize at 90-100C for 3-4 hours. Cool to 25C and
inoculate. Plastic rings make bottle-neck openings for easy handling, plugged
with cotton, paper, or cotton-topped plastic plugs. Sorghum seed is the most
common spawn carrier.
