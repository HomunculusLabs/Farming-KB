## The Corruption of Ikebana

The Japanese too have a strong affection for flowers and
have raised ikebana (flower arrangement) to an art. But
ikebana has changed. Lately, steel wire is used and dried
branches are colored with paint. People call this an art of
self-expression, but to Fukuoka's thinking, the true art of
ikebana has been discarded and replaced by an artificial,
Westernized sense of beauty.

A representative of a major ikebana school once interviewed
Fukuoka. He said he was entirely dissatisfied with the state
of ikebana today and suggested that the soul of nature was
better portrayed in the days when one spoke of the ten, chi,
and jin (heaven, earth, and man). He wondered what would
happen if one were to see nature from directly above,
behind, or below.

## The Loss of True Beauty

Fukuoka worries that man no longer sees what beauty is, from
where it arises, and why something is beautiful. Decorating
a room with lovely flowers or paintings is not what beauty
is all about. It is not that there are no beautiful flowers
outside, but rather that we no longer are able to see the
beauty of nature's flowers.

People should first grieve over the loss of beauty in their
own hearts. The flowers in the teahouse are not beautiful
because they have been handsomely arranged. If a single
flower is brought into a simple teahouse free of any
adornment, even a wild flower will immediately appear to be
a gorgeous chabana ("tea flower"). Beauty does not come from
the flower itself.
