# Permaculture Alternative Nation and Global Governance

Mollison envisions a fundamental rethinking of the concept of "nation"
and proposes a new representative body to speak for ethical communities
worldwide. This vision challenges the existing nation-state system and
the United Nations as inadequate to represent the true diversity of
human aspirations for peace and ecological responsibility.

## Critique of the Existing United Nations

The "United Nations" today, Mollison argues, is neither united nor
represents nations. Many true nations, such as the Iroquois
confederation or any tribal alliance with a common ethic, are not
represented by such a body. Nor are whole peoples such as the Basques,
Tartars, Kurds, Palestinians, Hawaiians, Hopi, Tibetans,
Pitjatjantjara, Misquito, Aranda, Basarwa, Herrero, and many others.
Most nations in the United Nations repress a majority of peoples on
earth.

## Redefining Nation

Mollison proposes defining a nation as a people subscribing to a
common ethic, and aspiring to a similar culture. Such nations may not
have a common land base or language, but do have a common ethic,
minimally comprising three commitments:

- To care for the earth; to repair and conserve
- To seek peace, and to guard human rights everywhere
- To invest all capital, intelligence, goodwill, and labour to these
  ends

## The Ethical Majority

Many thousands of organisations, affinities, tribes, bioregions, and
non-government organisations aspire to beneficial ends. In every
continent, a majority of people -- the "ethical majority" -- want
peace, a clean and forested earth, cessation of torture, malnutrition,
and oppression, and a right to work towards these ends. There are
far fewer paid-up or active members of political parties or oppressive
societies than there are organic gardeners whose life works seek peace
and plenty.

## Practical Steps Toward a New Governance

It would take very little additional organisation for these groups to
meet together, count their numbers, and recognise each other's rights.
As groups discuss and accept the minimal ethic, they can quickly
proceed to recognise each other. Initiatives had already commenced in
Amerindian groups subject to national oppression in both North and
South America. Throughout the world, groups were talking of issuing
their own passports, or adopting world citizen status given a common
aim.

## International Affinities

The long-term vision is a world of international affinities rather
than competitive nation-states. In several generations, if allowed
time, humanity may achieve a truly free world of international
affinities. The starting point is always where we are -- with existing
communities and their local challenges.

## The Right Not to Be in Debt

Mollison highlights that some of the most charming and climatically
appropriate houses on earth are built without bank loans, architects,
metals, concrete, or contractors. In Chile, while the economic system
guided by "experts" accumulated a $12 billion foreign debt, poor
people acting without loans together built at least $11 billion in
housing in slum areas by local cooperation, without incurring any
foreign debt. Stone, mud, bamboo, round timbers, rope, thatch, and
even baked brick and tiles are the age-old durable building materials
of mankind. All can be locally produced if energy from community
forests and people is provided.

## Root Causes of Shelter Deprivation

The real cause of a lack of shelter in any country is not finance, but
restrictive practices by a regulatory bureaucracy. State or private
ownership of forests, small mines, and lands devoted to corporate
profits denies basic biological and earth resources to the people who
work to produce or mine them. Truly basic rights to grow or protect
forests, to build shelter, grow food, or provide water from roof areas
are commonly denied by local or state regulations.

## Political Affiliations and Green Movements

There are two primary ways to ensure the political changes which will
bring ecological changes. The first is to mobilise ground support in
every electorate where a candidate of any party takes a stand on good
ecology or against nuclear and polluting industry. The second is to
form a local Ecological or Green Party, or a bioregional group. This
is easier if all intentional groups affiliate and subscribe to a
common policy. Common policy always leaves room for local issues but
gives strong principles for guidance.

## Evolving Common Policy

A common global policy can start with a general ethic and proceed to
specific policies for specific cultures, regions, and landscapes. The
structure for gathering common policy follows these steps:

1. Define the problem or concern and weight priorities on a scale
   from 1 to 10
2. State the intent of policy for your region and its principles
3. Collect strategies that have been proven to work as case
   histories
4. Frame policies based on all successful strategies
5. Set overall policy priorities weighted for urgency, public cost,
   global spread, long-term effect, and threats to basic resources

## Non-Growth Economics

The broad aims of an ecological political movement are ecological,
socially egalitarian, grassroots democratic, and non-growth. Mollison
notes that by 1983, the German Green Party already held 23 federal
and 48 state seats, demonstrating the political viability of
ecological governance principles. See
