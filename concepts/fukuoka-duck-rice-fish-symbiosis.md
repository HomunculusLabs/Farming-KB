# Fukuoka Duck, Rice, and Fish Symbiosis

Masanobu Fukuoka advocated the integration of ducks, fish, and other animals into rice paddies as a natural alternative to [[fukuoka-no-weeding-no-pesticides]] principle, which holds that the farmer should not need to weed or spray if the ecosystem is properly balanced.

## Fish in the Rice Paddy

Alongside ducks, Fukuoka suggested releasing young carp into the flooded rice paddies. The fish add another dimension to the paddy ecosystem:

- They consume mosquito larvae and other aquatic insects
- Their waste fertilizes the water and soil
- They can be harvested as an additional protein source
- They help cycle nutrients between water and soil layers
- They contribute to the overall biodiversity that keeps pest populations in check

## The Vision of Integrated Livestock

Fukuoka painted a vivid picture of his ideal farming landscape, an integrated ecosystem where multiple species coexist:

> "I see bees busily making the rounds of clover and vegetable blossoms thickly flowering beneath trees laden heavy with fruit; I see semi-wild chickens and rabbits frolicking with dogs in fields of growing wheat, and great numbers of ducks and mallards playing in the rice paddy; at the foot of the hills and in the valleys, black pigs and boars grow fat on worms and crayfish, and from time to time goats peer out from the thickets and trees."

## The Self-Sustaining Food Web

Fukuoka's vision of animal integration went beyond the rice paddy. He described a complete food web in which each element supports the others:

- Trees provide shade and leaf litter that enriches the soil
- Clover and vegetables beneath the trees fix nitrogen and provide forage
- Chickens scratch through the litter, eating insects and spreading manure
- Ducks in the paddy weed, fertilize, and stir the soil
- Pigs root through the ground like small tractors, preparing areas for new planting
- Bees pollinate the diverse flowering plants
- Goats graze on brush and undergrowth that might otherwise encroach

This interconnected system means that the farmer does not need to purchase feed, fertilizer, or pest control agents. Each animal's waste becomes another's food or the soil's enrichment. The system produces grains, vegetables, fruits, eggs, meat, and fish simultaneously from the same land area.
