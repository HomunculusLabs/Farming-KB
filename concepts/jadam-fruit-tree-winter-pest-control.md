# Fruit Tree Winter Pest Control (JADAM Recipe #13)

## Overview

JADAM pesticide recipe #13 is a dormant season spray for fruit trees. Applied after leaf fall and before bud break, this high-concentration treatment controls overwintering pests and diseases including scale insects, Valsa canker, moss disease, and pear sucker. The addition of fine red clay powder provides physical coating that enhances the pesticidal effect.

**Developed by:** Chanmo Gim

## Recipe

For 500 L (132 gal) of spray solution:

- [[jadam-wetting-agent-jwa]] 10 L (2.6 gal)
- [[jadam-sulfur-js]] 5 L (1.3 gal)
- Fine red clay powder 2 kg (4.4 lb)
- Water to make 500 L (132 gal)

## Target Pests and Diseases

- Valsa canker
- Scale insects (arrowhead scale, other scale)
- Pear sucker
- Moss disease
- Overwintering insect eggs and larvae
- Fungal spores on bark

## Application Schedule

1. **After leaf fall:** Apply once after all leaves have dropped
2. **Late winter to bud break:** Apply 1-2 times between late February and budding in spring

## Special Consideration for Evergreens

For fruit trees that do NOT shed leaves (evergreens):
- Reduce JWA to below 2 L (0.53 gal)
- The high JWA concentration in the standard recipe can damage foliage on evergreens

## Enhancement Options

- Add [[jadam-herb-solution-jhs]] for stronger pesticidal effect
- Increase JWA for stronger wetting and coverage on rough bark

## Fine Red Clay Powder

The fine red clay powder is critical to this recipe. It provides:
- Physical barrier coating on bark
- Enhanced adhesion of pesticide to tree surface
- Additional mineral nutrients
- Abrasive action against scale insects

See jadam red clay powder method for the detailed preparation method.

## Spraying Technique

When spraying with red clay powder:
- Keep stirring the solution continuously, OR
- Run an aerator/pump so clay particles do not sink to the bottom
- The clay tends to settle quickly -- constant agitation is essential

## Relative Strength

Recipe #13 uses the highest JS concentration (5 L per 500 L) of any standard JADAM recipe. This is possible because:
- Trees are dormant (no tender foliage to burn)
- Application is during cool weather
- There are no consumable fruits on the trees
- Bark is thick and resistant
