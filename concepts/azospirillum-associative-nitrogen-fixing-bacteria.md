## Azospirillum — Associative Nitrogen-Fixing Bacteria

Azospirillum is a genus of Gram-negative, associative nitrogen-fixing bacteria that colonize the
rhizosphere and root surfaces of a wide range of grasses, cereals, and other crops without
forming the specialized root nodules characteristic of the Rhizobium-legume symbiosis. First
isolated by Johanna Döbereiner and colleagues in Brazil during the 1970s from the roots of
Digitaria and other tropical grasses, Azospirillum has become one of the most extensively
studied and commercially applied plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) worldwide. The
genus occupies a unique ecological niche as an associative diazotroph — living in close
proximity to plant roots and benefiting from root exudates while providing nitrogen and
growth-promoting compounds in return.
