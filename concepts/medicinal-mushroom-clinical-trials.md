# Medicinal Mushroom Clinical Trials

## Overview

Medicinal mushrooms have been the subject of increasing clinical research, particularly in East Asia where several mushroom-derived preparations are approved pharmaceuticals. This page summarizes the current state of clinical evidence for the most widely studied medicinal mushrooms, distinguishing between well-established evidence, preliminary findings, and areas requiring further research.

## Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)

### Immune Function

Reishi is one of the most studied medicinal mushrooms globally, with a pharmacopoeia of traditional use spanning over 2,000 years in China, Japan, and Korea.

Clinical evidence:

- Immune modulation: Multiple RCTs demonstrate enhanced immune cell activity (NK cells, macrophages, T-cells) in healthy adults and immunocompromised patients
- A 2003 meta-analysis of 5 RCTs found reishi supplementation significantly improved immune parameters
- Doses used in clinical studies typically range from 1.5-3 g dried fruiting body per day
- Both beta-glucans and triterpenes contribute to immunomodulatory effects

### Anti-Cancer Adjunct

- Used as a complementary therapy alongside conventional cancer treatment in Japan and China
- Studies show improved quality of life, reduced chemotherapy side effects, and enhanced immune function in cancer patients
- Some evidence for direct anti-tumor effects but primarily supportive rather than curative
- Ganoderic acids have demonstrated apoptosis induction in cancer cell lines

### Anti-Inflammatory and Liver Health

- Human trials show reduced inflammation markers (CRP, IL-6, TNF-alpha)
- Hepatoprotective effects demonstrated in patients with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease
- Cholesterol reduction: meta-analyses show modest but significant LDL and total cholesterol reduction

### Current Evidence Level

Strong for immune modulation and quality of life improvement. Moderate for anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective effects. Preliminary for anti-cancer direct effects.

## Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor)

### Cancer Adjunct Therapy (PSK and PSP)

Turkey tail has the strongest clinical evidence of any medicinal mushroom due to its approval as a pharmaceutical in Japan (PSK, trade name Krestin) and China (PSP).

PSK clinical evidence:

- Approved in Japan since 1977 as an adjunct to chemotherapy for gastric, colorectal, esophageal, and breast cancers
- Multiple RCTs spanning decades demonstrate improved survival rates when added to standard chemotherapy
- A landmark meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (3,695 patients) found PSK significantly improved 5-year survival in colorectal cancer patients
- Mechanism: Enhanced NK cell activity, dendritic cell maturation, and T-cell response
- Dose in clinical settings: 3 g/day PSK

PSP clinical evidence:

- Approved in China as an adjunct cancer treatment
- Similar but not identical to PSK; different protein-polysaccharide complex
- Clinical evidence primarily from Chinese studies
- Dose: 3 g/day PSP

### Immune Function in Non-Cancer Populations

- Studies in healthy volunteers show enhanced immune cell proliferation
- Shown to improve immune response in immunocompromised patients (HIV, post-chemotherapy)
- Prebiotic effects: promotes growth of beneficial gut bacteria

### Notable Recent Trial

A 2018 University of Minnesota/ Bastyr University study (led by M. Torkelson) demonstrated that turkey tail mushroom improved immune function in women with stage I-III breast cancer undergoing chemotherapy.

### Current Evidence Level

Very strong for cancer adjunct therapy (PSK/PSP). Strong for immune modulation. Moderate for gut health.

## Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)

### Cognitive Function

The most researched application for lion's mane:

- Mori et al. (2009): 30-week RCT in 50 Japanese adults (50-80 years) with mild cognitive impairment. Lion's mane group (3 g/day dried powder) showed significant improvement vs. placebo in cognitive function scale. Effects diminished after discontinuation, suggesting ongoing use is needed.
- A 2020 double-blind RCT showed improved cognitive performance in healthy older adults taking lion's mane for 12 weeks
- Small studies suggest benefits for memory, concentration, and overall cognitive function
- Mechanism attributed to hericenones (fruiting body) and erinacines (mycelium) stimulating NGF and BDNF

### Nerve Regeneration

- Animal studies show accelerated peripheral nerve regeneration
- Preliminary evidence for benefit in diabetic neuropathy
- One small human trial showed improved nerve function in patients with nerve injuries
- Research is early-stage; larger human trials needed

### Mood and Anxiety

- Small studies suggest anti-anxiety and antidepressant effects
- A 2010 study in menopausal women found reduced anxiety and improved sleep quality
- Animal studies support anti-depressant-like effects via NGF/BDNF pathways
- Human evidence is limited but promising

### Current Evidence Level

Moderate for cognitive function in mild impairment. Preliminary for nerve regeneration and mood. Need for larger, longer human trials.

## Cordyceps (Cordyceps sinensis / militaris)

### Energy and Exercise Performance

Cordyceps is traditionally used for fatigue and vitality:

- Several RCTs show improved VO2 max and exercise endurance in healthy adults
- A 2016 meta-analysis found cordyceps supplementation significantly improved exercise performance
- Mechanism: Enhanced ATP production, improved oxygen utilization, increased blood flow
- CS-4 (fermented Cordyceps sinensis mycelium) is the most commonly studied commercial form
- Doses: 1-3 g/day in clinical studies

### Kidney Function

- Used in traditional Chinese medicine for kidney disorders
- Some clinical evidence for improved kidney function in chronic kidney disease patients
- May reduce creatinine levels and improve eGFR
- Evidence is preliminary and primarily from Asian clinical studies

### Sexual Function

- Traditional use as an aphrodisiac and vitality enhancer
- Limited clinical evidence; some small studies show improved libido and sexual function
- Likely related to energy enhancement and improved blood flow
- Not well-studied in rigorous trials

### Anti-Fatigue

- Multiple human trials show reduced fatigue markers and improved energy
- Particularly studied in elderly populations
- May work through improved mitochondrial function and oxygen utilization

### Current Evidence Level

Moderate for exercise performance and energy. Preliminary for kidney function and sexual function. Limited large-scale trials.

## Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)

### Immune Function

- Lentinan (beta-1,3/1,6-glucan) is approved in Japan as an injectable cancer therapy adjunct
- Oral shiitake consumption shows immune-enhancing effects in human trials
- A 2015 University of Florida study found daily shiitake consumption improved immune cell proliferation and reduced inflammation markers
- Antiviral properties: lentinan shows activity against various viruses in vitro

### Cardiovascular Health

- Eritadenine (a nucleoside derivative) lowers cholesterol
- Human trials show modest LDL cholesterol reduction
- May have anti-thrombotic effects

### Current Evidence Level

Strong for immune function. Moderate for cardiovascular health. Well-established as a nutritious food with medicinal properties.

## Maitake (Grifola frondosa)

D-fraction (beta-glucan extract) is the most studied preparation. Phase I/II trial showed improved immune function in breast cancer patients, though evidence is less extensive than turkey tail or reishi. Animal studies suggest blood sugar regulation via AMPK pathway activation, but human trials are limited with mixed results. Overall evidence: preliminary to moderate.

## Limitations of Current Research

Many studies are small, short-term, or single-institution. Standardization varies widely (strains, extraction, doses). Publication bias favors positive results, and many Asian studies aren't indexed in Western databases. Few head-to-head species comparisons exist, long-term safety data is limited, and synergistic compound effects are understudied.
