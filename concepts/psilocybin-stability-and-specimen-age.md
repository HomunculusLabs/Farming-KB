# Psilocybin Stability and Specimen Age Effects

## Overview

The stability of psychoactive compounds in neurotropic fungi is a critical but
often overlooked factor affecting both the efficacy of traditional use and the
reliability of [[guzman-allen-gartz-conflicting-chemical-analyses-neurotropic-fungi]]. Psilocybin, psilocin, and related indole
alkaloids degrade significantly over time, with profound implications for potency,
cultural practices, and scientific research. Multiple lines of evidence — from
[[psilocybin-chemistry-stability-indigenous-knowledge]] to controlled laboratory studies — demonstrate that specimen
age is one of the most important variables determining the psychoactive content
of collected fungi.

## Compound Volatility

Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT) and its dephosphorylated active metabolite psilocin (4-HO-DMT)
appear to be volatile or semi-volatile compounds. This was dramatically demonstrated
by Guzmán during fieldwork in Oaxaca, Mexico in 1958. While sleeping in a small,
closed-door room filled with freshly collected or semi-fermented specimens of
[[psilocybe-caerulescens]], P. cordispora, P. cubensis, P. mexicana, and P. zapotecorum,
he experienced colored hallucinations without having consumed any fungi. The room's
air was heavy with the strong, acrid aroma of the fungi, and the hallucinations
ceased immediately upon leaving the room for fresh air. This incident provides
direct evidence that psilocybin and/or psilocin can volatilize from fungal tissue
into the surrounding atmosphere at sufficient concentrations to produce perceptual
effects.

## Time-Dependent Degradation

### In Living Specimens

Repke et al. (1977) demonstrated that baeocystin content varies with specimen
age in living fungi. In Psilocybe baeocystis and P. cyanescens, no trace of
indole compounds could be detected in specimens analyzed 20-60 days after
collection. This finding has important implications for both chemical analysis
protocols and the traditional use of these fungi.

### In Dried Specimens

The degradation of psilocybin in dried [[herbarium-specimens-fungal-collections]] follows a clear
time-dependent pattern. Ohenoja et al. (1987) measured psilocybin content in
herbarium specimens of [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]] of different ages:

- **1869 specimen**: 0.014% dry weight
- **1954 specimen**: 0.67% dry weight
- **1976 specimen**: 0.84% dry weight

This represents a roughly 60-fold reduction in psilocybin content over a century
of storage, with the most significant losses occurring in older specimens. The
implications for chemical studies relying on herbarium material are profound —
many early reports of "inactive" specimens may simply reflect compound degradation
rather than true absence of psychoactive constituents.

### In Freshly Dried Material

Guzmán observed that specimens of Psilocybe mexicana and P. caerulescens kept
dried for approximately one year produced only slight or entirely inactive effects
when consumed, compared to fresh material. This observation aligns with indigenous
Mexican knowledge and the herbarium degradation data.

## Indigenous Knowledge of Potency and Age

The Mazatec Indians of Oaxaca possess detailed traditional knowledge about the
relationship between specimen age and psychoactive potency. They specifically
advise that old dried specimens kept for more than one year "are not good to
use" and should be discarded. This traditional practice is entirely consistent
with modern chemical evidence of time-dependent degradation of psilocybin and
psilocin. The indigenous knowledge likely developed through centuries of empirical
observation, representing a sophisticated understanding of pharmacokinetics that
predates modern analytical chemistry.

## Implications for Chemical Analysis
