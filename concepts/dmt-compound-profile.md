## N,N-Dimethyltryptamine

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is one of the most widely occurring psychedelic compounds in nature. It is found in scores of plant species across multiple families and is an endogenous compound in mammalian brains.

### Chemical Identity

- Molecular weight: 188.27 g/mol
- Melting point: 44-68°C (free base); 165-167°C (HCl salt)
- Active salt forms: picrate, oxalate, fumarate (FDA-approved for clinical IV studies)
- EI-MS: base peak at m/z 58 (C3H8N+), parent ion 188 (4%)

### Dosage and Administration

| Route | Dosage | Duration |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Oral | >350 mg (inactive without MAOI) | — |
| Intramuscular | 60-100 mg | ~1 hour |
| Subcutaneous | 60-100 mg | ~1 hour |
| Intravenous | 4-30 mg | ~1 hour |
| Smoked | 60-100 mg | 15-30 min |

Oral doses up to 350 mg produce no effects whatsoever due to rapid metabolism by monoamine oxidase (MAO) in the gut and liver. This is the fundamental pharmacological basis of [[ayahuasca-pharmacology]].

### The Experience

At threshold i.v. doses (~4 mg), no effects distinguishable from placebo. At 8 mg, physical effects without mental effects. At 15 mg (threshold psychedelic), nearly all subjects report visual hallucinations; auditory changes are rare. At 30 mg, effects are overwhelming in both speed and intensity — described as a "freight train" that is underway well before the 45-second infusion is complete.

Smoked DMT produces an extraordinarily rapid onset (30-60 seconds), with the full experience compressed into 5-15 minutes. Reports describe moving colored patterns, animal imagery, complete [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]], and encounters with seemingly autonomous entities. A common trajectory: "Up, up, out, out... expanding until I have become so large that I no longer exist."

### Natural Occurrence — "DMT is Everywhere"

DMT occurs in plants used across indigenous cultures:
- **Snuffs** (cohoba, epena, parica): ground beans of Piptadenia peregrina and related Anadenanthera species
- **Ayahuasca**: as a component combined with MAOI-containing plants (see [[ayahuasca-pharmacology]])
- **Jurema** ([[entheogen-jurema-mimosa-hostilis-ethnobotany]]): roots contain DMT (originally called "nigerine")
- **Acacia** species: numerous African and Australian species contain DMT
- **Phalaris** grasses: reed canarygrass species contain DMT and 5-MeO-DMT
- **Virola** species: Amazonian trees whose bark provides snuff material

### Metabolism

DMT is rapidly metabolized through several pathways:
- Oxidation by monoamine oxidase (MAO) to inactive indoleacetic acid (primary route)
- Oxidation to the N-oxide
- Cyclization to beta-carbolines
- N-dealkylation to N-methyltryptamine (NMT) and tryptamine
- Minor pathway: enzymatic conversion to N,N-dimethylkynuramine (DMK), analogous to tryptophan's kynurenine pathway

### Clinical Research

DMT is the only psychedelic tryptamine to complete FDA/DEA approval for modern human studies. Research at the University of New Mexico established:
- No tolerance development with repeated i.v. administration at half-hour intervals
- Smooth dose-response grading of subjective effects
- Blood levels and autonomic effects remained stable over repeated dosing

### Endogenous Presence

DMT has been detected in human urine, blood, and brain tissue. A methylating enzyme capable of forming DMT from tryptamine exists in blood plasma and is present in both normal subjects and schizophrenics. Blood level comparisons between psychotic patients and normals have shown no significant differences. The debate over whether DMT functions as an endogenous psychotogen or natural neurotransmitter remains unresolved.

### Key Derivatives

- **5-MeO-DMT** — more potent, faster (see [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]])
- **4-HO-DMT** (psilocin) — orally active (see [[psilocin-and-psilocybin-chemistry]])
- **Bufotenine** (5-HO-DMT) — found in Anadenanthera and Bufo toads
- **NMT** (N-methyltryptamine) — widely distributed in nature, less potent
- **DET, DPT, DIPT** — N,N-dialkyl homologues with distinct character
