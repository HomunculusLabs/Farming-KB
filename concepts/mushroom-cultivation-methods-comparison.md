# Mushroom Cultivation Methods Comparison

Side-by-side comparison of cultivation methods from beginner to advanced. Each method trades off difficulty, cost, yield, and species compatibility. See [[mushroom-grow-equipment]] for gear lists and [[mushroom-sterile-technique]] for contamination prevention.

## PF TEK (Brown Rice Flour Cakes)

The classic beginner method developed by Psilocybe Fanaticus. Requires no pressure cooker.

**Substrate**: 1/8 cup brown rice powder + 1/2-2/3 cup fine vermiculite + 40-45cc water per 1/2 pint jar. Finer vermiculite holds more water. Maximum fruiting formula: 1/4 cup BRP + 1/2 cup vermiculite + 60cc water.

**Jars**: Kerr wide-mouth 1/2 pint canning jars (tapered, no shoulders). 4 inoculation holes punched in lid with 3-penny nail, rubber seal up.

**Sterilization**: Steam in vegetable steamer pot at gentle boil for 1 hour. Tight-fitting pot lid essential. Jars larger than 1/2 pint unreliable for PF TEK.

**Key innovation**: Dry vermiculite barrier layer (1/2-3/4 inch) on top of wet substrate protects against airborne contamination while allowing gas exchange (PF's original discovery).

**Inoculation**: 1cc spore syringe per jar (10 jars per syringe). Tilt syringe to distribute spore water down jar wall. Wait 3-5 days at 70F for germination.

**Fruiting**: Dual-chambered terrarium (10 gallon aquarium with spray shield). Spray primary chamber 2+ times daily; mist molecularizes against shield before reaching secondary chamber. Never directly spray fungus. Do not heat terrarium — 60-85F is fine. Indirect light or small fluorescent.

**Timeline**: Spore to fruit in 4-6 weeks total. 2-3 weeks colonization, 1-2 weeks to pinning, 2 weeks fruiting.

**Pros**: No pressure cooker needed, minimal equipment, very forgiving, excellent for learning sterile technique
**Cons**: Small yields per jar, limited to BRF-loving species, manual humidity management

## Monotub (Bulk Substrate)

Scale up from PF TEK by spawning colonized grain to bulk substrate in a modified plastic tote.

**Process**: Colonize grain jars (rye or WBS) -> pasteurize bulk substrate (coir/verm, or coir/verm/manure) -> mix spawn 1:2 to 1:4 ratio in tub -> colonize 10-14 days -> introduce fruiting conditions -> harvest multiple flushes.

**Tub modification**: 6 holes (2 near substrate level on each long side, 1 high on each short side) stuffed with polyfill or covered with micropore tape for passive FAE.

**Pros**: Higher yields than PF TEK, self-contained, multiple flushes, good for many species
**Cons**: Requires pressure cooker for grain spawn, larger upfront space commitment

## Straw Logs

Inoculated straw packed into poly tubing or wire-reinforced columns. Classic for oyster production.

**Process**: Chop wheat straw to 2-4 inch pieces -> pasteurize at 160F for 20-30 min (hot water submersion or steam) -> drain to field capacity -> mix with grain spawn (10-15% by weight) -> pack into tubing -> incubate 14-21 days -> cut holes for fruiting.

**Peter Oei method**: Compact straw 6-12 inches deep in beds or logs. S. rugoso-annulata fruits well on compacted straw.

**Pros**: Pasteurization only (no PC), cheap materials, high biological efficiency, scalable
**Cons**: Can be messy, straw quality varies, seasonal if outdoor, shorter lifespan than logs

## Sawdust Blocks

Industry standard for shiitake, lion's mane, reishi, maitake. Supplemented hardwood sawdust in filter-patch bags.

**Substrate**: 4:1 hardwood sawdust:bran + 0.2% CaCO3 + 1% gypsum. 55-65% moisture. Sterilize at 15 psi for 90-120 minutes.

**Process**: Mix substrate -> load into polypropylene bags with filter patch -> sterilize -> inoculate with grain spawn -> incubate 2-4 weeks -> introduce fruiting conditions.

**Pros**: Highest yields per volume, reliable results, suited for high-value species
**Cons**: Requires pressure cooker, supplement increases contamination risk, single-use bags

## Outdoor Beds and Garden Integration

No sterile technique needed. Wood chips, straw, or mixed substrate layered in garden beds or pathways.

**Wine cap (S. rugoso-annulata)**: Layer soaked wood chips or straw 4-6 inches deep, inoculate with spawn, cover with cardboard during colonization. Fruits spring and fall. Produces for years as it spreads.

**King stropharia pathways**: Inoculate wood chip garden pathways. The mycelium network improves soil structure, filters runoff, and produces edible mushrooms from otherwise unused space.

**Pros**: No equipment investment, integrates with existing gardens, multi-year production, improves soil
**Cons**: Weather-dependent yields, pest/animal pressure, slower to establish, less control

## Synthetic Logs

A hybrid between natural log cultivation and indoor sawdust blocks. Supplemented sawdust substrate shaped into cylinders and wrapped in plastic, simulating a natural log with higher nutritional content.

**Process**: Prepare supplemented sawdust substrate (hardwood sawdust + bran at 4:1 to 5:1 ratio) -> pack into tubular form (PVC mold or log-shaped polypropylene bags) -> sterilize at 15 psi for 90-120 min -> inoculate with grain spawn -> incubate until fully colonized -> remove from mold, wrap in breathable plastic or perforated sleeve -> introduce fruiting conditions.

**Advantages over natural logs**: Faster colonization (2-4 weeks vs 6-18 months), higher biological efficiency, controllable substrate composition, can grow species not suited to local wood types.

**Advantages over sawdust blocks**: Natural log shape allows fruiting on all surfaces, extended production over multiple flushes, more natural appearance.

**Best species**: Shiitake (L. edodes), oyster (Pleurotus spp.), lion's mane (H. erinaceus), reishi (G. lucidum).

## Accessible Cultivation Methods

Developed through FAO programs for cultivators with physical or mental disabilities. These simplified approaches also benefit beginners, low-budget growers, and anyone seeking less physically demanding methods.

**Key adaptations** (FAO training manual, Thailand):
- **Low-cost mushroom houses**: A structure holding 1,000 spawn bags can be built for as little as 500 Baht (~US$12) using local bamboo, thatch, and recycled materials
- **Repetitive task design**: Many mushroom cultivation tasks (inoculation, bag packing, harvesting) are repetitive and easily learned, making them suitable for people with cognitive disabilities
- **Cool environment work**: Mushrooms grow in shade at lower temperatures, reducing physical exertion compared to open-field farming
- **Seated workstations**: Spawn bag inoculation and packing can be done seated with adapted tools
- **Quick returns**: Mushrooms can be produced and sold within 2-4 months, providing fast income feedback
- **Three-in-one approach**: Training combines rehabilitation (mind, spirit, body), cultivation skills, and enterprise development

**Simplified bag method**: Pasteurize straw or sawdust substrate by soaking in hot water (no pressure cooker needed) -> drain and pack into polypropylene bags with filter patches -> inoculate with purchased spawn -> incubate in warm dark area -> cut holes for fruiting when colonized. This method requires minimal physical strength and no specialized equipment beyond the spawn and bags.

## Choosing a Method

- **First-time grower, minimal budget**: PF TEK
- **Higher yields, willing to invest in PC**: Monotub
- **Want gourmet mushrooms cheaply**: Straw logs (oyster)
- **Shiitake, lion's mane, reishi**: Sawdust blocks or logs
- **No indoor space, have a garden**: Outdoor beds / wine cap pathways
- **Maximum yield per effort**: Sawdust blocks for indoor; log cultivation for long-term outdoor
