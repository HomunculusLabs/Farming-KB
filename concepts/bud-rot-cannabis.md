# Bud Rot (Botrytis cinerea) on Cannabis

Gray mold (Botrytis cinerea), commonly known as bud rot or bud mold, is the most common and destructive fungus that attacks [[query-how-to-set-up-an-indoor-cannabis-grow-on-a-budget]] plants. It flourishes in the moist temperate climates common to grow rooms and can spread through an entire crop in a matter of days. Botrytis is so prolific that it can reduce a harvest to a wisp of powdery foliage in short order. In one documented case, a grow room located near a swamp had omnipresent Botrytis spores; the grower lost four consecutive crops before moving to a new location.

## Identification

Botrytis starts within the bud and is difficult to detect at onset. Key identifiers:

- **In moist climates**: Grayish-whitish to bluish-green, hair-like growth resembling laundry lint
- **In less humid environments**: Dark, brownish spots on buds that are dry to the touch
- As the disease progresses, affected foliage turns somewhat slimy
- Severely affected buds crumble if rubbed
- The damage often starts deep inside the bud where it is invisible from the outside

Botrytis is not limited to buds — it also attacks stems, leaves, and seeds, causes damping-off in seedlings, and decomposes dry stored bud. It is transmitted via seeds and airborne spores that are present virtually everywhere. While most commonly found attacking dense and swelling flower buds, it also attacks stems and can cause stems to fold over.

## Life Cycle and Spread

Botrytis reproduces through airborne spores that are present virtually everywhere:

- Spores land on susceptible plant tissue and germinate when conditions are favorable
- The fungus penetrates plant tissue and begins decomposing it from the inside
- Botrytis needs UV light to complete its life cycle — without UV light it cannot live
- As the fungus grows, it produces more spores that spread to other plants
- Transported by air, contaminated hands, and tools; spreads very quickly indoors
- Can infect an entire grow room in less than a week when conditions are right
- Flower buds are quickly reduced to slime in cool, humid conditions or unsmokable powder in warm, dry rooms

The rapid spread of Botrytis makes constant vigilance essential, especially during the [[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]] when dense buds create ideal microclimates for fungal growth.

## Early Warning Signs

Watch for single leaves that mysteriously dry out on buds — these can be telltale signs of a Botrytis attack inside the bud. Constant observation, especially during the last two weeks before harvest, is essential. Other warning signs include:

- Individual leaves on buds turning yellow or brown without obvious cause
- Small dark spots appearing on bud surfaces
- A slight discoloration at the base of buds
- Unusual odors from affected areas

## Damage Symptoms

- Flower buds are quickly reduced to slime in cool, humid conditions
- In warm, dry rooms, buds become unsmokable powder
- Stem damage: stems turn yellow and cankerous growths develop, causing growth above to wilt
- Botrytis can destroy an entire crop in 7-10 days if left unchecked
- Transported by air, contaminated hands, and tools; spreads very quickly indoors
- Damping-off in seedlings when soil-borne Botrytis attacks at the soil line
- Decomposition of harvested, stored buds if drying conditions are inadequate

## Environmental Risk Factors

Botrytis thrives in:

- Humidity above 50% (cool below 70°F/21°C, moist climates are ideal)
- Dense, tightly packed buds that retain moisture internally
- Dead leaf stems and petioles left on stalks after defoliation — often harbored by dead, rotting foliage
- Low light levels that encourage weak growth
- Excessive nitrogen and phosphorus levels making foliage tender and susceptible
- UV light is required for Botrytis to complete its life cycle
- Heavy crowding of plants that restricts air circulation
- Stale, unventilated air that allows moisture to accumulate around buds

## Prevention

- **[[cervantes-humidity-control-dehumidification]]**: Keep humidity at 50% or less; install vent fans large enough to remove moist air quickly
- **Air circulation**: Ample circulation and ventilation are essential; a vent fan is the easiest and least expensive humidity control device
- **[[blesching-cannabis-strain-selection-receptor-targeting]]**: Grow varieties that do not produce heavy, tightly packed buds; many crosses are more resistant than pure indica varieties; some varieties seldom fall victim to gray mold
- **Sanitation**: Remove dead leaf stems and petioles when defoliating; keep the grow room clean
- **Fresh medium**: Use sterile growing medium for each crop
- **pH management**: Keep pH around 6 to facilitate calcium uptake
- **[[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]]**: Harvest when resin glands are still translucent — once glands turn amber, threat of gray mold increases substantially
- **Avoid excess nitrogen and phosphorus**: These make foliage tender, allowing Botrytis to get a foothold
- **Bright light**: Low light levels encourage weak growth and gray mold attack

## Organic Controls

- **Gliocladium roseum** and **Trichoderma** species — foliar spray application
- **Gliocladium** and **Trichoderma** species — soil application to prevent damping-off
- **Pichia guilliermondii** and **Candida oleophila** (yeasts) — experimental controls showing promise
