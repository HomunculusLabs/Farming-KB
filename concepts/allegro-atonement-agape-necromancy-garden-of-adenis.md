## Atonement Sacrifice, Agape, Necromancy, and the Garden of Adonis

## Substitution and Atonement

Christian theology centres on Jesus as the atoning sacrifice who reconciles God
and the world (II Cor 5:18). In the fertility-cult framework Allegro
reconstructs, a state of "sin" or cosmic imbalance arises whenever crops are
plundered from the earth — most critically when the Holy Plant itself is snatched
prematurely from the womb of the ground. This rupture demands reconciliation
through compensatory offerings, and only the god himself can fully atone for
the violation.

Josephus records that the [[allegro-star-of-the-morning-venus-and-the-sacred-mushroom]] is itself a manifestation of necromancy
— "divination by the dead." The mushroom springs from the grave of the god and
carries his revived presence.

The souls who return to the bowels of the earth dwell closer to the "waters of
knowledge," the subterranean abyss from which the mushroom springs. The dead are
not departed but are the source of hidden wisdom, accessible through the
sacred plant.

I Samuel 28:7-14 describes Saul consulting the witch at En-Dor, where Samuel's
ghost appears as an 'Elohim "coming out of the ground," wearing a robe that
ancient versions describe "like an erection" — a phallic [[psilocybin-mushroom-image-guide-habitat-clues]]. The
ghost rises from the earth itself, like the fungus pushing through soil.

The Sumerian NAM-TAR, "plague demon," passes into Greek as Nektar, the divine
drink of the gods. Lilith, the "night-hag" of Isaiah 34:14, is probably another
[[allegro-bible-morals-ten-commandments-mushroom-word-play]] — a chthonic female spirit associated with the underworld and
with the mushroom that grows in darkness.

The Rephaim, described as those "cast down from heaven," correspond to the
fallen angels of Genesis 6 who, according to Enoch, "taught them charms,
cutting of roots, acquaintance with plants." Isaiah 26:19 promises "Thy dead
shall live…on the land of the Shades (Rephaim) thou wilt let dew fall" — the
dew of light associated with Venus, the [[allegro-morning-star-lucifer-venus-dew-manna-st-elmo-fire]], whose cult is inseparable
from the mushroom.

## The Agape Feast

The New Testament "Agape" or Love Feast (Jude v.12; II Peter 2:13) was
connected by Syriac translators with rites of comforting the dead. The verb
agapaō was used by tragedians for affection shown toward the dead; it derives
from Sumerian AG_AG "love" combined with "stretch, measure" (corresponding to
Semitic m-sh-kh).

Related terms reinforce the funerary context: nekragogos, "leading forth the
dead," and psukhagogeo, "to conjure up souls from the nether world." The Agape
feast is fundamentally a meal shared with the dead — a communion between the
living and the departed god.

If Agape is itself a mushroom name, then the feast consists of eating the
mushroom's flesh and drinking its juice — identical to the "Lord's Supper" and
the consumption of the "crucified Christ." As Paul writes in Galatians 2:20:
"I have been crucified with Christ; I live, yet it is no longer I, but Christ
lives in me" — the mushroom-eater becomes one with the god who dies and rises
again.

## Speaking in Tongues

Necromancers and ventriloquists — literally "belly-speakers" — produced what
Isaiah 8:19 calls "chirping and muttering" when consulting the dead. The
practice was widespread in [[solomon-sprouting-seeds-and-early-season-nutrition]] that wither quickly — represent, in Allegro's interpretation,
the raising of the fungus itself: lamenting and simultaneously raising the dead
god.

Allegro claims to be the first to decipher the names involved and to draw
together the scattered references to [[mckenna-food-gods-minoan-crete-mushroom-religion]] in this context. The
worship encompassed every aspect of nature — it was not metaphor or symbol but
a life-or-death matter for the ancients.

The mushroom god's fate is summarised in a single image: "In the morning it
appeared, and by nightfall the worms had consumed it." This transient deity,
born from the earth at dawn and devoured by dusk, is the original of every
crucified and resurrected saviour in Western religion.
