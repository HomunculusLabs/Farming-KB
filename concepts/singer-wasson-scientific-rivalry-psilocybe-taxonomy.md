# Singer-Wasson Scientific Rivalry Over Psilocybe Taxonomy

## A Taxonomic Dispute That Rumbled for Decades

The scientific investigation of [[shroom-cultural-history-western-discovery-valentina-wasson]] over the naming and classification of [[cattle-dispersal-psychoactive-fungi-australia]].

## The Key Players and Their Motivations

[[rolf-singer]] was a distinguished mycologist who led his own research team
investigating the [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]] of Mexico. His boss, Sam
Stein, was interested in the therapeutic properties of psychedelics, and
was eager to conduct experiments with the newly discovered mushrooms.
On the other side stood [[roger-heim]], Professor of Mycology and director
of the Paris Natural History Museum, who had been recruited by Wasson to
systematically identify the species used by Mazatec curanderos.

## The Spark: Competing Publications

The rivalry came to a head when Singer published his treatise on the
hallucinogenic mushrooms just months ahead of Heim. This was a
significant blow to Heim, who had invested enormous effort in the
taxonomic work, travelling to Mexico on multiple occasions, collecting
and cultivating specimens in the laboratory, and carefully documenting
each species. The matter of scientific priority in naming species was
at stake, and both sides felt deeply invested in the outcome.

## Psilocybe Wassoniorum vs Psilocybe Muliercula

Heim had intended to honour his friend Gordon Wasson by naming one
particular species Psilocybe wassoniorum, as a tribute to Wasson's
pioneering role in bringing the magic mushroom to the attention of the
Western world. However, as a result of Singer's swiftly published paper,
Singer got priority and named the species Psilocybe muliercula instead.
In the rules of biological taxonomy, priority goes to the first validly
published name, regardless of the namer's motivations.

## A Personal and Professional Spat

A rather undignified spat ensued, both personally and in the press, with
much name-calling from both sides. The dispute rumbled on into the 1980s,
long after the initial discoveries had been made. The situation was
partly resolved and Wasson's dented pride partially restored when
Psilocybe wassoniorum was later placed into the scientific textbooks,
though the episode left lasting scars on professional relationships.

## Singer's Personal Caution About Self-Experimentation

Singer, by his own confession, was nervous about the effects of the
mushrooms, and avoided eating them himself. This put him in a curious
position: he was publishing taxonomic treatises on psychoactive fungi
without having personally experienced their effects. His reluctance stood
in contrast to the enthusiastic self-experimentation of Heim, Hofmann,
and many other researchers in this field.

## Heim's Interest: Not Purely Professional

Roger Heim's interest in the hallucinogenic mushrooms was not quite as
purely professional as it appeared. As luck would have it, this expert
mycologist, chosen by Wasson because of his glittering academic
credentials, had experimented with the fly-agaric as a young man way
back in 1923. Whether this had been an accident, the upshot of youthful
exuberance, or a calculated experiment remains unclear. Years later, he
wrote that consumed fresh and in sufficient quantities, the fly-agaric
can produce an agreeable sensation leading to a feeling of
contemplative well-being.
