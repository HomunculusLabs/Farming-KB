## Black Currants (Ribes nigrum)

Rich in vitamin C, producing heavy crops in sunny locations. Require plenty of minerals—prepare soil well. Tolerant of most conditions. Plant 5ft (1.5m) apart. Cropping begins in 2 years. Can suffer from iron deficiencies.

Black currants fruit best on one-year-old wood, so pruning involves removing a quarter of the oldest stems at ground level each winter. This encourages vigorous new growth from the base and maintains a productive balance of age classes.

## Raspberries (Rubus idaeus)

Easy to grow in temperate climates and respond well to organic methods. Grown as canes on wire support, planted 18in (45cm) apart. Prefer heavy, rich soil. Tolerant of varied conditions. Cropping begins in 1 year. Can suffer from iron deficiencies.

Summer-fruiting raspberries are pruned after harvest by cutting out the canes that have fruited down to ground level, leaving the new canes for next year. Autumn-fruiting varieties are cut to ground level in late winter, simplifying management considerably.

## Blackberries (Rubus sp.)

Bramble fruits take considerable room but are worth growing if space allows. Newer varieties taste much better. Grown on wire support, planted 10ft (3m) apart. Tolerant of most well-drained soils. Cropping begins in 1-2 years.

Thornless varieties such as 'Loch Ness' and 'Waldo' make cultivation and harvesting much more pleasant. Train the long canes onto a simple wire framework stretched between sturdy posts.

## Loganberries (Rubus loganobaccus)

Bramble fruits growing best in cooler climates. Need regular attention to tying in and training. Same cultivation as blackberries.

## Gooseberries (Ribes uva-crispa)

The earliest soft fruits of the season, flowering in early spring. May not suit very cold areas. Can be grown as cordons against a wall if space is limited. Prefer heavy, moisture-retentive soil in sun. Plant 5ft (1.5m) apart. Cropping begins in 1-2 years.

Gooseberry mildew can be problematic in humid conditions. Good [[comparison-chinampas-vs-raised-bed-gardening]] for acid-loving plants may be needed in alkaline gardens. Cropping details similar to gooseberries.
