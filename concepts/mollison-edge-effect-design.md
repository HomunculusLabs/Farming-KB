House protection: "You only need a hundred feet of non-fuel systems between the house and the forest." Select plants with high ash content, high water content, low total bulk, and dense growth.

Mulches are safe: "You don't need to worry about mulches." Sawdust, too, is "good safe mulch."

## Water Defense

"Primary protection in fire is to have good sprinklers down hill." Sprinklers must be turned on BEFORE the fire arrives -- once fire is present, water doesn't travel far from nozzles.

Roof sprinklers: "Put a monsoon sprinkler on the ridge of the roof... the tap to it should be outside. Turn it on, and the whole house is being washed down." Use a gravity system, "because if it is part of a public system... the system will be inadequate."

## Architecture for Fire

Wooden houses have 13-15% higher survival than stone or brick. "Stone transmits heat rapidly to the inner surfaces." White painted houses reflect radiation.

Roof design: rolled-under section on bottom edge, gutters placed below it. "Leaves will fall off. They can't get in the gutter. Snow will slide off... Fire ash will slide down and fall off."

Fire hardware mesh screens prevent large particles from entering houses.

Fire shelters: T-shaped or L-shaped pits, earthed over. "Go maybe six feet deep. Open your back door, and hop down into your little root cellar, which is also a fire shelter."
