# Oyster Mushroom Harvesting and Post-Harvest Storage

Fresh mushrooms are among the most perishable of all agricultural products. Understanding post-harvest biology is essential for any commercial grower who wants to deliver quality product to market. Oyster mushrooms present particular challenges due to their high respiration rate, thin flesh, and sensitivity to handling.

## Post-Harvest Biology

Mushrooms continue to **respire after harvest** at a rate approximately three times greater than most fruits. This elevated respiration rate is a good indicator of storage life — the higher the respiration, the shorter the shelf life. Respiration results in progressive changes in mushroom texture as the tissues break down internally.

### Spoilage Mechanisms

Several factors contribute to post-harvest deterioration:

- **Internal bacteria and fungi** — spoilage organisms present within the mushrooms continue to multiply during storage, causing rapid deterioration when mushrooms are removed from cold storage
- **Enzymatic browning** — enzymes react with oxygen to form brown pigments, seriously decreasing quality
- **Water loss** — mushrooms are 85–95% water with no barriers to water loss from their surface; when they wilt and shrivel, quality drops significantly
- **Texture softening** — loss of firmness and darkening of flesh occur as metabolic processes continue

## Shelf Life

The shelf life of fresh oyster mushrooms may vary from **1 day to 2 weeks** depending on storage conditions, handling, and the initial quality of the harvested mushrooms.

## Short-Term Storage

### Refrigeration

Shelf life can be extended by refrigeration at **1–4°C**. Cooling mushrooms results in lower rates of all physiological processes within the tissue. During initial cooling there is a high cooling load, but once mushrooms are pre-cooled, the cooling load is much reduced.

### Critical Cooling Window

The temperature of mushrooms at harvest equals the temperature in the fruiting area. Generally, the metabolic temperature of mushrooms is **15–18°C** after harvesting. Heat is generated by processes within the mushroom and is high during fruiting.

If mushrooms are not rapidly cooled but placed into boxes or covered by PVC film, their temperature will **increase** due to ongoing metabolic processes, potentially causing spoilage from internal bacteria and fungi.

To stop this metabolic process rapidly, mushrooms should be cooled to a storage temperature of **0–2°C within five hours** of picking.

### PVC Film Storage (Modified Atmosphere)

The best method for fresh storage of oyster mushrooms is to keep them at **8–10°C** in a packed container wrapped in plastic film — commonly called **PVC film storage**. Wrapping mushrooms with microporous or perforated plastic film improves storage life by:

- Reducing moisture loss
- Preserving mushroom quality and texture
- Creating a modified atmosphere inside the package

Carbon dioxide levels increase and oxygen levels decrease inside wrapped containers due to mushroom respiration. This modified atmosphere can further extend shelf life beyond what refrigeration alone provides.

## Harvesting Best Practices

Proper harvesting technique directly affects post-harvest quality:

1. **Trim mushrooms** — remove the substrate block and any tough stem base
2. **Wash gently** — clean mushrooms of any substrate debris
3. **Weigh and package promptly** — minimize the time between picking and packaging
4. **Cool immediately** — begin the cooling process as soon as possible after harvest

## Long-Term Storage

For long-term preservation, several methods are available:

- **Canning** — sealed in liquid; quality rarely comparable to fresh
- **Pickling** — preserved in acidic brine solution
- **Drying** — moisture content reduced from 70–95% to approximately 10%; inactivates enzymes and microorganisms

Drying is not commonly used for oyster mushrooms but is feasible. It preserves good flavor and is convenient for long-term storage and transportation.

## Market Considerations

Mushrooms are highly perishable, and in fresh produce markets, **freshness equals price**. The key to profitability is minimizing the time from the growing room to the store shelf. Efficient cold chain management, proper packaging, and rapid distribution are critical success factors.
