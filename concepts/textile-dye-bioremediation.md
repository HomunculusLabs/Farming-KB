## Bioremediation of Textile Dyes

Textile dye contamination of water resources is a major environmental concern, particularly in developing countries with significant textile manufacturing activity.

The [[mollison-designers-wastewater-treatment-wetlands]].

Bioremediation offers promising approaches for dye decolorization and degradation, with fungi playing a particularly important role due to their non-specific enzyme systems.

This page provides an overview of textile dye bioremediation, complementing the fungal-specific discussions in [[fungal-textile-dye-effluent-decolorization]] describes fungal approaches to treating textile dye effluents under realistic process conditions.

## Mechanisms of Dye Decolorization

Dye decolorization by microorganisms can occur through three principal mechanisms: adsorption, enzymatic degradation, and a combination of both.

Adsorption to microbial biomass removes dye from solution but does not destroy the dye molecules, creating secondary waste that requires disposal.

[[singh-ligninolytic-enzymes-fungal-bioremediation]] provides a more direct pathway to dye mineralization without the production of toxic aromatic amine intermediates.

The ligninolytic enzymes laccase, [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] can oxidize a wide range of synthetic dyes through radical-mediated mechanisms.

Laccase is particularly effective for dye decolorization because of its broad substrate specificity, its use of molecular oxygen as a cosubstrate, and its stability under a range of environmental conditions.

[[fungi-in-composting-and-agricultural-waste-conversion]] substrates provides a low-cost approach for producing dye-decolorizing enzyme preparations.

fungal-bioreactor-types-for-pollutant-removal describe reactor configurations for continuous treatment of dye-containing wastewaters.

Immobilized fungal bioreactors using fungal biomass entrapped in alginate, polyurethane, or other supports provide high cell density and enable reuse over multiple treatment cycles.

[[mycoremediation]] for dye treatment includes measurement of decolorization (spectrophotometric analysis), intermediate identification (HPLC, GC-MS), toxicity assessment (bioassays), and enzyme activity monitoring.
