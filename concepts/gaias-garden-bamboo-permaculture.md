## The Many Functions of Bamboo

### Windbreak and Privacy Screen

Bamboo grows quickly into a dense, tall screen
that provides excellent wind protection and
privacy. The rustling foliage creates a soothing
sound while filtering wind effectively. In the
ecological garden, a bamboo grove can serve as a
living fence that also produces useful material.

### Habitat Creation

Birds use bamboo leaves and twigs for nests, and
bamboo stands attract insects that become food
for birds and other wildlife. The dense growth
provides shelter from predators and weather for
many small creatures. In Hemenway's framework,
bamboo serves simultaneously as windbreak, privacy
screen, wildlife habitat, and food source.

### Construction and Craft Material

Bamboo canes have endless uses around the garden
and home:

- Garden stakes and trellises
- Tomato cages and plant supports
- Fence posts and fencing
- Tool handles
- Furniture, flutes, wind chimes
- Mats and handmade paper
- Edging and garden borders

Bamboo poles can be transformed into furniture
and useful objects by those with even modest
crafting skills.

### Food Source

The young shoots of most bamboo varieties are
edible and delicious when steamed or sauteed.
Hemenway notes that the best way to control
bamboo's potentially rampant growth is to eat
any shoots that venture outside the chosen bamboo
zone -- stacking the function of pest control and
food production.

### Erosion Control

Bamboo's dense, fibrous root system holds soil
firmly on slopes and banks. It has been used
successfully to control erosion on abused lands;
in Vietnam, bamboo has thrown a living net over
devastated landscapes.

### Microclimate Creation

A shady, sussurating grove of bamboo enfolds the
garden in green canes that arch overhead. The
dense shade creates cool microclimates that
benefit shade-loving plants and provide respite
from summer heat.

### Carbon Sequestration

Bamboo is one of the fastest-growing plants on
Earth, with some species growing several feet per
day during peak season. This rapid growth makes it
an exceptional carbon sink, pulling large amounts
of CO2 from the atmosphere.

## Running vs. Clumping Bamboo

The critical distinction in bamboo selection is
between running (monopodial) and clumping
(sympodial) types:

- **Running bamboos** spread aggressively
  underground via fast-growing rhizomes. They
  are the type that give bamboo its fearsome
  reputation. Many temperate varieties are
  runners, including the popular black bamboo
  (Phyllostachys nigra).

- **Clumping bamboos** expand slowly from a
  central root mass and are much easier to
  contain. They are ideal for gardeners who
  want bamboo's benefits without the spreading
  risk.
