# PF TEK Desiccant Drying Technique

Cool desiccation is the PF TEK's recommended method for drying harvested
mushrooms. Using chemical desiccants to absorb moisture from the surrounding
air, this technique dries mushrooms at room temperature or below without the
heat damage that degrades alkaloid content. [[psilocybe-fanaticus]] considered
desiccant drying superior to all other methods, including air drying, oven
drying, and frost-free refrigerator drying. When combined with young harvesting
and brown rice cultivation, cool desiccant drying produces mushrooms of the
highest possible quality.

## Why Heat-Free Drying Matters

The active compounds in [[psilocybin-compound]] mushrooms, primarily psilocybin and
[[psilocin]], are heat-sensitive. Drying mushrooms in an oven or with hot air
dryers leaches the chemical constituents and reduces their quality
significantly. The PF TEK explicitly warns against ever using heat-based
drying methods. Cool desiccation preserves the full alkaloid profile by
removing moisture through ambient or sub-ambient air moisture absorption rather
than thermal evaporation.

Mushrooms dried with the desiccant technique lose "hardly any chemical
constituents," and their thoroughly desiccated state preserves them in their
prime for months when properly stored.

## Desiccant Options

Three desiccant products are recommended in the PF TEK, each readily available
and effective:

### Drierite

Drierite is described as the "universal lab desiccant," available from chemical
and science supply retailers. This anhydrous calcium sulfate product is widely
used in laboratory settings and is highly effective for mushroom drying. It
often contains an indicating dye that changes color when saturated.

### Silica Gel

Silica gel granules are a standard desiccant available from chemical and
science supply sources. One of the most common desiccants in use, silica gel
works effectively for absorbing moisture in enclosed spaces and can be
regenerated by heating after use.

### Damp Gone

Damp Gone resembles kitty litter and is sold for drying closets and damp areas
in the home. Available at well-stocked hardware stores, PF noted that despite
being the least expensive option, Damp Gone works "as well as any" of the
recommended desiccants.

## Safety Considerations

Desiccant products may carry toxicity warnings regarding dust inhalation and
skin contact. However, when placed in an airtight container beneath a wire
screen with mushrooms on top, the desiccant poses no risk to the fungi or the
user. PF described this configuration as "completely safe for this use."

## Preparing Desiccant for Use

Freshly purchased desiccant is often somewhat damp from ambient moisture
absorption during storage and transport. Before use, the desiccant must be
pre-dried according to the manufacturer's instructions. This typically involves
heating the desiccant in an oven for a specified time at a specified
temperature. Skipping this pre-drying step will significantly reduce the
desiccant's effectiveness because it will already be partially saturated.

After pre-drying, the desiccant should be stored in an airtight container until
needed to prevent it from reabsorbing moisture from the air.

## Drying Setup

The desiccant drying apparatus consists of three components inside a plastic
container:

1. **Desiccant layer**: A 1-inch layer of pre-dried desiccant across the
   bottom of a plastic tub or container.

2. **Wire screen**: A piece of wire screen placed on top of the desiccant
   layer. The screen elevates the mushrooms above the desiccant, allowing air
   circulation on all sides while preventing direct contact.

3. **Mushrooms**: The harvested mushrooms are placed on the wire screen in a
   single layer. Air must be available to all surfaces of each mushroom for
   even drying.

The container with the mushrooms and desiccant is then placed inside a plastic
bag and sealed with a wire tie. A clear plastic bag is preferred so the drying
progress can be observed without opening the setup.

## The Drying Process

After 24 hours in the sealed desiccant setup, visible shriveling occurs.
Complete drying typically takes 4 to 5 days. The mushrooms are fully dried when
the stem snaps cleanly when bent, indicating rock-hard desiccation throughout.

## Pre-Drying Option

If ambient room humidity is not excessive, pre-drying mushrooms in open air on
a wire screen for a couple of days works well. This initial air drying removes
surface moisture and begins the shriveling process. The partially dried
mushrooms can then be transferred to the desiccant box for rapid and complete
drying. This two-stage approach extends the useful life of the desiccant.

## Cold Desiccation for Maximum Potency

For the best alkaloid preservation, the desiccant drying box can be placed
inside a refrigerator. Drying at near-freezing temperatures provides the
gentlest possible moisture removal. Cold desiccation combines the benefits of
cool processing with the thorough moisture extraction of chemical desiccants.
This represents the optimal drying approach in the PF TEK framework.

## Desiccant Regeneration and Storage

Desiccant can be reused indefinitely. After each use, heat it in an oven per
the manufacturer's instructions to drive off absorbed moisture. Once cooled,
return the desiccant to an airtight container for storage. This makes desiccant
drying economical despite the initial purchase cost.

## Post-Drying Storage

Once mushrooms are rock-hard dry, they must be stored in a way that prevents
moisture reabsorption. The PF TEK recommends two storage methods:

- Sealed plastic bags, with all air expelled before sealing.

- Canning jars with the rubber-edged canning lid tightened securely.

The freezer is recommended for long-term preservation. However, mushrooms must
be tightly sealed in their containers to protect against moisture inside the
freezer. Frozen, thoroughly desiccated mushrooms stored in airtight containers
can maintain their potency for many months.
