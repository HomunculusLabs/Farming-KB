# Cactus Cultivation from Seed and Cutting

Practical cultivation techniques for psychoactive and ornamental cacti, based on Gottlieb's 1977 guide.

## Growing from Seed vs Cutting

| Method | Pros | Cons | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed | Strong healthy stock, long-term supply | Very slow, years to usable size | San Pedro: ~0.5 inch year 1, ~1 inch year 2 |
| Cutting | Fast growth, immediate results | May carry disease, less genetic diversity | San Pedro: 4-6 inches + branching in 6 months |

## Soil Mix

### Basic Formula
- 3 parts coarse sand
- 1 part loam
- 1 part leaf mold

Bake the mixture at 400F for 1 hour to kill fungus, bacteria, weed seeds, and insect eggs. Cool before use.

## Planting Cuttings

### Preparation
1. Select firm, unbruised cuttings with at least 0.5 inch of taproot (for peyote)
2. If bottom is delicate/severed, place bottoms-up in partial shade 1-2 days until corky texture forms
3. Dip taproot in rooting mixture (e.g., ROOTONE) to enhance root development and hinder decay

### Planting
1. Place bottom just deep enough so soil does not touch the green part
2. For tall cacti (San Pedro), plant deeply enough for support
3. San Pedro can also be laid on ground -- sends roots from sides while buds grow upward
4. Keep soil slightly moist and even

## Growing from Seed

San Pedro from seed timeline:
- Year 1: ~0.5 inch height, 1/8-1/4 inch diameter
- Year 2: ~1 inch height
- Mature cutting: 2 feet x 4 inches diameter when planted

## Watering Schedule

- Spring/Autumn (active growth): Water 1-2 times per week
- Summer (root development): Moderate watering
- Winter (rest): Less than half of regular schedule
- Soil 1 inch below surface should always contain some moisture

## Planting Timing

- Any time of year works, but late spring gives best results
- Cacti grow mostly spring and autumn, root in summer, rest through winter

## Increasing Alkaloid Content

### Environmental Factors
- Trace minerals are important; occasional watering with Hoagland A-Z trace mineral concentrate (1:10 dilution, every 2 months)
- Rich humus soil with minerals aids mescaline production
- Harvest after dry spell for maximum potency

### Biochemical Precursor Method (Rosenberg, McLaughlin & Paul, 1966)

The biosynthetic pathway: Tyrosine -> Tyramine/Dopa -> Dopamine -> Nor-mescaline -> Mescaline

#### For Peyote
1. Deprive plant of water 1-2 weeks before injection
2. Prepare saturated solution of free base dopamine in 0.05N HCl
3. Inject 1-2 cc into root + 1-2 cc into green portion above root
4. Penetrate to center of plant, inject slowly, hold needle in place a few seconds
5. Harvest 4 weeks after injection

If dopamine unavailable, use tyramine + dopa mixture 6 weeks before harvest.

#### For San Pedro and Other Trichocereus
1. Inject at base of plant
2. Follow spiral pattern up the plant every 3-4 inches
3. Same dopamine solution and timing as peyote
4. Booster injections every 6-8 weeks, plus 4 weeks before harvest

#### For Doñana (Coryphantha macromeris)
- Use tyramine or DL-norepinephrine as precursors
- Inject 20-25 days before harvest
- Booster injections 45 days apart

## San Pedro Specific Notes

- Hardy; tolerates cold climates well
- Grows from sea level to 9000 feet in Andes (western slopes)
- Andean soil rich in humus and minerals aids mescaline production
- Skin and spines should be removed; green tissue near skin has highest mescaline concentration
- The woody core cannot be eaten but can be boiled for tea
- To dry: slice into 1/2 inch disks (stars), dry in sun or oven at 250F
