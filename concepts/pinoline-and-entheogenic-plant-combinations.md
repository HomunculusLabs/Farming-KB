# Pinoline and Entheogenic Plant Combinations

## Overview

Pinoline is an endogenous monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI) produced naturally by the pineal gland in the human brain. It plays a significant role in the study of entheogenic (psychoactive sacred) plants and their historical use in religious and shamanic contexts. The interaction between pinoline and dimethyltryptamine (DMT)—both produced internally—mirrors the pharmacological combinations found in traditional entheogenic brews like ayahuasca, suggesting a deep biochemical connection between human neurology and plant-based spiritual practices.

## The Pineal Gland and Endogenous Psychoactives

The pineal gland, a small endocrine structure in the brain, naturally produces both DMT and pinoline. During sleep, these compounds are excreted, potentially explaining the vivid visionary experiences associated with dreaming. The term *somnambulism* (sleepwalking) shares a linguistic root with *Soma*, the legendary entheogenic substance of Vedic tradition, hinting at an ancient recognition of this pineal chemistry.

Pinoline functions as an MAOI, meaning it inhibits the monoamine oxidase enzymes that would normally break down tryptamine compounds like DMT. Without MAO inhibition, DMT consumed orally is rapidly deactivated by the digestive system before it can reach the brain. This is why ayahuasca—an Amazonian brew containing both DMT-rich plants and MAOI-containing plants—produces powerful visionary effects when consumed orally, while DMT alone would be inactive by the same route.

## The Soma Hypothesis

The identity of Soma, the sacred drink described in the Hindu Vedas, has been debated for over a century. Various scholars have proposed different candidates:

- **Amanita muscaria** (fly agaric mushroom) — Gordon Wasson's influential theory
- **Psilocybin mushrooms** — Terence McKenna's proposal, emphasizing visionary states
- **Syrian Rue** (*[[mckenna-food-gods-peganum-harmala-soma]]*) — proposed by Flattery and Schwartz, containing [[harmala-alkaloids]] (MAOIs)
- **Opium Poppy** (*[[papaver-somniferum]]*) — noted for its linguistic connection (*Soma/Somniferum*) and euphoric properties

Each candidate individually falls short of producing the full range of effects described in Vedic texts. A compelling theory suggests that Soma was not a single plant but an **admixture of multiple plants** working in synergy—similar to how ayahuasca combines DMT-containing plants with MAOI-containing plants.

A medieval depiction shows five plants: four specific plants (interpreted as Amanita muscaria, Syrian Rue, Opium Poppy, and a Psilocybin mushroom) unified by a green vine representing Cannabis and other DMT-containing green plants. This five-plant combination could theoretically produce the comprehensive entheogenic experience described in ancient texts.

## The Biochemistry of Plant Combinations

### DMT-Pinoline Mimicry

Research by Jace Callaway and others has demonstrated that traditional brews like ayahuasca combine:

1. **DMT-containing plants** (e.g., *[[chacruna-psychotria-viridis]]*, *Diplopterys cabrerana*)
2. **MAOI-containing plants** (e.g., *Banisteriopsis caapi*, Syrian Rue / *Peganum harmala*)

This combination is necessary because DMT alone is orally inactive—it is broken down by MAO enzymes in the gut before reaching the bloodstream. The MAOI plants inhibit these enzymes, allowing DMT to cross the blood-brain barrier and produce psychoactive effects.

This plant combination **mimics** the naturally occurring DMT-pinoline system in the pineal gland. The pineal produces both compounds simultaneously, creating an endogenous visionary chemistry that indigenous peoples may have discovered and replicated through plant combinations.

## Individual vs. Combined Effects

A critical insight from entheogenic pharmacology is that **different "keys" open different doorways, and different combinations open different locks**. No single plant candidate for Soma produces the full spectrum of effects described in ancient texts. The synergistic combination of multiple plants with different pharmacological profiles creates an experience greater than the sum of its parts:

- **MAOIs** (Syrian Rue) enable oral activity and prolong effects
- **DMT** provides the core visionary dimension
- **Psilocybin** adds ecstatic, mystical qualities
- **Opiates** provide relaxation, euphoria, and ego dissolution
- **Cannabinoids** modulate the experience, reduce nausea, and potentiate other compounds

## Historical and Cross-Cultural Context

The use of psychoactive plant combinations is found across many cultures worldwide:

- **Ayahuasca** in the Amazon basin combines DMT and MAOI plants
- **Soma/Haoma** in Vedic and Zoroastrian traditions may have been a multi-plant brew
- **Kykeon** in [[plutarch-and-cicero-on-the-eleusinian-mysteries]] of ancient Greece may have contained ergot alkaloids
- Various **indigenous shamanic traditions** use multi-plant preparations

The recurrence of plant combinations across disparate cultures suggests either independent discovery of the same pharmacological principle, or diffusion from a common ancestral practice—potentially rooted in prehistoric shamanism.

## Implications for Understanding Shamanism

The biochemical synergy of entheogenic plant combinations provides a material basis for understanding the shamanic "[[shamanism-death-and-rebirth]]" experience. The MAOI-DMT interaction creates a prolonged, intense visionary state that indigenous cultures interpret as a journey through death to spiritual rebirth. This experience forms the core of shamanic practice worldwide and may represent humanity's oldest spiritual technology—one that works with, rather than against, the brain's natural psychoactive chemistry.
