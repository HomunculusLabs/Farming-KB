# ALEPH (DOT)

2,5-Dimethoxy-4-methylthioamphetamine

## Overview

ALEPH, also known as DOT (para-DOT), is a substituted amphetamine and the founding member of the ALEPH family of psychedelic phenethylamines. It was the first sulfur-containing phenethylamine ever evaluated for central nervous system activity. Named after the first letter of the Hebrew alphabet, ALEPH represents a historically significant compound in the exploration of sulfur-substituted psychedelics, predating the better-known [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]].

## Chemical Information

- Chemical name: 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-methylthioamphetamine
- CAS: Not widely registered
- Molecular formula: C12H19NO2S (free base), C12H20ClNO2S (HCl salt)
- Molecular weight: 225.33 g/mol (free base)
- Class: Substituted amphetamine (DOx family variant with sulfur substitution)
- Synonym: para-DOT, DOT

## Dosage and Duration

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Active dose | 5 - 10 mg |
| Duration | 6 - 8 hours |
| Threshold | ~5 mg |
| Potency | Approximately 5-10x mescaline by weight |

## Effects and Qualitative Commentary

At 5 mg, effects begin with physical warmth spreading from the legs throughout the body. Users report intense intellectual stimulation and non-verbal cognitive states. The experience produces a benign afterglow rich in empathy. Hunger typically returns by the sixth hour.

At 10 mg, effects become significantly more pronounced. Users report a rapid shift in frame of reference that makes ordinary tasks (reading, tuning a radio) feel alien. Visual perception is altered -- eye contact with animals can provoke startled reactions. Social interaction via telephone remains functional, but mechanical tasks (arranging flowers, alphabetizing) become impossible. Driving is absolutely contraindicated.

The 5 mg experience was notable enough to be featured as a case study in Chapter 14 of PIHKAL, illustrating thought processes associated with psychedelic intoxication, ego-inflation, and mild manic states.

## Extensions and Commentary

ALEPH was first trialed at the extreme sub-microgram level (0.25 micrograms) on September 3, 1975, due to uncertainty about the pharmacological role of the sulfur atom. Shulgin took 18 separate trials to work the experimental dosage up to 1 milligram -- a caution that proved excessive in retrospect.

The compound shows notable inter-individual variability in response. While 10 mg produced a full +3 experience for Shulgin, an experienced colleague found the same dose produced only mild, relatively uninteresting effects. This erratic response pattern became characteristic of the entire ALEPH family.

ALEPH remains relatively unexplored compared to its 2-carbon homologues in the [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]], which proved more predictable in dosing and richer in subjective effects.

## Synthesis Summary

Synthesized from 2,5-dimethoxy-4-(methylthio)benzaldehyde via nitropropene condensation with nitroethane and ammonium acetate (steam bath, 6h), yielding orange crystalline 1-(2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylthiophenyl)-2-nitropropene (mp 137-138 C). Reduction with LAH in THF/Et2O followed by standard workup gave the free base (mp 91-93 C). The hydrochloride salt crystallized from IPA/Et2O with mp 204-205 C.

## Pharmacological Class

ALEPH belongs to the sulfur-substituted variant of the [DOx family (2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted amphetamines). The sulfur-containing methylthio group at the 4-position distinguishes it from the more commonly known bromo (DOB), methyl (DOM), and iodo (DOI) substitutions. This 4-position substituent is the primary determinant of both potency and qualitative character in this series, as described in the [[2c-family-chemistry-and-sar-pihkal]] of phenethylamines.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

ALEPH occupies a unique position in the [DOx family as the sulfur-containing member. Compared to its better-known siblings:

- [DOM (STP): The methyl-substituted analog, active at 3-10 mg with a 14-20 hour duration. DOM was the compound responsible for the infamous "STP" incident of 1967 where tablets were overdosed. DOM produces more classical psychedelic effects with a substantially longer duration than ALEPH.
- [DOB: The bromo-substituted analog, active at 1-3 mg with a very long 18-30 hour duration. DOB is significantly more potent and longer-lasting than ALEPH.
- DOI: The iodo-substituted analog, active at 1.5-3 mg, also with extremely long duration. DOI is used as a radioligand in neuroscience research.
- [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]] (2-carbon homologue): The phenethylamine version of ALEPH's substitution pattern, part of the extensively explored 2C-T series.

The sulfur atom at the 4-position gives ALEPH a distinct pharmacological profile. The 2-carbon homologues in the [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]] proved more predictable and explored, suggesting the sulfur substitution may interact differently with the amphetamine backbone.

The [dosage ambiguity inherent in PIHKAL qualitative reports should be noted -- Shulgin's rating system uses +1 through +4 scale, and individual responses vary significantly.

## Historical Context

ALEPH represents a pivotal moment in Shulgin's research program. The introduction of a sulfur heteroatom into the phenethylamine skeleton was a deliberate exploration of chemical space that would later prove extraordinarily fruitful. The entire [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]] (2C-T-2, 2C-T-7, 2C-T-21, etc.) descends from the initial ALEPH exploration.

The extreme initial caution at 0.25 micrograms reflects the genuine uncertainty of exploring a completely novel pharmacological class. While this proved unnecessary, it demonstrates the methodological rigor that characterized Shulgin's approach to self-experimentation, as discussed in the [introduction and methodology of PIHKAL.

## Harm Reduction Notes

- Active at very low doses (5-10 mg). Accurate milligram scales are essential.
- Significant inter-individual variability -- start at the lower end of the dose range.
- Duration of 6-8 hours requires planning for a full day experience.
- Driving and complex mechanical tasks are dangerous at active doses.
- The [legal status of ALEPH varies by jurisdiction; it is unscheduled in many areas but may fall under analogue acts.
