# Five Endogenous Endocannabinoids

## Overview

The human body produces its own cannabinoids, called **endocannabinoids**, which serve as signaling molecules that activate [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2-tissue-distribution-blesching]] (CB1 and CB2). While anandamide (AEA) and 2-arachidonoyl glycerol (2-AG) are the most studied, researchers have identified five distinct endocannabinoid ligands produced endogenously. These molecules act as the body's internal "keys" that fit into the cannabinoid receptor "locks," initiating cascades of physiological and psychological changes.

## The Five Identified Endocannabinoids

### 1. Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamide, AEA)

Anandamide, often called the **"Bliss Molecule,"** was the first endocannabinoid discovered in the 1990s. Its name derives from the Sanskrit word *ananda*, meaning "bliss" or "joy." Key properties include:

- **Receptor affinity**: Binds relatively equally to both CB1 and CB2 receptors
- **Pleasure enhancement**: Initiates changes in the central nervous system that enhance feelings of well-being
- **Anti-cancer activity**: Demonstrated ability to destroy numerous types of cancer cells
- **Antipsychotic potential**: May help mitigate episodes of acute schizophrenia
- **Antitussive effects**: Soothes coughs through CB1-mediated pathways
- **Rapid degradation**: Broken down by the enzyme fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH), which limits its duration of action

Anandamide is present in the CNS (primarily via CB1), the immune system (primarily via CB2), and the [[endocannabinoid-autonomic-nervous-system-balance-blesching]] system, producing simultaneous effects across all three systems.

### 2. 2-Arachidonoyl Glycerol (2-AG)

2-AG is the most abundant endocannabinoid in the body and plays critical roles in:

- **Immune modulation**: Potent modulator of immune function via CB2 receptor activation
- **Neuroprotection**: Protects neurons from damage and supports brain homeostasis
- **Inflammation regulation**: Helps calibrate inflammatory responses
- **Full agonist activity**: Unlike anandamide (a partial agonist), 2-AG acts as a full agonist at both CB1 and CB2 receptors
- **Cardiovascular function**: Involved in blood pressure regulation and cardiovascular tone

2-AG is degraded primarily by monoacylglycerol lipase (MAGL), distinct from the FAAH enzyme that breaks down anandamide. This dual-enzyme degradation system provides separate regulatory pathways for the two primary endocannabinoids.

### 3. 2-Arachidonoyl-Glyceryl-Ether (Noladin Ether)

Noladin ether is a lesser-known endocannabinoid with the following characteristics:

- **CB1 receptor preference**: Shows selectivity for CB1 receptors over CB2
- **Neuroprotective properties**: Contributes to the [[blesching-thc-heart-protective-mechanisms-myocardial-protection]] of the ECS in neural tissue
- **Pain modulation**: Involved in the body's endogenous pain control pathways
- **Discovery**: Isolated and characterized after anandamide and 2-AG
- **Structural similarity**: Related to 2-AG but contains an ether linkage instead of an ester bond, which affects its metabolic stability

Research on noladin ether remains less extensive than for AEA and 2-AG, but its discovery expanded understanding of the diversity of endogenous cannabinoid signaling.

### 4. O-Arachidonoyl-Ethanolamine (Virodhamine)

Virodhamine is unique among endocannabinoids due to several distinctive properties:

- **Partial CB1 antagonist**: Unlike other endocannabinoids that activate CB1, virodhamine can act as a partial antagonist (blocker) at CB1 receptors
- **CB2 agonist**: Functions as an agonist at CB2 receptors
- **Dual regulatory role**: This opposing action at CB1 vs CB2 creates a unique modulatory function within the ECS
- **Name origin**: "Virodha" is a Sanskrit term meaning "opposition" or "hindrance," reflecting its antagonistic properties at CB1
- **Vasorelaxation**: Involved in regulating blood vessel tone through cannabinoid receptor-mediated pathways

The antagonistic property of virodhamine at CB1 receptors suggests the body has built-in mechanisms to modulate and fine-tune endocannabinoid signaling, preventing overactivation of the ECS.

### 5. N-Arachidonoyl-Dopamine (NADA)

NADA bridges the endocannabinoid system with the dopaminergic system:

- **Dual system activity**: Interacts with both cannabinoid receptors and the vanilloid receptor (TRPV1, also known as the capsaicin receptor)
- **CB1 affinity**: Activates CB1 receptors, linking cannabinoid signaling with dopamine-related pathways
- **Pain perception**: Modulates pain through both cannabinoid and vanilloid receptor systems
- **Neuroinflammation**: Involved in regulating inflammatory responses in neural tissue
- **Reward pathway connection**: By combining arachidonic acid (a cannabinoid precursor) with dopamine, NADA represents a biochemical bridge between the ECS and the brain's reward/motivation circuitry

NADA is particularly significant because it demonstrates the interconnected nature of the endocannabinoid system with other neurotransmitter systems, particularly those governing motivation, pleasure, and pain perception.

## Biosynthesis and Degradation

All five endocannabinoids are synthesized **on demand** rather than stored in vesicles like classical neurotransmitters. This "on-demand" synthesis means:

- They are produced rapidly in response to physiological needs
- Their production is triggered by intracellular calcium elevation
- They act locally near their site of synthesis (paracrine/autocrine signaling)
- They are quickly degraded by specific enzymes, ensuring transient signaling

The primary degradation enzymes are:
- **FAAH** (Fatty Acid Amide Hydrolase): Degrades anandamide and NADA
- **MAGL** (Monoacylglycerol Lipase): Degrades 2-AG
- **COX-2** (Cyclooxygenase-2): Can metabolize multiple endocannabinoids into prostaglandin-like compounds

## Clinical Significance

The diversity of endogenous ligands has important implications:

1. **Multiple therapeutic targets**: Each ligand offers a distinct pharmacological profile that can be leveraged for different conditions
2. **System complexity**: The five ligands create a highly nuanced regulatory system that can fine-tune responses across the nervous, immune, and endocrine systems
3. **Drug development**: Understanding each ligand's specific properties guides the development of targeted cannabinoid-based pharmaceuticals
4. **Individual variation**: Genetic differences in enzymes that produce or degrade these ligands may explain individual variation in ECS function

## Relationship to Phytocannabinoids

Plant-derived cannabinoids (THC, CBD) and [[blesching-cannabis-caryophyllene-dietary-cannabinoids]] like (E)-β-caryophyllene interact with the same receptor system as these endogenous ligands. THC mimics anandamide's activity at CB1 and CB2, while CBD inhibits FAAH to prolong anandamide's effects. This pharmacological overlap explains why plant-based cannabinoids can produce such diverse [[emcdda-psilocybin-cardiovascular-risks-acute-physiological-effects]] — they are tapping into an evolutionarily ancient and widespread regulatory system.

## Comparative Summary

| Endocannabinoid | Primary Receptor | Key Function | Degradation Enzyme |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anandamide (AEA) | CB1 ≈ CB2 | Bliss, pleasure, anti-cancer | FAAH |
| 2-AG | CB1 = CB2 (full agonist) | Immune modulation, neuroprotection | MAGL |
| Noladin Ether | CB1 > CB2 | Neuroprotection, pain | Unknown |
| Virodhamine | CB2 agonist, CB1 antagonist | Vasorelaxation, CB1 modulation | FAAH |
| NADA | CB1, TRPV1 | Pain, reward, neuroinflammation | FAAH |

## Research Frontiers

Active areas of investigation include:

- **Enzyme inhibitors**: Developing FAAH and MAGL inhibitors to boost endogenous cannabinoid levels without direct receptor activation
- **Entourage effect**: How the five ligands interact synergistically when present simultaneously
- **Tissue-specific profiles**: Mapping which endocannabinoids predominate in different organs and disease states
- **Genetic polymorphisms**: How individual genetic variation in biosynthetic and degradative enzymes affects health outcomes
