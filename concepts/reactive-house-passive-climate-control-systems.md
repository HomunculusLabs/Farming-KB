## Reactive House Passive Climate Control Systems

The reactive house eliminates or reduces the need for external energy input for climate control through passive [[aact-brewer-design-principles-aeration-systems-ingham]]. Mollison emphasizes that the whole thrust is to use structural control of heat and cold, storing solar heat in thermal masses and excluding draughts so that body warmth, cooking, and perhaps a small stove provide all needed heating.

## Essentials of the Reactive House

1. **Sheltered from cold winds**: Needs designed windbreak planting
2. **Oriented east-west, facing the sun**: Attached glasshouse is feasible
3. **No (or very small fixed) windows in E and W walls**: Available for external vine crop insulation, trellis, or shrubbery
4. **Few windows and doors in the S wall**: Shadehouse attachment facilitated
5. **Very well sealed for draughts**: Only essential vents in toilet and bathroom open at all times
6. **N aspect shaded by deciduous trees or vine crop** (hot summer areas): Omitted in cooler areas
7. **Draught-proofing and reduced ventilation**: Block all old ventilators
8. **Insulated ceilings**: Vines or trellis along E and W walls
9. **[[coleman-soil-heat-mass-passive-thermal-storage]]**: Concrete slabs, tanks, brick or stonework within glasshouse or insulated warm rooms

## Attached Glasshouse Design

Based on Fisher and Yanda and McCullagh:

### Essential Features
- Oriented to within 60° of due N (towards open mid sky)
- E and W walls insulated, solid construction
- Base insulated, especially around foundations
- Wooden frames (metal frames lose heat too quickly)
- Single glass panels: most durable and efficient glazing
- Pit dug below grade as base reduces heat loss to outside ground
- Very well-sealed top vent
- Water in small containers: best heat store; pools help, placed below benches
- Glass at about 45° to ground for greatest efficiency
- Plain white paint on S walls reflects heat efficiently

### Seasonal Operation

**Summer**: Open V.1 at top of glasshouse. Air escapes, drawing cool air from V.4 over damp mulch and through vine-covered, ferny shadehouse. Fine spray or drip of water on mulch keeps air cool.

**Winter**: Close V.4 and V.1. Open V.2 and V.3. Warm air from glasshouse circulates in insulated rooms by day. Close at evening, trapping warm air.

Both shadehouse (small fruit, brassicas) and glasshouse (spices, tropicals) yield food while cutting fuel costs.

## The Shadehouse

"Even the dripping of water helps, as does the sight of ferns in a droughted landscape." A school designed with Sweetnam and Godfrey showed that a shade area allows cool air to be drawn into all courtyard buildings and gives refuge in extreme summer heat.

### Water Tanks in Shadehouse
Water tanks, vine-covered in the shadehouse, serve as cool air/water blocks. (A Perth farmer kept his tank against snooty residents' petition—they got water restrictions and salted soil; he kept his tank.)

## Sun Trap Design

Structures breasting the wind should be like a vessel breasting the sea:
- "Easy" in entry like a sharp-bowed boat
- Permeable like a raft
- Or both

A 'U'-shaped suntrap, curved and permeable back to cold winds, facing N. The 'U' shape should be adjusted:
- If strong easterlies: higher and longer to E than W
- If sunny mornings and early sunsets behind western hill: swung towards NE
- On hostile shoreline or cold sites: whole series of interlinked sun traps

## The Earth House

Designed for bleak, cold, windswept, hostile areas. Mollison evolved the idea after roadmenders raised a 1.8 m [[earth-bank-windbreak-mollison]] behind a frigid cabin, dramatically changing the climate by insulating from S winds and trapping N sun heat.

### Features
- All insulation factors of vegetation and earth
- Moated water supply
- Indoor wells for waste disposal and water supply
- Frost-clear roof as indoor glasshouse
- Cost: less than $1,000 plus floor slab and roof trusses

### Desert Dwellings
"Desert dwellings need be of similar 'underground' construction." The Pioneer Australian dairy is a very cool, below-ground storage and fire refuge.

## Grown Structures

### Doernach Biostructure (Stuttgart)
- Conventional light steel and [[comparison-bamboo-construction-vs-timber-frame-basics]]
- Grown over with evergreen, waxy-leaved climbing plants (ivy, geranium, coastal climbers)
- Warm, cosy, weatherproof even in cold European winter
- Igloo-like form; only doors and windows kept clear of vine
- If designed for creepers, trimming is unnecessary

### Coralline Deposits
- Electrolyte (sea or fresh water) deposits chemicals in free-form metal mesh of any shape
- Result: coralline-cave appearance

### Totally Grown Structures
Plants used as integral parts of house structure. Only very light structural members necessary. A fully enclosed and vented [[compost]] box provides background heat—materials dry-stored in autumn and "charged" at 3-week intervals "burn" at about 150°C until composted.

## Biological Insulation

### Ivy on Brick Walls
Stephen Lesiuk measured: ivy suppressed ~70% of summer heat excess and prevented ~30% of heat escape at night. "Dozens of brick-cavity buildings could benefit from this simple biological insulation."

### Wind and Solar Heaters
Holgar Wishart notes wind passing over and around solar heaters is the main cause of their inefficiency. They should be encased inside the roof, lying on roof insulation and protected by a glass skylight ~50 mm above.

## The Sod Roof

### Construction
- Newly constructed or rolled over strong existing structures
- Plastic film stapled below as moisture barrier
- Metal roll-under carries water to spout; leaves drop off
- Slotted angle or log holds sod from slipping (indispensable on steep roofs)

### Benefits
- Great active insulator
- Never needs painting
- Easily repaired (add soil and seed)
- Should last 200+ years (Norwegian models), longer than the house
- Evapo-transpiration keeps summer heat out
- Air and foliage keep winter cold at bay
- Neither sod roofs nor ivy increase fire risk

### Planting
- Succulents in dry areas
- Daisies, bulbs, herbs and flowers elsewhere
- Sod root mass effectively insulates

### Alternative for Weak Roofs
For zinc or aluminium cladding, ivy over the roof serves well (guttering must be adapted).

## Sound Walls

### Noise Control
- Insulation helps with high-frequency noise
- Low-frequency sound waves behave like water; can "flow over" barriers
- Both can be reflected by dished or baffled systems, or absorbed in insulating material
- Earthworks, vegetation, or insulation all help
- Well-placed embankments of earthed-over tyres plus good house insulation answer intolerable noise levels

"It takes 100 m of forest to cut out 6–7 decibels of sound."

## Fire Mandalas

A fireproof array of plants: all "sappy" perennials that will not burn unless permeated by grasses. All green in midsummer. Sunflowers included as annual. If fenced and poultry stocked after midsummer, scratching and browsing greatly decrease fire risk from grasses.

## Windows and Aquariums

### Best Skylight
A clear fibre-glass pool or shallow aquarium: water insulates and gathers light from the horizon. May need 4% formalin to stay clear.

### Wall Aquariums
No nuisance deposits; stocked with plants and fish; shaded to prevent algal growth. Prevent heat loss, produce food, recycle nutrients.
