## Overview

Edaphic (soil-related) factors are the dominant environmental variables
determining the structure and composition of microbial communities
associated with [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]],
outweighing both plant cultivar and sample type
(endorhiza, rhizosphere, bulk soil) in shaping the root and soil microbiome.

## Key Edaphic Variables

### Nitrogen

Total nitrogen content showed the strongest correlation
with microbial community beta-diversity among all edaphic factors tested.
In weighted UniFrac analysis, nitrogen had r-statistic = 0.465 (p = 0.001);
in unweighted analysis, r-statistic = 0.630 (p = 0.001).
Nitrogen availability directly influences microbial growth rates
and competitive dynamics between bacterial taxa.

### Salinity

Soil salinity was the second most influential factor
(weighted r = 0.437, unweighted r = 0.620, both p = 0.001).
Salinity selects for salt-tolerant species and suppresses sensitive populations.
Study soils ranged from 1.73 to 7.44 salinity.

### Total Organic Carbon

Total organic carbon showed weighted r = 0.330, unweighted r = 0.512 (both p = 0.001).
Carbon availability determines the energy base for heterotrophic microorganisms.
Study soils ranged from 3.02% to 20.0% total organic carbon.

### Water Content

Soil water content showed significant but weaker correlations
(weighted r-statistic = 0.281, p = 0.001;
unweighted r-statistic = 0.466, p = 0.001).
Water availability affects oxygen diffusion,
[[ingham-anaerobic-organisms-role-soil]],
while excessively dry conditions limit all microbial activity.
The study soils ranged from 10.1% to 37.1% water content.

### pH

Soil pH had the weakest but still significant correlation
with community structure
(weighted r-statistic = 0.221, p = 0.001;
unweighted r-statistic = 0.292, p = 0.001).
This is notable because pH is often cited
as the dominant edaphic factor in other plant-microbiome systems.
In the cannabis system, pH effects appear to be subordinate
to nitrogen and salinity, though still relevant.
Study soil pH values ranged from 6.63 to 6.94,
all within the slightly acidic range preferred by many soil bacteria.
