    20|# Tryptamine Transtemporal Consciousness and the Nunc Stans
    21|
    22|Terence McKenna proposed that the [[tryptamine]] hallucinogenic state possesses a
    23|fundamentally **transtemporal** quality, an experience of timelessness that transcends
    24|the ordinary flow of chronological duration. This concept, articulated in his 1983 Esalen
    25|lecture "Tryptamine Hallucinogens and Consciousness," draws on Platonic philosophy,
    26|Thomistic theology, and personal [[dmt]] experience to argue that tryptamine states reveal
    27|a mode of consciousness that stands outside of time entirely. The framework connects the
    28|subjective phenomenology of the [[DMT experience phenomenology and cognitive dissonance-29|of wonder]] to a sweeping vision of human eschatology and the ultimate trajectory of
    30|consciousness in the cosmos.
    31|
    32|## Plato's Metaphor of Time as Moving Image
    33|
    34|McKenna explicitly invoked Plato's definition of time from the Timaeus: that time is
    35|"the moving image of eternity." In this framework, the ordinary experience of temporal
    36|flow, of past, present, and future as distinct and sequential, is merely a projected
    37|image of a deeper, eternal reality. The tryptamine experience, according to McKenna,
    38|represents a **stepping out of the moving image and into eternity itself**. The
    39|[[psychedelic-plant-sources]] state does not distort time in the way that boredom or terror might make
    40|time seem to crawl or race. Rather, it reveals that the apparent motion of time is
    41|itself a kind of illusion or secondary phenomenon, and that beneath it lies a "standing
    42|now" that is the true ground of being. This is not a metaphor or a poetic description
    43|but a literal phenomenological report of what the tryptamine state feels like from the
    44|inside.
    45|
    46|The significance of this Platonic reference extends beyond mere analogy. Plato's
    47|distinction between the realm of eternal Forms and the realm of temporal becoming
    48|provides exactly the philosophical vocabulary needed to describe the structure of the
    49|DMT experience. The hallucinogenic state reveals a reality that has the character of
    50|eternity rather than succession, of being rather than becoming. This is why the content
    51|of the experience feels not like a fantasy or a hallucination but like a revelation of
    52|what is genuinely and permanently real, behind the veil of temporal appearance.
    53|
    54|## The Nunc Stans of Thomas Aquinas
    55|
    56|McKenna identified this timeless ground with the **nunc stans**, the "standing now" of
    57|Thomas Aquinas. In Thomistic theology, the nunc stans refers to the eternal present of
    58|God, a mode of existence that is not stretched out in time as human experience is, but
    59|is rather an eternal, unchanging "now" that contains all moments simultaneously. The
    60|distinction between nunc stans (eternal now) and nunc fluens (flowing now) is central
    61|to medieval philosophy of time, and McKenna argued that the tryptamine hallucinogenic
    62|state provides experiential access to something functionally identical to the nunc stans.
    63|
    64|Under the influence of [[dmt]] or [[psilocybin]], the subject encounters a state in
    65|which all of human history is seen as leading toward a culminating moment, not as a
    66|distant prospect but as something immanently present. The distinction between
    67|anticipation and memory collapses. The future is experienced not as something that has
    68|not yet happened but as something that is happening now, in the same way that the past
    69|is experienced as still present. This is precisely the structure of the nunc stans: a
    70|present that does not pass away.
    71|
    72|## Acceleration of Novelty and the Eschatological Attractor
    73|
    74|A key feature of McKenna's transtemporal framework is the perception of **acceleration**
    75|visible in all processes surrounding human civilization. He noted a telescoping pattern
    76|of innovation: fire was discovered several million years ago; language came perhaps
    77|thirty-five thousand years ago; measurement emerged five thousand years ago; Galileo
    78|lived four hundred years ago; then Watson and Crick and the discovery of DNA. "What
    79|is obviously happening," he wrote, "is that everything is being drawn together." This
    80|accelerating compression of innovation is, in McKenna's view, a manifestation of the
    81|[[eschatology and acceleration of history]], an attractor that exists at the end of
    82|time, casting an enormous shadow over all of human history.
    83|
    84|This acceleration is not merely technological. McKenna saw it as evidence that the
    85|tryptamine dimension is somehow "feeding information to humanity about the larger
    86|picture." The [[tryptamine hypercontinuum]] is not a remote or inaccessible realm but is
    87|intimately connected to the trajectory of human cultural and cognitive evolution. The
    88|[[psychedelic-plant-sources]] experience reveals that history itself has a teleological character,
    89|drawing all becoming toward a single point of convergence. The tryptamine state
    90|provides direct experiential confirmation of this eschatological structure.
    91|
    92|## History as the Shockwave of Eschatology
    93|
    94|McKenna described history as "the shockwave of eschatology." Something exists at the
    95|end of time, and it is drawing all human becoming toward it. The wars, philosophies,
    96|migrations, cities, and civilizations all occupy what he called "a microsecond of
    97|geological, planetary, and galactic time." This framing inverts the conventional
    98|materialist understanding of time as a neutral container within which events occur
    99|randomly or mechanically. Instead, time is directional, purposeful, and convergent. The
   100|metaphor of the shockwave implies that the eschatological event has already occurred in
   101|some sense, and that what we experience as history is merely the outward propagation of
   102|its effects through human consciousness and culture.
   103|
   104|Within the hallucinogenic trance, one does not merely think about the nature of time
   105|but experiences a mode of consciousness in which temporal categories no longer apply.
   106|The subject encounters a reality in which the distinction between past, present, and
   107|future collapses, revealing what McKenna called "the eternity of the standing now."
   108|This is the phenomenological foundation upon which his entire eschatological theory
   109|rests: the tryptamine state provides direct, experiential access to the transtemporal
   110|ground of being.
   111|
   112|## The Primacy of the Perceiving Self
   113|
   114|McKenna argued that the most richly organized material in the universe is the human
   115|cerebral cortex, and that the densest and richest experience in the universe is the
   116|experience occurring in the present moment. This stands in stark contrast to the
   117|prevailing scientific worldview, which has spent five hundred years decentralizing
   118|humanity, moving us from the center of the universe to the edge of the galaxy. McKenna
   119|insisted that "everything should be constellated outward from the perceiving self" and
   120|that this perceiving self is "the primary datum."
   121|
   122|Under the influence of tryptamine hallucinogens, this primacy of consciousness
   123|becomes experientially undeniable. The hallucinogenic state reveals dimensions of being
   124|that are "transhuman, hyperdimensional, and extremely alien," yet are accessible
   125|through the human nervous system. The very fact that these experiences are possible
   126|suggests that consciousness is not an epiphenomenon of matter but a fundamental feature
   127|of reality, capable of collapsing "the state vector" and causing what Alfred North
   128|Whitehead called "the formality of actually occurring."
   129|
   130|## Consciousness and the Collapse of the State Vector
   131|
   132|McKenna drew a connection between psychedelic experience and quantum mechanics, arguing
   133|that "consciousness is somehow able to collapse the state vector and thereby cause the
   134|stuff of being to undergo what Alfred North Whitehead called the formality of actually
   135|occurring." This claim bridges the phenomenological observation of timelessness in the
   136|tryptamine state with the theoretical physics of quantum measurement. If consciousness
   137|can collapse the quantum state vector, then consciousness is not merely a passive
   138|observer of reality but an active participant in its creation.
   139|
   140|The tryptamine state, by revealing the transtemporal ground of consciousness,
   141|simultaneously reveals the role of consciousness in the constitution of reality itself.
   142|This is why the [[tryptamines and quantum mechanics]] connection is so significant for
   143|McKenna: it provides a framework in which the subjective experience of the DMT trance
   144|and the objective descriptions of quantum physics can be understood as two aspects of a
   145|single phenomenon. The transtemporal consciousness revealed by tryptamines is the same
   146|consciousness that, according to quantum theory, participates in the actualization of
   147|physical reality.
