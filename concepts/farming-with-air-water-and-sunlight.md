# Farming with Air, Water, and Sunlight

"Farming with Air, Water and Sunlight" is a foundational philosophy in [[korean-natural-farming]] (KNF),
biodynamic agriculture, and various indigenous farming systems throughout Southeast Asia.

## Core Principle: The 96/4 Paradigm

The central claim holds that carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen — obtained from atmospheric CO₂
and water — constitute the vast bulk of plant biomass. Through photosynthesis, plants
convert these freely available elements into carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins. Soil
minerals contribute trace amounts and catalytic ions, but the overwhelming volume of growth
material comes from air and water powered by sunlight. Conventional agriculture, by fixating
on soluble NPK inputs, addresses only a small fraction of what actually drives plant growth,
often at the expense of overall soil health and biological activity.

## Air: Soil Aeration and Root Zone Architecture

In [[natural-farming]] systems, air access to the root zone is considered as critical as water
or nutrients. The source material recommends [[plant-circadian-rhythms]] are entrained to lunar cycles.

## Application to Livestock: The Chicken Model

The philosophy extends comprehensively to animal husbandry. Natural chicken housing
incorporates several key design principles from the air-water-sunlight framework:

- **Orientation**: East-west alignment allows morning and evening sun penetration while
  providing midday shade.
- **Transparent roofing**: Permits ultraviolet light into the housing, supporting natural
  vitamin D synthesis and providing antimicrobial benefits.
- **Ventilation**: Mandatory exhaust ventilation removes ammonia, moisture, and pathogens.
  Natural ventilation relies on thermal buoyancy — warm air rising creates convection
  currents that draw fresh air through lower openings.
- **Deep litter substrate**: A layer approximately one yard deep of sawdust, wood shavings,
  and compost provides a microbial-rich environment that decomposes manure in situ,
  generates heat, and reduces odor.
- **Clean water**: Continuous access supports digestion, thermoregulation, and overall health.

### Contrast with Conventional Production

Conventional poultry production features high stocking densities, artificial lighting,
limited ventilation, and no direct sunlight. These conditions produce chronic stress, foul
odors from accumulated ammonia, and suppressed immune function — creating dependency on
antibiotics and chemical interventions. The natural farming approach argues that providing
proper access to air, water, and sunlight eliminates most disease pressure before it arises.

## Circadian Rhythms in Plants and Animals

Both plants and animals possess endogenous circadian clocks synchronizing physiological
processes with the 24-hour light-dark cycle. In plants, these rhythms regulate stomatal
opening, [[photosynthetic-efficiency]], hormone signaling, and flowering time. In animals, they
govern feeding behavior, body temperature, immune function, and reproductive cycles.
Natural farming's emphasis on outdoor access and natural light cycles allows these rhythms
to function without disruption. Conventional confinement systems, with artificial lighting
and controlled environments, frequently desynchronize these clocks, contributing to stress
and reduced productivity.

## The Philosophy of Completeness: Nature as Teacher

A recurring theme in natural farming traditions is that "Nature is complete." Ecosystems
possess inherent self-regulating mechanisms — pest-predator balance, nutrient cycling,
disease suppression — that function optimally when undisturbed. The farmer's role is not to
impose solutions but to observe, understand, and support these natural processes. The source
material encourages practitioners to "speak" with soil, plants, and animals — emphasizing
attentive observation and responsive management over prescriptive chemical applications.

## Biodynamic Agriculture and Cosmic Influences

Biodynamic agriculture, formulated by [[rudolf-steiner]] in 1924, extends the air-water-sunlight
philosophy to include broader cosmic influences. In addition to lunar cycles, biodynamic
practice considers planetary alignments and their purported effects on plant growth and soil
biology. Preparations from cow manure, silica, and herbs are applied to soil and compost in
specific seasonal patterns. While controversial within mainstream science, these practices
share natural farming's core conviction that farming should work with natural systems
rather than against them.

## Farming With Nature Versus Fighting Nature

The fundamental distinction between natural and conventional agriculture is one of
orientation. Conventional agriculture treats nature as an adversary — weeds eradicated with
herbicides, insects killed with pesticides, soil fertility maintained through synthetic
chemicals. Natural farming treats nature as a partner — weeds managed through competition
and mulching, pests controlled through biodiversity and biological balance, soil fertility
built through biological processes and organic matter cycling. The air-water-sunlight
philosophy encapsulates this partnership: by ensuring plants and animals have adequate access
