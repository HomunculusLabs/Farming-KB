# Cultivated Mushroom Difficulty Ranking and Agaricus Genus

## Follow-Up Professional Testing of Mycoremediation Results

After initiating mycoremediation trials—whether through lab work, cell counting, bean tests, or worm assays—results should be validated by a professional laboratory. The critical question is whether the contaminant underwent **complete degradation/inactivation** or merely **biotransformation** (conversion into different compounds). Biotransformation can produce metabolites more toxic than the original contaminant. All collected data informs recalibration of subsequent trials, enabling fine-tuning before committing to full-scale field installations where stakes are higher.

## Part IV: Meet the Cultivated Mushrooms — Introduction

There is no definitive list of all cultivated or edible mushrooms, as growing conditions, spawn/spore/tissue availability, and regional preferences vary worldwide. The profiles cover commonly cultivated species across several climates and regions, organized alphabetically by genus rather than by difficulty. Morels (*Morchella* spp.) are excluded (dedicated chapter in Part III). If a desired species is absent, growers can adapt the protocol for a related genus or one with similar climatic and habitat preferences. All cultivated mushrooms originated in the wild, refined through years of trial and error.

## Difficulty Ranking System

Each mushroom species presents unique challenges, yields, and cultivation preferences. The following 1–5 scale ranks difficulty based on the likelihood of successful fruiting:

| Rank | Level | Description |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **1** | Beginner | Easy to fruit |
| **2** | Intermediate | Likely to fruit |
| **3** | Advanced | Difficult to fruit |
| **4** | Expert | Requires precise methodology |
| **5** | Experimental | Unlikely to fruit |

Rankings are approximate—influenced by location, substrate availability, indoor vs. outdoor cultivation, and grower experience.

## The Genus *Agaricus*

### Common Species

- *A. augustus* — The Prince
- *A. bisporus* — White button, portabella, crimini (same species, harvested at different stages)
- *A. bitorquis*, *A. campestris* — Forest/field mushroom, pink bottom, rose bottom
- *A. blazei*, *A. brasiliensis*, *A. subrufescens* — Almond-scented agaric, almond portabella, royal sun agaric

**Difficulty:** Indoor 3 / Outdoor 2

### General Description and Ecology

*Agaricus* species are found worldwide, growing naturally on composted livestock manure, grassy fields, wood chip piles, and forest leaf litter. Species are typically specific to their food sources with little adaptability to alternative substrates. White button, portabella, and crimini are all *A. bisporus* at different harvest stages. The almond portabella has a distinctive almond/anise aroma and is highly prized but rarely cultivated at scale due to spawn scarcity and higher fruiting temperatures—making it ideal for small-scale outdoor cultivation in temperate to tropical climates.

All *Agaricus* mushrooms are **secondary decomposers**, inefficient at lignin degradation. Growing a primary decomposer (e.g., oyster mushrooms) first on a substrate, then using the spent media for *Agaricus*, is beneficial. Crucially, *Agaricus* species **require microbial interaction** in their environment to initiate [[pf-tek-primordia-formation-and-pinning-process]] fruitbody development, necessitating a microbe-enriched casing soil after substrate colonization.

### Mycelium and Spawn Characteristics

- **Appearance:** White, linear mycelium with irregular margins; cottony in pure culture, rhizomorphic when associated with microbial partners
- **Metabolite:** Light yellow exudate on overcolonized spawn/substrate
- **Growth rate:** Much slower than *Pleurotus* or *Volvariella*
- **Fragrance:** Species-specific, ranging from musty to sweet almond extract
- **Spawn types:** Cereal grain (commercial standard) or [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]] (preferred for home cultivators where full pasteurization isn't possible, as grain spawn is vulnerable to insects)

### Fruitbody Development

Cap is centered on the stem with **free gills** (not attached). Gills are pinkish-white initially, darkening at maturity as spores develop. Cap surface is white with fine fibrillose matting. Some species stain bright yellow when cut or bruised. **Spore print: chocolate black.**

### Fruiting Conditions by Species

| Species | Spawn Run Temp | Fruiting Temp | Notes |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------|
| *A. augustus* (The Prince) | 70–80°F (21–27°C) | 75–85°F (24–29°C) | |
| *A. bisporus* (Button/Portabella) | 70–80°F (21–27°C) | 60–65°F (16–18°C) | Industry standard |
| *A. bitorquis* | 77–91°F (25–33°C) | 75–91°F (24–33°C) | Most heat-tolerant; ideal for tropics |
| *A. campestris* | 70–80°F (21–27°C) | 60–65°F (16–18°C) | |
| *A. blazei* complex (Almond portabella) | 70–80°F (21–27°C) | 75–85°F (24–29°C) | Forms deep; perforated plastic barrier in casing increases yields |

Indoor cultivation follows a three-phase temperature regime: higher spawn run → lower middle phase for [[chen-maitake-primordia-initiation-fruiting]] → slightly higher for fruiting and maturation. Outdoor beds typically produce two flushes a few weeks apart, with timing dependent on local temperature fluctuations.

### Wild Spawn Expansion Techniques

*Agaricus* is difficult to perpetuate via natural expansion due to its reliance on soil microbes. Methods include:

- Transplanting rhizomorphic stem bases or plugs of colonized compost to start new beds (multiple transfers cause strain degeneration)
- Creating a slurry from stem bases mixed into fresh compost, carrying key microbial partners
- Storing stem bases under refrigeration for later use in [[casing-soils-and-microbial-slurries]] to seed casing soil

### Laboratory Isolation and Spawn Culture

- **Agar medium:** Potato dextrose yeast agar (2–3 g yeast per liter of boiled potato water + agar)
- **pH:** Near neutral (~7.0), unlike most other mushrooms (5.8–6.2)
- **Blended transfer:** Because growth on agar is slow, petri cultures can be blended with sterilized water and added directly to sterilized grain
- **Grain enhancement:** Add calcium (limestone or gypsum powder) to grain spawn
- **Storage of tropical strains:** *A. blazei*, *A. brasiliensis*, and *A. subrufescens* are extremely sensitive to prolonged refrigeration—store at room temperature or 50–55°F (10–13°C). Dehydrating grain spawn also works well for long-term storage

### Preferred Fruiting Substrates

Composted mixtures of manure and cereal straws, with agricultural by-products such as cotton hulls, bean hulls, cornstalks, cotton waste, beet pulp, and sugarcane bagasse. Almond portabellas prefer a **complex media matrix** over a basic substrate, so a diverse supplement regimen enhances fruiting and development.

### Casing Soil and Outdoor Cultivation

A **microbial casing soil is required** to stimulate primordia formation. Apply when the substrate surface shows continuous white mycelial mat. For outdoor beds: fill raised beds 8–10 inches deep in a shady location, cover with chicken wire or plywood to deter animals, use supplemented sawdust spawn (minimizes insect risk), and dress with microbial casing soil at or near full colonization.
