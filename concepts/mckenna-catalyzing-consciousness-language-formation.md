# Catalyzing Consciousness and Language Formation

The emergence of language and higher consciousness in the human species
represents one of the deepest mysteries in evolutionary biology. The
theory that psychoactive plants, particularly psilocybin-containing
mushrooms, catalyzed these uniquely human capacities challenges
conventional assumptions about the origins of cognition and culture.

## Hallucinogens as Catalysts of Consciousness

Psychedelic plants do not cause consciousness, which is a generalized
function present in some degree in all life forms. Rather, they catalyze
consciousness, that peculiar, self-reflecting ability that has reached
its greatest apparent expression in human beings. Catalysis is a speeding
up of processes that are already present.

Consciousness confers an immense adaptive advantage on any individual who
possesses it. In the search for a causal agent capable of synergizing
cognitive activity and thereby playing a role in the emergence of the
hominid, researchers might long ago have looked to plant hallucinogens
were it not for our strong, almost compulsive avoidance of the idea that
our exalted position in the hierarchy of nature might be somehow due to
the power of plants.

## The Stoned Ape Hypothesis

The first encounters between hominids and psilocybin-containing mushrooms
may have occurred on the African grasslands. The strategy of early hominid
omnivores was to eat everything that seemed foodlike and to test
everything for its food potential. The eminent ethnobotanist Richard
[[ceremonial-shamanic-use-psychoactive-mushrooms]] in
virtually every area of the world where cattle graze.

Hominids and hallucinogenic plants were in close association for a long
span of evolutionary time. The presence of psilocybin in the hominid diet
changed the parameters of the process of natural selection acting on the
brain. These alkaloids acted as mutagens, potentially affecting the rapid
evolution in brain size and complexity that occurred in hominid
development.

## Three Levels of Psychedelic Influence

The psilocybin-containing mushrooms that early hominids encountered would
have had three distinct levels of effect depending on dosage:

At low doses, the mushrooms act as mild stimulants, increasing visual
acuity, particularly edge detection. This would have given hunting
hominids a survival advantage, making them better hunters and more
successful foragers.

At moderate doses, the mushrooms cause sexual arousal and a general
increase in social bonding behavior. This would have promoted group
cohesion and reproductive success among groups that incorporated the
mushroom into their diet.

At high doses, the mushrooms dissolve the ego and produce visionary
experiences, ecstatic states, and the dissolution of boundaries between
self and other. These experiences form the basis of [[shamanism]] and
religion.

## Language as Synesthetic Activity

The ability to associate sounds with meaningful internal images is a
synesthetic activity. The most recently evolved areas of the human brain,
Broca's area and the neocortex, are devoted to the control of symbol and
language processing. The highly organized neurolinguistic areas of our
brain have made language and culture possible, but we do not know what
evolutionary pressures drove their development.

In a hallucinogenic state, one has the incontrovertible impression that
language possesses an objectified and visible dimension, ordinarily hidden
from awareness. Language under such conditions is seen, beheld, just as
we would ordinarily see our homes and surroundings. The collectively
designed cultural environment in which we all live is recognized as the
objectification of our collective linguistic intent.
