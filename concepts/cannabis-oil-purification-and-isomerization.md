# Cannabis Oil Purification and Isomerization

The transformation of crude cannabis extract into a highly potent, purified product
involves two major stages: **purification** (removing non-psychoactive plant
material from the oil) and **isomerization** (chemically converting less-active
cannabinoids into more potent forms). Together, these processes form the core of
what Ben Lemon calls "Cannabis Alchemy" — the art and science of transmuting
raw plant material into a refined psychoactive essence. These techniques can
increase the potency of cannabis oil by two to six times, depending on the
starting material's cannabinoid profile.

## Overview of the Process Pipeline

The full workflow proceeds through several sequential stages:

1. **Initial extraction** — Alcohol refluxing dissolves cannabinoids from plant
   material into a solvent (methanol or ethanol).
2. **Soxhlet washing** — Residual oil remaining in the spent plant matter is
   recovered through continuous solvent recycling.
3. **Solvent removal** — The alcohol is distilled off and collected for reuse,
   leaving behind crude cannabis oil.
4. **Liquid-liquid purification** — A three-phase separation removes water-
   soluble impurities, leaving purified cannabinoid oil in the petroleum ether
   layer.
5. **Isomerization** — Sulfuric acid catalyzes the conversion of CBD to THC and
   transforms lower-rotating THC isomers into higher-rotating, more potent forms.
6. **Post-isomerization purification** — The reaction product is washed, neutralized,
   and the ether evaporated to yield the final oil.

## Petroleum Ether: The Purification Solvent

Petroleum ether is a mixture of light aliphatic hydrocarbons (primarily pentane
and hexane) with a boiling range of 30–60°C. Despite its name, it contains no
ethanol — "ether" here refers to its extremely volatile, ethereal character. It
is the solvent of choice for the purification stage because of its exceptional
selectivity: cannabinoids are highly soluble in petroleum ether, while water,
alcohol, chlorophyll derivatives, sugars, and most plant impurities are not. This
differential solubility is what makes the liquid-liquid extraction possible.

Key properties that make petroleum ether ideal for this work:
- **Non-polar**: Dissolves lipophilic cannabinoids while rejecting polar compounds.
- **Low boiling point (30–60°C)**: Easy to remove by gentle evaporation, leaving
  no residue in the final product.
- **Immiscible with water**: Forms a clean phase boundary for liquid-liquid
  extraction, enabling straightforward separation.
- **No reaction with cannabinoids**: Unlike some solvents, petroleum ether does not
  chemically modify THC or CBD during the purification process.

## Soxhlet Washing: Recovering Every Last Drop

After the initial reflux extraction, a significant quantity of cannabis oil remains
trapped within the cellular structure of the spent plant material. Rather than
discarding this material, the soxhlet washing process recovers these residual oils
through continuous solvent recycling — the same apparatus used for refluxing is
reconfigured as a soxhlet extractor.

The procedure works as follows: the crude oil/alcohol mixture is drained from the
plant material into a collection pot. A colander lined with large coffee filter
paper is placed above the pot, and the wet cannabis material is transferred into
the colander. The apparatus is reassembled with an ice-cooled condenser lid. As
the alcohol solution in the pot boils, pure solvent vapor rises, recondenses on
the cold lid, and drips back down through the cannabis material in the colander.
Each pass of clean, recondensed solvent washes additional oil from the plant
fibers, which drains back into the collection pot below.

This process is inherently self-sustaining: the solvent is continuously distilled
and recycled, so the operator does not need to add fresh solvent during the wash.
Completion is determined by a simple visual test — when several drops of liquid
draining from the colander leave no colored residue when evaporated on a piece of
glass, all recoverable oil has been extracted.

## Solvent Distillation and Recovery

Once soxhlet washing is complete, the alcohol must be separated from the dissolved
cannabis oil. The apparatus is reconfigured for distillation: the alcohol/oil
solution is heated in the pot, alcohol vapor rises to the ice-cooled condenser lid,
recondenses into pure liquid, and drips into a collection container. Because the
boiling point of methanol (64°C) and ethanol (78.5°C) are far below the
decomposition temperature of cannabinoids, the oil remains in the pot undamaged
while the alcohol is cleanly separated.

This recovered alcohol is substantially pure and can be reused for subsequent
extractions, making the process economical. The dark, viscous residue remaining
in the pot is crude cannabis oil — potent but still contaminated with water-
soluble plant compounds, waxes, and pigments that must be removed through further
purification.

## Purification via Liquid-Liquid Extraction

The core purification technique is a three-phase liquid-liquid extraction that
exploits the differential solubility of cannabinoids versus water-soluble impurities.
The crude oil is dissolved in a mixture of alcohol and water, then petroleum ether
is added. When thoroughly mixed, the solution separates into three distinct layers:

### The Three Layers

1. **Bottom layer (water/alcohol)** — Contains water-soluble impurities: plant
   pigments, chlorophyll derivatives, sugars, tannins, water-soluble waxes, and
   other non-psychoactive plant constituents. This layer is discarded.

2. **Middle layer (emulsion)** — A cloudy, milky interface containing emulsified
   material that belongs partly to both phases. This layer contains some
   cannabinoids trapped in the emulsion and requires special handling. It can be
   broken by adding more alcohol or by gentle agitation, allowing the trapped
   cannabinoids to migrate to the petroleum ether layer.

3. **Top layer (petroleum ether/cannabis oil)** — The desired product. Cannabinoids
   preferentially dissolve in the non-polar petroleum ether phase, floating on top
   of the denser water/alcohol layer. This golden-to-amber colored solution
   contains the purified cannabinoid extract.

### Siphon Separation Technique

The petroleum ether/oil layer is carefully removed from the top of the separation
vessel using a siphon — a flexible tube that draws off the upper layer by gravity.
The operator must exercise care to avoid disturbing the emulsion layer, which would
contaminate the purified product. The siphoned petroleum ether solution is then
washed one or more times with fresh water to remove any residual alcohol or
water-soluble material that may have been carried over. After the final wash, the
petroleum ether is evaporated (using gentle heat in a water bath), yielding
purified cannabis oil significantly cleaner and more potent than the crude extract.

## Isomerization: Converting CBD to THC

Isomerization is the chemical process that transforms cannabidiol (CBD) — a non-
psychoactive cannabinoid — into tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), the primary
psychoactive component of cannabis. This reaction simultaneously converts
naturally occurring lower-rotating THC isomers into higher-rotating forms that
are significantly more potent.

### The Chemistry

CBD and THC are structural isomers — they share the same molecular formula (C₂₁H₃₀O₂)
