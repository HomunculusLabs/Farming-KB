# Ramsbottom Poisonous Fungi Species

Complete species descriptions from [[john-ramsbottom]]'s 1945 "Poisonous Fungi" with colour plates by Rose Ellenby. All measurements in inches. See [[mushroom-poisoning-guide]] for modern toxicological summaries.

## AMANITA PHALLOIDES -- Death Cap

**Cap**: 3.5" wide. Fleshy, hemispherical then flat. Olivaceous green or yellowish green, darker centre. Occasionally brownish, yellow, or rarely whitish. Satiny when dry, viscid in wet weather. Finely streaked with dark radiating fibrils.

**Gills**: Crowded, white, often greenish/yellowish reflection.

**Stem**: 4.5" tall, 5/8" wide. Whitish, often green tinge. Solid at first, often becoming hollow. Well-marked large white/greenish ring. Bulbous base surrounded by large white/yellowish persistent volva, free for half its length with lax margin.

**Flesh**: White, tinged with cap colour below readily separable skin. Practically tasteless, without smell until decay (then fetid).

**Habitat**: Common in woods and adjoining pastures, late summer to early autumn.

**Poisoning**: 10-12 hour incubation (almost diagnostic). Sudden intense abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhoea, extreme thirst. Quiescence after ~2 days (most dangerous period). Recurrence in more intense form; nervous system gradually paralysed, liver degenerates, delirium, collapse, death. Recovery is slow with prolonged convalescence.

**Key toxins**: Amanita-toxin (heat-resistant, causes liver degeneration), [[phalloidin]] (polypeptide, destroyed by heat), phallin (glucoside, attacks red blood cells, destroyed by heat/digestion -- NOT the active poison).

## AMANITA VERNA -- Fools' Mushroom

**Cap**: 3" wide. Usually entirely white, though centre may be slightly ochraceous. Closely resembles A. phalloides.

**Stem**: 5" tall, 3/8" wide. Fairly long and slender. Volva usually more sheathing than A. phalloides.

**Habitat**: Woods, especially beech. Summer and early autumn. Despite the name (verna = spring), rarely appears in spring.

**Notes**: J. Bauhin called it *Fungus stultorum* -- "the fungus of fools," presumably because in its young stage it was mistaken for A. caesarea. Rare in Britain. Just as poisonous as A. phalloides but more dangerous because being white, it is more readily mistaken for edible mushrooms.

## AMANITA VIROSA -- Destroying Angel

**Cap**: 5" wide. Conical at first, often asymmetrical, always rather umbonate when expanded.

**Stem**: 7.5" tall, 5/8" wide. Slender, floccosely scaly. Incomplete silky ring, usually lower on one side, often remains attached to cap edge. Volva lax and wide.

**Habitat**: Damp woods, summer and autumn. Rare in Britain.

**Notes**: Same toxicity as A. phalloides. The white colour and large size make confusion with edible species more likely.

## AMANITA MAPPA -- False Death Cap

**Cap**: 3" wide. Usually lemon-yellow or white with NO trace of green. No dark radiating fibrils. Irregular patches of volva remain on cap (the fancied resemblance to a map gives the specific epithet).

**Stem**: 3.75" tall, 0.5" wide. Bulbous base is truncate with short thick margin showing torn volva edge, separated from stem by a distinct groove.

**Flesh**: White, yellowish below separable cap skin. Smell of raw potatoes. Insipid then disagreeable taste.

**Habitat**: Woods, especially beech, summer and autumn.

**Important**: Until recently considered very poisonous. NOW KNOWN TO BE HARMLESS, though inedible due to offensive flavour. Frequently confused with A. phalloides.

## AMANITA MUSCARIA -- Fly Agaric

**Cap**: 7" wide. Scarlet or orange-red, slightly viscid, shiny, dotted with thick white/yellowish warts often arranged concentrically. Roundish when young, expands to flat with striate edge.

**Gills**: Crowded, white or yellow-tinted, thick, minutely toothed edge.

**Stem**: 10" tall, 1" wide. White or yellow-tinted, stuffed then hollow, often scaly, striate above ring. Bulbous base encircled by zones of white/yellowish squamules.

**Flesh**: White except orange-yellow under separable cap skin. Pleasant taste, no appreciable smell.

**Habitat**: Mostly under birches, also under pines. Autumn.

**Variation**: When damp, cap may slip through volva without tearing -- then without spots.

**Poisoning**: NEVER CAUSES DEATH in healthy people. 1-3 hours after eating: delirium and hallucinations, sometimes with intestinal disturbances. Followed by intense stupor and awakening to complete forgetfulness.

**Toxins**: Muscarine (small amounts, NOT matching actual symptoms), mycetoatropine/muscaridine (atropine-like CNS effects), choline (large amounts, probably causes GI symptoms). Poison resides principally in skin of the cap; considerable variation in amount.

**Ethnobotanical use**: Koryak tribes of northeast Siberia eat it for "excessive emotion." Urine recycling prolongs effects. Price in barren Steppes: 3-4 reindeer for a single specimen. Vikings said to eat it before battle ("berserk"). The name refers to Albertus Magnus (13th century) observation that the fungus broken up in milk kills flies.

**Homeopathic use**: Agaricus (Aga), formerly one of the antipsorics.

## AMANITA PANTHERINA -- False Blusher

**Cap**: 4" wide. Convex then spread out. Brownish grey, brown, or yellowish brown, darker centre. Slightly viscid when damp, shiny when dry, striate edge. Numerous small white warts.

**Stem**: 5" tall, 0.5" wide. White, stuffed then hollow. Bulbous base surrounded by membranous volva border with one or more scaly bracelets above it. Ring attached obliquely about middle, thin and often fugaceous.

**Flesh**: White. Slight smell of radish, mild taste.

**Habitat**: Woods, heaths, and pastures. Summer and autumn.

**Poisoning**: Similar to A. muscaria but apparently more serious.

**Confusion risk**: Edible A. rubescens (flesh and gills redden when cut; pantherina does not).

## LEPIOTA HELVEOLA -- Poisonous Lepiota

**Cap**: 1.25" wide. Rounded then flattened, sometimes slight central prominence. Ochre-brown cuticle becomes flushed pink/lilac in damp weather, tears into scales showing white/pinkish surface.

**Gills**: White then cream, crowded, fimbriate edge.

**Stem**: 1.5" tall, 0.25" wide. Cylindrical, hollow, concolorous with cap, silky fibrils. Ring as annular bracelet.

**Flesh**: White, often becoming pinkish when cut. Pleasant smell, sourish taste.

**Habitat**: Amongst grass, autumn. Rare in Britain.

**Poisoning**: Mild [[amanita-phalloides]]-type symptoms. Much less dangerous than the Amanitas.

## LACTARIUS TORMINOSUS -- Woolly Milk Cap

**Cap**: 6" wide. Convex, strongly inrolled margin hiding gills, covered with whitish woolly felt. Later depressed centre, viscid when moist. Rosy pink or pale orange-red, darker concentric zones.

**Milk**: White, very acrid taste.

**Flesh**: Pinkish. Firm, brittle, finally hollow stem.

**Habitat**: Summer and autumn, woods and heaths, frequently under birches.

**Important**: NOT really poisonous. Acrid principle destroyed by cooking. THE FAVOURITE TOADSTOOL IN FINLAND -- boiled in water, eaten at once or salted down. Also eaten in Norway (strongly roasted, added to coffee).

## RUSSULA EMETICA -- Sickener

**Cap**: 4" wide. Convex then flattened or depressed. Bright red, punk then shiny, readily losing colour. Skin easily removed. Edge at first smooth, later furrowed.

**Gills**: Shining white, more or less free, somewhat crowded.

**Stem**: 3" tall, 1" wide. Rigid, white or pink-tinged.

**Flesh**: Firm, white, pink under cap skin. Very acrid taste after a few seconds (persistent). Pleasant smell.

**Habitat**: Woods, summer and autumn.

**Notes**: Acrid substance dispersed in cooking, but best avoided.
