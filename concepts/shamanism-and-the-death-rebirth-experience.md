# Shamanism and the Death-Rebirth Experience

## Overview

Shamanism represents humanity's oldest and most indigenous form of spiritual practice, centered on the direct experience of [[shamanism-death-and-rebirth]] through entheogenic plants. According to the analysis in *[[mushrooms-and-mankind]]*, shamanism is not merely the precursor to organized religion but its foundational cornerstone — a tradition from which all subsequent religious systems ultimately derive, even as they have systematically suppressed and obscured their entheogenic roots.

## The Shamanic Paradigm

At the core of [[amazonian-boa-spirits-and-water-beings-in-shamanic-practice]] lies the journey into the death experience — a deep psychological introspection and judgment through plant-induced states, undertaken in preparation for physical death. This practice differs fundamentally from most organized spiritual traditions in several key respects:

- **Direct experience over belief**: Shamanism prioritizes firsthand knowledge of otherworldly realms rather than faith in doctrines about them.
- **Individual autonomy over institutional authority**: The shamanic path is personal and experiential, not mediated by priests or hierarchical religious structures.
- **Embrace of mortality**: Rather than downplaying or repressing references to death, shamanism places the conquest of death through spiritual experience at the center of practice.
- **Plant entheogens as tools**: Psychoactive plants are recognized as the primary mechanism for achieving expanded consciousness and accessing spiritual dimensions.

## The Hero's Journey and the Elixir of Immortality

World mythologies universally contain narratives of beings who have transcended death into a spiritual dimension through a quest for an elixir of immortality. The [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]] of these stories across ancient documents suggests a common historical source — the indigenous natural practice of shamanism.

Before the development of writing or organized religion, early humans who encountered consciousness-expanding plants became aware of realities beyond normal physical perception. This discovery likely catalyzed:

- A new contemplation of death and its meaning
- The desire for individual immortality
- The development of mythic hero narratives
- The recognition that the "death experience" produced by certain plants could serve as preparation for actual death

## The Death Experience in Practice

The shamanic death-rebirth experience involves several stages:

1. **Dissolution of the self**: Every atom of the self is described as being "dashed to pieces" during the plunge into the underworld vortex.
2. **Life review**: Each event of one's life surfaces into consciousness for examination — nothing remains hidden.
3. **Confrontation with conditioned beliefs**: Culturally embedded belief systems are recognized and, often painfully, rejected when they contradict directly experienced indigenous spiritual nature.
4. **Encounter with heaven and hell**: The practitioner experiences the full spectrum of spiritual realms, from transcendent heights to abyssal depths.
5. **Rebirth and reintegration**: The practitioner emerges transformed, having confronted and processed the fundamental fears and attachments that bind consciousness.

## The [[samsara]] Cycle

In Hindu Vedic tradition, the inability to let go of repressive belief at death creates the conflict that forces reincarnation — the revolving door of existence called **Samsara** (the wheel of life). The shamanic perspective holds that:

- Spiritual death consumes those who are not in contact with indigenous spirituality
- Those who choose (consciously or unconsciously) to remain in repressed states become both the repressed and the repressors
- Deep cultural conditioning creates fierce resistance to reshaping one's belief system
- Individuals may become enforcers of their own oppression, defending the very systems that limit their spiritual development

## Shamanism vs. Organized Religion

A fundamental tension exists between shamanic practice and organized religion:

| Aspect | Shamanism | Organized Religion |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| Authority | Individual direct experience | Institutional hierarchy |
| Access to the divine | Through plant entheogens | Through priests and sacraments |
| Knowledge | Open and experiential | Dogmatic and prescribed |
| Sexuality | Accepted as natural human expression | Regulated and often condemned |
| [[mckenna-consciousness-expansion-and-drug-war]] | Actively pursued | Systematically repressed |
| Death | Practiced and prepared for through direct experience | Theorized about through belief systems |

## Repression of the Five Natural Drives

Shamanism recognizes five fundamental human drives that patriarchal religious systems have historically condemned and suppressed:

1. **Expansion of consciousness**: The natural desire to explore altered states and expanded awareness
2. **Heightening of awareness**: The drive toward greater sensitivity and perceptual acuity
3. **Experience of plant substances**: The innate human attraction to psychoactive plants
4. **Freedom of sexuality**: Natural sexual expression without guilt or regulation
5. **Rhythmic dance and trance**: The urge toward ecstatic physical expression and communal ritual

Replacing these natural drives with moral judgments, condemning them as universal sins, and usurping authority over them is characterized as "an absolute atrocity against mankind."

## The [[amanita-muscaria]] as Shamanic Cornerstone

The **[[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]]** is identified as the cornerstone of shamanic practice, producing what is described as the quintessential death and rebirth experience. Despite shamanism's rejection of patriarchal religious structures, the text argues that shamanism is itself the cornerstone of all religions — though modern religion has "completely lost the natural indigenous freedom it professes to impart."

The stages of the Amanita experience are described as operating like an elevator:
- **Ascent**: Reaching indefinable realms of the heavens
- **Descent**: A sudden plunge to the depths of the underworld (Amenta)
- **Re-ascent**: An up-sweeping return toward higher states

This cyclical pattern of ascent and descent, combined with guided ritual practice, provides the practitioner with a structured method for confronting and transcending the fear of death.

## Samsara and Spiritual Evolution

The Hindu and Buddhist texts describe spiritual evolution through the process of reincarnation, where advancement is made according to knowledge gained and the discovery of key tools for enlightenment found at different levels of the path. The threshold of spiritual achievement is consistently preceded by the consumption of the tree of life or the drinking of the living waters — entheogenic references embedded within the [[christus-eros-osiris-mckenna-mythological-framework-suppression-psychedelic-knowledge]].

## The Golden Age Prophecy

Virtually every religion on the planet contains a forward-looking prophecy describing a future time of direct contact with the divine — the thousand-year reign, the new golden age, the return of enlightened beings. This shared expectation suggests a universal recognition that humanity's current spiritual condition is incomplete and that a restoration of direct entheogenic communion is anticipated as part of human spiritual evolution.

## [[wilhelm-reich]] and the Politics of Repression

The psychologist **Wilhelm Reich** provided a framework for understanding how the repression of natural human drives — particularly sexuality — serves systems of social control. Reich argued against Freud's thesis that cultural achievement requires the suppression of instinctual drives, pointing to highly cultured indigenous societies where sexual freedom was complete. Reich's own fate — imprisonment, mysterious death, and the burning of his books by the FDA in New York — is presented as an example of how challenging the apparatus of repression is met with systematic destruction.
