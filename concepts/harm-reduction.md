# Psychedelic Harm Reduction

## Overview

Psychedelic harm reduction is a set of principles, practices, and resources designed to minimize risks associated with psychedelic substance use. It is grounded in the understanding that people will use psychedelics regardless of legal status, and that providing accurate information, safety tools, and support is more effective than abstinence-only approaches. Harm reduction does not encourage use — it reduces harm when use occurs.

See [[psychedelic-integration]] for post-session practices and [[psychedelic-compounds-reference]] for substance-specific information.

## Core Principles

1. **Know your substance**: Verify what you have through testing
2. **Know your dose**: Start low, go slow
3. **Know your source**: Obtain from trusted sources
4. **Know yourself**: Screen for contraindications
5. **Know your setting**: Prepare a safe, comfortable environment
6. **Know your sitter**: Have a trusted, sober person present
7. **Know your limits**: Honor your comfort zone; you can always take more, never less

## Testing Kits

### Why Test

- Substances sold as psychedelics may be adulterated, substituted, or of unexpected potency
- Common adulterants: NBOMe compounds (sold as LSD; significantly more dangerous), 25I-NBOMe and related compounds have been associated with deaths
- Research chemicals may be mislabeled or of unknown purity
- Even "trusted" sources can make mistakes; supply chain errors occur

### Available Tests

- **Ehrlich reagent**: Detects indoles (LSD, psilocybin, DMT). LSD turns purple; NBOMe compounds do not react (indicating they are not LSD). Essential safety tool.
- **Hofmann reagent**: Turns blue-violet for LSD; helps distinguish LSD from other compounds that may react with Ehrlich
- **Marquis reagent**: Detects a broad range of substances; turns purple/black for MDMA, orange for amphetamines. Less specific for tryptamines but useful as a secondary check.
- **Mecke reagent**: Useful for distinguishing between different indole compounds
- **Fentanyl test strips**: While fentanyl is rarely found in psychedelic supplies, testing is recommended if there is any doubt about source

### How to Use

- Follow the manufacturer's instructions precisely
- Use a small sample (the size of a pinhead)
- Test in good lighting on a white ceramic surface
- Compare color change to the reference card within the specified time window
- No single test can identify every compound; use multiple reagents for better confidence
- Remember: a test indicates presence, not purity or dose

### Testing Services

- Organizations like **DanceSafe** and **Energy Control** provide mail-in testing services with more sophisticated analysis (GC/MS, HPLC)
- These provide quantitative results and identify compounds that field tests may miss
- **DrugsData** (formerly EcstasyData) provides anonymous laboratory testing

## Dosage Guidelines

### General Principle

- **Start low, go slow**: Begin with a sub-threshold or threshold dose; wait 60-90 minutes before considering more
- This is especially important with substances of unknown potency or when trying a new batch

### Substance-Specific Guidelines

- **Psilocybin mushrooms**: Light dose 0.5-1 g dried; moderate 1-2.5 g; strong 2.5-5 g; very strong 5+ g
- **LSD**: Threshold 15-25 micrograms; light 25-75 micrograms; common 75-150 micrograms; strong 150-300 micrograms
- **DMT (smoked)**: Light 10-20 mg; breakthrough 30-50 mg
- **Ayahuasca**: Variable; always follow the guidance of an experienced facilitator
- **Mescaline**: Threshold 100 mg; light 100-250 mg; common 200-400 mg; strong 400+ mg
- **Salvinorin A (smoked)**: Light 100-250 micrograms; moderate 250-500 micrograms; strong 500+ micrograms

### Potency Variables

- Mushroom potency varies enormously between species, strains, growing conditions, and even within a single specimen
- LSD blotter potency is highly variable; even within a single sheet, doses may range from 50-200 micrograms
- Synthetic substances may have inconsistent purity
- Always err on the side of less

## Contraindications

### Absolute Contraindications (Do Not Use)

- **Current use of MAOIs** (with ayahuasca or other MAOI-containing substances): risk of hypertensive crisis
- **Current use of SSRIs/SNRIs** with psychedelics: risk of serotonin syndrome (life-threatening); may also block psychedelic effects
- **Current use of tramadol** with psychedelics: lowers seizure threshold and risk of serotonin syndrome
- **History of psychotic disorders** (schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder): psychedelics may trigger or exacerbate psychosis
- **Pregnancy**: insufficient safety data; potential risk to fetus

### Strong Cautions (Consult Healthcare Provider)

- **Cardiovascular disease**: psychedelics can increase heart rate and blood pressure
- **Bipolar disorder**: risk of triggering manic episodes
- **Seizure disorders**: psychedelics may lower seizure threshold
- **Liver disease**: impaired metabolism may alter effects and increase risk
- **Personal or family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder**
- **Active substance use disorder**: using psychedelics while dependent on other substances carries additional risks

### Medication Interactions

- **SSRIs**: Block effects of psychedelics; risk of serotonin syndrome if combined — typically need 2-6 week washout period
- **Lithium**: Significantly increases risk of seizures and adverse reactions when combined with psychedelics
- **Stimulants** (amphetamine, cocaine): increased cardiovascular strain
- **Benzodiazepines**: will significantly reduce or eliminate psychedelic effects (sometimes used intentionally to end a difficult experience)
- **Antipsychotics**: will block psychedelic effects and may increase risk of adverse reactions
- **Tramadol, dextromethorphan, meperidine**: serotonin syndrome risk

## Trip Sitting

A trip sitter is a sober, trusted person who remains present throughout a psychedelic experience to provide support and safety.

### Trip Sitter Responsibilities

- Remain sober (no alcohol, cannabis, or other substances)
- Stay calm and present; your emotional state influences the experience
- Create and maintain a safe, comfortable environment
- Provide reassurance during difficult moments ("you are safe, this will pass")
- Offer water, snacks, blankets, or other comfort items as needed
- Do not direct or control the experience unless safety is at risk
- Know when and how to call for medical help if needed
- Never leave the person alone until they are fully grounded

### Handling Difficult Experiences

- **Stay present**: Do not leave; use a calm, reassuring voice
- **Change the setting**: Change music, lighting, move to a different room, go outside
- **Grounding techniques**: Hold hands, offer a warm object, focus on breathing
- **Reassurance**: "This is temporary," "You are safe," "Let it flow, don't resist"
- **Medication**: Benzodiazepines can be used to abort a severe adverse reaction (consult medical professional in advance)
- **Do not**: Argue, physically restrain (unless there is immediate physical danger), invalidate the experience, or express fear or panic
- Remember: most difficult experiences are not medical emergencies; they are psychological challenges that can be navigated with support

## Integration Resources

- **Psychedelic Support**: Online directory of psychedelic-integration therapists
- **MAPS**: Provides resources and referrals for psychedelic therapy and integration
- **Integration circles**: Facilitated group sessions available in many cities
- **Crisis resources**: Keep local emergency numbers accessible; know the nearest hospital
- **Zendo Project**: Provides psychedelic harm reduction services at festivals and events; their training materials are publicly available

## Festivals and Events

Psychedelic use is common at music festivals and events; specific harm reduction considerations apply:

- Test substances before the event (testing may be difficult on-site)
- Know the location of medical tents and harm reduction services
- Stay hydrated; eat regular meals
- Have a clear meeting point and communication plan with friends
- Be aware that heat, crowds, and loud music can amplify anxiety
- Many festivals have volunteer harm reduction organizations (Zendo Project, DanceSafe, etc.)

## Legal Considerations

- Harm reduction practices are legal; testing your own substances for personal safety is not illegal in most jurisdictions
- However, possession of scheduled substances remains criminal in most places
- If someone is experiencing a medical emergency, seek help immediately — most jurisdictions have Good Samaritan laws that protect people who call for medical assistance from prosecution
- Know your local laws and your rights
