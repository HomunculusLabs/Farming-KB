# Basidiomycete Mating Systems: Sexual Reproduction and Genetics

Basidiomycetes (club fungi) encompass mushrooms, bracket fungi, puffballs, rusts, and smuts. Their sexual reproduction is unique among fungi and involves the formation of a dikaryotic mycelium — a stage where each cell contains two genetically distinct nuclei that divide synchronously. Understanding mating systems is critical [[coprinellus-disseminatus]], Ustilago maydis (corn smut), Pholiota nameko (nameko mushroom)

In bipolar species, approximately 50% of random spore pairings are compatible (assuming a large number of alleles with roughly equal frequencies in the population). The A locus encodes homeodomain transcription factors (HD1 and HD2) that, when derived from different alleles, form a functional heterodimer that activates dikaryon-specific gene expression.

## Tetrapolar Mating Systems

In tetrapolar mating systems, compatibility is controlled by **two unlinked mating-type loci**, both of which must differ between mating partners. This is the more common system among Agaricales (gilled mushrooms):

- **A locus**: Controls nuclear pairing, clamp connection formation, and dikaryotic growth. Encodes homeodomain transcription factors (HD proteins).
- **B locus**: Controls nuclear migration and plasmogamy (fusion of hyphal cells). Encodes pheromone receptors (STE3-like) and pheromone genes.
- **Compatibility formula**: A₁B₁ × A₂B₂ = compatible; A₁B₁ × A₁B₂ = incompatible (same A); A₁B₁ × A₂B₁ = incompatible (same B)
- **Example species**: [[flammulina-velutipes]]

### Tetrapolar Mating Dynamics

In tetrapolar species, the probability of compatibility between two random monokaryons depends on the number of alleles at each locus:

- If A has 100 alleles and B has 100 alleles: compatibility = (1 - 1/nA) × (1 - 1/nB) ≈ 98% of random pairings
- If A has 50 alleles and B has 50 alleles: compatibility ≈ 96%
- If A has 9 alleles and B has 9 alleles: compatibility ≈ 80%

In nature, many tetrapolar species have dozens to hundreds of alleles at each locus, resulting in very high outcrossing rates. Schizophyllum commune has one of the most extreme systems known, with an estimated 288 A alleles and 81 B alleles, making nearly 99.95% of random matings compatible.

### Sequential Steps in Tetrapolar Mating

1. **Pheromone signaling (B locus)**: The B locus encodes multiple pheromone genes and pheromone receptor genes. A monokaryon produces pheromones that bind receptors on a compatible partner (different B alleles). This reciprocal signaling triggers:
   - Cell wall dissolution at points of hyphal contact
   - Plasmogamy (fusion of cytoplasm, not nuclei)
   - Formation of a conjugation pore between hyphae

2. **Nuclear pairing (A locus)**: After plasmogamy, the A locus-encoded homeodomain proteins form heterodimers only between different A alleles. These heterodimers activate the dikaryotic genetic program:
   - Synchronized nuclear division
   - Clamp connection formation
   - Dikaryon-specific gene expression
   - Formation of fruiting body primordia

3. **Dikaryon establishment**: A stable dikaryotic mycelium forms where each compartment contains exactly two nuclei (one from each parent) that divide in synchrony. Clamp connections ensure each daughter cell receives one nucleus of each type.

## The Dikaryotic Phase

The dikaryon is the dominant vegetative phase of most basidiomycetes and has several [[functional-redundancy-fungal-communities]]
- **Hybrid vigor**: Complementation between two different genomes allows expression of beneficial recessive alleles
- **Regulation of sexual reproduction**: The dikaryon is pre-adapted for immediate karyogamy (nuclear fusion) when conditions favor fruiting
