    11|# Cannabis for Cancer-Related Symptoms
    12|
    13|## Cancer Overview
    14|
    15|Every year, cancer takes credit for 13% of all deaths worldwide. According to
    16|the orthodox model of medicine, 30% of all cancers are attributed to known and
    17|avoidable risk factors. Cancer-inducing materials, called carcinogens, exist
    18|almost everywhere.
    19|
    20|Within the orthodox model, cancer (malignant neoplasm) starts with the
    21|corruption of genetic material (a mutation) inside a normally healthy cell.
    22|Evidence suggests that cellular cancer may occur quite frequently in everybody,
    23|but that normally the body's own proper immune defenses can cure these threats.
    24|
    25|## Cannabis and Cancer: Evidence Base (CHI Value 100, 50 Studies Reviewed)
    26|
    27|As early as 1974, the U.S. government knew of cannabis's effectiveness against
    28|certain types of cancer. While the publication of this particular study became a
    29|victim of the war on drugs, an article about the study published by the
    30|Washington Post survived. The story reported that "the active chemical agent in
    31|marijuana curbs the growth of three kinds of cancer (lung cancer, breast cancer,
    32|and viral-induced leukemia) in mice."
    33|
    34|Since then the scientific evidence of cannabinoids' ability to counteract
    35|certain types of cancer has grown in quantity and detail. Major scientific
    36|journals continue to report about the complex effects that cannabinoids have in
    37|preventing and treating specific cancer formations. Studies also lay claim to
    38|cannabinoids' abilities to mitigate common side effects of chemotherapeutic
    39|agents.
    40|
    41|The high overall CHI value of 100 (from 50 studies) suggests with relative
    42|confidence that cannabinoids may prevent, inhibit, and destroy cancer cells, as
    43|well as exhibit strong therapeutic influences to mitigate nausea, vomiting,
    44|pain, night sweats, and improve quality of life. For the pharmacological mechanisms, see [[blesching-cannabinoid-pharmacology]].
    45|
    46|### Anti-Tumor Properties of CBD
    47|
    48|CBD is able to produce significant anti-tumor activity both in vitro and in
    49|vivo. CBD has been shown to selectively produce oxidative stress in cancer
    50|cells, thus producing apoptosis (cancer cell suicide) without impacting normal
    51|cells.
    52|
    53|### Strain and Form Considerations for Cancer
    54|
    55|Research shows that CB1- and CB2-mediated therapeutic influences may vary
    56|depending on the patient's type of cancer or symptoms. In some cases,
    57|therapeutic impact occurred via CB1; at other times, via CB2. In all other
    58|cases, it was the synergistic effects induced by activation of both CB1 and CB2
    59|receptor sites that was therapeutic.
    60|
    61|Raw, fresh leaf plant matter, such as cannabis juice, has a significantly higher
    62|CBD content in the form of CBD-acid with a greater affinity to activate CB2.
    63|Sativa and sativa-heavy strains tend to present with a higher THC:CBD/CBN ratio,
    64|while indicas tend to contain a lower THC:CBD/CBN ratio.

    66|## Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting: Evidence Base (CHI Value 104, 30+
    67|Studies)
    68|
    69|### Overview of Evidence
    70|
    71|The evidence for cannabinoids in treating chemotherapy-induced nausea and
    72|vomiting spans a period of four decades. Studies conducted on animal, adult, and
    73|pediatric patients reached similar conclusions. Early studies focused on
    74|comparing the effectiveness of cannabinoids to prochlorperazine (generic of
    75|Compazine). Most studies reported cannabinoids as superior.
    76|
    77|Later studies broadened their focus and reported that cannabinoids proved more
    78|effective than other antiemetics such as metoclopramide, chlorpromazine,
    79|thiethylperazine, haloperidol, domperidone, or alizapride. More recent studies
    80|examined the ability of cannabinoids, especially cannabidiol, to markedly reduce
    81|anticipated oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death in the kidneys,
    82|therefore improving renal function in cancer pathologies.
    83|
    84|### Key Studies on Chemotherapy Nausea
    85|
    86|**Nabilone vs. Prochlorperazine (multiple studies):**
    87|
    88|Multiple studies consistently demonstrated Nabilone's superiority over
    89|prochlorperazine. In a study of 113 cancer chemotherapy patients, Nabilone was
    90|far more effective in reducing nausea and vomiting. In another study of 24 lung
    91|cancer patients, Nabilone was significantly superior to prochlorperazine in the
    92|reduction of vomiting episodes. Symptom scores were significantly better for
    93|patients on Nabilone for nausea, retching, and vomiting.
    94|
    95|**Pediatric Chemotherapy (Jerusalem, 1995):**
    96|
    97|THC was administered orally (18 mg in an edible oil) to children with cancer
    98|receiving chemotherapy. The result was complete prevention of vomiting with
    99|negligible side effects.
   100|
   101|**Dronabinol vs. Ondansetron (Florida, 2007):**
   102|
   103|Dronabinol proved as effective as ondansetron in reducing nausea and vomiting.
   104|Combination therapy was not more effective than either drug alone.
   105|
   106|**Inhaled Cannabis (1988):**
   107|
   108|78% of 56 cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy unresponsive to standard
   109|antiemetic drugs demonstrated a positive response to marijuana.
   110|
   111|**Nabilone in Pediatric Patients (UK, 1986):**
   112|
   113|Nabilone significantly reduced nausea and vomiting in 18 pediatric patients, and
   114|two-thirds of the children expressed a preference for the drug.
   115|
   116|### Therapeutic Window Consideration
   117|
   118|Research suggests that a therapeutic window exists which may depend on
   119|individual tolerance. Either a very high or low dose could offer a lack of
   120|antiemetic effects or adverse effects. To determine your optimal window, follow
   121|the advice of a licensed health care provider and your own subjective
   122|experience. See [[blesching-cannabis-dosing-strategies]] for detailed titration protocols.

   124|## Cancer-Induced Night Sweats
   125|
   126|Night sweats, relatively common in end-stage cancer patients, are partly
   127|responsible for disrupted sleep patterns, which further reduces the patient's
   128|overall quality of life.
   129|
   130|A synthetic orally administered cannabinoid, Nabilone (similar to THC), was
   131|effectively used in treating night sweats. Cancer patients who suffered the
   132|ill-effects of interrupted sleep experienced an improved quality of life during
   133|Nabilone treatment. Further, the cannabinoid positively affected pains,
   134|anorexia, and nausea. For appetite-specific mechanisms, see [[blesching-cannabis-appetite-regulation-ghrelin-leptin]].
   135|
   136|## Anorexia and Cachexia
   137|
   138|Synthetic drugs containing THC (including Sativex, Dronabinol, Marinol, and
   139|Nabilone) are FDA-approved and used to treat AIDS-related anorexia/cachexia. THC
   140|causes weight gain and an increase in walking distance in COPD patients. Studies
   141|indicate that cannabinoids are effective in reducing anorexia in AIDS and
   142|Alzheimer's patients. For cancer-related pain management, see [[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]].
