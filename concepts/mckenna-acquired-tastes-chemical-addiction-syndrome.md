actively defends the presence of a foreign chemical. These signals of alarm when the chemical
environment is imperiled may be both psychological and physiological, and they manifest

This defensive response is what distinguishes true addiction from mere habit. The body has
reorganized its biochemical equilibrium around the presence of the foreign substance. The
withdrawal symptoms that accompany cessation are not simply the absence of a pleasurable
stimulus but the active disruption of a new homeostatic state that the body has constructed
around the drug. McKenna's framing of this process emphasizes its biological sophistication:
the body does not merely fail to resist the drug, it actively incorporates the drug into its
regulatory systems.
