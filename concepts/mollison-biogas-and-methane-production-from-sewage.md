## Raw Sewage Composition

Raw sewage is a mixture of nutrients, elements, heavy metals, and
carbon compounds. It also contains dangerous levels of bacteria,
viruses, and intestinal worm eggs. A typical analysis from the
Werribee treatment works in Melbourne, Australia (city of 2,700,000
people) shows total dissolved solids of approximately 1,200 mg/L and
biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) of 170 to 570 mg/L. The sewage
contains significant plant nutrients including nitrogen (ammonia as N
at 5-32 mg/L), phosphorus (7-24 mg/L), and potassium (9-56.2 mg/L),
plus trace elements essential for plant growth.

## The Three-Stage Pond System

Sewage treatment using natural processes proceeds through three pond
types arranged in series, falling by gravity flow from one to the next:

1. **Anaerobic ponds** (methane-producing digesters): Raw sewage
   enters these settling ponds where sludge accumulates and
   decomposes without oxygen, producing biogas.

2. **Facultative ponds** (part methane, part oxygen-producing):
   Sludge passes over and becomes anaerobic at the pond base while
   surface water, due to wind or algae activity, remains aerobic.

3. **Aerobic ponds** (oxygen-producing): The final series is
   totally aerobic, completing the treatment process.

## Scale and Dimensions

Melbourne's sewage lagoons cover 1,500 hectares for 2.7 million
people, yielding approximately one hectare of pond per 1,800
inhabitants. Primary settling ponds rarely exceed 7 hectares each,
requiring about 100 ponds to receive and settle all raw sewage. Scaled
down, this means 1 hectare of settling pond to 3,800 people, or
about 270 square metres per 1,000 townspeople.

## Biogas Composition and Uses

Biogas produced in anaerobic ponds is a mixture of methane (CH4),
carbon dioxide (CO2), and ammonia gas (NH3), with traces of nitrous
sulphide or marsh gas (N2O). Biogas is a useful fuel gas for engines
or a cooking gas for homes. Mollison emphasises that biogas is also a
gaseous component of the atmosphere creating the greenhouse effect,
and should therefore be captured and used rather than released to air.

## Digester Pond Design

Deeper and more efficient than shallow settling ponds, digester ponds
can be constructed as 3 metres deep by 3 metres wide by 30 metres
long. Such a long, narrow pond is easily made totally anaerobic by
fitting water seals and a weighted cover of plastic, metal, butyl
rubber, or fibreglass. For deeper digester ponds, artificial agitation
using pumped biogas to stir the sludge is necessary; otherwise the
sludge settles and becomes inactive. See

## Practical Considerations

Successful implementation of Biogas and Methane Production from Sewage requires attention to
several practical factors including environmental conditions,
resource availability, and timing. Careful monitoring and
adaptive management help optimize outcomes across varying
conditions. Integration with other system elements enhances
overall effectiveness and creates beneficial synergies that
improve resilience and productivity over time.
