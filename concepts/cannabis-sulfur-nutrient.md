# Cannabis Sulfur (S)

## Overview

Sulfur is a [[plant-secondary-macronutrients-calcium-magnesium-and-sulfur]] classified as **immobile** in cannabis plants. Many fertilizers contain some form of sulfur, and for this reason, sulfur is seldom deficient. Sulfur deficiency occurs indoors when the pH is too high or when there is excessive calcium present and available. Growers should avoid elemental (pure) sulfur in favor of sulfur compounds such as magnesium sulfate, which mix better in water.

## Role in Plant Biology

Sulfur is an essential building block of many hormones and vitamins, including vitamin B1. It is a structural component of the amino acids cysteine and methionine, which are critical for protein formation. Sulfur-containing compounds (glucosinolates and thiols) contribute to the characteristic aroma and flavor profile of cannabis. Key functions include:

- Component of amino acids cystine and thiamine (building blocks of proteins)
- Involved in protein synthesis
- The sulfate form buffers water pH
- Essential in the formation of oils and flavors
- Necessary for respiration and synthesis/breakdown of fatty acids
- Found in virtually all ground, river, and lake water as sulfate
- Indispensable element in many plant cells and seeds
- Contributes to the terpene and cannabinoid production pathways

The sulfate form of sulfur buffers the water pH. Virtually all ground, river, and lake water contains sulfate. Sulfate is involved in protein synthesis and is part of the amino acid cystine and thiamine, which are the building blocks of proteins. Sulfur is essential in the formation of oils and flavors, as well as for respiration and the synthesis and breakdown of fatty acids.

## Mobility in Soil

Sulfur is **immobile** within the cannabis plant, meaning it cannot be relocated from older tissue to newer growth once deposited. In the soil, sulfur exists primarily as sulfate (SO4 2-), which is moderately mobile and subject to leaching, especially in sandy soils. Sulfur availability is highest in acidic to neutral soils (pH 5.5-7.0) and decreases in alkaline conditions. Sulfur can also be added to the soil as elemental sulfur, which soil bacteria gradually convert to sulfate, but this process is slow and pH-dependent.

## Interactions with Other Nutrients

Sulfur has an important chemical interaction with [[cannabis-calcium-nutrient]]. When sulfur and calcium are combined in concentrated form, they form crude, insoluble gypsum (calcium sulfate) that settles as residue. This is why hydroponic fertilizers separate sulfur from calcium in an "A" container and a "B" container. Always dilute nutrient concentrates before combining the A and B solutions.

Excess calcium in the growing medium can interfere with sulfur uptake. Conversely, at high EC (electrical conductivity), plants take up more available sulfur, which blocks the uptake of other nutrients. Sulfur is also linked to cannabis-nitrogen|nitrogen metabolism -- both are needed for protein synthesis.

## Organic Sources

[[organic-fertilizer-sources-guide]] of sulfur include:

- Mushroom composts
- Most animal manures (well-rotted)
- Epsom salts (magnesium sulfate -- also provides magnesium)
- Gypsum (calcium sulfate -- also provides calcium)
- Compost
- Fish meal
- Kelp and seaweed products

Apply only well-rotted manures to avoid burning roots. Gypsum is an excellent source that adds both calcium and sulfur without affecting soil pH.

## Deficiency

Sulfur deficiency resembles cannabis-nitrogen|nitrogen deficiency, which can lead to misdiagnosis. According to literature, youngest leaves should yellow first (consistent with immobile nutrient behavior). However, scientific research at Canna Coco in the Netherlands has repeatedly shown that symptoms were most obvious in the older leaves.

### Symptoms

- Young leaves turn lime-green to yellowish
- As shortage progresses, leaves yellow interveinally and lack succulence
- Veins remain green
- Leaf stems and petioles turn purple
- Leaf tips can burn, darken, and hook downward
- Acute sulfur deficiency causes elongated stems that become woody at the base

### Deficiency Progression Timeline

1. Similar to nitrogen deficiency: older leaves turn a pale green
2. Leaf stems turn purple; more leaves turn pale green
3. Entire leaves turn pale yellow
4. Interveinal yellowing occurs
5. Acute deficiency causes more and more leaves to develop purple leaf stems and yellow leaves

### Treatment

- Fertilize with a hydroponic fertilizer containing sulfur
- Lower the pH to 5.5-6.0
- Add inorganic sulfur to a fertilizer containing magnesium sulfate (Epsom salts)
- Apply organic sources including mushroom composts and most animal manures
- Apply only well-rotted manures to avoid burning roots

## Toxicity

An excess of sulfur in the soil causes no problems if the EC is relatively low. At a high EC, the plants tend to take up more available sulfur which blocks uptake of other nutrients.

### Symptoms

- Overall smaller plant development
- Uniformly smaller, dark-green foliage
- Leaf tips and margins could discolor and burn when severe

### Treatment

Flush the growing medium with a very mild, complete fertilizer. Check the pH of the drainage solution. Correct the input pH to 6.0. Severe problems require flushing a minimum of three times the volume of water for the volume of the growing medium.

## Important Notes on Sulfur in Hydroponics

Hydroponic fertilizers separate sulfur from [[cannabis-calcium-nutrient]] in an "A" container and a "B" container. If combined in a concentrated form, sulfur and calcium will form crude, insoluble gypsum (calcium sulfate) and settle as residue to the bottom of the tank. Always dilute nutrient concentrates before combining the A and B solutions.

Sulfur is also used as a fungicide. Sulfur and lime-sulfur sprays are a good prophylactic against powdery mildew. However, avoid applying sulfur-based sprays when temperatures exceed 85F (29C) to avoid plant damage.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing sulfur deficiency with nitrogen deficiency (they look very similar)
- Adding elemental sulfur without understanding the slow bacterial conversion process
- Combining concentrated calcium and sulfur solutions in hydroponics (forms gypsum)
- Applying sulfur-based fungicides in hot conditions (above 85F/29C)
- Ignoring the pH when sulfur deficiency occurs -- high pH blocks sulfur uptake
- Using uncomposted manures that can burn roots and introduce pathogens
