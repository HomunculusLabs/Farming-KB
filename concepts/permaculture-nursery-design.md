# Permaculture Nursery Design

updated: 2026-04-18

The nursery is the engine of any permaculture system. Bill Mollison's [[permaculture-design]] course covers nursery design as a critical infrastructure element, with functional groupings, integration with other systems, and practical management techniques.

## Functional Groupings

### Seed and Propagation Area
- Seed storage (cool, dry, dark)
- Propagation benches with heat mats
- Mist systems for cuttings
- Hardening-off area (transition from protected to outdoor conditions)

### Growing-On Area
- Potting-up benches
- Shade house for young plants
- Open frames for hardening
- Space for grafting and budding

### Soil and Potting Area
- Compost area
- Potting soil mixing station
- Storage for pots, trays, labels
- Tool storage and cleaning

### Stock Ponds
- Ponds integrated with nursery for aquaculture
- Provide water for irrigation
- Provide nutrient-rich water (fish waste as fertilizer)
- Serve as microclimate moderators (temperature buffer)
- Can grow water plants and edible species

## Integration with Other Systems

### Aquaculture Integration
Stock ponds within the nursery provide:
- Irrigation water enriched with fish waste (nutrient cycling)
- Additional food production (fish, water plants)
- Temperature moderation (water bodies reduce frost risk)
- Fire control (water source for fire fighting)
- Habitat for beneficial insects and amphibians

### Animal Integration
- Chickens in the nursery for pest control (insects in potting soil)
- Ducks for slug control
- Geese as weeders between nursery rows
- Rabbit manure as direct fertilizer for potting soil

### Compost Integration
- Nursery waste (prunings, spent potting soil) feeds compost
- Compost feeds potting soil
- Worm farms process organic waste into vermicompost
- Compost teas for liquid fertilization

## [[nursery-management]]

### Propagation Techniques
- **Seed propagation**: Direct sowing, stratification, scarification
- **Cuttings**: Softwood, semi-hardwood, hardwood
- **Division**: For clumping perennials, grasses, bamboos
- **Layering**: Air layering for difficult-to-root species
- **Grafting**: For fruit trees, combining rootstock and scion
- **Tissue culture**: For large-scale production of uniform plants

### Potting Mix Design
Permaculture nurseries use locally sourced materials:
- Compost as base
- Sand or perlite for drainage
- [[biochar]] for water retention and nutrient availability
- Worm castings for microbial activity
- Local mineral sources (rock dust, clay)
- Coconut coir or leaf mold for organic matter

### Hardening Off
The critical transition from protected nursery to field conditions:
- Gradual exposure to sun, wind, and temperature fluctuations
- Reduce watering frequency
- Increase light exposure progressively
- Protect from frost if moving from greenhouse to field

## Species Selection for Nursery

### Multi-Purpose Species
Priority species for permaculture nurseries:
- **Nitrogen fixers**: Leguminous trees for soil improvement ([[acacia]], mesquite, [[carob]], [[tagasaste]], albizia)
- **Food producers**: Fruit trees, nut trees, berry bushes, [[perennial-vegetables]]
- **Pioneer species**: Fast-growing species for site restoration
- **Windbreak species**: For shelter and microclimate creation
- **Animal fodder**: Species for livestock feed systems
- **Medicinal plants**: For home health care
- **Bamboo**: For construction, erosion control, and food

### Seed Saving
- Maintain local seed stocks adapted to site conditions
- Select for disease resistance, productivity, and flavor
- Store seeds properly (cool, dry, labeled with date)
- Participate in seed exchange networks

## Physical Design Considerations

### Location
- Close to water source
- Protected from strong winds
- Good drainage
- Accessible for deliveries and plant distribution
- Near Zone 1 (frequent attention needed)

### Layout
- Work flow: potting area -> propagation -> growing-on -> dispatch
- Shade house oriented to avoid hottest afternoon sun
- Paths wide enough for wheelbarrows
- Bench heights appropriate for working comfort
- Clear labeling system

### Structures
- Shade house (30-50% shade cloth)
- Greenhouse or cold frame for frost protection
- Potting shed with weather protection
- Storage for materials and tools
- Compost bays

## Commercial Nursery Considerations

### Local Economy Integration
The permaculture nursery serves the local community:
- Provide plants adapted to local conditions
- Supply food-producing species, not just ornamentals
- Offer advice on plant placement and care
- Sell mulch, compost, and other soil amendments
- Conduct workshops and training

### Scaling
From backyard to commercial:
- Start with species you know well
- Expand as demand develops
- Specialize in locally-adapted varieties
- Develop a reputation for quality and reliability
