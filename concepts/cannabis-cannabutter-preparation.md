# Cannabis Cannabutter Preparation

## Overview

[[oner-cannabis-edibles-infusion-dosing]]. According to Cervantes, cannabutter is simple to make, retains about 80 percent of the cannabinoids present in the original foliage, and can be processed in large batches for storage in the refrigerator or freezer. The psychoactive cannabinoids dissolve in butter, making them available for absorption by the body when consumed orally.

## Scientific Basis

Cannabinoids are lipophilic (fat-soluble) molecules that readily dissolve into fats and oils. When cannabis is heated in butter, THC and other cannabinoids transfer from the plant material into the butterfat. The water in the recipe helps regulate temperature and prevents burning, while the long simmering time ensures thorough extraction. After cooling, the butter separates from the water and can be used in any recipe calling for butter.

## Cannabutter Recipe

### Ingredients

- 1.6 quarts (1.5 L) of water
- 4 ounces (112 gm) of marijuana leaf
- 1 pound (454 gm) of butter or margarine

### Instructions

1. Heat 1.6 quarts (1.5 L) of water in a large pan on the stove
2. Add marijuana leaf and butter (or margarine), stir thoroughly
3. Bring liquid to a boil
4. Put lid on pot and simmer on low heat for 2 hours, stirring occasionally
5. Strain mix through a colander, catching liquid in a container below
6. Press wet foliage to extract all liquid from the plant material
7. Pour 0.5 quart (0.5 L) of boiling water over wet leaf to wash out remaining butter
8. Press leaves to squeeze out all liquid possible
9. Let mix stand and cool for 1-2 hours; butter separates and rises to top
10. Speed separation by placing the hot mix into the refrigerator
11. Pour off and discard water, retain the cannabis butter on top

### Storage

Cannabutter can be stored in the refrigerator or frozen for longer-term preservation. Making large batches at once saves time and ensures a consistent supply for cooking.

## Usage in Cooking

Use cannabutter in any recipe that calls for butter. This opens up a wide range of edible possibilities including baked goods, sauteed dishes, and any preparation that incorporates butter.

Important dosage notes:

- Start with small portions until familiar with dosage and effects
- The butter is quite potent due to the 80 percent cannabinoid retention rate
- Edible cannabis effects take longer to onset than smoking but last longer
- Effects are more body-centered and can be more intense than smoked cannabis
- Be careful when consuming; the butter is deceptively potent
