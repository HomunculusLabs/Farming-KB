# Fungal Sulfur Dioxide Effects on Decomposition

## Overview

Sulfur dioxide (SO2) is an atmospheric pollutant derived primarily from the combustion of
fossil fuels that has significant effects on fungal communities and their capacity for
decomposition. As a component of acid rain, SO2 influences fungal respiration, growth,
spore germination, community structure, and decomposition processes. Understanding SO2
effects on fungi is critical for assessing the
[[dighton-fungal-responses-climate-change]] and
predicting ecosystem responses to air quality changes.

## Effects on Fungal Growth and Spore Germination

Dursun et al. (1996a) and Boddy et al. (1996) demonstrated that sulfite at
environmentally realistic concentrations between 12.5 and 100 micromolar had negative
effects on the growth of mycelia and germination of spores of Mycena galopus, Phoma
exigua, Cladosporium cladosporioides, and Aureobasidium pullulans. The effects of sulfite
were greatest on mycelia in terms of both growth and respiration, while spores were found
to be more resistant to sulfites than vegetative mycelium.

This differential sensitivity between mycelia and spores has important ecological
implications: while established fungal populations may be suppressed by SO2, the spore
bank may persist and allow recolonization when pollution levels decrease. The
decomposition of Sitka spruce leaf litter in the presence of pure cultures of these fungi
was also significantly reduced in the presence of sulfite, directly linking SO2 exposure
to reduced decomposition capacity.

## Species-Specific Differences in SO2 Sensitivity

Not all fungal species respond equally to SO2 exposure. Dursun et al. (1996b) showed that
40 nanoliters per liter of SO2 had differential effects on the respiration of different
fungal species, and this effect differed between leaf litter species. Although SO2
reduced respiration of Mycena galopus on leaf litters, this reduction was only
statistically significant for Sitka spruce (Picea sitchensis) and after 10 weeks for
hazel (Corylus avellana).

There was effectively little reduction in respiration on Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris)
and ash (Fraxinus excelsior). The greatest effect of SO2 was on Sitka spruce litter
decomposition, in which respiration was reduced by over one-half. Respiration of Phoma
exigua, Cladosporium cladosporioides, and Aureobasidium pullulans was unaffected by this
concentration of SO2 on any of the leaf litter species tested.

## Substrate-Dependent Effects

The interaction between SO2 sensitivity and litter substrate type represents a complex
ecological relationship. The differential effects of SO2 on decomposition depending on
litter species suggest that the chemical composition of the litter either buffers or
exacerbates the toxic effects of sulfite on fungal metabolism. Litters with higher buffer
capacity or different organic acid profiles may protect fungal cells from sulfite
toxicity to varying degrees.

## Effects on Mycorrhizal Communities

Sulfur dioxide fumigation studies have demonstrated significant effects on
ectomycorrhizal-ecology|ectomycorrhizal community structure. In a field fumigation experiment where SO2 and ozone
were released over circular plots containing Scots pine, Sitka spruce, and Norway spruce,
multivariate analysis revealed that the only trend in mycorrhizal community change was
from SO2 fumigation on Scots pine, where a reduction in the occurrence of Paxillus
involutus mycorrhizae occurred (Shaw et al., 1992, 1993).

Paradoxically, Paxillus involutus fruited more abundantly in Scots pine plots that
received high SO2 loading, the converse of the root colonization pattern. This
discrepancy between fruit body production and root colonization highlights the complexity
of interpreting SO2 effects based on fruit body surveys alone, and underscores the
importance of direct root examination.

## Mechanisms of SO2 Toxicity

Sulfur dioxide dissolves in water to form sulfite (SO3 2-), which is the primary toxic
agent affecting fungal metabolism. Sulfite can interfere with enzyme function, disrupt
cellular redox balance, and damage cell membranes. The sensitivity of different fungal
species to sulfite likely reflects differences in their capacity for sulfite
detoxification, which may involve oxidation to sulfate or reduction to hydrogen sulfide.

The effects of acidifying pollutants on mycorrhizal formation operate through two
mechanisms: reduction in photosynthetic capacity reducing carbohydrate supply to roots,
and acid-induced increases in toxic metal ion availability in soil. Both mechanisms
ultimately reduce the energy available to support mycorrhizal symbiosis.

## Liming as Remediation

Experimental liming studies have been conducted as remediation treatments for acidified
forest soils. Veerkamp et al. (1997) found that liming increased the number of fungal
species but the resultant community was more similar to those found in deciduous
woodlands than in coniferous woodlands. The rationale for this shift was that elevated pH
increased nitrogen availability and stimulated decomposition of high C:N ratio woody
material, making coniferous wood more similar to higher resource quality angiosperm wood.

Antibus and Linkins (1992) showed that liming reduced acid phosphatase activity of the
mycorrhizal community, possibly through synergistic effects of increased nitrogen and
phosphorus availability reducing the need for phosphatase enzyme production. These
remediation studies demonstrate that the effects of SO2 pollution on fungal communities
are at least partially reversible through management interventions.

## Broader Ecosystem Implications

The effects of SO2 on fungal decomposition have cascading consequences for ecosystem
function. Reduced fungal respiration and decomposition rates slow [[fungal-decomposition-nutrient-cycling]],
potentially limiting nutrient availability for plant growth. Changes in mycorrhizal
community structure affect plant nutrient uptake capacity and competitive relationships.
These findings underscore the importance of maintaining air quality for the preservation
of functional [[fungal-ecosystem-roles]] in forest ecosystems.
