## Clarified Fats

Fat from homegrown meat or dairy is fundamentally different from
factory-processed fats. Saving and clarifying fat turns what many
consider waste into a valuable cooking resource that lasts for years.

### Making Clarified Fat (Lard)

Save all fats from butchering and store in the freezer until ready to
process. Cut the fat into small pieces and place in a saucepan. Simmer
on low heat for several hours until fully liquefied. For flavored fat,
add onions, carrots, leeks, turnips, herbs, and spices along with salt
and pepper — this creates a ready-to-use base for soups and stews.

Once liquefied, pour through cheesecloth or a fine mesh strainer into
clean containers. Allow to cool and solidify. Properly clarified fat
stored in a cool, dark place will last for a year or more. In the
refrigerator, it keeps indefinitely.

### Making Ghee (Clarified Butter)

Ghee has been a staple in Indian cooking for thousands of years and has
exceptional shelf stability — it can sit at room temperature for months
without spoiling. To make ghee, melt butter at very low temperature
until completely liquid. Do not stir. Raise heat slightly until the
butter steams gently but does not brown. The milk solids will rise to
the surface — skim these off carefully. Continue until the butter is
golden and completely clear. Pour into a clean container and allow to
solidify. Once cool and solid, seal tightly.

Ghee is ideal for cream sauces, frying, and any application where
butter flavor is desired without the milk solids burning.

### Uses for Clarified Fats

- Frying and sautéing (higher smoke point than unclarified fats)
