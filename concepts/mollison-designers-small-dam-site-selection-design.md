# Small Dam Site Selection and Design

[[backyard-water-harvesting]] in
permaculture-designers-manual-bamboo, Bill
Mollison provides detailed guidance on selecting suitable dam sites and
designing storage systems that maximise water availability while minimising
cost, maintenance, and ecological impact. Properly sited and constructed
small dams provide irrigation, stock water, fire protection, [[permaculture-earthworks]] strategies and integrate
naturally with [[biochar-and-fungi]] and [[soil-testing]]:** Simple field tests include the roll test (squeezing
  moist soil into a ribbon; longer ribbons indicate higher clay content)
  and the pond test (filling a small hole with water and observing the
  seepage rate).

- **Seepage problems:** Sandy, gravelly, or fractured rock sites may
  require clay lining, bentonite treatment, or geomembrane liners to
  achieve water retention.

### Topography

The ideal dam site has a narrow valley or depression with a broad upstream
area. This configuration maximises water storage volume while minimising the
length (and cost) of the dam wall. The valley should be relatively flat
immediately upstream of the dam site to maximise storage depth and area.

The slope of the valley floor upstream affects storage volume. For every
unit of depth, a broader flat area upstream stores more water than a steep
narrow area. Contour surveys and simple level measurements are essential for
accurate volume estimation.

### Spillway Design

Every dam must have an adequately sized spillway to safely pass flood flows
without overtopping the dam wall. Overtopping is the primary cause of dam
failure. Mollison emphasises the following spillway principles:

- **Location:** Spillways should be excavated into solid, non-erodible
  material at one end of the dam wall, never on the wall itself or over
  fill material.

- **Capacity:** The spillway must be sized to pass the maximum probable
  flood flow for the catchment area. For small rural dams, a common
  guideline is to size the spillway for a 1-in-50-year flood event.

- **Velocity control:** Spillway surfaces should be protected against
  erosion using rock, grass, or concrete. High-velocity flows require
  energy dissipation structures at the spillway outlet.

- **Freeboard:** The dam wall crest must be at least 0.5 to 1 metre above
  the spillway crest to provide freeboard against wave action and
  debris blockage.

## Storage Volume Calculations

Estimating the storage capacity of a proposed dam is essential for
determining whether it will meet water supply needs. Methods include:

- **Contour survey method:** Surveying the storage area at regular depth
  intervals and calculating the volume of each contour band using the
  trapezoidal rule. This is the most accurate method for significant dams.

- **Simplified formula:** For approximately rectangular or conical
  storage areas, Volume = (Surface Area x Maximum Depth) / 3 to 4
  (using 3 for conical shapes, 4 for rectangular shapes).

- **Water requirement matching:** Compare estimated storage volume with
  annual water demand (irrigation, stock, household) and expected inflow
  (rainfall, runoff) to determine if the proposed dam is adequately sized.
