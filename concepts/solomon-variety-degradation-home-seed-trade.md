## Regional Adaptation and Variety Maintenance

One positive aspect of variety degradation is that varieties grown in a
specific region for many generations tend to adapt to local conditions. A
gardener who consistently saves seed from the best-performing plants in their
own garden will, over several seasons, develop a locally adapted strain that
performs better than the original commercial variety. Solomon calls this
process informal plant breeding, and he considers it one of the most valuable
skills a home gardener can develop. The key is to always save seed from
multiple outstanding plants, never from a single specimen, to maintain genetic
diversity while selecting for desirable traits.
