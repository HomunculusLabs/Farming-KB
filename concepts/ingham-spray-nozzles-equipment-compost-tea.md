## Pump Selection

The pump must be organism-friendly. Diaphragm pumps are clearly
better for compost tea application, though some centrifugal pumps
do not harm organisms significantly. Organisms can be reduced by
50 percent when passed through bad pumps. Gentle-action pumps are
preferred. A good recirculation, agitation, or aeration system in
the tank is also important. Recirculation hoses serve double duty
by washing off any developing biofilm while spraying.

## Recommended Sprayer Types

Solo backpack sprayers are recommended because the nozzles have
large diameter openings and the pumps do not harm the organisms.
Boom sprayers are acceptable when equipped with floodjet nozzles
in the largest available size, with all screens removed. Flat fan
nozzles tend to plug. Diaphragm pumps with boomjet nozzles work
well at 35 psi with approximately 45 feet width of spray. Gravity
feed from a PVC boom with drilled holes is another viable option.

## Fogger Systems

Foggers operate on the venturi principle, where a fan pushes air
through a drum at approximately 74 mph, causing liquid to be sucked
out through a 6mm pipe. Droplet sizes range from 8.5 to 30 microns.
Venturi sprayers should be turned down to 30 to 60 psi for compost
tea application. The air and droplet mixture fills the target area
with fog, and the liquid floats onto leaf surfaces gently.

## Equipment to Avoid

Electrostatic sprayers kill organisms and should not be used for
compost tea application. High-pressure systems that create small
droplet sizes are problematic because the drops do not protect
organisms from UV and the impact pressure on leaves is too high.
Avoid any system that requires filtering through fine mesh screens
