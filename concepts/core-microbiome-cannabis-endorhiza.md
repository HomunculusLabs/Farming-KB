## Defining the Core Community

A core microbiome is defined as the set of microbial taxa found in all or nearly all samples within a given compartment. For the Cannabis endorhiza, Winston et al. identified six consistent taxonomic groups across all cultivars tested.

These included Pseudomonas, Cellvibrio, Oxalobacteraceae, Xanthomonadaceae, Actinomycetales, and Sphingobacteriales. All but Cellvibrio are well-established endophytic bacteria found in other plant systems as well.

The consistency of this core across five distinct cultivars suggests strong selection pressure by the host plant. Despite significant variation in soil chemistry between growing sites, the same six groups dominated endorhiza communities in every plant sampled.

## Pseudomonas as a Dominant Taxon

Pseudomonas species were among the most abundant and consistent members of the Cannabis endorhiza core. These bacteria are prolific plant colonizers known for producing growth-promoting hormones, siderophores, and [[endophytic-mycorrhizal-induced-systemic-resistance]] resistance, and suppression of soil-borne pathogens.

In the Cannabis system, Pseudomonas abundance in the endorhiza was significantly structured by cultivar identity. Different Cannabis strains hosted distinct relative abundances of Pseudomonas operational taxonomic units (OTUs), even when grown in identical soils.

## The Role of Cellvibrio

Cellvibrio was a notable member of the Cannabis endorhiza core community. Unlike the other core members, Cellvibrio is primarily known as an aerobic cellulolytic bacterium rather than a classic endophyte.

Its consistent presence in root tissue suggests it may play a role in degrading plant cell wall components during root colonization. Cellulolytic activity could facilitate entry into root tissue or provide a competitive advantage within the root environment.

The presence of Cellvibrio in the endorhiza but not as prominently in the rhizosphere indicates selection for cellulolytic capability during the transition from soil to root interior. This is an unusual feature of the Cannabis microbiome compared to other studied crops.

## Oxalobacteraceae and Xanthomonadaceae

Oxalobacteraceae and Xanthomonadaceae were consistent core members found across all Cannabis cultivars. Both families are common plant-associated bacteria with diverse metabolic capabilities.

Oxalobacteraceae includes genera such as Herbaspirillum and Massilia, which are known root colonizers of various crops. Some Herbaspirillum species fix nitrogen and produce plant growth-promoting phytohormones like indole-3-acetic acid.

Xanthomonadaceae members in the Cannabis endorhiza likely include Stenotrophomonas and related genera. These bacteria are recognized for their plant-growth-promoting properties and their ability to suppress fungal pathogens through antibiotic production.

## Actinomycetales in Root Tissue

Actinomycetales, particularly Streptomyces and related genera, formed part of the consistent endorhiza core. Actinomycetes are well-known for their production of bioactive [[fukuoka-microbial-ecology-decomposition]].

Their persistence in the Cannabis endorhiza across all cultivars suggests they provide a baseline defense function. The diverse [[soil-protozoa-nutrient-cycling-and-mineralization]] the processing of root exudates and decaying organic matter.

Their consistent presence in endorhiza tissue across cultivars is noteworthy. While less studied than Pseudomonas or Actinomycetales as endophytes, Sphingobacteriales appear to be reliable root colonizers in Cannabis.

## Cultivar-Specific Modulations of the Core

While the core six groups were present in all cultivars, their relative abundances varied significantly between strains. This cultivar-specific modulation suggests host genetic factors influence the composition of even the most conserved microbiome members.

For example, Methylophilus was highly abundant in the endorhiza of Bookoo Kush, comprising 13% of the [[sphingomonas-wittichii-cannabis-endorhiza-strain-specificity]] showed cultivar-specific enrichment in Maui Wowie. This species can metabolize phenazine-1-carboxylic acid, suggesting potential links between secondary metabolite production and microbiome assembly.

## Comparison to Other Plant Core Microbiomes

The Cannabis endorhiza core shares several features with core microbiomes described in other plant species. Arabidopsis thaliana, rice, and maize all show conserved Proteobacteria-dominant core communities in root tissue.

However, the prominence of Cellvibrio appears relatively unique to Cannabis among well-studied crops. This may reflect the distinct root chemistry or exudate profile of Cannabis compared to model plant systems.

The strong representation of Actinomycetales in the Cannabis endorhiza core is also noteworthy. While present in other plant microbiomes, Actinomycetales are not always among the most consistent root colonizers across all cultivars.

## Implications for Cultivation

Understanding the core Cannabis microbiome has practical implications for commercial cultivation. Microbial inoculants containing core community members could help establish beneficial root associations more reliably.
