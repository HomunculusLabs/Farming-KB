Cannabinoids show potential anti-cancer activity against colon cancer through multiple mechanisms, including COX-2 enzyme inhibition and CB1 receptor-mediated tumor growth suppression. The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-cannabinoid-terpene-synergy]] assigns a CHI value of 4 from 3 studies reviewed.

## Understanding Colon Cancer

Colon cancer develops in the epithelial cells lining the large intestine, from the junction between large and small intestine to the anus. Signs and symptoms depend on location and spread, including abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, narrow stools, and unexplained changes in bowel movements.

Risk factors include:
- Colon polyps, irritable bowel syndrome, ulcerative colitis
- Tobacco smoking, alcohol use
- Age over 50, male gender, obesity, sedentary lifestyle
- Environmental carcinogens, African American heritage
- Prior radiation therapy

The U.S. National Cancer Institute estimated over 100,000 new colon cancer patients and more than 50,000 fatalities from rectal and colon cancer combined in 2010.

## Cannabis and Colon Cancer

### COX-2 Inhibition Mechanism

Both Uppsala University (2011) and University of Bristol (2005) experiments indicated that cannabinoids may inhibit the enzyme cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), a well-established target for cancer prevention and treatment. The Uppsala study found that THC, THC-acid, CBD, CBD-acid, CBG, and CBG-acid all inhibited the COX enzyme in colon cancer cells.

### CB1 Receptor and Tumor Suppression

Vanderbilt University Medical Center (2008) conducted genetic and pharmacologic studies in mice demonstrating that activation of CB1 reduced intestinal tumor growth. This suggests endogenous cannabinoid receptors (CB1) as a possible target for a novel mechanism for prevention and treatment of colon cancer using the body's own anandamide.

### Anandamide-Induced Cell Death

The University of Bristol (2005) found that anandamide induced colorectal carcinoma cell death through a mechanism that was neither apoptosis nor necrosis -- suggesting a unique pathway of cannabinoid-mediated anti-cancer activity.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

THC, THC-acid, CBD, CBD-acid, CBG, CBG-acid, and endogenous anandamide all inhibited colon cancer cell proliferation. CB1 activation has been shown to reduce colon cancer in mice.

- **Sativa strains**: Higher THC:CBD ratio tends to activate CB1 in greater proportions
- **Indica strains**: Generally lower THC:CBD ratio; favor CB2
- **Raw cannabis juice**: Contains non-psychoactive THC-acid, CBD-acid, and CBG-acid from fresh leaves of both indica and sativa strains

Isolated synthetic cannabinoid medications (Sativex, Dronabinol, Marinol, Nabilone) activate both CB1 and CB2.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Colon Cancer

A study of 637 newly confirmed colon cancer patients by Australian psychiatrists identified a unique psychological framework:
- Denial and repression of anger and other negative emotions
- Commitment to prevailing social norms ("nice" or "good" person appearance)
- Suppression of reactions that may offend others
- Avoidance of conflict

Aggravating factors: suppressed emotions, repression of anger, seeking approval, suppression of reactions. Supporting factors: appropriate emotional release, raising self-esteem, adhering to one's own ideals and standards.

## Supporting Herbs

- **Basil**: Thwarted chemical attempts to produce stomach cancer in rodents
- **Caraway**: Controlled lipid peroxidation and antioxidant homeostasis, preventing chemically induced colon cancer in rats (optimal dose: 60 mg/kg)
- **Cardamom**: Aqueous suspensions showed protective effects on colonic tissue
- **Cumin**: Traditional digestive and anti-cancer spice
- **Nigella**: Broad anti-cancer properties
- **Saffron**: Anti-tumor activity demonstrated
- **Turmeric**: Curcumin's well-established anti-cancer mechanisms
