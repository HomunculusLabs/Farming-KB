## Permaculture Climate-Specific Design Strategies

Climate-specific design from Mollison's Permaculture Two and field implementations. The fundamental principle: "Design must be climate-specific. What works in humid cool temperate fails in arid lands." See [[permaculture-design-methods]] for the design toolkit and [[edge-effect-and-microclimate]] for microclimate creation.

## Arid Lands Strategies (Mollison, Ch. 5)

### Home Gardens for Desert Survival

Around the house (wiltja/shelter), where organic wastes accumulate and water from roofs/washing is available:

- **Sheet mulch is the key technique** — conserves water, reduces salt and carbonate accumulation, buffers pH
- **Trickle irrigation plus mulch** reduces evaporation by 80%+ compared to bare soil
- **Ploughing increases alkalinity** to intolerable levels — never till desert soils
- **Use all available mulch**: bones, ashes, cattle droppings, leaves, old clothes, cardboard

### Mulch Species for Arid Zones

Useful shade and mulch trees: mulga (Acacia aneura), tamarisk, desert oaks, casuarinas, cultivated bamboo, Paulownia, Acacia albida, Leucaena.

### House Climate Modification in Hot Climates

- Deciduous vine trellis (grape) to the north provides summer shade, admits winter sun
- Evergreen vine arbour to the south (Tecoma vine) blocks hot winds
- Tall tamarisk and white cedar screen cold SE winds and provide mulch
- Vines on walls or trellis set from walls reduce heat gain/loss
- Soil-covered roofs planted with ice-plants, cacti, succulents yield cool air in summer

### Erosion Control on Dry Slopes

- **Net and pan pattern**: Small containment basins on contour with diversion drains above
- **Cross-slope logs**: Staked on slight downhill grade so water zig-zags across erosion face
- **Pioneer plantings**: Pampas, bamboo, and root-mat pioneers on very steep slopes
- Avoid massive contour trenches — planned chiselling and planting achieves more permanent results

### Broad-Scale Planting for Desert Climate Modification

- Plant date palm, jujube, cork oak, pistachio, plum, tamarisk, carob, mesquite, Paulownia in trial niches
- Stone and desert pines on ridges
- **Runoff ratio**: 16-20:1 (runoff area to garden area). A dome of 8 hectares can support a 1-acre garden
- Road drains can be led to walled and chiselled enclosures planted with trees

### Desert Livestock

- **Ducks, hens, guinea fowl, pigeons** as primary protein sources
- Guinea fowl utilize many insect foods and natural seeds in mulga areas
- Poultry in mixed orchards greatly reduce fruit fly and termite larvae
- Pigeons in dovecotes are immune to fox predation
- Fish in tanks/dams control algae and mosquitoes; yabbies and freshwater mussels as filters

### Aquatic Species for Arid Zones

- Native wild rice, water lily (Nymphea gigantea) — stalks, tubers, seeds all edible
- Nardoo (fern), native arrowroot
- Trapa (water chestnut) and Eleocharis reduce summer evaporation from open tanks
- Sweet rush provides shoots and bulbs

### Termite-Proofing Timber

- Cold-soak (butt-soak) treatment in Tanalith (copper-chrome-arsenic salts)
- 3-day soak in hot weather
- Trim poles to length before treatment to minimize treated waste
- Treated timber is safe to handle but must never be burned

## Humid Tropics Strategies

### Multi-Strata Systems

- Design for maximum vertical stacking: canopy, understory, shrub, herb, ground cover, root, vine layers
- Rapid biomass cycling — organic matter decomposes quickly
- Tree crops are primary production (not annual vegetables)
- Poultry under tree crops for pest control and manure
- Bamboo as universal building material, mulch source, and windbreak

### Tropical Soil Management

- **Never leave soil bare** — constant cover crops or mulch
- Sheet mulch works but decomposes very quickly; needs constant renewal
- Green manure crops essential for fertility on depleted tropical soils
- Chickens convert kitchen waste to manure within days in tropical heat
- Living mulch (Dolichos, kudzu, sweet potato) protects soil between crops

### Sea Coasts (Section 5.3)

- Salt-tolerant species for coastal zones: casuarina, coconut, pandanus, mangrove
- Sand dune stabilization with spinifex, pigface, beach morning glory
- Tidal stone traps for fish (traditional technique)
- Seagrass beds (Zostera, Posidonia) protect coastline and provide mulch

## Creating Small Climates (Section 5.4)

### Windbreak Design for Climate Modification

From Mollison's detailed treatment:
- Windbreak height determines protected zone: 10-15x height downwind
- **Permeable windbreaks** (30-50% density) are more effective than solid barriers — they reduce wind speed without creating turbulence
- Multiple staggered rows better than single dense row
- Species selection: fast-growing pioneer protected by slower permanent species
