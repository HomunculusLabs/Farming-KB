# Fungal Cellulose Degradation

Cellulose is the most abundant organic polymer on Earth and the primary
structural component of plant cell walls. The enzymatic decomposition of
cellulose by fungi is fundamental to global carbon cycling and is a
cornerstone of both [[gourmet-medicinal-mushroom-substrate-preparation]] utilization.

## The Cellulase Complex

Knowledge of cellulose-degrading enzymes is based mainly on studies of
Trichoderma spp., the most prolific sources of cellulases known. When
enzymes from wood-rotting basidiomycetes have been screened for cellulases,
their composition closely resembled those of Trichoderma reesei. Cellulase
is a complex of synergistic enzyme activities:

- **Endoglucanases:** Hydrolyze internal beta-1,4-glycosidic bonds within
  the cellulose chain, creating new chain ends and disrupting crystalline
  structure
- **Exoglucanases (cellobiohydrolases):** Processively cleave cellobiose
  units from the reducing and non-reducing ends of the cellulose chain,
  composed of a cellulose-binding domain linked to a catalytic domain
- **Beta-glucosidases:** Convert cellobiose (a glucose dimer) to glucose,
  preventing end-product inhibition of the other cellulase components

These enzymes act in synergy to depolymerize cellulose fibrils, releasing
glucose and cellobiose. Glucose is readily taken up by the fungus, providing
carbon for energy and growth. Five endoglucanases, one exoglucanase, and two
beta-glucosidases have been identified from wood-rotting basidiomycetes.

## Regulation of Cellulase Production

Fungal cellulase production is subject to sophisticated regulatory controls.
Glucose represses and cellobiose or cellulose stimulates the production of
exo- and endocellulases through feedback mechanisms. Catabolite repression
by glucose and sucrose occurs at concentrations of 1 g/L, while induction
of cellulase occurs with as little as 1 mg/L of cellobiose or cellulose.

Genes for exo- and endocellulases have been isolated and characterized from
multiple fungal species, providing improved understanding of the biochemical
mechanisms involved. This molecular understanding has enabled genetic
