# Permaculture Zone 0 House Design

Zone 0 is the house or village itself -- the origin from which all permaculture design radiates outward. The available energy in Zone 0 is human, animal, piped-in, or created on site. To conserve energy, work, and time, components are placed in concentric zones of decreasing management intensity.

## What Belongs in Zone 0

In this zone belongs:
- Good [[permaculture-building-design|house design]]
- Attached glasshouse or shadehouse
- Integration of living components: sod roof, vines, trellis, pot plants, roof gardens
- Companion animals (poultry, rabbits, pigeons, guinea pigs)
- Rainwater catchment tanks

In some climates, many structures are formed of the natural environment and will in time return to it (bamboo and rattan, wattle and daub, thatch, earth-covered or sheltered structures).

## Zone 0 as Energy Centre

The principle governing Zone 0 is energy conservation. The house is where human and animal energy is concentrated; everything needed frequently (herbs, seedlings, eggs, water) should be within a few steps. Components needing continual observation, frequent visits, and work input are placed within 6 m (20 feet) or so of the home.

Within this small radius, householders can produce most of the food necessary to existence. Techniques include:
- Complete mulching
- Intensive pruning of trees
- Annuals with fast replacement of crop
- Full land use
- Nutrient recycling of household wastes

## The House as a Living System

### Energy Flows
To design for greatest energy efficiency, we need to understand energy flows through the house:
- **Input**: Solar radiation, water, food, human labour
- **Output**: Heat, greywater, sewage, food waste
- **Cycling**: Every output should be an input to another system

### Water
- Rainwater collected from every roof
- 22,500 litres provides a family with all needed water (drinking, showers, cooking, modest garden) for a year
- Hot water from solar collectors or black pipe on roof
- Greywater routed to garden mulch basins or reed beds

### Waste
- Kitchen scraps feed poultry, rabbits, or compost
- Dry toilet or composting toilet where no septic system exists
- All organic waste returned to soil

### Food Production
- Culinary herbs just outside the kitchen door
- Seedling trays and mother plants for cuttings
- Small domestic and quiet animals (fish, rabbits, pigeons, guinea pigs)
- Trellis crop on walls and fences for temperature moderation and food

## Climate-Specific Design

### Cool Climates
The greatest cost in house maintenance is space and water heating (up to 80% of domestic energy costs). Both settlement design and house design are critical conservation factors:
- 30-100% glazing on sunward walls depending on latitude
- Thermal mass floors (concrete slab or mud brick) insulated at edges
- Attached glasshouse for winter heat, summer ventilation, and year-round food production
- Clerestory windows heat rear rooms
- Vine-covered trellis on shade side provides cool air source

### Desert Climates
- Internal courtyards with shade
- Earth tunnels (1 m deep, 20 m long) for cool air
- Solar chimneys for induced cross-ventilation
- Attached shadehouse as the summer living area
- Winter kitchen opens onto shadehouse (summer kitchen)

### Tropical Climates
- Elevated structures for airflow
- Broad overhanging eaves
- Permeable walls (screens, louvres)
- Heavy vine coverage on sunward walls
- Earth-sheltered construction for hurricane resistance

## The Integrated House-Garden System

The house and garden function as one system. Mollison presents layouts where:
- The house accepts all water and wastes
- The garden provides most mulch and fodder
- House scraps plus garden produce feed animals
- Animals return manure to garden
- Trellis crop moderates house temperature
- Cool air from trellis enters living spaces
- The kitchen opens to both indoor and outdoor food production areas

## Retrofit

For existing houses, Mollison emphasises that retrofits are cost-effective:
- Glass and trellis are durable; costs amortise in 1-3 years
- Attached shadehouses save up to 80% of fuel energy
- Solar chimneys, earth tunnels, and vine trellis can be added to most structures
- Water tanks can be added to any roof
