# Hamilton Seasonal Gardening Calendar

## Overview

The gardening year is divided into four main seasons, each with specific tasks across the ornamental garden, vegetable garden, fruit garden, and greenhouse. Regional climatic variation across the US (32 distinct regions) affects timing significantly. The calendar serves as a guide rather than a rigid rulebook; experienced gardeners adapt based on local conditions.

## Seasonal Framework

- **Spring**: March through May — soil preparation, planting, early propagation
- **Summer**: June through August — growth management, harvesting, pest control
- **Autumn**: September through November — harvest completion, soil protection, planting bare-root stock
- **Winter**: December through February — planning, maintenance, pruning, early indoor sowing

## Spring Tasks

### Ornamental Garden
- Early spring: rake lawn with spring-tine rake, begin mowing, sow/lay new lawns, feed borders with blood/fish/bone meal, lift and divide perennials, prune hybrid bush roses, finish planting bare-root deciduous trees and shrubs, start outside sowing of hardy annuals
- Mid-spring: plant bare-root evergreens, prune shrubs flowering on one-year-old wood immediately after flowering, plant pond marsh/aquatic plants, take cuttings of overwintered half-hardy perennials, plant tubs and hanging baskets, spray roses if black spot has been a problem
- Late spring: support tall perennials, trim ground cover after flowering, plant out half-hardy annuals after all frost danger passes, start softwood cuttings, control aphids and caterpillars

### Vegetable Garden
- Early spring: feed spring cabbages with dried blood or [[hamilton-kelp-and-seaweed-in-organic-gardening]], sow peas/beets/green beans under cloches, sow leeks/Brussels sprouts/cabbages in stale seedbed, plant sea kale, shallots, garlic, potatoes, Jerusalem artichokes
- Mid-spring: plant asparagus crowns, sow sweet corn (under polyethylene or in greenhouse), sow main crop Florence fennel/peas/okra/turnips/kohlrabi/carrots/beets/cauliflower/broccoli/kale/chard, plant out globe artichokes/green beans/onions
- Late spring: finish planting squash and fruiting vegetables under cloches, plant out leeks, mound soil around potatoes, transplant celery/sweet corn/green and runner beans, erect carrot fly barriers

### Fruit Garden
- Early spring: feed currants with rock potash (red/white) and blood/fish/bone (black), check netting, remove winter fig protection, graft apples/pears/plums, hand-pollinate blossoms under fabric
- Late spring: water fruit bushes and trees, mulch strawberries with straw and net against birds, thin gooseberries for dessert fruit, put out codling moth traps

### Greenhouse
- Throughout spring: ventilate on sunny days, close at night, keep atmosphere fairly dry
- Early spring: sow last half-hardy annuals, take cuttings of overwintered perennials, sow peppers/eggplants/cucumbers
- Late spring: start shading, increase ventilation, feed tomatoes and remove sideshoots

## Summer Tasks

### Ornamental Garden
- Throughout summer: mow regularly (not in drought), deadhead perennials/shrubs/roses, tie in climbers, save seeds of spring flowers, top off pond in dry weather
- Early summer: remove rose suckers, cut back early flowering perennials, dig up heel-in spring bulbs, plant deep-water aquatics, transplant seedlings to nursery rows
- Midsummer: trim fast-growing hedges, cut back finished herbaceous plants, transplant biennials and perennials, plant autumn-flowering bulbs, harvest herbs for drying
- Late summer: lightly prune conifers, prune finished shrubs, deadhead annuals, plant spring-flowering bulbs, save perennial seeds, take cuttings of half-hardy perennials

### Vegetable Garden
- Throughout summer: check all netting, continue successional sowing until late summer
- Early summer: plant out tender vegetables (sweet corn, outdoor tomatoes, green beans, zucchini), transplant autumn/winter cole crops, finish transplanting leeks, harvest/store shallots
- Midsummer: put straw under bush tomatoes, start late sowings, draw soil away from onions, earth up main crop potatoes, spray potatoes against blight at two-week intervals
- Late summer: sow spring cabbages in stale seedbed, sow quick-maturing lettuce, save broad bean seeds, start pulling/storing onions

### Fruit Garden
- Throughout summer: check all fruit netting, tie in bramble/wall-trained fruit, spray aphids with insecticidal soap, watch for sawfly larvae on gooseberries
- Early summer: protect cherries from birds, start summer pruning trained soft fruit, continue tying in wall-trained trees, feed black currants, pin down strawberry runners
- Midsummer: thin fruit clusters after natural drop, harvest/prune summer-fruiting raspberries, support heavy fruit branches, summer prune soft fruit/trained pears
- Late summer: summer prune all trained apples/pears, harvest bramble fruits and cut back fruited branches, plant new strawberry beds

### Greenhouse
- Throughout summer: feed fruiting vegetables weekly, spray down regularly, maintain ventilation
- Midsummer: shade greenhouse, start pinching off/training/feeding fruiting vegetables, remove tomato sideshoots, summer prune vines, start harvesting
- Late summer: take fuchsia/geranium cuttings, sow cyclamen, start sowing winter lettuce

## Autumn Tasks

### Ornamental Garden
- Throughout autumn: water borders in drought, fork vacant ground and mulch with compost/manure
- Early autumn: prepare soil and sow new lawns, finish planting spring bulbs (except tulips), prune climbing/rambling roses, prepare hedge-planting trenches, continue trimming conifers
- Mid-autumn: stop mowing lawn, lay turf, cover pond with netting, dig up/divide/replant perennials, dig up/store half-hardy perennials and bulbs, plant bare-root evergreens, plant lilies, finish planting tulips, start hardwood cuttings
- Late autumn: plant bare-root deciduous trees/shrubs/hedging, dig perennials for forcing, remove fallen leaves from rock garden, continue planting perennials

### Vegetable Garden
- Throughout autumn: keep leaves/debris cleaned up to prevent disease spread
- Early autumn: dig and store root vegetables (except those needing frost), protect fruiting vegetables with cloches, earth up celery/leeks/celeriac, transplant spring cabbages, lift/store onions
- Mid-autumn: plant winter lettuce, transplant spring cabbages, cut down peas/beans leaving roots, earth up celery/leeks/celeriac, force chicory roots, cut down asparagus foliage and mulch bed
- Late autumn: secure bird netting over cabbages, start sowing broad beans outside, protect cauliflowers from frost, firm tall cole plants, finish earthing up celery

### Fruit Garden
- Throughout autumn: tie in new bramble shoots, check tree ties/stakes, cut out damaged/diseased wood
- Early autumn: order plants from nurseries, complete summer pruning of trained trees, mulch autumn-fruiting strawberries, pick blackberries, take hardwood cuttings of currants/gooseberries
- Mid-autumn: prepare planting areas, put greasebands around apple/cherry trees, pick/store late apples/pears/quinces, winter prune currants/gooseberries after leaf fall
- Late autumn: plant bare-root trees/bushes/rootstocks, start winter pruning bush/standard trees, cover soft fruit with bird netting, inspect stored fruit

### Greenhouse
- Throughout autumn: reduce ventilation and spraying down
- Early autumn: continue sowing winter lettuce, check heaters working
- Mid-autumn: sow short-day lettuce in cold greenhouse/frame, check all plants/remove diseased foliage, start closing at night, begin heating if necessary
- Late autumn: transplant short-day lettuces, plant vines/peaches/nectarines, bring worm compost inside for winter

## Winter Tasks

### Ornamental Garden
- Throughout winter: mulch between plants in borders, winter prune trees, plant bare-root trees/shrubs when soil allows, check newly planted shrubs after frost, examine stored dahlia tubers
- Early winter: replace worn lawn areas with turf, lay turf if ground not frozen, start winter pruning trees/shrubs
- Midwinter: trim heathers after flowering, sow alpines/tree seeds needing frost exposure, put frost-tender container plants in greenhouse
- Late winter: spike badly drained lawn areas with hollow-tined fork, start feeding borders and lawn, start pruning late-flowering climbers, dig and divide mint

### Vegetable Garden
- Throughout winter: pot chicory roots and sea kale for forcing every 3-4 weeks, test soil pH and add lime, continue digging and manuring, examine stored vegetables
- Early winter: dig up/replant rhubarb
- Midwinter: continue sowing broad beans, cover soil with cloches to warm it, set up potato tubers to sprout
- Late winter: feed spring cabbages with dried blood or kelp meal, start sowing early vegetables under cloches and hardy varieties outside, plant early potatoes under plastic, plant Jerusalem artichokes/shallots/garlic

### Fruit Garden
- Throughout winter: winter prune apple/pear/quince trees, cut out and burn cankered shoots, plant bare-root trees/bushes, inspect stored fruit, prune newly planted trees
- Midwinter: cut back branches for spring grafting, protect fig shoots in cold areas, prune autumn-fruiting raspberries
- Late winter: feed and mulch fruit trees/bushes, protect blossoms of early wall-trained fruit from frost, start training new bramble shoots, put potted strawberry runners in greenhouse for forcing

### Greenhouse
- Throughout winter: ventilate on sunny days, keep atmosphere dry, check thermometer regularly
- Early winter: take down vines and lay on borders, wash down greenhouse
- Midwinter: start heating for early sowing, sow globe artichokes/peppers/cucumbers/onions/radishes/carrots/turnips/summer cabbages/spinach in heated greenhouse
- Late winter: start sowing seeds of flowering pot plants, sow half-hardy annuals, sow celery/tomatoes/eggplants/melons/peas/sweet corn/early spinach, sow tomatoes for growing under glass

## Soil Management by Season

### Heavy Soils
- **Spring**: rake for sowing
- **Summer**: hoe regularly, mulch for weed control and moisture retention
- **Autumn**: start digging and working in manure/compost as land becomes vacant; keep off wet soil, use plastic sheeting
- **Winter**: continue digging from boards; dig new deep beds and manure existing ones

### Light Soils
- **Spring**: dig in overwintered green-manure cover crops in time to settle before sowing
- **Summer**: same as heavy soils
- **Autumn**: sow green-manure crops to cover vacant land; dig in spring-sown green-manures
- **Winter**: spread manure/compost over ground not sown with green manure

## General Year-Round Principles

- Maintain compost system: top off worm compost in summer, start new bins when full, collect autumn leaves for leaf mold
- Watch continually for pests and diseases; treat immediately
