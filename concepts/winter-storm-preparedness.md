# Winter Storm Preparedness

Winter storms bring heavy snow, ice, extreme cold, and prolonged power outages. Rural homesteads face the greatest challenges due to isolation, longer road clearing times, and reliance on external infrastructure for heating and water. This guide covers backup heating, pipe freeze prevention, [[windbreak-design-and-livestock-shelter]], and emergency supplies.

## Understanding Winter Storm Risk

**Winter Storm Watch:** Conditions favorable for significant winter weather — prepare
**Winter Storm Warning:** Life-threatening winter weather occurring or imminent
**Blizzard Warning:** 35+ mph winds with visibility below 1/4 mile for 3+ hours
**Ice Storm Warning:** 1/4 inch+ ice accumulation expected

Risk factors: distance from emergency services, dependence on electric heating and well pumps, exposed pipes, livestock care requirements, long driveways.

## Backup Heating

### Wood Stove
The most reliable backup heat source. A properly installed wood stove can heat an entire home indefinitely.

- Minimum 1 cord of firewood per month of winter (see [[firewood-seasoning-and-storage]])
- Annual chimney inspection and cleaning — creosote is a fire hazard
- Proper clearances: 36 inches from combustible walls, 18 inches from non-combustible
- Carbon monoxide detector mandatory in the same room
- Never burn treated lumber, painted wood, garbage, or plastics
- Dispose of ashes in a metal container stored outside, 10 feet from structures

### Pellet Stove
More automated but requires electricity. Battery backup or small inverter generator suffices. Annual cleaning required.

### Kerosene Heater
No electricity required. One square inch of open window per 1,000 BTU for ventilation. Use clear K-1 kerosene only. Carbon monoxide detector mandatory.

### Propane Heater (Indoor-Rated)
20-pound tank lasts 10-30 hours. Must have oxygen depletion sensor (ODS). Carbon monoxide detector mandatory.

### Generator Support
A 5,000W generator can run a small electric furnace or several baseboard heaters. Have an electrician install a transfer switch. Never operate generators indoors or in attached garages.

## Pipe Freeze Prevention

### Prevention
- Wrap all exposed pipes in unheated areas with foam insulation sleeves
- Use heat tape on the most vulnerable pipes
- Seal gaps and cracks where [[hemenway-cold-air-drainage-and-frost-management]] enters near pipes
- During extreme cold, allow faucets to drip slowly
- Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls
- Know the location of your main water shut-off valve
- Maintain thermostat at 55°F minimum during absences
- For wells: insulate the wellhead and pipe from well to house

### Thawing Frozen Pipes
1. Shut off water supply to the frozen section
2. Open the downstream faucet so melting water can flow
3. Apply heat gradually: hair dryer, heat lamp, or towels in hot water
4. Work from the faucet end toward the frozen section
5. Never use an open flame
6. Check for leaks after thawing

## Livestock Shelter

Wind + moisture is far more dangerous than cold alone.

**Goats:** Three-sided shelter blocking prevailing wind. Dry bedding is essential. Goats tolerate cold but not wind/rain combined. Shivering means provide more shelter.

**Ducks:** Draft-free shelter at night. Ensure water does not freeze — ducks need water to eat and maintain waterproofing. Use heated water bowls. Frostbite risk on feet and bills in extreme cold.

**Chickens:** Draft-free but ventilated coop. [[comparison-deep-litter-vs-hot-composting]] method provides warmth. Apply petroleum jelly to combs and wattles before extreme cold. Heated waterers or frequent replacement. Extra feed in late afternoon for overnight warmth.

**General:** Windbreak is the single most important shelter feature. Increase feed availability. Ensure continuous water access. Monitor animals twice daily during extreme cold.

## Emergency Supplies (Minimum 7-Day Supply)

**Water:** 1 gallon per person per day, additional for pets/livestock. Store indoors protected from freezing. Water purification supplies.

**Food:** Non-perishable food requiring no cooking. Manual can opener. Camp stove with fuel [[query-how-to-build-a-cob-oven-for-outdoor-cooking]]. High-calorie comfort foods and hot drinks.

**Warmth:** Sleeping bags rated to 0°F, extra wool blankets, warm layered clothing, waterproof boots and gloves, chemical heat packs, emergency thermal blankets (Mylar).

**Power and Light:** Generator with 20+ gallons fuel, flashlights/headlamps, lanterns, batteries, candles (use with caution), solar charger for phones.

**Communication:** NOAA weather radio (battery/hand-crank), charged phone with backup battery, ham radio (see [[homestead-communication]]), written emergency contacts.

**Tools:** Snow shovel, ice melt or sand, roof rake for heavy snow loads, chainsaw with fuel and chain oil, fire extinguisher (ABC type).

**Vehicle:** Full gas tank (keep above half during winter), winter emergency kit in every vehicle: blanket, food, water, flashlight, first aid, shovel, ice scraper, sand for traction, jumper cables.

## After the Storm

- Clear snow from roofs if accumulation exceeds 12 inches or snow is wet/heavy
- Clear snow from exhaust vents, gas meters, and propane tanks
- Clear paths to animal shelters and water sources
- Check on neighbors, especially elderly or isolated individuals
- Report downed power lines — stay far away
- Assess trees for broken limbs that may fall later
- Verify carbon monoxide detectors are functioning with backup heating in use
