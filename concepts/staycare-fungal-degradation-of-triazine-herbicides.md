# Fungal Degradation of Triazine Herbicides

Triazine herbicides constitute one of the most widely used classes of
herbicides globally, with atrazine and simazine being among the most
commonly applied agricultural chemicals. These s-triazine compounds are
relatively persistent in the environment and can contaminate both soil
and groundwater. Fungi, including both [[fungal-volatile-organic-compounds-communication]] containing
a symmetric 1,3,5-triazine ring with various substituents. Atrazine
(2-chloro-4-ethylamino-6-isopropylamino-s-triazine) and simazine
(2-chloro-4,6-bis(ethylamino)-s-triazine) are the most widely used
members of this class. Key environmental concerns include:

- **Groundwater contamination**: The moderate water solubility and
  low soil adsorption of atrazine and simazine facilitate their
  leaching into groundwater, where they can persist for months
  to years.
- **Persistence**: Triazine herbicides have soil half-lives
  ranging from weeks to months depending on environmental
  conditions, soil type, and microbial activity.
- **Ecological effects**: Triazines are known to affect photosynthesis
  in non-target plants and algae, and potential endocrine-disrupting
  effects in aquatic organisms have been documented.
