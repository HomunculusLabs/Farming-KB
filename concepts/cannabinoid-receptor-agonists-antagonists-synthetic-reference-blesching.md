## CB1 Agonists

### CP 55,940

CP 55,940 is a synthetic cannabinoid that binds **equally strongly to both CB1 and CB2 receptors**. Developed by Pfizer, it was one of the early synthetic cannabinoids used in research to characterize the cannabinoid receptor system. Its dual-receptor affinity makes it useful for studying the general effects of cannabinoid receptor activation without the confounding variable of receptor selectivity.

### HU-210

HU-210 is a synthetic cannabinoid that binds **primarily to CB1 receptors**, with a reported affinity 100 to 800 times greater than THC. Developed by Hebrew University researchers (hence the "HU" designation), it is one of the most potent cannabinoid receptor agonists known. HU-210 has been used extensively in research to study CB1-mediated effects including:

- Neuroprotection
- Pain modulation
- [[plant-memory-and-learning-mechanisms]] effects
- Psychoactive effects

The extreme potency of HU-210 means that it is used only in research settings and is not developed as a pharmaceutical agent.

- Pain pathways
- Inflammation

- Neuroprotection

WIN 55,212-2 has been a standard research tool in [[cannabis-for-inflammatory-conditions]]
