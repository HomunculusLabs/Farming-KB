# Volatile Solvent Extraction and Hash Oil

Volatile solvent extraction uses chemical solvents to dissolve
cannabinoids and terpenes from cannabis plant material. While
these methods can produce very concentrated and potent oils,
Cervantes does not recommend them because of significant safety
risks including explosion, fire, toxic fumes, and chemical
residue in the final product.

## Hash Oil Overview

Hash oil is a concentrate of hash or marijuana (cannabis oil)
that has been dissolved in hydrocarbon solvents such as ether
and alcohol to extract THC. Hash and cannabis oil frequently
retain residues from the solvents used to extract the THC.
These residues pose a health risk to consumers.

The oils can be very concentrated and potent. Honey oil was
somewhat popular in America in the early 1970s, with a
translucent golden-amber color that transformed from stiff,
toffee-like consistency to runny oil when warmed. Most cannabis
oils are dark in color because they contain chlorophyll and
other contaminants. Filtering the oil through charcoal will
remove virtually all of the impurities.

Hash oil never became super popular because it is inconvenient
to smoke and many users do not want to be exposed to the health
risks associated with processing solvents.

## Volatile Solvent Extraction Process

Cervantes explicitly warns against volatile solvent extraction
because it requires dangerous chemical solvents including
isopropyl alcohol, ethyl alcohol, and white gas. The basic
process involves:

1. Cannabis is soaked in the solvent.
2. The solvent dissolves cannabinoids, chlorophyll, plant
   waxes, and other compounds.
3. The solvent is removed by evaporation.
4. The resulting residual is a sludge containing chlorophyll,
   plant waxes, debris, and cannabinoids.

The crude nature of this process and the presence of residual
solvents make it inferior to mechanical separation methods.

## Butane Hash Oil (BHO)

Butane cannabis oil is made by passing butane gas through
chopped cannabis. The butane dissolves THC and transports it
out a drain where it is collected. The captured butane contains
THC in solution. The butane is evaporated away by setting the
collection container in a pan of warm water. Once butane has
dissipated completely, the residual honey-colored oil is
scraped from the bottom of the collection pan.

Dangers of butane extraction:
- The process uses a quantity of butane in an open container.
- Butane fumes are toxic to inhale.
- A small flame or spark could cause butane to explode.
- Residual butane in the final product poses health risks.
- Should only be made in well-ventilated or outdoor locations.

## Isomerization

Isomerization is a chemical resin extraction process that was
popular in the 1970s and early 1980s but has fallen out of
favor because of the harsh and dangerous chemical solvents
used: white gas, sulfuric acid, hydrochloric acid, and others.
The process converts CBD to THC through chemical
transformation, but the risks and complexity make it
impractical for most users.

## Jelly Hash

Jelly hash is a combination of high-quality hash and cannabis
oil, typically extracted with butane. The typical recipe
contains eight parts hash and one part cannabis oil (hash oil).
Although very strong, the dark jelly hash has a characteristic
sticky, oily consistency that makes it difficult to handle.
The combination of water-extracted hash purity with
concentrated oil potency produces one of the strongest cannabis
products available.

## Smoking Methods for Hash Oil

The oil can be consumed in several ways:
- Wiped on joint papers or cigarettes
- Applied to pipe screens
- Smoked using hot knives
- Vaporized in an oil pipe (a stem with a glass bubble on one
  end where a little oil is placed and heated until THC
  vaporizes)
- Used as an ingredient in jelly hash

## Safer Alternatives

Cervantes emphasizes that there are many safe ways to separate
THC-potent glands from foliage without using volatile solvents.
Water extraction, dry sieving, and hand rubbing all produce
quality hash without the explosion, fire, and health risks
associated with chemical solvents. For home extraction, these
mechanical methods are strongly preferred over solvent-based
approaches.
