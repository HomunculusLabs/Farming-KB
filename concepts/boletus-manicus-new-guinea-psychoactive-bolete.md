during the mid-20th century. The epithet "manicus" likely derives from the Manus
Island region or the broader Manus Province of Papua New Guinea, where the mushroom
was studied in the context of indigenous use.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Boletus manicus is classified as follows:
- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Division**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Boletales
- **Family**: Boletaceae
- **Genus**: Boletus
- **Subgenus**: Tubiporus

Within the Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (1998) catalogue, B. manicus is grouped with
several other boletes reported as neurotropic, all originally described by Heim from
New Guinea material:
- **Boletus flammeus** R. Heim (#197)
- **Boletus (Tubiporus) kumaeus** R. Heim (#198)
- **Boletus (Tubiporus) manicus** R. Heim (#199)
- **Boletus (Tubiporus) nigerrimus** R. Heim (#200)
- **Boletus (Tubiporus) nigroviolaceus** R. Heim (#201)
- **Boletus (Tubiporus) reayi** R. Heim (#202)

Additionally, two species of Heimiella (a closely related boletaceous genus) are
listed: H. anguiformis and H. retispora, both originally described from New Guinea.

## Distribution

Boletus manicus is endemic to New Guinea (Papua New Guinea). According to the
distribution records in Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (1998), the species has been
documented from the following sources and locations in New Guinea:

- Heim & Wasson (1958, 1965): Original ethnobotanical documentation
- Heim (1963, 1965a, 1978): Mycological monograph treatments
- Corner, E.J.H. (1972): Comprehensive agaric flora of the region

multiple unrelated genera, including boletes (Boletus, Heimiella), agarics
