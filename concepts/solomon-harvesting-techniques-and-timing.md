## Handling After Harvest

Post-harvest handling significantly affects storage life and quality.
Solomon offers several key guidelines:

-  Do not allow just-harvested sweet potatoes to sit in direct sun for
   more than one hour or they may scald, reducing storage potential.
-  Sweet potatoes still in the earth are badly damaged if they
   experience soil temperatures below 50 degrees F (10 degrees C).
-  Potatoes should be cured after harvest to toughen skins. The
   traditional method involves spreading them in a dry, ventilated
   area away from direct sunlight for several hours to days.
-  After curing, store root crops in cool conditions. For sweet
   potatoes, ideal storage is around 55 to 60 degrees F (13 to 15
   degrees C) with reasonable ventilation.

## Harvest Timing and Drought Response

In a drought year, Solomon recommends thinning as a harvesting strategy.
By harvesting every second carrot or beet in every row, regardless of
size, you instantly reduce moisture competition while providing double
the capillary moisture to the remaining plants. The result might not be
the loss of half the crop. By preventing moisture stress, you might end
up harvesting a lot more total weight.
