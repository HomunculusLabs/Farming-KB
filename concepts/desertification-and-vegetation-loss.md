## The East Coast: Hidden Soil Depletion

Fukuoka observed that even regions that appear green and forested may suffer
from deep soil degradation. On the American East Coast, traveling through
several states near New York City, he encountered an endless sea of trees
composed of birch, maple, and oak — all about the same height. While this
appeared healthy, he concluded that the soil beneath these trees was poor
and depleted. The glaciers were said to be responsible, but the last ice age
was ten thousand years ago. In Japan, two thousand years would be long enough
for a layer of soil five feet thick to form. The fact that this hadn't
happened on the East Coast indicated that the soil had not recovered from
past agricultural abuse — likely the ravages of livestock-based agriculture
by early settlers who kept driving west as their lands wore out from cattle
grazing.
