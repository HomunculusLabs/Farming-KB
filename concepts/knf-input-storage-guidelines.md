# KNF Input Storage Guidelines

Proper storage of [[korean-natural-farming]] inputs is essential for
maintaining their potency and effectiveness. Each preparation has
specific environmental requirements for optimal preservation. This
This guide consolidates storage recommendations from Cho's Global Natural
Farming for all major NF inputs. For the preparation of individual inputs, see [[cho-fermented-plant-juice-preparation]], [[query-how-do-i-use-beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]], and related preparation guides.

## General Storage Principles

Several universal principles apply across all KNF input storage:

- Store in cool, shaded places away from direct sunlight
- Use appropriate containers (clay jars, glass jars, brown glass
  bottles, or food-grade polyethylene)
- Maintain stable temperatures to prevent degradation
- Label all containers with contents and preparation date
- Observe inputs regularly for signs of spoilage or contamination

## Standard Environmental Conditions

Most KNF inputs share these storage parameters:
- Optimum temperature: 23 to 25 degrees C for preparation
- Storage temperature: 1 to 15 degrees C for long-term preservation
- Light: No direct sunlight; shaded or dark storage
- Containers: Clay jars, glass jars, or brown glass preferred

## Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) Storage

FPJ requires careful storage to maintain enzyme activity:
- Use polyethylene, glass products, or clay jars as containers
- Brown glass containers must be preferred over clear glass
- Select a shaded area with no direct sunlight and stable temperature
- Optimum storage: 1 to 15 degrees C for up to one year
- At room temperature, use within 30 days
- Small bubbles or fungi indicate insufficient sugar or unbalanced
  air-to-ingredient ratio; add brown sugar, stir, and re-filter

## Fish Amino Acid (FAA) Storage

FAA is rich in nitrogen and requires specific conditions:
- Optimum temperature: 23 to 25 degrees C
- No direct sunlight; cool place recommended
- Store in clay pot, plastic jar, or glass jar
- Keep tightly sealed to prevent odor release

## Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB) Storage

LAB serum needs careful preservation:
- Keep refined LAB serum at cool temperature (1 to 15 degrees C)
- Never store under direct sunlight
- For room temperature storage, mix with equal amount of brown sugar
  and stir with a wooden stick to extend shelf life
- The brown sugar acts as a preservative by maintaining osmotic
  pressure that prevents spoilage organisms from growing

## Water-Soluble Calcium (WS-Ca) Storage

WS-Ca should be stored to prevent calcium precipitation:
- Optimum temperature: 23 to 25 degrees C
- Cool and shaded place with no direct sunlight
- Keep in clay jar, glass jar, or polyethylene container
- Ensure container is properly sealed

## Water-Soluble Phosphoric Acid (WS-PA) Storage

WS-PA has similar requirements to other mineral inputs:
- Optimum temperature: 23 to 25 degrees C
- Cool and shaded place with no direct sunlight penetration
- Store in glass jar covered with porous paper and rubber band

## Water-Soluble Potassium (WS-K) Storage

WS-K from tobacco stems requires standard conditions:
- Optimum temperature: 23 to 25 degrees C
- Cool and shaded area with no direct sunlight
- Store in clay jar or mud pot

## Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WS-CaPo) Storage

WS-CaPo shares standard mineral input requirements:
- Optimum temperature: 23 to 25 degrees C
- Cool and shaded place with no direct sunlight
- Properly sealed container to prevent contamination

## Yeast Storage

Yeast has the shortest shelf life of all NF inputs:
- Keep cultivated yeast in the refrigerator at 1 to 15 degrees C
- Maximum storage: one month
- Recommended use: within one week for maximum potency
- Shake 1-2 times daily during cultivation to protect from fungi
- Maintain optimum temperature (23-25 degrees C) and humidity (65-70%)
- Do not seal container; cover loosely with lid
- Keep on a separate shelf to prevent unwanted microbial cross-
  contamination and odor permeation

## Fermented Mixed Compost (FMC) Storage

FMC is the most durable input when properly prepared:
- After 20 days of fermentation, FMC is complete
- Put FMC in sacks or gunny bags for storage
- Keep in cool shade
- Can be stored for approximately one year
- A sweet smell indicates properly fermented, storable compost
- A foul smell indicates deterioration; such compost should not be
  stored or used

For FMC preparation and floor/temperature management, see [[knf-fmc-floor-and-temperature-control]].

## IMO Storage

Indigenous Microorganisms require special storage conditions:
- IMO-3 should be kept in a well-ventilated place
- Store in a container with a net or porous cover for air circulation
- During storage, IMO-3 may become warm; this is normal
- IMOs must be prepared fresh every year for continued results
- Microorganisms must be continuously maintained in the soil

## Container Selection Guide

Choosing the right container affects input quality:
- Clay jars: Best for most inputs; breathable and naturally cool
- Glass jars: Good alternative; brown glass preferred over clear
- Polyethylene containers: Acceptable for some inputs like FAA
- Plastic containers: Avoid for IMO and FPJ preparation
- Wood or bamboo: Required for IMO collection, not for storage
- Porous paper covers: Allow gas exchange during fermentation
- Airtight lids: Used only after fermentation is complete for storage
