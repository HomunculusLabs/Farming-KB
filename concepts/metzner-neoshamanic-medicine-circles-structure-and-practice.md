## Neoshamanic Medicine Circles — Structure and Practice

## Overview

[[ralph-metzner]] coined and extensively documented the phenomenon of "neoshamanic
medicine circles" — hybrid ritual forms that blend elements of traditional
[[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], involving several
hundred participants, many of them repeatedly. His analysis provides the most
detailed [[mazatec-mushroom-ceremony-roman-estrada-ethnographic-account]] available of contemporary Western entheogenic
practice outside of organized religious contexts.

## Definition and Context

Neoshamanic medicine circles represent what Metzner called a "hybrid of the
psychotherapeutic and traditional shamanic approaches." They are distinguished
from both clinical [[metzner-holotropic-breathwork-and-grof-perinatal-matrices]], and various forms of
psychotherapy and body-oriented therapy.

## Core Structural Elements

Metzner identified a consistent set of structural elements that characterize
most neoshamanic medicine circles:

- **Circle formation**: Participants sit or lie in a circle, the universal
  shape of ceremonial gathering across cultures.

- **Central focus**: An altar in the center of the circle (or a fire if
  outside or in a tipi) serves as the ceremonial focal point. The altar
  typically holds objects of personal and spiritual significance.

- **Experienced guidance**: An experienced elder or guide presides, sometimes
  with one or more assistants. The guide"s role combines elements of the
  traditional shaman (ceremony leader, singer) and the Western therapist
  (facilitator, holder of space).

- **Low-light environment**: A preference for semidarkness or [[darkness-low-light-facilitation-entheogenic-ceremonies]]
  facilitates the emergence of visions. Eye-shades are sometimes used to
  deepen the interior focus.

- **Musical accompaniment**: Drumming, rattling, singing, or evocative
  recorded music provides a rhythmic and emotional container for the
  experience. The music is not merely atmospheric but actively structures
  the flow of consciousness during the ceremony.

- **Sacred space dedication**: The ritual space is dedicated through
  invocation of spirits of the four directions and elements, a practice
  drawn from both [[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] and European pagan ceremonial traditions.

- **Respectful attitude**: Participants cultivate a spiritual, reverent
  approach to the experience, distinguishing it from recreational drug use.

## The Council Practice

A distinctive feature of many neoshamanic circles is the use of the "talking
staff" or "singing staff," a practice that appears to have originated among
the Indians of the Pacific Northwest and is now more broadly referred to as
"council." In this format, only the person who holds the circulating staff may
sing or speak. There is no discussion, questioning, or interpretation of the
kind that would occur in standard group psychotherapy.

This structure has several important effects:

1. It gives each participant undivided attention and space for expression.
2. It prevents the analytic, interpretive patterns of group therapy from
   overriding the direct, experiential quality of the entheogenic state.
3. It creates a contemplative, non-judgmental atmosphere that supports
   vulnerability and honesty.
4. It honors the indigenous roots of the practice by preserving the
   ceremonial form of council.

Some group sessions, however, involve minimal or no interaction between
participants during the time of the expanded state of consciousness, allowing
each person to engage fully with their own interior process.
