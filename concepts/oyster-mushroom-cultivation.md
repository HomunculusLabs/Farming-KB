## Oyster Mushroom Cultivation

Oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.) are among the easiest and most rewarding mushrooms to cultivate. They are aggressive colonizers, grow on a wide variety of substrates, fruit prolifically, and have broad market appeal.

## Species and Varieties

- **Pleurotus ostreatus** (Blue/Gray oyster): The standard, cold-weather oyster. Fruits at 50-70 F.
- **Pleurotus pulmonarius** (Phoenix oyster): Warm-weather relative, more tolerant of higher temperatures.
- **Pleurotus eryngii** (King oyster/Trumpet): Thick-stemmed, meaty texture. Grows at 55-65 F.
- **Pleurotus citrinopileatus** (Golden oyster): Bright yellow, nutty flavor. Prefers warmer temperatures.
- **Pleurotus djamor** (Pink oyster): Vibrant pink color, tropical. Requires warm temperatures (70-85 F).

## Straw Pasteurization Method

The most common and accessible method for home cultivation:

1. Chop wheat straw into 2-4 inch lengths
2. Submerge in water at 160-170 F for 1-2 hours (pasteurization)
3. Drain thoroughly and cool to room temperature
4. Mix with grain spawn (10-20% by weight)
5. Pack into bags, buckets, or tubes
6. Poke holes for gas exchange
7. Incubate at 70-80 F until fully colonized (2-3 weeks)
8. Introduce fruiting conditions: high humidity, fresh air, light, cooler temperatures

## Totem Method (Outdoor)

A method for growing oyster mushrooms on logs or stumps outdoors:

1. Cut a hardwood log into sections (discs 6-12 inches tall)
2. Place spawn between each disc, restacking them
3. The mycelium jumps from disc to disc, colonizing the entire stack
4. Cover with straw or leaves to retain moisture
5. Mushrooms fruit from the sides of the totem when weather conditions are right

## Outdoor Beds

Oyster mushrooms can be grown directly in garden beds:

1. Dig a shallow trench in a shaded area
2. Layer pasteurized straw inoculated with spawn, 4-6 inches deep
3. Cover with cardboard or straw to retain moisture
4. Water regularly
5. Mushrooms fruit during cool, moist periods

## Supplemented Sawdust Blocks

For higher yields and commercial production:

1. Mix hardwood sawdust with 10-20% bran by dry weight
2. Add water to 60-65% moisture content
3. Sterilize at 15 psi for 60-90 minutes
4. Inoculate with grain or sawdust spawn
5. Pack into autoclave bags with filter patches
6. Incubate at 75 F until fully colonized
7. Cut holes in the bag for fruiting, or remove block and place in humidity-controlled chamber

## Mycoremediation Applications

Pleurotus ostreatus is the keystone species for many [[cotter-mycoremediation-overview]] applications. It is indigenous, aggressive, adaptable to various conditions, and documented to break down petroleum hydrocarbons, PAHs, and other contaminants while still producing edible mushrooms.
