# Fermented Plant Juice Preparation and Application

[[fermented-plant-juice-fpj]] is a core input in [[korean-natural-farming]]
(KNF) developed by Dr. Han Kyu Cho. FPJ is a fermented extract of a
plant's sap and chlorophylls, producing a rich enzyme solution full
of microorganisms such as [[lactic-acid-bacteria]] and yeast that
invigorates both plants and animals. As described in Cho's Global
Natural Farming guide, FPJ serves as a natural growth stimulant,
pest management tool, and plant health enhancer that can replace
commercial fertilizers and synthetic growth hormones.

## Understanding FPJ

FPJ works by extracting and concentrating the natural growth hormones,
enzymes, and beneficial microorganisms present in selected plants.
Through fermentation with brown sugar, the plant sap is preserved and
the microbial activity is enhanced. The resulting liquid contains
gibberellins (which promote thicker foliage and fruit development),
auxins, cytokinins, and various enzymes that support plant metabolism
and immune function.

## Selecting Plants for FPJ

The choice of plant material significantly affects the properties of
the final FPJ. Several criteria guide plant selection:

- Plants that are strong against cold and grow well in spring, to
  pass down the characteristic of enduring extreme climatic changes
- Plants that grow fast and are vigorous, as fast developing plants
  have highly active growth hormones that can improve plant
  weaknesses and support recovery from health problems
- Bamboo shoots and lateral buds of all kinds of plants, which have
  abundant growth hormone and vitality
- Thinned-out fruits, which contain lots of gibberellins that make
  plants healthy with thicker foliage and enhance fruit thickness

### Recommended Plant Sources by Growth Stage

- For germination to early vegetative growth: Mugwort (Artemisia
  vulgaris) and bamboo shoot FPJs help crops become resistant
  against cold and grow fast and strong
- For vegetative growth: Arrowroot and bamboo shoot FPJs, as well as
  reeds and water or marsh plants with firm stems, help crops obtain
  needed nitrogen to increase in volume
- For pest management: FPJ mixed with rice bran can be sprinkled
  around fruit trees to lure pests to the ground, away from fruits

### Crop-Specific FPJ Matching

Best results come from using FPJ made from the same crop family:
- Tomato: lateral buds of stems and leaves
- Squash and sweet potato: vines from the same crops
- Pinched shoots of plants (not contaminated by chemicals)

## Timing of Collection

When plant material is collected matters significantly:

- Avoid days with excessive sunshine or rainfall. Excessive sunshine
  may evaporate nutrients; too much rainfall may wash away important
  nutrients and microorganisms
- When it has been raining, collect only after two days have passed
- Collect ingredients just before sunrise, when plants have the
  perfect moisture level

## Preparation Method

### Materials Needed

1. Selected plant material (mugwort, water amaranth, bamboo shoot, etc.)
2. Jaggery or brown sugar (unrefined is best)
3. Clay jar or glass jar
4. Porous paper (paper towel)
5. Rubber band or thread
6. A weight (stone)

### Step-by-Step Process

1. Shake off dirt from the plants but do not wash in water, as
   washing removes useful microorganisms. If ingredients are too big,
   cut them to 3-5 cm sizes to increase contact surface area and
   promote osmotic pressure. Do not mix different kinds of ingredients
   in one container; use a separate container for each ingredient.

2. Measure the weight of the ingredient and brown sugar. Brown sugar
   should be approximately half the weight of the ingredient. Adjust
   sugar amount based on the plant's moisture level.

3. Put the ingredients and brown sugar in a large wide container and
   mix with your hands. Cover with porous paper and leave for 1-2
   hours to allow initial osmotic extraction.

4. Transfer the mixture into the clay pot, filling to three-quarters
   full. The jar must not be too full or under full, as the empty
   space is filled with air needed for optimum fermentation.

5. Place a weight (stone) on the mixture to control the amount of air
   in it. Cover with paper to prevent insects while allowing air
   exchange.

6. Remove the weight after 1-2 days once air has escaped, then
   replace the cover.

7. Store the jar in a cool and shaded place. Do not open, move, or
   stir the ingredients during fermentation.

## Application Rates

FPJ is normally used at a dilution rate of 1:800 to 1:1000 in water.
During germination and early vegetative growth, use lower
concentrations at 1:1000 dilution. During active vegetative growth,
1:800 to 1:1000 is appropriate. When FPJ is combined with other
Natural Farming inputs, additional water should be added to the
solution to maintain proper overall dilution.

## When NOT to Use FPJ

There are specific conditions where FPJ application should be
avoided:

- During excessive vegetative growth or overgrowth caused by prolonged
  rains or cloudy weather
- FPJ made from the crop itself should not be used on that same crop,
  as it will promote further unwanted growth
- During acidic or nitrogen-excessive crop conditions, which create a
  pest-attractive environment
- Conditions of too much moisture or bad ventilation, which promote
  fungal growth

## Storage and Preservation

FPJ can be stored in polyethylene, glass, or clay containers. It
should be kept in a cool, dark place to maintain potency. Properly
made FPJ has a characteristic sweet-sour fermented smell and retains
its effectiveness for several months when stored correctly. Label each
batch with the plant source and date of preparation for quality
