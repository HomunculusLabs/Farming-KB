# Cannabis Sativa Taxonomy and Botanical Classification

## Overview

The taxonomy of Cannabis has been debated for centuries, with classification systems varying from a single polymorphic species (*Cannabis sativa* L.) to multiple species (including *C. indica* Lam., *C. ruderalis* Janisch., and *C. afghanica*). The Cannabis Sativa series of guides by S.T. Oner documents hundreds of cultivars and strains, implicitly operating within the framework that recognizes sativa-dominant and indica-dominant phenotypes as practically meaningful categories for growers and consumers, even as molecular research continues to refine our understanding of the genus.

## Historical Classification Systems

### Lamarck (1785)

Jean-Baptiste Lamarck was the first to formally distinguish *Cannabis sativa* from what he named *Cannabis indica*. Lamarck's *C. indica* was described from specimens collected in India and characterized by:

- Shorter stature (3–4 feet)
- Dense, compact branching
- Broad leaflets
- Shorter flowering time
- Aromatic, more sedative effects

Lamarck's *C. sativa*, by contrast, was described from European hemp and characterized by taller stature, narrow leaflets, and longer flowering periods. This morphological distinction between tall, narrow-leafleted plants and short, broad-leafleted plants remains the basis of the sativa/indica dichotomy used today.

### Schultes et al. (1974)

[[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]]. Used primarily for fiber and seed.
- *C. indica*: Short, conical, broad leaflets, found in Central and South Asia. Used for hashish production.
- *C. ruderalis*: Very short, unbranched, small seeds, found in Central Asia and Eastern Europe. Low THC content, auto-flowering tendency.

### Anderson (1980) and Small & Cronquist (1976)

Small and Cronquist proposed a single-species framework (*C. sativa*) with two subspecies:

- *C. sativa* subsp. *sativa*: Plants with low THC, high fiber content, found in temperate regions. This corresponds to industrial hemp.
- *C. sativa* subsp. *indica*: Plants with high THC, found in drug-producing varieties from South Asia.

This system has been widely adopted by botanists and is reflected in modern legal definitions distinguishing hemp from marijuana based on THC content.
