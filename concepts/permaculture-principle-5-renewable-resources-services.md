## Permaculture Principle 5: Use and Value Renewable Resources and Services

*"Let nature take its course"*

Renewable resources are those renewed by natural processes over reasonable periods without major non-renewable inputs. In business terms, renewable resources are income, while non-renewable resources are capital assets. Spending capital for day-to-day living is unsustainable. [[permaculture]] should make best use of renewable natural resources to manage and maintain yields.

## Renewable Resources vs. Services

### Consuming Resources

When we use a tree for wood, we consume a renewable resource. The tree must regrow.

### Non-Consuming Services (Passive Functions)

When we use a tree for shade and shelter, we gain benefits from the living tree that are non-consuming and require no harvesting energy. These passive functions are often overlooked but powerful in redesigning systems.

The most important historical example of prosperity from non-consuming nature services: domestication of the horse for transport, cultivation, and power. Intimate relationships with [[permaculture-domestic-animals]] also provide empathetic context for extending ethical concerns to include nature.

## Criteria for Appropriate Use

### Resource Replacement Time and Product Half-Life

**Key question:** "Will the function or product which the resource is being used for last at least as long as it took nature to generate the resource?"

- **Sun, tides, water, wind:** Daily or seasonally renewable → appropriate for ephemeral daily use
- **Timber from trees:** Problematic due to growth time
  - Paper products (half-life: few years) from trees (growth: decades/centuries) → inappropriate
  - Fine dining table (half-life: centuries) from the same tree → appropriate
- **In a low-energy, low-waste future:** A high-quality book could last as long as an heirloom table, justifying use of slowly renewable resources

### Multiple Products from Every Resource

Be suspicious of any use achieving 100% conversion to a single product — this probably devalues many potential uses. The contractor cutting trees into firewood "foot blocks" when fence posts would be higher value; the same mentality drives whole-of-tree harvesting for wood fibre, ignoring higher-value uses.

### Capital Asset Size

The total size of a resource provides buffer against variations in consumption rate. Growth time × size/area gives a sense of abundance. A woodlot theoretically supplying household fuel needs may be difficult to manage in practice due to rotational thinning complexity.

### Total Process Evaluation

Cannot fully evaluate resource use in isolation from wider industrial context. A craftsperson using wood left behind after logging may be the best example of renewable resource use. Whole-systems approaches reveal important cross-subsidies and links.

## Investment of Non-Renewable Energies

### The Oil in a Glass of Milk

Modern agriculture transformed from prime means of harvesting renewable resources to one of the largest consumers of non-renewable resources:
- Glass of milk in Australia: ~20% oil
- Glass of milk in Europe: ~50% oil
- Glass of milk in Israel: ~80% oil

### Renewable Energy Harvesting Costs

EMERGY evaluations show different renewable energy sources require very different non-renewable inputs:

| Source | Non-Renewable Input Needed |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Tidal/hydropower (good sites) | Very low — landscape processes created most infrastructure |
| Biomass from forest waste | Low — photosynthesis and forest processes did most work |
| Wind power | Greater — lower quality, erratic energy requires more infrastructure |
| Solar electric (PV) | Highest — most abundant but lowest quality energy |

## Solar Cells: Salvation or Diversion?

### The Heretical View

Despite being the great symbol of renewable energy, a high-tech society running on solar cells may be the stuff of dreams. Key arguments:

1. **Net energy dispute:** Embodied energy studies show positive results, but [[emergy-analysis]] suggests solar cells may be a net loss. The difference is mostly in evaluation of human labor and services.

2. **Human service costs:** EMERGY methodology measures human service by national EMERGY use share. In the US, human services are 200× more valuable than metabolic energy and about 2× the national fuel share. Manufacturing solar cells requires highly skilled human input while electricity generated is relatively small.

3. **[[maximum-power-law]]:** Solar energy has been the primary external energy for life on earth. Billions of years of evolution have probably already optimized [[fukuoka-three-dimensional-solar-energy-capture-rice-paddy]]. Technological alternatives are unlikely to match that efficiency.

### Howard Odum on Solar vs. Photosynthesis

> The natural conversion of sunlight to electric charge that occurs in all green plant photosynthesis after 1 billion years of natural selection may already be the highest net EMERGY possible.

Chloroplasts (nature"s photovoltaic cells) show higher efficiency than hardware cells.

### Appropriate Role for Solar Cells

Solar cells may be appropriate:
- For small demand systems remote from the grid
- In sunny dry climates where rainfall limits wood/[[dighton-fungal-biomass-production-aquatic-ecosystems]]
- In sunny urban environments where they can double as roofing material

**Greatest value:** Forcing reassessment of how precious electricity is — we should use it only for high-quality functions (small motors, lighting, communications). People living with autonomous solar systems are pioneers in a new culture of modest electricity use.

## Trees: Nature's Solar Power Plants

### The Argument for Trees

Trees most efficiently convert dilute solar energy into wood that can substitute for many fossil fuel uses:

**Advantages:**
- Decentralized transport fuel production (wood gas, [[methanol]]) using [[holmgren-gigantism-intermediate-technology-and-small-scale]]
- Structural timber replacing high-energy concrete, metal, synthetic materials
- Honey and other products plus environmental services
- Sustainable growth on poorest land unsuited to food production
- Wood gasifiers and micro-gas turbines appear more economical for electricity than solar cells
- EMERGY analysis supports this comparison

**In 200 years:** The primacy of trees is likely to be entrenched, while solar cells may be a forgotten high technology with no way to manufacture.

## Sustainable Use of Renewable Resources

### The Clean Green Technology Illusion

The image of clean green technology where we don't need to mess with nature or kill anything is an illusion, having substance only because generations of affluent urbanites have been disconnected from nature. In the end, we have to get our hands dirty to adapt to energy descent.

### Sustainable Yield Definition

Sustainable yield = surplus above that required for the system's own needs. Complexity and seasonal fluctuations require very conservative harvesting relative to believed sustainable yield. How and when we harvest is just as important as how much.

### The Hemp vs. Trees Debate

Hemp is an excellent cotton substitute (cotton needs high water, fertilizer, pesticide inputs). But hemp as wood pulp substitute is questionable — it's an annual crop requiring arable land suited to food production. Wood pulp can be a by-product of timber production on less fertile, steeper land with greater environmental benefits.

### Fluctuating and Mobile Resources

Many resources disappear if not harvested at appropriate times:
- Stone fruit tree crop can vanish with birds or rot in a week
- Hay cutting timing maximizes nutrient value before seed set
- Yabbies in dams: constant inflow of organic material feeds them, reproductive capacity replaces harvested numbers, harvesting allows others to grow big
- Migratory water birds: large numbers at one place don't necessarily indicate a surplus

## Key Takeaways

1. Renewable resources = income; non-renewable = capital — spending capital for daily living is unsustainable
2. Passive functions (shade, shelter) from living systems provide non-consuming services with no harvest energy
3. Product half-life should match or exceed resource replacement time
4. A glass of milk ranges from 20% to 80% oil depending on farming intensity
5. Solar cells may be net EMERGY loss despite positive embodied energy studies — human service costs are the key difference
6. A billion years of evolution has likely optimized solar energy capture — chloroplasts outperform hardware cells
7. Trees are nature's optimal solar power plants — wood gasifiers more economical than solar cells for electricity
8. In 200 years, trees will be entrenched as primary energy source; solar cells may be forgotten
9. The "clean green technology" illusion exists only because urbanites are disconnected from nature
10. Sustainable yield = surplus above system's own needs; how and when we harvest matters as much as how much
