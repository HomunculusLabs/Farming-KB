# Fungal Ecology and Decomposition

Fungi are the primary decomposers of recalcitrant organic matter in
terrestrial ecosystems. While bacteria dominate the breakdown of simple
compounds, fungi excel at degrading complex plant polymers including lignin,
cellulose, and hemicellulose. This decomposer function makes fungi essential
to global carbon cycling, releasing approximately 50-60 billion tonnes of CO2
annually through respiration during organic matter decomposition.

## The Decomposition Process

Fungal decomposition proceeds through distinct phases:

### Primary Colonization

Pioneer fungi (often zygomycetes and some ascomycetes) colonize fresh organic
matter, utilizing readily available sugars and amino acids. These opportunists
have broad substrate ranges and rapid growth but limited enzymatic capacity
for recalcitrant polymers.

### Secondary Colonization

As simple substrates are depleted, succession shifts to basidiomycetes and
specialized ascomycetes that produce lignocellulolytic enzyme systems. White
rot basidiomycetes (e.g., *Phanerochaete chrysosporium*, *Trametes versicolor*)
are unique in their ability to degrade lignin completely, using oxidative
enzymes that generate highly reactive free radicals to break down the
non-enzymatic lignin polymer.

### Enzymatic Arsenal

The key enzyme systems deployed by decomposer fungi include:

- **Cellulases**: Endoglucanases, exoglucanases (cellobiohydrolases), and
  β-glucosidases that work synergistically to convert crystalline cellulose
  to glucose.
- **Lignin peroxidases (LiP)**: Heme-containing peroxidases that oxidize
  non-phenolic lignin subunits, generating aryl cation radicals.
- **Manganese peroxidases (MnP)**: Oxidize Mn(II) to Mn(III), which in turn
  oxidizes phenolic lignin components and mediates lipid peroxidation.
- **Laccases**: Copper-containing oxidases that degrade phenolic lignin
  structures and have broad substrate specificity for various aromatic
  compounds.
- **Hemicellulases**: Xylanases, mannanases, and other enzymes targeting
  the heterogeneous hemicellulose matrix.

## Brown Rot vs White Rot

Basidiomycete wood decay is classified into two major strategies:

### White Rot

White rot fungi degrade all major wood components (cellulose, hemicellulose,
and lignin), leaving a bleached, fibrous residue. This strategy is
phylogenetically ancestral and is found across most basidiomycete orders.
White rot species are essential for closing the terrestrial carbon cycle,
as no other organisms can mineralize lignin to CO2.

### Brown Rot

Brown rot fungi primarily depolymerize cellulose while modifying but not
completely removing lignin, leaving a brown, cracked residue rich in
modified lignin. They employ a Fenton chemistry system, generating hydroxyl
radicals (OH·) through the reaction of hydrogen peroxide with Fe(II) ions.
These radicals non-enzymatically cleave cellulose chains. Brown rot is
believed to have evolved multiple times from white rot ancestors and is
dominant in coniferous forests.

## Nutrient Cycling and Immobilization

During decomposition, fungi both release and immobilize nutrients. The
fungal biomass has a relatively high C:N ratio (approximately 15:1), meaning
that when decomposing nitrogen-poor substrates (like wood with C:N ratios
of 400-1000:1), fungi actively scavenge nitrogen from soil solution,
competing with plant roots and soil microbes. This nitrogen immobilization
can be a significant factor in forest nitrogen cycling.

Fungi also accumulate phosphorus, potassium, calcium, and micronutrients
in their biomass, temporarily sequestering these elements during the
decomposition process and gradually releasing them through hyphal turnover
and grazing by soil microfauna.

## Environmental Factors Affecting Decomposition

- **Moisture**: Fungal activity peaks at water potentials around -0.01 to
  -0.1 MPa; both drought and waterlogging suppress decomposition rates.
- **Temperature**: Most decomposer fungi have optima between 20-30°C, with
  Q10 values of 2-3 for decomposition rates.
- **pH**: Different fungal groups have distinct pH preferences, with many
  wood decomposers favoring slightly acidic conditions.
- **Substrate quality**: Lignin:nitrogen ratio is a strong predictor of
  decomposition rate; high-lignin, low-nitrogen substrates decompose slowly.
- **Oxygen availability**: White rot and brown rot are strictly aerobic,
  relying on O2 as the terminal electron acceptor and for peroxidase
  activity.

## Fungi in Soil Food Webs

Fungal hyphae and spores serve as food for numerous soil organisms including
nematodes, microarthropods (springtails and mites), and collembola. This
grazing stimulates fungal growth through compensatory responses and releases
nutrients locked in fungal biomass, making them available to plants and other
soil organisms. The fungal energy channel is a major pathway in detrital food
webs, particularly in acidic and forest soils where fungi dominate over
bacteria.
