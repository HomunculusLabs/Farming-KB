## Vegetative Growth Phase

The vegetative growth stage is the foundational period of [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-vegetative-phase-management]]. A plant stunted now could take weeks to resume normal growth. A strong, unrestricted root system is essential to supply the much-needed water and nutrients. The larger a plant gets and the bigger the root system, the faster the soil will dry out.

## Light Requirements

Vegetative growth is maintained with 16 or more hours of light. Research shows that vegetative plants grow faster under 24 hours of light. Marijuana will continue vegetative growth a year or longer (theoretically forever), as long as an 18-hour photoperiod is maintained. Cannabis is photoperiodic-reactive; flowering can be controlled with the light and dark cycle.

## Nutrient Demands

A plant's nutrient and water intake changes during vegetative growth. Transpiration is carried on at a more rapid rate, requiring more water. High levels of nitrogen are needed; potassium, phosphorus, calcium, magnesium, sulfur, [[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]]. Fast-growing clones develop more quickly than spider mites can reproduce.

## Perpetual Harvest System

Combine eight-week flowering/harvest cycles with continuous cloning to form a perpetual harvest. One easy-to-implement scenario: take two clones every four days, and harvest one ripe female every other day. Every time a plant is harvested, one or two rooted clones are moved from a constantly supplied vegetative room into the flowering room. Using this schedule, a grower would have 30 clones, 10 vegetative plants, and 30 flowering plants growing at all times.

## Container Sizing for Vegetative Plants

When growing short plants that reach full maturity in 90 days, there is little need for containers larger than three gallons (11 L). Large mother plants will need a large pot if they are kept for more than a few months. Mother plants grow quite well in five or ten-gallon (19-38 L) hydroponic containers for easy culture.

## Rejuvenation

Growers can rejuvenate harvested females by leaving several undeveloped lower branches with foliage on plants. Give her an 18/6 day/night photoperiod. The female will stop flowering and rejuvenate, reverting back to vegetative growth stage in four to six weeks. Give the harvested, leafy, buddy stubs an increased dose of high-nitrogen fertilizer to promote green, leafy growth.

## Root System Development

Root growth is critical during vegetative phase. A healthy root system is white, branching, and extensive. Roots grow in darkness in a rigid, secure environment. When roots are taken out of contact with soil for long, they dry up and die. Roots need to be in constant contact with moist soil to supply water and food to the plant. Cannabis roots develop and elongate quickly, growing down and out, away from the main taproot. The larger the root system, the faster the plant can grow and the more nutrients it can process.

## Temperature and Humidity

During vegetative growth, maintain temperatures between 70-85 degrees F (21-29 degrees C) during the day and 60-70 degrees F (15-21 degrees C) at night. Humidity should be 50-70 percent. Higher humidity during vegetative growth supports rapid leaf expansion and transpiration. Avoid humidity above 80 percent, which promotes mold and fungal growth. Good ventilation is essential to maintain consistent environmental conditions.

## Water During Vegetative Phase

Plants in the vegetative stage require frequent watering as they grow rapidly. Irrigate when soil is dry one-half inch below the surface. Small plants with small root systems in small containers must be watered often, as soon as the soil surface dries out. If exposed to wind, small plants will dry out very quickly. Use plenty of water and allow up to 10 percent runoff during each watering to prevent fertilizer buildup.

## CO2 Supplementation During Vegetative Growth

Elevating CO2 levels to 1200-1500 ppm during the vegetative phase can dramatically accelerate growth rates, with plants often showing 20-30% increases in [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]] requires warmer conditions for optimal enzyme function.

## Common Vegetative Phase Problems

Several problems commonly arise during the vegetative phase. Nutrient burn, caused by excess fertilizer, manifests as yellow or brown leaf tips and margins that eventually curl upward. Nitrogen deficiency during rapid vegetative growth causes lower leaves to yellow and drop prematurely, while nitrogen excess produces excessively dark green foliage with soft, succulent growth that is more susceptible to pests. Stretching (elongated internodal spacing) results from insufficient light intensity or too much distance between the light source and the canopy. Heat stress above 85 degrees F reduces growth rate and can cause leaf curling and tip burn. Overwatering is one of the most common mistakes, leading to oxygen-depleted root zones that promote root rot and fungal pathogens.

## Sexing and Early Detection

Identifying plant sex during the vegetative phase allows growers to eliminate males before they pollinate females, saving space and resources. Pre-flowers typically appear at nodes (branch-stem junctions) by the fourth week of vegetative growth under an 18/6 light schedule. Female pre-flowers are distinguished by two white, hair-like pistils emerging from a small, teardrop-shaped calyx. Male pre-flowers appear as small, rounded pods (pollen sacs) that lack pistils. A jeweler's loupe or magnifying glass (10-30x) is usually needed for reliable identification, especially for inexperienced growers. Removing males promptly prevents accidental pollination, which diverts female plants' energy from resin production to seed development.

## Training Techniques During Vegetative Growth

The vegetative phase is the ideal time to apply training techniques that shape the plant's architecture for maximum light exposure and yield. Topping (removing the apical meristem or growing tip) forces the plant to develop two main colas instead of one, increasing the number of bud sites. Low-stress training (LST) involves gently bending and tying branches to create an even canopy, which is particularly effective with sativa-dominant strains that tend to grow vertically. Screen of Green (SCROG) uses a horizontal screen or net to train branches into a flat, even canopy that maximizes light utilization.
