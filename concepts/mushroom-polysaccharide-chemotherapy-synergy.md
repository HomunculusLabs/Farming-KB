## PSK with Mitomycin C and 5-FU

Psk Polysacolaride K at 3g/day added to post-surgical chemotherapy
doubled the 2-year survival rate (Kaibara et al.; Fujimoto et al.). PSK
protected against immunosuppression associated with surgery and long-term
chemotherapy (Hattori et al., 1979; Kodama et al., 1982). A double-blind
RCT (n=144) found significant increases in disease-free and overall
survival with PSK, plus enhanced immune function measured by DTH skin tests
and neutrophil chemotaxis (Kondo and Torisu, 1985).

## PSP Synergistic Effects

### PSP with IL-2

Mao et al. (1998) demonstrated dramatic anti-tumour effects combining
[[mushroom-radiotherapy-adjunct]].

### PSP with Cyclophosphamide

PSP negated cyclophosphamide's inhibition of delayed-type
hypersensitivity, restoring immune response to normal levels despite
chemotherapy. Cyclophosphamide is a leading alkylating agent that
drastically affects immune cells.

## Maitake D-Fraction Combinations

In a 165-patient study, Maitake D-fraction plus crude powder combined with
chemotherapy increased response rates by 12-28% across cancer types.
Patients recovered from severe chemotherapy side-effects with D-fraction.
See [[schizophyllan-cancer-clinical-trials]] with tegafur or mitomycin C/5-FU in gastric cancer showed
increased median survival in one study. Combined with radiotherapy, it
prolonged Stage II cervical cancer survival. In a 312-patient multimodal
trial, patients with pre-existing immune activation showed highly
significant survival benefits.

## Mechanisms of Synergy

- **Immune restoration**: Counteracts chemotherapy-induced
  immunosuppression
- **Haematopoietic protection**: Ameliorates
  [[mushroom-polysaccharide-apoptosis-induction]])
- **Drug sensitisation**: Enzyme inactivation may increase intracellular
  drug concentrations in tumour cells
- **Dose reduction potential**: Enhanced efficacy may allow lower toxic
  drug doses

## Clinical Implications

As Kidd (2000) stated: "After a quarter century of trials indicating PSK
can improve cancer survival, the cumulative human findings amount to a
recommendation for its inclusion in standard anticancer protocols. With its
risk for adverse effects virtually nonexistent, PSK's contribution to the
benefit-risk profiles of these protocols can only be positive."
