The discovery of aflatoxins — among the most potent naturally occurring carcinogens — began with a catastrophic episode of animal suffering in England in 1960. One hundred thousand turkeys died from a mysterious disease characterized by loss of appetite, lethargy, and liver failure. ### Metabolic Activation

Aflatoxin B1 is not itself the ultimate carcinogen. It requires metabolic activation by liver enzymes (cytochrome P450 enzymes) to form the reactive epoxide intermediate that binds to DNA. This metabolic activation explains why the liver is the primary target organ — it is the site of both activation and accumulation.

### Crops at Risk

Traces of aflatoxins are present in numerous food products:

- **Corn (maize):** Major aflatoxin reservoir worldwide
- **Peanuts and peanut butter:** The original source identified in the turkey X disease
- **Tree nuts:** Almonds, pistachios, Brazil nuts, walnuts

### Global Burden

The global burden of aflatoxin-related disease is concentrated in developing nations where:
- Subsistence farmers store crops in conditions that favor mold growth
- Regulatory limits on aflatoxin levels are absent or unenforced
- Hepatitis B infection (which synergizes with aflatoxin to dramatically increase liver cancer risk) is prevalent
- Dietary reliance on corn and groundnuts maximizes exposure

### Synergy with Hepatitis B

Aflatoxin exposure and hepatitis B virus infection act synergistically to increase liver cancer risk. Individuals infected with hepatitis B who are also exposed to aflatoxin have a risk of liver cancer up to 30 times higher than those with either risk factor alone. This synergy makes aflatoxin a particularly severe public health problem in regions where both hepatitis B prevalence and aflatoxin-contaminated food are common.
