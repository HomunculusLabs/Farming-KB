# Fungal Ecosystem Processes Overview

Fungi are integral to virtually all ecosystem processes, playing central roles
in nutrient cycling, [[biodiversity-of-fungi-ecosystem-services-agriculture]] provided by fungi
can be organized into five major categories: making nutrients available
(decomposition and mineralization), assisting primary production (mycorrhizal
symbiosis), participating in food webs (fungal-faunal interactions), regulating
populations and communities (pathogens and competition), and interacting with
human activities (bioremediation, pathogens, and biocontrol). Fungi are not
merely decomposers but are active participants at every trophic level in
terrestrial ecosystems.

## Nutrient Cycling: Making Nutrients Available

Fungi are primary agents of decomposition and nutrient mineralization in most
terrestrial ecosystems. Through extracellular enzymes, saprotrophic fungi break
down complex plant polymers including cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, pectin,
and chitin, releasing nutrients in inorganic forms available for plant uptake.

The rate of decomposition is governed by the interplay between [[fungal-microaggregate-formation-and-soil-stability]], and production of soil-
stabilizing compounds (glomalin, extracellular polysaccharides).

## Assisting Primary Production

Mycorrhizal fungi are essential partners in terrestrial primary production.
Nearly all land plants depend on mycorrhizal associations for enhanced nutrient
uptake, particularly phosphorus and nitrogen. In exchange for plant-derived
carbon, mycorrhizal fungi extend the effective root surface area and produce
enzymes that mobilize nutrients from organic matter and mineral sources.

Ectomycorrhizal fungi dominate in forest ecosystems, producing extensive
extramatrical hyphal networks. Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi are ubiquitous in
grasslands and agricultural systems and play critical roles in soil aggregation
through glomalin production. The discovery of carbon transfer between plants
through common mycorrhizal networks has fundamentally changed our understanding
of plant community dynamics.

## Fungi in Food Webs

Fungi serve as food resources for a diverse array of soil fauna and higher
trophic levels. Fungal hyphae and fruiting bodies are consumed by collembola,
mites, nematodes, insects, and mammals. The fungal energy channel represents a
major pathway of carbon and energy flow in soil food webs, complementing
bacterial-based and root-based channels.
