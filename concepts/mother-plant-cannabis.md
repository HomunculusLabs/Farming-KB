# Mother Plant Cannabis

A mother plant is a female cannabis plant maintained in perpetual [[hamilton-pest-and-disease-control-organic]] resistance, the clone

per day. They require ongoing care including regular watering, fertilization, pruning,
and pest monitoring. Give mother plants about ten percent less nitrogen than actively
growing plants, because slightly lower nitrogen promotes rooting in the clones taken
from them.

Keep several mother plants in the vegetative stage for a consistent source of cloning
stock. Start new mothers from seed every year. Give mother plants 18-24 hours of light
per day to maintain fast growth.

## Container Size

Large mother plants need large containers. A 10-gallon (38 L) container or equivalent
hydroponic system supports a productive mother. This large mother plant can provide
more than a hundred clones every month. Root-bound mothers produce weaker clones. If
the root system outgrows its container, transplant to a larger size. Some growers
maintain mothers in large individual hydroponic containers for easy culture and
maintenance.

## Health Requirements

Mother plants must stay very healthy to produce many clones. Key health indicators:

- Strong white root system (visible in hydroponic systems)
- Vibrant green foliage without yellowing or spots
- Active new growth at branch tips
- No signs of pest infestation or disease
- Sturdy stem and branch structure

Spider mites are a common problem on mother plants. Regular inspection and preventive
treatment are essential because an infested mother will pass pests to every clone taken
from her. Well-illuminated, strong clones grow fast and have less chance of being
affected by [[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]]. Fast-growing clones develop more quickly than spider
mites can reproduce.

## Pruning and Training

Mother plants require regular pruning to maintain a manageable size and promote bushy
growth. Remove lower branches that receive little light. Top the plant periodically to
encourage lateral branching, which provides more cutting sites. Keep the plant at a
height that allows easy access to all branch tips.

## Replacing Mothers

Start new mothers from seed every year. Even well-maintained mothers can accumulate
stress, lose vigor, or become diseased over time. Rotating in fresh mothers from seed
periodically ensures genetic health and vigor. Keep backup mothers in case the primary
mother is lost to disease or other problems. Any female can become a mother; she can
be grown from seed or be a clone of a clone.
