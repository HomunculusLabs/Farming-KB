# Fukuoka's European Natural Farming Travels

In the 1980s, Masanobu Fukuoka undertook an extensive speaking and
demonstration tour through Europe that introduced natural [[four-principles-of-natural-farming]] in his personal
presentation, rejecting the Western convention of separating the farmer's
identity from the philosopher's.

The sound of his geta on European streets became a signature element of his
presence. He described it as a way of grounding himself — literally
connecting to the earth through wooden soles rather than rubber or leather.

## Milan Rice Experiments

In Milan, Fukuoka encountered Italian rice cultivation and saw parallels
with Japanese practices. Italian rice paddies in the Po Valley used
irrigation systems similar to those in Japan, but with heavier reliance on
chemical inputs.

Fukuoka proposed that Italian rice farmers could adopt his natural direct-
seeding method, growing rice in succession with winter grains without
tillage or fertilization. The Po Valley's climate was well-suited to such a
system.
