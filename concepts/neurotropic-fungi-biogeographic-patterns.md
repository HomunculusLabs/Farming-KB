## Southern Hemisphere Origin Hypothesis

The majority of neurotropic Psilocybe species occur in or near the southern hemisphere, primarily in
subtropical humid forests. Guzman (1983) observed that of 85 neurotropic Psilocybe species:
- 59 species in South America and Mexico (southern)
- 18 species in the USA and Canada (northern)
- 9 species in Europe

This pattern holds despite North America and Europe being more intensively mycologically surveyed.
The conclusion is that neurotropic Psilocybe fungi likely originated in the southern hemisphere,
mainly in South America, and subsequently dispersed northward to North America and Europe.

## Hongo's Nine Distribution Types

Hongo (1978) described nine types of fungal distribution in Japan, which apply broadly to
neurotropic fungi worldwide:

1. **Cosmopolitan species** - Panaeolus spp., Panaeolina foenisecii
2. **Northern hemisphere species** - Amanita spp., Cordyceps spp., Psilocybe pelliculosa, P. silvatica
3. **Eurasian species** - Amanita muscaria (primarily)
4. **North American and Eastern Asiatic species** - Some Psilocybe species with disjunct ranges
5. **Far Eastern species** - Various endemic species
6. **Southeastern Asiatic species** - Psilocybe subaeruginascens var. subaeruginascens (Japan + Java)
7. **Tropical and subtropical species** - P. cubensis, P. subcubensis, Copelandia cyanescens,
   C. tropicalis, Gerronema fibula
8. **Arctic and alpine species** - Psilocybe aztecorum var. aztecorum (Mexican high mountains at
   4,000 m elevation)
9. **Endemic species** - Many Mexican endemics (P. muliercula, P. chiapanensis, P. laurae),
   Colombian endemics (P. colombiana, P. guatapensis, P. pintonii), Brazilian endemics (P. brasiliensis,
   P. paulensis), European endemics (P. hispanica, P. serbica)

## Latitudinal and Habitat Patterns

### Altitude Range
Neurotropic fungi occur from sea level to approximately 4,000 m elevation (P. aztecorum in Mexican
high mountains). The highest diversity occurs at middle elevations in subtropical cloud forests.

### Habitat Preferences
The majority of neurotropic Psilocybe species grow in:
- **Subtropical, mesophytic, cloud, or deciduous humid forests** - Highest diversity
- **Tropical forests** - Moderate diversity, including dung-inhabiting species
- **Coniferous forests** - Lower diversity despite extensive survey effort
- **Temperate grasslands and pastures** - P. semilanceata, Panaeolus/Panaeolina species
- **Disturbed habitats** - P. cyanescens, P. cubensis

In Mexico specifically, of 42 neurotropic Psilocybe species: 34 from mesophytic forests, 4 from
tropical forests, 4 from coniferous forests.

### Climate
- Tropical and subtropical species: P. cubensis, P. subcubensis, most Copelandia spp.
- Temperate species: Amanita spp., Cordyceps spp., some Gymnopilus and Inocybe
- The majority of species favor humid conditions

## Trans-Pacific Connections

One of the most remarkable biogeographic findings is the close relationship between neurotropic
fungi of the Americas and those of Southeast Asia/Oceania:

- **Section Mexicanae:** P. mexicana (Mexico/Guatemala) ~ P. samuiensis (Thailand) ~ P. makarorae
  (New Zealand)
- **Section Zapotecorum:** P. zapotecorum (Mexico/South America) ~ P. aucklandii (New Zealand)
- **Psilocybe argentipes/subcaerulipes** (Japan) relate to P. graveolens, P. muliercula, P. pintonii,
  P. zapotecorum (Americas)

These relationships were also noted by Horak (1983) in agarics and boletes from the South Pacific,
suggesting ancient land connections or dispersal routes.

## The Mexico-Colombia Hotspot

The highest concentration of neurotropic fungal species occurs in:
- Mexico (76 total species, 44 Psilocybe taxa)
- Caribbean region
- Mesoamerica
- Colombia (12 Psilocybe species)

These areas correspond to subtropical humid forests and represent the likely center of diversity
for the group.

## Knowledge Gaps and Sampling Bias

Several regions remain critically undersurveyed:
- Southeastern Russia, Mongolia, Arabia, Turkey
- Most of Africa (only 6-8 Psilocybe species known)
- Korea, Malaysia (except Java and Sumatra), Hawaii (no native Psilocybe)
- Many US states (Arizona, Colorado, Maryland, Vermont, Massachusetts, etc.)
- Central Australia

As Gartz (1996) observed: "The mushrooms occur in abundance wherever mycologists abound." New
species continue to be described from previously unsurveyed areas.
