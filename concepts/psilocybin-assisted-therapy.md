## Psilocybin Assisted Therapy
Psilocybin assisted therapy (PAT) is a treatment modality in which
psilocybin is administered in a controlled clinical setting alongside
psychotherapeutic support. Unlike conventional pharmacotherapy, which involves
daily medication, PAT typically uses one to three administrations of a high
dose of psilocybin within a structured therapeutic framework. The approach
combines the acute neuroplastic and psychoactive effects of psilocybin with
psychotherapeutic preparation, guidance during the session, and integration

supportive presence and non-directive guidance but avoid actively steering
the experience.
The integration phase involves one to three follow-up sessions where the
client processes the experience, explores insights and emotions that arose,
and develops strategies for implementing meaningful changes. Therapists help
clients distinguish lasting insights from transient emotional states and
identify concrete behavioral or lifestyle changes supported by the
experience.

Psilocybin also stimulates neuroplasticity through increased expression of
brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and activation of mammalian target
of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling pathways. These effects promote the growth of
new dendritic spines and synapses, particularly in the prefrontal cortex, an

neuroplastic window opened by psilocybin may last days to weeks, during which
therapeutic interventions and behavioral changes can be more deeply

## Treatment-Resistant Depression
Psilocybin has shown particular promise for treatment-resistant depression
(TRD), defined as depression that has not responded to at least two adequate
courses of antidepressant medication. A Phase II trial at Imperial College
London found that a single 25 mg dose of psilocybin produced significant
reductions in depression scores that were sustained at twelve weeks
post-treatment. A multicenter Phase II trial published in the New England
Journal of Medicine in 2021 compared psilocybin (25 mg) to the SSRI
escitalopram and found comparable antidepressant effects with more rapid
onset.
