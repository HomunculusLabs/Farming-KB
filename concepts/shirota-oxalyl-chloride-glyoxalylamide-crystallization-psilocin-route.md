# Oxalyl Chloride Glyoxalylamide Crystallization in Psilocin Synthesis

## Overview

In the concise large-scale synthesis of [[glyoxalylamide-intermediate-in-tryptamine-synthesis]] (6) via oxalyl chloride
represents a critical two-step acylation-amidation sequence. This step
produces a distinctive yellow [[shirota-psilocybin-crystalline-product-catalytic-hydrogenolysis]] and proceeds in over
80% yield, making it highly suitable for gram-scale production without
chromatographic purification.

## The Two-Step Acylation-Amidation Sequence

The conversion from 4-acetylindole (4) to 3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-
acetylindole (6) involves two sequential reactions performed without
isolating the intermediate:

### Step 1: Oxalyl Chloride Acylation

The first step introduces the oxalyl group at the 3-position of the
indole ring:

- **Reagent**: Oxalyl chloride ((COCl)₂), added in excess
- **Solvent**: Anhydrous [[diethyl-ether]]
- **Temperature**: Ice bath (0°C) for addition, then n-hexane added
  and the flask placed in a freezer overnight
- **Product**: A yellow crystalline solid (5), the glyoxalyl chloride
  intermediate

The procedure involves dissolving 4-acetylindole (17.6 g, 100 mmol) in
anhydrous diethyl ether (100 mL) with stirring in an ice bath. Oxalyl
chloride (13 mL, 146 mmol) is added, representing approximately 1.5
equivalents. After stirring for 15 minutes, n-hexane (200 mL) is added
to precipitate the product, and the reaction flask is placed in a
freezer and stored overnight.

### Step 2: Dimethylamine Amidation

The second step converts the glyoxalyl chloride intermediate to the
glyoxalylamide:

- **Reagent**: 2M dimethylamine in tetrahydrofuran (THF)
- **Catalyst/base**: Pyridine
- **Temperature**: Ice bath for addition, then room temperature
- **Workup**: Filtration, washing with n-hexane, ethyl acetate, and
  water

The yellow crystal (5) is separated from solution by filtration and
dissolved in anhydrous THF (100 mL). To this solution in an ice bath is
added the dimethylamine solution (60 mL, 120 mmol, 1.2 equivalents) and
pyridine (10 mL, 123 mmol) over 15 minutes. Additional anhydrous ether
is added to prevent solidification. After stirring for 15 minutes at
room temperature, the product (6) is collected by filtration and washed
sequentially with n-hexane, ethyl acetate, and water.

## Product Characterization: Compound 6

The [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-intermediate-oxalyl-chloride-acylation-pathway]] (6) is obtained as an ivory white
crystalline powder in 80.0% yield (22.0 g from 17.6 g starting
material). Key spectroscopic data include:

- δ 10.40 (1H, br s, H-1): The indole NH proton, shifted downfield
  by the adjacent electron-withdrawing glyoxalylamide group
- δ 7.52 (1H, d, J = 3.2 Hz, H-2): The proton at the 2-position of
  the indole ring
- δ 7.15 (1H, t, J = 8.0 Hz, H-6): Aromatic proton
- δ 7.06 (1H, d, J = 8.0 Hz, H-7): Aromatic proton
- δ 6.91 (1H, d, J = 8.0 Hz, H-5): Aromatic proton
- δ 3.02 (3H, s, NCH₃) and δ 2.92 (3H, s, NCH₃): The two N-methyl
  groups, appearing as separate singlets due to restricted rotation
- δ 2.50 (3H, s, OCOCH₃): The acetyl protecting group methyl

### ¹³C NMR (CDCl₃, 100 MHz)
- δ 185.4: The ketone carbonyl of the glyoxalyl group
- δ 170.9: The amide carbonyl
- δ 168.4: The second carbonyl of the oxalyl bridge

- δ 139.2: C-7a bridgehead carbon
