# Mushroom Substrates

The substrate is the material on which mushroom mycelium grows and from which fruiting bodies derive nutrients. Selecting and preparing the right substrate is the single most important factor in successful cultivation.

Related: [[mushroom-cultivation-basics]], [[mushroom-sterile-technique]]

## [[mushroom-cultivation-grain-spawn|Grain Spawn]]

Grain spawn is the universal inoculum for expanding mycelium before introducing it to fruiting substrates.

### Grain Types

- **Rye (Secale cereale)** — gold standard for spawn. Consistent kernel size, good nutrition, 11% base moisture content. Recommended by Stamets as primary spawn grain.
- **Millet** — smaller kernels provide many more inoculation points per volume. Excellent for species needing thorough colonization. Can dry out faster; monitor moisture carefully.
- **Wheat** — widely available and affordable. Good alternative to rye. Kernels slightly larger, fewer inoculation points per volume.
- **Sorghum** — hard, round kernels resist over-cooking. Good for long storage.
- **Grass seed (Lolium perenne)** — preferred substrate for sclerotia-producing species (*P. tampanensis*, *P. mexicana*). Formula: 110g seed + 180mL water per 720mL jar. Must be fungicide-free (buy from animal feed stores). Different batches vary in water absorption — experiment. Can pre-soak overnight for more uniform hydration. See [[sclerotia-cultivation]] for full method.

### Moisture Content

Optimal grain moisture for spawn: 49-54%. Excessive water favors bacterial contamination and causes kernels to explode during sterilization, exposing starchy interiors. Balanced grain breaks apart easily into individual mycelium-covered kernels for maximum inoculation points (Stamets).

Spawn formula (quart jars): 1 cup rye grain + 170-200 ml water. Autoclave at 15-18 psi for 1 hour.

### Spawn Grain Research (Nwanze et al. 2005)

Grain choice interacts significantly with substrate medium to affect yield. Tested on Lentinus squarrosulus across 6 media:

| Spawn Grain | Best Medium For | Key Result |
|-------------|----------------|------------|
| **Corn** | Animal bedding + rice | Heaviest carpophores (0.62g dry), widest pileus (4.37cm) |
| **Millet** | Animal bedding + rice | Widest stipes (0.53cm) |
| **Wheat** | Sawdust | Best stipe diameter on sawdust (0.36cm) |

**Practical implication**: No single "best" grain — match spawn grain to your substrate. Corn spawn on nitrogen-rich media (animal bedding + rice) produced the largest overall mushrooms. Spawn preparation: boil grain 15 min (5 min for millet), drain, mix with 12g CaSO4 + 3g CaCO3 per kg grain, sterilize 20 min at 121C, incubate 2 weeks in darkness.

### Culture Medium Formulations (Nwanze et al. 2005)

Six non-composted media tested for Lentinus squarrosulus cultivation:

| Medium | Components (per 250g dry) | Preparation |
|--------|--------------------------|-------------|
| Sawdust (Carey 1974) | 62.5g sawdust, 62.5g wood chips, 125g brown rice | Mix, moisten, sterilize 15 min at 121C |
| Animal bedding + rice (Roxon & Jong 1977) | 125g wood chips, 125g brown rice | Same as above |
| Lime 1 (Cangy 1994) | 195g sawdust, 50g rice bran, 2.5g CaSO4, 2.5g CaCO3 | Same as above |
| Lime 2 (Oei 1991) | 235g sawdust, 10g rice bran, 2.5g corn meal, 2.5g CaCO3 | Same as above |
| Lime 3 (Oei 1991) | 182.5g sawdust, 62.5g corn cobs, 5g CaCO3 | Same as above |
| Formulated (Nwanze 1996) | 175g sawdust, 70g rice bran, 2.5g CaCO3, 2.5g oat meal | Same as above |

**Key finding**: Animal bedding + rice medium consistently produced the best results across all three spawn grains (heaviest carpophores, widest pileus diameters), attributed to the high protein, carbohydrate, fatty acid, and amino acid content of brown rice stimulating fruiting.

## Wood-Based Substrates

### Hardwood Sawdust

The backbone of commercial indoor cultivation for shiitake, lion's mane, reishi, and others.

- Preferred species: oak, alder, maple, beech. Alder is high in sugar content, low in resins, and abundant in the [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] (Stamets).
- Avoid conifer/softwood sawdust for most species — resins are inhibitory.
- Particle size matters: sawdust alone compacts too tightly. Mix with chips (1-3 inches) for better aeration and water retention.

### Wood Chips

- Ideal for outdoor beds, wine cap, and [[stamets-outdoor-natural-culture-methods]].
- Irregular chips 1-3 inches give better results than finely ground material — greater nutrient and water reservoir with substantial surface area for primordia formation.
- Fresh chips are preferable to aged; sawdust supports rapid initial growth but soon loses vitality.

### Straw

- Wheat straw is the standard for [[comparison-reishi-vs-oyster-mushroom-cultivation]]. Pasturized at 160F for 20-30 minutes.
- Straw can be used whole or chopped; chopped permeates more rapidly.
- Chopped or whole straw adequate for Stropharia rugoso-annulata; compacted 6-12 inches deep.

## Supplemented Substrates

Supplements boost nutritional value and accelerate colonization and yields.

- **Bran (rice or wheat)** — the most common supplement. Added at 10-20% by dry weight to sawdust blocks. For shiitake, sawdust:bran ratio of 4:1 is standard. Rice bran promoted fastest mycelial growth in shiitake trials (Han et al. 1981).
- **Gypsum (CaSO4)** — added at 1-3% to buffer pH and improve texture. Provides calcium and sulfur. Prevents substrate clumping.
- **[[peat-calcium-carbonate-perlite-casing-psilocybe-cubensis-miniculture]] (CaCO3)** — pH buffer, typically 0.2% for sawdust blocks.
- **Other supplements tested**: yeast powder (2%), soybean meal (5%), milk powder (2%), molasses (1.5%) — all increased mycelial growth over rice bran alone in shiitake trials.

## Pasteurization vs Sterilization

| Method | Temperature | Duration | Use Case |
|--------|-----------|----------|----------|
| Hot water pasteurization | 160F (71C) | 20-30 min | Straw, wood chip beds for oysters, wine cap |
| Steam pasteurization | 160-170F | 1-2 hours | Bulk substrates in barrels or steam chambers |
| [[boil-a-bag-pressure-sterilization-technique]] | 15-18 psi (~250F) | 1-2 hours | Grain spawn, [[stamets-supplemented-sawdust-substrate-preparation]] bags |

Pasteurization kills most competitors but leaves beneficial thermotolerant organisms. Sterilization kills everything — required for supplemented substrates which would otherwise be colonized by contaminants faster than mushroom mycelium.

## C:N Ratios

- **Grain spawn**: approximately 30:1 (inherent in whole grains)
- **Straw**: 80-120:1 (low nitrogen, good for pasteurization without supplements)
- **Hardwood sawdust**: 200-500:1 (very low nitrogen, requires supplementation)
- **Supplemented sawdust (4:1)**: approximately 40-60:1
- **Composted substrates**: 17-25:1 (fully composted horse manure for Agaricus)

High C:N substrates are more resistant to contamination but colonize slowly. Supplementation narrows the ratio, accelerating growth but requiring sterilization.

## Container Types

- **Mason jars (quart/0.5 gallon)** — standard for grain spawn. Narrow mouth preferred for spawn production (smaller opening = less contamination risk). Synthetic filter discs with 3/8-1/2 inch hole in lid.
- **Polypropylene bags with filter patches** — industry standard for sawdust blocks. Autoclavable. Various sizes from 2-20 lb capacity. Filter patches allow gas exchange while blocking contaminants.
- **Logs (3-4 ft x 4-6 in diameter)** — traditional method for shiitake. Oak and alder preferred. Inoculated with dowel or sawdust spawn. Produce for 3-5 years.
- **Outdoor beds** — for wine cap, Stropharia, and natural culture methods. 4-6 inches deep, 2 feet wide, covered with plastic or cardboard during colonization.

## Synthetic Log Substrate Formulation

Synthetic logs combine the natural log geometry with supplemented sawdust nutrition. Standard formulation (per log, approximately 5 lb wet weight):

| Ingredient | Dry Weight | Function |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Hardwood sawdust (oak/alder/maple) | 600g | Base substrate, carbon source |
| Wheat bran or rice bran | 120-150g | Nitrogen supplement (20-25%) |
| Gypsum (CaSO4) | 12g | pH buffer, calcium, texture |
| Calcium carbonate (CaCO3) | 6g | pH buffer |
| Water | 1.2-1.3L | 55-65% moisture content |

Mix thoroughly, pack into 3-4 inch diameter x 12-18 inch polypropylene tubes or lay flat bags. Sterilize at 15 psi for 90-120 min. Cool to room temperature before inoculation. Inoculate with 5-10% grain spawn by weight.

**Moisture test**: Squeeze a handful of substrate firmly -- a few drops of water should appear between fingers but no stream. Too dry = no drops; too wet = water streams freely.
