# Mycorrhizal Fertilizer Reduction

## Overview

One of the most compelling practical benefits [[query-what-are-the-different-types-of-mycorrhizal-fungi]] for
agriculture and horticulture is their potential to reduce or eliminate
the need for [[teaming-with-nutrients-natural-vs-synthetic-fertilizers]], particularly phosphorus.

As fertilizer costs rise and phosphorus supplies become constrained,
mycorrhizal partnerships offer a sustainable alternative that can
maintain or increase crop yields while reducing pollution from nutrient
runoff.

Source: Lowenfels and Lewis, *[[teaming-with-fungi-mycorrhizal-inoculation-techniques]]*.

## The Inhibition Threshold

### Critical Phosphorus Levels

The amount of phosphorus in the soil greatly affects [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-spore-extraction-methods]]
germination and formation:

- With too much phosphorus, spores are less likely to germinate
- Mycorrhizal growth is hindered
- Some experts recommend less than **80 parts per million** of
  phosphorus for establishing [[glomeromycota-arbuscular-mycorrhizae]]
- Other experts recommend fewer than **75 micrograms of phosphorus
  per gram** of soil
- These figures are contested, as some plants readily form
  mycorrhizae even with high phosphorus

### Nitrogen Effects

Excess nitrogen similarly inhibits mycorrhizal formation.

The relationship is dose-dependent: as more fertilizer is added,
[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]] generally declines, and as fungal colonies
decrease, phosphorus concentration in plant tissues also decreases.

This creates a negative feedback loop where synthetic fertilizers
gradually reduce the very fungal partnerships that could make them
unnecessary.

## Understanding Fertilizer Labels

### N-P-K Numbers

Most fertilizers are labeled with N-P-K percentages, but the numbers
are misleading:

- The nitrogen number represents actual nitrogen percentage by weight
- The phosphorus number represents phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5), not
  actual phosphorus
- The potassium number represents potassium oxide (K2O), not actual
  potassium

**To convert to actual nutrient weight:**

- Multiply phosphorus number by **0.44**
- Multiply potassium number by **0.83**

This matters for calculating actual phosphorus [[ingham-compost-tea-application-rates-and-timing]] and
staying below levels that inhibit mycorrhizae.

### Organic vs. Inorganic Fertilizers

Studies indicate that:

- **Organic fertilizers** are generally compatible with mycorrhizae
- **Phosphorus-rich inorganic fertilizers** can inhibit mycorrhizal
  growth
- Some [[cervantes-organic-amendments-chart]] (manure-heavy composts) can build up
  excessive phosphorus over time

## How Mycorrhizae Replace Fertilizer

### Phosphorus Acquisition

[[mycorrhizal-fungi]] extend hyphal networks far beyond the root depletion
zone, accessing phosphorus that roots alone cannot reach.
