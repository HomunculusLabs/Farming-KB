## The Gut-Brain Axis

The gut-brain axis is a bidirectional communication network linking the
enteric nervous system with the central nervous system through the vagus
nerve, immune signaling, and microbial metabolites. The ECS is a critical
mediator of this axis:

- Vagal afferent fibers expressing CB1 relay gut status to the brainstem.
- Central CB1 receptors in the hypothalamus and amygdala modulate gut
  function in response to stress and emotional states.
- Stress-induced endocannabinoid depletion in the gut contributes to
  stress-related GI symptoms ("nervous stomach," IBS flares).

This bidirectional relationship explains why psychological stress
exacerbates GI conditions and why gut dysfunction affects mood and
anxiety. Cannabis-based therapies can target both ends of the axis
simultaneously.

## Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

IBS is a functional GI disorder characterized by abdominal pain, bloating,
and altered bowel habits (diarrhea, constipation, or alternating) without
identifiable structural abnormalities. It affects 10-15% of the population
and is strongly associated with stress, anxiety, and visceral
hypersensitivity.

The ECS relevance to IBS includes:

- **Visceral pain modulation**: CB1 receptors on visceral afferent pathways
  reduce pain signaling from the gut to the brain. IBS patients show
  altered CB1 expression patterns.
- **Motility regulation**: CB1 activation slows gut motility (relevant to
  IBS-D), while CB2 activation may help normalize motility patterns.

by transmural inflammation that can affect any part of the GI tract from
mouth to anus. It involves immune dysregulation, gut barrier dysfunction,
and dysbiosis.
