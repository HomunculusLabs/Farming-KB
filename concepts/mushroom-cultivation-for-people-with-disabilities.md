## The Three-in-One Training Approach

The FAO manual proposes a "three-in-one" training model that integrates three objectives simultaneously:

### 1. Rehabilitation
- Building physical capabilities, confidence, and self-esteem through productive activity.
- Developing fine motor skills through tasks like inoculation, bag opening, and harvesting.
- Encouraging social interaction and reducing isolation.

### 2. Mushroom Cultivation Skills
- Complete technical training from [[substrate-preparation]] preparation and bagging.
- **Week 4** — Pasteurization, inoculation, incubation.
- **Week 5** — Bag opening, fruiting management, environmental control.
- **Week 6** — Harvesting, packaging, marketing.
- **Week 7** — Processing and preservation.
- **Week 8** — Business planning, financial management, review.

### From Theory to Practice

The manual stresses that mushroom cultivation is a practical skill that cannot be learned from lectures alone. The recommended approach is:

- Minimum 70% of training time spent on hands-on activities.
- Each trainee prepares their own substrate bags and manages them through the full cycle.
- Trainees experience real problems (contamination, environmental fluctuations) and learn to solve them.
- Continuous practice beyond the formal training period is essential.

## Responsibilities

### Training Team Duties

- **Technical instruction** — Teaching all cultivation steps accurately.
- **Adaptation** — Modifying tools, workspace, and techniques for individual needs.
- **Assessment** — Regularly checking each trainee's understanding and skill development.
- **Encouragement** — Maintaining motivation and addressing frustration.

### Administrative Duties

- Managing training materials, supplies, and equipment.
- Maintaining attendance records and progress documentation.
- Coordinating with supporting organizations and funding agencies.

### Financial Assistance

The manual recommends providing startup materials to trainees upon completion:

- Initial supply of substrate bags or spawn.
- Basic tools and equipment adapted to their needs.
- Construction materials or assistance for a home mushroom house.

### Monitoring and Evaluation

Post-training follow-up is critical for sustainability:

- **Home visits** — Check that trainees have set up their mushroom house correctly.
- **Knowledge verification** — Ensure cultivation techniques are being properly applied.
- **Material delivery** — Confirm trainees received their startup materials.
- **Sustainability check** — Assess whether the enterprise is economically viable.
- **Family and community engagement** — Encourage support from the trainee's social network.
- **Permanent support** — Ongoing technical assistance rather than one-time training.
- **Record-keeping** — Document all visits, outcomes, and lessons learned.

## Cultural and Social Context

The FAO manual was developed in the context of Thailand but its principles are universally applicable. Key cultural considerations include:

- **Stigma reduction** — Demonstrating that people with disabilities can be productive entrepreneurs changes community perceptions.
- **Family engagement** — In many cultures, family members play a central role in supporting or undermining the enterprise.
- **Gender dynamics** — Women with disabilities may face double discrimination; training programs must address this explicitly.
- **Opening and closing ceremonies** — Public ceremonies at the start and end of training build community recognition and trainee pride.
- **Certificates of achievement** — Formal recognition reinforces accomplishment and can be shown to potential customers.

## Impact and Significance

The FAO three-in-one approach represents a model for disability-inclusive rural development that goes beyond charity or welfare. By combining rehabilitation with practical skills and business training, it creates a pathway to genuine economic independence. Mushroom cultivation's low physical barriers make it an ideal entry point, while the business skills developed are transferable to other enterprises if the trainee chooses to diversify.
