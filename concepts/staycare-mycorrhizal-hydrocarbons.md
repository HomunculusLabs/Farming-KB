# Mycorrhizal Fungi and Hydrocarbon Contamination

## Impact of Hydrocarbons on Mycorrhizal Colonization

### VAM Fungi in Petroleum-Contaminated Soils

Vesicular-arbuscular mycorrhizal (VAM) fungi occur in both petroleum-polluted and nonpolluted soils, but contamination significantly alters [[cynodon-dactylon]], Solidago sp., [[edaphic-determinants-cannabis-microbiome-community-structure]], inhibiting some morphotypes while stimulating others. Some morphotypes appear only at highest oil concentrations

- **Key ECM genera**: Amanita, Cenococcum, Hebeloma, Tricholoma, Pisolithus, Laccaria, and Paxillus

## ECM Symbioses Tolerant to Petroleum Hydrocarbons

Several ECM associations demonstrate remarkable tolerance:

- **Pinus sylvestris-Suillus bovinus**: Unaffected by 2% (w/v) toluene

- **P. sylvestris-S. bovinus/[[dighton-fungi-mangrove-coastal-ecosystems]], likely because:
- Soil fungi and bacteria catalyze bioremediation processes
- Certain ECM fungi survive and utilize crude oil as a nutrient
- Soil [[staycare-chlorophenol-degradation-pathways-white-rot-fungi]]

## Knowledge Gaps and Research Needs

Key areas requiring further investigation:
- Quantitative contribution of mycorrhizal fungi to total petroleum hydrocarbon degradation
- Tolerance limits of different mycorrhizal species to various hydrocarbon fractions
- Role of nutrient amendments (nitrogen, phosphorus) in supporting mycorrhizal hydrocarbon degradation
- Host plant growth and survival in hydrocarbon-contaminated soils
- Viability and infectivity of mycorrhizal propagules in contaminated soils
- Synergistic interactions between mycorrhizal fungi and hydrocarbon-degrading bacteria

## Implications for Farm and Land Remediation

- **Land rehabilitation**: ECM associations are well-suited for establishing vegetation on oil-contaminated agricultural and forest land

- **Low-cost approach**: Natural mycorrhizal associations can be enhanced through inoculation without expensive soil treatment

- **Forestry applications**: Contaminated land can be turned over to forestry (timber or [[dighton-fungal-biomass-production-aquatic-ecosystems]]) while mycorrhizal associations facilitate gradual degradation

- **Sewage sludge application**: Established forests receiving sewage sludge can benefit from mycorrhizal-mediated waste incorporation into surface organic soil horizons
