# Moringa — Horseradish Tree (*Moringa oleifera*)

## Overview

The horseradish tree or drumstick tree (*Moringa oleifera*, family Moringaceae) is a small deciduous tree (to 8 m tall, 3 m wide) native to India. It is widely regarded as one of the most useful trees on Earth, with virtually every part of the plant serving human needs. The leaves are exceptionally nutritious, containing 7–10% protein and a full complement of vitamins and minerals. The long bean-like fruits are used in soups and curries, young leaves are eaten in salads with a mustard flavor, immature seeds are used like peas, mature seeds are roasted like peanuts, and the root is used as a horseradish substitute. The tree is [[drought-tolerant-planting-design]] but frost tender.

## Multi-Part Utility

### Edible Parts

- **Young leaves and twigs**: Eaten in salads with a mustard-like flavor
- **Leaves**: Contain 7–10% protein; cooked as a vegetable or dried into powder for nutritional supplementation
- **Long bean-like fruit**: Used in soups and curries throughout South and [[guzman-thailand-southeast-asia-psychoactive-fungi-distribution]]
- **Immature seeds**: Used like peas
- **Mature seeds**: Roasted or fried and eaten like peanuts
- **Root**: Used as a horseradish substitute (hence the common name)
- **Young seedlings**: Cooked as a vegetable

### Ben Oil

The fruit produces seeds that yield a remarkable oil called behen or ben oil that never goes rancid. This property makes it exceptionally valuable:

- **Cooking**: Used as a salad and cooking oil with extraordinary shelf stability
- **Precision machinery**: Known as "watchmakers oil" due to its use in delicate instruments — it does not gum up or oxidize
- **Cosmetics**: Valued in skin care products for its stability and skin-nourishing properties
- **Preservative**: The non-rancid quality makes it ideal for preserving foods

## Permaculture Applications

1. **Nutrition security**: Among the most nutritious leaf crops known — the dried leaf powder is used in international nutrition programs to combat malnutrition
2. **Drought adaptation**: Thrives in arid conditions where few other food trees survive
3. **Rapid growth**: Fast-establishing, providing quick yields
4. **Complete plant utilization**: Every part of the tree has utility, maximizing the yield per unit of land and water
5. **Medicinal**: Leaves, bark, and seeds have documented anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and nutritional [[raw-cannabis-thca-non-psychoactive-therapeutic-properties]]
6. **Water purification**: Moringa seed powder has been demonstrated as an effective natural coagulant for clarifying turbid water
7. **Animal fodder**: Leaf meal is high-quality livestock feed
8. **Living fence**: Branches can be used as cuttings to establish living barriers

## Cultivation Notes

Moringa is drought tolerant but frost tender. It grows quickly from seed or cuttings and begins producing leaves within months. For leaf production, the tree can be maintained at accessible height by regular harvesting or coppicing, which also promotes branching and leaf production.
