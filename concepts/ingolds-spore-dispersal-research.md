## Broader Impact on Mycology

Ingold's work established spore dispersal biology as a major subdiscipline
within mycology and influenced fields ranging from aerobiology to
biomimetics. His methods of observing and quantifying microscopic
biological processes became standard in the field. His textbooks, including
"The Biology of Fungi" (1961) and "Fungal Spores: Their Liberation and
Dispersal" (1971), educated generations of mycologists.

The drop discharge mechanism has attracted interest from engineers and
physicists as a model for microfluidic systems and microscale propulsion.
The principles Ingold elucidated — using surface tension and phase changes
to achieve controlled motion at microscale — have inspired research in
microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) and lab-on-a-chip technologies.

## A Century of Influence

Ingold's career spanned most of the 20th century, and he remained
scientifically active well into his 90s. His work exemplifies the power of
patient, detailed observation of natural phenomena — an approach that
complements and sometimes surpasses modern molecular and genomic methods.
The mechanisms he discovered continue to be studied with increasingly
sophisticated tools, and each new investigation reveals additional layers
of complexity and elegance in the systems he first described.
