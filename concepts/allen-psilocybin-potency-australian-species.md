## Primary Psychoactive Alkaloids

### Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT)

Psilocybin (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the primary
psychoactive compound in most magic mushrooms. It is a prodrug, meaning it
is converted to the active compound psilocine after ingestion by the action
of alkaline phosphatase enzymes. Psilocybin is relatively stable and
persists in dried mushroom material for extended periods if stored properly
in cool, dark conditions.

### Psilocine (4-HO-DMT)

Psilocine (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the dephosphorylated, active
form of psilocybin. It is present in smaller quantities than psilocybin in
most species but is the compound responsible for the actual psychoactive
effects. Psilocine is also the compound responsible for the characteristic
blue-staining reaction observed when psilocybin-containing mushrooms are
bruised. Its oxidation product is the blue pigment that gives bruised
mushrooms their distinctive coloration.

### Baeocystin (4-PO-NMT)

Baeocystin (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N-methyltryptamine) is a minor alkaloid
found in many psilocybin-containing mushrooms. Its pharmacological activity
is less well characterized than psilocybin or psilocine, but it is believed
to contribute to the overall psychoactive effects. Baeocystin is a
demethylated analog of psilocybin and is found in varying concentrations
across different species.

### Norbaeocystin

Norbaeocystin is the N-demethylated analog of baeocystin and is present in
trace amounts. Its psychoactive contribution, if any, is minimal and it is
typically not included in standard potency analyses.
