# Event Classification and Orders of Magnitude

## Overview

In [[permaculture]] pattern theory, all phenomena—whether geological, biological, social, or conceptual—can be classified systematically by their nature, stage of development, dimensionality, and location. This classification framework provides designers with a universal language for analyzing and comparing events across scales, from subatomic particles to galaxies. Understanding orders of magnitude and how they govern the behavior of systems at different scales is essential for creating designs that work with, rather than against, natural patterns.

## Orders and Dimensions in Branching

### The Principle of Order

At each point of branching (or size and volume change), everything changes simultaneously—pressures, flows, velocities, gaseous exchange rates, and the life forms that associate with the specific size of branches. This principle explains:

- **Fish species distribution in streams**: Each species occupies a specific stream order suited to its morphology and behavior
- **Bird species in trees**: Different bird species are associated with specific branch diameters and positions
- **Human settlement patterns**: Villages stabilize at approximately 1,000 people, becoming unstable at significantly smaller or larger scales
- **Trophic pyramids**: Each level supports approximately 10× the biomass of the level above

### Universal Scaling Laws

An array of orders is observed across a wide range of phenomena:

| Domain | Factor of Change Between Orders |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| River branches | ~3× |
| Trophic pyramids | ~10× |
| Human settlements | Variable, with stable thresholds |
| Physical entities (protons to universes) | Increasing ratios with scale |

Physical entities from protons to universes display order-dependent behavior, with consequent changes in ratios, dimensions, and associated behaviors. Giant and dwarf stars, for example, behave very differently despite being the same type of object at different scales.

### Design Applications of Order

Designers need to study and apply branching patterns and be aware of the stable orders inherent in natural systems:

- **Highways and roads**: Increasing the size of a highway alters the entire road network—upgrading one order affects all connected orders
- **Settlement size**: Choosing a settlement size determines the number and types of services, occupations, shops, offices, and access networks needed
- **Dam spillways**: Sequences should follow natural branching orders for stability
- **Functional hierarchies**: The number of people admitted to information-passing hierarchies should respect natural order ratios

### Function Over Status

A critical insight from pattern study is the need to distinguish **function** from **status**:

- A tree stem is not less important than its leaves in total—each needs the other
- Removing a dominant animal from a behavioral hierarchy simply creates a new dominant from lower orders
- Removing subordinates causes new subordinates to be created from within the dominants
- The same applies to streams: altering one aspect changes the entire regime

The permaculture view reframes hierarchy as functional reciprocity: *"It is not what you are; it is what you do in relation to the society you choose to live in."* Each part needs every other part, and none functions without the others. This principle has profound implications for social design, organizational structure, and ecological management.

## Classification of Events

All events in nature can be classified along four axes, creating a systematic annotation system for any phenomenon:

### A. Nature

| Code | Category | Examples |
|------|----------|----------|
| A1 | Explosive, disintegration, erosion, impact | Volcanic eruption, landslide, meteor impact |
| A2 | Growth, integration, construction, translation | Plant growth, crystal formation, animal development |
| A3 | Conceptual, idea, creative thought, insight | Scientific discovery, design inspiration |

### B. Stage of Development

| Code | Stage | Description |
|------|-------|-------------|
| B1 | Potential only | Ungerminated seed, unexplored idea, unexploded bomb |
| B2 | In process of evolution | Growing plant, developing concept, active erosion |
| B3 | Completed | Growth and expansion ceased, fully articulated form |
| B4 | Decaying | Disintegrative, replaced or invaded by new events, disarticulated |

### C. Dimension

| Code | Dimensions | Phenomena |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| C1 | One | Linear phenomena, curves, streams |
| C2 | Two | Surface phenomena, tessellations, dendrites |
| C3 | Three | Solid phenomena, trees, organisms |
| C4 | Four | Moving solid phenomena (includes time dimension) |
| C5 | More | Conceptual phenomena, models of particles or forces, energy states |

### D. Location

| Code | Location Type | Examples |
|------|--------------|----------|
| D1 | Generating across equi-potential surfaces | Storms at sea, wave propagation |
| D2 | Within media | Weather frontal systems, soil processes |
| D3 | Through surfaces at ~90° | Trees growing from ground, roots through soil |
| D4 | Englobements | Some explosions, organisms |
| D5 | Outside normal space-time | Ideas, conceptual entities |

### Annotation Examples

Using this system, any event can be given a compact classification:

- **A sapling**: A2, B2, C3, D3 — Growing (A2), evolving (B2), three-dimensional (C3), growing through a surface (D3)
- **A falling bomb**: A1, B1, C4, D2 — Explosive (A1), potential (B1), four-dimensional with time (C4), within media (D2)
- **A mature forest**: A2, B3, C3, D4 — Growth (A2), completed (B3), three-dimensional (C3), englobing (D4)
- **A design concept**: A3, B1, C5, D5 — Conceptual (A3), potential only (B1), multi-dimensional (C5), outside normal space-time (D5)

## Time and Relativity in the Pattern Model

### The Present as Origin

The seed is the origin of the tree, but the tree can also be understood as the current time-focus of its own genealogy. Before it in time lie its ancestors; after it, its progeny. The tree exists on the plane between past and future, determining and being determined by both. Just as the stem of a tree encapsulates its history as growth rings, universal time encapsulates the tree in its own evolutionary history.

This perspective—echoing Buddhist philosophy that all time is enfolded or implicate in the present—means that current events are part of a total sequence, all of which are enfolded in the present as ancestors or siblings.

### The Moving Origin

As we stand in the plane of the present, we are:
- The sum of all our ancestors
- The origin of all our descendants
- An ever-changing origin located on the boundary of past and future
- Spinning with the earth, spiralling with the galaxy, and expanding or contracting with the universe

All these movements have characteristic pulse rates, connecting human experience to cosmic rhythms.

### Design Implications of Temporal Perspective

Designers can "move sideways in the waves of time"—transporting seed from continent to continent, permitting natural or induced hybrid plants to weave an alternative future. This positions humans as active translators of both life and death, with the responsibility to act in ways that sustain the totality of living systems.

The critical design insight is that **the point where all important action takes place is through the point of origin, which is always in the present**. How we behave now may determine not only the future but reshape our understanding of the past.
