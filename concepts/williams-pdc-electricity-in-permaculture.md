# Electricity in Permaculture While electricity exists abundantly in nature, electrical systems as humans use them are not natural. We use machinery to convert other forms of energy into electricity, then convert that electricity into radical heat or work. This is why electricity is considered something we truly "take" from nature, since there is no natural loop by which we return it. Modern humans need electricity, and the goal of a permaculture designer is to take electricity from renewable sources in a way that does not degrade ecosystems. ## The Primary Strategy: Reduce, Reduce, Reduce The first and foremost strategy in any permaculture electrical design is reduction. The best way to generate electricity without harming the environment is to not need as much in the first place. Every watt saved is a watt saved. A permaculture electrical designer should use every trick available to reduce the electricity requirement of the design. ## Natural Lighting Strategies During the day the sun provides all needed light, yet buildings often require power-burning light bulbs even in daylight. Design tricks for natural lighting include: - **Skylights**: Well-known design elements allowing sunlight in - **Light Tubes**: Metal tubes in the roof that concentrate and focus sunlight into a room - **Lightshelves**: Features added to windows that bounce light from the window up to the ceiling, which then reflects deeper into the room - **Fiber Optics**: Using fiber optic cables to harness natural sunlight and distribute it through a building - **Interior Design**: Color choices and surface materials affect how well natural light is distributed within a space ## Solar Water Heaters Solar water heaters do not use any electricity; the heat from the sun directly heats the water. They work even in moderate to cold climates. The trick is to allow sunlight to hit the water storage while keeping the storage in an insulated envelope so ambient cold does not draw the heat out. DIY solar water heaters can be made from water tanks held within refrigerators with glass fronts. You open the door in the morning, sunlight enters through the glass and heats the water, and you close the door at night to keep the water insulated. Even when used as a pre-heater, solar water heaters reduce the energy needed by standard water heating systems. ## Earthtubes (Ground Source Heat Exchangers) Several feet below the ground, below the frost line, the earth is at a constant temperature, typically around 50 degrees F. Earthtubes, being buried below ground, are always at this constant temperature. By pumping air out of the tubes and into a structure, you help to heat or cool the structure: - If the ambient temperature is above 50 degrees, the earthtube cools - If the ambient temperature is below 50 degrees, the earthtube warms This system helps moderate temperature without using significant electricity. ## Photovoltaic Panels Solar panels are a great source of renewable electricity, though they can be cost prohibitive. A good strategy is to design a phased installation, building the entire array structure but filling it in one or two panels at a time over time, much like ecological succession. Solar panels can be used for more than just power generation. Creative applications include placing them over parking lots to generate power AND shade cars while reducing the local heat island effect. They can also serve as overhangs to keep sun out of structures. Always try to stack the functions of any solar panels used in your design. ## Other Renewable Electricity Sources Beyond photovoltaics, additional renewable sources include: - **Wind Generators**: Appropriate for windy areas - **Micro-hydro Systems**: Small water-powered generators that can fit in small water courses on sites with appropriate water flow - **Bio-diesel Generators**: Diesel generators powered by vegetable oil or bio-diesel as a backup or supplemental source ## Energy Efficient Appliances In addition to designing sustainable electricity sources, always choose energy efficient appliances to reduce overall consumption. Combined with passive design strategies, this can dramatically reduce the electricity needs of any design. ## Lifestyle and Electricity There is a direct connection between lifestyle and electricity consumption. Being sustainable requires adjusting lifestyle habits to reduce energy consumption. Architecture shapes habits, so designing spaces that encourage lower-energy lifestyles is part of the permaculture approach. ## The Off-Grid Goal The ultimate goal for many permaculture designers is to be entirely off-grid, not hooked into the local power company. While this is a laudable goal, it is not always feasible. However, you can always design to reduce electricity consumption regardless of whether full off-grid capability is achievable. ## Use and Value Diversity There is no single magic wand for solving electricity consumption. Use as many systems as possible, all working together in unison.
