## Cover Cropping Guide

Cover crops are plants grown primarily to benefit the soil rather than for harvest. They protect soil from erosion, add organic matter, fix nitrogen, suppress weeds, and feed the [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]].

## Why Cover Crop

### Benefits to Soil
- **Erosion prevention** — roots hold soil; foliage breaks rain impact
- **Organic matter** — roots and top-growth add humus when terminated
- **Nitrogen fixation** — legumes convert atmospheric N to plant-available form
- **Nutrient mining** — deep-rooted species bring subsoil minerals to surface
- **Soil structure** — root channels improve water infiltration and aeration
- **Weed suppression** — competitive canopy shades out weed seeds
- **Pest disruption** — breaks pest and disease cycles
- **Microbial food** — root exudates and residue feed [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]]
- **Moisture retention** — mulch layer reduces evaporation

### The Fallow Year Concept
Steve Solomon recommends letting garden beds go fallow every 3-4 years:

1. Plant a cover crop mix instead of vegetables
2. Let it grow the full season without harvesting
3. Terminate and incorporate in fall
4. This restores soil structure and nutrient reserves
5. Especially important for beds showing declining productivity

## Cover Crop Families

### Legumes (Nitrogen Fixers)

| Crop | N Fixed (lbs/ac) | Best Season | Hardiness | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| Field beans | 40 | Warm | Tender | Easy, reliable |
| Hairy vetch | 80 | Cool | Hardy | Excellent N fixer; climb structure needed |
| Crimson clover | 50-100 | Cool | Moderate | Beautiful red flowers; good bee forage |
| Red clover | 140 | Cool | Hardy | Biennial; can undersow in corn |
| White clover | 100 | Cool | Hardy | Living mulch; tolerates foot traffic |
| Cowpea | 80-120 | Warm | Tender | Heat/drought tolerant |
| Austrian winter pea | 100-140 | Cool/Fall | Very hardy | Excellent fall-planted cover |
| Sweet clover | 160 | Cool | Hardy | Deep taproot (6+ ft); biennial |
| Alfalfa | 250 | Cool | Hardy | Perennial; deep taproot; needs good drainage |

**Key rule**: Terminate legumes BEFORE they set seed. Nitrogen moves from leaves to seeds as pods form. Maximum nitrogen in leaves is at early flowering.

### Grasses (Soil Builders)

| Crop | Primary Benefit | Best Season | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------------|-------|
| Winter rye | Erosion control, weed suppression | Fall/Winter | Most cold-hardy; vigorous root system |
| Oats | Quick cover, biomass | Cool | Winter-kill in cold climates (easy termination) |
| Annual ryegrass | Quick establishment, erosion | Cool/Fall | Can become weedy if let go to seed |
| Barley | Quick cover, salt tolerant | Cool | Good in alkaline soils |
| Sorghum-sudangrass | Massive biomass, weed suppression | Warm | Excellent summer cover; requires warm soil |
| Buckwheat | P scavenger, quick cover | Warm | Flowers in 30 days; attracts pollinators; terminates at frost |

### Brassicas (Soil Conditioners)

| Crop | Primary Benefit | Best Season | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------------|-------|
| Daikon radish | Deep soil penetration, compaction break | Cool/Fall | "Tillage radish" — taproot goes 3-6 ft |
| Mustard | Biofumigation, P scavenging | Cool | Releases glucosinolates that suppress soil pathogens |
| Forage radish | Compaction break, N scavenging | Cool/Fall | Similar to daikon; winter-kills leaving channels |

### Broadleaves (Multi-Purpose)

| Crop | Primary Benefit | Best Season | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------------|-------|
| Phacelia | Bee forage, quick cover | Cool/Warm | Beautiful flowers; no known pests |
| Sunflower | Deep root, bee forage | Warm | Tall; good for chickens to forage under |
| Chicory | Deep taproot, mineral miner | Warm/Perennial | Multi-year; good in pastures |

## Cover Crop Strategies

### 1. Simple Fallow Year
- Plant one cover crop for entire season
- Good choice: clover + grass mix
- Terminate in fall, let residue decompose over winter
- Plant vegetables next spring

### 2. Relay Cover Cropping
- Undersow cover crop into standing vegetable crop
- Example: sow white clover under corn at last cultivation
- Clover grows after corn harvest, covers through winter
- No fallow period needed

### 3. Winter Cover
- After vegetable harvest, sow winter rye + Austrian winter pea
- Rye provides erosion control; peas fix nitrogen
- Terminate in spring (crimp or mow before planting)

### 4. Summer Cover (Between Successions)
- Quick covers between spring and fall crops
- Buckwheat (6 weeks) or cowpea (8 weeks)
- Terminate and plant fall crop directly into residue

### 5. Living Mulch
- Maintain low-growing cover (white clover) between crop rows
- Clover fixes N, suppresses weeds, moderates soil temperature
- Mow or clip to prevent competition with main crop

## Termination Methods

| Method | How | Best For | Notes |
|--------|-----|----------|-------|
| Crimping | Roll with crimper at flowering | Winter rye, vetch | No-till; leaves mulch mat |
| Mowing | Cut at soil level | Most covers | May need multiple passes |
| Tillage | Incorporate into soil | All | Destroys mycorrhizal network |
| Winter kill | Let frost kill | Oats, buckwheat, sorghum | Easiest; plan timing right |
| Herbicide | Spray (non-organic) | All | Defeats organic purpose |

**Best practice for [[no-till-gardening-method]]**: Use winter-kill species or crimper. Avoid tillage termination when possible.
