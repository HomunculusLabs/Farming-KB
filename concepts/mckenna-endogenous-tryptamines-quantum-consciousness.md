## Endogenous Tryptamines and Quantum Consciousness

The hypothesis that endogenous tryptamine compounds—particularly N,N-
dimethyltryptamine (DMT)—play a role in normal and altered states of
consciousness represents one of the most provocative intersections of
neurochemistry, consciousness studies, and quantum biology. This line of
inquiry asks whether the human brain manufactures its own psychedelic
substances and, if so, what functional role they serve.

## Historical Background

The search for endogenous psychotomimetic compounds dates to the 1950s, when
researchers first speculated that schizophrenia might be caused by an
endogenous hallucinogen. This "transmethylation hypothesis" proposed that
aberrant methylation of endogenous amines could produce psychedelic compounds
in situ. While this specific hypothesis was largely abandoned, the broader
question of whether the brain synthesizes tryptamine derivatives with
psychoactive properties remains open and actively investigated.
