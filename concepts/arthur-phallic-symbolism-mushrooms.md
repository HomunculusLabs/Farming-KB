# Phallic Symbolism of Mushrooms in Ancient Religions

## The Androgynous Nature of the Mushroom

The mushroom itself has very interesting features that resemble, and have been
related to, Gold, Flesh, Blood (and blood vessels), Phallus, Vulva, Fire,
Saucer, Cup, as well as a disc or orb. Anthropomorphically the mushroom is
androgynous -- in one sense, being one entity yet having both male and female
sexual organs visible. In another sense it is two separate beings, joined
together in the act of creation, albeit only the sexual organs are visible.

The red of the mushroom cap, as well as the juice, are anthropomorphized as
blood, and figuratively related to as fire. The cap is also considered the
female genital organ, as was the cup (like the Holy Grail) from ancient times.
While the mushroom stem is the phallus. This dual nature made the mushroom a
perfect symbol for the creative act throughout ancient religious traditions.

## Enki and Ninhursag: Stem and Cap

In the Sumerian tradition, Enki is the stem, Ninhursag the cap, the two
joining in accord to produce the Adama (humanity). The mushroom was
anthropomorphized into the two elementary archetypal creators. Enki and his
sister Ninhursag were the creators of the Adama. The substance fed to the
faithful was known as "Star-Fire," produced from the lunar essence, or blood
(from the womb), of the goddess Ninhursag. It is also called "the gold of the
gods" and "the red gold" -- all descriptors that map to the Amanita muscaria
and its effects.

## The Djed Pillar: Osiris and the Phallus

The Djed pillar of ancient Egypt is associated with the Cedars of Lebanon.
Osiris, the god of the underworld, the moon, of plants and vegetation, is
connected to it. Some say the Djed symbolizes the penis of Osiris, which was
never found after Set cut Osiris into 13 pieces and hid them.

Isis found all of his parts and re-assembled them, except she could not find
the penis. Using magic she fashioned a penis for him from a living tree (or
part of a tree) and used it to impregnate herself on December 21st (winter
Solstice). Osiris lay dead for 3 days (as the sun stopped its north/south
motion) and on December 25th Isis gave birth to Horus. Other stories say that
Set put Osiris into a coffin and floated his body down the Nile, and Isis found
him grown into a Cedar tree (the Djed). The Djed was used in fertility rites
and rites of passage for young females as a practice phallus.

## Rudra: The Red God of the Forest

The Hindu god Rudra provides a remarkable parallel to the Osiris myth. Rudra
is the red god of the forest whose red phallus grows in the forest. He was
murdered in the forest, his penis severed. As his revenge, his severed red
penis returns every year as the mushroom. This myth directly parallels the
Egyptian story of Osiris and the Djed pillar.

We cannot dismiss the similarities when we compare the phallus of Rudra (Red
Hindu god whose red phallus grows in the forest) with the Egyptian traditions.
Nor the appearance of the mushrooms in the mythology of Odin, wherein his
horse's spittle mingled with blood, as it hit the ground, sprouted the Amanita
mushrooms.

## Hu and Saa: Mushroom Gods of Nature

In the Egyptian Book of the Dead, Tchefau is the name of the food on which the
gods lived, comparable to the Ambrosia and Nectar of Olympus. Hu is called the
god of taste, and the papyrus of Ani states that the gods Hu and Saa were
created from the drops of blood that landed on the ground when Ra cut off his
phallus. These gods, created from blood falling to earth, are anthropomorphized
mushroom gods of nature. The mushroom cap's red color, associated with blood,
and the mushroom's appearance from the ground after rain, provide the natural
basis for these creation myths involving severed phalluses and spilled divine
blood.

## Mushroom Disc and Stem: Separation and Union

In Hindu and Buddhist temple art, a fine depiction of a mushroom disc separated
from its stem represents the separation of the sexes. The phallic symbol is
most obvious: the disc-shaped cap of the mushroom is the female organ. The
Nymphs are depicted worshiping the phallus by dancing in an enticing way to
provoke the phallus to join them (the universal female disc), in the holy act
of creation. An incredible imagery -- the hope being the phallus joining the
cap to form the union, sex, which is truly the universal process of creation.

## Hari Hari: Androgynous Creator Deity

Hari Hari is a Hindu deity that is Shiva and Vishnu combined. Many times Hari
Hari is portrayed as androgynous (containing both male and female organs),
which is another symbolic reference to the mushroom. Mushroom stem and cap,
anthropomorphically, are the organs of male and female joined together in the
act of creation. The mushroom as androgynous creator runs throughout Hindu
iconography, representing the fundamental creative principle of the universe.

## Fertility Rites and the Mushroom

The Djed was used in fertility rites and rites of passage for young females as
a practice phallus. This connects directly to the mushroom's role in ancient
fertility ceremonies across multiple cultures. The mushroom's phallic shape,
combined with its ability to "spring from the earth" after rain (seemingly
without seed), made it a natural symbol for fertility, procreation, and the
creative life force.

In the Sumerian tradition, the laws imposed by the Anunnaki included "You
will not have sex without the permission of the gods" -- a direct reference to
the control of fertility and reproduction by those who understood the mushroom's
role in consciousness expansion and sexual liberation. Enki, who represented
fertility and sexuality among his attributes, was the one who defied these laws
and gave humanity access to the fruit of knowledge.

## The Universal Pattern of Phallic Mushroom Mythology

Across cultures -- Egyptian, Sumerian, Hindu, Norse, and Christian -- the same
pattern emerges: a god is dismembered, his phallus is severed, and from the
blood or body that falls to earth, the sacred mushroom springs forth. Osiris
is cut into 13 pieces by Set; Rudra is murdered and his red phallus returns as
the mushroom; Odin's horse's spittle mixed with blood produces the Amanita; Ra
cuts off his phallus and the drops of blood create the nature gods Hu and Saa.

These parallel myths, spanning millennia and continents, point to a common
source tradition in which the mushroom's physical form -- phallic stem beneath
a rounded cap -- served as the basis for understanding the creative principle
of the universe, the union of male and female, and the mystery of generation
and regeneration.
