# Beneficial Insects and Pollinators in the Garden

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Attracting beneficial insects and pollinators is one of the most effective strategies for natural pest control and garden productivity. A garden that supports a diverse population of insects, birds, and other helpful animals requires far less intervention than one that relies on chemical controls. This approach is central to [[permaculture-insectary-plants]] and [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-guild-design-in-permaculture]] approach to garden design.

## Designing for Beneficial Insects

### Provide Continuous Bloom
Plant species that flower in succession:
- **Early spring:** Crocus, snowdrops, hellebore, fruit tree blossoms, clover
- **Late spring:** Phacelia, clover, chives, catmint, yarrow
- **Summer:** Dill, fennel, sunflowers, zinnias, coneflowers, basil, lavender
- **Fall:** Asters, goldenrod, sedum, Japanese anemone, witch hazel

### Create Habitat Structure
- **Bare ground:** Ground-nesting bees (70% of native bees) need patches of bare, undisturbed soil
- **Dead wood and logs:** Habitat for beetles, solitary bees, and other insects
- **Rock piles:** Shelter for predatory beetles, spiders, and reptiles
- **Brush piles:** Overwintering habitat for many beneficial insects
- **Water sources:** Shallow dishes with pebbles for landing (change water regularly)
- **Mud sources:** Needed by mason bees for nest construction

### Avoid Pesticides
Even organic pesticides can harm beneficial insects. If pest control is necessary:
- Use targeted methods (hand-picking, row covers, insecticidal soap for specific pests)
- Spray in the evening when bees are less active
- Accept some pest damage -- a healthy ecosystem tolerates low pest levels
- Focus on building soil health and plant diversity

## Birds as Pest Controllers

Birds are among the most effective pest controllers in the garden:
- **Insectivorous birds:** Chickadees, nuthatches, warblers, wrens consume enormous quantities of insects
- **Berry-eating birds:** Also eat insects during nesting season
- **Hawks and owls:** Control rodent populations

To attract birds:
- Provide water (birdbaths, shallow dishes)
- Plant berry-producing shrubs (elderberry, serviceberry, dogwood, juniper)
- Provide nesting sites (birdhouses, dead trees, dense shrubs)
- Avoid pesticides (birds eat insects -- pesticides poison birds indirectly)
- Leave some seed heads for winter food

## Specific Pest-Predator Relationships

| Pest | Natural Enemy | Attract With |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| Aphids | Ladybugs, lacewings, parasitic wasps, hover flies | Dill, fennel, yarrow, sweet alyssum |
| Codling moth | Trichogramma wasps | Umbelliferous flowers |
| Cabbage worm | Parasitic wasps | Dill, mint family plants |
| Tomato hornworm | Braconid wasps | Small-flowered plants |
| Slugs | Ground beetles, frogs | Ground cover, rock piles |
| Spider mites | Ladybugs, lacewings | Drought-stressed plants attract them; avoid over-fertilizing |
| Whiteflies | Encarsia wasps | Marigolds, thyme |
| Japanese beetle | Tiphia wasps, nematodes | White clover (attracts Tiphia) |
