Cannabis is a tall, erect, annual herb in the family
Cannabaceae. As described by Robert Connell Clarke, it is one
of the oldest cultivated plants, with thousands of years of
co-evolution alongside human [[mycorrhizal-agricultural-practices]]. The genus
Cannabis has been the subject of extensive taxonomic debate,
with varying classifications proposed by different authorities.
Understanding its biology is essential for both conservation
and breeding purposes.

## Taxonomic Classification

The taxonomy of Cannabis has long been controversial. Most
authorities recognize a single species, [[comparison-cannabis-sativa-vs-cannabis-indica]] L.,
with several subspecies: C. sativa subsp. sativa (tall,
narrow-leafleted plants adapted to tropical and subtropical
latitudes), C. sativa subsp. indica (shorter, broader
leafleted plants from Central Asia), and C. sativa subsp.
ruderalis (small, weedy plants from Central Europe and Russia).

Some taxonomists, notably Lamarck and later Schultes, have
proposed separate species status for [[cannabis-indica]]. The
ruderalis populations are nearly always high in CBD and low in
THC. The debate continues, but most modern botanical references
follow the single-species classification while acknowledging
the distinct phenotypic groups. See [[cannabis-sex-determination-floral-biology]] for detailed [[clarke-marijuana-botany-sex-determination-reproductive-biology]].

## Genetic Diversity and Conservation

Each strain of Cannabis is genetically unique, containing at
least a few genes not found in other strains. The pressures of
human and natural selection have produced many distinct strains
adapted to unique niches within ecosystems. This genetic
diversity is nature's way of protecting the species and provides
the raw material for breeding programs.

The forces of mutation and natural selection take thousands of
years to modify gene pools, while a Cannabis blight could spread
rapidly through genetically uniform crops. Clarke warns that
the spread of patented commercial hybrids could lead to
extinction of rare native strains. The 1970 Southern corn
leaf-blight epidemic demonstrates the vulnerability of
genetically uniform crops to disease. Cannabis [[clarke-marijuana-botany-cannabinoid-biosynthesis-pathway]] for the biochemistry of cannabinoid production.

## Propagation Methods

Cannabis can be propagated sexually through seeds or asexually
through cloning (cuttage, layerage, or root division). Sexual
propagation involves recombination of genetic material from two
parents, producing variation among offspring. Asexual propagation
allows exact replication of the parental genotype. Understanding
the distinction between genotype (genetic makeup) and phenotype
(visible expression influenced by environment) is fundamental
