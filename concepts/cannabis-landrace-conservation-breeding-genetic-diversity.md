## Tom Hill's Original Haze: Preserving a Four-Way Landrace Cross

Tom Hill's work with Original Haze represents one of the most significant
efforts in preserving a foundational hybrid that was itself composed entirely
of landrace genetics. The Original Haze, originally created at Positronics
in the mid-1990s, is a four-way cross of pure landrace varieties from Mexico,
Colombia, Thailand, and South India. Tom Hill's preservation of this exact
genetic combination maintains access to the unique trait combinations that
emerged from this historic cross, which has served as the basis for countless
modern cultivars. The Original Haze exemplifies how landrace genetics, when
combined thoughtfully, can produce progeny that surpass their parents in
certain desirable characteristics while retaining the complexity and depth
of their landrace heritage.

## Threat from Commercial Hybridization

The primary threat to landrace genetics is the pervasive spread of commercial
hybrids, which can cross-pollinate with landrace populations and dilute their
genetic purity. In regions where landraces have been cultivated for centuries,
the introduction of high-potency hybrid pollen can rapidly alter local
populations. Within a few generations, the unique traits defining a landrace
can be substantially diluted or lost. The Rev and other advocates argue this
genetic erosion is accelerating as global cannabis markets expand and
commercial genetics reach previously isolated growing regions.
