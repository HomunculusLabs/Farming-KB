# Changa — Smokeable DMT-Harmala Herb Blend

## Overview

Changa is a smokable herbal blend containing N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) infused into a plant base that has been enriched with beta-carboline alkaloids, primarily harmine and harmaline derived from Banisteriopsis caapi or Peganum harmala (Syrian rue). The beta-carbolines act as reversible monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), which temporarily inhibit the monoamine oxidase enzymes in the lungs and gastrointestinal tract that would otherwise rapidly degrade DMT. This allows DMT to become orally and pulmonary active when smoked or vaporized, producing a potent psychedelic experience without the need for the complex preparation required for ayahuasca. Changa was developed in the early 2000s by Australian ethnobotanists and has since gained global popularity as a more accessible and user-friendly alternative to smoked freebase DMT.

The primary risks include psychological distress during intense experiences, interaction with serotonergic medications (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, and some tricyclic antidepressants), and the potential for cardiovascular stress in individuals with pre-existing conditions. The MAOI component, while reversible and relatively short-acting when smoked, still poses a risk of serotonin syndrome if combined with certain medications. ## Comparison with Other DMT Administration Routes

Changa represents one of several methods for consuming DMT, each with distinct pharmacokinetic and experiential profiles:

**Smoked freebase DMT** provides the most rapid and intense onset, with effects beginning within seconds and a duration of approximately 5–15 minutes. The experience is often described as being launched into an entirely different reality, with minimal transition time. However, the intensity can be overwhelming and the brevity of the experience leaves little time for integration during the peak.

**Oral ayahuasca** produces a slower onset (30–60 minutes) and a much longer duration (4–8 hours). The experience is more gradual and integrative, with significant purgative effects and deep emotional processing. Preparation is complex and time-consuming, and the taste is notoriously unpleasant.

**Vaporized changa** occupies an intermediate position in nearly all parameters. The onset is rapid but slightly more gradual than freebase DMT, the duration is extended by the MAOI potentiation, and the experience tends to be warmer and more emotionally accessible. The herbal matrix also makes changa easier to smoke for those who find vaporizing freebase crystals technically challenging.

**Pharmahuasca** refers to the oral or sublingual administration of pharmaceutical-grade DMT combined with pharmaceutical MAOIs such as moclobemide or harmine extract. This approach provides precise dosing control but lacks the full-spectrum plant chemistry that many users find contributes to the therapeutic quality of natural preparations.

## Changa Formulations and Recipes

The diversity of changa formulations reflects the creativity of the global ethnobotanical community. Some well-known formulations include:

**"1:1 Changa"** refers to a blend where the DMT content by weight is roughly equal to the weight of the herbal base. This produces a very potent preparation and is typically reserved for experienced users. The high DMT concentration means that even small amounts produce strong effects.

**"Light Changa"** contains approximately 10–20% DMT by weight and is suitable for beginners and those seeking a more gentle, controllable experience. The lower concentration allows for larger bowl sizes and longer smoking sessions without reaching overwhelming intensity.

**"Ayahuasca Changa"** uses B. caapi leaf as the primary herbal base, maximizing the presence of naturally occurring harmala alkaloids and other caapi constituents. This formulation most closely approximates the ayahuasca experience in a smokable format and is favored by practitioners who value the traditional ayahuasca plant spirit.

**"Dream Changa"** incorporates dream-enhancing herbs such as Calea zacatechichi, mugwort, and blue lotus, and is intended for use before sleep to promote lucid dreaming and visionary dream states. The DMT content is typically low (5–10%) to avoid overwhelming the dream transition.

## Integration Practices

As with other powerful psychedelic experiences, changa users benefit from intentional integration practices following the experience. Recommended practices include journaling about the experience while the memory is fresh, engaging in quiet reflection or meditation, discussing the experience with trusted friends or a therapist, creating art or music inspired by the experience, and paying attention to insights or behavioral changes that emerge in the days and weeks following. Many experienced users recommend allowing at least one week between changa sessions to fully integrate each experience and avoid tolerance buildup. The harmala alkaloids in changa also contribute to the afterglow period, with many users reporting improved mood, enhanced sensory perception, and greater emotional openness for several days following a session.

## Legal Status

The legal status of changa is complex and varies by jurisdiction, as it combines DMT (which is Schedule I in most countries) with plant materials that may or may not be controlled. In most jurisdictions, the presence of DMT renders changa illegal regardless of the herbal base. However, the practical enforcement of these laws varies considerably, and in some countries the plant materials themselves occupy legal gray areas. Users should research and understand the specific legal status of DMT, harmala alkaloids, and psychoactive plant materials in their jurisdiction before obtaining or preparing changa.
