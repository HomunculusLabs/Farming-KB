# Holzer Frost Protection Techniques

The creation of various microclimates is a core principle in Holzer's
Permaculture. It gives plants a chance to grow and thrive in areas where
they otherwise could not, enabling a greatly biodiverse garden with a wide
variety of vegetables, herbs, and fruit.

## The Science of Frost Damage

Understanding why frost damages plants is essential to preventing it:

- It is coldest just before sunrise
- When sensitive plants are exposed to the first rays of sun while frozen,
  ice within them expands during melting, causing cells to burst
- If frost is allowed to melt slowly, out of full sun, plants are hardly
  damaged

Holzer illustrates this with a simple experiment: a jar of water placed in
a freezer does not break. It is only when taken out and placed in the sun
that the jar explodes as the ice expands unevenly. The same principle
applies to plant cells.

## Avoid Morning Sun

Grow sensitive plants on the west side of buildings or structures, or
protect them by planting taller plants in front of them on the east side.
This prevents the rapid warming that causes cell rupture in frozen plant
tissue.

### Using Fruit Trees for Natural Frost Protection

Plant a fruit tree next to a frost-sensitive plant. The tree's leaves fall
in autumn and create a natural mulch around the sensitive plant. This
strategy saves the labour of manual mulching while providing wind
protection through the tree's canopy.
