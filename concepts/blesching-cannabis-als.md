## Mechanisms of Action

Researchers suggest that cannabis, with its pain-killing, muscle-relaxing,
bronchodilating, saliva-reducing, appetite-stimulating, sleep-inducing,
antioxidative, and neuroprotective properties, may serve as a practical
therapeutic agent in ALS management. A landmark 2010 Seattle trial on mice led
scientists to propose an optimal ALS treatment regimen that would include
glutamate antagonists, antioxidants, centrally acting anti-inflammatory agents,
microglial cell modulators (including TNF-alpha inhibitors), antiapoptotic
agents, neurotrophic growth factors, and mitochondrial function-enhancing
agents. Remarkably, cannabis appears to have activity in all of those areas.

## Key Research Findings

- A 2010 Swiss trial with 27 ALS patients receiving 5 mg THC twice daily
  showed no change in cramps (CHI 0)
- A 2010 University of Washington mouse study demonstrated that cannabinoids
  prolonged neuronal cell survival, delayed onset of ALS symptoms, and slowed
  disease progression (CHI 2)
- A 2010 Italian review confirmed cannabinoids regulate immune responses and
  protect nerve cell function and integrity (CHI 4)
