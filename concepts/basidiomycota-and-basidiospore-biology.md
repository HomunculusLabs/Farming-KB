## Basidiomycota and Basidiospore Biology

The Basidiomycota is one of the major phyla of the fungal kingdom, comprising
approximately 30,000 described species and an estimated 1.5 million species
total. It includes many of the most familiar and conspicuous fungi — the
mushrooms, brackets, puffballs, rusts, and smuts — as well as the more
esoteric [[cage-fungi-and-polyhedral-structures]] that Nicholas Money explores with
particular enthusiasm in *Mr. Bloomfield's Orchard*. The defining feature of
the Basidiomycota is the production of basidiospores, a unique type of sexual
spore borne on specialized cells called basidia.

## Basidiospores: The Defining Spore Type

Basidiospores are haploid spores produced externally on the surface of basidia,
which are the terminal cells of specialized reproductive hyphae. Each basidium
typically produces four basidiospores, though the number varies among species.
The basidiospores are discharged forcibly from the basidium through a mechanism
involving the rapid movement of a droplet of fluid (Buller's drop) across the
spore surface. This "ballistospore discharge" propels the spore into the air,
where it can be carried by wind currents to new locations. The mechanism is one
of the fastest cellular movements in nature, achieving accelerations of up to
10,000 times the force of gravity.

Basidiospores are typically unicellular, thin-walled, and hyaline (colorless)
or pigmented, depending on the species. Their size, shape, ornamentation, and
color are important taxonomic characters used to distinguish species within
the Basidiomycota.
