# Cannabinoid Receptor Pharmacology

[[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2]] are G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) that serve as the molecular targets of the endocannabinoid system. Discovered through the search for the mechanism of action of cannabis, these receptors are now understood to be among the most abundant GPCRs in the human body. Their activation by endogenous, plant-derived, or [[blesching-cannabis-synthetic-cannabinoids]] produces a remarkably wide range of physiological and [[hallucinogenic-mushroom-acute-psychological-effects-and-bad-trips]].

## Receptor Classification

### CB1 Receptors (Cannabinoid Receptor 1)

CB1 was the first cannabinoid receptor identified, cloned in 1990 from a rat brain cDNA library. It is now recognized as one of the most abundant G protein-coupled receptors in the mammalian brain.

**Distribution**:
- **Central nervous system**: Highest density in the brain, particularly in the cerebellum, hippocampus, basal ganglia, cerebral cortex, and [[blesching-cannabis-spinal-cord-injuries]] dorsal horn.
- **Brain regions and associated functions**:
  - Hippocampus: Memory formation and learning (explains THC-induced memory impairment).
  - Cerebellum: Motor coordination (explains THC-induced ataxia and loss of coordination).
  - Basal ganglia: Movement control (relevant to Parkinson's disease and dystonia treatment).
  - Cerebral cortex: Higher cognitive functions, perception, and executive function.
  - Hypothalamus: [[blesching-cannabis-appetite-regulation-ghrelin-leptin]] (explains THC-induced hunger/munchies).
  - Amygdala: [[blesching-cannabis-emotional-processing-expansive-constricting-emotions]] (explains THC's anxiolytic or anxiogenic effects).
  - Spinal cord dorsal horn: Pain signal transmission (explains analgesic effects).
- **Peripheral tissues**: Also found in the lungs, liver, kidneys, GI tract, [[blesching-endocannabinoid-autonomic-nervous-system-adrenal-glands]], heart, and reproductive organs, though at lower density than in the CNS.
- **Neurons**: CB1 receptors are predominantly presynaptic, located on axon terminals where they function as retrograde signaling modulators of neurotransmitter release.

**Signaling mechanism**:
- CB1 is primarily coupled to Gi/o proteins (inhibitory G proteins).
- Activation inhibits adenylyl cyclase, reducing cAMP production.
- This inhibits voltage-gated calcium channels (reducing calcium influx) and activates inwardly rectifying potassium channels (hyperpolarizing the neuron).
- Net effect: Presynaptic inhibition of neurotransmitter release, reducing the firing of neurons that express CB1.
- Neurotransmitters modulated: GABA, glutamate, dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, and acetylcholine release are all suppressed by CB1 activation.

### CB2 Receptors (Cannabinoid Receptor 2)

CB2 was identified in 1993 and is primarily associated with the immune system.

**Distribution**:
- **Immune system**: High density in B cells, T cells, natural killer cells, monocytes, macrophages, and microglia (brain immune cells).
- **Immune organs**: Spleen, tonsils, thymus, and lymph nodes.
- **Peripheral tissues**: Lower levels found in bone, skin, and the gastrointestinal tract.
- **Brain under pathology**: Under normal conditions, CB2 is minimally expressed in the brain. However, CB2 expression increases dramatically in microglia and astrocytes in response to injury, inflammation, or neurodegeneration.

**Signaling mechanism**:
- Like CB1, CB2 is coupled to Gi/o proteins.
- Activation inhibits adenylyl cyclase and reduces cAMP.
- Modulates immune cell migration, proliferation, and cytokine production.
- Unlike CB1, CB2 activation does NOT produce psychoactive effects, making it an attractive therapeutic target.

**[[hemenway-mulch-makers-and-plant-functional-roles]]**:
- Suppresses excessive inflammatory responses.
- Modulates immune cell migration and activation.
- Protects against tissue damage from overactive immune responses.
- Promotes tissue repair and homeostasis after injury.

### Putative Additional Cannabinoid Receptors

Several non-CB1/non-CB2 receptors have been proposed:

- **GPR55**: An orphan GPCR that responds to certain cannabinoids. May mediate some of the effects attributed to "non-CB1/non-CB2" receptors. Its exact role remains controversial—some evidence suggests it is not a true cannabinoid receptor.
- **GPR18**: Proposed as a cannabinoid receptor activated by abnormal cannabidiol (abn-CBD). May be involved in immune function and intraocular pressure regulation.
- **GPR119**: Activated by oleoylethanolamide (OEA) and other lipid signaling molecules. Primarily expressed in the pancreas and GI tract, with potential roles in metabolism and appetite.
- **TRPV1 (vanilloid receptor)**: While not a classical cannabinoid receptor, TRPV1 is activated by anandamide, CBD, and other cannabinoids, linking the endocannabinoid system to pain and thermoregulation pathways.
