# Cannabis Endorhiza Microbiome

## Overview

The Cannabis endorhiza microbiome refers to the community of bacteria living
within root tissue of Cannabis plants. First characterized by Winston et al.
(2014), it represents the most intimate plant-microbe association in the
Cannabis root system, distinct from both the rhizosphere (soil adhering to
roots) and bulk soil communities. The endorhiza microbiome shows significant
cultivar-specificity, meaning different Cannabis strains harbor distinct
[[cannabis-endorhiza-bacterial-communities]] within their roots even when grown in identical soil.

## Defining the Endorhiza

The endorhiza (root endosphere) encompasses all microorganisms that have
successfully colonized the interior of root tissue. Unlike rhizosphere
bacteria
which colonize the soil zone surrounding roots, endorhiza bacteria have
crossed
the root epidermis and entered the root cortex and vascular tissues. These
[[endorhiza-endophytic-bacteria]] form a more intimate and stable association with their
host.

Endorhiza bacteria support plant fitness through multiple mechanisms: they
provide phytohormones (auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins) that regulate growth,
produce siderophores for iron acquisition, and generate antibiotics that
suppress pathogens. Some produce enzymes for nutrient cycling, while others
help plants tolerate environmental stressors including heavy metals and
drought.

Cannabis is of particular interest because it produces numerous secondary
metabolites including cannabinoids and terpenes that may interact with and
shape the microbial community, making it a unique model for studying
plant-microbiome interactions in the context of specialized metabolism.

## Cultivar-Specific Communities

The landmark finding was that different Cannabis cultivars maintain
significantly different endorhiza communities. With three strains (Burmese,
Bookoo Kush, Sour Diesel), division by strain was significant for both
weighted (ADONIS: R² = 0.59, p = 0.004) and unweighted (R² = 0.39, p = 0.003)
UniFrac analyses. This was the first demonstration of cultivar-specificity in
the [[cannabis-root-microbiome]] and provided early evidence for the two-tier
selection model in this crop species.

Methylophilus abundance explained a significant portion of the cultivar effect
(FDR: p = 0.012). It comprised 13% of the endorhiza community in Bookoo Kush,
0.13% in Burmese, and was absent in Sour Diesel. In the second experiment with
White Widow and Maui Wowie, [[sphingomonas-wittichii-cannabis-endorhiza-strain-specificity]] prevalence differentiated
Maui Wowie. S. wittichii metabolizes phenazine-1-carboxylic acid and has been
implicated in increased soil survival, potentially conferring host advantages.

## Core Endorhiza Community

Despite cultivar differences, all endorhiza samples shared a core community:
Pseudomonas, Cellvibrio, Oxalobacteraceae, Xanthomonadaceae, Actinomycetales,
and Sphingobacteriales. Except for Cellvibrio, all were well-known endophytes
primarily within Gammaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria, consistent with
observations from poplar and Arabidopsis systems.

Pseudomonas species act as plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR),
producing antibiotics, siderophores, and inducing [[endophytic-mycorrhizal-induced-systemic-resistance]].
Actinomycetales includes Streptomyces, known for producing bioactive
compounds.
Cellvibrio is a cellulolytic bacterium more commonly associated with soil
decomposition than endophytism, suggesting its presence may indicate early
stages of root tissue turnover or decay.

## Root Decay Effects on Endorhiza Composition

Sampling timing critically affects endorhiza characterization. Post-harvest
samples (8 weeks after harvest) showed anomalously high Cellvibrio abundance
(16.9% vs 0.095% in actively growing plants), indicating root decay. Post-
harvest alpha diversity was dramatically reduced (chao1: 916.1 vs 3325 in
living plants). Despite this, cultivar-specificity remained detectable,
showing
the host genotype imprint persists even after plant death and tissue
degradation. This persistence has implications for post-harvest soil health
and the legacy effects of Cannabis cultivation on soil microbial communities.

## Cannabinoid-Microbiome Correlations

THC concentration correlated significantly with endorhiza community structure
(r-stat: 0.863, p = 0.001). However, THC also correlated with soil edaphic
variables since plants from one soil type had both higher THC and different
chemistry. This confounding prevents determining whether cannabinoids directly
influence endorhiza communities, whether shared genetic pathways control both
cannabinoid production and root exudates, or whether correlation is entirely
soil-mediated. Decoupling these effects requires controlled experiments across
multiple soil types with standardized cannabinoid profiles.

## Functional Significance and Future Directions

Likely endorhiza functions include nutrient acquisition (nitrogen fixation by
Rhizobiales, phosphate solubilization by Pseudomonas), disease suppression
through antibiotic production and competitive exclusion, growth promotion via
phytohormone synthesis, and stress tolerance. Potential modulation of
cannabinoid production pathways remains speculative. Future work should
isolate
individual strains, test effects in gnotobiotic systems, and elucidate the
molecular mechanisms of host genotype-dependent community selection to enable
development of cultivar-specific microbial inoculants.
