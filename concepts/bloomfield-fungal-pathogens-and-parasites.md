# Fungal Pathogens and Parasites
## Overview
Fungi cause devastating diseases in plants, animals, and humans. From the [[bloomfield-potato-blight-phytophthora-infestans]] that triggered the Irish famine to the brain-infecting Cryptococcus that kills immunocompromised patients, fungal pathogens have shaped human history and continue to pose serious medical and agricultural challenges. [[cryptococcus-neoformans]]
A yeast that causes cryptococcal meningitis, primarily in immunocompromised patients:
### Biology
- Found worldwide in soil and pigeon droppings
- Sexual stage (Filobasidiella) produces basidiospores on the gills of tiny mushroom-like fruiting bodies that sprout from pigeon droppings
- Spores are inhaled and reach the lungs, then cross into the bloodstream and penetrate the blood-brain barrier
- Once in the brain, the yeast forms a protective polysaccharide capsule that shields it from the immune system
- The brain is an immunologically privileged site — white blood cells that might destroy the yeast elsewhere cannot operate effectively there
### Medical Significance
- Before AIDS, cryptococcosis was rare; now it is one of the most common causes of death in AIDS patients worldwide
- Kills hundreds of thousands annually in sub-Saharan Africa
- The polysaccharide capsule was discovered by Kwon-Chung in the 1970s; she showed the sexual stage produced infectious basidiospores, revolutionizing understanding of transmission
- Treatment targets ergosterol in fungal membranes (unlike human cholesterol), but the capsule limits drug effectiveness
## Melanized Human Pathogens
Dark-pigmented fungi that cause opportunistic infections:
### Wangiella dermatitidis
- Rare brain-infecting fungus; shows both yeast and hyphal forms
- Melanized strains are lethal to mice; albino mutants are not
- Infections tracked to Japanese hot tubs where contaminated water nozzles drove spores into skin
### Dematiaceous Fungi Generally
- Ubiquitous on surfaces where water is available: bathroom fittings, wallpaper, buildings, statuary
- Stain masonry (often blamed on automobile pollution)
- Pit and penetrate stone, accelerating disintegration
- Normally harmless to healthy people but dangerous to AIDS patients, cancer therapy patients, and organ transplant recipients
- Require trauma for infection (surgery, bullet wounds, splinters, nail gun accidents)
## Mucormycosis (Zygomycete Infections)
Caused by Mucorales (pin molds), usually encountered as food-spoilage organisms:
### Infection Process
- Usually begins with trauma (splinter, wound) or in patients with uncontrolled diabetes, burns, or chronic alcoholism
- Hyphae grow astonishingly fast in agar — and equally fast in human tissues
- Nasal passages are the customary location; from the nasal sinus, the mycelium is centimeters from the brain
- Works through tiny fissures in skull bones or by following blood vessel walls
### Danger
- Often diagnosed too late to save the patient
- Spores are everywhere — hundreds may be inhaled since you started reading this
- A testament to the importance of a functioning immune system
## Pythiosis (Pythium insidiosum)
An oomycete (water mold) pathogen affecting mammals:
### History
- First described as "swamp cancer" in horses by British army veterinarians in India (1842)
- Charles Jackson wrote about "burusauttee" (related to rain/monsoon) in The Veterinarian
- F. Smith described microscopic hyphae in ulcer scrapings in 1884
- Human cases first reported in Thailand in 1987
### Disease Process
- Zoospores are released into water from sporangia
- When a mammal (horse, human, dog, cat, even a polar bear) contacts contaminated water through a wound, zoospores sense the tissue, shed flagella, become sticky, and penetrate
- A slender hypha emerges from the spore and pushes into tissue
- Mycelium becomes thoroughly embedded in the warm, protein-rich environment
### Treatment Challenges
- Pythium lacks ergosterol in its membranes, so standard antifungal drugs are ineffective
- Iodine (used since 1842) may interfere with cellulose-based [[staycare-degradation-of-plant-cell-wall-polymers-by-fungi]] synthesis
- Vaccines developed by Leonel Mendoza at Michigan State University have shown promise
- Often requires radical surgical debridement
### Ecology
- May grow as a soil saprobe, but has not been isolated from anywhere other than mammalian tissues
- Likely an ancient pathogen — may have consumed prehistoric animal flesh for hundreds of millions of years
- Related oomycetes found in skin lesions on turtles and reptiles
## Histoplasmosis (Histoplasma capsulatum)
- Also called [[histoplasma-capsulatum-ohio-valley-disease-dimorphic-fungus]] disease
- Sexual stage: Ajellomyces (discovered by Kwon-Chung)
- Associated with bird droppings and starling roosts
- Most people exposed without symptoms; fungus becomes encapsulated in calcified lung nodules
- In immunocompromised patients, can spread from lungs to any tissue in the body
- Most Ohioans carry the dormant fungus in their lungs
## Ergotism ([[ergot-fungus]], a pathogen of rye:
### Toxins
- **Ergotamine**: causes powerful vasoconstriction, leading to gangrene of extremities — patients lose nails, then hands or feet
- **Isoergine ([[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] amide)**: a less potent relative of LSD, causes terrifying hallucinations
- Both toxins survive baking
### Historical Impact
- Caused by consuming rye bread contaminated with ergot sclerotia (dark, finger-like structures replacing grain kernels)
- Ergotism epidemics were common in Europe for centuries
- Linnda Caporael proposed ergot poisoning caused the Salem witch trials (1691-1692): warm wet spring/summer favored Claviceps, symptoms began after threshing in December
- The combination of gangrene and hallucinations produced the classic "bewitchment" symptoms
## Cordyceps: Insect Pathogens
Ascomycete fungi that parasitize and kill insects:
### Life Cycle
1. Spores land on insect and penetrate the chitinous exoskeleton
2. Hyphae colonize internal tissues
