# Parasitic Wasps — Biological Pest Control Agents

[[fukuoka-textdoc-arrowhead-scale-ladybug-biological-control]] agents used in agriculture, forestry, and [[economics-of-winter-greenhouse-production]].
Unlike true parasites, parasitoids develop on or inside a single host and
invariably kill it by emergence. This life history makes them uniquely suited as
precision biocontrol tools within IPM programs that minimize chemical inputs.

- **Aphelinidae** — includes *Encarsia formosa*, a cornerstone agent for whitefly

into host eggs; a single larva consumes the egg contents, preventing caterpillar

### Encarsia formosa

*Encarsia formosa* is an aphelinid parasitoid of greenhouse whitefly (*Trialeurodes
vaporariorum*) and silverleaf whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*). Females insert eggs
through the nymphal scale; the larva consumes the host from within, producing a
characteristic black mummy. Deployed commercially since the 1920s and refined in
the 1970s, it remains standard in greenhouse IPM on tomato, cucumber, pepper, and
ornamental crops.

### Aphidius colemani and A. ervi

These braconid aphid parasitoids are released in greenhouses and field crops to
suppress aphid populations. *A. colemani* targets smaller species (*Myzus persicae*,
*Aphis gossypii*), while *A. ervi* prefers *Acyrthosiphon pisum* and *Macrosiphum
euphorbiae*. Parasitized aphids swell into mummies (tan for *colemani*, brown for
*ervi*). Banker plant systems using cereal grass infested with non-pest aphids
sustain *Aphidius* populations between pest outbreaks.

### Cotesia glomerata and C. rubecula

Gregarious larval endoparasitoids of cabbage white butterflies (*Pieris rapae*,
*P. brassicae*) important in brassica crops. *C. glomerata* is more generalist while
*C. rubecula* specializes on *P. rapae*. Females inject eggs and calyx-fluid
polydnaviruses that suppress host immunity, allowing larvae to develop internally
before exiting to spin communal cocoons beside the dead caterpillar.

## Life-History Strategies

Parasitoid wasps exhibit diverse strategies that influence their suitability for
biocontrol:

- **Idiobiont vs. Koinobiont**: Idiobionts paralyze or kill the host at oviposition,
  halting further development. Koinobionts allow continued host feeding and growth
  while the parasitoid develops inside, reducing damage before peak injury.
- **Solitary vs. Gregarious**: Solitary species deposit one egg per host; gregarious
  species lay multiple eggs, producing several adults from one host. Gregarious
  species like *C. glomerata* achieve high suppression from fewer encounters.
- **Endoparasitoid vs. Ectoparasitoid**: Endoparasitoids develop inside the host body;
  ectoparasitoids feed externally on paralyzed hosts. Endoparasitoids must overcome
  host immunity, often using viral or venom-mediated strategies.
