# Cucumber Growing Organic

Cucumbers (Cucumis sativus) require a well-manured soil and can be
trained to grow up wigwams of poles to save space. They can be grown
both in the greenhouse and outside. New varieties of outdoor cucumbers
are now so good that many gardeners use the greenhouse only for the
very earliest crops. The newer all-female cucumber varieties are best
for growing under glass, producing abundant fruit without the need to
remove male flowers.

## Outdoor Cucumber Varieties

Marketmore produces long, dark green fruits and is a reliable outdoor
variety. Burpless Tasty Green is said to be easier to digest than
most varieties. Both prefer a pH of about 6.0 and a well-manured
soil. Dig in plenty of compost or manure before planting. In the
hamilton three year plan, grow cucumbers in Plot A.

## Sowing and Planting Outdoor Cucumbers

Sow inside at about 65 degrees F (18 degrees C) in mid-spring,
putting two seeds in each 3in (8cm) pot. Thin to the strongest
seedling if necessary. Plant outside in late spring, setting the
plants 2ft (60cm) apart. Alternatively, sow pairs of seeds outside
in late spring at the same spacings. Cover the sowing site with a
cut-off plastic bottle or a cloche to protect seedlings from cold
wind and slugs during their first weeks outdoors.

## Training and Maintenance

The most space-saving way to grow cucumbers is up a wigwam of poles.
Space four 8ft (2.5m) poles 2ft (60cm) apart and tie them together
at the top. Plant young plants at the base of each pole and tie them
in with soft string as they grow. This method keeps the fruits off
the ground and out of reach of slugs, and looks attractive enough
for flower borders.

If you decide to grow cucumbers flat on the ground, space them 3ft
(90cm) apart. For organic vegetable garden, cucumbers are well suited and should be
grown up poles at 2ft (60cm) spacings. Feed at two-week intervals
with liquid kelp or animal-manure fertilizer from midsummer until
harvesting. Pinch off the tops when the plants reach the top of the
poles to encourage bushy lateral growth.

If growing up poles, tie plants in often and trim the sideshoots
back to two leaves regularly to encourage compact, bushy growth.
Harvest by cutting the fruits when they are still young with a bloom
on them. Regular cutting encourages the plants to produce more fruit.

## Greenhouse Cucumber Cultivation

Greenhouse cucumber varieties are sweet-tasting, vigorous, resistant
to disease, and all-female. Hana produces an abundant crop of short
fruits. Carmen is a very heavy cropping variety. Cumlaude RZ will
grow in an unheated greenhouse. Athene is ideal for slightly cooler
conditions. All these grow well in the same and humidity
regime as tomatoes, so they can share the greenhouse space.

Sow from midwinter if the greenhouse can be heated to about 50
degrees F (10 degrees C), or in mid-spring for a cold house. Sow
individually in 3in (8cm) pots of potting compost and place in a
propagator at 75 to 80 degrees F (24 to 27 degrees C). When
germinated, move to a light, airy position at 60 to 65 degrees F
(15 to 18 degrees C). Prepare the border soil and plant as for
tomatoes, supporting plants in the same way.

Handle plants carefully when twisting them around support strings
because cucumber stems are delicate and can break easily. Cucumbers
like plenty of water and should never be allowed to dry out. They
prefer a humid atmosphere, so wet down the paths and plants at least
twice a day in the morning and early afternoon. Ventilate to keep
temperature around 65 to 75 degrees F (18 to 24 degrees C) during
the day. Keep vents shut at night until early summer.

## Greenhouse Cucumber Maintenance

After about eight weeks of growth, start feeding with liquid kelp or
animal-manure fertilizer at every watering. Trim the sideshoots
regularly to leave two leaves. The newer recommended varieties fruit
mainly from the main stem. On these types, remove any fruit that
forms on sideshoots. Older varieties may produce fruit mainly on the
sideshoots, in which case remove any on the main stem.

The most and common pests and diseases affecting cucumbers include slugs
and aphids (see general pest control pages), mildew, and cucumber
mosaic virus. Greenhouse plants can also be affected by the specific
pests and diseases found in the protected environment. Good
ventilation and hygiene are the best organic defenses against these
problems.

## Key Points

- Outdoor varieties like Marketmore and Burpless Tasty Green are
  reliable and produce well-trained up wigwam poles
- Sow indoors at 65 degrees F (18 degrees C) in mid-spring, two
  seeds per 3in (8cm) pot
- Train up poles spaced 2ft (60cm) apart; pinch tops at the top of
  the canes
- Feed every two weeks with liquid kelp or animal-manure fertilizer
  from midsummer
- Greenhouse varieties are all-female: Hana, Carmen, Cumlaude RZ,
  and Athene
- Greenhouse cucumbers share temperature requirements with tomatoes
- Keep greenhouse humidity high by wetting paths and plants twice
  daily
- Harvest outdoor cucumbers young with the bloom still on the skin
  to encourage continued production
