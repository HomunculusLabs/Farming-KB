# [[permaculture-climate-zones]] and Strategies

Climatic factors have their most profound effect on the selection of species and technology for site, and are thus the main determinant [[allegro-star-of-the-morning-venus-and-the-sacred-mushroom]] plant, animal, and structural assemblies we can use. There is an intimate interaction between site and local climatic factors, in that slope, valley configuration, proximity to coasts, and altitude all affect the operation [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] weather.

## Classification of Broad Climatic Zones

Most global climatic classifications are based on precipitation-radiation interactions as formulated by Koppen (1918) and subsequently modified by Trewartha (1954). The broad classifications used [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-pattern-understanding-in-permaculture]] are:

### Tropical
No month under 18C (64F) mean temperature. Encompasses the wet tropics, wet-dry tropics, and monsoon tropics.

### Subtropical
Coolest months above 0C (32F) but below 18C (64F) mean. Effectively frost-free areas.

### Temperate
Coldest months below 0C (32F), warmest above 10C (50F) mean temperature. Includes cool temperate and warm temperate sub-zones.

### Polar
Warmest month below 10C (50F) or in perpetual frost (8C or less) mean.

### Arid
Mean rainfall 50 cm (19.5 inches) or less. Includes sub-humid areas where evaporation exceeds precipitation.

### Desert
Mean rainfall 25 cm (10 inches) or less.

## The Wet Tropics (Latitudes 0-25 degrees)

### Characteristics
The river basins and wet coasts including [[yakuruna-water-spirits-of-the-amazon]] and Congo basins, [[stamets-mushrooms-world-mexico-central-america]], Sri Lanka, Malaya, and [[agara-galbulimima-belgraveana-new-guinea-tree]]. Temperature fluctuates little at about 21-32C (70-90F). Humidity is constantly high, frost unknown. Rainfall is 152-328 cm (60-129 inches), with rain most days and frequent thunderstorms.

### Landscape
Perennial streams, deeply weathered rock, rounded hills. Rapid water runoff and evaporation. Broadleaf rainforest with lianas and epiphytes, up to 800 tree species per square kilometre.

### Key Challenges
- Heat and high rainfall leach nutrients from soils
- 85% of nutrients are held in plants or animals, not soils
- Clear-cultivation leads to erosion and leaching to insoluble oxides
- Insect and disease pressure is constant
- Rampant growth management required

### Design Strategies
- Hygienic faeces disposal
- Clean water sources
- Integrated and benign insect control techniques
- Gradual replacement of ground crops by trees
- Preservation of natural stands of trees
- Development of river versus road traffic
- Evolution of natural products
- No-dig (mulch) techniques on root crops
- Domestic foragers for snail and insect pests
- Appropriate medicinal plants
- Complex and multi-storied plant systems designed to yield staples, create mulch, and preserve soil nutrients

### Housing
Raised, steep-roofed, thatched, with permeable walls, and screened. Cross-ventilation and careful construction for mosquito control are essential.

## The Wet-Dry Tropics

### Characteristics
Adjoin the wet tropics but poleward. The Campos, Llanos, Gran Chaco areas of South America, parts of Central America, and Hawaii. No frosts; temperatures range 21-27C in wet season, 32-38C in dry season. Rainfall is 25-152 cm, decreasing towards desert margins.

### Landscape
Intermittent streams, some wadis and flood plains, karst areas. Vast savannah grasslands with thorn-bush and flat-topped [[acacia]] trees, evolving to steppe grassland on plateaus.

### Key Challenges
- Erosion from episodic flooding
- Erratic and unpredictable rainfall
- Overgrazing and fire extending desert into these areas
- Serious soil erosion from short-term shifting cultivation

### Design Strategies
- Small domestic [[water-storage-techniques]] and reticulation
- Hedgerow against winds
- In-crop tree legumes such as Acacia albida
- Improved stock varieties and stock management
- Natural herding system of local herd species
- Mulch use of grasses
- Increased tree crop of high forage value
- Decreased fire frequency
- Tree stands for fuel and structural timber
- No-tillage (cut and mulch) grain techniques
- Low bunds for water retention
- Keyline techniques of flood control
- Soakage pits and impoundment of runoff by low bunds or swales
- Tree forage and tall grass hand-fed to domestic stock
- Reclamation of eroded lands using pioneer species

## The Monsoon Tropics

These are a sub-type of wet-dry tropics, influenced by nearby continental land masses and oceanic winds. Confined to the Indo-Thailand region, northern Australia, East Java, and extending to 35 degrees north in India. About 60% of rain falls in summer, but rain is erratic and varies from 102-1016 cm. Floods and droughts are equally unpredictable but common.

Design essentials are similar to those of the wet-dry tropics.

## Cool Temperate Climates

### Characteristics
These are places where greenhouses are most commonly used for specialty crop or aquatic species production, for house heating, and for winter production. Soils generally possess good structure and clay fractions, high natural humus, and good cation exchange capacity.

### Key Strategies
- Open water storages are appropriate (sufficient clay, evaporation does not exceed precipitation)
- [[water-management]] is the overriding design input
- Start water planning from the highest accessible slopes
- Gravity-flow irrigation from high to low
- Forest farm integration for fire control and nutrient cycling

### Settlement Design
- Village or streets aligned east-west at mid-slope of sun-facing slope
- Dense windbreak polewards or uphill of settlement
- Dense attached vines on masonry walls for insulation
- Deciduous trees to admit low winter light
- Siting above local frost lines
- Stepped aspect so each dwelling presents full facade to winter sun

### House Design
- 30-100% glazing on sunward walls (depending on latitude)
- No glazing on west walls
- Houses no more than 10 m deep on north/south axis
- Concrete slab or earth floors for thermal mass
- Insulate sub-floor earth mass (5-15 cm insulation in 1 m trench)
- Attached glasshouse or solarium for winter use

## Arid and Desert Climates

### Characteristics
Rain in the desert may fall as 2.5 cm or so per day for 23 days, producing severe local flooding. Flash-flood factors become critical when settlements are built in wadis, on the upstream side of restricted gaps in hills, or in hollows near dry watercourses.

In Australia, 88% of rainfall runs off or is evaporated from land and water surfaces. Only 12% of rainfall is available to plants.

### Key Strategies
- Oversize channels, drains, infiltration, and diversion systems
- Halt and divert water flows; create places for infiltration
- Tree lines, swales, deep-ripped contours, silt dams to halt runoff
- Trees recycle water through growth and transpiration
- Clay catchment areas sloping to central cisterns
- Water spreading on plains to increase infiltration
- Roof catchment and sheet metal collection surfaces
- Focus on forest rehabilitation to restore water cycles
