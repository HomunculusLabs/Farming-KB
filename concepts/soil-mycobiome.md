## Soil Mycobiome

## Overview

The soil mycobiome refers to the total fungal community inhabiting soil
environments. Soil is the primary reservoir of fungal diversity on Earth,
containing an estimated 1.5 million fungal species across virtually
every major taxonomic group. Despite its ecological importance, the soil
mycobiome remains largely uncharacterized, with the vast majority known
only from DNA sequences rather than formal species descriptions. A
single gram of forest soil may contain hundreds of species represented
by millions of hyphal meters and billions of spores.

## Functional Guilds

### Decomposers (Saprotrophs)

Saprotrophic fungi are the primary agents of organic matter decomposition,
breaking down cellulose, lignin, and other complex polymers.
Basidiomycetes such as white-rot and brown-rot fungi are particularly
important for lignin degradation. White-rot fungi employ peroxidases
and laccases to oxidatively cleave lignin, while brown-rot fungi use
non-enzymatic Fenton chemistry. Decomposer activity drives nutrient
cycling and carbon flux, and the balance between fungal and bacterial
decomposition has significant implications for soil carbon storage.

## Detection Challenges

Soil is heterogeneous, opaque, and three-dimensional. DNA-based methods
have revolutionized surveys, but extracting high-quality DNA from
humic-rich soils remains challenging due to PCR inhibitors. The vast
majority of soil fungal sequences in public databases do not match
described species. Fruit body surveys capture only a small fraction
of the community since most soil fungi rarely produce visible structures.

## Seasonal and Successional Dynamics

Soil fungal communities shift seasonally in response to temperature,
moisture, and plant phenology. Mycorrhizal fungi peak during the growing
season when plants allocate more carbon belowground. Decomposer
communities respond to litter inputs, with peaks following leaf fall
in temperate forests. Ecosystem succession involves predictable shifts,
with early successional communities dominated by fast-growing
Ascomycota and late successional communities enriched in Basidiomycota.
