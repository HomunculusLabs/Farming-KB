## Cannabis: History, Classification, and Chemistry

## Botany and Morphology

- Cannabis is a **dioecious** plant — male and female flowers grow on separate individuals
- Pistillate (female) flowers form in leaf axils
- Intoxicating constituents are concentrated in the **resin** of developing female flowers and adjacent leaves and stems
- The plant produces a sticky, aromatic resin that is the primary source of its psychoactive properties

## Taxonomic Classification

- The taxonomic placement of Cannabis has been **disputed for centuries**
- Some authorities place it in **Moraceae** (the fig and mulberry family)
- Others assign it to **Urticaceae** (the nettle family)
- Modern classification most commonly places it in its own family **Cannabaceae**, alongside the hop plant (*Humulus*)
- Long considered a single species (*C. sativa*) with many races and varieties
- [[chamovitz-plant-scent-communication-volatile-organic-compounds]]** have been isolated from Cannabis
- The fresh plant yields mainly **cannabidiolic acids**, which are precursors to the tetrahydrocannabinols (THC)
- The main psychoactive effects are attributable to **delta-1-tetrahydrocannabinol** (Δ¹-THC)
- THC is a **non-nitrogenous terpenophenolic compound**
- It belongs to the **dibenzopyran** (cannabinol) class
- **Crucially, THC is NOT an alkaloid** — it contains no nitrogen, which distinguishes it from most other psychoactive plant compounds

## Methods of Use

### Marihuana / Maconha
- Dried, crushed flowering tips of the plant
- **Smoked**, typically in cigarettes or pipes

### Hasheesh
- Concentrated **resin** collected from the female plant
- Eaten or smoked, often in **water pipes**

### Bhang
- Green plant material, dried and processed
- Made into a **drink** or formed into candy called **majun**

### Charas
- **Pure resin**, hand-rubbed from living plants
- Smoked or eaten

### Ganjah
- **Resin-rich dried tops** of the female plant
- Typically smoked **mixed with tobacco**

## Psychological Effects

- Primary effect: **euphoria**
- Responses are **highly variable** from person to person
- Range of experiences includes:
  - Mild ease and relaxation
  - Fantastic, dreamlike states
  - **Visual and auditory hallucinations** (in some users)
- Characteristic features:
  - **Dreamy state** with interrupted, flowing thought patterns
  - **Exaltation** alternating with **depression** or **panic**
  - Altered **time perception** — time may seem to slow or speed up
  - **Exaggerated sensitivity to sound**
- Cannabis is **not typically psychotomimetic** (it does not generally produce psychotic-like states)
- The plant is considered **not addictive** in the physical sense
- However, **psychological dependence** is possible with regular use

## Physical Effects

- **Increased pulse rate**
- **Elevated blood pressure**
- **Tremor** and unsteadiness
- **Vertigo**
- Difficulty with **muscular coordination**
- Increased **tactile sensitivity**
- **Pupil dilation** (mydriasis)

## Geographic Spread

- From its origins in central Asia, Cannabis spread to virtually every inhabited continent
- Hemp fiber cultivation preceded psychoactive use in many regions
- The dual utility of Cannabis — as a fiber crop and a psychoactive plant — contributed to its global dissemination
- European colonial expansion further distributed the plant to the Americas, Africa, and [[guzman-thailand-southeast-asia-psychoactive-fungi-distribution]]

## Key Distinctions

- **Hemp** varieties are bred for fiber and seed, with low [[thc-content-factors-cultivation-freshness-storage-degradation-blesching]]
- **Drug** varieties are bred for high resin and THC production
- The distinction between industrial hemp and psychoactive Cannabis is primarily a matter of **cultivar selection**, not separate species
