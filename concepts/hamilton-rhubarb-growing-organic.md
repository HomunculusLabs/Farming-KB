# Rhubarb Growing - Organic Methods

Rhubarb is included among the vegetables because we eat its stems
rather than its fruit. It is easy to grow and can be harvested from
late winter through to midsummer, making it one of the earliest
crops available from the garden. Rhubarb is a hardy perennial that,
once established, requires minimal attention and will continue
producing for many years. Its versatility in the kitchen and its
reliability in the garden make it a staple of the organic plot.

## Choosing Varieties

Recommended varieties include Cawood Delight, which is a modern
variety with excellent vigor; Victoria, a long-established favorite
with good flavor; Timperley Early, noted for its early readiness;
Red Champagne, which produces slender, brightly colored stems; and
Stockbridge Arrow, a high-yielding variety suitable for forcing.
Select varieties based on your desired harvest period and whether
you plan to force some plants for early crops.

## Soil and Site

Rhubarb likes a soil pH of 7.0, so add lime if necessary to attain
this level. It also prefers a well-drained soil with plenty of
well-rotted compost or manure dug in before planting. See
[[hamilton-organic-soil-management]] for soil improvement techniques
and [[hamilton-liming-and-ph-management]] for guidance on adjusting
pH. Apart from the pH requirement, rhubarb is fairly tolerant and
is frost hardy. Plant it in a bed reserved for perennials, as it
will remain in position for many years.

## Planting

Mature roots called crowns are generally planted in winter, although
potted plants are available for year-round planting. Most people
will not require more than two or three plants. If you wish to grow
more, set the plants 2ft 6in (75cm) apart with 3ft (90cm) between
rows. Cover the crowns with 1in (2.5cm) of soil. Do not plant too
deeply, as this can reduce the vigor of the plants. Water in well
after planting and mulch around the crowns to conserve moisture.

## Maintenance

Weed and water as necessary during the growing season. It is not
practical to dig in organic matter around established rhubarb
plants because of the risk of damaging the roots, so mulch each
autumn instead with well-rotted compost or manure. This annual
mulching provides all the nutrients the plants need. See
[[hamilton-mulching-materials-and-techniques]] for suitable
materials and application methods.

## Harvesting

Leave the plants to grow during the first year without harvesting,
to allow the root system to develop fully. In subsequent years,
pull the thickest sticks when they are long enough, but never
strip the plant completely. Always leave several stems to maintain
the plant's strength. Remove any flower spikes immediately, as
they divert energy from stem production. The leaves are toxic and
should not be eaten, but they can be added to the compost heap.

## Forcing

Forcing produces earlier, tender, pale stems with a sweeter flavor.
In late winter, cover selected plants with a forcing jar, bucket, or
black plastic pot to exclude light. The darkness encourages the plant
to produce elongated, pale stems. Forced rhubarb is typically ready
about four to six weeks after covering. Do not force the same plant
for more than one year in succession, as it weakens the crown.
Alternate forcing between different plants each year.

## Pests and Diseases

Rhubarb is susceptible to aphid attack and certain viruses. Aphids
can be controlled with organic sprays or by encouraging natural
predators. Virus diseases cause yellowing and distortion of the
leaves; affected plants should be removed and destroyed to prevent
spread. Crown rot can occur in waterlogged soils, highlighting the
importance of good drainage. See [[hamilton-organic-pest-control]]
for comprehensive pest and disease management strategies.

## Organic Liquid Feed

Rhubarb responds well to organic liquid feeds during the growing
season. A solution made from soaking comfrey leaves in water for
several weeks produces a potassium-rich feed ideal for promoting
strong stem growth. Alternatively, liquid kelp or animal-manure
fertilizer can be applied every two weeks during spring and early
summer. See [[hamilton-concentrated-organic-fertilizer-products]]
for prepared organic fertilizer options.

## Summary

Rhubarb is one of the easiest and most reliable perennial vegetables
for the organic garden. Key success factors include planting at the
correct depth in well-drained soil at pH 7.0, annual autumn mulching
with compost or manure, leaving the first year for establishment,
never stripping plants completely when harvesting, and promptly
removing flower spikes. Forcing provides an early delicacy, while
regular harvesting from established plants provides a steady supply
throughout spring and early summer.

Rhubarb leaves contain oxalic acid and should never be eaten. However,
they can be composted safely as the acid breaks down during decomposition.
Forced rhubarb crowns should be allowed a full recovery year without
harvesting to rebuild their energy reserves for the following season.
