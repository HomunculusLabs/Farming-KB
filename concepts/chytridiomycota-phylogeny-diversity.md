## Molecular Phylogenetics

James and colleagues (2000) used SSU rDNA characters to test
the order-level classification and distinguished four
monophyletic clades corresponding to the major orders, plus
four small independent groups. Their analysis showed:

- Orders recognized on the basis of zoospore morphology
  generally are well supported by molecular characters
- A core group of Chytridiomycota forms a clade
- The Chytridiales can be grouped with rDNA, and the
  clades are consistent with zoospore ultrastructure
- The Blastocladiales may be independent of other orders

Several chytrids with unique zoospore morphologies have not
been classified in previously existing orders. Most notably,
[[bloomfield-amphibian-decline-fungal-disease-climate-interactions]], does not fall within any of the
groups defined on the basis of DNA characters. This highlights
how incomplete our understanding of chytrid diversity remains.

James and colleagues (2000) predicted that additional chytrid
diversity will be uncovered when more chytrids with unique
zoospore types are included in phylogenetic analyses.

## Relationship to Other Fungal Phyla

The Chytridiomycota is sister to the Zygomycota in many
phylogenetic analyses, although both groups are paraphyletic.
Key relationships include:
