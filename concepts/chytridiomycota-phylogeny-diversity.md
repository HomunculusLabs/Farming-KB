# Chytridiomycota Phylogeny and Diversity

The Chytridiomycota represents the most basal lineage of the
Kingdom [[symbiotic-fungi-mycorrhizal-carbon-trading]], distinguished by the
ancestral trait of flagellated cells (zoospores or gametes) at
some stage in their life cycles. Historically defined as a
monophyletic group sharing a single posteriorly inserted smooth
flagellum, molecular phylogenetic analyses have revealed that
the phylum is not monophyletic, although some clade within it
occupies the most basal branch of the fungal tree of life.

## Phylogenetic Position

Phylogenetic analyses indicate that some chytrid lineage
represents the basal member of Kingdom Fungi, a finding
consistent with a choanoflagellate-like ancestor. Current
information confirms that approximately 800 species of
Chytridiomycota, largely defined by the ancestral character
state of a smooth posterior flagellum, do not form a single
monophyletic group. This has significant implications for
understanding the evolution of the [[symbiotic-fungi-mycorrhizal-carbon-trading]].

Key phylogenetic findings include:

- Some clade of the Chytridiomycota is basal to all other
  fungal lineages
- Multiple independent losses of the flagellum occurred
  during [[fungal-evolution]]
- The Zygomycota and Chytridiomycota both show
  paraphyletic patterns in molecular analyses

## Taxonomic Framework

Barr and Desaulniers (1988) established five orders of chytrids
based on zoospore ultrastructure:

1. **Blastocladiales** -- may represent a lineage independent
   of the other orders of Chytridiomycota
2. **Monoblepharidales** -- a well-supported monophyletic clade
3. **Neocallimastigales** -- [[anaerobic-gut-fungi]]
4. **Spizellomycetales** -- soil-inhabiting chytrids
5. **Chytridiales** -- the best-sampled order, with clades
   consistent between rDNA phylogenies and zoospore
   ultrastructure

Their implicit evolutionary hypothesis based on zoospore
morphology is congruent with phylogenies based on mitochondrial
DNA. Relationships among the clades, however, have not been
fully resolved by current data.

## Molecular Phylogenetics

James and colleagues (2000) used SSU rDNA characters to test
the order-level classification and distinguished four
monophyletic clades corresponding to the major orders, plus
four small independent groups. Their analysis showed:

- Orders recognized on the basis of zoospore morphology
  generally are well supported by molecular characters
- A core group of Chytridiomycota forms a clade
- The Chytridiales can be grouped with rDNA, and the
  clades are consistent with zoospore ultrastructure
- The Blastocladiales may be independent of other orders

Several chytrids with unique zoospore morphologies have not
been classified in previously existing orders. Most notably,
Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis, the chytrid associated with
global amphibian decline, does not fall within any of the
groups defined on the basis of DNA characters. This highlights
how incomplete our understanding of chytrid diversity remains.

James and colleagues (2000) predicted that additional chytrid
diversity will be uncovered when more chytrids with unique
zoospore types are included in phylogenetic analyses.

## Relationship to Other Fungal Phyla

The Chytridiomycota is sister to the Zygomycota in many
phylogenetic analyses, although both groups are paraphyletic.
Key relationships include:

- Basidiobolus ranarum has been placed within core chytrids
  in some rDNA analyses but falls within zygomycetes in
  analyses of tubulin genes
- The Glomales ([[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]]) was
  once considered part of the Zygomycota but is now recognized
  as a sister clade to the Ascomycota/Basidiomycota lineage
- Crown fungi (Glomales + Ascomycota + Basidiomycota)
  represent the most derived monophyletic grouping

## Ecological Diversity

Chytrids occupy diverse ecological niches:

- **Aquatic habitats** -- many species are found in freshwater
  and marine environments, functioning as saprobes or parasites
  of algae, pollen, and other organisms
- **Soil ecosystems** -- Spizellomycetales and Chytridiales are
  abundant in soils worldwide
- **Gut symbionts** -- Neocallimastigales are anaerobic chytrids
  found in the digestive tracts of herbivorous mammals,
  particularly ruminants
- **Pathogens** -- Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis causes
  chytridiomycosis in amphibians, contributing to global
  amphibian declines
- **Plant parasites** -- some chytrids parasitize aquatic
  plants and algae

## Zoospore Biology

Zoospore ultrastructure has been the primary basis for
classifying chytrids at the ordinal level. Key features
include:

- Flagellar insertion (posterior in most groups)
- Presence and arrangement of microbody-lipid globule
  complexes
- Ribosomal arrangement within the zoospore body
- Type and position of the rumposome
- Rootlet morphology associated with the kinetosome

These ultrastructural characters correspond well with
molecular phylogenies, validating their utility as taxonomic
markers despite the limitations of morphological classification
alone.
