# Permaculture Duck Integration for Orchards and Gardens

Ducks are among the most versatile and gentlest poultry species in a
[[permaculture-design]] system. They serve multiple functions
simultaneously: pest control (especially slug and snail management),
soil fertilization, pond aeration, weed management, egg and meat
production, and feather harvesting. Unlike chickens, ducks require less
direct care and can thrive on forage when their environment is properly
designed.

Their integration into orchards, gardens, and aquaculture systems
exemplifies the permaculture principle of each element performing
multiple functions. This approach aligns with [[permaculture-principles]]
of closing loops and maximizing beneficial connections between system
elements.

## Species Selection

Several duck breeds are popular with permaculture growers:

### Egg Breeds

- **Khaki Campbell:** The most common domestic breed and a prolific
  egg layer, producing nearly as many eggs as chickens (250-340
  eggs per year)
- **Indian Runners:** Similarly productive egg layers with a distinctive
  upright posture; not good meat birds

### Meat Breeds

- **Muscovy:** Large, quiet breed that forages extensively; excellent
  for pest control
- **Rouen:** Large and colorful, similar appearance to wild Mallards
- **Pekin:** Fast-growing commercial meat breed

### Dual-Purpose Breeds

For dual-purpose production (both eggs and meat), heritage breeds are
recommended:

- **Ancona:** Hardy and active foragers with good egg production
- **Appleyard:** Large dual-purpose breed with attractive plumage
- **Buff:** Calm temperament, good layer and reasonable table bird
- **Magpie:** Excellent foragers, good layers, and attractive markings
- **Saxony:** Particularly valued for its foraging ability and
  reliable egg laying

Ducks are social creatures and need a flock of at least two to remain
healthy and content. Single ducks become stressed and may stop eating
or laying.

## System Design

The ideal duck system centers around a small pond with an island for
nesting, located in or adjacent to [[permaculture-zone-planning-guide]]
Zone 2. The enclosure should include a forage garden around the pond
where ducks can graze on greens, bugs, and weeds.

Stocking rates are approximately 25 ducks per acre of pond surface.
If fish are present in the pond, duck manure fertilizes the water,
feeding algae-eating organisms and supporting pond plant growth that in
turn feeds the fish.

When the duck area borders the Zone 1 garden, the flock can be allowed
periodic access to consume slugs and pests, but only after garden
plants are well established and taller than the ducks. This staged
introduction prevents damage to young seedlings.

## Feeding and Nutrition

Ducks with adequate water, young grass, and a forage garden require
minimal supplemental feed. Their diet should include wheat as the
primary grain, supplemented with oats. Hard fruits and vegetables must
be crushed before feeding.

Protein sources include liquid milk, hard-boiled eggs, and the insects
and snails they forage. All ducks require calcium from eggshells or
seashells, plus grit for digestion. Unlike other poultry, ducks need
additional niacin, which fresh greens and peas typically provide in
sufficient quantities.

### Homemade Poultry Chick Feed Formula

- 30% finely ground grain (wheat, corn, oats)
- 20% protein (fish meal, meat meal, yogurt, cottage cheese, worms,
  bugs, grubs)
- 50% greens (alfalfa meal or fresh chopped greens)
- Extras: wheat germ, sunflower seeds, linseed meal, sand for grit,
  and ground shells for calcium

## Housing and Protection

Each duck needs four square feet of housing. During the day, a
three-sided shelter near the pond provides adequate protection. At
night, ducks must be secured from predators in a barn room with straw
bedding or a dedicated shed.

Most domestic ducks can fly and need wing clipping after each molt;
clip only the long flight feathers of one wing. During winter when
forage is scarce, additional mulch such as fallen leaves or hay should
be spread in their area. Ducks tolerate freezing temperatures if they
can retreat to their shelter from wind.

### Predator Protection

Key predators to manage include:

- **Raccoons and weasels:** The most common nocturnal predators; secure
  housing with hardware cloth is essential
- **Foxes and coyotes:** Require fenced enclosures with buried wire to
  prevent digging
- **Birds of prey:** Provide overhead cover for ducklings and young
  ducks
- **Snakes and rats:** May take eggs; secure nesting areas

## Breeding and Egg Production

Distinguishing males from females can be done by voice: females have a
loud, raspy quack while males are quieter or nearly silent. Some males
become protective of females during breeding season.

Ducks begin laying in spring at six to seven months of age and continue
for three years or longer. They lay consistently in the morning, making
a predictable schedule possible. Let ducks out after 10:00 AM to allow
egg collection first, then lure them back in the evening with grain.

A motherly duck will raise her own ducklings more successfully than
artificial methods. Separate mother and ducklings from the flock for
six to eight weeks after hatching to prevent aggression from other
flock members.
