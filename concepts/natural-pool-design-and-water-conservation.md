# Natural Pool Design and Water Conservation

## Overview

Natural pool design represents a fundamental shift away from chemically treated swimming pools toward integrated biological systems that purify water through natural processes. In permaculture, water is the most critical resource to manage on any property, and natural pools serve multiple functions beyond recreation—they become reserves for fire-fighting, heat sources for heat pumps, barriers to fire, and emergency water supplies.

## The Problem with Chemical Pools

Conventional swimming pools rely on chlorine for disinfection. Chlorine, originally developed as a war gas, is routinely dumped into drinking, bathing, and swimming water where it forms carcinogenic chloroform. This approach treats water as a sterile commodity rather than as a living system within a broader ecological context.

## Natural Pool Design Principles

Innovative natural pool designers use a multi-stage biological filtration approach:

1. **Pebble Bed Filtration**: Water is filtered below a base pebble bed, using the pebbles as algal and bacterial cleaners that break down organic matter and nutrients.

2. **Reed-Bed Treatment**: After initial filtration, water cycles through a constructed reed-bed (or similar wetland planting) to remove excess nutrients before being returned to the pool.

3. **Cascading Return**: Water cascades back into the pool freshly oxygenated, completing a self-sustaining purification cycle.

### Biological Components

A well-designed natural pool supports a thriving ecosystem including:
- Tame fish that help control algae and insect larvae
- Crayfish that clean surfaces and process organic debris
- Rock ledges that provide habitat and biological surface area
- Over-arching ferns and marginal plants that shade and filter
- Algae and beneficial bacteria that form the core of the water purification system

## Multi-Functional Water Design

In permaculture, every element should serve multiple functions. Natural pools exemplify this principle:

- **Recreation**: Swimming without chemical exposure
- **Fire-fighting Reserve**: Standing water available for emergency use
- **Heat Source**: Thermal mass for heat pump systems
- **Fire Barrier**: Water body that can slow or stop fire spread
- **Emergency Water Supply**: Rechargeable from roof catchment
- **Aquaculture**: Potential food production (lobster, trout)
- **Wildlife Habitat**: Support for local biodiversity

Natural pools can be recycled by photovoltaic pumps, eliminating the need for grid-connected energy and ongoing chemical purchases.

## Water Conservation on Property

### Designer's Checklist

On any property, the permaculture designer should:

1. **Identify all water sources** and analyse for both quality and quantity
2. **Reserve sites** for tanks, swales, and dams
3. **Use slope benefits** (or raise tanks) to enable gravity flow to use points
4. **Detail plant lists** that can grow unirrigated as mature plants or trees

### Dam Construction

Soil samples revealing 40% or more clay content indicate sites suited to earth-dam construction. Such sites must be reserved for future water storages. A sequence of primary valleys may enable a Keyline system for downhill fire control and irrigation.

### Arid vs. Humid Strategies

- **Arid areas** (evaporation exceeds precipitation): All water runoff must be infiltrated to soil storages via soil conditioning (rip-lines), swales, pits, or sandfield soakages.
- **Humid areas**: Open-surface dams can be used effectively.

### Key Design Principles

- Define water "pathways" in use so that water consumption is economical in houses
- Greywater should be directed to gardens via filtration beds, forests, or common effluent schemes
- Maximum use of all water through methane production, plant production, and irrigation
- Get good advice on and supervise construction of all dams
- Do not impede normal stream flow or fish migration
- Site houses out of the way in case of dam failure
- Allow adequate stable spillway flow for worst-case rain intensity
- Plant all earth storages and swales with trees to remove infiltrated water and prevent salting in arid areas
