# Harm Reduction Guide

## Overview

Harm reduction is a set of practical strategies and principles aimed at reducing the negative consequences associated with psychoactive substance use, without necessarily requiring abstinence. In the context of psychedelics and entheogens, harm reduction encompasses drug checking, dosing best practices, set and setting optimization, contraindication screening, and overdose response. These practices can meaningfully reduce risks while respecting individual autonomy.

## Core Principles

1. Substance-specific education over fear-based messaging
2. Honest, evidence-based information about risks and benefits
3. Empowerment of individuals to make informed choices
4. Non-judgmental support regardless of use decisions
5. Community care and mutual aid

## Drug Checking

### Reagent Testing

Reagent testing uses chemical solutions that change color in the presence of specific compound classes. It cannot identify every substance but can detect the presence or absence of expected compounds and flag dangerous adulterants.

Common reagents for psychedelic harm reduction:

- Ehrlich reagent: Turns purple with indoles (DMT, psilocybin, LSD)
- Marquis reagent: Turns black/dark with MDMA; orange/brown with amphetamines
- Hofmann reagent: Specific to LSD and other lysergamides
- Mecke reagent: Detects MDMA, MDA, and related compounds
- Simon's reagent: Distinguishes MDMA from MDA (secondary amine detection)
- Froehde reagent: Useful for detecting NBOMe compounds

Procedure: Place a small scraping of material on a ceramic plate or in a test tube. Add 1-2 drops of reagent. Observe color change over 30-60 seconds. Compare against reference charts.

Limitations: Reagent tests cannot detect fentanyl, 25I-NBOMe in trace amounts mixed with LSD, or novel compounds not in the reference database. They are a screening tool, not a guarantee.

### Fentanyl Test Strips

Fentanyl test strips detect the presence of fentanyl and many fentanyl analogs. While primarily associated with the opioid supply, testing is recommended for any substance obtained from unverified sources, as cross-contamination can occur.

Usage: Dissolve a small amount of the substance in water, dip the test strip, and read results within the specified time window (usually 2-5 minutes).

### Laboratory Analysis

The gold standard for drug checking is full laboratory analysis via GC/MS (gas chromatography-mass spectrometry) or HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography). Services like DrugsData (formerly EcstasyData), Energy Control, and various harm reduction organizations offer mail-in or drop-off testing.

## Dosing Principles

### Start Low, Go Slow

The single most important harm reduction principle. Begin with a sub-threshold or low dose and wait the full duration before considering redosing. This applies regardless of prior experience — potency varies between batches, and individual sensitivity changes over time.

### Know Your Substance

- Verify identity through reagent testing or lab analysis
- Research the specific substance's dose range, duration, and interactions
- Understand the difference between freebase and salt forms (e.g., DMT freebase vs. DMT fumarate)

### Weigh Accurately

- Use a milligram-accurate scale (0.001 g resolution) for potent compounds
- Volumetric dosing (dissolving a known amount in a known volume of liquid) for very potent substances like LSD or 5-MeO-DMT
- Never eyeball doses of potent compounds

### Redosing Considerations

- Understand the onset time before considering redosing
- Many psychedelics have strong tachyphylaxis (tolerance develops within hours)
- Redosing MDMA is particularly risky for serotonin neurotoxicity
- Wait at least 1.5-2x the onset time before considering additional doses

## Set and Setting

Set (mindset) and setting (environment) are the two most influential factors in determining the character and safety of a psychedelic experience.

### Set (Mindset)

- Current mental state: Avoid psychedelics during acute grief, instability, or crisis
- Intention: Having a clear but flexible intention can provide grounding
- Preparation: Mental preparation through meditation, journaling, or discussion
- Physical state: Adequate sleep, nutrition, and hydration beforehand

### Setting (Environment)

- Physical safety: Secure, comfortable space free from hazards
- Trusted companions: At least one sober sitter for full-dose experiences
- Minimal interruptions: Phone off, door locked, expectations communicated
- Sensory environment: Consider music, lighting, temperature, and comfort items
- Access to nature: Many people find natural settings deeply supportive

## Contraindications

Absolute contraindications for psychedelic use:

- Active psychosis or schizophrenia
- Current manic episode (bipolar I)
- Use of MAOIs without understanding interaction risks
- Severe cardiovascular disease (uncontrolled hypertension, recent heart attack, arrhythmias)
- Pregnancy

Relative contraindications (use with caution and ideally under professional guidance):

- Family history of psychotic disorders
- Current use of SSRIs or other psychiatric medications (interaction and efficacy risks)
- History of trauma (may be overwhelming without therapeutic support)
- Personality disorders (borderline, antisocial)
- Active substance use disorder (risk of destabilization)

### Medication Interactions

- SSRIs: May blunt psychedelic effects; abrupt discontinuation risks serotonin syndrome
- MAOIs: Dangerous hypertensive crisis when combined with tyramine-containing substances
- Lithium: Increased risk of seizures and neurotoxicity
- Antipsychotics: May block psychedelic effects and mask adverse reactions
- Stimulants: Increased cardiovascular strain
- Tramadol: Seizure risk and serotonin syndrome potential

## Overdose and Crisis Response

### Acute Psychological Crisis (Bad Trip)

1. Remain calm — your calm presence is the most important intervention
2. Change the setting: move to a quieter room, change music, go outside
3. Reassure the person that the experience is temporary and will pass
4. Offer water and a comfortable place to lie down
5. Use grounding techniques: hold their hand, have them describe what they see around them, breathe together
6. Avoid restraint unless there is imminent physical danger
7. Do not contradict their experience but gently remind them of the present moment
8. If the crisis persists beyond the expected duration or the person becomes a danger to themselves, seek emergency medical care

### Medical Emergency

Call emergency services if:

- Chest pain, difficulty breathing, or signs of hypertensive crisis
- Seizures
- Loss of consciousness lasting more than a few minutes
- Severe hyperthermia (especially with MDMA)
- Signs of serotonin syndrome (agitation, confusion, rapid heartbeat, high blood pressure, muscle rigidity, sweating)

Be honest with medical professionals about what substances were taken. Most jurisdictions have Good Samaritan laws that protect people seeking medical help from drug-related charges.
