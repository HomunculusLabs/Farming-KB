# Vegetable Gardening in Hard Times

## Overview

Steve Solomon's *Gardening When It Counts: [[growing-food-in-hard-times]]* is a practical guide to vegetable gardening designed for periods of economic difficulty, resource scarcity, and self-reliance. Unlike many gardening books that assume abundant resources—plentiful water, expensive inputs, and ideal conditions—Solomon's approach focuses on productive, low-input methods that work when it truly matters. The book emerged from Solomon's decades of experience growing food year-round, including during the economic challenges of the 1970s and through periods of both hardship and prosperity.

## The Philosophy of Gardening When It Counts

Solomon's core premise is that most popular gardening advice is optimized for easy times—when water is cheap, soil amendments are readily available, and the consequences of failure are merely inconvenience. "Gardening when it counts" means growing food under conditions where success genuinely matters: when you need the calories, when inputs are scarce, and when you cannot afford to lose your crop to preventable problems.

The book is deliberately written for "Mr. and Ms. Everybody Else"—people who look to conventional wisdom for guidance but may find that standard advice fails when conditions are less than ideal. Solomon challenges many common gardening assumptions and provides alternatives rooted in his own extensive experience.

## Historical Context

Solomon draws on [[palmer-mushroom-cooking-and-personal-experience]] spanning the economic cycles of the 1970s through the 2000s:

- **The 1970s**: High inflation and unemployment drove many people to home food production as a necessity
- **The 1980s–2000s**: Economic good times reduced the urgency of home gardening for many, though Solomon continued year-round production
- **The book's premise**: Hard times will return, and gardeners should be prepared with methods that work under constraint

Now retired to Tasmania—a temperate South Pacific island with a climate similar to Oregon's—Solomon continues to grow the majority of his household's food 12 months a year, demonstrating that the methods he advocates are not theoretical but proven over decades of practice.

## Book Structure and Key Topics

### Getting Land
The first practical challenge is securing growing space. Solomon discusses strategies for obtaining land whether through ownership, rental, community gardens, or making use of marginal spaces. The emphasis is on working with whatever land is available rather than waiting for ideal conditions.

### Becoming a Vegetable Gardener
Solomon addresses the mindset and commitment required to transition from casual or ornamental gardening to serious food production. This involves understanding that vegetable gardening for sustenance requires different skills and expectations than hobby gardening.

### Soil Basics
Understanding what soil is—and what plants need from it—forms the foundation of productive gardening. Solomon covers:
- **What is a vegetable?**: Botanical and practical classifications of garden crops
- **Helping plants grow**: The basic requirements all plants share (light, water, nutrients, temperature)
- **Increasing soil fertility**: Practical methods for building and maintaining productive soil without expensive inputs

### Tools and Tasks
Appropriate tool selection and efficient task management distinguish productive gardeners from frustrated ones. Solomon emphasizes simplicity and durability in tool choices, favoring traditional hand tools over powered equipment that requires fuel and maintenance.

### Seeds
The seed chapter addresses the entire seed lifecycle for the self-reliant gardener:
- **The seed business**: Understanding how the commercial seed industry works, including hybrid vs. open-pollinated varieties
- **Who to buy from**: Recommendations for reliable seed suppliers
- **Making seeds come up**: Germination requirements, seed treatment, and planting techniques
- **Saving on seed purchases**: Strategies to reduce costs without sacrificing quality
- **Growing your own seed**: The ultimate self-reliance skill—saving seed from your own crops

### Watering... and Not
Water is often the limiting factor in productive gardening. Solomon covers:
- **Four spacing systems**: Different approaches to plant spacing that balance water use with productivity
- **Not suffering drought**: Techniques for maintaining production during water scarcity
- **A gardener's textbook of sprinkler irrigation**: Practical irrigation methods for when supplemental water is needed

### Compost
[[soil-fertility-management]] through composting is central to Solomon's approach:
- **Why compost?**: The role of decomposed organic matter in soil health
- **Making low-grade compost**: Simple methods for producing useful compost with minimal effort
- **Medium-quality compost**: The once-a-year heap method for better results
- **Humanure**: Safe composting of human waste for closed-loop fertility
- **Green manure and cover crops**: Growing fertility in place through strategic plantings

### Insects and Diseases
Pest management without relying on synthetic chemicals:
- **Avoiding trouble**: Preventive strategies that reduce pest and disease pressure before it becomes a problem
- **Insects and their remedies**: [[solomon-disease-identification-and-organic-remedies]] management of common garden insects
- **Diseases and their remedies**: Recognition and treatment of common plant diseases

### What to Grow and How to Grow It
The culminating practical section provides crop-specific guidance:
- **Some general tips**: Cross-cutting advice applicable to many crops
- **Crops that are easiest to grow**: Recommendations for beginners and for ensuring reliable production
- **Crops that are harder to grow**: Intermediate crops requiring more skill or attention
- **[[solomon-difficult-vegetables-celery-cauliflower-high-demand-crops]]**: Advanced crops for experienced gardeners seeking a challenge

## Key Principles

### Low-Input High-Output
The overarching goal is maximum food production with minimum purchased inputs. This means [[building-soil-fertility-long-term]] from on-site resources, saving seeds, minimizing irrigation needs, and choosing crops that are productive under less-than-ideal conditions.

### Wide Spacing
One of Solomon's more controversial recommendations is wider plant spacing than commonly advised. This reduces competition for water and nutrients, decreases disease pressure through better air circulation, and allows individual plants to reach their full productive potential—critical when water is limited.

### Practical Self-Reliance
The book emphasizes skills that reduce dependence on external systems: seed saving, composting, water conservation, and tool maintenance. The goal is a garden that can continue producing even if supply chains are disrupted.

### Adaptation to Local Conditions
Rather than prescribing a universal method, Solomon encourages gardeners to observe their local conditions—climate, soil, rainfall patterns—and adapt their practices accordingly. His Tasmanian experience informs the book, but the principles are globally applicable.

## Relevance to Sustainability

Solomon's approach aligns closely with broader sustainability principles:
- **Closed-loop fertility**: Composting and green manures eliminate the need for [[teaming-with-nutrients-natural-vs-synthetic-fertilizers]]
- **Water conservation**: Spacing and irrigation strategies minimize water use
- **Genetic diversity**: Open-pollinated seed saving preserves crop genetic diversity
- **Local food production**: Reduces transportation energy and increases food security
- **Low energy input**: Hand tools and manual methods minimize fossil fuel dependence
