# PF TEK Contaminant Identification and Non-Germination Troubleshooting

## Overview

Contamination is the most common cause of failure in the PF TEK cultivation
method. Psilocybe Fanaticus (PF) identified the key [[mushroom-contamination-vectors]] and
developed systematic approaches to diagnose and prevent them. The PF TEK's
reliance on steam sterilization (rather than [[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]]) and its
exposure of jars during inoculation make contamination awareness critical.
This page covers contaminant types, the control jar technique, and systematic
troubleshooting for spore non-germination.

## The Dry Vermiculite Contaminant Barrier

The single most important [[cervantes-cannabis-contamination-prevention]] innovation in the PF TEK
is the dry vermiculite layer. After loading the substrate (brown rice powder
mixed with moistened vermiculite) into the jar, a 1/2 to 3/4 inch layer of
plain dry vermiculite is placed on top and leveled. This layer serves multiple
functions:

1. **Contaminant barrier** — Dry vermiculite is inhospitable to airborne
   mold spores and bacteria. Any contaminant spores that land on the surface
   cannot germinate without moisture, preventing them from reaching the
   nutrient-rich substrate below.
2. **Moisture regulation** — The dry layer absorbs excess moisture that
   transpires upward from the colonizing substrate, preventing condensation
   from pooling at the top of the jar.
3. **Inoculation shield** — During inoculation, the needle passes through
   the dry layer to reach the substrate. The dry vermiculite acts as a filter,
   reducing the chance that airborne contaminants follow the needle track
   into the wet substrate.

## Contaminant Types and Identification

Contaminant invaders appear in various colors from pastels to black. When
contamination is detected, the culture is doomed and the jar should be
discarded carefully.

### Mold Contamination

Mold appears as fuzzy or powdery growths in various colors:

- **Green/blue-green** — Penicillium or Aspergillus, the most common airborne
  contaminants. Indicates breached vermiculite barrier or insufficient
  sterilization.
- **Black** — Rhizopus or Mucor species. Appearance within days suggests
  contaminated syringes or failed sterilization.
- **White (fast-growing, cottony)** — If not rhizomorphic like mushroom
  mycelium, may be Trichoderma or Dactylium contaminants.
- **Pink/orange** — Often Neurospora, aggressive and fast-spreading.
- **Yellow** — May indicate bacterial contamination or spent substrate.

### Bacterial Contamination

[[pf-tek-bacteria-contamination-detection]] is detectable through the dry vermiculite layer as a
sour, foul odor within two days after inoculation, typically accompanied by
no spore germination. Key indicators:

- Rancid or sour smell detectable when loosening the jar lid
- Substrate may appear unusually wet, slimy, or discolored
- No white mycelial growth appears despite successful inoculation
- In severe cases, the substrate may collapse or produce gas bubbles

Bacteria contamination is dangerous. When cleaning contaminated jars,
maintain a safe distance, avoid inhaling bacteria, and wash thoroughly
after contact.

## The Control Jar Technique

The control jar technique is PF's systematic method for isolating the source
of contamination. It distinguishes between sterilization failures and
inoculation-related contamination.

### Procedure

1. After steam sterilizing the jars, let them cool completely
2. Tighten the lids and leave jars uninoculated for several days (3-7 days)
3. Observe for any colored growths or changes in substrate appearance
4. Loosen jar lids periodically to check for the tell-tale rancid odor
   of bacteria

### Interpretation

- **Control jar stays clean** — Contamination after inoculation means the
  source is the spore syringe or breached vermiculite barrier. Sterilization
  was successful.
- **Control jar shows contamination** — Increase steaming duration (at least
  one hour at a gentle boil). The [[boil-a-bag-pressure-sterilization-technique]] needs adjustment.

## Non-Germination Troubleshooting

When spores fail to germinate despite clean jars, several factors may be
responsible:

### 1. Inoculation Too Shallow

The spore solution may have been absorbed by the non-nutritive dry
vermiculite layer instead of reaching the substrate. The needle tip must be
below the dry layer so solution flows down the side of the substrate cake.
Solution: Insert the needle deeper, ensuring the tip contacts the glass below
the vermiculite barrier before depressing the plunger.

### 2. Heat-Killed Spores

If jars were not allowed to cool after sterilization, the residual heat will
kill the spores on contact. Always verify jars are cool to the touch before
inoculation. PF recommends letting jars cool to room temperature, which may
take several hours after steaming.

### 3. Transit Heat Damage

Rare, but spore syringes can be killed by sustained heat during shipping
(overheated cargo holds, sun-exposed mail trucks, or hot mailboxes). Spores
that survive normal freezing may be killed by sustained temperatures above
approximately 45°C (113°F).

### 4. Freezing and Viability

Extreme cold may damage spores. Properly stored spores last months to years,
but old or improperly stored syringes may have reduced viability. PF
guaranteed syringes to be clean and viable with replacement or refund.

## Sterilization Best Practices

PF TEK uses steam sterilization (not pressure canning) because the substrate
contains no grain requiring thorough cooking. Key practices include maintaining
a gentle boil for at least one hour in a tightly covered pot, keeping jar lids
loose during sterilization for steam penetration, and using a steamer insert
to keep jar bottoms off the pot bottom to prevent cracking. Avoid overheating
— excessive heat dries the substrate, evidenced by initial mycelial growth
that halts at a consistent line with no contamination visible.

## Halted Growth Diagnosis

When mycelium colonizes but then stops with no visible contamination, the
cause is almost certainly dried substrate. Growth halts at a consistent line
and the substrate may appear lighter than expected. The remedy is to increase
water content in the substrate formula. Keep notes on formulations, as
vermiculite coarseness varies between brands and affects water-holding
capacity.
