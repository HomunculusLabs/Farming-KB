# High Island and Tropical Valley Design Systems

High islands, particularly volcanic ones, present unique
opportunities for integrated permaculture design. The radial
valley form, from summit to shore, creates a complete water
and nutrient gradient that can be managed as a single
productive system. The [[mollison-pdc-high-island-design]] model, exemplified by the Hawaiian Ohana system,
demonstrates how traditional societies achieved sustainable
abundance through careful zonation.

## The Ohana System Model

The Ohana system of Hawaii represents one of the most
sophisticated traditional permaculture designs. One radial
valley of a volcanic island integrates the following
elements from summit to sea:

- A: "Taboo" forest of upper slopes, preserving watershed
  and cloud capture
- B: Stream diversion to ridge or high terraces, main
  village settlement
- C: Sacrificial forests of palm to modify hurricanes
- D: Net fish ponds to retain leached nutrients, growing
  mullet and shellfish
- E: Reef modified with coral blocks to shelter crayfish
  and fish

## Zonation by Slope Position

### Upper Slopes and Ridgetops
The upper slopes are preserved as forest, often declared
taboo or sacred in traditional systems. These forests
serve critical functions:

- [[mollison-designers-forest-condensation-and-micro-climate-rainfall]] for water supply
- Protection of the watershed from erosion
- Supply of timber and specialty forest products
- Wind modification and hurricane buffering

### Keyline and Keypoint Zones
The [[mollison-pdc-keypoint-keyline-humid]] in the
valley profile, where the slope changes from convex to
concave, is the critical point for water storage. Human
occupation and complex cultivated forests and gardens can
be established downslope from this point.

### Stable Plateaus and Bench Sites
The stable plateau, the hill rising above the valley, and
bench sites above the reach of flood and sheltered from
[[mollison-hurricane-and-cyclone-resilient-design]] are prime cluster settlement (village) sites,
with some scattered housing higher on ridges and the forest
edge.

### Low Slopes (2-15 Degrees)
Low slopes are well suited to [[mollison-designers-tropical-terrace-design-and-earth-shaping]]
as terrace and padi, with limited grazing and innovative
forestry. These are the sustainable agricultural areas,
where attention to sub-contoured agriculture,
[[mollison-windbreak-design]], and access will
help direct run-off and water to crop.

### Coastal Flats and Valley Floors
Rich and often deep humus soils can accumulate on valley
floors. Greywater and processed manures from settlement
and livestock add to fertility, where extensive
[[mollison-aquaculture-designers-checklist]]
or rich forest/orchard crop can be developed.

## Shoreline Design Principles

Shorelines are preserved for 100 m (330 feet) or so inland
for essential windbreak, shore stability, and cloud
generation over land. Key design elements include:

- Selected quiet harbours and refuges accept fishing
  settlements based on sea resources
- Shores with 15-20 year tsunami history need
  sacrificial palm/Casuarina barriers to reduce storm
  damage ashore
- Lightly-built and temporary shore housing is used only
  when working the coast
- More substantial homes and sheltered gardens are placed
  safely inland or behind earth embankments

## Lagoon and Estuary Systems

Where shallows or a coral-based lagoon/reef system stretches
offshore, several categories of use apply:

### Sea Impoundments
Ancient and yet innovative sea-walls (usually of
semi-permeable coral blocks with entries, flood-tide gates,
and a series of inner pens) are an excellent use of the
shallows. Restricted [[mollison-permaculture-two-coastal-marine-aquaculture]]
plantations help stabilise the banks and provide sea mulch
while catching any silt that washes down from above.

### Marine Production
Warm-water lagoons are rich in algae and good sites for
cultivated seaweed. They also impound and fatten mullet
and mangrove fish, oysters and eels, shellfish and prawns.
Mobile phosphates are fixed in plants and mud in a few
days, and are then available for growth. Such rich
estuaries are invaluable as managed maricultures.

### Artificial Reef Systems
[[mollison-designers-ocean-reef-restoration-mariculture]] systems of
tyres (on sand), coral blocks, and boxes of palm trunks
greatly enlarge the habitat for sedentary fish and
lobsters. Within a year, these constructs are consolidated
or cemented by corals. Weak electrolysis on metal mesh
creates an artificial but permanent coralline deposit more
rapidly.

## Elements of a Village Complex

Based on traditional and modern villages, a complex in the
humid tropics includes:

- A well-arranged array of housing around a compound
- Processing and storage areas, threshing floors, meeting
  house, cooperative store
- A well, piped water, tanks, or dam integrated for clean
  water supply
- A [[mollison-designers-plant-nursery-propagation-bed-design]] to serve gardens and forest
- 0.25-1.0 ha of [[mollison-tropical-home-garden-design]] and orchard at each house
- Domestic animal ranges (chickens, guinea pigs, ducks,
  rabbits, pigs, pigeons) as manure resources
- [[mollison-duck-and-waterfowl-integration]]
  with some animals housed above them
- Fuelwood plantation integrated with windbreak
- Careful zonation of tree polyculture
- Special facilities: boat landing, freezer, solar pond,
