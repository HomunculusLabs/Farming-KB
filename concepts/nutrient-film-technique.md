# Nutrient Film Technique (NFT) for Cannabis

Nutrient Film Technique (NFT) is a high-performance active recovery hydroponic system in which a thin film of aerated nutrient solution flows continuously over plant roots housed in enclosed gullies or channels. NFT is classified as an active hydroponic system because it actively moves the nutrient solution through the growing apparatus using a pump.

## How NFT Works

In an NFT system, seedlings or cuttings with established root systems are placed on capillary matting located on the bottom of covered channels (gullies). The capillary matting stabilizes nutrient solution flow and holds roots in place. Constantly aerated nutrient solution is pumped from a reservoir to the upper end of the inclined gullies. The solution flows down the channel over and around the roots, forming a thin film, and drains back into the reservoir for recirculation.

Irrigation in NFT systems is most often constant, running 24 hours a day. Roots receive plenty of oxygen and are able to absorb a maximum of nutrient solution in this continuously flowing environment.

## Key Design Elements

Three factors are critical to successful NFT operation:
1. **Gully incline**: The slope of the gully must be adequate to prevent water from stagnating but gentle enough to maintain a consistent film of nutrient solution.
2. **Volume**: The flow rate and volume of nutrient solution must be sufficient to keep roots moist at all times.
3. **Flow consistency**: Proper flow of nutrient solution ensures all plants receive adequate moisture and nutrition.

## Gully Construction and Design

NFT gullies come in several designs:
- **Flat-bottom gullies**: The most common type, with capillary matting placed on the bottom beneath the growing cubes to anchor roots and distribute nutrient solution evenly.
- **Rounded-corner gullies**: Popular in Australia, these promote smoother nutrient solution flow and reduce stagnation.
- **Reinforced gullies**: Double-reinforced bottoms make gullies durable and rigid enough to support large plants, heavy root systems, and large volumes of nutrient solution.

Many NFT gullies have ribs on the underside that provide structural support, prevent warping, and function as drainage channels to direct nutrient solution evenly along the bottom of the gully.

## Advantages of NFT

- **High performance**: NFT systems deliver excellent growth rates and yields when properly fine-tuned.
- **Water efficiency**: The thin film of nutrient solution minimizes water usage compared to [[ebb-and-flow]] systems.
- **Oxygen availability**: Roots receive abundant oxygen in the humid air space above the nutrient film.
- **Easy to clean**: NFT systems are easy to clean and lay out after each crop cycle.
- **Scalable**: Systems can be expanded by adding more gullies to the manifold.

## Disadvantages and Risks

- **No buffering ability**: NFT offers practically no buffering capacity. In the absence of a substantial Cannabis Growing Mediums, roots must be kept perfectly moist by the nutrient solution at all times.
- **Pump dependency**: If the pump fails, roots dry out and die quickly. Even a single day without flow can kill small feeder roots, causing grave consequences.
- **Experience required**: Only growers with several years of experience should attempt an NFT system if working alone. With assistance, NFT systems are easier to master.
- **Nozzle clogging**: In hybrid systems that use spray nozzles, clogged nozzles can be difficult to access and maintain.

## Hybrid NFT Systems

Many NFT systems incorporate hybrid designs:

1. **Spaghetti tubing delivery**: Nutrient solution is delivered via spaghetti tubing to each plant individually, ensuring more irrigation sites and better per-plant coverage. The solution flows through a small basket of growing medium before running down the gully.

2. **Spray nozzle systems**: Some hybrid NFT systems employ spray nozzles inside the gully. The nozzles spray nutrient solution on and around roots to keep the root zone environment at or near 100 percent humidity. The solution then flows down a PVC pipe, over roots, and back to the reservoir.

Cervantes cautions that many hybrid NFT systems are poorly planned. Systems constructed from white four-inch PVC pipe can allow enough light through the thin walls to illuminate roots, causing them to turn green or rot. Nozzle-based systems inside PVC pipe present maintenance challenges if nozzles become clogged.

## Net Pots in NFT

Small net pots are preferred for most NFT systems. They hold the growing medium and plant while allowing roots to extend into the gully. Larger net pots are also used in some NFT and [[rockwool-cannabis]] cubes are common starting points. The capillary matting in the gully bottom also serves as a root anchor and moisture distributor.

## Cannabis Cultivation in NFT

NFT is best suited for [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-cloning-and-seed-propagation]] gardens.

Proper [[cervantes-passive-wick-hydroponic-systems]] in several important ways:

- **vs. DWC**: [[ebb-and-flow]] floods and drains the growing medium periodically, while NFT maintains constant flow. Ebb and flow provides more buffering through the growing medium.
- **vs. Aeroponics**: banisteriopsis caapi mists roots with nutrient solution, while NFT flows solution over roots. Both lack medium buffering, but aeroponics provides more oxygen at the root zone.
- **vs. Drip**: [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] and flowering of established plants with strong root systems. Clones should be well-rooted before being placed in NFT gullies. The system performs well for shorter plants and can be configured for Sea of Green gardens.
