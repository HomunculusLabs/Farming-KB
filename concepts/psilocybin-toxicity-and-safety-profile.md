# Psilocybin Toxicity and Safety Profile

Psilocybin and its active metabolite [psilocin are among the safest known psychoactive substances in terms of acute toxicity. The gap between active and lethal doses is enormous, making fatal overdose from psilocybin-containing mushrooms practically impossible in adults. This safety profile, however, does not mean the substances are without risk -- particularly for vulnerable populations such as children and individuals with pre-existing neurological conditions.

## Acute Toxicity Data

### LD50 and Active Dose Comparison

Extensive animal research during the 1950s established that both psilocybin and psilocin are alkaloids of negligible acute toxicity. The specific findings:

- **LD50 in mice:** 280 mg/kg body weight for psilocybin
- **Typical human active dose:** approximately 0.02 mg/kg body weight
- **Safety margin:** Several hundred times the active dosage

This means that, proportionally, an adult human would need to consume their own body weight in fresh mushrooms to approach the lethal dosage limit. J. Ott independently calculated similar figures, reinforcing the enormous therapeutic index of psilocybin.

### Comparison with Mescaline and LSD

Animal tests revealed that, on average, psilocybin was only half as toxic as [mescaline, while being 50 times more potent as a psychoactive substance. For example:

- Up to 2 grams of mescaline were administered with no dangerous side effects
- The usual dose of psilocybin ranged from 3 to 30 mg in psychological testing and psychotherapy sessions

This extraordinary ratio of low toxicity to high potency makes psilocybin one of the most favorable profiles among all known [hallucinogenic compounds.

### The Only Known Fatality

The only death ever associated with psilocybin-containing mushrooms occurred in the fall of 1960 in Milwaukee, Oregon. A child picked several mushrooms from grassy soil below a cluster of conifers and ate them raw. The child experienced cramps and a high fever, with symptoms resembling "status epilepticus." Despite treatment with medications, the child died within three days.

Several important caveats apply to this case:

1. **Species identification dispute:** The mushroom sample was originally identified as [[psilocybe-baeocystis]]. However, [P. Stamets contested this finding, claiming the species had been misidentified based on photographs of the mushrooms, which he believed showed [[psilocybe-cyanescens]] -- a species known for exceptionally high levels of psilocybin and psilocin.

2. **Possible latent epilepsy:** It remains uncertain whether toxic concentrations of alkaloids were the cause of death, or whether ingestion triggered a latent case of epilepsy in the form of an acute episode that could not be controlled. If a similar incident happened today, fatal outcomes could be prevented with modern anticonvulsant medications discovered in the intervening decades.

3. **Small children are an exception:** Multiple sources confirm that small children have abnormal reactions to psilocybin, including loss of consciousness, cramps, and danger of death. This vulnerability is unique to children and has not been observed in adults.

Despite the hundreds of thousands of voluntary self-experiments taking place every year in the U.S. alone, no fatalities caused by magic mushrooms have ever occurred among adults.

## Debunked Myths: Baeocystin and Norbaeocystin

Due to publicity generated by the 1960 fatality, two alkaloids first isolated from Psilocybe baeocystis -- [baeocystin and norbaeocystin -- acquired reputations as being extremely poisonous as well as strongly psychoactive. Both claims are unsubstantiated:

- Both baeocystin and norbaeocystin are present in other mushroom species, such as [[psilocybe-semilanceata]], at generally higher levels compared to the alkaloid content of Psilocybe baeocystis
- No evidence supports claims of exceptional toxicity or psychoactivity
- [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]] has been consumed widely without incidents attributable to these alkaloids

## Physical Addiction and Tolerance

Comprehensive investigations have long proven that psilocybin, [mescaline, and LSD are not physically addictive, nor do they cause withdrawal symptoms of any kind. Key findings:

- Repeated self-experimentation over long periods is rare
- Most long-term users eventually reduce frequency of use due to the nature of the experiences, which initiate powerful transpersonal processes and facilitate the emergence of personal conflicts
- Habitual daily users quickly develop tolerance to the point of being unable to experience any psychoactive effects at all
- This rapid tolerance development is why clinical trials are spaced to allow intervals of at least one week between repeated applications

## Concerns About Adolescent Use

Most researchers, including gordon-wasson, strongly advised against usage of hallucinogens by youngsters, even in controlled experimental settings. The reasoning is compelling:

- Adolescence is a time of upheaval, a developmental stage when youngsters struggle with themselves and their surroundings
- Young people are searching for a purpose and a firm direction in life
- Numerous internal conflicts arise during this period that need to be dealt with and integrated
- [Hallucinogenic substances, including psilocybin, are bound to release a stream of new emotions and conflicts
- Such experiences often serve to confuse teenaged users and compound existing areas of conflict
- The escapist aspect of psychedelic use, portrayed against a backdrop of political upheaval, has historically fueled reactionary [[eu-legislative-responses-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-2001-2006]]

## Mechanism of Action: Beyond "Toxic Psychosis"

Biochemical research efforts during the 1950s and 1960s sought to discover the [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] sites for hallucinogenic compounds in the brain. S. Grof undertook the tedious task of analyzing 5,000 experimental LSD protocols in an effort to isolate "absolute" symptoms that are reported or occur all of the time. His results were negative.

According to Grof, hallucinogenic substances are non-specific triggers causing a sequence of altered states of consciousness, which do not fit the syndrome labeled "toxic psychosis." Rather, it is the individual's personality, along with the experimental setting, that significantly shapes the nature of the [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]. This view is shared by a majority of experts with considerable experience in conducting [psychedelics-assisted psychotherapy.
