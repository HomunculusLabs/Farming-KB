# Mobile Greenhouse Design and Operation

## Overview

The mobile greenhouse is an innovative approach to season extension that Eliot Coleman
revived from historical European practices. Rather than building multiple permanent
greenhouses, a single structure is moved along a track system to cover different
growing areas throughout the year. This approach dramatically increases the productive
capacity of a given greenhouse footprint while reducing construction costs and
improving soil health through crop rotation. The concept was first documented in 1898
in England, where a large glasshouse on railroad wheels running on steel rails proved
that greenhouse mobility was both practical and safe.

## Historical Origins

According to Coleman's research, the first mobile greenhouse was built in 1898 in
England. The original design used an iron framework supported by railroad wheels
running on steel rails — an engineering approach borrowed directly from railway
technology. This early adoption demonstrates that the concept of moving entire
greenhouse structures is not a modern invention but rather a return to proven
historical practices that were largely abandoned during the era of cheap fossil
fuels and heated greenhouse production.

Coleman revived this concept at Four Season Farm in Harborside, Maine, adapting it
to modern materials and the specific demands of a four-season organic vegetable
operation. The adaptation replaced glass and iron with lightweight plastic film and
pipe-frame construction, dramatically reducing costs while maintaining the core
benefit of mobility.

## How Mobile Greenhouses Work

The fundamental principle is simple: instead of covering a fixed area year-round,
the greenhouse moves to where it is needed. A typical cycle might follow this pattern:

1. **Winter position:** The greenhouse covers cold-hardy crops (spinach, lettuce,
   claytonia, mache) planted in late summer through fall. These crops are harvested
   throughout the winter months.
2. **Spring move:** As weather warms, the greenhouse is moved to a second area where
   early spring crops (tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers) have been started. The winter
   area is then exposed to natural rainfall and can be replanted.
3. **Summer position:** The greenhouse covers warm-season crops through the growing
   season, protecting them from cool nights and extending the harvest period.
4. **Fall move:** The greenhouse returns to the original area (or a third position)
   to cover fall-planted crops for the winter harvest cycle.

This rotation means that a single greenhouse structure can serve multiple distinct
growing areas, effectively multiplying its productive value. A farm that could
afford only one greenhouse can now manage multiple crop successions under cover.

## Structural Design

Coleman's mobile greenhouses at Four Season Farm follow these design principles:

- **Frame:** Standard pipe-frame construction (gothic-style hoop house), identical
  to stationary greenhouse design. The mobility system is an addition to, not a
  modification of, standard greenhouse structure.
- **Covering:** UV-resistant plastic film, single layer. The lightweight covering
  reduces the overall weight that must be moved.
- **Dimensions:** 30 feet wide by 96 feet long — the same dimensions as Coleman's
  stationary cold houses. The size is manageable for manual or mechanized movement.
- **Movement system:** The greenhouse runs on a rail or track system. The original
  English design used railroad wheels on steel rails. Modern adaptations use various
  low-friction systems including pipe rails, ground-level tracks, and skid systems.
- **Alignment:** East-west axis orientation, which maximizes winter light capture
  on the southern exposure.

## Soil and Crop Rotation Benefits

The primary agronomic advantage of mobile greenhouses is the ability to rotate
covered crops, which addresses one of the major limitations of permanent greenhouse
production:

- **Prevents soil degradation:** Permanent greenhouses on the same ground year after
  year develop nutrient imbalances, salt accumulation, and soil structure problems.
  Moving the greenhouse allows each area to recover under open-field conditions.
- **Enables true crop rotation:** Different crop families can follow each other in
  the same covered space across years, breaking disease and pest cycles.
- **Natural rainfall access:** When the greenhouse moves away, the exposed soil
  receives natural rainfall that leaches accumulated salts and recharges soil
  moisture profiles that irrigation alone cannot replicate.
- **Cover cropping:** Areas not under the greenhouse can be cover-cropped, building
  soil organic matter and nitrogen during the off-season.
- **Reduced disease pressure:** Pathogens that build up under continuous greenhouse
  cultivation are interrupted when the area is exposed to weather and UV light.

## Economic Advantages

The mobile greenhouse approach offers significant economic benefits for small-scale
farms:

- **Lower capital cost:** One mobile greenhouse replaces multiple stationary
  structures. The cost of the movement system is far less than the cost of building
  additional greenhouses.
- **Increased revenue per greenhouse:** By covering multiple growing areas, a single
  greenhouse generates revenue from two to four times the area it physically covers.
- **Reduced heating costs:** The mobile greenhouse is used in Coleman's system as
  an unheated or minimally heated structure, aligned with his "cold house" philosophy
  of passive climate modification.
- **Flexible crop planning:** Growers can adjust the annual rotation based on market
  conditions, weather patterns, and crop performance without being locked into a
  fixed greenhouse location.

## Integration with the Winter Harvest System

At Four Season Farm, the mobile greenhouse is integrated with the broader winter
harvest system. The double-layer protection concept (greenhouse + interior row cover
over wire wickets) works identically whether the greenhouse is stationary or mobile.
The mobile structure simply allows this climate modification to be applied where it
is most needed at any given time of year.

The goal remains the same: "never leaving a greenhouse bed unplanted." Within
twenty-four hours of harvesting a crop, the residues are removed, the soil is
re-prepared, and the bed is replanted. The mobile greenhouse facilitates this
intensive management by ensuring that covered growing space is always available
where and when it is needed.

## Key Takeaways

1. Mobile greenhouses originated in 1898 England and were revived by Coleman using
   modern lightweight materials and standard hoop house construction.
2. A single mobile structure covers multiple growing areas through the year,
   effectively multiplying its productive value by 2-4x.
3. Movement enables true crop rotation, natural rainfall access, and cover cropping
  — solving the soil degradation problems of permanent greenhouses.
4. Lower capital cost than building multiple stationary structures, with the
   additional benefit of flexible crop planning.
5. Integrates seamlessly with Coleman's double-layer winter harvest system of
   greenhouse plus interior row cover for passive cold-climate production.
