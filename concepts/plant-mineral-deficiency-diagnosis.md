## Plant Mineral Deficiency Diagnosis

## Seasonal Nutrient Behaviour in Plants

Analysis of leaf tissue across the growing season reveals distinct nutrient behaviour patterns. Research on persimmon trees (Clark and Smith, 1986) identified four types of nutrient behaviour, which are broadly applicable to most plants:

### Type 1: Zinc, Iron, Copper, Sulphur
High in new spring growth, falling over summer, and finally becoming more concentrated in the last leaves as uptake by roots concentrates these elements before leaf drop. These elements show similar curves with slight increases in late season.

### Type 2: Boron, Manganese, Magnesium, Calcium
These are non-mobile elements — once in the plant, they are not readily translocated. Their concentrations increase steadily throughout the whole season of growth. Magnesium is an exception as it is not normally regarded as non-mobile.

### Type 3: Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Sulphur
These show rapid early uptake, then a gradual decline over the season. They are highly mobile within the plant.

### Type 4: Potassium
Remains fairly steady over the year, then declines as wood storage and root reserves build up in autumn. Potassium is "withdrawn" from leaves in autumn.

These findings have direct implications for timing foliar sprays, selecting the season for leaf [[soil-ph]], drainage, salt, viral disease |

### Third Level: Leaf Colour Pattern

**D — Uniform colour change:**
- Leaves uniformly yellowish or pale (even veins), poor and spindly plants, especially in heavily cropped or poor leached sandy areas: Sulphur deficiency {1}
- Leaves not uniformly affected, veins or centres still green: E

**E — Wilting and colour loss:**
- Leaves wilted, light-coloured, then dying. Onions undersized, peas with barely-formed seeds. Common on coastal sands. Black sheep may show brown wool tinge: Potassium deficiency {11}
- Wilted and dying leaves not the primary problem: F

**F — Interveinal [[soil-life]]. Do not overdo it — nitrates in green plants can cause blue-baby syndrome.

### {9} Magnesium
Check if potash is not too high. Add clay to sandy acid soils. Use dolomite for first dressing, then limestone. Epsom salts can be used as foliar spray for severe deficiency.

### {10} Molybdenum
Mix 10 g of sodium molybdate with 5 kg of sand. Apply 1/100 of this per square metre every 10 years.

### {11} Potassium
Use wood ashes, diluted urine in early growth, mulches including seaweed, fly ash from cement works, bird manures, and comfrey.

### {12} Phosphorus
Bring pH to 6-6.5 using lime in acid soils and humus in alkaline. Use bone meal, bury bones, or use tested [[rock-phosphate]] free of cadmium or uranium. Stop deep digging and start mulching. Water with comfrey tea and dilute bird manure. All animal manures contain some phosphorus.

### {14} Potassium (root crops)
Same remedies as {11}, but note that potassium is translocated to roots late in growth, so symptoms may appear late for root crops.
