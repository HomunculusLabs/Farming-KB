## Fungal Biosorption of Heavy Metals by Filamentous Fungi
Biosorption by filamentous fungi represents a promising approach
for removing heavy metals from contaminated effluents. Dead fungal
biomass offers several advantages over live systems: no nutrient
requirements, no toxicity limitations, easy regeneration, and
predictable performance across varied environmental conditions.
## Biosorbent Selection Criteria
The characteristics determining suitability of fungal biomass for
biosorption are:
1. Biosorption efficiency and metal uptake capacity for metal ions
2. Specificity for target metal ions
3. Flocculation and sedimentation efficiency
4. Desorption efficiency for biomass regeneration
5. Preservation of biosorptive capacity through reuse cycles
Fungal melanins contribute significantly to [[chitin-and-chitosan-in-agriculture]] components, serves as the
predominant target for metal binding.
## Mechanisms of Metal Binding
Fungal biosorption involves several physicochemical mechanisms at
the cell wall surface:
- Ion exchange: Metal ions displace lighter ions (H+, Na+, K+)
  from cell wall binding sites
- Complexation: Metal ions form coordination complexes with
  functional groups such as carboxyl, amino, sulfhydryl, and
  phosphate groups
- Surface adsorption: Physical adsorption onto the cell wall
  surface driven by van der Waals forces
- Precipitation: Metal ions may precipitate on the cell wall as
  hydroxides or other insoluble compounds
Cell wall composition varies among species and includes chitin,
chitosan, glucans, mannans, and proteins, all providing binding
sites for metal ions.
## Metal Uptake Performance by Species
### Mucorales (Highest Priority — Uranium)
Mucorales are excellent uranium biosorbents. Uptake capacities
in mg/g dry weight:
- Mucor javanicus: 343 mg/g UO2(II)
- [[aspergillus-niger]]: 580 mg/g Pb(II)
- Phellinus badius: 170 mg/g Pb(II)
- Rhizopus nigricans: 68 mg/g Pb(II) at 500 mg/l
- [[mucor-hiemalis]] follows: Cr(III) > Cu(II)
> Pb(II) > Ag(I) > Al(III) > Co(II) > Zn(II) > Ni(II) > Fe(II).
## Biomass Pretreatment and Regeneration
Common metal eluants for regeneration include:
- Counterions: Na/K/Ca salts (chlorides, carbonates) at 0.1-1 N
  for 1-2 hours
- Proton exchangers: HCl, H2SO4 at 0.01-1 N for 1-2 hours
- Complexing agents: EDTA, nitriloacetic acid at 0.01-0.1 N for
  2 hours
- Heat treatment: Boiling for 5 minutes
- Combinations: Basic followed by acidic treatment or vice versa
Pretreatment with alcohol, acetone, and HCl improved uptake
capacity of Rhizopus nigricans. More than 90% of biosorbed metal
was eluted, achieving a 25-fold copper concentration increase.
## Reactor Types for Biosorption
Several reactor configurations have been applied:
- Batch stirred-tank reactors: Simple operation for small-scale
- Continuous-flow stirred-tank: Better for continuous treatment
- Fixed packed-bed reactors: Most common commercially; high
  surface area contact
- Immobilized reactors: Enhanced stability and reusability
Mathematical models include Langmuir, Freundlich, and
Redlich-Peterson isotherms for predicting sorption performance.
