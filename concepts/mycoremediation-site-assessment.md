# Mycoremediation Site Assessment

Bioremediation is site-specific, and feasibility studies are required before implementing full-scale remediation. Proper site assessment is the foundation for successful mycoremediation, determining whether fungal approaches are appropriate and how they should be designed and implemented.

## Preliminary Site Characterization

### Contaminant Identification

The first step in site assessment involves identifying the types, concentrations, and distribution of contaminants. Petroleum-contaminated sites require measurement of total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPHs) and BTEX components. Several field kits exist for TPH measurement, including immunoassay-based EnviroGard, turbidimetric-based PetroFlag test, and DR/2000 field kits, though these have certain limitations. The heterogeneity of contaminants and extreme concentrations present challenges for remediation design.

### Soil and Groundwater Analysis

Soil type (loam, clay loam, sandy clay loam) affects remediation approach and timeline. Soil texture influences aeration, water-holding capacity, and mycelial penetration. Groundwater contamination must be assessed, as petroleum-contaminated aquifers threaten drinking water supplies. The EPA has published monographs on different technologies useful for petroleum-contaminated site cleanup.

### Environmental Conditions

Temperature, moisture, pH, and oxygen availability at the site must be characterized. These parameters directly affect fungal growth rates and enzymatic activity. Seasonal variations must be considered for outdoor applications. Cleanup priorities depend on threats to soil fertility, drinking water, and groundwater (ASTM, 1995).

## Fungal Assessment Methods

### Fungal Enumeration

Preliminary analyses of total heterotrophic and specific hydrocarbon-degrading fungal counts provide understanding of soil biological conditions and the extent of acclimatization of indigenous fungal populations. These indicate the viability of indigenous fungal populations capable of supporting bioremediation. Methods include heterotrophic plate counts, epifluorescence direct counts, hydrocarbon-degrader plate counts, most probable number (MPN) techniques, DNA probes, lipid assays, and metabolic indicators.

### Culture Media

Acidified wort agar is employed for yeast enumeration in soil. Fungi are isolated by placing small pieces of contaminated material on potato dextrose agar, V-8 agar, and Gorodkowa's media. Basal salts medium supports fungal growth on fuel oils. Malt-agar chloramphenicol medium supports general fungal populations, while media specific for hydrocarbon-adapted fungi (HAF) use hydrocarbons as the carbon source.

### Respirometric Tests

Mineralization studies measuring total CO2 production assess the potential for hydrocarbon degradation. This approach provides a rapid test for determining biological treatment options such as nutrient addition and fungal inoculation effects. Simple respirometric flask methods or automated equipment monitor O2 consumption and/or CO2 evolution rates. The Biometer flask test assesses mineralization rates from small soil samples.

Radiorespiratory methods using carbon-14 labeled hydrocarbons verify mineralization by filamentous fungi. Recovery of radiolabeled carbon in aqueous, organic, and cell fractions following incubation quantifies degradation pathways.

### Soil Microcosm Tests

Soil microcosm tests range from simple jars to sophisticated systems. Good results are expected when real environmental conditions are incorporated into the microcosm design. Sterile treatments serve as controls to separate abiotic hydrocarbon loss from actual biodegradation. Mathematical equations can describe transformation kinetics. A soil microcosm test using fungi was designed by Yateem et al. (1998) using eight 4-L sterilized glass flasks with 1 kg of contaminated soil each.

### Miscellaneous Tests

Dehydrogenase activity assays indicate the possible inhibitory effect of contaminants on soil microbial activities. Ratios of hydrocarbon compounds within complex mixtures can assess degradation progress. Biomarker compounds serve as indicators of biodegradation activity.

## Treatment Technology Selection

### Available Technologies

A wide variety of physical, chemical, and biological technologies exist for decontamination. Table 4.1 in Singh lists technologies applicable to soil and groundwater: air sparging, air biosparging, carbon adsorption, soil vapor extraction, bioventing, incineration, natural attenuation, bioremediation, phytoremediation, mycorrhizas, and white-rot fungi. Each has specific limitations and advantages at a given site.

### Engineered In Situ Bioremediation

Engineered in situ bioremediation has received wide acclaim because it requires little energy, preserves soil structure, and can detoxify contaminants. It usually requires stimulation of indigenous microflora by oxidants and nutrients. Complete mineralization to innocuous end products is preferred over partial degradation or phase transfer.

### Alternative Approaches

Natural attenuation has become a popular and preferred cost-effective remedy. Phytoremediation has received wide public acclaim. Marsh macrophytes play a role in petroleum hydrocarbon cleanup. The choice among technologies depends on site conditions, remediation timeline, regulatory requirements, and cost considerations.

## Feasibility Assessment

### Bioremediation Potential

Feasibility studies determine whether indigenous or introduced fungi can degrade target contaminants at the site. Prior exposure of an ecosystem to contamination has been correlated with hydrocarbon degraders. Monitoring fungal populations during remediation discerns fungal activity in contaminant degradation.

### Modeling and Prediction

Modeling technologies to achieve contaminant removal efficiencies is an important component of site assessment. Kinetic models can predict the fate of specific hydrocarbon constituents. Mathematical equations describe transformation processes and degradation rates. These models inform remediation design and timeline predictions.

### Cost-Benefit Analysis

The cost of mycoremediation varies by approach. A. niger-based heavy metal leaching costs approximately $5 per cubic meter. White-rot fungi cultivation on waste substrates (wood chips, sawdust) reduces costs. The projections indicate more cost-effective bioremediation than current competitive technologies. Implementation costs must be weighed against remediation timeline and regulatory compliance requirements.

## Monitoring During Remediation

### Performance Metrics

Key monitoring parameters include contaminant concentration trends, fungal population dynamics, CO2 evolution rates, enzyme activity levels, and soil physicochemical parameters. The ratios of hydrocarbon compounds within complex mixtures serve as degradation indicators.

### Adaptive Management

Site conditions may require adjustment of remediation strategies. Nutrient addition, pH modification, moisture management, and re-inoculation may be needed. The ability to adapt treatment based on monitoring data improves outcomes.
