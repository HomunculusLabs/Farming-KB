# EMCDDA Internet Shop Pricing Snapshot for Hallucinogenic Mushrooms 2006

In January 2006, the EMCDDA conducted a systematic snapshot of online shops
selling hallucinogenic mushroom products across Europe. Using Google and
AltaVista search engines with English language keywords including "magic
mushrooms shop," "buy magic mushrooms," "psilocybe cubensis shop," and "buy
psilocybe cubensis," researchers analyzed the first 300 hits for each
search engine and keyword combination to produce a comprehensive market
overview.

## Search Results and Shop Distribution

The four keyword searches returned a combined total of 39 unique online
shops selling hallucinogenic mushroom products. Analysis of contact
addresses revealed a striking geographic concentration: 82% of identified
shops were based in the Netherlands. The remaining 18% included four
Austrian online shops selling growkits and spore prints, two German sites,
and a UK site linked to a Dutch online shop. A Polish site was also
encountered that primarily sold cannabis products and noted hallucinogenic
mushrooms as temporarily unavailable.

Website statistics from one Dutch online shop showed access patterns
between May 2004 and February 2006: 73% of visitors came from servers
based in the Netherlands, followed by Belgium at 14%, Germany at 2%, and
France at 2%. This distribution suggests the Netherlands served as the
primary hub for the European online hallucinogenic mushroom market.

## Product Pricing Ranges

Online shops offered a variety of hallucinogenic mushroom products
including fresh mushrooms, spore prints, spawnbags, and growkits. The
following price ranges were recorded in euros:

| Product | Quantity | Price Range |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| Fresh Psilocybe cubensis (Mexican) | 30 gr | 8.50 - 45.28 |
| Fresh Copelandia cyanescens | 10 gr | 13.00 - 17.50 |
| Psilocybe mexicana (Philosopher stones/truffles) | 15 gr | 10.00 - 17.50 |
| Spawnbag (Psilocybe azurescens) | 1 unit | 24.00 - 25.00 |
| Growkits | 1 unit | 25.00 - 100.00 |
| Spore prints | 1 print or syringe | 9.00 - 28.00 |

Prices for growkits and spore prints varied according to the hallucinogenic
mushroom species for which they were available. The wide price variation for
fresh cubensis (8.50 to 45.28 euros for 30 grams) likely reflected
differences in brand positioning, product quality claims, and packaging.

## Free Spore Ring Europe

Users could also obtain various strains including Psilocybe cubensis and
Panaeolus cyanescens for a price of 1 euro per print from a non profit
organization called the Free Spore Ring Europe (fsre.org). Instructions on
cultivating mushrooms using a spore print and substrate were easily found on
the internet, lowering the barrier to home cultivation significantly.

## International Shipping and Language Adaptation

The majority of online shops offered international shipping. However, many
sites did not ship to countries where sales were prohibited and advised
customers to verify the legal status of products before ordering. Many
sites featured different language versions, primarily English, French, and
German, to attract foreign customers across the European Union.

## Post Prohibition Market Shifts

Following the UK ban in July 2005, internet sites in the UK stopped selling
hallucinogenic mushrooms. One site (allsalvia.co.uk) claimed to be "sold out"
of growkits and posted a notice that fresh mushrooms, truffles, and growkits
were about to become Class A controlled drugs. The Irish online headshop THC
similarly noted that mushroom products were no longer available after
becoming illegal on January 31, 2006.

UK retailers pivoted to legal alternatives. EveryOneDoesIt.com and
Allsalvia.co.uk began selling Amanita muscaria (fly agaric) and Amanita
pantherina (panther cap). Another product increasingly marketed as a legal
alternative was Purple Ohms capsules, whose main ingredient was Argyreia
nervosa (Hawaiian Baby Woodrose) containing Lysergic Acid Amide (LSA).

## Retailer Information Quality

In a snapshot of 21 online shops, the majority warned against combining
hallucinogenic mushrooms with medication, alcohol, or stimulant drugs. Only
two thirds recommended against use when the user suffered from depression
or psychosis. About half provided dosage information and safe use practices
such as consuming on an empty stomach, drinking liquids during the trip, and
consuming in a quiet safe environment.

Most sites provided information on sought after effects but few described
how to enhance them. The vast majority excluded information on possible
negative side effects. Only a minority explicitly mentioned nausea and
sickness. This pattern showed that retailer information was biased toward
positive effects, with considerable variation in the extent and quality of
risk communication.
