# Keyline Design
#permaculture #earthworks #water #keyline #irrigation

Updated: 2026-04-18

> From: Bill Mollison, *Permaculture: A Designers Manual* (1988), Chapter 7, Section 7.3.

## Overview

P.A. Yeomans' Keyline system, first published in 1954, provides drought-proofing for farms with very low maintenance and operating costs. His was the first book in English on total water design for foothill farms, access, tree belts, soil creation, low tillage, and creative water storage. *Water for Every Farm/The Keyline Plan* (1981) remains the pioneering modern text on landscape design for water conservation and gravity-fed flow irrigation.

## The Keypoint Concept

The keypoint is the point in a primary valley where the slope changes from convex (steeper near the ridge) to concave (flatter near the valley floor). This place can be judged by eye, and a descending contour will then pick up all other keypoints on the main valley. The keyline is the line linking keypoints across the landscape.

## Landscape Zones

The landscape can be seen (as though sliced into layers through contours) as a set of zones:
- **Ridges and Skyline**: catchment area where most water falls
- **Wiper Slopes**: steeper slopes (12-20 degrees)
- **Keypoint**: stores water at 4-12 degrees slope
- **Bottom Lands**: 0-4 degrees -- revitalisation of water, biological storage, and intercept

## Keyline Strategies

### Water Conservation
There are two basic strategies of water conservation in run-off areas:
1. The diversion of surface water to impoundments (dams, tanks) for later use
2. The storage of water in soils

Both result in a recharge of groundwater. The [[permaculture-designers-manual-dam-construction]] is placed at the keypoint of each primary valley for maximum storage efficiency.

### Gravity-Flow Irrigation
The keyline system uses gravity-fed flow from high dams to irrigate lower slopes. A large earth dam feeding irrigation canals in the keyline system can be fitted with a base pipe. Smaller dams use a siphon over the dam wall to gardens and houses.

### Tree Belts and Access
The system also involves patterning, tree planting, soil treatment, and fencing alignment. Access roads follow keyline contours. Tree belts are placed on contour ridges to assist in water distribution and soil conditioning.

## Soil Conditioning

The keyline plough (a chisel plough following the keyline pattern) is used to:
- Aerate compacted soils without inversion
- Increase water infiltration into subsoils
- Break up hardpans
- Enable tree roots to penetrate deeper
- Increase soil carbon through root decomposition

This low-tillage approach preserves soil structure while dramatically improving water retention. The plough follows contour lines descending from the keypoint, gently steering water into the soil profile rather than allowing rapid runoff.

## Keypoint Dams in Series

A second or third series of dams can be run below the primary series, and the spillway of the last dam in a series can be returned "upstream" to meet the main valley, effectively spilling surplus to streams. This cascade system maximises water storage while distributing it across the landscape.

## Contour Dams on Keyline

Contour dam walls can be built on contour wherever the slope is 8% or less. Contours and dam walls can be concave or convex to the fall line across the slope. Used for irrigation, aquaculture, or flood-flow basins in semi-arid areas.

## Integration with Permaculture

The keyline system is fundamental to permaculture earthworks design because it:
- Provides the framework for total water management on foothill properties
- Integrates water storage, tree planting, access roads, and fencing
- Enables gravity-fed irrigation reducing energy inputs
- Creates drought-resilient farms with low operating costs
- Works with natural landscape patterns rather than against them

## Property Layout

On Yeomans' former property "Yobarnie" (about 307 ha / 758 acres), after 17 years of keyline development, the system demonstrated the effectiveness of integrated water design including:
- Primary valleys and primary ridges with planned road systems
- Multiple dam sites connected by gravity-flow channels
- Tree belts on ridge lines and contour banks
- Irrigation layouts for flood or sheet irrigation

## Appropriate and Inappropriate Uses

As with all technologies, earthworks have quite specifically appropriate and inappropriate uses. The main productive earthwork features created in the keyline system are:
- Dams and tanks (storages)
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-swale-design-detailed]] (absorption beds)
- Diversion systems or channels
- Irrigation layouts, particularly those for flood or sheet irrigation
