## Raised Bed Gardening Guide

Raised beds elevate the growing surface above ground level, improving drainage, reducing soil compaction, extending [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]], and providing better control over soil quality. They are the foundation of most home vegetable gardens.

## Benefits

- **Improved drainage** — soil above grade drains faster than in-ground beds.
- **Reduced compaction** — you never walk on the soil; roots grow freely.
- **Earlier planting** — raised soil warms faster in spring (2-4 weeks ahead of ground soil).
- **Custom soil** — fill with optimal mix regardless of native soil quality.
- **Higher yields** — intensive spacing in loose soil produces more per square foot.
- **Easier access** — less bending, better for gardeners with mobility limitations.
- **Fewer weeds** — dense planting shades out weeds; clean soil mix has fewer weed seeds.

## Standard Dimensions

### Width
- 4 feet wide is ideal — you can reach the center from both sides without stepping in.
- 3 feet for beds against a wall or fence (one-sided access).
- Never wider than 5 feet unless you add internal stepping stones.

### Length
- Any length. Common: 8, 10, 12, or 16 feet.
- Keep under 20 feet for structural integrity (bowing).

### Height
- 6-8 inches: minimum for most vegetables; good over decent native soil.
- 10-12 inches: ideal for most crops, allows deep root growth.
- 18-24 inches: best for root vegetables (carrots, parsnips, potatoes), accessible gardening, or building over poor soil/pavement.
- Deeper is NOT always better — filling very deep beds is expensive.

### Spacing Between Beds
- 24-36 inches between beds for wheelbarrow and walking access.
- 18 inches minimum for tight spaces.

## Mel's Mix (Square Foot Garden Soil)

Developed by Mel Bartholomew for [[comparison-keyhole-garden-vs-square-foot-gardening]], this soil mix works well in any raised bed:

**1/3 blended compost** (mix 4-5 types: leaf, manure, mushroom, kitchen, worm castings)
**1/3 peat moss** (or coco coir for sustainability)
**1/3 vermiculite** (coarse grade, for water retention and aeration)

For a 4x8 foot bed, 10 inches deep (~27 cubic feet):
- 9 cu ft compost (about 9-10 bags of 1 cu ft each)
- 9 cu ft peat moss or coco coir
- 9 cu ft vermiculite

### Alternative Soil Mix
If vermiculite is expensive or unavailable:
- 50% quality topsoil
- 30% compost
- 20% aeration (perlite, pumice, or rice hulls)

## Construction Materials

### Wood
- **Cedar** — naturally rot-resistant, lasts 10-15 years. Expensive but premium.
- **Redwood** — excellent rot resistance, long-lasting. Very expensive.
- **Pine (untreated)** — affordable, lasts 3-5 years. Replace when rotting.
- **Douglas fir** — moderate rot resistance, 5-7 years.
- **Black locust** — extremely durable, 20+ years. Hard to find and work.

### Treated Lumber
- Modern ACQ (alkaline copper quaternary) treated wood is considered safe for vegetable beds by most extension services.
- Line the inside with plastic (HDPE pond liner) if concerned about copper leaching.
- Avoid older CCA (chromated copper arsenate) lumber — contains arsenic.

### Other Materials
- **Corrugated metal** (galvanized steel) — modern look, very durable, conducts heat (warms soil fast).
- **Concrete blocks (cinder blocks)** — inexpensive, durable, the holes can be planted with herbs or flowers.
- **Stone** — beautiful, permanent, expensive, labor-intensive.
- **Composite lumber** — recycled plastic/wood blend, very durable, moderate cost.

### Bed Liner
- Optional but recommended over grass or weeds.
- Use cardboard (over sod) or landscape fabric (porous, not plastic).
- Avoid solid plastic sheeting — prevents drainage and soil biology.

## Irrigation

### Drip Irrigation (Recommended)
- 1/2 inch main line with 1/4 inch drip line with emitters.
- One emitter per plant, or soaker hose snaked through bed.
- Connect to a timer for consistent watering.
- Reduces disease by keeping foliage dry.
- Uses 50-70% less water than overhead watering.

### Soaker Hoses
- Lay on soil surface, cover with mulch.
- Low pressure, slow seepage along entire length.
- Replace every 3-5 years (clog and degrade).

### Hand Watering
- Fine for small gardens but labor-intensive.
- Water at soil level, not on foliage.
- Deep, infrequent watering is better than frequent shallow watering.

## Seasonal Care

- **Spring**: Top-dress with 1-2 inches of compost. Refresh mulch. Plant cool-season crops first.
- **Summer**: Maintain mulch layer. Monitor irrigation. Side-dress heavy feeders.
- **Fall**: Plant cover crops or garlic. Add fallen leaves as mulch. Clean up diseased plant material.
- **Winter**: Leave beds covered with mulch or cover crop. Avoid bare soil over winter.
