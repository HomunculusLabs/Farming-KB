# Mycorrhizal Fungi Biome Surveys

Updated: 2026-04-18

Mycorrhizal fungi surveys across different biomes reveal the fundamental importance of plant-fungal symbioses in ecosystem structure and function, as documented in fungal biodiversity literature.

## Mycorrhizal Types Across Biomes

### Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF)

The most widespread mycorrhizal type, found in:

- **Tropical forests** — extremely high AMF diversity; roots of most canopy trees are colonized
- **Temperate grasslands** — AMF dominate; diversity correlates with plant species richness
- **Agricultural fields** — often reduced diversity due to tillage and fertilization
- **Arid and semi-arid lands** — AMF critical for plant water and nutrient uptake

AMF diversity surveys have identified hundreds of species in the order Glomerales, with many more awaiting description.

### Ectomycorrhizal Fungi (EMF)

Primarily associated with forest trees in temperate and boreal biomes:

- **Boreal forests** — EMF dominate; key genera include *Suillus*, *Lactarius*, *Russula*, *Cortinarius*
- **Temperate deciduous forests** — mixed AMF and EMF systems; EMF on oaks, beeches, pines
- **Mediterranean woodlands** — EMF diversity highest under oaks; many endemic species
- **Tropical montane forests** — EMF occur on Dipterocarpaceae and Fagaceae

EMF diversity surveys show strong host specificity and seasonal patterns in fruiting.

### Ericoid Mycorrhizal Fungi

Found in heathlands, boreal forests, and alpine meadows:

- Associated with Ericaceae (heaths, blueberries, cranberries)
- Enable plants to access organic nitrogen in acidic, nutrient-poor soils
- Survey data show distinct communities in different heathland types

## Survey Methodology

Mycorrhizal surveys employ multiple complementary methods:

- **Fruiting body surveys** — record macrofungal fruiting over seasons; biased toward visible species
- **Root tip analysis** — direct examination of colonized root tips under microscope
- **Molecular identification** — DNA extraction from root tips or soil; detects non-fruiting species
- **Spore extraction** — soil washing and spore counting for AMF
- **Trap plant cultivation** — grow bait plants in soil samples to capture AMF

## Biome-Specific Patterns

### Tropical vs. Temperate Diversity

Tropical forests show the highest overall mycorrhizal diversity, but the type composition shifts. Tropical lowland forests are dominated by AMF, while tropical montane and temperate forests show increasing EMF importance.

### Elevation Gradients

Elevation affects mycorrhizal community composition:

- AMF diversity generally decreases with elevation
- EMF diversity may peak at mid-elevations
- Ericoid mycorrhizae increase in importance at high elevations

### Successional Changes

Mycorrhizal communities shift during ecosystem succession:

- Early successional sites often show lower mycorrhizal diversity
- Late successional forests accumulate diverse mycorrhizal networks
- Disturbance reduces mycorrhizal diversity, with recovery taking decades
