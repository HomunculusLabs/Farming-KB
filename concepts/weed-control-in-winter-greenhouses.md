# Weed Control in Winter Greenhouses

Weed management in intensive winter greenhouse production requires a proactive, multi-strategy approach. Because crops are planted at very close spacing on fertile greenhouse soil, even small weeds—especially fast-growing species like chickweed—can dramatically slow harvesting. The yield gains from close spacing justify significant investment in weed prevention and control.

## Prevention First

The primary weed control strategy is preventing weeds from ever going to seed:

- **Hand-weed whenever necessary** in both fields and greenhouses.
- **Weed green manures** before they set seed, or till them under and restart if weed pressure is too high.
- Research shows that a serious weed-seed load in soil can be greatly reduced over **three to five years** if no new seeds are introduced.
- Despite best efforts, weeds will always appear and must be managed.

## Preemergence Flaming

Flaming is the most effective technique for dispatching weeds after germination but before crop emergence. The crop seeds remain insulated underground while propane flame kills weed seedlings at the surface.

### How Flaming Works

- Flaming involves **wilting, not burning** the weeds. Exposure to **160°F (71°C) for one second** is sufficient to melt protein cells in small weeds under 1 inch tall.
- Weeds appear normal for an hour or two after flaming, then wilt and die.
- Burning (rather than wilting) uses three times as much propane and is wasteful.

### Timing

Two methods determine the ideal flaming moment:

1. **Glass pane method**: Place square-foot panes of glass about an inch off the ground in a wooden frame after sowing. The glass warms the soil and speeds crop germination. Flame immediately upon seeing the first crop seed germinate under the glass. Works well in early spring but can overheat in summer.

2. **Day-count method**: Keep careful records of days-to-germination for each crop at each season. Leave a one- or two-day safety margin, then flame at the calculated time. This is simpler and works year-round.

### Best Applications

Flaming is most effective for slow-germinating crops like carrots, onions, and parsley, which give weeds time to germinate first. It is difficult to use with quick-germinating salads like lettuce and arugula because there is little window between weed and crop emergence.

### Chickweed: The Primary Winter Greenhouse Weed

Chickweed (*[[stellaria-media]]*) is the most troublesome weed in winter greenhouses because:

- It thrives on fertile soils.
- It can germinate and set seed at surprisingly low temperatures.
- Coleman has observed chickweed in full bloom in unheated greenhouses on January 15.
- Its rapid growth can overwhelm baby-leaf salad crops.

### Flamer Design Considerations

Most commercially available hand flamers have oversized burners that waste propane. An efficient design should feature:

- Properly sized nozzles matched to the area being covered.
- A hood over the flame to contain heat and improve efficiency.
- A roller behind to maintain optimal height above the soil and reduce arm fatigue.
- A 15-inch-wide model is effective on a 30-inch bed with one pass down and back.

Excessively fast propane burn rates cause the tank to freeze (evaporation is cooling), inhibiting flow. Large open flames are also hazardous near greenhouse plastic.

## Cultivating with Specialized Hoes

Once weeds emerge within the crop, control follows the principle: **do it early and often**.

### The Collinear Hoe and Wire Weeder

Coleman designed two hoe models for precise, efficient weeding:

- **[[collinear-hoe]]**: Blade aligned with the handle for accurate cutting parallel to the soil surface.
- **Wire weeder**: A thin wire blade that slices just below the soil surface.

Both are designed to be used **like razors rather than axes**—sharpened blades make shallow, rapid cuts that sever weeds at the soil surface. The cut residues wilt quickly afterward.

### Cultivation Technique for Small Weeds

1. Work on the morning of a sunny day for best wilt results.
2. Stand upright in the path between beds, holding the hoe handle with thumbs pointing up like gripping a broom.
3. Always work **forward toward uncut weeds**.
4. Position the blade 3–6 inches away, place it on the soil, and shave just under the surface back toward you.
5. Move the blade forward another 3 inches and repeat.
6. Each stroke takes about one second; short strokes enable accuracy close to crop stems.

### Rapid Cultivation for Tiny Weeds

When weeds are very small on a sunny day, a faster technique works:

- Walk down the path holding the collinear hoe or wire weeder between rows, just barely under the soil surface.
- Maintain consistent depth (never more than ¼ inch).
- Adjust the forward blade angle to compensate for slight row spacing discrepancies.
- All tiny weeds are cut off or tipped out to wilt on the surface.

### Key Principles

- **Shallow hoeing** minimizes damage to crop roots.
- **Adjust technique** to weed size, type, and weather conditions.
- **If weeds re-root after rain**, cultivate again immediately.
- **Teach employees** proper technique—efficient cultivation is a learned skill.
- **Attention to detail** is the major secret to success in weed control and all farming endeavors.
