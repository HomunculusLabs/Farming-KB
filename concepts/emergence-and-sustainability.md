# Emergence and Sustainability

Emergence is a key concept from systems theory describing how self-organisation within complex systems results in activity, structures, and behaviors that clearly arise from within the system but have the effect of either transforming it or producing something completely new. This concept bridges ecological evolution, technological disruption, and cultural transformation, with profound implications for how societies navigate sustainability during energy descent.

## The Nature of Emergence

Emergence suggests that complex systems generate novel properties that cannot be predicted from the characteristics of their individual components. This is analogous to the Reorganisation phase of Holling's model of ecosystem succession, or the creation of new species in biological evolution.

An obvious example is the global network of personal computers which increasingly dominates world communication and economy; it arose out of the information technology industry quite unpredicted by mainstream players or commentators. Most experts instead predicted a world controlled through a hierarchical structure from a few centrally located mainframe computers. The corporations and governments were strongly committed to this centralized technology, yet had to adapt rapidly to the anarchic emergence of the networked world of personal computers and the Internet.

The phrase "having a life of its own" captures the essence of emergence. Many proponents of networked systems see global computer networks as the beginning of a global human consciousness, a hive mind (as in beehives), with either utopian or sinister connotations.

## Emergent Possibilities in Human Systems

Emergent possibilities are all around us as a natural outcome of the extraordinarily fast co-evolutionary growth of human systems based on fossil fuels. Many of them, like information technology and genetic engineering, show the potential to overturn many assumptions about the trajectory of civilization.

As evidence mounts for the climax and rapid energy descent of industrial civilization, the particular and local nature of that future is filled with uncertainty and emergent possibilities. The choices taken in responding to large-scale forces — fight to the death, uneasy compromise, going with the flow — may or may not make any difference.

Even the grand assumption that the future of descending energy is inevitable may be mistaken. There is the possibility that some combination of computer networks, human culture, and biotechnology will create a runaway world of accelerating energetic and informational growth (at least for another few hundred years).

## Genetic Engineering as Evolutionary Emergence

Of all the emergent factors generated by human systems, genetic engineering of novel lifeforms is perhaps most interesting because it feeds back into nature's well-established biological foundations for evolutionary change. Unlike information technology, it does not depend on complex infrastructure and energy flows to have impact once released into nature.

Because gene transfer between bacteria in nature has been identified as common, the idea that the genetics of novel lifeforms will stay contained in GMOs released on farms, or even used as medicines, is untenable. New microbes will spread to the limits set by ecological constraints, not those set by human management or edict, much faster than the spread of introduced plants and animals.

### Two Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Genetic Engineering Succeeds**
If genetic engineering manages to mine the world's genetic diversity to generate real wealth rather than more economic illusions, the Maximum Power Law would predict a rapid economic and technological reorganisation around genetic engineering (probably combined with computer technology) to create a massive acceleration in change. This would include the transformation of humanity into some new recombinant species.

**Scenario 2: Genetic Engineering Fails**
If, like so many favoured and pampered introduced species, genetic engineering turns out to be a failure, then the resources devoted to it will have been just another lost opportunity for creative and graceful adaptation to energy descent. The vast and pervasive nature of genetic promiscuity may mean genetically engineered organisms are lost in the sea of much more robust genetics responding to real environmental factors, rather than the cocoon of human illusions created in the laboratory and the boardroom.

The grand illusion on which virtually all genetic engineering is founded is the faith in an ability to manipulate the environment using high energy and technology. Whether the biotechnology industries can continue to attract massive investment capital once the high cost of energy becomes entrenched is uncertain.

## Gender Balance and Sustainability

The balance and quality of change may be one of the most important characteristics of a sustainable culture. Industrial culture shows a bias toward episodic change, while sustainable culture is biased toward rhythmic change. The classical view of history as a series of episodic crises and conflicts, with great men as the leading actors, can be seen as an elitist and patriarchal view that ignores the less dramatic, rhythmic, cyclical nature of ordinary life — the greater part of human experience across time and space.

### Working with Rhythmic Cycles

Environmental concepts, including Permaculture, emphasise working with the rhythmic cycles of change in nature, rather than excessive reliance on the episodic intervention that kicks the system into some hopefully preferable state. This view of nature is more in tune with feminine rather than masculine culture.

The pulsing ecosystem model demonstrates that in any system, a small amount of episodic, even chaotic, change goes a long way. Any system that persists is dominated by small-scale rhythmic patterns that maintain and conserve a larger stability. Episodic change is a two-edged sword: it can lead either to a spiral of degradation, or to novel and often unintended transformative change.

Rather than simply rejecting episodic change as destructive, the pulsing model shows how it can be used creatively rather than being captive to its dynamics.

### The Dominance of Domestic Rhythms

The patterns of traditional life — focused on the home and a domestic connection to nature, the cycles of the seasons, and even the mundane aspects of childcare and education, housework and building maintenance, plant and animal husbandry, community support and maintenance — must dominate any notions of sustainable culture.

Ivan Illich argued that industrialisation has transformed patriarchy into a genderless culture that has absorbed both men and women but curiously leaves men structurally dominant. Despite the achievements of feminism, modern industrial culture has condensed around a core of hard-to-budge ways of thinking, doing, and being, which most men and women take for granted as normal but which are, in essence, masculine in origin.

One of the most important of these is the bias toward seeing all systems as passive and inactive until a powerful actor or force pushes the system somewhere. This assumption blinds modern culture to the self-organising, emergent properties of complex systems.

### Ambiguous Complementarity

Society eventually evolves a new structure of "ambiguous complementarity" between the genders, reflecting a fundamental energetic efficiency for organisation of households. Before this is likely in any enduring form, restoring the asymmetrical balances explored in permaculture principles is a task for all people.

The Indian eco-feminist Vandana Shiva identified the cultures of women, indigenous, and peasant peoples as having a common invisibility that is consumed by the dominant culture. The progressive rebalancing of feminine and masculine characteristics within a culture of sustainability will be different to that in any traditional sustainable culture. Much feminist orthodoxy, like that of the environment movement, unconsciously accepts the high-energy genderless society as some sort of natural enduring state within which new values must be developed.

## Long-Term Thinking and Large-Scale Cycles

Making sense of the current era demands moving past the limited notion of change driven by the inexorable arrow of progress to recognize that nature and humanity are governed by cycles, both large and small, nested within each other like Russian nesting dolls (matryoshka) and overlapping as depicted by Venn diagrams.

### The Pulsing Patterns of Life on Earth

The history of life on Earth includes vast periods of tens of millions of years when all land masses were worn down, shallow seas covered most of the surface, and the climate was almost uniformly mild and moderately wet. Biodiversity was low and ecosystems changed little. This stability has been punctuated by shorter periods of great change. The last 1.6 million years (the Quaternary) has been characterised by tectonic uplift, global ice caps, varied climates, ice ages and warmer interglacials, massive extinctions and prolific speciation.

Much of the wondrous biological and geographic diversity of the earth exists only in these periods of great change. It is unlikely that a species such as *Homo sapiens* could emerge, or even survive, the long, slow, stable periods that dominate geological history. On that scale, humanity is a short-lived microbial explosion.

Ice ages lasting hundreds of thousands of years represent the stable norm of the Quaternary, with interglacials lasting roughly ten thousand years representing the explosive pulse of life. Within this current benign interglacial climate, humanity has developed agriculture, civilization, and global industrial culture — all dependent on the fragile stability of the interglacial paradise.

### Breaking the Delusion of Linear Progress

We need to break out of the delusion of apparently linear acceleration of human material and numerical progress to a worldview in which everything is contained by cycles, waves, and pulses that flow between polarities of great stability and intense change, all nested one within another.

The ancient Hindu cosmology gives a breathtaking perspective on the cyclical nature of time and proportional pulsing balance between change and stability. A 60,000-year cultural cycle is dominated by a long matriarchy phase composed of three great enduring ages; it collapses in a vortex of chaos before the birth of the patriarchy phase (the Kali Yuga, or Age of Conflict), lasting only 6,000 years before another vortex of change leads to the next cycle.

### Permaculture as Dynamic Interplay

Permaculture is a dynamic interplay between two phases: on the one hand, sustaining life within the cycle of the seasons, and on the other, conceptual abstraction and emotional intensity of creativity and design. The relationship between these is like the pulsing relationship between stability and change. The steady, cyclical, and humble engagement with nature provides the sustenance for the spark of insight and integration (integrity), which, in turn, informs and transforms the practice. The first is harmonious and enduring; the second is episodic and powerful. The joyful asymmetric balance between the two expresses our humanity.
