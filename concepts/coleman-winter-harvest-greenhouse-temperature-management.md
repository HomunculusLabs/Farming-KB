# Coleman Winter Harvest Greenhouse Temperature Management

## Overview

Eliot Coleman's winter harvest system at Four Season Farm in coastal Maine (Zone 5) demonstrates that fresh vegetables can be produced year-round in cold climates without expensive heating systems. The system relies on a sophisticated understanding of how different levels of greenhouse protection interact with temperature, and how temperature management (or the deliberate absence of it) can be leveraged for economic and biological advantage. Uses minimal supplemental heat—typically propane set to keep the house just above the freezing point at night.

### British vs. American Conventions

In British usage, a "cold greenhouse" has a minimum of 32°F and a "cool greenhouse" has a minimum of 45°F. Coleman's definitions are different: his cold house is truly unheated, and his cool house is maintained at the lowest possible temperature above freezing to minimize fuel costs while gaining the benefits of even slight warming. (see [[coleman-greenhouse-summer-crops]]).

### The Double-Layer System

The combination of outer plastic covering and inner row cover is the technical foundation of the winter harvest concept:

- **Outer layer**: UV-resistant greenhouse plastic on a gothic-style hoop house, typically 30 feet wide by 96 feet long, oriented east-west
- **Inner layer**: Lightweight floating row cover supported by flat-topped wire wickets at approximately 12 inches (30 cm) above the soil, spaced every 4 feet along 30-inch-wide growing beds
- **Relative humidity**: The double coverage increases relative humidity in the protected zone, providing additional protection against freezing damage
- **Soil as heat storage**: No water, stone, or insulation-based heat storage systems are used; the soil itself serves as the thermal mass

## The Cool House with Minimal Heat

### Rationale

Coleman installed one heated greenhouse as a laboratory for exploring minimal-heat parameters, and because a greenhouse was needed as a covered area for washing and packing produce. The heater was originally installed to prevent the washing system from freezing.

### Temperature Setting

The thermostat is set just above 32°F (0°C)—the lowest temperature that still prevents freezing. This minimal intervention provides significant benefits:

- Two additional crops per winter compared to unheated houses (5-6 crops vs. 3-4 per year)
- Enables production of crops that cannot tolerate freezing, such as baby turnips and crisp radishes
- Allows early spring carrot harvest six weeks earlier (April 1 vs. May 5 in cold houses)

### Economics of Minimal Heat

The economic calculation is straightforward: when fuel prices are low enough that one additional crop can pay for the cost of the propane to fuel the heater, minimal heat becomes financially worthwhile. The key insight is that even a few degrees of warming at the coldest times can significantly accelerate plant growth and expand the range of marketable crops.

### Cool House Crop Calendar

The cool house extends the harvest season for a wide range of crops from October through May:
- Beet (Bull's Blood), carrot, celery, chard, claytonia
- Endive (Bianca Riccia), lettuce, mache, mizuna
- Green onion, pak choi, parsley, radish, sorrel
- Spinach, tatsoi, and turnip (Hakurei)

## The Summer Cooling Problem

### The Mobile Greenhouse Solution

One advantage of the mobile greenhouse is avoiding the expense of cooling the house when starting winter crops in August. Instead of starting winter crops inside a hot greenhouse:
- Winter crops are sown outdoors in the field over which the greenhouse will eventually move
- The greenhouse remains over heat-loving summer crops (tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, eggplants, sweet potatoes)
