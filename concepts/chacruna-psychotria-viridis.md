# Chacruna (Psychotria viridis)

Chacruna (*Psychotria viridis*) is a perennial shrub of the Rubiaceae
family found throughout the Amazon basin. It is the essential companion
plant to ayahuasca (*Banisteriopsis caapi*) in the preparation of the
ayahuasca brew, providing the psychoactive compound DMT
(N,N-dimethyltryptamine) that produces the visionary experiences central
to Amazonian plant medicine [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]].
It belongs to the coffee family (Rubiaceae) and shares the genus
*Psychotria* with approximately 2,000 species worldwide. The leaves are
opposite, elliptic to obovate, 5-15 cm long, with a glossy dark green
upper surface. Small greenish-white flowers are followed by dark purple
to black drupes. The plant is easily propagated from stem cuttings and
prefers partial shade and humid conditions typical of the Amazon
understory.

## Role in the Ayahuasca Brew

Chacruna is the DMT-containing admixture that transforms the ayahuasca
vine from a purgative medicine into a visionary one. In the traditional
preparation described by [[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]]. DMT is a potent serotonergic psychedelic that
binds to 5-HT2A receptors, producing vivid visual imagery, altered
sense of time and self, and emotional catharsis. When consumed orally,
DMT is normally rendered inactive by monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes
in the gut and liver. The beta-carboline alkaloids in B. caapi
(harmine, harmaline, harmalol) are reversible MAO-A inhibitors that
block this enzymatic breakdown, allowing DMT to reach the brain in
pharmacologically active concentrations.

This pharmacological synergy was discovered by Amazonian peoples
through millennia of empirical experimentation — an extraordinary
achievement of ethnopharmacology that was unknown to Western science
until the mid-20th century.
