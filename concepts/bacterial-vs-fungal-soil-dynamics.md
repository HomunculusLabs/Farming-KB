# Bacterial vs Fungal Soil Dynamics

Updated: 2026-04-18

The ratio of fungi to bacteria in soil -- the fungal-to-bacterial biomass ratio,
or F:B ratio -- is one of the most important indicators of soil health and a
key determinant of which plants will thrive. Research by Elaine Ingham and
others at Oregon State University demonstrated that the least disturbed soils
(those supporting old-growth timber) had far more fungi than bacteria, while
disturbed soils (such as rototilled ground) had far more bacteria than fungi.

## F:B Ratios by Plant Type

Agricultural soils typically have an F:B ratio of 1:1 or less, while forest
soils can have ten times or more fungi than bacteria. The ratio is closely tied
to the type of plant being grown and how long it lives.

### Vegetables and Annuals

Most vegetables and annuals prefer bacterially dominated soils because they
live for only a single season. Their litter is mostly cellulose, which bacteria
decompose efficiently. Specific F:B ratios:

- Carrots, lettuce, broccoli, and cole crops: F:B 0.3:1 to 0.8:1
- Tomatoes, corn, wheat: F:B 0.8:1 to 1:1
- Lawns: F:B 0.5:1 to 1:1

These plants generally prefer nitrogen in nitrate form, which is produced by
bacterial-dominated systems.

### Perennials and Grasses

Most perennials prefer fungally dominated soils. Prairie plants tend to fall
in the middle, preferring a balance between bacteria and fungi -- analogous to
lawn grass.

### Trees and Shrubs

Trees require significantly higher F:B ratios:

- Deciduous trees (maples, oaks, poplars): F:B 10:1 to 100:1
- Orchard trees: F:B 10:1 to 50:1
- Conifers: F:B 50:1 to 1000:1
- Riparian trees (alder, beech, aspen, cottonwood): F:B 5:1 to 100:1 when
  mature; prefer bacterially dominated soils when young

Forest soils have a [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] over 100 times that of bacteria. Shrubs
generally prefer higher fungal dominance than perennials. Rhododendrons, for
example, require very strong fungal dominance, while cotoneaster or lilac
require less.

## Why the Pattern Exists

The general rule: the longer a plant lives, the more it benefits from fungal
dominance. This is because:

1. Fungi are fragile organisms that take time to grow
2. [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]] need a live root to associate with; longer-lived roots
   support longer fungal relationships
3. Longer-lived plants produce litter with more lignin and cellulose, which are
   good fungal food sources
4. Bacteria numbers stay roughly the same across environments (100 million to
   1 billion per teaspoon); the F:B ratio changes primarily because fungal
   biomass increases, not because bacterial biomass decreases
