# Mushroom Immunomodulation Mechanisms

Mushroom-derived polysaccharides and proteoglycans act primarily as biological response modifiers (BRMs), enhancing the host's immune defence rather than directly attacking cancer cells. This immunomodulatory approach is the foundation of medicinal mushroom therapy in both traditional Chinese medicine and modern clinical oncology.

## Immune Cell Activation

### Macrophage Activation

Mushroom β-glucans are potent activators of macrophages, the body's first-line cellular defence:
- Enhanced phagocytic activity — macrophages more aggressively engulf pathogens and tumour cells
- Increased production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and nitric oxide (NO) — cytotoxic mediators
- Secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines: IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α
- Upregulation of MHC class II expression — improved antigen presentation
- Augmented tumoricidal activity maintained over extended periods

### Natural Killer (NK) Cells

NK cells are critical for immune surveillance against tumours and virus-infected cells. Mushroom polysaccharides enhance NK cell activity through:
- Direct activation via β-glucan receptor binding
- Indirect activation through macrophage-derived cytokines (IL-2, IFN-γ)
- Increased NK cell proliferation and cytotoxic granule release
- Enhanced antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC)

### T-Cell Modulation

Mushroom compounds influence both CD4+ helper and CD8+ cytotoxic T-cell populations:
- Promotion of T-helper cell differentiation (Th1 bias)
- Enhanced CD8+ cytotoxic T-lymphocyte (CTL) activity
- Increased production of T-cell-derived cytokines
- Restoration of T-cell function in immunocompromised states

### B-Cell and Humoral Immunity

- Stimulation of antibody production
- Enhancement of B-cell proliferation
- Activation of the alternative complement pathway

## Receptor Biology

### Dectin-1

The primary β-glucan receptor on immune cells. Dectin-1 is a C-type lectin receptor expressed on macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells. Binding of β-glucans to dectin-1 triggers:
- Syk kinase activation
- NF-κB nuclear translocation
- Pro-inflammatory gene transcription
- Reactive oxygen species production

### Complement Receptor 3 (CR3 / CD11b-CD18)

CR3 is a β₂ integrin that binds β-glucans. The lectin-like domain of CR3 recognizes β-glucan structures. When immune complexes opsonized with iC3b bind to CR3, co-engagement of the β-glucan binding site primes the receptor for cytotoxic activation. This dual-recognition mechanism allows mushroom β-glucans to "prime" neutrophils and macrophages to kill iC3b-opsonized tumour cells.

### Other Receptors

- **Toll-like receptors (TLR2, TLR4)** — some mushroom compounds activate TLR signalling
- **Scavenger receptors** — involved in polysaccharide uptake
- **Mannose receptor** — binds mannose-containing glycoproteins from mushrooms

## Cytokine Cascade

Mushroom polysaccharides trigger a cascade of cytokine production:

| Cytokine | Primary Source | Effect |
|----------|---------------|--------|
| IL-1β | Macrophages | Fever, T-cell activation, inflammation |
| IL-2 | T-cells | T-cell proliferation, NK activation |
| IL-6 | Macrophages, T-cells | B-cell differentiation, acute phase response |
| IL-12 | Macrophages, dendritic cells | Th1 differentiation, IFN-γ induction |
| TNF-α | Macrophages | Tumour necrosis, inflammation, apoptosis |
| IFN-γ | T-cells, NK cells | Macrophage activation, antiviral activity |
| G-CSF, GM-CSF | Macrophages, T-cells | White blood cell production |

## Antitumour Mechanisms

Mushroom immunomodulators combat cancer through multiple mechanisms:

1. **Host defence potentiation** — activate immune cells to recognise and destroy tumour cells
2. **Immunosurveillance enhancement** — improve detection of malignant cells
3. **Anti-angiogenesis** — some compounds inhibit tumour blood vessel formation
4. **Direct cytotoxicity** — recent evidence shows some compounds (e.g., [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-maitake]] D-fraction showing 81-91% reduction in hepatic metastases

## Oral Bioavailability

A critical question for dietary mushroom supplements. High molecular weight polysaccharides (>100 kDa) are not efficiently absorbed intact through the intestinal epithelium. However, evidence suggests:
- Intestinal macrophages and Peyer's patch immune cells may be directly activated
- Smaller polysaccharide fragments may cross the gut barrier
- Some proteoglycans (PSK, PSP) demonstrate measurable oral bioavailability
- Clinical efficacy of orally administered preparations has been documented
