# Mushroom Immunomodulation Mechanisms
Mushroom-derived polysaccharides and proteoglycans act primarily as
[[medicinal-mushrooms-biological-response-modifiers]] modifiers (BRMs), enhancing the host's immune
defence rather than directly attacking cancer cells. This
immunomodulatory approach is the foundation of [[medicinal-mushrooms]]
therapy in both traditional Chinese medicine and modern clinical
oncology.

## Immune Cell Activation

### Macrophage Activation

Mushroom beta-glucans are potent activators of macrophages, the body's
first-line cellular defence:

- Enhanced phagocytic activity — macrophages more aggressively engulf
  pathogens and tumour cells
- [[fukuoka-textdoc-increased-production-not-increased-yields]] of [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]] species (ROS) and nitric
  oxide (NO) — cytotoxic mediators
- Secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines: IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha
- Upregulation of MHC class II expression — improved antigen
  presentation
- Augmented tumoricidal activity maintained over extended periods

## Receptor Biology

### Dectin-1

The primary beta-glucan receptor on immune cells. Dectin-1 is a C-type
lectin receptor expressed on macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic
cells. Binding of beta-glucans to dectin-1 triggers:

- Syk kinase activation
- NF-kappaB nuclear translocation
- Pro-inflammatory gene transcription
- Reactive oxygen species production

### Complement Receptor 3 (CR3 / CD11b-CD18)

CR3 is a beta-2 integrin that binds beta-glucans. The lectin-like
domain of CR3 recognizes beta-glucan structures. When immune complexes
opsonized with iC3b bind to CR3, co-engagement of the beta-glucan
binding site primes the receptor for cytotoxic activation. This
dual-recognition mechanism allows mushroom beta-glucans to "prime"
neutrophils and macrophages to kill iC3b-opsonized tumour cells.

### Other Receptors

- **Toll-like receptors (TLR2, TLR4):** some [[growing-gourmet-medicinal-polysaccharides-mushroom-compounds]]
  activate TLR signalling pathways
- **Scavenger receptors:** involved in polysaccharide uptake by
  immune cells
- **Mannose receptor:** binds mannose-containing glycoproteins
  from mushrooms, facilitating recognition and internalization

## Cytokine Cascade

Mushroom polysaccharides trigger a cascade of cytokine production:
| Cytokine | Primary Source | Effect |
|----------|---------------|--------|
| IL-1beta | Macrophages | Fever, T-cell activation, inflammation |
| IL-2 | T-cells | T-cell proliferation, NK activation |
| IL-6 | Macrophages, T-cells | B-cell differentiation, acute phase |
| IL-12 | Macrophages, DCs | Th1 differentiation, IFN-gamma |
| TNF-alpha | Macrophages | Tumour necrosis, inflammation |
| IFN-gamma | T-cells, NK cells | Macrophage activation, antiviral |
| G-CSF, GM-CSF | Macrophages, T-cells | White blood cell production |

## Antitumour Mechanisms

Mushroom immunomodulators combat cancer through multiple mechanisms:

1. **Host defence potentiation** — activate immune cells to recognise
   and destroy tumour cells
2. **Immunosurveillance enhancement** — improve detection of malignant
   cells by the immune system
3. **Anti-angiogenesis** — some compounds inhibit tumour blood vessel
   formation, starving tumours of nutrients
