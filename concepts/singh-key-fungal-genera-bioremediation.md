# Singh Key Fungal Genera in Bioremediation
#mycoremediation #aspergillus #penicillium #trichoderma #ganoderma #pleurotus #phanerochaete #bjerkandera
Updated: 2026-04-18
Source: [[harbhajan-singh]], Mycoremediation: [[fungal-bioremediation]] (2006), Chapters 1-12

## Overview

This page summarizes the key fungal genera documented in Singh's comprehensive mycoremediation reference, covering their specific roles in degrading different classes of contaminants. The book covers nearly 2000 references spanning industrial wastewaters, petroleum hydrocarbons, PCBs, pesticides, phenols, PAHs, dyes, and heavy metals. See also [[fungal-ecology-mycoremediation]], [[singh-ligninolytic-enzymes-fungal-bioremediation]], [[brown-rot-and-white-rot-fungi-in-mycoremediation]].

## [[phanerochaete-chrysosporium]] -- The Model White-Rot Fungus

The most extensively studied fungus in bioremediation. Discovered as the source of lignin peroxidase (LiP) by Glenn et al. (1983) and Tien and Kirk (1983).

### Enzyme System
- Produces LiP, MnP, H2O2-generating oxidases
- 10+ LiP isozyme genes (lipA through lipJ)
- 3 MnP isozyme genes coordinately regulated
- Enzyme production favored by nitrogen limitation
- Optimal growth temperature: 30C; sporulates easily

### Contaminant Degradation
- PAHs: degraded all compounds in creosote mixtures; 94% benzo[a]pyrene in 4 months; 90% three-ring, 80% four-ring PAHs in 2 months
- PCBs, dioxins, phenols, pulp mill effluents
- Attached to polyamide-6 fibers; reduced 50% polymer molar mass in 3 months
- Synergistic with indigenous soil microorganisms for PAH oxidation (43% enhancement)
- 75% increase in degradation rate at 30C vs 20C

### Limitations
- Inhibited by nitrogen above certain levels
- Unable to thrive in highly contaminated soils (remains metabolically inactive with other white-rot fungi)
- Limited PAH oxidation of high-molecular-weight compounds without surfactant

## [[trametes-versicolor]]

### Enzyme System
- Produces LiP, MnP, and laccase
- Simultaneous (nonselective) rot type
- Four LiP gene clones characterized (LPG1, LPGII, VLGI, etc.)
- Laccase + HBT mediator: up to 100% degradation of multiple PAHs

### Contaminant Degradation
- Highest PAH mineralization in straw cultures: 34.1% pyrene
- 15.5% phenanthrene mineralization on wheat straw
- Laccase-catalyzed: 91-100% PAH degradation with mediator (vs 3-37% without)
- Effective dye decolorization (most extensively studied)
- Produces oxalate crystals on metal-containing substrates
- Key species for pulp and paper mill effluent treatment

### Advantages
- Widely distributed and easily cultivated
- Robust enzyme production
- Effective in both liquid and solid-state fermentation

## Pleurotus Species (Oyster Mushrooms)

### Enzyme System
- MnP, laccase, and hybrid MnP/LiP forms (P. eryngii)
- Unusual: MnP produced without Mn(II); Mn(II) addition inhibits MnP production
- Ligninolytic enzymes higher in straw than liquid culture

### Species Documented
- P. ostreatus: extensive PAH degradation in creosote-contaminated soils; humification of PAHs; kills indigenous soil bacteria
- P. eryngii: laccase + HBT achieves 70% benzo[a]pyrene degradation; hybrid MnP/LiP enzymes
- P. florida: 53% [14C]pyrene mineralization; can grow from straw into soil; degrades 40%+ wheat straw substrate
- P. sajor-caju: Cd(II) biosorption 90% (dead mycelia > live mycelia at 78%)

### Unique Properties
- Degrades xenobiotics even in nitrogen-rich soils (unlike P. chrysosporium)
- Suitable for spent mushroom compost as inoculum
- All four Pleurotus species showed low mineralization after 4 weeks on wheat straw (Rodriguez et al., 2004)

## Ganoderma Species

### G. lucidum (Reishi)
- Produces MnP in poplar wood media but NOT pine wood media
- Produces both types of wood decay (simultaneous and selective)
- Important medicinal mushroom with remediation potential
- See also [[reishi-cultivation-methods]]

### G. applanatum
- Produces both nonselective and selective rot in same wood (with Heterobasidion annosum)
- MnP gene cloned and sequenced
- Contributes to wood decay in forest ecosystems

## Bjerkandera Species

### B. adusta
- Produces LiP (4 clones characterized)
- Hybrid MnP/LiP forms
- Degrades xenobiotics even in presence of nitrogen
- Effective in soil bioremediation studies

### B. sp. strain BOS55
- Hybrid MnP/LiP forms recognized (Mester and Field, 1998)
- 8.5% 14CO2 from [14C]benzo[a]pyrene after 15 days
- Indigenous microflora enhanced mineralization by 20-27%
- Significant mutagenic potential reduction (Salmonella assay)

### B. fumosa
- Produces high levels of oxalate crystals on metal-containing substrates (ZnO, Co3(PO4)2, CaCO3)
## Aspergillus Species

### A. niger
- Biosorption: >99% Cu(II) removal in chemostat; >50% Pb, Cd, Cu, Ni in polysulfone matrix
- Produces acids that solubilize metals: 18% Cu, 7% Ni, 4% Co from solutions
- Two mechanisms for Cr(VI) removal by dead biomass (direct reduction + three-step binding/reduction)
- Degrades concrete via acid production
- Grows upstream in continuous flow (rheotaxis)
- Used in production-scale fermentations

### A. oryzae
- Fractal analysis applied to colony growth studies
- Production-scale fermentation applications

### A. flavus
- Colonizes and degrades chitosan-graft polymethyl methacrylate (45% in 25 days)
- Enzymes change mechanical properties of disposable polyethylene bags
## Penicillium Species

### P. chrysogenum
- Biosorption: 87% Cu(II) removal in plug-flow continuous mode
- Mineralized 27.4% of 14C-labeled lignin from wheat straw in 4 weeks
- Secretes laccase; 7.9% DHP mineralization in 29 days
- Clump area correlates with broth rheology

### P. simplicissimum
- Degrades polyethylene (MW 4000-28000) -- bioremediation of plastics may be possible
- Zinc adsorption accompanied by citric acid production

### P. janthinellum
- Fungal-bacterial co-culture: 53% benzo[a]pyrene mineralization to 14CO2 in 100 days
- Extremely high Al resistance (F-13 strain)
- Methylates arsenic compounds to volatile forms
## Trichoderma Species

### T. atroviride
- Produces esterases for hydrocarbon degradation (with [[fusarium-oxysporum]])
- New mold that modifies petroleum hydrocarbons
