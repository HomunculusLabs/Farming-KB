## Translinguistic Objects and Glossolalia in Tryptamine Experience

## Overview

One of the most terence-mckenna, is the encounter with what he calls
"translinguistic objects"—phenomena that carry meaning and convey
information but cannot be adequately captured by any human language.
These objects exist at the boundary between perception and cognition,
presenting as "feeling-toned, meaning-toned, three-dimensional

## The Nature of Translinguistic Objects

McKenna describes these objects as emerging from the interaction
between the tryptamine-activated brain and whatever dimension of
being the tryptamines provide access to. They are perceived as
three-dimensional, evolving, self-transforming structures composed
of light and color. Unlike ordinary visual hallucinations, which are
typically distortions of familiar perceptual elements, translinguistic
objects possess an inherent quality of meaning that is immediately
felt rather than intellectually decoded.

The key phenomenological feature is that these objects communicate.
They are not merely seen but understood, though the understanding
occurs through a mode of cognition distinct from ordinary linguistic
processing. McKenna writes of encountering "a language of alien
meaning that is conveying alien information that cannot be Englished."
The information is present, palpable, and apparently coherent, but
exists in a cognitive format for which human language has no adequate
representational capacity.

## Cognitive Dissonance and Wonder

The encounter with translinguistic objects produces a characteristic
cognitive state that McKenna describes as productive dissonance.
"Being monkeys, when we encounter a translinguistic object, a kind
of cognitive dissonance is set up in our hindbrain. We try to pour
language over it and it sheds it like water off a duck"s back." The
brain"s default response—categorization through language—fails,
generating the states of wonder, astonishment, and awe that
characterize the tryptamine experience.

This "wow" or "flutter" builds as the cognitive machinery repeatedly
attempts and fails to process the translinguistic input. The entities
actively coach the voyager to manage this dissonance: "Don"t get a
loop of wonder going that quenches your ability to understand. Try
not to be so amazed. Try to focus and look at what we're doing."
This instruction suggests the translinguistic objects present
learnable content requiring focused, receptive attention rather than
passive wonder.

## Glossolalia and the Assembly Language of Mind

McKenna connects translinguistic objects to glossolalia (speaking in
tongues) as it manifests in the tryptamine state. Unlike classical
Pentecostal glossolalia, where practitioners are often unaware of
what they have said, tryptamine-induced glossolalia is fully conscious
and intentional. McKenna calls it "the assembly language that lies
behind language."

This concept draws on multiple traditions. mckenna-primal-ursprache-kabbalistic-zohar-and-white-goddess-glossolalia*, described a primal poetic language underlying all
human tongues. The Kabbalistic *Zohar* describes a primal *Ursprache*
(original language) preceding the confusion of Babel. McKenna suggests
tryptamines provide access to this pre-linguistic cognitive substrate—
a mode of meaning-making prior to the particular languages we have
learned.

The practical consequence is that under tryptamines, "one discovers
one can make the extra-dimensional objects." The capacity to produce
translinguistic objects through vocalization suggests these phenomena
are not purely receptive but involve a creative capacity of the mind
that ordinarily remains inaccessible.
