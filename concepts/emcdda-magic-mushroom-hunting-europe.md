# EMCDDA Report on Magic Mushroom Hunting in Europe

## Overview

Magic mushroom hunting — the practice of foraging for naturally growing
[[emcdda-eu-legislative-timeline-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-2001-2006]] — represents one of the oldest methods of obtaining
psychoactive fungi. The EMCDDA thematic paper on hallucinogenic mushrooms (2006)
documented evidence [[european-prevalence-and-patterns-of-magic-mushroom-use]] hunting across multiple European Union member
states, identifying it as a significant but poorly quantified component of
mushroom availability. Despite the emergence of cultivated [[duggar-wild-mushroom-markets-and-seasonal-harvesting]]
internet sales, wild foraging remained an important source of hallucinogenic
mushrooms in Europe, particularly in regions where psychoactive species grow
naturally.

## Definition and Context

Among users, "magic mushroom hunting" refers to the practice of seeking out,
identifying, and collecting naturally growing psilocybin-containing mushrooms
from wild habitats. This activity is distinct from cultivation ([[falconer-growing-mushrooms-in-sheds]]
from spores or mycelium on prepared substrates) and from purchasing pre-harvested
mushrooms through retail or online channels. Mushroom hunting requires knowledge
of [[psilocybin-mushroom-identification-guide]], seasonal timing, and suitable habitat types.

The EMCDDA noted that evidence on the extent of magic mushroom hunting across the
EU was sparse, making it difficult to quantify the practice or assess its
contribution to overall mushroom availability. However, qualitative reports from
multiple member states confirmed that the practice was occurring.

## European Evidence of Mushroom Hunting

### Poland

The EMCDDA received qualitative reports from Poland indicating that young persons
aged 15 to 19 were observed actively seeking magic mushrooms in the southern
regions of the country. Local community residents and shepherds reported observing
these foraging activities. The primary target species was identified as Psilocybe
semilanceata (the liberty cap), which is the most widespread and commonly sought
[[gartz-conocybe-cyanopus-rare-psychoactive-mushroom]] in temperate Europe. This information was based on research
by Szymański (unpublished at the time of the EMCDDA report).

### Ireland

Representatives of local communities in the Kilkenny region of Ireland reported
that magic mushrooms were "popular when in season," suggesting regular seasonal
consumption of wild-growing hallucinogenic mushrooms. This observation, attributed
to Finane (1999), indicates that mushroom hunting was an established practice in
at least some rural Irish communities, with consumption patterns tied to the
natural fruiting season of psychoactive species.

### Czech Republic

Consumption of locally grown hallucinogenic mushrooms was reported in the Czech
Republic, based on information from Kalina (2003). The Czech Republic has a
notable mycological tradition, and several psychoactive Psilocybe species are
known to occur in the region, including P. bohemica, P. serbica, P.
coprinifacies, and P. semilanceata. The country's strong general interest in
mycology likely facilitates knowledge transfer about psychoactive species.

## Online Resources Supporting Foraging

The EMCDDA documented the existence of websites that specifically supported magic
mushroom hunting by providing identification information and location data:

- **Shroommap** (shroommap.tk): A UK-based website where users shared
  information on known magic mushroom locations, effectively creating a
  crowdsourced foraging map.
- **The Shroomery** (shroomery.org): A comprehensive resource providing
  identification guides for wild hallucinogenic mushrooms.

These websites contributed to the democratization of foraging knowledge, enabling
users who might not have had access to traditional mycological expertise to
identify psychoactive species in the wild. However, the EMCDDA also implicitly
acknowledged the risks associated with amateur identification, as misidentification
of toxic mushroom species can have serious or fatal consequences.

## Target Species in Europe

The primary target species for magic mushroom hunters in Europe is Psilocybe
semilanceata, commonly known as the liberty cap. This species is the most widely
distributed psychoactive [[gartz-gymnopilus-purpuratus-south-american-mushroom-in-europe]], found across the continent from
Scandinavia to the Mediterranean. It typically fruits in autumn (September to
November) in grassland habitats, particularly pastures, meadows, and coastal
dunes enriched by livestock dung.
