# Hallucinogenic Mushroom Mental Health Risks

The EMCDDA 2006 thematic paper provides a comprehensive assessment
of the mental health risks associated with psilocybin-containing
mushrooms. While acute toxicity is low, psychological risks are
significant and warrant careful consideration.

## Acute Psychological Risks

### Bad Trips
The most common adverse psychological outcome is the "bad trip,"
characterized by intense anxiety, fear, confusion, and distress.
UK clubbing survey data documented:

- Nearly 25% of recent mushroom users reported experiencing a
  panic attack during use
- Younger users and first-time users were at higher risk
- The experience can be profoundly distressing but is typically
  self-limiting, resolving within hours

### Risk Factors for Bad Trips
- Inexperience with psychedelic substances
- High dose or unexpectedly potent material
- Anxious or unstable pre-existing mental state
- Unfamiliar or threatening setting
- Being alone or without a trusted guide
- Combining with alcohol or other substances
- Sleep deprivation before use

### Management of Acute Distress
Management of acute bad trips involves:

- Moving to a calm, quiet, comfortable environment
- Reassurance and verbal support from a trusted person
- Reducing sensory stimulation (dim lights, reduce noise)
- Reminding the user that the effects are temporary
- Seeking medical attention only if the user cannot be calmed
  or poses a danger
- Benzodiazepines may terminate the experience in clinical settings
