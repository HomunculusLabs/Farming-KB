# [[eu-hallucinogenic-mushroom-legislation-timeline]] Prevalence in Europe

## Data Challenges

Historically, drug surveys conducted in the EU among national general
and school populations collected and reported data on LSD consumption or
a general category of hallucinogenic drugs, [[integrate-rather-than-segregate]] specific data on
hallucinogenic mushroom use. Consequently there is a paucity of data on
mushroom use. Most school surveys and some general population surveys
now include questions about [[hallucinogenic-mushrooms-european-trends]].

## General Population Surveys (Ages 15-24)

Recent general population survey data from 12 EU Member States indicate
that lifetime prevalence of hallucinogenic mushroom use among young
people aged 15 to 24 ranges from less than 1% to 8%.

**Highest prevalence estimates:**
- Netherlands
- Czech Republic
- UK
- Germany
- Ireland

**Lowest prevalence estimates:**
- Lithuania
- Hungary
- France

A 2003 survey of 17-18 year old French residents reported higher
prevalence than earlier general population surveys: 4.3% lifetime, 2.9%
last year, and 1% last month prevalence. Prevalence of hallucinogenic
mushroom use is generally much lower than for cannabis (15-45% lifetime
in 11 EU states for ages 18-36). The proportion of current users among
those who have ever used is lower for mushrooms than for cannabis and
ecstasy, as the effects tend to limit the appeal of regular use.

## School Surveys (Ages 15-16)

ESPAD school survey data from 2003 across 22 EU Member States, Norway,
and four EU candidate countries (Croatia, Bulgaria, Turkey, Romania)
show that lifetime use among 15-16 year olds ranges from 0% to 8%.

**Highest prevalence estimates:**
- Czech Republic
- Netherlands
- France
- Belgium

Three out of 27 countries reported zero lifetime prevalence: Cyprus,
Finland, and Romania. Lifetime use of hallucinogenic mushrooms is equal
to, or higher than, lifetime use of ecstasy in nine countries.

## User Group Correlations

Analysis of ESPAD data from 11 EU Member States plus Norway and Croatia
demonstrates that school students aged 15-16 are much more likely to
have used hallucinogenic mushrooms if they have used LSD or other
hallucinogens, ecstasy, amphetamines, or cocaine than if they have used
only cannabis or legal substances. Following common gender distributions
for illegal drug use, male students generally have higher prevalence
rates than females.

## Clubbing Surveys

Targeted surveys in dance music settings in Belgium, France, Hungary,
Italy, the Netherlands, and UK show substantially higher prevalence
than general or school population surveys. Lifetime prevalence ranges
from 9% of clubbers in Budapest to 55% in France.

Within club sample populations, polydrug use tends to cluster in the
same individuals. In a 2000 survey of 760 clubbers from six south east
England nightclubs, overall lifetime prevalence was 24%. Among current
drug users in these samples, prevalence rose to 44%. In comparison,
lifetime prevalence among 16-19 year olds in the general population was
much lower at 11%.

## Trend Data

The only trend data available come from six consecutive annual Mixmag
readership surveys (2000-2005). Lifetime prevalence estimates increased
between 2003 and 2004. Last month prevalence increased significantly
from 2.4% in 2002 to 18% in 2004. Caution is required interpreting
these data as different sample characteristics and methodological
changes may account for some of the increase.

The average age for first use was between 18 and 19 years according to
the 2004 Mixmag sample. Over half of users said they planned to take
mushrooms again, and the most commonly used variety was "Mexican."

## Telephone Helplines

According to FESAT reports from 26 drug telephone helplines in 16
European countries, there was no significant increase in calls regarding
hallucinogenic mushrooms during 2004. Belgium reported 1% of all
inquiries to Druglijn in Flanders in 2003-2004 were mushroom-related.
The Swedish Poisons Information Centre reported calls peaked at 96 in
1998, then stabilised at 30-40 calls annually.

## Perceived Availability

ESPAD 2003 data show that rates of 15-16 year olds perceiving
mushrooms as "very" or "fairly easy" to obtain range from 4% to 28%.
Under 10% in Cyprus, Finland, Greece, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania,
Romania, and Turkey report easy access. Over 20% in the Czech Republic,
Ireland, Italy, Poland, and UK report easy access. In the Netherlands,
despite legal sales, only 16% report easy access.
