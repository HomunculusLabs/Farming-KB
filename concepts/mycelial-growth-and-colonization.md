## Hyphal Structure and Growth

Individual hyphae are tubular cells, typically 1 to 10 micrometers in diameter,
that grow at their tips through a process called apical extension. The tip
contains vesicles packed with cell wall material and enzymes. As the hypha
extends, new cell wall is deposited behind the advancing tip, and septa
(cross-walls) form at regular intervals to create individual cells. In many
cultivated species, these septa have central pores that allow cytoplasm and
even nuclei to flow between cells, enabling rapid resource distribution across
the network.

## Growth Patterns

Mycelium displays distinct growth morphologies that indicate health and
vitality.

**Rhizomorphic growth** appears as thick, rope-like strands with defined
directionality. This pattern indicates vigorous, healthy mycelium with strong
fruiting potential.

**Tomentose growth** appears as fluffy, cotton-like matting with no clear
direction. While still viable, tomentose growth is generally less vigorous and
may indicate suboptimal conditions or older genetics.

**Aerial mycelium** grows upward from the substrate surface, often appearing
as wispy or fluffy growth; some aerial growth is normal but excessive amounts
can indicate high humidity or insufficient gas exchange.

## Colonization Phases

Colonization proceeds through recognizable stages.

**Lag phase** (days 1 to 3) is the adaptation period after inoculation where
mycelium recovers from the stress of transfer and begins producing enzymes
appropriate for the new substrate. Little visible growth occurs.

**Acceleration phase** (days 3 to 10) shows rapidly expanding growth as enzyme
production ramps up and the mycelium establishes nutrient pathways.

**Deceleration phase** (days 10 to 14) occurs as the mycelium approaches full
colonization; growth slows as accessible nutrients decrease and metabolic
waste accumulates.

**Maturation** involves the consolidation phase where mycelium densifies its
network, often signaled by a color change from bright white to a slightly
off-white or cream tone.
