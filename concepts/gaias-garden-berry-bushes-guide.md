# Berry Bushes Guide in Gaia's Garden

> Catching the sunlight farther down, dancing with birds, are flowering shrubs and berry bushes. -- Toby Hemenway, Gaia's Garden

## Overview

Berry bushes occupy the shrub layer of the [[gaias-garden-food-forest-layers]], one of the most productive and wildlife-friendly tiers in the food forest. Hemenway emphasizes that berry bushes provide food for people, habitat and forage for birds, beneficial insects, and other wildlife, and can be integrated throughout the garden design -- from dedicated hedgerows to scattered plantings beneath fruit trees. Many berry bushes are shade-tolerant, making them excellent understory plants that still produce abundant crops with less direct sun.

## Berry Bushes in the Forest Garden Design

Berry bushes fit naturally into multiple zones:
- **Zone 1:** Strawberries as ground cover, small berry bushes near the kitchen door for daily grazing
- **Zone 2:** Mixed berry plantings with shrubs, insectary plants, and dwarf fruit trees
- **Zone 3:** Larger berry plantings, bramble thickets for wildlife and abundant harvest

## Recommended Berry Species

### Shade-Tolerant Berries (Understory)

These thrive in partial shade beneath fruit trees and larger shrubs:
- **Currants** (Ribes nigrum, R. rubrum): Black, red, and white currants. Among the most shade-tolerant of fruiting shrubs. Both full-sun and understory plantings produce well, though sunnier plants bear more fruit. Guild-grown shrubs require less water and have lusher, darker foliage.
- **Gooseberries** (Ribes uva-crispa): Excellent shade tolerance, similar to currants. Thorny varieties provide wildlife shelter; thornless varieties are easier to harvest.
- **Elderberry** (Sambucus nigra, S. canadensis): Tolerates partial shade, produces large clusters of berries for wine, jelly, and medicinal use. Flowers are also edible and attractive to beneficial insects. Pirate bugs particularly like elderberry.
- **Huckleberry** (Vaccinium species): Native woodland berry that thrives in acidic, shaded conditions. Blueberries sometimes root on floating logs in natural pond margins.

### Sun-Loving Berries (Openings and Edges)

- **Blueberry** (Vaccinium corymbosum): High-bush blueberry for garden settings. Requires acidic soil (pH 4.5-5.5). Can be planted in sunnier openings between trees. Near water features, blueberry bushes can benefit from the moist microclimate.
- **Raspberry** (Rubus idaeus, R. occidentalis): Red, black, and purple varieties. Best in full sun but tolerate light shade. Summer-bearing and everbearing varieties extend the harvest season.
- **Blackberry** (Rubus fruticosus, R. ursinus): Vigorous, thorny or thornless varieties. Can form dense thickets that provide excellent wildlife habitat. The Bullock brothers' property on Orcas Island featured blackberries that once sprawled in impenetrable tangles before being integrated into the food forest.
- **Strawberry** (Fragaria x ananassa, F. vesca): Ground cover layer species. Creeping varieties fill edges and spaces between shrubs. Wild strawberry (F. vesca) is more shade-tolerant and lower-maintenance than cultivated varieties.

### Wildlife and Multi-Use Berries

- **Serviceberry / Juneberry** (Amelanchier species): Native shrub producing small, sweet berries prized by birds. Ornamental value with white spring flowers and fall color.
- **Chokeberry** (Aronia melanocarpa): Very high in antioxidants. Tolerates partial shade and wet soils.
- **Chokecherry** (Prunus virginiana): Wildlife shrub producing tart berries suitable for jelly and wine.
- **High-bush cranberry** (Viburnum trilobum): Not a true cranberry but produces tart red berries used in sauces and jellies.
- **Buffaloberry** (Shepherdia argentea): Nitrogen-fixing shrub producing tart red berries. Both buffaloberry and sea buckthorn are nitrogen-fixers, adding soil fertility while producing fruit.
- **Nanking cherry** (Prunus tomentosa): Ornamental flowering shrub whose fruits are good for both wildlife and jam. Part of deer-resistant hedgerow designs.
- **Manchurian apricot** (Prunus mandshurica): Used in food-bearing hedge designs, providing fruit and wildlife habitat.

## Berry Bushes in Plant Guilds

Berry bushes serve multiple functions within plant guilds:
- **Food production** for humans and wildlife
- **Wildlife habitat** -- dense shrubs provide nesting sites, escape cover, and food
- **Insect attraction** -- flowers attract pollinators; berries attract birds that control pest insects
- **Soil protection** -- roots hold soil on slopes and prevent erosion
- **Edge creation** -- shrubs planted along boundaries create the productive edge zones that [[gaias-garden-edge-effect]] describes

Hemenway's guild designs frequently include berry bushes. For example, Jerome Osentowski's deer fence guild included gooseberry in the shrub layer, alongside hops and sweet peas on the trellis, sunflowers, clover and strawberries on the ground, and garlic in the root zone.

## Berry Bushes in Hedgerows and Food-Bearing Hedges

Hemenway describes food-bearing hedges that incorporate multiple berry species:
- Deer-resistant side: Manchurian plum, Nanking cherry, roses, Manchurian apricot, buffaloberry, osage orange, gooseberry, currant, Siberian pea shrub
- Bird-attracting side: Species chosen for abundant berries that attract insectivorous birds
- These hedges provide wind protection, privacy screening, wildlife corridors, and food simultaneously

## Wildlife Value of Berry Bushes

Berry bushes are among the mainstays of bird-attracting gardens. Varieties for bird gardens include:
- Blackberry, blueberry, cherry, chokecherry, honeysuckle, raspberry, serviceberry, mulberry, elderberry, and wild strawberry

For winter food (persistent fruits that remain on shrubs into cold months):
- Black chokecherry, snowberry, sumac, highbush cranberry, many varieties of crabapple, barberry, hawthorn, strawberry tree, and chinaberry

Broadleaf evergreens such as holly and large bamboo offer winter shelter, summer nesting sites, and escape cover alongside the berry-producing deciduous shrubs.

## Planting and Care Tips

- Berry bushes come in all sizes, from dwarf blueberries to nearly tree-sized hazelnuts, and can be plugged into edges, openings, and niches of many forms
- Shade-tolerant varieties grow beneath trees; sun-loving types fill sunny spaces between
- Include nitrogen-fixing companions (Siberian pea shrub, Elaeagnus species) near berry plantings
- Mulch heavily to retain moisture and suppress weeds
- Prune regularly to maintain productivity and airflow
