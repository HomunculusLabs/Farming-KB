## Overview

Mexico is the global center of [[mexico-center-neurotropic-fungal-biodiversity]], harboring 76
species of neurotropic fungi, of which 44 belong to Psilocybe -- representing
39% of all known Psilocybe species worldwide. This concentration makes Mexico
the most important country for neurotropic mycodiversity and the cradle of
traditional mushroom-using cultures. The Central American and Caribbean regions
surrounding Mexico, while less diverse, contain significant assemblages of
neurotropic species with their own ethnographic importance.

## Mexico: Global Center of Neurotropic Fungal Diversity

### Species Counts and Significance

Mexico's 76 neurotropic species include representatives from nearly all major
neurotropic genera: Psilocybe (44 taxa), Copelandia (3 species: C.
cyanescens, C. mexicana, C. tropicalis), Panaeolus (7 species), Amanita
(2 species: A. muscaria, A. pantherina), Gymnopilus (5 species), Hypholoma
(2 species), Conocybe (C. siliginoides), and others. The country also harbors
ergot fungi (Claviceps paspali, C. purpurea), Cordyceps species, and sacred
gasteromycetes used in indigenous ceremonies.

The 44 Mexican Psilocybe taxa represent 39% of the world's 116 Psilocybe
species, remarkable considering that Mexico occupies only about 1.5% of the
world's land surface area. In contrast, the USA and Canada together have only
21 taxa, and Europe has 14 species of neurotropic Psilocybe.

### Distribution by Vegetation Type

In Guzman's 1983 monograph, the 42 neurotropic Psilocybe species then known
from Mexico were distributed across vegetation types as follows:

- **Mesophytic forest**: 34 species -- The dominant habitat for Mexican
  neurotropic Psilocybe, these humid mountainous forests support the
  greatest diversity. Mexico serves as a corridor where northern and
  southern mycobiotas converge. Northern species like P. caerulipes reach
  northeastern Mexico through Fagus-dominated forests, while southern
  species like P. yungensis reach Mexico through Alnus-dominated forests.
- **[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-in-tropical-forest-restoration]]**: 4 species -- Despite the vast extent of Mexico's
  tropical forests, relatively few neurotropic Psilocybe species occur.
- **Coniferous forest**: 4 species -- Surprisingly few despite coniferous
  forests being more intensively studied mycologically in Mexico.
