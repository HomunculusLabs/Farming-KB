# Copelandia and Panaeolus Genera

## Taxonomic Relationship

Copelandia is treated in the paper by Guzman, Allen, and Gartz as a
separate genus from Panaeolus, following the classification established by
Singer (1951) and supported by microscopic and chemical evidence. Some
authors have proposed merging the two genera, but the paper maintains the
distinction based on the presence of cheilocystidia with prominent apical
appendages characteristic of Copelandia species. Together, these genera
include 13 neurotropic species, making them the third-largest group of
hallucinogenic fungi after Psilocybe and Gymnopilus.

## Copelandia Species

Copelandia contains 7 documented neurotropic species, all of which are
tropical or subtropical in distribution and known for their high potency.
The genus is pantropical, found in Central and South America, the
Caribbean, Africa, Asia, and Oceania. Key species include:

- **[[copelandia-cyanescens]]**: The most widespread species in the genus, found
  throughout the tropics worldwide. It is common on bovine dung in
  pastures and is one of the most potent [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]], with
  significant [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] concentrations. It occurs in
  Hawaii, the Caribbean, Central America, South America, Africa, Asia,
  and Australia.

- **[[copelandia-tropicalis]]**: Similar to C. cyanescens but distinguished
  by spore morphology and cystidial characteristics. Found in tropical
  Asia, the [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]], and tropical America.

- **[[copelandia-mexicana]]**: Originally described from Mexico, this species
  occurs in the Caribbean and Central America. It is one of the more
  poorly known species in the genus.

- **Copelandia bispora**: A distinctive species producing only two spores
  per basidium rather than the typical four. Found in tropical America.

- **[[copelandia-anomalus]]**: Described from Cambodia, this species
  is widespread in Southeast Asia. It is commonly found on bovine dung
  in rice paddies and pastures.

- **Copelandia venezolana**: Described from Venezuela, this species is
  known from tropical South America.

## Panaeolus Species

Panaeolus sensu stricto (excluding Copelandia) contains 6 documented
neurotropic species. These are generally less potent than Copelandia
species but are widespread and commonly encountered. Key species include:

- **[[gartz-panaeolus-subbalteatus-taxonomy-and-intoxication-history]]** (P. cinctulus): One of the most widespread
  neurotropic fungi, found across temperate and subtropical regions
  worldwide. It occurs on horse and cow dung, compost, and enriched
  soils. It has a broad distribution across [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Europe,
  and parts of Asia. This is the most commonly encountered Panaeolus
  species in temperate regions.

- **Panaeolus sphinctrinus**: A cosmopolitan species found on dung
  worldwide. Its neurotropic status has been confirmed by chemical
  analysis, though potency is generally low compared to Copelandia
  species.

- **Panaeolus tropicalis**: Found in tropical and subtropical regions
  of the Americas, this species occurs on dung in pastures.

- **Panaeolus africanus**: As the name suggests, this species is
  primarily African in distribution, found on dung in tropical
  grasslands. It has also been reported from parts of Asia.

- **Panaeolus fimicola**: A widespread species with a complex
  taxonomic history. Some collections have tested positive for
  psilocybin while others have not, suggesting either chemical
  variation or cryptic species within the current concept.
