# 4-Hydroxyindole as a Tryptamine Synthesis Building Block

## Overview

4-Hydroxyindole is the commercially available starting material that anchors the Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) concise large-scale synthesis of psilocin and psilocybin. Its selection as the synthetic starting point is driven by its structural similarity to the target molecules, its commercial availability in multi-gram quantities, and the straightforward protection chemistry it enables at the 4-hydroxy position.

## Structural Relevance

Psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) differs from serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) only in the position of the hydroxyl substituent on the indole ring. 4-Hydroxyindole provides the complete indole nucleus with the hydroxyl group already in the correct position for psilocin synthesis. The core synthetic challenge then becomes:

1. Protecting the 4-hydroxy group during subsequent transformations
2. Introducing the dimethylaminoethyl side chain at the 3-position
3. Deprotecting to reveal the final phenol

This strategy avoids the need for late-stage aromatic hydroxylation, which is notoriously difficult to achieve with positional selectivity on indole substrates.

## Commercial Availability and Practical Considerations

Shirota et al. sourced 4-hydroxyindole from Tokyo Kasei Kogyo Co., Ltd., available in bottles containing more than 25 g (greater than 185 mmol). This commercial availability in bulk quantities is a practical prerequisite for the gram-scale synthesis. The reagent was used without further purification, indicating acceptable commercial purity for the synthetic route.

The availability of 4-hydroxyindole contrasts with many other substituted indoles that may require multi-step preparation before they can serve as synthetic starting materials. This accessibility significantly reduces the overall step count and cost of the psilocin synthesis.

## Acetyl Protection Strategy

The first synthetic step involves protecting the 4-hydroxy group of 4-hydroxyindole (compound 3) by acetylation. The procedure uses acetic anhydride and pyridine in anhydrous dichloromethane at 0 degrees Celsius, warming to room temperature.

The choice of acetyl protection over benzyl ether protection is deliberate. While benzyl ether protection had been used in prior syntheses, it requires a separate deprotection step at the end of the sequence. Acetyl protection, by contrast, is removed in situ during the LiAlH4 reduction step, eliminating a discrete deprotection operation and reducing the total step count.

This decision exemplifies the principle of convergent protecting group strategy, where the choice of protecting group is made not in isolation but with consideration of downstream transformations.

## Conversion to 4-Acetylindole

The protected 4-acetylindole (compound 4) is obtained as ivory white crystals in constant yield (the yield is described as "constant" rather than quantitative, likely indicating it is highly reproducible across batches). The product crystallizes directly from the reaction workup, enabling isolation by simple filtration without chromatography.

The 1H NMR spectrum of 4 in CDCl3 confirms the structure with characteristic signals at delta 8.27 (br s, H-1), 7.22 (d, H-7), 7.15 (t, H-6), 7.11 (t, H-2), 6.85 (dd, H-5), and 6.41 (m, H-3), along with the acetyl methyl at delta 2.39. The 13C NMR shows the acetyl carbonyl at delta 169.6 and the C-4 carbon at delta 143.6.

The ESI mass spectrum shows the molecular ion at m/z 176.1 [M+H]+ and a characteristic fragment at m/z 134.0 [M-Ac+H]+ resulting from loss of the acetyl group.

## Advantages Over Alternative Starting Materials

Several alternatives to 4-hydroxyindole exist for psilocin synthesis, each with tradeoffs:

1. **Tryptamine:** Requires regioselective hydroxylation at C-4, which is synthetically challenging
2. **5-Hydroxyindole (serotonin precursor):** The hydroxyl is at the wrong position; rearrangement chemistry would be needed
3. **Indole itself:** Would require both hydroxylation and side chain installation, increasing step count

4-Hydroxyindole uniquely combines correct substitution pattern with commercial availability, making it the optimal starting material for psilocin synthesis. The choice underscores the importance of retrosynthetic analysis in designing efficient synthetic routes.

## Role in the Complete Synthetic Sequence

In the Shirota synthesis, 4-hydroxyindole serves as the foundation for a 4-step sequence to psilocin and a 6-step sequence to psilocybin:

1. 4-Hydroxyindole to 4-acetylindole (acetyl protection)
2. 4-Acetylindole to 3-glyoxalyl-4-acetylindole (oxalyl chloride acylation)
3. Glyoxalylindole to glyoxalylamide (dimethylamine amidation)
4. Glyoxalylamide to psilocin (LiAlH4 reduction)
5. Psilocin to dibenzyl phosphate (phosphorylation)
6. Dibenzyl phosphate to zwitterionic intermediate (intramolecular rearrangement)
7. Zwitterionic intermediate to psilocybin (catalytic hydrogenolysis)

## Historical Context

The use of 4-hydroxyindole as a starting material for psilocin synthesis has precedent in earlier work by Troxler, Hofmann, and colleagues at Sandoz in the late 1950s, as well as subsequent reports through the 1990s. Shirota et al. built upon this foundation but optimized the protection strategy and phosphorylation methodology to achieve the first truly chromatography-free gram-scale synthesis.
