[[lactic-acid-bacteria]] serum (LABS) is a fermented microbial inoculant
that has gained attention among organic cannabis cultivators for its
potential to increase trichome density, terpene production, and overall
cannabinoid yield. The hypothesis connecting LABS application to
enhanced cannabinoid biosynthesis centers on hexanoic acid, a
short-chain fatty acid produced during the fermentation of ruminant
milk fats, which serves as a direct biochemical precursor in the
cannabinoid synthesis pathway. This article examines the preparation of
LABS, the proposed mechanisms of action, and the biochemical basis for
the observed effects.

## Background: Effective Microorganisms and LABS

The concept of using beneficial microbial consortia in agriculture was
popularized by Teruo Higa through his work on Effective Microorganisms
(EM). Higa's original EM formulation described an inoculant of multiple
species of [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]] providing suppression of plant
pathogens and diseases, conservation of energy in plants, solubilization
of soil minerals, and soil microbial-ecological balance,
[[photosynthetic-efficiency]], and [[biological-nitrogen-fixation-agriculture]]. The
original blend included lactic acid bacteria, purple bacteria, yeast,
actinomycetes, and fermenting fungi. Higa later revised the mixture to
include only lactic acid bacteria, purple bacteria, and yeast.

LABS represents a simplified, DIY-accessible version of this concept,
focusing specifically on the lactic acid bacteria component. These
bacteria have demonstrated value as bio-fertilizers: in greenhouse
tomato trials, a bio-fertilizer containing Lactobacillus casei,
Lactobacillus lactis, [[rhodopseudomonas-palustris]], and Saccharomyces
cerevisiae increased total tomato yield by 19 to 21 percent after soil
application and by 13 to 14 percent after foliar application when
combined with humic fertilizer from vermicompost. Beyond their
fertilizing role, lactic acid bacteria exhibit antifungal activity
against a broad range of plant pathogens.

## LABS Preparation Method

The preparation of homemade lactic acid bacteria serum follows a
two-stage fermentation process using readily available ingredients.

### Stage One: Rice Wash Fermentation

Fresh rice is soaked in a jar of water and shaken vigorously. The rice
is then strained out and discarded, while the now-milky rice wash water
is retained. This water contains carbohydrates washed from the rice
surface along with wild lactic acid bacteria naturally present on the
grain. The jar is covered loosely to exclude insects and placed in a
dark cupboard for several weeks. Fermentation is complete when three
distinct layers have formed: a floating skin on top, a cloudy middle
layer, and settled solids at the bottom. The middle layer, which
contains the concentrated population of lactic acid bacteria, is
recovered using a siphon.

### Stage Two: Milk Fermentation

The extracted rice wash solution is mixed with ten parts milk and
sealed in a jar fitted with a one-way fermentation valve. Over seven to
eight days, the lactic acid bacteria multiply and ferment the milk
sugars (lactose). As the pH drops during fermentation, excess milk fats
and carbohydrates precipitate, forming a solid curd that floats on top.
Below the curd, a clear golden liquid remains: this is the lactic acid
bacteria serum, a probiotic whey rich in lactic acid bacteria and their
metabolic byproducts.

### Stabilization

The strained serum is mixed with an equal volume of molasses (one part
serum to one part molasses) to stabilize the bacteria and extend shelf
life. The molasses provides a food source that keeps the bacteria viable
during storage. The stabilized serum can be stored for extended periods
and diluted for application as a soil drench or foliar spray.

The solid curd byproduct has its own value and can be incorporated into
compost, blended into a sprouted seed tea (SST), or used as animal
feed, provided the fermentation process was conducted successfully.

## The Hexanoate Hypothesis

The central hypothesis for why LABS-treated cannabis plants may produce
superior cannabinoid profiles involves hexanoic acid (caproic acid), a
six-carbon short-chain fatty acid with the molecular formula C6H12O2.

### Biochemical Pathway

Cannabinoid biosynthesis in [[cannabis-sativa]] begins with two precursor
molecules: geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP) and olivetolic acid (OA). GPP
provides the terpene moiety and OA provides the phenolic moiety. The
enzyme cannabigerolic acid synthase (CBGAS) joins these to form
cannabigerolic acid (CBGA), the universal cannabinoid precursor from
which THCA, CBDA, and CBCA are derived.

The production of olivetolic acid depends on hexanoyl-CoA, a coenzyme A
thioester of hexanoic acid. Hexanoyl-CoA serves as the primer for the
polyketide synthase enzyme olivetolic acid cyclase (OAC), which
catalyzes the formation of olivetolic acid through sequential
condensation and cyclization reactions. Hexanoyl-CoA is itself derived
from hexanoate through the action of an acyl-activating enzyme. In
Cannabis sativa, the enzyme CsAAE1, which shows high transcript
abundance specifically in glandular trichomes, activates hexanoate and
other short- to medium-chain fatty acids. Research has confirmed that
CsAAE1 is the hexanoyl-CoA synthetase supplying the cannabinoid pathway.

### Source of Hexanoate in LABS

Hexanoic acid comprises 1 to 2 percent of the total fatty acids in
ruminant milk triacylglycerols. Ruminant milk fat has a uniquely
diverse composition among terrestrial mammals due to ruminal
biohydrogenation of dietary unsaturated fatty acids and de novo fatty
acid synthesis in the mammary gland. During the milk fermentation stage
of LABS production, the lactic acid bacteria break down milk
triacylglycerols through microbial esterification, releasing hexanoic
acid as a byproduct.

The proposed mechanism is that when LABS is applied to cannabis
plants, the hexanoic acid in the serum is taken up by plant tissues
and converted to hexanoyl-CoA by the plant's acyl-activating enzymes.
This increased supply of hexanoyl-CoA stimulates olivetolic acid
production, which in turn increases the flux through the cannabinoid
[[clarke-marijuana-botany-cannabinoid-biosynthesis-pathway]], resulting in higher concentrations of THCA, CBDA,
and other cannabinoids in the glandular trichomes.

## Hexanoic Acid as a Plant Defense Priming Agent

Beyond its direct role as a cannabinoid precursor, hexanoic acid has
been identified as a potent natural priming agent for plant defense
responses. Research has demonstrated that hexanoic acid can activate
broad-spectrum defenses in plants by inducing callose deposition and
activating both the salicylic acid (SA) and jasmonic acid (JA)
[[plant-signaling-pathways]]. These pathways are central to the plant's ability
to resist pathogen attack.

Of particular relevance to cannabis cultivation, hexanoic acid primes
redox-related genes to produce an antioxidant protective effect, which
may be critical for limiting infection by necrotrophic pathogens. The
JA signaling pathway is especially noteworthy because jasmonic acid is
known to stimulate trichome production in many plant species. If
hexanoic acid application primes the JA pathway in cannabis, this could
represent a secondary mechanism for the increased trichome-to-calyx
ratio observed by cultivators using LABS.
