The naming of cannabis varieties is a complex and often inconsistent
practice within the cannabis industry. Cervantes addresses the

## Variety and Cultivar

### Variety

A variety is a subdivision of a kind, group, or family that is
distinct in some characters. Within the variety, all plants exhibit a
set of defined morphological, physiological, or chemical
characteristics that differentiate it from all other varieties. The
variety must be uniform, and variations in essential characteristics
are described and characterized by the breeder. The variety remains
unchanged to a reasonable degree of reliability when reproduced.

### Cultivar (cv)

Cultivar is derived from "cultivated variety." It refers to a
population of cultivated plants clearly distinguished by morphological,
physiological, cytological, or chemical characteristics. When
reproduced sexually or asexually, the population retains its
distinguishing characters. The 1978 Guidelines for Classifying
Cultivated Plants stipulate that variety and cultivar are considered
equivalents.

New selections derived from a cultivar that show sufficient variation
from the parent to be worthy of a name are regarded as distinct
cultivars.
