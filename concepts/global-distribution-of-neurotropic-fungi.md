# Global Distribution of Neurotropic Fungi

Gastón Guzmán, John W. Allen, and jochen gartz published a landmark
comprehensive survey in 2000 documenting the worldwide geographical
distribution of neurotropic (hallucinogenic) fungi. Their paper analyzed
216 known species across four major groups, providing the most complete
picture available of where these fungi occur naturally and how they are
used by human cultures.

## The Four Groups of Neurotropic Fungi

The authors classified neurotropic fungi into four distinct groups based
on their chemical composition and cultural use:

### Group 1: Psilocybin-Containing Species

The largest group, containing species with psilocybin and related indole
compounds (psilocin, baeocystin, norbaeocystin). This group spans 13
genera with the following species counts:

- Psilocybe: 116 species (the overwhelming majority)
- Gymnopilus: 14 species
- Panaeolus: 13 species
- Copelandia: 12 species
- Hypholoma: 6 species
- Pluteus: 6 species
- Inocybe: 6 species
- Conocybe: 4 species
- Panaeolina: 4 species
- Gerronema: 2 species
- Agrocybe, Galerina, Mycena: 1 species each

Total: 183 species across 13 genera.

### Group 2: Ibotenic Acid Species

Three Amanita species containing ibotenic acid: [[psilocybe-aztecorum-entity-the-mushroom-cultivator]], for
example, grows in the high mountains of Mexico at 4,000 meters.
