# Laccase Mediator Systems in Fungal Bioremediation

Laccase mediator systems (LMS) represent one of the most important
biochemical strategies for extending the substrate range of fungal laccase
enzymes beyond their natural phenolic substrates. By employing low
molecular weight [[fungi-in-the-environment-bioremediation-applications]].

## Principle of Laccase Mediator Action

Laccases are multicopper oxidases that typically catalyze the oxidation
of phenolic substrates, transferring electrons to molecular oxygen which
is reduced to water. The standard redox potential of laccase alone limits
its activity to substrates with lower oxidation potentials. However, in
the presence of synthetic or natural mediators, laccase can generate
 diffusible oxidizing species that attack a much broader range of
substrates including non-phenolic compounds.

The mediator cycle involves three key steps: (1) laccase oxidizes the
mediator molecule to its radical cation form; (2) the oxidized mediator
diffuses away from the enzyme active site to reach substrates that are
too large or too hydrophobic to directly interact with laccase; and (3)
the mediator oxidizes the target substrate and is regenerated to its
reduced form, allowing the catalytic cycle to continue.

## Synthetic Mediators

### ABTS (2,2-Azino-bis(3-ethylbenzthiazoline-6-sulfonate))

ABTS is the most extensively studied synthetic laccase mediator. When
oxidized by laccase, ABTS forms a stable radical cation (ABTS+.) that
can oxidize non-phenolic substrates via hydrogen atom abstraction rather
than single-electron oxidation. The laccase-ABTS couple was first
demonstrated to depolymerize kraft lignin and oxidize [[staycare-veratryl-alcohol-as-lignin-peroxidase-mediator]],
a substrate normally inaccessible to laccase alone.

The ABTS-mediated system has been successfully applied to PAH
degradation. Anthracene was oxidized to anthraquinone, and benzo[a]pyrene
was also oxidized by the laccase-ABTS couple. ABTS has further been used
to enhance the oxidation of nerve agents VX and Russian VX, as well as
the insecticide analog diisopropylamiton by [[pycnoporus-cinnabarinus]] secretes 3,4-
hydroxyanthranilic acid along with laccase as part of its ligninolytic
system. This natural mediator enables cleavage of non-phenolic model
compounds and depolymerization of soluble lignin. The discovery of
3,4-DHAA demonstrated that fungi naturally produce their own mediators
to extend laccase functionality.

### Other Natural Mediators

Natural mediator systems include various phenolic compounds, organic
acids, and fungal metabolites that can serve as intermediaries in
oxidation reactions. Manganese(III)-chelates generated by manganese
peroxidase also function as natural mediators, creating diffusible
oxidants that can penetrate substrate matrices. The search for
additional natural mediators continues, with researchers screening for
fungal metabolites that enhance laccase activity.
