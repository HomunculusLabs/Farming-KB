# Backyard and Small-Scale Cannabis Cultivation

For growers fortunate enough to live in countries or regions that tolerate
cannabis, backyard cultivation offers the opportunity to give plants the
tender loving care they deserve. Cervantes describes the advantages and
techniques for integrating cannabis into existing home garden setups.

## Advantages of Backyard Growing

Growing cannabis in a flower and vegetable garden is ideal because you can
care for all your plants at the same time. Benefits include:

- Close proximity allows daily attention and monitoring
- Integration with existing garden infrastructure (irrigation, composting)
- Natural sunlight provides full-spectrum lighting at no cost
- Larger root zone in ground soil vs. containers
- Less need for artificial climate control
- Lower costs compared to indoor operations
- Ability to grow larger plants with higher yields per plant

## Soil Preparation

Prepare soil in the fall for spring planting:

- Remove weeds and dig planting holes or garden beds
- Turn soil over and ensure plenty of amendments (see organic growing
  practices)
- Always put a heavy layer of mulch on any soil that will be planted
- A 12-inch-plus layer of mulch retains moisture and suppresses weeds

Fall soil preparation allows amendments to break down and integrate
during winter months, creating optimal growing conditions by spring.

## Garden Integration

Cannabis can be integrated into existing gardens alongside vegetables,
flowers, and herbs. When planted among other garden plants:

- Cannabis benefits from the biodiversity of a healthy garden ecosystem
- Companion plants can deter pests (see
  [[cervantes-companion-planting-pest-deterrent]])
- The garden looks natural and less suspicious to observers
- Regular garden maintenance routines cover cannabis care

## Rogue Pollen Protection

Backyard growers must be aware of the threat from rogue pollen, which can
travel from commercial hemp farms or wild males across considerable
distances. Strategies include:

- Research wind patterns and plant accordingly
- Use wind-shadows (large divots in a hillside) for protection
- Plant early or late crops to avoid peak pollen months (June-July)
- Cover greenhouse intakes with moist towels (humidity kills pollen)
- Wet down exterior surfaces of greenhouses

## Security Considerations for Backyard Gardens

Even in tolerant regions, backyard growers should consider discretion:

- Blend cannabis plants with other tall garden plants (tomatoes, sunflowers)
- Maintain a tidy garden that looks like a normal residential landscape
- Avoid conspicuous structures or unusual garden layouts
- Keep plant numbers modest to avoid drawing attention
- Be aware that over-fence visibility can expose plants to neighbors

## Container vs. In-Ground Growing

Backyard gardens offer the option of both in-ground and container growing:

### In-Ground Advantages
- Larger root zones support bigger plants
- Natural soil biology and mycorrhizal networks
- Less frequent watering needed
- Lower cost (no containers or soilless media to purchase)

### Container Advantages
- Portable - can be moved for security or weather protection
- Complete control over soil mix and nutrients
- Easier to isolate sick plants
- No soil-borne disease carryover between seasons

## Site Selection

Choose backyard sites considering:

- **Sunlight** - minimum 6-8 hours of direct sun daily
- **Wind protection** - avoid exposed, windy locations
- **Drainage** - ensure soil does not become waterlogged
- **Privacy** - consider visibility from neighbors and street
- **Access to water** - proximity to hoses or irrigation
- **Soil quality** - test and amend as needed

## Seasonal Planning

- Fall: Prepare beds, add amendments, apply mulch
- Early spring: Start seeds indoors or in cold frames
- Spring: Transplant seedlings after last frost
- Summer: Monitor growth, train plants, manage pests
- Late summer/fall: Monitor trichomes for harvest readiness
- Fall: Harvest, dry, cure, and prepare beds for next season
