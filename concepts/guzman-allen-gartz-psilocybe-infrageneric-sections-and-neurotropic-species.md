## Psilocybe Infrageneric Sections and Neurotropic Species Classification

## Overview

Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz (1998) identified 116 species of *Psilocybe* as neurotropic out of 216 total neurotropic fungal species worldwide. The neurotropic species of *Psilocybe* are concentrated within specific infrageneric sections, many of which are characterized by the bluing reaction — a reliable indicator of psilocybin and related indole alkaloid presence. Understanding these sections is critical for taxonomic identification and for predicting which species may contain psychoactive compounds.

## Bluing Sections (Confirmed Neurotropic)

The following sections of *Psilocybe* contain species with confirmed or strongly suspected neurotropic properties, primarily identified by their bluing reaction and/or chemical analysis:

### Section Aztecorum

- Contains high-elevation species growing in subalpine and alpine habitats
- *P. aztecorum* var. *aztecorum*: Found only in the high mountains of Mexico at elevations up to 4,000 m. This is one of the most iconic neurotropic species, named after the Aztec civilization.
- *P. baeocystis*: Known from the [[european-hallucinogenic-mushroom-prevalence-and-trends]]. Found in temperate grasslands across Europe, India, Russia, North America, Chile, Peru, New Zealand, and Australia. Notably absent from Mexico despite its abundance elsewhere.
- This species has a wide temperate distribution but shows an unusual gap in its range where Mexico is concerned.

### Section Subaeruginosae

- Species from this section are found primarily in the [[biogeography-austral-hemisphere-fungi]]
- The highest diversity centers are in Mexico, the Caribbean, Mesoamerica, and Colombia
- South America, New Guinea, eastern Australia, and New Zealand also show high diversity but remain underexplored

## Biogeographic Patterns Across Sections

The distribution of *Psilocybe* sections reveals interesting biogeographic patterns. Sections Aztecorum and Mexicanae are predominantly Mesoamerican in origin but show disjunct populations in North America and Australasia, respectively. Section Zapotecorum demonstrates a continuous distribution from Mexico through the Andes into temperate South America, with remarkable outliers in New Zealand. Section Semilanceatae is primarily a Northern Hemisphere temperate group, absent from tropical regions entirely. Section Subaeruginosae is concentrated in the Southern Hemisphere, particularly Australia and New Zealand.

These patterns suggest an ancient Gondwanan origin for some lineages, with subsequent dispersal and speciation events. The close relationships between geographically distant species (e.g., *P. samuiensis* from Thailand and *P. mexicana* from Mexico within Section Mexicanae; *P. aucklandii* from New Zealand and *P. zapotecorum* from Mexico within Section Zapotecorum) are consistent with Horak's (1983) observations of fungal biogeographic connections between South America/Mesoamerica and Southeast Asia/Australasia.

## Significance for Identification

The infrageneric section classification provides a practical framework for field identification. When encountering a *Psilocybe* specimen, determining which section it belongs to (based on macroscopic features like bluing, habitat, spore print color, and microscopic characteristics) can reliably predict whether the species is likely neurotropic. The bluing reaction remains the single most useful field indicator, as all confirmed neurotropic sections contain bluing species, while excluded sections do not.
