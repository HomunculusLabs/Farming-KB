# Jajarkot Advanced Polyculture

## Overview

The Jajarkot Permaculture Program (JPP), a village self-development organization in Nepal, created an expanded polyculture system that builds upon Ianto Evans's foundational seven-variety temperate polyculture. Modified by Toby Hemenway for broader North American application, the Jajarkot polyculture incorporates a larger number of species and a more complex successional pattern. With modifications, it can be planted in spring in most North American gardens and yields food for six to eight months of the year. Their polyculture designs drew on traditional Nepali interplanting knowledge combined with modern ecological understanding. The system was brought to wider attention through Toby Hemenway's documentation in Gaia's Garden, where he adapted it for temperate North American conditions.

## Species Composition

The Jajarkot polyculture uses a multi-layered approach with species selected for different phenological stages and [[hamilton-beneficial-insects-and-biological-pest-control]] provide more sustained nectar sources.
- **Several legume species** (fava beans, bush beans, bush peas) fix nitrogen through the season and attract parasitic wasps.
- **Dense greenery** shades and protects soil, excludes weeds, and maintains moisture.
- **Alliums** provide pest-repellent properties through their sulfur compounds.
- **Extended harvest** from a single major sowing and a few minutes of later care reduces labor inputs compared to successive monoculture plantings.

## Yield Comparison

In Hemenway's experience, the overall yield of the Jajarkot polyculture is greater than the combined yield of the same species grown in monocultural blocks covering equivalent area. This yield advantage comes from:
- More complete use of vertical space and root zones
- Reduced competition through niche partitioning
- Lower pest losses due to diversity
- Extended harvest period reducing waste and gluts
- Self-mulching effect reducing water stress
