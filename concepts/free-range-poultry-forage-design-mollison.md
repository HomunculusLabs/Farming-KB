# Free-Range Poultry Forage System Design

updated: 2026-04-18

Permaculture Two (1979) Chapter 8 presents the most comprehensive free-range poultry forage system design in permaculture literature. This extends carob (Ceratonia siliqua) -- Seeds and pods stored for milling
- [[permaculture-honey-locust-pod-fodder-system]] (Gleditsia triacanthos)
- [[comparison-black-locust-vs-honey-locust]] (Robinia pseudoacacia) -- Seed; leaves may poison stock
- Mesquites (Prosopis and Strombocarpa) -- Resistant to salt and drought
- Acacias: A. giraffae, A. albida, A. aneura, A. kempeana, A. boloserica, A. cowleana, A. victoriae, A. binervata, A. longifolia, A. peuce, A. oswaldia

### Trees (Nuts and Acorns)
- Black walnut (Juglans nigra) -- Store for 12 months
- Persian walnut (J. regia) -- Store for 12 months
- Chestnut ([[morus-alba]], M. nigra) -- High protein value
- Elderberries (Sambucus spp.)
- Emu berry (Owenia reticulata, O. acidula)
- Palms -- Many species yield nuts and fruit in subtropical/tropical areas
- Karreboom (Rhus lancea) -- Hardy poultry berry bush
- Hawthorn (Crataegus spp.) and serviceberries

### Vines
- Dolichos spp. -- Well-suited to pigeons, temperate to tropical
- Passionfruit (Passiflora spp.) -- Banana and black passionfruit eagerly eaten; banana passionfruit frost-resistant
- Kudzu vine (Thunbergia) -- Free-seeding rambler for [[knf-poultry-feed-preparation]]
- Chayote (Choko) -- Waste piles or low trellis
- Gabizura (Actinostemma lobatum) -- Scrambler with oily seeds

### Roots
- Nut-grass (Eleocharis) and sour-grass (Oxalis spp.) -- Poultry eat leaves, stems, tubers
- [[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]] (Helianthus) -- Pulled as needed, flourishes under oak forest

### Greens and Herb Layer
- Clovers, medics, lucerne, chicory, asparagus, plantain, fennel, mixed grasses
- Pokeweed (Phytolacca Americana) -- Especially pigeons
- Wood millet (Milium effusum), lupin species, perennial buckwheat (Fagopyrum cyanosum)
- Partridge berry (Mitchella repens) under pines or acid conditions
- Checkerberry (Gaultheria procumbens)
- Wild rice (Zizania aquatica) -- Heavy yields in ponds for duck forage
- Lotus spp., strapweed (Triglochin), algal pond weeds

### Broadcast Sowing in Strawyards
- Sunflower (heads stored in autumn), millets (Panicum), corn (Zea), buckwheat (Fagopyrum)
- Wheat, rye, barley, oats, teff
- Chenopodium spp., chick pea, lentils, lab-lab, field peas, Centrosema
- Amaranthus grains (1000 varieties), Eragrostis, Portulaca seeds, linseed

### Specific Herbs
- Shepherd's purse (Capsella bursa-pastoris) -- Stimulating effect on egg production (Turner)
- Cleavers (Galium aparine) -- Iron and iodine content; needs protection from free-range poultry
- Plantain (Plantago major, P. lanceolata) -- Absorbs chicken manure waste, produces green fodder
- Chard -- First choice green; succession sowings in throwover area

## Establishment

As for orchards: area controlled for browsing herbivores, sown to herbal leys, nitrogenous/leguminous plants established as manure crop for slower-evolving trees, shrubs, herbs, and root crops. Initial costs in fencing, initial mulching, buildings, water supply, and stock provision.

## Regulation of Yield

### Seasonal Forage Pattern
- Hard seed (carob yields 3-6 beans daily for 3 goats all year = at least 1,000 G.F.D. (goat [[poultry-forage-days-mollison]]) concentrate value. This enables preferential planting of highest-value species.

### Egg Production Through Variety Selection
- Dorkings for winter
- Ducks for general production
- Heavy breeds for spring
- Leghorns for summer laying
- Pigeon and quail yield most of year if age classes kept mixed
- Wyandottes and/or silky bantams for brooding eggs of other species

## Pest Control Functions

Poultry on range as weeding mechanisms: if plant species useful to people but not much damaged by poultry are chosen, a work-free garden results. Oxalis spp. eagerly eaten by hens but pest to small-fruit growers. Poultry will kill out persistent but selected herbs over time.

## Predator Control

Tree-forage systems allow ducks, pigeons, guinea-fowl, and hens to escape into shrubberies or roost in trees. Hens at roost used as "baits" for ground predators with sheds fitted with ground-level live-traps. Large flocks of pigeon in secure, raised lofts are largely immune from predation and may be most useful for "wild" areas.
