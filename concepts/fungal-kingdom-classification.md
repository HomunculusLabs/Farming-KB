### Zygomycota

A group of more than 1,000 species defined by the presence of a meiospore (zygospore) and absence of a flagellum. As currently defined, zygomycetes are polyphyletic — they do not form a natural evolutionary group. Major orders include Mucorales, Entomophthorales, and Trichomycetes. Some genera like Basidiobolus have uncertain phylogenetic placement. The coenocytic mycelium and formation of zygospores through sexual reproduction characterize this diverse group. See zygomycete phylum guide for expanded treatment.

### Ascomycota

The largest phylum of fungi, containing the majority of described species. Classes include Archiascomycetes, Saccharomycetes (yeasts), and Euascomycetes (filamentous ascomycetes). Members range from single-celled yeasts (Saccharomyces) to complex cup fungi (Peziza) and truffles (Tuber). Many economically important fungi (Penicillium, Aspergillus, Fusarium) and plant pathogens belong to this phylum. Lichenized Ascomycota represent approximately one-fifth of all described species in the phylum. See [[basidiomycete-phylum-guide]] for further detail.

### Glomales (Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi)

Formerly placed in Zygomycota, molecular evidence now supports Glomales as a distinct ancient lineage. These are the arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi, critical for plant nutrient uptake worldwide. About 163 species in 7 genera have been described, divided into two suborders (Glomineae and Gigasporineae). They are obligate symbionts that cannot be cultured apart from plant hosts and have co-evolved with plants over approximately 400 million years.

## Detailed Ascomycota Classification

The classification of Euascomycetes is currently in flux. Key clades include Sordariales, Xylariales, Hypocreales, Diaporthales, and Pyrenomycetes. The Pyrenomycetes clade encompasses numerous perithecial fungi. Lichenization occurred early in Ascomycota evolution, and lichenized forms are found across multiple clades. Saccharomycetes (yeasts) form a separate well-supported clade distinct from filamentous ascomycetes. Approximately 23,000+ described species of non-lichenized Ascomycota are recognized. Archiascomycetes includes Taphrinales (about 100 species) and represents a basal lineage within the phylum.

## Detailed Basidiomycota Classification

Within Basidiomycota, three major classes are recognized:

- **Ustilaginiomycetes** (smuts): Plant pathogens producing teliospores
- **Urediniomycetes** (rusts): Obligate plant pathogens with complex life cycles; includes Septobasidiales (parasitic on scale insects) and a mix of yeast-like and filamentous forms
- **Hymenomycetes** (mushroom-forming): The most conspicuous class, including Tremellales, Auriculariales, and typical mushroom-forming orders (Agaricales, Boletales, Russulales). The cantharelloid and gomphoid-phalloid clades are also recognized within this class

Approximately 3,500+ ectomycorrhizal species belong to Basidiomycota. Basidiomycetous yeasts are polyphyletic, having evolved from multiple filamentous ancestors. See [[ectomycorrhizal-ecology]] for further detail.

## Kingdom Straminipila

Several groups traditionally studied by mycologists are NOT true fungi but are historically important in mycology. Kingdom Straminipila includes:

- **Oomycota**: Water molds including *Phytophthora* species; *Phytophthora infestans* caused the Irish potato famine. Oomycetes are among the most destructive plant pathogens known.
- **Hyphochytriomycota**: Small group of aquatic organisms
- **Labyrinthulomycota**: Including thraustochytrids, marine organisms of ecological and biotechnological interest

These groups are characterized by heterokont flagellation (two unequal flagella, one with tripartite tubular hairs). See [[myxomycetes-slime-molds]] for further detail.

## Classification Challenges and Future Directions

The tension between classification stability and phylogenetic accuracy continues to challenge mycologists. Some researchers advocate using clade names rather than formal taxon designations until relationships are better resolved. Taxonomic coverage across higher taxa is highly uneven, with most molecular studies focusing on easily cultured or economically important species. As DNA barcoding and environmental sequencing become routine, the gap between described and actual diversity will likely narrow dramatically.

## Merged: Tulasne Brothers
Louis-Rene Tulasne (1815-1885) and Charles Tulasne (1816-1884) were two French bachelor brothers who revolutionized mycology with their recognition that individual fungi could produce different types of spores in different phases of their life cycle -- a phenomenon termed pleomorphy.
