# Entheogenic Knowledge and Secret Societies

The theory that secret societies and fraternal orders have preserved — and suppressed — knowledge of entheogenic substances is a recurring theme in alternative scholarship on religion and pharmacology. Proponents argue that institutions including Freemasonry, the Catholic Church, and various political hierarchies have systematically controlled access to consciousness-altering plants and fungi to maintain power over populations.

## Historical Suppression of Entheogenic Knowledge

The campaign against entheogenic practice is traced by James Arthur and others to the early consolidation of religious and political power. - **The [[solanaceous-hallucinogens-of-medieval-european-witchcraft]] craft guilds. Several features of medieval guilds are consistent with the preservation of esoteric knowledge:

- **Guild secrecy:** Craft guilds maintained strict secrecy about their methods, techniques, and trade knowledge. Guild members took oaths binding them to silence, and revealing guild secrets could result in expulsion or worse.
- **Herbal knowledge:** Apothecaries, herbalists, and brewers' guilds all possessed extensive knowledge of psychoactive plants. Guild control of this knowledge created a de facto monopoly on pharmacological expertise.
- **Masonic evolution:** Freemasonry itself evolved from operative stonemason guilds into a speculative philosophical society. The transition from practical craft secrets to symbolic knowledge may have provided cover for preserving older, pre-Christian botanical and pharmacological traditions.
- **Witchcraft accusations:** Many individuals accused of witchcraft during the medieval and early modern periods were herbalists, midwives, and folk healers — precisely the people most likely to possess knowledge of psychoactive plants. The witch hunts may have served to eliminate non-guild holders of pharmacological knowledge.

## From Matriarchal to Patriarchal Control

A key element of the suppression narrative involves the transition from matriarchal to patriarchal social structures. Entheogenic scholars argue that early Goddess-worshipping cultures, in which women often served as priestesses and healers with direct access to entheogenic plants, were systematically overthrown by male-dominated religious and political hierarchies. The new patriarchal order reclassified entheogenic knowledge as forbidden — connecting it to the biblical narrative of the fruit of the Tree of Knowledge, denied to humanity by a jealous deity.

This transition is dated to approximately eight thousand years ago and is associated with the rise of Mesopotamian civilizations where, according to the narrative, priestly classes monopolized both political power and restricted pharmacological knowledge while the general population was kept in subservient roles.

## Modern Fraternal Orders

Several modern fraternal and initiatory organizations have been connected to entheogenic knowledge preservation, though evidence remains largely circumstantial:

- **The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn:** A late 19th-century occult society that drew on Kabbalistic, alchemical, and Hermetic traditions. Some scholars note that alchemical texts contain plant and fungal symbolism that could encode entheogenic knowledge.
- **The Rosicrucians:** Their manifestos (published 1614-1616) describe the recovery of ancient secret knowledge. Whether this knowledge includes entheogenic pharmacology remains speculative.
