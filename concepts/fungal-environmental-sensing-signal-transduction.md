# Environmental Sensing and Signal Transduction in Filamentous Fungi

Filamentous fungi have evolved sophisticated systems for detecting and
responding to environmental signals. Because they are both non-motile
and heterotrophic, fungi must rapidly sense and adapt to the
heterogeneous microenvironments through which their hyphae grow. This
sensing capacity underpins all aspects of [[symbiotic-fungi-endophytic-fungi-in-plants]] and fungal-quorum-sensing-autoregulation mechanisms.

## Autoregulatory Signals

Autoregulators are the least understood class of fungal signals but play
critical roles in colony organization. During germination, some act as
self-inhibitors while others influence germ-tube emergence patterns.

## Signal Transduction Machinery

Genomic analysis of Neurospora crassa, the first sequenced filamentous
fungus, revealed a signal transduction apparatus of remarkable
complexity. Compared with Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Neurospora possesses
expanded upstream sensory proteins with conserved downstream components,
suggesting extensive networking and novel signaling interactions not
found in yeasts — a theme also relevant to fungal-environmental-sensing systems reflects
the evolutionary pressure faced by organisms that must locate and
exploit patchily distributed resources across complex soil
environments without the benefit of motility. Signal transduction
networks in fungi rival those of higher eukaryotes in their
complexity and integration capacity.
