## Conditional Truth vs. Unconditional Truth

Fukuoka draws a sharp distinction between two types of truth:

**Conditional truth** is what scientific farming produces. It depends on specific experimental conditions, assumptions about time and space, and the prior framework of the researcher. It is always subject to revision and cannot be generalized beyond its narrow parameters.

**Unconditional truth** is [[cannabis-chemical-fertilizers]], preparing compost, and spraying pesticides. This was not a series of independent discoveries but a single intuitive insight that revealed the interconnected nature of all farming practices.

## The Holistic Principle: One Thing Is All Things

Central to Fukuoka's epistemology is the principle that all aspects of agriculture are organically interrelated. In rice cultivation, preparing the field, sowing the seed, tilling, covering the seed with soil, fertilizing, weeding, and disease and pest control are all organically interrelated. No problem in any one area is truly solved unless a common solution is found for all areas.

Scientific farming divides rice cultivation into narrow specialties: experts on germination attend to problems of seed germination, specialists in tillage address tilling problems, and likewise with seeding, transplanting, and other areas. Solving these independently is totally meaningless. Natural farming treats everything as part of a whole.

## The Problem of Causal Attribution

Fukuoka repeatedly warns against arbitrarily settling upon causal relationships based on subjective human notions. When the first plant dies in a land turning to desert, it may have been triggered by a microorganism. But botanists rarely study microorganisms because they lie outside their field of specialization. The chain of interwoven events and factors is beyond man's comprehension.

Similarly, when farmers in Kochi concentrated all efforts on eradicating the visible rice borer pest, they doubled back before finding the true cause. The connection between rice stubble and chaotic planting practices was never clearly established, and the deeper reasons why yellow rice borers were numerous in the region remained uninvestigated. There must have been other reasons why the yellow rice borer, rice stem borer, and other insect pests were so numerous in Kochi Prefecture. The cause had less to do with the environment than with poor methods of cultivating rice.

## The Maze of Relative Subjectivity

Fukuoka extends his critique to the fundamental nature of human perception. Even nature stripped of all human action and influence is still only nature as seen through man's relativity, a nature clothed in the subjective notions of man.
