# Psilocybe quebecensis Profile

## Taxonomy and Discovery

Psilocybe quebecensis Ola'h and Heim is a moderately active
[[emcdda-psilocybin-non-addictive-profile-tolerance-dynamics]] first described by Ola'h and Heim in 1967.
The species was named for the Canadian province of Quebec, where it
was originally discovered in the Jacques Cartier river valley. It
belongs to the genus [[stamets-psi-psilocybe-baeocystis-knobby-tops]] for related
[[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] species with similar ecological niches.

## Macroscopic Features

### Cap

The cap measures 1 to 3.5 cm broad. It is nearly hemispheric at
first, soon expanding to convex, then becoming broadly convex to
plane without an umbo. The margin is incurved at first and usually
not markedly undulated. It is translucent-striate when moist. The
cap color is pale straw yellow and often has brownish or tawny
hues, becoming more grayish in drying. The cap bruises bluish
when touched or disturbed, an important field characteristic
related to tropisms-psilocybe-cubensis-gravitropism.

### Stem

The stem measures 20 to 35 mm long, occasionally reaching 45 mm,
by 1 to 2.5 mm thick. It is brittle, tough, and fibrous. The stem
is slightly enlarged at the apex and flared at the base, which is
often swollen and furnished with rhizomorphs. Coloration is
yellowish tawny, drying to a distinct straw yellow, becoming
bluish where bruised. The partial veil is cortinate, fugacious,
and soon disappearing. The presence of rhizomorphs at the base is
a notable feature that aids in field identification.

## Microscopic Features

Spores are purplish brown to black in deposit, ellipsoid to
subovoid in side and face view, with some spores described as
mango-shaped, measuring 8 to 14 (occasionally 16) by 6 to 8.8
micrometers. The basidia are 4-spored. Pleurocystidia are present,
measuring 12 to 35 by 9 to 15 micrometers, and are very
distinctive by their swollen apices, similar to those seen in
[[stamets-psi-psilocybe-cyanescens-species-guide]]. Cheilocystidia
measure 18 to 36 by 5.5 to 10 micrometers and are
fusoid-ampullaceous with an extended neck approximately 2 to 8
micrometers thick.

## Habitat and Distribution

Psilocybe quebecensis grows in sandy soils, particularly in
outwashes of streams, and in the decayed wood substratum of alder,
birch, fir, and spruce. It fruits in the late summer and fall. The
species was originally reported from Quebec, specifically in the
Jacques Cartier river valley. As a flood-plain specialist, P.
quebecensis is well adapted to riparian habitats where periodic
flooding deposits organic material and creates the moist, woody
substrates this species prefers.

## Chemical Constituents and Potency

Psilocybe quebecensis is classified as moderately active according
to Ola'h and Heim (1967). The [[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]] upon bruising
confirms the presence of psilocin, which is consistent with
moderate activity levels. Exact quantitative analyses of psilocybin
and psilocin content do not appear in the literature reviewed by
Stamets, making this one of the less chemically characterized
active Psilocybes. The distinctive pleurocystidia with swollen
apices suggest a close phylogenetic relationship to more thoroughly
studied species like P. cyanescens and P. cubensis.
