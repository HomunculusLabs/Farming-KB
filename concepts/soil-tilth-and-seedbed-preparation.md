# Soil Tilth and Seedbed Preparation

## Understanding Soil Tilth

Soil tilth refers to the physical condition of soil in relation to its suitability for growing plants. Good tilth means soil has a [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]], called [[composting-systems]].

### For the Seedbed Only

Fortunately, it is not necessary to turn the entire garden into the highest-quality topsoil to get a good result. The focus should be on the "seedbed" — the top inch or two of soil where seeds are placed.

Most small garden seeds are sown about one-quarter to one-half inch (six to twelve millimeters) deep. A few larger seeds, such as beans and spinach, are sown about three-quarters of an inch (two centimeters) deep. Creating excellent tilth in only this top layer requires a thin layer of manure or compost only a quarter inch (six millimeters) thick.

One pickup truck load of compost would cover fourteen 100-square-foot growing beds a quarter inch deep — a manageable amount even if it must be transported with a wheelbarrow.

## The Four-in-One Raking Technique

Once you have your bed covered with a quarter inch of well-rotted manure or compost, use a bow rake to accomplish four things simultaneously:

1. **Level the surface precisely** — creating a uniform seedbed
2. **Uniformly blend the manure/compost into the top inch** — through the action of the rake's teeth passing through the soil repeatedly
3. **Break up less solidly cemented clods** — the rake's teeth fracture weak aggregates
4. **Comb out lumps** — resistant lumps are pulled to the edge of the bed where they roll down the side

### The Bow Rake

The bow rake is essential for creating seedbeds. Key requirements:

- Head should be at least 12 inches (30 cm) wide for efficiency
- Teeth should be about 2 inches (5 cm) long
- Handle should be long and slender to reduce arm fatigue
- Avoid narrow-headed child rakes and overly wide (>16 inch) rakes

### Raking Technique

There is a quality to raking that is not unlike what athletes call "being in the zone." Raking is a meditative action that improves with practice over many years. The weight that the rake's head puts on the bed must be carefully controlled so the teeth comb out the lumps without digging in too deeply. Your complete attention must be on where the teeth are combing through the soil.

## Seed Germination Requirements

Understanding seedbed preparation requires understanding what seeds need to germinate:

### Moisture

When a seed is moistened, the embryo starts producing complex chemical substances that convert the stored food surrounding it into water-soluble sugars and amino acids — essentially digestion. Because drying out is one of the biggest dangers, the first thing a clever embryo does is send down a root. Once the root system is working, there is less chance the seed will die from lack of moisture.

### Oxygen

A sprouting seedling needs significantly more oxygen than it did in dormancy. The embryo will die if there is no oxygen in the soil, which is what happens when soil is waterlogged for more than a few hours. This is why overwatering kills seeds — not drowning, but suffocation.

### Temperature

The speed at which the embryo grows and develops is determined by temperature. If the soil is too cold, the seedling is slowed by the chill and becomes vulnerable to fungi and other soil-dwelling diseases that thrive in cold conditions. Watering lowers soil temperature, providing another reason to avoid overwatering.
