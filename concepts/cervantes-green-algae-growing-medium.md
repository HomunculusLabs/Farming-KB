# Green Algae in Cannabis Gardens

## Overview

Green algae are simple photosynthetic organisms that need
nutrients, light, and a moist surface on which to grow. In
cannabis gardens, they are found growing on moist
[[cervantes-rockwool-growing-substrate]] and other
growing mediums exposed to light. While algae cause little
direct damage to cannabis plants, they create secondary problems
that can significantly impact plant health and yield.

## Identification

Green algae appear as a slimy green film or layer on exposed
moist growing surfaces. They colonize the top surface of
rockwool cubes and slabs, nutrient solution reservoirs,
hydroponic channels, and any moist surface where light reaches
the growing medium. In severe cases, the green coating can be
quite thick and may produce an unpleasant stagnant odor.

## Direct vs. Indirect Damage

Algae themselves cause little direct damage to cannabis plants.
They do not attack roots, foliage, or flowers. However, their
presence creates several important indirect problems:

- Algae attract [[cervantes-fungus-gnats]] and
  other small insects that lay eggs in the moist algae layer.
  Fungus gnat larvae then feed on cannabis root hairs and
  scar larger roots, creating entry points for root diseases
- Once roots have lesions and abrasions from insect feeding,
  diseases including [[cervantes-root-rot-pythium-damping-off]]
  and [[cervantes-fusarium-vascular-wilt-diseases]]
  enter easily through the wounds
- Algae compete with cannabis plants for nutrients in the
  growing medium and nutrient solution
- Thick algae growth can create anaerobic zones in the growing
  medium that promote harmful bacterial activity
- Algae can clog drip emitters, irrigation lines, and pumps in
  hydroponic systems

## Prevention

Prevention is straightforward and highly effective:

- Cover exposed moist rockwool and growing mediums to exclude
  light. Algae cannot grow without light
- Use light-blocking covers on rockwool slabs and cubes
- Keep nutrient reservoirs covered and light-tight
- Ensure irrigation lines and channels are opaque
- Avoid overwatering that keeps the medium surface constantly
  wet
- Use a layer of dry growing medium on top of moist zones to
  block light from reaching moisture

## Control Methods

When algae are already established, several control options
are available:

- Physical removal: Scrape or wipe away algae from surfaces.
  This provides temporary relief but does not address the
  underlying cause of light exposure to moist surfaces
- Algaecides: Run an algaecide in the nutrient solution or
  water. Commercial algaecides are available at hydroponic
  supply stores
- Hydrogen peroxide: A dilute solution of
  [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] can help control algae and simultaneously
  oxygenate the root zone
- Reduce nutrient concentration slightly: Algae need nutrients
  to grow, and reducing available nutrients can slow their
  proliferation
- UV sterilization: Some growers use UV sterilizers on
  recirculating nutrient solutions to control algae and
  pathogens

## Algae in Hydroponic Systems

Algae are particularly problematic in [[cervantes-passive-wick-hydroponic-systems]] where nutrient-rich water is constantly exposed to
light. In [[cervantes-nft-aeroponic-systems]],
algae can grow in channels and on root mats exposed through
channel openings. In [[cervantes-water-culture-systems]], reservoir surfaces exposed to light grow
algae quickly.

The key to algae prevention in all hydroponic systems is
ensuring that nutrient solution is never exposed to light
while it remains in contact with the system. Light-proof
reservoirs, opaque tubing, and covered channels eliminate
the algae problem entirely.

## Relationship to Fungus Gnats

The connection between algae and [[cervantes-fungus-gnats]] is one of the most significant reasons to control algae.
Adult fungus gnats are attracted to the moist algae environment
to lay eggs. The larvae that hatch feed on algae initially but
quickly move to cannabis roots when algae are consumed. Root
feeding creates wounds that allow root rot pathogens to enter,
establishing a cascade of problems that began with simple algae
growth.
