# Hypocreales Mycoparasites

The order Hypocreales is one of the most important groups of fungicolous fungi, encompassing the families Hypocreaceae, Nectriaceae, Bionectriaceae, Clavicipitaceae, and Niessliaceae. A large proportion of hypocrealean fungi are mycoparasitic or mycosaprotrophic, a fact often underrecognized. They are extremely versatile in their abilities to exploit fungal substrata.

## Hypocreaceae

### Hypocrea and Trichoderma

The genus *Hypocrea* is reported less frequently as a mycoparasite than its anamorphs in *Trichoderma*, but includes fungicolous species that are either host-specific or occur on various aphyllophorales in addition to woody substrata. Several *Trichoderma* species are polyphagous mycoparasites that are notoriously difficult to distinguish morphologically, even using refined criteria.

**Trichoderma harzianum** serves as both a beneficial biocontrol agent of plant pathogens and, in genetically distinct strains, an aggressive competitor of the cultivated mushroom. Accurate strain reference in experiments is therefore crucial. *T. harzianum* produces a volatile alkyl pyrone identified as a fungal inhibitor (Claydon et al. 1987).

**Trichoderma virens** (formerly classified in *Gliocladium*) was redescribed and is best classified as *T. aggressivum* fide Bissett (1991b) and Rehner & Samuels (1994). It produces **gliotoxin**, which has a very specific fungitoxic effect.

### Peptaibols and Secondary Metabolites

Trichoderma species produce a great diversity of secondary metabolites that, beyond their enzymatic actions, antagonize host fungi over some distance, causing cell vacuolization, collapse, and disintegration of the cytoplasm. **Peptaibols** are a class of potent polypeptide antibiotics commonly found in *Trichoderma*, *Gliocladium*, and *Clonostachys*. The often-observed bursting of hyphal tips induced by Trichoderma has been ascribed to such compounds.

### Mycoparasitic Interaction Stages

The interaction between *T. harzianum* and *Lentinula edodes* on agar involves three stages:

1. **Parasitic contact** — direct physical contact with host hyphae
2. **Direct penetration** of host cells
3. **Production of diffusible toxic agents** prior to physical contact

### Hypomyces

The genus *Hypomyces* comprises the most characteristic mycoparasites on macromycetes. It is heterogeneous, with two main ecological groups:

- **Systemic colonizers** — colonize host sporocarps systemically, are not known to have an anamorph, and produce ascospores that do not germinate in vitro
- **Sporocarp decay agents** — cause decay of sporocarps at various developmental stages, form copious anamorphs, and grow easily in culture

Species can be grouped by host type: discomycetes, boletes, aphyllophorales, or agarics. The largest number (23 species) parasitizes aphyllophorales and are usually not host-specific. Species parasitizing boletes or agarics tend to be specific to a host family or genus.

### Cladobotryum Anamorphs

Rogerson & Samuels (1993) combined most anamorphs of agaricicolous and aphyllophoricolous *Hypomyces* into the expanded anamorph genus *Cladobotryum*, including species of *Arnoldiomyces*, *Eurasina*, *Helminthophora*, *Pseudohansfordia*, *Sibirina*, *Sympodiophora*, and part of *Trichothecium*. Chlamydosporic or aleurial synanamorphs often are formed and classified in *Sepedonium*, *Mycogone*, and *Blastotrichum*.

### Other Hypocreaceae Genera

- **Hypocreopsis** — species are fungicolous but cannot be cultured; *H. lichenoides* occurs with *Hymenochaete* on tree bark, *H. xylariicola* on old *Xylaria* perithecia
- **Sporophagomyces** — characterized by a subiculum that traps host basidiospores (mainly Ganodermataceae), which serve as nutrients
- **Sphaerostilbella** (anamorph *Gliocladium* sensu stricto) — grows mainly on *Stereum* species
- **Arachnocrea**, **Protocrea**, and **Podostroma** — also include fungicolous species

### Mycotrophein

**Mycotrophein** is a growth factor found in extracts of various fungal cultures, consisting of a mixture of tetraethyleneglycol and pentaethyleneglycol mono-(nonylphenyl) ethers. It may act as a carrier of a biologically active factor. Many obligate mycoparasites can be grown axenically on media supplemented with thiamine, biotin, and fungal extracts containing mycotrophein.

### Biotrophic Contact Mycoparasites

Several well-studied species deserve mention:

- **Gonatobotrys simplex** (teleomorph *Melanospora damnosa*) — grows on *Alternaria* and *Cladosporium*, contacts host via fingerlike branches
- **Gonatobotryum fuscum** — grows on *Polyporus*, *Poria*, *Ganoderma*, *Tremella*, *Leptographium*, *Hypocrea*, and *Hypomyces*
- **Hansfordia parasitica** — unusual in developing 0.2–1.0-mm-wide pores at the point of host contact; has a unique mutualistic relationship with *Graphium fuscum* (parasite produces pyridoxine, host produces biotin)
- **Stephanoma phaeosporum** — biotrophic contact mycoparasite of *Fusarium* and 14 other fungi

## Nectriaceae

The genus *Nectria* was subdivided into several distinct genera. **Cosmospora** (~60 species, formerly *Nectria* subgenus *Dialonectria*) generally are fungicolous. The most conspicuous species occur on carbonized perithecia of pyrenomycetes, notably members of the Xylariales and Diatrypales. A few species are restricted to certain hosts: *C. leptosphaeriae* mainly on *Lepidosphaeria* and *C. coccinea* on polypores.

## Bionectriaceae

Centered around *Bionectria* (formerly *Nectria ochroleuca* group), this family comprises destructive fungicolous species with anamorphs in *Clonostachys* (*Gliocladium* sensu lato), particularly *C. rosea* and related species. Species with *Sesquicillium* anamorphs also are classified here. **Pronectria** (14 species) is mostly lichenicolous.

**Nectriopsis** includes 39 species that grow on myxomycetes, pyrenomycetes, loculoascomycetes, discomycetes, and lichens. White to yellow perithecia are commonly observed in fungicolous taxa.

## Clavicipitaceae

While numerous *Cordyceps* species are entomogenous, three commonly grow on *Elaphomyces* species. **Lecanicillium** (formerly *Verticillium* sect. *Prostrata*) includes species with high chitinase activity and great potential as entomogenous, nematophagous, and fungicolous biocontrol agents.

## Niessliaceae

Although mostly saprotrophic, some species are fungicolous, particularly *Monocillium* anamorphs of *Niesslia*. *Circinoniesslia* and *Valetoniella* have been found only on perithecia of *Nectria* species. *Trichosphaeriella decipiens* is mostly reported on sporocarps of aphyllophorales. Three *Niesslia* species are lichenicolous.

## Biocontrol Significance

Hypocrealean mycoparasites are among the most important biological control agents against plant-pathogenic fungi. *Trichoderma harzianum* and *T. virens* are widely used commercially. Their success derives from:

- Polyphagous host range
- Production of cell-wall-degrading enzymes (chitinases, glucanases)
- Antibiotic metabolites (peptaibols, gliotoxin, volatile pyrones)
- Rapid mycelial growth and competitive colonization
- Ability to induce systemic resistance in plants
