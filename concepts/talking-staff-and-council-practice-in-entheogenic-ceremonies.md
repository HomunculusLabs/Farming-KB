## Contrast with Western Group Psychotherapy

Metzner specifically contrasts the talking staff format with "the usual group psychotherapy
formats." The differences are fundamental and reveal the deep structural divide between the
psychotherapeutic and shamanic paradigms. In group psychotherapy, participants are
encouraged to react to and interpret each other's contributions; the therapist actively
facilitates discussion and guides interpretation; the goal is verbal insight and cognitive
restructuring; multiple conversations can occur simultaneously. In the talking staff
council, each person speaks in isolation, heard but not responded to; the guide may hold
the staff and sing but does not interpret others' experiences; the goal is direct experience
and nonverbal integration; only one voice is active at any time. Psychotherapy assumes that
verbal processing is the primary mechanism of healing. Shamanic practice assumes that direct
experience, received in silence and held in the body, is the primary mechanism.

## Complementary Pre-Ceremony Practices

Metzner describes additional pre-ceremony practices that complement the talking staff
intention-sharing circle. These include meditation, solo time in nature, and expressive arts
modalities such as drawing, painting, or journal work. These practices deepen the
preparatory process and give participants additional tools for engaging with the entheogenic
experience. The combination of verbal intention-setting through the staff and nonverbal
preparation through meditation and art creates a multi-modal readiness that addresses both
the conscious and unconscious dimensions of the upcoming journey.

## Integration Phase

After the circle ritual, the talking staff returns for what Metzner calls an "integration
practice of some kind." Participants share something of the lessons learned and how they
might be applied in their lives. This integration phase is crucial: without it, the insights
of the entheogenic experience may dissipate without being translated into lasting change.
Research on psychedelic therapy consistently shows that the integration period is as
important as the experience itself in determining long-term outcomes. The talking staff
format ensures that integration sharing, like the journey itself, is received without
judgment or interpretation. The insights need time to settle, to deepen, to connect with the
fabric of daily life.

## Participant Profile in Hybrid Circles

Metzner notes that most participants in the hybrid circles he observed were experienced in
one or more psychospiritual practices, including shamanic drum journeying, Buddhist vipassana
meditation, tantra yoga, and holotropic breathwork, and most had experienced various forms
of psychotherapy and body-oriented therapy. The insights from these practices are woven by
participants into their work with the entheogenic medicines. Participants tend to confirm
that the entheogenic plant medicines, when combined with meditative or therapeutic insight
processes, function to amplify awareness and sensitize perception, particularly amplifying
somatic, emotional, and instinctual awareness.
