## The Science: How Fungi Degrade Toxins

The powerful enzymes secreted by certain fungi digest lignin and
cellulose -- the primary structural components of wood. These same
enzymes break down many recalcitrant toxins with chemical bonds similar
to those in wood. Mushrooms are classified into two key groups:

**Brown rot fungi** (about 7 percent of mushrooms): Break down white
cellulose, leaving brownish lignin. Examples include agarikon
(Fomitopsis officinalis), sulphur tufts (Laetiporus), and velvet
polypore (Phaeolus schweinitzii).

**White rot fungi** (more numerous): Break down recalcitrant brown
fiber, leaving cellulose intact. Key [[fungal-bioremediation-mechanisms]] are evident in cost
comparisons for petroleum hydrocarbon remediation:

- Mycoremediation: approximately $50 per ton
- Bioremediation (bacterial): ~$100-200 per ton
- Phytoremediation: ~$200-400 per ton
- Soil washing: ~$200-400 per ton
- Soil venting: ~$400-600 per ton
- Indirect thermal: ~$600-800 per ton
- Solidification: ~$800-1,000 per ton
- Incineration: ~$1,200-1,600 per ton

## Multi-Kingdom Approach

Stamets advocates a multi-kingdom sequence for decontamination:

1. Fungi (vanguard species like oyster mushrooms)
2. Vertebrates and invertebrates (insects attracted to mushrooms)
3. Bacteria (fed by rotting mushrooms)
4. Plants (enabled by bacterial nutrient release)
5. Vertebrates and invertebrates return
6. Cycle repeats with new fungal populations
