# Nonsystemic Endophytic Fungi of Nongrass Hosts
Nonsystemic endophytic fungi colonize plant tissues internally
without causing visible disease symptoms, in contrast to the
well-known [[fungal-biodiversity-marine-ecosystems-coastal-deep-sea]].
They are primarily Ascomycota and their anamorphic states.

## Definition and Scope

Nonsystemic endophytes are defined as fungi that:
- Colonize internal plant tissues (leaves, stems, roots,
  bark, and reproductive structures)
- Spend a significant portion of their life cycle within
  plant tissues without causing apparent harm
- Are horizontally transmitted (between individuals) rather
  than vertically transmitted (through seeds)
- Belong predominantly to the Ascomycota and their
  anamorphs, with some Basidiomycota
The distinction from [[rust-fungi-biodiversity-life-cycles]] and [[clavicipitaceous-grass-endophytes]]. Examples include:
- Endophytic Fusarium species producing fusaric acid
  derivatives in some host plants
- Various endophytes producing antifungal and
  antibacterial compounds that protect against pathogens

### Pathogen Antagonism

Endophytes can protect hosts from pathogens through several
mechanisms:
- Competition for space and nutrients within host tissues
- Production of antifungal compounds
- Induction of host defense responses (priming)
- Physical barriers created by endophyte hyphae

### Stress Tolerance

Some endophytes confer enhanced tolerance to environmental
stresses including drought, heat, and heavy metals. The
best-studied examples are from grass systems, but similar
effects have been documented in nongrass hosts.

## Isolation and Study Methods

Standard methods for studying nonsystematic endophytes
include:
- **Surface sterilization** of plant tissues (sequential
  washes in ethanol and sodium hypochlorite) to eliminate
  epiphytic fungi
