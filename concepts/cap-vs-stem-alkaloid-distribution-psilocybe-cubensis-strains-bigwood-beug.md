# Cap vs Stem Alkaloid Distribution in Psilocybe Cubensis Strains (Bigwood & Beug)

## Overview

The 1982 Bigwood and Beug study included a detailed analysis of [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] distribution between the cap (pileus) and stem (stipe) of Psilocybe cubensis fruiting bodies. This analysis was conducted on three different strains — M.R., Equadorian, and Amazon — all cultivated on rye-grain substrate and harvested from the first flush. The results revealed consistent patterns as well as intriguing strain-specific differences in how alkaloids are distributed within the mushroom.

## The Three Strains Analyzed

### Amazon Strain

The Amazon strain was the primary study strain, originating from a [[spore-print]] collected near Pucallpa, Peru (Repke et al., 1977). This strain was used for the multi-flush variation study and was also included in the cap-versus-stem analysis.

### M.R. Strain

The M.R. strain was obtained from an outside cultivator. Its specific geographic origin is not detailed in the study, but it was cultivated under the same rye-grain miniculture conditions as the Amazon strain.

### Equadorian Strain

The Equadorian strain was also obtained from an outside source. Like the M.R. strain, it was grown on rye-grain substrate under controlled conditions for comparison purposes.

## Cap vs Stem Distribution Data

All three strains were analyzed from first-flush harvests, with caps and stems separated and analyzed independently:

| Strain | Tissue | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|--------|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| M.R.   | Caps   | 8.7               | 0               |
| M.R.   | Stems  | 4.2               | 0.35            |
| Equadorian | Caps | 7.6            | 0               |
| Equadorian | Stems | 4.7            | 0.4             |
| Amazon | Caps   | 5.7               | 0.1             |
| Amazon | Stems  | 5.7               | 0               |

## Psilocybin Distribution Pattern

### M.R. and Equadorian Strains: Cap Concentration

In both the M.R. and Equadorian strains, caps contained approximately **twice as much psilocybin** as stems. The M.R. strain showed 8.7 mg/g in caps versus 4.2 mg/g in stems (a 2.07:1 ratio), while the Equadorian strain showed 7.6 mg/g in caps versus 4.7 mg/g in stems (a 1.62:1 ratio).

This cap-concentration pattern is consistent with general mycological understanding: the cap is the reproductive structure of the mushroom and tends to concentrate [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]], including the [[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]] that may serve defensive or signaling functions in the fruiting body.

### Amazon Strain: Equal Distribution

The Amazon strain showed a notably different pattern, with **equal psilocybin levels** in both caps and stems (5.7 mg/g each). This deviation from the cap-concentration pattern is significant because it suggests that the distribution of alkaloids within the fruiting body is not a fixed biological rule but varies depending on the genetic characteristics of the strain.

The equal distribution in the Amazon strain could reflect several factors:

- **Genetic variation**: Different strains may express different enzyme distributions or transport mechanisms that affect where alkaloids accumulate.
- **Morphological differences**: The Amazon strain may have different cap-to-stem proportions or tissue densities that affect alkaloid concentration per gram.
- **Developmental timing**: The Amazon strain may reach peak alkaloid levels at a different point in fruiting body development than the other two strains.

## Psilocin Distribution Pattern

### M.R. and Equadorian Strains: Stem-Only Psilocin

In both the M.R. and Equadorian strains, the small amount of psilocin present was **entirely in the stems**. Caps contained zero detectable psilocin in these two strains, while stems contained 0.35 mg/g (M.R.) and 0.4 mg/g (Equadorian).

This stem-localized psilocin pattern is unexpected and not easily explained by conventional biosynthetic models. Psilocin is the dephosphorylated form of psilocybin, and its presence in the stem but not the cap suggests that the enzymatic conversion of psilocybin to psilocin may occur preferentially in stem tissue, or that psilocin may be transported to the stem after formation.

### Amazon Strain: Cap-Only Psilocin (Trace)

The Amazon strain showed the opposite pattern from the other two strains: a trace amount of psilocin (0.1 mg/g) was found in the caps, while stems contained zero detectable psilocin. This reversal of the distribution pattern reinforces the finding that alkaloid distribution is strain-dependent rather than following a universal rule.

## Biosynthetic Implications

### Psilocybin-Psilocin Conversion

The strain-dependent distribution of psilocin raises questions about the biosynthetic relationship between psilocybin and psilocin in different tissues:

- **Enzyme localization**: The enzyme(s) responsible for dephosphorylating psilocybin to psilocin may be differentially expressed in different tissues depending on the strain.
- **Transport mechanisms**: Psilocin produced in one tissue may be transported to another tissue for storage or metabolism, with the direction of transport varying by strain.
- **Degradation pathways**: The absence of psilocin in certain tissues could reflect rapid degradation rather than lack of synthesis.

### First-Flush Context

All three strains were analyzed from first-flush harvests, a context in which psilocin levels are typically at their lowest. The study's multi-flush data showed that psilocin is generally absent or minimal in the first flush and increases in subsequent flushes. If these strains had been analyzed from third or fourth flushes, the psilocin [[fungal-biogeography-distribution-patterns]] might have been more pronounced and potentially different.
