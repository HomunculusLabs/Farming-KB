# Fungal Decomposition of Saltmarsh Cordgrass — *Spartina alterniflora*

## Overview

Fungi are the primary decomposers of standing-dead shoots of smooth cordgrass (*[[spartina-alterniflora]]*) in saltmarsh ecosystems. A specialized community of ascomycetes — dominated by *Phaeosphaeria spartinicola*, *Phaeosphaeria halima*, and *Mycosphaerella* sp.2 — colonizes decaying cordgrass blades and drives lignocellulose breakdown through mechanisms analogous to both white rot and soft rot decay. These fungi play a critical role in carbon and nutrient cycling in coastal wetland ecosystems.

## The Cordgrass Decomposer Community

### Dominant Species

Three ascomycete species are the major members of the smooth-cordgrass shoot-decomposer mycobiota:

| Species | Notes |
|---|---|
| *Phaeosphaeria spartinicola* | Primary decomposer of standing-dead blades |
| *Phaeosphaeria halima* | Co-dominant with *P. spartinicola* |
| *Mycosphaerella* sp.2 | Third major decomposer (Kohlmeyer & Kohlmeyer, 1979) |

### Identification Methods

- **Direct observation** of ascomata (fruiting bodies) on decaying blades.
- **Capture of expelled ascospores** from standing-decaying blades.
- Percentage area of decaying blades occupied by at least one species can be extensive.

## Lignocellulose Decomposition

### Decay Types

Cordgrass ascomycetes bring about **two types of soft rot**:

- **Type 1 soft rot**: Cavitation within the secondary cell wall.
- **Type 2 soft rot**: Very similar to the **white rot** of basidiomycetes — includes lignin degradation.

This was confirmed by both:
1. **In vitro experiments**: Incubation with radiolabelled, extracted cordgrass lignocellulose (LC) showed >20% mineralization after 6 weeks at ~23°C, with the rate not declining at that time point.
2. **Direct electron microscopy**: Examination of changes in LC of smooth-cordgrass fibre tissue during natural ascomycetous decay.

### Laccase Genes

The chapter on laccase genes in saltmarsh decomposition examines:

- The role of **laccases** (multi-copper oxidase enzymes) in lignin breakdown by cordgrass ascomycetes.
- Laccases catalyze the oxidation of phenolic compounds in lignin.
- The expression of laccase genes during standing-decay of *Spartina* blades.
- Comparison of laccase gene families between cordgrass ascomycetes and wood-decay basidiomycetes.

## Ecological Context

### Standing-Dead Decomposition

- *Spartina alterniflora* shoots die back annually but often remain **standing** (not washed away) for extended periods.
- Decomposition of standing-dead shoots is a **fungal-dominated process** — bacteria contribute less.
- This is distinct from the decomposition of detached litter in the sediment, which involves both fungi and bacteria.

### Carbon Cycling

- Microbial secondary production from saltmarsh-grass shoots represents a significant carbon flux.
- Fungal biomass production and respiration contribute to the carbon budget of saltmarsh ecosystems.
- The known and potential fates of this fungal production include:
  - Consumption by detritivorous invertebrates.
  - Incorporation into sediment organic matter.
  - Respiration as CO₂.

## Comparison with Other Decomposition Systems

| Feature | Saltmarsh Cordgrass | Forest Wood | Leaf Litter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary decomposers | Ascomycetes | Basidiomycetes | Mixed fungi/bacteria |
| Decay type | Soft rot (types 1 & 2) | White/brown rot | Primarily soft rot |
| Substrate | Graminoid (grass) | Lignin-rich wood | Mixed lignocellulose |
| Environment | Intertidal, saline | Terrestrial, humid | Terrestrial |
| Lignin degradation | Yes (type 2 soft rot) | Major pathway | Variable |

## Significance

### Ecosystem Function

- [[fungal-decomposition]] of standing-dead *Spartina* is a **rate-limiting step** in saltmarsh nutrient cycling.
- Releases nitrogen and phosphorus bound in plant tissue.
- Provides food resources for detritivorous food webs.

### Biogeochemical Importance

- Saltmarshes are among the most productive ecosystems per unit area.
- Fungal-mediated decomposition determines how much of this production enters detrital food webs versus being buried as refractory organic matter.
- Implications for **blue carbon** storage in coastal ecosystems.
