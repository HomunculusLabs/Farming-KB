# Cannabis Safety Profile and Risk Assessment

Extensive evidence indicates that cannabis is neither dangerous nor
harmless. The safety profile of cannabis must be evaluated through a
risk-versus-benefit analysis focused on the specific therapeutic needs and
health challenges of the individual. Compared to commonly used substances
and FDA-approved pharmaceutical alternatives, cannabis presents a
distinctive safety profile that warrants careful examination.

## Fatal Overdose Risk

The risk of fatal overdose from cannabis is extraordinarily low. A 1973
laboratory study reported the median lethal dose of oral THC in rats as
800-1,900 mg/kg. Extrapolated to humans by body weight, this suggests
approximately 200 grams of herb per kilogram of body weight would be
needed to approach a lethal dose. A person weighing 70 kg (154 lbs) would
need to consume roughly 14 kg of cannabis herb.

A more conservative 2004 study estimated that "628 kg of cannabis would
have to be smoked in 15 minutes to induce a lethal effect." This physical
impossibility underscores the fundamental safety distinction between
cannabis and substances like alcohol, opioids, or [[cannabis-subjective-therapeutic-window-dosing]]
window.

## Mental Health Considerations

### Schizophrenia

Observational studies suggest that consuming cannabis during adolescence
may increase the risk of developing schizophrenia later in life. Cannabis
is not considered a causal factor but may act as a co-factor in
genetically predisposed individuals. Based on current evidence,
adolescents or young adults with a known family history of psychosis or
schizophrenia should avoid cannabis and other mind-altering substances.

### Addiction Potential
