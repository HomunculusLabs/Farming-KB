# Host Plants for Beneficial Insects Reference

In Gaia's Garden, Toby Hemenway provides a comprehensive table of host
plants for beneficial insects, organized to help gardeners select the
right species for attracting predatory insects, parasitic wasps,
hoverflies, and other pest-controlling organisms. This reference page
distills that table into a practical guide for creating insectary
habitat throughout the garden.

## Why Host Plants Matter

Beneficial insects need more than just pests to eat. Adult parasitic
wasps and many predators feed on pollen and nectar for energy and
reproduction. Without flowers providing these resources, beneficial
insect populations cannot build to levels where they effectively control
pests. Flowers and wild vegetation near or in the garden are critical
for their survival. This is the foundation of [[gaias-garden-living-plant-systems-insectary]].

## Spring-Blooming Insectary Plants

These early bloomers support beneficial insects emerging from
overwintering:

- **Alpine cinquefoil** (Potentilla villosa) - blooms spring
- **Angelica** (Angelica gigas) - blooms early spring
- **Basket of gold** (Alyssum saxatilis) - blooms early fall
- **Bugle** (Ajuga reptans) - blooms summer
- **Crimson thyme** (Thymus serpyllum) - blooms summer
- **Dwarf alpine aster** (Aster alpinus) - blooms late summer
  through fall
- **English lavender** (Lavandula angustifolia) - blooms summer
- **Poached-egg plant** (Limnanthes douglasii) - blooms summer
  through early fall

## Summer-Blooming Insectary Plants

The peak season for beneficial insect activity requires abundant
flowers:

- **Alfalfa** (Medicago sativa) - blooms summer through fall
- **Buckwheat** (Fagopyrum esculentum) - blooms late spring or
  early summer
- **Butterfly weed** (Asclepias tuberosa) - blooms summer
- **Caraway** (Carum carvi) - blooms summer through fall
- **Coriander** (Coriandrum sativum) - blooms summer through fall
- **Cosmos** (Cosmos bipinnatus) - blooms summer
- **Dill** (Anethum graveolens) - blooms summer through fall
- **Fennel** (Foeniculum vulgare) - blooms summer through fall
- **Fern-leaf yarrow** (Achillea filipendulina) - blooms summer
- **Golden marguerite** (Anthemis tinctoria) - blooms summer
- **Lemon balm** (Melissa officinalis) - blooms summer
- **Masterwort** (Astrantia major) - blooms summer
- **Parsley** (Petroselinum crispum) - blooms summer through fall
- **Phacelia** (Phacelia tanacetifolia) - blooms summer
- **Sweet alyssum** (Lobularia maritima) - blooms summer
- **Zinnia** (Zinnia elegans) - blooms summer through fall

## Fall-Blooming Insectary Plants

Late-season flowers sustain beneficial insects preparing for winter:

- **Butter and eggs** (Linaria vulgaris) - blooms summer through
  early fall
- **Feverfew** (Chrysanthemum parthenium) - blooms summer through
  fall
- **Gladiolus daisy** (Rudbeckia fulgida) - blooms summer through
  fall
- **Goldenrod** (Solidago virgaurea) - blooms late summer through
  fall
- **Hairy vetch** (Vicia villosa) - blooms spring, fall
- **Lobelia** (Lobelia erinus) - blooms summer through fall
- **Marigold** (Tagetes tenuifolia) - blooms summer through fall
- **Maximilian sunflower** (Helianthus maximilianii) - blooms late
  summer through fall
- **Orange stonecrop** (Sedum kamtschaticum) - blooms summer through
  fall
- **Rocky Mountain penstemon** (Penstemon strictus) - blooms late
  spring through summer
- **Spike speedwell** (Veronica spicata) - blooms summer
- **Statice** (Limonium latifolium) - blooms summer through fall
- **Stonecrops** (Sedum spurium and S. album) - blooms summer through
  fall
- **Sulfur cinquefoil** (Potentilla recta) - blooms summer through
  fall
- **Tansy** (Tanacetum vulgare) - blooms summer through fall
- **Wild bergamot** (Monarda fistulosa) - blooms summer through fall
- **Yarrow** (Achillea millefolium) - blooms summer through fall

## Beneficial Insects Attracted

These plants attract four main categories of beneficial insects:

- **Lady beetles**: Both adults and alligator-like larvae are voracious
  predators of aphids and other soft-bodied pests.
- **Lacewings**: Their larvae consume aphids, mealybugs, thrips, and
  other small insects.
- **Parasitic wasps**: Tiny, mostly stingless wasps that lay eggs
  inside pest insects. Adults feed on nectar.
- **Hoverflies**: Adults pollinate flowers; larvae feed on aphids and
  other pests.
- **Big-eyed bugs**: Generalist predators of many small insect pests.
- **Tachinid flies**: Parasitic flies that attack caterpillars and
  other pests.

## Design Tips

Plant insectary species in clumps of at least 4 to 6 plants for visual
impact and efficient foraging by insects. Ensure blooms span the entire
growing season by including spring, summer, and fall species. Integrate
insectary plants throughout the garden, not just in dedicated beds, so
beneficial insects are always near the plants needing protection. Many
of these plants are multifunctional, providing food, medicine, or
[[gaias-garden-multipurpose-plants]] functions alongside insect support.
