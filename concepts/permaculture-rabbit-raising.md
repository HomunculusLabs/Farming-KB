## Role in the Permaculture System

In a permaculture system, rabbits are not kept as pets. They provide meat, manure, and fur. Unlike other animals, they can never be allowed to roam the gardens because they will eat everything and multiply beyond control. Instead, they live in a hutch with a mesh floor so that their droppings will fall down for easy cleanup. A worm bin can be placed underneath for even easier cleanup.

Rabbits eat grass, leaves, twigs, hay, vegetables and kitchen scraps. They are especially practical for urban homesteaders because rabbit hutches are legal in most places. Any rabbit breed can be eaten, but breeds designed specifically for meat grow much larger. Recommended meat breeds include:

- California

- New Zealand

- Champagne d'Argent

- Florida White

## Disease Prevention and Health Care

Rabbits are generally hardy animals when kept in clean, dry conditions, but several common ailments require vigilance. Prevention through good husbandry is far more effective than treatment.

Common health issues:

- Sore hocks: Inflammation of the foot pads caused by wire floors or wet bedding; provide solid resting boards

- Ear mites: Cause head shaking and crusting inside ears; treat with mineral oil drops

- Respiratory infections: Snuffles (Pasteurella) causes sneezing and nasal discharge; ensure good ventilation and quarantine affected animals

- GI stasis: A slowdown or stoppage of gut motility, often triggered by stress or insufficient fiber; provide unlimited hay and monitor droppings

- Wool block: In Angora breeds, swallowed fur can block digestion; regular grooming prevents this

Preventive measures include cleaning hutches weekly, providing fresh water daily, quarantining new animals for two weeks, avoiding sudden dietary changes, and handling rabbits regularly to detect early signs of illness. Keep a basic first-aid kit with antiseptic, mineral oil, and oral syringes for administering medications.

## Processing and Slaughter

Rabbits are typically processed at 10-12 weeks of age for fryers (3-5 pounds live weight) or 6-8 months for roasters. The dispatch method must be quick and humane. Common methods include cervical dislocation for experienced practitioners or a pellet rifle to the base of the skull.

Processing steps:

- Hang the rabbit by the hind legs and dispatch quickly

- Bleed by cutting the jugular veins

- Remove the head, feet, and tail

- Make a shallow incision around the anus and carefully remove the intestines without puncturing them
