# Leucaena Alley Cropping Systems

Alley cropping with [[leucaena-leucocephala]] is a productive agroforestry
system that integrates fast-growing nitrogen-fixing trees with annual crops
or pasture. Described extensively in [[bill-mollison]]'s [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]]
Course, this system demonstrates how tree crops can be intercropped with
annual plants to create high-intensity food production that eliminates the
need for artificial fertilizer while producing multiple yields from the same
land area. Leucaena provides nitrogen, mulch, fodder, and shade while
annual crops grow between the tree rows.

## Leucaena: The Multi-Purpose Tree

Leucaena leucocephala is a leguminous tree native to [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]] that
has been widely adopted throughout the tropics and subtropics. It is often
called "ipil-ipil" in [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]]. Key characteristics include:

- Extremely fast [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]], reaching 15 to 20 feet in the first year
- Deep taproot system that accesses subsoil nutrients and moisture
- [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]] through rhizobial root nodules, adding 100 to 200 kg
  of nitrogen per hectare per year to the soil
- High protein foliage (20 to 30% crude protein) excellent for livestock
  fodder
- Ability to coppice vigorously, regrowing quickly after cutting
- Tolerance of a wide range of soil conditions including degraded soils
- Multiple products: fodder, [[coleman-mobile-greenhouse-green-manure-soil-building]], fuelwood, and poles

## The Cliff Adam Dairy System

Mollison describes a remarkable example of Leucaena alley cropping
implemented by Cliff Adam in a tropical dairy operation. Adam established
an acre of elephant grass (Pennisetum purpureum) growing about four feet
high, resembling sugar cane. Between the rows of elephant grass, he grew
Leucaena trees. Under those trees he grew annual crops. The entire field
was bordered with coconuts, providing shelter and additional yields.

The dairy system ran ten cows to the acre, an extraordinarily high stocking
rate. Adam did not take the cows to pasture; he took the pasture to the
cows. He cut the Leucaena and elephant grass daily, carrying it to the
barn where cows were milked. All manure from the dairy was washed through
a simple row-flowing system back into the crop fields. The system operated
for two years and eliminated artificial fertilizer entirely.

## Alley Cropping Design Principles

### Row Spacing and Layout

Leucaena trees are planted in rows spaced 4 to 8 meters apart, with trees
spaced 0.5 to 2 meters within rows. The wider spacing allows sufficient
light for [[root-crops-growing-guide]] between the tree rows. Narrower spacing produces
more biomass but shades out understory crops. In intensive systems like the
Adam dairy, closer spacing is used because the tree foliage is harvested
and carried to livestock rather than left to shade crops.

### Integration with Annual Crops

Annual crops are grown in the alleys between Leucaena rows. The system
works through a cycle of cutting the trees back, growing crops in the
alleys, and allowing the trees to regrow. When Leucaena is cut to ground
level, its root system releases nitrogen into the soil, fertilizing the
alley crop. Common alley crops include maize, beans, sweet potatoes, and
vegetables [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]].

### Comfrey Integration

Mollison specifically recommends integrating comfrey into Leucaena alley
cropping systems. Comfrey does not care if people are walking up and down
on it, making it ideal for high-traffic alley systems. Five cuts per year
of comfrey can be obtained, providing additional high-protein fodder for
livestock and nutrient-rich mulch for soil building. The comfrey taproot
mines minerals from deep soil layers and brings them to the surface.

## Fodder Production and Livestock Integration

Leucaena's high-protein foliage makes it one of the world's most important
fodder trees. It can provide up to 50% of the diet for cattle, goats, and
sheep when supplemented with grass. The protein content significantly
