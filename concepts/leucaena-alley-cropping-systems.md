## The Cliff Adam Dairy System

Mollison describes a remarkable example of Leucaena alley cropping
implemented by Cliff Adam in a tropical dairy operation. Adam established
an acre of elephant grass (Pennisetum purpureum) growing about four feet
high, resembling sugar cane. Between the rows of elephant grass, he grew
Leucaena trees. Under those trees he grew annual crops. The entire field
was bordered with coconuts, providing shelter and additional yields.

The dairy system ran ten cows to the acre, an extraordinarily high stocking
rate. Adam did not take the cows to pasture; he took the pasture to the
cows. He cut the Leucaena and elephant grass daily, carrying it to the
barn where cows were milked. All manure from the dairy was washed through
a simple row-flowing system back into the crop fields. The system operated
for two years and eliminated artificial fertilizer entirely.

## Alley Cropping Design Principles

### Row Spacing and Layout

Leucaena trees are planted in rows spaced 4 to 8 meters apart, with trees
spaced 0.5 to 2 meters within rows. The wider spacing allows sufficient
light for [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]].

### Comfrey Integration

Mollison specifically recommends integrating comfrey into Leucaena alley
cropping systems. Comfrey does not care if people are walking up and down
on it, making it ideal for high-traffic alley systems. Five cuts per year
of comfrey can be obtained, providing additional high-protein fodder for
livestock and nutrient-rich mulch for soil building. The comfrey taproot
mines minerals from deep soil layers and brings them to the surface.

## Fodder Production and Livestock Integration

Leucaena's high-protein foliage makes it one of the world's most important
