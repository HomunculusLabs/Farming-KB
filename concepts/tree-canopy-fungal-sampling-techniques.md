# Tree Canopy Fungal Sampling — Techniques for Accessing Upper Forest

To obtain a full species list of fungi in a forest, sampling must extend many meters above the ground. The upper canopy hosts unique microclimates and fungal communities distinct from those near the forest floor. This chapter reviews techniques for accessing and sampling tree canopies.

## Why Canopy Sampling Matters

- Different moisture, light, and temperature conditions at different heights
- Few tree trunks are perfectly vertical — upper vs. lower sides receive different moisture
- Upper canopy has more extreme light intensity, temperature, and moisture fluctuations
- Some foliar fungi occur only in the upper canopy
- Sooty molds can cover trunks to 9+ meters in some forests
- Black mildews abundant in upper canopy but rare in dense rain forest undergrowth

## Canopy Access Techniques

### Climbing
- **Ropes and pulleys with semi-permanent platforms** (Canadian Forest Service, Vancouver Island)
- **Jumars and mountaineering rope** — arrow with nylon rope shot through crown, then climb
- **Spiked foot griffes** — punctures tree; not recommended
- **Foot strap (for small-diameter trunks only)**
- A team of 3 experienced climbers can rig a tall tree in 1.5 days
- Once rigged, ascent takes approximately 20 minutes
- **Warning:** Climbing old-growth trees is extremely dangerous; a fall would probably be fatal

### Shooting
- Shotguns and rifles used to obtain branches from upper canopy
- Useful for voucher specimens and grafts
- May damage specimens but provides access to otherwise unreachable material

### Other Methods
- Trained monkeys (E.J.H. Corner trained monkeys in Malaysia to climb trees and break off branches)
- **Cutting down trees is counterproductive** — bark and branches usually shattered on impact

## Sampling Design for Tree Epiphytes

### Pike et al. Method (Douglas Fir)
Divided 450-year-old Pseudotsuga menziesii into six zones:
1. Base
2. Moist side of trunk
3. Dry side of trunk
4. Upper trunk
5. Axes of branch systems >4 cm diameter
6. Branchlets <4 cm diameter

Found 74 lichen species and 32 bryophyte species. Techniques applicable to fungi.

### Marking and Quadrat Sampling
- Mark tree trunks with tape at 5-meter intervals
- Map location of each branch system
- Sample two 10 x 25 cm quadrats at each 5-m mark, at 45 and 135 degrees from trunk
- Harvest all epiphytes from quadrat for laboratory examination
- Large branches sampled at 0.5-m intervals using "cylindrat" (cylindrical surface of 1-dm axis section)
- 20% of branchlets removed for examination

### Denison et al. Branch System Selection
- Map each branch system on tree; assign "importance value" based on size and features
- Small, uniform trees: select 2 branch systems randomly
- Normal full-sized trees: select 3
- Large trees with multiple leaders: select 4-5

### Lesica et al. Plot-Based Method (Grand Fir Forests)
- 0.081-ha circular plots
- 32-m reference line through plot center
- Three 30-m sampling lines perpendicular to reference line
- Three strata: lower canopy, trunk, ground
- Lower canopy: 24 branches (closest to each line at 8 equidistant points)
- Trunk: epiphytes from 0.5 x 0.2 m flexible quadrats 1.5 m above ground, both sides
- Ground: three 30-m x 1-m belt transects

## Special Considerations for Fungal Sampling

### Obligate Parasites
- Usually infect healthy, vigorously growing plants
- Symptoms (stunting, chlorosis) may not be evident at low infection levels
- Easily overlooked on tree foliage — need careful inspection

### Fresh vs. Air-Dried Specimens
- When most material is close to sporulation, dry with warm air
- Save a small amount dried with ambient air for later culturing attempts

### Representative Sampling
- Must sample all parts of each tree (different heights, orientations)
- Trunk angle affects moisture and epiphyte flora
- A single fungal colony per patch in black mildews; colonies can become confluent

## Disease Incidence vs. Severity

### Incidence (Percent Diseased)
- Count diseased vs. healthy sampling units
- Fewer samples required than severity
- Useful for early epidemics and systemic infections

### Severity (Percent Tissue Covered)
- Measures proportion of tissue with symptoms
- More samples required
- Used for rusts, [[biodiversity-powdery-mildews]], foliar spot fungi

### Sample Size Determination
- Plot means and standard deviations against sample size
- When curves for both stabilize, adequate sample size reached
- Minimum 5 points of measurement needed for reliable curves
