## Management

Nitrogen fixers are fast-growing and can become dominant if not managed.
Ecological gardeners typically slash them periodically for mulch, coppice
them for fuel wood, or allow taller desired species to eventually
outshade them. In a "two plants in every hole" strategy, a nitrogen
fixer is planted alongside every fruit tree and cut back as needed.

## Legumes vs. Actinorhizal Plants

The largest group of nitrogen fixers is the legume family (Fabaceae),
which includes peas, beans, clovers, lupines, wisteria, black locust,
mesquite, and acacia. Legumes associate with Rhizobium bacteria, which
are abundant in most soils but can be inoculated for better results.

A second important group uses actinorhizal fungi instead of bacteria.
This group includes Elaeagnus species (goumi, autumn olive, Russian
olive), Ceanothus (wild lilac), Casuarina, alder, and mountain
mahogany. Actinorhizal plants are especially valuable in permaculture
because many are also drought-tolerant, wildlife-friendly, and produce
edible fruit.
