# Multi-Purpose Trees for Permaculture

In permaculture, every element should serve multiple functions. Multi-purpose trees exemplify this principle -- providing timber, fodder, [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]], bee forage, [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]], fire protection, and sometimes food, all from a single planting. This guide covers key multi-purpose species from "Permaculture Plants: A Selection" organised by their primary functions. Multi-purpose trees can also be integrated into broader tagasaste (Chamaecytisus palmensis)
One of the most versatile [[multipurpose-trees-in-permaculture-design]]. Fast-growing pioneer and nurse tree providing:
|- **Fodder**: Abundant leafy growth, palatable to livestock
|- **Nitrogen fixation**: Adds fertility to the system -- complementary to carob (Ceratonia siliqua)
- **Food**: Pods (40-50% sugar) eaten fresh, ground into flour, or used as chocolate substitute
- **Fodder**: Pods are excellent stock feed
- **Bee forage**: Produces both nectar and pollen; pods fed to bees for winter survival
- **Timber**: Heartwood hard and durable
- **Fire retardant**: Low flammability
- **[[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]]**: Thrives in Mediterranean climates with 250-500 mm rainfall

### Casuarina spp (Sheoaks)
- **Timber**: Hard, dense wood suited to fine crafts and cabinet making
- **Erosion control**: Extensive root systems stabilise soil
- **[[fungal-salt-tolerance-and-ion-homeostasis]]**: Many species thrive in saline conditions
- **Nitrogen fixation**: Through Frankia spp (actinorhizal)
- **Firewood**: Dense wood, hot burning
- **Bee forage**: Produces nectar and pollen

Notable species: C. cunninghamiana (river sheoak), C. glauca (swamp sheoak), C. obesa (salt sheoak), C. cristata (black sheoak), C. stricta (sheoak), C. fraserana (Fraser's sheoak), C. torulosa (forest sheoak).

### Acacia spp (Wattles)
- **Timber**: Many species produce valuable craft and construction timbers (A. melanoxylon -- blackwood, is world-class cabinet timber)
- **Nitrogen fixation**: Root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen
- **Fodder**: Several species provide nutritious browse (A. saligna, A. aneura)
- **Bee forage**: Winter/spring pollen from many species
- **Soil stabilisation**: Deep root systems prevent erosion
- **Tannin**: Bark yields commercial tannin
- **Edible seed**: Some species produce edible seeds

## Fodder Multi-Purpose Trees

### Willow (Salix spp)
- **Fodder**: Extremely nutritious; weeping willow contains 15.9% crude protein, 2.9% crude fat. NZ hybrid willow produces >30 tonnes dry matter/ha (versus 10-15 for lucerne)
- **Erosion control**: Root systems reach 50 m laterally; excellent for sheet, gully, and slump erosion
- **Salinity control**: Lower water tables in waterlogged areas
- **Bee forage**: Provides nectar and pollen when few plants flowering
- **Medicinal**: Bark contains salicin (source of aspirin)
- **Craft**: Osier willow (S. viminalis) for basketry
- **Rooting hormone**: Willow water surpasses commercial rooting powders
- **Timber**: Soft, light timber from several species

### Poplar (Populus spp)
- **Fodder**: Nutritionally superior to lucerne; stock eat all foliage and fallen leaves. Composition: 60% water, 6% nitrates, 5% cellulose, 4% minerals, 2% fats
- **Timber**: Soft wood for pulp, paper, furniture interiors, matches, fruit boxes, plywood
- **Erosion control**: Root systems similar to willows
- **Salinity control**: Suckering varieties can colonise salt scalds
- **Bee forage**: Both nectar and pollen
- **Fast growth**: Harvest-ready in just 15 years
- **Shelter**: Allows understorey growth of bana grass, elephant grass, pampas grass, and bamboo

### Mulberry (Morus spp)
- **Fodder**: Leaves are the primary food of silkworm; also excellent livestock fodder
- **Food**: Fruit eaten fresh, dried, or in preserves
- **Timber**: Useful light timber
- **Companion**: Good companion to grapes; also forms natural trellis
- **Bee forage**: Provides nectar and pollen

## Timber Multi-Purpose Trees

### Oak Species (Quercus spp)
Oaks are among the most multi-functional tree genera:
- **Timber**: Cabinet making, furniture, barrels, flooring, construction, boat building, carving
- **Fodder**: Acorns from many species are edible (after leaching tannins) and excellent stock feed
- **Bee forage**: Pollen source
- **Wildlife habitat**: Support hundreds of insect species and provide food for birds and mammals
- **Ecosystem support**: Foundation species in many forest ecosystems

Key species: Q. robur (English oak), Q. suber (cork oak), Q. alba (white oak), Q. macrocarpa (burr oak), Q. ilex (holm oak), Q. acutissima (Japanese sawtooth oak), Q. lobata (valley oak).

### Eucalyptus spp
- **Timber**: Many species produce hard, durable timber for construction, furniture, and craft
- **Bee forage**: Major source of nectar and pollen across multiple seasons
- **[[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]]**: Many species yield commercial essential oils
- **Firewood**: Dense, hot-burning wood
- **Fodder**: Leaves browsed by koalas and some livestock species
- **Erosion control**: Deep root systems stabilise soil

### Chestnut (Castanea spp)
- **Food**: Nuts are nutritionally similar to rice (high carbohydrate, low fat). Edible raw or cooked; dried and ground into flour
