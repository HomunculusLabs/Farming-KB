# Stoned Ape Theory

The relationship between early humans, cattle, and dung-loving mushrooms created what McKenna called "a deep-rooted custom, an extremely powerful natural habit" that he estimated to be no more than a million years old, with the most culturally significant period spanning the last 100,000 years.

Whatever this relationship is called, he insisted, "it has not been a static relationship, but rather a dynamic through which we have been bootstrapped to higher and higher cultural levels and levels of individual self-awareness."

## The Ur Plant: Stropharia cubensis

McKenna made a detailed case for *Stropharia cubensis* as the specific mushroom species involved, based on several converging lines of evidence:

- It is pandemic throughout tropical regions wherever zebu cattle graze
- It contains psilocybin in concentrated amounts without nausea-producing compounds
- Archaeological evidence from Non Nak Tha in Thailand (dated to 15,000 B.P.) shows zebu cattle bones coincident with human graves; the mushroom is common in the area today
- The species likely originated in Southeast Asia and spread with cattle domestication
- Its presence wherever human populations and cattle evolved together suggests a very ancient association
- It is the only candidate that combines palatability, concentrated psychoactivity, and wide [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]]* theory for the Vedic Soma, arguing that *Amanita* does not grow in grasslands, is ambiguous in its psychoactivity, and requires elaborate processing (such as urine drinking by Siberian tribes) that is unlikely to have characterized the original human-mushroom interaction.

Wasson himself expressed private doubts about the Amanita theory, writing to McKenna in 1977: "Inebriation from Str. cubensis and the other psilocybin species is clearly, in my opinion, superior to [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]]."

## Symbiosis Rather Than Biological Evolution

McKenna was careful to distinguish his theory from claims of true biological symbiosis. The human-mushroom relationship, while deeply rooted, was not genetically programmed. Instead, he described it as a "psychosymbiotic" relationship — a cultural habit of such power that it functioned ecologically like a symbiosis.

He contrasted it with true symbioses such as clown fish and sea anemones, or mitochondria within animal cells (which have separate genetic components traceable to free-swimming bacteria that merged with other cellular structures hundreds of millions of years ago).

The theory draws on the broader concept of coevolution: as plants influenced the development of humans, humans influenced plant distribution through cattle herding and habitat management. McKenna noted that alkaloids are not inert byproducts but are in "a dynamic state, fluctuating in both concentration and in their rate of metabolic decay," suggesting they play essential roles in the life strategy of the organisms that produce them.

## The Transcendent Other

Central to the theory is the concept of the "Transcendent Other" — the entity or dimension encountered during powerful psychedelic experiences. McKenna defined this as:

> "Nature correctly perceived to be alive and intelligent... the awesomely unfamiliar union of all the senses with memory of the past and anticipation of the future... Nature without her cheerfully reassuring mask of ordinary space, time, and causality."

He proposed that where plant hallucinogens are present, cultures are "quickly introduced to ever more novel information, sensory input, and behavior and thus bootstrapped to higher and higher states of self-reflection." This encounter with the Other represents what McKenna called the "crucible of the Mystery of our being, both as a species and as individuals."

## Exopheromones and Chemical Communication

McKenna introduced the concept of "exopheromones" — chemical messengers that act across species lines — to explain how plant alkaloids might function as a form of interspecies communication. He proposed that nature "appears to maximize mutual cooperation and mutual coordination of goals" and that "diplomacy is largely a matter of language," with psychoactive plant compounds serving as the chemical vocabulary of this ecological diplomacy.

The idea that plant hallucinogens function as information-transfer agents between species challenges the conventional view of alkaloids as mere defensive toxins or evolutionary byproducts. If correct, it implies that the human-mushroom relationship is not merely exploitative but communicative — an exchange of information between two very different forms of life.

## Weeding Out Alternative Candidates

McKenna systematically eliminated other candidates for the role of consciousness catalyst in Africa:

- **Tabernanthe iboga**: Not a grassland plant, no evidence of use before the 19th century, at low doses diminishes ordinary vision by facilitating afterimages and visual "streaking"
- **DMT-containing grasses**: Cannot supply sufficient amounts without extraction and concentration, which was beyond the technical reach of early humans
