# Beneficial Insects Gallery for the Ecological Garden

In a healthy ecological garden, over 90% of insect species are either beneficial
or harmless. Only a small fraction cause noticeable damage, and many of these
are held in check by predators and parasitoids that share the same habitat. The

## The Decline of Beneficial Insects

Historically, farms and gardens supported vast populations of beneficial insects.
Hedgerows, fence rows, meadows, and woodlots provided undisturbed habitat where
predators and parasitoids overwintered. Pesticide use and the removal of these
habitat features devastated beneficial populations. Without natural enemies, pest
species exploded, requiring ever more pesticides — a vicious cycle that
conventional agriculture has yet to escape. The ecological garden reverses this

**Lacewings** are delicate, green-winged insects whose larvae are called
aphid lions. Lacewing larvae consume aphids, mealybugs, thrips, whiteflies,
caterpillar eggs, and mites. They are attracted to gardens with nectar-rich
flowers.

**Spiders** are not insects but the most abundant predators in most gardens.
A mulched garden may support 30 times more spiders than a bare-soil garden.
Wolf spiders, jumping spiders, and crab spiders are common garden residents
that consume enormous quantities of pests.

## Parasitic Insects (Parasitoids)

Parasitoids lay their eggs inside or on other insects. The developing larvae
consume the host from within, killing it. Though gruesome, parasitoids are
extraordinarily effective and specific in their prey.
