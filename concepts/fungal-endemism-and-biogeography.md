# Fungal Endemism and Biogeography

Fungal biogeography studies the distribution of fungal species across
space and time, while endemism refers to species restricted to
particular geographic areas. For many decades, fungi were assumed to
have cosmopolitan distributions due to microscopic [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungal-diversity-patterns-distribution]] and high levels of endemism, particularly
in tropical and island ecosystems.

## The Cosmopolitan Hypothesis

The traditional view that most fungi are globally distributed was based
on the observation that many morphologically defined species appeared
on multiple continents. Molecular phylogenetic studies have revised
this picture. Genetically distinct populations of what were considered
single species have been found on different continents. Many species
once thought cosmopolitan are now recognized as species complexes
containing geographically restricted taxa. The apparent
cosmopolitanism of many fungi was largely an artifact of insufficient
taxonomic resolution.
