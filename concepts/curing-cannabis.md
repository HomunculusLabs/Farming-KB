# Curing Cannabis

Curing is the post-drying process that allows cannabis buds to complete moisture
equalization and develop full aroma, flavor, and smoothness. After [[drying-cannabis]],
buds still contain internal moisture. Curing removes this excess moisture gradually and
converts virtually all remaining THCA into its psychoactive form. Like a fine wine,
proper curing dramatically improves quality.

## Why Cure

The first week of curing affects potency by evenly removing moisture within the bud so
that virtually all THC is psychoactive. Curing also allows buds to dry enough that mold
does not grow during storage. Well-cured buds have an even glow when burned and smoke
smooth. Uncured or poorly cured buds taste harsh, green, and burn unevenly.

Curing allows buds to continue to dry slowly. After plants, branches, and/or buds have
dried on screens or hung in a drying room for five to seven days and appear to be dry,
they still contain moisture inside. This moisture affects taste and potency. Curing will
remove this excess moisture and make the bud uniformly dry.

## The Curing Process

### Initial Container

Cut stems into manageable lengths less than 12-18 inches (30-45 cm) and place them in
airtight containers. Airtight glass containers (canning jars) with rubber seals are the
best choice. Avoid Ziploc bags that are not truly airtight. For large quantities,
airtight plastic bins may be the only practical option. Some growers avoid plastic
containers such as Rubbermaid bins, saying the plastic imparts an undesirable flavor,
but for large amounts they are the best practical option.

### Sweat and Monitor

Enclose buds in a container to create a microclimate that allows moisture to even out
within the buds. Internal moisture will migrate to the dry portions [[allegro-star-of-the-morning-venus-and-the-sacred-mushroom]] bud. Gently
pack as many buds into the container as possible without crushing them. Place
containers in a cool, dry, dark place. Check in 2-4 hours to see if buds have "sweated"
moisture. Gently squeeze buds to feel if they are moister than before -- be careful,
resin glands bruise easily.

### Managing Moisture

If stems fold instead of snapping and buds feel moist, remove them from the container
and place [[allegro-death-and-resurrection-in-the-mushroom-cult]] bottom of a paper bag. Stack buds no higher than six inches (15 cm).
Fold the bag top once or twice to close. Check 2-3 times daily, turning buds to expose
different surfaces. When dry enough, return to the sealed container.

If buds appear fairly dry and stems snap when bent, leave them [[allegro-plants-and-drugs-in-the-ancient-world]] container and open
the lid for a few minutes every few hours to let excess moisture escape. At this point
you can add an orange or lemon peel to the container to impart a slight citrus aroma.

### Final Dry-Down

Check containers several times daily, opening for 5-10 minutes each time. Depending on
moisture content, buds should be totally dry in a few days to two weeks. Once evenly
dry, seal in airtight containers for storage.

## Ideal Curing Conditions

- **Temperature:** 60-70 degrees F (15-21 degrees C); cool is better
- **Humidity:** 40-60 percent relative humidity
- **Light:** Dark -- light degrades THC and terpenes
- **Container:** Airtight glass preferred; check seals regularly

## How Long to Cure

Minimum effective curing is 2-4 weeks. Many growers cure for 4-8 weeks for optimal
flavor development. Some connoisseurs cure for 6 months or longer. The first week has
the most dramatic effect on potency and smoothness. Extended curing continues to improve
flavor complexity.

## Flavor Enhancement

During curing, chlorophyll [[allen-gymnopilus-and-other-psychoactive-genera]] pigments break down further, eliminating the
"green" taste. Terpenes and other aromatic compounds redistribute and stabilize.
Adding an orange or lemon peel to the container can impart a subtle citrus aroma, though
this is a matter of personal preference.

## Mold Prevention

Mold is the primary risk during curing. Watch for:

- White fuzzy growth on buds
- Musty or mildew smell when opening containers
- Damp spots that do not dry out

If mold is detected, remove affected buds immediately and increase air exposure for
remaining buds. Ensure buds are adequately dry before sealing in containers for extended
periods. Curing also allows buds to dry enough that mold does not grow when stored.

## Storage After Curing

Once properly cured and dry, store buds in airtight glass containers in a cool, dark
place. Glass jars do not impart plastic or metal odors and contain fragrance. For
long-term storage, refrigeration slows degradation. Ensure containers are truly airtight
-- oxygen exposure degrades THC over time.

## Common Curing Mistakes

- Curing before buds are dry enough (mold risk)
- Skipping curing entirely and going straight to storage
- Opening containers too infrequently to monitor moisture
- Using non-airtight containers
- Storing in warm or light-exposed locations
- Not turning buds in paper bags when they are too moist

## Long-Term Storage

For long-term storage after curing, keep containers in a cool, dark location. Glass
jars with airtight rubber seals are ideal because they do not impart plastic or metal
odors and contain fragrance well. Some growers vacuum-seal cured buds in bags for
extended storage. Avoid frequent opening of storage containers, as each opening
introduces fresh oxygen and moisture that can degrade quality over time.

## Checking Cure Quality

Well-cured buds exhibit several characteristics:

- Stems snap cleanly when bent (no folding)
- Buds are slightly springy to the touch but not wet
- Aroma is rich and complex, not "green" or hay-like
- Buds burn evenly with a smooth, white ash
- No harshness or chemical taste when smoked

## Common Curing Mistakes

- Curing before buds are dry enough (mold risk)
- Skipping curing entirely and going straight to storage
- Opening containers too infrequently to monitor moisture
- Using non-airtight containers
- Storing in warm or light-exposed locations
- Not turning buds in paper bags when they are too moist
- Adding too much fruit peel (can introduce mold)
