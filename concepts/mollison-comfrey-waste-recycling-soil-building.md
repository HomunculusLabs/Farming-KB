## Mineral Accumulation

Comfrey's deep root system (reported to reach 2-3 metres) acts as a
dynamic accumulator, mining minerals from deep subsoil layers and
bringing them to the surface in its leaves. When comfrey is cut and
used as mulch or compost, these accumulated minerals become available
to shallow-rooted garden plants. This function is particularly
valuable in [[mollison-tropical-home-garden-design]],
comfrey and lemongrass provide green mulch and help suppress grasses.
Comfrey serves multiple functions:

- Ground cover and weed suppression
- Nutrient cycling from deep soil layers
- Mulch production through regular cutting
- Waste absorption from animal pens
- Fodder for poultry and livestock
- "Instant compost" when trenched under crops

## Integration with Banana Circle Systems

Comfrey is frequently planted around [[mollison-dry-toilet-composting-systems]] area.
Nicotiana and Leucaena are also noted as excellent species for this
purpose. This demonstrates comfrey's role in closing the nutrient loop
from human waste back to soil fertility.

## Comfrey as Mulch and Soil Builder

Comfrey leaves contain high levels of potassium, making them
particularly valuable as a mulch for fruiting crops. Regular cutting
encourages continued growth and provides a steady supply of nutrient-
rich mulch material. When comfrey is trenched under potatoes or other
crops, it provides:

- Rapid decomposition into soil organic matter
- Balanced mineral nutrition
- Moisture retention in the trench
- Weed suppression during decomposition
- Improved soil structure through root channel formation

## Care in Use

Mollison's broader discussion of compost material selection applies to
comfrey as well. When comfrey is used to absorb waste, care must be
taken with:

- Heavy metal contamination from the waste source
- Buildup of copper, zinc, or cadmium in the comfrey tissue
- Appropriate dilution or composting before use as fodder
- Monitoring of soil pH and mineral levels where comfrey is heavily
  harvested

## Multi-Function Principle

Comfrey exemplifies the permaculture principle that every element should
serve multiple functions. In the Mollison framework, comfrey is not
merely a plant but a system component that:

- Absorbs and transforms waste nutrients
- Accumulates minerals from deep soil
- Produces biomass for mulch and compost
- Provides fodder for animals
- Suppresses weeds and grasses
- Serves practical functions (toilet paper)
- Integrates waste treatment with food production
