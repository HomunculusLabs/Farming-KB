## The Weevil Blight

Japanese red pines were suffering severe damage from an outbreak of pine bark weevils. Foresters responded by using helicopters to aerial-spray pesticides in an attempt to stop the damage. Fukuoka did not deny that spraying was effective in the short run, but insisted there must be another way.

Research had revealed that the weevil blight was not a direct infestation but followed upon the action of mediating nematodes. The nematodes breed within the pine trunk, blocking the transport of water and nutrients, eventually causing the tree to wither and die. But the ultimate cause was not clearly understood.

## The Infinite Regress of Causality

Fukuoka traced the chain of causation as far as science could take it:

- Nematodes feed on a fungus within the tree"s trunk
- Why did this fungus begin to multiply so prolifically?
- Did the fungus multiply after the nematode appeared, or did the nematode appear because the fungus was present?
- Furthermore, there is another microbe about which very little is known, which always accompanies the fungus
- There is also a virus toxic to the fungus
- "Effect following effect in every direction"
- The only certainty is that pine trees are withering in unusual numbers

This infinite regress of causes and effects leads nowhere. Each answer raises new questions. The more closely you look, the more the causal chain dissolves into a web of interrelationships with no clear beginning or end.
