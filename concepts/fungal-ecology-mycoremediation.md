## Fungi in Ecosystem Function

Fungi are among the major decomposers in any ecosystem and have been harnessed by humans for diverse applications including [[fungal-ecology]] of contaminated sites differs from that of pristine environments due to the selective pressure exerted by pollutants, which can either inhibit or stimulate specific fungal populations.

The ability of fungi to form extensive mycelial networks is ecologically significant for bioremediation. These networks allow fungi to explore large volumes of soil, access pollutants that may be poorly available to bacteria, and translocate nutrients and metabolites across considerable distances.

Plant-fungal interactions in contaminated soils involve complex signaling and metabolic exchanges. The combined system can achieve remediation outcomes that neither partner could accomplish alone.
