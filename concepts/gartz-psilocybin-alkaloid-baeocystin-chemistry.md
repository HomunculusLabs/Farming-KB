## Psilocybin Alkaloid Chemistry and Baeocystin

## Overview

Jochen Gartz's *[[fungi-around-the-world-species-distribution]]* provides extensive
documentation of the alkaloid chemistry of psychoactive mushrooms,
particularly the relationship between **psilocybin**, **psilocin**, and
**baeocystin**. Understanding these compounds and their varying
concentrations across species is essential for comprehending the
pharmacological effects, potency differences, and stability
characteristics of psychedelic mushrooms worldwide.

## Primary Psychoactive Compounds

The three principal indole alkaloids found in psychoactive mushrooms are:

- **Psilocybin** (4-PO-DMT): The phosphate ester of psilocin and the
  primary psychoactive compound. It is remarkably stable as part of dried
  mushroom material. Gartz notes that psilocybin's decay rate is slowest
  in [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] samples. The
presence of this additional psychoactive compound may explain the
discrepancy between measured psilocybin content and experienced potency.

## Alkaloid Content Variability

Gartz presents data from the Dubener Marshlands of Eastern Germany
showing significant variation in alkaloid content between individual
mushrooms from the same location:

| Dry Weight (mg) | Psilocybin (%) | Baeocystin (%) |
|----------------|---------------|---------------|
| 18             | 1.25          | 0.34          |
| 30             | 0.96          | 0.21          |
| 70             | 0.72          | 0.19          |
| 85             | 0.90          | 0.10          |

Key observations from this data:
- Smaller mushrooms almost always contained more alkaloids than larger
  specimens, a finding confirmed by subsequent studies using 40
  mushrooms.
- Exceptionally high concentrations of baeocystin accumulate in the caps
  of fruiting bodies that contain the alkaloid.
- One sample from Finland showed an unusually high psilocybin content
  of 2.37%.
