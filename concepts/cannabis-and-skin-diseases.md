## Overview

The endocannabinoid system (ECS) of the skin is now recognized as a key regulator of skin health, including hair growth, sebaceous gland function, inflammation, and wound healing. Multiple skin conditions show potential benefit from cannabinoid-based therapies. See also cannabis and skin diseases, cannabis and skin diseases, and cannabinoid-receptors.

## The Skin Endocannabinoid System

The seminal paper by Biro et al. (2009) established that the skin has its own functional endocannabinoid system with CB1 and CB2 receptors, endogenous ligands (anandamide, 2-AG), and metabolizing enzymes. This system plays roles in:

- **Hair follicle cycling** — endo- and exocannabinoids inhibit hair follicle growth (Telek et al. 2007)
- **Sebaceous gland function** — regulation of sebum production
- **Inflammation control** — CB2 receptors mediate anti-inflammatory responses
- **Sensory perception** — modulation of itch (pruritus) and pain
- **Cell proliferation and differentiation** — relevant to psoriasis and wound healing

## Clinical Evidence

### Skin Diseases (General): CHI 10, 4 Studies

### Eczema/Dermatitis: CHI 3 (N/A), 1 Study

- The ECS regulates cutaneous inflammation and allergic responses
- Topical cannabinoid agonists show promise for inflammatory skin conditions
- Psychological stress is a known trigger for eczema flares (Grace and Graham 1952)
- Anandamide levels in the skin may modulate inflammatory responses

### Psoriasis: CHI 3-4 (N/A), 1-3 Studies

- Psoriasis involves hyperproliferation of keratinocytes
- CB1 receptor activation may slow excessive cell proliferation
- Anti-inflammatory effects of cannabinoids relevant to psoriatic inflammation
- Curcumin (turmeric) shows anticancer potential that may extend to anti-proliferative effects on skin cells (Aggarwal et al. 2003)

### Pruritus (Itching): CHI 10, 3 Studies

- Topical cannabinoid agonists are effective for treating chronic pruritus (Stander et al. 2006)
- Dronabinol showed preliminary benefit for intractable pruritus secondary to cholestatic liver disease (Neff et al. 2002)
- CB1 and CB2 receptors in the skin modulate sensory nerve endings that transmit itch signals

### Acne: CHI 3 (N/A), 1 Study

- Sebum production is influenced by the ECS
- Psychological stress increases sebum production and acne severity (Yosipovitch et al. 2007)
- Cannabinoid modulation of sebaceous gland lipogenesis is an active area of research

### Hirsutism (Unwanted Hair Growth): CHI 3 (N/A), 1 Study

- Endocannabinoids inhibit hair follicle growth
- Fennel extract shows antihirsutism activity in double-blind placebo-controlled study (Javidnia et al. 2003)
- Psychological factors in hirsute women documented (Fava et al. 1989)

### Hair Loss/Baldness: CHI 3 (N/A), 1 Study

- Endocannabinoids inhibit hair follicle growth
- This suggests both risks (cannabis use could worsen hair loss) and potential therapeutic targets
- CB1 antagonists may promote hair growth by blocking the inhibitory effect

## Administration Methods

- **Topical:** Creams, salves, and oils applied directly to affected areas — preferred for localized conditions
- **Oral:** For systemic inflammatory conditions
- **Sublingual:** For rapid systemic absorption

## Safety

- Topical application has minimal systemic absorption and side effects
- Oral cannabinoids may cause psychoactive effects at higher doses
- CBD-dominant topicals avoid psychoactivity entirely
