# Flush Count Variation in Brassinosteroid-Treated Cultures

## Overview

Flush count variation describes the observed differences in the number and distribution [[annulus-loss-morphological-effect-brassinosteroid-treatment]] with 22S,23S-homobrassinolide at 10⁻² ppm dramatically altered the flush pattern of *[[beber-mycelial-network-graph-theory-cord-routing]]:

- **First flush**: Often the largest, as the mycelium has accumulated maximum nutrient reserves during colonization.
- **Subsequent flushes**: Generally smaller, representing progressively depleted nutrient reserves. Each flush requires a recovery period during which the mycelium reaccumulates resources.
- **Total flushes**: Most cultivated species produce 3–5 flushes before nutrient exhaustion. Some species may continue producing smaller flushes for longer periods if moisture and environmental conditions are maintained.

The flush pattern is influenced by substrate composition, environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, fresh air exchange), species genetics, and strain characteristics.

## Observed Variation in the Gartz (1990) Study

### Control Cultures (No Brassinosteroid)
- **Time to first flush**: 4–5 weeks after inoculation
- **First flush mushroom count**: 1–3 fruiting bodies
- **Total flushes observed**: 5 flushes during the fruiting period
- **Total dry mass**: 2.3–2.9 g per 10 g substrate

### Brassinosteroid-Treated Cultures (10⁻² ppm 22S,23S-homobrassinolide)
- **Time to first flush**: 3–3.5 weeks after inoculation
- **First flush mushroom count**: 4–7 fruiting bodies
- **Total flushes observed**: 5 flushes during the fruiting period
- **Total dry mass**: 3.4–3.9 g per 10 g substrate

### Key Observations on Variation

The study noted "a variation of the number of formed fruit bodies in the five flushes during fructification." This variation manifests in several ways:

1. **First flush dominance**: The most striking difference is the 2–4× increase in first flush yield. While controls produced 1–3 mushrooms in the first flush, treated cultures produced 4–7.
2. **Redistributed productivity**: The enhanced first flush suggests that brassinosteroids may shift the reproductive strategy of the fungus, front-loading fruiting body production into the earliest possible flush rather than distributing it evenly across all flushes.
3. **Consistent flush count**: Both treated and control cultures produced the same number of flushes (5), but the total biomass produced per flush differed substantially.

## Cultivation Implications

### Harvest Planning
The front-loaded flush pattern of brassinosteroid-treated cultures has implications for harvest scheduling:
- A larger first harvest requires more labor at once but may simplify scheduling by concentrating the most productive period.
- The shortened time to first flush (3–3.5 weeks vs 4–5 weeks) reduces total cultivation time by approximately 25–30%.

### Substrate Efficiency
The increase in dry mass per unit substrate (3.4–3.9 g vs 2.3–2.9 g per 10 g substrate) represents a 48–70% improvement in [[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]* on dung/grain substrate. Key unknowns include:
- Whether other dung-inhabiting species show similar brassinosteroid responsiveness
- Whether the flush redistribution effect occurs on non-dung substrates
- Whether different strains of *P. cubensis* show different magnitudes of response
- Whether the effect is maintained over multiple cultivation generations
