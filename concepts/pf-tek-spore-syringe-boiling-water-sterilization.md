# PF TEK Spore Syringe Boiling Water Sterilization

The PF TEK describes a straightforward boiling water method for sterilizing syringes used in spore print harvesting and spore syringe preparation. This technique requires no pressure canner or autoclave, making it accessible to home cultivators using common kitchen equipment. The method leverages extended exposure to boiling water to achieve sterility adequate for syringe preparation work.

## Equipment Required

The complete list of equipment needed for syringe boiling water sterilization:

- Plastic syringes (10cc or larger recommended; 20cc to 65cc preferred)
- 18 gauge 1.5 inch needles (extra long needles if available)
- Large pot with tight fitting lid for boiling
- Tin foil for wrapping syringes during sterilization
- Needle guard caps (original caps that come with the syringes)
- Tongs or a slotted spoon for retrieving syringes from boiling water
- Clean workspace for handling sterile syringes after cooling

All items should be clean before beginning the sterilization process. While the boiling itself provides the primary sterilization, starting with visibly clean equipment reduces the thermal demand and ensures the most reliable outcome.

## Boiling Water Sterilization Procedure

The PF TEK syringe sterilization uses a simple pot of boiling water rather than a pressure vessel. The detailed steps are as follows:

1. Fill a pot with water and bring it to a full rolling boil. Ensure there is enough water to fully submerge all syringes.
2. Draw boiling water into each syringe through the needle. Fill the barrel completely.
3. Vigorously squirt the boiling water out of the syringe several times. This flushes the interior of the syringe barrel and needle with scalding water, physically removing and thermally destroying contaminant organisms.
4. Refill the syringe completely with boiling water after the final flush cycle.
5. Replace the needle guard over the needle tip to maintain the water inside and protect the needle.
6. Wrap the entire syringe (barrel, plunger, and capped needle) in tin foil. The foil serves as both insulation to keep the contents hot during the boil and as a contamination barrier after removal.
7. Prepare several syringes in this manner. The PF TEK recommends preparing extra syringes beyond the minimum needed.
8. Carefully drop the foil wrapped syringes into the pot of boiling water.
9. Boil the wrapped syringes for a minimum of one hour with the pot covered.

After the boiling period, allow the pot to cool gradually. Do not remove the syringes while the water is still hot, as rapid cooling can draw contaminants into the syringe through the needle tip. The tin foil wrapping should remain in place until the syringes are needed for the spore syringe preparation procedure.

## Syringe and Needle Specifications

The PF TEK recommends specific syringe sizes for optimal results in the spore syringe preparation workflow. Selecting the right equipment from the start improves outcomes significantly:

- **Minimum size:** 10cc syringes with 18 gauge 1.5 inch needles are the baseline acceptable size
- **Preferred size:** 20cc to 65cc syringes, which hold more spore solution per fill and reduce handling
- **Needle length:** Extra long needles are advantageous for reaching deep into substrate jars during inoculation and for reaching the bottom of spore print jars during syringe loading
- **Gauge:** 18 gauge provides a good balance between flow rate and precision. Finer gauges clog more easily, while coarser gauges may damage delicate mycelium structures.

Larger syringes reduce the number of fill cycles needed when preparing multiple spore syringes from a single spore print jar, minimizing the number of times the sterile jar must be opened and reducing contamination risk.

## Comparison with Pressure Canner Sterilization

The PF TEK also notes that pressure canners can sterilize syringes at 12 PSI for 10 minutes. However, the boiling water method achieves adequate sterilization for syringe preparation without specialized equipment. The key advantages of the boiling method include:

- **Accessibility:** Any cultivator with a kitchen pot can produce sterile syringes
- **Cost:** No capital investment in a pressure canner is required
- **Simplicity:** The procedure involves only boiling water and tin foil
- **Effectiveness:** The one hour boil provides a generous safety margin
- **Scalability:** Multiple syringes can be sterilized simultaneously in a single pot

The boiling water method is sufficient for spore syringe work because syringes are simple hollow devices with no complex internal geometry. The interior is fully exposed to boiling water during the flush cycles, and the extended boil time provides thermal penetration adequate for this application. No special equipment beyond a kitchen pot is needed.

## Integration with Spore Syringe Preparation

Sterile syringes are used in two distinct roles during the PF TEK spore syringe preparation process:

1. **Water injection syringe:** A sterile syringe filled with sterile water is used to inject water into the spore print jar through the needle hole in the special two hole lid. This water suspends the spores for collection.
2. **Spore collection syringe:** A second sterile syringe is inserted through the smaller edge hole to draw the spore laden water back out of the jar.

Using two separate syringes prevents cross contamination between the water source and the collected spore solution. Both syringes must be prepared using the boiling water sterilization method before the spore syringe preparation begins.

## Storage of Sterile Syringes Before Use

After boiling and cooling, sterile syringes should be kept wrapped in their tin foil until the moment they are needed. The foil wrapping is the primary contamination barrier between the sterile syringe interior and the ambient environment. When working with the sterile syringes during spore print harvesting, the tin foil should be removed only at the moment of use.

The needle hole in the jar lid should be covered with tin foil or sterile surgical tape both before and after the syringe is inserted through it. This prevents airborne contaminants from entering the sterile environment of the spore print jar.

## Storage After Spore Loading

Once a spore syringe has been loaded with spore solution, the PF TEK recommends storing it at cool temperatures in a dark place. A properly prepared spore syringe will remain viable for several months and up to a year or more. The needle guard should be replaced after loading, and the needle should be resterilized with an alcohol flame before any subsequent use.

## Syringe Reuse Considerations

The PF TEK describes resterilizing needles with an alcohol flame between uses during inoculation. However, the complete boiling water sterilization of the full syringe assembly is typically performed as a batch process before the spore syringe preparation session. For routine needle cleaning between jar inoculations, the alcohol flaming technique is sufficient.

## Troubleshooting Syringe Sterilization

Several issues can arise during the boiling water sterilization process:

- **Syringe melting:** Plastic syringes must not contact the bottom of the pot directly. Ensure the pot has a vegetable steamer insert or that the syringes are fully suspended in the water column. Direct contact with the hot metal bottom can warp or melt the plastic barrel.
- **Water level:** Maintain enough water to fully submerge all wrapped syringes throughout the one hour boil. If the water level drops significantly, add more boiling water to maintain coverage.
- **Tin foil integrity:** Check that the tin foil wrapping remains intact during boiling. Loose or torn foil can allow water to infiltrate the syringe interior, potentially introducing contaminants during cooling.
- **Incomplete flushing:** If the initial water flush cycles are too brief or too few, residual contaminants inside the syringe barrel may survive the boil. Perform at least three full flush cycles with boiling water before the final fill.

## Syringe Reuse and Resterilization

The PF TEK describes resterilizing needles with an alcohol flame between uses during the inoculation process. However, for complete syringe resterilization between different cultivation sessions, the full boiling water procedure should be repeated. A syringe that has been used to inoculate contaminated jars should not be reused without full re sterilization, as the interior may harbor contaminant organisms.

## Safety Considerations

- Syringes must be fully cooled before use to avoid killing spores on contact
- The one hour boil time should not be shortened under any circumstances
- Tin foil wrapping must remain intact until the syringe is actually needed
- Always use fresh tin foil for each batch of syringes
- Boiling water can cause severe burns; handle pots and syringes with appropriate caution
- Use tongs or a slotted spoon when placing or removing syringes from boiling water
- Keep children and pets away from the boiling pot during the sterilization cycle
