Agaricus bitorquis (Rodman's Agaricus) is a temperate species naturally
found in lawns, gardens, roadside areas, pastures, and on hard-packed
soil. It fruits primarily in spring and to a lesser degree in fall.
Distinguished from close relatives by a double membranous annulus,
this thick-fleshed mushroom has a smooth white convex cap, pinkish
gills darkening to chocolate brown with spore maturity, and dark
chocolate brown spores in mass. Commercial strains include Horst B30
(the original Dutch strain), K26, and K32 (second generation strains
with earlier fruiting, higher yields, and slightly longer stems).

## Growth Parameters and Spawn Preparation

Mycelial types are rhizomorphic to linear, whitish to pale whitish.
The spawn medium is rye grain buffered with calcium carbonate and/or
calcium sulfate. Fruiting substrate is nitrogen-enriched wheat straw
and/or horse manure based compost balanced to 71-74% moisture content.
Preparation follows standard composting procedures with pasteurization
through live steam exposure for 2 hours at 140 degrees F throughout
the substrate. Compost should be filled to a depth of 6-12 inches.

Spawn run parameters: relative humidity 90-100%, substrate temperature
84-86 degrees F (thermal death limits at 93 degrees F over prolonged
period), duration 2 weeks, CO2 at 5,000-10,000 ppm, fresh air
exchanges 0 per hour, and incubation in total darkness. The high spawn
run temperature of 84-86 degrees F is notably higher than Agaricus
brunnescens (77 degrees F), making A. bitorquis a warmth-loving
species within the Agaricus genus.

## Casing and Pinning Requirements

The type of casing is standard peat based with limestone, buffered to
pH 7.5-7.8. A distinctive feature of A. bitorquis is its relatively
long post-casing/pre-pinning period. After casing application, the
substrate temperature should be maintained at 84-86 degrees F with
90+% relative humidity and 5,000-10,000 ppm CO2 for a duration of
3-4 weeks. Fresh air exchanges remain at 0 per hour during this case
run period. The extended case run reflects the species' slower
transition from vegetative to generative growth compared to A.
brunnescens.

Pinhead initiation requires a significant temperature drop from the
spawn run optimum. Relative humidity should be 95%, air temperature
66-70 degrees F, duration 7-14 days, CO2 below 1,000 ppm, and fresh
air exchanges of 2-4 per hour. Light requirements are ambient natural
light or optimally 10 lux in the 370-420 nanometer range. The
temperature differential between spawn run (84-86 degrees F) and
pinning (66-70 degrees F) is approximately 16-20 degrees, providing
a strong fruiting trigger.

## Cropping Cycle and Yield Potential

During cropping, relative humidity is maintained at 85-92% with air
temperature at 66-70 degrees F. CO2 must be kept below 1,000 ppm with
2-4 fresh air exchanges per hour. The cropping duration extends
20-30 days, substantially shorter than many other cultivated species.
Harvest stage is directly before the veil breaks. Flushing intervals
are approximately 7-10 days. Light requirements remain the same as
during pinning.

Average commercial yields are 3-4 pounds per square foot. The
moisture content of harvested mushrooms is approximately 92% water and
8% dry matter. The cropping efficiency potential positions A. bitorquis
as a competitive commercial species, though it has not achieved the
same industry dominance as A. brunnescens. The shorter total cropping
cycle from spawning to harvest completion is an advantage for growers
seeking faster turnover.

## Distinctive Characteristics and Advantages

Agaricus bitorquis possesses several characteristics that distinguish
it from the common button mushroom A. brunnescens. It tolerates
higher substrate temperatures during spawn running (84-86 degrees F
versus 74-78 degrees F), making it more suitable for warm climates or
seasons where cooling capacity is limited. The species is also more
resistant to certain viral diseases that affect A. brunnescens. The
double annulus is a reliable field identification feature.

However, the species has limitations. The extended post-casing period
of 3-4 weeks before pinning requires patience and stable environmental
control. Total cropping duration of 20-30 days is relatively short,
potentially limiting total yield compared to species with longer
cropping periods. The species has not been as extensively bred for
commercial traits as A. brunnescens, meaning strain selection is more
limited. The natural habitat in compacted soils suggests good
tolerance for denser substrate configurations, which has implications
for substrate preparation and filling depth.

## Nutritional Content and Comments

Nutritional content includes crude protein at 26-32% of dry weight
and niacin levels comparable to other Agaricus species. The mushroom
is considered choice edible with a firm texture suitable for various
culinary applications. The species has attracted interest from
researchers studying heat-tolerant mushroom cultivation, particularly
in regions where A. brunnescens production is challenged by warm
growing conditions. Genetic characteristics include basidia that are
bisporic (2-spored), with a secondary homothallic mating system. Clamp
connections are absent. The bisporic nature means each spore receives
one nucleus of each mating type, generating a dikaryotic colony from
the moment of spore germination, similar to A. brunnescens.

## Key Points

- Spawn run temperature: 84-86 degrees F (notably higher than A.
  brunnescens at 74-78 degrees F)
- Thermal death limit: 93 degrees F over prolonged exposure
- Post-casing period: 3-4 weeks before pinning initiation
- Pinning temperature: 66-70 degrees F (16-20 degree drop from spawn
  run)
- Cropping duration: 20-30 days with flushing intervals of 7-10 days
- Average yield: 3-4 lbs per square foot
- Bisporic with secondary homothallic mating system (clamp
  connections absent)
- More heat-tolerant and virus-resistant than A. brunnescens
