# Fungal Degradation of Pesticides (Singh)

## Overview

Synthetic pesticides have been used since 1939 (DDT discovery). Pesticides are classified into three categories: insecticides (35% of use in developed countries), herbicides (45%), and fungicides (15%). Agricultural practices have contaminated many terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems, with pesticide residues in soil and leaching to groundwater.

Fungal degradation of pesticides occurs through cometabolism (best known pathway in fungi). Key biochemical reactions include: alkylation, dealkylation, amide/ester hydrolysis, dehalogenation, dehydrogenation, hydroxylation, ether cleavage, ring cleavage, oxidation, reduction, condensation, and conjugate formation. Complete degradation may occur through the Krebs cycle.

Factors affecting biodegradation: soil type, pH, organic matter, fungal biomass, moisture, and aeration.

## Fungal Degradation of Insecticides

### Chlorinated Insecticides

**DDT:**
- P. chrysosporium mineralizes DDT to 10% as CO2 in 30 days; other white-rot fungi (Pleurotus ostreatus, Phellinus weirii, Polyporus versicolor) achieve 5-7% mineralization
- DDT degraded through co-oxidation by LiP mediator veratryl alcohol
- Mineralization depends on carbon source; cellulose and starch support DDT mineralization best
- LiP inhibitors (EDTA, TEMED) inhibit DDT mineralization
- Formation of DDD during initial non-ligninolytic phase, subsequently degraded

**Lindane (gamma-HCH):**
- P. chrysosporium mineralizes 23.4% as CO2 in 30 days (liquid culture) and 22.8% in 60 days (soil-corncob matrix)
- White-rot fungus DSPM95 degrades 82% at 5-10 ppm within 30 days
- Metabolites include tetrachlorocyclohexene, tetrachlorocyclohexene epoxide, tetrachlorohexenol

**Endosulfan:**
- P. chrysosporium achieves >95% degradation in C-deficient/N-deficient conditions, >90% in N-rich conditions, 61% in C-rich conditions (50 days)
- Trichoderma harzianum degrades 39% in 13 days; produces endosulfan sulfate and endosulfan diol
- Degradation catalyzed by oxidation to endosulfan sulfate followed by hydrolysis to endosulfan diol
- Exogenous NADPH enhances endosulfan metabolism

**Other organochlorines:**
- P. chrysosporium causes significant bioconversion of aldrin, dieldrin, heptachlor, and mirex (poorly mineralized)
- Soil fungi (Fusarium, Penicillium, Rhizopus, Trichoderma) convert heptachlor to heptachlor epoxide
- Chlorpyrifos: 27.5% degradation by P. chrysosporium in 18 days

### Organophosphorus Insecticides
- P. chrysosporium degrades chlorpyrifos (27.5%), fonofos (12.2%), and terbufos (26.6% as CO2)
- Coriolus versicolor degrades chlorpyrifos 36% in biobed matrix over 42 days

## Fungal Degradation of Herbicides

### Phenoxyalkanoate Compounds (2,4-D, 2,4,5-T)
- P. chrysosporium mineralizes 62% of [14C]-2,4,5-T in nitrogen-limited cultures (30 days)
- P. chrysosporium mineralizes 32.5% of 2,4,5-T in contaminated soil amended with ground corncobs
- Mixture of 2,4-D and 2,4,5-T mineralized simultaneously at 46.5% with no mutual inhibition
- LiP and MnP are NOT required for 2,4-D mineralization (peroxidase-negative mutant also mineralizes)
- Cunninghamella elegans degrades 2,4-D 33% in 5 days; Rhizoctonia solani achieves 60% in 5 days
- Aspergillus penicilloides degrades 2,4-D 52% in 7 days, producing 2,4-dichlorophenol

### Acylanilide Herbicides (alachlor, butachlor, metolachlor, propanil)
- **Alachlor**: Ceriporiopsis subvermispora completely degrades alachlor in 122 days; Cunninghamella elegans transforms 98.6% in 4 days; mineralization slow in agricultural soils (<3% in 30 days)
- **Butachlor**: Fusarium solani degrades 70-85% producing 30+ metabolites through dechlorination, dehydrogenation, debutoxymethylation, C-dealkylation, N-dealkylation, O-dealkylation, hydroxylation, and cyclization
- **Propanil**: Penicillium, Pullularia, and Fusarium solani hydrolyze via acylamidase forming anilines and aliphatic groups; F. solani releases 3,4-dichloroaniline (DCA)
- **Metolachlor**: C. elegans metabolizes to hydroxylated products less toxic than parent compound
- No isolated microorganism known to mineralize alachlor aromatic ring to CO2

### Phenylurea Herbicides (diuron, linuron, chlortoluron, isoproturon)
- **Rhizoctonia solani**: Degrades diuron, chlorbromuron, fenuron, fluometuron, linuron, metobromuron; broad substrate specificity indicates specific enzymatic systems; stepwise demethylation of fluometuron
- **Bjerkandera adusta**: Produces MnP and LiP; degrades chlortoluron 98%, diuron 92%, isoproturon 88% in 15 days
- **Coriolus versicolor**: Degrades diuron almost completely in 42 days in liquid culture; 52% in biobed matrix
- **Botrytis cinerea**: Depletes linuron almost completely and metobromuron 92%
- Of 100 micromycete strains tested, 17 degraded isoproturon, 4 degraded diuron, and 2 degraded chlortoluron by 50%+
- No correlation between ligninolytic activity and diuron degradation observed

### s-Triazine Herbicides (atrazine, simazine, terbuthylazine)
- **Atrazine**: Highly persistent (half-life 300-500 days); P. chrysosporium mineralizes 24% after 16 days through ethyl group
- **Pleurotus pulmonarius**: Produces mainly N-dealkylated metabolites (deethylatrazine, deisopropylatrazine, deethyldeisopropylatrazine); solid-state fermentation on cotton/wheat straw degrades 74% in 42 days
- N-dealkylation is more common than hydroxylation in fungi
- **Hypholoma fasciculare**: Best degrader of terbuthylazine (97% in liquid culture, 37% in biobed matrix)
- **Coriolus versicolor**: Highest atrazine degradation in liquid media (86%); 51% in biobed matrix

## Fungal Degradation of Fungicides

### Organomercurial Compounds
- Mercury-resistant fungi detoxify organomercurial fungicides through cleavage of the carbon-mercury bond
- Volatilization of metallic mercury occurs

### Organosulfur Compounds
- Fungi degrade dithiocarbamate fungicides through hydrolysis and carbon disulfide release
- Trichoderma species are effective degraders

## Biobeds: On-Farm Pesticide Bioremediation

Biobeds are on-farm bioremediation systems designed to retain pesticides and facilitate natural attenuation:
- Mixed substrate (straw, soil, peat) provides organic matter and microbial diversity
- Selected white-rot fungi enhance degradation of pesticide residues
- Coriolus versicolor, Hypholoma fasciculare, and Stereum hirsutum effectively degrade atrazine, terbuthylazine, and diuron in biobed matrices
- Biobeds offer practical, low-cost solution for treating pesticide rinsate and spills at farm level
- Solid-state fermentation with white-rot fungi on agricultural waste substrates (cotton/wheat straw) provides effective herbicide detoxification

## Biosensors for Pesticide Detection

Fungal enzyme-based biosensors enable rapid pesticide monitoring:
- **Acetylcholinesterase-based**: Detect organophosphorus pesticides (paraoxon, chlorpyrifos, diazinon) at 0.2 ng sensitivity
- **Tyrosinase-based**: Detect atrazine and carbamate pesticides through enzyme inhibition
- **Laccase from Coriolus hirsutus**: Used for ELISA detection of 2,4-D (10-20 ng/ml in 1.5-2 hours)
- **Mushroom-based biosensor**: Monitoring diethyldithiocarbamates
- **Lipid membrane biosensor**: Rapid analysis of triazine herbicide mixtures
