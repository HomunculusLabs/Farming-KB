# [[paddy-straw-mushroom]] Cultivation ([[volvariella-volvacea]])

Volvariella volvacea is one of the most prodigiously fast-growing
cultivated mushrooms, prized across tropical and subtropical Asia
for its rich flavor and rapid life cycle. Once you have tasted fresh
Paddy Straw mushrooms, it is easy to understand the high esteem this
mushroom has attained in Asian cuisine. Fresh V. volvacea is
considered one of the best of all edible mushrooms, though canned
specimens lack the richness of fresh fruitbodies.

## Taxonomy and Common Names

- Paddy Straw Mushroom, Straw Mushroom, Chinese Mushroom
- Fukurotake (Japanese)
- V. volvacea and V. bakeri are considered synonymous
- Cap 5-15 cm broad, egg-shaped at first, expanding to campanulate
- Smoky brown to cigar-brown, fading with age and light exposure
- Gills free, white at first, soon pinkish, close to crowded
- Spores pink to salmon brown, 7.5-9 x 4-6 microns
- Encased in a distinctive thick volva at the stem base

## Temperature Requirements

This mushroom thrives at warm temperatures between 75-95 degrees F
(24-35 degrees C) and dies when temperatures drop below 45 degrees F
(7 degrees C). This temperature range limits its cultivation in all
but the warmest climates or months of the year. Most cultures die
under cold storage; many cultivators store cultures at ambient room
temperature (above 45 degrees F / 7 degrees C). Cold weather strains
have yet to be developed.

## Natural Habitat and Distribution

Thriving throughout tropical and subtropical Asia, this mushroom
grows singly or in groups on [[composting]] rice straw, sugar cane
residue, leaf piles, and compost heaps during periods of warm
weather from spring through autumn. Reports of this species in
northern temperate climates have been traced to "escapees" from
mushroom cultivators.

## two Cultivation Methods

Two methods have evolved for cultivating V. volvacea. The first is
outdoors, simple, and low-tech, owing its success to the rapidity
of the life cycle. The second method has been developed for
intensive indoor commercial cultivation, more closely resembling
the composting procedures practiced by the [[agaricus-bisporus-cultivation-biology]] industry.

## Outdoor Cultivation on Rice Straw

After the rice harvest, farmers mulch their rice straw into mounds
and inoculate them with commercial spawn. This companion method of
farming has given economic stability to many rural populations,
providing much needed supplementary income to rice farmers during
the off-season. The mushroom is an ideal species for low-tech
cultivation by rural people in subtropical and tropical climates.

Rice straw is chopped into 4-5 inch pieces, soaked and stacked
outside to tenderize for no more than two days. A moisture content
of 75% is desired. Cottonseed hulls are soaked for 2-7 days,
allowed to ferment, and layered onto the straw at 10-20% of the
rice straw mass. Spawn is added directly to the cottonseed hulls
as each layer is built. The mass is covered to achieve at least
90 degrees F (32 degrees C) for 5-7 days.

## Indoor Commercial Cultivation

For indoor cultivation, the mass is mixed together and bulk steamed.
The nitrogen-rich cottonseed hulls contribute to self-heating.
When the mass cools to 90-100 degrees F (32-38 degrees C), grain
spawn is thoroughly mixed through. A moist, shallow casing layer
can stimulate even mushroom formation 4-6 days later.

## Growth Parameters

### Spawn Run
- Temperature: 75-95 degrees F (24-35 degrees C)
- Humidity: 80-95% RH
- Duration: 5-10 days
- CO2: >5000 ppm; Fresh Air: 1 per hour; No light

### Primordia Formation
- Temperature: 80-90 degrees F (27-32 degrees C)
- Humidity: 90-100%; Duration: 4-6 days
- CO2: 1000-5000 ppm; Fresh Air: 4-5 per hour
- Light: 250-500 lux

### Fruitbody Development
- Temperature: 80-90 degrees F (27-32 degrees C)
- Humidity: 85-95%; Duration: 6-10 days
- CO2: 1000-5000 ppm; Fresh Air: 4-5 per hour
- Light: 500-750 lux

### Cropping Cycle
7-12 days total. The pH optimum for fruiting is 7.5-8.0.

## Yield and Harvest

On average, V. volvacea produces two substantial flushes in quick
succession, with the first giving 75% of total yield and the second
producing the remaining 25%. Mushrooms should be picked before the
universal veil breaks, i.e. in the egg form. In the matter of hours,
egg-shaped fruitbodies develop into expanded caps. Light governs
color and overall quality of the harvestable crop.

## Nutritional Content

- Protein: 26-30%; Carbohydrates: 45-50%
- Fiber: 9-12%; Ash: 9-13%
- Rich in vitamins C and B, minerals and amino acids

## "Green Composting" Technique

Green composting is the simultaneous inoculation of the compost
while it is being formulated. The heat generated within the
composting straw/cottonseed hull mass accelerates the growth of
the thermophilic and heat-tolerant V. volvacea. This mushroom can
be grown on uncomposted straw-based substrates although yields are
substantially improved if the substrate is fermented or short-cycle
composted for 4-5 days, pasteurized, and inoculated.

## Economic Importance

This mushroom is widely cultivated by farmers in China, Thailand,
Vietnam and Cambodia for supplemental income. The egg-form is
self-preserving, limiting moisture loss and extending shelf life.
Cultivating the Paddy Straw mushroom is difficult in most regions
of North America. Louisiana, Georgia, Alabama, and certain coastal
regions of Texas have climates suitable for outdoor cultivation.
