# Purdue Vegetable Harvest and Storage Guide

A [[vegetable-storage-conditions-by-temperature-and-humidity]] category. Based on Purdue University Cooperative Extension Service recommendations (HO-32-W).

## Storage Categories Overview

Vegetables are grouped into four storage categories based on their [[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]] requirements:

| Category | Temperature | Relative Humidity |
|----------|------------|-------------------|
| Cold, Moist | 32-40 degrees F | 90-95% |
| Cool, Moist | 45-50 degrees F | 80-90% |
| Cool, Dry | 45-55 degrees F | 50-60% |
| Warm, Dry | 55-60 degrees F | 60-70% |
| Warm, Moist | 55-60 degrees F | 80-85% |

## Cold, Moist Storage (32-40 degrees F, 90-95% RH)

### Root Crops

**Beets:** Begin harvesting when roots reach 1 inch in diameter for tender greens. Main harvest at 2 to 3 inches. Harvest spring plantings before hot weather in July; fall beets before the first moderate freeze. For storage, wash roots, trim tops to 1/2 inch, place in perforated plastic bags. Storage in refrigerator, cold moist cellar, or pit. Storage life: 2 to 4 months.

**Carrots:** Harvest spring carrots before hot weather (July). Fall-planted carrots should be harvested before the first moderate freeze. For storage, wash roots, trim tops to 1/2 inch, place in perforated plastic bags. Storage life: 2 to 4 months.

**Horseradish:** Harvest after several severe freezes. Can be stored in the ground all winter with a mulch of straw or leaves, digging as needed. May also be stored like carrots.

**Parsnips:** Harvest in late fall after several moderate freezes. Cold exposure develops the sweet flavor. Same storage requirements as carrots.

**Irish Potatoes:** Harvest when tops have yellowed or died. Do not leave in ground — high soil temperatures accelerate over-ripening. Cure for about one week in a shaded, well-ventilated place. Remove excess soil and discard diseased or damaged tubers. Avoid exposing to light (causes greening). Store above 40 degrees F in a cool basement with high humidity and good ventilation. Storage life: 2 to 4 months.

**Radishes:** Harvest at 1/2 to 1 inch diameter. Wash, trim both tap root and tops, store in plastic bags in the refrigerator for up to 1 month. Winter or black radishes stored the same as carrots.

**Salsify:** Same harvest and storage procedures as parsnips.

**Turnips:** Can be harvested from 1 inch diameter. Best as a fall crop, withstands several light freezes. Store same as carrots.

### Greens

**Swiss Chard:** Harvest continuously by breaking off outer leaves. A spring planting provides greens from early summer to the first moderate freeze. Store up to 2 weeks in refrigerator.

**Collards, Kale, Mustard, and Spinach:** Harvest leaves at suitable size — either the whole plant or outer, larger leaves. Wash and trim. Greens do not store well but may be kept in plastic bags in the refrigerator for up to 2 weeks. Freeze any surplus.

**Endive (Escarole):** Harvest the whole plant. Wash thoroughly, gather leaves, and tie with a rubber band. Store in plastic bags in refrigerator for up to 3 weeks.

**Lettuce:** Head, semi-head, and leaf lettuce store up to 2 weeks in perforated plastic bags in the refrigerator.

**Parsley:** Snip stems close to the ground for successive harvests. Do not cut more than half the plant at once. Over-winters in protected places. Stores 1 or more weeks in plastic bags in the refrigerator.

### Cole Crops

**Broccoli:** Harvest terminal head while florets are tight and green-blue. Smaller side heads develop afterward. Store in perforated plastic bags for up to 1 week. Freeze surplus.

**Brussels Sprouts:** Harvest sprouts when firm, starting from the bottom. Can withstand moderate freezes. Harvest all before the first severe freeze. Store in perforated bags for up to 3 weeks. Freeze surplus. Best grown for fall harvest.

**Cabbage:** Harvest when heads are solid. Store in refrigerator, cold cellar, or outdoor pit in plastic bags for up to 2 months.

**Cauliflower:** Tie outer leaves above the head when curds are 1 to 2 inches in diameter. Heads ready about 2 weeks after tying. Store in perforated plastic bags for up to 2 weeks. Freeze surplus.

**Chinese Cabbage:** Best grown in fall. Harvest after the first moderate frost. Stores up to 2 months in cold conditions.

**Kohlrabi:** Harvest when swollen stems are 2 to 3 inches in diameter. Stems become woody if left too long. Cut off root and leaf stalks, store in plastic bags. Storage life: 2 to 4 weeks.

## Cool, Moist Storage (45-50 degrees F, 80-90% RH)

**Cucumbers:** Harvest before seeds become half-size — typically 1.5 to 2.5 inches in diameter and 5 to 8 inches long. Store slicing cucumbers in the warmest part of the refrigerator in plastic bags for about 1 week. Pickling cucumbers cooled quickly in ice water, stored up to 2 days.

**Eggplant:** Harvest when fruits are nearly full grown but color is still bright. Not adapted to long storage — keep in warmer part of refrigerator for about 1 week.

**Peppers, Sweet:** Harvest when fruits are firm and full size. Leave on plant for red color if desired. Store 2 to 3 weeks in the warmer part of the refrigerator in plastic bags. Cool [[winter-rye]]:** Harvest before frost when skin is hard and colors darken. Remove from vine with a portion of stem attached. Store on shelves in single layers so air can circulate around them. Properly cured squash can store for several months.

## Warm, Moist Storage (55-60 degrees F, 80-85% RH)

**Sweet Potatoes:** Harvest in fall before frost and freezing temperatures. Handle carefully during digging. Cure for 1 week at 80-85 degrees F. Ideal storage is at 55 degrees F and 85% relative humidity. This can be accomplished in a basement using ventilated boxes covered with periodically moistened burlap sacks.
