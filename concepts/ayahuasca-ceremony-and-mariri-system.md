# Ayahuasca Ceremony and the Mariri System

## Overview

The ayahuasca ceremony, as described in [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]]'s visionary paintings and accompanying text, is a deeply structured [[powell-shamanic-wisdom-and-ritual-practice]] of Amazonian vegetalismo (plant-based shamanism). The ceremony involves the ingestion of the ayahuasca brew under the guidance of a vegetalista (plant healer), accompanied by the singing of icaros (sacred melodies), and incorporates a complex system of magical substances called mariris. These ceremonies serve both therapeutic and initiatory purposes, connecting participants with an extensive spirit world populated by serpents, nymphs, extraterrestrial beings, and elemental forces.

## The Ayahuasca Session

### Preparation and Gathering

Ayahuasca sessions take place in a house, typically at night around nine or ten o'clock. Participants include men, women, and young people, many of whom bring children suffering from illnesses not normally addressed by [[fukuoka-eastern-western-medicine-healing-world-crisis]] — such as mal aire (illness produced by an evil breeze) or sorcery-induced afflictions. Before the ceremony begins, people exchange stories about the yakuruna (water people), the pink dolphin, the Chullachaki (forest spirit), the tanehi (spirit of a deceased person), and other marvels experienced when the spiritual world touches the physical.

The vegetalista advises participants about dietary requirements and other prescriptions necessary when ingesting ayahuasca.

### The Ritual of Ingestion

The vegetalista first sings an icaro to strengthen the brew, ensuring beautiful and wise visions for the recipient. Before drinking, the person must pray to the ayahuasca spirit — "Grandfather, please, let me see all those things I want to see" — which may include wishes to see a distant relative, find something lost, or understand an illness.

The vegetalista indicates the exact spot on the gourd where he blew and sang, because the recipient must drink from that spot. After ingesting the brew, participants retire to a central room and sit in ritual order. Complete silence is maintained.

### Signs and Omens

During the session, the vegetalista blows smoke (sometimes with perfume or camphorated water) on each person's head. In the darkness, participants begin to yawn — a single yawn portends beautiful visions, while multiple yawns indicate either very strong visions or an incoming attack by enemies. Contractions in the left arm suggest a sorcerer's attack, while contractions in the right arm mean the person will see everything they asked of the ayahuasca spirit.

### The Role of Prayer and Icaros

The vegetalista begins with a prayer to Jesus or another divinity, according to his faith, requesting protection from evil. He then sings an icaro to bring forth the visions. The icaros are the central technology of the ceremony — melodic incantations that structure and direct the visionary experience.

## Shamanic Hierarchy and Initiation

### Types of Shamans

Amazonian vegetalismo recognizes a hierarchy of shamanic practitioners:

- **Sumiruna**: The highest level, capable of levitation, astral travel, and commanding the deepest esoteric knowledge. Sumirunas wear elaborate ceremonial dress incorporating animal skins and plant materials.
- **Muraya**: Masters of the aquatic realms, able to descend to the bottom of rivers and command water spirits. They work through perfume and possess great spiritual power.
- **Banco**: Possess sublime powers, armed with flying wheels and diamond swords. They are specialists in high alchemy.
- **Vegetalista/Curandero**: A plant healer who works with ayahuasca and other plant-teachers for healing.

### The Initiation Ceremony (Vision 5)

During initiation, an apprentice is seated upon a golden throne while queens of the great Tion dress him in royal clothes, gold bracelets, and precious stones, and finally the emperor's crown. Queen Sittja fits the crown while Queen Baroty places flowers on the coronation altar. Queen Caracol carries a diamond scepter and roses from Berenjena. Caracol Camira is described as a powerful lady whose task is the correction of great wizards, psychometrics, masters of telekinesis, sorcerers, rabdomantes, murayas, bancos, and sumirunas.

Muses descend from above with harps, lyres, and lutes, intoning the cadence of the icaro huiksa-nanay.

## The Curandera's Transformation (Vision 7)

In one remarkable vision, a great vegetalista curandera transforms into a beautiful queen wearing a golden crown with the body of a blue serpent marked with disc-shaped patterns. This transformation demonstrates the shaman's power and frightens less courageous companions. The curandera reveals a cosmic ship — a disc-shaped craft of very complicated structure with flashing violet, orange, and yellow luminescence — built by beings of intelligence superior to humans and capable of fantastic speeds.

The vision includes chiripa-machacos (rainbow snakes with red horns) whose icaro can elucidate anything in the subconscious; flying nymphs with magic nets that penetrate the earth to capture intruding sorcerers; and the yana-cocodrilo (black crocodile) with the strength of a leviathan, capable of destroying enemy armies.

## The Mariri System (Vision 8)

### Queen Inamullo

The Queen Inamullo (Necklace of Thorns), also known as Huacra-chukcha (Hair of Thorns), is the master teacher of the mariri arts. She carries a jar of delicate balsams for the protection of mariris that can harmonise with rainbow vibrations. Her feathered head-dress absorbs curses and evil spells.

### Types of Mariris

The mariri is a magical phlegm — a substance developed within the shaman's body that serves as a primary tool in vegetalismo. Several distinct types exist:

- **Yana-mariri (Black mariri)**: Received by the vegetalista in the red shirt. Associated with the deepest and most powerful workings.
- **Yura-mariri (White mariri)**: Received by the white-shirted vegetalista as a white cord. Used for purification and healing.
- **Puka-mariri (Red mariri)**: Received by the blue-shirted shaman. Associated with vigorous defensive and offensive capabilities.
- **Orange-colored mariri**: Received by the yellow-shirted vegetalista.

Each mariri plays a specific role in the vegetalista's ciencia palística (plant science). The mann is described as being "spilled forth like a colored cord."

### Tutelary Spirits of the Mariri

Queen Inamullo is served by tutelary spirits including the sacha-supay (jungle demon), the supayisnachi (maquisapa ghost), the yura-huacra (white-horned deer), the wiracocha-ailco (dog of the water froth), the canero fish, the puka-cakila (red-mouthed fish), the yahuarachi (slime fish allied to the boa), and the boa-sacha plant.

### Associated Serpent Beings

Above the queen appear the killo-caranchi (yellow skins) whose hair takes the form of cobras, engaged in magical tambourine dances. Three great serpents are depicted:

- **Yakumama**: The uppermost serpent, blowing forth its power as fiery-headed people.
- **Purahua**: The centre serpent, spitting magnetic charges like chains of energetically armoured men locking arms.
- **Acoron/Boa mama**: The bottom serpent, releasing waves of molecules in the form of maggots, worms, snakes, leeches, fowl, and lizards.

## El Solitario — The Shaman in Solitude (Vision 9)

When a shaman takes ayahuasca alone, he may encounter three celestial globes with guardian spirits. One spirit is a pinsha (toucan) standing near the tamshi plant (*Heteropsis jenmanii*), believed to grow from the body of an isula (a large ant, possibly *Paraponera clavata*). The icaro of this bird is sung to make a distant loved one return home. The globe represents this icaro. Behind the toucan appears a Yakumama water snake.

## Key Plant Teachers Mentioned

- **Yahuar-piripiri** (*[[eleutherine-bulbosa]]*): Used with the yahuar-toro to cure mal aire
- **Caballo piripiri** (*Cyperus sp.*): Mixed with jagua/huito (*[[genipa-americana]]*) to acquire great strength; requires eight days of strict dietary restrictions
- **Pichanamachaco** (unidentified): Cures poisonous snake and animal bites
- **Bobinzana** (*Calliandra angustifolia*): Gives the ayahuasca purge more power to cleanse impurities
- **Sacha-cebolla** (wild onion): Cures wounds; crushed root used as a warm plaster to extract wooden splinters
- **Lupuna** (*Ceiba sp.*): Tree whose spirit is a wise old nymph; its canopy serves as a sanctuary for forest nymphs

## Dietary Restrictions in Vegetalismo

Dietary discipline (dieta) is fundamental to Amazonian plant medicine. After applying the caballo piripiri preparation, practitioners must avoid the sun, salt, sweets, garlic, liquor, and pig fat for eight days, while abstaining from sex and socializing with sexually active individuals. Such restrictions recur throughout the text as essential to the efficacy of plant medicines and the development of shamanic powers.
