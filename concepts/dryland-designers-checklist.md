## Dryland Designers' Checklist

This checklist, drawn from [[drip-irrigation]] (targeted delivery with minimal evaporative loss).
- **Broadscale cropping** requires both windbreak protection and water harvesting infrastructure. Mollison recommends a ratio of **20 hectares of runoff collection to 1 hectare of sown crop**, reflecting the vast catchment area needed to concentrate sufficient water for field-scale production.
- **Safe dam sites** should be identified near settlements, ideally in shaded valleys or on streams that can be diverted to drop silt before water enters storage. Shade reduces evaporative losses from open water surfaces.
- **Water can be stored in sand-filled dams or gabion-stabilised terraces**, both of which reduce evaporation and siltation compared to conventional open reservoirs.
- **Greywater should be routed to toilet systems** for secondary use, conserving fresh water for drinking and food production.
- **Deep bores or pumped wells must never be exploited beyond the recharge capacity** of the local aquifer. Over-extraction leads to saline intrusion, land subsidence, and the permanent degradation of groundwater resources.
- **All bore water must be tested** for salts, radioactives, nitrates, fluorine, and biological contamination before use for domestic or agricultural purposes.
- **Wherever water is infiltrated into the ground** — whether via swales, dams, or irrigation — trees should be planted to transpire excess water and maintain low salt levels in the root zone.
