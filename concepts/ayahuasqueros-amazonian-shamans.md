## Ayahuasqueros — Amazonian Ayahuasca Shamans

Ayahuasqueros are Amazonian shamans who specialize in the preparation and ceremonial administration of ayahuasca, the psychoactive brew used for healing, divination, and spiritual exploration throughout the Amazon basin. The term comes from the Spanish *ayahuasquero*, literally "one who makes or works with ayahuasca." These practitioners serve as the primary guides for both indigenous communities and the growing international population of ayahuasca tourists seeking the brew's effects.

## Roles and Functions

The ayahuasquero occupies a complex and multifaceted role within Amazonian society that extends well beyond the simple administration of a psychoactive substance. In traditional contexts, the ayahuasquero functions simultaneously as healer, diagnostician, herbalist, spiritual counselor, community leader, and intermediary between the human world and the spirit world.

The diagnostic process in an ayahuasquero's practice often begins during the ceremony itself. Through their own heightened sensitivity under the influence of ayahuasca, the shaman perceives the energetic state of each participant, identifying blockages, attachments, illness, or spiritual imbalance. This diagnostic vision then guides the selection and singing of specific [[amazonian-shamanic-battles-and-defensive-powers]] practices. Vegetalismo emerged as a distinct tradition in the mid-twentieth century, particularly in the [[sacred-mushroom-use-mexico-teonanacatl-indigenous-traditions]] as well as Catholic, Espiritista, and other spiritual influences. This syncretism makes vegetalismo particularly adaptable and has contributed to its popularity among both mestizo Peruvians and foreign visitors.

## Training and Apprenticeship

The training of an ayahuasquero is a demanding process that typically spans many years, though the specifics vary considerably between indigenous and mestizo traditions. In both cases, however, the training involves far more than the mere memorization of recipes and chants. It requires the development of extraordinary perceptual capacities, emotional resilience, and a deep relationship with the plant world.

### Mestizo Vegetalista Training Through Dietas

For mestizo vegetalistas, the dieta is the central training method. During a dieta, which typically lasts from several weeks to several months, the practitioner isolates themselves in a simple jungle shelter, consumes a specific teacher plant regularly, and follows a strict regimen.

The dietary restrictions during a dieta typically exclude salt, sugar, oil, alcohol, pork, spicy foods, and sexual activity. Some dietas also prohibit cold water, bathing in rivers, and exposure to strong sunlight. These restrictions are believed to make the body "clean" and receptive to the plant's teachings.

Over the course of many dietas with different plants, the vegetalista builds their pharmacological knowledge, their repertoire of icaros, and their spiritual power. The process is understood as building relationships with plant spirits who become allies in the practitioner's healing work.
