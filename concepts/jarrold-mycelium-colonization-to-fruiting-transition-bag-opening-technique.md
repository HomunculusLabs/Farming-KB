# Jarrold Mycelium Colonization to Fruiting Transition and Bag Opening Technique

The **Jarrold mycelium colonization to fruiting transition and bag opening technique** is
a set of procedures described by D.H. Jarrold in *Indoor Mushroom Growing Technique for
the Boil-A-Bag*. It covers the critical phase between full [[mycelium]] colonization of a
boil a bag substrate and transition into a [[aquarium-humidity-chamber-mushroom-fruiting]] for fruiting. The
method is notable for its practical bag-opening approach that retains a small base for
stable placement within the chamber.

## Overview

After spawn inoculation, the substrate bag enters a colonisation period during
which the mycelium consumes the straw-based medium. Jarrold's method provides clear visual
indicators for determining when colonisation is complete and how to transition the bag into
the [[fruiting-environment-parameters]] with minimal disturbance to the [[beber-mycelial-network-graph-theory-cord-routing]].

## Monitoring Mycelium Growth

Within a few days of inoculation, visible white [[mycelium]] growth appears at the points
where spawn was introduced into the substrate. This initial growth confirms that
the spawn is viable and the sterilisation procedure was successful. Growers should
inspect bags periodically without opening them or disturbing the filter can.

### Signs of Successful Colonisation vs. Contamination

**Successful colonisation** shows uniform white coverage, dense rope-like mycelial strands,
no off-colours (green, black, pink, or slimy patches), and a clean earthy odour.
**Contamination** indicators include mould colonies (*Trichoderma*, *Aspergillus*), slimy
discoloured patches, foul odours, or a sudden halt in mycelium expansion. Contaminated bags
must be discarded immediately to prevent the spread of contaminants.

## Full Colonisation and Timing

Full colonisation is reached when the mycelium has **completely covered** the straw
substrate with a solid white mat. Jarrold recommends placing inoculated bags inside a
cardboard box in a **warm place** for germination and growth. The box blocks light to
prevent premature pinning and maintains a stable microclimate.

The colonisation period typically spans two to four weeks depending on temperature, spawn
rate, and substrate volume. Warmer temperatures within the optimal range accelerate
colonisation, but excessive heat promotes bacterial growth and stresses the mycelium.

## Transition to Fruiting

Once fully colonised, the bag is ready for the [[mushroom-fruiting-environment]]:

1. **Remove the filter can** from the top of the bag
2. **Transfer the colonised bag** to the prepared humidity chamber
3. **Open the bag** using the technique described below

Removing the filter can exposes the mycelium to the elevated humidity and reduced
carbon dioxide levels necessary to initiate fruiting.

## The Humidity Chamber

Jarrold's humidity chamber is a **4 ft × 2 ft fish tank** lined with soaked hydro pellets (expanded clay pellets) at the bottom. The pellets slowly release moisture into
the chamber air, maintaining the high humidity levels that mushroom primordia require.

A **plastic sheet** draped over the top creates a seal that **keeps humidity inside** the
chamber while **keeping flies and airborne contaminants out**.

## Fresh Air Exchange

fresh air exchange (FAE) is essential for healthy fruiting. Jarrold instructs growers
to **fan the tank twice daily** by briefly lifting the plastic sheet and wafting air into
the chamber. This introduces oxygen and expels excess carbon dioxide, which would otherwise
inhibit proper [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]]. The twice-daily schedule should continue through the
entire fruiting cycle.

## Bag Opening Technique

The bag opening technique is a distinctive element of Jarrold's method:

1. **Cut the bag down both sides** from the top toward the base
2. **Fold back or remove** the upper plastic to expose the colonised substrate surface
