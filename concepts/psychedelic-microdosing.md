# Psychedelic Microdosing

Psychedelic microdosing is the practice of taking sub-perceptual doses of psychedelic compounds — doses small enough that they do not produce overt hallucinations or altered states, but large enough to potentially influence mood, cognition, and creativity. The practice has gained enormous popular interest since the mid-2010s, though scientific evidence for its claimed benefits remains limited and mixed.

## Definition

A microdose is typically defined as 1/10th to 1/20th of a standard recreational dose — approximately:

- **LSD**: 5-15 micrograms (a full recreational dose is 100-200 micrograms). See [[lsd-compound-profile]] for full compound details.
- **Psilocybin mushrooms**: 0.05-0.25 grams dried (a full dose is 1-3+ grams). See [[psilocybin-compound-profile]] for details.
- **DMT**: Very rarely microdosed due to short duration; if used, typically 1-2 mg orally

The key principle is sub-perceptual: if you can clearly feel the effects, it is not a microdose. Effects should be subtle enough that a naive observer would not notice any change.

## Microdosing Protocols

### Fadiman Protocol (Most Widely Used)
Developed by psychologist James Fadiman based on self-reports:

- **Dose day**: Take microdose on day 1
- **Off days**: Days 2 and 3 off
- **Repeat**: Day 4 dose, then 2 days off
- **Schedule**: Every 3 days (dose on days 1, 4, 7, 10, etc.)
- **Duration**: Typically 4-8 weeks, then evaluate and take a break
- **Tracking**: Users are encouraged to keep detailed notes on mood, energy, focus, and social interactions

The 2-day interval is designed to prevent tolerance buildup and allow for baseline comparison days.

### Stamets Stack
Developed by mycologist [[mycoremediation-stamets-approach]], this combines microdosing with functional mushrooms:

- **Components**: Psilocybin microdose (0.1g dried) + Lion's Mane mushroom (100mg) + Niacin (vitamin B3, 100-200mg)
- **Schedule**: 4 days on, 3 days off
- **Rationale**:
  - Psilocybin: neuroplasticity and mood enhancement
  - Lion's mane (Hericium erinaceus): contains erinacines and hericenones that stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF) production, potentially synergizing with psilocybin's neuroplastic effects
  - Niacin (flush): causes vasodilation that Stamets theorizes helps distribute compounds across the blood-brain barrier and into peripheral nervous tissue
- **Niacin flush**: Users should expect and understand the niacin flush (redness, warmth, itching) — it is not an allergic reaction

### Other Protocols
- **Every other day**: Simpler but less tested
- **5 days on, 2 days off**: Mimics work week, but tolerance may develop
- **As needed**: Occasional use before specific tasks or events

## Reported Benefits

Based primarily on self-report surveys (the largest being from Imperial College London, n=~2800):

### Most Commonly Reported
- Improved mood and overall well-being
- Increased focus and concentration
- Enhanced creativity (both convergent and divergent thinking)
- Increased energy and motivation
- Improved social confidence and ease
- Greater mindfulness and present-moment awareness

### Less Commonly Reported
- Reduced anxiety (some report increased anxiety)
- Improved physical performance (exercise)
- Reduced substance use (alcohol, caffeine)
- Improved sleep quality
- Spiritual or philosophical insights

## Research Status

### What the Science Says (as of 2026)

The evidence for microdosing remains preliminary and mixed:

**Supportive findings:**
- Consistent self-report benefits across multiple large surveys
- Some controlled studies show improvements in mood and cognitive flexibility
- Animal studies demonstrate neuroplastic effects at low doses (increased dendritic spine density, BDNF expression)
- EEG studies show altered brain connectivity patterns

**Limiting findings:**
- Several well-designed placebo-controlled studies (particularly from the University of Chicago) found no significant differences between microdose and placebo groups on most measures
- Strong expectation/placebo effects likely account for a significant portion of reported benefits
- Self-selection bias in surveys (people who microdose tend to be those who find it helpful)
- No large-scale, long-term clinical trials have been completed
- Optimal dose, frequency, and duration have not been established scientifically

**What is established:**
- Microdosing is generally safe in the short term for healthy adults
- Sub-threshold doses do produce measurable neurobiological changes
- The practice is widespread and growing in popularity

## Substances Used for Microdosing

### LSD
- Most commonly microdosed substance
- Precise dosing is challenging (liquid solutions offer best accuracy)
- Long half-life means effects may carry over into off days
- Duration of active effects: 8-12 hours even at microdose levels

### Psilocybin
- Second most common
- Dosing accuracy depends on mushroom potency (variable)
- Shorter duration than LSD (4-6 hours)
- More sedating at sub-threshold doses

### Other Substances
- **DMT**: Rarely microdosed; very short duration
- **Mescaline**: Occasional use; longer duration may interfere with daily activities
- **Ibogaine**: Not recommended for microdosing due to cardiac risks
- **MDMA**: Not typically microdosed; dose-response curve is steep
- **Non-classical psychedelics**: Ketamine microdosing has been studied; Amanita muscaria microdosing is emerging

## Risks and Unknowns

### Known Risks
- **Serotonin syndrome**: Risk when combined with SSRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic medications. See [[psychedelic-safety-and-contra-indications]]
- **Anxiety increase**: Some users report increased anxiety, especially during early sessions
- **Sleep disruption**: LSD's long duration can interfere with sleep even at microdoses
- **Overdose**: Accidentally taking a full dose instead of a microdose is the most common acute risk
- **Dependence on the practice**: Psychological dependence on microdosing for productivity/mood is possible

### Unknowns
- **Long-term effects**: No data on effects of months or years of regular microdosing
- **Optimal protocol**: No scientific consensus on best dose, frequency, or duration
- **Individual variation**: What works for one person may not work for another
- **Valvular heart disease**: Theoretical concern from chronic 5-HT2B receptor activation (the mechanism linked to fenfluramine heart valve damage). LSD has some 5-HT2B affinity. This risk is theoretical and unconfirmed at microdose levels
- **Effects on developing brains**: No data for use by individuals under 25

## Practical Considerations

- Start with the lowest effective dose (Fadiman protocol: 1/10th standard dose)
- Maintain a daily journal to track effects objectively
