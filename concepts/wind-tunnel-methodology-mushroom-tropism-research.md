# Wind Tunnel Methodology for Mushroom Tropism Research

## Overview

The study of [[adaptive-significance-mushroom-tropisms-spore-dispersal]] — directional growth responses to environmental
stimuli — requires carefully controlled [[darwin-five-seedling-phototropism-experimental-conditions]]. Edmond R.
Badham's 1982 study of tropisms [[cap-and-stem-alkaloid-distribution-in-psilocybe-cubensis-strains]] (Mycologia, 74(2):
275-279) established a methodological standard using a closed-circuit wind
tunnel system controlling wind velocity, humidity, temperature, and light.

This apparatus remains a reference for fungal tropism research, providing a
template for investigating anemotropism (wind response), phototropism (light
response), and geotropism (gravity response) in basidiomycete fungi.
Approximately 100 developing basidiocarps were studied.

## Historical Context

Prior studies of mushroom tropisms were confined to photo- and geotropism
(Plunkett, 1961; Carlile, 1970; Leach, 1971; Schwalb and Shanler, 1974).
Wind as a tropic stimulus received little attention despite ecological
relevance to spore dispersal.

Several workers postulated a mushroom hormone involved in tropisms (Gruen,
1963; Hagimoto, 1963), and decapitation experiments linked these hormones to
the lamellae. Agar diffusates from gills could partially replace pileus
influence (Hagimoto and Konishi, 1960). No evidence suggested higher plant
hormones (auxins, gibberellins) regulated mushroom growth.

## Wind Tunnel Design

The tunnel featured a 43 x 30 x 20 cm working area in closed-circuit
configuration. Airflow was generated by a Dayton 4C564 blower (0.07 m³/sec),
passed through a polyester fiber filter, then through a Thermotrack II
temperature proportioner and spin-type humidifier with integrated heater.

An eight-sided air baffle ensured laminar flow, with soda straws as flow
straighteners. A heat cable provided perimeter [[bloomfield-mushroom-evaporative-cooling-and-thermal-regulation]]. Water
surrounding the working area served as both light filter and thermal
insulation, with a dedicated heater maintaining temperature stability.

Lighting was a "cool white" fluorescent fixture at 60 cm on a 12-hour
photoperiod at 0.002 cal/cm²/min (Eppley radiometer Model PSI) —
approximately one-tenth of field conditions. Humidity was monitored via wet/dry
copper constantan thermocouples connected to a two-pen strip-chart recorder
with automatic humidifier relay control.

## Controlled Environmental Parameters

**Wind velocity**: 9 ± 3 cm/sec (TSI hot wire anemometer Model 1650),
simulating gentle indoor air movement sufficient for measurable tropic
responses over 72 hours.

**Humidity**: 94 ± 3% RH, preventing desiccation while minimizing confounding
evaporative gradients unrelated to wind stimulus.

**Temperature**: 24.5 ± 1.5°C, within optimal P. cubensis growth range,
stabilized to prevent temperature-gradient-induced curvature.

**Light**: Cool white fluorescent at deliberately low intensity to minimize
phototropic interference while supporting normal development and sporulation.

## Mushroom Culture Preparation

An isolate from Badham (1980) was inoculated onto autoclaved brown rice (6 g
per 20 mL deionized water) in 50 mm Petri plates. After colonization, the
mycelial mass was transferred to 13 cm pie tins with vermiculite (25 mL
water), covered with glass, and maintained under 2685 lux cool white light
for approximately 10 days until primordia formed.

Cultures were watered with distilled water when weight loss exceeded 10 mL.
A sterile mutant was cultured in parallel for comparative experiments on the
spore production-geotropism relationship. Primordia of 15-25 mm height were
transferred to the wind tunnel.

## Developmental Staging System

**Stage 1** (0 h): Slight pileus swelling. Active [[evaporative-demand-gradient-stipe-elongation-mechanism-badham-1982]] with
strongest anemotropic response, growing into the wind.

**Stage 2** (~24 h): Nearly spherical pileus. Negative [[spore-formation-geotropic-response-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]
emerges in fertile basidiocarps — the critical transition between wind-
directed and gravity-directed growth.

**Stage 3** (~48 h): Maturity with open hemispherical pileus and active
spore production. Full reorientation for optimal spore dispersal.

**Stage 4** (~72 h): Late maturity with plane pileus. Growth essentially
ceased.
