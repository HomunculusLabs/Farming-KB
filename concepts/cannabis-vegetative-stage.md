## Overview

The vegetative stage is the growth phase where cannabis plants develop structure, foliage, and root mass before flowering. During this period, plants focus energy on producing leaves, stems, and branches rather than buds. A strong vegetative foundation directly correlates with final yield potential.

## Light Cycle

The standard vegetative light schedule is 18 hours on / 6 hours off (18/6). This extended photoperiod keeps plants in vegetative growth by preventing the hormonal shift triggered by longer dark periods.

- **18/6** — most common, allows good growth with rest period
- **20/4** — slightly faster growth, minimal added electricity cost
- **24/0** — continuous light, debatable benefits; some growers report diminished returns after week 3
- **Auto-flowering strains** — typically kept on 18/6 or 20/4 from seed through harvest since they flower based on age, not photoperiod

Light intensity during veg should target 300-600 PPFD. Too high can cause light stress; too low leads to stretching and weak stems.

## Training During Vegetative Stage

The vegetative stage is the window for structural training. Once flowering begins, plants become less flexible and training options narrow.

### Low-Stress Training (LST)

LST involves gently bending and tying down branches to create an even canopy. Benefits include:

- Exposes lower bud sites to more light
- Controls plant height in restricted spaces
- Promotes lateral branching
- Can be started as early as week 2-3 from seed

Use soft wire ties, pipe cleaners, or garden twine. Bend slowly over several days to avoid snapping stems.

### Topping

Topping means cutting the apical meristem (main growing tip) above a node. This forces the plant to split into two main colas instead of one.

- Best done when plant has 4-6 nodes (typically week 3-4)
- Wait until the plant is healthy and actively growing
- Use clean, sharp scissors or a razor blade
- Expect 2-3 days of slowed growth while the plant recovers

### FIM (Fuck I Missed)

A variation of topping where the cut is made through roughly 80% of the new growth rather than below the node. This often produces 4+ colas instead of 2.

- Less predictable than a clean top
- Can result in uneven growth if cut is inconsistent
- Slightly less recovery time than a full top

### Super Cropping

High-stress training technique that involves intentionally damaging the inner tissue of stems while leaving the outer skin intact. The stem is pinched and bent at a 90-degree angle, creating a knuckle of scar tissue.

- Increases overall hormone distribution
- Strengthens the stem at the bend point
- Best performed during mid-vegetative when stems are thick enough
- Supports should be used if the bend doesn't hold on its own

## Nutrient Requirements

Vegetative cannabis plants are heavy nitrogen feeders with moderate phosphorus and potassium needs.

### Macronutrient Ratio

A common vegetative NPK ratio is 3-1-2 or similar. Key points:

- **Nitrogen (N)** — drives leaf and stem growth; deficiency shows as pale lower leaves
- **Phosphorus (P)** — supports root development and energy transfer
- **Potassium (K)** — supports overall plant health and enzyme function

### Micronutrients

Calcium, magnesium, iron, and manganese are critical during veg. Calcium deficiency causes leaf tip curl; magnesium deficiency presents as interveinal chlorosis on lower leaves. Cal-Mag supplements are commonly added to feed water, especially in RO or distilled water setups.

### Feeding Schedule

- Week 1 from seed: plain water or very light nutrient solution (EC 0.4-0.6)
- Week 2-3: gradually increase to half-strength (EC 0.8-1.0)
- Week 4+: full vegetative strength (EC 1.2-1.8 depending on medium)
- Watch for nutrient burn — tip curling and clawing leaves indicate excess

Living soil beds with [[cannabis-nutrient-recipes]] may not require bottled nutrients during vegetative growth if the soil is properly amended pre-plant. See [[cannabis-nutrient-recipes]] for organic and synthetic feeding approaches.

## Environment

### Temperature

Ideal vegetative temperatures range from 72-82 F (22-28 C) during lights on, and 62-72 F (17-22 C) during lights off. A 10-15 F temperature differential between day and night supports healthy metabolic cycling.

### Humidity

Higher humidity during vegetative stage (60-70% RH) supports rapid transpiration-driven nutrient uptake and leaf expansion. Young clones and seedlings prefer 70-80% RH.

As plants grow larger, humidity can be gradually reduced. Transitioning toward 50-60% RH before the flowering flip helps prevent mold issues later.

### Air Circulation

Oscillating fans should run continuously to:

- Strengthen stems through wind stress
- Prevent microclimates where mold or pests can establish
- Even out temperature and CO2 distribution
- Promote transpiration and gas exchange at leaf surfaces

Fans should create gentle movement of all leaves without causing the plant to whip aggressively.

### Medium and Watering

In living soil beds, water when the top inch of soil feels dry. Overwatering during vegetative stage is common and leads to:

- Stunted growth and drooping leaves
- Root rot risk in poorly drained media
- Nutrient lockout from anaerobic root zones

Allow the root zone to partially dry between waterings. This encourages roots to search for moisture, building a denser root network.

## Vegetative Duration

Indoor growers typically veg for 3-8 weeks depending on:

- Available vertical space
- Desired final plant size (plants roughly double or triple in height during stretch)
- Training method (main-lining and manifolding require longer veg)
- Strain genetics (indicas stay shorter; sativas stretch significantly more)

Longer vegetative periods yield larger plants but increase electricity costs and time to harvest. In a [[cannabis-environment-control]] optimized space, 4-6 weeks of veg is a common balance.

## Transition to Flowering

When the grower is satisfied with plant size and structure, the light cycle is switched to 12/12 to initiate flowering. See [[cannabis-flowering-stage]] for details on the transition and flowering development.
