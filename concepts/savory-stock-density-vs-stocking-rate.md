# Stock Density Versus Stocking Rate

The distinction between stock density and stocking rate is one of the

## Stock Density Defined

Stock density reflects the concentration of animals on the unit of land
where they actually are at a given moment. It is expressed as animals
per acre or hectare at a specific point in time. Unlike stocking rate,
stock density can vary enormously from moment to moment depending on
paddock size and the number of paddocks in a grazing system.

For example, three hundred cows on four thousand acres represents a
stocking rate of one cow per thirteen acres. But if those same three
hundred cows are concentrated in a single forty-acre paddock, the stock
density at that moment is seven and a half cows per acre, a dramatic
difference that profoundly affects both the animals and the land.
