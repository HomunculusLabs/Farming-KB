---# Fungal Biogeography and Distribution Patterns

Fungal biogeography examines the spatial distribution of fungal species
across landscapes, continents, and ecosystems. Understanding these
patterns is essential for [[dighton-fungi-climate-change-feedback]], and interpreting how historical events such
as glaciations and plate tectonics have shaped [[cryptic-fungal-species]] with more
restricted ranges. At the same time, genuinely endemic fungi exist,
particularly in isolated habitats such as oceanic islands, mountain
ridges, and intermountain regions where host-species endemism is
high.

## Environmental Filtering and Habitat Preferences

fungal similarity is an outstanding indicator of environmental
similarity. Ecological similarity among habitats supersedes
geography in determining community composition, meaning that similar
habitats on different continents may harbor more similar fungal
communities than different habitats on the same continent.

## Dispersal Limitations

Dispersal is a critical factor shaping fungal biogeography. Most
fungi produce airborne spores that can travel considerable distances,
yet many species show surprisingly limited distributions. Narrow
habitat requirements, host specificity, and limited dispersal
ability may restrict species ranges. Species that depend on animals
for spore dispersal face additional constraints, as the loss of
dispersal agents through habitat change or fragmentation can
