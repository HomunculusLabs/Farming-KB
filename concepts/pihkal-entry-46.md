## Comparative Analysis Within the 2C-T Series

Within the broader 2C-T family, 2C-T-13 occupies an interesting position
with its 25-40 mg dosage range and 6-8 hour duration. For comparison,
2C-T-2 (ethylthio) is active at 12-25 mg with 6-8 hour duration, while
2C-T-7 (propylthio) requires 20-30 mg and lasts 8-12 hours. The
methoxyethylthio chain of 2C-T-13 appears to confer intermediate potency,
neither as potent as the shorter chain members nor as demanding as the
longer chain compounds. The ether oxygen in the side chain likely increases
the compound's polarity relative to purely hydrocarbon-substituted members,
which may influence both blood-brain barrier penetration and receptor
binding kinetics. The moderate duration is consistent with metabolic
cleavage at the ether linkage, potentially yielding a shorter-lived active
metabolite.

## Historical Context

2C-T-13 was synthesized during the period of systematic exploration of the
2C-T series in the Shulgin laboratory. This era of research, conducted
primarily in the 1980s and early 1990s, was characterized by methodical
variation of the alkyl chain on the 4-position sulfur atom. The decision
to incorporate an oxygen atom into the chain reflected a maturation of the
SAR understanding, moving beyond simple chain-length variation to explore
electronic and hydrogen-bonding effects. The compound represents one of
approximately 15 members of the 2C-T series evaluated in PiHKAL.
