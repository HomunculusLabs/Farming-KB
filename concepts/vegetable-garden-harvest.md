## Vegetable Garden Harvest and Storage

Harvest timing, [[spawn-storage-conditions]], and detailed crop-specific notes for home [[maintaining-organic-matter-in-vegetable-gardens]], based on Purdue Extension guidelines.

Related: [[vegetable-garden-guide]], [[food-preservation-canning]], [[seasonal-planning]]

## Storage Conditions Summary

### Cold, Moist (32-40F, 90-95% RH)
- **Beets**: Harvest at 1 inch diameter (greens too); main harvest at 2-3 inches. Store up to 4 months.
- **Carrots**: Harvest spring crop before July heat. Store up to 4 months.
- **Parsnips**: Harvest after several moderate freezes (cold develops sweetness).
- **Irish potatoes**: Harvest when tops yellowed/died. Cure 1 week in shaded, ventilated place. Do not expose to light (greening). Store up to 4 months.
- **Radishes**: Harvest at 1/2-1 inch diameter. Store up to 1 month.
- **Broccoli**: Harvest while florets tight and green-blue. Side shoots follow. Store up to 1 week.
- **Brussels sprouts**: Harvest from bottom up when firm. Best as fall crop. Store up to 3 weeks.
- **Cabbage**: Harvest when heads solid. Store up to 2 months.
- **Cauliflower**: Tie leaves above head when curds 1-2 inches. Store up to 2 weeks.
- **Chinese cabbage**: Best as fall crop. Store up to 2 months.
- **Kohlrabi**: Harvest at 2-3 inches diameter. Store up to 4 weeks.

### Cold, Moist (45-50F, 80-90% RH)
- **Cucumbers**: Harvest before seeds are half-size (1.5-2.5 inches diameter). Store up to 1 week.
- **Eggplant**: Harvest when nearly full grown but color still bright. Store about 1 week.
- **Greens** (chard, collards, kale, mustard, spinach): Harvest leaves at suitable size. Store up to 2 weeks.
- **Lettuce**: Store up to 2 weeks.
- **Green beans**: Most tender when seed is 1/4 mature. Store up to 1 week.
- **Okra**: Harvest at 2-3 inches (over-mature = woody). Store about 1 week.
- **Sweet corn**: Harvest when kernels plump, silks dry, milky juice when pressed. Process immediately.
- **Sweet peppers**: Harvest when firm and full size. Store 2-3 weeks.
- **Tomatoes**: Ripe keep 1 week at 45-50F. Mature green tomatoes wrapped in newspaper keep 3-5 weeks at 55-58F.

### Cool, Dry (45-55F, 50-60% RH)
- **Dry onions**: Harvest when 2/3-3/4 of tops fallen. Cure 3-4 weeks. Store in mesh bags.

### Warm, Dry (55-60F, 60-70% RH)
- **Pumpkins and winter squash**: Harvest when skin is hard and colors darken, before frost. Store in single layers on shelves.

### Warm, Moist (55-60F, 80-85% RH)
- **[[query-how-do-i-grow-sweet-potatoes-from-slips-to-harvest]]**: Harvest before frost. Cure 1 week at 80-85F. Store at 55F, 85% RH.

### Perennial Vegetables
- **Asparagus**: Snap/cut 6-8 inch spears at ground level. Store up to 1 week.
- **Rhubarb**: Harvest stalks at 1/2-1 inch diameter. DO NOT USE LEAFY BLADES (contain oxalates). Store up to 3 weeks.

## Detailed Crop-Specific Harvest Notes

### Root Vegetables
- **Beets**: Harvest greens at any size; main harvest at 1 inch diameter (baby) to 2-3 inches (full). Store in perforated bags, refrigerator or cold moist cellar. Up to 4 months.
- **Carrots**: Spring crop must be harvested before July heat. Fall-planted carrots: harvest before first moderate freeze. For storage, wash roots, trim tops to 1/2 inch, perforated plastic bags. Up to 4 months.
- **Parsnips**: Harvest after several moderate freezes — cold exposure develops sweetness. Same storage as carrots.
- **Horseradish**: Harvest after several severe freezes. Can store in ground all winter, mulched with straw/leaves; dig as needed. Also stores like carrots.
- **Salsify**: Same as parsnips.
- **Turnips**: Harvest from 1 inch diameter. Best as fall crop; withstands several light freezes. Store as carrots. Up to 4 months.
- **Radishes**: Harvest at 1/2-1 inch. Winter/black radishes store as carrots. Standard radishes up to 1 month in plastic bags.

### Cole Crops
- **Broccoli**: Harvest terminal head while florets tight and green-blue. Smaller heads develop from side shoots — enables extended harvest. Up to 1 week; freeze surplus.
- **Brussels Sprouts**: Harvest sprouts from bottom up when firm. Can stand several moderate freezes. Harvest all before first severe freeze. Up to 3 weeks; freeze surplus. Best as fall crop.
- **Cauliflower**: Tie outer leaves above head when curds 1-2 inches (except purple types). Heads ready ~2 weeks after tying. Up to 2 weeks; freeze surplus.
- **Chinese Cabbage**: Best as fall crop. Harvest after first moderate frost. Up to 2 months.
- **Kohlrabi**: Harvest at 2-3 inches diameter — becomes woody if left too long or grown poorly. Up to 4 weeks.

### Greens
- **Swiss Chard**: Harvest continuously by breaking off outer leaves. Spring planting provides greens from early summer to first moderate freeze.
- **Collards, Kale, Mustard, Spinach**: Harvest whole plant or outer larger leaves. Up to 2 weeks in refrigerator; freeze surplus.
- **Endive/Escarole**: Harvest whole plant, wash thoroughly, tie leaves with rubber band. Up to 3 weeks.
- **Lettuce**: Head, semi-head, and leaf types all store up to 2 weeks in perforated bags.
- **Parsley**: Snip stems close to ground; never cut more than half the plant at once. Overwinters in protected places (cold frames). Can be lifted with soil ball before freeze, potted indoors. Up to 1+ weeks in refrigerator.

### Legumes
- **Lima Beans**: Harvest when pods filled. A bit immature for tender limas; dry and mature for "meaty" limas. Shelled: up to 1 week refrigerated.
- **Garden Peas**: Same maturity principle as limas. Unshelled: up to 1 week. Snow peas: harvest when pods fully elongated but flat-sided. Snap peas: when peas reach full size and pod is cylindrical.
- **Southern Peas** (black-eyed, crowder, etc.): For fresh use, harvest when seeds large and plump but moist. For dry storage, harvest after pods and seeds have dried.
- **Green Beans**: Most tender when seed is 1/4 mature. Increasingly fibrous with maturity. Up to 1 week. Cool cellar storage also possible.
- **Okra**: Harvest at 2-3 inches. Over-mature = woody. Up to 1 week; freeze surplus.

### Vine Crops
- **Muskmelon**: Harvest when stem slips easily from fruit — lift melon; if ripe, separates easily. Up to 10 days.
- **Summer Squash**: Harvest small (6-8 inches long, 3-4 inches across round types), young, tender. Skin should be penetrable with thumbnail. Up to 1 week.
- **Watermelon**: Harvest when underside turns whitish to yellowish; tendril at fruit stem junction dies. Immature = ringing metallic thump; mature = dull thud. Up to 1 week room temperature, 2-3 weeks at 45-50F.

### Fruiting Vegetables
- **Tomato**: Ripe keep 1 week at 45-50F. Mature green (full size, whitish-green skin): wrap individually in newspaper, store 55-58F cellar, inspect weekly. Keep 3-5 weeks. For faster ripening, raise to 65-70F.
- **Sweet Pepper**: Harvest when firm and full size. Leave on plant until red if desired. Up to 2-3 weeks in warmer part of refrigerator or cool cellar.
- **Hot Pepper**: Pull plants late season or string together and hang to dry. Store dry, cool place (usually basement).
- **Eggplant**: Harvest when nearly full grown but color still bright. Not adapted to long storage. About 1 week.
- **Sweet Corn**: Harvest when kernels plump and tender, silks dry. Test by pressing a few kernels — milky juice = ready. Process immediately after harvest.
- **Cucumber**: Harvest before seeds half-size (1.5-2.5 inches diameter, 5-8 inches long for slicers; blockier for pickling). Slicing: up to 1 week. Pickling: cool in ice water, up to 2 days.

### Other
- **Irish Potato**: Harvest when tops yellowed/died. Do not leave in ground (high soil temps accelerate over-ripening). Cure 1 week in shaded, ventilated place. Remove excess soil; discard diseased/damaged. Avoid light exposure (causes greening/solanine). Store cool (above 40F), high humidity, good ventilation. Up to 4 months.
- **Dry Onions**: Harvest when 2/3-3/4 tops fallen and necks shriveled. Remove tops, cure 3-4 weeks in open garage/barn. Store in mesh bags, as cool as possible. Ventilate during humid weather.
- **Sweet Potatoes**: Harvest before frost. Handle carefully. Cure 1 week at 80-85F. Ideal storage: 55F, 85% RH (basement with ventilated boxes covered with moistened burlap).
- **Pumpkins/Winter Squash**: Harvest when skin hard and colors darken, before frost. Remove with portion of stem attached. Store in single layers on shelves for air circulation.
- **Green Onions**: Harvest when sufficient size. Wash, cut roots, leave 1+ inch green. Up to 2 weeks.
