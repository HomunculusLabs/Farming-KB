## Design Requirements

### Temperature: 32-40F (0-4C)
Most [[hamilton-root-vegetables-growing]] need 90-95%; [[hamilton-greenhouse-heating-and-temperature-control]] and ethylene gas removal (ripening produce
emits ethylene, accelerating nearby spoilage).
- Two vents: one low (near floor, intake), one high (near ceiling, exhaust).
- Intake on north side. Adjustable covers to regulate airflow.
- Hardware cloth screens on both vents to exclude rodents/insects.

### Drainage
Water infiltration causes rot and mold. Build above local water table. Slope
floor toward drain or sump. Grade surrounding ground away from entrance.
Install gravel drainage bed around exterior walls.

## Root Cellar Types

### Basement Cellar
Converts existing basement space. Choose a corner with two exterior walls.
Insulate ceiling and interior walls; close heating ducts. Install
ventilation through exterior wall. May be warmer than standalone cellars.

### Outdoor Standalone
Freestanding structure in a hillside or excavated ground.
- North-facing hillside ideal (cooler, no afternoon sun).
- Earth bermed on three sides, entrance on south.
- Roof: 2-3 ft soil cover with grass/groundcover.
- Foundation: poured concrete or blocks.
- Insulated, weather-stripped door with airlock vestibule.
- Standard size: 8x8 to 8x10 ft for a family of four.

### Earth Bag
Low-cost DIY: fill polypropylene bags with damp subsoil, lay in courses,
barbed wire between for grip. Plaster with earthen plaster. Dome shape is
strongest. Excellent [[comparison-bamboo-construction-vs-timber-frame-basics]] with straw bale walls, plastered with earthen or [[comparison-lime-plaster-vs-earthen-plaster]].
R-30+ insulation. Excellent humidity regulation (breathable walls). Must keep
bales dry. Best for earth-bermed designs where bales don't contact ground.

## What Stores Best

### High Humidity (90-95%, 32-40F)
- **Potatoes:** 4-6 months. Dark storage. Keep away from apples (sprouting).
- **Carrots:** 4-6 months. Damp sand or sawdust. Remove tops.
- **Beets:** 3-5 months. Damp sand. Remove tops, leave 1 in stem.
- **Turnips/Rutabagas:** 2-4 months. Damp sand.
- **Parsnips:** 2-6 months. Best after frost (starch to sugar conversion).
- **Celeriac:** 3-4 months. Damp sand or individual wrapping.

### Moderate Humidity (80-90%, 32-40F)
- **Cabbage:** 2-4 months. Wrap individually in newspaper. Don't wash.
- **Brussels sprouts:** 3-5 weeks. Best on the stalk.
- **Leeks:** 1-3 months. Heel into damp sand or soil.

### Warm/Dry Zone (50-55F, 60-70% humidity)
- **Winter squash:** 2-6 months. Cure at 80F for 10 days first.
- **Sweet potatoes:** 4-6 months. Cure at 85F/85% humidity for 10-14 days.
- **Onions:** 6-8 months. Cure thoroughly (papery skins). Mesh bags.
- **Garlic:** 6-8 months. Cure thoroughly. Braid or mesh bags.

### Special Notes
- **Apples:** Store at 32-35F, 90-95%. Always separate — ethylene causes
  rapid spoilage in other produce. Keep away from potatoes.
- **Store only sound, unblemished produce.** One rotting item spreads
