# [[pleurotus-ostreatus]] Medicinal and Anticancer Properties

Pleurotus ostreatus, commonly known as the Oyster Mushroom, is one of
the most widely cultivated edible mushrooms worldwide. Its fruit-body is
oyster-shaped, growing in layered clusters on deciduous trees in many
parts of the world. In the Sung dynasty (A.D. 420-479) it was referred
to as "the mushroom of flower heaven" (Stamets, 1993; Hobbs, 1995).

## Historical and Geographic Discovery

The medicinally beneficial effects of Pleurotus spp. were discovered
independently on different continents. The awareness of their medicinal
properties comes not only from Asia but from the folklore of central
Europe, South America and Africa (Gunde-Cimerman, 1999). While first
artificially cultivated in the USA, production is now worldwide with
Pleurotus accounting for 14.2% of total world mushroom production.

## Key Active Constituents

- Beta-glucans: antitumour and immunomodulation properties
- Lovastatin (mevinolin): natural cholesterol-lowering ability
- Acidic polysaccharide fractions: antitumour activity from fruiting
  body
- Beta-glucan and heteroglucan complexes from submerged cultured
  mycelium

## Anticancer Activity in Experimental Models

Several studies have demonstrated a role for P. ostreatus in cancer
prevention and treatment. Kurashiga et al. (1997) investigated the
effects of Lentinus edodes, [[grifola-frondosa]] and P. ostreatus
administration on cancer outbreaks and activities of macrophages and
lymphocytes in mice treated with the carcinogen N-butyl-N'-butanolitroso-
amine. The study demonstrated significant immunomodulatory effects and
reduced tumour incidence across all three mushroom species.

P. ostreatus has been shown to contain antitumour acidic polysaccharide
fractions from its fruiting body, as well as beta-glucan and
heteroglucan from its submerged cultured mycelium. The antitumour
polysaccharides from the fruit-body have demonstrated activity in
xenograft models, confirming their biological activity.

## Cancer Chemoprevention

Professor Ikekawa (2001) confirmed previous studies with Lentinus edodes
and P. ostreatus showing that regular feeding of powdered medicinal
mushrooms can have a cancer prevention effect, demonstrating both high
antitumour activity and restriction of tumour metastasis in experimental
animals. In Ikekawa's study, control mice were bred on ordinary feed
while treated mice received feed containing 5% dried fruit-bodies of
Hypsizygus marmoneus, confirming the broader principle that regular
mushroom consumption has chemopreventive effects.

## Dietary Fibre and Colorectal Cancer Prevention

The dietary fibre content of P. ostreatus (as much as 10-50% in dry
matter) is composed of beta-glucans, chitin and heteropolysaccharides
(pectinous substances, hemicellulose, polyuronides). Many of the active
polysaccharides, water soluble or insoluble, can be classified as
dietary fibres and their protein complexes. These compounds can absorb
possible carcinogenic substances and hasten their excretion from the
intestine, suggesting a preventative action for colorectal carcinoma
(Mizuno, 1996).

## Cholesterol-Lowering Properties

The major cause of death in the Western hemisphere is coronary artery
disease with hypercholesterolaemia as a primary risk factor. Drug therapy
for lowering cholesterol has made considerable use of lovastatin
(mevinolin) and its analogues. Species of the genus Pleurotus are
excellent producers of lovastatin and as such, Pleurotus could be
considered as a functional food with natural cholesterol-lowering
ability (Gunde-Cimerman, 1999).

The addition of 4% dried Pleurotus to a high cholesterol diet
effectively lowered cholesterol levels. Pleurotus supplement in the
daily diet over a one-month period reduced serum cholesterol, suggesting
it could be recommended as a natural cholesterol-lowering agent.
However, large scale production of lovastatin from fruit-bodies is not
deemed commercially viable because of variability in fruit-body
composition. Lovastatin is normally found only in the lamella and
basidiospores and not in the stipe and cap.

## Cultivation and Production

P. ostreatus is one of the easiest mushrooms to grow, most often on
straw or sawdust logs. Worldwide production reached approximately
875,600 tonnes. Mycelial cultivation could be the way ahead for
consistent lovastatin and polysaccharide production. Lovastatin has also
been detected in submerged fermentation broth of P. saca and in surface
fermentation broth of P. sapidus (Gunde-Cimerman et al., 1993).
