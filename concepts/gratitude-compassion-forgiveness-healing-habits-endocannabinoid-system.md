## The Emotional Habit Framework

Blesching's framework distinguishes between expansive and constricting
emotions. Expansive emotions (associated with anandamide, serotonin, GABA,
oxytocin, and [[blesching-cannabis-emotional-processing-expansive-constricting-emotions]]
(associated with cortisol, epinephrine, glutamate, and vasopressin)
promote sympathetic dominance, chronic inflammation, and immune
suppression.

The three healing habits are designed to shift the emotional baseline from
constricting toward expansive patterns, with measurable physiological
consequences:

**Gratitude** shifts attention from what is lacking to what is present,
activating reward circuits (dopamine, endogenous opioids) and reducing
stress hormone production (cortisol, epinephrine).

**Compassion** activates empathy circuits (oxytocin, vasopressin in their
prosocial rather than aggressive configurations) and reduces the
inflammatory cascade associated with social isolation and hostility.

**Forgiveness** releases the physiological grip of resentment and anger,
reducing chronic sympathetic activation and allowing the parasympathetic
nervous system to restore equilibrium.

## Neurobiological Basis of Gratitude

Gratitude practices activate the ventromedial prefrontal cortex, anterior
cingulate cortex, and reward circuitry including the nucleus accumbens and
ventral tegmental area. Neuroimaging shows gratitude activates the same
regions stimulated by social reward and prosocial behavior.

Physiologically, gratitude practice has been associated with reduced
cortisol output, increased heart rate variability (HRV), enhanced sleep
quality, increased natural killer cell activity, reduced inflammatory
markers (IL-6, TNF-alpha), and increased serotonin synthesis in the dorsal
raphe nucleus.

The endocannabinoid system mediates several of these effects. Anandamide
(AEA) levels increase with positive social emotions, and CB1 activation in
the prefrontal cortex facilitates the cognitive reappraisal processes
central to gratitude practice.

## Neurobiological Basis of Compassion

The interplay between compassion and the endocannabinoid system is
relevant because both modulate social behavior through overlapping neural
circuits. CB1 receptors in the amygdala modulate fear responses, and
compassion practice may enhance the endocannabinoid system's capacity to
downregulate amygdala hyperactivity.

## Neurobiological Basis of Forgiveness

Forgiveness involves deliberate release of resentment and cognitive
reappraisal of transgressive experiences. Neuroimaging shows activation of
the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (executive control), anterior cingulate
cortex (conflict monitoring), and ventral striatum (reward processing).

Physiological consequences include reduced sympathetic activation and lower
resting blood pressure, decreased cortisol and epinephrine, improved
cardiovascular health, enhanced immune function, improved sleep quality,
and decreased perceived pain intensity in chronic conditions.
