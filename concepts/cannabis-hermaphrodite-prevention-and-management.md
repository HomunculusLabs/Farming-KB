Hermaphroditism in cannabis — the development of both male and female sex organs on the same plant — is one of the most dangerous threats to a sinsemilla harvest. Even a single hermaphrodite can release enough pollen to seed an entire crop.

## What Causes Hermaphroditism

### Genetic Predisposition
Some strains carry hermaphroditic traits in their genetics. These strains may "throw bananas" (produce male pollen sacs) even under ideal conditions. Stable, well-bred strains from reputable breeders are less prone to this problem. See [[cannabis-seed-selection-and-strain-types]] and [[cannabis-breeding-basics]].

### Environmental Stress
Stress is the most common trigger for hermaphroditism in otherwise healthy plants:

- **Light leaks during dark period**: The number one cause. Even a small LED indicator light or light seeping under a door during the 12-hour dark cycle can trigger male flower development.
- **Temperature extremes**: Temperatures too high or too low cause stress responses. Maintain 70–85°F (21–29°C) during lights-on and 60–75°F (15–24°C) during lights-off.
- **Nutrient stress**: Severe deficiencies, toxicities, or pH imbalances can trigger hermaphroditism. See [[cannabis-nutrient-deficiency-and-toxicity-guide]].
- **Physical damage**: Accidental breakage, topping trauma, or pest damage can stress the plant enough to trigger hermaphroditic response.
- **Irregular light schedules**: Missing a dark period, accidental light interruption, or inconsistent timing can confuse the plant's hormone system.
- **Root stress**: Being root-bound, overwatering, or underwatering can all contribute.
- **Re-vegging stress**: A plant that is switched back to vegetative lighting after flowering has started may develop hermaphroditic traits when re-flowered.

## Identification

Hermaphrodite male organs appear as:

- **"Bananas"**: Yellowish, banana-shaped protrusions (stamens) emerging from female buds. These are the most common and dangerous form — they can produce viable pollen quickly.
- **Pollen sacs**: Round male sacs appearing at nodes alongside female pistils, similar to early male plant sex organs.
- **Mixed nodes**: Nodes showing both pistils and round sacs.

## Prevention Strategies

### Environmental Discipline
- **Eliminate ALL light leaks**: Seal your grow room completely. Sit in the dark room for 5–10 minutes to let your eyes adjust and check for any light intrusion. See [[cannabis-photoperiodism-and-light-cycles]].
- **Use timers**: Never rely on manually switching lights. Use a quality timer.
- **Maintain stable temperatures**: Use fans, ventilation, and if necessary, heaters to maintain consistent temperatures. See [[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]].

### Plant Care
- **Maintain proper pH**: Keep soil pH between 6.0–7.0 and hydroponic pH between 5.5–6.5. See [[cannabis-ph-management-and-soil-flushing]].
- **Feed consistently**: Avoid large swings in nutrient strength. Follow a regular feeding schedule.
- **Avoid root stress**: Transplant before plants become root-bound. See [[cannabis-transplanting-guide]].
- **Handle plants gently**: Minimize physical damage during training and pruning. See [[training-cannabis]].

### Genetics
- **Choose stable strains**: Research strain stability before purchasing. See [[cannabis-seed-banks-and-genetics]].
- **Use clones from proven mothers**: If a clone performs well without herming, it's a reliable indicator of genetic stability. See [[cannabis-cloning-and-mother-plants]].
- **Avoid unknown genetics**: Bagseed and untested genetics carry higher hermaphrodite risk.

## Management

### Immediate Action
If you discover hermaphroditic organs on your plant:

1. **Remove the plant entirely** if bananas or sacs are open or numerous. This is the safest approach.
2. **If very early detection**: Some growers carefully remove male organs with tweezers, but this is risky. The plant will likely produce more.
3. **Monitor closely**: If you choose to keep the plant, check it multiple times daily with a magnifying glass. Any open pollen sac means the plant must go.
4. **Isolate**: If possible, move the plant to a separate space away from your other females.

### Emergency Pollination Exposure
If you suspect your females have been exposed to pollen:
- Watch for swelling calyxes and slowed resin production in the following weeks
- Pollinated flowers will produce seeds instead of maximizing bud and resin development
- The resulting seeds can be collected and used — they will be crosses of whatever male pollen reached your females
- Pollinated bud is still smokable but less potent and less pleasant

## Re-Vegging and Hermaphrodite Risk

Re-vegging (returning a flowering plant to vegetative light cycle) is possible but increases hermaphrodite risk. See [[cannabis-sinsemilla-life-cycle]]. If re-vegging, monitor the plant especially carefully during the second flowering cycle.
