# Hongo's Nine Distribution Types Applied to Neurotropic Fungi: Detailed Species Examples

## Overview

Akira Hongo (1985, 1989) proposed a classification system of nine distribution types for fungal biogeography. Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (1998) applied this framework to neurotropic fungi worldwide, providing concrete species assignments to each type. This page presents the detailed application of Hongo's system with specific species examples drawn from the Guzmán et al. study.

## The Nine Distribution Types

### Type 1: Pantropical

Species distributed throughout tropical regions of both hemispheres. These are the most widely dispersed neurotropic fungi, typically associated with warm climates and often with cattle dung as a substrate.

- ***Psilocybe subcubensis* Guzmán** — A close relative of *P. cubensis* with a pantropical distribution, though less commonly encountered. Distinguished by smaller spores and slightly different microscopic features.
- ***Panaeolus tropicalis* Ola'h** — A tropical species found across multiple continents, associated with herbivore dung in warm, humid environments.

### Type 2: Paleotropical

Species restricted to the tropical regions of the Eastern Hemisphere (Africa, Asia, Oceania).

**Species examples:**

- ***Psilocybe mairei* Singer** — Documented from North Africa and the Mediterranean region, representing a paleotropical distribution with temperate extensions. This species illustrates the difficulty of cleanly separating paleotropical from Mediterranean distributions.
- ***Psilocybe natalensis* Gartz, Reid, Smith & Eicker** — Described from South Africa, this species is found in sub-Saharan Africa and represents the African component of the paleotropical neurotropic mycobiota.
- ***Panaeolus africanus* Ola'h** — As the name suggests, this species is primarily African in distribution, found in dung of herbivores across the continent's tropical and subtropical regions.
- ***Psilocybe moelleri** — A species found primarily in African tropical regions, growing on dung and enriched soils.

### Type 3: Neotropical

Species restricted to the tropical regions of the Western Hemisphere (Central and South America, Caribbean).

**Species examples:**

- ***Psilocybe zapotecorum* Heim** — One of the most important neotropical species, used traditionally by Zapotec and Mazatec peoples of Oaxaca, Mexico. Found throughout Central and South America in subtropical and tropical forests, typically growing on muddy soils near streams or in landslide areas.
- ***Psilocybe hoogshagenii* Guzmán, Escalona & Guzmán-Dávalos** — Named after the Dutch mycologist Rolf Singer's student, this species is found in Mexico and Central America, fruiting in cloud forests and subtropical oak-pine forests.
- ***Psilocybe muliercula* Singer & Smith** — A neotropical species documented from Mexico and Central America, typically found in forest litter and soils.
- ***Psilocybe wassoniorum** — Found in the neotropical forests of Mexico and Central America, part of the [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].
