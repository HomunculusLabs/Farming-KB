## Morishima Acacia

## Overview

The Morishima acacia is a fast-growing nitrogen-fixing tree that Masanobu Fukuoka integrated into his citrus orchard as a multi-purpose soil-building and ecosystem-support tree. Originally introduced to Japan from Australia, this acacia species plays a central role in Fukuoka's three-dimensional orchard design, providing deep soil improvement, wind protection, insect predator habitat, [[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]] system, where the goal was to build soil fertility without external inputs.

### Growth Rate and Size

The Morishima acacia grows faster than any tree Fukuoka had ever encountered. Its growth trajectory is remarkable:

- **Root development**: Sends down a deep root in just a few months
- **Vertical growth**: Stands as tall as a telephone pole in six to seven years
- **Wood quality**: Hard, dense wood suitable for multiple uses

This extraordinary growth rate means the tree quickly begins performing its soil-building functions, making it valuable for farmers who need rapid results in degraded or compacted soils.

## Nitrogen Fixation

Like other leguminous plants, the Morishima acacia forms a symbiotic relationship with rhizobium bacteria living within its root system. ### Eliminating External Inputs

With the green manure fertilizing the topsoil and the Morishima acacia roots improving the soil deep down, Fukuoka demonstrated that no external fertilizer was needed. There was also no need to cultivate between the orchard trees. The combination of tall trees for windbreaks, citrus in the middle, and green manure cover below created a self-maintaining system.
