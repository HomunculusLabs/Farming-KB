# Cannabis for Cancer Symptom Management

The Cannabis Health Index reviewed 50 studies relevant to cannabinoids and the [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] in the context of 15 types of cancer, yielding a high overall CHI Value of 100. This evidence base suggests cannabinoids may prevent, inhibit, and destroy cancer cells in pre-clinical models, as well as exhibit strong therapeutic influences to mitigate treatment side effects and improve quality of life.

## Important Disclaimer

The research described here primarily involves pre-clinical (laboratory and animal) studies. While results are promising, cannabis should be viewed as a complementary approach to cancer symptom management, not a replacement for conventional oncology treatment. Always consult with your oncology team.

## Symptom Management

### Nausea and Vomiting
Cannabinoids have well-established anti-emetic properties, particularly for chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV). Both THC and CBD contribute to anti-nausea effects through CB1 receptor activation in the brainstem's vomiting center. Dronabinol (synthetic THC) and Nabilone are FDA-approved for CINV. Cannabis is effective for both acute and anticipatory nausea, and is particularly valuable for patients who do not respond to conventional antiemetics. CBD also has antiemetic properties through 5-HT1A (serotonin) receptor activation, providing a second mechanism of action.

### Pain
Cannabis provides analgesic effects for cancer-related pain, particularly neuropathic pain from nerve damage, inflammatory pain from tumor-associated inflammation, and visceral pain from organ involvement. Cannabinoids used alongside reduced opioid doses produce effective pain control while reducing opiate-related adverse effects and habit-forming potential. Research cited in the Cannabis Health Index shows that both THC and the synthetic cannabinoid CP 55,940 reduce pain by inducing the release of endogenous opioids dynorphin A and dynorphin B. Pre-clinical studies indicate that THC and morphine can be useful in low-dose combination as an analgesic. See [[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]] for detailed pain management approaches.

### Appetite and Cachexia
THC is an effective appetite stimulant through CB1 receptors in the hypothalamus. This is particularly valuable for cancer patients experiencing cachexia (wasting syndrome), appetite loss, and weight loss. Enhanced sensory perception of food (taste and smell) also contributes to appetite stimulation. The body's own endogenous cannabinoid, anandamide, also plays a role in appetite regulation -- CBD inhibits FAAH (the enzyme that breaks down anandamide), potentially supporting appetite through this indirect pathway.

### Night Sweats
Nabilone has been successfully used for paraneoplastic night sweats. Maida (2008) reported four cases where nabilone effectively reduced cancer-induced night sweats, improving patient comfort and sleep quality.

### Anxiety and Depression
Cancer patients frequently experience anxiety and depression. Cannabinoids' mood-modulating properties may help address these psychological symptoms, improving overall quality of life. CBD has demonstrated anxiolytic effects through multiple receptor systems: CB1 activation, 5-HT1A (serotonin) receptor agonism, and GABA modulation. See [[cannabis-for-mental-health]] for broader mental health applications.

## Pre-Clinical Anti-Cancer Research

As early as 1974, the U.S. government knew of cannabis's effectiveness against certain cancer types. Research has since expanded significantly across multiple cancer types. The Cannabis Health Index reviews evidence for cannabinoids across 15 cancer types.

### Mechanisms of Action
- **CB1 and CB2 receptor-mediated effects**: Therapeutic impact varies by cancer type -- sometimes via CB1, sometimes via CB2, and often through synergistic activation of both
- **Apoptosis induction**: Cannabinoids trigger programmed cell death in cancer cells through multiple pathways including caspase activation, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction
- **Anti-angiogenic effects**: Cannabinoids inhibit the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) pathway, starving tumors of blood supply
- **Anti-metastatic effects**: Cannabinoids increase expression of tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases-1 (TIMP-1), reducing cancer cell invasion and migration
- **Anti-proliferative effects**: Cannabinoids inhibit cell cycle progression (e.g., through Cdc2 regulation in breast cancer, E2F1 downregulation in glioblastoma)
- **Metastasis inhibition**: Anandamide has been shown to inhibit the movement of cancer cells and may prevent metastasis without negative effects on the patient's immune system

### Cancer Types Studied

- **Brain cancer (glioma/glioblastoma)**: CBD triggers caspase activation and oxidative stress; THC inhibits VEGF; a pilot clinical study by Guzman et al. (2006) administered THC to patients with recurrent glioblastoma. The U.S. government patent on cannabinoids as neuroprotectants notes their application in limiting neurological damage following ischemic insults.
- **Breast cancer**: CBD inhibits Id-1 gene expression in aggressive breast cancer; anandamide inhibits cell proliferation via prolactin and Trk receptor suppression
- **Prostate cancer**: Cannabinoid receptors identified as novel targets; endocannabinoids suppress Trk and prolactin receptors
- **Lung cancer**: THC inhibits EGF-induced lung cancer cell migration and metastasis in vivo
- **Colon cancer**: Anandamide induces cell death in colorectal carcinoma cells; loss of CB1 accelerates intestinal tumor growth
- **Leukemia**: CBD-induced apoptosis via regulation of p22phox and Nox4; THC-induced apoptosis through Bad translocation to mitochondria. Leukemia and lymphoma are closely related -- both involve white blood cells (natural killer cells, T-cells, B-cells) and lymph nodes, which are important parts of the body's immune system.
- **Lymphoma**: Potentiation of cannabinoid cytotoxicity through ceramide metabolism modulation; CB1/CB2 expression confirmed in non-Hodgkin lymphoma
- **Liver cancer**: Anti-tumoral action via AMPK-dependent autophagy activation (Vara et al., 2011)
- **Pancreatic cancer**: Cannabinoids induce apoptosis via endoplasmic reticulum stress-related genes
- **Melanoma**: CB1 modulation induces apoptosis of human melanoma cells
- **Skin cancer (non-melanoma)**: Cannabinoid receptor activation inhibits skin tumor growth and angiogenesis in vivo (Casanova et al., 2003)
- **Bone cancer**: CB2 receptor agonists attenuate bone cancer-induced pain and bone loss. CBD and other primarily CB2-activating non-psychoactive cannabinoids have the capacity to make fractured bones stronger while they heal.
- **Cervical cancer**: Anandamide induces apoptosis via vanilloid receptor-1
- **Kaposi's sarcoma**: CB1/CB2 agonist WIN-55,212-2 reduces viability of KS cells
- **Thyroid cancer**: Metabolically stable anandamide analog inhibits thyroid carcinoma via apoptosis

## Chemotherapy Side Effect Management

Beyond the FDA-approved anti-emetic applications, cannabis shows promise for managing chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), one of the most debilitating and treatment-limiting side effects of many chemotherapeutic agents. The immunomodulatory effects of cannabinoids may also help support immune function during immunosuppressive treatments. Cannabis has been used historically in the treatment of cancer, and the anti-inflammatory and immunomodulating properties of the plant have prompted numerous experiments relevant to supporting patients through conventional cancer treatments.

## Palliative Care Applications

In palliative settings, cannabis addresses multiple symptoms simultaneously: pain, nausea, appetite loss, insomnia, anxiety, and depression. This multi-symptom approach makes it particularly valuable in end-of-life care, where polypharmacy is a significant concern. The cannabinergic system's involvement in calming inflammation and initiating pain control provides a biological basis for cannabis's broad palliative utility.

## Strain and Form Considerations

- **Sativa strains** (higher THC:CBD ratio): May be preferred for daytime appetite stimulation and mood elevation
- **Indica strains** (lower THC:CBD ratio): Better for pain, sleep, and relaxation
- **Raw cannabis juice**: Provides non-psychoactive CBD-acid and THC-acid for patients who cannot tolerate psychoactive effects; particularly relevant for patients with compromised immune systems
- **THC:CBD combinations**: The synergistic effects of both receptors activated together may be particularly relevant for cancer applications. Sativex consists of roughly equal parts THC (2.7 mg) and CBD (2.5 mg), though formulation ratios are very similar to actual Cannabis sativa ratios.

## Safety with Conventional Treatment

Patients undergoing chemotherapy or radiation should discuss cannabis use with their oncologist. Cannabis may interact with certain chemotherapy agents and can affect the metabolism of some pharmaceuticals through CYP450 enzyme interactions. The body's own endogenous cannabinoids signal cannabinoid receptors to regulate a wide variety of physiological processes -- understanding these interactions with conventional treatments is important for safe integration.
