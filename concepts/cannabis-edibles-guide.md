## Overview

Cannabis edibles are food and beverage products infused with cannabinoids. They offer a smoke-free consumption method with longer-lasting effects compared to inhalation. The key challenges with edibles are proper [[decarboxylation]], accurate dosing, and understanding the significantly different onset time and duration compared to smoking.

## Decarboxylation

Raw cannabis contains non-intoxicating acidic cannabinoids (THCA, CBDA). Decarboxylation applies heat to convert these to their active forms (THC, CBD). This step is essential for any edible or tincture preparation.

### Oven Method

1. Preheat oven to 240 F (115 C)
2. Grind cannabis to a coarse consistency (not powder — too fine burns easily)
3. Spread in an even layer on a parchment-lined baking sheet
4. Bake for 30-40 minutes
5. Gently mix or shake the tray halfway through
6. The cannabis should be lightly toasted with a golden-brown color [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-a-monotub-and-a-shotgun-fruiting-chamber]] nutty aroma
7. Allow to cool before infusing

### Temperature Guide

- Below 220 F (105 C): Incomplete decarboxylation, wasted material
- 220-245 F (105-118 C): Optimal range — full decarboxylation with minimal cannabinoid degradation
- Above 250 F (121 C): Degradation of THC to CBN begins; terpene loss increases

### Decarboxylation Efficiency

Approximately 85-90% of THCA converts to THC under optimal conditions. Overheating or extended baking reduces this percentage. For maximum potency, use a controlled temperature with an oven thermometer.

## Cannabutter

Cannabutter (cannabis-infused butter) [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-fungal-dominant-and-bacterial-dominant-compost-tea]] uery-what-are-the-most-common-mushroom-contaminants-and-how-do-i-identify-them base for homemade edibles.

### Recipe

**Ingredients**:
- 1 cup (2 sticks) unsalted butter
- 7-14 grams decarboxylated cannabis (medium to strong potency)
- 1 cup water
- 1 teaspoon sunflower or soy lecithin (optional, improves bioavailability)

**Instructions**:
1. Add butter [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-aquaculture-and-water-systems]] to a saucepan or double boiler
2. Heat on low (160-180 F) until butter is melted
3. Add decarboxylated [[query-what-causes-nutrient-burn-in-cannabis-and-how-to-prevent-it]] lecithin
4. Simmer on low heat for 2-3 hours, stirring every 15-20 minutes
5. Do NOT boil — high heat degrades cannabinoids and creates off-flavors
6. The mixture should never exceed 200 F
7. Strain through cheesecloth-lined strainer into a container
8. Squeeze the cheesecloth firmly to extract all butter
9. Refrigerate the container; butter will solidify on top, water will remain below
10. Separate the solid butter from the water
11. Store [[query-what-are-the-benefits-of-straw-mulch-in-the-garden-and-how-do-i-use-it]] refrigerator for up to 2 months or freezer for up to 6 months

### Potency Estimation

For 14g cannabis at 20% THC:
- Total THC: 2,800 mg
- After decarboxylation (~88% conversion): ~2,464 mg THC
- Butter extraction efficiency (~60-70%): ~1,478-1,725 mg THC
- Per tablespoon (12 tablespoons per cup): ~123-144 mg THC per tablespoon
- Per teaspoon: ~41-48 mg THC per teaspoon

Always calculate and label potency. See [[cannabis-tinctures-guide]] for more detailed potency math.

## Coconut Oil Infusion

Coconut oil is an excellent alternative to butter for vegan edibles and those who prefer a dairy-free option. Coconut oil has a high fat content that efficiently absorbs cannabinoids.

### Recipe

**Ingredients**:
- 1 cup coconut oil (refined for neutral taste; unrefined for coconut flavor)
- 7-14 grams decarboxylated cannabis
- 1 teaspoon lecithin

**Instructions**:
1. Combine coconut oil and [[query-how-do-i-grow-cannabis-in-a-greenhouse]] double boiler or mason jar
2. Heat on low (160-180 F) for 2-3 hours
3. Stir every 15-20 minutes
4. Add lecithin in the last 30 minutes
5. Strain through cheesecloth
6. Pour into a container and allow to cool
7. Solidifies at room temperature; melts at 76 F

### Uses

- Substitute for butter in any recipe at 1:1 ratio
- Add to coffee or tea (stir well — oil does not dissolve in water)
- Use in smoothies
- Direct consumption by the spoonful

## Onset Time and Duration

Understanding the pharmacokinetics of edibles is critical for safe consumption:

- **Onset**: 30 minutes to 2 hours (average 1-2 hours)
- **Peak effects**: 2-4 hours after consumption
- **Duration**: 4-8 hours (up to 12 hours for strong doses)
- **Residual effects**: Can persist for 12-24 hours

### Why Edibles Are Different from Smoking

When cannabis is inhaled, THC enters the bloodstream through the lungs and reaches the brain within minutes. When consumed orally:

1. THC is absorbed through the digestive tract
2. It travels to the liver via the portal vein
3. The liver metabolizes THC into 11-hydroxy-THC, which is significantly more potent and psychoactive
4. 11-hydroxy-THC crosses the blood-brain barrier more efficiently
5. This produces a more intense, longer-lasting, and more body-focused effect

### The Golden Rule

Start low, go slow. Wait at least 2 hours before consuming more. The most common mistake with edibles is re-dosing too soon because effects haven't peaked yet.

## Dosing Guide

| Experience Level | Recommended Dose | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| First-time user | 2.5-5 mg THC | Mild relaxation, minimal psychoactivity |
| Occasional user | 5-10 mg THC | Moderate effects, functional |
| Regular consumer | 10-20 mg THC | Strong effects, potentially impairing |
| Experienced | 20-50 mg THC | Very strong, for high tolerance |
| Medical (pain, sleep) | 10-30 mg THC | Therapeutic, often combined with CBD |

### CBD Edibles

CBD does not produce psychoactive effects at any reasonable dose:
- **Mild**: 5-15 mg
- **Moderate**: 15-30 mg
- **Strong**: 30-60 mg
- **Therapeutic**: Up to 100+ mg (for epilepsy, severe pain)
