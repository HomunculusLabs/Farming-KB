# Ground Cover Plants

Ground cover plants form living carpets over soil, suppressing weeds, retaining moisture, preventing erosion, and adding [[bloomfield-fungal-fruiting-body-morphology-ecological-function]] to the landscape. They serve as living mulches in [[edible-landscaping]] and [[permaculture-design|permaculture]] systems, filling the lowest layer of the plant stack.

## Benefits of Ground Covers

- **Weed suppression**: Dense foliage blocks light to weed seeds, preventing germination
- **Moisture retention**: Shade soil surface, reduce evaporation by 25-50%
- **Soil protection**: [[query-how-do-i-garden-on-a-slope-and-prevent-erosion]] from rain impact and wind
- **Temperature moderation**: Keep soil cooler in summer, warmer in winter
- **Soil building**: Add organic matter as leaves decompose; some fix nitrogen
- **Biodiversity**: Provide habitat for ground beetles, spiders, and other [[beneficial-insects-and-biological-pest-control]]
- **Aesthetic**: Fill gaps between larger plants, soften hardscape edges
- **Living mulch**: Replace wood chip mulch with plants that perform the same function while adding beauty and ecological value

## Creeping Thyme (Thymus serpyllum)

A low-growing, aromatic ground cover that thrives between stepping stones, along pathways, and as a lawn alternative.

- **Height**: 2-4 inches tall, spreads 12-18 inches
- **Sun**: Full sun
- **Soil**: Well-drained, poor to average soil (prefers lean, sandy soil)
- **Hardiness**: Zones 4-9
- **Features**: Tiny leaves, pink/purple/white flowers in summer, aromatic when walked on, tolerates light foot traffic
- **Varieties**: 'Elfin' (tiny leaves, very compact), 'Red creeping thyme' (deep pink flowers), 'Woolly thyme' (silvery foliage)
- **Planting**: Space 6-12 inches apart; propagate by division or cuttings
- **Maintenance**: Shear after flowering to maintain compact shape; virtually pest-free

## Clover (Trifolium spp.)

Nitrogen-fixing ground covers that [[query-how-does-rock-dust-improve-soil-fertility]] while providing nectar for bees.

### White Clover (Trifolium repens)
- **Height**: 4-6 inches
- **Sun**: Full sun to part shade
- **Soil**: Adaptable, tolerates compacted soil
- **Hardiness**: Zones 3-10
- **Features**: White flowers (bee favorite), fixes 50-150 lbs nitrogen per acre annually, tolerates mowing and foot traffic
- **Use**: Excellent lawn alternative or interplanted with grass; reduces fertilizer needs
- **Microclover varieties**: Smaller leaves, fewer flowers, blends better with turf

### Red Clover (Trifolium pratense)
- **Height**: 12-24 inches (taller than white clover)
- **Sun**: Full sun to part shade
- **Features**: Pink/purple flower heads, deeper root system than white clover, fixes more nitrogen
- **Use**: Better for meadow plantings, orchard understory, and cover cropping than as a lawn alternative
- **Biennial**: Lives 2 years, reseeds readily

## Vinca (Vinca minor — Periwinkle)

An evergreen ground cover for shaded areas with glossy leaves and blue flowers.

- **Height**: 4-6 inches, spreads by trailing stems
- **Sun**: Part shade to full shade (tolerates deep shade)
- **Soil**: Any well-drained soil; tolerates dry shade once established
- **Hardiness**: Zones 4-9
- **Features**: Glossy dark green leaves, blue-purple flowers in spring, evergreen in most zones
- **Warning**: Can be aggressive and invasive in some regions — check local status before planting
- **Planting**: Space 12-18 inches apart; fills in within 1-2 years
- **Maintenance**: Shear annually to keep tidy; pull runners that escape intended area

## Dead Nettle (Lamium maculatum)

A shade-tolerant, variegated ground cover with silver-marked leaves and long-blooming flowers.

- **Height**: 6-8 inches, spreads 12-24 inches
- **Sun**: Part shade to full shade (scorch in hot afternoon sun)
- **Soil**: Average to rich, well-drained soil; tolerates dry shade
- **Hardiness**: Zones 3-8
- **Features**: Silver-variegated foliage, pink/purple/white flowers from spring through fall, deer resistant
- **Varieties**: 'Beacon Silver' (pink flowers, silver leaves), 'White Nancy' (white flowers), 'Golden Anniversary' (gold-edged leaves)
- **Planting**: Space 12 inches apart; propagate by division in spring or fall
- **Maintenance**: Shear back if foliage gets raggy in midsummer; new growth flushes quickly
- **Note**: Despite the name, it is not related to stinging nettle and is completely harmless

## Additional Ground Cover Plants

### For Sun
- **Sedum (stonecrop)**: Succulent, drought-tolerant, many varieties; excellent for rock gardens and green roofs
- **Creeping phlox (Phlox subulata)**: Masses of pink/purple/white flowers in spring, evergreen, 4-6 inches tall
- **Ice plant (Delosperma)**: Succulent with daisy-like flowers, extremely drought-tolerant, zones 5-10
- **Ajuga (bugleweed)**: Bronze or purple foliage, blue flower spikes, fast-spreading, 4-6 inches
- **Yarrow ([[achillea-millefolium]])**: Ferny foliage, flat flower clusters, drought-tolerant, 6-12 inches in ground cover form
- **Strawberry (Fragaria spp.)**: Edible fruit, runners fill gaps, 6-8 inches, sun to part shade

### For Shade
- **Sweet woodruff (Galium odoratum)**: Delicate white spring flowers, fragrant foliage, 6-8 inches, dry shade tolerant
- **Wild ginger (Asarum canadense)**: Heart-shaped leaves, spreads slowly, 4-6 inches, deep shade
- **Epimedium (barrenwort)**: Heart-shaped leaves, delicate flowers, drought-tolerant once established, 8-12 inches
- **Bunchberry (Cornus canadensis)**: Dogwood relative, white flowers, red berries, 4-6 inches, woodland native
- **Tiarella (foamflower)**: Clumping, fuzzy leaves, white/pink flower spikes, 6-12 inches

### Nitrogen-Fixing Ground Covers
- **White clover**: Best all-purpose nitrogen fixer
- **Vetch (Vicia spp.)**: Vigorous climber, excellent for slopes and banks
- **Crown vetch (Coronilla varia)**: Aggressive spreader [[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]] (can be invasive — use with caution)
- **Bird's-foot trefoil (Lotus corniculatus)**: Drought-tolerant, yellow flowers, good for poor soils

## Weed Suppression Strategies

Ground covers suppress weeds most effectively when planted densely and managed properly:

- **Plant close together**: Spacing depends on species, but tighter spacing fills in faster
- **Prepare soil thoroughly**: Remove existing weeds before planting (sheet mulch or solarize for 6-8 weeks)
- **Mulch between plants**: Use 2-3 inches of mulch to suppress weeds while ground cover fills in (first 1-2 years)
- **Water and fertilize during establishment**: Stressed plants fill in slowly, leaving weed gaps
- **Edge management**: Install metal or plastic edging to prevent grass from encroaching
- **Select aggressive spreaders for tough areas**: Clover, vinca, and ajuga outcompete most weeds once established

## Using Ground Covers in Garden Design

- **Under fruit trees**: Clover, creeping thyme, or comfrey (in [[food-forest-design|food forest]] systems)
- **Between raised beds**: Clover pathways reduce mowing and fix nitrogen
- **Slope stabilization**: Deep-rooted covers like crown vetch, creeping juniper, or native grasses
- **Lawn replacement**: Clover, creeping thyme, or native sedges for low-maintenance alternatives
- **Pathways**: Creeping thyme between stepping stones releases fragrance when walked on
- **Orchard floor**: Clover or vetch between tree rows reduces mowing and builds soil
