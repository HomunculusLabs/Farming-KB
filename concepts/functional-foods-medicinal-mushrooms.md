# Functional Foods and Medicinal Mushrooms The intersection of functional food science and medicinal mushroom research represents a growing area where nutrition meets pharmacology. Medicinal mushrooms occupy a unique position between traditional food and therapeutic medicine, straddling regulatory categories that vary dramatically across countries. ## Functional Foods: Definition Functional foods are defined as foods that provide health [[cotter-mushroom-products-and-value-added-applications]] (e.g., maitake extract, reishi extract) can be registered as FOSHU
- Separate pharmaceutical pathway exists for purified compounds (PSK, lentinan, schizophyllan)

### United States: Dietary Supplement Framework

- Mushroom extracts marketed as [[ingham-root-disease-treatment]] claims
- Can make "structure/function" claims with appropriate disclaimer
- Quality control varies widely between manufacturers
- No pre-market approval required
- FDA can take action against unsafe products but does not evaluate efficacy

### European Union

- Novel Food Regulation applies to mushroom extracts not consumed significantly before 1997
- Health claims require EFSA (European Food Safety Authority) approval
- EFSA has rejected many mushroom health claims due to insufficient evidence quality
- More restrictive than Japan, similar to US for dietary supplements

### China

- Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) framework provides a pathway for [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]]
- The traditional delivery method (hot water decoction) aligns with the chemistry of water-soluble polysaccharides
