Cannabis has been widely used by HIV/AIDS patients for symptom management, with research supporting its effectiveness for appetite stimulation, pain relief, nausea control, and mood improvement. The [[cannabis-for-night-sweats]].

### Immunomodulatory Effects

Research on cannabinoids and the immune system in the context of HIV/AIDS:
- CB2 receptor activation modulates immune function
- Anti-inflammatory effects may help with chronic immune activation
- Effects on viral replication are complex and not fully understood

### Multi-Symptom Relief

A key advantage of cannabis for HIV/AIDS patients is its ability to address multiple symptoms simultaneously with a single therapeutic agent -- appetite, pain, nausea, mood, sleep, and night sweats can all be improved. This "polypharmacy reduction" benefit is particularly valuable given the complex medication regimens many HIV/AIDS patients already manage.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

HIV/AIDS patients use both indica and sativa strains, often choosing specific strains in response to the severity and presence of their unique symptoms:

- **Indica strains**: For pain relief, sleep promotion, appetite stimulation, relaxation
- **Sativa strains**: For daytime energy, mood elevation, social engagement
- **Hybrid strains**: For balanced multi-symptom management

## Mind-Body Medicine and HIV/AIDS

The AIDS epidemic was identified in an era of social rejection, marginalization, and moral judgment. The mind-body perspective examines:
- AIDS as associated with victimhood -- "a victim of ever-present social rejection, vulnerable to a multitude of ever-present pathogens"
- Issues surrounding love, hate, rage, punishment, vulnerability, and defensiveness
- "Through AIDS, they found out who really loved them -- or not"

Aggravating factors: defensiveness, defenselessness, love=sex mindset, self-destructive guilt, anger, rage, self-hatred, self-punishment.

Supporting approaches: working with personal power, appropriate defenses, love as more than sex, constructive release of guilt and rage, self-forgiveness, self-love and acceptance.
