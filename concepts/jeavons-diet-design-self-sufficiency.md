# Diet Design and Sustainable Food Self-Sufficiency

The [[mycorrhizal-agricultural-practices]] require 7,000 to 63,000 square feet per person
- GROW BIOINTENSIVE can feed one person on 4,000 square feet at intermediate yields

## The 60/30/10 Diet Model

### For One Person (~4,000 sq ft / ~40 beds of 100 sq ft)

**60% — Carbon-and-Calorie Crops** (~24 beds)
- Purpose: Maximum [[solomon-self-sufficiency-calorie-production-economics]]
- Crops: Wheat, cereal rye, oats, barley, triticale, corn, sorghum, amaranth, quinoa, fava beans, sunflowers
- These crops must be interplanted with legumes for nitrogen

**30% — High-Calorie [[solomon-garden-planning-calorie-production]]: Carrots, beets, radishes (too many beds needed)

**10% — Vegetable Crops** (~4 beds)
- Purpose: Vitamins and minerals
- Up to 50-75% of this area may be used for income crops

## Calorie Efficiency Definitions

- **Area-efficient crop:** Can provide total calories with 1,600 sq ft or less (700 sq ft for a woman, 850 sq ft for a man)
- **Weight-efficient crop:** Can provide total calories in 6 lbs or less for a man, 5.5 lbs or less for a woman

## Practical Diet Design Considerations

### Weight Limits
Some calorie crops have daily consumption limits:
- **Potatoes:** Maximum 2.5 lbs/day (potassium toxicity)
- **Sunflower seeds:** Maximum 0.62 lbs/day (copper toxicity)

### Diversity vs. Efficiency
For diet diversity, you may choose less weight-efficient crops. Compensate by:
- Including significant amounts of more weight-efficient crops
- Increasing your design area

### The Complete Diet
A complete sustainable diet must include:
1. Sufficient calories from root crops and grains
2. Vitamins and minerals from vegetable crops
3. Protein from grains and legumes
4. Carbon production from compost crops for soil sustainability
5. Enough area for [[jeavons-transplanting-techniques]].

**Year 2:** Expand by 300 square feet. Begin adding calorie crops (potatoes, garlic).

**Year 3:** Add more beds. Begin compost crops and simple grains.

**Year 4:** Condense vegetable area (from 200 sq ft to 100 sq ft as skills improve), freeing space for protein crops (wheat, rye, peanuts, lentils, soybeans, rice), fibers (cotton, flax), or special interest crops.

For a family of four, start with approximately 300 square feet and add 300 more each year until the entire garden is developed.

## Garden Year Calendar

**Winter:** Plan the garden; order open-pollinated, untreated seeds; make flats, trellises, mini-greenhouses, shade-netting units.

**Spring:** Plant flats while soil is prepared; start compost piles; harvest compost crops; dig beds; spread cured compost; plant cool-weather crops early, warm-weather crops late.

**Summer:** Plant summer crops; keep watered and weeded; harvest.

**Fall:** Start additional compost piles; harvest summer crops; plant fall/winter compost crops. In mild-winter areas, plant fall gardens of cool-weather crops.
