## Strains

Many strains are available from commercial and private stocks, including
the American Type Culture Collection. Several commercial companies sell
strains of this species. However, researchers have noted difficulty in
maintaining high-yielding strains for extended periods. The mycelium has
limited transfer potential and should be stored at moderate temperatures
(50 degrees F). Cultures are frequently renewed through multispore
germinations as described in
[[stamets-spawn-making-and-grain-culture]] for detailed
procedures.

### Fruiting Substrate

Traditionally grown on rice straw composted for 1-2 days. Hu (1974)
developed a higher-yielding substrate of cotton waste supplemented with
wheat bran (5%) and calcium carbonate (5-6%) by weight, composted for
3 days, pasteurized for 2 hours at 140 degrees F, conditioned at 125
degrees F for 8 hours, then gradually cooled to 77 degrees F over 8-12
hours. A moisture content of 65-70% is recommended for rice straw and 70%
for cotton waste mixtures. Chang (1978) recommended combining rice straw
and cotton waste in a 2:1 or 1:1 ratio by weight.

### Spawn Run

- Relative Humidity: 90%+
- Substrate Temperature: 88-95 degrees F (fastest growth)
- Duration: 4-6 days for thorough colonization
- CO2: 5,000-10,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 0 per hour
- Light: Total darkness during incubation

### Pinhead Initiation

- Relative Humidity: 95%+
- Air Temperature: 82-88 degrees F
- Duration: 4 days
- CO2: less than 1,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 2-4 per hour
- Light: Diffuse natural or grow-light fluorescent, 12-18 hours/day
- Watering: Regular misting once to twice daily

### Cropping Cycle

- Relative Humidity: 85-92%
- Air Temperature: 82-88 degrees F
- Duration: 5-7 weeks
- CO2: less than 600 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 2-4 per hour
- Harvest Stage: Before the universal veil ruptures
- Flushing Interval: 5-10 days
- Light: Same as pinning (12-18 hours daily)
- Watering: Regular misting to prevent cap cracking

## Yield Potential and Nutrition

Average commercial yields on rice straw are 22-28 kg fresh mushrooms per
100 kg dry straw. Optimum yields on cotton waste compost reach 25-35 kg
per 100 kg substrate, with maximum yields approaching 45 kg. Mushrooms
are 88-90% water, 10-12% dry matter, with crude protein at 21.2% of
dry weight and 91 mg niacin per 100 grams dry weight.

## Genetics

Basidia are tetrapolar, producing 4 haploid spores; the species is
primarily homothallic. Clamp connections are present and chlamydospores
form. See
