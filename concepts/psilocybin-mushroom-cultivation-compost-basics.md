# Psilocybin Mushroom Cultivation Compost Basics

F.C. Ghouled's 1972 *Field Guide to the Psilocybin Mushroom* included a
foundational overview of [[psilocybin-mushroom-cultivation-basics]] techniques, focusing on [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]] as the target species. The guide described horse manure compost
preparation, spawn production, and bed management -- representing one of the
earliest published practical cultivation instructions in the underground
psychedelic literature.

## The Horse Manure Compost

Ghouled identified horse manure compost as the primary growing medium for
[[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]]:

> "Mushrooms require a special compost. By far the most common is the
> manure compost manufactured from horse manure, wheat-straw and added
> chemicals to provide the best growing medium."

This three-component system -- manure for nitrogen and microorganisms,
wheat straw for carbon and structure, and supplementary chemicals for
nutrient balance -- remains the basis for traditional mushroom composting.

## Composting Process

Ghouled described the compost preparation: pile the materials together
after soaking the wheat-straw well in water. The ingredients decompose,
generating heat up to 180 degrees Fahrenheit and killing any spores,
insects, larvae, and molds growing in the medium.

This thermophilic phase is critical for contamination prevention.
Self-generated heat of 180 degrees Fahrenheit (82 degrees Celsius) kills
most competing organisms, nematodes, insect eggs, and weed spores.

As the compost breaks down: "the straw breaks into short pieces and the
manure crumbles. The entire pile then loses its smell and takes on the
odor characteristic of the woods in autumn."

This transformation indicates composting completion. The loss of raw
manure odor and development of an earthy scent signals that ammonia has
been converted and the compost is suitable for mushroom growth.

## Moisture Testing

Ghouled's practical moisture test: "The compost should ball when
squeezed in the hand but no excess water should be observed." It
should not be compact, as straw serves to aerate it and add nutrients.

## Spawn Production

Ghouled described a malt-enriched manure spawn technique:

> "The prepared compost must then be inoculated with some stage of the
> mushroom's life-cycle. This is usually done by composting horse-manure
> alone that has been enriched with malt-extract sugar."

The spawn production process involves shredding the malt-enriched
composted manure, packing it loosely into wide-mouth jars, and
inoculating with pieces of fresh cap of the preferred variety:

> "The mycellium will then be observed to run, growing all through the
> manure. When it has completely permeated the compost the bulk is
> removed from the jar and dried."

This jar-based spawn production is a direct precursor to the
[[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] and other jar-based cultivation
techniques that became popular in later decades.

## Bed Preparation and Inoculation

Once spawn is prepared, it is introduced into the main growing bed:

> "Small chunks of this spawn (as it is called) are inserted into the
> compost at regular intervals. They will begin to grow, and after a
> number of weeks will completely dominate the prepared mushroom-bed."

The spawn-to-bulk ratio and distribution are critical. Even distribution
ensures complete colonization and prevents competing organisms from
establishing in uncolonized areas.

## Casing Layer

Ghouled described the casing technique:

> "The bed is then covered with a 1-inch layer of sterilized soil or
> acceptable substitute, and watered lightly now and again."

The casing layer serves moisture retention, microclimate creation, and
physical support for developing fruiting bodies. Ghouled emphasized:

> "The compost MUST NOT be flooded with water. This will kill the
> mycellium and ruin the compost."

> "The casing will serve to hold all of the moisture that the compost
> will need and prevent the excess from sinking."

## Bed Dimensions and Yield

> "The beds should be made about 12 inches deep in a container small
> enough to be handled easily."

> "The traditional compost will last for 4 to 6 months with a yield of
> two or more pounds of mushrooms per-square-foot."

These yield figures are consistent with commercial mushroom cultivation
benchmarks. The 12-inch depth provides adequate substrate mass for
sustained fruiting over multiple flushes.

## Ghouled's Planned Perpetual Bed System

Ghouled mentioned plans for an advanced text describing "a perpetual
compost-box that will produce many pounds a month" and "a newly
discovered and yet (commercially) unproved method for constructing a
perpetual bed that will be self-regulating." This suggests Ghouled was
developing continuous-flow cultivation concepts later refined by
cultivators like Oss and Oeric ([[oss-oeric-psilocybe-cubensis-strain-guide]])
and Paul Stamets.
