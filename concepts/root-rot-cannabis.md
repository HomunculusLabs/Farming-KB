## Root Rot on Cannabis

Root rot is a fungal condition that attacks the root system of cannabis plants, progressively destroying the plant's ability to absorb water and nutrients. It is most common in hydroponic systems and overwatered soil gardens where roots are deprived of oxygen. Root rot is particularly insidious because the damage is hidden below the growing medium surface, and above-ground symptoms often do not appear until the infection is well advanced. By the time foliage symptoms are noticeable, root damage is often severe.

## Identification

Root rot progresses through visible stages that can be observed by examining the root system:

- **Early stage**: Roots turn from healthy white to light brown
- **Mid stage**: Roots progressively darken to darker brown
- **Advanced stage**: Rot progresses up to the base of the plant stock, turning it dark
- **Severe stage**: Roots become slimy and may disintegrate when touched; the characteristic foul odor of anaerobic decomposition may be present

Above-ground symptoms include:

- Leaf chlorosis (yellowing) beginning with older leaves and progressing upward
- Wilting across the entire plant, even when soil or solution appears adequately moist
- Slowed growth — foliage is very slow to grow when roots are rotten
- Overall loss of plant vigor and reduced [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]] appearing despite adequate [[root-anatomy-structure-and-nutrient-uptake]]
- Nitrogen overfertilization weakens root health and makes tissue more susceptible
- [[aact-dissolved-oxygen-fine-bubble-aeration-ingham]] available to roots
- Containers lack adequate drainage, allowing water to pool around roots

## Damage Symptoms

Root rot causes both direct and indirect damage to cannabis plants:

- **Direct root damage**: Progressive browning and decay of the root system, starting with fine root hairs and advancing to larger roots
- **Nutrient uptake failure**: As roots die, the plant cannot absorb water or nutrients, leading to wilting and [[hamilton-nutrient-deficiencies-and-treatment]] when the actual problem is that roots cannot take up available nutrients.

## Prevention

- Use fresh, sterile growing medium for each crop
- Ensure adequate calcium levels in the nutrient solution
- Avoid overfertilizing with nitrogen
- Maintain pH above 6.5 in soil and approximately 6.0 in hydroponic mediums
- Control all insects, fungi, and bacteria that eat roots — soil pests create entry wounds
- Prevent overwatering and ensure proper drainage in all growing media
- Do not let nutrient solution become stagnant; ensure adequate aeration and circulation
- Keep nutrient solution temperature between 60-75°F (15-24°C)
- Never let nutrient solution temperature climb above 85°F (29°C) — above this, solution holds little oxygen and roots are easily damaged
- Inspect roots regularly during reservoir changes or transplanting
- Use clean tools and containers to avoid introducing pathogens
- Ensure containers have adequate drainage holes and that water does not pool

## Organic Controls

Several commercial biological products are effective against root rot:

- **[[trichoderma-harzianum]]** (trade names: RootShield, Trichopel, SoilGuard)
- **Binab Bio-Fungus** (contains Trichoderma species)
- **Supresivit** (contains Trichoderma species)

[Trichoderma species work by colonizing the root zone and outcompeting pathogenic fungi for space and nutrients. They also produce compounds that inhibit the growth of root rot organisms. These products are most effective when applied preventatively — introducing beneficial fungi before pathogens become established gives them the best chance to protect the root zone. Apply at planting time or at the first sign of stress.

## Chemical Controls

- **Sprays are NOT effective against root rot** — treatment must be applied directly to the root zone
