# Distribution of Neurotropic Fungi

The global distribution of neurotropic fungi reveals striking patterns that
reflect evolutionary history, climate, and the ecology of their preferred
substrates. The most comprehensive analysis, by Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz
(1998), documented 216 species across all continents except Antarctica, with
concentrations strongly correlated with subtropical and tropical humid forests.

## Continental Overview

### Mexico

Mexico is the world epicenter of neurotropic fungal diversity, hosting **76
species** of which 44 belong to the genus Psilocybe — representing 39% of
all known Psilocybe species worldwide. This extraordinary concentration is
attributed to Mexico's varied topography, tropical and subtropical climates,
and the extensive history of indigenous mushroom use that has driven
mycological exploration. The states of Oaxaca, Puebla, and Veracruz are
particularly rich. See [[entheogenic-fungi-in-mexico]] for details on
ethnobotanical use.

### Central and South America

The neotropics harbor significant diversity. Notable species include
[[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]] (pantropical), Psilocybe semilanceata|P.
semilanceata in highland Colombia, and numerous species in Brazil, Colombia,
Ecuador, Peru, and Venezuela. The Amazon basin remains underexplored
mycologically, suggesting that actual diversity may be substantially higher
than documented.

### Asia

Asia has a rich but unevenly documented neurotropic mycoflora. Japan reports
several Gymnopilus and Psilocybe species. Southeast Asia (Thailand,
Cambodia, Vietnam, Indonesia, the Philippines) contains significant
populations of [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]], Copelandia species,
and Panaeolus species, particularly in rice paddy regions where bovine
grazing provides dung substrates. [[psilocybe-samuiensis-profile]] was
described from Thailand. Nepal and India report species at high elevations.

### Oceania

Australia and [[psilocybin-mushrooms-new-zealand-guide]] both have notable neurotropic fungi
diversity. Australia hosts [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]],
[[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]] (one of the most potent known
species), [[psilocybe-australiana]], and several
Gymnopilus species. New Zealand is known for Psilocybe
aucklandii|P. aucklandii, Psilocybe weraroa|P. weraroa, and
[[psilocybe-semilanceata]].

### Europe

European neurotropic fungi are predominantly temperate species.
[[psilocybe-semilanceata]] (the liberty cap) is the most
widespread and well-known, found across much of northern and western Europe.
Other European species include [[stamets-psi-psilocybe-cyanescens-species-guide]],
[[psilocybe-bohemica]], Inocybe aeruginascens|I.
aeruginascens, and [[pluteus-salicinus]]. The genus
Gymnopilus is also represented with several species.

### North America (outside Mexico)

The United States and Canada have moderate diversity. The Pacific Northwest
is particularly rich, with [[stamets-psi-psilocybe-cyanescens-species-guide]], Psilocybe
azurescens|P. azurescens (one of the most potent species known),
[[psilocybe-stuntzii-profile]], and Psilocybe baeocystis|P.
baeocystis. [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] occurs in northern
latitudes. The southeastern US has [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]] in dung
and subtropical habitats. Gulf Coast states harbor species in lawns and
pastures.

### Africa

Africa is the most poorly documented continent for neurotropic fungi. Reports
exist from several countries but systematic surveys are rare. Psilocybe
cubensis|P. cubensis is widespread in tropical and subtropical Africa.
Panaeolus and Copelandia species are reported from various regions.
The continent's vast mycological diversity remains largely unexplored.

## Ecological Correlates

### Hemispheric Bias

The majority of Psilocybe species are found in or near the **Austral
(southern) hemisphere**, primarily in subtropical humid forests. This
latitudinal bias is one of the most striking patterns in the distribution.
The exact reasons are debated but may relate to historical biogeography,
rainfall patterns, and the distribution of specific substrate types.

### Substrate and Habitat

Most neurotropic fungi fall into ecological categories:

- **Coprophilous** (dung-inhabiting): Panaeolus, Copelandia,
  [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]], Panaeolina. These follow grazing
  animals and are often pantropical in distribution.

- **Lignicolous** (wood-inhabiting): Gymnopilus, Pluteus, many
  Psilocybe species. Found on decaying wood, wood chips, mulch, and
  forest debris.

- **Terricolous** (soil-inhabiting): [[psilocybe-semilanceata]], [[psilocybe-mexicana-cultivation]], and many montane
  species. Often associated with grasslands, mosses, or forest soils.

- **Mycorrhizal**: Some Inocybe species form associations with tree
  roots, complicating cultivation potential.

## Biogeographic Patterns

The pantropical distribution of species like Psilocybe cubensis|P.
cubensis reflects the global movement of cattle and the ubiquity of suitable
dung substrates. In contrast, species with restricted ranges (such as
[[stamets-psi-psilocybe-azurescens-species-guide]], limited to the Pacific Northwest coast)
may reflect specialized habitat requirements or evolutionary isolation. The
highest species richness occurs in montane tropical and subtropical forests,
where moisture, temperature, and substrate diversity create optimal conditions
for fungal fruiting.
