# Fertilizer Selection and Application Rates

## The Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF) Recipe

[[foliar-feeding]]

Solomon discusses [[comparison-foliar-feeding-vs-compost-tea-nutrients]] (spraying [[jadam-liquid-fertilizer-nutritional-perspective]] on leaves) as a
supplement but not a replacement for soil fertility. The most effective foliar
spray is a dilute solution of fish emulsion or liquid kelp, applied early in the
morning when leaf stomata are open. Foliar feeding provides a quick boost during
critical growth stages but cannot sustain a crop through an entire season.

## Fertigation Rates

When using fertigation (water + dissolved fertilizer delivered through a bucket
or drip system), Solomon recommends:

- Fish emulsion: dilute 1:100 (one part emulsion to 100 parts water).  - Compost
tea: one bucketful of compost per barrel of water, brewed one week.  - COF
solution: dissolve about one cup of COF in 5 gallons of water. This is too
concentrated for most applications; dilute further for sensitive plants.

Fertigation rates by soil type: - Sandy soil (under 10 percent clay): about 2.5
gallons every 10 days per plant.  - Loam soil (10-20 percent clay): about 4
gallons every two weeks.  - Clay soil (over 20 percent clay): about 5 gallons
every three weeks.

## Sacked Manure as Fertilizer

Sacked steer manure from [[solomon-garden-centers-seedling-traps]] has little fertilizing value but feeds
soil microbes and improves soil structure. Sacked chicken manure (approximately
NPK 4-3-2) is far better but should not be overused -- it is high in nitrogen
and can burn plants if applied too heavily. Apply chicken manure at about half
the rate of COF.
