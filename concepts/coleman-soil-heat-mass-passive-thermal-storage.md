## Overview

The unheated winter greenhouse system developed by Eliot Coleman at Four
Season Farm relies on a fundamental but often overlooked principle: the soil
itself serves as the primary heat storage medium. Unlike heated greenhouses
that depend on furnaces, heat pumps, or thermal mass walls, the cold house
system stores heat in the soil mass beneath the growing beds and releases it
slowly through the winter months. This passive thermal storage is the

These systems typically involved water tanks, rock beds, or masonry walls
designed to capture and store heat during the day for release at night.
They required pumps, fans, thermostats, and control systems. They were
expensive to install and maintain, and they frequently failed because
the control systems were not robust enough for agricultural conditions.

Coleman explicitly rejected these complicated systems, following what he
called his "minimalist preferences." The soil was already there. It was
already absorbing heat. The only thing needed was to protect it from
rapid heat loss — and two layers of translucent material accomplished
that with no moving parts, no controls, and no energy input. The
simplicity of the system was not a compromise forced by budget constraints;
it was a deliberate design choice based on the recognition that the
simplest solution is usually the most reliable.
