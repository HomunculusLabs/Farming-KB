# Soil as Water Storage

Toby Hemenway makes a compelling case that the cheapest and
most effective place to store water is in the soil itself.
Rather than investing in tanks, ponds, and irrigation systems,
ecological gardeners can build soil that acts as a giant
sponge, holding thousands of gallons of water and releasing it
slowly to plants over weeks and months.

## Contouring to Catch Water

Beyond building soil, Hemenway describes sculpting the land
to hold water. A simple technique is creating concavities in
the landscape where rainwater collects and soaks in. In Mary
Zemach's New Mexico garden, circular patches of buffalo grass
were contoured like shallow dishes, the center a few inches
lower than the edges. These gentle depressions are invisible
to the eye but the rain knows they are there.
