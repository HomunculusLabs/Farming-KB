# Kingdom Fungi - Overview

## Definition and Characteristics

Fungi are heterotrophic organisms that permeate our environment. With few exceptions they have:

- Filamentous bodies enclosed by cell walls
- Nonmotile vegetative states (except zoosporic groups)
- Reproduction both sexually and asexually by spores
- Absorptive nutrition (no phagocytosis or ingestion)

Fungi are more closely related to animals than to plants, possibly through a choanoflagellate-like ancestor (Barr 1992; Baldauf and Palmer 1993; Wainright et al. 1993). Historically classified within botany, contemporary molecular studies have firmly established Kingdom Fungi as a monophyletic group distinct from plants and straminipiles.

## Scope of Fungal Diversity

Current estimates of fungal diversity are staggering:

- Approximately 74,000 to 120,000 species have been formally described
- Conservative estimates place total species at 1,500,000
- Evidence from tropical studies and molecular investigations suggests even this may be too low
- We may know at most 5% of fungal species on Earth
- Estimated six times more fungal species than land plant species
- Fewer than 800 taxa have been included in any single phylogenetic reconstruction (less than 1% of described species)

## The "Fungi" Are Polyphyletic

The broad concept of "fungi" traditionally included several groups now known to be polyphyletic (convergent morphologies derived independently). The major groups previously classified as fungi include:

### True Fungi (Kingdom Fungi)

A monophyletic, well-supported group comprising:

- **Chytridiomycota**: Flagellated zoospores; not monophyletic but basal to other fungi
- **Zygomycota**: Defined by zygospore formation; polyphyletic in modern analyses
- **Glomales**: Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi; sister to Ascomycota/Basidiomycota
- **Ascomycota**: Largest phylum (~32,000 species); ascospore production in asci
- **Basidiomycota**: Second largest phylum; basidiospore production on basidia

### Crown Fungi

Glomales, Ascomycota, and Basidiomycota comprise a large monophyletic clade of "crown" fungi -- the most derived, higher grouping within Kingdom Fungi. The appearance of this clade probably coincided with the origin and diversification of land plants. An estimated 80% of all plant species are associated with Glomales. The crown fungi predominate in terrestrial environments today.

### Straminipila (Heterokont Organisms)

Previously classified as fungi but now placed in a separate kingdom:

- **Oomycota**: Water molds, downy mildews; heterokont flagellation
- **Hyphochytriomycetes**: Anterior tinsellated flagellum
- **Labyrinthulales and Thraustochytriales**: Marine organisms

### Slime Molds

Also once considered fungi but now placed separately:

- **Plasmodiophorales**: Parasitic slime molds; near alveolates
- **Myxomycota**: Plasmodial slime molds
- **Dictyosteliomycota**: Cellular slime molds
- **Acrasiomycota**: Acrasid slime molds

## Key Phylogenetic Findings

Based on analyses of SSU rDNA, LSU rDNA, and protein-coding genes (alpha-tubulin, beta-tubulin, actin, EF-1a):

- Kingdom Fungi is monophyletic
- Microsporidia are a sister group to Fungi, together forming a clade with Metazoa (animals)
- The traditional four phyla do not accurately represent phylogeny:
  - Chytridiomycota is not monophyletic
  - A core monophyletic group of Zygomycota does not include Glomales
  - Ascomycota and Basidiomycota are monophyletic sister taxa
  - Glomales + Ascomycota + Basidiomycota = crown fungi

## Classification Challenges

Fungal classification remains in flux. Problems include:

- Relationships of all groups have not been resolved
- Many groups not represented in molecular analyses
- Fewer than 1% of species included in phylogenetic trees
- Only rDNA widely used; multi-gene approaches increasing
- Formal classifications may vary in inclusiveness
- A moratorium on higher-level taxonomic changes until 2050 has been suggested

Hibbett and Donoghue (1998) proposed using clade names rather than formal taxon designations for higher taxa where relationships are uncertain.

## Importance of Fungi

Fungi are crucial for:

- Nutrient cycling and decomposition
- Plant nutrition through mycorrhizal associations
- Food sources for animals
- Breakdown of woody materials
- Insect digestion of plant materials
- Soil structure formation
- Sources of antibiotics (Penicillium) and pharmaceuticals
- Food and beverage production (Saccharomyces)
- Biological control of weeds and pests
- Sustainable development ("Non-Green Revolution")

As habitats are degraded and destroyed each year, hundreds or perhaps thousands of fungal species are being lost before they are known to science.
