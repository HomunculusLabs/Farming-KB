# Fungal Treatment of Olive Mill Wastewater

Olive mill wastewater (OMW) is one of the most problematic agro-industrial effluents in Mediterranean countries, which produce about 98% of global olive output. Fungal biotreatment offers a promising approach for OMW detoxification and phenolic compound removal.

## OMW Composition and Characteristics

- Production: approximately 1-2 tons of OMW per ton of olives, or 2.5 L waste per liter of oil produced

- Chemical oxygen demand (COD): up to 82,000 mg/l

- Total phenol content: 1,200-10,000 mg/l

- Contains phenolics, tannin, and lignin

- Exhibits high phytotoxic and antibacterial activity

- Conventional treatment methods (anaerobic digestion, evaporation) are often ineffective

## Conventional Treatment Limitations

- Anaerobic digestion: high toxicity, low biodegradability, reactor acidification

- Chlorine-based bleaching: produces toxic organochlorine compounds

- Ozonolysis: effective but expensive (high electricity consumption)

- Physical treatments (flocculation, adsorption, ion exchange): do not destroy contaminants, merely relocate them

## Microbial Community of OMW

Since OMW contains phenolics, tannin, and lignin, attention is focused on microorganisms capable of degrading such compounds. Microbial composition from four disposal ponds led to the identification of several species of bacteria, yeasts, and molds. Strains of Fusarium, Paecilomyces, Penicillium, and Scopulariopsis exhibited a marked capacity for the detoxification of OMW, removing its antibacterial activity completely.

## Fungal Species for OMW Treatment

### Phanerochaete chrysosporium

- Eliminates more than 50% of color and phenols from OMW within 6 days

- Achieves 92% phenol reduction, 75% COD and TOC reduction in 150 hours

- Aerobic pretreatment reduces phenolics and toxic aromatic compounds before anaerobic treatment

- Produces lignin peroxidase (LiP) and manganese peroxidase (MnP)

- Pretreatment reduced COD from 107 g/l to 55 g/l and to 85 g/l after sedimentation

- About 70% of color and 80% of COD removed when supplemented with veratryl alcohol and flushed with oxygen

### Pleurotus ostreatus

- Reaches similar results to P. chrysosporium but in 12 days

- Shows 49% decolorization efficiency

- Highest laccase activity among eight white-rot fungi tested

- At 10% dilution: 90% phenol reduction in 100 hours

- MnP activity necessary for efficient decolorization

- Phenol detoxifying activity is concomitant with increasing phenol oxidase production

### Other Effective Fungi

- Penicillium strain P4: 54% phenol reduction, 61% COD reduction, 80% decolorization; produces 21.5 g dry weight/L biomass

- Phanerochaete flavido-alba: 51% phenol reduction, 70% decolorization, eliminates toxicity up to 70%

- Aspergillus niger: 76% phenol reduction, 73% COD reduction; tolerates toxic substances in undiluted OMW

- Aspergillus terreus: 64% phenol reduction, 63% COD reduction

- Lentinula edodes: 90% phenol reduction, 85% TOC reduction at 20% dilution

- Pycnosporus coccineus: 47% COD reduction, 58% decolorization

### Additional Fungi for Decolorization

Pycnosporus coccineus, Pleurotus sajor-caju, Coriolopsis polyzona, and Lentinula tigrinus were effective in decolorization and COD removal of crude OMW diluted to 50 and 75 g/l after 20 days. P. sajor-caju and C. polyzona showed 72 to 75% decolorization of effluent diluted to 50 g/l COD.

## Enzyme Systems Involved

Laccase is the predominant enzyme detected in OMW treatment, followed by manganese-independent peroxidase (MIP). Substantial MnP activity is found only with P. ostreatus and Abortiporus biennis. LiP and veratryl alcohol oxidase (VAOx) are not detected in most OMW systems. Early detection of laccase activity correlates with final reduction of total phenolics.

LiP and MnP of P. chrysosporium contribute to decolorization. More than 70% of OMW was decolorized when P. chrysosporium grows in the presence of low Mn(II) concentration resulting in high LiP levels. The highest decolorization occurred at low initial COD with high extracellular LiP.

## Immobilized Bioreactor Systems

Immobilized bioreactor configurations have been developed for OMW treatment:

- P. chrysosporium on polyurethane foam: 70% color and COD removal in repeated-batch fermentation

- A. niger on polyurethane sponge cubes: 67% phenol removal in 8 days; also enables enrichment with soluble rock phosphate for fertilizer production

- Lentinula edodes laccase on chitosan/glutardehyde: 67% total phenol and 72% ortho-phenol removal in 24 hours

- L. edodes laccase in oxirane fluidized-bed reactor: 70% total phenols, 88% ortho-phenols, 22% decolorization in 2 hours

## Combined Treatment Approaches

Combined fungal pretreatment followed by anaerobic digestion improves overall treatment efficiency. Phanerochaete flavido-alba produces MnP and laccase simultaneously, enabling concurrent decolorization and aromatic compound reduction. A novel method combining A. niger and electrochemical treatment in the presence of H2O2 removed 66-86% COD and 65% phenols.

## Modeling and Kinetics

Fermentation follows Monod kinetics on COD or TOC as substrate. Removal of phenolic compounds follows the sequence P. chrysosporium > A. niger > A. terreus. Anaerobic digestion of pretreated OMW follows first-order kinetics, with the kinetic constant increasing as phenolic content and biotoxicity decrease.
