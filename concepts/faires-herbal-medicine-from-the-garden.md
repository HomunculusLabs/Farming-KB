## Wild Medicinal Plants

### Pyretheum Daisy (Chrysanthemum cinerariifolium)

Grown as a natural pesticide, not internally consumed:

- Prefers dry, somewhat sandy soils and needs weeding but few other interventions
- Harvest on a warm, sunny day when flowers have been open for a few days
- Dry by hanging upside down or removing heads for sun drying
- Store whole in a dark, airtight container
- Grind into fine powder when ready to use — dust or spray (mixed with water) on affected plants
- Insects die almost immediately without harm to humans

### Bunchberry (Cornus canadensis)

A perennial found in northern areas around stumps and mossy wet spots:

- Berries are edible though somewhat bland — best in sauce, jams, and pudding
- Berries help thicken jam naturally (reduces need for added pectin)
- **Berry tea is a traditional herbal remedy**
- Soil temperature must stay below 65°F (18°C)
- Enjoys soil full of rotting wood (add as mulch)

### American Linden (Tilia americana)

A traditional multi-use tree:

- Leaf and bark used in traditional medicine
- Provides valuable timber when seedlings are protected from deer
- Historically used to make fiber for baskets and nets
- Serves as bee forage
