# Mushroom Cultivation for Food and Medicine

Updated: 2026-04-18

Mushroom cultivation is a practical and rewarding skill for homesteaders,
providing nutritious food and potentially medicinal fungi from relatively
small spaces. Unlike plants, mushrooms do not require light for growth (they
need it only for fruiting body orientation) and can be grown indoors
year-round in unused spaces like basements, closets, or spare rooms.

### Log Cultivation (Outdoor)

Hardwood logs (oak, maple, beech, alder) are inoculated with mushroom spawn:

1. Cut fresh logs in late winter/early spring before leaf-out
2. Drill holes and insert plug spawn (wooden dowels colonized with mycelium)
3. Seal holes with wax to prevent drying and contamination
4. Stack logs in a shady, moist location
5. Wait 6-18 months for colonization
6. "Shock" logs by soaking in cold water for 24-48 hours to initiate fruiting
7. Harvest multiple flushes over several years

### Straw Bale Cultivation

Pasteurize straw by soaking in hot water, pack into bags or buckets,
inoculate with grain spawn, and incubate. Oyster mushrooms are particularly
productive on straw.

## Nutritional Value

- Protein (20-35% dry weight)

- Minerals (selenium, potassium, copper, zinc)
- Beta-glucans (immune-stimulating polysaccharides)
