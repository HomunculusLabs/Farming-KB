## Junkus Weed and Mechanical Difficulty

Junkus (*Juncus*) is a thick, rhizomatous weed that spreads
aggressively through pasture land. It forms dense mats of roots and
runners that are extremely resistant to mechanical removal. Tractors
and mowers struggle to cut through it effectively, and tilling often
breaks the rhizomes into pieces that simply regrow.

For farmers looking to establish new orchard sites on junkus-infested
land, conventional clearing methods are both expensive and largely
ineffective. Rhizomatous weeds like junkus are particularly challenging
because their underground storage organs allow them to survive
disturbance. Each piece of broken rhizome can generate a new plant,
meaning that mechanical tilling can actually spread the infestation
rather than control it. Chemical herbicides may kill the tops but
rarely reach the full depth of the root system, and repeated
applications contaminate soil and water.

## The Pig Solution

Pigs love to dig with their snouts, rooting up whatever they can find.
When fenced into an area dominated by junkus, they systematically work
through the soil, tearing up rhizomes and eating roots and tubers. The
longer they remain in the area, the more thoroughly the soil is cleared.

The process works because pigs are highly motivated foragers that will
dig through tough root masses all day long. They consume the rhizomes,
