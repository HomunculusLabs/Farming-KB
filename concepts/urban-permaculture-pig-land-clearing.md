# Pig Land Clearing

Pigs offer a remarkably effective biological solution for clearing
stubborn weed infestations and preparing land for orchard planting.
Rather than relying on [[jadam-soil-compaction-heavy-machinery]] or chemical herbicides,
permaculture systems leverage pigs& [[mollison-designers-willow-water-and-natural-rooting-propagation]] behavior to
transform dense weed mats into workable soil. This approach aligns with
the broader principle of using animals as partners in land management.

## Junkus Weed and Mechanical Difficulty

Junkus (*Juncus*) is a thick, rhizomatous weed that spreads
aggressively through pasture land. It forms dense mats of roots and
runners that are extremely resistant to mechanical removal. Tractors
and mowers struggle to cut through it effectively, and tilling often
breaks the rhizomes into pieces that simply regrow.

For farmers looking to establish new orchard sites on junkus-infested
land, conventional clearing methods are both expensive and largely
ineffective. Rhizomatous weeds like junkus are particularly challenging
because their underground storage organs allow them to survive
disturbance. Each piece of broken rhizome can generate a new plant,
meaning that mechanical tilling can actually spread the infestation
rather than control it. Chemical herbicides may kill the tops but
rarely reach the full depth of the root system, and repeated
applications contaminate soil and water.

## The Pig Solution

Pigs love to dig with their snouts, rooting up whatever they can find.
When fenced into an area dominated by junkus, they systematically work
through the soil, tearing up rhizomes and eating roots and tubers. The
longer they remain in the area, the more thoroughly the soil is cleared.

The process works because pigs are highly motivated foragers that will
dig through tough root masses all day long. They consume the rhizomes,
eliminating the regrowth mechanism. Their rooting aerates compacted soil
and breaks up hardpans. Their manure adds [[savory-soil-organic-matter-and-decomposition-dynamics]] fertility
to the freshly turned soil. They accomplish all of this without diesel
fuel, mechanical wear, or soil compaction from heavy equipment.

Pigs are among the most intelligent domesticated animals, and they
thrive when given complex environments to explore. A junkus-infested
paddock is not a problem for a pig -- it is an adventure playground and
a buffet. This natural enthusiasm for rooting makes them ideal partners
in land preparation.

## Integrated Benefits

The pig land-clearing system creates multiple simultaneous benefits:

1. **Feed recycling** -- pigs are fed kitchen and farm garbage,
   converting waste into meat and soil fertility
2. **Land preparation** -- the rooted-up soil is ready for orchard
   planting without additional tilling
3. **Soil improvement** -- pig manure and aeration create favorable
   conditions for newly planted fruit [[holzer-sweet-chestnut-and-nut-trees]]
4. **Animal welfare** -- pigs engage in natural behaviors (rooting,
   foraging, exploring) rather than being confined to concrete pens
5. **Food production** -- the pigs themselves become a protein source
   for the farm community

This is a perfect example of the [[permaculture-principle-apply-self-regulation]] that "every
element should perform multiple functions." The pigs are not just
land-clearing tools -- they are waste recyclers, soil builders, and
food producers simultaneously.

## Planning the Rotation

Effective pig clearing requires careful planning:

- **Fencing** -- electric fencing or sturdy panels contain pigs in
  the target area; portable electric fence allows easy movement between
  paddocks
- **Duration** -- leave pigs long enough to thoroughly work the soil
  but not so long they compact it or create excessive erosion;
  typically 2-4 weeks per paddock depending on density
- **Water** -- provide wallows and drinking water; pigs need access
  to mud for thermoregulation and skin health
- **Shelter** -- shade structures protect pigs from sun and rain;
  simple A-frame shelters work well and can be moved with the pigs
- **Follow-up** -- once pigs are moved out, plant [[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos]]
