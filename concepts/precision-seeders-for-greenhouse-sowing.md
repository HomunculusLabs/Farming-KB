# Precision Seeders for Greenhouse Sowing

Precision seeders are essential tools for intensive greenhouse vegetable production, enabling growers to maximize yield per square foot by placing seeds at optimal spacing. Eliot Coleman's experience at [[four-season-farm]] demonstrates that switching from single-row and homemade multi-row seeders to precision models practically doubled yields per square foot.

## The Pinpoint Seeder

The Pinpoint Seeder is a manually operated tool (not a machine) whose effectiveness depends on the skill and care of the user. It uses two seed-selection mechanisms:

1. **Seed-hole sizes**: Four hole sizes (labeled A through D, with A being the smallest) on a movable axle. The axle is positioned so the desired seed-hole size centers under the hoppers.
2. **Adjustable brushes**: Each hopper contains a small brush that removes extra seeds from the seed hole. Brush tension is adjusted with a thumbscrew, rated as T (tight), M (medium), or L (loose).

### Seed Hole Specifications

- Sizes A, B, and C have nine holes per rotation of the axle, dropping approximately one seed per inch (wheel circumference ~9 inches).
- Size D has seven holes per rotation.
- A modified axle with deeper seed holes (D deepened by ⅛ inch, A/B/C by 1/16 inch) can accommodate larger seeds like beets and chard that fail with the standard axle.

### Seedbed Preparation Requirements

Precision seeders demand finely prepared soil free of rocks, undecomposed compost chunks, stringy weeds, or root residues. After raking beds smooth, a light rolling with a 30-inch-wide mesh-surfaced roller is recommended. The mesh surface prevents soil glazing that a smooth roller would cause. Rolling also prevents the seeder wheels from sinking too deeply and bogging down.

### Determining Seeding Settings

Before sowing any seed lot, growers should test hole and brush combinations:

1. Fill one hopper and turn the wheel, counting seeds dropped per turn.
2. Record the optimal combination on the seed packet using a code system (e.g., "4BT" for four-row seeder, hole size B, brush tight).
3. Different settings may be needed for different seasons—arugula is sown at 4AM in low winter light but 4BT in summer.

### Recommended Settings by Crop

| Crop | Hole Size | Brush Setting | Notes |
|------|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Baby-leaf lettuce (greenhouse) | B | T | Standard indoor rate |
| Baby-leaf lettuce (outdoor) | B | M | Slightly higher rate |
| Arugula (winter) | A | M | Lower light conditions |
| Arugula (summer) | A | T | Standard rate |
| Claytonia, minutina, sylvetta | A | T | Very small seeds; multiple seeds per drop acceptable |

### Modifications for Small Seeds

Seeds like claytonia, minutina, and sylvetta are so small that even the smallest hole drops multiple seeds. Coleman sealed the seams in the seed hoppers with liquid solder to prevent these tiny seeds from falling through. Tight brush settings and the crops' tolerance for close spacing make this workable.

### Operating Technique

- Walk backwards down the path beside the bed while pulling the seeder, watching for wheel rotation and seed drop.
- Sowing depth is controlled by the handle angle: raising the handle sows shallower, lowering it sows deeper.
- After sowing, lightly tamp the bed with a mesh roller to firm seeds and create a smooth surface that facilitates close cutting at harvest.
- Use the tooth marks left by seeder wheels as guides for lining up subsequent passes. The four-row model's 2¼-inch spacing aligns wheel marks at 2⅝ inches beyond the outside rows.

### Maintenance and Storage

- Empty seed from all hoppers into a 9-inch pan and return to seed packets.
- Return brushes to a loose setting before storing to prevent deformation.
- Store in a dry location to prevent corrosion.
- In cold, moist conditions, warm and dry the seeder in front of a heater before use to prevent seed sticking from condensation. A form-fitting Plexiglas cover helps in greenhouses where condensation drips from the roof.

## The Six-Row Seeder

The six-row seeder is a pushed (rather than pulled) upgrade incorporating several improvements:

### Key Design Features

- **Integrated rollers**: Small mesh rollers mounted front and rear eliminate the need for separate bed-rolling passes. The front roller levels soil; the rear roller drives the seed axle and presses seeds into the soil.
- **Drive pulley system**: Three different drive pulleys with a belt allow seed delivery at one per inch, one every two inches, or one every four inches.
- **Six rows**: A 30-inch-wide bed can be planted in two passes (one down, one back).
- **Seed-depth adjustment**: A screw knob on the front roller allows precise depth control.

### Efficiency Gains

The six-row seeder reduces the workflow from three passes (roll, seed, roll) with the four-row model to two passes (seed with integrated rolling), significantly speeding up greenhouse bed preparation and sowing.

## Yield Impact

Coleman credits precision seeders with practically doubling yields per square foot compared to previous methods. The 2¼-inch row spacing for most greenhouse salad and root crops (with wider 4½-inch spacing for spinach, claytonia, and turnips using every other hopper) maximizes use of valuable greenhouse space. These relatively inexpensive seeders compare favorably with European multi-row greenhouse seeders costing ten to fifteen times more.
