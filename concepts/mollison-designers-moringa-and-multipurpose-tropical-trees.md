## Multipurpose Functions

Although the Manual's references to moringa are brief, the tree's
inclusion across multiple sections indicates its importance in permaculture
design:

1. **Food**: The leaves are highly nutritious, containing significant
   protein, vitamins A and C, calcium, and iron. Young pods (drumsticks)
   are edible as vegetables. Seeds can be pressed for oil.
2. **Fodder**: Leaves serve as high-protein fodder for livestock
   including cattle, goats, and poultry.
3. **Water purification**: Moringa seeds contain natural coagulants that
   can clarify turbid water, a traditional practice in many tropical
   regions.
4. **Soil improvement**: Rapid leaf fall contributes to mulch and soil
   building. The deep taproot brings nutrients from subsoil layers.
5. **Windbreak and shade**: Fast growth makes moringa useful as a
   temporary windbreak and shade tree while slower species establish.

## Broader Context of Multipurpose Trees

Mollison consistently advocates for tree species that serve multiple
functions. The discussion of moringa fits within this broader design
philosophy that values:
- Species suited to soils and local conditions
- Species with wide potential for processing and use
- Species that serve present and future essential needs
- Species compatible with the wider polyculture
