## PSK, PSP, and AHCC: Mushroom-Derived Anticancer Compounds

Three major mushroom-derived preparations—PSK (Krestin), PSP (polysaccharide-peptide), and [[ahcc]] (Active Hexose Correlated Compounds)—represent the most clinically advanced mushroom-based cancer therapeutics. This concept examines their chemistry, mechanisms, and clinical profiles.

## PSK ("Krestin") from Trametes versicolor

### Discovery and Development
- Derived from mycelial cultures of Trametes versicolor ([[coriolus-versicolor]])
- A water-soluble **protein-bound polysaccharide**
- Developed and extensively used in Japan
- In 1987, accounted for **25% of total expenditure on anti-cancer agents in Japan**

### Chemical Properties
- Protein-bound polysaccharide
- Molecular weight approximately **100,000 daltons**
- Contains fucose
- Lacks arabinose and rhamnose

### Mechanisms of Action
PSK acts through **both direct and indirect** mechanisms:

#### Direct Action
- **Cytostatic and cytotoxic** effects on tumour cells
- Antiviral activity through stimulation of interferon production

#### Indirect Action (Host-Mediated)
- **Boosts cellular immunity** through multiple immune cell activations
- Restores impaired immune responsiveness in cancer patients
- Enhances the therapeutic effects of radiotherapy and chemotherapy

### Clinical Significance
"When taken with traditional chemotherapy or radiotherapy there have been significant increases in patient longevity." PSK has been approved as a cancer drug in Japan and is covered by national health insurance.

### Market Impact
PSK"s commercial success demonstrated the pharmaceutical viability of mushroom-derived compounds and paved the way for investigation of other mushroom polysaccharides.

## PSP from Trametes versicolor

### Discovery
First isolated in **1983** from cultured deep-layer [[mycelium]] of the **COU-1 strain** of Trametes versicolor (Yang, 1999).

### Chemical Properties
- True proteoglycans—polysaccharide chains are bound to polypeptides
- May contain at least **four discrete molecules**
- Molecular weight approximately **100,000 daltons**
- **Orally bioavailable**
- Rich in aspartic and glutamic amino acids

### Structural Differences from PSK
| Feature | PSK | PSP |
|---------|-----|-----|
| **Fucose** | Present | **Lacking** |
| **Arabinose** | Lacking | **Present** |
| **Rhamnose** | Lacking | **Present** |
| **Saccharide makeup** | Distinct | Distinct |

### Polysaccharide Chain Composition
- True β-glucans with mainly 1-4, 1-2 and 1-3 glucose linkages
- Small amounts of 1-3, 1-4 and 1-6 galactose linkages
- 1-3 and 1-6 mannose linkages
- 1-3 and 1-4 arabinose linkages

### Binding Characteristics
"Sephadex gel chromatography, DEAE-[[cellulose]] column chromatography and HPLC reveal that the polysaccharides and peptides of PSP are clearly bound and not separated. Where there is polysaccharide there is polypeptide."

### Clinical Profile
"PSP is rapidly gaining recognition with many successful human cancer trials." It can be easily delivered by oral route, making it more practical for widespread use than intravenous alternatives.

### Intellectual Property
"Up to now at least 10 kinds of "protein bound' polysaccharides have been isolated, e.g. coriolan I and II—most are covered by US and Japanese patents." However, only PSK and PSP have been used in clinical trials.

## AHCC (Active Hexose Correlated Compounds)

### Composition
A **proprietary extract** prepared from the co-cultivation of several Basidiomycete mushrooms including:
- **Lentinus edodes** (Shiitake)
- **Trametes versicolor** (Turkey Tail)
- **[[schizophyllum-commune]]** (Split Gill)

Grown on **rice** as substrate (Ghoneum et al., 1995).

### Characteristics
- Proprietary blend—exact composition not fully disclosed
- Contains multiple mushroom-derived bioactive compounds
- Active hexose (sugar) correlated compounds as the primary bioactive fraction
- Available as dietary supplement

### Research Status
While AHCC is commercially available and has been studied for immune enhancement, the proprietary nature of the preparation makes independent verification challenging.

## Nomenclature Note
"Japanese and Chinese scientists still prefer to use the **Coriolus** generic name instead of Trametes." This can cause confusion in the literature—both names refer to the same mushroom species.

## Significance for Cancer Treatment

All three preparations work primarily through **immunomodulation** rather than direct cytotoxicity. They enhance the host's own immune defenses to recognize and destroy cancer cells. This mechanism differs fundamentally from conventional chemotherapy and radiation, which directly attack tumour cells but often damage healthy tissue in the process.

The mushroom-derived approach offers:
- **Lower toxicity** than conventional chemotherapy
- **Synergistic effects** when combined with conventional treatments
- **Immune restoration** in immunocompromised cancer patients
- **Oral bioavailability** (especially PSP and AHCC)
