## Magical Illness in Peruvian Amazon Folk Medicine

## Overview

In the Peruvian Amazon urban slum of Belén (Iquitos), disease is understood primarily through a **magical framework**. Rather than asking "how" an illness occurred, residents ask **"why me?"** — seeking to discover exactly why they and not someone else are afflicted. This magical illness framework operates alongside — and often in competition with — twentieth-century medical science, particularly pharmaceutical medicines and store-bought tonics used by slum residents alongside magical treatments.

## The Magical Causality Framework

### When Magic Is Invoked

When a simple fever, pain, or ache does not respond well to an injection, tonic, or other medicine from poorly trained medical aides (*sanitarios*), illness is frequently attributed to the **malice of another person** or to the **punishment of a natural spirit**. Perhaps a taboo has been broken by a person who has offended a spirit of nature. For instance, a menstruating woman who bathes before three days have passed may hemorrhage because of the punishment of an offended forest spirit.

### Two Categories of Illness

Belén residents distinguish between two broad categories:

1. **Simple, God-given illnesses**: Colds, upper-respiratory infections, and some skin disorders — easily treated with penicillin or antibiotic injections from sanitarios
2. **Magical illnesses**: Those that afflict patients whose anxieties, fears, projections of hostility and hatred toward others would propel them toward psychiatric help in Western medicine

Drug-healing in the Peruvian Amazon represents a very old and time-honored tradition of dealing with psychological problems that **predates Freudian analysis by many centuries**.

## Major Categories of Magical Illness

### Susto (Soul Loss)

*Susto* is a common illness found throughout Peru and Latin America, characterized by:

- Profound alteration of metabolism or nervous disorder
- Intense psychic trauma provoked by an emotion of fear
- Lack of appetite and energy
- Caused by the **loss of the sick person"s soul**

Susto is one of the most frequent types of illness treated by Peruvian folk healers (Sal y Rosas, 1958). The concept of soul loss as a disease etiology is widespread across indigenous and mestizo communities throughout the Americas.

### Daño (Magical Harm)

*Daño* arises when people are **envied by others** (*envidia*) or when someone harbors feelings of **vengeance** (*despecho*) toward them. Belén residents recognize the important role that interpersonal strife and stress play in generating such infirmities. *Daño* can manifest as:

- Hemorrhaging
- Muscular pain
- Loss of consciousness
- Suffocation
- Tumors
- Consistent bad luck (*saladera*)

The crucial element is the **cause** of such syndromes, not the actual manifestation of the disease.

#### Mechanisms of Daño

*Daño* is believed to be caused either by:

- A **powerful potion** slipped into a drink or thrown across a doorstep late at night
- A **witch** (*brujo*) paid to cause magical harm by taking ayahuasca for harmful purposes
- **Chonta or thorn** carrying disease-producing substances, shot through the air by witches at their enemies

Some witches as well as ayahuasqueros are believed to control special spirits or familiars whom they can send to do evil or retribute evil magic. As with all magical illness, it is imperative that a sick person seek a healer to **neutralize the harm before it is fatal**.

### Pulsario

*Pulsario* is an infirmity marked by:

- Restlessness and hyperactivity
- Free-floating anxiety
- A painful "ball" located at the mouth of the stomach
- Prevention of normal digestive action

*Pulsario* attacks mainly women, who become irritable and feel generally unhappy. Informants say this lump can also be **repressed pain, sorrow, or anger** — suggesting that the concept maps closely onto what Western psychiatry would classify as anxiety or depressive disorders with somatic manifestations.

### Mal de Ojo (Evil Eye)

*Mal de ojo* is found throughout Peru and is characterized by:

- Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea
- Fever
- Loss of weight
- Insomnia
- Sadness

In popular belief, the cause is the **magical action of one person"s glance upon the other**, not necessarily with evil intentions in mind. However, mal de ojo can be motivated by envy. This illness is quite frequent among **children** whose personal attraction may catch the evil eye. Mothers often place charms or amulets on their children's wrists or necks, or exorcise them daily with tobacco smoke, believing they can ward off this disaster.

## The Social Context of Magical Illness

### Conditions in Belén

The prevalence of magical illness beliefs in Belén must be understood against the backdrop of extreme socioeconomic deprivation:

- Immense overcrowding in a flood-prone slum
- Chronic underemployment
- High levels of malnutrition
- Family breakdown and fragile relationships
- Formal medical consultation far too costly for most residents
- The city hospital's reputation as "a place one goes in order to die," especially because of its casual treatment of poor people

### The Role of the Ayahuasquero

The ayahuasquero plays a central role in healing magical diseases. He must **determine their origin** before any treatment can proceed — bringing to light the cause of the patient's disease without merely focusing upon external symptoms. Once a sick person has found an ayahuasquero, both work closely together to **analyze the visionary content** of the drug experience to determine the agent responsible for the infirmity.

### Witches and Harm

Most ayahuasqueros acknowledge being visited by possible clients who wish not only to be healed but indeed to **cause harm to others**. Some refuse this trade, but others specialize in the use of psychedelic drugs for socially defined evil purposes. They maintain that while intoxicated by the drug, they can **leave their bodies** and inflict harm and even incurable disease upon their clients' enemies.

## Therapeutic Implications

The magical illness framework in the Peruvian Amazon represents a sophisticated system for understanding and treating psychological distress. Several features are notable:

- **Externalization of distress**: By attributing illness to external agents (witches, envy, the evil eye), patients can articulate suffering without stigma
- **Social diagnosis**: The framework naturally encodes interpersonal and social conflicts as disease etiology
- **Community-based healing**: Group ayahuasca sessions address illness within a social context
- **Integration with biomedical treatment**: Magical and rational treatments are used complementarily rather than exclusively

Drug-healing in the Amazon predates Western psychotherapy by centuries and addresses what Western medicine would classify as psychosomatic and anxiety-related disorders through culturally meaningful frameworks that provide both explanation and treatment within a single coherent system.
