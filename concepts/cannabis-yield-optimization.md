# Cannabis Yield Optimization

## Overview

Yield optimization in cannabis cultivation involves maximizing the quantity and quality of harvested material per unit of resources invested. According to Cervantes, a baseline target for indoor growers is 0.5 grams of dried bud every month for each watt of light in the flowering room. Growers who do not achieve this benchmark need to master the basics of growing.

## Yield Per Watt Benchmark

The yield-per-watt metric is the primary standard for evaluating indoor cannabis cultivation efficiency:

- Target: 0.5 grams dried bud per watt of light per month
- This applies to the flowering room specifically
- Growers not meeting this target should focus on fundamental growing skills
- Some strains and methods can exceed this benchmark significantly

## Factors Affecting Yield

### Light

Light is the single most important factor for yield:

- Horizontal lamp orientation yields up to 40 percent more light than vertical
- Uniform light distribution across the canopy increases yield
- Light movers can allow fewer lamps to achieve the same yield
- More plants per square foot can yield more with less space
- 75-watt HID yields enough light for a 2x2-foot garden
- Air-cooled reflectors allow lamps to be placed closer to plants

### Strain Selection

Different strains produce vastly different yields:

- Indica-dominant strains tend to be heavier yielders
- 'Big Bud' is noted for exceptional yields
- Some strains yield several pounds per plant outdoors
- Equatorial sativas may have lower yields due to long flowering period and leggy stature
- Hybrid strains bred for yield combine the best traits of both subspecies

### Plant Management

Several techniques directly affect harvest weight:

- Pruning lower branches can redirect energy to top colas
- Screen of green (SCROG) methods maximize light utilization per square foot
- Sea of green (SOG) enables faster turnover with many small plants
- Removing large fan leaves before harvest speeds drying without affecting quality
- Proper spacing prevents overcrowding and light competition

### Environmental Control

Environmental factors that impact yield:

- Temperature range of 65-75F (18-24C) during flowering
- Humidity control at 45-55 percent during drying
- Adequate CO2 enrichment can boost yields significantly
- Proper ventilation prevents mold and supports healthy growth
- Clean water with appropriate pH and nutrient levels

### Nutrient Management

- Flushing with plain water 10-14 days before harvest improves taste
- Proper NPK balance during flowering supports bud development
- Avoid fertilizer burn, which reduces both yield and quality
- Sodium at low levels appears to bolster yields

## Harvest Timing

Optimal harvest timing preserves maximum yield and potency:

- THC content cannot increase after harvest; it stays the same or decreases
- Trichomes should be monitored starting the sixth week of flowering
- Harvest when 50 percent of trichomes turn creamy translucent for peak effect
- Waiting too long allows THC to degrade, reducing both yield quality and quantity

## Post-Harvest Yield Considerations

Proper post-harvest handling preserves the yield that was grown:

- Slow, even drying over 5-7 days preserves THC content
- Fast drying causes weight loss through uneven moisture distribution
