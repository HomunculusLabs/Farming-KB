# Purple and Color-Changing Cannabis Strains
Colorful cannabis strains have fascinated growers and consumers for decades. The purple, blue,
red, and pink hues that develop in certain varieties are the result of anthocyanin pigments —
the same compounds that produce colors in blueberries, grapes, and autumn leaves. While color
does not directly correlate with potency or quality, these visually striking strains have become
some of the most sought-after and celebrated varieties in cannabis culture.

## The Science of Cannabis Color

Anthocyanins are water-soluble flavonoid pigments that appear in plant tissues in response to
environmental conditions. In cannabis, anthocyanin production is triggered by several factors:
- **Temperature**: Cool nighttime temperatures below 10 degrees Celsius (50 degrees Fahrenheit)
  during late flowering are the primary trigger for purple coloration. The stress of cooler
  temperatures causes the plant to produce more anthocyanins as a protective response.
- **Genetics**: The plant must carry the genes for anthocyanin production; not all strains have
  this genetic potential regardless of growing conditions or environmental manipulation.
- **pH level**: Slightly acidic growing conditions (lower pH) can enhance anthocyanin
  expression and intensify the purple, blue, and red colors.
- **Light intensity**: High light levels, particularly UV exposure, can influence pigment
  development and contribute to more vivid coloration.
- **Photoperiod**: The transition to shorter days during flowering naturally stimulates some
  anthocyanin production as the plant approaches maturity.
The intensity of coloration varies significantly between phenotypes. Even within a single strain
grown under identical conditions, some plants may turn deep purple while others remain
predominantly green, depending on the specific genetic expression of each individual plant. This
variability is one of the reasons that consistently purple strains command such respect.

## Notable Purple and Color-Changing Strains

### Purple Paro Valley

Bred by Mandala Seeds from a Bhutanese landrace sourced from the Paro Valley, Purple Paro
Valley is described as one of the most beautiful cannabis plants in existence. The buds look
like they have been covered in Velcro and rolled in a field of heather, with veins of purple
running through valleys of green — so gorgeous that growers barely dare to touch them.
This incredibly rare strain is suitable only for outdoor cultivation and can withstand both high
humidity and extreme temperature drops with impressive mold resistance. It requires minimal
nutrients and responds very well to Low Stress Training. In hot climates, plants can reach 6 to
7 feet even in containers with very heavy yields.
THC content ranges from 8.7% to 11% with CBD at 0.14%. Despite its delicate, almost fragile
appearance, the strain is extremely potent and will have consumers flying above the clouds
before they know it.

### Purple Haze Thai

ACE Seeds created this 100% sativa variety by crossing Purple Haze with Meo Thai F3 genetics.
This stabilized hybrid of sativas dating to the 1970s offers the Purple Haze characteristics
in their most extreme expression. The plant grows tall and lanky with branching that does
whatever it wants, yet its flexible stems allow it to withstand harsh weather and it enjoys a
remarkable resistance to botrytis.
After the long 16 to 20 week flowering period, the beautiful purple buds deliver a blackberry
bubblegum and liqueur flavor. The yields are average but the quality is phenomenal. The high is
extremely cerebral and massively psychedelic. THC content is approximately 15%.

### Pink Genghis

Malama Aina Seeds of Hawaii crossed Blue Genghis with Genetics Gone Madd's Redd Cross to create
Pink Genghis. The name references the explosive color displays of both parent strains. Balanced
between indica and sativa but expressing more sativa growing characteristics, this compact strain
develops hints of pink, purple, and even blue toward the end of flowering.
The plant feeds heavily without experiencing nutrient burn, so growers are encouraged to push
their boundaries a little and see what the plant can handle. Topping is always recommended, and
the strain loves aerated soils. Organic growing methods really bring out the gorgeous flavor,
described as fresh berries topped with sugar and cream. The high is mind-expanding with enough
body buzz to keep you nicely relaxed.

### Shaman

Dutch Passion's Shaman is a combination of Purple #1 and Skunk genetics dating back to the
1980s. Bred particularly for outdoor and greenhouse cultivation, this strain reaches 5 to 6
feet in height. The 25% indica genetics tame the sativa stretch while the plant retains a
gorgeous, classical appearance that might just be the most beautiful in any garden.
While the breeders claim about 50% of Shaman plants will turn blue, many crops end up dark
purple with barely a touch of green or white. The strain is highly resistant to spider mites and
mold, flowers in about 8 weeks, and produces resin-dripping, colorful buds that will make
growers cry when they pull them down.
THC content is approximately 13.7%. The flavor is typically "purple" with a sharp sweetness, and
the heady, thoughtful, cerebral high is the true draw of this strain. Let this Shaman take you
on a path of discovery around your inner self.

### Purpurea Ticinensis

Owls Production of Switzerland bred this sativa-dominant variety from Oldschool Swiss Sativa
genetics that have been worked with since 1988. The plant was allegedly received from old German
women in a mountain village who had grown it for decades in their garden, making it a true
heirloom variety with deep European roots.
Purple stripes appear on the stems from the vegetative period, and the coloration carries
through to lilac buds that are pale green with bright purple hints. The plant is designed for
high altitude growth and enjoys good mold resistance with a relatively short flowering time.
Indoor flowering takes 5 to 6 weeks, while outdoor crops are ready around early September. THC
content is approximately 15%. The dried buds smell of fruit and chocolate with a strange
vegetable note, and the balanced high is perfect for daytime use without keeping you up at night.

### Terciopelo Lavanda (Lavender Velvet)

Asturjaya Seeds' pure sativa from undisclosed genetics produces large, compact plants with
extensive branching. In colder climates, the buds exhibit the purple color referenced in the
name, and the huge yield makes it seem like the plants are already wearing coats of luxurious
velvet.
Indoor yields are massive, ranging from 750 to 1500 grams per square yard. The aroma is very
floral, deep, and delicious, with sweet and fruity smoke. THC content is approximately 15%.

### Sweet Pink Grapefruit

Alpine Seeds spent 4 years perfecting this inbred SPG line for indoor and greenhouse
cultivation. The sativa-dominant structure has slightly bushier growth thanks to indica
influence. Toward the end of the 65-day flowering period, buds may turn purple or blue, and the
flowers become so resinous they appear to be dreaming.
A long curing period brings out the sweet, tangy grapefruit flavor at its strongest. After the
grapefruit taste subsides, the strong, intense high spreads a relaxing stone through the body
while the brain goes wandering. THC content is approximately 19%.

### Starship

Dynasty Seeds' Starship combines Kali Snapple with Pineapple Kush genetics. The 60-day
flowering period and cold nighttime temperatures bring out bright purple hues in the buds,
making them look almost alien with trichomes so prominent they resemble eyes-on-stalks from
1960s drawings of extraterrestrials.
Despite the burnt rubber and gasoline aroma during growth, the smoke delivers a surprising
sweet, tangy pineapple flavor with a creamy hash aftertaste. THC content is approximately 19%.

### Holy Purple Thai

Resin Seeds' Holy Purple Thai represents another expression of purple Thai genetics, combining
the psychedelic potency of Thai landrace sativas with the visual appeal of anthocyanin-rich
buds. This strain is a testament to the natural color potential inherent in many Southeast
Asian cannabis varieties.

### Haze Plum Purple Passion

This strain combines the Haze lineage's legendary cerebral effects with purple coloration,
demonstrating that color and potency can coexist in a single variety. The complex flavor profile
includes both the spicy, earthy notes of Haze and the fruity sweetness associated with purple
varieties.

### Serious 6

Serious Seeds' African sativa cross produces beautifully resinous buds with purple tinges. This
fast-flowering variety finishes in just 58 days indoors and is remarkably mold-resistant, making
it an excellent choice for cold, humid climates where the temperature drops that trigger purple
coloration occur naturally.

## Cultivation Tips for Maximizing Color

Growers seeking to maximize purple and color expression should consider the following
techniques:
- **Temperature manipulation**: Exposing plants to nighttime temperatures between 10 and 15
  degrees Celsius (50 to 59 degrees Fahrenheit) during the final 2 to 3 weeks of flowering is
  the most effective technique for triggering anthocyanin production
- **Genetic selection**: Choose strains known for anthocyanin production; color cannot be
  induced in plants that lack the genetic potential regardless of environmental manipulation
- **Avoid excessive nutrients**: High nitrogen levels can suppress anthocyanin production, so
  reducing nitrogen in late flowering may help enhance color
- **Organic cultivation**: Many breeders report that organic growing methods enhance color
  expression, possibly due to more balanced nutrient availability
- **Extended flowering**: Allowing plants to flower slightly longer than the minimum
  recommended time can intensify coloration as anthocyanins accumulate in the plant tissues
- **Patience**: The most dramatic color changes typically occur in the final week or two
  before harvest, when temperatures naturally drop and the plant redirects energy toward
  pigment production

## Color vs. Quality

It is important to note that colorful cannabis is not inherently more potent or better-tasting
than green cannabis. Anthocyanin production is an aesthetic trait governed by specific genes and
environmental triggers, independent of cannabinoid and terpene production. A green plant and a
purple plant of the same strain can have identical THC levels and flavor profiles.
However, many purple strains do happen to be exceptional varieties — not because of their color,
but because the breeders who developed them selected for overall quality alongside visual appeal.
The best purple strains combine stunning aesthetics with genuine potency and complex flavors.
