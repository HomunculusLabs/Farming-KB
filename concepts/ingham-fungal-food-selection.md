# Ingham — Fungal Food Selection for Compost Tea

Updated: 2026-04-18

The foods added to compost tea during brewing determine whether bacteria or fungi will dominate the final product. Dr. [[aact-fungal-dominant-tea]].

## The Fungal Food Gradient

Ingham ranks common food resources by their ability to grow fungi, from least to most effective:

| Food Source | [[ingham-paper-cardboard-fungal-foods-compost]] but is primarily bacterial |
| Malt | Good | More structurally complex sugars favor fungal growth |
| Soy sauce | Highest (listed) | Complex proteins and amino acids support fungi well |
| Humic acids | Very high | Long-chain carbon molecules are ideal fungal foods |
| Kelp | High | Complex carbohydrates support both bacteria and fungi |

The pattern is clear: the more structurally complex the carbon source, the better it supports fungi. Simple sugars are immediately available and favor the rapid growth that characterizes bacteria. Complex molecules require enzymatic breakdown that fungi are specifically adapted to perform.

## Why Complexity Matters

Bacteria and fungi differ fundamentally in how they obtain nutrients:

- **Bacteria**: Absorb simple, dissolved molecules directly through their cell walls. They thrive on simple sugars, amino acids, and mineral nutrients in solution. They grow fast but cannot access complex polymers.

- **Fungi**: Secrete extracellular enzymes that [[comparison-fish-hydrolysate-vs-fish-emulsion]]), bacteria cannot fully utilize them, leaving food resources available for fungi.

## Bacterial Foods

For situations requiring bacterial dominance -- which is 75% of agricultural soils according to Ingham -- the following foods are appropriate:

### Molasses

The most common bacterial food for compost tea. Ingham notes that molasses has "some fungal foods, but less fungal food than soy sauce." It remains the standard choice for bacterial teas because it:

- Dissolves easily in water for rapid bacterial access
- Supports explosive bacterial growth within hours
- Is inexpensive and widely available
- Contains trace minerals
- Should be added at approximately 1 tablespoon per gallon

### Simple Sugars

White and brown sugars provide the most readily available carbon for bacteria. Use these when:

- Maximum bacterial biomass is needed quickly
- Brewing time is short
- The target crop prefers bacterial soil (most annual vegetables, grasses, brassicas)

For crops requiring fungal dominance -- perennials, trees, shrubs, and many ornamentals -- fungal foods are essential.

## Reducing Molasses to Increase Fungi

Ingham describes a practical observation: fungal numbers keep rising as molasses is reduced from the brew. This is because:

- Less molasses means less bacterial growth consuming all available oxygen and food
- Bacteria that do grow have less competitive advantage
- Fungi can access the remaining complex food resources
- The F:B ratio shifts toward fungal dominance

This does not mean molasses is bad -- it means molasses determines the bacterial ceiling. To increase fungi, you must simultaneously reduce bacterial food AND add fungal food.
