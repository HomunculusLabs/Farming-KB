# Cancer Prevention Through Dietary Mushroom Intake

## Overview

An accumulating body of evidence from epidemiological studies and animal
research suggests that regular dietary intake of certain mushrooms may
reduce cancer risk. This concept positions mushrooms not merely as
therapeutic agents for existing cancer but as preventive functional foods
that can be incorporated into the daily diet for long-term health
protection.

## The Chemoprevention Concept

Cancer chemoprevention may be considered as the prevention of cancer in
human populations by ingestion of chemical agents that prevent
carcinogenesis. It is important to differentiate this concept from
primary cancer prevention (such as cessation of cigarette smoking) and
cancer chemotherapy (therapy used after diagnosis).

In most Western countries, cancer incidence increases gradually after
age 30 and is greatest for the age group between 70 and 80. The
occurrence of cancer is strongly associated with ageing, and a
preneoplastic condition could have already started in the cells many
years earlier. Consequently, an important strategy for preventing
certain cancers could be to inhibit the development of the first clonal
expansions and delay the clinical onset of tumour development.

## Epidemiological Evidence

Certain Japanese growers of [[medicinal-mushrooms]] who have been regular
consumers of their produce show a lower cancer incidence compared with
the national incidence of cancer. While this is observational data rather
than a controlled study, it provides a compelling starting point for
investigating the chemopreventive properties of dietary mushrooms.

Evidence is accumulating from human epidemiological studies and animal
studies that dietary factors may reduce the incidence of some cancers,
possibly through as-yet-unknown chemopreventive mechanisms.

## Animal Studies

### Ikekawa's Prevention Study (2001)

Professor Ikekawa demonstrated the preventive effects of the edible
mushroom *Hypsizygus marmoreus*. Control mice were bred on ordinary feed
while treated mice received feed containing 5% dried fruit-bodies of the
mushroom. All mice were injected with methylcholanthrene (a strong
carcinogen). After 76 weeks:

- 21 of 36 control mice developed tumours
- Only 3 of 36 treated mice developed tumours

This 86% reduction in tumour incidence confirmed previous studies with
[[lentinula-edodes]] (shiitake) and [[pleurotus-ostreatus]] (oyster
mushroom).

### Shon and Nam Skin Carcinogenesis Study

Using the mouse skin carcinogenesis model, polysaccharides derived from
*Phellinus igniarius* and *Agrocybe cylindracea* were applied topically
to TPA-induced tumour promotion in mouse epidermis previously initiated
with DMBA. Results showed:

- *P. igniarius*: 69.7% inhibition in the number of skin tumours per
  mouse, 70.0% reduction in mice with tumours
- *A. cylindracea*: 88.2% inhibition in the number of skin tumours per
  mouse, 30.0% reduction in mice with tumours

## Mushrooms as Functional Foods

While mushroom-derived polysaccharide anticancer compounds will be used
at various levels of purity in clinical applications as adjuncts to
chemotherapeutic compounds, another important role could be as
[[functional-foods-medicinal-mushrooms]] -- either consumed whole, or
as concentrated extracts, as dietary supplements.

There is now increasing evidence with experimental animals that regular
feeding of powdered [[medicinal-mushrooms]] can have a cancer prevention
effect, demonstrating both high antitumour activity and restriction of
tumour metastasis.

## The First Truly Functional Foods

The abundance of scientific evidence confirms the role of certain
mushrooms as valuable functional foods and dietary supplements in the
human diet. The very considerable historical evidence with edible
medicinal mushrooms would strongly suggest that they are the very first
truly functional foods.

It is perhaps unlikely that there will be a major increase in fresh or
processed [[medicinal-mushrooms]] consumption in Western countries in
line with Asian culinary practice. The imbalance could be reduced by
consuming medicinal mushrooms as dietary supplements in the form of
capsules or elixirs.

## Key Chemopreventive Mushroom Species

Several mushroom species have accumulated substantial evidence for
cancer-preventive properties through dietary intake.

### Lentinula edodes (Shiitake)

[[lentinula-edodes]] is the most studied mushroom in cancer prevention
research. Regular dietary consumption of shiitake has been associated
with reduced cancer incidence in Japanese population studies. The key
compound lentinan, a beta-1,6-glucan with beta-1,3 branches, activates
macrophages and natural killer cells. Shiitake also contains lentinula
edodes mycelia extract (LEM) and lentination, both of which have
demonstrated immunomodulatory and antitumour activity. Regular
consumption of whole shiitake mushrooms provides these compounds in a
food matrix that may enhance bioavailability compared with isolated
extracts.

### Agaricus blazei (Himematsutake)

[[agaricus-blazei]] has attracted particular interest for cancer
prevention following epidemiological observations in a Brazilian
community where regular consumers showed unusually low cancer rates.
The mushroom contains high concentrations of beta-glucans with potent
immunomodulatory activity. Animal studies have confirmed that dietary
inclusion of [[agaricus-blazei]] powder reduces tumour incidence and
growth across multiple carcinogenesis models. The mushroom is now widely
consumed as a dietary supplement in Japan and other Asian countries.

### Pleurotus ostreatus (Oyster Mushroom)

[[pleurotus-ostreatus]] provides a combination of beta-glucans, ergosterol
(a provitamin D2 precursor), and antioxidant phenolic compounds. Its
chemopreventive effects have been demonstrated in animal models of
chemically induced carcinogenesis. As one of the most widely cultivated
mushrooms globally, oyster mushroom is readily available and affordable,
making it a practical choice for regular dietary cancer prevention.

effects of dietary mushrooms. Understanding these mechanisms helps
explain why regular consumption is more effective than sporadic intake.
