## The Scale of Insect Damage

Worldwide, termite species number at least 2,500, of which about 150 species can attack buildings, but within those 150 species, only a few cause widespread economic losses. There are nearly 600 species of carpenter ants, of which about a dozen attack buildings. Carpenter ants are nature's way of recycling wood. Aboveground colonies of Formosan termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*) can contain 500,000 to 3.5 million individuals; this species does not need to forage outside the infested structure.

The damage from native subterranean termites in the United States alone exceeds $10 billion per year, while damage from the imported Formosan termite exceeds $1 billion annually. Whole neighborhoods and even entire cities such as New Orleans are being consumed. That city loses $300 million per year to termites, with forecasts of greater damage as colonies penetrate deeper into the city's wooden infrastructure. On an individual level, the threat of losing one's house drives homeowners to spend whatever is necessary to prevent insect damage.

The environmental persistence and inherent toxicity of conventional pesticides have only recently been recognized. The market value of the pesticide industry in the United States has swollen to $9 billion; of that, 5 percent or $450 million is from biopesticides. The biopesticide market share is increasing 15 percent per year.

Termites contacting the mycelium then recruited others, creating a compounding recruitment effect. In one alarming example, fire ant workers enthroned the queen on a bed of mycelium, where she expired along with her subjects.

4. **Delayed sporulation**: Once the mycelium is distributed throughout the colony, it eventually sporulates, completing the fungal life cycle and producing new spores for future infections.

The active constituents causing attraction, feeding, dispersion, recruitment, and eventual death have not yet been fully molecularly characterized. They may be suites of synergistic compounds working in concert rather than a single molecular compound.

## Texas A&M University Research Trials

Stamets initiated research trials at Texas A&M University's Entomology Department under Dr. Roger Gold. These trials demonstrated that:
- **Formosan termites** (*Coptotermes formosanus*) were attracted to the mycelium, fed upon it, and carried fragments back to the nest
- **Eastern subterranean termites** (*Reticulitermes flavipes*) showed the same behavior
- **Fire ants** (*Solenopsis invicta*) were attracted and infected
- Death occurred in 2 to 3 weeks from fungal infection

With termites, the mycelium appears to kill the large protozoa in their digestive tract, which thwarts their ability to metabolize cellulose and effectively starves them. The insects then sicken and become susceptible to infection both internally and externally.
