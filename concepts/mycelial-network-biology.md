# Mycelial Network Biology Mycelium is the vegetative body of a fungus — a vast network of branching, thread-like **hyphae** that colonize substrates, decompose organic matter, absorb nutrients, and serve as the biological foundation for mushroom cultivation. A single mycelial network can cover enormous areas and persist for centuries, making it one of the largest and oldest living organisms on Earth. ## Hyphal Structure and Growth Individual **hyphae** are tubular cells typically 1-10 μm in diameter, growing by extension at the **apex** (tip). Key structural features: - **Apical vesicle cluster** (Spitzenkörper) — concentrated vesicles at the growing tip that deliver cell wall materials and enzymes - **Septa** — cross-walls dividing hyphae into compartments; pores in septa allow cytoplasmic streaming between cells - **Cell wall composition** — primarily chitin, β-glucans, and glycoproteins; provides rigidity and protection - **Dikaryotic stage** — many cultivated mushrooms maintain two genetically distinct nuclei per cell (n + n), fusing only during fruiting Growth occurs via **tip extension** at rates of 0.1-1.0 mm/hour depending on species, temperature, and nutrient availability. Branching creates a dense mycelial mat that maximizes surface area for nutrient absorption. ## Rhizomorphic vs. Cottony Mycelium Mycelial morphology varies significantly and has practical implications for cultivation: - **Rhizomorphic (linear) mycelium** — organized, rope-like strands that grow rapidly and colonize aggressively; preferred for cultivation as it indicates vigor and often correlates with higher yields - **Cottony (tomentose) mycelium** — fluffy, aerial growth that is slower to colonize; may indicate suboptimal genetics, poor nutrition, or contamination susceptibility Strain selection for rhizomorphic growth is a key consideration in commercial [[oyster-mushrooms]]) can decompose both cellulose and
lignin, while **brown-rot fungi** primarily target cellulose. This capability
makes certain mushroom species valuable for **mycoremediation** — using fungi
to degrade environmental pollutants including petroleum hydrocarbons, PAHs,
and even some pesticides.

## Nutrient Transport and Cytoplasmic Streaming

Mycelium moves nutrients, water, and signaling molecules across its network
through **cytoplasmic streaming** — the [[diffusion-osmosis-and-active-transport-in-plants]] of cytoplasm within
hyphae. This allows:

- Reallocation of resources from nutrient-rich zones to growing fronts
- Rapid distribution of enzymes and metabolites
- Communication between distant parts of the network
- Phenomena like **translocation** where nutrients from decomposing material in
  one location support growth elsewhere

## Mycorrhizal Networks

Many forest mushrooms form **mycorrhizal associations** with tree roots,
creating symbiotic relationships:

- The fungal partner provides mineral nutrients (phosphorus, nitrogen) and
  water to the tree
- The tree provides carbohydrates (sugars) produced through photosynthesis
- These networks can interconnect multiple trees, facilitating **resource
  sharing** between individuals
