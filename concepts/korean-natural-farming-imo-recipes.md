# Korean Natural Farming IMO Recipes

Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO) are the foundation of Korean Natural Farming (KNF). Master Han-Kyu Cho developed a 5-stage process to collect, multiply, and apply native soil microorganisms. IMO restores soil biology, decomposes organic matter, and suppresses disease — all from materials found on any farm.

## IMO #1: Collection

### Purpose
- Collect naturally occurring microorganisms from your local environment
- Local organisms are adapted to your climate and soil — more effective than purchased products

### Method
1. Cook short-grain rice until slightly overcooked (sticky)
2. Fill a wooden lunch box or bamboo basket with the cooked rice (1-2 inches deep)
3. Don't use metal containers (metal inhibits microbial growth)
4. Place the box in a location with diverse vegetation:
   - Under bamboo grove (preferred — high microbial diversity)
   - Near a forest edge
   - Under leaf mold
   - In a field with diverse plants
5. Cover loosely with breathable material (cheesecloth, newspaper)
6. Place on the ground, elevated slightly (on rocks or branches)
7. Wait 4-10 days (depending on temperature)
8. Done when rice is covered with white fungal mycelium (cottony growth)
9. Discard if: black/green/blue mold appears (wrong organisms, try different location)

## IMO #2: Multiplication

### Purpose
- Multiply the collected microorganisms using sugar as food
- Preserve them in a stable form

### Method
1. Take the rice with white mycelium (IMO #1)
2. Mix 1:1 by weight with brown sugar (unrefined preferred)
3. Pack into a clay jar (onggi) — fill to 2/3 (leave room for gas)
4. Cover with breathable cloth
5. Keep in cool, dark place at 23-25°C (73-77°F)
6. Ferment for 5-7 days
7. Liquid will separate to the top — this is the active extract
8. Done when it smells sweet-sour (like fermenting fruit)

## IMO #3: Expansion on Grain

### Purpose
- Expand the culture onto a bulk carrier for field application

### Method
1. Mix rice bran or wheat bran to 65-70% moisture (squeeze test: ball holds shape, no water drips)
2. Mix IMO #2 liquid into the bran at 1:10 ratio (IMO to bran by weight)
3. Build a pile 35-40 cm (14-16 inches) high on a clean floor
4. Cover with breathable material (burlap, straw)
5. Monitor temperature: should reach 40-50°C (104-122°F)
6. Turn the pile when temperature reaches 50°C (prevents overheating)
7. Ferment for 5-7 days
8. Done when the pile is covered with white mycelium and smells sweet/earthy

## IMO #4: Soil Integration

### Purpose
- Introduce IMO into the farm's soil ecosystem

### Method
1. Mix IMO #3 with equal parts good quality soil (1:1 ratio)
2. Add small amounts of other KNF inputs:
   - Oriental Herbal Nutrient (OHN): 1-2% by volume
   - Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ): 1-2%
   - Fish Amino Acid (FAA): 1%
   - Brine Water or Sea Water: 1%
3. Form into baseball-sized balls
4. Place in shade, cover with breathable material
5. Ferment for 4-5 days
6. White mycelium should cover the surface

## IMO #5: Field Application

### Purpose
- Distribute IMO throughout the farm soil

### Method
1. Mix IMO #4 with finished compost at 1:10 ratio
2. Apply to fields at 100-200 kg per 1,000 sq meters
3. Apply 1-2 weeks before planting
4. Can also be applied as a top-dressing during the growing season
5. Can be dissolved in water (1:500 dilution) for foliar spray or soil drench

## Quality Indicators
- **Good IMO**: Sweet, sour smell. White fungal mycelium visible. No foul odors
- **Bad IMO**: Rotten, putrid, ammonia smell. Green/black mold. Discard and start over

## Storage
- IMO #2: Can be stored in cool, dark place for several months
- IMO #3: Use within 2-3 weeks (active, perishable)
- IMO #4: Use within 2-3 weeks
- IMO #5 in compost: Stable once mixed into compost
