# Excavation Equipment — Buckets, Augers, and [[holzer-water-power-hydraulic-ram|hydraulic-conductivity|Hydraulic]] Jets

Beyond the primary blade machines, a range of specialized excavation [[excavation-tools-and-drilling-methods|tools]] extends the earthworker's capacity for precise shaping, deep excavation, and work in challenging terrain. Each tool type addresses specific conditions that blade machines cannot handle effectively.

## Bucket Machines

Buckets come in simple fixed forms and complex swivelling variants, each suited to different tasks.

### Loading Buckets

Simple, fixed buckets often fitted to wheeled tractors. Of great use in:

- **Quarries** — loading loose rock and aggregate
- **Nurseries** — moving soil, mulch, and potting media
- **General material handling** — picking up and loading loose material to trucks at heights up to 4 m

Loading buckets can lift and tip but cannot swivel sideways, limiting their use to straightforward load-and-carry operations.

### Swivel and Scoop Buckets

A very useful tool for drains and for work in marshes. These machines can:

- **Scoop out** material from the working face
- **Swivel around** to deposit loads without repositioning the machine
- **Dig wells** to 9 m (30 feet) using hydraulic extension arms
- **Dig sharp-edged pits or drains** for special uses
- **Reach out 6-10 m** (20-32 feet) from the machine's position
- **Work from dry land** to take silt from canals or ponds

### The Dragline

For reaching distances beyond the capacity of hydraulic arm machines, two tools extend reach:

- **Crane dragline** — a giant fishing rod or crane that casts out a loose or tractor-tethered bucket dredge up to 18-24 m (60-80 feet) and hauls it in full
- **Two-tractor dragline** — one tractor hauls an endless rope with a bucket across greater distances than the crane dragline can achieve

### Animal-Drawn Scoops

Scoops are often used behind horses or oxen to excavate small ponds or clean out ponds of silt. Unless fitted with a rear spill-door or chain tipper, they are very hard work to tip by hand and tire the operator quickly. This traditional technology remains practical where fuel machines are unavailable or impractical.

## The Spinner

A special tool of great efficiency in flatlands and on low slopes. The spinner consists of a large wheel with small peripheral buckets spun at high speed, which throws earth out as small clods over a considerable distance.

### Key Characteristics

- **Most economical and speedy machine** for flatland (shallow) drains
- **Distributes spoil as small clods** as it travels — no banks form beside spinner drains
- Much used in the Netherlands to drain polders
- The even distribution of spoil eliminates the need for secondary spreading operations

## Trench Diggers

A special **chain-bucket system** that continuously digs narrow trenches. Commercially known as "Ditch-Witch®" or "Trench-Wench®." Primary uses include:

- **Pipe-laying** — narrow, clean trenches for water, gas, or electrical conduits
- **Foundation insulation** — trenches for damp-proofing or thermal insulation around building foundations
- Continuous operation makes them far faster than periodic excavation for long linear trenches

## Augers and Drills

Augers and drills are hand or mechanical post-hole diggers suited to most soils, with applications extending beyond simple fence post installation.

### Augers

- **Hand or mechanical** operation
- Uses include **tree and tuber planting**, though care must be taken not to compact soils or create water-filled holes in clay
- **Limited lift** — normally operated to approximately 2 m (6 feet) depth
- Prime use remains posts and fencing

### Drills

- **Extension tubes** allow operation to great depths
- Borings removed by liquid flushing or compacted and tube-lined
- **Purely hydraulic drills** work to very great depths
- Bores and hydraulic nozzles are often combined for deep drilling

### Large-Bore Machines

Some of the largest vertical and horizontal earth holes (mines and qanats) were once hand-cut by primitive tools. Wells to 30 or even 60 m (100-200 feet) are still hand-dug throughout the world. Modern large-bore technology includes:

- **Large-bore augers to 11 m** used at mines, most carrying extensions to make a 2 m wide shaft
- **Pneumatic blowers** crane-mounted to remove loose materials, often locally made (e.g., Coober Pedy opal mines in Australia)
- **Caldwells** — large-bore drills (named after the inventor) used for underground housing, excavation, shafts, and tunnels up to 1.5 m diameter

## Explosives in Earth Moving

Explosives serve specialized purposes in earthworks:

- **Assisting auger holes** in hard ground
- **Making holes in unstable ground** such as marshes
- **Loosening rock** in quarries

With the advent of the swamp tractor, marsh blasting is less common, but the basic ease and effectiveness of explosives should not be overlooked for otherwise intractable problems.

### Types and Materials

- **Nitroglycerine-based** — gelignite, dynamite, or plastique (cellulose treated with acid in glycerine, absorbed into wood dust or clay earths)
- **[[plant-available-nitrogen-ammonium-nitrate]] and dieseline** mix — known in the trade as "chickenshit" — an inexpensive option
- Nitrates have from ancient times been gathered from manures, toilet pit soils, or extracted from guano

### Safety Considerations

Today, a "powder monkey" certificate is required to set explosives independently, or skilled professionals should be hired. The unpredictable effects of improvised explosives make professional handling essential.

## Hydraulic Jets

The quiet but powerful action of water jets assists earthworks in several ways:

### Applications

1. **Water bore drilling (water spear)** — a simple jet nozzle fixed inside a pipe and connected to a garden tap can drill a bore in sands, gravels, or deep soils in minutes to hours
2. **Jetty piling** — an old hose-pipe fastened to a sharpened pile or post and connected to a hose will sink pilings in silt or sand rapidly
3. **Dam silt removal** — a jet pump removes silt, sediment, mulch, and even large gravel from a dam, depositing these behind a retaining wall as rich terrace soil while water flows back to the dam
4. **Gravel and soil washing** — jets fired at loose sediment wash down gravels, sands, and soils for mining, [[holzer-terrace-construction-guide]], or removal of land slips from roads and fields

### Hydraulic Nozzles and Systems

- Hydraulic nozzles, "drills," and jet pumps are manufactured by industry for these purposes
- Used to **steer deep drills** in boreholes to specific locations underground for tapping oil, [[phase-change-materials-thermal-energy-storage]], or venting mines and caves
- A jet plus a cutting drill will cool the area and remove waste in one operation

### Safety Warning

Inadvertent jet drilling has a powerful pulling action. Jet pumps with their motors, rafts, or platforms can be dragged below water or across the ground if left unattended. A garden hose will bury itself in sandy soils and be lost if left running uncontrolled.

### Pneumatic Systems

Powerful air jets take gravels from mines using similar principles. A whole series of turbines and powerful pistons operate from pressure applied by water, air, or oil — the sciences of applied hydraulics and pneumatics.

## Finishing Tools

### Rakes

Rakes are used for **fine levelling** of excavated earth surfaces, creating the smooth, even grades needed for final surfaces.
