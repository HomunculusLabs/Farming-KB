## Fungal Endophytes in Agricultural Crops

Fungal endophytes -- fungi that live within plant tissues without causing
visible disease symptoms -- represent a vast and largely untapped resource
for improving agricultural productivity, stress tolerance, and
sustainability. These symbiotic fungi colonize the intercellular spaces
and even intracellular compartments of aboveground and belowground plant
tissues, forming cryptic associations with virtually all plant species
studied to date.

## Types of Fungal Endophytes in Crops

  water-use efficiency, deeper rooting, and maintenance of growth under
  water-limited conditions. The endophyte may modulate host hormone
  levels (particularly abscisic acid) and osmotic adjustment.

- **Herbivore deterrence**: Clavicipitaceous endophytes in forage
  grasses produce alkaloids (ergot alkaloids, lolines, peramine) that
  are toxic or deterrent to insect herbivores and grazing mammals.
- **Heat tolerance**: Some endophytes confer thermotolerance to their
  hosts, an increasingly important trait under climate change scenarios.
- **Salinity tolerance**: Root endophytes including *Piriformospora
  indica* have been shown to improve plant growth under saline
  conditions, potentially through modulation of ion uptake and

  the plant's immune system, enabling faster and stronger responses to
  subsequent pathogen attack without the growth costs of constitutive
  defense activation.
