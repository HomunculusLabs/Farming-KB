# Psilocybin and Psilocin Chemical Synthesis

Psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) and psilocybin (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) are the principal psychoactive constituents of Psilocybe "magic mushrooms." Both compounds closely resemble the neurotransmitter serotonin, and their hallucinogenic effects are likely caused by interference with normal brain serotonin actions, possibly through a similar mechanism to LSD. The concise large-scale synthesis developed by Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) at Japan's National Institute of Health Sciences represents a significant improvement over prior routes, achieving gram-scale production of both compounds without any chromatographic purification.

## Structural Background

Psilocin (compound 1) is a simple tryptamine derivative with a hydroxyl group at the 4-position of the indole ring and dimethyl substitution on the amine nitrogen. Its molecular formula is C12H16N2O with a molecular weight of 204.27 g/mol. Psilocybin (compound 2) is the 4-O-phosphate ester of psilocin, making it a prodrug that is dephosphorylated in vivo to the active psilocin. Its molecular formula is C12H17N2O4P with a molecular weight of 284.25 g/mol.

The structural similarity to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) is notable: both share the indole-ethylamine core, differing primarily in the position of the hydroxyl group (4-position for psilocybin/psilocin versus 5-position for serotonin). Minor constituents of magic mushrooms include baeocystin (4-hydroxy-N-methyltryptamine) and norbaeocystin (4-hydroxytryptamine), which are often present in smaller amounts.

## Challenges in Synthesis

Several practical challenges motivated the development of improved synthetic routes:

- **Psilocin instability**: Psilocin decomposes readily, making isolation from mushroom material on a gram scale impractical for use as an analytical standard compound.
- **Psilocybin polarity**: The high polarity of the phosphate ester makes chromatographic isolation difficult, often requiring specialized anion-exchange resins.
- **Need for standards**: Regulatory identification of magic mushrooms using morphological and microscopic analysis is quite difficult without experts, so chromatographic methods (TLC, GC, HPLC) requiring pure standards are typically employed for forensic work.
- **Prior low yields**: Previous syntheses of psilocybin reported yields of only 20% (Hofmann et al., 1958) and 47% (Nichols and Frescas, 1999), making large-scale production impractical.
- **Regulatory urgency**: Since June 6, 2002, fungi containing psilocin and psilocybin have been regulated by the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law in Japan, increasing demand for analytical standards.

## Psilocin Synthesis Route

The Shirota synthesis of psilocin proceeds from commercially available 4-hydroxyindole (compound 3) in three steps with no chromatographic purification required at any stage:

### Step 1: Acetylation (3 to 4)

4-Hydroxyindole (available in quantities greater than 25 g per bottle, over 185 mmol) is treated with acetic anhydride (20 mL, 246 mmol) in pyridine (20 mL) within anhydrous dichloromethane (200 mL), cooled from 0 degrees C to room temperature. After stirring for 2 hours, the reaction is worked up with water, concentrated, and the product crystallized from ethyl acetate. 4-Acetylindole (compound 4) forms as ivory white crystals (34 g constant yield) with a 1H NMR spectrum showing characteristic signals at delta 8.27 (br s, H-1), 7.22 (d, H-7), and 6.41 (m, H-3).

A benzyl ether protection route was also explored but had disadvantages: a separate deprotection step was needed, the 4-O-benzyl derivative was somewhat unstable without careful reaction condition control, and it was not isolable in crystal form. The acetylation approach proved more practical for large-scale work.

### Step 2: Acylation-Amidation (4 to 6)

4-Acetylindole (17.6 g, 100 mmol) is treated with oxalyl chloride (13 mL, 146 mmol) in cold anhydrous diethyl ether (100 mL) with an ice bath. After 15 minutes, n-hexane (200 mL) is added and the mixture stored in a freezer overnight. The resulting yellow crystals of the 3-glyoxalyl chloride intermediate (compound 5) are filtered and dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran (100 mL).

Dimethylamine (60 mL of 2M solution in THF, 120 mmol) and pyridine (10 mL, 123 mmol) are added over 15 minutes with ice-bath cooling. The 3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-acetylindole (compound 6) precipitates as ivory white crystalline powder (22.0 g, 80.0% yield). Key NMR features include two N-methyl singlets at delta 3.02 and 2.92 ppm and the glyoxalyl carbonyl at delta 185.4 ppm.

### Step 3: Reduction (6 to 1)

The glyoxalylamide intermediate (22.0 g, 80 mmol) is reduced with lithium aluminum hydride (approximately 12 g) in anhydrous THF (250 mL, plus 300 mL for addition) under argon atmosphere. Addition occurs dropwise over 2 hours, followed by 2 hours of reflux. After cooling, careful quenching with anhydrous Na2SO4 powder (approximately 10 g) and saturated Na2SO4 solution is performed.

Psilocin crystallizes as white crystals (14.3 g, 87.5% yield) with a melting point of 169 to 174 degrees C (decomposition), matching literature values of 173 to 176 degrees C. The product is collected by filtration and washed with ethanol, requiring no chromatography.

## Psilocybin Synthesis Route

The conversion of psilocin to psilocybin proved more challenging than the psilocin synthesis itself. Several phosphorylation methodologies were screened:

- **Phosphoramidite method** (using phosphite, I2, and DMAP): Did not consume the starting psilocin at all.
- **Phosphoryl iodide method** (using tribenzyl phosphite): Showed some promise but was not selected.
- **Pyrophosphate method** (using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-BuLi): Selected for easy handling and reagent stability. The tetrabenzylpyrophosphate was prepared in almost 100% yield from dibenzyl phosphate using a modified literature procedure.

### Key Discovery: The Zwitterionic Intermediate

When the pyrophosphate reaction was run on psilocin (5.4 g, 26.4 mmol) at -78 degrees C in THF, TLC showed the starting material was consumed and a new spot appeared. After aqueous workup, however, an additional spot at the origin was observed — a whitish material insoluble in dichloromethane. Nichols and Frescas had previously observed a similar phenomenon, concluding that hydrolytic cleavage of one O-benzyl group had occurred.

Purification by preparative reversed-phase HPLC afforded a single compound (8), which was fully characterized by 2D NMR:

- **1H NMR (CD3OD, 400 MHz)**: Two sets of benzyl signals — one shifted to high field at delta 4.56 (2H, s, NCH2C6H5) compared to the other at delta 4.98 and 4.96 (each 1H, s, OCH2C6H5). Proton signals of the N,N-dimethyl part shifted to low field compared to psilocin itself.
- **13C NMR (CD3OD, 100 MHz)**: Characteristic signals at delta 69.2 (NCH2C6H5) and 69.1 (OCH2C6H5) for the benzyl methylenes.
- **31P NMR (CD3OD, 162 MHz)**: delta -5.45 ppm confirming the phosphate moiety.
- **HMBC**: Showed one benzyl group directly linked at the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethyl part, forming a quaternary ammonium ion.
- **NOESY**: Supported the spatial relationships proposed by the zwitterionic structure.

The compound was identified as a zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative, formed by intramolecular migration of one benzyl group from the phosphate oxygen to the dimethylammonium nitrogen during the workup.

### Practical Significance of the Zwitterion

The zwitterionic nature of compound 8 made large-scale isolation trivially simple. By suspending the worked-up reaction mixture in dichloromethane overnight, the zwitterion precipitated while excess dibenzyl phosphate remained soluble. The precipitate was collected by filtration and washed with dichloromethane, yielding 10.5 g (85.2%) of compound 8 as a white powder. This completely eliminated the need for chromatography, which had been the major practical obstacle in previous psilocybin syntheses.

### Final Deprotection (8 to 2)

Catalytic hydrogenolysis using 10% palladium on carbon (approximately 1 g) in methanol (225 mL) under a hydrogen atmosphere at room temperature removed both benzyl groups. After 2 hours, water was added due to product deposition, and stirring continued for one additional hour. The reaction was filtered through Celite and concentrated to yield psilocybin as a white needle crystalline powder (5.6 g, 87.5%) with a melting point of 190 to 198 degrees C, consistent with literature values of 185 to 195 degrees C.

## Historical Context

The first isolation and structural elucidation of psilocybin was reported by Hofmann, Heim, Kobel, and colleagues at Sandoz in 1958 to 1959. The first total synthesis was achieved by Troxler, Seemann, and Hofmann (1959). Subsequent synthetic efforts by Ono and Shimamine (1973), Repke et al. (1981), and Nichols and Frescas (1999) variously improved yields but typically required chromatographic purification steps. The Shirota route represents the first gram-scale synthesis achievable without any chromatographic purification, making it the most practical route for producing analytical standards.

## Comparison with Previous Synthetic Routes

The Shirota synthesis represents a significant practical advance over all previous routes to psilocybin:

| Route | Year | Psilocybin yield | Chromatography required |
|-------|------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Hofmann et al. (Sandoz) | 1959 | ~20% | Yes |
| Troxler, Seemann, Hofmann | 1959 | ~20% | Yes |
| Ono and Shimamine | 1973 | Not reported | Yes |
| Repke, Ferguson, Bates | 1981 | Not reported | Yes |
| Nichols and Frescas | 1999 | ~47% | Yes (anion-exchange) |
| Shirota, Hakamata, Goda | 2003 | >72% | No |

The key innovations that eliminate chromatography are: (1) the crystalline nature of each intermediate allowing simple filtration isolation, and (2) the serendipitous discovery of the zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate intermediate, whose insolubility in dichloromethane allows clean separation from soluble byproducts.

## Analytical Applications

The pure compounds produced by this synthesis serve as essential reference standards for forensic identification of magic mushrooms. Common analytical methods include:

- **Thin layer chromatography (TLC)**: Using precoated silica gel 60 F254 or aminopropyl silica gel, with visualization by Ehrlich's reagent (producing a characteristic purple color with indoles) or UV light at 254 nm.
- **Gas chromatography (GC)**: Suitable for psilocin after derivatization; psilocybin requires thermal conversion to psilocin or silylation.
- **High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)**: The most widely used method for both compounds, with UV detection. Psilocybin's phosphate group makes it amenable to ion-pair or reverse-phase separation.
- **Mass spectrometry**: ESI-MS provides molecular weight confirmation and fragmentation patterns for both compounds.
