Fungi are capable of degrading or causing deterioration of an extraordinarily wide range of materials, processes known respectively as mycodegradation and mycodeterioration. These dual capabilities make fungi both valuable agents for [bioremediation and significant agents of material degradation.

## Materials Subject to Fungal Biodegradation

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Wood and wooden artifacts | Timber, structural lumber, processed wood products |
| Plastics | Polyethylene, polyamide-6, plasticized PVC, chitosan-graft polymers |
| Paper and library materials | Stored paper, books, documents |
| Textiles | Natural and synthetic fibers |
| Leather | Tanned and untanned leather goods |
| Building materials | Concrete, marble, electroinsulating materials |
| Glass surfaces | Biofilm colonization and etching |
| Coal | Fungal solubilization and biotransformation |
| Rubber | Ground waste tire rubber detoxification |
| Wrapping materials | Various packaging substrates |

## Notable Examples of Fungal Polymer Degradation

### Polyethylene Degradation
- Penicillium simplicissimum YK degrades polyethylene (MW 4,000-28,000)
- Mucor rouxii NRRL 1835 and Aspergillus flavus enzymes change mechanical properties of disposable polyethylene bags
- White-rot fungi are efficient in polyethylene degradation
- Aspergillus flavus colonized and degraded chitosan-graft polymethyl methacrylate film by 45% in 25 days

### Polyamide Degradation
- [Phanerochaete chrysosporium attached to polyamide-6 fibers and reduced 50% of the polymer's molar mass after 3 months

### Rubber Detoxification
- Of 15 species of white- and brown-rot fungi, Resinicium bicolor was most effective for detoxification of ground waste tire rubber prior to devulcanization

### Concrete Deterioration
- Fusarium species contribute to calcium loss and weight reduction in concrete
- Aspergillus niger and Mycelia sterilia cause cement porosity increase and bending strength loss through acid production

### Stone and Marble Damage
- Black fungi such as Phoma and Alternaria cause physical, chemical, and aesthetic damage to marble surfaces

### PVC Degradation
- Aureobasidium pullulans colonizes plasticized polyvinyl chloride within 25-40 weeks, growing on intact pPVC as sole carbon source, secreting extracellular esterase

## Fungal Characteristics Enabling Biodegradation

Several properties make fungi exceptionally capable degraders:

- **Ubiquity**: Found in virtually all habitats
- **Extremotolerance**: Many species tolerate extreme pH, temperature, and toxic conditions
- **Totipotency**: Entire organisms can regenerate from spores or hyphal fragments
- **Filamentous growth**: Mycelial networks efficiently colonize solid substrates
- **Enzymatic versatility**: Production of diverse extracellular enzymes including [cellulases, ligninases, peroxidases, laccases, and esterases
- **Longevity**: Some fungal organisms (e.g., [Armillaria bulbosa) are among the largest and longest-living organisms
- **Mycelial extent**: Networks may cover several hectares of forest floor

## Ecosystem Engineering Role

Fungi are considered natural ecosystem engineers, regulating nutrient and energy flow through mycelial networks that may cover vast areas. Despite their enormous environmental impact, fungal contributions are often neglected by engineers, scientists, and the public. See [[fungal-ecosystem-roles]] and [[fungal-roles-in-mass-extinction-recovery]].

## Distinction: Biodegradation vs. Biodeterioration

- **Biodegradation**: Intentional or beneficial breakdown of materials, as in [bioremediation or [waste conversion
- **Biodeterioration**: Unwanted or damaging breakdown of materials in service, including cultural heritage, building materials, and manufactured goods

## Applications in Bioremediation

The same enzymatic systems that cause biodeterioration can be harnessed for:
- [PAH degradation
- [Synthetic pollutant transformation
- [Plastic waste treatment
- [Coal bioconversion
- [Petroleum hydrocarbon metabolism
