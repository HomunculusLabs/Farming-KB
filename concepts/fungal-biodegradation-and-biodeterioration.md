Fungi are capable of degrading or causing deterioration of an
extraordinarily wide range of materials, processes known
respectively as mycodegradation and mycodeterioration. These dual
capabilities make fungi both valuable agents for bioremediation and
significant agents of material degradation.
## Materials Subject to Fungal Biodegradation
| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Wood and wooden artifacts | Timber, structural lumber, processed wood |
| Plastics | Polyethylene, polyamide-6, plasticized PVC |
| Paper and library materials | Stored paper, books, documents |
| Textiles | Natural and synthetic fibers |
| Leather | Tanned and untanned leather goods |
| Building materials | Concrete, marble, electroinsulating materials |
| Glass surfaces | Biofilm colonization and etching |
| Coal | Fungal solubilization and biotransformation |
| Rubber | Ground waste tire rubber detoxification |
## Notable Examples of Fungal Polymer Degradation
### Polyethylene Degradation
- Penicillium simplicissimum YK degrades polyethylene
- Mucor rouxii and Aspergillus flavus enzymes change mechanical
  properties of disposable polyethylene bags
- White-rot fungi are efficient in polyethylene degradation
- Aspergillus flavus degraded chitosan-graft polymethyl
  methacrylate film by 45% in 25 days
### Polyamide Degradation
Phanerochaete chrysosporium attached to polyamide-6 fibers and
reduced 50% of the polymer's molar mass after 3 months.
### Rubber Detoxification
Of 15 species of white- and brown-rot fungi, Resinicium bicolor
was most effective for detoxification of ground waste tire rubber
prior to devulcanization.
### Concrete and Stone Deterioration
- Fusarium species contribute to calcium loss and weight reduction
- Aspergillus niger causes cement porosity increase through acid
  production and bending strength loss
- Black fungi such as Phoma and Alternaria cause physical, chemical,
  and aesthetic damage to marble surfaces
### PVC Degradation
Aureobasidium pullulans colonizes plasticized polyvinyl chloride
within 25-40 weeks, growing on intact PVC as sole carbon source,
secreting extracellular esterase enzymes.
## Fungal Characteristics Enabling Biodegradation
Several properties make fungi exceptionally capable degraders:
ubiquity across virtually all habitats, extremotolerance of pH,
temperature, and toxic conditions, totipotency allowing regeneration
from spores or hyphal fragments, filamentous growth enabling
efficient colonization of solid substrates, enzymatic versatility
including cellulases, ligninases, peroxidases, and laccases,
extraordinary longevity, and mycelial networks covering hectares.
## Ecosystem Engineering Role
Fungi are ecosystem engineers, regulating nutrient and energy flow
through mycelial networks that may cover vast areas. Despite their
enormous environmental impact, fungal contributions are often
neglected by engineers, scientists, and the public.
## Distinction: Biodegradation vs. Biodeterioration
- Biodegradation: Intentional or beneficial breakdown of materials,
  as in bioremediation or waste conversion
- Biodeterioration: Unwanted or damaging breakdown of materials in
  service, including cultural heritage and manufactured goods
## Mechanisms of Enzymatic Polymer Degradation
Fungal degradation of synthetic polymers relies on both enzymatic
and non-enzymatic mechanisms. Extracellular ligninolytic enzymes
including lignin peroxidase, manganese peroxidase, and laccase from
white-rot fungi oxidize aromatic and phenolic compounds including
components of synthetic polymers. Non-enzymatic degradation involves
extracellular Fenton chemistry, where fungal-generated hydrogen
peroxide reacts with iron to produce hydroxyl radicals that attack
polymer backbones. Brown-rot fungi are particularly effective at
this approach.
## Biodeterioration of Cultural Heritage
Fungal biodeterioration poses a serious threat to cultural heritage
materials including paper documents, canvas paintings, stone
monuments, and wooden artifacts. Black dematiaceous fungi colonize
stone surfaces of monuments and frescoes, causing both aesthetic
damage through discoloration and physical damage through hyphal
penetration of microcracks. Prevention requires environmental
control of humidity and temperature, regular monitoring, and
targeted application of antifungal treatments.
## Strategies for Preventing Biodeterioration
Multiple strategies exist for controlling unwanted fungal
degradation. Physical methods include controlling ambient humidity
below 65% relative humidity, maintaining air circulation, using UV
filtration, and applying protective coatings. Chemical methods employ
fungicidal treatments including borates for wood and quaternary
ammonium compounds for surfaces. Biological control using antagonistic
fungi such as Trichoderma species can suppress deteriorative fungi
on cultural heritage objects.
## Emerging Contaminants and Future Targets
New classes of synthetic materials present both challenges and
opportunities for fungal biodegradation research. Microplastics from
environmental contamination are being investigated as substrates for
fungal colonization and breakdown. Pharmaceutical residues, personal
care products, and flame retardants represent emerging contaminant
classes that fungal enzyme systems may help address. Advances in
metagenomics and synthetic biology are expanding the catalog of
fungal enzymes available for bioremediation applications.
