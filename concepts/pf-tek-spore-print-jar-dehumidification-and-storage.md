# PF TEK Spore Print Jar Dehumidification and Long Term Storage

The PF TEK provides detailed guidance on the critical post printing phase of spore collection, including an essential dehumidification step and specific long term storage conditions that directly affect spore viability. Proper handling during this phase determines how long the harvested spores remain capable of germination and successful cultivation.

## The Dehumidification Step

After the mushroom cap has released its spores onto the bottom of the jar (typically requiring a couple of days in a draft free area away from direct sunlight), the jar still contains residual moisture from the fresh mushroom cap. This residual moisture is a significant threat to long term spore viability.

The PF TEK dehumidification procedure follows these steps:

1. Quickly remove the mushroom cap from the jar with as little air disturbance as possible
2. Place the jar cap back on loosely (do not seal tightly at this stage)
3. Let the jar sit in a draft free place for several days to dehumidify
4. The loose cap allows residual moisture to escape gradually while preventing airborne contaminants from entering
5. After the dehumidification period is complete, seal the jar tightly with tape to create an airtight closure

Skipping the dehumidification step is one of the most common and consequential errors in spore print preparation. Sealing a jar while moisture is still present creates a humid microclimate that promotes bacterial growth, mold development, and accelerated spore degradation. The difference between a dehumidified and a non dehumidified print can be months of additional viability.

## Optimal Storage Conditions

The PF TEK specifies precise storage conditions for the sealed spore print jar:

- **Temperature:** Room temperature is specified. The PF TEK explicitly advises against refrigeration for spore print storage.
- **Light exposure:** Dark place, completely away from sunlight. UV radiation from sunlight can damage spore genetic material over time.
- **Seal integrity:** Airtight seal maintained with tape after the dehumidification period. Any breach allows moisture and airborne contaminants to enter.
- **Orientation:** Store upright to prevent spores from shifting or clumping.

The PF TEK advice against refrigeration is notable. While refrigeration might intuitively seem beneficial for biological preservation (similar to food storage), the PF TEK found that room temperature storage in a dark, dry, sealed environment produces better long term germination results for Psilocybe Cubensis spores. Refrigeration may introduce condensation issues when the jar is removed from the cold environment, potentially rehydrating the spores and initiating premature metabolic activity.

## Spore Degradation Timeline

The PF TEK provides a clear and practical timeline for Psilocybe Cubensis spore viability:

- **Freshly harvested spores:** Germination is massive and rapid. Fresh spores from a recently taken print produce the most vigorous and uniform colonization.
- **A few months after printing:** Spores begin to show measurable signs of degradation. Germination rates decline and colonization speed decreases.
- **Approximately 1.5 years (18 months):** Spore germination is greatly reduced or may not occur at all. At this age, spores are effectively nonviable for practical cultivation purposes.

This timeline has direct practical implications for cultivation planning. Spore prints should ideally be used to make syringes within the first several months after printing for best results. Prints approaching one year in age should be used with awareness that germination may be weak or inconsistent. Prints older than a year are likely spent.

## Factors Affecting Spore Viability

Several environmental and handling factors influence how quickly spores degrade over time:

- **Residual moisture:** The single most important factor. Moisture inside the sealed jar accelerates degradation dramatically. The dehumidification step is essential for maximizing storage life.
- **Temperature extremes:** Consistent room temperature is optimal. Extreme heat is particularly destructive. The PF TEK documents that syringes exposed to killing heat during transit (overheated airplane cargo holds during heat waves, superheated mail trucks parked in the sun, or sun heated mailboxes) can lose all viability. Extreme cold is also probably destructive to spores, though they can survive freezing temperatures.
- **Light exposure:** Direct sunlight exposure causes progressive damage to spore DNA and cellular structures. Dark storage is nonnegotiable for long term preservation.
- **Seal integrity:** Any breach of the airtight seal allows ambient moisture and airborne microbial contaminants to enter the jar, both of which accelerate spore degradation.
- **Time:** Degradation is progressive and inevitable, even under ideal storage conditions. No preservation method can halt the natural aging process of spores entirely.

## Spore Syringe Longevity After Preparation

Once a spore syringe is prepared from the print, the PF TEK indicates different longevity characteristics:

- A properly prepared spore syringe will remain viable for several months under proper storage
- Viability can extend to a year or more in some cases
- Syringes should be stored at cool temperatures in a dark place
- The needle guard must be replaced after each use to maintain sterility
- The needle must be resterilized with an alcohol flame before any subsequent inoculation

The liquid suspension in a spore syringe appears to provide some protection for spores compared to dry storage on glass. However, the PF TEK does not provide specific data comparing syringe longevity to print longevity.

## Spore Print Jar Pre Sterilization

Before a jar is used for collecting a spore print, the PF TEK specifies a sterilization step to ensure the collection surface is free from contaminants:

1. Presterilize the jar and a regular metal canning lid (rubber edge up) in a small toaster oven
2. Temperature: approximately 300 degrees Fahrenheit
3. Duration: about 30 minutes
4. Keep the lid loose during the sterilization cycle to allow air circulation
5. When the jar has cooled to room temperature, tighten the lid until it is time to use the jar for the spore print
6. The rubberized sealing edge may become slightly melted from the oven heat, but this does not affect its function for spore printing purposes

## The Two Hole Lid System for Syringe Preparation

The PF TEK describes a specially modified lid used when converting a sealed spore print into a spore syringe. This lid has two holes serving distinct purposes:

- **Center hole:** Drilled to fit a Pyrex glass stirring rod. This hole allows the rod to be inserted into the jar to scrape spores from the glass bottom into the sterile water without removing the lid.
- **Edge hole:** Punched near the rubberized edge to fit a syringe needle. This hole provides access for both the water injection syringe (adding sterile water to the print) and the collection syringe (drawing spore laden water out).

Both holes must be protected with tin foil or sterile surgical tape before and after the procedure to maintain sterility. The center hole allows spore harvesting with minimal exposure of the jar interior to ambient room air.

## Integration with Syringe Preparation Workflow

The dehumidified and sealed spore print jar connects directly to the spore syringe preparation workflow in a sequence of sterile operations:

1. Replace the sealed storage lid with the two hole lid
2. Inject sterile water into the spore print jar through the edge hole
3. Flame sterilize the glass stirring rod, let it cool, and insert through the center hole to scrape spores into the water
4. Tilt the jar until the spore water reaches the needle tip at the edge hole
5. Draw the spore laden water into a sterile collection syringe
6. Seal the holes with tape and store the loaded syringe in a cool dark place

## Mature Specimen Selection for Spore Printing

The PF TEK notes a tradeoff between harvest quality and spore production. Mature specimens (darker colors, upturned caps, dark brown gills, purple spore deposits on stems) are past their prime for consumption potency but are ideal for spore production. These fully mature mushrooms release spores by the millions, creating a visible deepening purple color in the surrounding area. This purple spore deposit is the signal that the mushroom has entered its sporulation cycle and is ready for spore print collection.

## Identifying When a Spore Print Is Ready for Collection

The PF TEK provides clear visual indicators that a mature mushroom has entered its sporulation cycle and is ready for print collection:

- The cap begins to upturn, resembling an inverted umbrella
- The gills on the underside of the cap darken to a deep brown color
- Purple deposits of falling spores become visible on the stem and surrounding surfaces
- The mushroom overall becomes darker with bluish colors appearing on both caps and stems
- A deepening purple color spreads in the area around the mushroom as spores accumulate

This purple spore deposit is the definitive signal that the mushroom has matured and is actively releasing spores. At this point, the cap should be removed with sterile scissors and placed gill side down on the sterile jar bottom for print collection.

## Environmental Conditions During Print Collection

The PF TEK specifies environmental conditions for the print collection phase:

- **Draft free:** Minimize air movement to prevent spores from being blown away from the collection surface
- **Away from direct sunlight:** Light exposure can damage spores during the collection period
- **Room temperature:** Standard indoor temperatures are suitable for the 1 to 2 day collection period
- **Clean environment:** Wash hands and sterilize work surfaces before handling. Run a HEPA air cleaner in a closed room for a few hours before beginning sterile work.

The combination of sterile technique, draft free conditions, and a pre sterilized jar ensures that the collected spore print is as clean as possible, free from bacterial or fungal contamination that could compromise future cultivation attempts.
