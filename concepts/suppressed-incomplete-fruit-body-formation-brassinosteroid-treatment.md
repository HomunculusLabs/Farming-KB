# Suppressed Incomplete Fruit Body Formation by Brassinosteroid Treatment

## Overview

One of the most intriguing observations in the Gartz et al. (1990) study on brassinosteroid effects [[cap-and-stem-alkaloid-distribution-in-psilocybe-cubensis-strains]] was the near-complete [[brassinosteroid-suppression-of-incomplete-fruit-body-formation]] (abnormal) fruit body formation in [[flush-count-variation-brassinosteroid-treated-cultures]].

While the growth-promoting and yield-enhancing effects received more attention, this morphological normalization effect has significant implications for understanding fungal development and improving cultivation reliability.

The finding suggests that brassinosteroids don't merely accelerate growth — they fundamentally alter the morphogenetic program of the fungus, producing more uniform developmental outcomes.

## What Are Incomplete Fruit Bodies?

Incomplete fruit bodies — also called aborts, pins that fail to mature, or malformed primordia — are a common problem in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] across many species.

They manifest as small, stunted mushroom-like structures that stop developing and eventually rot or desiccate on the substrate surface.

In P. [[psilocybe-cubensis-cultivation-miniculture]], aborts are frequently observed, particularly under suboptimal environmental conditions or with certain strains that are prone to irregular fruiting patterns.

Aborts represent wasted biological energy. The mycelium has already invested significant resources into initiating [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]], forming the hyphal aggregates (primordia) that represent the earliest stage of mushroom formation.

But the developmental program stalls before producing a harvestable mushroom. This results in several practical problems:

- **Reduced harvest weight per flush** — resources diverted to aborts are not recovered
- **Increased contamination risk** — decaying aborts serve as nutrient sources for bacteria and molds
- **Greater labor for cleaning** the casing between flushes to remove dead tissue
- **Unpredictable yield variability** from run to run, making planning difficult

## The Gartz Observation

In the Gartz et al. experiment, brassinosteroid-treated cultures of P. cubensis on dung-grain substrate showed "almost completely suppressed" formation of incomplete fruit bodies compared to untreated controls.

This was observed across multiple flushes during the full fruiting period.

The treated cultures were supplemented with 10⁻² ppm (0.01 ppm) of synthetic 22S,23S-homobrassinolide, prepared from stigmasterol.

The control cultures produced the typical pattern of mixed development: some fully formed mushrooms alongside numerous aborts at various stages of development.

The treated cultures, by contrast, showed a higher proportion of initiated primordia reaching full maturity. This was a qualitative observation noted alongside the quantitative yield and timing data in the paper.

## Mechanism 1: Improved Nutrient Mobilization

Brassinosteroids in plants are known to enhance cell elongation and division by modulating gene expression related to cell wall loosening, [[plant-cell-membrane-transport-proteins-channels-carriers-and-pumps]], and [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]].

They activate proton pumps, increase ATPase activity, and upregulate genes involved in carbohydrate metabolism.

If a similar mechanism operates in fungi, brassinosteroids may improve the mycelium's ability to sustain the high metabolic demands of fruiting body maturation.

Primordia that would otherwise stall due to insufficient nutrient supply from the colonized substrate may receive adequate resources to complete development through enhanced translocation mechanisms.

## Mechanism 2: Enhanced Developmental Signaling

The transition from vegetative mycelium to reproductive fruiting body involves a complex developmental signaling cascade.

[[fungal-fruiting-body-formation-environmental-triggers]] (light, temperature shift, CO₂ reduction) initiate the program, but its completion depends on sustained internal signaling through transcription factors, MAP kinase pathways, and cAMP signaling.

Brassinosteroids may act as co-regulators of this signaling pathway, improving the fidelity of the developmental switch and reducing the incidence of program abortion.

In plants, brassinosteroids interact with auxin, gibberellin, and ethylene signaling — similar cross-talk may exist in fungi, though this remains unexplored.

## Mechanism 3: Stress Mitigation

Brassinosteroids in plants are well-documented anti-stress agents, conferring resistance to drought, temperature extremes, salinity, pathogen attack, and oxidative damage.

They upregulate antioxidant enzymes (superoxide dismutase, catalase, peroxidase) and stress-responsive proteins.

In fungal cultivation, aborts are often triggered by environmental stressors such as fluctuating humidity, suboptimal temperature, or microbial competition at the casing surface.

If brassinosteroids provide similar stress protection in fungi, treated cultures may maintain developmental momentum through conditions that would otherwise cause primordia to abort.

## Mechanism 4: Morphogenetic Regulation

The Gartz study also noted that treated fruit bodies lacked the typical [[membranous-annulus-loss-brassinosteroid-induced-morphological-change]] (ring) on the stem.

This indicates that brassinosteroids alter the expression of morphogenetic genes involved in tissue differentiation during fruiting body development.

The suppression of aborts may be part of a broader shift in developmental regulation, where the normal variability in fruit body morphology is reduced and the developmental program proceeds more uniformly from primordium initiation through mature sporocarp formation.

## Practical Implications for Cultivators

### Yield Predictability

When aborts are suppressed, a higher proportion of initiated primordia convert to harvestable mushrooms.

This reduces the variability between flushes and between cultivation runs, making outcomes more consistent and planning more reliable for cultivators.

### Reduced Contamination

Aborts that decay on the casing surface create entry points for bacterial and mold contamination.

By reducing the number of aborts, brassinosteroid treatment could indirectly lower contamination rates, though this was not explicitly tested.
