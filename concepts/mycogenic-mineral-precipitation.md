# Mycogenic Mineral Precipitation

## Overview

Fungi can precipitate a diverse array of **secondary organic and inorganic minerals** through both metabolism-dependent and metabolism-independent processes. These "mycogenic minerals" play important roles in soil aggregation, nutrient cycling, metal detoxification, and carbon sequestration. The formation of secondary minerals occurs through precipitation, nucleation, and deposition of crystalline material on and within fungal cell walls, influenced by environmental pH and cell wall composition.

## Carbonates

### Importance in CO₂ Sequestration

Microbial [[fungal-calcium-oxalate-and-carbonate-precipitation]] coupled with silicate weathering provides an important **terrestrial CO₂ sink**. Fungi and lichens contribute significantly to this process:

- [[peat-calcium-carbonate-perlite-casing-psilocybe-cubensis-miniculture]] (calcite, CaCO₃) is the most common mycogenic carbonate.
- Many near-surface limestones (calcretes), calcic and petrocalcic [[soil-horizons]] are secondarily cemented with calcite and [[fungal-calcium-oxalate-crystals]].
- Fungal filaments mineralized with calcite have been reported in limestones and calcareous soils from diverse localities.

### Calcite Precipitation Pathways

1. **Direct precipitation**: Calcium carbonate precipitates on and within hyphal walls.
2. **Oxalate transformation**: Calcium oxalate degrades to calcium carbonate, especially in semi-arid environments, acting to cement pre-existing limestones.
3. **Nucleation sites**: During decomposition of [[fungal-hyphae]], calcite crystals act as sites for further secondary calcite precipitation.
4. **Chitin nucleation**: Chitin, the major component of fungal cell walls, is a substrate on which calcite readily nucleates.

### Carbonate-Forming Conditions

Fungal carbonate precipitation is favoured by:
- Alkaline pH environments
- Availability of Ca²⁺ and CO₂/HCO₃⁻
- Respiratory CO₂ contributing dissolved inorganic carbon
- The presence of pre-existing mineral nuclei on hyphal surfaces

## Oxalates

### Calcium Oxalate

Calcium oxalate is the most common mycogenic oxalate and occurs in two main crystalline forms:

| Form | Formula | Stability |
|---|---|---|
| **Weddellite** | CaC₂O₄·2H₂O (dihydrate) | Less stable |
| **Whewellite** | CaC₂O₄·H₂O (monohydrate) | More stable |

Calcium oxalate crystals can exhibit various crystalline habits depending on physicochemical conditions: tetragonal, bipyramidal, plate-like, rhombohedral, or needle-shaped. Precipitation of calcium oxalate:

- Acts as a **reservoir for calcium** in the ecosystem.
- Influences **phosphate availability**.
- Can form **crusts** and **tubular crystalline sheaths** around hyphae.

### Metal Oxalates

Fungi produce oxalates with a wide variety of metals: Ca, Cd, Co, Cu, Mn, Sr, Zn, Ni, and Pb. The formation of **toxic metal oxalates** provides a detoxification mechanism:

- *Beauveria caledonica* (an oxalate over-excreting fungus) transformed cadmium, copper, lead, and zinc from toxic metal minerals into oxalates and was tolerant to all tested minerals.
- **Moolooite** (copper oxalate) and **zinc oxalate hydrate** are examples of mycogenic metal oxalates observed in experimental systems.

### Factors Enhancing Oxalate Excretion

- **NO₃⁻ as nitrogen source** enhances oxalate production compared to NH₄⁺.
- **HCO₃⁻, Ca²⁺** stimulate oxalate formation.
- **Toxic metals** (Cu, Al) and certain minerals (pyromorphite, zinc phosphate) upregulate oxalate excretion as a detoxification response.

## Reductive and Oxidative Precipitation

### Reductive Precipitation

Many fungi can reduce [[staycare-fungal-biomethylation-of-metals-and-metalloids]], precipitating them in their elemental forms:

- **Silver (Ag)**: Precipitated as elemental silver, visible as black colouration of colonies.
- **Tellurium (Te)**: Elemental tellurium precipitated as black deposits.
- **Selenium (Se)**: Elemental selenium precipitated as red colouration.

### Oxidative Precipitation

Fungi can oxidize metals in minerals and precipitate them as oxides:

- **Manganese**: Oxidized from Mn(II) in rhodochrosite (MnCO₃) to Mn oxides.
- **Iron**: Oxidized from Fe(II) in siderite (FeCO₃) to Fe oxides.
- **Desert varnish**: Dark Fe(II)- and Mn(II)-rich patinas on rocks in arid regions, believed to be partly of microbial origin.

## Other Mycogenic Minerals

A wide variety of additional secondary minerals have been documented in association with fungi:

- **Birnessite** (Mn oxide)
- **Todorokite** (Mn oxide)
- **Ferrihydrite** and **goethite** (Fe oxides/hydroxides)
- **Iron gluconate** and **calcium formate** (organic metal compounds)
- **Forsterite** (Mg silicate)
- **Halloysite** and **montmorillonite** (clay minerals)
- **Glushinskite** (MgC₂O₄·2H₂O — magnesium oxalate)
- **Hydroserussite** (basic zinc carbonate)

The specific combination of biotic and abiotic factors determines which secondary minerals form in any given environment.

## Soil Aggregation and Stabilization

Precipitation of mycogenic minerals has important consequences for soil structure:

- **Carbonates, phosphates, and hydroxides** increase soil aggregation by acting as bonding agents between soil particles.
- **Cations** released through dissolution (Si⁴⁺, Fe³⁺, Al³⁺, Ca²⁺) stimulate precipitation of compounds that cement soil particles.
- **Hyphae** physically enmesh soil particles, alter particle alignment, and release organic metabolites that enhance aggregate stability.
- **Mucilaginous sheaths** around hyphae provide an interconnected microenvironment that promotes mineral nucleation.

## Environmental Significance

Mycogenic mineral precipitation has broad implications:

1. **Carbon sequestration**: Carbonate formation provides a long-term CO₂ sink.
2. **Metal immobilization**: Precipitation transforms mobile toxic metals into chemically inert forms, limiting bioavailability and detoxifying [[singh-fungal-biofilms-in-contaminated-environments]].
3. **Soil formation**: Mineral precipitation contributes to the cementation and stabilization of developing soils.
4. **Nutrient cycling**: Oxalate formation influences calcium and phosphate dynamics in ecosystems.
5. **Bioremediation**: Metal precipitation can be harnessed for treatment of contaminated land and industrial wastes.
