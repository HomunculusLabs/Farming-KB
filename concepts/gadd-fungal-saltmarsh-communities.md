# Fungal Communities in Salt Marsh Ecosystems

**Source:** Gadd, Watkinson & Dyer, *Fungi in the Environment* (2006), Chapter 18 (Newell et al.)

## Overview

Fungal communities in salt marshes play a critical role in the decomposition of standing-dead marsh grasses, particularly smooth cordgrass (Spartina alterniflora). These ascomycete-dominated communities are among the most productive microbial decomposer systems known, rivaling forest-floor ecosystems in their rates of secondary production and carbon flow through marine food webs.

## The Smooth Cordgrass Standing-Decay System

Spartina alterniflora is the dominant macrophyte in Atlantic and Gulf coast salt marshes of [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] production in smooth-cordgrass marshes exceeds 0.5 kg m-2 yr-1
- This is comparable to forest-floor ecosystem production rates
- Production flows to litter-comminuting invertebrates (gastropods, arthropods) at the base of the marsh food web
- Cordgrass ascomycetes expel approximately 5.9 billion ascospores m-2 yr-1

## Dominant Fungal Species

Three predominant ascomycete species dominate blade decomposition:
1. **Phaeosphaeria spartinicola** Leuchtmann
2. **Phaeosphaeria halima** (Johnson) Shoemaker & Babcock
3. **Mycosphaerella sp.2** (Kohlmeyer & Kohlmeyer, 1979)

These species occupy more than 90% of decaying blade area at sites with low grazing pressure from periwinkle snails.

### Secondary Species
- **Buergenerula spartinae** Kohlm. & Gessner (encountered at 40%+ of blade area)
- **Hydropisphaera erubescens** (Desm.) Rossman & Samuels (<1% blade area)
- **Koorchaloma spartinicola** Sarma, Newell & Hyde (<1% blade area)
- Several undescribed species
