# Vermicompost Tea

> Note: This page is compiled from general knowledge, not sourced from a specific file in the wiki's raw/ directory.

Vermicompost tea (also called worm castings tea or vermicast tea) is a liquid extract made from worm castings (vermicompost). It is used as a foliar spray or soil drench to deliver beneficial microorganisms, soluble nutrients, and plant growth compounds directly to plants and soil. It is distinct from compost tea, though the brewing methods overlap.

## Vermicompost Tea vs. Compost Tea

| Aspect | Vermicompost Tea | Compost Tea |
|---|---|---|
| Source material | Worm castings (vermicompost) | Thermophilic compost |
| Microbial profile | Higher bacterial diversity, more beneficial fungi (especially in well-managed vermicompost) | Variable, depends on compost quality |
| Nutrient content | Higher in plant-available nitrogen (nitrate), humic acids, plant growth hormones | Variable, often higher in phosphorus |
| Pathogen risk | Very low (worm gut processing eliminates most pathogens) | Higher if compost is not fully finished |
| Consistency | More consistent due to controlled worm composting process | Variable depending on compost ingredients and maturity |
| Cost | Worm castings are more expensive per unit | Compost is cheaper and more available |

## Worm Castings: The Starting Material

Vermicompost is produced by earthworms (primarily Eisenia fetida — red wigglers, and Eisenia andrei) feeding on organic waste. The worm gut acts as a bioreactor:

- Organic matter is partially digested and excreted as castings
- Castings are rich in beneficial bacteria (Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Azotobacter), fungi (Trichoderma, mycorrhizal species), and actinomycetes
- High in humic and fulvic acids
- Contains plant growth regulators: cytokinins, auxins, gibberellins
- Nutrient-dense: typically 1.5-2.5% N, 1-2% P, 1-2.5% K, plus calcium, magnesium, and trace minerals
- C/N ratio typically 12-15:1 (similar to soil humus)
- pH near neutral (6.5-7.5)

## Brewing Methods

### Method 1: Non-Aerated (Passive) Vermicompost Tea

The simplest approach, suitable for small-scale use:

1. Place 1-2 cups of worm castings in a 5-gallon (20 litre) bucket
2. Fill with dechlorinated water (rainwater, well water, or tap water left to off-gas for 24 hours)
3. Stir vigorously
4. Let steep for 24-48 hours, stirring occasionally
5. Strain through a fine mesh or cloth
6. Apply immediately

**Pros**: Simple, no equipment needed. **Cons**: Lower microbial diversity, less oxygen means fewer aerobic organisms survive.

### Method 2: Aerated Compost Tea (ACT) — Recommended

This is the standard method for serious growers. Aeration supports the growth of aerobic microorganisms:

**Equipment needed**:
- 5-20 gallon bucket or tank
- Aquarium air pump with airstone(s) and tubing
- Fine mesh bag or strainer
- Unsulfured molasses (food source for microbes)

**Recipe (5 gallons / 20 litres)**:

1. Dechlorinate water — use rainwater, or bubble tap water for 1-2 hours before adding ingredients
2. Add 2-4 cups of high-quality worm castings to a mesh bag
3. Suspend the bag in the water
4. Add 1-2 tablespoons of unsulfured molasses (feeds bacteria)
5. Optional additions:
   - 1 teaspoon liquid kelp (feeds fungi, adds micronutrients)
   - 1 teaspoon fish hydrolysate (nitrogen source, feeds bacteria)
   - 1 teaspoon rock dust (minerals, fungal food)
6. Turn on the air pump — maintain vigorous bubbling
7. Brew for 24-36 hours at room temperature (65-80F / 18-27C)
8. Strain and apply immediately

**Key principles**:
- DO NOT brew longer than 36 hours — anaerobic conditions develop, producing harmful organisms and unpleasant odours
- If the tea smells bad (sour, sewage-like), do NOT apply — discard and start over. Good tea smells earthy, sweet, or like fresh compost.
- Water temperature matters: cold water slows microbial growth, hot water kills them

## Application Rates

### As a Soil Drench
- Dilute 1:1 with water (optional — undiluted is also fine)
- Apply 1-2 cups per plant at the base
- Apply every 2-4 weeks during the growing season
- Especially beneficial at transplanting, flowering, and fruiting stages
- Soak the root zone thoroughly

### As a Foliar Spray
- Apply undiluted or diluted 1:1
- Spray in early morning or late evening (stomata open, UV less damaging to microbes)
- Cover both sides of leaves
- Apply every 2-3 weeks
- Good for disease suppression when applied preventively

## Benefits

### Disease Suppression
The beneficial microorganisms in vermicompost tea compete with pathogens for leaf and root surface space, produce antibiotics, and trigger plant systemic acquired resistance (SAR):
- Suppresses powdery mildew, downy mildew, botrytis, and various foliar diseases
- Reduces soil-borne pathogens (Pythium, Rhizoctonia, Fusarium)
- Trichoderma species in the tea parasitize pathogenic fungi

### Nutrient Delivery
- Provides immediately available nitrogen (nitrate form), potassium, and micronutrients
- Humic and fulvic acids chelate minerals, improving plant uptake
- Foliar application provides rapid nutrient absorption

### Plant Growth Stimulation
- Cytokinins, auxins, and gibberellins in vermicompost promote root growth, cell division, and overall vigour
- Improved root branching and depth
- Enhanced seed germination when seeds are soaked in diluted tea

### Soil Biology Enhancement
- Inoculates soil with diverse beneficial microorganisms
- Improves soil structure through microbial glues and hyphal networks
- Supports mycorrhizal associations
- Enhances nutrient cycling in the soil food web

## Quality Indicators

Good vermicompost tea:
- Smells earthy, sweet, or like forest floor
- Appears dark brown, not cloudy or murky
- Forms a thin foam on the surface (microbial activity)
- No foul or sewage-like odour
- Active bubbling surface when aerating

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Foul/sour smell | Anaerobic conditions, brewed too long | Discard, ensure vigorous aeration, reduce brew time |
| No foam or bubbles | Insufficient food source, cold water | Add molasses, ensure water is 65-75F |
| Slime on surface | Excessive molasses, bacteria overgrowth | Reduce molasses to 1 tbsp, ensure adequate aeration |
| No visible results | Poor quality castings, chlorinated water | Source high-quality vermicompost, use dechlorinated water |
