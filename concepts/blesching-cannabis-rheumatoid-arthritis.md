# Cannabis and Rheumatoid Arthritis

## Overview

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is the most crippling form of arthritis, deforming
joints and bending bodies. It is considered an autoimmune disorder that occurs
when something goes wrong with the body's immune system and it attacks healthy
parts of the body such as joints. Long-time rheumatoid arthritis sufferers can
often be identified from a distance because their joints take on a gnarly
appearance.

According to Uwe Blesching's Cannabis Health Index (CHI), the evidence-based
confidence level for cannabis in treating rheumatoid arthritis is based on 3
studies reviewed with a total CHI value of 10, yielding a CHI score of 3.33.

## Cannabis and Rheumatoid Arthritis: The Evidence

### Key Clinical Trial (2006, UK)

In 2006, researchers from the UK enrolled 58 patients with rheumatoid arthritis
in a double-blind, placebo-controlled study to determine the effect of
cannabinoids on pain and other symptoms. The results indicated that patients
who received the cannabinoids slept better and experienced a reduction in pain
when compared to the placebo group.

The researchers wrote: "In the first ever controlled trial of a CBM
[cannabis-based medicine] in RA [rheumatoid arthritis], a significant analgesic
effect was observed and disease activity was significantly suppressed following
Sativex treatment." The Sativex used was an oromucosal mouth spray consisting
of roughly equal parts of THC (2.7 mg) and CBD (2.5 mg).

### Immune System Research (2006, Boston)

Boston scientists acknowledged that "Habitual cannabis use has been shown to
[positively] affect the human immune system, and recent advances in
endocannabinoid research provide a basis for understanding these
immunomodulatory effects."

After testing numerous novel molecules that exert their biological effects
through the endocannabinoid system, the researchers suggested the therapeutic
potential of cannabinoids on inflammatory diseases such as rheumatoid
arthritis. Cell-based experiments and in vivo animal testing suggest that
regulation of the endocannabinoid circuitry can impact almost every major
function associated with the immune system.

### Meta-Analysis (2009, Worcester, Massachusetts)

A meta-analysis of studies on cannabinoids in the context of pain revealed
that all types of cannabinoids as well as non-cannabinoid parts of the plant
effectively reduce pain from inflammation found in post-surgery patients,
rheumatism, rheumatoid arthritis, chronic neuropathic pain, and fibromyalgia.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Sativex is not synthetic but rather made from cannabis. The formulation ratios
are very similar to actual Cannabis sativa ratios. Sativa and sativa-prominent
hybrids contain relatively higher THC:CBD ratios and thus activate both CB1
and CB2 receptors.

Given the autoimmune and inflammatory nature of rheumatoid arthritis, indica
or indica-dominant strains with a lower THC:CBD ratio may be preferable for
CB2 receptor activation and anti-inflammatory effects.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Rheumatoid Arthritis

Researchers from Ann Arbor examining psychological factors in the development
of rheumatoid arthritis "suggested that the rheumatics communicate poorly with
their relatives about their hurt feelings."

Autoimmune diseases may mirror an intense and likely longstanding conflict of
belief and the dueling emotions this elicits. A case study of three female
patients with the disease revealed shared mental/emotional denominators: upon
onset, each woman was in a relationship that produced at once intense rage and
love for her partner. They hated their relationships but were too dependent to
leave. Neither woman acted out her anger but had to endure the abuse that
elicited the rage.

In another study of 33 patients with rheumatoid arthritis, researchers
concluded: "In these cases the general psychodynamic background is a chronic
inhibited hostile aggressive state as a reaction to the earliest masochistic
dependence on the mother that is carried over to the father and all human
relationships."

### Aggravating Factors

- Enduring abuse perpetrated by a perceived authority
- Suppressed hurt and unexpressed rage
- Unexpressed feelings combined with conflicting beliefs
- Hostility composed of anger and hurt directed at oneself

### Suggested Approaches

- Discover and release repressed and/or suppressed feelings
- Develop appreciation of the emotional spectrum
- Replace conflicting beliefs with those engendering love and compassion
- Find, embrace, feel, release, and forgive lingering hurt and anger

## Dietary Support

Cayenne, Myrrh, Nigella, Rosemary, and Turmeric are recommended. These
include (E)-beta-caryophyllene-containing spices that activate CB2 receptor
sites and initiate potent anti-inflammatory actions.

Cayenne stimulates peripheral circulation. In Cuba, topical tinctures and
creams are used to treat chronic aches and pains of lumbago, arthritis, and
rheumatism. Myrrh modulates inflammatory responses and has been used for
thousands of years in the treatment of arthritis. Nigella's volatile oil can
suppress artificially induced arthritis in rats.
