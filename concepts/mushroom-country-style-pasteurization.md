## Country-Style System

### Equipment
- 200-liter drums (steel oil drums, cleaned)
- Iron or bamboo screen (round, approximately 5 inches high to fit inside drum)
- Firewood (or branches as green fuel)
- Double layers of old rice bags
- Plastic sheeting
- Thermometer (optional but recommended)

### Procedure

1. **Set up the drum** - Clean the steaming pot and add approximately 4 inches (10 cm) of water to the bottom.

2. **Place the screen** - Set the iron or wooden screen inside the drum so it sits approximately 1 inch above the water level. This prevents bags from sitting in boiling water.

3. **Load substrate bags** - Place bags in the pasteurization system until full. Each drum can hold 80-100 bags at a time.

4. **Cover with insulation** - Cover everything with double layers of old rice bags, then place plastic sheeting over the rice bags and close tightly. This creates an insulated steam chamber.

5. **Steam** - Light the fire and maintain temperature constant. The critical parameter is to hold 90-100C (when steam comes out continuously) for 3-4 hours.

### Critical Points
- Temperature must remain constant throughout the steaming period
- There must always be water in the drum - check periodically and refill if needed
- Do not open the cover during steaming as this releases heat and compromises pasteurization
- The timing of 3-4 hours starts only when steam is coming out continuously

6. **Cool down** - Remove firewood from the stove. Let cool for approximately 20 minutes. Remove cover bags and let cool further until bags can be handled.

7. **Transfer** - Transfer bags to the already cleaned inoculation area.

## Commercial Pasteurization System

For larger operations, a dedicated pasteurization chamber is used:

1. Clean the water reservoir at the bottom by releasing the valve; dry with cloth
2. Refill water to the marked level
3. Transfer iron racks into the chamber one by one until full
4. Close door securely and verify thermometer reading
5. Light burner and maintain 98-100C for 3-4 hours
6. Release all air from the chamber before starting the timing
7. Cool down by opening the chamber door for 1.5-2 hours
8. Transfer bags to the cleaned inoculation area

Commercial chambers typically hold 500 bags. Two types exist: direct-heat (burner underneath) and boiler-generated steam systems.

## Solar Heating Pasteurization (Experimental)

An optional alternative to steam pasteurization:
- Fill substrate bags into the solar chamber
- Close the cover
- Uses solar energy to achieve pasteurization temperatures
- More variable and climate-dependent than steam methods

## Cost Estimate

From the training program (Thailand, 2000):
- 200-liter oil drums: 400-700 Baht per set
- Gas tank and burner set: 2,000-3,000 Baht (optional; most farmers use tree branches instead)
- Cost per bag for pasteurization: approximately 0.03 Baht (using free branch fuel)
- Cost per bag for pasteurization labor: approximately 0.07 Baht

## Troubleshooting

Common problems with country-style pasteurization include incomplete heat penetration (symptom: green mold Trichoderma within days of inoculation) — caused by packing substrate too tightly or underheating the core. Solution: use a thermometer probe to verify internal temperature reaches 60-65°C throughout. Burnt substrate (symptom: dark brown color, ammonia smell) indicates overheating above 80°C — reduce heat and check water level. Wet bags after pasteurization mean poor drainage — elevate bags on a rack above the water line during the process.

## Adaptations for Disabilities

- Pasteurization chamber should be built lower than standard height for easy access by people in wheelchairs and those unable to stand
- The drum system is ideal as it can be operated from a seated position
- Fire management may need assistance from another team member
