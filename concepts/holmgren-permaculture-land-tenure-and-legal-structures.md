## Examples and Precedents

Several intentional communities have successfully implemented innovative land tenure models that align with permaculture principles. Crystal Waters Permaculture Village in Australia uses a body corporate structure where common land is managed collectively. Ecovillages in Denmark and Germany have pioneered co-housing models that balance private living space with shared agricultural land. These models demonstrate that legal innovation can enable the kind of integrated land use that permaculture designs require.

## Body Corporate and Strata Title

Holmgren learned from New Zealand planner and resource ecologist Haikai Tane about strata or body corporate title being used in shopping centres and industrial estates. Key benefits include:

- Small businesses gain economies of scale for shared services (marketing, accounting)
- More bargaining power with large chain stores
- Shared waste management services provide economies of scale
- Potential for complex industrial ecology where output of one business is input of another

The critical insight was that this modern form of land tenure could allow integration of forestry, horticulture, aquaculture and apiculture into existing broadacre pastoral farms without subdividing the land. The desire to live in a rural environment provided the economic opportunity for reorganising farms into rural residential communities while expanding productive land use potential.

## Commons and Collective Management

Most pre-industrial societies had broadacre commons with varying rights for community members. The Enclosure Acts in England began the global process of privatising the commons, still proceeding in poorer countries.

In rich countries, remaining commons in state forests and national parks are managed by bureaucratic and increasingly corporatised structures, leading to carve-ups of territory and functions, and in many cases to privatisation. Models for commons management are more likely to emerge from innovations within intentional communities.

## The Feudal Alternative

Without models of common ownership, redevelopment and management for broadacre farmland commons, the default model for a low-energy future will be some form of feudalism. Holmgren notes that ownership of vast tracts by a single family or company has potential to institute "baronial sustainability" where land is worked by non-owning farm labourers. Large farms will again become communities of some sort in energy descent, with more diverse and labour-intensive uses.

History shows that relying on the benevolence of elites is hardly desirable. The model shows how:

- Private residential lots coexist with commonly managed forest land
- Legal structures provide clear rights and responsibilities for land use
- Community governance can balance individual autonomy with collective management
- The economic model makes sustainable land use viable within the current economy

## Reform of Land Tenure

Holmgren argues that reform of land tenure is one of the central sustainability and justice issues worldwide. First World governments and corporations have resisted positive initiatives that might provide livelihoods and justice, let alone new models of collective management. The task requires innovative legal structures that:

- Enable community ownership without state management
- Allow productive integration of diverse land uses
- Provide for succession and long-term stewardship
- Balance individual rights with collective responsibility
- Can evolve as energy descent progresses
