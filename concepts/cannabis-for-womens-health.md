## Menstrual Pain (Dysmenorrhea)

### Cannabis for Period Pain
**Grotenhermen and Schnelle (2003)**: "Survey on the medical use of cannabis and THC in Germany." Journal of Cannabis Therapeutics 3(2):17-40.

Survey found menstrual pain to be one of the most common reasons women use medical cannabis in Germany.

### Fennel vs. Mefenamic Acid
**Modaress Nejad and Asadipour (2006)**: "Comparison of the effectiveness of fennel and mefenamic acid on pain intensity in dysmenorrhoea." Eastern Mediterranean Health Journal.

Fennel extract was comparable to mefenamic acid (a standard NSAID) for menstrual pain relief, without the gastrointestinal side effects.

### Greek Plant Extracts as SERMs
**Kassi et al. (2004)**: "Greek plant extracts exhibit selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM)-like properties."

Several plant extracts showed SERM-like activity, selectively activating estrogen receptors -- relevant to the hormonal basis of menstrual pain.

### Historical Context
**Russo (2002)**: "Cannabis treatments in obstetrics and gynecology: A historical review."

Cannabis was widely used for menstrual cramps and other gynecological conditions in 19th-century medicine. It was a standard component of "female remedies" in pharmacopoeias.

## Endometriosis

### Endocannabinoid Involvement
**Dmitrieva et al. (2010)**: "Endocannabinoid involvement in endometriosis." Pain 151(3):703-10.

The endocannabinoid system is directly involved in endometriosis. Abnormal endocannabinoid signaling may contribute to both the pain and the abnormal tissue growth characteristic of endometriosis.

### Antiproliferative Effects
**Leconte et al. (2010)**: "Antiproliferative effects of cannabinoid agonists on deep infiltrating endometriosis." American Journal of Pathology 177(6):2963-70.

Cannabinoid agonists showed antiproliferative effects on endometrial tissue -- meaning they could potentially slow or reverse the growth of endometrial implants outside the uterus. This is a disease-modifying effect, not just pain relief.

### Trauma and Chronic Pelvic Pain
**Heim et al. (1998)**: "Abuse-related posttraumatic stress disorder and alteration of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis in women with chronic pelvic pain." Psychosomatic Medicine 60:302-18.

Women with chronic pelvic pain (including endometriosis) showed high rates of abuse-related PTSD and HPA axis dysregulation, connecting trauma history to gynecological pain conditions.

## Morning Sickness

### Cannabis Use in Pregnancy
**Westfall et al. (2006)**: "Survey of medicinal cannabis use among childbearing women: Patterns of its use in pregnancy and retroactive self-assessment of its efficacy against morning sickness." Complementary Therapies in Clinical Practice 12(1):27-33.

Survey of women who used cannabis during pregnancy for morning sickness found it was reported as effective. However, the safety of cannabis use during pregnancy remains controversial and understudied.

### Endocannabinoid System in Pregnancy
**Paria et al. (2001)**: "Dysregulated cannabinoid signaling disrupts uterine receptivity for embryo implantation." Journal of Biological Chemistry.

**Paria, Wang, and Dey (2002)**: "Endocannabinoid signaling in synchronizing embryo development and uterine receptivity for implantation."

The endocannabinoid system plays a critical role in embryo implantation and pregnancy maintenance. Anandamide levels must be precisely regulated -- too high or too low disrupts implantation. This creates a complex picture for cannabis use during pregnancy.

### Anandamide and the Menstrual Cycle
**El-Talatini et al. (2010)**: "The relationship between plasma levels of the endocannabinoid, anandamide, sex steroids, and gonadotrophins during the menstrual cycle."

Anandamide levels fluctuate during the menstrual cycle in relationship to estrogen, progesterone, and gonadotropins, confirming the endocannabinoid system's role in female reproductive physiology.
