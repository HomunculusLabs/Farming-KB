# Syncretic Folk Religious Hallucinogenic Ceremonies

## Overview

Ralph Metzner's 1998 review identified syncretic folk religious ceremonies as the third of four paradigms for the therapeutic or healing use of psychoactive substances. In these ceremonies, the focus is on community bonding, celebratory worship, and the maintenance of cultural and spiritual traditions that blend indigenous plant medicine practices with elements of Christianity, African religions, and other spiritual systems.

## Defining Syncretism

Religious syncretism is the blending of different religious traditions to create a new, hybrid form.

In the context of hallucinogenic ceremonies, syncretism typically involves the combination of indigenous Amazonian plant medicine practices — particularly the use of ayahuasca — with elements of Catholic Christianity, African spiritual traditions, and sometimes esoteric or Spiritist practices.

The resulting ceremonies are neither purely indigenous nor purely Christian but represent something genuinely new.

They are a religious form that could only have emerged from the collision of cultures in the colonial and post-colonial Amazon basin.

Metzner noted that in these ceremonies, the focus is primarily on community bonding and celebratory worship, rather than on the kind of individual healing or divination that characterizes shamanic or psychotherapeutic approaches.

## The Santo Daime Tradition

The Santo Daime church was founded in the 1930s by Raimundo Irineu Serra, a Brazilian of African and indigenous descent who encountered ayahuasca in the Amazonian rubber plantations.

Serra, known as Mestre Irineu, developed a ritual practice centered on the consumption of ayahuasca, which the church calls "daime" (from the Portuguese "dai-me" — "give me").

The Daime ritual involves the communal drinking of ayahuasca while singing hymns ("hinos") that are received through spiritual inspiration.

The hymns are performed in call-and-response format, accompanied by rhythmic dancing and the playing of maracas.

The liturgical structure of the ceremony draws heavily on Catholic Mass, with processions, altars, and prayers to Christian figures — but the experience produced by the ayahuasca is unmistakably shamanic.

## Celebratory Worship

The celebratory dimension of syncretic ceremonies is also significant.

While Western psychotherapy approaches the [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]] as a therapeutic tool — something to be used for the treatment of specific conditions — syncretic ceremonies approach it as a form of worship.

The ayahuasca experience is understood as a gift from the divine, a sacrament that brings the participant into direct contact with spiritual reality.

The joy, gratitude, and celebration that characterize these ceremonies reflect this understanding.

The experience is not primarily about healing or personal growth, though these may occur as side effects.

It is about praising, thanking, and communing with the divine — a fundamentally religious orientation.
