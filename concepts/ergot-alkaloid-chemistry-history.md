## Historical Ergotism Epidemics

Documented ergotism outbreaks have caused devastating mortality
throughout history. Two clinical types have been described:

- **Gangrenous ergotism (St. Anthony's Fire):** Begins with tingling
  in the fingers, followed by vomiting and diarrhea, then gangrene
  in toes and fingers within days. Entire limbs were affected by dry
  gangrene, followed by separation. The condition was named for the
  Order of St. Anthony, which operated hospitals specializing in its
  treatment.

- **Convulsive ergotism:** Starts similarly but progresses to painful
  spasms of limb muscles culminating in epileptic-like convulsions.
  Many patients become delirious.

In A.D. 994, an outbreak of ergotism killed nearly 40,000 people in
France. A 1129 outbreak killed approximately 1,200. The historian Mary
Kilbourne Matossian argued that La Grande Peur of 1789 -- a peasant
uprising pivotal in the French Revolution -- had roots in ergot-infected
rye bread constituting the bulk of the rural peasantry's diet. Ergot-
infected flour has also been proposed as a factor in the decline of the
Roman Empire and in the Salem witch trials of 1692.
