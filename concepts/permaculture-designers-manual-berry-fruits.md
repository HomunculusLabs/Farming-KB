Berries pioneer for, and protect, seedling trees, so that advancing forest edges often develop bramble and cane thickets. In these thickets oak, chestnut, plum, apple, and birch thrive. Brambles, in particular, protect and nourish young fruit trees. ## Cage Culture for Intensive Production

Growers of sweet cherries, blueberry, and table-quality raspberry report economic advantage in completely caging over the area of crop. Such an advantage gives yields of up to 30 times that of open field conditions.

In these cages (about 20 m by 10 m by 2.4 m high, using 10 mm mesh), a polyculture of small fruit plus espalier fruits (nectarine, peach) is safe from birds and even large moth species. One or two such cages of piping plus mesh will support a grower aiming for high quality markets.

Ground preparation for cage culture:
- Ridging for species such as blueberry (ridges of 1.5 m wide, 0.6 m high, with a drainpipe at base is ideal)
- Fitting of drip [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-guild-design-in-permaculture]], and to select plants of high fruit yield. Cage walls may support trellis of bramble fruits (youngberry, boysenberry).

## Field Culture and Self-Pick Sales

In field conditions, losses to birds are unavoidable but may be minimised by using tethered predator hawk kites above the crop at critical ripening periods. Such kites imitate local hawks or eagles and are very effective bird repellents. They should be removed after the crop is picked so birds do not get accustomed to them.

As much of the berry crop cost is in picking, it is preferable to plant for self-pick sales where farms are within 15-20 km of town markets. The berries (currant, gooseberry, raspberry, strawberry) are best planted double row on contour mounds 1-1.5 m high, with grassed paths and wider spacing of mounds or ridges than in cage culture, so pickers have easy access.

Self-pick farms need parking and weigh-out centres, buckets for pickers, and produce tubs for crop.

## Small Fruit in Cool Areas

Small fruit -- currants, gooseberry, raspberry, trellis blackberry -- is a feature of cool-area gardens. Six to fifteen plants of 78 species yields a great deal of fresh or frozen food of high vitamin C value. ### Blackberry (Rubus fruticosus agg.)
Blackberries are among the most vigorous and productive berries, tolerating a wide range of soils (pH 5.5-7.0) but preferring deep, well-drained loam. Thornless cultivars like 'Navaho' and 'Triple Crown' are preferred for pick-your-own operations. They fruit on floricanes; prune after harvest by removing all canes that bore fruit. Blackberries can become invasive -- see the control section above. Train on a wire fence or trellis spaced 3m apart. Excellent companions include comfrey (dynamic accumulator), yarrow (attracts beneficial insects), and clover (nitrogen fixation). They are tolerant of partial shade, making them useful for forest garden edges.

### Strawberry (Fragaria x ananassa)
Strawberries require well-drained soil (pH 5.5-6.8) rich in organic matter. They are shallow-rooted and sensitive to waterlogging. In permaculture systems, strawberries function as excellent ground covers beneath fruit trees. The perennial matted-row system allows runners to fill in naturally, while the annual hill system removes runners for maximum fruit production. Replace plants every 3-4 years as virus load accumulates. Mulch with straw or pine needles to keep fruit clean and suppress weeds. The primary pests are slugs, tarnished plant bugs, and spider mites. Companion with borage (attracts pollinators), thyme (deters worms), and lettuce (living mulch).

### Currant and Gooseberry (Ribes spp.)
Currants and gooseberries thrive in cool climates with partial shade and slightly acidic to neutral soil (pH 5.5-7.0). Red currants (R. rubrum) produce tart berries excellent for preserves and jelly. Black currants (R. nigrum) are rich in vitamin C and grow best in deep, fertile soil. Gooseberries (R. uva-crispa) tolerate heavier soils and some drought. Prune in late winter by removing old wood and maintaining an open goblet shape. White pine blister rust can be a problem in areas near five-needle pines; check local regulations before planting. Companion plants include chives (deter aphids), nasturtiums (trap crop for aphids), and comfrey.

### Elderberry (Sambucus nigra)
Elderberry is a hardy, adaptable shrub growing in almost any soil (pH 5.5-6.5) including wet sites. It is among the most productive berries per unit area, yielding 10-15 kg per mature bush. Elderflowers are also harvested for cordials and fritters. Plant at least two varieties for cross-pollination (e.g., 'Adams' and 'York'). Prune in early spring by removing the oldest canes (3+ years). Elderberry is relatively pest-free but can suffer from elder shoot borer.
