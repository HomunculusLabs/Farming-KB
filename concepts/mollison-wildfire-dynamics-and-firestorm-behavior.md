## The Physics of Firestorms

Intense wildfires, both urban and rural, can generate powerful vortices
when fanned by dry winds. The mass ignition of large forest and building
areas feeds a powerful updraught. Cold dry air rushes in to replace the
air consumed in burning, and fire tornadoes (firestorms) result. These
carry large burning particles aloft on "smoke nimbus" clouds. Whole house
sections can pinwheel across the sky and drop out ahead of the main fire
front, where they in turn set up secondary firestorm conditions. The
effects on people and property are very much like those of tornadoes,
but with the additional danger of intense heat.

Wildfire can create fire tornadoes especially at hill crests, spreading
burning particles downwind for many kilometres. This phenomenon makes
[[permaculture-fire-management]] a whole-landscape concern, not merely a property-edge
concern.

## Key Principles

1. Identify fire sectors early in the [[hemenway-garden-design-process-observation-and-visioning]].
2. Place flammable species away from structures and fire pathways.
3. Create redundant fire protection through multiple elements.
4. Ensure water access for firefighting at high points in the landscape.
5. Use fire-resistant species as primary structural and shelter plantings
  near settlements.
6. Design for rapid recovery after fire events through soil protection
  and reseeding capacity.
