## Sources of Contamination

When contamination occurs, identifying the source is critical for preventing
recurrence. If contamination appears in an uninoculated control jar, the
problem lies in the sterilization process. The jars were not steamed long
enough, or the pot was not tightly covered, or the jars were not properly
sealed during steaming. If contamination appears after inoculation but the
control jars remained clean, there are two primary suspects. The first is a
contaminated spore syringe. Spore syringes can pick up contaminants during
manufacture, shipping, or storage, and a contaminated syringe will introduce
contaminants directly into the substrate through the inoculation holes. The
second suspect is a breach of the dry vermiculite barrier layer during
inoculation, which would expose the wet nutritious substrate to airborne
contaminant spores.

## The Dry Vermmiculite Barrier

The dry vermiculite barrier layer is the single most important contamination
defense in the PF TEK. After the substrate is loaded into the jar, a half to
three-quarter inch layer of plain dry vermiculite is placed on top and
leveled off at the rim. This layer serves as a physical and moisture barrier
between the outside environment and the wet nutritious substrate below. It
protects the substrate from airborne contaminants that might otherwise settle
on the moist surface and germinate. The barrier also absorbs and regulates
moisture transpiration and condensation within the jar, preventing excess
water from pooling on the substrate surface where it could harbor bacteria.
During inoculation, the needle must pass through this dry layer to reach the
substrate below, and disturbing the layer as little as possible is critical.
