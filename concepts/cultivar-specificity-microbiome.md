## Cultivar Specificity in the Plant Microbiome

## Overview

**Cultivar specificity** refers to the phenomenon where different plant
cultivars (distinct varieties within a species) maintain significantly
different [[cannabis-rhizosphere-endorhiza-communities]]
differ significantly between strains even when grown in identical soil.

## Evidence for Cultivar Specificity

### Statistical Evidence

In Cannabis, cultivar-level differentiation of microbial communities was:

- **Not significant in bulk soil**: ADONIS R² = 0.11, p = 0.25 (weighted
  UniFrac) — soil type, not plant identity, structures bulk communities.
- **Not significant in rhizosphere**: ADONIS R² = 0.11, p = 0.15 (unweighted)
  — the rhizosphere remains primarily soil-driven.
- **Highly significant in endorhiza**: ADONIS R² = 0.59, p = 0.004 (weighted);
  R² = 0.39, p = 0.003 (unweighted) — host genotype is the dominant factor.

This gradient — increasing cultivar effect from soil to rhizosphere to root
interior — is a core prediction of the [[alpha-diversity-gradient-bulk-soil-cannabis-endorhiza]]. Its distribution across cultivars:

- **Bookoo Kush**: 13% of [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]], which are exuded into the rhizosphere and
  endorhiza at different concentrations.
- **Cell wall composition**: Pectin, lignin, and suberin content varies between
  cultivars, affecting both the chemical environment and the degradation
  products available to microbes.
- **Carbon allocation patterns**: Cultivars differ in how much photosynthate
  they allocate to root exudation vs. growth vs. defense.

### Immune Recognition Variation

Plant immune systems can distinguish between microbial taxa and respond
differentially:

- **Pattern-triggered immunity (PTI)**: Activated by conserved microbial
  molecular patterns (MAMPs). Different cultivars may express different
  pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), recognizing different MAMP repertoires.
- **Effector-triggered immunity (ETI)**: Specific gene-for-gene interactions
  that vary between cultivars. A microbe recognized as pathogenic by one
  cultivar may be tolerated by another.

### Physical Architecture Variation

Root architecture differs between cultivars and creates different microbial
habitats:

- **Root branching patterns**: Dense, highly branched root systems create more
  colonization sites and microaerobic niches.
