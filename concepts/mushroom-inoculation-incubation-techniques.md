# Mushroom Inoculation and Incubation Techniques

Inoculation is the critical step where mushroom spawn is introduced into pasteurized substrate bags. Combined with proper incubation, these stages determine the success and yield of the entire mushroom crop. Strict cleanliness and careful handling are essential to prevent contamination and ensure vigorous [[mushroom-mycelium-growth-monitoring-colonization-cues]].

## Inoculation Room Requirements

The inoculation room must be treated as a near-sterile environment. Contamination at this stage can ruin an entire batch of substrate bags.

**Key requirements:**

- **Cleanliness:** The room must be kept very clean and entirely free of diseases, molds, and pests. Any contaminants present during inoculation will compete with the [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] for nutrients and can overtake the substrate before the desired fungus is established.
- **No direct sunlight:** Avoid sunshine entering the area. UV light is not necessary and can introduce heat fluctuations; more importantly, keeping the room enclosed helps limit airborne contaminant spores from drifting in.
- **Controlled air movement:** Minimize drafts and keep doors and windows closed during inoculation to reduce the chance of airborne contamination.

**Required materials:**

| Material | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Alcohol lamp | Provides a sterile flame zone for opening spawn bottles |
| Sorghum spawn (bottle) | Grain-based spawn colonized with mushroom mycelium |
| Fire / matches | To light the alcohol lamp |
| Square paper (5" × 5") | Clean cover for the top of each inoculated bag |
| Rubber bands | Secure the paper cover over the bag opening |
| Cotton | Plugs the bag neck immediately after inoculation |
| Alcohol (70%) | Disinfects hands, tools, and substrate bag surfaces |
| Pasteurized substrate bags | Prepared substrate ready to receive spawn |

## Inoculation Procedure (13 Steps)

Follow these steps in order. Speed and minimal movement are critical once the spawn bottle is open.

1. **Clean the inoculation room thoroughly** using a broom or vacuum cleaner. After sweeping, rub the floor with a disinfecting solution to kill any remaining microorganisms.
2. **Bring the substrate bags** from the pasteurization chamber into the clean inoculation room. Allow them to cool to room temperature if they are still warm.
3. **Light the alcohol lamp** and place it on a stable surface in the inoculation area. The flame creates a sterile air column around it.
4. **Disinfect your hands and the substrate bags** by rubbing them with alcohol. Pay special attention to the neck and opening of each bag.
5. **Take the bottle of sorghum spawn** that is fully coated with mycelium (it should appear completely white with no uncolonized grain visible).
6. **Shake the bottle** firmly against a car tire or similar firm surface to release individual [[mushroom-spawn-production-sorghum-seeds]] from the mycelial mass. Bring the bottle to the inoculation room but **do not open it yet**.
7. **Flame the bottleneck** by passing it through the alcohol lamp flame. Open the bottle **very close to the flame** — this sterilizes the air entering the bottle and prevents contaminants from falling in.
8. **Quickly drop 10–20 sorghum seeds** coated with spawn into the opening of the [[mushroom-incubation]] house. Carry bags carefully without tipping or compressing them.
3. **Place bags on shelves** — they can be arranged horizontally (lying flat) or vertically (standing upright), depending on available space and mushroom species requirements. Ensure bags are not packed so tightly that air cannot circulate around them.
4. **Follow a graduated ventilation and lighting schedule:**

| Incubation Stage | Ventilation | Lighting |
|---|---|---|
| **Beginning (days 0–10)** | Very little ventilation — mycelium needs high humidity and calm air to establish | Minimal light — darkness or very dim conditions |
| **After ~10 days** | Begin ventilation to regulate temperature and prevent CO₂ buildup | Low light acceptable |
| **After 20–25 days** | Well ventilated — good airflow helps strengthen mycelium and prepares substrate for fruiting | More light for visual monitoring of colonization progress |

## Incubation Period and Harvest Schedule by Species

Different mushroom species have different colonization speeds and harvest patterns. The table below summarizes expected timelines from inoculation through the final flush.

| Species | Scientific Name | Incubation Period | Number of Flushes | Total Harvest Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oyster mushroom | *[[auricularia-polytricha]]* | 4 weeks | 5 flushes | ~15 weeks | Beware of mites on the 5th flush |
| Hed khon kao | *Lentinus squarrosulus* | 4–5+ weeks | 5 flushes | ~18 weeks | Tropical species; may need extra incubation |
| Hed Lom | *Lentinula polychrous* | 4–5+ weeks | 5 flushes | ~18 weeks | Tropical species; may need extra incubation |
| Straw mushroom | *[[paddy-straw-mushroom-volvariella-volvacea-cultivation]]* | 3–4 days mycelium + 4–5 days fruiting | 3 flushes | ~23 days | Extremely fast cycle; requires warm temperatures |

**Note:** "Flush" refers to each wave of [[aquarium-humidity-chamber-mushroom-fruiting]]. After each flush is harvested, the substrate rests before producing the next flush.

## Contamination Management

Contamination is the most common cause of crop failure. Daily vigilance is required throughout the incubation period.

### Daily Visual Inspection

Check every bag every day for signs of abnormal growth. Look for:

- **Color spots** on the substrate or inside the bag: black, green, brown, orange, or red
- **Slimy or wet patches** that differ from the surrounding substrate
- **Foul or sour odors** when bags are briefly opened near the nose
- **Abnormally fast colonization** that does not match the white, threadlike mycelium of the desired species

### Handling Contaminated Bags

When contamination is detected, **immediately separate** the affected bag from clean bags to prevent spread. Three remediation options are available:

1. **Re-pasteurize the bag** — If contamination is caught early, the bag can be returned to the pasteurization chamber and treated again to kill contaminants, then re-inoculated with fresh spawn.
2. **Open partially contaminated bags** — If only a small section is affected, carefully cut away the contaminated portion in a clean area. The remaining clean substrate may still be viable if re-inoculated.
3. **Tear off plastic and reuse the substrate** — For heavily contaminated bags, remove the plastic entirely. The sawdust or compost can be composted or, after thorough re-pasteurization, reused in a new substrate batch.

## Pasteurization Methods (Reference)

Pasteurization is performed **before** inoculation to eliminate competing organisms in the substrate. Two common methods are:

### Commercial (Boiler) Pasteurization

- Heat substrate to **98–100°C** for **3–4 hours**
- Uses a purpose-built pasteurization chamber connected to a boiler
- Provides consistent, reliable results suitable for larger operations
- Allow substrate to cool completely before inoculation

### Solar Heating Pasteurization

- Place substrate bags inside a chamber or enclosure and expose to full sun for **one entire day**
- Solar heat builds up inside the chamber, pasteurizing the contents
- Cost-effective and accessible where electricity or fuel is limited
- Effectiveness depends on weather conditions and chamber insulation

## Key Principles for Success

- **Speed is safety:** The faster inoculation is completed, the less time contaminants have to enter open bags. Practice the procedure before attempting a full batch.
- **Consistent monitoring:** Check incubation bags daily. Early detection of contamination gives the best chance of saving substrate.
- **Species-specific timing:** Not all mushrooms grow at the same rate. Adjust expectations based on the species table above and local environmental conditions.
- **[[fungal-herbarium-record-keeping-specimen-transactions]]:** Track inoculation dates, species, batch numbers, and any contamination events. This helps identify recurring problems and improve future yields.
