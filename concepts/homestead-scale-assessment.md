# Homestead Scale Assessment

Determining the right scale for a homestead operation depends on goals, available resources, land, labor, and risk tolerance. This guide defines four common scales -- hobby, side hustle, part-time farm, and full-time farm -- with revenue benchmarks, resource requirements, and decision criteria for each level.

## The Four Scales

### 1. Hobby Homestead
- Revenue: $0-2,000/year
- Time commitment: 5-15 hours/week
- Primary goal: personal food production, learning, enjoyment
- Products: home consumption with occasional surplus given away or sold informally
- Labor: self and family only
- Land: 0.25-2 acres
- Investment: $500-5,000 startup
- Tax implications: generally not reportable (below self-employment threshold); cannot claim farm deductions
- Insurance: standard homeowner's policy likely sufficient
- Risk: minimal financial risk; time and emotional investment only

### 2. Side Hustle Homestead
- Revenue: $2,000-15,000/year
- Time commitment: 10-25 hours/week (evenings and weekends)
- Primary goal: supplemental income, cost recovery, building skills
- Products: eggs, produce, baked goods, value-added products sold at market or online
- Labor: self + family; occasional volunteer help
- Land: 1-5 acres
- Investment: $2,000-15,000 startup
- Tax implications: file Schedule F, track expenses, quarterly estimated payments
- Insurance: farm liability policy recommended (market requirement)
- Risk: moderate; losses are manageable but noticeable

### 3. Part-Time Farm
- Revenue: $15,000-50,000/year
- Time commitment: 25-45 hours/week (one partner working off-farm)
- Primary goal: significant income, building toward full-time, professional operation
- Products: diversified: produce, eggs, meat, value-added products, CSA shares
- Labor: self + family + 1-3 part-time employees or regular volunteers
- Land: 5-25 acres
- Investment: $15,000-75,000 in infrastructure, equipment, animals
- Tax implications: Schedule F, significant deductions, consider LLC, professional tax help
- Insurance: full farm liability, property, workers comp (if employees)
- Risk: significant; livelihood partially dependent on farm income

### 4. Full-Time Farm
- Revenue: $50,000-200,000+/year
- Time commitment: 50-70+ hours/week
- Primary goal: primary income source, profitable business, long-term career
- Products: diversified production, wholesale accounts, CSAs, markets, online, agritourism
- Labor: farm owner(s) + 2-10+ employees (seasonal and year-round)
- Land: 10-100+ acres
- Investment: $50,000-500,000+ in land, infrastructure, equipment
- Tax implications: complex; LLC or S-Corp, professional bookkeeping and tax preparation
- Insurance: comprehensive coverage (liability, property, crop, workers comp, umbrella)
- Risk: high; financial livelihood depends on farm success

## Assessing Your Situation

### Key Questions
1. What are your goals? (food security, income, lifestyle, community)
2. How much time can you commit? (be realistic about other obligations)
3. How much capital do you have available? (savings, loans, family support)
4. How much land do you have or can you access? (own, lease, borrow)
5. What are your skills and experience? (farming, business, marketing)
6. What is your risk tolerance? (can you absorb a bad year?)
7. Who is your market? (local, regional, online)
8. What is your timeline? (building a farm takes 3-7 years to reach full potential)

### Resource Assessment Matrix

Score each category 1-5 (1 = very limited, 5 = abundant):

- Available time (hours/week available for farm work)
- Capital (money available for startup and operations)
- Land (acreage, quality, infrastructure)
- Skills and experience
- Market access (proximity to customers)
- Support network (family, mentors, community)
- Physical health and energy
- Business/marketing skills

Total score interpretation:
- 8-16: start at hobby level, focus on learning
- 17-24: side hustle is achievable, plan for growth
- 25-32: part-time farm is realistic with planning
- 33-40: full-time farm is possible but still requires 3-5 years to build

## Growth Path

### Year 1: Foundation
- Start at hobby or side hustle level
- Focus on 2-3 products that match your skills and market
- Build infrastructure incrementally
- Establish market presence (1 farmers market, online presence)
- Track all income and expenses
- Learn what grows well and what sells

### Year 2-3: Expansion
- Add products based on demand and skill
- Increase production of best sellers
- Hire first help (part-time or volunteer)
- Add CSA if produce-focused
- Improve infrastructure (irrigation, fencing, storage)
- Diversify sales channels
- Revenue target: $15,000-30,000

### Year 3-5: Professionalization
- Formalize business structure (LLC)
- Hire employees or long-term apprentices
- Expand to part-time or full-time (see [[homestead-labor-and-volunteers]])
- Wholesale accounts with restaurants and stores
- Comprehensive insurance and tax planning (see [[homestead-financial-planning]], [[homestead-insurance-guide]])
- Revenue target: $30,000-75,000

### Year 5+: Maturation
- Full-time farm income achievable
- Land acquisition or long-term lease
- Equipment upgrades
- Niche specialization or diversification
- Possible agritourism, education, or consulting revenue
- Mentor others
- Revenue target: $75,000+
