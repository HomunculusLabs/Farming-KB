# Plant Nursery and Seedling Production

Plant nurseries are foundational infrastructure in [[bill-mollison]] discusses nursery systems in Permaculture: A Designers'
Manual (Chapter 6: Trees and Their [[permaculture-energy-cycling-and-resource-efficiency]]. Common options include:

  - Modular seedling trays: Standard 128-cell or 200-cell plastic
    trays for germinating large numbers of seedlings efficiently
  - Tubes (forestry tubes): Deep, narrow containers (5-15 cm deep)
    that encourage downward root growth and reduce circling, ideal
    for tree seedlings and deep-rooted species
  - Punnets and small pots: 5-10 cm diameter for vegetable and herb
    seedlings destined for garden transplanting
  - Large bags: 15-30 cm bags for advanced tree seedlings requiring
    longer nursery periods before field planting
  - Air-pruning containers: Pots with perforated sides or raised
    bottoms that air-prune roots at the container wall, preventing
    circling and promoting a dense, fibrous root system

compost-based fertility is generally sufficient for the first 4-8
weeks of growth.

## Seed Treatment and Germination

Many permaculture species require pre-treatment to break dormancy:
  - Stratification: Cold-moist treatment (refrigeration in moist
    medium for 30-90 days) for temperate tree seeds such as apples,
    pears, and many nuts
  - Scarification: Nicking, sanding, or acid treatment of hard seed
    coats (acacia, carob, [[permaculture-honey-locust-pod-fodder-system]], tagasaste)
  - Hot water treatment: Pouring boiling water over seeds and soaking
    for 12-24 hours for leguminous species with impermeable coats
