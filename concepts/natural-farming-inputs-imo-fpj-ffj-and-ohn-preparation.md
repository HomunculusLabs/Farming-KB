## Natural Farming Inputs: IMO, FPJ, FFJ, and OHN Preparation

This concept covers the practical preparation methods for Dr. Cho"s core Natural Farming inputs: [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]] (IMO) at multiple stages, [[fermented-plant-juice]] (FPJ), Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ), and [[oriental-herbal-nutrient]] (OHN). These inputs form the backbone of the NF system, replacing all [[cannabis-chemical-fertilizers]] and pesticides.

## Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO) Multi-Stage Preparation

### IMO-1: Initial Collection
Collected from native soil using steamed rice in wooden boxes buried in forest/field locations for 2-6 days depending on temperature. Mixed 1:1 with jaggery/brown sugar.

### IMO-2: Expansion
IMO-1 mixed with equal weight rice bran at 65-70% moisture, covered with straw for 5-7 days. The rice bran should "lump when pressed and loosen when shaken."

### IMO-3: Mass Cultivation
**Materials**: Water, rice bran/flour, paddy straw, IMO-2, NF inputs diluted 1:1000

**Procedure**:
1. Dilute IMO-2 with water (1:1000) and mix with rice bran or flour at 65-70% moisture
2. Stack on **soil floor** (not concrete) in heaps 30-40cm high
3. Cover with paddy straw; maintain 70% shade, 30% light
4. [[cervantes-reservoir-temperature-management]] is critical:
   - **Below 40°C**: Anaerobic condition, excessive moisture
   - **40-50°C**: Ideal — turn the heap
   - **Above 70°C**: Proteins broken down by thermophiles, nutrients lost
5. Takes 5-7 days; surface covered with whitish spores when complete
6. Emits pleasant smell (Absida, Aspergillus, Mucor, Rhizopus)
7. Moisture drops from 65-70% to ~40%

**Key Microbes**: Aspergillus oryzae, [[bacillus-subtilis]]

**Storage**: In jute/gunny/cloth bags in shaded, cool place. IMOs enter dormant phase at 20-30% moisture. No turning needed during storage.

## Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ)

### Principle
Extracts nutrients from fast-growing plant tips during their peak growth period. The plant's growth energy is captured in the [[mushroom-compost-fermentation-process]].

### Materials
- Plant parts (shoot tips, young leaves of fast-growing plants)
- Brown sugar/jaggery (1:1 ratio by weight)
- Clay pot, glass jar, or PE container
- Porous paper cover

### Key Plants and Uses

| Growth Stage | Recommended FPJ | Dilution |
|-------------|----------------|----------|
| Germination/early vegetative | Mugwort, bamboo shoot | 1:1000 |
| Vegetative growth | Arrowroot, bamboo shoot, reeds | 1:800-1:1000 |
| Pest presence | Any FPJ + rice bran sprinkle | As needed |

### Critical Timing Rules
- **Pick plants before sunrise** when plant moisture is at its highest
- Pick **2-3 days after rain** (rain washes away lactic acid bacteria and yeast)
- Avoid picking during or after rainfall
- Avoid picking near roadsides (pollution)
- Avoid picking during excessive heat or rain

### Important Notes
- **Do NOT use molasses** — too much moisture, insufficient [[fungal-fruiting-body-mechanics-osmotic-pressure]]
- **Do NOT wash ingredients** — natural surface microorganisms needed for fermentation
- Fill jar only **2/3 full** for gas expansion space
- Small bubbles or fungi indicate insufficient brown sugar or unbalanced volume

### Storage
- Optimum: 1-15°C (refrigerator) for up to 1 year
- Room temperature: use within 30 days
- Brown glass containers preferred
- Cool, shaded place; no direct sunlight or temperature fluctuations

## Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ)

### Principle
Similar to FPJ but uses fruits instead of plant parts. Captures the fruit's nutritional peak.

### Key Differences from FPJ
- Uses sweet fruits at their peak ripeness
- Same 1:1 ratio with brown sugar
- Same fermentation and storage principles
- Used particularly during reproductive/fruiting growth stages

## Oriental Herbal Nutrient (OHN)

### Components
OHN is made from three herbal ingredients:
1. **Garlic** — whole bulb crushed (including skins and roots for moisture content)
2. **Ginger** — crushed with soil shaken off (not washed)
3. **Cinnamon** — bark preparation

### Preparation Method
1. Crush 1kg of garlic or ginger (not too finely)
2. Add 1kg jaggery (1:1 ratio) in a jar filled **2/3 full**
3. Cover with porous paper; leave 4-6 days
4. Stir gently clockwise every morning for a week
5. Filter and keep extraction in another jar
6. Add distilled liquor for long-term storage (or water for 45-day use)

### Dilution and Application
- Base dilution: **1:1000**
- Mix all three OHN types (garlic, ginger, cinnamon) in **1:1:1:1000** ratio
- Used throughout the entire Nutritive Cycle
- Can be combined with FPJ (1:500) and BRV (1:500) for weakened crops
- Add WCA (1:1000) for plants with soft rot or anthracnose

### Uses
- Making IMO-3 and IMO-4
- Soil treatment solution
- Seed treatment solution
- All growth periods: nutritional, changeover, and reproductive

## Integration: The Nutritive Cycle

These inputs are not applied randomly but follow the **Nutritive Cycle Theory**:
- **Early growth**: FPJ at lower concentration (1:1000)
- **Vegetative growth**: FPJ for nitrogen and volume increase
- **Changeover period**: Adjust input ratios
- **Reproductive/fruiting**: FFJ and WS-K for fruit quality
- **Throughout**: OHN as a general tonic and disease preventive

## Key Principles Across All Inputs

1. **Local materials**: Everything can be sourced from the farm or neighborhood
2. **No washing**: Preserve natural surface microorganisms
3. **2/3 rule**: Never fill containers more than 2/3 to allow gas expansion
4. **Brown sugar over molasses**: Higher osmotic pressure for better fermentation
5. **Cool, dark storage**: Extends shelf life significantly
6. **Flexible substitution**: Farmers can experiment with local plant alternatives
7. **Timing matters**: Harvest plant materials at their peak growth energy
