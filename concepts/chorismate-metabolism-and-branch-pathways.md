# Chorismate Metabolism and Branch Pathways

Chorismate is a central metabolic intermediate produced at the end
of the [[tryptamine-family-overview]] alkaloids,

### 2. Phenylalanine Biosynthesis

Chorismate mutase catalyzes the pericyclic Claisen rearrangement
of chorismate to prephenate, which is subsequently converted to
phenylpyruvate and then to phenylalanine by transamination. In
fungi, chorismate mutase exists in both monofunctional and
bifunctional forms (fused with prephenate dehydratase). Phenylalanine
serves as a precursor to phenylpropanoids, flavonoids, lignin, and
numerous [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] including various fungal pigments.

### 3. Tyrosine Biosynthesis

Prephenate can also be converted to 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate via
prephenate dehydrogenase (or the equivalent arogenate pathway in
some fungi), and then transaminated to form tyrosine. Tyrosine is
the precursor to dopa, dopamine, melanin, and the catecholamine
neurotransmitters. In [[singh-fungal-metabolism-pahs]], tyrosine-derived melanin
is a critical cell-wall component providing UV protection and
structural integrity.

### 4. Folate (Vitamin B9) Biosynthesis

Chorismate serves as the precursor for para-aminobenzoic acid
(pABA) through the action of aminodeoxychorismate synthase and
lyase. pABA is then conjugated with pteridine diphosphate and
glutamate to form folate cofactors. Folate derivatives (THF) are
essential carriers of one-carbon units in nucleotide biosynthesis
and amino acid metabolism. The folate branch is essential for
fungal cell division and is a target of antifungal agents such
as sulfonamides.

### 5. Ubiquinone (Coenzyme Q) Biosynthesis

Chorismate contributes the benzoquinone ring of ubiquinone
through a series of reactions involving prenylation, hydroxylation,
methylation, and decarboxylation steps. Ubiquinone functions as an
essential electron carrier in the mitochondrial respiratory chain,
participating in both complex II (succinate dehydrogenase) and
complex III (cytochrome bc1) electron transport. This branch is
indispensable for aerobic energy metabolism in fungi.

## Regulation of Chorismate Partitioning

The distribution of chorismate among its five branch pathways is
regulated through multiple mechanisms:

- **Feedback inhibition:** End products of each branch inhibit
  their respective first committed enzymes. Tryptophan inhibits
  anthranilate synthase, phenylalanine inhibits chorismate mutase
  (via prephenate dehydratase), and tyrosine inhibits prephenate
  dehydrogenase.

- **Transcriptional regulation:** The expression of genes encoding
  branch-specific enzymes is modulated by [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]]
  and end-product concentrations. In fungi, the general control
  response (GCN pathway) coordinates amino acid biosynthetic genes.

- **Enzyme compartmentalization:** Some fungi localize different
  branch enzymes to distinct subcellular compartments, providing
  spatial regulation of chorismate flux.

- **Enzyme isoforms:** Multiple isoforms of chorismate mutase and
  other branch-point enzymes with different regulatory properties
  allow fine-tuning of metabolic flux.
