## Fermentation Is a Biological Requirement

The necessity of fermentation lies in its biological effects rather than its chemical
improvements. Fresh manure contains highly soluble and relatively unstable organic products
that support intense bacterial activity. In this environment of rapid bacterial proliferation,
mushroom mycelium cannot establish itself — and this inhibition is not merely due to the
elevated temperatures that accompany bacterial fermentation.

Duggar proved this by using manure in quantities so small that no significant temperature
increase occurred, yet the mycelium still failed to grow in the fresh, unfermented material.
This established that the competition from bacteria, not heat, was the primary inhibiting
factor.
