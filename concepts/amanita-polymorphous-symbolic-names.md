# Polymorphous Amanita muscaria: The Mushroom of Many Names

James Arthur compiled a list of over forty symbolic names and representations for the Amanita muscaria mushroom, drawn from diverse cultures and historical periods. This extraordinary proliferation of names for a single species reflects what Arthur calls the "polymorphous" nature of the mushroom, its ability to be represented through virtually unlimited symbolic forms while maintaining a consistent core identity across all of them. The mushroom is, in Arthur's phrase, "the God of a thousand names."

This polymorphous symbolism is central to the arguments in [[mythological-mushroom-symbolism]] and ethnomycology.

## The Complete Symbolic Inventory

Arthur's list of Amanita muscaria representations includes the following:

Soma, the Hindu plant-god (Soma is also Greek for "body").

Amrita, the Buddhist magical sacrament and elixir of immortality.

Ambrosia, the Greek "food of the gods."

The Holy Grail, note the Grail-like shape of the upturned muscaria mushroom as a "vessel containing the blood of the God."

Fruit of the Tree of Life, the mushroom being literally the fruit that grows from the mycelium attached to tree roots in a symbiotic relationship with the tree.

The Golden Fleece, sought by Jason and the Argonauts.

The Fountain of Youth, with the red muscaria growing under Longleaf Pine in northern Florida in December, which Ponce de Leon may have unknowingly encountered.

Haoma, the Islamic/Zoroastrian sacrament.

Manna, meaning "mushroom," with two kinds described in the Bible (John chapters 6 through 14).

Bread of Life, described as the "loaf of bliss."

Fountain of Living Waters, the mushroom being alive, composed of 90% water, and shaped like a fountain.

Hidden Manna, referenced in Revelation 2:17.

The Cosmic Egg and the Easter Egg, encoding the mushroom hunt tradition.

The Prima Materia and Philosopher's Stone, the secret substance of the alchemists.

The Flesh of God, the mushroom being very flesh-like in its texture and appearance.

The Fruit of the Tree of Knowledge, identified in original traditions as the same substance as the Fruit of the Tree of Life.

The Flesh of Jesus and other Gods, connected through the statement "Take and eat, This is my body" (Greek: "SOMA").

The Hammer of Thor, obvious in its mushroom shape, thrown to earth in a bolt of lightning.

The Small White Stone, the infant button state of the mushroom resembling a small white stone.

The Elixir of Immortality, produced through the churning of the Milky Ocean.

The Feathered Serpent, progressing from egg-state through snake-like second state to feathered final state.

The Phoenix, cycling from ashes (spores) through egg to gold-and-red bird and back to ashes.

The Ankh, representing "waters and life" or "the waters of life" in Egyptian tradition.

Rudra, the Hindu red god of the forest.

The Djed, the phallus or pillar of Osiris.

The One-Eyed Howler, the round eye shape representing the vision of the universe.

The Eye of Horus (Djed-Eye), connecting the Djed pillar with the eye symbolism.

The World Tree, the mushroom thought to be the creator of the world in many cultures.

Celestial Food, the food of the gods in the Egyptian Book of the Dead.

The Aten, the Egyptian winged disc.

The Fly Agaric, where "Fly" refers not only to the house-fly but to the act of "flying" in spiritual travel.

The Star-Fire, the "gold of the gods" and "red gold" of Sumerian tradition.

The Christmas Tree ornament, with red and white colors under a green tree.

## The Polymorphous Principle

The key insight underlying this proliferation of symbols is that the Amanita muscaria is "polymorphous" in its symbolism, meaning it can be represented through an extraordinary variety of forms, each capturing a different aspect of its nature. The mushroom's lifecycle (egg, serpent, feathered creature, phoenix), its appearance (red cap, white spots, stem, volva, gills), its habitat (under trees, emerging after rain), its preparation (drying by fire), its effects (vision, flight, death-and-rebirth, knowledge), and its biology (mycorrhizal relationship, spore reproduction) each provide material for symbolic representation.

Because the mushroom shares features with so many other natural objects and processes (eggs, stones, snakes, birds, trees, fountains, cups, hammers, fire), it can be encoded within virtually any symbolic system. This polymorphous quality is what has allowed the mushroom to persist as a hidden presence within religious art and mythology across thousands of years and across virtually every civilization.

## The Mushroom as "God of a Thousand Names"

The Egyptian term "the God of a thousand names" takes on literal significance in Arthur's framework. As research encompasses more traditions, mythologies, and religions, the mushroom appears under an ever-expanding list of names. Each culture that used the mushroom created its own symbolic vocabulary for it, drawing on local natural features, cultural associations, and artistic conventions. Yet beneath the enormous diversity of names and symbols, the same core set of characteristics recurs: the connection to trees, the red-and-white coloration, the death-and-rebirth lifecycle, the visionary effects, the association with knowledge and immortality.

This consistency beneath diversity is what Arthur identifies as the signature of a genuine entheogenic practice. Cultural diffusion alone cannot explain why cultures with no known contact independently developed symbolic systems that encode the same specific set of mushroom characteristics.

## Multiple Representations in Single Artworks

Arthur notes that these mushroom representations are frequently used "severally in one depiction," meaning that multiple mushroom symbols appear together in single works of religious art. A single painting might contain the Grail cup shape, the feathered serpent, the world tree, and the celestial food, all referring to the same underlying mushroom sacrament.

This layering of symbols creates what Arthur calls a "secret language" embedded in religious art, accessible only to those who understand the individual symbols and their common reference. Artists who were "in the know" could communicate the entheogenic truth through their work while maintaining the surface-level religious or mythological interpretation for the uninitiated.

## The Five-Color Theme Across Traditions

A recurring pattern in mushroom symbolism is the five-color theme: red, white, blue, green, and gold/yellow. This color combination appears in Christmas decorations (red, white, green), Hindu temple art at Angkor Wat (where mushroom caps appear in multiple colors), Buddhist Vajrayana ritual objects, medieval Christian illuminated manuscripts, and numerous other sacred art traditions.

Arthur interprets this five-color theme as representing the five-plant Soma combination: Amanita muscaria (red), Psilocybe species (blue), opium poppy (white/gold), Syrian Rue (brown/green), and cannabis or other green DMT-containing plants. The persistence of this color pattern across cultures and millennia supports the theory of a shared entheogenic tradition that used a multi-plant sacramental brew.

## Implications for Religious History

The polymorphous nature of Amanita muscaria symbolism has profound implications for understanding the history of religion. If Arthur's identification is correct, then a significant portion of the world's religious art, mythology, and ritual encodes references to entheogenic mushroom use that have been hidden in plain sight for thousands of years. The "secrets" guarded by priestly classes, secret societies, and initiatory orders may refer not to abstract theological truths but to the concrete knowledge of a psychoactive natural substance and its visionary effects.

The transition from genuine entheogenic experience to symbolic ritual, which Arthur traces through multiple traditions, represents one of the most significant transformations in human spiritual history, one that has largely been rendered invisible by the very process of symbolic substitution that replaced the original sacrament.
