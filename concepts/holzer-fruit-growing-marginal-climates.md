## Growing Fruit Above the Conventional Limit

According to expert opinion, fruit growing ends at 1,000m above sea level in Lungau. Holzer cultivates a large variety of [[holzer-old-varieties-and-seed-saving]]. He has even grown apricots, sweet chestnuts, grapes, and kiwi fruit in particularly warm and sheltered areas.

### Key Advantages of High-Altitude Fruit

- Fruit develops very intense flavour due to harsh nights (highly valued by distillers)
- Ripening times shift later: cherries ripen in September at altitude vs. June at lower elevations
- Less competition in the market when most other growers' harvest is long over
- Health-conscious consumers and specialists pay premium prices

## Holzer's No-Prune Method

This is perhaps Holzer's most controversial technique:

### Why He Does Not Prune
- Unpruned trees retain branch resilience -- branches can sink to the ground under fruit or snow weight without breaking
- Trees stabilise themselves and are less likely to grow at an angle
- When branches sink under fruit weight, sunlight reaches the crown naturally
- Pruning creates wounds that invite [[holzer-browsing-protection-methods]]:
- Ground-level twigs: eaten by hares
- Middle twigs: eaten by roe deer
- Upper twigs: eaten by red deer
- Animals feed at their natural height, leaving the trunk undamaged

## The Shock Method

An emergency transplant technique for bare-root trees in full leaf, flower, or fruit:

1. Lay trees in the sun so leaves dry out; keep roots covered with a wet jute sack
2. After ~1 day, leaves are dry; replant without soaking soil or watering afterwards
3. Protect only with a mulch layer to retain moisture
4. Trees concentrate energy on root development rather than supporting leaves
5. New fibrous roots develop quickly; trees become independent
6. Best results when planted between raised beds (moisture collects in hollows)

Holzer has planted thousands of trees this way, including remainder stock from nurseries purchased cheaply.
