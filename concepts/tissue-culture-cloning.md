# Tissue Culture and Cloning

Tissue culture (cloning) is the process of isolating living mycelium from the interior flesh of a mushroom fruitbody and transferring it to a sterile growth medium. This preserves the exact genetic identity (dikaryon) of the parent mushroom, unlike multispore inoculation which produces genetic diversity.

## Why Clone

- **Genetic consistency**: Preserves the exact dikaryotic genotype of a proven [[cold-shock-fruiting-strain-specificity]]
- **Fruiting guarantee**: Unlike multispore cultures, a dikaryotic tissue isolate is already mated and capable of fruiting (bypassing the illegitimate mating problem)
- **Speed**: Mycelium from tissue culture is typically visible on agar within 3-7 days, versus 3-14 days for [[growing-gourmet-spore-germination-strain-isolation]]
- **[[mushroom-strain-improvement-and-breeding-methods]]**: Enables selection of the best-performing individuals from a population

## Tissue Isolation Procedure

### Materials

- Fresh, healthy mushroom specimen
- Flame-sterilized scalpel or biopsy tool
- Prepared agar plates (MEA or PDA)
- Alcohol lamp or butane torch
- Glove box or [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]] hood
- 70% isopropyl alcohol

### Specimen Selection

Select a mushroom that exhibits desirable characteristics:
- Vigorous growth and large size
- Normal morphology (no abnormalities or disease symptoms)
- Collected at or near maturity (younger specimens have less differentiated tissue)
- Cap not yet fully expanded for species where veil integrity matters

### Surface Sterilization

The exterior of the mushroom is heavily contaminated with spores and microorganisms. Surface sterilization reduces but does not eliminate this contamination:

1. Dip the mushroom or the section to be excised in 70% isopropyl alcohol for 10-30 seconds
2. Briefly flame the exterior to burn off remaining alcohol (optional, for thick-fleshed species)
3. Work quickly to minimize recontamination

### Tissue Excision

1. Tear or cut the mushroom in half longitudinally to expose the interior flesh
2. The interior of a fresh, healthy mushroom is essentially sterile
3. Using a flame-sterilized scalpel, excise a small piece of tissue (2-3mm cube) from the cap flesh, stem interior, or the junction of cap and stem
4. Cap flesh is preferred for most species as it contains the most actively growing hyphae

### Transfer to Agar

1. Flame-sterilize the scalpel between cuts
2. Open the Petri dish minimally
3. Place the tissue piece onto the center of the agar surface
4. Close the dish and seal with parafilm or tape
5. Incubate at the species' optimal temperature for [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]]
6. Visible mycelial growth typically appears within 3-7 days

### Contamination Management

If bacteria or mold appear on the plate alongside the mushroom tissue, the tissue piece can be re-transferred to a fresh plate. Mycelial growth radiating from the tissue is cut ahead of any advancing contaminant and moved to clean media. This serial transfer may need to be repeated several times to obtain a pure culture.

## Tissue Culture vs. Spore Culture

| Characteristic | Tissue Culture | Spore Culture |
|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| Genetics | Identical to parent | New genetic combinations |
| Fruiting ability | Guaranteed (dikaryon) | Variable (illegitimate matings possible) |
| Growth speed on agar | Fast (3-7 days) | Slower (3-14 days for germination) |
| Genetic diversity | None | Maximum |
| Strain improvement potential | None (preservation only) | High (new combinations) |
| Best used for | Preserving proven strains | Developing new strains |

## Strain Preservation

Once a clean tissue culture is established on agar, it should be subcultured periodically (every 1-2 months) onto fresh agar to maintain vitality. Long-term storage options include:

- **Refrigeration**: Slant cultures at 35-40°F slow growth dramatically; subculture every 6-12 months
- **Mineral oil overlay**: Sterile mineral oil poured over the agar surface prevents desiccation and limits gas exchange, extending viability to 1-2 years
- **Freezing**: Mycelium in glycerol solution at -80°C for long-term preservation
- **Lyophilization**: Freeze-drying for very long-term storage (more complex)

## Practical Workflow

For cultivators seeking reliable, repeatable results:
1. Start with multispore germination to generate genetic diversity
2. Grow to fruiting on substrate
3. Select the best-performing mushroom(s)
4. Take tissue cultures from selected specimens
5. Test tissue isolates for vigor, colonization speed, and fruiting consistency
6. Maintain the best isolate as a master strain
7. Periodically re-fruit and re-clone to refresh the culture
