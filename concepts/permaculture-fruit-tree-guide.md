# Permaculture Fruit Tree Guide

Fruit trees form the canopy layer of [[permaculture-energy-systems]], providing long-term yields with minimal maintenance once established. This guide covers key species from the Permaculture Plants reference suited to diverse climates and [[permaculture-climatic-zones-design]] placement.

## Figs (Ficus spp)
Family: MORACEAE. One of the most ancient plants in civilization, with ~700 varieties of Ficus carica alone. Deciduous, 5-10 m, spread to 25 m. Frost tolerant to -10C. Tolerant of drought; better fruit quality with ample water. Begin bearing at 2-3 years; can live 100 years. Two crops per year in some climates -- first crop tends to be larger, second more numerous. Restricting roots improves fruit quality -- excellent for container growing.

Propagation: Layering or cuttings from short-jointed shoots in spring. 15-25 cm cuttings in sandy soil in warmth. Sugar content rises from 12% to 50% on drying. Best dried by letting them partially dry on the tree, then drop naturally to trays.

Key cultivars:
- **Brown Turkey** -- Medium, mahogany brown with purple tinge. Rich flavour, cold hardy.
- **Celeste** -- Violet coloured, light coloured flesh, firm, juicy, sweet.
- **Conadria** -- Vigorous, warmer climates. Best variety for drying.
- **Desert King** -- Cooler climates. Large, dark green, excellent quality.
- **Kadota** -- Hot climates. Yellowish green, tough skin. Popular for commercial canning.
- **Mission** (Black Mission) -- Dependable, excellent flavour. Purplish black, fresh/dried/canned.
- **White Genoa** -- Cooler areas, very hardy. Whitish skin, excellent fresh eating.

Medicinal: Mild laxative. Mixed with senna for stronger preparations. Roasted split figs used as poultice for dental abscesses and boils. Milky juice applied to warts. Figs are fire retardant.

## Mulberries (Morus spp)
Family: MORACEAE. Small to medium, long-lived (300 years), deciduous. Hardy, drought resistant, frost tolerant, shade tolerant, suited to most soils. Heavy bearers at 19-24 t/ha. Fruit delicious raw; can be dried and powdered into cooking flour. Self-harvest pig food supporting 2-3 pigs per tree (86 trees/ha, 60-day season). Excellent poultry forage. Coppices readily.

- **Morus alba** (white mulberry) -- to 25 m, traditional silkworm food. Leaves: 15.1% crude protein, 5.7% fat. On well-drained soils, one of the best trees for cultivation. Bark used for paper and textile fibre. Wood used for house building, furniture, tool handles, turnery.
- **Morus nigra** (black mulberry) -- to 12 m, timber prized for turnery and carving.
- **Morus rubra** (red mulberry) -- native to eastern/central USA. Fruit for preserves, jellies, pies.

Good companion to [[permaculture-vine-crops]], for which they also form a trellis.

## Persimmon (Diospyros kaki)
Deciduous, 5 m. Striking autumn foliage. Prefers medium loam, tolerates some waterlogging but not dry or acid soils. Frost hardy to -10C. Orange fruit, slightly larger than tomato -- must be fully ripe before eating (unripe is very astringent due to tannic acid). Eaten fresh, dried, or made into jam. Bears at ~3 years (grafted). Dioecious -- needs male + female. Minimum spacing 5 m. Seedlings variable -- buy grafted for true-to-type. Other species: D. virginiana (American, smaller yellow fruit) and D. lotus (date plum, 1-2 cm purple-black).

## Cherimoya (Annona cherimola)
Andean native, semi-evergreen to 9 m. Does best in warm areas outside the tropics with high humidity during flowering. Frost tolerant to -3C once established. Deep, fertile, light-textured soil preferred. Hand pollination common. Flesh is a cross between pineapple and strawberry. First crop at 3 years (grafted). Companion pollinator: A. senegatensis. Related species include A. atemoya (custard apple) to 8-10 m, and A. squamosa (sweetsop).

## Blueberries (Vaccinium spp)
Family: ERICACEAE. Small bush to small tree (0.3-6 m), deciduous, native to North America. Require well-drained, very acid soil (pH 4.0-5.5) with plenty of humus. Propagate from seed or cuttings. Excellent bee forage. See [[permaculture-berry-crops]] for full berry crop profiles.

## Feijoa (Feijoa sellowiana)
Family: MYRTACEAE. Evergreen shrub or small tree. Good companions: tagasaste, lucerne. Tolerant of a range of conditions. Fruit eaten fresh or made into preserves.

## Guava (Psidium guajava)
Family: MYRTACEAE. Small evergreen 2-10 m. Tolerant of drought (4-5 months dry), periodic flooding, and somewhat salt tolerant. Fruit contains 2-5 times the Vitamin C of fresh orange juice; also a good source of Vitamin A. Grown from seed, cuttings, or suckers; 70% of seedlings are true to type. Minimum spacing 3 m. Leaves are used medicinally for gastroenteritis, diarrhoea, and dysentery. Heavy wood useful for tool handles and firewood. See [[permaculture-berry-crops]] for additional guava species.

## Pawpaw (Asimina triloba)
Family: ANNONACEAE. Not a true pawpaw but related to custard apples. Deciduous shrub or low tree to 12 m, native to eastern North America. Produces the largest native fruit of North America (up to 300 g). Creamy, custard-like flavour reminiscent of banana custard. Grows naturally as a thicket-forming understory species in open woodland. Wood ash is a good fertiliser for pawpaw plantings. Fruit composition: carbohydrate 16.8%, protein 5.2%. Seeds contain the asimine alkaloid. Propagation from seed (requires cold stratification), grafting, root cuttings, or layering. Cross-pollination between different clones is beneficial for good fruit set.

## Soursop (Annona muricata)
Family: ANNONACEAE. Small upright tree to 5 m. Drought tolerant; makes a good windbreak and shade tree. Largest of the custard apples with a more acid flavour -- often best stewed with sugar. Produces abundant, sweet juice. Bears fruit from approximately 3 years from seed or grafted stock. Seeds have insecticidal properties and can be used in [[insectary-plants-for-pest-control]] strategies.

## Carob (Ceratonia siliqua)
Family: CAESALPINIACEAE. The largest of Europe’s leguminous trees, reaching up to 15 m. Mediterranean native, growing in areas with as little as 250 mm annual rainfall. Prefers slightly alkaline sandy soils. Exceptionally long-lived (500+ years). Dioecious -- both male and female trees are needed for pod production. Pods are highly nutritious: crude protein 2.25-21%, total sugars 37.1-56.6%. Excellent stockfeed and a popular chocolate substitute. Tap roots have been traced to 20 m depth. Fire retardant -- useful in [[fire-resistant-plants]]. Good companion to grapes, tagasaste, and Albizia. Direct seeding is possible, making it easy to establish in [[permaculture-ground-cover-plants]].

This nutritional diversity highlights the value of selecting a range of fruit tree species in [[food-forest-layers-and-species]]. Species like fig (especially dried) provide dense caloric energy, while guava and persimmon deliver exceptional vitamin content. ## Design Notes

- Interplant fruit trees with [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] like tagasaste, Acacia, and Albizia for sustained fertility
- Use [[permaculture-companion-planting]] with garlic, chives, and tagasaste beneath most fruit trees
- Place grafted fruit trees near the house (Zone 1-2) for maximum attention and harvest convenience
- Seedling trees planted closer together can be selectively thinned as production characteristics become known
- Many fruit trees benefit from restricted root zones for better quality fruit production
- Select diverse cultivars to extend harvest seasons and reduce pest pressure
- Incorporate [[permaculture-livestock-forage-systems]] like mulberry, carob, and fig for integrated animal systems
- Use [[permaculture-herb-plants]] plants to suppress weeds and attract beneficial insects
- Practice [[chop-and-drop-mulching]] with leguminous tree prunings to build soil fertility over time
- Consider [[salt-tolerant-plant-guide]] when selecting species for coastal sites
- Apply [[zone-and-sector-analysis]] to position fruit trees for optimal microclimate advantage
