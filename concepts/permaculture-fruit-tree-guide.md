# Permaculture Fruit Tree Guide Fruit trees form the canopy layer of permaculture-climatic-zones-design placement. ## Figs (Ficus spp) Family: MORACEAE. One of the most ancient plants in civilization, with ~700 varieties of Ficus carica alone. Deciduous, 5-10 m, spread to 25 m. Frost tolerant to -10C. Tolerant of drought; better fruit quality with ample water. Begin bearing at 2-3 years; can live 100 years. Two crops per year in some climates -- first crop tends to be larger, second more numerous. Restricting roots improves fruit quality -- excellent for container growing. Propagation: Layering or cuttings from short-jointed shoots in spring. 15-25 cm cuttings in sandy soil in warmth. Sugar content rises from 12% to 50% on drying. Best dried by letting them partially dry on the tree, then drop naturally to trays. Key cultivars: - **Brown Turkey** -- Medium, mahogany brown with purple tinge. Rich flavour, cold hardy. - **Celeste** -- Violet coloured, light coloured flesh, firm, juicy, sweet. - **Conadria** -- Vigorous, warmer climates. Best variety for drying. - **Desert King** -- Cooler climates. Large, dark green, excellent quality. - **Kadota** -- Hot climates. Yellowish green, tough skin. Popular for commercial canning. - **Mission** (Black Mission) -- Dependable, excellent flavour. Purplish black, fresh/dried/canned. - **White Genoa** -- Cooler areas, very hardy. Whitish skin, excellent fresh eating. Medicinal: Mild laxative. Mixed with senna for stronger preparations. Roasted split figs used as poultice for dental abscesses and boils. Milky juice applied to warts. Figs are fire retardant. ## Mulberries (Morus spp) Family: MORACEAE. Small to medium, long-lived (300 years), deciduous. Hardy, drought resistant, frost tolerant, shade tolerant, suited to most soils. Heavy bearers at 19-24 t/ha. Fruit delicious raw; can be dried and powdered into cooking flour. Self-harvest pig food supporting 2-3 pigs per tree (86 trees/ha, 60-day season). Excellent poultry forage. ... [truncated]

## Carob (Ceratonia siliqua)
Family: CAESALPINIACEAE. The largest of Europe's leguminous trees, reaching up to 15 m. Mediterranean native, growing in areas with as little as 250 mm annual rainfall. Prefers slightly alkaline sandy soils. Exceptionally long-lived (500+ years). Dioecious -- both male and female trees are needed for pod production. Pods are highly nutritious: crude protein 2.25-21%, total sugars 37.1-56.6%. Excellent stockfeed and a popular chocolate substitute. Tap roots have been traced to 20 m depth. Fire retardant -- useful in permaculture-ground-cover-plants.

## Grafting and Propagation Notes

Grafting is a fundamental skill for establishing productive fruit trees in
permaculture systems. Most commercial fruit varieties are clones propagated
onto rootstocks selected for disease resistance, dwarfing, or soil adaptation.
Key considerations for the home grower include:

- **Rootstock selection**: Dwarfing rootstocks (M9 for apples, Quince A for
  pears) produce smaller trees suited to small spaces, while standard rootstocks
  produce larger, longer-lived trees better suited to [[permaculture-plant-guilds-companion-planting|companion planting]]
  beneath trees can support pollinator populations
- **Triploid cultivars**: Some apple varieties (e.g., Bramley's Seedling)
  produce sterile pollen and require two other pollinators nearby

## Design Notes

- Interplant fruit trees with permaculture-companion-planting with garlic, chives, and tagasaste beneath most fruit trees
- Place grafted fruit trees near the house (Zone 1-2) for maximum attention and harvest convenience
- Seedling trees planted closer together can be selectively thinned as production characteristics become known

- Select diverse cultivars to extend harvest seasons and reduce pest pressure
