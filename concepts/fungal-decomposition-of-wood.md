# Fungal Decomposition of Wood

Fungi are the primary decomposers of wood on Earth. Through extracellular enzymes, they dismantle lignin and cellulose — the two most abundant organic polymers on the planet — recycling carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, phosphorus, and minerals into nutrients for living organisms. From *Mycelium Running* by [[stamets-mycoremediation-principles]].

## Overview

Wood decomposition by fungi is a foundational ecological process. Fungi secrete enzymes and acids that degrade large molecules of dead plants into simpler molecules, which they reassemble into building blocks such as polysaccharides for cell walls. The "inefficiency" of this process — releasing a fraction of nutrients into the environment — is the foundation of nutrient mineralization in all terrestrial ecosystems. Most gourmet and medicinal mushrooms are wood decomposers, the premier recyclers on the planet.

## Wood Composition

Wood is composed primarily of three structural polymers:
- **Cellulose** (40-50%): linear glucose chains with crystalline structure
- **Hemicellulose** (20-30%): branched polysaccharides of various sugars
- **Lignin** (15-35%): complex, irregular phenolic polymer — the most recalcitrant component

## White Rot vs Brown Rot Fungi

Wood-decay mushrooms are classified into two major groups based on which component they primarily attack:

### White Rot Fungi (~93% of wood-decay species)
White rot fungi produce enzymes that break down the recalcitrant brown lignin, leaving behind white cellulose. They are the premier mycoremediators because their enzymes can also break down many synthetic toxins with similar hydrogen-carbon bonds.

**Key enzymes**: Lignin peroxidase (LiP), manganese peroxidase (MnP), laccase, H2O2-generating enzymes

**Examples**: Oyster (*Pleurotus ostreatus*), shiitake (*Lentinula edodes*), maitake (*Grifola frondosa*), turkey tail (*Trametes versicolor*), reishi (*Ganoderma lucidum*), artist conk (*Ganoderma applanatum*), cauliflower mushroom (*Sparassis crispa*)

**Remediation power**: Only white rot mushrooms produce manganese-dependent peroxidase, an enzyme that mineralizes wood and is particularly efficient at breaking hydrogen-carbon bonds in petroleum, PAHs, PCBs, and other persistent pollutants.

### Brown Rot Fungi (~7% of wood-decay species)
Brown rot fungi break down the white cellulose, leaving behind brownish lignin. They cause the characteristic checkered cubical cracking seen on downed conifer trees.

**Mechanism**: Primarily use Fenton chemistry (hydroxyl radicals from H2O2 and iron) to depolymerize cellulose without direct enzymatic contact — causing up to 50% strength loss with only 5% mass loss.

**Examples**: Sulphur shelf (*Laetiporus sulphureus*), velvet polypore (*Phaeolus schweinitzii*), agarikon (*Fomitopsis officinalis*), split-gill polypore (*Schizophyllum commune*)

### Some Species Cause Both Rots
Some mushrooms produce both white and brown rot, leaving mottled discoloration. Bear's head (*Hericium abietis*) causes a white rot in the core of a stump while the outer regions become brown.

## The Decomposition Process

1. Fungi secrete extracellular enzymes (ligninases, cellulases, hemicellulases) into the wood
2. Enzymes break polymer bonds, releasing simple molecules (sugars, phenols, organic acids)
3. Fungi absorb dissolved nutrients across hyphal membranes
4. Resources are redistributed through the mycelial network
5. More than 50% of organic mass cleaves off as CO2; 10-20% as water
6. Remaining material: chitin, polysaccharides, proteins, enzymes become soil organic matter
7. Bacteria consume rotting mycelium and fruiting bodies
8. Bacterial activity prepares soil for plant colonization

## Decomposer Succession

Wood decomposition follows predictable fungal succession:

### Primary Colonizers (Pioneer Species)
- Fast-growing, low-nutrient demand, broad substrate tolerance
- Often molds and ascomycetes (*Trichoderma*, *Hypoxylon*, *Xylaria*)
- Colonize fresh wood within weeks; use simple sugars
- Some morels (*Morchella*) and cup fungi appear after disturbances (fire, logging)
- Modify substrate pH and chemistry for secondary colonizers

### Secondary Decomposers
- Replace pioneers as enzymatic degradation makes nutrients available
- Include many white rot basidiomycetes
- Begin lignin and cellulose decomposition in earnest
- Competitive interactions intensify; resource partitioning develops

### Late-Stage Decomposers
- Specialized for chemically modified, nutrient-poor residues
- Brown rot specialists continue cellulose removal
- Succession culminates in humus incorporation into soil

## Mycelium as the "Grand Demolecularizer"

Mycelium is the grand demolecularizer of plant fibers (lignin and cellulose), creating soil as an end consequence. Foot-deep beds of wood chips decompose into 1 to 2 inches of rich soil in 2 to 3 years when inoculated with mycelium, or in 4 to 5 years from natural mycoflora alone.

## Saprophytic Sentinels for Forest Health

Select saprophytic mushrooms can defend forests from parasitic blights like *Armillaria*:

| Species | Role | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| *Sparassis crispa* (cauliflower) | Parasitizes *Armillaria* in vitro | Secretes sparassol antibiotic |
| *Hypholoma capnoides/fasciculare* | Outcompetes *Armillaria* | Reduced root rot up to 67% in BC field trials |
| *Trametes versicolor* (turkey tail) | Overgrows *Armillaria* | Fastest-growing mycelium; also breaks down PCBs |
| *Pleurotus ostreatus* (oyster) | Aggressive colonizer | Strong antibacterial/antiviral properties |
| *Stropharia rugoso-annulata* (garden giant) | Soil defender | 100-fold drop in fecal coliforms in field trials |
