Summer temperatures in the mountains can dip to

## Taking Advantage of Microclimates

The easiest and most cost-effective approach to
extending the [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]] is to locate and
take advantage of microclimates such as areas
that warm up faster or retain heat longer.
Orientation to the sun, wind breaks, and walls
made out of materials like bricks, mortar, or
stone will hold the heat and can prevent freeze-
thaw cycles.

Dark rocks can moderate temperature in a very
small area by soaking up the heat of the day then
releasing it slowly as the evening temperature
cools. Dark walls and soil will absorb and hold
more heat than light-colored counterparts. A
lake, pond, or small creek will also moderate air
temperature, keeping it warmer in winter and
cooler in the summer.

## Frost Protection Methods

### Cloches

Cloches are individual protective coverings that
keep plants warm at night. A simple cloche is a
milk container with the bottom cut off and the lid
removed. Placed over a plant, the plastic captures
and retains heat while allowing ventilation through
the open top. You can make cloches out of wax
paper, glass, and jars, or buy commercial units
made of rigid transparent plastic or heavy-duty
wax paper.
