# Dynamic Accumulator Plants — Potassium

Potassium (K), often referred to as "potash" in its mineral form, is one of the three primary macronutrients essential for plant health. It is as difficult to liberate from its mineral state in soil as phosphorus. Dynamic accumulator plants can concentrate potassium in their tissues, making it available for recycling through composting, mulching, and green manuring.

## Why Potassium Matters

Potassium plays critical roles in plant health:

- Regulation of water movement through plants (stomatal control and [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]])
- Activation of enzymes involved in photosynthesis and protein synthesis
- Disease resistance and pest pressure tolerance
- Fruit quality, flavor, sugar content, and shelf life
- Root development and overall plant vigor
- Cold hardiness and winter survival of perennial plants
- Stem strength and resistance to lodging (falling over)

[[knf-potassium-deficiency-and-excess]] symptoms include yellowing or browning of leaf edges and tips, weak stems, poor fruit quality, and increased susceptibility to disease.

## The Bracken Fern: A Historical Potassium Source

The bracken fern (*[[pteridium-aquilinum]]*) is a remarkable potassium accumulator with a long history of practical use. In early 1900s England, bracken fern was commercially harvested as a source of potash for washing:

- During summer, leaves were collected green, dried, then burned
- The resulting ash was moistened and molded into balls for use as a potassium-based cleaning agent
- Many current detergents are potassium-based, continuing this tradition
- The bracken-based potash industry was eventually displaced by cheaper soda-based products

Alan Chadwick, father of biodynamic French [[solomon-wide-spacing-versus-intensive-gardening-debate]], had his students collect green bracken ferns each spring for composting with root crops. He was practicing English folklore with good reason: green bracken ferns average 25 percent potash and can contain as much as 55 percent — transforming a common weed into a significant garden asset.

## Key Potassium-Accumulating Plants

### Top Accumulators (Highest Concentrations)

- **Bracken fern** (*Pteridium aquilinum*) — 25-55% potash in green foliage; the most exceptional potassium accumulator documented
- **Comfrey** (*[[comfrey-symphytum-officinale]]*) — widely regarded as one of the best all-around accumulators; very high potassium content in leaves; can be cut 4-5 times per season
- **[[comparison-stinging-nettle-vs-horsetail]]** (*Urtica urens*) — exceptional potassium accumulator; ideal for compost teas and liquid feeds; also very high in nitrogen
- **Dandelion** (*Taraxacum vulgare*) — deep taproot mines subsoil potassium; ubiquitous in gardens
- **Lamb's quarters** (*Chenopodium album*) — fast-growing summer annual with high K; pioneer species

### Comprehensive List of Potassium Accumulators

The following plants have been documented as accumulating potassium in their foliage:

**Legumes and Cover Crops**
- Alfalfa (*Medicago sativa*)
- Clovers (*Trifolium* spp.) — white, red, crimson, alsike, hop, and rabbit foot clovers
- Vetches (*Vicia* spp.)
- Lupine (*Lupinus* spp.)

**Herbs and Edible Plants**
- Borage (*Borago officinalis*)
- Caraway (*Carum carvi*)
- Chives (*Allium* sp.)
- Fennel (*Foeniculum vulgare*)
- Garlic (*Allium sativum*)
- Parsley (*[[petroselinum-crispum]]*)
- Peppermint (*Mentha piperita*)
- Purslane (*[[portulaca-oleracea]]*)

**Common Weeds and Wild Plants**
- Burdock (*Arctium minus*)
- Chickweed (*Stellaria media*)
- Chicory (*[[cichorium-intybus]]*)
- Docks (*Rumex* spp.) — including broad-leaved dock
- Fat hen (*Atriplex hastata*)
- Groundsel (*Senecio vulgaris*)
- Mullein (*Verbascum* spp.)
- Mustards (*Brassica* spp.)
- Pigweed (*Amaranthus retroflexus*)
- Plantains (*Plantago* spp.)
- Shepherd's purse (*Capsella bursa-pastoris*)
- Sow thistle (*Sonchus arvensis*)
- Thistles (*Cirsium arvense*, *Carduus nutans*, *Salsola pestifer*)
- Toadflax (*Linaria vulgaris*)

**Ferns, Grasses, and Wetland Plants**
- Bracken fern (*Pteridium aquilinum*)
- Carrot leaves (*Daucus carota*)
- Cattail (*Typha latifolia*)
- Coltsfoot (*[[tussilago-farfara]]*)
