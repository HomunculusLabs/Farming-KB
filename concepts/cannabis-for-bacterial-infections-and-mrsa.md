Cannabinoids demonstrate antibacterial properties against a range of pathogenic bacteria, including antibiotic-resistant strains. The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-cannabinoid-terpene-synergy]] assigns a combined CHI value of 31 from 15 bacterial and viral studies reviewed.

## Cannabis Antibacterial Mechanisms

Five major cannabinoids have demonstrated antibacterial activity:
- **THC** (delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol)
- **CBD** (cannabidiol)
- **CBG** (cannabigerol)
- **CBC** (cannabichromene)
- **CBN** (cannabinol)

The precise mechanism through which these plant constituents destroy even antibiotic-resistant bacteria remains elusive. Research suggests cannabinoid acids (particularly CBDA, cannabidiolic acid) may play significant roles, with selective COX-2 inhibition as one pathway.

## Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA)

MRSA is a "superbug" strain of Staphylococcus aureus resistant to most antibiotics. It primarily targets the skin, forming abscesses, but can affect internal organs leading to sepsis and death. Risk factors include weakened immune systems, chronic wounds, surgical implants, and close-contact environments (hospitals, nursing homes, prisons).

### Evidence

| Agent | Study Type | Key Results | CHI |
|-------|-----------|-------------|-----|
| CBD, CBC, CBG, THC, CBN | Laboratory (2008, multi-center international) | All five major cannabinoids showed potent activity against various MRSA strains of current clinical relevance | 1 |

Combined CHI Value: 1

Both indica and sativa flowers contain these five major cannabinoids. Possible synergistic effects of complete cannabinoid profiles against MRSA have not yet been tested.

### Supporting Therapies

- **Garlic**: Allicin is highly effective against MRSA in laboratory studies; garlic extract containing diallyl sulphide and diallyl disulphide showed multiple protective functions in mice; topical and internal allicin has cured MRSA patients in case studies
- **Essential oils**: Grapefruit seed extract (Citricidal) with geranium oil showed greatest antibacterial effects; geranium + tea tree oil most active against methicillin-sensitive S. aureus; peppermint, spearmint, Japanese mint oils inhibit MRSA; tea tree, lavender, lemongrass, cinnamon, melissa, and mountain savory oils also effective
- **Maggot debridement therapy**: Sterile maggots effectively clean infected wounds, disinfect tissue, and stimulate healing; MRSA is killed in the topical area of application

## Gonorrhea

Caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae bacteria, gonorrhea is primarily sexually transmitted. Symptoms include burning urination, discharge, and pelvic inflammatory disease. Standard antibiotics are increasingly ineffective, with gonorrhea potentially becoming a "superbug" resistant to all known antibiotics.

### Historical Cannabis Use

- Arabic and Indian Ayurvedic physicians applied cannabis diuretic, sedative, and anti-inflammatory properties to treat gonorrhea
- Prior to penicillin, cannabidiolic acids were commonly prescribed for bacterial infections including gonorrhea
- An 1892 treatment guideline: "it lessens the discharge, inflammation, burning pains, and restlessness"
- Cannabis preparations were sold as urethral injections until 1935
- CBD-acid selectively inhibits COX-2 (inflammation and pain enzyme)

### Supporting Therapy

Coconut oil contains medium-chain fatty acids (capric acid, lauric acid) that kill all strains of Neisseria gonorrhoeae in laboratory studies and effectively inactivate chlamydia.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Bacterial Infections

MRSA's primary target is the skin, possibly suggesting underlying vulnerability connected to image and identity. Its rapid destructiveness suggests extreme defenselessness. The mind-body perspective examines:
- How body image and identity affect susceptibility

Mental-emotional constructs increasing vulnerability include: sexual guilt, sex without care or love, emotional manipulations, suppressed emotions around sexuality.

Protective constructs include: developing healthy sexual principles, releasing guilt/fear/shame, adding care and love to balance desire.
