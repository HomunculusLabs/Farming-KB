# Cover Crop Selection Guide

## Why Cover Crops

Cover crops are planted specifically to build and hold soil and to smother weeds. Their leaves shield soil from hammering rains and carpet the earth with humus-building litter. Their roots drive deep into soil, loosening earth, drawing up nutrients, and placing organic matter farther down than even the deepest plowing. Many send roots 10 to 15 feet deep.

## Selection Principles

### Diversity Is Key

As in so much of ecological gardening, diversity provides the key. Seed a mélange of varieties and record which thrive. Each plant type secretes its own array of sugars and compounds from its roots, attracting a different community of soil organisms. The more species of cover crop planted, the more varied the soil life's diversity, which subdues disease and boosts plant growth.

### Annual vs Perennial

- **Annuals:** For building fertility in a temporarily dormant garden bed (e.g., over winter)
- **Perennials:** For preparing a future orchard's soil, or areas not ready to develop for a few years. Perennial clovers can cover garden paths.

### Nitrogen Fixers vs Non-Fixers

Most legumes host symbiotic microorganisms in root nodules that fix nitrogen gas from the air. William King found in the *Journal of Agronomy* that 80% of the nitrogen in interplanted ryegrass came from companion clover -- the clover was actively feeding the ryegrass through root sloughing.

**Important:** Balance nitrogen with carbon. Soil organisms consume 10 to 50 times more carbon than nitrogen. Farmers blend a grass or other nonlegume into cover crops. Commercial mixes contain 10 to 40% oats, annual ryegrass, or other nonlegume. A too-rich nitrogen fuel can actually deplete more organic matter than the cover crop adds.

### Legumes Transfer Nitrogen While Alive

Hemenway disputes the claim that nitrogen fixers only benefit companion plants after dying. He has observed corn planted with beans growing decidedly larger, and the Bullock brothers plant nitrogen-fixing shrubs in the same hole as fruit trees with faster growth.

## Annual Cool Weather Cover Crops

Planted late summer or fall; mowed or tilled in spring while flowering and before seed set (time of maximum root growth, nitrogen content, and biomass). Most hardy to 0-20°F.

## Perennial Cover Crops

Excellent for no-till gardens; mowed to generate mulch and compost. Shorter varieties like white Dutch clover can be used as living mulch interplanted with other crops.

| Crop | N-fixer | Height | Notes |
|------|---------|--------|-------|
| White Dutch clover | Yes | 6-10 in | Many soils |
| White Ladino clover | Yes | 1 ft | Many soils |
| Alfalfa | Yes | 3-5 ft | Heavy soil, drought resistant |
| Bird's-foot trefoil | Yes | 2-3 ft | Needs moisture |
| Chicory | No | 2-3 ft | Opens heavy soil |
| Orchard grass | No | 2-3 ft | Needs moisture |
| Creeping red fescue | No | 1-2 ft | Many soils |
| Timothy grass | No | 2-3 ft | Heavy soil, needs moisture |
