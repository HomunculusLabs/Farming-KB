# Arthropod-Dispersed Fungi and Spore Interactions

Many free-living fungi and lichens are dispersed by arthropods in addition to
wind. These dispersal associations range from obligate symbioses to casual
encounters. The study of arthropod-dispersed fungi encompasses ophiostomatoid
ascomycetes, mycoparasites, capilliconidia-producing fungi, and a diverse array
of spore-dispersal mechanisms. [[bark-beetle-fungi-ophiostomatales-collection-isolation]] and [[insect-arthropod-associated-fungi]] species living in beached
seaweeds.

Many of the spores described cannot withstand desiccation and must be processed
immediately if [[fungal-life-cycles]] are often synchronized with those of their
arthropod dispersers, so mature diaspores are available when arthropods are
ready to disperse.

Transfer of sticky spores is accomplished using the coverslip shard technique:
breaking a cover glass and picking out isosceles triangles with heat-sterilized
fine forceps. The shard is flamed briefly, cooled, touched to the spore mass at
the tip of an ophiostomatoid perithecium, and placed spore surface down on an
agar plate. This method allows observation of spores through the cover glass
for germination and contamination checks.

## Cultivation Challenges

Culture of some arthropod-dispersed species is routine, requiring only transfer
to common media such as cornmeal agar or MEA. However, many grow only or best
with their arthropod host, which is difficult when the host is unknown. Some
species are uncommon in culture collections because they may not grow vigorously
without a host and may not sporulate in pure culture. Such fungi can be
deposited as pure or two-membered cultures in general collections.

## Molecular Methods

The need for nucleic-acid-derived taxonomic characters is especially great for
arthropod-associated fungi, where morphological convergence and lack of known
teleomorphs frequently obscure phylogenetic relationships. Molecular characters
potentially may be gleaned from minute quantities of and/or nonculturable types
using PCR amplification and characterization of discrete genomic DNA targets.

For mycoparasitic, insect-dispersed fungi such as some Pyxidiophora species,
which must be cultured with a fungal host, culture conditions are manipulated to
favor [[fungal-destructive-sampling-herbarium-dna-extraction]] from minute thalli is performed by crushing three to five
thalli dry between silanized microscope slides, with primers designed to
discriminate between insect and ascomycete DNA templates.
