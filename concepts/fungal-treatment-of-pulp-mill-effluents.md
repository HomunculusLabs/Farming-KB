### By T. versicolor -- Laccase

Laccases play the primary role in BPE decolorization by T. versicolor:

- Laccases generate Mn(III) chelates similar to those produced by MnP when in the presence of phenolic substrates

- Several T. versicolor laccases dechlorinate polychlorinated phenols, major BPE constituents

- Laccase-mediated dechlorination reduces both color and toxicity

The ability of T. versicolor laccase to generate Mn(III)-like diffusible oxidants from phenolic substrates in the effluent provides a self-sustaining redox cycle: laccase oxidizes phenolic compounds to phenoxy radicals, which in turn oxidize other effluent components. This auto-catalytic mechanism means that initial phenolic compounds in the effluent can serve as natural mediators, extending the effective treatment range of the laccase system.

## Biomimetic Systems

The understanding that Mn(III)-chelates mediate BPE decolorization may lead to the design of effective biomimetic systems capable of generating chelated Mn(III) for degradation without requiring living fungal cultures. Such systems could be more practical for industrial-scale application.

Biomimetic approaches include the use of synthetic metalloporphyrins and phthalocyanines that mimic the catalytic cycle of MnP. These catalysts can generate Mn(III)-chelates or other reactive oxygen species when combined with H2O2 or other oxidants. While biomimetic systems avoid the biological complexity of fungal cultures, they currently face challenges of catalyst stability, H2O2 cost, and incomplete pollutant mineralization compared to living fungal systems.

## Treatment Performance and Scale

Treatment performance varies with reactor configuration, fungal species, and effluent characteristics:

- **Laboratory shake flasks** -- 60-90% color removal in 3-6 days with T. versicolor, P. chrysosporium, and P. flavido-alba

- **Packed-bed reactors** -- sustained decolorization over weeks using immobilized T. versicolor on polyurethane foam

- **Rotating biological contactors** -- continuous treatment with consistent 70-80% color removal

- **Sequential fungal-bacterial treatment** -- fungal pretreatment followed by activated sludge achieves higher overall treatment efficiency than either system alone

The evolution of chlorine-free bleaching technologies (TCF, ECF) has reduced but not eliminated the need for effluent treatment.
