## Overview

Carrots are a root vegetable prized for their sweetness, versatility, and storage capability. They require specific soil conditions but reward the gardener with a high-yielding, nutrient-dense crop. This guide covers soil preparation, variety selection, planting, care, harvesting, and common problems.

## Soil Preparation

Carrots are demanding about soil texture. Poor soil is the primary cause of forked, stunted, or deformed roots.

### Ideal Soil Conditions
- Sandy loam is ideal — loose, well-draining, and free of rocks
- Soil depth of at least 10-12 inches of workable, loose earth
- pH 6.0-6.8; slightly acidic is best
- Low in fresh nitrogen — excess nitrogen causes hairy, forked roots
- Moderate phosphorus and potassium levels for root development
- Work in 2-3 inches of finished compost but avoid fresh manure

### Bed Preparation Steps
1. Remove all rocks, stones, and debris from the top 12 inches
2. Break up compacted soil layers with a broadfork — never rotary-till deeply
3. Incorporate compost and any needed amendments (phosphorus if deficient)
4. Rake the surface smooth and level
5. Create raised rows or beds 4-6 inches tall to improve drainage and depth
6. For heavy clay soils, consider double-digging or building tall raised beds

### Improving Heavy Soils
- Add coarse sand (not fine playground sand) to improve drainage
- Mix in generous amounts of compost and leaf mold
- Consider growing shorter varieties like Paris Market or Little Finger
- Over multiple seasons, consistent organic matter additions transform heavy soil

## Varieties

### By Shape and Size
- **Nantes**: Cylindrical, blunt tip, 6-7 inches, sweet and tender. Varieties: Bolero, Touchon, Nelson
- **Imperator**: Long, tapered, 8-11 inches. Store well. Varieties: Sugarsnax, Atomic Red
- **Chantenay**: Broad-shouldered, conical, 5-6 inches, good for heavy soil. Varieties: Red Cored Chantenay, Hercules
- **Danvers**: Tapered, 7-8 inches, heat-tolerant. Varieties: Danvers Half Long
- **Mini/Round**: Short, 2-4 inches, ideal for containers and rocky soil. Varieties: Paris Market, Thumbelina, Little Finger

### By Color
- **Orange**: Standard; rich in beta-carotene
- **Purple**: Purple Haze, Cosmic Purple — antioxidants from anthocyanins
- **Red**: Atomic Red, Red Samurai — high lycopene
- **Yellow**: Yellowstone, Solar Yellow — mild, sweet flavor
- **White**: Lunar White, White Satin — mild, crisp

### By Season
- **Early**: 50-60 days; sow in early spring
- **Main Season**: 65-75 days; sow late spring for summer harvest
- **Storage**: 70-80 days; sow mid-summer for fall/winter storage

## Planting

### Seed Sowing
- Direct sow only — carrots do not transplant well (damaged taproots fork)
- Sow seeds 1/4 inch deep in furrows spaced 2-3 inches apart
- Rows should be 12-18 inches apart
- Seeds germinate slowly: 10-21 days depending on temperature
- Optimal germination temperature: 50-85F, with 75F ideal
- Carrot seeds are tiny — mix with dry sand for even distribution

### Germination Tips
- Keep soil surface consistently moist until germination (critical period)
- Cover rows with a board, burlap, or row cover to retain moisture
- Remove cover immediately upon germination to prevent etiolation
- Some gardeners pre-sprout seeds on damp paper towels, then plant carefully
- Interplant with radishes — radishes germinate fast and mark rows; harvest radishes before carrots need the space

## Thinning

Thinning is essential for properly sized roots. Crowded carrots will be small, twisted, or deformed.

### Thinning Schedule
1. First thinning: When seedlings are 1-2 inches tall, thin to 1 inch apart
2. Second thinning: When roots begin to swell (4-6 inches tall), thin to 2-3 inches apart
3. Final spacing depends on variety — mini types need 1-2 inches, full-size need 3-4 inches

### Technique
- Thin in the evening when pests are less active (carrot smell attracts carrot rust flies)
- Snip seedlings with scissors at soil level rather than pulling (pulling disturbs remaining roots)
- Water after thinning to settle disturbed soil

## Growing Care

### Watering
- Consistent, even moisture is critical — fluctuations cause splitting
- Aim for 1-1.5 inches per week
- Mulch heavily (2-3 inches of straw or grass clippings) to retain moisture
- Reduce watering as harvest approaches to prevent cracking

### Fertilizing
- Too much nitrogen causes excessive top growth and hairy, forked roots
- If soil was properly amended at planting, additional fertilizer is rarely needed
- If needed, side-dress with low-nitrogen compost or wood ash (potassium) at mid-season

### Weed Management
- Carrots compete poorly with weeds, especially when small
- Hand-weed carefully to avoid disturbing shallow roots
- Mulch suppresses weeds after seedlings are established
- Avoid deep cultivation near the carrot row

## Common Problems

### Forking
- Cause: Rocks, compacted soil, overcrowding, or fresh manure in soil
- Prevention: Proper bed prep, thinning, and using finished compost only

### Hairy Roots
- Cause: Excess nitrogen in soil
- Prevention: Avoid nitrogen-heavy fertilizers; use balanced or low-N amendments

### Splitting
- Cause: Irregular watering — dry periods followed by heavy rain/irrigation
- Prevention: Consistent moisture, mulching

### Green Shoulders (Greening at Top)
- Cause: Sunlight exposure on the root shoulder
- Prevention: Hill soil up around the crown as roots grow

### Pests
- **Carrot rust fly**: Eggs laid near crown; larvae tunnel into roots. Row covers prevent egg-laying. Avoid thinning on windy days when the fly is active
- **Nematodes**: Cause knotty, galled roots. Rotate crops; marigolds may help repel
- **Wireworms**: Tunnel into roots. Remove decaying organic matter before planting

### Diseases
- **Alternaria leaf blight**: Brown spots on leaves; improve air circulation, remove affected foliage
- **Cercospora leaf spot**: Similar to alternaria; same management approach

## Harvesting

### When to Harvest
- Carrots are usually ready 60-80 days after sowing depending on variety
- Check size by brushing soil away from the shoulder of a test carrot
- Best flavor develops after a light frost (sugars concentrate)
- Do not leave in ground through deep winter freezes in cold zones

### How to Harvest
- Loosen soil with a fork beside the row before pulling
- Pull straight up gently; twist slightly if resistant
- Harvest in the morning for best flavor and crispness

## Storage

- Remove tops immediately — they draw moisture from roots
- Do not wash before storage; brush off loose soil only
- Store at 32-38F with 95-100% humidity
- Pack in damp sand, sawdust, or leaves in a root cellar or refrigerator crisper
- Properly stored carrots last 4-6 months
- Carrots can overwinter in the ground in zones 5-6 with heavy mulch (straw, leaves)
