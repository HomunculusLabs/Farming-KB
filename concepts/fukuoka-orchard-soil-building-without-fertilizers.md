# Orchard Soil Building Without Fertilizers

updated: 2026-04-18

Fukuoka's method of building soil in orchards without any chemical or prepared fertilizers took over forty years to develop. It represents a complete inversion of conventional orchard management, where the soil gradually enriches itself through living processes.

## The Starting Point: Depleted Soil

When Fukuoka set out to revive his father's old citrus orchard after World War II, the topsoil had been completely washed away, leaving only red clay. Applying lots of fertilizer, root-grafting, and thinning blossoms would only have invited further decline. Nor would planting new saplings have worked in such poor soil.

A review of his father's finances revealed the orchard had been run at a loss for the first thirteen years, made money for twenty years, then lost money again for the following ten. The sturdy, celebrated citrus trees had been grown at the expense of soil depletion.

- Five to ten trees per quarter-acre among fruit trees
- Grows five feet or more per year, creating a shelterbelt in 3-4 years
- After 5-6 years, each tree provides a half-ton of high-quality organic material for burying
- Felled trunks and tops are buried in trenches within the orchard
- A 100-square-yard area of previously hard, lean soil around each tree becomes soft and porous

## Timeline of Soil Recovery

- **5-10 years** are needed to build up six inches of topsoil through cover crop cultivation
- Fruit trees on naturally improved soil live two to three times longer than those grown with artificial fertilizers
- Fruit trees grown in artificially prepared soil are inevitably weak, becoming either dwarfs or leggy, unable to live out their natural lifespan

## Trench Burial of Organic Matter

Along with cover crops, Fukuoka dug trenches and filled them with organic matter: straw, hay, twigs, small branches, ferns, wood and bark chips, and lumber. After comparing results, wood chips proved most cost-effective. The best material was lumber produced right in the orchard from black wattle trees.
