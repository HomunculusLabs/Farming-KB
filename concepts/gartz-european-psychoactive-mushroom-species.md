## Psilocybe semilanceata: The Liberty Cap

The most important European psychoactive species by far is
[[psilocybe-bohemica-central-european]] (syn. Psilocybe serbica) is a
significant European species found primarily in the former Czechoslovakia
and surrounding regions. It is more commonly used in that country than
Psilocybe semilanceata. The species is notable for displaying the most
impressive bluing reaction among the species Gartz examined -- the caps
stain very quickly in reaction to pressure, despite containing low or
non-existent levels of free psilocin. This paradox is explained by the
rapid enzymatic removal of the phosphate group from psilocybin, which
produces psilocin that immediately oxidizes into blue-colored compounds.

Psilocybe bohemica has a distinctive flour-like smell reminiscent of
radishes or poppies. Cross-breeding experiments demonstrated that mycelia
from Psilocybe bohemica can never be fused with those of
[[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]], known colloquially as "Blue
Wavy" or "Cyan," has been found at numerous locations in Europe, including
Germany (Saarland, the Black Forest, Vogtland, Rheinland, Hamburg, and
Bremen from 1982-1983), Switzerland (1972), Austria (Steiermark, 1976), and
Corsica (1972, 1984). This species is particularly associated with bark
mulch and wood debris in parks and gardens. It develops very intensely blue
stains on its stems, while its caps exhibit a characteristic wavy margin.
