## Brown Rice Vinegar (BRV)

Brown Rice Vinegar is a KNF input made by fermenting brown rice into natural acetic acid (vinegar). It serves multiple functions: as a growth stimulant, an extraction solvent for calcium and phosphate inputs, a sour nutrient for the changeover period, and a promoter of root development. BRV is one of the most versatile KNF inputs.

## Why Brown Rice Vinegar?

Acetic acid (the active compound in vinegar) has several roles in plant growth:
- **Extraction solvent**: Dissolves calcium from eggshells and calcium phosphate from bones (see [[water-soluble-calcium-wca]] and [[water-soluble-calcium-phosphate-wcp]])
- **Root development**: Stimulates root growth and branching
- **Growth regulation**: In combination with FPJ, prevents over-luxurious vegetative growth
- **Sour nutrient**: Provides the "sour" nutrient type needed during the changeover period (see [[nutritive-cycle-theory]])
- **Mineral delivery**: Carries trace minerals from the fermentation process

BRV is preferred over commercial white vinegar because:
- Made from whole grain brown rice (contains germ, bran, and endosperm — more nutrients)
- Natural fermentation produces a complex mixture of organic acids (not just acetic acid)
- Contains amino acids, vitamins, and minerals from the rice
- No chemical additives or preservatives

## BRV Recipe

### Materials
- Brown rice (unpolished, whole grain) — 1 kg
- Water — non-chlorinated
- [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]] — IMO-2 or IMO-4 for inoculation
- Glass or ceramic vessel (not metal — acid reacts with metal)
- Airlock or breathable cover (porous paper + rubber band)
- Sugar (optional, see process)

### Process

**Step 1: Rice Wine (Alcohol Fermentation)**
1. Rinse brown rice and soak in water for 4-6 hours
2. Steam or boil rice until soft but not mushy (like sushi rice consistency)
3. Spread rice on clean cloth to cool to room temperature
4. Mix with IMO-2 or IMO-4 (the indigenous microorganisms drive fermentation)
5. Place in fermentation vessel, add water to cover rice
6. Cover with porous paper, secure with rubber band
7. Store in warm place (25-30C / 77-86F)
8. Ferment 5-7 days — rice will become liquid as enzymes break down starches
9. Sugar (jaggery/brown sugar) can be added to boost alcohol production (1:1 sugar to rice)
10. The liquid becomes rice wine (alcohol content ~5-8%)

**Step 2: Vinegar (Acetic Acid Fermentation)**
1. Once rice wine is actively bubbling, transfer to a wider vessel with more surface area
2. Cover with breathable cloth (acetic acid bacteria need oxygen)
3. Acetic acid bacteria (Acetobacter) from the IMO and air convert alcohol to acetic acid
4. Ferment 2-4 weeks
5. Vinegar is ready when alcohol smell is gone, replaced by sharp vinegar aroma
6. Taste should be sour but not harsh
7. Strain through cheesecloth
8. Store in glass bottles

### Signs of Quality
- Clear to amber liquid
- Sharp, clean vinegar aroma (not musty or putrid)
- Sour taste without harshness
- Slight sweet undertone from residual rice sugars

### Storage
- Glass bottles, cool dark place
- Lasts 1-2 years (high acidity prevents spoilage)
- No refrigeration needed

## BRV as Extraction Solvent

BRV's primary technical use in KNF is extracting minerals from natural sources:

### For WS-Ca (Water-Soluble Calcium)
- Crushed eggshells + BRV → calcium acetate (plant-available calcium)
- Chemical reaction: CaCO3 + 2CH3COOH → Ca(CH3COO)2 + H2O + CO2
- See [[water-soluble-calcium-wca]] for full recipe

### For WS-CaPo (Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate)
- Animal bones + BRV → calcium phosphate acetate
- See [[water-soluble-calcium-phosphate-wcp]] for full recipe

## Application Rates

| Use Case | Dilution | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Changeover period | 1:200-500 | Sour nutrient for transition |
| Root development | 1:500 | Soil drench for seedlings |
| Growth regulation | 1:500-1000 | Mix with FPJ to prevent overgrowth |
| Seed treatment (SES) | 2 ml/L | Part of seed treatment solution |
| Crack prevention | 2 ml/L | With OHN + FPJ + LAB |
| Soil treatment (SOS) | 500 ml per acre | Part of full soil treatment |
| Extraction solvent | Undiluted | For making WS-Ca and WS-CaPo |

Apply as foliar spray or soil drench. BRV is acidic — avoid direct contact with sensitive new growth at high concentrations.

## BRV in the Nutritive Cycle

According to [[nutritive-cycle-theory]], BRV provides "sour nutrients":

- **Vegetative stage**: Generally not needed (plants want sweet nutrients)
- **Changeover period**: BRV at 1:200-500 — critical sour nutrient for transition
- **Reproductive stage**: Can continue at 1:500 if needed
- **As extraction solvent**: Used year-round for making WS-Ca and WS-CaPo

## Pairing with Other Inputs

BRV works synergistically with:
- [[fermented-plant-extract-fpe]] — FPJ + BRV prevents over-luxurious growth

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Wine won't ferment | No active IMO, chlorinated water | Use active IMO-2, non-chlorinated water |
| Wine smells bad | Anaerobic contamination | Ensure breathable cover, start over |
| Vinegar not forming | Not enough oxygen/ surface area | Use wider vessel, ensure airflow |
| Weak acidity | Insufficient fermentation time | Wait longer, ensure warm temperature |
| Harsh taste | Fermented too long or too hot | Dilute before use, shorten fermentation next time |
