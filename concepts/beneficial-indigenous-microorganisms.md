# Beneficial Indigenous Microorganisms (BIM)

## Overview

**Beneficial Indigenous Microorganisms (BIM)** are locally sourced, naturally occurring microorganisms cultured and applied in farming to build soil fertility, control [[mushroom-pests-and-diseases]], and improve plant and animal health. The practice was formalized by **Dr. [[han-kyu-cho]]** of the [[korean-natural-farming]] Association and popularized in the Philippines by **Gil A. Carandang** of Herbana Farms. The core philosophy holds that farmers should be self-reliant — producing their own inputs rather than purchasing them — and that **living soil** is the foundation of all agricultural productivity.

## Philosophy and Principles

Dr. Han Kyu Cho articulated three key principles:

- Farmers have lost independent authority over farming techniques and become dependent on purchased inputs
- The spirit of farming must be recovered so farmers regain subjectivity in their methods
- A new vision of farming must utilize **local materials actively** to create necessary inputs

Natural farmers of Asia focus on **microbial farming** — cultivating microorganisms and enzymes, working with energy (chi), balance, and harmony (yin and yang). This contrasts with Western organic approaches that emphasize composts, cover crops, and rotations.

## Why Microorganisms Matter

Without microorganisms, soil fertility and health cannot be created or sustained. Key roles include:

- **Decomposition** of organic matter, releasing nutrients for plant uptake
- **Nutrient cycling** — converting wastes into forms usable by plants
- **Disease suppression** — beneficial microbes outcompete pathogens
- **Toxin elimination** — breaking down harmful substances in soil and digestive systems
- **Odor control** — arresting foul smells from anaerobic decomposition

Ninety-six percent of plant and animal growth is attributed to air, water, and sunlight; only four percent comes from nutrients. Microorganisms are the bridge making those nutrients available.

## Lacto Bacilli — The Primary Workhorse

[[lactic-acid-bacteria]] are the most important microorganism in the BIM system, comprising 50% of the standard BIM recipe.

### Functions and Applications

- **Composting**: Feed on ammonia released during decomposition, eliminating foul odors
- **Natural piggery**: Sprayed on deep-litter floors (compost + soil + sawdust); no cleaning needed as wastes convert to fertilizer
- **Organic chickens**: Added to water and feeds for better nutrient assimilation and feed conversion
- **Aquaculture**: Minimizes ammonia from fish excretions, improving water quality and fish health
- **Plant health**: Sprayed on soil and leaves to aid decomposition and increase [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]]
- **Antibacterial properties**: Produces enzymes and natural antibiotics; controls salmonella and E. coli
- **Phyllosphere fortification**: Increases beneficial bacterial populations on leaf surfaces

### Collection and Culture Method

1. **Rice wash fermentation**: Pour rice wash into a container with 50-75% air gap, cover loosely, leave 5-7 days at 20-25°C in a shaded area
2. **Sign of readiness**: Rice bran separates and floats as a thin film; liquid smells sour
3. **Strain** the liquid into a larger container
4. **Add milk** (10 parts milk to 1 part rice wash liquid); fresh milk is best, though skim or powdered work
5. **Ferment** 5-7 days until carbohydrate, protein, and fat float, leaving yellow **serum**
6. **Preserve**: Add equal amount crude sugar or molasses (never refined sugar); store at room temperature
7. **Dilute for use**: Mix 1:20 with non-chlorinated water (chlorine kills microbes)
8. **Application rate**: 2-4 tablespoons per gallon of water for spraying or animal use

The milk saturation step eliminates all microorganisms except pure [[bionutrients-lab-lactic-acid-bacteria-serum]], achieving isolation without laboratory equipment.

## Forest Beneficial Microorganisms

### Collection Method

1. Locate a healthy, old, robust tree in a local forest with deep humus accumulation (2 feet to 1 yard)
2. Place cooked rice in a flat container with lid, allowing air space
3. Bury the container in the humus litter, protecting it with metal netting from animals
4. After 2-10 days (depending on temperature), uncover to find rice contaminated with white and colored molds
5. Add 1/3 crude sugar or molasses to the infected rice
6. After one week, the mixture becomes sticky and liquid
7. Add equal amount sugar/molasses for storage; dilute 1:20 for use

### Alternative Method

Spread forest litter, humus, or leaf mold sparingly over cooked rice and follow the same fermentation procedure.

## Bamboo Microorganisms

Bamboo roots exude sugary substances that attract powerful beneficial microorganisms. The collection method is identical to forest microorganisms — bury cooked rice in bamboo litter and follow the standard fermentation protocol.

## Plant-Specific Microorganisms

To culture microbes associated with a particular crop:

1. Select healthy, vigorous specimens of the target plant
2. Cut the plant and place an inverted container of cooked rice over the cut stem
3. Plant-specific beneficial microorganisms are attracted to the rice
4. Follow the standard fermentation procedure (add sugar/molasses, wait, dilute 1:20)

This technique allows farmers to inoculate crops with their ideal microbial partners.

## Rhizobium Nitrogen-Fixing Bacteria

Rhizobium forms nodules on legume roots, fixing atmospheric nitrogen into the soil. A simple culture method:

1. Pull legume plants at their flowering/fruiting stage when nodules are well-developed
2. Collect soil containing rhizobia from around the root zone
3. Mix soil with an equal ratio of crude sugar
4. Rhizobia proliferate feeding on the sugar
5. Use the mixture to coat legume seeds before planting

## Standard BIM Recipe

The recommended BIM formulation for plant application:

- **50% Lactic acid bacteria** (the dominant component)
- **50% Other microorganisms**, divided among:
  - Forest microorganisms (1 part)
  - Bamboo microorganisms (1 part)
  - Plant-specific microorganisms (1 part)

For animals, use pure lactic acid bacteria only. Bamboo microorganisms are specifically useful for fermenting animal feeds.

## Key Considerations

- **Water quality**: Never use chlorinated water — it kills beneficial microorganisms
- **Sugar type**: Use crude sugar or molasses only; refined sugar is chemically bleached and harmful to microbes
- **Temperature**: Fermentation proceeds at 20-25°C; cooler temperatures slow the process
- **Diversity**: The more diverse the microbial sources, the better the results
- **Combination with bionutrients**: BIM is typically mixed with [[korean-natural-farming-imo-collection-fermented-plant-extracts]] for enhanced effectiveness
- **Local adaptation**: Indigenous microorganisms are already adapted to local conditions, giving them advantages over imported commercial cultures
