# Vine Systems
**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison (1988)
**Updated:** 2026-04-18
## Overview
Vine crops occupy a unique niche [[mollison-lawn-reform-and-alternatives-in-permaculture-design]], using
vertical space on trellises, fences, and buildings to produce
food, modify microclimate, and provide wind protection. Mollison
treats vines as a critical component of both temperate and
tropical garden systems, greatly enlarging crop options and
controlling climate around the home.
## Trellis Systems (Figure 12.4)
Mollison presents several trellis designs for vine crops in
mesothermal (temperate) climates:
### Fedge (Fence-Hedge)
- A living fence-hedge against winds
- Uses strong, straight bearers (e.g., Tetragonia, Mexican tea)
- Spaced 2-3m apart with wire or mesh between
- Acts as both windbreak and productive structure
- Especially valuable on coastal sites for salt winds
### Inter-Trellis for Beans
- Zigzag wire between posts in garden rows
- Supports climbing beans, peas, and small cucurbits
- Aligned north/south where possible for even sun effects
- Vertical zigzag or inverted "V" designs work best in
  [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]]
### Wire Top with Mesh
- Horizontal wire framework with mesh below
- Supports heavier vines (grapes, kiwi, passionfruit)
- Can be attached to buildings or freestanding frames
- Mesh base supports climbing vegetables and provides shade
### Mortlock Trellis
- Pruned wall core design
- Uses existing walls as [[comparison-windbreak-vs-thermal-mass]]
- Good for espalier fruits and deciduous vines
- Provides summer shade and winter sun access
### Multi-Storey Building Trellis
- Trellis on multi-storey buildings
- Shade from climbing vines on most tree species
- Creates cool air space between building and vine layer
- Insulative dead air maintained by vigorous vine growth
## Vines Over Garden (Arid Climates)
In desert systems (Section 11, Figure 11.61), horizontal
trellis bars at 1-2m spacing furnished with grape vines create
a shade system over the [[aphid-management-vegetable-garden-solomon]], relieving light
saturation:
- **Eastern side:** More completely closed with herbaceous
  vines to defend from early sun
- **Western side:** Needs fleshy vines, as great heat builds up
- **Between:** Horizontal grape vine shade at 1-2m spacing
- **Result:** Soil temperatures remain cooler for longer
Suitable vines for the sides include beans, climbing tomatoes,
yams, and cucurbits.
## Trellis Crops in Temperate Gardens
In mesothermal climates, trellis crops are important. Many
species will trellis:
- **Legumes:** Peas, beans, four-winged bean, winged bean
- **Cucurbits:** Cucumber, melon, pumpkin, chayote
- **Tomatoes:** Climbing varieties
- **Brambles:** Youngberry, boysenberry, raspberry on fence
- **Grapes:** [[vitis-vinifera]] and related species
- **Kiwi:** [[actinidia-chinensis]] and hardy kiwi (A. arguta)
- **Passionfruit:** Passiflora spp. including banana passionfruit
Trellis over the garden is inappropriate in mesothermal climates
(unlike deserts). Vertical zigzag, inverted "V", or house wall
and garden fence trellis is best, aligned north/south where
possible.
## Vine Crops in Humid Tropics
In tropical village complexes, vines form part of the
multi-layered food garden:
- **Canopy layer:** Tall trees and palms
- **Mid-layer:** Fruit trees, legumes
- **Vine layer:** Climbing between trees — yams, passionfruit,
  winged bean, choko ([[sechium-edule]])
- **Ground layer:** Sweet potato, cassava, taro
Passionfruit appears in [[chicken-tractor-systems]] designs as a fence/trellis
crop grown on the chicken yard perimeter.
## Nitrogen-Fixing Vines
Several vine species fix atmospheric nitrogen, making them
valuable companions in food forests and garden systems:
- Butterfly pea ([[clitoria-ternatea]])
- Hyacinth bean ([[lablab-purpureus]])
- Scarlet runner bean (Phaseolus coccineus)
