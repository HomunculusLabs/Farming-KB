# Vine Systems

**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison (1988)
**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Vine crops occupy a unique niche in permaculture design, using vertical space on trellises, fences, and buildings to produce food, modify microclimate, and provide wind protection. Mollison treats vines as a critical component of both temperate and tropical garden systems, greatly enlarging crop options and controlling climate around the home.

## Trellis Systems (Figure 12.4)

Mollison presents several trellis designs for vine crops in mesothermal (temperate) climates:

### Fedge (Fence-Hedge)
- A living fence-hedge against winds
- Uses strong, straight bearers (e.g., Tetragonia, Mexican tea)
- Spaced 2-3m apart with wire or mesh between
- Acts as both windbreak and productive structure
- Especially valuable on coastal sites for salt winds

### Inter-Trellis for Beans
- Zigzag wire between posts in garden rows
- Supports climbing beans, peas, and small cucurbits
- Aligned north/south where possible for even sun effects
- Vertical zigzag or inverted "V" designs work best in temperate climates

### Wire Top with Mesh
- Horizontal wire framework with mesh below
- Supports heavier vines (grapes, kiwi, passionfruit)
- Can be attached to buildings or freestanding frames
- Mesh base supports climbing vegetables and provides shade

### Mortlock Trellis
- Pruned wall core design
- Uses existing walls as thermal mass
- Good for espalier fruits and deciduous vines
- Provides summer shade and winter sun access

### Multi-Storey Building Trellis
- Trellis on multi-storey buildings
- Shade from climbing vines on most tree species
- Creates cool air space between building and vine layer
- Insulative dead air maintained by vigorous vine growth

## Vines Over Garden (Arid Climates)

In desert systems (Section 11, Figure 11.61), horizontal trellis bars at 1-2m spacing furnished with grape vines create a shade system over the vegetable garden, relieving light saturation:

- **Eastern side:** More completely closed with herbaceous vines to defend from early sun (dew, distillation, and guttation moisture are important to plants early in the day)
- **Western side:** Needs fleshy vines, as great heat can build up
- **Between:** Horizontal grape vine shade at 1-2m spacing
- **Result:** Soil temperatures remain cooler for longer periods

Suitable vines for the sides include beans, climbing tomatoes, yams, and cucurbits.

## Trellis Crops in Temperate Gardens

In mesothermal climates, trellis crops are important. Many species will trellis:
- **Legumes:** Peas (Pisum spp.), beans (Phaseolus spp., Dolichos lablab, Vigna spp.), four-winged bean (Psophocarpus tetragonolobus), winged bean
- **Cucurbits:** Cucumber, melon, pumpkin, chayote (Sechium edule)
- **Tomatoes:** Climbing varieties
- **Brambles:** Youngberry, boysenberry, raspberry on fence trellis
- **Grapes:** Vitis vinifera and related species
- **Kiwi:** Actinidia chinensis (Chinese gooseberry) and hardy kiwi (A. arguta)
- **Passionfruit:** Passiflora spp. including banana passionfruit (P. mollissima)

Trellis over the garden is inappropriate in mesothermal climates (unlike deserts). Vertical zigzag, inverted "V", or house wall and garden fence trellis is best, aligned north/south where possible.

## Vine Crops in Humid Tropics

In tropical village complexes, vines form part of the multi-layered food garden:
- **Canopy layer:** Tall trees and palms
- **Mid-layer:** Fruit trees, legumes
- **Vine layer:** Climbing between trees -- yams, passionfruit, winged bean, choko (Sechium edule)
- **Ground layer:** Sweet potato, cassava, taro

Passionfruit appears in chicken tractor designs as a fence/trellis crop grown on the chicken yard perimeter.

## Berry Fruit and Vine Nursery

The farm forestry section lists berry fruit and vine nurseries as a valid enterprise in mesothermal climates: "a great many species grow in these climates." Combined plant nurseries provide seed, bud, scion materials, vegetative propagules, and grafted, layered, or tissue-cultured plants.

## Farm Forestry and Vine Crops

Mollison notes a "wider market for relatively exotic vine crops, fruits, and nuts" as a driver of farm transition to perennial systems. The range of potential vine crops is, for all practical purposes, unlimited.

## Key Design Principles

1. **Vertical space utilisation:** Trellis systems multiply productive area without increasing ground footprint
2. **Climate modification:** Vines on buildings cool interiors in summer, allow sun in winter (deciduous)
3. **Wind protection:** Fedges serve as productive windbreaks on exposed sites
4. **Microclimate creation:** Shade from vines reduces soil temperature and evaporation
5. **Orientation:** Align trellis north/south for even sun; close eastern side more completely
6. **Species diversity:** Combine food vines with nitrogen-fixing climbers
7. **Structural integration:** Attach trellis to buildings, fences, and animal housing for multiple functions
