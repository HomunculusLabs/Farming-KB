Updated: 2026-04-20

Establishing and maintaining a lawn organically is entirely practical and produces excellent results. ### Organic Feeding
- **Slow-release method**: Apply blood, fish, and bone meal once or at most twice per year -- in early spring and again in early summer
- **Quick green-up**: Use a liquid manure feed high in nitrogen if desired (see [[cervantes-compost-tea-organic-brewing]] to boost microbial competition
- **Moss**: A symptom of poor drainage, excessive shade, or low fertility. Address the underlying cause rather than treating the moss itself. Scarify in spring, improve drainage, and feed the lawn

## Repairing Lawns
When coarse grass patches appear, hatch and reseed with an equal-parts mixture of grass seed, moist peat, and good soil. Score the area in a criss-cross fashion with a penknife first. For bare patches caused by wear or disease, top-dress with a mixture of sieved soil, sand, and compost before overseeding.

## Seasonal Lawn Care Calendar

- **Spring**: Rake to remove thatch, aerate compacted areas, apply first feed, reseed bare patches, mow as growth begins
- **Summer**: Mow regularly, water deeply during dry spells (infrequent deep watering encourages deep roots), control weeds by hand
- **Autumn**: Raise mowing height, apply final feed (low-nitrogen), aerate, top-dress worn areas, collect fallen leaves
- **Winter**: Keep off the lawn when frozen or waterlogged, clear leaves and debris, service mowing equipment

## Choosing Grass Seed Mixtures

Select grass seed mixtures appropriate to your site conditions and intended use:

- **General-purpose lawns**: A blend of perennial ryegrass and fescues provides durability and reasonable appearance
- **Fine lawns**: Chewings fescue and browntop bent create the close, velvety turf of formal lawns but require more maintenance
- **Shady areas**: Include shade-tolerant varieties such as rough stalk meadow grass and creeping red fescue
- **Drought-prone sites**: Hard fescue and tall fescue have deeper root systems and better [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]]

## Watering Wisely

Lawns are remarkably resilient and can survive extended dry periods by going dormant. When watering is necessary, water deeply and infrequently rather than lightly and often. This encourages roots to grow deeper in search of moisture, producing a more drought-resistant lawn. A thorough soaking once or twice a week is far better than daily sprinkling. Water early in the morning to reduce evaporation and prevent fungal diseases that thrive in damp overnight conditions.
