# Organic Lawn Care (Hamilton Method)

Updated: 2026-04-20

Establishing and maintaining a lawn organically is entirely practical and produces excellent results. The key principles are proper soil preparation, appropriate feeding with organic fertilizers, correct mowing technique, and natural weed management. [[geoff-hamilton-organic-techniques]] demonstrated that a beautiful lawn can be achieved without any synthetic chemicals whatsoever.

## Establishing a New Lawn

### Seed Lawns
Sow grass seed in early spring or early fall when some wet weather can be expected. Avoid the driest months.

1. Start with a [[mollison-designers-plant-nursery-propagation-bed-design]] -- prepare the soil and let weed seeds germinate, then hoe them out before sowing
2. If soil is light or lacks organic matter, apply a 2in (5cm) layer of [[organic-garden-soil-preparation]] and rake it in; if heavy soil, use coarse sand instead
3. Rake in two handfuls of blood, fish, and bone meal per square yard/meter
4. Walk over the whole area with weight on heels to consolidate the soil
5. Rake level, squinting across the surface to check for undulations
6. Sow seed at 1-1.5 oz (25-35g) per square yard/meter (about two handfuls)
7. Rake seed in, aiming to cover about half with soil
8. Cover with perforated plastic to speed germination and protect from birds; remove when first seed germinates

### Turf Lawns
Turf provides an "instant lawn" ready for use about six weeks after spring laying. Always work from boards (scaffolding boards are ideal) to avoid compacting the soil. Lay rows parallel, butting pieces together tightly. Keep well-watered after laying to prevent shrinking.

## Maintaining an Established Lawn

### Mowing
- Mow only during the growing season; grass stops growing in winter and does not need cutting
- Begin by raking earthworm casts around the lawn (otherwise the mower flattens them, creating seedbeds for weeds)
- Cut in opposite directions on alternate cuts
- Never mow too close -- this encourages bare patches colonized by moss and weeds
- Always leave grass 1/2in (1cm) long
- Remove cuttings to prevent dead material buildup and fungal diseases
- Grass cuttings are the best possible composting material -- never throw them away (see [[composting-science-solomon]])
- Rake with a spring-tine rake at least once a year in spring to remove dead grass (thatch)
- Trim edges with long-handled shears after mowing

### Organic Feeding
- **Slow-release method**: Apply blood, fish, and bone meal once or at most twice per year -- in early spring and again in early summer
- **Quick green-up**: Use a liquid manure feed high in nitrogen if desired (see [[solomon-complete-organic-fertilizer-guide]])
- For a natural approach, simply leaving grass clippings on the lawn returns nitrogen via the Law of Return
- Seaweed meal can be applied as a tonic, supplying trace minerals that promote disease resistance
- Avoid feeding in late summer or autumn -- this encourages soft growth vulnerable to frost and fungal disease

### Drainage
Proper drainage is essential. Improve by adding sand or organic matter. If soil is waterlogged, air cannot reach roots. If it drains too quickly, grass becomes brown and unhealthy. Use a hollow-tined fork to remove cores of soil: fill with sand for heavy soils, or garden compost for light soils. Aerate compacted areas annually in autumn.

### Weed Control (Organic Methods)
- Keep grass growing well and cut regularly to minimize weeds
- **Rosetted weeds** (dandelions, daisies): Cut out regularly with a knife, or drop ordinary table salt on the growing point -- this causes them to shrivel up and die overnight
- Coarse grass patches can be repaired by "hatching and reseeding": score the coarse patch in a criss-cross fashion with a penknife and reseed using an equal-parts mixture of seed, moist peat, and good soil
- Hand-pull individual weeds after rain when the soil is soft
- Maintain dense grass cover to outcompete weed seedlings

### Disease Control Without Chemicals
- **Fusarium patch (snow mould)**: Improve drainage, reduce shade, avoid late-autumn nitrogen feeding, and collect clippings to remove infected material
- **Red thread**: Usually indicates nitrogen deficiency -- feed with blood, fish, and bone meal. Improve air circulation by pruning overhanging shrubs
- **Fairy rings**: Aerate the affected area heavily and water with diluted [[cervantes-compost-tea-organic-brewing]] to boost microbial competition
- **Moss**: A symptom of poor drainage, excessive shade, or low fertility. Address the underlying cause rather than treating the moss itself. Scarify in spring, improve drainage, and feed the lawn

## Repairing Lawns
When coarse grass patches appear, hatch and reseed with an equal-parts mixture of grass seed, moist peat, and good soil. Score the area in a criss-cross fashion with a penknife first. For bare patches caused by wear or disease, top-dress with a mixture of sieved soil, sand, and compost before overseeding.

## Seasonal Lawn Care Calendar

- **Spring**: Rake to remove thatch, aerate compacted areas, apply first feed, reseed bare patches, mow as growth begins
- **Summer**: Mow regularly, water deeply during dry spells (infrequent deep watering encourages deep roots), control weeds by hand
- **Autumn**: Raise mowing height, apply final feed (low-nitrogen), aerate, top-dress worn areas, collect fallen leaves
- **Winter**: Keep off the lawn when frozen or waterlogged, clear leaves and debris, service mowing equipment

## Choosing Grass Seed Mixtures

Select grass seed mixtures appropriate to your site conditions and intended use:

- **General-purpose lawns**: A blend of perennial ryegrass and fescues provides durability and reasonable appearance
- **Fine lawns**: Chewings fescue and browntop bent create the close, velvety turf of formal lawns but require more maintenance
- **Shady areas**: Include shade-tolerant varieties such as rough stalk meadow grass and creeping red fescue
- **Drought-prone sites**: Hard fescue and tall fescue have deeper root systems and better drought tolerance

## Watering Wisely

Lawns are remarkably resilient and can survive extended dry periods by going dormant. When watering is necessary, water deeply and infrequently rather than lightly and often. This encourages roots to grow deeper in search of moisture, producing a more drought-resistant lawn. A thorough soaking once or twice a week is far better than daily sprinkling. Water early in the morning to reduce evaporation and prevent fungal diseases that thrive in damp overnight conditions.
