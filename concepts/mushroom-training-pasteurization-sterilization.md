# [[mushroom-pasteurization]] and Sterilization Methods

Pasteurization and sterilization are essential steps in mushroom cultivation that eliminate
competing organisms from [[mushroom-training-substrate-preparation]] and equipment.
The FAO training manual describes three pasteurization methods for substrate bags (country
style, commercial, and solar) as well as autoclave sterilization for PDA medium and spawn
production. Each method is accessible to farmers with disabilities with appropriate
adaptations.

## Why Pasteurization Matters

Pasteurization kills or weakens harmful microorganisms in the substrate while preserving
enough heat tolerance for the mushroom mycelium to dominate after
[[mushroom-training-inoculation-techniques]]. Without proper pasteurization,
competing fungi and bacteria will colonize the substrate before mushroom mycelium can
establish itself, leading to crop failure.

The manual's troubleshooting section emphasizes that bad pasteurization is a common cause of
mycelium failure. Common errors include not releasing all air before starting the timing,
not maintaining constant temperature, and opening the chamber too quickly.

## Country-Style Pasteurization

This low-cost method uses 200-liter oil drums and is most accessible for small-scale farmers
and new entrepreneurs.

### Equipment

- Steaming pot made from 200-liter drums
- Firewood (or branches, corncobs, and wood as green fuel)
- Iron or bamboo screen (round shape, 5 inches high, to fit inside drum)

### Procedure

1. Clean the steam pot and add about 4 inches of water

2. Place the iron or wooden screen so it sits about 1 inch higher than the water level

3. Place pasteurized substrate bags in the system until full (80 to 100 bags per drum)

4. Cover everything with double layers of old rice bags

5. Place plastic over the rice bags and close tightly

6. Light the fire and maintain temperature constant for 3 to 4 hours from the time
   temperature reaches 90 to 100 degrees Celsius (when steam comes out continuously)

7. Important: Temperature must remain constant and there should always be water in the drum

8. When completed, remove the firewood from the stove

9. Let cool down for approximately 20 minutes, then remove cover bags and cool further

10. Transfer bags to the [[mushroom-training-inoculation-techniques]]

## Commercial Pasteurization

Commercial pasteurization chambers are used at training centers and larger farms.

### Equipment

- Pasteurization chamber with water reservoir
- Thermometer
- Steam boiler system (optional, can use direct heating)
- Gas burner or green fuel system

### Procedure

1. Clean the water reservoir at the bottom of the pasteurization chamber by releasing the
   valve. Dry with cloth

2. Refill water up to the marked level

3. Transfer iron racks into the chamber one by one until full (approximately 9 bags per rack)

4. Close the door securely and verify the thermometer

5. Light burner and maintain 98 to 100 degrees Celsius for 3 to 4 hours. Make sure all air
   is released from the chamber when starting to keep the timing accurate

6. For boiler-generated steam, open the valve to send steam to the pasteurization chamber.
   Make sure all air is released. Adjust surrounding air using valve

7. Leave to cool down by releasing the door of the chamber (1.5 to 2 hours)

8. Transfer bags to the cleaned inoculation area

## Solar Heating Pasteurization

Solar pasteurization is an optional alternative that uses sunlight instead of fuel.

### Procedure

1. Fill substrate bags into the chamber. Clear the area and bring substrate bags

2. Close the cover and ensure that sunlight will reach under the cover

3. Leave under the sun for one whole day

4. Transfer bags to the inoculation area

This method works best in hot climates with consistent sunlight and is most practical during
dry seasons. It is not reliable during rainy or cloudy periods.

## Autoclave Sterilization

For [[mushroom-training-spawn-production]] and sorghum grain [[spawn-production]],
higher temperature sterilization in an autoclave is required.

### PDA Medium Sterilization

- Place prepared PDA bottles in the autoclave
- Sterilize at 121 degrees Celsius for 20 to 30 minutes
- Let cool down to approximately 37 degrees Celsius
- Place bottles in a slanted position to increase the medium surface area
- Check for contamination after settling

### Sorghum Seed Sterilization

- Transfer all prepared sorghum seed bottles to the sterilization chamber
- Close the chamber and fire up the burner or stove
- Release all air from the chamber before starting the timing
- Keep pressure at 15 lb/sq.inch (121 degrees Celsius) for 30 minutes for small chambers
  and 45 minutes for medium chambers
- Let bottles cool down before transfer to a clean area

## Adaptations for People with Disabilities

The pasteurization chamber must be built lower than standard height for easy access by
people in wheelchairs and those unable to stand. This is one of the key
[[mushroom-training-adapted-cultivation]] recommended in the
manual. Country-style pasteurization using drums can also be adapted with raised platforms
or modified to reduce heavy lifting.
