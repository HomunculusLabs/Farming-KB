## Strategies for Drought Years

If rain stops and you face a developing drought, Solomon recommends
this sequence of responses:

**First:** Thin the crop. Harvest every second carrot or beet in every
row, regardless of size. This instantly reduces moisture competition
while providing double the capillary moisture to the remaining plants.
By preventing moisture stress, you might end up harvesting more total
weight than if you had left everything crowded.

**Second:** Hoe thoroughly at least weekly. This creates a surface dust
mulch that prevents moisture loss through evaporation and helps the
soil accept any chance showers that may come.

**Third:** Start fertigation if possible. Dissolving fertilizer in
water and delivering it directly to the root zone of each plant
provides both moisture and nutrients where they are needed most. This
practice is valuable even when the plants are otherwise getting enough
water, because it dramatically increases water-use efficiency.

The key is to ensure that the water sinks straight in, making a wet
spot no larger than the plant's root zone. This way, moisture
penetrates deeply into the subsoil. In sandy soil, moisture spreads
widely, so smaller volumes given more frequently work best. In clay
soils, which accept moisture more slowly, larger volumes spread
broadly. About five gallons (20 liters) every three weeks works well
for clay soils with 20 percent or more clay content.
