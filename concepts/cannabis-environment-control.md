## Overview

Environment control is the practice of managing temperature, humidity, CO2, airflow, and odor in a cannabis grow space. Consistent environmental conditions directly impact plant health, yield, potency, and terpene production. An uncontrolled environment leads to pest and disease problems, slowed growth, and reduced quality.

## Temperature

### Ideal Ranges

| Stage | Lights On | Lights Off |
|---|---|---|
| Seedling | 75-82 F (24-28 C) | 68-75 F (20-24 C) |
| Vegetative | 72-82 F (22-28 C) | 62-72 F (17-22 C) |
| Flowering | 68-80 F (20-27 C) | 60-70 F (16-21 C) |

A temperature differential of 10-15 F between day and night supports healthy metabolic cycling. Night temperatures below 55 F (13 C) can cause purple discoloration and slow growth. Temperatures above 90 F (32 C) stress plants, reduce terpene production, and increase transpiration demands beyond root capacity.

### Temperature Control Equipment

- **Air conditioners**: Mini-split or portable units for tent/room cooling
- **Exhaust fans**: Remove hot air; sized to exchange the room volume every 1-3 minutes
- **Intake fans**: Passive or active intake to replace exhausted air
- **Heaters**: Seedling heat mats for root zones; space heaters for cold rooms (use with caution near plants)

### Leaf Temperature

Infrared thermometers or thermal cameras measure actual leaf surface temperature, which can differ significantly from ambient air temperature. Under high-intensity lighting, leaf temperature can be 5-15 F above ambient. This is one reason PPFD targets and temperature must be balanced together.

## Humidity and VPD

### Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)

VPD is the most precise way to manage humidity relative to temperature. It measures the difference between the moisture in the air and the moisture the air could hold at saturation. VPD drives transpiration, which in turn drives nutrient uptake and cooling.

| Growth Stage | Target VPD (kPa) | Approximate RH at 78 F |
|---|---|---|
| Clones / Seedlings | 0.4-0.8 | 75-85% |
| Vegetative | 0.8-1.2 | 60-70% |
| Early Flower | 1.0-1.3 | 55-65% |
| Late Flower | 1.2-1.5 | 45-55% |

Low VPD (too humid) means plants transpire slowly, reducing nutrient uptake and inviting mold. High VPD (too dry) causes plants to close stomata to conserve water, halting growth and photosynthesis.

### Humidity Control Equipment

- **Humidifiers**: Ultrasonic or evaporative units to raise humidity during vegetative and seedling stages
- **Dehumidifiers**: Essential during flowering to keep humidity below 55%. Size for at least 30-50 pints/day per 4x4 tent.
- **Hygrometers**: Place multiple units at canopy height for accurate readings
- **Controller systems**: Inkbird, AC Infinity, or similar controllers automate humidifier/dehumidifier based on setpoints

## CO2 Enrichment

Supplemental CO2 can increase growth rates by 20-30% when all other factors (light, nutrients, temperature) are also elevated to match.

### CO2 Requirements

- **Ambient**: ~400 ppm
- **Supplemented target**: 1,000-1,500 ppm
- **Above 1,500 ppm**: Diminishing returns; above 2,000 ppm is hazardous to humans

### When to Use CO2

CO2 enrichment only makes sense when:

- Light intensity is above 800 PPFD (plants can't process extra CO2 without more light)
- Temperature can be maintained at 80-85 F (higher CO2 allows plants to tolerate warmer temps)
- The grow space is sealed or semi-sealed (ventilated rooms exhaust CO2 before it accumulates)

### CO2 Equipment

- **Compressed CO2 tanks**: Regulator + timer or controller. Most precise method.
- **CO2 generators**: Burn propane or natural gas. Produce heat and water vapor as byproducts — only for large rooms.
- **Mycelium / mushroom bags**: Produce small amounts of CO2. Insufficient for meaningful enrichment but can supplement small tents.
- **Controllers**: Sensors that monitor ppm and automatically release CO2 to maintain target levels

## Air Circulation

Proper air movement is critical for plant health. Stagnant air creates microclimates that promote mold, pests, and uneven growth.

### Oscillating Fans

- Minimum 1 oscillating fan per 2x2 area of canopy
- Fans should create gentle leaf movement without aggressive whipping
- Position fans to avoid constant direct wind on any single plant
- Run continuously, including during dark periods

### Canopy-Level Airflow

- Horizontal airflow across the canopy prevents hot spots
- Floor-level airflow prevents cold pockets
- Ensure intake air mixes well throughout the space

### Exhaust and Intake

- **Passive intake**: Open vents or holes near the bottom of the space. Relies on negative pressure from exhaust.
- **Active intake**: Powered fan pushing air in. Necessary for larger rooms or sealed environments.
- Sizing rule: Exhaust CFM should be able to exchange the room volume in 1-3 minutes.

## Odor Control

Cannabis produces strong aromas, especially during late flowering. Odor management is often legally required and is a basic courtesy.

### Carbon Filtration

- **Inline carbon filters**: Connected to the exhaust system. Air passes through activated carbon which adsorbs odor molecules.
- Size the filter to match or exceed the exhaust fan CFM rating.
- Replace carbon every 6-12 months depending on usage.
- Pre-filters (mesh screens) extend carbon life by trapping dust and particulates.

### Additional Methods

- **ONA (Odor Neutralizing Agents)**: Gel or spray neutralizers. Backup to carbon filtration.
- **Ozone generators**: Effective but potentially harmful to plants and humans if not used correctly. Must be placed in exhaust ducting, not in the grow space.
- **Negative ion generators**: Neutralize odor molecules. Less effective than carbon for strong cannabis odors.

## Integrated Control Systems

Modern grow controllers integrate temperature, humidity, CO2, and light management:

- **AC Infinity Controller 69**: Controls fans, heaters, and humidifiers based on temp/humidity setpoints with VPD monitoring
- **TrolMaster**: Full environmental control with CO2, lighting, and irrigation integration
- **Inkbird ITC-308**: Budget-friendly temperature controller for heating/cooling

Consistent environmental control during [[cannabis-vegetative-stage]] and [[cannabis-flowering-stage]] reduces stress and pest susceptibility, as covered in [[cannabis-pest-management]] and [[cannabis-disease-guide]]. Proper [[cannabis-nutrient-recipes]] also depend on stable environmental conditions for optimal uptake.
