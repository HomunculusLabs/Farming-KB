## The Stropharia Cubensis Relationship

McKenna identifies [Stropharia cubensis
([[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]) as the central mushroom in human evolutionary history.
This species alone among grassland psilocybin mushrooms contains the
compound in concentrated amounts without nausea-producing side effects. It
is pandemic throughout tropical regions wherever zebu-type cattle graze. The
relationship between humans, cattle, and this mushroom -- a quasi-symbiotic
custom -- is estimated at roughly one million years old, extending no
further than the era of the nomadic human hunter.

The Non Nak Tha archaeological site in Thailand (dated to 15,000 B.P.)
revealed zebu cattle bones coincident with human graves, and S. cubensis
remains common there today. This suggests mushroom use was a human trait
that emerged wherever human populations and cattle evolved together. Ample
evidence supports the notion that S. cubensis is the Ur plant, "our
umbilicus to the feminine mind of the planet."

## Mesoamerican Mushroom Cults

The Oaxacan mushroom cult of Mexico is three millennia old -- the oldest
known continuous psychedelic mushroom tradition. [Mushroom stones of Guatemala, dating from approximately 1000 B.C. to 900 A.D.,
provide archaeological evidence of a sophisticated mushroom religion in
Mesoamerica. These stone artifacts suggest a cultic practice centered on
psilocybin mushrooms that persisted for nearly two thousand years.

## The Wasson Expedition and Its Aftermath

Gordon Wasson's 1955 experience with the Mazatec curandera Maria Sabina
in Huautla de Jimenez, Mexico, and the subsequent 1957 LIFE magazine article
"Seeking the Magic Mushroom," catalyzed Western interest in psilocybin.
Albert Hofmann subsequently isolated and identified psilocybin and psilocin
from these mushrooms. Roger Heim, the French mycologist, had already
identified the species involved. The Wasson-Hofmann collaboration was the
beginning of serious scientific attention to the Mazatec mushroom practice.
