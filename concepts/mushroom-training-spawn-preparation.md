## Overview

Spawn preparation involves three sequential stages:

1. **PDA medium production** — preparing the nutrient agar growth medium
2. **Tissue culture selection** — isolating pure mycelium from a healthy mushroom
3. **Grain spawn multiplication** — expanding mycelium onto sorghum seeds for substrate inoculation

Not all trainees need to master every stage. Many buy inoculated substrate bags ready for fruiting. However, understanding the full process enables future expansion and provides supplemental income through selling spawn bags. Expected contamination loss is approximately 3%.

## Stage 1: PDA Medium Production

PDA (Potato Dextrose Agar) is the standard growth medium for mushroom tissue culture. It provides the nutrients mycelium needs to grow in a controlled laboratory setting.

### Materials

- Potatoes: 200g
- Dextrose: 20g (commercial grade)
- Agar powder: 20g (commercial grade)
- Water: 1 liter
- Cotton (gauze) for plugging bottles
- Small flat bottles (small whiskey bottles work well as containers)

### Procedure

1. Visually inspect potatoes for spots or rot; use only sound potatoes
2. Purchase commercial-grade dextrose and agar
3. Wash and cut potatoes into 1cm cubes (skin on or off)
4. Clean bottles thoroughly
5. Place potatoes in 1 liter of water; simmer for 15-20 minutes
6. Remove potatoes; keep the broth as clear as possible
7. Add water to broth to reach exactly 1 liter
8. Return to stove; add dextrose first, then agar
9. Stir continuously at regular speed until completely dissolved
10. Pour liquid PDA into bottles to a depth of 5-10mm
11. Plug each bottle with cotton
12. Autoclave at 121C for 20-30 minutes to ensure complete sterilization
13. Let cool to approximately 37C
14. Place bottles at a slant to increase medium surface area — PDA should approach the neck but must not touch the cotton plug
15. Once PDA is settled, transfer bottles to a clean shelf in the clean room
16. Check for contamination (dark spots or lines indicate contamination)
17. Wait 2-3 days; if no contamination appears, PDA is ready for tissue culture

## Stage 2: Tissue Culture Selection

Tissue culture isolates pure mushroom mycelium from a fruiting body. This is the most contamination-sensitive step — work in a laminar flow cabinet whenever possible.

### Materials

- Special needle with insulated handle
- Alcohol lamp and alcohol
- Cotton (gauze)
- Matches or lighter
- PDA bottles
- Laminar flow cabinet (or protected clean environment)
- UV lamp

### Selecting the Mushroom

Choose a strong, healthy mushroom specimen:
- Not too mature, not too young
- Not too humid — wait at least 2-3 hours after watering
- Stiff stalk
- Clean, far from any contaminated mushrooms

### Procedure

1. Clean the room, all tools, inside and outside of laminar flow cabinet with alcohol
2. Transfer PDA bottles and tools into the cabinet
3. Turn on UV lamp and laminar flow; after 10-15 minutes, turn off UV but leave laminar flow running
4. Clean both hands and bottles with alcohol; insert hands into cabinet
5. Hold needle with 2 fingers at a 45-degree angle; flame until needle turns red hot
6. Let needle cool 15-20 seconds — do not touch any surface
7. Using other fingers, tear the mushroom lengthwise (DO NOT use a knife to cut)
8. With the needle, cut a small piece (2mm x 2mm) of fleshy tissue from inside the mushroom, in the middle between cap and stalk — ensure it is clean and did not touch the outside
9. Flame around the bottle mouth; remove cotton plug near flame
10. Insert needle and place tissue piece in the middle of the PDA surface
11. Close bottle immediately near the flame with cotton plug
12. Critical: bottle bottom must always be lower than the mouth; mouth stays near flame at all times
13. Label with date, mushroom type, and mother spawn number

### PDA-to-PDA Transfer

Because initial tissue culture carries high contamination risk, start with only a few bottles. Then multiply from verified clean mycelium:

1. Cut a 5mm x 5mm piece of clean white mycelium from PDA
2. Transfer to a new PDA bottle using the same sterile technique
3. Incubation to full mycelium: 10-15 days (species dependent)
4. Check daily for infection; monitor growth rate
5. Store mature mycelium in a cool place or the vegetable compartment of the refrigerator
6. Keep detailed observation notes

## Stage 3: Grain Spawn on Sorghum Seeds

### Grain Selection

Various grains can be used: sorghum, millet, or wheat. Requirements for all grains:
- Recently harvested
- Few broken kernels
- Little contamination
- No fungi, no insects
- No more than 12% humidity

Sorghum is the preferred grain for tropical Asian cultivation.

### Materials

- Sorghum seeds
- Flask-type bottles
- Cotton, paper squares (7cm x 7cm), rubber bands
- Alcohol lamp, alcohol

### Preparation

1. Soak sorghum overnight: 2 liters water per 1 kg grain
2. Wash and strain to remove all water
3. Steam 30-45 minutes to soften grains (cook approximately 25%)
4. Drain and spread to cool and decrease moisture
5. Fill bottles 3/4 full with sorghum seeds
6. Carefully prepare and tightly plug with cotton

### Sterilization

7. Transfer all prepared bottles to the sterilization chamber
8. Close chamber; fire up burner or stove
9. Release all air from the chamber before starting
10. Maintain pressure at 15 lb/sq.inch (121C) for 30 minutes (small chamber) or 45 minutes (medium chamber)
11. Let bottles cool down

### Inoculation

12. Transfer bottles to a clean and cool place
13. Clean laminar flow chamber with alcohol; light UV lamp for 10-15 minutes; turn off UV (leave laminar flow on)
