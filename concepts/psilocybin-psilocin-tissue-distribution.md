# Psilocybin and Psilocin Tissue Distribution

The distribution of psilocybin and psilocin between the cap (pileus) and stem (stipe) of *[[fungal-biogeography-distribution-patterns]] were observed for psilocybin:

1. **Cap-enriched strains** (M.R. and Ecuadorian): Caps contained approximately 1.6× more psilocybin per gram dry weight than stems. In the M.R. strain, caps had 6.7 mg/g vs. stems at 4.2 mg/g. In the Ecuadorian strain, caps had 7.6 mg/g vs. stems at 4.7 mg/g.

2. **Equal distribution strain** (Amazon): Caps and stems contained equal amounts of psilocybin (5.7 mg/g each). This strain showed no tissue-specific concentration pattern for psilocybin.

The fact that different strains show different cap-to-stem ratios indicates that tissue-specific alkaloid distribution is a genetically controlled trait rather than a universal feature of the species.

### Psilocin Concentration

Psilocin distribution showed a pattern that was largely the inverse of psilocybin:

1. **M.R. and Ecuadorian strains**: The small amount of psilocin present was **entirely in the stems**. Caps contained zero detectable psilocin, while stems had 0.35–0.4 mg/g.

2. **Amazon strain**: A trace of psilocin (0.1 mg/g) was found in the cap, while the stem contained none. This strain showed the opposite pattern from the other two.

The Amazon strain's distribution is notable because it also differed from the other strains in [[isolation-metabolically-active-arbuscules-intraradical-hyphae]]. If the biosynthetic pathway for psilocybin is most active in basidial tissue, higher concentrations in the cap would be expected.

However, the Amazon strain's equal distribution between cap and stem suggests that [[psilocin-emergence-in-later-flushes]] (see [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]]. It is possible that later-flush mushrooms have more psilocin in caps, or that the cap-stem ratio shifts as the culture ages.

## Analytical Methodology Considerations

The tissue-specific distribution has important implications for researchers measuring alkaloid levels:

- **Whole-mushroom homogenates**: Most studies analyze entire dried mushrooms (caps + stems ground together). This gives a weighted average that depends on the cap-to-stem mass ratio, which varies with strain, flush number, and growing conditions.
- **Cap-only analysis**: Would give higher psilocybin readings for cap-enriched strains but might miss psilocin that is concentrated in stems.
- **Stem-only analysis**: Would give lower psilocybin readings for cap-enriched strains but might show psilocin levels not detectable in cap tissue.
- **Reporting standard**: Analytical studies should always specify whether they analyzed caps, stems, or whole mushrooms, and should report the cap-to-stem mass ratio of their samples.
