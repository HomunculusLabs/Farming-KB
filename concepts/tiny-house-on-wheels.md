# Tiny House on Wheels

A Tiny House on Wheels (THOW) is a dwelling built [[query-how-do-i-start-a-profitable-market-garden-on-a-small-plot]] trailer chassis, typically 8.5 feet wide and 13.5-22 feet long, designed to be mobile yet function [[query-can-bamboo-be-used-as-a-building-material-and-how-do-i-get-started]] permanent home. THOWs are a popular entry point for homesteaders seeking affordable, self-built housing with the flexibility to relocate. Building codes generally exempt structures on wheels, giving builders freedom in design.

## Legal Considerations

- Width: Maximum 8.5 feet without a special transport permit
- Height: Maximum 13.5 feet from ground to roof peak for highway travel
- Length: No federal limit, but many states require permits over 40 feet combined (truck + trailer)
- Weight: Most states require CDL for trailers over 26,000 lbs GVWR
- Parking: Zoning varies widely — some areas allow THOWs as accessory dwelling units, others prohibit them
- Insurance: Specialty insurers cover THOWs; standard homeowner policies do not
- DMV registration: Register as a travel trailer or homemade trailer in your state

## Trailer Selection

### Types of Trailers

**Flatbed Utility Trailer ([[query-what-are-the-most-common-mushroom-contaminants-and-how-do-i-identify-them]])**
- Readily available, customizable
- Cost: $3,000-8,000 new, $1,500-5,000 used
- Look for: 10,000-14,000 lb GVWR, 5/8 inch or thicker deck, heavy-duty axles
- Deck-over design preferred [[query-how-do-i-prune-brambles-for-maximum-fruit-production]] headroom

**Custom Tiny House Trailer**
- Purpose-built with integrated metal subfloor, wheel wells, and tie-down points
- Cost: $5,000-12,000
- Advantages: Better weight distribution, designed [[query-how-to-read-cannabis-trichomes-for-the-perfect-harvest-time]] application

**Dropped-Axle Trailer**
- Axles positioned lower for reduced overall height
- Saves 4-6 inches of height — valuable for staying under the 13.5-foot limit

### Key Trailer Specifications

- GVWR (Gross Vehicle Weight Rating): Minimum 10,000 lbs recommended
- Axle capacity: Dual 5,000-7,000 lb axles
- Brakes: Electric brakes on both axles (required by law above 3,000 lbs)
- Coupler: 2-5/16 inch ball coupler for weights over 6,000 lbs
- Safety chains and breakaway cable included
- DOT-approved lighting and reflectors

## Weight Management

Weight [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-fungal-dominant-and-bacterial-dominant-compost-tea]] critical constraint in THOW design. Every material choice has weight implications.

### Typical Weight Breakdown

| Component | Weight (lbs) |
|-----------|-------------|
| Trailer (empty) | 2,500-4,000 |
| Framing (lumber + metal) | 1,500-3,000 |
| Subfloor and flooring | 500-1,000 |
| Sheathing and siding | 800-1,500 |
| Insulation | 200-400 |
| Roofing | 400-800 |
| Windows and doors | 300-600 |
| Plumbing system | 300-500 |
| Electrical system | 200-400 |
| Interior finish | 500-1,500 |
| Furniture and belongings | 1,000-2,000 |
| Water (full tanks) | 400-800 |
| **Total** | **8,600-16,500** |

### Weight Reduction Strategies

- Use lightweight framing: 2x4 instead of 2x6 where possible
- Steel framing weighs 30% less than wood but costs more
- Lightweight insulation: rigid foam instead of fiberglass
- Thin-profile flooring: engineered hardwood or vinyl plank instead of solid hardwood
- Aluminum windows instead of vinyl-clad wood
- Compact appliances: RV-rated refrigerator, tankless water heater

## Insulation Options

### Spray Foam (Best Performance)

- Closed-cell spray foam provides R-6.5 per inch
- Acts as air barrier, vapor barrier, and structural reinforcement
- Eliminates thermal bridging through framing
- Cost: $2,000-4,000 for a typical THOW
- [[query-what-are-the-different-phytoremediation-mechanisms-and-which-plants-are-best-for-each]]: Cold climates where insulation performance is critical

### Rigid Foam Board (Best Value)

- XPS or polyiso foam boards, R-5 to R-6.5 per inch
- Install between framing members, tape all seams
- Cost: $500-1,000
- Best for: Budget-conscious builders in moderate climates

### Wool Insulation (Best Natural Option)

- Sheep's wool batts, R-3.5 to R-4 per inch
- Naturally moisture-wicking, fire-resistant, sound-dampening
- Cost: $1,500-2,500
- Best for: Natural builders prioritizing non-toxic materials

### Mineral Wool (Good All-Around)

- Rockwool or mineral wool batts, R-3.3 to R-4.2 per inch
- Fire-resistant, moisture-resistant, sound-dampening
- Cost: $800-1,500
- Best for: Balance of performance, cost, and safety

## Off-Grid Systems

### Solar Power

- Roof-mounted panels: 400-800 watts typical
- Battery bank: 200-400 Ah lithium (LiFePO4 preferred)
- Charge controller and inverter: 2000-3000W pure sine wave
- Daily capacity: 2-4 kWh (enough for lights, laptop, phone, water pump)
- See [[homestead-internet-options|connectivity options]] for off-grid communication

### Water System

- Fresh water tank: 30-50 gallons
- 12V water pump and pressure tank
- Propane tankless water heater
- Greywater tank: 20-40 gallons (or [[off-grid-waste-management|greywater]] disposal system)

### Composting Toilet

- Nature's Head, Separett, or Air Head are popular THOW models
- No blackwater tank needed — significant weight and complexity savings
- Vent fan requires small 12V power draw
- See [[off-grid-waste-management|waste management]] for detailed comparison

### Heating and Cooling

- Propane heater: Dickinson Newport or similar marine heater (vented)
- Mini-split: 9,000 BTU unit heats and cools (requires 1500W power)
- Wood stove: Cubic Mini or similar small wood stove (requires proper clearance and venting)
- [[passive-solar-design|Passive solar]] orientation helps with both heating and cooling

## Design Considerations

- Loft vs. main floor bedroom: Lofts save space but limit headroom and accessibility
- Stairs vs. ladder: Stairs provide storage underneath and are safer; ladders save space
- Kitchen layout: Galley (along one wall) maximizes counter space in narrow footprint
- Bathroom: Minimum 30x36 inches for shower; wet baths combine toilet and shower
