## Beneficial Insects and Biological Pest Control

The [[fukuoka-pesticide-cascade-collapse-natural-balance]] ensures
that predators exist to feed on garden pests. A simple rule for
distinguishing friend from foe is that pests are generally slow-moving
while predators are faster and more agile. By careful observation and
deliberate encouragement of helpful creatures, gardeners can let nature
handle much of the pest control work.

## The Principle of Natural Balance

In nature, no single species dominates because predators keep pest
populations in check. The chemical gardener disrupts this balance by
killing both pests and their predators with broad-spectrum pesticides,
creating a vicious cycle where stronger and more poisonous chemicals are
needed. The organic gardener, by contrast, cultivates a diverse miniature
ecosystem of pests and predators where no species builds up to
unacceptable levels, provided the balance is not disturbed.

Growing a wide diversity of plants attracts and builds up populations of
beneficial insects. By mixing ornamental plants and vegetables in borders,
gardeners can camouflage host plants from pests that locate them by sight.
There is also evidence that vegetable plots left slightly weed-infested
suffer less pest damage, though yields may be lower due to competition.

## Key Beneficial Insects

### Ladybugs

Both the easily identifiable adult ladybug and its slate-gray larvae eat
vast quantities of aphids. They cannot be attracted by specific plants but
are encouraged by cultivating a varied selection of plant species. A single
ladybug can consume hundreds of aphids during its lifetime. Their presence
is a reliable indicator of a [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]] to hide
under during the day. Using the closely planted [[fukuoka-dutch-priest-vegetable-garden-spiritual-renewal]] and growing green-manure crops between main crops helps
maintain the conditions ground beetles need. Keeping the ground covered
with mulch also provides daytime shelter.

### Centipedes

Centipedes are fast-moving predators of many small insects and slugs.
They are light brown with longer legs than millipedes. Like ground
beetles, centipedes need ground cover to hide under during the day and
emerge at night to hunt, even climbing plants to reach their prey.

## Beneficial Animals

### Birds

Although sometimes regarded as garden pests, birds do more good than harm
overall. They eat grubs, caterpillars, slugs, and aphids in large
quantities. Encourage them with food tables, bird baths, and nest boxes.
Different birds have different nesting requirements, so providing a
variety of options attracts more species.
