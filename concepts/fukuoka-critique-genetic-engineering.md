## The Hubris of Creation

Fukuoka viewed genetic engineering as the ultimate expression of human hubris
— the
attempt to create life according to human specifications. This represents
a qualitative
escalation from previous agricultural interventions. Even chemical agriculture
"merely"
adds substances to the environment; genetic engineering modifies the fundamental
blueprints of life itself.

He would likely have seen modern gene editing (CRISPR) as a further step
in the same
direction — more precise, but still operating from the same flawed premise
that nature
needs human improvement.

## Ecological Consequences

Fukuoka predicted that genetically modified organisms would disrupt ecological
relationships
in unpredictable ways. When a modified crop crosses with wild relatives,
or when
modified organisms escape cultivation, the consequences ripple through ecosystems
in ways that cannot be foreseen. The precautionary principle is not sufficient
because
the relevant time scale for ecological consequences is far longer than any
testing
period.
