## Summer Fruiting Species

### Reishis (Ganoderma species)

The genus Ganoderma includes several species that fruit during
warm summer months. G. lucidum, G. curtisii, G. oregonense,
G. resinaceum, G. tsugae, and G. applanatum each have their
own fruiting windows within the summer season. Reishis fruit
on hardwood logs or stumps and prefer warm, humid conditions.
Their perennial conks persist for months, allowing extended
harvests from a single colonization event.

### Garden Giant (Stropharia rugosoannulata)

The garden giant, also known as the wine-cap stropharia, is
ideal for first-time outdoor cultivators. It fruits through
summer on wood chip beds and is remarkably vigorous once
established. Its large, burgundy-capped mushrooms are both
edible and visually striking. The garden giant is a heliotrope,
thriving in sunnier locations than most mushrooms, often in
association with grasses that provide microclimate benefits.

### Elm Oyster (Hypsizygus ulmarius)

The elm oyster fruits in summer on hardwood logs and stumps,
producing substantial clusters with a firmer texture and longer
shelf life than many Pleurotus relatives. Its preference for
elm and other hardwoods makes it a natural companion for
properties with mature shade trees.

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)

While often associated with spring and fall, shiitake can fruit
throughout summer on properly managed logs. Maintaining adequate
moisture through regular soaking or irrigation is the key.
Many cultivators use a shock approach, submerging logs in cold
water for 24 hours to simulate rain and trigger a coordinated
flush approximately one week later.
