# Oyster Mushroom Green Mold Disease (Trichoderma)

Green mold disease is one of the most serious fungal diseases affecting oyster mushroom cultivation.
Any disease caused by green-colored mold fungi on mushroom bags or beds is called green mold
disease. The characteristic green color comes from spores, not from hyphae -- the hyphal color of
these fungi is usually white, making early detection difficult. More than 30 fungi have been reported
as causal agents of green mold disease on mushrooms.

## Pathogens

### Major Trichoderma Species

The primary pathogens reported for oyster mushroom green mold disease include:

- *Trichoderma virens* (= *Gliocladium virens*)
- *T. viride*
- *T. harzianum*
- *T. koningii*

### Regional Variations

Major pathogens can vary depending on region, cultivation method, and substrate medium, since more
than thirty fungi are known to cause green mold on mushrooms and each has different pathogenicity
and proliferation conditions:

- *T. harzianum* biotype 2: major pathogen in **Europe**
- *T. harzianum* biotype 4: major pathogen on button mushrooms in the **USA**

### Hypocrea spp. (Sexual Stage)

Some *Trichoderma* species have both an asexual cycle (producing green conidiospores) and a sexual
stage (*Hypocrea* spp.). *Hypocrea* species form **white or brown stromata** in which sexual spores
(ascospores) are produced. Recently, *Hypocrea* species that do not produce an asexual stage in
their life cycle have been shown to cause severe problems in oyster mushroom cultivation in Korea.
These are particularly dangerous because they do not turn the infected area green, making them
much harder to detect.

## Symptoms

### Early Stage (White Mycelial Phase)

The hyphal growth stage of green mold pathogens is difficult to distinguish from mushroom mycelium
by color since both are white. However, careful observation reveals key differences:

- Green mold fungi form **denser mycelia** than oyster mushroom
- More **aerial hyphae** are produced compared to mushroom mycelium
- The texture appears more compact and cottony

### Green Spore Stage

The characteristic green color appears when the pathogen produces **conidiospores** from aerial
hyphae. If the pathogen was introduced at the spawning stage, green patches typically appear
**10-15 days later** on the cultivation bed.

### Hypocrea Infection

- White stroma formed by *Hypocrea* spp. is visually similar to **primordia** of oyster mushroom
- Brown stromata may also form
- By the time stroma appears, the pathogen has already colonized the substrate deeply and widely
- No green color is produced, making identification extremely difficult

### Progression

Mushroom hyphae stop growing around the green patch and are gradually covered by green mold.
Left untreated, the contamination spreads to consume the entire substrate.

## Control Measures

### Prevention (Most Important)

**Sanitation and hygiene are the most important control methods for green mold disease.** Strict
adherence to basic practices for disease and pest management is essential.

Key preventive measures:

- **Substrate quality**: Severe infestations are found in poorly pasteurized substrates with
  uneven moisture content. Ensure thorough and even pasteurization.
- **Spawn integrity**: Never use green mold-contaminated spawn. Any green patch in or around spawn
  bottles is a major source of pathogenic spores. Dust from a green patch can inoculate entire
  bags or beds during the inoculation stage.
- **Clean environment**: Maintain the highest levels of hygiene in all mushroom operations.

### Active Treatment

- **Spot removal**: Observe mushroom substrates carefully during the hyphal growth stage. Remove
  or treat any spot with dense white mycelial growth that may indicate green mold.
- **Sporgon treatment**: Spray or drench the affected area with **500 ppm Sporgon**
  (prochloraz-manganese complex; 50%). This is reported as the most effective chemical treatment.
- **Preventive Sporgon**: Spraying affected parts with **250-500 ppm Sporgon before pasteurization**
  is reported to prevent green mold and *Hypocrea* disease.

### Chemical Control Agents

| Chemical | Status | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Sporgon (prochloraz-manganese complex, 50%) | **Most effective** | 250-500 ppm; use preventively or as spot treatment |
| Benomyl | Known but declining | Resistant strains common in Korea |
| Thiabendazole | Known but declining | Resistant strains common in Korea |

Resistant strains of the pathogens are increasingly common in Korea, and recent experimental results
show that prochloraz-manganese complex (Sporgon) is by far the most effective treatment.
