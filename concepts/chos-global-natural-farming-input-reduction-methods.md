## Cho's Global Natural Farming - Input Reduction Methods

Dr. Cho Han Kyu's Global Natural Farming (CGNF) system was founded in the 1960s in South Korea as a direct response to the rising costs and environmental damage of chemical-intensive agriculture. At its core, CGNF replaces expensive commercial fertilizers and pesticides with locally sourced, microbially produced inputs that can be made on-farm at minimal cost. The philosophy is that farmers should not feed the plant directly, but rather nurture the soil so that the soil nurtures the plant through [[cervantes-chemical-fertilizers-guide]], while capable of boosting short-term yields, gradually destroy the soil's native microbial populations and organic matter, requiring ever-increasing applications to maintain productivity.

## The NF Input Philosophy

Natural Farming uses natural materials instead of chemicals to create its unique inputs. Materials are locally available and cheap. The system offers several advantages over conventional agriculture:

- **Cost reduction**: All inputs are produced from locally available materials -- rice, brown sugar, fish waste, eggshells, plant matter, seawater, and common herbs
- **Self-reliance**: Farmers become producers of their own agricultural inputs rather than consumers of commercial products
- **Soil health**: IMOs decompose complex [[cho-nutritive-cycle-theory]]:

### Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO)

The foundation of the entire system. IMOs are collected from local environments (forest soil, bamboo stumps, paddy fields) using steamed rice as a culture medium. The process involves placing rice in wooden boxes, burying them in strategic locations, and allowing local microorganisms to colonize the rice over 2-6 days. The collected IMO-1 is then mixed with jaggery (unrefined sugar) in a 1:1 ratio to produce IMO-2, beginning a multi-stage cultivation process that ultimately produces IMO-4, a finished inoculum rich in diverse microbial life.

Key principles of IMO collection:
- Microbes that have survived in the local area for a long time are most powerful and effective
- Organisms from different microenvironments (sunny vs. shaded) provide microbial diversity
- Aerobic IMOs are preferred; plastic containers are avoided in favor of wood or bamboo
- Black molds indicate the collection period has been exceeded

### Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ)

Made by extracting juice from fast-growing plant tips during their active growth phase. FPJ provides growth hormones, enzymes, and micronutrients that stimulate plant development.

### Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ)

Produced from ripe fruits, FFJ supplies phosphorus and potassium to plants during their reproductive growth stage.

### Oriental Herbal Nutrient (OHN)

A potent mixture of fermented herbs including garlic, ginger, licorice, cinnamon, and angelica. OHN serves as a natural pest deterrent and plant immune booster.

### Fish Amino Acid (FAA)

Produced by fermenting fish waste with brown sugar. FAA provides nitrogen and amino acids essential for vegetative growth.

### Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB)

Cultured from rice wash water, LAB suppresses harmful microorganisms, aids digestion in livestock, and helps decompose organic matter in soil.

### Water-Soluble Calcium, Phosphoric Acid, and Calcium Phosphate

These mineral preparations are made by extracting nutrients from animal bones, eggshells, and rock phosphate using natural acids (vinegar). They address specific micronutrient deficiencies at different plant growth stages.

### Brown Rice Vinegar (BRV)

Used to revitalize crops, livestock, and humans. BRV aids in mineral solubilization and is used as a drinking water additive for livestock to recover digestive function.
