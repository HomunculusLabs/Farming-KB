## Flush Dynamics

Mushroom crops typically produce multiple "flushes" or crops of mushrooms from a single inoculated substrate. Understanding flush dynamics is critical for maximising total yield:

- The first, second, and third flushes are usually the most productive, with each successive flush declining in yield.
- For indoor cultivators using sterilised sawdust blocks, Stamets recommends removing blocks after the third flush to a specially constructed open-air "yield recapture" environment fitted with overhead misting. These overflow buildings provide bonus crops with minimal maintenance.
- Growing on pasteurised straw typically yields 2-3 significant flushes.
- Substrates that produce many small flushes over a long period may achieve a high total yield but with poor efficiency of space and labour.

## The Yield-Quality Trade-off

Biological efficiency depends on the stage of mushrooms at harvest, creating a fundamental tension between yield and quality:

- **Young mushrooms ("buttons")**: More delectable, store better, command higher prices, but represent a substantial loss in yield potential.
- **Mature mushrooms**: Maximise biological efficiency but have very short shelf life and limited marketability.
- **Optimal harvest stage**: Just prior to full maturity, when mushroom margins begin to decurve (pointing downward) or become slightly incurved, and well before spore generation peaks.

Each species passes through an ideal stage for harvesting. As mushrooms mature, features are transformed through the reproportionment of cells without substantial increase in total weight. Over time, cultivators learn the ideal stage for harvest for each species and strain they grow.

## Practical Strategies for Improving B.E.

### Increase Spawn Rate

The simplest and most direct way to improve yields is to increase the spawn rate (the proportion of substrate that is inoculated with colonised grain or sawdust). However, spawn itself has a cost, and the economic optimum may differ from the biological optimum.

### Optimise Substrate Supplementation

Adding nitrogen-rich supplements (bran, soy meal, coffee grounds) to the base substrate can dramatically increase B.E., particularly for species grown on wood-based substrates. [[stamets-supplemented-sawdust-substrate-preparation]] blocks can yield 2-3 times more than unsupplemented blocks.

### Environmental Precision

Investment in environmental control (temperature regulation, humidity management, CO₂ monitoring) consistently pays dividends in improved B.E. The difference between a well-managed and poorly-managed growing room can easily be 50-100% B.E.

### Species Sequencing

After one species has finished fruiting, a different species can be inoculated into the spent substrate. This "species sequencing" extracts additional value from the substrate and improves overall biological efficiency of the cultivation system.
