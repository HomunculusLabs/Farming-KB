## Three Modes of Interaction

### 1. Predation

Predatory fungi deploy **extensive mycelial networks** equipped with specialized capture organs:

- **Adhesive knobs** — sticky structures that glue prey on contact
- **Adhesive nets** — three-dimensional networks of hyphae that ensnare nematodes
- **Constricting rings** — three-celled hyphal rings that swell inward upon nematode entry, trapping the animal within seconds
- **Nonconstricting rings** — passive traps that physically impede prey escape

Predatory fungi produce **noninfective conidia** — their spores germinate to form mycelial networks and trapping structures rather than directly infecting hosts. This distinguishes them clearly from parasitic species.

### 2. Parasitism of Adult Invertebrates

Parasitic species maintain **limited external mycelia** and produce **infective conidia or zoospores** that actively seek out and penetrate host organisms. Once inside, the fungus proliferates, eventually killing the host and emerging to sporulate. These species are central to [[cotter-oyster-mushroom-cultivation]] page covers the well-documented nematode-trapping abilities of *Pleurotus ostreatus*, which produces toxic droplets that paralyze nematodes before colonization.

### Endoparasites

These fungi complete their entire life cycle inside the host:

- *Cystopage* — attacks amoebae and small invertebrates
- *Stylopage* — obligate endoparasite of amoebae
- *Rhopalomyces* — parasitic on various soil microfauna

### Parasites of Amoebae

Only **6 hyphomycetous species** are known to parasitize amoebae, making this one of the most understudied fungal-invertebrate interactions:

- *Dactylella tylopaga*
- *Tridentaria carnivora*
- And four additional rarely collected species

## Sampling and Isolation

### Drechsler's Technique

The classic method for isolating nematode- and amoebae-destroying fungi:

1. **Soil sprinkling** — distribute small amounts of soil onto agar plates
2. **Bait addition** — introduce live nematodes or amoebae as prey
3. **Incubation** — monitor for trapping structures and hyphal proliferation
4. **Isolation** — transfer predatory hyphae to fresh media

This technique is effective for recovering **both** nematophagous and amoebophagous species simultaneously.

### Bdelloid Rotifer Baiting

Bdelloid rotifers, obtained as bait from moss, soil, and tree bark, can be used to selectively isolate fungi that parasitize rotifers and other micro-invertebrates. This approach has revealed previously unknown diversity in fungal-invertebrate interactions.

## Ecological Significance

Fungal parasites and predators of invertebrates are crucial regulators of soil and freshwater food webs. They help control nematode populations that can be agricultural pests, contribute to [[fungal-enzymatic-capabilities]] with predator-prey dynamics, as many species produce potent proteases, collagenases, and neurotoxins to subdue their prey.
