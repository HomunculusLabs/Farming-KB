## Definition and Philosophy

Ecological design (ecodesign) is the overarching framework within which Permaculture
Design operates. As Ozier articulates, Permaculture Design provides the principles
and methods for ecological design, even if many ecodesigners are unaware of this lineage.
Ecodesign is fundamentally anthropocentric—it is about creating sustainable, beneficial
microclimates that meet the needs of human inhabitants, including shelter, food, water,
and social space.

The core insight is that ecological design is not about designing individual elements
in isolation—a garden here, a building there—but about creating comprehensive local
ecological systems. Just as a beehive or bird's nest is a natural habitat, ecodesign
creates [[permaculture-designers-manual-wildlife-corridors]] and habitat connectivity

## Ecological Design Checklist

Ozier provides a comprehensive checklist for evaluating an ecological design:

- Use as little energy as possible
- Provide comfortable shelter for habitation
- Generate and/or harness as much energy as possible
- Provide as much clean water as possible
- Provide as much food as possible
- Provide space for social interaction and meaningful interaction with nature
- Include appropriate support structures for planned endeavors
- Include habitat for other species as much as possible
- Be easily maintained by the inhabitants
- Be designed for maintenance, upgrade, and disposal/obsolescence
- Manage all of its own waste
- Follow the Triple Bottom Line (ecological, social, economic)

## Relationship to Sustainability

The link between sustainability and ecodesign is direct: Permaculture seeks to create
sustainable agricultural systems and ultimately a sustainable culture. Sustainability
is the goal, ecological design is the method, and Permaculture provides the guiding
principles. Without a coherent philosophy of sustainability, the resulting designs
and their manifestations in the world will be incomplete.

Ozier frames this as: architecture is literally the physical manifestation of
philosophy. The International Style of architecture, born from the Bauhaus school,
rejected regionalism and traditional design wisdom in favor of universal industrial
materials. This led to buildings that require constant energy input (HVAC systems)
rather than being comfortable by design. Ecological design reverses this by designing
structures that work with local climate, materials, and ecological processes.
