## Genomics of Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Development

## Overview

The arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) symbiosis is an ancient association (origin ~460 MYA) between most vascular plants and Glomeromycota fungi. The development of this symbiosis involves the coordinate differentiation of both partners to create unique structures — particularly the arbuscule, a finely branched fungal structure within root cortical cells that serves as the primary site of nutrient exchange. Genomic approaches, including EST sequencing, transcriptional profiling, and suppression subtractive hybridization (SSH), have revealed the complex molecular dialogue between plant and fungus during symbiosis establishment.

## The AM Symbiosis — Background

- **Plant benefit**: Receives phosphorus (P), zinc, and nitrogen from the fungus. Up to 100% of plant P may come via the AM pathway.
- **Fungal benefit**: Obligate symbiont — entirely dependent on host plant for carbon (up to 20% of net photosynthesis).
- **Phylogeny**: AM fungi are all members of the Glomeromycota, a sister clade to Ascomycota and Basidiomycota. One non-mycorrhizal member: *Geosiphon pyriforme* (symbiosis with cyanobacterium).
- **Host range**: Angiosperms, gymnosperms, pteridophytes, and some bryophytes.
- **Fossil evidence**: Devonian Rhynie Chert provides direct evidence of ancestral AM associations with earliest land plants.

## Developmental Stages

1. **Pre-contact**: Fungal spore germination → hyphal branching near root → diffusible signals ("Myc factor" from fungus; sesquiterpenes from plant).
2. **Appressorium formation**: Hyphal contact with root epidermis → appressorium differentiation (root-specific signal required).
3. **Root colonization**: Hyphal penetration via appressorium → intercellular growth through root cortex.
4. **Arbuscule formation**: Hyphal penetration of cortical cell wall → dichotomous branching within the cell → envelopment by plant peri-arbuscular membrane.
5. **Arbuscule turnover**: Arbuscule lifespan ~10 days → collapse → cortical cell returns to original state.

### Key Model Systems

- **Plant**: *Medicago truncatula*, *Lotus japonicus*, rice (*[[oryza-sativa]]*).
- **Fungi**: *[[glomus-versiforme]]*, *G. intraradices*, *G. mosseae*.

### Challenges

- Asynchronous infection process.
- Minor proportion of root cells actually infected.
- Fungal biomass is relatively small even in highly colonized roots.
- AM fungi cannot be cultured without a plant host.

## Early Gene Expression — Pre-Contact and Appressorium Stages

### Plant Genes Induced Before Contact

Genes induced in plant roots separated from fungus by nitrocellulose or Cellophane membranes indicate perception of a **diffusible fungal signal** (analogous to Nod factor):

- **Fiber annexin**: Pre-contact induction.
- **MAP kinase**: Pre-contact induction.
- **Nitrate reductase**: Pre-contact induction.
- **DEAD box RNA helicase**: Pre-contact induction.
- **EF-1 alpha**: Pre-contact induction (49 ESTs in mycorrhizal libraries).
- **MtENOD11**: Induced by fungal signal through Cellophane membrane.

### Cluster 2 — Transiently Induced Defence Genes

Transcriptional profiling identified a cluster of genes showing **transient expression** during early stages:

- Receptor-like kinase Xa21-binding protein 3
- Syringolide-induced protein B15-3-5
- Ubiquilin 4
- Chalcone synthase
- Phi-1 protein
- HMGR (3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl CoA reductase)
- Protein kinases
- Arabinogalactan proteins

Many of these show similarity to **defence- and stress-related genes** and are also present in pathogen-infected root libraries — suggesting they coordinate symbiotic and defence programmes.

### Mycorrhiza-Induced Genes from SSH

Brechenmacher et al. (2004) identified 12 plant genes induced in the *M. truncatula/G. mosseae* symbiosis:

- At 5 dpi (appressoria only): Nodulin 26-like MIP, germin-like protein 1, extensin 3, and several hypothetical proteins.
- At 20 dpi (fully developed): Glutamine synthetase, PR10, glutathione-S-transferase.
- Three genes present **only** in mycorrhizal libraries (not in non-mycorrhizal libraries).

## Arbuscule-Associated Gene Expression

### Arbuscule Structure

- Hyphal branches penetrate cortical cell wall → repeated dichotomous branching fills the cell.
- Plant plasma membrane extends around branches → **peri-arbuscular membrane** (4-fold increase in membrane length).
- Extracellular matrix deposited: pectins, xyloglucans, polygalacturonans, arabinogalactans, HRGP.
- H⁺-ATPase activity demonstrated in the peri-arbuscular membrane.

### Cortical Cell Remodeling

- Cytoskeleton rearrangement: microtubules orient towards and cover the developing arbuscule.
- Large vacuole fragments into smaller vacuoles.
- Nucleus migrates towards the developing arbuscule.
- Increase in number of organelles.

### Key Arbuscule-Expressed Genes

**Transporters:**
- **MtPT4**: Mycorrhiza-induced phosphate transporter, arbuscule-specific, located in peri-arbuscular membrane.
- **OsPT11**: Rice phosphate transporter, expressed only in mycorrhizal roots.
- **MtST1**: Hexose transporter.
- **LePT1**: Phosphate transporter.
- **Mtha1**: Plasma membrane H⁺-ATPase.

**Cell Wall Modification:**
- **MtXTH1**: Xyloglucan endo-trans-glycosylase/hydrolase (systemic expression throughout root).
- **MtCel1**: Cellulase.
- **Extensin 3**: Proline-rich protein precursor.

**Defence/Stress:**
- **MtGST1**: Glutathione-S-transferase (arbuscule-containing cells + adjacent cells).
- **PAL**: Phenylalanine ammonia lyase.
- **CHT**: Chitinase (class I and III).
- **AOS**: Allene oxide synthase (jasmonate pathway).

**Carbon Metabolism:**
- **Acid invertase**
- **Sucrose synthase** (nodulin 100)
- **DXR**: 1-Deoxy-D-xylulose 5-phosphate reductoisomerase (MEP pathway)
