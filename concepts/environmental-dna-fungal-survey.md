## Lab Processing

The typical metabarcoding pipeline involves DNA extraction from the
environmental matrix, PCR amplification of the ITS region using fungal
specific primers (such as ITS1F/ITS2), addition of sample-specific
barcodes and sequencing adapters, and high-throughput sequencing on
Illumina platforms.

Bioinformatic processing clusters sequences into OTUs or amplicon
sequence variants (ASVs). A major limitation is that many environmental
sequences cannot be confidently assigned to described species because

entire years, revealing previously invisible phenological dynamics.

Spatial patterns show strong habitat partitioning at very fine scales,
with distinct communities in soil horizons separated by centimeters. The
approach enables large-scale monitoring that would be impossible with
conventional field collecting.

## Emerging Applications

Beyond biodiversity surveys, fungal eDNA is increasingly applied to
soil health assessments, plant pathogen surveillance networks, and
restoration ecology monitoring. Soil health assessments use fungal
community composition as a bioindicator, tracking shifts toward
saprotrophic or pathogenic dominance that signal soil degradation.
