# The Rig Veda Soma Hymns and Amanita Muscaria

The Rig Veda, the world's oldest and largest religious text, contains over one thousand hymns with more than ten thousand verses. Among these, one hundred and fourteen hymns in Book Nine are specifically dedicated to Soma -- a divine substance that the Herb of Immortality source identifies as [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]]. These hymns provide what may be the oldest surviving literary descriptions of a psychedelic mushroom ceremony.

## The Rig Veda: Overview

### Structure and Age

The Rig Veda consists of ten books containing over one thousand hymns. The name means "Praise (Rig) Knowledge (Veda)" -- Veda being an ancient Indo-European root word that in many modern Indo-European languages still means knowledge of a sacred nature.

- **Language:** Archaic Sanskrit -- a foundation document for Indo-European linguistics
- **Dating:** Usually dated to the late Bronze Age, but many hymns may be considerably older
- **Format:** Traditionally handwritten with ink on birch bark sheets bound with wooden covers painted red

### The Birch Bark Connection

The Herb of Immortality author notes a significant detail: the Rig Veda was traditionally written on birch bark imported from Kashmir and the Himalayan mountains. Birch trees are well known to be symbiotic with Amanita muscaria. This material connection between the sacred text (written on birch bark) and the sacred substance (Amanita muscaria, associated with birch trees) is presented as evidence for the identification.

## Soma in the Rig Veda

### The Dual Nature

Soma is described simultaneously as:

1. **A plant** -- Growing in the mountains
2. **A god** -- A living deity
3. **A drink** -- The pressed juice of the plant
4. **Immortal** -- Capable of conferring immortality

This tripartite identity (plant/god/drink) is central to understanding the Vedic Soma and is directly paralleled in the Amanita muscaria preparation process described in the Herb of Immortality source.

### Book Nine: The Soma Book

Book Nine of the Rig Veda is the Soma book, containing:

- One hundred and fourteen hymns specifically about Soma
- Additional Soma hymns scattered through other books
- Composed by "Soma intoxicated Seers and Sages"
- Rich in naturalistic poetic imagery and animal metaphors
- Descriptions of Soma's mountainous habitat and red/gold appearance
- Descriptions of the processing sequence in the Soma sacrifice ceremony
- References to healings and increased life spans

## Soma and Healing

### Hymn 8 XLVIII 3-5, 11, 12

The most explicit healing hymns include:

**Verse 3:** "We have drunk Soma and become immortal; we have attained the light, the Gods discovered. Now what may a foeman's malice do to harm us? What, O Immortal, a mortal man's deception?"

This verse directly states the claimed effects: immortality, divine light, and protection from harm. The author interprets "immortal" as both literal (the [[amanita-muscaria-resurrection-experiments]] property of the mushroom) and spiritual (the experience of transcending death).

**Verse 5:** "These glorious drops that give me freedom have I drunk. Closely they knit my joints as straps secure a chariot. Let them protect my foot from slipping on the way: yea let the drops I drink preserve me from disease."

The physical healing effects described -- joint strengthening, foot protection, disease prevention -- are attributed to the mushroom's [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]] and its general tonic effects.

**Verses 11-12:** "Our maladies have lost their strength and vanished; they feared, and passed away into the darkness. Soma hath risen in us, exceedingly mighty, and we have come to where men prolong existence."

The reference to "prolonged existence" is interpreted as both increased lifespan from the mushroom's health benefits and spiritual immortality from the mystical experience.

### Hymn 8 LXVIII 1, 2

**Verse 1:** "This here is Soma, never restrained, active, all conquering bursting forth, a Seer and Sage by sapience."

**Verse 2:** "All that's bare he covers over; all the sick he medicines, the blind man sees, the cripple walks."

This verse claims miraculous healing powers -- blindness and lameness cured by Soma. The author argues that while these claims may seem fantastical, they are consistent with the broad-spectrum [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]] of muscimol and the mushroom's antibiotic compounds.

## Soma and the Grail

### The "Iron Fashioned Home"

**Hymn 9 I 1-2:** "In sweetest and most gladdening stream flow pure, O Soma, on thy way, pressed out for Indra, to be his drink. Fiend-queller, Friend of all men, he hath with the wood attained unto His place, his iron fashioned home."

The author interprets "iron fashioned home" as a large carved wooden bowl carved with an iron chisel -- "iron was a very special metal in the Bronze Age, so in effect they are bragging." This is the [[amanita-muscaria-living-vessel-grail]].

### The "Eternal Fleece"

**Hymn 9 I 6:** "By means of this eternal fleece, may faith purify Thy Soma that is foaming forth."

The author identifies the "eternal fleece" as the mycelium of Amanita muscaria, which resembles sheep's wool. This interpretation connects the Soma preparation directly to the [[amanita-muscaria-resurrection-experiments]] process, where dried mushroom material produces white mycelium that resembles fleece.

### The Bird Imagery

**Hymn 9 III 1:** "Here present this Immortal God flies, like a bird upon her wings, to settle in the vats of wood."

The bird imagery connects Soma to the [[amanita-muscaria-feathered-serpent-phoenix]] and [[amanita-muscaria-feathered-serpent-phoenix]] symbolism -- the immortal substance that "flies" into its container.

### The Self-Purifying Nature

**Hymn 9 XXVIII 1-6:** Describes Soma as "Pavamanna" -- "the pressed one, self-purifying." The author believes the Old Testament Bible's word "Manna" is a Hebrew version of Pavamana, "perhaps inherited from long forgotten Indo-European ancestors or from the Persian religion of Zoroaster."

The self-purifying nature of Soma corresponds to the [[amanita-muscaria-resurrection-experiments]] process, where the mushroom culture purifies itself through the growth of mycelium that excludes contaminants.

### The Filtration Process

**Hymn 9 XII 1-8:** Describes the filtering of Soma through a "woolen strainer" and its flow into "wooden vats." The filtration process parallels the [[amanita-muscaria-sacramental-wine-preparation]] method described in the source, where mycelium is strained from the liquid preparation.

## Soma Pavamanna: The Self-Purifying Pressed One

The term "Soma Pavamanna" appears throughout Book Nine. Pavamana means "self-purifying," and the full term refers to Soma that has been pressed and is in the process of purifying itself. This concept maps onto the Amanita muscaria preparation process:

1. **Pressed:** The mushroom is dried and powdered (pressed between stones, as described in the Rig Veda)
2. **Self-purifying:** The resurrection process produces clean mycelial growth that excludes contaminants
3. **The pressed one:** The dried, powdered mushroom material
4. **Immortal:** The resurrected culture can produce sacrament indefinitely

## The Mountainous Habitat

Several hymns describe Soma's habitat as mountainous:

- The mushroom grows in association with trees at altitude
- The Himalayan and Kashmir regions (source of birch bark) are within Amanita muscaria's range
- The "red or gold appearance" matches the mushroom's cap colors
- Mountainous terrain with mixed coniferous/birch forests is ideal Amanita muscaria habitat

## Limitations and Critique

### The Wasson Hypothesis Debate

The identification of Soma with Amanita muscaria remains debated:

- **Supporters:** Wasson, the Herb of Immortality author, and others
- **Critics:** Some scholars favor ephedra, cannabis, or other candidates
