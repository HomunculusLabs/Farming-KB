# Faires Grain Harvesting and Processing

Complete guide to harvesting, threshing, winnowing, storing, and processing grain on a small scale using hand tools.

## Grain Growth Stages

Seed -> sprout -> seedling -> tiller (thicker shoots) -> booting (forming head) -> heading -> flowering -> milk phase (squeeze milky fluid) -> dough stage -> dead ripe

**Harvest at late dough stage** — past milk stage but still dentable. Dries to dead ripe after cutting.

## Harvesting Tools

- Sickle: for very small areas
- Scythe: for larger areas. Large blade on long wooden handle (snath). Hold nibs (handgrips), keep arms straight, twist at waist while stepping forward. Keep snath tip down to prevent blade going into ground. Try not to cut weeds/grass. Cradle attachment collects cut grain.
- Horse-drawn mower: fastest but requires horse care

## Harvesting Procedure

1. Cut grain with scythe, sickle, or mower
2. Make windrows (long rows) or piles
   - Dead ripe grain: make piles for shocking
   - Not dead ripe: make windrows for drying
3. Sheave and shock in field
   - Sheave: large handful of stalks tied near top with barren tillers using binder's knot
   - Several sheaves leaned against each other, one on top = shock
4. Dry in field or barn until no green stems, corn stalks sound hollow when tapped

## Threshing

Remove hulls from grain:
- Hand flail: wood handle with swinging piece (swingle) attached by loose ring
- Swing back and forth, hit grain spread on barn floor
- Flip grain with pitchforks, flail again
- Do flailing in winter
- Heavy swingle for oats, light swingle for beans
- Oats and barley: cannot be hulled by flailing — must be steamed and ground in mill

## Winnowing

Separate chaff from grain:
- Toss grain up with basket, catch again on windy day
- Toss in air with pitchfork
- Use winnowing tray (frame with screen)
- For beans: pour back and forth between two tubs on windy day
- Save chaff for livestock feed
- Save straw or pile in field for animals

## Seed Saving

- Save seed from second crop (not hybrids)
- Select best seed heads: unbroken, healthy
- Dry in shock at least 1 month until totally dry
- Thresh and ensure no leaves or twigs mixed in

## Storage

- Rodent-proof containers in the house (not shed)
- Cats help keep mice away
- Grain must be very dry before packing
- Free of foreign material except bay leaves (pest deterrent)
- Cool, dry place
- Keeps 1+ years until ground
- Check periodically for mold, bugs, rodent signs
- Once ground, use immediately

## Bug Control in Stored Grain

- **Dry ice**: 1 tablespoon per 5 gallons (19 liters). Airtight container, dry ice on bottom, grain on top, wait 1 hour, seal. CO2 kills bugs.
- **Heat** (eating grain only, not seed): spread quarter inch (0.6 cm) on pan, oven at 140 degrees F (60 degrees C) for 30 minutes.

## Ergot Fungus Warning

- Occurs if grain gets damp
- Turns grain hard, black, and purple inside
- NEVER eat moldy grain or feed to animals (can be fatal)
- Seed grain: throw out if moldy
- Emergency: soak in very salty water — sclerotia (ergot masses) float to top, skim off

## Grain Processing Methods

- **Cracking**: breaking kernel in pieces (usually corn)
- **Crimping**: flattening kernel slightly (usually oats)
- **Flaking**: heat/moisture treatment then flattening
- **Grinding**: through rollers and screens
- **Rolling**: smashed between rollers at different speeds

### Grinding at Home
- Mortar and pestle (slowest)
- Hand grinder (must sift and re-pass multiple times for desired texture)
- Electric mill (quickest, one pass)
- Electric mill coarsest setting = grits (cereal, animal feed)
- Electric mill finest setting = cake flour
- General ratio: 1 cup grain makes 1.5 cups flour

## Land Efficiency Comparison

To keep one human alive entirely on one food:
- 1,800 sq ft of grain
- 5,400 sq ft of potatoes
- 13,500 sq ft of dairy farming
- 36,000 sq ft of pigs
- 90,000 sq ft of beef
