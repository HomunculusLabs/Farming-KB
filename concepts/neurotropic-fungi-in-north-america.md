# Neurotropic Fungi in North America

North America (primarily the United States and Canada) has 21 reported taxa of neurotropic
[[psilocybe-subaeruginosa-australia]], plus numerous species from other genera among the 216 [[neurotropic-fungi-world-checklist]]
documented by Guzman, Allen & Gartz (1998).

## Pacific Northwest

The Pacific Northwest (Oregon, Washington, British Columbia) is a major center of neurotropic
fungal diversity in North America:

### Psilocybe Species
- **P. cyanescens** - Common in disturbed habitats, particularly woodchip mulch. Originally
  described from this region
- **P. pelliculosa** - Common in conifer forests; shares distribution with Europe
- **P. silvatica** - Conifer forest species; also found in Europe
- **P. stuntzii** - Known only from the Pacific Northwest; belongs to Section Stuntzae
- **P. baeocystis** - Contains baeocystin; related to P. aztecorum (Mexico) and P. quebecensis
  (Canada) in Section Aztecorum
- **P. semilanceata** - The liberty cap, abundant in coastal pastures and meadows

### Other Genera
- **Gymnopilus** species: G. aeruginosus, G. luteofolius, G. spectabilis, G. sapineus, G. viridans
- **Panaeolus** species: P. castaneifolius, P. cyanescens, P. fimicola, P. papilionaceus, P. sphinctrinus
- **Conocybe** species: C. cyanopus, C. smithii
- **Pluteus** species: P. salicinus

## Eastern North America

- **Psilocybe caerulipes** - Known from deciduous forests of the eastern United States. Reaches
  northeastern Mexico (Zacualtipan, Hidalgo) through mesophytic forest corridors
- **P. quebecensis** - From Quebec, Canada; related to P. aztecorum and P. baeocystis
  (Section Aztecorum)
- **Panaeolina foenisecii** - Common haymaker's mushroom in pastures
- **Panaeolus subbalteatus** - Widespread in North America
- **Gymnopilus spectabilis** and **G. sapineus** - Wood-inhabiting species
- **Amanita muscaria** - Widespread in boreal and temperate forests

## Southern and Central United States

- **Psilocybe cubensis** - Common in Gulf Coast states and throughout the subtropical Southeast,
  growing on cow dung in pastures
- **Copelandia cyanescens** - Found in subtropical disturbed habitats
- **Panaeolus** species widespread in pastures
- **Claviceps purpurea** - Common ergot on cereal grasses

## Canada

- **P. quebecensis** - Endemic to Quebec
- **P. semilanceata** - Widespread in temperate regions
- **P. baeocystis** - Pacific Northwest
- **Panaeolina foenisecii**, **Panaeolus** species
- **Gymnopilus** species: G. spectabilis, G. sapineus, G. liquiritiae

## Alaska

- Very poorly surveyed for neurotropic fungi despite vast territory
- **Claviceps purpurea** documented
- Likely underrepresents true diversity

## Knowledge Gaps

Several US states have no records of neurotropic Psilocybe species despite suitable habitat:
Arizona, Colorado, Illinois, Maryland, Vermont, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania.
This reflects limited mycological survey effort rather than true absence.

## Comparison with Other Regions

North America's 21 Psilocybe taxa compares with:
- Mexico: 44 taxa (highest globally)
- Europe: 14 species
- South America: High but incompletely surveyed

The Pacific Northwest of North America shares several species with Europe (P. cyanescens,
P. semilanceata, P. pelliculosa, P. silvatica), suggesting circumboreal distribution patterns.
