# Hofmann's Self-Experiment with Ergonovine

On April 1, 1976, Albert Hofmann conducted a self-experiment to test whether ergonovine (an [[golden-guide-morning-glory-and-ololiuqui]] used since ancient times) contained the same alkaloids found in ergot: lysergic acid amide, lysergic acid hydroxyethylamide, and ergonovine. If ololiuhqui was hallucinogenic, and it contained ergonovine, then ergonovine itself might be hallucinogenic.

## Why This Had Not Been Discovered Before

Ergonovine had been used in obstetrics for decades (stopping postpartum hemorrhage) at doses of 0.1 to 0.25 mg. The effective hallucinogenic dose of lysergic acid amide is 1-2 mg orally. The obstetric dose was simply too low to produce noticeable psychoactive effects.

## The Experiment

Date: April 1, 1976

- **12:20**: 2.0 mg ergonovine hydrogenmaleinate (containing 1.5 mg ergonovine base) ingested in a glass of water
- **13:00**: Slight nausea, same initial effects as LSD or psilocybin experiments. Tired, need to lie down. With eyes closed, colored figures appear.
- **13:30**: Trees in nearby forest seem alive, branches moving in a threatening way
- **14:30**: Strong desire to dream, unable to do systematic work. With eyes closed or open, afflicted by mollusk-like forms and feelings
- **16:00**: Motives and colors clearer, but still bearing hidden dangers
- **17:00**: After short sleep, awoke by a kind of inner explosion of all senses
- **18:00**: Unexpected visit forced activity, but throughout the evening lived more in inner than outer world
- **22:00**: All effects worn off, normal feeling

## Significance

This experiment proved that ergonovine possesses psychotropic, mood-changing, slightly hallucinogenic activity when taken at the same dose as an effective dose of lysergic acid amide (the main constituent of ololiuhqui). Its potency is approximately one-twentieth that of LSD and about five times that of psilocybin.

## The Water-Solubility Factor

The critical chemical insight is that the hallucinogenic ergot alkaloids (lysergic acid amide, lysergic acid hydroxyethylamide, and ergonovine) are water-soluble, while the non-hallucinogenic medicinal alkaloids (ergotamine and ergotoxine groups) are not. This means:

- Ancient Greeks could have prepared a hallucinogenic extract using only water
- The toxic alkaloids would remain in the barley solids
- The psychoactive alkaloids would dissolve into the kykeon liquid
- This separation was achievable with ancient technology

## Chemical Races of Ergot

Ergot is not uniform in composition. It occurs in biological/chemical races differing in alkaloidal makeup:

- In Switzerland: three varieties of rye ergot with different alkaloid profiles (ergotamine, ergotoxine, and no alkaloids)
- Ergot on wheat and barley: basically the same alkaloids as rye ergot, including ergonovine and sometimes traces of lysergic acid amide
- Ergot on Paspalum distichum: contains only hallucinogenic alkaloids
- Ergot on Lolium temulentum (darnel): variable, sometimes with substantial ergonovine

The chemistry of ergot varies by host grass and geography. We cannot know the exact chemistry of barley ergot from the Rarian plain in the 2nd millennium BC, but it is not unreasonable to assume it contained water-soluble hallucinogenic alkaloids.

## Connection to LSD Discovery

Hofmann's entire ergot research program stemmed from his 1943 discovery of LSD's hallucinogenic properties. His work on ergot alkaloids included:
- Isolation of psilocybin and psilocin from Mexican [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]] of ololiuhqui morning glory seeds
- Synthesis of Methergine (lysergic acid butanolamide) for obstetrics
- Development of Hydergine for geriatric disorders

The ergonovine experiment was a natural extension of decades of ergot alkaloid research.

This experiment has taken on legendary status in psychedelic history, often discussed alongside Hofmann's more famous LSD-25 discovery. The ergonovine self-experiment demonstrated that even closely related ergot alkaloids could produce profoundly different psychoactive effects — ergonovine produced a gentle, pleasant inebriation compared to LSD's overwhelming intensity. Modern analytical chemistry has confirmed that ergonovine is present in the ergot sclerotia that would have been used in ancient Eleusinian ceremonies, lending support to the [[entheogenic-eleusis-theory-scholarly-reception]]
