2. **[[microwave-exposure-system-architecture-surface-sterilization]]. Several methods can be used to achieve the target dose:

**Spray application:**
- Fine mist spray delivers uniform coverage
- Spray distance and nozzle type affect droplet size and distribution
- Calibrated [[ingham-spray-nozzles-equipment-compost-tea]] can deliver consistent dosing

**Aerosol delivery:**
- Ultrasonic humidifiers generate fine aerosol droplets
- Provides very uniform coverage on complex geometries
- May require enclosed application chamber to prevent overspray

**Direct pipetting:**
- For small surfaces or laboratory applications
- Micropipettes can deliver precise volumes to known surface areas
- Impractical for large or complex surfaces

**Wick or capillary application:**
- A water-saturated wick is drawn across the surface
- Provides consistent, controlled water delivery
- Suitable for flat surfaces and simple geometries

**Pre-moistened surfaces:**
- Surfaces are pre-treated with a known volume of water
- Excess is removed to leave the target film thickness
- Useful for validation studies

The choice of method depends on the specific application, surface geometry, and required precision.

## Verification of Water Coverage

Ensuring that the target water dose has been achieved uniformly across the surface is important for reliable sterilization. Verification methods include:

**Gravimetric measurement:**
- Weigh the surface before and after water application
- Calculate water volume from mass difference (1 mg = 1 µL)
- Most accurate method but requires access to the surface

**Visual inspection:**
- A uniform sheen indicates adequate coverage
- Dry patches are visible as matte areas on the surface
- Quick and simple but qualitative

**Water-sensitive indicators:**
- Paper or film indicators that change color when moistened
- Provide a permanent record of coverage
