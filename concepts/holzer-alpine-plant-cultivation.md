# Holzer Alpine Plant Cultivation

Over years of experience cultivating plants from Alpine regions, Holzer's most important lesson: all events in nature should be observed closely. This is a wonderful activity because you never cease to learn and profit from it.

## Yellow Gentian (Gentiana lutea)

### Germination Requirements
- Seeds ripen at altitude of 2,000m in the Alpine region
- In their natural habitat, seeds experience: frosty mornings, dry warm days, rain and snow, then a short hot period in July
- Seeds get wet, warm up, and are frozen many times before germinating in July warmth
- First-year seedlings can hardly be seen
- Young plants exposed to same extreme conditions as seeds

### Cultivation Method
- Sow in autumn, winter, or spring while frosty nights remain
- If no frost available: place seeds in plastic bag mixed with soil from parent plant's roots, add water, put in freezer at -10 to -15C for several weeks before sowing
- Take soil from near parent plant roots (contains [[mycelium-running-symbiotic-fungi-relationships]] the plants require)
- Seeds need exposure to light to germinate -- do NOT cover or push into soil (they will rot)
- In nature, seeds fall to ground around parent plant, left exposed to elements
- Churned-up soil areas are good for germination (created naturally by cattle, sheep, deer)

### Site Selection
- Sow on the poorest, most barren soil possible
- Not worth watering or using fertiliser
- A 1,000m height difference means vegetation is very different -- sow 3-4 weeks later per 1,000m of descent (compensates for later frosts at lower altitudes)

### Harvest and Propagation
- After 4-5 years, roots reach sufficient size for harvesting (over half a kilo fresh weight)
- Dig up for schnapps production (main or secondary ingredient) or dry for health/pharmaceutical purposes
- Seed harvesting: when first seed pods begin to burst open, cut plants down, place in paper bags; when dry, hit the bags and seeds fall to bottom, pull out stems
- Root rejuvenation: dig old roots to half-metre depth with root digger (30-cm pointed shovel); small secondary roots remain undamaged; fill hole with earth and stones; following year, thick growth of young plants from remaining roots

### Conservation Philosophy
- Forty years ago: large number of gentian roots in mountain pasture
- Now illegal to dig up roots or remove plants from own land
- Without digging, roots wear out from age (30-40 years), soil mats and compacts, roots die out
- Holzer argues protectors should be trained to cultivate and propagate rather than just restrict access

## Other Alpine Plants Cultivable by This Method

- Spotted gentian ([[gentiana-punctata]])
- Hairy alpine rose (Rhododendron hirsutum)
- Arnica ([[arnica-montana]])
- Alpine bellflowers (Campanula alpina)
- Mountain pasque flowers (Pulsatilla montana)
- Cowberries (Vaccinium vitis-idaea)
- Bilberries ([[vaccinium-myrtillus]])

## Key Principles for Alpine Plant Propagation

1. Always take native soil from near parent plant roots ([[auxin-production-by-symbiotic-fungi]] required)
2. Expose seeds to repeated freeze-thaw cycles
3. Do not cover seeds -- they need light
4. Sow on poor, barren soil
5. Compensate for altitude differences by adjusting sowing time
6. When cultivating at lower altitudes, introduce native soil for [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]]
7. When sowing in natural environments, native soil is not necessary (fungi already present)

## General Lesson

"You simply tried too hard with your plants. Nature cannot be 'improved' upon." The same principle applies to many aspects of ecological growing: observe nature, understand conditions, and work with them rather than imposing artificial methods.
