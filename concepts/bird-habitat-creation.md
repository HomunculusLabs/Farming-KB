# Bird Habitat Creation

## Overview

Creating bird habitat on the homestead provides ecological benefits including insect pest control, pollination, seed dispersal, and ecosystem monitoring. Birds are also indicators of environmental health—their presence and diversity reflect the quality of habitat, water, and air. A thoughtfully designed homestead can support 50-100+ bird species through the year by providing food, water, shelter, and nesting sites across all seasons. This connects to [[pollinator-conservation]], [[pollinator-conservation|conservation]], and [[invasive-species-management]] practices.

## Habitat Requirements

### The Four Essentials
All bird habitat must provide four fundamental elements:

1. **Food**: Natural food sources (insects, seeds, fruits, nectar) supplemented by feeders
2. **Water**: Clean drinking and bathing water; essential year-round but especially critical in winter and during drought
3. **Shelter**: Protective cover from predators and weather; trees, shrubs, brush piles, and built structures
4. **Nesting sites**: Safe locations for building nests and raising young; natural cavities, vegetation, and nest boxes

### Habitat Structure
Birds use different layers of vegetation:

- **Canopy**: Mature trees for raptors, warblers, and vireos
- **Mid-story**: Saplings and tall shrubs for flycatchers, thrushes, and catbirds
- **Shrub layer**: Dense shrubs for sparrows, thrashers, and cardinals
- **Herbaceous layer**: Grasses and wildflowers for ground-nesting birds and foraging insectivores
- **Ground layer**: Leaf litter, bare soil, and moss for thrushes, towhees, and sparrows

## Nest Boxes

### Selecting the Right Box

### Construction Guidelines
- **Wood**: Use untreated 1-inch lumber (cedar, pine, or fir); avoid plywood for exterior surfaces
- **Ventilation**: Drill small holes near the top of the sides for air circulation
- **Drainage**: Drill small drainage holes in the floor corners
- **Access**: Hinged or removable side panel for cleaning
- **Interior**: Rough interior surfaces or grooves below the entrance hole for fledglings to climb
- **No perch**: Do not add perches below the entrance; perches help predators access the box
- **Roof**: Sloped roof with overhang to shed rain; extend at least 1 inch beyond sides and front
- **Fasteners**: Galvanized or stainless steel screws (nails loosen over time)
- **Paint**: Natural wood or light-colored exterior paint; never paint the interior

### Mounting and Placement
- **Height**: 5-15 feet depending on species; bluebirds prefer 4-6 feet; kestrels prefer 10-20 feet
- **Orientation**: Face entrance away from prevailing winds (east or southeast is ideal)
- **Mounting**: Attach to poles (best), trees, or buildings; pole mounting with predator baffle is most effective
- **Spacing**: Space boxes 100+ feet apart for territorial species (bluebirds); 25-50 feet for tree swallows and wrens
- **Predator guards**: Cone or cylinder baffles on mounting poles prevent raccoon, snake, and cat predation
- **Avoid**: Near bird feeders (competitive species dominate), near dense brush (predator cover), facing west (hot afternoon sun)

### Nest Box Management
- **Clean annually**: Remove old nests after fledging season (September-October); disinfect with 10% bleach solution
- **Monitor weekly**: During nesting season, check boxes briefly (30 seconds) to monitor progress; record species, egg count, and fledging success
- **Remove house sparrow nests**: House sparrows are non-native nest-site competitors that destroy native bird eggs and young; remove nests and eggs immediately; this is legally permitted
- **Remove wasp nests**: Scrape wasp nests from interior surfaces; rub bar soap on the ceiling to deter rebuilding
- **Close or remove in winter**: Leave some boxes open for winter roosting; clean and close others to prevent mice

## Feeders

### Feeder Types
Different feeders attract different birds:

- **Tube feeders**: Sunflower seeds; attract chickadees, finches, titmice, nuthatches
- **Hopper feeders**: Mixed seed; attract jays, cardinals, and sparrows
- **Platform feeders**: Seeds, fruit, suet; attract ground-feeding birds
- **Suet feeders**: Attract woodpeckers, nuthatches, chickadees, and warblers in winter
- **Nyjer feeders**: Small seeds for finches (goldfinch, purple finch, pine siskin)
- **Hummingbird feeders**: Sugar water (4:1 ratio); attract hummingbirds

### Feeder Placement
- **Visibility**: Place where you can observe and enjoy the birds
- **Shelter**: Near cover (10-15 feet from shrubs or trees) so birds can escape predators
- **Distance from windows**: Within 3 feet or beyond 30 feet to reduce window collisions
- **Cleanliness**: Clean feeders every 2-4 weeks with 10% bleach solution to prevent disease transmission
- **Winter access**: Place feeders near the house in winter for easy refilling during snow

### Seed Selection
- **Black oil sunflower**: The single best all-around seed; preferred by the widest variety of species
- **Safflower**: Cardinals, chickadees, and titmice love it; squirrels and grackles tend to avoid it
- **White proso millet**: Preferred by ground-feeding birds (doves, sparrows, juncos)
- **Nyjer (thistle) seed**: Essential for goldfinches; requires specialized feeder
- **Cracked corn**: Attracts doves, jays, blackbirds, and game birds; also attracts squirrels
- **Avoid**: Cheap mixed seed with filler (red milo, wheat) that most birds discard

## Water Features

### Birdbaths
- **Shallow**: 1-2 inches maximum depth; birds do not swim—they wade and splash
- **Rough surface**: Textured bottom provides secure footing
- **Dripping or moving water**: The sound of dripping water attracts birds from great distances
- **Placement**: Near cover for quick escape from predators; with clear sight line to detect approaching cats
- **Winter**: Heated birdbaths or water wigglers prevent freezing; open water is a scarce resource in winter
- **Clean weekly**: Scrub with brush and 10% bleach solution; rinse thoroughly

### Ponds and Water Gardens
- **Shallow edges**: Gradual slopes allow birds to wade in safely
- **Native vegetation**: Plants around the edges provide cover and attract insect prey
- **Moving water**: Small waterfall, bubbler, or stream feature increases attractiveness to birds
- **Fish**: Avoid large fish (koi) that may prey on ducklings or small birds; small native fish are fine
- Connects to [[amphibian-habitat-creation]] for dual wildlife benefit

### Misters and Drippers
- **Misters**: Fine spray nozzles attached to garden hoses; attract hummingbirds and warblers that prefer leaf bathing
- **Drippers**: Simple devices that drip water into birdbaths or ponds; attract birds with sound and motion

## Native Plants for Bird Habitat

### Trees
- **Oaks (Quercus spp.)**: Support 500+ caterpillar species; acorns feed jays, woodpeckers, turkeys
- **Serviceberry (Amelanchier spp.)**: Early summer berries; eaten by 40+ bird species
- **Cherry (Prunus spp.)**: Fruit eaten by many species; caterpillar host plant
- **Dogwood (Cornus spp.)**: Fall berries; important for fall migration fuel
- **Eastern red cedar (Juniperus virginiana)**: Winter berries; cover for many species
- **Pine (Pinus spp.)**: Year-round cover; seeds for crossbills, finches, and chickadees
- **Birch (Betula spp.)**: Seeds for finches and siskins; catkins for spring migrants

### Shrubs
- **Elderberry (Sambucus canadensis)**: Summer berries; cover and nesting
- **Winterberry (Ilex verticillata)**: Persistent red berries through winter; critical winter food
- **American cranberrybush (Viburnum opulus)**: Fall/winter berries; excellent cover
- **Bayberry (Morella pensylvanica)**: Wax-coated berries; winter food for tree sparrows and yellow-rumped warblers
- **Blackberry/Raspberry (Rubus spp.)**: Summer berries; dense thorny cover for nesting
- **Sumac (Rhus spp.)**: Late-fall/winter berries; important for winter survival

### Herbaceous Plants
- **Native sunflowers (Helianthus spp.)**: Seeds for finches, sparrows, juncos
- **Goldenrod (Solidago spp.)**: Seeds for winter birds; host for many insect species
- **Asters (Symphyotrichum spp.)**: Seeds; late-fall nectar for migrating butterflies
- **Native grasses**: Seeds for sparrows; cover for ground-nesting birds; see [[prairie-ecology]]
