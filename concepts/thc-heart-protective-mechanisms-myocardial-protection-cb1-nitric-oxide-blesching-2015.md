topic: cardiology, [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]]*, Blesching highlights an emerging body of
evidence that tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) may protect the heart from damage and
mitigate injury from myocardial infarction (heart attack). Multiple distinct
mechanisms have been identified through preclinical research, suggesting that
cannabinoid-based cardiac protection operates through several complementary
pathways rather than a single mechanism.

## Mechanism 1: Reduction of Heart Attack Size

Preclinical studies in murine models have demonstrated that THC can reduce the
size (area) of myocardial infarction when administered before or during ischemic
events. This infarct-sparing effect is dose-dependent and mediated primarily
through CB1 receptor activation. The reduction in infarct size translates to
preservation of functional cardiac tissue and improved post-infarction cardiac
output.

The clinical significance is substantial: even modest reductions in infarct
size can dramatically improve long-term outcomes by preventing the progression
to heart failure that typically follows large myocardial infarctions.

## Mechanism 2: Nitric Oxide-Mediated Preconditioning

One of the most significant discoveries highlighted by Blesching is that THC
may protect heart cells against hypoxia through the induction of nitric oxide
(NO) production. Nitric oxide is a key signaling molecule in cardiovascular
physiology with several cardioprotective roles:

- **Vasodilation**: NO relaxes vascular smooth muscle, improving coronary blood
  flow and oxygen delivery to ischemic myocardium
- **Anti-platelet activity**: NO inhibits platelet aggregation, reducing the
  risk of thrombus formation in coronary arteries
- **Anti-inflammatory**: NO suppresses neutrophil adhesion to vascular
  endothelium, limiting inflammatory damage during ischemia-reperfusion
- **Mitochondrial protection**: NO modulates [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]] during ischemia

Blesching frames this as THC "preparing heart cells to better withstand
hypoxia" — essentially a pharmacological preconditioning effect analogous to
ischemic preconditioning, where brief episodes of ischemia protect against
subsequent prolonged ischemic insult.

## Mechanism 3: Neuroprotection via CB1

Blesching notes that THC is neuroprotective via CB1 receptor activation, which
has indirect cardioprotective implications. The cardiac nervous system,
including vagal afferents and sympathetic efferents, modulates heart rate,
contractility, and coronary vascular tone. CB1-mediated neuroprotection may:

- Preserve the cardiac autonomic regulatory network during ischemic stress
- Prevent maladaptive sympathetic activation that exacerbates cardiac injury
- Maintain vagal tone, which is cardioprotective through
  acetylcholine-mediated anti-inflammatory pathways
- Reduce central [[cannabis-for-cachexia-and-appetite-stimulation]] ensures adequate caloric and protein intake for
  cardiac tissue maintenance

## Context: Heart Disease as the Leading Cause of Death

Blesching emphasizes that heart disease is the number-one killer in the United
States, framing THC's cardioprotective properties as potentially
practice-changing. The multifaceted nature of THC's cardiac effects — operating
through CB1-mediated neuroprotection, nitric oxide induction, bronchodilation,
and metabolic support — distinguishes it from conventional cardiac drugs that
typically target a single pathway.

## Important Caveats

Several caveats apply to the current evidence base:

- Most studies are preclinical (animal models), and human [[blesching-cannabis-hypertension-cardiovascular-effects]] are complex and include tachycardia
  (increased heart rate) and orthostatic hypotension, which could be
  deleterious in acute cardiac settings
- The dose-response relationship is critical: cardioprotective doses may
  differ from psychoactive or adverse-effect doses
- The [[cbd-therapeutic-effects]] through entourage mechanisms.
