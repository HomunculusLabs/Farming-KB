# Oyster Mushroom Cultivation on Straw Substrate Bags

Practical guide to growing oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.) in substrate bags, as taught in the FAO disability training program in Thailand. Oyster mushrooms are the most commonly and easily cultivated mushrooms in Southeast Asia.

See also: [[mushroom-training-adapted-cultivation]], [[mushroom-training-spawn-production]], [[mushroom-training-pest-disease-management]], [[oyster-mushroom-short-log-cultivation]]

## Nutritional Value (Oyster Mushrooms)

- Water: 90.7%, Calories: 32.4, Fat: 0.043g, Carbs: 5.67g, Protein: 2.13g, Fiber: 0.396g
- Vitamins: B1 (0.004mg), B2 (0.06mg), C (0.82mg)
- Minerals: Ca (1.32mg), Fe (1.08mg), P (55.76mg)
- High protein and essential amino acids, low fat, high fiber
- Eating 2-3 types of mushroom per day provides proper essential amino acid intake
- One adult can consume 200-800g per day

## Substrate Bag Production

### Substrate Recipe (Sawdust-Based)

Per 100 kg sawdust, add:
- 5 kg rice bran
- 2 kg calcium sulfate (gypsum)
- 1 kg calcium carbonate (limestone)
- 0.2 kg magnesium sulfate
- 0-1 kg sugar

Moisture content: 65-75%. Mix all ingredients thoroughly until no lumps remain.

**Note**: Recipe is a reference — can be modified with rice flour, sticky rice flour, corn flour, cassava peels, cotton waste, soybean residue. In hot climates, do not exceed 7.5% additives. In cool climates, up to 20% additives possible.

### Rice Straw Substrate (Lower Cost Alternative)

Rice straw generally generates higher yield and better quality (texture and taste) than sawdust but requires more preparation:

1. Put straw in grinder to reduce size
2. Soak 100 kg paddy straw with water, mix with 1-2% urea by weight
3. Ferment 3 days, turn over pile
4. Mix with 2% lime, ferment 3 more days
5. Turn over, mix with 0.2% magnesium sulfate, ferment 3 more days (total 9-12 days)
6. Check: no urea smell + moisture 65-75% = ready for packing
7. Pack in 8" x 12" PP bags

### Bag Assembly

1. Fill plastic bags (polypropylene 7" x 12.5") with substrate
2. Compact by hitting with bottle or using compacting machine
3. Place plastic ring on bag neck
4. Pull top through plastic neck, fold down
5. Tie with rubber band
6. Insert pointed stick through neck to make aeration hole (NOT touching bottom)
7. Check weight: 800-1000g per bag
8. Close with cotton plug and paper filter, tie with rubber band
9. Transfer to pasteurization chamber

## Pasteurization

### Country-Style (200-Liter Drum)

1. Clean drum, add ~4 inches water
2. Place screen 1 inch above water
3. Load 80-100 bags
4. Cover with double layers of old rice bags, then plastic, seal tightly
5. Light fire; maintain 90-100C for 3-4 hours from when steam flows continuously
6. Critical: temperature must remain constant; water must always be in drum
7. Remove firewood, cool ~20 minutes, remove covers
8. Transfer to inoculation area

### Commercial Pasteurization

1. Clean water reservoir, refill to marked level
2. Load iron racks into chamber
3. Close door, verify thermometer
4. Heat to 98-100C for 3-4 hours; release all air before timing
5. Cool by opening door (1.5-2 hours)
6. Transfer to inoculation area

### Solar Heating (Optional Alternative)

1. Load bags in solar chamber
2. Ensure sunlight reaches under cover
3. Leave under sun for one full day
4. Transfer to inoculation area

## Inoculation

Inoculation room must be kept very clean and free of disease. Avoid direct sunlight.

1. Clean room with broom; disinfect floor
2. Light alcohol lamp
3. Disinfect hands and bags with alcohol
4. Shake spawn bottle against tire to release sorghum seeds
5. Flame bottleneck, open near flame
6. Drop 10-20 spawn-coated sorghum seeds into bag (act quickly, minimal movement)
7. Close with cotton immediately; keep bottle near flame until all bags done
8. Cover with filter paper, tie with rubber band
9. Transfer to incubation room

## Incubation

- Place bags on shelves (horizontal or vertical)
- Initially: little ventilation and light
- After ~10 days: regulate temperature via ventilation
- After 20-25 days: well ventilated, more light for monitoring
- Visually check daily for abnormal mycelium (black/green/brown/orange/red spots)
- Separate contaminated bags immediately

**Oyster mushroom incubation**: ~4 weeks

## Opening Bags for Fruiting

For oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus ostreatus): simply remove cotton plugs. The bag remains open at the neck.

## Fruiting Body Management

- Humidity: 75-90% (spray often but lightly; no water in bags)
- Temperature: comfortable for humans = good for mushrooms
- Open/close doors and windows for light and ventilation
- If too hot, leave doors open at night
- Check for mites and pests at least twice weekly
- Keep bags clean at all times

## Harvesting

- Mushrooms ready 2-3 days after first appearance
- Harvest at least twice daily
- Pick gently by grasping stalk, pulling slowly
