# Nitrogen-Fixing Green Manures

Nitrogen-fixing green manures are plants -- primarily legumes -- that convert atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available forms through symbiotic relationships with rhizobia bacteria in their root nodules. When these plants are cut and incorporated into the soil (or left as surface mulch), they release stored nitrogen and organic matter, building soil fertility without synthetic fertilizers.

## Why Green Manures Matter

In a closed-loop permaculture system, fertility must be generated on-site. Nitrogen is the most commonly limiting plant nutrient, and green manures are the primary biological mechanism for [[comparison-sheet-mulching-vs-chop-and-drop]], break pest cycles, suppress weeds, improve soil structure, prevent erosion, and feed pollinators. They are the cheapest and most sustainable form of fertilizer available.

## Sunn Hemp (Crotalaria juncea)

A tall shrubby annual reaching 1-3 meters, suited to subtropics and tropics. Frost-sensitive. Quick-growing with large leaves. Hardy and drought-resistant.

**Uses:** Cultivated for fibers used as twine, paper, nets, and sacking (better quality than jute). Root exudates are said to control nematodes in the soil. Easily grown in gardens with leaves used for mulch. The related species C. brevidens is used as annual fodder in tropical Africa.

**As green manure:** Often grown in rotation with rice, maize, and cotton, and interplanted with coffee and pineapple. When thickly sown, it will smother all weeds, even vigorous grass weeds. One of the fastest biomass producers among tropical green manures -- can produce significant organic matter in as little as 60 days.

**Caution:** Sunn hemp seeds and mature plants contain alkaloids toxic to livestock if consumed in quantity. Use as green manure only -- do not graze mature plants.

## Pigeon Pea (Cajanus cajan)

A leguminous woody shrub of the dry subtropics and tropics. Frost-sensitive. Quick-growing, short-lived perennial (sometimes grown as an annual), reaching 1-4 meters.

**Uses:** Major tropical food grain -- green seeds and pods used as vegetables. Ripe seeds ground into flour or dhal. Sprouts are highly nutritious (22% protein, 10% calcium). Important forage plant eaten green or made into hay or silage. Sometimes planted in pastures as a browse plant. Ideal windbreak and shade for vegetables. Leaves cut for mulch on garden beds. Used as shade tree in coffee, cacao, and vanilla plantations in India. Useful windbreak hedge species.

**As green manure:** Excellent green manure and cover crop. Used in erosion control on slopes. Dried stalks provide firewood, thatching, and basket-making material in India. Deep taproot brings up subsoil nutrients.

**Medicinal:** Used in Asian medicine as a treatment for skin irritations and cuts. Leaves used for silkworm culture in Madagascar.

## Lab-Lab Bean (Dolichos lablab, syn. Lablab purpureus)

A vigorous twining legume that stays green in the dry season. Young leaves are eaten raw or cooked. Ripe seeds used as split peas, or sprouted, boiled, and mashed into paste, then fried. High biomass forage crop (either green or as hay or silage). A useful dryland trellis crop as a sun shield (requires water). Excellent green manure plant and cover crop -- cut and use as mulch. Often grown in rotation with commercial crops to provide nitrogen.

## Lucerne/Alfalfa (Medicago sativa)

A perennial leguminous herb with a life expectancy of 10 years. One of the most important temperate forage and green manure plants.

**Uses:** Human food: foliage as alfalfa tea; alfalfa sprouts for salads. Major temperate animal fodder plant. Excellent bee forage, blooming just after sweet clover. Soil improver -- draws up subsoil nutrients. Useful ground cover or living mulch under trees.

**Related species:** Tree medic (Medicago arborea) is a perennial leguminous shrub to 4 meters growing in temperate zones. Important fodder shrub with foliage equivalent to lucerne. Can be netted and sheep allowed to browse.

## Lespedeza (Lespedeza spp.)

Sericea lespedeza (L. cuneata) is a perennial legume similar to clover, common in temperate zones. High-value animal fodder and hay. Soil improver through nitrogen fixation. Tolerates poor, acidic soils and drought -- useful for reclaiming degraded land. Particularly valuable for wildlife habitat and erosion control on marginal lands.

## Winged Bean (Psophocarpus tetragonolobus)

A valuable, nutritious tropical garden bean. Excellent nitrogen-fixer with heavy nodulation. Used as a soil conditioner and cover crop for the tropics. Edible pods, young leaves, shoots, and flowers are eaten as vegetables. Immature tuberous roots are eaten raw or cooked. Very high protein content. Can substitute for soy beans in processing to bean cake. Seeds contain oil for cooking, soap, and lighting. Dried flowers can be used like mushrooms.

## Clover Species

Clovers are among the most widely used [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-forest-gardens-and-food-forests]] because its low growth habit means it does not compete with tree roots for light.

## Integration Strategies

Green manures are most effective when integrated into rotation plans:

1. **Pre-planting** -- grow a green manure crop, cut it, and plant food crops directly into the mulch (no-till method)
2. **Interplanting** -- grow green manures between rows or between individual crop plants
3. **Undersowing** -- sow green manure seed beneath a standing crop before harvest; it emerges as the main crop is removed
4. **Living mulch** -- maintain a permanent stand of low-growing nitrogen fixers (like white clover) beneath perennials
5. **Cover cropping** -- plant green manures during fallow periods to protect soil and build fertility

## Inoculation

Legume seeds should be inoculated with the appropriate rhizobia strain before planting. Inoculation dramatically increases nitrogen fixation rates. Different legume species require different rhizobia strains -- check with local agricultural extension services for the correct inoculant.

Inoculant is applied as a slurry coating on seeds just before planting. Store inoculant in a cool, dark place and use before the expiration date. Many legume seeds sold commercially come pre-inoculated, but for maximum effectiveness, re-inoculate if seeds have been stored for more than a year.

## Selecting Green Manures by Climate

**Tropical:** Sunn hemp, pigeon pea, lab-lab bean, winged bean, cowpea, crotalaria, sesbania
**Subtropical:** Pigeon pea, lab-lab bean, velvet bean, jack bean
**Temperate (warm season):** Cowpea, soybean, buckwheat (non-legume but excellent cover crop)
**Temperate (cool season):** Clover (all types), vetch, field peas, winter rye (non-legume nurse crop), alfalfa
**Arid/Dryland:** Pigeon pea, mesquite, lab-lab bean (with irrigation), drought-adapted clovers

Match the green manure to the season and climate. In most temperate areas, a year-round program uses cool-season legumes in fall/winter and warm-season legumes in spring/summer, with no bare soil exposed at any time.
