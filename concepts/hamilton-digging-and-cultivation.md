# Digging and Cultivation Techniques

Hand digging is the main method of cultivating the soil in organic
gardening. It breaks up compacted land, introduces air, allows water
to drain away and roots to penetrate, and enables the gardener to
work [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] into the lower levels, increasing the depth of
the topsoil. While digging can cause severe back strain if done
poorly, it can also be a healthy, invigorating, and enjoyable
exercise when approached with common sense and proper technique.

## Basic Rules of Digging

Never dig when the soil is wet enough to stick to your boots, as you
risk spoiling its structure. Use a spade and fork that are the correct
size for you; oversized tools tire you quickly. Never take spadefuls
that are too big to handle comfortably. By taking smaller amounts and
not straining yourself, you can dig more for longer. Always take your
time and adopt a rhythmic, methodical approach. Stop as soon as you
feel you have had enough or begin to find it difficult to straighten
up. Keep tools clean and oiled to prevent rust.

## Double Digging

Prepare all new ground by double digging, and repeat at intervals of
about five years depending on soil type. Mark out the plot with lines
down either side. Mark out the first 2ft (60cm) trench and dig out
soil to the depth of the spade, putting it in a wheelbarrow. Then
fork the subsoil at the bottom of the trench to the depth of the
fork without inverting it. Put a 2 to 3in (5-8cm) layer of organic
matter in the bottom. Dig the next trench and throw soil forward to
cover the [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]]. Continue until the last trench is refilled
with soil from the first trench.

## Single Digging

After initial double digging, single dig every year: in the fall for
heavy soils and in spring for light soils. With single digging it is
only necessary to dig one spade deep and incorporate one layer of
manure. There is no need to mark out trenches as carefully as for
double digging. The best approach is to dig at least one-fifth of
your land each year on a rotation basis, ensuring the entire garden
is renovated over a five-year cycle.

## Digging Heavy Soils

Heavy soils are best dug in the fall, before winter rains make
cultivation difficult. Choose timing carefully, when the soil is
neither too dry and hard nor too wet and sticky. Cover an area with
heavy plastic to keep it dry if necessary. When digging heavy soils
in winter, throw spadefuls forward, leaving them rough and unbroken.
This exposes maximum soil surface to drying winds and frosts. By
spring, weather will have broken the surface to a fine tillage. The
rough surface also allows rain and frost to kill weeds and pests.
