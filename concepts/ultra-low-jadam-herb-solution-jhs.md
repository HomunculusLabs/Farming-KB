## JADAM Herb Solution (JHS)

JADAM Herb Solution (JHS) is a [[ultra-low-jadam-sulfur-js]]. JHS provides insecticidal power derived
from plants, enabling farmers to create effective pest control without any
synthetic chemicals.

## Philosophy of Herbal Pest Control

Plants have evolved chemical defenses against [[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]]**: Controls aphids, mites, and moths. The most
  commonly used herb in JADAM pesticide. Readily available, easy to grow,
  and highly effective.

- **Ginkgo**: Controls everything Jerusalem artichoke does, plus mulberry
  suckers, citrus latid planthoppers, and leafhoppers. Ginkgo leaves are
  widely available and potent.

- **Korean pasque flower (Pulsatilla koreana) root**: Controls almost all
  pests that Jerusalem artichoke and ginkgo fail to control. The root is
  the most powerful part of this medicinal plant.

- **Other effective plants**: Peppermint, clove, Korean mint, Japanese
  pepper, and tobacco (in greenhouses via herbal smoker). Farmers are
  encouraged to discover and test local plants with strong pesticidal
  scents or properties.

## Making JHS (Boiled Herb Extraction)

The basic method for making JHS is boiling herbs in water to extract
their active compounds:

1. Collect fresh or dried herb material. For Jerusalem artichoke, use
   the above-ground parts of the plant. For ginkgo, use leaves. For
   Korean pasque flower, use the root.

2. Chop or crush the plant material to increase surface area for
   extraction. Dried herbs can be used but fresh herbs generally
   have higher concentrations of active compounds.

3. Boil the herb material in a large pot. Use approximately 10 to 20
  kg of herb material per 100 L of water. Boil for 30 to 60 minutes
  to fully extract the active compounds.

4. Let the solution cool, then filter through a fine mesh to remove
  all plant debris. Clear liquid is essential; particles will clog
  spray equipment and leave marks on produce.

5. Store in a sealed container. JHS can be stored for extended periods.
   Use only very clear liquids when mixing pesticide.

## Combining JHS into JADAM Pesticide

JHS is always used in combination with [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]] that repel or kill insects. The herbal smoker method
shows promise with gingko, peppermint, clove, Korean mint, and
Japanese pepper. This is described as "a very interesting and
promising area that remains to be studied." Farmers worldwide can
adapt JHS to their local flora.
