# Greenhouse Design
#permaculture #greenhouse #solar #food-production #cold-climate

Updated: 2026-04-18

> From: Bill Mollison, *Permaculture: A Designers Manual* (1988), Chapter 12, Section 12.8.

## Overview

The cool temperate climates are those places where greenhouses are most commonly used for specialty crop or aquatic species production, for house heating, and for the winter production of vegetable and fruit crops. Modern glasshouses are becoming very sophisticated with respect to heat energy conservation and heat absorption, as 70-90% of the cost of crop can be that of artificial heating.

## Insulation

### Ground Insulation
Insulation is essential for the ground below the greenhouse, usually as a trench 1 m or so deep provided with a panel insulation, or with straw or sawdust-filled plastic bags.

### Wall and Roof Insulation
- Endwalls should be solid insulated walls
- Poleward walls should be solid insulated
- Poleward slope of roof should be insulated
- Sunward roof slope and wall can be air-insulated using trapped bubble insulation as standard plastic sheets, or by keeping air at pressure between double plastic walls

### Night Insulation
Insulated shutters at night can cover the glazed areas. Dark curtains within the greenhouse also act as heat (long wave) absorbers and radiators.

## Heat Mass and Sources

Inside the greenhouse, where the whole earth floor inside the walls is insulated from the cold earth outside, several additional heat masses can be provided:

### Water Mass
Plastic or metal tanks or drums of water (some can be fish ponds).

### Animal Heat
Areas below plant benches, if used to house rabbits, guinea pigs, poultry, or any small domestic animals at night, will provide considerable winter heat.

### Compost Heat
Long compost boxes along the poleward wall, or external to but below the level of the greenhouse itself. Large [[permaculture-designers-manual-compost-methods]] of 10-50 cubic metres can provide heat over a long period in winter and greatly reduce glasshouse and house heating costs.

### Active Solar
Hot water storages filled from panel or trough-concentrator solar heat collectors.

## Ventilation and Humidity Control

Manual or automatic venting of the greenhouse, fans to create air circulation inside, and a close watch on excessive humidity (relieved by a heat exchanger to the exterior, or by venting hot air) completes the design of efficient greenhouse units.

## Attached Greenhouses

Quite small (in cross-section) solar chimneys or attached greenhouses on the sunward side of dwellings can create a cross-draught sufficiently strong to blow out a candle. If a fully-enclosed and totally vined shadehouse is constructed on the shade side of the house, a continuous cool and humid air cross flow results. Air can be cooled to 10-15 degrees C below ambient temperature by this combination of shade, vines, and induced air-flow.

It is a simple matter to close the house at night, or on cold days, and to retain glasshouse heat inside. Such designs save up to 80% of fuel energy, a particularly important factor for low-income groups, where energy can be 30-40% of total household expenditure.

## Frost-Free Environments

Glazing over an earth trench in a sunward slope, a narrow street between houses or shops, or the sunward side of a massive stone or brick wall also provides a frost-free and warm plant environment.

## Entry Design

All entries to greenhouses and houses need to be porches or covered areas, provided with two successive doors, preventing heat escape as air loss to the exterior.

## Crop Selection

Since greenhouse construction is relatively expensive, crop selection in early years should be oriented towards high-value crops such as:
- Bell pepper
- Tomato
- Snow pea
- Ginger
- Kiwifruit

It is manifestly less profitable to grow low-value or bulky crop, but a domestic greenhouse can profitably provide a wide range of family food (animal and vegetable) rather than a commercial market crop.

## Desert House Integration

White-painted exterior walls help to reflect excessive heat, and strategic shade trees, palms, vine trellis, and courtyard ponds or fountains assist in buffering heat extremes. Houses can be very compact, especially where shaded outdoor trellis areas are extensive.

### Essentials of the Desert House
- No west windows. West wall painted white and fully shaded, vine-screened, evergreen trees to the west
- Cold air tunnel or cold air source as a fully enclosed, bark-mulch shadehouse
- Positive hot air exhaust as a solar chimney or small attached greenhouse on sunward side
- Cool air source as fully vined shadehouse on shade side
