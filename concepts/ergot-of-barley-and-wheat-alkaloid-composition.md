# Ergot of Barley and Wheat — Alkaloid Composition

**Source:** The [[the-road-to-eleusis-wasson-hofmann-ruck]], Chapter II — Albert Hofmann

## Overview

Hofmann's laboratory analysis of ergot growing on wheat and barley — the grains cultivated on the sacred [[wasson-hofmann-ruck-collaboration]].

## Laboratory Findings

"No rye grew there [in ancient Greece], but wheat and barley did and [[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]] flourishes on both. We analyzed ergot of wheat and ergot of barley in our laboratory and they were found to contain basically the same alkaloids as ergot of rye, viz. alkaloids of the ergotamine and ergotoxine group, ergonovine, and sometimes also traces of [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] amide."

## Two Categories of Alkaloids

The ergot alkaloids fall into two pharmacologically distinct categories:

1. **Water-soluble psychoactive alkaloids**:

   - Lysergic acid amide (ergine)

   - Lysergic acid hydroxyethylamide

   - Ergonovine (ergometrine)

   - These are closely related to LSD and possess hallucinogenic properties

2. **Water-insoluble medicinal alkaloids**:

   - The ergotamine and ergotoxine groups

   - These are primarily uterotonic and vasoconstrictive

   - Not hallucinogenic in normal use

## The Critical Extraction Property

"Ergonovine and lysergic acid amide, both psychoactive, are soluble in water whereas the other alkaloids are not." This differential solubility was the key to the [[claviceps-paspali-alkaloids]] growing on Paspalum distichum, a wild Mediterranean grass, and in [[rarian-plain-sacred-grain-eleusis]], the sacred grain fields dedicated to Demeter, ergot contamination would have been an annual occurrence. The [[eleusinian-hierophantic-families-eumolpidae-kerykes]] managing these fields may have learned to identify and selectively harvest ergotized grain, developing specialized knowledge about which contaminations produced desirable effects and which were dangerous.

## Ergot in Classical Greek Medicine

Ancient Greek medical writers were aware of ergot's effects on reproduction, though they did not identify the fungus itself. The uterotonic properties of ergot — primarily from the ergotamine group alkaloids — were exploited in folk obstetrics throughout the Mediterranean world. Midwives likely used crude ergot preparations to hasten labor, though at sub-psychoactive doses.

This medical tradition meant that the basic technology for processing ergotized grain — grinding, steeping, and straining — was already familiar in the ancient Greek world. The [[kykeon-potion-and-ergot-hypothesis-at-eleusis]]: over the two millennia of the [[mckenna-food-of-the-gods-kykeon-and-eleusinian-mysteries]], the hierophants could have selected and cultivated ergot strains with favorable alkaloid profiles, much as modern agricultural science selects crop varieties for desired characteristics.
