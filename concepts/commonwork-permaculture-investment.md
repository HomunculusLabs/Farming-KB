## Commonwork and Permaculture Investment

[[commonwork]] is a [[permaculture]] strategy for engaging wealthy investors and large corporations in large-scale regenerative land management. Rather than opposing capital, commonwork redirects it toward biological resource production by offering investors returns from specific yield streams while all other productive functions — food, housing, materials, [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]] — remain with the people living on and managing the land.

## The Concept of Functional Stacking

Commonwork is built on the principle of **functional stacking** — layering multiple independent functions onto a single site. The word originates from painting, where one image is laid on top of another, building up colors and depth. In permaculture, what is being layered is functions.

A single well-designed site can simultaneously produce:

- Alcohol or biofuel (the investor's return)
- [[faires-beekeeping-and-honey-production]]
- Aquaculture
- Bean [[cover-crops]] leaf production
- Cattle grazing
- [[gadd-fungal-biodegradation-concrete-building-materials]] (thatch, timber)
- Food crops through intercropping
- Housing for workers and families

None of these functions need interfere with the others. The investor owns only the specific yield they financed; every other function belongs to the people on site. This is the essence of commonwork.

## The Babassu Palm Example

The [[babassu-palm]] of India's exposed coast demonstrates the commonwork model:

- Grows under the worst conditions on eroded seaside land.
- Produces 10,000–12,000 liters of high-sugar sap per acre annually.
- Provides building and thatch material (waterproof, low-mass, ideal for tropical dwellings).
- Furnishes excellent shade cover for intercropping.
- Exists across vast areas of degraded coastal land.

A company leasing a large section of Indian foreshore from the state of Maharashtra could establish an automatic-harvest sugar-palm production system using keyline dams on valley slopes, with the entire system running downhill to a single collection point. Within this framework, workers graze cattle, grow beans, keep bees, and maintain aquaculture — all while the investor earns returns solely from alcohol production.

## Engaging the Investment Community

### Who Are the Investors?

The people most interested in futures — and therefore most receptive to permaculture propositions — are those making large investments. They are not idealists or counter-culture figures; they are investment bankers, corporate principals, and businesspeople who think in terms of decades-long returns. Each businessman has a network of 10–20 intimates — long-term associates who do deals together. Reaching one person means accessing their entire network.

### The Pitch

The pitch to investors is straightforward:

1. **Biological resources can outproduce oil wells** — indefinitely, with far less capital.
2. **The investor doesn't need to own the whole site** — only the right to the specific product they finance.
3. **All other productive functions are free** to the people living on the land.
4. **A supervising permaculture designer** ensures the system's health and productivity.
5. **The system is self-maintaining** once established, requiring minimal ongoing investment.

### Why Investors Are Receptive

Investors face a genuine problem: they are not immune from acid rain, environmental collapse, or ecological disaster. They own property in a world being stripped of forests and soils. They have no one advising them on environmental strategy — their advisors are graduates of economics and management schools who inherited their wealth.

When presented with data on soils, forests, pollution, and acid rain, these networks can verify it within days. They possess enormous capacity for rapid information gathering; they simply had never been directed to look at biological solutions.

## The Permaculture Institute as Holding Structure

The proposed structure places sites into the [[permaculture-institute]], which holds and manages production rights. Each person or group receives the eternal right to the specific production function in which they are interested. This means:

- Very low overhead for all participants.
- Enriched crops and ecological restoration.
- A supervising permaculture designer on site ensures system health.
- Additional functions are continuously stacked (e.g., vanilla orchids on palm trees, cared for by yet another group).

## Overcoming Objections

### "Dirty Money"

Some activists reject engaging wealthy investors as "dirty money." The counterargument is pragmatic: opposition doesn't work. War replaces one set of oppressors with another. Cooperation is the only path that produces results. There is no organized opposition in high echelons to permaculture propositions — only ignorance and lack of structured methodology.

### Implementation Failure

The principal challenge is not at the top (where agreements are made) but at the implementation level. Underlings in middle management are in desperate financial positions and resist change because they see no personal benefit. For this reason, **principals themselves must become involved** in large-scale permaculture conversion.

### The Need for Designers

Thousands of qualified [[bill-mollison]] are needed to handle the implementation and managerial aspects of systems involving two million to several billion dollars of investment capital. These systems take many designers working full-time for many years to establish, tune, and extend.

## Large-Scale Opportunities

### Desert Agriculture

In the Australian desert, land can be purchased for $120,000 per 700 square miles or $200,000 per 1,000 square miles. These areas contain hundreds of miles of date washes with enormous sugar production potential, while leaving most of the land untouched.

Deserts offer unique advantages:
- Constant winds of 15 mph enable **sail freighters** — a 100-foot wide sown strip allows large freighters to sail across deserts to the coast.
- Everything produced is self-stored in dry conditions.
- Development cuts out diesel engines, highways, and trucks.

### Cattle and Sheep Ranch Conversion

Large cattle and sheep ranching operations in Australia are barely financial:
- Land was acquired for free or pennies per acre.
- Stocking with half-wild cattle destroyed the sod.
- More cattle die than are harvested; if the market is poor, they aren't harvested at all.
- Thousands of cattle are raised for every one harvested — yet they probably don't produce as many cattle as Essex, England.

These vast areas need to be taken out of destructive grazing and converted to productive permaculture systems. In one such area, it would take ten people just to see half of it, let alone direct operations.

### The Financial Case

A sugar-palm system producing 10,000 liters per acre at $5,000 per acre per year creates a $2.5 million annual business from just 500 acres. There are thousands of acres available — currently supporting only starving people, dying cattle, and a few pariah dogs.

## Commonwork Principles

1. **Functional stacking**: Layer multiple non-interfering productive functions on each site.
2. **Investor specificity**: Each investor owns only the yield stream they finance.
3. **People's ownership**: All other productive capacity belongs to the workers and residents.
4. **Designer supervision**: A trained permaculture designer oversees system health.
5. **Demonstration value**: Each successful site becomes proof of concept for further investment.
6. **Biological replacement of fossil resources**: The goal is rapid generation of vast biological resources to replace coal and petroleum.
7. **[[energy-descent-action-planning]] compatibility**: Capital can be switched to energy-decreasing modes without conflict.

## The Bigger Vision

The ultimate goal is to create visible nuclei of regenerative production across the globe that appear to be joining up. When this works:

- Financiers earn returns from biological production.
- People in occupancy enjoy food, housing, and meaningful livelihood.
- Ecological restoration proceeds at scale.
- The model becomes self-replicating through demonstration.

The challenge is persuasion, not opposition. The methodology exists; the financial structures are compatible; the ecological need is urgent. What remains is connecting informed permaculture designers with capital that has nowhere else productive to go.
