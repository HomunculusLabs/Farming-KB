## Pasteurization Techniques and Growing Space Management

From [[tradd-cotter]]'s *[[organic-mushroom-farming-and-mycoremediation-overview]] and [[mycoremediation]]*, detailed methods for [[substrate-pasteurization]] and efficient use of growing space in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] operations.

## Hot Water Immersion Pasteurization

### Equipment
- 55-gallon drum with heat source
- Wire basket system for loading/unloading substrate
- Digital probe thermometers
- Heavy gloves
- Waterproof tool storage bin

### Basket System Design
"Our baskets are built of two parts. The rigid outer frame is the shell, and the smaller inner wire frame forms a removable cylinder." Cost approximately $400 to build using an engine hoist from a boat mechanic.

### Temperature Parameters
- **Range**: 165-175°F (74-79°C)
- **Duration**: 1-2 hours
- Monitor temperature with floating probe thermometers

### Safety Warnings
"Gloves are cheap; new skin is not." The lid may not seem hot, "but as soon as you crack it, steam will jet outward and sear everything within a few inches."

### Process
1. Immerse [[cervantes-green-algae-growing-medium]] in heated water
2. Maintain temperature for 1-2 hours
3. Pull out and drain
4. Sanitize a clean table/tarp with rubbing alcohol
5. Spread medium to cool before spawning

## Steam Pasteurization

### Method
1. Hydrate growing medium by submersion or overhead irrigation
2. Drain excess water briefly
3. Load into sealed barrel/container/room modified for live steam injection
4. Generally not under pressure — steam must be constantly injected

### Commercial Scale
"Large commercial growers use an insulated shipping container with a porous floor; the steam is injected from below and works its way up and through the [[green-cannabis-rockwool-hydroponic-growing-media]]."

### Small-Scale 55-Gallon Drum Method
1. Modify drum with locking metal lid and ½-inch hole for steam escape
2. Place brick over hole to allow pressure buildup without explosion risk
3. Stack bricks at bottom for platform, suspending basket ~8 inches above bottom
4. Add ~6 inches of water
5. Monitor depth every 30 minutes; replenish as needed
6. [[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]]: **180-190°F (82-88°C)**
7. When live steam escapes, maintain for 1-2 hours

## Growing Space Management

### Space Allocation Calculation
Example for shiitake operation in 100 sq ft:

| Stage | Duration | % of Space | Area |
|-------|----------|-----------|------|
| Colonization | 2 weeks | 7.4% | 7.4 sq ft |
| Curing/browning | 4 weeks | 14.8% | 14.8 sq ft |
| Fruiting | 21 weeks | 77.8% | 77.8 sq ft |
| **Total** | **27 weeks** | **100%** | **100 sq ft** |

### Tunnel Systems
"Many larger commercial operations are now using long 'tunnels,' similar to shipping containers linked end to end."
- Fill with single batch for entire spawn run and fruiting cycles
- Empty and sanitize between batches
- **Eliminates pesticide need** by preventing pest accumulation
- Isolated batches prevent cross-contamination
- Outbreak management: shut down individual tunnels independently

### Cleanliness Protocols
1. **Floors**: Keep well swept of organic debris (mushroom bits, cut stems)
2. **Growing medium**: Never leave mushroom remnants — they rot and attract flies/bacteria
3. **Drains**: Clean plumbing traps weekly with bleach
4. **Standing water**: Never allow — breeds pests, bacteria, and mold
5. **Debris in plumbing**: Concentrates and creates ideal fly/gnat breeding grounds

### Proactive Pest and Disease Control
"Approach pest and disease control proactively to improve your overall risk reduction."

## Key Principles

1. **Assembly-line workflow**: Position equipment in sequence of use
2. **Eliminate before automating**: Fix problems before scaling up
3. **Isolation prevents spread**: Separate batches reduce contamination risk
4. **Clean as you go**: Prevent organic debris accumulation
5. **Monitor temperature constantly**: Both pasteurization and growing rooms
