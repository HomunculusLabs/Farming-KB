# Shiitake (Lentinus edodes) and Lentinan Clinical Trials

## Overview

Lentinus edodes (Shiitake) is indigenous to Japan, China, and other Asian
countries with [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]], found in the wild on fallen deciduous
trees. It is the second most cultivated mushroom worldwide (16.8% of total
production). Beyond its culinary importance, Shiitake is the source of two
well-studied pharmacological preparations: Lentinan (from fruiting bodies) and
LEM (Lentinus edodes mycelium extract).

## Lentinan: Structure and Properties

Lentinan is a highly purified, high molecular weight polysaccharide (~500,000
daltons) in a triple helix structure containing only glucose molecules. It
has mostly beta-(1->3)-Glc linkages in the regularly branched backbone with
beta-(1->6)-Glc side chains. Lentinan is:

  - Protein-free (no nitrogen, phosphorus, or sulphur beyond C, H, O)
  - Water-soluble, heat-stable, acid-stable, alkali-labile
  - Active only in single helical conformation

The triple helix configuration of glucose molecules is thought critical for
biological activity. A new biochemical [[lemon-cannabis-alchemy-high-volume-extraction-method]] (Yap and Ng, 2001)
achieves 325 mg yield from 100g fresh mushrooms in 5 days versus 4 mg in
14 days with Chihara's traditional method, at 87.50% purity.

## Lentinan Does Not Attack Cancer Cells Directly

Lentinan produces its antitumour effect by activating [[fungal-virulence-factors-host-immune-evasion]] responses.
It is a T-cell-oriented immunopotentiator requiring functional T-cell
compartments. Evidence: anti-tumour activity is abolished in neonatally
thymectomised mice and decreased by anti-macrophage agents (carrageenan).

## Immune Effects of Lentinan

Lentinan activates a broad spectrum of immune responses:

  - Activates macrophages, NK cells, and lymphocytes (T and B cells)
  - Stimulates helper T cell production and cytotoxic T cells
  - Increases IL-2-mediated LAK cell and NK cell activity
  - Inhibits suppressor T-cell activity
  - Inhibits prostaglandin synthesis (which slows T-cell differentiation)
  - Increases killer T cell / suppressor T cell ratio
  - Enhances antibody production and opsonin production
  - Increases complement C3 production
  - Stimulates acute phase [[fungal-single-cell-protein-production]]
  - Induces vascular dilation and haemorrhage-inducing factor
  - Binds to CR3 (iC3b) receptors on phagocytic cells and NK cells

## Clinical Trials

Lentinan has been approved as a drug in Japan and is considered an important
adjuvant treatment for several cancers. Key clinical findings:

  - Phase III randomised trial with tegafur chemotherapy in [[lentinan-gastric-cancer-combination-therapy]]:
    combined therapy significantly prolonged 50% survival across all
    prognostic factors (abdominal localisation, hepatic/peritoneal metastasis,
    distant metastasis, histological type)
  - Response rate: 14.9% with Lentinan + tegafur vs 2.0% with tegafur alone
  - 19.5% survived more than one year; 10.4% more than two years; 6.5%
    more than three years
  - Proven successful in prolonging survival, especially gastric and
    colorectal cancer patients
  - Lentinan combined with other chemotherapeutic agents shows efficacy in
    a variety of settings
  - Responders showed 2.5x higher killer T/suppressor T cell ratio and
    elevated active NK cell ratios correlating with survival
  - Large Phase III trials found no adverse reactions or drug-drug interactions

## Safety Profile

In a detailed study of 469 patients, only 32 (6.8%) experienced adverse
reactions (46 episodes, 9.8%); none serious. Only 2 patients required
treatment discontinuation. Reactions included rash (1.9%), chest pressure
(1.7%), nausea/vomiting (1.7%), and headache (0.6%). Lentinan appears to
reduce chemotherapy side effects (nausea, pain, hair loss, lowered immune
status).
