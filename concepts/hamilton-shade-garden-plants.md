# Shade Garden Plants

updated: 2026-04-18

Shady areas in the garden -- under trees, against north-facing walls, between buildings -- present a design challenge but also an opportunity. Many beautiful and useful plants thrive in shade, and a well-designed shade garden can be one of the most tranquil and attractive areas of any garden. In the organic garden, shade-loving plants play an important role because many also attract wildlife and provide the [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]] that suppresses weeds and retains moisture.

## Understanding Shade

Not all shade is equal. The type of shade affects which plants will thrive:

### Light Shade
Areas that receive direct sun for part of the day (morning sun, dappled shade under open-canopy trees). Many sun-loving plants will tolerate light shade, and most shade plants thrive in it.

### Partial Shade
Areas that receive 2-4 hours of direct sun or bright indirect light. A wide range of plants grow well in partial shade.

### Full Shade
Areas that receive no direct sun at all (deep shade under dense evergreens, north-facing walls with overhangs). Fewer plants tolerate full shade, but some are specifically adapted to these conditions.

### Dry Shade
The most challenging condition -- shade combined with dry soil, typically under large trees whose roots compete for moisture and nutrients. This requires the toughest, most adaptable plants.

## Plants for Shady Areas

### Perennials for Shade

- **Lily-of-the-valley** (Convallaria majalis): Very fragrant flowers; grow in a cool, shady spot. The roots should always be shaded.
- **Hosta** (Plantain lily): Large, bold foliage; many varieties with different leaf colors and patterns. If grown on rich, damp soil, the foliage forms large, dense clumps. Excellent for masking the dying foliage of spring bulbs.
- **Solomon's seal** (Polygonatum hybridum): Elegant arching stems with dangling white flowers; roots should be shaded.
- **Bugle** (Ajuga reptans "Burgundy Glow"): Useful ground cover; requires moist soil.
- **Astrantia** (Masterwort): Attractive flower shapes; spreads by underground runners.
- **Hellebores**: Late winter and [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] flowers; tolerate dry shade under trees.
- **Foxglove** (Digitalis purpurea): Tall flower spikes; can be grown as a biennial or left year after year in peaty soil.
- **Bittersweet** (Solanum dulcamara "Variegata"): Climber with variegated foliage; berries are poisonous so avoid in gardens with children.

### Shrubs for Shade

- **Periwinkle** (Vinca minor): Rapidly spreading ground cover; can become invasive. Height up to 12 inches (30cm), unlimited spread.
- **Rhododendrons**: Grow in a [[comparison-chinampas-vs-raised-bed-gardening]] if your soil is alkaline. Many varieties available.
- **Camellias**: Shade-loving evergreens with beautiful flowers.
- **Pieris** ("Forest Flame"): Leaves pass from red through pink to creamy white to green; needs acid soil.
- **Euonymus** (Euonymus fortunei varieties): Shade-tolerant evergreens with variegated foliage.
- **Cotoneaster**: Tolerates shade; berries provide winter bird food.

### Bulbs for Shade

- **Bluebells**: Naturalize under deciduous trees; flower before the tree canopy closes
- **Snowdrops** (Galanthus): Early spring flowers in light shade
- **Daffodils** (Narcissus): Many dwarf varieties suitable for shade, especially under deciduous trees
- **Winter aconite** (Eranthis): Early yellow flowers in light shade
- **Grape hyacinth** (Muscari armeniacum): Good for edging in light shade

### Climbing Plants for Shade

- **Ivy** (Hedera sp.): Excellent for north-facing walls; self-clinging and evergreen
- **Climbing hydrangea** (Hydrangea petiolaris): Self-clinging, white flowers, tolerates full shade
- **Virginia creeper** (Parthenocissus quinquefolia): Dense foliage, red fall color; tolerates shade

### Ferns and Foliage Plants

Ferns and grasses make an attractive change from the usual choices, particularly when combined with other plants. A tub of plantain lilies (Hosta) mixed with ferns and one or two colored grasses makes a fine foliage display for any shady area.

## Design Tips for Shade Gardens

- **Mask bulb foliage**: Plant daffodils next to large-leaved plants like hostas. When daffodils are flowering, hostas are dormant. As daffodils finish, hostas produce large leaves to cover the dying daffodil foliage.
- **Use variegation**: White or yellow variegated foliage brightens dark corners.
- **Layer planting**: Combine tall, medium, and low plants for a naturalistic look.
- **Use containers**: If tree roots make planting difficult, use containers and pots in the shady area.
- **Improve soil**: Add [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] to shade areas; tree roots deplete soil rapidly.
- **Water in dry shade**: Areas under trees need supplemental watering, especially when establishing new plants.
