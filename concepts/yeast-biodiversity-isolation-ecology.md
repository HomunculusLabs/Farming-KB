# Yeast Biodiversity — Isolation, Ecology, and Global Distribution

Yeasts are a polyphyletic group of predominantly unicellular fungi found in virtually every habitat on Earth. Their study requires specialized isolation and characterization methods distinct from those used for filamentous fungi.

## Isolation Methods

### From Plant Surfaces and Tissue
Leaf surfaces, bark, flowers, and other plant parts are swabbed or rinsed to dislodge yeasts. Confirmed yeast growths are transferred to growth medium. Streak cultures purify to single-cell colonies.

### From Water and Aquatic Habitats
Water samples filtered through 0.45-um nitrocellulose filters. Filter placed face-up on solid nutrient agar with bacterial inhibitors. Volume filtered ranges from a few milliliters to 2-3 liters depending on population density.

### Soil Dilution
Known quantity of soil mixed with sterile water in a test tube, then serial dilutions made. Suspensions spread onto isolation media; colonies recorded and enumerated.

### Enrichment Cultures
Soil or other samples placed in flasks with nutrient medium tailored to target yeast preferences. Medium customized by adding specific carbon/nitrogen compounds, vitamins, or growth factors. Shake culture causes filamentous fungi to grow in pellets while yeasts remain free cells. Incubated 12-24+ hours with continuous shaking.

## Key Challenges

### Overgrowth
Filamentous fungi constantly overgrow yeast cultures. Solutions:
- Shake culture (filamentous fungi grow in pellets)
- Incubation at lower temperatures (12C slows filamentous fungi)
- Daily examination to isolate yeasts before overgrowth
- Bacterial contamination reduced by pH manipulation (3.5-3.8) or antibiotics (chloramphenicol; penicillin + streptomycin)

### Sampling Bias
- All isolation media are selective and artificial
- Dominant "weed" species rapidly overgrow rare species on plates
- Maximum ~300 cells per plate — rare species (1% or less) easily missed
- Cell numbers represent standing crop, not turnover rates or consumption rates
- Single time/place samples cannot be extrapolated broadly

## Isolation Media

- **Yeast Isolation Medium (PYG):** Commonly used standard
- **Wickerham's YM medium:** Popular alternative
- **Marine sampling:** Sea water at 37 per mil salinity; brackish at 15 per mil
- **Antibiotics:** Chloramphenicol (200 mg/liter) or penicillin G + streptomycin sulfate (150-500 mg/liter each)

## Specialized Yeast Ecology

### Extreme Environments
- **Cyniclomyces guttulatus:** Requires specific amino acids, 35-40C incubation, elevated CO2 (common in rabbit feces)
- **Rhodosporidium lusitaniae:** Isolated from forest soil using media with lignin-related phenolic compounds as sole carbon source
- **Polar yeasts:** Must be kept cold (~5C) during incubation and examination

### Marine and Aquatic
Deep-sea samples collected through water column using Sterile Bag Samplers. Temperature and salinity must be recorded simultaneously. 50ml samples usually adequate.

## Molecular Characterization

Conventional taxonomic characters are often inadequate for yeasts. Molecular methods are increasingly essential:
- Automated sequencing of specific gene regions
- Gene scanners for rapid determination of sequence lengths
- Species- or genus-specific probes for [[ingham-soil-food-web-diversity-assessment-dna-molecular-methods]] on microscope slides
- Molecular traits must be used due to inadequacy of conventional characters

## Culture Collections

Major yeast culture collections worldwide include:
- CBS (Netherlands) — largest
- DBVPG (Italy)
- ATCC and NRRL ([[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]])
- IFO and JCM (Japan)
- NCYC (United Kingdom)
- And many national collections in China, Russia, Slovakia, and elsewhere

## Ecological Importance

Yeasts play critical roles in:
- Fermentation (bread, beer, wine)
- Food spoilage
- Plant-microbe interactions
