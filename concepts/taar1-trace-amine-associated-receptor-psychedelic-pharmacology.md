## Psychedelic Tryptamines at TAAR1

Several psychedelic tryptamines have been evaluated at TAAR1 in vitro. DMT acts as a
TAAR1 agonist with moderate potency. Given that DMT is an endogenous compound found
in mammalian brain, cerebrospinal fluid, and peripheral tissues, its TAAR1 activity
may reflect a physiological signaling function. Psilocin, the active metabolite of
psilocybin, shows measurable TAAR1 affinity though with substantially higher EC50
than at 5-HT2A, suggesting TAAR1 activation occurs primarily at higher dose ranges.

5-MeO-DMT demonstrates TAAR1 agonist activity, and some researchers have speculated
that its distinct subjective profile — often described as more implosive and less
visual than DMT — may partially reflect differential TAAR1 versus 5-HT2A engagement.
The relative contributions of TAAR1 versus 5-HT2A remain unclear, but TAAR1
activation may modulate emotional, cognitive, and somatic components of the
experience without directly producing visual hallucinations.

## Functional Interactions with Monoaminergic Systems

TAAR1 activation produces several downstream effects on monoaminergic
neurotransmission. In the ventral tegmental area, TAAR1 agonists reduce dopamine
neuron firing rates, suggesting an inhibitory neuromodulatory role that may
contribute to anxiolytic effects during psychedelic experiences. TAAR1 is co-
expressed with SERT in the dorsal raphe nucleus and can modulate SERT activity,
potentially affecting synaptic serotonin clearance and psychedelic duration.

Beyond SERT, TAAR1 modulates DAT and NET activity, giving it broad regulatory
influence over monoaminergic tone. TAAR1-mediated cAMP signaling can activate
downstream pathways including PKA and CREB phosphorylation, which regulate
neuroplasticity-related gene expression. The overlap between TAAR1 downstream
signaling and 5-HT2A neuroplasticity pathways may create synergistic effects
amplifying lasting behavioral changes from [[metzner-amplifier-microscope-metaphors-psychedelic-therapy]].
