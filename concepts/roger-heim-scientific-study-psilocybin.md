## Roger Heim and the Scientific Study of Psilocybin

The scientific investigation of magic mushrooms in the late 1950s was led by Roger Heim (1900-1979), the eminent French mycologist and director of the National Museum of Natural History in Paris, working in collaboration with Albert Hofmann at Sandoz Laboratories in Switzerland.

## Heim"s Background

[Gordon Wasson chose Heim for his glittering academic credentials. Unknown to Wasson, Heim had personal experience with the fly-agaric dating back to 1923 — whether accidental or experimental remains unclear. Years later he wrote that consumed fresh and in sufficient quantities, the fly-agaric "can produce an agreeable sensation that lends itself to a feeling of contemplative well-being."

Heim"s dual identity — as both a rigorous academic mycologist and an experienced psychonaut — made him uniquely suited to bridge the gap between indigenous knowledge and Western science. His reputation lent scientific credibility to what might otherwise have been dismissed as a curiosity of ethnobotany.

## The Paris Research Program

After the 1957 Life magazine article, Heim cultivated Mexican mushroom species and undertook systematic self-experiments, eventually published in the Annals of the Natural Sciences. His meticulous writing style rendered his mushroom trips somewhat clinical, especially compared to Wasson's purple prose.

### Notable Experiments

- Consumed 32 fresh specimens of Psilocybe mexicana (same dose as Hofmann) — was forced to soothe his feelings with a hot-water bottle
- On 18 June 1958, took five fresh Stropharia (Psilocybe) mushrooms that "tasted strongly of radish"
- Despite his analytic approach, "this dry and serious man found that he always got the giggles, and that he would laugh and joke hysterically until the tears rolled down"

Heim's self-experiments were part of a broader tradition of self-experimentation in pharmacology. Albert Hofmann's famous bicycle ride after his discovery of LSD in 1943 established the precedent for researchers personally testing psychoactive compounds on themselves.

## Transition to Clinical Research

As more subjective accounts accumulated, scientists realized that the addition of yet more descriptions of scintillating colours and indescribable visions was doing little to further scientific understanding. A controlled and clinical approach was called for, one where the occluding variables of setting and personality could be filtered out.

Professor Jean Delay (1907-1987) and his team at Sainte-Anne Hospital in Paris conducted much of the early pharmacological research, testing psilocybin on rabbits, mice, healthy volunteers, and psychiatric patients.

## Species Identification

Heim's taxonomic work was foundational. He identified and described several new species of psilocybin-containing mushrooms from Mexico, including [Psilocybe mexicana, Psilocybe caerulescens, and Psilocybe aztecorum. His collaboration with Wasson brought these species to international attention and established the scientific framework within which all subsequent [[metzner-psycholytic-psychedelic-therapy-models]].
