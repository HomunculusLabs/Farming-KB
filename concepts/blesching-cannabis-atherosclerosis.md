# Cannabis and Atherosclerosis

## Overview

Atherosclerosis is a disease in which plaque builds up inside arteries, causing
them to narrow and restrict blood flow. It is a leading cause of heart attacks,
strokes, and peripheral vascular disease. The condition develops gradually as
low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol deposits accumulate in artery walls,
triggering an inflammatory response that progressively narrows the vessels.

Risk factors include high cholesterol, hypertension, smoking, diabetes, obesity,
sedentary lifestyle, and chronic inflammation. While the connection between
psychological factors and cardiovascular disease has been well demonstrated,
fewer experiments exist to specifically elucidate this connection in
atherosclerosis.

## Cannabis Research Findings

### Immune System Modulation

In 2006, researchers acknowledged important benefits of cannabis use: "Habitual
cannabis use has been shown to positively affect the human immune system, and
recent advances in endocannabinoid research provide a basis for understanding
these immunomodulatory effects." Testing numerous novel molecules acting through
the [[blesching-endocannabinoid-system-overview]], results suggested therapeutic potential of
cannabinoids on inflammatory diseases such as atherosclerosis.

### CBD Inhibits 15-LOX Enzyme

Researchers observed that the enzyme 15-LOX can oxygenate LDL, leading to
production of oxidized LDL, which plays a factor in developing atherosclerosis.
In 2009, researchers from Kanazawa, Japan, examined cannabinoids' impact on
15-LOX. Results indicated that CBD and its derivatives CBD-2'-monomethyl ether
and CBD-2',6'-dimethyl ether (CBDD) inhibit 15-LOX enzyme activity to varying
degrees. CBDD, which had the strongest enzyme inhibitory effects, could be a
useful prototype for producing medicines for atherosclerosis.

### Direct Reduction of Lesions

A 2010 study from Shaanxi, China, examined the impact of synthetic cannabinoid
WIN55,212-2 on atherosclerosis development in mice. Results revealed a
significant and direct reduction in the size of aortic atherosclerotic lesions.

## Strain and Form Considerations

WIN55,212-2 binds to both CB1 and CB2 receptors but with higher affinity to
CB2. The same is true for CBD. Indica and indica-heavy strains tend to possess
a lower THC:CBD ratio, thus favoring CB2 expression. Raw cannabis, fresh leaf,
and leaf juice contain CBD-acid and thus favor CB2 activation.

## Mind-Body Medicine Perspective

A five-year study of 1,592 men and women determined that social deprivation and
hostility were significantly associated with progression of atherosclerosis. Data
from the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (6,814 persons aged 45-84)
revealed that pessimism relates to higher levels of inflammation.

One theory holds that if plaque build-up is a response to artery lining
inflammation causing the body to deposit material as a "band-aid" attempt, the
original inflammation likely still lingers beneath. An underlying emotional
architecture of social deprivation, isolation, or loneliness could be a wall
built to keep away emotions such as pain and shame that seem too difficult to
confront. While initially protective, over time this wall can become larger
than the problem it was built to contain.

Aggravating factors include social deprivation, hostility, loneliness, isolation,
and pessimism. Consider undoing the internal architecture of loneliness, seeking
optimism, meaningful social interaction, and releasing suppressed emotions.

## Supporting Herbs and Spices

- **Cacao**: Dark chocolate may have protective impact on heart and vascular
  illness connected to oxidized LDL cholesterol.
- **Cardamom**: Protects platelets from aggregation and lipid peroxidation
  associated with cardiovascular disease.
- **Coconut**: Death from ischemic heart disease is lowest where coconut fat
  intake is highest.
- **Garlic**: Helpful for peripheral heart disease signs including water
  retention, spasms, thrombophlebitis, and hypertension.
- **Turmeric**: Overview of decades of studies confirms beneficial effects in
  cardiovascular disease.
- **(E)-beta-Caryophyllene**: FDA-approved dietary cannabinoid activating CB2
  with potent anti-inflammatory actions.
