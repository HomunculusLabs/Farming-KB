# Kraft Pulp Bleach Effluent Treatment by White Rot Fungi

The pulp and paper industry releases large volumes of intensely colored
bleach plant effluents (BPEs) that contain chlorophenols, chlorolignols,
resin acids, guaiacols, and other environmentally harmful pollutants.
White rot fungi have shown significant promise for the biological
treatment of these complex waste streams, offering an alternative or
supplement to conventional physical and chemical treatment methods.

## Guaiacol Degradation

Guaiacols are persistent bleaching process by-products in both
terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems. The dechlorination pathway of
tetrachloroguaiacol by laccase of Coriolus (Trametes) versicolor begins
with a demethylation step producing tetrachlorocatechol. This is
subsequently dechlorinated through a series of intermediates including
2,3,5-trichloro-6-hydroxy-p-benzoquinone, 2,5-dichloro-3,6-dihydroxy-
p-benzoquinone, and dichloro-6-hydroxy-p-benzoquinone.

## Biomimetic System Potential

The elucidation of enzyme systems responsible for BPE degradation by
P. chrysosporium and T. versicolor may lead to the design of effective
biomimetic systems capable of generating chelated Mn(III) for
degradation of BPEs. Such systems could combine the advantages of
biological specificity with the reliability and controllability of
chemical processes, potentially offering industrial-scale treatment
solutions.
