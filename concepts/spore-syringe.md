# Spore Syringe

A **[[spore]] syringe** is a suspension of mushroom spores in sterile water, drawn
into a syringe for the purpose of inoculating a substrate. It is the primary
inoculation method used in [[pf-tek]] and remains one of the most common ways to
start a [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] cultivation project. The spore syringe
concept was popularized and standardized by Psylocybe Fanaticus as part of the
original PF Tek method.

## What Is a Spore Syringe

A spore syringe consists of a sterile syringe (typically 10-12 cc) filled with
sterile distilled water containing millions of microscopic mushroom spores. The
syringe is fitted with a needle that allows precise injection through small holes
in jar lids. Spores are essentially the "seeds" of fungi, though unlike plant
seeds they are haploid and must germinate and find a compatible mate before
forming the mycelial network that produces mushrooms.

## How Spore Syringes Are Made

The production of a spore syringe follows a standard process:

1. **Spore collection**: A mature mushroom cap is placed gills-down on a piece
   of sterile foil or glass. As the cap releases spores, they deposit as a dark
   print on the surface. This spore print can be stored dry for years.

2. **Hydration**: Under sterile conditions, the spore print is scraped with a
   sterile scalpel or inoculation loop, and the spores are transferred into a
   container of sterile distilled water.

3. **Loading**: The spore solution is drawn into a sterile syringe through the
   needle. The syringe is capped and ready for use.

The entire process must be performed in a sterile environment, ideally using a
laminar flow hood, though a still air box (a simple container with arm holes)
is sufficient for hobbyist purposes.

## Using a Spore Syringe with PF Tek

In the standard [[pf-tek]], the spore syringe is used immediately after the
substrate jars have cooled following [[sterilization]]. The needle is flame-
sterilized with a lighter or alcohol lamp until glowing red, then allowed to
cool for a few seconds. Approximately 0.25-0.5 cc of spore solution is injected
through each of the four inoculation holes in the jar lid. A single 10 cc
syringe can inoculate approximately 10-20 jars.

After injection, the jars are shaken gently to distribute the spores and then
placed in a warm, dark location for colonization. Germination typically becomes
visible as white fuzzy [[mycelium]] within 3-7 days at optimal temperatures of 75-80
degrees F.

## Genetics and Variability

A critical concept for cultivators is that a spore syringe contains genetic
material from two parent mushrooms. Each individual spore carries a unique
genetic complement, so a single syringe represents enormous genetic diversity.
This means that different cakes inoculated from the same syringe, or even
different mushrooms from the same cake, can exhibit significant variation in
growth speed, size, potency, and appearance. For consistent, reproducible
results, advanced cultivators use liquid culture or [[agar]]-isolated strains rather
than multi-spore inoculation.

## Legal Status

The legal status of spore syringes varies widely by jurisdiction. In most
countries, mushroom spores themselves are legal to possess and distribute
because they do not contain psilocybin or psilocin, the controlled substances.
However, in some jurisdictions (including several US states), the intent to
cultivate is itself illegal, and spore syringes may be treated as drug
paraphernalia. Psylocybe Fanaticus was ultimately prosecuted not for the
spores themselves but for conspiracy to manufacture controlled substances,
based on the cultivation instructions he provided alongside the spore sales.


## Limitations and Storage

Spore syringes have several limitations that cultivators should understand.
Multispore inoculation produces genetic variability — each fruiting body is
a unique genotype with potentially different growth characteristics, potency,
and fruiting patterns. For consistent, reproducible results, tissue culture
from a selected fruiting body (cloning) is preferred. Spore syringes also
carry a higher contamination risk than liquid culture because the spores
themselves cannot be sterilized — only the suspension water and syringe can
be sterilized, while spores may carry contaminants from the parent mushroom's
gill environment.

Storage longevity is another important consideration. While refrigerated spore
syringes remain viable for 6-12 months, viability declines gradually. Freezing
can extend viability to several years but risks damaging spore cell walls.
The best practice for long-term storage is to make a spore print on
sterile paper or foil, which can remain viable for decades when kept dry,
cool, and dark. Spore syringes are best used within a few months of
preparation for optimal germination rates.
## Cultivation Applications

This technique has broad applicability across commercial and hobbyist cultivation. Small-scale growers benefit from low startup costs and minimal equipment requirements. Commercial operations scale through standardized protocols and environmental controls. The method integrates well with other techniques and adapts to various species with modifications to substrate, temperature, and humidity parameters.
