# Appropriate Technology Philosophy

Appropriate Technology (AT) is a paradigm for finding technological solutions that align with ecological design principles and sustainability goals. It represents the bridge between permaculture's nature-first approach and the human need for tools and devices.

## The Problem with High Technology

While [[holmgren-permaculture-education-training-and-pdc]] seeks to reduce humanity's environmental impact, modern high technology tends to increase it. High technology is typically:
- Resource consumptive
- Pollutive
- Expensive
- Requires dependency on outside agents for maintenance, repair, or upgrade

Therefore a new technological paradigm is needed that fits with the goals and principles of sustainability.

## The Design Approach

When using [[mollison-windbreak-design]], the design method for a sustainable future, one turns to natural solutions first. See if the local natural environment can be harnessed to provide for needs. Not all challenges can be overcome by purely natural means, and there will be times when a device or machine is needed. When these times arise, turn to Appropriate Technology solutions.

## Definition

Appropriate Technology does not refer to any one particular device, but rather is a new paradigm in finding technological solutions to meet needs. Appropriate Technologies are devices or machines that are at the DIY scale, meaning they can be built by the layperson with easily available resources and techniques.

## Key Characteristics

**DIY Scale**: Can be built by the people on the site who will use it. This ensures that no matter what happens, the devices can always be maintained without great expense or wait time.

**Ecological Energy**: To reduce fuel consumption, AT seeks to harness as much readily available ecological energy as possible -- mechanical forces, people or animal power, sunlight, thermal forces, or wind.

**Small Scale**: Almost always small scale and meant for personal use, or use by only a close few.

**Mechanical Over Electrical**: Usually mechanical rather than electrical in nature (though not exclusive of electricity). By harnessing existing kinetic energy in the local environment, mechanical systems can operate without electricity.

**Local Materials**: Made from local materials following the 4Rs: reduce, reuse, recycle, renew. This ensures maintainability and reduces environmental impact.

**Imminently Repairable**: Because the people who use it built it, they can maintain, repair, and upgrade it with readily available resources.

## The Efficiency Question

A factory-produced wind generator will be more "efficient" in raw power output than a DIY version. But which is truly more efficient in a holistic sense: working and saving to buy a $5,000 unit shipped across the continent that costs thousands to fix, or spending $50 and an afternoon to build a lower-power version yourself? Consider how much energy could be generated by all the alternators sitting inert in the world's junked cars.

## Historical Roots

**Sri Aurobindo** (early proponent): Called it "swadeshi," meaning self-reliance. His teachings influenced the creation of the ecocity Auroville and greatly influenced Mahatma Gandhi.

**Mahatma Gandhi**: Advocated smaller technological systems appropriate to Indian villagers -- looms for clothes, solar distilleries for salt. By leveraging their own skills and local ecological resources, citizens broke systems of oppression.

**E.F. Schumacher** (1970s): Popularized AT in the West through his influential book "Small is Beautiful" and the concept of "Buddhist Economics." He proposed shifting toward local AT (which he called "Intermediate Technology") as a means for developing countries to bolster themselves and for developed nations to build resilience.

## Categories of Appropriate Technology

AT solutions exist for a vast array of goals:
- Building construction and environmental maintenance
- Cooking (rocket stoves, cob ovens, hayboxes)
- Energy production
- Food preparation and production (DIY hydroponics, aquaponics, 55-gallon drum gardens)
- Lighting
- Refrigeration
- Transportation
- Ventilation and air conditioning
- Water supply and treatment (greywater systems, eco-machines, sand filters)

## Current Examples

- Bicycle-powered blenders, washing machines, and sewing machines
- Solar cookers and solar hot water heaters
- Water wheels and wind mills
- Solar food dehydrators
- Play pumps (water pumps powered by children's playground equipment like teeter-totters)
- Home composters (Sun-Frost Scrap Eater, Green Cone)
- Rocket stoves and cob ovens
- Hand-crank flashlights

## AT for the Developed World

AT is often discussed only for the "developing" world, but the developed world causes most ecological destruction, making AT most appropriate there. Examples:
- Pedal power combined with low-energy electronics
- Gym treadmills that feed into the power grid
- Playground equipment that feeds child-play energy into the grid
- Greywater systems, eco-machines, and solar cookers built into modern homes

The goal is to keep many "modern conveniences" but in ways that respect the environment and empower people to build their own lives rather than destroying the environment and enslaving people into debt.

## The Philosophy of Empowerment

Like permaculture design in general, AT seeks to empower people to design their own solutions that fit their unique situations. What is most important is not the specific devices themselves, but rather the user's understanding of the basic mechanical and ecological principles that underlie them. Armed with this knowledge, each person can design and create original devices that most perfectly fit their situation, goals, and beliefs.
