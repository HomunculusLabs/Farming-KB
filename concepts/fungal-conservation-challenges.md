# Fungal Conservation Challenges

Fungi are among the most threatened yet least protected groups of
organisms on Earth. Despite their fundamental ecological roles as
decomposers, mutualists, and nutrient cyclers, fungi have been largely
overlooked in conservation policy and practice. The IUCN Red List
includes fewer than 600 fungal species as of the mid-2020s,
representing a minuscule fraction of even described fungal diversity,
let alone the estimated millions of undescribed species.

## Why Fungi Are Overlooked

Several factors contribute to the marginalization of fungi in
conservation. Their ephemeral fruiting bodies make population
monitoring difficult. Most fungal species have no common names,
limiting public awareness. Conservation legislation in most countries
is framed around animals and plants, with no explicit provision for
fungi. The taxonomic impediment is especially severe for fungi. It is
impossible to assess the conservation status of a species that has not
been described, and most fungal species have not been.

## Lack of Baseline Data

One of the most significant barriers to fungal conservation is the
absence of reliable baseline data on species distributions and
population sizes. For most fungal species, even described ones, there
are no systematic records of where they occur or how abundant they are.
This makes it impossible to detect population declines or assess
extinction risk. Conservation biologists cannot protect what they

## Invasive Species

Invasive plants, animals, and fungi themselves threaten native fungal
diversity through competition, habitat modification, and pathogen
introduction. Invasive trees alter soil chemistry and leaf litter
composition, favoring generalist fungi over specialists. Introduced
fungal pathogens have caused catastrophic declines in host trees, with
cascading effects on associated mycorrhizal and saprotrophic fungi.

## Conservation Strategies

Habitat protection is the most effective strategy for fungal
conservation. The concept of fungal refugia, areas with high fungal
diversity and low disturbance, is gaining traction as a framework for
prioritizing conservation effort. Ex situ conservation through culture
collections is possible but resource-intensive. Citizen science programs
generate valuable distribution data and raise public awareness.
