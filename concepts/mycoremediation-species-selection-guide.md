## Primary Mycoremediation Species

### Oyster Mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.)
- **Best for:** Petroleum hydrocarbons, PAHs, PCBs, E. coli, coliform bacteria
- **Species:** P. ostreatus (pearl oyster), P. pulmonarius (phoenix oyster), P. eryngii (king oyster)
- **Key advantage:** Aggressive colonizer, grows on wide range of substrates
- **Heavy metals:** Accumulates cadmium, lead (use with caution — don't eat fruitbodies from contaminated sites)
- **Temperature:** 55-80°F
- **Additional benefit:** Edible and medicinal; produces natural lovastatin (cholesterol-lowering)

### Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor)
- **Best for:** Lignin and cellulose decomposition, general bioremediation
- **Key advantage:** Extremely hardy, widespread, tolerates diverse conditions
- **Medicinal:** PSK (krestin) — widely used cancer adjunct therapy; anti-HIV properties
- **Temperature:** 55-80°F
- **Additional benefit:** One of the most studied medicinal mushrooms; available as commercial spawn

### King Stropharia / Garden Giant (Stropharia rugoso-annulata)
- **Best for:** Wood chips, straw decomposition; companion cultivation
- **Key advantage:** Large, robust, excellent for garden integration
- **E. coli destruction:** Shown to destroy E. coli in experimental settings
- **See also:** [[companion-cultivation-of-mushrooms-with-garden-vegetables]]

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)
- **Best for:** Wood decomposition, general mycoremediation
- **Key advantage:** Edible, widely cultivated, vigorous lignin decomposer
- **Medicinal:** Antiviral (water-soluble lignin); LEM extract; immune-enhancing lentinan
- **Temperature:** 50-80°F
- **Note:** Mycelial extracts have 70x the antiviral activity of fruitbody extracts

### Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)
- **Best for:** General decomposition, antimicrobial properties
- **Key advantage:** Broadest hardwood range of any mushroom; perennial; cosmopolitan
- **Medicinal:** Immunomodulator, anti-inflammatory, anticancer, antiviral, cardioprotective
- **See also:** [[trametes-versicolor-entity]]

## Spawn Types

### Sawdust Spawn
- Most versatile; can be used for logs, stumps, and beds
- Longer shelf life than grain spawn
- Less susceptible to contamination

### Grain Spawn
- Fastest colonization
- Best for indoor cultivation
- Most susceptible to contamination in outdoor settings

### Plug Spawn
- Standard for log inoculation
- Can be supplemented with rope spawn for stumps

### Bunker Spawn
- Large-scale inoculation for extensive remediation projects
- Can be prepared in drums or bunkers
