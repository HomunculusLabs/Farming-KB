# Growing Legumes Organically

The pod and [[comparison-crop-rotation-vs-cover-cropping]], grow all legumes in Plot B (the plot that grew peas and beans the previous year, or the second year of rotation).

## Peas (Pisum sativum)

Peas taste best when eaten minutes after picking, before sugar changes to starch.

**Varieties:** Need two types -- early and maincrop. Early: Douce Provence, Meteor. Maincrop: Hurst Green Shaft, Onward. Also try snow/snap pea varieties like Oregon Sugar Pod and Sugar Bon, where the whole pod is eaten.

**Soil and site:** Make own nitrogen; too rich soil means lots of foliage, small crop. pH 6.5.

**Sowing:** Earliest can be sown in fall and overwintered (somewhat risky). Alternatively, sow in plastic guttering in the greenhouse in late winter, then slide the contents into prepared furrows under cloches. Open ground sowing starts in mid-spring in wide trenches about 2in (5cm) deep, seeds roughly 2in (5cm) apart. Sow at two-week intervals until early summer.

**Deep beds:** Use semi-leafless varieties like Bikini -- they have few leaves but masses of tendrils, forming self-supporting blocks. Sow in blocks with rows 6in (15cm) apart and seeds 2in apart.

**Support:** All types except leafless deep-bed varieties need pea and bean netting or sticks.

**Maintenance:** Hoe regularly, mulch with well-rotted compost or manure.

**Harvest:** Pick pods slightly young for sweetness. When all peas harvested, cut foliage and leave roots in ground to release nitrogen.

**Pests:** Birds, mice, mildew, pea moths, pea and bean weevils.

## Broad Beans (Vicia faba)

Wonderful value, producing early vegetables of unique flavor and just about the best [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]]. For deep beds, stagger rows 6in apart.

**Maintenance:** Support in exposed gardens with string between posts. Water if necessary, mulch between rows.

**Harvest:** Pull before pods become tough. In warm climates, cut plants back to 2in to encourage a second crop. Dry beans can be stored in airtight bottles for winter.

**Pests:** Blackfly, pea and bean weevils, chocolate spot.

## Runner Beans (Phaseolus coccineus)

One of the most prolific summer vegetables. Flowers and foliage are attractive enough for ornamental borders.

**Varieties:** Painted Lady (red/white flowers for borders), Polestar (best yield and quality), Mergoles (stringless, freezes well), Pickwick (new dwarf, no support needed).

**Soil:** Thorough preparation needed. Dig a trench one spade deep and 2ft (60cm) wide. Half fill with compost, manure, or liquid manure. pH 6.5.

**Sowing:** Start in greenhouse mid-spring, one seed per 3in pot. Plant out late spring after hardening off. Alternatively, sow against poles 12in (30cm) intervals in double rows 2ft apart, about 2 weeks before last expected frost. Or make wigwams of 4-6 poles spaced 18in-2ft, tied at top.

**Maintenance:** Mulch with coarse pine bark against slugs. Pinch off growing tips when plants reach top of support.

**Harvest:** Pick young before stringiness develops to encourage further production.

**Pests:** Slugs, blackfly, halo blight, failure to set.

## Green Beans (Phaseolus vulgaris)

Available in bush (dwarf) and climbing varieties. Dwarf types include flat-podded (Masterpiece) and pencil-podded (Tendergreen). Best climbing: Hunter, Cobra, Purple Podded.

**Sowing:** Need warm soil. Start first in greenhouse in early spring; plant out under cloches mid-spring, rows 12in (30cm) apart, 8in (20cm) between plants. Next crop under cloches early spring, 2 seeds every 8in. Outside sowings from mid-spring at same distances. Bush varieties in deep beds: stagger rows 6in apart.

**Harvest:** Pick regularly when young, before stringiness. Leave some pods to dry for winter storage. After harvesting, cut plants but leave roots to release nitrogen.

## Lima Beans, Soybeans, and Peanuts

All require warm climates. Lima beans need soil temperature of 65F (18C) to germinate. Soybeans prefer well-drained soil with pH above 6.5. Peanuts need light, sandy soil -- the plants push their seeds underground where nuts develop inside protective shells.
