## Pharmacology

### Receptor Interactions

Psilocin acts primarily as a post-synaptic agonist or partial agonist at the 5-HT2A serotonin receptor subtype. This receptor is densely expressed in the cerebral cortex, particularly in layer V pyramidal neurons, and activation of this receptor is believed to be the primary mechanism underlying the psychedelic effects of psilocybin mushrooms.

Additional receptor interactions contribute to the overall pharmacological profile:

- 5-HT2C receptor activation may contribute to anxiety and changes in mood

## The Blue Bruising Reaction

### Mechanism

The blue bruising reaction is one of the most recognizable field characteristics of many psilocybin-containing mushrooms. When the flesh of these fungi is bruised, cut, or otherwise damaged, a distinctive blue to blue-green coloration develops within seconds to minutes. This reaction is caused by the enzymatic oxidation of psilocin and related phenolic compounds.

### Limitations as an Identification Tool

While the bluing reaction is a useful field indicator, it has important limitations:

- Not all psilocybin-containing species exhibit blue bruising
- Some non-psychoactive species can also develop blue discoloration
- The intensity of the reaction varies with the age and condition of the specimen
- Blue bruising alone should never be used as the sole criterion for identifying a mushroom as psychoactive

## Major Genera

The EMCDDA case study identified several genera that contain psilocybin and related indole alkaloids:

- **Psilocybe** (family Strophariaceae): The largest and most well-known genus, containing the majority of psilocybin-producing species
- **Conocybe** (family Bolbitiaceae): Small, fragile mushrooms containing psilocybin in several species
- **Copelandia/Panaeolus** (family Coprinaceae): Dung-inhabiting tropical and subtropical species
- **Inocybe** (family Cortinariaceae): Mycorrhizal mushrooms, some of which contain psilocybin

## Common Species

Among the many psilocybin-containing species, several are particularly common or well-known:

- **Psilocybe cubensis**: The most widely cultivated species, native to tropical and subtropical regions
- **Psilocybe semilanceata**: The liberty cap, widely distributed across temperate grasslands in Europe and North America
- **Psilocybe cyanescens**: Known for its potency, found in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] on decaying wood
