## Rainwater Catchment

Rainwater catchment from roofs is a fundamental permaculture strategy. In design, we plan to catch water from roof areas and store it in tanks close to points of use. Roof catchment areas can be supplemented by:
- Sheet metal roofing areas of up to 600 square metres at ground level
- Slopes covered with roof metal for additional catchment
- Factory and warehouse roofs (many lie unharvested)
- Clay catchment areas sloping to central cisterns

## Water Spreading

In arid and semi-arid areas, the fate of rainwater is usually that most of the water runs off over the land. So little is absorbed (less than 12% in bare hill country) that even modest rains create sudden and turbulent flood flow.

If we are to creatively use this rare water resource, we must halt and divert the flows, and make places for water to soak in to the soils while it is still fresh enough to be of use to plants. Once fresh water is infiltrated, it forms a soil reserve or aquifer that travels very slowly underground.

Tree lines, swales, deep-ripped contours, silt dams, dams, and lines of stones or palisades of natural rock all help to halt run-off and let it infiltrate into the soil. Trees in particular help recycle water through growth and transpiration, keeping deeper salty water below root level.

## Runoff Percentages

Normal estimates of water runoff from various substrates:
- 12% runoff from forested areas
- 20% runoff from non-forested areas with sandy or friable soils
- Up to 80% runoff from concreted, compacted, or clay-sealed bare sites

These figures enable us to estimate the volume of run-off water, and hence the capacity needed in channels or flood bypasses in any area.

## Irrigation Strategies

### Gravity-Flow Irrigation
The most energy-efficient irrigation uses gravity to move water from high storage to lower use points. Keyline design, contour channels, and elevated tanks all enable gravity-fed distribution.

### Drip and Micro-Irrigation
Where water is scarce, drip irrigation delivers water directly to root zones with minimal losses to evaporation or runoff. Particularly appropriate for:
- Arid and semi-arid areas
- High-value crops
- Steep slopes where surface irrigation would cause erosion
- Greenhouse systems

### Flood Irrigation
In humid areas, controlled flooding of paddocks can:
- Recharge soil water reserves
- Control fire by creating moist barriers
- Deposit nutrient-rich sediments on paddocks
- Create temporary wetland habitat

## Wastewater Recycling

Greywater from kitchens, bathrooms, and laundries can be recycled through:
- Reed bed filtration systems
- Mulch basins and infiltration trenches
- Constructed wetlands
- Direct application to orchards and gardens (with appropriate precautions)

## Purification of Polluted Waters

Natural systems for water purification include:
- Constructed wetlands and reed beds
- Aquatic plant systems (water hyacinth, duckweed)
- Biological filtration through soil and plant root zones
- Nutrient-recycling aquaculture systems

## Water Tasks in the Landscape

Water serves multiple functions:
- As soil and pond storage, to irrigate crops and forests
- In homes, for cooking, drinking, cleaning, and toilet uses
- As a source of energy via turbines or hydraulic pressure
- In growing fish and aquatic plants as a growing medium
- As a carrier for nutrients from upstream to downstream crops
- For recreational and aesthetic uses
