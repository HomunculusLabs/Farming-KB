## Set: Internal Factors

Set encompasses the psychological state, personality structure, intentions, and
expectations that a person brings to a psychedelic experience. Key components of
set include:

Current mood and emotional state — pre-existing anxiety, depression, or
emotional turbulence can shape the direction of a psychedelic experience, often
amplifying the dominant emotional tone. This is why preparation protocols
emphasize emotional readiness and encourage participants to address acute
psychological stressors before embarking on a psychedelic session.

Expectations and beliefs — what a person anticipates will happen strongly
influences what actually happens. In clinical settings, expectations of
therapeutic benefit correlate with better outcomes. In recreational or ceremonial
contexts, cultural beliefs about the nature of the experience (as a sacrament, a
recreational drug, a medicine, or a spiritual tool) shape the interpretive
framework applied to the experience.

Personality factors — traits such as absorption (the capacity for immersive
experience), openness to experience, and baseline psychological flexibility
predict both the intensity and the quality of psychedelic experiences. People
high in absorption tend to have more vivid and transformative experiences.

Intention — the purpose a person brings to the session (healing, insight,
creativity, recreation, spiritual connection) serves as a guiding thread that
influences what aspects of the experience become salient. Setting clear
intentions during preparation is a standard practice in both clinical and
traditional contexts.

Physical state — fatigue, hunger, illness, and recent substance use all affect
the quality of the experience. Adequate rest, appropriate food, and abstinence
from alcohol and other drugs are standard pre-session recommendations.

## Setting: External Factors

Setting refers to the physical environment, social context, and cultural
framework surrounding the psychedelic experience. Key components include:

Physical environment — a comfortable, aesthetically pleasing space that feels
safe and private is essential. In [[metzner-shamanic-ceremony-versus-psychotherapy-structural-features]] of music during psychedelic sessions
significantly influence the experience. Classical, ambient, and sacred music
traditions are commonly used in clinical protocols, with a curated playlist that
builds in emotional intensity during the peak period.

Cultural framework — the interpretive lens through which the experience is
understood (as a medical treatment, a spiritual communion, a creative
exploration, a scientific experiment) is part of the setting. Indigenous
traditions embed psychedelic experiences within rich cosmological frameworks that
provide meaning and guidance, while clinical settings frame them within
therapeutic models.
