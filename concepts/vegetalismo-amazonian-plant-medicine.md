# Vegetalismo — Amazonian Plant Medicine Tradition

## Overview

Vegetalismo is the Amazonian tradition of plant-based shamanism that
forms the spiritual and healing framework documented throughout Pablo
Amaringo's *Ayahuasca Visions*. Unlike traditions centered on animal
spirits or ancestral worship, vegetalismo derives its power and
knowledge exclusively from the plant kingdom. The practitioners, known
as *vegetalistas*, are healers who have undergone rigorous training
through *dietas* — extended periods of isolation and dietary restriction
during which they commune with the spirit mothers of medicinal plants.
Amaringo himself was a vegetalista, and his paintings serve as a visual
encyclopedia of the spiritual ecology underlying Amazonian plant medicine.

## The Vegetalista

A vegetalista is a person initiated into the knowledge of plant spirits
through sustained dieta and ceremonial work. Unlike Western herbalists
who study plants through chemistry, vegetalistas learn from the plants
themselves — receiving songs, healing techniques, diagnostic abilities,
and protective practices directly from the spirit consciousness of each
species. The relationship between a vegetalista and their plant teachers
is deeply personal and ongoing; the plants choose the healer as much as
the healer chooses the plants.

The path requires months or years of dieta in isolation, often deep in
the jungle, consuming preparations of specific plants while maintaining
strict dietary and behavioral restrictions. The plants reveal teachings
through dreams, visions, and direct spiritual communication. Each plant
teacher confers specific abilities: some grant the power to see illness,
others provide protective spirits, and still others teach the magical
songs known as icaros.

## Shamanic Hierarchy

Amaringo's tradition recognizes a hierarchy of mastery among
vegetalistas, with three principal degrees representing increasing
levels of spiritual development, healing power, and knowledge.

### Banco

The *banco* is the highest degree — a master of extraordinary power.
Bancos have complete mastery over the spirit world, able to travel at
will through supernatural dimensions and command vast legions of plant
spirits. They treat the most serious forms of spiritual illness and
defend communities against the most powerful sorcerers. In Amaringo's
paintings, bancos appear as radiant beings surrounded by celestial
light, their bodies intertwined with luminous vines and cosmic serpents.

### Sumiruna

The *sumiruna* occupies the second rank. Sumirunas are accomplished
healers with deep knowledge of plant medicine, able to journey to
spiritual dimensions beneath the earth and water, accessing hidden
knowledge and powerful supernatural plants. Amaringo's art often depicts
sumirunas submerged in aquatic landscapes, surrounded by underwater
spirit beings and luminous plants.

### Muraya

The *muraya* is the third highest degree, specializing in aerial and
celestial realms. Murayas ascend into upper spiritual dimensions,
communicating with bird spirits, cloud beings, and stellar entities.
They command the powers of the sky and are particularly skilled at
diagnosing illness through spiritual perception.

## Healing Practices

### Mal Aire

*Mal aire* (bad air) refers to spiritual illness caused by exposure to
harmful spiritual energies, encountered in certain locations, times, or
through contact with contaminated people. It manifests as headache,
fatigue, dizziness, and general malaise. Vegetalistas treat mal aire
through plant remedies, icaros, and ceremonial practices designed to
extract harmful energy and restore spiritual equilibrium.

### Snake Bite Treatment

Vegetalistas frequently treat venomous snake bites, addressing both the
physical and spiritual dimensions. The shaman sings icaros to call the
mother of the snake species, negotiating with this spirit to withdraw
the venom's spiritual component while administering plant remedies that
work on both levels.

### Sorcery Defense — Arkana

*Arkana* refers to the spiritual defenses vegetalistas develop against
sorcery. In Amazonian cosmology, sorcerers (*brujos*) use magical darts
called *virotes* or *chontas* to cause illness and death at a distance.
Vegetalistas develop arkana — spiritual armor of plant spirits and
protective entities — to deflect these attacks. Building arkana is a
primary goal of advanced dieta training.

## Mariri — Magical Phlegm

The *mariri* is a magical phlegm that develops in the throat of an
experienced vegetalista. It is not ordinary mucus but concentrated
spiritual energy derived from accumulated plant teachings. The mariri
can be blown onto patients for healing and protection, transferred to
apprentices during training, or used as a weapon against sorcerers. Its
quality and power measure a vegetalista's experience and development.

## Indigenous Influences

Vegetalismo draws from multiple indigenous Amazonian cultures,
particularly the Shipibo, Conibo, and Shetebo peoples of the Peruvian
Amazon. The Shipibo are renowned for their complex geometric designs
(*quené*) representing the songs of plant spirits — visual
manifestations of icaros. Amaringo's paintings incorporate elements from
all these traditions, creating syncretic visionary art transcending any
single cultural framework.

## The Dieta as Foundation

The dieta is the foundational practice of vegetalismo. Through dieta, a
vegetalista establishes relationships with plant teachers, develops
their mariri, builds arkana defenses, and acquires the icaros forming
the core of their healing practice. During dieta, the apprentice
consumes preparations of a specific plant while isolating from social
contact and maintaining strict dietary restrictions. The plant spirit
communicates through dreams and visions, teaching its songs, healing
properties, and specific applications. Each dieta deepens the
vegetalista's connection to the plant world and expands their
capabilities as a healer.
