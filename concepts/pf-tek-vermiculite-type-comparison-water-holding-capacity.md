## Overview

The type of vermiculite used in the PF TEK [[pf-tek-measurement-calibration-and-substrate-formula-precision]] significantly affects water holding capacity and overall substrate performance. [[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] explicitly addressed vermiculite selection in the PF TEK instructions, noting that vermiculite particle size varies considerably between brands and that the coarser type holds less water than the finer type. This variation is critical because the PF TEK substrate relies on vermiculite as both the structural base and the primary water reservoir for [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]].

## The Role of Vermiculite in PF TEK

### Why Vermiculite Is Essential

Vermiculite is described by PF as the "magic ingredient" of the PF TEK and its "secret." The substrate formula is fundamentally a [[cho-brown-rice-vinegar-preparation]] method improved by using vermiculite as a base and adding pulverized brown rice on top. The vermiculite serves several critical functions:

1. **Air space creation:** When mycelium is cultured in just grain, it turns into a dense mass with little air space. Vermiculite creates air gaps that allow mycelial threads to stretch across space rather than compacting
2. **Water reservoir:** The expanded mineral structure of vermiculite absorbs and retains water, providing consistent moisture to the growing mycelium
3. **Contaminant barrier:** A [[pf-tek-dry-vermiculite-contaminant-barrier]] layer on top of the substrate protects the wet substrate from airborne contaminants (a key PF innovation)
4. **Moisture regulation:** The top vermiculite layer absorbs and regulates moisture transpiration and condensation within the jar
5. **Structural support:** Provides the physical matrix that holds the brown rice powder and water together

## Vermiculite Types

### Fine Vermiculite (Recommended)

Fine vermiculite is recommended over the coarser type because it holds more water. The particle characteristics are:

- **Average particle size:** Approximately 1 millimeter across (some larger, some smaller)
- **Water holding capacity:** Higher than coarse vermiculite
- **Air space:** Still adequate for mycelial growth despite finer particle size
- **Suitability:** Ideal for the standard PF TEK substrate formula

### Coarse Vermiculite (Not Recommended)

Coarse vermiculite has larger particles that hold less water:

- **Average particle size:** Approximately 4-5 millimeters across, up to 8 millimeters maximum
- **Water holding capacity:** Lower than fine vermiculite
- **Air space:** Excellent, but at the cost of reduced moisture retention
- **Suitability:** Not recommended for PF TEK; may work with modified (wetter) formulas

### Assessing Vermiculite Type

PF provided a specific method for determining vermiculite particle size:
1. Observe the particles under a photo magnifier
2. Place a millimeter ruler next to the particles for scale
3. Measure several particles to determine the average size
4. Classify as fine (~1mm) or coarse (~4-8mm)

## Impact on Substrate Formulation

### Standard Formula with Fine Vermiculite

For a half-pint jar using fine vermiculite:
- 1/8 cup brown rice powder
- 1/2 to 2/3 cup horticultural vermiculite (medium grade)
- 40-45 cc (ml) water, or slightly less than 1/4 cup

### Adjustments for Coarse Vermiculite

When using coarser vermiculite, the reduced water holding capacity means:
- The standard water amount may result in water pooling at the bottom of the jar
- The substrate may be too dry despite correct water measurement
- More water may be needed to compensate for lower retention
- However, excess water can make the substrate "really wet or sloppy," leading to contamination

### The Maximum Fruiting Formula

PF provided an alternative "maximum fruiting" formula:
- 1/4 cup brown rice powder (double the standard)
- 1/2 cup vermiculite (same as standard)
- 60 cc water (increased from 40-45 cc)

This formula with the finer type of vermiculite produces a wetter substrate that PF associated with maximum fruiting potential. However, when using this formula with fine vermiculite, the jar lid should be kept loose during incubation to allow excess moisture to escape and prevent water collection at the bottom of the jar.

## Troubleshooting Water Content Issues

### Signs of Too Much Water

- Substrate appears "really wet or sloppy"
- Water collects at the bottom of the jar during incubation
- Slow or stalled mycelial growth
- Increased contamination risk (bacteria thrive in waterlogged substrate)

**Solution:** Use less water in the formula. PF advised keeping notes on formulas for replicating the substrate that fruits best.

### Signs of Too Little Water

- Substrate appears dry and crumbly
- Spores germinate but mycelial growth halts at a certain point
- No contamination but no further progress

**Solution:** Increase the water content. PF noted that "any halted growth (with no contamination) is a sign of dried substrate" and that the remedy is to increase water in the formula.

### The Lid Tightness Factor

The relationship between water content and lid tightness is critical:
- **High moisture substrate (good for fruiting):** Tight lid may cause water to collect at the bottom; keep lid loose
- **Dry substrate:** Tight lid preserves moisture content
- **Balance:** The right balance depends on [[pf-tek-jar-size-importance-half-pint-reliability]], available air space, and vermiculite type

## Regional Brand Variations

PF noted that stores usually carry one type of vermiculite, but there are regional differences between brands. Practical advice:

- Shop around and try to obtain both types for comparison
- Even within the "fine" category, different brands may vary in particle size distribution
- The coarseness varies "quite considerably among different brands"
- Testing and note-taking are essential for finding the optimal substrate for local conditions

## Making Homemade Brown Rice Powder

PF recommended making brown rice powder fresh rather than using prepackaged:
1. Place regular brown rice in a small canister-type coffee bean grinder
2. Grind to fine powder
3. Freshly ground brown rice is recommended over prepackaged type
4. "The freshness sometimes makes a big difference"

This is relevant to vermiculite selection because the freshness and fineness of the rice powder also affects water absorption and distribution within the substrate.

## Measurement Accuracy

PF emphasized that accurate measurement is critical for consistent results:
- 1 cup = 237 milliliters = 1/2 pint = 8 liquid ounces
- 2 cups in a pint, 2 pints in a quart, 4 cups in a quart
- "If the measuring cup specs aren't true, the formulas will be off, setting up certain failure or diminished growth"
- Each jar's substrate should be mixed individually for loading to ensure accurate formula rendering
