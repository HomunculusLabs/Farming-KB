# Brassinosteroid Effects on Fungal Growth

## Overview

Brassinosteroids are a class of steroidal plant-growth regulators recognized as a distinct group of phytohormones. First identified in pollen extracts of Brassica napus, approximately 30 members have been detected across higher plants including angiosperms, gymnosperms, and a green alga.

Their growth-promoting activity involves complex physiological effects including increased cell elongation and cell division. A landmark 1990 study by Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt demonstrated for the first time that brassinosteroids also exert powerful growth-promoting effects on fungi.

## Brassinosteroid Background

Brassinosteroids were initially isolated from rapeseed (Brassica napus) pollen in 1979. They represent the sixth major class of plant hormones alongside auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, ethylene, and abscisic acid.

Their diverse effects on crop yields, stress resistance, and plant development had been documented prior to the fungal studies. Promising results in applying brassinosteroid compounds to improve crop yields and confer anti-stress effects had been reported.

Synthetic brassinosteroids prepared from plant sterols such as stigmasterol have been developed for agricultural applications. Their effects include promotion of cell elongation and division, enhanced photosynthetic efficiency, and improved resistance to environmental stresses.

## The Gartz Study: Experimental Design

### Brassinosteroid Used

The study employed synthetic 22S,23S-homobrassinolide, prepared from stigmasterol. This compound contains the characteristic brassinosteroid structural features: a 5alpha-cholestane skeleton with hydroxyl groups at C-2, C-3, and C-22, C-23, a ketone at C-6, and a lactone ring bridging C-22 and C-23.

The 22S,23S stereochemistry was specifically selected for its known high biological activity in plant systems.

### Cultivation Systems

Two cultivation systems were tested, each with ten experimental runs:

1. **Malt agar**: 6% malt agar medium supplemented with 5 g/L KH2PO4
2. **Dung and grain substrate**: horse dung and rice grain mixture in water, also with 5 g/L KH2PO4

In both systems, the brassinosteroid was applied at 0.01 ppm (10 parts per billion), consistent with the known high potency of brassinosteroids in biological systems.

### Fungal Strain

A strain of [[psilocybe-cubensis]] (Earle) Singer previously described by Gartz was used. This strain was selected for its characteristically high stability in growth and fruiting performance across various substrates.

## Results

### Mycelial Growth Rate

On malt agar, mycelia treated with 0.01 ppm brassinosteroid grew two to three times faster than untreated controls. This dramatic acceleration suggests that brassinosteroids interact with fungal cellular machinery in ways analogous to their effects on plant cells.

Possible mechanisms include modulation of membrane properties, activation of signal transduction pathways, or direct effects on gene expression related to cell wall biosynthesis and hyphal extension.

### Fruiting Acceleration

On the dung and grain substrate, the first flush appeared remarkably earlier:

- **Control**: first sporocarps in 4 to 5 weeks after inoculation
- **Brassinosteroid-treated**: fruiting in 3 to 3.5 weeks after inoculation

This represents a 25 to 30% reduction in time to first harvest, significant for commercial cultivation where faster turnover directly impacts profitability.

### Increased Yield

The number of fruit bodies per flush was significantly enhanced:

- **Control first flush**: 1 to 3 mushrooms per container
- **Treated first flush**: 4 to 7 mushrooms per container

Over five flushes, total dry mass was substantially higher:

- **Control**: 2.3 to 2.9 g dry weight per 10 g substrate
- **Treated**: 3.4 to 3.9 g dry weight per 10 g substrate

This represents approximately a 40 to 50% increase in biological efficiency from treatment with a compound at 10 ppb concentration.

### Suppression of Abnormalities

Brassinosteroid treatment almost completely suppressed the formation of incomplete fruit bodies, a common problem in mushroom cultivation where some primordia fail to develop into mature sporocarps.

This normalization effect suggests improved physiological health and developmental coordination, possibly through enhanced nutrient transport or hormonal signaling within the mycelial network.

### Morphological Changes

An unexpected morphological alteration was observed: fruit bodies from treated cultures consistently lacked the typical membranous annulus (ring) on the stems.

The annulus normally forms from remnants of the partial veil connecting the cap margin to the stem. Its absence suggests brassinosteroids may interfere with the developmental program for partial veil formation and rupture.

Whether this represents accelerated development or specific disruption of veil tissue differentiation remains unclear.

## Implications and Significance

### Physiological Role in Fungi

The strong growth-promoting effect at nanomolar concentrations suggests brassinosteroids may play a natural physiological role in higher fungi. No studies on endogenous brassinosteroids in fungi had been published prior to this work.

Several possibilities explain the fungal response to plant brassinosteroids:

1. Fungi may possess endogenous brassinosteroid biosynthetic pathways not yet identified
2. Fungal receptors may share homology with plant brassinosteroid receptors (such as BRI1 kinase)
3. Brassinosteroids may interact with conserved sterol signaling pathways present across eukaryotes

The authors concluded that brassinosteroids could play a physiological role in higher fungi and called for further studies including commercially important mushroom species.

### Agricultural and Commercial Potential

The application of brassinosteroids to mushroom cultivation could offer several advantages:

- **Faster colonisation**: reducing the window of vulnerability to contamination
- **Earlier fruiting**: shortening production cycles and improving facility throughput
- **Higher yields**: increasing biological efficiency by 40 to 50% per unit substrate
- **Reduced abnormalities**: minimising losses from incomplete fruit body formation
- **Extreme potency**: the very low effective concentration (0.01 ppm) makes treatment highly economical
