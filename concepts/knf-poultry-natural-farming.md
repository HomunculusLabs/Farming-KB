# KNF Poultry Natural Farming

Korean Natural Farming (KNF), developed by [[cho-han-kyu-life-and-philosophy]], extends its principles beyond crops into livestock management.

The KNF approach to poultry represents a fundamentally different philosophy from conventional factory farming.

## Overview

The aim of modern commercial poultry operations is to attain maximum efficiency and productivity for the highest profit. However, this approach comes with a total disregard for the chickens' quality of life. Chickens are raised in narrow cages under artificial lighting and heating, and are fed with chemicals and medicines. They are treated as mere tools of production and profit, not as living beings worthy of respect.

Natural Farming also aims for efficiency, productivity, and profit — but it returns to chickens their right to a better life. Chickens are provided houses with ample space and natural heating, fresh water, and natural feeds. The housing design eliminates the need for cleaning and produces no wastewater. No chemicals are used and very little labour is required to maintain the system.

This stands in contrast to the Knf Vs Jadam Vs Em that dominates modern agriculture.

## Three Principles of NF Poultry

Three core principles govern all aspects of Natural Farming poultry management.

1. The floor of the chicken housing should be soil, not concrete. Separating chickens from the soil can cause disease by disrupting their natural relationship with the earth.

2. Temperature should not be artificially controlled. Heat from fermenting compost will suffice, even for small chicks, eliminating the need for heating machines.

3. Feed for hatchlings should consist of whole brown rice grains and bamboo leaves to strengthen the intestines.

These principles work together to create an environment where chickens can develop their natural vitality and resistance to disease.

## Housing Design

The NF chicken housing is designed so that there is no bad smell, no emission, no cleaning needed, and no disease.

Each component of the housing serves a specific purpose in maintaining a healthy ecosystem.

### Roof

The roof is made of galvanized zinc sheets.

The metal roof conducts heat into the house and features a skylight that allows sunlight to illuminate approximately one-third of the floor throughout the day.

As the sun moves across the sky, it shines on different parts of the floor.

This enables the entire floor to receive some sunlight and get naturally disinfected over the course of a day.

The greater proportion of shade to sun enables the growth of beneficial microorganisms on the floor surface.

### Wall

Walls are constructed from steel wire mesh.

The mesh disperses the heat conducted by the metal roof and allows continuous passage of warm and cool air into the interior.

This airflow takes away moisture from the floor and controls dampness naturally.

The walls have curtains that can be adjusted to regulate air flow as conditions change.

The air convection allowed by the open walls provides an ideal moisture level for microorganisms to thrive.

### Floor

The flooring of the poultry shed must be soil-based.

It is carpeted with rice straw chopped to approximately 3 cm in length.

The general floor depth is 7 cm, though for broilers a depth of 3 cm is sufficient.

[[fermented-plant-extract-fpe]], [[lactic-acid-bacteria-knf]], and [[query-how-do-i-use-beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]] are added to the soil floor mixture.

It is vital to maintain a rich population of microorganisms on the floor to break down chicken feces.

This microbial activity is the primary reason there is no smell in the chicken house.

The feces do not need to be removed unless they are needed for [[composting-materials-alternative]].

Remarkably, the chickens feed on the fermented products of their own feces.

The floor thus serves as feed producer, fertilizer factory, and waste treatment plant all in one.

### Water System

The water delivery system is made of PVC pipe drilled with holes.

Each hole serves one chicken to prevent fighting over access to water.

The size of the hole and pipe is increased as the chickens grow.

Each hole is drilled at a slight backward tilt from the chicken's drinking position.

This angle is designed to wipe the chicken's beak as it lifts its head, preventing water from dripping onto its chest.

Water should always flow through the pipe to ensure freshness and cleanliness.

## Brooding Box Design

Chicks are raised in brooding boxes that also serve as exercise and play areas.

In front of the box is a chicks' playground, which is gradually enlarged as they grow.

The brooding box is divided into three distinct rooms labeled A, B, and C.

Room A is kept hot and serves as the resting area for the chicks.

Room B is warm and serves as the feeding area.

Chicks drink water in Room C.

The distance from Room A to Room C is approximately 710 cm for medium-sized chicks.

This design compels chicks to run back and forth between the rooms 50 to 60 times per day, ensuring vigorous exercise.

A cloth curtain resembling a mother hen's feathers is draped between Rooms B and C.

This curtain provides a psychological comfort that makes the chicks feel cared for, reducing stress and promoting healthy development.

## Perch and Nesting

### Perch

The perch is slightly arched in shape, sloping gently with the highest point at the center.

This design prevents chickens from bumping into each other when settling in for the night.

### Nesting Box

Nesting boxes are placed inside the house so that chicks become familiar with them from an early age.

Bright lighting is used at first, then the area is gradually darkened so that hens will not become frightened when using the boxes.

This gradual acclimation ensures that hens comfortably use the nesting boxes for egg-laying.

## Heating Philosophy

Artificial heating is not provided in Natural Farming, even when temperatures drop below zero.

Chicks that are exposed to cold develop short, dense feathers and strong natural resistance to temperature fluctuations. Under artificial heating, chicks will grow long feathers and develop weak resistance. When these artificially heated chicks are later exposed to cold, they tend to gather in a corner, significantly increasing the chances of being crushed to death.

In colder regions where temperatures are consistently low, heat from fermenting compost can be used as a natural heat source. The compost is situated directly below the brooding box, providing gentle, radiant warmth from below. This approach eliminates the need for any electrical or fossil-fuel heating equipment.
