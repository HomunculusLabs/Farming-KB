## Overview

Cannabis pest management uses Integrated Pest Management (IPM) principles to prevent, identify, and eliminate insect pests in the grow space. IPM prioritizes prevention and least-toxic interventions before resorting to chemical controls. Early detection is critical — small pest populations are manageable; large infestations can destroy entire crops.

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM) Framework

### Prevention (First Line of Defense)

- **Quarantine**: Isolate new plants, clones, or soil for 7-14 days before introducing to the grow space
- **Cleanliness**: Remove dead plant material, sanitize surfaces between grows, wash hands before handling plants
- **[[eclss-environmental-control-life-support]]**: Proper [[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]] make the environment less hospitable to pests. See [[cannabis-environment-control]].
- **Physical barriers**: HEPA intake filters, sticky traps, and sealed grow spaces prevent pest entry
- **[[coleman-beneficial-insects-and-ecological-pest-control]]**: Introduce predatory insects preventively in living soil or greenhouse grows
- **Regular inspection**: Check undersides of leaves, soil surface, and bud sites with a loupe or magnifier at least twice weekly

### Monitoring

- **Yellow sticky traps**: Place throughout the grow space at canopy height. Monitor weekly for pest types and population trends.
- **Blue sticky traps**: More attractive to thrips specifically.
- **Visual inspection**: Systematic examination of leaves, stems, soil, and buds with each watering.

### Response Levels

1. **Cultural controls**: Adjust environment, remove affected leaves
2. **Mechanical controls**: Spray off with water, physical removal
3. **Biological controls**: Beneficial insects, microbial sprays
4. **Organic/biorational sprays**: Neem oil, insecticidal soap, Spinosad
5. **Chemical controls**: Synthetic pesticides (last resort, avoid during flowering)

## Common Cannabis Pests

### Spider Mites (Tetranychus urticae)

The most damaging and common cannabis pest. Tiny arachnids that feed on plant sap from the undersides of leaves.

**Identification**:
- Tiny dots on leaf undersides (adults are ~0.5mm)
- Fine webbing between leaves and branches
- Yellow stippling (pinprick dots) on upper leaf surface
- Leaves eventually turn bronze/yellow and die

**Life cycle**: Eggs hatch in 3-5 days, adults reproduce in 5-10 days. Populations explode in warm, dry conditions (low humidity). Complete life cycle in as little as 7 days.

**Management**:
- Increase humidity (spider mites thrive in dry conditions)
- Spray with water to physically dislodge (daily for heavy infestations)
- Neem oil or azadirachtin sprays every 3-5 days for 2-3 weeks (must hit all life stages)
- Spinosad (Captain Jack's Dead Bug Brew) — effective against adults
- [[comparison-predatory-mites-vs-ladybugs-biocontrol]] (Phytoseiulus persimilis, Amblyseius californicus) for biological control
- For severe infestations: Essentria IC3 or other commercial organic miticides

### Fungus Gnats (Bradysia spp.)

Small dark flies that hover around the soil surface. Larvae feed on organic matter and roots in the growing medium.

**Identification**:
- Tiny black flies (1/8 inch) flying near soil surface
- Small white larvae in the top inch of soil
- Slowed growth, wilting, poor seedling establishment

**Life cycle**: Adults live 7-10 days, laying eggs in moist soil. Larvae feed for 10-14 days before pupating. Entire cycle: 3-4 weeks.

**Management**:
- Allow soil surface to dry between waterings (larvae need moisture)
- Yellow sticky traps to catch adults and monitor population
- Mosquito Bits ([[cervantes-bacillus-thuringiensis-bt-control]] israelensis — Bti) as a soil drench
- Diatomaceous earth (DE) sprinkled on soil surface (desiccates larvae)
- [[ingham-beneficial-nematodes-soil-food-web]] (Steinernema feltiae) applied to soil
- Cinnamon powder on soil surface as a deterrent

### Thrips (Order Thysanoptera)

Tiny, slender insects that scrape leaf surfaces and feed on the sap. They can also feed on flower petals.

**Identification**:
- Tiny (1-2mm) elongated insects, yellow to brown
- Silver or bronze streaking on leaves
- Black specks (frass) on leaf surfaces
- Deformed new growth

**Management**:
- Blue sticky traps (more attractive to thrips than yellow)
- Spinosad sprays every 3-5 days
- Neem oil for prevention
- Predatory mites (Amblyseius cucumeris) for biological control
- Stratiolaelaps scimitus (Hypoaspis miles) mites for soil-dwelling thrips pupae

### Aphids (Family Aphididae)

Soft-bodied insects that cluster on new growth and undersides of leaves, sucking plant sap and excreting honeydew.

**Identification**:
- Clusters of small green, black, or yellow insects on new growth
- Sticky honeydew on leaves below infestation
- Sooty mold growing on honeydew
- Curled, distorted new leaves

**Management**:
- Spray with water to dislodge
- Insecticidal soap (direct contact required)
- Neem oil
- Ladybugs (Hippodamia convergens) — release 1,000+ for a 4x4 space
- Pyrethrin-based sprays for heavy infestations

## Organic Spray Recipes

### Neem Oil Spray

- 1 teaspoon cold-pressed neem oil
- 0.5 teaspoon mild liquid soap (Dr. Bronner's or castile) as emulsifier
- 1 quart warm water
- Shake vigorously and spray immediately
- Apply in lights-off period or evening to avoid leaf burn
- Repeat every 3-5 days for 2-3 weeks

### Insecticidal Soap

- 1 teaspoon mild liquid soap (not detergent)
- 1 quart water
- Spray directly on pests — must make contact to be effective
- No residual activity
- Safe to use up to 2 weeks before harvest

### Spinosad Spray

- Follow label directions for Captain Jack's Dead Bug Brew or Monterey Garden Insect Spray
- Effective against thrips, spider mites, and caterpillars
- Apply every 3-5 days
- Relatively safe for beneficial insects once dry

## Pest Prevention Checklist

- Inspect all new plants before bringing them into the grow space
- Maintain proper environment per [[cannabis-environment-control]]
- Use yellow/blue sticky traps at all times
