# Psilocybin and the Understanding of Consciousness

Powell's central thesis is that psilocybin is not merely a substance that alters consciousness but a tool for understanding what consciousness actually is. By analysing the chemical changes that accompany the altered state of mind, we can use this data to comprehend how normal consciousness works. Psilocybin represents the key to solving what Powell calls the "twin mysteries of consciousness and reality."

## The Mutability of Consciousness

The mutability of consciousness is the foundational insight. Consciousness, whatever it is exactly, is the "stuff" which mediates all science and all types of reasoning and all of our theories about the world. Consciousness can therefore be understood as the very ground of our being, the "factor x" which makes us what we are.

Consider the following thought experiment: imagine that all scientists wore identical spectacles and that these spectacles determined the perceptual view of the things being scrutinised. All the data amassed by these scientists would be related in some intimate way to the effects of their spectacles. It would be quite a breakthrough for these scientists to suddenly cease their traditional research in order to focus upon the nature of the factor mediating their research, namely, their glasses.

This imaginary situation is not unlike the real world, only this time it is our consciousness, or rather our state of consciousness, through which we view and experience Nature. We can call this "normal consciousness," a kind of shared lens through which science and scientific interpretation proceeds.

## Chemistry and Consciousness: The Bridge Between Mind and Matter

The reason why consciousness is mutable is because it is mediated by chemistry. Mutable or transformable chemical processes underlie consciousness. Our normal ways of thinking are arguably constrained due to the chemical hardware (or "wetware") of the brain. It is therefore conceivable that certain aspects of the world with which we interface remain hidden to us because of the limitations of our everyday type of consciousness.

Taken together, "chemistry and consciousness" directly address the boundary between the physical and the psychological. Chemistry implies chemicals and substances -- clearly "material" things -- whereas altered states of awareness lie in the realm of the intangible mind. Psilocybin bridges perfectly the conceptual gap between the two seemingly incompatible worlds of mind and matter, psychological and physical.

## The Informational Nature of Mindstuff

Powell's proposed solution to the consciousness problem is that mindstuff is information. Consciousness is an informational pattern embodied within the neuronal wetware of the brain. Entheogens like psilocybin work by enabling novel informational patterns to emerge which are not normally "permitted" due to the normal constraints operating in the brain.

This means that everything -- whether atoms, molecules, muscles, or thoughts -- could be described in purely informational terms. The mind and body could then be seen as consisting of essentially the same kind of substance: particular forms of information.

Consciousness emerges as a type of global information whose form is constantly undergoing change due to the integration and accessing of other types of information through the senses. For us, there is only information. Our minds are uniquely enduring patterns spun from it. Information from other patterns, or systems, is continually being absorbed, integrated, and given out again. In this process, the form of the mind changes through changes in the ways in which neurons are formally connected to one another.

## Consciousness as Neuronal Information in Process

The brain's 13 billion neurons, each potentially connecting to 10,000 others, create a system of such informational complexity that "somewhere amongst such informational complexity lies our consciousness -- who and what we are." Consciousness can therefore be viewed as a neuron-mediated form of information in process. As the overall state of the brain changes so too does consciousness change.

The dictionary definition of information is instructive: it comes from the Latin word informare, which means "to give form to." When information informs us, it alters the form, or pattern, of the informational system that is our mind. The mind is therefore an information-based system constantly re-forming itself through the accessing of information deriving from other kinds of information-based media.

## The Dual Function of Entheogenic Investigation

The virtue of investigating the perceptual effects of entheogenic agents is twofold:

1. **Enhanced perception of Nature**: Through their dramatic action within the brain we might come to perceive Nature in a new and arguably more enhanced way.

2. **Understanding consciousness itself**: We might come to understand more about the underlying chemistry which is bound up with normal conscious processes. The modus operandi of entheogenic substances reveals the delicate chemical mechanisms which govern consciousness and our perceptions of reality.

If through the study of entheogens we can understand more about the interface between the mind and the "world out there," then we shall know more clearly what consciousness is, how it is formed, and how it can come to experience transcendence.

## Psychedelics as Probes of Consciousness

Strassman argues that psychedelic drugs, since they alter consciousness, should be able to tell us something about how consciousness is formed in the normal brain. In other words, since psychedelics alter higher cognitive functions connected with what it is to be human, then they can essentially be employed as probes to study the mind/brain interface.

Strassman concludes: "Scientists ought to take all claims about the mind seriously. The DMT and psilocybin states are basically non-material. They are not dependent upon the body moving through space, or interaction with other material objects. Thus, they are windows into consciousness, which, while it may have structural underpinnings, is essentially a movement of energy, rather than of matter."

## The Mystery Remains

Despite these theoretical advances, the ultimate nature of consciousness remains elusive. As Powell acknowledges, "what exactly does it mean that there is an increase in neuronal informational activity? Just how valid and 'real' are the novel patterns of neuronal firing orchestrated by psilocybin? Indeed, how can novel patterns of neuronal firing actually be conscious thoughts?"

These questions point to what philosophers call the "hard problem" of consciousness -- the difficulty of explaining why and how physical processes in the brain give rise to subjective experience. Psilocybin does not solve this problem, but it illuminates it by demonstrating the intimate relationship between chemical processes and conscious experience.

## The Radical Implication

The most radical implication of Powell's analysis is that consciousness is not merely an epiphenomenon of brain activity but a fundamental feature of reality. If mindstuff is information, and if information is the fundamental currency of reality itself (as Powell argues in his broader cosmological framework), then consciousness is not something that "emerges" from matter but is rather a particular form that information takes when organised in certain ways.

Psilocybin, by demonstrating the mutability of consciousness and its dependence upon chemical/informational processes, opens the door to this more radical understanding. The mushroom does not merely alter consciousness; it reveals consciousness to be something far stranger and more significant than the materialist paradigm allows.

## The Philosophical Significance of Consciousness Mutability

Since the philosophical musings of Descartes, philosophers have had a veritable free-for-all in their attempts to either defend or do away with the mind/body problem. Yet, not one professional mind/body philosopher has become seriously involved with entheogenic experimentation in order to further their knowledge and insight into the dynamic interplay between chemistry and altered states of awareness. Most books purportedly dealing with the issue of consciousness patently ignore psychoactive substances altogether, as if they had nothing whatsoever to tell us.

This omission is remarkable, because psilocybin addresses the mind/body problem directly. Chemistry implies chemicals and substances -- clearly "material" things -- whereas altered states of awareness lie in the realm of the intangible mind. Psilocybin bridges perfectly the conceptual gap between these two seemingly incompatible worlds. The more we can understand the psilocybin modus operandi, the closer we get to divining the actual design of the bridge linking mind to matter.

## Altered States and the Nature of Consciousness

Altered forms of awareness demand altered brain processes, and such a change in brain state can be achieved in many different ways whether through psilocybin mushrooms, endogenous DMT, yoga, meditation, fasting, or spontaneous epileptic disturbances. Mystical experience is therefore not to be conveniently disposed of with a label. The fact that such diverse methods can produce similar states of consciousness suggests that these states represent fundamental capacities of the human brain -- latent modes of consciousness that exist as potential configurations of the brain's informational architecture.

Psilocybin reveals these latent configurations by chemically perturbing the system into new regions of its state space. The altered state is not a pathological aberration but a genuine mode of consciousness -- one that the brain is capable of sustaining and which may carry valid information about the nature of reality.

## The Significance of Human Consciousness

Powell argues that if we are genuinely interested in the decisive nature of reality and the decisive nature of human consciousness then we are obliged to follow all and any paths of enquiry. The untrammelled path laid out by entheogenic plants and fungi is, perhaps, the last viable route to evidence that shows that human consciousness is somehow central to reality.

The very fact that human consciousness can be altered by natural chemical agents to produce experiences of such profound significance -- experiences that feel "more real than real" and that carry lasting epistemological value -- suggests that consciousness is not a mere biological accident but plays some functional role in the broader informational processes of reality. Psilocybin does not merely change consciousness; it reveals consciousness as a phenomenon of potentially cosmic significance.
