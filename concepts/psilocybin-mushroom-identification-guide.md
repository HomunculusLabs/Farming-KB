# Psilocybin Mushroom Identification Guide

Key features for identifying psilocybin-containing mushrooms in the field, with emphasis on distinguishing them from potentially dangerous lookalikes.

## Critical Safety Warning

Proper identification requires training, patience, and ideally expert guidance. Many [[palmer-poisonous-mushrooms-deadly-species]] can cause serious illness or death. Never consume a mushroom based solely on written descriptions. This guide covers general features only.

## Key Identification Features

### 1. Blue Bruising (Bluing Reaction)

The most characteristic feature of [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] is the tendency of the flesh to turn blue or blue-green when bruised, cut, or handled. This reaction is caused by the oxidation of psilocin (the active compound) and is the single most useful field indicator.

- Bluing may appear on the stem, cap, gills, or flesh when damaged.
- The reaction is not instantaneous; it may take seconds to minutes to develop.
- Bluing intensity varies by species and age; older specimens may show less reaction.
- Not all psilocybin mushrooms blue equally strongly, and some non-psychoactive mushrooms can also show bluing (e.g., some *Boletus* species), so bluing alone is not definitive.

### 2. Spore Print Color

A spore print is essential for identification. Place the cap gills-down on paper (white and dark) for several hours to collect the deposit:

- Most psilocybin mushrooms produce a **purple-brown to dark purple-black** spore print.
- *Psilocybe cubensis*: Purple-brown to nearly black.
- *[[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]]*: Dark purple-brown.
- *[[growing-gourmet-caramel-capped-psilocybe-cyanescens-cultivation]]*: Dark purplish-brown.
- *[[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]]*: Black.

### 3. Gill Characteristics

Examine the gills (lamellae) on the underside of the cap:

- **Attachment**: Most psilocybin species have gills that are attached to the stem (adnate) or extend slightly down it (adnexed to subdecurrent). In *Psilocybe* species, the gills are typically adnate to adnexed.
- **Color**: Young gills are typically light-colored (whitish, grayish) and darken to purple-brown or nearly black as spores mature.
- **Spacing**: Gills are usually close to moderately spaced.
- Important: Many deadly *Galerina* species also have attached gills and dark spore prints, making careful microscopic or genetic identification necessary.

### 4. Cap Characteristics

- **Size**: Varies enormously from species to species. *P. cubensis* caps are 1.5-8 cm; *P. azurescens* can reach 10 cm.
- **Shape**: Conical to convex when young, often expanding to broadly convex or plane with age. *P. semilanceata* retains a distinctive conical or bell-shaped cap with a pronounced nipple (papilla) at the center.
- **Color**: Varies from golden-brown (*P. cubensis*) to olive-brown, chestnut, or bluish. Caps often become lighter as they dry (hygrophanous).
- **Surface**: Typically smooth, sometimes slightly sticky when moist. *P. cyanescens* has a wavy cap margin that gives it the name "wavy cap."
- **Pellicle**: A thin, gelatinous skin (pellicle) covers the cap of many *Psilocybe* species and can be peeled.

### 5. Stem Characteristics

- **Texture**: Stems of most psilocybin mushrooms are fibrous, hollow (or becoming hollow with age), and typically whitish to yellowish, often bluing when handled.
- **Ring/Annulus**: Some species develop a partial veil that leaves a ring on the stem. *Psilocybe cubensis* has a well-developed membranous ring that darkens with falling spores.
- **Base**: May be slightly thickened or bulbous.

### 6. Odor

Most psilocybin mushrooms have a mild, farinaceous (flour-like or mealy) odor. A strong unpleasant or chemical odor is a warning sign.

## Major Psychoactive Genera

| Genus | Spore Print | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| *Psilocybe* | Purple-brown to black | Blue bruising, hygrophanous caps, usually on soil or dung |
| *Panaeolus* | Black | On dung, thin caps, *P. cyanescens* is notably potent |
| *Gymnopilus* | Rusty orange | On wood, large, orange-brown, bitter taste |
| *Pluteus* | Pink to salmon | On wood, free gills (not attached to stem) |
| *Inocybe* | Brown to olive-brown | Fibrous caps, unpleasant odor, many are toxic |

## Dangerous Lookalikes

### *Galerina marginata* (Deadly Galerina)
- Grows in similar habitats (wood chips, forest floor, near rotting wood)
- Has a brown cap and similar gill attachment
- Spore print is rusty brown (distinguishing from purple-brown psilocybin species)
- Contains **amatoxins**, the same lethal toxins found in *Amanita phalloides* (death cap). Ingestion can cause liver failure and death.
- This is the single most dangerous lookalike.

### *Inocybe* species
- Some *Inocybe* species contain **muscarine** and can cause serious poisoning.
- Share grassland and forest habitats with some psilocybin species.
- Distinguished by brown spore prints and fibrous caps.

### Non-psychoactive *Panaeolus* species
- Several *Panaeolus* species growing on dung are not psychoactive but are not dangerous.
- Accurate identification requires spore print examination and sometimes microscopy.

## Identification Steps

1. Note the habitat (dung, grassland, wood chips, forest floor)
2. Observe the cap shape, color, and surface texture
3. Check for blue bruising when the stem is handled or the flesh is cut
4. Make a spore print (purple-brown to black for most psychoactive species)
5. Examine gill attachment and spacing
6. Check stem characteristics (hollow, ring present, bluing)
7. Note the odor
8. Compare with multiple reliable field guide references
9. When in doubt, do not consume
