# Permaculture Solar Energy Inventions

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Bill Mollison described several low-technology, high-effectiveness solar energy inventions during his Permaculture Design Course. These are practical, buildable systems that outperform expensive commercial alternatives at a fraction of the cost.

## Source
Bill Mollison Permaculture Design Course, Pamphlet IX (Permaculture Techniques)

## Mollison's Black Sand Solar Heater

### Discovery
Mollison discovered this while walking along the beach at Molokai in bare feet. His feet started cooking on the black sand, which was intolerably hot. The black sand had been dehydrating materials for ages through solar absorption.

### How It Works
1. Run water pipes through a box filled with black sand
2. If your sand isn't naturally black, blacken it
3. Put glass on top of the box
4. The result: far more efficient heat transmission than metal collectors, producing endless hot water at no cost

### Advantages Over Commercial Collectors
- Far more efficient than $3,000 metal collectors
- Self-regulating -- won't overheat
- Uses locally available materials (sand, glass, pipe)
- No moving parts
- Can be built on top of existing water tanks

### Applications
- Domestic hot water heating
- Space heating via radiant floors
- Greenhouse heating
- Could be installed on top of people's water tanks; wouldn't even need a glass top

## Parabolic Focusing House

### Design
1. Build a glass house front as a focusing system
2. Place sun-reflecting mirror systems under the eaves
3. Focus point is about eight feet off the ground in front of the house (where the driveway is)
4. Run a copper pipe around the focus hole in the ground

### How It Works
- Your car sits under the focus; put a magnet on it and bring it up into focus
- The metal melts and drips into the hole
- Copper pipe around the hole heats all winter from molten metal stored underground
- Cover the hole at end of autumn; stored heat runs all winter

## Soapstone Thermal Storage System

### Design
1. Heat up a soapstone block using a fold-out focusing mirror
2. Move the mirror on a pickup truck to the site
3. Insert a tube into the soapstone
4. Read the calories delivered with a meter

### Practical Application
- A non-polluting system using old Indian tricks: carry a hot stone slab from the communal fire to a cooking location
- Cook on it for hours, then carry it back on green branches
- Modernized with mirrors and soapstone blocks

## Titanium Oxide Nitrogen Fertilizer Factory

### Discovery
A researcher noted in Chemical Abstracts (around 1977) that titanium oxide catalyzes atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia. He connected this to the excess of ammonia in the atmosphere and calculated that three acres of desert under this system would supply as much as a commercial fertilizer plant.

### How It Works
1. Build a sandbox with a trickle-in water system
2. Add subsurface barriers to make water dodge about
3. Fill with white sand and about 1/4 ounce of titanium oxide (common white paint pigment)
4. In sunlight, titanium oxide catalyzes atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia
5. This is a catalytic reaction -- you don't use up the sand or titanium oxide
6. Ammonia is highly water soluble; run the solution off

### Operation
- Don't run continuously (prevents algae buildup)
- Flush with water periodically
- Water your garden with the nitrogen-rich solution
- The ammonia is carried down into sand, establishing plants in dunes

### Practical Scale
- Three square feet in a peasant's garden can make a meaningful difference
- Titanium oxide is cheap (common filler in white paint after lead removal)
- In deserts, nitrogen evaporates into the atmosphere; this system captures it back

## Saline Pond Sky Focuser

### Design
1. Fill a pond with seawater to twice the normal salt concentration
2. When it rains, fresh water sits on top of the dense salt water
3. The surface acts as a total sky lens, focusing all light into a "hole" in the center
4. At the bottom, a high heat capacity solution sits insulated by water above

### What Happens Down There
- A hot saline alga grows (violent looking stuff)
- Different layers of mosquito larvae from different species occupy specific strata
- Demonstrates how common these natural sites must have been over ages

### Applications
- Could cook food down in the heated zone
- Build from black concrete with a straw around it
- Cook potatoes, or generate steam for power

## Key Principle

All these inventions share a common theme: they are "old Indian tricks" -- simple observations of natural phenomena turned into practical technology. Mollison emphasized that the body is a sensor: when your feet get cooked, your finger gets burned, take note. Eureka moments come from paying attention to what nature is already doing.
