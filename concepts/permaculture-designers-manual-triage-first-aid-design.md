## Priority Setting Principles

### The Designers Manual Approach

Mollison emphasizes that the first priority for any settlement is to locate its water supply, then arrange for access. The sequence of design decisions follows a clear hierarchy:

1. **Water supply** - locate and secure water sources
2. **Access** - establish roads, paths, and movement corridors
3. **Earthworks** - shape the land for water management and erosion control
4. **Structures** - place buildings and infrastructure
5. **Zone 1 gardens** - establish food production closest to the home
6. **Windbreaks and shelter** - protect the site from extreme elements
7. **Zone 2-4 production** - extend productive systems outward
8. **Wildlife corridors** - establish connectivity for biodiversity
9. **Community systems** - develop shared resources and social infrastructure

### The One Percent Rule

The manual suggests that designers should first identify the one percent of the site that gives ninety percent of the results - the critical leverage points where small interventions produce large outcomes. This might be:
- A single swale that irrigates an entire hillside
- A windbreak that protects the entire homestead
- A dam that provides all domestic and irrigation water
- A single access road that serves all zones

## Triage for Degraded Sites

### Assessment Categories

When approaching a degraded or damaged site, the designer must triage problems into categories:

**Critical (Immediate Action Required)**
- Active erosion or landslides
- Contaminated water supplies
- Structural instability
- Loss of remaining soil
- Fire risk to structures and forests

**Important (Action Within First Season)**
- Soil compaction and surface sealing
- Loss of vegetation cover
- Invasive species establishment
- Water table depletion
- Nutrient deficiency

**Desirable (Action Within First Few Years)**
- Biodiversity enhancement
- Productive system establishment
- Income generation systems
- Community infrastructure
- Education and demonstration

**Optional (Long-Term Development)**
- Fine-tuning species selections
- Advanced polyculture development
- Craft and cottage industry
- Research and monitoring
- Aesthetic improvements

## The Designers Manual Design Sequence

### Step 1: Site Analysis

Before any intervention, conduct thorough site analysis:
- Read the landscape for its story (erosion patterns, water flow, vegetation zones)
- Map existing resources (water, soil, vegetation, views, access)
- Identify problems (erosion, weeds, contamination, exposure)
- Note opportunities (microclimates, existing trees, water features)
- Research local climate, soils, and native species

### Step 2: Design the Access

The second priority is access:
- Place roads and tracks along contours where possible
- Avoid cutting across water flow paths
- Design for minimum maintenance and maximum utility
- Connect all zones efficiently
- Plan for future expansion

### Step 3: Water Management

Water is the primary design element:
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-swale-design-detailed]] and contour banks for infiltration
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-pig-systems]] for food production
- [[graywater-systems]] for waste utilization

### Step 4: Earthworks

Earthworks are the skeleton of the design:
- [[mollison-designers-herb-spiral-design-construction]] at the kitchen door
- [[gaias-garden-keyhole-beds-and-mandala-gardens]] for tropical systems
- Compost systems for waste recycling
- Small animal systems (chickens, rabbits)

### Step 7: Windbreaks

Windbreaks are critical and should precede broader planting:
- Plant windbreaks as priority, preceding planting of other species
- Design for species diversity within the windbreak
- Use multi-strata designs (tall trees, understory, ground cover)
- Orient perpendicular to prevailing winds
- Allow for air flow (not solid barriers)

### Step 8: Extended Production

Progressively develop outer zones:
- Zone 2: Orchard, small livestock, intensively managed garden
- Zone 3: Main crop, pasture, commercial production
- Zone 4: Forage, timber, extensive grazing
- Zone 5: Wilderness, wildlife, minimal intervention
