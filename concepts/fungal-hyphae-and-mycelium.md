## From Mycelium to Fruiting Body

A fungus produces a fruiting body only when its mycelium has accumulated
sufficient resources to support the energetically expensive process of
reproduction. The mycelium must gather enough carbon, nitrogen, and other
nutrients from its environment to construct the complex tissues of a mushroom
or other reproductive structure. Environmental cues — including temperature,
moisture, light, and substrate chemistry — trigger the transition from
vegetative growth to reproductive development.
