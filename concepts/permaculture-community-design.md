# Permaculture Community Design

Practical framework for designing and organizing permaculture communities, from William Ozier Williams' PDC course. Covers governance, communication, and social structures alongside physical design. See [[permaculture-design]] for the technical design process and [[permaculture-design]] for zone/sector basics.

## The Ten-Step Community Building Process

### 1. Define Shared Community Intention

Every community needs a clearly articulated shared purpose. This is not a detailed plan but a guiding vision that all members agree on. The intention should answer: Why does this community exist? What is it trying to create?

### 2. Create Organizational Mandala

The community organization mandala is a circular diagram where each "lobe" represents a functional domain of the community. Unlike hierarchical org charts, the mandala is a living pattern that can scale:

- Each lobe represents a function (food production, housing, governance, education, etc.)
- Each lobe is empowered with a mission, function, and purpose
- A willing Steward oversees each lobe's implementation
- Lobes operate semi-autonomously but coordinate through a central council
- The mandala pattern scales: each community sends representatives to a Local Community Council; local councils form Watershed Councils; watershed councils form Bio-Regional Councils; bioregional councils form Continental Councils

### 3. Establish Synergistic Communication

Decision-making procedures based on facilitation:

- **Three-round discussion model**:
  1. Proposal made; everyone gives initial statements (facilitator ensures fair air time)
  2. Clarifying statements and questions round
  3. Final comments round
- **Silent agreement signals** (thumbs up) prevent wasting air time on reiteration
- **Consensus with reasoned paramount objections**: objections must be objective and solid, not whim or opinion
- If no paramount objection exists, even dissenters should support the proposal
- "Synergistic communication is about giving other people the support, personal respect and opportunity for self-actualization that you yourself will someday want in return"

### 4. Non-Violent Communication (NVC)

Core principles for conflict resolution:

- Avoid insulting, threatening, or belittling remarks
- Do not throw blame -- express how actions make you feel, seek understanding
- **Reflecting**: say back your interpretation of what the other person is experiencing
- Each person states the issue AND something positive about the other person (reminds that one issue does not define the person)
- Requires restraint, compromise, compassion, honesty, and strength
- Use a neutral third-party facilitator when possible
- Escalating consequences for persistent agreement violations (three-strikes model, decided by community council)

### 5. Set Community Goals and Strategy

Once the organizational structure is in place, the Steward Council answers: What are our group goals? What, when, how, and why? With clear goals, each lobe can function independently but in a coordinated manner toward common objectives.

### 6. Create Standards, Norms, and Agreements

- The goal is agreed-upon interaction standards, not telling people how to be
- Typical standards: no physical violence, NVC adherence, respect for people/property/environment, fair air sharing
- Escalating consequences process decided by community council, not individuals

**Avoid "XYZ Communities"**: Communities defined by shared identity markers ("we are all vegetarian, neopagan, polyamorous") tend to fail because they:
- Are exclusionary to diverse good people
- Prohibit cultural evolution
- Promote "queen-bee syndrome" where one person polices who is a "proper" member
- Permaculture values diversity in ecological systems -- social systems should too

### 7. Create the Permaculture Design

Apply the standard [[permaculture-design]] to the community's physical space.

### 8. Plan Programs and Events

Classes, work parties, potlucks, celebrations to implement community goals.

### 9. Spread the Word

Build networks between communities. The organizational mandala enables standardized interconnection at watershed, bioregional, and continental scales.

## The Bigger Agenda: Networked Communities

Williams proposes a scaling governance model:

1. **Community Stewardship Council** (each community, max 10 members per council level)
2. **Local Community Council** (representatives from each community in a local area)
3. **Watershed Council** (local councils within the same watershed)
4. **Geo-Regional Council** (watershed councils at state scale, not bound by political borders)
5. **Bio-Regional Council** (e.g., "American Southwest" scale)
6. **Continental Council**
7. **Global Council**

Each council level should have no more than ten lobes for effective decision-making.

## Known Communities Referenced

Arcosanti, Auroville, Damanhur, The Farm, Findhorn -- all studied as models with varying degrees of success and different governance approaches.
