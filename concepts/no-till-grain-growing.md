## The Rethinking Question

Rather than never touching the soil, Fukuoka's method uses soil that is aerated by natural means. The question becomes: what are the weeds doing for the soil? If the plant you want is not harmed, the weed may be providing a beneficial function.

## Land Required Per Person

To keep one human alive entirely on one food:
- **1,800 square feet** of grain
- **5,400 square feet** of potatoes
- **13,500 square feet** of dairy farming
- **36,000 square feet** of pigs alone
- **90,000 square feet** of just beef

This comparison powerfully argues for plant-based diets in terms of land efficiency.

## Energy Efficiency

The Fukuoka system is extraordinarily energy-efficient:

- **Human labor**: A farmer eating a 2,000-calorie per day diet can produce 1,300 pounds (590 kg) of rice (22 bushels) on a quarter acre
- **Animal labor**: Traditional animal-powered farming uses at least 5x as many calories for the same results
- **Tractor farming**: Uses at least 10x as many calories as human labor
- **Conclusion**: Human-powered no-till is the most energy-efficient food [[clay-seed-pellet-production-method-concrete-mixer]] known

## Rice Paddy Setup

### Initial Preparation (First Year Only)

1. **Level the ground**
2. **Build a low mud retaining wall** around the plot that can hold 2 inches (5 cm) of water
3. **Soil conditioning** (first year only): Chisel plow if soil is extremely compacted, then spread lime or dolomite and a thin layer of chicken manure and water it in
4. **Boundary planting**: On the outer perimeter, grow weed-control plants such as comfrey, lemongrass, or citrus, mulched with sawdust

### No-Till Rice Strategy — Annual Cycle

1. **Plant combinations**: Each plot always grows rice and white clover, plus one additional grain: rye, barley, millet, winter wheat, or oats

| Crop | Seed per acre |
|------|--------------|
| Clover | 1 lb (0.5 kg) |
| Grains | 6–14 lbs (2.7–6.3 kg) |
| Rice | 5–10 lbs (2.3–4.5 kg) |

2. **Early fall**: Sow rice seed — broadcast and cover with straw, or make into seed balls (seeds mixed with clay dust and pressed into ball shapes using potter's clay, compost, and sometimes paper mush)
3. **Mid-fall**: Harvest last year's rice, dry on rice racks for 2 weeks, thresh off husks and straw, save the straw
4. **Within a month of rice harvest**: Sow unhusked rice in the field and spread saved husks and straw over it
5. **Winter**: If rice has grown to 6 inches, allow 40 ducks per acre to graze. Keep drained to prevent freezing.
6. **Spring**: Check for thin spots, sow more seed if needed
7. **Late spring**: Harvest the winter grain (rye, barley, etc.) — walking on the rice is fine. Stack grain to dry for a week, then thresh.
8. **Spread threshed straw**: If you have multiple plots, spread oat straw in the rye field and vice versa (cross-spreading prevents disease buildup)
9. **Early summer**: Flood the paddy for about a week until clover turns yellow (without killing it). The field should be at least half rice.
10. **Summer**: Prepare seeds for fall sowing; choose a different grain than the previous year for rotation.

### Cold Climate Adaptation

In areas too cold for rice:
- Use shorter cycle crops
- Plant spring wheat with oats or barley as the winter crop
- Experiment with squash, melons, tomato, cotton, vetch, or sunflowers as no-till crops
