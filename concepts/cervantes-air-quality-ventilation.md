## Air Quality and Ventilation

Good air quality and ventilation are essential for [[cervantes-ozone-generators-negative-ion-odor]], ONA (odor neutralizing agents), and negative ion generators. These can supplement carbon filtration but are generally less effective on their own. Sealing the grow room and maintaining negative air pressure (exhaust pulls harder than intake pushes) prevents odor from escaping through cracks and openings.

## Temperature Control

Ventilation is the primary tool for temperature management. In warm climates, the exhaust fan removes hot air while cooler intake air replaces it. If intake air is too warm, air conditioning may be necessary. For cooler climates or winter months, heating may be needed. Maintain daytime temperatures of 70-85 degrees F (21-29 degrees C) and nighttime temperatures 10-15 degrees cooler.

## Humidity Management

Ventilation removes humid air and replaces it with drier fresh air. During [[bloomfield-mushroom-evaporative-cooling-fungal-frigidity]] systems work well in dry climates to supplement fan-based ventilation.

## Negative Pressure

Maintaining negative air pressure in the grow room (where the exhaust pulls harder than the intake pushes) ensures that all air exits through the filtered exhaust rather than escaping through cracks. This is critical for odor control. A room with negative pressure will draw air in through any small openings, while positive pressure pushes air (and odors) out through every crack.

## Air Circulation Within the Canopy

Internal air circulation is separate from air exchange. Oscillating fans should create gentle, consistent movement throughout the plant canopy. Stagnant air pockets between plants promote mold, mildew, and pest problems. Position multiple fans at different heights to ensure air movement from top to bottom of the canopy. Avoid directing strong air streams directly at individual plants; instead, create a gentle swirling pattern throughout the room.

## Ductwork

Use rigid or semi-rigid ducting for maximum airflow efficiency. Flexible aluminum ducting is common but creates more air resistance due to corrugation. Insulated ducting prevents heat loss/gain and reduces condensation. Keep duct runs as short and straight as possible; each bend reduces airflow. Use duct tape or clamps to secure all connections. Avoid kinks or sharp bends that restrict airflow.

## Fan Sizing Guide

For grow rooms, size the exhaust fan to provide at least one air exchange per minute. For carbon filter setups, oversize the fan by 25 percent to compensate for filter resistance. Inline fans rated 4-6 inches are suitable for small rooms (4x4 feet). Larger rooms (8x8 feet and up) may require 8-10 inch fans. Match intake area to be at least as large as the exhaust area for efficient air exchange.

## Sealed Room vs. Ventilated Room

Two main approaches to grow [[eclss-environmental-control-life-support]]. Sealed rooms are preferred for commercial operations and growers using CO2 enrichment, while ventilated rooms work well for hobbyists and those on a budget. Hybrid approaches that use partial air exchange combined with supplemental dehumidification are also common.
