# Bee Product Medicinal Uses

Honey bees produce several products with documented medicinal properties.
Beyond honey's well-known antibacterial effects, propolis, pollen, royal
jelly, and beeswax each offer therapeutic benefits. This guide covers the
evidence and practical applications of each product.

## Honey

### Wound Healing
Honey has been used as a wound treatment for millennia. Modern clinical
research supports several mechanisms:

- **Osmotic effect** — high sugar concentration draws water from wounds,
  reducing edema and creating an unfavorable environment for bacteria
- **Low pH (3.2-4.5)** — acidic environment inhibits bacterial growth
- **[[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] production** — the enzyme glucose oxidase produces
  low levels of H2O2 when honey is diluted by wound fluids, providing
  sustained antibacterial action
- **MGO (methylglyoxal)** — unique to manuka honey; non-peroxide
  antibacterial activity

### Clinical Applications
- **Burns** — honey dressings reduce healing time, infection rates, and
  scarring compared to silver sulfadiazine
- **Diabetic ulcers** — promotes debridement, reduces odor, and stimulates
  granulation tissue
- **Surgical wounds** — reduces post-operative infection
- **Oral mucositis** — reduces severity in cancer patients undergoing
  radiation

### Using Honey for Wounds
- Use raw, unpasteurized honey with verified antibacterial activity
- Medical-grade honey (Medihoney, Manuka UMF 15+) is standardized
- Apply directly to cleaned wound, cover with non-adherent dressing
- Change dressing every 1-3 days

### Respiratory Health
- **Cough suppression** — buckwheat honey outperforms dextromethorphan in
  pediatric studies for nighttime cough
- **Sore throat** — honey coats and soothes; effective alone or in warm
  tea with lemon
- **Dosage**: 1-2 teaspoons for adults; 0.5-1 teaspoon for children over
  1 year

### Digestive Health
- **Gastric ulcers** — honey has anti-inflammatory and antibacterial
  effects against H. pylori
- **Gut microbiome** — prebiotic oligosaccharides in honey promote
  beneficial bifidobacteria
- **Manuka honey** — specifically studied for IBS symptom relief

### Safety
- Never give honey to infants under 12 months (botulism risk from
  Clostridium botulinum spores)
- Diabetics should use honey cautiously — it does raise blood sugar, though
  less than refined sugar in some studies

## Propolis

Propolis is a resinous substance bees collect from tree buds and sap flows.
They use it to seal gaps in the hive and as an antimicrobial agent.

### Composition
Over 300 compounds identified, including flavonoids, phenolic acids,
terpenes, and aromatic compounds. Exact composition varies by botanical
source and region.

### Medicinal Properties
- **Antibacterial** — active against Gram-positive bacteria including
  Staphylococcus aureus and Streptococcus species
- **Antifungal** — effective against [[bloomfield-candida-albicans-yeast-hyphal-dimorphism]] and dermatophytes
- **Antiviral** — shows activity against influenza and herpes simplex virus
- **Anti-inflammatory** — inhibits prostaglandin and leukotriene synthesis
- **Antioxidant** — flavonoids scavenge free radicals
- **Wound healing** — stimulates collagen production and tissue regeneration

### Applications
- **Sore throat lozenges** — propolis throat sprays and lozenges are
  commercially available and effective
- **Oral health** — mouthwashes reduce plaque and gingivitis
- **[[cannabis-for-skin-conditions-detailed]]** — creams for cold sores, minor burns, and acne
- **Immune support** — tinctures taken during cold and flu season
- **Wound care** — propolis ointments for minor cuts and abrasions

### Preparation
- **Tincture** — steep propolis chunks in 70% ethanol for 2-4 weeks;
  strain and dilute for internal use
- **Salve** — infuse propolis in oil, combine with beeswax for topical use
- **Powder** — freeze propolis, grind in a coffee grinder, encapsulate

## Bee Pollen

Bee pollen is flower pollen collected by foraging bees, packed into
corbicular pellets on their hind legs, and brought back to the hive as
a protein source.

### Nutritional Profile
- 20-35% protein (all essential amino acids)
- 30-55% carbohydrates
- 1-10% lipids
- Vitamins: B complex, C, D, E, K
- Minerals: calcium, iron, magnesium, zinc, potassium
- Enzymes, coenzymes, and antioxidants

### Medicinal Uses
- **Nutritional supplement** — broad-spectrum micronutrient support
- **Allergy desensitization** — anecdotal evidence suggests consuming local
  pollen may reduce seasonal allergies; limited clinical evidence
- **Antioxidant** — flavonoid and carotenoid content reduces [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]]
- **Liver protection** — animal studies show hepatoprotective effects
- **Athletic recovery** — some evidence for improved endurance and recovery

### Dosage and Use
- Start with 1/4 teaspoon per day and increase gradually to 1-2 teaspoons
- Granules can be eaten plain, added to smoothies, yogurt, or cereal
- Store in refrigerator or freezer to preserve nutrients
- Contraindicated for those with pollen allergies — start with very small
  amounts and monitor for reactions

## Royal Jelly

Royal jelly is the exclusive food of the queen bee throughout her
development. It is secreted by nurse bees from their hypopharyngeal and
mandibular glands.

### Composition
- 60-70% water
- 12-15% protein (including unique royalactin)
- 10-16% sugars
- 3-6% lipids
- B vitamins, minerals, and [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]]

### Medicinal Properties
- **Neuroprotective** — animal studies show improved cognitive function
