## Gymnopilus validipes

Gymnopilus validipes is a species of neurotropic mushroom in the family
Cortinariaceae. It is one of approximately 15 Gymnopilus species known to
contain [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

## Taxonomy and Classification

Gymnopilus validipes belongs to the family Cortinariaceae, order Agaricales.
The genus Gymnopilus contains approximately 200 species worldwide, though
only about 15 contain psychoactive compounds. The genus name derives from
Greek "gymnos" (naked) and "pilus" (cap), referring to the typically bare
cap surface characteristic of many species.

G. validipes is classified among the "big gymnopilus" species, an informal
grouping including several large-growing members known for impressive size
and bright orange to reddish coloration. Other neurotropic species in this
group include G. spectabilis (the spectacular rustgill), G. purpuratus, and
G. luteofolius, all sharing similar ecological roles as wood-decomposing
fungi.

The species epithet "validipes" likely refers to the robust stipe (stem),
which distinguishes it from more slender-stemmed Gymnopilus species. Within
the genus, G. validipes is placed near G. spectabilis and G. junonius based
on morphological similarities, though molecular studies would clarify exact
relationships.

## Macroscopic Identification Features

Gymnopilus validipes produces medium to large basidiocarps. The cap (pileus)
is typically convex to broadly convex, sometimes developing a slight umbo,
ranging from orange-yellow to orange-brown. The surface is smooth to finely
fibrillose, becoming slightly viscid when moist. Cap diameter typically ranges
from 5 to 15 centimeters in mature specimens.

The gills (lamellae) are adnate to subdecurrent and initially yellowish,
developing rusty-brown as spores mature. This rusty-brown spore color is a
key diagnostic feature of the entire genus Gymnopilus. The gills are
moderately close to crowded.

The stipe (stem) is central, robust, typically equal or slightly tapered toward
the base. It is pallid to yellowish, sometimes with ring zone or fibrillose
partial veil remnants. The flesh is yellowish to pale orange, firm, and may
develop greenish-blue staining when bruised, though this reaction is often
variable.

The spore print is rusty-brown to ferruginous, consistent with all Gymnopilus
species. Spores are roughly ellipsoid to amygdaliform with roughened surface,
measuring approximately 7-10 x 4-6 micrometers.

## Chemical and Psychoactive Properties

G. validipes has been confirmed to contain psilocybin and/or psilocin, placing
it among neurotropic fungi outside Psilocybe. The concentration of active
compounds is generally lower than in potent Psilocybe species like P.
semilanceata or P. azurescens, typical for Gymnopilus as a whole.
