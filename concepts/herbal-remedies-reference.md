# Herbal Remedies Reference

A practical reference for common medicinal herbs, their uses, preparations, and safety considerations. This guide covers herbs suitable for home cultivation on a homestead and addresses first-aid and everyday wellness applications.

## Disclaimer

This reference is for educational purposes only. It does not replace professional medical advice. Always consult a healthcare provider before using herbs medicinally, especially during pregnancy, nursing, or when taking medications.

## Preparations Overview

| Preparation | Description | Shelf Life |
|------------|-------------|------------|
| Infusion | Hot water extract of leaves/flowers (like tea) | 24-48 hours (fresh) |
| Decoction | Simmered extract of roots/bark/seeds | 24-48 hours (fresh) |
| Tincture | Alcohol extract (see [[herbal-tinctures-guide]]) | 2-5+ years |
| Glycerite | Glycerin extract (alcohol-free) | 1-2 years |
| Salve/Balm | Oil + beeswax topical (see [[herbal-salves-and-balms]]) | 1-2 years |
| Infused oil | Oil extract for salves or culinary use | 6-12 months |
| Capsule | Dried powdered herb in gelatin/vegetarian capsules | 1 year |
| Poultice | Fresh or dried herb applied directly to skin | Use immediately |
| Compress | Cloth soaked in infusion/decoction | Use immediately |
| Syrup | Sugar/honey-based preparation | 2-6 months (refrigerated) |

## Core Homestead Herb Profiles

### Calendula (Calendula officinalis)

**Parts used**: Flower heads (petals)
**Key actions**: Anti-inflammatory, wound healing, antimicrobial, lymphatic
**Preparations**: Salve, infused oil, tincture, tea
**Uses**: Wounds, burns, rashes, diaper rash, sunburn, lymphatic support
**Dose (tea)**: 1-2 tsp dried flowers per cup, 2-3x daily
**Cultivation**: Annual, full sun, self-seeds readily, flowers continuously with deadheading

### Chamomile (Matricaria recutita — German)

**Parts used**: Flower heads
**Key actions**: Calmative, anti-inflammatory, carminative, mild sedative
**Preparations**: Tea, tincture, infused oil, salve
**Uses**: Insomnia, anxiety, digestive upset, teething, colic, skin inflammation
**Dose (tea)**: 1-2 tbsp flowers per cup, steep 10 min, 2-3x daily
**Safety**: Avoid with ragweed allergies (Asteraceae family)

### Echinacea (Echinacea purpurea, E. angustifolia)

**Parts used**: Roots (E. angustifolia), aerial parts (E. purpurea)
**Key actions**: Immunostimulant, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory
**Preparations**: Tincture (best from fresh root), tea, capsule
**Uses**: Cold/flu prevention and early treatment, immune support, wound infection
**Dose (tincture)**: 30-60 drops (1:5, 65% alcohol), 3-6x daily at first sign of illness
**Note**: Most effective when taken at onset of symptoms; limited efficacy once illness is established

### Peppermint (Mentha x piperita)

**Parts used**: Leaves (aerial parts)
**Key actions**: Carminative, antispasmodic, analgesic, decongestant
**Preparations**: Tea, tincture, infused oil, essential oil
**Uses**: Nausea, indigestion, IBS, headaches, sinus congestion, fever
**Dose (tea)**: 1-2 tsp dried leaf per cup, 2-3x daily
**Safety**: Avoid in GERD (relaxes esophageal sphincter); avoid with infants

### Lemon Balm (Melissa officinalis)

**Parts used**: Leaves (aerial parts)
**Key actions**: Calmative, antiviral (herpes), carminative, mild antidepressant
**Preparations**: Tea, tincture, infused oil
**Uses**: Anxiety, insomnia, cold sores (topical), digestive upset, concentration
**Dose (tincture)**: 20-40 drops, 2-3x daily
**Note**: Infused oil applied topically at first sign of cold sore outbreak

### Garlic (Allium sativum)

**Parts used**: Cloves (bulb)
**Key actions**: Antimicrobial (broad spectrum), antiviral, cardiovascular support, expectorant
**Preparations**: Raw clove, infused oil, honey garlic, tincture
**Uses**: Colds/flu, infections, cardiovascular health, immune support
**Dose**: 1-2 raw cloves daily for prevention; more for active infection
**Note**: Allicin is destroyed by heat — use raw for antimicrobial effect

### Elderberry (Sambucus nigra)

**Parts used**: Ripe berries (flowers also medicinal)
**Key actions**: Antiviral, immunostimulant, anti-inflammatory, diaphoretic
**Preparations**: Syrup, tincture, decoction, jelly
**Uses**: Influenza prevention and treatment, colds, fever
**Dose (syrup)**: 1 tbsp daily for prevention; 1 tbsp every 2-3 hours during illness
**Safety**: Raw berries contain cyanogenic glycosides — always cook before consuming

### Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)

**Parts used**: Aerial parts (flowering tops)
**Key actions**: Hemostatic, anti-inflammatory, diaphoretic, carminative, bitter tonic
**Preparations**: Poultice, tincture, tea, salve
**Uses**: Wounds (stops bleeding), fevers (promotes sweating), digestive bitter, menstrual cramps
**Dose (tincture)**: 20-60 drops, 3x daily
**Note**: Powdered yarrow applied to wounds is an excellent styptic

### Valerian (Valeriana officinalis)

**Parts used**: Roots
**Key actions**: Sedative, anxiolytic, antispasmodic
**Preparations**: Tincture (strongest), decoction, capsule
**Uses**: Insomnia, anxiety, muscle spasms, nervous restlessness
**Dose (tincture)**: 20-40 drops before bed
**Safety**: Can cause grogginess; avoid with sedatives/alcohol

### Mullein (Verbascum thapsus)

**Parts used**: Leaves and flowers
**Key actions**: Expectorant, demulcent, anti-inflammatory
**Preparations**: Infused oil (flowers for ear drops), tea (leaves), tincture
**Uses**: Respiratory congestion, coughs, ear infections
**Dose (tea)**: 1-2 tsp dried leaf per cup, 2-3x daily
**Note**: Mullein flower oil is a traditional ear infection remedy — 2-3 drops in affected ear

### Plantain (Plantago major — broadleaf; P. lanceolata — narrowleaf)

**Parts used**: Leaves
**Key actions**: Wound healing, anti-inflammatory, astringent, drawing
**Preparations**: Poultice, salve, tincture, tea
**Uses**: Insect bites, stings, wounds, diarrhea, urinary tract support
**Application**: Fresh chewed leaf applied to bee stings/insect bites provides immediate relief

### Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica)

**Parts used**: Aerial parts (young leaves, dried), roots
**Key actions**: Anti-inflammatory, antihistamine, nutritive, diuretic
**Preparations**: Tea (dried), tincture, cooked green, soup
**Uses**: Allergies (hay fever), arthritis, iron deficiency, prostate health, spring tonic
**Dose (tea)**: 1-2 cups daily of dried leaf infusion
**Safety**: Wear gloves when harvesting fresh; cooking or drying eliminates sting

### Holy Basil / Tulsi (Ocimum sanctum)

**Parts used**: Leaves and flowering tops
**Key actions**: Adaptogen, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, calmative
**Preparations**: Tea, tincture
**Uses**: Stress adaptation, immune support, respiratory health, cognitive function
**Dose (tea)**: 1-2 tsp dried herb per cup, 2-3x daily

## Drying and Storing Herbs

### Harvest Timing

- Leaves: Harvest before flowering, mid-morning after dew dries
- Flowers: Harvest at peak bloom, before seed set
- Roots: Harvest in fall after plant dies back (perennials) or early spring before growth (biennials)
- Bark: Harvest in spring when sap flows

### Drying Methods

- **Air drying**: Hang bundles in warm, dark, well-ventilated space. 1-2 weeks.
- **Dehydrator**: 95-115°F for leaves/flowers, 125°F for roots. 4-12 hours.
- **Screen drying**: Spread thin on screens in single layer.

### Storage

- Store in airtight glass jars, away from light and heat
- Label with herb name, date, and part used
- Most dried herbs retain potency for 1 year (flowers, leaves) to 2 years (roots, bark)
- Test potency by crushing — should still have strong aroma

## Building a Home Apothecary

Start with these 10 versatile herbs: calendula, chamomile, echinacea, peppermint, lemon balm, garlic, elderberry, yarrow, plantain, and mullein. Add valerian, nettle, and holy basil as you expand.

Equipment needed: glass jars, tincture bottles with droppers, fine mesh strainer, kitchen scale, measuring spoons, labels.

## Safety Principles

1. Start with low doses and increase gradually
2. Research contraindications for each herb before use
3. Avoid during pregnancy unless specifically indicated as safe
4. Watch for allergic reactions, especially with Asteraceae family herbs
5. Keep a log of what you take, doses, and effects
6. Store all preparations out of reach of children
7. Label everything clearly with contents and date
8. When in doubt, consult an herbalist or healthcare provider
