## Stinkhorn Fungi

## Overview

Stinkhorn fungi are a group of bizarre, foul-smelling mushrooms belonging
to the order Phallales within the Basidiomycota. The most notorious species
is **[[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]]
emerges from an underground egg as a phallic structure topped with a
green-black, stinking mass of spores. Approximately thirty species of
truly phallic-looking mushrooms have been described by mycologists, along
with related [[adaptive-anemotropism-spore-dispersal-optimization-mushroom-evolution]]. Stinkhorns have fascinated and repelled
humans since antiquity, featuring in Pliny the Elder's *Natural History*
(first century A.D.) and John Gerard's seventeenth-century herbal.

## Life Cycle

The stinkhorn life cycle begins with an **egg stage** — a white, spherical
structure partially buried in soil or [[fungal-fruiting-body-mechanics-osmotic-pressure]], not muscular action like a
mammalian penis) and ruptures the outer skin, carrying the spore mass on
its tip into the air. The expansion is driven by pressurized water within
the hyphal cells — mechanically comparable to the mammalian penis in that
both erections are maintained by pressurized fluid rather than solid tissue,
but fundamentally different in that stinkhorn pressure is osmotic in origin.

Most of the volume of the erect fruiting body is air. The receptacle is
built from masses of corrugated hyphae stretched into a weft of filaments,
creating a lightweight but strong honeycomb structure supported by internal
water pressure.

### Spore Dispersal

The gleba (spore mass) is embedded in slime containing a cocktail of
volatile chemicals:
- **Hydrogen sulfide** (rotten egg smell)
- **Formaldehyde**
- **Methylmercaptan**
- **Unique compounds called phallic acids**

This chemical blend impersonates the smell of rotting flesh, making the
stinkhorn irresistible to flies, which swarm on the head, and to slugs,
which have been observed gliding 20 or more feet toward the scent. Within
a few hours, insects consume and clean the head down to the dimpled white
surface of the receptacle tissue. Flies and slugs carry and defecate the
spores elsewhere, dispersing the fungus to new locations.

After the spore mass is consumed, the shaft wilts. The entire fruiting
body may last only a day or two above ground.

## Diversity

### Dog Stinkhorn (*Mutinus caninus*)
Smaller than *[[bloomfield-stinkhorns-phallus-impudicus-osmotic-fruiting]]*, with a pink shaft and lacking the
bulbous head. Still produces a strong odor and attracts flies.

### Bamboo Fungi and Lacy Veils (*Dictyophora* species)
Recognized by a lacy veil (indusium) hanging as a skirt beneath the
spore-bearing head. The crinoline structure may function as a ladder,
allowing wingless insects to reach the spores by crawling from surrounding
plants. The eggs of some *Dictyophora* species are sold as delicacies in
China and marketed as aphrodisiacs.
