tags: [ayahuasca, [[ayahuasca-visions]]*. These ranks represent ascending levels
of spiritual power, esoteric knowledge, and genuine metaphysical capacity.
They are not merely honorific titles but denote real abilities cultivated
through years of rigorous dieting, fasting, and communion with plant
teacher spirits.

## Attaining the Ranks

One can only become a good vegetalista by keeping a diet or fasting for
years. Through this sustained discipline the practitioner gradually
acquires the science of the muraya, the sumiruna, and the banco. Each
rank builds upon the last, requiring deeper mastery of plant medicines,
greater spiritual purification, and more demanding visionary work. The
initiate who reaches these heights is described in Amaringo's paintings
as being imbued with sacred power — seated upon a golden throne while
queens dress him in royal clothes, bracelets of gold, and the emperor's
crown, symbolizing the sovereign spiritual authority conferred by the
medicine.

## The Muraya

The muraya is a master of the aquatic and subterranean realms. Amaringo
places the muraya "behind" the sumiruna in his visionary hierarchy of
the three powers, describing him as one who "dominates through perfume
and great spiritual power." The muraya's specialty is descent — the
ability to travel down into underwater cities, to enter the depths of
rivers and lagoons, and to commune with the beings who dwell there.

The muraya commands the subtle dimension of scent and fragrance as a
vehicle of healing and influence. This perfumed dominion connects to the
broader Amazonian understanding that smell is the most primal and
spiritually potent of the senses, capable of bridging the material and
immaterial worlds. Murayas are counted among the crew of the great
esoteric steamship Aceropunta, where they serve alongside bancos as
practitioners of "high alchemy."

## The Sumiruna

The sumiruna combines the abilities of both the muraya and the banco,
able to move freely between the subaquatic depths and the celestial
heights. Amaringo devotes an entire vision (Vision 21) to what he calls
"The Sublimity of the Sumiruna," revealing the extraordinary scope of
this rank's powers.

A sumiruna can enter water effortlessly, descending through openings
into subaquatic worlds. From there, with the help of ancash sylphs, the
sumiruna is transported upward into space inside a glass tube — the
lupuna colorada. He stands on a ball of high-pressure gas, ready for
levitation. His regalia is drawn from the animal kingdom: skins of the
boa, trousers of ray-fish, feet of yangunturo (giant armadillo), and a
hat that is a macaw called yura-guacamayo. White mermaids cause his
capsule to levitate, and flying saucers accompany his astral travel.

This vision illustrates the sumiruna as a being who has transcended the
ordinary boundaries between elements — equally at home in water, on
land, and in the sky — and who moves through cosmic space with the
assistance of spirit allies.

## The Banco

The banco represents the pinnacle of the three ranks, possessing
"sublime powers" as Amaringo describes in Vision 14. The banco is
depicted with a flying wheel and a diamond sword — symbols of mastery
over cosmic movement and cutting spiritual discernment. In Vision 5,
the banco is the one who holds the pipe, suggesting the role of the
senior ceremonialist and keeper of the most ancient ritual knowledge.

The banco specializes in space and celestial travel. Like the muraya,
the banco serves aboard the Aceropunta, the great esoteric steamship
visible only under a very strong mareacion (the visionary state induced
by ayahuasca). There, bancos are described as "specialized in high
alchemy," working alongside doctors, fairies, mermaids, sibyls, and
fortune tellers in a floating hospital of extraordinary spiritual
technology.

## The Three Powers

In Vision 14, "The Three Powers," Amaringo arranges the three ranks in
a hierarchical sequence that reveals their relationships. A sumiruna
appears "on top," entering water effortlessly. Behind him sits a muraya
who dominates through perfume and great spiritual power. Behind the
muraya stands a banco with sublime powers. This arrangement positions
the banco as the most elevated rank — the one who stands behind and
above the others, holding the accumulated authority of the tradition.

The vision contrasts the three genuine ranks with lesser practitioners:
a witch doctor with flames from his head, a sorcerer, a witch sitting
in the patiquina plant. The true healer among them sits in the toe
plant with celestial fire burning straight upwards, suggesting that the
higher ranks are distinguished not by flamboyant displays but by the
quality and orientation of their spiritual fire.

## Apprenticeship and Transmission

The path to these ranks begins with apprenticeship. In Vision 5, two
apprentices are shown learning the medicine under the guidance of the
three master shamans. The apprentice undergoes a profound ritual of
empowerment — being seated upon a golden throne and dressed in royal
garments — symbolizing the transmission of sacred knowledge from one
generation of vegetalistas to the next. This initiatory framework
underscores that the three ranks are earned through lineage, discipline,
and direct experiential knowledge of the plant spirits.
