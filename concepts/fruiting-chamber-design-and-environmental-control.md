**Temperature drop** of 5 to 10 degrees Celsius below colonization temperature

ambient light or a 12 hour on/off cycle with fluorescent or LED lighting at
5000 to 6500 Kelvin is sufficient. Direct sunlight is harmful.

## Simple Chamber Designs

**The [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-a-monotub-and-a-shotgun-fruiting-chamber]] chamber (SGFC)** is a transparent plastic tote with
quarter-inch holes drilled on all six sides in a two-inch grid pattern, filled
with two to four inches of damp perlite. The holes provide passive FAE while
the perlite evaporative surface maintains high humidity. The SGFC works
reasonably well for small-scale oyster and [[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]] grows but
provides inconsistent results with species that demand precise environmental
control.

**The monotub** is a larger plastic tote modified with holes near the substrate
surface (intake) and near the top (exhaust), often stuffed with polyfill for
passive air filtering. Monotubs are popular for bulk substrate grows because
the large internal volume buffers environmental fluctuations. The substrate
itself generates humidity, reducing the need for external humidification.

**The Martha tent** uses a commercial greenhouse shelving unit enclosed in
humidified plastic. A cool mist humidifier connected to a timer or hygrostat
provides humidity and FAE simultaneously. Martha setups offer the best balance
of capacity and environmental control for intermediate-scale grows.

## Automated Environmental Control

Commercial growers and serious hobbyists use automated systems that maintain
setpoints for humidity, temperature, and CO2. Key components include:

- **Ultrasonic or centrifugal humidifiers** that produce fine mist without
  raising temperature. These are preferred over evaporative pad humidifiers
  for precision work.

- **Exhaust fans** on timers or CO2-triggered controllers that cycle stale air
  out and draw fresh air in. CO2 controllers (using infrared sensors) are the
  gold standard but are expensive; timer-based exchange at 2 to 4 air changes
  per hour works for most species.

- **Intake HEPA filters** to prevent contaminant spores from entering with
  fresh air. This is especially important in environments with high ambient
  mold spore counts.

- **Temperature control** via space heaters, mini-splits, or simply selecting
  appropriate growing spaces (basements stay cool year-round).

## Species-Specific Fruiting Requirements

**Oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.)** demand aggressive FAE and will develop
long, fuzzy stems and small caps in stagnant air. They are the most sensitive
species to inadequate ventilation and the most rewarding when conditions are
correct.

**Shiitake ([[staycare-lentinula-edodes-in-bioremediation]])** requires a cold shock or temperature fluctuation
to initiate pinning. After pinning, it prefers 80 to 85 percent humidity rather
than the 90 percent plus preferred by oysters. Excess humidity on shiitake caps
causes bacterial rot and brown blotch.

**Lion's mane (Hericium erinaceus)** is more forgiving of environmental
fluctuations than most species. It fruits well at standard room temperature with
moderate humidity. However, it requires good FAE to develop proper tooth
formation; low air exchange produces round, cauliflower-like masses.

**Reishi (Ganoderma lingzhi)** is unique in preferring high CO2 environments.
Low CO2 levels cause it to form antler-like growths rather than the classic
conk form. Growers exploit this to produce different morphological forms for
different markets.
