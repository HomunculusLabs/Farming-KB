## Cannabis Hexanoate and Cannabinoid Biosynthesis

The relationship between hexanoate (hexanoic acid) and cannabinoid biosynthesis represents a fascinating biochemical link between plant nutrition, microbial activity, and the production of THC, CBD, and other cannabinoids. Research into this pathway suggests that the short-chain fatty acid hexanoate, which can be delivered to cannabis plants through microbial fermentation products, may serve as a rate-limiting precursor for cannabinoid production in glandular trichomes.

## Cannabinoid Biosynthesis Overview

Cannabinoids are terpenophenolic compounds, meaning they are composed of both terpene and phenol group components. Their biosynthesis in cannabis plants begins with two precursor molecules: geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP), a universal terpene precursor, and olivetolic acid (OA), a polyketide-derived compound unique to cannabis.

GPP provides the terpene portion of the cannabinoid molecule, while olivetolic acid provides the phenolic portion. The enzyme cannabigerolic acid synthase (CBGAS) joins these two precursors to form cannabigerolic acid (CBGA), the central precursor from which all other cannabinoids are derived. CBGA is then converted by specific enzymes to THCA, CBDA, or CBCA, which are the acidic forms of the major cannabinoids found in fresh plant material.

## The Role of Hexanoyl-CoA

The biosynthesis of olivetolic acid depends critically on hexanoyl-CoA, a coenzyme A thioester of hexanoic acid. Hexanoyl-CoA serves as the primer for a polyketide synthase (PKS) enzyme called olivetolic acid cyclase (OAC), which catalyzes the formation of olivetolic acid through a series of condensation and cyclization reactions.

Hexanoyl-CoA is derived from hexanoate (hexanoic acid, also known as caproic acid) through the action of an acyl-activating enzyme (AAE). Research published on CsAAE1 in Cannabis sativa demonstrated that this enzyme activates hexanoate and other short- to medium-chain fatty acids by converting them to their CoA thioester forms. CsAAE1 shows high transcript abundance specifically in glandular trichomes, the sites of cannabinoid biosynthesis, suggesting it is the primary hexanoyl-CoA synthetase supplying the cannabinoid pathway.

## Hexanoate in Nature

Hexanoic acid (C6H12O2) is a short-chain fatty acid found naturally in various animal fats and oils. It comprises 1 to 2 percent of the total fatty acids in ruminant milk triacylglycerols, where it is esterified primarily at position 3 of the triacylglycerol molecules. It is also found as a minor component in certain seed oils rich in medium-chain saturated fatty acids, including coconut oil and palm kernel oil.

Ruminant milk fat has a unique composition among terrestrial mammals due to its great diversity of component fatty acids. This diversity arises from the effects of ruminal biohydrogenation on dietary unsaturated fatty acids and the range of fatty acids synthesized de novo in the mammary gland.

## The LAB Serum Connection

The hypothesis connecting LAB serum to enhanced cannabinoid production proposes that homemade lactic acid bacteria serum contains some quantity of hexanoate as a byproduct of microbial esterification during the fermentation of ruminant milk fats. When this serum is applied to cannabis plants, the hexanoate may be taken up by the plant and converted to hexanoyl-CoA, thereby increasing the pool of olivetolic acid precursor available for cannabinoid biosynthesis.

This proposed mechanism creates a direct biochemical pathway from a simple fermentation product to increased cannabinoid production: LAB fermentation of milk produces hexanoate, hexanoate is taken up by roots or leaves, hexanoyl-CoA synthetase converts hexanoate to hexanoyl-CoA, the PKS enzyme uses hexanoyl-CoA to produce olivetolic acid, and olivetolic acid combines with GPP to form CBGA, the precursor to all cannabinoids.

## Hexanoic Acid as a Plant Defense Primer

Beyond its direct role as a cannabinoid precursor, hexanoic acid has been identified as a potent natural priming agent for plant defense responses. Research on hexanoic acid as a priming agent has demonstrated that it can activate broad-spectrum defenses by inducing callose deposition and the salicylic acid and jasmonic acid signaling pathways. It primes pathogen-specific responses according to the pathogen's lifestyle and also primes redox-related genes to produce an antioxidant protective effect.

## Olivetolic Acid and UV-B Exposure

The production of olivetolic acid in cannabis is increased when plants are subjected to UV-B spectrum light. This is the mechanism behind claims that UV-B lighting technology increases trichome production. UV-B exposure upregulates the expression of genes in the polyketide pathway, leading to increased olivetolic acid synthesis and, consequently, increased cannabinoid production.

This UV-B effect operates at the same metabolic node as the hexanoate pathway. Both UV-B exposure and hexanoate availability influence the rate of olivetolic acid production, suggesting that combining UV-B supplementation with hexanoate delivery could have additive or synergistic effects on cannabinoid yield.

## Practical Implications for Cultivation

The hexanoate-cannabinoid biosynthesis connection suggests several cultivation strategies. Applying LAB serum during flowering may deliver hexanoate directly to developing flowers. Foliar sprays of dilute hexanoic acid solutions could bypass root uptake limitations. Selecting growing media amended with compost containing ruminant-derived fatty acids could provide a slow-release source of hexanoate through continued microbial activity.

However, the concentration of hexanoate in LAB serum and its actual uptake by cannabis plants under practical growing conditions remain to be quantified. The hypothesis, while biochemically plausible, requires controlled experimentation to confirm its significance in real-world cultivation.
