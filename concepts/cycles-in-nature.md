## Cycles in Nature

**Cycles** are recurring events or phenomena that represent an interruption in the straight-line progression towards entropy. It is the special provenance of life to cycle materials — so efficiently that in a tropical forest, almost all material nutrients exist in cycle within living forms. This cycling opens so many yield opportunities that thousands of species have evolved to exploit them.

## Cycles as Time Niches

If niches are opportunities in **space**, cycles are opportunities in **time** — a time-slot where both together give harbour to many events and species. Every cyclic event increases the opportunity for yield. To increase cycling is to increase yield.

Geese eat grass, digest it, moult, produce waste products containing parasites, digestive enzymes, acids and alkalis, and defecate. The ground receives this rejecta, the sun shines, and rain may fall. Fungi, bacteria, grass roots and foliage work on feathers and faeces, re-metabolising them into life. If we reorganise and encourage such cycles, our opportunities to obtain yields multiply.

Every peasant farmer who keeps pigeons — as they still do around the Mediterranean — knows this truth. Each pigeon loft is, in effect, a self-built phosphate factory.

## Every Cycle Is Unique

Each cycle is a unique event: diet, choice, selection, season, weather, digestion, decomposition, and regeneration differ every time. It is the **number** of such cycles, great and small, that determines the potential for diversity.

If, as physical scientists assure us, we all contain a few molecules of Einstein, and if the atomic particles of our physical body reach to the outermost bounds of the universe, then we are all de facto components of all things. We are of the diversity we experience in every real sense.

## Human Connection to Cycling

People are built up molecule by molecule, cycling through themselves the materials of their environment: its air, soils, foods, minerals, and pathogens. Over time, people create their own local ecology — their wastes, exudae, and rejecta eventually create the very soils in which they garden.

Tribal peoples are deeply aware of, and tied to, their soil and landscapes, so that their mental and physical health depend on these ties being maintained. The rest of us have suffered forcible, historic dislocations from home sites, and many no longer know where home is.

Travel itself causes stress and morbidity. Travellers both carry and acquire pathogens and spread them to other cultures. New settlers bring new species, new timetables, and new concepts. Local systems must readjust or fail.

## Nutrient Cycling Across Climates

Nutrient cycling is continuous in the tropics, but is interrupted wherever drought, cold, or low nutrient status reduces the "base opportunity" — just as killing fish stocks reduces the yield. Such cycles are slowed or stopped by climatic factors or by human interference.

Cycles in nature are diversion routes away from entropic ends. Life itself cycles nutrients, giving opportunities for yield and thus opportunities for species to occupy time niches.

## Cycle Schedules

Cycles, like comets, have schedules or times to occur:

- **Frequent and obvious**: day and night, tides
- **Seasonal**: migration patterns, flowering times, breeding seasons
- **Long-term**: sunspot cycles, climatic oscillations
