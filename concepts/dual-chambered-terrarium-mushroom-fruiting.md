# Dual Chambered Terrarium for Mushroom Fruiting

## Concept and Design

The dual chambered terrarium is the fruiting chamber design developed by
Psylocybe Fanaticus as the second stage of the PF TEK cultivation method. Built
from a standard 10-gallon aquarium, it uses a transparent plexiglass partition
to create two distinct chambers: a primary (spray) chamber and a secondary
(fruiting) chamber where the colonized substrate cakes are placed.

The design addresses a fundamental requirement of mushroom fruiting: the
surrounding air must be highly humidified, ideally approaching 100% humidity
where maximum water molecules float among air atoms. The fungus needs to bathe
in what PF described as a "shroud of floating water molecules." The dual
chamber design achieves these conditions through a simple but elegant physical
arrangement that molecularizes water droplets into fine mist.

## Airtight Lid Construction

The aquarium must be sealable with no air leaks for humidity retention. The lid
is constructed from flat, unwarped 1/4-inch thick board or four wood strips
connected by screws. The inner rectangular cutout must be larger than the
aquarium top. Clear polyethylene plastic film is tacked to the underside of the
frame so it hangs by the film tension over the aquarium top. A simpler
alternative is covering the top with plastic wrap, provided a tight seal is
maintained.

## Spray Shield / Chamber Partition

The partition is made from 1/8-inch thick clear acrylic (plexiglas) window
insulation, cut to approximately 15 x 18 inches (dimensions may vary depending
on the specific aquarium). A tight fit is desirable. The partition divides the
aquarium into the primary chamber (where spraying occurs) and the secondary
chamber (where cakes fruit). The shield is slanted, serving dual purpose as both
a spray deflector and a drip shield.

## Humidification Mechanism

The fundamental principle is that mushroom mycelium receives water from two
sources: the substrate it grows on and the surrounding air. However, direct
spraying of water onto fungal tissue is harmful — water droplets are
comparatively enormous relative to the fine mycelial networks and can drown or
damage the micro-structures, potentially inhibiting growth or introducing
contamination.

The dual chamber solves this by molecularizing the spray. When water is sprayed
into the primary chamber, it strikes the plexiglas shield and is broken down
into a finer mist. This mist flows around the sides of the spray shield into
the secondary chamber where the fungus is bathed in fine humidity, safely
isolated from damaging water droplets. Condensation that forms on interior
surfaces drips down and is caught by the slanted shield, functioning as a drip
roof. More condensation is actually beneficial.

## Spraying Procedure

Before placing cakes, all interior surfaces including the shield and lid are
sprayed. After cakes are inserted and the shield and lid are positioned, the
sprayer nozzle is inserted between the lid and the aquarium top and directed
downward into the center of the shield. About 5 seconds of vigorous spraying is
followed by immediate lid replacement to seal the swirling mist inside.

Spraying 2+ times daily is recommended, with more being better. If daytime
spraying is missed, it should be compensated with multiple evening sprays. All
interior surfaces should appear foggy or dripping with water. PF noted that
mushrooms can grow with as little as one good spraying per day in a properly
set up terrarium, though optimal results require more frequent attention.

## Ventilation

Each spraying session should include ventilation. The lid is removed, the spray
shield held vertically, and the chamber fanned with a piece of cardboard before
spraying. Standing water that collects in the terrarium bottom must also be
siphoned out periodically to prevent bacterial buildup. A rubber bulb battery
filler from an auto parts store or a rubber bulb enema bottle works well for
this task.

## Temperature Management

The primary rule is to avoid directly heating the terrarium. Direct heating
works against humidification and introduces a drying influence. Heating cables,
heat pads, and blankets should not be used. Plant grow lights should be kept at
a safe distance. These fungi grow well at 60°F (15°C) and have been observed
growing at even lower temperatures, though growth slows with cooling.

Optimal growing temperature is between 65°F and the upper 80s°F (18-31°C).
Excessive heat causes spreading mycelium but no fruiting — the organism
invests energy in vegetative growth rather than reproductive structures. Normal
indirect room light or a small low-wattage fluorescent plant light provides
sufficient illumination and makes phototropic mushrooms grow upward.

## Low Humidity Symptoms

When humidity drops below optimal but not enough to halt fruiting entirely,
mushrooms may develop fuzzy white mycelium growing on top of their caps, giving
a "crown of white hair" appearance. This is not contamination and does not
affect quality. More severe humidity deficiency produces deformed, convoluted,
and withering mushrooms and primordia.

## Timeline from Inoculation to Harvest

The complete PF TEK cycle spans 4-6 weeks:
1. Spore inoculation to germination: within 1 week at 70°F (21°C)
2. Germination to complete colonization: 2-3 weeks
3. Colonization to fruiting initiation: within 2 weeks
4. Fruiting cycle: approximately 2 weeks

The fruiting cycle begins with "pinning" — tiny white pin-like structures
appearing on the colonized cake surface, followed by small round growths that
turn yellow, then "primordia" (tiny worm-like structures with reddish heads)
that develop into mature mushrooms. After the initial flush, the mycelium cake
begins to turn blue and no more mushrooms form.

## Cake Removal (Birthday)

The optimal time to remove the colonized cake from the jar is when primordia
appear while still inside. The dry vermiculite layer is scraped away with a
clean fork, an old jar lid is placed over the jar mouth, and the jar is inverted
and lightly slammed onto a cushioned surface. The cake slides onto the lid
which serves as its base. Water droplets are daubed off with tissue paper and
the cake is immediately placed into the terrarium.
