## Central and South America: The Heartland

Mexico remains the historical center of psychoactive mushroom culture.
The *Psilocybe* species of Mexico -- including *P. mexicana*,
*P. caerulescens*, and *P. aztecorum* -- have been used in sacred
ceremonies for centuries, a tradition made famous by Albert Hofmann,
R. Gordon Wasson, and Maria Sabina in the 1950s. Since then, the influx
of "hippie" tourism has damaged the cultural fabric of indigenous
mushroom worship in many areas.

In South America, [[panaeolus-cyanescens-bali-omelette]]* are
the most commonly used species. Colombia's archaeological park at
San Augustin near Bogota has been dubbed the "heart of magic mushroom
land," and golden mushroom-shaped pendants found in the region suggest
centuries of traditional use. Communes were established in the Andes
Mountains, the most famous known as La Miel ("honey"). The species also
occur throughout Amazonia where Colombia, Peru, and Brazil share borders.
