## EMERGY Analysis vs. Other Methods

Studies using various embodied energy methodologies show positive net energy results for solar cells. However, EMERGY analysis suggests solar cells may be a net energy loss. The differences arise mainly from evaluation of human labour and services:

- Most methods rate human labour by metabolic food energy use or national fuel share

- EMERGY methodology measures human service input by national EMERGY use share

- In the United States, human services are rated 200 times more valuable by EMERGY than by metabolic energy and about double the simple national fuel share

- Manufacturing solar cells is very high-tech and requires highly skilled input, while the quantities of electricity generated to "pay" for that input are relatively small

The EMERGY methodology is based on universal energy laws and uses an energy symbol language to describe natural systems. Within the field of energy accounting methodologies, it is the most wholistic but complex to understand.

A more fundamental argument rests on the Maximum Power Law (Principle 3: [[holmgren-trees-nature-solar-power-plants]] as the superior renewable energy pathway. Forest trees are the ultimate development of biological solar energy capture and storage. Modern technology for wood gasifiers and micro-gas turbines appears to be a much more economical pathway for electricity production than solar cells.

## EMERGY Yield Ratios of Biomass

EMERGY studies of biomass fuels reveal a consistent pattern: annual crops have yield ratios little better than 1, while wood plantations yield 1.5 to 4, and 300-year-old rainforest yields 12. The more the work is left to nature, the greater the net yield but the longer the time required. This fundamental pattern strongly supports long-term investment in tree-based energy systems over technological alternatives.

Perhaps it is not surprising that the more the work is left to nature, the greater the net yield but the longer the time required. It is unfortunate that people down the ages, from Neolithic grain farmers to forestry economists, have failed to recognise this basic pattern as they look for higher yield from shorter rotations of crops.

## Limited Appropriate Uses

Holmgren does not reject solar cells entirely. They may be appropriate:

- Where small demand systems are remote from the grid

- In sunny dry climates where low rainfall limits wood and biomass production

- In sunny urban environments where solar cells can double as roofing material

These limited appropriate uses reflect the permaculture principle of matching technologies to their context rather than assuming universal solutions.

## Greatest Value: Cultural Shift

The greatest value from solar cells may be their role in forcing a reassessment of how precious electricity is. The inevitable conclusion is that we should use it only for high-quality functions such as small electric motors, lighting, and communications. People living with autonomous solar power systems are pioneers in a new culture of modest electricity use.
