# JADAM Co-existing with Wild Grass

## Overview

JADAM fundamentally rethinks the relationship between crops and wild grass. Rather than treating weeds as enemies to be eradicated, JADAM views wild grass as a valuable resource: a free source of fertilizer, a contributor to soil microbial diversity, and a partner in sustainable agriculture.

## The Problem with Weed Eradication

Conventional farming treats weeds as competitors that must be completely eliminated. This leads to:
- Expensive herbicide purchases
- Soil degradation from chemical exposure
- Loss of soil microbial diversity
- Removal of potential [[jadam-liquid-fertilizer-nutritional-perspective]] (JLF) is the primary additional fertilizer in the JADAM system.

The nitrogen content of various JLF materials shows the range:
- Wild grass: 0.01% nitrogen
- Purslane: 0.2%
- Tomato residue: 0.27%
- Spinach residue: 0.29%
- Food waste: 2.93%
- Human feces: 2.48%
- Urine: 3.92%
- Raw anchovy: 5.08%
- Fish meal: 7.44%

Wild grass may be lower in nitrogen than other inputs, but it provides the complete [[query-how-do-i-design-habitat-for-beneficial-insects]] microorganisms
- Eventually decomposes and feeds soil

## The Complementary Relationship

Soil that is rich and biologically active naturally suppresses problematic weeds while supporting beneficial plants. The more you manage soil through JMS, seawater, and phyllite solution, the more the weed profile shifts toward cooperative species.

## The Cost Equation

Wild grass is free. The only cost to convert it into fertilizer is water and a container (essentially free). Compare this to:
