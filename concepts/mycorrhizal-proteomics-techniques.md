## Sample Preparation

Proteomic analysis requires carefully characterized biological samples.

For soil-grown plants, roots are carefully removed, rinsed to eliminate soil particles, checked for mycorrhizal infection, and either frozen in liquid nitrogen or directly processed for protein extraction.

Monoxenic mycorrhizal cultures of transformed roots offer advantages including axenic conditions and the ability to separate root and fungal tissues.

Protein extraction protocols must be optimized to extract the full range of proteins from both plant and fungal compartments.

## Integration with Other Omics Technologies

Proteomics is increasingly integrated with transcriptomics (gene expression analysis), metabolomics (metabolite profiling), and bioinformatics to provide a systems-level understanding of mycorrhizal function.

This multi-omics approach allows researchers to correlate changes in gene expression with changes in protein abundance and metabolite levels, providing a more complete picture of the molecular events underlying symbiotic interactions.

## Emerging Gel-Free Proteomic Approaches

Recent advances in liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) and isobaric labeling techniques (such as iTRAQ and TMT) are enabling gel-free quantitative proteomic studies of mycorrhizal systems.

These approaches overcome some limitations of 2-DE, including poor representation of membrane proteins, extreme pH range proteins, and low-abundance proteins, offering a more comprehensive view of the mycorrhizal proteome.
