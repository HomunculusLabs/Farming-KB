## Housing

- Cold climate: three-sided shed with soft dry floor
- Farrowing pen needed for breeding (sow uses 3 months/year; two sows can take turns)
- Lactating sow: 7 pounds (3.2 kg) grain per day at birth, increasing to 12 pounds (5.4 kg) when piglets are a few weeks old
- Each sow needs at least 2.5 acres (1 hectare) for forage for herself and piglets
- Automatic waterer that pigs can activate to spray themselves keeps them happy
- Pigs do not sweat -- constant water supply is essential

## Breeds

### Heritage Breeds (Recommended)
British Saddleback, Large Black, Tamworth -- both good mothering instincts and foraging ability.

### Commercial White Breeds
Chester White, Yorkshire, Landrance -- good mothering instincts and large litters, but not known for foraging ability. Avoid Chester White for boars (more aggressive).

## Breeding

- Sows are generally gentle if handled often; boars are not
- Boars can be aggressive and injure easily -- consider not keeping your own
- Sow goes into heat when not nursing; bring together then
- Gestation: 4 months
- Farrowing pen: well-ventilated shed with door big enough for sow to turn around in
- Two dividers on each side so piglets can walk through (prevents sow from lying on them)
- Creep feeder needed when piglets are about 1 month old for weaning
- Piglets need to be kept warm: about 86F (30C); they huddle together
- Wean at about 1 month old using creep feeder accessible only to piglets

## Fattening and Butchering

- Market weight: around 250 pounds (113 kg), yielding 140 pounds (63.5 kg) of meat
- Takes about 6 months depending on grain supplementation
- Feed grain for a couple of weeks at end for bacon; if you just want healthy pork, forage alone is fine
- If close to winter and pigs have not put on weight, supplement with grain
- Butchering is very labor-intensive and requires extra sanitary procedures
- Recommended to delegate to a professional unless running a commercial facility

## Pig Systems Integration

Pig-cattle rotation:
1. Pigs clear the pasture (20 pigs/acre)
2. Remove pigs, replant area
3. Allow cattle in to graze
4. After cattle, replant and return pigs

Blackberry clearing strategy:
- Fence off quarter acre or more of blackberries
- First year: 20 pigs per acre
- Next year: 12 goats per acre
- Following year: pigs again
- Blackberries will not return if you keep animals or plant trees/hay

## Permaculture Functions

- Natural plowing: rooting action prepares beds before planting
- Pest control: eat fallen fruit (deters pest insects)
- Waste recycling: eat all food waste
- Fertility: manure is excellent for compost and gardens
- Land clearing: remove blackberries, scrub, and unwanted vegetation
