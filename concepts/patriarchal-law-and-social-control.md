## Overview

The analysis of patriarchal law as an instrument of social control forms a central thesis in the entheogenic interpretation of religious history. Drawing connections between Sumerian commandments, the Babylonian Code of Hammurabi, Egyptian moral codes, and the Biblical Ten Commandments, this framework argues that many religious laws were not divinely inspired universal truths but deliberately constructed mechanisms for maintaining hierarchical power structures over enslaved or subjugated populations.

## The Code of Hammurabi Connection

The Babylonian king Hammurabi"s legal code contains an earlier version of the Biblical Ten Commandments. While some laws appear based upon the philosophy of the Golden Rule, many others are described as "completely absurd" — geared toward totalitarian control rather than genuine moral guidance. The text argues that Hammurabi could not have invented these patriarchal laws independently; they must have been adopted from an older source — the [[enki]]) and framing them as evil. The control of spiritual authority is the foundation upon which all other forms of control rest — if one controls the population"s understanding of the divine, one controls their entire moral framework.

## The Golden Rule as Sufficient Law

The text suggests that the Golden Rule — treating others as one wishes to be treated — answers the genuine need for social order more than adequately. All laws that go beyond this principle, particularly those regulating private behavior (sexuality, desire, worship), are characterized as instruments of control rather than genuine moral guidance.

This position aligns with a broader argument that human societies functioned well — even flourished — with far fewer restrictive laws. Indigenous societies are cited as examples of "highly cultured societies in which sexual suppression is non-existent and whose members enjoy completely free sexual lives," directly contradicting Freud"s thesis that cultural achievement requires sexual repression.

## Secret Societies and the Engineering of Consent

The text describes a sophisticated mechanism of social control employed by secret societies: "Ordo Ab Chao" — Order Out of Chaos. The process operates as follows:

1. **Agenda formulation**: The powerful identify desired expansions of their authority
2. **Chaos creation**: Events are orchestrated that create public fear or instability
3. **Media amplification**: The chaos is publicized to create demand for solutions
4. **Solution delivery**: Laws are passed that could never have been accepted without the preceding chaos
5. **Order established**: The new legal framework grants additional power to the controlling authority

This pattern is described as recurring throughout history and accelerating in modern times. The increasing prevalence of "morality" in political discourse is interpreted as evidence of this acceleration — as more and more laws are passed under the guise of moral necessity, individual freedom is progressively eliminated.

The text draws a direct line from ancient Mesopotamian governance to modern fascist tendencies:

- The **Mesopotamian** system: Humans as literal slaves of the "Man Gods" who kept [[freemasonry]] and Biblical Engineering

The mechanism operates through:

- Carefully edited scripture that reinforces obedience to authority
- Symbolism that appears meaningful but conceals the true nature of the sacramental substances
- A moral framework that positions questioning as sinful and obedience as virtuous
- The gradual expansion of moral legislation under the pretext of social improvement

## The Suppression of Dialogue

A telling observation is made about the cultural prohibition against discussing religion and politics: "This is because dialogue between people is the real enemy of the state." Uncensored conversation allows people of different levels and fields of understanding to learn from one another, potentially arriving at conclusions that threaten established authority. The suppression of open dialogue about these subjects is therefore identified as a deliberate strategy for maintaining control.

## The Pattern Across Civilizations

The text identifies a recurring pattern that spans millennia of human civilization:

- **Knowledge acquisition**: Ruling powers acquire or develop knowledge of entheogenic substances, spiritual practices, and expanded consciousness
- **Knowledge hoarding**: This knowledge is restricted to an elite class, with severe penalties for unauthorized access or distribution
- **Public destruction**: Public remnants of the knowledge are systematically destroyed — from the burning of libraries in [[wilhelm-reich]]"s books by the FDA in 20th-century New York
- **Substitute provision**: Inert symbols replace active substances, maintaining the ritual form while eliminating the transformative content
- **Moral enforcement**: Laws and religious doctrines are established to make the desire for the original knowledge itself a sin

This pattern is observed across Sumerian, Babylonian, Egyptian, Greek, Roman, and modern Western civilizations. Each iteration preserves the [[george-washington-carver-plant-work]]'s personal doctor and a signer of the Declaration of Independence:

> "Unless we put medical freedom into the Constitution, the time will come when medicine will organize into an undercover dictatorship. To restrict the art of healing to one class of men and deny equal privileges to others will constitute the Bastille of medical science. All such laws are un-American and despotic and have no place in a republic. The Constitution of this republic should make special privilege for medical freedom as well as religious freedom."

This quote connects the broader theme of knowledge suppression to the specific regulation of healing substances — including entheogenic plants. The argument extends beyond religious freedom to encompass medical and cognitive liberty: the right of individuals to determine what substances they consume for spiritual, psychological, or physical healing.

## The Question of Universal Law

The text poses a fundamental challenge to religious authority: if the laws given to humanity by nearly every religious system are not universal truths but rather "lies and detrimental to the development of humanity," then the entire moral framework upon which these religions rest is called into question. The analysis distinguishes between:

- **Sincere moral principles**: Laws genuinely designed to promote the Golden Rule and reduce harm between individuals
- **Control mechanisms**: Laws designed to enforce obedience, suppress individual freedom, and maintain hierarchical power structures

The argument is not that all moral law is oppressive, but that specific categories of law — particularly those regulating private behavior, desire, and spiritual practice — serve control functions rather than moral ones. The ability to distinguish between these categories requires exactly the kind of independent moral discernment that the controlling laws themselves prohibit.

## Carl Sagan on Knowledge Destruction

The text quotes Carl Sagan: "History is full of people who out of fear, or ignorance, or lust for power have destroyed knowledge of immeasurable value which truly belongs to us all. We must not let it happen again." This sentiment encapsulates the broader argument: that the suppression of [[mckenna-sect-of-the-phoenix-and-secret-entheogenic-knowledge]], along with the systematic destruction of indigenous spiritual traditions, represents an ongoing loss to humanity that must be reversed.
