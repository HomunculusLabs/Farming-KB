# Water-Soluble Potassium (WS-K)

Water-Soluble Potassium (WS-K) is a Korean [[fukuoka-hotel-analogy-crop-yield-model]] and quality. In KNF, WS-K is extracted
through a simple water-percolation method using dried tobacco stems,
providing a natural source of this critical nutrient without
[[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]], the size of
fruit, and the moisture content in tissues. When stomata are open,
potassium content in guard cells is high; when closed, it is low.
Guard cells produce ATP through photosynthesis and absorb potassium
using this energy. Potassium accumulation creates [[mushroom-cultivation-jar-lid-management-loose-fit-vacuum-gas-exchange]].

Additionally, potassium promotes synthesis of carbon dioxide-fixing
enzymes and activates various enzyme reaction systems. It functions to
regulate moisture absorption and enhance translocation of
photosynthates throughout the plant.

## Causes of Potassium Deficiency

Potassium deficiency can occur even when sufficient potassium exists
in the soil. Excessive lime and magnesium suppress potassium
absorption. Sandy soils with less humus are also prone to deficiency.
The absorption rate is high and plants can rapidly deplete available
supplies. Potassium is highly mobile, easily translocating from old
to young tissues, which can leave older leaves deficient.

## Symptoms of Potassium Deficiency

According to the source, potassium deficiency manifests as:

- Branch growth deterioration, stunted maturity, and poor yield.
- Symptoms rarely appear in initial growth; they emerge once the
  plant reaches a certain size.
- Chlorosis starts from older leaves whose edges become yellowish
  brown, or appear as spots in the middle of leaves.
- Roots and stems become thin, reducing resistance to cold.
- Seeds and fruits become small and mature late.
- Leaf tips may discolor and dry out even at peak growth.

## Preparation of WS-K

The preparation of WS-K is straightforward, using tobacco stems as
the potassium source:

Materials needed:
- Tobacco stems (dried)
- Water
- Hemp or cotton cloth bag
- Clay jar or mud pot
- Porous paper and rubber band

Steps for preparation:
1. Dry tobacco stems and cut them into pieces. Do not grind too
   fine to avoid making powder, which would complicate the
   percolation process.
2. Put 1 kg of tobacco stem pieces in a hemp or cotton cloth bag.
3. Dip the bag in 5 liters of water to dissolve potassium into the
   water, creating a crude liquid of natural potassium.
4. Allow seven days for the potassium to fully dissolve into the
   water.
