# Mescaline Compound Profile

## Chemical Identity

**Mescaline**: 3,4,5-Trimethoxyphenethylamine. The prototypical natural phenethylamine psychedelic, found in several cactus species including peyote (Lophophora williamsii), San Pedro (Echinopsis pachanoi), and the Peruvian torch (Echinopsis peruviana).

Molecular formula: C11H17NO3

## Dosage

- **Threshold:** 100 mg
- **Light:** 100-200 mg
- **Common:** 200-400 mg
- **Strong:** 400-600 mg

Mescaline requires significantly higher doses than most synthetic phenethylamines, with a full psychedelic dose typically being 300-500 mg.

## Duration

Effective duration: 10-12 hours.

## Qualitative Effects

Mescaline is described in PIHKAL and throughout the literature as producing a classic psychedelic experience:

- Rich, colorful visual effects with flowing patterns and enhanced color perception
- Deep emotional and philosophical content
- A warm, organic quality often described as "earthy" or "natural"
- Enhanced appreciation of nature, music, and art
- Contemplative, introspective mental state
- Moderate stimulant effects

Shulgin's qualitative notes describe the mescaline experience as foundational — it is the reference point against which all other phenethylamines are compared. Its natural origin and long history of ceremonial use give it a unique cultural context among psychedelic compounds.

## History and Ethnobotany

Mescaline has been used for at least 5,700 years in the ceremonial practices of indigenous peoples of Mexico and the American Southwest. It was first isolated in 1897 by Arthur Heffter and first synthesized in the laboratory in 1919 by Ernst Späth. It was the first psychedelic compound to have its chemical structure determined and the first to be recognized as a phenethylamine.

## Role in PIHKAL

Mescaline serves as the conceptual starting point for Shulgin's entire research program. It is the parent compound of the 3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine series, and Shulgin's modifications of the mescaline structure led directly to many of the compounds documented in PIHKAL. The key insight was that altering the substitution pattern (shifting from 3,4,5 to 2,4,5 or 2,5) could dramatically increase potency while producing novel subjective effects.

## Comparison to Synthetic Phenethylamines

- **Potency**: Mescaline is much less potent than synthetic analogues; [[2c-b-compound-profile]] is active at 15-20 mg vs. 300-500 mg for mescaline
- **Duration**: Mescaline's 10-12 hour duration is comparable to many synthetic phenethylamines
- **Character**: Mescaline is often described as having a gentler, more "natural" quality compared to the more intense synthetic compounds
- **Body load**: Mescaline is noted for significant nausea during onset, a characteristic not shared by most synthetic phenethylamines

## SAR Significance

The 3,4,5-trimethoxy pattern of mescaline was the starting point for systematic SAR exploration. Shulgin demonstrated that:

- Shifting methoxy groups from 3,4,5 to 2,4,5 positions dramatically increases potency
- The 4-position is the most sensitive to modification, with halogens, alkyl groups, and thioalkyl groups all producing active compounds
- Alpha-methylation converts mescaline to TMA (trimethoxyamphetamine), increasing both potency and stimulant character
