# Mushroom Spawn Vitality Testing and Quality Assessment

## Overview

The ability to assess the quality and vitality of [[mushroom-spawn]] is essential for every grower, whether purchasing from commercial suppliers or producing their own. Dead or moribund spawn is the single most common cause of crop failure, making the evaluation of spawn quality a critical skill. This page covers the visual indicators of spawn quality, the effects of storage on viability, and the economic considerations of spawn purchasing.

## Visual Assessment of Brick Spawn Quality

### Indicators of Good Spawn

When [[brick-spawn]] is properly inoculated and has undergone adequate growth, it will show a characteristic **mold-like growth throughout the brick**. This uniform, dense, white [[mycelial-colonization]] indicates that the spawn is vigorous and has thoroughly permeated the nutrient medium. Key quality indicators include:

- **Uniform penetration**: The mycelium should be visible throughout the entire brick, not just on the surface or at inoculation points
- **Mold-like texture**: The desirable growth pattern is a fine, mold-like mycelium rather than coarse, thread-like strands
- **Absence of premature fruiting**: Evidences that the bricks have begun to produce small mushrooms are unfavorable—spawn of this character will frequently produce mushrooms directly without spreading throughout the beds

### Indicators of Poor Quality

Several visual signs indicate inferior or declining spawn:

- **Abundance of coarse thread-like elements**: While even good spawn may have some coarser threads, an abundance of these heavier strands alone is usually indicative of undesirable quality
- **Dark or discolored appearance**: May indicate contamination or decay
- **Dry, crumbly texture**: Excessive drying has desiccated the mycelium
- **Premature mushroom production**: Small mushrooms forming on the brick surface suggest the spawn has begun to exhaust itself

## Storage and Growth of Brick Spawn

### Stack Size Considerations

The size of stacks made during the spawn growth phase depends on circumstances. When thorough fermentation of the manure has not been given, or when relatively little loam is used, an after-fermentation may result. Under these conditions, the bricks should **never be put in large stacks**, as the heat generated by continued fermentation could damage the mycelium.

### Temperature and Moisture During Growth

Bricks are usually stored for mycelial growth at a temperature about the same as that most favorable for mushroom growing (approximately 54–56°F). During this period:

1. **Examine occasionally** and carefully control moisture
2. **Keep the surface slightly moist** to encourage growth throughout the brick—spraying the brick or the walls of the storage cellar with water may be required
3. **Avoid extremely moist conditions** which promote contamination
4. **For the last stages of growth**: Place bricks on edge and space them to allow free circulation of air
5. **Shield stacks** with good clean straw or cheesecloth when necessary

### Final Drying

Once well penetrated, bricks should be **dried gradually but considerably**, to the extent that no more growth may occur. This is preferably accomplished under cover. The spawn should subsequently be stored where it is both cool and dry.

### Storage Conditions

- **Cool, moist cellar**: Would encourage production of little mushrooms upon the bricks—undesirable
- **Very warm situation**: More injurious to the vitality of the product
- **Ideal**: Cool and dry, protected from temperature extremes and moisture fluctuation

## Spawn Vitality and Age

### Maximum Storage Period

Extensive testing of spawn of various ages and stored under various conditions yielded clear conclusions:

- **One year**: Under ordinary circumstances, mushroom spawn is unquestionably so badly injured after being stored for one year as to be **unfit for commercial purposes**
- **Two years**: In no instance have any mushrooms been obtained in experimental beds by the use of spawn two years old
- **Six to eight months**: It is preferable for seedsmen and growers to use spawn not more than six or eight months old

While there are persons who claim to have successfully carried spawn over from one season to another, the conditions must have been unusually favorable. In only one single instance has a satisfactory yield been obtained from spawn over one year old, and the author has never had the opportunity of testing commercial spawn kept at a uniformly low temperature (50–60°F) over extended periods.

### Brick vs. Flake Spawn Vitality

Under the same conditions, the **vitality of brick spawn is greater than that of [[flake-spawn]]**. This is attributed to the compact, low-moisture form of the brick, which better protects the mycelium during storage. This fact explains the early general use of English brick spawn rather than French flake spawn in America—the imported French product proved unsatisfactory time and again due to its more rapid loss of vitality during importation.

### Material Experiments

Experiments aimed at reducing the weight of the brick by employing a more spongy material in its preparation gave **unsatisfactory results from the vitality standpoint**. The denser, more compact brick formulation provided better long-term viability.

## Spawn Pricing

### Standard Pricing (Early 1900s)

The price of mushroom spawn varied considerably depending upon quality. Standard spawn was available at commodity rates, while spawn of higher quality—where all bricks had been inoculated directly from pure culture—commanded premium prices. English brick spawn could be purchased from importers at a few cents less per brick than American spawn, but had been subjected to longer shipment and storage.

### Purchasing Strategy

It is a mistake for the grower to purchase spawn in a hurry and be forced to take whatever the market affords. Best practices include:

1. **Place orders well in advance**: If spawn is needed in quantity, order from the manufacturer several months before needed to secure the best quality
2. **Avoid reliance on advertisements alone**: So much false advertising had been practiced that no inexperienced grower should depend upon advertisements alone to determine where to purchase spawn
3. **Seek reliable sources**: Appeal to the director of the experiment station of the state, or to the U.S. Department of Agriculture for information and to be put in touch with reliable dealers or manufacturers

The consistent advice: above everything else, the spawn maker should realize that **vigorous spawn, free from insects and disease**, is required. Any practical method of manure fumigation that can be employed in [[spawn-production]] would be a step in advance.
