# Harvest Techniques

Proper harvesting technique preserves the potency, flavor, and quality of cannabis buds. Knowing exactly when to harvest and how to handle plants during the harvest process is critical to producing top-quality medicine.

## When to Harvest

The best gauge of peak ripeness is the color of the resin glands or trichomes. Trichomes change color as they ripen. At first, glands are clear. As they mature, they turn a translucent milky color, and finally they turn amber. Senescing glands start to turn brown and get smaller; they are decomposing, and THC content diminishes. Check buds every day starting the sixth week of flowering. Check several buds from different plants to ensure maximum trichomes are ripe.

## Trichome Inspection

A trichome is a "plant hair." The glandular trichomes found on drug cannabis are comprised of a stalk with a resin head. They look like a post with a knob at the top. They form on buds and small leaves. The highest concentration of THC is located at the base of the resin head. The best time to harvest is when trichomes have developed a spherical head and are still transparent. Use a magnifying glass or jeweler's loupe (20-100x) to inspect trichomes closely.

## Harvest Timing by Trichome Color

Harvest when half (50 percent) of the trichomes have turned a creamy translucent color for absolute peak ripeness. At this point, the high will be both cerebral and physical with a good head and body stone. Harvest when the majority of trichomes have turned amber for a body stone. Pure indica and afghani-dominant strains harvested at this late point will possess a heavy, couch-lock stone. Pure sativa and sativa-dominant varieties are best harvested when resin glands are clear to milky colored.

## Flowering Duration by Strain Type

Pure indica varieties and many indica/sativa crosses are picked six to ten weeks after inducing flowering. Indica crosses with more dominant sativas may not be ready for ten weeks. Pure sativa varieties, especially Thai and Asian strains, take longer and could require four months to finish blooming. These types tend to form buds at an even rate throughout flowering with no marked decline in growth rate.

Buds at the top of the plant often reach peak potency a few days to a couple of weeks before lower buds. Long-blooming equatorial sativas may require several harvests. Commercial growers often pick immature six-week-old buds so they can harvest one more crop every year, but this sacrifices quality.

## Pre-Harvest Flush

Stop fertilization seven to ten days prior to harvest. Latent nutrient accumulation in foliage imparts a fertilizer-like taste. Leach nutrients from the growing medium by flushing with plain water. Some growers continue to fertilize until three days before harvest if using a product such as Final Flush, which expedites removal of built-up chemicals. If sprays have been applied during the last week, mist plants heavily to wash off residues.

## 24-Hour Darkness

Many growers give plants 24 hours of total darkness before harvest. Growers report that buds are a little more resinous afterward. This technique may cause the plant to produce a final burst of resin as a stress response.

## Harvest Time of Day

Harvest in the morning when THC content is at its peak. During the night, THC-degrading enzymes break down some of the active compounds. Morning harvest captures the maximum potency. Harvest entire plants or one branch at a time by cutting near the base with clean pruners.

## Harvesting Methods

Remove large leaves one or two days before cutting plants down. This gets them out of the way and makes manicuring easier. Harvest entire plants by cutting them off at the base before manicuring. Alternatively, cut each branch into 6-24 inch lengths. Manicure freshly harvested tops by trimming away leaves with clippers or scissors. Hang manicured branches until dry. Once dry, cut tops from branches, handling tender buds as gently as possible.

Leaving larger leaves on branches acts as a protective sheath for flower buds. Tender resin glands are protected from bruises and rupture until final manicuring, but trimming dry foliage is much slower and more tedious. It is not necessary to hang plants upside down so resin drains into foliage; once formed, resin does not move. However, drying the entire plant upside down is very convenient.

## Pistil Color as an Indicator

Pistils turn from white to brown or brownish-red as flower tops ripen. Pistil color change indicates plants are turning ripe; however, it is not the best indicator of peak ripeness. After more hands-on research, it is difficult to tell peak ripeness by pistil color in all strains. Trichome inspection remains the most reliable method.

## Manicuring

Once harvested, carefully manicure buds by cutting large leaves where they attach to the stem. Leaving the petiole (leaf stem) can cause an uneven, lumpy bud that does not cure properly. Trim small leaves close to the bud surface but leave a layer of frosty trim leaves to protect trichomes during handling and drying. Use sharp, clean scissors or shears. Work over a clean surface to collect trim for hash making.

## Handling Buds

Handle harvested buds as gently as possible. Trichomes are very delicate and can be ruptured easily. Note the torn head on one trichome and the one in the foreground has lost its head completely. Minimize touching buds directly. Use tools rather than hands whenever possible. Place manicured buds on a clean surface and avoid stacking or compressing them. Excessive handling degrades quality by damaging trichomes.

## Post-Harvest Plant Rejuvenation

After harvest, plants can be rejuvenated for future use. Leave several undeveloped lower branches with foliage on the harvested plant. Give her an 18/6 day/night photoperiod and increased nitrogen fertilizer. The female will stop flowering and revert back to vegetative growth in four to six weeks. This technique is useful for preserving exceptional genetics without maintaining a separate mother plant.

## Manicuring and Trimming

After cutting branches, individual buds must be manicured by removing excess fan leaves and sugar leaves that surround the flowers. A sharp pair of scissors or curved trimming shears works best. Wet trimming (trimming immediately after harvest while leaves are still turgid) is faster and easier because leaves are firm and easier to cut. Dry trimming (waiting until buds have dried before trimming) preserves the appearance of trichomes but is more time-consuming as leaves become brittle. Trimmed leaf material can be saved for making hash, edibles, or extracts rather than discarded.
