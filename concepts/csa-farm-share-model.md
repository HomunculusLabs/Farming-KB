# CSA Farm Share Model

[[community-supported-agriculture-csa]] (CSA) connects farmers directly with consumers through prepaid seasonal subscriptions. Members receive a weekly or biweekly box of fresh produce (and sometimes meat, eggs, herbs, flowers) throughout [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]]. The model provides farmers with upfront capital, predictable demand, and a committed customer base.

## How CSAs Work

1. Farmer plans the season's production based on expected membership
2. Members sign up and pay before the season begins (typically November-March)
3. Farmer grows, harvests, and packs boxes weekly
4. Members pick up boxes at designated locations (farm, delivery sites, farmers market)
5. Season typically runs 18-26 weeks (May-October in most regions)
6. Some farms offer winter shares (storage crops, greenhouse greens) extending to 40+ weeks

## CSA Structure Options

### Traditional CSA
- Fixed box contents each week (what the farm harvests)
- Members share in abundance and scarcity
- One size fits all (or 2-3 size options)
- Most common and simplest model
- Risk: members may not like unfamiliar vegetables

### Market-Style CSA
- Members choose items from a selection each week
- Setup like a mini farmers market at pickup
- Members use credits or points (e.g., $30/week credit)
- Higher member satisfaction, more labor at pickup

### Custom CSA
- Online ordering system; members select preferences
- Farmer packs based on selections (within harvest availability)
- Most flexible, most complex logistics
- Software options: Harvie, Small Farm Central, Barn2Door

### Add-On Shares
- Egg share: 1 dozen/week (from [[pastured-egg-production]])
- Meat share: monthly (beef, pork, chicken)
- Herb share: fresh or dried ([[homestead-herb-business]])
- Mushroom share: weekly or biweekly ([[homestead-mushroom-business]])
- Flower share: weekly bouquets
- Bread share: weekly loaf (from [[wild-yeast-bread-baking]])
- Value-added: jam, hot sauce, honey, soap

## Box Contents Planning

### Planning Principles
- Plan for 6-10 items per box (varies by size)
- Balance staples (lettuce, carrots, onions, potatoes) with specialties
- [[coleman-succession-planting]] ensures continuous harvest
- Include a newsletter with storage tips, recipes, and farm updates
- "U-pick" options for herbs, flowers, cherry tomatoes reduce packing labor

### Sample Weekly Box (Full Share, Mid-Summer)
Week 1: lettuce mix, radishes, sugar snap peas, kale, scallions, herbs (basil, cilantro)
Week 2: head lettuce, carrots, zucchini, beets, green beans, herbs
Week 3: tomatoes, cucumbers, bell peppers, eggplant, potatoes, basil
Week 4: heirloom tomatoes, sweet corn, green beans, squash, onions, garlic

### Seasonal Flow
- Spring (weeks 1-6): lettuce, spinach, radishes, turnips, peas, herbs, asparagus
- Early summer (weeks 7-12): lettuce, carrots, beets, broccoli, zucchini, herbs, strawberries
- Mid-summer (weeks 13-18): tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, corn, beans, cucumbers, basil
- Late summer (weeks 19-24): tomatoes, peppers, potatoes, onions, melons, winter squash
- Fall (weeks 25-30): winter squash, root crops, Brussels sprouts, kale, cabbage, pumpkins

## Pricing

### Share Sizes and Pricing (Typical Ranges)
- Small share (1-2 people): $300-500 for 20-week season ($15-25/week)
- Full share (3-5 people): $500-800 for 20-week season ($25-40/week)
- Premium share (includes add-ons): $700-1,200 for 20-week season ($35-60/week)

### Pricing Strategy
- Calculate total cost of production per share
- Price at 10-20% below equivalent farmers market retail (the CSA discount)
- Members accept risk of crop failure in exchange for lower price
- Offer early-bird discounts (pay by January = 10% off)
- Offer payment plans (3-4 installments)
- Work-trade shares: reduce cost by 4-8 hours/month of farm labor

### Financial Example (50-member CSA)
- 50 full shares x $600 average = $30,000 gross revenue
- 20-week season
- Cost of production: $8,000-12,000
- Net income: $18,000-22,000 (before personal labor)
- Additional revenue from add-on shares: $5,000-15,000

## Delivery and Pickup

### Pickup Models
- On-farm pickup: simplest, builds community, members visit the farm
- Farmers market pickup: convenient for members already shopping there
- Neighborhood host sites: member volunteers host pickup at their home/driveway
- Workplace pickup: deliver to office buildings (growing model in urban areas)

### Delivery
- Home delivery adds significant logistics and cost
- Charge $3-5/week delivery fee
- Delivery routes: optimize with mapping software
- Insulated boxes with ice packs for perishables in hot weather
- Minimum 10-15 members per delivery route for efficiency

## Member Management

### Communication
- Weekly newsletter: what's in the box, storage tips, recipes, farm news
- Email is standard; some farms use Instagram or text groups
- Share the story: members who feel connected renew
- Transparency about challenges (pests, weather) builds loyalty

### Member Retention
- Industry average retention rate: 50-60% annually
- Top farms achieve 80%+ retention
- Keys to retention: consistent quality, communication, flexibility, community
- End-of-season survey for feedback
- Early renewal incentive (discount or bonus item)
- New member orientation: explain how CSA works, what to expect

### Handling Missed Pickups
- Clear policy in member agreement
- Options: donate to food bank, credit for another week, no refunds for missed weeks
- Designate a pickup window (e.g., 3-8pm Wednesday)
- Leftover boxes: staff/volunteer food banks, compost if no other option

## Legal Considerations

- Member agreement: outlines share terms, pickup policies, risk acknowledgment
- Not a contract for specific produce (CSA is a shared-risk model)
- Liability insurance: see [[homestead-insurance-guide]]
- Food safety: follow GAP (Good [[mycorrhizal-agricultural-practices]]) guidelines
- No warranties implied -- members accept crop variability
- Check state regulations on direct farm sales

## Scaling

- Start with 15-25 members and grow 20-30% per year
- Each 10 additional members adds ~2 hours/week of harvest and packing
- Hire help at 50+ members (see [[homestead-labor-and-volunteers]] volunteers)
- Software helps manage memberships: Harvie, Barn2Door, CSAware
- Winter shares extend revenue and maintain member relationships year-round
