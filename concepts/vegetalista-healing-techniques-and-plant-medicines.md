# Vegetalista Healing Techniques and Plant Medicines

## Overview

The *vegetalista* is the Amazonian plant healer — a practitioner whose medical knowledge derives entirely from plants and the spirit beings associated with them. Through ayahuasca visions, dietas (plant dieting regimens), and direct communication with plant spirits (*genios* or *madres*), the vegetalista acquires a comprehensive pharmacopoeia and a set of therapeutic techniques that address illnesses spanning the physical, psychological, and spiritual dimensions. Pablo Amaringo's visionary paintings document dozens of specific plants, their medicinal applications, and the spirit entities that govern their use.

## Core Healing Techniques

### Icaros (Sacred Songs)

The *icaro* is the primary therapeutic instrument of the vegetalista. These are sacred songs received during ayahuasca visions or through extended dietas with specific plants. Each icaro has a defined function:

- **Diagnostic icaros**: Enable the healer to perceive the nature and location of illness
- **Extracting icaros**: Direct the removal of pathogenic agents (darts, phlegm, sorcery) from the patient's body
- **Protective icaros** (*arkanas*): Create defensive energy fields around the healer and patient
- **Plant-specific icaros**: Invoke the healing properties of particular medicinal plants
- **Animal icaros**: Channel the powers of spirit animals for healing purposes
- **Funerary icaros** (*sepultura tonduri*): Used by sorcerers to kill — illustrating that icaros themselves are morally neutral technologies

The icaro operates through vibrational resonance: when sung, blown upon (with tobacco smoke), or whistled, it directs specific energies toward the patient or against the disease agent.

### Sucking (*Chupada*)

Sucking is the most direct physical healing technique. The vegetalista places his mouth on or near the affected area and uses his *mariri* (magical phlegm) to extract the disease. The healer's beneficial phlegm neutralizes and draws out the harmful substance introduced by sorcery or environmental exposure. This technique is used for cungatuya, virote extraction, mal aire, and many other conditions.

### Blowing (*Soplada*)

Blowing tobacco smoke or perfumes onto the patient, accompanied by icaros, serves multiple functions: it transfers healing energy, implants [[shamanic-fights-and-protective-arkanas]], and neutralizes harmful substances. The combination of breath, smoke, and song represents the convergence of the healer's physical vitality, spiritual power, and botanical knowledge.

### Pulsation Diagnosis

Queen Pulsarium Coya is the spirit entity who grants vegetalistas the ability to diagnose illness through pulse-reading. This "intuitive ability to interpret the pulse, connecting the hands with the brain" allows the healer to form an immediate mental image of the patient's condition upon physical contact. The pulsations are classified by the healer's rank:

- **White and pink**: Apprentice pulsations
- **Blue**: Muraya (healer, not sorcerer) pulsations
- **Red and yellow**: Banco (master of the three realms: atmospheric/sidereal, terrestrial, aquatic) pulsations
- **Black and red**: Evil bancos, witches, and sorcerers

### Incorporation

A specialized technique in which the healer enters a trance state and "gets into" the patient's body to cure from within. During incorporation, the patient may feel as if dying, but the healer works internally while maintaining consciousness. The technique involves four rays of light around the healer's head — lower ones belonging to consciousness, upper ones to subconsciousness — with small waves representing sensory vibrations flowing along the spinal cord.

### Boa Extraction Protocol (Vision 32)

The treatment for *boa chichuchishca* ("begot by a boa") — a condition caused when a menstruating woman leaves wet underwear in a canoe — involves a particularly elaborate protocol:

1. The healer cuts a jagua/huito fruit (Genipa Americana) in halves, scrapes it, and prepares it with warm water
2. He sings many icaros over the preparation, blows tobacco on it, and implants arkanas
3. He calls on the conmachaco (meandering serpent with golden disks), the rainbow, and icaros of diamond, gold, silver, and all precious stones
4. He invokes the tigers (stronger hypnotism than the boa), the maniri tree, and the cabiniri
5. He uses pucunucho and rocoto (Capsicum spp.) to stun the boa
6. The achiapu (princes with spears) arrive to destroy all microbes in the boa's phlegm to prevent vaginal hemorrhage
7. A spaceship from the galaxy of Aponia ensures the boa cannot overpower the vegetalista

## Advanced Healing Modalities

### Geotherapy (Earth Medicine)

The use of earth from caves for medicinal purposes is described as an ancient technique employed by Egyptians, Mayans, pre-Incas, Incas, and other civilizations. Earth is applied in specific preparations to cure various illnesses, reflecting a cross-cultural recognition of mineral and microbial [[raw-cannabis-thca-non-psychoactive-therapeutic-properties]].

### Hydrotherapy

Ceramic jars filled with water are used in [[shamanic-entheogenic-healing-rituals]]. The jars absorb solar rays, purifying the water within. This practice is attributed to the Tiahuanaco, Chanca, Chimú, and Inca cultures, and represents both a literal therapeutic technique (sun-purified water) and a symbolic one (the jars also represent flying objects used by spiritual beings).

### Heliotherapy

The sylphs of thunder and lightning teach healers to cure through sunlight. This includes not only direct sun exposure but also the therapeutic use of "the influence of the moon, the night dew, and other atmospheric phenomena" — a comprehensive system of environmental medicine.

### Atmospheric Medicine

Beings called *puyo-runas* (cloud men, mist men) teach healing through winds, lunar influences, night dew, and atmospheric phenomena. The *huaira-kuros* (worms of the air), though malevolent when sent by sorcerers, represent the recognition that air itself carries both healing and harmful agents.

### Underwater Realm Medicine

The *raya balsa* plant (with round, ray-fish-shaped leaves) is smoked with tobacco to teach the practitioner how to enter the underwater realm. After dreaming of underwater travel, the practitioner takes ayahuasca to develop the ability to physically travel in the aquatic dimension — a specialized form of shamanic practice for those who work with water-associated illnesses.

## The Spirit Teachers

Plants are not passive ingredients but active teachers. In the visions, the spirits or guardians inside the plants "explain to the vegetalistas how these plants are to be used." This direct transmission of knowledge — from plant spirit to human practitioner — is the epistemological foundation of vegetalismo. The healer's authority derives not from book learning or [[solomon-soil-testing-laboratory-analysis-low-input-gardening]] but from personal visionary experience validated by therapeutic results.

The plant teachers include the chicuas (guardians of ayahuasca), the bun-bun monkeys (invisible by day, used for difficult cases), and the sylphs of the pashaquilla sensitiva vine (experts in love magic). Each plant maintains a relationship with specific spirit beings who serve as intermediaries between the plant's pharmacological properties and the healer's practical application.
