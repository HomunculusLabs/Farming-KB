## Lentinus edodes (Shiitake)

One of the most commercially important cultivated mushrooms worldwide. A wood-decomposing fungus typically saprophytic on deciduous trees of the Fagaceae family (oak, chestnut, shiia, beech). Indigenous to Japan, China, and the Indo-Chinese temperate zone.

### Key Characteristics
- Mycelium: white, cottony, becoming brown with age
- Cap: convex to plane, 50-80mm diameter, brown to dark brown with cracking pattern
- Surface: dry, covered with whitish fibrils
- Gills: white to cream, adnate to slightly decurrent
- Stem: central, solid, white, scaly below the annulus
- Spore print: white
- Flesh: firm, white, pleasantly fragrant

### Strains
- Wide range of strains available from commercial suppliers
- Japanese strains (Donko, Koshin types) most widely cultivated
- Strains differ in fruiting temperature, speed, cap colour, and quality

### Substrate: Natural Log Culture
- **Species**: Oak, alder, sweetgum, ironwood, poplar, and other hardwoods
- **Log size**: 4-6 inches diameter, 3-foot lengths
- **Cutting season**: Spring or fall for maximum sap content (sap rise = more nutrients for mycelium)
- **Inoculation timing**: Can inoculate immediately after cutting or season for 1 month in shaded stacks
- **Expected production**: Logs may produce for several years (typically 3-6 years)

### Inoculation Methods
1. **Sawdust spawn**: packed into holes drilled in logs, sealed with wax
2. **Wood dowel (plug) spawn**: pre-soaked wooden dowels inserted into drilled holes, sealed with wax
3. **Natural inoculation**: logs placed in contact with existing mycelial networks

### Spawn Media
- Pre-soaked wooden dowels
- 4:1 sawdust/bran mixture
- Rye grain (for laboratory transfer)

### Spawn Run
- **Temperature**: 72-78F optimum
- **Duration**: 6-12 months in natural log culture (depends on species, log size, temperature)
- **Conditions**: Shaded, moist environment; logs stacked or leaned to allow air circulation
- **Moisture**: Logs must not dry out; periodic soaking or misting may be needed
- **Protection**: From wind, direct sun, and extreme temperatures

### Fruiting Induction
- **Temperature shock**: Lowering temperature 10-15F stimulates fruiting
- **Soaking**: Immersion of logs in cold water for 24-48 hours is the standard commercial trigger
- **Physical shock**: Some growers use striking or dropping logs
- **Light**: Shiitake is photosensitive; requires light for normal cap development

### Cropping
- **Temperature**: 50-68F depending on strain
- **Humidity**: 85-95% RH during fruiting
- **Fresh air**: important during cropping
- **Light**: diffuse natural light or fluorescent
- **Flush cycle**: typically 2-4 flushes per year from natural logs
- **Yield**: varies widely; 1-2 lbs fresh per log per year is reasonable

### Nutritional Value
- Protein: 17-20% of dry weight
- Known medicinal properties: lentinan (anti-tumor polysaccharide), immune-boosting compounds
- Vitamin D precursor (ergosterol) increases with UV light exposure during drying

### Sawdust Block Culture (Alternative)
- In Japan and increasingly elsewhere, sawdust-based synthetic blocks replace natural logs
- Substrate: hardwood sawdust + bran (4:1), 60-65% moisture
- Sterilized at 15 psi for 1-2 hours
- Faster cycle (2-3 months spawn run vs 6-12 for logs)
- Shorter production life (2-3 flushes)
- Lower capital cost but ongoing substrate and sterilization costs

## Stropharia rugosoannulata (The Wine-Cap Stropharia)

A large, robust mushroom suitable for outdoor cultivation on wood chips and straw. Increasingly popular in permaculture systems.

### Key Characteristics
- Mycelium: thick, rope-like, rhizomorphic
- Cap: 50-200mm, convex becoming broadly convex to plane; burgundy-wine colour when young, fading to tan
- Gills: attached, initially grey, becoming purplish-black with spore maturity
- Stem: thick, solid, white, with a prominent annulus (ring)
- Spore print: dark purplish-black
- Habitat: wood chips, straw, garden mulch; often found along paths

### Cultivation
- **Primary decomposer**: grows readily on fresh wood chips, straw, and mixed substrates
- **Spawn**: sawdust/bran or grain spawn
- **Method**: Outdoor beds or paths, 4-6 inches deep, wood chip/straw mixture
- **Inoculation**: Mix spawn with substrate, cover with cardboard or straw, keep moist
- **Spawn run**: 2-4 months depending on temperature
- **Fruiting**: naturally triggered by rain and temperature changes in spring and fall
- **Yield**: prolific once established; beds can produce for several years
- **Suitable species**: especially productive with alder, willow, oak, and mixed hardwood chips

### Permaculture Applications
- Excellent for garden paths (fruit while functioning as pathway surface)
- Compatible with companion planting
- Decomposes wood chip mulch into humus
- The mycelium forms beneficial associations with plant roots
- Hardy and low-maintenance once established
- Edible when young; also valued as an ornamental mushroom

### Notes
- Very robust and easy to cultivate outdoors
- Minimal infrastructure required
- Good choice for first-time outdoor cultivators
- Slugs may be a problem (same as for other outdoor mushrooms)
- Spore print is distinctive dark purplish-black (do not confuse with toxic species)
