## Safety Considerations

Hofmann's experiment raises important questions about [self-experimentation safety. The dose he chose (2 mg) was within the range he estimated to be psychoactive based on his knowledge of related compounds. His medical training and extensive experience with psychedelic substances gave him a framework for assessing and managing the experience.

Modern researchers caution that self-experimentation with ergot alkaloids carries risks beyond those of more commonly studied psychedelics. The potential for vasoconstrictive effects from contamination with non-water-soluble ergotamine-group alkaloids makes purity essential. The [broader research program at Sandoz had established extensive safety data on ergot compounds, informing Hofmann's risk assessment.

## Influence on Subsequent Research

The 1976 ergonovine experiment stimulated further research into the [psychoactive properties of ergot alkaloids that had been previously overlooked. Researchers began investigating lysergic acid amide and ergonovine as psychedelics in their own right, rather than mere precursors to LSD.

The experiment also contributed to the growing recognition that many "medicinal" plant compounds used at sub-psychoactive doses in modern medicine may have been known for their psychoactive properties in traditional contexts. This insight has implications for the broader [study of psychoactive plants in ancient cultures, where modern pharmaceutical dosing may have obscured traditional knowledge of psychoactive effects.
