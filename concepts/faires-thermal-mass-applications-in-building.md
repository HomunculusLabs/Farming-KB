## Thermal Mass Fundamentals

**Definition:** Thermal mass is a large area of heat-absorbing material, usually stone or brick, which absorbs solar energy during the day and releases that heat at night.

**What works:** Stone, brick, tile, concrete — materials that are very thick with lots of mass.

**What does NOT work:** Wood, carpet, and furniture do not function efficiently as thermal mass despite being solid materials.

**Design principle:** Once solar energy enters the living room through big double-glazed windows, it should fall upon thermal mass. Build the far wall or floor out of stone, brick, or tile. A wall built directly behind windows with small openings will radiate heat into the room behind it.

## Underground and Earth-Bermed Houses

Underground houses have been built of stone and dirt in every climate for thousands of years. While dirt is not necessarily a great insulator by itself, the sheer quantity of earth used creates thermal mass:

- **Winter:** The earth mass stores heat and slowly releases it, keeping the house warm
- **Summer:** The earth mass absorbs heat, keeping the house cool
- **Desert:** Houses set far into the soil or into rock faces maximize this effect
- **Temperate climates:** Only the back half of the house may need to be earth-sheltered

Most modern underground homes are actually earth-berm houses — the front is exposed while the back and roof are covered with an embankment. The front of the house can be shaded with vines, and the roof may support plant growth.

## Earthbag Construction

Earthbag construction is an alternative building method that explicitly creates thermal mass:

- Stacked bags full of soil create the building walls
- The mass of the earth-filled bags absorbs and stores heat
- Incredibly strong and long lasting
- Can be covered with adobe or plaster
- Bags can be recycled sacks, misprinted bags, burlap, or polypropylene
- Approximately 140 bags per 100 square feet (9 square meters) of wall
- A thousand bags can build roughly 700 square feet (65 square meters)
- Shape is flexible: spheres, cones, or conventional rectilinear forms

## Strawbale Insulation

Strawbale construction uses a different insulation mechanism but works alongside thermal mass:

- Bales must be tightly twined and held with rebar framework
- While straw itself is not the best insulator, the bales' thickness provides excellent insulation
- Strawbale walls complement thermal mass floors and interior walls

## Tire Walls as Thermal Mass

A practical, low-cost thermal mass application for garden windbreaks:

- Stack old tires filled with earth, compost, hay, or scraps
- The tires absorb heat during the day and release it at night
- Provides instant wind protection while hedges are establishing
- Ground should be prepared first with newspaper and mulch
- At the top, plant a wind-tolerant species
- Serves dual purpose: windbreak and thermal battery for adjacent garden beds

This is particularly useful for protecting gardens from strong winds before permanent hedge windbreaks have matured. The tire wall also creates a warm microclimate on its leeward side.

## Desert Rock Thermal Mass

In arid climates, rocks serve as thermal mass for orchard and garden protection:

- Rocks placed around tree roots protect from heat and physical damage
- They act as thermal mass that keeps roots warm at night
- Desert temperature swings between day and night are extreme; rock thermal mass moderates this
- Palm leaves or brush propped over saplings provide additional sun protection
- Interplant leguminous species between trees for nitrogen fixation

## Compost as Thermal Mass

Compost piles generate heat through bacterial decomposition, creating thermal mass that can be harnessed for building heating:

- A compost pile placed in the greenhouse provides both thermal mass and CO2 enrichment
- Composting toilet solids can be placed in the greenhouse for the same purpose
- For compost used as thermal mass, the pile should be at least 5 feet (1.5 meters) in diameter
- The heat output is modest but consistent over months
- Combines waste management with passive heating

## Water as Thermal Mass

Water is one of the most effective thermal mass materials due to its high specific heat capacity:

- A dam or pond keeps plants and people warmer in cold weather and cooler in heat
- Water holds and reflects heat — as temperature drops at night, heat radiates into surrounding gardens
