# Oss & Oeric: Harvesting and Crop Management The final active stage of the Oss & Oeric cultivation method involves managing the fruiting process to maximize yield across multiple flushes. The 1976 guide describes the progression from primordia formation through mature harvest, including strategies for handling aborted mushrooms, managing multiple flushes, and dealing with common fruiting problems. ## Pin Formation and Development After casing, the mycelium grows up into the soil, penetrating just beneath the surface over 2-3 weeks. The mycelium may occasionally break through to the surface, which is controlled by daily fine-mist spraying to "knock down" the surface growth. By the 14th to 20th day after casing, the mycelial network at the soil-glass interface develops visible intersecting nodes that differentiate into tiny white dots distributed along the jar perimeter. These are mushroom primordia, which gradually enlarge and take on the appearance of tiny mushrooms with squat, fat stems and dark brownish heads, reaching the "pin" stage at approximately 1-2mm in length. ## Harvest Timing Pins continue to enlarge and eventually thrust above the casing surface, both at the sides and in the center of the jar. Once young mushrooms have penetrated the surface, an additional 5-10 days is required for them to reach full maturity. Mature mushrooms are several inches tall with caps ranging from one to several centimeters in diameter. The key harvest indicator is veil rupture. As the cap enlarges, the partial veil connecting the cap margin to the stipe detaches, forming an annulus (ring) on the stem. Mushrooms should be harvested as soon as veil rupture occurs. For spore collection, specimens should be allowed to mature further until the cap flattens to an umbrella shape. The authors recommend grasping the stalks firmly when harvesting and removing any soil from the bottom of the stems. ## Aborted Mushrooms A significant portion of mushrooms may grow to approximately half size and then stop developing. These "aborts" are not diseased but have ceased growth due to genetic or environmental factors. The Oss & Oeric guide emphasizes that aborts should not be discarded — they are often as potent per gram as mature mushrooms and can be harvested alongside mature specimens.

## Managing Multiple Flushes

After the first flush is harvested, the cased jar enters a recovery
period of 7-14 days before the second flush appears. During this
time, the mycelium consolidates energy reserves and reorganizes its
network. Maintaining humidity above 90% is critical during the
interflush period. Some cultivators report improved second flush
yields by dunking the cased substrate in clean water for 4-12 hours
between flushes, which rehydrates the mycelial mat. The second flush
typically yields 50-70% of the first, with subsequent flushes
diminishing further. After three flushes, substrate nutrients are
largely depleted and [[oyster-mushroom-spent-substrate-recycling]] can be composted or used in outdoor garden
beds as a nutrient-rich amendment.

## Comparison with Modern Harvest Practices

Modern cultivators have developed additional techniques. "Twist and
pull" remains standard, while some prefer harvesting entire clusters
by cutting at the base for faster processing. Food dehydrators are
now preferred over gas ovens for precise temperature control.
