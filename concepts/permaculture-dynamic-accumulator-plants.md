# Permaculture Dynamic Accumulator Plants

Dynamic accumulators are plants with deep taproots that mine minerals from subsoil layers and concentrate them in their leaves and above-ground tissues. When these plants are cut and used as mulch or compost, they cycle nutrients from deep in the soil profile to the surface where other plants can access them. Toby Hemenway discusses nutrient accumulators as a critical guild component in Gaia's Garden.

## How Dynamic Accumulators Work

In undisturbed ecosystems, a diverse plant community naturally cycles nutrients from deep soil to the surface. Deep-rooted plants access minerals that shallow-rooted plants cannot reach. When the accumulator's leaves fall or are cut, those minerals become available at the soil surface. In a permaculture guild, accumulator plants serve this role deliberately.

Hemenway explains: "The deep taproots of these plants plunge far into the mineral soil and dredge up important nutrients: potassium, magnesium, calcium, sulfur, and others."

## Key Dynamic Accumulator Species

### Comfrey (Symphytum officinale)

The archetype of dynamic accumulators and the most valued in permaculture design:

- **Mineral accumulation**: Particularly rich in potassium, calcium, and other trace minerals. Leaves contain 2-3 times more potassium than farmyard manure.
- **Biomass production**: Extraordinarily productive; can be cut 4-5 times per summer
- **Multiple functions**: Mulch production, medicine (wound healing, bone repair), bee forage, liquid fertilizer ([[compost-tea-brewing|comfrey tea]])
- **Fortress plant**: Dense growth suppresses weeds; deep roots break compacted soil
- **Guild role**: Central mulch producer in fruit tree guilds; ring around drip line of fruit trees
- **Culinary note**: Some varieties have edible young leaves; Russian comfrey (S. x uplandicum) is most common in permaculture

### Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)

- **Mineral accumulation**: Accumulates potassium, phosphorus, and copper
- **Medicinal**: Wound healing, fever reduction, digestive tonic
- **Insectary**: Attracts beneficial insects including predatory wasps, ladybugs, and hoverflies
- **Drought tolerant**: Deep roots make it resilient in dry conditions
- **Ground cover**: Spreads slowly to form a useful living mulch

### Borage (Borago officinalis)

- **Mineral accumulation**: Accumulates potassium, calcium, and trace minerals
- **Bee forage**: One of the best bee plants; long flowering season
- **Companion plant**: Particularly beneficial with strawberries and tomatoes
- **Edible**: Flowers and young leaves are edible; cucumber-flavored
- **Self-seeding**: Readily returns year after year

### Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica)

- **Mineral accumulation**: Among the richest accumulators; high in nitrogen, iron, calcium, magnesium, and trace minerals
- **Fertilizer**: Nettle tea is a potent liquid fertilizer
- **Compost activator**: Adding nettles to compost piles accelerates decomposition
- **Medicinal**: Anti-inflammatory, iron supplement, hay fever remedy
- **Fiber**: Historically used for making cloth and rope
- **Wildlife habitat**: Supports over 40 species of insects
- **Caution**: Stinging hairs require careful handling; gloves recommended

### Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale)

- **Mineral accumulation**: Deep taproot (can reach 3+ meters) mines calcium, potassium, iron, copper, and other minerals
- **Edible**: Leaves (salads when young), flowers (wine, fritters), roots (coffee substitute, roasted)
- **Medicinal**: Liver tonic, digestive aid, diuretic
- **Bee forage**: Important early spring nectar and pollen source; long flowering season
- **Soil breaker**: Taproot opens compacted soil and creates channels for water infiltration
- **Pioneer species**: One of nature's primary soil builders

### Chicory (Cichorium intybus)

- **Mineral accumulation**: Deep taproot accumulates minerals from subsoil
- **Edible**: Leaves for salads (blanched), roots as coffee substitute
- **Stock feed**: High-quality fodder for livestock
- **Drought tolerant**: Very deep roots provide resilience
- **Bee forage**: Produces nectar and pollen through summer and autumn

### Plantain (Plantago major)

- **Mineral accumulation**: Accumulates calcium, sulfur, and trace minerals
- **Medicinal**: Wound healing, drawing agent, anti-inflammatory
- **Edible**: Young leaves in salads; seeds are psyllium-like
- **Soil indicator**: Common in compacted soils; its presence suggests soil compaction
- **Tolerant**: Grows in foot traffic and disturbed areas

## Using Dynamic Accumulators in Design

### In Guilds

Place accumulators around the drip line of fruit trees and among garden beds. Their deep roots do not compete significantly with shallow-rooted crops.

### As Mulch

Cut accumulator plants several times per growing season and leave the foliage as mulch. As it decomposes, minerals become available to surrounding plants.

### As Compost Material

Add accumulator plants to compost piles to increase mineral content of finished compost.

### As Liquid Fertilizer

Soak accumulator leaves in water for 2-4 weeks to create mineral-rich liquid fertilizer (especially effective with comfrey and nettle).

### As Green Manure

Grow accumulators as cover crops before establishing gardens or orchards to bring deep minerals to the surface.

## Important Notes

- As guilds mature and nutrient cycling becomes self-sufficient, accumulator plants naturally become less critical and may decline -- this is a sign of a healthy, functioning system
- The term "dynamic accumulator" is based on traditional use and some analytical data, though comprehensive scientific studies of mineral content for all species are limited
- Not all deep-rooted plants are good accumulators; the key is both deep rooting AND high mineral concentration in the leaves
