# Cannabis Light Spectrums

Cannabis plants perceive and respond to light across a broader spectrum than humans can see. Understanding which wavelengths drive photosynthesis, vegetative growth, and flowering is essential for maximizing yield and potency with indoor LED lighting.

## Key Light Metrics

### PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)

PAR is the range of light (400-700nm) that plants use for photosynthesis. It includes blue, green, red, and some far-red wavelengths. PAR is measured as PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density) in micromoles per square meter per second (umol/m2/s).

### PPFD Targets for Cannabis

| Growth Stage | PPFD Target (umol/m2/s) | Daily Light Integral (DLI) |
|---|---|---|
| Seedling/Clone | 100-250 | 8-12 mol/m2/d |
| Vegetative | 400-600 | 18-25 mol/m2/d |
| Early Flowering | 600-900 | 25-35 mol/m2/d |
| Peak Flowering | 800-1200 | 30-45 mol/m2/d |

Daily Light Integral (DLI) is the total amount of PAR delivered in a 24-hour period. It matters more than intensity at any single moment.

## Light Spectrum by Wavelength

### Blue Light (400-500nm)
- Drives chlorophyll production, compact growth, and phototropism
- Essential for vegetative stage — prevents stretching
- Darwin and von Sachs (1864) proved blue light is the primary color inducing phototropism in plants
- Plants "see" blue light at the shoot tip to determine light direction

### Green Light (500-600nm)
- Partially absorbed — penetrates deeper into the canopy than blue/red
- Contributes to lower leaf photosynthesis
- Often undervalued; modern LED fixtures include some green for canopy penetration

### Red Light (600-700nm)
- Primary driver of photosynthesis and flowering
- Most efficient wavelength for energy-to-biomass conversion
- Red light is measured by phytochrome to determine day length

### Far-Red Light (700-750nm)
- Critical for the phytochrome switch that controls flowering
- A few seconds of far-red light can cancel the flowering-inducing effect of red light (Borthwick et al., 1950s)
- The last light a plant sees at dusk is far-red — this signals the plant to "turn off"
- In the morning, red light "wakes up" the plant
- Far-red at end-of-day can accelerate the transition to dark period and reduce internode stretching

### UV Light (<400nm)
- UV-B (280-315nm) stimulates olivetolic acid production, a precursor in cannabinoid biosynthesis
- Can increase trichome density as a plant defense response
- Use sparingly — too much UV damages plant tissue
- Some LED fixtures include a small UV channel; supplement with dedicated UV-B bulbs if desired

## Spectrum by Growth Stage

### Vegetative Stage
- Higher proportion of blue light (30-50% of spectrum)
- Promotes compact internodes, strong stems, and lateral branching
- 18/6 light cycle (18 hours on, 6 hours off)
- Some growers use 20/4 or even 24/0 for rapid veg; 18/6 is most common

### Flowering Stage
- Shift toward red-heavy spectrum (50-70% red)
- Red + far-red combination promotes flowering hormone production
- 12/12 light cycle is standard — plants measure continuous dark period, not day length
- Important: any light leak during the dark period can delay flowering or cause hermaphroditism

### Transition Period
- Gradually shift from veg to flower spectrum over 3-5 days
- Some growers reduce blue gradually while increasing red/far-red
- Far-red at the end of each light period during transition can speed flower onset

## LED vs. Other Light Sources

### LED Advantages
- Tunable spectrum — can adjust channels per growth stage
- Lower heat output — can be placed closer to canopy
- Longer lifespan (50,000+ hours)
- Higher efficiency (more PAR per watt)
- Multi-channel fixtures allow independent control of blue, red, white, and UV/far-red

### Photon Efficiency
When comparing lights, look at umol/J (micromoles per joule). Modern quality LEDs produce 2.5-3.0 umol/J. HPS typically produces 1.5-1.9 umol/J.

## Light Height and Coverage

- Start LEDs at manufacturer-recommended height (usually 24-36 inches)
- Raise as plants grow to maintain even PPFD across canopy
- Use a light meter or PAR meter to measure actual intensity at canopy level
- Aim for no more than 30% variation in PPFD across the canopy

## Practical Notes

- Clean reflectors and lenses regularly — dust reduces output 10-20%
- Replace LEDs when output drops below 80% of original (usually after 3-5 years)
- The "inverse square law" applies: doubling the distance from light reduces intensity to 25%
- Photoperiodism in cannabis: the plant measures the length of the continuous dark period through phytochrome in its leaves, not the day length
