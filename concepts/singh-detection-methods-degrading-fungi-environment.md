---# Detection Methods for Degrading Fungi in the Environment

Fungi are important in the functioning of soil ecosystems, especially in
decomposition processes of organic matter and bioremediation of
contaminants. However, relatively little is known about the population
structure, dynamics, and diversity of fungi in soil. Direct cultivation
methods are employed for assessment but fungal growth on agar plates may
not present an accurate picture of the in situ structure and diversity
of fungal communities. Novel methods are needed to study the in situ
behavior of fungi in processes related to ecology, decomposition, and
bioremediation.

## Immunological Assays

Immunological assays have been used for the detection of fungi in culture
and the detection of decay fungi in wood since the mid-1980s. These
methods rely on the specificity of antigen-antibody interactions for
[[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]]
(MnP) in wood by [[fungal-serpula-lacrymans-dry-rot-buildings]]
  from other wood decay basidiomycete fungi.

- **Agglutination assay**: Considered the most rapid immunodiagnostic
  technique, serving as a good field test for brown-rot decay detection.

- **ELISA and modified ELISA**: Employed for quantitative detection of
  Ophiostoma sp. C28 within wood using polyclonal antibody. Strong
  ELISA reactions have been obtained for copper-treated wood infected
  by Phialophora mutabilis. ELISA has also been discussed for
  quantitative estimation of fungal biomass during solid-state
  fermentation.

- **Dipstick immunoassay**: Developed for detection of Phytophthora
  cinnamomi in soils, providing rapid field-deployable capability.

- **Chromatographic assay**: Used alongside other immunological methods
  for specific fungal identification.

## Molecular Assays

Molecular strategies for the detection of fate and diversity of fungi in
soils have been developed that can identify fungi at the species level.
These methods are based on the use of variable internally transcribed
spacer (ITS) regions of ribosomal DNA.

### Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)

PCR is used to amplify rDNA sequences to determine total fungal diversity
and taxonomy using universal primers. Specific primers can be designed to
amplify and analyze fungal DNA from environmental samples. Fungal-
specific primer pairs EF4f/EF3r and EF4f/Fung5r can amplify a wide range
of fungi from the major [[coprophilous-fungi-taxonomic-groups-distribution]].
