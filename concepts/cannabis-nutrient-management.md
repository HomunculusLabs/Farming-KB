## Cannabis Nutrient Management

## Overview

Proper nutrient management is fundamental to successful [[solomon-liquid-fertilizers-and-foliar-feeding]], the remainder is typically water.

Cannabis plants have different NPK requirements during vegetative growth
versus flowering. During vegetative growth, plants like good levels of
nitrogen and normal levels of phosphorus and potassium. Suitable ratios
include 12-12-12, 20-20-20, 12-6-6, and 18-4-5, where the first number is
equal to or higher than the others. Something like 8-20-20 is not suitable
for vegetative growth; bags with higher phosphorus are designed for
flowering plants.

During flowering, phosphorus needs increase significantly. Rather than
transplanting to new soil, most growers use liquid bloom feeds to raise P
levels during the [[solomon-fertigation-bucket-drip-water-nutrient-delivery]] without the stress of transplanting a flowering plant.

## Soil pH Management

pH is a measurement of acidity and alkalinity on a scale of 1-14, where 7
is neutral. Cannabis plants in soil prefer a pH of approximately 7. Going
above or below this range can cause significant plant problems including
[[aact-nitrogen-forms-microbial-nutrient-cycling-ingham]], and microbial activity.

## Common Soil Types

**Sand and Silts**: Sandy soil drains too quickly, wasting water and
nutrients. Silt soils are more clay-like and darker but share similar rapid
drainage problems. Neither is suitable alone; they are typically mixed with
other soil types.

**Clay**: A stiff, fine-grained earth that becomes flexible when wet.
Marijuana roots do not like clay, which is rarely used alone. It can be
amended with sand [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-cannabis-cultivation]]. Most
commercial potting soils are loam-based. Always buy bagged soil from
gardening shops rather than using outdoor soil, which may contain pests and
pathogens.
