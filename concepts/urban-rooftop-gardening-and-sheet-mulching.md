# Urban Rooftop Gardening and Sheet Mulching

The [[sheet-mulching-methods-urban-permaculture]] that can transform urban spaces into productive food systems.

## Rooftop Gardens

Rooftop vegetable systems use chicken-wire columns and other container methods to grow food on building rooftops. Key considerations:

- Weight limits of the roof structure
- Water access and drainage
- Wind exposure at height
- Sun exposure (typically excellent on rooftops)
- Soil depth requirements for different crops

### Chicken-Wire Column Method
A cylinder of chicken wire filled with layered organic material creates a vertical growing system suitable for rooftops and balconies where ground space is limited.

## Sheet Mulching (Lasagna Gardening)

Sheet mulching is a no-till method that converts existing ground (even lawns or weeds) into garden beds by layering organic materials:

### Steps
1. **Rough original surface**: Slash existing weeds, shrubs, and grasses; lay woody plants flat
2. **Apply nitrogen source**: Blood and bone, decayed leaf, or thin scatter of lawn clippings
3. **Lay barrier layer**: Carpet with cardboard, newspapers, old carpet, lino, or cloth (all organic materials)
4. **Add organic layers**: As weeds yellow and die, soil fauna begin working
5. **Hard layer**: Pine needles, lemongrass, straw, seaweed, stable sweepings, or manure (~75mm)
6. **Cosmetic layer**: Woodchips, bark, sawdust, nut husks, rice husks
7. **Plant immediately**: Tubers, large seeds, and trees/shrubs can be planted as soon as mulch is completed

### Benefits
- Suppresses weeds without herbicides
- Builds soil organic matter
- Retains moisture
- Requires no digging or tilling
- Can be done on any surface (concrete, lawn, weeds)
- Worms and soil organisms do the work of integrating layers

## Plant Stacking in Urban Context

From the forest model: interconnected levels from understorey ferns and bushes to upper canopy trees. In urban settings this translates to:
- Vertical growing (trellises, walls, columns)
- Time stacking (successive plantings through seasons)
- Layered plantings (shade-tolerant under sun-loving plants)

## Species for Urban Sites

### Very Dry Sites (Rooftops, Containers)
Almond, black locust, carob, fig, mesquite, olive, pomegranate, rosemary, prickly pear, most aromatic herbs

### Container-Friendly Food Plants
- Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant (summer)
- Leafy greens, herbs (year-round)
- Strawberries, blueberries (small fruits)
- Dwarf fruit trees (citrus, apple, fig)
- Sweet potato (trailing, good for edges)
- Quinoa (cold-hardy, drought-tolerant grain)
