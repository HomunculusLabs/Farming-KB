# Steve Solomon

Steve Solomon is an American vegetable gardening author, seed company founder, and advocate for low-cost, self-sufficient food production. He is best known as the founder of [[territorial-seed-company]] and author of *Growing Vegetables West of the Cascades* and *[[gardening-when-it-counts]]*.

## Early Life and Homesteading

Solomon began growing a large vegetable garden in the San Fernando Valley, California, in 1973. During his early years he explored various homesteading pursuits, including raising chickens, rabbits, and fattening a steer. He attempted market gardening using biodynamic French intensive methods but failed, an experience that shaped his later pragmatic approach to vegetable gardening. These early failures convinced him that conventional intensive gardening methods were often impractical for home gardeners seeking genuine food self-sufficiency.

In 1978, Solomon relocated to a five-acre homestead in Oregon, west of the Cascade Range. This move proved pivotal: the climate west of the Cascades — characterized by cool, wet winters and dry summers — required gardening techniques quite different from those suited to California or the eastern United States. Solomon's experience adapting to this specific growing region became the foundation for his most influential work.

## Territorial Seed Company

After searching for a viable cash crop on his Oregon homestead, Solomon decided to focus on garden education and seed supply. He founded Territorial Seed Company, which quickly grew into a significant regional seed business. The company specialized in vegetable varieties well-suited to the maritime Pacific Northwest climate, filling a gap left by larger seed companies that catered primarily to other regions.

By 1985, Territorial Seed had grown into what Solomon described as a "monster" — the business had become too large and demanding. His book *Growing Vegetables West of the Cascades* was in its third edition by this time. Solomon sold the company to devote himself to an independent homestead-based lifestyle, free from the demands of running a large enterprise.

## Gardening Philosophy

Solomon's gardening philosophy, most fully articulated in *Gardening When It Counts*, centers on several core principles:

### Low-Cost, High-Yield Methods

Solomon advocates for gardening techniques that minimize financial investment while maximizing food production. He emphasizes that truly self-sufficient gardening must be economically sustainable, not merely a hobby that costs more than buying food at the store. His approach includes careful variety selection, efficient use of water, and maximizing output from limited garden space.

### Water Conservation

Solomon's book *Waterwise Vegetables* reflects his deep concern with water efficiency in food production. He argues that most home gardeners dramatically over-water their crops and provides detailed guidance on reducing water use through proper timing, mulching, plant spacing, and drought-adapted variety selection. He recommends specific irrigation schedules and methods including soaker hoses, fan nozzles, and fertigation (fertilizer delivery through irrigation water).

### Soil Fertility and Human Health

A recurring theme in Solomon's work is the connection between soil fertility and human nutrition. He argues that depleted soils produce nutrient-poor food and that home gardeners who build healthy soil can grow superior produce. He advocates for complete organic fertilizers, compost, and mineral amendments rather than relying solely on NPK synthetic fertilizers. His online library, soilandhealth.org, was created to disseminate key books on these connections.

### Practical Self-Sufficiency

Unlike some gardening authors who promote intensive raised-bed systems requiring significant inputs, Solomon advocates for wider plant spacing, lower-input methods that are more resilient and less labor-intensive. He is critical of transplant-dependent gardening and encourages direct seeding when possible. He emphasizes that true food self-sufficiency requires growing calorie-dense staple crops like potatoes, beans, and grains alongside vegetables.

## Major Works

- **Growing Vegetables West of the Cascades** — A regional guide that became the standard reference for Pacific Northwest vegetable gardening, reaching its third edition.
- **Gardening When It Counts** — A comprehensive guide to growing vegetables with minimal inputs, covering tools, soil preparation, plant spacing, watering, and variety selection.
- **Waterwise Vegetables** — Focused on reducing water use in vegetable production.

## Later Life

Solomon moved to Canada in 1994 and to Australia in 1998, continuing his work as a writer, teacher, and advocate for home food production. He describes himself as still pursuing the homesteader lifestyle, writing gardening books, and teaching classes aimed at helping people become financially independent through producing their own necessities.

## soilandhealth.org

Solomon created the online library soilandhealth.org as a free resource for key books connecting soil fertility and human health. The library makes available historical and contemporary texts on organic agriculture, soil science, nutrition, and homesteading that might otherwise be difficult to access.

## Legacy

Solomon's influence on [[home-vegetable-gardening]], particularly in the Pacific Northwest, has been substantial. Territorial Seed Company (under subsequent ownership) continues to be a major regional seed supplier. His books remain in print and are widely recommended by gardeners seeking practical, low-cost approaches to food production. His emphasis on the soil-health-human-nutrition connection anticipated broader public interest in nutrient density and regenerative agriculture.
