## Piule (Rhynchosia spp.)

Piule is the name given to several species of Rhynchosia that have
beautiful red and black seeds which may have been valued as a narcotic by
ancient Mexicans. What appear to be these seeds have been pictured, together
with mushrooms, falling from the hand of the Aztec rain god Tlaloc in the
Tepantitla fresco dating to approximately A.D. 300-400. This archaeological
evidence provides one of the earliest known depictions of psychoactive seed
use in Mesoamerica. Modern Indians in southern Mexico still refer to these
seeds as piule, one of the names also applied to the hallucinogenic morning
glory seeds.

## The Tepantitla Fresco: Ancient Evidence

The Tepantitla fresco is one of the most important archaeological artifacts
documenting pre-Columbian psychoactive plant use in Mesoamerica. Located at
the archaeological site of Teotihuacan near modern Mexico City, the fresco
dates to approximately A.D. 300-400 and depicts the Aztec rain god Tlaloc
with what appear to be psychoactive seeds and mushrooms falling from his
hand. The seeds depicted in the fresco closely resemble the red and black
seeds of Rhynchosia species, suggesting that the use of these seeds as a
narcotic or hallucinogen may extend back at least 1,600 years.

The association of the seeds with the rain god Tlaloc is significant, as
Tlaloc was one of the most important deities in the Mesoamerican
pantheon. His association with rain, fertility, and agricultural abundance
made him a central figure in [[entheogenic-folk-religious-ceremonies]], and the depiction of
psychoactive seeds in his hand suggests that these plants played a role in
rituals aimed at ensuring rainfall and agricultural productivity.

## Botanical Description of Rhynchosia

The genus Rhynchosia comprises approximately 300 species belonging to the
bean family, Leguminosae (Fabaceae). These plants are distributed throughout
the tropics and subtropics of both hemispheres and include both herbaceous
and woody species. The seeds of some Rhynchosia species are visually
distinctive, with contrasting red and black coloration that makes them
easily recognizable and potentially attractive as decorative or ceremonial
objects in addition to any psychoactive use.
