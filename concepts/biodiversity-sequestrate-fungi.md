# Sequestrate Fungi

Sequestrate fungi are fleshy, leathery, rubbery, or cartilaginous
species that sequester their spore-bearing tissues, preventing
spores from discharging into the air. Usually, the spore-bearing
tissues are enclosed within a persistent peridium. Sequestrate
fungi, with the exception of "secotioid" species, are hypogeous,
without a stipe or with only a rudimentary one (true and false
truffles). The secotioid species are partly to almost entirely
exposed at maturity and may have a well-developed stipe.
