The interaction between 2.45 GHz microwave radiation and microorganisms
involves dielectric heating through coupling with dipolar water
molecules. Effects vary depending on organism type, physiological state,
and environmental moisture. Understanding these mechanisms is essential
for designing effective sterilization protocols.

## Dielectric Heating Fundamentals

Microwaves at 2.45 GHz (wavelength ~12.2 cm) coincide with a strong
absorption band of water molecules — the rotational transition energy
of the water dipole closely matches the microwave photon energy.

When water molecules are exposed to 2.45 GHz radiation:

1. The oscillating electric field causes water dipoles to rotate,
   attempting to align with the field
2. Continuous field reversal (2.45 billion times/second) creates
   molecular friction as molecules collide
3. Friction converts electromagnetic energy into thermal energy
4. Heating efficiency depends on power density and exposure duration

The dielectric loss factor is exceptionally high for water at 2.45 GHz,
which is why microwave energy couples so strongly with biological
systems — all living cells contain substantial water.

### Water Enhancement for Spore Kill
The NASA researchers discovered that adding trace water (~9 µL/cm²)
to the surface dramatically improves spore kill:

1. Added water absorbs microwave energy and flashes to steam
2. The steam penetrates the spore coats and rehydrates the spore core
3. Rehydration converts bound water to free water, making it
  available for microwave coupling
4. The combination of external steam heat and internal dielectric
  heating destroys the spore

This water-enhanced microwave treatment achieves complete kill of even
the most resistant spore formers at total exposures of 13.1 W-hr/cm².

## Interaction with Fungi (Yeasts and Molds)

Yeasts and molds are intermediate in their microwave susceptibility:

- **Yeasts**: Similar to vegetative bacteria in water content and
