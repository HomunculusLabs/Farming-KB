# Mushroom House Design and Accessibility

The mushroom house (growing room) is the controlled environment where inoculated substrate bags colonize, fruit, and produce harvestable mushrooms. Proper design is essential for consistent yields and disease management. The FAO training manual for mushroom cultivation by people with disabilities emphasizes that mushroom houses must be designed not only for optimal growing conditions but also for accessibility by farmers with physical disabilities.

## Environmental Requirements

Mushroom fruiting requires specific conditions the growing house must provide:

- **Temperature** — 22–28°C for tropical oyster species; 15–20°C for temperate species.
- **Relative humidity** — 80–95% during fruiting; 60–70% during spawn run.
- **Fresh air exchange (FAE)** — Ventilation for oxygen supply and CO₂ removal. Stale air causes elongated stems and small caps.
- **Light** — Indirect natural light or 50–200 lux artificial light. No direct sunlight needed.
- **Cleanliness** — Protection from dust, insects, and contaminant spores.

## Basic Design

### Location

- Shaded area protected from direct sun and strong wind.
- Access to clean water for misting/humidification.
- Good drainage to prevent waterlogging.
- Proximity to residence for convenience (important for farmers with mobility limitations).
- Away from compost piles and animal housing (contamination sources).

### Size

Beginner houses are typically 15–30 m², sufficient for 200–500 bags. The FAO manual recommends starting small and expanding as skills and market develop.

### Structure Types

1. **Bamboo/thatch** — Lowest cost, locally available, good insulation. Lifespan: 2–3 years.
2. **Wooden frame with netting** — Lightweight, easy to modify for accessibility. 5–7 year lifespan.
3. **Concrete block/brick** — Most durable, higher cost. 15–20+ year lifespan.
4. **Converted building** — Adapting a spare room or shed reduces startup costs.

### Roofing

- Thatch or palm leaf: excellent shade and insulation, requires replacement every 1–2 years.
- Corrugated iron with insulation layer: durable but can overheat without shade.
- Double-layer roofing with air gap significantly reduces heat gain.

### Floor

- Concrete: ideal — easy to clean, impervious, durable.
- Packed earth: cheaper but harder to sanitize, may harbor pests.
- Raised concrete platforms keep bags off the ground for better airflow.

## Ventilation Systems

- **Windows/vents** at high and low levels create convective airflow (fresh air in low, CO₂ out high).
- **Doors** closable for humidity maintenance but openable for ventilation.
- **Exhaust fans** improve air circulation in larger houses.
- **Mesh screens** on all openings prevent insect entry.

## Humidity Management

- **Manual misting** — Hand sprayer on walls/floors 2–4 times daily. Low-cost but labor-intensive.
- **Misting systems** — Timer-controlled foggers for uniform humidity.
- **Water trays** — Shallow trays on floor evaporate slowly for baseline humidity.
- **Wet curtains** — Hanging damp burlap adds moisture as it evaporates.

## Shelves and Suspended Systems

Substrate bags can be arranged on shelves or suspended from overhead supports. The choice affects space efficiency, airflow, and accessibility.

### Tiered Shelves

- Wooden or metal shelving with 3–5 tiers.
- Each shelf holds 20–40 bags depending on bag size and spacing.
- Space between tiers: 40–60 cm for airflow and access.
- Bags are placed upright or laid on their sides.

### Suspended Systems

- Bags hung from ropes, wires, or chains attached to the ceiling frame.
- Excellent airflow around all surfaces; efficient vertical space use.
- Hanging bags at comfortable working height for wheelchair users.
- Particularly recommended in the FAO manual for accessibility.

## Accessibility Adaptations

The FAO manual specifically addresses mushroom house design for farmers with disabilities. Key adaptations include:

### Mobility Impairments

- **Wide doorways** (minimum 90 cm) for wheelchair access.
- **Smooth, level floors** — No steps, uneven surfaces, or tripping hazards.
- **Ramps** — Where level changes are unavoidable, install ramps with gentle gradient (1:12 or less).
- **Appropriate shelf height** — Shelves and hanging systems at heights reachable from a wheelchair or seated position (70–120 cm from floor).
- **Adequate turning space** — Minimum 150 cm × 150 cm clear floor area for wheelchair maneuvering.

### Visual Impairments

- **High-contrast markings** — Color-coded labels and tactile markers on shelves and equipment.
- **Consistent layout** — Fixed placement of tools and materials so locations can be memorized.
- **Tactile guides** — Rope or rail guides along pathways within the mushroom house.

### Upper Body Impairments

- **Lightweight bags** — Smaller substrate bags (2–3 kg) reduce lifting requirements.
- **Low-hanging systems** — Suspended bags at comfortable reaching height minimize overhead work.
- **Adapted tools** — Long-handled scissors for harvesting; modified spray bottles.

### General Accessibility

- **Shaded rest areas** adjacent to the mushroom house.
- **Water access** at comfortable height for filling spray bottles.
- **Tool storage** within easy reach, clearly organized.
- **Work surfaces** at appropriate heights for inoculation and harvesting.

## Pest and Disease Prevention

- **Screened openings** — Fine mesh on all vents, windows, and doors to exclude flies, beetles, and mites.
- **Sealed floor-wall junctions** — No gaps for pests to enter or hide.
- **Easy-clean surfaces** — Smooth walls and floors facilitate regular disinfection.
- **Drainage** — Proper drainage prevents standing water that breeds mosquitoes and fungus gnats.
- **Foot baths** — Shallow disinfectant trays at entrance reduce contaminant introduction.
- **Separate zones** — Physical separation between inoculation and fruiting areas reduces cross-contamination.

## Maintenance

- **Cleaning** — Sweep and wash floors weekly; disinfect monthly or between crops.
- **Structural inspection** — Check roof, walls, screens for damage; repair promptly.
- **Pest monitoring** — Regular inspection; sticky traps detect flying pests early.
- **Record-keeping** — Log temperature, humidity, production data, and problems observed.

## Cost Considerations

| Material | Cost (per m²) | Lifespan |
|----------|--------------|----------|
| Bamboo/thatch | Low ($5–15) | 2–3 years |
| Wood frame + net | Medium ($15–40) | 5–7 years |
| Concrete block | High ($40–100) | 15–20+ years |

The FAO manual recommends starting with a low-cost structure and upgrading as the enterprise grows, funded by mushroom sales income.
