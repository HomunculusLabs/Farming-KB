# Terrace Systems for Slope Agriculture

Terrace systems are among the most productive and enduring earthworks in permaculture, transforming steep, erosion-prone slopes into stable, productive agricultural land. From the ancient rice paddies of the Ifugao people in the Philippines—some continuously productive for over 5,000 years—to modern garden terraces, these systems demonstrate the power of thoughtful earth-shaping for soil and [[vetiver-grass]], comfrey, bamboo, palm fronds, Aleurites spp., and Cinnamomum spp.

### Slope Stability with Grass Strips

Contour strips of Vetiver grass, lemongrass, and banna grass combined with tree legumes provide a cost-effective alternative to formal terracing. These clumping grasses trap soil particles, have few seeds (reducing weediness), provide mulch material, and effectively "terrace" even steep slopes. Vetiver and lemongrass strips can yield a 6× increase in total efficiency for semi-arid regions when combined with sorghum and tree crops.

## Growing Situations on Terraced Land

Terraces create multiple distinct microenvironments, each suited to different crops:

1. **Banks and bunds** (terrace rims): tree crops, vine crops
2. **Slope faces and walls**: chayote, cucurbits, beans, passionfruit, kiwifruit
3. **Trellis structures off bunds**: climbing vegetables and fruits
4. **Ditches and drains**: water-tolerant species
5. **Steep unterraced slopes**: forest crops, mulch-producing trees
6. **Flat terrace area**: the main cropping zone for grains, vegetables, and staples

## Integration with Other Systems

### Vine Crops from Bund Faces

Bamboo on borders provides trellis material, as do rot-resistant timbers. Vine crops including chayote, cucurbits, beans, passionfruit, and kiwifruit can be trained up bund faces, increasing yield without consuming flat terrace space.

### Animal Integration

Animal sheds for ducks, pigeons, poultry, pigs, or bees can be placed over top terraces, with manure managed on a washdown system that fertilizes lower terraces. In-crop mulches such as beans, Azolla, and clovers support both soil health and animal forage.

### Fish and Aquaculture

Deep areas in terraces serve as fish, crayfish, or shellfish refuges. Stone splash-ponds are needed at water entry points, and deep border ditches can hold fish or shellfish. This polyculture approach maximizes the productive use of water within the terrace system.

## Essential Design Details

- **Splash stones or plates** at water entry points to prevent erosion from falling water
- **Adjustable drain pipes** to dry out terraces between crops or for seasonal management
- **Border drains** within terraces to keep soil dry for mid-season crop transitions
- **Bunds planted** with clover, beans, comfrey, lemongrass, and fruit crops
- **Vine coverage** over all or part of the terrace to aid shade-loving crops like taro
- **Staggered terrace sets** for steep slopes and high rainfall areas

## Fodder Systems

Terrace borders and slopes can produce significant fodder for livestock. Species include tagasaste, banna grass, comfrey, Leucaena, and crop wastes and straw. The integration of livestock with terraced systems closes nutrient loops and provides additional yield from the landscape.

## Longevity and Sustainability

The terraces of the Ifugao people demonstrate that well-designed terrace systems can remain productive for millennia. Key factors in this longevity include continuous mulch application, vegetative stabilization of all structural elements, integration of tree crops that provide both stability and fertility, and careful water management. When terraces combine tree plantings on borders and steep slopes with diverse cropping on the flat areas, they create self-renewing agricultural systems that improve rather than degrade over time.
