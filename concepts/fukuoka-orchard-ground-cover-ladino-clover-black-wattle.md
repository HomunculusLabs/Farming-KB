5. **Resilient**: It "grows back easily after being cut, and remains healthy and hardy even when trampled upon."

### Disadvantages

Ladino clover is susceptible to summer-killing and sclerotium disease in hot, dry weather. Its growth is also retarded in heavy shade, making it less suited to densely canopied orchards.

### Seeding Guidelines

- Drill seed during the first autumn after planting the orchard.
- Delayed seeding invites insect damage; do not postpone beyond the optimal window.
- Do not cover seeds with soil—broadcast them on the surface.
- Broadcasting in late autumn among dying weeds allows the clover to establish as other vegetation recedes.
- If sown in spring, cut the clover back after one year to prevent excessive competition with young trees.

### Ongoing Management

Clover does not choke out other vegetation by aggression. Instead, it "gradually becomes dominant by growing so thickly as to prevent germination of other weeds." When trampled or cut, most weeds weaken while the clover grows more vigorously.

However, if left alone after flourishing, the clover "becomes excessively luxuriant, leaving it open to attack by diseases such as leaf spot and the reemergence of weeds in five to six years." To maintain it over the years, "clover requires the same meticulous care that one gives a lawn."

The payoff is significant: "This clover cover will eliminate the need for weeding, and mowing will be incomparably easier than in an orchard overrun with weeds."

## Alfalfa for Arid Land

"Nothing surpasses ladino clover in dealing with weeds, but in warm regions where it tends to lose its vigor in the summer, and in cold, dry areas, mixed seeding with alfalfa is desirable."

### Deep Soil Improvement

Alfalfa is deep-rooted, "sends roots down to depths of six feet or more," making it ideal for improving deeper soil strata that ladino clover cannot reach.

### Hardiness

It is a hardy perennial, resistant to drought, cold, and high temperatures—far more resilient than clover under extreme conditions.

Fukuoka advocated that "wider use of this valuable legume should be made in Japan for soil improvement and as a feed and forage crop."

### Bur Clover

Bur clover serves a complementary role: it controls spring weeds, withers in summer, grows back in fall, and suppresses winter weeds. It is particularly useful in rotation before summer vegetables, providing weed suppression during its active growth phases and then retreating before warm-season crops need the space.

## Black Wattle (Acacia)

### Planting Density and Role

Fukuoka recommends planting up to roughly ten black wattle trees per quarter-acre among the fruit trees. A member of the pea family, the black wattle serves multiple functions within the orchard ecosystem.

### Five Core Benefits

1. **Rapid deep soil improvement**: Black wattle roots penetrate and aerate deep soil layers far more aggressively than any herbaceous [[cover-crop]].
2. **Shelterbelt and windbreak**: Its fast growth provides wind protection for fruit trees.
3. **Summer shade**: In warm regions, it shields soil from intense sun, protecting it from depletion and maintaining moisture.
4. **Pest prevention**: It helps suppress orchard pests, especially mites, by supporting populations of predatory insects.

### Additional Economic Values

The bark is rich in tannin and can be sold for a good price. The wood is excellent for making desks and chairs. The flowers produce nectar valued for honey production.

### Growth Rate

"No other evergreen tree of the pea family grows as quickly as the black wattle." It "grows five feet or more in a year, creating a shelterbelt in just three to four years and becoming about the size of a telephone pole in seven to eight years."

### Soil Transformation

After five to six years, the trees should be felled and their trunks and tops buried in trenches within the orchard. Each tree yields approximately half a ton of high-quality organic material.

Fukuoka observed that "five or six years after planting acacias, an area of more than 100 square yards of what had been hard, lean soil about each tree had become soft and porous."

### Planting Method

Saplings do not take well—Fukuoka recommends planting seed directly. Scatter seed throughout the orchard and allow the trees to emerge where conditions suit them.

## Black Wattle Protects Natural Predators

### Supporting Beneficial Insects

The black wattle grows constantly throughout the year, always sending new shoots. This continuous fresh growth attracts aphids and scales, which in turn sustain a population of ladybugs.

"One important role of the black wattle then is to serve as a protective tree [[botanical-pesticides-and-natural-pest-control]] remains active even when fruit trees are dormant.

"Planting five or so of these trees per quarter-acre keeps scales and mites down to a minimum."

### Replanting Old Orchards

The black wattle is also invaluable for rehabilitating old, rundown orchards. Plant many acacias among the existing fruit trees, then five to six years later fell them all and replant with three- to four-year saplings. Fukuoka considered this "far better method of replenishing the soil than running a bulldozer through the orchard."

## Ground Cover Basics

### Longevity and Succession

Ladino clover typically lasts about six to seven years before its growth begins to slow. By ten years, weeds begin to reemerge—bindweed, kudzu, and sorrels being common invaders. "Those herbs resistant to clover survive and reestablish themselves."

### Flexibility in Cover Choice

Despite his advocacy for ladino clover, Fukuoka remained pragmatic: "I have no intention of insisting on a cover of clover; a weed cover will probably do just as well." He emphasized that "what should or should not be used as a cover crop for soil improvement depends largely on local conditions. All plants emerge for a reason."

### Natural Succession

A succession of different herbs over the years enriches the soil progressively and is more natural than maintaining a single crop monoculture. This [[ecological-succession]] mirrors the way undisturbed ecosystems build soil fertility over time.

### Replacing Weeds with Vegetables

Fukuoka also discovered that by sowing vegetable seeds of the same family as the dominant weeds, the vegetables can gradually replace the weeds. This technique exploits the [[ecological-niche]] already established by the weeds, allowing cultivated crops to fill the same role without requiring soil disturbance.
