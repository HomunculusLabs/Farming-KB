# homestead poultry health

Maintaining healthy poultry on the homestead requires proactive management,
biosecurity protocols, and the ability to recognize disease early. Poultry
(including chickens, ducks, turkeys, and quail) are susceptible to a range of
bacterial, viral, fungal, and parasitic diseases. Prevention is always more
effective and economical than treatment.

## Common Diseases

### Coccidiosis
- **Cause:** Protozoan parasites (Eimeria species) that infect the intestinal
  lining.
- **Species affected:** All poultry. Most severe in chickens and turkeys.
- **Symptoms:** Bloody diarrhea, lethargy, hunched posture, ruffled feathers,
  weight loss, death. Most common in young birds (3-8 weeks).
- **Transmission:** Fecal-oral. Spread through contaminated feed, water,
  litter, and equipment. Survives for months in the environment.
- **Treatment:** Amprolium (Corid) in water — 9.6 mg/ml for 5-7 days.
  Toltrazuril (Baycox) is also effective. Sulfa drugs for secondary
  infections.
- **Prevention:** Medicated starter feed (amprolium). Dry litter. Clean
  water. Avoid overcrowding. Gradual exposure builds natural immunity.
- **Herbal preventatives:** Oregano oil, garlic, apple cider vinegar in water,
  grapefruit seed extract. Limited evidence but widely used by homesteaders.

### Marek's Disease
- **Cause:** Herpesvirus (highly contagious, oncogenic).
- **Species affected:** Chickens. Turkeys can carry but rarely show symptoms.
- **Symptoms:** Paralysis of legs/wings, weight loss, irregular pupil,
  tumors on internal organs. Mortality up to 80% in unvaccinated flocks.
- **Transmission:** Feather dander, dust, contaminated environment. Highly
  resistant — persists in the environment for months.
- **Treatment:** No cure. Supportive care only.
- **Prevention:** Vaccination at hatch (hatchery-vaccinated chicks). This is
  the single most important vaccine for backyard flocks. Once birds are
  infected, the virus persists on the property. Buy only vaccinated chicks.
- **Note:** Marek's does not affect egg safety for human consumption.

### Respiratory Diseases
- **Infectious Bronchitis (IB):** Coronavirus. Coughing, sneezing, drop in
  egg production, wrinkled eggs. Vaccination available. No treatment.
- **Newcastle Disease (ND):** Paramyxovirus. Respiratory, nervous, digestive
  symptoms. High mortality. Reportable disease. Vaccinate in endemic areas.
- **Mycoplasma gallisepticum (MG):** Chronic respiratory disease. Coughing,
  nasal discharge, sinus swelling. Treat with tylosin or tetracycline.
  Carrier birds persist.
- **Infectious Coryza:** Bacterial (Haemophilus). Facial swelling, foul
  odor, drop in egg production. Treat with antibiotics. Carrier birds.
- **Aspergillosis:** Fungal infection from moldy litter or feed. Respiratory
  distress. Prevent by keeping litter and feed dry.
- **General prevention:** Good ventilation (ammonia damages respiratory
  tracts), dry conditions, biosecurity, avoiding mixing species.

### Other Common Diseases
- **Fowl Pox:** Viral, spread by mosquitoes. Dry scabs on comb, wattles,
  unfeathered areas. Wet form (diphtheritic) affects mouth/throat. No
  treatment. Vaccine available.
- **Pullorum:** Salmonella. High mortality in chicks. White diarrhea,
  pasty vents. Reportable. No treatment — cull and depopulate.
- **Fowl Cholera:** Bacterial (Pasteurella multocida). Sudden death, green
  diarrhea, swollen joints. Antibiotics may help. Vaccinate in endemic areas.
- **Avian Influenza:** Viral. Highly pathogenic strains cause high mortality.
  Reportable disease. Prevent wild bird contact.
- **Blackhead (Histomoniasis):** Protozoan. Primarily affects turkeys, rarely
  chickens. Transmitted by cecal nematodes (often from chickens). Yellow
  diarrhea, darkened head, high mortality. Keep turkeys separate from
  chickens. Treat with nitarsone (where available) or metronidazole.

### External Parasites
- **Northern Fowl Mite:** Most common external parasite. Small dark mites
  on feathers around vent. Causes anemia, reduced egg production.
  Treatment: permethrin dust, diatomaceous earth, poultry sprays.
- **Scaly Leg Mite:** Burrows under leg scales. Raised, crusty scales.
  Treatment: petroleum jelly or vegetable oil on legs (suffocates mites).
  Repeat every 3 days for 2-3 weeks.
- **Poultry Lice:** Bite feathers and skin. Cause irritation, reduced
  production. Treatment: permethrin dust.
- **Red Roost Mite:** Nocturnal — feeds on birds at night, hides in
  crevices during day. Causes anemia, restlessness. Clean and treat
  housing with permethrin or essential oils.
- **Fleas and Ticks:** Less common but occur. Treat with appropriate
  insecticides.

### Internal Parasites
- **Roundworms (Ascaridia galli):** Most common. Intestinal worms causing
  weight loss, diarrhea, reduced production. Treat with fenbendazole
  (SafeGuard) or ivermectin.
- **Tapeworms:** Require intermediate hosts (earthworms, beetles). Segments
  visible in droppings. Treat with praziquantel.
- **Capillaria (Threadworms):** Small, hard to see. Causes diarrhea and
  weight loss. Treat with fenbendazole.
- **Gapeworms:** Worms in trachea, cause gasping and open-mouth breathing.
  Treat with fenbendazole or ivermectin.

## Biosecurity

### The Foundation of Poultry Health

Biosecurity is the set of practices that prevent the introduction and spread
of disease on your homestead.

### Entry Protocols
- **Quarantine** all new birds for 2-4 weeks in a separate area, as far
  from the existing flock as possible.
- Observe quarantined birds daily for signs of disease.
- Test for common diseases (Marek's, MG, pullorum) if possible.
- Change clothing and footwear between quarantine area and main flock.
- Do not share equipment between quarantined and established birds.

### Visitor Protocols
- Limit visitors to the poultry area.
- Provide dedicated footwear (rubber boots) for visitors.
- Do not allow visitors who have been to other poultry operations within
  48 hours.

### Daily Practices
- Clean water daily. Scrub waterers weekly.
- Remove wet or soiled litter. Add fresh bedding.
- Collect eggs daily.
- Remove dead birds immediately. Submit to diagnostic lab if cause is unknown.
- Control rodents and wild birds — they are disease vectors.
- Clean and disinfect feeders and waterers regularly.
- Keep feed stored in sealed containers to prevent contamination by rodents
  and wild birds.

### Sanitation
- Disinfect housing between flocks (all-in/all-out management).
- Remove all organic material, scrub with detergent, rinse, apply
  disinfectant (bleach, Virkon, Oxine).
- Allow housing to dry completely before restocking.
- Lime (agricultural lime) on floor between flocks helps reduce pathogen load.
