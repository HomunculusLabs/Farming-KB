# Psilocybin Distribution in Cap vs Stem

The distribution of psychoactive alkaloids between the cap (pileus) and stem
(stipe) of *[[psilocybe-cubensis]]* varies significantly depending on the strain
and the specific alkaloid being measured. Bigwood and Beug's 1982 study
provided quantitative data on this distribution across three different
strains, revealing patterns that challenge common assumptions about mushroom
potency.

## Overview of Findings

Bigwood and Beug analyzed the caps and stems separately for three strains
[[psilocybe-cubensis-distribution-and-ecology]] nsis* grown on [[psilocybe-cubensis-miniculture-on-rye-grain]] substrate. The results showed
that [[cap-vs-stem-alkaloid-distribution]] is not uniform across the [[fruiting-body-development]] and that
the pattern differs between psilocybin and psilocin, as well as between
strains.

## Strain-by-Strain Analysis

### M.R. Strain (First Flush)

| Part | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Caps | 6.7 | 0 |
| Stems | 4.2 | 0.35 |

The M.R. strain showed the classic pattern: caps contained approximately
60% more psilocybin than stems (6.7 vs 4.2 mg/g). All psilocin detected
was exclusively in the stems, with none found in the caps.

### Equadorian Strain (First Flush)

| Part | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Caps | 7.6 | 0 |
| Stems | 4.7 | 0.4 |

The Equadorian strain followed the same pattern as the M.R. strain. Caps
contained roughly 60% more psilocybin (7.6 vs 4.7 mg/g). Again, all
psilocin was found exclusively in the stems.

### Amazon Strain (First Flush)

| Part | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Caps | 5.7 | 0.1 |
| Stems | 5.7 | 0 |

The Amazon strain showed a distinctly different pattern. Psilocybin was
distributed equally between caps and stems (5.7 mg/g each). A trace of
psilocin was found in the caps (0.1 mg/g) but none in the stems — the
reverse of the pattern seen in the other two strains.

## Key Patterns

### Psilocybin Distribution
Two of three strains showed higher psilocybin concentration in caps than
stems, with caps containing roughly 1.4-1.6× the stem concentration. The
Amazon strain was an exception with equal distribution. This suggests that
the common belief that caps are significantly more potent than stems has
a biochemical basis, but it is not universal across all strains.

### Psilocin Distribution
The psilocin distribution pattern was more complex and strain-dependent:

- **M.R. and Equadorian strains**: All psilocin was in the stems, none in
  the caps. This is a counterintuitive finding, as most users assume caps
  are the most potent part for all active compounds.
- **Amazon strain**: A small amount of psilocin was found in the caps
  (0.1 mg/g) with none in the stems — the opposite pattern.

The strain-specific nature of psilocin distribution suggests that the
enzymatic pathways for psilocin biosynthesis or conversion from psilocybin
may differ between genetic lines.

### First Flush Context
All cap-vs-stem analyses were performed on first-flush mushrooms. As the
flush study showed, psilocin levels are typically minimal in the first
flush and increase in subsequent flushes. The cap-vs-stem distribution in
later flushes may differ from these first-flush results.

## Implications for Consumption and Research

### Whole Mushroom vs. Separated Parts
Since psilocybin is the predominant alkaloid (always at least 2× psilocin
levels) and caps generally have higher psilocybin concentration, consuming
whole mushrooms (caps and stems together) provides a reasonable average
potency. Selectively consuming only caps would provide somewhat higher
psilocybin content but would miss the stem-localized psilocin found in
some strains.

### Dosage Estimation
The roughly 1.4-1.6× difference in cap vs stem psilocybin concentration
means that a "dose" consisting entirely of caps would be somewhat more
potent than one consisting entirely of stems. However, given the 4× overall
variability in psilocybin levels observed across flushes, the cap-vs-stem
difference is relatively minor by comparison.

### Strain Selection
For cultivators seeking specific alkaloid profiles, the choice of strain
may influence not just overall potency but also the distribution pattern
of alkaloids between cap and stem tissues. The Amazon strain's unique
equal-distribution pattern suggests genetic factors play a significant role.

### Sample Preparation for Analysis
The finding that alkaloid distribution varies between caps and stems has
implications for analytical methodology. Researchers analyzing whole
mushrooms should note that homogenizing caps and stems together provides an
average value that may mask the differential distribution pattern. Studies
reporting only whole-mushroom values cannot capture this intra-mushroom
variation.

## Limitations

- Only three strains were examined; broader generalization requires more
  strain sampling.
- Only first-flush mushrooms were analyzed for cap-vs-stem distribution.
- The study used rye-grain substrate; results may differ with other
  substrates (compost, manure, etc.).
- Sample sizes were small; statistical power for strain comparisons is
  limited.
