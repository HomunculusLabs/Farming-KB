# Flexibility in Management

Putting holism into practice means accepting the fact that any resource management
problem has many solutions. Like so many other aspects of [[savory-holistic-goal-definition]] and the whole it encompasses, and you must be
free from rules, systems, and restraints to manage the whole effectively.

Given this degree of flexibility, people often don't know what to do with it. Like
the imprisoned bird whose cage has been opened suddenly, we remain on our perches,
confused by the new possibilities. Holistic management divides flexibility into three
broad levels for planning and management purposes.

## Tactical Flexibility

Tactical level flexibility is required in those infrequent but catastrophic years
when we are forced to change routine practices and yet do not want to abandon any of
our long-term goals. Where livestock are not involved, the greatest urgency is the
need to replan financially what is now going to be a very different picture for the
year than planned. Where livestock are involved, the biological plan gets attention
first as it will have considerable bearing on financial replanning.

### Biological Replanning in Emergencies

Even where ranchers do the necessary biological planning each year, they still usually
favor a particular way of running their livestock through most years. If the situation
changes in any major way, you must snap out of the routine. If you have monitored
progress well through the season, you should know early on when your planned moves have
become improper due to some catastrophe: drought, flood, pest outbreak, fire, or
whatever else. Do not continue with the plan-monitor-control procedure of normal years
but replan immediately with a completely new look.

### Drought Response

Droughts creep up on people, and most consider them acts of God rather than matters
for timely replanning. Each growing season, especially in [[savory-drought-planning-and-reserves]]. If that drought
persists, you face a whole growing season that may not produce enough feed. You can
do a lot about that only if you replan without delay.

Think particularly about all of the tools that you could apply to improve the
effectiveness of any rain that should fall. Fire clearly should be avoided, likewise

without any overgrazing is commonly a good booster of the [[savory-livestock-grazing-planning]] chart and the [[savory-recovery-and-grazing-periods]] need
adjustment, the operational level is where those changes are made.

A common mistake is to confuse operational flexibility with the absence of planning.
The rancher who merely reacts to daily conditions without a plan or monitoring framework
is not exercising flexibility but is operating without management. True operational
flexibility requires a plan to deviate from, monitoring to detect when deviation is
needed, and the knowledge of when to replan versus when to control.

## Flexibility and the [[savory-weak-link-analysis]]

Flexibility interacts with the weak link analysis guideline by requiring managers to
reassess which link is weakest as conditions change. In a good year, the financial
weak link may dominate. In a drought year, the biological weak link becomes paramount.
The flexibility to shift attention and resources between these different constraints
is what separates holistic managers from those following rigid systems.
