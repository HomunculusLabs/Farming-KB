# Amanita Muscaria Ambrosia Preparation

Ambrosia -- the "elixir of life" -- is a cold-water extraction of dried [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]] that produces a potent, sweet-tasting, dark amber liquid. The preparation method exploits the mushroom's natural structure as a water pump, creating an efficient extraction of the water-soluble [[muscimol-and-amanita-muscaria]] and related compounds while leaving behind less desirable constituents.

## Name and Etymology

The name "Ambrosia" connects directly to the ancient Indo-European root meaning "not-death" (immortality). Greek Ambrosia, Persian Amarta, and Sanskrit Amrita all share this etymology. The similarity between the word "ambrosia" and "amber" likely relates to the color of the liquid, which is always a shade of amber -- yellow-amber in transmitted light and reddish-amber in reflected light.

In ancient Greek mythology, ambrosia was the food or drink of the gods that conferred immortality. The author of this source argues that the mythical ambrosia was literally this mushroom extract, a claim supported by the Rig Veda's references to Soma being mixed with water to create the divine beverage.

## Preparation Method

### Ingredients

- Dried [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]] cap and stem pieces (properly dried and aged)
- Cool drinking water (reverse osmosis or distilled water yields the best flavor)

### Equipment

- Small Mason-type jar or glass container
- Strainer
- Second glass or jar for collecting the ambrosia
- Fork (for pressing)

### Procedure

1. **Weigh** the desired amount of dried Amanita muscaria pieces
2. **Prepare the jar:** Place cap pieces upside down in the bottom of a small Mason-type jar. Break stem pieces into 0.5 to 1 inch lengths and place on top of cap pieces. Shake gently to settle pieces into the smallest volume
3. **First extraction:** Add just enough cool drinking water to cover the herb. Let soak for at least 3 hours at room temperature. The water should turn a dark amber color
4. **Collect first run:** Pour off the ambrosia into a second glass or jar using a strainer. Cover and set aside
5. **Second extraction:** Add the same amount of fresh drinking water to the Amanita muscaria pieces in the first jar. Let sit for at least 2 hours. The water should become almost as colored as the first run. Strain into the second jar with the first run
6. **Optional third run:** A third extraction may be taken but will be considerably weaker than the first two
7. **Press remaining liquid:** Press the pieces with a fork to squeeze out more ambrosia. Rinse pieces and jar with a small amount of water, strain, and add to the collected liquid

### The Mushroom as Water Pump

The preparation method exploits a biological principle. The mushroom is, in effect, a pump. When rehydrated with water, it goes back to pumping water through its body, leading to a fairly efficient water extraction of the water-soluble chemicals. This is why the original pieces are left relatively large rather than powdered -- the intact mushroom structure enhances extraction efficiency.

## Properties

### Taste

The concentrated ambrosia is sweet to the taste. The flavor of well-made ambrosia is unique, described as similar to a weak honey, malt, and water solution. Just like coffee, tea, or beer making, the quality of the water used has a significant effect on the taste. Reverse osmosis or distilled water yield the best flavor.

### Potency

It takes slightly more dried Amanita muscaria per dose when making ambrosia than when consuming the dried mushroom directly. However, the resulting experience seems clearer and cleaner with the ambrosia, with only the most water-soluble chemicals and sugars being present in the extract.

### Consumption

The ambrosia is best when sipped slowly and allowed to lie on the tongue for a few seconds before swallowing. This sublingual absorption may enhance onset and reduce gastrointestinal effects.

## Seer's Milk (Milk Fermentation Variation)

The mixing of milk with the herb or expressed juice is a very ancient practice dating back at least 4,000 years. Reports from Siberia dating from the 1900s describe the fermenting of dried Amanita muscaria with reindeer milk. In the Rig Veda, the expressed juice is mixed with soured milk and curds before consumption.

### Preparation

1. Place small pieces of the herb into a bowl or jar
2. Add whole homogenized fresh milk: 3/4 to 1 cup milk per one average-sized (5-7 grams) shredded dried specimen
3. Place in a cool room (60-70 F); cover with a clean cloth or paper towel
4. Wait for fermentation -- at 60 F, this may take 12 hours or more

### Testing Readiness

- If flies are present, they will show you when it is ready, drawn to it like iron to a magnet
- A drop placed on a plate will intoxicate any flies that find it
- This property explains the ancient Biblical name Beelzebub, "Lord of the Flies"
- Alternatively, pull a drop out every hour with an eyedropper and taste

### Properties

- Properly fermented milk is much stronger than the herb or ambrosia alone
- The milk will not readily spoil and still tastes sweet after 24 hours (showing antibiotic properties)
- At about 30 hours it turns to a non-psychoactive yogurt that then spoils
- The lactic acid in soured milk probably reacts with muscimol, converting it to a more potent form

## Sacred Wine (Grape Juice Variation)

A variation of the standard ambrosia substitutes grape juice for water.

### Preparation

1. Use approximately 1 oz (28 grams) of dried Amanita muscaria per quart of grape juice
2. Soak for 8-12 hours
3. Strain off the juice and press the mushroom pieces to recover more juice
4. A few drops of vinegar added per cup of juice at the start seems to make it more potent

### Remarkable Property

This wine has the unique property of getting more potent (up to a point) from being diluted with water. For best results, dilute 2-4 cups water for every cup of wine before drinking. When properly diluted, it will slack thirst; if not diluted enough, it will dry the mouth within minutes.

This dilution property connects to the author's theory about ancient wine (see [[amanita-muscaria-ancient-wine-question]]), where ancient wine was always diluted with water in ratios of 2:1 to 20:1 -- impossible for alcoholic wine but consistent with a water-soluble psychoactive extract.

### Slightly acidic grape juice

A slightly acidic grape juice seems to work best. The author speculates the acidity aids in the extraction or conversion of the active principles.

## Honey Drops

Once the technique of producing herb flour has been mastered (see Eucharist wafer preparation), honey drops can be prepared by combining the herb flour with honey. This creates a sweet, portable sacrament that is easy to dose and share.

## Magic Food (Culinary Use)

The simplest method of consumption described in Wasson's "Soma: Divine Mushroom of Immortality" involves placing dried mushroom into soups and stews, creating what Siberian tribes called "magic food." Crushed or powdered dried Amanita can easily be added to stew, soup, or oatmeal and actually makes the food taste better. This is recommended as the best way for beginners to start using the herb.

## Holy Oil (Transdermal Use)

### Preparation

1. Powder some dried specimens and sift through a fine cloth
2. Return pieces that did not pass through to be repowdered
3. Place fine herb powder into a clean, dry, small sealable vial or bottle
4. Slowly add just enough olive oil to cover the powder
5. Using a piece of wire, gently stir the powder and olive oil together
6. Add more oil as needed to create a thin paste
7. Seal and let the herb soak in the oil for 1-2 hours

### Application

Place a fingertip on the surface of the oil and apply to the center of the forehead, about an inch above the eyebrows (the "third eye" location). Effects should start in a few minutes and last until the oil is fully absorbed.

### Advantages

- Most controllable method of using dried Amanita muscaria
- Most efficient way of using limited mushroom for a number of people
- Effects are never too intense unless too much oil is applied
- Can also be used as a topical antibiotic on infected wounds, inflamed insect bites, and other skin infections

The active principle can be absorbed through the skin. If you gently hold a fresh Amanita muscaria stem in your hand for about 5 minutes, you will start feeling a light giddiness -- the longer the contact, the more intense the effects.
