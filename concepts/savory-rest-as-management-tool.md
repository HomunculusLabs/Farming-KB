# Rest as a Management Tool

Rest as a tool in Holistic Resource Management differs fundamentally
from the short physiological rest a plant requires to recover from
severe defoliation. Here, rest means rest from major physical
disturbance and applies to the community as a whole. It is one of
the six tools in the HRM model and perhaps the most misunderstood,
as the common assumption that "resting land lets nature heal it"
proves catastrophically wrong in brittle environments.

## Total Rest Versus Partial Rest

Total rest means withholding all major physical disturbance for
considerable time. This includes disturbance from large animals,
domestic or wild, fire, and machinery. Partial rest is applied when
some livestock or large game are present but so few and with such
calm behavior, in the absence of predators, that a large proportion
of plant life and soil surface remains undisturbed.

Partial rest is a widespread policy, evident anywhere small numbers
of livestock or game animals are widely scattered and seldom agitated.
That rest in either form functions as a tool of the same order as fire
or a plow comes as a new concept. We considered rest natural until we
registered the fact that brittle and nonbrittle environments react to
it in very different ways.

## Rest in Nonbrittle Environments

In nonbrittle environments, plant material decomposes quickly through
biological decay starting close to the ground where the microenvironment
supports abundant decomposer organisms. This process suits perennial
grass plants particularly well because old leaves and stems weaken at
the base and fall aside, allowing light to reach basal growing points
and new growth to begin unimpeded.

Communities under total rest in nonbrittle environments maintain a
high degree of stability and complexity of species. Water, if it runs
off at all, carries no silt or debris. Even very prolonged rest from
fire or physical impact has little or no adverse effect on the water
cycle, mineral cycle, succession, or energy flow. Desertification is
seldom a danger.

## Rest in Brittle Environments

The effects are entirely opposite in brittle environments. Old plant
material breaks down slowly through oxidation and weathering rather
than biological decay. Being most exposed to the elements, leaf tips
break down first so that basal growth points remain shaded and
obstructed for years. Old material that lingers into the next growing
season weakens grass plants, and several years of accumulation can
kill them.

If the soil surface also remains undisturbed, new plants cannot easily
replace dying ones. Outside the tropics, prolonged rest allows freezing
and thawing to create a puffiness that inhibits establishment of young
grasses. The crust that forms on rested soil offers poor opportunities
for germinating seed.

Brittle environments subjected to extended rest characteristically have
wide bare spaces between vestigial perennial plants. The remaining
plants survive only because light reaches growing points around the
edges of each clump, while the centers may already be dead. Despite
years of seed production, no seedlings establish on the bare, undisturbed
surface.

## Successional Shift Under Rest

When closely-spaced perennial grasses that established after some
disturbance are subsequently rested, they begin to kill each other as
old growth shades even the edges of neighboring clumps. This creates
ideal conditions for woody plant invasion. Dead grass clumps provide
a good microenvironment for seed germination, and dead root systems
create excellent medium for taproot penetration.

Most of the brush and tree encroachment considered a problem today owes
its existence to heavy doses of the tool of rest, either total or
partial. Continued rest in low rainfall brittle environments eventually
destroys even the woody species once all grass has oxidized and the
bare surface becomes capped.

## Rest in Semibrittle Environments

Environments in the middle of the brittleness scale (5 or 6 on the
1-to-10 scale) show adverse symptoms more slowly but the pattern is
the same. At Nebraska's Crescent Lake Wildlife Refuge, twelve years of
rest showed weakened grasses and invasion of tap-rooted plants. After
fifty years of rest, all four ecosystem foundation blocks had visibly
suffered. Grasses were dead or dying, bare ground had expanded, and
only weed species remained to colonize openings.

## The Historical Pattern

When humans first settled in permanent villages, they unintentionally
subjected surrounding lands to rest by driving wild herds away. Modern
managers intentionally rest land hoping it will recover, but have
changed natural relationships no less than those first settlers did.
Since over half the earth's land surface is brittle to varying degrees,
and it has carried livestock under management that paradoxically
produces both partial rest and overgrazing, the remorseless growth of
deserts is no mystery.

## When to Use Rest

Whether to use rest depends entirely on goals and environment type. In
nonbrittle environments, rest is a viable tool for allowing succession
to advance toward forest. In brittle environments, rest generally
subverts most goals and ensures that deserts will keep expanding.
If the goal involves maintaining grassland in brittle environments,
rest of any form should be avoided.
