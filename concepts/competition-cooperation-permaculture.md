## Ecology of Competition and Cooperation

Holmgren synthesizes a key ecological pattern: "In nature, systems that are immature and growing rapidly, in a situation of surplus free energy, tend to be dominated by competitive relationships; mature ecosystems, in which there is little free or surplus energy, show a high degree of mutualistic and symbiotic relationships."

A rainforest clearing -- with abundant sunlight and fertility -- produces "a profusion of competing annual weeds, vines and trees." In the mature forest, adjacent plants develop "mutual or symbiotic relationships, such as trees that exchange nutrients via mycorrhizal fungi." The lesson for design is that cooperation is the hallmark of mature, energy-efficient systems.

This ecological pattern underpins a central argument of Holmgren's book: "Co-operative and symbiotic relationships will be more adaptive in a future of declining energy." Permaculture can be seen as part of a long tradition of concepts that emphasise mutualistic and symbiotic relationships over competitive and predatory ones. Declining energy availability will shift the general perception of these concepts from romantic idealism to practical necessity.

## Pioneer vs. Climax Communities

This ecological pattern parallels human social organization:
- **Pioneer communities** (rapidly growing, tapping new resources) tend toward competition -- whether colonial settlement or economic expansion
- **Stable traditional societies** (resources fully allocated) develop "defined roles, mutual obligations, gifting, taxation and other social mechanisms" that prevail over competitive ones
