# Spider Mites - Identification and Control

## Overview

The spider mite is the most common pest found on [[jorge-cervantes]].
Spider mites have eight legs and are classified as arachnids
(spider relatives) rather than insects, which have six legs.
They are found on leaf undersides sucking away life-giving plant
fluids. To an untrained naked eye they are hard to spot, but
their telltale feeding signs, including yellowish-white stippling
on leaf tops, are easy to see.

## Identification

Spider mites appear as tiny specks on leaf undersides. Their
webs are easily seen when misted with water on stems and under
leaves as infestations progress. A magnifying glass or low-power
microscope (10-30X) helps identify the yellow-white, two-spotted
brown or red mites and their translucent eggs. The most common
species indoors is the two-spotted spider mite (Tetranychus
urticae).

After a single mating, females are fertilized for life and
reproduce about 75 percent female and 25 percent male eggs.
Females lay about 100 eggs each. This reproductive capacity
makes early detection and rapid response critical.
