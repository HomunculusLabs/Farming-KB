# Permaculture Mapping and Reading the Landscape

## Overview

Reading the landscape is the first and most essential skill in permaculture design. Before any design work begins, the designer must learn to observe and interpret the patterns, processes, and history written on the land. Mapping translates these observations into a usable design base. (see [[pdc-landform-and-topography-reading]]).

## Reading the Landscape

### Indicators of Past and Present Conditions

The landscape itself tells the story of its history and current health:

- **Tree flagging and wind pruning** reveal prevailing wind directions and strength
- **Erosion patterns** indicate water flow, soil instability, and past disturbance
- **Vegetation types** indicate soil conditions, moisture levels, and microclimates
- **Soil color** reveals organic matter content, drainage, and mineral composition
- **Rock outcrops and stone placement** indicate geological history
- **Animal trails and tracks** show movement patterns and wildlife corridors

### Slope and Aspect

Slope determines water movement, erosion potential, frost drainage, and access. Key observations:
- **Convex slopes** shed water and are typically drier
- **Concave slopes** collect water and are typically moister
- **Ridge tops** are exposed to wind but have good frost drainage
- **Valley bottoms** are frost pockets but collect water and cold air
- **North-facing slopes** (in northern hemisphere) are cooler and moister
- **South-facing slopes** (in northern hemisphere) are warmer and drier

### Contour Reading

Contour lines connect points of equal elevation. Understanding contour is essential for:
- Placing swales and water harvesting earthworks on contour
- Designing access roads to follow contours (minimizing grade)
- Understanding water flow and accumulation patterns
- Planning terraces and garden beds on level ground

## Map Making

### Base Maps

A base map records the existing conditions of a site. It should include:
- Property boundaries
- Topographic contours
- Existing structures (buildings, fences, roads)
- Water features (streams, springs, wetlands, existing ponds)
- Existing vegetation (trees, shrubs, significant plantings)
- Soils information
- North arrow and scale

### Sector Analysis Maps

Sector maps overlay information about external energies and influences:
- Sun angles (summer and winter solstice paths)
- Prevailing wind directions (winter and summer)
- Fire risk sectors
- Noise, pollution, or visual intrusion sectors
- Wildlife movement corridors
- Flood zones and water flow patterns

### Overlay Mapping

Multiple transparent overlays can be used to analyze different factors:
- Water flow and catchment
- Microclimates (frost pockets, heat zones)
- Access and circulation
- Zone planning (Zone 0-5 placement)
- Soil types and conditions

Each overlay isolates one factor for analysis before combining them into a final integrated design. (see [[land-selection-for-homesteading]]).

## Topographic Reading

### Key Patterns to Identify

- **Keylines** -- the line of maximum change in slope (where convex becomes concave), critical for water management design
- **Ridge lines and drainage lines** -- the skeleton of the watershed
- **Saddle points** -- low points on ridges, useful for road placement and dam sites
- **Re-entrant corners** -- where water naturally concentrates, potential dam sites
- **Spur lines** -- protruding ridges between drainage lines

### Watershed Assessment

Understanding the watershed above and below your site is critical:
- What is the total catchment area?
- Where does water enter and exit the site?
- What are the soil types and their infiltration rates?
- Are there existing erosion gullies or deposition areas?

## Tools for Mapping

- A-frame level or water level for finding contour
- Clinometer for measuring slope angle
- Compass for determining aspect and magnetic north
- Measuring tape (long tape for distance measurements)
- GPS (optional, for larger properties)
- Aerial photographs and topographic maps (USGS or equivalent)
- Field notebook for recording observations
