The mycorrhizal requirement also explains the species' urban habitat
preference: many European towns and parks have mature poplars and mixed
deciduous plantings with sandy, well-drained soils that suit the fungus. The
expansion of the species through Brandenburg during the 1980s likely followed
the root networks of planted poplar trees in parks and along streets.

## Comparison With Other Psychoactive Inocybe Species

Inocybe aeruginascens is the only confirmed psychoactive European Inocybe
species. Other Inocybe species have been tested for alkaloid content but
generally contain only muscarine or physiologically inactive compounds. The
discovery of aeruginascin as a species-specific marker alkaloid means that
chemical fingerprinting can serve as a reliable identification tool,
distinguishing Inocybe aeruginascens from potentially dangerous lookalikes
that contain muscarine.
