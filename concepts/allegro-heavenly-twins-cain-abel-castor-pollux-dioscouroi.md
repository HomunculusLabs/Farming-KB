## The Heavenly Twins: Cain and Abel, Castor and Pollux, and the Dioscouroi

In John Allegro's interpretation, the recurring mythological motif of the "Heavenly Twins" — appearing in Genesis as Cain and Abel, in Greek myth as Castor and Pollux, and in the New Testament as the sons of Zebedee — derives ultimately from the physical appearance of the Amanita muscaria mushroom, whose volva splits into two halves at maturity. Each twin represents one sexual aspect of the androgynous fungus: the womb and the phallus. The motif recurs across cultures because the mushroom itself was a cult object of extraordinary importance throughout the ancient Near East and Mediterranean.

## Cain and Abel

The names of the first biblical brothers are not Hebrew personal names at all but Sumerian words describing the mushroom's anatomy.

- **Cain** (Hebrew *Qayin*) corresponds to Sumerian **\*GAR_EN**, meaning "seed-container" — that is, the womb. The volva's lower cup, cradling the base of the stem, is the female element. In the mushroom's physical structure, the volva is the remnant of the universal veil that once enclosed the entire fruiting body; when it ruptures, it forms a cup at the base — the "seed-container."
- **Abel** (Hebrew *Hebel*) corresponds to Sumerian **BAL**, meaning "borer; phallus." The erect stem rising from the volva is the male element. BAL is a multivalent Sumerian word also meaning "to pierce, to bore through," linking the mushroom's stem to all penetrative crafts.

The two together — womb and phallus — are the divided volva of the mushroom, now personified as rivalrous brothers. Their conflict is not moral but botanical: the two halves of one organism, dramatized as a tale of fratricide.

One half "kills" the other because, in reality, the mushroom's development separates them — the stem pushes up through and away from the volval cup. The "blood" of Abel that "cries out from the ground" (Genesis 4:10) is the red juice of the Amanita, which stains the hands and surfaces where it is prepared.

### Tubal-Cain and the Language of Forging

**Tubal-Cain** (Genesis 4:22) is a fuller form of Abel's name, incorporating the Sumerian element **TI** "organ." As "patron of metal-workers," his trade is not arbitrary: the mushroom's stem — the borer (BAL) — was conceptually linked to drilling and metal-working. The act of boring, drilling, and forging all converge on the same phallic imagery. Tubal-Cain thus preserves a complete Sumerian description of the mushroom's anatomy in a single biblical name, disguised as a genealogical ancestor.

### Zillah and the Axe-Head

Their mother **Zillah** yields **"Bar-Zillah"** — "son of Zillah." In Aramaic, **barzelã'** means "iron" or "axe-head," itself a pun on "womb" or "groin." The axe-head shape mirrors the volva's cup. Thus two fungus names — Tubal-Cain (TI-BAL-GAR_EN) and barzelã' (the axe-head/womb) — were spun by storytellers into a hero, his mother, and his trade. The result is a genealogical narrative that is, at root, a botanical description preserved under the guise of family history.

## Eryngion — The Androgynous Plant

The compound Sumerian **\*ERUM_GAR_EN** joins **ERUM** ("Hermes," i.e. phallus) with **GAR_EN** ("Cain," i.e. womb) to name the mushroom as an androgynous entity — simultaneously male and female. This is the most explicit ancient naming of the mushroom's dual sexuality, combining the phallic and uterine elements into a single botanical designation.

### Pliny on the Eryngion Root

Pliny the Elder records that the eryngion root **"grows into the likeness of one sex or the other."** The male form, when gathered, was said to make men **"become lovable in the eyes of women"** — a power famously attributed to Phaon of Lesbos, the youth beloved by Sappho. The plant was also credited with correcting **"deficiency or excess in menstruation, all affections of the uterus,"** reinforcing its association with female fertility and genital anatomy. Pliny's account preserves genuine folk-knowledge of the mushroom's reputed aphrodisiac and gynecological properties, transmitted from much older Sumerian sources.

### Alternate Names

The plant was also called **Hermaion** (from ERUM "penis") and **Moly** ("knobbed-plant"), the latter being the herb Hermes gave to Odysseus to ward off Circe's enchantments — itself a mushroom-reference in Homeric narrative. The knobbed cap of the Amanita, with its distinctive white warts, matches Moly's description as a "difficult to dig" plant with a black root and white flower. Homer's "drug" is the same substance known to the Sumerians by its sexual names.

### Aloe, Amphibion, and Tiresias

Eryngion was equated with **Aloe/Amphibion** — "double-life" — reflecting the fungus's dual sexual nature. This same amphibious quality is the defining trait of **Tiresias**, the blind seer of Thebes who lived as both man and woman, confirming that the "heavenly twin" androgyne pattern was understood across the entire ancient Mediterranean. Tiresias's sexual transformation and prophetic vision both derive from the mushroom's capacity to reveal hidden knowledge through its dual nature.

## "Am I my brother's keeper?"

After Cain kills Abel, the text pronounces that **"every slayer-of-Cain (horeg-Qayin) will be subject to sevenfold vengeance"** (Genesis 4:15). The Hebrew phrase **horeg-Qayin** is phonetically reminiscent of **Eryngion** (\*ERUM_GAR_EN; Greek *Eruggion*), suggesting the whole murder narrative is a garbled transmission of the mushroom's double name — the "slayer" and the slain are two halves of one fungus. The sevenfold vengeance echoes the seven days of the mushroom's growth cycle and the seven-starred Pleiades, under whose rising the fungus was harvested.

### Persian samrūkh

The Persian word **samrūkh**, meaning "mushroom," derives from Sumerian **\*ShU_MAR_UGU/AGA**, meaning **"crown of the womb-favourer"** — a description of the glans or top of the fungus. The same androgynous anatomy underlies yet another language's naming of the mushroom, demonstrating the pan-Near-Eastern spread of this botanical vocabulary from its Sumerian origins.

## Castor and Pollux

The Greek Dioscouroi, Castor and Pollux, are another manifestation of the heavenly-twins motif, transported from Semitic mushroom-lore into Hellenic mythology through centuries of cultural transmission.

### Birth from the Egg

They were said to have been **born from an egg**, laid by their mother Leda after her union with Zeus in the form of a swan. Their sister **Helen** (of Troy) was born from the same egg and is connected with **pine resin** — one of the known carriers of Amanita muscaria preparations. The egg itself is a volva-motif: the universal veil enclosing the nascent mushroom, from which the "twins" emerge at maturity.

### Dioscouroi: Not "Sons of Zeus"

Allegro argues that **Dioscouroi does NOT mean "sons of Zeus."** Instead, the name is a jumbled form of Sumerian **\*USh_GU_RI_UD**, meaning **"erect phallus of the storm."** Through Greek phonological rearrangement: **\*ud-ush-gu-ri → \*di-us-ku-roi → Dioskoroi**. The "storm" element (UD) connects to the mushroom's association with thunder — it was believed to grow where lightning struck.

Remarkably, the same Sumerian phrase underlies **Iscariot** (USh-GU-RI-UD) and the Persian **saqrātiyūn**, another word for "mushroom." The traitor's name and the divine twins share a single origin in fungal anatomy.

Judas Iscariot, like Cain before him, is one half of a mushroom pair — the "slayer" of the other twin.

### Etymology of the Individual Names

- **Castor** is cognate with Greek *gaster* "belly, womb," hence "pod." The **castor oil plant** acquired its name through folk etymology involving the beaver's testicles (Latin *castor* = "beaver"), a classic example of phallic substitution in plant naming. The "pod" is again the volva — the cup-shaped base of the mushroom.
- **Pollux** derives from Sumerian **\*LU-GEShPU**, "strong man; guardian," yielding Greek *phulax*. He is depicted as a **boxer** — the "short arm" being a phallic allusion. When the element **PU** is detached and placed before the root: **pu-lu-gesh → Poludeukes**, with **DU** "adversary" added, producing the full Greek name.

## Boanerges — "Sons of Thunder"

James and John, the sons of Zebedee, receive the nickname **Boanerges** (Mark 3:17), traditionally rendered "Sons of Thunder." Allegro decomposes this differently.

### The Real Meaning

The name encodes Sumerian **\*GEShPU_AN_UR**, meaning **"mighty man (holding up) the arch of heaven"** — a description of the mushroom's **stem supporting the canopy**. The cap of the Amanita, spread wide, is the "arch of heaven" born aloft by the sturdy stalk. The image is precisely that of the risen mushroom: stem erect, cap expanded like a dome over the world.

### A Spurious Translation

**"Boanerges" is not Aramaic and does not mean "Sons of Thunder."** The phrase is a spurious translation attached to a genuine mushroom name for narrative purposes. Yet "Sons of Thunder" is itself not meaningless: **keraunion** ("thunder-fungus") is a genuine ancient name for mushrooms believed to grow where lightning struck. The pattern is consistent: **a real mushroom name is preserved, then cloaked with a fabricated translation that suits the story.**

### The Secret Layer

Allegro stresses the hermeneutic principle at work:

> "If the writer has gone to the trouble purposefully to conceal his secret name... behind the story of Jesus and his companions there lies a secret layer of meaning... the secret level of understanding is the significant one."

The surface narrative — brothers, twins, apostles — is a fictional veil over botanical and pharmacological knowledge.

## The Risen Mushroom Configuration

The physical appearance of the mature Amanita muscaria encapsulates the entire twin mythology in a single image.

### Anatomy as Iconography

- The **canopy spread** represents the **upper half of the volva** — the open groin of the woman.
- The **stem** is the **phallus** supporting that open groin.
- The **shaft driven into the axe-head** (the volva's base) completes the sexual image: penetration of womb by phallus.

The mushroom, in its fully risen state, is a depiction of the sexual act — and therefore of fertility, generation, and divine creative power.

This is the "resurrection" the early cult celebrated: not a human returning from death, but the mushroom erupting from the earth in its full sexual glory.

### Burden and Harmony

Sumerian **GUN** "burden" yields Latin **cunnus** "cunt." The compound **AR-GUN** "organ of burden" gives us **Mount Hermon**, the sacred mountain.

The **plane tree** with its broad crown symbolized sexual powers; Hebrew **'armōn** derives from the same AR-GUN root, and English **"harmony"** — from Greek *harmonia* "joining together" — originally meant the union of bearer and burden, carpenter and joint.

### Jesus, Son of the Carpenter

Jesus is called **"son of a carpenter"** (Matthew 13:55). In Greek this renders him **Harmonidēs**, because the mushroom is simultaneously a **"drill"** (the boring phallus, BAL) and a **"joint"** (the point where stem meets cap).

The **carpenter's Sumerian ideogram** depicts a notched whorl for a bow-string drill — and the phallus as BAL "borer" doubles as the **weaver's spindle**, itself identified with the mushroom. The trades of carpenter, weaver, and metal-forger all converge on the same tool: the mushroom-as-borer.
