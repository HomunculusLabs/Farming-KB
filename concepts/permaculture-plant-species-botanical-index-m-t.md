# Permaculture Plant Species Botanical Index (M–T)

This index catalogs permaculture-relevant plant species from **Macadamia** through **Tamarindus**, drawn from [[bill-mollison]]'s *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual* appendix. Species are organized by botanical family and functional group to illustrate how taxonomic knowledge supports guild design, companion planting, and ecological succession planning.

## Why Botanical Taxonomy Matters in Permaculture

Understanding scientific naming is a practical design tool. Species within the same family share root architectures, pest relationships, nutrient requirements, and allelopathic properties. When a designer knows that *Phaseolus*, *Pisum*, *Macroptilium*, and *Pueraria* all belong to **Fabaceae**, they can predict nitrogen-fixing capacity, rhizobial compatibility, and appropriate companion pairings. Similarly, recognizing that *Solanum tuberosum*, *S. melongena*, and *S. annuum* share **Solanaceae** alerts designers to shared disease vectors (e.g., late blight) making crop rotation between them inadvisable.

Mollison emphasizes that guild design depends heavily on family-level relationships. A fruit tree guild centered on *Prunus persicae* benefits from rosaceous companions like *Rosa multiflora* while avoiding other *Prunus* species that compete for identical resources and pests.

## Fabaceae (Legume Family) — Nitrogen Fixers & Ground Covers

The legume family is arguably the most critical in permaculture, providing biological nitrogen fixation through rhizobial symbiosis. The M–T section is exceptionally rich in legumes:

- ***Macroptilium atropurpureum*** (Siratro) — Perennial tropical ground cover, erosion control
- ***Macroptilium lathyroides*** (Phasey bean) — Green manure, tropical pasture
- ***Medicago sativa*** (Lucerne/Alfalfa) — Deep-rooted perennial forage, breaks compacted subsoil
- ***Phaseolus acutifolius*** (Tepary bean) — Desert-adapted food crop, extreme drought tolerance
- ***Phaseolus lunatus*** (Lima bean) — Food crop, warm-climate green manure
- ***Phaseolus vulgaris*** (Common bean) — Staple food, short-cycle nitrogen fixer
- ***Pisum sativum*** (Snow pea) / ***P. arvense*** (Dun pea) — Cool-season food and cover crops
- ***Pongamia pinnata*** — Multipurpose leguminous tree, oilseed, shade
- ***Prosopis pallida*** (Kiawe/Mesquite) — Nitrogen-fixing tree, food pods, fuelwood
- ***Psophocarpus tetragonolobus*** (Goa bean) — Tropical high-protein crop with edible tubers
- ***Pueraria phaseoloides*** (Puero) — Tropical ground cover, green manure
- ***Sesbania*** / ***Stylosanthes guianensis*** (Stylo) — Fast-growing green manure, drought-hardy pasture

Legumes are foundational to succession design. Pioneer species like *Macroptilium* and *Stylosanthes* stabilize degraded soils and build nitrogen capital, enabling later establishment of demanding canopy species. Deep-rooted perennials like *Medicago sativa* access subsoil nutrients and cycle them to the surface through leaf fall.

## Poaceae (Grass Family) — Mulch, Forage & Erosion Control

Grasses serve essential functions as mulch producers, erosion controllers, and animal forage:

- ***Panicum*** (Guinea grass), ***Paspalum*** — Tropical forage and pasture grasses
- ***Pennisetum clandestinum*** (Kikuyu) — Aggressive slope stabilization
- ***Pennisetum purpureum*** (Napier grass) — Giant biomass producer, animal fodder, thatch
- ***Phyllostachys*** (Bamboo) — Building material, erosion control, edible shoots, windbreak
- ***Phragmites*** (Common reed) — Water filtration, thatch, constructed wetlands
- ***Saccharum officinarum*** (Sugar cane) — Mulch, fuel, food, living fence
- ***Secale cereale*** (Rye) — Winter cover crop, weed suppression
- ***Setaria*** (Millet) / ***Sorghum*** — Quick-maturing grain, biofumigation, livestock feed
- ***Spartina*** (Cordgrass) — Saltmarsh restoration, coastal erosion control
- ***Oryza sativa*** (Rice) — Aquatic staple integrated with duck and fish production

Grass-legume polycultures (e.g., *Panicum* with *Stylosanthes*) outperform monocultures in yield and soil health.

## Solanaceae (Nightshade Family) — Food Crops & Caution

The Solanaceae family contains vital food plants but also toxic species requiring careful management:

- ***Solanum tuberosum*** (Potato) — Staple tuber; avoid rotation with other solanaceous species
- ***Solanum melongena*** (Eggplant), ***S. annuum*** (Sweet pepper), ***S. fasciculatum*** (Chili) — Warm-climate food crops; companion with *Tagetes* for pest deterrence
- ***Solanum nigrum*** (Nightshade) — **Toxic**; useful indicator but dangerous near food gardens
- ***Nicotiana*** (Tobacco) — Botanical insecticide; use cautiously in [[integrated-pest-management]]

## Major Fruit-Producing Families

### Rosaceae
- ***Malus pumila*** (Apple) — Central canopy tree in temperate food forests
- ***Prunus*** spp. (Cherry, Plum, Peach) — Fruit production, careful siting for frost avoidance
- ***Pyrus*** (Pear) — Long-lived fruit tree, fire blight considerations
- ***Rosa multiflora*** (Hedgerow rose) — Barrier plant, wildlife shelter, nitrogen-fixing rootstock
- ***Rubus*** (Bramble/Raspberry) — Pioneer berry, edge species, poultry forage
- ***Ribes*** (Gooseberry, Blackcurrant, Redcurrant) — Shade-tolerant understory fruits

### Myrtaceae & Moraceae
- ***Psidium guajava*** (Guava), ***Pimenta dioica*** (Allspice) — Tropical fruit and spice, understory companions
- ***Morus*** (Mulberry) — Poultry forage, silkworm production, pioneer fruit tree with deep taproot

## Aquatic & Wetland Species

Mollison devotes significant attention to aquaculture integration and constructed wetlands:

- ***Nymphaea*** (Water lily) — Pond shading, reduces algal growth
- ***Nypa fruticans*** (Nypa palm) — Coastal stabilization, sugar/sap production
- ***Pistia*** (Water lettuce), ***Rhizophora*** (Mangroves) — Nutrient bioaccumulation, coastal nursery habitat
- ***Sagittaria*** (Arrowhead) — Edible tubers, water purification
- ***Salicornia*** (Samphire) — Halophyte forage for salt marshes
- ***Scirpus*** (Sedge), ***Phragmites*** (Common reed) — Wastewater treatment, bank stabilization
- ***Posidonia*** (Seagrass) — Submerged habitat, sediment stabilization

## Multipurpose Trees & Timber Species

Permaculture treats trees as multi-functional elements. Key species in this range:

- ***Moringa oleifera*** (Horseradish tree) — Edible leaves, seed pods, water purification, fast growth
- ***Samanea saman*** (Monkeypod) — Nitrogen-fixing canopy tree, pasture shade, edible pods
- ***Persea americana*** (Avocado), ***Mangifera indica*** (Mango) — Subtropical/tropical food trees
- ***Melia azedarach*** (White cedar) — Timber, insect-repellent wood, drought-tolerant shade
- ***Pinus*** spp. — Windbreak, timber; select species carefully for fire risk
- ***Populus tremuloides*** (Aspen), ***Salix*** (Willow) — Pioneer species, water management, living structures
- ***Swietenia mahogani*** (Mahogany), ***Sequoia sempervirens*** (Redwood) — High-value timber, long-lived canopy

## Medicinal, Culinary & Companion Herbs

- ***Ocimum basilicum*** (Basil) — Companion to tomatoes, pest deterrence
- ***Rosmarinus officinalis*** (Rosemary), ***Salvia officinalis*** (Sage) — Hedging, bee plants, drought-tolerant
- ***Symphytum officinale*** (Comfrey) — Dynamic accumulator (K, Ca), compost activator; Mollison's most-referenced herb
- ***Tagetes*** (African marigold) — Root nematode suppression, companion to solanaceous crops
- ***Ruta graveolens*** (Rue) — Pest deterrent companion (use sparingly)
- ***Petroselinum crispum*** (Parsley) — Beneficial insect attractor

## Root Crops & Ecological Support Species

**Root crops:** *Manihot esculenta* (Cassava), *Maranta arundinaceae* (Arrowroot), *Pachyrhizus tuberosus* (Yam bean), *Oxalis crenata* (Oca), *Sechium edule* (Chayote), *Raphanus sativus* (Daikon radish — subsoil breaker, biofumigation).

**Ecological support:** *Pleurotus* (Wood fungi — decomposition, food), *Pyrethrum* (natural insecticide), *Quillaja saponaria* (saponin-rich bark for bio-sprays), *Mikania* (caution: aggressive tropical invader illustrating risks of unmanaged species).

## Botanical Classification & Guild Design

Mollison's appendix implicitly teaches a design methodology: group species by family, then by function. A *Prunus persicae* guild surrounded by *Medicago sativa* (nitrogen fixer), *Tagetes* (pest deterrent), and *Symphytum* (nutrient accumulator) creates a self-maintaining polyculture. A *Persea americana* guild uses *Musa paradisiaca* as nurse shade, *Ocimum basilicum* for pest deterrence, and *Paspalum* grasses as living mulch.

Understanding family relationships enables climate-appropriate species substitution while preserving functional roles—replacing *Phaseolus vulgaris* with *P. acutifolius* in arid zones, or *Pennisetum purpureum* with *Sorghum bicolor* in cooler regions—without losing the guild's ecological integrity.

## Succession & Species Selection

This M–T range illustrates the full succession spectrum: pioneer nitrogen-fixers (*Macroptilium*, *Stylosanthes*) establish first on degraded land; fast-growing biomass species (*Pennisetum*, *Sorghum*, *Salix*) build organic matter; mid-succession fruit trees (*Morus*, *Malus*, *Prunus*) establish as canopy begins to close; climax and long-lived timber (*Sequoia*, *Swietenia*, *Quercus*) define the mature system. Mollison's approach treats each species not as an isolated element but as a temporal node in a self-renewing landscape.
