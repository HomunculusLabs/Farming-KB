# Mushroom Harvest and Post

## The Growing Period

Under favorable conditions a bed of straw-manure compost should come into bearing in from six to eight weeks. One must begin to look for the source of trouble if after ten weeks there is no sign of fruiting. With shaving-manure compost, the beds may bear in eight weeks, but it is just as likely that twelve weeks will be required. At 58 to 60 degrees F the bed will come into bearing earlier than when kept at 54 degrees F, but the length of the period of production will usually be shorter and the mushrooms smaller at the higher temperature.

### Flushes of Growth
When the mushrooms begin to form, they appear first almost directly over the inoculum of spawn inserted, so under uniform conditions the first patches of buttons will be as regularly distributed as the spawn pieces. As these groups or clusters all push toward maturity simultaneously, it may be called a "flush" of fruiting. When these are picked, a little fresh earth placed where necessary, and the bed sufficiently watered, it may be a week or more before a second flush occurs. If the temperature is irregular, flushes may characterize the whole bearing period.

### Period of Production
The period of productiveness of a bed will vary from a few weeks to five months, depending upon conditions. A satisfactory bed will bear for two to four months.

### Stimulating Renewed Production
After a bed has apparently ceased to bear it should not immediately be discarded. An application of water heavier than usual may cause it to yield an additional supply. If the examination reveals fresh-looking spawn in the bed, the removal of the old soil and the substitution of a thin layer of fresh, rich earth may be a stimulus to renewed productiveness. Watering with dilute saltpeter solution or liquid manure has been tried but not found sufficiently beneficial to justify general recommendation.

## Harvesting

### Care During Production
Beds which are yielding heavily will require considerable water. No grower can make a greater mistake than to follow the now almost discarded custom of adding no water to the beds so long as mushrooms are evident. The sole cause of failure to get adequate returns is sometimes in the dearth of water at the right time.
