# Mnemonics of Meaning and Sacred Pattern

## Overview

Across cultures, sacred patterns, chants, and symbolic systems have served as powerful mnemonic devices that encode complex philosophical, cosmological, and ecological knowledge into compact, memorable forms. These systems demonstrate that the deepest insights of human understanding — from quantum physics to ecology — find their earliest and most enduring expression in pattern, symbol, and ritual.

## The Buddhist Mantra: Om Mani Padme Hum

Buddhists remind themselves of the pattern of events with their oft-repeated chant "Om mani padme hum," pronounced "Aum ma-ni pay-may hung" by Tibetans and Nepalese. Each syllable encodes a layer of meaning that [[maps]] onto the relationship between the implicate (enfolded) and explicate (unfolded) orders of reality.

### Syllable-by-Syllable Interpretation

**Om (Aum)**: The awakening or beginning harmonic — the sound of all stillness and the sounds of all time. It is the fundamental harmonic that recalls the universe itself.

**Ma-ni**: The unchanging essence or diamantine core of all phenomena; the truth, represented as a diamond, jewel, or thunderbolt. Sometimes represented in paintings as a blue orb or radiant jewel, and sometimes as a source of lightning or fire.

**Pay-may**: "Enfolded in the heart of the lotus" (mani enfolded). The visible and everyday unfolding of events, petals, or patterns thus revealing the essential unchanged core (mani) to our understanding. The core itself, or the realisation of it, is nirvana (the ideal state of Buddhism). The lotus represents the implicate order of tessellated and annidated events, and the process of unfolding the passage of time to successive revelations. At the core is unchanging understanding.

**Hung**: "It is here, now." A declamation of belief by the chanter in the words. It also prefaces the "Om" or beginning of the new chant cycle. In a long sequence of such short chants, all words follow their predecessors. This is the reminder mnemonic of implicate time — all events are present now, and forever repeated in their form.

## Dorje: The Primordial Buddha

Dorje, or Dorje-chang, is the Tibetan Buddha-figure who holds the dorje (thunderbolt), represented as a radiant stone symbolising cosmic energy. Dorje is "the primordial Buddha of Tibet," who began the great succession of current and past reincarnations. His colour is blue, for eternity, and he may carry a bell to signify the voiceless wisdom of the inanimate, or the sound of the void.

### Cross-Cultural Thunder God Connections

Dorje is an alter ego of Thor of the Norsemen, Durga of the Hindu, and of thunderbolts and "thunderers" of other tribal peoples. The mani or stone of Thor was Mjollnir, his hammer, from which derives Mjollnirstaun, and eventually Mollison (by way of invasions into Scotland and migrations). Even personal names may thus remind us of the essential oneness of the events and beliefs around us.

## Convergence of Tribal and Modern Understanding

From tribal chants, arts, and folk decoration, many mnemonic patterns can be selected that in their evolution over the ages express very much the same world concept as modern physics and biology. Such thoughtful and vivid beliefs come close to realising the actual nature of observed events and are derived from contemplation of those events, indicating a way of life and a philosophy rather than a dogma or set of measures.

Beliefs so evolved precede and transcend the emphasis on the individual, or the division of life into disciplines and categories. When searching for the roots of belief, or more specifically meaning, one comes again and again to the oneness underlying science, word, song, art, and pattern: "The jewel in the heart of the lotus."

## The Drift from Universal Systems

Many world beliefs share an essential core, but a drift is observable from such nature-based, essentially universal systems toward personalised or humanoid gods, dogma, and fanaticism, and to symbols without meaning or use in our lives or understanding of life. Many other world-concepts based on the analogies of rainbows, serpents, and song cycles relate to aspects of the integrated world view and are found in Amerindian and Australian tribal cultures.

## Patterns of Society: The Boat Pattern

Social hierarchies can be patterned to represent aspects of their structure. A study of occupational levels in Tasmania (1969–1974) revealed a characteristic "boat" pattern form based on status and resource distribution.

### Occupational Levels and Sex Ratios

The central pattern represents the orders or castes of occupational level (status) in its long axis. There are seldom more than seven major occupational levels even in rigorously-stratified hierarchies such as the army. The width represents the number of people at each level, sampled from approximately 35,000 people across organizations including the local army, a multinational company, churches, and small businesses.

**Level 1 — Low Occupational (Resource) Area**: Manual and unskilled urban workers. Characteristics include general dearth of material resources, low status, part-time occupations, and a remarkable preponderance of male births and survived male children (about 140 males per 100 females). Large families with serial polyandry common or acceptable.

**Level 2 — Central/Middle Class**: Adequate resources, nine-to-five jobs, some job tenure, and a "normal" birth ratio of 104 males per 100 females. Mixed white collar and skilled technical workers, average family sizes. Monogamy is an ideal, but often expressed as serial monogamy.

**Level 3 — Upper Levels**: Few people, extensive resources, flexible and often self-set times, and a high proportion of female children (about 100 females to 70 males or less). Urban professionals or managers typify this group, with small families and effective polygyny via concubines or mistresses.

**Level 4 — Very High Levels**: Executive directors and landed nobility. Variable family sizes with a preponderance of female children, and a habit of lateral intermarriage for economic alliances, facilitated by exclusive schools and resorts.

### Genetic Streaming

The imbalance of sex ratios in these strata ensures genetic turnover or diffusion between classes — a streaming of genetic materials between levels over generations. This genetic flow between occupational levels creates a dynamic social pattern that maintains both stability and adaptability within the social structure.
