# Lichenicolous Fungi

## Overview

Lichenicolous fungi are fungi that grow on lichens, either as parasites, commensals, or saprotrophs. They represent a diverse and specialized group found across multiple taxonomic lineages of Ascomycota, Basidiomycota, and their anamorphic states. The number of lichenicolous fungi now probably exceeds 1,500 species, with many yet to be described, particularly from tropical regions.

## Taxonomic Distribution

### Ascomycota Orders Rich in Lichenicolous Fungi

**Arthoniales**: Most species of Arthonia, Lecanographa, and Opegrapha are lichenized, but significant numbers are lichenicolous:
- ~45 species of Arthonia are lichenicolous
- 3 species of Lecanographa are lichenicolous
- 35 species of Opegrapha are lichenicolous
- At least 23 species of Plectocarpon are lichenicolous

**Caliciales**: Species can be lichenized, lichenicolous, fungicolous, or saprotrophic on bark/wood:
- Several genera (Chaenothecopsis, Microcalicium, Sphinctrina) comprise high percentage of lichenicolous species
- Many grow on other lichenized species of Caliciales
- Microcalicium arenarium develops on saxicolous lichens Psilolechia lucida and P. clavulifera

**Lecanorales**:
- Toninia: Numerous lichens including several parasitic on other lichens
- Buellia, Carbonea, Catillaria, Rhizocarpon, Tephromela, Thelocarpon: Mostly lichenized with some lichenicolous species
- Phacopsis: Exclusively lichenicolous (14 species); most grow on Parmeliaceae

**Ostropales**:
- Odontotrema (including Lethariicola): 15 lichenicolous species
- Nanostictis: 3+ undescribed species

**Verrucariales**:
- Several species of Verrucaria are parasitic lichens
- Adelococcus (3 species), Merismatium (9 species), Norrlinia (2 species): Exclusively nonlichenized, lichenicolous
- Stigmidium: Approximately 70 species
- Muellerella: 12 species (lichenicolous or hepaticolous)

**Dothideales (Pleosporales)**:
- Tubeufiaceae: Large number of fungicolous taxa on meliolaceous (18 species), phyllachoraceous (9 species) leaf-inhabiting ascomycetes, and rusts (3 species)
- Dacampiaceae: Exclusively lichenicolous genera include Clypeococcum (6), Dacampia (3), Polycoccum (30), Pyrenidium (15, mostly undescribed), Weddellomyces (8)
- Most species specialized on single host genus or species
- Lichenopeltella (Microthyriaceae): Large number of lichenicolous species, mostly on macrolichens
- Additional genera: Buelliella, Cercidospora, Didymellopsis, Endococcus, Lichenostigma, Sagediopsis, Sphaerellothecium, Zwackhiomyces

### Hypocreales and Nectriaceae

- Nectriopsis: 39 species, many lichenicolous; grow on myxomycetes, pyrenomycetes, loculoascomycetes, discomycetes, and lichens
- White to yellow perithecia commonly observed in fungicolous taxa
- Trichonectria: 3 species on nonlichenized ascomycetes; 6 species on lichens

### Clavicipitaceae

- Cordyceps: Three species commonly grow on Elaphomyces species
- Associated anamorphs: Verticillium sect. Prostrata (now Lecanicillium)
- High chitinase activity; potential as entomogenous, nematophagous, and fungicolous biocontrol agents

### Niessliaceae

- Monocillium anamorphs of Niesslia: Some fungicolous
- Circinoniesslia and Valetoniella: Found only on perithecia of Nectria species
- Trichosphaeriella decipiens: Mostly on sporocarps of aphyllophorales
- Three species of Niesslia are lichenicolous

### Basidiomycota (Urediniomycetidae, Platygloeomycetidae)

- Parasite-host contact through haustoria
- Lenticular bodies: Specialized organelles in periplasmic region; vesicular structures with electron-opaque core
- Found in Sporidiobolus, Cryptomycocolax, Colacogloea, Bensingtonia, Rhodosporidium
- Chionosphaera and Stilbum (Atractiellaceae): May be mycoparasites; two species grow on lichens

### Anamorphic Conidial Ascomycota

Commonly found in fungicolous associations:
- **Acremonium**: Mostly anamorphs of Hypocreales; new section Lichenoidea for 9 lichenicolous species
- **Verticillium/Lecanicillium**: Anamorphs of Hypocreales and Clavicipitaceae
- **Chalara**: Anamorphs of Leotiales
- A. strictum, A. berkeleyanum, A. domschii, V. luteo-album, V. fungicola, L. lecanii, L. psalliotae grow on diverse substrata

## Ecological Interactions

- **Parasitism**: Many species cause visible damage to host lichen thalli (discoloration, gall formation, necrosis)
- **Commensalism**: Some species use the lichen surface as physical substrate without apparent harm
- **Saprotrophy**: Growing on dead parts of lichen thalli
- **Host specificity**: Most species specialized on single host genus or species
- **Geographic patterns**: Many species with broad distributions; some restricted to specific regions

## Conservation Concerns

Lichenicolous fungi face the same threats as their lichen hosts:

- Air pollution (especially sulfur dioxide)
- Habitat loss and fragmentation
- Forest management practices
- Climate change affecting host lichen distributions

Because of their host specificity, loss of a host lichen species may result in co-extinction of its associated lichenicolous fungi.

## Research Challenges

- Many species are extremely small and difficult to detect in the field
- Taxonomic expertise required across multiple fungal groups
- Molecular methods revealing previously undetectable diversity
- Tropical regions remain largely unexplored
- Culture of many species has never been achieved
