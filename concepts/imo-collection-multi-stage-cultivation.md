# IMO Collection and Multi-Stage Cultivation

## Overview

[[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]] (IMO) are the foundation of [[query-jadam-vs-korean-natural-farming]]
Farming (KNF) as developed by Master Han Kyu Cho. IMO are defined as
organisms that have lived in harmony with the soil in a particular
region, contributing to establishing the environment they inhabit. They
are the main protagonists that have brought vigor to the land through
proliferation in diversity over time. IMO have the ability to withstand
any weather conditions and, given the right environment, perform their
functions with great vitality and adaptability to changing conditions.

The KNF system processes IMO through multiple stages - from initial
collection (IMO #1) through foundation stock preparation (IMO #2) to
expanded cultivation (IMO #3) and final field application (IMO #4).
Each stage builds upon the previous one, progressively increasing
microbial biomass while maintaining the indigenous character of the
original collection. This multi-stage approach ensures that the microbes
applied to fields are both locally adapted and present in sufficient
numbers to transform soil biology.

The practical importance of IMO lies in their ability to decompose
complex organic materials, catalyze chemical processes in soil through
[[ligninolytic-enzyme-production-bioremediation]], revitalize entire ecosystems by restoring food chains
from bacteria to earthworms, and suppress diseases by maintaining
natural microbial balance. [[cho-fermented-plant-juice-preparation]] of Japanese mugwort and
dropwort (1:500 dilution), brown rice vinegar (1:500), and
Mineral A (1:1,000) are added as microbial activators. A small amount
of Makgeolli (Korean rice wine) may be added to raise temperature.
The mixture is piled on a rice straw mat under shade, covered with
straw and a breathable fabric, and fermented for 5-7 days.

### IMO #4: Final Field Preparation

IMO #3 is further mixed with soil to create the final application
material. This stage brings the microbial cultures into direct contact
with the farm's soil matrix, allowing the microbes to establish
themselves in their permanent environment. The soil-based preparation
ensures survival during field application and provides immediate
benefits to soil structure and biology.

### Microbial Functions in Soil

IMO perform four critical soil functions. First, they decompose complex
organic materials into simpler compounds that undergo ionic interactions.
Second, they produce enzymes, antibiotics, organic acids, and various
complexes that catalyze chemical reactions. Third, they revitalize the
ecosystem - bacteria and fungi appear first, followed by nematodes,
earthworms, mole crickets, and moles. Fourth, they suppress diseases
by maintaining naturally active material circulation.

### Storage and Quality Indicators

IMO #2 should be stored at 1-15 degrees Celsius with good air
circulation. The appearance of bubbles indicates that IMO are losing
effectiveness through unwanted fermentation. When IMO #2 is healthy, it
maintains a consistent texture and sweet-sour aroma. Temperature
management is critical throughout all stages, with 23-25 degrees
Celsius being optimal for most processes.

## Applications

### Soil Restoration

IMO application transforms degraded agricultural soils by restoring
microbial diversity. Fields recover fertility and soil becomes soft and
fluffy when IMO mixed with lactic [[lactic-acid-bacteria-knf]] preparation knf
is sprayed. This approach addresses the fundamental problem of modern
agriculture: the destruction of soil biological communities through
chemical inputs.
