# AACT Brewing Science

Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) is a liquid extract produced by
brewing high-quality compost in aerated water with supplemental food
sources for 18-24 hours. Developed and standardized by Dr. Elaine
Ingham and the Soil Foodweb community, AACT concentrates and multiplies
the ingham-beneficial-nematodes-soil-food-web:** Present and diverse
- **No detectable pathogens:** Verified by direct microscopy
- **No persistent herbicides:** Clopyralid, aminopyralid, and similar
  compounds survive composting and will kill broadleaf plants when
  applied in tea

Thermal compost (hot composted at 131-160°F / 55-71°C) and vermicompost
(worm compost) both work, but they produce different microbial profiles.
Vermicompost tends to be more bacterial; thermal compost with woody
inputs tends to be more fungal. For fungal-dominated teas, include woody
materials (wood chips, sawdust, leaf mold) in the compost pile.

## Brew Duration

Standard AACT brew time is 18-24 hours at 65-75°F (18-24°C). Brewing
beyond 24 hours risks oxygen depletion and anaerobic conditions as food
sources are consumed and microbial populations peak then crash. The
"sweet spot" is typically 20-24 hours when bacterial populations reach
maximum density.

Temperature significantly affects brew speed:
- **Below 55°F (13°C):** Very slow microbial growth; extend brew to
  30+ hours with additional food
- **65-75°F (18-24°C):** Optimal range for most beneficial organisms
- **Above 85°F (29°C):** Accelerated metabolism; oxygen demand exceeds
  supply, risking anaerobic conditions even with adequate aeration

## Water Quality

Chlorinated municipal water kills beneficial microorganisms. Dechlorinate
by:
- Aerating water for 1-2 hours before adding compost (most effective)
- Adding ascorbic acid (vitamin C): 1 teaspoon per 100 gallons
- Using rainwater, well water, or reverse osmosis water

Water pH should be between 6.0-7.5. Extremes of pH inhibit microbial
growth. Hard water is generally acceptable.

## Quality Indicators

A successful AACT brew should:
- Smell earthy, sweet, or like fresh compost — NOT sour, putrid,
  sulfurous, or like sewage
- Have a DO level above 6 mg/L at the end of the brew
- Show abundant bacterial biomass under direct microscopy
- Show fungal hyphae (if fungal foods were included) — hyphae should
  be 10+ micrometres in diameter with bright, refractive cytoplasm
