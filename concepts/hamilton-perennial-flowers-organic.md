# Growing Perennial Flowers Organically

updated: 2026-04-18

Perennials are plants with soft stems that generally die down every winter and produce new growth in the spring, lasting for many years. Since perennials number in the hundreds, there is a good selection for every soil type and sun preference. They form the backbone of the ornamental border, providing reliable color year after year with relatively low maintenance.

## Choosing Perennials

Perennials are confusingly sold under many names: "[[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] through late fall)
- Height and spread at maturity (think 10 years ahead)
- Color scheme and combinations
- Sun or shade requirements
- Soil preferences (acid or alkaline, wet or dry)

## Buying Perennials

Most perennials can be raised from seed or propagated by division of an existing clump. They can be bought all year-round as container-grown plants or bare root in the spring and the fall. Check all plants for signs of pests or disease before purchasing.

## Planting

Unlike shrubs, perennials are best planted in the spring, just as new growth is starting. Planted in the fall, they may suffer from cold, frozen soil, or from waterlogging, which can rot the soft parts of the plant. In spring they will start to grow and make roots right away and, provided you water them regularly, they will flourish.

### Planting Technique

1. Remove the plant from its pot and dig a hole large enough to accommodate the root ball
2. Sprinkle a handful of a general fertilizer such as blood, fish, and bone meal around the rim of the hole
3. Place the plant in the hole at the level of the old soil mark on the stem
4. Gently press the soil down
5. Give the plant enough water so a puddle forms on the soil surface (deep soaking)

If you have a large area to fill, plant in groups of three to five to create blocks of color.

## Maintenance

### Feeding

General border feeding should be sufficient to maintain perennials. If you can provide an annual mulch of well-rotted manure or compost, they will require little more. If not, give the whole border a dressing of blood, fish, and bone meal in early spring, and a dressing of [[primula-veris]]), drumstick primrose ([[anemone-blanda]]), hellebores

### Mid-Spring
Forget-me-not (Myosotis alpestris), alyssum, forsythia (shrub), grape hyacinth (Muscari), euphorbia

### Late Spring
Magnolia, clematis, lilac, peony, Solomon's seal, lily-of-the-valley, columbine, catmint, bugle

### Early Summer
Lilac, fuchsia, rosemary, potentilla, escallonia, lupin, delphinium, rose, plantain lily (Hosta)
