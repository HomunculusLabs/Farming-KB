# Seed Sowing Techniques in Organic Gardening

The cheapest method of raising plants is by sowing seeds directly into
prepared soil. This is the method to adopt for most vegetables, hardy
annuals, and many herbaceous perennials. Geoff Hamilton provides
detailed guidance on both outdoor and indoor sowing techniques to
ensure reliable germination and strong seedlings.

## When to Sow

The correct time to sow varies from plant to plant and is normally
shown on the back of the seed packet. There is no point in sowing too
early. Seeds sown in soil below 45 degrees F (7 degrees C) will not
germinate until the soil warms up. Seeds sown in mid-spring will often
germinate at the same time as, or even before, seeds sown earlier in
cold, wet soil. Exceptions include alpine seeds and some trees that
require a period of freezing before germinating.

By sowing seeds under cloches you can start sowing outside in early
spring. Place cloches in position two weeks or so before sowing to
warm the soil up. See [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]]. A greenhouse is ideal, but a windowsill works too. Fill
a pot or tray with moist seed compost, firm it lightly, level the
top, and sow seeds. Cover with vermiculite, then cover the container
with opaque plastic in a warm place. Check daily and remove cover when
the first seedling appears.

## Pre-Germinating Seeds

Some plants benefit from pre-germination. Sow seeds onto moistened
tissue in a plastic container. When the first roots show (no longer
than 1/8 inch), sow them immediately. For small seeds, suspend them in
alginate gel and "sow" the mixture in a seed row.

## Thinning and Transplanting

When seedlings are large enough to handle, thin to prevent
overcrowding. Water rows well before thinning. [[comparison-cold-frame-gardening-vs-container-gardening-guide]].
