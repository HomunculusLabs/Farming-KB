## AACT Nematode and Protozoa Management (Ingham)

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: The Field Guide I for Actively [[ingham-beneficial-nematodes-soil-food-web]] and protozoa in compost, compost tea, and soil — critical components of the soil foodweb that are often overlooked.

## Nematode Types

### Beneficial Nematodes
Three functional groups of beneficial nematodes:

1. **Bacterial-feeding nematodes**: The easiest to culture. Relatively simple to get going. Need old-growth forest conditions for optimal habitat.

2. **Fungal-feeding nematodes**: Require fungal foods in the compost/worm bin. Most [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] for these nematodes — "too active or total fungal biomass" is the problem.

3. **Predatory nematodes**: Feed on other nematodes including root-feeders. Critical for root-knot and lesion [[cover-crops]] biological controls
- Cyst nematodes: Specific to certain crops. Managed through rotation and biological controls

## Managing Nematode Populations

### From Near-100 to Undetectable
Ingham's documented approach has reduced root-feeder nematodes from nearly 100 per gram to less than detectable within 6 months, using:

1. VAM (mycorrhizal) inoculation of root systems
2. [[comparison-free-living-vs-parasitic-nematodes]], the compost must contain fungi that form:
- Tiny lassos
- Fishing-line-like structures
- Adhesive networks

These are produced by certain beneficial fungal species. The best source is forest soil from undisturbed areas. Testing with a Baermann funnel extraction and DIC microscope is needed to confirm presence.

### Root-Knot Nematode Protocol
1. Get soil tested to identify the specific root-feeder
2. Ensure root systems are colonized with VAM fungi (inocula available from EPM, Mycorrhizal Applications)
3. Ensure compost contains trapping fungi (check SFI website for certified composts)
4. Apply beneficial nematodes if present in compost, or collect and add them
5. Use non-host cover crops (consult nematologist for crop-specific recommendations)
6. Document with soil testing to track reduction

## Worm Compost: Best Nematode Habitat

Worm compost (vermicompost) provides ideal conditions for most beneficial nematodes:

- Consistent moisture maintenance (critical — nematodes die if too dry)
- High numbers of bacteria and fungi (food for bacterial- and fungal-feeding nematodes)
- Worm activity modifies bacterial, fungal, and protozoan communities
- Paper and cardboard added to worm bins promotes beneficial fungi
