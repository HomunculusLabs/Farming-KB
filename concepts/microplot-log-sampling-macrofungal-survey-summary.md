## Introduction to Lichenized Fungi (Chapter 9)

*By Susan Will-Wolf, David L. Hawksworth, Bruce McCune, Roger Rosentreter, and Harrie J. M. Sipman.*

### What Are Lichens?

[[lichens]] are **composite organisms** of a **fungal partner (mycobiont)** and a **photosynthetic partner (photobiont)** forming a **coherent thallus**. Through symbiosis, lichenized fungi pursue an **autotrophic, photosynthetic lifestyle** like plants. They are more physically compact, spatially circumscribed, and distinguishable from their substratum than other fungi, making them the subject of **far more quantitative sampling studies** than any other fungal group.

### Diversity

- More than **13,500 species** (~20% of all described fungi; Sipman and Aptroot 2001, Hawksworth et al. 1995).
- Not a cohesive taxonomic group; belong to distantly related orders/families.
- Lichenization is a **biological strategy** evolved independently multiple times.
- Cohesiveness relates to **similar ecological/functional roles** and common constraints of the [[lichen]] habit.

### Symbiotic Relationship

- **Mutualism**: fungus gains carbohydrates; photobiont is protected from desiccation.
- **Controlled parasitism**: photobiont grows more slowly within the thallus than in isolation.
- Ecologically usually **mutualistic**: the thallus survives in more habitats and is more abundant than either partner alone.
- An individual thallus: **one fungal species + one algal or cyanobacterial species**.
- Thousands of fungal species but only ~**150–200 photobiont species**.
- Only the fungal partner reproduces sexually while in association.
- The thallus has a definable boundary, distinctly layered structure, and species-specific morphology.

### Five Morphological Categories

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| **Leprose** | Mealy or powdery particles |
| **Crustose** | Encrusted on or embedded within the substratum surface |
| **Squamulose** | Small, scale-like flakes |
| **Foliose** | Leaf-like with distinct upper and lower surfaces |
| **Fruticose** | Shrubby or string-like, often erect or pendulous |

The **fungal partner** produces the characteristic thallus structure; the photobiont does not determine morphology.
