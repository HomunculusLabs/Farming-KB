## Site Selection and Planting

Citrus trees require well-drained soil and full sun for optimal
production. They are shallow-rooted and sensitive to waterlogging, which
promotes root rot pathogens. Mollison recommends planting citrus on
mounds or raised beds in areas with heavy soils or poor drainage. Sites
should be sheltered from strong winds, which can damage branches, strip
flowers, and increase moisture stress.

Planting pits should be wide rather than deep, encouraging lateral root
development. Incorporating compost and rock phosphate into the backfill
improves establishment. Mulching is essential to retain soil moisture and
suppress weed competition, but Mollison cautions against placing mulch
directly against the trunk to prevent collar rot. Spacing varies by
species and intended use: 4 to 6 metres between standard trees, or 2 to
3 metres for hedgerow plantings.

## Pruning and Canopy Management

Citrus requires less pruning than most deciduous fruit trees, but
strategic canopy management improves light penetration, air circulation,
and harvest access. Mollison recommends removing dead, diseased, and
crossing branches as the primary pruning objective. In humid climates,
opening the canopy centre reduces fungal disease pressure by improving
airflow.
