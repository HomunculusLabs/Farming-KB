# Design Methods and Site Analysis

There is no one true [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]] process follows these major phases:

1. **Get a client and a site** -- the best way to look for Permaculture clients is to be part of a community, such as your local [[holmgren-ecological-succession-and-pulsing]] stages?
- Health needs and dietary preferences
- Physical or mental limitations
- Budget and time frame
- Vision and mission statement for the design
- How hands-on does the client want to be?
- How many people will the design serve? Pets?
- Social habits and intermittent impact patterns

### General Climate Data

Any Permaculture analysis should include solar altitude and azimuth, prevailing seasonal winds, flood plains, average winter and summer temperatures, average annual rainfall, and identification of any microclimates on the base map.

### History of the Site

Permaculture Principle #1 calls for designing from patterns to details, including understanding the historical patterns of the site over time. Research major climactic events and human habitation. Look for historical plants and crops, design precedence from the past. Putting the history together tells the story of the site.

### Indicator Species

Observe the site for plant and animal indicator species. Their presence or absence tells you about the local environment.

### Inventories

Lists of available resources that already exist on the site: plants, animals, potential [[natural-building-materials-guide]], salvageable materials, water sources, people power, community resources, and any other relevant resources.

### Observation and Interaction

Permaculture Principle #1 calls for observation and interaction. The hardcore will tell you to observe for a full year to see how the site acts in each season. Observe as often as possible under all different conditions: day, afternoon, evening, morning, during rain, after rain, when dry. Camp out on the site several times. Do small projects to see what effect they have. Observe with all your senses.

### Sectors

Analytical tool to understand the flow of forces across the site. Presented as a circle centered on Zone 0 with pie slices representing forces.

### Soil Samples

Take soil samples and test infiltration rates from around the site. Create a grid or sample where you observe differences. Clearly number samples and map them onto the working base map.

### Surface Water Flow

Chart the flow of water across the site following topographical information. This determines what earthworks are needed to slow, spread and drop water flow.

### Zones

Analytical graphic tool ranked 0-5 denoting the amount of human impact on an area.
