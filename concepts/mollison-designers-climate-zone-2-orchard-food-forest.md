# Zone 2 - Orchard and Food Forest

Zone 2 in the [[permaculture-design]] system is the area of less frequent but
still regular attention -- visited daily to weekly. Mollison places the main
food-producing perennial systems here: fruit and nut trees, berry bushes, small
livestock (chickens, ducks, goats), and the supporting understory plants that
form a productive food forest. Zone 2 is the bridge between intensive gardening
and broad-scale farming.

## The Food Forest Concept

Mollison draws heavily on Robert Hart's forest garden model and traditional
Asian multi-strata gardens. A mature Zone 2 food forest mimics natural forest
architecture with seven layers:
- Canopy: tall fruit and nut trees (chestnut, walnut, standard apples)
- Understory: dwarf fruit trees, smaller nut trees (almond, filbert)
- Shrubs: berries, currants, gooseberries, fruiting shrubs
- Herbaceous: perennial vegetables, companion herbs, comfrey, artichokes
- Ground cover: strawberries, creeping thyme, nitrogen-fixing clovers
- Root: Jerusalem artichokes, yacon, other edible tubers
- Vine: kiwi, grapes, passionfruit, climbing beans on trellises

This layered structure is detailed in [[food-forest-design]] and relates to
[[mollison-pattern-understanding]] principles of edge and stacking.

## Fruit Tree Selection and Placement

Mollison recommends selecting fruit tree varieties for:
- Extended harvest season (early, mid, late varieties of each species)
- Disease resistance suited to local climate
- Multiple uses (fruit, timber, animal fodder, mulch)
- Rootstock appropriate to soil conditions and desired tree size

Standard-sized trees go on the outer edge of Zone 2; dwarf and semi-dwarf
trees are placed closer to Zone 1 for easier harvesting. Pollination partners
must be included -- most fruit trees require cross-pollination from a
different variety of the same species.

## Nitrogen Fixers and Support Species

A healthy Zone 2 includes 20-30% nitrogen-fixing support species. Mollison
lists deep-rooted trees like tagasaste (tree lucerne), black locust, alder,
and carob as valuable canopy-level fixers. Understory fixers include
clover, lucerne, and groundcover beans. These support species are periodically
chopped for mulch, providing nitrogen and organic matter to food-producing
plants. See nitrogen fixing plants for species lists.

## Berry Production

Berries are high-value Zone 2 crops. Mollison recommends planting a range of
bramble fruits (raspberries, blackberries, boysenberries), bush fruits
(blueberries, currants, gooseberries), and cane fruits. Berberries, elderberry,
and seaberry provide additional yields. Berry bushes serve as edge species
between tree rows, increasing total edge length and habitat diversity.

## Small Livestock Integration

Zone 2 is the primary zone for integrating small livestock into the system:

**Chickens** are the most common Zone 2 animal. Mollison describes mobile
chicken tractors rotated through the orchard, where chickens control pests,
scratch manure into soil, and fertilize trees. Fixed chicken houses with
access to orchard paddocks also work well. Chicken forage includes
tagasaste, comfrey, mulberry, and black soldier fly larvae.

**Ducks** are excellent for orchard pest control, particularly snails and
slugs. They require access to water but integrate well with small ponds that
also serve irrigation and habitat functions.

**Goats** can be integrated for weed control and milk production, but require
strong fencing to protect trees. Mollison recommends careful breed selection
-- smaller dairy goats (Nigerian Dwarf) are more manageable than full-sized
breeds.

## Mulch Crops and Green Manure

Zone 2 produces much of its own fertility through mulch crops. Comfrey
(Symphytum spp.) is Mollison's most frequently recommended mulch plant -- it
draws nutrients from deep subsoil layers, produces large biomass yields (up to
4-5 cuts per year), and its leaves break down rapidly. Other mulch crops
include tagasaste, Italian buckthorn, dock, and Jerusalem artichoke tops.

Green manure crops are sown between tree rows and periodically slashed to
build soil organic matter. This connects to soil building practices and
[[cover-cropping-guide]].

## Understory Planting Design

The understory beneath fruit trees serves multiple functions:
- Pest confusion through aromatic herbs (tansy, wormwood, garlic chives)
- Nutrient accumulation (comfrey, yarrow, dandelion, chicory)
- Insect attraction (umbellifers like dill, fennel, coriander for
  predatory wasps)
- Ground cover and weed suppression (clover, mint family, strawberry)
- Soil protection and moisture retention (strawberry, sweet woodruff)

Mollison calls these plant groupings "guilds" -- see [[gaia-garden-plant-guilds]].

## Water Management in Zone 2

Swales planted along contour capture and infiltrate runoff into the root zone
of food forest trees. Drip irrigation under mulch can supplement rainfall
during establishment. Once mature, a well-designed food forest is largely
self-watering due to canopy interception, mulch retention, and deep root
systems. This relates to [[keyline-design]] principles for broader water
management.

## Pest Management

Zone 2 pest management relies on biological diversity rather than chemical
inputs. Mollison emphasizes habitat creation for predatory insects through
umbelliferous flowers, native insectary plants, and undisturbed ground cover.
Bird perches, bat boxes, and lizard habitat all contribute. Poultry ranging
under trees provides significant pest control.

## Harvest and Processing

Zone 2 crops require less frequent harvesting than Zone 1 but more than
Zone 3. Fruit is harvested seasonally, berries weekly during peak season.
Mollison recommends locating processing and storage facilities (dehydrators,
cider presses, root cellars) at the Zone 1/Zone 2 boundary for convenient
handling of bulk harvests.
