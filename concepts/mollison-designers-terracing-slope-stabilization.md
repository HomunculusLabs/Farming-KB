## Principles of Slope Management

Before constructing terraces, Mollison urges designers to evaluate whether
terracing is the most appropriate strategy. On gentle slopes (under 10
degrees), contour planting with groundcovers and mulch may suffice. On moderate
slopes (10-25 degrees), swales combined with contour planting are often
preferable to full terracing. Terracing becomes essential on steep slopes
(25-45 degrees) where agriculture is desired and on any slope exhibiting active
erosion, landslip, or mass wasting.

The key principle is to reduce the effective slope to a level where water
infiltration exceeds runoff generation. Every terrace is both a water-harvesting
structure and a growing platform.

## Terrace Types

### Bench Terraces

Bench terraces create a level or nearly level platform by cutting into the
slope and using excavated material to build an outer retaining wall. They are
the most productive type, providing flat planting surfaces suitable for row
crops, trees, or intensive gardens. Mollison describes them as the preferred
form for steep agricultural slopes in humid and subhumid regions.

Each bench should slope slightly inward (1-2% grade) toward the backcut to
prevent overflow over the outer wall. A drainage channel or swale at the back
collects excess water and directs it to a safe outlet or the next terrace
below. Bench width depends on slope and intended use: narrow benches (1-3 m)
for tree crops, wider benches (4-8 m) for vegetable production.

### Contour Terraces (Channel Terraces)

Contour terraces are broad, gently graded channels constructed on the contour
with a low ridge on the downhill side. They do not create a level platform but
instead slow and pond water behind the ridge, allowing infiltration. They are
simpler and cheaper to construct than bench terraces and are suited to
pastoral and tree crop systems on moderate slopes.

Contour terraces should have a uniform grade of 0.5-2% to prevent ponding
beyond the infiltration capacity. Water is safely conveyed along the terrace to
a grassed waterway or rock-lined spillway.

### Sloping Terraces (Farm Terraces)

Sloping terraces are narrow ridges thrown up across the slope with a plow or
contour implement. They are the simplest and least expensive form, suitable for
pasture improvement and erosion control on moderate slopes. Mollison considers
them a temporary or transitional measure, best used while establishing permanent
vegetative cover.

## Retaining Walls

Retaining walls hold the outer edge of bench terraces and must resist the
lateral pressure of the soil behind them. Mollison covers several wall types:

**Dry Stone Walls:** The traditional and most durable option. Constructed
without mortar using locally quarried stone. Well-built dry stone walls drain
freely, flex slightly without cracking, and can last centuries. They provide
habitat for lizards, insects, and small mammals. Stone should be placed with
the longest dimension into the wall, courses should be level, and walls should
batter (lean) into the slope at approximately 6:1 ratio (6 cm back for every
1 m height).

## Practical Considerations

Successful implementation of Terracing and Slope Stabilization requires attention to
several practical factors including environmental conditions,
