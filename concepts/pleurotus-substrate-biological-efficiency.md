# Pleurotus Substrate and Biological Efficiency

Biological efficiency (BE) is the standard metric for evaluating
substrate performance in [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]] in terms of substrate
utilization, producing crops on an enormous range of agricultural
wastes and plant materials.

## Understanding Biological Efficiency

The formula for calculating biological efficiency is:

    BE = (fresh weight of mushrooms / dry weight of substrate) x 100

For example, if 1 kg of fresh mushrooms is harvested from a bag
containing substrate with 1.5 kg dry weight, the BE is 67%. This
metric allows comparison across different substrates, cultivation
methods, and strains, though it does not account for differences
in mushroom quality, nutritional content, or number of flushes.

## Substrate Chemistry Fundamentals

[[bloomfield-mushroom-foraging-immigrant-poisonings-paddy-straw-confusion]], one of the most common substrates, contains
approximately 14% lignin, 37% cellulose, 0.4% P2O5, 0.55% total
N, 1.6% K2O, 12% SiO2, and has a C/N ratio of about 70. Wheat
straw contains 1% protein, 13% lignin, 39% hemicellulose, and 40%
cellulose, and can be sourced at USD 0.1-0.2 per kg.

## High-Performing Substrates

Several substrates have demonstrated particularly strong biological
efficiency with Pleurotus species:

- **Soybean stems**: The best substrate for P. sajor-caju in
  Indian trials, achieving 77% BE. Soybean husks and straw also
  performed well for P. ostreatus in Yugoslav trials.
- **Sesame stems**: A satisfactory substrate for P. sajor-caju
  with 60% BE in Indian trials.
- **Spent brewer's grain**: Widely available from breweries, this
  nitrogen-rich material can produce excellent yields when
  properly supplemented.
- **Cotton waste**: When amended with spent Agaricus compost,
  satisfactory cultivation of Volvariella has been achieved; also
  effective for Pleurotus.
- **Coffee pulp**: A widely studied tropical substrate with good
  BE values for multiple Pleurotus species.
- **Banana waste**: Both pseudostem and leaves can serve as
  substrate, achieving up to 90% BE in some formulations.

## Moderate-Performing Substrates

Substrates with moderate biological efficiency include:

- **Sorghum stover**: Selected substrate for P. sajor-caju in
  Africa, used alone or combined with cotton waste.
- **Sawdust substrates**: Various hardwood sawdusts support
  Pleurotus cultivation, though BE varies with wood species and
  supplementation.
- **Willow (Salix) stumps**: Gave slightly higher P. ostreatus
  production compared to poplar stumps in Italian trials.
- **Sugar cane bagasse**: Readily available in tropical regions;
  performance depends on particle size and supplementation.
- **Rubber tree sawdust**: Used successfully in Southeast Asian
  cultivation operations.
- **Groundnut shells**: An [[volvariella-volvacea]].
Successive crops of mushrooms can be grown on spent substrate,
though biological efficiency typically decreases with each cycle.

## Applications and Practical Uses

The principles behind pleurotus substrate biological efficiency have been applied across various
domains, from research laboratories to field-scale operations. Key
applications include integration with existing workflows and adaptation
to specific environmental conditions and goals.

## Key Considerations

Several factors influence the effectiveness and outcomes of
pleurotus substrate biological efficiency. Environmental conditions, timing, and material
selection all play critical roles. Proper preparation and monitoring
