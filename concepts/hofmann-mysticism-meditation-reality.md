## Critique of Ecclesiastical Christianity

Hofmann argues that ecclesiastical Christianity, determined by the
duality of creator and creation, has with its nature-alienated
religiosity largely obliterated the Eleusinian-Dionysian legacy of
antiquity. In the Christian sphere, only special blessed individuals
have attested to a timeless, comforting reality experienced in a
spontaneous vision. In antiquity, by contrast, the elite of
innumerable generations had access to such experience through
initiation at Eleusis. The unio mystica of Catholic saints and the
visions described by Jakob Boehme, Meister Eckhart, Angelus Silesius,
Thomas Traherne, and William Blake are "obviously essentially related
to the enlightenment that the initiates to the [[fukuoka-dutch-priest-vegetable-garden-spiritual-renewal]] of occidental
civilization and culture.

## Meditation as Path to Deeper Reality

As a path to the perception of a deeper, comprehensive reality in which
the experiencing individual is also sheltered, meditation occupies a
prominent place. The essential difference between meditation and prayer
in the usual sense is that meditation aspires to the abolishment of the
I-you-barrier by a fusing of object and subject, of sender and
receiver, of objective reality and self. Prayer, by contrast, is based
upon the duality of creator and creation.

## Scientific Worldview as Modern Myth

"Objective reality, the world view produced by the spirit of scientific
inquiry, is the myth of our time. It has replaced the ecclesiastical
Christian and mythical-Apollonian world view." Yet this factual
knowledge "need not be a desecration." If it advances deep enough, it
inevitably leads to the inexplicable, primal ground of the universe:
the wonder, the mystery of the divine, in the microcosm of the atom,
in the macrocosm of the spiral nebula, in the seeds of plants, in the
body and soul of people.
