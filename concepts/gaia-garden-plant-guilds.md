# Plant Guilds — Designing Productive Plant Communities

From Toby Hemenway's Gaia's Garden, guild design is the art of grouping plants so they support each other's growth, reduce maintenance, and create resilient ecosystems. A guild is a "mutually beneficial group of species."

## What Is a Guild?

A guild is a designed plant community where each member:
- Provides at least one function for other members
- Receives at least one function from other members
- Fills a unique niche (root zone, canopy, ground cover, etc.)

Guilds differ from simple [[comparison-companion-planting-reference-vs-jadam-monoculture-critique]] by being complete, self-supporting systems with specific functional roles.

## The Apple Guild — Hemenway's Master Example

The classic apple-centered guild from Gaia's Garden:

### Core
- **Apple tree** (semi-dwarf for home scale) — the central element, food producer

### Supporters — Suppress weeds, attract pollinators
- **Comfrey** (Symphytum officinale) — [[nutrient-dynamic-accumulator-reference]], mulch producer, bee attractor
- **Dill, fennel, cilantro** — attract parasitic wasps that control codling moth
- **Clover** (white or crimson) — living mulch, nitrogen fixer, bee forage

### Protectors — Repel pests, trap crops
- **Garlic, chives, alliums** — repel apple borers and codling moth
- **Nasturtium** — trap crop for aphids, repels woolly apple aphid
- **Tansy** — repels codling moth, attracts [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]]

### Nutrient Accumulators
- **Comfrey** — deep taproot mines calcium, potassium, phosphorus from subsoil
- **Yarrow** — accumulates phosphorus, potassium, copper
- **Chicory** — deep-rooted accumulator, [[permplant-carob-drought-tolerant-food-tree]]

### Mulchers — Provide biomass for soil
- **Comfrey** — chop 4-5 times per season, leaves decompose rapidly into nutrient-rich mulch
- **Artichokes** — large leaves provide biomass
- **Cardoon** — similar to artichoke, excellent mulch producer

### Nitrogen Fixers
- **White clover** — living ground cover, fixes 100-200 lbs N/acre/year
- **Lupines** — deep-rooted nitrogen fixer, beautiful flowers
- **Pea shrub (Caragana)** — perennial nitrogen-fixing shrub, wind protection

## Designing Custom Guilds

Follow this process for any fruit tree or garden center:

1. **Choose your central element** (fruit tree, vegetable bed, pond edge)
2. **Identify the needs**: pest control, pollination, fertility, mulch, weed suppression
3. **Assign functional roles**:
   - Nitrogen fixers (legumes)
   - [[comparison-dynamic-accumulators-vs-cover-crops]] (deep-rooted herbs)
   - Insectary plants (flowers attracting beneficials)
   - Mulch producers (fast-growing biomass plants)
   - Ground covers (weed suppression, moisture retention)
   - Root crops (break compaction, mine subsoil)
4. **Select species** suited to your climate and each niche
5. **Arrange spatially**: root zone, understory shrub layer, herb layer, ground cover layer
6. **Observe and adjust** over time

## Insectary Guilds

Dedicated guilds for biological pest control:
- **Core plants**: Dill, fennel, yarrow, alyssum, buckwheat, cosmos, zinnias
- **Purpose**: Provide continuous bloom from early spring through late fall
- **Beneficials attracted**: Ladybugs, lacewings, parasitic wasps, hover flies, predatory bugs
- **Design tip**: Plant in clusters of 3-5, not single specimens, so beneficials can find them easily

## Nutrient-Accumulating Guilds

Plants that mine nutrients from deep subsoil and bring them to the surface:
- **Comfrey** (Bocking 14 variety, sterile) — calcium, potassium, phosphorus
- **Yarrow** — phosphorus, potassium, copper
- **Chicory** — calcium, magnesium
- **Dandelion** — calcium, iron, silicon
- **Nettle** — iron, nitrogen, calcium
- Use as chop-and-drop mulch or add to compost for nutrient cycling

See [[dynamic-accumulators]] for a comprehensive list.

## Habitat Guilds

Creating [[query-how-do-i-design-habitat-for-beneficial-insects]] wildlife:
- **Beneficial insect hotels**: Bundles of hollow stems, drilled wood blocks
- **Bee nests**: Bare soil for ground-nesting bees, pithy stems for cavity nesters
- **Rock piles**: Habitat for snakes, lizards, ground beetles
- **Water features**: Even a small dish of water attracts beneficials
- **Bird houses and perches**: Birds are major insect predators

## The Walnut Guild (Allelopathic Guilds)

Walnut trees secrete juglone, a toxic substance that suppresses many competing plants. Designing guilds for walnuts requires special knowledge of juglone-tolerant species. Permaculture designer Tim Murphy developed this guild based on observation of natural walnut-containing plant communities in Arizona.

### Core
- **Walnut tree** (Juglans nigra or related) — central element, provides nuts, timber, shade

### Juglone-Tolerant Companions
- **Hackberry** (Celtis spp.) — shrub that thrives under walnuts; its own allelopathic toxins complement walnut's by suppressing grasses
- **Currants** — found under walnuts only when hackberries are present; benefit from citronella fumes of decomposing walnut leaves that repel aphids
- **Wolfberry** (Lycium spp.) — thorned shrub, member of Solanaceae (nightshade family)
- **Chiltepine** (Capsicum aviculare) — perennial wild chile, also Solanaceae

### The Solanaceae Connection
The Solanaceae family is "narcissistic" — they thrive on the leaf litter of members of their own family. Tomatoes and peppers (also Solanaceae) are less sensitive to juglone than potatoes, making them good guild candidates. Place solanaceous plants in the interstices between shrubs.

### Nitrogen Fixers
- **Russian olive** (Elaeagnus angustifolia) — drought-tolerant N-fixer, insensitive to juglone
- **Goumi** (Elaeagnus multiflora) — edible berries, N-fixing
- **Ceanothus** — native N-fixing shrub
- **Apache plume** (Fallugia paradoxa) — Southwest native N-fixer

### Buffer Plants
Plants placed at the margins to protect neighboring guilds from juglone:
- **Mulberry** — unharmed by walnut, buffers apples from juglone; research shows apples benefit from mulberry companionship
- **Black locust** — N-fixing, rot-resistant timber, bee forage
- **Elderberry** — wildlife food, hedgerow plant
- **Acacia** — N-fixing buffer tree

### Arrangement
Within the roughly 20-foot circle of the walnut's future drip line, plant 1-3 each of hackberry, currant, wolfberry, and Elaeagnus. Scatter solanaceous plants in the interstices (toward brighter margins in northern climates). Radiating outside this circle, place buffer plants.
