# Fermented Plant Extract (FPE)

[[bionutrients-lab-fermented-plant-juice-fpj]] (FPJ) and [[bionutrients-lab-fermented-fruit-juice-ffj]] Juice (FFJ) are core KNF inputs that capture plant nutrients and growth energy. Made by fermenting plant material with sugar, they provide bioavailable nutrients, enzymes, and plant hormones.

FPJ and FFJ are functionally the same process — FPJ uses vegetative plant parts, FFJ uses fruits. The key difference is the sugar content and stage of the source plant.

## FPJ — Fermented Plant Juice

### Purpose
Provides energy, nitrogen, enzymes, and plant growth hormones. Fuels [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] and overall plant vitality. Different plants contribute different properties — see plant selection below.

### Plant Selection (Cho's Guidelines)

**Choose plants that grow vigorously** in your local environment — these are naturally adapted and nutrient-rich:

**Vegetative growth FPJ** (nitrogen-rich, for leafy growth):
- Mugwort (Artemisia) — excellent general-purpose FPJ, high in minerals and medicinal compounds
- Bamboo shoots — vigorous growth, high silicon
- Arrowroot — strong vegetative energy
- Fast-growing wild grasses and weeds
- Comfrey — high potassium, but be cautious of high N during flowering
- Sweet potato vines
- Duckweed — very fast growing, high protein

**[[knf-reproductive-growth-stage]] FPJ/FFJ** (for flowering/fruiting):
- Pumpkin/squash vines
- Yam leaves
- Tomato plant tips

**When NOT to use FPJ from certain plants**:
- Avoid FPJ from cucumber, melon, watermelon, ginseng, and strawberry — these plants already have high sugar content, making fermentation difficult and producing poor quality FPJ
- Red bean and mung bean sprouts: the seed itself has low sugar content but high nitrogen, making FPJ difficult

### FPJ Recipe

**Materials**:
- Fresh plant material (leaves, growing tips, young stems)
- Brown sugar or jaggery (crude/unrefined preferred over white sugar)
- Glass jar with lid

**Ratios**:
- Plant material to sugar: 1:1 by weight (minimum)
- For plants with higher water content, use 1:0.8 (less sugar)
- For plants with lower water content, use 1:1.3 (more sugar)
- Fill jar 2/3 to 3/4 full — leave headroom for expansion

**Process**:
1. **Harvest at dawn** (before sunrise) when plant sugars and energy are highest — plants produce maximum nutrients during night via photosynthesis, peaking at dawn
2. Do NOT wash the plant material — washing removes the [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]] on leaf surfaces that drive fermentation
3. Shake off dust gently if needed
4. Chop or crush plant material to increase surface area (do not blend — preserves microbial diversity)
5. Alternate layers of plant material and sugar in the jar
6. The bottom 1/3 should be mostly sugar (it acts as a filter)
7. Cover jar with breathable paper for first 1-2 days, then close lid loosely
8. Store in cool, dark place
9. Ferment 5-7 days (summer) or 7-10 days (winter)
10. Extract liquid by pressing through cheesecloth or straining
11. Store liquid in glass bottles, label with date and plant source

**Signs of quality**:
- Sweet, pleasant fermented aroma
- Clear golden-amber liquid (not cloudy)
- No putrid or alcoholic smell
- Bubbles of CO2 during active fermentation (normal)

**Storage**: Keep in cool dark place. Lasts 6-12 months. Refrigeration extends life.

### FPJ Application

| Growth Stage | Rate | Purpose |
|-------------|------|---------|
| Vegetative | 1:800-1000 | Leaf growth, energy |
| Changeover | 1:1000 | Transition support |
| Reproductive | 1:1000 (sparingly) | General vitality |
| Seed treatment | 2 ml/L | SES solution |
| Stress recovery | 1:500 | Revive stressed plants |
| Soil drench | 1:500-800 | Feed soil microbes |

Apply as foliar spray early morning or late evening. Never spray in direct sunlight — UV kills microbes and enzymes.

## FFJ — Fermented Fruit Juice

### Purpose
FFJ uses the sugar and nutrients from ripened fruits to provide concentrated energy, especially for reproductive growth (flowering, fruiting). The riper the fruit, the higher the sugar content and the better the FFJ.

### Fruit Selection

**For reproductive growth (fully ripened)**:
- Banana — highest sugar content, excellent for fruiting
- Mango — rich in sugars and growth compounds
- Papaya — good enzyme content
- Peach, apple, grape, strawberry, melon
- Pumpkin — technically a fruit, high energy

**For changeover period (less ripe/sour)**:
- Grapes — acidic when less ripe, helps vegetative-to-reproductive transition
- Papaya (green/yellow) — acid content helps transition
- Mulberry
- Unripe banana or mango

### FFJ Recipe

Same process as FPJ but with fruit:
1. Use fully ripened fruits (for reproductive FFJ) or less-ripe fruits (for changeover FFJ)
2. Remove stems/seeds if large
3. Crush or slice fruit (do not blend)
4. Mix with sugar at 1:1 ratio (ripe fruits have their own sugars, so 1:1 is sufficient; less ripe fruits may need slightly more sugar)
5. Ferment 5-7 days
6. Strain and store

### FFJ Application

| Growth Stage | Rate | Purpose |
|-------------|------|---------|
| Changeover | 1:1000 | Acidic FFJ helps transition |
| Reproductive | 1:1000 | Sugar for fruit/flower development |
| Pre-harvest | 1:1000 | Sweetness promotion |
