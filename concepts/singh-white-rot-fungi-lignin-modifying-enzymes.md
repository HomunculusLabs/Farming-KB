# White-Rot Fungi and Lignin-Modifying Enzymes

White-rot basidiomycetes are the principal organisms capable of complete
[[phanerochaete-chrysosporium]] and remains the most potent lignin-
degrading enzyme known. LiP directly oxidizes aromatic substrates through
long-range electron transfer, generating aryl radicals that undergo various
non-enzymatic reactions including C4-ether cleavage, aromatic ring opening,
and demethoxylation.

[[staycare-veratryl-alcohol-as-lignin-peroxidase-mediator]] (3,4-dimethoxybenzyl alcohol) serves as a [[staycare-fungal-redox-mediator-chemistry-pollutant-degradation]] for
LiP, cycling between oxidized and reduced states to shuttle electrons between
the enzyme active site and bulky substrates that cannot directly access it.
This mediator system greatly expands the range of compounds susceptible to
LiP-catalyzed oxidation.

LiP production is typically induced under nitrogen or carbon limitation, with
peak activity occurring during secondary metabolism. Molecular oxygen serves as
the terminal electron acceptor, with [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] generated by
extracellular oxidases as the obligatory cosubstrate.

## Manganese Peroxidase (MnP, EC 1.11.1.13)

Manganese peroxidase oxidizes Mn(II) to Mn(III), which forms stable complexes
with [[fungal-organic-acid-metal-solubilization]] chelators (oxalate, malonate, lactate). These diffusible
Mn(III)-chelate complexes serve as low-molecular-weight oxidants capable of
penetrating lignin matrices and oxidizing phenolic substructures. The
Mn(III)/Mn(II) redox cycle is central to MnP-mediated degradation.

MnP is more widely distributed among white-rot fungi than LiP and plays a
particularly important role in degradation of phenolic compounds and
[[fungal-biodegradation-chlorinated-aromatics-and-btex]]. It is essential for [[singh-bioreactor-design-for-fungal-bioremediation]] systems.
- **Biopulping**: LME pretreatment of [[fukuoka-textdoc-sawdust-wood-chips-orchard-soil-acidification-experiment]] reduces energy requirements
  in mechanical pulping while decreasing chemical usage.
- **Textile processing**: Laccase-based bleaching of denim and decolorization
  of textile effluents, offering eco-friendly alternatives to chlorine-based
  processes.
- **Food and beverage**: Laccase applications in wine stabilization, beer
  clarification, and juice processing through phenolic oxidation.
- **Biosensors**: Immobilized laccase electrodes for detecting phenolic
  compounds in environmental monitoring.
