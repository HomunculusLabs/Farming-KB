### Mulch and Organic Matter

Many vine crops produce substantial biomass. When pruned or at the end
of their productive life, vine material contributes to mulch and compost
systems. Beans and other leguminous vines additionally fix nitrogen.

### Pest Management

Some vine crops serve pest management functions. Certain cucurbits
repel specific pests, while the dense foliage of vine canopies provides
habitat for predatory insects. Passionfruit vines, for example, can
shelter main crop potatoes while their berries are loved by birds that
also control insect pests.

## Vine Species in Specific Contexts

### Tropical House and Garden Systems

In the wet-dry tropics, vine crops form part of a carefully layered
system. The typical tropical polyculture includes groundcovers, herb
layers, shrub layers, tree crops, and vine crops occupying the spaces
between. Mollison emphasises that vine crops, are very economical for
watering, and leave a minimal ground footprint while producing
substantial yields.

### Desert and Arid Systems

In arid environments, vine crops on walls and fences provide shade for
buildings and animal shelters while producing food. A kiwifruit vine
crop on shade structures can serve both functions simultaneously.

### Cool Climate Systems

In cooler climates, vine crops such as grapes and kiwifruit require
specific trellis and training systems. Mollison notes that dwarf fruit,
vine, and flower crops will be barren without adequate wind protection,
making windbreak design a prerequisite for successful vine production.

- Substantial trellis can be a more immediate alternative to tree
  windbreaks, but should be made sinuous (if of brick or mud brick) or
  zig-zagged (if of timber) to withstand persistent wind forces.

## Integration with Animal Systems

Vine crops integrate well with animal systems in several ways:
- Pigeon lofts can be positioned beneath vine-covered trellises
- Poultry forage for insects among vine foliage
- The fruit of some vines provides supplementary feed for poultry and
  pigs
- Vine-covered structures provide shade for animal shelters

## Design Considerations

When planning vine crops in polyculture, Mollison emphasises several
factors:
- Choose species suited to the climate and the supporting structure
- Ensure the supporting structure can bear the weight of mature vines
  laden with fruit
- Plan for access to harvest, pruning, and maintenance
- Consider the shade effects on neighbouring crops
