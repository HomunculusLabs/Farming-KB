## Microdissection and Mosaic Colonization Patterns

Microdissection studies have revealed that endophyte occupation is far more
heterogeneous than macroscopic isolation suggests. Bissegger and Sieber (1994)
divided host leaf tissue into 25 micro-units and found eight distinct endophyte
species distributed across these minute compartments. This discontinuous mosaic
pattern demonstrates that individual leaves can harbor multiple endophyte
species simultaneously, each occupying its own micro-niche. The mosaic
distribution likely reflects competitive interactions, spatial and temporal
variation in tissue quality, and stochastic colonization events. This has
profound implications: bulk tissue homogenization can obscure the true
diversity present in a single leaf.

## Sampling Guidelines and Best Practices

Effective endophyte surveys require careful [[macrofungal-sampling-design-plots-transects]]. Key principles
from decades of research include:

- **Smaller sampling units recover greater diversity**: Dividing material into
  smaller fragments increases detection of rare or spatially restricted
  endophyte species that might be overwhelmed by faster-growing species.

- **Older foliage supports more species**: As leaves age, they accumulate
  colonists. Mature and senescent leaves harbor richer communities than
  young, newly expanded foliage.

- **Perennials support greater diversity than annuals**: Longer lifespan
  provides more time for colonization and greater tissue diversity.

- **Sampling many host species is more productive than extensive single-host
  sampling**: [[fungal-endemism-host-specificity]] is common, so surveying multiple species
  captures more total [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungal-diversity]].

## Sample Collection and Processing

Samples should be processed within 48 hours of collection to prevent
desiccation or overgrowth by saprobic contaminants. Between collection and
processing, material should be air-dried rather than refrigerated in sealed
containers. Recommended storage includes cotton bags, Tyvek envelopes, or paper
bags, all allowing airflow while protecting from physical damage. Sealed
plastic bags should be avoided because they trap moisture and promote surface
mold and bacterial growth that can overwhelm or kill endophytes before
isolation.

cladosporioides* are extremely common, though they also occur as epiphytes on
plant surfaces, raising questions about whether some isolates represent true
internal colonists or surface contaminants. *Fusarium* species are frequently
recovered but seldom dominant, with roles ranging from latent pathogens to
mutualists enhancing host fitness.

## Microfungi Life Strategies: Adaptation of Grime's CSR Framework

The [[fungal-life-history-strategies]] of microfungi, including endophytes, have been
conceptualized using frameworks adapted from plant ecology. Grime's (1977) CSR
model was adapted for fungi by Pugh (1980) and refined by Cooke and Rayner
(1984). Three primary strategies are recognized:

1. **Ruderal**: Fungi adapted to disturbed environments, characterized by rapid
  growth and reproduction. Ruderal species colonize newly available substrata
  quickly, produce abundant spores, and have short generation times.

2. **Competitive**: Fungi adapted to productive, resource-rich environments
  where the primary challenge is outcompeting other organisms. Competitive
  species are fast-growing, aggressive colonizers capable of dominating
  substrata under favorable conditions.

3. **Stress-tolerant**: Fungi adapted to persistent, stressful conditions
  including low nutrients, extreme temperatures, or desiccation. Stress-
  tolerant species grow slowly but persist where ruderal and competitive fungi
  cannot survive. They are enzymatically competent, capable of degrading
  recalcitrant substrates.

Many endophytes, particularly those inhabiting exposed leaf tissue subject to
fluctuating conditions, exhibit stress-tolerant characteristics, enabling
persistence within variable microenvironments of living plant tissues while
maintaining growth capacity when conditions permit.
