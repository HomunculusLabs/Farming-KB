## Desertification and Soil Salting

Desertification is fundamentally a **land-use problem**, hence a political problem, reflecting the priorities of governments. While often attributed to long-term climatic change, the primary drivers are human activities that degrade soil and vegetation systems beyond their capacity for self-repair.

## Causes of Desertification

Le Houerou (1968) identified these causes, independent of any long-term or cyclic climatic change:

### Primary Causes
1. **Overgrazing (well-drilling enabled)**: Herds cannot adjust to climatic factors when maintained at high numbers by artificial water supplies, persisting until well into drought
2. **Overgrazing (vegetation stress)**: Stressing vegetation beyond recovery, especially near wells
3. **Fuel use**: Removing timbers cut but not replanted; fencing also takes thousands of trees; frequent burning
4. **Settling of nomads**: Local grazing and fuel-cutting stress creates zones of destruction centred on wells; no regeneration is possible as people are no longer nomadic
5. **Cropping**: Often a result of failing herd support and population increase; extends down to 15 cm rainfall, whereas 40 cm is the accepted lower limit for dependable cropping
6. **Extension of clearing**: Following low crop returns, less fallow; greatly increases soil compaction and topsoil loss
7. **Excessive water use** (modern phenomenon): Deep wells and powered pumps dry up upper aquifers; local excess water use leads to salination of surface soils
8. **Surface drying**: Desert becomes irreversibly abandoned when oases dry up due to groundwater removal

### Additional Contributing Factors
- **Wind effects**: Drying, wind erosion, and sand-blasting damage to plants
- **Soil effects**: [[cyclic-salt]]**: Brought in by sea winds as rain nucleii; provides plant nutrients but can form salt ponds or crusts if evaporated
- **[[drip-irrigation]]
- The intractable problem: **disposal of water from drains**

### Dryland Salting
The more insidious and widespread problem:
- **Not related to arid-area irrigation**
- Occurs anywhere we crop or graze arid areas
- Self-generating into even sub-humid areas of up to 100 cm average rainfall
- Occurs anywhere that **evaporation equals or exceeds precipitation**

## Two Competing Theories of Dryland Salting

### Theory 1: Groundwater Rising
- Salt rises from deep soil reserves due to tree removal
- Permanent trees pump out rising groundwaters, keeping salt water tables below damage levels
- Solution: plant trees to "pump down" water and salt
- This model presumes deep soil waters freely rise to the surface by infiltration, evaporation, and capillary action

### Theory 2: Soil Collapse (W.A.L.S.A.L.T. Model)
Developed by Harry Whittington of Brookton, West Australia, this theory explains desertification through **soil structural failure**:

**Primary causes of soil collapse:**
- Clearing of vegetation
- Cultivation
- Hoofed animal compaction

**Secondary cause:**
- Swamping of compacted, damaged soils by surface (overland) flow of rainwater carrying small quantities of salt from cloud or raindrop nucleii

**The sodium deflocculation mechanism:**
1. Salt (sodium chloride) releases chloride ions (rapidly washed away) and sodium ions
2. Sodium ions bind to clay crumb particles and **displace calcium ions**
3. When sodium exceeds 15% of exchangeable cations, rapid **deflocculation** occurs — the clay crumb structure disintegrates
4. Not only is the soil surface sealed, but deeper clay particles and minerals migrate and cement the subsoil into a **hydrophobic (water-repellent) block** of rock-like consistency
5. This creates an ever-increasing **swamping cascade** — rapid deep infiltration is blocked, surface soils flood easily in rain

**The hot wind effect:**
- Clearings over 500 ha (more than 5 km across) experience hot wind drying
- Newly cleared land degrades in about **20 years**
- Older farms (clearings in the bush) can take **100-150 years** to degrade

The W.A.L.S.A.L.T. society (Whittington Interceptor Salt-Affected Land Treatment Society), founded March 1978, grew to **4,100 farmer-members** who train consultants and contractors to build interceptor banks correctly. Bulldozers are even modified to create a 1-metre blade drop for delving ditches and ramming banks to seal them.

## Key Principles

1. **Desertification is preventable**: Early signs must be heeded
2. **Trees are essential**: Their removal triggers cascading soil failure
3. **Soil structure matters**: Deflocculation is the critical mechanism, not just water table rise
4. **Scale accelerates degradation**: Large clearings degrade much faster than small ones
5. **Sodium is the enemy**: When sodium displaces calcium in clays, soil dies
6. **Collaborative action works**: Farmer organisations like W.A.L.S.A.L.T. demonstrate that community-scale response is effective
