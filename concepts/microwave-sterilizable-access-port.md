# Microwave Sterilizable Access Port

The Microwave Sterilizable Access Port (MSAP) is a three-subsystem device conceived at NASA Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center in Houston, Texas, to solve a critical contamination problem in biologically sensitive closed systems. The technology was developed collaboratively by James E. Atwater (Technical Director), Neil D. Streech (Project Engineer), and Frank C. Garmon (Microbiologist), and formally documented under NASA Tech Brief MSC-22484 as part of the Technology Transfer Program established to make aerospace developments available for wider technological, scientific, and commercial applications.

The MSAP represents an entirely unique application of microwave energy from the electromagnetic spectrum, specifically engineered for the purpose of aseptic surface sterilization at access ports and mating fixtures on biologically sensitive systems. The proposed unit uses microwave energy to sterilize all mating surfaces before and after specimen transfer, achieved using a combination of microwave reflective and transparent materials in conjunction with control of radiation patterns and subsystem geometries for sufficient exposure of all desired surfaces.

## The Core Problem Motivating Development

Biologically sensitive systems, including Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) waters used in spacecraft and in-flight biological experiments, require reliable aseptic access for removing samples, extracting products, or adding materials to sterile or susceptible systems. The ability to aseptically perform these operations has always been compromised by the lack of a reliable means of sterilizing the mating fixtures at the point of system access.

Every time a connection is made or broken between external equipment and a sterile interior, the mating surfaces at that interface represent a potential contamination pathway. If these surfaces are not completely sterilized both before and after the transfer event, microorganisms can cross the barrier and compromise the biological integrity of the enclosed system. This contamination risk is unacceptable for spaceflight applications where mission-critical biological experiments and life support water systems must maintain strict sterality throughout extended missions.

## Limitations of Traditional Sterilization Techniques

Each of the conventional surface sterilization approaches available at the time of MSAP development carried significant drawbacks that made them individually unsuitable for repeated in-line access port sterilization on biologically sensitive systems:

- **Autoclaving**: Provides the most reliable sterilization assurance but produces excessive thermal impact on vulnerable systems and requires extended cycle times impractical for frequent access operations
- **Gamma irradiation**: Demands specialized heavily shielded facilities and can damage sensitive biological materials and electronic components through radiolysis
- **Chemical disinfection**: Methods including ethylene oxide gas, alcohols, quaternary ammonium compounds, hydrogen peroxide, and iodine all introduce foreign chemical contaminants that cannot be tolerated in closed biological systems
- **UV irradiation**: Rapid and residue-free but suffers from an inherent line-of-sight limitation preventing it from reaching shadowed areas, crevices, and complex surface geometries typical of valve ports and mating fixtures

The three subsystems work together to provide complete aseptic transfer capability while maintaining the sterility of the enclosed system throughout the access cycle. The innovative means of surface sterilization using microwave irradiation stems directly from the initial stage of the MSAP development effort, with the surface sterilization technology being a spinoff innovation from the larger access port system design.

## Three Subsystem Architecture

The MSAP addresses these combined limitations through an integrated three-subsystem design that uses microwave energy to sterilize all mating surfaces before and after specimen transfer.

### Subsystem 1: In-Line Valve Port Assembly

The in-line valve port assembly serves as the permanent interface mounted directly to the biologically sensitive system, providing a sealed connection point that mates with both the portable microwave sterilization chamber and the specimen transfer assembly. The port must maintain an absolute sterile barrier on the system side at all times during normal operation, opening only after all mating surfaces have been sterilized through microwave irradiation.

The valve port assembly is designed to withstand repeated sterilization cycles without degradation of sealing integrity. Materials are carefully selected for both biological compatibility and appropriate microwave dielectric properties to support the sterilization function. The port geometry is engineered in conjunction with the microwave system to ensure all critical mating surfaces receive adequate microwave field exposure during the sterilization cycle.

### Subsystem 2: Portable Microwave Sterilization Chamber

The portable microwave sterilization chamber is the central subsystem that couples to the valve port assembly and contains the complete microwave irradiation equipment needed to sterilize all mating surfaces. The chamber design employs a carefully engineered combination of microwave-reflective and microwave-transparent materials, with controlled radiation patterns and subsystem geometries ensuring sufficient microwave exposure of all desired surfaces.

The microwave generation and delivery system within the chamber consists of:

- **Power supply**: Provides regulated electrical power to the microwave generator
- **Magnetron oscillator**: Generates 2.45 GHz microwave radiation
- **Rectangular waveguide**: Conducts electromagnetic energy to the treatment zone
- **Waveguide-to-coaxial adapter**: Interfaces between waveguide and antenna system
- **Coaxial power splitter**: Distributes energy to multiple treatment points
- **Dipole antennas**: Provide uniform field distribution across mating surfaces

The complete system delivers microwave energy at an exposure rate of 3.6 watts per square centimeter of surface area, with a total exposure of 13.1 watt-hours proven effective for complete sterilization.

### Subsystem 3: Specimen Transfer Assembly

The specimen transfer assembly provides the mechanical means to move materials in and out of the sterile system through the sterilized access port. All surfaces of the transfer assembly that will contact the sterile system interior must pass through the microwave sterilization process both before entry and after removal from the system, ensuring bidirectional contamination prevention.

The transfer assembly is designed to be compatible with the microwave chamber geometry so that all external surfaces receive adequate field exposure during the sterilization cycle, including areas that may be recessed or otherwise difficult to reach with line-of-sight sterilization methods.

## Operational Sequence

The MSAP operational cycle proceeds through carefully defined phases to maintain sterility throughout the access event:

1. The portable microwave sterilization chamber is coupled to the in-line valve port assembly on the biologically sensitive system
2. Microwave irradiation at 2.45 GHz sterilizes all mating surfaces between the chamber and the port, using a total exposure of 13.1 watt-hours delivered at 3.6 watts per square centimeter, with trace water present at approximately 9 microliters per square centimeter to ensure sporicidal efficacy
3. Once sterilization is confirmed, the specimen transfer assembly is introduced through the sterile connection point
4. The sample or material transfer operation occurs through the aseptic pathway
5. The transfer assembly is withdrawn and the connection is re-sterilized through a second microwave exposure cycle before the chamber is decoupled from the port

This sequence ensures that every surface exposed during the transfer process is sterilized both before and after the access event, maintaining the biological integrity of the enclosed system. The bidirectional sterilization approach provides redundancy, as any organisms that might have been introduced during the transfer phase are eliminated by the post-transfer sterilization cycle before the system is resealed.

## Microwave System Components

The microwave generation and delivery system within the sterilization chamber is specifically designed for surface treatment applications. Unlike conventional microwave ovens that use a resonant cavity to create a diffuse field, the MSAP uses a directed waveguide and antenna system to deliver controlled microwave energy to specific mating surfaces. This directed approach provides several advantages including higher power density at the treatment surface, more predictable exposure uniformity, and the ability to treat specific surfaces while shielding adjacent components.

The system uses a magnetron oscillator as the microwave source, which is the same type of device used in consumer microwave ovens but operated under more carefully controlled conditions. The magnetron output is coupled into a rectangular waveguide that conducts the electromagnetic energy to the treatment zone with minimal radiation losses.

## Engineering Significance

The MSAP engineering approach demonstrates several important principles applicable beyond aerospace systems:

- **Material combination strategy**: Using microwave-reflective and microwave-transparent materials together allows precise spatial control of which surfaces receive irradiation and which are shielded from the field
- **Waveguide and antenna engineering**: Careful design of the electromagnetic delivery system ensures uniform field distribution across complex mating geometries that would create shadow zones with line-of-sight methods
- **Portable chamber design**: Enables a single sterilization unit to serve multiple access points throughout a facility or spacecraft, reducing equipment redundancy and cost
- **Bidirectional sterilization**: The system sterilizes both before and after each access event, providing two independent sterilization assurances per transfer cycle

## Sterilization Performance Parameters

The NASA documentation provides specific quantitative parameters for the sterilization protocol used in the MSAP system. Total microwave exposure of 13.1 watt-hours at an exposure rate of 3.6 watts per square centimeter of surface area has been proven effective at surface sterilization. Initial surface populations of approximately 2 times 10 to the 5th power colony forming units (CFU) have been reduced to zero after this standardized exposure protocol.

The sterilization effectiveness depends on three primary variables identified in the study: the duration and intensity of microwave exposure, the amount of water present on the surface, and the kind and number of microorganisms present. The trace water introduction system within the chamber delivers precisely controlled amounts of water to the surfaces being sterilized, ensuring reliable and repeatable spore destruction across multiple sterilization cycles.

## Broader Applications

For pharmaceutical manufacturing, bioprocessing facilities, and biological research laboratories, the MSAP concept offers a pathway to rapid, chemical-free, low-thermal-impact sterilization of access ports and transfer connections. The principle of using microwave irradiation with controlled trace water addition to sterilize complex mating surfaces is broadly applicable to [[sterilization-techniques-mushroom-cultivation]], [[aseptic-inoculation-technique-fungal-cultures]], and [[sterile-cultivation-technique]] where contamination prevention during material transfer is essential.

Additional potential application domains include:

- **Pharmaceutical aseptic processing**: Sterilizing connections between bioreactors and downstream processing equipment without chemical residues
- **Food and beverage processing**: Sanitizing sealed pipe connections and valve ports between production batches
- **Hospital and clinical environments**: Sterilizing access ports on isolation systems and containment equipment
- **Cleanroom operations**: Maintaining sterile barriers during material passage through controlled environments
- **Laboratory research**: Enabling aseptic sampling from closed culture systems without disassembly
