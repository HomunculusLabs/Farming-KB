# JADAM Vegetable Farming Techniques

## Overview

JADAM vegetable farming applies the same foundational principles as all [[knf-seed-treatment-and-soil-foundation]] with JMS, co-existence with weeds, [[jeavons-natural-pest-control]]icides -- with specific attention to greenhouse management, transplanting techniques, and vegetable-specific pest control.

## Soil Foundation for Vegetables

### Pre-Transplanting (Critical)
Apply soil foundation builder 3-4 times before transplanting. For 0.33 ha (0.8 ac):

- JMS -- 500 L (132 gal)
- Sea salt -- 1.5 kg (3.3 lb)
- Phyllite solution -- 60 L (16 gal)
- GC-JLF -- 60 L (16 gal)

**For greenhouses:** Mix with generous amount of water so inputs reach deeper than 1 meter (3.3 ft). Fully soak the soil.

**For open fields:** Apply mixed with water or undiluted before rain. Rainwater carries microbes and nutrients deep into the soil.

### During Growth
- Apply JMS with every watering/irrigation
- Supplement with seawater/salt and phyllite solution
- Apply 3-4 times per month
- For greenhouse vegetables: JMS is essential because rainwater does not fall -- repeat with every irrigation

## Transplanting

The moment of transplanting is the most critical in vegetable farming. Root settlement at this point determines yield. JADAM methods ensure "root settlement will be robust as never before."

### Seedling Treatment
- Soak seedlings in JMS dilution or leaf mold solution for 2-3 minutes before planting
- Or use: JMS 100 L + water to make 500 L; soak 2-3 minutes

### Root Zone Problems
- In conventional farming, pepper roots are often found "coiled up" -- desperate to stay in the nursery soil
- This happens because the field soil is compacted and hostile to roots
- JADAM soil foundation eliminates this problem by creating ideal conditions before transplanting

## Crop Residue Management

Vegetable crop residues are the best fertilizer for that vegetable:
- **Cucumber leaves** are not a source of disease -- they contain nutrients for cucumbers. Thin thread-like roots approach decomposing leaves to absorb nutrients.
- **Tomato vines, pepper plants** -- all should be returned to the soil
- Do NOT remove or burn crop residues
- The practice of "field sanitation" (removing all residues) removes nutrients from the soil and increases pesticide dependency

## Greenhouse Management

### Key Differences from Open Fields
- No rainwater falls -- must add JMS with every irrigation
- Temperature control is important -- culture JMS at greenhouse temperature
- Pests can build up faster in enclosed space
- Herbal smokers are particularly useful in winter (avoids humidity from water-based sprays)

### Irrigation with JMS
- Mix JMS into irrigation water every time
- Wet soil fully and deeply
- Supplement with seawater/salt and phyllite solution
- If using foliar application in greenhouse, filter JMS thoroughly and always use JWA

### Winter Greenhouse Pest Control (Herbal Smoker)
- Use oil tin can with holes punched on sides and bottom
- Fill with tobacco, gingko, peppermint, clove, or Korean mint
- Spray water on herb for more smoke production
- Place 1-2 smokers per 330 sq m
- Smoke after 7 PM on cloudy day; close greenhouse
- Remove beehives before smoking; return after ventilation next morning
- Do not use tobacco near harvest (smell on fruit)
- Spray JMS afterward to remove smell quickly

## Fertilizer Programs for Vegetables

### Basic Additional Fertilizer (per 0.33 ha, continuous)
- JMS -- 500 L + Seawater -- 60 L + Phyllite solution -- 60 L + GC-JLF -- 30 L
- Use seawater 3-4 times per month

### Body Growth Promoter
- Above + Food scrap JLF -- 60 L (or urine, feces, fish JLF)

### Foliar Application (if needed)
- Reduce total JMS + JLF to below 30 L per 500 L solution
- Add over 1.5 L JWA to prevent marks on leaves/fruits
- If using more than 20 L JMS, JWA will be broken down

## Pest and Disease Control

### Powdery Mildew, Downy Mildew, Fungal Diseases (Formula #4)
- JWA -- 3 L + JS -- 1.2 L + water to make 500 L
- Apply in early stage of disease outbreak
- Do not use JS throughout season; only 2-3 times when disease present
- For greenhouse: start from 0.5 L JS; increase in 0.2 L increments
- Caution: JS can cause concentration damage to young, soft leaves in greenhouses

### General Insect Control (Formula #12)
- JWA -- 5-10 L + KPFR JHS -- 5-10 L + water to make 500 L
- For greenhouse: start from lower concentrations
- For stinkbugs: increase JWA and JHS amounts
- Effective against: stinkbug, thrips, pear psylla, aphid, cucumber moth, onion fly maggot, turnip moth

### Concentration Guidelines
- JWA: 3-8 L per 500 L (standard); up to 10 L for serious infestation (once only)
- JS: start from 0.5 L (greenhouse) or 1 L (open field); increase in 0.2 L increments
- JHS: increase together with JWA for stronger effect
- Grapes, persimmons, walnuts are sensitive to JS

## Customized JLF for Specific Vegetables

The "nutritional perspective" means the best fertilizer for a vegetable is made from that vegetable:

| Vegetable | JLF Material | Dilution |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Tomato | Tomato fruits/vines | 20-300x |
| Strawberry | Strawberry fruits/runners | 20-300x |
| Cucumber | Cucumber vines/fruits | 20-300x |
| Pepper | Pepper plants/fruits | 20-300x |
| General | Wild grass + crop residues | ~100x |
| Nitrogen boost | Food waste | 30-500x |

### Food Waste JLF
- Nitrogen content ~20x higher than wild grass JLF
- Dilution: 30-500x
- Use in early to mid-growth period
- Estimated 2,000 L per household per year
- An excellent way to recycle kitchen waste

## Specific Vegetable Notes

### Strawberry
- Use strawberry JLF throughout growth
- Apply both soil and foliar
- Takes over 1 month to make
- Watch for powdery mildew and grey mold (Formula #4)

### Cucumber/Melon
- Watch for powdery mildew (Formula #4)
- Return cucumber leaves to soil -- they contain perfect cucumber nutrition
