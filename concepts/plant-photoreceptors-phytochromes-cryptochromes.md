# Plant Photoreceptors: Phytochromes, Cryptochromes, and Beyond

Plants detect and respond to light through a sophisticated array of photoreceptor
proteins, each tuned to specific wavelength ranges and mediating distinct physiological
responses. Unlike animals, which concentrate photoreception in specialized organs (eyes),
plants distribute light-sensing proteins throughout their tissues — in leaves, stems,
roots, and even seeds. This distributed architecture allows every part of the plant to
independently monitor its light environment.

## Overview of Plant Photoreceptor Families

| Photoreceptor | Wavelength Range | Primary Functions |
|---|---|---|
| Phytochromes | Red (660nm) / Far-red (730nm) | [[cannabis-seed-germination]], flowering |
| Cryptochromes | Blue (320-500nm) / UV-A | Circadian clock, flowering, de-etiolation |
| Phototropins | Blue (390-500nm) | Phototropism, chloroplast movement |
| UVR8 | UV-B (280-315nm) | UV protection, DNA repair activation |

## Phytochromes: The Red/Far-Red Switches

Phytochromes are the most extensively studied plant photoreceptors and play central
roles in nearly every aspect of plant development.

### Molecular Structure

Phytochromes are dimeric proteins consisting of:

- **Chromophore-binding domain:** Covalently bound to a linear tetrapyrrole
  (phytochromobilin) chromophore that absorbs light
- **PAS and GAF domains:** Involved in [[fungal-environmental-sensing-signal-transduction]]
- **Histidine kinase-related domain:** The output module that transmits the light
  signal to downstream signaling components
- **C-terminal output domain:** Interacts with signaling partners (PIFs, COP1, etc.)

### The Pr/Pfr Photoconversion

Phytochrome exists in two stable, photo-interconvertible forms:

- **Pr (P660):** The "resting" form that absorbs [[arabidopsis-thaliana]]*, five phytochrome genes (PHYA-PHYE) have specialized roles:

- **PhyA:** Light-labile; mediates very-low-fluence responses and far-red high-irradiance
  responses; critical for seedling de-etiolation
- **PhyB:** Light-stable; primary phytochrome for shade detection and photoperiodic
  flowering in mature plants
- **PhyC-E:** Specialized roles in specific developmental contexts

## Cryptochromes: Blue Light Sensors

Cryptochromes are flavoprotein photoreceptors that detect blue and UV-A light.
They share evolutionary ancestry with bacterial DNA photolyase enzymes — enzymes
that repair UV-damaged DNA using blue light energy. While plant cryptochromes
lost their DNA repair function, they retained the light-absorbing flavin chromophore
and evolved new signaling roles.

### Structure and Mechanism

- Contain a flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD) chromophore and a methenyltetrahydrofolate
