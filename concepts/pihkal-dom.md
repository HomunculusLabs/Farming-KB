# DOM

## Overview

DOM (4-methyl-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine), also known as STP ("Serenity,
Tranquility, and Peace"), is one of the most infamous compounds in Shulgin's
PIHKAL catalog. Listed as compound #35, DOM is a substituted amphetamine with
extremely potent and long-lasting psychedelic effects. It holds a unique place
in psychedelic history due to a widely publicized 1967 overdose incident that
nearly derailed [[mckenna-suppression-psychedelic-research-lsd-therapy]].

DOM represents the alpha-methylated version of 2C-D. Its effects persist for
14 to 20 hours or more, making it one of the longest-acting classical
psychedelics. This extreme duration, combined with high potency, demands the
utmost respect and caution. Despite its controversial history, DOM remains
pharmacologically interesting for understanding structure-activity
relationships of psychedelic amphetamines.

## Chemical Structure and Classification

DOM's IUPAC name is 1-(4-methyl-2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)propan-2-amine. It
belongs to the DO (dimethoxyamphetamine) family, which includes DOB (bromine),
DOI (iodine), DOC (chlorine), DOM (methyl), and DON (nitro), each
distinguished by the 4-position substituent.

DOM's relationship to 2C-D is direct: adding an alpha-methyl group to 2C-D
yields DOM. This methylation dramatically increases potency and duration, a
consistent pattern throughout Shulgin's work. The alpha-methyl group provides
resistance to monoamine oxidase metabolism, the primary reason for extended
duration.

## Dosage and Administration

Shulgin's recommended oral dosage is 3 to 10 milligrams, with 5 to 8 mg
considered standard. The low active dose range underscores DOM's high potency.
Doses above 10 mg are strongly discouraged.

Onset occurs within 1 to 2 hours, peaking at 3 to 5 hours. Total duration
ranges from 14 to 20 hours, with residual effects persisting up to 24 hours.
The long onset can be deceptive, leading users to take additional doses
before feeling full effects, a dangerous practice that can produce
unexpectedly intense and prolonged experiences.

## Effects Profile

DOM produces classic psychedelic effects including intense visual
hallucinations, altered time and space perception, and profound cognitive
shifts. Visual intensity is comparable to moderate-dose LSD. Cognitive
effects include deep philosophical thinking, [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]], and mystical
or transcendental experiences.

Physical effects are pronounced: significant stimulation, pupil dilation,
elevated heart rate and blood pressure, appetite loss, and insomnia. The
prolonged stimulation can be physically exhausting, with many users feeling
drained for a day or more afterward.

## Shulgin's Commentary

DOM's story is among the most dramatic in Shulgin's career. Originally
developed in the early 1960s and tested by Dow Chemical, DOM's notoriety
came from a 1967 incident where 20 mg tablets were distributed at a San
Francisco love-in under the name STP. This massive overdose (Shulgin had
determined 3 to 5 mg as appropriate) resulted in terrifying 16 to 24 hour
ordeals, hospitalizations, and widespread media coverage.

Shulgin expressed deep frustration about this incident, as the compound
was not inherently dangerous at appropriate doses. In PIHKAL, he used DOM's
story as a powerful illustration of why careful dosage determination is
essential for any new psychoactive compound.

## Pharmacology

DOM acts as a potent agonist at [[serotonin]] 5-HT2A receptors, with additional
activity at 5-HT2B and 5-HT2C. Binding affinity is in the low nanomolar
range. The alpha-methyl group provides metabolic resistance against MAO,
resulting in extraordinarily slow hepatic processing through alternative
pathways including aromatic hydroxylation and conjugation.
