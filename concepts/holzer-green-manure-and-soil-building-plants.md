# Holzer Green Manure and Soil Building Plants

Sepp Holzer places enormous importance on soil building through green manure crops, permanent plant cover, and minimal soil disturbance. His approach is detailed in [[holzer-green-manure-crop-compendium]] and is central to both his [[holzer-kitchen-garden-layout-and-traditional-gardening]] and [[holzer-grafting-fruit-forest]] practices.

## Philosophy of Soil Building

Holzer's core soil-building principle: nature never leaves soil bare. In forests, a continuous layer of leaves, twigs, and decomposing organic matter blankets the earth. This layer protects soil life, retains moisture, prevents erosion, and slowly releases nutrients. Holzer replicates this natural pattern using green manure crops and permanent plant covers.

Key principles:
- Never leave soil exposed to sun, rain, or frost
- Manure goes on the surface, never buried deep -- "cow dung does not naturally work 30 cm underground"
- Green manure plants are left to decompose in place or lightly worked in
- Composting is done on the surface (sheet composting), not in enclosed heaps
- Earthworms and soil organisms are the primary agents of soil building

## Green Manure Crop Functions

### Nitrogen Fixation
Leguminous green manures fix atmospheric nitrogen through symbiotic bacteria (Rhizobia) in their root nodules:
- Sweet clover (Melilotus officinalis)
- Lupin (Lupinus sp.) -- deep taproot breaks compacted subsoil
- Lucerne/alfalfa (Medicago sativa) -- deep-rooted, drought-tolerant, fixes large amounts of nitrogen
- Golden bird's-foot trefoil (Lotus corniculatus)
- Common vetch (Vicia sativa)
- Crimson clover (Trifolium incarnatum)
- White clover (Trifolium repens)
- Field beans (Vicia faba)

### Soil Structure Improvement
Deep-rooted plants break up compacted layers and bring minerals from deep subsoil to the surface:
- Lupin (Lupinus sp.) -- taproot can reach 1-2 metres deep
- Sweet clover (Melilotus officinalis) -- very deep-rooted
- Lucerne/alfalfa (Medicago sativa) -- root system can extend 3-6 metres
- Black salsify (Scorzonera hispanica)
- Radish (Raphanus sativus) -- especially daikon-type forage radishes
- Good King Henry (Chenopodium bonus-henricus)

### Organic Matter Addition
High-biomass plants add substantial organic matter when cut and left on the surface:
- Pumpkin and courgette (Cucurbita sp.) -- massive leaf canopy
- Maize (Zea mays) -- tall stalks provide winter mulch
- Jerusalem artichoke (Helianthus tuberosus) -- prolific above-ground and below-ground biomass
- Sunflower (Helianthus annuus) -- tall, high biomass
- Buckwheat (Fagopyrum esculentum) -- fast-growing, accumulates phosphorus

### Ground Cover and Erosion Prevention
Low-growing, spreading plants protect soil surface:
- White clover (Trifolium repens) -- living mulch between rows
- Wild rocket (Diplotaxis tenuifolia) -- self-seeding annual ground cover
- Lettuce (Lactuca sativa) -- temporary ground cover in vegetable beds
- Spinach (Spinacia oleracea) -- early-season ground cover
- Chard (Beta vulgaris) -- excellent persistent ground cover

### Weed Suppression
Rapidly establishing, dense plants outcompete weeds:
- Buckwheat (Fagopyrum esculentum) -- germinates in days, shades out weeds
- Phacelia (Phacelia tanacetifolia) -- fast growth, excellent bee forage
- Mustard (Sinapis alba) -- quick germination, biofumigation effect
- Oilseed radish (Raphanus sativus var. oleiferus) -- rapid canopy closure

### Pest and Disease Suppression
Some green manures have biofumigant properties when chopped and incorporated:
- Mustard (Sinapis alba) -- releases glucosinolates that deter soil-borne pathogens
- Marigold (Tagetes sp.) -- deters nematodes
- Radish (Raphanus sativus) -- biofumigation effect
- Garlic (Allium sativum) -- antifungal properties

## Holzer's Recommended Green Manure Strategies

### In Vegetable Gardens
- Between main crops, sow quick-growing green manures like buckwheat, phacelia, or mustard
- After harvest, leave crop residues on the surface as mulch rather than removing them
- In autumn, sow winter-hardy green manures like winter rye, clover, or vetch rather than leaving beds bare
- Use leguminous ground covers (white clover, bird's-foot trefoil) between permanent plantings

### In Orchards and Fruit Gardens
- Under fruit trees, maintain a permanent living ground cover of clover, trefoil, and herbs
- Deep-rooted green manures (lupin, lucerne) are planted between tree rows to break up subsoil
- [[holzer-polycultures-catch-crop-combinations]] around fruit trees include nitrogen-fixing shrubs, aromatic herbs, and [[holzer-problem-plant-regulation-with-livestock]]
- Green manure is cut and left in place to decompose, feeding soil organisms

### On Slopes and marginal land
- Deep-rooted plants (lupin, sweet clover) stabilise slopes
- Dense ground covers prevent erosion
- Pioneer species are used first to build soil, followed by more demanding plants

## Liquid Manure and Plant Extracts

Holzer also uses liquid fertilisers made from green manure plants:
- Comfrey (Symphytum officinale) liquid manure -- rich in potassium, excellent for fruiting plants
- Nettle (Urtica dioica) liquid manure -- rich in nitrogen and iron, general growth stimulant
- Oak leaf tea -- for fungal disease prevention
- Horsetail (Equisetum arvense) tea -- silica-rich, strengthens plant cell walls against fungal attack

These are made by steeping plants in water for several weeks, then diluting before application. They supplement the composting system.

## Connection to Holzer's Broader System

Soil building through green manures is inseparable from Holzer's [[holzer-farming-philosophy]]. Healthy soil is the foundation of everything else: productive [[holzer-vegetable-garden-polycultures]], disease-resistant [[holzer-grafting-fruit-forest]], and effective [[holzer-livestock-integration-pigs-cattle]]. Without chemical inputs, soil health depends entirely on biological processes supported by diverse plant covers.
