# Cannabis Health Index Scoring Methodology

## Overview

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) is an evidence-based rating system
developed by Uwe Blesching, PhD, that compiles and evaluates the
available scientific literature on medical cannabis for more than 100
chronic diseases and symptoms. The CHI provides a standardized scoring
methodology that allows patients and practitioners to quickly assess the
current state of scientific evidence supporting cannabis as a treatment
for specific conditions. The system accounts for the practical value of
different study designs, ranging from laboratory experiments to
double-blind placebo-controlled human trials, and rates the therapeutic
evidence on a graduated scale from "possible" to "actual."

## Rationale and Context

The CHI was developed in response to a critical gap in medical cannabis
literature. While over 15,000 studies on cannabinoids existed in the
National Library of Health at the time of the index's creation, a
systematic evidence-based model for the plant's safe and targeted use
was largely lacking. Only about 15% of medical interventions overall are
based on solid scientific evidence, and the CHI aimed to bring
evidence-based rigor to cannabis therapy. The system follows the
principles articulated by the Cochrane Collaboration: "Evidence-based
medicine is the conscientious, explicit, and judicious use of current
best evidence in making decisions about the care of individual patients."

## CHI Score Categories

The Cannabis Health Index rates the therapeutic evidence for cannabis on
a graduated scale that reflects increasing confidence in the evidence:

- **Possible**: Preliminary or preclinical evidence suggesting potential
  therapeutic benefit, typically from in vitro studies or animal models.
  These findings are promising but have not yet been validated in human
  subjects.
- **Probable**: Moderate evidence from observational studies, case
  reports, or early-phase [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] suggesting likely therapeutic
  benefit. The evidence supports cannabis use but is not yet definitive.
- **Actual**: Strong evidence from well-designed clinical trials,
  ideally double-blind and placebo-controlled, demonstrating measurable
  therapeutic benefit in human patients.

## Study Design Hierarchy

The [[blesching-cannabis-chi-scoring-evidence-based-medicine]] system accounts for the type of research study, placing
greater weight on study designs that provide stronger evidence:

- **In vitro studies**: Laboratory experiments conducted in cell
  cultures or tissue samples provide the foundation for understanding
  mechanisms of action but cannot predict clinical outcomes.
- **Animal studies**: Research conducted in animal models offers insight
  into efficacy and safety but may not translate directly to humans due
  to species differences in [[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]] distribution and
  metabolism.
- **Observational studies**: Human studies that observe outcomes without
  controlled intervention provide real-world evidence but are subject
  to confounding variables.
- **Clinical trials**: Controlled human studies, with double-blind
  placebo-controlled designs representing the gold standard of
  evidence for therapeutic efficacy.

## Scope of Conditions Covered

The CHI evaluates evidence for cannabis therapy across an extensive
range of conditions organized into the following categories:

- **Aging and anti-aging**: General age-related health optimization.
- **Infectious diseases**: [[cannabis-for-bacterial-infections-and-mrsa]] (including MRSA and
  gonorrhea) and viral infections (including hepatitis, herpes, HIV/AIDS,
  and influenza).
- **Cancer**: Multiple cancer types including brain, breast, colon,
  liver, lung, pancreatic, prostate, and skin cancers, plus cancer-induced
  symptoms like night sweats and chemotherapy side effects.
- **Cardiovascular disease**: Heart disease, hypertension, and stroke.
- **Inflammatory diseases**: Arthritis, atherosclerosis, inflammatory
  bowel disease, and periodontitis.
- **Mental disorders**: ADHD, anxiety, autism, depression, bipolar
  disorder, PTSD, and schizophrenia.
- **Neurological diseases**: Alzheimer's, ALS, epilepsy, Huntington's,
  multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's, and Tourette syndrome.
- **Pain conditions**: [[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]], migraine, neuropathies, and cancer
  pain.
- **Skin diseases**: Acne, dermatitis, psoriasis, and related conditions.

## Integration with Mind-Body Medicine

A distinctive feature of the CHI is its integration of evidence-based
cannabis research with mind-body medicine techniques. The index
acknowledges that the [[endocannabinoid-system-overview|endocannabinoid system]] serves as a biological bridge between physical and psychological
processes, and that therapeutic outcomes are influenced by factors
beyond pharmacology alone. The CHI framework incorporates considerations
of setting, intention, emotional state, and conscious exploration as
modifiers of therapeutic response, reflecting the understanding that
a patient's frame of mind affects physiological healing processes.

## Practical Application

The CHI is designed for practical use by patients and practitioners. For
each condition, the index provides a CHI score, key study references,
and therapeutic evidence summaries. Users are advised to consider the
CHI score alongside individual clinical expertise and patient
preferences. The index is supported by a companion app and monthly
eMagazine that provide ongoing updates as new research is published,
ensuring that the evidence base remains current. The system empowers
patients to make more informed decisions about medical cannabis by
providing accessible, evidence-based information at a glance.
