## CHI Score Categories

The CHI assigns one of three primary ratings to each condition, reflecting the degree of confidence in cannabis as a therapeutic option:

### Possible

The "possible" rating indicates that preliminary evidence exists suggesting cannabis may have therapeutic value for the condition. This level typically rests on:

- Laboratory studies showing plausible mechanisms of action
- Animal models demonstrating relevant effects
- A small number of case reports or observational studies
- No contrary evidence from well-designed human trials

At this level, cannabis is considered a reasonable option for patients who have not responded to conventional treatments, but the evidence is not yet strong enough for broad recommendation.

### Probable

The "probable" rating reflects a moderate body of evidence supporting cannabis efficacy. Requirements typically include:

- Consistent results across multiple study types
- At least some controlled human trials showing benefit
- Plausible and well-characterized mechanisms of action
- Animal data that correlates with human findings
- No significant safety concerns at therapeutic doses

At this level, cannabis can be more confidently recommended, particularly for patients seeking alternatives to pharmaceuticals with undesirable side effects.

### Actual

The "actual" rating represents the strongest level of evidence, indicating that cannabis has demonstrated therapeutic efficacy in well-designed human studies. This requires:

- Multiple randomized controlled trials showing statistically significant benefit
- Consistent findings across different research groups
- Well-understood dose-response relationships
- Established safety profiles at therapeutic doses
- Evidence from large-scale or long-term studies

This is the highest confidence level in the CHI system and indicates robust scientific support for cannabis as a treatment option.
