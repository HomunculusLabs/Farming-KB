# Mushroom Humidity Chamber Design

## Overview

A humidity chamber (also called a fruiting chamber or shotgun
fruiting chamber) provides the high-humidity environment
required for mushrooms to transition from mycelial colonization
to fruiting body production. In the Jarrold boil-a-bag method,
the humidity chamber is a simple and inexpensive setup using
commonly available materials.

## Basic Chamber Design

### Standard Fish Tank Chamber

The simplest design uses a standard glass fish tank (4 ft x 2 ft)
as the enclosure:

**Humidity Base**
A layer of soaked hydro pellets (hydroton/expanded clay) is
placed on the bottom of the tank. These clay pebbles hold water
and release it slowly through evaporation, maintaining consistent
high humidity (90%+ relative humidity is ideal for most species).

**Sealing**
Plastic sheeting is placed over the top of the tank, creating
a seal that prevents humidity escape and excludes flies and
other contaminants. The plastic should be easy to lift for
daily maintenance.

**Fresh Air Exchange**
The chamber requires manual fanning twice daily to introduce
fresh air and remove accumulated CO2. Without adequate air
exchange, mushrooms grow poorly, developing long stems and
small caps (etiolation). CO2 buildup above 1000 ppm
significantly impairs fruiting.

## Automated Self-Humidifying Chamber

A more advanced design automates humidity maintenance:

### Components
- Fish tank or similar transparent enclosure
- Air stone (aquarium bubbler)
- Air tubing
- Small aquarium air pump
- 2mm plastic sheet (for angled drip lids)
- Car registration plate nuts and bolts (for assembly)

### Construction
1. Create a small hole near the top of the tank
2. Feed the air tube through to a water container at the
   bottom of the chamber
3. Attach the air stone to the tube inside the water container
4. Connect the air pump outside the tank
5. Fashion angled drip lids from 2mm plastic sheet to
   distribute humidity evenly

The air pump forces air through the submerged air stone,
creating fine bubbles that humidify the chamber air
continuously. This eliminates the need for manual fanning
and provides more consistent conditions.

## Warm Humid Air Delivery Chamber

For cold climates or winter growing, a heated delivery system
provides warm, humid air to the fruiting chamber:

### Design
- A water-filled container serves as the humidity source
- A water heater with timer maintains water temperature
- An input fan pushes warm humid air into the growing chamber
- Multiple small ducts serve as outlets for even distribution
- One large duct serves as the main input

### Setup Considerations
- The water heater timer may require several hours of
  adjustment to find the right temperature balance
- Too much heat can dry the substrate or promote bacterial
  contamination
- The goal is chamber temperatures of 22-26°C (72-79°F)
  with humidity above 85%
- Multiple small outlets distribute air more evenly than
  a single large outlet

## Bag Placement in the Chamber

When transferring colonized bags from the colonization phase:

1. Remove the filter can from each bag
2. Cut the bag down the sides
3. Remove as much of the bag material as needed while leaving
   at least 2 cm of the bottom intact
4. The remaining bag base serves as a stand, keeping the
   substrate block elevated above the humidified floor
5. Space blocks to allow air circulation between them

## Monitoring and Maintenance

### Daily Tasks
- Fan fresh air into the chamber twice daily (for manual
  setups)
- Check humidity levels (hygrometer recommended)
- Remove any contaminated blocks immediately
- Monitor for signs of pinning (initial mushroom formation)

### Environmental Parameters
- **Humidity**: 85-95% relative humidity for pinning and
  fruiting
- **Temperature**: 22-26°C for most Psilocybe species
- **Light**: Indirect ambient light or 12-hour fluorescent
  cycle; mushrooms do not need bright light
- **CO2**: Below 1000 ppm; regular air exchange is essential
- **Air circulation**: Gentle, not drafty

## Contamination Prevention

- Seal the chamber against flies and other insect vectors
- Remove any moldy substrate blocks immediately
- Maintain clean hands when handling colonized blocks
- Ensure the humidification water is fresh (change weekly)
- Avoid over-saturating the hydro pellet base
