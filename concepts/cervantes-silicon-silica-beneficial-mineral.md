# Silicon and Silica Benefits for Cannabis

Silicon is a beneficial mineral element that, while not classified as
essential for all plants, provides significant advantages for cannabis
cultivation. Available in most soils and water, silicon does not
typically cause cannabis complications due to deficiencies or excesses.
However, supplemental silicon can dramatically improve plant vigor and
resistance to stress.

## How Silicon Works in Plants

Silicon is absorbed by plants as silicic acid. Once absorbed, silicon
assists in keeping iron and manganese levels consistent within plant
tissues. It is found mainly in the epidermal cell walls where it
collects in the form of hydrated amorphous silica. Silicon also
accumulates in the walls of other cells throughout the plant.

The physical deposition of silica in cell walls creates a mechanical
barrier that strengthens the overall plant structure. This mineral
reinforcement is similar to adding rebar to concrete, making cell
walls thicker and more rigid.

## Benefits of Silicon Supplementation

Adequate and soluble silicon guarantees stronger cell walls that resist
pest attacks and increase heat and drought tolerance. Specific benefits
include:

- **Structural Strength**: Silicon-strengthened stems and branches are
  better able to support heavy bud development during the
  [[cervantes-flowering-phase]].

- **Pest Resistance**: Pests and diseases have a difficult time
  penetrating plants that have adequate silicon levels. The hardened
  cell walls make it physically harder for insects to feed and for
  fungal pathogens to penetrate.

- **Heat Tolerance**: Silicon helps plants cope with high temperatures
  by reducing transpiration rates and maintaining cellular integrity.

- **Drought Resistance**: Silicon-enhanced plants manage water more
  efficiently and recover faster from drought stress.

- **Disease Resistance**: Silicon deposition in cell walls creates a
  physical barrier against fungal pathogens like powdery mildew and
  bud rot.

## Silicon Deficiency

A lack of silicon has been proven to decrease yields of some fruits
and cause new leaves to deform. While silicon deficiency is uncommon
in soil gardens because silicon is abundant in most soils, it can
occur in hydroponic systems and soilless media where silicon is not
present in the nutrient solution.

Deficient plants may show weaker stems, increased susceptibility to
pests and diseases, and reduced overall vigor. Leaves may appear
thinner and more prone to environmental damage.

## Silicon Excess

Silicon excess is rarely a problem. The body is unable to accumulate
harmful levels of silicon through normal cultivation practices. Plants
naturally regulate silicon uptake, and excess is simply not absorbed.

## Silicon-Based Pest Control

Pests and diseases have a difficult time penetrating plants that are
sprayed with a silicon-based repellent/insecticide. Foliar
applications of soluble silicon products create an additional
protective barrier on leaf surfaces, making it harder for spider mites,
thrips, and other pests to establish infestations.

## Sources of Silicon

Silicon can be supplied through several sources:

- **Potassium Silicate**: The most common silicon supplement for
  hydroponic and soilless cultivation. Also provides potassium.

- **Silicic Acid**: A readily available form that plants absorb
  directly.

- **Diatomaceous Earth**: Contains fossilized silica shells that
  provide silicon when incorporated into soil. See
  [[diatomaceous-earth-cannabis]] for details.

- **Rice Hulls**: An organic source of silicon that also improves
  soil structure and aeration.

## Integration with Nutrient Programs

Silicon should be included as part of a comprehensive nutrient
management program. See [[cervantes-nutrient-management]] for
complete feeding guidance. Silicon works synergistically with other
beneficial minerals; see [[cervantes-micronutrient-deficiency-chelation]]
for information on micronutrient interactions.

## Silicon and the Entourage Effect

While silicon does not directly interact with the cannabinoid or
terpenoid systems, its role in plant defense indirectly affects the
quality of the final product. Healthier, more pest-resistant plants
produce better-quality flower with higher terpenoid and cannabinoid
content. Silicon supplementation during the [[cervantes-vegetative-phase]] establishes a strong foundation for robust flowering.

## Foliar vs. Root Application

Silicon can be applied through both foliar sprays and root drenches.
Foliar application provides immediate surface protection and pest
deterrence. Root application builds systemic silicon deposits in cell
walls throughout the plant. For best results, use both methods:
apply silicon to the nutrient solution for root uptake and occasionally
spray foliage for additional pest protection.
