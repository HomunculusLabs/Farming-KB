# Psilocybin and Psilocin Chemistry: Serotonin Relationship and Pharmacological Mechanism

## Overview

Psilocybin and psilocin are the primary psychoactive compounds in hallucinogenic
mushrooms. They belong to the indolealkylamine (tryptamine) class, the same chemical
family as serotonin, LSD, and DMT. Understanding their [[cannabis-safety-profile-and-overdose-risk]] compared
to other hallucinogenic substances.

## Chemical Classification

Hallucinogens are classified by chemical structure into three major groups:

1. **Indolealkylamines (tryptamines)**: LSD, psilocin, psilocybin, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT
2. **Phenylethylamines**: Mescaline, 2C-B, DOI
3. **Cannabinoids**: THC and related cannabis compounds

Psilocybin and psilocin fall squarely in the tryptamine group. Their structural
similarity to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT) is the key to their
pharmacological activity.

## Molecular Structures

### Psilocybin
- **IUPAC name**: 4-Phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-PO-DMT)
- **Molecular formula**: C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P
- **Molecular weight**: 284.25 g/mol
- **Properties**: More stable in air than psilocin; water-soluble; phosphate ester
  of psilocin

### Psilocin
- **IUPAC name**: 4-Hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-HO-DMT)
- **Molecular formula**: C₁₂H₁₆N₂O
- **Molecular weight**: 204.27 g/mol
- **Properties**: Less stable than psilocybin; the pharmacologically active compound
  that acts on [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]]:
- **Baeocystin**: 4-Phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine (4-PO-NMT)
- **Norbaeocystin**: 4-Phosphoryloxytryptamine (4-PO-T)

Both are considered less active than psilocybin and psilocin, though their
contributions to the overall psychoactive effect profile are not fully understood.

## Structural Relationship to Serotonin

The structural similarity between psilocin and serotonin is striking. Both molecules
share the indole ring system and a hydroxyl group at the 4-position of the benzene
ring. The key difference is the dimethyl substitution on the terminal amine nitrogen
in psilocin versus a single hydrogen in serotonin.

This structural resemblance allows psilocin to bind to serotonin receptors, particularly
the 5-HT2A subtype, where it acts as a post-synaptic agonist or partial agonist.
The additional methyl groups on psilocin increase its lipophilicity, facilitating
blood-brain barrier penetration and contributing to its psychedelic potency.

## Pharmacokinetics: Psilocybin as a Prodrug

Psilocybin functions as a prodrug: it is the inactive, more stable phosphate ester
that is converted in the body to the active psilocin. This conversion occurs through
enzymatic dephosphorylation, primarily in the gut and liver.

The prodrug nature of psilocybin has several practical implications:

- **[[first-pass-metabolism-and-oral-bioavailability]]**: Psilocybin is readily absorbed and converted after
  ingestion
- **Onset of action**: Effects typically begin 20-60 minutes after oral ingestion,
  corresponding to the time needed for absorption, conversion, and brain penetration
- **Duration**: Effects generally last 4-6 hours, shorter than LSD (8-12 hours)
- **Stability**: The phosphate ester form is more stable during storage, making
  dried mushrooms relatively durable compared to pure psilocin

## The 5-HT2A Receptor Mechanism

Psilocin's primary mechanism of action is as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist/partial agonist.
This receptor is densely expressed in the cerebral cortex, particularly in layer V
pyramidal neurons. Activation of 5-HT2A receptors produces:
