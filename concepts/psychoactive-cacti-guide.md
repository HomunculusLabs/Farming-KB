# Psychoactive Cacti Guide

Mescaline-containing cacti have been used ceremonially for thousands of years. The principal species are peyote (Lophophora williamsii), San Pedro (Echinopsis pachanoi), and Peruvian Torch (Echinopsis peruvianus).

Related: [[psychedelic-compounds-reference]], [[entheogen-culture]]

## Species Identification

### Peyote (Lophophora williamsii)
- Small, spineless, blue-green button-shaped cactus
- Grows in a narrow band along the Texas-Mexico border into the Chihuahuan Desert
- Crown ("button") visible above ground with a large tuberous root below
- Over 60 alkaloids; principal active: mescaline (~1.5% dry weight)
- Extremely slow growing: 10-15 years from seed to maturity in the wild
- Legally protected; sacramental use permitted for Native American Church members

### San Pedro (Echinopsis pachanoi)
- Tall, columnar, fast-growing cactus (6-20 ft in cultivation)
- Native to the Andes of Peru and Ecuador
- Mescaline content: 0.33-2.375% dry weight (highly variable by specimen)
- Common ornamental plant, legally sold in garden shops
- Traditional use: "cimora" brew prepared by Peruvian curanderos

### Peruvian Torch (Echinopsis peruvianus)
- Similar to San Pedro but with longer spines and bluish-green coloration
- Mescaline content: 0-0.82% dry weight (variable; some specimens contain none)
- Less commonly used than San Pedro

## Chemistry

Mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) is a phenethylamine alkaloid. Effective dose: 200-500mg (approximately 5-15g dried peyote buttons). Peak intoxication at 2-4 hours, duration 4-12 hours. Typically induces nausea, visual hallucinations, altered perception, and introspective states.

## Cultivation from Seed

1. Fill shallow trays with well-draining cactus soil (50% sand/grit, 50% organic matter)
2. Scatter seeds on surface, press gently, do not bury
3. Cover with clear plastic to maintain humidity
4. Provide indirect light, 70-85F
5. Germination in 1-4 weeks
6. Transplant seedlings after 6-12 months

## Grafting for Accelerated Growth

Peyote"s slow growth rate makes grafting attractive. Pereskiopsis spathulata or P. bleo are ideal rootstocks, providing 10x or more growth acceleration.

1. Cut the top off a Pereskiopsis rootstock at a clean angle
2. Cut the scion (peyote button or Trichocereus tip) to match
3. Press cut surfaces together and hold with rubber bands or weights
4. Keep in warm, bright conditions — no water for 1-2 weeks
5. Graft should take in 1-2 weeks; growth acceleration is immediately apparent

## Preparation

- **Traditional**: Buttons are dried, ground to powder, consumed with water
- **Tea**: Fresh or dried buttons/cactus boiled in water, strained
- **Extraction**: Mescaline can be extracted using acid-base chemistry (not covered here)
- San Pedro/Peruvian Torch are typically prepared as tea due to large volume of material needed

## Legal Status

- **Peyote**: Schedule I in the US, but legally protected for members of the Native American Church (NAC) under the American Indian Religious Freedom Act (1978, amended 1994). NAC has approximately 300,000 members.
- **San Pedro / Peruvian Torch**: Not specifically scheduled at the federal level, though containing a Schedule I substance (mescaline) makes them technically illegal to prepare for ingestion. Widely sold as ornamentals.
- **International**: Varies by country. Legal in some South American countries for traditional use.

## Traditional and Ceremonial Use

- Native American Church: All-night ceremonies in a tipi, guided by a "Road Man," with singing of peyote songs throughout the night. Peyote is consumed as a sacrament for healing, prayer, and community bonding.
- Huichol (Wixarika): Annual pilgrimage to Wirikuta to harvest peyote, followed by ceremonial use with singing and storytelling.
- Andean curanderos: San Pedro ceremonies sometimes around a fire, with an altar of symbolic figurines representing light and dark spirits.
