## Gourmet and Medicinal Mushroom Shiitake Cultivation on Logs

Shiitake (Lentinula edodes) has been cultivated for over a thousand years, with records dating to the Sung Dynasty in China (960-1127 AD) when Wu Sang Kwuang first described [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]] in 1904. Today, Shiitake is cultivated both outdoors on hardwood logs and indoors on [[stamets-fruiting-substrate-materials]] formula for Shiitake sawdust blocks:

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Hardwood sawdust | 100 lbs (45.5 kg) |
| Wood chips (1/2-4 inches) | 50 lbs (22.7 kg) |
| Rice or wheat bran | 40 lbs (18.1 kg) |
| Gypsum | Buffer as needed |
| Water | To achieve 65-75% moisture |

At make-up, the substrate pH is 5.5-6.0. By the time of fruiting, the pH drops to 3.5-4.5 (optimal fruiting range: 4.2-4.6 per Chang and Miles, 1989). Alternative supplements include various grains, cereal brans, flours, tea leaves, yeast, and molasses.

Pine (Pinus radiata) has been successfully used when combined with hardwood at a ratio of 6 parts pine to 3 parts hardwood to 1 part barley grain.

### Spawn Selection

Stamets recommends:
- **1st and 2nd generation spawn**: Rye, wheat, sorghum, or corn grain
- **3rd (final) generation spawn**: Sawdust spawn (produces better results for the final expansion)

### Block Preparation and Colonization

1. Substrate is mixed, moistened, and packed into polypropylene bags with microporous filter patches (or bottles fitted with cotton plugs)
2. Blocks are sterilized in a retort or autoclave
3. Inoculated with grain or sawdust spawn
4. Incubated in controlled environment growing rooms

### Colonization Timeline

The progression of Shiitake colonization on supplemented sawdust blocks:

- **~20 days post-inoculation**: "Popcorning" occurs, where white mycelial masses become visible throughout the block
- **~25 days**: Browning phenomenon begins; the surface of the block turns brown as the mycelium secretes enzymes that degrade the wood components
