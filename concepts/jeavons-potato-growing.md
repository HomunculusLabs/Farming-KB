# Potato Growing: Irish and Sweet Potatoes

Potatoes are among the most important calorie crops in the GROW
BIOINTENSIVE mini-farm. Both Irish potatoes (Solanum tuberosum) and
sweet potatoes (Ipomoea batatas) are area-efficient calorie producers,
yielding large amounts of food per unit of growing area. They are
classified as [[jeavons-tree-cane-crops]]
because of their high yields per square foot. In the Jeavons system,
about 90% of a diet-growing area should eventually be planted in
nutritious calorie crops, and potatoes play a central role.

## Irish Potato Seed Preparation

Be sure to obtain certified "seed" Irish potatoes. Many potatoes sold
in stores have been treated with sprout retardants and will not grow
well. The sprouting process is called "chitting" and should be done
without soil in a 3-inch-deep flat or box with small air spaces
between the tubers.

### Sprouting Conditions

- Place tubers in a warm, dry, airy location with indirect light
- Sprout for up to 1 month until sprouts are about 1/4 inch long
- **Critical caution**: Avoid conditions of 90% humidity and 70°F or
  more for a period of 24 hours — these conditions encourage blight
- After harvest, tubers are in dormancy for 5 to 20 weeks; plan
  your planting schedule accordingly

### Cutting Seed Pieces

For planting, use pieces of potato weighing at least 1.5 to 2 ounces.
Each piece should optimally have 2 or 3 sprouted eyes. This ensures
vigorous growth and adequate food reserves for the emerging plant.
Larger seed pieces produce stronger plants and higher yields, making
the extra seed potato investment worthwhile.

## Sweet Potato Seed Preparation

Sweet potatoes require a different sprouting approach than Irish
potatoes. Like Irish potatoes, be sure to obtain "seed" sweet potatoes
from a reliable source, as store-bought varieties are often treated
to retard sprouting.

### The Jar Method

1. Use wide-mouth canning jars filled with water
2. Insert toothpicks into sweet potatoes around their outside to hold
   the upper half out of the water
3. Roots form on the submerged portion in the water
4. Small plantlets grow from the eyes on the upper portion
5. Each 8-ounce sweet potato will produce 3 to 4 of these "starts"

### Transplanting Sweet Potato Slips

When a shoot is about 1 to 1.5 inches long:

1. Nick it off along with a very small piece of the sweet potato where
   it is attached
2. Plant it in a [[jeavons-flat-preparation-seedling-raising]] on 2-inch centers
3. Only the last set of leaves should be above the surface of the flat
   soil
4. Approximately 4 to 8 flats are needed for a 100-square-foot bed

### The Flat Sprouting Alternative

Whole sweet potatoes may also be sprouted side by side in a flat
rather than using the jar method. This can be more convenient when
large numbers of slips are needed. Monitor moisture levels carefully
to prevent rotting while encouraging root and shoot formation.

## Planting Sweet Potatoes in the Growing Bed

When the seedlings are 7 to 9 inches tall, transplant them into the
growing bed. A critical technique: plant them so at least 6 inches of
the stem is beneath the soil surface. This deep planting encourages
additional root formation along the buried stem, significantly
increasing the harvest.

## Potatoes in the Crop Rotation

In the [[jeavons-crop-rotation-system]], potatoes
are technically root crops and would normally be classified as light
feeders. However, Jeavons notes that potatoes actually consume one of
the largest amounts of nitrogen of any vegetable, making them
functionally heavy feeders. This is an important caveat: the standard
heavy-feeder / heavy-giver / light-feeder rotation system is not
always perfectly accurate for every crop. Potatoes are the notable
exception that proves the rule.

## Nutritional Advantages

Potatoes are exceptionally productive in the GROW BIOINTENSIVE system.
They are area-efficient calorie crops, meaning they produce a large
number of calories in a given area due to their high yields per unit
of area. Other area-efficient crops include garlic, parsnips, burdock,
and salsify. The combination of area-efficient crops like potatoes
with weight-efficient [[jeavons-grain-growing-mini-farms]]
like wheat ensures a balanced and sustainable calorie production
system for any size mini-farm. Potatoes also provide significant
vitamin C, potassium, and dietary fiber, making them nutritionally
valuable beyond their caloric contribution.

## Integration with the Biointensive Method

Potatoes benefit from the [[jeavons-double-digging-bed-preparation]] created by double-digging, which allows tubers to expand
without obstruction. The loose, well-structured soil also makes
harvesting easier and reduces damage to the tubers. Proper
[[jeavons-watering-technique]] ensures consistent moisture
without waterlogging, which can cause rot and encourage disease. The
[[jeavons-composting-methods]] produced on-site provides the
nutrients needed for these heavy-feeding crops while maintaining
sustainable soil fertility. Following [[jeavons-crop-rotation-detail]] principles is essential to prevent soil depletion and reduce
pest and disease pressure in subsequent growing seasons. For planting
spacing and yield expectations, consult the Master Charts in the
source text.
