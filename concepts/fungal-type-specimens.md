# Fungal Type Specimens and Taxonomic Reference Material

Type specimens are the physical reference material on which the scientific name of a fungal species is based. They serve as the ultimate standard for taxonomic identification and nomenclatural stability, anchoring fungal taxonomy to verifiable physical evidence.

## Importance of Type Specimens

Type specimens are critical to fungal taxonomy because:
- They provide objective reference points for the application of scientific names
- They allow verification of identifications by any researcher
- They enable resolution of taxonomic disputes
- They document the geographical and ecological context of species discovery
- They serve as sources of DNA for molecular phylogenetic studies

The type specimen concept in mycology follows the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICN), which governs the naming of all fungi.

## Types of Type Specimens

Several categories of type exist in fungal nomenclature:
- **Holotype**: the single specimen designated by the original author as the nomenclatural type
- **Isotype**: a duplicate of the holotype collected at the same time and place
- **Syntype**: any one of two or more specimens cited by the original author when no holotype was designated
- **Lectotype**: a specimen selected from among syntypes to serve as the unique type
- **Neotype**: a specimen selected to serve as the type when the original material is lost or destroyed
- **Epitype**: a specimen selected to serve as an interpretive type when the existing type is ambiguous

## Holomorph vs. Anamorph Types

Fungi with pleomorphic life cycles (producing both sexual teleomorph and asexual anamorph states) present special challenges. The type may be based on either the teleomorph or the anamorph, and linking these states is essential for stable nomenclature. Historically, many fungal names were based only on the anamorph state, and connecting these to their teleomorphs has been a major activity in fungal systematics.

With the move toward "one fungus, one name" in the ICN (effective since 2013), the practice of maintaining separate names for anamorph and teleomorph states has been abandoned, simplifying type-based nomenclature.

## Preservation of Fungal Specimens

Fungal specimens are preserved in several ways:
- **Dried herbarium specimens**: the standard method; sporocarps dried, labeled, and stored in herbarium cabinets
- **Liquid preservation**: in ethanol or FAA (formalin-acetic acid-alcohol) for microscopic studies
- **Culture collections**: living cultures maintained in cryopreservation or mineral oil
- **DNA extracts**: purified DNA stored at ultra-low temperatures
- **Microscope slides**: permanent mounts of microscopic features

Proper drying of specimens is essential to prevent mold growth and insect damage. Many older herbarium specimens remain valuable for molecular work despite their age, though DNA quality decreases over time.

## Major Fungal Herbaria

Important herbaria holding significant fungal collections include:
- **Kew (K)**: Royal Botanic Gardens, one of the world's largest fungal collections
- **BPI**: USDA National Fungal Collections, Beltsville, Maryland
- **NY**: New York Botanical Garden
- **FH**: Farlow Herbarium, Harvard University
- **OSC**: Oregon State University, with major collections of Pacific Northwest fungi
- **UPS**: Uppsala University, Sweden, with important Nordic fungal collections
- **BR**: National Botanic Garden of Belgium

## Herbarium Specimens as Sources of DNA

Dried herbarium specimens are increasingly important as sources of DNA for molecular systematic studies. Challenges include:
- DNA degradation over time; older specimens yield fragmented DNA
- Chemical treatments (e.g., mercuric chloride, formaldehyde) may inhibit PCR
- Contamination from handling or storage
- Non-target DNA (e.g., from associated bacteria or insects)

Despite these challenges, ITS sequences have been successfully obtained from herbarium specimens over 100 years old, and many type specimens have been sequenced for barcoding purposes.

## Digital Repositories and Virtual Herbaria

Digitalization of fungal herbarium specimens has accelerated in recent years:
- High-resolution imaging of specimens
- Online databases with collection data and images
- Georeferencing of collection localities
- DNA barcode sequences linked to voucher specimens
