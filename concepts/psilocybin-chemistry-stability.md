# Psilocybin Chemistry and Stability

The psychoactive compounds in neurotropic fungi are primarily indole alkaloids structurally related to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT). Understanding their chemistry, biosynthesis, and degradation is essential for [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]], ethnobotanical documentation, and the interpretation of historical reports of mushroom use and potency.

## Principal Active Compounds

### Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT)

Psilocybin, or 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, is the phosphate ester of psilocin. It is the primary indole alkaloid found in [[non-psilocybin-neurotropic-fungi-and-ergot-alkaloids]] serves as a stable precursor that is enzymatically dephosphorylated in the body to produce the active compound psilocin. Psilocybin is more stable in air and is water-soluble, making it the predominant form in dried fungal tissue.

### Psilocin (4-HO-DMT)

Psilocin, or 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, is the pharmacologically active compound. It acts on the serotonin system as a 5-HT2A post-synaptic agonist or partial agonist. Psilocin is less stable than psilocybin and is the compound responsible for the characteristic bluing reaction when fungal tissue is bruised or damaged — the blue color results from oxidation of psilocin and related hydroxylated indole compounds.

### Baeocystin and Norbaeocystin

Two additional tryptamines — baeocystin (4-PO-NMT) and norbaeocystin (4-OH-NMT) — are present in many psilocybin-containing species but are considered less active than [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]]. Baeocystin was first isolated from Psilocybe baeocystis by Leung & Paul (1968). The relative contribution of these compounds to overall psychoactivity remains incompletely understood.

### Ibotenic Acid and Muscimol (Amanita Species)

The Amanita species (A. muscaria, A. pantherina, A. regalis) contain an entirely different chemical profile: ibotenic acid (a glutamate receptor agonist) and its decarboxylation product muscimol (a GABA-A receptor agonist). These compounds are water-soluble and can be partially removed by boiling and discarding the cooking water — a practice documented in Siberian Amanita use. Muscarine, a third compound sometimes mentioned, is present in only trace amounts and does not contribute significantly to the psychoactive effects.

## Chemical Classification of Hallucinogens

Hallucinogens can be grouped by chemical structure:

- **Indolealkylamines (tryptamines)** — LSD, psilocin, psilocybin, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT. Structurally similar to serotonin.
- **Phenylethylamines** — mescaline.
- **Cannabinoids** — THC and related compounds.
- **Isoxazole derivatives** — ibotenic acid, muscimol (unique to Amanita section).

The [[dmt-tryptamine-hallucinogens]] share structural similarity with serotonin, which explains their activity at serotonergic receptor sites. [[psilocybin-and-psilocin-chemical-synthesis]] belong to the same broad chemical class as LSD, though their effects differ in intensity and duration.

## Interspecies Variation in Potency

### Potency by Species

Mushroom potency varies enormously by species:

- **[[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]]** (liberty cap) — averages approximately 1% psilocybin/psilocin content by dry weight; considered one of the most potent naturally occurring species.
- **Psilocybe azurescens** — reported to be among the most potent, with concentrations potentially exceeding 1%.
- **Psilocybe bohemica** — also reported with high potency.
- **Psilocybe cubensis** — typically 0.3-0.6% psilocybin/psilocin; the most commonly cultivated species but relatively moderate in potency.
- **[[copelandia-cyanescens]]** — higher potency than P. cubensis.
- **[[gartz-panaeolus-subbalteatus-taxonomy-and-intoxication-history]]** — lower potency but widely distributed.

### Intraspecies Variation

Within a single species, potency is affected by:

- **Genetics** — different strains and collections show variable alkaloid profiles.
- **Growing conditions** — substrate composition, temperature, humidity, and light exposure all influence biosynthesis.
- **Age at harvest** — younger specimens are significantly more potent than mature ones (discussed below).
- **Part of the fungus** — alkaloid distribution is not uniform throughout the fruiting body.

## The Critical Role of Specimen Age

### Degradation Over Time

The age of fungal specimens is one of the most important variables affecting alkaloid content. This has been demonstrated through multiple lines of evidence:

**Herbarium studies:** Ohenoja et al. (1987) measured psilocybin in [[herbarium-specimens-fungal-collections]] of P. semilanceata and found dramatic decline with age:
- 1869 specimen: 0.014% dry weight
- 1954 specimen: 0.67% dry weight
- 1976 specimen: 0.84% dry weight

**Time-course studies:** Repke et al. (1977) showed that baeocystin content varies with specimen age in P. baeocystis and P. cyanescens. No trace of indoles was detected in specimens analyzed 20-60 days after collection, suggesting complete degradation.

**Clinical observation:** Guzmán observed that normal doses of hallucinogenic fungi (P. mexicana and P. caerulescens) were only slightly neurotropic or entirely inactive when the fungi had been dried for almost a year. This is consistent with [[psilocybin-chemistry-stability-indigenous-knowledge]].

### Indigenous Knowledge of Degradation

Mexican Indian users of sacred mushrooms possess traditional knowledge about specimen age that aligns with modern chemical findings. They say that old dried specimens kept for more than one year "are not good to use" and discard them. This empirical knowledge likely developed over centuries of use and represents an early understanding of alkaloid stability.

### Volatility of Active Compounds

Psilocybin and psilocin appear to be at least partially volatile. Guzmán experienced colored hallucinations without consuming any fungi while sleeping in a small room filled with fresh and semifermented specimens of multiple Psilocybe species (P. caerulescens, P. cordispora, P. cubensis, P. mexicana, and P. zapotecorum) that had been collected for commercial purposes. The room had a strong acrid fungal aroma. The hallucinations stopped upon leaving the room and breathing fresh air.

This observation has implications for storage, drying, and the interpretation of historical accounts of mushroom potency.
