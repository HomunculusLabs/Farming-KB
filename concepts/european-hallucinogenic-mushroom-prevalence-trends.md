# European Hallucinogenic Mushroom Prevalence and Trends

## Overview

The use [[health-risks-and-harm-reduction-for-hallucinogenic-mushroom-use]] across the 12 reporting EU member states ranged from approximately 1% to 8%. This wide range reflects genuine differences in availability, cultural attitudes, and enforcement approaches between countries.

### Highest Prevalence Countries

The countries reporting the highest lifetime prevalence among young adults included:

- **Netherlands**: Among the highest rates in Europe, attributable to the well-established smartshop retail infrastructure and permissive legal environment during the survey period
- **Czech Republic**: Elevated prevalence potentially related to cultural familiarity with foraging traditions and relatively liberal attitudes toward drug use
- **United Kingdom**: Significant use driven by the growth of alternative retail channels and an established drug-using population
- **Germany**: Notable prevalence reflecting both urban drug cultures and rural foraging traditions
- **Ireland**: Measurable prevalence linked to the availability of imported mushrooms and local interest in alternative psychoactive substances

### Lowest Prevalence Countries

Countries reporting the lowest lifetime prevalence among young adults included:

- **Lithuania**: Low prevalence reflecting both limited availability and more restrictive cultural attitudes toward drug experimentation
- **Hungary**: Low rates consistent with generally conservative drug use patterns in Central and Eastern Europe
- **France**: Despite proximity to the Netherlands, lower prevalence suggesting that legal availability in one country does not automatically translate to equivalent use in neighboring states

## School Survey Data (ESPAD 2003)

The European School Survey Project on Alcohol and Other Drugs (ESPAD) 2003 survey collected data specifically from 15-16 year old students across European countries. This age group is particularly important for identifying emerging trends before they become established in the broader population.

### Key Findings

- Lifetime prevalence among 15-16 year olds ranged from 0% to 8% across surveyed countries
- In several countries, school-age use was substantially lower than young adult use, suggesting that experimentation typically begins after secondary school
- The wide variation between countries indicated that cultural and regulatory factors played a significant role in shaping use patterns

## Comparison with Ecstasy Use

A notable finding of the EMCDDA analysis was that in nine of the surveyed countries, the lifetime prevalence of hallucinogenic mushroom use was equal to or exceeded that of ecstasy (MDMA) use. This comparison is significant because ecstasy was, at the time, the most widely used synthetic stimulant-hallucinogen in Europe.

The parity or superiority of mushroom prevalence relative to ecstasy in so many countries suggests that mushrooms had become a mainstream alternative to synthetic drugs for a substantial portion of European recreational drug users.

## Social Settings and Contexts of Use

### Club and Festival Settings

Club and festival environments showed consistently higher prevalence of mushroom use compared to general population surveys. The association between mushroom use and dance music culture, particularly in settings associated with alternative and psychedelic trance events, was identified as a significant factor in the diffusion of mushroom use across Europe.

### Outdoor and Nature Settings

Unlike many synthetic drugs, hallucinogenic mushrooms are also associated with outdoor and nature-based recreational settings. The tradition of mushroom foraging in many European countries, combined with the appeal of natural psychedelic experiences in outdoor environments, distinguishes mushroom use from that of purely synthetic substances.
