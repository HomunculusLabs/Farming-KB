## Cultivation and Spore Dispersal

Today, P. cubensis is the most commonly cultivated psilocybin mushroom in
the world. Underground cultivation centers function as invisible spore
geysers, gushing germplasm into immediate surroundings. Uplifted into
jetstreams, spores have spread across the continents. With the emission of
so much spore mass, the range of distribution is likely to continue to
expand.

New strains could evolve with tolerances for cooler and/or drier
environments. With modern means of travel, spores can be carried thousands
of miles in the course of a day, simply hitchhiking upon unknowing airline
passengers.

## Ecological Intelligence

The evolutionary success of psilocybin mushrooms is remarkable. Their
ability to exploit disturbed habitats, coevolve with human civilization,
and spread through both natural and anthropogenic means demonstrates a
sophisticated ecological strategy. The production of psilocybin itself may
be selected for as a beneficial evolutionary trait, as it guarantees human
interest in cultivation and propagation of the species.
