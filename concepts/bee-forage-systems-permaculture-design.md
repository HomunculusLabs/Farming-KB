# Bee Forage Systems in Permaculture Design

## Overview

A well-designed bee forage system provides year-round nectar and pollen for honeybees, eliminating the need for migratory beekeeping. Commercial apiarists in Australia sometimes transport hives up to 1,000 km to follow honey flows — an enormous expense that essentially converts fossil fuel to honey with poor efficiency. A permanent, integrated bee forage system eliminates this cost while improving pollination of all crops on the property.

Bees can effectively forage up to 2 miles (3.2 km) from the hive. The bee forage system should be integrated within the whole property design, prioritizing nectar (N) and pollen (P) producing plants over utility plants with no bee benefit.

## Design Principles

### Hedgerow Systems

Hedgerows serve dual functions: providing bee forage and creating wind-free flight paths. Wind-free flight allows bees to convert nectar to honey at greater efficiency, directly increasing honey production.

### Canopy and Tree Bee Forage

Larger trees provide major nectar flows:

- **Citrus**: High honey producers
- **Grey mangrove (*Avicennia marina*)**: Autumn–winter, good light honey
- **Pride of Madeira (*Echium candicans*)**: Masses of winter flowers
- **Prunus spp** (stone fruits): Spring nectar and pollen
- **Tupelo (*Nyssa* spp):** Premium honey source
- **[[permaculture-japanese-raisin-tree-hovenia-dulcis]] (*Hovenia dulcis*)**: Late season
- **Palms**: Various species provide seasonal flows
- **[[chinese-tallow-tree]] (*Sapium sebiferum*)**: Major honey plant; honey is moderately dark and exceptionally flavorful

## Medicinal Herbs for Livestock

The bee forage hedgerow can be designed to simultaneously serve as [[query-how-do-i-build-a-medicinal-herb-drying-rack-and-optimize-drying-conditions]] system for livestock. Animals will browse herbs as needed for self-medication if a broad spectrum is made available at all times.

### Herbaceous Medicinals

Anise, asparagus, beet, balm, borage, chicory, comfrey, cress, daisy, dandelion, dill, dock, fennel, fenugreek, flax, garlic, herb Robert, hollyhock, kale, lavender, lucerne, mallow, marjoram, mint, nettle, parsley, plantain, rosemary, rue, sage, southernwood, sorrel, tansy, thistle, thyme, valerian, violet.

**Note**: Foxglove (*Digitalis*) is listed but should be used with extreme caution due to cardiac glycoside toxicity.

### Trees with Medicinal Qualities

Alder, ash, beech, birch, elder, elm, hawthorn, hazel, holly, juniper, lemon, lime, quince, raspberry, senna, walnut (leaves promote good buttermilk in dairy animals), witch hazel.

## Fire-Retardant Hedgerows

Species combining bee forage, livestock medicine, and fire retardancy are ideal for property boundary plantings:

- **Coprosma repens**: One of the most effective fire retardant plants; dense windbreak; orange berries for poultry; dioecious (plant both sexes)
- **Dogwood (*Cornus capitata*)**: Hardy evergreen shrub, fire retardant, strawberry-like fruits for poultry
- **Hawthorn**: Traditional hedgerow species, fire retardant, bee forage, medicinal
- **Oaks (*Quercus* spp)**: Generally low combustibility, acorns for fodder, timber

## Integration with Companion Planting

The bee forage system naturally integrates with companion planting for fruit trees. Tagasaste and garlic appear as companions for nearly every fruit and nut tree species. Lucerne (alfalfa) serves simultaneously as bee forage, nitrogen fixer, and companion to apples, citrus, figs, grapes, mulberries, peaches, and walnuts.

## Design Process

1. **Map existing vegetation**: Identify current bee forage sources and flowering gaps
2. **Fill seasonal gaps**: Ensure nectar and pollen availability in every month
3. **Layer vertically**: Ground covers, understorey herbs, hedgerow shrubs, canopy trees
4. **Multi-function every plant**: Select species that provide bee forage plus at least one other function (livestock medicine, fire retardancy, windbreak, timber, fruit)
5. **Create wind-free flight corridors**: Hedgerows aligned perpendicular to prevailing winds
6. **Site hives centrally**: Within 2 miles of all forage sources, protected from wind
