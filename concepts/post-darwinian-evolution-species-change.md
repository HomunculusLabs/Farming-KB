## Post-Darwinian Evolution and Species Change

Post-Darwinian evolution encompasses the developments in evolutionary theory since Darwin's *Origin of Species* that challenge the classical model of gradual, incremental change driven solely by natural selection acting on random mutations. [[david-holmgren]] explores these ideas within the [[preface-and-author-context-of-permaculture-designers-manual]]'s twelfth principle — Creatively Use and [[creatively-use-and-respond-to-change]] — connecting evolutionary theory to [[ecological-succession]], chaos theory, and the practical work of plant and animal breeding.

## Plant and Animal Selection in Permaculture

### Ancient Technology of Selection

The application of deliberate selection pressure to create and change cultivated plant varieties and domestic animal breeds is perhaps the most ancient and important use of genetic change for building a sustainable future. Principle 10 (Use and Value Diversity) explained some factors of plant and animal selection in terms of balancing productivity and diversity.

### Uncertainty and the Case for Diversity

As human-induced change in environmental conditions becomes faster and more erratic, predicting which selected forms will prove adaptive becomes more uncertain. This argues for the primacy of diversity, including the chaotic wild diversity of weeds. The spectrum of plant, animal, and human diversity allows enormous scope both to conserve and to create new selected diversity suited to emerging conditions.

### Energy Availability and Breeding

Just as industrialization led to a surge in plant and animal breeding suited to higher energy availability, a decline in energy availability will lead to wider use of traditional varieties and breeds (where they still exist) and the creation of new ones. Permaculture and organic agriculture are at the vanguard of this trend.

### The Limits of Breeding

Plant and animal breeding have greatly contributed to humanity's expansion over 10,000 years, but breeding has failed to create any new species. Until [[fukuoka-critique-genetic-engineering]], selection was confined to working within the pool of natural diversity of each species.

## Species Evolution and Extinction

### The Scale of Extinction

The vast majority of species that have ever existed are now extinct. The 3,800-million-year history of life on earth, etched in the fossil record, is a sobering reminder of the finality of extinction. The process of extinction is well understood — humanity holds the dubious distinction of being one of the most powerful agents of species extinction in planetary history.

### The Mystery of Speciation

Conversely, the evolution of new species and larger families of life over that same time is astonishing. The processes involved in this explosion of life remain a mystery 150 years after Darwin's *Origin of Species*.

## From Gradualism to Punctuated Equilibrium

### Darwin's Gradualism

Darwin's theory of evolution changed thinking fundamentally toward acceptance of deep and persistent change at the heart of the natural world. The classical model held that evolution proceeds by gradual small increments — slow, steady accumulation of favorable mutations.

### Punctuated Equilibrium

The idea of gradual increments has now been overturned by **Punctuated Equilibrium**, where evolution proceeds by occasional jumps rather than smooth transitions. This view aligns with parallel shifts in other fields:
- **Geomorphology**: Gradualism → Catastrophism
- **Systems ecology**: Climax → [[ecological-succession-and-pulsing-ecosystems]]
- **General theory**: Chaos Theory

### The Inadequacy of Natural Selection Alone

There is widespread doubt among evolutionists that natural selection, even operating on clustered genetic mutations, is adequate as a sole mechanism to explain the evolution of species. Natural selection might be the **editor** of life, but it is not the **author**. The possibility that randomly generated genetic mutations are the lone creator of life's variety has been problematic since Darwin first proposed it.

This idea is uncomfortable for most biologists because it mirrors Creationist arguments. Even Punctuated Equilibrium can be seen as acknowledging that creative events are the real drivers in evolution.

## Mechanisms Beyond Mutation

Several processes may be generating variation in addition to random mutation:

### Gene Transfer Between Bacteria

Recent evidence about the widespread nature of gene transfer between bacteria suggests it may be a major mechanism of evolution. This horizontal gene transfer allows organisms to acquire novel genetic material from other species, bypassing the slow accumulation of mutations.

### Symbiogenesis

Lynn Margulis, who co-wrote the Gaia Hypothesis with James Lovelock in the 1970s, is well known for her 1965 hypothesis that the nucleated cell — the basis of all complex organisms — originated from a novel symbiosis. Her hypothesis, now widely accepted, suggested that membraned cells incorporated free-living bacteria along with their own millions of years of evolutionary history. Evidence about gene transfer between bacteria shows one mechanism by which symbiotic unions between species may be a continuing source of evolutionary innovation.

### Internally Directed Mutations

Some evidence suggests organisms may generate mutations internally in response to environmental pressures, rather than relying solely on random mutations. This challenges the central dogma that mutations are purely stochastic events.

### Internal Selection

Internal selection operating on mutations — where the cell or organism itself filters which mutations are tolerated before natural selection ever acts — represents another layer of the evolutionary process.

## The Billiard Table Analogy

Each species can be thought of as representing a **design solution** — an archetypal form called an "Attractor" in Chaos Theory. Minor variations are possible, but over time the archetypal form holds sway. Whenever selection pressure is released, the archetypal form re-emerges, as described in animal breeding.

### Depressions and Balls

These design solutions can be visualized as depressions in a flat table (billiard table analogy). Evolving species are balls rolling around on the table until captured in a design solution depression. The range of possibly viable design solutions is limited by:
- **Physical and metabolic limits**: An elephant with legs as thin as a horse's is not possible
- **Organizational and ecological limits**: Ecosystem self-organization provides niches into which species are constrained to fit

### Getting Out of the Depression

Plant and animal breeding moves balls around within depressions (microevolution). Some large force or different mechanism is needed to get a ball (a species) out of a depression and onto the table surface (macroevolution). Once out on the table, the slightest force can cause the ball to go anywhere, and sooner rather than later it will be captured by another depression.

### New Zealand's Flightless Wrens

A compelling example of ecological constraint on design solutions: in most temperate forests, rodents occupy the forest floor. In New Zealand's relatively recent ecosystems, there were no rodents or mammals, but there was a need for a small animal scurrying in forest litter eating seeds and insects. A series of flightless wrens were the evolutionary solution. The arrival of rodents with Maoris and Europeans drove the wrens to extinction — the same niche, different design solution, but the new solution outcompeted the old.

## The Current Planetary Situation as Evolutionary Stimulus

The extensive extinction of specialized species and their replacement by hardy generalist species has led many biologists to believe we are witnessing the "McDonaldisation of the world's biota." However, the catastrophic changes causing species extinctions also create novel conditions that may stimulate or accelerate mechanisms leading to new species.

### Factors Driving Potential Speciation

If evolution proceeds by diverse processes in jumps analogous to the pulsing ecosystems model, it is hard to imagine a greater stimulation for evolutionary jumps than the current planetary situation:
- Mining and use of almost half of fossil fuels accumulated over the last 1,000 million years
- Six billion people highly connected and concentrated into one global population
- World trade leading to unintentional transfer of lifeforms on a scale never possible in the history of life
- Energetic intentional introductions of species across the planet

These factors — the same ones driving extinction — may simultaneously generate the conditions for a flood of new species. This paradox mirrors the [[permaculture-principle-1-observe-interact]] that "the problem is the solution" — the destruction of existing ecological capital simultaneously creates the conditions for reorganisation and new productive systems.
