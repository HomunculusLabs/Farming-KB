### Stage 3: Shrubland

In five to fifteen years, the weedy field is clothed with perennial shrubs.
Biomass and biodiversity continue to increase as the three-dimensional
structure of shrubs creates new niches for wildlife. Root systems penetrate
deeper, breaking up subsoil and bringing minerals to the surface.

### Stage 4: Young Forest

With enough rain and fertility, in two or more decades the shrubs give way to
a young forest. Canopy trees begin to dominate, and the full complement of
forest layers develops. This is the endpoint of natural succession in most
temperate regions.
