## Overview

Known as pioppino, black poplar mushroom, or yanagimatsutake (Japanese), Agrocybe aegerita is an excellent edible and medicinal mushroom. It is one of Stamets' favorite edible mushrooms and an excellent candidate for stump recycling and log cultivation. See also [[antiviral-properties-of-mushrooms]], [[mycoremediation-species-selection-guide]], and [[medicinal-mushroom-extraction-preparation]].

## Description

- **Cap:** Often up to 12 inches (30 cm) in diameter; convex to hemispheric, expanding to plane
- **Cap surface:** Smooth, yellowish gray to grayish brown to tan, darker toward center
- **Gills:** Gray at first, becoming chocolate brown with spore maturity
- **Stem:** White, adorned with well-developed membranous ring; spores color the ring brown
- **Spores:** Brown, 9-11 by 5-7 μm

## Habitat and Cultivation

- **Distribution:** Mexico, southern US (Mississippi, Louisiana, Georgia), southern Europe, Far East
- **Natural habitat:** Saprophytic, often in clusters, on stumps of hardwoods
- **Preferred trees:** Cottonwoods, willows, poplars, maples, box elders; in China: tea-oil trees
- **Type of rot:** White rot (unclear)
- **Mycelium fragrance:** Mealy, farinaceous, but not pleasant
- **Temperature:** 60-70°F, primarily fall fruiting
- **Cultivation:** Easily cultivated on stumps or half-buried logs; scarify logs with chainsaw and pack sawdust spawn into wounds

## Medicinal Properties

### Antitumor and Immunomodulatory
- Yoshida et al. (1996) reported administration of 3 high molecular weight polysaccharides (carboxymethylated (1,3)-alpha-D-glucans) induced significant antitumor activity
- Polysaccharides stimulate immune function and enhance natural killer cell activity
- One of the species with confirmed antiviral properties

### Anti-inflammatory
- Contains bioactive compounds with anti-inflammatory effects
- Traditional use in Asian medicine for various inflammatory conditions

### Other Properties
- Antioxidant compounds present
- Potential hepatoprotective effects
- Studied for cardiovascular benefits

## Mycorestoration

- Excellent candidate for stump recycling — colonizes and decomposes tree stumps efficiently
- Can be used to accelerate decomposition of felled trees
- Log cultivation on cottonwood, willow, and poplar stumps
- Contributes to nutrient cycling in forest ecosystems

## Culinary Notes

- Excellent edible — one of Stamets' favorites
- Firm texture, good flavor
- Versatile in cooking — sauteed, grilled, in soups and stir-fries
- Caps and stems both edible
- Market value good due to large size and attractive appearance

## Taxonomic Notes

- Variable mushroom that may be split into several distinct taxa after more research
- Once called Pholiota aegerita
- Synonyms: Pholiota cylindracea Gillet, A. cylindracea (DC. ex Fr.) Maire
- Related species A. molesta and A. praecox can be cultivated using the same methods
- Asian mycologists still prefer A. cylindracea nomenclature
