## Permaculture Plant Index M-R

This index covers plants from the letter M through R as catalogued in *Permaculture Plants: A Selection*. Entries are organized by common or scientific name with their primary uses and page references.

## M (continued)

### Medicinal and Therapeutic Plants
- **Medicine** — page 63, 101. Traditional plant-based medicine systems drawing on numerous species in the text.
- **Menstrual disorders** — pages 75, 87, 88. Several species address menstrual complaints: dong quai (87), raspberry leaf (87).
- **Mesembryanthemum** (*Mesembryanthemum crystallinum*) — page 133. Ice plant; edible succulent leaves; accumulates salt in tissues.
- **Mint** (*Mentha* spp.) — pages 114, 115, 119. Aromatic culinary herbs for teas, cooking, and companion planting. Spreads vigorously; best contained.
- **Mosquitoes** — page 121. Aquatic plants that reduce mosquito breeding include mosquito fern (*Azolla*) and duckweed (*Lemna*) which cover water surfaces.

### Mulch and Soil Building
- **Mulch** — page 17. Organic material applied to soil surface for moisture retention, weed suppression, and soil improvement. Many species in the text provide mulch material through leaf litter, prunings, or dedicated green manure crops.
- **Horse mulga** (*Acacia farnesiana*) — pages 17, 20, 134. Prickly acacia of arid Australia; fodder in drought; mulch producer.

### Musicals and Bamboo
- **Musical instruments** — pages 93, 97. Bamboo produces traditional wind instruments (pan pipes, flutes); eucalypt wood used in didgeridoo construction.
- **Musical reeds** — page 124. Giant reed (*Arundo donax*) provides reeds for woodwind instruments.

### Myoporum and Native Shrubs
- **Myoporum insulare** (Boobialla) — pages 112, 136. Australian coastal shrub with edible berries; salt tolerant; hedge plant.
- **Myoporum platycarpum** — page 136. Inland Myoporum species.
- **Myrtle berry** — page 73. See lilli pilli and myrtus.
- **Myrtus ugni** (Chilean guava) — page 73. Low-growing shrub producing aromatic, sweet berries; excellent for hedging and edible landscaping.

## N

### Nasturtium and Edible Flowers
- **Nasturtiums** (*Tropaeolum majus*) — page 119. Edible flowers, leaves, and seeds (capers); companion plant for pest deterrence.
- **[[tetragonia-tetragonioides]]*) — page 131. Edible coastal ground cover; heat-tolerant spinach substitute.

## O

### Oak Species (*Quercus* spp.)
- **Oak** — pages 80-82, 113, 115, 136. One of the most important timber and ecological genera in [[drought-tolerant-planting-design]]; important permaculture staple.

### Oils and Wax
- **Oil** — pages 33, 40, 60, 68, 81, 83, 87, 90, 92, 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103, 104, 105, 107, 108, 117, 125. Plant oil sources include: olive (60), walnut (32-33), macadamia (31), jojoba wax (45), neem oil (87), and numerous palms (98-108).
- **Oil palm** (*Elaeis guineensis*) — page 102. Tropical palm producing the world's most widely traded vegetable oil.

### Old Man Saltbush
- **Old man saltbush** (*Atriplex nummularia*) — pages 128, 134. Australian halophyte shrub for livestock fodder on saline land; drought and salt tolerant; erosion control.

### Opuntia (Prickly Pear)
- **Opuntia spp.** — page 112. Cacti producing edible pads (nopales) and fruits (tunas); fodder in arid regions; living fence.

## P

### Palm Species (*Palmaceae/PALMACEAE*)
- **Palms** — pages 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103, 104, 106, 107, 108, 117. One of the most diverse and useful plant families in permaculture:
  - **Bamboo palms** (*Chamaedorea* spp.) — shade, ornamental
  - **Cabbage palms** — edible heart (palm cabbage)
  - **Coconut** (*Cocos nucifera*) — page 103. The "tree of life": food, oil, fibre, timber, thatch
  - **Date palm** (*[[bactris-gasipaes]]*) — pages 99, 102. Edible fruit and heart of palm
  - **Piassaba palms** — fibre for rope and brooms
  - **Raphia palms** — raffia fibre from leaves
  - **Royal palms** (*Roystonea* spp.) — ornamental, thatch, timber
  - **Sago palm** (*Metroxylon sagu*) — page 104. Edible sago starch from pith
  - **Sugar palm** (*Arenga pinnata*) — page 99. Sugar, sap, fibre, thatch

### Passionfruit (*Passiflora* spp.)
- **Passiflora edulis** (Purple passionfruit) — pages 68, 112. The most widely cultivated passionfruit; vigorous vine producing aromatic pulp.
- **[[passiflora-incarnata]]** (Maypop) — page 69. North American native; hardy vine with edible fruit and sedative medicinal properties.
- **Passiflora herbertiana** (Herbert River passionfruit) — page 69. Australian native passionfruit.
- **Passiflora antioquiensis** (Banana passionfruit, Curuba) — page 70. Andean species with banana-like fruit.

### Pecan
- **Pecan** (*Carya illinoinensis*) — pages 38, 42, 111. Large North American hickory producing rich, buttery nuts; important agroforestry tree; valuable timber.

### Persimmon
- **Persimmon** (*Diospyros* spp.) — pages 59, 60, 118.
  - **Diospyros kaki** (Japanese persimmon) — page 59. Most widely cultivated species; sweet fruit when astringency resolves.
  - **[[phragmites-australis]]** (Common reed) — page 124. Tall perennial grass of wetlands worldwide; used for thatch, biofuel, water filtration, and phytoremediation.

### Photinia
- **Photinia glabra** — page 112. Evergreen hedging shrub with red new growth.
- **Photinia serrulata** — page 112. Larger photinia species for screens and hedges.
- **Photinia spp.** — page 137. Genus of evergreen shrubs popular as ornamental hedges.

### Pine Species (*Pinus* spp.)
- **Pinus pinea** (Stone pine) — pages 42, 44. Mediterranean pine producing large edible pine nuts (pignoli); iconic landscape tree.
- **Pinus edulis** (Colorado pinyon) — pages 42, 43. Southwestern North American pine; edible nuts.
- **Pinus cembroides** (Mexican pinyon) — pages 42, 43. Mexican pine nut species.
- **Pinus cembra** (Swiss stone pine) — page 42. Alpine European pine with edible seeds.
- **Pinus monophylla** (Single-leaf pinyon) — pages 42, 44. Great Basin pine nut species.
- **Pinus coulteri** (Coulter pine) — page 43. Produces the heaviest cones of any pine.
- **Pinus jeffreyi** (Jeffrey pine) — page 43. Western North American pine; resembles ponderosa pine.
- **Pinus lambertiana** (Sugar pine) — page 44. Produces the longest cones of any pine; edible seeds.
- **Pinus radiata** (Monterey pine) — page 77. Widely planted plantation species; fast-growing timber.
- **Pinus sabiniana** (Gray pine/Foothill pine) — pages 42, 44. California endemic with edible large nuts.

### Pistachio
- **Pistacia vera** (Pistachio) — page 40. Desert nut tree requiring hot dry summers and cold winters; commercially important in Iran, California, and Mediterranean regions.
- **Pistacia atlantica** (Mt. Atlas mastic) — pages 40, 136. Wild relative; source of mastic resin.
