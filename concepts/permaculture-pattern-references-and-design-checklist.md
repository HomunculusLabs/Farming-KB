## Designer's Checklist

When applying pattern analysis in [[permaculture-design]], study the relationships of **orders** and **forms** in nature. The following principles should guide patterned design systems:

### Size and Scale

Patterned systems must be of appropriate size, or of the right order for their context. A pattern that works at one scale may fail at another. The designer must match pattern scale to the intended function and site conditions.

### Key Design Principles

1. **Read in pattern analysis**: Before designing, study the existing patterns of the site — water flow, wind patterns, sun angles, vegetation distribution, and animal movement corridors.

2. **Study orders and forms**: Understand the hierarchy of pattern orders (from point to line to branch to net to spiral) and how natural forms express these orders.

3. **Match pattern to function**: Select pattern forms that serve the intended function — spirals for compact accessibility, nets for distribution, branches for collection and dispersal, waves for edge effects.

4. **Work with existing patterns**: Design interventions that enhance and redirect existing natural patterns rather than imposing arbitrary geometries.

5. **Create multi-functional patterns**: Each pattern application should serve multiple functions simultaneously — the herb spiral provides herbs, microclimates, water conservation, and aesthetic value.

6. **Test against natural models**: Compare designed patterns against their natural counterparts to ensure they capture the essential efficiencies of natural systems.

7. **Consider the total system**: Every pattern imposition affects the whole system. Evaluate cascading effects before implementing any pattern-based design change.
