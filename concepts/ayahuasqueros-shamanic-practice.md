## Overview

## Training and Lineage

Traditional shamanic training among Shipibo and other Amazonian peoples
typically involves lengthy apprenticeships, dietary restrictions, and
periods of isolation in the jungle. Knowledge of plant medicines, icaros,
and diagnostic techniques is passed down through family lines or through
formal apprenticeship relationships.

In the tourism context, training programs have emerged that compress this
knowledge into shorter courses. Some practitioners offer two-week training
programs, though the quality and authenticity of such condensed training
is debatable. One observation noted two visiting ayahuasqueros attending
a two-week training program—one described as a "sweet and wonderful guy"
whose partner constantly hustled visitors for spare change.

## The Role of Icaros

Icaros are sacred songs sung during ayahuasca ceremonies. They serve
multiple functions: calling protective spirits, directing the visionary
experience, and facilitating healing. The icaros are considered one of
the most powerful elements of the ceremony and represent a major component
of a shaman's training and expertise.

In tourist-oriented ceremonies, icaros may be performed but their impact
can be diminished by linguistic barriers and the shallower ceremonial
context. In community ceremonies, icaros are sung with deeper intention
and cultural resonance.
