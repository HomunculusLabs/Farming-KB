# Willows for Erosion Control and Fodder in Permaculture

Willows (Salix spp) are a genus of over 300 species with the widest
natural geographical distribution of any tree genus, spanning the entire
northern hemisphere plus [[water-management]] [[riparian-zone-restoration]] planting and wetland restoration.

Important design note: willows should not be planted close to gardens,
septic systems, or orchards due to their aggressive water-seeking root
systems.

### Cricket Bat Willow (Salix alba 'Coerulea')
A female cultivar yielding light timber that withstands sudden impact.
Requires good soil by stream sides for rapid upright growth, ready for
cutting from 12 to 15 years. Also shows potential in the craft industry.

### Weeping Willow (Salix babylonica)
Probably the best known of all willows, reaching 10-15 metres. Originally
from the Far East, introduced to Europe over two centuries ago. Because
of relative cold sensitivity, hybrids with S. alba and S. fragilis are
often grown in temperate regions.

### Osier Willow (Salix viminalis)
Specifically grown for the basket and willow weaving trade. Cultivars
have been developed to produce different coloured and textured rods.
Osier stool beds are raised from cuttings and harvested annually as
rods. There is considerable potential for this tree in basket and
furniture production.

### Corkscrew Willow (Salix matsudana 'Tortuosa')
A female clone where branches are twisted, providing ornamental interest
and unique craft material.
