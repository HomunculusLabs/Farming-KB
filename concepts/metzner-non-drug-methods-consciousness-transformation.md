## Eastern Traditions: Yoga and Meditation

During the 1970s, Metzner's focus shifted to the exploration of nondrug
methods derived from Eastern spiritual traditions, particularly yoga and
meditation. These practices, which have been refined over thousands of years,
offer systematic methods for altering consciousness through attention,
breath control, postural manipulation, and contemplative inquiry. The states
accessed through sustained meditation practice — states of deep concentration,
absorption, and non-dual awareness — share phenomenological features with
psychedelic states, including the dissolution of ordinary self-other
boundaries, the experience of luminous or formless awareness, and the
perception of reality as fundamentally interconnected.

Metzner's engagement with these traditions reflected a recognition that
the psychedelic experience, while powerful, was not the only or necessarily
the most reliable path to expanded consciousness. The systematic nature
of meditative practice — its repeatability, its progressive development,
its integration with ethical and philosophical frameworks — offered
advantages that the comparatively abrupt and uncontrolled psychedelic
experience did not.

## Shamanic Non-Drug Methods

The 1980s brought Metzner into contact with the anthropological literature
on shamanism, where he encountered a wide range of non-drug methods for
inducing altered states. These included rhythmic drumming, the technique
most widely associated with shamanic journeying across Northern Hemisphere
cultures; fasting and wilderness vision questing, practiced by indigenous
cultures of the Americas; and sweat lodge ceremonies, which combine heat,
darkness, and communal ritual to produce profound states of physical and
psychological purification.
