## Coleman Wire Wicket Row Covers and Protective Cultivation Systems

Eliot Coleman's protective cultivation system represents one of the most
practical and cost-effective approaches to year-round [[coleman-unheated-greenhouse-design]], this double-layer protection enables
harvest of cold-hardy vegetables throughout the winter months in USDA zones 4–6.

## The Layered Protection Principle

Coleman's winter harvest system relies on a concept he calls **"double
protection"** — two layers of protection between the crop and the open air:

1. **Layer 1 — The greenhouse or high tunnel**: Provides the primary wind
   barrier and traps solar heat gain during daylight hours
2. **Layer 2 — The row cover**: A secondary fabric layer directly over the crop
   that provides additional insulation [[cervantes-microclimate-frost-protection]] per layer
- **Durability**: Can last 2–4 seasons with careful handling

### The Synergistic Effect
The greenhouse provides primary protection while the row cover adds secondary
insulation directly over the crop. The synergy between these two layers is the
key innovation — neither layer alone would provide sufficient protection for
mid-winter harvest, but together they create a microclimate where cold-hardy
crops continue slow growth even during the darkest, coldest months.

### Daytime Heat Management
- During sunny winter days, the greenhouse air temperature can rise
  significantly even when outside temperatures are below freezing
- The row cover traps this warm air close to the crop, maintaining soil
  temperatures that support root function
- On overcast days, the protection effect is reduced but still provides
  meaningful frost protection
- The soil itself acts as a thermal mass, absorbing heat during sunny periods
  and releasing it slowly during cold nights

- Pull back row covers on warm sunny days to prevent overheating
