# Van Life Off Grid

Camper van living off-grid combines mobility with self-sufficiency. A well-built camper van provides shelter, power, water, cooking, and sanitation without hookups or campground facilities. The compact space demands efficient design and multi-purpose systems, but the result is freedom to live and travel independently.

## Van Selection

### Cargo Vans (Best for Custom Builds)

**Ford Transit**
- Available in 150, 250, 350 configurations
- High roof and extended length options
- EcoBoost gas or diesel engines
- Good parts availability
- Price: $30,000-55,000 new, $15,000-35,000 used

**Mercedes Sprinter**
- Industry standard for van conversions
- Excellent fuel economy (diesel)
- High roof standard on 2500/3500 models
- Most expensive to purchase and maintain
- Price: $35,000-60,000 new, $20,000-40,000 used

**Ram ProMaster**
- Front-wheel drive gives lowest floor height
- Wide interior (75.6 inches between wheel wells)
- Gas engine only (3.6L V6)
- Most affordable of the big three
- Price: $28,000-50,000 new, $15,000-30,000 used

### Key Selection Criteria

- High roof: Essential for standing (6+ feet interior height)
- 144-170 inch wheelbase: Balance of space and drivability
- AWD/4WD: Consider for winter and off-pavement travel
- Mileage: Under 100,000 miles preferred for used vans
- Rust: Inspect frame, rocker panels, and wheel wells carefully

## Insulation

Van insulation must address heat, cold, condensation, and sound. Metal van walls conduct temperature aggressively and create severe condensation issues.

### Havelock Wool (Best Overall)

- Sheep's wool batts cut to fit between van ribs
- Naturally absorbs and releases moisture (prevents condensation)
- R-3.5 per inch
- Fire-resistant, sound-dampening
- Cost: $1,000-1,500

### Thinsulate (Popular)

- 3M automotive insulation, originally designed for vehicles
- Easy to install — adhesive backing
- Good sound dampening
- Cost: $400-800

### Closed-Cell Spray Foam (Most Effective)

- Sprayed directly to metal — no gaps
- Prevents condensation completely
- R-6.5 per inch
- Must be done professionally or with rental equipment
- Cost: $1,500-2,500

### Reflectix Alone (Insufficient)

- Reflectix is a radiant barrier, not insulation
- Only effective with an air gap on one side
- Do not rely on Reflectix as your only insulation

### Vapor Barrier

Regardless of insulation type, include a vapor barrier on the warm (interior) side to prevent condensation inside the wall cavity. Use Reflectix or plastic sheeting taped at all seams.

## Solar Power System

### Panel Options

- Roof-mounted rigid panels: 200-400 watts total, permanently mounted
- Roof-mounted flexible panels: Conform to curved roof, slightly less efficient
- Portable panels: Deploy on the ground when parked, can be aimed at the sun
- Combination: 200W roof-mounted + 100-200W portable

### Battery Bank

- **LiFePO4 (Recommended)**: 100-200 Ah, lasts 3,000-5,000 cycles, lightweight
- **Lead-acid AGM**: 200-400 Ah, cheaper but heavier, 500-1,000 cycles
- Battery management system (BMS) required for lithium

### System Components

- Solar charge controller (MPPT preferred): 30-40A
- Inverter: 1000-2000W pure sine wave
- DC-DC charger: Charges house batteries from alternator while driving
- Battery monitor: Victron BMV-712 or similar
- Fuses and disconnects: Properly sized for all circuits
- Wiring: 10 AWG minimum for solar runs, 2/0 AWG for battery-to-inverter

### Daily Power Budget (Typical)

| Item | Watts | Hours/Day | Wh/Day |
|------|-------|-----------|--------|
| Fridge (compressor) | 40 | 8 (duty cycle) | 320 |
| LED lights | 20 | 6 | 120 |
| Laptop | 65 | 4 | 260 |
| Phone charging | 10 | 3 | 30 |
| Vent fan | 30 | 4 | 120 |
| Water pump | 50 | 0.5 | 25 |
| **Total** | | | **875** |

A 200W solar array with 100Ah LiFePO4 battery handles this budget comfortably in most weather.

## Water System

### Fresh Water

- Tank: 10-20 gallons (more = heavier, affects fuel economy)
- Mount below floor or in a lower cabinet for center of gravity
- 12V diaphragm pump with pressure switch
- PEX or vinyl tubing with quick-connect fittings
- Spigot at kitchen, optional shower

### Greywater

- 5-10 gallon greywater tank (or direct drain with [[off-grid-waste-management|greywater]] filter)
- For off-grid use: greywater can drain through a simple grease trap into a dispersal area
- Greywater garden: direct greywater to mulch basins around plants when stationary

### Water Heating

- Portable propane camp shower (simplest)
- 12V immersion heater element in a tank
- Propane instant water heater (more complex but convenient)
- Heat water on stove and transfer to shower bag
