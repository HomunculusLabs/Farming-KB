## Pre-Inoculation Requirements

Before inoculation, the following conditions must be met:

1. **Substrate must be fully cooled**: Jars should be at room
   temperature before inoculation. Hot jars will kill spores or
   mycelium. Patience is critical — rushing this step is a
   common beginner mistake that leads to failed inoculations.

2. **Work area must be clean**: The inoculation should be performed
   in the cleanest possible environment. A simple still-air box
   made from a cardboard or plastic container with arm holes
   provides significantly better contamination control than open-
   air inoculation.

3. **Tools must be sterilized**: Syringe needles must be flamed
   with a butane lighter or alcohol lamp before use. Scalpels
   for agar work must also be flame-sterilized. Caution is
   essential: alcohol and spray Lysol are highly flammable and
   should not be used near open flames.

## Inoculation Procedure

The basic procedure for inoculating grain jars is as follows:

1. Shake the inoculant syringe well to distribute spores or
   mycelial fragments evenly
2. Flame the needle tip until glowing red, then allow to cool
   (a few seconds, or touch to a clean surface briefly)
3. Wipe the injection port or jar lid with an alcohol wipe
4. Insert the needle through the port or a small hole in the lid
5. Inject 1-2 ml of inoculant into the substrate
6. Withdraw the needle and re-flame the tip before proceeding
   to the next jar
7. Close the jar lid loosely

For agar square inoculation, the procedure involves flaming the
scalpel, opening the petri dish only briefly to cut a small
section of colonized agar, and transferring it to the jar with
minimal air exposure. The jar lid is opened only a crack and the
agar piece is dropped in quickly.

## Post-Inoculation Handling

After inoculation, jars should be shaken to distribute the
inoculant throughout the substrate. This is particularly
important for spore syringes and liquid culture, as it places
inoculum at multiple points within the jar, dramatically
speeding up colonization. After shaking, the lid should be
loosened again to allow gas exchange for the growing mycelium.

Jars are then placed in a clean, draft-free location at room
temperature (approximately 20°C) or slightly higher for
colonization. Light is not required during this phase and some
species actually colonize better in darkness.
