# Soil Protozoa and Nutrient Cycling

Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotic organisms that play a critical role in
the soil food web as the primary consumers of bacteria. In "Teaming With
Microbes," Lowenfels and Lewis highlight protozoa as essential players in
nutrient cycling, particularly through their role in [[lowenfels-mycorrhizal-network-nutrient-sharing]].

This process, called the microbial loop or protozoan grazing pathway, is one
of the most efficient natural mechanisms for nitrogen mineralization. A
single protozoan can consume thousands of bacteria per day, and the collective
grazing activity of protozoan populations can mineralize significant
quantities of nitrogen. In healthy soils, protozoan grazing may supply 30 to
80 percent of plant-available nitrogen, far exceeding the contribution from
abiotic decomposition processes alone.

## Soil Moisture Effects on Protozoan Activity

Protozoa are highly sensitive to soil moisture conditions, and their activity
follows a pattern closely tied to water availability. In dry soils, most
protozoa encyst, forming dormant resting stages that can survive for months
or years. When soil moisture increases, cysts germinate rapidly and protozoan
populations can explode within hours to days. This rapid response means that
protozoan-mediated nitrogen mineralization spikes after rainfall or irrigation,
delivering a pulse of plant-available nitrogen precisely when plants are best
able to use it.

This moisture dependence has important practical implications. Overwatering
can create anaerobic conditions that favor ciliates but harm flagellates and
amoebae, potentially disrupting the balanced protozoan community. Well-drained
soils with good [[lowenfels-soil-fungi-decomposition]] pathways, as some amoebae feed on
fungal hyphae, contributing to [[lowenfels-cover-crops-soil-biology]], creates favorable habitat.
Avoiding excessive tillage preserves the soil pore structure protozoa depend
on for movement and feeding. The key principle from Lowenfels and Lewis is
that protozoa thrive when the entire soil food web is intact and functioning.
