# Cannabis Iron (Fe)

## Overview

Iron is a [[query-how-to-fix-zinc-deficiency-in-cannabis]], and [[cannabis-copper-nutrient]]

### Progression of Deficiency

1. Younger leaves and growing shoots turn pale green, progressing to yellow between veins starting at the petiole, while veins remain green
2. More and more leaves turn yellow and develop interveinal chlorosis
3. Larger leaves finally yellow and develop interveinal chlorosis
4. In acute cases, leaves develop necrosis and drop

### Treatment

- Lower the soil pH to 6.5 or less
- Avoid fertilizers with excessive manganese, zinc, and copper, which inhibit iron uptake
- High levels of cannabis-phosphorus|phosphorus compete with iron uptake
- Improve drainage; excessively wet soil holds little oxygen to spur iron uptake
- Damaged or rotten roots also lower iron uptake
- Increase root-zone temperature
- Apply chelated iron in liquid form to root zone
- Protect [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]] from light, which decomposes chelates
- Leaves should green up in four to five days
- Complete, balanced hydroponic nutrients contain iron; deficiencies are seldom a problem with quality fertilizers

### Organic Sources

Organic sources of iron, as well as chelates, include cow, horse, and chicken manure. Use only well-rotted manures to avoid burning plants.

## Toxicity

Excess iron is rare. High levels of iron do not damage cannabis directly, but it can interfere with phosphorus uptake.

### Symptoms

- Leaves turn bronze accompanied by small, dark brown leaf spots
- If iron chelate is over-applied, it will kill the plant in a few days

### Treatment

Leach plants heavily to remove excess iron.

## Nutrient Requirements

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Optimal range | 2.0-10 ppm |
| Average target | 5.0 ppm |
