# Cannabis Iron (Fe)

## Overview

Iron is a [[cannabis-indoor-soil-mix-and-preparation]] classified as **immobile** in cannabis plants. It is available in a soluble chelated form that is immediately available for absorption by roots. Iron deficiency is common in alkaline soils (pH above 6.5) and uncommon when the pH is below 6.5. Iron, [[query-how-to-fix-zinc-deficiency-in-cannabis]], and [[cannabis-manganese-nutrient]] are the three most common micronutrients found deficient in cannabis gardens, especially in arid climates with alkaline soil and water.

## Role in Plant Metabolism

Iron is fundamental to enzyme systems and to transport electrons during photosynthesis, respiration, and chlorophyll production. Specific functions include:

- Permits plants to use the energy provided by sugar
- Acts as a catalyst for chlorophyll production
- Necessary for nitrate and sulfate reduction and assimilation
- Assists in nitrogen utilization alongside manganese
- Colors the earth from brown to red, according to concentration

Plants have a difficult time absorbing iron. Acidic soils normally contain adequate iron for cannabis growth. Iron colors the earth from brown to red, according to concentration.

## Chelated Iron

Iron is available in a soluble chelated form that is immediately available for absorption by the roots. Chelates keep metal ions soluble in water and suppress reactions with other materials. Roots take in chelated metals in a stable, soluble form. Important considerations for chelates:

- **DTPA** -- Most effective in pH below 6.5
- **EDDHA** -- Effective up to pH 8.0
- **EDTA** -- Slow to cause leaf burn

Chelates decompose rapidly in low levels of UV light, including light from HID bulbs and sunlight. Keep chelates out of light to protect them from rapid decomposition. Exposing the nutrient solution to light causes depleted iron. Sterilizing the nutrient solution with UV light causes iron to precipitate.

## Deficiency

Iron deficiencies are common when pH is above 6.5. Symptoms may appear during rapid growth or stressful times and can disappear by themselves. Young leaves cannot draw immobile iron from older leaves, even when it is present in the soil.

### Symptoms

- First symptoms appear on smaller leaves
- Veins remain green while areas between veins turn yellow (interveinal chlorosis)
- Chlorosis starts at the opposite end of the leaf tip (at the apex attached by the petiole)
- Leaf edges can turn upward as deficiency progresses
- Leaves fall off in severe cases
- Iron deficiency is sometimes traced to an excess of [[cannabis-copper-nutrient]]

### Progression of Deficiency

1. Younger leaves and growing shoots turn pale green, progressing to yellow between veins starting at the petiole, while veins remain green
2. More and more leaves turn yellow and develop interveinal chlorosis
3. Larger leaves finally yellow and develop interveinal chlorosis
4. In acute cases, leaves develop necrosis and drop

### Treatment

- Lower the soil pH to 6.5 or less
- Avoid fertilizers with excessive manganese, zinc, and copper, which inhibit iron uptake
- High levels of cannabis-phosphorus|phosphorus compete with iron uptake
- Improve drainage; excessively wet soil holds little oxygen to spur iron uptake
- Damaged or rotten roots also lower iron uptake
- Increase root-zone temperature
- Apply chelated iron in liquid form to root zone
- Protect nutrient solution from light, which decomposes chelates
- Leaves should green up in four to five days
- Complete, balanced hydroponic nutrients contain iron; deficiencies are seldom a problem with quality fertilizers

### Organic Sources

Organic sources of iron, as well as chelates, include cow, horse, and chicken manure. Use only well-rotted manures to avoid burning plants.

## Toxicity

Excess iron is rare. High levels of iron do not damage cannabis directly, but it can interfere with phosphorus uptake.

### Symptoms

- Leaves turn bronze accompanied by small, dark brown leaf spots
- If iron chelate is over-applied, it will kill the plant in a few days

### Treatment

Leach plants heavily to remove excess iron.
