# Reishi and Maitake — Medicinal Polypore Cultivation

Reishi (*Ganoderma* spp.) and maitake (*[[grifola-frondosa]]*) are two of the most valued medicinal
mushrooms in the world. Reishi, known as the "mushroom of immortality" in traditional Chinese
medicine, has been revered for thousands of years for its broad-spectrum health benefits. Maitake,
also called hen of the woods, is both a premier edible and a potent medicinal mushroom. Both are
polypores that can be cultivated outdoors and indoors, though each presents unique challenges and
rewards for the grower.

## Reishi (Ganoderma spp.)

### Species and Ecology

The genus *Ganoderma* includes several important species: *G. lucidum* (the classic reishi or ling
chi), *G. tsugae* (hemlock reishi), *G. curtisii* (golden reishi), *G. applanatum* (artist conk), and
*G. oregonense*. Reishis are versatile in their ability to grow on many hardwood types, with the
exception of *G. tsugae*, which prefers hemlock. The mycelium is aggressive, white and linear,
hugging agar surfaces almost flat, and produces a signature yellow metabolite that darkens to orange.
The mycelium has such extreme tensile strength that it can be formed into bricks and used for
construction materials.

### Fruiting Body Development

Reishi begins as an antler with a whitish tip, flattening to a cap positioned off-center with white
pores underneath. The growing edge is white or off-yellow when actively growing, dulling and darkening
at maturity. The spore print is cinnamon to chocolate brown, and spores typically cover the entire
cap surface due to convecting air currents.

### Cultivation

**Outdoor:** Reishi will not fruit aboveground — use buried hardwood logs and stumps. Trenched log
cultivation works well: inoculate small-diameter logs, colonize for six to eight months, then bury
them tightly packed. Fruiting takes ten to twelve months, typically from spring through fall. Yields
are good; reishis are dense and prolific fruiters.

**Indoor:** [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]] sterilized for two hours. Sawdust fruits almost immediately after
colonization, typically in two to three weeks. Antlers forming inside a bag keep growing for weeks
until the bag is opened to allow flattening and sporulation. Yields are good.

### Harvesting and Storage

Mushrooms can be harvested at any stage. Antlers are believed to contain more [[amanita-muscaria-medicinal-properties-heart-anxiety-dreams]]
than fully developed caps, though this is unsubstantiated. Harvest mature specimens while they still
have their growing edge, before spore release. Fresh reishi keeps one month at 38–42°F (3–6°C).
Best preserved by drying, powdering, or making medicinal extracts while fresh.

### Medicinal Properties

Reishi has one of the highest beta-glucan levels of any medicinal fungus. Key benefits include immune
modulation, blood pressure regulation, cholesterol reduction, cardiovascular and digestive support,
antiviral and antibacterial activity, anti-inflammatory effects, and cancer cell line suppression.
It is particularly noted for blood sugar regulation, making it one of the best natural treatments for
diabetes patients.

### Mycoremediation

Reishi mycelium has extremely high tensile strength and affinity for inhibiting and lysing bacterial
cells. Myceliated substrate forms an almost rubber-like material that can be dried and cut into shapes
for manufactured filter modules, making it ideal for [[mycofiltration-of-water]].

## Maitake (Grifola frondosa)

### Species and Ecology

Maitake is a polypore with flat, overlapping petals that curl back like large fractals. Wild specimens
typically weigh 12–15 pounds and have been found exceeding 100 pounds. The mushroom fruits from buried
wood, most prominently around the bases of large dying oaks. It develops a "knot" right at the soil
surface, filling its space with carbon dioxide, causing elongated portions to explore upward through
leaf litter. Once aboveground, stems and petals expand and develop grayish-brown pigments triggered by
filtered light.

### Light Exposure and Quality

An interesting observation from wild cultivation is that maitakes covered with sticks and leaves
while growing produced "albino" specimens — similar to white asparagus. This raises questions about
how light exposure affects the nutritional [[maitake-mushroom-research]] of fruitbodies. Some companies
cultivate medicinal fungi on grain in complete darkness, powdering them before any light stimulation,
which may significantly affect the quality of [[cordyceps-cancer-bioactive-compounds]].

### Cultivation

**Outdoor:** This mushroom will not fruit aboveground. Use buried hardwood logs, rounds, and stumps.
Inoculate, colonize for six to eight months, then bury. Fruiting takes one to two years at the
ground-wood interface on the shadier side. Expect one flush per year outdoors.

**Indoor:** Supplemented sawdust, sterilized two hours. Inoculation to fruiting takes 30–50 days.
Gas exchange is critical for transitioning from knotting to petal formation. Fruiting blocks should be
incubated in bags to allow CO₂ pooling. After colonization, cut top corners or small slits to allow
modest gas exchange while preserving high humidity. This triggers "knotting" — an irregular dark gray
mass that gradually elongates and curls into recognizable maitake fronds as oxygen exposure increases.
Yields are fair, with two flushes three to four weeks apart.

### Harvesting and Storage

Pull the entire cluster from the substrate and trim the base. Inspect carefully — maitakes engulf
objects like sticks and twigs, wrapping tissue around them. Fresh maitakes keep one month at 38–42°F
(3–6°C). Maitake is one of the best mushrooms to dry; rehydration restores its crunchy texture almost
entirely.

### Medicinal Properties

Maitake has protein content of 27–30% (dry weight) and the highest vitamin D levels of any known
mushroom — over 1,250 IU per 100 grams. Benefits mirror reishi: immune modulation, blood sugar and
pressure regulation, cholesterol reduction, cardiovascular support, antimicrobial activity, and cancer
cell line suppression.

### Mycoremediation

Like reishi, maitake mycelium has extremely high tensile strength, making it a good candidate for
mycofiltration. It is also excellent for recycling stumps in forested temperate habitats.
