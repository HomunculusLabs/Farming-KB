# Fungal Bioremediation Case Studies
The translation of laboratory-scale fungal bioremediation to field
applications has been a central challenge in environmental mycology.

limitations of [[mycoremediation-basics]] for foundational concepts and

- Field conditions involve variable weather, non-sterile soils,
  competing microorganisms, suboptimal nutrient status, and
  heterogeneous contamination distribution.
- Laboratory degradation rates are typically orders of magnitude
  higher than field rates, making extrapolation from bench
  studies to field timelines unreliable.
- Site-specific factors including soil type, climate, contamination
  age, and co-contaminant presence all influence feasibility and
  duration of treatment in field applications.
## Pentachlorophenol (PCP) Field Trials
Pentachlorophenol was widely used as a wood preservative and has
contaminated numerous sites worldwide. Several successful field trials
have demonstrated fungal remediation of PCP-contaminated materials.
- Lamar and Dietrich (1990) conducted one of the most successful
  fungal field applications using [[ligninolytic-enzyme-systems-white-rot-fungi]]
  production in soil environments.
- Field trials addressing PCP in treated wood waste showed that
  fungal inoculation of wood chips from PCP-treated utility poles
  achieved PCP reduction through composting with fungal inocula.
- Challenges included variable wood particle size affecting fungal
  penetration, heterogeneous PCP distribution, and the need for
  extended treatment periods to achieve regulatory standards.
## PAH Field Trials
PAH contamination from creosote, manufactured gas plants, and petroleum
refining has been a major target for fungal remediation. PAHs range
from simple two-ring to complex six-ring molecules, with degradation
difficulty increasing with molecular weight and hydrophobicity.
- At creosote-contaminated sites, [[staycare-phanerochaete-chrysosporium-in-bioremediation]] soil demonstrated degradation of
  pyrene and benzo[a]pyrene, confirming fungi can attack high-
  molecular-weight PAHs resistant to bacterial degradation alone.
- At manufactured gas plant sites, Pleurotus ostreatus degraded
  naphthalene, phenanthrene, and fluoranthene in field soil,
  though treatment periods of six months or more were needed.
- Bioavailability of aged PAHs was a primary limitation, as
  weathered contaminants become sequestered within soil organic
  matter and are less accessible to both enzymes and hyphae.
## Explosives and Munitions Contamination
Trinitrotoluene (TNT) and related compounds present unique remediation
challenges due to toxicity, incomplete transformation tendency, and
military site cleanup regulatory requirements.
- Phanerochaete chrysosporium and other white-rot fungi tested in
  TNT-contaminated soil achieved reduction to triaminotoluene and
  subsequent immobilization within soil organic matter.
- The fungal pathway for TNT transformation produces bound residues
  that may be environmentally preferable to soluble intermediates
  but complicate regulatory assessment of remediation completeness.
- Field results for explosives remediation have been less consistent
  than laboratory results, with environmental variables including
  soil pH, moisture, and nitrogen sources significantly affecting
  outcomes.
- Composting-based approaches combining fungal inoculation with
  thermophilic bacterial activity have shown the most reliable
  field results for mixed explosives contamination.
