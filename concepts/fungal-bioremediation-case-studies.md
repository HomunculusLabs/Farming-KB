# Fungal Bioremediation Case Studies and Field Trials The translation of laboratory-scale fungal-bioremediation field trials is the disparity between laboratory and field results: - **Laboratory studies** -- typically use sterile conditions, optimal nutrient media, controlled temperature and moisture, and axenic cultures - **Field conditions** -- variable weather, non-sterile soils, competing organisms, suboptimal nutrient status, heterogeneous contamination - Laboratory degradation rates are typically orders of magnitude higher than field rates This gap is not unique to fungi but is particularly pronounced for fungal bioaugmentation because the most effective degraders are wood-decay organisms introduced into soil environments. ## Pentachlorophenol (PCP) Field Trials ### Lamar and Dietrich (1990) One of the most successful fungal field applications: - **Organism:** Phanerochaete chrysosporium - **Inoculum:** Pre-grown on grain-sawdust mixture - **Site:** PCP-contaminated soil - **Amendments:** Straw and corn cobs as lignocellulosic carbon source - **Results:** Significant PCP degradation over several months - **Key lesson:** Lignocellulosic amendments were critical for fungal establishment and activity ### PCP-Treated Wood Field trials have also addressed PCP in treated wood waste: - Fungal inoculation of wood chips and sawdust from PCP-treated utility poles - Composting approaches with fungal inocula showed PCP reduction - Challenges included variable wood particle size and PCP distribution ## Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon (PAH) Field Trials ### Creosote-Contaminated Sites Creosote contains high concentrations of PAHs from wood preservation: - **P. sordida trials** -- inoculated with wood chip carriers showed PAH reduction - **P. chrysosporium in soil** -- degradation of pyrene, benzo[a]pyrene, and other high-molecular-weight PAHs - Results were more modest than laboratory predictions but demonstrated feasibility ### Manufactured Gas Plant (MGP) Sites Former MGP sites are contaminated with complex PAH mixtures: - Biopile and land treatment approaches with fungal amendments - Composting with white rot fungal inocula showed promise - Long treatment times (months to years) required for significant PAH reduction ### PAH-Contaminated Sediments Marine and freshwater sediments contaminated with PAHs: - Oxygen limitation in sediments constrains aerobic fungal degradation - Nutrient delivery to saturated sediments is challenging - Mixing/aeration may be necessary for effective fungal activity ## Dye and Effluent Decolorization ### Pulp and Paper Mill Effluents [[singh-explosives-tnt-rdx-fungal-degradation]] field applications:
- Phanerochaete chrysosporium and other white [[comparison-white-rot-fungi-vs-brown-rot-fungi-biodegradation]] tested in TNT-contaminated soil
- Composting approaches with fungal inocula
- Reduction of TNT to triaminotoluene and subsequent coupling/immobilization
- Field results were less consistent than laboratory results

## Comparative Performance Across Pollutant Classes

| Pollutant Class | Lab Success | Field Success | Primary Limitation |
|----------------|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Chlorophenols | High | Moderate | Fungal establishment in soil |
| PAHs (2-3 ring) | High | Moderate | Bioavailability |
| PAHs (4+ ring) | Moderate | Low | Low solubility, slow kinetics |
| BTEX | High | Low | Volatility, preferential bacterial degradation |
| Dyes | High | High | Bioreactor systems well-developed |
| TNT | Moderate | Low | Complex transformation pathways |

## Factors Affecting Field Trial Outcomes

### Site-Specific Variables
- **Contaminant concentration and distribution** -- heterogeneous contamination requires adapted approaches
- **Soil type and properties** -- texture, [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] content, pH, and moisture retention
- **Climate** -- temperature and rainfall patterns determine treatment duration
- **History of contamination** -- aged contaminants are less bioavailable than fresh spills
