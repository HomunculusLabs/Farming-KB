# Home Vegetable Garden Planning

Planning a home vegetable garden is the first and most important step in
successful [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]].

## Garden Location and Site Selection

Vegetables grow best in an open, level area where the soil is loose, rich,
and well drained. Most vegetables require a minimum of six hours of direct
sunlight daily. Avoid heavy clays, sandy soils, and shady spots.

If soil quality is poor, mix in two to three inches of topsoil, peat moss,
strawy manure, compost, or leaves plus fertilizer. The more [[soil-building-permaculture]] for detailed approaches to building soil health.

## Site Hazards: Black Walnut Toxicity

Black walnut trees produce a substance called juglone that is toxic to many
vegetable plants, including tomatoes, potatoes, eggplants, and peppers.
Juglone-sensitive plants may eventually wilt and die when grown near walnut
trees. Because roots remain active even after the tree is cut, a distance of
at least 50 feet is recommended between walnut trees and sensitive garden
plants. See Black [[seasonal-gardening-calendar]] for regional timing guidance.

## Fertilization Program

### Step 1: Establish Basic Fertility

A soil test indicates the kind and amount of fertilizer to apply. Testing in
autumn gives time to receive results and prepare materials for spring. The
best method is to apply half the recommended fertilizer, then deep-plow or
spade, followed by the other half disked or raked in lightly. This distributes
fertilizer throughout the upper seven inches of soil.

### Step 2: Annual Maintenance Fertilizer

Each year after the basic fertility level is established, apply a balanced
fertilizer containing nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K). A
fertilizer bag is labeled with numbers like 8-24-8 or 12-12-12, indicating
the percentage of each nutrient by weight. The maintenance application needs
only to be worked into the upper three to five inches of soil. See [[ingham-compost-tea-application-rates-and-timing]] and testing guidance.

## Maintaining Organic Matter

Sandy soils should contain two to three percent organic matter; clay soils
need four to five percent. Annual applications of organic matter are required
to reach and maintain these levels. Peat moss provides excellent soil
conditioning. Compost reuses plant materials and adds organic matter.
Well-rotted animal manures are excellent but may introduce weed seeds.
