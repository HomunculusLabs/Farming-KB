# [[cannabis-seed-production]] and Storage

Seed production is the culmination of a breeding program, and proper seed handling
ensures that the genetic work invested in creating a new variety is preserved. Oner's
strain guide references seed production practices throughout.

## Seed Production Methods

### Controlled Pollination

Serious breeders use controlled pollination rather than open pollination to ensure
known parentage:

1. **Isolation**: Male and female plants are separated to prevent unwanted
   cross-pollination
2. **Pollen collection**: Male flowers are harvested and pollen is collected before
   applying to selected female plants
3. **Targeted pollination**: Pollen is applied only to specific branches or entire
   plants of the chosen mother
4. **Timing**: Pollination occurs during the first 2-3 weeks of flowering for optimal
   seed set

### Outdoor Pollination (Historical)

Early breeders used wind-assisted pollination, keeping males in remote, isolated patches
and allowing prevailing winds to carry pollen to female plants. This produced the
"Trainwreck" generation of seeds with mixed genetics. While imprecise, it generated
enormous genetic diversity that breeders then sorted through over subsequent
generations.

### Feminized Seed Programs

Many modern breeders produce feminized seeds by chemically inducing male flowers on
female plants (typically using colloidal silver or silver thiosulfate). These "fem"
seeds produce only female plants, which is ideal for growers who don't want to identify
and cull males. However, breeding programs typically require regular (non-feminized)
seeds to access male genetics.

### Auto-Flowering Seed Production

Auto-flowering strains (containing Cannabis ruderalis genetics) present unique
considerations:

- Auto AK-47 crosses demonstrate that auto-flowering traits can be combined with
  premium genetics
- Seeds from auto-flowering parents will typically produce auto-flowering offspring
- These strains flower based on age rather than photoperiod, simplifying production

## Seed Maturation

For viable seeds, pollinated females must be allowed to flower significantly longer than
plants grown for sinsemilla (seedless bud):

- Seeds typically require 4-6 weeks after pollination to fully mature
- Mature seeds are dark brown, hard, and have visible striping or mottling
- Immature seeds are green or white and will have poor germination rates

## Seed Storage

While Oner's guide does not detail specific storage protocols, best practices drawn
from the breeding community include:

- **Temperature**: Store seeds in a cool, dark place. Refrigeration (not freezing)
  significantly extends viability.
- **Humidity**: Seeds should be kept dry. Excess moisture promotes mold and premature
  germination. Ideal relative humidity is around 20-30%.
- **Containers**: Airtight glass jars with desiccant packs provide the best
  long-term storage.
- **Light exposure**: Seeds degrade when exposed to light. Opaque containers are
  preferred.
- **Viability timeline**: Properly stored cannabis seeds can remain viable for several
  years, though germination rates decline over time.

## Rarity and Value of Breeding Seeds

Oner notes that seeds from exceptional breeding programs are often rare and highly
sought after:

- Seeds of certain strains like Psycho Killer Bubba Kush are described as "rare" and
  breeders advise growers not to "let them slip by"
- Breeders like Sannie's Seeds are described as having genetics "as heavily sought after
  as the Stanley Cup" due to their dedication and selection standards
- Some strains are available "in clone form only," meaning the genetics are preserved
  as living cuttings rather than seeds

## Seed Banks and Distribution

Modern cannabis breeding relies on seed banks for distribution:

- Hemp Depot and other online seed banks carry varieties from multiple breeders
- European breeders (Spain, Holland) have become major centers of cannabis seed
  production
- Forum communities and seed bank waiting lists are common methods for acquiring rare
  genetics
