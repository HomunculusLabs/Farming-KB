### Summer (June through August)

In June and July, the field is left unirrigated. In August, Fukuoka runs water
through the drainage ditches once every week or ten days. The rice grows
through the clover cover, competing successfully against weeds thanks to the
clover's suppression and the straw mulch. Spiders, frogs, dragonflies, and
other natural predators keep insect populations in balance.

## The Symbiosis of Rice, Barley, and Clover

Fukuoka summarized his entire method as the symbiosis of rice and barley or
wheat in clover. "Were I to say that all my method of farming boils down to
is the symbiosis of rice and barley or wheat in clover, I would probably be
reproached: 'If that's all there is to growing rice, then farmers wouldn't be
out there working so hard in their fields.' Yet, that is all there is to it."

The system works because each component plays multiple roles:
- **Clover** fixes atmospheric nitrogen, suppresses weeds, retains soil
  moisture, and provides a living mulch that decomposes into organic matter.
- **Rice straw** returns carbon and silicon to the soil, protects germinating
  seeds, and moderates soil temperature.
- **Winter grain** provides a second crop from the same land, its roots
  aerating the soil during the non-flooded season.
- **Clay pellets** protect rice seeds from predation and premature
  germination during winter.

farmers are constantly introduced. Fukuoka took the opposite tack: "I don't
