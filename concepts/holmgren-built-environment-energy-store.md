# Holmgren Built Environment Energy Store

Updated: 2026-04-19

David Holmgren discusses the built environment as a form of stored energy in *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* (2002), extending the concept of [[holmgren-household-energy-storages]] to encompass buildings, infrastructure, and human settlements as energy investments.

## Buildings as Embodied Energy

The tools, infrastructure, and technology needed to support human society, whether simple or complex, all derive from primary energy sources collected from the natural environment. The more useful and durable forms into which we transform those sources are stores of high quality energy — or, in ordinary language, "real wealth." Buildings represent enormous embodied energy investments.

This concept connects directly to [[holmgren-catch-and-store-energy-detailed]] and the broader framework of [[permaculture-principles]]. Every structure, road, pipe, and wire represents energy that has been captured, transformed, and stored in a durable form. Understanding this helps permaculture designers make better decisions about what and how to build.

## Passive Solar as Energy Storage

Holmgren cites the Melliodora example: "Every sunny winter day charges our mud-brick, passive solar house so we stay warm overnight." This illustrates how the built environment can function as an energy storage device. Mud-brick (or other thermal mass materials) absorbs solar energy during the day and releases it slowly at night, reducing or eliminating the need for active heating systems.

The mud-brick house at Melliodora exemplifies [[holmgren-design-patterns-details-detailed]] applied to buildings. Passive solar design represents one of the highest-leverage permaculture interventions because it uses the free energy of the sun to reduce or eliminate purchased energy inputs for space heating over the entire lifetime of the building.

## Depreciation of Built Infrastructure

A critical insight is that stored energy in the built environment depreciates. Buildings decay, roads crumble, pipes corrode. The maintenance of built environment storages requires ongoing energy investment. Holmgren notes that economic rationalism in business and government has led to the decline of large-scale stores of essential infrastructure in the interests of economic efficiency, which in turn increases the likelihood of disruption and disaster.

This insight is central to [[permaculture-retrofit-suburbia]] because it reveals the hidden cost of deferred maintenance. The modern tendency to build disposable structures with short lifespans represents a massive waste of embodied energy that cannot be recovered as easily as biological systems recover from disturbance.

## The Y2K Lesson

The vulnerability of modern built environment systems to slight disturbance was highlighted by Y2K concerns. Modern urban living features no stored food or fuel in the house, with purchasing power dependent on credit tied to permanent employment. This represents a maximum-flow, minimum-storage pattern that is extremely fragile.

This vulnerability connects to broader themes of [[permaculture-disaster-resilience]]. Systems designed purely for efficiency with no redundancy or storage capacity are inherently fragile. Permaculture design for the built environment must account for disruption and build in reserves.

## Low-Energy Building Materials

In the context of [[holmgren-tripartite-altruism]], Holmgren implicitly advocates for building with low-embodied-energy materials. Trees provide structural timber products, fibre, and wood chemical products to replace high-energy concrete, metal, and synthetic materials. This connects built environment design to [[holmgren-trees-nature-solar-power-plants]] as the primary renewable resource pathway.

## Infrastructure as Natural Capital

The built environment can be understood as one form of [[holmgren-household-energy-storages]] — an energy store that supports human activity. Rebuilding and maintaining this capital, especially using locally available, renewable materials, is central to permaculture design for [[permaculture-retrofit-suburbia]].

Holmgren's concept of [[holmgren-household-energy-storages]] extends to buildings and infrastructure. Just as natural capital in ecosystems provides ongoing services, well-designed buildings provide shelter, thermal comfort, and productivity enhancement over decades or centuries.

## Space and Water Heating

Holmgren specifically notes that space and water heating are the most energy-demanding household activities. Designing buildings that minimize these demands through passive solar design, thermal mass, insulation, and efficient layout represents one of the highest-leverage permaculture interventions for reducing energy consumption.

At Melliodora, the energy-demanding functions of space heating, water heating and cooking are provided with renewable energy (passive solar and wood). Electricity use is kept to less than 3 kilowatt hours a day, less than one-fifth of typical household use, demonstrating what is possible with thoughtful design.

## Design Criteria for Built Environment

Holmgren proposes specific design criteria for buildings and infrastructure that emulate natural energy storages:

- Modest in scale

- Well-designed for long life and/or made from easily renewable materials

- Simple to maintain (not necessarily maintenance-free)

- Multi-purpose and easy to adapt to other uses

These criteria connect to [[permaculture-pattern-language]] and [[holmgren-design-science-discipline]]. Along with the creative retrofitting of existing buildings and infrastructure, applying these design criteria in all new developments could help contain the overdevelopment problem where the costs of maintaining what we have are beginning to erode economic wealth.

## Energy Storage in Culture

Despite the massive scale of technology and built assets today, the largest stores of high-quality embodied energy are in forms that people do not recognise as containing energy. Information and organisation in government, economy, community and culture are thought to be largely ethereal "goods" unrelated to the physical world. Holmgren argues these too are forms of stored energy.

## Maintenance Engineering

Maintenance engineering has a bright future. The creative retrofitting of existing buildings and infrastructure is as important as new construction. In the context of [[permaculture-retrofit-suburbia]], the adaptive reuse of suburban housing stock represents a vast opportunity for permaculture design intervention.
