## Macrofungal Documentation — Photography, Spore Prints, and Description

Thorough documentation of macrofungal specimens is essential for accurate identification and lasting scientific value. Fresh specimens provide the most information, but proper techniques allow preservation of key features for later study.

## Color Documentation

### Standardized Color Names
Accurate color notation is critical because fungi lose natural color during drying. Standard color guides:
- **Ridgway (1912):** Classic but out of print
- **Kornerup and Wanscher (1978):** Widely used; may be out of print
- **Munsell (1966):** Most comprehensive; still in print but expensive
- **Rayner (1970), Maerz and Paul (1950), Kelly (1965):** Alternatives

Record colors of:
- All [[coral-fungi-clavariaceae]]:** Place paper directly under branches
- Only portion of large pileus needed

### Conditions
- Mature sporocarps at 10-15C in relatively humid environment
- Large box (cigar box) or plastic container
- Moisten with damp paper towel if particularly dry
- **Do NOT place sporocarp in water**
- Include collection number with each deposit

## Micromorphological Features

### Field Laboratory
Compound microscope can help initially assign specimens to [[coprophilous-fungi-taxonomic-groups-distribution]]:
- Spore characteristics
- Pileus anatomy
- Assess condition and maturity of small ascomycetes
- Determine if specimen should be kept
- Check features lost on drying

### Fresh vs. Preserved Material
- Complete micromorphological descriptions in fresh condition are time consuming
- Well-preserved specimens work as well as fresh material
