# Solomon Planting and Transplanting Guide

## Direct Seeding vs Transplanting

Solomon has a clear preference for direct seeding over transplanting for most vegetables, with some important exceptions. His reasoning is based on root system development: transplanting destroys the taproot and converts the plant into more of a surface feeder. Direct-seeded crops endure rainless spells better because their deeper root systems can access subsoil moisture.

## Seed Starting

### Seed Quality

Solomon emphasizes buying quality seed from reputable suppliers. Important qualities of seed:
- High germination rate (seed must pass germination regulations)
- Vigor (ability to emerge strongly under less-than-ideal conditions)
- Genetic purity (especially important for OP varieties)
- Disease-free

Defective seed can often be identified visually. Seed that is cracked, discolored, or excessively small compared to normal should be discarded.

### Planting Depth

A general rule: plant seed at a depth of 2-3 times the seed's diameter. However, this varies by soil type:
- In sandy soils, plant slightly deeper (moisture is lower near the surface)
- In heavy/clay soils, plant slightly shallower (emergence is harder through dense soil)
- In hot weather, plant slightly deeper (moisture retreats deeper)

### Presprouting (Chitting)

Presprouting seeds before planting can speed germination and improve success rates, especially for slow-germinating species. Presprouting is particularly recommended for:
- Large seeds of vining crops (squash, cucumbers, melons)
- Peas in cool soil
- Parsnips and other slow germinators

Method: Place seeds between layers of moist paper towel in a sealed container at room temperature. Check daily. Plant immediately when the first root (radicle) emerges -- do not allow the sprout to grow long, as it is fragile and easily damaged during planting.
