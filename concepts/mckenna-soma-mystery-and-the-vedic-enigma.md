# McKenna's Soma Mystery and the Vedic Enigma

One of the most enduring mysteries in the history of psychoactive
substances is the identity of Soma — the divine plant sacrament of the
ancient Vedic religion of India. [[fly-agaric]] mushroom, [[syrian-rue]]) as a more plausible
candidate. This plant contains harmine and harmaline — beta-carboline
alkaloids that are MAO inhibitors. When combined with DMT-containing
plants, the harmala alkaloids make the DMT orally active (the same
pharmacological principle as Amazonian ayahuasca).

The Haoma of the Zoroastrian tradition in Persia (cognate with Vedic
Soma) was associated with Peganum harmala, providing cultural and
geographical support for this identification. The plant's distribution
across Central Asia and the Middle East fits the Indo-European migration
patterns better than Amanita muscaria.

## The Wasson Contradictions

McKenna documented contradictions in Wasson's own thinking about Soma.
Wasson initially championed Amanita muscaria with great confidence but
later acknowledged significant problems with his theory. The strain
between Wasson's public certainty and private doubts reflects the
difficulty of the question.

Wasson also considered other fungal candidates, including Stropharia
species, suggesting that even he recognized limitations in the Amanita
theory. The possibility that Soma was not a single species but a complex
of preparations involving multiple plants has gained support in recent
scholarship.

## Soma as Male Moon God

The Rig Veda connects Soma with the moon in complex ways. Soma is
described as dwelling on the mountains, being brought by eagles, and
associated with the celestial realm. McKenna explored the possibility
that the moon-god association reflected the cyclical, visionary nature
of the Soma experience — a journey to the lunar, reflective dimension
of consciousness distinct from the solar, rational mode.

## Soma and Cattle

A recurring theme in the Rig Veda is the connection between Soma and
cattle. Soma is pressed in the same manner as milk is churned, and the
language surrounding both processes shares significant vocabulary. This
connection suggests a pastoral cultural context for the original Soma
ceremonies, consistent with the nomadic, cattle-herding lifestyle of the
early Indo-European peoples.

## The Indo-European Context

The Soma/Haoma question cannot be separated from the broader question of
Indo-European origins. The similarity between Vedic Soma and Iranian
Haoma suggests a common Indo-Iranian plant sacrament that was carried by
migrating peoples from a Central Asian homeland. The identity of this
original sacramental plant would illuminate the spiritual life of the
people whose language family gave rise to most European languages.

## A Historical Watershed

McKenna framed the Soma mystery as a historical watershed — the moment
when humanity's original partnership with visionary plants began to be
lost. The forgetting of Soma's identity represents the beginning of the
end of the Archaic worldview, to be replaced by the dominator culture's
suppression of direct ecstatic experience in favor of priestly mediation
and dogmatic authority.

## Modern Research

The question of Soma's identity remains open and actively debated.
Recent candidates have included various Psilocybe species, Ephedra, and
composite preparations. The most compelling modern theories suggest that
Soma may have been a preparation combining Peganum harmala (as the MAO
inhibitor) with a DMT-containing plant, essentially making the Vedic
Soma ceremony a form of proto-ayahuasca.
