# Mushroom-Infused Beer, Wine, and Spirits

Combining the health benefits of medicinal mushrooms with the world's most widely consumed alcoholic beverages creates a unique category of functional brews. Mushroom-infused beer, wine, and spirits offer an accessible and enjoyable delivery system for fungal extracts and bioactive compounds. This practice, which gained public attention at the 2012 Telluride Mushroom Festival where 240 gallons of medicinal mushroom beer sold out in under 48 hours, bridges the gap between mycology and craft brewing.

## Brewing Fundamentals

Before adding mushrooms to beverages, brewers should have several successful batches of standard beer under their belt. Mushroom brewing introduces additional variables that can affect flavor, fermentation, and contamination risk.

### Cleanliness

As with mushroom cultivation, cleanliness is critical in brewing. While brewing is more forgiving than sterile tissue culture, contamination can accumulate across batches and alter the dynamics of fermented beverages. Strive for complete cleanliness at every stage.

### Yeast Management

Beer fermentation begins with yeast. Key considerations for yeast cultures parallel those for mushroom cultures:

- **Pure cultures** from reliable companies are available worldwide
- **Liquid yeast** is more active and invades the brewed medium quickly (best for 1–3 day delivery)
- **Dry yeast** is better for remote locations with longer shipping times
- **Local microbrewers** may share fermentation discharge to perpetuate yeast strains at minimal cost
- **Strain senescence** occurs when yeasts are expanded indefinitely on the same formula — just as with mushroom cultures, maintain masters and backups
- Alternate growing media to keep yeast strains strong and viable

### Sourcing Grains and Hops

**Organic malted grains** are increasingly available and worth the premium price. Mushroom-infused beer is about quality over quantity. Growing organic grains locally creates a symbiotic relationship — cereal straw left in fields can be used for cultivating oyster mushrooms.

**Organic hops** are expensive due to vulnerability to powdery mildew. Growing your own hops from rhizomes (ordered in early spring for March delivery) provides a sustainable supply. Key practices:

- Release beneficial insects (ladybugs, predatory mites, aphid predators, assassin bugs) for pest control
- Use organic fungicides like liquid copper as foliar sprays to suppress molds and mildews
- Plant hops in multiple locations for redundancy
- Perpetuate rhizomes from cuttings by digging in early March and cutting sections with branching near the surface

## Incorporating Mushrooms

### Fresh vs. Dried Mushrooms

**Fresh mushrooms** carry active yeasts and bacteria on their damp tissues. The wort temperature (160°F/71°C) is not high enough to sterilize fruiting bodies, creating contamination risk. Autoclaving fresh mushrooms for one hour before adding them to beer is recommended.

**Dried mushrooms** are preferred. Most potential contaminants go dormant during drying, but autoclaving dried mushrooms for one hour at 250°F (121°C) is still recommended to eliminate any possibility of contamination.

**Powdered dried mushrooms** are the most versatile form. The dry powder has enormous surface area, facilitating exposure to hot water and alcohols for improved extraction of flavor and medicinal compounds. The powder can even be gently roasted just before adding to impart unique flavors.

### Addition Stages

Mushrooms can be incorporated at several points in the brewing process:

1. **In the grain and mash**: Extracts flavors, bitterness, and water-soluble medicinal compounds during the cook
2. **Dry shrooming** (equivalent to dry hopping): Add powder or whole dried fruiting bodies to the fermentation container with hops for unique flavors, complex aromas, and varied bitterness. Use a hops bag for easy removal before kegging or bottling.
3. **At kegging or bottling**: Add mushroom extract, shaking well to ensure all active ingredients are incorporated

### Extracts vs. Whole Mushrooms

**Alcohol extracts** are preferred over hot water extracts for brewing because alcohol better releases beta-glucans and other molecules trapped in the chitin matrix — precisely the compounds desired for medicinal brews. Extracts can be added at any stage but are most conveniently added during kegging or bottling.

## Choosing the Right Mushroom

Several factors influence mushroom selection for brewing:

### Flavor

Mushrooms provide preliminary and lingering flavors when they hit the palate, much like hops. Start with small amounts in small batches, as some beers take weeks to months to fully develop. Experiment with mushroom species, form (fresh, dried, powdered), quantity, and stage of addition.

### Aroma

Many mushrooms contain vibrantly aromatic essential oils that mix and bind with oily molecules in hops. Consider pungent and sweet species for brown ales and lagers, or savory and hearty species for darker brews like stouts. Birch polypore (*Piptoporus betulinus*) is notable for its aromatic essential oils. Pouring beer into a glass is the best way to appreciate aroma.

### Medicinal Properties

The original motivation for mushroom beer was infusing medicinal compounds from species like reishi and shiitake into a palatable beverage with high market demand that stores compounds safely for years. Drink a bottle of mushroom beer weekly, not daily, and alternate recipes for different mushroom properties. Observe your body's response and compare with taking the extract alone.

### Bitterness Values

Many mushrooms, particularly polypores, are naturally "hoppy" and can impart controlled bitterness. Wild *Tylopilus* species are extremely bitter and should be used sparingly. To test bitterness, bite a small chunk of a fresh mushroom and chew for at least 60 seconds, noting initial and lingering qualities.

### Hops Substitution Potential

Hops are experiencing increasing disease problems in commercial production, encouraging pesticide use and potentially impacting beer prices. Some mushroom species could partially or entirely substitute for hops in certain beer styles, offering both bitterness and medicinal properties.

### Cost Efficiency

Use powdered mushrooms or extracts rather than bulk fresh material to minimize costs. Substantially less material is needed to achieve the same end product when using concentrated forms.

## Mushroom-Infused Beer Recipes

Each recipe produces 5 gallons of beer.

### Turkey Tail Ale — A golden pale ale

- 6 pounds extra-light malted grain
- 4 ounces fresh or 2 ounces dried turkey tail (*Trametes versicolor*)
- 1 ounce hops
- Ale yeast

### Iceman Amber — An amber ale

- 6 pounds extra-light malted grain
- 1 pound amber malt
- 12 ounces carrot juice (preferably organic)
- 4 ounces fresh or 2 ounces dried birch polypore (*Piptoporus betulinus*)
- 4 ounces fresh or 2 ounces dried amadou (*Fomes fomentarius*)
- 1 ounce hops
- Ale yeast

### Reishi Red — A natural red lager

- 6 pounds extra-light malted grain
- 1 pound amber malt
- 12 ounces red beet juice (preferably organic)
- 4 ounces fresh or 2 ounces dried reishi (*Ganoderma* spp.)
- 1 ounce hops
- Ale yeast

### Agarikon Stout — A dark, earthy brew

- 1 pound chocolate malted grain
- 1 pound crystal malted grain
- 5 pounds pale malted grain
- 2 ounces liquid agarikon (*Laricifomes officinalis*) mushroom extract (from colonized grain)
- 1 ounce hops
- Trappist yeast

**Note**: Agarikon is endangered and should not be wild-harvested. Allow myceliated cakes to supercolonize over several months until thick with biomass, then prepare an extract.

## Mushroom-Infused Wine

Wines can be infused with mushrooms or extracts following similar principles. Sterilize dried mushroom powder by autoclaving for one hour at 250°F (121°C). Portion the powder into wine fermentation tanks or aging barrels at a starting dosage of 5 grams per liter of wine for a basic medicinal dose. Adjust concentration upward as desired.

## Mushroom-Infused Spirits

Infusing spirits is the simplest method since the high alcohol concentration eliminates spoilage risk — no sterilization of the mushrooms is needed.

### Chanterelle Vodka Method

1. Add cultivated or wild mushrooms (whole or chopped) to a large mason jar
2. Fill the jar with spirit (vodka, gin, or other clear spirits)
3. Optionally add spices: lemon peel, hot peppers, cinnamon sticks
4. Steep for about one month, shaking occasionally
5. Mushrooms will initially float, then become saturated and sink
6. Strain out the mushrooms

The infused mushrooms can be consumed as a "mushroom shot" or cooked to remove alcohol by searing in a hot pan for a few minutes. Chopping mushrooms increases surface area and improves color and flavor extraction.
