# HPLC Analysis of Psilocybin and Psilocin

High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) is the primary
analytical method for quantifying psilocybin and psilocin levels in
mushroom tissue. Bigwood and Beug's 1982 study exemplifies the
application of reversed-phase HPLC to the analysis of *Psilocybe
cubensis*, establishing methodology that has influenced subsequent
research on [[hplc-quantification-psilocybin-psilocin-bigwood-beug-1982]] with thin-layer chromatography (TLC)
for qualitative confirmation.

## Reversed-Phase HPLC Method

Bigwood and Beug used reversed-phase HPLC for the primary
quantitative analysis. In reversed-phase chromatography, the
stationary phase is nonpolar (typically C18-bonded silica) and the
mobile phase is polar. Psilocybin and psilocin, being moderately
polar tryptamine alkaloids, interact with the stationary phase
through their aromatic and indole ring systems while being carried
along by the polar mobile phase. The different affinities of
psilocybin and psilocin for the stationary phase cause them to elute
at different retention times, allowing separation and individual
quantification.

The specific conditions were not detailed in the 1982 paper (having
been described in their companion paper on [[psilocin-biosynthetic-delay-hypothesis-successive-flushes]].

## Data Processing

Chromatogram quantification was performed using a Hewlett-Packard
8380A reporting integrator-plotter. This instrument automatically
integrates the area under each peak in the chromatogram, converting
the raw detector signal into numerical peak area values that can be
compared against the calibration curve to determine concentration.
The integrator-plotter also produced a visual record of the
chromatogram, allowing visual verification of peak identification
and baseline resolution.
