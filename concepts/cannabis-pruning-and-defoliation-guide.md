# Cannabis Pruning and Defoliation Guide

Pruning and defoliation are essential skills for cannabis cultivators seeking to maximize yield, control plant shape, and improve airflow. These techniques range from removing individual leaves to deliberately damaging stem tissue, each with specific purposes and timing requirements. Understanding when and how to cut is as important as knowing what to cut.

## Defoliation During Flowering

Selective defoliation during early flowering can improve bud development by exposing bud sites to more light. Removing large fan leaves that cast shadows on developing bud clusters allows those sites to receive direct light, increasing their growth rate. The standard practice is to remove no more than 20-30% of total foliage at any single session, and to wait at least one week between defoliation sessions.

Heavy defoliation in the final weeks of flowering is generally discouraged because the plant is relying on its remaining leaf mass to fuel the final swell of bud development. Some growers perform a final light defoliation 2-3 weeks before harvest to remove yellowing lower leaves and improve airflow around the densest bud sites. This final cleanup also makes harvest easier by reducing the leaf material that must be trimmed.

## Lollipopping

Lollipopping is a defoliation technique performed just before or during the first two weeks of flowering. The lower 30-40% of the plant's branches are stripped of all fan leaves and small bud sites, leaving the upper portion bare like a lollipop stick. This redirects the plant's energy to the top colas that receive the most light. The lower bud sites that are removed would typically produce underdeveloped "popcorn" buds of low quality.

## Healing Broken Stems

If a stem breaks during training, it can often be saved. Hold the break in place using a stake or stick for support, and bind it with cheesecloth or a porous bandage wrap. Applying honey to the wound can accelerate healing. Monitor the break area for new growth at the injury site, and trim away any new shoots that emerge, as these will divert energy and potentially TOP the plant at the break point.

Most stem breaks heal within one to two weeks if properly supported. The plant will form a callus over the break and eventually develop a thickened node at the injury site. In many cases, healed plants are actually stronger at the break point than the original stem.

## Defoliation and Pest Prevention

Removing dead or dying foliage from the grow area reduces hiding places for pests and eliminates potential sites for mold colonization. Leaves with visible mold, rust, or unusual spotting should be removed immediately and disposed of outside the grow space. Good defoliation hygiene is a component of [[query-how-to-use-neem-oil-for-cannabis-pest-control]] management.

Mold looks like a rust or fluff on leaves and will spread to other parts of the plant if left unchecked. Any leaves showing signs of mold should be cut away during thinning sessions. Maintaining good airflow through proper pruning and plant spacing is one of the most effective mold prevention strategies available.

## Tools for Pruning

Clean, sharp tools are essential for successful pruning. Dull blades crush and tear plant tissue, creating larger wounds that heal slowly and are more susceptible to infection. The standard tools for cannabis pruning include curved trimming scissors for precision work, straight scissors for larger cuts, and a sharp razor blade for topping cuts.

All tools should be sterilized with isopropyl alcohol between plants to prevent the spread of pathogens. In environments where viral or bacterial diseases are a concern, tools should be flamed with a lighter after alcohol sterilization for maximum protection.
