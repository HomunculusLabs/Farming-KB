# Tetrahydrocannabivarin (THCV)

Tetrahydrocannabivarin (THCV) is a propyl analogue of thc found in certain [[cannabis-chemotypes]], particularly those originating from southern and central Africa. Unlike its close relative THC, THCV exhibits a distinctly different pharmacological profile — it acts as a CB1 receptor antagonist at low doses (blocking THC's psychoactive effects) while functioning as a CB1 agonist at higher doses. This dual-action mechanism, combined with CB2 agonist activity, makes THCV one of the most pharmacologically complex and therapeutically promising minor cannabinoids currently under investigation.

## Chemical Profile and Occurrence

THCV shares the same molecular scaffold as THC but differs by having a three-carbon propyl side chain rather than a five-carbon pentyl chain. This seemingly minor structural difference produces significantly different [[african-landrace-sativa-modern-hybrids]] known for its energetic, clear-headed effects and measurable THCV content
- **Doug's Varin**: A specially bred variety developed in California specifically for high THCV production, often exceeding 3% THCV
- **Malawi Gold**: A Southeast [[african-landrace-sativa-genetic-contributions-modern-breeding-oner]] traditionally cultivated for its stimulating and euphoric properties
- **Willie Nelson**: A hybrid strain that has shown measurable THCV content in analytical testing
- **Pink Kush African**: Certain phenotypes have demonstrated THCV levels above 1%

## Mechanism of Action

THCV's pharmacology is uniquely dose-dependent, producing markedly different effects at different concentrations:

**At low doses (1-5 mg):**
- Acts as a **CB1 receptor antagonist**, competitively binding to CB1 sites without activating them
- Blocks the psychoactive effects of THC when co-administered, potentially reducing intoxication, anxiety, and cognitive impairment
- Does not produce intoxicating effects on its own at these doses
- May enhance the [[cbd-therapeutic-effects]] of other cannabinoids by modulating CB1 signaling

**At higher doses (10-20+ mg):**
- Converts to a **CB1 receptor agonist**, activating the receptor similarly to THC
- Produces mild psychoactive effects, though generally described as clearer and less sedating than THC
- Activates CB2 receptors, contributing to anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects
- Stimulates 5-HT1A [[cannabis-for-cachexia-and-appetite-stimulation]] (the "munchies"). Clinical and preclinical studies have demonstrated that THCV:

- Reduces food intake and increases satiety signals in animal models
- Decreases the rewarding value of high-calorie foods without producing nausea or aversion
- May increase energy expenditure and fat oxidation through activation of sympathetic nervous system pathways
- Shows potential as a treatment for obesity [[blesching-cannabis-inflammatory-bowel-disease]] models by reducing intestinal inflammation

## Dosing Considerations

THCV dosing requires careful attention to its biphasic nature:

- **Appetite suppression**: Low doses of 5-10 mg, typically taken before meals
- **Metabolic benefits**: 5-15 mg daily, divided into two or three doses
- **Neurological applications**: 10-20 mg, with higher doses needed for seizure management
