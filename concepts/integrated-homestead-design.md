# Integrated Homestead Design

The capstone synthesis of this wiki — how every system on the regenerative homestead connects, feeds, and supports every other. A well-designed homestead is not a collection of independent operations but a closed-loop ecosystem where the output of one system is the input of another. See [[integrated-homestead-annual-calendar]] for the seasonal detail.

## The Six Loops

Every homestead can be understood as six interlocking nutrient and energy loops. When these loops are closed, external inputs drop to near zero.

### Loop 1: Animals → Compost → Soil → Plants → Animals

The foundational cycle. Animals eat plants, produce manure and bedding, which becomes compost, which builds soil, which grows more plants.

- **Ducks** ([[duck-keeping]]) consume slugs, insects, duckweed, kitchen scraps. Manure-rich bedding (NPK ~0.6-1.4-0.4) feeds [[composting-systems]].
- **Goats** ([[goat-keeping]]) browse brush, weeds, tree leaves from [[pasture-management]] and [[food-forest-design]] edges. Manure (NPK ~0.7-1.0-0.6) is safe to apply relatively fresh.
- **Bees** ([[beekeeping]]) pollinate [[food-forest-design]], [[orchard-management]], [[cover-cropping]], and gardens — directly increasing yields.
- **Compost** from [[composting-systems]], [[vermicompost]], or [[bokashi]] feeds [[soil-food-web]], which feeds [[cannabis-indoor-living-soil]] and garden beds.
- **Cover crops** ([[cover-cropping]]) fix nitrogen, build organic matter, and provide grazing before termination.

### Loop 2: Mushrooms → Decomposition → Soil → Plants → Substrate

Fungi turn woody waste, crop residues, and animal bedding into available nutrients.

- **Spent mushroom substrate (SMS)** ([[mushroom-composting-and-recycling]]) is nitrogen-rich, partially decomposed — excellent [[composting-systems]] input.
- **Substrates** ([[mushroom-substrates]]) use on-farm straw ([[grain-and-staple-crops]]), sawdust ([[orchard-management]] prunings), and grain.
- **[[mycorrhizal-fungi]]** extend plant root networks, increasing nutrient and water access 10-1000x.
- **[[fungal-decomposition]]** breaks down lignin and cellulose that bacteria cannot.

### Loop 3: KNF/EM → Microbes → Soil Biology → Plant Health → On-Farm Inputs

A self-replicating microbial inoculant system using only on-farm materials.

- **[[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]]** captures local forest microbes adapted to your climate.
- **[[fermented-plant-extract-fpe]]** uses on-farm weeds and crop plants as nutrient sources.
- **[[fish-amino-acid-faa]]** uses fish scraps ([[pond-and-aquaculture]]) as nitrogen source.
- **[[water-soluble-calcium-wca]]** uses duck eggshells; [[water-soluble-calcium-phosphate-wcp]] uses animal bones ([[animal-byproducts]]).
- **[[fermented-mixed-compost-fmc]]** bridges [[knf-preparations]] and [[composting-systems]].
- **[[lactic-acid-bacteria]]** from rice wash and goat milk ([[goat-keeping]]) boosts soil biology.
- **[[jadam-methods]]** provides ultra-low-cost alternative: JMS from leaf mold, potato, sea salt.
- **[[nutritive-cycle-theory]]** prescribes when to apply each input for maximum effect.
- **[[effective-microorganisms-em]]** provides commercial alternative while on-farm production scales.

### Loop 4: Water → Pond → Irrigation → Plants → Runoff → Pond

Water cycles through the homestead, picking up and distributing nutrients.

- **[[pond-and-aquaculture]]** collects runoff, stores water, provides fish, supports ducks, creates microclimate.
- **[[water-management]]** (swales, keyline, rainwater) slows, spreads, and sinks water.
- **[[homestead-water-systems]]** handles purification, distribution, emergency storage.
- Duck pond overflow is nutrient-rich — irrigates [[food-forest-design]] and garden beds.
- Greywater from [[dairy-and-cheesemaking]] irrigates food forest and [[cover-cropping]].
- [[greenhouse-design]] and house rooftops feed rainwater catchment.

### Loop 5: Kitchen → Fermentation → Preservation → Food → Scraps → Compost

The kitchen is the processing hub where raw products become preserved food, medicine, and compost inputs.

- **[[food-preservation]]** extends harvests year-round.
- **[[dairy-and-cheesemaking]]** transforms goat milk — whey feeds soil biology or animals.
- **[[herbal-medicine]]** processes herbs into tinctures, salves, teas.
- **[[bokashi]]** ferments ALL kitchen scraps (meat, dairy, bones) — nothing wasted.
- Kitchen scraps also feed [[vermicompost]] and [[duck-keeping]].
- **[[lactic-acid-bacteria]]** for food fermentation is the same LAB used in KNF soil applications.

### Loop 6: Energy → Sun → Plants → Biomass → Carbon → Soil

Energy flows from sun through photosynthesis into biomass, then into soil carbon.

- **[[homestead-renewable-energy]]** powers the homestead with minimal grid dependence.
- **[[greenhouse-design]]** captures solar energy via passive solar design and thermal mass.
- **[[no-till-farming]]** and [[regenerative-agriculture]] sequester carbon — 1% OM increase stores ~20,000 gal water/acre.
- **[[cover-cropping]]** and [[food-forest-design]] maintain living roots year-round.
- **[[composting-systems]]** stabilizes carbon that would otherwise off-gas.

## The Zone Integration Map

How systems distribute across permaculture zones (see [[permaculture-design]]):

### Zone 0 — House & Indoor Spaces
- [[cannabis-indoor-living-soil]] grow room with [[cannabis-light-spectrums]] and [[think-grow-model-h-plus]]
- [[vermicompost]] bin, [[bokashi]] kitchen bin
- [[dairy-and-cheesemaking]], [[food-preservation]], [[herbal-medicine]] processing areas
- [[knf-preparations]] brewing area (FPJ, FAA, LAB fermentation)

### Zone 1 — Intensive Garden (Daily Visit)
- Annual vegetables with [[companion-planting]], [[no-till-farming]] beds
- [[greenhouse-design]], compost bins, seedling nursery
- Duck pond, rain barrels

### Zone 2 — Animals & Perennials (Daily-Weekly)
- [[duck-keeping]], [[goat-keeping]], [[beekeeping]] housing
- Dwarf fruit trees ([[orchard-management]]), berry bushes
- [[food-forest-design]] edge plantings

### Zone 3 — Orchard & Pasture (Weekly-Monthly)
- Full-size fruit/nut trees, [[pasture-management]] rotational paddocks
- [[cover-cropping]], [[grain-and-staple-crops]], [[mushroom-log-cultivation]]

### Zone 4 — Woodlot & Forage (Monthly-Seasonal)
- Firewood/timber, wild foraging ([[edible-wild-mushrooms-of-north-america]])
- [[pond-and-aquaculture]], [[mycoremediation-basics]]

### Zone 5 — Wildland (Minimal Intervention)
- Wildlife habitat, [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]] collection site
- Observation and learning ([[natural-farming-fukuoka]], [[sepp-holzer-methods]])

## The 80/20 Integration Priorities

If you can only close a few loops, these have the highest impact:

1. **Animal manure → compost → garden** — Eliminates purchased fertilizer. Set up [[composting-systems]] before animals.
2. **Kitchen scraps → bokashi/vermicompost → soil** — Closes the waste loop. [[bokashi]] handles what [[vermicompost]] can't.
3. **Rainwater → garden/animals** — Reduces well dependence 30-60%. ([[water-management]])
4. **Cover crops → nitrogen + organic matter** — Free fertilizer. Never leave soil bare. ([[cover-cropping]])
5. **Ducks in garden rotation** — Chemical-free pest control. ([[duck-keeping]], [[cannabis-pest-management]])
6. **Mushrooms on waste substrate** — Straw/sawdust → food + compost input. ([[mushroom-cultivation-basics]])
7. **KNF inputs from on-farm materials** — Self-made from weeds, eggshells, fish, rice. ([[knf-preparations]])

## Startup Sequence

1. **Water** ([[homestead-water-systems]], [[water-management]])
2. **Compost** ([[composting-systems]], [[vermicompost]])
3. **Animals** ([[duck-keeping]] first, then [[goat-keeping]])
4. **KNF/EM** ([[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]], [[knf-preparations]], or [[jadam-methods]])
5. **Garden & food forest** ([[no-till-farming]], [[food-forest-design]], [[orchard-management]])
6. **Mushrooms** ([[mushroom-cultivation-basics]])

8. **Dairy** ([[dairy-and-cheesemaking]])

## Measuring Integration Success

| Metric | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|--------|----------|--------------|----------|
| External fertilizer | 100% | 10-30% | Near zero |
| Kitchen waste → compost | 0% | 50% | 95%+ |
| Animal feed on-farm | 0% | 20-40% | 60%+ |
| Irrigation from rain/pond | 0% | 30-50% | 70%+ |
| KNF inputs self-made | 0% | 50% | 90%+ |
| Cover crops on bare soil | 0% | 50% | 90%+ |

The goal: reducing external inputs while increasing yields, soil health, and ecosystem function. See [[regenerative-agriculture]] and [[permaculture-principles]].
