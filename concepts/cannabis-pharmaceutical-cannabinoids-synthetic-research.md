# Pharmaceutical and Synthetic Cannabinoids

## Overview

cannabis pharmaceutical cannabinoids synthetic research are purified or synthetic cannabinoid preparations approved for medical use. They offer precise dosing, standardized potency, and legal prescription access — but may lack the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] as of publication, variable absorption depending on application technique

### Rimonabant (Acomplia) — Withdrawn
- **Type**: Synthetic CB1 receptor antagonist/inverse agonist
- **Form**: Oral tablet
- **Original indication**: Weight loss, [[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]] and inflammation.

### Potential CB1 Agonist
- **WIN55,212-2**: Binds with both CB1 and CB2 receptors but is stronger at CB1. Widely used in preclinical research. Has demonstrated neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and analgesic properties in animal models. Not approved for human use due to safety concerns.

### CB1 Antagonists
These compounds block CB1 receptor activation:

- **SR 141716A (Rimonabant)**: The most well-known CB1 antagonist. Originally developed as a weight-loss drug before being withdrawn. Continues to be used in research to understand CB1 receptor function and the consequences of blocking endocannabinoid signaling.

- **AM 251**: A structural analog of rimonabant with similar CB1 antagonistic properties. Used extensively in neuroscience research to investigate the role of CB1 receptors in memory, anxiety, addiction, and pain perception.

### CB2 Agonists
These compounds selectively activate CB2 receptors without psychoactive effects:

- **AM 1241**: A selective CB2 agonist used in pain research. Has demonstrated significant analgesic effects in animal models of inflammatory and neuropathic pain without the psychoactive side effects associated with CB1 activation.

- **CP 50556-1**: An early CB2-selective research compound used to characterize CB2 receptor pharmacology and differentiate CB2-mediated effects from CB1-mediated effects.

- **CP 55,940**: Listed here again because it activates both CB1 and CB2 receptors equally, making it useful for studying combined receptor activation versus selective activation.

- **JWH-015**: A selective CB2 agonist from the JWH (John W. Huffman) series. Used to study CB2-mediated anti-inflammatory effects, particularly in microglial cells in the central nervous system. Has shown neuroprotective properties in models of neurodegenerative disease.

- **JWH-133**: Another selective CB2 agonist from the Huffman series. Has been investigated for its anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer, and neuroprotective properties. Particularly notable for research into CB2's role in tumor growth inhibition.

## The Entourage Effect and Its Relevance

Pharmaceutical isolates like dronabinol (pure THC) lack the entourage effect — the synergistic interaction between cannabinoids, terpenes, and other cannabis constituents. Key considerations:

- **CBD moderates THC's psychoactivity**: In whole-plant preparations, CBD can reduce anxiety, paranoia, and cognitive impairment sometimes caused by THC alone
- **Terpenes modify receptor activity**: Compounds like (E)-β-caryophyllene, myrcene, and limonene influence how cannabinoids interact with receptors
- **Flavonoids contribute therapeutically**: Cannabis-derived flavonoids like cannflavin A and B have anti-inflammatory properties independent of cannabinoid receptors

Sativex, as a whole-plant extract, preserves much of the entourage effect while providing pharmaceutical-grade consistency. This positions it as a middle ground between isolated synthetic cannabinoids and raw plant material.
