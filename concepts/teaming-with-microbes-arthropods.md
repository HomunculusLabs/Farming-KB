# Teaming with Microbes — Arthropods

In *Teaming with Microbes*, Lowenfels and Lewis describe soil arthropods as the shredders, predators, and ecosystem engineers of the [[lowenfels-earthworms-soil-food-web]] — the visible (barely) organisms that physically break down organic matter and create habitat for smaller organisms.

## Scale in the Soil

Using the book's scaling metaphor:
- Soil mites and springtails (100 micrometers to 5 millimeters) would be "the diameter of a good-sized tree" if a bacterium were a piece of spaghetti
- They are large enough to be seen with the naked eye under ideal conditions

## Major Groups

### Springtails (Collembola)
- Among the most abundant soil arthropods
- Named for their forked appendage (furcula) that snaps against the ground to launch them into the air
- Primarily fungivores — they feed on fungal hyphae and spores
- Also consume decaying organic matter and bacteria
- Their feeding regulates fungal populations and distributes fungal spores

### Soil Mites (Acari)
- Extremely diverse and abundant
- Include oribatid mites (slow-moving decomposers), mesostigmatid mites (predators), and prostigmatid mites (mixed feeding)
- Oribatid mites are major decomposers of tough organic materials
- Predatory mites feed on nematodes, springtails, and other small arthropods
- Some mites are symbiotic with fungi, helping disperse spores

### Beetles and Other Insects
- Various beetles, ants, and insect larvae participate in soil food webs
- Dung beetles, ground beetles, and rove beetles are significant soil predators
- Ants aerate and mix soil through their tunneling activities
- Some beetles are major shredders of coarse organic matter

## The Shredder Role

Arthropods serve as the "shredders" of the soil food web:
- They physically break up large pieces of organic matter (leaves, twigs, woody debris)
- This shredding dramatically increases surface area for [[teaming-with-microbes-overview]] and [[teaming-with-microbes-overview]] colonization
- Without shredders, decomposition of coarse organic matter would be extremely slow
- They are the first step in the decomposition cascade

## Soil Structure Engineering

Arthropods create soil structure through their movement:
- Their burrowing creates pores and channels in the soil
- These channels improve water infiltration and air circulation
- The pores they create become habitat for smaller organisms
- Their waste contributes to soil organic matter

## The Fungal Connection

Many soil arthropods have intimate relationships with fungi:
- Springtails are primary consumers of fungal hyphae
- Some mites cultivate or disperse fungal spores on their bodies
- [[teaming-with-microbes-overview]] have evolved to trap and consume certain arthropods
- The predator-prey relationship between fungi and arthropods regulates both populations

## Bioindicators

Soil arthropod diversity indicates soil health:
- Diverse arthropod communities indicate a functioning soil food web
- Their absence suggests chemical contamination or severe physical disturbance
- They are sensitive to pesticides and other chemicals
- Their presence generally indicates adequate organic matter and moisture
