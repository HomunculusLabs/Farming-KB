# Soil Protozoa Nutrient Cycling and Mineralization

## Overview

Soil protozoa play a central role [[fukuoka-natural-soil-fertility]] and

### Carbon Flow Through the Loop

Carbon entering the soil microbial loop through root exudates and organic matter
decomposition follows distinct pathways depending on the dominant microbial group and
grazer community. In bacteria-dominated soils (typical of disturbed or annually
tilled systems), carbon flows primarily through the bacterial channel: labile carbon
substrates are rapidly taken up by bacteria, which are then consumed by flagellates
and small amoebae. The protozoan growth efficiency on bacterial carbon is typically
10–40%, meaning 60–90% of consumed carbon is respired as carbon dioxide. In fungal-
dominated soils (typical of perennial systems, no-till agriculture, and forest
soils), carbon flows through the fungal channel at a slower rate but with greater
efficiency, as fungal biomass turns over more slowly and supports a different suite
of protozoan grazers. The balance between these two channels significantly influences
overall soil carbon storage and nutrient release patterns.

## Nitrogen Mineralization by Protozoan Grazing

### Mechanism of Protozoan Nitrogen Release

Nitrogen mineralization by protozoa occurs through two primary mechanisms. First,
when protozoa consume bacteria, they catabolize bacterial proteins and nucleic acids
to meet their metabolic energy requirements. The carbon component is oxidized to
carbon dioxide, but the nitrogen component cannot be eliminated through respiration
and is instead excreted as ammonium (NH4+). This process is called protozoan
ammonification. Second, protozoa have a lower carbon-to-nitrogen ratio (C:N) than
their bacterial prey (protozoan C:N is typically 5–10:1, while bacterial C:N ranges
from 3–8:1 for active cells but the biomass as consumed includes non-protein
components). Because protozoa assimilate carbon less efficiently than nitrogen
relative to their biomass requirements, they must excrete excess nitrogen to maintain
their internal C:N balance. The combination of metabolic ammonification and
stoichiometric excess excretion results in rapid and continuous nitrogen release
during protozoan feeding.

### Quantification of Mineralization Rates

Laboratory and field studies have quantified protozoan nitrogen mineralization rates
with considerable precision. Individual flagellates mineralize approximately
0.01–0.05 pg N per hour. Large amoebae mineralize 0.1–0.5 pg N per hour. At typical
soil population densities, protozoan communities mineralize 0.5–2.0 μg N per gram of
soil per day, which can represent 20–60% of total nitrogen mineralization in
undisturbed soils. In the rhizosphere, where bacterial and protozoan populations are
enriched by root exudates, mineralization rates can be 5–10 times higher than in bulk
