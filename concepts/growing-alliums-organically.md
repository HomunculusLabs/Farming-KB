# Growing Alliums Organically

The allium family includes onions, garlic, leeks, shallots, and chives. These are among the most widely grown vegetables and are relatively straightforward in an organic [[comparison-crop-rotation-vs-cover-cropping]] and their strong scent can help deter certain pests.

## General Growing Principles

Alliums generally prefer an open, sunny position with well-drained soil. They do best in firm soil that has been well-prepared the previous season. Most alliums are grown from sets (small bulbs) rather than seed, which gives more reliable results and earlier maturity. In rotation, they fit well after brassicas or in the root crop plot.

## Onions (Allium cepa)

**Soil and site:** Firm, well-drained soil, sunny position. Avoid recently manured ground (can cause thick necks and poor storage). Apply blood, fish, and bone meal before planting.

**Planting sets:** Plant in mid-spring, pushing sets gently into prepared soil so just the tip shows. Space 4-6in (10-15cm) apart in rows 12in (30cm) apart. Birds may pull up sets -- cover with netting or cloches until rooted.

**Sowing from seed:** Sow in mid-spring in shallow drills 1/2in (1cm) deep, thin to 4-6in apart. Seed-grown onions store better than set-grown.

**Maintenance:** Hoe carefully between rows -- alliums have shallow roots and do not compete well with weeds. Water in dry spells, especially when bulbs are swelling. Stop watering when bulbs begin to ripen (foliage starts to yellow and fall over).

**Harvest:** When foliage has yellowed and fallen over naturally, lift bulbs on a dry day. Leave on the ground to dry in the sun for a few days, then hang in nets or strings in a cool, dry, well-ventilated place.

**Pests:** Onion fly (maggots attack roots -- [[brassinosteroid-fungal-growth-promotion]] -- do not compost infected material), thrips (tiny insects -- spray with insecticidal soap).

## Garlic (Allium sativum)

**Planting:** Plant individual cloves in late fall or early winter, pushing them 1in (2.5cm) into the soil, pointed end up, 6in (15cm) apart in rows 12in (30cm) apart. A cold period is essential for proper bulb development.

**Soil:** Well-drained, sunny position. Add compost or manure before planting.

**Maintenance:** Keep weed-free. Water in dry spells. Remove flower stalks (scapes) as they appear to direct energy into bulb formation.

**Harvest:** When foliage yellows and falls over in midsummer, lift carefully. Dry thoroughly before storing. Good ventilation is essential.

## Leeks (Allium porrum)

**Soil:** Deep, rich soil. Leeks are often planted in the trenches vacated by early peas or beans. Dig a trench 6-8in (15-20cm) deep, fill the bottom with compost or manure, replace soil.

**Sowing:** Sow seeds in a seedbed in mid-spring, 1/2in deep in drills 6in apart. Transplant when pencil-thick to their permanent positions in early summer.

**Planting:** Make holes 6in (15cm) deep and 6in apart with a dibber. Drop a leek seedling into each hole and water to settle the soil around it. Do not fill the holes with soil -- leeks will blanch naturally as they grow.

**Maintenance:** Keep weed-free. Earth up gradually as leeks grow to increase the blanched white stem. Water in dry weather.

**Harvest:** Can be left in the ground through winter and lifted as needed. Harvest from early autumn through late winter.

**Pests:** Leek moth (larvae mine leaves), onion fly, rust (orange pustules on leaves -- remove affected leaves).

## Shallots (Allium cepa var. aggregatum)

**Planting:** Plant sets in late winter or [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]. Leeks are best left in the ground and lifted as needed.

## Shallots (Allium ascalonicum)

Shallots are earlier to mature and have a milder flavor than main crop onions. They require similar growing conditions and are easy to grow from sets, rarely troubled by diseases, and store well for winter use.

**Varieties:** Golden Gourmet (Dutch Yellow) and Pikant (Dutch Red) have flavorful, large bulbs with excellent storage potential. The exhibition variety Hative de Niort produces bigger bulbs which do not keep as well.

**Planting:** Remove any dead foliage and plant sets in rows in early spring, 6in (15cm) apart in rows 12in (30cm) apart. Ensure the tip of the bulb is just below soil level. Do not press sets into the ground or they will push themselves out again when they grow roots. For deep beds, plant in staggered rows with 6in between sets.

**Maintenance:** Weed and water as required. In early summer, draw the soil away from the bulbs to assist ripening.

**Harvesting:** Dig when foliage dies down in summer. Clean the bulbs and dry them on a piece of chicken wire raised off the ground so air can circulate. Store in nets in a frost-free place.

## Green Onions (Salad Onions)

Green onions are picked before the mature bulb forms. They have a milder flavor than the larger types and do not store well. White Lisbon and Ishikuro are recommended varieties. Sow under cloches in late winter, and in the open at three-week intervals beginning in early spring. They will not need thinning. For deep beds, scatter seeds in a wide row.
