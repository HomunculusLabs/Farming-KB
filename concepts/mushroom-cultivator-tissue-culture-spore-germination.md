# Tissue Culture and Spore Germination Techniques

Two primary methods exist for starting mushroom cultures: tissue
culture (cloning from a living mushroom) and multispore germination
(from spores). Each method has distinct advantages and
characteristics that influence strain genetics, vigor, and
consistency.

## Tissue Culture: Cloning Mushrooms

Tissue culture is an assured method of preserving the exact genetic
character of a living mushroom. In tissue culture a living specimen
is cloned, whereas in multispore culture new strains are created from
genetic recombination.

### Timing and Specimen Selection
Tissue cultures must be taken from mushrooms within 24 to 48 hours
of being picked. If specimens are several days old, too dry, or too
mature, a pure culture will be difficult to isolate. The best
locations for excising clean tissue include:
- The cap interior
- The upper region of the stem
- The area where the gill plate joins the underside of the cap

Some mushrooms have a thick cuticle overlaying the cap that can be
peeled back to access the underlying flesh.

### Procedure
1. Wipe the mushroom surface with an alcohol-soaked cotton swab,
   removing dirt and damaged external tissue
2. Break the mushroom cap or stem, exposing interior hyphae
3. Flame a scalpel until red-hot and cool it in a media-filled petri
   dish
4. Cut into the flesh, removing a small fragment of tissue
5. Transfer the tissue fragment to the center of a nutrient-filled
   petri dish as quickly as possible, minimizing air exposure
6. Repeat into at least three, preferably five more dishes
7. Label each dish with species, date, culture type (tissue), and
   agar medium

### Contamination Expectations
An overall contamination rate of 10% is tolerable for most cultivators.
In primary cultures from wild specimens, 25% contamination is not
unusual. The most commonly encountered contaminants in tissue culture
are bacteria. If sporulating mold colonies develop adjacent to the
growing mycelium, the culture should be promptly isolated through
successive transfers.
