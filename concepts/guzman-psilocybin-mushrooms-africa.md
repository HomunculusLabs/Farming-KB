# Psilocybin Mushrooms of Africa

Africa has a relatively poorly documented neurotropic mycobiota compared to other
continents. Guzman, Allen & Gartz (2000) noted that many regions of Africa lack
mycological surveys, and records are sparse outside of North Africa, South
Africa, and a few scattered tropical locations. The continent likely harbors
many more species than currently recorded.

## Widely Distributed African Species

Amanita muscaria, A. pantherina, Claviceps paspali, C. purpurea, Copelandia
tropicalis, Gymnopilus spectabilis (northern Africa), Inocybe corydalina
(northern Africa), Panaeolina foenisecii, Panaeolus africanus, P. fimicola, P.
microsporus, P. papilionaceus, P. retirugis, P. sphinctrinus, P. subbalteatus,
P. tropicalis, Pluteus salicinus, Psilocybe cyanescens, P. goniospora, P.
mairei (southwest).

## Distribution by Country/Region

### Algeria
Claviceps purpurea, Psilocybe mairei (originally described from North Africa).

### Chad
Panaeolus africanus.

### Ethiopia
Claviceps purpurea.

### Ivory Coast
Claviceps paspali, Conocybe sp. (?), Psilocybe sp. (?) -- Samorini (1995)
reported unidentified neurotropic fungi.

### Kenya
Panaeolus sp., P. aquamarina, Psilocybe cubensis (?) (reported as Stropharia
sp. cf. cubensis by Vedcourt & Trump 1969), Psilocybe sp. (reported as
Stropharia sp. by Charters 1957, 1958). The Kenyan records remain tentative.

### Madagascar (Malagasy Republic)
Copelandia cyanescens, Dictyophora indusiata.

### Mauritius Island
Claviceps paspali, C. purpurea.

### Morocco
Morocco has the richest documented North African mycobiota:
- **Amanita muscaria, A. pantherina**
- **Copelandia bispora**
- **Inocybe calamistrata, I. corydalina**
- **Panaeolus fimicola, P. papilionaceus**
- **Pluteus cyanopus, P. atricapillus, P. salicinus, P. villosus**
- **Psilocybe mairei** -- Originally described from North Africa; well-documented
  in Morocco.

### Republic of Central Africa
Panaeolus africanus, P. microsporus, Pluteus atricapillus.

### Rhodesia (Zimbabwe)
Claviceps paspali.

### South Africa
South Africa has the best-documented subtropical African mycobiota:
- **Amanita muscaria, A. pantherina**
- **Panaeolina foenisecii, Panaeolus papilionaceus, P. retirugis, P.
  subbalteatus**
- **Pluteus salicinus**
- **Psilocybe natalensis** (endemic to Natal/KwaZulu-Natal region), P.
  semilanceata (?)

### Sudan
Panaeolus africanus.

### Tanzania
Amanita muscaria, Copelandia tropicalis, Pluteus atricapillus.

### Uganda
Panaeolus papilionaceus, Psilocybe goniospora.

### Zaire (Democratic Republic of Congo)
Copelandia cyanescens, Panaeolina foenisecii.

## Key species

- **Psilocybe mairei** -- Originally described from North Africa. Also found in
  Morocco and Algeria. One of the few Psilocybe species with an African type
  locality.
- **Psilocybe natalensis** -- Endemic to the Natal region of South Africa.
- **Panaeolus africanus** -- An African Panaeolus species found in Chad,
  Central African Republic, and Sudan.
- **Psilocybe goniospora** -- Found in Uganda and Kenya; also in India and Sri
  Lanka, suggesting an Afro-Asian tropical distribution.

## Distribution patterns and knowledge gaps

Africa remains the most poorly surveyed continent for neurotropic fungi. The
recorded species are concentrated in:
1. **North Africa** (Morocco, Algeria) -- Temperate Mediterranean species
   shared with southern Europe (Amanita spp., P. mairei, Pluteus spp.).
2. **South Africa** -- Subtropical species including the endemic P. natalensis.
3. **East Africa** (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda) -- Scattered tropical records,
   many tentative.

Vast regions including West Africa, Central Africa, the Congo Basin, and the
Sahel have virtually no records. The authors note that wherever mycologists
conduct systematic surveys in under-explored regions, new species are
inevitably found, suggesting Africa's true neurotropic fungal diversity is
likely far greater than currently documented. As Gartz (1996) observed: "The
mushrooms occur in abundance wherever mycologists abound."
