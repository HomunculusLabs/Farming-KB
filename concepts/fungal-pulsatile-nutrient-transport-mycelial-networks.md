# Pulsatile Nutrient Transport in Fungal Mycelial Networks

## Overview

Research by Bebber, Tlalka, and colleagues (Chapter 1 of *Fungi in the
Environment*) revealed that saprotrophic [[pcsi-scintillation-imaging-mycelial-nutrient-transport-bebber]] (PCSI) of radiolabelled compounds.

## Photon-Counting Scintillation Imaging (PCSI)

The technique tracks movement of ¹⁴C-labelled nitrogen compounds
(specifically the non-metabolized amino acid analogue
α-amino-isobutyrate, or ¹⁴C-AIB) through living mycelial networks
growing on an inert scintillation screen. Key advantages over previous
methods (autoradiography, destructive scintillation counting):

- **Non-invasive**: The colony remains intact and functional throughout
  measurement
- **Continuous real-time tracking**: Produces time-series data rather
  than endpoint snapshots
- **Extended duration**: Measurements can continue for over 6 weeks in
  larger microcosms with wood-block inocula and sand/soil substrata

## Discovery of Pulsatile Transport

When ¹⁴C-AIB was supplied to a central inoculum, the radiolabel was
taken up and distributed through the growing colony. Analysis of the
time-series data revealed:

- A **net translocation pattern** — overall movement of nitrogen from
  source to growing margins
- A **strong pulsatile component** superimposed on this net movement,
  particularly through corded (differentiated) transport systems
- Oscillations that continued for 5–7 days after initial loading

### Phase Relationships

Fourier analysis of pixel-by-pixel time-series data produced colour-coded
maps of oscillation frequency, amplitude, and phase across the colony:

- **Assimilatory hyphae** on the inoculum and new resources oscillated
- **Foraging hyphae** at the colony margin also oscillated
- These regions were **out of phase with each other** — when one pulsed,
  the other was quiescent
- Distinct **phase domains** formed with locally synchronized behaviour
- The amplitude pulsing centre shifted toward newly added resources

The out-of-phase relationship between assimilatory and foraging regions
suggests a coordinated pumping mechanism rather than random diffusion.

## Transport Speed and Scale

In larger microcosms with wood-block inocula and compressed sand:

- Within **1 hour** of ¹⁴C-AIB loading, the label had travelled
  **250 mm** along a major cord — a remarkable speed for cytoplasmic
  transport
- Within **4 hours**, signal was present in most of the growing mycelium
  subtended by that cord
- Not all cords transported simultaneously, suggesting selective
  prioritization of [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]] and developmental age
- Described by a logistic equation

### Phase 2: Asymmetric Foraging
- Transition to sparser, more asymmetric growth
- **Controls without added resources** spontaneously switched to
  asymmetric growth and selectively allocated resources to broad sectors
- **Added damp cellulosic resources** (filter paper) induced marked
  nitrogen accumulation and tightly focused asymmetric growth directed
  toward the new resource
