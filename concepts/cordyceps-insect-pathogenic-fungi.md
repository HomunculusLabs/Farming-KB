## Cordyceps — Insect Pathogenic Fungi

## Introduction to Cordyceps

If you can muster any empathy toward an insect or spider, Cordyceps can seem a terrifying adversary.

The genus Cordyceps comprises fungi that specialize in parasitizing insects and other arthropods, ultimately killing their hosts and erupting from their bodies to produce spore-bearing fruiting structures.

These are among the most dramatic and visually striking of all [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]].

Rather than decomposing dead organic matter, Cordyceps actively attacks living insects.

The fungus typically begins its assault when a spore lands on the surface of an insect host.

The spore germinates and produces hyphae that penetrate the insect's exoskeleton using a combination of mechanical pressure and enzymatic digestion.

Once inside the host, the fungus proliferates through the hemocoel (body cavity), consuming the insect's internal tissues while the host continues to live.

The fungus is remarkably precise in its consumption, avoiding vital organs until late in the infection to keep the host alive and mobile for as long as possible.

This extended survival period benefits the fungus by allowing the infected insect to move to locations favorable for [[cordyceps-militaris]], a brighter orange species, infects pupae and larvae of various Lepidoptera and Coleoptera.

Other species target specific ant genera, with the precision of a biological assassin.

This host specificity is thought to be the result of long co-evolutionary relationships between the fungi and their insect hosts.
