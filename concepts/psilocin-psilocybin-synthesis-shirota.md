# Concise Large-Scale Synthesis of Psilocin and Psilocybin

## Overview

Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) reported a concise, gram-scale synthesis of both [[psilocybin]], the principal hallucinogenic constituents of magic mushrooms, from commercially available [[psilocybin-synthesis]] was made possible by the discovery of a zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate intermediate that could be isolated by simple filtration, avoiding the anion-exchange chromatography required in prior methods. The work was published in the Journal of Natural Products (Vol. 66, No. 6) and conducted at the National Institute of Health Sciences, Tokyo, Japan.

## Background and Motivation

Psilocin and psilocybin are tryptamine alkaloids structurally related to the neurotransmitter [[serotonin]] and likely exert hallucinogenic effects by interfering with normal brain serotonin signaling. Since June 6, 2002, fungi containing these compounds have been regulated under the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law in Japan. Chromatographic identification methods (TLC, GC, HPLC) require pure standard compounds, which are difficult to isolate directly from mushrooms because psilocin readily decomposes and psilocybin has very high polarity. Prior syntheses of psilocybin were scarce and low-yielding, with Hofmann and Troxler (1959) reporting approximately 20% and Nichols and Frescas (1999) achieving 47%. These factors motivated the development of a practical large-scale route suitable for producing certified reference standards.

## Why Chromatography-Free Synthesis Matters

Traditional synthesis of psilocybin requires purification by anion-exchange resin chromatography, which is labor-intensive, difficult to scale, and requires specialized equipment. By eliminating all chromatographic steps, the Shirota route becomes accessible to standard organic chemistry laboratories and is readily scalable. Every intermediate in this synthesis is a crystalline solid isolated by filtration and washing, making the process robust, reproducible, and well-suited to multi-gram production.

## Synthesis Route: 4-Hydroxyindole to Psilocin

The entire sequence begins with commercially available 4-hydroxyindole (compound 3) and proceeds through four steps to psilocin (compound 1).

### Step 1: Acetyl Protection (3 to 4)

4-Hydroxyindole is acetylated with acetic anhydride (20 mL, 210 mmol) in pyridine (20 mL, 246 mmol) and anhydrous dichloromethane (200 mL) at 0 °C to room temperature over 2 hours. The product, 4-acetylindole (4), is isolated by aqueous workup (ethyl acetate extraction, water and brine washes, Na2SO4 drying) and collected by filtration as ivory white crystals, washed with water and ethyl acetate. The reaction is run on a scale exceeding 185 mmol (>25 g starting material), yielding 34 g of crystalline product. Characterization includes 1H NMR (δH 8.27, H-1; 7.22, H-7), 13C NMR (δC 169.6, OCOCH3; 143.6, C-4), and ESIMS (m/z 176.1 [M+H]+).

### Step 2: Oxalylation (4 to 5)

4-Acetylindole (17.6 g, 100 mmol) is treated with oxalyl chloride (13 mL, 146 mmol) in anhydrous diethyl ether (100 mL) at 0 °C for 15 minutes. After addition of n-hexane (200 mL), the reaction flask is stored at -20 °C overnight. The glyoxalyl chloride intermediate (5) crystallizes as yellow crystals, which are separated by filtration. This intermediate is sufficiently stable for isolation but is not carried forward independently; it is used directly in the next step.

### Step 3: Amidation (5 to 6)

The yellow crystals of compound 5 are dissolved in anhydrous THF (100 mL) and treated with dimethylamine (2 M in THF, 60 mL, 120 mmol) and pyridine (10 mL, 123 mmol) at 0 °C over 15 minutes. Additional ether is added as needed due to solidification. After stirring for 15 minutes at room temperature, 3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-acetylindole (6) precipitates and is collected by filtration, washed sequentially with n-hexane, ethyl acetate, and water, yielding 22.0 g (80.0%) as an ivory white crystalline powder. Characterization includes 1H NMR (δH 10.40, H-1; 3.02, 2.92, NCH3), 13C NMR (δC 185.4, C-1 prime; 170.9, OCOCH3; 168.4, C-2 prime), and ESIMS (m/z 275.1 [M+H]+).

### Step 4: Reduction to Psilocin (6 to 1)

Compound 6 (22.0 g, 80 mmol) in anhydrous THF (250 mL) is added dropwise over 2 hours to a suspension of lithium aluminum hydride (ca. 12 g) in anhydrous THF (300 mL) under argon, then refluxed for 2 hours. This single reduction simultaneously cleaves the acetyl protecting group and reduces the oxalylamide to the ethylamine side chain, directly yielding psilocin. After careful quenching with anhydrous Na2SO4 powder and aqueous Na2SO4, the mixture is filtered through an aminopropyl silica gel Celite pad and concentrated to afford psilocin (1) as white crystals (14.3 g, 87.5%), mp 169-174 °C (dec), matching literature values. Key spectral data: 1H NMR δH 2.94 (H2-1 prime), 2.70 (H2-2 prime), 2.38 (NMe2); ESIMS m/z 205.1 [M+H]+.

## Synthesis Route: Psilocin to Psilocybin

Converting psilocin to psilocybin requires selective phosphorylation of the 4-hydroxyl group on the indole ring while leaving the aliphatic amine untouched. This regioselectivity challenge is central to the difficulty of psilocybin synthesis.

### Screening of Phosphorylation Methods

Several phosphorylation methodologies were applied. Most did not consume psilocin at all, reflecting the poor nucleophilicity of the phenol in this electron-rich indole system. Two methods showed promise:
- The phosphoryl iodide method (Stowell and Widlanski, 1995): using tribenzyl phosphite, I2, and DMAP
- The pyrophosphate method (Almeida et al., 1999): using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-BuLi

The pyrophosphate method was selected for its easier handling and superior reagent stability, making it more suitable for large-scale work.

### Step 5: Phosphorylation (1 to 8 via 7)

Psilocin (5.4 g, 26.4 mmol) is dissolved in anhydrous THF (265 mL) at -78 °C under argon. n-Butyllithium (2.6 M in hexane, 11.5 mL, 29.9 mmol) is added to deprotonate the phenolic hydroxyl. After 5 minutes, tetrabenzylpyrophosphate (18.0 g, 33.4 mmol), prepared in nearly quantitative yield from dibenzyl phosphate, is added in one portion. The mixture is stirred for 1 hour while warming to 0 °C. After checking for production of the dibenzyl phosphate derivative (7), aminopropyl silica gel (ca. 20 g) is added, and the mixture is worked up by dilution with ethyl acetate and filtration through Celite. The filtrate is concentrated, redissolved in CH2Cl2, and stored overnight, during which the key zwitterionic rearrangement occurs.

### Discovery of the Zwitterionic Intermediate (8)

Nichols and Frescas had previously observed that hydrolytic cleavage of one O-benzyl group occurred rapidly, yielding a zwitterionic O-monobenzyl phosphate as a mixture that was difficult to purify. The Shirota team discovered that by suspending the worked-up reaction mixture in CH2Cl2 overnight, a clean intramolecular rearrangement takes place: the liberated benzyl group migrates from oxygen to the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethylamino group, forming a quaternary ammonium ion. The resulting compound (8) is thus a zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative with both positive and negative charges within the same molecule.

The zwitterionic nature of compound 8 renders it insoluble in CH2Cl2, while excess dibenzyl phosphate byproduct remains soluble. This solubility difference is the purification principle: the white solid is collected by filtration and washed with CH2Cl2, yielding 10.5 g (85.2%) as a white powder. No chromatography of any kind is required.

Key NMR evidence for the structure of 8:
- 1H NMR shows two distinct benzyl environments: OCH2C6H5 at δH 4.96-4.98 (1H each, s) and NCH2C6H5 at δH 4.56 (2H, s, shifted upfield due to the quaternary ammonium)
- The N,N-dimethyl and ethylene protons are shifted downfield compared to psilocin itself, consistent with the positive charge
- 31P NMR confirms the phosphate moiety at δP -5.45
- HMBC correlations directly link the upfield benzyl methylene to the N-methyl carbons, confirming N-alkylation
- NOESY correlations support spatial proximity of the N-benzyl to the N,N-dimethyl group
- ESI-MS: m/z 465.2 [M+H]+ (calcd for C26H30N2O4P: 465.1943, found 465.1883)

### Step 6: Catalytic Hydrogenolysis (8 to 2)

The zwitterionic intermediate 8 (10.5 g, 22.5 mmol) is dissolved in methanol (225 mL) with 10% palladium on carbon (ca. 1 g) under argon, then stirred under a hydrogen atmosphere at room temperature for 2 hours. Water (ca. 50 mL) is added to dissolve deposited product, and stirring continues for one more hour. After filtration through Celite and evaporation, psilocybin (2) crystallizes as a white needle powder (5.6 g, 87.5%), mp 190-198 °C, matching literature values. Both benzyl groups are cleanly removed in a single hydrogenolysis step. Key spectral data: 1H NMR (D2O) δH 7.22 (H-7), 7.18 (H-2), 3.44 (H2-2 prime), 3.28 (H2-1 prime), 2.86 (NMe2); 31P NMR δP -4.48; ESIMS m/z 285.1 [M+H]+.

## Yield Summary

| Step | Transformation | Isolated Yield |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| 1 | 4-Hydroxyindole to 4-acetylindole (3 to 4) | Quantitative (crystalline) |
| 2-3 | Two-step acylation-amidation (4 to 6) | 80% overall |
| 4 | Reduction to psilocin (6 to 1) | 87.5% |
| 5 | Phosphorylation and zwitterion formation (1 to 8) | 85.2% |
| 6 | Hydrogenolysis to psilocybin (8 to 2) | 87.5% |

The overall yield from psilocin to psilocybin exceeds 72% on gram scale, compared to 20% (Hofmann/Troxler, 1959) and 47% (Nichols/Frescas, 1999) in prior work.

## Key Innovations

1. **Acetyl over benzyl protection**: Acetyl groups are cleaved simultaneously with oxalylamide reduction by LiAlH4, eliminating a separate deprotection step. Benzyl-protected 4-hydroxyindole derivatives are unstable and require careful reaction control without yielding crystalline products.

2. **Crystallization-based isolation at every step**: All six isolated compounds (4, 5, 6, 1, 8, 2) are crystalline solids isolated by filtration and washing, enabling practical scale-up without column chromatography or preparative HPLC.

3. **Zwitterionic intermediate 8 as a purification handle**: The spontaneous intramolecular benzyl migration transforms the phosphorylation byproduct mixture into a single zwitterionic compound with distinct solubility properties, enabling isolation by simple filtration. This elegantly circumvents the need for anion-exchange resin chromatography.

## Broader Significance

This synthesis represents the most practical route to analytical standard quantities of psilocin and psilocybin available at its time of publication. The chromatography-free design makes gram-scale production feasible in laboratories without specialized purification equipment. The zwitterionic rearrangement strategy for isolating phosphate esters may be generalizable to other phosphorylated indole alkaloids or phenolic natural products facing similar purification challenges. The work was directly motivated by regulatory needs in Japan for certified reference standards used in forensic identification and food safety testing of magic mushroom materials.
