# Fertigation: Bucket Drip Water and Nutrient Delivery System

## Overview

Fertigation combines fertilization with irrigation — delivering dissolved
nutrients directly to plant root zones through a simple bucket-drip system.
This approach is particularly valuable for gardeners using wide plant spacing
who need to provide supplemental water and nutrition during dry periods without
investing in expensive irrigation infrastructure.

## Bucket Drip Fertigation Method

### Equipment Needed

- **Five-gallon (20-liter) buckets**: Recycled institutional kitchen buckets
  are ideal and inexpensive
- **Small holes or drip emitters**: Near the bottom of each bucket
- **[[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof]] dissolved in water

### How It Works

1. Fill buckets with water and dissolved fertilizer
2. Place one bucket near each hill or group of plants
3. Water slowly drips from the bucket into the root zone
4. The slow delivery allows deep penetration without runoff
5. Refill buckets as needed during dry periods

### Advantages Over Overhead Watering

- **Deep root zone penetration**: Water goes where roots need it most
- **Reduced evaporation**: No spray lost to wind or surface evaporation
- **Targeted delivery**: Each plant receives precise nutrition
- **Reduced disease pressure**: Foliage stays dry, reducing [[dryland-vegetable-gardening]]:
