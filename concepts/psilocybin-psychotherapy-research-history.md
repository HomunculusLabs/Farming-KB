## [[psilocybin-psychotherapy]] Research History

The use of [[psilocybin]] and other hallucinogenic substances in psychotherapy represents one of the most promising yet politically suppressed areas of psychiatric research in the 20th century. Beginning in the 1950s and continuing until legislative restrictions halted most work in the late 1960s, researchers across Europe and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] demonstrated remarkable clinical success rates using psilocybin as an adjunct to psychotherapeutic treatment.

## Psycholytic Therapy

### Origins and Principles

Oftentimes, the origins of chronic neuroses are rooted in early childhood. Neurotic behavior can evolve under a wide variety of circumstances that include combinations of unfortunate personal and social conditions. According to various schools of traditional psychoanalytic thought, the dissolution of neuroses is a lengthy process whose progress depends on penetrating layers of "character armoring" and identifying the root causes of unhealthy fixations.

Hoping to shorten the duration of treatment by accelerating the pace of resistance dissolution, practitioners turned to pharmaceuticals as an adjunct to the therapeutic process. Upon discovery of the unique properties of hallucinogenic agents during the 1950s, LSD gained widespread popularity as a therapeutic agent, while [mescaline remained obscure, used by comparatively few practitioners.

Patients under the influence of these substances confronted long-repressed traumatic events as they began to surface and enter conscious awareness. In some cases, such emerging traumatic experiences had been repressed since early childhood. Medical records and case histories indicate that many patients not only remembered but actually relived traumatic experiences. The alkaloid was considered quite safe, based on existing toxicological data.

### Psilocybin as the Drug of Choice

Following its initial isolation and discovery of its chemical structure, psilocybin quickly joined the group of hallucinogens used in psychotherapy. Several factors established psilocybin as the preferred therapeutic agent:

- **Extremely [[psilocybin-toxicity-and-safety-profile]]
- **Accurate dosing below 10 mg** -- Dosages below 10 mg can be measured with accuracy, and effects within this range can always be brought under control
- **Therapeutically efficient range** -- Dosages up to 10 mg are known to be therapeutically efficient
- **Manageable duration** -- States of consciousness induced by psilocybin last only five hours on average, compared to much longer periods with LSD
- **Less patient fatigue** -- Participants experienced less fatigue with psilocybin than with LSD

H. Leuner still considers psilocybin the most effective alkaloid for use as a pharmaceutical aid in psychotherapy, despite recent progress in developing other substances designed for therapeutic applications, such as the substituted phenethylamines like MDMA.

### Therapeutic Mechanism

The psycholytic approach is based on the understanding that hallucinogenic substances are non-specific triggers causing a sequence of [[weil-the-natural-mind-altered-states-normalcy]] of consciousness. According to S. Grof"s analysis of 5,000 experimental protocols, it is the individual's personality along with the experimental setting that significantly shapes the nature of the experience.

Grof identified a developmental sequence of experiences:

1. **Abstract experiences** -- Geometric patterns, sensory enhancement
2. **Aesthetic experiences** -- Artistic appreciation
3. **Psychodynamic experiences** -- Surfacing of repressed memories
4. **Perinatal experiences** -- Birth-related states
5. **Transpersonal experiences** -- Beyond individual ego boundaries

With repeated administrations of relatively low doses, participants typically progressed through these stages one by one, eventually attaining and lingering at the transpersonal level. Such landmark experiences stand out as significant personal milestones whose impact does not diminish for many years, even if the individual never uses the substance again.

Due to the profound, far-reaching nature of the psycholytic process, skillful and responsible guidance is crucial to preventing the genesis of new sets of neurotic symptoms. Whenever a new therapy is adopted, occasional mistakes are not uncommon during the initial phases. The medical use of hallucinogenic substances was no exception.

## Clinical Success Rates

### Statistical Evidence

According to statistical data compiled from multiple studies, psycholytic therapy proved to be a remarkably efficient form of treatment, most notably in patients suffering from neuroses:

- **Follow-up study of 82 patients:** 65% showed long-term, clinically significant improvement
- **28 authors** described 1,600 therapy cases covering 42 investigations from 1953 to 1965
- **Course of illness** was judged very grave and chronic in 68% of the cases
- **Success rates of up to 70%** ("good and substantial improvement") were achieved, especially in:
  - Neurotic anxieties
  - Depressive-neurotic dysphorias
  - Neurotic character disorders

### Czechoslovak Research Program

In the former Czechoslovakia, M. Hausner conducted extensive work with LSD-assisted psychotherapy. His findings included important observations about [flashbacks:

- Several patients who went through a series of LSD sessions did experience flashback episodes between sessions
- However, therapeutic administration was continued in these cases
- Flashback episodes were far less spectacular than expected based on dramatic descriptions of the phenomenon
- Within the context of Hausner"s studies, flashbacks turned out to be merely temporary manifestations of issues that had reached the conscious mind
- Flashbacks disappeared as therapy progressed with continued administrations and did not recur after conclusion of the therapy program

These observations contradicted the biochemical theory which predicted that repeated administrations would increase storage of the substance inside the body, causing increasingly powerful flashback episodes.

## Psilocybin as a Diagnostic Tool

Beyond psychotherapy, psilocybin was investigated as a diagnostic tool for revealing brain damage in the former Czechoslovakia during the 1960s. The benefits of diagnosing brain-damaged patients with psilocybin were significant:

- **Minimal toxicity** -- Psilocybin was chosen specifically for this reason
- **Visual hallucinations suppressed** -- Found to be almost completely suppressed in patients suffering from lesions to parts of the central nervous system known to mediate visual-sensory functions
- **Potentiation of neurological processes** -- Psilocybin tended to potentiate a variety of neurological processes, effectively turning the alkaloid into a diagnostic tool used to reveal latent paralyses and other subtle types of damage to the central nervous system

While LSD could also have been used, researchers preferred psilocybin because dosage measurement was simpler, effects were short-term, and patients experienced less fatigue.
