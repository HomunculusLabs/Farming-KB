# Permaculture Plant Resources and Selection

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Permaculture design relies on selecting the right plants for the right functions in the right places. Rather than choosing plants primarily for ornamental value, permaculture selects plants for their ecological functions: food, nitrogen fixation, insect attraction, mulch production, soil building, wind protection, and wildlife habitat.

## The Function-Based Approach

In conventional gardening, plants are chosen for appearance. In permaculture, plants are chosen for function. Every plant in the system should serve at least three functions. Before selecting plants, ask:

1. What function does this plant perform?
2. What needs does this plant have?
3. What products does it yield?
4. What does it produce that other plants or animals can use?
5. How does it interact with other elements in the system?

## Plant Functions Categories

### Food Producers
Plants that produce edible yields for humans:
- **Fruit trees:** Apple, pear, plum, cherry, peach, apricot, fig, persimmon, pawpaw, citrus
- **Nut trees:** Walnut, pecan, hazelnut, almond, chestnut, pistachio
- **Berry bushes:** Blueberry, raspberry, blackberry, currant, gooseberry, elderberry, strawberry
- **Perennial vegetables:** Asparagus, rhubarb, globe artichoke, Good King Henry, sorrel, sea kale, walking onion
- **Annual vegetables:** All standard garden vegetables
- **Herbs:** Culinary and medicinal herbs
- **Root crops:** Potato, sweet potato, carrot, parsnip, beet, turnip, Jerusalem artichoke

### Nitrogen Fixers
Plants that convert atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available form through symbiotic bacteria:
- **Trees:** Black locust, honey locust, Siberian pea shrub, seaberry, autumn olive, mesquite, alder, acacia
- **Shrubs:** Ceanothus, elaeagnus, goumi, Russian olive, broom, gorse
- **Herbaceous:** Clover (white, red, crimson), alfalfa, vetch, lupine, peas, beans, fenugreek
- **Ground covers:** Clover, crown vetch, birdsfoot trefoil

### Insectary Plants
Plants that attract pollinators and beneficial predatory insects:
- **Umbellifers:** Dill, fennel, coriander, parsley, yarrow, Queen Anne's lace
- **Aster family:** Sunflowers, cosmos, zinnias, marigolds, calendula, coneflowers, asters
- **Mint family:** Lavender, rosemary, thyme, oregano, sage, basil, bee balm
- **Other:** Sweet alyssum, phacelia, buckwheat, borage, echinacea

### Dynamic Accumulators
Deep-rooted plants that draw minerals from subsoil and deposit them on the surface:
- **Comfrey:** Potassium, calcium, phosphorus (deepest taproot of any temperate herb)
- **Dandelion:** Calcium, iron, copper, magnesium, silicon
- **Chicory:** Copper, potassium, zinc
- **Yarrow:** Potassium, phosphorus, copper
- **Burdock:** Iron, magnesium, potassium
- **Nettle:** Iron, nitrogen, calcium, magnesium, sulfur
- **Plantain:** Calcium, magnesium, sulfur, zinc

### Mulch Producers
Plants that generate large quantities of biomass for sheet mulching:
- **Comfrey:** Cut 4-6 times per season; produces enormous biomass
- **Artichoke:** Large leaves produce heavy mulch
- **Cardoon:** Similar to artichoke; even larger
- **Bamboo:** Leaf drop provides continuous mulch
- **Banana:** Pseudostem and leaves produce massive organic matter
- **Sweet woodruff:** Low-growing living mulch

### Ground Covers
Low-growing plants that protect soil surface:
- **Clover:** Nitrogen-fixing ground cover for orchards and gardens
- **Creeping thyme:** Aromatic, edible, drought-tolerant
- **Oregano:** Vigorous spreader, culinary use
- **Nasturtium:** Edible flowers and leaves; traps aphids
- **Strawberry:** Edible fruit; living mulch
- **Ajuga:** Fast spreader; shade-tolerant
- **Violets:** Edible flowers and leaves; shade-tolerant

### Pest Repellents
Plants that deter harmful insects through aromatic compounds:
- **Allium family:** Garlic, onions, chives, leeks (deter borers, aphids, Japanese beetles)
- **Marigolds:** Deter nematodes; attract beneficial insects
- **Mints:** Deter cabbage moths, ants, flea beetles
- **Lavender:** Deter moths, fleas, mosquitoes
- **Rosemary:** Deter cabbage moths, bean beetles, carrot flies
- **Nasturtium:** Traps aphids; deters squash bugs, whiteflies

## Selection Criteria

### Climate Adaptation
Choose plants suited to your climate zone:
- **Hardiness zone:** Minimum winter temperature tolerance
- **Heat tolerance:** Summer heat survival
- **Chill hours:** For deciduous fruit trees (hours below 45°F needed for fruit set)
- **Rainfall:** Match to local precipitation patterns or provide irrigation

### Soil Adaptation
- **pH preference:** Acid-loving (blueberry), alkaline-tolerant (asparagus), or adaptable
- **Drainage:** Well-drained (lavender, rosemary), moisture-loving (willow, elderberry)
- **Fertility:** Heavy feeders (tomatoes, corn) vs. light feeders (most herbs, native plants)

### Site Conditions
- **Sun:** Full sun (6+ hours), partial shade (3-6 hours), full shade
- **Wind:** Wind-tolerant vs. sheltered
- **Space:** Mature size must fit the available space
- **Root patterns:** Compatible with neighboring plants

### Function Stacking
Prioritize plants that serve multiple functions:
- **Seaberry (sea buckthorn):** Nitrogen fixer + edible berries + wildlife habitat + windbreak
- **Comfrey:** Dynamic accumulator + mulch producer + bee attractant + medicinal
- **Elderberry:** Edible berries + wildlife habitat + insectary + medicinal + ornamental
- **Black locust:** Nitrogen fixer + timber + fence posts + bee forage + windbreak
