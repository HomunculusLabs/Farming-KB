# Permaculture Plant Resources and Selection
**Updated:** 2026-04-18
Permaculture design relies on selecting the right plants for the right
functions in the right places. Rather than choosing plants primarily
for ornamental value, permaculture selects plants for their ecological
functions: food, [[biological-nitrogen-fixation-agriculture]], insect attraction, mulch
production, soil building, wind protection, and [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]].

## The Function-Based Approach

In conventional gardening, plants are chosen for appearance. In
permaculture, plants are chosen for function. Every plant in the system
should serve at least three functions. Before selecting plants, ask:

1. What function does this plant perform?
2. What needs does this plant have?
3. What products does it yield?
4. What does it produce that other plants or animals can use?
5. How does it interact with other elements in the system?

## Plant Functions Categories

### Food Producers

Plants that produce edible yields for humans:

- **Fruit trees:** Apple, pear, plum, cherry, peach, apricot, fig,
  persimmon, pawpaw, citrus
- **Nut trees:** Walnut, pecan, hazelnut, almond, chestnut, pistachio
- **Berry bushes:** Blueberry, raspberry, blackberry, currant,
  gooseberry, elderberry, strawberry
- **[[comparison-perennial-vegetables-vs-cover-crops]]:** Asparagus, rhubarb, globe artichoke,
  Good King Henry, sorrel, sea kale, walking onion
- **Annual vegetables:** All standard garden vegetables
- **Herbs:** Culinary and medicinal herbs
- **Root crops:** Potato, sweet potato, carrot, parsnip, beet, turnip,
  [[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]]

### Nitrogen Fixers

Plants that convert atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available form
through symbiotic bacteria:

- **Trees:** Black locust, honey locust, Siberian pea shrub, seaberry,
  autumn olive, mesquite, alder, acacia
- **Shrubs:** Ceanothus, elaeagnus, goumi, Russian olive, broom, gorse
- **Herbaceous:** Clover (white, red, crimson), alfalfa, vetch, lupine,
  peas, beans, fenugreek
- **Ground covers:** Clover, crown vetch, birdsfoot trefoil

### Insectary Plants

Plants that attract pollinators and beneficial predatory insects:

- **Umbellifers:** Dill, fennel, coriander, parsley, yarrow, Queen
  Anne's lace
- **Aster family:** Sunflowers, cosmos, zinnias, marigolds, calendula,
  coneflowers, asters
- **Mint family:** Lavender, rosemary, thyme, oregano, sage, basil,
  bee balm
- **Other:** Sweet alyssum, phacelia, buckwheat, borage, echinacea

## Selection Criteria

### Climate Adaptation

Choose plants suited to your climate zone:

- **Hardiness zone:** Minimum winter [[mycorrhizal-drought-and-temperature-tolerance]]
- **Heat tolerance:** Summer heat survival
- **Chill hours:** For deciduous fruit trees (hours below 45F needed
  for fruit set)
- **Rainfall:** Match to local precipitation patterns or provide
  irrigation

### Soil Adaptation

- **pH preference:** Acid-loving (blueberry), alkaline-tolerant
  (asparagus), or adaptable
- **Drainage:** Well-drained (lavender, rosemary), moisture-loving
  (willow, elderberry)
- **Fertility:** Heavy feeders (tomatoes, corn) vs. light feeders
  (most herbs, native plants)

### Site Conditions

- **Sun:** Full sun (6+ hours), partial shade (3-6 hours), full shade
- **Wind:** Wind-tolerant vs. sheltered
- **Space:** Mature size must fit the available space
- **Root patterns:** Compatible with neighbouring plants

### Function Stacking

Prioritize plants that serve multiple functions:

- **Seaberry (sea buckthorn):** Nitrogen fixer + edible berries +
  wildlife habitat + windbreak
- **Comfrey:** [[comparison-hyperaccumulator-vs-dynamic-accumulator]] + mulch producer + bee attractant +
  medicinal
- **Elderberry:** Edible berries + wildlife habitat + insectary +
  medicinal + ornamental
- **Black locust:** Nitrogen fixer + timber + fence posts + bee forage
  + windbreak

## Climate Zones and Plant Palettes

### Temperate Maritime (USDA 8-9, UK Equivalent)

Mild winters and moderate rainfall support a wide plant palette.
Prioritise evergreen perennials and winter-hardy annuals:

- **Fruit:** Apple, pear, plum, fig, persimmon, kiwi, citrus (in
  sheltered spots)
- **Nuts:** Chestnut, hazelnut, walnut
- **Biomass:** Comfrey, cardoon, bamboo, artichoke
- **Nitrogen fixers:** Alder, seaberry, autumn olive, tagasaste
- **Ground cover:** Clover, creeping thyme, oregano, strawberry

### Continental Cold (USDA 3-5)

Short growing seasons and extreme cold require hardy selections with
fast maturity:

- **Fruit:** Apple, plum, cherry, currant, gooseberry, haskap,
  saskatoon
- **Nuts:** Hazelnut, butternut, heartnut (with protection)
- **Biomass:** Comfrey, Jerusalem artichoke, nettle
- **Nitrogen fixers:** Caragana, Russian olive, alder, honey locust
- **Ground cover:** Clover, crown vetch, bearberry, wintergreen
