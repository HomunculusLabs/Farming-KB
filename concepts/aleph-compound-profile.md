# ALEPH (DOT) — 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Methylthioamphetamine

## Overview

ALEPH (PIHKAL #3), also designated DOT (para-DOT), holds a special place in psychedelic chemistry history as "probably the first sulfur-containing phenethylamine to have been evaluated as a potentially active CNS stimulant or psychedelic." The introduction of sulfur (as a methylthio group) into the phenethylamine framework was a completely novel concept at the time, and Shulgin's extreme caution during initial testing — starting at 0.25 micrograms — reflects both the unknown territory and the anxiety of discovery.

ALEPH pioneered the exploration of sulfur-containing psychedelics, which eventually led to the [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] and the ALEPH series of higher homologues. Its name, drawn from the first letter of the Hebrew alphabet, was chosen because Shulgin believed these compounds "might have extraordinary properties in common" — reflecting both his ambition for the series and his characteristic blend of scientific rigor and symbolic naming. See also [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]].

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical name:** 2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylthioamphetamine
- **PIHKAL number:** #3
- **Code names:** ALEPH, DOT (para-DOT)
- **Class:** Substituted amphetamine (thioether series)
- **HCl melting point:** 204-205 °C (recrystallized from IPA)

## Dosage and Duration

| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| **Threshold** | ~3 mg |
| **Common dose** | 5-10 mg |
| **Strong dose** | 10+ mg |
| **Duration** | 6-8 hours |
| **Onset** | 30-60 minutes |

For Shulgin's general approach to establishing dosage ranges, see [[shulgin-dosage-guidelines]].

## Effects

- At 5 mg: physical warmth spreading over the body, intense intellectual stimulation, rich afterglow with empathy — yet the experience is "almost entirely non-verbal"
- At 10 mg: rapid shift of frame of reference, mechanical tasks become "quite alien," interaction with people on telephone is fine but physical tasks (arranging flowers, alphabetizing) become impossible
- Distinctly intellectual/cognitive character rather than purely visual
- The sulfur atom imparts a unique quality not found in oxygen or carbon-substituted analogues

## Testing History

ALEPH was first tested on September 3, 1975, at the extraordinary starting dose of 0.25 micrograms. A total of 18 separate trials were conducted to gradually work up to a 1 mg threshold. Shulgin described the extreme precaution as partly due to "the uniqueness of a new heteroatom in a phenethylamine system" and partly due to a strange manic excitement experienced during laboratory isolation of the compound — later recognized as entirely placebo response.

This anecdote illustrates both the careful methodology and the human element of Shulgin's research program. The initial "strange manic excitement" during isolation, though purely psychosomatic, led Shulgin to adopt a degree of caution that he himself acknowledged was excessive. It was only after 18 incremental trials that sufficient confidence was established to explore active dose ranges. This methodology is described more fully in [[pihkal-introduction-and-methodology]].

## Experience Reports (from PIHKAL)

### At 5 mg

> The initial hints of action were physical — warming of first the legs, and then a comfortable warmth spread over the entire body. Intense intellectual stimulation...14 pages of handwritten notes...almost entirely non-verbal. The afterglow was benign and rich in empathy.

The production of 14 pages of notes, described as "almost entirely non-verbal," suggests that ALEPH's cognitive effects operate in a mode distinct from ordinary analytical thought — perhaps closer to a form of non-linguistic ideation or pattern recognition. The benign, empathic afterglow contrasts with the intense intellectual stimulation of the peak.

### At 7 mg

> Uneventful +1.

A minimal response, suggesting that the threshold between 5 mg and 10 mg is quite steep for this compound.

### At 10 mg

> There was a rapid shift of frame of reference that made simple tasks such as reading and tuning the radio quite alien...mechanical things, such as arranging flowers or alphabetizing names, are beyond me. Driving would be impossible.

The disruption of mechanical and routine cognitive tasks at 10 mg is notable. The fact that telephone conversation remained coherent while physical-world tasks became impossible suggests a dissociation between social cognition and procedural motor planning — an effect profile not commonly emphasized in other psychedelic compounds.

### Independent Assay at 10 mg

A colleague independently assayed ALEPH at 10 mg and reported "only mild effects which he found relatively uninteresting." This inter-individual variability is common among psychedelic phenethylamines and underscores the importance of set and setting. See [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] for pharmacological factors underlying individual differences.

## The ALEPH Series

ALEPH itself is the methylthio member of a broader series that includes higher homologues with varying alkylthio chain lengths:

- **ALEPH** (methylthio) — the original compound, PIHKAL #3
- **ALEPH-2** (ethylthio) — the ethyl homologue
- **ALEPH-4** (isopropylthio) — the isopropyl homologue
- **ALEPH-6** (phenylthio) — the phenyl homologue, introducing an aromatic sulfur substituent
- **ALEPH-7** (propylthio) — the n-propyl homologue

Shulgin noted that "three higher homologues have been more thoroughly looked at," though the ALEPH series as a whole remains less extensively characterized than its 2-carbon counterparts. The positional isomers of ALEPH are catalogued as ORTHO-DOT and META-DOT in the PIHKAL naming system.

## Synthesis (from PIHKAL)

Synthesized from 2,5-dimethoxy-4-(methylthio)benzaldehyde (prepared via the 2C-T synthesis route) through condensation with nitroethane and LAH reduction. The methylthio group is introduced early in the synthesis through reaction of 2,5-dimethoxythiophenol with methyl iodide.

## Pharmacology

The methylthio (S-CH3) group at the 4-position introduces sulfur's unique electronic properties — larger atomic radius, lower electronegativity, and different polarizability compared to oxygen. These properties affect both receptor binding and blood-brain barrier penetration. ALEPH is a 5-HT2A agonist. See [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] and [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]].

## Legacy

ALEPH's most enduring legacy lies not in the compound itself but in what it opened the door to. As Shulgin reflected:

> ALEPH remains relatively unexplored. Its two positional isomers are entered as ORTHO-DOT and META-DOT. Three higher homologues have been more thoroughly looked at. The real treasure came in the exploring of the 2-carbon homologues, the compounds that make up the 2C-T family.

The 2C-T family — including [[2c-t-7-compound-profile]] and [[2c-t-2-compound-profile]] — ultimately proved far more rewarding than the ALEPH amphetamine series. The two-carbon chain (phenethylamine rather than amphetamine) generally produces effects with a more favorable balance of visual richness, emotional warmth, and physical comfort. The ALEPH series thus represents an important stepping stone in the development of sulfur-substituted psychedelics, even if the compounds themselves were somewhat eclipsed by their phenethylamine descendants.

## Harm Reduction

- Moderate potency — accurate milligram scales required
- Shorter duration (6-8 hours) than the DOx compounds
- Distinctly cognitive/intellectual character — may be challenging for those expecting primarily visual effects
- Standard psychedelic harm reduction applies
