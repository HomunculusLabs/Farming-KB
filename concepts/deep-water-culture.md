# Deep Water Culture (DWC) for Cannabis

Deep Water Culture (DWC) is a hydroponic growing method in which plant roots are submerged directly in an oxygenated nutrient solution. It is one of the simplest and most productive active hydroponic systems available for cannabis cultivation. DWC systems are classified as active recovery hydroponic systems because the nutrient solution is constantly recirculated and aerated.

## How DWC Works

In a typical DWC system, seedlings or clones are held in net pots filled with expanded clay pellets, [[rockwool-cannabis]], or another inert Cannabis Growing Mediums. The net pots sit snugly in holes cut into a lid that covers the reservoir. Plant roots dangle down from the net pots into the nutrient solution below.

A submersible pump lifts nutrient solution to the top of a discharge tube, where it splashes into the access lid. The cascading action wets roots and splashes back into the reservoir, increasing dissolved oxygen levels. Many DWC gardens also incorporate an air stone connected to an external air pump, which continuously bubbles fresh air into the reservoir to supply additional oxygen to the root zone.

## Advantages of DWC

- **Simplicity**: DWC systems are simple by design, with no complex plumbing or multiple irrigation lines to manage.
- **Low maintenance**: No timer is required because the pumps run 24 hours a day. This makes the system perfect for casual gardeners and hydroponic enthusiasts alike.
- **Rapid growth**: The constant availability of oxygenated nutrient solution allows roots to absorb nutrients and water as fast as the plant can use them, leading to accelerated growth rates.
- **Productivity**: DWC systems are highly productive and capable of producing heavy yields of dense flower buds.
- **Fewer components**: Compared to [[ebb-and-flow]] or [[nutrient-film-technique]] systems, DWC has fewer moving parts and less that can go wrong.

## Components of a DWC System

1. **Reservoir**: A container that holds the nutrient solution. For outdoor gardens, an overflow drainage hole should be cut in the side to prevent rainwater from causing overflow.
2. **Lid with net pot holes**: A cover that supports plants and blocks light from reaching the nutrient solution, preventing algae growth.
3. **Net pots**: Containers filled with growing medium that hold plants in place while allowing roots to pass through into the solution.
4. **Air pump and air stone**: Provides continuous aeration to maintain dissolved oxygen levels in the nutrient solution.
5. **Submersible pump**: Circulates and oxygenates the nutrient solution through splashing action.
6. **Nutrient solution**: A complete hydroponic fertilizer diluted in water, maintained at the proper [[cannabis-nutrient-lockout-and-ph]] and EC levels.

## Nutrient Solution Management

The pH in DWC systems should be maintained between 5.5 and 6.5, with 5.8 to 6.0 being ideal. Because roots are constantly submerged in the nutrient solution, pH and EC fluctuations can affect plants more rapidly than in systems where roots have a growing medium buffer. Regular monitoring of pH, EC/TDS, and reservoir water level is essential.

The reservoir temperature should be kept in a range that supports healthy root activity. Warmer water holds less dissolved oxygen, so reservoirs should be kept cool, typically between 65 and 70 degrees F (18 to 21 degrees C). An aquarium heater can be used if temperatures drop too low.

## Growing Medium Selection

DWC systems can use a variety of growing media in the net pots:
- expanded clay pellets (hydroton) are the most popular choice due to excellent drainage and reusability. They drain well, retain nutrient solution while holding lots of oxygen, and can be reused for multiple grows.
- [[rockwool-cannabis]] cubes or plugs provide good initial support for seedlings and clones. Pre-condition rockwool before use to stabilize its naturally high pH.
- [[coco-coir-cannabis]] can also be used but may retain more moisture than ideal for DWC applications.
- A combination of rockwool starter cube surrounded by expanded clay pellets in the net pot is a common and effective approach.

## Cannabis Cultivation in DWC

DWC is well-suited for all stages of cannabis growth. Clones and seedlings can be started in net pots and transplanted directly into the system once roots reach the nutrient solution. The system supports both vegetative growth and flowering effectively.

Small flowering plants grow well in DWC, and the system is also suitable for larger plants given adequate reservoir volume. DWC systems pair well with [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] training techniques such as [[cannabis-propagation-techniques]] and can produce harvest-ready plants efficiently. For Sea of Green gardens, multiple small DWC buckets can be arranged under a single lighting system.

## Maintenance Tips

- Check that the reservoir has adequate solution levels daily and replenish as needed.
- Monitor pH and EC at least every one to two days, adjusting as necessary.
- Clean air stones regularly to prevent clogging; replace them when bubbling becomes uneven.
- Change the nutrient solution completely every one to two weeks to prevent salt buildup and maintain nutrient balance.
- Ensure the air pump is running at all times to prevent root suffocation.
- Inspect roots periodically for signs of [[cannabis-root-rot-prevention-and-treatment]], which can occur if oxygen levels drop too low.
- Keep the reservoir covered and light-tight to prevent algae growth.
- Check net pots to ensure roots are not blocking drainage or overflow.

## DWC Compared to Other Systems

DWC is simpler and requires less equipment than [[nutrient-film-technique]] (NFT) or banisteriopsis caapi. Unlike [[ebb-and-flow]] systems, DWC has no flood and drain cycles to time and manage. However, DWC shares with banisteriopsis caapi the characteristic of having roots directly exposed to the nutrient solution, making both systems sensitive to pump or air pump failures.

DWC differs from [[bubbleponics]] in that standard DWC relies solely on root submersion and aeration, while bubbleponics adds a top-feed drip component for faster early growth. DWC also contrasts with [[wick-system-cannabis]] systems, which are passive and rely on capillary action rather than active pumps.

## Suitability for Different Cannabis Stages

DWC is effective across all stages of cannabis cultivation:

- **Seedling stage**: [[cannabis-seedling-care-guide]] started in rockwool cubes or small net pots can be placed in DWC once roots show through the bottom. Start with a reduced-strength nutrient solution at lower EC.
- **Vegetative stage**: DWC excels during the [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]], producing rapid growth rates. Plants can be trained with low-stress techniques while the root system develops in the reservoir.
- **Flowering stage**: Heavy flowering plants can be supported if the reservoir is large enough. Monitor nutrient demand closely, as flowering cannabis has high phosphorus and potassium requirements.
- **Mother plants**: Large DWC reservoirs can support mother plants for extended clone production. The continuous nutrient availability supports heavy growth and frequent [[cannabis-clone-rooting-and-transplanting]].

## Reservoir Sizing

Larger reservoirs provide more stable conditions. A minimum of two to five gallons per plant is recommended. Larger reservoirs dilute the effects of plant water uptake on nutrient concentration and pH, reducing the frequency of adjustments. For outdoor DWC gardens, an overflow drainage hole prevents rainwater from causing the reservoir to overflow.

## Common Problems

- **Pump failure**: If the air pump fails, dissolved oxygen drops rapidly and roots can suffocate within hours. A backup air pump is recommended.
- **Algae growth**: Light entering the reservoir promotes algae. Keep the reservoir covered and light-tight at all times.
- **Root rot**: Caused by insufficient oxygenation or high reservoir temperatures. Maintain proper aeration and keep reservoir temperatures between 65 and 70 degrees F (18 to 21 degrees C). Healthy roots should be white and crisp; brown, slimy roots indicate [[cannabis-root-rot-prevention-and-treatment]].
- **Nutrient imbalances**: Rapid pH and EC shifts can occur because there is no growing medium to buffer changes. Monitor frequently and adjust as needed.
- **Temperature fluctuations**: Reservoir temperatures can swing with ambient conditions. Use a reservoir heater or chiller to maintain stable temperatures.

## Troubleshooting Guide

| Symptom | Possible Cause | Solution |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Slow growth | Low EC or low oxygen | Increase nutrient strength; check air pump |
| Yellowing leaves | Nutrient deficiency or pH imbalance | Check pH and adjust; add missing nutrients |
| Brown, slimy roots | Root rot from low oxygen | Increase aeration; add hydrogen peroxide; lower temperature |
| Algae in reservoir | Light exposure | Cover reservoir completely; block all light entry |
| Wilting despite full reservoir | pH lockout or root disease | Check pH; inspect roots for disease |
