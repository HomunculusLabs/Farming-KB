# Aquarium-Based Humidity Chamber for Mushroom Fruiting

In the Boil-a-Bag [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] method described by D.H. Jarrold, the humidity chamber
is the environment where fully colonized substrate bags are transitioned from the mycelial
growth phase to the fruiting (mushroom production) phase. Jarrold's design uses a standard
glass aquarium as the foundation of a simple, effective fruiting chamber that can be
constructed from readily available materials at minimal cost.

## The Fruiting Environment

Mushroom fruiting requires specific environmental conditions that differ significantly from
those needed for [[mycelial-colonization]]. During colonization, the priority is maintaining
sterility and providing a stable temperature for mycelial growth. Once the substrate is fully
colonized, the mycelium needs a shift in environmental cues to trigger the formation of
mushroom primordia (pins) and subsequent fruit body development. These cues include:

- **High humidity**: Mushrooms are approximately 90% water and require a consistently humid
environment to prevent drying of the developing fruit bodies. Relative humidity should be
maintained above 85%, with 90-95% being optimal for most species.

- **Fresh air exchange**: Mushrooms produce carbon dioxide during growth, and elevated CO2
levels inhibit proper fruit body formation. Regular introduction of fresh air is essential
for healthy development and prevents the formation of abnormal, elongated stems.

- **Light**: While mushrooms do not photosynthesize, most species require some ambient light
to trigger fruiting and to guide the direction of growth (mushrooms grow toward light).

- **Temperature**: Fruiting typically occurs at slightly lower temperatures than colonization,
though the optimal range varies by species.

## Basic Aquarium Chamber Design

Jarrold's primary [[mushroom-humidity-chamber-design]] is built around a standard glass aquarium measuring
approximately 4 feet long by 2 feet wide (approximately 120cm × 60cm). This size provides
ample space for multiple substrate bags to fruit simultaneously. The construction is
straightforward:

- **Base layer**: A layer of pre-soaked hydroponic clay pellets (hydro pellets) is placed at
the bottom of the aquarium. These expanded clay pellets serve as a humidity reservoir — their
porous structure absorbs and retains water, gradually releasing moisture into the enclosed air
space through evaporation. The pellets should be thoroughly soaked before placement and
maintained with regular watering to ensure continuous humidity output.

- **Sealing cover**: A layer of plastic sheeting is placed over the top of the aquarium to
create a seal. This serves two critical functions: it traps humidity inside the chamber by
preventing moist air from escaping, and it prevents flying insects from entering the chamber
and potentially contaminating the fruiting mushrooms. The plastic should be large enough to
fully cover the aquarium opening with some overlap on the sides.

- **Air exchange**: The sealed environment requires manual fresh air exchange. Jarrold
recommends fanning fresh air into the tank twice daily by briefly lifting the plastic cover
and waving air through the chamber with a piece of cardboard or similar object. This twice-daily
air exchange is sufficient to maintain adequate oxygen levels and prevent CO2 buildup.

## Transitioning Colonized Bags to Fruiting

When the mycelium has fully colonized the substrate bag — visible as a complete white
covering throughout the bag contents — the filter can is removed from the bag neck and the bag
is prepared for introduction to the humidity chamber. The transition process involves:

- **Opening the bag**: The bag is cut down the sides to expose the colonized substrate to the
humid [[oyster-mushroom-fruiting-chamber-environment]]. Jarrold recommends leaving at least 2cm of the bottom of the bag
intact, which allows the bag to stand upright as a base and prevents the substrate from
sitting directly in the hydro pellets at the bottom of the tank.

- **Placement**: The opened bags are stood upright in the humidity chamber on top of the
hydro pellet layer. The exposed substrate surface is now the fruiting zone where mushrooms
will form.

- **Monitoring**: Over the following days, the cultivator should watch for signs of mushroom
[[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]] — small white dots appearing on the substrate surface that will develop
into mushroom pins. Once pins are observed, the fruiting process is underway.
