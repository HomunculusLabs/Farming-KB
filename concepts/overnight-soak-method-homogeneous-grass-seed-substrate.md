### Sources of Moisture Variability

The guide specifically notes that "different varieties of grass seed and even batches of the same variety can differ greatly in their ability to absorb water." This variability arises from:

- **Seed coat thickness** — thicker seed coats absorb water more slowly and may retain more internal dryness after standard hydration
- **Seed size variation** — larger kernels absorb proportionally less water per unit mass than smaller kernels within the same batch
- **Processing history** — seed that has been heat-treated, polished, or aged may have altered water absorption characteristics
- **[[solomon-seed-quality-and-germination-testing]] before loading into jars; moldy or damaged seeds can be removed

### Potential Disadvantages

The soak method also has some potential drawbacks that the guide does not explicitly address:

- **Longer preparation time** — the overnight soak adds 8–12 hours to the preparation timeline, requiring advance planning
- **Less precise water control** — the total water content of the substrate is less precisely controlled compared to measuring 180 ml per jar, as it depends on the absorption characteristics of each seed batch
- **Contamination risk during soaking** — the soaking container and water source must be clean to avoid introducing contaminants during the extended hydration period
- **No specified water-to-seed ratio for soaking** — the guide does not specify how much water to use for soaking, leaving the cultivator to determine this empirically

## Water Content Calibration

The guide explicitly recommends experimentation: "You should experiment a little with these" (water-to-seed ratios). This advice applies equally to both the direct addition and soak methods, as the optimal moisture content depends on the specific seed characteristics of each batch.

### Practical Calibration Approach

Cultivators can calibrate moisture content through:

- **Visual inspection** — properly hydrated grain should be plump but not swollen to the point of splitting; surface moisture should be minimal
- **Squeeze test** — a handful of hydrated grain squeezed firmly should produce a few drops of water but not a stream
- **Shake test** — grain should shake loose within the jar after sterilization; grain that clumps together is too wet
- **Test jars** — running a small number of test jars with different hydration levels before committing to a full batch allows optimization

## Source of Grass Seed

The guide recommends purchasing grass seed from "an animal feed store where it is sold as birdfeeding," noting the importance of obtaining inexpensive, fungicide-free seed. This practical sourcing advice is relevant to the soak method because:

- **Feed store seed** — often sold in bulk at lower prices than garden center seed, making the cost of experimentation (and potential failure) more manageable
- **Fungicide check** — seed treated with fungicides must be identified and avoided; the guide notes that [[fungicide-treated-seed-detection-and-avoidance]] "should say so on the packaging"
- **Species variety** — feed store seed may be a single species (*Lolium perenne* ryegrass) or a mixture; both have been used successfully according to the guide
