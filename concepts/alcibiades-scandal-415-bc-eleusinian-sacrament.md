## The Eleusinian Mysteries

The Eleusinian Mysteries were the most sacred religious rites of ancient
Greece, celebrated annually at Eleusis near Athens for nearly two
thousand years.
Initiates drank a potion called the kykeon and witnessed something
utterly unexpected and transformative that remained with each
participant as a powerful memory for the rest of their life.
The Greek poet Pindar captured the profundity of the experience:
"Happy is he who, having seen these rites, goes below the hollow earth;
for he knows the end of life and he knows its god-sent beginning."

The secrecy surrounding the Mysteries was absolute.
Initiates were bound by oath never to reveal what they had seen or
consumed, and the penalty for violation was death.
This secrecy was maintained successfully for nearly two millennia, from
approximately 1500 BC until the destruction of the Eleusinian temple
by Christian forces in the fourth century AD.
