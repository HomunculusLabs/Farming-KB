## Ayahuasca: Vine of the Dead

The word ayahuasca is Quechua, translating roughly as "vine of the dead" or
"vine of souls." This term refers both to the prepared hallucinogenic beverage
and to Banisteriopsis caapi, the woody liana rich in beta-carboline
alkaloids. The most important of these, harmine, is an indole that is not
overtly psychedelic in normal amounts but is an effective short-acting
monoamine oxidase inhibitor.

Native Amazonian peoples have brilliantly exploited this pharmacological
mechanism. By combining DMT-containing plants with Banisteriopsis in the
ayahuasca brew, they have long used MAO inhibition to render oral DMT
highly psychoactive -- a mechanism not described by Western science until the
1950s. In the presence of harmine, DMT enters the bloodstream, crosses the
blood-brain barrier, and competes with serotonin for synaptic bonding sites.
The slow release produces a four-to-six-hour visionary experience.
