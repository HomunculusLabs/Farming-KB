## Historical Background

### Gruen (1963) — Endogenous Growth Regulation

H.E. Gruen demonstrated endogenous growth regulation in carpophores of
*[[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] is controlled
by internally produced growth factors. This work established that mushroom
growth is not simply a response to external stimuli but is modulated by
hormone-like substances produced within the fruiting body itself.

### Hagimoto (1963) — The Mushroom Growth Hormone

H. Hagimoto coined the term "mushroom growth hormone" and demonstrated its
connection to geotropic responses in *Agaricus bisporus*. Decapitation
experiments (removal of the pileus) showed that the stipe lost its
gravitropic sensitivity after the cap was removed, suggesting the hormone
originates in or near the pileus.

### Hagimoto and Konishi (1960) — Gill Diffusate Activity

Hagimoto and Konishi showed that agar diffusates from gills could partially
restore pileus influence in decapitated fruiting bodies, strongly suggesting
the active substance is produced in the lamellae and diffuses downward to
regulate stipe growth.

## The Decapitation Experimental Paradigm

Decapitation involves removing the pileus (cap) from a developing
basidiocarp and observing the effect on stipe growth and tropic responses:

**Normal (intact) basidiocarp**: Stipe exhibits phototropic and geotropic
responses; negative geotropism produces upright growth and horizontal
pileus orientation at maturity.

**Decapitated basidiocarp**: Stipe continues elongating but loses
directional control; [[adaptive-significance-mushroom-tropisms-spore-dispersal]].
As with IAA, these compounds appear to be present without functional
role in tropic regulation.
