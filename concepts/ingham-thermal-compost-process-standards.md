## Thermal Composting — Process Standards and Microbial Biology

## Overview

Thermal composting uses the heat generated by microbial metabolism to create conditions that kill pathogens, weed seeds, and unwanted organisms while preserving beneficial microbial diversity. ## Moisture Requirements

Optimal moisture content for thermal composting: **45-75%**

- Below 45%: Microbial activity slows dramatically; decomposition stalls
- Above 75%: Anaerobic conditions develop; beneficial organisms are lost; foul odors result
- Ideal range: 50-60% for most feedstock combinations

The squeeze test provides a practical field assessment: compost should feel damp but not drip water when a handful is squeezed firmly.

## Oxygen Requirements

Maintaining aerobic conditions is critical throughout the composting process:

- **Minimum dissolved O₂**: 5.5 ppm
- **Daily CO₂ or O₂ monitoring** should be recorded
- Pleasant smell indicates aerobic conditions; foul odors signal anaerobic zones
- [[ingham-anaerobic-organisms-role-soil]] produce toxic metabolites (alcohols, phenols, organic acids) that can be phytotoxic and harm beneficial [[ingham-aact-beauveria-fruit-fly-beneficial-bacteria-inoculant]] and fungi include:

1. **Adequate oxygen**: 5.5+ ppm dissolved gases throughout the cycle
2. **Proper moisture**: 45-75% throughout the process
3. **Appropriate temperature**: 55-70°C for pathogen kill, but not exceeding 70°C to preserve beneficials
4. **Pleasant smell**: Indicates aerobic conditions are maintained
5. **Required organism set**: The compost must contain the specific organisms needed to compete with, inhibit, and consume target diseases or pests

## Turning Strategy

Turning serves multiple purposes: redistributes oxygen throughout the pile, equalizes temperature by moving hot exterior material to the cooler center, breaks up anaerobic zones, and mixes materials to ensure all feedstock reaches proper temperatures. Turn compost every time the temperature approaches 68-70°C to prevent overheating and maintain aerobic conditions.
