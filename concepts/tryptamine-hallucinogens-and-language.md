# Tryptamine Hallucinogens and Language

## Overview

A distinctive feature of tryptamine hallucinogens — particularly DMT and
psilocybin — is their profound effect on language processing. Terence
McKenna argued that these compounds "work directly on the language
centers" of the brain, producing phenomena including translinguistic
visual information, glossolalia (speaking in tongues), and encounters
with entities that communicate through an alien language of meaning.
This linguistic dimension sets tryptamines apart from other
hallucinogenic classes.

## The Translinguistic Object

The core linguistic phenomenon of the [[dmt-black-hole-effect-and-ineffability-of-tryptamine-experience]] is what
McKenna calls the "translinguistic object" — information that is:

- Experienced as simultaneously visual and linguistic
- "Conveyed fourth-dimensionally, coded as light and as evolving
  surfaces"
- Meaning-toned and feeling-toned in three dimensions
- "A language of alien meaning that is conveying alien information
  that cannot be Englished"

When humans encounter a translinguistic object, [[mckenna-cognitive-dissonance-translinguistic-object]]
arises in the "hindbrain" — we instinctively attempt to pour language
over it, but "it sheds it like water off a duck's back." This creates a
cycle of failed comprehension that generates wonder, astonishment, and
"awe at the brink of terror."

## Glossolalia Under Tryptamines

### Distinct from Classical Glossolalia

McKenna distinguishes tryptamine-induced glossolalia from the
classical religious phenomenon:

- **Classical glossolalia**: Studied in South American churches where
  practitioners produce pools of saliva and afterward ask "Did I do
  it?" — suggesting lack of conscious awareness during the act
- **[[mckenna-tryptamine-glossolalia-and-primal-language]]**: "Simply a brain state that allows the
  expression of the assembly language that lies behind language"
  — a fully conscious, intentional engagement with a primal language

### The Primal Language (Ursprache)

The language accessed under tryptamines is described as:

- An "assembly language" underlying all human languages
- A primal "Ursprache" comparable to that described in the Zohar
  (Kabbalah)
- Related to the primal language discussed by Robert Graves in
  *The White Goddess*
- Something that "passes without any quantized moment of distinction"
  — flowing seamlessly from things heard to things beheld

### Active vs. Passive Glossolalia

Unlike classical glossolalia (which appears to be an involuntary,
unconscious phenomenon), tryptamine glossolalia is active and
exploratory:

- One discovers the ability to "make the extra-dimensional objects —
  the feeling-toned, meaning-toned, three-dimensional rotating
  complexes of transforming light and color"
- This is a learned skill within the tryptamine state, not an
  automatic effect
- The entities encourage this practice: "Don't get a loop of wonder
  going that quenches your ability to understand. Try not to be so
  amazed. Try to focus."

## Language as Extension of Consciousness

McKenna proposes that tryptamine experiences reveal language as something
more fundamental than a communication tool:

- Language may be "the shadow of" a deeper cognitive ability revealed
  by tryptamines, or "a further extension of language" may be possible
- "Perhaps a human language is possible in which the intent of meaning
  is actually beheld in three-dimensional space"
- "Given ten thousand years and high cultural involvement in such a
  talent, does anyone doubt that it could become a cultural
  convenience in the same way that mathematics or language has become
  a cultural convenience?"

## The Noosphere and Self-Replicating Information

McKenna extends his linguistic analysis to the nature of language itself:

- "What is loose on this planet is language, self-replicating
  information systems that reflect functions of DNA: learning,
  coding, templating, recording, testing, re-testing, re-coding
  against DNA functions"
- Language may be "a quality of an entirely different order" — not
  merely a tool but an autonomous, evolving system
- Language is "in us monkeys now and moving through us and moving
  out of our hands and into the noosphere with which we have
  surrounded ourselves"

This framing anticipates later ideas about memes, [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]],
and information theory by several decades.

## Interior Dialogue and the I/Thou Relationship

The tryptamine experience is fundamentally dialogic. McKenna insists on
the importance of engaging the encountered intelligence rather than
passively receiving it:

- One must maintain an "I/Thou relationship" with the entities
- The proper stance is: "You say you are omniscient, omnipresent.
  You're long on talk, but what can you show me?"
- "There is no dignity in the universe unless we meet these things
  on our feet"
- Magicians "have always understood that one must go into such
  encounters with one's wits about oneself"

## Implications for Linguistics and Neuroscience

The tryptamine-language connection raises questions for multiple fields:

- **Linguistics**: Do all human languages share a deeper structural
  foundation that becomes accessible in [[harner-shamanic-journey-and-altered-states]]?
- **Neuroscience**: Which brain regions mediate the translinguistic
  phenomenon, and how do tryptamines alter their function?
- **Anthropology**: Do the linguistic phenomena reported across
  cultures (glossolalia, speaking in tongues, mystical utterance)
  share a common neurobiological basis?
- **Philosophy of mind**: Can the relationship between thought and
  language be inverted — does language sometimes determine thought
  rather than express it?
