## Tryptamine Hallucinogens and Language

## Overview

A distinctive feature of tryptamine hallucinogens — particularly DMT and
psilocybin — is their profound effect on language processing. Terence
McKenna argued that these compounds "work directly on the language
centers" of the brain, producing phenomena including translinguistic
visual information, glossolalia (speaking in tongues), and encounters
with entities that communicate through an alien language of meaning.
This linguistic dimension sets tryptamines apart from other
hallucinogenic classes.

## The Translinguistic Object

The core linguistic phenomenon of the [[mckenna-cognitive-dissonance-translinguistic-object]]
arises in the "hindbrain" — we instinctively attempt to pour language
over it, but "it sheds it like water off a duck"s back." This creates a
cycle of failed comprehension that generates wonder, astonishment, and
"awe at the brink of terror."

## Language as Extension of Consciousness

McKenna proposes that tryptamine experiences reveal language as something
more fundamental than a communication tool:

- Language may be "the shadow of" a deeper cognitive ability revealed
  by tryptamines, or "a further extension of language" may be possible
