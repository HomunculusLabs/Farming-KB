# God, Nature, and Man as One

In *The Road Back to Nature*, [[natural-farming-fukuoka]] devotes an entire chapter to his deepest philosophical and spiritual reflections on the relationship between God, nature, and humanity. This represents the most explicitly spiritual section of his later work.

## Do Not Name the Nameless God

Fukuoka begins with a paradox:

- God is fundamentally beyond naming, beyond conceptualization
- All religions that attempt to define God inevitably distort the reality they seek to describe
- "Do not name the nameless God" — the moment you name God, you have already lost sight of God
- This echoes the [[fukuoka-do-nothing-farming-philosophy-and-practice]] (nothingness/emptiness)

## There Is Only One God

Fukuoka asserts monotheistic unity, but not in a conventional religious sense:

- There is only one God, but this God is not separate from nature
- "God and Nature Are One" — they are not two things but one reality
- The separation of God from nature is the fundamental error of Western civilization
- This error leads to the exploitation of nature as a "resource" rather than reverence for it as sacred

## The Wandering God

Fukuoka describes God as "wandering" — present everywhere yet belonging nowhere:

- God cannot be found in any particular place, religion, or doctrine
- God is not "up there" or "out there" but everywhere and nowhere
- The search for God through religion or philosophy is ultimately misguided
- God is found not through seeking but through cessation of seeking

## God Knows Neither Space Nor Time

Fukuoka challenges conventional notions of divine transcendence:

- God exists outside the categories of space and time that humans use to organize experience
- Human conceptions of God are limited by these categories
- "Misconceptions over space and time" lead to fundamental misunderstandings about reality
- True understanding requires transcending these categories

## The Death of God

Fukuoka addresses Nietzsche's proclamation that "God is dead":

- In one sense, the anthropomorphic God of religion is indeed dead — and should be
- But the death of the religious God opens the possibility of encountering the real God
- The real God is not a being but the ground of all being
- "Nature Creates God" — God emerges from the living world, not the other way around

## Children, God, and Nature

Fukuoka sees children as naturally close to God and nature:

- Children have not yet learned the conceptual separation between self and world
- Their innocence is not ignorance but a form of wisdom
- "The Innocence of Children" represents the state of consciousness that natural farming seeks to recover
- Washing the naked body and returning to simplicity are paths back to this state

## A Humanity Forsaken by God

Fukuoka concludes with a warning:

- Humanity has not forsaken God; rather, humanity's actions have made it impossible to perceive God
- The destruction of nature is simultaneously the destruction of the divine
- "The Last Road Left for Humanity" is the path back to unity with nature and God
- This road is the path of [[fukuoka-road-do-nothing-farming]] — of ceasing to interfere
