following World War II, he planted the seeds of thirty legumes, crucifers,
and grasses throughout his orchard. From observations of these many
species, he came to the general conclusion that he should grow a weed cover
using ladino clover as the primary crop, with herbs such as alfalfa,
lupine, and bur clover as secondary crops. His reasons were specific and
practical.

First, ladino clover eliminates weeds effectively. Annual weeds are
displaced in one year, and biennials disappear in two years. After two to
three years, almost all garden weeds have vanished, leaving a solid field
of clover. Second, it improves soil down to a depth of sixteen to eighteen
inches through its root action and nitrogen-fixing activity. Third, seed
does not have to be sown again for another six to eight years, reducing
labor costs significantly. Fourth, it does not compete strongly with fruit
trees for fertilizer or moisture. Fifth, it grows back easily after being
cut and remains healthy and hardy even when trampled upon. Sixth, it does
not hinder farming operations such as harvesting and pruning.

## Seeding Technique and Timing

The seed of ladino clover should be drilled the first autumn. Delayed
seeding invites insect damage. Fukuoka specifically warns against covering
the seeds with soil, as this often hampers germination. Instead, the
farmer should merely firm the soil after drilling. If the clover seed is
broadcast in late autumn among the dying weeds and grasses on levees and
roadsides, clover growth gradually thickens. When the clover is sown
initially in the spring among the weeds, it should be cut back a year later
to stimulate growth. Ladino clover vine may additionally be planted in
