## Shipibo-Conibo People

The Shipibo-Conibo are an indigenous people of the Ucayali River basin in
the Peruvian Amazon. They are known for their distinctive geometric textile
designs (kené), their ceramic arts, and their rich shamanic tradition.
Historically, the Shipibo lived along the banks of the Ucayali and its
tributaries, maintaining a subsistence lifestyle based on fishing, hunting,
and small-scale agriculture.

Contact with Western missionaries, particularly Pentecostal, Adventist, and
Catholic groups, has led to many conversions in Shipibo communities. However,
the relationship between Christianity and traditional Shipibo shamanism is
complex—some converts publicly disavow shamanism while continuing to
privately consume ayahuasca and maintain native religious practices. This
dual identity reflects the deep cultural roots of ayahuasca in Shipibo life.

### The Purge

A key element of Shipibo ayahuasca practice is the purge—vomiting,
diarrhea, or emotional release that occurs during the ceremony. The purge
is understood not as a side effect but as a central therapeutic mechanism,
through which physical toxins, spiritual impurities, and emotional blockages
are expelled from the body.

## Shamanic Training and Lineage

Shipibo shamanic training traditionally involves years of dieting specific
plant teachers under the guidance of an experienced onanya. The diet
(dieta) requires isolation in the jungle, abstinence from certain foods and
social contact, and regular consumption of plant preparations. Through
this process, the apprentice develops relationships with plant spirits who
become allies in their healing work.

The training typically includes:

- **Plant dietas**: Extended periods consuming specific plants to develop
  healing capacities
- **Apprenticeship**: Learning icaros, diagnostic techniques, and
  ceremonial protocols from a master shaman
- **Visionary learning**: Direct instruction received during ayahuasca
  experiences about the nature of illness and healing
