# Ayahuasca Analogs and DMT-Containing Plants

Ayahuasca analogs (also called Anahuasca) are non-traditional combinations of plants that replicate the pharmacological action of traditional Amazonian [Ayahuasca. The concept was formalized by the chemist Jonathan Ott, who recognized that the psychoactive effects of Ayahuasca depend on two pharmacological components working in synergy: a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI) and a DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine) supplier. Any plant combination providing these two ingredients can produce similar visionary effects.

## Pharmacological Basis

The key insight behind Ayahuasca analogs is the enzyme inhibitor theory of Ayahuasca pharmacology. DMT is normally inactive when taken orally because monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes in the gut rapidly break it down before it can reach the bloodstream. The beta-carboline alkaloids in the Ayahuasca vine (Banisteriopsis caapi) — primarily harmine and harmaline — inhibit these enzymes, allowing DMT to pass through the digestive system intact and produce psychoactive effects.

Combinations made from isolated or synthesized ingredients (rather than whole plants) are called "pharmahuasca." Ott observed that psychonautic pharmahuasca research, though distant from the scientific mainstream, stands at the center of research on the biochemistry of consciousness and the genetics of pathological brain functions.

## Required Components

All formulas for Ayahuasca analogs must contain two essential elements:

**1. An MAO inhibitor (beta-carboline source):**

Traditional preparations primarily use Banisteriopsis caapi and Banisteriopsis spp., but the most widely used alternative is [Peganum harmala (Syrian Rue), which contains high concentrations of harmine and harmaline. Other natural MAO inhibitors include Tribulus terrestris (caltrop) and certain Passiflora species.

**2. A DMT supplier:**

The preferred traditional DMT source is [Psychotria viridis (chacruna), though numerous other botanical sources exist (see tables below).

## DMT-Containing Plants

The following plants have been documented as containing DMT or 5-MeO-DMT in pharmacologically significant concentrations:

### Grasses (Poaceae)

- **Arundo donax** — Rhizome contains DMT
- **Phalaris arundinacea** — Roots contain DMT; the Turkey Red variety is particularly rich
- **Phalaris tuberosa** (Italian strain) — Leaves contain DMT
- **Phragmites australis** — Rhizome contains DMT and 5-MeO-DMT

### Legumes (Fabaceae)

- **Acacia maidenii** — Bark contains 0.36% DMT; native to Australia, easily cultivated in temperate climates
- **Acacia phlebophylla** — Leaves contain 0.3% DMT; extremely rare, grows on only one mountain in Australia
- **Acacia simplicifolia** — Leaves and bark contain 0.81% DMT
- **Anadenanthera peregrina** — Bark contains DMT and 5-MeO-DMT
- **Anadenanthera colubrina** — Seeds primarily contain bufotenine; see [Cebil
- **Desmanthus illinoensis** — Root bark contains up to 0.34% DMT
- **Desmodium pulchellum** — Root bark contains DMT
- **Desmodium spp.** — Root bark contains DMT
- **Lespedeza capitata** — Contains DMT
- **Mimosa scabrella** — Bark contains DMT
- **Mimosa tenuiflora** (syn. M. hostilis) — Root bark contains 0.57-1% DMT; one of the most popular analog ingredients
- **Mucuna pruriens** — Seeds contain DMT and 5-MeO-DMT

### Other Families

- **Diplopterys cabrerana** (Malpighiaceae) — Leaves contain DMT and 5-MeO-DMT; traditional Ayahuasca additive
- **Virola sebifera** (Myristicaceae) — Bark contains DMT
- **Virola theiodora** (Myristicaceae) — Flowers contain 0.44% DMT; see [Virola
- **Virola spp.** (Myristicaceae) — Bark and resin contain DMT and 5-MeO-DMT
- **Psychotria poeppigiana** (Rubiaceae) — Leaves contain DMT
- **Psychotria viridis** (Rubiaceae) — Leaves contain DMT; the most common traditional additive
- **Dictyoloma incanescens** (Rutaceae) — Bark contains 0.04% 5-MeO-DMT

## Juremahuasca (Mimohuasca)

One of the most well-known Ayahuasca analogs is Juremahuasca, also called Mimohuasca, considered by experienced practitioners to be among the most psychoactive and easiest to tolerate preparations. Per person, the formula calls for:

- 3g Peganum harmala seeds, finely ground (MAOI source)
- 9g root bark of Mimosa tenuiflora (DMT source)
- Lemon or lime juice

The ground Syrian Rue seeds are soaked in water and swallowed or taken in a gelatin capsule. Fifteen minutes later, the boiled mixture of lemon or lime juice and Mimosa root bark is consumed. After 45 to 60 minutes — often preceded by brief nausea or vomiting — the visions begin, typically taking the form of fireworks, kaleidoscope-like designs, flashing colors, mandalas, or visionary journeys. The effects are reported as equal to traditional Amazonian Ayahuasca preparations.

## Cultural and Spiritual Context

The value of Ayahuasca analogs lies in the entheogenic effects that lead to deeper spiritual ecology and mystical insight. Ott described the experience as "shamanic ecstasy" — a true ancient religion of which modern churches are merely pale imitations. The ecstatic experience reveals the unity and integrity of the universe, and entheogens such as Ayahuasca are considered by some to be appropriate medicine for addressing the ecological and spiritual crises of modern hypermaterialistic culture.

Several syncretic churches in Brazil, including Santo Daime and Uniao do Vegetal, legally use Ayahuasca in their rituals. Their sacramental preparations follow traditional Indian recipes using Banisteriopsis caapi vine and Psychotria viridis leaves. These churches have expanded to Europe, with congregations in Germany and the Netherlands, where Ayahuasca's potential for treating addictions is also being studied.
