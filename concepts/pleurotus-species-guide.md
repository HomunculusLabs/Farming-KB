# Pleurotus Species Guide

## Introduction to Pleurotus

The genus *Pleurotus* (family Pleurotaceae, order Agaricales) comprises commercially important oyster mushrooms found worldwide. The type species is *P. ostreatus*. Approximately 70 species have been recorded globally as primary decomposers of hardwood trees.

Taxonomically, the genus exhibits a tetrapolar mating system with basidia bearing four basidiospores. Mycelia possess clamp connections and employ a mostly monomitic hyphal system. See [[mushroom-genetics-strain-selection]].

Eleven established intersterility groups distinguish *Pleurotus* species, based on the inability of isolates from different groups to produce fertile offspring.

## Intersterility Groups

Biological intersterility provides the framework for classifying *Pleurotus* species:

- **Group I**: *P. ostreatus* (syn. *P. columbinus*, *P. florida*, *P. salignus*, *P. spodoleucus*)

- **Group II**: *P. pulmonarius* (syn. *P. sajor-caju*, *P. sapidus*)

- **Group III**: *P. populinus*

- **Group IV**: *P. cornucopiae* (syn. *P. citrinopileatus*)

- **Group V**: *P. djamor* (syn. *P. flabellatus*, *P. ostreatoroseus*, *P. salmoneostramineus*, *P. euosmus*)

- **Group VI**: *P. eryngii* (syn. *P. ferulae*, *P. nebrodensis*, *P. hadamardii*, *P. fossulatus*)

- **Group VII**: *P. cystidiosus* (syn. *P. abalonus*)

- **Groups VIII–XI**: *P. calyptratus*, *P. dryinus*, *P. purpureo-olivaceus*, *P. tuber-regium*

*P. tuber-regium* uniquely forms sclerotia; see [[pleurotus-tuberregium-sclerotia-cultivation]].

## World Distribution

*P. ostreatus* occurs across all continents in temperate zones and is the second most cultivated mushroom worldwide after [[agaricus-bisporus-cultivation-biology]].

*P. pulmonarius* and *P. cystidiosus* inhabit tropical and subtropical regions.

*P. eryngii* is found in southern Europe, northern Africa, and central Asia, with major commercial cultivation in Korea and Japan.

*P. djamor* ranges across Asia, the Americas, Africa, and Australasia.

## Species Cultivation Parameters

### *P. ostreatus* (Tree Oyster)

Spawn 25°C; primordia 10–15°C; fruiting 10–17°C; CO₂ <1000 ppm. Autumn species requiring cold shock.

As of 2003, 66 commercial strains were registered in Korea. Cultivated by log, shelf, box, bottle, and bag methods (see [[oyster-mushroom-bag-cultivation]], [[oyster-mushroom-shelf-cultivation]]).

### *P. florida*

Spawn 25°C; primordia 10–25°C; fruiting 15–25°C; CO₂ <800 ppm.

Does not require cold shock. Has the widest fruiting temperature range and highest yield among *Pleurotus* species. Cap color is light brown at low temperatures, pale at higher temperatures.

Two subspecies groups exist — one compatible with *P. ostreatus*, one with *P. pulmonarius*. See [[mushroom-yield-optimization]].

### *P. eryngii* (King Oyster)

Spawn 25°C; primordia 10–15°C; fruiting 13–18°C; CO₂ <2000 ppm.

Wild in southern Europe, northern Africa, and central Asia. Known for unique flavor and firm texture. Cold shock required. More disease-prone and sensitive than *P. ostreatus*.

Cream to grey-brown cap with a thick whitish stipe (10–14 cm). Cultivated by bottle and bag methods using sawdust (see [[oyster-mushroom-bottle-cultivation]]).

### *P. sajor-caju* (Indian Oyster)

Spawn 25°C; primordia 10–25°C; fruiting 18–25°C; CO₂ 400–800 ppm.

Subtropical to tropical species formerly confused with *P. pulmonarius*.

### *P. cornucopiae*

Spawn 25–30°C; primordia 20–25°C; fruiting 20–30°C; CO₂ <1000 ppm.

Summer species with yellow cap (4–12 cm), white stipe, and characteristic wheat flour odor. Valued for good taste and color.

### *P. cystidiosus*

Spawn 25–35°C; primordia 20–25°C; fruiting 25–30°C; CO₂ <1000 ppm.

Summer species unique in producing conidia (asexual spores) on its mycelium. White coremia with black arthroconidia heads form on the colony surface — may appear contaminated but are harmless and diagnostic.

## Temperature Effects on Cap Color

Lower temperatures within the recommended range produce darker caps. Fruiting at 23°C yields light brown caps; at 13°C, caps are markedly darker. Cap color is a critical quality factor for market acceptance. See [[mushroom-environmental-parameters]] and [[monotub-cultivation]].

## CO₂ Effects on Morphology

Elevated CO₂ promotes elongated stipes with small, underdeveloped caps — undesirable for market. Low CO₂ produces short stipes with broad, well-developed caps. For *P. ostreatus*, CO₂ exceeding 1000 ppm yields inferior mushrooms; *P. eryngii* tolerates up to 2000 ppm.

## Breeding Considerations

Breeding programs evaluate morphological/physiological traits, cultivation suitability, yield potential, disease resistance, culinary value, shelf life, and processor demands. Methods include introduction of wild germplasm, mating and selection, protoplast fusion, and mutagenesis. See [[mushroom-genetics-strain-selection]] and [[stamets-pleurotus-oyster-cultivation]].
