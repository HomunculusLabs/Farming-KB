## Live Resin Processing

Live resin is a premium cannabis concentrate known for its exceptional terpene content and flavor profile. Unlike traditional extraction methods that use dried and cured flower, live resin is made from freshly harvested plant material that has been immediately frozen to preserve the living terpene and cannabinoid profile. This page covers the process from harvest through extraction.

## What Is Live Resin

Live resin is a type of butane hash oil (BHO) or propane hash oil (PHO) made from fresh frozen cannabis biomass. The term "live" refers to the fact that the plant material is frozen immediately after harvest — often within minutes — before any drying or curing occurs. This preserves volatile terpenes and other compounds that would otherwise degrade or evaporate during the traditional drying and curing process.

**Characteristics of quality live resin**:
- Sauce-like or budder consistency with visible terpene-rich liquid layer
- Intensely aromatic — the most fragrant cannabis concentrate
- Full-spectrum cannabinoid and terpene profile
- Preserves the "essence" of the living plant
- Typically more expensive than other concentrates

## Fresh Frozen Harvest

The live resin process begins at harvest — this is the most critical step for terpene preservation.

### Harvest Timing

Harvest slightly earlier than you would for traditional drying/curing — when trichomes are mostly cloudy with just a hint of amber. This captures peak terpene production before any degradation begins.

### Immediate Freezing

**Speed is everything**. Terpenes begin degrading the moment the plant is cut. The goal is to get plant material below freezing as fast as possible.

**Process**:
1. Harvest whole plants or large branches — do not trim or process.
2. Immediately place material into airtight, food-grade vacuum-sealed bags or containers.
3. Submerge in a cryogenic bath or place directly into a freezer set to -20°F (-29°C) or colder.
4. Some processors use dry ice or liquid nitrogen for ultra-rapid freezing.
5. Freeze for a minimum of 24 hours before extraction, though most processors freeze for weeks to months.

**Key principles**:
- Minimize time between cutting and freezing (ideally under 30 minutes)
- Prevent frost-freezer cycling (freeze-thaw cycles destroy trichome integrity)
- Store at consistently cold temperatures (-20°F or lower)
- Keep material sealed to prevent freezer burn and moisture loss

## Hydrocarbon Extraction

Live resin is typically produced using butane, propane, or a butane/propane blend as the solvent.

### Equipment

Professional live resin extraction requires significant equipment:

- **Closed-loop extraction system**: A sealed, pressurized system that recycles solvent. Never use open-blasting — it is extremely dangerous and produces inferior results.
- **Recovery pump**: Captures and recycles solvent back into the system.
- **Vacuum oven**: For post-extraction solvent purging.
- **Deep freezer or cryogenic freezer**: For biomass storage.
- **Personal protective equipment**: Safety glasses, solvent-resistant gloves, butane-rated ventilation, fire suppression.

### Safety Considerations

Hydrocarbon extraction with butane and propane is inherently hazardous:

- **Flammability**: Both butane and propane are highly flammable. Extraction must occur in a properly ventilated, spark-free environment.
- **Building codes**: Commercial extraction facilities must meet fire and safety codes (C1D1 or C1D2 hazardous occupancy classification in the US).
- **Training**: Operators must be trained in handling pressurized, flammable gases.
- **Residual solvents**: Improper purging leaves harmful solvent residues. State-regulated limits for residual butane are typically 500-1000 ppm.

This is not a DIY project. Improper hydrocarbon extraction has caused explosions, fires, and serious injuries.

### Extraction Process

1. **Loading**: Fresh frozen biomass is loaded into the extraction column without any thawing. Some processors pre-freeze the extraction column as well.
2. **Solvent wash**: Chilled butane, propane, or a blend is passed through the frozen biomass. The solvent dissolves cannabinoids and terpenes from the plant material. Temperature control during this step is critical — warmer washes extract more waxes and fats, while colder washes are more selective for terpenes and cannabinoids.
3. **Collection**: The solvent-cannabinoid-terpene solution drips into a collection vessel. The extreme cold from the fresh frozen material causes the solution to form a frosty, slushy consistency (hence the early live resin product was called "frost").
4. **Solvent recovery**: The collection vessel is warmed, and a vacuum pump recovers the solvent gas, pulling it back into the storage tank for reuse. What remains in the collection vessel is crude live resin.
5. **Winterization** (optional): For a more refined product, the crude extract may be winterized — dissolved in ethanol and placed in a freezer to precipitate waxes and lipids, which are then filtered out.
6. **Purging**: The crude extract is placed in a vacuum oven at controlled temperatures (85-115°F at -29 inHg vacuum) to remove any remaining solvent. This step takes 24-72+ hours depending on the volume and consistency desired.

## Terpene Preservation Strategies

The entire live resin process is designed around terpene preservation. Key strategies:

- **Cold extraction**: Running solvent at sub-zero temperatures minimizes terpene loss during the wash.
- **Short path processing**: Minimizing the distance and time the extract travels reduces terpene evaporation.
- **Low-temperature purging**: Vacuum ovens set to lower temperatures with deep vacuum remove solvents without volatilizing terpenes.
- **Terpene separation**: Some processors use [[fractional-distillation]] or chromatography to separate and recombine terpenes in optimized ratios.
- **Opaque containers**: Store final product in UV-blocking glass to prevent light degradation of terpenes.

## Product Forms

Live resin is finished into several forms:

- **Sauce**: A two-phase product with a crystalline THC-A layer (diamonds) floating in a terpene-rich liquid layer. The most sought-after form.
- **Badder/Budder**: Whipped into a creamy, malleable consistency.
- **Sugar**: A granular, crystalline texture with high terpene content.
- **Diamonds and sauce**: Large THCA crystals separated from the sauce fraction.
- **Live resin cartridges**: Thinned with terpenes for vape pen use.

## Quality Indicators

- **Aroma**: Should be intensely fragrant — if it smells weak, terpenes were lost during processing.
- **Appearance**: Translucent to opaque. Color ranges from light gold to amber. Dark brown or black indicates degradation or poor purging.
- **Consistency**: Properly purged live resin has a stable, non-runny consistency at room temperature.
- **Taste**: Clean, complex, true-to-plant flavor. Harsh or chemical taste indicates residual solvents or degradation.
