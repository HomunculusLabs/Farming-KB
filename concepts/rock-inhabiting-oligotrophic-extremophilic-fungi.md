# Rock-Inhabiting and Oligotrophic Fungi

Rocks exposed to high solar radiation, extreme temperatures, low nutrients, high electrolyte concentrations, and low humidity represent extreme environments colonized by unique species of stress-tolerant fungi. These organisms challenge our understanding of the limits of fungal life.

## Rock-Inhabiting Fungi (Microcolonial Fungi)

### Characteristics
- Black yeasts and dematiaceous mycelial fungi
- Nonlichenized (distinct from lichens)
- Grow on bare surfaces of diverse rock types in different climates
- Phylogenetically diverse but share convergent adaptations:
  - **High melanin production**
  - **Resistance to high temperatures and UV radiation**
  - **Restricted, meristematic growth morphology** (hence "microcolonial fungi")

### Habitats
- Rock surfaces and crevices
- Stone monuments and buildings
- Arid region rock surfaces (sometimes with "desert varnish")
- Various rock types and climatic conditions

### Desert Varnish
- Brown, black, or orange coating on rock surfaces in arid regions
- Rich in manganese and iron oxides and clay minerals
- Formed by activities of:
  - Lichenized epilithic and endolithic cyanobacteria
  - Chemoorganotrophic bacteria and fungi
- Fungi and bacteria produce extracellular slime protecting hyphae from desiccation
- Capable of enriching substrata with iron and manganese
- Dark pigment or dark layer is adaptation to reduce UV exposure

### Oligotrophic Growth
- Fungi can grow on carbon-free agar from soil particles
- Suggests soils contain sufficient nutrients/atmospheric carbon for slow growth
- Oligotrophic fungi likely prevalent in deep soils and low-carbon habitats
- Not detected by traditional nutrient-rich isolation methods
- Represent new frontiers for fungal ecology

## Halophilic and Xerophilic Fungi

### Xerophilic Fungi
- Adapted to low water activity (aw) environments
- Important in stored products and dried foods
- Many Aspergillus and Penicillium species are xerotolerant
- Some grow at aw as low as 0.70-0.75

### Halophilic Fungi
- **Aspergillus halophilicus** and **Scopulariopsis halophilica:** isolated from saline and arid-region soils
- Cellulose decomposition by some Aspergillus species increases at 3-10% salt
- No strictly halophilic fungi found along Dead Sea valley (only halotolerant)
- **Hortaea werneckii, Phaeotheca triangularis, Aureobasidium pullulans:** black yeastlike fungi from hypersaline saltpans (15-30% salinity)
  - Phaeotheca triangularis: obligate halophile
  - Others: facultatively halotolerant
- Typical marine fungi NOT detected in saltpan environments

## Thermotolerant and [[fungi-stressful-environments-thermophilic-xerophilic-halophilic]]

### Definitions
- **Thermotolerant:** Can grow at high temperatures but optimum is moderate
- **Thermophilic:** Require high temperatures for growth; optimum typically 40-50C

### Isolation
- Remove and subculture spores from plant material (Cooney and Emerson 1964)
- Incubation in moist chambers
- Selective washing and enrichment procedures
- Incubation at elevated temperatures (37-50C) selects against mesophiles

### Safety
- Fungi from high-temperature sites can be serious human pathogens
- Appropriate safety measures essential when working at/near 37C
- Tansey and Brock (1973): thermophilic fungi from hot springs as potential pathogens

### Ecological Roles
- Compost and self-heating organic matter
- Solar-heated soils
- Hot springs and geothermal areas
- Wood-chip piles and silage
- Birds' nests (maintained at 40C+ by body heat)

## Psychrophilic Fungi

### Adaptations
- Grow at temperatures below 5C
- Some fruit at near-freezing temperatures
- Important in Arctic, Antarctic, alpine, and cold-water environments
- Produce cold-adapted enzymes (ice avoidance, antifreeze proteins)

### Ecology
- Decompose organic matter in cold environments
- Associated with polar soils, snow, ice
- Some are plant pathogens of cold-climate crops

## Sampling Extreme Environment Fungi

### General Principles
- Most methods are modifications of existing practices:
  - Incubation at high/low temperatures
  - Modified media pH
  - Salt-amended media
  - Low-nutrient media
- Particle-washing technique preferred for community assessment
- Suspension plating selects only for spore-forming species — use alongside particle methods

### Challenges
- Traditional nutrient-rich media favor fast-growing generalists
- Extreme-environment specialists grow slowly and are overgrown
- May need extended incubation (weeks to months)
- Some taxa cannot be cultured at all
