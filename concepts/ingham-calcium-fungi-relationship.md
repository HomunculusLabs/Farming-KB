# Ingham — Calcium and Fungi Relationship

Elaine Ingham identifies a critical but often overlooked dependency between calcium availability in soil and the presence of functional [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]]. Simply adding calcium amendments (lime, gypsum, eggshells) without improving soil biology is ineffective because the calcium will leach rather than becoming plant-available.

## The Problem: Calcium Without Biology

There is little point in adding calcium to soil unless the fungi and bacteria are improved as well. Without adequate biology:
- Added calcium leaches through the soil profile
- It ends up at excessively high levels in drinking water
- Plants cannot access it despite its presence in soil
- The calcium-plant-available conversion system is broken

The calcium cycle in soil depends on organisms that hold, process, and release calcium in forms plants can use.

## The Biological Calcium Pathway

Calcium becomes plant-available through several biological mechanisms:
1. **[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]]** absorb calcium from soil solution and deliver it directly to plant roots in exchange for carbon
2. **Fungal hyphae** physically hold calcium on and within their cell walls
3. **Protozoa** release calcium when consuming calcium-containing bacteria
4. **Microarthropods** fragment calcium-rich [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]], making it accessible to decomposers
5. **Bacteria** solubilize calcium from mineral sources through [[staycare-fungal-organic-acid-production-metal-leaching]]

Without adequate levels of protozoa, nematodes, microarthropods, and/or mycorrhizal fungi, calcium may remain locked in fungal biomass instead of being transferred to the plant.

## Calcium and Weed Competition

Ingham specifically links calcium availability to competitive dynamics between desired plants and weeds like dandelions. The mechanism:
- Many weeds thrive in bacterial-dominated, low-calcium soils
- Improving fungal biomass shifts the soil toward conditions favoring desired plants
- Adequate calcium helps desired plants outcompete weed species
- The right fungal:bacterial ratio for the target crop is essential

The difference between lawns that can utilize calcium versus those without plant-available calcium forms is "quite noticeable."

## Steps to Restore Calcium Availability

1. **Improve soil biology first** — add bacteria, fungi, protozoa through compost or AACT
2. **Add increasing amounts of calcium** incrementally as biology improves
3. **Monitor plant tissue** to confirm calcium uptake is occurring
4. **Test soil biology** to verify the food web is cycling calcium properly

Simple calcium sources like eggshells can be effective when biology is functional.

## Calcium and Soil Structure

Calcium plays a structural role in soil aggregation:
- Calcium ions help flocculate clay particles, improving soil structure
- Fungal glues combined with calcium create stable aggregates
- Well-structured soil allows roots to access calcium throughout the profile
- Compacted, anaerobic soils disrupt both fungal activity and calcium cycling

## Calcium in Compost Tea

When applying AACT to calcium-deficient soils:
- The tea delivers the organisms needed to cycle calcium
- Fungal biomass in the tea is particularly important for calcium retention
- Compost and compost tea provide food resources for mycorrhizal fungi
- Repeated applications build the biology over time, making calcium amendments increasingly effective
