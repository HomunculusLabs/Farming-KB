## Cannabinoid Receptors CB1 and CB2

Cannabinoid receptors are cell membrane proteins that serve as the binding sites for endocannabinoids (produced by the body), phytocannabinoids (from cannabis), and [[cannabis-for-cachexia-and-appetite-stimulation]], which is why cannabis use typically increases hunger.

Because CB2 activation does not produce psychoactive effects, CB2-selective agonists may offer therapeutic benefits without cognitive side effects.

- **Balanced CB1/CB2 activation**: THC activates both receptors simultaneously, producing both neurological (CB1) and immune-modulating (CB2) effects. The ratio of CB1 to CB2 activation may contribute to the different effects of different cannabis strains and preparations.

## Receptor Dynamics and Adaptation

Cannabinoid receptors are not static — their density and sensitivity can change in response to various conditions:

- **Upregulation**: Receptor density can increase in response to chronic underactivation, potentially enhancing sensitivity to endocannabinoids
- **Downregulation**: Chronic THC exposure leads to reduced CB1 receptor density and signaling efficiency, which contributes to tolerance development
- **Disease-related changes**: Various diseases alter cannabinoid receptor expression patterns. For example, CB2 receptors are upregulated in areas of inflammation, and CB1 receptors show altered expression in neurodegenerative diseases

Understanding these dynamic changes is critical for developing effective cannabinoid-based therapies and for predicting how the ECS will respond to therapeutic intervention in different disease states.

## Additional Cannabinoid Receptors

Scientists suspect that at least three additional cannabinoid receptors exist beyond CB1 and CB2, currently referred to as non-CB1/non-CB2 receptors. These include:

- **GPR55**: An orphan G-protein-coupled receptor that responds to certain cannabinoids and may be involved in inflammation and pain
- **TRPV1 (Vanilloid receptor)**: Not a classical cannabinoid receptor but activated by anandamide and other endocannabinoids, linking the ECS to pain and temperature perception
- **GPR18 and GPR119**: Additional putative cannabinoid receptors with potential roles in immune function and metabolism

The full characterization of these additional receptors represents an active area of research that may expand our understanding of the ECS significantly.
