# Steroid-Induced Anatomical Changes in Psilocybe cubensis Fruiting Bodies

## Overview

The 1990 study by Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt documented a remarkable and
previously unreported [[annulus-loss-morphological-effect-brassinosteroid-treatment]] of [[suppressed-incomplete-fruit-body-formation-brassinosteroid-treatment]] on the
fruiting bodies of Psilocybe cubensis: the complete or near-complete loss of
the [[membranous-annulus-loss-brassinosteroid-induced-morphological-change]] (ring) on the stem. This observation, alongside
suppressed formation of incomplete fruit bodies, demonstrates that plant
steroidal hormones can profoundly alter fungal developmental anatomy.

## The Annulus in Psilocybe cubensis

The annulus (plural: annuli) is a ring-like remnant of the partial veil
(velum partiale) that originally connected the cap (pileus) margin to the
stem (stipe) in agaricoid mushrooms. In Psilocybe cubensis, the annulus is
typically a prominent membranous structure that:

- Persists as a double-layered ring on the upper portion of the stem
- Serves as a key [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]] feature for the species
- Represents the zone where the partial veil ruptured as the cap expanded
- May or may not be movable (depending on how firmly attached to the stipe
  surface)

The presence, form, and persistence of the annulus are standard characters
used in the morphological identification of Psilocybe species and are
documented in most field guides and taxonomic treatments.

## Observed Effect of Brassinosteroid Treatment

In the Gartz et al. experiments, cultures treated with 22S,23S-
homobrassinolide at 10⁻² ppm produced fruiting bodies that "lacked the
typical membranous annulus on the stems." This was a consistent finding
across the brassinosteroid-treated cultures, not an occasional aberration.

The absence was notable because:

1. The control cultures (without brassinosteroid) produced normal fruiting
   bodies with typical annuli
2. All other aspects of [[fruiting-body-development]] appeared normal — cap
   expansion, stem elongation, spore production
3. The effect was specific to the annulus structure rather than a general
   developmental disruption

## Suppression of Incomplete Fruit Bodies

Alongside the annulus loss, brassinosteroid treatment almost completely
suppressed the formation of incomplete fruit bodies. In standard mushroom
cultivation, it is common for a proportion of primordia to abort or develop
incompletely — forming small, stunted pins that never mature. The
brassinosteroid-treated cultures showed markedly fewer of these aborts.

This dual effect — loss of annulus plus suppression of incomplete development
— suggests that the steroid influences the developmental program of the
fruiting body at a fundamental level, possibly affecting the timing or
expression of genes involved in tissue differentiation.

## Possible Mechanisms

The mechanism by which a plant steroidal hormone suppresses [[brassinosteroid-suppressed-annulus-formation-psilocybe-cubensis]]
in a fungus remains unknown, but several hypotheses can be proposed:

### Veil Development Disruption

The partial veil forms during the early stages of fruiting body development.
If brassinosteroid treatment accelerates overall growth (as the 2-3x
mycelial growth rate suggests), the veil may develop more rapidly and either
fail to form properly or detach prematurely and disintegrate before the
fruiting body matures.

### Hormonal Crosstalk with Fungal Steroid Receptors

Fungi produce their own sterol compounds (ergosterol being the most
prominent) and possess steroid-related [[plant-signaling-pathways]]. Brassinosteroids
may interact with fungal sterol receptors or enzymes, potentially
interfering with normal sterol metabolism required for tissue
differentiation in the annulus region.
