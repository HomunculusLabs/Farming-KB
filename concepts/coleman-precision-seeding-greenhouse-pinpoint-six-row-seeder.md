# Precision Seeding in the Winter Harvest Greenhouse

## Overview

Chapter 13 of *The Winter Harvest Handbook* details Coleman's approach to precision seeding
in greenhouse beds, centering on the Pinpoint Seeder and its six-row evolution. These tools
represent a critical efficiency gain for intensive winter salad production, practically
doubling yields per square foot over less precise sowing methods. The chapter provides
detailed practical guidance on seed selection, seeder settings, and technique.

## The Economic Case for Precision Seeding

In the winter-harvest greenhouse, every square foot of protected growing space is valuable.
Precision seeding delivers measurable advantages:

- **Yield doubling**: Multi-row seeders on 2.25-inch row spacing roughly double per-square-
  foot yields compared to single-row or hand-sown methods
- **Cost efficiency**: At roughly $300, the four-row Pinpoint Seeder costs 10-15 times less
  than European multi-row greenhouse seeders while delivering comparable results
- **Labor savings**: Mechanical seed metering eliminates tedious hand sowing and reduces
  thinning labor
- **Consistent quality**: Uniform spacing produces more uniform, marketable baby-leaf product

## The Pinpoint Seeder: Design and Operation

### Seed Selection Mechanisms

The Pinpoint Seeder uses two complementary seed-metering systems:

1. **Seed hole sizes**: Four hole sizes (coded A, B, C, D from smallest to largest) on a
   movable axle. The operator positions the desired hole size centered under the hoppers.
2. **Brush adjustment**: Each hopper has a small adjustable brush that sweeps excess seeds
   away from the seed hole. Brush tightness is set by thumbscrew—tight (T), medium (M), or
   loose (L).

### Determining Seed Settings

Before sowing any seed, Coleman recommends calibrating the seeder:

- Fill one hopper and turn the wheel one complete rotation, counting seeds dropped
- The wheel circumference is approximately 9 inches
- Hole sizes A, B, and C have nine holes per rotation (≈1 seed per inch); size D has seven
- Record the ideal hole/brush combination on each seed packet using a coded system
  (e.g., "4BT" = four-row seeder, hole B, brush Tight)

### Soil Preparation Requirements

Precision seeders demand finely prepared soil. Rocks, undecomposed compost chunks, stringy
weed remnants, or root residues will jam or deflect the seeder. Coleman's preparation
sequence:

1. Remove crop residues and rake bed smooth
2. Lightly roll with a 30-inch-wide mesh-surface roller (prevents soil glazing)
3. Set the seeder on the bed and verify free wheel rotation before sowing
4. After sowing, firm seeds with a light roller pass or gentle raking

### Seed Depth Adjustment

Sowing depth is controlled by handle angle relative to the ground:

- Raising the handle → shallower sowing (for small seeds like lettuce, arugula)
- Lowering the handle → deeper sowing (for larger seeds like beets, chard)
- Irrigation after sowing can wash additional soil over seeds if deeper coverage is needed

## Crop-Specific Sowing Guidelines

### Baby-Leaf Lettuce
- **Row spacing**: 2.25 inches (standard four-row setting)
- **Hole setting**: 4BT in greenhouse; 4BM (looser) outdoors for slightly higher rate
- **Timing**: Staggered sowings from late August through October for winter harvest

### Arugula
- **Hole setting**: 4AM in lower winter light; 4BT in summer
- **Notes**: Arugula is a fast germinator; careful timing needed to avoid weeds overtaking

### Carrots
- **Row spacing**: 2.25 inches (4 square inches per plant)
- **Hole setting**: Standard axle may not handle beet/chard seed; consider custom deepened axle

### Claytonia, Minutina, and Sylvetta
- These ultra-small seeds overwhelm even the smallest hole (A)
- Set brushes tightly; multiple seeds per drop are acceptable as these crops tolerate
  close spacing
- Modification: Seal hopper seams with liquid solder to prevent seed loss through gaps

## The Six-Row Seeder: Evolution

Coleman and Johnny's Selected Seeds developed a six-row push seeder with improvements:

- **Integrated rollers**: Front mesh roller levels soil; rear roller drives seed axle and
  presses seeds in—eliminating separate rolling passes
- **Drive pulleys**: Three pulley ratios deliver seeds at 1 per inch, 1 per 2 inches, or
  1 per 4 inches
- **Six rows**: Plants a 30-inch bed in two passes (down and back) instead of three
- **Depth adjustment**: Screw-knob controlled front roller for precise seed depth setting

## Moisture Management During Sowing

In cold, moist greenhouse conditions, condensation can wet seeder surfaces causing seeds
to stick. Coleman's solutions:

- Warm and dry the seeder in front of a heater before use in humid conditions
- Install a form-fitting Plexiglas cover over seed hoppers for condensation protection
- Store seeder indoors (closet) to prevent corrosion
- Release brush tension before storage to prevent brush deformation

## Sowing Technique

Coleman's preferred technique:

1. Walk backwards down the path beside the bed, pulling the seeder toward you
2. Watch the seeder operation continuously—stop immediately if wheels stop turning or
   seeds stop dropping
3. Use wheel tooth marks (2.25 inches beyond outside rows) to align subsequent passes
4. For very tiny weeds, switch from forward/backward stroke technique to single-direction
  rapid pass, keeping blade just under soil surface
