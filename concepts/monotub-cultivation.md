## Overview

The monotub is the most widely used fruiting method for Psilocybe cubensis cultivation. A single plastic storage bin serves as both the colonization vessel and the fruiting chamber, eliminating the need to transfer substrates between containers. The design is simple, scalable, and capable of producing impressive yields with minimal daily maintenance. The monotub method was popularized in online mycology communities during the 2000s and has become the de facto standard for home cultivators.

## Monotub Design

### Tub Selection

The standard monotub is a 50-110 quart (47-104 liter) plastic storage bin with a lid. The most common sizes:

- **54-quart Sterilite or IRIS**: Good for beginners, manageable substrate volume (4-6 quarts of spawn).
- **66-quart Sterilite**: The workhorse. Holds 5-8 quarts of spawn with proportional substrate. Fits well on standard shelving.
- **80-110 quart**: For experienced growers running large substrates. Requires more spawn investment.

Key features to look for:
- Opaque or translucent sides (clear tubs cause side pinning from light exposure)
- Snug-fitting lid
- Straight sides (no tapering at the bottom)

### Hole Configuration

Holes provide passive fresh air exchange (FAE). The standard configuration uses two sizes:

**Large holes (2 inches)**: Placed near substrate level (2-4 inches from the bottom) on all four long sides. These are the primary FAE holes and are stuffed loosely with polyfill.

**Small holes (1 inch)**: Placed near the top of the tub on all four sides. These are for passive humidity exchange and are stuffed tightly with polyfill.

A typical 66-quart tub has 4 large holes (two per long side) and 4-8 small holes (two per long side, sometimes one per short side).

### Alternative FAE Methods

- **Micropore tape**: Cover holes with multiple layers of micropore tape instead of polyfill. Easier to work with but less adjustable.
- **Modified lid with no holes**: Some growers use an unmodified tub during colonization, then introduce FAE by cracking the lid or replacing it with a modified version for fruiting.
- **Shoebox tek**: Mini monotubs using 6-quart shoebox containers. No holes needed — the loose-fitting lid provides sufficient FAE. Excellent for testing isolates.

## Substrate: CVG

The standard monotub substrate is CVG — coconut coir, vermiculite, and gypsum. This mixture is extremely contamination-resistant because coco coir is naturally clean and the other components are inert. See [[bulk-substrate-preparation]] for detailed preparation.

Standard ratio by volume:
- 1 part coconut coir (compressed brick, usually 650g)
- 1 part vermiculite
- 0.1 part gypsum (approximately 5% by volume)

## Spawn Ratio

The spawn-to-substrate ratio determines how quickly the bulk substrate colonizes and how resistant it is to contamination:

- **1:1** — Maximum speed and contamination resistance. Expensive in terms of spawn.
- **1:2** — The standard recommended ratio. Good balance of speed, safety, and efficiency.
- **1:3** — Common for experienced growers with clean spawn. Slower colonization but more efficient use of spawn.
- **1:4 or higher** — Risky for beginners. Slow colonization gives contaminants more time to establish.

Example: A 66-quart monotub with 4 quarts of grain spawn uses 8-12 quarts of CVG substrate at a 1:2 to 1:3 ratio.

## The Monotub Process

### 1. Preparation

- Clean the tub thoroughly with soap and water, then wipe down with 70% isopropyl alcohol.
- Prepare the CVG substrate (hydrate coir brick with boiling water, mix in vermiculite and gypsum). Allow to cool to room temperature.
- The substrate should be at field capacity — squeeze firmly and only a few drops of water should emerge.

### 2. Spawning

- In a still-air environment (see [[mushroom-sterile-technique-detailed]]), break up colonized grain spawn in the jar by shaking.
- Add a thin layer (1/2 inch) of CVG to the bottom of the tub — this prevents grain from touching the bare plastic.
- Mix the grain spawn and CVG substrate thoroughly in the tub. Some growers layer spawn and substrate alternately.
- Add a 1/4 to 1/2 inch top layer of plain CVG (no grain) — this is called a "casing layer" and helps maintain surface humidity while protecting exposed grain from contamination.

### 3. Colonization

- Put the lid on tightly. Tape over holes with duct tape or cover with micropore tape to restrict FAE during colonization.
- Store at 70-78F (21-26C) in a dark or ambient-light location.
- Colonization typically takes 7-14 days. The surface should be fully white with mycelium before proceeding.
- Do not open the tub during colonization unless you see obvious contamination.

### 4. Fruiting Initiation

- Once fully colonized, introduce fruiting conditions:
  - Remove tape from holes, stuff with polyfill (loose at bottom, tight at top).
  - Introduce light on a 12/12 schedule (6500K daylight LED or ambient room light).
  - Optionally, wait for hyphal knots and primordia to form on the surface before introducing full FAE.
- Some growers recommend a "fruiting from spawn" approach where FAE is introduced immediately, skipping the taped colonization phase. This can work but is less predictable.

### 5. Fruiting and Maintenance

- Mist the surface lightly if it appears dry (no pooling water).
- Fan the tub by waving the lid over the opening 2-3 times per day if FAE seems insufficient (condensation pattern changes).
- Monitor for side pinning — if mushrooms form on the sides, the surface may be too dry or CO2 too high.
- First pins typically appear 3-7 days after fruiting initiation.
- Mushrooms mature in 5-10 days from pin to harvest.

## Expected Yields

- **First flush**: 2-6 oz dry (56-170g) from a 66-quart tub with 4 quarts of spawn, depending on strain, genetics, and conditions.
- **Second flush**: Typically 40-60% of first flush yield.
- **Third flush**: Typically 20-30% of first flush yield.
- **Total**: 4-10 oz dry (113-283g) over 2-3 flushes from a single tub.

See [[mushroom-harvesting-potency]] for strategies to maximize each flush.

## Common Problems

- **Side pinning**: Caused by the microclimate between the substrate and tub wall being more favorable than the surface. Prevention: line the tub with a trash bag that shrinks with the substrate as it consolidates.
- **Overlay**: A thick, matted mycelial layer on the surface that resists pinning. Caused by excessive CO2 during colonization. Prevention: introduce FAE at the right time.
- **Blob mutations**: Deformed, blob-like growth common with certain strains (especially Penis Envy). Not a contamination issue — these blobs are still potent and usable.
