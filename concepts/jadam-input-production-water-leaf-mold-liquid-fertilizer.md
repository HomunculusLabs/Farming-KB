## JADAM Input Production — Water Quality, Leaf Mold, and Liquid Fertilizer

## Overview

JADAM teaches farmers to produce all necessary inputs on-farm using locally available materials. This concept page covers three key aspects of JADAM input production: the importance of water quality, leaf mold collection and creation, and liquid fertilizer production from wild plants.

## Water Quality

### Water as Critical Element

"The quality of water determines the health of life forms—and farming produce. As earth, air, water all became polluted, it became harder to get good water."

JADAM identifies water quality as a fundamental but often overlooked factor in farming success. Because microorganisms are predominantly water, the quality of irrigation and solution water directly affects microbial health and, consequently, crop health.

### The Spring Water Experience

"We know by experience that after staying in a hot spring and drinking spring water for a few days, our skin becomes much smoother. These waters are renowned for their life-bringing qualities."

Cho draws a parallel between human and plant health: just as the quality of water we drink affects our bodies, the quality of water applied to crops affects plant health. "Our bodies and plants are one."

### Farm Water Sources

Most farms will have access to water of varying quality. JADAM advises farmers to evaluate their water sources and, where possible, use the best available water for preparing microbial solutions and liquid fertilizers.

## Leaf Mold

### What Is Leaf Mold?

Leaf mold is the soft, dark soil found beneath decomposing leaf litter in forests and grasslands. It is rich in [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]] that have adapted to the local environment.

### Collection

"Remove covering leaves and collect leaf mold soil from the underparts. It is full of indigenous microorganisms that have lived in an environment similar to my field."

The collection technique is simple: find an area with decomposing leaf litter, remove the surface leaves, and collect the soft soil beneath. This soil contains a diverse community of locally adapted microorganisms.

### Making Leaf Mold

"If you cannot collect leaf mold: You can make them. Find an uncontaminated piece of land. Cut wild grass and cover the soil with it. Keep it moist by frequently watering. Microorganisms in the soil and air will start propagating on the grass and soil surface. Soil will become soft and you can use it as leaf mold."

This technique allows leaf mold creation anywhere—even on farms with no forested areas. The process requires only grass, water, and time.

## JADAM Microorganism Solution (JMS)

### Culturing Process

JMS is produced by [[culturing-indigenous-microorganisms-techniques]] from leaf mold in a [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]]. The basic process involves:
1. Collecting leaf mold
2. Adding it to water with a food source (potato, sweet potato, etc.)
3. Maintaining appropriate temperature for microbial growth
4. Waiting for the culture to mature (typically 3-7 days)

### Temperature Management

For low-temperature conditions (below 18°C/64°F), heating may be required. JADAM provides practical guidance: "Use 3kw heater for 500 L, 1.5kw for 100-300 L." Insulation wraps prevent heat loss.

### Readiness Indicators

"Bubbles are forming on the entire surface. A clear disc has formed; it is time for use." Visual indicators—surface bubbling and a clear separation layer—signal that the microbial culture is mature and ready for application.

### Foliar Application

"JMS can be applied on soil or leaves. If you spray JMS regularly on the leaves and branches, microbial diversity will be maintained on the plants." Foliar application of JMS creates a protective microbial barrier on plant surfaces, competing with potential pathogens for space and nutrients.

## JADAM Liquid Fertilizer (JLF)

### Basic Principle

JLF is produced by fermenting plant material in water with leaf mold as a microbial inoculant. The plant material provides nutrients, while the microorganisms break down the tissue into soluble forms.

### The Purslane Example

"Purslane is one of the fastest disintegrating wild grasses. Fill the container with purslane, fill up water, put in a handful of leaf mold, close lid. Decomposition is almost complete in 10 days."

Purslane (*[[portulaca-oleracea]]*) is an ideal JLF ingredient because:
- It is widely available (grows as a weed worldwide)
- It has relatively high nitrogen content
- Its soft tissue decomposes quickly
- It functions as a fruit [[jadam-color-enhancer]]

### Aging

"The older the liquid fertilizer, the better." JLF improves with age as microbial decomposition progresses. Some farmers prepare JLF in the spring for use throughout the year.

### Filtration

"Prepare a plastic bowl with many holes, wrap it with fine net, place a rock in it (to make it sink), and put it in the JLF. Filtered liquid will gather in the bowl so it is easy to use."

This simple filtration system allows easy extraction of liquid fertilizer without expensive equipment.

### Container Management

"Put the [[jadam-liquid-fertilizer]] containers in the same environment as the crops. That is how you propagate microbes adapted to that local environment." By storing JLF containers in the field, the microorganisms in the solution are continuously adapted to local conditions.

### Sequential Use

"Prepare many containers for liquid fertilizer; use them in sequence." Staggered production ensures a continuous supply of mature liquid fertilizer throughout [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].

## Cost Analysis

The JADAM approach to input production eliminates virtually all input costs:
- **Water**: Available on-farm
- **Leaf mold**: Collected from nearby land or created on-farm
- **Plant material**: Wild grasses and weeds, free
- **Containers**: Simple plastic vessels
- **Labor**: Minimal, integrated into daily routine

The result is a farming system in which the major recurring costs of conventional and mainstream organic agriculture—purchased fertilizers, pesticides, and biological inputs—are eliminated entirely.
