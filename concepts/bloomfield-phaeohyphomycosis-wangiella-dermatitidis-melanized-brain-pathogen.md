# Wangiella dermatitidis — Phaeohyphomycosis and Brain-Invasion by Melanized Fungi

Among the thousands of known fungal species, only a tiny fraction cause
human disease. Within this small group of pathogens, a subset distinguished
by their dark pigmentation — the **dematiaceous** or melanized fungi —
exhibit a peculiar and dangerous affinity for the human central nervous
system. *Wangiella dermatitidis* (pronounced huan-gee-ella,
derm-at-eh-tide-diss) is one of the most notable members of this group
and a rare but devastating cause of **phaeohyphomycosis**.

## What Is Phaeohyphomycosis?

Phaeohyphomycosis is the clinical term for infections caused by
[[dematiaceous-fungi-melanin-fungal-ecology]] that grow in host tissues as dark-walled yeast cells,
pseudohyphae, or true hyphae. Unlike chromoblastomycosis, in which the
fungus produces characteristic thick-walled sclerotic (copper-penny)
bodies in the skin, phaeohyphomycosis encompasses a broader range of
tissue presentations. The common thread is the presence of melanin in
the fungal [[alpha-1-3-glucan-fungal-pathogen-cell-walls]], visible as dark brown or black pigmentation in
biopsy specimens.

The clinical spectrum of phaeohyphomycosis ranges from superficial
skin infections to invasive disease of the brain, lungs, and other
internal organs. *Wangiella dermatitidis* is unusual among the
phaeohyphomycosis-causing fungi because of its pronounced neurotropism
— a tendency to seek out and colonize the central nervous system.

## Melanin as a Virulence Factor

The melanin pigment that gives *Wangiella* its dark color is far more
than decorative. It functions as a molecular shield that furnishes the
fungal cell with a formidable barrier between the cytoplasm and its
hostile surroundings. This barrier confers multiple survival advantages:

- **Ultraviolet resistance:** Melanized cells withstand UV radiation
  that would kill unpigmented competitors
- **Thermal tolerance:** Dark cells survive temperature fluctuations
  that destroy albino mutants
- **Enzyme resistance:** The melanin layer prevents cell-wall-degrading
  enzymes produced by [[fungal-virulence-factors-host-immune-evasion]] cells from reaching the plasma membrane
- **Chemical shielding:** Antifungal drugs and oxidative burst products
  from neutrophils are neutralized by the pigment layer

The importance of melanin has been demonstrated decisively through
laboratory experiments with **albino mutants** of *Wangiella*. When
melanin production is blocked — by chemical inhibitors or gene
disruption — the resulting colorless strains grow perfectly well on
stress-free [[query-how-to-make-agar-plates-for-mushroom-cultivation]], but shrivel under UV light, die when
temperature changes, or lyse when attacked by cell-wall-degrading
enzymes. In mouse infection experiments, laboratory animals survive
inoculation with albino mutants but perish when injected with
melanized wild-type strains.

## Brain Invasion and Neuropathology

*Wangiella dermatitidis* shares with *[[bloomfield-cryptococcus-neoformans-opportunistic-mycoses-aids]]* the
alarming ability to invade the human brain. The mechanism by which
fungi cross the blood-brain barrier remains incompletely understood,
but melanin appears to play a role in protecting the fungal cells from
host immune attack during transit through the bloodstream.

presents a striking pathological appearance: **tiny islands of yeasts

dramatically against the pale brain tissue, creating a pattern that is
