## Basidiocarp Four-Stage Development Classification in Psilocybe Cubensis

## Overview

Edmond R. Badham (1982) established a four-stage developmental classification
for the basidiocarp of *Psilocybe cubensis* during wind tunnel tropism
experiments at Lehman College, City University of New York. This staging
framework correlates [[emcdda-psilocybin-mushroom-species-and-identification]].

## The Four Developmental Stages

### Stage 1: Pileus Initiation

Characterized by slight swelling of the pileus (cap). The primordium has
risen above the substrate and the first cap tissue differentiation is
underway. The stipe is relatively short, resembling an elongated primordium.
In Badham's experiments, primordia were placed in the wind tunnel at 15-25 mm
height, typically transitioning from Stage 1 to Stage 2.

Stage 1 mushrooms exhibit strong positive anemotropism (growth into the
wind). Stipe elongation occurs through acropetal expansion of existing cells,
with limited cell division at the upper stipe. The boundary layer
redistribution mechanism dominates tropic behavior, directing growth toward
the greatest evaporative demand regardless of gravitational orientation.

### Stage 2: Spherical Pileus

Defined by a nearly spherical pileus. The cap has expanded significantly
but has not yet begun to flatten. Gill (lamellae) tissue develops internally
without active spore production. This stage occurs around 24 hours after
Stage 1 under experimental conditions (24.5 ± 1.5°C, 94 ± 3% RH, 12h
photoperiod).

Stage 2 marks a critical tropic shift. The initial anemotropic response is
masked by [[evaporative-demand-hypothesis-mushroom-tropism]], where cells on one side of the
stipe expand faster due to localized drying effects.

## Correlation with Tropic Responses

The staging maps directly onto distinct tropic behaviors:

- **Stages 1-2**: Anemotropism dominates (wind-directed growth)
- **Stage 2 transition**: Negative geotropism emerges, linked to spore
  production
- **Stages 3-4**: Full [[four-stage-basidiocarp-development-psilocybe-cubensis-badham]]
