# Hydroponic Cannabis Cultivation

Hydroponics is the science of growing plants without soil, most often in a soilless mix. In cannabis cultivation, [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]] of fertilizer diluted in water. This solution passes over or floods around roots at regular intervals, then drains off. The extra oxygen trapped in the soilless medium and around the roots speeds nutrient uptake by tiny root hairs. Cannabis grows fast hydroponically because it is able to take in food as fast as it can be used.

In soil, as in hydroponics, roots absorb nutrients and water. However, even the best soil rarely has as much oxygen in it as a soilless hydroponic medium, which is one reason hydroponic growth rates exceed soil-based cultivation.

## pH Management

The pH in hydroponic gardens is critical and requires somewhat vigilant monitoring. Marijuana grows well hydroponically within a pH range of 5.5 to 6.5, with 5.8 to 6.0 being ideal. In hydroponics, nutrients are in solution and more available than when in soil. The pH can fluctuate a half point and not cause any problems, but regular checks are recommended.

Roots take in nutrients at different rates, which causes the ratios of nutrients in solution to change the pH. When the pH is above 7 or below 5.5, some nutrients are not absorbed as fast as possible. Different mediums perform best at different pH levels. Always follow manufacturer guidelines for pH level and correction chemicals.

## EC and Nutrient Concentration

Nutrient concentration is measured using EC (Electrical Conductivity), CF (Conductivity Factor), or ppm (Parts Per Million). EC is the most accurate and consistent scale, measured in milliSiemens per centimeter (mS/cm). The nutrient solution concentration should be adjusted based on plant growth stage, with lower concentrations for seedlings and higher concentrations during the [vegetative and [flowering stages.

The pH and EC gradually change between irrigations as plants absorb nutrients and water transpires and evaporates into the air. If enough time passes between irrigations, the nutrient concentration might change so much that the plant is unable to draw it in efficiently.

## Irrigation Schedules

Irrigation frequency varies by system and growing medium:
- **Top-feed systems**: Cycle for about five minutes or longer, irrigated at least three times daily. Often cycled 24 hours a day in fast-draining expanded clay.
- **Drip irrigation in coco coir**: Four or five times daily.
- **Flood and drain**: Five to ten times daily.
- **NFT**: Often continuous, 24 hours a day.
- **DWC**: Continuous aeration, 24 hours a day.
