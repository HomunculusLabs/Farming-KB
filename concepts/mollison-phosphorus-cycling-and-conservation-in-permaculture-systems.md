# Phosphorus Cycling and Conservation in Permaculture Systems

Bill Mollison's *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* identifies
phosphorus as one of the two most critical limiting resources for
human civilisation, alongside clean water. Chapter 8 presents a
detailed analysis of phosphorus cycling, loss pathways, and
conservation strategies that are central to sustainable design.

## The Phosphorus Crisis

Mollison warns that phosphorus will be one of the inexorable limits to
human occupancy on this planet. We must not defer solving problems of
phosphorus conservation or we betray our own children. The analysis of
phosphorus in the Manual is one of the most urgent and prescient
sections of the work.

Deep-rooted trees and perennial plants are responsible for bringing
phosphorus up to the topsoil wherever it is rare in more
shallow-rooted plants. This function of [[phosphorus-availability]] in agricultural soils.

## Sources of Phosphorus

Mollison identifies several natural and traditional sources of
phosphorus:

- **Seeds**: Phosphorus is concentrated in seeds, making seed-eating
  animals and birds important phosphorus vectors.

- **Bones of vertebrates**: Especially fish bones, which are
  phosphorus-rich. Bone meal from land animals is a traditional
  source.

- **Freshwater mussels**: Mussels concentrate phosphorus in their
  bodies and the mud they live in. In aquatic cultures, phosphorus was
  recovered from the mud of ponds stocked with mussels.

- **Bird manures**: Bat guano is a favoured phosphate source. Pigeon
  droppings are particularly phosphorus-rich due to their seed-based
  diet. Historically, most farms kept a flock of pigeons as their
  phosphate factory.

- **Fish and waterfowl wastes**: Aquaculture systems recycle
  phosphorus through fish manure and waterfowl droppings back to
  plants and soils.

- **Rock phosphate**: Contains 8-15% phosphorus in various
  combinations. Mined from marine deposits, it is the primary
  commercial source but is a finite resource.

## Phosphorus Loss Pathways

Mollison quantifies phosphorus loss in different systems:

- **Uncut forests**: Lose approximately 0.1 kg/ha/year of phosphorus.
- **Clear-cropped agriculture**: Can lose 100 kg/ha/year or more, a
  1000-fold increase in mineral loss never accounted for in the cost
  of woodchipping and newspapers.

- **Detergents**: About 15-20% of inessential phosphorus use is in
  detergents. Older potash soaps are the answer to this misuse, or the
  recovery of phosphorus from greywater.

- **One-way sewage systems**: Sewage carries phosphorus from
  settlements directly to the sea, a permanent loss from agricultural
  cycles.

## Conservation Strategies

Mollison presents a hierarchy of phosphorus conservation strategies:

1. **Conservation farming** loses about one-half to one-third the
   phosphorus of contemporary agriculture, even without non-tillage.

2. **Non-tillage farming** would lose even less phosphorus, but is
   rarely assessed except in dollar yield terms. See [[acacia]] species
- Casuarina
- Banksia
- Pultenaea
- Gigasperma calaspora

These plants, with their mycelial associates, fix and return phosphate
via leaf mould to surface soils. Several plants pump sugars or
carbohydrates into soils, while leaf-sucking insects and scale insects
exude sugars that feed soil biology and facilitate phosphorus cycling.

## Integrating Phosphorus Conservation in Design

Mollison recommends several design-level strategies:

- **Maintain tree cover**: Trees prevent phosphorus loss through
  erosion and leaching while cycling phosphorus from deep soil layers.

- **Close nutrient loops**: Return all organic wastes to the soil
  through composting, mulching, or direct application. Avoid
  one-way sewage systems that export phosphorus to waterways.

- **Integrate animals**: Keep pigeons, poultry, and other seed-eating
  animals that concentrate phosphorus in their manure. Apply manure
  to gardens and orchards.

- **Use greywater recovery**: Recover phosphorus from household
  greywater rather than allowing it to flow to sewers. See
  [[mycorrhiza]]

critical role in phosphorus uptake. These fungal partnerships extend
