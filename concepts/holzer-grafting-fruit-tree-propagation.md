# Grafting and Fruit Tree Propagation

[[comparison-sepp-holzer-vs-biodynamic]]'s approach to fruit tree propagation through grafting reflects
his broader philosophy of working with nature rather than against it. While
he employs standard grafting techniques, his choices of rootstock, scion
selection, and overall strategy differ significantly from commercial fruit
growing practices. His methods prioritise tree longevity, hardiness, and
independence over early fruiting and ease of harvest. This approach is
central to [[sepp-holzer-fruit-tree-varieties]].

## Why Graft?

Cultivated [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]] are not usually propagated using seeds, because the
characteristics of the desired variety will not be passed on true through the
seeds. Many fruit cultivars can now only be pollinated by different varieties,
meaning every flower can produce different seeds and consequently different
characteristics. To preserve the qualities of a certain variety, it must be
propagated vegetatively through [[holzer-fruit-forest-establishment]] that will continue to provide food for the next
generation. This long-term perspective is fundamental to the [[holzer-fruit-trees-no-pruning]] philosophy. By using vigorous rootstocks and allowing
trees to grow to their natural size, he avoids the need for the extensive
pruning that dwarf trees require. The resulting trees may be taller and
slightly harder to harvest, but they are far more self-sufficient, disease-
resistant, and long-lived.

This approach also means that grafted trees are better suited to
integration with livestock. Tall, vigorous trees can withstand browsing
pressure better than small dwarf trees, making them more compatible with
[[holzer-livestock-integration]] systems.
