# Phanerochaete chrysosporium in Bioremediation

## Overview

Phanerochaete chrysosporium (syn. Sporotrichum pulverulentum) is a white rot basidiomycete and the most extensively studied fungal organism for [[fungi-in-the-environment-bioremediation-applications]]. Originally isolated and characterized for its ability to degrade lignin, P. chrysosporium has become the model organism for understanding [[singh-fungal-biodegradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]] environmental pollutants. Its [[ligninolytic-enzyme-production-bioremediation]] system — comprising [[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]] (MnP), and H₂O₂-generating oxidases — has been shown to degrade an extraordinarily wide range of xenobiotic compounds.

## Biological Characteristics

### Taxonomy and Growth

- **Classification**: Basidiomycota, Corticiaceae
- **Growth form**: Filamentous fungus forming cottony white mycelial mats
- **Temperature**: Unusual optimum at 37-40°C (most white rots: 27-30°C); thermotolerance advantageous for large-scale reactors (reduced cooling costs)
- **pH**: Optimal growth at acidic pH; reduces medium pH to 2-3 during growth on carbohydrates
- **Nutrition**: Secondary metabolism and ligninolytic [[query-how-does-lignin-degradation-by-fungi-work]] by P. chrysosporium, as MnP alone is not sufficient (Pérez & Jeffries, 1992; Hammel et al., 1993).
3. **Low laccase production**: Unlike most [[white-rot-fungi]], P. chrysosporium produces little or no laccase under standard conditions. Laccase production has been reported only with cellulose as carbon source (Srinivasan et al., 1995).
4. **Temperature optimum**: Higher than typical white rot fungi, which reduces cooling requirements in bioreactors.

## Ligninolytic Enzyme System

### Lignin Peroxidase (LiP)

- **Molecular weight**: ~42 kDa (haem peroxidase)
- **pH optimum**: 2.3-2.5 (based on initial rates); enzyme is unstable at this pH
- **Unique capability**: Only peroxidase known to oxidize non-phenolic aromatic compounds directly (redox potential >1.4V)
- **Mechanism**: One-electron oxidation of aromatic nuclei generates cation radicals; [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] (VA) serves as both mediator (diffusible electron carrier) and enzyme protector (prevents inactivation by H₂O₂ excess)
- **Isozymes**: Family of structurally similar genes with complex regulation patterns (Stewart et al., 1992; Brooks, Sims & Broda, 1993; Gaskell et al., 1994)
- **Genetics**: lip genes are differentially expressed based on growth conditions; molecular genetics well characterized with established protocols for auxotroph production, DNA/RNA purification, and genetic transformation (Cullen, 1997)

### Manganese Peroxidase (MnP)

- **Presence in ALL white rot species**: MnP is more universally distributed than LiP among white rot fungi
- **Mechanism**: Converts Mn²⁺ → Mn³⁺; Mn³⁺ is a strong, diffusible oxidant
- **Role**: Essential for lignin degradation in vivo; Mn³⁺ oxidizes phenolic lignin units, and lipid peroxidation initiated by MnP can attack non-phenolic structures
- **Induction/repression**: Mn²⁺ presence induces MnP production and represses LiP production in P. chrysosporium (Reddy & D'Souza, 1994)

### H₂O₂ Sources

- **Glucose oxidase**: Oxidizes glucose to gluconic acid + H₂O₂
- **Glyoxal oxidase**: Oxidizes glyoxal and related aldehydes to oxalic acid + H₂O₂ (Kersten & Kirk, 1987)
- **Aryl alcohol oxidase**: Present in many white rot fungi (Gutierrez et al., 1994)

## Pollutant Degradation Capabilities

### Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs)

P. chrysosporium degrades multiple PAHs through its ligninolytic system:

- **Anthracene**: Anthracene → 9,10-anthraquinone → phthalic acid → protocatechuic acid → CO₂
- **Phenanthrene**: Multiple pathways — ligninolytic (peroxidase-mediated) and non-ligninolytic (cytochrome P450)
- **Pyrene**: Degraded through quinone intermediates
- **Benzo[a]pyrene**: Oxidized to quinone derivatives

### Chlorinated Compounds

- **PCBs**: Degraded Aroclor 1242 in nitrogen-deficient cultures (Bumpus et al., 1987); also degraded Aroclor 1254, 1260 and mixtures (Yadav et al., 1995). Prefers lesser-chlorinated congeners.
- **2,4,5-T**: Degraded in both liquid culture and contaminated soil with corn cob supplement (Ryan & Bumpus, 1989)
- **2,4-D**: Degraded alone and in mixtures with 2,4,5-T (Yadav & Reddy, 1993)

### Nitroaromatics

- **TNT**: Degrades 30-50% at concentrations <20 mg/L; metabolites (not TNT itself) inhibit LiP production; MnP appears after TNT disappearance, suggesting TNT inhibits MnP production initially

### Pesticides

- **Atrazine**: Transformed to hydroxylated and N-dealkylated metabolites (Mougin et al., 1994); approximately 30% immobilized as [[fungal-bound-residue-formation]] (Hickey, Fuster & Lamar, 1994)
- **Alachlor**: Co-metabolic transformation with ~10% degradation (Ferry et al., 1994)
- **Anilazine**: Aided biodegradation and remobilization in humic soil fractions (Liebich, Buranel & Fuhr, 1999)
