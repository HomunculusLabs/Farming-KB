# Psilocybin Mushroom Lookalikes and Deadly Identification Warnings

Source: [[psilocybin-mushroom-identification]] can be lethal. Since few little brown mushrooms have ever been sought after as food, little was known about their biochemistry until recently. As more people sought psilocybin varieties, poisonings from mistaken identifications increased.

## Deadly Galerina Species

The primary [[psilocybin-mushroom-deadly-lookalikes]] are species of Galerina that resemble Psilocybes and contain amatoxins (cyclopeptides) similar to those in lethal Amanita species:

- **G. autumnalis**: Stamets considers this an indicator species — when present, Psilocybe are likely nearby. Colonies of G. autumnalis and P. stuntzii have been found overlapping with stems actually touching.
- **G. marginata**: Contains the same deadly amatoxins
- **G. venenata**: Equally dangerous

**Key distinguishing feature**: Galerina spores are rusty brown in deposit; Psilocybe spores are purplish brown. However, when caps are dried and/or frozen, color can be very similar.

## Pholiotina filaris (= Conocybe filaris)

Another deadly rusty brown-spored mushroom containing amatoxins. Produces the same cyclopeptide toxins as Galerina and deadly Amanitas. Stamets frequently finds P. stuntzii growing within inches of P. filaris.

## Amatoxin Poisoning Timeline

Similar effects expected from eating Galerina, Conocybe, or Amanita species containing cyclopeptides:

1. **6-24 hours** (average 10-14 hours) after ingestion: First symptoms appear — sharp abdominal pains, violent vomiting, cholerarlike diarrhea (often containing blood and mucus)
2. **3-4 days**: Symptoms temporarily subside; patient appears to improve (dangerous false recovery)
3. **7-10 days**: Condition worsens with liver and kidney failure leading to death
4. **Autopsy findings**: Marked gastro-intestinal edema, hemorrhagic gastro-enteritis, lymphoid hyperplasia, fatty degeneration of heart and liver, tubular necrosis of kidneys, swollen brain with multiple hemorrhages and degenerative nerve cell damage

## Case History: Whidbey Island, 1981

Three people picked and ingested deadly Galerina species, mistaking them for Psilocybes. Fearing prosecution for possession, they delayed going to hospital for two days — the most critical period for effective treatment. The 16-year-old girl died six days later (Beug and Bigwood 1982b).

## Other Poisonous Genera

Other brown-spored genera with abundant poisonous species:
- **Inocybe**: Several bluing species (e.g., I. aeruginascens) contain psilocybin but close relatives can be very poisonous — treat with caution
- **Cortinarius**: Many poisonous species
- **Hebeloma**: Contains poisonous species
- **Entoloma**: Contains poisonous species

## Safety Guidelines

- Spore prints are essential for identification
- Pay attention to detail and follow keys carefully
- Members of Psilocybe and Panaeolus generally seem safe — only psilocybin, psilocin, and [[lsd-structure-activity-relationships-related-compounds]] detected thus far
- Some species (e.g., formerly [[stropharia-aeruginosa]] = [[psilocybe-aeruginosa]]) have not been fully tested
- Bluing Inocybes should be treated with extreme caution due to poisonous close relatives
