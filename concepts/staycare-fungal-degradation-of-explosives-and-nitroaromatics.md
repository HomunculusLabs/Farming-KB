# Fungal Degradation of Explosives and Nitroaromatics

Nitroaromatic compounds are used in the manufacture of explosives,
pesticides, pharmaceuticals, dyes, and plastics. They are commonly found
in groundwater and soil near production sites, with nitrotoluenes and
explosive residues being frequent pollutants at military facilities. Many
nitroaromatics are highly toxic, carcinogenic, or mutagenic. Fungi,
particularly white rot basidiomycetes, have demonstrated significant
ability to transform and degrade these recalcitrant compounds.

## Trinitrotoluene (TNT) Degradation

TNT (2,4,6-trinitrotoluene) is the most widely studied explosive
pollutant in [[staycare-phanerochaete-chrysosporium-in-bioremediation]] degrades 30 to
50% of added TNT at concentrations below 20 mg/l in liquid culture. Higher
concentrations inhibit degradation, with hydroxylamino-dinitrotoluene
intermediates accumulating and inhibiting further TNT breakdown.

The degradation pathway involves oxidative, reductive, and methyl transfer
reactions. An aromatic nitroreductase catalyzes the reduction of nitro
groups, converting TNT, dinitrobenzenes, and chloronitrobenzenes to their
corresponding hydroxylamino or amino congeners. Key intermediates include
2-amino-4,6-dinitrotoluene and 4-amino-2,6-dinitrotoluene.

## Ligninolytic Enzyme Interactions with TNT

Addition of TNT to ligninolytic cultures causes disappearance of lignin
peroxidase (LiP) activity, though this is caused by metabolites rather
than TNT itself. Hydroxylamino intermediates are the actual LiP inhibitors.
[[biochemistry-of-ligninolytic-fungi-bioremediation]] for TNT
bioremediation, as the very enzymes that could degrade the compound are
suppressed during the process.

## Alternative Fungal Species for TNT Degradation

Phlebia radiata degrades both TNT and 2-amino-4,6-dinitrotoluenes
(22% and 76%, respectively), suggesting potential for munitions-contaminated
site remediation. [[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]] degrade TNT
in solid-state systems. The brown rot fungus [[hplc-detection-limits-calibration-psilocybin-psilocin-bigwood-beug]] within 3 days and can grow
with TNT concentrations exceeding 40 mg/l, showing greater tolerance than
P. chrysosporium. Nematoloma frowardii MnP degrades amino-dinitrotoluene
to carbon dioxide in the presence of reduced glutathione.

## Dinitrotoluene Degradation

Dinitrotoluenes contaminate sites from TNT manufacturing and propellant
production. P. chrysosporium degrades 2,4-dinitrotoluene through a
reductive pathway yielding 2-amino-4-nitrotoluene, with subsequent
elimination of the amine group as ammonium and the nitro group as nitrite.
Ceratocystis coerulescens, Lentinus lepideus, and [[trichoderma-harzianum]]
also degrade dinitrotoluene, with C. coerulescens and T. harzianum
degrading it more rapidly and completely. Lignolytic conditions favour
rapid and complete degradation.

## RDX and HMX (Nitramines)

RDX (hexahydro-1,3,5-trinitro-1,3,5-triazine) is one of the predominant
military explosives. P. chrysosporium recovers 67% of radiolabelled RDX
as carbon dioxide under nitrogen-limiting conditions over 30 days. In soil
amended with corn cobs, recovery increases to 76%. Cladosporium resinae
transforms the greatest amount of RDX among tested fungi, though
metabolites were not identified. HMX, a co-contaminant with RDX, has
received less study but is expected to be partially transformed during RDX
bioremediation.

## Nitroglycerin Degradation

Geotrichum candidum stoichiometrically transforms glyceryl trinitrate
(GTN) to glyceryl dinitrate and glyceryl mononitrate. P. chrysosporium
exhibits the most efficient and extensive transformation of GTN among
tested fungi, with regioselectivity for the 2-mononitrate species. EPR
analysis showed nitric oxide production directly from GTN, with heme
protein-nitric oxide complexes detected in mycelial samples. A glutathione
transferase-like system was proposed for nitrite evolution from GTN.
