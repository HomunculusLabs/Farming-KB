## Tremellales and Basidiomycete Mycoparasites

Mycoparasitism is particularly widespread among Heterobasidiomycetes (Urediniomycetes, Ustilaginomycetes, and phragmobasidial Hymenomycetes). This page covers the major basidiomycete groups involved in fungicolous interactions, with emphasis on the Tremellales.

## Tremellales

The order Tremellales includes species with a dimorphic life cycle, a septal pore apparatus with sacculate caps, and a tremelloid basidium. Most or all species are believed to be mycoparasites. The dikaryotic somatic phase shows restricted growth, reflecting the mycoparasitic nature of many species.

### Haustorial Structures

Many Tremellales species have monokaryotic "haustorial branches" subtended by a clamp consisting of a bulbous base and a tubular appendage. The tubular appendage attaches to and/or penetrates the hyphal wall of the host fungus. The haustorium has an absorptive role, eventually leading to degeneration of the host cytoplasm.

**Micropore openings** have been demonstrated in haustoria of some species (e.g., *Syzygospora*, *Phragmoxenidium*), where the pore membrane appears continuous with the [[plasmalemma]] of both cells. Somewhat larger pores are present in *[[tetragoniomyces-uliginosus]]*.

## Tremellaceae

### Tremella

*Tremella* (170+ species) is the largest and most heterogeneous genus, including mycoparasitic species growing on:

- Hymenia of aphyllophorales
- Basidiomes of Dacrymycetales
- Ascomata and/or stromata of pyrenomycetes
- Hymenomycetes and lichens

**Tremella encephala** — its firm nucleus is nothing other than its host, *Stereum sanguinolentum* (Bandoni 1984). *T. polyporina* can replace the hymenium on basidiocarps of *Postia* species.

### Other Tremellaceae Genera

- **Holtermannia** — found on a polypore
- **Sirotrema** — on conidial masses of *Arthrinium* or on hymenia of *Hypodermataceae*
- **Trimorphomyces** — on *Arthrinium*
- **Xenolachne** — on inoperculate discomycetes
- **Bulleromyces albus** — yeastlike with Tremella-type haustoria, suggesting mycoparasitic capacity
- **Biatoropsis usnearum** — lichenicolous

## Syzygosporaceae

The Syzygosporaceae (synonym Carcinomycetaceae) comprise parasites on *[[phanerochaete]]* species, *Gymnopus* species, and *Marasmius* species, often causing gall-like deformations. The genus includes species with holobasidia of various shapes, and in some species, "zygoconidia" arise from the fusion of blastoconidia thrust forward by adjacent cells.

Ginns (1986) synonymized *Syzygospora*, *Christiansenia*, and *Carcinomyces* into *Syzygospora*, recognizing nine species in three subgenera. Notable species:

- **Christiansenia mycetophila**, **C. effibulata**, **C. tumefaciens** — grow on *[[gymnopus-dryophilus]]*, inducing gall-like fructifications
- **Syzygospora alba** — produces tremelloid sporocarps on unknown host
- **S. bachmannii** and **S. physciacearum** — common lichenicolous species on *Cladonia* and Physciaceae

## Filobasidiaceae

*Filobasidium*, *Filobasidiella*, and *Cystofilobasidium* are more or less mycoparasitic. The yeastlike genera *Filobasidium* and *Cystofilobasidium* have hyphal structures with haustoria only in the dikaryotic phase. *Filobasidiella* (best known as the *[[cryptococcus-neoformans]]* anamorph) also has haustoria. *Cystofilobasidium capitatum* was found in the gleba of Phallaceae.

## Rhynchogastremaceae

*Rhynchogastrema* species contact various soil hosts with haustoria, but axenic growth is possible.

## Platygloeomycetidae (Sporidiales Clade)

This subclass comprises the majority of species formerly in Auriculariales sensu lato, mostly parasites of fungi, mosses, ferns, and flowering plants. It includes four orders with mycoparasitic genera.

### Colacosomes (Lenticular Bodies)

Parasites in this group contact hosts through haustoria. Some have large numbers of a specialized organelle in the periplasmic region called **colacosomes** (or lenticular bodies). These are vesicular structures with an electron-opaque core and an electron-transparent sheath; the vesicular content projects through the cell wall of the parasite and contacts the host plasmalemma.

Colacosomes were first described by Kreger-van Rij & Veenhuis (1971) for *Sporidiobolus* and later from *Cryptomycocolax* and *Colacogloea*. Their presence in *Bensingtonia* and *Rhodosporidium* suggests those yeasts may also have mycoparasitic capabilities.

### Platygloeales

- **Platygloea fimetaria** and **Colacogloea peniophorae** — parasitize corticiaceous hosts, also *Dacrymyces* and *Poria*
- **Cystobasidium lasioboli** — on *Lasiobolus*; other species elicit conspicuous galls on lichens
- **Naohidea** — forms pustular basidiomes on old pyrenomycetes
- **Occultifur** and **Kryptastrina** — intrahymenial mycoparasites in basidiomata of *Dacrymyces* and corticioid fungi

## Hymenomycetidae

### Aphyllophorales as Mycoparasites

Destructive necrotrophic mycoparasites of ascomycetes have been found among *Bjerkandera*, *Lenzites*, *Trichaptum*, *Trametes*, *Schizophyllum*, and *Pleurotus*. Mutual displacement of aphyllophorales is an important phenomenon in wood decay.

*Geotrichopsis mycoparasitica* (arthroconidial basidiomycete) attacks nematophagous fungi, some Mucoraceae, *Mortierella*, *Pythium*, *Chalara elegans*, and *Rhizoctonia solani*, but not *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Geotrichum*, *Drechslera*, or *Nematoctonus*.

### Boletales and Agaricales

A few fungicolous boletes and agarics show host-specific associations, notably in the genera *Asterophora* and *Squamanita*.

## Urediniomycetes

### Ustilaginomycetes

**Tilletiopsis** contains mycoparasites of powdery mildews. **Pseudozyma** (characterized by dimorphic growth and variously shaped blastoconidia) includes anamorphs of *Ustilago* and mycoparasites of powdery mildews formerly described as *Stephanoascus* species.

## Cryptomycocolacales

*Cryptomycocolax* is possibly one of the most primitive basidiomycetes, with simple septal pores and apparent ascomycete features. It parasitizes an unidentified ascomycete in *Cirsium* culms in Costa Rica. The host produces botryose outgrowths that are engulfed by the parasite.

## Heterogastridiales

*Heterogastridium pycnidioideum* (anamorph *Hyalopycnis blepharistoma*) parasitizes various fungi on rotting plant material, including decaying walnuts in Japan. It can be grown axenically but some strains require a host for pycnidium formation.
