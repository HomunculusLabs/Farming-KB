## Zone-Based Planting

You can plant a keyhole bed using the [[nitrogen-fixing-plants]] such as Dutch clover, wind-and-weed barriers,
robust mulch-providers such as crotolaria or comfrey, or small fruit
trees. The bed can also be expanded to fill the corners, since a
keyhole bed can be square rather than round. It is the central path
that defines it.

## Multiple Keyhole Beds

Several keyhole beds can extend to the left and right of a central
walkway. An undulating path flanked by keyhole beds can wrap around a
house to make an attractive Zone 1 garden.

## Mandala Gardens

A mandala garden is a set of four to eight keyhole beds arranged in a
circle with one more bed in the center, and a path entering from one
side. A mandala garden combines beauty and efficiency to create a
magical effect. Few designs can fit more growing space into less area,
and the pattern brings a spiritual quality to a piece of ground.

## Herb Spirals

An herb spiral is a further evolution of pattern-based garden design. It
coils 20 to 30 linear feet of pathside herbs into a roundish spiral
about 5 feet across, built as a mound of soil about 3 feet high with
rocks placed in a spiral pattern from bottom to top, leaving about a
foot of soil between tiers.
