# The Stamets P Value System for Strain Age

## Why Strain Age Matters

Mushroom strains degrade over successive generations of subculturing.
As mycelium is transferred from one agar plate to another, and then to
grain spawn, and then to production substrate, genetic mutations
accumulate and vigor declines. Older strains produce smaller yields,
slower colonization, weaker immune responses to contamination, and
may fail to fruit altogether. Understanding and tracking strain age is
critical for maintaining productive mushroom cultures.

## The P Value Definition

Paul Stamets developed the "P Value" system as a practical method for
tracking the generational age of a mushroom strain through successive
subcultures. Each transfer of mycelium from one nutrient medium to
another increments the P value by one:

- **P-1**: The original isolation from a wild specimen or spore
germination. This is the youngest, most vigorous culture.
- **P-2**: First subculture from the P-1 mother culture.
- **P-3**: Second subculture from P-2.
- And so on, with each numbered transfer representing one
generational step away from the original isolation.

## Generational Expansion

The P value tracks transfers within the same medium type. When mycelium
moves to a different medium type — for example, from agar to grain
spawn — this may or may not increment the P value depending on the
specific convention used. The key principle is that each transfer
represents a cell division bottleneck: only a small fragment of the
original mycelium is carried forward, and with it goes only a subset of
the genetic diversity present in the parent culture.

## Signs of Strain Degeneration

As P values increase, several visible and measurable changes occur:

**Slower colonization**: Mycelium takes progressively longer to fully
colonize agar plates, grain jars, or production substrates. What once
took 7-10 days may stretch to 14-21 days at higher P values.

abnormal pigmentation. Sectoring — the appearance of distinct zones
with different growth characteristics on the same plate — is a classic
sign of genetic instability.
