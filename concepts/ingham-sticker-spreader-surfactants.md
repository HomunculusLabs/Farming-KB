# Sticker-Spreaders and Surfactants for Compost Tea Application

## Why Sticker-Spreaders Matter

When applying [[cervantes-compost-tea-organic-brewing]] as a [[cervantes-foliar-feeding]], the tea needs to coat
the leaf surface adequately. The leaf surface is not smooth; it has
hairs, ridges, wax layers, and other structures that can cause
liquids to bead up and run off. A sticker-spreader helps the tea
spread evenly across the leaf surface and remain in place long
enough for the organisms to establish themselves.

## Molasses as a Natural Sticker

[[ingham-molasses-types-brewing-effects]] can serve as a sticker-spreader at appropriate
concentrations. At around 5% concentration, molasses provides good
adhesion to leaf surfaces while also serving as a fungal food
source. This dual function makes molasses an attractive option for
compost tea applications. However, be aware that high concentrations
of molasses can be sticky and may clog spray equipment if not
properly managed.

## Commercial Surfactant Concerns

Many commercial sticker-spreader products contain compounds that may
be harmful to the very organisms you are applying with the tea.
Synthetic biosurfactants-in-bioremediation|surfactants, particularly those derived from petroleum
products, can damage bacterial and fungal cell membranes. Always test
any commercial product with your tea before using it at full scale.
Mix a small amount of the product with tea and check organism
activity under a microscope before and after addition.

## What Makes a Good Sticker

A good sticker-spreader for compost tea should: (1) be non-toxic to
the microorganisms in the tea, (2) help the liquid spread evenly
across the leaf surface, (3) not evaporate too quickly, (4) not
interfere with the biological activity of the tea, and (5) be
compatible with any other additives in the spray tank. Natural
products like yucca extract, saponin-based products, and molasses
generally meet these criteria better than synthetic alternatives.

## Yucca Extract

Yucca extract is a natural surfactant derived from the yucca plant.
It contains saponins, which are natural compounds that reduce surface
tension and help liquids spread more evenly. Yucca extract is
generally considered compatible with compost tea organisms and has
been used successfully by many compost tea applicators. It provides
gentle spreading action without the harsh effects of synthetic
detergents.

## Leaf Surface Considerations

The effectiveness of any sticker-spreader depends on the leaf surface
characteristics of the target plant. Waxy leaves, such as those on
many brassicas and evergreens, are more challenging to coat than
hairy or matte leaves. The age of the leaf also matters; younger
leaves typically have less wax buildup and are easier to coat.
Understanding the leaf surface biology helps in selecting the right
sticker-spreader product and application rate.

## Application Timing

Sticker-spreaders work best when applied under conditions that allow
the tea to remain on the leaf surface long enough for organisms to
establish. Avoid applying during the heat of the day when rapid
evaporation can concentrate the tea and stress organisms. Early
morning or late afternoon applications, when humidity is higher and
temperatures are moderate, provide the best conditions for
successful foliar establishment of compost tea organisms.

## Spray Coverage and Volume

Good coverage is essential for effective foliar application of compost
tea. The goal is to cover as much of the leaf surface as possible
without excessive runoff. Too little volume means poor coverage; too
much volume wastes tea and may cause runoff. The sticker-spreader
helps maximize the coverage achieved per gallon of tea applied. Most
recommendations suggest applying enough tea to just before the point
of runoff.

## Testing Compatibility

Before using any sticker-spreader with your compost tea program, test
it thoroughly. Mix the product with a small batch of tea, apply it
to a few plants, and monitor both the spray coverage and the plant
response. Check the tea under a microscope after adding the product
to verify that organisms remain active and intact. This testing
approach prevents costly mistakes on a larger scale. See also [[aact-applications-guide-ingham]].

## Fish Hydrolysate as a Sticker

Fish hydrolysate products contain enough oil to help organisms be
protected on the leaf surface while providing foods to help them
grow quickly once established. Products like Organic Gem and
Neptune's Harvest work well at the dilution rates recommended by
their manufacturers. The oil content helps the tea adhere to leaf
surfaces while the protein and nutrient content feeds the organisms
after application. This makes fish hydrolysate a dual-purpose
additive for foliar compost tea programs.

## Nufilm and Commercial Products

Nufilm is mentioned as a good sticker-spreader product for use with
compost tea, particularly when spraying during conditions that might
wash the tea off, such as during light mist rain. If hard rain
follows application, a sticker-spreader becomes even more important
to ensure the organisms remain on the leaf surface long enough to
establish. Always verify that any commercial sticker-spreader is
compatible with your tea biology before widespread use.

## The Role of Oils in Spreader-Stickers

A small amount of oil in a spreader-sticker product serves multiple
functions. The oil helps the liquid spread across waxy leaf surfaces
by reducing surface tension. It also provides a thin protective film
that slows evaporation, giving organisms more time to establish.
Additionally, oils can serve as food sources for certain fungi and
bacteria on the leaf surface. The key is using just enough oil to
aid spreading and retention without creating conditions that might
smother leaf tissue or favor undesirable organisms.

## Sprayer Tank Considerations

When adding sticker-spreaders directly to the sprayer tank rather
than the brew tank, consider the total volume and concentration.
The sticker-spreader needs to be diluted to the correct ratio in
the final spray volume. Some products are designed to be added at
very low rates, and exceeding these rates can cause problems. Follow
the manufacturer's recommendations for dilution and be aware that
your tea already contains organic materials that may interact with
the sticker-spreader product.
