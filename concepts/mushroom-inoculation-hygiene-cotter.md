# Mushroom Inoculation Hygiene and Sanitation Protocols

Inoculation is the most contamination-vulnerable stage of mushroom
cultivation. Tradd Cotter emphasizes that "it only takes one set of
dirty hands to ruin an entire day, or more, of work." This page covers
the hygiene protocols, tools, and workspace setup for safe inoculation.

## Why Hygiene Is Critical at Inoculation

The inoculation stage involves direct contact with prepared (possibly
pasteurized) growing medium and pure culture spawn. At this point:

- The medium has been heat-treated, killing competitors
- The medium is now a blank slate — vulnerable to any contaminant
- Spawn is exposed during mixing and transfer
- Every surface, tool, and hand that contacts the media is a vector
- Contamination here means total loss of the batch

## Sanitizing Agents

### 70% Isopropyl Alcohol

The standard sanitizer for mushroom cultivation:
- Spray lightly on hands before and during mixing
- Wipe tools between uses
- Sanitize gloves and surfaces
- **70% concentration is optimal** — more concentrated is actually less
  effective (water is needed to denature proteins)
- Keep spray bottles stocked at every inoculation station
- Flammable — keep away from open flames and heat sources

### Diluted Bleach (50% Strength)

- Mix household bleach 1:1 with water for a 50% dilution
- Keep a tray of bleach solution near the mixing area
- Dunk tools (rakes, scoops) rather than spraying — more efficient
- Use in spray bottles for misting hands between bag fills
- Also effective for cleaning floors and large surfaces
- Bleach solutions lose potency over hours — mix fresh daily
- Allow bleach-cleaned surfaces to dry before use (residue can harm
  mycelium)

### Tool Protocol

At Cotter's farm, all tools stay in the inoculation area permanently:
- Bags, hole pokers, and alcohol bottles never leave the area
- Wash with fresh water and sanitize with alcohol after each use
- This prevents cross-contamination between species and batches
- A rake left in the clean zone touching only media/spawn needs no
  cleaning mid-batch
- If a tool leaves the clean space, sanitize before returning it

## Hand Hygiene

### During Mixing

- Hands may contact substrate directly if clean
- Spray hands lightly with 70% isopropyl alcohol before starting
- Re-sanitize between batches or if hands touch non-clean surfaces
- Alternatively, use diluted bleach spray on hands

### Glove Use

- **Latex gloves:** disposable, good dexterity, single-use
- **Tight-fitting rubber gloves:** reusable, more dexterity, Cotter's
  preference to minimize disposable products
- Loose gloves reduce dexterity and can snag on substrate
- Sanitize gloves between batches just like bare hands
- Replace torn or damaged gloves immediately

## Setting Up an Inoculation Room

### Physical Requirements

Converted sheds or dedicated rooms work well:
- **Floor:** sweepable surface — concrete or professional landscaping
  fabric
- **Airflow:** minimize air movement to reduce spore transfer
- **Incoming air:** prefilter with particulate filter (HEPA ideal)
- **Partitions:** plastic curtains or walls to separate work zones
- **Location:** adjacent to pasteurization/sterilization room to minimize
  exposure time during transfer

### Clean Room vs. Open Inoculation

**Pasteurized substrates:**
- Can inoculate in a nonsterile environment
- Clean surface required (bleached tarp or table)
- Much easier to work with than sterilized media
- Lower cost and energy input
- Most oyster mushrooms and some other species thrive on pasteurized
  substrate

**Sterilized substrates:**
- Require a clean room with HEPA filtration
- Full laboratory protocols needed
- See Cotter chapter 17 for clean room design
- Necessary for shiitake, maitake, and many other species
- Much more prone to contamination than pasteurized media

### Ergonomics and Workflow

- Surfaces should be easy to clean and hold significant weight
- Chlorinated tarp, concrete floor, or waterproof table
- Pre-stage all materials before starting (gloves, alcohol, twist ties)
- Minimize distance between pasteurization and inoculation areas
- Every second of exposure increases contamination risk

## Inoculation Technique

### Small-Scale: Tarp Method

For shredded straw with oyster mushrooms:

1. Allow media to cool after pasteurization on a clean, washed tarp
2. Break spawn into smallest fragments possible
3. Broadcast spawn evenly across the media surface
4. Lightly rake spawn into the substrate
5. For larger volumes, add media and spawn in alternating layers
6. Fill containers immediately after mixing

### Mixing with Tools

- Spread media in even layer, 3–4 inches deep
- Use a **dedicated rake** (never used for yard/garden work)
- Dunk rake in 50% bleach tray between uses
- Push inoculated media into a pile for easier bagging
- If rake touches anything outside the clean zone, sanitize before
  returning
