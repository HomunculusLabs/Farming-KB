## Historical Context

Cannabis has been used for recreational and medicinal purposes for 10,000
years or more. Professor John Graham predicted in 1976 that "a limited but
respectable niche will be established for it in therapeutics by the end
of the century." Human interaction and coevolution with cannabis over
millennia created a complex, heterogeneous medicine by selecting plants
that provide maximal benefits and minimal side effects.

## Qualifying Conditions

The medical indications of marijuana are manifold:

- **Glaucoma** - reduces intraocular pressure
- **Muscle spasticity** - particularly [[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]] conditions
- **Nausea and vomiting** - opposes side effects of cancer chemotherapy
- **Appetite enhancement** - helps people with AIDS and wasting syndrome
- **Anxiety and depression** - widely recognized therapeutic benefit
- **Neuroprotection** - research shows it can prevent death of injured
  neurons

## DEA Scheduling Conflict

Despite marijuana"s unambiguous medical benefits, the US Drug Enforcement
Administration (DEA) classifies marijuana as a prohibited Schedule I drug
("no currently accepted medical use"). However, THC, the primary active
ingredient, is classified as a synthetic Schedule III drug (dronabinol,
Marinol). The DEA"s hypocritical classification is rejected by many
Americans.

Currently (at time of publication) ten US states allowed patients to
possess marijuana for medical use, contingent upon a physician's
recommendation, but possession remains illegal under Federal law. Federal
officers have raided medical marijuana patients with valid state
prescriptions.

## Herbal vs. Synthetic Medicine

The DEA and FDA criticize herbal medicines because plants contain a
variable mix of compounds. The pharmaceutical industry favors synthetic,
single-ingredient "silver bullets" that target solitary metabolic pathways.
Herbalists counter with two advantages of herbal remedies:

1. Multiple constituents may work by multiple mechanisms to improve
   therapeutic activity in a cumulative or synergistic manner
2. Herbs may contain compounds that mitigate side effects of their
   predominant active ingredients

Marijuana has been characterized as a "synergistic shotgun," in contrast
with synthetic, single-ingredient "silver bullets." The many ingredients
in marijuana modulate health via several metabolic pathways, gently
nudging the system toward homeostasis.
