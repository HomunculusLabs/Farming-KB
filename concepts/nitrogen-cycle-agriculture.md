## Overview

[[biological-nitrogen-fixation-agriculture]] converts atmospheric N₂ (triple bond, 941 kJ/mol) into NH₃ via **nitrogenase** (MoFe protein + Fe protein, *nifHDK* genes), requiring 16 ATP per N₂. **Symbiotic fixation** by *Rhizobium* and *Bradyrhizobium* spp. in legume root nodules contributes 50–300 kg N ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ (soybean) or 25–100 kg N ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ (grain legumes). Nodules contain **leghemoglobin** to maintain optimal O₂ (~3–10 nM). Other symbioses include *Frankia* (actinorhizal, *Alnus*, *Casuarina*) and *Nostoc/Anabaena* in *Azolla* fronds (rice paddies, ~20–40 kg N ha⁻¹ per season). Free-living fixers (*Azotobacter*, *Azospirillum*, cyanobacteria) contribute 5–30 kg N ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹. Total terrestrial biological fixation: ~100 Tg N yr⁻¹.

### Mineralization and Immobilization

Heterotrophic microorganisms decompose organic N (SOM, residues, manure) into NH₄⁺ using proteases, deaminases, nucleases, and ureases. Rate depends on temperature (Q₁₀ ~1.5–2.5), moisture (~50–60% WFPS), substrate quality, and pH (optimal 6.5–8.0). **Immobilization** — microbial assimilation of inorganic N into biomass — competes with plant uptake and is favored by high C:N ratio residues (>25–30:1). Net mineralization occurs at C:N < 20:1; net immobilization at C:N > 30:1.

### Nitrification

Aerobic oxidation of NH₄⁺ to NO₃⁻ via two steps. **Step 1** (ammonia oxidation): ammonia monooxygenase (AMO) in *Nitrosomonas*, *Nitrosospira* (AOB), *Nitrososphaera* (AOA), or comammox bacteria (*Nitrospira inopinata*, discovered 2015) converts NH₃ to NO₂⁻. **Step 2** (nitrite oxidation): *Nitrobacter* (r-strategist) and *Nitrospira* (K-strategist) oxidize NO₂⁻ to NO₃⁻ via nitrite oxidoreductase (NXR). Optimal conditions: pH 7.5–8.0, 25–35°C, 50–60% WFPS. Each mole of NH₄⁺ nitrified releases 2H⁺, contributing to soil acidification.

### Denitrification

Facultative anaerobes (*Pseudomonas*, *Paracoccus*, *Bradyrhizobium*) reduce NO₃⁻ → NO₂⁻ → NO → N₂O → N₂ using nitrate reductase (NAR), nitrite reductase (NirK/NirS), nitric oxide reductase (NOR), and [[plant-available-nitrogen-ammonium-nitrate]] (34% N) provides both NH₄⁺ and NO₃⁻ immediately. UAN solutions (28–32% N) combine urea-N, NH₄⁺-N, and NO₃⁻-N for flexible application. Anhydrous ammonia (82% N) requires injection equipment. Ammonium sulfate (21% N + 24% S) acidifies soil. Enhanced-efficiency fertilizers include polymer-coated urea, nitrification inhibitors (DCD, DMPP, nitrapyrin reducing N₂O by 20–50%), and urease inhibitors (NBPT reducing NH₃ volatilization by 30–70%).

## Nitrogen Use Efficiency

### Metrics

Agronomic efficiency (AE) = (Y_N − Y₀) / N_applied, typically 10–30 kg grain/kg N. Recovery efficiency (RE) = (U_N − U₀) / N_applied × 100, typically 30–65%. Physiological efficiency (PE) = (Y_N − Y₀) / (U_N − U₀), typically 40–80 kg grain/kg N. Partial factor productivity (PFP) = Y_N / N_applied, typically 40–80 kg grain/kg N. Global cereal NUE averages ~50%, meaning half of applied N is lost.

### Crop-Specific Values

Wheat RE: 40–60% at 120–250 kg N/ha. Flooded rice RE: 30–50% at 100–200 kg N/ha. Maize RE: 40–65% at 150–250 kg N/ha. NUE has declined in China and India despite yield gains, due to excessive application rates. Global cropland receives ~120 Tg N/yr; ~60 Tg N/yr is lost to the environment.

## Plant Nitrogen Uptake and Assimilation

### Transporters

Nitrate uptake via NRT1/NPF family (low-affinity, e.g., NRT1.1/CHL1 with dual-affinity switching at Thr101) and NRT2 family (high-affinity, e.g., NRT2.1 requiring NAR2.1 accessory protein). Ammonium uptake via AMT1/AMT2 families (Km ~10–100 µM), subject to trans-inhibition by C-terminal phosphorylation. Most crops prefer NO₃⁻; rice is relatively NH₄⁺-tolerant.

### GS/GOGAT Cycle and Beyond

NH₄⁺ enters the GS/GOGAT cycle: glutamine synthetase (GS1 cytosolic, GS2 chloroplastic) combines NH₄⁺ + glutamate → glutamine; glutamate synthase (NADH-GOGAT in roots, NADPH/Fd-GOGAT in leaves) converts glutamine + 2-oxoglutarate → 2 glutamate. For NO₃⁻, nitrate reductase (NR, cytosolic, Mo cofactor) reduces NO₃⁻ → NO₂⁻, then nitrite reductase (NiR, chloroplastic, siroheme) reduces NO₂⁻ → NH₄⁺. Glutamate donates amino groups via transaminases to produce aspartate, alanine, and other amino acids. Asparagine synthetase produces asparagine, a major N transport form.

### Remobilization During Grain Filling

Fifty to ninety percent of grain N comes from vegetative remobilization, not current uptake. Leaf proteins (especially Rubisco, 25–50% of soluble protein) are degraded by proteases into amino acids transported via phloem as glutamine, asparagine, and arginine. Stay-green traits (e.g., *sgR* in rice) extend assimilation and improve grain protein. Wheat grain protein target: 11–13%.

## Environmental Impacts

N₂O has a GWP₁₀₀ of 273 (IPCC AR6), atmospheric lifetime ~114 years, current concentration ~336 ppb. Agriculture emits ~60% of anthropogenic N₂O (~3.8 Tg N₂O-N yr⁻¹). Nitrate leaching contaminates groundwater (methemoglobinemia risk). Combined N and P enrichment causes eutrophication; >500 coastal dead zones exist globally (Gulf of Mexico averages ~14,000 km²). Ammonia volatilization (~50 Tg N yr⁻¹ from agriculture) forms secondary PM₂.₅. Soil acidification from nitrification requires 20–60 kg CaCO₃/ha/yr for neutralization under intensive fertilization.
