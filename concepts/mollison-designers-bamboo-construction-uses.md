Bamboo is one of the most versatile and rapidly renewable building materials
available to permaculture designers. In the Permaculture Designers Manual,
Mollison highlights bamboo as a strategic plant for both production and
construction, noting its extraordinary growth rate (some species grow up to
1 meter per day), its tensile strength comparable to steel, and its
suitability for a vast range of structural and non-structural applications.
Bamboo serves simultaneously as a building material, erosion control plant,
windbreak element, fodder source, and food crop, making it a quintessential
multi-function element in [[mollison-designers-windbreak-design-species]] due to its dense clumping
habit. The designer should select species that match both the construction
requirements and the broader site design objectives.
## Harvesting and Treatment
### Harvest Timing
The timing of bamboo harvest significantly affects durability. Mollison
recommends harvesting bamboo during the dry season when sap content is
lowest, as starch-rich bamboo is more susceptible to borer attack. Bamboo
should be harvested at 3 to 5 years of age, when culms have reached maximum
density and strength. Culms younger than 2 years are too soft and
susceptible to decay; those older than 5 years begin to deteriorate from
the inside. Selective harvesting of individual mature culms from clumps,
rather than clear-cutting, ensures continuous production.
### Preservation Treatment
Untreated bamboo has a service life of only 2 to 5 years in exposed
conditions. Treatment dramatically extends this. Mollison describes several
methods. The most common is a boron-based preservative treatment, where
culms are immersed in or pressure-injected with a solution of borax and
boric acid. This treatment is non-toxic, effective against insects and
fungi, and relatively simple to apply. Alternatively, the traditional
smoking method, where culms are cured over a fire in a sealed chamber,
deposits creosote-like compounds that deter insects. For ground-contact
applications, a combination of boron treatment and a protective barrier
such as concrete footings is recommended.
### Seasoning and Storage
After treatment, bamboo must be properly seasoned to prevent cracking and
warping. Culms should be stored vertically or horizontally on raised racks
in a well-ventilated, shaded area for 4 to 8 weeks. Direct sun exposure
during seasoning causes rapid moisture loss and splitting. Once seasoned,
bamboo should be stored under cover, protected from rain and ground moisture,
until needed for construction.
## Non-Construction Uses
Beyond building, bamboo serves many functions in permaculture systems. Young
shoots of many species are edible and nutritious, providing a seasonal vegetable
crop. Bamboo leaves are valuable fodder for [[mollison-designers-house-garden-integration]], bamboo provides immediate building
materials for sheds, trellises, fences, and garden structures. In Zone 5
and conservation areas, native bamboo species stabilize stream banks and
provide wildlife habitat.
