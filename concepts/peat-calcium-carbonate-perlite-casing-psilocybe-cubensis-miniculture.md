# Peat-Calcium Carbonate-Perlite Casing Layer for Psilocybe Cubensis Miniculture

## Overview

The casing layer is a non-nutritive covering applied to colonized grain substrate
to initiate and sustain fruiting in *Psilocybe cubensis*. Bigwood and Beug (1982)
employed a casing mixture of peat, calcium carbonate, and perlite and/or vermiculite
in their miniculture system, which supported continuous fruiting for over 20 weeks.
The casing layer serves multiple functions: providing moisture reservoir, buffering
pH, maintaining gas exchange, and creating the microclimate necessary for primordia
formation and [[cervantes-organic-mineral-amendments]] improve
  aeration and water management. Perlite provides drainage and air space,
  while vermiculite retains additional moisture. The combination creates a
  well-structured casing with both water-holding capacity and gas exchange.

The casing was applied approximately 2 cm deep over the colonized rye grain in
half-pint (~250 ml) wide-mouth jars.

## Role of Each Component

### Peat Moss

Peat moss provides the bulk of the casing layer and its primary functions:

- **Water reservoir**: Peat absorbs and slowly releases water, maintaining
  consistent surface moisture without waterlogging. This is critical for
  primordia formation, which requires high humidity at the substrate surface.

- **Microbial substrate**: Peat supports a community of bacteria and
  microorganisms that may contribute to the fruiting trigger. Some researchers
  have proposed that bacteria in the casing layer produce volatile organic
  compounds that stimulate fruiting.

- **Structure**: The fibrous nature of peat creates a loose, open structure
  that allows gas exchange between the substrate and the atmosphere.

### Calcium Carbonate

The buffering function of calcium carbonate is essential:

- **pH neutralization**: Without buffering, the peat's natural acidity inhibits
  mycelial growth at the surface and suppresses primordia formation.
- **Calcium nutrition**: Calcium ions may play a role in cell wall integrity
  and membrane stability in developing fruiting bodies.
- **Microbial environment**: Near-neutral pH favors [[cation-exchange-capacity]].
