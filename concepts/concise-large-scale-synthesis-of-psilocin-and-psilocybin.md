# Concise Large-Scale Synthesis of Psilocin and Psilocybin

Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) developed concise large-scale syntheses of [[psilocin]] and [[psilocybin]], the principal hallucinogenic constituents of "magic mushrooms," achieved **without chromatographic purification**. Published in the Journal of Natural Products.

## Background

### Psilocin and Psilocybin
- **Psilocin (1)**: 4-hydroxy-N,N-[[dimethyltryptamine]]
- **Psilocybin (2)**: 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine
- Principal psychoactive constituents of Psilocybe mushrooms
- Closely resemble the neurotransmitter [[serotonin]]
- Hallucinogenic effect "probably caused by their interference with the normal actions of brain serotonin"

### Regulatory Context
Since June 6, 2002, fungi containing psilocin and psilocybin have been regulated by the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law in Japan.

### Motivation
- Identification of magic mushrooms using morphologic/microscopic analysis is "quite difficult without experts"
- Chromatographic methods (TLC, GC, HPLC) are usually employed and require standard compounds
- Difficult to isolate psilocin and psilocybin from mushrooms on gram scale (psilocin easily decomposes, psilocybin has high polarity)

## Synthetic Route

### Starting Material
Commercially available **4-hydroxyindole** (3)

### Key Steps
1. **Protection**: Hydroxyl group protection by acetylation (simpler than benzyl ether protection, which requires a separate deprotection step)
2. **Oxalylation**: Treatment with oxalyl chloride to afford compound 5 as yellow crystals
3. **Phosphorylation**: The key step — using the pyrophosphate method with tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-BuLi

### The Key Discovery: Zwitterionic Intermediate
During the phosphorylation of psilocin, a critical observation was made:
- After aqueous workup, the NMR spectrum showed complicated signals
- TLC showed an additional spot at the origin — whitish material that no longer dissolved in CH₂Cl₂
- This material was identified as a **zwitterionic O-monobenzyl phosphate** (compound 8)

### Structural Elucidation of the Zwitterion (8)
The zwitterionic structure was confirmed by comprehensive 2D NMR analysis:
- **¹H and ¹³C NMR** (in CD₃OD): Showed signals for two sets of benzyl groups and a psilocin core
- One benzyl methylene shifted to high field (δH 4.56, 2H, s) compared to the other (δH 4.98, 1H, s; 4.96, 1H, s)
- N,N-dimethyl methylene signals shifted to low field compared to psilocin itself
- **³¹P NMR**: Confirmed presence of phosphate moiety
- **HMBC experiment**: Revealed one benzyl group was directly linked at the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethyl part (quaternary ammonium ion)
- **NOESY spectrum**: Supported the linkage assignments

### Significance
This zwitterionic intermediate represents **intramolecular conversion of the benzyl-bearing sites** on the dibenzyl-protected intermediate. The discovery was essential for understanding the phosphorylation pathway and achieving the final deprotection to psilocybin.

## Synthesis Highlights

- **No chromatographic purification required** — a major advantage for large-scale production
- **Concise route** from commercially available starting materials
- The pyrophosphate method was selected over Mitsunobu-type conditions (diphenyl phosphite, I₂, DMAP) for "easy handling and reagent stability"
- Suitable for producing gram-scale quantities of both psilocin and psilocybin as analytical standards

## Chemical Classification
- **Minor constituents**: Baeocystin and norbaeocystin are often present alongside psilocin and psilocybin in Psilocybe mushrooms
- **Mechanism**: Similar to LSD — interference with brain serotonin receptors
