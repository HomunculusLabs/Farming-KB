## Dorothy Retallack's Controlled Experiments

Dorothy Retallack, a professional organist who enrolled at Temple Buell
College, conducted the most rigorous Western experiments on music and
plants. Using Biotronic Mark III Environmental Chambers with precise
control of light, temperature, and humidity, she tested multiple musical
genres:

**Rock Music:** Plants exposed to Led Zeppelin, Vanilla Fudge, and Jimi
Hendrix leaned away from the speakers. When pots were rotated 180
degrees, plants leaned in the opposite direction. All marigolds died
within two weeks. Roots averaged only about one inch versus four times
that length in the classical group.

**Classical Music (Bach, Beethoven, Brahms):** Summer squashes grew
toward the radio, one twining around it. Plants leaned 35 degrees
toward Bach preludes.

**Indian Classical (Ravi Shankar):** Plants bent more than halfway to
horizontal -- over 60 degrees -- the nearest "almost embracing the
speaker."

**Jazz (Ellington, Armstrong):** 55% of plants leaned 15-20 degrees
toward the speaker with more abundant growth than silent controls.

**Country-Western:** No more reaction than the silent chamber.

## Percussive vs. Melodic Elements

Retallack tested the percussive hypothesis by playing "La Paloma" on
steel drums versus strings. The percussion caused a 10-degree lean away;
the strings produced a 15-degree lean toward the source. She concluded
that the percussive component in rock music was a key harmful factor.

## Evaporation Rates

Different musical styles affected water evaporation dramatically:
silent chambers lost 14-17 ml; Bach, Shankar, and jazz caused 20-25 ml;
but rock music caused 55-59 ml to evaporate -- plants under rock were
using much more water while apparently "enjoying it less."
