# N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) — Botanical Sources and Pharmacology

## Overview

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a potent naturally occurring
[[mckenna-tryptamine-hallucinogen-classification-four-groups-plants]] found in numerous plant species worldwide.
Indigenous peoples of the Amazon have used DMT for spiritual and
medicinal purposes for thousands of years. DMT is unique among
hallucinogens for its extremely [[savory-short-duration-grazing-and-cell-systems]] of action when
smoked and its requirement for monoamine oxidase inhibition
to be orally active.

## Chemical Properties

DMT is a simple tryptamine derivative (N,N-dimethyltryptamine)
structurally related to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) and the
neurotransmitter bufotenin. It acts primarily as a serotonin
5-HT2A receptor agonist, the same receptor subtype targeted by
LSD and psilocin.

## Routes of Administration

### Smoking/Inhalation
Smoked DMT rapidly intoxicates with peak effects within 2-5
minutes, clearing over the next 20-30 minutes. Typically 40-50 mg
is smoked, with anecdotal reports ranging from 20-100 mg. The
brevity of the experience led to the nickname "businessman's trip"
in the 1960s.

### Intravenous
Effective dosage range estimated at 0.1-0.4 mg/kg. At 0.6 mg/kg,
the experience was described as "too much" with difficulty
recalling the experience (Strassman, 2001).

### Oral (with MAOI)
DMT is orally active only when monoamine oxidase enzymes in the
gut and liver are inhibited. Without inhibition, DMT is rapidly
metabolized and rendered inactive before reaching systemic
circulation. This is the basis of [[schultes-ayahuasca-preparations]].

### Other Routes
DMT can also be administered as an enema, snuffed (as in South
American yopo/cohoba preparations from [[yopo-anadenanthera-peregrina-snuff]]),
or injected.

## Ayahuasca

The most well-known DMT preparation is ayahuasca, a brewed tea
combining DMT-containing plants (typically [[chacruna-psychotria-viridis]]
leaves) with MAOI-containing plants (typically Banisteriopsis
caapi vine). The brew imparts a 3-4 hour intoxication peaking
within the first hour.

### Composition
An average 100 mL dose of ayahuasca contains approximately:
- 24 mg DMT (from Psychotria viridis, 0.1-0.66% dry weight)
- 170 mg harmine (from [[ayahuasca-and-banisteriopsis-caapi]], 0.05-1.95% dry weight)
- 20 mg harmaline
- 107 mg 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroharmine

### Beta-Carboline MAOIs
The MAOIs in ayahuasca are beta-carboline compounds — harmaline,
harmine, and tetrahydroharmine — all reported to have mild sedative
and hallucinogenic properties of their own (Pennes & Hoch, 1957;
Shulgin & Shulgin, 1997).

## Botanical Sources in the United States

### Phalaris Species (Canary Grass)
DMT can be obtained through simple juice extraction of Phalaris
arundinacea, P. tuberosa, or P. aquatica — common grasses found
in fallow fields, sidewalk cracks, and lawns across the entire
United States. DMT content is highly variable in Phalaris spp.,
but high-percentage strains can be ordered through the Internet.

### Desmanthus illinoensis (Prairie Bundleflower)
This prairie legume has wide American distribution. [[iboga-root-bark-preparation]]
contains approximately 0.34% DMT (Thompson et al., 1987).
