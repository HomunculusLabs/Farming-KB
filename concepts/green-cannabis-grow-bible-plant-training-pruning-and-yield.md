# Plant Training, Pruning, and Yield Maximization
Chapter 11 of [[greg-green]]'s Cannabis Grow Bible covers the art and science
of plant care techniques including pruning, training, light bending,
thinning, and strategies for increasing yield.
## Understanding Plant Structure
The cannabis plant develops in three noticeable sections:
- Top cola: Develops a large amount of bud, very flower-dense.
- Mid-section: Contains bud, stems, and leaves. Buds grow between
  nodes near the stem and are generally smaller than the main cola but
  just as potent.
- Bottom section: Large fan leaves and small amounts of bud. Small
  leaves may yellow and fall away.
Each section can be treated differently to maximize overall production.
## Fan Leaf Management
Fan leaves collect light for plant growth but are generally not smoked
and are the least potent part of the plant. Key rules:
- Do not cut away healthy fan leaves. They produce sugar used in bud
  production. Removing them may stunt bud growth and reduce harvest.
- Only remove fan leaves that are dying, badly burnt, or covering a
  large bud mass (preventing light from reaching it).
- In SOG or ScrOG setups, you may need to remove a fan leaf that is
  blocking light from another plant's bud mass.
- Remove dead leaves from the bottom and throw them away. Never leave
  dead leaves on soil as fertilizer, as they attract pests.
## Thinning
Thinning maintains uniform plant height across the grow area. When
plants are at different heights, light distribution becomes uneven:
- Uniform height allows optimal light positioning for all plants.
- Racy (overly tall) plants should be cut down to the same level as
  others (pruning) or removed entirely.
- Cuttings from thinning can be cloned into new plants.
- Taller plants are generally male and shorter ones female when growing
  from seed. Consider this before removing shorter plants.
- Also address dead leaves and mold build-up during thinning.
## Light Bending
Plants bend toward light sources. Excessive bending causes problems:
- Bent plants may grow into neighboring plants, blocking their light.
- Heavy buds during flowering can topple the plant and pot.
- Solutions: Rotate plants daily, move leaning plants toward the
  middle, or turn them around.
- In fixed setups (hydroponics + ScrOG), tie plants up for support.
- Outdoors, cut surrounding foliage or use bamboo stakes and thread.
- If plants are bending, they are telling you they need more direct
  light.
## Pruning (Topping)
Pruning involves removing the top of the plant's main stem above the
last branch formation during the 3rd or 4th week of vegetative growth:
- The main stem splits into two or more directions, creating a V shape.
- Result: two or more top colas instead of one.
- Some growers have produced more than six top colas this way.
- Each strain has a genetic threshold for bud production that cannot be
  exceeded. Some strains benefit from topping, others do not.
- Example: Blueberry will not reach maximum bud production without
  topping, while other strains share the same volume between two colas
  that a single cola would have produced.
### Pruning Guidelines
- Experiment with 2 out of 10 females per grow.
- Plants are generally pruned at 3 to 4 weeks of vegetative growth but
  can be pruned sooner, later, or more than once.
- Pruning during flowering diverts energy from bud production into
  branch and leaf growth, slowing bud development.
- For every stem or branch pruned, the cut area develops two more
  branches (like tree branching patterns).
- Prune cuts are made at a 45-degree angle to the shoot being cut.
- Learning to prune correctly allows creating small bushy Sativa
  plants that fit in tiny spaces.
- There is a genetically determined limit to how many times you can
  subdivide branches. Not all strains respond the same.
## Bushes
Creating a bushy plant through staged pruning:
1. During week 3 of vegetative growth, prune half the plant's branches.
   Never remove all branches. Cannabis needs at least 50% of its
   leaves to continue growing without fatal stunting.
2. Prune per side to achieve 50%. You may also prune the main top cola.
3. During weeks 4 or 5, prune the other half (only if previous cuts
   have grown new branches and leaves).
4. By week 7, the plant will be growing outward more than upward.
A plant with 8 shoots (4 nodes high) can potentially be pushed to 32
shoots through progressive pruning, though this is strain-dependent.
Each node should produce bud during flowering. With the right strain,
a single plant can cover an entire desk using this method.
## Training
Training is the art of tying down the main stem to control height:
- Attach thread to the stem and bend the plant over, locking the thread
  to another stem part or the grow room frame.
- Plants can be shaped into S-curves, corkscrews, or even full circles.
- Some growers train plants to grow horizontally during vegetative
  stage, then move the light above during flowering for thicker bud
  growth along the main stem.
### Repairing Stem Breaks
If a stem breaks during training:
1. Hold the break in place with a stake or stick support.
2. Bind with cheesecloth or a cloth bandage wrap with pores.
3. Apply honey to the wound to speed healing.
4. Watch for new growth at the break area and trim it away, as it will
  try to break away the upper stem (effectively topping the plant).
