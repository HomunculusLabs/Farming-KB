# Psilocybe cubensis: Cattle Dung Substrate and Introduction in the Americas

[[psilocybe-cubensis]] is one of the most widely distributed and well-known
[[ethnobotanical-use-of-neurotropic-fungi-in-mexico]] the world. According to the comprehensive survey by
[[four-groups-neurotropic-fungi-global-distribution-guzman-1998]] classification, which are predominantly
lignicolous or humicolous.

## Distribution in the Americas

The Guzmán, Allen & Gartz survey documents P. cubensis from virtually every
tropical and subtropical region of the Americas:

### Mexico

P. cubensis has one of the most extensive publication histories of any
[[hypholoma-naematoliformis-endemic-mexican-neurotropic-fungi-guzman-1998]] fungus, documented by Singer (1949, 1959, 1978), Heim
(1956a, 1956b, 1957a, 1958a, 1958b, 1978), Wasson (1957), and dozens of
subsequent researchers. The species is found in the tropical lowlands of
southern and eastern Mexico, particularly in the states of Oaxaca, Chiapas,
Veracruz, and Tabasco. Its presence in Mexico predates the formal
documentation of the species.

### Central America

Documented throughout the Central American isthmus:
- **Guatemala**: Recorded by Guzmán (1983) and Torres (1984)
- **Costa Rica**: Documented by Sáenz et al. (1983) and Guzmán (1995)
- **Belize**: Listed by Heim (1956b, 1978) and Singer & Smith (1958)
- **El Salvador**: P. subcubensis recorded by Guzmán (1983)
- **Honduras**: P. subcubensis documented by Guzmán (1983, 1997)
- **Panama**: P. caerulescens and P. dumontii recorded by Guzmán (1983)

### Caribbean

P. cubensis is widespread throughout the Caribbean islands:
- **Cuba**: The type locality, documented by Earle (1906), Heim (1956b,
  1978), Singer & Smith (1958), Guzmán (1983), and Stamets (1996)
- **Puerto Rico**: Recorded by Heim (1956b, 1978), Singer & Smith (1958),
  and Navarro & Betancourt (1992)
- **Dominican Republic**: Documented by Rodríguez-Gallart (1989) and Guzmán
  (1995)
- **Trinidad**: Recorded by Singer & Smith (1958) and Dennis (1970)
- **Jamaica, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Granada**: All with confirmed records

### South America

The species is found across tropical South America:
- **Brazil**: Recorded by Rick (1961), Guzmán (1983), Stijve & de Meijer
  (1993), and Pegler (1997)
- **Colombia**: Documented by Heim (1978), Guzmán (1983), and Pulido (1983)
- **Venezuela**: Recorded by Dennis (1970) and Guzmán (1983)
- **Bolivia**: Documented by Singer & Smith (1958), Dennis (1970), and
  Guzmán (1983)
- **Ecuador**: Listed by Guzmán (1983)
- **Peru**: Recorded by Repke et al. (1977a) and Gartz (1996)
- **Argentina**: Documented by Singer & Smith (1958), Singer (1960b), and
  Guzmán (1983)
- **French Guiana**: Recorded by Courtecuisse et al. (1996)

## The Cattle Introduction Hypothesis

A critical question addressed implicitly in the Guzmán, Allen & Gartz paper
concerns whether P. cubensis is native to the Americas or was introduced
following the arrival of cattle. The timeline is significant:

1. Cattle were introduced to the Americas by Spanish colonists beginning in
   the early 16th century, with the first imports to the Caribbean and
   Mexico around 1493-1521
2. Before European contact, the Americas lacked bovine cattle; the only
   large herbivores providing comparable dung would have been bison in
   [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] and camelids (llamas, alpacas) in South America
3. P. cubensis shows a strong preference for bovine dung over other dung
   types

originated in Africa, where bovine cattle and their ancestors have existed
for millions of years alongside a rich coprophilous [[air-pollution-fungal-community-responses]]. The
species could have been transported to the Americas during the Atlantic
slave trade, either in contaminated dung, bedding straw, or on the cattle
