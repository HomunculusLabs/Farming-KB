# Earthworm Breeding

Earthworms are among the most important helpers in every garden. Breeding them is inexpensive, requires very little time, and produces high-quality compost while disposing of organic waste.

## Earthworm Species

Three main species complement each other in their work:

| Species | Habitat | Function |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Brandling worm (Eisenia foetida) | Epigeal — lives on soil surface | Processes large amounts of organic material, provides the best compost. Dark red with distinctive yellow bands. |
| Red earthworm (Lumbricus rubellus) | Surface in youth, burrows into deeper layers as adult | Aerates soil, produces nutrient-rich compost. No distinctive banding. |
| Common earthworm (Lumbricus terrestris) | Creates burrows at depths up to 3 metres | Creates drainage system, aerates soil deeply. The "typical" earthworm. |

## Benefits of Earthworms

- Tunnels work as an ingenious drainage system
- Soil retains more moisture, does not dry out quickly
- Better protected from surface erosion
- Plant roots can extend through earthworm tunnels more easily
- Earthworm casts contain much more nitrogen, potassium, phosphorus and calcium than the best garden soil
- Crumbly consistency provides good soil structure
- Vegetation develops much better — plants are healthier and more disease resistant

## Breeding Setup

### Small Scale (Wooden Box)

A wooden box with a capacity of one cubic metre is enough.

### Substrate

A mixture of:
- Straw
- Cardboard
- Soil
- A little dung
- Natural fabrics (cotton, hemp, etc.)
- Layers of branches, leaves and roots incorporated for aeration

The soil should be loose and well aerated.

### Feeding

- Any cooking waste can be used as food
- Onions and garlic should be avoided — worms do not seem to like them
- Worms particularly like used coffee filters complete with coffee grounds
- Provide a regular supply of organic matter
- Adjust the amount of food to the number of earthworms
- If worms can break down food as quickly as new food accumulates, the rate is optimal
- This prevents harmful build-ups of mould

### Environmental Conditions

- Room temperature is ideal
- Steady balance of moisture needed — soil should be neither completely dry nor completely wet
- Too much water makes worms pale
- Good supply of oxygen essential
- Drill holes in the bottom of the worm box to avoid water buildup
- Protect from UV rays — earthworms are sensitive to them

### Monitoring

Observe the worms regularly. You will quickly recognise whether or not they are comfortable in their environment. Intuition is an important factor in creating optimal conditions.

### Greenhouse Method

In greenhouses, worms can be bred directly in the soil instead of boxes: cover the substrate with earth, put worms in a pile, make a shallow depression in the middle, put fresh organic waste there each day and cover with a few handfuls of soil. Feeding every second or third day is enough if well ventilated.

## Using Worm Products

- Scatter bred worms along with soil and worm eggs over new terraces and raised beds (in the evening, during wet weather)
- Use nutrient-rich, fine crumbly worm humus for especially valuable and demanding plants
- Also suitable for flowers on balconies

## Promoting Earthworms Naturally

- Maintain permanent soil cover (mixed crop selected to avoid large areas being harvested all at once)
- Mulch provides soil cover and attracts earthworms
- A healthy soil with permanent cover will naturally support large earthworm populations
