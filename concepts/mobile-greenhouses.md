# Mobile Greenhouses

The mobile greenhouse is a key innovation in Eliot Coleman's [[cold-house-winter-harvest-system]] system,
reviving a concept with roots in nineteenth-century European horticulture. Rather than
keeping a greenhouse permanently in one location, the mobile [[coleman-mobile-greenhouse-crop-rotation-soil-health]] greenhouse can be moved
between two (or more) sites, allowing the same structure to serve different purposes
in different seasons. This approach solves several persistent problems in year-round
greenhouse production simultaneously. (see [[chickshaw-and-poultry-tractors]]).

## Historical Origins

The first mobile greenhouse was built in 1898 in England, where a large glasshouse
was supported on railroad wheels running on steel rails. This allowed the structure
to be moved along tracks to cover different sections of growing ground. The concept
was practical but expensive, requiring iron framework and rail infrastructure. Coleman
adapted the idea using modern materials — lightweight pipe-frame hoop houses on
plastic film coverings — to create an affordable version suitable for small-scale
market farming. (see [[coleman-double-cover-microclimate-protection]]).

## How It Works

In Coleman's system, the mobile greenhouse sits on two adjacent growing areas. During
the summer, the greenhouse covers heat-loving crops such as tomatoes, peppers,
cucumbers, eggplants, or sweet potatoes. Meanwhile, winter-harvest crops — spinach,
carrots, leeks, Asian greens — are sown outdoors in the adjacent field where the
greenhouse will move. These crops grow through the late summer and fall without any
protection, reaching near-maturity by mid-October.

When the summer crops finish and cold weather approaches, the entire greenhouse is
physically moved — on skids, runners, or wheels — to cover the winter crops. The
transition typically occurs in mid to late October in coastal Maine. The following
spring, after the winter crops are harvested, the greenhouse is moved back to the
original site, now planted with a new round of summer crops. This annual migration
means each growing site is covered by the greenhouse for only part of the year.

## Advantages Over Permanent Greenhouses

### Pest and Disease Break

One of the most significant advantages of the mobile system is the natural break in
pest and disease cycles. When the greenhouse moves away, the soil is exposed to sun,
rain, wind, snow, and freezing temperatures for six months or more. This exposure
kills or drives away many greenhouse pests (whiteflies, aphids, spider mites) and
reduces pathogen populations that build up in the warm, humid, sheltered environment
of a permanent greenhouse. Soilborne diseases that plague permanent greenhouse
operations — such as various root rots and wilts — are naturally suppressed by the
period of exposure.

### Soil Building Opportunity

During the uncovered period, the exposed site can be planted to a long-term green
manure crop. Coleman typically sows a deep-rooting leguminous cover crop (such as
field peas, vetch, or clover) on the uncovered site after the greenhouse moves away.
This cover crop can grow for ten to thirteen months before being turned under and
replaced by the returning greenhouse. The benefits are substantial: [[biological-nitrogen-fixation-agriculture]],
deep soil aeration by cover crop roots, addition of [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]], and the breaking
of disease cycles through non-host crop rotation.

### Avoiding Summer Cooling Costs

In a permanent greenhouse, summer crops must be grown inside the same structure used
for winter crops. During hot summer months, the greenhouse can become extremely hot,
requiring ventilation systems, shade cloth, or even [[bloomfield-mushroom-evaporative-cooling-and-thermal-regulation]] to keep
temperatures tolerable. The mobile system avoids this problem entirely — the
greenhouse provides protection only when protection is needed, and the uncovered
site benefits from full sun exposure during the summer [[extending-growing-season]].

### Weed Management

Moving the greenhouse disrupts weed patterns. Weeds that germinate and establish
under the greenhouse in one season are exposed to outdoor conditions the next,
reducing their competitive advantage. The uncovered period allows for thorough
cultivation [[hamilton-green-manure-crops-and-cover-cropping]], which suppresses perennial weeds that might otherwise
become problematic in the sheltered greenhouse environment.
