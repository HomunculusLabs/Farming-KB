## The Reality of Contamination

Contamination is described as "a fact of life for every cultivator." Contaminants become a problem when their populations spiral above tolerable levels. A 5-15% contamination rate may be normal; if this level escalates without any alteration of regimen, new control measures should be introduced immediately. An overall contamination rate of 10% is one most cultivators can tolerate, while primary cultures from wild specimens may see rates of 25%.

## Major Contaminant Categories

### Bacterial Contaminants

- **Bacillus (Wet Spot)**: Causes grain to appear greasy and emit a sour "rotten apple" odor. Jars with this scent should be discarded, though partially contaminated spawn can sometimes be cased and fruited.
- **Pseudomonas (Bacterial Blotch and Pit)**: Results from mushroom caps remaining wet for extended periods. Common where excessive humidity meets inadequate air movement. Control by increasing air circulation, lowering humidity, and using slightly chlorinated water (150 ppm). Stop watering once caps reach adolescence.
- **Streptomyces (Firefang)**: Creates a distinctive firefang appearance in compost.
- **Actinomyces (Firefang)**: Another firefang-causing organism.

### Mold Contaminants

The Mushroom Cultivator catalogs over twenty mold contaminants:

- **Trichoderma (Forest Green Mold)**: One of the most common and destructive. Saturated stagnant air pockets are breeding areas. Promoted by excessive humidity without adequate air movement.
- **Aspergillus (Green Mold)**: Common airborne contaminant.
- **Penicillium (Bluish Green Mold)**: Frequently encountered in poorly sterilized media.
- **Neurospora (Pink Mold)**: Particularly aggressive; spreads rapidly through exposed cultures.
- **Dactylium (Cobweb Mold)**: Overgrows mushroom mycelium with cobweb-like mycelial strands.
- **Fusarium (Pink Mold)**: Can cause serious crop losses.
- **Mycogone (Wet Bubble)**: Causes deformities in developing mushrooms.
- **Verticillium (Dry Bubble)**: Another bubble-forming disease organism.
- **Chaetomium (Olive Green Mold)**: Often indicates incomplete pasteurization.
- **Cladosporium (Dark Green Mold)**: Common in damp environments.
- **Coprinus (Inky Cap)**: A mushroom weed that competes for substrate.
- **Geotrichum (Lipstick Mold)**: Forms distinctive lipstick-colored colonies.
- **Mucor and Rhizopus (Black Pin Mold)**: Rapid colonizers of exposed media.
- **Botrytis (Brown Mold)**: Can affect both cultures and fruiting bodies.

### Viral Contaminants

- **Virus (Die-Back Disease)**: Causes progressive decline in crop yields over successive flushes. Particularly devastating to agaricus-brunnescens. agaricus-bitorquis shows resistance to virus.

## Environmental Factors in Contamination

Several environmental conditions promote contamination:

- **Excessive humidity** without adequate air movement creates breeding areas for Trichoderma and Pseudomonas
- **Drying of the cropping surface** exposes substrate to possible colonization by contaminants
- **CO2 stratification** and temperature unevenness weaken mycelial defenses
- **Seasonal spore loads**: winter has drastically fewer free spores; spring and summer see remarkable increases
- **Insufficient fresh air** leads to stagnant conditions favoring contaminants

## Integrated Control Strategy

1. **Prevention first**: A regimen of cleaning MUST precede every set of inoculations
2. **Dispose of contaminated materials** in a fashion that poses no risk to the sterile lab
3. **Do not water contaminated areas** — this spreads infection further
4. **Lower relative humidity** and run casing drier than normal during serious disease outbreaks
5. **Use chlorinated water** (150 ppm) as a preventive measure
6. **Maintain evaporative cooling** at the cropping surface — evaporation aids nutrient transport from substrate to growing mushrooms
7. **Fill holes** in the casing left during harvesting with moist casing material to prevent substrate exposure

## Harvesting to Minimize Contamination Spread

- The best pickers are meticulous, unhurried, and treat mushrooms with care
- Damaged pinheads are easily parasitized by fly larvae and contaminants
- High spore loads from mature mushrooms can inhibit further pinhead development and cause allergic reactions
- All trimmings should be placed in sealed plastic bags and removed from the cropping area

## When to Abandon a Culture

The Mushroom Cultivator advises that cultures isolated from contaminated dishes can harbor hidden spores. Only when this contaminant-laden mycelium is inoculated into sterile grain do inherent bacteria and molds become evident. The sooner a cultivator is no longer dependent upon a partially contaminated culture dish, the easier it is to maintain pure cultures.
