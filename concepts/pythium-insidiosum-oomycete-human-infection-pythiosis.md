## Taxonomic Position and Cell Biology

Pythium and its relatives are oomycetes, not true fungi. The key
differences include:

- **[[alpha-1-3-glucan-fungal-pathogen-cell-walls]] contain cellulose
  microfibrils (staining blue with iodine solution), whereas true fungi
  have chitin-based walls that do not bind iodine.
- **Membrane sterols**: Pythium lacks ergosterol, the target of most
  antifungal pharmaceuticals including azoles and polyenes.
- **Genetic heritage**: Oomycetes are more closely related to brown algae
  and diatoms than to fungi, belonging to the stramenopile lineage.
- **Zoospore motility**: Pythium produces biflagellate zoospores with
  both anterior tinsel flagella and posterior whiplash flagella, capable
  of swimming through water toward [[low-cost-mushroom-growing-house-construction-developing-countries]] where [[fukuoka-textdoc-energy-efficiency-agricultural-labor]] is essential.

### Vascular Pythiosis
When Pythium infects arteries, particularly the femoral or iliac
arteries, it causes arterial occlusion and aneurysm formation. This
form carries extremely high mortality. Clinical presentation includes
claudication, pulsatile abdominal masses, and acute limb ischemia.
Cases have been documented in Thailand, where arterial pythiosis has
been treated successfully with immunotherapy using crude antigens
prepared from the pathogen.

### Ocular Pythiosis
Corneal infections can lead to ulceration and perforation of the eye.
These cases are particularly challenging to diagnose because the
organism may be mistaken for fungal keratitis.

## Pythiosis in Animals

Pythiosis is far more common in animals than in humans:

- **Equine pythiosis**: The best-documented form, causing cutaneous
  lesions on the legs, abdomen, and face of horses. Lesions are
  typically granulomatous, ulcerative masses with necrotic cores and
  yellow-gray "kunkers" (fungal granules). First described by
  Charles Jackson in a letter to *The Veterinarian* in 1842.
- **Canine pythiosis**: Dogs develop gastrointestinal or cutaneous
  forms, with the gastrointestinal variant causing weight loss,
  vomiting, and intestinal obstruction.
