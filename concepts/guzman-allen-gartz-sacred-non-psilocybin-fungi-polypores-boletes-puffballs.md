The Mixtec Indians of Oaxaca present an even more complex case. Heim and Wasson (1962) and Heim et al. (1967) reported the use of *[[dictyophora-indusiata]]: The Veiled Stinkhorn of the Chinantec

The Chinantec Indians of Oaxaca use *Dictyophora indusiata* — the "veiled lady" or "long-net stinkhorn" — as a special narcotic fungus for divination purposes. This is one of the most visually dramatic of all fungi: a tall, phallic fruiting body emerges from an egg-like volva, and a lacy white indusium (veil) descends from the cap to the ground like a bridal train. The fungus produces a strong, putrid odor designed to attract flies for spore dispersal.

The choice of such a visually and olfactorily striking fungus for divination purposes reflects the indigenous principle that sacred objects should command attention through multiple sensory channels. *D. indusiata* is impossible to ignore — its appearance is otherworldly, its smell is overwhelming, and its sudden emergence from the forest floor mimics supernatural manifestation. These qualities make it an ideal focus for divinatory attention, regardless of whether it contains psychoactive compounds.
