## Ganoderma lobatum and the Mushroom Cult of Chignahuapan

## Overview

One of the most unusual and least understood phenomena in Mexican ethnomycology
is the existence of a church in Chignahuapan, Puebla, Mexico, built specifically
in honor of the fungus Ganoderma lobatum. Local indigenous people regard this
fungus as a saint, and the church was erected as an act of devotion to it. The
inner surface of the fungal specimen used in the cult bears an arresting sketch
portraying a Christ figure with a sun and moon on each side, though there is no
evidence of neurotropic properties associated with Ganoderma lobatum.

## Species Profile: Ganoderma lobatum

Ganoderma lobatum (Schwein.) G.F. Atk. is a bracket fungus (polypore) belonging
to the family Ganodermataceae. It is a wood-decomposing basidiomycete with the
following characteristics:

- Large, perennial, semicircular bracket-shaped fruiting bodies
- Upper surface typically dark brown to reddish-brown with a lacquered
  appearance (resembling the better-known reishi, G. lucidum)
- Pore surface white to cream-colored, bruising brown
- Found growing on dead or dying hardwood trees in tropical and subtropical
  forests
- Double-walled, ornamented basidiospores characteristic of the genus
- Woody, tough texture — not edible in the culinary sense

Ganoderma lobatum is closely related to [[psychoactive-cacti-of-central-mexico]] with a strong indigenous heritage. The region is known
for its rich biodiversity and its [[amanita]] of fungi as a food
   source and ecological presence in the region.

## Sun and Moon Iconography

The depiction of Christ flanked by the sun and moon on the Ganoderma specimen
is rich in symbolic meaning that bridges indigenous and Catholic traditions:

- In pre-Hispanic Mesoamerican cosmology, the sun and moon are among the most
  important celestial bodies, associated with cycles of life, death, and
  rebirth. The Aztec sun stone (Calendar Stone) prominently features solar
  symbolism.
- In Christian iconography, Christ is sometimes associated with solar imagery
  (Sol Invictus, "light of the world"), and the sun and moon can represent
  the cosmic scope of divine power.
- The combination suggests a deliberate fusion of indigenous astronomical
  symbolism with Christian religious art, a pattern common in Mexican folk
  Catholicism.

## Other Polypores in Indigenous Contexts

The veneration of Ganoderma lobatum has parallels in the broader literature:

- Thoen (1982) documented polypores used in [[entheogenic-folk-religious-ceremonies]] including
  Polyporus tuberaster, [[poria-cocos]], [[ganoderma-lucidum]], and Fomes fomentarius.
- Oldridge et al. (1989) considered polypores containing hordenine and
  tyramine as potentially psychotropic, though they cause dizziness rather
  than true hallucinations.
- In Asia, Ganoderma lucidum has been venerated for millennia as the
  "mushroom of immortality," though its role is medicinal and spiritual
  rather than psychoactive.

## Ecological and Geographic Context

The Sierra Norte de Puebla is characterized by cloud forests and pine-oak
woodlands at elevations of 1,500-3,000 meters, with high rainfall favoring
fungal diversity. Rich indigenous Nahua, Totonac, and Otomi communities
maintain strong traditions of mushroom gathering. The region is known habitat
for multiple neurotropic Psilocybe species including P. caerulescens and
P. mexicana. This ecological richness means local people encounter a wide
variety of fungal species daily, contributing to cultural familiarity with
fungi that may underpin the veneration of Ganoderma lobatum.

## Significance

The Ganoderma lobatum cult of Chignahuapan represents one of the most unusual
documented cases of fungal veneration in the world. It challenges the common
assumption that mushroom-centered spiritual practices are exclusively linked to
psychoactive species. The phenomenon suggests that fungi can occupy roles of
cultural and religious significance beyond their chemical properties, rooted in
their visual prominence, ecological importance, and symbolic associations with
growth, decay, and transformation.
