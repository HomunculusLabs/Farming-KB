To field-test these anecdotal reports, researchers devised a placebo-controlled
double-blind trial using volunteers. Volunteers were given either placebo or
oral THC in the form of Marinol or smoking kif. Improvements in night vision
were noted after the ingestion of THC and after the smoking of kif.

The authors write: "It is believed that this effect is dose-dependent and
cannabinoid-mediated at the retinal level. Further testing may assess possible
clinical application of these results in retinitis pigmentosa or other
conditions."

The study received a CHI value of 3 based on a single study of 4 healthy
subjects, conducted by the international research team in 2004.

## Mechanism of Action

The endocannabinoid system is present in retinal tissue. Cannabinoid receptors
CB1 and CB2 have been identified in human retinal pigment epithelial cells.
The improvement in night vision (scotopic sensitivity) appears to be mediated
through cannabinoid activity at the retinal level. This cannabinoid-mediated
effect on retinal function suggests potential therapeutic applications not only
for night vision enhancement but possibly for retinitis pigmentosa and other
retinal degenerative conditions.

## Potential Applications

While the evidence base is currently limited to a single study, the findings
suggest several potential areas for future investigation:

- Retinitis pigmentosa -- a group of genetic disorders causing retinal
  degeneration and progressive vision loss
- General age-related decline in night vision
- Occupational applications for those working in low-light environments
- Enhanced scotopic sensitivity for patients with compromised retinal function

Further research with larger sample sizes and controlled conditions is needed
to confirm these preliminary findings and establish clinical protocols.

## Historical and Ethnobotanical Context

The use of cannabis to enhance night vision has deep roots in traditional
practices. Jamaican fishermen and Moroccan mountain dwellers independently
discovered that cannabis use improved their ability to see in low-light
conditions. These observations, passed down through generations, provided the
anecdotal foundation for the 2004 scientific investigation.

In the Rif mountains of northern Morocco, kif (a blend of sifted Cannabis
