## Mimicking Forest Patterns

To mimic the natural pattern, pick plants to grow together based on height,
climbing ability, tolerance to shade, and water requirements. In a dry or cool
climate, space plants farther apart for better light and water distribution. In
warm, humid climates, put them closer together, but not too close to avoid
fungus problems.

In very fertile places with lots of water, put in all plants at once: walnuts
(long-lived), fruit trees like peaches (shorter-lived), legumes like autumn
olive (nutritious mulch), and perennials like comfrey and yarrow (weed
control).

## The Polyculture Advantage

Monoculture is concerned with the individual plant and how to push each one to
greatest yield. Polyculture, and particularly a forest garden, is concerned
with the whole. Pushing as many species as possible to work together yields
more in total than one species grown alone. Plants may not produce as much
fruit individually, but when collected in total, the quantity of food will be
greater, with the additional benefit of being self-reliant: more food for less
work.
