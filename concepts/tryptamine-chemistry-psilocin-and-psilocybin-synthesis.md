# Tryptamine Chemistry — Psilocin and Psilocybin Synthesis

## Overview

Psilocin (1) and psilocybin (2) are the principal hallucinogenic constituents
of "magic mushrooms" (*Psilocybe* species). First isolated by Albert Hofmann
and colleagues at Sandoz in 1958–1959, these compounds are structurally similar
to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) and likely exert their hallucinogenic effects
through interference with normal brain serotonin signaling. Shirota, Hakamata,
and Goda (2003) developed concise gram-scale syntheses of both compounds
without chromatographic purification — a significant practical improvement over
prior methods.

## Structural Relationship to Serotonin

Psilocin is 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-HO-DMT), while psilocybin is
its 4-phosphoryloxy derivative (4-PO-DMT). Serotonin is 5-hydroxytryptamine
(5-HT). The close structural resemblance between these tryptamine derivatives
and serotonin underlies their pharmacological activity at [[psilocin-chemical-synthesis]]
provides the necessary standards.

Since June 6, 2002, fungi containing psilocin and psilocybin have been
regulated by the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law in Japan.

## Psilocin Synthesis Route
