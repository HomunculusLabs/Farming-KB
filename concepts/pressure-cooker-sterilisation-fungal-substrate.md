
# Pressure Cooker Sterilisation for Fungal Substrate Jars

Sterilisation is arguably the most critical step in [[mycelium]].

## Post-Sterilisation Handling

1. **Depressurise naturally**: Allow the cooker to return to atmospheric pressure on its own. Quick-release risks cracking jars. This takes 30-60 minutes.
2. **Check for cracks**: Always inspect jars before handling. Cracked jars should be discarded. Use heat-resistant gloves.
3. **Shake immediately**: While still hot, shake jars vigorously to mix wet and dry kernels into a homogeneous substrate. Hot kernels separate more easily as starches have not yet gelatinised.
4. **Cool in a clean location**: Place jars in a draft-free environment to reach room temperature.
5. **Keep lids loose during cooling**: As jars cool, they contract. Tight lids pull a vacuum, deforming lids, cracking jars, or sucking contaminated air through foil gaps.

## Common Mistakes

**Tight lids** are the most common beginner error. They prevent steam penetration, create dangerous pressure differentials, and can cause explosions. Always verify lids are finger-loose.

**Insufficient time** leaves endospores alive. These may not show immediately but outcompete mycelium during colonisation, causing stunted or failed cultures. When in doubt, sterilise longer.

## Scaling Considerations

For cultivators working with larger batches, several scaling factors apply:

- **Batch processing**: Multiple cooker loads can be processed sequentially, with jars from completed loads moved to the cooling area while the next batch sterilises.
- **Spawn bags**: Polypropylene autoclave bags allow much larger substrate volumes per unit but require 2-hour sterilisation cycles and careful handling to avoid punctures.
- **Consistency**: Maintaining identical preparation parameters across all jars in a batch ensures uniform colonisation speeds and predictable fruiting or sclerotia timelines.

## Pressure Cooker Selection

When choosing a pressure cooker for [[inoculation-methods]] in the cultivation workflow. Jars should ideally be inoculated within 24 hours of sterilisation. The longer the interval, the greater the contamination risk through microscopic foil gaps. Planning inoculation immediately after cooling minimises this risk. A well-organised cultivator times the sterilisation cycle to complete in the evening, allowing overnight cooling with inoculation first thing the next morning.
