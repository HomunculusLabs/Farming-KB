# Cannabis for Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting

Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) remains one of the most
distressing side effects of cancer treatment, affecting up to 80% of patients
receiving emetogenic chemotherapy regimens. Cannabis and its constituent
cannabinoids have demonstrated significant anti-emetic efficacy, leading to the
development and FDA approval of multiple cannabinoid-based pharmaceuticals.
The Cannabis Health Index documents extensive evidence supporting cannabinoid
use for CINV, with combined CHI values among the highest for any condition
covered.

## THC Anti-Emetic Mechanism

Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) exerts its anti-emetic effects primarily through
CB1 [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2-tissue-distribution-blesching]] located in the brainstem's vomiting center,
specifically the nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) and the dorsal vagal complex.
The mechanism involves:

- **CB1 receptor activation in the dorsal vagal complex**: THC binds to CB1
  receptors in the brainstem region that controls the vomiting reflex,
  effectively suppressing the emetic response at its neurological origin.
- **Reduction of serotonin release**: THC modulates serotonin 5-HT3 receptor
  signaling, the same pathway targeted by conventional anti-emetics such as
  ondansetron (Zofran). This dual mechanism provides anti-emetic coverage
  through complementary pathways.
- **[[cannabis-for-cachexia-and-appetite-stimulation]] via hypothalamic CB1**: Beyond stopping nausea, THC
  activates CB1 receptors in the hypothalamus, stimulating appetite through
  release of hunger-promoting hormones including ghrelin. This dual action makes
  THC uniquely valuable [[cannabis-for-cancer-patients]] who experience both nausea and
  appetite loss.

## FDA-Approved Cannabinoid Pharmaceuticals

Several pharmaceutical derivatives of THC have received FDA approval for CINV
and related conditions:

### Dronabinol (Marinol)
- Synthetic THC isomer approved by the FDA
- Available in capsule form for oral administration
- Indicated for nausea and vomiting associated with chemotherapy in patients
  who have failed to respond adequately to conventional anti-emetics
- Also approved for anorexia associated with weight loss in AIDS patients
- Typical dosing: 2.5 mg to 10 mg taken 1-3 hours before chemotherapy

### Nabilone (Cesamet)
- [[synthetic-cannabinoid-pharmaceuticals-sativex-dronabinol-marinol]] with structural similarity to THC
- Approved for nausea and vomiting associated with chemotherapy
- May be more effective than Dronabinol for some patients due to differences in
  metabolism and [[beta-glucan-receptor-binding]] profile
- Typical dosing: 1-2 mg taken 1-3 hours before chemotherapy, then every 12
  hours as needed

### Sativex
- Plant-derived whole-cannabinoid extract (approximately 1:1 THC:CBD ratio)
- Administered as an oral mucosal spray
- Not yet FDA-approved in the United States but approved in numerous other
  countries for spasticity in [[blesching-cannabis-multiple-sclerosis]] and cancer pain
- Retains [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect]] the [[blesching-cannabis-cannabinoid-terpene-entourage-effect]] of additional cannabinoids and terpenes
- CBD component may reduce the psychoactive effects of THC while providing
  additional anti-nausea benefits

## Clinical Trial Evidence

The Cannabis Health Index compiles multiple human [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-schizophyllan-spg-clinical-trials]] demonstrating
cannabinoid efficacy for CINV:

### Dronabinol in HIV/AIDS (CHI: 5)
A landmark 1995 study at St. John's Hospital (Tulsa, Oklahoma) enrolled 139
patients with AIDS. Those receiving 2.5 mg of Dronabinol twice daily showed
increased appetite, improved mood, and weight gains with significant reduction
of nausea. The authors concluded Dronabinol was safe and effective.

### Nabilone in Cancer (CHI: 3)
A 2006 Canadian study at the William Osler Health Center (Toronto) collected
data from 82 cancer patients who received Nabilone for 53 days for cancer-related
pain and other symptoms. Results indicated potential therapeutic benefit with
improvement in pain levels, nausea, depression, anxiety, insomnia, and night
sweats. Side effects were mostly mild (drowsiness, tiredness).
