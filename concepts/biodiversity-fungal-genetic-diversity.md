## Nuclear Genome Analysis
The nuclear genomes of fungi contain the genetic information
that defines species and controls the expression of developmental
cycles. Nuclear genomes are very large (1.5 x 10 to the 7th to
820 x 10 to the 7th base pairs), represent thousands of genetic
loci, and contain both single-copy and repetitive DNA. Nuclear
DNA is therefore excellent for measuring genetic diversity and
deriving molecular fingerprints for taxonomic purposes.
The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) has so increased the
sensitivity and resolution of genetic analyses that genetic
diversity may be assessed using very small amounts of starting
material. Samples can be collected from individuals in situ
without impacting community structure.
## Arbitrarily Primed PCR (apPCR)
apPCR analysis (also known as RAPD) can discriminate among
fungal taxa with few distinguishing morphological characters.
Short oligonucleotide primers (10 to 20 base pairs) anneal to
complementary DNA sequences to produce double-stranded DNA
products. The number and size of amplified products depend on
primer hybridization site frequency and distribution.
In fungi, 80 to 100 percent of DNA products generated from
apPCR amplification are shared among individuals of the same
species for any given primer 15 to 16 base pairs long and
composed of simple sequence repeats. Individuals from different
species share only 0 to 20 percent of DNA products. This has led
to species-specific apPCR band patterns for unequivocal
taxonomic identification.
A major source of nonreproducibility in apPCR is the inaccuracy
of thermocyclers. Some machines never come within 10 degrees C
of the programmed denaturing temperatures, causing incomplete
DNA denaturation and unreliable primer annealing.
## Genetic Diversity Assessment
The intraspecific variation in apPCR products allows investigators
to assess genetic diversity among individuals within and between
populations. As the number of primers increases, the probability
of generating polymorphic genetic markers also increases. At least
15 to 20 polymorphic markers are required for accurate measures
of genetic diversity.
The sensitivity of apPCR is such that small amounts of fungal
material (0.5 cubic cm) are sufficient for hundreds to thousands
of genetic analyses. This allows analysis of population structure
without disturbing sporocarps or underground mycelia.
