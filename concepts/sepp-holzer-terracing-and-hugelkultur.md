# Sepp Holzer Terracing and Hugelkultur

Sepp Holzer, the "Agri-Rebel" of Austria, farms 110 acres at 1,500m elevation in the Austrian Alps. His methods include terracing steep slopes, creating [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]]
- **[[fungal-wood-decomposition-spatial-variation]] provides slow-release nutrients for 10-20 years
- **Soil building**: [[mycorrhizal-fungi-soil-carbon-sequestration-by-type]]**: Buried wood stores carbon long-term
- **Uses "waste" material**: Fallen trees, branches, brush piles

### Variations
- **[[soil-ph-management-for-vegetable-gardens]]
- **Hugelkultur swale**: Hugel bed on contour, intercepting water runoff
- **Hugelkultur terrace**: Mound built into a slope, creating a level growing area
- **Crater garden**: Sunken center with hugel mound rim (Holzer's variation for arid climates)

### Planting
- Year 1: Nitrogen may be temporarily tied up (wood decomposition uses nitrogen). Add extra nitrogen (compost, manure, nitrogen-fixing plants)
- Top of mound: drought-tolerant plants (rosemary, thyme, squash)
- Sides: vegetables, herbs, berries
- Base: moisture-loving plants (mint, comfrey, elderberry)
- As wood decomposes, all zones become more fertile and moisture-retentive

## Terracing

### Holzer's Approach to Terraces
- Unlike conventional terraces (flat, bare, engineered), Holzer creates living terraces
- Terraces are planted immediately with diverse vegetation
- Slope stability comes from root systems, not just engineering
- Pigs are used to create initial terraces on slopes (they root along contour)

### Construction Methods

#### Pig-Created Terraces
1. Fence pigs into a slope area with contour as the boundary
2. Pigs naturally root along the contour (easier than going uphill)
3. Over weeks/months, pigs create a terrace (level area, berms of soil)
4. Move pigs to next terrace
5. Plant the completed terrace immediately
6. No machinery needed — pigs are the "excavators"

#### Hand-Built Terraces
1. Mark contour lines with A-frame or water level
2. Cut into the slope on the uphill side
3. Use excavated soil to build up the downhill side
4. Compact the wall (stone, log, or earth wall)
5. Plant immediately (root systems stabilize the terrace)
6. Start at the top and work down

### Terrace Planting
- **Upper edge (berm)**: Fruit trees, nitrogen fixers, deep-rooted plants
- **Terrace face**: Ground covers, herbs, trailing plants (strawberry, thyme)
- **Terrace floor**: Vegetables, grains, pasture
