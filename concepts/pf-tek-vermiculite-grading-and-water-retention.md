# Vermiculite Grading and Water Retention in PF TEK Substrate

## Overview

Vermiculite is the foundational ingredient of the [[pf-tek-guide|PF TEK]] [[pf-tek-measurement-calibration-and-substrate-formula-precision]] assumes fine grade vermiculite is being used. The proportions are:

- 1/8 cup [[cho-brown-rice-vinegar-preparation]] powder
- 1/2 to 2/3 cup fine vermiculite
- 40 to 45 cc water (slightly less than 1/4 cup, approximately 1.5 ounces or 3 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon)

This formulation has been tested extensively and produces reliable results when fine vermiculite is used. The water volume is calibrated to fully hydrate the vermiculite and rice powder mixture without leaving excess free water that could pool at the bottom of the jar. The substrate should feel damp and cohesive when mixed, holding together when squeezed gently but not dripping water.

### Adjusting for Coarse Vermiculite

When using coarse vermiculite, the water holding capacity drops noticeably. The standard formulation may result in a substrate that is wetter or drier than intended depending on the exact grade used. If the substrate appears really wet or sloppy after mixing the ingredients, reduce the water volume in subsequent batches. Conversely, if the substrate appears dry and crumbly rather than damp and cohesive, slightly increase the water.

The key diagnostic principle from the original TEK is to keep detailed notes on formulations and compare fruiting results across batches to find the optimal water ratio for the specific vermiculite being used. Each brand and each regional source may require slightly different water volumes, and only direct experimentation and comparison can identify the ideal balance for a given set of conditions.

### Maximum Fruiting Formula

The maximum fruiting formula uses higher nutrient and water content for increased yields:

- 1/4 cup brown rice powder
- 1/2 cup vermiculite
- 60 cc water

When using the maximum fruiting formula with fine vermiculite, the jar lid should be kept loose during incubation to prevent excess moisture accumulation at the bottom of the jar. Water can collect in the bottom when the lid is tight and the substrate moisture content is very high, creating conditions that can promote bacterial growth. See [[pf-tek-guide|PF TEK]] substrate system:

1. **Moisture retention**: It absorbs and slowly releases water to the mycelium throughout colonization and fruiting, providing a consistent moisture supply over the entire growth cycle without requiring external watering.

2. **Aeration**: Unlike pure grain substrates where mycelium grows into a dense mass with little air space, vermiculite creates physical gaps throughout the substrate. Mycelial threads stretch across and between vermiculite particles, maintaining access to oxygen which is essential for healthy mycelial respiration and growth.

3. **Contaminant barrier**: A layer of plain [[pf-tek-dry-vermiculite-contaminant-barrier]] on top of the wet substrate protects against airborne contaminants entering through the inoculation holes. See [[pf-tek-cake-birthing-technique|birthing]]. Without vermiculite's structural contribution, the substrate would be too fragile to handle without crumbling.

5. **Moisture regulation**: The dry vermiculite top layer absorbs and regulates moisture transpiration and condensation within the jar, preventing excess moisture buildup while maintaining humidity at the substrate surface where the mycelium meets the barrier.

## Vermiculite Compared to Perlite

Vermiculite and perlite are related horticultural products with fundamentally different water handling properties that make them suitable for different roles in the PF TEK system. Perlite does not actually absorb and hold water internally; rather, water adheres to its porous surface and evaporates gradually into the surrounding air. Vermiculite, by contrast, absorbs water into its expanded layered structure and holds it within the mineral itself.
