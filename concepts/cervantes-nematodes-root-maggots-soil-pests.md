# Nematodes, Root Maggots, and Soil Pests

Soil-borne pests that attack cannabis roots can be
among the most difficult problems to diagnose and
control. Because the damage occurs underground, symptoms
often resemble [[cervantes-growing-medium]]

## Nematodes

### Identification

Of the hundreds of species of microscopic nematodes,
sometimes called eelworms, only a few are destructive to
plants. Most nematodes attack roots and are found in the
soil. A few species attack stems and foliage. Root
nematodes can often be seen with the help of a 30X
microscope. Often growers diagnose the damage caused by
destructive nematodes rather than actually seeing the
pests themselves.

### Damage

- Slow growth and leaf chlorosis
- Wilting several hours during daylight from lack of
  fluid flow
- Symptoms can be difficult to discern from nitrogen
  deficiency
- Root damage is often severe by the time it is
  examined
- Root knot nematodes cause roots to swell with galls
- Other nematodes scrape and cut roots, compounded
  by fungal attacks
- Roots turn soft and mushy

### Control

- **Cleanliness** is the primary defense. Use new,
  sterilized potting soil or soilless mix to exclude
  nematodes.
- **French marigolds** (Tagetes patula) repel soil
  nematodes and can be planted as a companion crop.
- **Beneficial fungi** (Myrothecium verrucaria, trade
  name DiTera ES) attack pest nematodes.
- **[[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]]** applied as a soil drench can help
  suppress nematode populations.
- Nematodes rarely cause problems indoors in clean
  grow rooms with fresh growing medium.
