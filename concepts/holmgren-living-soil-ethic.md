## Technical and Ethical Entanglement

How to care for the soil remains controversial. These technical questions are entwined with ethical ones because we do not know how far it is possible to improve the soil's capacity to support nature and provide for human needs. What is certain is that careless and profligate use of the soil can result in a rapid loss of its capacity to support life.

## Stewardship Concept

The stewardship concept demands that we constantly ask the question: "Will the resource be in better shape after my stewardship?" One cannot go far in this process without challenging the ethical validity of the ownership of land and natural resources that lies at the heart of our legal system. Control of land and natural resources has been central throughout history; in a low-energy future it will again become the primary focus for ethics, politics and culture.

Wendell Berry, the American writer, organic farmer and environmentalist, has eloquently criticized the notion of planetary stewardship as a product of our arrogant disconnection from nature. He argued the question is not how to care for the planet, but how to care for each of the planet's millions of human and natural neighbourhoods, each of its millions of small pieces and parcels of land.

## Connection to Permaculture Ethics

Holmgren locates living soil care within a broader ethical framework:

- It is rooted in stewardship concepts from indigenous cultures and bioregionalism

- Wendell Berry's critique of planetary stewardship as arrogant disconnection supports the focus on specific soil and place

- The ethic demands asking: "Will the resource be in better shape after my stewardship?"

- It challenges the ethical validity of private land ownership at the heart of Western legal systems

## Beyond Conservation to Restoration

Within the more recent tradition of the organic movement, land management to repair and improve long-term soil fertility — especially by increasing humus content — has been regarded as a form of stewardship where one leaves the land in better shape than it was when one received it.

Permaculture strategies and whole design principles (such as [[holmgren-catch-and-store-energy-detailed]]) are predicated on the idea that increasing long-term soil fertility is possible. The more activist permaculture concepts demand that we make all reasonable efforts to increase, and even transform, the biological capacity of soil for the benefit of future generations.

## The Terrestrial Constraint

Holmgren uses the word "terrestrial" to mean belonging to the land rather than to the sea or the air. Despite humanity's vigorous exploitation of the open oceans, we can no more escape our dependence on land and soil than we can realistically escape the planet. This biological reality grounds the living soil ethic in physical necessity rather than abstract idealism.

## Soil as Measure of Society

The state of soils as the best measure of the future health and well-being of society is a powerful framing. Civilizations throughout history have risen and fallen with their soils. The living soil ethic insists that attention to soil health is not merely a technical agricultural concern but a fundamental ethical imperative that connects personal action to civilizational outcomes.

## Connection to Biodiversity

Care for the Earth can also encompass caring for all the diverse lifeforms that inhabit the earth. This care is not dependent on the current usefulness to us of those lifeforms, but accepts them all as valid parts of the living earth with intrinsic value. The living soil ethic naturally extends to caring for the soil food web — the billions of organisms that make soil living rather than merely dirt.

## Practical Implications

The living soil ethic has direct practical implications for permaculture designers:

- Prioritize building soil organic matter through composting, mulching, and cover cropping

- Minimize soil disturbance to protect soil structure and microbial communities

- Design systems that cycle nutrients back to the soil rather than exporting them
