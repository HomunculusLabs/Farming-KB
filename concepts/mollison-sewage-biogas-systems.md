# Mollison on Sewage and Biogas Systems

**Source:** Bill Mollison, Permaculture Designers Manual, Ch.7; Permaculture Design Course, Pamphlet XI

## Overview

Mollison presents detailed designs for converting sewage from a waste problem to a productive resource through multi-stage pond systems and biogas digestion. This content extends [[mollison-fish-pond-construction]] and [[appropriate-technology]]. The core philosophy is that sewage is not waste but a misallocated nutrient stream -- when properly managed, it becomes the foundation for productive [[pond-and-aquaculture]], energy generation, and crop irrigation.

## Sewage Treatment Pond Design

### Three-Stage System

Based on the Werribee, Victoria system (serving 2.7M people on 1,500 ha of ponds):

1. **Anaerobic (settling) ponds**: 1 ha per 3,800 people; methane-producing; 1m deep average; 7-10 ha max per pond

2. **Facultative ponds**: Part methane, part oxygen-producing; algae blooms develop; zooplankton thrive on algae

3. **Aerobic ponds**: Totally oxygen-producing; clean water output

### Sealed Digester Design

- Long narrow pond (3m wide x 30m long x 3m deep) serves 1,000 people

- Cover with weighted plastic, metal, butyl rubber, or fibreglass

- Agitate sludge using pumped biogas to prevent settling and acid conditions

- 20-day retention at 25-30C converts high percentage of mass to methane

- 90-94% of worm eggs destroyed; many harmful bacteria eliminated

## Biogas Composition and Uses

- **Composition**: Methane (CH4), carbon dioxide (CO2), ammonia (NH3), traces of nitrous sulphide

- **Energy**: 1 cubic meter methane removes ~2.89 kg solids; can run motors for electricity or compress for cooking

- **Scale**: Motor supplies heat for digester, compresses gas for agitation and energy supply

- **Biogas from composting**: Two technologies extract methane -- composting twigs and distillation of wood

## Heavy Metal Removal

Anaerobic ponds achieve remarkable metal precipitation:

- Copper: 97% removed (78% anaerobically)

- Cadmium: 70% removed (all anaerobically)

- Zinc: 97% removed (83% anaerobically)

- Lead: 95% removed (90% anaerobically)

- Chromium: 87% removed (47% anaerobically)

Hydrogen sulphide from marsh gas combines with soluble heavy metals to form insoluble sulphides above pH 7.

## Integrated System Outputs

A complete sewage-to-crop system yields: gas (energy), algae, fish, wildlife habitat, crop irrigation, and thoroughly cleansed water for release to streams. Mollison's town sewage design saved $30,000/year in engineering costs plus equivalent annual fuel savings, converting waste into an industrial base for mint and bamboo production.

## Design Philosophy: Sewage as Resource

Mollison fundamentally reframes sewage treatment as a design opportunity rather than an engineering problem.

- **Nutrient cycling:** Human waste contains nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and trace minerals essential for plant growth. Conventional sewage treatment destroys or wastes these nutrients. Permaculture systems capture and redirect them to productive use.

- **Energy production:** The organic matter in sewage represents stored chemical energy. Anaerobic digestion converts this energy into methane, which can power generators, cooking stoves, and heating systems. A community of 1,000 people can produce enough biogas from sewage to significantly offset energy costs.

- **Water purification:** Multi-stage pond systems produce water clean enough for irrigation and, in some cases, for release to natural waterways. The purification is achieved through biological processes rather than chemical or mechanical treatment.

- **Wildlife habitat:** The pond system creates wetland habitat that supports birds, amphibians, insects, and aquatic plants. This biodiversity provides additional ecosystem services including pest control and pollination.

## Integration with Permaculture Zones

The sewage treatment system can be strategically placed within the [[permaculture-zones-system]] framework for maximum efficiency.

- **Zone 0-1 (household):** Greywater from kitchen and bathroom can be directed to mulch basins and ornamental plantings. Small-scale biogas digesters can process kitchen scraps alongside human waste.

- **Zone 2-3 (food production):** Treated effluent from the aerobic pond stage provides nutrient-rich irrigation water for orchards, food forests, and intensive gardens. The nutrients in treated sewage are more bioavailable than synthetic fertilizers.

- **Zone 4 (forage and forestry):** Sewage effluent can irrigate fuel wood plantations and animal forage systems, closing the nutrient loop between human settlements and agricultural production.

- **Zone 5 (wilderness):** Final-stage treated water can be released to natural waterways, recharging groundwater and supporting stream ecosystems.

## Practical Considerations for Implementation

- **Climate and temperature:** Biogas production is temperature-dependent, with optimal rates at 25-30C. In cold climates, digesters may need heating (which can be supplied by the biogas itself), insulation, or greenhouse enclosure.

- **Sizing:** The Werribee system allocates 1 hectare of anaerobic pond per 3,800 people. For smaller communities, the sealed digester design (3m x 30m x 3m per 1,000 people) is more appropriate and produces methane more efficiently.

- **Maintenance:** Biogas systems require regular monitoring of gas production, pH levels, and sludge accumulation. Agitation prevents scum formation and ensures uniform digestion. Periodic sludge removal provides excellent fertilizer.

- **Regulatory compliance:** Municipal sewage treatment is heavily regulated. These designs are most practical for rural homesteads, eco-villages, and communities where conventional sewage infrastructure is unavailable or prohibitively expensive.

- **Public health:** Proper retention time (20+ days) and multi-stage treatment ensure pathogen destruction. The 90-94% helminth egg destruction rate in sealed digesters meets WHO guidelines for safe agricultural reuse.
