# Filter Can Design for Mushroom Grow Bags

The filter can is a simple but essential component of the boil-a-bag [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] method. It serves as a gas exchange port that allows the [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] to breathe while preventing airborne contaminant spores from entering the sterile substrate. The design balances two competing needs: adequate airflow for mycelial respiration and effective filtration to exclude microscopic contaminants.

## Purpose and Function

During colonization, mushroom mycelium is an active living organism that requires oxygen for respiration and produces carbon dioxide as a waste product. Without adequate gas exchange:

- Mycelial growth slows dramatically or stalls entirely
- Carbon dioxide buildup creates conditions favorable for bacterial contamination
- The substrate may develop anaerobic pockets that promote souring

However, opening the bag for gas exchange would expose the sterile substrate to airborne contaminant spores. The filter can solves this problem by providing filtered ventilation — allowing gas molecules to pass through while trapping fungal spores and bacterial cells.

## Materials Required

The Jarrold filter can uses readily available, inexpensive materials:

### The Can
- A clean, empty 3-4 inch round food can (approximately 7.5-10 cm diameter)
- The can lid should be left partially attached when first opened — connected by about 1 inch (2.5 cm) of metal to serve as a closable lid
- Cans from soup, beans, vegetables, or pet food work well
- The can must be thoroughly cleaned and dried before use

### Filtration Media
Two filtration materials are specified, either of which can be used:

**Rock wool (mineral wool)**:
- Originally developed for building insulation, available at hardware stores
- Also sold as [[cannabis-grow-hydroponic-growing-systems]] medium at garden centers
- Fibrous structure creates a dense mat that traps particles while allowing gas flow
- Inert and heat-resistant — survives [[boil-a-bag-pressure-sterilization-technique]] without degradation
- Handle with gloves: rock wool fibers can cause skin and respiratory irritation

**Loft insulation fiberglass**:
- Readily available as building insulation
- Similar filtration properties to rock wool
- Must be unfaced (no paper or foil backing)
- Same handling precautions apply — wear gloves and a mask when cutting

### Vermiculite
- Horticultural grade vermiculite (available at garden centers)
- Serves as a secondary filtration layer and [[comparison-silicon-vs-calcium-in-plant-defense-and-structural-support]]
- Lightweight, inert, and heat-resistant
- Helps prevent the rock wool from shifting during handling

## Assembly Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the Can Bottom
Using a hammer and nail, punch numerous holes throughout the bottom of the can. The holes should be evenly distributed and approximately 2-3 mm in diameter. These holes allow air to pass through the filtration media from the outside into the bag.

The bottom of the can (with holes) will face downward into the bag, while the open top (with the attached lid) faces upward and outward, exposed to the room air.

### Step 2: Install Bottom Rock Wool Layer
Line the inside bottom of the can (over the punched holes) with approximately 1 cm of rock wool or fiberglass. This layer provides the primary filtration barrier and prevents vermiculite from falling through the holes.

The rock wool should be firmly but not tightly packed. Over-compression reduces airflow; too loose a pack allows contaminants through.

### Step 3: Fill with Vermiculite
Fill the can with vermiculite until it is approximately 1 cm below the rim. The vermiculite serves as:

- A secondary filtration layer that catches any particles that penetrate the rock wool
- Ballast that keeps the filter assembly stable and properly oriented
- A moisture buffer that helps moderate humidity inside the bag

Alternatively, instead of vermiculite, the can can be filled entirely with small pieces of rock wool, eliminating the need for two separate materials.

### Step 4: Install Top Rock Wool Layer
Cover the vermiculite with a final layer of rock wool or fiberglass at the top of the can. This layer prevents the vermiculite from shifting or spilling out when the can is handled, and provides an additional filtration surface at the air entry point.

### Step 5: Close the Lid
Close the partially attached lid of the can. The filter can is now complete and ready for use.

## Installation in the Grow Bag

### Bag Preparation
- Fill a boil-a-bag (polypropylene, heat-safe) with prepared substrate, packing firmly but not compacting
- Leave sufficient space at the top of the bag to accommodate the filter can
- The bag should not be more than three-quarters full to allow room for the filter assembly

### Securing the Filter Can
- Insert the filter can into the neck of the bag, with the punched bottom facing down into the substrate and the lid (top) facing outward
