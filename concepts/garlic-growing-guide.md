
# Garlic Growing Guide

Garlic (Allium sativum) is one of the easiest and most rewarding crops to grow. Planted in fall and harvested in summer, garlic requires minimal maintenance and produces a high-value crop that stores for months. This guide covers varieties, planting, care, harvesting, and curing.

## Softneck vs. Hardneck

| Feature | Softneck | Hardneck |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Climate | Mild winters (zones 5-9) | Cold winters (zones 3-6) |
| Cloves per head | 12-20 (many, smaller) | 4-12 (fewer, larger) |
| Flavor | Milder, longer-lasting | Richer, more complex |
| Storage | 6-9 months | 4-6 months |
| Scapes | No scapes produced | Produces edible scapes |
| Braiding | Yes — flexible stems | No — stiff stem |

### Softneck Varieties
- **Artichoke** — most common supermarket garlic; mild, many cloves (e.g., California Early, California Late).
- **Silverskin** — strongest-flavored softneck; longest storage (up to 12 months); best for braiding (e.g., Silver Rose, Nookta Rose).

### Hardneck Varieties
- **Rocambole** — rich, complex flavor; easy to peel; doesn't store as long (e.g., Spanish Roja, German Red).
- **Porcelain** — large cloves (4-7 per head), bold flavor, good storage, produces large scapes (e.g., Music, Georgian Fire).
- **Purple Stripe** — beautiful purple-striped wrappers, rich flavor (e.g., Chesnok Red — excellent for roasting).
- **Creole** — adapted to warm climates, long-storing, moderate clove count (e.g., Ajo Rojo).

### Elephant Garlic
- Not true garlic — a leek relative ([[allium-ampeloprasum]]).
- Produces very large heads (4-6 inches) with 4-6 massive cloves.
- Milder, sweeter flavor than true garlic.
- Plant 6 inches deep and 12 inches apart (larger than true garlic).

## Planting

### Timing
- **Fall planting** (recommended): Plant 4-6 weeks before the ground freezes.
  - Zones 3-4: Late September to early October.
  - Zones 5-6: Mid-October to early November.
  - Zones 7-8: November to December.
- Spring planting is possible but produces smaller heads and may not vernalize properly.

### Soil Preparation
- Garlic needs well-drained, fertile soil with pH 6.0-7.0.
- Work 2-3 inches of compost into the bed before planting.
- Add bone meal or [[rock-phosphate]] for phosphorus (bulb development).
- Avoid fresh manure — can promote disease.

### Clove Selection and Preparation
- Use only seed garlic from a reputable source (not supermarket garlic — may be treated or poorly adapted).
- Break heads into individual cloves just before planting.
- Plant only the largest, healthiest cloves. Small cloves produce small heads.
- Keep the papery wrapper intact on each clove.

### Planting Depth and Spacing
- Plant cloves 2-3 inches deep (pointy end up, flat end down).
- Space cloves 4-6 inches apart in rows 12-18 inches apart.
- In raised beds, can space 6 inches apart in all directions (9 per square foot in SFG).

### Mulching After Planting
- Apply 4-6 inches of straw or shredded leaves after planting.
- Mulch insulates soil, prevents frost heaving, and suppresses weeds.
- Leave mulch in place through winter and spring.

## Spring Care

- **Remove mulch partially** in spring when new shoots emerge — pull back to 1-2 inches to let soil warm.
- **Side-dress with nitrogen** when shoots are 6 inches tall: [[blood-meal]], composted manure, or [[fish-emulsion]]. Repeat every 3-4 weeks until bulbing begins.
- **Keep consistently watered** — garlic needs 1-2 inches of water per week during active growth.
- **Stop watering** 2-3 weeks before expected harvest date (reduces disease and improves curing).
- **Weed diligently** — garlic is a poor competitor against weeds.

## Scaping (Hardneck Only)

- Scapes are the curly flower stalks produced by hardneck garlic in late spring.
- **Remove scapes** when they curl into a full loop (1-2 loops).
- Scaping directs plant energy from flower production to bulb sizing.
- Removing scapes increases bulb size by 15-30%.
- Scapes are edible — use them like garlic scallions (pesto, stir-fry, grilled, pickled).

## Harvesting

### Timing
- Harvest when 40-60% of the lower leaves have turned brown and dried.
- Too early: wrappers are thin, heads don't store well.
- Too late: cloves begin to separate, heads fall apart, storage suffers.
- **Don't wait for all leaves to die** — that's too late.
- Typical harvest window: late June to mid-July in zones 5-6.

### How to Harvest
- Loosen soil with a garden fork (not a shovel — too easy to cut heads).
- Lift gently from below — do not pull by the stem.
- Shake off excess soil — do NOT wash garlic (moisture promotes rot).
- Handle gently — bruised garlic does not store well.

## Curing

Proper curing is essential for long storage. Garlic needs to dry slowly in a warm, dry, well-ventilated space.

### Curing Process
1. Tie garlic in bundles of 8-12 (bunch by stems).
2. Hang bundles or lay on wire racks in a dry, shaded, well-ventilated area.
3. Ideal conditions: 70-80°F, 50-60% humidity, good airflow, no direct sun.
4. Cure for 3-4 weeks (hardneck) or 2-3 weeks (softneck).
5. Garlic is cured when the stem is completely dry and brittle, the outer wrapper is papery, and the neck is tight.

### After Curing
- Trim roots to 1/2 inch.
- Cut stems to 1-2 inches (or leave long for braiding softneck varieties).
- Do NOT remove all wrappers — they protect the cloves.
- Brush off remaining soil gently.

## Storage

- Store in a cool (55-65°F), dry (40-50% humidity), dark, well-ventilated area.
- **Do NOT refrigerate** — cold + moisture triggers sprouting.
- Mesh bags, paper bags, or open baskets work well.
- Hang braided softneck garlic in a kitchen or pantry.
- **Softneck**: 6-9 months (Silverskin up to 12 months).
- **Hardneck**: 4-6 months — use these first.
- **Elephant garlic**: 8-10 months.
- Inspect monthly; remove any heads showing mold or sprouting immediately.

## Common Problems

| Problem | Cause | Prevention |
|---------|-------|------------|
| Small heads | Planted small cloves; too close; insufficient nitrogen | Plant largest cloves, space properly, fertilize |
| Rotting in ground | Poor drainage, overwatering | Ensure well-drained soil, reduce watering late |
| White rot | Soil-borne fungus (Sclerotium cepivorum) | Rotate alliums 4+ years; avoid planting near old allium beds |
| Bulb mites | Tiny mites in stored garlic | Cure thoroughly; inspect before storage; freeze seed garlic 48h before planting |
