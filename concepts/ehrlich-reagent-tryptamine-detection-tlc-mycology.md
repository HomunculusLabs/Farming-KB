# Ehrlich's Reagent Detection of Tryptamine Compounds in Mycology

## Overview

Ehrlich's reagent typically consists of p-dimethylaminobenzaldehyde (p-DMAB) dissolved in a solution of hydrochloric acid and ethanol. The standard preparation involves dissolving approximately 1 gram of p-DMAB in 50 mL of 95% ethanol, followed by the addition of 50 mL of concentrated hydrochloric acid. Alternative formulations may adjust the acid concentration or substitute ethanol with methanol depending on the specific analytical application and the substrate being tested.

The reagent should be prepared fresh or stored in amber glass bottles at low temperature to prevent photodegradation of the aldehyde component. Over time, the reagent may develop a yellow or brown discoloration indicating decomposition, at which point it should be replaced. Freshly prepared reagent provides the most reliable and intense color reactions, which is particularly important when working with low-concentration samples or when quantitative comparison between spots is required.

## Application in Thin-Layer Chromatography (TLC)

In the Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) synthesis work published in the Journal of Natural Products, Ehrlich's reagent was employed alongside UV visualization at 254 nm for spot detection on TLC plates. The researchers utilized two types of stationary phases: precoated silica gel 60 F254 (manufactured by Merck) and aminopropyl silica gel (Chromatorex NH, produced by Fuji Silysia Chemical, Ltd., Aichi, Japan). The dual-detection approach provides complementary analytical information, as UV absorption detects aromatic compounds generally, while Ehrlich's reagent specifically identifies indole-containing molecules.

The standard TLC protocol involves spotting the sample alongside reference standards on the plate, developing the plate in an appropriate solvent system, drying the plate thoroughly, and then spraying or dipping the plate in Ehrlich's reagent. Heat may be applied to accelerate color development, though this must be done carefully to avoid charring the plate. The resulting colored spots are compared to reference standards by their Rf values and color characteristics.

TLC with Ehrlich's reagent visualization remains one of the most accessible methods for identifying psilocin and psilocybin in mushroom samples and synthesis intermediates. The technique requires minimal equipment, consisting only of TLC plates, a developing chamber, the reagent, and a UV lamp. This accessibility makes it particularly valuable in resource-limited settings and for preliminary screening in forensic laboratories.
