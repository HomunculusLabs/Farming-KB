# Cattle Health and Production

Detailed health management, vaccination schedules, growth and finishing data, dairy management, manure management, and draft power for cattle. See [[cattle-husbandry]] for breed selection, housing, feeding, and breeding.

## Health

### Common Health Issues

| Problem | Symptoms | Prevention |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Bloat | Distended left side, distress | Avoid sudden pasture changes, bloat blocks |
| Respiratory | Coughing, nasal discharge, fever | Good ventilation, vaccination |
| Pinkeye | Weepy, red, swollen eyes | Face [[jadam-fly-control-livestock]], [[grassland-pasture-management-mollison]] |
| Footrot | Lameness, swelling above hoof | Dry footing, foot baths (zinc sulfate) |
| Mastitis | Swollen, hot udder, clumpy milk | Clean milking hygiene, dry cow treatment |
| Coccidiosis (calves) | Bloody diarrhea, dehydration | Clean calf housing, coccidiostat |
| Lice/worms | Rubbing, rough coat, weight loss | Deworming schedule, pour-on treatments |
| Grass tetany | Staggering, convulsions (spring) | Magnesium supplementation |

### Vaccination Schedule

| Timing | Vaccine |
|--------|---------|
| Calf (2-4 months) | IBR, BVD, PI3, BRSV (respiratory complex) |
| Calf (4-6 months) | Clostridial 7-way + 8-way (blackleg) |
| Calf (6-8 months) | Leptospirosis, Brucellosis (heifers) |
| Pre-breeding (heifers) | Vibriosis, Lepto 5-way booster |
| Annually (all) | Clostridial booster, respiratory booster |
| Pre-calving (cows) | Scours vaccine (to protect calf via colostrum) |

### Deworming

- Strategic deworming: treat based on fecal egg counts, not calendar
- Rotate drug classes to prevent resistance
- Oral drenches, pour-ons, or injectables available
- Pasture management ([[comparison-rotational-grazing-vs-continuous-grazing]], mixed species) reduces parasite load
- Multi-species grazing with sheep or goats breaks cattle parasite cycles

## Growth and Finishing

### Growth Timeline (Beef)

| Age | Weight | Notes |
|-----|--------|-------|
| Birth | 60-100 lbs | Depends on breed |
| 6 months (weaning) | 400-600 lbs | Move to grower diet |
| 12 months | 700-900 lbs | Growth phase |
| 18 months | 900-1200 lbs | Finishing phase |
| 20-24 months | 1000-1400 lbs | Slaughter weight |

### Finishing

- Grass-finished: 24-30 months, slower but premium market
- Grain-finished: 18-22 months, faster gain, conventional
- Grass-finished requires high-quality pasture through the entire finishing period
- Dressing percentage: 55-65% of live weight

### Cuts of Beef

- Chuck: 25-30% (roasts, ground beef)
- Rib: 8-10% (steaks, roasts)
- Loin: 15-18% (T-bone, porterhouse, sirloin)
- Round: 20-22% (roasts, steaks)
- Flank/plate/brisket: 10-12%
- Shank/sweetbreads/organ meats: remainder

## Dairy (Brief)

- See [[goat-husbandry]] for dairy management principles that apply similarly
- Milking: 1-2 times daily, clean equipment essential
- Mastitis prevention: dip teats before and after milking
- Milk must be cooled to 38F within 1 hour of milking
- Raw milk laws vary by state -- check local regulations
- See [[selling-farm-products-and-business]] for regulations

## Manure Management

- One cow produces approximately 80-100 lbs of manure per day
- NPK (fresh): approximately 0.6-0.2-0.4
- Must be composted before garden use
- [[comparison-bokashi-vs-hot-composting-method]]: reaches 130-160F, kills pathogens and weed seeds
- Composting time: 3-6 months minimum
- Composted cow manure is excellent for [[soil-fertility-management]]
- See [[animal-byproducts]] for detailed composition data

## Draft Power (Oxen)

- Any breed can be trained as oxen (steers castrated after training begins)
- Start training at 6-12 months
- Yoke: neck yoke or head yoke
- Can plow, haul logs, pull carts, cultivate
- One pair of oxen can do the work of a small tractor
- Require consistent daily handling and training
- Dexters and Devons are popular for oxen due to manageable size

## Integration with Homestead Systems

- Cattle graze pasture that sheep have clipped short
- Manure compost feeds garden fertility
- Multi-species grazing reduces parasite load for all species
- Cow-calf operation provides meat, income, and manure
- Dairy cow provides milk for cheese, butter, yogurt
- Oxen provide draft power for farm work
- See [[homesteading-livestock-comparison]] for comparison with other livestock
