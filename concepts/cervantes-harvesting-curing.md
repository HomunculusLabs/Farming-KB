# [[cannabis-harvesting-and-curing-techniques]]

**Source:** Marijuana Horticulture: The Indoor/Outdoor Medical Grower's Bible — Jorge Cervantes

## Harvest Timing

Harvest when plants are at peak ripeness. The peak harvest window is open for about five to seven days.

**Trichome-based harvest timing:**
- Harvest when half (50%) of trichomes have turned creamy translucent for absolute peak ripeness — high will be both cerebral and physical
- Harvest when majority of trichomes are amber for a heavy body stone (best for pure indica and afghanica-dominant strains)
- Pure sativa and sativa-dominant varieties are best harvested when resin glands are clear to milky

## Pre-Harvest (7-14 Days Before)

1. **Flush nutrients:** Stop fertilization 7-10 days before harvest. Leach nutrients from growing medium. Use distilled water or water treated with reverse osmosis. Some growers use a clearing solution (e.g., Final Flush) until 3 days before harvest.
2. **Stop watering 1-2 days before harvest** — soil should be fairly dry but not so dry plants wilt. This speeds drying.
3. **Optional:** Give plants 24 hours of total darkness before harvest.
4. **Wash foliage** if sprays were applied (not recommended in last week). Do NOT wash if bud mold is a threat.

## Harvest Step-by-Step

1. Harvest in the morning when THC content is at peak
2. Cut entire plant or branches at the base with pruners
3. Remove large leaves (or remove after cutting)
4. Manicure buds by cutting leaves where they attach to the stem (leave no petiole stubs — they cause mold)
5. Hang manicured branches to dry

## Manicuring

Manicuring is easiest when leaves are soft and supple immediately after harvest. Wear rubber gloves to collect "finger hash." Scrape resin from scissors periodically. Manicure over a fine silkscreen or glass table to collect fallen resin glands.

Time estimate: 1 pound (454 g) takes 4-6 hours by hand with scissors, 1-2 hours with automatic trimmer.

## Drying

Drying converts THC from its non-psychoactive acidic form to its psychoactive neutral form. Slow drying (5-7 days or longer) yields uniformly dry buds with minimal THC decomposition. Slowly dried buds taste sweet and smoke smooth.

**Ideal drying conditions:**
- Temperature: 65-75°F (18-24°C)
- Humidity: 45-55%
- Light: None (complete darkness)
- Air circulation: gentle, no direct fan on plants

**What to avoid:**
- Above 75°F: buds dry too fast, smoke becomes harsh
- Below 65°F: drying slows, humidity climbs
- Humidity above 80%: extends drying, mold threat imminent
- Humidity below 30-40%: buds dry too fast, retain chlorophyll ("green" taste)

**Check for dryness:** Bend the stem — it should snap rather than fold. The bud should be dry to touch but not brittle.

## Curing

Curing allows buds to continue drying slowly, removing excess internal moisture and converting virtually all THC to its psychoactive form. Well-cured buds have an even glow when burned and smoke smooth.

**Curing process:**
1. Cut stems to less than 12-18 inches and place in airtight glass containers
2. Pack buds gently without forcing or damaging
3. Check in 2-4 hours for "sweating" — gently squeeze to feel moisture
4. If stems fold (too moist): remove buds, place in paper bag, check 2-3 times daily, turn buds. Return to container when dry.
5. If stems snap (fairly dry): open container 5-10 minutes every few hours to let moisture escape
6. Check several times daily; buds should be totally dry in a few days to two weeks
7. Once evenly dry, seal in airtight container for storage

**Storage:** Dark, airtight glass containers in the refrigerator. Ordinary canning jars work well. Avoid Ziploc bags (not airtight). Adding an orange or lemon peel adds citrus aroma.
