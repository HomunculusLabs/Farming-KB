# Recurrent Selection and Pedigree Breeding in Cannabis

Recurrent selection and pedigree selection are two systematic breeding
methods used to improve cannabis populations over multiple generations.
Both approaches emphasize cyclical evaluation and progressive
improvement of breeding stock toward defined goals.

## Recurrent Selection

Recurrent selection is any breeding program designed to concentrate
favorable genes scattered among a number of individuals by repeated
cycles of selection for favorable traits. Rather than a single pass of
selection, recurrent selection operates in ongoing cycles that
progressively enrich the population for desired characteristics.

The process follows three repeated steps:
1. Identify superior genotypes for the trait under selection.
2. Inter-mate the superior genotypes and select improved progeny.
3. Repeat steps 1 and 2 over a series of generations.

Each cycle of selection and inter-mating increases the frequency of
favorable alleles in the population. Over time, the average performance
of the population improves. This method is particularly useful for
traits controlled by many genes (polygenic traits) where progress
through single-generation selection is slow. Improvement is cumulative;
each cycle builds upon the gains of the previous one.

## Advantages of Recurrent Selection

Recurrent selection maintains more genetic diversity than methods like
[[cervantes-selfing-single-seed-descent]]. By inter-mating multiple
superior individuals rather than a single plant, the breeder preserves
more of the gene pool. This reduces the risk of [[cervantes-genetic-bottleneck-and-diversity-loss]] while still making
steady progress toward the breeding goal.

The method is well-suited to cannabis, a naturally out-crossing
species that evolved in wild populations of hundreds or thousands of
individuals. Working with broader populations preserves the genetic
variability that narrow breeding schemes often sacrifice.

## Combining Ability in Recurrent Selection

When breeding cross-pollinators, hybrid performance is discussed in
terms of combining ability. General combining ability (GCA) is the
average performance of a line across many hybrid combinations. Specific
combining ability (SCA) is the performance of a specific cross
combination. See [[cervantes-progeny-testing-combining-ability]] for
details on testing combining ability.

## Pedigree Selection

Pedigree selection is a system of breeding in which individual plants
are selected in the segregating generations from a cross on the basis
of their desirability, judged individually, and on the basis of a
pedigree record. Unlike mass selection, pedigree selection tracks the
ancestry and performance of each family line across generations.

The pedigree method requires the breeder to maintain detailed records
of each cross, including the parent plants used, the traits observed in
the progeny, and the performance of each family line over time. This
record-keeping enables the breeder to make informed decisions about
which families to continue and which to discard.

An advantage of pedigree selection over mass selection is the ability
to distinguish between family means and individual plant performance. A
plant from a poor family may look good individually but still carry
hidden deleterious alleles, while a plant from a consistently
performing family is more likely to breed true for desirable traits.

## Selection Pressure

Breeders select plants for future breeding on the basis of their
desirable characteristics. There is no universally correct ranking of
priority for selecting one trait over another; this is purely based on
what traits are most important to the breeder's goal.

For indoor cultivation, priority traits include short, squat, bushy
growth; large, densely formed buds; discernible taste or particular
flavors and aromas; high THC content and quality of effect; and
resistance to specific insects or diseases. It is always a good general
rule to look for plants with overall vigor and good health.

## Applying Selection Pressures

Suitable plants can be exposed to particular pathogens or environments
as a selection pressure. Growing potential breeding parents in a mold-
prone environment, for example, exposes genetic strengths or weaknesses
associated with disease resistance. For potent varieties, only the most
potent plants should be selected after harvest evaluation.

Selecting the earliest of the most potent plants is a better way to
preserve potency than selecting the most potent of the early plants.
The priority depends on what traits the breeder ranks in importance.
