# Recurrent Selection and Pedigree Breeding in Cannabis

Recurrent selection and pedigree selection are two systematic breeding
methods used to improve cannabis populations over multiple generations.
Both approaches emphasize cyclical evaluation and progressive
improvement of breeding stock toward defined goals.

## Recurrent Selection

Recurrent selection is any [[cervantes-breeding-program-design-goal-setting]] designed to concentrate
favorable genes scattered among a number of individuals by repeated
cycles of selection for favorable traits. Rather than a single pass of
selection, recurrent selection operates in ongoing cycles that
progressively enrich the population for desired characteristics.

The process follows three repeated steps:
1. Identify superior genotypes for the trait under selection.
2. Inter-mate the superior genotypes and select improved progeny.
3. Repeat steps 1 and 2 over a series of generations.

Each cycle of selection and inter-mating increases the frequency of
favorable alleles in the population. Over time, the average performance
of the population improves. This method is particularly useful for
traits controlled by many genes (polygenic traits) where progress
through single-generation selection is slow. Improvement is cumulative;
each cycle builds upon the gains of the previous one.

## Advantages of Recurrent Selection

Recurrent selection maintains more genetic diversity than methods like
[[cervantes-genetic-bottleneck-and-diversity-loss]] while still making
steady progress toward the breeding goal.

The method is well-suited to cannabis, a naturally out-crossing
species that evolved in wild populations of hundreds or thousands of
individuals. Working with broader populations preserves the genetic
variability that narrow breeding schemes often sacrifice.
