# Recommended Protocols for Sampling Macrofungi

Standardized sampling protocols are essential for producing comparable data across sites and studies. Three complementary approaches are recommended for comprehensive macrofungal inventories.

## 1. Opportunistic Sampling

Carefully walking through a study site and collecting conspicuous sporocarps across as many habitats as possible. This technique does not yield quantitative data but is an important adjunct to plot-based methods because additional species will be seen "off plot" due to the patchy distribution of sporocarps.

## 2. Fixed-Size Plot Sampling for Conspicuous Macrofungi

### Plot Setup
1. Select representative areas for permanent plots in important forest or grassland types
2. Each plot: 10 transects, 100 m long, parallel at 10-m intervals
3. Transects marked every 5 m with flags/stakes (lettered A-J, numbered 1-20)
4. Total: 200 subplots of 5 m2 each = 0.1 ha per plot

### Sampling
- Circumscribe 5-m2 circular subplots around each flag using a 1.262-m rope
- Collect all macrofungi in each subplot
- Label with transect letter and subplot number
- Note substratum (soil, leaf litter, wood) for each specimen
- Transport to field station for sorting, describing, photographing, and drying

### Sampling Frequency
- Ideally every 2 weeks during fruiting season
- If resources limited, determine peak fruiting times and adjust schedule accordingly
- Based on Chicago area data, peak fruiting times can be determined from one year of intensive sampling plus herbarium records

## 3. Microlot Sampling for Small Ascomycetes

### Purpose
Not to cover a lot of area but to obtain quantitative samples of microfungi growing on small substrata, especially rarely collected species.

### Protocol
1. Establish microplots adjacent to macrofungal subplots
2. Use 0.56-m or 1.128-m pipe to circumscribe 1-m2 circular subplots
3. Collect ALL microfungi on twigs, branches, and leaves within the plot
4. Sort under stereomicroscope; determine fertility
5. Describe, divide, and dry specimens
6. Note substratum (branch, twig, leaf) for each

### Sampling Intensity
- Most individuals can sample only 2-3 plots per week due to time involved
- Minimum: sample twice per year (middle and end of fruiting season)

## 4. Fixed-Number Log Sampling

For fungi on large woody substrata (>20 cm diameter, >2 m length):
1. Select 30 logs per decay class per site (3 classes)
2. Mark, number, map, measure, and identify host species
3. Collect 2-3 sporocarps of common species; multiple of rare species
4. Section large polypores radially (1.0-1.5 cm width) for drying
5. Sample 4 times per year in tropical areas

## Data Management and Analysis

- Extrapolate site richness from samples
- Complementarity tests to evaluate sampling efficiency
- Archive voucher specimens in recognized herbaria
- Standardized data enables comparison across studies and sites

## Key Constraints

The greatest constraints on macrofungal diversity studies are:
1. Paucity of fungal taxonomists
2. Limited identification resources
3. No region has a complete mycota equivalent to a vascular-plant flora
