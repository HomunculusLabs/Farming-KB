# Korean Natural Farming: IMO Collection and Fermented Plant Extracts

## Overview

Korean Natural Farming (KNF), developed by Master Han Kyu Cho (Cho Han Kyu) in the 1960s-2000s, is a sustainable agricultural system that uses locally sourced, naturally fermented inputs to build soil biology, nourish crops, and manage [[mushroom-pests-and-diseases]] without external chemical inputs. The two foundational inputs in KNF are [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]] (IMO) and Fermented Plant Extracts (FPE), which together establish and feed a diverse, self-sustaining soil food web.

## Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO)

IMO are naturally occurring microorganisms collected from the local environment and cultured through a series of progressive fermentation stages. The principle is that locally adapted microorganisms are more effective than imported laboratory strains because they are already acclimated to local soils, climate, and plant communities.

### IMO Collection: Stage 1

The process begins with collecting microbes from a pristine forest or undisturbed area:

- **Site selection**: Choose a shaded, undisturbed area under deciduous trees in a mature forest. The site should have deep leaf litter, good drainage, and minimal human disturbance. Bamboo groves and old-growth forest edges are preferred.
- **Medium preparation**: Fill a wooden box (30 × 20 × 10 cm) with steamed rice (not overcooked — grains should be separate, not mushy). The rice provides a starch-rich substrate for initial microbial colonization. Do not add salt or oil.
- **Placement**: Place the box in the selected forest site and cover loosely [[building-with-natural-materials]] — first with unwaxed paper or broad leaves, then with a layer of fallen leaves, and finally a piece of cardboard or thin wood to protect from rain while allowing air exchange.
- **Collection period**: Leave for 3-7 days (typically 5 days). [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]] should be 20-30°C. The rice will develop a fuzzy growth of bacteria, yeasts, and fungi.
- **Selection criteria**: Successful IMO shows white (fungal), yellow (yeast), or mixed white-pink-orange growth. Blue, green, or black molds indicate undesirable contamination — discard and retry at a different site or time.
- **Season**: Best collected in warm, humid conditions (late spring through early fall). Winter collection is possible but slower.

### IMO Stage 2: Expansion with Brown Sugar

The collected microbes are expanded and preserved through a lactic acid fermentation:

- Mix the colonized rice with equal weight of brown sugar (or palm sugar) in a ceramic, glass, or wooden container. Do not use metal containers.
- The sugar creates osmotic conditions that favor [[cho-lactic-acid-bacteria-preparation]] (LAB) over competitors, while providing carbon for fermentation.
- Fill the container to ¾ capacity to allow gas expansion.
- Cover with breathable cloth (not airtight). Ferment at room temperature (20-30°C) for 7-14 days.
- Successful fermentation produces a sweet-sour aroma (like sweet wine or vinegar) and a dark amber liquid. The rice grains become soft and may float.
- This liquid is "IMO-2" or "LAB serum," containing a diverse community of bacteria, yeasts, and fungal spores.

### IMO Stage 3: Soil-Based Expansion

The microbial community is further diversified and increased by incorporating local soil microorganisms:

- Mix IMO-2 with approximately equal weight of fine soil from the top 5-10 cm of a well-managed garden or the original forest collection site.
- Add roughly 3× this combined weight of rice bran or wheat bran as a carbon source.
- Moisten to 60-70% [[pf-tek-vermiculite-type-comparison-water-holding-capacity]] (firm squeeze produces a few drops).
- Build into a mound or pile (30-40 cm height, covered with straw or breathable cloth).
- Maintain at 30-35°C for 5-7 days, turning daily to ensure even fermentation and prevent overheating.
- White mycelial growth indicates successful fermentation. A sweet, fermented aroma should develop. Sour, putrid, or ammonia smells indicate problems (too wet, anaerobic, or contaminated).

### IMO Stage 4: Final Field Application Preparation

The final stage prepares IMO for field application at scale:

- Mix Stage 3 material with additional rice bran, native soil, and optional amendments (finely crushed eggshells for calcium, charcoal for microbial habitat).
- Build into a larger pile (40-60 cm height), cover, and ferment for 5-7 days with daily turning.
- The finished product is a dark, crumbly, sweet-smelling material teeming with diverse microorganisms.
- Apply to fields at 100-200 kg per 1,000 m², typically incorporated into the top 5-10 cm of soil or applied as a top-dressing mulch.
- Best applied in early morning or evening, or before rain, to minimize UV damage to surface microbes.

## Fermented Plant Extracts (FPE)

FPEs are made by extracting [[cordyceps-cancer-bioactive-compounds]] from specific plants through lactic acid fermentation with brown sugar:

### FPE Preparation Method

- **Plant selection**: Choose plants with desired properties based on their growth stage:
  - **Vegetative stage plants** (young, actively growing): Rich in nitrogen, growth hormones (auxins, gibberellins), and amino acids. Examples: young comfrey, nettles, bamboo shoots, legume tips.
  - **Reproductive/fruiting stage plants**: Rich in phosphorus, potassium, and trace minerals. Examples: banana trunks, pumpkin, papaya, ripe fruits.
  - **Specific medicinal plants**: Selected for bioactive compounds targeting particular functions.
- **Harvesting**: Collect plant material in the early morning when sugar and nutrient content is highest. Use fresh material — do not wash (surface microbes contribute to fermentation).
- **Processing**: Chop or crush plant material to increase surface area. Do not blend to a puree (excess liquid dilutes the fermentation).
- **Mixing**: Alternate layers of chopped plant material and brown sugar (1:1 by weight) in a container. Fill to ¾ capacity.
- **Fermentation**: Cover with breathable cloth. Ferment at room temperature for 7-14 days. The plant material releases liquid as cells rupture and sugar draws out sap through osmosis.
- **Extraction**: Strain the liquid through cloth. This is the Fermented Plant Extract. The solid residue can be added to compost or used as livestock feed supplement.

### Common FPEs and Their Uses

- **Comfrey FPE**: High in potassium, allantoin, and growth hormones. Used during flowering and fruiting phases. Promotes cell division and wound healing.
- **Banana FPE**: Rich in potassium and phosphorus. Supports fruit development and sweetness. Made from pseudostem and fruit peels.
- **Bamboo FPE**: High in silica. Strengthens cell walls, improves disease resistance and structural integrity. Applied during vegetative growth.
- **Fish Amino Acid (FAA)**: Made from fish waste fermented with brown sugar. Rich in amino acids, nitrogen, and minerals. Powerful growth stimulant applied during vegetative growth.
- **[[cho-oriental-herbal-nutrient-preparation]] (OHN)**: A concentrated multi-herb extract made from garlic, ginger, licorice, cinnamon, angelica, and other medicinal herbs. Used as a general vitality booster and pest deterrent. Prepared by sequential fermentation of individual herbs, then blending.

## KNF Input Application Philosophy

- **Growth stage matching**: Different inputs are applied at different plant growth stages. Early vegetative: nitrogen-rich inputs (FAA, young-plant FPEs). Transition: balanced inputs. Flowering/fruiting: P/K-rich inputs (banana FPE, comfrey FPE, OHN).
- **Dilution rates**: Most liquid KNF inputs are applied at 1:500 to 1:1000 dilution. OHN is used at 1:1000. Over-application can cause nutrient burn or microbial imbalance.
