# Design Ethics and Principles

The ethical foundation of permaculture was derived from research into community
ethics adopted by older religious and cooperative groups. Although many such
guidelines contained up to 18 principles, most can be included in the three
foundational ethics of permaculture.

## The Three Ethics of Permaculture

1. **CARE OF THE EARTH:** Provision for all life systems to continue and
   multiply.

2. **CARE OF PEOPLE:** Provision for people to access those resources
   necessary to their existence.

3. **SETTING LIMITS TO POPULATION AND CONSUMPTION:** By governing our own
   needs, we can set resources aside to further the above principles.

This ethic is a very simple statement of guidance, and serves well to
illuminate everyday endeavours. It can be coupled to a determination to make
our own way: to be neither employers nor employees, landlords nor tenants,
but to be self-reliant as individuals and to cooperate as groups.

## Rules of Necessitous and Conservative Use

From case histories, Mollison derives practical rules of use:

**RULE OF NECESSITOUS USE:** Leave any natural system alone until we are, of
strict necessity, forced to use it.

**RULES OF CONSERVATIVE USE:** Having found it necessary to use a natural
resource, insist on every attempt to:

- Reduce waste, hence pollution
- Thoroughly replace lost minerals
- Do a careful energy accounting
- Make an assessment of the long-term, negative, biosocial effects on
  society, and act to buffer or eliminate these

These rules evolve from assessing our best course for survival. We widen the
self-interested idea of human survival to include the survival of natural
systems, recognising that our fates are intertwined.

## The Law of Return

The only law offered to us by education derived from nature is the law of
return: "Whatever we take, we must return." Inherent in this law are the
concepts of replanting, recycling, durability, and the correct or beneficial
disposal of wastes. Nature has extreme penalties for those who break such
laws, and for their descendants and neighbours.

## Principles of Natural Systems

Birch states six principles that govern natural systems:

1. Nothing in nature grows forever
2. Everything has a limit to its growth and development
3. Everything is in some way connected to everything else
4. Nothing is constant; change is the only constant
5. Resources are finite and ultimately limited
6. There is no free lunch; everything costs something

## Thermodynamics and Design

The basic law of thermodynamics states that all energy entering an organism,
population or ecosystem can be accounted for as energy which is stored or
leaves. No energy conversion system is ever completely efficient. The total
energy of the universe is constant and the total entropy is increasing.

The question for the designer becomes: "How can I best use energy before it
passes from my site or system?" Our strategy is to set up an interception net
from source to sink. This net is a compound web of life and technologies,
designed to catch and store as much energy as possible.

## Energy Pathways in Design

Life systems constantly organise and create complex storages from diffuse
energy and materials, accumulating, decomposing, building, and transforming
them for further use. For example, manure can be routed through multiple
transformations:

1. Fermented and distilled to alcohol
2. Routed through a biogas digester for methane
3. Liquid effluent sent to fields
4. Solid sludge fed to worms for horticultural soil
5. Worms used to feed fish or poultry

Each step creates additional useful resources from what would otherwise be a
single-use product.

## From Earth Care to People Care

Having developed an earthcare ethic, we observe that cooperative species and
associations of self-supporting species make healthy communities. This leads
to a resolve to cooperate and take support roles in society, to foster an
interdependence which values individual contributions. We expand people care
to species care, for all life has common origins.

## Evolution of Ethics

Ethics are evolved by:

1. Information gathering about natural systems
2. Reflection on the long-term effects of behaviours
3. Careful investigation of case histories
4. Moderating, abandoning, or forbidding harmful behaviours
5. Expanding care from self to family to community to all life

These then are the ethics expressed in permaculture. Having evolved ethics,
we can devise ways to apply them to our lives, economies, gardens, land, and
nature. This is the mechanisms of mature ethical behaviour, or how to act
to sustain the earth.
