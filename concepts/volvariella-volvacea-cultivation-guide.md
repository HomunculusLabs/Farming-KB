# [[volvariella-volvacea]] Cultivation Guide

Volvariella volvacea, the [[paddy-straw-mushroom]] or Chinese Mushroom, is
a tropical and subtropical species commonly occurring in decomposing
straw throughout the Orient. Mushrooms are whitish at first, becoming
dark tan as the veil tears, eventually fading to pale tan with age.
Fruitbodies are enveloped by a sheath-like universal veil that breaks
as they mature, leaving an irregular sack (volva) at the stem base.
The cap is egg-shaped at first, expanding through hemispherical to
convex to plane. Spores are pinkish to pinkish brown in mass. This
warmth-loving species is one of the most commercially important
mushrooms in Asia.

## Growth Parameters and Spawn Preparation

Mycelial types range from fast-growing rhizomorphic to slow cottony,
typically white to grayish white. Spawn medium is rice straw or rye
grain. The traditional fruiting substrate is rice straw composted for
1-2 days. More recently, Hu (1974) found that cotton waste
supplemented with wheat bran (5%) and calcium carbonate (5-6%) by
weight, composted for 3 days, pasteurized for 2 hours at 140 degrees
F, conditioned for 8 hours at 125 degrees F, then gradually lowered to
77 degrees F over 8-12 hours, produced higher yields than traditional
rice straw. Moisture content should be 65-70% for rice straw and 70%
for cotton waste mixtures. Chang (1978) recommended combining rice
straw and cotton waste in 2:1 or 1:1 proportions by weight.

Spawn run parameters are notably warm: relative humidity 90+%, substrate
temperature 88-95 degrees F for fastest growth, duration 4-6 days for
thorough colonization, CO2 at 5,000-10,000 ppm, 0 fresh air exchanges
per hour, and incubation in total darkness. The extremely fast spawn
run of 4-6 days and high temperature preference reflect the species'
tropical origins. No casing layer is needed, which simplifies
cultivation considerably.

## Pinning and Cropping Requirements

Pinhead initiation parameters: relative humidity 95+%, air temperature
82-88 degrees F, duration 4 days, CO2 below 1,000 ppm, fresh air
exchanges 2-4 per hour, light from diffuse natural or direct grow-light
fluorescent for 12-18 hours per day, and regular misting once to twice
daily. The warm pinning temperature of 82-88 degrees F is among the
highest of any cultivated mushroom species.

Cropping parameters: relative humidity 85-92%, air temperature 82-88
degrees F, duration 5-7 weeks, CO2 below 600 ppm (the lowest CO2
tolerance of any species in the book), fresh air exchanges 2-4 per
hour, flushing intervals 5-10 days, harvest directly before rupture of
the universal veil. The harvest timing is critical because the volva
must be intact for proper identification to distinguish V. volvacea
from deadly Amanita species. Regular misting prevents cap cracking and
keeps resting pinheads viable.

## Yield Potential and Substrate Efficiency

Average commercial yields on rice straw are 22-28 kilograms of fresh
mushrooms per 100 kilograms of dry straw. Optimum yields on cotton
waste compost are 25-35 kilograms per 100 kilograms of substrate.
Maximum yields approach 45 kilograms per 100 kilograms on cotton waste
compost, making this substrate clearly superior. The moisture content
of harvested mushrooms is 88-90% water and 10-12% dry matter.
Nutritional content includes crude protein at 21.2% of dry weight and
91 milligrams of niacin per 100 grams dry weight.

In contrast to other straw-growing species, V. volvacea does not
compare favorably in terms of yield per unit substrate. The smaller
crop figures are likely a result of early picking when mushrooms are
most flavorful. However, the extremely short total crop cycle from
spawning to first harvest (approximately 8-10 days) allows multiple
cropping cycles per year, potentially compensating for lower per-cycle
yields.

## Strain Maintenance and Difficulties

Several researchers have noted the difficulty of maintaining high-yielding
strains of V. volvacea for any length of time. The mycelium appears to
have limited transfer potential and should be stored at moderate
temperatures (50 degrees F). Cultures are frequently renewed through
multispore germinations. This senescence problem means that growers
cannot maintain a productive strain indefinitely through serial
transfers and must periodically return to spore cultures to restore
vigor. The genetic characteristics include basidia that are tetrapolar
(4-spored) with a primary homothallic mating system. Clamp connections
are present and chlamydospores form readily.

The species is primarily grown in the Orient and is well-suited to
regions with warm climates where other commercial species fail. The
requirement for no casing layer, combined with the fast spawn run and
rapid cropping cycle, makes it one of the simplest mushrooms to
cultivate provided adequate temperatures can be maintained. However,
the need for precise identification at harvest (to distinguish from
Amanita phalloides and similar deadly species) requires that
cultivators be thoroughly familiar with the genus Volvariella.

## Practical Considerations for Home Cultivation

For home cultivators in temperate climates, V. volvacea presents the
challenge of maintaining high temperatures (82-95 degrees F)
throughout the crop cycle. This typically requires dedicated heating
equipment. The use of cotton waste supplemented with wheat bran and
calcium carbonate is recommended over plain rice straw for higher
yields. The complete absence of casing requirements simplifies the
process significantly. Multiple crops can be grown in quick succession
during warm months. For further reference, consult S.T. Chang (1972)
"The Chinese Mushroom" and Chang (1978) in The Biology and Cultivation
of Edible Mushrooms.

## Key Points

- Spawn run temperature: 88-95 degrees F; duration only 4-6 days
- No casing layer required, simplifying cultivation
- Pinning temperature: 82-88 degrees F; CO2 tolerance below 600 ppm
- Harvest before universal veil rupture for identification safety
- Cotton waste substrate outperforms rice straw (25-35 vs 22-28 kg/
  100 kg)
- Maximum yields approach 45 kg per 100 kg on cotton waste compost
- Strain senescence requires frequent renewal through multispore
  germination
- Primary homothallic with clamp connections and chlamydospore
  formation
- Cropping duration: 5-7 weeks with 5-10 day flushing intervals
