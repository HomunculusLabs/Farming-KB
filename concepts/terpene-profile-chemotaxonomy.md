# Terpene Profile Chemotaxonomy

Chemotaxonomy uses chemical composition profiles to classify organisms and
establish evolutionary relationships. In [[cannabis-terpene-profiles]] into functional
categories based on dominant terpenes and their associated effects:

1. **Myrcene-dominant**: Sedative, relaxing, analgesic. Dominant in
   "indica" commercial categories. Includes cultivars like Granddaddy
   Purple, Northern Lights, and Bubba Kush.

2. **Limonene-dominant**: Energizing, mood-elevating, creative. Dominant
   in "sativa" commercial categories. Includes Super Lemon Haze, Tangie,
   and Sour Diesel.

3. **Caryophyllene-dominant**: Anti-inflammatory, analgesic with
   minimal sedation. Includes Girl Scout Cookies, OG Kush, and White
   Widow.

4. **Linalool-dominant**: Anxiolytic, calming, sleep-promoting. Includes
   Amnesia Haze, Special Kush, and Master Kush.

5. **Pinene-dominant**: Focus-enhancing, bronchodilatory, alert.
   Includes Blue Dream, Dutch Treat, and Romulan.

6. **Terpinolene-dominant**: Complex effects, often described as
   simultaneously relaxing and stimulating. Includes Jack Herer and
   Golden Goat.

## Analytical Methods

Terpene profiling relies on gas chromatography with flame ionization
detection (GC-FID) or mass spectrometry (GC-MS). Headspace sampling
at 80-100 degrees Celsius captures the volatile fraction. Solid-phase
microextraction (SPME) fibers allow rapid screening without solvent
extraction.

Sample preparation significantly affects results. Fresh-frozen material
preserves monoterpenes that would otherwise volatilize during drying.
Cured flower shows higher proportions of sesquiterpenes relative to
monoterpenes compared to fresh material. This complicates direct
comparisons between fresh and cured product [[cannabis-terpene-profiles-sativa]].

## Chemotaxonomy vs. Folk Taxonomy

The traditional sativa/indica/hybrid classification system shows poor
correlation with actual terpene or cannabinoid profiles. Genetic studies
have demonstrated that "indica" and "sativa" designations primarily
reflect historical breeding lineages and morphological differences in
plant architecture rather than chemical composition.

Terpene chemotaxonomy offers an evidence-based alternative that directly
relates chemical composition to consumer experience. However, terpene
profiles are highly plastic, varying significantly with growing conditions,
[[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]], and post-harvest processing. This environmental
sensitivity means terpene-based classification requires standardized
production and analytical protocols to achieve reproducible categorization.
