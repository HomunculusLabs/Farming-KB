# Foliar Feeding Plants

## Overview

Foliar feeding is the practice of spraying dissolved nutrients onto plant leaves for absorption through leaf surfaces. While it can be useful for specific situations, it has significant limitations and should not be relied upon as a primary fertilization method.

## How Foliar Absorption Works

Nutrients in solution can enter leaves through:

- **Cuticle**: The waxy outer layer is the primary barrier. Thin cuticles (on young leaves, shade-grown plants) allow more absorption than thick cuticles (on older leaves, sun-grown plants).
- **Stomata**: Pores on leaf surfaces, primarily on the underside of leaves, allow gas exchange and can take in some dissolved nutrients
- **Epidermal cells**: Some nutrients pass directly through cell walls

Most nutrients must be in ionic (charged) form to be absorbed, the same requirement as for root uptake.

## The Mobility Limitation

The effectiveness of foliar feeding depends critically on nutrient mobility within the plant:

- **Mobile nutrients** (N, P, K, Mg, Mo, Ni, Zn): Can be transported from leaves where they are absorbed to other parts of the plant. Foliar feeding has some value for these.
- **Immobile nutrients** (Ca, B, S, Fe, Mn, Cu): Once absorbed into a leaf cell, they stay there. Spraying these nutrients only benefits the leaves that receive the spray -- they cannot be moved to growing tips, roots, or new leaves where they may be needed most.

This is why foliar feeding is of limited value for most nutrients. For macronutrients, it is impossible to supply what a plant really needs simply by spraying leaves -- plants require far more macronutrients than they could ever absorb through foliage.

## When Foliar Feeding Is Useful

### Quick Fix for Mobile Micronutrient Deficiencies

Foliar spraying is most effective as a temporary quick fix for deficiencies of iron and zinc, which are mobile in plants. Chelated micronutrient sprays can rapidly correct visible deficiency symptoms.

### Emergency Supplementation

When soil conditions (compaction, extreme pH, waterlogging) prevent root uptake, foliar feeding can bridge the gap temporarily while soil problems are corrected.

### Growth Stimulation

Some gardeners use dilute seaweed extracts or fish emulsion as foliar sprays during critical growth periods (transplanting, flowering, fruit set) for a boost of micronutrients, hormones, and enzymes.

## Surfactants

Surfactants (wetting agents) reduce the surface tension of the spray solution, allowing it to spread more evenly across leaf surfaces and penetrate the waxy cuticle more effectively. Many commercial foliar products include surfactants. A few drops of mild liquid soap can serve as a homemade surfactant, but avoid detergents that can damage leaf tissue.

## Timing Considerations

- **Early morning or late evening** is best -- stomata are open and temperatures are cool, reducing evaporation and leaf burn risk
- **Avoid midday spraying** -- high temperatures can cause rapid drying and leaf burn
- **Young leaves** absorb more readily than older leaves with thicker cuticles
- **Undersides of leaves** should be targeted -- more stomata are located there
- **Humidity** affects absorption -- very dry conditions reduce uptake

## Limitations

- Cannot supply adequate macronutrient quantities for sustained plant growth
- Immobile nutrients absorbed by leaves cannot be translocated to where they are needed
- Repeated applications are needed because foliar nutrients are temporary
- Over-application can burn leaf tissue
- Does not build soil health or support the soil food web
- Spray drift wastes material and may affect non-target plants

## Best Practices

1. Use foliar feeding as a supplement to soil-based nutrition, never a replacement
2. Focus on mobile micronutrients (iron, zinc) when deficiency symptoms appear
3. Always dilute solutions properly -- more is not better
4. Add a surfactant for better coverage
5. Spray early morning or late evening
6. Target the undersides of leaves
7. Use [[chelated-nutrients|chelated]] forms for micronutrients at higher pH

## Key Points

- Foliar feeding is a supplementary technique, not a primary fertilization method
- Only mobile nutrients can be redistributed from leaves to the rest of the plant
- Most useful for quick correction of iron and zinc deficiencies
- Timing (early morning/late evening) and surfactants improve effectiveness
- Soil-based nutrition through healthy biology is always the superior long-term approach
