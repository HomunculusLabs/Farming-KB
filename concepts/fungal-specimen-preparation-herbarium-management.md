# Fungal Specimen Preparation and Herbarium Management

Proper preservation of fungal specimens is essential for biodiversity studies, taxonomic research, and long-term documentation of species distributions. Herbarium specimens serve as the foundation for identification, phylogenetic analysis, and monitoring of environmental change.

## Specimen Preservation Methods

### Air Drying
The most common method for sporocarpic specimens:
- **Fleshy fungi:** Dry under constant flow of warm air (40-50C, preferably 42C)
- Electric fruit dryers with wire mesh shelves work well
- Large or watery specimens sliced longitudinally to hasten drying
- In areas without electricity: shelves over heat sources (fire, gas burner, oil lamp)
- Small specimens on dry substrata can be air-dried indoors, in sun, or under incandescent bulb
- Special care to avoid burning specimens over open flames
- **Dry specimens:** Keep dry and mold-free; silica gels remove moisture from storage containers
- Wrap in wax paper or place in zipping plastic bags for transport

### Silica Gel Drying
- Specimens and field labels placed in sealed glass tubes or plastic bags with silica crystals
- Only suitable for small, easily desiccated specimens
- Rapid and effective

### Freeze-Drying
- Specimen frozen, then frozen moisture vaporized under vacuum
- Expensive equipment required
- Specimens maintain shape — good for displays
- Become brittle and absorb moisture readily
- After 5-6 years storage, cells often collapse
- Must note preservation method on label

### Liquid Preservation
For delicate fungi (Phalloid, Clathroid):
- Submerge in fixative for 2-7 days, then transfer to storage solution
- Options: 70% ethanol, Kew Mixture, Copenhagen Solution (contain glycerol)
- Store in glass jars with neoprene caps; seal with wax or Parafilm
- **Important:** Chemical fixation alters DNA — fixed specimens not suitable for molecular studies
- Warm-air drying preferred when molecular analysis anticipated
- Safety: wear gloves, goggles, mask; work in fume hood

### Fungal Cultures as Vouchers
- Mature colony in Petri plate placed in frost-free freezer for 4-6 weeks
- Dried colony transferred to archival-paper envelope or thin Petri plate
- Data written on plate bottom or lid
- Paper envelopes for pyrenomycetes, many discomycetes, all loculomycetes
- Delicate taxa (zygomycetes, hyphomycetes, coelomycetes) best in thin Petri plates

## Special Specimen Types

### Plant Pathogens
Rusts, smuts on leaves/delicate stems dried as plant specimens:
- Host tissue with fungi spread on drying paper
- Interspersed blotter sheets absorb moisture
- Corrugated aluminum/cardboard sheets enhance air circulation
- Press between wooden frames with straps; check twice daily
- Place in sun or over heat source (35-45C)

### Lichens
- Air-dry on open shelves over gentle heat (42C) or in sun
- Leaflike/branched lichens can be flattened and pressed
- Brittle lichens dried with substrata; soil glued and mounted on card
- Rock-attached lichens wrapped separately in tissue paper

### Myxomycetes
- Air-dry with substrata; keep collections upright during drying
- Store dried specimens separately in small tins or boxes
- Substratum glued to herbarium paper box insert
- Folded ends allow specimen removal without handling

## Field Labels and Notes

### Minimum Required Information
1. Field identification or tentative name
2. Collector's name, collection number, date
3. Detailed locality (coordinates, elevation)
4. Habitat, substratum, and host descriptions
5. Fresh specimen appearance (color, stature, shape)

### Best Practices
- Small tag with collector's initials and collection number with specimen at all times
- Detailed field notes on archival-quality paper with permanent ink
- Computer entries need backup files and hard copies
- Color notes essential — fungi usually lose natural color during drying
- Separate notes for fluid-preserved specimens

## Herbarium Filing

- Non-lichenized fungi typically filed vertically in drawers of special cabinets
- Arrangement saves space and allows easy rearrangement
- Archival-quality materials throughout (acid-free paper, permanent ink)
- Climate control (temperature, humidity) essential for long-term preservation
