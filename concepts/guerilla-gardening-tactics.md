# Guerilla Gardening Tactics

Guerilla gardening is the act of gardening on land that the gardener does not legally own — vacant lots, roadside strips, abandoned properties, traffic islands, and other neglected urban spaces. It combines food production, ecological restoration, and community activism.

## History and Philosophy

### Origins
- Modern movement traced to Liz Christy's "Green Guerrillas" in New York City (1973)
- The Bowery-Houston Community Farm: first sanctioned guerilla garden
- Richard Reynolds: London-based guerilla gardener, author, and organizer (2004-present)
- Concept has spread globally — guerilla gardening groups in hundreds of cities

### Philosophy
- Land belongs to everyone — neglect is not a valid use
- Food should grow everywhere, not just on farms
- Green spaces improve community health, safety, and property values
- Direct action: don't wait for permission — plant first, apologize later
- Beautification as political statement

## Community Organizing

### Building Support
- Start small: one garden bed, visible to neighbors
- Talk to nearby residents — explain what you're doing and why
- Invite participation — people who help plant will protect the garden
- Share harvest — food builds community
- Organize regular work days (social events disguised as gardening)
- Create signs: "[[mollison-designers-community-garden-design-urban-permaculture]] — All Welcome to Harvest"

### Legal Considerations
- Technically trespassing in most jurisdictions
- Enforcement varies: some cities embrace guerilla gardens, others prosecute
- Best approach: garden first, seek permission/lease after establishing value
- Many guerilla gardens have become officially recognized community gardens
- Document everything: photos, community involvement, harvest totals

## Planting Public Spaces

### Street Trees
- Plant flowering herbs and bulbs around street tree bases
- Add compost to improve tree health
- Install small rain gardens to capture runoff

### Traffic Islands and Medians
- Plant drought-tolerant perennials (low maintenance)
- Native wildflowers support pollinators
- Add a few vegetables if accessible and safe

### Railway Embankments
- Plant fruit trees and berry bushes
- Hardy perennials that need no maintenance
- Train vines (grapes, kiwi) along fences
