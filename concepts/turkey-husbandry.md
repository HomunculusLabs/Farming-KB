# Turkey Husbandry

Turkeys are rewarding homestead poultry that produce premium meat and serve
as excellent foragers and pest controllers. The choice between heritage and
commercial broad-breasted strains has significant implications for
management, feed efficiency, reproduction, and meat quality. Heritage turkeys
can reproduce naturally and forage well, while broad-breasted turkeys grow
faster but cannot breed naturally.

## Heritage vs. Broad-Breasted

### Broad-Breasted White (BBW)
- The standard commercial turkey. Reaches 20-40 lbs in 16-20 weeks.
- Cannot breed naturally (too heavy to mate). Requires AI for reproduction.
- Feed conversion: 2.5-3.0 lbs feed per lb of gain (very efficient).
- Prone to leg problems, heart attacks, and heat stress at large sizes.
- White feathers — clean-looking carcass.
- Best for: one-time harvest, maximum efficiency, lowest feed cost per pound.

### Broad-Breasted Bronze (BBB)
- Similar to BBW but with bronze coloring. Slightly slower growth.
- Same breeding limitations and health issues as BBW.

### Heritage Breeds
- **Standard Bronze** — The original turkey. 16-25 lbs (toms), 10-16 lbs
  (hens). Slow-growing (24-28 weeks). Breed naturally, excellent foragers.
- **Narragansett** — Beautiful gray/tan/black pattern. 16-22 lbs (toms).
  Hardy, good mothers, calm.
- **Bourbon Red** — Rich reddish-brown. 16-25 lbs (toms). Good foragers,
  flavorful meat.
- **Royal Palm** — Striking black and white. Smaller (10-16 lbs toms).
  Primarily ornamental but edible.
- **Midget White** — Small (8-16 lbs toms). Good for small families.
  Calm, good layers.
- **Blue Slate** — Blue-gray. 18-25 lbs (toms). Good foragers, hardy.
- **Black Spanish** — All black. Hardy, good foragers.
- **Jersey Buff** — Buff-colored, hardy, good mothers.

### Comparison Summary
- Heritage turkeys can breed naturally, forage for significant portion of
  diet, and have superior flavor. They require more time and feed per pound.
- Broad-breasted turkeys grow twice as fast on half the feed but cannot
  reproduce and have more health issues.

## Raising Poults

### Brooding
- Poults are more fragile than chicks. Temperature: start at 95-100°F for
  first week, reduce 5°F per week until feathered (6-8 weeks).
- Use red heat lamps (white light causes cannibalism in poults).
- Poults often starve because they don't find feed and water easily. Place
  marbles in waterers and scatter feed on paper towels for the first few
  days.
- Add poultry vitamins/electrolytes to water for the first week.
- Poults are prone to "pasting" (feces stuck to vent). Clean gently with
  warm water.
- Provide 28-30% protein game bird starter for first 8 weeks (turkeys need
  higher protein than chickens).

### Transition to Grower Feed
- At 8 weeks, switch to 20-24% protein grower feed.
- After 16 weeks (or when fully feathered), switch to 16% protein finisher
  feed.
- Gradually introduce pasture and forage starting at 4-6 weeks.

### Early Challenges
- Starvation: poults don't instinctively eat and drink. Must be shown.
- Piling: poults crowd together, smothering bottom birds. Prevent with
  adequate space and temperature management.
- Cold sensitivity: poults chill easily. Ensure draft-free brooder.
- Coccidiosis: common in young poults. Provide medicated feed or herbal
  preventatives.

## Housing

### Brooder
- 1 sq ft per poult for first 4 weeks, expanding to 2-3 sq ft by 8 weeks.
- Dry, draft-free, well-ventilated.
- Pine shavings bedding (avoid cedar — toxic to poultry).

### Grow-Out and Adult Housing
- Minimum 4-5 sq ft per bird indoors, 10+ sq ft per bird in outdoor run.
- Turkeys prefer to roost — provide sturdy roosts at 2-4 ft height.
- Heritage turkeys can fly well — cover outdoor runs with netting or clip
  one wing.
- Nests: 1 nest per 3-4 hens, floor-level, 14x14x14 inches minimum.
- Shelter from rain and wind is essential. Turkeys are hardy in cold but
  suffer in wet conditions.
- Separate from chickens if possible — turkeys are susceptible to
  blackhead disease transmitted by chicken cecal nematodes.

## Feeding

### Commercial Feeds
- Starter (0-8 weeks): 28-30% protein game bird starter.
- Grower (8-16 weeks): 20-24% protein.
- Finisher (16+ weeks): 16% protein.
- Breeder/layer: 16-18% protein with calcium supplementation.

### Foraging
- Heritage turkeys are excellent foragers and can meet 20-40% of their
  needs on good pasture.
- They eat grass, clover, insects, seeds, and small rodents.
- Turkeys in orchards provide pest control and fertilization.
- Broad-breasted turkeys forage less and rely more on grain.

### Supplements
- Grit (insoluble granite) for digestion if on pasture.
- Oyster shell free-choice for laying hens.
- Scratch grains (corn, wheat, oats) as treats, not exceeding 10% of diet.

## Processing

### Harvest Age and Weight
- Broad-breasted: 16-20 weeks, 20-40 lbs (toms), 14-22 lbs (hens).
- Heritage: 24-28 weeks, 16-25 lbs (toms), 10-16 lbs (hens).

### Processing Steps
- Restrict feed 12-24 hours before processing (water OK).
- Dispatch: axe, killing cone, or cervical dislocation for smaller birds.
- Scald at 145-150°F for 30-60 seconds (heritage feathers are harder to
  remove than BBW).
- Pluck by hand or with a mechanical plucker.
- Singe remaining hairs over open flame.
- Eviscerate carefully. Save heart, liver, gizzard.
- Chill in ice water for at least 1 hour (longer for better texture).
- Age in refrigerator for 1-3 days before cooking for best flavor.
- See [[animal-harvest-and-processing]] for detailed procedures.

### Yield
- Dress-out percentage: 72-80% for turkeys (higher than chickens due to
  heavier breast meat).
- Breast meat is 30-35% of carcass weight.
