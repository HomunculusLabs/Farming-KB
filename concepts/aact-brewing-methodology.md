# Actively Aerated Compost Tea Brewing

Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) is a liquid biological inoculant
produced by brewing high-quality compost in water with continuous
aeration. The process extracts [[bacterial-vs-fungal-compost-teas]] by its strict reliance on
sustained aeration throughout the entire brewing process. This aerobic
environment selectively supports the growth of beneficial bacteria,
fungi, protozoa, and nematodes extracted from the compost starting
material.

The primary purpose of AACT is to rapidly increase both the population
and diversity of beneficial soil organisms and deliver them in a liquid
form that can cover large areas efficiently. This allows biological
inoculation of soil, foliage, [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-bacterial-and-fungal-dominant-soil]]

concern for compost tea brewers. These chemicals survive the composting
process and can damage or kill sensitive plants when contaminated tea
is applied. Source-aware compost selection is essential.

## Brewing Equipment Requirements

AACT brewers range from simple five-gallon home setups to large
commercial units holding hundreds of gallons. Regardless of size,
every system requires a vessel, an aeration source capable of
maintaining aerobic conditions, and a means of suspending compost
in the water column.

The air pump or compressor must deliver sufficient [[ingham-dissolved-oxygen-anaerobic-aact-brewing-management]] levels should remain above 6 mg/L at all
times during the brew.

Compost is typically placed in a mesh bag or basket suspended in the
water. This allows organism extraction while preventing large
particulate matter from clogging sprayers and application equipment.
The mesh size should be fine enough to retain compost particles while
allowing organisms to pass through.

## Brewing Time and Temperature Control

Standard AACT brew cycles run between 24 and 36 hours. During this
period, organism populations multiply significantly — bacteria can
increase from millions to billions per milliliter of tea. Brewing
beyond 36 hours risks depleting food resources and shifting the
biology toward anaerobic conditions.

composition. Ideal brewing temperatures range from 65 to 75 degrees
