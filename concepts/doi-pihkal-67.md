# DOI

## Overview

DOI (2,5-dimethoxy-4-iodoamphetamine) is one of the most potent members of the DOx family of substituted amphetamines. First documented by [[neurochemical-probes-brain-imaging-pihkal]] studies. It is a [[doet-pihkal-66]]: One subject used LSD to abort a difficult DOI experience at 9 hours, with rapid restoration of emotional connection. DOI lacks the "sparkle" characteristic of LSD but provides excellent eyes-closed imagery.

## Harm Reduction

- DOI shares all the risks of the DOx family: extremely long duration (16-30 hours), slow onset that can lead to redosing, and potential for dangerous overdose when misrepresented
- The active dose is measured in milligrams -- confusion with substances requiring 50-100x higher doses has led to hospitalizations
- Due to the extended duration, ensure a safe environment for at least 24 hours
- The S-isomer is active but less potent -- optical purity of samples is a variable
- Some subjects reported depressive emotional content, particularly at lower doses -- consider [[neurochemical-probes-brain-imaging-pihkal]]
