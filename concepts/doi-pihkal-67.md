# DOI

## Overview

DOI (2,5-dimethoxy-4-iodoamphetamine) is one of the most potent members of the DOx family of substituted amphetamines. First documented by Alexander Shulgin, DOI has become an important pharmacological research tool due to its extremely high binding affinity for 5-HT2 receptors and the utility of its radioactive iodine-labeled analogues for brain imaging studies. It is a [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] derivative with both psychedelic and mild stimulant properties.

DOI is among the longest-acting psychedelic substances known, with effects lasting 16-30 hours at active doses of 1.5-3.0 mg. Like other DOx compounds, its heavy halogen atom (iodine) contributes to both its potency and its extended duration of action.

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical name:** 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-iodoamphetamine
- **PIHKAL entry:** #67
- **Family:** DOx (2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted amphetamines)
- **Legal status:** Schedule I (US) / Controlled in most jurisdictions

## Synthesis (Summary)

DOI synthesis begins with the formation of N-(1-(2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)-2-propyl)phthalimide from 2,5-DMA and phthalic anhydride heated to ~150 degrees C. The phthalimide intermediate is then iodinated using iodine monochloride in acetic acid, yielding N-(1-(2,5-dimethoxy-4-iodophenyl)-2-propyl)phthalimide. Deprotection is achieved via Gabriel synthesis using hydrazine hydrate in ethanol under reflux overnight. The free base is converted to the hydrochloride salt (mp 200.5-201.5 degrees C).

The four-carbon butylamine homologue (ARIADNE analogue) has also been synthesized and tested; it shows no central activity at up to 4 mg, with radioisotope studies showing accumulation in the gut and liver but almost none reaching the brain.

## Dosage and Duration

| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| **Threshold** | ~0.6 mg |
| **Light dose** | 1.0 - 1.5 mg |
| **Common dose** | 1.5 - 3.0 mg |
| **Strong dose** | 3.0 - 3.5 mg |
| **Duration** | 16 - 30 hours |
| **Onset** | 2 - 3 hours |

**Isomer potency:** The R-isomer is approximately 4x more potent than the S-isomer in human trials (rat studies suggest ~2x). The S-isomer is still active but requires higher doses (6.3 mg produced a ++ in one subject). The R-isomer at 1.0 mg produces a clear ++ for 6+ hours.

## Qualitative Commentary (from PIHKAL)

**0.6 mg:** Nice spacey light-headedness for a few hours with time moving slowly. A period of generic sadness and reminiscence, resolving to a healthy mood by the seventh hour.

**1.6 mg:** Generally depressing experience with a sad view of life. Inability to connect with emotions. Aborted at 9 hours with 125 micrograms of LSD, which rapidly restored emotional presence. The subsequent erotic experience was described as deeply involved with an archetype of orgasm "shaped like a flower, richly colored, with an unusual S shape."

**3.0 mg:** Clear, clean psychedelic with excellent eyes-closed imagery -- clearly delineated patterns, pictures, and colors. Described as "perfect for an artist." Total physical ease. Still at 1.5+ at 16 hours.

**3.5 mg:** Full crashing +++ for 3-4 hours. No LSD-like sparkle, but moments of "light-headedness" where one could move sideways with reality -- an out-of-body-like experience where the body comes along. No trace of body impact. Intense imagery during sleep.

**1.0 mg R-isomer:** Clear ++ from the second to eighth hour. Sensible and functional for technical chores. Easy sleep at 15 hours.

**2.3 mg R-isomer:** Definite +++. Slight down mood toward the end. Extremely long-lasting with residual effects well into the next day.

**6.3 mg S-isomer:** Benign 1.5+ at two hours, flattening to ++. Slow-motion imagery during attempted sleep -- fantasies described as slow-paced and sluggish.

## Effects Summary

### Visual and Perceptual
- Excellent eyes-closed imagery with clearly delineated patterns, pictures, and colors
- Described as "perfect for an artist" at the 3.0 mg level
- Moments of "light-headedness" at higher doses allowing movement "sideways with reality"
- Slow-motion imagery during sleep at high doses -- fantasies described as slow-paced and sluggish
- Out-of-body-like experiences where the body accompanies consciousness rather than staying behind

### Cognitive and Emotional
- Variable emotional character: can range from spacey contentment to distinctly depressing
- At lower doses (0.6-1.6 mg), subjects reported sadness, nostalgia, and emotional disconnection
- At higher doses (3.0+ mg), the experience becomes "clear and clean" with more positive valence
- No particular help with behavioral patterns (one subject noted no help for smoking cessation)

### Physical
- Generally very easy on the body -- "total ease" reported at 3.0 mg
- "No trace of body impact" at 3.5 mg
- Extended residual stimulation making sleep difficult for 15+ hours post-dose
- Some guardedness due to intense imagery during attempted sleep

## Pharmacological Significance

DOI has played a major role in neuroscience research. Its extremely high binding capacity for 5-HT2 receptors made it invaluable for receptor mapping and characterization studies. Serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, or 5-HT) is strongly implicated in the action of all phenethylamine psychedelics, and the development of receptor subtypes (5-HT1A, 5-HT1B, 5-HT2A, 5-HT2B, etc.) was significantly advanced through studies using DOI and related compounds.

The compound has been synthesized with various radioactive iodine isotopes (including 131I), enabling researchers to track brain distribution patterns and, by extrapolation, estimate the localization of other psychedelic compounds that cannot be easily labeled.

DOI's research utility led a neurochemical company to offer it commercially as a research tool, though Shulgin noted wryly that the price had nearly doubled and telephone orders were no longer accepted -- suggesting awareness of its recreational psychedelic potential.

The four-carbon butylamine homologue of DOI has been synthesized and found completely inactive at up to 4 mg. Whole-body scanner studies with 131I-labeled material showed accumulation in the gut and liver with almost none reaching the brain, suggesting that extending the chain beyond the alpha-methyl group eliminates central activity.

## Comparisons

- vs. [[dob-pihkal-62]]: DOI (iodine) is slightly more potent than DOB (bromine) and shares the characteristic slow onset and extremely long duration. Both accumulate in the lungs before reaching the brain. DOB has been more frequently implicated in overdose cases.
- vs. [[dom-pihkal-68]]: DOM is less potent and somewhat shorter-acting. DOI and DOB represent the halogenated, high-potency end of the DOx spectrum.
- vs. [[doet-pihkal-66]]: One subject used LSD to abort a difficult DOI experience at 9 hours, with rapid restoration of emotional connection. DOI lacks the "sparkle" characteristic of LSD but provides excellent eyes-closed imagery.

## Harm Reduction

- DOI shares all the risks of the DOx family: extremely long duration (16-30 hours), slow onset that can lead to redosing, and potential for dangerous overdose when misrepresented
- The active dose is measured in milligrams -- confusion with substances requiring 50-100x higher doses has led to hospitalizations
- Due to the extended duration, ensure a safe environment for at least 24 hours
- The S-isomer is active but less potent -- optical purity of samples is a variable
- Some subjects reported depressive emotional content, particularly at lower doses -- consider [[psychedelic-set-and-setting]] carefully
- DOI has been sold commercially as a research chemical; verify source and identity of any substance
- See [[psychedelic-harm-reduction]] and [[harm-reduction-guide]] for general principles
