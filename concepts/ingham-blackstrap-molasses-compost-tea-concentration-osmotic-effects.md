## Molasses Type: Why Unsulfured Blackstrap Is Essential

### The Sulfur Problem

Ingham is emphatic that only unsulfured blackstrap molasses should be used in compost tea. The reason is simple and critical:

"Sulfur is a very potent fungicide."

Sulfured molasses contains sulfur dioxide, which was historically added as a preservative and clarifying agent during sugar refining. Even trace amounts of sulfur compounds in the tea can suppress or kill the beneficial fungi that are essential for [[air-pollution-fungal-community-responses]] on plant leaves and in soil, adding a fungicide to the brew is directly counterproductive.

### Blackstrap vs. Other Molasses Grades

Sugar refining produces several grades of molasses, each with different properties:

- **First molasses**: Lightest, highest in sugar, lowest in mineral content. Grows mostly bacteria
- **Second molasses (dark molasses)**: Intermediate. Better mineral content, some fungal benefit
- **Blackstrap molasses**: The final byproduct of sugar extraction. Lowest in simple sugars, highest in minerals, trace elements, and complex carbohydrate "residue" (primarily [[ultra-low-cost-foliar-application-methods]] of compost tea:

- At concentrations above 3-5%, molasses helps organisms adhere to leaf surfaces
- It enhances fungal growth on the leaf after application
- It reduces anaerobic bacterial growth on the leaf surface
- The complex sugars provide a food resource that helps the applied organisms establish before they exhaust their internal reserves

## Year-to-Year Variability

Ingham notes that molasses quality varies from year to year and between brands. Visual inspection of color and consistency provides clues:

- Darker, thicker molasses generally has higher mineral and humic acid content
- Lighter, thinner molasses has simpler sugar profiles that favor bacteria over fungi
- When switching brands, retest the tea biology to ensure the new product supports the desired organism balance

## Troubleshooting Molasses-Related Problems

### Anaerobic Conditions Despite Adequate Aeration

If the tea goes anaerobic during brewing and molasses rates were high:

- Reduce molasses concentration by 25-50% in the next batch
- Ensure the aeration system is adequate for the volume being brewed
- Check that the water temperature is not excessively high (above 75°F/24°C)
- Verify that the molasses is truly unsulfured — sulfur compounds can shock the aerobic microbial community
