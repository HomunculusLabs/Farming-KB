# Thermophilic Fungi

Thermophilic fungi are defined as organisms with optimum growth
temperatures above 40 degrees Celsius and maximum temperatures at or
above 50 degrees Celsius. They play critical roles in self-heating
ecosystems such as compost piles, stored grains, and various
[[fungal-decomposition-processes]]. Their [[fungal-decomposition-nutrient-cycling]]
capabilities are essential for nutrient turnover in warm environments.

## Definition and Temperature Ranges

Thermophilic fungi occupy a distinct ecological niche above the range
of most mesophilic fungi. Three categories of temperature-adapted fungi
are recognized:

- **[[thermophilic-thermotolerant-fungi-ecology-isolation]]:** grow well at mesophilic temperatures but
  can also grow at elevated temperatures up to approximately 40-45
  degrees C
- **Moderate thermophiles:** have temperature optima between 40-50
  degrees C
- **True thermophiles:** require temperatures above 40 degrees C for
  growth and have optima at 45-55 degrees C

The ability to grow at elevated temperatures confers a competitive
advantage in habitats where self-heating occurs, such as decomposing
[[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]], since few organisms can compete at these temperatures.

## Natural and Artificial Habitats

Thermophilic fungi are found in both natural and human-influenced
environments:

- **Compost and decaying organic matter:** The most common habitat.
  Self-heating during microbial decomposition creates temperature
  gradients favoring thermophiles. Common genera include
  *Thermomyces*, *Malbranchea*, *Myriococcum*,
  *Scytalidium thermophilum*, and *Thermoascus* species.
- **Desert soils:** Several thermophilic species are found in hot
  desert soils, including *Chaetomium thermophilum* varieties,
  *Rhizomucor microsporus*, *Thermoascus aegyptiacus*, and
  *Thermophymatospora fibuligera*.
- **Soil:** General soil habitats harbor *Corynascus thermophilus*,
  *Talaromyces byssochlamydoides*, *T. emersonii*, *T. thermophilus*,
  and *Thielavia* species.
- **Hot springs and coal refuse piles:** Thermophilic fungi such as
  *Ochroconis gallopava* have been isolated from geothermally heated
  environments.
- **Stored grain [[query-how-do-i-start-black-soldier-fly-farming-for-composting-and-animal-feed]]:** Heating during storage creates
  conditions suitable for thermophiles.

## Physiological Adaptations

Thermophilic fungi possess specialized physiological adaptations that
enable growth at elevated temperatures:

### Heat-Stable Enzymes

Thermophilic fungi produce enzymes with enhanced thermal stability
compared to mesophilic organisms. Key adaptations include:

- Increased number of hydrogen bonds and salt bridges in protein
  structures
