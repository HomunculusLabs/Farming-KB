# Mollison The Permaculture Alternative

**Source:** Bill Mollison, Permaculture Design Course, Pamphlet XIV (1981)
**Last Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Pamphlet XIV presents Mollison's vision for building a viable alternative to the mainstream economic system. Rather than employing people, the permaculture community offers "enablements" -- ways for people to earn their way through cooperative enterprises that require little or no capital.

## The Problem with the Mainstream

Mollison grew up in a village with no rich or poor -- no fear of starving, no hope of becoming rich. Money accumulation created a detached class. Centralization of resources, energy (acid rain), and transport (freeways) are the root problems.

**The alternative movement failed** because it didn't offer a real alternative. People said "I wish I could do what you're doing -- can you employ me?" The answer: "No, I can't employ you, but I can give you a job in which you can earn your way, and leave your present job right now."

## Enterprise Models (Zero/Low Capital)

### Travel Agency
- Anyone can begin; book flights for consultants and community members
- 15% agency fee on all bookings
- Example: 20 people flying to China at $2,000 each = $6,000 in fees
- Not a full-time job -- keeps one person digging gardens most of the time

### Real Estate
- Gives measure of control over land without necessarily owning it
- Qualified agents can manage properties: leasing, oversight, pruning schedules
- Management fees: $1,000-$2,000/year per property, no risk
- "Most of their time digging the garden, occasionally driving down the road to check nobody's bulldozing the trees"

### Publishing
- Issue cards soliciting pre-orders; spend pre-order money on printing
- Pre-orders = retail price; additional copies are pure inventory
- Zero capital required

### Manufacturing (Yeoman's Plow Example)
- Components contracted to 3 small manufacturers (shoe, shank, frame)
- Unemployed groups can make frames, 10 at a time
- Final assembly by one manufacturer; print brochure; appoint distributors
- Designers become distributors carrying only small brochures

### Seed Company (Self-Reliance Model)
- Open-pollinated seeds only; varieties people can save
- Growers collect seed using stamped envelopes, receive near-retail price
- Catalog organized by function: Bee Forages, Chicken Forages, Cattle Forages, Deer Forages, Fireproof Plants, by zone
- Thompson & Morgan (world's largest) runs from a small house
- Free seed exchange section in catalog

## Self-Reliance Philosophy

- "We give fishhooks, not fish" -- enablement over charity
- "We give orange trees to aborigines; we won't give them money to buy orange trees"
- Fund colleges to teach skills, don't give money
- Everything can be started without capital or with very little
- "There is more money than we can handle right in these systems, if we manage them ourselves"

## International Seed Exchange

- Institute gets free seed from anywhere in the world (4,600 institutes, 680 botanical gardens)
- Cannot sell donated seed; must distribute through exchange
- Seed cataloger is the only one who profits (printing charge)
- Goal: reduce selling aspect, expand exchange path until every wanted plant is grown locally
- Catalog lists all plants wanted for permaculture, organized by function and zone

## Community Economics

- Permaculture associations in most Australian cities
- City farms established through Institute land gifts
- Members serve as consultants AND participate in structuring local social milieu
- "Some are working hard in the center of town, and they won't come out"
- Distribute Permaculture Two through own people
- "Our business is to teach people self-sufficiency. We do not handle anything that doesn't promote self-reliance."
