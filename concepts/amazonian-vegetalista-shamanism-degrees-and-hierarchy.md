# Amazonian Vegetalista Shamanism Degrees and Hierarchy

The Amazonian vegetalista tradition recognizes three highest degrees of mastery in plant medicine, each representing progressively deeper levels of knowledge, spiritual power, and connection to the plant world. Becoming a true vegetalista requires years of disciplined dieting and fasting, after which one gains knowledge of the science of the muraya, the sumi, and the banco.

## The Three Highest Degrees

### Banco

The banco is the highest degree in the traditional Amazonian vegetalista medicine. A banco possesses sublime powers and commands the forces of the land, the water, and the sky. Bancos are depicted with formidable weapons -- a flying wheel and a diamond sword -- and operate on a cosmic scale. They have dominion over all three realms (terrestrial, aquatic, celestial) and can summon spirits from any domain.

The banco sits behind the muraya in the hierarchy of the three powers, indicating supreme authority. Bancos are said to travel through space and time, commanding extraterrestrial beings and navigating between worlds.

### Muraya

The muraya is the second-highest degree. Murayas dominate through the use of perfumes and possess great spiritual power. They are particularly associated with water realms -- murayas use the Yakumama (water spirit) to descend to the deepest parts of rivers, where she transforms into a submarine to carry them to the golden cities beneath the waters.

Murayas receive guidance from mermaids and aquatic spirits. They can navigate the subaquatic world, entering cities guarded by Yakumamas with magnetic power in their tongues. Murayas command spirits of snakes, purahuas, and other water beings.

### Sumiruna

The sumiruna is the third-highest degree in the vegetalista hierarchy. Sumirunas possess extraordinary powers of perception and healing. They are associated with the control of powerful animal spirits and serve as masters of esoteric knowledge. The sumiruna can command the yana-cocodrilo (black crocodile) and other formidable beings.

## The Vegetalista Path

Becoming a vegetalista is not simply learning about plants. It requires years of dieta (dietary restriction and fasting), during which the apprentice ingests specific plant-teachers under strict conditions:

- No contact with ill-tempered or bad-living people
- No salty, sweet, or bitter food
- No alcohol
- Sexual abstinence (typically three days minimum before working with ayahuasca)
- Solitary preparation of medicines
- Spiritual contact with the mothers (spirits) of the plants

The mothers or spirits of the vegetation are the conservers and caretakers of the plants. They are the ones who give visions to those who comply with all the requirements of the ciencia vegetalista (plant alchemy). Without proper discipline, the spirits do not come to help.

## The Role of Icaros

Icaros (power songs) are central to the vegetalista's practice. Each plant, spirit, and animal has its own icaro. The vegetalista learns these songs through the visions granted by the plant-teachers. Icaros serve multiple functions:

- Calling spirits and directing their actions
- Protecting against sorcery and enemy attacks
- Curing illness by harmonizing the patient's energy
- Strengthening the ayahuasca brew
- Controlling animals (snakes gather when the snake icaro is sung)
- Setting arkana (defensive barriers) around practitioners

## Defensive Systems: Arkana

Arkana are spiritual defenses that protect the vegetalista from enemy sorcerers. Every shaman develops arkana through their plant diet. The nature of a shaman's arkana reflects their level and specialty -- healers have white or golden serpent arkanas, while sorcerers have red or black boas.

## Healers vs Sorcerers

[[mckenna-food-gods-amazonian-shamanism-ayahuasca]] recognizes both healers (curanderos) and sorcerers (brujos). The distinction is important:

- **Curanderos** work to heal and save lives, using their plant knowledge for medicine
- **Sorcerers** use virotes (magical darts), marupas (animal familiars), and huani (crystal arrows) to cause harm

Some practitioners, like the Conibo shaman in ochre-colored cushma, possess both healing and destructive capabilities. The Shetebo sorcerer, dressed in black, works exclusively with dark forces.

## The Mother Spirits of Plants

Every plant has a mother spirit -- a conscious being that is the essence of the plant. These spirits appear in visions in human or animal form. They bestow knowledge upon those who have properly dieted, but bring death upon the unprepared. The remocaspi tree spirit appears as a wise old Chinese king; the puka-lupuna spirit is a dark-complexioned woman with cat eyes.

The diet (dieta) is the central practice for establishing relationships with these mother spirits. A vegetalista retreats to isolation, consuming only the plant teacher and simple foods, abstaining from salt, sugar, alcohol, and sexual activity. This purification process can last weeks to months.

Through repeated dietas, the shaman accumulates an arsenal of plant allies, each providing specific healing capacities and protective powers. The icaros received during these dietas are the vocal means of invoking each plant spirit's specific energy during healing ceremonies. Different degrees of shaman (banco, vegetalista, muraya) are distinguished by the number and power of their plant allies.
