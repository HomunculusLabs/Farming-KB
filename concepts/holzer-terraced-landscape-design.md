## Holzer Terraced Landscape Design

Terraces are a central feature of [[small-scale-intensive-food-systems-mollison]].
- **Water retention**: Terraces store and hold moisture so that plants have access to rainwater and meltwater for longer.
- **Access**: They are more pleasant to work and far easier to access than steep slopes, and can even accommodate medium-sized machinery.
- [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]] and bushes establish deep roots into the embankment, contributing greatly to its stability.

### Working with Water

In low-rainfall areas, Holzer angles terraces slightly into the hill to retain water. When securing against heavy rainfall, alignment must not channel water. On heavy loam soils, surface water must be drained over a large area by creating terraces with a slight downward angle. Ditches between terraces raise moisture levels in surrounding soil by storing surface water and runoff.

### Gradual Stabilisation

Large-scale projects are carried out gradually over a long period. In the first year, terraces are made at the top, middle, and bottom of the slope and planted. In the second year, more terraces are created between the original ones once completely stable. On steep slopes, work begins at the lowest point and moves upward. New terraces are sowed and planted immediately, as erosion danger is greatest right after construction.
