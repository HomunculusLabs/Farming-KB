## Overview

Porphyrins are a class of heterocyclic macrocycles derived from four pyrrole-like subunits
interconnected via three methine bridges at their alpha-positions. The parent compound, porphine
(C20H14N4), is highly planar and fully conjugated, comprising an 18 pi-electron aromatic system
following Hückel's 4n+2 rule (n=4). The 22 pi-electrons of four pyrrole rings plus four methine
bridges, minus 4 electrons used for C-N bonds, yield the 18 aromatic electrons. Porphyrins are
among the most ubiquitous and functionally diverse molecules in biology, serving as the core of
heme, chlorophyll, and vitamin B12, and have extensive applications in medicine and catalysis.

## Core Structure and IUPAC Numbering

IUPAC numbering assigns positions 1-20 around the macrocycle, with nitrogen atoms at positions
21-24. The methine bridges (meso positions) are numbered 5, 10, 15, and 20. The eight beta-
positions on the pyrrole rings are 2, 3, 7, 8, 12, 13, 17, and 18. The inner nitrogen cavity has
a diameter of approximately 2.0 Å and readily coordinates metal ions including Fe2+, Mg2+, Co2+/3+,
Zn2+, and Ni2+. The four nitrogen atoms form a nearly square-planar coordination environment for
the central metal, with axial ligands possible above and below the macrocycle plane. The extensive
conjugation gives porphyrins intense absorption in the visible region and characteristic
fluorescence, making UV-vis spectroscopy the primary identification tool.

## Biosynthesis Pathway

Tetrapyrrole biosynthesis is one of the most ancient and conserved metabolic pathways. In animals,
delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) is formed from glycine and succinyl-CoA by ALA synthase (ALAS),
the rate-limiting step requiring PLP cofactor, occurring in mitochondria. In plants, algae, and
bacteria, ALA is synthesized from glutamate via the C5 pathway. Two ALA molecules condense via
ALA dehydratase (ALAD) to form porphobilinogen (PBG), a monopyrrole. Lead poisoning inhibits
ALAD. Four PBG molecules are linked head-to-tail by porphobilinogen deaminase (HMBS) to form the
linear tetrapyrrole hydroxymethylbilane. Uroporphyrinogen III synthase (UROS) catalyzes the
critical ring D inversion to form uroporphyrinogen III (without this enzyme, non-enzymatic
cyclization yields the biologically useless type I isomer). Uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase
(UROD) decarboxylates four acetate side chains to methyl groups, yielding coproporphyrinogen III.
Coproporphyrinogen III oxidase (CPOX) oxidatively decarboxylates two propionates to vinyl groups,
forming protoporphyrinogen IX. Protoporphyrinogen oxidase (PPOX) produces the fully conjugated
protoporphyrin IX, the last common intermediate before branching to heme, chlorophyll, or B12.

## Heme Chemistry

Ferrochelatase inserts ferrous iron (Fe2+) into protoporphyrin IX, the terminal step of heme
biosynthesis. The Fe2+/Fe3+ redox couple (E approximately -50 to +50 mV depending on environment)
is central to heme's biological versatility. Hemoglobin, a tetramer (alpha-2-beta-2) with four
heme b groups, reversibly binds oxygen with cooperative sigmoidal kinetics (Hill coefficient
approximately 2.8). Fe2+ shifts from high-spin (deoxygenated) to low-spin (oxygenated),
triggering the T-to-R conformational change. Myoglobin stores oxygen in muscle tissue with
hyperbolic binding. Cytochrome c transfers electrons between Complexes III and IV via covalently
attached heme c (E approximately +250 mV). Cytochrome P450 uses a cysteine thiolate proximal
ligand instead of histidine, enabling monoxygenation reactions through the reactive iron-oxo
Compound I intermediate, central to drug metabolism and steroid synthesis. Catalase dismutates
hydrogen peroxide with turnover approximately 10^7 s^-1, one of the fastest enzymes known.
Peroxidases use H2O2 to oxidize organic substrates through the same Compound I mechanism.

## Chlorophyll

Mg-chelatase inserts Mg2+ into protoporphyrin IX, committing the pathway to chlorophyll.
Subsequent steps include methyl esterification, ring E cyclization to form the chlorin macrocycle
(reduced ring B), and phytyl esterification. Chlorophyll a (C55H72O5N4Mg), the primary
photosynthetic pigment, absorbs at approximately 430 nm (Soret band) and 662 nm (Q_y band).
Chlorophyll b differs by having a formyl group at C7 instead of methyl, absorbing at 453 nm
and 642 nm, expanding the light-harvesting range as an accessory pigment.

Photosystem II contains the P680 special pair that donates electrons to pheophytin, initiating
the electron transport chain extracting electrons from water via the Mn4CaO5 cluster for oxygen
evolution. Photosystem I's P700 special pair reduces ferredoxin for NADPH production. Antenna
complexes (LHCII, CP29, CP43, CP47) funnel excitation energy to reaction centers via Förster
resonance energy transfer. Accessory carotenoids absorb blue-green light, transfer energy to
chlorophyll, and provide photoprotection by quenching triplet chlorophyll and singlet oxygen.

## Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)

Cobalamin features a corrin ring, a contracted partially reduced porphyrin with 15 inner atoms
and six double bonds, coordinated to cobalt. The lower axial ligand is 5,6-dimethylbenzimidazole
linked via a nucleotide tail. Methylcobalamin (MeCbl) with a methyl upper ligand is the cofactor
for methionine synthase, catalyzing homocysteine remethylation using 5-methyltetrahydrofolate.
Adenosylcobalamin (AdoCbl) with a 5'-deoxyadenosyl upper ligand is the cofactor for methylmalonyl-
CoA mutase (propionate metabolism) and other radical rearrangement enzymes. The Co-C bond is
unusually weak (approximately 30 kcal/mol), enabling homolytic cleavage to generate the
5'-deoxyadenosyl radical. Cyanocobalamin (synthetic supplement) and hydroxocobalamin
(pharmaceutical) are converted to MeCbl and AdoCbl in vivo. Deficiency causes megaloblastic
anemia and subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord.

## Other Biologically Important Tetrapyrroles

Siroheme, an iron-containing tetrahydroporphyrin (isobacteriochlorin) with eight carboxylate
groups, is the cofactor for sulfite reductase (SO3^2- to H2S) and nitrite reductase (NO2- to
NH4+). Heme d, found in cytochrome bd quinol oxidase, contains conjugated oxo and formyl groups
and plays a critical role in microaerobic bacterial respiration. Heme o, with a hydroxyethyl-
farnesyl side chain, functions in cytochrome bo3 ubiquinol oxidase. Chlorins (dihydroporphyrins
with one reduced pyrrole ring) and bacteriochlorins (two reduced rings) shift absorption to
longer wavelengths (650-800 nm), enabling anoxygenic photosynthetic bacteria to harvest near-
infrared light in low-light ecological niches.

## Porphyrin Disorders (Porphyrias)

Porphyrias are inherited or acquired enzyme deficiencies causing accumulation of toxic pathway
intermediates. Acute intermittent porphyria (AIP), the most common acute form, results from
porphobilinogen deaminase deficiency, accumulating ALA and PBG with neurovisceral attacks
(abdominal pain, motor neuropathy, psychiatric symptoms) triggered by barbiturates, fasting, or
hormonal changes. Porphyria cutanea tarda (PCT), the most common overall, involves UROD
deficiency with uroporphyrin accumulation causing chronic blistering photosensitivity, often
associated with liver disease, alcohol, and iron overload. Erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP)
from ferrochelatase deficiency causes acute burning photosensitivity from protoporphyrin IX
accumulation in erythrocytes and skin.

## Medical and Industrial Applications

Photodynamic therapy uses porphyrin photosensitizers that accumulate in tumor tissue. Upon
irradiation at 630-690 nm, they generate reactive singlet oxygen destroying cancer cells.
Photofrin (porfimer sodium) was the first FDA-approved PDT agent (1995). ALA-based therapy
exploits tumor upregulation of ALAS for endogenous PpIX accumulation. Manganese(III) porphyrins
serve as tumor-targeted MRI contrast agents. Porphyrins are excellent chemosensors for gases
(NO, CO, O2), metal ions, and pH due to spectral shifts upon analyte binding.

Metalloporphyrins (Fe, Mn, Co, Ru) serve as biomimetic catalysts for olefin epoxidation, CO2
reduction, oxygen reduction in fuel cells, water oxidation, and C-H activation. Phthalocyanines,
synthetic porphyrin analogs with four isoindole units, are used as dyes (Pigment Blue 15), in
organic electronics, and as photocatalysts. Porphyrinic metal-organic frameworks enable
heterogeneous catalysis with tunable active sites.

## Spectroscopic Properties

The Soret band (B band) appears at approximately 380-420 nm with extreme intensity (epsilon
100,000-500,000 M^-1cm^-1) from the S0 to S2 transition. Metal insertion typically blue-shifts
the Soret band by 5-20 nm. Q bands (500-700 nm) arise from S0 to S1 transitions: metal-free
porphyrins show four Q bands, while D4h metalloporphyrins show only two due to increased symmetry.
Fluorescence emission occurs at approximately 615 and 680 nm for metal-free porphyrins (quantum
yield 0.1-0.2). Cu and Fe porphyrins are non-fluorescent due to paramagnetic quenching.
Resonance Raman provides oxidation and spin state information, and EPR characterizes paramagnetic
metalloporphyrins. The UV-vis spectrum is the primary tool for porphyrin identification,
metalation monitoring, and aggregation state determination.
