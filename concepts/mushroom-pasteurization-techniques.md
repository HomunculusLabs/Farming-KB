## Mushroom Substrate Pasteurization Techniques

Pasteurization is a critical step [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] for nutrients and space. In nearly all cases, the competitors overwhelm the [[evaporative-demand-hypothesis-mushroom-growth]]
- Complete spoilage of the substrate bag
- Wasted time, labor, and materials

Pasteurization addresses this by raising the substrate temperature high enough to kill most harmful organisms while preserving beneficial thermotolerant microbes. These surviving beneficial organisms can actually help defend the substrate against later contamination, giving the mushroom mycelium a significant head start in colonizing the bag.

## Country-Style Pasteurization Method

This method uses steam generated inside a large drum to pasteurize filled substrate bags. It requires no electricity and can be assembled from inexpensive, widely available materials.

### Required Equipment

| Item | Specification |
|------|---------------|
| Steaming pot | 200-liter drums |
| Fuel | Firewood |
| Internal screen | Round iron or bamboo screen, 5 inches in height, sized to fit inside the drum |
| Insulation cover | Double layers of old rice bags |
| Outer wrap | Plastic sheeting to seal over the rice bags |

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Clean the steam pot and add water.** Thoroughly clean the inside of the 200-liter drum, then add approximately 4 inches of water to the bottom.

2. **Position the screen.** Place the iron or bamboo screen inside the drum so that it sits about 1 inch above the water level. This keeps substrate bags elevated out of the water while allowing steam to circulate freely around and through them.

3. **Load the substrate bags.** Stack filled substrate bags into the drum on top of the screen until the drum is full. A standard 200-liter drum can hold approximately 80–100 bags per batch.

4. **Cover with rice bags.** Once all bags are loaded, cover the entire opening with double layers of old rice bags. These create the first layer of thermal insulation.

5. **Seal with plastic.** Place a plastic sheet over the rice bags and tie or clamp it tightly closed. This outer layer traps heat and steam inside the drum, ensuring even temperature distribution throughout the pasteurization period.

6. **Light the fire and begin timing.** Light the firewood beneath the drum. Heat the system until the internal temperature reaches 90–100°C — you will know this moment has arrived when steam begins escaping continuously from under the cover. **Start the 3–4 hour timer from this point.**

7. **Maintain constant temperature.** **CRITICAL STEP.** Keep the fire going steadily so that the temperature remains between 90–100°C for the full 3–4 hour pasteurization period. The drum must never run dry — always ensure there is water at the bottom. If the water evaporates completely, the bags will scorch and be ruined.

8. **Remove firewood.** Once the pasteurization time is complete, remove all firewood from the stove to stop heating.

9. **Initial cooling.** Allow the system to cool down for approximately 20 minutes with the covers still in place. This gradual cooling prevents thermal shock to the substrate.

10. **Remove covers and continue cooling.** Take off the plastic and rice bag covers, and let the substrate bags cool further until they reach [[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]]. Without proper insulation, heat escapes and pasteurization will be uneven or incomplete.
- **Cool before inoculation.** Mushroom spawn is a living culture. Introducing spawn into a hot substrate bag will kill it. Always allow bags to cool to ambient temperature before proceeding to inoculation.

## Low-Cost Accessibility

One of the greatest strengths of the country-style method is its accessibility. Every component is inexpensive and widely available, particularly in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]].

## Scaling Considerations

- A single standard 200-liter drum processes **80–100 bags per batch**, which is appropriate for small to medium operations.
- **Multiple drums can be run simultaneously** for larger-scale production, though this requires additional labor for fire management and monitoring.
- The **key variable at any scale is temperature consistency**. Regardless of how many drums are in use, each one must maintain 90–100°C for the full 3–4 hour window once continuous steam flow begins.
- For operations scaling beyond a few drums, a simple temperature log for each batch helps identify problems and maintain [[blesching-cannabis-contaminants-testing-quality-control]] over time.
