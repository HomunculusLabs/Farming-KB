## The Stages of the Psychedelic Experience

D.M. Turner's [[turner-essential-psychedelics-lsd-cosmic-consciousness]] Guide provides a practical phenomenological map of the psychedelic experience, breaking it into recognizable stages. Understanding these stages helps users navigate what can be otherwise overwhelming experiences.

### Stage 1 — Onset

The onset begins with the first perceptible changes, typically 20-60 minutes after oral ingestion (faster for smoked or i.v. routes). Initial signals include:

- Physical: warmth, tingling, nausea, changes in heart rate, pupil dilation
- Perceptual: subtle color intensification, geometric patterning, shifts in depth perception
- Emotional: anticipation, anxiety, excitement, or calm depending on set

This is the most critical decision point: whether to surrender or resist the emerging experience. Turner emphasizes that resistance during onset often leads to difficult experiences, while acceptance allows smoother transition.

### Stage 2 — Buildup / Coming Up

The intensity increases progressively. Waves of sensation may alternate between periods of intensity and temporary relief. Characteristic features:

- Rapidly shifting perceptions and emotions
- Body awareness becomes prominent — sometimes pleasant, sometimes uncomfortable
- Difficulty maintaining ordinary thought patterns
- Visual field begins to transform significantly
- Time perception begins to distort

Turner describes this as the period where the ego begins to lose its grip on ordinary reality. Anxiety peaks here for many users, particularly those approaching higher dose ranges (see [[psychedelic-dose-response]]).

### Stage 3 — Peak

The most intense phase. Duration and character depend heavily on the compound and dose:

- At lower doses: enhanced colors, pattern recognition, mood elevation, philosophical reverie
- At moderate doses: complete visual transformation of the visual field with eyes closed, ego softening, emotional openness, enhanced music appreciation, synesthesia
- At high doses: complete ego dissolution, mystical/peak experiences, encounters with apparently autonomous entities, profound insights or terror, complete loss of ordinary reality framework

Turner distinguishes several peak-quality categories:
- **Aesthetic**: primarily visual and sensory enhancement
- **Emotional**: deep feeling states — joy, love, grief, compassion
- **Intellectual**: complex philosophical or scientific insights
- **Transpersonal**: mystical, religious, or archetypal content; sense of unity; ego death

### Stage 4 — Plateau

After the peak intensity, effects stabilize at a lower but still significant level. This is typically the longest phase for longer-acting compounds (LSD, psilocybin):

- More manageable, less overwhelming than peak
- Opportunity for reflection and integration of peak experiences
- Visual effects continue but are less intense
- Emotional state tends toward calm, gratitude, or gentle contemplation
- Enhanced ability to communicate and interact

### Stage 5 — Comedown / Return

Gradual return to ordinary consciousness:

- Effects diminish in waves rather than linearly
- "Windows" of clarity alternate with brief returns of psychedelic state
- Physical fatigue often becomes apparent
- Emotional afterglow: lingering sense of peace, connection, or wonder
- Some users experience a "crash" or mild depressive phase as the neurotransmitter effects resolve

### Stage 6 — Afterglow / Integration

The period hours to days after the acute experience:

- Residual perceptual sensitivity (colors still appear brighter, music sounds fuller)
- Integration of insights and emotional processing
- Dreams may be unusually vivid for several nights
- Some users report lasting positive changes in mood, perspective, or behavior
- Difficult experiences may require additional time to process

### Cross-Reference with Leary

Turner's stage model parallels [[leary-psychedelic-manual-approach|Leary's Bardo model]] but uses more phenomenological rather than Tibetan Buddhist terminology. Where Turner describes "onset" and "peak," Leary maps these onto the Chikhai and Chonyid Bardos. Both models serve the same purpose: providing a conceptual framework to navigate non-ordinary states.
