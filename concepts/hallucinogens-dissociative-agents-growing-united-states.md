# Hallucinogens and Dissociative Agents Naturally Growing in the United States

## Overview

This review by John H. Halpern (Harvard Medical School, McLean Hospital), published in *Pharmacology & Therapeutics* (2004), catalogues naturally occurring hallucinogenic and dissociative plants and fungi that grow wild or in gardens throughout the United States. It covers their pharmacology, preparation, intoxication effects, health risks, and legal status, including religious exemptions for sacramental use.

## Major Categories

### 1. Mescaline-Containing Cacti

- **Peyote** (*[[lophophora-williamsii]]*): Native to South Texas and Northern Mexico. Used sacramentally by the **[[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] (NAC)** — ~300,000 members. Typical dose: 6–10 dried buttons (~12–20 mg mescaline). Potent intoxication threshold: ~3.75 mg/kg. Peak effects in 2–4 hours, lasting 4–6 hours.
- **San Pedro** (*[[trichocereus-pachanoi]]*): Native to Andes; contains mescaline at 0.3–1.2%. Often prepared as a soup; higher risk of nausea than peyote due to larger volume of plant material.

### 2. Psilocybin/Psilocin-Containing Mushrooms

- **~109 known species** across *Conocybe*, *Panaeolus*, *Psilocybe*, and *Stropharia* genera.
- Cosmopolitan distribution from Arctic to tropics.
- **Misidentification risk** is the primary health concern — toxic species can be mistaken for psychoactive ones.
- Blueing reaction is not definitive — some non-psychoactive species also blue.

### 3. *Amanita* Species

- ***Amanita muscaria*** and ***A. pantherina*** contain **muscimol** and **ibotenic acid**.
- Different pharmacological class from psilocybin — act on GABA receptors, not serotonin.
- Traditional preparation involves boiling/parboiling to convert ibotenic acid to the more psychoactive muscimol and reduce toxicity.
- Effects include sedation, altered perception, and dissociation.

### 4. DMT-Containing Plants

- **N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)** is found in numerous plant species.
- **Ayahuasca**: DMT-containing plants combined with **MAO inhibitors** (β-carboline alkaloids from *Banisteriopsis caapi*) to enable oral activity. Used by Brazilian religions **Santo Daime** and **União do Vegetal (UDV)**, which are seeking legal protection for sacramental use in the US.
- DMT itself is not orally active without MAO inhibition.

### 5. Morning Glory and Hawaiian Baby Woodrose

- **Morning Glory** (*Ipomoea* spp.): Seeds contain **lysergic acid amide** (LSA).
- **Hawaiian Baby Woodrose** (*[[argyreia-nervosa]]*): Seeds contain LSA and related ergoline alkaloids.
- LSA is chemically related to LSD but significantly less potent.

### 6. *Salvia divinorum*

- From the mint family (Lamiaceae).
- Contains **salvinorin-A**, a **non-nitrogenous κ-opioid receptor agonist** — unique among hallucinogens.
- Unlike virtually all other hallucinogens, salvinorin-A has **no activity at 5-HT2A [[psilocybin-pharmacology-and-serotonin-receptors]]**.
- Traditionally used by Mazatec Indians of Oaxaca, Mexico (chewing fresh leaves or drinking leaf juice).
- Modern use: smoking dried leaves or concentrated extracts.
- **Smoked**: Psychoactive for ~15 minutes at 200–500 µg doses.
- **Oral**: Less intense, lasting up to 1 hour.
- DEA has not scheduled it but monitors it as a potentially emerging drug of abuse.

### 7. Anticholinergic Plants (Datura, Brugmansia)

- Contain **atropine** and **scopolamine** — anticholinergic deliriants.
- Effects are markedly different from classical hallucinogens: true hallucinations (user cannot distinguish from reality), amnesia, tachycardia, hyperthermia.
- **Highly toxic** — overdose can be fatal. Narrow therapeutic index.
- Includes *Datura stramonium* (jimsonweed), *Datura inoxia*, *Brugmansia* spp.

## Religious Use and Legal Status

### Native American Church (NAC)

- **~300,000 members** in the US and Canada.
- Protected legal right to sacramental peyote use (exempt from Schedule I).
- All-night ceremonies in tipis, led by a "Road Man."
- Peyote is not forced; freely offered. Even children may receive a blessing dose.
- Use of all drugs and alcohol is proscribed.

### Brazilian Ayahuasca Churches

- **Santo Daime** and **União do Vegetal (UDV)** use ayahuasca as a sacrament.
- Seeking legal protection in the US similar to NAC's peyote exemption.
- In 2006, the UDV won a Supreme Court case (Gonzales v. O Centro Espirita Beneficente Uniao do Vegetal) protecting their religious use.

## Public Health Considerations

- The primary threat is **not** from religious practice but from irresponsible, illicit drug abuse.
- **Drug education** should be expanded to cover these indigenous botanicals.
- Physicians should question younger patients about information obtained online about drugs.
- [[halpern-internet-dissemination-psychoactive-plants-public-health]] has dramatically increased access to information about identification, preparation, and cultivation of these substances.
