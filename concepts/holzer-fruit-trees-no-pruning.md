# Fruit Tree Cultivation Without Pruning

From "Sepp Holzer's Permaculture" by Sepp Holzer.

## No-Pruning Philosophy

I do not prune the trees. The branches retain their resilience, which means that they can support themselves on the ground when they are weighed down by fruit or snow. The trees can stabilise themselves and are less likely to grow at an angle. They can adapt themselves to the terrain. When the branches are weighed down by fruit, it allows sunlight to reach the crown.

If I were to prune and fertilise the trees in the way that experts recommend, then they would put the excess energy into growing water sprouts, which results in a vicious circle. If I prune the fruit trees, they lose their resilience. The branches cannot sink when they are weighed down; instead they stick up rigidly in the air. At altitudes up to 1,500m these branches would not be able to withstand the weight of snow and would break.

Pruning also creates wounds which increase the risk of disease (fungal diseases, fire blight), causes unnecessary stress to the tree and requires a great deal of work.

## High-Altitude Fruit Quality

Fruit grown at high altitude develops a very intense flavour because of the harsh nights, commanding a much higher price than fruit grown at lower altitudes. Cherries ripen on the mountain pasture in September, by which point the harvest is long over at lower altitudes. Plums, pears and apples all develop intense flavours.

## Protection Against Browsing

The side shoots and branches between the graft and the ground fulfil an important function: they provide protection against browsing. The twigs just above the ground are eaten by hares, the ones above by roe deer, and the ones above those by red deer. The animals find food at the right height, so they do not damage the trunk.

To prevent large-scale damage, I introduce a large number of distraction plants which the animals prefer. Jerusalem artichokes, various kinds of clover and buckwheat make good distraction plants and also serve as green manure crops. Extra willow trees work well to distract deer from stripping fruit trees.

For particularly high browsing danger, a homemade salve of bone salve, linseed oil, slaked lime, fine quartz sand and fresh cow dung is painted or sprinkled on the trees. The bone salve has an intense and long-lasting odour which repels deer for many years.

## Key Points

1. Sepp Holzer's approach to Holzer Fruit Trees No Pruning demonstrates the power of working with natural landscape features rather than against them.
2. Holzer's Krameterhof farm at 1,500m elevation in Austria proves that extreme climates can support abundant food production.
3. The method relies on creating diverse microclimates through strategic use of water, earthworks, and plant placement.
4. Raised beds and hugelkultur are central techniques that improve drainage, warmth, and soil fertility simultaneously.
5. [[savory-livestock-grazing-planning]] patterns with crop rotation to naturally fertilize and manage vegetation.
- Plant fruit trees and berry bushes on mounds and slopes where water drainage is optimal.
- Use stone walls and large rocks as thermal mass to create warm microclimates for heat-loving crops.
- Establish polyculture strips with diverse root depths, growth habits, and maturity dates.
- Allow natural succession to inform planting schemes rather than imposing rigid crop plans.
