## Other Ancient Testimony

Porphyry was not alone among ancient authors in assigning special status to mushrooms. The Greek ambivalence toward mushrooms is well-documented, with some traditions revering them and others fearing them:

- **Nicander** (2nd century BCE): Referenced mushrooms in his pharmacological works

- **Dioscorides** (1st century CE): Classified mushrooms by their effects, distinguishing edible from dangerous species

- **Galen** (2nd century CE): Discussed mushrooms as food and medicine, noting their unusual nutritional properties

- **Athenaeus** (3rd century CE): Compiled [[entheogenic-eleusis-theory-scholarly-reception]] draws heavily on this mycophilic tradition, arguing that the Greek reverence for mushrooms was not merely metaphorical but reflected a genuine understanding of their psychoactive properties.
