## Discovery

The phenomenon was discovered when Nicholas Money inserted thin wire thermocouples between gills
and spines, or up inside the tubes of boletes and other fruiting bodies. Students measured
temperatures from the hymenial surfaces of eighteen types of mushroom in beech-maple woodland in
Ohio. Contrary to earlier reports, the mushrooms were consistently colder than the surrounding air.

## Key Findings

- **Cooling magnitude:** Mushrooms chill as much as 5 degrees C below ambient air on warm days
- **Continued cooling:** The effect persists even on cold November mornings
- **Evaporative mechanism:** Cooling is stimulated by air movement around the fruiting body,
  confirming an evaporative origin
- **Thermal mosaic:** The forest floor is a "thermal mosaic" with mushrooms as its coldest
  inhabitants

## Mechanism

Mushroom cooling is an evaporative phenomenon, analogous to the chill experienced after swimming
or the cooling of sweat on skin:

1. Water evaporates from all mushroom tissues into the air surrounding the fruiting body
2. Evaporation removes latent heat, cooling the remaining tissue
3. The gills provide enormous surface area for evaporation
4. The humid microclimate between gills is maintained by the cap's umbrella shape

## Earlier Misconception: Mushroom Heating

A German mycologist, Richard Falck, had reported around 1900 that some mushrooms could be up to
9 degrees C warmer than surrounding air. He proposed this warming created convection currents
assisting spore dispersal. Buller contested these findings.

Money showed that Falck's warming observations were almost certainly artifacts of decomposition
in plucked mushrooms brought into the laboratory. Fresh mushrooms in situ are consistently cold.

## Importance for Spore Discharge

The cooling effect is essential for the [[gasteromycete-fungi-overview]] (stinkhorns,
puffballs, earth-stars) do not cool. Their temperature matches ambient air. This is consistent
with their independence from the water-dependent catapult mechanism. For gasteromycetes,
water conservation makes more sense than evaporative cooling, and many colonize drier habitats.
