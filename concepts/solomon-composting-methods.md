### Medium-Quality Compost: The Once-a-Year Heap

Solomon's recommended approach for most home gardeners:

- Build one large heap per year, ideally in early autumn after summer cleanup
- Minimum size: 4 feet (1.25 m) across, 3 feet (90 cm) high
- Layer green materials with manure; avoid excessive woody material
- Keep moist but not saturated
- Turn 2-3 times over several months
- Final product: useful for medium-demand vegetables, supplement with COF for high-demand

### High-Quality Compost

N content exceeding 3%, C/N below 12:1, N+P+K exceeding 6%. Requires careful ingredient selection: large proportions of manure, seedmeal, or other nitrogen-rich materials. Possible but demanding to produce at home.

## Compost Bins: What Works and What Doesn't

**Plastic compost bins** (common suburban units): convenient and rat-proof, but the product is usually low-grade. Material does not heat uniformly, turning is awkward, and too much woody material typically goes in. Use the output for flowers; supplement vegetable beds with COF.

**Tumblers**: marketed for speed, but research shows that the more frequently a compost heap is turned beyond the minimum necessary, the lower its value as fertilizer. Tumblers replicate municipal composting -- fast recycling but low nutrient retention.

**Shredder-grinders**: expensive, noisy, and counterproductive. Finely ground material decomposes too fast and too hot, driving off nitrogen. Let microbes work at their own pace in an ordinary heap.
