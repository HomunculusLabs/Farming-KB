## Organic Propagation Methods

Plant propagation is the process of creating new plants from existing ones. Understanding propagation methods allows gardeners to multiply plants for free, preserve [[fukuoka-natural-seedlings-versus-grafted-nursery-stock]].

## Seed Sowing

### Direct Sowing Outdoors
- Best for: [[query-how-to-prevent-transplant-shock-when-moving-cannabis-plants]], less equipment needed
- Timing: After last frost for tender crops; as soon as soil is workable for hardy crops

### Indoor Sowing (Module/Tray Method)
- Best for: tomatoes, peppers, aubergines, brassicas, leeks, onions
- Advantages: Earlier start, [[cmh-grow-lights]]
- Sowing depth: Generally 2x seed diameter; very fine seeds on surface (light needed)
- Pricking out: When seedlings have 2 true leaves, transplant to individual modules

### Seed Compost
- Use fine, low-nutrient compost (seedlings don't need fertilizer until true leaves appear)
- Commercial seed compost or make your own: 2 parts sieved garden compost + 1 part leaf mold + 1 part sand/vermiculite
- Sterilize homemade compost (bake at 180°F/82°C for 30 min) to kill weed seeds and pathogens

## Cuttings (Vegetative Propagation)

### Softwood Cuttings (Late Spring-Summer)
- From current season's soft, flexible growth
- Best for: lavender, rosemary, sage, thyme, pelargoniums, fuchsias, hydrangeas
- Method: Cut 3-4 inch stem below a node, remove lower leaves, dip in rooting hormone, insert in free-draining compost
- Environment: High humidity (propagator or plastic bag), bright indirect light, 65-75°F (18-24°C)
- Rooting time: 2-6 weeks

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Late Summer-Autumn)
- From current season's growth that has started to firm up
- Best for: camellia, rhododendron, box, rosemary, bay, hebe
- Method: Similar to softwood but slightly woody base
- Rooting time: 4-12 weeks; may overwinter before rooting

### Hardwood Cuttings (Late Autumn-Winter)
- From previous season's mature, woody growth
- Best for: fig, grape, currant, gooseberry, willow, poplar, rose, forsythia
- Method: 6-12 inch cuttings, pencil thickness. Bottom cut straight (below bud), top cut angled (above bud). Insert 2/3 in soil or directly outdoors
- Rooting time: By spring or the following autumn

### Root Cuttings
- Best for: horseradish, comfrey, mint, Japanese anemone, Oriental poppy, echinacea
- Method: Dig up roots in dormancy. Cut into 1-3 inch sections. Plant horizontally 1 inch deep
- Rooting time: Spring emergence

## Division

### When to Divide
- Perennials: Every 3-5 years to maintain vigor
- Signs: Center dying out, reduced flowering, overcrowded clumps
- Best time: Spring (for summer-flowering) or autumn (for spring-flowering)

### Method
- Dig up entire clump
- Divide into sections with 3-5 growth points each
- Replant at original depth
- Water well
- Best for: hostas, daylilies, irises, grasses, asters, rudbeckia, sedum, echinacea

## Layering

### Simple Layering
- Bend a low branch to the ground, peg it down, cover with soil
- New roots form at the buried node
- Sever from parent when rooted (1-2 years)
- Best for: rhododendron, hazelnut, blackberry, raspberry, honeysuckle, jasmine

### Air Layering
- Make an upward cut in a stem, apply rooting hormone, wrap with moist sphagnum moss and plastic
- New roots form inside the wrap
- Best for: difficult-to-root [[old-fruit-variety-selection]])
- Essential for: most [[comparison-grafting-vs-budding-fruit-trees]], roses, grapes
