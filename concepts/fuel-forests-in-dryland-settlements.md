# Fuel Forests in Dryland Settlements

Fuel forests represent one of the most critical design elements for sustainable dryland settlements. They provide cooking fuel, power supply, essential oils, mulch, honey, and other tree products. Without planned fuel forests, settlements inevitably strip surrounding vegetation for firewood, accelerating [[eucalyptus-camaldulensis]]*), *[[swale]] systems:

- A series of **close-spaced swales** from 4–8 m wide
- Banks between swales only **3–5 m wide**
- Swales supplied by **extensive interceptor banks** to harvest broadscale runoff
- This passive water harvesting can support productive fuel forests without any sewage input

## Comprehensive Settlement Forestry

The complete forestry system for a dryland settlement includes multiple zones:

### Zone 1: Fuel Forests
Sewage or swale-fed coppice forests on 6-year rotations, surrounded by belts of perpetual forests, with gravity flow of sewage or swale-diversion water to the site.

### Zone 2: Windbreak Forests
400 m deep shelter forests protecting settlement from dust and desiccating winds. These are the broad tree parks (300–400 m deep) around or even within the settlement that eliminate devastating dry desert winds and provide fuels, mulch, medicines, and supplement domestic animal forage.

### Zone 3: Broad Buffer Zones
Very large buffer zones of ungrazed or lightly grazed desert can be developed around villages. Pitted or swaled, these areas completely defend against dust, ensuring both public and environmental health is preserved.

### Zone 4: Rangeland
An outer zone reserved for 6–9 year rotation of domestic rangeland species supplying meat, wool, and other products. This area is fenced out of the main settlement.

## The First Line of Defence

The planting of broad tree parks is described as the first line of defence against the desert. Within their shelter, gardens and crops can thrive without wind damage or excessive water loss. These forests must be established before or concurrently with settlement — retrofitting is far more expensive and difficult.

The fuel forest system transforms what would be a waste product (sewage) into the foundation of settlement energy independence while simultaneously growing the windbreak and microclimate infrastructure that makes all other settlement agriculture possible.
