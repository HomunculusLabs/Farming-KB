## Overview

The psychoactive effects of [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] fall within the indolealkylamine
(tryptamine) group, alongside LSD, DMT, and 5-MeO-DMT. The other two
major groups are phenylethylamines (e.g., mescaline) and cannabinoids.

**Psilocybin** (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, 4-PO-DMT) is
the phosphate ester of psilocin. It is more stable in air and water-soluble,
making it the primary storage form in fungal tissue. **Psilocin**
(4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, 4-HO-DMT) is the pharmacologically
active compound that is produced when psilocybin is converted in the body
through dephosphorylation.

Both compounds are structurally similar to the neurotransmitter serotonin
(5-HT), which explains their ability to interact with [[herbarium-specimens-fungal-collections]] with increasing age of
collection.

## Mechanism of Action: 5-HT2A Receptor

Psilocin acts on the serotonin system as a 5-HT2A post-synaptic agonist
or partial agonist. The 5-HT2A receptor is a G-protein coupled receptor
widely distributed in the cerebral cortex, particularly in layer V
pyramidal neurons. Activation of these receptors modulates glutamate
release and alters thalamocortical signaling, producing the characteristic
effects on perception, mood, and cognition.

The agonist activity at 5-HT2A receptors is the primary mechanism
responsible for the hallucinogenic effects, though psilocin also has
affinity for other serotonin receptor subtypes including 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C,
and 5-HT2B, which may contribute to the overall experiential profile.
The partial agonist nature of psilocin means that it does not fully
activate the receptor, which may explain the generally manageable
intensity of mushroom experiences compared to full agonists.
