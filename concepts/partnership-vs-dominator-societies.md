## Eisler's Framework

Eisler advanced the notion that "partnership" models of society preceded
and later competed with, and were oppressed by, "dominator" forms of
social organization. This framework challenges the conventional view of
human social evolution as a linear progression from primitive to
advanced.

**Partnership societies** are characterized by:
- Egalitarian  without rigid hierarchies
- Gender equality and balance between masculine and feminine principles
- Reverence for nature and ecological sustainability
- Emphasis on community, cooperation, and mutual aid
- Openness to  nna-food-gods-designer-states-of-consciousness multiple-forms-of-consciousness and visionary experience
- Goddess worship and earth-centered spirituality
- Absence of warfare as a social organizing principle

**Dominator societies** are characterized by:
- Hierarchical, paternalistic power structures
- Male dominance and suppression of the feminine
- Nature viewed as a resource to be exploited
- Emphasis on competition, conquest, and control
- Fear and suppression of [[mckenna-food-gods-designer-states-of-consciousness]] but more generally
explains the suppression of the feminine, the foreign and exotic, and
transcendental experiences.

control structures. Encounters with psychedelic plants throw into
