## Vapor Pressure Deficit and Transpiration in Cannabis

Vapor pressure deficit (VPD) is the difference between the amount of
moisture in the air and the amount the air could hold when fully
saturated. It is one of the most important [[plant-nutrient-uptake-mechanisms]], and overall plant
health in [[query-what-causes-cannabis-leaves-to-curl-and-how-to-fix-it]] transpire through tiny breathing pores called
stomata located on the underside of leaves. These stomata open and
close to regulate moisture flow and prevent dehydration. During
photosynthesis, the stomata funnel carbon dioxide into contact with
water drawn up from the roots. The stomata also permit the outflow of
water vapor and waste oxygen. Cervantes compares dirty, clogged
stomata to breathing with a sack over your head -- growth suffers
dramatically when stomata are obstructed by dust, pesticide residue,
or foliar sprays.

Transpiration rate depends on several factors:

- Air temperature: higher temperatures increase the rate at which
  water evaporates from leaf surfaces
- Relative humidity: low humidity accelerates transpiration; high
  humidity slows it
- Air movement: gentle airflow across leaf surfaces speeds moisture
  loss
- Root zone moisture: adequate water availability must match
  transpirational demand

## Humidity and Growth Stages

Cervantes outlines specific humidity ranges for different stages of
growth. During cloning, humidity levels of 95-100 percent are ideal
for the first two days, gradually reduced to 80-85 percent during the
following week. A humidity tent helps maintain these levels. Cut
leaves in half to lower the transpiration surface area on cuttings,
which helps traumatized clones retain moisture that would otherwise be
unavailable from nonexistent roots.
