## Nitrogen-Fixing Trees

Nitrogen-fixing trees (NFTs) form symbiotic relationships with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, primarily Rhizobium species in legumes and Frankia species in actinorhizal plants, enabling them to convert atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available forms.
Bill Mollison considered nitrogen-fixing trees and shrubs essential components of every [[ingham-compost-tea-application-rates-and-timing]] of synthetic nitrogen fertilizers.

## Major Groups of Nitrogen-Fixing Trees

### Leguminous Trees (Fabaceae)

The largest group of nitrogen-fixing trees, the legume family includes hundreds of species used in agroforestry and permaculture worldwide.
These form nodules with Rhizobium bacteria and typically fix significant quantities of nitrogen.
Key genera and species include:

- **Acacia**: Fast-growing, drought-tolerant. Used for windbreaks, fodder, and soil improvement. Species include A. nilotica, A. senegal, A. mearnsii.
- **Albizia**: Large canopy trees, excellent for shade and nitrogen. A. lebbeck is widely used in tropical agroforestry.
- **Leucaena**: L. leucocephala is one of the most productive NFTs, providing fodder, fuelwood, and green manure. Fast-growing but can become invasive.
- **Gliricidia**: G. sepium is a versatile tropical NFT used for living fences, fodder, shade for crops, and green manure.
- **Robinia**: Black locust (R. pseudoacacia) is the primary temperate NFT. Hardy, fast-growing, produces excellent rot-resistant timber and abundant nitrogen.
- **Caragana**: Siberian peashrub (C. arborescens) is extremely cold-hardy, making it valuable in cold climate permaculture.
- **Prosopis**: Mesquite species are drought-adapted NFTs for arid regions, producing pods for animal fodder and human food.

### Actinorhizal Trees

A smaller but ecologically important group that forms nodules with Frankia bacteria rather than Rhizobium.
These are especially valuable in temperate and cold climates where leguminous NFTs are less common.

- **Alnus**: Alder species are among the most important actinorhizal trees. They thrive in moist, nitrogen-poor soils and fix 100-300 kg N/ha/year. Used extensively in riparian restoration.
- **Elaeagnus**: Russian olive (E. angustifolia) and autumn olive (E. umbellata) are hardy, drought-tolerant NFTs producing edible fruit and providing wildlife habitat.
- **Casuarina**: She-oaks are tropical and subtropical NFTs adapted to sandy, saline soils. Used widely in coastal stabilization.
- **Hippophae**: [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]].

## Functions in Permaculture Design

### Fertility Building

NFTs serve as the biological foundation of soil fertility in permaculture systems.
