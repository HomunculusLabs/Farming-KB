# Basidiomycete Diversity and Taxonomy

The Basidiomycota is the second largest phylum of Kingdom
Fungi, with approximately 23,000 described species (Hawksworth
et al. 1995). The phylum includes many of the most familiar
macroscopic forest fungi, such as mushrooms and shelf fungi, and
is characterized by the production of meiospores (basidiospores)
on club-shaped cells called basidia. Along with the Ascomycota,
the Basidiomycota comprises more than 95% of all known fungal
taxa, and together the two phyla form a monophyletic sister
grouping within the crown fungi.

## Historical Classification

The morphology of the basidium has played a central role in
past classifications. Fungi possessing septate basidia were
assigned to the Phragmobasidiomycetes (Heterobasidiomycetes),
while those with nonseptate basidia were classified in the
Homobasidiomycetes (Holobasidiomycetes). However, molecular
phylogenetic studies have shown that the Phragmobasidiomycetes
is not monophyletic and that the septate basidium is probably
an ancestral character state for the entire phylum. Today, the
Basidiomycota generally includes three classes: Urediniomycetes
(rusts and relatives), Ustilaginiomycetes (smuts), and
Hymenomycetes (mushrooms and relatives).

## Convergent Evolution

A striking finding from molecular studies is the greater
degree of convergent evolution among macro- and
micromorphological traits than previously appreciated. The
teliospore and overall "smut" morphology are examples of
convergent evolution in fungal form. Traditional
classifications based largely on basidiocarp morphology and
hymenophore type are not supported by molecular data. The
consensus from numerous studies is that basidiocarp
morphology is not a phylogenetically informative trait at
higher taxonomic levels because of repeated episodes of both
convergent and divergent evolution.

## Class Ustilaginiomycetes

Members are characterized by the production of teliospores
(diploid overwintering spores) and dimorphic life histories
including a saprobic yeast state and a pathogenic filamentous
state. Ultrastructural studies and molecular phylogenetic
analyses support three [[basidiomycota-classification-major-clades]], or subclasses:

- **Entorrhizomycetidae**: Including Entorrhizales
- **Ustilaginiomycetidae**: Including Ustilaginales and
  Urocystales
- **Exobasidiomycetidae**: Including Doassansiales,
  Entylomatales, Exobasidiales, Georgefisherales,
  Graphiolales, Malasseziales, Microstromatales, and
  Tilletiales

Numerous taxa once assigned to the Ustilaginales (e.g.,
Microbotryum species) have been demonstrated convincingly
to be derived members of the Urediniomycetes rather than
true smuts.

## Class Urediniomycetes

A large group of dimorphic, yeastlike fungi including the
subclasses Urediniomycetidae (Uredinales and Septobasidiales),
Microbotryomycetidae, and Agaricostilbomycetidae, as well as
the Atractiellales and the enigmatic genus Mixia. The
Uredinales (rusts) contains plant pathogens displaying some
of the most complex [[bloomfield-rust-fungi-life-cycles-barberry-wheat]] among fungi, with heteroecious
species requiring two distantly related hosts and macrocyclic
forms possessing up to five distinct spore-producing states.

Sporidiales including members of no fewer than three clades.
