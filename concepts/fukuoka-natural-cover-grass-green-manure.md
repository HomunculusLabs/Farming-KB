The solution is a form of rice-barley succession cropping in which barley is
seeded together with clover over the standing heads of rice, and rice seed
and green manure are scattered while the barley is up. This more nearly
approaches nature and eliminates weeding. The reason for trying such a method
was not being tired of weeding or wanting to prove that cultivation is
possible without weeding, but rather dedication to understanding the true
form of rice and barley and achieving more vigorous growth and higher yields
by cultivating these grains in as natural a way as possible.

## Results with Multiple Crops

What was found through decades of practice is that, like fruit trees, rice
and barley too can be grown without weeding. Vegetables can be grown in a
state that allows them to go wild, without fertilizer or weeding, and yet
attain yields comparable to normal methods. The clover serves as a living
mulch, fixing nitrogen, suppressing unwanted weeds through competition, and
providing habitat for the predators of insect pests.

## The Philosophy of Coexistence

The deeper principle behind cover grass cultivation is the recognition that
plants in nature do not exist in isolation but form interconnected
communities. The modern agricultural practice of monoculture, which eliminates
all other plant species to grow a single crop, works against this natural
tendency and creates the very problems, such as pest outbreaks and soil
degradation, that it then tries to solve with chemical inputs.
