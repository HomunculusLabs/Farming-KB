## Regional Distribution Patterns

### Mexico: The Global Epicenter

Mexico has the highest diversity of neurotropic fungi of any country, with 76 known species. Of these, 44 belong to the genus Psilocybe, representing 39% of the world's total Psilocybe species. This extraordinary concentration is attributed to Mexico's varied topography, which includes tropical lowland forests, subtropical cloud forests, temperate pine-oak woodlands, and high-altitude grasslands — each providing distinct habitat types for different Psilocybe species.

### Central America

The countries of Central America — Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, and Panama — form a land bridge between the North and South American mycological provinces. The region shares many species with both Mexico to the north and Colombia to the south, while also harboring its own endemic species. The highland forests of Guatemala and Costa Rica are particularly rich in Psilocybe diversity.

### Asia and Oceania

Asia has significant neurotropic fungal diversity, particularly in Southeast Asia (Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam, Indonesia, the Philippines) and the Indian subcontinent. *Psilocybe cubensis* is widespread throughout tropical Asia, occurring in cattle pastures from India to Japan. The highlands of Southeast Asia, including the mountains of Thailand, Vietnam, and the Indonesian archipelago, harbor numerous endemic species.

Oceania — particularly Australia and New Guinea — has a distinctive neurotropic mycota. New Guinea is notable as the only region outside Mexico where indigenous use of neurotropic fungi has been well-documented, with several Aboriginal and Papuan groups known to use Psilocybe, Boletus, and Russula species in traditional ceremonies.
