## Shamanic Healing and Divination Paradigms

Ralph Metzner"s comparative analysis of hallucinogenic drug use across cultures
identified shamanic rituals of healing and divination as one of four major
paradigms for working with psychoactive plants and fungi. While Western
psychotherapy and indigenous shamanism may use similar substances, Metzner found
"profound differences between them in underlying worldview and assumptions about
the nature of reality."

## The Shamanic Ceremonial Form

Traditional shamanic [[shamanic-vs-western-psychedelic-healing]] therapy:
minimal or no talking among participants (except in preparatory or integrative
phases); singing or chanting by the shaman, considered essential to success; and
darkness or low light, which facilitates the emergence of visions. The exception
is the peyote ceremony, conducted around a fire, where participants may see
visions as they stare into the flames.

The rhythmic chanting, like shamanic drumming widespread in Northern Hemisphere
shamanic societies, "seems to give support for moving through the flow of visions,
and minimizes the likelihood of getting stuck in frightening or seductive
experiences."

## Variations Across Indigenous Traditions

Metzner documented several major variations of [[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]]: participants sit in a circle
around a blazing fire in a tipi, with a roadman presiding, assisted by a drummer,
firekeeper, and cedar-man. Almost constant singing of traditional peyote songs
continues throughout the night.

**[[metzner-amazonian-ayahuasca-ceremonies-icaros-and-healing-methods]]:** a small group sits in a circle in
semidarkness while initiated healers sing *icaros*—songs through which healing
and diagnosis takes place. Shamanic "sucking" methods for extracting toxic psychic
residues or sorcerous implants are sometimes employed.

**[[metzner-three-distinctive-features-shamanic-entheogenic-ceremonies]] experiences:

1. **The central role of the guide:** must be a person with extensive personal
   experience, who provides initiatory experiences. The guide does much or all
   of the singing, which profoundly shapes the quality and content of the
   experience.

2. **Multi-level healing:** physical, psychic, and spiritual healing occur,
   with three main variations—extraction of a toxic implant placed by sorcery,
   retrieval of a split-off psychic fragment or "soul," and dismemberment
   followed by reconstitution with a healthier body.

3. **Access to hidden knowledge:** divination, "seeing," prophecy, or
   visioning—equivalent to diagnosis in [[fukuoka-eastern-western-medicine-healing-world-crisis]]. Indigenous healers
   refer to the plants as "plant teachers" that communicate interiorly.

4. **Access to metaphysical realms:** the "inner world," "spirit world," "upper
   or lower world," "dreamtime," or "otherworld." Boundaries between worlds
   become more permeable during the experience.

5. **Perception of spirit beings:** entities associated with particular animals,
   plants, trees, places, ancestors, or other nonordinary beings. Healing is
   experienced as done by or with the assistance of these "allies," "power
   animals," or "guardians."
