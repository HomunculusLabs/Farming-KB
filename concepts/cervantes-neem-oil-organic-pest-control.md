# Neem Oil for Organic Cannabis Pest Control

Neem oil is one of the most versatile and widely used organic pest
control products in cannabis cultivation. Derived from the seeds of the
neem tree (Azadirachta indica), neem oil offers effective control
against a broad spectrum of pests while maintaining a relatively low
toxicity profile for humans and beneficial organisms.

## Active Ingredients

The primary active ingredient in neem oil is azadirachtin, a complex
limonoid that acts as an insect growth regulator, antifeedant, and
reproduction disruptor. Always buy cold-pressed neem oil, which is
stronger and contains all the natural ingredients including
azadirachtin. Do not use heat-processed neem oil, which has reduced
efficacy.

Brand names include Neemguard, Triact, and Einstein Oil. Einstein Oil
has been reported to work the best of all brands tested.

## Pests Controlled

Neem oil is effective against:
- Spider mites (primary use in cannabis cultivation)
- Fungus gnats
- Aphids
- Whiteflies
- Thrips
- Caterpillars and larvae

It is also a fungistat against powdery mildew and rust, making it
useful for both pest and disease management. Neem oil can be used as a
foliar spray or as a soil drench to control soil-dwelling pests
including nematodes and root maggots.

## How Neem Oil Works

Neem oil works through multiple mechanisms:

1. **Antifeedant**: Pests that ingest neem-coated foliage stop eating,
   leading to starvation.

2. **Growth Disruption**: Azadirachtin interferes with insect hormone
   systems, preventing larvae from molting and developing into adults.

3. **Reproduction Interference**: Neem disrupts mating behavior and
   reduces fertility in pest populations.

4. **Smothering**: The oily coating can suffocate soft-bodied pests
   and their eggs on contact.

## Mixing and Application

Mix neem oil just before using in water with a pH below 7. Use a
spreader-sticker to help the solution adhere to foliage. Agitate
constantly while using to keep the solution emulsified. Throw out any
excess mixture, as it does not store well once mixed.

Spray on foliage, especially under leaves, where mites live. Apply
every few days so hatching larvae will eat it immediately. Spray
heavily so mites have little choice but to eat it. For persistent
infestations, alternate neem oil applications with pyrethrum to
prevent pest resistance from developing.

## Persistence and Timing

Contact neem stays on foliage for up to a month or until washed off.
When absorbed via roots (soil drench), neem stays in the plant system
up to a month. Avoid spraying the last few days before harvest. Some
growers report a foul taste when applied just before harvest.

## Toxicity and Safety

Neem oil is not toxic to humans. However, toxicity to beneficial
insects has been reported, so use caution when applying in gardens
where predatory insects are present. Neem oil irritates eyes; wear a
mask and gloves when handling and applying.

## Neem Oil as Soil Drench

Beyond foliar spraying, neem oil can be applied as a soil drench to
control soil-dwelling pests. When absorbed through the roots, neem's
systemic action protects the entire plant from the inside out. This
method is particularly effective against fungus gnats (larvae in the
soil) and root-feeding nematodes. Apply as a diluted soil drench every
one to two weeks for preventive control.

## Resistance Management

To prevent pests from developing resistance to neem oil, avoid
continuous use as the sole pest control method. Rotate neem oil with
other organic controls including pyrethrum, insecticidal soap, and
biological controls. Pests can adapt to any single control method when
exposed to it repeatedly over multiple generations.
