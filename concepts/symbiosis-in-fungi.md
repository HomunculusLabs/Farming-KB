## Mutualism

**Lichens:** Among the most iconic fungal symbioses, lichens are composite
organisms formed by a partnership between a fungus (usually an Ascomycete)
and a photobiont, either a green alga or a cyanobacterium. The fungal partner
provides structure, moisture retention, and mineral acquisition, while the
photobiont supplies fixed carbon through photosynthesis. Lichens are
pioneers of bare rock surfaces, secreting acids that weather stone and begin
the process of soil formation. They are remarkably tolerant of desiccation,
temperature extremes, and UV radiation, colonizing environments from arctic
tundra to desert rocks.

## Endophytes

Endophytic fungi live asymptomatically within plant tissues. Grass
endophytes (Epichloe species and their asexual Neotyphodium relatives)
produce alkaloids that deter herbivores and sometimes confer drought or
disease resistance. These endophytes are transmitted vertically through seeds
and can significantly affect plant community dynamics. In some cases, the
fungal presence is so beneficial that infected plants outcompete uninfected
neighbors, and the endophyte becomes effectively integrated into the plant's
defense strategy.
