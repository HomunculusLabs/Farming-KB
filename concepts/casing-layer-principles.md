# Casing Layer Principles and Techniques

The casing layer is a non-nutritious covering applied to fully colonized substrate that provides the microenvironment necessary for mushroom pinhead initiation and fruitbody development. paul stamets provides comprehensive guidance on casing preparation and management in The Mushroom Cultivator.

## Purpose of Casing

The casing layer serves several critical functions:
- Provides a humid microclimate at the substrate surface
- Supports development of primordia (pinheads)
- Supplies moisture for maturing mushrooms
- Protects the colonized substrate from desiccation
- Provides a physical buffer against contamination

The casing must be non-nutritious — if it provides significant nutrition, competing organisms will colonize it preferentially, and the casing will become a breeding ground for contaminants.

## Standard Casing Materials

### Peat-Based Casing
The most common commercial casing material:
- Sphagnum peat moss mixed with limestone (calcium carbonate) for pH adjustment
- Standard ratio: peat with limestone at 10-20% by weight
- Target pH: 7.0-7.5 for most species
- Excellent water retention
- Must be pasteurized before use

### Vermiculite-Based Casing
- Lightweight, inert, and sterile when properly prepared
- Can be used alone or mixed with peat
- Excellent aeration
- Lower water-holding capacity than peat

### Soil-Based Casing
- Garden loam or topsoil can be used but varies greatly in quality
- Must be free of toxic chemicals and fertilizers
- May harbor contaminant organisms requiring thorough pasteurization

## pH Adjustment

The casing layer pH is critical for both mushroom growth and contamination resistance:
- Most species prefer pH 7.0-7.5 in the casing
- Agricultural limestone (calcium carbonate) is the standard buffer
- Gypsum (calcium sulfate) can be added for structure without significantly affecting pH
- Excessive liming can cause overlay — a dense mycelial mat that prevents mushroom formation
- Lower pH (5.0) water baths are used for [[cotter-mycoremediation-overview]] growers
- Surface drying during cropping can create conditions favorable to contaminants; evaporation must be maintained at an optimal rate

## Watering the Casing

The casing must be kept moist throughout the cropping cycle:
- Light misting before button stage; no substantial waterings that could damage pins
- Once mushrooms reach button size, build moisture back up with daily light-to-moderate waterings
- Water 2-4 times per day rather than once, allowing slow absorption
- Stop watering once caps reach adolescence (wet caps breed Bacterial Blotch)
- After first [[post-flush-watering-regime-mushroom-casing-moisture-management|flush]], keep casing moist with light mistings until next flush reaches button stage
- Never allow the casing to dry out completely — a dry surface is difficult to recondition

## Species-Specific Casing Requirements

Different species have different casing preferences:
- Agaricus Bitorquis: Standard peat/limestone casing
- Agaricus Brunnescens: Standard peat/limestone casing
- [[panaeolus-cyanescens-bali-omelette]]: Typically cased for optimal yields
