## Cannabinoid Mechanisms for Herpes

### CB1-Mediated Analgesia

The herpes virus establishes latency in nerve ganglia, and outbreaks follow nerve pathways. When lesions appear, the associated nerve inflammation produces significant pain. CB1 receptors are abundant in the peripheral nervous system, and their activation:
- Reduces neurotransmitter release from pain-signaling neurons
- Decreases inflammatory signaling at the nerve ending
- Modulates the perception of pain in the central nervous system

Topical cannabinoids can activate CB1 receptors on nerve fibers in the skin and mucous membranes without producing systemic psychoactive effects, making them ideal for localized herpes pain.

### CB2-Mediated Anti-Inflammatory Action

CB2 receptors, primarily found on immune cells, modulate the inflammatory response:
- Reduce the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines
- Decrease immune cell infiltration at the outbreak site
- May help resolve the inflammatory component of herpes lesions more quickly

### Potential Direct Antiviral Activity

The laboratory evidence of cannabinoid antiviral effects against herpes suggests additional mechanisms:
- Possible interference with viral entry into host cells
- Potential disruption of viral replication machinery
- Modulation of the immune response to better control viral reactivation
