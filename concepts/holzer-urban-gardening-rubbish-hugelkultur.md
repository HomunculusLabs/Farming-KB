### Results and Benefits

When Holzer returned to Tamera two months later, tomatoes, pumpkins,
melons, cabbages, radishes, and lettuces were growing in abundance from the
rubble-filled mound. The rotting process itself has a detoxifying function.

Regarding potential contamination from waste materials, Holzer takes a
pragmatic stance: when living on or near a rubbish tip, potential toxins
are already part of the environment. The prospect of obtaining decent food
far outweighs the danger of eating contaminated foods. When faced with
starvation, potential contamination is clearly the lesser evil.

## Edible Tubes and the Bypass Method

This innovative system was developed for growing on rubbish tips using
available materials.

### Construction

1. Take a bale of geotextile and sew tubes approximately 30cm in diameter
2. Insert a long, perforated, thin hose through the centre for watering
   and feeding
3. Fill the tube with soil, kitchen waste, straw, and leaves

### Watering and Feeding System

The inner hose connects to a bucket above, protected by chicken wire to
prevent clogging. The bucket is filled with stinging nettles, orach, and
other leaves. When it rains, the bucket fills with water to make liquid
manure. A valve between bucket and tube controls watering flow.

### Installation Options

Small holes are made in the outer geotextile tube for sowing seeds or
planting young plants (radishes, lettuces, cabbages). The tubes can be:
- Wrapped around telegraph poles
- Hung from windows
- Arranged along house walls

The tubes become living pieces of edible art. When using the bypass method
along a house wall, protect the wall with plastic sheeting to prevent moss
growth from increased moisture.

## The Rubbish Tower

Built during a Tamera workshop from materials found at a junkyard:
- Three old metal poles create a 3-metre tripod
- A hose for watering and feeding hangs inside
- The structure is wrapped in geotextile
- Filled with straw, soil, leaves, and kitchen waste
- Small holes in the geotextile receive seeds of melons, tomatoes,
  cabbages, and other vegetables

Watering can be done from the top via ladder or with a small [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] from the top.
