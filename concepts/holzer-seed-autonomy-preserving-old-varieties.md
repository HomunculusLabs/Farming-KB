# Holzer Seed Autonomy and Preserving Old Varieties

Everyone talks about biodiversity, yet supermarket shelves tell a different
story: monotony. The same products are sold across the globe, and the vast
majority of human beings live off just 20 kinds of foods.

## The Scale of Diversity Loss

The statistics on variety loss are staggering:
- 97% fewer fruit and [[solomon-vegetable-varieties-self-sufficiency]] today compared to 1900
- India: from 30,000 [[fukuoka-insects-create-new-rice-varieties]] down to 12
- Philippines: from several thousand rice varieties down to 2
- China: from 8,000 rice varieties down to 50; from 10,000 wheat varieties
  down to 1,000

Vegetables and cereals are now bred for uniformity, easy packaging,
marketing appeal, and tolerance to pesticides and artificial fertilisers.
Resilience, vitality, taste, and nutrition have become unimportant to the
industrial system.

## Corporate Seed Monopolisation

The worldwide monopolisation and privatisation of [[cervantes-seed-production-storage]] is
responsible for the disappearance of diversity. The age-old right to
produce and sell one's own seeds has been taken from farmers and given to
multinational agricultural companies.

Since the TRIPS agreement (Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property
Rights) in 1995, the World Trade Organisation has granted patent rights on
genetic properties of plants to corporations. This is as if broccoli and
rice did not evolve naturally but were invented in the laboratories of
Monsanto, Pioneer, and Syngenta.

### Consequences for Farmers

- Farmers become dependent on buying new seeds every year
- [[cervantes-hybrid-varieties-types]] only produce for one generation before degenerating
- Increasing amounts of fertiliser and pesticides are required
- Traditional regional varieties are strictly controlled
- Farmers cannot sell uncertified seed or produce grown from it
- Selling [[cervantes-open-pollinated-heirloom-seeds]] has been made illegal in many jurisdictions
  (though giving them away for free remains permitted)

### Human Cost

17,368 farmers in India committed suicide in 2009 alone (reported by the
Indian government). Families cite increasing debt from high seed and
fertiliser prices as the main reason. Monsanto employs detectives and spies
worldwide to ensure farmers comply with their contracts.

## Regionally Adapted Varieties

Regionally adapted varieties are essential for sustainable natural
agriculture. They cope with regional weather and give good crops without
needing artificial fertilisers and pesticides. In traditional agriculture,
each region had unique varieties of potatoes, herbs, and cereals.

### Siberian Grain

Holzer's rye from Siberia exemplifies the value of regionally adapted
varieties. The seed was given to him in 1957 by a former prisoner of war:
- Ideal for bread-making, [[query-how-do-i-start-black-soldier-fly-farming-for-composting-and-animal-feed]], and straw
- Grows anywhere without artificial fertilisers or pesticides
- Shows regional variation: straw reaches 2.5m in Scotland but only
  1.2-1.5m in Colombia or Spain
- Through wind pollination, new genetic material is added in each region,
  increasing resilience

Holzer has sown this grain all over the world and it thrives everywhere.
It is not the same grain it was decades ago; it has continuously evolved
through natural selection in diverse environments.
