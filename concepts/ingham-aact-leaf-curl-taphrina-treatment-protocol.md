## AACT Leaf Curl (Taphrina) Treatment Protocol

## Overview

Peach leaf curl, caused by the fungus *Taphrina deformans*, is one of the most recognizable [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] compost tea (AACT), emphasizing that successful control requires a multi-season, multi-application approach rather than a single spray event. The protocol addresses both foliar protection and soil biology restoration.

## Understanding Taphrina Biology

*Taphrina deformans* overwinters on bark surfaces and in bud scales as a yeast-like form. During cool, wet spring conditions, the fungus infects emerging leaf tissue, causing the characteristic curling, reddening, and thickening of leaves. The pathogen then produces spores on the distorted leaf surfaces, which wash or blow to bark surfaces where they persist until the next infection cycle.

### Key Infection Factors

- **Temperature** — optimal infection occurs at 50-70°F (10-21°C) during bud break
- **Moisture** — extended wetness (rain, dew, fog) during leaf emergence promotes infection
- **Timing** — the window of vulnerability is narrow, concentrated around bud break and early leaf expansion
- **Overwintering site** — spores survive on bark, bud scales, and fallen leaf debris

## The AACT Protocol for Leaf Curl

### Core Principle: Fungal-Dominant Tea

Ingham emphasizes that **with a tea that has the beneficial fungi growing well in it, leaf curl (Taphrina) is a breeze to shut down**. The key is ensuring the tea contains active, growing fungal hyphae — not just fungal spores. This requires:

- Compost with visible white mycelial growth as the tea base
- Fungal food sources in the brew (humic acids, fish hydrolysate)
- Extended brew time (24-36 hours) for hyphal development
- Minimal molasses (excessive sugar favors bacteria over fungi)

### Fall Application Program

The fall application is the most critical component of the protocol. Ingham writes:

> "Get a spray on in the fall, before the leaves drop, so you get healthy organisms going on the leaves and get decomposition going well."

The fall spray serves multiple purposes:

1. **Surface colonization** — beneficial organisms establish on leaf surfaces and bark before winter
2. **Decomposition of infected debris** — organisms break down infected fallen leaves, destroying overwintering Taphrina spores

### Why Single Sprays Fail

- Transitioning from conventional to biological management means the soil and plant surfaces start with depleted biology
- A single application cannot establish a self-sustaining microbial community
- Pathogens already present have a numerical advantage that requires multiple applications to overcome
- Organisms need time to colonize, reproduce, and build biofilm communities
