## Central America and the Caribbean

Mexico City stands at 2,220 meters altitude where Copelandia cyanescens grows in disturbed zones.

Psilocybe cubensis and P. subcubensis grow throughout Central America on cow dung.

The Caribbean region has a high concentration of species alongside Mexico and Mesoamerica.

Colombia has several endemic species including P. columbiana, P. guatapensis, and P. pintonii.

Psilocybe portoricensis is endemic to Puerto Rico.

## South American Diversity

South America has the highest number of neurotropic Psilocybe species as a region, with 59 species.

Brazil has endemic species including P. brasiliensis and P. paulensis.

Psilocybe yungensis and P. subyungensis are common in South America and reach Mexico through mesophytic forests with Alnus.

The cattle in America were introduced by Spanish colonists in the 16th-17th centuries, and P. cubensis does not grow in Europe.

It is probable that this fungus was introduced to America through the slave commerce during Spanish colonial times.
