## [[staycare-lentinula-edodes-in-bioremediation]]
- Difficulty: Indoor 3 / Outdoor 1
- One of the most recognized and marketable mushrooms globally
- Fruiting temperatures: 50-75°F (10-24°C) depending on strain
- Prefers hardwood trees, especially oak, but also maple, beech, birch, and ironwood
- Strain-specific wood preferences: each strain may prefer its own unique wood chemistry

## Mycelium and Spawn Characteristics

- White, cottony mycelium that becomes brown with age
- Exudes a light yellow metabolite on overcolonized substrates
- Slower growing than [[comparison-grain-spawn-vs-grain-to-grain-transfer]] preferred for [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]

### Inoculation with Sawdust Spawn

1. Drill holes slightly larger than for plug spawn (to accommodate loose sawdust)
2. Use an inoculation tool to pack sawdust spawn into each hole
3. Seal with wax as with plug spawn
4. Sawdust spawn colonizes faster than plug spawn due to more inoculation points

### Log Management

- Stack logs in a shaded location (under trees or behind a building)
- Keep logs off the ground (on pallets, rocks, or a log rack) to prevent contamination from soil organisms
- Maintain moisture: water during dry periods, or rely on natural rainfall in humid climates
- Avoid direct sunlight which dries the logs
- Full colonization takes 6-18 months depending on log size, species, and climate
- Logs can produce mushrooms for 3-6 years

### Forcing Fruiting

To stimulate a flush of mushrooms on colonized logs:

1. Soak logs in cold water (ice water works well) for 12-24 hours
2. Remove logs and stack in a fruiting position
3. Mushrooms should appear in 7-14 days
4. Allow logs to rest for 6-8 weeks before forcing another flush
5. Approximately 2-4 flushes per year is sustainable
