## Moist Chamber Technique — Inducing Fungal Fruiting

The moist chamber technique is one of the most versatile and widely used methods in [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]] and spores from collected substrata. It is essential for studying microfungi, myxomycetes, and many other groups that cannot be identified in the field.

## Basic Setup

### Standard Moist Chamber
1. Petri dishes or fingerbowls (100mm diameter x 15-20mm high typical)
2. Sterile filter paper or absorbent paper toweling on bottom
3. Substrata placed on paper in single, nonoverlapping layer
4. Sterile distilled water added (10-15 ml) to wet thoroughly
5. Soak 10-12 hours
6. Remove excess water carefully

### For Corticolous Mycetozoans and Fungi
1. Petri dishes with sterile filter paper
2. Bark placed on paper covering the bottom
3. Water amount varies with bark absorptive capacity
4. After soaking, excess removed; sometimes more added
5. Begin observations after ~24 hours

## Incubation Conditions

### Temperature
- Ambient room temperature adequate for most purposes
- Cool temperatures preferred (not above 18C) to prevent aggressive invaders
- Some species from subtropical/arctic sites may need warmer or cooler conditions
- Varying temperature can stimulate additional species

### Light
- Normal daylight generally adequate
- Slight air movement desirable (near open window or slow fan)
- 12h/12h light/dark cycle for standardization
- UV light may stimulate additional species

### Moisture Cycles
- Wet/dry moisture cycle may encourage species adapted to different moisture levels
- Allowing bark to dry completely, then rewetting, stimulates further fruiting
- Some species must go through an encystment stage before fruiting

## Applications by Fungal Group

### Corticolous and Herbaceous Myxomycetes
- Begin observations after ~24 hours for small species (Echinostelium may appear first day)
- Longer incubation favors aphanoplasmodial and phaneroplasmodial species
- Observe daily for 7-10 days
- If [[fukuoka-textdoc-sawdust-wood-chips-orchard-soil-acidification-experiment]] for increased humidity
- Closed plastic bags also effective

### Spore Prints
- Separate cap from stipe, place lamellae/pore-side down on white paper
- Place in deep container, cover with paper toweling
- Maintain at room temperature
- Reclosable plastic bags also work (e.g., for Cortinarius)

## Potential Problems

### Aggressive Fungal Invaders
- **[[trichoderma]] species** especially problematic with [[coprophilous-fungi]]
- Cool incubation (<=18C) helps prevent overgrowth
- Limited water (not saturated) also helps
- Acidification of media can help
