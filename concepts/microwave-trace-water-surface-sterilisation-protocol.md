# Microwave Sterilisation of Contaminated Surfaces Using Trace Water Enhancement

Microwave irradiation at 2.45 GHz can sterilise surfaces contaminated with
bacteria, yeasts, and molds through a dual mechanism involving direct coupling
with intracellular water in vegetative cells and trace-water-enhanced steam
generation for more resistant organisms including spores. This technology was
developed at NASA's Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center by James E. Atwater
(Technical Director), Neil D. Streech (Project Engineer), and Frank C. Garmon
(Microbiologist) for aseptic access to biologically sensitive closed systems
such as spacecraft environmental control and life support systems (ECLSS).

## Motivation: The Aseptic Access Problem

The technology was developed to address a specific need in spaceflight
operations: reliable access to biologically sensitive systems without
introducing contamination. Traditional sterilisation techniques each
had significant drawbacks for this application:

- **Autoclaving**: Too great a thermal impact on vulnerable systems
  and mating fixtures
- **Gamma irradiation**: Requires specialised facilities and poses
  operational safety concerns
- **Chemical disinfection**: Introduces chemical contaminants
  (ethylene oxide, alcohols, quaternary amines, hydrogen peroxide,
  elemental iodine) that are unacceptable in closed life support systems
- **UV irradiation**: Cannot sterilise complex surface geometries
  or reach enclosed mating surfaces

The proposed Microwave Sterilizable Access Port (MSAP) system was
designed to overcome all these limitations simultaneously. It consists
of three subsystems: an in-line valve port assembly, a portable
microwave sterilization chamber, and a specimen transfer assembly.

## Mechanism of Microwave Surface Sterilisation

Microwave sterilisation exploits the interaction between 2.45 GHz
electromagnetic radiation and dipolar water molecules. The mechanism
operates through two distinct pathways:

### Vegetative Cell Killing (Direct Coupling)

Active vegetative microbial cells contain significant intracellular
water. Microwaves of sufficient intensity and duration penetrate the
cell wall and couple with this intrinsic water, generating localized
heating that kills the organism. This mechanism works effectively on
damp surfaces without additional water introduction. The water naturally
present in and around the cells is sufficient for microwave absorption
and lethal heating. The 2.45 GHz frequency is specifically chosen
because it directly couples with the rotational transitions of dipolar
water molecules.

### Spore Killing via Trace Water Enhancement (Steam Flash)

Bacterial and fungal spores are relatively resistant to dry microwave
irradiation because they contain minimal free water for energy coupling.
Their dehydrated state and protective coat structures shield them from
the direct heating mechanism. The innovation in this system is the
introduction of approximately 9 microlitres per square centimetre of
contaminated surface. This precisely controlled small quantity of water
absorbs microwave energy and rapidly flashes to steam, contacting all
exposed surfaces including crevices and complex geometries where spores
may shelter. The steam generation is highly localised, and because the
water volume is minimal, little total energy is added to the system,
minimising thermal impact on the underlying surfaces and materials.

## System Architecture

The microwave surface sterilisation system consists of several
integrated components:

- **Power supply**: Provides regulated electrical input to the
  microwave generator
- **Magnetron oscillator**: Generates 2.45 GHz microwave radiation,
  the standard frequency chosen for optimal coupling with water molecules
- **Rectangular waveguide**: Conducts electromagnetic energy from the
  magnetron to the target area while maintaining proper wave propagation
- **Coaxial power splitter**: Distributes microwave energy from the
  waveguide to multiple antenna elements for uniform coverage
- **Waveguide to coaxial adapters**: Interface components that
  transition between waveguide and coaxial transmission modes
- **Dipole antennas**: Multiple antenna elements ensure uniform
  coverage of the target surface area, eliminating shadow zones
- **Trace water introduction system**: Delivers controlled, measured
  amounts of water to the contaminated surface prior to irradiation

## Proven Sterilisation Parameters

The protocol validated through experimental testing uses specific
parameters:

- **Frequency**: 2.45 GHz, chosen for optimal rotational coupling
  with water molecules
- **Exposure rate**: 3.6 watts per square centimetre of surface area
- **Total exposure**: 13.1 watt-hours to achieve complete sterilisation
- **Water requirement**: approximately 9 microlitres per square
  centimetre of surface for spore kill enhancement

These parameters have been demonstrated to reduce initial surface
populations from 2 times 10 to the 5th power Colony Forming Units
(CFU) to zero.

## Challenge Organisms Tested

The system was validated against a mixed surface population of three
organisms representing different categories of microbial contamination:

- **Bacillus pumilus**: A spore-forming bacterium known for extreme
  environmental resistance, including tolerance to UV radiation,
  desiccation, and hydrogen peroxide. Represents the hardest-to-kill
  fraction of surface contamination.
- **Escherichia coli**: A Gram-negative vegetative bacterium
  representing common environmental contamination.
- **Pseudomonas cepacia**: An opportunistic pathogen with notable
  environmental persistence and resistance to multiple disinfectants.

Kill kinetics show progressive reduction with increasing exposure,
with vegetative organisms succumbing more rapidly than spore-formers.

## Unique Capability: Enclosed System Sterilisation

A distinctive feature is the demonstrated ability to penetrate
elastomeric materials and sterilise fully enclosed systems. Mating
surfaces inside sealed assemblies, O-rings, and other inaccessible
interfaces can be sterilised without disassembly. This capability is
particularly valuable for spacecraft ECLSS where maintaining aseptic
access is critical.
