# Pure Culture Spawn Method — Duggar's USDA Breakthrough (1902)

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: Benjamin Minge Duggar, *[[duggar-mushroom-growing-history-and-methods]]* (1915), Chapter VIII: Spawn-Making.

## The Problem: Unreliable Commercial Spawn

### The American Spawn Crisis (1901-1904)
When Duggar began his work at the Bureau of Plant Industry, U.S. Department of Agriculture, the [[gartz-gymnopilus-purpuratus-south-american-mushroom-in-europe]] industry faced a critical problem: the majority of commercially available [[spawn-production]]. The key innovation was the **tissue culture technique**:

1. **Select a vigorous, disease-free mushroom specimen** — from fields, woods, or established beds
2. **Remove a small piece of tissue from the interior of the mushroom cap** — using [[cultivator-sterile-technique-and-agar-culture]] to avoid contamination
3. **Place the tissue on a sterile culture medium** — typically nutrient [[mycelium]] to grow from the tissue** — the mushroom tissue itself serves as the inoculum
6. **Subculture to fresh media** as needed to maintain vigorous growth
7. **Use the pure culture to inoculate spawn substrate** — brick or flake compost

### Advantages Over Previous Methods
- Eliminated dependence on unreliable commercial spawn
- Allowed selection of superior strains and varieties
- Enabled year-round spawn production independent of seasonal fruiting
- Made possible the perpetuation of desirable characteristics through selection
- Spawn could be produced from any vigorous specimen, regardless of source

### Costantin and Matruchot's [[agaricus-campestris]] spores do not germinate readily — they germinate "sporadically at most" under normal conditions
- The tissue method is far more easily applicable and reliable
- Many practical growers had tried sowing gill portions in specially constructed beds without satisfactory results
- Duggar had grown "thousands of cultures" by tissue method and stated "there is no hesitation in saying that it is entirely practicable"

## Selection and Strain Improvement

### Perpetuating Desirable Characteristics
The pure culture method opened the possibility of selective mushroom breeding:

"Whether these varieties vary as to color, whether they differ in quality or productivity, or show diversity in other characters, the method permits a perpetuation of the kinds which promise something of interest or of value."

Any individual difference indicating the possibility of a permanent desirable departure could be selected and propagated. Duggar believed this method could be "fully relied upon for further progress in selective mushroom growing."

### The "Running Out" Question
Some growers feared that continuous tissue [[stamets-cultivator-liquid-culture-propagation-methods]] might cause varieties to "run out" (lose vigor). Duggar tested this by:
- Propagating one strain continuously for five years through successive tissue cultures
- Consistently observed high yield and quality
- Concluded that "running out" is likely due to improper care of pure cultures rather than inherent genetic degeneration
- Analogous cases are known in plant pathology, where continuous laboratory culture of pathogenic fungi reduces their virulence

## Spawn Production Details

### Culture Substrate for Spawn
Duggar found that a mixture of:
- **Leaf mould** — provides organic matter and favorable physical structure
- **Fermented manure** — provides nutrients
- Produced faster [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]] than manure alone

The ideal mixture combined the best properties of both materials.

### Brick Spawn Manufacturing

**Materials**: Composted mixture of stable manure, cow manure, and sometimes loam. At least two-thirds should be stable manure free of heavy straw. Good success was obtained with stable manure alone.

**Moulding**:
- Hand moulds of iron or strong oak frame (lined with steel plates)
- Two methods: (1) puddled (thoroughly wet) manure compressed into moulds, or (2) moist but not puddled manure compressed with a mallet
- Brick dimensions: approximately 5.5 x 8.5 x 1.5 inches (allow for shrinkage during drying)
- Final pressing with a brick machine improves quality

**Inoculation**:
Two methods: (1) Dry bricks slowly to slightly moist state, stack with 4-5 bits of spawn between each pair in successive layers; (2) Insert spawn into moist bricks via a cut, nearly close the slit, then continue drying and stack for growth.

**Growth conditions**:
- Temperature about the same as for mushroom growing (55-60°F)
- Keep surface slightly moist by spraying water on bricks or walls
- Avoid extremely moist conditions
- For last growth stages, place bricks on edge with free air circulation
- Shield stacks with clean straw or cheesecloth if needed

**Quality indicators**:
- Good spawn shows throughout the brick as a mold-like growth
- Some coarser thread-like elements are acceptable but an abundance of heavy threads alone indicates poor quality
- Small mushrooms beginning to form on bricks is NOT favorable — this spawn will fruit directly without spreading through beds

**[[psilocybin-mushroom-drying-and-storage-methods]]**:
- Dry gradually but considerably, to the point where no more growth can occur
- Store cool and dry
- A warm situation is more injurious to vitality than cool
- A cool, moist cellar would encourage mushroom production on the bricks (undesirable)

### Spawn Vitality and Shelf Life

Duggar's testing revealed critical limitations on spawn age:

- **One year old**: Unquestionably injured beyond commercial usefulness under ordinary circumstances
- **Two years old**: No mushrooms obtained in experimental beds
- **Recommended maximum**: Not more than 6-8 months old
- **Optimal**: Use as fresh as possible
- **Brick spawn vitality**: Greater than flake spawn under same conditions
- **Temperature effect**: Brick spawn kept at 50-60°F may retain some viability beyond one year, but satisfactory yields were obtained in only one single instance

Experiments to reduce brick weight by using more spongy material gave unsatisfactory results from the vitality standpoint — this explains the early preference for dense brick spawn over flake spawn in America.

### Spawn Pricing and Purchasing
- Price varies considerably depending on quality
- Premium spawn inoculated directly from pure culture commands higher prices
- English brick spawn costs a few cents less per brick than American but has been subjected to longer shipment and storage
- Order several months before needed to secure best quality
- Never purchase in a hurry — avoid being forced to take whatever the market offers
