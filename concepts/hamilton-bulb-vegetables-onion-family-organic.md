# Bulb Vegetables and the Onion Family

The edible bulbs of the onion family are in fact compacted layers of
swollen leaf bases in which the plant stores food. They prefer a rich
soil so are particularly suited to organic cultivation. The bulbs are
among the easiest of all vegetables to grow and most of them store
well, so it is not difficult to maintain a year-round supply.

## Soil Preparation for Bulb Vegetables

All the bulb vegetables prefer sunshine and a soil rich in organic
matter, so dig in plenty of well-rotted compost or manure. If
necessary, add lime to raise the soil pH above 6.5. If you are using
the [[hamilton-deep-bed-system]], plant the cloves 6in (15cm)
apart in staggered rows. Keep the rows weed free and, if the site is
exposed, support the stems to keep them from breaking off in the wind.

### Harvesting and Storage

Dig up the bulbs in summer, clean them, and dry them in the sun before
stringing or storing in nets in a frost-free place. Garlic is generally
trouble free from [[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]].

## Onions - *Allium cepa*

One of the most useful vegetables in the kitchen, onions store well
and can be used all year-round if a combination of main crop and other
varieties are planted. Plant with 9in (23cm) between the rows and 6in
(15cm) between each onion in the row.

## Leeks - *Allium porrum*

A superb [[coleman-french-historical-winter-vegetable-production]] that is hardy in all but the very coldest
climates, leeks are easy to grow, undemanding, and a valuable source of
fresh greens in the winter.

### Varieties

Musselburgh is a popular variety, very hardy with thick stems. Titan
has a very long stem and a good blanched base. The Lyon Prizetaker has
an excellent flavor and is also very hardy.

### Sowing and Planting

Leeks can be multiple sown or grown in seed boxes started in the
greenhouse in mid-spring at about 60F (15C). Alternatively, sow them in
a seedbed outside in a shallow row 6in (15cm) apart. Starting in early
summer, make holes with a dibber about 6-8in (15-20cm) deep and 6in
(15cm) apart, with 12in (30cm) between rows.

Trim the roots by about two-thirds and the tops by about half, and
drop a plant into each hole. Do not refill the holes, but pour a
little water into each to wash some soil over the roots. On [[organic-vegetable-garden-rotation-deep-beds]],
plant in a block of staggered rows with 6in (15cm) between plants each
way.

### Maintenance and Harvesting

Hoe to keep the rows weed free. As the leeks grow, pull a little soil
around the base of each stem to blanch them, taking care to prevent
soil from getting between the leaves. Leeks are quite hardy and can
usually be left in the ground until required.

## Shallots - *Allium ascalonicum*

Earlier to mature and with a milder flavor than main crop onions,
shallots require similar growing conditions. They are easy to grow from
"sets," rarely troubled by diseases, and store well for winter use.
