# Cannabis and Fibromyalgia

updated: 2026-04-18

Fibromyalgia is a chronic pain condition characterized by widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, sleep disturbances, and cognitive difficulties. Research from The Cannabis Health Index documents the potential of cannabinoids, particularly nabilone (a synthetic cannabinoid), for fibromyalgia symptom management.

## Key Clinical Studies

### Nabilone for Fibromyalgia Pain
**Skrabek, Galimova, and Ethans (2008)**: "Nabilone for the treatment of pain in fibromyalgia." Journal of Pain 9(2):164-73.

This study found that nabilone significantly reduced pain in fibromyalgia patients compared to placebo. Nabilone is a synthetic cannabinoid (CB1/CB2 agonist) approved for chemotherapy-induced nausea.

### Nabilone for Sleep in Fibromyalgia
**Ware, Fitzcharles, Joseph, and Shir (2010)**: "The effects of Nabilone on sleep in fibromyalgia: Results of a randomized controlled trial." Anesthesia and Analgesia 110(2):604-10.

Nabilone improved sleep quality in fibromyalgia patients in a randomized controlled trial. Sleep disturbance is one of the most debilitating aspects of fibromyalgia, and poor sleep perpetuates the pain cycle.

### Cannabinoids, Endocannabinoids, and Inflammation
**Burstein and Zurier (2009)**: "Cannabinoids, endocannabinoids, and related analogs in inflammation." AAPS Journal 11(1):109-19.

This comprehensive review establishes the mechanistic basis for cannabinoid therapy in fibromyalgia, which has a significant inflammatory component.

## Understanding Fibromyalgia

### "A Disorder of the Brain?"
**Schweinhardt, Sauro, and Bushnell (2008)**: "Fibromyalgia: A disorder of the brain?" The Neuroscientist 14(5):415-21.

Modern research increasingly views fibromyalgia as a central nervous system disorder characterized by:
- Central sensitization (amplified pain signaling)
- Altered pain processing in the brain
- Dysregulation of neurotransmitters
- Autonomic nervous system dysfunction

### Endocannabinoid Deficiency Hypothesis
Fibromyalgia may involve a deficiency in endocannabinoid signaling. The endocannabinoid system (ECS) regulates:
- Pain perception (via CB1 receptors in the nervous system)
- Inflammation (via CB2 receptors in immune cells)
- Sleep-wake cycles
- Mood and emotional processing
- Stress response

If the ECS is underactive (clinical endocannabinoid deficiency), supplementing with phytocannabinoids may help restore balance.

## Psychosomatic Research

### Deficits in Positive Affect Regulation
**Zautra et al. (2005)**: "Fibromyalgia: Evidence for deficits in positive affect regulation." Psychosomatic Medicine 67:147-55.

Fibromyalgia patients show specific deficits in regulating positive emotions -- not just managing negative ones. This suggests the condition involves broader emotional processing difficulties.

### Written Emotional Expression
**Broderick, Junghaenel, and Schwartz (2005)**: "Written emotional expression produces health benefits in fibromyalgia patients." Psychosomatic Medicine 67:326-34.

Expressive writing about emotional experiences produced measurable health benefits in fibromyalgia patients. This connects to the [[cannabis-for-anxiety-disorders]] paradigm.

### Trauma and Abuse History
**Walker et al. (1997)**: "Psychosocial factors in fibromyalgia compared with rheumatoid arthritis: II. Sexual, physical, and emotional abuse and neglect." Psychosomatic Medicine 59(6):572-77.

Fibromyalgia patients showed significantly higher rates of sexual, physical, and emotional abuse and neglect compared to rheumatoid arthritis patients. This suggests that trauma history may be a contributing factor or that the body stores traumatic experience as chronic pain.

### Tai Chi for Fibromyalgia
**Wang et al. (2010)**: "A randomized trial of Tai Chi for fibromyalgia." New England Journal of Medicine 363:743-75.

Published in the NEJM, this study found that Tai Chi significantly improved fibromyalgia symptoms, suggesting that mind-body movement practices can address the condition.

## Mind-Body Perspective

Fibromyalgia poses the question: What pain have you been carrying that has become so heavy it can no longer be localized? The condition represents a kind of total-body distress signal -- the body's alarm system stuck in the "on" position. The association with trauma history suggests that unprocessed emotional pain becomes physical pain when the psychological defenses can no longer contain it.

The connection between positive affect regulation deficits and fibromyalgia is particularly striking: these patients have difficulty experiencing joy, pleasure, and satisfaction -- not just managing pain. This is where cannabis may have a unique role, as it is well-known for enhancing the capacity for pleasure, music appreciation, food enjoyment, and social connection.

## How Cannabis May Help

### Pain Reduction
Cannabinoids activate CB1 receptors in pain pathways, reducing the perception of pain signals. This is particularly relevant for central sensitization in fibromyalgia.

### Sleep Improvement
Both THC and CBD have sedative properties that can help restore normal sleep architecture. Better sleep breaks the pain-insomnia cycle that perpetuates fibromyalgia.

### Mood Enhancement
Cannabis may help address the positive affect regulation deficit by enhancing the capacity for pleasure and reducing the psychological burden of chronic pain.

### Anti-Inflammatory Action
While fibromyalgia is primarily a central pain condition, peripheral inflammation may still play a role. CB2-mediated anti-inflammatory effects may help.

### Muscle Relaxation
THC is a well-documented muscle relaxant that can help with the muscle spasms and tension that often accompany fibromyalgia.
