# Fungal Spore Biology

Fungal spores are the primary vehicles for dispersal, survival, and reproduction in the fungal kingdom. They range from microscopic single cells to elaborate multi-armed structures adapted to specific environments. This page covers spore formation, discharge mechanisms, dispersal strategies, and the remarkable diversity of spore forms documented by [[nicholas-money]] and others.

## Spore Discharge — Buller's Drop

The spore catapult mechanism (Buller's drop) is one of the most elegant processes in biology, first detailed by A.H.R. Buller in his seven-volume Researches on Fungi:

1. A fluid droplet (Buller's drop) condenses on the spore surface due to the spore's hygroscopic (water-absorbing) sugars
2. The droplet swells until it contacts a neighboring spore or the basidium, forming a liquid bridge
3. Surface tension rapidly pulls the spore toward the bridge, launching it from the basidium
4. The spore is propelled at roughly 1 m/s — too fast for human perception but clearly documented with high-speed photography

### Evaporative Cooling

[[nicholas-money]] discovered that mushrooms actively cool during spore release (up to 5 degrees C below ambient air temperature). This cooling:
- Promotes condensation on spore surfaces (cold surfaces collect condensation more readily)
- Is driven by evaporation from the gill surfaces
- Is stimulated by airflow around the fruiting body
- Creates a "thermal mosaic" on the forest floor, with mushrooms as the coldest inhabitants

The cooling mechanism is essential: if mushrooms warmed, Buller's drops would never form and spores would remain trapped on the gills.

### Aerodynamics of Mushroom Caps

Wind tunnel studies by Money revealed:
- Mushroom stems elevate caps above the ground-level boundary layer of still air
- Airflow divides at the cap's leading edge and accelerates above and below (like an aircraft wing)
- An "air shadow" of calm air forms immediately beneath the cap, allowing spores to fall freely without being blown back
- **Campanulate (bell-shaped) caps** are particularly effective at this — found commonly in meadow species like Psilocybe semilanceata, Conocybe, and Coprinus comatus

## Gasteromycete Spore Release

Gasteromycetes (puffballs, earthstars, phallic fungi) have lost the catapult mechanism:

- **Puffballs:** Discharge spores through pores when disturbed by rain drops or animals. Spores are spherical/ellipsoidal with no hilar appendix (the hump found on catapult-launched spores).
- **Earthstars:** Star-shaped structures that respond to humidity, lifting the spore sac above wet ground.
- **Phallic fungi (stinkhorns):** Attract insects with putrid odor; spores adhere to insect bodies and are carried away.
- **False truffles:** Evolved from boletes; produce spores underground and rely on rodents for dispersal. Genetic data confirm some false truffles are closely related to boletes.

The loss of water-dependent spore propulsion enables gasteromycetes to colonize drier habitats — sandy soils, deserts — where other basidiomycetes cannot fruit.

## Aquatic Spores — Ingold's Jewels

C.T. Ingold's pioneering work on aquatic hyphomycetes revealed an astonishing array of spore shapes:

### Tetraradiate (Four-Armed) Spores
- Star-shaped spores with arms radiating from a central hub
- **Actinospora** produces the largest fungal spore known — 0.5 mm diameter, visible with a hand lens
- Arms increase the "search area" for finding submerged leaves in flowing water (based on search theory from antisubmarine warfare mathematics)
- When an arm strikes a surface, water flow pivots the spore until a three-point landing is achieved, then adhesive is secreted
- Found in unrelated fungi (ascomycetes, basidiomycetes, zygomycetes) — a case of **evolutionary convergence**

### Sigmoidal (S-Shaped) Spores
- Tumble in water currents to explore large volumes
- Roll sideways or somersault until attachment is made
- Secrete adhesive at contact points

### Why Not Spherical?
Contrary to intuition, arms do NOT slow spore descent in water — water is a viscous medium at the microscale. The real advantage is increased probability of encountering a substrate (larger "search vehicle" = higher hit probability).

### Aero-Aquatic Spores
- Barrel-shaped and cage-shaped spores that trap air bubbles
- Buoyancy lifts them along the water surface
- Specialize in leaf decomposition in stagnant water

### Marine Spores
- **Nia** (a gasteromycete): tiny orange globes studied on the wreck of the Tudor warship Mary Rose (sank 1545)
- Marine ascomycetes outfit spores with mucilage rings and tails for attachment
- The terrestrial ascospore cannon mechanism works perfectly underwater

## Zoospores — Self-Propelled Spores

### Chytrid Zoospores
- Single whiplash flagellum (undulates base to tip)
- Only flagellated cells in Kingdom Fungi
- Drive through water at impressive speed relative to body size
- Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis: chytrid parasitizing amphibian skin, implicated in global frog declines
- Olpidium brassicae: parasitizes root hairs of vegetables, transmits plant viruses
- Anaerobic chytrids live in cow rumen, decomposing cellulose

### Oomycete Zoospores (Water Molds)
- Two flagella: anterior (hairy, pulls through water like breaststroke arms) and posterior (smooth, pushes like tadpole tail)
- Two types produced sequentially: primary (teardrop, flagella at pointed end) and secondary (kidney-shaped, flagella from side groove)
- Speed: up to 1 meter per hour, or 27 body lengths per second
- Triggered by starvation — when sugars in growth medium are exhausted

### Zoospore Production Process
1. Hyphal tips cease extending and darken (cytoplasm transfer)
2. Tips isolated by septa to form sporangia
3. Cytoplasm cleaved into spore initials
4. Tip refashioned into a nozzle
5. Spores discharged into water

## Spore Germination and Survival

- Many soil fungi persist as resting spores for decades (Synchytrium endobioticum spores survive 40+ years)
- Spore germination requires specific cues: nutrients, temperature, moisture, sometimes chemical signals from host organisms
- Lichen spores require algal partners for germination — most fail to establish
- See [[fungal-biodiversity]] for estimates of undiscovered species that exist only as spores in soil
