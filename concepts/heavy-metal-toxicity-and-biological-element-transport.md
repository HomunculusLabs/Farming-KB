# Heavy Metal Toxicity and Biological Element Transport in Soils

## Overview

Soils contain both essential [[permaculture-design]]. Living organisms continuously concentrate, disperse, and transform elements, creating complex patterns of nutrient cycling and pollutant accumulation that the [[earthworms]]** may concentrate lead to lethal levels in polluted soils
- Salt in vegetable cooking water blocks uptake of some heavy metals on plant proteins — if lead pollution is suspected, add salt to cooking waters

### Radon (Rn)
- A gas from the decay of uranium
- Seeps up in most soils, especially over volcanic areas and igneous rock
- Can pollute super-insulated buildings if air is not exchanged
- Several million homes in the UK and USA have high radon levels
- **Avoid** building poorly ventilated homes in areas of radioactive ores, granites, and basalts, or using such rocks/crushed metals in buildings
- If houses are to be super-insulated or draught-proofed, adequate **heat exchangers** must be fitted to bring in clean air

## Biological Element Transport

Beyond simple chemical leaching, living organisms are the primary agents of element concentration and dispersal in soils. These biological effects are myriad but include several well-documented mechanisms:

### Concentration by Selective Species

Fungi, bacteria, and invertebrates can seek out, assemble, and change specific compounds to stable new forms as concretions or nodules. The following elements are known to be concentrated by specific organisms:

### Concentration by Accumulation of Detritus

Diatoms, swamp peats, whole forests, sponges (and their spicules of silica), molluscs, and vertebrates are at times buried by vulcanism, sedimentation, or deposition in oceanic deeps. This forms specifically concentrated sediments that eventually become ore bodies or rock types:

- **Coal** from buried forests
- **Rare earths** from specific marine deposits
- **Manganese nodules** on sea floors from leached manganese

### Dispersal by Transpiration

Many water plants can dissociate and transpire a great variety of substances to the atmosphere:

- **Reeds** do this with mercury, hydrogen, and other elements ranging from phosphates to chlorine
- Not all substances are vaporised to drift off in winds; many are deposited in special leaf repositories, or evaporated to a wax, dust, or efflorescence on leaves and stems
- These are then washed down to earth by rain throughfall
- This mechanism concentrates both major plant nutrients and minor elements in the **top 4 cm (1–2 inches) of soil** below trees
- Metals, oxides, halogens, acids, alkalis, and salts are all concentrated by this process

### Concentration by Metabolic Processes

All living things build their bodies by ingesting a range of complex foods. From these, materials are selected to build bones, flesh, blood, brain tissue, organs, nails, hair, fats, and milk. Thus, animal bodies are complex storages of elements.

Even faeces contain very different concentrations of potash, nitrates, and pollutants from the different concentrations of foods consumed. By behaviours (culturally-determined or species-specific), these are variously disposed of in the environment. Plants and animals ceaselessly ingest and defecate, reflect and exude over their whole lifetimes, thus altering the concentration of nutrients in their immediate environment.
