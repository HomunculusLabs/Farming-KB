## Clinical Trials of Mushroom Polysaccharides in Cancer Treatment

Mushroom-derived polysaccharides have undergone extensive clinical testing, with several achieving regulatory approval as cancer therapeutics, particularly in Japan. This concept reviews the clinical trial evidence for the major mushroom polysaccharides.

## Clinical Development Overview

### Regulatory Status
"Many of the mushroom polysaccharides have proceeded through Phase I, II and III clinical trials." With the exception of [[lentinan]], PSK, and PSP, most remain at the experimental stage.

### Route of Administration Challenge
- Lentinan and [[schizophyllan]] are large molecules **only effective by i.v. or i.p. administration**
- Oral administration becoming increasingly important for cost reduction and patient quality of life
- "A simple acid treatment of the whole β-1,6 glucan produced molecular masses of c 10k Da which when administered orally demonstrated activity" (Fujimiya et al., 2000)

### Safety Profile
"In almost all the examples the polysaccharides act mainly as immune-stimulants with little or no adverse drug reactions."

### Growing Western Acceptance
"There has been a major increase in publications in peer-reviewed Western Journals by Asian scientists and a perceptible change in the attitude of Western medical doctors and scientists towards the pharmaceutical developments derived from traditional Chinese medicines."

## Lentinan Clinical Trials

### Xenograft Results
Extensive testing across multiple tumour types in mice showed significant inhibition:

| Tumour Type | Mouse Strain | Inhibition Rate | Complete Regression |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **MM-46** | C3H/He | 100.0% | 9/9 |
| **A/Ph.MC.S1** | A/Ph(A/J) | 100.0% | 18/18 |
| **P-815** | DBA/2 | 89.0% | 2/8 |
| **L-5178Y** | DBA/2 | 84.0% | 3/9 |
| **MH-134** | C3H/He | 100.0% | — |
| **DBA/2.MC.CS-T** | DBA/2 | 94.2% | — |

### Oncogenesis Prevention
- MC-induced tumours in SWM/Ms mice: **83% → 31%** tumour incidence
- MC-induced tumours in DBA/2 mice: **78% → 37%** tumour incidence
- Adenovirus-induced tumours: **79% → 40%** tumour incidence

### Gastric Cancer Clinical Trial (Taguchi et al., 1983)
Randomised controlled study of Lentinan combined with tegafur vs. tegafur alone in gastric [[cannabis-for-cancer-patients]]:

| Parameter | Tegafur Alone | Lentinan + Tegafur |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| **50% survival** | 83 days | 173 days |
| **>1 year survival** | — | 19.5% |
| **>2 year survival** | — | 10.4% |
| **>3 year survival** | — | 6.5% |
| **Response rate** | 2.0% | 14.9% |

### Sub-Group Analysis by Prognostic Factors
- **Abdominal localisation**: 166 → 237 days (50% survival)
- **Hepatic/peritoneal metastasis**: 68 → 169 days (P < 0.01)
- **Well-differentiated adenocarcinoma**: 105 → 199 days (P < 0.01)

"With each prognostic factor the addition of Lentinan significantly prolonged 50% survival."

## Schizophyllan Clinical Trials

### Cervical Cancer
"Schizophyllan significantly prolonged the overall survival of Stage II cervical cancer patients but not Stage III." When injected intratumorally, significant infiltration of **Langerhans cells and T-cells** observed.

### Head and Neck Cancer
"Recently Schizophyllan has also been shown to increase overall survival of patients with head and neck cancers" (Kimura et al., 1994).

### Recurrent/Inoperable Gastric Cancer
Randomised controlled study of 367 patients with Schizophyllan plus chemotherapy (tegafur or mitomycin C + 5-fluorouracil) resulted in "significant increase in median survival." However, a similar study was unable to confirm this success.

### Multi-Modal Cancer Trial
Prospective, randomised clinical trial of 312 patients with surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy (fluorouracil) and Schizophyllan in various combinations:
- Patients treated with Schizophyllan had better overall survival
- Subgroup with ≥10% activated CD4+ cells and >25% activated CD8+ cells showed **highly significant** Schizophyllan-induced survival increase

### Regulatory Status
"Schizophyllan has been approved for clinical use in Japan." Currently produced commercially by several Japanese pharmaceutical companies.

## Grifola frondosa (Maitake) Clinical Evidence

### Xenograft Results
"β-D-glucan and glycoprotein complexes derived from this mushroom have strong antitumour activity in xenografts."

### Metastasis Inhibition
Maitake D-fraction and crude Maitake powder demonstrated "remarkable inhibition of metastasis in an immuno-competent mouse model":
- **Hepatic metastases reduced by 81%** (Maitake powder)
- **Hepatic metastases reduced by 91%** (D-fraction)

### Grifron-D (GD)
- Highly purified β-glucan extract (β-1,6 glucan branched with β-1,3-linkage)
- **Orally bioavailable**
- Considerable immunomodulating and antitumour activities in animal models
- Cytotoxic effect on human [[blesching-cannabis-prostate-cancer]] cells (PC9) in vitro through [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]]

## PSP Clinical Trials (China)

Extensive clinical trials conducted primarily in China:
- Used as adjunct to chemotherapy and radiotherapy
- Demonstrated improved survival in gastric, oesophageal, and lung cancers
- Effective in treating leukopenia induced by chemo/radiotherapy
- Available as oral preparation

## PSK Clinical Trials (Japan)

### Approved Drug Status
PSK is an approved cancer drug in Japan, covered by national health insurance. In 1987, it accounted for 25% of total expenditure on anti-cancer agents in Japan.

### Cancer Types Studied
Clinical trials have demonstrated efficacy in:
- Gastric cancer
- Colorectal cancer
- [[blesching-cannabis-breast-cancer]]
- Lung cancer
- Cervical cancer
- Oesophageal cancer

### Chemo/Radiotherapy Adjunct
Consistently demonstrated that PSK combined with conventional therapy provides better outcomes than conventional therapy alone.

## Key Considerations for Clinical Development

### Oral Bioavailability
The inability of large β-glucan molecules (Lentinan, Schizophyllan) to be absorbed orally is a significant limitation. The finding that acid treatment can reduce molecular weight while maintaining activity is a potential breakthrough for oral formulations.

### Combination Therapy
All successful clinical applications involve combination with conventional chemotherapy or radiotherapy, not mushroom polysaccharides as standalone treatments.

### Patient Selection
Subgroup analyses suggest that immune competence markers (activated CD4+ and CD8+ cell percentages) may predict which patients will benefit most from mushroom polysaccharide therapy.

### Study Quality
Some studies have been criticized for inadequate blinding or confounding treatment variables, highlighting the need for rigorous Western-style randomized controlled trials.
