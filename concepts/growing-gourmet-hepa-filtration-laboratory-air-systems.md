# HEPA Filtration and Laboratory Air Systems

Before the advent of HEPA (High Efficiency Particulate Air)
filters, sterile culture work succeeded only by constantly
battling legions of contaminants with toxic disinfectants,
presenting real health hazards to the laboratory personnel.
Now, the use of disinfectants is minimized because the air
is constantly being re-filtered and cleaned.

## HEPA Filter Specifications

HEPA filters eliminate particulates down to 0.3 microns
with an efficiency rating of 99.99%. ULPA (Ultra-Particulate
Air) filters screen out particles down to 0.1 microns with
99.9999% efficiency. HEPA filters are the workhorse of the

## Positive Pressurization System

The laboratory should be continuously positive-pressurized
with fresh air. The fresh, outside air is serially filtered
through three stages:

1. **Coarse pre-filter** (30% efficient at 1 micron)
2. **Electrostatic filter** (95-99% efficient at 1 micron)
3. **HEPA filter** (99.99% efficient at 0.3 microns)

Blowers must be properly sized to overcome the cumulative
static pressures of all the filters. In most cases, the
combined static pressure approaches 1.25 inches of water
gauge. Air velocity off the face of the final filter should
be at least 200 feet per minute.

For a 400 sq. ft. [[oyster-mushroom-clean-room-design]], 2 ft. x 2 ft. x 6 in.
filters should be employed. The construction of the intake
air system should allow easy access to the filters so they
can be periodically removed, cleaned, and replaced.

Fresh [[growing-gourmet-growing-room-ventilation-air-exchange]] is essential to displace the carbon
dioxide and other gases being generated by the mushroom
[[mycelium]] during incubation. Should [[carbon-dioxide-retention-jar-lid-mushroom-cultivation]] levels
escalate, the growth of contaminants becomes more likely.

## Laminar Flow Bench

A stand-alone laminar flow bench constantly recirculates
the air within the laboratory. The air entering the
laboratory has already been filtered from the positive
pressurization system. By having two independent systems,
the lifespan of the filter in the laminar flow bench is
greatly extended.

The laminar flow bench recirculates the entire [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]
once every 1.3 minutes in a well-designed laboratory. This
is far more than what is minimally necessary.

## Turbulent Air Concept

There is a strong preference for creating a laboratory
characterized by turbulent air streams, with a high rate
of impact through micron filters. Turbulent, filtered air
in the laboratory is far more desirable than a still air
environment.

The key idea: if airborne particles are introduced, they
are kept airborne from turbulence. If kept airborne,
particles are soon impacted into the micron filters. This
reduces the stratification of contaminant populations in
the laboratory.

This concept is diametrically opposite to the "old school"
concept that still-air in the laboratory is the ideal
environment for handling pure cultures.
