### Mulga (Acacia aneura)

Mulga is an outstanding Australian fodder tree surviving on 200 to 500mm
annual rainfall across all states except Victoria and Tasmania. Some trees
reach 9 meters with straight trunks. During drought conditions, mulga
provides critical emergency fodder for cattle and sheep. The foliage is
palatable and nutritious, and the tree's deep root system allows it to
remain productive when surface pastures have completely dried.

### Leucaena (Leucaena leucocephala)

[[permaculture-mulberry]] serves a dual role as a fodder
tree and the primary food source for silkworms in sericulture systems. The
leaves are highly nutritious and palatable to livestock. Mulberry grows
rapidly, coppices readily, and produces abundant leaf biomass that can be
harvested multiple times per growing season.

## Designing Fodder Tree Systems

Successful integration of fodder trees requires attention to several design
principles. Trees should be planted at appropriate densities for the
targeted stocking rate, with alleys wide enough for machinery access if
needed. Nurse species like tagasaste can be interplanted with longer-term
fodder trees to accelerate establishment. Fodder trees should be chosen
to fill seasonal feed gaps, with species selected for winter, summer, and
autumn production to extend the year-round availability of green feed.

Integration with bee forage is an important secondary function. Many
fodder trees, including tagasaste and various Acacia species, flower
during periods when little else is available, supporting honey production
and pollinator populations that benefit the entire farm system.

## Nutritional Considerations

When designing fodder tree systems, growers should be aware of toxicity
issues. Some otherwise excellent fodder species contain secondary compounds
that limit their use. Leucaena's mimosine, tree lupin's alkaloids, and
the tannins found in many Acacia species all require management through
appropriate species selection, grazing rotation, or processing methods.
Testing new fodder species with small numbers of livestock before large-
scale planting is always advisable.
