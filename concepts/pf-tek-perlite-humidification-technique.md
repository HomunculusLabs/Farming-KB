# PF TEK Perlite Humidification Technique

## Overview

The perlite humidification technique is an alternative [[ectomycorrhizal-mushroom-fruiting-ecology]] chambers that uses a layer of wetted perlite to create and maintain high humidity conditions. Originally described as an anonymous internet newsgroup posting, this low tech approach gained rapid popularity among cultivators for its simplicity and effectiveness. When used in conjunction with the PF dual [[fruiting-conditions]].

## What Is Perlite

Perlite is a bright white, lightweight volcanic glass that has been expanded through rapid heating. It is used as an ingredient in potting soil for aeration and is readily available at any plant shop or garden center. Perlite has a distinctive appearance often compared to Rice Krispies, with a porous, irregular structure made up of tiny air filled bubbles.

## The Science of Perlite Humidification

### How It Works

The perlite humidification process relies on several physical properties of the material:

- Perlite absorbs water into its porous structure through [[bloomfield-buller-drop-surface-tension-spore-catapult-basidiospore-discharge]], then released through evaporation at a steady, controlled rate. This gradual release is what makes perlite ideal for humidification: it provides consistent moisture without oversaturation.

### The Water Layer Dynamic

Within a perlite layer, water distributes itself with gravity pulling the bulk of it to the bottom. The upper portions of the layer remain damp but not saturated. This creates a gradient from wetter at the bottom to drier at the top, which produces a steady rate of evaporation across the entire surface area.

## Setting Up Perlite Humidification

### Required Materials

- Horticultural perlite (available at plant shops)
- A suitable [[dual-chambered-terrarium]] works well)
- Clean water (distilled or filtered preferred)
- Mushroom cakes ready for fruiting

### Installation

1. Place a layer of perlite at the bottom of the fruiting chamber
2. Wet the perlite thoroughly with clean water
3. Drain off any excess standing water (the perlite should be damp, not submerged)
4. Position the mushroom cakes on top of the perlite layer
5. Seal the terrarium

### Direct Contact Benefit

When cakes are placed directly on the perlite, wick like action transfers moisture directly to the cakes at the contact points. This direct moisture transfer supplements the ambient humidity and helps keep the cake surface hydrated.

## Performance Characteristics

### Humidity Levels

A properly set up perlite layer creates and maintains near 100% humidity within a sealed terrarium. The consistent evaporation from the perlite surface saturates the air with water molecules, creating the humid shroud that [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] requires for fruiting.

### Duration

The perlite layer provides sufficient humidification for multi flushing without replenishment. The estimated duration of effective humidification from a single wetting is weeks, making it a low maintenance approach compared to manual spraying multiple times per day.

### Comparison with Spray-Based Humidification

The traditional PF TEK dual chambered terrarium relies on manual spraying through the spray shield to maintain humidity. The perlite technique offers several advantages:

- Eliminates the need for frequent manual spraying
- Provides more consistent humidity levels without fluctuations
- Reduces the risk of water droplets directly hitting the cakes
- Requires less daily attention from the cultivator
- Works effectively even without the spray shield in place

The spray-based approach retains some advantages for cultivators who want precise control over humidity cycles, and the spray shield provides ventilation opportunities during each spraying session.

## Integration with the Dual Chambered Terrarium

The perlite technique becomes even more effective when used in conjunction with the standard PF dual chambered terrarium. The sealed environment of the aquarium terrarium prevents moisture loss, allowing the perlite evaporation to accumulate and maintain saturation levels.

### With Aquarium Bubbler

The original report described a setup using a small PF aquarium bubbler alongside the perlite layer. However, the observer noted that the wet perlite was clearly the primary humidity source, with the cakes flushing much better than with the bubbler alone. The bubbler is optional and not necessary for effective perlite humidification.
