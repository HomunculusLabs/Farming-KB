# Brown Rot Fungi in Bioremediation

Brown rot fungi are a physiological group of basidiomycetes that
primarily degrade cellulose and hemicellulose while modifying lignin
through demethylation rather than depolymerization. Although traditionally
studied for their role in wood decay, brown rot fungi have demonstrated
significant potential for the bioremediation of environmental pollutants
including chlorophenols, [[fungal-chlorinated-aromatic-degradation]] compounds.

## Distinction from White Rot Fungi

The primary distinction between white rot and brown rot fungi lies in their
approach to lignocellulose degradation. White rot basidiomycetes degrade
all cell wall polymers including lignin, whereas brown rot fungi remove
cellulose and hemicellulose while only modifying lignin. The Bavendamm
test, which monitors polyphenol oxidase activity on agar plates containing
guaiacol or gallic acid, differentiates white rots (positive) from brown
rot species (negative). This difference in ligninolytic capability has
important implications for pollutant degradation strategies.

## Degradation of Chlorophenols

Wheat straw cultures of the brown rot fungi Gloeophyllum striatum and G.
trabeum degrade 2,4-dichlorophenol (2,4-DCP) and pentachlorophenol (PCP).

pollutant concentrations, as demonstrated with TNT, is advantageous for
heavily contaminated sites. Their capacity to grow under nutrient-poor
conditions makes them suitable for treatment of low-nutrient waste streams.

## Fenton Chemistry and Pollutant Degradation

The Fenton-type chemistry employed by brown rot fungi for wood decay
generates highly reactive hydroxyl radicals that can non-specifically
attack a wide range of organic pollutants. This non-enzymatic mechanism
may be particularly effective for pollutant classes that are poor
substrates for [[singh-ligninolytic-enzymes-fungal-bioremediation]], including some heavily chlorinated
compounds and high-molecular-weight PAHs. The iron-reducing metabolites
produced by brown rot fungi could potentially be harnessed for in situ
chemical oxidation of contaminated soils.
