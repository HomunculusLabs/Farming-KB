# Soil Fertility and Sustainable Soil Management

The GROW BIOINTENSIVE approach to soil fertility centers on building and maintaining soil humus through the closed-loop cycling of compostable materials grown on-site.

## The Soil Crisis

Topsoil is being lost 18 to 80 times faster than it is formed. When soil erodes or is degraded:
- Crop yields decrease
- Fertilizers become less effective
- Water quality suffers
- The land eventually becomes unfarmable

GROW BIOINTENSIVE aims to address this by growing soil fertility along with crops, rather than mining soil and importing fertility.

## The Humus Foundation

Humus is the key to sustainable soil fertility. It:
- Provides nutrients in a form plants can use
- Improves soil structure (aeration, water retention, drainage)
- Supports beneficial microbial life
- Buffers pH and [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]]
- Increases [[mollison-designers-tropical-soils-and-cation-exchange-management]] capacity (CEC)

### Target Humus Level
GROW BIOINTENSIVE aims to build soil humus to approximately **6%** through regular compost applications.

## Nutrient Cycling: The Closed Loop

### The Challenge
Most agricultural systems are open-loop: nutrients are removed with harvests and not fully returned. This leads to progressive soil depletion.

### The GROW BIOINTENSIVE Solution
1. Grow **compost crops** on 60% of the growing area
2. Return all crop residues and compost crop materials to the soil
3. Minimize exports from the mini-farm
4. Use humanure (where legally permitted) to close the nutrient loop

## Soil Fertility from Compost

Compost provides balanced nutrition in slow-release form:

### Major Nutrients
- **Nitrogen (N):** From compost, legume cover crops, and [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]]
- **Phosphorus (P):** From compost; rock phosphate can supplement if needed
- **Potassium (K):** From compost; wood ash can supplement in small amounts

### Minor Nutrients and Trace Minerals
- Calcium, magnesium, sulfur, iron, manganese, zinc, copper, boron, molybdenum
- All provided in adequate amounts by well-made compost from diverse materials

### The Nutrient Release Pattern
Compost provides nutrients gradually over the [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]], reducing leaching losses and matching plant uptake patterns better than synthetic fertilizers.

## Biological vs. Chemical Fertility

### Chemical Fertility Problems
- Synthetic fertilizers provide only a subset of nutrients (typically N-P-K)
- They acidify soil over time
- They harm beneficial soil microorganisms
- They create soluble salts that can burn plants
- They produce runoff that pollutes waterways
- They destroy soil structure (especially when applied to uncultivated soil)

Testing soil helps track progress and identify problems:

### What to Test
1. **pH** (target 6.0-7.0 for most vegetables)
2. **Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium** levels
3. **Calcium and Magnesium** ratio
4. **[[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] / humus** percentage
5. **Trace minerals** if problems are suspected

### When to Test
- Before starting a new garden
- Every 1-3 years once established
- When plant growth shows unexplained problems
