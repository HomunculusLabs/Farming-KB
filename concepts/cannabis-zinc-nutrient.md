## Cannabis Zinc (Zn)

## Overview

Zinc is a [[cannabis-manganese-nutrient]] and [[organic-fertilizer-sources-guide]] of zinc include:

- Well-rotted manures (cow, horse, chicken)
- Compost (variable content)
- Seaweed and kelp extracts
- Zinc sulfate (inorganic but commonly used)
- Rock dusts (some varieties contain zinc)

Natural chelates such as [[query-how-to-fix-zinc-deficiency-in-cannabis]] is severe, new leaf blades contort and dry out.

### Symptoms

- Interveinal chlorosis of young leaves
- New leaves and growing tips develop small, thin blades that contort and wrinkle
- Leaf tips and later margins discolor and burn
- Burned spots on leaves could grow progressively larger
- Often confused with manganese or iron deficiency
- When severe, new leaf blades contort and dry out
- Flower buds also contort into odd shapes, turn crispy dry, and are often hard
- Lack of growth including buds

### Deficiency Progression Timeline

1. Interveinal chlorosis of young leaves
2. New leaves develop thin, wispy leaves
3. Leaf tips discolor, turn dark, and die back
4. New growth contorts horizontally
5. New bud and leaf growth stops

### Treatment

- Flush the growing medium with a diluted mix of a complete fertilizer containing chelated trace elements, including zinc, iron, and manganese
- Add a quality-brand hydroponic micronutrient mix containing chelated trace elements
- Zinc is also necessary for proper utilization of cannabis-phosphorus|phosphorus
- Because all three (iron, zinc, manganese) often occur concurrently, treat with a chelated dose of all three

## Toxicity

Zinc is **extremely toxic in excess**. Severely toxic plants die quickly.
