## Cyanide Biodegradation by Fungi
Cyanide is one of the most rapidly acting metabolic poisons known, yet certain
fungi possess remarkable abilities to degrade and detoxify both free cyanide
and its metal complexes. Fungal cyanide biodegradation represents an important
capability for remediation of industrial sites contaminated with this compound.

## Cyanide Chemistry and Environmental Significance
Hydrogen cyanide (HCN) may account for more human deaths throughout history
than any other toxin due to its use in executions and genocide. Key
environmental facts:
- HCN inhibits respiration by binding to Fe3+ in cytochrome c oxidase
- Inhalation of concentrations greater than 0.3 ppm is toxic; 300 ppm is
  fatal without prompt treatment
- Free cyanide (HCN/CN-) and simple cyanides (KCN, NaCN) are the most toxic
  forms
- Twenty-eight elements can react with cyanide to form 72 different
  metallocyanides
- Iron cyanide complexes (hexacyanoferrates) are the most stable but can
  dissociate under acidic conditions
- Nitriles (organic cyanides, RCN) are used widely in industry as chemical
  precursors

Major sources of environmental cyanide contamination include:
- Gold and silver mining (cyanide leaching processes)
- Metal plating and finishing industries
- Gas works and coking plants
- Iron and steel manufacturing
- Petroleum refining
- Photographic film processing
- Pharmaceutical manufacturing

## Fungal Cyanide Degradation Pathways
Fungi can degrade cyanide through several biochemical routes:

### 1. Hydrolytic Pathway
Cyanide is converted to formamide and then to formic acid, eventually yielding
carbon dioxide and ammonia. This pathway involves the enzyme cyanidase
(cyanide dihydratase).

### 2. Oxidative Pathway
Cyanide is oxidized to cyanate (OCN-) and then hydrolyzed to carbon dioxide
and ammonia. This involves cyanide mono-oxygenase.

### 3. Rhodanese Pathway
Cyanide is converted to thiocyanate (SCN-) by transfer of sulfur from a sulfur
donor. While thiocyanate is less toxic than cyanide, it still requires further
microbial processing for complete detoxification.

### 4. Nitrilase Pathway
Nitrilase enzymes can hydrolyze nitriles directly to carboxylic acids and
ammonia, bypassing the intermediate formation of free cyanide. This is
particularly relevant for organocyanide compounds used in industrial synthesis.
