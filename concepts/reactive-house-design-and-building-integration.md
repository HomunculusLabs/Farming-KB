## Reactive House Design and Building Integration

The "reactive house" is Mollison's concept for buildings that respond to and work with their environment rather than fighting against it. In [[permaculture]], buildings sit at the core of the zonation system and must be constructed to supply their own heat, manage water, integrate with gardens, and reduce energy consumption.

## The Building at the Core

"Man lives in a [[artillery-fungus-and-built-environment-fungi]], in all climates. His shelters are at the core of the zonation system of permaculture, and whether he builds for himself or his livestock, it is essential that the new buildings are so constructed as to supply their own heat and at least some food."

### Why Buildings Matter
- Most domestic energy is consumed in buildings
- Buildings are where we survive extremes of heat and cold
- Buildings are where we supervise and plan life-support systems
- A diverse garden supplies foods that don't need cooking, reducing fuel demand

## Fuel Conservation Through Food Choice

"It is self-evident that a diverse garden becomes a source of a variety of foods that do not need to be cooked, so reducing the need for cooking fuels."

- Nuts, fruits, greens, and many root crops need no cooking
- Equal or superior food value to grains and pulses
- Old emphasis on grains/pulses creates fuel demand countries like India cannot afford
- Strategies: one-pot cooking, cooking for larger community, reheating rather than cooking from scratch

## Building-Climate Integration

### Aspect and Slope
Aspect of slopes is critical in deciding between drought-resistant and damp-tolerant species. Every building has "aspects," and by trellis construction, their effects can be increased.

### Trellis as Climate Modifier

#### Vertical Trellis Applications
- Built at near right-angles to E, W, and N walls
- Separates functional space: recreational, garden, or service area
- Prevents cold wind flow along walls; acts as sun trap
- Provides structure for vine crop
- More effective on earth banks, tyre walls, stone walls, rockery bases
- Can curve out from house corners or break up large building facades

#### Institutional and Urban Applications
- Large building surfaces converging create wind tunnels—trellis is often the only answer
- Arrowed entries baffle wind further
- Roads of no thoroughfare value designed as wind tunnels can be converted with boulders, plants, and trellis to sheltered, sinuous access
- Block dust, cold, and noise as side effects

#### Horizontal Trellis Uses
- Shade windows from full summer sun
- Create overhead vine shelter for tender crops in extreme climates
- Increase [[albedo-and-solar-radiation]] on crops

### The Shadehouse
"The refreshing coolness of a shadehouse in the hot Australian summer has to be experienced to be believed." Even tiny pools, a few ferns, and spray or drip of water increase the effect:
- **Physically**: By evaporation
- **Psychologically**: Air in such places has "a different quality, an aliveness normally missing from the languid air of still areas"

### Microclimate Manipulation
"By just rounding a corner, the climate alters from one suited to the soft herbs such as celery, parsley, chives (and strawberries) to a dry, hot site suited to the aromatic herbs, and producing many more oils."

### Additive Features
- Reflection or radiation from walls and ponds
- Glasshouses could be constructed with reflective pond to the north
- Solar ponds as heaters
- Trellis backed up by permanent windbreak as trees grow

## Desert House Design

### Wiltja Climate Modification
The traditional Aboriginal wiltja can be productively enhanced:

#### North Side
- Deciduous vine trellis (grape) or trees ([[acacia-albida]]) for summer shade/winter sun
- Light-foliaged Paulownia or A. albida shade both house and crops

#### South Side
- Evergreen vine trellis as arbour (Tecoma vine)
- Cool arbours for strawberries, mint, blackcurrant, gooseberry, soft herbs in deep mulch

#### Wind Protection
- Tall tamarisk, white cedar, giant bamboo screen cold SE winds
- Smaller bamboo or screens prevent cold air-flow along house walls in winter
- The "yuu" or windbreak near the wiltja provided by smaller bamboo

#### Roof
- Vine trellis over wiltja roof (ivy or trellis on house walls) modifies heat loss and gain
- Small or strongly-constructed roof can be soil-covered, mulched, and planted to ice-plants, cacti, succulents, and hardy desert species
- Watered roof plantings yield cool air in summer, external insulation in winter

### Grown Shelters
- Ngumpa (shade house), yuu (windbreak), and wiltja (house) are "fairly sophisticated designs for comfort"
- Making these permanent 'grown' shelters saves cutting mulga at camps
- Combinations of bamboo, trellis, spinifex, and vine would make very comfortable outdoor living
- Spinifex thatched shelters should go to mulch after use (good insulation for roofing)

### Water Integration
- Permanent houses: shower water led to slotted pipe drains under shadehouse or garden
- Wiltjas: pebble mounds with showers overhead provide water treatment and garden moisture
- "Many showers, so placed, make [[vegetable-garden-watering-systems-spacing]] an automatic process"
- Washing-up, shower, bath, washing water containing soap led under mulch is beneficial

### Energy Systems
- Hot water from solar collectors proving very satisfactory
- Light for wiltjas from solar panels plus battery certainly possible
- Solar-electric fences for wild cattle and camels
- Dry toilet systems at outstations or areas reserved for faeces burial in tree-crop sites
- Windmills (petrol-free) very effective in raising water to height
- Windmill gears lubricate with castor oil or jojoba oil (grows well in arid areas; needs only crude press)

## Redundant Structures as Climate Tools

"Even redundant structures and earthworks can be designed to modify climatic extremes, to provide niches for important or preferred plants, and to reduce active energy needs."

## The Reactive House Philosophy

The reactive house concept encompasses:
1. **Self-heating**: Buildings designed to capture and store [[fukuoka-textdoc-three-dimensional-solar-energy-methodless-method]]
2. **Self-cooling**: Shade, ventilation, and evaporation designed in
3. **[[water-management]]**: All wastewater directed to productive use
4. **Food production**: At least some food grown on or around the building
5. **Energy minimization**: Reducing both heating/cooling needs and cooking fuel demands
6. **Integration with landscape**: Buildings as part of the permaculture system, not separate from it

## Energy Hierarchy

While household [[desert-house-water-energy-conservation]] is important, Mollison places it in context:
- Greatest waste: irresponsible industries producing packaging, newspapers, gas-guzzling vehicles
- Household aim: least energy use for own sake
- Simultaneously: attack the rationale of energy-wasting at the industrial level
