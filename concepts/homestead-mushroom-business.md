# Homestead Mushroom Business

Growing and selling mushrooms on a homestead scale offers high returns on low capital investment. Gourmet mushrooms (shiitake, oyster, lion's mane, wine cap) command $8-20/lb retail and can be produced in small spaces using low-tech methods. This guide covers production, pricing, licensing, and scaling.

## Species Selection for Beginners

### Oyster Mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.)
- Fastest to fruit (2-4 weeks from inoculation)
- Multiple varieties: blue, pink, golden, king, phoenix
- Grow on straw, coffee grounds, cardboard, hardwood sawdust
- Yield: 50-100% [[biological-efficiency]] (1 lb mushrooms per lb dry substrate)
- Price: $8-12/lb retail

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)
- Most recognized gourmet mushroom
- Grow on hardwood logs (oak, maple, beech) or [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]]
- Log method: 6-12 month incubation, fruit for 3-6 years
- Block method: 2-3 month incubation, fruit in 6-8 weeks
- Yield: 25-40% on logs (per year), 75-100% on blocks
- Price: $10-16/lb retail

### Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)
- Growing demand for [[holzer-medicinal-and-culinary-herbs-compendium]] use
- Grow on supplemented hardwood sawdust
- Slower than oyster but higher value
- Yield: 50-75% biological efficiency
- Price: $14-22/lb retail

### Wine Cap (Stropharia rugosoannulata)
- Outdoor mushroom, grows in wood chip beds
- Very low tech: inoculate wood chips, mulch garden paths
- Fruits in spring and fall for 2-4 years
- Price: $8-12/lb retail

## Production Methods

### Indoor Cultivation (Oyster, Shiitake, Lion's Mane)

Setup requirements:
- Clean space (basement, spare room, outbuilding)
- [[hamilton-greenhouse-heating-and-temperature-control]]: 60-75F for most species
- Humidity: 80-95% (humidifier or misting system)
- Fresh air exchange: fan or ventilation
- Light: ambient room light (no grow lights needed for most)
- Shelving: wire shelves with space between blocks

[[bulk-substrate-preparation]]:
1. Hardwood sawdust + wheat bran (80:20 ratio) + water to 60% moisture
2. Sterilize in pressure cooker or autoclave (15 psi, 90 min for bags)
3. Cool to room temperature in clean environment
4. Inoculate with grain spawn (5-10% of substrate weight)
5. Seal bags with filter patch and tape inoculation point
6. Incubate at 70-75F in dark until fully colonized (2-4 weeks)

Fruiting:
1. Move to fruiting area with light, humidity, and fresh air
2. Cut slits in bag or remove block entirely
3. Maintain 85-95% humidity (mist 3-5x daily or use humidifier)
4. Mushrooms form in 7-14 days
5. Harvest before caps fully flatten (best quality)
6. After first flush, soak block in cold water 12-24 hours for second flush
7. Expect 3-4 flushes per block; yield declines each flush

### Log Cultivation (Shiitake)

1. Cut fresh hardwood logs (4-8 inch diameter, 3-4 feet long) in late winter/early spring
2. Let rest 2-3 weeks (avoid using freshly cut logs)
3. Drill holes 1 inch deep, spaced 6 inches apart in diamond pattern
4. Inoculate with shiitake plug spawn or sawdust spawn
5. Seal holes with food-grade wax (cheese wax, beeswax)
6. Stack logs in shade ("log cabin" or parallel stacking)
7. Keep moist (water during dry periods); do not let dry out
8. Incubate 6-12 months until fully colonized (look for white mycelium at cut ends)
9. Force fruit by soaking logs in cold water for 24 hours
10. Fruit 2-4 times per year for 3-6 years

## Pricing Strategy

### Farmers Market Pricing
- Oyster: $10-12/lb
- Shiitake: $12-16/lb
- Lion's Mane: $16-20/lb
- Mixed boxes: $12-15/lb (variety pack)

### Wholesale Pricing
- 50-60% of retail: $5-8/lb
- Sell to restaurants, co-ops, natural food stores
- Volume discounts: 10% for standing weekly orders

### Value-Added Products
- Dried mushrooms: $30-50/lb (10 lb fresh = 1 lb dried)
- Mushroom powder: $15-25 for 4oz jar (seasoning blends)
- Mushroom tinctures: $20-35 for 2oz dropper bottle
- Grow kits: $25-40 each (inoculated block for home growers)

## Licensing and Regulations

Requirements vary by state. Common requirements:
- Cottage food operation registration (for dried products, powders)
- Commercial kitchen or dedicated facility for fresh sales (some states)
- Business license (county/city)
- Sales tax permit
- [[mushroom-identification]] certification (some states require)
- Labeling: species, weight, farm name, address, "keep refrigerated"

Check your state's Department of Agriculture for specific requirements. Some states (Pennsylvania, for example) have specific mushroom growing licenses.

## Startup Costs

### Low-Tech Indoor (Oyster mushrooms, small scale)
- Pressure cooker (large): $100-200
- Grow bags with filter patches: $50-100
- Substrate materials (sawdust, bran): $50-100
- Spawn: $50-100
- Humidifier: $30-80
- Shelving: $50-100
- Misc (thermometer, hygrometer, spray bottles): $50
- Total: $400-800

### Log Cultivation (Shiitake)
- Fresh logs: $0-50 (if you have trees) or $2-5 each purchased
- Plug spawn: $30-50 per 100 plugs (does 3-5 logs)
- Drill and bits: $50-100
- Wax: $10-20
- Total: $150-400 for 20-50 logs

## Scaling Considerations

- Start with one species (oyster mushrooms recommended)
- Master the process before expanding
- Add species as confidence grows
- Automate humidity/ventilation when production exceeds manual capacity
- Consider outdoor wine cap beds for zero-energy production
- Seasonal: oysters year-round indoors, shiitake seasonal on logs
- Target: $1,000-3,000/month at medium scale (50-200 blocks/week)

## Sales Channels

- Farmers markets: see [[farmers-market-selling-guide]]
- Restaurants: chefs seek local mushrooms, approach directly
- CSAs: add mushroom share or include in vegetable boxes
