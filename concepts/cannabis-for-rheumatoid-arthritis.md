Cannabis and cannabinoids show significant promise for managing rheumatoid arthritis (RA) pain and inflammation through [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] immune modulation. The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-cannabinoid-terpene-synergy]] assigns a CHI value of 10 from 3 studies reviewed.

## Understanding Rheumatoid Arthritis

Rheumatoid arthritis is the most crippling form of arthritis, deforming joints and bending bodies. It is an autoimmune disorder in which the immune system attacks healthy joint tissue. Long-time RA sufferers develop characteristic gnarly joint appearances.

Conventional treatments include NSAIDs, corticosteroids, disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs), and biologics -- all with significant side effect profiles. Many patients seek complementary approaches.

## Cannabis and Rheumatoid Arthritis

### Sativex Clinical Trial (2006)

The first-ever controlled trial of a cannabis-based medicine (CBM) in RA enrolled 58 patients in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study using Sativex (sublingual spray). Results:
- Significant analgesic effect observed
- Disease activity significantly suppressed
- Improved sleep quality
- Patients receiving cannabinoids slept better and experienced pain reduction vs. placebo

Researchers wrote: "In the first ever controlled trial of a CBM in RA, a significant analgesic effect was observed and disease activity was significantly suppressed following Sativex treatment."

### Immunomodulatory Mechanisms

Boston scientists (2006, Northeastern University) acknowledged that "Habitual cannabis use has been shown to [positively] affect the human immune system, and recent advances in endocannabinoid research provide a basis for understanding these immunomodulatory effects." They found that regulation of the endocannabinoid circuitry can impact almost every major function associated with the immune system, suggesting therapeutic potential for inflammatory diseases like RA.

### Pain Meta-Analysis (2009)

A University of Massachusetts Medical School meta-analysis (2004-2009) concluded that all types of cannabinoids (endogenous, plant-based, and synthetic) as well as non-cannabinoid parts of the plant effectively reduce pain from inflammation found in:
- Post-surgery patients
- Rheumatism
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Chronic neuropathic pain
- Fibromyalgia

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Sativex consists of roughly equal parts THC (2.7 mg) and CBD (2.5 mg) -- ratios very similar to actual Cannabis sativa. Sativa and sativa-prominent hybrids contain relatively higher THC:CBD ratios, activating both CB1 and CB2 receptors. The anti-inflammatory effects are primarily mediated through CB2 receptors on immune cells, suggesting that strains with balanced THC:CBD profiles may be optimal.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Rheumatoid Arthritis

Research from the University of Michigan found that "the rheumatoids communicate poorly with their relatives about their hurt feelings."

Autoimmune diseases may mirror intense, longstanding conflicts of belief and dueling emotions. Case studies by Dr. H. Levitan revealed common patterns in RA onset:
- Patients in relationships producing simultaneous intense rage and love
- Unable to leave but hating the relationship
- Unexpressed anger that had to be endured

A study of 33 RA patients (4 male, 29 female) described the psychodynamic background as "a chronic inhibited hostile aggressive state as a reaction to the earliest masochistic dependence on the mother that is carried over to the father and all human relationships."

Aggravating factors: poor communication of hurt feelings, suppressed anger, dependency conflicts, hostile aggressive states. Supporting factors: open communication, emotional release, addressing dependency patterns, conflict resolution.

## Supporting Herbs

(E)-beta-caryophyllene activates CB2 receptors for anti-inflammatory action. Found in black pepper, rosemary, basil, clove, cinnamon, oregano. Traditional anti-arthritis herbs include turmeric (curcumin), ginger, boswellia, and willow bark.
