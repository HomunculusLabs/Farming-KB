# Mycorrhizal PAH and TNT Degradation

Mycorrhizal fungi contribute to the degradation of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and 2,4,6-trinitrotoluene (TNT) through their enzymatic capabilities and symbiotic relationships with host plants. While generally slower than [[white-rot-fungi-effluent-decolorization]] in laboratory assays, mycorrhizal fungi offer the advantage of sustained activity in field conditions through host plant carbon supply.

## Mycorrhizal PAH Degradation

### Ectomycorrhizal Fungal Capabilities

Sixteen species (27 strains) of [[fungi-in-the-environment-fungal-endophytes-plant-communities]] have been tested for PAH degradation ability:

| Fungus | PAH Target | Degradation (%) | Duration |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Amanita muscaria | Phenanthrene | 50 | 4 weeks |
| Paxillus involutus | Phenanthrene | 50 | 4 weeks |
| Suillus grevillei | Phenanthrene | 50 | 4 weeks |
| Boletus edulis | Chrysene | 35 | 4 weeks |
| A. muscaria | Chrysene | 35 | 4 weeks |
| Suillus grevillei | Pyrene | 50 | 4 weeks |
| Amanita excelsa | Benzo[a]pyrene | 50 | 4 weeks |
| Leccinum versipelle | Benzo[a]pyrene | 50 | 4 weeks |
| Suillus luteus | Benzo[a]pyrene | 50 | 4 weeks |
| Hebeloma crustuliniforme | Fluoranthene | 52 | 14 days |
| Lactarius deliciosus | Fluoranthene | 39 | 14 days |

No correlation exists between the number of aromatic rings and the rate of metabolism. In broader screening of 58 fungal isolates, over half degraded all five tested PAHs, and only one of 21 ECM species failed to degrade at least one PAH.

### VAM Influence on PAH Remediation

[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]] is not influenced by anthracene at 10 g/kg soil in leek plants. However, addition of contaminated soil containing 8 g/kg total PAHs from a coal production plant decreases VAM colonization in both clover and leek. VAM fungal species Glomus aggregatum and G. mosseae colonize roots in both contaminated and uncontaminated soils, suggesting their utility in remediation.

### Mechanism

Mycorrhizal PAH degradation likely involves extracellular oxidative enzymes including [[fungal-lignin-degradation-enzymology]], tyrosinase, and peroxidase. The oxidative enzymes responsible for PAH oxidation by mycorrhizal fungi are similar to those of [[white-rot-fungi-effluent-decolorization]], but the enzymatic repertoire is less extensive. ECM fungi such as Suillus granulatus produce intracellular tyrosinase, laccase, and peroxidase, while Paxillus involutus produces mainly intracellular laccase.

## Mycorrhizal TNT Degradation

### Initial Reduction Steps

TNT degradation by both [[singh-mycorrhizal-fungi-rhizosphere-remediation]] and white-rot fungi follows similar initial steps:

1. Reduction of one nitro group on the aryl ring to form hydroxylaminodinitrotoluenes
2. Further reduction to form aminodinitrotoluenes
3. Subsequent transformations including condensation reactions

In [[white-rot-fungi-effluent-decolorization]], plasmalemma redox potential causes the initial TNT reduction. Intact cell systems are more effective than cell-free extracts for TNT transformation.

### Ectomycorrhizal TNT Metabolism

ECM fungi have demonstrated TNT transformation capabilities. The mechanism of initial reduction steps is shared across bacteria, saprophytic fungi, and mycorrhizal fungi. The formation of azo compounds, azoxy compounds, and tetranitroazoxytoluenes as dead-end products represents an important detoxification pathway even without complete mineralization.

### Role of Symbiosis

Mycorrhizal fungi show more rapid mineralization of aromatic compounds when in symbiosis with host plants compared to mycelium alone in batch experiments. This implies that ligninolytic enzymes are not a strict requirement for initial degradation steps by mycorrhizal fungi. The host plant provides a continuous carbon supply that sustains enzyme production and fungal biomass in contaminated soils.

## Comparison with White-Rot Fungi

Key differences between mycorrhizal and [[white-rot-fungi-effluent-decolorization]] in PAH/TNT degradation:

- White-rot fungi produce more extensive extracellular ligninolytic enzyme systems (LiP, MnP, laccase)
- Mycorrhizal fungi depend on host plants for carbon, limiting independent operation
- Mycorrhizal fungi can survive for years at contaminated sites through host plant support
- White-rot fungi achieve faster initial degradation rates in controlled conditions
- Mycorrhizal fungi may be more practical for long-term field remediation due to plant-fungus mutualism
- The degradative ability of PCBs by mycorrhizal fungi is equaled or exceeded by P. chrysosporium

## Field Application Considerations

Successful application of [[singh-mycorrhizal-fungi-rhizosphere-remediation]] for PAH/TNT requires:

- Selection of compatible host-fungus-substrate combinations
- Establishment of mycorrhizal infection before or during contamination exposure
- Management of tree growth including fertilization and irrigation
- Consideration of soil characteristics, contaminant concentration, and exposure duration
- Recognition that remediation is slow, depending on root system development and fungal biomass accumulation
