The Spitzenkörper (German for "apical body") is a dense, membrane-
associated organelle found at the growing tips of filamentous fungi.
First described by Brunswik in 1924 and later characterized in detail
by Bartnicki-Garcia and colleagues, the Spitzenkörper functions as the
primary vesicle distribution center that orchestrates polarized hyphal
growth. It is considered the fungal equivalent of the animal cell
centrosome in terms of its role as a spatial organizer of growth
machinery, though it is structurally and functionally distinct.

## Structure and Composition

The Spitzenkörper appears as a spherical, phase-dark body when viewed
under phase-contrast microscopy, typically 1-3 micrometers in diameter,
situated 1-5 micrometers behind the hyphal apex. Ultrastructural
studies using electron microscopy reveal a complex, multilobed
organization. The core consists of an accumulation of secretory
vesicles of heterogeneous size and origin, surrounded by a dense mesh
of actin microfilaments and associated actin-binding proteins.

Two distinct zones are recognizable. The core region contains
microvesicles (40-70 nm diameter) enriched in cell wall-synthesizing
enzymes including chitin synthase, beta-1,3-glucan synthase, and cell
wall precursors such as N-acetylglucosamine and UDP-glucose. The
peripheral zone contains larger macrovesicles (70-150 nm diameter)
that carry plasma membrane components, cell wall proteins, and
enzymes for extracellular matrix modification. This spatial
segregation of vesicle populations allows coordinated delivery of
different materials to specific regions of the extending [[gadd-hyphal-tip-growth-and-branching-mechanisms]].

## Role in Polarized Growth

Hyphal tip extension in filamentous fungi is a highly polarized
process driven by continuous fusion of secretory vesicles at the
apex. The Spitzenkörper serves as the [[gadd-spitzenkorper-vesicle-supply-centre-hyphal-tip-growth-direction]] center (VSC),
a concept formalized by Bartnicki-Garcia's mathematical model of
hyphal morphogenesis. According to this model, the Spitzenkörper
acts as a point source from which vesicles are delivered to the
apex, establishing a shape-generating gradient that maintains the
tubular hyphal form.

Vesicles derived from the Spitzenkörper fuse with the apical plasma
membrane via SNARE-mediated exocytosis, delivering cell wall-synthesizing
enzymes and precursor materials to the growing tip. The spatial pattern
of vesicle delivery determines the shape of the hyphal tip: a centrally
positioned Spitzenkörper produces a hemispherical tip, while lateral
displacement produces curvature and branching. This relationship has
been confirmed experimentally by micromanipulation of the Spitzenkörper
using laser tweezers, where displacement of the organelle reliably
predicts the direction of subsequent growth.

## Cytoskeletal Interactions

The Spitzenkörper is intimately associated with both the actin and
microtubule cytoskeletal networks. Actin cables, nucleated by formins
at the hyphal tip, provide the primary tracks for vesicle transport
to the Spitzenkörper. The class V myosin motor Myo2 transports
secretory vesicles along these actin filaments, delivering them to
the Spitzenkörper for sorting and redistribution. Disruption of actin
polymerization with latrunculin A causes rapid dissolution of the
Spitzenkörper and cessation of tip growth.

Microtubules play a complementary role in delivering vesicles and
organelles from subapical regions to the apical dome. The microtubule
plus-end tracking proteins (+TIPs) CLIP-170 and EB1 localize to
microtubule tips near the Spitzenkörper, suggesting a role in
coordinating microtubule guidance with the actin-based vesicle
delivery system. Dynein-mediated retrograde transport recycles
membrane components from the apex, maintaining vesicle pool homeostasis.

## Regulation and Dynamics

The Spitzenkörper exhibits remarkable dynamic behavior during hyphal
growth. It oscillates laterally at the apex with a periodicity of
30-90 seconds, producing subtle fluctuations in growth direction that
contribute to exploratory growth patterns. During branch initiation,
the Spitzenkörper fragments and a new Spitzenkörper assembles at the
