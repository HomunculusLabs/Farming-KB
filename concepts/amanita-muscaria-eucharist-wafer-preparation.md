# Amanita Muscaria Eucharist Wafer Preparation

The Eucharist wafer preparation is a method of creating shelf-stable, portable sacrament from dried [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]]. The technique produces dried fungal "wafers" or "bread" that can be stored for extended periods, divided into precise doses, and consumed as the original Christian Eucharist -- the "body of Christ."

## Historical Context

The author argues that the Christian Eucharist -- the central sacrament of Christianity involving consumption of bread (the "body of Christ") and wine (the "blood of Christ") -- originated as a literal consumption of dried [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]] mushroom material and its [[amanita-muscaria-ambrosia-preparation]].

Evidence supporting this interpretation:

- **"This is my body"** -- The mushroom IS the body; the living bread IS the mushroom
- **"This is my blood"** -- The [[amanita-muscaria-ambrosia-preparation]] or [[amanita-muscaria-ambrosia-preparation]] is the blood
- **"Do this in remembrance of me"** -- A commandment to continue the practice
- **The Last Supper timing** -- Occurred at Passover; the sacramental meal replaced the traditional Passover meal
- **Universal prohibition against eating the mushroom directly** -- In all three religions (Christian, Zoroastrian, Vedic), the mushroom was always processed before consumption; no religion allowed eating the raw mushroom

## The Herb Flour Method

### Equipment Needed

- Dried [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]] pieces (properly dried and aged)
- Clean coffee grinder or mortar and pestle
- Fine mesh strainer or screen (window screen works well)
- Sealable container for storage

### Procedure

1. **Place dried Amanita muscaria pieces into a clean, dry coffee grinder**
2. **Grind to the finest possible powder** -- The finer the grind, the more efficient the extraction
3. **Sift through a fine mesh strainer** -- Collect the powder that passes through; return larger pieces to the grinder
4. **Repeat grinding and sifting** until all material passes through the strainer
5. **Store herb flour in an airtight container** away from heat, light, and moisture

### Important Notes

- Do not grind the mushroom before it is thoroughly dried -- moisture will gum up the grinder
- A clean grinder is essential; cross-contamination with coffee or other substances affects taste
- The powder degrades faster than intact pieces due to increased surface area exposure to air
- Only powder as much as will be used within a few weeks
- Herb flour has a mild, sweet, nutty taste

### Uses for Herb Flour

Once the herb flour is prepared, it serves as the base material for several sacramental preparations:

- **Direct consumption** -- Encapsulate in gelatin capsules or mix with food
- **Honey drops** -- Mix herb flour with honey to create sweet, portable sacrament
- **Wafer preparation** -- Press herb flour into solid forms
- **Magic food** -- Add to soups, stews, or oatmeal (Siberian method described by Wasson)

## The Living Bread Method (Advanced)

This method creates actual fungal bread by colonizing grain with Amanita muscaria mycelium, producing a solid fungal loaf. See [[amanita-muscaria-living-bread-sacrament]] for full details.

### Summary of Living Bread Process

1. Boil grain (barley, rice) until soft
2. Place in a shallow, wide vessel
3. Place dried Amanita muscaria pieces on the grain
4. Cover with cloth, maintain at 65-70 F
5. Within 4-7 days, mycelium colonizes the grain completely
6. Dry the resulting fungal loaf
7. Slice into wafer-sized pieces for sacramental use

The resulting "bread" is a solid mass of grain colonized by Amanita muscaria mycelium, containing [[muscimol-and-amanita-muscaria]] throughout its structure.

## The Sacred Wine (Blood of Christ)

The wine component of the Eucharist is the [[amanita-muscaria-ambrosia-preparation]] or [[amanita-muscaria-ambrosia-preparation]] preparation. The most historically relevant version substitutes grape juice for water in the extraction:

### Preparation

1. Use approximately 1 oz (28 grams) of dried Amanita muscaria per quart of grape juice
2. Soak for 8-12 hours
3. Strain and press the mushroom pieces to recover more juice
4. Add a few drops of vinegar per cup of juice at the start (seems to increase potency)

### The Dilution Paradox

This wine has a unique property: it gets more potent (up to a point) from being diluted with water. For best results, dilute 2-4 cups water per cup of wine before drinking.

This explains the ancient practice of always diluting wine before drinking:

- **Greek practice:** 2:1 to 3:1 water-to-wine ratio
- **Roman practice:** 2:1 to 4:1 ratio
- **Ancient Near East:** Up to 20:1 ratio

These dilution ratios are impossible for alcoholic wine but perfectly consistent with a water-soluble psychoactive extract. See [[amanita-muscaria-ancient-wine-question]] for detailed analysis.

## The Complete Eucharist Service

Based on the author's reconstruction, the original Christian Eucharist would have consisted of:

1. **The bread** -- Dried Amanita muscaria pieces, herb flour, or living bread wafers
2. **The wine** -- Ambrosia or sacred wine, diluted with water
3. **The ritual** -- A communal meal shared among believers
4. **The experience** -- Healing, prophecy, visions, spiritual communion

The transformation of bread and wine into the "body and blood" is not metaphorical but literal: the mushroom material IS the body, and the extract IS the blood.

## Dosing for Sacramental Use

### Bread Component

- **Maintenance communion:** 1-2 grams dried equivalent
- **Standard sacrament:** 3.5 grams (1/8 oz)
- **Full sacrament:** 7-10 grams (1/4 to 3/8 oz)
- **Maximum:** 14 grams (1/2 oz) per 200 lb person

### Wine Component

- Sip slowly and allow to coat the tongue before swallowing
- Sublingual absorption enhances onset
- The wine and bread can be consumed together or separately

## Connection to Other Traditions

The Eucharist parallels are found in all three major descendant traditions of ancient Amanita muscaria use:

| Tradition | "Body" | "Blood" | Ritual Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| Christian | Bread/wafers | Wine | Eucharist/Communion |
| Zoroastrian (Yasna) | Haoma twigs pressed | Haoma juice | Yasna ceremony |
| Vedic (Rig Veda) | Soma stalks pressed | Soma juice filtered | Soma sacrifice |

In all three traditions, the plant material is pressed or extracted, never consumed whole. The pressed "body" and filtered "blood" are the two components of every sacramental meal.

## Storage and Preservation

- **Herb flour:** Store in airtight container, cool dark place, use within weeks
- **Honey drops:** Longer shelf life due to honey's preservative properties
- **Living bread wafers:** Dried thoroughly, store like dried mushrooms
- **Sacred wine:** Best used fresh; can be refrigerated for short periods
- **Ambrosia:** Best used fresh; refrigerate for up to 24 hours
