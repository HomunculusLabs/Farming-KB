## Creating a Rice Paddy

Modern machinery for rice paddy construction carries significant drawbacks: it produces uneven topsoil thickness, causes excessive settling that leads to groundwater stagnation, and concrete levees destroy the microbe community essential to healthy paddy ecology.

The traditional Japanese approach was wiser. Farmers chose mountain valleys over flat river banks because valleys suffered less flooding and wind damage. They built terraced fields on hillsides, spreading the field with ridge grasses, bordering weeds, and young foliage. Rice was grown in this enriched environment without any fertilizers.

> "A tiny field of maybe a hundred square yards supplied the food needs for one individual indefinitely."

> "The spiritual peace and security, the simple joy of creating a rice paddy were greater than can be imagined."

## Crop Rotation and Integration

On the complete natural farm, all elements work together:

> "On the complete natural farm, fruit trees, vegetables, grains, and other crops must all be planted and grown in an organic and mutually favorable arrangement."

Fruit trees, combined with a bordering wood and natural weed undergrowth, achieve normal healthy growth without human assistance. Vegetables are surprisingly hardy when given the right conditions. Cruciferous winter vegetables suppress weeds and send deep roots into the soil, while leguminous plants provide green manure.

Fukuoka envisions a three-dimensional orchard integrating fruit trees, vegetables, fowl, livestock, and human beings into a single organically connected microcosm — a system capable of self-sufficient living.

> "Nature is sufficient in and of itself; there never was a need for human effort and knowledge."
