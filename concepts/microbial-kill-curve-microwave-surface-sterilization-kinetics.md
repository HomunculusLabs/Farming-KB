
# Microbial Kill Curves in [[microwave-sterilization]] Mechanism

[[nasa-microwave-sterilization-challenge-organisms-kill-kinetics]] at 2.45 GHz inactivates microorganisms primarily
through thermal effects generated by dielectric heating of water molecules.
When a surface is damp or exposed to microwave-induced steam, absorbed
microwave energy rapidly raises local temperatures beyond the thermal
tolerance of resident microorganisms. Unlike dry-heat sterilization,
microwave energy penetrates into surface irregularities and biofilm
structures, achieving more uniform thermal delivery.

The presence of water is essential. Dry surfaces absorb minimal microwave
energy at 2.45 GHz, resulting in negligible microbial kill. Damp surfaces
or surfaces exposed to saturated steam couple efficiently with the
incident field, converting electromagnetic energy into heat within the
microbial [[staycare-degradation-of-plant-cell-wall-polymers-by-fungi]] and surrounding moisture layer.

## Factors Affecting Kill Efficiency

Four principal variables govern the steepness and position of microbial
kill curves under [[dry-microwave-irradiation-spore-resistance]]:

1. **Exposure duration** — Cumulative irradiation time determines total
   energy delivered to the microbial population.
2. **Intensity (power density)** — Expressed in W/cm², power density
   controls the rate of energy deposition.
3. **Water availability** — The amount of free water on or near the
   surface determines how effectively microwave energy is converted to
   lethal heat. Saturated steam environments outperform damp-surface
   conditions, which far exceed dry-surface performance.
4. **Organism type and initial population** — Different species exhibit
   distinct thermal tolerances. Spore-forming organisms such as *Bacillus*
   species are markedly more resistant than vegetative Gram-negative cells.

Mixed surface populations were prepared by inoculating test surfaces with
approximately equal proportions of the three challenge organisms to a
combined loading of 2 × 10⁵ colony-forming units (CFU). Surfaces were
maintained in a damp state throughout irradiation to ensure consistent
energy coupling.

## Differential Susceptibility of Challenge Organisms

The three test organisms displayed clearly stratified susceptibility:

- ***Escherichia coli:*** Most susceptible. As a vegetative Gram-negative
  rod lacking a protective spore coat, *E. coli* showed significant kill
  at intermediate exposure levels.

- ***Pseudomonas cepacia:*** Intermediate susceptibility. Although also
  a vegetative Gram-negative, *P. cepacia* exhibits marginally greater
  thermal tolerance, likely due to membrane lipid composition differences.

- ***Bacillus pumilus:*** Most resistant. A spore-forming Gram-positive
  bacterium whose endospores required the highest cumulative energy dose
  to achieve complete inactivation, defining the practical sterilization
  endpoint for the protocol.
