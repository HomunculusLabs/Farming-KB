# Soil Burrowing Organisms

## Overview

Before humans learned to plough, the earth was thoroughly dug, aerated, and overturned by multitudes of industrious burrowers. The unploughed meadows of Europe and America were as soft as mattresses, well-aerated by moles, gophers, worms, prairie dogs, rodents, and larvae working eternally below ground, even under snow. Understanding the role of these organisms is fundamental to [[permaculture]] soil management.

## The Major Burrowing Groups

### Termites and Ants

Termites and ants are the **[[earthworms]] of the deserts and drylands**. They perform critical ecosystem functions:

- Carry tons of organic material to **underground [[compost]] piles**
- Some species **grow fungi** within their colonies for food
- Their upthrown earth creates specific niches for annuals to seed on
- Create favourable sites for wind-blown pioneer trees

### Earthworms and Crustaceans

Worms and crustaceans in their damp burrows operate as **billion-fold pump plungers**:

- Move up and down, sucking in and expelling air (and thus nitrogen) to roots
- Give the soil its **daily breath** — analogous to alveoli in human lungs
- Create stabilised mudbrick soils with body secretions
- From swallows to termites, creatures create homes from soil they stabilise

### Mammalian Burrowers

**Gophers and moles** industriously carry out multiple roles:

- Transport roots and bulbs to secret stores, often forgetting hoards — spreading plants like comfrey, sunroot, gladioli, daffodils, and hyacinths to unexpected locations
- Create extensive tunnel systems with nests, mating chambers, air vents, nursing chambers, disposal chutes, and escape hatches
- Old burrows fill with plant remains and faecal wastes, enriching soil
- Their mounds provide dug-over areas where foxes defecate after eating fruits, creating perfect seedbeds for new trees

**Wombats** may tunnel, overturn, and topple many hectares of trees, leaving richly-manured, open, fertile beds for new forest evolution.

**Rabbits** garden thistles and create tunnels that shelter possums, squirrels, bandicoots, snakes, and frogs.

### Insect Larvae

Several insect groups spend most of their lives underground:

- **Swift moths (Hepialidae)** spend 7-8 years underground as large bardi grubs
- **Ghost moths and witchetty grubs** occupy root sheaths and soil for years
- They have only a few days of nocturnal, foodless aerial life for mating and egg-laying
- These larvae open **thousands of shafts to the air** and cycle tons of nutrient underground

## Burrowers as Ecosystem Engineers

### Seed and Spore Distribution

Burrowers perform essential dispersal functions:

- **Seeds and spores** are buried, excavated, hidden, activated, and forgotten
- They are recycled to life or humus as chance and nature dictate
- Underground **fungal spores** eaten by squirrels or wallabies are activated by digestive enzymes, breaking hibernation to occupy new ground

### Soil Structure Creation

- Burrows create channels for water infiltration and air exchange
- Body secretions stabilise soil particles into aggregates
- Mixing of organic material with mineral soil creates humus-rich zones
- Abandoned burrows become pathways for new roots and organisms

### Nutrient Cycling

- Organic material is carried underground for decomposition
- Faecal wastes enrich burrow walls and abandoned tunnels
- Dead organisms add nutrients at depth
- Predator-prey relationships create complex underground food webs

## Design Implications for Permaculture

### Working With Burrowers

For designers, the activities of burrowing organisms can be utilised directly:

- Plant **Acacia** for the by-product of swift moth or ghost moth larvae, just as one plants mulberry for silkworms
- Use **moles instead of mole ploughs** for soil aeration
- Rely on **gophers as unpaid daffodil gardeners** for bulb distribution
- Design water flow to **flood prairie dog burrows** as water sinks, germinating stored underground seed

### The Prairie Dog Warning

The Navajo warning — *"If you kill off the prairie dogs, there will be no one to cry for rain"* — was vindicated by events in Arizona. In the 1950s, scientists recommended extermination of all burrowing animals to protect desert grass roots. The result was a **virtual wasteland** of fierce run-off, soil compaction, and no fresh seedbed.

Conversely, the Page ranch used prairie dog burrows as water sinks, directing run-off to flood down them and germinate stored seed — creating a thriving **dryland pasture**.

### Aquatic Burrowers

Even on shores and lake bottoms, burrowers work to carry nutrients below to roots and bring up fresh minerals for decomposition, while assisting the flux of liquids and gases across mud surfaces.

## Soil Life and House Foundations

Burrowing organisms also interact with built structures. Understanding their role is critical:

- Soils cause **60-80% of all house cracks** and insurance claims
- About **20% of building soils** subside or heave depending on water content
- Black cracking clay, surface clays, and red-brown clay loams are most problematic
- **Over-irrigation** causing rising water tables is a primary cause of soil swelling
- **Tree removal** near buildings in clay soils worsens the problem
- Trees should be kept at least **half their mature height** from houses on high-clay soils
- Sands, sand-loams, rotten rock, and sandstones are generally stable

## The Underground Topography

Roots have their own characteristics that parallel aerial plant features:

- **Penetrations** (depth) — some trees reach 50 m in deep desert sands
- **Patterns** of horizontal spread
- **Seasonal schedules** and migrations to/from the surface
- **Underground storage organs** — ligno-tubers, tubers, bulbs, rhizomes
- **Root space sharing** is scheduled so spring bulbs complete their cycle before tree roots begin upward thrust

### Allelopathic Root Interactions

Some species actively inhibit competitors through root chemistry:

- **Phenol/creosote secreters**: Bracken, tamarisk, Juglandaceae, Brassicas
- **Physical encapsulators**: Eucalyptus, willows, tamarisks
- **Nematode trappers**: Marigolds, fungi, Crotalaria

## Key Principles

1. **Burrowers are the original soil engineers** — they predate human agriculture by millions of years
2. **Extermination of burrowers causes desertification** — the prairie dog lesson is definitive
3. **Design systems to work with** burrowing organisms rather than against them
4. **Root diversity underground** parallels canopy diversity aboveground — design for both
5. **[[soil-respiration]] depends on** burrows — they are the alveoli of the earth
