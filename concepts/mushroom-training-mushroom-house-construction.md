# Mushroom House Construction for Small Farms

The mushroom house is the central infrastructure of any mushroom farming operation. The FAO
training manual provides guidance on building mushroom houses at two scales: commercial
training centers and low-cost private farms for individual entrepreneurs with disabilities.

## Design Considerations

Mushroom houses must account for four critical [[mushroom-training-environmental-control]]: ventilation (air exchange for CO2 control), humidity (75 to 90
percent), temperature (species-appropriate ranges), and light (filtered for fruiting).
Buildings also need to be adapted for accessibility by people with disabilities.

## Sizing the Mushroom House

| Capacity | Approximate Size |
|----------|-----------------|
| 1,000 bags | 2m x 3m |
| 2,000 bags | 3m x 4m |
| 3,000 bags | 4m x 5m |
| 4,000 bags | 5m x 6m |

A 3m x 4m house for 2,000 bags can be built for as little as 500 Baht (about US$12),
depending on creativity and use of readily available materials. More durable construction
costs up to 2,000 Baht but lasts about 2 years.

## Low-Cost Construction Materials

The manual strongly encourages using local, inexpensive materials:
- Rice straw, grass, dried leaves
- Bamboo, tree branches
- Old rice and other grain bags
- Shade mesh

These materials can be used efficiently and are readily available in rural areas.

## Roof Construction Methods

- Using old rice bags and dried leaves layered on a branch or bamboo frame
- Grass thatch layered in two layers (Lamperate cylindrical style)
- Shade mesh for filtered light
- Combined materials for waterproofing and insulation

The roof should provide shade while allowing some filtered light to enter.

## Accessibility Features

### Doors and Entrances
- Doors wide enough for wheelchairs and tricycles
- Paved roads and access ramps to all buildings
- Water trench around house limits ants/bugs while allowing wheelchair access (clean
  twice monthly)

### Interior Layout
- Aisles at least 1.20 meters wide for wheelchair and trolley access
- Shelf heights must allow people in wheelchairs to reach the highest level
- Floors should be level and free of obstacles

### Floor Options
- Cement floor (optional, better hygiene, approximately 4,000 Baht)
- Compacted earth (free, usable for about one year)

## Shelving Systems

### Traditional Bamboo Shelves
Low-cost, locally constructed using bamboo poles. Suitable for small-scale operations.

### Suspended Systems
Bags hung from ceiling or frame, keeping them off the floor and at accessible heights for
wheelchair users.

### Steel Racks
More durable but expensive. Used in commercial enterprises. May need accessibility
modifications.

## Country-Style Pasteurization Setup

Farmers making their own [[mushroom-training-substrate-preparation]] need a
[[mushroom-training-pasteurization-sterilization]] using 200-liter oil
drums, iron or bamboo screens, and firewood or green fuel.

## Site Selection

- Land must be above flooding level
- Good ventilation available
- Access to water source for misting
- Shade from direct sun exposure
- Proximity to home for daily management
- Access to roads for transporting mushrooms to market

## House Ratios

For larger operations, one incubation house for every three fruiting body houses allows
rotation and continuous production. Small private farmers may start with a single house
serving both purposes, managed through timing.

## Maintenance

- Keep the mushroom house clean to reduce disease and pest pressure
- Monitor humidity and light constantly
- Repair damage to walls, roof, or shelving promptly
- Clean the water trench at least twice monthly
- Replace spent substrate bags and disinfect shelving between cycles
