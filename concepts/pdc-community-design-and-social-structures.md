# Permaculture Design Course -- Community Design and Social Structures

## Invisible Structures

The world is shaped by both visible and invisible structures. Invisible structures are forces that govern behavior -- they cannot be pointed at as discrete things, but they manifest through physical structures. Examples include seasons (natural) and the 8-hour work day (human-made). Every invisible structure has a concurrent physical structure, and every physical structure creates invisible structures (like ripples of cause and effect).

Invisible structures include:
- Systems of governance and rules
- Cultural traditions
- Economic patterns (see [[pdc-economics-and-livelihoods]])
- Legal structures (zoning ordinances, building codes)
- Social patterns and community norms

Understanding invisible structures is essential because placing a physical structure in a design creates invisible structures around it.

## Ecovillage Design Diagram

A pattern language for ecovillage design organized into four **Community Layers**:

### 1. Public Layer (outermost)
Areas open to the general public: tour routes, offices, parks. Rules and uses crafted to present the public face of the community.

### 2. Community Layer
Areas open to all community members but not the general public. Rules designed to make spaces open and comfortable for all members to use freely.

### 3. Intimate Space Layer
Spaces not freely open to the general community -- "invite only" areas for subcommunities within the larger community. Examples: porches, front yards. Important for sub-dividing out of the community and congregating with a more intimate circle.

### 4. Private Layer (innermost)
Personal private space: the home (Zone 0) surrounded by the individual's private yard (Zone 1). Should be surrounded by visual barriers for privacy.

### Friendly Barriers
Grey rings between each layer represent "friendly barriers" -- elements that deter wrong people from wandering between layers without being hostile. Examples:
- Rows of hedges and trees
- Cob benches with high walls (you can't see past but can sit)
- Hoop houses
- Multi-functional elements (a barrier is only one function -- ask what else it does)

### Pathways
- **Formal pathways** -- sidewalks with lights, clear signage
- **Informal pathways** -- dirt trails, minimal markers
- **Gates/thresholds** -- trellised arches, posts, natural transitions between layers (not necessarily literal gates unless containing animals or children)

## Food Systems in Community Design

Food systems arranged in wedges flanking pathways:
- **Production area** (Public/Community Layer) -- commercial food systems for CSA, community cafe, farmers market
- **Shared community** -- food systems shared by community members for community consumption
- **Personal systems** -- individual food gardens

Pathways should take one through food system spaces, integrating food into the aesthetic landscaping, with traffic control to prevent damage to groundcovers and beds.

## The Design Process for Community

### Permaculture Guilds
Join your local Permaculture guild and introduce community-organizing principles. Permaculture guilds are community groups that share knowledge, resources, and labor.

### Co-Creative Design
The main goal of a Permaculture Designer is not to design FOR a client but to **co-create** the design with them. This applies equally to community design -- the designer enables a co-creative process by establishing rapport and communicating synergistically.

### Charettes
Organize design charettes -- collaborative design sessions where community members participate in creating their own design. This builds ownership and ensures the design reflects the community's philosophy.

### Social Permaculture
While Permaculture's principles were developed for ecological design, they apply equally to social systems:
- **Observe and interact** -- understand community dynamics before intervening
- **Integrate rather than segregate** -- create spaces that bring people together
- **Use small and slow solutions** -- incremental community development
- **Use and value diversity** -- diverse community members strengthen the whole
- **Creatively use and respond to change** -- communities evolve over time

## Physical Structures in Community Design

- **Community house** -- central gathering space (the black box in the center of the design diagram)
- **Commercial interfaces** -- studios, galleries, storefronts at the community-public boundary
- **Food systems** -- integrated throughout, from production to personal gardens
- **Friendly barriers** -- multifunctional elements creating transitions between layers
