# Pollinator Habitat

Creating and managing habitat for native bees, butterflies, hummingbirds, and other pollinators. Based on Xerces Society guidelines and university extension research. Over 80% of flowering plants and one-third of human food crops depend on animal pollinators.

## Native Bees

[[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] has ~4000 native bee species. Most are solitary, non-aggressive, and far more efficient per-visit than honeybees for many crops.

| Species | Nesting | Forage | Life Cycle | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mason bees (Osmia) | Hollow stems, wood tunnels, bee hotels | Early spring bloomers (fruit trees, redbud) | 1 generation/year; overwinter as adult in cocoon | Superb fruit tree pollinators; active Mar-May |
| Leafcutter bees (Megachile) | Hollow stems, rotting wood | Summer bloomers (legumes, sunflowers) | 1-2 generations/year | Cut neat leaf circles to line nests |
| Bumblebees (Bombus) | Small cavities underground, tussock grass | Wide range; buzz-pollinate tomatoes/peppers | Annual colony; queen overwinters alone | Only native social bees; cold-tolerant |
| Sweat bees (Halictidae) | Bare/minimal soil, small ground nests | Wide range including small flowers | Mostly solitary; some social | Small, metallic green/blue; attracted to sweat |
| Carpenter bees (Xylocopa) | Soft/dead wood, bore tunnels | Large flowers, legumes | 1 generation/year | Can damage untreated wood; valuable pollinators |

## Honeybees

Managed pollinators with social colony structure. See [[beekeeping-basics]] for management details. Key integration points:

- Provide diverse, uninterrupted bloom from early spring through late fall
- Avoid insecticide application during bloom periods
- Place hives with morning sun, afternoon shade, [[cervantes-wind-protection-outdoor-cannabis]]
- Access to clean water within 0.25 miles
- Forage needs: 2-4 acres of diverse bloom per hive for surplus honey

## Butterflies

| Species | Host Plants | Nectar Plants |
|---|---|---|
| Monarch | Milkweed (Asclepias spp.) — essential | Goldenrod, asters, Joe-Pye weed, blazing star, ironweed |
| Black Swallowtail | Dill, fennel, parsley, rue | Butterfly bush, zinnias, phlox, lantana |
| Eastern Tiger Swallowtail | Tulip poplar, cherry, ash, birch | Joe-Pye weed, butterfly bush, coneflowers |
| Spicebush Swallowtail | Spicebush, sassafras | Joe-Pye weed, bee balm, milkweed |
| Pearl Crescent | Asters | Asters, dandelion, fleabane |

Plant both host plants (for caterpillars) and nectar plants (for adults). Avoid pesticides entirely in butterfly zones.

## Hummingbirds

- **Zones 7-8 species**: Ruby-throated Hummingbird (east), Rufous/Anna's (west)
- **Feeders**: 4:1 sugar water (1/4 cup sugar per 1 cup water). No honey, no red dye. Clean weekly.
- **Plants**: cardinal flower, bee balm, columbine, trumpet vine, salvia, penstemon, native honeysuckle (Lonicera sempervirens), coral bells, phlox
- **Habitat**: shrubs/trees for perching, water source, spider webs for nesting material
- **Arrival**: Ruby-throated arrives zone 7 in April; depart by October

## Creating Nesting Habitat

- **Bare ground**: leave patches of exposed, well-drained soil for ground-nesting bees (sweat bees, mining bees). 1-2 sq ft patches in sunny spots.
- **Bee hotels**: drilled wood blocks or bamboo bundles. Hole diameters 1/8-5/16" for mason/leafcutter bees. Place south-facing, 3-5 ft high. Replace tubes/blocks annually to prevent disease buildup.
- **Hollow stems**: leave 6-12" of dead perennial stems standing through winter (rudbeckia, elderberry, raspberry, sumac)
- **Brush piles**: small piles of twigs/branches provide nesting cavities
- **Dead wood**: retain standing dead trees/snags where safe; fallen logs provide nesting tunnels
- **Avoid**: heavy mulch over bare ground areas (blocks ground nesters)

## Planting for Pollinators (Zones 7-8 Bloom Calendar)

| Month | Blooming Plants | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mar-Apr | Crocus, dandelion, redbud, serviceberry, pussy willow, plum/cherry | Critical early food for emerging bees |
| May-Jun | Clover, chives, catmint, yarrow, baptisia, penstemon, blueberry, blackberry | Peak bumblebee and mason bee activity |
| Jul-Aug | Coneflower, bee balm, sunflower, Joe-Pye weed, milkweed, anise hyssop, lavender | Peak butterfly season; sweat bees active |
| Sep-Oct | Asters, goldenrod, ironweed, sedum, Joe-Pye weed, witch hazel | Late forage critical for overwintering bees |
| Nov-Dec | Witch hazel, winter honeysuckle | Sparse but important for late bumblebees |

**Native plant priorities** (Zone 7-8): New England aster, purple coneflower, wild bergamot, butterfly milkweed, black-eyed Susan, Joe-Pye weed, blue lobelia, boneset, golden alexanders, wild geranium, pussy willow, serviceberry, American plum, buttonbush, spicebush.

**Flower types by pollinator**:
- **Tubular/deep**: hummingbirds, long-tongued bees (cardinal flower, bee balm, penstemon)
- **Flat/open**: butterflies, short-tongued bees (asters, yarrow, daisies, umbels)
- **Composite**: diverse bee species (sunflower, coneflower, goldenrod, coreopsis)
- **Landing platform**: butterflies prefer clusters of flat flowers

## Pesticide Impacts and Alternatives

- **Neonicotinoids**: systemic — persist in pollen/nectar for months. Lethal and sublethal effects on all bees. Avoid entirely.
- **Broad-spectrum sprays** (pyrethroids, organophosphates): kill pollinators directly. Never spray during bloom.
- **Fungicides**: generally lower toxicity but some (captan, chlorothalonil) harm bee larvae in combination with other chemicals.
- **Timing**: if spraying is necessary, apply after dusk when bees are inactive. Use products with short residual toxicity.
- **Alternatives**: see [[insect-allies]] for biological controls (Bt, spinosad, neem — use with caution); [[cannabis-pest-management]] for IPM framework; hand-picking, row covers, [[comparison-companion-planting-reference-vs-jadam-monoculture-critique]].

## Seasonal Needs

- **Early spring**: bare ground for emerging ground nesters; early-blooming bulbs and trees
- **Summer**: continuous bloom (no gaps >2 weeks); water source; mud for mason bees
- **Fall**: late bloomers; reduce mowing; leave leaf litter for overwintering insects
- **Winter**: standing dead stems, brush piles, fallen leaves — do not clean up completely. Many pollinators overwinter in plant debris.

## Pollinator Corridors and Hedgerows

- Connect habitat patches with linear plantings (hedgerows, fence lines, riparian buffers)
- Minimum corridor width: 10-20 ft for effective bee movement
- Hedgerow species (Zone 7-8): serviceberry, elderberry, red cedar, dogwood, viburnum, blackberry, pussy willow, chokecherry
- Mow hedgerows only once per year in late winter (before emergence)
- Integrate with [[food-forest-layers-and-species]] understory plantings

## Integrating with Garden and Orchard

- **[[fukuoka-dutch-priest-vegetable-garden-spiritual-renewal]]**: interplant flowers (calendula, borage, alyssum) between rows. Leave some crops to bolt (arugula, radish, cilantro) as bee forage.
- **Orchard**: underplant with clover, dandelion, wildflowers as living mulch. See [[orchard-management]] for [[fukuoka-orchard-floor-management]] management. Avoid tillage that destroys ground nests.
- **Food forest**: diverse flowering layers provide continuous forage. See [[food-forest-layers-and-species]] for species lists.
- **Companion planting**: specific pollinator-attracting companions per crop. See [[companion-planting-reference]].
