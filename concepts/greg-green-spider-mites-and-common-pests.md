Pest management is one of the most critical aspects of successful cannabis
cultivation. [[greg-green]]'s Cannabis Grow Bible identifies spider mites as the
number one threat to cannabis plants, capable of reducing an entire crop to
garbage within a matter of days. The book also covers several other common
pests including whitefly, slugs, snails, fungus gnats, and caterpillars.
See also [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-pest-disease-prevention]] and
[[green-cannabis-pests-prevention]].

Green's approach to pest management emphasizes prevention over treatment.
He includes spider mite spray as a mandatory item on every grower's
equipment checklist, alongside basic supplies like soil, pots, and
nutrients. This proactive mindset reflects the reality that once a pest
infestation takes hold, treatment becomes far more difficult and less
effective than prevention would have been.

## Spider Mites

Spider mites are described by Green as the most dangerous pest a cannabis
grower will encounter. These tiny arachnids, about half the size of a
period on a printed page, can devastate plants in just a couple of days.
Marijuana plants are very vulnerable to mite attacks because spider mites
love to suck marijuana plants dry.

Green's most emphatic warning is to never bring a plant indoors that has
been outside. Growers who have done this have managed to kill nearly every
plant in their house because they brought a plant indoors that had spider
mites. The mites spread rapidly from the introduced plant to all others in
the grow space. This single mistake is responsible for countless destroyed
gardens.

Always have a bottle of pesticide that kills spider mites on hand. Spider
mite spray should be part of every grower's essential equipment list.
Prevention is far more effective than treatment, as by the time mite
damage is visible, the infestation is usually severe. Regular inspection
of leaves, especially the undersides, with a magnifying glass is
recommended.

Spider mites can also go undetected in their incubation nests inside
buds and leaf crevices. This makes thorough inspection important even on
plants that appear clean on the surface. A 30x magnifier or jeweler's
loupe is invaluable for early detection of these nearly invisible pests.

## Whitefly

Whitefly is described as deadly and capable of reducing plants to trash
in days. These small white flying insects congregate on the undersides of
leaves and suck plant sap, weakening the plant and potentially
transmitting diseases. They are particularly problematic in indoor grows
where they can reproduce rapidly in the warm conditions.

Safer's soap is recommended as a treatment for whitefly infestations.
This product can be purchased from most grow shops and works by
suffocating the insects. Yellow sticky traps placed near the plants can
help monitor and reduce whitefly populations. Early detection is key to
preventing a whitefly problem from becoming uncontrollable.

## Slugs and Snails

Slugs and snails are primarily outdoor pests that can cause significant
damage to cannabis plants, especially young seedlings. They feed on
leaves and stems, leaving large holes and ragged edges. Green recommends
making a circle of table salt about 4 feet away from the base of the
plant, and then making another circle a foot in from that. Salt is deadly
to snails and slugs and will keep them out of the growing area.

Other methods for slug and snail control include copper tape barriers,
beer traps, and hand picking during nighttime inspections. These pests
are most active at night and after rain, making those the best times to
check for and remove them.

## Caterpillars and Cutworms

Caterpillars and cutworms can be devastating to cannabis plants,
especially during the vegetative stage. Cutworms in particular are known
for severing young plants at the soil line, effectively killing them
overnight. The only sure way to get rid of cutworms is to use a cutworm
repellent product specifically designed for them.

Growers should try to find a product that works on cutworms and cutworms
only, to avoid harming beneficial insects. All of these cutworm products
work very well and can be bought in most grow shops. Physical barriers
such as collars around the base of seedlings can also prevent cutworm
damage.

## Fungus Gnats, Ants, and Termites

Fungus gnats are small flying insects whose larvae feed on organic matter
in the soil and can damage root systems. They are often found in
overwatered soil or soil with excessive organic content. Reducing
watering frequency and allowing the soil surface to dry between
waterings helps control gnat populations.

Termites do not like water. If you over water the soil around your
plant they will leave. While termites are not a common cannabis pest,
they can be problematic for outdoor grows in certain regions.

Ants themselves do not typically damage cannabis plants directly, but they
can farm other pest insects like aphids and may damage root systems by
creating tunnels in the soil. Controlling ant populations helps manage
the overall pest ecosystem around the grow.

## General Pest Prevention

Green's overarching advice on pest management is prevention over cure.
Keeping a clean grow space, never introducing outdoor plants to an indoor
garden, maintaining proper air circulation, and having appropriate pest
control products on hand before problems arise are the foundations of
good [[integrated-pest-management]]. Regular inspection of plants, especially
the undersides of leaves, should be part of every grower's routine.

## Summary

Spider mites represent the single greatest pest threat to cannabis
plants, but whitefly, slugs, snails, caterpillars, and various other
insects can also cause serious damage. Prevention through clean growing
practices and vigilant monitoring is far more effective than trying to
eradicate an established infestation. Every grower should keep appropriate
pest control products readily available.
