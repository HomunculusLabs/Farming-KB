# Essential Plant Micronutrients

While required only in trace amounts, micronutrients are every bit as essential as macronutrients. According to **Von Liebig's [[law-of-the-minimum]]**, if even the least-used nutrient is absent, no amount of the others will matter. Plants cannot grow and reproduce without each of these eight micronutrients: boron, chlorine, copper, iron, manganese, zinc, molybdenum, and nickel.

## Boron (B)

### Functions
- **Cell wall integrity**: Connects [[carbohydrate]] chains within cell walls, providing [[mycelium-running-rhizomorphic-mycelium-structural-strength-erosion-control]]
- **Pollen tube formation**: Necessary for proper pollen development — without boron, pollen doesn't form correctly
- **Sugar-starch balance**: Involved in regulating the balance and translocation of sugars and starches throughout the plant
- **Stomatal regulation**: Helps transport potassium ions across cell membranes, influencing stomatal opening and closing

### Uptake and Mobility
- Usually taken up as **boric acid** (B[OH]₃) — notably, the **only mineral nutrient that does not need to be an ion** to enter plant cells
- Uses three transport mechanisms: diffusion, facilitative transport, and active transport
- **Not mobile** once incorporated into the plant, because it readily bonds to sugar molecules

### Deficiency Symptoms
- Damage at **growing tips**
- Problems with **flower and fruit formation**

### Sources
Borax (Na₂B₄O₇) is the primary ore of boron.

## Chlorine (Cl)

### Functions
- **Stomatal operation**: Chloride (Cl⁻) serves as the counterbalance to potassium ions (K⁺) in guard cells, enabling stomatal function
- **Electrical balance**: Maintains ionic balance throughout the plant
- **Photosynthesis**: Chlorine atoms supply the electrons needed to break water molecule bonds during the light-dependent reactions

### Mobility and Deficiency
- **Not mobile** in plants — symptoms appear in **newer leaves**
- Deficiency causes **wilting and bronzing** of plant tips, often with mottled chlorotic spots
- Excess chlorine causes **yellowing of leaf margins** in older leaves

### Sources
Chlorine is widely available and deficiencies are rare in most environments.

## Copper (Cu)

### Functions
- **Oxidation reactions**: Key component of enzymes used in oxidation processes
- **Photosynthesis and respiration**: Present in enzymes critical to both processes
- **Protein synthesis**: Involved in enzymes that assemble amino acids into proteins
- **[[lignin]] synthesis**: Plays a key role in building lignin, the structural polymer that reinforces cell walls

### Mobility and Deficiency
- **Immobile** once assimilated
- Deficiency causes **chlorosis** (due to reduced photosynthesis), **leaf curling** in some species, and **excessive branching** in others

### Soil Behavior
- Relatively immobile in soil — tends to bind to soil particles through chemical reactions

## Iron (Fe)

### Functions
- **Electron transport**: Iron atoms serve as electron carriers, with electrons easily passed between them. Essential for respiration and oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions
- **Chlorophyll function**: Required for chlorophyll synthesis and proper function, though not a structural part of the chlorophyll molecule itself
- **Nitrogen fixation**: Critical to the process — conversion of atmospheric nitrogen and nitrate depends on iron-containing enzymes
- **Enzyme component**: Part of numerous important enzymes and proteins

### Plant Iron Acquisition Strategy
Plants have developed a sophisticated system to secure iron: they release ions into the soil to **lower pH** around the root zone, preventing iron from becoming chemically unavailable (iron becomes insoluble at higher pH).

### Mobility and Deficiency
- **Not mobile** in plants
- **Chlorosis** (yellowing) from chlorophyll loss — young leaves may become **bleached** while older leaves and veins remain green
- Eventually, older leaves lose color especially at **margins**

### Sources
Common in most soils from weathered minerals.

## Manganese (Mn)

### Functions
- **Oxygen release during photosynthesis**: Accepts electrons from water molecules during the light-dependent reactions, facilitating oxygen release
- **Carbohydrate metabolism**: Works with specific enzymes to break apart carbohydrates

### Mobility and Deficiency
- **Not mobile** in plants
- Deficiency causes **interveinal chlorosis** — yellow leaves with green veins (visually similar to iron deficiency)

### Soil Behavior
Relatively immobile in soil, though can become more available when chelated.

## Zinc (Zn)

### Functions
- **Enzyme component**: Constituent of numerous enzyme systems
- **Redox reactions**: Involved in donating or accepting electrons (oxidation and reduction)
- **Auxin production**: Helps produce **auxins**, the primary [[gibberellins-plant-growth-hormones]]. This makes zinc directly tied to growth regulation
- **Chlorophyll and carbohydrate synthesis**: Needed to synthesize both
- **[[nucleic-acid]] synthesis**: Activates enzymes for making RNA and DNA
- **Cold tolerance**: Helps plant cells withstand cold temperatures

### Mobility and Deficiency
- **Mobile** in plants — symptoms appear in **older leaves**
- Deficiency causes **stunted growth**, lack of **stem elongation**, and **yellowing**
- The connection to auxin production explains why zinc deficiency so directly impacts growth

### Soil Behavior
Relatively immobile in soil; availability depends on pH and organic matter.

## Molybdenum (Mo)

### Functions
- **Organic phosphorus synthesis**: Necessary for converting inorganic phosphorus into organic phosphorus compounds once inside the plant
- **Nitrogen fixation**: Required by **Rhizobia** and **Frankia** bacteria to fix atmospheric nitrogen in legume root nodules

### Mobility and Deficiency
- **Mobile** in plants — deficiency symptoms appear in **older leaves** (chlorosis)
- A distinctive symptom is **nitrate buildup** in leaves, causing some leaves to curl — a condition called **whiptail**

### Soil Behavior
Molybdenum availability increases with higher [[soil-ph]], unlike most other micronutrients.

## Nickel (Ni)

### Functions
- **Urea metabolism**: When nickel is absent, nitrogen in the form of urea accumulates in leaves, causing **leaf tip burns**. Nickel is needed for the enzyme **urease**, which breaks down urea

### Mobility and Deficiency
- **Immobile** in plants
- Still not universally recognized as essential because its functions are relatively obscure
- Present in several types of rock

## Nutrient Mobility Summary

### Mobile in Plants (deficiency in older leaves)
Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, Magnesium, Molybdenum, Nickel, Chlorine

### Somewhat Immobile in Plants (deficiency in new growth)
Zinc (middle-aged leaves), Iron, Manganese, Copper, Sulfur

### Immobile in Plants (deficiency in youngest parts)
Calcium, Boron
