# Fungal Nanoparticles for Environmental Remediation

Fungal-mediated synthesis of nanoparticles represents a convergence of
mycology and nanotechnology, offering an eco-friendly ("green") route to
produce nanomaterials with significant potential for environmental
cleanup. Unlike conventional chemical and physical methods requiring
toxic reagents and high temperatures, fungal biogenesis operates under
ambient conditions and produces nanoparticles with unique surface
properties.

## Mechanisms of Fungal Nanoparticle Biosynthesis

Fungi produce nanoparticles through intracellular and extracellular
mechanisms. In intracellular synthesis, fungal cells take up metal ions
and reduce them within the cytoplasm or periplasmic spaces. Extracellular
synthesis occurs when fungal secretions, including reductase enzymes and
organic acids, reduce metal ions deposited on the cell surface.

Key fungal biomolecules involved in nanoparticle formation include:

- **Nitrate reductase enzymes**: Catalyze metal ion reduction by
  transferring electrons from NADPH, widely implicated in silver and
  gold nanoparticle biosynthesis
- **Proteins and peptides**: Cysteine and methionine residues bind metal
  ions and facilitate nucleation and growth control
- **Exopolysaccharides**: Act as capping and stabilizing agents,
  preventing nanoparticle aggregation
- **Organic acids**: Citric, oxalic, and other acids chelate metal ions
  and contribute to size-controlled synthesis

## Types of Fungal-Synthesized Nanoparticles

### Silver Nanoparticles (AgNPs)

Silver nanoparticles produced by *Fusarium oxysporum*, *Aspergillus
niger*, and *Trichoderma harzianum* exhibit strong antimicrobial and
catalytic properties. In remediation, AgNPs catalyze degradation of
organic dyes and serve as sensing platforms for heavy metal detection.
