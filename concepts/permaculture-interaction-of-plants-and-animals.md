# Permaculture Interaction of Plants and Animals

## Beyond Monocultural Thinking

Mollison observes that most gardening books treat each species as though it lived alone. Notable exceptions exist in the literature on companion planting and in isolated papers on plant antagonists, but the dominant approach remains species-by-species.

In permaculture, any system that pretends to be designed must account for the synergistic nature of plants and animals — placing them in the correct array to obtain a third benefit or interaction beyond what either provides alone.

## Plant-Plant Interactions

Plants interact with one another in numerous ways:

- **Trellis support**: Grape vines climb on mulberry, fig, and boxthorn

- **Screening and shade**: Coffee grows beneath palms

- **Nutrient provision**: Comfrey leaves fertilise potato sets; deep-rooted perennials probe below leached upper soil levels

- **Cross-fertilisation**: Different varieties of plums and nuts interbreed

- **Obligate relationships**: Fungi live in essential association with oaks and pines

- **Rejection or acceptance**: Some plants are incompatible neighbours (see [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]])

- **Grafting compatibility**: Apple, pear, and nut species provide spare parts for each other

## Atsatt and O'Dowd's Plant Defense Guilds

Peter Atsatt and Dennis O'Dowd (Science, 193, 24-29, July 1976) categorised plants acting to protect other plants into three defense guild categories:

### 1. Insectary Plants

Some plants breed the predators that attack pests on other plants. They act as breeding grounds for beneficial insects.

- Phacelia planted in orchards reduces the incidence of the Prospatella pest

- Sorghum or lucerne interplanted with cotton

- Umbelliferae (dill, fennel) with brassicas (cabbage, cauliflower)

- Strawberries in or near peach orchards

- Wild blackberry near grapes

These operate on single pest species but also attract generalist predators that range over a garden eating many insects.

Conversely, some plants carry over diseases of others. Berberis, for example, carries rust on grain and acts as a reservoir of re-infection. Such plants are either contra-indicated or used deliberately to suppress pest plants.

### 2. Repellent Plants

Plants repel browsers or pests by physical means (spines) or chemical means (phenols):

- Ranunculus protects grasses from cattle — the lactone ranunculin irritates herbivore mucous tissue

- Trifolium fragiferum protects white clover from hares

- Aboriginal gardeners use Haplopappus tenuirostris to deter or kill feral donkeys and horses

- Feral goats are killed by browsing on rhododendron and azalea shoots

Olfactory interference may explain why brassicas grown with tomatoes suffer less pest attack. An alternative explanation from New Scientist is that plants scattered among others appear scattered to pests, but once pests breed on them, the scattered plants — surrounded by insectary species — appear to predators as concentrated food sources.

At any rate, the dispersal of plants among other and varied species leads to far less pest infestation in all cases studied.

Shade cast by plants may also protect insects or other plants from predator attack. Atsatt and O'Dowd note that shade protects Klamath weed from leaf-eating beetles, and shade cast on ponds by fringing trees protects mosquito larvae from notonectids (backswimmers).

### 3. Attractant or Decoy Plants

Decoy plants draw off pests from crop plants and may even reduce the pest population:

- Nepenthes (catmint) has a fatal attraction to cats

- Solanum (S. nigrum, etc.) grown with potatoes attracts egg masses of the Colorado beetle as a lethal decoy

- Wallabies in Tasmania are attracted to Pittosporum bicolor ("wallaby wood")

- Meat flies are attracted to the malodiferous Arum dracunculus ("dead donkey" lily)

- Gladiolus traps and clears up onion rot by preventing fungus sporulation

Alternate or interplanting of non-resistant and resistant plants prevents pest build-up, rather than relying on the endless chase for more resistant varieties. Mollison has grown spring and summer broad beans specifically to attract aphids and hence their predators.

## Root and Gaseous Interactions

Plants also interact through root associations and gaseous exchanges:

- Apples and dandelions release ethylene gas that causes pineapples and bananas to ripen

- Quandong can germinate and establish only in root association with another plant

- Marigolds prevent nematode attack on citrus roots

- Deep shade under Coprosma prevents weed establishment

- Phenols produced by bracken, walnuts, and pine trees act as growth inhibitors

- Grasses secrete substances which kill young trees

## Manurial and Elemental Factors

In remote gardens where commercial fertilisers are unavailable, certain species provide essential elements:

- [[comfrey-multifunctional-guild-plant]] and Coprosma provide potash for potatoes as mulch or trenched leaves

- Tree lucerne and Leucaena provide essential nitrogen as mulch

- Smaller legumes provide nitrogen through root nodulation (see [[legume-nitrogen-dynamics]])

- Vigorous deep-rooted weeds and trees probe below leached upper soil levels to cycle rare elements

## Newman Turner's Medicinal Pasture Approach

Newman Turner exemplifies the observant grazier. By deliberately including medicinal and vermifuge plants in pasture, he kept his stock healthy despite themselves. There are now excellent books on herbal medicines to guide the designer in this approach.

## Design Implications

The conclusion from Atsatt and O'Dowd's research is that "a little powerful diversity" of the right kind is the key component of stability. Permaculturists can design and strengthen the plant defense guilds that naturally exist, using the mechanisms described above.

The permaculturist can look on garden design as an intellectual exercise with many more moves and infinitely more pieces than any person or computer can deal with. Good farmers are always good observers, and can strengthen the chance alliances they find at work while allowing weaker associations to wither if they will.

Physical factors such as shelter, shade, reflection, and water usage interact plant to plant, with some plants being essential to others as windbreak, shade, host, or nurse species. Many rampant plants nurse others to maturity, a principle central to [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-guild-design-in-permaculture]] in permaculture systems.
