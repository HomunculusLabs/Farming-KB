# Greenhouse Design for Homestead Scale

A greenhouse extends the growing season, protects crops from weather, and enables year-round production. For homestead scale (500-2000 sq ft), design choices balance cost, durability, and passive climate management.

## Types by Scale and Complexity

**Cold Frame** — Simple bottomless box with angled glazing. 2-8 sq ft. Extends season 4-6 weeks in spring/fall. Best for hardening off seedlings, growing cold-hardy greens. DIY cost: $50-150.

**Low Tunnel** — Metal or PVC hoops covered with row cover or poly film. 3-4 ft tall, any length. Portable, excellent for [[cover-cropping-guide]] beds and season extension. Cost: $0.50-1.00/linear ft.

**High Tunnel / Hoop House** — Quonset or gothic arch, 12-30 ft wide, any length. No permanent heating. Roll-up sides for ventilation. The core homestead workhorse — Eliot Coleman"s "unheated greenhouse" model. Cost: $2-6/sq ft (DIY) to $8-15/sq ft (kit).

**Attached Lean-To** — Shares a wall with an existing structure. Good passive solar gain from the house wall as thermal mass. Limited by available wall length. Structural attachment must handle wind/snow loads.

**Freestanding Greenhouse** — Full独立 structure with foundation. Highest control over environment. Suitable for [[composting-systems]] integration, [[seed-saving-techniques]], and year-round growing. Cost: $10-30/sq ft.

## Passive Solar Design

**Orientation** — Long axis runs east-west. This maximizes southern exposure for winter light gain and minimizes summer overhead sun. Ridge perpendicular to prevailing wind for cross-ventilation.

**Glazing** — Double-wall polycarbonate (8mm twin-wall) is the homestead sweet spot: R-value ~1.5, 90% light transmission, 10+ year lifespan, shatterproof. Single-layer poly film is cheapest ($0.15/sq ft) but lasts only 1-4 seasons and has R ~0.8. Glass is beautiful but expensive, fragile, and heavy.

**Thermal Mass** — Water barrels (55 gal, painted black) along the north wall absorb daytime heat and release it at night. Stone, concrete, or earth floors also store heat. Rule of thumb: 2-3 gallons water per sq ft of glazing.

**Insulation** — North wall should be opaque and insulated (R-10+). Use bubble wrap on interior glazing in winter. Foundation insulation (2" XPS) prevents frost heave and ground heat loss.

## Ventilation

Ventilation is the #1 priority — more plants die from heat and humidity than cold.

- **Roll-up sides** — Manual or motorized. Essential on any tunnel over 50 ft long. Opens 3-4 ft on each side.
- **Ridge vents** — Hot air rises; a continuous ridge vent 2-3 ft wide provides 50%+ of ventilation needs.
- **Exhaust fans + intake shutters** — Thermostat-controlled. Calculate: 1 CFM per sq ft of floor area minimum, 2 CFM for summer crops.
- **End walls** — Screened doors or roll-up panels for cross-flow.

## Heating Options

**Compost Heating** — Jean Pain method: compost mound with embedded water coils circulates hot water. Maintains 60-70°F for months. Requires large biomass input (chipped brush, manure). See [[composting-systems]].

**Geothermal / Ground Source** — Air tubes buried 4-6 ft deep circulate 50-55°F earth-tempered air. Passive, no fuel, but installation is labor-intensive.

**Rocket Mass Heater** — Ultra-efficient wood combustion with thermal mass bench. Burns 1/4 the wood of a conventional stove. Outputs radiant heat for 12+ hours per firing. Requires skilled construction.

**Electric** — Simple but expensive to run. Propane or electric unit heaters with thermostats. Reserve for deep freezes in otherwise passive structures. Budget $0.50-1.50/sq ft/month in winter.

## Cooling

- **Shade cloth** — 30-50% woven polypropylene on exterior. Remove for winter. Essential above 85°F.
- **Evaporative cooling** — Fan-and-pad systems (wet wall on one end, exhaust on other). Effective in dry climates; less so in humid zones.
- **Natural ventilation** — Sufficient roll-up sides + ridge vents handle cooling to ~85°F in most climates without power.

## Year-Round Growing Schedule (Zone 7-8)

See [[seasonal-planning]] for full monthly calendar. Greenhouse-specific schedule:

**Winter (Dec-Feb)** — Cold-hardy greens (spinach, kale, mizuna, claytonia, mâche) under row cover inside tunnel. Carrots, leeks, parsnips overwinter. Min soil temp 40°F; most greens survive air to 20°F inside an unheated tunnel.

**Spring (Mar-May)** — Seed starting (onions, peppers, tomatoes in Feb-Mar). Transplant hardy crops in March. Direct sow radishes, peas, beets.

**Summer (Jun-Aug)** — Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, basil in ground. Remove shade cloth for full sun crops; add for greens. Maximize ventilation.

**Fall (Sep-Nov)** — Second planting of greens, carrots, brassicas. Sow cover crops in beds being rotated out. See [[cover-cropping-guide]].

## Integrated Systems

**Rainwater Catchment** — Gutters on greenhouse feed cisterns. 1" rain on 500 sq ft roof = 310 gallons. Use for irrigation, [[water-management]].

**Worm Bins** — [[vermicomposting]] bins under benches process greenhouse waste. Worm castings go directly into beds. Optimal temp 55-77°F — greenhouse provides winter warmth.

**Seed Starting Station** — Heat mats + grow lights on shelving. 4-tier shelf (2x4 ft footprint) holds 16 standard flats. Start seeds 6-8 weeks before transplant date.

## Materials and Costs

**Frame: PVC vs Metal**
- PVC ($1-3/ft): Cheap, easy, UV-degrades in 3-5 years, poor snow load
- Galvanized steel ($4-8/ft): Durable, 20+ year lifespan, handles snow, requires tools

**Covering: Poly Film vs Polycarbonate**
- 6 mil poly film ($0.15/sq ft): 1-4 year life, single use, good light transmission
- Twin-wall polycarbonate ($2-4/sq ft): 10-15 year life, insulated, diffused light

**Homestead Scale Budget (20x48 ft high tunnel)**:
- DIY with metal frame + poly film: $2,000-4,000
- Kit with polycarbonate: $8,000-15,000
- Foundation, end walls, ventilation: add $1,000-3,000

## Sizing for Homestead

For a family of 2-4 with some surplus:
- **Minimum**: 12x20 ft (240 sq ft) — seed starting + winter greens
- **Recommended**: 20x48 ft (960 sq ft) — serious year-round production
- **Optimal**: 30x72 ft (2,160 sq ft) — includes space for [[composting-systems]], worm bins, seed starting area

Orient long axis east-west. Allow 50 ft minimum from buildings and trees. Plan for expansion — most homesteaders wish they'd built bigger.
