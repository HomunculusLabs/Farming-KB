# Mycetozoan Survey Methods and Substratum Plating

Updated: 2026-04-18

Mycetozoans ([[dighton-fungal-decomposition-leaf-litter-dynamics]] from forest floors
- Dung of herbivorous mammals
- Soil humus layers
- Living plant surfaces (bark, leaves, flowers)
- Air-dried plant debris

Samples should be collected in paper bags (not plastic, to prevent condensation and mold growth). Record collection data: date, location, habitat type, substratum description, and associated plant species.

### Moist Chamber Incubation

1. Place substratum samples in Petri dishes or other moist chambers
2. Add sterile distilled water or weak nutrient agar to maintain humidity
3. Line chambers with filter paper if needed to maintain moisture
4. Seal with Parafilm or place in plastic bags with ventilation
5. Incubate at room temperature (20-25C) or at temperature approximating collection site
6. Incubate in darkness or low light to simulate natural conditions

### Observation Protocol

Fruiting structures are typically small (0.1-5 mm) and may appear within 1-7 days. Critical observation requirements:
- Examine daily or every 2-3 days using a dissecting microscope (20-60x)
- Many species fruit nocturnally; check early morning for best results
- Fruiting is ephemeral -- structures may degrade within 24-48 hours
- Some protostelids produce fruiting structures only 0.05-0.5 mm tall, requiring careful scanning

### Clone Identification

When fruiting bodies are found, isolation to clonal culture is important for documentation:
- Transfer individual sporangia or fruiting structures to fresh culture plates
- Use fine needles under dissecting microscope for transfer
- Maintain on weak nutrient agar (0.1-0.5% oatmeal agar or water agar with bacteria)
- Protostelids often require their bacterial food source; do not use antibacterial agents
- Photographic documentation of developmental stages is essential for identification

## Direct Field Observation

Some larger myxomycetes (particularly those in the Physarales, Trichiales, and Stemonitales) can be found by direct field search:
- Search decaying logs, stumps, and coarse [[aminopropyl-silica-gel-chromatography-indole-alkaloid-purification]] or by lyophilization.

## Challenges

- Many species are rare and may not fruit in every collection period
- Taxonomic expertise is limited; few active systematists for these groups
- [[biodiversity-fungal-molecular-identification-dna-barcoding]] methods are still being developed
