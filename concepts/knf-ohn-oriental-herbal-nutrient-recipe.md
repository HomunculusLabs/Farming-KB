# KNF Oriental Herbal Nutrient Recipe

Oriental Herbal Nutrient (OHN) is a core Korean [[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]] (KNF) input
made from a specific combination of [[permaculture-plants-medicinal-herbs-healing]]. Developed by Master
Han-Kyu Cho, OHN functions to increase plant robustness, sterilize against
pathogens, and keep plants warm during cold periods. OHN revitalizes crops and
activates their growth, making it one of the most versatile inputs in the KNF
system. It is used as a nutrient supplement, disease preventative, and growth
stimulant across all stages of plant development and in livestock care.

## Ingredients

The five ingredients for OHN are divided into dry-state and raw-state herbs:

### Dry-State Herbs
- Gigantic angelica root (Angelica gigans)
- Cinnamon bark
- Licorice root ([[glycyrrhiza-glabra]])

### Additional Materials
- Makgeolli (Korean rice wine) for initial extraction
- Brown sugar for fermentation
- Soju (Korean distilled liquor, 30 to 35% alcohol) for preservation
- 20-liter jars (one for each herb)
- Porous paper (Korean paper or paper towel) and rubber bands
- Vinyl film for sealing during alcohol preservation
- Wooden sticks for stirring

## Preparation Conditions

- Optimum season: spring or fall
- Preparation temperature: 23 to 25 degrees Celsius
- Storage temperature: 1 to 15 degrees Celsius

## Dry-State Herb Preparation

Each dry herb (angelica, cinnamon, licorice) is prepared separately in its
own jar. The process for each is identical. Cinnamon OHN is described here as
the example:

### Step 1: Initial Soaking

Put 2 kg of cinnamon bark into a 20-liter jar with 6 liters of rice wine
(Makgeolli). Fill the jar with raw rice wine 2/3 full. The amount of rice
wine should fully wet the cinnamon bark but not submerge it excessively.
Cover the jar with porous paper and tie with a rubber band. Let the cinnamon
bark absorb moisture for 1 to 2 days.

### Step 2: Sugar Fermentation

Add brown sugar equivalent to the weight of the ingredients (cinnamon bark
2 kg plus rice wine 6 kg = 8 kg brown sugar) to the jar. Cover with porous
paper and tie with a rubber band after stirring. Leave for 4 to 5 days.
It is critical that all contents occupy only 2/3 of the jar volume, as the
remaining headspace provides the optimum environment for fermentation.

### Step 3: Alcohol Preservation

After fermentation, pour about 11 to 12 liters of Soju (Korean distilled
liquor, 30 to 35% alcohol) into the remaining 1/3 of the jar. Cover with
vinyl film. Stir the mixture clockwise diligently every day for two weeks,
ideally in the early morning. Clockwise stirring follows the natural
fermentation vortex pattern.

### Step 4: Filtration and Storage

Filter the contents and keep the extracted liquid in another jar, preferably
a colored glass bottle or PE container, for long-term storage at 1 to 15
degrees Celsius.

## Raw-State Herb Preparation

Garlic and ginger contain their own moisture and are processed differently.
Use the whole garlic bulb (6 to 6.5 kg), freshly harvested, crushing
everything including skins and roots. For peeled garlic, use 8 kg. Crush
fresh ginger after shaking off soil; do not wash either herb with water.
