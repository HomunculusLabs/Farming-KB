## Soft Seedlings: The Hardening Off Problem
When plants are grown at high temperatures, particularly at night, they grow
lushly — leaves and stems get much larger — but a goodly part of this extra size
is nothing but water stored in weak-walled cells. When plants don't experience
wind, they aren't exercised. The gentle battering caused by light air movement
actually exercises the plant and causes it to reinforce its connective and
supportive tissues.

Seedling growers crank up the heat (and there is no wind inside a hothouse), so
after four weeks they have large, soft, lush-looking seedlings. When these are
moved directly outdoors:

### Sequential Shocks
1. **Temperature shock**: The first night outdoors, even if not freezing, is a
   severe shock that sets the seedling back so hard it can't grow for a week.
2. **Wind damage**: The seedling never experienced wind before, so leaves and
   stems get badly bruised or torn by even light gusts.
3. **Sun shock**: Seedlings grown under plastic that filtered out about 15
   percent of the energy get full sun for the first time. Underdeveloped root
   systems can't supply enough water, and the seedling wilts.
4. **Disease vulnerability**: A weakened seedling becomes susceptible to diseases
   and insects that wouldn't normally harm a healthy plant.
