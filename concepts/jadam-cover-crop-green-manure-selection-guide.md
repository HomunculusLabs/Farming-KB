# JADAM Cover Crop and Green Manure Selection Guide

Cover crops are central to JADAM's approach to self-sufficient organic
farming. Rather than purchasing [[hamilton-organic-fertilizers-and-plant-nutrition]] from the market, JADAM
teaches farmers to grow their own [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] through carefully selected
cover crops. Nature always grows vegetation that turns into organic
fertilizer, and JADAM follows this pattern. Cover crops provide organic
matter, soften compacted soil, reduce salinity, boost microbial activity,
suppress weeds, and can even fix atmospheric nitrogen.

## Hairy Vetch

Hairy vetch is a leguminous cover crop that fixes nitrogen from the air,
providing approximately 20 kg (44 lb) of nitrogen per 0.1 ha ([[fukuoka-agriculture-tomorrow-quarter-acre-vision]])
with a total biomass of about 17 tons (37,500 lb). Its low carbon-nitrogen
(C/N) ratio of around 10 means it breaks down rapidly in soil. One of its
valuable qualities is [[fukuoka-natural-weed-management]] suppression.

Sowing season for southern Korea is between early September and early
October. Seeds must be sown by early October at the latest. The seeding rate
is 3-5 kg (7-11 lb) per 0.1 ha. Sow more heavily if climate conditions are
unfavorable or sowing is late. In orchards, cut hairy vetch after seed
formation; then you do not need to re-sow annually as they self-seed and
wither in summer.

Hairy vetch grows well in sandy soil or loam with good drainage but is weak
to excessive moisture. For fields where the main crop is planted later, hairy
vetch can be left to die naturally and serve as mulch.

## Milk Vetch

Milk vetch is another leguminous crop similar to hairy vetch. It fixes
approximately 15 kg (33 lb) of nitrogen per 0.1 ha (quarter acre) with a
total biomass of about 17 tons. In Asia, milk vetch has been used as a green
manure plant for thousands of years, traditionally composted after being
mixed with soil.

Sowing season is before September 20-25 in southern areas and before
mid-September in central areas. Good germination requires sufficient soil
moisture. Milk vetch is vulnerable to cold and can freeze to death if
temperatures stay below minus 5 degrees C (23 degrees F) for extended
periods. The vetch can be turned into soil after May 25, after seeding, so
re-sowing is unnecessary.

## Rye

Rye is a gramineous (grass family) crop extremely tolerant to cold,
surviving temperatures below minus 25 degrees C (minus 13 degrees F) in
central and northern regions. It provides about 15 kg (33 lb) of nitrogen
per 0.1 ha with a total biomass of 20 tons (44,092 lb).

Rye has strong nutrient absorption capacity, making it an excellent green
manure source. It is particularly helpful for eliminating salt from soil.
Its vigorous root growth greatly improves soil physical characteristics and
helps break up compacted soil layers. Sowing season is late September to
early October for highlands, mid-October to late October for other regions.
Rye sprouts in just four days even in soil temperatures of 4-5 degrees C.
Seeding rate is approximately 15 kg (33 lb) per 0.1 ha.

Some experts warn that rye's low nitrogen content causes nitrogen
immobilization during decomposition, potentially damaging fruit tree growth.
However, JADAM field experience consistently dismisses this concern. Orchard
owners who have practiced tree-rye symbiosis for over 20 years report
[[fukuoka-textdoc-increased-production-not-increased-yields]] every year. Rye seeds are broadcast before harvesting
persimmons so they sprout between the fallen persimmon leaves, ensuring no
nutrient is lost and everything is recycled.

## Sudan Grass

Sudan grass is a typical annual summer crop effective as green manure and
for removing salt from soil in protected cultivations (greenhouses). It is
tolerant of high temperatures and drought, making it easy to grow. Early
growth is slow but accelerates rapidly after rooting. Its fast growth rate
allows four to five cuttings per year, and it can also serve as a feed crop.

Sudan grass helps reduce nematode damage and is effective for problems from
repeated monoculture. The best sowing time is summer when average temperature
exceeds 15 degrees C (59 degrees F). Seeding rate is 4-5 kg (9-11 lb) per
0.1 ha for broadcast seeding and 2-3 kg (4.4-6.6 lb) for line seeding with
thin soil covering.
