# Fungal Sampling Methods

Standardized methods for documenting [[fungal-biodiversity]] across habitats. Fungal diversity assessment requires specialized techniques that differ markedly from plant or animal survey methods because most fungi are microscopic for much of their life cycle, and fruiting is often episodic.

## General Principles

### Sampling Design
- **Habitat-based**: Sample all major habitat types (forest, grassland, aquatic, soil, dung)
- **Microhabitat-based**: Within habitats, sample distinct microhabitats (litter, bark, wood, soil, aerial plant parts, dung)
- **Temporal**: Sample across seasons and multiple years; many fungi fruit irregularly
- **Spatial**: Adequate geographic coverage; minimum plot sizes depend on habitat
- **Multi-method**: No single method captures all fungal diversity; combine approaches

### Essential Equipment
- GPS, camera, field notebook, collection bags
- Hand lens (10x), knife, pruning shears
- Sterile collection containers (paper bags, not plastic for mushrooms)
- Moist chamber supplies (Petri dishes, filter paper, distilled water)
- Microscope slides, coverslips, mounting media
- Temperature/humidity data logger

## Habitat-Specific Methods

### Macrofungal Fruit Body Surveys

**Transect/plot methods**:
- Fixed-area plots (e.g., 100m x 20m) searched systematically
- Timed searches (all macrofungi collected within set time period)
- Line transects (search within fixed distance of transect line)
- Record: species, substrate, abundance, GPS, photographs

**Seasonal considerations**:
- Most temperate macrofungi fruit autumn (Sept-Nov in N. Hemisphere)
- Some species fruit spring (morels, Verpa)
- Tropical fungi may fruit year-round or seasonally with rains
- Fruiting triggered by temperature, moisture, and substrate availability

**Documentation**:
- Photograph in situ before collection
- Collect complete specimens including base of stipe
- Note substrate, associated tree species, habitat
- Spore prints made in field when possible
- Dry specimens promptly or process for culture

### Soil Fungi

**Direct plating**:
- Soil dilution series (1:10, 1:100, 1:1000, 1:10000)
- Spread on selective media (DRBC, GAN, PDA + antibiotics)
- Count colony-forming units (CFUs) per gram
- Incubate at 20-25°C (ambient) or 37°C (thermophiles)

**Soil washing technique**:
- Agar plate method: soil particles washed and spread on selective agar
- Removes soil particles while retaining fungal propagules

**Molecular methods**:
- DNA extraction from soil samples
- High-throughput sequencing (metagenomics/metabarcoding)
- ITS, 18S, or 28S rDNA markers
- Reveals "hidden" diversity not detected by culturing

**Baiting**:
- Buried substrates (toothpicks, wood pieces, hair, seeds) recovered after weeks
- Soil samples incubated in moist chambers

### Litter and Leaf Litter Fungi

**Litter bag method**:
- Standardized mesh bags filled with known litter type
- Placed in field and retrieved at intervals (weeks to months)
- Retrieved litter examined for fungi (moist chambers, direct plating)
- Quantifies fungal succession on decomposing litter

**Direct examination**:
- Collect litter samples from defined plots
- Examine under dissecting microscope for fruiting bodies
- Incubate in moist chambers

**Bubble chamber/foam spore traps**:
- Collect airborne and waterborne spores
- Useful for detecting Ingoldian hyphomycetes in streams

### Bark and Corticolous Fungi

**Sampling bark microhabitats**:
- Collect bark from living trees at different heights
- Focus on different tree species, aspects (N/S), bark textures
- Myxomycetes and ascomycetes often show bark specificity
- Place bark pieces in moist chambers; examine for 4-8 weeks

### Dung Fungi (see [[coprophilous-fungi]])

**Direct collection**:
- Fresh dung collected from field, placed in separate containers
- Species of herbivore recorded
- Incubate in moist chambers at 20-25°C
- Examine every 1-3 days for 4-8 weeks

**Dung baiting**:
- Air-dried dung stored for months; rehydrated to trigger fruiting
- Dung extract agar for obligate coprophiles
- Rodent dung from live-trapped animals

### Freshwater Fungi (see [[freshwater-fungi-biodiversity]])

Chytrids: bait with chitin, cellulose, keratin, or pollen (in situ mesh bags 1 week-1 month); examine algae, aquatic plants, insect exuviae. Peronosporomycetes: hemp seed/snake skin baiting in water; dilution plating for Pythium. Ascomycetes/hyphomycetes: collect submerged wood and plant debris, stream leaf litter bags, bubble chamber for aquatic spores. See [[freshwater-fungi-biodiversity]] for detailed protocols.

### Endophytic Fungi

**Surface sterilization protocol**:
1. Collect healthy-looking leaves/stems
2. Wash in 70% ethanol (30 sec) - 2.5% sodium hypochlorite (2-5 min) - 70% ethanol (30 sec)
3. Rinse in sterile distilled water
4. Cut into segments, plate on [[malt-extract-agar]] + antibiotics
5. Incubate at room temperature; examine daily for 4-8 weeks
6. Isolate emerging colonies to pure culture

### Mycetozoans (see [[mycetozoans]])

**Moist chamber method**:
- Standard for protostelids, dictyostelids, and smaller myxomycetes
- Substrata placed in Petri dishes on moist filter paper
- Incubate at ambient temperature
- Examine microscopically for fruiting bodies over 4-12 weeks

**Soil plating** (dictyostelids):
- Soil/litter samples plated on weak nutrient agar with E. coli
- Emerging colonies documented
- Most clones isolated from surface humus layers

## Quantitative Methods

### Diversity Indices
- Species richness (S): total number of species
