## The Spore Print

The spore print is the single most important identification tool. Spore
printing should be done within the first few hours from picking, as spore
production declines as the mushroom dries. Place the cap, gills down, on a
piece of paper (half white, half black). After several hours, the spore
deposit color reveals critical taxonomic information.

Key spore colors by genus:
  - Psilocybe, Panaeolus: purplish brown to black (generally safe to
    identify; no known poisonous species in these two genera)
  - Galerina, Conocybe: rusty brown (DANGER — Galerinas contain deadly
    amatoxins)
  - Amanita, Entoloma: whitish to pinkish brown (DANGER)
  - Hebeloma, Inocybe: yellowish brown to clay brown (DANGER)

## The Deadly Look-alikes

Two genera pose the greatest risk to collectors: Galerina and Pholiotina.
Both produce rusty brown spores. The Psilocybes have purplish brown to black
spores, and by this distinction alone you cannot make a deadly mistake between
a Psilocybe and a Galerina.

### Galerina Species

Galerinas often grow gregariously or in clusters on decaying logs or in moss,
with short brittle stems that often darken from the base. They may or may not
have a partial veil leaving a membranous ring or fibrillous annular zone. The
gills are rusty brown at spore maturity. At least 199 species are recognized,
many distinguishable only microscopically.
