# JADAM Natural Weed Control Strategies

JADAM fundamentally redefines the relationship between farmers and weeds. Rather than treating wild grass as an enemy to be eliminated, JADAM seeks symbiosis -- using weeds as partners that provide [[aact-disease-suppression-mechanisms-ingham]].

## The Paradigm Shift

The conventional view: weeds absorb water and nutrients meant for crops and cause disease. JADAM acknowledges this can be true in the short term (1-2 years) but argues that over 4-5 years of co-existence, soil with wild grass becomes much moister, more fertile, and has less disease. Recent studies support these findings.

Treating grass as enemy makes farming "very, very painful." Letting crops grow with grass is "more natural, healthy and beautiful. Grass is not your enemy; it is your partner."

## Cover Crop Method

The primary JADAM strategy for weed management is intentional cover crop cultivation.

### Autumn-Winter-Spring Method

Around October, before fruit trees drop their leaves, sow grass seeds in the field. Grass sprouts before leaves fall, catching leaves between the grass. This:
- Prevents loss of precious leaf nutrients (which would otherwise blow away)
- Covers the entire orchard green before winter
- Protects soil from spring sun, keeping temperature moderate
- Provides organic matter (decomposed by soil fauna and microorganisms within one year)

### Summer Management

Cut grass 5 cm (2 in) high, 3 times per year from spring to autumn. Use a sickle for small fields; mower for larger plots. Do not try to be perfect or cut every single grass. Some remaining uncut grass is useful.

Leaving 5 cm of grass height helps stop the spread of aphids and mites.

### Leave Ample Space

Leave sufficient space between crop rows so grass growing between rows does not compete with crops for water and nutrients. With proper spacing, the symbiotic relationship thrives.

### Nutrient Cycling

Grass catches fallen leaves (concentrated nutrients from deep soil), preventing nutrient loss. Cut grass decomposes on the surface, returning all nutrients to the soil.

## Practical Weed Management Options

### Hay/Plant Material Mulching

The best method for soil temperature control and weed suppression. Mulch the field with hay or plant materials. Traditional but may be labor-intensive for large areas.

### Weed Mats

UV-coated mats with small holes that allow air passage. Advantages over plastic:
- Temperature rise is much smaller
