# 2C-Family Chemistry and Structure-Activity Relationships

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

The 2C-family represents the phenethylamine (two-carbon chain) counterparts to the DO-family amphetamines. Named for the two-carbon chain between the aromatic ring and the amine group (as opposed to the three-carbon chain of amphetamines), these compounds form one of the most extensively explored chemical families in PiHKAL. The general structure is 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted phenethylamine.

## Nomenclature

The "2C" prefix was Shulgin's encoding system for this class. The letter following designates the substituent at the 4-position:

- [[dob-compound-profile]] -- 4-bromo (the most broadly explored of all halides)
- 2C-I -- 4-iodo (the most potent halide)
- 2C-C -- 4-chloro (less potent, dosage 20-40 mg, duration 4-8 h)
- 2C-D (LE-25) -- 4-methyl (dosage 20-60 mg, "pharmacological tofu")
- 2C-E -- 4-ethyl (dosage 15-20 mg, "a complete psychedelic")
- 2C-N -- 4-nitro
- 2C-T series -- 4-alkylthio (sulfur) compounds
- 2C-CN -- 4-cyano (unexplored in man)
- 2C-COOH -- 4-carboxy (unexplored in man)

## Potency Relationship to Amphetamine Counterparts

A consistent and dramatic loss of potency occurs when shortening from the amphetamine (3-carbon) to the phenethylamine (2-carbon) chain. Shulgin called 2C-D "probably the most dramatic example" of this phenomenon: DOM is active at approximately 3 mg while 2C-D requires 20-60 mg -- roughly one-tenth the potency. This pattern holds across the family:

- DOI vs 2C-I
- DOB vs 2C-B
- DOC vs 2C-C

## Halogen Substitution Patterns

The halogens are unique in the periodic table -- monovalent and negative, stable on aromatic nuclei, but monovalent means they cannot be modified or extended. They are "a kind of dead end" for the 2C-X series. The trend in potency follows atomic weight:

- Fluorine (2C-F) -- hardest to synthesize, requires indirect procedures; fluorine is not only a gas but dangerous to handle and does not react in the usual halogen way
- Chlorine (2C-C) -- less potent; "perhaps because chlorine is a gas and troublesome to handle"
- Bromine (2C-B) -- "by far the most broadly explored"
- Iodine (2C-I) -- the most potent

## The 2C-D Anomaly: Pharmacological Tofu

2C-D occupies a unique position in the 2C-family. Shulgin called it "pharmacological tofu" because it "doesn't seem to do too much by itself, always teasing, until you get to heroic levels." However, multiple experimental therapists reported that it was "excellent in extending the action of some other materials," boosting waning action "without adding its own color to the experience." At 150 mg (in German therapeutic research where it was called LE-25), it was described as "a truly remarkable psychedelic" comparable to 2C-B. It was also called a "smart pill" at low doses (10 mg) for its reported cognitive enhancement.

## Structure-Activity Relationships

### Chain Length Effects

The relationship between chain length and activity is one of the most important SAR findings in PiHKAL:
- 2-carbon (2C series) -- active but less potent
- 3-carbon (amphetamine/DO series) -- more potent
- 4-carbon -- intermediate or reduced activity in most cases
- Beyond 4 carbons -- generally inactive

### 4-Position Substituent Effects

The nature and size of the 4-position substituent dramatically affects potency and character:
- Small alkyl groups (methyl, ethyl) -- moderate potency, stimulant qualities
- Halogens -- high potency, classic psychedelic character
- Larger alkyl groups (propyl, butyl) -- high potency but increased physical side effects and hypnogogic qualities
- Polar groups (cyano, carboxy) -- generally inactive or unexplored

### The Template Concept

The 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted pattern served as a template for systematic exploration. Shulgin described TMA-2 (2,4,5-trimethoxyamphetamine) as the original template from which "an immense family of very active and fascinating drugs have arisen." The 2,5-dimethoxy aspect was kept intact while the 4-position was systematically modified, giving rise to the DO compounds, the 2C-X compounds, and the Aleph compounds.

## Synthetic Approaches

The standard synthetic route involves:
1. Starting from appropriate benzaldehyde precursors
2. Condensation with nitromethane (for 2C compounds) or nitroethane (for amphetamines) to form nitrostyrenes
3. Reduction with lithium aluminum hydride or other reducing agents
4. Isolation as hydrochloride salts

The Vilsmeier reaction (POCl3 + N-methylformanilide) was frequently used for aldehyde synthesis. For halogenated compounds, direct electrophilic halogenation of the aromatic ring was employed.
