## Poultry Forage Species for Arid Areas

### Sunflower
Does well everywhere. Heads can be cut off and fed entire to poultry. Resists fire. Acts as a trellis for lab-lab and pole bean crop. A good short-term windbreak, provides some mulch. Almost wild at Alice Springs, Papunya, Emabella. Husked, it provides good oil and food for humans. Deserves broadscale trials as a grain crop. Unopened heads can be eaten as a vegetable.

### Millets
Sorghum, sweet corn, sudax. As above. Sudax as a mulch/border species, sorghum for sugar and seed. All can be used as human food or forage.

## Exotic Additions for Arid Forage Systems

- **Tree lucerne** (Chaemocytisus proliferus) -- nitrogen-fixing forage
- **Black locust** (Robinia pseudoacacia) -- high-durability timber and forage
- **Honey locust** (Gleditsia triacanthos) -- pole timbers and forage
- **Banana passionfruit** (Passiflora mollissima) -- stands frost
- **Mesquites** (Prosopis spp.) -- arid-adapted forage
- **Comfrey** -- mineral accumulator, poultry fodder
- **Olive** -- forage and oil
- **Lespedeza** -- nitrogen-fixing forage
- **Lucerne** -- for sprouts, green forage
- **Mulberry** -- first-class poultry fodder, especially white mulberry
- **Chinquapin oak** (Quercus muhlenbergii) -- sweet acorn
- **Helm oak** (Q. ilex, evergreen) -- evergreen forage
- **Cork oak** (Q. suber) -- forage and cork
- **Chestnut oak** (Q. prinus) -- forage
- **Acacia albida** -- reference species for arid systems

## Aquatic Species for Arid Water Bodies

As dams, tanks, and lagoons are developed in arid areas, attention should be given to aquatics:

- **Native wild rice** (Gollan reference)
- **Water lily** (Nymphaea gigantea) -- stalks, tubers, seeds
- **Nardoo** (a fern) -- aquatic food plant
- **Native arrowroot** -- aquatic food plant
- **Trapa** or **Eleocharis** (water chestnuts) -- do well in arid climates
- **Lotus** -- aquatic food and forage
- **Sweet rush** -- shoots and bulbs

Trapa should reduce summer evaporation from open tanks or dams. Sweet rush is present in arid Australian regions.

## Grain Processing

Using the CSIRO "ripple-flow" process, all grains, sunflower, and legumes can be hulled and ground to flour in one machine. The machine needs a 16 h.p. (tractor) motor and hulls or grinds 4-5 tonnes of grain per hour, suited to central processing in small settlements and communities.
