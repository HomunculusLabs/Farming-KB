### Physical Shelter
Hardy windbreak species of equal or greater height exclude frost, nullify salty or hot winds, provide mulch, and moderate the environment. In-crop shade shelter of legumes is needed by avocado, citrus, cocoa, or coffee.

### Culinary Associates
Keeping common culinary associates together (tomatoes with parsley and basil; potatoes with mint; dill with cucumbers and apples) reduces work in gathering.

## Analytic Approach to Guild Selection

### Species Interactions
- About 80% of all plant species can co-mingle without ill effect
- 10-15% of species greatly assist others in beneficial ways
- About 5% of species show antagonistic behaviour

### Guild Components for Pome and Stone Fruit Orchards

#### Spring Bulbs
Narcissus, hyacinth: flower and die back by early summer, as does Allium triquetrum (onion weed). Create a grass-free area below trees in fruit, plus a crop of bulbs, flowers, and honey. Iris and tuberous-rooted flowers also assist grass control.

#### Spike Roots
Comfrey, dandelion, globe artichoke: cover the ground and encourage worms, yield mulch and crop. Soil below their foliage is soft, free-draining, open to roots feeding near the surface, cool.

#### Insectary Plants
Umbelliferae and small-flowered plants: fennel, dill, Queen Anne's lace, tansy, carrot, and parsnip flowers. Tachynid and other predatory wasps, robber flies, ladybirds, jewel beetles, and pollinator bees or wasps are attracted. Quillaja, Photinia, some Tamarix and Acacia species placed in windbreaks or interplanted.

#### Herb Layer
Catnip, fennel, dill (any Umbelliferae), small daisy varieties (Compositae), Phyla (Lippia) nodiflora, and flowering ground covers attract wasps, pollinator bees, and insectivorous birds.

#### Nitrogen and Nutrient Crop
Clovers, and interplants of tagasaste or Acacia provide root-level nitrogen. Crotalaria controls root nematodes, as do marigolds (by "fumigation" of soil).

## Vertebrate Guild Members

### Ground Foragers
Pigs or poultry used to clear up fallen fruit that hosts fruit fly or larval forms of pests. Can be run in orchards for the relatively short period of the year when fruit is falling.

### Insectivorous Birds
Woodpeckers, honey-eaters search bark crevices for resting larvae and egg masses. Encourage with scattered flowering shrubs: Kniphofia, Banksia, Salvia, Buddleia, Fuchsia, Echium fastuosum.

### Mollusc Control
Snails and slugs are almost totally controlled by a duck flock on range. Ducks can be ranged seasonally (autumn to spring) in plant systems.

## Pest Reduction Results

By a combination of guild strategies, pest species in orchards can be reduced to 4-7% of monocrop orchard:
- Selection of disease resistant stock
- Minimal damage by pruning, or no pruning
- Predation by birds, frogs, lizards, wasp and predatory insects
- Interplant of leguminous trees and other than main crop species
- Reducing crop stress with [[permaculture-designers-manual-mulch-systems]]
- Ground foraging by chickens, pigs, geese to clean up windfalls

## Guild Design Philosophy

Every designer and every gardener can plan such guilds for specific target species, specific pests and weed control, and specific garden beds or orchards. Maximise the floristic or flowering components of orchard interplant, to reduce or even eliminate grasses, to attract pollinators and predatory insects, insectivorous birds, and to provide ground cover for frogs and insectivorous lizards.

## Practical Considerations

Successful implementation of Food Forest Guilds requires attention to
several practical factors including environmental conditions,
resource availability, and timing. Careful monitoring and
adaptive management help optimize outcomes across varying
conditions. Integration with other system elements enhances
overall effectiveness and creates beneficial synergies that
improve resilience and productivity over time.
