# Trellis and Shadehouse Design in Permaculture

updated: 2026-04-18

Permaculture Two (1979) Section 5.3 details trellis and shadehouse design for climate modification, food production, and wind protection around buildings.

## Why Trellis

In new gardens, the great lack is wind shelter. Species such as citrus, avocado, and macadamia struggle to survive. The fastest possible assistance is to build trellis at near right-angles to E, W, and N walls.

## Multiple Effects of Trellis

Trellis has a multiple effect on the building environment:

- Separates functional space into recreational, garden, or service area
- Prevents the flow of cold winds along walls
- Acts as a sun trap
- Presents a basic structure for vine crop production

## Trellis Construction

### Building Integration

Trellis built on earth banks, tyre or stone walls, at rockery bases are even more effective as early protection. They may:

- Curve out from house corners
- Break up a facade on a school or large building
- Afford several places for benches, lawns, and gardens

### Wind Tunnel Conversion

Frequently in institutions, large building surfaces converge to make wind tunnels. Trellis is often the only answer. Arrowed entries baffle the wind further. Similarly, roads of no real thoroughfare value are mostly designed as clear wind tunnels. Large boulders, plants, and trellis convert them to sheltered and sinuous access, blocking dust, cold, and noise as a side effect. This is true of all driveways, service roads, blind entries, and minor trafficways.

## Horizontal Trellis

Horizontal trellis has several uses:

- Shade windows from full summer sun
- Create overhead vine trellis to shelter tender crops in desert or extreme summer climates
- Increase solar radiation on crops by reflection from horizontal surfaces

## The Shadehouse

### The Shadehouse Effect

The refreshing coolness of a shadehouse in the hot Australian summer has to be experienced to be believed. Even tiny pools, a few ferns, and a spray or drip of water increase the effect, both physically (by evaporation) and psychologically. Air in such places has a different quality, an aliveness normally missing from the languid air of still areas.
