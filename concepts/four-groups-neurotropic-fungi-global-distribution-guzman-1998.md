## Four Groups of Neurotropic Fungi and Their Global Distribution

## Overview

The 1998 comprehensive survey by Guzman, Allen, and Gartz catalogued **216 known species of neurotropic fungi** worldwide and organized them into four distinct chemical and cultural groups. This classification remains the most authoritative global accounting of [[biogeography-austral-hemisphere-fungi]].

This is by far the largest group, containing species that produce [[norbaeocystin]]. The genera and their species counts within this group are:

## Group 4: Sacred Fungi Without Confirmed Chemistry

This group encompasses fungi traditionally used in sacred or ceremonial contexts for which **no reliable chemical studies** have confirmed neurotropic constituents. The cultural importance of these fungi may stem from actual psychoactive properties yet to be characterized, from confusion with genuinely psychoactive species, or from symbolic rather than pharmacological significance:

### Boletes (8 species in 2 genera)
Several bolete species were reported by Heim (1963, 1966, 1978) as hallucinogenic among New Guinea aborigines. However, Ott (1993) stated that these boletes do not possess any confirmed neurotropic properties, and their ceremonial use may involve confusion with Psilocybe species that grow in the same habitats.

### Russula (6 species)
Various Russula species have been reported in ceremonial contexts, though chemical analysis has not confirmed psychoactive constituents. Like the boletes, their sacred status may reflect cultural associations rather than pharmacological effects.
