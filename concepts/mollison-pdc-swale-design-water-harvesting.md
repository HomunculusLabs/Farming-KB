# Mollison PDC Swale Design and Water Harvesting

Bill Mollison's comprehensive guide to swale construction and water harvesting in humid landscapes. From the 1981 Permaculture Design Course, Pamphlet II: "Permaculture in Humid Landscapes."

## What Is a Swale

A swale is a shallow blade trench cut on true contour, with no movement of water along the trench. It is "a critical technology for winter-wet America that is not much used."

## Construction

- Walk it out along contour (no laser level needed for small systems)
- Trenches are quite broad: hardly ever less than four feet wide, often much wider
- Use on moderate to shallow slopes, not steep slopes
- In clay: deepen the swale in profile
- In sandy and gravelly places: widen it
- "You could do it with a couple of kids and a spade"

## How Swales Work

1. Rain falls and runs down the slope
2. Water enters the swale and soaks into the earth
3. When the swale is saturated, water enters a reverse siphon and runs down to the next level
4. Excess water can be directed off the landscape through the same system

## Water Absorption

- In three to four years, you will have 17 to 20 feet of fully charged soil
- Your forest above the swale has access to this water
- Your forest will be alive when your neighbor's groundwater has flowed away
- The Davis, California project initially absorbed 40%, then 85%, and now 100% of its water
- "Now it is absorbing water from off-site into its swales"

### Aging Improves Efficiency

The efficiency of absorption increases as swales age and trees grow along them because tree roots penetrate the subsurface and carry water down. Swales do not need to be renewed.

## Urban Swale Design

### The Davis, California Model

A remarkable demonstration of urban permaculture:

- Hard-top roads, swales, little bridges, houses back to back, footpaths, down pipes
- All roof run-off goes into swales
- All road run-off goes into swales
- No guttering, no curbs
- Swales sometimes pass under roads
- Little ponds in swales with frogs croaking

### Design Variations

- Block stepping stones across swales
- Little rocks or graveled areas across swales
- Little ponds within swales
- "Great places for children to run in the storms and hop into them"

## Multiple Benefits of Swales

1. **Water storage:** Charge groundwater over enormous areas
2. **Mulch accumulation:** Leaves collect and rot quickly in the moist swale environment
3. **Forest fire prevention:** Swales collect fuel and rot it quickly, making for a more moist forest
4. **Growing sites:** Ideal for moisture-loving plants (blueberries below pine forests, ginseng)
5. **Irrigation:** One person can water hundreds of trees in about an hour using swale-based irrigation
6. **Soil building:** Mulch in swales can be periodically scraped up and placed around trees
7. **Pond creation:** Widen areas in the swale where clay holds water to create small ponds

## Swales in Existing Forests

- Amazingly few trees need to be removed to run a swale through existing forest
- Better to swale before planting a new forest
- Some trees can stand in the swales
- Trees planted above the swale provide mulch that falls into it

## Integration with Other Systems

### Irrigation from Swales

- Plant white clover in the swale, mow the grass strip and throw all grass on the swale
- When rich and good, regrade by bringing rich topsoil up to trees
- Creates a mound of black earth with tree roots and a walking platform

### Swales with Dams

- Gullies encountered while running swales easily become ponds
- Swales fill ponds by gravity
- Diversion drains from ponds can irrigate lower areas

### Fences and Access

- Fencing and access roads naturally follow water systems
- If fences run valley to slope, animals walk the fences and their tracks keyline automatically
- "Animals always walk ridge down to valley" and can become major erosive influence if not managed

## The Double-Duty Diversion Drain

- A descending diversion drain around valley slope keeps drain bottom dry in winter
- The same drain, plugged, irrigates the valley in summer
- Also prevents bog situations and seepage below

## Slope Planting Strategy

- **Upper slope:** Narrow leafed species (peaches, apricots) — more drought resistant
- **Lower slope:** Broad leafed species — getting wetter all the way down
- **Exceptions:** Fig goes higher because it is drought resistant
- **Ridge tops:** Hardy, drought-proof trees
- Drought-resistant ridge species (tamarisks, pines) provide good mulch layer to bring downhill

## Ponds for Microclimate

- Pond in front of house: fire barrier, light reflector, pleasant environment
- Absorbs all but 15% of summer sunlight
- Absorbs very little winter sunlight, reflecting most into living space
- "A pond is a beautiful, automatic, self-regulating heating surface"
- Can attach glasshouses to pond for additional heat buffering
