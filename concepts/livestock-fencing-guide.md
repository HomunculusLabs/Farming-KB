# Livestock Fencing Guide

Fencing is one of the most important investments on a homestead. Good fencing
keeps animals where they belong, protects them from predators, and enables
rotational grazing systems that improve pasture health. The right fence depends
on the species, terrain, budget, and management style. This guide covers the
major fencing types used in homestead livestock systems.

## Electric Fencing

### Overview
Electric fencing is the most versatile and cost-effective option for
rotational grazing and temporary paddocks. It works by delivering a brief,
high-voltage pulse that trains animals to respect the barrier. The psychological
barrier is as important as the physical one.

### Components
- **Energizer (fence charger):** The heart of the system. Joule rating
  determines power: 0.5-1 joule for small paddocks, 1-3 for multi-wire
  permanent, 6+ for perimeter fencing. AC plug-in, DC battery, or solar.
- **Grounding system:** Critical — 50% of fence problems are grounding
  issues. Minimum 3 ground rods, 6-8 ft long, spaced 10 ft apart, connected
  with galvanized wire. In dry soil, add more ground rods or a ground
  wire return system.
- **Fence wire:** Poly wire (stainless steel filaments woven into polyethylene
  strands), poly tape (more visible for horses), poly rope, or high-tensile
  smooth wire.
- **Posts:** Fiberglass step-in posts for temporary, wood or steel for
  permanent. T-posts with insulators are a cost-effective permanent option.
- **Insulators:** Prevent voltage loss at posts. Required on all wood and
  metal posts.

### Design Considerations
- Minimum 2 wires for cattle, 3-4 for sheep and goats, 5-6 for pigs.
- Wire heights: adjust for species. Bottom wire at nose height for the
  target species.
- Train animals to electric fence in a small, secure paddock before turning
  them out on larger electric fences.
- Test fence regularly with a fence tester (digital voltmeter). Minimum
  3000V at the furthest point from the energizer.
- Vegetation touching the fence causes voltage loss. Mow or trim under wires.
- Lightning protection: install lightning arrestors on the energizer and
  disconnect during severe storms.

### Portable Electric Fencing
- **Electric net fencing:** Pre-fabricated nets with posts built in. 12-48
  inch heights. Ideal for poultry, sheep, and goats in rotational systems.
  Quick to set up and move.
- **Step-in post + poly wire:** More flexible than net fencing. Use 3-5
  strands for larger livestock.
- **Key consideration:** net fencing requires strong energizers (1+ joule)
  and diligent vegetation management. Grass touching the bottom strand
  grounds out the entire net.

## Woven Wire

### Overview
Woven wire (field fence) provides a permanent physical barrier. It consists
  of horizontal wires woven with vertical stay wires in a fixed mesh pattern.
  Best suited as perimeter fencing or permanent paddock divisions.

### Specifications
- **Mesh size:** 4x4 inch is standard for cattle and sheep. 2x4 inch (tighter
  at bottom) prevents young animals from passing through. 6x6 inch is too
  loose for small livestock.
- **Height:** 47-52 inches (standard rolls). Add a top rail or barbed wire
  strand for extra height if needed.
- **Wire gauge:** 12.5 gauge (lighter) to 10 gauge (heavier, more durable).
- **Coating:** Class 1 galvanized (light duty) or Class 3 galvanized
  (heavy duty, 2-3x the life). Always choose Class 3 for homestead use.

### Installation
- Corner posts: 6-8 inch diameter wood posts, set 3-4 ft deep in concrete.
- Line posts: wood (4-6 inch) or steel T-posts, spaced 8-12 feet apart.
- Brace assemblies: H-braces or N-braces at all corners, ends, and gates.
  This is the most critical structural element — failure here means fence
  failure.
- Tension the fence wire using a fence stretcher. Do not over-tighten.
- Staple wire to wood posts with enough room for expansion/contraction
  (wire moves under staples).
- Add an electric offset wire at the top and/or bottom for added security
  and predator deterrence.

### Pros and Cons
- Pros: permanent, visible physical barrier, contains all livestock species,
  predator deterrent (especially with electric offset).
- Cons: expensive (materials + labor), permanent (cannot easily relocate),
  difficult on rocky or uneven terrain.

## High-Tensile Fencing

### Overview
High-tensile fencing uses heavy-gauge (12.5 gauge) spring-steel wire
stretched to very high tension (200-300 lbs). It is the most durable and
longest-lasting fencing option, with a lifespan of 40+ years.

### Types
- **All-electric:** 4-8 strands of smooth high-tensile wire, all hot.
  Requires strong energizer (6+ joules) and excellent grounding.
- **Non-electric / physical barrier:** 5-8 strands, alternating hot and
  ground wires, or all non-electric with stays (flexible battons between
  posts that keep wires spaced).
- **Hybrid:** Physical barrier (woven wire or V-mesh) with high-tensile
  electric offset wires.

### Installation
- Requires specialized tools: high-tensile wire spinner, crimper, in-line
  strainers, and tension meter.
- End posts must be extremely strong — large wood posts (8+ inch) set in
  concrete, with massive brace assemblies.
- Line posts can be wood, steel, or fiberglass, spaced 20-30 feet apart.
- Splicing is done with crimp sleeves (not knots).
- In-line strainers allow re-tensioning as needed. Check tension seasonally.

### Pros and Cons
- Pros: extremely durable, lowest long-term cost, clean appearance, easy
  maintenance, excellent for large properties.
- Cons: high initial cost, requires specialized knowledge and tools, less
  forgiving of poor installation, dangerous if broken wire snaps back.

## Portable Fencing for Rotational Grazing

### Systems
- **Electric net fencing:** Best for sheep, goats, poultry, and pigs.
  Available in various heights (42-48 inch standard). Set up in 15-30
  minutes for a typical paddock. Use multiple nets for larger areas.
- **Poly wire + step-in posts:** Cheaper and more flexible than nets. Use
  3-5 strands at appropriate heights for the species.
- **Quick-closing systems:** Gallagher SmartFence and similar products
  combine posts and wire in a reel system for rapid deployment.

### Design for Rotational Grazing
- Calculate paddock size based on stocking density and rest period needs.
  See [[pasture-management]].
- Design lanes (permanent alleys) for animal movement between paddocks.
- Water access in every paddock, or lane access to a central water point.
- Use permanent fencing for the perimeter and division lanes; portable
  fencing for internal paddock divisions.
- Plan gate placement to allow easy movement between paddocks.
