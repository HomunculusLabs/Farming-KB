# Faires City Desert and Cold Climate Garden Strategies

Practical strategies for gardening in challenging environments — urban spaces, deserts, and cold climates — plus season extension techniques and replanting methods.

## City Gardens

### Container Gardening
- Any container works with drainage holes cut in bottom
- Root crops (potatoes): grow in deep metal drum, wood box, or stacked tires
- Mulch potatoes: as they grow, add more mulch around stem forcing it upward, producing more potatoes

### Super Nutritious Container Varieties
Pepper, parsley, tomato, chive, Swiss chard, lettuce

### Space Maximization
- Hang baskets, build greenhouse extending from window, build shelving up walls
- Always grow sprouts in kitchen and mushrooms in dark place
- Fruit trees: use dwarf varieties, or train against wall/fence (espalier)
- Prune existing trees to prevent shading garden

### Urban Resources
- City parks, construction projects, neighbors, Freecycle — sources of compost, wood brush, grass clippings
- Bartering with neighbors for variety

### Lead Contamination
- Sources: old paint, car exhaust near busy roads
- Test soil if suspected
- Solution: add maximum organic material (makes lead less available/absorbable)
- Avoid collecting leaves next to busy highways; choose quiet side streets

## Desert Gardens

### Three Key Strategies
1. Create successions that are self-shaded
2. Use edible tree species thriving in dry climates as staples
3. Use boxes and trellises close to or attached to the house

### Desert Staple Foods
Olives, palms, citrus, avocado, apricot, banana, papaya — "provide half your diet when in season"

### Growing System
- Graywater marsh and rainwater runoff feed into swales directly to trees
- Trees simultaneously shelter the house and create cooler climate
- Well-mulched raised beds of mud, shaded by wood trellises, vines, or trees
- Plant peas and fava beans in succession with celery, onion, carrots, spinach, tomatoes, peppers, melons
- Climbing vines like grapes grown year-round in succession

## Cold Climate Gardens

### Season Extension Strategy
- Plant cold-hardy salad vegetables in early spring
- Summer vegetables follow
- More cold-hardy salad plants in fall
- Root crops and winter-hardy vegetables stay in ground during winter
- Greenhouse supplementation
- Two approaches: extending growing season + food preservation

### Frost Protection Devices
- **Cloche**: miniature greenhouse — hoop house over raised bed with plastic, or tiny glass box
- **Plastic milk jugs**: cut bottom off, place over plant at night
- **Cold frame**: wooden box (raised bed) with glass lid on hinge; old house windows work well

### Cold-Hardy Plants
Anise, basil (cherry tomato), broccoli, caraway, celery, chamomile, chervil, chive, cilantro, cucumber, endive, Japanese greens, lettuce, mint, nasturtium, okra, oriental cabbage, parsley, rosemary, sage, Swiss chard, tarragon, thyme, turnip tops

### Winter Storage
- Root crops, leeks, kale left in ground covered with thick hay layer
- Unripe green tomatoes: pull whole plant, hang upside down in basement/cellar to ripen

## Growing Schedule (Mild Climate)

- **2 weeks before first frost**: sprout cold-hardy brassicas indoors (cabbage, broccoli)
- **After last frost**: plant cold-hardy plants outside (lettuce, kale, dill, radish, parsnip, mustard, arugula, carrots)
- **1 month after last frost**: radishes ready; transplant brassica seedlings into radish space
- **Early summer**: remove some lettuce heads, plant bush beans; harvest cabbage quickly before bolting
- **Fall**: harvest parsnips; plant fava beans or push garlic cloves into ground

## Replanting Annuals the Easy Way

### Plants That Can Be Made "Perennial"
- **Leek**: cut at ground at harvest instead of pulling — grows second time. Or dig up secondary bulbs from stem base
- **Garlic**: leave in ground 2+ years for everlasting crop
- **Broad bean**: large pods near base left to dry, mulch with straw in late summer, sprout in fall
- **Potato**: leave seed potatoes in ground, mulch well in fall, sprout in spring
- **Lettuce**: allow to go to seed, self-scatters and replants
- **Tomatoes/pumpkins/melons**: leave in garden covered with mulch at harvest, rot and spill seeds
- **Carrot**: keep tops, store dark/cool, they sprout — set out to grow
- **Cabbage**: cut stalk high leaving a few leaves, small heads spring from stalk
