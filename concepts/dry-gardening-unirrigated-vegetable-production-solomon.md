## Capillarity: How Soil Moisture Reaches Plant Roots

Capillarity is the movement of water upward through the tiny channels between
soil particles. Understanding this phenomenon is essential for dry gardening.
When soil is loosened by tilling, its capillary channels are broken, and
moisture cannot rise from below as quickly. This is why newly tilled soil
feels dry even though moisture exists deeper down.

After tilling, the gardener must wait for the soil to resettle and capillary
connections to be restored. Pressing down a furrow for sowing seeds
reestablishes capillary contact with deeper moisture, allowing seeds to
sprout even in apparently dry conditions. Filling a seed furrow with pure
fine compost amplifies this effect — compost holds many times more water than
even clay will, and seeds sown in compressed soil and covered with compost
may need watering perhaps once, or not at all.
