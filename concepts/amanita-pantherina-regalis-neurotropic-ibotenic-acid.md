**Chemistry.** The principal bioactive constituents are **ibotenic acid**, **muscimol**
(the likely primary psychoactive agent, described as emetic and sedative), and
**muscarine** — a cholinergic alkaloid that is highly toxic (reversed by atropine
sulfate). Muscarine is responsible for the parasympathomimetic "muscarinic" poisoning
syndrome (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI distress, emesis) distinct
from the CNS effects of muscimol.

**Ethnopharmacology.** *A. muscaria* holds a central place in Siberian shamanism among
the Chukchee and Koryak peoples (Wasson & Wasson 1957). A remarkable practice involved
the consumption of psychoactive urine — from both reindeer and humans who had eaten the
mushroom — which was preferred over eating the mushroom directly, as the kidneys convert
ibotenic acid into muscimol while eliminating much of the toxic muscarine and other
gastrointestinal irritants. R. Gordon Wasson controversially proposed *A. muscaria* as
the identity of **Soma**, the divine sacrament of the ancient Vedic hymns.
