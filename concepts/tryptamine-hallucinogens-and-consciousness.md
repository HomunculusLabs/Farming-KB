## The I/Thou Relationship with the Unknown

McKenna emphasized the importance of approaching the tryptamine experience with what Martin Buber called an "I/Thou" relationship—meeting the unknown on one's feet with dignity and critical intelligence, rather than in genuflection. The entities encountered in the tryptamine state may present themselves as omniscient or from extraterrestrial origins, but the responsible explorer must maintain discernment.

He paraphrased magicians who have always understood that one must enter such encounters "with one's wits about oneself." This stance of engaged skepticism—neither blindly accepting nor reflexively dismissing the content of the experience—is perhaps the most productive approach to the epistemological challenge that tryptamines present.

The problem of distinguishing genuine information from noise in these states is real and significant. McKenna acknowledged that "the demons are of many kinds" and that the head can be "filled with information" that is "incoherent or mad." The challenge is to develop criteria for evaluating the content of psychedelic experience without either succumbing to gullibility or dismissing the entire phenomenon as meaningless.

## Extraterrestrial Communication and Cultural Bias

McKenna critiqued the scientific search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI) as culturally biased, arguing that searching for radio signals from space is "probably as culture-bound a presumption as to search the galaxy for a good Italian restaurant." Meanwhile, people worldwide—psychics, shamans, mystics, schizophrenics—have heads "filled with information" that has been ruled irrelevant a priori.

The tryptamine experience raises the question of whether communication with non-human intelligence might occur through consciousness itself rather than through electromagnetic signals in space. If the entities encountered in the DMT state represent genuine forms of intelligence (whether extraterrestrial, extradimensional, or arising from deep structures of the mind), then the paradigm of interstellar radio communication may be addressing the wrong channel entirely.

This does not mean accepting all psychedelic content as literally true. Rather, it suggests that the phenomenon of apparent communication with non-human intelligence through altered states of consciousness deserves serious scientific investigation rather than categorical dismissal.

## The Stochastic Nature of Experience

McKenna described himself as "an explorer rather than a scientist" because the area of tryptamine research contains "insufficient data to support even the dream of being a science." He compared the situation to early cartographers mapping rivers without ascending them—"we must leave many rivers unascended and thus can say nothing about them."

This Baconian approach—collecting data without premature assumptions about what it might yield—has led McKenna and others to conclusions they did not anticipate. The consistency of certain phenomenological features (entity encounters, the Logos, hyperspace) across different users, cultures, and contexts suggests patterns that any adequate theory of consciousness must account for, whether or not one accepts McKenna's more speculative interpretations.
