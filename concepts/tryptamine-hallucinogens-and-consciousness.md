# Tryptamine Hallucinogens and Consciousness

Terence McKenna's 1983 lecture at the Lilly/Goswami Conference on Consciousness
and Quantum Physics at [[Esalen Institute]] presents a phenomenological
exploration of the tryptamine hallucinogens — dimethyltryptamine ([[DMT]]),
[[psilocybin]], and [[harmala-alkaloids-and-ayahuasca-preparation]] — and their implications for understanding
human consciousness. The talk was later published in *The Archaic Revival*
(1992).

## The Tryptamine Family

The tryptamine hallucinogens are derived from [[tryptophan]] and share a
structural relationship with [[serotonin]] (5-hydroxytryptamine), the major
neurotransmitter in the human brain. [[Psilocybin]] is 4-phosphoraloxy-N,N-
dimethyltryptamine, while [[DMT]] is an endogenous hallucinogen present in
small amounts in the human brain. This structural similarity to serotonin is
likely the basis for their hallucinogenic effects through interference with
normal serotonin signaling.

## Quantum Mechanical Correlates

McKenna argued that hallucinations and dreams are quantum mechanical phenomena.
He cited work by John Smythies and Alexander Shulgin demonstrating that moving
a single atom on the molecular ring of an inactive compound can make it highly
active. This extreme sensitivity of hallucinogenic activity to molecular
structure suggests a dynamic linkage between quantum mechanically described
matter and mind at the formative level.

## DMT Phenomenology

McKenna described the [[DMT]] experience with particular vividness:

- **Onset**: 15 seconds after smoking, with a sound described as "ripping
  cellophane" and an ascending tone
- **Transition**: A mode shift occurring over approximately 30 seconds as all
  available receptor sites become occupied
- **Arrival**: Entry into a space described as underground, insulated, and
  domed, reminiscent of the "merry go raum" from [[Finnegans Wake]]
- **Entities**: Contact with "self-transforming machine elves" — dynamically
  contorting topological modules distinct from the transforming background,
  described as fractal reflections of a previously hidden autonomous part of
  the psyche

The experience invokes what McKenna called the [[Logos]] — direct engagement
with language centers producing interior dialogue with an apparently alien
intelligence that conveys translinguistic information.

## The Logos and Language

A central theme is that [[psilocybin]] and [[DMT]] "invoke the Logos," working
directly on the language centers of the brain. An important aspect of the
experience is glossolalia — not the classical form studied in religious
contexts, but a brain state allowing expression of the assembly language
underlying normal speech. McKenna compared this to Robert Graves' primal
language concept in *The White Goddess* and the Kabbalistic language of the
[[Zohar]].

The tryptamine state may reveal a latent human ability: a form of
three-dimensional language where meaning is beheld rather than heard. McKenna
suggested that with cultural development over thousands of years, such an
ability could become a cultural convenience comparable to mathematics.

## The Stropharia cubensis Hypothesis

McKenna proposed that [[Psilocybe cubensis]] did not evolve on Earth, based
on information received during mushroom experiences. According to this
hypothesis, the mushroom achieved symbiosis with human society through
association with domesticated cattle and nomadic peoples. The classic mushroom
cults of Mexico, where the mushroom was called [[teonanacatl-aztec-codices-archaeology]] ("flesh of the
gods"), were largely destroyed by the Spanish conquest and the Inquisition,
surviving only in a few villages in the mountains of [[Oaxaca]] until
rediscovered by Valentina and Gordon Wasson in the 1950s.

McKenna offered an alternative interpretation: that what appears alien may
actually be the human soul itself, so alienated from modern culture that it is
experienced as extraterrestrial.

## Historical Acceleration and Eschatology

McKenna framed human history as the "shockwave of eschatology" — a drawing
toward a culminating moment. He identified a visible acceleration: fire
discovered millions of years ago, language perhaps 35,000 years ago, measurement
5,000 years ago, Galileo 400 years ago, Watson-Crick and DNA in the modern era.
He interpreted this acceleration as evidence that everything is being drawn
together toward a transformation of consciousness.

## The Black Hole Effect

McKenna described a "black hole effect" surrounding [[DMT]]: once someone
knows about the experience, it becomes very difficult to communicate it to
others. The more articulate one becomes about the phenomenon, the less others
are able to understand — a metaphor for how information cannot escape a black
hole.

## Shamanism

McKenna distinguished shamanism as primarily a set of techniques rather than
rituals. Signs of shamanic potential in archaic societies include unusual
ordeal survival, epilepsy, or near-death experiences. He described shamans as
the true phenomenologists of their world — sophisticated practitioners who
understand plant chemistry while describing energy fields as "spirits" in what
McKenna considered a technical gloss for complex concepts.
