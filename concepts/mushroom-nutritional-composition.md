# Mushroom Nutritional Composition

Mushrooms are nutritionally dense foods that serve as excellent sources of protein, fibre, vitamins, and minerals while maintaining low caloric content. Their nutritional profile makes them valuable both as dietary staples and as functional foods with medicinal properties.

## Macronutrients

### Protein

- Content varies from 10-40% on dry weight basis
- Above most vegetables, somewhat less than most meats and milk
- Contains all essential amino acids
- Can be limiting in sulphur-containing amino acids (cystine, methionine)
- Biological value enhanced by combination with complementary protein sources

### Carbohydrates and Fibre

- Fresh mushrooms contain 3-21% carbohydrates (dry weight)
- 3-35% dietary fibre (dry weight)
- A significant proportion of carbohydrates consist of dietary fibre that cannot be easily digested by humans
- Chitin in cell walls contributes to the fibre content
- Low glycemic impact due to high fibre and low available carbohydrate

### Fat

- Generally low: 2-8% of dry weight
- Contains all main lipid classes: free fatty acids, mono-/di-/triglycerides, sterols, sterol esters, phospholipids
- Low total fat contributes to low caloric value
- Ergosterol (vitamin D precursor) is a notable lipid component

### Ergosterol and Vitamin D

A unique feature of mushrooms is their ergosterol content. Ergosterol is a fungal sterol that converts to active vitamin D₂ (ergocalciferol) when exposed to ultraviolet irradiation. This means:
- Mushrooms can be a significant dietary source of vitamin D when UV-exposed
- Commercial UV-treated mushrooms are increasingly available
- Dried mushrooms exposed to sunlight during processing retain vitamin D content
