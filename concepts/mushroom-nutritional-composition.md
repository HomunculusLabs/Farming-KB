# Mushroom Nutritional Composition Mushrooms are nutritionally dense foods that serve as excellent sources of protein, fibre, vitamins, and minerals while maintaining low caloric content. Their nutritional profile makes them valuable both as dietary staples and as functional foods with medicinal properties. ## Macronutrients ### Protein - Content varies from 10-40% on dry weight basis - Above most vegetables, somewhat less than most meats and milk - Contains all essential amino acids - Can be limiting in sulphur-containing amino acids (cystine, methionine) - Biological value enhanced by combination with complementary protein sources ### Carbohydrates and Fibre - Fresh mushrooms contain 3-21% carbohydrates (dry weight) - 3-35% dietary fibre (dry weight) - A significant proportion of carbohydrates consist of dietary fibre that cannot be easily digested by humans - Chitin in cell walls contributes to the fibre content - Low glycemic impact due to high fibre and low available carbohydrate ### Fat - Generally low: 2-8% of dry weight - Contains all main lipid classes: free fatty acids, mono-/di-/triglycerides, sterols, sterol esters, phospholipids - Low total fat contributes to low caloric value - Ergosterol (vitamin D precursor) is a notable lipid component ## Vitamins | Vitamin | Presence | Notes | |---------|----------|-------| | Thiamine (B1) | Good source | Varies by species | | Riboflavin (B2) | Good source | Varies by species | | Niacin (B3) | Good source | Varies by species | | Biotin (B7) | Present | Varies by species | | Ascorbic acid (C) | Detectable amounts | Varies by species | | Vitamin A | Uncommon | Some species contain β-carotene | | Vitamin D | From ergosterol | Converted by UV irradiation | ### Ergosterol and Vitamin D A unique feature of mushrooms is their ergosterol content. Ergosterol is a fungal sterol that converts to active vitamin D₂ (ergocalciferol) when exposed to ultraviolet irradiation. ## Mineral Content

Mushrooms are notable sources of several essential minerals,
with concentrations often exceeding those found in vegetables:
- **Selenium** — certain species accumulate selenium to
  levels comparable to Brazil nuts. Button mushrooms can
  provide over 20% of daily selenium requirements per
  serving, supporting antioxidant enzyme function and
  thyroid hormone metabolism in the human body.
- **Potassium** — mushrooms contain more potassium per
  calorie than bananas. Adequate potassium intake supports
  cardiovascular health, blood pressure regulation, and
  proper muscle and nerve function throughout the body.
- **Copper and zinc** — essential [[dynamic-accumulator-plants-trace-minerals]] involved
  in immune function, [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]], and enzyme
  systems. Mushroom copper content contributes to iron
  metabolism and connective tissue formation pathways.
- **Ergothioneine** — a unique antioxidant found almost
  exclusively in mushrooms. This amino acid derivative
  accumulates in human tissues and provides cellular
  protection against [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]] and inflammation,
  with potential implications for longevity research.

## Species-Specific Nutritional Profiles

Nutritional composition varies considerably across commonly
cultivated and wild mushroom species:
- **Shiitake** — particularly rich in B vitamins, copper,
  and the polysaccharide lentinan. Contains eritadenine,
  a compound that may support healthy cholesterol levels
  through effects on lipid metabolism in the liver.
- **Oyster mushrooms** — high in beta-glucans, protein
  relative to other cultivated species, and several
  statin-like compounds. Among the easiest species to
  cultivate at home on diverse [[growing-gourmet-substrate-materials-wood-types]].
- **Maitake** — notable for its D-fraction beta-glucan
  content and relatively high protein concentration.
  Contains compounds that may support healthy blood
  sugar regulation through insulin sensitization pathways.
- **Button and portobello** — the most consumed mushrooms
  globally. When UV-exposed, they become excellent
  vitamin D sources. Good all-around nutritional profiles
  with high B vitamin and mineral content per serving.

## Bioactive Compounds Beyond Nutrition

Mushrooms contain non-nutritive compounds with significant
health-promoting biological activity:
- **Beta-glucans** — the primary immunomodulatory compounds
  in mushrooms. These polysaccharides activate innate
  immune cells and are the basis for many medicinal
  mushroom preparations used in complementary medicine.
- **Ergothioneine and glutathione** — mushrooms are the
  premier dietary source of these two antioxidants. Both
  compounds protect cellular components from oxidative
  damage and decline with age, making regular mushroom
  consumption relevant to healthy aging strategies.
- **Triterpenoids** — found primarily in [[growing-gourmet-polypore-mushrooms-cultivation-overview]]
  like reishi and turkey tail. These compounds exhibit
  anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, and potential anti-
  tumor activities through multiple molecular pathways.

## Culinary Preparation and Nutrient Retention

How mushrooms are prepared affects both nutrient
availability and the concentration of bioactive compounds:
- **Cooking improves digestibility** — heat breaks down
  chitin in cell walls, releasing trapped nutrients and
  beta-glucans that would otherwise pass through the
  digestive system unavailable to the human body.
