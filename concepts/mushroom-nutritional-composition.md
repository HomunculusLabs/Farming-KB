# Mushroom Nutritional Composition

Mushrooms are nutritionally dense foods that serve as excellent sources of protein, fibre, vitamins, and minerals while maintaining low caloric content. Their nutritional profile makes them valuable both as dietary staples and as functional foods with medicinal properties.

## Macronutrients

### Protein

- Content varies from 10-40% on dry weight basis
- Above most vegetables, somewhat less than most meats and milk
- Contains all essential amino acids
- Can be limiting in sulphur-containing amino acids (cystine, methionine)
- Biological value enhanced by combination with complementary protein sources

### Carbohydrates and Fibre

- Fresh mushrooms contain 3-21% carbohydrates (dry weight)
- 3-35% dietary fibre (dry weight)
- A significant proportion of carbohydrates consist of dietary fibre that cannot be easily digested by humans
- Chitin in cell walls contributes to the fibre content
- Low glycemic impact due to high fibre and low available carbohydrate

### Fat

- Generally low: 2-8% of dry weight
- Contains all main lipid classes: free fatty acids, mono-/di-/triglycerides, sterols, sterol esters, phospholipids
- Low total fat contributes to low caloric value
- Ergosterol (vitamin D precursor) is a notable lipid component

## Vitamins

| Vitamin | Presence | Notes |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Thiamine (B1) | Good source | Varies by species |
| Riboflavin (B2) | Good source | Varies by species |
| Niacin (B3) | Good source | Varies by species |
| Biotin (B7) | Present | Varies by species |
| Ascorbic acid (C) | Detectable amounts | Varies by species |
| Vitamin A | Uncommon | Some species contain β-carotene |
| Vitamin D | From ergosterol | Converted by UV irradiation |

### Ergosterol and Vitamin D

A unique feature of mushrooms is their ergosterol content. Ergosterol is a fungal sterol that converts to active vitamin D₂ (ergocalciferol) when exposed to ultraviolet irradiation. This means:
- Mushrooms can be a significant dietary source of vitamin D when UV-exposed
- Commercial UV-treated mushrooms are increasingly available
- Dried mushrooms exposed to sunlight during processing retain vitamin D content

## Minerals

Mushrooms contain virtually every mineral present in their growth substrate:
- **Phosphorus** — substantial quantities
- **Potassium** — substantial quantities
- **Calcium** — lesser amounts
- **Iron** — lesser amounts
- **Selenium** — some species are excellent sources (notably [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]])
- **Copper**, **zinc**, **manganese** — present in trace amounts

## Caloric Value

The combination of high protein, high fibre, and low fat gives most mushrooms a low caloric value. This makes them ideal for:
- Weight management diets
- Diabetic diets (low glycemic impact)
- Vegetarian and vegan diets (protein and B vitamin source)

## Species-Specific Nutritional Highlights

- **Shiitake** — high in B vitamins, good protein content, ergothioneine (antioxidant)
- **Oyster mushroom** — high in β-glucans, good protein, lovastatin (cholesterol-lowering)
- **Maitake** — rich in β-glucans, potassium, and vitamin D precursor
- **Reishi** — not typically consumed as food (too tough/bitter) but valued for extracts
- **Enoki** — excellent selenium source
- **Button mushroom (Agaricus bisporus)** — good general nutrition, widely available

## Culinary and Nutritional Synergies

The Chinese concept of "Yakuzen" refers to medicinal food dishes incorporating mushrooms. This tradition recognizes that the nutritional and therapeutic properties of mushrooms are best utilized when:
- Combined with other nutrient-dense ingredients
- Prepared using methods that enhance bioavailability (long cooking for polysaccharides)
- Consumed regularly rather than occasionally
- Paired with complementary foods (e.g., vitamin C-rich vegetables to enhance absorption)
