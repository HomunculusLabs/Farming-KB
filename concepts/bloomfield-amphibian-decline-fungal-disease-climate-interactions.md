### The Climate-Disease Chain

The Nature study was significant not only for identifying the pathogen but for elucidating the environmental chain of causation linking climate change to amphibian mortality:

1. **Global warming**: Warming of the Pacific Ocean altered regional weather patterns.
2. **Decreased rainfall**: Changes in precipitation reduced the depth of high-elevation breeding ponds.
3. **UV-B exposure**: Shallower water exposed eggs to higher levels of ultraviolet-B radiation (the wavelengths responsible for skin cancer in humans). Ozone thinning may have intensified this effect.
4. **Immune compromise**: UV-B damage to egg membranes weakened the eggs' natural defenses against microbial colonization.
5. **Fungal infection**: Saprolegnia ferax, a ubiquitous water mold normally held in check by intact egg defenses, colonized and killed the compromised eggs.

### Anaerobic Chytrids in the Rumen

Not all chytrids are pathogens. Anaerobic chytrids inhabit the rumen — the largest of the four stomach chambers — of large herbivores including cattle. Poisoned by atmospheric oxygen, these chytrids thrive in the fermentative soup of the rumen, where they decompose plant fiber (cellulose). By altering the chemical composition of plant material, anaerobic chytrids facilitate the growth of other microorganisms critical to digestion, including the methanogenic bacteria responsible for methane production. This food-processing role parallels the ecological function of Ingoldian fungi in forest streams — both groups of fungi break down refractory plant material in specialized habitats.

### Chytrids as Insect Pathogens and Algae Parasites

Chytrid species also infect mosquito larvae and other aquatic insects, algae, and even other fungi including oomycetes. This ecological breadth reflects the ancient evolutionary history of the Chytridiomycota, which are believed to be the earliest diverging lineage of true fungi. Their flagellated zoospores, single smooth flagellum, and simple thallus organization represent the ancestral condition from which all more complex fungal body plans evolved.

The chytrid's role as a parasite of mosquito larvae has attracted interest from public health researchers, as it raises the possibility of [[mycelium-running-conservation-biology-and-fungi]] and [[bloomfield-saprolegnia-water-mold-zoospore-discharge]] are genuine pathogens, but their impact is amplified by environmental changes that stress host populations and compromise their immune defenses. Conservation efforts must therefore address both the proximate cause (the pathogen itself) and the ultimate causes (climate change, habitat degradation, and other environmental stressors) that create conditions favorable for disease emergence — challenges explored in fungal biodiversity and conservation.
