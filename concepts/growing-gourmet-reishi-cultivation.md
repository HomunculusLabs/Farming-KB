# Reishi Cultivation

Reishi ([[metzner-three-distinctive-features-shamanic-entheogenic-ceremonies]] of Reishi cultivation is the ability to control morphology through environmental conditions, particularly CO2 concentration:

### Antler Growth (High CO2)
- Maintained at elevated CO2 levels (above 1,000 ppm)
- Produces elongated, antler-like structures with minimal cap development
- The antler form is preferred in some Asian markets for its concentrated medicinal compounds
- Stems grow upward in search of fresh air, similar to etiolation in plants
- Requires careful management of the growing environment to maintain consistent high CO2

### Cap Growth (Low CO2)
- Requires reduction of CO2 below 1,000 ppm with adequate fresh air exchange
- Produces the classic "conk" or bracket-like fruitbody with a distinct cap
- The familiar kidney or fan-shaped Reishi with a lacquered, varnished appearance
- Cap surface develops the characteristic reddish-brown to deep mahogany color
- Produces spores from the underside of the cap when mature

### Tradeoffs
The choice between antler and cap cultivation involves tradeoffs in yield, morphology, market preference, and possibly medicinal compound profile. Antler growth is generally considered to produce a more concentrated product per unit weight, while cap growth yields greater total biomass. Some cultivators grow both forms sequentially, starting with high CO2 for antler development and then reducing CO2 to trigger cap formation.

## Fruiting Parameters

Reishi demonstrates strong photosensitivity, similar to Oyster and Shiitake mushrooms. Light is required for proper [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]] substrates, it develops a thick, durable mycelial mat that binds the substrate firmly.

## Substrate Preferences

Reishi grows best on hardwood substrates, particularly oak, maple, and similar species. Supplementation with rice bran or wheat bran at approximately 20% of dry substrate weight significantly improves yields. The addition of gypsum (calcium sulfate) at 5% aids in substrate structure and provides essential minerals. Moisture content should target 60-70% for optimal colonization and fruiting.

## Harvesting and Processing

Reishi fruitbodies are harvested when fully mature, typically after 2-3 months of growth from initiation. The mature mushroom develops a hard, woody texture that makes it unsuitable for fresh consumption in the manner of gourmet mushrooms. Instead, Reishi is almost exclusively processed for medicinal use:

- Drying and grinding into powder for teas and extracts
- Hot water extraction for polysaccharide concentrates
- [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] include:
- Beta-glucans (polysaccharides) with immunomodulatory activity
- Triterpenoids (ganoderic acids) with anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective effects
- Ganoderma lucidum peptide with antioxidant properties
- Adenosine and other nucleotide compounds

Traditional uses include immune system support, fatigue reduction, liver protection, and cardiovascular health. Modern research has validated many of these traditional applications through clinical and preclinical studies.
