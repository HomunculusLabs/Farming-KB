### Spent Substrate Cascades

After mushroom fruiting and harvest, the substrate undergoes further transformations rather than becoming waste:

1. Livestock feed — spent substrate with increased mycelial protein content becomes palatable and nutritious animal feed, improving growth rates and meat quality
2. vermicomposting|Vermicomposting — earthworms convert spent substrate into nutrient-rich natural fertilizer and soil conditioner
3. Bread production — spent grain is incorporated into bread to increase food output, demonstrating "value added" at each processing stage
4. Organic compost — substrate becomes excellent compost for plant and vegetable crops, turning a waste product into a valuable resource

## SCZ-NM Forest Material Cascades

The Sustainable Communities/ZERI-New Mexico projects extend the cascade model to forest ecosystems. Forest thinning to reduce wildfire hazard generates woody debris that becomes substrate for mushroom cultivation. After fruiting, the mycelium-enriched substrate feeds livestock, earthworms, or fish. The final output—vermicompost—improves soil fertility for crop production.
