# Hybrid Shamanic Therapeutic Rituals

In his 1998 framework, Ralph Metzner describes the emergence of hybrid
shamanic-therapeutic circle rituals as a fourth paradigm of hallucinogen
use, blending Western psychotherapy with traditional shamanic practices.
Metzner's own research focused on what he calls "neoshamanic medicine
circles," representing a synthesis of indigenous healing traditions and
modern psychospiritual approaches.

## Defining the Hybrid Paradigm

Modern Western settings feature a wide spectrum of entheogenic practices,
from casual recreational use to large-scale rave events combining MDMA
with techno music. Metzner distinguishes the hybrid therapeutic-shamanic
circles by their deliberate integration of ceremonial structure,
experienced facilitation, therapeutic intention, and spiritual reverence
drawn from both traditions.

In the decade preceding the paper, Metzner participated in and observed
over 100 such circle rituals in Europe and North America, involving
several hundred participants, many repeatedly. Plant entheogens used
included psilocybe mushrooms, [[ayahuasca-preparation-and-ceremony]],
San Pedro cactus, iboga-plant|iboga, and others. His research focused
on the nature of psychospiritual transformation undergone by participants.

## Core Ceremonial Elements

Metzner identifies basic elements from traditional shamanic healing that
are typically preserved in hybrid circles:

- **Circle structure**: Participants sit or lie in a circle, the
  universal communal ritual form across indigenous traditions.
- **Central altar or fire**: An altar (indoors) or fire (outside or
  in tipi) serves as a focal point for the group's attention.
- **Experienced guide**: An elder or guide, sometimes with assistants,
  holds the ceremonial space and attends to participants' needs.
- **Low light**: Preference for semidarkness or eye-shades to
  facilitate inner visions.
- **Music**: Drumming, rattling, singing, or recorded music provides
  rhythmic structure and supports the flow of experience.
- **Invocation**: Dedication of ritual space through calling in spirits
  of the four directions and elements, creating a sacred container.
- **Spiritual attitude**: Cultivation of a respectful, reverent
  approach to the medicines and the experience.

## The Talking Staff Practice

A distinctive feature of many hybrid ceremonies is the talking staff or
singing staff. This practice, originating among the Indians of the
Pacific Northwest and also known as "council," establishes a
non-interpretive communication format.

When the talking staff is used, only the person holding the circulating
staff may sing or speak. Crucially, there is no discussion, questioning,
or interpretation of what is shared, as there might be in standard group
psychotherapy formats. This creates a safe container where participants
express experiences without analysis or judgment.

This contrasts sharply with typical therapeutic group dynamics, where
facilitators actively engage with and interpret participants' sharing.
The talking staff preserves the shamanic emphasis on direct experience
while allowing communal witnessing and support.

## Participant Profile

Most participants in the circles Metzner observed were experienced in
one or more psychospiritual practices: [[ceremonial-shamanic-use-psychoactive-mushrooms]], Buddhist vipassana meditation, tantra yoga, and
holotropic breathwork. Most had also practiced
various forms of psychotherapy and body-oriented therapy.

Experienced entheogenic explorers understand [[psychedelic-mushroom-set-and-setting]] and devote considerable attention to clarifying intentions
and arranging a peaceful place free from distractions. The insights from
these various practices are woven into their work with plant medicines.
Participants confirm that entheogenic medicines, when combined with
meditative or therapeutic insight processes, function to amplify
awareness and sensitize perception, particularly somatic, emotional, and
instinctual awareness.

## Preparation and Integration Practices

In preparation for the circle ritual, participants share intentions and
purposes. This preparatory phase may include meditation, solo time in
nature, or expressive arts modalities such as drawing, painting, or
journal work. Preparation helps clarify healing intentions and enter the
ceremony in a centered, receptive state.

After the ritual, sometimes the following morning, there is usually an
integration practice. Participants share lessons learned and how they
plan to apply them in their lives. This bridges ceremonial experience
with everyday life, paralleling the emphasis on integration in modern
[[psychedelic-therapy-current-research]].

## Worldview Transformation

Metzner observes that most Westerners who develop an ongoing entheogenic
practice expand their belief systems beyond the conventional materialistic
paradigm. While accepting Western psychological insights from Freud,
Jung, and Reich, they come to accept the reality of nonmaterial spirit
beings and multiple worlds of consciousness, paralleling indigenous and
esoteric traditions.

He suggests Western psychology may be returning to William James's
observation that normal waking consciousness is but one type, and that
"no account of the universe in its totality can be final which leaves
these other forms of consciousness quite disregarded."

## Distinction from Other Paradigms

The hybrid paradigm differs from standard Western psychotherapy in its
ritual elements, spiritual invocation, the talking staff format, and
acceptance of nonordinary realities. It differs from traditional shamanic
practice in drawing on Western psychological insights and therapeutic
preparation and integration. It differs from [[metzner-syncretic-entheogenic-folk-ceremonies]] in its smaller scale, greater emphasis on
individual healing, and integration of psychotherapeutic elements.
