## Four Paradigms of Psychoactive Use

Metzner organized his comparative analysis around four distinct paradigms for the therapeutic or healing use of psychoactive substances.

The first paradigm is psychedelic psychotherapy within the standard Western model, where the drug amplifies and intensifies internal self-analysis and self-understanding.

The second is traditional shamanic ritual, where the shaman takes the medicine to "see" the causes of illness and determine remedies.

They are guided by individuals who may have training in both Western psychotherapy and indigenous shamanic practice, or who function as bridges between these two worlds.

The therapeutic goals are broader than those of standard Western psychotherapy, encompassing not only symptom relief and psychological insight but also spiritual growth, connection to nature, and a sense of meaning and purpose.

Participants are often prepared through specific dietary restrictions, intention-setting practices, and educational sessions about what to expect — a practice drawn from both shamanic traditions and modern psychedelic therapy preparation guidelines.

## The Worldview Question

Metzner identified two fundamental points where the worldview of shamanic and hybrid ceremonies differs radically from the accepted Western worldview.

The first is the belief in [[metzner-amazonian-ayahuasca-ceremonies-icaros-and-healing-methods]] conducted by various [[entheogenic-syncretic-churches-brazil]] in Brazil and elsewhere represent another form of hybrid practice.

The Santo Daime and União do Vegetal churches combine Amazonian shamanic practices with Christian theology, African spiritual traditions, and elements of Western esotericism.

These churches have been the subject of legal proceedings in several countries, testing the boundaries of religious freedom and drug policy.

Their ceremonies involve the ritual consumption of ayahuasca in a context that includes music, prayer, and community — elements drawn from both indigenous and Western traditions.

## Therapeutic Mechanisms

The therapeutic mechanisms of hybrid rituals differ from those of standard Western psychotherapy in several important ways.

Standard psychotherapy operates primarily through verbal communication and cognitive insight. The patient talks about their problems, and the therapist helps them understand and restructure their patterns of thinking and behavior.

Hybrid rituals operate through multiple channels simultaneously: the pharmacological effects of the psychoactive substance, the emotional effects of music and chanting, the social effects of group ceremony, and the spiritual effects of engaging with non-ordinary realities.

This multi-channel approach may be particularly effective for conditions that resist standard psychotherapeutic treatment, such as addiction, trauma, and existential distress.

The communal dimension of hybrid rituals is especially significant.

In standard psychotherapy, the therapeutic relationship is dyadic — between therapist and patient. This intimate setting has strengths but also limitations.

In hybrid rituals, the therapeutic context is communal, drawing on the healing power of shared experience, collective intention, and group support.
