# Cannabis Flowering and Photoperiodism: Clarke's Analysis

Robert Connell Clarke's "[[clarke-marijuana-botany-cannabinoid-biosynthesis-pathway]]" provides a detailed botanical description of the Cannabis life cycle, with particular emphasis on the photoperiodic control of flowering -- the critical transition from [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] to reproductive development that determines the quality and timing of the harvest.

## The Cannabis Life Cycle

Cannabis is a **tall, erect, annual herb** that can grow to 6 meters (about 20 feet) in a 4-6 month [[extending-growing-season]] given adequate sunlight, well-drained soil, and irrigation. The life cycle proceeds through distinct phases:

### Germination and Seedling Stage

Seeds germinate in 3 to 7 days. The seedling emerges via straightening of the hypocotyl (embryonic stem), 1-10 cm long. Cotyledons (seed leaves) are slightly unequal, narrowed at the base and rounded at the tip. Subsequent leaf pairs develop increasing numbers of leaflets: the first pair has 1 leaflet each, the second pair 3, the third 5, up to 11 leaflets. If not crowded, limbs grow from buds at petiole intersections along the main stem.

### Juvenile Vegetative Stage

During the first 2-3 months, Cannabis responds to increasing daylength with vigorous [[green-cannabis-vegetative-growth]]. Under favorable conditions, plants can grow up to 7 cm per day during long summer days. This stage is characterized by opposite (decussate) phyllotaxy.

## Photoperiodic Control of Flowering

Cannabis exhibits a **dual response to daylength**:

- **[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-vegetative-phase-management]]**: The plant responds to increasing daylength with more vigorous growth during the first 2-3 months.
- **Reproductive phase**: The plant requires **shorter days** (or equivalently, longer uninterrupted dark periods) to initiate flowering and complete its life cycle.

### Critical Daylength

Different strains have different critical daylengths. The concept applies only to plants that fail to flower under continuous illumination -- those that flower under continuous light have no critical daylength. Most strains have an absolute requirement for inductive photoperiods (short days or long nights) to produce fertile flowers.

### Timing of Primordia Formation

The time to form flower primordia varies dramatically with photoperiod length:
- At **10 hours of light per day**: a strain may flower in approximately 10 days
- At **16 hours per day**: the same strain may take up to 90 days
- Below **8 hours per day**: no further acceleration of primordia formation occurs

### Dark Period Requirements

Dark (night) cycles **must be uninterrupted** to induce flowering. Even brief light interruptions during the dark period can prevent or delay floral induction. This is the physiological basis for light-leak problems in [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]].

## Sexual Differentiation

Cannabis is normally **dioecious** -- male and female flowers develop on separate plants -- though monoecious individuals (both sexes on one plant) occur.

### First Signs of Flowering

The initial sign is the appearance of **undifferentiated flower primordia** along the main stem at the nodes, behind the stipules. In the prefloral phase, sexes are indistinguishable except for general shape trends.

### Male (Staminate) Flowers

Males are identified by curved claw-shaped primordia, soon followed by rounded pointed flower buds with five radial segments. At maturity:
- Flowers hang in long, loose, multi-branched clusters up to 30 cm long
- Five petals (~5 mm) form the calyx (yellow, white, or green)
- Five stamens (~5 mm) emerge with slender anthers splitting upward
- Pollen grains are nearly spherical, slightly yellow, 25-30 microns in diameter
- Exterior surface has non-glandular trichomes
- Males tend to flower up to one month earlier than females

### Female (Pistillate) Flowers

Females are recognized by symmetrical tubular calyx enlargement. At maturity:
- Two long white, yellow, or pink pistils protrude from a thin membranous calyx
- Calyx is covered with **glandular trichomes** (resin-producing hairs)
- Flowers are borne in pairs at nodes, concealed by bracts
- Calyx measures 2-6 mm in length and completely contains the ovary
- Females tend to be shorter with more branches than males
- Females remain leafy to the top with many leaves surrounding the flowers

### Phyllotaxy Changes

Before flowering begins, leaf arrangement reverses from decussate (opposite) to alternate (staggered), and the number of leaflets per leaf decreases until a small single leaflet appears below each pair of calyxes. This change persists throughout the floral stages regardless of sex.

## Sex Determination and Environmental Factors

Under normal conditions with proper inductive photoperiod, Cannabis produces approximately equal numbers of pure staminate and pure pistillate plants, with a few hermaphrodites. However, sex ratios can be dramatically altered by:

- **Nutrient excess or deficiency**
- **Mutilation** (pruning stress)
- **Altered [[cannabis-photoperiodism-and-light-cycles]]** (extremely short 6-8 hour photoperiods can cause [[cervantes-ethylene-hormonal-sex-reversal]])
- **Chemical intervention**: Gibberellic acid (GA3) promotes stamen production; indoleacetic acid (IAA), ethrel, and kinetin promote pistil production
