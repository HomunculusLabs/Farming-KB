# Honey, Mead, and the Dionysian Cult

McKenna traced the ancient relationship between honey, mead, and the Dionysian
mysteries as a critical link in the devolution from the original mushroom cults
of the [[mckenna-dominator-vs-partnership-society-model]] to the alcohol-based religions of the dominator
culture. Honey served as both a preservative for [[mexican-sacred-mushrooms-indigenous-use-and-ethnobotany]] and, when
fermented, as mead — the first recreational intoxicant of the Indo-European
tribes.

## Honey as Sacred Substance

Honey is a magical substance in virtually all traditional cultures. Its medicinal
properties, its association with the divine, and its role in preserving both
human bodies and mushrooms made it central to ancient religious practice.
McKenna noted several connections between honey and [[allegro-the-sacred-mushroom-amanita-identification]] cults:

- Honey was used to preserve [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]] for ceremonial use,
  maintaining their potency between seasons.
- Honey was used to preserve human bodies in mummification practices, linking
  the idea of bodily preservation with the preservation of sacred knowledge.
- The belief that bees were generated from cattle carcasses makes more sense
  if seen as connecting bees (source of honey and mead) with cattle and the
  older mushroom cult — an effort to link the supplanting intoxicant with
  the original one.

## The Minoan Connection

Among the Minoan civilization of Crete, honey occupied a central position in
religious life. One of the most astonishing murals unearthed at Çatal Hüyük
apparently depicts the life cycle and metamorphosis of honeybees. This suggests
that [[faires-beekeeping-and-honey-production]] cultivation were among the earliest forms of
agricultural practice, predating even cereal cultivation.

Mycenaean gold rings from Isopata near Knossos depict bee-headed dancing
goddesses — figures with the heads and hands of insects. These images, drawn
from Marija Gimbutas's work on the Goddesses and Gods of Old Europe, suggest
that honey and the bee were associated with divine feminine power and ecstatic
dance.

Kerenyi, speaking of honey offerings in Minoan religion, observed that "the
honey offering given to the 'mistress of the labyrinth' carries the style of a
much earlier period: that stage in which Minoan culture was still in contact
with an 'age of honey.'" This "age of honey" preceded the age of wine and the
age of grain, and may have been contemporaneous with the original mushroom
cults.

## The Dionysian Honey Motif

Dionysus, the god of ecstasy and wine, was surrounded by honey symbolism:

- Dionysus was said by the Roman poet Ovid to have invented honey.
- The sacred ground where the maenads performed their ritual dance was said to
  have flowed with milk, wine, and the "Nectar of bees."
- Honey dripped from the thyrsos staffs carried by the maenads.
- The thyrsos itself — a fennel stalk topped with a pinecone — may have
  originally been a honey-drip device used in mushroom preservation.

The connection between Dionysus and honey suggests that the Dionysian cult
originated in an earlier honey-mead-mushroom complex, only later becoming
associated with wine as grape cultivation spread through the Mediterranean.

## The Devolution of Sacraments

McKenna proposed that each intoxicant in the ancient Near East represented a
paler imitation of the original mushroom sacrament:

1. **Mushrooms** — the original plant of the Goddess, producing full visionary
   ecstasy and boundary dissolution.
2. **Fermented honey (mead)** — a gentler intoxicant that preserved the social
   bonding function but lacked the visionary intensity.
3. **[[bionutrients-lab-fermented-fruit-juice-ffj]] juices (wine)** — a further dilution, producing
   disinhibition without ecstasy.
4. **Distilled alcohol** — the final degradation: a toxic, addictive substance
   that produces stupefaction rather than insight.

This devolution followed the broader historical transition from partnership
society to dominator society. As the Goddess was displaced by male gods, the
visionary mushroom was displaced by the stupefying cup of wine. Ecstasy gave
way to intoxication. Community bonding gave way to hierarchical authority.
