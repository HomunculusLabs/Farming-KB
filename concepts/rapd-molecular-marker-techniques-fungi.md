# RAPD and Molecular Marker Techniques for Fungi
Random Amplified Polymorphic DNA (RAPD) and related molecular marker techniques
provide powerful tools for [[aspergillus-fumigatus-population-genetics]], and diversity
assessment. These PCR-based methods require no prior knowledge of DNA sequence
and can generate genetic fingerprints from small amounts of tissue.
## Arbitrarily Primed PCR (apPCR/RAPD)
RAPD analysis (also called arbitrarily primed PCR or single-primer PCR) uses
short oligonucleotide primers (10-20 bp) that anneal to complementary DNA
sequences in arbitrary locations throughout the genome. When complementary
sequences are found in opposite orientation on separate strands, a
double-stranded PCR product is generated.

## RFLP Analysis
Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism analysis involves digestion of
PCR-amplified DNA (or genomic DNA) with restriction enzymes, separation of
fragments by gel electrophoresis, and visualization of banding patterns. RFLP
of ribosomal DNA regions (ITS, IGS) was historically important for
distinguishing among fungal strains and species. However, RFLP data have
limited phylogenetic resolution compared to sequence data.
RFLP remains useful for rapid screening of known polymorphisms in fungal
populations, particularly when large numbers of isolates need to be
characterized for specific genetic markers identified through prior sequencing
efforts.
