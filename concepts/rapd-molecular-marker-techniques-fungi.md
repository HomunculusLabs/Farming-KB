# RAPD and Molecular Marker Techniques for Fungi

Random Amplified Polymorphic DNA (RAPD) and related molecular marker techniques provide powerful tools for fungal taxonomy, population genetics, and diversity assessment. These PCR-based methods require no prior knowledge of DNA sequence and can generate genetic fingerprints from small amounts of tissue.

## Arbitrarily Primed PCR (apPCR/RAPD)

RAPD analysis (also called arbitrarily primed PCR or single-primer PCR) uses short oligonucleotide primers (10-20 bp) that anneal to complementary DNA sequences in arbitrary locations throughout the genome. When complementary sequences are found in opposite orientation on separate strands, a double-stranded PCR product is generated.

### Applications of RAPD in Mycology
- **Taxonomic discrimination**: distinguishing among fungal taxa with few morphological characters
- **Population genetics**: assessing genetic diversity within and between fungal populations
- **Strain identification**: differentiating among isolates of the same species
- **Monitoring species**: tracking temporal and/or spatial patterns of fungal populations

### Advantages of RAPD
- Requires no prior sequence knowledge of the target organism
- Uses very small amounts of starting material (single spores or small tissue pieces)
- Relatively rapid and inexpensive compared to DNA sequencing
- Can accommodate large numbers of samples in a short period
- Applicable to any organism without species-specific primer design

### Limitations of RAPD
- Reproducibility can be affected by reaction conditions (DNA concentration, Mg2+ concentration, thermal cycler, Taq polymerase)
- Dominant markers: cannot distinguish heterozygous from homozygous dominant genotypes
- Results require comparisons to apPCR patterns from known taxa for species identification
- Non-orthologous bands may confound comparisons between distant taxa

## Dual-Primer PCR (dpPCR)

Dual-primer PCR uses two arbitrary primers simultaneously in a single reaction, increasing the number of amplified fragments and potentially providing greater discriminatory power than single-primer RAPD. This approach can generate more complex but more informative banding patterns.

## Nested-Primer PCR (npPCR)

Nested-primer PCR (Haff 1994) uses a second set of primers internal to the first amplification product, increasing specificity and sensitivity. This is particularly valuable when:
- Starting DNA is limited or degraded
- Non-specific amplification is problematic
- Detection of specific genetic markers within complex genomes is required

## Allozyme Analysis

Allozyme analysis was the first widely used molecular technique for assessing fungal genetic diversity:
- Screens functional enzymes for differences in electrophoretic mobility due to amino acid substitutions
- These reflect mutations in genomic DNA
- Limitations: only assesses functional proteins, so the number of detectable polymorphisms is limited
- Environmental conditions influence gene expression, so morphological and physiological variation may not reflect genetic variation at the DNA level

## AFLP (Amplified Fragment Length Polymorphism)

AFLP combines restriction enzyme digestion with PCR amplification:
- Genomic DNA is digested with two restriction enzymes
- Adapters are ligated to the restriction fragments
- Selective PCR amplification using adapter-specific primers with selective nucleotides
- Generates highly reproducible, multi-locus fingerprints
- More reproducible than RAPD but more technically demanding

AFLP has been used to assess genetic diversity in fungal populations, including studies of Laccaria and other ectomycorrhizal fungi.

## RFLP Analysis

Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism analysis involves:
- Digestion of PCR-amplified DNA (or genomic DNA) with restriction enzymes
- Separation of fragments by gel electrophoresis
- Visualization of banding patterns

RFLP of ribosomal DNA regions (ITS, IGS) was historically important for distinguishing among fungal strains and species. However, RFLP data have limited phylogenetic resolution compared to sequence data.

## PCR-Based Species Detection

Species-specific PCR primers can be designed from known DNA sequences to amplify 200-300 bp regions for genetic analysis (Erlich 1992). This approach enables:
- Rapid detection and identification of target species in environmental samples
- Quantitative assessment through real-time PCR (qPCR)
- Monitoring of specific fungal pathogens or biocontrol agents in field settings

## Choosing a Molecular Method

The choice of method depends on the research question:
- **Species identification**: ITS sequencing is the gold standard
- **Population genetics**: RAPD, AFLP, or microsatellites
- **Phylogenetics**: multi-gene DNA sequencing
- **Rapid screening**: RAPD or species-specific PCR
- **Environmental detection**: qPCR or metabarcoding
