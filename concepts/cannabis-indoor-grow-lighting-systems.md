# Cannabis Indoor Grow Lighting Systems

Lighting is the single most critical environmental factor in [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-strain-selection-genetics]].

## Light Spectrum and Plant Requirements

Light can be separated into a spectrum forming the colors of the rainbow: red,
orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet. Each color represents a
different wavelength of electromagnetic radiation. Plants appear green because
they reflect green light while absorbing light primarily in the middle of the
spectrum, from orange through indigo. Cannabis plants need this portion of the
light spectrum to reach full growing potential.

The intensity of light is equally important. Sunlight provides the full
bandwidth of all light spectrums and is the ideal reference point. When
dealing with artificial lights, growers should choose systems that concentrate
their intensity in the portions of the spectrum most useful to plants. The
electromagnetic spectrum describes the distribution of electromagnetic
radiation by reference to energy, providing wavelengths, frequencies, and
energy levels for each color band.

## Poor Lighting Systems

### Domestic Incandescent Bulbs

Standard household light bulbs come in sizes from 15 to 150 watts. These are
not suitable for growing Cannabis because of their low light intensity and poor
color spectrum. Incandescent bulbs produce mostly red and infrared light with
very little blue, and their efficiency is extremely low, converting most
electrical energy into heat rather than usable light.

### Halogen Lights

Halogen lights are the small floodlights often seen outside factories. They
range from 75 watts to 4000 watts. Halogens get extremely hot and create
unsuitable conditions for indoor [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-flowering-phase-and-harvest]]. MH lights tend to give off a blue tone while HPS gives off an orange
tone, making them visually distinguishable.

## Choosing Between MH and HPS

Some growers maintain a dual-system approach, using MH lighting for seedling
and [[bloomfield-fungal-life-cycle-spiral-versus-circle]].

When purchasing a lighting system, growers should examine the ballast and
bulb to identify whether the system is MH or HPS. The type is usually printed
on the ballast housing or the bulb itself.
