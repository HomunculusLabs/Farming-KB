# Pleurotus tuber-regium - The African Sclerotial Oyster Mushroom

## Overview

Pleurotus tuber-regium (Fr.) Singer is a tropical Oyster mushroom species
notable for its ability to form large, underground sclerotia -- hardened
masses of mycelial tissue that serve as nutrient storage organs. Native to
West Africa, particularly Nigeria, this species plays an important role in
traditional medicine and local cuisine. The sclerotia can reach several
inches in diameter and have a wood-like texture, resembling a hardened
tuber. According to Singer (1986), native peoples use the sclerotia for
diverse medicinal purposes including treatment of stomach pain,
constipation, fever, high blood pressure, and even smallpox.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Pleurotus tuber-regium was originally described as Agaricus tuber-regium
by Fries. It was later transferred to the genus Pleurotus by Singer.
The species epithet "tuber-regium" (royal tuber) refers to the
impressively large sclerotia it produces. This species is one of the
few Oyster mushrooms that reliably forms sclerotia, placing it in a
category with species like Collybia tuberosa, [[gadd-mycelial-network-dynamics]].

## Cultivation Methods

Research on the cultivation of P. tuber-regium has been conducted by
several researchers. Oso (1977) provided the foundational study from
Nigeria. Subsequent work by Okhuoya et al. (1988, 1990) and Omoanghe
(1992) developed methods for sporocarp development under different
watering systems and on various farm wastes.
