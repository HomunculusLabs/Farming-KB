## Nitrogen as the Strongest Edaphic Factor Structuring Cannabis Microbial Communities

## Overview

In cannabis (*Cannabis sativa* L.) [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]].

## Key Statistical Findings

### BEST Analysis

A Bio-Env + Stepwise (BEST) analysis identified the optimal combination of
edaphic variables explaining microbial beta-diversity. The best predictor
subset included **Nitrogen**, **Carbon**, and **Water**, achieving **rho =
0.632** with the community dissimilarity matrix.

### Weighted Unifrac Correlations

Testing each factor individually against weighted UniFrac beta-diversity
distances, nitrogen ranked as the strongest single predictor:

| Factor     | Correlation (r) | Significance |
|------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Nitrogen** | **0.465**     | p = 0.001    |
| Salinity     | 0.437          | p = 0.001    |
| Carbon       | 0.330          | p = 0.001    |
| Water        | 0.281          | p = 0.001    |
| pH           | 0.221          | p = 0.001    |

### Unweighted Unifrac Correlations

Unweighted UniFrac distances, which emphasize presence/absence rather than
relative abundance, showed the same ranking with stronger correlations:

| Factor     | Correlation (r) | Significance |
|------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Nitrogen** | **0.630**     | p = 0.001    |
| Salinity     | 0.620          | p = 0.001    |
| Carbon       | 0.512          | p = 0.001    |
| Water        | 0.466          | p = 0.001    |
| pH           | 0.292          | p = 0.001    |

In both analyses, **all five [[leake-mycorrhizal-carbon-sequestration-plant-community-composition]] (Fierer and
Jackson, 2006). In cannabis, pH ranked last among five tested factors in both
weighted (r = 0.221) and unweighted (r = 0.292) analyses. Several hypotheses
explain this departure:

1. **Narrow pH range**: Sites may have occupied a narrow pH range, limiting
   discriminatory power while nitrogen varied widely.
2. **Cannabis-specific root exudation**: Cannabis may allocate disproportionate
   root exudate carbon to nitrogen-mobilizing functions, recruiting
   nitrogen-cycling microbial taxa.
3. **Agricultural nitrogen inputs**: Fertilization may create steep rhizosphere
   nitrogen gradients that overwhelm pH-driven selection.
4. **Plant host filtering**: Cannabis may impose stronger selection for
   nitrogen-related microbial functions than for pH tolerance.

## Mechanistic Considerations

Nitrogen's dominance likely operates through multiple mechanisms:

- **Substrate-driven selection**: Microbial taxa differ in nitrogen utilization
  strategies (ammonia oxidation, denitrification, fixation), and local nitrogen
  availability directly filters the community toward matching metabolisms.

- **Carbon-nitrogen coupling**: Microbial growth requires balanced C:N
  stoichiometry; nitrogen constrains heterotrophic processing of organic
  carbon. The BEST model's inclusion of both factors reflects this coupling.

- **Root exudate mediation**: Plant roots modulate exudate composition in
  response to nitrogen status, indirectly shaping the microbial community.

## Implications for Cannabis Cultivation

Understanding nitrogen as the primary edaphic driver has practical implications:

- **Fertilization strategy**: Nitrogen rates and forms (ammonium vs. nitrate vs.
  organic) may be the most powerful levers for shaping beneficial rhizosphere
  communities.
