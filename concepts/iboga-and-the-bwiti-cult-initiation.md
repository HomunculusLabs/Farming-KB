# Iboga and the Bwiti Cult Initiation

Iboga ([[plants-of-the-gods-tabernanthe-iboga-chemistry]]), native to Gabon and the Congo, is the only
member of the dogbane family (Apocynaceae) known to be used as a
hallucinogen. The plant is of growing importance in West Central Africa,
providing the strongest single force against the spread of Christianity
and Islam in this region. Its psychoactive [[iboga-root-bark-preparation]] has served for
centuries as the central sacrament of the Bwiti cult, one of the most
important indigenous religious movements in sub-Saharan Africa.

## Botanical Description

Tabernanthe iboga is a perennial shrub that grows in the understory of
tropical West African forests. The plant is not visually remarkable,
but its yellowish root contains a powerful cocktail of indole alkaloids.
The shrub is native to the forested regions of Gabon, the Congo, and
Cameroon, where it has been used for centuries in religious and
healing contexts.

## The Bwiti Cult

The yellowish root of the iboga plant is employed in the initiation
rites of a number of secret societies, the most famous being the Bwiti
cult. Entrance into the cult is conditional on having "seen" the god
plant Bwiti, which is accomplished through the ingestion of large
doses of iboga root bark. The initiation is an arduous ordeal lasting
several days, during which the initiate consumes massive quantities of
iboga while being attended by experienced cult members.

The Bwiti religion is a syncretic spiritual tradition that combines
elements of indigenous Pygmy forest spirituality with ancestor worship
and, in some variants, Christian symbolism. The cult views iboga as a
sacred tool for communicating with ancestors, accessing the spirit
world, and gaining knowledge about the nature of reality and death.

## Historical Discovery

The drug was discovered by Europeans toward the middle of the nineteenth
century. Early explorers and missionaries reported on the use of iboga
among the Fang people of Gabon and surrounding regions. These accounts
described iboga as both a powerful stimulant and a visionary entheogen
used by hunters, healers, and religious practitioners.

## Pharmacology

Ibogaine is the principal indole alkaloid among a dozen others found in
iboga root bark. The pharmacology of ibogaine is well characterized.
In addition to being a hallucinogen, ibogaine in large doses is a
strong central nervous system stimulant, leading in extreme cases to
convulsions, paralysis, and arrest of respiration. The drug has gained
considerable attention in recent decades for its reported ability to
interrupt drug addiction, particularly addiction to opiates, through a
mechanism that may involve resetting neurochemical receptor
sensitivities.

## Traditional Uses

Beyond its central role in [[mckenna-iboga-tabernanthe-ibogaine-fang-bwiti-initiation-cult]], iboga has several
traditional applications. Hunters use it to stay awake during
all-night vigils. "Sorcerers" take the drug to seek information from
ancestors and the spirit world. The plant has a reputation as a
powerful stimulant and aphrodisiac at lower doses. The distinction
between stimulant, aphrodisiac, and full visionary entheogen effects
depends primarily on dosage, with larger quantities producing the
profound visionary experiences central to Bwiti religious practice.
