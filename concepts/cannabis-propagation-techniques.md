# Cannabis Propagation Techniques

Cannabis can be propagated either sexually (through seeds) or asexually (through cloning). Seeds are the result of [[spore-vs-vegetative-cell-resistance-microwave-sterilization]] in a Cannabis plant has the same genotype and a plant resulting from asexual propagation will have the same genotype as the mother plant. Cloning can preserve a genotype indefinitely. Only mutation of a gene in a vegetative cell that then divides will permanently affect the genotype.

### Cuttings

Cuttings of relatively young vegetative limbs 10 to 45 centimeters (4 to 18 inches) are made with a sharp knife or razor blade and immediately placed in a container of clean, pure water to prevent embolism (air bubble blocking the transpiration stream).

**Rooting Material Selection:**
- Young, firm, vegetative shoots 3 to 7 mm in diameter root most easily
- Weak, unhealthy plants, large woody branches, and reproductive tissues are avoided
- High carbohydrate content cuttings root most easily; the iodine starch test can determine carbohydrate levels
- High nitrogen content cuttings root more poorly
- Lower branches that have ceased lateral growth and begun to accumulate starch are best

**Preparing Clone Stock (Low Nitrogen/High Carbohydrate Balance):**
1. Reduce nitrogen supply by leaching soil, withholding nitrogenous fertilizer, allowing full sunlight
2. Select portions with highest carbohydrate-to-nitrogen ratio (lower on the limb)
3. Etiolation: wrap selected limbs in dark material for two weeks before taking cuttings
4. Girdling: cut or crush the phloem to block downward mobility of carbohydrates and auxins

**Rooting Media:**
- Vermiculite and perlite mixtures provide excellent drainage and neutral pH
- Equal portions of medium and large grade promote greatest root growth due to increased [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]] with micro-nutrients needed for artificial media
- Oxygen enrichment of irrigation water significantly increases rooting rates

**[[ingham-natural-growth-regulators-soil-microbes]] are often more effective than one alone
- Captafol fungicide may be applied in powdered form along with growth regulators

### Layering

Layering allows roots to develop while the stem remains attached to the parent plant, providing water and nutrients throughout the rooting process.

**Soil Layering:** A long, supple vegetative lower limb is bent to touch the ground, stripped of leaves, placed in a narrow trench, and buried. The buried section may be girdled or treated with growth regulators. Rooting usually begins within two weeks; layers may be removed after four to six weeks.

**Air Layering:** Roots form on aerial portions of stems that have been girdled, treated with growth regulators, and wrapped with moist sphagnum moss in clear polyethylene film. Air layers are most useful for cloning many individuals in limited space. Rooting begins within two weeks and roots are visible through clear plastic within four weeks.

### Grafting

Intergeneric grafts between Cannabis and Humulus (hops) have been studied since 1943. Crombie and Crombie (1975) entirely disproved the theory that cannabinoid production occurs in roots and is transported across grafts. Detailed chromatographic analysis showed "no evidence of transport of intermediates or factors critical to cannabinoid formation across the grafts." Multiple seedling grafts can create interesting specimen plants.

## Pruning Techniques

**Meristem Pruning (Topping):** Removing the growing tip causes the next pair of axial growing tips to elongate, dividing growth energy and creating a shorter, bushier plant. Auxin produced in the tip meristem inhibits branching; removing it allows branching to proceed. Pruning late in life can delay or prevent flowering.

**Trellising:** Plants are trained along wire frames for maximum light exposure. East-west orientation is ideal. Floral clusters grow upward from the wire for maximum sun exposure and weather resistance.

**Limbing:** Small, shaded lower limbs are removed so the plant can devote more energy to the top parts with most sun exposure. Removing entire limbs causes less metabolic imbalance than removing only flowers, leaves, or meristems.
