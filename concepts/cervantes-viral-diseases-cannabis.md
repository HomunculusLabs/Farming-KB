## Why Viruses Cannot Be Cured

Once a plant is infected with a virus, there is nothing that
can be done to cure it. Viruses are not living organisms that
can be killed with fungicides or antibiotics. They are
sub-microscopic particles of genetic material (DNA or RNA)
wrapped in a protein coat that hijack plant cells. No spray,
drench, or treatment can remove them from plant tissue without
destroying the plant itself.

## Control and Prevention

Since cure is impossible, prevention is the only strategy:

- Always use fresh, sterile growing medium
- Disinfect tools before cutting foliage on different plants
  to prevent mechanical transmission
- Destroy all plants infected with virus immediately
- Remove and destroy infected plants entirely; do not compost
- Wash hands thoroughly before handling plants, especially
  after using tobacco products
- Control insect vectors (aphids, whiteflies, mites) that
  transmit viruses between plants
- Maintain clean grow room conditions
- Quarantine new plants before introducing them to the garden
- Avoid using tobacco-based sprays due to TMV risk
