## Overview

Psoriasis is a chronic autoimmune skin condition characterized by rapid overproduction of skin cells, leading to thick, red, scaly plaques. It affects 2-3% of the global population and is associated with significant quality-of-life impairment. The endocannabinoid system (ECS) of the skin modulates cell proliferation and inflammation, making cannabinoids a logical therapeutic target. See also [[cannabis-and-skin-diseases]], [[cannabis-and-eczema]], and cannabinoid-receptors.

## Clinical Evidence

### CHI Score: 3-4 (Emerging Evidence)

One to three studies support cannabis for psoriasis, with strong preclinical rationale.

### Key Findings

**CB1 Receptor and Keratinocyte Proliferation**
- CB1 receptor activation may slow excessive keratinocyte (skin cell) proliferation
- Psoriasis involves hyperproliferation of keratinocytes — the ECS normally keeps this in check
- Dysregulated endocannabinoid signaling may contribute to psoriatic pathology
- Restoring ECS function through exogenous cannabinoids may normalize cell turnover

**Anti-inflammatory Effects**
- CB2 receptor activation reduces inflammatory cytokine production (TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-17)
- These cytokines are central to psoriatic inflammation
- CBD demonstrates broad anti-inflammatory activity through multiple receptor targets
- THC activates both CB1 and CB2, providing dual anti-inflammatory pathways

**Curcumin Synergy (Aggarwal et al., 2003)**
- Curcumin shows anticancer potential through anti-proliferative effects
- These same anti-proliferative mechanisms may benefit psoriasis by slowing skin cell overproduction
- Curcumin and cannabinoids may act synergistically on inflammatory pathways

**Pruritus (Itch) Relief**
- Psoriatic itch is often severe and treatment-resistant
- Topical cannabinoid agonists are effective for chronic pruritus (Stander et al., 2006)
- CB1 and CB2 receptors on sensory nerve endings in skin modulate itch signals
- Dronabinol showed preliminary benefit for intractable pruritus (Neff et al., 2002)

**Immune Modulation**
- Psoriasis is a T-cell mediated autoimmune condition
- Cannabinoids modulate T-cell function and cytokine profiles
- May help shift immune response from pro-inflammatory to regulatory phenotype

## Integrative Approaches

### Vitamin D
- Vitamin D analogues are first-line topical treatments for psoriasis
- Oral vitamin D supplementation (4,000-10,000 IU daily) supports immune regulation
- Sun exposure (moderate) naturally increases vitamin D and may improve psoriasis

### Anti-inflammatory Diet
- Mediterranean diet rich in omega-3 fatty acids
- Reduce processed foods, sugar, and alcohol (known triggers)
- Gluten-free diet helps some patients

### Stress Management
- Stress is a known trigger for psoriasis flares
- Cannabis may help break the stress-flare cycle
- Meditation, yoga, and cognitive behavioral therapy complement cannabinoid therapy

## Administration Methods

- **Topical:** CBD/THC creams, salves, and oils applied directly to plaques — preferred method
- **Oral:** For systemic immune modulation and stress relief
- **Sublingual:** Rapid absorption for breakthrough symptoms
- **Combination:** Topical for plaques + oral for systemic inflammation and stress

## Safety

- Topical cannabinoids are well-tolerated with minimal side effects
- Avoid smoking — nicotine and smoke may worsen psoriasis
- Monitor for skin irritation with topical preparations
- Consult dermatologist; cannabinoids may complement but not replace conventional treatments
