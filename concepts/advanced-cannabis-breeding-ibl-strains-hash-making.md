# Advanced Cannabis Breeding — IBL Strains, Population Genetics, and Hash Making

## Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium and Population Genetics

Five factors can disturb Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium in a cannabis breeding population:

1. **Mutation** — Change in genetic material creating heritable variations. In nature, radiation exposure causes mutations. In breeding, a mutant effectively introduces "foreign" genetic material through migration into the population.
2. **Migration** — Introduction of genes from external populations.
3. **Genetic drift** — Random changes in allele frequencies, especially significant in small populations.
4. **Non-random mating** — Selective breeding by the grower.
5. **Natural selection** — Environmental pressures favouring certain traits.

## IBL (Inbred Line) Strains

### Creating an IBL

An IBL (Inbred Line) is created by selectively breeding a population until specific genotypes are locked in:

1. Select plants with desired traits.
2. Cross selected plants within the population.
3. Grow out offspring and select again for desired traits.
4. Repeat over multiple generations.

### Example: Creating "Silver Kush"

- Start with a Silver Haze × Kush cross.
- Select offspring displaying desired phenotypes (e.g., silver bud colour).
- Continue crossing and selecting until only desired genotypes remain.
- Result: A Silver Kush strain with locked-in traits.

### True Breeding

- **100% true breeding** for every phenotype is extremely unlikely — such a strain would be "A perfect IBL."
- **90% phenotypic stability** is considered an acceptable threshold to claim IBL status.
- Phenotypes that may vary even in IBLs: stem colour (purple vs. green), potency levels.

## Hash Making

### Silk Screen Method

- Rub dried bud material back and forth over a **silk screen** placed over a glass surface.
- Produces 2 grades of sieved residue, mostly **trichomes** (the resin glands containing cannabinoids).
- The screen separates trichome heads from plant matter based on size.

### Multiple Screening Method

A refined technique using 4–5 screens of decreasing micron size:

1. Start with the **largest** screen — removes large plant matter.
2. Sieve through progressively **smaller** screens.
3. Each screen captures progressively finer material.
4. The **last screen** contains the finest, most potent trichome collection.
5. The final product is essentially separated glandular trichomes — high-quality hashish.
