## Shamanic Fights and Protective Arkanas

## Overview

Shamanic fights (*peleas de brujos* or *shitanería*) form a central category of visionary experience in [[chullachaki-and-soul-recovery-in-amazonian-shamanism]], the boundary between healing and sorcery is not absolute but relational: the same knowledge, spirit allies, and techniques can be used for either purpose depending on the practitioner's intent. A *banco* (master of the three realms) may cure, but an evil banco "usually does harm." This duality is embedded in the very structure of [[query-how-do-plants-defend-themselves-chemically]].
- **Huaira-umas (forgetful minds)**: Snakes that cast spells driving victims crazy and making them forget their defensive knowledge.
- **Sepultura tonduri**: A "very sad and frightening icaro" sung by sorcerers to kill their enemies, literally meaning "grave funeral." It summons the Sachamama (mother of the forest) to swallow the victim, with the motelo-mama (tortoise) serving as the funeral table, mushrooms as candles, and fireflies as their flames.

### Defensive Systems

Healers counter these attacks with elaborate defensive networks:

- **Tingunas**: Luminous energy fields of "surprising colours" raised around the healing space. These serve as force fields that dazzle and disorient attacking spirits.
- **Arkanas**: Individualized protective defenses given by spirit allies — animals, weapons, or energetic barriers specific to each vegetalista.
- **Magnetic disks**: Concentrated magnetic powers raised to prevent disease agents from escaping treatment areas.
- **Electric chains**: Binding energies that can trap and immobilize sorcerers and their spirit allies.
- **Laser threads and pyramid-shaped lasers**: Advanced defensive technologies described in the visions, often deployed from spaceships.

## Key Battles in the Visionary Record

### Shipibo vs. Shetebo Shaman (Vision 43)

This battle between a Shetebo sorcerer and a Shipibo healer illustrates the full range of combat techniques. The healer deploys, from right to left:

1. **Mantona mama**: A large snake with hypnotic magnetism
2. **Supay-yangunturo**: A gigantic diabolic armadillo that burrows through earth at great speed to re-emerge near the enemy
3. **Sinchi-ahui tiger**: A tiger with magnetic power in its eyes that immobilizes rivals
4. **Chicua**: A bird that determines enemy intentions
5. **Allpa-raya**: A land ray that stings and can fly in pursuit
6. **Ishkay-huahua-supay**: A two-headed creature that confuses enemies by producing weeping sounds from multiple locations
7. **Mocagua**: A pot-shaped vessel in which the healer flies at great speeds
8. **Black tibes**: Water-diving birds that attack the sorcerer's eyes
9. **Guacamayo-machaco**: A snake with a woman's breasts and human arms that throws poisoned daggers
10. **Sapo-machaco**: A snake-toad with rapid biting and deadly poison

The sorcerer's counter-forces include the huaira-umas (forgetful-mind snakes), ayanahuis (eyes of the dead used as cigarettes), and huasi-ukullucos (house-lizards that enter through the anus during defecation, causing death).

### The Fight of the Aceropunta (Vision 48)

The Aceropunta — a spirit battleship that appears in multiple visions — is called upon by a master vegetalista to repel a sorcerer armed with a kabbalistic lance and escorted by shape-shifting animals. The sorcerer sings the icaro sepultura tonduri, causing vomiting and fainting among the vegetalista's companions. The defense involves:

- **Alipa-tren** (train of the earth): Subterranean forces
- **Huiturunas**: Dark blue-colored warrior people
- **Macanun-caballas**: Battle horses with lance-carrying riders
- **Sylphids**: Steering the Aceropunta steamer, leaving in military formation with magic nets and pyramid-shaped lasers
- **Yakurunas and mermaids**: Throwing darts through peashooters at enemies

The battle is described as "a great battle between the esoteric powers of good and evil."

### Vegetalistas Transforming into Wolves (Vision 45)

When a sorcerer attacks a group peacefully taking ayahuasca, the vegetalistas transform into wolves to hide from him. They are protected by six named queens (*dueñas*) — Cushirima, Silove, Gabat, Manchahuarmi, Allimpaica, and Callpaican-Kapak — each possessing specialized esoteric knowledge: beautiful icaros, perfume-making, clairvoyance, warfare, compassionate healing, and rapid travel for hunting enemies.

## Cultural Context

Shamanic fights are not merely metaphorical. In Amazonian communities, accusations of sorcery are common and can lead to social conflict, displacement, and even violence. The elaborate defensive systems described in the visions reflect real social dynamics in which healers must maintain active defenses against perceived spiritual attacks. The visions also serve a pedagogical function: by visualizing the spirit world in such detail, they train apprentices in the taxonomy of threats and the repertoire of available defenses.
