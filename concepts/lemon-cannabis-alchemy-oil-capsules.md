# Preparation of Cannabis Oil Capsules

Chapter 6 of *Cannabis Alchemy* by Ben Lemon describes the preparation
of cannabis oil capsules for oral ingestion, sometimes called "pot pills."
This method provides a discreet, precisely dosable way to consume
cannabis extracts, making it one of the most practical applications of
the extraction and refinement techniques described in the earlier
chapters.

## Overview

Capsules of oil for oral ingestion are prepared by first mixing the
purified cannabis oil with an equal amount of butter. The butterfat
carries the oil through the membranes of the stomach and intestine,
facilitating absorption into the bloodstream. The oil and butter mixture
is then blended into two volumes of an edible inert powder, and the
resulting mixture is stuffed into large gelatin capsules.

## The Role of Butterfat

The addition of butter is a critical step in the capsule preparation
process. THC and other cannabinoids are lipophilic (fat-soluble)
compounds, meaning they dissolve readily in fats and oils but poorly in
water. When consumed orally, the cannabinoids must pass through the
digestive system and be absorbed through the intestinal walls.

Butterfat serves as a carrier molecule that helps transport the
cannabinoids across the lipid membranes of the stomach and intestinal
lining. Without adequate fat content, much of the consumed THC would
pass through the digestive system unabsorbed, significantly reducing
the effective dose.

The use of butter rather than other fats is likely due to its
availability, pleasant taste, and high saturated fat content, which
provides excellent solubility for cannabinoids at body temperature.

## Preparation Steps

### Step 1: Prepare the Purified Oil

The cannabis oil used for capsules should ideally be at the highest level
of refinement available. Oil that has been extracted, purified with
petroleum ether, isomerized, and possibly acetylated will produce the
most potent capsules. Even oil from a simple alcohol extraction can be
used, though the resulting capsules will be less potent.

### Step 2: Mix with Butter

The purified cannabis oil is mixed with an equal amount of butter. The
mixture should be blended thoroughly to ensure uniform distribution of
the cannabinoids throughout the butter. Gentle heating may be used to
facilitate mixing if the oil is thick or viscous.

The equal ratio of oil to butter provides an optimal balance between
cannabinoid content and fat carrier. Using more oil relative to butter
could result in incomplete absorption, while using more butter would
dilute the active ingredients unnecessarily.

### Step 3: Blend with Inert Powder

The oil and butter mixture is blended into two volumes of an edible
inert powder. Lemon suggests several options for the inert powder:

- **Marijuana powder**: Finely ground, defatted cannabis material can
  be used, adding a mild cannabis flavor and potentially synergistic
  plant compounds.

- **Parsley**: Dried and finely powdered parsley provides bulk with a
  neutral flavor that helps mask the taste of cannabis oil.

- **Lactose**: A milk sugar commonly used in pharmaceutical
  preparations. It has excellent powder properties and is nearly
  tasteless.

- **Any edible inert powder**: Other options include powdered sugar,
  flour, or other food-grade powders that absorb oil well.

The purpose of the inert powder is to absorb the oil/butter mixture
and create a workable paste or solid that can be packed into capsules.
Without the powder, the liquid oil would leak from the capsules.

### Step 4: Fill Gelatin Capsules

The resulting mixture is stuffed into large gelatin capsules. Large
capsules (typically size 00 or size 0) are used to accommodate
sufficient quantities of the active ingredients. The capsules can be
filled manually or with a capsule-filling machine.

## Dosing Considerations

One of the primary advantages of oil capsules is the ability to control
dosage precisely. Since the amount of purified oil in each capsule can
be measured during preparation, consistent dosing is achievable. This is
particularly important with highly refined oils that have undergone
isomerization and acetylation, where the difference between a
therapeutic and an overwhelming dose may be small.

The delayed onset of oral cannabis preparations (typically 30 minutes
to 2 hours) means that users must wait before assessing the effect of
their dose. Capsules make it easy to standardize the amount consumed
and avoid accidental overconsumption.

## Advantages of Capsule Form

- **Discreet**: Capsules look like ordinary vitamin or medication
  supplements and produce no telltale odor.
- **Precise dosing**: Each capsule contains a measured amount of active
  ingredients.
- **No smoke**: Oral ingestion avoids the respiratory risks associated
  with smoking.
- **Long-lasting effects**: Oral cannabis typically produces effects
  that last longer than inhaled cannabis.
- **Portable**: Capsules are easy to carry and store without special
  equipment.
- **Consistent**: Unlike smoking, where potency can vary with
  technique, capsules deliver a consistent dose.
