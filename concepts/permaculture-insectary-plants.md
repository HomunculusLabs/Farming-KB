# Permaculture Insectary Plants

Insectary plants are those specifically included in a design to attract, shelter, and sustain [[beneficial-insects-and-biological-pest-control]] -- predators and pollinators that provide [[biological-pest-control-balance]] and crop pollination services. Throughout the Designers' Manual, Mollison emphasises that any innovative designer can see ways to add "a vine, a fungus, a beneficial insect" to a seemingly fully-planted area.

## The Role of Beneficial Insects

Permaculture relies on biological pest management rather than chemical intervention. Beneficial insects include:

- **Predators**: Ladybirds, lacewings, predatory mites, ground beetles, assassin bugs, praying mantises, dragonflies
- **Parasitoids**: Parasitic wasps (Trichogramma, Braconid, Ichneumonid), tachinid flies
- **Pollinators**: Bees (honeybees and native bees), butterflies, moths, hoverflies, beetles

These insects require specific habitat conditions: nectar and pollen sources (often different from crop flowers), shelter from wind and rain, overwintering sites, and alternate prey/hosts when pest populations are low.

## Insectary Plant Strategies

### Umbelliferous Plants

The umbelliferae (Apiaceae) family is among the most important for attracting beneficial insects. Their shallow, open flower umbels provide easy access to nectar and pollen for tiny parasitic wasps. The Manual notes that when saving seed from any umbelliferous plant (fennel, parsley, parsnip, etc.), the strong mature seed from the terminal panicle germinates best, and these plants self-seed readily in garden systems.

Key umbellifers for insectary use:
- Fennel ([[foeniculum-vulgare-entity]]) -- self-seeds readily, perennial
- Parsley ([[petroselinum-crispum]]) -- biennial, flowers in second year
- Parsnip (Pastinaca sativa) -- biennial, left to flower provides major insectary value
- Dill ([[anethum-graveolens]]) -- annual, attracts parasitic wasps
- Coriander ([[coriandrum-sativum]]) -- annual, succession planting extends bloom period
- Queen Anne's lace (Daucus carota) -- wild carrot, excellent insectary
- Yarrow ([[achillea-millefolium]]) -- flat flower heads attract many beneficials

### Composite Flowers

Daisy-family plants with open centres provide landing platforms and accessible nectar:
- Chamomile
- Calendula (pot marigold)
- Cosmos
- Sunflower
- Goldenrod
- Asters

### Legume Flowers

Many pasture and cover crop legumes serve dual purpose as insectary plants:
- Clover (white and red)
- Lucerne/alfalfa (allowed to flower in strips)
- Vetch
- Sweet pea

### Companion Planting

Home gardens deserve intensive bed-by-bed planning in which companion plants, seasonal succession, bed soil treatments, and permanent watering methods are all designed. Companion planting places insectary species among or adjacent to crops to provide continuous habitat for beneficials.

### Hedgerow Insectaries

Perennial insectary hedges along garden edges and between crop zones provide permanent habitat. These can include mixed plantings of:
- Flowering shrubs (butterfly bush, lilac)
- Herbs (rosemary, thyme, lavender, sage, mint family)
- Native wildflowers suited to the region

## Design Considerations

1. Ensure continuous bloom through the growing season by selecting species with staggered flowering times
2. Provide shallow water sources (saucers with pebbles) for drinking
3. Leave some areas undisturbed for overwintering habitat (dead stems, leaf litter, hollow stems)
4. Avoid broad-spectrum pesticides that kill beneficials alongside pests
5. Include a diversity of flower shapes and sizes to serve different insect mouthparts
6. Plant insectary species in clumps rather than scattered individuals for greater impact

## Integration Across Zones

- **Zone 1**: Interplant insectary herbs among vegetables and in kitchen garden beds
- **Zone 2**: Flowering shrubs and perennial herbs in orchard understory
- **Zone 3**: Allow strips of clover and lucerne to flower in pasture; hedgerow insectaries along field borders
- **Zone 4**: Native flowering species in windbreaks and forest edges
