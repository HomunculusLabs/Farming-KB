# trametes versicolor bioremediation Applications

## Overview

[[trametes-versicolor]] (syn. [[coriolus-versicolor]], Polyporus versicolor), commonly known as the turkey tail fungus, is a white rot basidiomycete widely recognized for its strong constitutive laccase production and broad-spectrum pollutant degradation capabilities. Along with [[phanerochaete-chrysosporium]], T. versicolor is one of the two most extensively studied white rot fungi for bioremediation applications. Its ability to produce abundant laccase under a range of conditions, without requiring nitrogen limitation, makes it particularly attractive for practical applications.

## Biological Characteristics

- **Classification**: Basidiomycota, Polyporaceae
- **Growth**: Forms distinctive bracket-like fruiting bodies with concentric color zones (hence "versicolor")
- **Temperature optimum**: 27-30°C (typical of most white rot fungi)
- **pH**: Grows optimally at pH 4-5.5; self-buffers in this range during growth on carbohydrates
- **Nutrition**: Does NOT require nitrogen limitation for ligninolytic enzyme production (unlike P. chrysosporium)
- **Laccase**: Strong constitutive laccase producer; laccase is the dominant extracellular oxidative enzyme

## Laccase System

### Laccase Production and Regulation

T. versicolor is one of the best-known laccase-producing fungi:

- **Copper induction**: Cu²⁺ stimulates laccase production (Collins & Dobson, 1997). At 0.1 mmol/L, Cu²⁺ led to greater decolorization by T. versicolor, although at a slower rate (Pointing, Bucher & Vrijmoed, 2000)
- **Aromatic compound induction**: 2,5-xylidene and 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HBT) induce laccase synthesis (Collins & Dobson, 1997). HBT has dual effect — inducing synthesis AND acting as a mediator for indirect oxidation
- **Manganese interaction**: Mn²⁺ presence can affect decolorization outcomes; in some cases Mn²⁺ increased intensity of undesirable red-colored by-products during decolorization, possibly by altering enzyme production balance

### Laccase-Mediator Systems

T. versicolor laccase with mediators extends oxidative capability to non-phenolic substrates:

- **ABTS**: Enables oxidation of non-phenolic lignin model compounds and PAHs
- **HBT**: Promotes decolorization of a range of dyes
- **Natural mediators**: Simple aromatics like phenol, aniline, 4-hydroxybenzoic acid (0.1 mM) mediate PAH oxidation by T. versicolor laccase (Johannes & Majcherczyk, 2000)

## Bioremediation Applications

### Dye Decolorization

T. versicolor is among the most effective white rot fungi for dye decolorization:

- **Reactive Black 5**: Nearly 100% reduction in A₄₄₆ achieved, though residual red coloration (A₅₅₀ ~80% reduction) can persist due to new chromophore formation from degradation product coupling
- **Broad dye spectrum**: Effective against azo, triphenylmethane, anthraquinone, and other dye classes
- **Mechanism**: Initial rapid adsorption to mycelium followed by slower enzymatic degradation; laccase is the primary enzyme responsible
- **Advantage**: Laccase is constitutively produced without need for nitrogen limitation

### PCB Degradation

T. versicolor degrades polychlorinated biphenyls:

- **Aroclor 1254 and Delor 106**: Small amounts degraded in liquid culture (Eaton, 1985; Novotny et al., 1997)
- **Bioavailability enhancement**: Low concentrations of Triton X-100 surfactant increased bioavailability of PCB congener 2,4',5-trichlorobiphenyl for oxidation (Beaudette et al., 2000)
- **Preference**: Like bacterial systems, preferentially degrades lesser-chlorinated biphenyls

### Chlorophenol Polymerization

T. versicolor laccase polymerizes 4-chloroaniline to oligomers (dimers to pentamers) (Hoff, Liu & Bollag, 1985). This oxidative coupling transforms toxic intermediates of pesticide/herbicide decomposition into less mobile, less bioavailable polymeric forms — a potential detoxification mechanism.

### Fungicide Transformation

T. versicolor stereospecifically reduces the fungicide triadimefon to the less fungitoxic diastereoisomeric triadimenol (Deas & Clifford, 1982; Deas, Clark & Carter, 1984). This demonstrates the fungus's ability to transform xenobiotics through reductive as well as oxidative pathways.

### PAH Degradation

T. versicolor laccase, with appropriate mediators, oxidizes PAHs including anthracene, phenanthrene, and benzo[a]pyrene. The mediator system extends the laccase's redox potential beyond its native range, enabling attack on non-phenolic PAH structures.

### Heavy Metal Tolerance

T. versicolor shows interesting metal interactions:
- Decolorization of Poly R 478 dye enhanced by Cu²⁺ at 0.1 mmol/L
- Mn²⁺ effects are complex: can promote or inhibit depending on conditions and substrate

## Comparison with P. chrysosporium

| Aspect | T. versicolor | P. chrysosporium |
|--------|---------------|------------------|
| Primary enzyme | Laccase | LiP |
| N-limitation needed | No | Yes |
| Laccase production | Strong, constitutive | Rare |
| Temperature optimum | 27-30°C | 37-40°C |
| Dye decolorization | Excellent | Good |
| PAH oxidation | Via laccase + mediators | Via LiP/MnP |
| PCB degradation | Moderate | Moderate |
| Scale-up advantage | Simpler nutrition requirements | Higher temperature = less cooling |

## Practical Advantages

1. **No nitrogen limitation required**: Simplifies media formulation and allows treatment of nitrogen-rich waste streams
2. **Strong laccase production**: Constitutive production reduces need for induction
3. **Robust growth**: Tolerant of a range of environmental conditions
4. **Established research base**: Extensive literature on culture conditions, enzyme production, and pollutant degradation
5. **Potential as biological filter**: Fungal mycelial mat could serve as biological filter for low-concentration pollutants (Thomas, Carswell & Georgiou, 1992)

## Limitations

1. **No LiP**: Lack of lignin peroxidase limits direct oxidation of non-phenolic compounds without mediators
2. **Mediator cost**: Effective treatment of non-phenolic pollutants requires added mediators
3. **New chromophore formation**: Laccase-mediated coupling can produce new colored products during dye treatment
4. **Mn²⁺ interactions**: Complex and sometimes adverse effects of manganese on treatment outcomes
