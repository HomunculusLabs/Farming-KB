# trametes versicolor bioremediation Applications

## Overview

[[phanerochaete-chrysosporium]], T. versicolor is one of the two most extensively studied [[ligninolytic-enzyme-production-bioremediation]] (unlike P. chrysosporium)
- **Laccase**: Strong constitutive laccase producer; laccase is the dominant extracellular oxidative enzyme

## Laccase System

### Laccase Production and Regulation

T. versicolor is one of the best-known laccase-producing fungi:

- **Copper induction**: Cu²⁺ stimulates laccase production (Collins & Dobson, 1997). At 0.1 mmol/L, Cu²⁺ led to greater decolorization by T. versicolor, although at a slower rate (Pointing, Bucher & Vrijmoed, 2000)
- **Aromatic compound induction**: 2,5-xylidene and 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HBT) induce laccase synthesis (Collins & Dobson, 1997). HBT has dual effect — inducing synthesis AND acting as a mediator for indirect oxidation
- **Manganese interaction**: Mn²⁺ presence can affect decolorization outcomes; in some cases Mn²⁺ increased intensity of undesirable red-colored by-products during decolorization, possibly by altering [[enzymatic-degradation-in-mycoremediation]]; laccase is the primary enzyme responsible
- **Advantage**: Laccase is constitutively produced without need for nitrogen limitation

### PCB Degradation

T. versicolor degrades polychlorinated biphenyls:

- **Aroclor 1254 and Delor 106**: Small amounts degraded in [[comparison-liquid-culture-vs-grain-spawn]] (Eaton, 1985; Novotny et al., 1997)
- **Bioavailability enhancement**: Low concentrations of Triton X-100 surfactant increased bioavailability of PCB congener 2,4',5-trichlorobiphenyl for oxidation (Beaudette et al., 2000)
- **Preference**: Like bacterial systems, preferentially degrades lesser-chlorinated biphenyls

### Chlorophenol Polymerization

T. versicolor laccase polymerizes 4-chloroaniline to oligomers (dimers to pentamers) (Hoff, Liu & Bollag, 1985). This oxidative coupling transforms toxic intermediates of pesticide/herbicide decomposition into less mobile, less bioavailable polymeric forms — a potential detoxification mechanism.

### Fungicide Transformation

T. versicolor stereospecifically reduces the fungicide triadimefon to the less fungitoxic diastereoisomeric triadimenol (Deas & Clifford, 1982; Deas, Clark & Carter, 1984). This demonstrates the fungus's ability to transform xenobiotics through reductive as well as oxidative pathways.

### PAH Degradation

T. versicolor laccase, with appropriate mediators, oxidizes PAHs including anthracene, phenanthrene, and benzo[a]pyrene. The mediator system extends the laccase's redox potential beyond its native range, enabling attack on non-phenolic PAH structures.

### Heavy Metal Tolerance

T. versicolor shows interesting [[gadd-fungal-metal-interactions]]:
- Decolorization of Poly R 478 dye enhanced by Cu²⁺ at 0.1 mmol/L
- Mn²⁺ effects are complex: can promote or inhibit depending on conditions and substrate
