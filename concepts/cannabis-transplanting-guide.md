Transplanting — moving a plant from a smaller container to a larger one — is a critical skill. Done properly, it promotes explosive [[query-how-to-choose-the-right-pot-size-for-cannabis]]**: The plant is clearly too large for its current container (e.g., plant is 2 feet tall in a 1-gallon pot)

### When NOT to Transplant

- **During late flowering**: Root disturbance during late flower can cause significant stress and yield loss
- **When the plant is already stressed**: Disease, pest damage, or severe deficiency should be addressed first
- **If the final container is already large enough**: Some growers start in the final pot to avoid transplanting entirely

## Container Progression

A typical transplant schedule:

1. **Starter container**: 1-liter (or 16 oz cup, rockwool cube, or Jiffy pellet)
2. **First transplant**: 3–5 gallon container
3. **Final container**: 5–10+ gallon container (for soil-grown plants intended for full size)

You do not have to use every size — some growers go from 1-liter straight to a 7-gallon final pot. The key is that each step should be manageable.

## Step-by-Step Transplanting Procedure

### Preparation

1. **Prepare the new container**: Fill with your soil mix, leaving 2–3 inches of space at the top for watering
2. **Create a hole**: Dig a hole in the center of the new container, sized to fit the root ball
3. **Water the current plant lightly**: Moist soil holds together better during transplant (but do not saturate)
4. **Have everything ready**: Work quickly to minimize root exposure time

### Removal from Old Container

1. Place your hand over the soil surface with the stem between your fingers
2. Invert the container while supporting the root ball with your hand
3. Gently tap or squeeze the sides of the container to release the root ball
4. For stubborn containers, slide a knife along the inner edges
5. If roots are severely bound and the plant is stuck, you may need to cut the container away

### Root Ball Inspection and Preparation

1. **Examine the root ball**: Check for circling roots, root density, and overall health
2. **Loosen circling roots**: Gently tease apart roots that are spiraling around the root ball
3. **Score the root ball**: For heavily root-bound plants, make 3–4 shallow vertical cuts (1/4 inch deep) around the sides with a clean knife. This forces roots to grow outward into new soil.
4. **Remove problem roots**: Trim away any brown, mushy, or dead-looking roots with sterilized scissors

### Planting in the New Container

1. Place the root ball into the prepared hole in the new container
2. The top of the root ball should be level with or slightly below the soil surface (you can bury the stem up to the first set of leaves for additional root growth along the buried stem)
3. Fill in around the root ball with fresh soil mix
4. **Gently firm** the soil — press it down lightly to eliminate large air pockets, but do NOT compact it tightly
5. Water thoroughly with plain water (no nutrients) until 10–20% runoff

## Post-Transplant Care

- **Reduce light intensity for 1–2 days**: Lower the lights or move plants further away to reduce stress
- **No nutrients for 3–5 days**: Let the disturbed roots recover before feeding
- **Maintain moderate temperature**: 72–78°F (22–26°C)
- **Maintain moderate humidity**: 50–60%
- **Avoid training or pruning**: Give the plant time to recover before any additional stress
- **Resume normal care** once new growth appears (usually 3–7 days)
