# Sarcoma 180 Antitumour Assay

The Sarcoma 180 (S-180) ascites tumour bioassay in ICR mice is the standard and most widely used method for assessing the antitumour activity of mushroom-derived polysaccharides and other natural products. Developed in Japan, it has been the primary screening tool responsible for identifying virtually all clinically relevant mushroom antitumour compounds.

## Assay Protocol

### Primary Screening (Intraperitoneal Administration)

1. **Animals**: Female JCB/Jcl strain mice, 7 weeks old (5-7 per group)
2. **Tumour inoculation**: Sarcoma 180 cells (~5 x 10^6) collected from a donor mouse on day 7 post-transplantation, implanted subcutaneously on the right groin
3. **Test compound**: Dissolved in physiological saline, injected intraperitoneally at 1-100 mg/kg body weight, once daily for 10 consecutive days
4. **Monitoring**: Tumour size (diameter in mm or weight in g) recorded weekly
5. **Assessment**: At week 5, tumours enucleated and weighed; inhibitory rate (%) calculated: `100(1 - B/A)` where A = average tumour size in control, B = average tumour size in treatment group
6. **ID50 determination**: The 50% inhibitory dose calculated from semilogarithmic plots of dose vs. tumour inhibitory ratio

### Secondary Screening (Oral Administration)

If intraperitoneal screening is effective, oral administration follows:
- Sarcoma 180 cells (2 x 10^6) or solid tumour fragment (~2 mm) grafted
- Test sample administered orally on days 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, and 14 (10 doses total)
- Tumour diameter measured at day 25 to calculate growth inhibitory ratio
- Complete tumour disappearance counted at day 45
- Body weight changes (days 3-15) examined for toxicity assessment

## Historical Significance

The S-180 assay was instrumental in the discovery of the major mushroom-derived antitumour compounds:
- **1968-1969**: Ikekawa et al. and Chihara et al. first demonstrated that hot water extracts of multiple mushroom species showed remarkable host-mediated antitumour activity against Sarcoma 180 xenografts, bringing "immediate public attention"
- **1970**: Chihara isolated and characterized [[psk-psp-polysaccharide-peptides]], schizophyllan-research|schizophyllan, grifolan, and dozens of other active polysaccharides

## Limitations and Criticisms

- The S-180 model is a rapidly growing transplantable tumour that may not accurately predict efficacy against spontaneous human cancers
- Intraperitoneal administration (primary screen) bypasses first-pass metabolism and may overestimate oral bioavailability
- The assay does not distinguish between immunomodulatory and direct cytotoxic mechanisms
- Results in this model have not always translated to clinical success in human trials
- While many mushroom polysaccharides show "considerable antitumour activity in several xenographs only a limited number have undergone [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]]"
