# Classification of Fleshy Fungi by Spore-Bearing Surface

[[julius-auboineau-palmer]] organized all fleshy fungi into **four grand divisions** based on the nature of their spore-bearing surface. This classification provides a practical framework for identification, ranging from the safest groups for beginners to those requiring the greatest expertise.

## The Four Grand Divisions

### 1. Agaricini (Agarics) — Gilled Fungi

The spore-bearing surface consists of **thin, radiating plates** resembling the spokes of a wheel. These gills hang from the underside of the cap, increasing the surface area for spore production.

Agarics represent the **largest and most diverse** division, but they are also the **least safe** for amateurs. Many deadly poisonous species belong here (e.g., *Amanita*), and edible and toxic forms can closely resemble one another.

#### Notable Species

- **Meadow Mushroom (*[[agaricus-campestris]]*)** and **Horse-Mushroom (*[[agaricus-arvensis]]*)**: Both feature a ring or veil (annulus) on the stem and gills that are pink when young, darkening progressively to black-brown as spores mature.
- **Maned Mushroom (*[[coprinus-comatus]]*)**: Ovate cap covered in white, shaggy scales (the "mane"), hollow stem resembling macaroni. Notably, it deliquesces — turning to ink as it matures. Palmer emphasizes it has **no poisonous counterpart**, making it one of the safest gilled mushrooms to learn.
- **Elm-Tree Mushroom (*[[agaricus-ulmarius]]*)**: Found on elm trees in autumn. Distinguished by a thick stem, broad white gills, and a light-yellow cap.

#### Russuleae (Brittle-Gilled Agarics)

A distinctive subgroup of agarics whose gills are **brittle and snap cleanly** when bent, rather than peeling or tearing. Key identification rules:

- **If milky juice exudes when cut — reject.** Milky species (Lactarii) are too dangerous for novices.
- **Taste test**: Place a small piece on the tongue. An **acrid or peppery** taste indicates poison; a **mild or nutty** taste suggests edibility.

  - *Russula alutacea* — mild, edible
  - *R. emetica* and *R. rosacea* — acrid, poisonous lookalikes

### 2. Boleti and Polyporei — Pored Fungi

The spore-bearing surface is a **fine sponge-like texture** composed of tubes. The tubes are **separably peelable** from the flesh beneath — a key diagnostic trait. Palmer divides these into two groups:

#### Boleti

- Grow from the **ground** (terrestrial)
- Possess a **distinct central stem**
- Tubes are **readily separable** from the cap flesh

#### Polyporei

- Grow mostly **on wood** (lignicolous)
- Stems are **abortive or absent**

#### Rules for Pored Fungi

| Feature | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Brown, yellowish, or greenish pores + russet cap + white flesh | **Edible** |
| Brilliant red or yellow coloring | **Reject** |
| Flesh that bruises **blue** on handling | **Reject** |
| **Red tube orifices** | **Doubtless poisonous** |

### 3. Hydnei — Toothed Fungi

The spore-bearing surface consists of **small points, spines, or teeth** hanging downward from the cap. These teeth are the equivalent of gills in other groups.

#### Notable Species

- ***Hydnum repandum***: Yellow-colored with firm, solid flesh and a stem that is **eccentric** (attached off-center). A choice edible species.

Tooth fungi are relatively few in number, making them **easier to learn** than gilled fungi. Most are edible.

### 4. Lycoperdon (Puffballs) — Enclosed Spores

The spore-bearing surface is **entirely enclosed** within a globular or pear-shaped body. Spores are released only when the outer wall ruptures or disintegrates at maturity.

Puffballs are the **safest division for beginners**. The critical rule:

> A puffball is edible **only when the interior is pure white** throughout. Any yellow, green, purple, or dark discoloration means it is past its prime or potentially harmful.

#### Notable Species

- ***Lycoperdon giganteum*** (Giant Puffball): Can reach the size of a football. When young and white-fleshed, it is excellent eating — Palmer considered it perhaps the safest mushroom for a beginner to gather.

## Palmer's Theory of Taste as a Safety Test

Palmer advanced a bold principle:

> **No fungus that tastes good plainly cooked with dry heat can be dangerous.**

The reasoning: truly poisonous species possess an inherently disagreeable or acrid flavor that persists even through simple cooking. A mushroom that is palatable when broiled or roasted with nothing but salt is, in Palmer's view, safe to eat.

### Critical Warning

This test is **invalid** if the mushroom's natural flavor is **disguised** by:

- Rich gravies or sauces
- Batter or breading (as in frying)
- Spices, herbs, or strong seasoning

These preparations can mask the warning bitterness or acridity of a poisonous species. Palmer's taste test requires **plain, dry cooking** — nothing more.

## Summary: Safety Ranking by Division

1. **Puffballs (Lycoperdon)** — Safest; check white interior only
2. **Tooth fungi (Hydnei)** — Few species; most edible
3. **Pored fungi (Boleti/Polyporei)** — Good rules; watch for red pores and blue bruising
4. **Gilled fungi (Agaricini)** — Least safe; requires the most study and caution
