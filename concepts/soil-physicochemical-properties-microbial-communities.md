# Soil Physicochemical Properties and Microbial Communities

Soil physicochemical properties are the primary drivers of microbial [[edaphic-determinants-cannabis-microbiome-community-structure]], making them the dominant Tier 1 selective force in the two-tier model of plant [[cannabis-endorhiza-bacterial-communities]]. Proteobacteria, Actinobacteria, Bacteroidetes, and Planctomycetes are all well-represented.
- **Alkaline soils (pH > 7.5)**: Favor Firmicutes, Chloroflexi, and certain Gammaproteobacteria groups.

The mechanisms behind pH's dominant influence include direct effects on enzyme activity, membrane integrity, and nutrient solubility, as well as indirect effects on the availability of toxic aluminum and manganese in acidic soils.

### Total Organic Carbon (TOC)
Organic carbon content reflects the amount of decomposed plant and animal material in the soil. It directly influences microbial communities by:

- Providing the primary energy source for heterotrophic bacteria
- Improving soil structure through aggregation, creating habitat diversity
- Enhancing water-holding capacity and [[cation-exchange-capacity]]
- Supporting higher microbial biomass and diversity

Soils with high organic carbon (5-20% TOC) support larger and more diverse microbial communities than low-carbon soils (<2% TOC). The [[winston-cannabis-microbiome-study-design]] found organic carbon values ranging from 3.02% to 20.0% across sampling sites, reflecting this range.

### Total Nitrogen
Soil [[nitrogen-availability-in-legumes]] affects microbial [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] through several pathways:

- **Ammonium vs. nitrate dominance**: Different microbial groups prefer different nitrogen forms. Ammonia-oxidizing bacteria (AOB) and archaea (AOA) are directly influenced by ammonium availability.
- **C:N ratio**: The carbon-to-nitrogen ratio of [[humus-and-soil-organic-matter]] influences whether nitrogen is immobilized (high C:N) or mineralized (low C:N) by microbial activity.
- **[[dighton-fungal-greenhouse-gas-nitrogen-saturation]]**: Excessive nitrogen inputs from fertilizers can reduce microbial diversity by favoring copiotrophic taxa at the expense of oligotrophs.

### Salinity
Soil salinity (electrical conductivity) affects microbial communities through osmotic stress:

- Halotolerant and halophilic bacteria dominate saline soils
- High salinity reduces overall microbial diversity and activity
- Specific groups like Halomonas, Salinibacter, and certain Bacillus species are salt-tolerant
- The Cannabis study reported salinity values ranging from 1.73 to 7.15 dS/m across sites

### Soil Texture
The proportions of sand, silt, and clay determine the physical habitat available to microorganisms:

- **Clay particles** (<0.002 mm): Provide large surface area for bacterial attachment, protect bacteria from predation, and enhance water retention. Clay-rich soils support higher microbial biomass.
- **Silt particles** (0.002-0.05 mm): Contribute to soil structure and water-holding capacity.
- **Sand particles** (0.05-2 mm): Provide good drainage and aeration but limited surface area for microbial attachment. Sandy soils tend to have lower microbial biomass.
- The Cannabis study soils were all sandy loam to loam textures with 62-66% sand, 16-18% silt, and 18-21% clay.

### Water Content
Soil moisture influences microbial communities through its effects on:

- Oxygen availability (waterlogged soils become anaerobic)
- Substrate diffusion (nutrients move more slowly in dry soils)
- Osmotic potential (dry conditions increase osmotic stress)
- Microbial activity rates (enzyme kinetics are moisture-dependent)

## Interactions Between Variables

Soil physicochemical properties do not act in isolation. Important interactions include:

### pH-Organic Matter Interaction
- Organic matter buffers soil pH, moderating the effects of acid or alkaline inputs
- In acidic soils, adding organic matter can raise pH by releasing base cations
- pH affects the rate of organic matter decomposition, creating feedback loops

### Texture-Organic Matter Interaction
- Clay particles stabilize organic matter through physical protection and chemical bonding
- Fine-textured soils (high clay) retain more organic carbon than coarse-textured soils
- This interaction creates the well-established correlation between clay content and carbon storage

### Salinity-Moisture Interaction
- Salinity effects are most pronounced when soil moisture is low (salt concentration increases as water evaporates)
- Irrigation can leach salts below the root zone but may also raise the water table
- Seasonal rainfall patterns modulate salinity stress on microbial communities

## Measuring Soil Physicochemical Properties

Professional soil testing laboratories provide standardized measurements:

- **pH**: Measured in 1:1 or 1:2 soil:water suspension using a calibrated pH meter
- **Electrical conductivity (salinity)**: Measured in saturation extract or 1:1 soil:water extract
- **Total carbon and nitrogen**: Measured by combustion analysis (Dumas method) on dried, ground soil samples
