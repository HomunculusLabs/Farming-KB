# Psilocybe Cubensis Strain Varieties and Characteristics

## Overview

*Psilocybe cubensis* is the most widely cultivated [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-amanita-muscaria-emerging-alternative]]
species, and numerous named "strains" or "varieties" are available through
commercial and underground sources. It is important to understand that these
strains are not distinct taxonomic varieties in the botanical sense — they
represent selectively isolated lineages and phenotypic variants within the
same species. Genetic diversity within *P. cubensis* is considerable, and
differences in appearance, growth characteristics, and potency between
strains reflect genetic variation rather than species-level differences.

The EMCDDA thematic paper notes that marketed strain names (Mexican, Thai,
Colombian, Amazonian, Hawaiian, etc.) primarily reflect geographic origin of
the original wild specimen from which the lineage was isolated. Commercial
marketing has created an extensive nomenclature that can be confusing for
consumers.

## The Nature of "Strains"

In mycology, a "strain" typically refers to an isolated genetic lineage
maintained through clonal propagation or consistent multispore cultivation.
Unlike plant breeding, mushroom strain development relies on selecting
individual fruiting bodies with desirable characteristics and isolating their
spores for subsequent generations.

## Potency Considerations

The EMCDDA notes that the most potent mushrooms can contain up to approximately
1% psilocybin/psilocin by dry weight. *P. cubensis* strains typically range
from 0.3% to 0.6%. Within-species variation means that a well-grown "low
potency" strain may exceed a poorly-grown "high potency" strain in actual
alkaloid content.

Factors affecting potency include: substrate composition (more nutritious
substrates tend to produce more potent fruits), [[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]] (potency
peaks just before or as the veil tears and declines as spores mature), drying
method (heat degrades psilocybin; air-drying or [[mushroom-desiccant-drying-methods]] is preferred),
and [[vegetable-storage-conditions-by-temperature-and-humidity]] (light, heat, and moisture all degrade alkaloids).

## Species Beyond Cubensis

Several other genera contain psilocybin but are less commonly cultivated:
*[[psilocybe-azurescens]]* and *P. cyanescens* are among the
most potent species but require specific outdoor growing conditions. *Psilocybe
semilanceata* (liberty cap) is primarily foraged rather than cultivated due
to its complex mycorrhizal requirements.
