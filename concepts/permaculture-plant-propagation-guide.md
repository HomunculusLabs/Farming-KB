# Permaculture Plant Propagation Guide Propagation is the foundation of establishing [[query-what-are-the-best-nut-trees-for-a-permaculture-food-forest]].

## Cutting Propagation

Many species grow from cuttings. Root hormone can be purchased or derived naturally from willows.

### Willow Water Rooting Hormone
Willow cuttings contain natural [[cervantes-rooting-hormones-cloning]] that surpass commercial rooting powders:
1. Place willow cuttings in a bucket of water
2. After ~1 week, roots develop and hormones leach into water
3. Soak cuttings from other plants in this water overnight
4. Place treated cuttings in cutting beds as usual

### Cutting Best Practices
- Cuttings from young trees strike root more easily than from older trees
- Terminal shoots are generally not suitable
- Autumn is the most successful time for many species (e.g., Populus deltoides)
- Phosphorus promotes root development in cuttings
- Even 10 m high poplar cuttings will root if they can access water

## Layering and Aerial Layering

Advanced [[vegetative-propagation-methods-mollison]] for difficult-to-root species:
1. Make small slits into the bark of a stem
2. **Layering**: Lay the stem into a trench and cover with soil
3. **Aerial layering**: Surround the slit stem with medium and wrap with plastic
4. Once rooted, remove from parent plant

## Suckers

Many species form suckers that can be cut from the parent tree and planted out directly. Common in:
- Black locust (Robinia pseudoacacia)
- Poplars (Populus spp)
- Willows (Salix spp)
- Acacia decurrens

## Grafting and Budding

Used to propagate select plant material onto hardy rootstock. Common for:
- Fruit trees (apples, stone fruits, citrus)
- Passionfruit (though rootstock can become a nuisance)
- Carob (known cultivars grafted for earlier fruiting)

## Species-Specific Notes

| Species | Method | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Carob | Seed (scarify), graft | Direct seeding with protective markers possible; deep pots (1L milk cartons) essential for taproot development |
| Fig | Cuttings, layering | 15-25 cm cuttings from short-jointed previous year's growth, spring, sandy soil in warmth |
| Mulberry | Cuttings, easily transplanted | Very easy to propagate |
| Persimmon | Seed, graft, cuttings | Seedlings variable -- buy grafted for true-to-type |
| Blueberry | Seed, cuttings | Acid soil required from the start |
| Bamboo | Division of clumps | For clumping types; running types spread aggressively |
| Pampas grass | Division of clump | Simply divide and plant out |
| Bana grass | Stem cuttings | Lay whole stems in shallow trenches in damp soil |
| Acacia | Seed (scarify) | Boiling water treatment standard |
| Passionfruit | Seed (digestive scarification), cuttings | Fresh seed best; many species difficult from seed |

## Design Notes

- Propagate more plants than needed to allow for selection of the best specimens
- Start a nursery area in [Zone 1 for intensive propagation management
- Use willow water as a natural, free alternative to commercial rooting hormones
- Time propagation activities with seasonal rhythms for maximum success
- Keep detailed records of what works in your specific climate and soil conditions
