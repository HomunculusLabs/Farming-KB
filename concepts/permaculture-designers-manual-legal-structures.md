# Legal Structures
**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, [[bill-mollison]] (1988)
**Updated:** 2026-05-09

## Overview
Mollison devotes significant attention to legal and financial structures
that support permaculture aims. Chapter 14 (Strategies for an
Alternative Nation) covers trusts, cooperatives, credit unions, ethical
investment, and community financial systems. The central message is that
appropriate legal structures enable earth care and people care at scale. These structures build on the [[permaculture-ethics-and-principles]] and are closely related to [[community-land-trusts]] and [[ethical-investment-and-earth-banking]].

## Trust Structures

### Trust A: The Land Trust (For the Public Good)
Trust A holds all real property which is not at risk -- land,
buildings, copyrights, leases, and important equipment clear of debt.
This trust:
- Takes no risks (does not trade)
- Holds assets for the public good
- Is immune from company tax
- Accepts volunteer or contract labour
- Can pay employees
- Can fund any other charity or trust
- Is set up by a foundation director (settlor) who should retire once
  the site has 10-12 residents

Trustees should be limited to 3-4 directors, as larger boards give
endless trouble and necessitate agreement among many people, involving
distasteful accommodations and compromises.

### Trust B: The Trading Trust
Trust B is a separate unit discretionary trust for trading operations.
It:
- Accepts volunteer or contract labour and can pay employees
- Takes normal business risk
- Can rent or lease real property from Trust A
- Is currently immune from company tax
- Reduces family tax
- Enables a wide variety of enterprises under one organisation
- Can duplicate or triplicate itself for new enterprises
- Can insulate successful operations from risk
- Has Trust A as one of its beneficiaries
- Can handle financial systems such as leasing and lending units

## Developmental and Property Trusts (Section 14.8)
No investment in glamour stocks (coal, oil, uranium, properties, paper
pulp, agrochemicals, mining) is likely to yield anything but more
pollution and global collapse. Instead, Mollison advocates turning
money resources to truly rehabilitative ends.

### Purpose
A property trust purchases real estate for improvement, lease, or
rental on behalf of many small investors who cannot afford to
individually own or develop such properties. By improving properties,
their value increases, and (under present rulings) taxation is not
incurred on that increase if the property is held for 10 or more years.

### Structure
- Low unit price ($100) enables even poorer people to invest
- A single unit can be held by a partnership, society, or corporate
  group
- No limit to the number of units held by any person
- Costs should not exceed 4-8% of funds for management
- Investors given opportunity for on-site involvement

### Ethical Trust Project Areas
- Purchase of threatened wildlife and forest habitat
- Purchase and rehabilitation of eroded, salted, deforested, or
  misused land
- Development of energy-efficient, sustainable village developments
- Purchase of selected properties for assisting [[low-cost-mushroom-growing-house-construction-developing-countries]]
- Bioregional clean energy systems or clean transport methods
