# Amazonian Sorcery, Illness, and Curing Practices

In Amazonian shamanic cosmology, illness is rarely viewed as a purely physical phenomenon. Most diseases are attributed to sorcery -- the deliberate harmful actions of witch doctors (brujos) or sorcerers against their victims. The vegetalista (plant-healer) serves as both diagnostician and therapist, using ayahuasca visions to identify the nature and source of illness and employing icaros, plant medicines, and spiritual defenses to cure it.

## Sorcery Weapons

Amazonian sorcerers employ a sophisticated arsenal of magical weapons:

### Yachay (Magical Phlegm)

Yachay is a magical phlegm that the sorcerer or healer stores in their body, typically in the throat or stomach. It can be projected as an invisible weapon to cause illness from a distance, or used by the healer to extract disease from a patient through sucking (chupada). Different types of yachay include:

- **Yachay-huani**: A bow with an arrow used by both sorcerers and healers
- **Yachay-trueno** (thunder phlegm): Used by powerful sorcerers
- White yachay: Medicinal, used for healing

### Mariri

Mariri refers to the magical darts and substances used in sorcery. They appear as vibrant tubes in ayahuasca visions from which different types of sorcery emerge. Types include:
- Snake-type mariri (Sachamama sorcery)
- Marupa sorcery mariri (animal-influenced)
- Chonta sorcery mariri (made from palm needles, fish horns, snake teeth, bird beaks)

### Virotes

Virotes are magical darts or needles prepared from various materials: palm trees (chonta, pona, inchahui, huasai, inayuga), thorns, fish horns, snake teeth, and bird beaks. The banco swallows virotes to gain power. Virotes are central to chonta sorcery.

## Types of Sorcery Illness

### Kapukiri

Kapukiri is a substance that comes from rotten leaves -- a dark brown vapor that rises and collects in the atmosphere. Different trees produce different types of kapukiri with distinct symptoms:

- **Puka-lupuna** kapukiri: Head feels enlarged, smarting all over body, illness in stomach. Newborns of affected pregnant women appear rachitic.
- **Ajosquiro** kapukiri: Extreme nervousness, fear, sensation of worm biting
- **Catahua negra** kapukiri: Dry mouth, cracked lips, nerve contraction, person shrinks, fever, stuttering
- **Huairacaspi** kapukiri: Most dangerous -- walking in slow motion, ear pain, skin turns grey as if burned. Death in 3-5 days with heavy vomiting.

The kapukiri can only be cured by a vegetalista who knows the specific icaro for each type. Catahua negra leaves (Hura crepitans) are used in treatment.

### Cungatuya (Cuncatuya)

A disease contracted from contaminated water. A sorcerer sends his mashu (bat) to drop yachay (magical phlegm) into a water jar. The victim becomes weak and cannot eat or drink, eventually dying. The worms of this disease suck blood and act as ticks that open wounds in the throat.

### Boa Chichuchishca

"_begot by a boa" -- caused when a menstruating woman leaves wet underwear in a canoe at the riverside. A boa places itself on the clothes and excretes something living that later gives birth inside the woman. The vegetalista must sing icaros to make the boa come out, using jagua fruit (Genipa Americana) and the icaros of diamonds, gold, silver, and precious stones as protection.

## The Diet (La Dieta)

Central to Amazonian curing is the rigorous dietary regimen (dieta) that the patient must follow after treatment. The diet is essential -- if the patient breaks it (e.g., by eating hen's meat, which is very bad during a diet), the sickness returns with greater force and may cause death.

The diet is also a prerequisite for becoming a vegetalista. An initiate who breaks the diet will see the angash-machohualipa (brave blue cock) appear as a warning sign.

## Curing Methods

### Sucking (Chupada)

The vegetalista uses the yachay of his throat to suck out the illness from the patient's body. The extracted illness is contained and disposed of.

### Icaros

Specific songs for specific illnesses. Each sorcery and disease type has its corresponding icaro. The vegetalista also uses icaros to call spirit allies (arkana) for defense.

### Plant Medicines

Various plants are used in treatment, often prepared with icaros and blowing (soplada):
- Catahua negra (Hura crepitans) for kapukiri
- Jagua/huito (Genipa Americana) for boa-related illness
- Pucunucho and rocoto (Capsicum sp.) to stun snakes
- Jergon-sacha (Dracontium loretense) for snakebites

### Spiritual Defenses

During curing, the vegetalista raises tingunas (colored energy barriers), posts animals of prey as guardians, and creates magnetic power disks to prevent disease microbes from escaping. The arkana (spiritual defense) is raised to protect against enemy attacks.

## The Millaipa-Huarmi (Ugly Women)

These two women -- one young (sky-blue dress), one old (lilac dress) -- perform a crucial role in curanderismo. The younger one picks up the sickness extracted from the patient. If the patient does not follow the diet, she cannot hold back the evil spell and returns it, making the patient worse. The older woman places the evil spell in the whirlwind of her large dress, making it disappear permanently.
