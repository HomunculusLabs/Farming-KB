## Amazonian Sorcery, Illness, and Curing Practices

In [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]] to identify the nature and source of illness and employing icaros, plant medicines, and spiritual defenses to cure it.

## Sorcery Weapons

Amazonian sorcerers employ a sophisticated arsenal of magical weapons:

### Yachay (Magical Phlegm)

Yachay is a magical phlegm that the sorcerer or healer stores in their body, typically in the throat or stomach. It can be projected as an invisible weapon to cause illness from a distance, or used by the healer to extract disease from a patient through sucking (chupada). Different types of yachay include:

- **Yachay-huani**: A bow with an arrow used by both sorcerers and healers
- **Yachay-trueno** (thunder phlegm): Used by powerful sorcerers
- White yachay: Medicinal, used for healing

### Mariri

Mariri refers to the magical darts and substances used in sorcery. They appear as vibrant tubes in [[genipa-americana]]) and the icaros of diamonds, gold, silver, and precious stones as protection.

## The Diet (La Dieta)

Central to Amazonian curing is the rigorous dietary regimen (dieta) that the patient must follow after treatment. The diet is essential -- if the patient breaks it (e.g., by eating hen's meat, which is very bad during a diet), the sickness returns with greater force and may cause death.

The diet is also a prerequisite for becoming a vegetalista. An initiate who breaks the diet will see the angash-machohualipa (brave blue cock) appear as a warning sign.

## Curing Methods

### Sucking (Chupada)

The vegetalista uses the yachay of his throat to suck out the illness from the patient's body. The extracted illness is contained and disposed of.

### Icaros

Specific songs for specific illnesses. Each sorcery and disease type has its corresponding icaro. The vegetalista also uses icaros to call spirit allies (arkana) for defense.

### Plant Medicines

Various plants are used in treatment, often prepared with icaros and blowing (soplada):
- Catahua negra (Hura crepitans) for kapukiri
- Jagua/huito (Genipa Americana) for boa-related illness
- Pucunucho and rocoto (Capsicum sp.) to stun snakes
- Jergon-sacha (Dracontium loretense) for snakebites

### Spiritual Defenses

During curing, the vegetalista raises tingunas (colored energy barriers), posts animals of prey as guardians, and creates magnetic power disks to prevent disease microbes from escaping. The arkana (spiritual defense) is raised to protect against enemy attacks.

## The Millaipa-Huarmi (Ugly Women)

These two women -- one young (sky-blue dress), one old (lilac dress) -- perform a crucial role in curanderismo. The younger one picks up the sickness extracted from the patient. If the patient does not follow the diet, she cannot hold back the evil spell and returns it, making the patient worse. The older woman places the evil spell in the whirlwind of her large dress, making it disappear permanently.
