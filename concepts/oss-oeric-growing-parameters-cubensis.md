# Oss & Oeric: Growing Parameters for Psilocybe Cubensis

Successful indoor [[mushroom-cultivation-environmental-parameters]]. The 1976 guide provides

earliest documented environmental requirements for indoor fruiting.

## Temperature Requirements

**[[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]] is 70-80 degrees F. The authors emphasize
maintaining roughly constant temperature within this range to promote
rapid and complete colonization. Temperature variability is suspected
as a factor in slowed growth, particularly in high-altitude, low-humidity
environments like Colorado, where mycelial growth appears retarded
compared to warmer coastal regions like California.

**Fruiting Stage:** The guide does not specify a distinct fruiting
temperature but implies continued growth at room temperature with the
casing in place. Modern cultivators typically find that a slight
temperature drop (to 70-75 degrees F) after casing can stimulate pin
formation, though this technique is not explicitly described in the
original guide.

**Sterilization Temperatures:** Media and substrates are sterilized at
15-20 lbs pressure, achieving approximately 250 degrees F for 45-60
minutes. The extended time beyond the standard 15 minutes was found
necessary for complete sterilization under home conditions. See
[[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] and development.
The guide specifies approximately 95% relative humidity for rye
colonization, and implies similar or higher levels during fruiting.
Daily fine-mist spraying is required, with 2-3 good squirts per cased
jar per day.

**Casing Soil Moisture:** The casing soil must be maintained at field
capacity, meaning thoroughly moistened but not saturated. No water should
pass through the casing into the mycelium below.

## Lighting Parameters

The authors found that mushrooms respond favorably to light, with a daily
10-hour Grow-lux fluorescent cycle producing larger caps and shorter
stems. Direct sunlight should be avoided, as it causes approximately
half the developing mushrooms to abort. Prolonged exposure to strong
light also causes mature mushrooms to age and rot, since they are over
90% water. The 10-hour photoperiod aligns with later research by Badham
(1980) which found blue and UV wavelengths (370-460nm) most effective
for fruitbody initiation. See

and proper aeration is essential for growth. Tight lids prevent gas
exchange and stall colonization.

**During Fruiting:** The styrofoam cooler with polyethylene lid covering
provides passive [[growing-gourmet-growing-room-ventilation-air-exchange]]. The semi-permeable covering allows CO2
to escape while maintaining humidity. Modern understanding emphasizes
that during colonization, elevated CO2 (above 10,000 ppm) promotes
mycelial growth, while during fruiting, lower CO2 levels (400-1000 ppm)
are needed for normal fruitbody development.
