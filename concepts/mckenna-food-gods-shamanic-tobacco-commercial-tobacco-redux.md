## The Smoking Revolution: From Maya Cigars to Global Pandemic

Four years after Columbus's first voyage, the hermit Romano Pane, whom Columbus
had left in Haiti, described in his journal the native habit of inhaling tobacco
fumes through a bird-bone instrument inserted into the nose and held over tobacco
strewn on a bed of coals. McKenna calls the consequences of this simple
ethnographic observation incalculable. It introduced into Europe an extremely
efficient method for conveying drugs into the human body—one that made possible
the worldwide pandemic of tobacco smoking, provided a fast-acting route for
administering opium and hashish, and became the distant ancestor of the crack
cocaine and PCP smoker. "It also, it must be said, makes possible the most
profound of the indole-hallucinogen-induced ecstasies, the rarely encountered but
incomparable practice of smoking dimethyltryptamine."
