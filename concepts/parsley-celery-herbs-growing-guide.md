# Parsley and Culinary Herbs in the Vegetable Garden

Updated: 2026-04-18

Steve Solomon considers parsley a misunderstood vegetable that is easy to grow once you understand its [[query-what-is-seed-starting-and-when-should-i-start-seeds-indoors]] in mid-spring, about when the daffodils have faded.
- Sow seed about a quarter inch deep in a furrow.
- Cover with finely textured mellow soil or compost.
- Germination is slow -- can take 2-3 weeks.
- Thin progressively to about 4 inches (10 cm) apart.
- Once plants are established, begin plucking outer leaves.

### Growing Parsley

- Parsley does not aggressively mine the subsoil like its relatives carrot and parsnip.
- If production is disappointing, side-dress with something containing nitrogen and ensure adequate moisture.
- In mild-winter areas, parsley may continue producing all winter.
- In hot-summer areas, start after summer heat passes and erect temporary shade.
- Parsley is a biennial and a relative of the carrot. Make seed as you would for carrots.

### Hamburg Parsley

A variety grown for its edible roots rather than greens. The roots taste much like parsnip and may be eaten raw, grated into salads, or cooked. Grow these slow-to-mature roots with about 5 by 18 inches (13 by 45 cm) spacing.

### Herbal Celery Alternative

For gardeners with clay soil where regular celery will not thrive, Solomon recommends "Chinese" celery or "cutting" celery -- a short-stalked type with a stronger flavor, used wherever regular celery might be included in a recipe. This herbal form is far better adapted to poor soil conditions.

## Celery

### Soil Requirements

Celery requires deep sandy loam to grow to perfection. Soils containing more than about 25% clay will not grow great celery. Clay soils (more than half clay) are out of the question entirely.
