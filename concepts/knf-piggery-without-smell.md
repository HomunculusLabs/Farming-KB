# KNF Piggery Without Smell

updated: 2026-04-18

One of Dr. Cho Han Kyu's most celebrated achievements in Korean Natural Farming (KNF) is the development of an odorless piggery system. The conventional pig farming industry has long struggled with the problems of foul odor, wastewater pollution, and the need for expensive waste treatment facilities. Dr. Cho's experiments related to pollution-free piggery are described as "simply superb and beyond the imagination of mainstream agriculture scientists."

## The Problem with Conventional Pig Housing

In conventional pig farming, facilities require expensive waste treatment infrastructure to manage the massive amounts of manure and wastewater produced. The smell is offensive to neighbors, the waste pollutes waterways, and the cost of maintaining hygiene is significant. Cleaning pig pens is labor-intensive and the use of chemicals and antibiotics is common. The livestock housing itself becomes a source of environmental contamination rather than a closed-loop system.

## The KNF Approach: Zero Emission

The KNF piggery system is designed around the concept of "zero emission" -- no wastewater is emitted and all wastes are recycled and converted to resources. The housing itself serves as a waste treatment facility, fertilizer factory, and feed mixer all in one. This is accomplished through the use of [[knf-indigenous-microorganism-culture]], deep bedding, and careful management of the microbial environment.

## Deep Litter Bedding System

The core of the odorless piggery is a deep litter floor system. Instead of concrete floors (which are standard in conventional operations), the KNF piggery uses a soil-based floor covered with organic bedding material. The system works as follows:

1. **Floor construction:** The flooring must be soil-based, not concrete. Separating the animals from soil contact can cause disease and prevents the natural microbial processes from functioning. A deep layer of bedding material is laid over the soil floor.

2. **Bedding materials:** Rice straw, sawdust, rice husks, and other organic materials are used as bedding. These materials serve as carbon sources for microbial fermentation. The bedding is typically 50 to 70 cm deep, providing sufficient mass for the composting process.

3. **IMO inoculation:** [[knf-indigenous-microorganism-culture]] (the advanced stage of prepared indigenous microorganisms) is mixed into the bedding along with [[lactic-acid-bacteria-knf]] and other KNF inputs. These microorganisms rapidly colonize the bedding and begin the fermentation process.

4. **Continuous fermentation:** As pigs excrete manure and urine onto the bedding, the IMOs and other beneficial microorganisms immediately begin breaking down the waste through aerobic fermentation. This process is exothermic (generates heat), which further accelerates decomposition and creates a warm, comfortable environment for the pigs.

5. **No cleaning required:** The feces do not need to be removed unless the fermented material is needed for compost. The microorganisms break down the waste so completely that there is no offensive odor. The pigs effectively live on a continuously composting floor.

## How Odor Elimination Works

The absence of smell is achieved through several mechanisms:

- **Competitive exclusion:** The beneficial IMOs and LAB dominate the microbial ecology of the bedding, outcompeting putrefying (odor-causing) bacteria. When good microorganisms are in control, bad microorganisms cannot proliferate.

- **Aerobic fermentation:** The deep litter system is designed to maintain aerobic conditions (unlike the anaerobic conditions of manure pits, which produce foul-smelling gases like hydrogen sulfide and ammonia). The carbon-rich bedding absorbs moisture and maintains air spaces for oxygen flow.

- **Rapid decomposition:** The high microbial activity ensures that waste is broken down quickly before it can putrefy. The end products of aerobic fermentation are carbon dioxide, water, and humus -- none of which produce offensive odors.

- **pH management:** The fermentation process maintains an appropriate pH that inhibits the growth of odor-producing organisms. [[knf-brown-rice-vinegar]] can be added to the bedding to help maintain optimal conditions.

## Additional Management Practices

- **Moisture control:** The bedding must be monitored for moisture content. If it becomes too wet, additional dry bedding material is added. If too dry, small amounts of water mixed with LAB can be sprayed to maintain microbial activity.

- **Temperature monitoring:** The fermentation generates heat, which keeps pigs warm in cold weather. In hot climates, the bedding depth and composition may need adjustment to prevent excessive heating.

- **Occasional turning:** The bedding may need to be turned periodically to maintain aeration, though the pigs' rooting behavior often provides sufficient mixing.

- **Feed management:** The pigs' feed influences the composition of their manure. KNF-fed pigs produce waste that is more easily broken down by the microbial community.

## The End Product: Premium Compost

After several months to a year of operation, the deep litter bedding is transformed into a rich, dark, humus-like compost. This material, sometimes called IMO-5 when prepared with additional inputs, is an excellent soil amendment. It contains not only decomposed organic matter but also a thriving community of beneficial microorganisms that can be applied to fields to improve soil health. The composted litter from the piggery floor is a complete fertilizer, providing macronutrients, micronutrients, and beneficial biology.

## Integration with Crop Production

The KNF piggery is designed as part of an integrated farming system. The composted litter is used to fertilize crop fields, crop residues are used as bedding material, and agricultural by-products can supplement pig feed. This creates a closed nutrient loop where nothing is wasted.

## Benefits and Economic Advantages

The odorless piggery system offers significant economic advantages over conventional pig farming:

- **Eliminated waste treatment costs:** No need for expensive wastewater treatment infrastructure, slurry tanks, or manure separation equipment. The bedding system itself handles all waste processing.
- **Reduced labor:** The deep litter system requires minimal daily maintenance. No regular pen cleaning is needed, freeing labor for other farm tasks.
- **Premium compost output:** The end product is high-quality, microbially-rich compost that can be sold or applied to farm fields, reducing or eliminating the need for purchased fertilizers.
- **Improved animal health:** The natural environment reduces stress and disease incidence, lowering veterinary costs and medication use. Pigs raised in this system are generally healthier and require fewer interventions.
- **Community acceptance:** The absence of odor allows pig farming to be located closer to residential areas without complaints, expanding site options for smallholders.

## Scalability and Adaptation

Dr. Cho's system has been successfully adapted across diverse climates and scales, from small family operations with 5-10 pigs to larger commercial facilities. The key principles -- soil-based floors, IMO inoculation, deep carbon bedding, and aerobic management -- remain constant regardless of scale. In tropical regions, increased bedding depth and more frequent moisture management may be needed. In temperate zones, the heat generated by fermentation provides supplemental warmth during cold months, reducing heating costs.

## Comparison with [[knf-chicken-house-design]]

Both the odorless piggery and the KNF chicken house share the same fundamental philosophy: soil-based floors, IMO inoculation, zero cleaning, and zero emission. However, the piggery uses a deeper bedding system (50-70 cm vs. 3-7 cm) due to the higher volume of waste produced by pigs. The chicken house design emphasizes behavioral enrichment (brooding boxes, perches, curtain "mother hen"), while the piggery focuses more on large-scale waste processing and compost production.
