 The Other and Sentient Presence

Central to Simon G. Powell's thesis is the concept of the "Other," a
transcendental sentient presence that is perceived during entheogenic
experiences. Powell argues that through the action of psilocybin upon the
brain, individuals encounter what appears to be an intelligent, intentional
communicating entity distinct from the self or ego. This Other manifests as
a powerful flow of organised symbolic information that compels the experiencer
to infer a communicating intelligence of some kind.

## Encountering the Other

During the psilocybin-induced visionary state with eyes closed, one is
confronted with what Powell describes as "a powerful communicatory flow of
organised symbolic information." This overwhelming impression of intentional
communication leaves no room for doubt during the experience itself. The
Reverend S.J., one of the subjects of the Good [[powell-gaian-mind-and-entheogenic-flora]] or Other, he argues, is
information, or at least the creative organising principle underlying
brain-based information-integration and informational patterning. Its
language is that of symbols and cultural images. Through the "redemptive
action" of sacramental Gaian alkaloids like psilocybin, the Other is able
to manifest itself and flow through the neuronal architecture of the brain.
Information appears as if alive and intent upon self-organisation.

## Big O and Little O

Powell draws an analogy between inferring the transcendental Other and the
everyday inference that other human minds exist. Both the big "O" (the Other)
and the little "o" (other people) are equally conceived of as focus points
of intentional information processing. Just as we cannot absolutely prove
that other minds exist apart from our own, we cannot definitively prove or
disprove the existence of the Other. Yet the inference "works" in both
cases, providing a coherent framework for understanding experience.
