## The Tryptamine Family

The tryptamine family comprises a vast array of compounds built on the indole-ethylamine scaffold. As cataloged in TIHKAL (see [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]]), 55 compounds span the range from simple unsubstituted tryptamine through complex lysergamides and beta-carbolines.

### Core Structure

The basic tryptamine skeleton consists of:
- An indole ring (benzene fused to pyrrole)
- A 3-(2-aminoethyl) side chain at the 3-position of the indole

The indole ring provides substitution positions (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7), and the side chain nitrogen can be mono-, di-, or tri-substituted. This gives enormous combinatorial potential.

### Major Subfamilies

**Simple dialkyltryptamines** (D, N series):
- T (tryptamine), NMT (N-methyltryptamine), DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine)
- DET, DPT, DIBT — extending the alkyl groups (ethyl, propyl, isobutyl)
- Activity generally decreases with alkyl chains longer than propyl

**Alpha-substituted tryptamines** (alpha series):
- alpha-MT (alpha-methyltryptamine), alpha-ET
- Alpha-methyl substitution dramatically extends duration (8-12 hours vs 1-2 hours)
- Trade names existed: Indopan (alpha-MT monotherapy)

**Ring-substituted tryptamines**:
- 4-HO-DMT (psilocin) — see [[psilocin-and-psilocybin-chemistry]]
- 5-MeO-DMT — see [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]]
- 5-MeS-DMT (methylthio analogue)
- 4,5-MDO-DMT, 5,6-MDO-DMT (methylenedioxy bridges)
- Ring substitution at 4-position confers [[tihkal-oral-activity-mao-inhibition]]; at 5-position increases smoked potency

**Thiophenes** (furan bioisosteres):
- 5-thienyl substituted tryptamines — isosteric replacements of the indole benzene ring

**Lysergamides** (ergoline derivatives):
- LSD, AL-LAD, ETH-LAD, LSM ([[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] morpholide), LSP
- Dramatically more potent (microgram range vs milligram range)
- Complex tetracyclic structure incorporating the tryptamine pharmacophore
- See [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] for comparison with phenethylamine psychedelics

**Beta-carbolines** (C series):
- Harmine, harmaline, tetrahydroharmine
- Primarily MAO inhibitors (see [[ayahuasca-pharmacology]])
- Some have mild psychedelic activity in their own right

### Structure-Activity Relationships

Key SAR principles from the Shulgin data:

1. **Oral activity**: Only 4-hydroxylated tryptamines (psilocin, psilocybin) are reliably orally active without an MAOI. The 4-OH group confers metabolic resistance.
2. **Route dependence**: Non-4-substituted dialkyltryptamines are inactive orally (>350 mg DMT) but potent parenterally (60 mg i.m.) or by smoking (60 mg).
3. **Potency**: Adding a 5-methoxy group increases potency 4-6x for smoking but does not confer oral activity.
4. **Duration**: Alpha-methyl substitution on the ethyl side chain extends duration from ~1 hour to 8-12+ hours by blocking MAO access to the alpha-carbon.
5. **N-alkyl chain**: Activity drops sharply beyond N,N-dipropyl. Bulky groups decrease receptor affinity.
6. **Visual character**: 4-substituted compounds tend toward richer, more colorful visual experiences. 5-methoxy compounds tend toward [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]] with less visual content.
7. **Ion mass signatures**: Tertiary amines show 130+ as a minor MS peak; secondary amines show 131+ exceeding 130+ — useful analytical fingerprint.

### Naming Convention

See [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]] for full details. In brief:
- First letter(s): substitution pattern on nitrogen (D=di, N=mono, T=tri)
- Middle: alkyl group abbreviations (M=methyl, E=ethyl, P=propyl)
- Last: T for tryptamine, C for carboline, S for serotonin
- Ring substitutions prefixed: 4-HO-, 5-MeO-, 2-Me-, etc.
- Alpha-substitution denoted with "alpha" prefix

### Natural Occurrence

Tryptamines are among the most widely distributed psychoactive alkaloids in nature. DMT alone has been identified in over 65 plant species spanning at least 8 botanical families. The distribution extends across every continent except Antarctica, suggesting an ancient evolutionary role.
