## Saprophytic Mushrooms

Saprophytic mushrooms are fungi that obtain their nutrients by decomposing
dead organic matter. They are the primary recyclers in terrestrial ecosystems,
breaking down complex plant materials — particularly lignin, cellulose, and
hemicellulose — and converting them into simpler compounds that can be
reabsorbed by plants and other organisms. Saprophytic mushrooms include the
majority of cultivated gourmet and medicinal species, as their nutritional
mode makes them far more amenable to artificial cultivation than mycorrhizal
or parasitic fungi.

## Key Cultivated Saprophytic Species

### Oyster Mushrooms (*Pleurotus* spp.)

Oyster mushrooms are among the easiest and most rewarding species to cultivate.
The genus includes many species with diverse colors, flavors, and growing
preferences:

- **Tree Oyster** (*P. ostreatus*): The most widely cultivated, grows on a
  broad range of hardwood substrates
- **King Oyster** (*P. eryngii*): Large, thick-stemmed mushroom with
  excellent shelf life and culinary qualities
- **Golden Oyster** (*P. citrinopileatus*): Bright yellow, fast-growing,
  and highly productive
- **Pink Oyster** (*P. djamor*): Tropical species requiring warm temperatures,
  vivid pink coloration
