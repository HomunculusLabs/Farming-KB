## JADAM Nutrient Cycling — Traditional Farming and Modern Mineral Science

## Overview

JADAM bridges traditional farming wisdom with modern mineral nutrition science, arguing that the traditional approach of returning to the soil what was harvested is more scientifically sound than complex fertilizer programs based on growth stage analysis.

## Traditional Self-Sufficient Farming

### What Worked Before

"They did not have complicated fertilizer programs, back-breaking turning of compost, sophisticated pest control technologies, no difficult books or trainings; but still they did well. They were self-sufficient down to the seeds. It was a farming completely free from commercial capital."

JADAM holds up pre-industrial farming as evidence that effective agriculture does not require complex inputs or expensive equipment. The key was self-sufficiency—from seeds to fertilizers to pest control.

### Against Growth Stage Fertilization

"Fertilizer experts will tell you that in the early stage of plant growth (nutritional growth cycle), the plant needs a lot of nitrogen... When the plant flowers and fruits, it needs a lot of phosphorus... Learn all this, and farmer"s brain will overload."

Cho critiques the increasingly complex growth-stage fertilization programs promoted by agricultural advisors. While scientifically sound in principle, these programs create dependency on purchased inputs and require expertise that most farmers don"t have.

## The Nutrient Loop

### Open vs. Closed Systems

"You see, farming is critically different to nature; its nutrient loop is open. There cannot be zero-input agriculture."

This is a key concession from JADAM: unlike natural ecosystems, which recycle nutrients in closed loops, agriculture removes nutrients at harvest. Therefore, some input is always necessary to replace what is removed.

### The Filling Principle

"Fertilizer is to bring back the nutrients taken out of the soil from harvest. What you have emptied, you need to fill back in. When filling it back, you have to fill it up with something similar to the thing that has been taken out."

This principle guides JADAM"s approach to fertilizer selection: the best fertilizer is one that resembles the composition of the harvested crop. If you harvest grain (high carbon), return carbon-rich materials; if you harvest fruit (high mineral content), return mineral-rich materials.

### Against Conventional Organic Fertilizing

"They bring in all kinds of organic matter that is totally different from the nutrients that have been taken out. Rice bran, perilla pulp, oil cakes and livestock manure are their favorites. This popular method of organic fertilizing comes from not understanding the basic principle of soil nutrient management."

JADAM argues that mainstream organic farming commits the same error as conventional farming: applying inputs based on what is cheap and available rather than on what the soil actually needs.

## Climate Change and Leaching

"Climate change and changed or increased pattern in rainfall accelerates the leaching of nutrients from the soil. It makes additional fertilizer all the more important."

JADAM acknowledges that changing precipitation patterns are increasing nutrient losses, making the need for efficient fertilizer management even more critical.

## Mineral Elements

### The Optimum Level Table

JADAM provides detailed tables of optimum element concentrations in plant tissue, covering both macronutrients and micronutrients. These tables serve as reference guides for diagnosing nutrient deficiencies and excesses.

### Key Minerals Described

**Silicon (Si)**: "Plants accumulate a significant amount of silicon in their tissues. An adequate supply of silicon stimulates growth and reproduction. If silicon is deficient, plants easily topple over and become vulnerable to pathogens."

**Boron (B)**: "Its deficiency causes black necrosis on young leaves, and terminal buds. Stems become abnormally stiff and crumbly."

**Potassium (K)**: Essential for fruit quality, disease resistance, and water regulation.

JADAM's approach to mineral nutrition is pragmatic: understand what each element does, observe deficiency symptoms in the field, and address deficiencies through the liquid fertilizer system.
