# Trichome and Resin Production

The THC chemical is produced in leaves, flowers, and stalked glandular trichomes, lovingly referred to as "resin glands" or simply "trichomes." Understanding trichome types, development stages, and factors affecting resin production is essential for maximizing cannabinoid content and determining optimal harvest time.

## Types of Trichomes

A trichome is a "plant hair." Cannabis produces several distinct types of trichomes:

### Stalked Glandular Trichomes (Capitate Trichomes)
These are the primary resin-producing glands found on drug cannabis. They are glandular and secretory in character, comprised of a stalk with a resin head that looks like a post with a knob on top. They form on buds and small leaves surrounding buds. The highest concentration of THC is located at the base of the resin head. These are the trichomes that contain the psychoactive cannabinoids and are the primary target of harvest timing decisions.

### Cystolith Trichomes
Cystolith trichomes have a pointed tip and are often long and hair-like. These protective trichomes are most common on leaf undersides. Cystolith glands exude insecticidal and miticidal substances that gum up pest mouthparts and repel them, but they contain virtually no THC. They are defensive structures, not cannabinoid factories.

### Bulbous Trichomes
Small, barely visible to the naked eye, bulbous trichomes appear on the surface of leaves and stems. They produce minimal resin compared to capitate trichomes.

## Trichome Development and Color Changes

Resin glands change color as they ripen, providing the best indicator of harvest readiness:

1. **Clear**: Immature glands. THC content is still developing. Harvesting at this stage produces a more racy, cerebral high but lower overall potency.
2. **Translucent milky/creamy**: Peak ripeness. The high will be both cerebral and physical with a good head and body stone. Harvest when half (50%) of trichomes have turned this color for absolute peak ripeness.
3. **Amber**: Late stage. Senescing glands start to turn brown and get smaller; they are decomposing and THC content diminishes. Harvest when the majority of trichomes are amber for a heavy body stone. Pure indica, afghani, and indica-dominant strains harvested at this stage possess a heavy, couch-lock stone. Sativa-dominant varieties are best harvested earlier when glands are clear to milky.

Trichomes are very delicate and can be ruptured easily. Even minor handling can tear the head off a trichome or cause it to lose its head completely. Once the glandular head is damaged, the cannabinoids within begin degrading.

## Factors Affecting Resin Production

### Light Intensity
Higher light intensity promotes greater resin production. Plants receiving intense, quality light produce more glandular trichomes than those in low-light conditions. UV light in particular may stimulate additional cannabinoid production as a plant defense mechanism.

### Temperature
Moderate temperatures (70-80°F / 21-27°C) favor optimal resin production. Excessive heat above 85°F (29°C) can reduce resin production and accelerate THC degradation. Cool nighttime temperatures during late flowering (55-65°F / 13-18°C) may enhance resin production and color development.

### Nutrient Status
Adequate phosphorus and potassium during flowering support resin gland development. However, excessive nitrogen late in flowering can reduce resin production and impair bud quality. Withholding water slightly in the final days before harvest can stress plants into producing more resin as a survival response.

### Genetics
The genetic makeup of the plant is the primary determinant of resin production potential. Some strains naturally produce abundant, large glandular trichomes while others produce relatively few. Indica and afghani strains typically produce denser resin coverage than sativa strains.

### Stress
Moderate stress (slight underwatering, temperature fluctuations, UV exposure) may increase resin production as a defense response. However, severe stress reduces overall plant health and can decrease total cannabinoid yield despite potentially higher concentrations per gland.

## Trichome Inspection

Check buds every day starting the sixth week of flowering. Check several buds from different plants to ensure the maximum amount of trichomes are ripe for harvest. Use a 30x-100x magnifying loupe or jeweler's scope for accurate assessment. Long-blooming equatorial sativas may require several harvests as buds at the top of the plant often reach peak potency a few days to a couple of weeks before lower buds.

## Protecting Resin During Harvest

Tender resin glands are protected from bruises and rupture by leaving larger leaves on branches to act as a protective sheath until final manicuring. Handle buds as gently as possible during harvest and manicuring. Friction from fondling hands degrades THC. Prolonged periods of light, temperatures above 80°F (27°C), and damp, humid conditions should be avoided because they all degrade THC.
