# Psilocybe cubensis

Psilocybe cubensis (also known as Stropharia cubensis) is the most
commonly cultivated and commercially marketed hallucinogenic mushroom in
the world. It belongs to the Strophariaceae family and contains the
psychoactive compounds psilocybin and psilocin, described in detail in
[[psilocybin-chemistry-pharmacology]]. Of all psilocybin-containing
mushrooms, the cubensis varieties are the most frequently sold through
smartshops, online retailers, and market stalls across Europe.

## Taxonomy and Classification

There are more than 100 known hallucinogenic mushroom species. The
complexity of their mycological classification, together with differences
in chemical composition and effects, has led to inconsistencies in their
description. The list of species and their geographical distribution is
constantly revised by mycologists. The major genera of psilocybin-containing
fungi include:

- **Strophariaceae family**: Genus Psilocybe (the predominant genus in
  recreational use)
- **Bolbitiaceae family**: Genus Conocybe
- **Coprinaceae family**: Genera Copelandia and Panaeolus
- **Cortinariaceae family**: Genus Inocybe

Psilocybe cubensis is the most important species in commercial trade.
Named varieties sold by retailers include Mexican, Thai, Colombian, and
Amazonian strains. These varieties do not grow wild in Europe; they are
cultivated primarily in the Netherlands for the retail market.

## Appearance and Identification

Nearly all psilocybin-containing mushrooms are small, brown, or tan
coloured, and could easily be mistaken for non-psychoactive, inedible, or
poisonous mushrooms in the wild. The primary distinguishing feature of
most psilocybin-containing mushrooms is that they bruise blue when
handled, caused by the oxidation of psilocin and related compounds.

This blueing reaction is an important but not infallible identification
marker. Some non-psychoactive mushrooms also exhibit blueing, and not
all psilocybin mushrooms show the reaction equally. Proper identification
requires knowledge of gill structure, spore print colour, cap shape, and
habitat.

## Potency

Psilocybe cubensis typically contains psilocybin and psilocin at
concentrations up to 0.6% by dry weight. This places it in the
moderate potency range among psilocybin mushrooms. More potent species
include Psilocybe semilanceata (up to 1% psilocybin) and Psilocybe
azurescens, which has been reported with even higher concentrations.
The potency of any individual mushroom depends on its species, variety,
origin, growing conditions, and age.

## Cultivation

Most recreationally used mushrooms are cultivated rather than picked wild.
The cubensis varieties are cultivated specifically for the market because
they are easy to grow in terrariums and possess a low water content that
allows them to stay fresh for 7 to 10 days. Several cultivation products
are sold commercially:

- **Fresh mushrooms**: Whole fruiting bodies, sold in quantities
  typically ranging from 10 to 30 grams per pack
- **Dried mushrooms**: Dehydrated for longer shelf life
- **Spore prints**: Created by pressing the gills of a mature mushroom
  onto paper; used to inoculate substrate for home cultivation
- **Spore syringes**: Spores suspended in sterile solution for easier
  inoculation
- **Spawn bags**: Sterile hardwood chips colonised with mushroom mycelium
- **Growkits**: Complete kits containing colonised substrate, a bag with
  air filter, and instructions; capable of producing multiple flushes of
  mushrooms

The Free Spore Ring Europe (fsre.org) distributes various strains for
approximately 1 EUR per print as a non-profit service. Instructions for
cultivation using spore prints and substrate are widely available on the
internet.

## Sclerotia and Truffles

Some varieties of Psilocybe, notably Psilocybe mexicana and Psilocybe
tampanensis, form sclerotia, which are hardened masses of mycelium more
resistant to adverse environmental conditions (dryness, cold, heat,
excessive moisture) than normal mycelium. These sclerotia are sold
commercially as "truffles" or "philosopher's stone" and contain
psilocybin and psilocin.

## Wild European Species

A variety of psilocybin-containing mushroom species grow wild in Europe,
particularly throughout central and northern Europe. Their habitats
include wet grassy fields and uncultivated pastures. The most common wild
European species is Psilocybe semilanceata (liberty caps), found in
countries including the UK, Norway, and Germany. Other wild species
include Psilocybe cyanescens (wavy caps), and Psilocybe bohemica and
Psilocybe moravica, particularly reported in the Czech Republic.

Evidence suggests that while wild mushroom hunting does occur in some EU
countries (Poland, Ireland, Czech Republic), the majority of
recreationally consumed mushrooms in Europe are cultivated rather than
foraged.

## Route of Administration

Users typically ingest mushrooms by chewing and swallowing them raw or
dried, or by brewing them into tea. Because the taste is generally
unpleasant, it is common to chop mushrooms into smaller pieces before
consumption. Eating mushrooms mixed or cooked into food may increase
nausea and vomiting. Some users report smoking dried mushrooms, said to
induce faster but milder effects, though it is unclear whether this
route effectively delivers psychoactive compounds. Onset of effects
occurs within 10 to 60 minutes, and the experience lasts 2 to 6 hours.

## Related Species

Copelandia cyanescens (also classified as Panaeolus cyanescens) is
another commercially available species, sold fresh in smaller quantities
(10 grams per pack). It is considered more potent than Psilocybe
cubensis and commands higher prices. Psilocybe azurescens spawn bags
are also available through some retailers.

The genus [[Amanita muscaria]] represents a chemically distinct group
of hallucinogenic mushrooms with different active compounds and
significantly different risk profiles.
