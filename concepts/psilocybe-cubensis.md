# Psilocybe cubensis

Psilocybe cubensis, also known as [[mckenna-stropharia-cubensis-extraterrestrial-hypothesis-spore-dispersal]], is the most commonly cultivated and commercially marketed species of psilocybin-containing mushroom worldwide. It is the predominant species found in smartshops and online retail outlets throughout Europe and is the variety most frequently referenced in recreational use contexts.

## Taxonomy and Synonyms

The species has been classified under both the Psilocybe and Stropharia genera at different periods in mycological history. Despite the ongoing taxonomic debate, Psilocybe cubensis remains the most widely used name in both the scientific literature and among recreational users. The species epithet "cubensis" refers to Cuba, where the species was first formally described.

## Named Varieties

A large number of cultivated varieties of Psilocybe cubensis are recognized in the commercial market, typically named after their geographic origin. Common varieties include:

- Mexican
- Thai
- Colombian
- Amazonian
- Ecuadorian

These varieties are distinguished primarily by their physical appearance, growth characteristics, and relative potency, though they all belong to the same species. The "Mexican" variety has historically been the most commonly used in Europe, according to data from the UK Mixmag clubbing magazine readership surveys conducted between 2000 and 2005.

## Cultivation

Psilocybe cubensis is cultivated specifically for the commercial market, predominantly in the Netherlands. The species is particularly suited to commercial cultivation for several reasons:

- It grows readily in controlled terrarium environments
- It possesses a low water content that allows fresh mushrooms to remain viable for 7 to 10 days after harvest without significant degradation
- It produces reliable and abundant fruiting bodies under standardized conditions
- It is relatively forgiving of minor cultivation errors compared to other species

The majority of recreationally used mushrooms in Europe are believed to be cultivated rather than wild-picked, with Psilocybe cubensis varieties making up the bulk of cultivated supply. This is significant because the species does not grow wild in Europe, meaning virtually all specimens encountered in European markets originate from deliberate indoor cultivation.

### Cultivation Products

Several product formats are sold through retail channels for home cultivation:

- **Fresh mushrooms**: The whole fruiting body, sold as a perishable product requiring refrigeration
- **Dried mushrooms**: Dehydrated for longer shelf life and easier transport
- **Spore prints**: Created by pressing the gills of a mature mushroom onto paper under sterile conditions
- **Spore syringes**: Spores transferred from a print into a syringe for inoculation of substrate
- **Spawn bags**: Sterile hardwood chips inoculated with active mushroom mycelium
- **Growkits**: Complete cultivation kits containing a box with colonized substrate, a bag with an air filter, and paperclips

The Free Spore Ring Europe, a non-profit organization, distributed various strains of Psilocybe cubensis and [[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]] for approximately 1 EUR per print, further facilitating home cultivation across the continent.

Other species growing wild in Europe include [[growing-gourmet-caramel-capped-psilocybe-cyanescens-cultivation]] (wavy caps) as well as Psilocybe bohemica and Psilocybe moravica, particularly reported in the Czech Republic. Nearly all psilocybin-containing mushrooms are small brown or tan mushrooms that bruise blue when handled, but this feature is shared with many non-psychoactive species, making wild identification unreliable and potentially dangerous.

## Prevalence Data

Among the 305 individuals in the 2004 Mixmag survey who had used [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]], over half said they planned to take them again. In the general population of 12 EU Member States surveyed, lifetime prevalence of hallucinogenic mushroom use among young people aged 15 to 24 ranged from less than 1% to 8%, with the Netherlands, Czech Republic, UK, Germany, and Ireland showing the highest estimates. [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-clubbing-surveys-and-prevalence]] found much higher rates, ranging from 9% in Budapest to 55% in France, reflecting the concentration of mushroom use among recreational drug users in dance music settings.

## Barriers to Diffusion

Several factors have acted as barriers to the widespread diffusion of hallucinogenic mushroom use in Europe. User accounts suggest that the experience may not be viewed in a sufficiently favourable light to repeat it or to promote the trend. Unpredictable potency, negative effects such as nausea and panic attacks, and the lack of sociable entactogenic effects may all contribute to limiting recreational use.

The route of administration is also a barrier. Unlike ecstasy tablets that can be conveniently swallowed in a party setting, mushrooms must be chewed or brewed in hot water. The unfamiliar and unpleasant taste, combined with this cumbersome preparation, serves as a deterrent. Opportunities for potential users to witness others using mushrooms are therefore limited, reducing the potential for diffusion compared to more convenient recreational drugs.
