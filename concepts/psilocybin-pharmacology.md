# Psilocybin Mushroom Health Risks and Pharmacology

## Overview

[[set-and-setting]]
- Philosophical or spiritual insights
- Enhanced appreciation of music and art
- Difficulty distinguishing imagination from external perception

### Unpleasant Effects

- Anxiety and panic attacks, particularly at higher doses or in unfamiliar
  settings ("bad trips")
- Confusion and disorientation
- Fear of losing one's mind or of death

## Acute Physiological Effects

The somatic effects of psilocybin mushrooms are generally mild compared to
the psychological effects:

- **Nausea**: Most commonly reported physical effect, typically during onset
  (first 30–60 minutes)
- **Pupil dilation** (mydriasis): Consistent and dose-dependent
- **Increased heart rate** and blood pressure: Mild to moderate
- **Increased body temperature**: Mild hyperthermia
- **Muscle weakness** and ataxia: Impaired coordination
- **Dizziness**: Particularly during onset
- **Increased reflexes**: Hyperreflexia
- **Tremor**: Fine motor tremor at higher doses

## Mental Health Risks

### Short-Term Risks

The most significant acute mental health risk is the **panic reaction** or
"bad trip," characterized by overwhelming anxiety, paranoid ideation, and
loss of behavioral control. Risk factors include:

- High doses or unexpectedly potent mushrooms
- Unfamiliar or threatening environment ("set and setting")
- Pre-existing anxiety disorders or unstable mental health
- Use without adequate preparation or knowledge
- Polydrug use, especially with stimulants or alcohol

### Post-Acute Risks

- **Flashback phenomena**: Brief re-experiences of psychedelic effects days
  to weeks after use. Relatively rare with mushrooms compared to LSD.
- **Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD)**: Persistent visual
  disturbances following hallucinogen use. Very rare with psilocybin.
- **Triggered psychotic episodes**: Psilocybin can precipitate psychosis in
  individuals with latent schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.

## Somatic Health Risks

The physical toxicity of psilocybin is remarkably low. The LD50 in rodents
is extremely high relative to recreational doses. However, indirect risks
include:

- **Accidental injury**: Impaired coordination and judgment leading to
  falls, traffic accidents, or other injuries
- **Poisoning from misidentification**: Confusion with toxic species,
  particularly [[psilocybe-cubensis]]**: 0.5–1.0% dry weight (most commonly cultivated)
