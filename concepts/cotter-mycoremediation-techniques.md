### Testing Chemical Contaminants

**The Bean Test (for soil and water toxicity):**
1. Obtain 70 bean seeds of known viability from the same stock
2. Prepare 14 containers: 1 control + 1 full-strength + 12 serial dilutions (1:10 ratio each)
3. Add 5 beans to each container with the appropriate solution
4. Soak beans for 24 hours in the solutions
5. Plant beans in sterile soil; water with the saved soaking solution
6. Observe for 2+ weeks: abnormal leaves, color variations, wilting, growth rates
7. The "safe" level is the highest concentration where beans grow normally, matching the control
8. Water from a mycofiltration system can be tested against this reference spectrum

**The Worm Assay (for soil toxicity):**
1. Use sanitized cups with perforated lids (250ml capacity)
2. Fill with soil samples moistened with contaminant solutions at known ppm concentrations
3. Prepare 5 replicates per concentration level plus controls
4. Add 20 cultivated worms (Eisenia fetida or Lumbricus terrestris) per cup
5. Record initial color, light reaction, and touch sensitivity
6. Incubate at 65-75°F (18-24°C) with a small light to prevent escape
7. Observe daily for 14 days; record behavioral changes, color, mortality
8. On day 14, sift through soil to find and count surviving worms
9. Compare final observations to controls

### Professional Follow-Up Testing

- After initial low-tech testing, consider professional laboratory analysis
- Determine whether the contaminant was fully degraded or merely biotransformed
- Biotransformation could produce compounds more toxic than the original
- All data (labwork, cell counting, bean tests, worm assays) guides recalibration
- Good data is the foundation of successful full-scale installations

## Species for Specific Contaminants

### Heavy Metals
- Agaricus spp.: Hyperaccumulate cadmium, copper, zinc
- Blewits (Clitocybe nuda): Hyperaccumulate metals in mycelium

### Hydrocarbons and Oil
- [[white-rot-fungi]]: Degrade chlorinated compounds found in pesticides

### Biological Pathogens
- Oyster and king stropharia: Effective against fecal coliforms
- Turkey tail ([[staycare-trametes-versicolor-bioremediation-applications]]): Antibacterial activity
- Hericium spp.: Inhibits Staphylococcus and other bacterial species
- Hypholoma spp.: Effective at lower temperatures

### Dyes and Textile Waste
- Hericium: Treatment of paper bleaching and dye waste
- Oyster mushrooms: Originally researched for indigo carmine remediation from textile mills
