# Edaphic Factors Structuring Cannabis Microbiome Communities

## Overview

[[soil-physicochemical-properties-cannabis-microbiome-assembly-winston]] are the primary determinant of microbial
[[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] across all sample types in the cannabis root-soil system.
Edaphic factors outweigh both sample type (bulk soil, rhizosphere, endorhiza)
and cultivar effects on overall community composition. Understanding which
soil parameters most strongly structure these communities is critical for
developing soil [[weed-management-strategies]] that promote beneficial root-associated
microbiomes.

## Ranking of Edaphic Factor Importance

Mantel tests correlating edaphic factors with community beta-diversity across
all samples identified a clear hierarchy of soil parameter importance:

### Weighted UniFrac Analysis (Abundance-Based)

1. **Nitrogen** (r-stat: 0.465, p = 0.001) - The single strongest edaphic
   predictor of [[edaphic-determinants-cannabis-microbiome-community-structure]]. Total nitrogen concentration drives both
   the composition and relative abundance of microbial taxa across all sample
   types.
2. **Salinity** (r-stat: 0.437, p = 0.001) - The second most important
   factor. Electrical conductivity and salt content significantly shape
   community membership, likely through osmotic selection pressure on
   sensitive microbial groups.
3. **Total Organic Carbon** (r-stat: 0.330, p = 0.001) - Carbon availability
   influences community structure, though its effect is weaker than nitrogen
   or salinity.
4. **Water Content** (r-stat: 0.281, p = 0.001) - Soil moisture affects
   microbial activity and community composition.
5. **pH** (r-stat: 0.221, p = 0.001) - While significant, pH showed the
   weakest individual correlation with community structure among the tested
   edaphic factors.

### Unweighted UniFrac Analysis (Presence/Absence-Based)

The relative importance ranking remained identical, though correlation
strengths were uniformly higher:

1. **Nitrogen** (r-stat: 0.630, p = 0.001)
2. **Salinity** (r-stat: 0.620, p = 0.001)
3. **Carbon** (r-stat: 0.512, p = 0.001)
4. **Water Content** (r-stat: 0.466, p = 0.001)
5. **pH** (r-stat: 0.292, p = 0.001)

The stronger correlations in the unweighted analysis suggest that edaphic
factors primarily determine which microbial taxa can be present (presence/
absence), while their relative abundances are subsequently modified by plant
host selection.

## Soil Physical Composition

All soils in the study were classified as sandy loam with similar proportions
of sand (62-66%), silt (16-18%), and clay (17-21%). Despite this similar
texture classification, significant differences in edaphic chemistry existed
between the two main soil types used in the second experiment:

- **Mo-Bio soil**: Higher salinity (5.12-7.44), higher total nitrogen
  (0.26-1.51%), lower organic carbon (3.02-5.00%), and lower water content
  (0.101-0.178).
- **Orange County soil**: Much lower salinity (1.73), moderate nitrogen
  (0.53%), very high organic carbon (20.0%), and higher water content
  (0.371).

These chemical differences, despite similar physical texture, produced
significantly different [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]] in both experiments.

## BEST Analysis

A Best Subset of Environmental Variables with Maximum (Rank) Correlation with
Community Dissimilarities (BEST) analysis was conducted to evaluate the
combined effect of edaphic factors. This multivariate approach confirmed that
the combination of nitrogen, salinity, carbon, water content, and pH together
provided the best explanation of community variation, but that nitrogen and
salinity contributed the most explanatory power to the combined model.
