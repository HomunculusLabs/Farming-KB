## Urban Permaculture Principles

Mollison argues that cities are already ecosystems -- highly modified,
energy-dependent ones -- and that permaculture design can redirect
urban flows of water, nutrients, materials, and human energy toward
productive, sustainable patterns. Key urban strategies include:

- Converting vacant lots, rooftops, and marginal spaces to food
  production
- Capturing and cycling urban waste streams (organic waste, greywater,

- Creating social structures that enable cooperative land management
- Reducing dependence on distant food supply chains

## Social Design and Governance

Mollison considers social design as important as physical design in
community gardens. Successful community gardens require:

- **Clear governance structure:** Written agreements covering plot
  allocation, maintenance standards, conflict resolution, and
  decision-making processes. Consensus-based models work well for
  small groups; elected committees for larger ones.

- **Inclusive membership:** Welcoming diverse participants regardless
  of gardening experience, age, ability, or income. Mentorship
  programs pair experienced gardeners with newcomers.

- **Equitable resource distribution:** Shared tools, compost, seeds,
  and water distributed fairly. Sliding-scale fees or labor exchange
  systems ensure economic accessibility.

- **Celebration and ceremony:** Seasonal festivals, harvest meals,
  and work parties build social cohesion and maintain motivation.
