# Fungal Enzyme Systems for Xenobiotic Degradation

Fungi deploy a diverse arsenal of enzyme systems to degrade xenobiotic compounds. These range from the highly non-specific extracellular [[fungal-enzymes-degradation]] of [[white-rot-fungi-effluent-decolorization]] to more specific intracellular cytochrome P450 monooxygenases. Understanding these systems is essential for developing effective [[fungal-bioremediation]] strategies.

## The Ligninolytic System of White Rot Fungi

The lignin-degrading enzyme system is the most important fungal weapon against xenobiotics. It evolved to degrade lignin -- the most abundant renewable organic polymer on earth -- and its intrinsic non-specificity means it can attack many pollutant structures that resemble lignin substructures.

### Lignin Peroxidase (LiP)
- A haem-containing extracellular peroxidase that catalyzes one-electron oxidations
- Requires H2O2 and veratryl alcohol as a redox mediator
- Capable of oxidizing compounds with ionization potential below approximately 9.0 eV
- Catalyzes oxidative dechlorination of polychlorinated phenols to 1,4-benzoquinones
- Important for PAH oxidation and azo dye decolorization
- Optimum pH approximately 2.3-2.5 (though unstable at this pH for long periods)
- Produced mainly under nitrogen-limited (secondary metabolic) conditions by P. chrysosporium

### Manganese Peroxidase (MnP)
- A haem-containing extracellular peroxidase dependent on Mn(II)
- Generates Mn(III) chelates that act as diffusible oxidants
- Capable of decolorizing Kraft effluent; correlated with decolorization by several white rot fungi
- Important for oxidation of reduced TNT metabolites
- Some fungi produce more MnP under nitrogen-sufficient conditions (unlike P. chrysosporium)
- Enhanced by reduced thiols like glutathione and L-cysteine for TNT metabolite oxidation

### Laccase
- A copper-containing extracellular oxidase that uses molecular oxygen directly (no H2O2 required)
- Widely distributed among fungi, not limited to white rot species
- Catalyzes oxidative coupling of phenolic compounds (polymerization of chlorophenols, anilines)
- Can decolorize dyes and effluents, sometimes through mediator systems
- Pycnoporus cinnabarinus laccase has optimum at 65C (unstable) or 45C (90% activity over 24 hours)
- 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HBT) acts as a mediator to expand laccase substrate range
- Laccase from T. versicolor polymerized 4-chloroaniline to oligomers (dimers to pentamers)

### Supporting Enzymes
- **Glucose oxidase and glyoxal oxidase** -- generate H2O2 for peroxidase activity
- **Aryl alcohol oxidase** -- produces H2O2 and aldehydes
- **Veratryl alcohol oxidase** -- generates the LiP mediator veratryl alcohol
- **Quinone reductases** -- reduce quinones, regenerating substrates for further peroxidase attack
- **Laccase-like multicopper oxidases** -- found in some brown rot fungi

## Cytochrome P450 Monooxygenases

Non-ligninolytic fungi metabolize xenobiotics through intracellular P450 systems:
- Catalyze the formation of trans-dihydrodiols from PAHs
- Highly regio- and stereoselective
- Also produce phenols, quinones, and conjugates
- Similar to mammalian metabolic profiles (raising concerns about toxic intermediate formation)
- Cunninghamella species are particularly active P450-mediated xenobiotic degraders

## Dioxygenases

Fungal dioxygenases catalyze aromatic ring cleavage:
- **Ortho-fission** (intradiol cleavage) -- oxygen inserted between ortho-oriented hydroxyl groups
- **Meta-fission** (extradiol cleavage) -- appears unusual among fungi
- Products channeled to beta-ketoadipate pathway for entry into central metabolism
- Catechol 1,2-dioxygenase and protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase are key ring-cleavage enzymes

## Nitroreductases

For nitroaromatic explosives like TNT:
- Aromatic nitroreductase activity reduces nitro groups stepwise (nitro to nitroso to hydroxylamino to amino)
- May be membrane-bound (Stahl and Aust, 1993b) or soluble (Michels and Gottschalk, 1995)
- Maximum activity correlates with lignolytic conditions

## Esterases and Hydrolases

- Hydrolyze ester linkages in phenylcarbamate herbicides and nitrate ester explosives
- Contribute to initial attack on complex pesticide molecules
- Important in the stepwise degradation of nitroglycerin by fungi

## Enzyme Complement Variation

Not all white rot fungi produce the same enzyme complement. Three groups exist:
- Some produce LiP, MnP, and laccase
- Some produce only LiP
- Some produce no peroxidases at all

This variation means that members of the same taxonomic group may behave very differently in terms of [[fungal-biodegradation-of-synthetic-pollutants]]. Organism selection must be based on enzyme profiling, not taxonomy alone.
