## Naranjo Harmaline Yage Experiment

### Soul Flight and the Disembodied Self

The experience of the soul separating from the body was reported with striking explicitness. One 25-year-old woman described her soul as "a sphere of some 7 cm. in diameter, pure energy, and it rotates on itself at such enormous speed that it would be the same if it didn"t. It can displace itself in any direction at the speed it wishes."

A 21-year-old journalist described a progressive dissociation: "I was realizing that my body and my mind were such autonomous forces that if they had ever converged in me it seemed pure chance. What during my entire lifetime I had sensed like mingled confusion now appeared to be divided in three precise domains: outside lay the world; closer, as a boundary, stood my organism; inside, at last, was I."

Eight of thirty-five subjects experienced visions or feelings of their own death. Some saw themselves as corpses; others reported the sensation of physical death followed by rebirth or transformation.

### Flying

Ten subjects reported experiences related to flying. One subject who took harmaline with mescaline described: "The first thing I did, involuntarily, was lift my hand. It seemed to lose weight, it rose... And then I felt that it was no longer a hand but the tip of a wing. I was turning into a winged being. I then stretched my wings and felt extreme freedom and expansion."

Some subjects described visions viewed from above, from great altitude, without explicitly identifying the experience as flying. One reported: "I was flying over a city that I didn"t recognize... From a great height, like a bird."

### Predatory Animals

Tigers, leopards, or jaguars were seen by seven subjects even though big cats are not found in Chile. These appeared sometimes as aggressors, sometimes as graceful companions, and in one instance the subject experienced true impersonation: "I moved as a tiger in the jungle, joyously, feeling the ground under my feet, feeling my power; my chest grew larger. I then approached an animal... I only saw its neck, and then experienced what a tiger feels when looking at its prey."

Reptiles were seen by six subjects, including three who saw dragons and one a dinosaur. Snakes were reported by three subjects.

### The Center and Periphery

A recurring structural motif involved the contraposition of center and periphery — a geometric center around which motion revolves, or a source from which light or phenomena emanate. One subject described "tiny dots, like those on a television screen, transparent dots that agitated and turned around a cone forming a sort of funnel... In this swirl of particles lies all my visual experience."

Naranjo interpreted this as expressing "the polarity of being and becoming, freedom and necessity, spirit and matter" — the deeper self and the multiplicity of experience, encompassing but transcending the duality of mind and body.

### The Religious and Mythical

Fifteen of thirty-five subjects reported imagery explicitly religious in character: five saw the Devil, three saw angels, three had visions of the Virgin Mary, two of Christ, three of Paradise or Heaven, two of Hell, three described priestly figures, while others saw churches, altars, or crosses. Two additional subjects had ecstatic feelings described in religious terms.

Naranjo noted that the mythico-religious element "is more pervasive in the experiences than what appears from their outward descriptions and may be completely unrelated to the visual imagery." One subject who had merely visualized "the depth of the ocean" later described it as the most important experience: "The feeling of being myself. The sea was in myself. The idea of God was in everything. I think that must be what is called a mystical experience."
