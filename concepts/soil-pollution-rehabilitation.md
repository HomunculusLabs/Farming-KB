# Soil Pollution and Rehabilitation

Soil pollution from intensive agriculture, industry, and urban activity has created a legacy of contaminated land that may require decades or centuries to remediate. Understanding the sources, behaviour, and potential remedies for soil contamination is essential for permaculture designers working on land that has been previously farmed or developed.

## Sources of Agricultural Soil Pollution

### Orchards and Plantation Crops

Orchards, sugarcane areas, pineapple, cotton, tobacco, and banana plantations have received heavy applications of mineral additives, arsenicals, Aldrin, DDT, copper salts, and dioxins. The persistence of these chemicals is remarkable:

- Apple orchards in West Australia still produce **unsafe levels of Aldrin and Dieldrin** in free-range chicken eggs, even after **18-20 years** of no chemical use
- Attempts to grow prawns in ponds on such lands have **failed due to residual Dieldrin** levels in soils
- Many farms will need to be put into **non-food production** (fuel forests or structural timber) for decades

### Heavy Metal Accumulation

Large quantities of lead, arsenic, copper, and persistent biocides are applied on most apple orchards. Data on metal behaviour in soils:

- **Copper and lead** stay at or near the soil surface in high concentrations
- **Arsenic** may stay at the surface in clay soils, or wash down to subsoil (50-60 cm deep) in acid sands
- Leaching from clays or organic profiles (10-20 cm) is unlikely, although phosphate application may dislodge arsenic to deeper levels
- **Copper in pasture** under orchards can reach 50 ppm, which is poisonous to sheep, causing toxaemic jaundice (liver poisoning) and blood in the urine
- Molybdenum, zinc, and sulphur may buffer copper uptake in sheep
- Uptake by plants increases with temperature and acidity (for lead and copper)

## Urban and Industrial Pollution

### Roadway Contamination

Soils within 100 m of roadways where leaded petrol is used (and where 800-1000 cars pass daily) may be contaminated with lead. Lead at 1,100 ppm can be present and is both taken up by and dusted on the surface of vegetables.

### Radioactive Contamination

Lock-up periods of tens or thousands of years may be needed for radioactives from:

- Waste dumps and strategic stockpiles of yellowcake uranium (Iowa, Kentucky, Russia, UK, France)
- Accidents at atomic power plants
- Nuclear waste storage facilities

### Industrial Waste

Cadmium and uranium-polluted soils of chemically-based and heavily-fertilised market gardens, waste dumps of industry, and long-term effects of nitrate-polluted soil waters add to the list of already-dangerous areas. Even now, applied health levels would close down many farms and factories.

## Rehabilitation Strategies

### Lead-Contaminated Soils

For urban areas with lead contamination from car exhausts and lead paint:

1. Use **cracked bricks or gravel as a base** to prevent plant root contact with contaminated subsoil
2. **Build raised beds 30 cm deep** with rich composted soil of over 40% organic content
3. Grow vegetables and have **leaf analyses done** for lead content
4. **Wash vegetables in dilute vinegar** if lead is still used locally in petrol

### Persistent Biocide Contamination

For old orchards, canefields, or plantations (banana, pineapple, cotton, tobacco):

- It is unlikely that any **animal product** (milk, eggs, meat) will be free of high levels of biocides
- The only option is to go into **forestry** and produce non-food crops until other methods are developed
- **Test your own vegetables** for residual toxic materials
- Consider the site for timber, fuelwood, or fibre production only

### Groundwater Protection

Groundwater beneath farms can contain 80-90 biocidal substances including those derived from fertilisers, sprays, and fuels. Near industrial waste dumps, dioxins, radioactives, and heavy metal wastes (cadmium, chromium, mercury) may be present.

Guidelines:
- **Do not use untested wells or bore waters** for any purpose
- **Drink tank water** (rainwater harvested from clean roofs)
- Harvest **surface run-off for gardens**
- Several decades may be needed to clear most aquifers of pollutants
- Rainwater harvest and strict water conservation is indicated for the long-term future

### General Prevention

- Avoid all biocides
- Avoid high levels of nitrates
- Watch on-farm disposal of oils and fuels
- Do not add to existing contamination

## Soil and Water Elements

Of the 103 known elements, soil is predominantly composed of aluminium, silica, iron, and (in certain shell, sand, or limestone areas) calcium. Only mineral ore deposits have large quantities of other elements.

Designers in any field need a basic knowledge of:

- **Nutrients** — essential elements for plant, animal, and human health
- **Poisons** — elements toxic at low concentrations
- **Tolerable levels** — safe concentrations of trace elements in food, water, and the built environment
- **Essential trace elements** — micronutrients required in minute but necessitous amounts

Health of plants, animals, and soil environments depends on the balance of elements, radioactive substances, radiation, and water quality. Of particular concern are radioactives in clays, bricks, paints, and stone, emissions from domestic appliances, and microwave radiation from TV transmission and power lines.

## The Cost of Degradation

The costs of rehabilitation already far outstrip the profits of degradation, and may be prohibitive for areas developed for farming from 1950 to the present — the age of agricultural pollution. This economic reality, combined with growing public awareness, will increasingly drive the transition away from chemically-intensive agriculture toward biological and permaculture-based systems.
