# Permaculture Forage Systems and Animal Integration

updated: 2026-04-18

"Never in the history of the world has anybody designed and implemented animal forage systems." Bill Mollison's Permaculture Design Course (Pamphlet IX) presents a comprehensive approach to integrated animal forage that goes far beyond conventional pasture management.

## Poultry Forage Systems

### Chicken-Orchard System
White mulberry as chicken forage equals a double crop of grain at 17% protein. Chickens get seed long after mulberries are gone. Two or three mulberry varieties extend harvest season. Four-foot cuttings root in first autumn; full production by second year.

### Chicken Stocking Rates
- Maximum 400 chickens per acre (break-even point); 300 is better
- Maximum 80 per flock; 50-60 suits social conditions best
- Need 5 roosters per flock; otherwise hens wander
- On an acre, run 4 breeds of 60-chicken flocks
- Heavy breeds lay better in winter; light breeds are spring/summer layers

### Flock Separation
Separate chicken houses by 150 feet (200 feet maximum). Beyond that, no mixing of flocks occurs. Each house needs its own forage system.

### Guinea Pigs Under Fruit Trees
A tropical grower places a five-gallon can (with hole) at the base of each lychee tree with four guinea pigs. Guinea pigs:
- Cut grass, build high/low litter, manure the tree
- Allow free water penetration
- Provide very cheap cultivation
- Enable a 4-year-old to make a millionaire from the lychee crop

### Pest Control Integration
- Chickens with plums and cherries sharply reduce crawling/flying pests
- Ducks eat snails carrying fluke (fatal to sheep) and fluke eggs
- Ducks are great fly catchers; bait flies in, ducks catch them
- Geese maintain lawn systems under nut trees (geese-and-walnuts is ancient)
- Bantams under trees reduce codling moth to ~1%
- Woodpeckers on bark, bantams under trees
- Slender blue-tongue lizard eats nothing but slugs

### Protective Structures
- African boxthorn provides predator-proof nesting (even cats can't enter)
- Escapement governs populations; protects breeders
- Brambles (blackberry, raspberry) provide chicken escapement from predators
- Thorny patches permit hens to raise chicks safely
- Slash piles and thorny branches protect food plants until established

## Cattle Forage

### Tree Crop Forage
- Carob: One 17-year-old tree maintains 8 milking goats year-round (3 pods/day each)
- Honey locust, mesquite: Sugar pod group for dry season carry
- Willow and poplar: Good cattle forage
- Oaks and chestnuts: Winter carbohydrate source

### Carob in East Africa
Carob trees planted on cattle range increased carrying capacity from 1 cow per 12 acres to 12 cows per acre -- a 144x increase.

### Herbal Pasture Components
- Dandelion, chicory, comfrey for high mineral mobilization
- Evergreen high-nutrition tree crops within forage range
- Perennial grasses better than annual for winter fodder value
- Siberian pea tree: Hardy nitrogen fixer; peasants of Siberia fed poultry on this alone

### Shelter Requirements
- Cattle lose 8-10 pounds on bad days without shelter
- Dense shading or shelter blocks essential; not just hedgerows
- Pampas grass ideal for sheep shelter (survival equal to insulated sheds)
- Milk/meat losses reach 20% in unsheltered environments

### Native vs. Selected Animals
- Below 18 inches rainfall: Switch to black buffalo, antelope, or gazelles
- Original American stocking (buffalo, white tail deer, prairie dogs) was 10-20x current yield
- Prairie dogs were "chisel plows" -- their tunnels aerated soil
- Cattle are forest animals, not pasture animals; they belong in cool forest swamplands

## Animal Propagation of Ranges
- Cattle plant honey locust: seeds pass through, get acid bath + heat treatment, 90-100% germination from cow manure
- Blue jays plant acorns (50-60 per cache, often forgotten)
- Squirrels accumulate nuts in forgotten caches
- Nearly every animal extends its own garden

## The Trophic Pyramid Shortcut
Every trophic step consumes 9/10 of food energy. Seek shortcuts:
- Leaf algae to phreatocide to brown trout: 100 lbs leaf produces 1 lb trout (vs. 10,000 lbs via standard chain)
- Grey mullet browses diatoms at 15 lbs -- a short-cut to fish protein
- Primary decomposers (phreatocides, algae browsers, diatom eaters) are the most efficient converters

## Bee Forage Systems

### Key Principles
- Clump forages; don't dot about landscape
- Keep forages 100+ yards from hives
- Low hedgerow (4 feet) between hive sites and forage enables work in bad weather
- Start with rape, buckwheat, sunflowers; add Tilia (basswood/linden) later

### Bee Forage Plants
- Basswood (Tilia americana): Reliable honey tree, 60 lbs honey/season
- Leatherwood (Tasmania): 100 lbs honey every 3 days; evergreen, flowers last season
- Purple loosestrife: Problem to marshes but excellent bee fodder
- Water tupelo, marsh marigold, Caltha: Wetland bee plants
- Bee pollen equals honey production by weight

### The Bee Barn
Sedentary beekeeping -- insulate whole structure, multiple exits, process inside. Step-down shed system (extractor at waist level, storage below). 60 hives supports a family.
