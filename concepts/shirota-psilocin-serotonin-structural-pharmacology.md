## Comparison with LSD

Shirota et al. noted that "it is likely that LSD works in a similar fashion"
to psilocybin. Both compounds are partial agonists at the 5-HT2A receptor,
and both produce their primary effects through modulation of serotonergic
signaling in the cortex. However, LSD has a broader receptor profile, with
significant activity at dopamine D2 receptors and other serotonergic
subtypes, which contributes to its longer duration and more complex effects.

LSD's phenethylamine moiety (absent in psilocin) allows it to bind to
receptors that psilocin cannot access, producing effects that extend beyond
pure serotonergic modulation. The structural simplicity of psilocin relative
to LSD makes it a cleaner pharmacological tool for investigating the
serotonergic basis of consciousness alteration.

## Indole Chemistry and Drug Design

The shared indole ring of serotonin and psilocin places both compounds within
the tryptamine class of organic molecules. The indole ring is a bicyclic
structure consisting of a benzene ring fused to a pyrrole ring, and it is
one of the most important heterocycles in biochemistry and pharmacology.
The indole scaffold is found in serotonin, melatonin, tryptophan, and
numerous psychedelic alkaloids.

The chemistry of indole functionalization is well understood, which is why
Shirota et al. were able to design an efficient synthesis starting from
