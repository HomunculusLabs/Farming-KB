# AACT Compost Organism Levels

Elaine Ingham and Soil Foodweb Inc. (SFI) established specific organism biomass thresholds for compost used in AACT production. These levels ensure that compost tea will contain adequate beneficial organisms for disease suppression and soil improvement. The measurements use direct microscopy on fresh weight compost, expressed per gram dry weight.

## Desired Organism Levels in Aerobic Compost or Vermicompost

### Bacteria
| Parameter | Minimum Level |
|---|---|
| Active bacteria | 15–30+ µg/g dry weight |
| Total bacteria | 150 µg (fungal compost) to 300+ µg (bacterial compost) per g dry weight |

Total bacterial biomass below 300 µg/ml in finished tea indicates insufficient leaf surface coverage.

### Fungi
| Parameter | Minimum Level |
|---|---|
| Active fungi | 2–10+ µg/g dry weight |
| Total fungal biomass | 150 µg (bacterial compost) to 500+ µg (fungal compost) per g dry weight |
| Hyphal diameter | 2.5 µm or greater on average |

For fungal-dominant compost: above 150 µg fungal biomass, preferably 300+ µg, with 10+ µg active fungi.

### Protozoa
| Parameter | Minimum Level |
|---|---|
| Total protozoa | 50,000+ per gram dry weight |
| Flagellates | 25,000+ per gram |
| Amoebae | 25,000+ per gram |
| Ciliates | 50–100 per gram |

High ciliate numbers (above range) indicate anaerobic conditions from compaction, water-logging, or soil discontinuities. Ciliates are anaerobic indicator organisms.

### Nematodes
| Parameter | Minimum Level |
|---|---|
| Total beneficial nematodes | 20–100 per gram dry weight |
| Bacterial-feeding | 10–15 per gram |
| Fungal-feeding | 5–10 per gram |
| Predatory | 1–5 per gram |
| Root-feeding | **Zero** |

The presence of root-feeding nematodes indicates a sick compost with reduced aerobic conditions.

### Maturity Indicator
- Less than 10% active bacteria and active fungi indicates a mature, stable compost
- Higher activity levels suggest the compost is still actively decomposing

## Habitat Requirements for Pathogen Control

To obtain death of pests and pathogens, compost must maintain:

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| Dissolved oxygen | 5.5+ ppm throughout compost cycle |
| Odor | Pleasant (no putrid or sour smells) |
| Moisture | 45–75% |
| Organism set | Complete foodweb: bacteria, fungi, protozoa, beneficial nematodes |

### Thermal Compost Requirements
- Temperature of 55°C or higher for at least 3 days in ALL parts of the pile
- Maximum temperature not exceeding 70°C
- Turn every time compost approaches 68–70°C to maintain air throughout
- Temperature data must be submitted for each batch

### Vermicompost Requirements
- Room temperature maintenance
- Adequate earthworm population
- At least 75–80% of material must pass through worm digestive systems
- No weed seeds unless materials are pre-composted

## Testing Requirements

### Starting Materials
- All starting materials must be documented
- Fungal:bacterial ratio must match intended plant use
- Clear indication of intended use requirements

### For Thermal Compost
- Turning times, daily temperature, end moisture, daily CO2 or O2 data per batch

### For Vermicompost
- Worm count per unit volume, temperature, and oxygen data

### Chemistry and Pathogen Testing
- Must meet city, county, state, and federal standards

## Compost Types Comparison

Both thermal compost and vermicompost can produce equally good results. Either can be fungal or bacterial dominated. The more important factor is finding a high-quality local source and verifying it has the biology you need through testing.

### Mixing Compost Types
A 50:50 or 25:75 mix of thermal compost with worm compost is recommended. This combines the broad organism diversity of thermal compost with the concentrated beneficial biology of vermicompost.

## Paper and Cardboard as Compost Inputs

Paper and cardboard (cellulose-based materials) help fungi and often select for beneficial fungal species. Key considerations:
- Most inks this century are soy-based (safe), but verify for older paper
- Heavy-metal based inks from before the 1980s should be avoided
- Cardboard glues support growth of certain beneficial bacteria and fungi
- Shredding allows more rapid decomposition when soil biology is present
