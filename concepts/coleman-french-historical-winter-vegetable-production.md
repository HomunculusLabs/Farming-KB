## The 49th Parallel and Winter Production

Paris sits at approximately 48.9° North latitude — nearly identical to
Coleman's farm in Harborside, Maine. This geographic coincidence is not
trivial. It means that Paris receives essentially the same quantity and
quality of winter sunlight as coastal Maine, and faces similar winter
temperature challenges (though moderated somewhat by the Gulf Stream's
influence on Western European climate). The maraîchers demonstrated that
year-round vegetable production is possible at this latitude, given the right

## The Pagan Agricultural Calendar: The Wheel of Life

Coleman draws a connection between the winter harvest system and the ancient
pagan agricultural calendar, often called the Wheel of Life. This calendar
marks the year with four cross-quarter festivals that fall midway between the
solstices and equinoxes. These dates align remarkably well with practical
farming observations in the winter harvest system:

### Imbolc — February 2 (Spring Growth Onset)
Imbolc marks the first stirring of spring. In the winter harvest system, this
corresponds to the period when day length has increased enough to trigger
perceptible new growth in the overwintered crops. The transition from
maintenance harvesting to active growth is typically observed around this date.

### Beltane — May 1 (Summer Pastoral Season)
Beltane celebrates the beginning of the warm season and the movement of
livestock to summer pastures. In the farming calendar, it marks the point at
which winter protection is fully removed, cold frames are repurposed, and the
summer growing season begins in earnest.
