# Psilocybin and Psilocin in Magic Mushrooms

## Overview

Psilocybin and psilocin are the principal psychoactive constituents of
"magic mushrooms" — hallucinogenic fungi of the genus *Psilocybe* and related
genera. Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) serves as a
prodrug that is dephosphorylated in the body to produce psilocin
(4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine), the pharmacologically active compound
responsible for the psychedelic effects. These compounds were first isolated
and identified by [[gordon-wasson-comparative-method-ethnomycology]]'s
discovery of the pre-Columbian [[african-mushroom-cult-history]] in Mexico.

## Chemical Structures

### Psilocybin

- **IUPAC name**: [3-(2-Dimethylaminoethyl)-1H-indol-4-yl] dihydrogen
  phosphate
- **Molecular formula**: C12H17N2O4P
- **Molecular weight**: 284.25 g/mol
- **Appearance**: White needle crystalline powder
- **Melting point**: 185-212°C (literature values vary)
- **Solubility**: Soluble in water due to the phosphate group; moderately
  soluble in methanol

Psilocybin is the 4-phosphate ester of psilocin. The phosphate group
confers higher stability, greater water solubility, and oral
bioavailability compared to psilocin alone. When ingested, alkaline
phosphatase enzymes in the body cleave the phosphate group to release
the active psilocin.

### Psilocin

- **IUPAC name**: 3-[2-(Dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-ol
- **Molecular formula**: C12H16N2O
- **Molecular weight**: 204.27 g/mol
- **Appearance**: White crystals
- **Melting point**: 169-176°C (decomposes)
- **Solubility**: Soluble in alcohols and organic solvents; less
  water-soluble than psilocybin

Psilocin is the dephosphorylated, pharmacologically active metabolite. It
is relatively unstable and decomposes readily, particularly when exposed to
heat, light, or oxygen. This instability complicates both natural isolation
from mushroom material and long-term storage of analytical standards.

## Relationship to Serotonin

Both psilocybin and psilocin closely resemble the neurotransmitter serotonin
(5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT) in molecular structure. The key differences are
the substitution of a phosphate (psilocybin) or hydroxyl (psilocin) group at
the 4-position of the indole ring instead of serotonin's 5-hydroxy group, and
the presence of two methyl groups on the terminal amine (dimethyl vs. primary
amine in serotonin).

This structural similarity underlies the [[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]]:

### Baeocystin

- **Structure**: 4-phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine
- **Activity**: Weakly psychedelic; pharmacological significance uncertain
- **Named after**: *[[stamets-psi-psilocybe-baeocystis-knobby-tops]]*, the species in which it was
  first identified

Baeocystin is the N-monomethyl analog of psilocybin, lacking one of the
two methyl groups on the terminal amine. It is typically present at 0.1-0.3%
of the dry weight of the mushroom, compared to 0.5-1.5% for psilocybin.

### Norbaeocystin

- **Structure**: 4-phosphoryloxytryptamine
- **Activity**: Very weakly psychedelic or inactive
