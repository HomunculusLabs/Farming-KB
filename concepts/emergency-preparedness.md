# Emergency Preparedness

Planning for power outages, severe weather, food and water shortages, and communication disruptions on the rural homestead. See [[homestead-first-aid-guide]] for first aid kits, common injuries, and poison control.

## Emergency Preparedness

### Power Outage Planning
Rural areas experience more frequent and longer outages. See [[homestead-energy-conservation]] for generation options.
1. **Short-term (hours)**: Flashlights, headlamps, battery-powered radio, charged power bank for phones
2. **Medium-term (1-3 days)**: Generator (portable or standby), fuel reserve (5-10 gallons minimum), extension cords
3. **Long-term (days-weeks)**: Solar generator (Jackery, EcoFlow, Goal Zero), solar panel array, battery bank, inverter
4. **Water**: No power = no well pump. Keep 1 gallon per person per day minimum. See [[homestead-water-systems]] for storage solutions. Fill bathtub before expected storms.
5. **Food**: Refrigerator stays cold ~4 hours if unopened; freezer ~24-48 hours if full. Have a plan for [[food-preservation-canning]] of perishables during extended outages (pressure can on a camp stove, solar dehydrator, etc.)
6. **Heat**: Wood stove is the best backup. Kerosene heater with ventilation. Sleeping bags rated to 0°F. Never use generators or grills indoors — carbon monoxide kills.

### Severe Weather
- **Tornado**: Interior room, lowest floor, away from windows. Have a weather radio (NOAA). Know your safe room — consider reinforcing during [[natural-building]].
- **Winter storms**: Maintain 2+ weeks of food, water, firewood, animal feed. See [[seasonal-planning]] for winter preparedness. Insulate exposed pipes (see [[homestead-water-systems]]). Animals need shelter, extra feed, and water access (break ice on troughs).
- **Flooding**: Know your flood risk. Move animals and critical supplies to high ground. Never drive through floodwater. Document damage for insurance.
- **Wildfire**: Create defensible space (30-100 ft clearance). Have evacuation plan for animals. Metal roofs (see [[natural-building]]) are fire-resistant.
- **Extreme heat**: Shade structures for animals, extra water access, avoid midday outdoor work.

### Emergency Food and Water Storage
Minimum 2-week supply; 1 month is better for remote homesteads.

**Water storage**:
- 1 gallon/person/day for drinking + cooking
- Additional 1-2 gallons/person/day for hygiene
- Animals: ducks ~0.5 gal/day, goats 2-4 gal/day
- Store in food-grade containers, rotate every 6 months
- Treatment options: bleach (8 drops/gallon, wait 30 min), boiling (1 min), filtration (0.2 micron), iodine tablets
- See [[homestead-water-systems]] for larger storage solutions

**Food storage**:
- Canned goods (vegetables, beans, meats, fruits) — rotate stock, use FIFO
- Dry goods (rice, beans, oats, flour, pasta, salt, sugar) — store in airtight containers, cool/dark
- Freeze-dried meals — lightweight, 25+ year shelf life, expensive
- [[food-preservation-canning]] methods extend garden harvests year-round
- MREs or emergency ration bars — compact, long shelf life
- Cooking fuel: propane tank, camp stove, wood, solar cooker

### Communication Plan
- **Cell phone**: Keep charged; car charger, solar charger, or hand-crank charger
- **NOAA weather radio**: Battery or hand-crank — receives weather alerts, AM/FM
- **HAM radio**: For long-range communication when cell towers are down. Requires license (FCC Technician class — easy to obtain). Community emergency networks often exist in rural areas
- **Satellite communicator**: Garmin inReach or similar — SOS beacon, text messaging via satellite. Annual subscription. Essential for remote homesteads
- **Landline**: If available, corded phones work during power outages (unlike cordless)

### Emergency Contact List
Post in a visible location (kitchen, mudroom):
- 911 / local emergency services
- Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222
- Nearest hospital / urgent care (address, phone, directions, distance)
- Veterinarian (large animal and small animal — phone, after-hours)
- Utility companies (electric, water, gas)
- Insurance agent
- Nearest neighbors (for mutual aid)
- County extension office
- Local fire department (non-emergency number)

### First Aid Training
- **Wilderness First Aid (WFA)**: 16-hour course through NOLS, SOLO, or Red Cross. Best investment for rural homesteaders — covers assessment, stabilization, and evacuation when help is far.
- **CPR/AED**: American Red Cross or American Heart Association. Renew every 2 years.
- **Stop the Bleed**: Free 1-hour course on hemorrhage control (tourniquet, packing wounds). Increasingly available through hospitals and fire departments.
- **Veterinary first aid**: Basic courses or books for livestock emergencies. Knowing when to call the vet vs. treat yourself saves money and animal lives.

### Emergency Fund
- Keep $500-$1,000 in small bills (power outages disable card readers)
- Separate emergency fund covering 3-6 months of expenses
- Livestock emergency fund (vet bills can be $200-$2,000+ per incident)
- Insurance review annually: property, liability, livestock, crop, health

## Seasonal Emergency Preparation

Integrate with [[seasonal-planning]]:

| Season | Key Preparations |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Spring** | Restock first aid supplies, check fire extinguishers, test generator, review evacuation plans, clear winter debris from fire breaks |
| **Summer** | Heat preparedness (shade, water), wildfire defensible space, snake awareness, first aid kit in vehicle, check water storage |
| **Fall** | Winterize buildings and pipes (see [[homestead-water-systems]]), stock firewood, restock food/water, animal winter shelter, generator maintenance |
| **Winter** | Monitor weather, keep emergency supplies accessible (not buried in storage), ice melt/sand, vehicle winter kit (blanket, shovel, food, water, charger), check on animals frequently |
