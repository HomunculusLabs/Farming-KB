# Cannabis for Gastrointestinal Conditions

updated: 2026-04-18

The gastrointestinal tract has one of the highest densities of endocannabinoid receptors in the body. Research from The [[blesching-cannabis-inflammatory-bowel-disease]] disease (IBS/IBD), GERD, pancreatitis, periodontitis, and nausea/vomiting.

## Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBS/IBD)

### Cannabinoids and the Gut
**Izzo and Sharkey (2010)**: "Cannabinoids and the gut: New developments and emerging concepts." Pharmacology and Therapeutics 126(1):21-38.

Comprehensive review establishing that the gut endocannabinoid system is a major regulator of gastrointestinal function, including motility, secretion, inflammation, and gut permeability.

### Endocannabinoid Degradation Inhibition
**Storr et al. (2008)**: "Targeting endocannabinoid degradation protects against experimental colitis in mice: involvement of CB(1) and CB(2) receptors." Journal of Molecular Medicine.

Inhibiting the enzymes that [[comparison-cannabis-sativa-vs-cannabis-indica]], selectively inhibits inflammatory hypermotility in mice." British Journal of Pharmacology.

CBD selectively reduced excessive intestinal motility (diarrhea) associated with inflammation, without affecting normal gut motility. This suggests CBD could treat IBS-related diarrhea without causing constipation.

### Curcumin for Ulcerative Colitis
**Hanai et al. (2006)**: "Curcumin maintenance therapy for ulcerative colitis: Randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial." Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology.

Curcumin (from turmeric) showed significant benefit as maintenance therapy for ulcerative colitis in a rigorous [[blesching-cannabis-irritable-bowel-syndrome]] syndrome." PLoS ONE.

**Kaptchuk et al. (2008)**: "Components of placebo effect: Randomized controlled trial in patients with irritable bowel syndrome." BMJ.

These landmark studies demonstrated that placebo effects in IBS are real and measurable, even when patients know they are receiving a placebo. This has implications for understanding the mind-gut connection.

### Psychosomatic Research
**Whitehead, Palsson, and Jones (2002)**: "Systematic review of the comorbidity of irritable bowel syndrome with other disorders." Gastroenterology 122(4):1140-56.

**Grace and Graham (1952)**: "Relationship of specific attitudes and emotions to certain bodily diseases."

IBS shows high comorbidity with anxiety, depression, and other mind-body conditions, and specific emotional patterns are associated with gut dysfunction.

## GERD (Gastro-Esophageal Reflux Disease)

### THC and Lower Esophageal Sphincter
**Beaumont et al. (2009)**: "Effect of Delta(9)-tetrahydrocannabinol, a cannabinoid receptor agonist, on the triggering of transient lower oesophageal sphincter relaxations in dogs and humans." British Journal of Pharmacology.

THC reduced transient lower esophageal sphincter relaxations (TLESRs) -- the primary mechanism of acid reflux. This is a direct pharmacological effect on the underlying mechanism of GERD, not just symptom masking.

### Stress and Heartburn
**Naliboff et al. (2004)**: "The effect of life stress on symptoms of heartburn." Psychosomatic Medicine.

**Sharma et al. (2010)**: "Anxiety increases acid-induced esophageal hyperalgesia." Psychosomatic Medicine.

Life stress increases heartburn symptoms, and anxiety amplifies the pain perception of acid reflux. This mind-gut connection means that stress reduction should be part of any GERD treatment plan.

## Pancreatitis

### Cannabinoids Reduce Pancreatic Inflammation
**Michalski et al. (2008)**: "Cannabinoids reduce markers of inflammation and fibrosis in pancreatic stellate cells." PLoS ONE 3(2):e1701.

Cannabinoids reduced inflammatory markers and fibrosis in pancreatic stellate cells -- the cells responsible for the scarring and inflammation in chronic pancreatitis.

### Dual Role of Anandamide
**Dembiinski et al. (2008)**: "Dual, time-dependent deleterious and protective effect of anandamide on the course of cerulein-induced acute pancreatitis."

The endocannabinoid anandamide has both protective and deleterious effects in acute pancreatitis depending on timing, illustrating the complexity of endocannabinoid signaling.

### Psychosomatic Component
**Nakai et al. (1983)**: "Chronic pancreatitis as psychosomatic disorder." Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics.

Chronic pancreatitis has been characterized as a psychosomatic disorder, with emotional factors playing a role in both onset and course.

## Periodontitis

### CBD Reduces Bone Loss in Periodontitis
**Napimoga et al. (2009)**: "Cannabidiol decreases bone resorption by inhibiting RANK/RANKL expression and pro-inflammatory cytokines during experimental periodontitis." International Immunopharmacology.

CBD reduced the bone loss associated with periodontitis by inhibiting RANK/RANKL signaling -- the same pathway involved in arthritis bone erosion and osteoporosis. This is relevant because periodontitis is the leading cause of tooth loss in adults.
