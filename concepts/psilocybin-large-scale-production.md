# Large-Scale Psilocybin Production — Room Design and Workflow

Gottlieb's design for a dedicated 10x15 ft production room for bulk [[psilocybin-mushroom]] cultivation. This represents the practical scale-up from individual jar cultivation to a coordinated production schedule. See [[psilocybin-fruiting-chambers]] for fruiting chamber details and [[psilocybin-liquid-culture]] for culture preparation.

Related: [[psilocybin-mushroom-cultivation]], [[psilocybin-extraction-guide]]

## Room Design (Gottlieb, 1976)

### Specifications
- **Dimensions**: 10 ft x 15 ft (3m x 4.5m)
- **Walls**: Clean, smooth, easily washable surface (plastic sheeting, epoxy paint, or fiberglass)
- **Floor**: Concrete or sealed surface, easily cleaned
- **Ventilation**: 12-inch air exhaust fan in wall, with filtered intake
- **Temperature control**: Electric heater, thermostat, thermometer
- **Humidity control**: Ultrasonic humidifier or misting system
- **Lighting**: Fluorescent ceiling light on timer (12 hours on/12 hours off)
- **Shelving**: Four-tier shelving units, each shelf approximately 12 inches apart
- **Door**: Sealed to maintain environmental control

### Equipment Checklist
- 50+ quart Mason jars (or equivalent)
- Pressure cooker (large enough for multiple jars)
- Syringes for inoculation
- Alcohol lamp or Bunsen burner for flame sterilization
- Rubber hose with plastic funnel for substrate transfer
- Ultrasonic humidifier
- Fluorescent light fixture with timer
- Thermometer and hygrometer
- Clean cloth or [[hepa-filter]] for air quality
- Substrate ingredients (rye grain, water, lime)

## Production Schedule (Gottlieb)

### The 10-Day Cycle
Gottlieb designed a rotating 10-day cultivation cycle for continuous production:

**Day 1 — Inoculation**
- Load sterile substrate jars (rye grain prepared by boiling 45 min, drained, lime added)
- Inoculate each jar with [[liquid-culture]] or [[spore-syringe]]
- Place jars on shelves in the production room
- Room temperature: 70-75F

**Day 2-4 — Incubation (Colonization Phase)**
- Mycelium spreads through substrate
- No light needed during this phase
- Maintain temperature at 70-75F
- Check jars daily for contamination (discoloration, unusual odors)
- Remove any contaminated jars immediately

**Day 5-7 — Continued Colonization**
- Most jars should be substantially colonized by day 5-6
- Fully colonized jars appear white with mycelium throughout
- Reduce temperature slightly if growth seems too fast (lower temp = higher potency)

**Day 8-9 — [[fruiting-initiation]]**
- Move fully colonized jars to [[fruiting-conditions]]
- Introduce light (12h on/12h off cycle)
- Increase humidity (80-95% relative humidity)
- Introduce fresh air exchange
- Mushrooms typically begin appearing within 48-72 hours

**Day 10 — Harvest**
- Harvest mushrooms as veils begin to tear (before spore release)
- This is also the day to inoculate the next batch of jars
- Repeat cycle continuously

### Continuous Production
With this 10-day rotation, approximately 3 production cycles can run simultaneously in staggered phases:
- Batch A: Harvesting (Day 10)
- Batch B: Fruiting (Day 5-9)
- Batch C: Colonizing (Day 1-4)

This allows for harvesting approximately every 3-4 days from a single room.

## Yield Estimates

### Per Jar
- Wet mycelium: 50-100g per quart jar
- Dried mycelium: 5-10g per quart jar
- Crude alkaloid yield: 25-35mg per jar
- Mushroom yield: 5-15g dried per jar per flush

### Per Room (50 jars per cycle)
- Monthly production (3 cycles/month): 150 jar-batches
- Approximate dried mushroom yield: 750g-2.25kg per month
- Approximate crude alkaloid extract: 3.75-5.25g per month
- Equivalent doses (10mg standard): 375-525 doses per month

## Environmental Parameters

| Parameter | Colonization Phase | Fruiting Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | 70-75F (21-24C) | 68-72F (20-22C) |
| Humidity | 60-70% (ambient) | 80-95% (critical) |
| Light | Not required | 12h on/12h off fluorescent |
| Air exchange | Minimal (gas exchange holes) | Frequent (2-4x daily fanning) |
| CO2 level | High (promotes mycelial growth) | Low (triggers fruiting) |

## Contamination Prevention

### Room Hygiene
- Wipe all surfaces with 10% bleach solution before and after each cycle
- Spray room with Lysol or similar disinfectant
- Keep door closed; minimize traffic in and out
- Wear clean clothes; consider a lab coat
- Wash hands thoroughly before handling jars
- Use a dust mask when opening jars or handling substrates

### Jar Sterilization
- Pressure cook substrate jars at 15 PSI for 45-60 minutes
- Allow jars to cool completely before inoculation (overnight recommended)
- Never open jars in the production room — open only under sterile conditions (still air box or in front of HEPA flow hood)
- Flame-sterilize syringes between each jar inoculation

### Identifying Contamination
- **Green/blue mold**: Trichoderma — most common contaminant; discard immediately
- **Black spots**: Aspergillus — discard; some species produce harmful spores
- **Slimy appearance**: Bacterial contamination — sour smell; discard
- **Pink/orange growth**: Various molds — discard
- **Healthy mycelium**: White, fluffy, organized growth with mushroom-like smell
