# Planting Schedules and Seasonal Timing for Cold Climates

## The Persephone Period

The central concept governing winter planting schedules is the "Persephone months" — the period when days are shorter than ten hours. Named after the Greek myth where Demeter makes the earth barren during Persephone's absence in the underworld, this period causes plant growth to slow dramatically.

### Persephone Dates by Latitude

|----------|----------|-------------------|

| Washington, D.C. | 39°N | Nov 17 – Jan 24 |
| Charlotte, NC | 35°N | Dec 1 – Jan 10 |
| Atlanta, GA | 33°N | Dec 7 – Jan 5 |
| Savannah, GA / El Paso, TX / San Diego, CA | 32°N | Never below 10 hrs |

The winter harvest could be successful as far north as Anchorage, even with the extended Persephone period.

## The Growth Pattern

A crop that takes 40 days to mature for a September 1 or June 1 harvest requires:
- **80 days** from sowing to maturity for a midwinter harvest in a cool (minimally heated) house
- **120 days** from sowing to maturity in a cold (unheated) house

This pattern holds for most winter crops. Growers can use this to calculate planting dates: trace from the desired harvest date back along the appropriate curve to determine days needed, then count back from the harvest date.

### Latitude Adjustment
Gardeners at higher or lower latitudes than approximately the 45th parallel should adjust expected days to harvest based on the ratio between day length at their latitude and Coleman's. Days to harvest will be longer at higher latitudes and shorter at lower latitudes.

## The Pagan Agricultural Calendar

Coleman uses the traditional eight-point agricultural calendar (Wheel of Life) to frame the seasonal rhythm:

**Quarter days:**
- Spring equinox (March 20/21)
- Summer solstice (June 21/22)
- Autumn equinox (September 20/21)
- Winter solstice (December 20/21)

**Cross-quarter days:**
- **Imbolc** (~February 2) — onset of lactation, growing of the light
- **Beltane** (~May 1) — first day of summer, peak of spring plantings
- **Lughnasadh** (~August 1) — first day of fall, start of fall planting season for winter crops
- **Samhain** (~November 1) — end of outdoor-harvest season, beginning of the Persephone period

August 1 (Lughnasadh) is the start of fall planting season for winter crops. November 1 (Samhain) is the end of outdoor-harvest season.

## Critical Timing Principles

1. **Crops must reach minimum size before Persephone period**: To be dependably productive for winter harvesting, crops need to make sufficient growth in the fall. Even true winter annuals are slow to get started if sown during short days of late fall.

2. **Cutoff for new sowings**: End of October. After that, seeds will germinate but grow very slowly — seedlings "just sit there waiting expectantly." Once the ten-hour day returns in February, growth speeds up.

3. **Precision matters more for fall than spring**: The choice of precise sowing dates for fall planting is much more crucial than for spring planting. Dates are also very crop-specific.

4. **Sow often and keep notes**: The best advice for any grower determining their own dates.

## Quick Reference: Key Dates

- **August 1**: Start of "second spring" — begin planting winter-harvest crops
- **September**: Peak planting month for winter greenhouse crops
- **October 31**: Cutoff for new sowings
- **November 5**: Start of Persephone period at 44°N
- **February 5**: End of Persephone period at 44°N
- **Late October**: Move greenhouses over winter crops
