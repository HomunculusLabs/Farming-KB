## Geographic Distribution

Psilocybe cubensis has a pantropical and subtropical distribution. It is
extremely common throughout Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean, and South
America. In North America, it occurs along the Gulf Coast states (Florida,
Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Texas) where warm, humid conditions prevail.
In the United States, it has been reported from Florida, Louisiana, Mississippi,
Georgia, Alabama, and Texas, typically fruiting on cow dung in pastures during
warm, wet seasons.

The species is notably absent from Europe in natural settings. Reports of P.
cubensis from European countries almost invariably represent illegally
cultivated or dried specimens imported from tropical source countries. This
absence is ecologically significant and is related to the historical
introduction of cattle to the Americas (see Historical Biogeography below).

In Africa, P. cubensis has been reported from Kenya and other tropical regions,
though early reports by Cullinan et al. (1945) and Charters (1957, 1958) were
somewhat confused. The species is also known from across tropical Asia, where
it has been reported from Thailand, Cambodia, Indonesia, and other Southeast
Asian countries. In Oceania, it occurs in eastern Australia and has been
reported from the Pacific Islands.

## Ecology and Habitat

Psilocybe cubensis is a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species, primarily
associated with the dung of herbivorous mammals, especially cattle (Bos taurus
and Bos indicus). This ecological specialization is central to understanding
both its distribution and its biogeographical history. The fungus fruits
abundantly on well-rotted dung in pastures, grasslands, and disturbed tropical
and subtropical habitats.

The species thrives in warm, humid environments with temperatures typically
between 24 and 30 degrees Celsius. Fruiting is triggered by heavy rainfall
following dry periods. In its natural range, P. cubensis can be found year-round
in truly tropical climates, while in subtropical areas it is seasonal, typically
fruiting during the warmest and wettest months.
