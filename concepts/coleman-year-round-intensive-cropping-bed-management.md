## Year-Round Intensive Cropping and Bed Management

## Overview

At the heart of Eliot Coleman"s four-season farming system is the principle of
year-round intensive cropping — the practice of keeping every greenhouse bed in
continuous production throughout the entire year. Rather than treating winter as a
dormant period or accepting reduced productivity during cold months, Coleman's system
treats every season as a [[coleman-succession-planting]], and
a commitment to rapid turnover between crops.

## Succession Planting Strategy

Succession planting is the technique of sowing vegetables at multiple dates to ensure
continuous harvest over an extended period. In Coleman"s winter harvest system,
succession planting is even more critical than in spring/summer growing because the
seasonal dynamics are reversed:

- **Spring growing:** Days are lengthening and temperatures are warming. Crops
  planted later mature faster than those planted earlier.
- **Fall/winter growing:** Days are shortening and temperatures are dropping. Crops
  planted later mature more slowly than those planted earlier. The choice of precise
  sowing dates for fall planting is "much more crucial than for spring planting."
- **Timing is crop-specific:** Each vegetable variety has an optimal fall planting
  window. Some crops (like mache and claytonia) can be planted late and still produce.
  Others (like certain lettuces and chicories) must be planted earlier to reach
  adequate size before day length becomes limiting.

### 2. Protected Cultivation
Standard plastic-covered gothic-style hoop houses provide the primary protection.
An inner layer of floating row cover supported by wire wickets at 12 inches above
the soil creates a "twice-tempered" climate. This double layer can maintain
temperatures 25-30°F above outdoor extremes.

### 3. Cut-and-Come-Again Harvesting
Many winter crops are harvested using cut-and-come-again methods, where outer leaves
are removed while the center continues growing. This approach allows a single planting
to produce multiple harvests over weeks or months, maximizing the productive value
of each bed.
