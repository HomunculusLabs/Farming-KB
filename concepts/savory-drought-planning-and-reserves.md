# Drought Planning and Reserves

In [[savory-holistic-resource-management-animal-impact]],
drought planning represents a fundamental shift from conventional ranching
wisdom. Rather than withdrawing land from grazing as a physical reserve,
Savory advocates holding drought reserve as time rather than area. This
approach has profound implications for [[savory-energy-flow-and-solar-capture]], livestock performance, and overall ranch
productivity.

## The Traditional Approach: Area Reserve

Typically, prudent ranchers withdraw certain areas from grazing as a
drought reserve to give themselves a margin of time should poor growth
conditions lead to depletion of forage elsewhere. They fence off two or
more paddocks and leave them ungrazed, hoping that if drought strikes,
those paddocks will carry the herd.

This approach seems logical but creates several hidden problems. In a
ten-paddock cell where two paddocks are withdrawn, only eight remain for
grazing. Given an average recovery period of sixty days, this means
8.5-day average grazing periods as opposed to 6.6 days if all ten paddocks
had been grazed. The shorter grazing periods for the fully utilized cell
would have meant greater energy flow with better livestock performance,
better plant growth, and less pressure on the land that was grazed.

## Time Reserve: The Holistic Approach

Since all forage can be measured in [[savory-animal-impact-and-herd-effect]], no rule says where drought reserves must be located.
You only really need to know when they will be necessary. Instead of
withdrawing area, think thusly: "My whole spread has X number of animal
days of forage. Normally I will need Y, so I will have X minus Y animal
days of reserve."

This reflects the fact that drought is measured in days: days until the
rain comes, days until growth starts. Your banker does not ask how many
acres you have in reserve, but simply how long you can hold out.

## Production Losses from Area Reserves

Even before the drought ever occurs, the area reserve approach usually
means a production loss. The growth in the two rested paddocks probably
does not offset the amount not grown in the other eight. In addition, the
two long-rested paddocks may suffer from lack of [[savory-animal-impact-and-herd-effect]], and the forage itself becomes stale,
excessively fibrous, and low in protein and energy.

The problem continues into the dormant season as livestock move through
fewer paddocks, meaning longer grazing periods and more grazing on fouled
ground. Fire risk also increases as all the eggs rest in only two paddocks.

## Comparing the Two Approaches

In the dormant season, the area reserve cell has less forage in each
grazed paddock than the time reserve cell, because the same number of
animal days have been taken from less land over a longer grazing period.
The stock start off in worse condition due to longer grazing periods
during the months of most promising gain.

When the growing season should begin, all eight paddocks in the area
reserve cell may be grazed down to scratch. If new growth does not start,
the reserve paddocks will carry the herd, but the animals have only one
move and stay on fouled ground for a prolonged time without stimulation.

The time reserve cell starts the dormant season in better condition due to
faster moves previously made over more ground. All paddocks can be grazed
with shorter grazing periods and less time on fouled ground. If new growth
does not start on schedule, all paddocks still contain a total of sixty
days of forage. The animals reached that point in better condition and now
have frequent stimulating moves onto fresh ground.

## Successional Risk of Area Reserves

Considering that over half the time the growing season begins on schedule,
the ungrazed paddocks may become a liability. In many environments, long-
rested paddocks will encourage [[savory-plant-succession-and-community-dynamics]] shifts toward woody brush that you do not want.
Old grass that accumulates can also choke plants and slow growth.

## Drought and Ecosystem Health

Savory emphasizes that what is often blamed on drought is frequently the
result of poor management. In one case, an area was blamed for drought,
but in the year of worst so-called drought the records showed one of the
best rainy seasons in both total volume and distribution. The real cause
was gradual deterioration of the [[savory-water-cycle-management]] and [[savory-ecosystem-foundation-blocks]].

When the [[savory-water-cycle-management]] is ineffective, most
years of below-average or poorly distributed rain become droughts, and the
rainy times become flood years. Effective precipitation, which soaks in
and becomes available to plant roots, is what truly matters.

## Planning for Drought Resilience

Holistic [[savory-livestock-grazing-planning]] builds
drought resilience through several mechanisms. Shorter grazing periods
maintain higher energy flow, which means better root systems and better
soil cover. Better soil cover means more effective rainfall and more
moisture stored in the soil. More moisture means plants can continue
growing longer into dry spells.

The biological planning process allows managers to foresee grazing needs
at all times of the year and decide which paddocks will supply them. This
enables accurate advance knowledge of whether the land can provide the
forage needed, with enough margin to feed wildlife and buffer drought risk.

## Measuring Drought in Animal Days

The key insight is that drought reserves should be thought of in terms of
time, not area. Any paddock in the cell can serve as part of the reserve.
By planning grazing so that all paddocks are used but forage is budgeted
carefully, the manager retains flexibility. If rain comes on schedule, no
old forage accumulates as a problem. If drought persists, the full
complement of paddocks provides maximum flexibility for the herd.

Savory advises: think twice and again before continuing with old-fashioned
drought reserves. The time reserve approach aligns with the [[savory-testing-and-management-guidelines]] and supports
all four [[savory-ecosystem-foundation-blocks]].
