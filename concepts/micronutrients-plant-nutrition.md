# Micronutrients in Plant Nutrition — Boron, Iron, Manganese, Zinc, Copper

While plants require macronutrients (N, P, K) in large quantities, micronutrients are needed only in trace amounts — yet they are equally essential for plant health. Deficiencies of micronutrients can cause severe growth problems even when macronutrients are abundant.

## Overview of Micronutrients

The eight essential micronutrients (in rough order of plant requirement):
1. Iron (Fe)
2. Manganese (Mn)
3. Zinc (Zn)
4. Copper (Cu)
5. Boron (B)
6. Molybdenum (Mo)
7. Chlorine (Cl)
8. Nickel (Ni)

## Boron (B)

### Functions
- Essential for cell wall formation (cross-links pectin polysaccharides)
- Important for pollen tube growth and seed development
- Involved in sugar transport and membrane integrity
- Required for nitrogen metabolism

### Deficiency Symptoms
- Death of terminal growing points (apical meristem)
- Thick, brittle, curled leaves
- Hollow stems and heart rot in root vegetables
- Poor flower and fruit set
- Reduced seed production

### Sources
- Organic matter (boron is released during decomposition)
- Borax (sodium borate) — caution: very small amounts needed; toxic in excess
- Compost and seaweed extracts

### pH Relationship
Boron availability decreases at high pH. Liming can induce boron deficiency. Sandy soils with low organic matter are most prone to deficiency.

## Iron (Fe)

### Functions
- Essential for chlorophyll synthesis
- Component of many enzymes (cytochromes, catalase, peroxidase)
- Required for electron transport in photosynthesis
- Involved in nitrogen fixation

### Deficiency Symptoms
- Interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between veins) in young leaves
- Veins remain green while leaf tissue turns yellow
- In severe cases, leaves become almost white
- Most common in alkaline soils (high pH)

### Iron Chlorosis
The classic micronutrient deficiency problem. Iron may be abundant in soil but unavailable at high pH due to precipitation as insoluble iron oxides/hydroxides.

### Solutions
- Lower soil pH (add sulfur, organic matter)
- Use chelated iron (iron-EDDHA) for rapid correction
- Apply acidifying organic amendments
- Select iron-efficient plant varieties

## Manganese (Mn)

### Functions
- Activates many enzyme systems involved in photosynthesis, nitrogen metabolism, and respiration
- Essential for the water-splitting reaction of photosystem II
- Involved in chlorophyll synthesis

### Deficiency Symptoms
- Interveinal chlorosis similar to iron deficiency
- But occurs on younger leaves less than iron deficiency
- Gray speck in oats; marsh spot in peas
- Most common in high-pH, sandy, or organic soils

### Toxicity
More common than deficiency in acid soils. Manganese toxicity causes brown spots on older leaves and can induce iron deficiency.

## Zinc (Zn)

### Functions
- Essential for auxin (growth hormone) synthesis
- Activates many enzymes
- Involved in protein synthesis and seed production
- Important for membrane integrity

### Deficiency Symptoms
- "Little leaf" — reduced leaf size
- Interveinal chlorosis
- Shortened internodes (rosetting)
- Most common in high-pH, phosphorus-rich, or sandy soils

### Interaction with Phosphorus
Excessive phosphorus can induce zinc deficiency by reducing zinc availability or uptake. This is a common problem in heavily fertilized soils.

## Copper (Cu)

### Functions
- Essential for lignin synthesis (cell wall strength)
- Component of plastocyanin (electron transport in photosynthesis)
- Involved in pollen formation and seed viability
- Activates several enzymes

### Deficiency Symptoms
- Wilting of younger leaves (despite adequate water)
- Tip death
- Reduced seed set
- Most common in peaty or sandy soils

### Toxicity
Can occur in soils with high copper from repeated fungicide applications. Causes iron deficiency-like symptoms.

## General Principles

### pH is Critical
Most micronutrients become less available at high pH. Maintaining appropriate [[ozier-pdc-composting-and-soil-building]] and organic amendments are excellent micronutrient sources.

### The Danger of Excess
Because micronutrients are needed in tiny amounts, it is very easy to over-apply and cause toxicity. Symptoms of micronutrient toxicity are often similar to deficiency symptoms of other nutrients, making diagnosis difficult.
