# Insect-Associated Fungi Sampling and Diversity

Source: Biodiversity of Fungi (Hyde et al., Ch. 18)

Vast numbers of fungi are associated with a variety of insects and other arthropods, forming symbioses of various types including necrotrophic and biotrophic parasitism, direct fungal food sources, enzyme production, and spore-dispersal interactions. The number of species is so large and their morphologies so specialized that only a few specialists study them.

## Types of Associations

- **Necrotrophic parasites**: Kill host cells and use them as nutrient source; dispersed by their hosts
- **Biotrophic parasites**: Require living host cells
- **Fungus-feeding associations**: Insects use fungi directly as food or enzyme sources, enabling use of refractory nutrient resources
- **Spore-dispersal interactions**: Fungi merely dispersed by arthropods

## Sampling Methodologies

### Flight-Interception Traps
Large-area traps (~3m across) of black fine-mesh netting suspended vertically above preservative trays. Effective for Coleoptera and Diptera. In lowland tropical moist forest in Indonesia, average weekly catches ranged from 100-250 beetle species.

### Light Traps
UV and mercury vapor lamps attract a wide range of insects including water-associated beetles (Gyrinidae, Dytiscidae, Hydrophilidae), which are rich sources of Laboulbeniales.

### Pitfall Traps
Containers sunk into ground for trapping ground-dwelling invertebrates such as carabid beetles. Placed in grid systems for predefined time periods.

### Canopy Fogging
Synthetic pyrethroid (Reslin E) applied to individual trees. Yields large numbers of Chrysomelidae and Corylophidae, hosts of Corylophomyces, Dimeromyces, Laboulbenia, and Rickia (Laboulbeniales).

### Litter Samples
Known volumes of litter processed through Berlese funnels yield Carabidae, Staphylinidae, Ptiliidae, ants, millipedes, and mites.

### Malaise Traps
Rich source of arthropod material, though comparison of catches between sites is difficult.

## Necrotrophic Parasite Taxonomy

Major groups of fungi that kill arthropods (Table 18.1 from Hyde et al.):

### Chytridiomycota
- Coelomomyces (Blastocladiales) - pathogens of mosquito and chironomid larvae, with a two-host life cycle (mosquito larva + copepod)

### Zygomycota
- **Entomophthorales**: Entomophaga, Entomophthora, Erynia, Furia, Massospora, Pandora, Zoophthora (Entomophthoraceae); Neozygites (Neozygitaceae); Conidiobolus (Ancylistaceae)

### Ascomycota
- **Hypocreales (Clavicipitaceae)**: Cordyceps (>200 species), Cordycepioideus, Ophiocordyceps, Torrubiella, Gibellula, Pseudogibellula, Akanthomyces, Nomurea, Hymenostilbe, Hirsutella, Paraisaria
- **Hypocrealean anamorphs**: Aschersonia, Beauveria, Fusarium, Hirsutella, Metarhizium, Nomuraea, Paecilomyces, Tolypocladium, Verticillium
- **Tubeufiaceae**: Podonectria

### Cordyceps Biology
Species infect hosts via partspores (fragmented ascospores) or conidia. The fungus fills the hemocoel, kills the host, and produces an endosclerotium that may fill the inside of the corpse. Endosclerotium produces one to several stromata that rupture the exoskeleton at joints or protrude through orifices. Highest species diversity in the tropics. C. militaris is the most commonly encountered species (vibrant red to reddish orange).

## Specimen Preservation

- Most important: dry specimens quickly (air-drying, desiccants, gentle heating)
- Do NOT preserve in alcohol unless drying is impossible
- Spores remain viable for weeks/months on properly dried specimens
- Entomophthoralean fungi isolated most readily from very fresh specimens
- Mounting medium: aceto-orcein is outstanding for entomopathogens
- Sabouraud dextrose agar + 1% yeast extract commonly used for culture
- USDA ARSEF collection: ~5,000 isolates of 300+ fungal taxa (world's largest repository for insect fungi)
