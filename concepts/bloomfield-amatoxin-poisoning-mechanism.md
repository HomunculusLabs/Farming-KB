# Amatoxin Poisoning Mechanism

Amatoxins are among the most lethal natural poisons produced by any
organism. Found primarily in species of Amanita (death caps and
destroying angels) and Galerina (deadly galerinas), these miniproteins
or peptides are absorbed from the intestine and lay waste to the liver
with devastating efficiency.

## Amatoxin Chemistry

Amatoxins are cyclic octapeptides, meaning they consist of a ring of
eight amino acids. The ring structure contains several unusual amino
acids not found in typical proteins, including a cross-bridged
tryptophan residue that is essential for toxicity.

The principal amatoxins include alpha-amanitin, beta-amanitin,
gamma-amanitin, and epsilon-amanitin. Alpha-amanitin is the most toxic
and most abundant in [[blesching-cannabis-apoptosis-and-cancer-cell-death]].

## Symptoms and Timeline

Amatoxin poisoning follows a characteristic and deceptive timeline.
Phase 1 is an asymptomatic latent period lasting 6 to 24 hours. The
patient feels fine after consuming the mushrooms. This delayed onset is
precisely what makes these toxins so dangerous.

Phase 2 brings gastrointestinal symptoms at 24 to 48 hours, including
abdominal cramping, vomiting, and violent diarrhea. These symptoms are
often mistaken for ordinary food poisoning and are severe and depleting.

Phase 3 is the "honeymoon" at 48 to 72 hours. Symptoms abate and the
patient may appear well enough to leave the hospital. Unfortunately,
liver damage has been progressing silently throughout the earlier phases.

Phase 4 is hepatic failure and death at 72 to 120 hours. Liver failure
becomes clinically apparent, coma ensues, and death is a frequent
outcome without intervention.
