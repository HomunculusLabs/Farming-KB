## Bare Root Planting Protocol

For new bare root plantings, Ingham recommends creating a slurry mixture of compost
and worm castings with additional nutrients (if recommended by SFI testing) combined
with compost tea. The bare roots are dipped into this slurry to thoroughly coat the
root system before planting. This accomplishes several things simultaneously: the
compost and worm castings provide [[cannabis-cultivar-microbial-community-effects]] throughout the root zone. The key principle is
to avoid sharp demarcations between the amended planting hole and the surrounding
native soil, which can create water and root-growth barriers. Ingham recommends
gradually mixing the compost and worm castings into the native soil away from the
root plug.

## Transplant Inoculation

For all transplanting operations, Ingham recommends a standard amendment ratio of
30-50% compost and/or worm castings mixed with site soil. This applies equally to
trees, vines, vegetable seedlings, and ornamental transplants. The compost provides
both the microbial inoculant and the organic substrate for the biology to colonize
and persist. After planting, all transplants should be watered in with compost tea
to jump-start the soil foodweb in the root zone.

## VAM Establishment Through Tea

The vesicular-arbuscular mycorrhizal (VAM) fungi are a critical component of the
transplant inoculation strategy. VAM fungi extend the effective root zone of plants
by forming symbiotic associations with root cells, improving phosphorus uptake,
water absorption, and disease resistance. Compost tea, when brewed from
fungal-dominated compost, contains VAM spores and hyphal fragments that can
initiate colonization of new plant roots.

Ingham specifically recommends dipping bare vine roots in tea with VAM spores and

planting. The growth hormones in the tea stimulate root initiation and extension,
creating more colonization sites for VAM fungi. For orchard trees, the same approach
applies: the bare roots are dipped before planting, and the planting hole is
watered in with compost tea to ensure thorough inoculation.
