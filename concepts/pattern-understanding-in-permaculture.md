# Pattern Understanding in Permaculture

## Overview

Pattern understanding is a foundational concept in [[james-lovelock]] (1979) expressed a combination of scientific insights and older tribal beliefs asserting the interdependence of animate and inanimate events. The universe and the Earth behave as self-regulating and self-generated constructs, very much akin to a single organism or a thought process. The conditions making life possible are balanced about such fine tolerances that many processes likely exist solely to preserve this equilibrium in its dynamic stability.

### Birch and Cobb's Liberation of Life

Biologists Birch and Cobb (1984) denied the validity of the existence of individual organisms or separate events; all exist in a field of events or as an expression of one life force. Organisms exist only as an inseparable part of their event environments, in continual process of exchange with the animate and inanimate entities that surround them. We are acted upon and acting, created and creating, shaped and shaping.

### Capra's Tao of Physics

Theoretical physicist Fritjof Capra (1976) contributed to world views in conflict with the ethics governing political, educational, and economic systems, but all contributing to an increasing effort to unify and cooperate in a common ethic of earth-care—without which we have no meaning to the universe.

## Pattern Application

Pattern understanding has two aspects:

1. **Perception** of existing patterns and how they function
2. **Imposition** of pattern on sites to achieve specific ends

Both are skills of sophisticated design, potentially resulting in specific strategies, harmonious resolution of problems, or the production of local resources.

### Critique of Rectilinear Patterns

A bird's-eye view of centralised and disempowered societies reveals a strictly rectilinear network of streets, farms, and property boundaries. This is patterning designed to suit survey instruments rather than to serve human or environmental needs. The straight-line patterns that result prevent sensible landscape planning and produce landscapes that are neither aesthetically nor functionally satisfactory.

### Traditional Approaches to Land Division

Hawaiian villagers used natural ridgelines as boundaries, creating stable and resource-rich landscapes reaching from cloud-forests to outer reefs. Their conic and radial volcanic landscapes with radial water lines suited this method of land division, enabling whole valleys of people to:

- Maintain clean catchments
- Store and divert mid-slope water resources
- Catch lost nutrients in shallow ocean enclosures (converting to algae, then crabs and fish)
- Preserve offshore reef areas and marine environments

Zulus and American Indians adopted circular or zonal modes in their plains settlements. Such models can be studied and adopted by future bioregional societies as sane, caring people become the majority.

## Significance for Design

Sensible land division is a long-delayed but essential precursor to a stable society. Pattern analysis teaches designers to study the relationships of orders and forms in nature, ensuring that patterned systems are of appropriate size and the right order for their context.
