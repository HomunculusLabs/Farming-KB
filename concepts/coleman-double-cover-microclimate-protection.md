## The Diurnal Heat Cycle

A key factor in making this system work is the diurnal (daytime)
influx of [[coleman-winter-day-length-and-persephone-months]]
warms the covered beds above freezing during daylight hours. With the
exception of one or two extremely cold winter days each year -- when
an unusually cold night is followed by a heavily overcast day -- the
soil in the beds is always unfrozen by 10 AM and ready to be raked
into a fine new seedbed for replanting.

This daily thawing cycle means that even though the crops may be
frozen solid at dawn, by mid-morning they have thawed and resumed
growth. During the dawn visit to a cold house, all the crops are
stiff and frost-coated, bleak enough to convince anyone that the
idea is foolhardy. Yet a few hours later, after even weak winter
sunlight has warmed the interior, the plants stand upright and
crisp, ready for harvest.
