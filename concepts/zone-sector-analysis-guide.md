### Zone 1: The Intensive Garden
Visited daily. This is the most intensively managed area, placed immediately outside the kitchen door. Everything here needs frequent attention or harvesting.

Elements:
- Kitchen herb garden
- Salad greens and frequently harvested vegetables
- Seedling nursery
- Small greenhouse or cold frame
- Compost pile (close to kitchen for convenient scraps)
- Tool storage
- Sitting area for relaxation and observation
- Chicken coop (for easy daily care)

### Zone 2: The Productive Garden
Visited several times per week. Less intensive than Zone 1 but still actively managed. This is the main food production area.

Elements:
- Main [[beneficial-insect-habitat]]
- Chicken forage area

### Zone 3: The Farm
Visited weekly to monthly. Managed at lower intensity with larger plantings and some grazing.

Elements:
- Large fruit trees (standard size)
- Field crops (grains, beans, potatoes)
- Pasture for larger animals
- Main water storage (dams, large cisterns)
- Large-scale composting
- Firewood production
- Windbreaks

### Zone 4: The Forage Zone
Visited monthly or seasonally. Managed for foraging and harvesting of wild resources.

Elements:
- Timber production
- Wild food foraging (mushrooms, berries, wild greens)
- Beekeeping
- Grazing (cattle, sheep, goats)
- Large-scale water harvesting
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-wildlife-corridors]]

### Zone 5: The Wilderness
Visited rarely (for observation, education, and inspiration). Left undisturbed as a reference ecosystem.

Elements:
- Natural forest or native vegetation
- [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]]
- [[mycofiltration-watershed-protection]]
- Seed source for native plants
- Educational and spiritual resource

## Sector Analysis

Sectors are external energies and influences that affect the site from outside. Mapping sectors helps position elements to take advantage of beneficial energies and protect against harmful ones.

### Sun Sector
- Track the sun's path through the year (winter low angle, summer high angle)
- Identify sunny and shady areas at different seasons
