## Overview

Eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a chronic inflammatory skin condition characterized by itchy, red, dry, and cracked skin. It affects up to 20% of children and 3% of adults worldwide. The skin's endocannabinoid system (ECS) plays a key role in regulating cutaneous inflammation and allergic responses, making cannabinoid therapies a promising approach. - Anandamide (endogenous cannabinoid) modulates inflammatory responses in skin cells

**Anti-inflammatory Mechanisms**
- CB2 receptor activation reduces inflammatory cytokine production in skin cells
- Cannabinoids inhibit mast cell degranulation — a key trigger of eczema flares
- CBD demonstrates potent anti-inflammatory effects through multiple pathways beyond CB1/CB2 (TRPV1, GPR55, PPAR-gamma)
- THC also shows anti-inflammatory properties through CB1/CB2 activation

**Psychological Stress Connection**
- Psychological stress is a well-documented trigger for eczema flares (Grace and Graham, 1952)
- Cannabis may address both the inflammatory component and the stress-anxiety component of eczema
- Anxiolytic effects of CBD may reduce stress-induced flare cycles

## Integrative Approaches

### Curcumin (Turmeric)
- Curcumin has significant anti-inflammatory and anticancer properties (Aggarwal et al., 2003)
- May complement cannabinoid therapy for skin inflammation
- Topical curcumin preparations also show benefit

### Skin Barrier Support
- Moisturizers with ceramides support the skin barrier
- Probiotics may modulate immune responses relevant to atopic dermatitis
- Avoiding known triggers (harsh soaps, allergens, extreme temperatures)

### Essential Fatty Acids
- Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil, flaxseed) reduce systemic inflammation
- Gamma-linolenic acid (evening primrose oil, borage oil) has shown benefit in some eczema studies

## Administration Methods

- **Topical CBD creams and salves:** Direct application to affected areas — preferred for localized eczema
- **CBD oil sublingual:** For systemic anti-inflammatory effects
- **Balanced THC:CBD oral preparations:** For combined anti-inflammatory and anti-anxiety effects
- **Bath soaks with cannabis-infused salts:** For full-body coverage

## Safety

- Topical application has minimal systemic absorption
- CBD-dominant preparations avoid psychoactive effects
- Patch testing recommended before widespread topical use
- Consult dermatologist for severe cases

## Endocannabinoid Tone and Skin Health

The concept of endocannabinoid tone — the baseline activity level of the ECS — is particularly relevant to chronic skin conditions like eczema. Research suggests that individuals with atopic dermatitis may have deficient cutaneous endocannabinoid signaling, which contributes to the chronic inflammation cycle.

A 2019 study published in the journal Molecules examined the effects of a topical CBD formulation on patients with eczema and psoriasis, finding significant improvements in skin parameters including hydration, elasticity, and reduced transepidermal water loss.
The formulation contained CBD in a lipid-based carrier system designed to penetrate the stratum corneum effectively.

## Comparison with Conventional Treatments

Conventional eczema treatments include topical corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, pimecrolimus), and systemic immunosuppressants for severe cases.
While effective, these treatments carry significant risks with long-term use: corticosteroids cause skin thinning, telangiectasia, and hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis suppression.
Calcineurin inhibitors carry FDA black box warnings regarding theoretical lymphoma risk.
