# Entheogen Vendors and Sources
## Overview
Sourcing entheogenic plants, seeds, spores, and extraction materials requires careful navigation of legal, ethical, and practical considerations. Many entheogenic plants are legal to grow and possess (only becoming illegal when consumed for their psychoactive content), while others occupy legal gray areas. This page provides guidance on legitimate sourcing channels, vendor types, and important safety and ethical considerations.
Disclaimer: This page is for informational purposes only. Verify the legality of any purchase in your jurisdiction before ordering.
## Legal Plant Sources
### Ayahuasca Plants
**Banisteriopsis caapi (Ayahuasca Vine)**
- Available from many ethnobotanical suppliers as dried vine, powdered vine, or live plants
- Legally sold as ornamental, botanical specimen, or incense material
- Not explicitly scheduled in the US or most countries
- Common sources: specialty ethnobotanical shops, Amazon importers
**Psychotria viridis (Chacruna)**
- Available as dried leaf or live plant
- Not scheduled in the US
- Live plants require tropical greenhouse conditions
- Available from ethnobotanical nurseries
**Diplopterys cabrerana (Chaliponga)**
- Less commonly available than chacruna
- Available from specialty ethnobotanical suppliers
### Cactus Sources
**Trichocereus pachanoi (San Pedro)**
- Widely available as live plants from nurseries and online cactus vendors
- Sold as ornamental landscaping cactus
- Legal to grow in the US and most countries
- Common sources: cactus nurseries, Etsy, eBay, specialty succulent shops
- Cuttings are readily available and easy to root
**[[trichocereus-peruvianus]] (Peruvian Torch)**
- Available from the same sources as San Pedro
- Slightly less common but still widely available
**Lophophora williamsii (Peyote)**
- Significantly harder to obtain legally
- Conservation status is a major concern — wild populations are threatened
- Some nurseries sell cultivated specimens, but legality varies
- In the US, peyote is controlled and only available through NAC supply chains
### Other Entheogenic Plants
**Salvia divinorum**
- Available as live plants, cuttings, and dried leaf from many vendors
- Not federally scheduled in the US (state laws vary)
- Commonly found on eBay, Etsy, and specialty ethnobotanical shops
- Cuttings are the most common propagation method (the plant rarely produces viable seeds)
**Peganum harmala (Syrian Rue)**
- Seeds are widely available — sold as botanical specimens, dye material, and incense
- Legal in the US and most countries
- Available from herb shops, ethnobotanical suppliers, and online seed vendors
- Very easy to grow from seed — hardy, drought-tolerant plant
**Mimosa hostilis / Mimosa tenuiflora**
- Root bark is available from ethnobotanical suppliers
- Legal status has become more restrictive in some countries
- Sold as a dye material or botanical specimen
- Availability fluctuates based on regulatory pressure
**Amanita muscaria**
- Dried caps available from many online vendors
- Legal in the US and most countries (not a controlled substance)
- Also available as extracts and tinctures
- Sold as herbal supplement or ethnobotanical specimen
**Tabernanthe iboga**
- Seeds and root bark available from specialized ethnobotanical suppliers
- Iboga is a controlled substance in many jurisdictions — verify legality
- Conservation status is a concern — wild populations in Gabon are under pressure
## Mushroom Spore Vendors
Psilocybin mushroom spores do not contain psilocybin or psilocin and are therefore legal to possess in most US states. They are sold for "microscopy research" or "educational purposes."
### Legal Status of Spores
- **Legal**: Most US states (excluding California, Georgia, Idaho)
- **Banned**: California, Georgia, Idaho
- **Europe**: Legal in most EU countries; check local laws
- **Canada**: Spores are in a legal gray area
### Major Spore Vendor Types
- Online spore shops specializing in microscopy supplies
- Syringe vendors (sterile water + spores in a syringe)
- Print vendors (spore prints on foil or paper)
- Liquid culture vendors (more advanced — living mycelium in nutrient solution)
### What to Look For
- Reputable vendor with good reviews
- Clean, sterile packaging
- Species identification (not all vendors identify strains accurately)
- Proper storage instructions
- Discrete shipping
### Common Species Available
- Psilocybe cubensis (dozens of named varieties: Golden Teacher, B+, Ecuador, Thai, etc.)
- Psilocybe cyanescens (more specialized vendors)
- Psilocybe semilanceata (rarely available commercially — mostly foraged)
- Panaeolus cyanescens ("copelandia")
- Gymnopilus species
## Extraction Chemical Suppliers
For those performing plant extractions (see [[dmt-extraction-guide]] and [[mescaline-extraction-guide]]):
### Common Chemicals Needed
- **Sodium hydroxide (NaOH / lye)**: Available from soap-making suppliers, hardware stores
- **Naphtha**: Available as lighter fluid (Ronsonol, Zippo) — must be 100% light petroleum distillate, unscented
- **Xylene / Toluene**: Available from hardware stores as paint thinner (ensure pure, no additives)
- **Citric acid**: Grocery stores, brewing supply shops
- **Hydrochloric acid (HCl)**: Hardware stores (muriatic acid), pool supply stores
- **Ethanol**: High-proof everclear (190 proof) from liquor stores; denatured ethanol from hardware stores (ensure no toxic denaturants if product will be consumed)
- **Fumaric acid**: Brewing supply shops, online chemical suppliers
- **Acetone**: Hardware stores, nail polish remover (ensure pure)
### Reputable Supplier Types
- Soap-making and cosmetic ingredient suppliers (for NaOH, citric acid, oils)
- Hardware stores (for solvents, lye, acids)
- Chemical supply companies (for laboratory-grade reagents)
- Brewing supply shops (for acids, ethanol, pH testing)
- Photographic supply shops (for some solvents)
## Ethical Considerations
### Sustainability
- **Peyote**: Do not purchase wild-harvested peyote — populations are critically threatened. Only cultivated specimens should be considered
- **Iboga**: Wild iboga harvesting is putting pressure on Gabonese forests. Seek sustainably cultivated sources
- **Mimosa hostilis**: Brazilian harvesting has raised ecological concerns
- **Salvia divinorum**: Wild populations in Oaxaca are limited — cultivate rather than wild-harvest
### Cultural Respect
- Many of these plants are sacred to indigenous communities
- Commercial exploitation of traditional medicines raises ethical questions
- Consider supporting indigenous communities and conservation efforts
- Educate yourself on the cultural context before using these substances
### Vendor Red Flags
- Claims of "legal highs" or "designer drugs"
- Products marketed for consumption that are sold as "not for human consumption"
- No physical address or contact information
- Poor reviews or no reviews
- Prices significantly below or above market rate
- Unsolicited advertising or aggressive marketing
## Safety Considerations
- Verify the identity of any plant material you receive
- Start with low doses — potency varies enormously
- Test substances with reagent tests when possible
- Be aware of legal risks in your jurisdiction
- Never consume unidentified plant material
- See [[psychedelic-plants-legal-status]] for legal information by jurisdiction
