# 2C-I (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Iodophenethylamine)

## Chemical Identity

2C-I is a synthetic phenethylamine and a member of the 2C family of [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] binding studies.

## Chemical Extensions

### 2CI-2EtO (Iodotweetio)

One homologue of 2C-I is known: the 2-ethoxy analog (2-ethoxy-4-iodo-5-methoxyphenethylamine,
or 2CI-2EtO). The hydrochloride salt is a white crystalline product with a melting point of
175-175.5°C. The threshold of activity was observed at 5 mg orally, with effects dispersing
within a couple of hours. Unusually, larger doses (up to 50 mg) did not produce more intense
effects but simply extended the threshold duration by an additional couple of hours. At no tested
dose did 2CI-2EtO produce a plus-two response.

### Theoretical: 2C-A (Astatine Analog)

Shulgin speculated about the hypothetical astatine analog, 2,5-dimethoxy-4-astatophenethylamine.
Astatine is the fifth halogen, discovered in the nuclear age, and all its isotopes are
radioactive. The longest-lived isotope has a half-life of approximately 8 hours. Shulgin noted
that accumulating the necessary 10-20 mg of astatine would be virtually impossible (the maximum
ever produced at once was approximately 0.05 micrograms), and the resulting radiation would be
lethal to the user.

## Legal Status

2C-I has been scheduled as a controlled substance in many jurisdictions. In the United States, it
was placed under Schedule I of the [[shulgin-designer-drugs-analysis]]. It is also controlled in many other countries under analog drug
legislation or specific scheduling.
