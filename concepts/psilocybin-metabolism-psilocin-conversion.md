# Psilocybin Metabolism and Psilocin Conversion

Psilocybin and psilocin are the primary psychoactive compounds in
[[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]]. Understanding their pharmacokinetics —
how they are absorbed, metabolized, and act on the nervous system
— is essential for understanding both their effects and their
relative [[cannabis-safety-profile-and-overdose-risk]]. This page draws primarily on the EMCDDA
2006 thematic paper on hallucinogenic mushrooms.

## Chemical Classification

Psilocybin and psilocin belong to the indolealkylamine (tryptamine)
class of hallucinogens, the same broad chemical family as LSD, DMT,
and bufotenin. They are structurally similar to serotonin
(5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT), the neurotransmitter that regulates
mood, appetite, sleep, and cognition.

Chemical identifiers:

| Compound | IUPAC Name | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Psilocybin | 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-PO-DMT) | C12H17N2O4P |
| Psilocin | 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-HO-DMT) | C11H16N2O |

Psilocybin is the phosphate ester of psilocin. It is more stable
in air and is water-soluble. Psilocin is the pharmacologically
active compound.

## Prodrug Relationship: Psilocybin to Psilocin

Psilocybin functions as a prodrug — it is not itself psychoactive
but is converted in the body into the active compound psilocin.
This conversion occurs through dephosphorylation, primarily mediated
by alkaline phosphatase enzymes in the liver and possibly in the
digestive tract and blood.

The conversion pathway:

```
Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT)
    → alkaline phosphatase → dephosphorylation
Psilocin (4-HO-DMT) [active]
    → monoamine oxidase (MAO) → oxidative deamination
4-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (inactive metabolite)
```

The prodrug nature of psilocybin has practical implications:

- Psilocybin is more stable for storage and transport than psilocin
- The onset of effects is delayed while the body converts the
  prodrug to its active form
- Oral bioavailability is high because the phosphate group protects
  the molecule during first-pass metabolism
- Dried mushrooms containing psilocybin retain potency longer than
  those where psilocin has begun to predominate

## Minor Tryptamine Constituents

Besides psilocybin and psilocin, two additional tryptamines may be
present in hallucinogenic mushrooms:

- **Baeocystin** (4-phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine): A
  monomethylated analog of psilocybin, thought to be less active
- **Norbaeocystin** (4-phosphoryloxytryptamine): A desmethyl
  analog, also thought to be less active

The relative concentrations of these four compounds vary
significantly between species and even between individual specimens,
contributing to the unpredictable potency of [[edible-wild-mushrooms-by-region]].

## Serotonin Receptor Pharmacology

Psilocin acts primarily on the serotonin system as a 5-HT2A
post-synaptic receptor agonist (or partial agonist). This is the
same receptor target as LSD and other classical hallucinogens.

The 5-HT2A receptor mechanism:

1. Psilocin binds to 5-HT2A receptors, primarily in the cerebral
   cortex (especially the prefrontal cortex and visual processing
   areas)
2. Receptor activation triggers intracellular signaling cascades
   (phospholipase C pathway)
3. This modulates glutamate release and thalamocortical signaling
4. The result is increased cross-modal connectivity between brain
   regions that normally function independently
5. This increased connectivity produces the characteristic
   hallucinogenic effects: synesthesia, geometric visuals, altered
   sense of self, and enhanced pattern recognition

Additional receptor interactions may contribute to the overall
experience:

- **5-HT1A**: May contribute to the anxiolytic and mood-elevating
  aspects of the experience
- **5-HT2C**: May modulate anxiety and the "body load" sensation
- **Sigma-1 receptors**: Possible contribution to psychedelic
  effects, though this is less well characterized

## Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption**: Oral bioavailability of psilocybin is approximately
  50-60%. Onset of effects occurs within 10-60 minutes depending on
  preparation method and stomach contents.
- **Distribution**: Psilocin readily crosses the blood-brain barrier
  due to its lipophilic properties and structural similarity to
  serotonin.
- **Metabolism**: Primarily hepatic, via MAO-mediated oxidative
  deamination. The half-life of psilocin is approximately 2-3 hours.
- **Elimination**: Metabolites are excreted renally. The total
  duration of detectable effects is typically 4-6 hours.

## Tolerance and Cross-Tolerance

Rapid tolerance develops to psilocybin's effects — a second dose
taken within 24-48 hours will produce significantly diminished
effects. This tolerance is mediated by downregulation of 5-HT2A
receptor sensitivity. Cross-tolerance exists between psilocybin
and other 5-HT2A agonists (LSD, mescaline, DMT). Tolerance
typically resolves within 3-7 days of abstinence.

## Toxicity Profile

Psilocybin has a very high [[cannabis-therapeutic-index-overview]]. The estimated lethal
dose (LD50) in humans is extremely high relative to the active dose
— roughly 1,000 times the recreational dose. No confirmed deaths
from psilocybin toxicity alone have been documented. The primary
risks are psychological (bad trips, precipitating psychosis in
predisposed individuals) rather than physiological.
