## Dryland Strategies and Desert Ecology

## Overview

The development of conservative strategies for preserving dryland species and the responsible human use and management of arid lands is arguably the world"s most pressing problem in landscape management. Arid areas face compounding stresses: natural background fluctuations of rainfall due to earth-moon orbital cycles and solar radiation variance are now amplified by deforestation, soil erosion, and the consequent salting of waters and soils caused by human activity. All desert areas are extending, many dryland areas are being created, and antecedent plant and animal species are being driven to extinction.

## Desert Vegetation: Adaptation vs. Evasion

A critical distinction in desert ecology is between **drought-evading** and **drought-adapted** species:

### Drought-Evading Species (Most Common)

The great majority of arid-area species are not truly dry-adapted but drought-evading. Their survival strategies include:

- **Drying off and ceasing growth**: Above-ground parts senesce, leaving only seeds and tubers to survive
- **Dry-deciduous trees**: Shed leaves during drought, evading the worst effects in a leafless state
- **Seed banking**: Producing copious seed with long viability, often wind-dispersed

### Drought-Adapted Species (Minority)

Truly drought-adapted species employ structural and physiological mechanisms:

- **Wax coatings**: Reduce cuticular [[transpiration]]
- **Insulation**: Hairy or reflective leaf surfaces
- **Reduced transpiration**: Small leaves, sunken stomata, leaf rolling
- **Water storage organs**: Succulent stems, leaves, or roots (cacti, baobabs, bottle trees)

## Animal Adaptations to Aridity

Desert animals employ diverse strategies for [[desertification]] devastating to useful plant diversity:

- **Sonoran and Great Basin deserts**: Gary Nabham (1986) documented 450 edible plant species (20% of total species)
- **West Australian wheat-belt**: Of 936 woody plants, 45% are at risk from agricultural expansion
- **Cold high mountain deserts**: The Gobi has ~60 flowering plants and ~360 bird species
- **Species-poor deserts**: The Sahara has as few as 6-8 plant species in large areas; the Thar has 15-18

Existing reserves in arid lands are "entirely inadequate to conserve even a usual proportion of the genetic variation." Large reserves are urgently needed.

## Rethinking Desert Occupation

### The Failure of Pastoralism

Livestock herding has been both traditional and devastating across deserts worldwide—Australians, Peruvians, Africans, Arabs, and Tibetans have all used arid areas for herding with destructive results. Aboriginal Australians managed better by harvesting the natural abundance rather than superimposing artificial herd stress on natural drought stress.

### Income Reality Check (Central Australian Desert)

| Land Use | Area Allocation | Income Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Pastoralism | 73% | 15% |
| Tourism | — | 76% |
| Mining | Small areas | 7-8% |

Pastoralism and mining are publicly subsidized, producing largely surplus products for the world market. This represents a grossly unwarranted system of direct and indirect supports.

### The Corridor Strategy

A viable approach to desert settlement involves:

1. Establishing a carefully-developed core settlement
2. Setting out hardy plants for many kilometers along favorable areas or **corridors**
3. Taking advantage of rare rains to establish a wide biological resource for dry years

Permanent cropping or herding cannot be sustained in regions that experience one good year in every 4-9 years. The most effective and cheapest revegetation strategy is to **exclude browsing animals from headwater areas**, after which thousands of young plants may establish within a few years.

## Deserts as Design Inspiration

Despite their harshness, deserts are profoundly inspirational for designers. The hills reveal patterns, rock types, and erosion processes. As light changes from dawn to evening, new insights arise from shadowed and illuminated areas. What is unclear on the ground becomes part of a coherent pattern when seen from hills or the air. Desert peoples, in the great silences and vastness of nature, arrive at profound mystical pattern concepts that reflect sensitive reaction to environmental imperatives.
