# Brix Testing — Measuring Plant Quality and Compost Tea Response

## Overview

Brix (°Bx) is a scale measuring the soluble solids content — primarily sugars — in plant sap, fruit juice, or other liquids. Originally developed for the wine and sugar industries, Brix testing has been adopted by organic and [[ingham-aact-field-guide-index]] as one tool for assessing whether soil biology improvements are translating into improved plant quality.

## How Brix Testing Works

A Brix measurement is taken using a handheld refractometer, an inexpensive optical instrument:

1. A few drops of plant sap (squeezed from leaf tissue) or fruit juice are placed on the refractometer's glass plate
2. The instrument is held up to light and the user reads the scale where a boundary line appears
3. The reading indicates the percentage of dissolved solids (primarily sugars) in the sample
4. Most agricultural refractometers have a 0-32°Bx range

The principle is simple: light bends (refracts) differently depending on the concentration of dissolved solids in a liquid. More dissolved solids = more refraction = higher Brix reading.

## What Brix Measures

Brix is not a direct measure of any single plant compound. It reflects the total concentration of dissolved solids in sap, which includes:

- **Simple sugars**: Glucose, fructose, sucrose — the primary products of photosynthesis
- **Amino acids**: Building blocks of proteins, indicators of nitrogen metabolism
- **Minerals**: Dissolved calcium, potassium, magnesium, [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]]**: Including some [[aact-nitrogen-forms-microbial-nutrient-cycling-ingham]]
2. Complete nutrient cycling → balanced [[ingham-compost-quality-assessment-visual-microbial-indicators]] |
|---|---|
| 0-6 °Bx | Poor — likely nutrient-deficient, pest-susceptible |
| 6-10 °Bx | Average — conventional production typical range |
| 10-14 °Bx | Good — healthy plants with adequate nutrition |
| 14+ °Bx | Excellent — nutrient-dense, pest-resistant produce |

These ranges vary by crop type. Leafy greens typically have lower Brix (4-10) while fruits and root vegetables can range much higher (8-20+). The key is tracking relative changes in the same crop over time.

## Using Brix to Evaluate Compost Tea Programs

Ingham suggests using Brix as one of several tools for monitoring compost tea effectiveness:

1. **Establish baseline**: Take Brix readings from the target crop before beginning compost tea applications
2. **Apply AACT**: Follow recommended application schedules for the crop
3. **Monitor trends**: Take regular readings (weekly) from the same plant parts, at the same time of day
4. **Compare**: Compare treated vs. untreated areas, or compare readings over multiple seasons

Important: Brix readings fluctuate throughout the day. Readings are highest in late afternoon when photosynthesis has been active all day. Always take readings at consistent times for meaningful comparison.

## Crop-Specific Brix Targets

Different crops have different Brix potentials. Some general reference values for excellent-quality produce:

- **Leafy greens** (lettuce, spinach): 8-12 °Bx
- **Carrots**: 10-14 °Bx
- **Tomatoes**: 10-14 °Bx (cherry tomatoes can reach 16+)
- **Peppers**: 10-14 °Bx
- **Berries**: 12-18 °Bx
- **Melons**: 12-16 °Bx
