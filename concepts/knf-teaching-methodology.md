# KNF Teaching Methodology

updated: 2026-04-18

Dr. Cho Han Kyu's approach to teaching Korean Natural Farming reflects his philosophy that farmers themselves are the best teachers and that practical, hands-on learning is essential. His teaching methodology has been central to the global spread of KNF from its origins at the Janong Natural Farming Institute in South Korea to over fifteen countries worldwide.

## Dr. Cho's Background as Teacher

Dr. Cho developed KNF through decades of systematic inquiry, examining the best practices adopted by the farming community for three generations in South Korea and Japan. He treated enlightened farmers as his real GURUs -- his teachers. This fundamentally shaped his approach: KNF knowledge comes from observation of nature and practical farming experience, not from academic theory divorced from field reality.

His own educational background was in agriculture and veterinary technology (final year graduate student, 1965), which gave him both scientific understanding and practical farming knowledge. As a student, he had the unusual courage to boycott the special paper on Green Revolution Technologies, recognizing even then that these methods were unsuitable for Asian conditions. This independent thinking characterizes KNF's teaching approach.

## The Janong Natural Farming Institute

The primary center for KNF education is the Janong Natural Farming Institute in South Korea, where Dr. Cho developed and refined his system. The Institute serves as:

- A training center where farmers learn KNF methods hands-on
- A research facility where KNF practices are demonstrated and documented
- A demonstration farm showing KNF principles in action across crops and livestock
- An accreditation body -- the book states that "only farmers who have been educated in the institute or have been trained by Janong Natural Farming Institute in South Korea or by authorized personnel are called Natural Farmers"

This formal training requirement ensures consistency and quality in KNF practice while maintaining the system's integrity.

## Hands-On Demonstration Method

KNF teaching emphasizes practical demonstration over theoretical instruction. The book describes how training involves:

- **Live demonstrations** of input preparation (IMOs, FPJ, FAA, LAB, etc.)
- **Field application** showing proper techniques for soil treatment, foliar spraying, and compost application
- **Livestock housing tours** demonstrating the design principles of odorless piggeries and natural poultry houses
- **Direct observation** of results -- trainees see healthy crops, clean livestock housing, and fertile soils firsthand

The book's own structure reflects this teaching approach: each input is described with specific materials needed, step-by-step preparation instructions, usage guidelines, timing recommendations, and storage requirements -- a complete practical manual.

## Empowering Farmer Innovation

A distinctive feature of Dr. Cho's teaching is his insistence that farmers are free to experiment on their own. Alternative local materials should be found as ingredients for various preparations. This flexibility and scope for farmers to innovate makes the system more acceptable and accessible to farmers and is very rewarding for them indeed.

This is not a rigid system with fixed recipes. The book repeatedly notes that Korean ingredients can be substituted with locally available equivalents. The principles are universal; the specific materials are local.

## SARRA and International Training

The South Asia Rural Reconstruction Association (SARRA), Bangalore, India, played a key role in spreading KNF internationally. SARRA had the extraordinary opportunity to work with Dr. Cho Han Kyu from 2006 onwards, collaborating on:

- Organizing workshops and training programmes on Natural Farming with IMOs in India and other Asian countries
- Establishing a demonstration and training center at Pulicherla, near Tirupathi, Andhra Pradesh
- Publishing KNF documentation in regional languages (Telugu and Hindi)
- Training trainers who could carry the knowledge to farming communities

The training center at Pulicherla provided practical demonstrations of various aspects of IMO technology, serving as a model for similar centers in other countries.

## Training Structure

KNF training typically follows a progressive structure:

1. **Introduction to principles:** The Nutritive Cycle Theory, the concept of IMOs, and the philosophy of working with nature
2. **Input preparation workshops:** Hands-on learning to make each KNF input (IMO, FPJ, FFJ, OHN, FAA, LAB, WS-Ca, WS-K, WS-PA, BRV, etc.)
3. **Application training:** When, where, and how to apply inputs for different crops and growth stages
4. **Livestock management:** Housing design, feeding protocols, and health management
5. **System integration:** How to connect crops, livestock, and aquaculture into integrated farming systems
6. **Independent practice:** Farmers are encouraged to adapt methods to their local conditions

## Authorized Training Network

KNF maintains quality control through its training authorization system:

- Training is conducted at the Janong Natural Farming Institute in South Korea
- Authorized trainers conduct courses in other countries
- Trainees who complete the program are recognized as Natural Farmers
- A network of authorized trainers continues to expand as more farmers complete the program

## Documentation and Knowledge Preservation

Dr. Cho ensured his knowledge was preserved through multiple channels:

- Published books on Natural Farming methods
- Training handbooks (such as the one by Roel Revenare referenced in the book)
- Regional language publications (Telugu, Hindi editions)
- Demonstration farms that serve as living classrooms
- Standardized recipes and procedures that ensure reproducibility

## Challenges in Teaching KNF

The book candidly acknowledges obstacles faced in spreading KNF:

- **Chemical industry opposition:** Multi-national companies dominating Green Revolution Technology could not digest the challenge posed by Dr. Cho. They pressurized government authorities, and Dr. Cho was jailed several times.
- **Government resistance:** The technology appeared too subversive when first promoted, and Dr. Cho was dubbed as anti-national. Truth prevailed finally when results were too transparent for everybody to see.
- **Cultural barriers:** Teaching farmers to abandon chemical inputs requires overcoming deeply ingrained habits and beliefs
- **Scalability perception:** KNF is sometimes perceived as suitable only for small-scale operations, though the book demonstrates its applicability at any scale

## Philosophy of Teaching

Ultimately, KNF teaching embodies a philosophy of farmer empowerment. The goal is not to create dependence on experts or purchased inputs, but to give farmers the knowledge and confidence to manage their own farming systems using locally available resources. As the book states, "Dr. Cho's insistence that farmers are free to experiment on their own and alternative local materials should be found as ingredients for various preparations lends flexibility and scope for farmers to innovate."
