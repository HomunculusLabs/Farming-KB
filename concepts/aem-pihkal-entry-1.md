# AEM (alpha-Ethylmescaline)

**PIHKAL Entry #1**

## Chemical Identity

- **Full name:** alpha-Ethylmescaline; 2-Amino-1-(3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl)butane;
  1-(3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl)-2-aminobutane
- **Class:** Phenethylamine (alpha-alkylmescaline homologue)
- **Molecular formula:** C13H21NO3 (free base)
- **Hydrochloride salt:** crystalline, white
- **Isolation method:** Picrate salt (mp 176-181 °C) followed by
  liberation as HCl salt

## Dosage and Duration

- **Dosage:** Greater than 220 mg (inactive at tested levels)
- **Duration:** Unknown (never achieved threshold activity)

## Qualitative Effects

No qualitative effects were observed at 220 mg. AEM proved
completely inactive, effectively ending the exploration of the
entire alpha-alkylmescaline homologous series. The rationale for
the series was the hypothesis that extending the alpha-carbon side
chain of mescaline by one carbon at a time would double potency at
each step, as had occurred going from mescaline (2-carbon chain) to
TMA (3-carbon chain via alpha-methylation). This doubling pattern
failed immediately at AEM.

Shulgin speculated playfully that if the doubling pattern had held,
by the alpha-octyl homologue (256x mescaline potency), a single
milligram would be intoxicating. By the 70-carbon alpha-heptacontyl
analogue, "a single molecule would intoxicate." With an 80-carbon
chain, "perhaps one-thousandth of a single molecule would be enough
for a person? Or might a single molecule intoxicate a thousand
people?" The logical endpoint was that "by merely writing down the
structure on a piece of paper, you would get high. Maybe just
conceiving the structure in your mind would do it. That is, after
all, the way of homeopathy."

## Synthesis Notes

The synthesis proceeds from 3,4,5-trimethoxybenzaldehyde via
condensation with nitropropane using t-butylammonium acetate as
catalyst in IPA, held at reflux for 16 hours. The product,
2-nitro-1-(3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl)butene-1, was obtained as
slightly sticky bright yellow crystals (mp 72-73 °C after two
recrystallizations from hexane, 8.7 g yield).

Conventional ammonium acetate/acetic acid conditions failed,
producing either a white adduct C23H30N2O8 composed of one
molecule each of nitrostyrene, benzaldehyde, and ammonia, or
3,4,5-trimethoxybenzonitrile from reaction with decomposition
products of nitropropane.

Reduction was carried out with LAH in anhydrous ether (5.9 g LAH
in 310 mL ether, 6 h reflux). After careful quenching with 1.8 N
H2SO4, the amine was isolated via picrate salt formation (8.0 g,
mp 176-181 °C from water), then liberated as the hydrochloride
(4.72 g white crystals).

The nitroalkanes were prepared straightforwardly by reaction of
alkyl halides with nitrous acid, separating the unwanted nitrite
ester from the desired nitroalkane by fractional distillation.
Nitrostyrenes formed reasonably (often in poor yields) and
reduced to amines that formed crystalline picrates and
hydrochloride salts. The project was dropped once AEM proved
inactive.

Several higher homologues (AOM, ANM, AUM) were never pursued
beyond the nitrostyrene stage. AOM was never made because no
nonylbromide could be located to prepare the needed nitrononane.

## Safety and Pharmacology

No pharmacological activity was detected at 220 mg. The compound
was not pursued further. The entire alpha-alkyl series was
deprioritized based on this negative result and was never
published, as there was no reason to publish presumably inactive
and uninteresting material.

## Significance

AEM represents an important negative data point in the
structure-activity relationship (SAR) of phenethylamines. It
disproved the hypothesis of progressive potency doubling with
alpha-carbon chain extension, a logical but incorrect
extrapolation from the mescaline-to-TMA comparison. This finding
helped focus subsequent research on other structural variables
such as ring substitution patterns rather than side chain
elongation.
