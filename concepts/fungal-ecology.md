# Fungal Ecology

Fungi are the grand recyclers of the planet — keystone organisms that create soil, cycle nutrients, and govern ecological equilibrium. From *Mycelium Running* by [[paul-stamets]].

## Evolutionary Context

- Animals are more closely related to fungi than to any other kingdom (diverged ~650M years ago)
- Fungi colonized land over 1 billion years ago
- Fungi enabled plants to inhabit land ~700M years ago through symbiotic partnerships
- After mass extinctions (Permian-Triassic, Cretaceous), fungi surged to recycle debris fields
- Fungi outnumber plants at least 6:1; estimated 1-2 million species
- Only ~10% of mushroom species have been identified

## The Four Nutritional Strategies

### 1. Saprophytic (Decomposers)
Break down dead organic matter into simpler molecules. The premier recyclers.

- **Primary decomposers**: First on fresh debris. Fast-growing. Include oyster (Pleurotus), shiitake (Lentinula edodes), maitake (Grifola frondosa).
- **Secondary decomposers**: Work after primary fungi + bacteria. Include button mushroom (Agaricus bisporus), garden giant (Stropharia rugoso-annulata).
- **Tertiary decomposers**: End-stage decomposers in complex microbial soil. Include Agrocybe, Mycena, Clitocybe.

### 2. Parasitic
Feed on living hosts. Often transition to saprophytic after host death. Honey mushrooms (Armillaria) are the classic example — they can form the largest organisms on Earth (2,400 acres, 2,200+ years old).

### 3. Mycorrhizal
Symbiotic partnerships with plant roots. See [[mycorrhizal-networks]].
- **Ectomycorrhizal**: Form sheath around roots (matsutake, boletus, chanterelles)
- **Endomycorrhizal (VAM)**: Invade root cells interior

### 4. Endophytic
Live inside plant tissues without causing disease. Enhance growth and disease resistance. Still poorly understood — may be the largest and most important group.

## White Rot vs Brown Rot

| | White Rot | Brown Rot |
|---|---|---|
| **Enzymes** | Lignin peroxidase, manganese peroxidase, laccase | Primarily cellulases |
| **Effect on wood** | Breaks down lignin, leaves white cellulose | Breaks down cellulose, leaves brown lignin |
| **Remediation** | Breaks H-C bonds in petroleum, PAHs, PCBs | Less effective for toxin degradation |
| **% of species** | ~93% | ~7% |
| **Examples** | Oyster, turkey tail, reishi, maitake | Sulphur tuft, split-gill polypore, agarikon |

## Soil Building

Fungi create soil from rock and debris:
- Mycelium secretes oxalic acid and chelating enzymes that mineralize rock
- A cubic inch of topsoil contains 8+ miles of fungal cells
- Every footstep impacts 300+ miles of mycelium
- Mycelium produces **glomalin** — a polysaccharide that acts as soil glue
- Glomalin also boosts the human immune system when ingested via mushrooms
- Temperate mushrooms produce **antifreeze glycoproteins** that protect soil from freezing
- Thermogenesis from decomposition warms soil, extending growing seasons

## The Nutrient Cycle

1. Fungi break down lignin and cellulose into simple molecules
2. Byproducts: CO2 (50%+ of mass) and water (10-20%)
3. Remaining material: chitin, polysaccharides, proteins, enzymes
4. Bacteria consume rotting mycelium and fruiting bodies
5. Bacterial activity prepares soil for plant colonization
6. Plants grow, drop debris, feeding the next fungal generation
7. The cycle repeats, building ever-deeper soil

## Fungi and Bacteria

- Mycelium "sweats" antibiotics that shape microbial populations
- Each mushroom species predetermines which bacteria thrive after it fruits
- Merck survey (Suay et al. 2000): 204 mushroom species assessed for antibacterial activity
- Mycelium can selectively inhibit pathogenic bacteria while promoting beneficial ones
- Stropharia rugoso-annulata caused 100-fold drop in fecal coliforms in field trial

## The Mycelial Archetype

The branching network pattern appears throughout nature:
- Neurons in the mammalian brain
- The Internet's architecture
- Dark matter filaments in the universe
- Hurricanes and spiral galaxies
- Blood vessel networks
- River deltas

Stamets argues this is not coincidental — the mycelial form is an energy-conserving architecture that the universe tends toward.

## Ecological Succession

After catastrophe (fire, flood, clear-cut):
1. **Pioneer fungi** appear first (morels, cup fungi) — within weeks
2. Insects attracted to mushrooms bring diverse organisms
3. Birds eat mushrooms/insects, deposit seeds
4. Bacteria flourish on rotting fruiting bodies
5. Plants colonize the new soil
6. The fungal-plant partnership strengthens
7. Complex food webs develop

This is why [[paul-stamets]] calls primary decomposers "vanguard species for habitat restoration."

## Antimicrobial Properties of Mycelium

Mycelium "sweats" antibiotics that shape microbial populations. Cold-water extracts from mycelial exudates of 10 mushroom species were tested against E. coli O157:H7 and Staphylococcus aureus. The most active — [[oyster-mushroom-pleurotus-ostreatus|oyster mushroom]] (Pleurotus ostreatus), birch polypore (Piptoporus betulinus), and agarikon (Fomitopsis officinalis) — reduced CFUs by more than 99.99% in 48-72 hours. Each mushroom species predetermines which bacteria thrive after it fruits. See [[medicinal-mushrooms-guide]] for the full antimicrobial species catalog.

## Forest Blight Competition

Select saprophytic mushrooms can outcompete parasitic forest pathogens:
- **Sparassis crispa** (cauliflower mushroom) parasitizes Armillaria in vitro
- **Hypholoma capnoides/fasciculare** reduced Armillaria root rot by up to 67% in field trials
- **Trametes versicolor** (turkey tail) overgrows Armillaria mycelium
- By erecting "mycelial perimeters" of beneficial species, foresters can create species barriers against devastating parasites
