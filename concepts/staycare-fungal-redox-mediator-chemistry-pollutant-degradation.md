# Fungal Redox Mediator Chemistry in Pollutant Degradation

Redox mediators are low-molecular-weight compounds that play essential
roles in extending the catalytic reach of fungal [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]], and its production is
stimulated by low nitrogen stress. VA has a standard redox potential of
1.36 V/NHE, which places it out of the substrate range for oxidation
by laccase alone.

LiP oxidizes VA to the veratryl alcohol radical cation (VA+.), which
serves as a non-specific redox mediator. This radical cation can
oxidize a wide range of non-phenolic aromatic compounds whose redox
potentials exceed the direct oxidation capacity of the enzyme. VA
radicals thus extend both the substrate range and redox capacity of LiP.

These organic acids are common [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] of wood-rotting
basidiomycetes, secreted simultaneously with MnP. Oxalate shows unique
effects in chelating and stabilizing Mn(III).

Unchelated Mn(III) has a high standard redox potential of 1.5 V/NHE,
but this is reduced by chelation with organic acids to approximately
1.12 V/SCE (1.362 V/NHE). Mn(III)-chelates that are generated by MnP
oxidize monomeric phenols, phenolic lignin dimers, and synthetic lignin
to phenoxy radicals. The fact that MnP reactions are mediated by a
small, diffusible, and non-specific redox agent ensures its role in
pathways leading to metabolism of inaccessible pollutants.

## ABTS as Laccase Mediator

2,2-Azino-bis(3-ethylbenzthiazoline-6-sulfonate) (ABTS) is the most
widely studied laccase mediator. When oxidized by laccase, ABTS forms
a stable radical cation that extends laccase activity to non-phenolic
substrates. The laccase-ABTS couple operates through hydrogen atom
abstraction rather than single-electron oxidation, distinguishing it
from the LiP-VA system.

The laccase-ABTS couple has been demonstrated to oxidize anthracene
to anthraquinone and to oxidize benzo[a]pyrene. ABTS-mediated reactions
have been applied to dye decolorization and PAH degradation. The
stability of the ABTS radical cation contributes to its effectiveness
as a mediator over extended reaction periods.
