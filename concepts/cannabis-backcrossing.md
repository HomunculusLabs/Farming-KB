## Definition and Purpose

Backcross breeding involves crossing progeny back to one of the
original parental genotypes across multiple generations. The parent that
is repeatedly used is called the recurrent parent, while the parent
contributing the desired new trait is called the donor parent. Any time
a generation is crossed to a previous generation, it constitutes a form
of backcross breeding.

The principal goal is to create a population derived mainly from the
genetics of the recurrent parent while incorporating one or more
specific desired traits from the donor parent. This makes backcrossing
ideal for improving an already excellent variety by adding a single new
characteristic.

## Why Backcrossing Is Popular

Backcrossing has become a staple method for clandestine cannabis
breeders for several practical reasons:

- It is simple and rapid when using growrooms or greenhouses.
- It requires only small populations, suitable for space-constrained
  operations.
