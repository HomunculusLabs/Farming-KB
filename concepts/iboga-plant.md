## Botanical Description

rich in ibogaine and related compounds, and it is this part that is
harvested for ceremonial use.

The plant grows in the understory of tropical rainforests in
equatorial Africa, preferring the warm, humid conditions of the Congo
Basin. It is found in shaded locations on well-drained, acidic soils,
often as part of the complex understory vegetation of primary and
secondary rainforest.

## The Bwiti Cult

Iboga is basic to the Bwiti cult and other [[iboga-bwiti-religion]] is a syncretic spiritual tradition that
combines elements of indigenous Fang cosmology with Christian
symbolism, creating a unique religious system that has served as a
powerful force for cultural preservation in the face of colonial and
post-colonial pressures.

The religion has spread widely in Gabon and the Congo, where it
serves as a unifying cultural trait deterring the intrusion of
foreign customs from Western society. Bwiti has become one of the
dominant religious movements in Gabon, with an estimated 200,000 to
500,000 adherents.

The drug is taken in two primary ways, corresponding to different
levels of involvement:

1. **Regular ceremonial doses**: Limited amounts consumed before and 
  during ceremonies, followed by a smaller dose after midnight. These 
  regular doses allow participants to experience mild visionary states
   and maintain their spiritual connection.
2. **Initiation doses**: Excessive doses of one to three basketfuls c
  onsumed over an 8-to-24-hour period, intended to "break open the hea
  d" (dissolve the ego) and induce direct contact with the ancestors. 
  This intense experience is the central rite of passage for Bwiti ini
  tiates.

### Mythological Origin

According to Bwiti mythology: "Zame ye Mebege [the last of the
creator gods] gave us Eboka. One day he saw the Pygmy Bitamu, high in
an Atanga tree, gathering its fruit. He made him fall. He died, and
Zame brought his spirit to him. Zame cut off the little fingers and
the little toes of the cadaver of the Pygmy and planted them in
various parts of the forest. They grew into the Eboka bush."

This myth establishes iboga as a divine gift from the creator god,
linking it to the primal relationship between humans and the forest.
The association with the Pygmy (Bushmen) reflects the deep antiquity
of iboga use in Central Africa.

## Traditional Use

The root bark is rasped and eaten directly as raspings or as a
powder, or it is drunk as an infusion or cold-water extract. Iboga
enters many aspects of Bwiti's control of events. Sorcerers take the
drug to seek information from the spirit world, and leaders of the
cult may consume iboga for a full day before asking advice from
ancestors.
