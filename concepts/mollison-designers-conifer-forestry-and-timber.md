## Conifer Species and Their Characteristics

Conifers (Coniferae) are cone-bearing seed plants that dominate the
forests of cooler climates worldwide. Key genera used in forestry include
Pinus (pines), Picea (spruces), Abies (firs), Pseudotsuga (Douglas fir),
Larix (larches), Tsuga (hemlocks), and Sequoia (redwoods and sequoias).
Each genus has distinct growth characteristics, timber properties, site
requirements, and ecological roles that inform their use in permaculture
design.

Pines are the most widely planted conifers globally, valued for their
rapid growth on poor soils and their versatile timber. Douglas fir
produces some of the strongest softwood timber available and grows well
on a range of sites. Larches are unusual among conifers in being
deciduous, allowing light through their canopy in winter and producing
very durable timber. Redwood and sequoia offer exceptional durability and
dimensional stability but require specific climatic conditions. Selection
of appropriate species depends on local climate, soil conditions, intended
timber use, and integration with other system elements.
