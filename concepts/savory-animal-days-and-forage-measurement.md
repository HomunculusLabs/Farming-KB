# Animal Days and Forage Measurement

In [[savory-livestock-grazing-planning]]. The system relies
on the concept of Animal Days (AD) and Animal Days per Acre (ADA), which
provide a practical language for planning, monitoring, and adjusting
grazing operations across diverse environments and conditions.

## The Animal Day Concept

Given unfettered access to good grazing, animals will eat a predictable
amount in a day, depending on their size, weight, reproductive status, and
other factors. In feedlots and confined situations, managers easily compute
the amount of feed needed to carry a given number of animals for a given
number of days.

The common American practice of basing grazing fees on Animal Unit Months
(AUMs) parallels this relationship, but the AUM is too crude a figure for
detailed and well-planned grazing. Instead, Savory uses the Animal Day. An
Animal Day can be based on any animal type: cow, sheep, donkey, or other
stock.

You can think of an Animal Day as the forage one animal harvests in a day.
Ignoring the volume of forage present, the amount animals take from a
given paddock depends on two factors:

1. The number of animals
2. The number of days of grazing

## Computing Total Animal Days

The calculation is straightforward: multiply the number of animals by the
number of days they graze in a paddock. For example, 100 animals grazing
for 5 days equals 500 animal days. Given adequate and similar forage, an
equal number of animals will harvest the same amount in the same time
regardless of paddock size.

## Animal Days Per Acre (ADA)

Naturally, equal treatment depletes a smaller paddock more than a larger
one. To compare grazing pressure across paddocks of different sizes, divide
total animal days by the acreage. If 500 animal days are taken from an
83-acre paddock, the ADA is 6.0. If the same 500 animal days are taken
from a 250-acre paddock, the ADA is only 2.0.

ADA shows the amount of forage that each acre of land will have to render
on average. This metric enables managers to compare grazing pressure across
paddocks of varying size and to plan future operations with precision.
