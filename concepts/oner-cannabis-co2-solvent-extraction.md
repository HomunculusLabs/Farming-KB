# Cannabis CO2 and Solvent Extraction Techniques

Solvent-based extraction uses chemical solvents to dissolve cannabinoids and terpenes
from and recovery material, producing concentrated oils and other products. Oner's
guide references oil production in the context of medical cannabis and evaluates strains
for their extraction suitability.

## Overview of Solvent Extraction

While Oner's oss oeric psilocybe does not provide detailed extraction methodology, it
establishes important context about which strains produce the best extraction results
and why solvent extraction matters for medical cannabis.

### Strains Bred for Oil Production

Oner specifically identifies strains developed with extraction in mind:

- **Harlequin**: "Bred specifically to produce some high-grade hash and oils for
  medical patients." This strain (THC 2% / CBD 4%) was created with the explicit
  goal of producing concentrated medicine.
- Multiple resin-heavy strains throughout the guide are noted as ideal for "hash and
  oils" production, indicating that the breeding community targets extraction
  suitability.

### CO2 Supercritical Extraction

CO2 extraction uses pressurized carbon dioxide at supercritical temperatures to
dissolve cannabinoids and terpenes from plant material:

- CO2 is a non-toxic solvent that leaves no residual traces in the final product
- The process can be tuned (temperature and pressure) to selectively extract
  different compounds
- Supercritical CO2 produces a golden, viscous oil suitable for vaporization,
  capsules, or edibles
- This method is considered one of the cleanest forms of solvent extraction and is
  widely used in licensed medical cannabis production

### Hydrocarbon Extraction (BHO)

Butane Hash Oil (BHO) and propane extraction use light hydrocarbon gases as solvents:

- These solvents efficiently extract cannabinoids and terpenes at low temperatures,
  preserving delicate aromatic compounds
- The process produces shatter, wax, budder, and other consistency types depending
  on post-processing
- Proper purging (vacuum oven) is critical to remove all residual solvent
- This method is valued for its ability to produce very high-potency concentrates

### Ethanol Extraction

Ethanol (food-grade alcohol) extraction is another common method:

- Ethanol is a safe, widely available solvent
- It extracts a broad range of compounds including chlorophyll (which can be
  removed through winterization)
- The process is scalable and suitable for both small and large production
- Ethanol-extracted oils can be used in tinctures, topicals, and edibles

## Factors Affecting Extraction Quality

Based on Oner's strain evaluations, several factors determine extraction outcomes:

### Resin Quality and Quantity

The single most important factor is the starting material's resin profile:

- Strains described as "caked in resin," "dripping with resin," or producing buds so
  resinous they look "like someone just threw a bag of sugar at a tree" yield the
  most oil
- Trichome density directly correlates with extraction yield
- The "resin profile" (ratio of cannabinoids to terpenes) determines the quality of
  the final extract

### Terpene Preservation

comparison cannabis extraction that operate at lower temperatures preserve more terpenes:

- Oner notes that "a warm environment will bring out a rich terpene signature,"
  suggesting that terpene development during growth affects extraction quality
- Proper curing before extraction enhances terpene profiles in the final product
- organic growing methods are recommended for producing the best-flavored extracts

### Cannabinoid Ratios

Different strains produce different cannabinoid profiles that affect extraction:

- **High-THC strains** (Jack 47 at 18-24%, ChemDawg, White Diesel at 20%): Produce
  potent psychoactive extracts
- **High-CBD strains** (Harlequin at THC 2% / CBD 4%): Produce non-intoxicating
  medicinal oils
- **Balanced strains** (Jack 47 at CBD 1.6%): Produce extracts with both
  psychoactive and therapeutic properties

## Medical Applications of Extracted Oils

Oner places significant emphasis on the medical value of cannabis extracts:

- Harlequin was developed specifically so medical patients could access
  "high-grade hash and oils"
- Oil production allows patients to consume precise, measured doses of cannabinoids
- Concentrated oils provide an alternative to smoking for patients with respiratory
  concerns
- The ability to extract CBD-dominant oils (like from Harlequin) provides
  non-intoxicating therapeutic options

## Safety Considerations

While Oner's guide does not detail extraction safety, standard precautions include:

- Professional-grade solvent extraction requires proper ventilation and explosion-proof
  equipment
- CO2 extraction requires high-pressure vessels and specialized knowledge
- Residual solvent testing is essential for any solvent-based extract
- Solventless methods (water extraction, dry sift, rosin press) avoid solvent
  handling risks entirely
