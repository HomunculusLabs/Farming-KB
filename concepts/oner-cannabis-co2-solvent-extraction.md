# Cannabis CO2 and Solvent Extraction Techniques

Solvent-based extraction uses chemical solvents to dissolve cannabinoids and terpenes
from and recovery material, producing concentrated oils and other products. Oner's
guide references oil production in the context of medical cannabis and evaluates strains
for their extraction suitability.

## Overview of Solvent Extraction

While Oner's oss oeric psilocybe does not provide detailed extraction methodology, it
establishes important context about which strains produce the best extraction results
and why solvent extraction matters for medical cannabis.

### Strains Bred for Oil Production

Oner specifically identifies strains developed with extraction in mind:

- **[[chemdawg]], White Diesel at 20%): Produce
  potent psychoactive extracts
- **High-CBD strains** (Harlequin at THC 2% / CBD 4%): Produce non-intoxicating
  medicinal oils
- **Balanced strains** (Jack 47 at CBD 1.6%): Produce extracts with both
  psychoactive and therapeutic properties

## Medical Applications of Extracted Oils

Oner places significant emphasis on the medical value of cannabis extracts:

- Harlequin was developed specifically so medical patients could access
  "high-grade hash and oils"
- Oil production allows patients to consume precise, measured doses of cannabinoids
- Concentrated oils provide an alternative to smoking for patients with respiratory
  concerns
- The ability to extract CBD-dominant oils (like from Harlequin) provides
  non-intoxicating therapeutic options

## Safety Considerations

While Oner's guide does not detail extraction safety, standard precautions include:

- Professional-grade solvent extraction requires proper ventilation and explosion-proof
  equipment
- CO2 extraction requires high-pressure vessels and specialized knowledge
- Residual solvent testing is essential for any solvent-based extract
- Solventless methods (water extraction, dry sift, rosin press) avoid solvent
  handling risks entirely
