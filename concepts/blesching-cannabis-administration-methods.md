# Cannabis Administration Methods

The therapeutic efficacy of cannabis depends not only on the choice of
cannabinoids and terpenes but critically on the method of administration.
Different routes of delivery produce dramatically different pharmacokinetic
profiles, affecting onset time, duration of effects, bioavailability, and
the ratio of active compounds that reach systemic circulation. The
[[cannabis-health-index]]," [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-cannabinoid-terpene-synergy]] for how
route of delivery affects the entourage effect:

- **Onset**: 30-120 minutes, with peak effects typically at 2-3 hours
- **Bioavailability**: 4-20%, the lowest of all administration methods due
  to extensive first-pass metabolism and variable absorption
- **Duration**: 4-8 hours or longer, making this the longest-acting method
- **Advantages**: Extended duration ideal for sustained symptom relief;
  discrete and convenient; no lung exposure; precise dosing with
  standardized capsules; well-suited for baseline/around-the-clock therapy
- **Considerations**: Delayed onset increases risk of overconsumption (the
  "I didn't feel anything so I took more" problem); 11-hydroxy-THC
  metabolite is more psychoactive than THC itself, leading to stronger
  subjective effects; bioavailability varies significantly based on
  fasting state, GI health, and individual metabolism

Blesching particularly recommends oral administration for conditions
requiring sustained relief, including chronic pain, spasticity, and sleep
disorders, as explored in [[blesching-cannabis-sleep-disorders]].

## Sublingual/Tinctures

Sublingual administration involves placing cannabis tinctures or extracts
under the tongue for absorption through the oral mucosa:

- **Onset**: 15-45 minutes, faster than oral but slower than inhalation
- **Bioavailability**: 15-30%, higher than oral due to partial bypass of
  first-pass metabolism through direct absorption into the bloodstream
  via the sublingual venous plexus
- **Duration**: 3-6 hours, intermediate between inhalation and oral
- **Advantages**: Faster onset than oral with longer duration than
  inhalation; better bioavailability than oral; easy dose titration by
  counting drops; no special equipment required; discrete
- **Considerations**: Requires holding liquid under the tongue for 60-90
  seconds for optimal absorption; swallowing before absorption negates
  the sublingual advantage; taste can be unpleasant with some preparations

## Topical Application

Topical cannabis preparations (creams, salves, balms, oils) are applied
directly to the skin for localized effects:

- **Onset**: 15-60 minutes for noticeable local effects
- **Bioavailability**: Primarily local; minimal systemic absorption (though
  transdermal patches can achieve systemic delivery)
- **Duration**: 2-6 hours for local effects; longer with transdermal patches
- **Advantages**: No psychoactive effects at typical doses; ideal for
  localized pain, inflammation, and skin conditions; can be applied as
  needed without systemic effects; well-suited for
  [[blesching-cannabis-rheumatoid-arthritis]] pain
- **Considerations**: Limited to surface and near-surface tissue
  penetration; not effective for systemic conditions; efficacy depends on
  preparation quality and cannabinoid concentration

## Raw Cannabis Juice

Juicing raw, unheated cannabis provides a unique administration method
that delivers cannabinoids in their acidic (non-psychoactive) precursor
forms -- THCA and CBDA -- along with a concentrated source of terpenes
and other phytonutrients:

- **Onset**: 30-60 minutes
- **Bioavailability**: Similar to oral administration (4-20%)
- **Duration**: 4-6 hours
- **Advantages**: No psychoactivity (THCA does not activate CB1 receptors);
  delivers maximum anti-inflammatory benefits of acidic cannabinoids;
  provides concentrated nutrition including chlorophyll, vitamins, and
  minerals; high doses (multiple grams per day) are tolerable without
  intoxication
- **Considerations**: Requires access to fresh cannabis plant material;
  acidic cannabinoids have different pharmacological profiles than their
  decarboxylated forms; preparation is labor-intensive; stability and
  storage challenges

Blesching notes that raw juice is particularly valued by patients seeking
high-dose anti-inflammatory effects without any psychoactivity, making it
suitable for daytime use and patients sensitive to THC.
