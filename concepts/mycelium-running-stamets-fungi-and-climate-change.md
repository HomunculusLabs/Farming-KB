## Calcium Oxalate and Carbon Sequestration

Stamets describes how mycelia form oxalic acid crystals, which combine with calcium and other minerals to form oxalates such as calcium oxalate (CaC2O4). Notably, calcium oxalate sequesters two carbon dioxide molecules. Carbon-rich mushroom mycelia unfold into complex food webs, crumbling rocks as they grow and creating dynamic soils that support diverse populations. This mineralization process represents a direct mechanism of geological carbon sequestration driven by fungal metabolism.

## Mycoforestry as Climate Strategy

In the mycoforestry framework, Stamets recommends chipping excess wood in replanted forests and leaving chips on the forest floor rather than burning or hauling away debris. Mycoforesters spread mulch around young trees, along trails, and in mycofiltration buffer zones near watersheds to fortify forests with beneficial fungi. Where wood chips touch the ground, fungi grow into them and transport moisture with threadlike mycelium. The myceliated wood chips act like a sponge, retaining water for neighboring plants and reducing fire danger.

He argues that the current practice of burning or hauling wood debris robs biomass from the carbon cycle. The argument that wood debris fuels forest fires does not hold if debris is brought into contact with decomposing mycelium already residing in the forest floor. Moist wood does not easily burn. Chipping wood and leaving it in place may increase fire risk for the first few years, but thereafter fire potential is drastically reduced as the wood decomposes. This approach maintains carbon in the biological cycle rather than releasing it rapidly through combustion.
