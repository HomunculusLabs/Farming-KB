# Dematiaceous Fungi and Fungal Melanin

Dematiaceous fungi are a diverse ecological group characterized
by dark pigmentation in their cell walls, hyphae, conidia, or
fruiting structures. The dark pigmentation results primarily
from melanin deposition, which confers remarkable resistance to
environmental stresses including ultraviolet radiation,
desiccation, extreme temperatures, and enzymatic degradation.

## Dematiaceous Fungi in Desert Soils

Desert soils harbor characteristic assemblages of dematiaceous
fungi. Mitosporic fungi with melanized, often thick-walled
conidia (e.g., Alternaria, Ulocladium, Curvularia, Periconia,
Papulaspora species), fungi with melanized pycnidia,
dark-spored ascomycetes, and fungi producing chlamydospores,
bulbils, or sclerotia are well represented in desert soils
(Nicot 1960; Gochenaur 1970).

Thick cell walls and melanized spores and cells are considered
adaptations to the extreme conditions of arid environments.
Desert soils generally are characterized by low propagule
densities but high species diversity (Christensen 1981a;
Mouchacca 1995). In some temperate deserts and grasslands and
in some [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-in-tropical-forest-restoration]] soils, dematiaceous fungi may account
for up to 20% of total species isolated.
