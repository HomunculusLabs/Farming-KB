## Fish Farming as Factory Production

Large quantities of feed are strewn over the water at fish farming centers, creating
pollution that degrades the marine environment.

This pollution triggers outbreaks of pelagic disease — epidemics among fish populations in
the Inland Sea and similar waters.

The industry responds with large doses of chemicals to prevent disease, frightening Fukuoka
with the dangers posed to the entire marine ecosystem.

Microorganisms, plankton, and smaller species are all impacted, creating cascading effects
throughout the food chain.

This perfectly parallels agriculture's [[fungal-biodiversity-marine-ecosystems-coastal-deep-sea]] to
recover their natural productivity by removing industrial fishing and aquaculture pressures.

## The Fragility of Nature

Nature is fragile and easily harmed.

It is far more difficult to protect than everyone seems to think.

And once destroyed, it cannot be restored through human knowledge and action.

The only way forward is to relinquish human knowledge and action and allow nature to restore
its natural bounty.
