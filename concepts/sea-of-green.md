## Sea of Green (SOG)

Sea of Green (SOG) is a high-yield [[cervantes-cannabis-flower-anatomy]] production correlates directly with light interception by the canopy. By keeping plants short and packing them closely, the entire light footprint is filled with bud-producing tops. Rather than growing a few large plants over several months, SOG growers flower dozens or hundreds of small clones, each producing a single main cola. The collective yield of many small plants under optimal lighting often exceeds that of fewer large plants in the same space.

## Layout and Spacing

In a typical SOG layout, 60-80 or more plants can fit under a single 1000-watt HID lamp. Plants are grown in small containers -- three-gallon (11 L) pots, four-inch (10 cm) rockwool cubes, or three-gallon [[filter-can-design-mushroom-grow-bags]] -- arranged on flood and drain tables or simple trays. Walls should be covered with reflective material (flat white paint or Mylar) to maximize light return to the lower canopy.

Cervantes describes a practical SOG system: ten plants per tray, eight trays total (80 plants) under a single 1000-watt HID. Each week one tray of ten plants is harvested and ten new rooted clones take their place. The weaker clone in each pair is culled after two weeks. This creates a perpetual harvest cycle delivering consistent yields.

## Container and Medium Choices

- Three-gallon (11 L) pots with soilless mix (coco peat, perlite, etc.)
- Four-inch (10 cm) rockwool cubes on flood and drain tables
- Three-gallon grow bags filled with soilless medium
- Small hydroponic containers or horizontal tubes

Small flowering plants grow well in small hydroponic containers. Mother plants need larger containers -- five to ten gallons (19-38 L) -- to support their extensive root systems and heavy clone production schedules.

## Strain Selection

Indica-dominant strains work best for SOG due to their naturally compact structure and short [[what-a-plant-knows-photoperiodism-flowering-time-measurement]]. Sativa-dominant strains stretch significantly during flowering and may require earlier induction (flowering at a shorter height) to prevent plants from outgrowing the space. Uniform clones from the same mother ensure an even canopy and predictable flowering times.

## Advantages

- Faster turnover: plants reach maturity in 60-90 days from clone to harvest
- Maximum light utilization: entire light footprint filled with bud-producing canopy
- Shorter plants: no need for tall grow spaces; works in attics and low basements
- Perpetual harvest: staggered planting yields regular, predictable harvests
- Less vegetative time: minimal veg phase saves electricity and space
- Higher yield per watt: efficient use of electrical consumption
- Simplicity: short clone crops require no pruning to increase light penetration

## Limitations

- High plant count: may conflict with legal plant-count restrictions
- Requires reliable cloning operation: dependent on mother plants and clone success
- Small individual yield per plant: each plant produces a single main cola
- Labor intensive at scale: harvesting and manicuring many small plants takes time
- Clones must be uniform: inconsistent genetics create uneven canopy

## Mother Plant Requirements

A successful SOG operation depends on a healthy, productive mother plant. Cervantes emphasizes that mother plants "must have a huge root system to take in lots of nutrients to keep up with the heavy growth and clone production schedule." A mother plant grown hydroponically in its own five to ten gallon (19-38 L) container can produce "thousands of clones during her lifetime."

Mother plants should be:
- Grown from verified genetics with known growth characteristics
- Maintained under 18/6 or 24/0 light for continuous vegetative growth
- Fed a balanced vegetative nutrient regimen with adequate nitrogen
- Kept healthy and pest-free to ensure clone quality
- Pruned regularly to maintain a manageable size and promote branching
- Allowed to rest periodically if clone production is heavy, as "small, spindly branches root poorly"

## Perpetual Harvest System

Cervantes describes a practical perpetual harvest model:

- A vegetative/cloning room about a quarter the size of the flowering room
- Two clones taken every four days, one ripe female harvested every other day
- 30 flowering clones on a 91-day schedule at any given time
## Cultivation Methods

Cannabis cultivation varies by scale, environment, and end product.
Indoor methods like Sea of Green maximize yield per square foot.
Screen of Green trains plants horizontally using trellis nets.
Outdoor cultivation leverages natural sunlight but requires pest
management and weather protection. Organic living soil methods
build microbial communities supporting terpene production.

## Quality Factors

Cannabinoid and terpene profiles define quality and effects.
Light spectrum, temperature, nutrients, and harvest timing
influence secondary metabolite production. Proper drying and
curing preserves terpenes while converting THCA. Testing for
potency and contaminants ensures consumer safety.

## Emerging Research

Cannabis research has expanded significantly with legalization
trends worldwide. The endocannabinoid system mediates effects
through CB1 and CB2 receptors throughout the body. Studies
investigate therapeutic applications for chronic pain, epilepsy,
multiple sclerosis, and inflammatory conditions. Entourage effect
research examines synergistic interactions between cannabinoids
and terpenes. Genetic mapping and marker-assisted breeding
accelerate development of targeted cultivars.

Ongoing regulatory evolution continues to shape cultivation practices
and market structures across legal jurisdictions.

The SOG methodology remains one of the most space-efficient indoor cultivation approaches available.
