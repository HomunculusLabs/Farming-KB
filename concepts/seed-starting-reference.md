
# Seed Starting Reference

Starting seeds indoors extends the growing season, provides stronger transplants, and offers access to varieties unavailable as starts. This reference covers germination conditions, lighting, soil blocks, and hardening off.

## Germination Temperatures by Crop

| Peppers | 75-85 | 7-14 |
| Tomatoes | 70-80 | 5-10 |
| Eggplant | 75-85 | 7-14 |
| Basil | 65-75 | 5-10 |
| Cucumbers | 75-90 | 3-7 |
| Melons | 80-90 | 3-7 |
| Squash | 80-95 | 3-7 |
| Beans | 70-85 | 4-8 |
| Corn | 70-85 | 4-7 |

| Broccoli | 65-75 | 5-10 |

| Beets | 55-75 | 5-10 |

### Heat Mat Tips
- Remove heat mat once seeds have germinated and first true leaves appear.
- Excessive bottom heat after germination causes leggy, weak seedlings.
- Use a thermostat-controlled mat (most are 10"x20" for standard trays).
- Place mat on a waterproof surface; moisture can damage furniture and floors.

## Lighting

Seedlings need 12-16 hours of light per day once germinated. Windowsill light is insufficient in most climates — supplemental lighting is standard.

### T5 Fluorescent Tubes
- Affordable, low heat, good coverage.
- Hang 2-3 inches above seedlings; raise as plants grow.
- Use one warm (3000K) and one cool (6500K) tube per fixture for balanced spectrum.
- Good for leafy greens and early seedling stages.

### LED Grow Lights
- Higher efficiency, longer lifespan, more spectrally tunable.
- Full-spectrum white LEDs (4000-5000K) work well for seed starting.
- Hang 12-18 inches above seedlings (LEDs are more intense than fluorescents).
- Look for lights with a good PPF/PPFD rating, not just wattage.
- Dimmable fixtures allow adjustment for seedling growth stages.

### Light Schedule
- 14-16 hours on, 8-10 hours off for most seedlings.
- Use a timer — consistency matters for plant development.
- Closer light = stockier, stronger seedlings. Farther light = leggy, reaching plants.

## Seed Starting Media

### Seed Starting Mix (Store-bought)
- Light, sterile, fine-textured ([[peat-moss]] + perlite + vermiculite).
- No nutrients — seedlings feed from seed until first true leaves.
- Good for beginners; convenient.

### DIY Seed Starting Mix
- 1 part peat moss or coco coir
- 1 part perlite or vermiculite
- 1 part compost (sifted, fine)
- Wet to consistency of a wrung-out sponge.

### Soil Blocks
Eliminates plastic trays. A [[soil-block]] tool compresses wet mix into cubes; seeds are planted directly into blocks. Roots air-prune at block edges, preventing circling.

**Soil block mix ([[eliot-coleman]] recipe):**
- 3 buckets peat moss (or coco coir)
- 1 bucket perlite
- 1 bucket compost (sieved)
- 1/2 cup lime (if using peat)
- 1/4 cup [[blood-meal]]
- 1/4 cup [[rock-phosphate]]
- 1/4 cup kelp meal
- Water until the mix holds shape when squeezed.

**Block sizes:**
- 3/4 inch (mini-block) — for lettuce, herbs, small seeds. Transplant to 2-inch block.
- 2 inch (micro-block) — for most vegetable starts.
- 4 inch (maxi-block) — for larger transplants (squash, melons).

**Advantages of soil blocks:**
- No transplant shock — roots never circle.
- No plastic trays to buy, clean, or store.
- Air-pruning produces dense, fibrous root systems.
- Blocks can be potter up (placed into larger blocks) seamlessly.

## Sowing Guidelines

- Plant seeds 2-3 times their diameter deep (or follow packet instructions).
- Very fine seeds (lettuce, basil, oregano) — press into surface, do not bury.
- Large seeds (squash, beans, peas) — plant 1 inch deep.
- Label every variety immediately. Use pencil on wooden stakes or tape on trays.
- Cover trays with a humidity dome or plastic wrap until germination.
- Remove cover as soon as 50% of seeds have sprouted to prevent damping off.

## Hardening Off

Transplants grown indoors must be gradually acclimated to outdoor conditions over 7-10 days. Skipping this step causes transplant shock, sunburn, and often death.

### Hardening Off Schedule

| Day | Outdoor Time | Conditions |
|-----|-------------|------------|
| 1 | 1 hour | Shade, calm, mild |
| 2 | 2 hours | Shade, calm |
| 3 | 3 hours | Shade, light breeze |
| 4 | 3-4 hours | Partial sun |
| 5 | 4-6 hours | Partial sun, breeze |
| 6 | 6-8 hours | Full sun |
| 7 | 8-10 hours | Full sun |
| 8+ | Overnight | Full exposure |

- Start when nighttime lows are above 50°F for warm-season crops.
- Bring plants inside if temperatures drop unexpectedly.
- Reduce watering slightly during hardening off — let plants toughen up.
- Watch for wind damage — use a cold frame or sheltered spot for first few days.

## Common Problems

### Damping Off
- Fungal disease that kills seedlings at soil line.
- Caused by overwatering, poor air circulation, contaminated media.
- Prevention: use sterile media, clean trays, good airflow, chamomile tea spray.
- Once symptoms appear (pinched stems, flopped seedlings), affected plants cannot be saved.

### Leggy Seedlings
- Caused by insufficient light or excessive heat after germination.
- Solution: increase light intensity, lower light fixture, reduce temperature to 60-65°F.
- Can partially recover by burying stem up to first leaves when transplanting (tomatoes).
