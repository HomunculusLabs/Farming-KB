- Zones determine WHERE (based on frequency of use)
- Sectors determine HOW (based on directional forces)
- An element's placement must satisfy both zone and sector requirements

For example, a greenhouse needs daily attention (Zone 1) and maximum winter sun (south-facing sector). Place it in Zone 1 within the winter sun sector.

## Urban Zone Considerations

Urban designers must be especially careful:
- Don't force all five zones where they don't fit
- Recognize that Zone 5 may be a nearby park rather than on-site wilderness
- Access paths may create non-concentric zone shapes
- Apartment buildings have different zone patterns than standalone houses
- Community gardens can serve as shared Zone 1-2 for multiple households

## Drawing Zones on the Base Map

1. Start with Zone 0 (the house/structures)
2. Work outward based on actual paths of movement and areas of activity
3. Use the shapes that naturally form from topography, access, and existing features
4. Remember that zones are tools, not rigid boundaries
5. The resulting map guides element placement in the design
