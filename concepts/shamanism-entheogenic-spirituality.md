## The Individual Nature of Shamanic Practice

Unlike organized religions which operate through congregations, hierarchies, and standardized doctrines, shamanism operates on an **individual basis**. Each practitioner's journey is unique, and the teachings received through entheogenic experiences are directly opposed to societal, religious, and political norms. This individualism is both the strength and the vulnerability of shamanic practice:

- It cannot be co-opted by institutions
- It resists standardization and control
- It empowers individuals rather than authorities
- It requires personal courage and direct engagement

## Levels of Understanding

The text describes encountering different levels of understanding about entheogenic spirituality among people:

1. **Complete unawareness** — People whose paradigm cannot accommodate the concept
2. **Passing interest** — Those who find it intriguing but never revisit it
3. **Familiar resonance** — Those who sense truth in it and begin independent research
4. **Intellectual knowledge** — Those who have studied it but never experienced it directly
5. **Limited experience** — Those who have tried entheogens but only at low doses (Stage 1 effects)
6. **Moderate experience** — Those who have reached Stage 2 effects
7. **Deep experience** — The rare individuals who have reached Stage 3 — the full death and rebirth

This spectrum suggests that the shamanic knowledge, while ancient, remains accessible only to those willing to pursue it beyond intellectual understanding into direct experiential knowledge.
