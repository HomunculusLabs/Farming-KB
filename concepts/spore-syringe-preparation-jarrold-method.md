# Spore Syringe Preparation (Jarrold Method)

## Overview

Spore syringe preparation is a fundamental technique in amateur mushroom
cultivation that allows growers to create inoculant from a mature mushroom
spore print. In the Jarrold method, this process involves collecting spores
from a cap-printed specimen, suspending them in sterile water, and loading
the suspension into a sterile syringe for subsequent injection into
[[mushroom-spore-syringes]], each capable of inoculating several [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]] or boiling pot
for sterilizing equipment and water; a flat-bladed implement (such as a
sterilized scalpel, flat scraper, or inoculation loop) for dislodging
spores from the print surface; and a butane lighter or alcohol lamp for
[[jarrold-pressure-cooker-sterilization-boil-bag-mushroom-cultivation]] and sterilized at 15 psi for 20 minutes.
If a pressure cooker is not available, the wrapped syringe can be boiled in
water for at least 30 minutes, though this method is less reliable at
eliminating all contaminant organisms, particularly bacterial endospores
which can survive prolonged boiling. After sterilization, the syringe is
allowed to cool while still wrapped in foil to prevent airborne
contamination from entering through the needle tip.

## Sterile Water Preparation

The water used to create the spore suspension must also be sterile. In the
Jarrold method, a jar of distilled water is sterilized alongside the syringe
in the pressure cooker or boiling pot. The jar lid should be loosely
tightened to allow steam penetration during sterilization, then tightened
fully immediately upon removal to maintain sterility as the water cools.
The sterile water should be allowed to cool to room temperature before use,
as hot water can damage or kill [[pf-tek-spore-viability-degradation]] or
introduce unwanted organisms into the cultivation system.

## Spore Collection from the Print

Once all tools and materials are sterilized and cooled, the process of
transferring spores from the print to the sterile water begins. The spore
print jar is opened in a clean environment — ideally in a still-air box or
in front of a [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]], though a carefully cleaned and draft-free
room can suffice for experienced practitioners. A small volume of sterile
water (typically 5-10 mL) is poured from the sterile jar into the spore
print container, covering the surface of the print. The flat scraper or
scalpel is flame-sterilized until it glows red, allowed to cool for a few
seconds, and then used to gently scrape and rub the surface of the spore
print to dislodge spores into the water. The operator should wear a
breathing mask during this step to prevent exhaled contaminants from
settling on the print or in the water.

## Loading the Syringe

With the spores suspended in the sterile water, the next step is to draw the
suspension into the sterilized syringe. The foil is carefully removed from
the cooled syringe, taking care not to touch the needle tip or the barrel
opening. The syringe needle is flame-sterilized by passing it through a
butane lighter or alcohol lamp flame until it glows red, then allowed to
cool for several seconds. While still warm but no longer glowing, the needle
is inserted into the spore suspension and the plunger is slowly drawn back
to fill the syringe barrel. The needle is then flame-sterilized again before
being capped or re-sheathed to maintain sterility. The loaded syringe should
show a visible purple-brown to dark purple tint from the suspended spores —
a darker color generally indicates a higher spore concentration, which
translates to faster colonization of the target substrate.
