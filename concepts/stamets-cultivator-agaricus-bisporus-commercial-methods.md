# Button Mushroom (Agaricus bisporus) Commercial Cultivation

Agaricus bisporus (= Agaricus brunnescens), the common Button Mushroom,
is the most widely cultivated mushroom in the world and the mainstay of
the commercial mushroom industry. Historically one of the first mushrooms
cultivated in Europe during the late 1700s, it remains commercially
dominant today. The species name brunnescens refers to the flesh browning
upon bruising, while bisporus denotes its characteristic two-spored
basidia. Three main varieties exist: Brown (var. bisporus), White (var.
albidus), and Cream (var. avellaneous).

## Natural Habitat

A. bisporus is naturally found in soils enriched with dung, on compost
piles, and in horse stables. As a temperate species widely distributed
across the northern hemisphere, it fruits from May through November
outside the tropical zone. As a secondary decomposer, it fruits best on
substrates transformed by specific microbial successions, making proper
[[stamets-compost-preparation]] absolutely essential.

## Growth Parameters

### Mycelial Characteristics

Mycelium is moderately rhizomorphic, dingy white, sometimes with brownish
hues. This species does not form pinheads on agar media unless activated
charcoal or select bacteria are present, indicating its complex fruiting
requirements. See
[[stamets-mycelial-morphology-strain-evaluation]].

### Spawn Medium

Rye grain buffered with calcium carbonate and/or calcium sulfate, as
described in [[stamets-spawn-making-and-grain-culture]].

### Fruiting Substrate

Nitrogen-enriched wheat straw and/or horse manure based compost balanced
to 71-74% moisture content. The composting process, including both
[[stamets-compost-supplements-nitrogen-management]] and
[[stamets-phase-ii-composting]], is critical. Pasteurization is
achieved through exposure to live steam for 2 hours at 140 degrees F
throughout the substrate. Compost is filled to a depth of 6-12 inches.

### Spawn Run

- Relative Humidity: 90-100%
- Substrate Temperature: 76-78 degrees F
- Thermal death limit: 96 degrees F; damage occurs as low as 90 degrees F
- Duration: 2 weeks
- CO2: 5,000-10,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 0 per hour

### Casing Layer

After full colonization, cover with standard peat-based casing balanced
to pH 7.0-7.5, layered 1-2 inches deep. See
[[stamets-casing-layer-design]] for detailed
preparation. Post-casing parameters:
- Relative Humidity: 90-100%
- Bed Temperature: 76-80 degrees F
- Case Run Duration: 8-12 days
- CO2: 5,000-10,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 0 per hour

### Primordia Formation

- Relative Humidity: 95-100%
- Compost Temperature: 65-70 degrees F
- Air Temperature: 62-65 degrees F
- CO2: less than 1,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 4 per hour
- Light: None required

### Cropping Cycle

- Relative Humidity: 85-92%
- Air Temperature: 62-65 degrees F
- CO2: less than 1,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 4 per hour
- Flushing Interval: 7-10 days
- Harvest Stage: Directly before partial veil stretches
- Light: None required
- Duration: approximately 5 weeks

## Yield Potential and Nutrition

Average commercial yields are 3 lbs per square foot over a 5-week cropping
period, with maximum yields reaching 6 lbs per square foot. Mushrooms are
92% water, 8% dry matter, with 24-44% protein (dry weight) and 56 mg
niacin per 100 grams dry weight.

## Commercial Considerations

A broad range of commercially available strains exist, many genetically
selected for advantageous characteristics including yield, color, and
stature. The mating of compatible dikaryons typically results in more
vigorous and higher-yielding strains. A. bisporus is sensitive to high
CO2 levels and fruits only within narrow environmental parameters,
requiring precise control of temperature, humidity, and air exchange
in the [[stamets-mushroom-growing-room-design]].

## Genetics

Basidia are bipolar (2-spored), forming diploid spores; the species is
secondarily homothallic. Clamp connections are absent. Each spore can
evolve into fully capable mycelium that produces mushrooms. See
[[stamets-mushroom-genetics-mating-systems-reproduction]] for details on this unique reproductive strategy.
