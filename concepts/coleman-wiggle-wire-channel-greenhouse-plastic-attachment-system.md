# Wiggle-Wire Channel: Greenhouse Plastic Attachment System

## Overview

The wiggle-wire channel (also known as wire-lock channel, spring lock channel, or poly-lock channel) is the standard mechanical fastening system for attaching polyethylene greenhouse coverings to metal framework. Throughout Coleman's greenhouse designs — from the original sliding houses to the current rail-based rolling houses — the wiggle-wire channel serves as the primary method for securing plastic to the structure. Its combination of secure grip, easy installation and removal, and reusability makes it the preferred attachment system for both permanent and [[coleman-mobile-greenhouse-crop-rotation-design]] applications.

## System Components

### The Channel
- A continuous metal extrusion (usually aluminum or galvanized steel) shaped to accept the locking wire
- Mounted to the greenhouse frame at any point where plastic needs to be secured
- Available in single-channel and double-channel configurations
- Double-channel versions allow two layers of plastic to be attached at the same point (e.g., inflation layer and exterior layer)

### The Wire
- A spring-steel wire (typically galvanized) that is "wiggled" or spiraled into the channel
- The wire's spring tension forces it against the channel walls, gripping the plastic between the wire and the channel
- Can be installed and removed by hand without special tools
- Reusable across multiple seasons

### How It Works
1. The plastic film is draped over the greenhouse frame and positioned over the channel
2. The wiggle wire is inserted into the channel on top of the plastic, pressing the film into the channel groove
3. The spring tension of the wire locks the plastic firmly in place
4. To remove, the wire is pulled out by flexing it out of the channel — the plastic can then be slid out

## Applications in Coleman's Greenhouses

### Sidewall Attachment (Wheeled Houses)
On the rail-based rolling greenhouses, double wiggle-wire channel runs along the bottom of both sides of the house to attach the plastic cover. Since the greenhouse sits on wheels, there is a small gap between the bottom of the channel and the ground. This gap must be sealed in cold weather.

### End-Wall Door Attachment
The wide doorways (12 feet wide) of the wheeled houses are covered with plastic sheeting attached to pipes at both top and bottom, creating a roll-up/roll-down [[coleman-greenhouse-end-wall-ventilation-design-mobile-houses]]. The top and upper sides of this door plastic are secured with wiggle-wire channel, while the bottom edge is held with sandbags for flexibility.

### Plastic Flange System
When skinning a greenhouse on wheels, a 2-foot flange of plastic is left below the wiggle-wire channel along both sides. This flange can be:
- Weighted with sandbags (15 lbs of road gravel each) for a semi-permanent seal
- Buried in soil for a more permanent seal
- The sandbag approach allows the house to be moved without unburying plastic

### Winter Ventilation Adjustment
For winter ventilation of the wheeled houses, the wiggle wire is unclipped across the top and upper sides of one or both ends, and the plastic is dropped down to open the desired area. This is possible precisely because the wiggle-wire system allows rapid release and re-securing of the plastic.

## Advantages Over Alternative Attachment Methods

### vs. Wood Battens
- No wood deterioration or splitting over time
- No nail holes that weaken the plastic
- Faster installation and removal
- More even pressure distribution along the plastic

### vs. Screws and Clips
- No punctures in the plastic film
- Uniform grip along the entire channel length
- Easier seasonal adjustments (removing and reattaching for ventilation changes)
- Reusable without degradation

### vs. Buried Edges
- Allows rapid access for ventilation management
- Does not require trenching or soil displacement
- Suitable for mobile structures that must be moved periodically
- Can be combined with buried or sandbagged flanges for dual security

### vs. Tape or Adhesive Systems
- No adhesive degradation from UV exposure
- No residue on plastic
- Instant installation and removal
- Performs reliably in both hot and cold conditions

## Installation Considerations

### Channel Placement
- Channels should be mounted on the outermost surface of the framing to avoid plastic-to-metal contact that can cause abrasion
- On curved hoop houses, the channel follows the curve of the hoops
- At corners and transitions, the channel can be cut and mitered

### Wire Insertion Technique
- Start at one end of the channel and work along, pressing the wire in with a zigzag motion
- Ensure the plastic is smooth and taut before inserting the wire — wrinkles become permanent once locked
- Pre-stretching the plastic before locking improves the final appearance and reduces flapping
