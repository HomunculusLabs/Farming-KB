# Endophytic Fungi — Biology, Ecology, and Taxonomic Diversity

Endophytic fungi colonize living plant tissues without causing immediate, overt negative effects. They represent a vast reservoir of undiscovered fungal diversity and blur traditional boundaries between saprobes, pathogens, and mutualists.

## Definition

- Fungi capable of symptomless occupation of apparently healthy plant tissue
- Includes parasitism, commensalism, and mutualism
- Characterized by prolonged, inconspicuous periods with temporarily ceased growth
- Growth resumes after physical or maturational change in host
- Distinct from epiphytes (surface colonizers) and rhizosphere fungi (root-associated)

## Distribution

Endophytic colonization of land plants is **ubiquitous**:
- Tropical, temperate, and boreal forests
- Arctic and alpine environments
- Xeric (desert) environments
- Mosses, hepatics, liverworts, and pteridophytes
- Lichens (506 taxa from 17 lichen samples in one study)

## Ecological Roles

### Protective Mutualists (Grass Endophytes)
- Primarily Neotyphodium anamorphs of Balansiae (Clavicipitaceae)
- Colonize leaf, culm, and root tissues of cool-season grasses (Poaceae)
- Benefits to host: protection against herbivory, pathogens, drought, heat
- Increase host fitness
- Transmitted vertically through seeds
- Sporulation on host completely suppressed
- Host and fungus function as single organism
- Interspecific hybrid strains in Lolium, Festuca, and other pooid grasses

### Latent/Quiescent Pathogens
- Fungi capable of symptomless occupation during portion of infection cycle
- "Quiescent infections" resume active growth after host stress or tissue maturation
- Boundary between endophyte and latent pathogen is narrow — often just degree of virulence
- Example: Lophodermium and Coccomyces on Mahonia — anamorphs isolated from healthy leaves; teleomorphs cause disease after leaf senescence

### Saprobic Commensals
- Many endophytes appear to have no significant effect on host
- May be awaiting host tissue senescence to assume saprobic role
- Cryptic occupation of healthy tissue provides strategic position for colonization

## Taxonomic Diversity

### Foliar Endophytes of Woody Perennials
Major genera by order:
- **Leotiales:** Pezicula, Cryptosporiopsis, Phlytema, Chloroscypha, Rhabdocline
- **Dothidiales:** Hormonema, Stagonospora, Phyllosticta
- **Pleosporales:** Pleospora, Alternaria, Curvularia, Stemphyllium
- **Diaporthales:** Diaporthe, Phomopsis, Apiognomonia, Discula
- **Xylariales:** Hypoxylon, Biscogniauxia, Xylaria, Nodulisporium
- **Hypocreales:** Fusarium, Nectria, Trichoderma
- **Rhytismatales:** Lophodermium, Tryblidiopsis
- **Amphisphaeriales:** Pestalotiopsis, Seiridium

### Bark and Shoot Endophytes
Major genera:
- **Leotiales:** Mollisia, Pezicula, Tympanis, Godronia, Brunchorstia
- **Dothidiales:** Botryosphaeria, Sphaeropsis, Diplodia
- **Diaporthales:** Cytospora, Fusicoccum, Phomopsis
- **Hypocreales:** Fusarium, Nectria, Trichoderma, Gliocladium
- **Xylariales:** Hypoxylon, Daldinia, Rosellinia, Xylaria
- **Basidiomycetes:** Coniophora, Peniophora, Rhizoctonia, Stereum

### Xylariaceae — Ubiquitous Endophytes
- Exceptionally speciose group of endophytes
- Most diverse in tropics
- Anamorphic states commonly isolated in culture
- Teleomorphs more restricted in occurrence (may suggest false host specificity)
- Produce variety of bioactive metabolites
- Significant but unelucidated ecosystem role

## Methods for Study

### Surface Sterilization and Culture
1. Collect healthy-looking tissue
2. Surface sterilize (ethanol + bleach/H2O2 or HgCl2)
3. Cut into small pieces
4. Place on agar media
5. Incubate 18-25C
6. Isolate emerging fungal colonies

### Selective Media
- Weak media prevent overgrowth by fast-growing species
- Cyclosporin A (2-10 ppm): suppresses fast-growing filamentous fungi
- Antibiotics: suppress bacteria (chloramphenicol, streptomycin, ampicillin)
- Selective fungicides: target specific groups (dichloran, benomyl, rose bengal)

### Molecular Approaches
- ITS sequencing for phylogenetic placement of sterile isolates
- Enables identification without sporulation
- PCR amplification from plant tissue (fungal-specific primers)
- RAPD fingerprinting for connecting anamorphs and teleomorphs

## Factors Affecting Endophyte Communities

- **Tissue age:** Young vs. old tissue; senescing tissue changes endophyte composition
- **Tissue type:** Leaves, bark, wood, roots each harbor distinct communities
- **Host species:** Different hosts support different endophyte assemblages
- **Season:** Temporal variation in detectable endophytes
- **Position:** Within-leaf and within-branch spatial patterns
- **Geography:** Tropical hosts generally harbor more diverse endophyte communities

## Endophytes and Global Species Diversity

Endophytes likely represent a massive proportion of undescribed fungal diversity:
- Single tropical leaf species can harbor dozens of endophyte species
- Many isolates are sterile — identifiable only by molecular methods
- Conservative estimates suggest millions of undescribed endophyte species
- Endophyte surveys have repeatedly revealed new taxa and new records
