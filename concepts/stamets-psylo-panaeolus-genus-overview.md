Common names: Copelandia Hawaiian

Cap: 1.5-3.5 cm broad, hemispheric to campanulate to convex. Light brown
at first, becoming pallid gray or nearly white with tawny center, cracking
horizontally in age. Flesh readily bruising bluish. Gills mottled grayish
black. Stem: 85-115 mm long by 1.5-3 mm, readily turning bluish when
bruised. No partial veil.

Spores: Black, 12-14 by 7-11 um, non-transparent, without granulations.
Pleurocystidia fusoid-ventricose with acute apex, 40-60 by 12-17 um.

Habitat: Dung in pastures and fields. Found in Hawaii, Louisiana, Florida,
Mexico, Brazil, Bolivia, the Philippines, and eastern Australia.

Potency: Moderately potent. One study found 0.85% psilocybin, 0.15%
psilocin, and 0.01% baeocystin. Highly variable in content.

### Panaeolus cambodginiensis (Copelandia cambodginiensis)

Cap: 1.2-2.5 cm, conic-convex to broadly convex. Nearly chocolate brown,
fading to yellowish brown with olive greenish gray tones. Rapidly bruising
bluish. Stem: 5-95 mm by 3.5-5 mm, bruising bluish when injured.

Spores: Blackish brown, 10.5-12 by 6.5-9 um, lemon shaped, with central
germ pore. Pleurocystidia fusoid-ventricose with sharp apex, 48-60 by
13-19 um.

Habitat: Dung of water buffalo. Originally from Cambodia, widespread
through Asian subtropics. Also reported from Hawaii.

Potency: Strongly bluing. Contains psilocybin (0.55%) and psilocin
(0.6%). Larger statured than P. tropicalis, smaller than P. cyanescens.

### [[panaeolus-subbalteatus]]**: One of the most common Panaeolus species.
  Known to be active, containing psilocybin and psilocin. Grows on dung
  and in well-manured grounds. Widely distributed across North America
  and temperate Europe.

## Inactive Panaeolus Species

- **Panaeolus acuminatus**: Not known to produce psilocybin or psilocin.
  Common in temperate North America and Europe.
- **Panaeolus foenisecii**: Not believed active. Dark brown (not black)
  spore print distinguishes it. Roughened spores separate it from most
  other Panaeoli. Common in grassy areas, not on dung.
- **Panaeolus antillarum**: Edible but not psilocybin active. Large
  species with hemispheric caps, 4-10 cm broad. Spores 18-22 by 11-12.5
  um. Found from northern North America through Mexico.

## Safety Note

All Panaeolus species have black spore prints, which distinguishes them
from the deadly Galerinas (rusty brown spores). No poisonous species are
known in the genus Panaeolus, making it one of the safest genera for
psilocybin mushroom hunters.
