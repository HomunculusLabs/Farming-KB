## Key Principles

1. **Plant by height**: Choose plants based on height, climbing ability, shade tolerance, and water needs
2. **Spacing by climate**: Wider spacing in dry/cool climates; closer in warm/humid (but not too close -- fungus risk)
3. **All-at-once planting** in fertile areas: walnuts (long-lived), fruit trees (shorter-lived), legumes (mulch), perennials (weed control)
4. **Total yield exceeds monoculture**: Individual plants may be smaller, but combined yield is greater with more security
5. **No rotation needed**: When many species grow together mimicking a forest, crop rotation is unnecessary
6. **Create relationships**: Arrange plants and animals so they form as many beneficial connections as possible

## Plant Selection Criteria

When choosing species for a forest garden, evaluate:
- Succession stage (pioneer, Stage 2, or Stage 3)
- Deciduous or evergreen
- Mature height and root invasiveness
- Growth rate and lifespan
- Leaf density (shade or light)
- Disease resistance and pruning sensitivity
- Climate and soil compatibility
- Spread tendency and rarity
- Yield timing and productivity duration
- Multiple products (leaves, roots, seeds, fruit)
- Self-storing ability (nuts, roots)

**Strategic plant functions**:
- **Attract predators**: Provide food/shelter to pest-eating insects
- **Sacrifice plants**: Attract pests away from main crops
- **Trap pests**: Attract and kill or trap pests
- **Provide nutrients**: Fix nitrogen, create bacteria, mulch when cut
- **Create shelter**: Prevent frost, block wind, create microclimates

## Animal Integration

Animals play key roles in a productive forest garden:

- **Chickens**: Forage fallen fruit, control pests, fertilize soil — see [[comparison-chicken-keeping-vs-duck-keeping]]
- **Geese**: Graze grass between trees
- **Bees**: Pollinate fruit trees and other crops — see beekeeping-basics|beekeeping
- **Rabbits**: In movable pens, they fertilize and mow — see [[edge-effect-in-permaculture]] — the productive zone where two ecosystems meet. The transition from forest edge to open ground creates diverse microclimates supporting sun-loving and shade-tolerant species simultaneously. [[woodlot-and-coppicing]] and [[succession-in-permaculture]] for understanding how the system matures over time.
