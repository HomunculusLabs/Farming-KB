# Plant Propagation

Methods for multiplying plants — seeds, cuttings, layering, division, grafting, and tissue culture. Based on Hartmann & Kester's *Plant Propagation: Principles and Practices* and university extension guidelines (NC State, Cornell, Oregon State).

## History of Plant Science

Modern plant propagation rests on centuries of botanical research. Key milestones:

- **Marcello Malpighi (1675)** — *Anatome Plantarum*, one of the first comprehensive plant anatomy texts with detailed microscopic drawings
- **Nehemiah Grew (1682)** — *The Anatomy of Plants*, "Father of Plant Anatomy." First known microscopic depiction of pollen. Outlined morphological differences in stems and roots; proposed that stamens are the male reproductive organs of plants
- **Stephen Hales (1727)** — *Vegetable Staticks*, pioneer of plant physiology. Studied water transport in plants using simple tools; developed models still useful today
- **Charles & Francis Darwin** — *The Power of Movement in Plants*, studied phototropic movement in *Phalaris canariensis*, setting the stage for the discovery of auxin

## Seed Propagation

Foundation method. See [[seed-starting]] for indoor techniques and [[seed-saving]] for source material.

- Direct sow: beans, peas, corn, carrots, radishes, squash — after last frost
- Indoor start: tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, onions, herbs — 6-10 weeks before last frost
- Stratification: cold-moist treatment for seeds needing winter simulation (elderberry, pawpaw, many natives). Refrigerate in damp medium 30-120 days.
- Scarification: nick or soak hard-coated seeds (locust, morning glory, lupine). Hot water soak or sandpaper.
- Seed viability: test 10 seeds on damp paper towel. >70% germination = good. Store cool/dry/dark.

## Cuttings

### Softwood Cuttings (late spring-early summer)
- From current season's new growth, still flexible
- Take 3-6" cuttings, remove lower leaves, dip in rooting hormone
- Stick in moist medium (peat/perlite 1:1), keep humidity high (humidity dome or mist)
- Bottom heat 70-75°F speeds rooting. Shade from direct sun.
- Plants: lavender, rosemary, thyme, fuchsia, hydrangea, blueberry, dogwood
- Root in 2-6 weeks

### Semi-Ripe Cuttings (midsummer-early fall)
- Current season growth that has started to firm (partially woody base)
- 4-6" with heel if possible, 2-3 node pairs
- Same technique as softwood but slightly slower rooting (3-8 weeks)
- Plants: camellia, rhododendron, holly, fig, olive, rosemary

### Hardwood Cuttings (dormant season, late fall-winter)
- Fully matured wood from previous season's growth
- 6-12" cuttings, pencil-thickness, 2-4 bud pairs
- Store in moist medium or stick directly outdoors in nursery bed
- Slower but highest success for many woody species
- Plants: fig, grape, currant, gooseberry, forsythia, willow, poplar, elderberry

## Layering

### Air Layering (spring-early summer)
1. Select healthy 1-2 year old branch
2. Make upward 1.5" cut through bark and cambium (not through wood)
3. Wedge matchstick in cut to keep open, dust with rooting hormone
4. Wrap 4-6" section with moist sphagnum, cover with plastic wrap
5. Seal both ends with tape or twist ties
6. Check in 4-8 weeks for root visibility through plastic
7. Cut below root ball, pot in shade, harden off
- Best for: magnolia, citrus, fig, lychee, rhododendron, rubber plant

### Tip Layering (mid-late summer)
- Bend growing tip to ground, bury 3-4", peg in place
- New plant at tip; sever from parent next spring
- Best for: blackberries, raspberries, trailing blackberry

### Trench Layering (dormant season)
- Bury entire parent cane in shallow trench, peg down
- New shoots emerge and root along buried stem
- Sever individual plants in fall
- Best for: gooseberries, currants, some fruit rootstocks

### Mound Layering (early spring)
- Cut plant back to 1-2" above ground
- Mound 6-8" of soil/compost over new shoots as they grow
- Roots form on buried shoots; sever and transplant in fall
- Best for: apple rootstocks, gooseberry, currant, hazelnut

## Division

Divide clumping perennials when center dies out or plant becomes crowded:

- **When**: early spring (most perennials) or early fall (irises, peonies, oriental poppies)
- **How**: dig entire clump, use sharp spade/knife to cut into sections with 3-5 buds each, replant immediately
- **Perennials**: daylily, hosta, bee balm, chrysanthemum, astilbe, sedum — divide every 3-5 years
- **Grasses**: ornamental grasses divide in early spring as growth resumes
- **Bulbs**: dig after foliage dies back, separate offsets, replant at correct depth. Works for garlic (cloves), tulips, daffodils, lilies.

## Grafting

### Whip and Tongue (late winter, dormant)
- Best for: scion and rootstock of similar diameter (1/4-3/8")
- Makes strong, rapid-healing union
- Used for: most fruit trees (apple, pear, cherry)

### Cleft Graft (late winter-early spring)
- Best for: top working existing trees, larger rootstocks
- Split rootstock, insert 2 scions with wedge cuts
- Used for: changing varieties on mature trees

### Bark Graft (spring, as bark slips)
- Best for: large-diameter rootstocks when bark is actively growing
- Insert scion between bark and wood
- Used for: pecan, walnut, top working older trees

### Budding / T-Budding (mid-late summer)
- Single bud inserted under bark flap on rootstock
- Most common commercial method for fruit trees
- High success rate, efficient use of scion wood
- Used for: peach, plum, cherry, citrus, rose, apple

### Top Working
- Grafting new varieties onto existing mature trees
- Use cleft or bark graft; 2-4 years to full production
- Best done when tree is healthy and actively growing

## Tissue Culture Basics

- **Media**: Murashige and Skoog (MS) basal salts + sucrose + agar + growth regulators (cytokinin for shoot multiplication, auxin for rooting)
- **Sterilization**: 70% ethanol (30 sec) then 10% bleach (10 min) for explant surface sterilization in laminar flow hood
- **Stages**: initiation (sterile explant on media) → multiplication (subculture every 4-6 weeks) → rooting (auxin media) → acclimatization (gradual humidity reduction)
- **Acclimatization**: transfer to potting mix under dome, gradually open over 2-3 weeks
- Best for: mass propagation of berries, bananas, orchids, potatoes (disease-free), rare plants

## Rooting Hormones

| Type | IBA Concentration | Cost | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial powder | 0.1-0.8% | $5-15 | General use, easy application |
| Commercial gel (Dip 'N Grow, Clonex) | Variable | $12-25 | Cuttings, cloning cannabis |
| Willow water (DIY) | Natural auxins | Free | Softwood cuttings, herbs |

**Willow water recipe**: cut 1 cup young willow twigs (any Salix species), chop, steep in 1 gal boiling water. Cool, use within 24-48 hours. Contains natural indolebutyric acid (IBA) and salicylic acid (anti-fungal).

## Propagation by Plant Type

| Type | Primary Methods | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Woody herbs (rosemary, thyme, sage, lavender) | Semi-ripe/hardwood cuttings, layering | Cuttings easiest; bottom heat helps |
| Soft herbs (basil, cilantro, dill) | Seed | Many bolt quickly; succession sow |
| Fruit trees | Grafting, budding, hardwood cuttings (fig) | See [[orchard-management]] |
| Berries | Hardwood cuttings, tip layering, division | See [[berry-growing]] |
| Perennials | Division, seed, softwood cuttings | Divide every 3-5 years |
| Annual vegetables | Seed (direct or transplant) | See [[seed-starting]] |

## Equipment and Supplies

- **Essential**: bypass pruners, rooting hormone, propagation trays with domes, seed starting mix, labels, spray bottle, clean cutting tools (rubbing alcohol)
- **Recommended**: heat mat with thermostat, grow lights, misting system, perlite/vermiculite, peat or coir, nursery pots (multiple sizes), shade cloth
- **Advanced**: propagation greenhouse, bottom-heat cables, mist timers, tissue culture supplies, cold frames
- **Startup cost**: $50-150 for home scale; $500+ for serious propagation setup
