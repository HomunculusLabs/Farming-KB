# Flowering Phase Cannabis

The flowering phase is the reproductive stage of cannabis growth, triggered by switching
the [[photoperiod-cannabis]] to 12 hours of light and 12 hours of darkness. During this
stage, female plants produce the resinous buds that are harvested for consumption. The
flowering stage typically lasts 6-10 weeks depending on genetics, with indica-dominant
strains finishing faster than sativa-dominant varieties.

## Inducing Flowering

Flowering is induced by changing the [[light-schedule-cannabis]] to 12/12. Plants should
be at least two months old with well-developed sexual characteristics before triggering
flowering. Visible signs of flowering -- primarily white pistil (hair) formation at nodes
-- appear within one to three weeks after the photoperiod switch. Older plants tend to
show signs sooner.

Always wait to induce flowering until after pre-flowers appear. Inducing flowering with
12 hours of uninterrupted darkness and 12 hours of light before pre-flowers develop
will stress the plant and could cause odd growth or hermaphroditism.

## The Stretch Phase

The first two to four weeks of flowering are characterized by rapid vertical growth
called "the stretch." Plants can double or triple in height during this period. This
stretch is why indoor growers must plan vertical space carefully. Indica-dominant
strains typically stretch less than sativa-dominant varieties.

## Nutrient Shift

During flowering, the plant's nutrient needs change. Nitrogen takes a backseat to
potassium, phosphorus, and calcium intake. Using a super bloom fertilizer with less
nitrogen and more potassium, phosphorus, and calcium promotes fat, heavy, dense buds.
Cannabis needs some nitrogen during flowering, but very little. With no nitrogen, buds
do not develop to their full potential. In the final weeks, many growers reduce or
eliminate nitrogen entirely. [[cannabis-nutrient-lockout-and-ph]] monitoring ensures
nutrient levels stay appropriate.

## Bud Development

After the stretch phase, plants redirect energy from vertical growth to bud formation.
Calyxes (the flower pods) swell and accumulate resin glands (trichomes). Pistils emerge
from calyxes -- initially white, they gradually darken to orange, red, or brown as
maturity approaches. Bud development proceeds from the top of the plant downward.

Female flowers initially appear near the top of the terminal bud and gradually develop on
lower branches starting at the tips and moving downward. Flowers have two small
one-quarter to one-half inch (6-12 mm) fuzzy, white hairs, called pistils that form a
"V." Pistil-packed calyxes form dense clusters or buds along stems. A cluster of buds
is often called a top or cola. The masses of calyxes develop rapidly for the first four
or five weeks, after which they grow at a slower rate. Buds put on much of their
harvest weight as they swell during the last two or three weeks of growth.

## Flowering Timeline

- **Weeks 1-2:** Stretch phase; pistils begin forming; calyxes start to swell
- **Weeks 3-4:** Bud sites become defined; resin production begins; pistils are white
- **Weeks 5-6:** Buds increase in size and density; trichomes become visible
- **Weeks 7-8:** Buds reach peak density; pistils begin darkening; trichomes turn
  from clear to milky
- **Weeks 8-10+:** Pistils darken significantly; trichomes turn amber; harvest window
  approaches

## Sinsemilla Production

Sinsemilla (Spanish for "without seed") is flowering female cannabis that has not been
fertilized by male pollen. Unpollinated female plants continue to flower until calyx
formation and resin production peak out, six to ten weeks after turning the lights to
12 hours. During this period, calyxes develop and swell along the stem, yielding more
high quality buds than pollinated, seeded flowers. Removing males virtually guarantees
that male pollen will not fertilize succulent female pistils.

## Strain-Dependent Duration

Indica/sativa crosses typically finish in six to ten weeks after inducing flowering.
Pure indica varieties may finish in 6-8 weeks. Indica crosses with dominant sativa
genetics (e.g., 'Skunk #1') may take up to ten weeks. Pure sativa varieties,
especially Thai and Asian landraces, can take four months or longer to finish blooming.

## Environmental Requirements

- **Temperature:** 65-80 degrees F (18-27 degrees C); avoid temperatures above 85
  degrees F (29 degrees C) which can reduce resin production
- **Humidity:** 40-50 percent during mid-flowering; reduce to 30-40 percent in final
  weeks to prevent bud mold
- **Dark period:** Must be total and uninterrupted -- dim light during the dark period
  prevents marijuana from blooming
- **Light:** HPS spectrum is preferred for flowering; see [[hps-grow-lights]]

## Water Needs During Flowering

Flowering marijuana uses high levels of water to carry on rapid floral formation. Irrigate
larger plants in the vegetative and flowering stages when soil is dry one-half inch
below the surface.

## Common Problems During Flowering

- **Hermaphroditism:** Light leaks or stress can cause female plants to develop male
  pollen sacs, leading to seeded buds (sinsemilla lost)
- **Bud mold (botrytis):** High humidity during late flowering causes gray mold inside
  dense buds
- **Nutrient burn:** Overfeeding during flowering can affect taste and harshen smoke
- **Nutrient deficiency:** Calcium and magnesium demands increase; see [[cal-mag-supplementation]]

## Pre-Harvest: Flushing

Ten to fourteen days before harvest, begin [[flushing-nutrients]] with plain water or a
clearing solution to remove residual fertilizers from the growing medium and plant
tissue. This improves the taste and smoothness of the final product.

## Resin Production

Trichomes (resin glands) develop on calyxes, leaves, and stems during flowering.
These tiny mushroom-shaped glands produce the cannabinoids (THC, CBD, etc.) and
terpenes that give cannabis its potency and aroma. Trichome production increases
throughout flowering, peaking near harvest. Dense resin production is a sign of plant
health and proper growing conditions.

## Male Plants During Flowering

Male plants flower too, producing pollen sacs that open to release pollen. Males must
be removed from the grow space before pollen release to prevent fertilization of
female plants. Once a male plant is identified, it should be removed or isolated
immediately. Some breeders collect and store male pollen for deliberate breeding
programs.

## Branching Changes During Flowering

Branching changes when a seedling enters the flowering stage. The vegetative plant
pattern of opposite phyllotaxy may shift as the plant redirects energy toward flower
production. Understanding this transition helps growers plan canopy management and
predict final plant structure.
