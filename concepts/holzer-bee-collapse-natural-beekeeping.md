# Holzer Bee Collapse and Natural Beekeeping

Bees are the most important insects on Earth. Should the bee die out, humans
will follow. This is not about honey and medicinal products but primarily
about their essential function in pollinating crops.

## The Bee Collapse Crisis

The scale of bee population decline worldwide is alarming:
- In the United States, several bee species are already extinct
- Many remaining species have shrunk to 4% of their former populations
- In England, beekeepers report one in four colonies die annually
- If trends continue, bees could be extinct within 10 years

## Causes of Bee Collapse

Holzer identifies human error as the primary cause, not varroa mites,
viruses, or winter freezing.

### Pesticides

The massive use of pesticides is the primary killer. Small amounts of
neonicotinoids are enough to destroy a whole bee colony. These chemicals
disturb bees' ability to communicate with one another. The neonicotinoid
bee-killer is produced by Bayer, banned in several European countries
including Germany, but exported worldwide. Holzer calls this hypocrisy and
demands a worldwide ban.

### Beekeeper Overexploitation

Many beekeepers overexploit their bees, just as industrial agriculture
overexploits land. Bees are being overused and abused, treated as honey
production machines rather than living organisms with complex needs.

### Disruption of Hive Environment

Many beekeeping practices inadvertently harm bees:
- Opening hives disturbs the fine internal balance
- Bees get stressed, becoming prone to disease
- Industrial frames made of plastic or metal do not breathe and lack
  antibacterial properties
- This leads to mould, especially in humid regions
- Beekeepers cut ventilation slots, increasing draught and temperature drop
- Bees close the openings; beekeepers reopen them, creating a cycle of
  conflict

## How Bees Naturally Maintain Health

### Propolis

Bees produce a resin called propolis to fill cracks and protect hives from
drafts. Propolis has well-known healing properties and changes the
composition of air in the hive. It strengthens the bees' immune system and
keeps the air clean, protecting against mites and diseases.

### Natural Comb Building

In nature, bees build their own combs from wax they produce themselves.
The wax has antibacterial components. The process of building keeps bees
fit and healthy. Many industrial beekeepers remove this work to focus bees
on honey production, which creates short-lived success followed by declining
health and productivity.

### Winter Survival

The last bee generation each year is especially robust. These winter bees
work through the cold season by constantly beating their wings, maintaining
the hive temperature at 25 to 27 degrees Celsius. They die in spring and
are replaced by a new generation of summer bees.

## Natural Beekeeping: Practical Guidelines

### Hive Construction

- Build hives from untreated wood that can breathe
- Use a double layer for better insulation
- No disinfection is needed; bees do this themselves
- Avoid angular internal structures that only help the beekeeper
- Ensure constant temperature; bees will create it themselves
- Place no plastic in the hive
- The entrance must not be too big, or mice, wasps, or hornets will enter

### Hive Management

- Keep bees fit and healthy by letting them do all the work themselves
- Open the hive as infrequently as possible
- Leave enough natural food (honey, not sugared water)
- The highest yield comes from having happy bees
- Ensure sufficient biodiversity in plant life for forage
- Flowers should not be more than 2km away; otherwise bees get exhausted

### Toxic Plants for Healing

Bees need access to toxic plants and herbs. They use these to make healing
honey. Plant herbs in front of the hive as a precaution against varroa
mites, at least 3 to 4 metres wide and deep. Recommended species:
- Thyme
- Marjoram
- Any plants containing essential oils

### The Herb Barrier Enhancement

Install a sloping piece of wood above the hive entrance that forces bees to
fly through the herbs before entering. This ensures their wings contact the
disinfecting essential oils. Regularly cutting the herbs back releases more
essential oils for the bees to pick up.

## Traditional Beekeeping Methods

### Russian and Ukrainian Log Hives

Holzer particularly admires traditional beekeeping in Russia and Ukraine:
1. Split a freshly felled tree trunk (about 1.2m length) in half
2. Hollow out the centre
3. Put the two halves back together
4. Poplar and willow are particularly suitable
5. Close both ends with wood pieces
6. Stand the trunk at an angle to allow moisture release
7. No artificial combs needed; bees build natural ones

This system is simple, easy to build, cheap, and bee-friendly. The thick
insulation allows constant temperature. It works in all climates because it
is self-regulating. Bees are healthy, happy, and yields are high.
