# Partnership vs Dominator Culture Models

## Overview

The partnership/dominator framework is a model of human [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]]
developed by Riane Eisler in *The Chalice and the Blade* (1987) and
adopted by [[terence-mckenna]] in *Food of the Gods* (1992) to explain the
historical suppression of sacred plant use and the rise of patriarchal
civilizations. The model proposes that human societies have oscillated
between two fundamentally different organizational patterns, and that the
triumph of the dominator model is responsible for modern alienation from
nature and suppression of ecstatic religious experience.

## The Partnership Model

### Characteristics

Partnership societies, according to Eisler, exhibit the following features:

- **Gender equality** or feminine primacy — women hold positions of
  social, religious, and economic power
- **Non-hierarchical social organization** — decisions made through
  consensus rather than coercion
- **Goddess worship** — the divine is conceived as female, associated
  with fertility, nature, and the earth
- **Peaceful coexistence** — absence of warfare as a social organizing
  principle
- **Ecological harmony** — nature viewed as alive, sacred, and worthy of
  reverence
- **Shamanic religion** — direct, experiential access to the sacred
  through plant sacraments, trance, and ecstatic practice

### Archaeological Evidence

Eisler points to Çatal Höyük in Anatolia (7500-5700 BCE) as the
best-preserved example of a [[mckenna-dominator-vs-partnership-society-model]] — extensive Goddess
figurines, no fortifications, no evidence of warfare, elaborate shrines,
and art depicting female spiritual power. It was abandoned around 6500
BCE after fires, marking what McKenna calls "the end of the age of
undefended cities."

### Connection to Sacred Plants

McKenna argues that partnership societies maintained their cohesion
through the communal use of [[ceremonial-shamanic-use-psychoactive-mushrooms]]. The ecstatic
experience produced by psilocybin:

- Dissolves ego boundaries, fostering empathy and social bonding
- Generates shared visionary experiences that reinforce group identity
- Promotes a worldview in which nature is experienced as alive and
  intelligent
- Reduces aggressive tendencies and territorial behavior

## The Dominator Model

### Characteristics

[[partnership-vs-dominator-societies]] are characterized by:

- **Male dominance** — patriarchy, patrilineal descent, male control of
  resources and reproduction
- **Hierarchical social organization** — rigid class structures with
  power concentrated at the top
- **Sky God worship** — the divine is conceived as male, remote, and
  authoritarian
- **Warfare and conquest** — military power as a primary social value
- **Nature as resource** — the natural world is viewed as raw material
  for exploitation
- **Institutional religion** — access to the sacred mediated through
  priests, dogma, and ritual rather than direct experience

### Historical Transition

The transition from partnership to dominator is associated with:

- The Indo-European migrations from the Eurasian steppes (4th-2nd
  millennium BCE)
- The domestication of the horse, which reinforced mobility, male
  dominance, and a social economy based on raiding
