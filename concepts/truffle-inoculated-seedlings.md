## Truffle Inoculated Seedlings

Truffle inoculated seedlings are young tree seedlings that have been deliberately colonized by truffle fungi (Tuber species) in a controlled nursery environment. These seedlings form the foundation of all [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]], but quality control remains essential because the seedling is the only vehicle for introducing the desired truffle fungus to the orchard site.

## Inoculation Process

### Seed Preparation

Seeds of the chosen host tree species are collected from reliable sources, stratified if required, and surface-sterilized to eliminate competing fungi. Common host species include hazelnut (Corylus avellana), English oak (Quercus robur), holm oak (Quercus ilex), and downy oak (Quercus pubescens) for European truffle species.

Seeds are germinated in sterile conditions, typically on agar media or in sterilized potting substrate. Maintaining sterility during germination is critical to prevent contamination by other fungi that could outcompete the truffle inoculant.

### Spore and Mycelium Preparation

The truffle inoculant can be prepared from fresh truffle fruiting bodies or from laboratory-maintained mycelial cultures. The traditional method uses macerated fresh truffle tissue, which contains millions of spores along with mycelial fragments.

Spore slurries are prepared by blending mature truffle tissue with sterile water and sometimes a [[truffle-cultivation-basics]] for detailed host tree [[fukuoka-textdoc-production-costs-not-coming-down]] and space requirements.
