# Seed Starting and Transplanting

Starting seeds indoors extends the growing season, allows control over growing conditions, and provides access to varieties not available as transplants. Proper technique minimizes transplant shock and produces sturdy seedlings. This connects to [[home-garden-seed-saving]] for sourcing seed and [[organic-propagation-methods]] for alternative approaches.

## Seed Starting Media

### Soil Blocks

Soil blocks are compressed cubes of seed-starting mix formed with a blocker tool. Advantages over modules:

- No containers needed -- reduces plastic waste
- Roots air-prune at block edges, preventing circling
- Blocks can be transplanted into progressively larger blocks (potting up)
- Better oxygen availability to roots

Standard mix: 1 part peat (or coco coir), 1 part perlite, 1 part compost. Add a handful of sand and lime per batch. Moisten to the consistency of putty -- squeeze should produce a few drops of water.

Block sizes: mini (3/4 inch) for small seeds, standard (2 inch) for most vegetables, large (4 inch) for larger transplants.

### Module Trays

Plug trays are convenient and widely available. Use cell sizes appropriate to the crop:

- 128-cell or 200-cell trays: lettuce, onions, herbs
- 72-cell or 50-cell trays: tomatoes, peppers, brassicas
- 36-cell trays: cucurbits, larger transplants

Fill cells loosely, tap to settle, do not compress. Sow at the recommended depth (generally 2x seed diameter).

## Germination Conditions

### Heat Mats

Most vegetable seeds germinate best at 70-85 F (21-29 C). Soil temperature matters more than air temperature:

| Crop | Optimal Germination Temp |
|------|------------------------|
| Peppers | 80-85 F |
| Tomatoes | 75-80 F |
| Eggplant | 80-85 F |
| Cucurbits | 80-90 F |
| Lettuce | 60-68 F |
| Brassicas | 65-75 F |
| Onions | 65-75 F |

Use a heat mat with a thermostat. Remove the mat once 50-70% of seeds have germinated to prevent leggy growth.

### Grow Lights

Seedlings need 12-16 hours of light per day. Windowsill light is insufficient for most species.

- **T5 fluorescent**: economical, good for flats, keep 2-4 inches above seedlings
- **LED panel**: efficient, full-spectrum options available, adjustable height
- **Distance**: keep lights 2-3 inches from seedling tops; raise as they grow

If seedlings stretch (etiolate), increase light intensity or lower the fixture.

## Seedling Care

- Water from below (bottom-watering) to prevent damping-off
- Maintain 50-70% humidity -- use a humidity dome for the first week
- Provide gentle air movement with a small fan to strengthen stems
- Fertilize with diluted liquid feed (quarter strength) once true leaves appear
- Thin to one seedling per cell -- snip extras, do not pull

## Hardening Off

Transplants grown indoors must be gradually acclimated to outdoor conditions over 7-10 days:

1. Days 1-2: Outside in shade for 1-2 hours during warm calm weather
2. Days 3-4: Outside in partial sun for 3-4 hours
3. Days 5-6: Outside in full sun for 4-6 hours, leave out overnight if frost risk is low
4. Days 7-10: Full exposure, plant out

Protect from wind and hard rain during the hardening-off period. If frost is forecast, bring indoors or cover with row fabric.

## Transplanting Technique

1. Water seedlings thoroughly 1-2 hours before transplanting
2. Prepare the planting hole -- dig wider than the root ball, add compost
3. For tomatoes, plant deeper (up to the first true leaves) to encourage adventitious roots
4. Handle seedlings by the leaves, never the stem
5. Firm soil gently around roots, eliminating air pockets
6. Water in well with a dilute kelp or mycorrhizal solution
7. Apply a light mulch to retain moisture

## Avoiding Transplant Shock

- Do not transplant during the heat of midday -- early morning or evening is best
- Match soil temperature: cold soil slows root establishment
- Disturb roots as little as possible -- soil blocks and peat pots reduce root disturbance
- Water immediately after transplanting
- Provide temporary shade (row cover or shade cloth) for 2-3 days after setting out
- Avoid fertilizing for the first week -- let roots establish before demanding nutrients

## Direct Seeding vs. Transplanting

Some crops resent transplanting and should be direct-seeded:

- Root crops: carrots, radishes, turnips, parsnips
- Taproot crops: beans, peas, corn
- Cucurbits (can be transplanted but do best direct-seeded)
