## Fungal Bioaccumulation and Biosorption of Metals

## Overview

Fungi can accumulate extraordinary concentrations of heavy [[alpha-1-3-glucan-fungal-pathogen-cell-walls]], intracellular accumulation
via transport, and extracellular precipitation.

## Biosorption: Passive Metal Binding to Cell Walls

Biosorption is the passive binding of metal ions to [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]].

### Cell Wall Binding Sites

Fungal cell walls provide abundant binding sites for metal ions:

- [[fungal-melanin-environmental-protection-stress-resistance-gadd]] contain phenolic groups with high metal affinity
- Carboxyl groups from uronic acids in cell wall polysaccharides
- Extracellular polymeric substances extend the biosorptive surface
  beyond the cell itself

### Metals Accumulated

Fungi can biosorb a wide range of metals including nickel (Ni), zinc
(Zn), silver (Ag), copper (Cu), cadmium (Cd), and lead (Pb). They are
especially efficient at accumulating metals from dilute external
concentrations, making them effective at cleaning up low-level
contamination that is difficult to address by other means (Gadd, 1993,
2000, 2001a,b; Baldrian, 2003).

## Intracellular Accumulation: Active Metal Transport

Beyond passive biosorption, living fungi can actively transport metal
ions across their plasma membranes and accumulate them intracellularly.
This energy-dependent process allows fungi to concentrate metals far
above external environmental levels. Intracellular accumulation may
serve various biological purposes including:

- Detoxification through sequestration in vacuoles
- Use of essential metals as enzyme cofactors
- Production of metal-binding peptides (metallothioneins, phytochelatins)
- Deliberate concentration for unknown metabolic reasons

## Extracellular Precipitation

Fungi can immobilize metals through extracellular precipitation,
forming [[fungal-hyphae]] serve as nucleation sites for calcite (CaCO3)
precipitation, and chitin — the major component of fungal cell walls —
is a substrate on which calcite readily nucleates (Manoli et al., 1997).
