# The Nature of Food

## Overview

Fukuoka explores the profound connection between food and the human soul,
arguing that we normally think of food only as something needed by the body
to live and grow, but its true significance extends far beyond mere nutrition.
Drawing on Buddhist philosophy, he establishes that matter and spirit are one,
and that food is the meeting point where the physical and spiritual dimensions
of human existence converge.

## The Unity of Matter and Spirit

Buddha said, "Form is emptiness and emptiness is form." Since "form" refers
to matter and "emptiness" to spirit, matter and spirit are one. Matter has
many aspects, such as color, shape, and quality, each of which affect the
spirit in many ways. Chief among the aspects of matter serving as food are
color and flavor.

## Color: The Seven Prismatic Colors

The world appears filled with the seven prismatic colors, but when combined
these become white. Viewed with detachment, all things are colorless and white.
But to one distracted, seven moods engender seven colors. Matter is spirit and
spirit is matter. Both are one.
