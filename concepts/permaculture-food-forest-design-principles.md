# Permaculture Food Forest Design Principles A food forest (forest garden) is a perennial polyculture system modeled on the structure and function of a natural forest, but designed to produce food, fuel, fiber, and medicine. Mollison's Designer's Manual and Hemenway's Gaia's Garden both provide extensive guidance on food forest design. ## The Seven Layers (Expanded) ### 1. Canopy Layer (30-50+ ft) - Large overstory trees: nut trees (walnut, chestnut, pecan, hickory, hazelnut), timber trees - Provides: shade, mulch, microclimate, wildlife habitat - Spacing: 30-50 ft between trees - Consider: Allelopathy (walnut juglone suppresses many plants) - Productive cycle: Years 10-50+ ### 2. Understory Layer (15-30 ft) - Medium-sized trees: fruit trees (apple, pear, plum, cherry, peach, persimmon, pawpaw) - The main food production layer in most food forests - Spacing: 12-25 ft between trees - Choose disease-resistant, low-maintenance varieties - Productive cycle: Years 3-10+ ### 3. Shrub Layer (5-15 ft) - Berry bushes and fruiting shrubs: blueberry, raspberry, blackberry, currant, gooseberry, elderberry, seaberry, aronia - Also: nitrogen-fixing shrubs (Siberian pea, goumi, seaberry) - Planted between and under trees - Productive cycle: Years 2-5+ ### 4. Herbaceous Layer (1-5 ft) - Perennial vegetables and herbs: asparagus, rhubarb, comfrey, artichoke, hosta, good king henry, sorrel - Also: medicinal herbs (echinacea, yarrow, calendula, mint) - Fill gaps between shrubs - Productive cycle: Years 1-3+ ### 5. Ground Cover Layer (0-1 ft) - Spreading plants: strawberry, clover, creeping thyme, mint, sweet potato, nasturtium - Living mulch — replaces wood chip mulch in established forests - Suppresses weeds, retains moisture, builds soil - Productive cycle: Year 1+ ### 6. Root Layer - Edible roots and tubers: Jerusalem artichoke, potato, sweet potato, garlic, shallots, horseradish - Root crops can be interplanted in openings between tree roots - Productive cycle: Year 1+ (annuals), Year 2+ (perennials) ### 7. Vine Layer (Vertical) - Climbing plants: grape, kiwi, passionflower, hops, hardy kiwi, climbing beans, cucumber - Use trees as living trellises, or install dedicated trellises/fences - Productive cycle: Years 2-5+ ## Design Process ### Site Assessment - Sun/shade mapping throughout the year - Soil type, depth, drainage - Existing vegetation (what to keep, remove, or work around) - Water: rainfall, springs, swales, ponds - Wind: prevailing direction, windbreaks needed - Access: paths, roads, maintenance access - Zone analysis: place food forest in appropriate zone ### Species Selection Criteria 1. **Edible productivity**: How much food does it produce? 2. **Nutritional value**: Caloric density, protein, vitamins 3. **Multiple functions**: Does it fix nitrogen? Attract pollinators? Provide mulch? Suppress weeds? 4. **Low maintenance**: Disease-resistant, self-fertile, minimal pruning 5. **Market value**: Can it generate income? 6. **Season extension**: Does it produce at different times of the year? 7. **Regional adaptation**: Is it adapted to local climate and soil? ### Succession Planning - Food forests mature over time — design for all stages - **Year 1-2**: Lots of sun, annual vegetables, herbs, ground covers establish - **Year 3-5**: Shrub layer fills in, tree canopy begins to close, herbaceous layer shifts to shade-tolerant species - **Year 5-10**: Tree canopy closes, understory becomes shade garden, some sun-loving plants are replaced - **Year 10+**: Mature forest ecosystem, self-regulating, minimal input needed ## Guild Design for Trees ### The Apple Tree Guild (Classic Example) - **Apple tree** (center — canopy/understory) - **Comfrey** (dynamic accumulator — mines potassium, mulch source) - **Daffodil/nasturtium** (pest deterrent under tree) - **Clover** (ground cover, nitrogen fixation) - **Garlic/chives** (pest deterrent, allium family) - **Yarrow** (attracts beneficial insects, dynamic accumulator) - **Seaberry or goumi** (nitrogen-fixing shrub nearby) - **Grape or kiwi** (vine on the tree) ### See Also

## Large-Scale Considerations

### 1 Acre Food Forest
- 5-8 canopy/nut trees
- 15-25 fruit trees
- 30-50 berry bushes
- 100+ perennial herbs and vegetables
- Ground cover over entire area
- Vine system along fences or on trees
- Annual vegetable patches in sunny openings (early years)
- Path system for access and harvesting

### Maintenance Through Time
- **Year 1-3**: Heavy mulching, watering, weeding, replacing failures
- **Year 3-7**: Reducing inputs, ground covers fill in, first significant harvests
- **Year 7-15**: Mature system, mostly harvesting, annual pruning, mulch top-up
- **Year 15+**: Self-regulating, minimal maintenance, full productivity

## Merged: Fuchsia Species as Food Plants
updated: 2026-04-18 Fuchsias are a genus of over 100 species native to South America and New Zealand, ranging from small shrubs to small trees up to 6m. While primarily known as ornamental plants, many fuchsia species produce edible fruit and have useful applications in permaculture systems, particularly in cool, moist climates where many fruit trees struggle. - **Shade-tolerant fruit**: One of the few fruiting plants that produces well in partial to full shade - **[[mollison-designers-apple-guild-and-cool-climate-fruit-guilds]] food**: Extends the range of edible plants into cooler, moister climates - **Container growing**: Excellent for [[sepp-holzer-composting-and-humus-building]] and mulch. In food forest systems, fuchsias occupy the ground layer in cool, shaded positions beneath taller trees.
