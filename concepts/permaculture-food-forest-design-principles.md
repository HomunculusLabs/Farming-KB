# Permaculture Food Forest Design Principles

A food forest (forest garden) is a perennial polyculture system modeled on the structure and function of a natural forest, but designed to produce food, fuel, fiber, and medicine. Mollison's Designer's Manual and Hemenway's Gaia's Garden both provide extensive guidance on food forest design.

## The Seven Layers

### 1. Canopy Layer (30-50+ ft)
Large overstory trees: nut trees (walnut, chestnut, pecan, hickory, hazelnut), timber trees. Provides shade, mulch, microclimate, and wildlife habitat. Spacing: 30–50 ft between trees. Consider allelopathy (walnut juglone suppresses many plants). Productive cycle: Years 10–50+.

### 2. Understory Layer (15-30 ft)
Medium-sized trees: fruit trees (apple, pear, plum, cherry, peach, persimmon, pawpaw). The main food production layer in most food forests. Spacing: 12–25 ft between trees. Choose disease-resistant, low-maintenance varieties. Productive cycle: Years 3–10+.

### 3. Shrub Layer (5-15 ft)
Berry bushes and fruiting shrubs: blueberry, raspberry, blackberry, currant, gooseberry, elderberry, seaberry, aronia. Also nitrogen-fixing shrubs (Siberian pea, goumi, seaberry). Planted between and under trees. Productive cycle: Years 2–5+.

### 4. Herbaceous Layer (1-5 ft)
Perennial vegetables and herbs: asparagus, rhubarb, comfrey, artichoke, hosta, good king henry, sorrel. Also medicinal herbs (echinacea, yarrow, calendula, mint). Fill gaps between shrubs. Productive cycle: Years 1–3+.

### 5. Ground Cover Layer (0-1 ft)
Spreading plants: strawberry, clover, creeping thyme, mint, sweet potato, nasturtium. Living mulch — replaces wood chip mulch in established forests. Suppresses weeds, retains moisture, builds soil. Productive cycle: Year 1+.

### 6. Root Layer
Edible roots and tubers: Jerusalem artichoke, potato, sweet potato, garlic, shallots, horseradish. Root crops can be interplanted in openings between tree roots. Productive cycle: Year 1+ (annuals), Years 2–5+ (perennials).

### 7. Vine Layer
Climbing plants trained on trees, fences, or trellises: grapes, kiwi, passionfruit, hops, climbing beans, hardy kiwi. This vertical dimension adds significant production without using additional ground space.

## Design Integration

- [[permaculture-food-forest-equivalency]]
- [[permaculture-farm-design-principles]] emphasizes observing the site through at least one full year before major plantings. Design should begin with the physical framework — access paths, water management, windbreaks — before selecting specific plant species. See [[holmgren-permaculture-twelve-design-principles-framework]] principle of "use small and slow solutions" applies here — start with a small pilot area and expand as knowledge and confidence grow. Nitrogen-fixing trees and shrubs act as nursery plants, creating shade and improving soil conditions that later support fruit and nut trees. Over 10–20 years, the system transitions from an open, herb-dominated planting to a closed-canopy forest with productive yields at every layer.

## Notable Food Forest Species

### Irish Strawberry Tree (Arbutus unedo)
An evergreen shrub or small tree native to the Mediterranean. Winter fruit ripens when few other fruits are available. Year-round [[permaculture-autumn-olive]] plantings.

### Fuchsia Species as Food Plants
A genus of over 100 species producing edible fruit, useful in cool, moist climates. Shade-tolerant fruit production extends the range of edible plants into cooler climates. In food forest systems, fuchsias occupy the ground layer in shaded positions beneath taller trees. Excellent for [[mollison-designers-chestnut-as-multipurpose-tree-crop]] for Mediterranean and warm temperate climates. Tolerates urban conditions, pollution, and poor soils. Fruits feed birds and poultry. Fallen fruits serve as chicken forage. Component of [[brown-rot-white-rot-fungi-remediation]] for disease management.
