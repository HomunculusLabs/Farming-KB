# Cho's Poultry Natural Farming Methods

Dr. Cho's [[korean-natural-farming]] includes a comprehensive poultry system that
eliminates chemical feed, artificial heating, and manual waste removal. The
system produces healthy chickens and eggs while generating fertilizer for
crops and rehabilitating the environment.

## Philosophy and Purpose

The NF poultry system contrasts sharply with industrial operations. While
conventional farming treats chickens as production tools, Natural Farming
provides them the opportunity to develop natural potentials. The system
serves four purposes:

1.  **Food and soil integration** — Poultry provide food and manure;
    agricultural by-products become chicken feed, creating
    interdependence among humans, animals, and crops.
2.  **Family harmony** — Working together to care for chickens
    strengthens family bonds.
3.  **Environmental rehabilitation** — NF poultry enriches land and
    boosts the local economy.
4.  **Self-sufficiency** — All feed and inputs are produced on-farm.

## Three Core Principles

### 1. Soil Floor, Not Concrete

The floor must be soil-based. Separating chickens from soil causes disease.
Direct soil contact allows natural behaviors (dusting, pecking) and
exposure to diverse microorganisms supporting immune function.

### 2. No Artificial Heating

Temperature is not artificially controlled. Heat from fermenting compost
provides warmth even for small chicks. Chicks exposed to natural
temperature variations develop short, dense feathers and strong disease
resistance, unlike artificially heated chicks with weak resistance.

### 3. Natural First Feed

Hatchlings receive whole brown rice grains and bamboo leaves rather than
commercial starter feed. This toughens intestines and builds robust
digestive organs from the earliest days.

## Housing Design

The NF chicken house achieves zero odor, zero emissions, and zero cleaning
through biological design:

### Roof
Galvanized zinc sheets with a skylight. Sunlight illuminates one-third of
the floor as the sun moves, naturally disinfecting different areas
throughout the day. The shade-to-sun ratio enables beneficial
microorganism growth.

### Walls
Steel wire mesh for maximum ventilation. Mesh disperses heat; continuous
air passage removes moisture. Adjustable curtains regulate airflow.

### Floor
Soil-based, carpeted with rice straw chopped to 3 cm (7 cm thick; 3 cm
for broilers). Treated with cho indigenous microorganisms collection  and cultivation, LAB, and IMO. Water sprayed 1-2 times weekly
during dry periods. The floor serves simultaneously as feed producer,
fertilizer factory, and waste treatment plant.

### Water System
PVC pipe with one hole per chicken (prevents fighting). Holes tilted
backward to wipe beaks and prevent dripping. Continuous flow ensures
fresh supply.

### Brooding Box
Three temperature zones: Room A (hot, resting), Room B (warm, feeding),
Room C (drinking). The 710 cm distance from A to C compels chicks to run
50-60 times daily. A cloth curtain between B and C simulates a mother
hen's feathers.

## Feeding System

### Brown Rice and Bamboo Leaves Protocol
-   Day 1-3 (layers) or Day 1 (broilers): Whole brown rice, unlimited.
-   Day 3+ (layers) or Day 2+ (broilers): Add bamboo leaves.
-   Day 50: Add rice husk, gradually increasing to 20-25% by 6 months.
-   Rice husks at 15-20% from Feb-May control laying rate; at 25%,
    maintain 65-70% laying rate for three years.

Cho argues that soft, over-nutritious commercial feed prevents chicks from
developing strong digestive organs. Undigested nutrition from weak
digestion causes the smell and disease that plague conventional operations.

### Homemade Feed
-   Any material except mineral matter can serve as feed.
-   Fresh green grass comprises approximately one-third of adult feed.
-   Floor fermented products (rich in microorganisms) make up 7-10%.
-   Feed given once daily, 2 hours before sunset.

## Microbial Waste Management

The most remarkable feature is the approach to waste. Chicken feces are
not removed unless needed for compost. Microorganisms on the floor break
down feces continuously, resulting in zero odor, zero waste removal, and
production of valuable compost. This living floor system also generates
heat that supplements the chickens' environment during cooler months.
Chickens feed on fermented fecal products, closing the nutrient loop
entirely.
