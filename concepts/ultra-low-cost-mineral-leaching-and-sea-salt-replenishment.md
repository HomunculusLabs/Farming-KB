## Mineral Leaching and ultra low cost mineral leaching and sea salt replenishment in JADAM Farming
JADAM has coined the term "natural leaching of minerals" to describe the
inevitable loss of minerals through agriculture. The surface-layer soil where
most crop roots are located (the "plow layer," about one meter deep) gradually
loses its mineral content as fruits are harvested and nutrients are taken out.
## The Scale of Mineral Depletion
In the 1940s in the US, the saying was "an apple a day keeps the doctor away"
because an apple had all the minerals a person needed in a day. Today you would
need thirty-two apples to get the same minerals, because present-day apples
are so much poorer in minerals. All vegetables and fruits today are highly
deficient in minerals compared to the past.
There are two types of mineral loss:
- **Natural leaching:** Deprivation of nutrients because fruits take them from
  soil through normal harvesting.
- **Forced leaching:** Nutrient loss caused by removing crop residues, a practice
  promoted by conventional agriculture as "field sanitation."
## Where Leached Minerals Go
Taking a macro-view, minerals taken from soil through leaching eventually end
up in the sea. To date, 83 elements have been found present in sea water. The
mineral content of sea water is strikingly similar to that of amniotic fluid.

## Phyllite and Loess Powder
In addition to sea salt, JADAM uses phyllite powder and loess powder as mineral
inputs. Phyllite solution is applied at 500 g per 1/4 acre alongside sea salt.
These rock-based minerals provide slow-release inorganic nutrients that
microorganisms break down into plant-available forms, mimicking how nature
breaks down rock sediments below leaf mold layers in forests.
## The Importance of Nutritional Balance
[[coleman-deep-organic-farming-philosophy]] is not simply about adding good things to soil. The principle
of "the good and the bad are one" means finding balance rather than tilting
toward either extreme. Putting in nutritionally unbalanced food (like skin
fertilizers from rice bran or perilla pulp) disrupts nutrient balance.
There are two types of balance in JADAM's "underground optimization":
1. **Balance of microorganisms:** Wide variety and abundance of
   microorganisms.
2. **Balance of fertilization:** Fertilizer should be balanced in its nutrients
   to give the best food for microorganisms.
## Humus and Sugar Content (Brix)
Organic material content in soil has a deterministic effect on Brix (sugar
content). In an organic matter-rich environment, active microbial activity
produces sugar that accumulates in the soil, helping produce top quality
fruits. Humus content holds nutrients (K, Ca, Mg, Fe, Zn) tightly so that
even frequent rain cannot wash them away, making soil resistant to erosion.
## Practical Implications
- Always return crop residues to soil to prevent forced mineral leaching.
- Use whole plants as fertilizer rather than processed byproducts.
- Apply sea salt and phyllite as part of autumn soil foundation work.
- Provide ample clean organic matter to feed microorganisms.
- Use sun-dried sea salt (not purified salt) for full mineral spectrum.
## The Essential Elements Debate
JADAM challenges the claim that only eighteen elements are required for plant
growth. This conclusion was reached by "today's" [[fukuoka-critique-agricultural-science-laws-liebig]], which
is always advancing. For humans, the number of essential minerals was sixty
ten years ago and is now over seventy. As science progresses, more essential
plant nutrients will be discovered. This is why JADAM advocates providing a
broad spectrum of minerals through sea salt and rock powders rather than
targeting only the currently "known" essential elements.
