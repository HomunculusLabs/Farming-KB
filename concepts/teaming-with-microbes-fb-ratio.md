# Teaming with Microbes — The Fungal to Bacterial Ratio

In *Teaming with Microbes*, Lowenfels and Lewis present Dr. Elaine Ingham's research on the [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-bacterial-and-fungal-dominant-soil]] biomass in soils. Dr. Ingham at Oregon State University published research showing:
- The least disturbed soils (old growth timber) had far more fungi than bacteria
- Disturbed soils (rototilled soil) had far more bacteria than fungi
- The ratio of fungi to bacteria correlates with the type of plant community

## The Succession Pattern

Soil succession follows a predictable trajectory:

| Stage | F:B Ratio | Plant Type |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| Early succession | Very low (bacterial) | Weeds, annuals |
| Mid succession | ~1:1 | Grasses, vegetables, lawns |
| Late succession | 10:1+ (fungal) | Perennials, shrubs, trees, forests |

## Plant Preferences

Ingham noticed that different plants prefer soils with different F:B ratios:
- **Annuals, grasses, and vegetables** prefer bacterially dominated soils
- **Perennials, shrubs, and trees** prefer fungally dominated soils
- **Conifers and old-growth forests** require highly fungally dominated soils

healthier soils and more resilient plants. Conversely, practices that disrupt
