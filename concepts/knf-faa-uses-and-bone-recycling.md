# KNF FAA Uses and Bone Recycling

## FAA Overview

FAA is absorbed directly by crops and stimulates microorganism
activity. Blue-back colored fishes yield the highest quality FAA due
to their superior amino acid profiles. The effects of FAA are more
observable when mixed with a pinch of urea, though this is optional.

## Primary Use: Nitrogen for Vegetative Growth

FAA is rich with nitrogen and serves as an excellent fertilizer for
both soil and foliage application:
- Enhances crop growth during the vegetative growth period
- Should be used with other Natural Farming materials for best
  results
- Can be applied to soil or sprayed directly on foliage
- Standard dilution: 1:1000 with water when making IMO or mixed
  compost

## Leafy Vegetable Production

For leafy vegetables, FAA can be used continuously throughout the
growing season:
- Increases overall yield significantly
- Improves taste and fragrance of leafy greens
- Can be applied at regular intervals without the restrictions
  that apply during reproductive growth stages of fruiting crops

## IMO and Compost Activation

FAA activates microorganisms when added to other preparations:
- When making IMO or mixed compost, apply FAA after diluting with
  water at 1:1000 ratio
- The FAA helps activate the microorganisms, accelerating
  fermentation and increasing biological activity
- Particularly useful when starting new compost piles or IMO
  cultures that need a nitrogen boost

## Pest Control: Mites and Whitefly

Mackerel FAA is very effective for controlling specific agricultural
pests:
- Highly effective against mites on a wide range of crops
- Controls greenhouse whitefly (Trialeurodes vaporariorum) in
  protected cultivation
- Dilute FAA with water and spray on both sides of the leaf for
  complete coverage
- The amino acids and compounds in mackerel FAA appear to disrupt
  pest feeding behavior and reproduction

## Reproductive Growth Precaution

FAA should NOT be used during the reproductive growth period because
it may induce overgrowth. During flowering and fruiting, nitrogen-
rich inputs like FAA redirect the plant's energy away from flower
and fruit development toward additional vegetative growth. Switch to
calcium and phosphoric acid inputs during this stage instead.

## Bone Recycling into Calcium Phosphate

One of the most innovative aspects of FAA production is the recycling
of leftover bones into water-soluble calcium phosphate, creating a
valuable input from what would otherwise be waste.
