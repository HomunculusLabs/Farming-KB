# KNF FAA Uses and Bone Recycling

[[fish-amino-acid-faa]]) is a liquid made from fish that contains an
abundant amount of nutrients and various types of [[cho-fish-amino-acid-preparation]]s. Beyond
its primary role as a [[jadam-natural-nitrogen-fertilizer]], FAA has several specialized
uses in [[korean-natural-farming]], including pest control applications
and an innovative bone recycling method for producing calcium phosphate.

## FAA Overview

FAA is absorbed directly by crops and stimulates microorganism
activity. Blue-back colored fishes yield the highest quality FAA due
to their superior amino acid profiles. The effects of FAA are more
observable when mixed with a pinch of urea, though this is optional.

## Primary Use: Nitrogen for Vegetative Growth

FAA is rich with nitrogen and serves as an excellent fertilizer for
both soil and foliage application:
- Enhances crop growth during the vegetative growth period
- Should be used with other Natural Farming materials for best
  results
- Can be applied to soil or sprayed directly on foliage
- Standard dilution: 1:1000 with water when making IMO or mixed
  compost

## Leafy Vegetable Production

For leafy vegetables, FAA can be used continuously throughout the
growing season:
- Increases overall yield significantly
- Improves taste and fragrance of leafy greens
- Can be applied at regular intervals without the restrictions
  that apply during reproductive growth stages of fruiting crops

## IMO and Compost Activation

FAA activates microorganisms when added to other preparations:
- When making IMO or mixed compost, apply FAA after diluting with
  water at 1:1000 ratio
- The FAA helps activate the microorganisms, accelerating
  fermentation and increasing biological activity
- Particularly useful when starting new compost piles or IMO
  cultures that need a nitrogen boost

## Pest Control: Mites and Whitefly

Mackerel FAA is very effective for controlling specific agricultural
pests:
- Highly effective against mites on a wide range of crops
- Controls greenhouse whitefly (Trialeurodes vaporariorum) in
  protected cultivation
- Dilute FAA with water and spray on both sides of the leaf for
  complete coverage
- The amino acids and compounds in mackerel FAA appear to disrupt
  pest feeding behavior and reproduction

## Reproductive Growth Precaution

FAA should NOT be used during the reproductive growth period because
it may induce overgrowth. During flowering and fruiting, nitrogen-
rich inputs like FAA redirect the plant's energy away from flower
and fruit development toward additional vegetative growth. Switch to
calcium and phosphoric acid inputs during this stage instead.

## Bone Recycling into Calcium Phosphate

One of the most innovative aspects of FAA production is the recycling
of leftover bones into water-soluble calcium phosphate, creating a
valuable input from what would otherwise be waste.

### The Recycling Process
1. After extracting the FAA liquid from fermented fish, the bones
   remain as a byproduct.
2. Put the leftover bones into [[brown-rice-vinegar-brv]]) that has a
   volume 10 times larger than the bones.
3. The bones will decompose in the vinegar over approximately 7 days.
4. The acetic acid in BRV dissolves calcium phosphate from the bones,
   producing high-quality water-soluble calcium phosphate.
5. Small bubbles will appear during the process; when bubbling stops,
   the extraction is complete.

### Why This Works
- Calcium phosphate is insoluble in water but soluble in acids
- The acetic acid in BRV acts as a weak solvent, extracting calcium
  phosphate from the bone matrix
- This process mirrors the commercial production of WS-CaPo but uses
  waste bones from FAA production instead of fresh bones
- The resulting product is applied at 1:500 to 1:1000 dilution

### Integration with FMC
The bone-recycling method integrates perfectly with FMC production:
- Cow, pig, or fish bones from FAA production contain much calcium
  phosphate
- After burning at low temperature to remove remaining organic
  substances, bones are placed in BRV for extraction
- The resulting calcium phosphate can be added directly to FMC
  mixtures during preparation
- This creates a closed-loop system where fish waste becomes both
  FAA (nitrogen source) and WS-CaPo (phosphorus and calcium source)

## Storage Requirements

- Optimum temperature: 23 to 25 degrees C
- No direct sunlight; cool place recommended
- Store in clay pot, plastic jar, or glass jar with secure cover
- Properly sealed containers prevent odor release

## Summary of FAA Applications

FAA serves multiple functions beyond basic nitrogen fertilization:
1. Vegetative growth promotion (soil and foliar)
2. Continuous yield enhancement for leafy vegetables
3. Microorganism activation in IMO and compost production
4. Biological pest control (mites and whitefly)
5. Bone recycling into calcium phosphate fertilizer
