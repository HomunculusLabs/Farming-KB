# Holzer Extremadura Water Paradise - Sixteen Lakes in Spain

The project at Princess Nora von Liechtenstein's property in Extremadura,
Spain, demonstrates one of the most dramatic landscape transformations in
Holzer's career. What was once desert-like, degraded land with little water
was converted into a water paradise with sixteen interconnected lakes.

## The Challenge

Extremadura sits in western Spain, near the Portuguese border, in one of the
hottest and driest regions of Europe. The property suffered from severe
desertification: eroded soils, sparse vegetation, and virtually no surface
water. Conventional agriculture had failed. The land was considered essentially
useless for productive purposes.

## The Transformation

Holzer applied his core principle: water retention is 70% of the work. By
studying the contour lines and understanding how water naturally moves across
the landscape, he designed a cascading system of sixteen lakes and ponds:

- Each lake was positioned along a contour line, with overflow from higher
  lakes feeding lower ones through natural channels.
- The lakes were shaped with proper deep and shallow zones, aligned with
  prevailing winds for natural aeration (see
  [[desertification-causes-reversal]] as practiced through Holzer's methods.

## Lessons from Extremadura

The Extremadura project demonstrates several key principles of Holzer's
approach. First, water retention is the foundation of landscape restoration
in arid climates. By creating multiple water bodies at different elevations,
the entire water table of a valley can be raised. Second, construction
methods should use local, natural materials wherever possible. The lake
beds were sealed with local clay and compacted earth rather than synthetic
liners. Third, patience is essential. Ecological systems take time to
establish, and the first years after construction may show limited results
before the biological systems mature and begin self-regulating.
