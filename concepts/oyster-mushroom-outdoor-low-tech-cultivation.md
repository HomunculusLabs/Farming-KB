## Outdoor pleurotus natural [[log-cultivation-methods]]

### Wood Selection

At least 75 hardwood species can support Pleurotus cultivation. Logs should be cut from healthy, living trees during the dormant season (late fall through early spring).

**Preferred species**: Oak, poplar, alder, maple, birch, beech, wild cherry, elm, and mango. Pine, cedar, and redwood contain inhibitory resins and should be avoided.

### The MushWorld Log Method (Thailand)

1. **Log preparation**: Cut logs of suitable hardwood into manageable lengths
2. **Inoculation**: Drill holes into logs and insert spawn (plug or sawdust spawn)
3. **Incubation**: Stack logs in a shaded area under trees; cover with plastic sheet to maintain moisture. Logs need consistent moisture but not waterlogging.
4. **Fruiting**: After full colonization (several months), remove covering and increase irrigation. Mushrooms first appear at the boundary between bark and wood.
5. **Harvest**: Pick when caps reach approximately 5 cm diameter. Stop irrigation 1-2 days before harvest.
6. **Subsequent flushes**: Clean mushroom remains after harvest, maintain moist conditions. Second flush appears approximately 15 days later.
7. **Winter management**: Cover logs with plastic sheet when temperatures drop; uncover when warm weather returns.

Logs can produce multiple flushes per year for several years, though Pleurotus log cultivation is generally less productive than Shiitake log cultivation.

## Outdoor Bed Cultivation

### King Stropharia Method (Adaptable to Oysters)

While Stropharia rugoso-annulata is the premier species for outdoor bed culture, similar techniques can be adapted for oyster mushrooms on wood chips and straw:

1. Select a shaded area with good drainage
2. Prepare beds of wood chips and straw, 6-12 inches deep
3. Inoculate with grain or sawdust spawn, mixing thoroughly
4. Cover with cardboard, shade cloth, or straw to retain moisture
5. Water regularly to maintain consistent moisture
6. Fruiting occurs naturally with seasonal [[vegetable-storage-conditions-by-temperature-and-humidity]] changes

### Philippines Outdoor Method

1. Select a pest-free site under trees with wide canopy
2. Spread rice hulls and burn to ash to eliminate pests and soil-borne pathogens
3. Collect and sun-dry bedding materials (paddy straw, rice stubbles, banana leaves)
4. Bundle substrates into 12-inch lengths, 2-inch diameter bundles
5. Soak bundles for 12 hours, wash with clean water
6. Drain to 65% moisture content
7. Layer bundles into beds, sprinkling spawn thinly over each layer
8. Ideal bed: 6 layers, 3 meters long
9. Cover entire bed with plastic sheet
10. Maintain temperature at 30-35 degrees C for mycelial ramification; 28-30 degrees C for fruiting
11. First harvest appears 10-14 days after spawning

## Cardboard and Paper Substrate Cultivation

Oyster mushrooms thrive on paper products, especially cardboard, which is rich in cellulose. This method requires no heat treatment and minimal equipment:

1. Collect unprinted or minimally printed cardboard
2. Tear or cut into pieces
3. Soak in water until fully saturated (30 minutes to several hours)
4. Drain excess water
5. Layer soaked cardboard with grain or sawdust spawn in a container (plastic bag, bucket, or directly on ground)
6. Cover loosely to maintain humidity while allowing gas exchange
7. Keep in a shaded location at appropriate temperature
8. Mycelium will colonize the cardboard in 2-4 weeks
9. Initiate fruiting by increasing humidity, light, and fresh air

This method is particularly well-suited for educational programs, home cultivation, and situations where traditional substrate materials are unavailable.

## Simple Bag Methods for Low-Tech Operations

### Vietnam Hanging Bag Method

1. Prepare straw: soak in limewater (2 kg slaked lime per 100 kg dry straw) for 30 minutes
2. Pile on cement floor, cover with plastic, ferment 7-10 days, turning every 3 days
3. Fill plastic bags (20 x 30 cm) with layers of fermented straw, pressing each layer to 3-5 cm thickness
4. Final layer 5-7 cm below bag mouth
5. Place clean cotton plug in bag mouth, seal, and tie
6. Hang bags in groups of 3-5 with nylon rope
7. Incubate until fully colonized, then open bags for fruiting

### India Frame Block Method

1. Place wooden frame (60 x 45 cm) on smooth floor with jute ropes and sterilized plastic sheet underneath
2. Add 5 cm layer of boiled/cooled straw, compress with wooden lid
3. Sprinkle spawn over surface
4. Repeat for 5 layers (25-30 cm total depth)
5. Fold plastic sheet over top, tie with ropes
6. Punch 2 mm breathing holes throughout block
7. Place on shelves in single layer for incubation at 25 degrees C
8. After 12-15 days of full colonization, hang blocks (plastic removed) in humidity-controlled room
9. Pins visible approximately 9 days after opening

### South Africa Column Method

1. Mix chopped wheat straw with lime, gypsum, and water
2. Fill large polyethylene ducting cut into 2-meter columns (50 cm diameter) with metal supports
3. Each column holds approximately 50 kg of substrate
4. Punch holes for aeration and mushroom emergence
5. Hang columns vertically for colonization and fruiting

## Growing House Design

### Low-Cost Structures (Thailand Model)

falconer [[falconer-mushroom-growing-house-design]] houses for outdoor or semi-outdoor cultivation are typically:

- Constructed from thatch, bamboo, or wooden poles with shading nets
- Roof and wall materials replaced on a regular schedule (1-5 year durability)
- Designed for passive ventilation and shade
- Brick houses with steam pipes used for operations requiring in-situ pasteurization

### Essential Environmental Controls

Even low-tech operations benefit from monitoring:

- **Temperature**: Monitor with simple thermometers; adjust with shade, ventilation, and misting
- **Humidity**: Maintain above 85% during fruiting through frequent misting and water on floors
- **Ventilation**: Provide fresh air, especially during fruiting, to control CO2 levels
- **Pest control**: Fly control is critical; oyster mushrooms attract Sciarid and Phorid flies more than any other cultivated mushroom

## Advantages of Outdoor and Low-Tech Methods

- **Minimal capital investment**: No autoclave, HEPA filters, or climate-controlled rooms needed
- **Local materials**: Uses agricultural wastes readily available in the community
- **Scalable**: Can range from a single bag to large outdoor operations
- **Sustainable**: Converts waste into food and valuable by-products
- **Community appropriate**: Techniques can be learned and practiced with minimal training
- **Multiple by-products**: Spent substrate serves as animal feed, compost, soil amendment, and even nematicide

## Economic Considerations

Retail prices for fresh oyster mushrooms vary significantly by region:

- **India**: INR 30-120 (USD 0.66-2.65) per kg
- **Thailand**: THB 30-40 (USD 0.77-1.03) per kg for P. ostreatus; THB 150-200 for P. citrinopileatus
- **Thailand (bag production)**: A sawdust/rice bran bag costs approximately THB 6-8 to produce; sells for THB 100-120 per kg of mushrooms
