### Wood Selection

At least 75 hardwood species can support Pleurotus cultivation. Logs should be cut from healthy, living trees during the dormant season (late fall through early spring).

**Preferred species**: Oak, poplar, alder, maple, birch, beech, wild cherry, elm, and mango. Pine, cedar, and redwood contain inhibitory resins and should be avoided.

### The MushWorld Log Method (Thailand)

1. **Log preparation**: Cut logs of suitable hardwood into manageable lengths
2. **Inoculation**: Drill holes into logs and insert spawn (plug or sawdust spawn)
3. **Incubation**: Stack logs in a shaded area under trees; cover with plastic sheet to maintain moisture. Logs need consistent moisture but not waterlogging.
4. **Fruiting**: After full colonization (several months), remove covering and increase irrigation. Mushrooms first appear at the boundary between bark and wood.
5. **Harvest**: Pick when caps reach approximately 5 cm diameter. Stop irrigation 1-2 days before harvest.
6. **Subsequent flushes**: Clean mushroom remains after harvest, maintain moist conditions. Second flush appears approximately 15 days later.
7. **Winter management**: Cover logs with plastic sheet when temperatures drop; uncover when warm weather returns.

Logs can produce multiple flushes per year for several years, though Pleurotus log cultivation is generally less productive than Shiitake log cultivation.

## Outdoor Bed Cultivation

### King Stropharia Method (Adaptable to Oysters)

While Stropharia rugoso-annulata is the premier species for outdoor bed culture, similar techniques can be adapted for oyster mushrooms on wood chips and straw:

2. Prepare beds of wood chips and straw, 6-12 inches deep

### Essential Environmental Controls

Even low-tech operations benefit from monitoring:

- **Temperature**: Monitor with simple thermometers; adjust with shade, ventilation, and misting
- **Humidity**: Maintain above 85% during fruiting through frequent misting and water on floors
- **Ventilation**: Provide fresh air, especially during fruiting, to control CO2 levels
- **Pest control**: Fly control is critical; oyster mushrooms attract Sciarid and Phorid flies more than any other cultivated mushroom

## Advantages of Outdoor and Low-Tech Methods

- **Minimal capital investment**: No autoclave, HEPA filters, or climate-controlled rooms needed
- **Local materials**: Uses agricultural wastes readily available in the community
- **Scalable**: Can range from a single bag to large outdoor operations
- **Sustainable**: Converts waste into food and valuable by-products
- **Community appropriate**: Techniques can be learned and practiced with minimal training
- **Multiple by-products**: Spent substrate serves as animal feed, compost, soil amendment, and even nematicide
