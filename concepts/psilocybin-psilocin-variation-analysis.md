## Species and Strains Tested

The primary study focused on one Amazonian strain of *Psilocybe cubensis*
(Earle) Singer, cultivated across three replicate minicultures. Two additional
strains were also analyzed for cap-versus-stem [[edible-wild-mushrooms-by-region]] in the Pacific Northwest, USA, which motivated the present
controlled study after observing large inter-collection variability.

## Flush-by-Flush Variation Patterns

### Psilocybin

Psilocybin levels showed no consistent upward or downward trend as fruiting
progressed. Values ranged from approximately 3.2 to 13.3 mg/g dry weight across
the three minicultures. The first flushes ranged from 5.1 to 8.3 mg/g, while
later flushes remained broadly comparable. Psilocybin was consistently at least
twice the level of psilocin in every flush measured. The overall fourfold
variation under controlled conditions contrasts sharply with the tenfold
variation observed in street samples (0.7 to 6.2 mg/g).

### Psilocin

Psilocin exhibited a clear temporal [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]] flushes. It was generally
absent (zero) in the first flush, sometimes also in the second, then rose to a
maximum by the fourth flush, after which it appeared to begin declining. Across
the three minicultures, maximum psilocin levels reached 1.7 to 2.9 mg/g at the
fourth flush. This pattern suggests that psilocin biosynthesis matures with
successive flushes, while the decline in later flushes may reflect nutrient
depletion or metabolic shifts in aging mycelium.

## Cap Versus Stem Distribution

Analysis of caps and stems separately in the M.R. and Equadorian strains
revealed an uneven alkaloid distribution. Caps contained roughly twice as much
psilocybin as stems (M.R.: 6.7 vs 4.2 mg/g; Equadorian: 7.6 vs 4.7 mg/g).
Notably, the small amount of psilocin present in these strains was found
entirely in the stems. The Amazon strain showed a different pattern, with a
trace of psilocin in the cap but none in the stem, and roughly equal psilocybin
levels between cap and stem (5.7 mg/g each). This suggests strain-dependent
differences in alkaloid compartmentalization.
