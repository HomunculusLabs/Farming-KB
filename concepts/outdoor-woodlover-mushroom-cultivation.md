# Outdoor Woodlover Mushroom Cultivation

Wood-loving (lignicolous) [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] -- including [[psilocybe-azurescens]], P. cyanescens, P. subaeruginosa, P. cyanofibrillosa, and allies -- are among the most potent psychedelic mushrooms known. They cannot be reliably fruited indoors and require outdoor cultivation on wood-based substrates. This page covers the complete outdoor cultivation cycle.

## Species Overview

The "cyanescens complex" includes closely related species that grow on decaying hardwood chips and debris in temperate coastal regions:

- **P. azurescens**: Columbia River basin near Astoria, Oregon. First found 1979. Largest and most potent of all psilocybin mushrooms (up to ~25mg/g total alkaloids).
- **P. cyanescens**: Pacific Northwest, also naturalized in Europe. Very potent.
- **P. subaeruginosa**: Australia and New Zealand.
- **P. cyanofibrillosa**: Pacific Northwest.

These species fruit in cool, wet autumn conditions and form perennial mycelial networks that can produce mushrooms for decades.

## Cultivation Overview

The process follows three stages:
1. Start mycelium on [[agar]]
2. Transfer to sterilized grain, then to sterilized wood chips
3. Expand into outdoor beds with non-sterile wood chips

## Stage 1: Agar Culture and Strain Isolation

1. Prepare [[malt-extract-agar]] (4g malt extract + 2g agar per 100ml water)
2. Sterilize at 121C for 20 minutes
3. Germinate spores or clone tissue on agar
4. Incubate at 18-24C; spores germinate within a few days
5. Isolate pure mycelium by successive transfers (prefer thick rhizomorphic growth)
6. Repeat until a clean, uniform culture is obtained

Wet sterilized cardboard can be used as a selective medium if contamination problems persist.

## Stage 2: Wood Chip Spawn Preparation

### Wood Selection
- **Best species**: Beech (Fagus sylvatica), alder, hazelnut
- Use smaller chips for spawn; larger chips for outdoor beds
- Fresh cut is ideal

### Preparation
1. Soak chips in water for 48 hours (do not exceed -- fungi may start growing)
2. Drain thoroughly
3. Optional: supplement with 1/2 cup oatmeal or boiled grass seed per bag (speeds growth)
4. Load into autoclavable bags
5. Place rack in pressure cooker (prevents bags from blocking vent)
6. Sterilize at 121C for 3 full hours
7. Cool in flow cabinet

### Inoculation
1. Open cooled bags in laminar flow hood (touch only outside)
2. Pour in colonized grain spawn
3. Seal with impulse sealer; test seal
4. Label with strain and date
5. Shake to distribute spawn
6. Incubate at 20C for 3-4 weeks

### Alternative: Jars
- Fill large glass jars 2/3 full with soaked chips (60-65% moisture)
- Do not let wood sit in water
- Sterilize at 121C for 1+ hours (depends on container size)
- Inoculate with agar wedges or wood spawn
- Shake jars after a few days to redistribute
- Caps should be loose during [[sterilization]] but unscrewed slightly after inoculation for gas exchange

**Ready when**: Completely colonized with white mycelium, compact mass with characteristic mushroom odor when broken up.

## Stage 3: Outdoor Bed Preparation

### Timing
- Beds can be prepared any time except frozen ground
- Best results: prepare in spring for autumn fruiting the same year
- Beds prepared in autumn of the preceding year give excellent results
- Later preparation = more spawn needed to secure fruiting

### Site Selection
- Half-shaded location under shrubs or bushes
- Protected from direct sun and strong wind
- Natural leaf litter is beneficial

### Bed Construction

1. Dig a shallow hole 15-20 cm deep
2. Soak coarse wood chips (same species as spawn) for 24 hours, drain
3. Mix wood chip spawn with soaked chips at 1:20 ratio (higher if possible)
4. Fill hole with spawn/chip mixture
5. Water thoroughly (but do not overwater)
6. Cover with wet cardboard and perforated plastic
7. Cardboard itself serves as an additional medium for the mycelium

### Summer Maintenance
- Check moisture weekly; water if needed
- Replace plastic after watering
- Do not overwater during summer -- mycelium may grow aggressively but produce no mushrooms in autumn
- Various insects will live under the plastic; these are generally not a concern
- White mycelium should be visible on chips by mid-summer

### Fruiting (Autumn)
- Remove plastic in September
- Increase watering to once or twice weekly
- First mushrooms expected when temperatures drop to 7-10C (45-50F)
- Typically two flushes about a month apart
- Fruiting continues until temperatures drop below 0C
- A tent over the bed can prolong the fruiting season into January

### Overwintering and Perennial Maintenance
- When temperatures drop below 0C, add a layer of fresh wood chips to the top of the bed
- This provides nutrition for the following year
- Treated properly, a bed can last for decades
- Cardboard from the bed surface can be used to inoculate new beds

### Fruiting from Contaminated Spawn
Even contaminated spawn that is discarded in a garden (near a compost pile, half-shaded) can produce mushrooms. As little as 0.3 liters of spawn has been observed to produce "marvellous mushrooms."

## Comparison: Azurescens Bed Method vs. General Woodlover Method

| Feature | Azurescens Guide | General Woodlover Guide |
|---------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Spawn expansion | Jars with wood chips | Autoclavable bags |
| Sterilization time | 1+ hours (jars) | 3 hours (bags) |
| Bed depth | 15-20 cm | Shallow hole |
| Spawn ratio | 1:5 minimum | 1:20 minimum |
| Moisture | 60-65% for spawn | Soak 48 hours, drain |
| Fruit temp | 7-10C (45-50F) | Autumn temperatures |
| Cover | Wet cardboard + perforated plastic | Plastic sheet |

## P. azurescens Species Profile

Discovered 1979 by Stamets near Astoria, Oregon. Latin description published by Stamets and Gartz, 1995.

- Cap: ochraceous-brown, hygrophanous, viscid with separable pellicle, conic then convex to plane, 30-100mm, umbonate
- Gills: sinuate-adnate, pale to brown
- Stem: white, strict and elongated, 90-200mm, with bluish strigose fibers at base
- Flesh: bluing
- Spores: 13-13.5 x 6.5-8.0 microns
- Cystidia: fusoid-ventricose
- Cheilocystidia: 23-28 x 6.5 microns

Typical analysis of dried P. azurescens (Gartz, 1995): psilocybin 1.40-1.71%, psilocin 0.28-0.34%, [[baeocystin]] 0.28-0.41%. Total alkaloid content up to ~25mg/g -- the highest of any known psilocybin mushroom.
