## Cultivar Effects on Microbiome Assembly

The cannabis cultivar (genetic strain) exerts a secondary but significant influence on the endorhiza microbiome. Different cultivars selectively recruit or exclude different microorganisms from the available soil pool, resulting in cultivar-specific microbiome profiles.

This selective recruitment is mediated by root exudates — the complex mixture of sugars, amino acids, organic acids, and [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] by selecting for acidophilic or alkaliphilic organisms. Many cannabis-associated microorganisms have specific pH optima.
- **Organic matter content**: Higher organic matter generally supports greater microbial biomass and diversity, providing more carbon sources and habitat complexity.
- **Nutrient availability**: Nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium levels affect which microbial functional groups can thrive. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria, for example, are more competitive in nitrogen-poor soils.
- **Soil texture**: Clay content affects water retention and root penetration, which in turn influence the microenvironments available for microbial colonization.

These factors operate as environmental filters, determining which organisms from the soil source pool can survive and proliferate in the root zone. The interaction between these filters and the plant's selective recruitment through exudates determines the final composition and structure of the endorhiza community.

## Core Microbiome and Proteobacterial Enrichment

Despite the variation driven by soil type and cultivar, a core set of microbial taxa is consistently associated with cannabis roots across diverse growing conditions. This core microbiome is dominated by Proteobacteria, particularly Alpha-proteobacteria (such as Methylophilus) and Beta-proteobacteria.

The consistent enrichment of Proteobacteria suggests a functional role in cannabis root health — potentially in nutrient cycling, pathogen suppression, or plant hormone modulation.

The core microbiome concept implies that certain plant-microbe associations are conserved across cannabis genotypes and environments. These core taxa may represent the most functionally important members of the community — the ones the plant cannot do without.

Understanding which taxa are core versus which are contingent on soil type or cultivar is essential for developing targeted microbiome management strategies.

## Community Structure and Functional Implications

The structural organization of the microbiome has functional consequences that go beyond simple taxonomic composition. A well-connected, positively correlated network suggests a cooperative community where taxa support each other's metabolic activities.

A fragmented, negatively correlated network suggests competition and antagonism, which may indicate stress or instability.

In cannabis, the network structure of the endorhiza microbiome varies with both soil type and cultivar, suggesting that the ecological dynamics of the community are context-dependent. A cultivar that promotes a cooperative network in one soil may not do so in another.

This highlights the importance of matching cultivar selection to growing conditions for optimal microbiome function.
