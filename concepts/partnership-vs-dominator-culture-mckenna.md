## Drugs and Cultural Style

McKenna argued that the drugs a culture chooses to embrace or suppress
reveal its deepest values. Dominator cultures have consistently
suppressed visionary plants (psychedelics, cannabis, mushrooms) while
promoting substances that reinforce social hierarchy and compliance
(alcohol, tobacco, sugar, coffee).

The transition from [[allegro-the-sacred-mushroom-amanita-identification]] use to alcohol consumption, in
McKenna's historical narrative, represents the transition from
partnership to dominator culture. Alcohol, unlike psilocybin, reinforces
ego boundaries, promotes aggression, and supports male dominance
patterns. The shift from a mushroom-eating, goddess-worshipping culture
to a beer-drinking, sky-god-worshipping one marks the fall from the
Archaic partnership paradigm.

## The Ego and Its Defenses

Substance-induced changes in consciousness dramatically reveal that
mental life has physical foundations. Psychoactive drugs thus challenge
the Christian assumption of the inviolability and special ontological
status of the soul. Similarly, they challenge the modern idea of the ego
and its inviolability and control structures.

Encounters with psychedelic plants throw into question the entire world
view of the dominator culture. This is why the dominator culture responds
with such anxiety and repression when contemplating psychoactive
substances — they threaten the very foundations of the ego-based
worldview that sustains hierarchical social organization.

## The Archaic Revival

McKenna proposed an "Archaic revival" as the antidote to dominator
culture. This revival would recover the pre-industrial, pre-literate
attitude toward community, substance use, and nature that served nomadic
ancestors for thousands of years. It represents a return to partnership
values: ecological awareness, direct spiritual experience, gender
equality, and a near-symbiotic relationship with the natural world.

The suppression of the natural human fascination with altered states of
consciousness and the perilous situation of all life on earth are
intimately and causally connected. When we suppress access to shamanic
ecstasy, we close off the refreshing waters of emotion that flow from
having a deeply bonded, almost symbiotic relationship to the earth.
