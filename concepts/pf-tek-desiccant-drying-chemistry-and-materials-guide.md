## The Chemistry of Desiccant Drying

### Hygroscopic Absorption

Desiccants function through hygroscopic absorption — the physical or chemical process by which a material absorbs water molecules from the surrounding air. The drying process in the PF TEK desiccant chamber works as follows:

1. **Mushroom transpiration**: Freshly harvested mushrooms continuously release moisture from their cellular structures into the surrounding air
2. **Vapor pressure gradient**: Water evaporating from the mushrooms creates a zone of elevated water vapor pressure immediately surrounding the fruiting bodies
3. **Desiccant absorption**: The desiccant, having an extremely low equilibrium relative humidity, creates a strong vapor pressure gradient that pulls water molecules from the air toward the desiccant material
4. **Continuous drying cycle**: As long as the desiccant has remaining absorption capacity, it continuously lowers the ambient relative humidity, driving further moisture loss from the mushrooms

The process is essentially a competition for water molecules between the mushroom tissue and the desiccant material. Because the desiccant has a much stronger affinity for water than the dried fungal tissue, the equilibrium state is reached only when the mushrooms are thoroughly desiccated.

### Silica Gel

Silica gel granules are available from chemical and science supply retailers.

**Chemical properties:**
- Chemical composition: amorphous silicon dioxide (SiO₂)
- Porous structure with enormous internal surface area (approximately 700–800 m²/g)
- Adsorption capacity: approximately 30–40% of its weight in water
- Works through physical adsorption (van der Waals forces) in its pore structure
- Available with indicating cobalt chloride or non-indicating forms
- Regeneration temperature: approximately 120–150°C for several hours

**Advantages:**
- High absorption capacity due to enormous surface area
- Reusable indefinitely with proper regeneration
- Non-toxic and chemically inert
- Granular form provides good air contact

**Disadvantages:**
- Can release absorbed moisture if ambient humidity exceeds the desiccant's equilibrium point
- Fine dust can be irritating if inhaled
- Some formulations contain cobalt chloride (toxic if ingested)

### Damp Gone (Calcium Chloride-Based)

"Damp Gone" is described as resembling kitty litter and being available at well-stocked hardware stores for drying closets and damp areas. The PF TEK notes it is "inexpensive desiccant but works as well as any."

**Chemical properties:**
- Likely based on calcium chloride (CaCl₂), a common consumer desiccant
- Calcium chloride is deliquescent: it absorbs moisture and can eventually dissolve into a brine solution
- Extremely high absorption capacity: up to 300% of its weight in water (due to hydration and deliquescence)
- Works through both physical absorption and chemical hydration (forming CaCl₂·2H₂O and CaCl₂·6H₂O)
- Regeneration: difficult to fully regenerate once deliquesced; typically discarded and replaced

**Advantages:**
- Very inexpensive and widely available
