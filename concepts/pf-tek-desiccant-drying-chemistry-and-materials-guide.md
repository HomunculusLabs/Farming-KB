# PF TEK Desiccant Drying: Chemistry, Materials, and Cool Drying for Alkaloid Preservation

## Overview

Desiccant drying is the final critical step in the PF TEK [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] cycle. The method employs chemical desiccants to absorb moisture from harvested mushrooms at cool or ambient temperatures, preserving the sensitive psychoactive alkaloids ([[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]]) that would be degraded by heat-based drying methods. PF Tek author Robert McPherson identifies desiccant drying as "overall, the best drying technique" for preserving mushroom quality.

## The Chemistry of Desiccant Drying

### Hygroscopic Absorption

Desiccants function through hygroscopic absorption — the physical or chemical process by which a material absorbs water molecules from the surrounding air. The drying process in the PF TEK desiccant chamber works as follows:

1. **Mushroom transpiration**: Freshly harvested mushrooms continuously release moisture from their cellular structures into the surrounding air
2. **Vapor pressure gradient**: Water evaporating from the mushrooms creates a zone of elevated water vapor pressure immediately surrounding the fruiting bodies
3. **Desiccant absorption**: The desiccant, having an extremely low equilibrium relative humidity, creates a strong vapor pressure gradient that pulls water molecules from the air toward the desiccant material
4. **Continuous drying cycle**: As long as the desiccant has remaining absorption capacity, it continuously lowers the ambient relative humidity, driving further moisture loss from the mushrooms

The process is essentially a competition for water molecules between the mushroom tissue and the desiccant material. Because the desiccant has a much stronger affinity for water than the dried fungal tissue, the equilibrium state is reached only when the mushrooms are thoroughly desiccated.

### Why Heat Drying Is Harmful

The PF TEK explicitly warns: "Never dry them in an oven or use hot air dryers. The heat leaches the [[guzman-magic-mushrooms-chemical-constituents]] and reduces their quality." This warning reflects the thermal sensitivity of the primary psychoactive compounds:

- **Psilocybin** (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine): The phosphorylated prodrug begins to undergo dephosphorylation to psilocin at elevated temperatures. While psilocybin itself is relatively heat-stable compared to psilocin, prolonged heat exposure can accelerate [[fungal-pah-degradation-pathways]] including oxidation of the indole ring.

- **Psilocin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine): The active metabolite is significantly more heat-labile than psilocybin. The phenolic hydroxyl group at the 4-position is susceptible to oxidative degradation, particularly at elevated temperatures where oxidation kinetics accelerate exponentially. Heat drying can convert a significant fraction of psilocin to inactive oxidation products including blue-colored quinoid compounds (responsible for the [[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]]).

The temperature dependence of these degradation reactions follows the Arrhenius equation — reaction rates approximately double for every 10°C increase in temperature. Cool drying at refrigerator temperatures (approximately 4°C) versus oven drying (60–100°C+) represents a temperature difference that could mean orders of magnitude slower degradation.

## Desiccant Materials

### Drierite (Anhydrous Calcium Sulfate)

Drierite is described in the PF TEK as "the universal lab desiccant" and is available from chemical and science supply retailers.

**Chemical properties:**
- Chemical formula: CaSO₄ (anhydrous calcium sulfate)
- Indicator grade contains cobalt(II) chloride, which changes from blue (dry) to pink (moist)
- Adsorption capacity: approximately 6–10% of its weight in water
- Works through surface adsorption rather than chemical reaction
- Non-deliquescent: does not dissolve or liquefy as it absorbs moisture
- Regeneration temperature: approximately 200–210°C for 1–2 hours

**Advantages:**
- Chemically inert and non-reactive with [[chamovitz-plant-scent-communication-volatile-organic-compounds]]
- Does not release absorbed moisture easily
- Available with moisture indicator for visual assessment of capacity
- Widely regarded as the gold standard for laboratory desiccation

**Disadvantages:**
- More expensive than consumer-grade alternatives
- Less readily available outside of scientific supply channels
- Lower total moisture absorption capacity compared to calcium chloride

### Silica Gel

Silica gel granules are available from chemical and science supply retailers.

**Chemical properties:**
- Chemical composition: amorphous silicon dioxide (SiO₂)
- Porous structure with enormous internal surface area (approximately 700–800 m²/g)
- Adsorption capacity: approximately 30–40% of its weight in water
- Works through physical adsorption (van der Waals forces) in its pore structure
- Available with indicating cobalt chloride or non-indicating forms
- Regeneration temperature: approximately 120–150°C for several hours

**Advantages:**
- High absorption capacity due to enormous surface area
- Reusable indefinitely with proper regeneration
- Non-toxic and chemically inert
- Granular form provides good air contact

**Disadvantages:**
- Can release absorbed moisture if ambient humidity exceeds the desiccant's equilibrium point
- Fine dust can be irritating if inhaled
- Some formulations contain cobalt chloride (toxic if ingested)

### Damp Gone (Calcium Chloride-Based)

"Damp Gone" is described as resembling kitty litter and being available at well-stocked hardware stores for drying closets and damp areas. The PF TEK notes it is "inexpensive desiccant but works as well as any."

**Chemical properties:**
- Likely based on calcium chloride (CaCl₂), a common consumer desiccant
- Calcium chloride is deliquescent: it absorbs moisture and can eventually dissolve into a brine solution
- Extremely high absorption capacity: up to 300% of its weight in water (due to hydration and deliquescence)
- Works through both physical absorption and chemical hydration (forming CaCl₂·2H₂O and CaCl₂·6H₂O)
- Regeneration: difficult to fully regenerate once deliquesced; typically discarded and replaced

**Advantages:**
- Very inexpensive and widely available
- Extremely high moisture absorption capacity
- Effective at low relative humidity levels
- Readily accessible at hardware and home improvement stores

**Disadvantages:**
- Deliquescent nature means it can liquefy if overloaded, potentially contaminating the drying chamber
- More difficult to regenerate than silica gel or Drierite
- May require a barrier between the desiccant and the wire screen to prevent contact
- Dust can be irritating

## Safety Considerations

The PF TEK includes important safety notes about desiccant handling:

- **Dust inhalation**: "Don't breathe the dust" — desiccant dust is a respiratory irritant
- **Skin contact**: "Try not to touch it directly — it dries skin" — desiccants will extract moisture from skin on contact
- **Container isolation**: "Desiccant in an airtight box and under a screen will do nothing to the fungi except dry them. It is completely safe for this use"
- The physical separation provided by the wire screen ensures that desiccant material does not contact the mushrooms directly

## The Desiccant Drying Setup

### Materials Required

1. **Desiccant**: Drierite, silica gel, or Damp Gone
2. **Wire screen**: For supporting mushrooms above the desiccant layer
3. **Plastic tub or container**: To hold the desiccant and mushrooms in an enclosed space
4. **Plastic bag with tie-off**: To seal the container and prevent ambient humidity intrusion

### Assembly

1. **Desiccant layer**: Place a **1-inch layer** of desiccant on the bottom of the container
2. **Wire screen**: Position a wire screen over the desiccant layer
3. **Mushroom placement**: Arrange harvested mushrooms on the wire screen with air available to all sides
4. **Enclosure**: Place the container inside a plastic bag and seal with a wire tie
5. **Visibility**: A clear plastic bag is preferred for observing the drying process

### Pre-conditioning the Desiccant

A critical step that is easily overlooked: "This preheating should be done before the desiccant is used because when it is purchased — it is usually somewhat damp which will thwart its function for drying air."

- Heat the desiccant in an oven according to manufacturer instructions before first use
- This drives off any moisture absorbed during storage and shipping
- Pre-conditioned desiccant will be at maximum absorption capacity

### The Drying Timeline

The PF TEK describes the drying progression:

| Time | Observation |
|---|---|
| 24 hours | Slight shriveling visible |
| 4–5 days | Mushrooms dried "rock hard" |
| Final test | Stem should snap cleanly when bent |

### Reusing Desiccant

"Desiccant can be reused and lasts indefinitely":

1. After use, heat the desiccant in an oven per manufacturer instructions
2. Store in an airtight container to prevent re-absorption of ambient moisture
