### The Garden Fork
A four-tined fork is essential for soils that are not excessively stony. The fork:

- Loosens soil without inverting it (preserving soil structure)
- Aerates compacted ground
- Harvests [[hamilton-seed-sowing-techniques]]
- Incorporate fine amendments into the surface

### The Trowel
For transplanting and small-scale work. A forged trowel with a substantial blade is preferred over thin stamped models.

## Seed Sowing and Thinning Tools

### The Seed Row Marking Tool
A simple implement for making uniform furrows at consistent depth. Can be as simple as a stick with a notch cut at the desired planting depth, or a purpose-made tool with adjustable depth.

### The Dibber
A pointed stick or purpose-made tool for making holes for transplanting seedlings or large seeds. The depth of the hole can be marked on the dibber to ensure consistent planting depth.

## Weeding Tools

### The Hand Cultivator (Three-Pronged)
A small hand tool with three curved tines, useful for:

- Weeding close to crop plants where a hoe cannot reach
- Breaking soil crust after rain
- Scratch-weeding tiny weed seedlings

### The Collinear Hoe
A narrow-bladed hoe with the blade aligned with the handle (developed by [[jadam-liquid-fertilizer-nutritional-perspective]]

### Hose and Watering Wand
For larger gardens, a hose with an adjustable watering wand allows efficient watering with control over flow rate and pattern.
