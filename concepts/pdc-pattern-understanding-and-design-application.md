The Williams PDC identifies pattern understanding as one of the most fundamental skills in permaculture design. Williams describes pattern recognition as both a practical design tool and a way of perceiving the world that distinguishes the permaculture designer from conventional designers.
## The Nature of Patterns
### Definition
Williams defines a pattern as a set of recurring things, shapes, or actions that happen again and again. Patterns are the means by which energy and matter move through space and time. Understanding patterns allows the designer to work with natural forces rather than against them.
### Pattern vs. Detail
Williams draws a critical distinction between patterns and details:
- **Patterns** are the large-scale, overall shapes and flows that organize a system
- **Details** are the specific elements placed within those patterns

## Pattern Application in the Design Process
### Reading Patterns on Site
The site analysis phase involves reading existing patterns:
- **Topographic patterns**: Ridges, valleys, saddles, slopes -- how does water and wind move across the land?
- **Vegetation patterns**: Where do plants grow naturally? What does this reveal about soil, moisture, and microclimate?
- **Animal patterns**: Where do animals move, feed, and rest? What corridors do they use?
- **Human patterns**: Where do people walk, gather, and work? What invisible structures shape their behavior?
- **Temporal patterns**: What cycles occur daily, seasonally, annually?
