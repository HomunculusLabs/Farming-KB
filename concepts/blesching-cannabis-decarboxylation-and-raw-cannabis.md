## Cannabis Decarboxylation and Raw Cannabis Use

Decarboxylation is the chemical process by which the acidic precursors of
cannabinoids in fresh cannabis are converted into their active forms through
heat, time, or light exposure. Understanding this process is essential for
anyone using cannabis therapeutically, as it determines whether the medicine
will produce psychoactive effects and influences the [[cannabis-leaf-morphology-and-phyllotaxy]], CBD and THC cannabinoids exist as CBD-acid
and THC-acid, both of which are non-psychoactive in their raw state. Once the
plant cannabinoids become heated, dried, or stored, decarboxylation takes
place, changing the molecular structure and its resultant properties. While
CBD remains non-psychoactive after decarboxylation, THC-acid converts to THC,
the main psychoactive molecule in cannabis. This transformation fundamentally
limits the maximum potential dose of psychoactive cannabis that can be safely
consumed, since excessive THC produces [[carbon-dioxide-retention-jar-lid-mushroom-cultivation]] molecule (CO2) when exposed to sufficient heat,
converting it into delta-9-THC (tetrahydrocannabinol). This reaction typically
occurs at temperatures between 200 and 310 degrees Fahrenheit (93 to 154
degrees Celsius), depending on the method and duration of heating.

Vaporization provides a controlled form of decarboxylation, heating plant
material to a precise temperature that evaporates cannabinoids just below the
burning point of the plant matter. This method allows patients to inhale
active cannabinoids while minimizing exposure to combustion byproducts. Smoking
provides rapid and complete decarboxylation, while cooking with cannabis-infused
oils or butter at moderate temperatures achieves partial decarboxylation over
longer periods.

The rate of decarboxylation is affected by several factors including
temperature, duration of heat exposure, moisture content, and the specific
cannabinoid being converted. THC-A decarboxylates more readily than CBD-A,
meaning that under identical conditions, more THC will be produced relative to
CBD. This has practical implications for patients who wish to maximize either
the psychoactive or non-psychoactive components of their medicine.
