## Overview

large-scale synthesis of [[4-hydroxyindole]] to psilocin. This intermediate is formed through
a two-step acylation-amidation conversion starting from 4-acetylindole (compound
4). The key to this pathway is the treatment of 4-acetylindole with oxalyl
chloride, which yields a crystalline oxalyl chloride adduct (compound 5) that
can be isolated by filtration and directly converted to the glyoxalylamide
through reaction with dimethylamine. This crystallization step is central to the
synthesis's practicality, enabling gram-scale production without chromatographic
purification.

### 1H NMR Data
The glyoxalylamide intermediate (compound 6) shows characteristic 1H NMR
signals in CDCl3 at 400 MHz: a broad singlet at delta 10.40 (H-1, the indole
NH), a doublet at delta 7.52 (H-2, J = 3.2 Hz), a triplet at delta 7.15 (H-6,
J = 8.0 Hz), a doublet at delta 7.06 (H-7, J = 8.0 Hz), a doublet at delta
6.91 (H-5, J = 8.0 Hz), two N-methyl singlets at delta 3.02 and 2.92, and
an acetyl methyl singlet at delta 2.50.

### 13C NMR Data
The 13C NMR spectrum shows the characteristic carbonyl carbon of the
glyoxalylamide group at delta 185.4 (C-1'), the acetyl carbonyl at delta
170.9, the amide carbonyl at delta 168.4, the C-4 oxygenated aromatic carbon
at delta 144.2, and the C-7a bridgehead carbon at delta 139.2. The N-methyl
carbons appear at delta 37.4 and 34.2, and the acetyl methyl at delta 21.6.
