# Zwitterionic N,O-Dibenzyl Phosphate Intermediate Discovery

## Overview

The key innovation in Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda's 2003 synthesis
of psilocybin is the discovery and exploitation of a zwitterionic
N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (compound 8) as an isolable
intermediate. This compound forms spontaneously during the
phosphorylation of psilocin with tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and can
be isolated in high yield by simple filtration. Its discovery
enabled gram-scale [[gottlieb-psilocybin-production-extraction-methods]] without any chromatographic
purification—a significant practical advance over previous routes.

## The Phosphorylation Challenge

Converting psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) to psilocybin
(4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) requires selective
phosphorylation of the 4-hydroxy group while leaving the tertiary
amine untouched. This is chemically challenging because both the
phenol and the amine are nucleophilic sites that can react with
phosphorylating agents.

Shirota and colleagues evaluated several phosphorylation
methodologies: the phosphite/I2/DMAP method, the pyrophosphate
method using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-BuLi, and others.
Most methods either failed to consume psilocin or produced
intractable mixtures. The pyrophosphate method was selected
because of "easy handling and the reagent stability."

## Formation of the O,O-Dibenzyl Phosphate

The phosphorylation of psilocin (1) with tetrabenzylpyrophosphate
in THF at -78°C to 0°C in the presence of n-butyllithium produces
the O,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (7). TLC analysis confirmed
that psilocin was consumed and a new product formed. However,
the 1H NMR spectrum of the crude product showed "complicated
signals," and rechecking TLC revealed "an additional spot at the
origin" of material that no longer dissolved in dichloromethane.

## The Zwitterionic Conversion

This insoluble material turned out to be the zwitterionic
N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (8), formed by intramolecular
migration of one benzyl group from the phosphate oxygen to the
nitrogen of the N,N-dimethylamino group. The migration converts
the neutral O,O-dibenzyl phosphate (7) into a zwitterionic species
with a quaternary ammonium cation and a phosphate anion.

Nichols and Frescas had previously observed this phenomenon and
"concluded that hydrolytic cleavage of one of the O-benzyl groups
rapidly occurred and the resulting zwitterionic O-monobenzyl
phosphate was obtained as a mixture." Shirota's contribution was
to fully characterize the compound and demonstrate that its
zwitterionic [[coleman-first-rule-nature-makes-sense]] it exploitable for large-scale isolation.

## Structural Elucidation by 2D NMR

Purification by preparative reversed-phase HPLC afforded pure
compound 8, which was fully characterized by NMR. The key
spectroscopic features include:

### 1H NMR (CD3OD, 400 MHz)
- Two sets of benzyl signals: δ 7.56-7.45 (5H, m, NCH2C6H5)
  and δ 7.31-7.20 (5H, m, OCH2C6H5)
- N-benzyl methylene shifted to high field: δ 4.56 (2H, s)
- O-benzyl methylene at normal position: δ 4.98, 4.96 (each 1H, s)
- Psilocin core protons shifted to low field relative to free
  psilocin, consistent with the positive charge on nitrogen

### 13C NMR (CD3OD, 100 MHz)
- Characteristic signals for two benzyl groups and the psilocin
  core, with the quaternary carbon of the ammonium ion at δ 139.3

### 31P NMR
- Confirmed the presence of the phosphate moiety in the molecule

### HMBC Correlations
The HMBC experiment was decisive. It revealed that one benzyl
group was "directly linked at the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethyl
part (a quaternary ammonium ion)." The NOESY spectrum further
supported these assignments by showing spatial correlations
between the N-benzyl group and the dimethylamino protons.

## The Filtration Advantage

The zwitterionic nature of 8 is what makes this synthesis
practically useful. After the phosphorylation reaction, the
worked-up mixture is suspended in CH2Cl2 overnight, during which
the zwitterionic compound precipitates as a white solid. The
excess dibenzyl phosphate remains soluble and is removed by
washing with CH2Cl2. Compound 8 is collected by simple filtration
"in over 85% yield."

This is a remarkable practical advantage. In most synthetic
routes, the challenge is not the chemical transformation but the
purification—removing the product from a complex mixture of
starting materials, reagents, and byproducts. The zwitterionic
precipitation accomplishes this purification in a single,
low-technology step: stir, filter, wash. No chromatography, no
recrystallization from exotic solvents, no ion-exchange resins.

## Catalytic Hydrogenolysis to Psilocybin

The final step is catalytic hydrogenolysis of 8 over Pd/C in
methanol at room temperature. This simultaneously removes both
benzyl groups—the N-benzyl group and the O-benzyl group—to yield
psilocybin (2) as a white needle crystalline powder in 87.5% yield.
The reaction is monitored by TLC for "disappearance of 8 and its
mono debenzyl derivative, and the appearance of 2."

The isolated yield of psilocybin from psilocin is "greater than
72%, even for a gram-scale production." This compares favorably
with previously reported yields of 20% (Troxler, 1959), 20%
(Hofmann, 1959), and 47% (Nichols and Frescas). The combination
of high yield and chromatography-free purification makes this
route the most practical published method for producing
psilocybin [[shirota-forensic-standard-compounds-magic-mushroom-identification]] for analytical use.

## Significance for Forensic and Research Applications

The practical significance of this route lies in the need for
pure psilocybin and psilocin as reference standards for the
chromatographic analysis (TLC, GC, HPLC) of seized mushroom
samples. As Shirota notes, "It is difficult to isolate 1 and 2
from the mushroom on a [[shirota-gram-scale-chromatography-free-psilocybin-synthesis]] for use as pure standard
compounds because 1 easily decomposes and 2 has a high polarity."
The synthesis described provides a reliable, scalable route to
these essential analytical standards.
