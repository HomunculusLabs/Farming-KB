# Reed Bed Water Treatment

Reed bed systems represent the most sophisticated biological water treatment stage, using higher plants to remove heavy metals, human pathogens, and even break down halogenated hydrocarbons synthesised as herbicides and pesticides. These systems serve as final treatment before water release to streams or reuse.

## Faculative and Aerobic Pond Progression

Sewage treatment progresses through three pond stages from intake to outlet:

### Anaerobic (Methane-Producing) Ponds
- pH: 6.2–7.8
- Primary settling and biogas production
- Sulphur-loving bacteria (*Thiosporallum*, *Chromatium*, *Rhodopseudomonas*)
- Destroys 90–94% of worm eggs

### Faculative Ponds
- pH: 7.5–8.2
- Part methane-producing at base, oxygen-producing at surface (from wind and algae)
- Rich algal blooms develop: *Euglena*, *Chlamydomonas*, *Chlorella*, *Scenedesmus*
- Bacteria include *Cyclotella*, *Pinnularia*, *Hypnodinium*, *Rhodomonas*
- Combined algal and bacterial flora called **phytoplankton**

### Aerobic Ponds
- pH: 7.5–8.5
- BOD reduced to only 3–57 mg/l
- Suspended solids 32–50 mg/l (mainly algae and zooplankton)
- About 80% of contaminants removed and incorporated into life forms
- Metal levels reduced to World Health Organisation standards

## Zooplankton: Living Water Purifiers

With rich algal food available, zooplankton thrive in faculative ponds:
- **Rotifers**: *Brachionus*, *Trichocerca*, *Hexarthra*, *Filinia*
- **Cladocerans**: *Daphnia*, *Moina*, *Chydorus*, *Pleuroxus*
- **Copepods**: *Mesocyclops*
- **Ostracods**: *Candonocypris*, *Cypridopsis*
- Protozoan flagellates, ciliates, and some nematodes

### Heavy Metal Accumulation in Zooplankton

Zooplankton concentrate metals in their bodies (mg/l dry weight):
- Iron: 1,200
- Zinc: 152
- Copper: 37
- Chromium: 28
- Nickel: 12.2
- Lead: 10.3
- Cadmium: 1.7

These are harvested and added to foodstuffs for poultry, or pumped into forests and fields as manure and trace elements. Dense blooms of *Daphnia* can reach 100 mg/l. Zooplankton are self-controlled by consuming their algal food supply and are in turn consumed by fish in subsequent pond systems.

## Advanced Aeration Techniques

### Alternating Aerobic and Anaerobic (AAA) Digestion

It has been found that artificial aeration of faculative ponds is most efficient if run at intervals of **two hours in six** (30% of the time). Faculative bacteria follow two digestive modes and operate best with a rush of air after a four-hour anaerobic period, excreting carbon dioxide and reducing sludge bulk.

Results with AAA digestion:
- Nitrogen reduced from 20 mg/l to <5 mg/l
- Phosphorus reduced from 8.5 mg/l to <1 mg/l (with ferric chloride addition)
- Corresponding reductions in energy costs for aeration

## Algal Harvesting Systems

### Canalised Pond Systems (Israel and Holland)

Israel leads sewage waters to long canalised ponds agitated by slowly-revolving paddle-wheel aerators. Ponds are 0.5 m or less deep. Under bright sunlight, dense algal mats form and are:
1. Broken up with aluminium sulphate (a pollutant trade-off)
2. Skimmed off and drained
3. Centrifuged and steam-dried
4. Fed to carp or chickens

Algal protein replaces 50% of soya bean protein in poultry feed rations. Total treatment takes about 4 days. Holland runs similar canals, reaping reeds or plants as green crop or for craft supplies.

Microwave radiation can also break up algal mats and sterilise algal products, eliminating toxic aluminium salts.

## Reed Bed Canal Systems

Final treatment uses a sinuous, sealed canal of various rushes and floating water plants. For untreated sewage, a **holding time of 10–12 days** is necessary, or travel through maze-like gravel-filter canals with floating weeds and sedges.

### Recommended Species

| Species | Function |
|---------|----------|
| *Phragmites communis* and spp., *Typha* spp. | Flocculate colloids, dry out sludges, eliminate pathogens |
| *Schoenoplectus* spp. | Takes up copper, cobalt, nickel, manganese; exudes mould antibiotics |
| *Scirpus* spp. | Breaks down phenols, including toxic pentachlorophenol |
| *Water hyacinth* (*Eichhornia crassipes*) | Removes residual metals, surplus nutrients, and coli group bacilli |

### Water Hyacinth Systems

Water hyacinth in the aerobic pond stage removes residual metals, surplus nutrients, and the coli group of bacilli. The plants can be harvested periodically and the nutrient-rich biomass used as mulch, compost, or even biogas feedstock.

## Swimming Pool Natural Filtration

For less polluted systems, a pumped cycle of water through ferns, rushes, and watercress removes urine and leaves. Such natural swimming pools need:
- 23–30 cm (9–12 inch) coarse river gravel base
- Intake pipes below the gravel
- Skimming notch for leaves

### Pathogen Removal Results

Low to zero populations of these organisms are found after reed bed treatment:
- *E. coli*
- Coliform bacteria
- *Salmonella*
- *Enterococci*

## Solar Pond Integration

Solar ponds used in conjunction with compact anaerobic ponds supply the low-grade heat necessary for efficient sludge digestion. Methane drives motors needed for both aeration and gas compression for sludge agitation. The whole processing system can be made very compact, with aerobic pond throughflow led to firewood or fuel forest systems, irrigated grasslands, or trickle irrigation to crops in arid areas.

## Complete Treatment Chain Summary

The ideal layout produces from heavily-polluted influent:
1. **Gas** (methane energy)
2. **Algae** (high-protein animal feed)
3. **Fish** and wildlife habitat
4. **Crop** irrigation water
5. **Thoroughly cleansed water** safe for release to streams

No single stage achieves this alone — it is the synergistic combination of anaerobic digestion, faculative processing, aerobic polishing, and reed bed final treatment that produces beneficial effluent.
