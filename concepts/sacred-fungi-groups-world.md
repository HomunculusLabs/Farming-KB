### Chemical Profile
The primary active compounds are:
- **Psilocybin** (4-PO-DMT) - The most common and stable indole alkaloid
- **Psilocin** (4-HO-DMT) - The dephosphorylated, more active metabolite
- **Baeocystin** (4-PO-NMT) and **Norbaeocystin** (4-HO-NMT) - Minor constituents

The bluing reaction (oxidation of psilocin) is the primary field indicator for this group, though
not all bluing species contain psilocybin and not all psilocybin-containing species blue strongly.

## Group 2: Ibotenic Acid and Muscimol Fungi

Contains three [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]]
- **A. pantherina** (panther cap) - Contains ibotenic acid and muscimol
- **A. regalis** (royal fly agaric) - Less common, similar chemistry

These are confined to temperate regions and have a very different pharmacological profile from the
psilocybin-containing species. Their active compounds are isoxazole derivatives rather than indoles.

## Group 3: Ergot Fungi (Clavicipitaceae)

Seven species producing ergot alkaloids:
- **Claviceps purpurea** - The classic ergot fungus on cereal grasses, producer of ergotamine and
  related alkaloids. See [[cordyceps-and-entomopathogenic-fungi]]

Ergot alkaloids include lysergic acid amides, which are chemically related to LSD. The ergot fungi
have a long history of significance in human affairs, from ergotism (St. Anthony's Fire) to the
discovery of LSD.

## Group 4: Sacred Fungi Without Confirmed Chemistry

Fungi used traditionally as sacred or ceremonial organisms but without reliable chemical studies:

### Boletes
- **Boletus** - 6 species
- **Heimiella** - 2 species
- Used traditionally in Southeast Asia and Oceania

### Russula
- **Russula** - 6 species
- Some used ceremonially in various cultures

### Gasteromycetes (Puffballs and Allies)
- **Lycoperdales** (puffballs, earth-stars) - Several species used traditionally
- **Phallales** (stinkhorns) - Including Dictyophora indusiata (veiled stinkhorn), used in Mexico
  and China. The eggs are sold as delicacies in China and marketed as aphrodisiacs

## Traditional and Ceremonial Use

The main ethnic groups using neurotropic fungi ceremonially are located in:
- **Mexico** - Mazatec, Mixtec, Zapotec, Nahuatl, Chinantec peoples (most well-documented)
- **New Guinea** - Various indigenous groups
- Possibly **Africa** (Samorini, pers. comm.)
- Possibly **Colombia** - Ancient gold pectorals suggest pre-Columbian use (Schultes & Bright, 1979)

The term "teonanacatl" (divine mushroom), first recorded by Sahagun (1569-1582), is the most famous
indigenous name for sacred mushrooms, though it has been indiscriminately applied to any Mexican
hallucinogenic fungus.
