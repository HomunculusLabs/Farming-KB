# Ergot Fungi Claviceps and Ergot Alkaloids

## [[ergot-fungus]], parasitic on grasses and cereals worldwide. Unlike
the basidiomycete [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]] (mushrooms), Claviceps is an
ascomycete that produces dark, hard sclerotia (ergots) in place of grain
kernels in infected grass heads. The paper by Guzman, Allen, and Gartz
includes C. purpurea in its catalog of neurotropic fungi due to the potent
psychoactive alkaloids it produces, which include lysergic acid amide (LSA)
and related ergoline compounds.

## Additional Claviceps Species

Beyond C. purpurea, the paper documents several other Claviceps species
with neurotropic potential:

- **Claviceps paspali**: Parasitic on Paspalum grasses, this species
  produces paspaline and related clavine alkaloids. It is found
  throughout tropical and subtropical regions where Paspalum grasses
  grow. It has been associated with animal toxicosis (paspalum
  staggers) and may have psychoactive properties.

- **Claviceps fusiformis**: Parasitic on pearl millet (Pennisetum
  glaucum) in Africa and India. It produces clavine alkaloids and
  ergotamine-like compounds. It is a significant agricultural pest
  in regions where pearl millet is a staple crop.

- **Claviceps gigas**: A species documented from Asia with limited
  information on its alkaloid profile and distribution.

## Chemistry of Ergot Alkaloids

The ergot alkaloids are a complex and diverse group of compounds
derived from the tetracyclic ergoline ring system. They are classified
into several structural groups:

- **Clavine alkaloids**: Simple ergoline derivatives including
  agroclavine, elymoclavine, and lysergol. These are produced by
  several Claviceps species and serve as biosynthetic precursors to
  more complex alkaloids.

- **Lysergic acid amides**: Lysergic acid amide (LSA, also called
  ergine) and isolysergic acid amide are found in C. purpurea and
  several [[golden-guide-morning-glory-and-ololiuqui]] species (Convolvulaceae). LSA is
  approximately 1/10th as potent as LSD but produces qualitatively
  similar effects at appropriate doses.

- **Ergopeptine alkaloids**: Complex peptides including ergotamine,
  ergocristine, ergocryptine, ergovaline, and ergostine. These are
  the primary alkaloids of C. purpurea and have potent
  vasoconstrictive and oxytocic properties in addition to
  neurological effects.

## Historical Significance

Ergot has had a profound impact on human history. Ergotism, the disease
caused by consuming ergot-contaminated grain, has been responsible for
numerous epidemics throughout European history. Two forms of ergotism are
recognized: convulsive ergotism (St. Anthony's Fire), characterized by
hallucinations, spasms, and psychosis; and gangrenous ergotism, causing
vasoconstriction and tissue necrosis leading to limb amputation. Some
historians have proposed that ergotism contributed to the Salem witch
trials of 1692, though this hypothesis remains debated.

## Connection to LSD

The most significant pharmacological legacy of ergot is the synthesis of
lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD-25) by gordon-wasson at Sandoz
Laboratories in 1938. LSD was first synthesized from ergotamine, an
alkaloid produced by [[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]]. Hofmann's accidental discovery
of LSD's psychoactive properties in 1943 launched the modern era of
[[mckenna-suppression-psychedelic-research-lsd-therapy]] and had enormous cultural consequences throughout
the 20th century. All subsequent synthetic ergoline psychoactives are
derived from or modeled on the ergot alkaloid framework.
