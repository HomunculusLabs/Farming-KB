# Psychedelic Plants Indoor Growing

## Overview

## San Pedro Cactus ([[trichocereus-pachanoi]])

### Setup Requirements
- Light: Full sun equivalent — grow lights (full spectrum LED or MH/HPS) at 200–400 µmol/m²/s, 12–14 hours/day
- Temperature: 60–90°F (15–32°C) — can tolerate brief cold snaps down to 40°F
- Soil: Well-draining cactus mix — 50% mineral (pumice, perlite, sand) + 50% organic (potting soil, coco coir)
- Pots: Terracotta or fabric pots with drainage holes; tall pots accommodate taproot
- Humidity: Low (20–40%) — San Pedro is adapted to arid conditions

### Propagation from Cuttings
1. Obtain a cutting (6–12+ inches) from a mature, healthy cactus
2. Allow the cut end to callus: place upright in a dry, shaded area for 2–6 weeks
3. The callus should be dry, hard, and slightly shriveled at the cut surface
4. Once callused, place the cutting 2–4 inches deep in dry cactus soil
5. Do not water for 2–4 weeks — roots form best in dry conditions
6. After roots form (gently tug to check resistance), begin light watering
7. Water thoroughly, then allow soil to dry completely between waterings

### Growing from Seed
1. Sow seeds on the surface of moist, sterile cactus soil
2. Cover with a humidity dome or plastic wrap
3. Maintain temperature at 70–85°F (21–29°C) and high humidity
4. Provide indirect light — seedlings scorch easily
5. Germination: 1–4 weeks
6. Gradually remove humidity cover over 2–3 months
7. Growth rate from seed is slow: 1–2 inches per year for the first few years

### CarneemFertilize monthly during the growing season (spring–fall) with low-nitrogen cactus fertilizer (1-7-6 or similar)
- Reduce or stop watering and fertilizing in winter (dormancy period)
- Watch for pests: mealybugs, scale, spider mites — treat with neem oil or insecticidal soap
- Rotate pots quarterly for even growth

### Expected Growth
- 12–24 inches per year under good indoor conditions
- A cutting can reach ceremonial size (3+ feet) in 2–4 years
- Mature height indoors: 6–10 feet (will eventually need staking or pruning)

## Salvia Divinorum

### Setup Requirements
- Light: Indirect bright light — Salvia burns in direct sun. Dappled light or LED at 100–200 µmol/m²/s, 12–14 hours/day
- Temperature: 60–80°F (15–27°C) — sensitive to both cold and extreme heat
- Humidity: High (50–80%) — this is the most critical parameter. Low humidity causes leaf curling and die-off
- Soil: Rich, well-draining potting mix — standard potting soil amended with perlite and peat
- Pots: Standard nursery pots with drainage; 3–5 gallon for mature plants
- Water: Keep soil consistently moist but not waterlogged

### Propagation
Salvia rarely produces viable seeds. Propagation is by stem cuttings:

1. Select a healthy, actively growing stem 4–8 inches long
2. Cut below a node at a 45° angle
3. Remove lower leaves, leaving 2–4 pairs at the top
4. Place the cutting in a glass of water (or moist potting soil)
5. Keep in a warm, humid, bright indirect light location
6. Roots form in 2–4 weeks [[ingoldian-hyphomycetes-spore-dispersal-in-water]]
7. Once roots are 1–2 inches long, transplant [[query-how-do-legumes-add-nitrogen-to-soil]]
8. Maintain high humidity during the transition — a humidity dome or misting system helps

### Care
- Maintain high humidity: use a humidifier, humidity dome, or pebble tray
- Mist daily if ambient humidity is below 60%
- Fertilize lightly every 2–4 weeks during the growing season with balanced liquid fertilizer at half strength
- Pinch growing tips to encourage bushier growth
- Support tall stems with stakes — Salvia can become leggy indoors
- Watch for pests: aphids, whiteflies, spider mites — neem oil treatment

### Challenges
- Salvia is sensitive to environmental changes — moving the plant can cause leaf drop
- It does not tolerate drought — do not let soil dry out completely
- It is not cold-hardy — temperatures below 40°F will damage or kill the plant
- A greenhouse or dedicated humid growing space produces the best results

## Psychotria viridis (Chacruna)

### Setup Requirements
- Light: Partial shade to bright indirect — simulate jungle understory. LED at 150–250 µmol/m²/s, 12–14 hours/day
- Temperature: 65–85°F (18–29°C) — tropical plant, sensitive to cold
- Humidity: High (60–90%) — native to Amazon rainforest understory
- Soil: Rich, acidic potting mix — peat-based with perlite, orchid bark, and worm castings. Target pH 5.0–6.5
- Pots: 3–10 gallon as the plant matures
- Water: Keep soil consistently moist

### Propagation
From seed:
1. Fresh seeds germinate best — viability drops rapidly with age
2. Surface sow on moist, sterile seed-starting mix
3. Cover with humidity dome, maintain 75–85°F
4. Germination: 3–8 weeks (can be erratic)
5. Seedlings are slow-growing initially

[[query-how-to-propagate-woody-herbs-from-cuttings]]:
1. Take semi-hardwood cuttings 4–6 inches long
2. Dip in rooting hormone
3. Place in moist propagation mix under high humidity
4. Roots in 4–8 weeks
5. Bottom heat (75°F) improves success rate

### Care
- High humidity is essential — use a greenhouse cabinet or grow tent with humidification
- Maintain warm temperatures year-round
- Fertilize monthly during growing season with balanced fertilizer
- Prune to maintain manageable size — P. viridis can become a large shrub
- Leaves can be harvested once the plant is well-established (1+ years old)

## Banisteriopsis caapi (Ayahuasca Vine)

### Setup Requirements
- Light: Bright indirect to partial sun — jungle conditions. LED at 200–300 µmol/m²/s, 12–14 hours/day
- Temperature: 65–85°F (18–29°C) — strictly tropical, no cold tolerance
- Humidity: High (60–90%) — rainforest conditions preferred
- Soil: Rich, well-draining tropical mix — potting soil, peat, perlite, orchid bark
- Support: B. caapi is a climbing vine — provide a trellis, pole, or wire support
- Pots: Large — 5–20 gallon as the vine matures

### Propagation
From seed:
1. Fresh seeds are essential — viability is very short-lived
2. Soak seeds in water for 24 hours before planting
3. Sow 1/4 inch deep in moist, sterile seed mix
4. Maintain 75–85°F and high humidity
5. Germination: 2–6 weeks (highly variable)

From cuttings:
1. Take woody stem cuttings 8–12 inches long with at least 2 nodes
2. Dip in rooting hormone
3. Place in moist propagation medium under high humidity and bottom heat
4. Roots in 4–12 weeks
5. Success rate is moderate — this is not the easiest plant to propagate

### Care
- Provide a sturdy trellis or support structure — the vine will climb aggressively
