# Spore Printing and Spore Syringe Preparation for Mushroom Cultivation

## Overview

Spore collection and preservation are fundamental skills in mushroom cultivation. The PF TEK described detailed methods for producing **spore prints** from mature mushrooms and preparing **spore syringes** for inoculation. These techniques enable cultivators to maintain genetic material across generations, share strains with other growers, and sustain cultivation operations without relying on commercial spore suppliers. The PF TEK's approach emphasized simplicity and sterility, using basic household materials to achieve reliable results.

## Mushroom Reproduction and the Role of Spores

### Spore Biology

Mushrooms reproduce sexually through the production and dispersal of **spores**:

- A single mature mushroom cap can produce **millions to billions** of spores
- Spores are microscopic (typically 10–20 micrometers) and are dispersed by air currents
- Each spore is a single cell containing half the genetic material of the parent (haploid)
- When a spore germinates, it produces a monokaryotic mycelium with a single nucleus per cell
- Two compatible monokaryotic mycelia must fuse to form a dikaryotic mycelium capable of producing mushrooms
- This is why multispore inoculation produces genetic diversity — each spore represents a unique genotype

### The Spore Print as a Genetic Snapshot

A spore print captures the genetic diversity of a single mushroom's reproductive output:

- The print contains spores from a single fruiting body, representing the genetic recombination products of the dikaryotic mycelium that produced it
- Different mushrooms from the same grow (even from the same cake) can produce genetically distinct spore prints
- A spore print is therefore a snapshot of one specific reproductive event, not a clone of the parent
- This genetic variability is both a strength (preserves reproductive fitness) and a limitation (cannot guarantee identical results across grows)

## Spore Print Production

### Harvest Timing

The quality of a spore print depends critically on harvest timing:

- The cap must be mature enough to have begun dropping spores but not so mature that it has released most of its spores
- Ideal timing: just after the **veil tears** and the cap begins to flatten, or when the cap is fully expanded but before heavy spore deposit is visible on the stem
- The gills should be visible and dark-colored (purple-brown to nearly black in *[[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]*)

### The Spore Print Method

The PF TEK spore print procedure:

1. **Harvest the mushroom**: Cut or twist the stem from the cake, leaving the cap intact
2. **Prepare a clean surface**: Use a sheet of clean white paper, aluminum foil, or glass for the print surface. Some cultivators pre-sterilize the surface with alcohol.
3. **Place the cap**: Set the cap gill-side-down on the printing surface. If the cap has a long stem, trim it close to the gills.
4. **Cover**: Place a clean glass or bowl over the cap to reduce airflow and contamination while allowing minimal gas exchange
5. **Wait**: Leave undisturbed for 6–24 hours. During this time, the cap will drop spores onto the surface in a pattern that mirrors the gill structure.
6. **Remove the cap**: Carefully lift the cap away. A visible deposit of spores (dark purple-brown) should remain on the printing surface.
7. **Dry and store**: Allow the print to dry completely (several hours to overnight), then fold or roll the paper/foil and store in a sealed bag with desiccant.

### Print Quality Assessment

A good spore print should show:

- **Even, dense deposit**: Spores covering the full area beneath the gills
- **Deep color**: Dark purple-brown to black in *Psilocybe* species
- **Clean appearance**: No visible mold growth, discoloration, or contamination on the print
- **Dry texture**: Fully dried prints store better and are less prone to contamination

## Spore Syringe Preparation

### Purpose and Function

A spore syringe is a suspension of mushroom spores in sterile water, drawn into a syringe for convenient inoculation:

- Each syringe typically contains 10–12 cc of spore solution
- The concentration is usually 1 print per 4–10 syringes, depending on print density
- Spore syringes can be stored for several months at room temperature or longer under refrigeration
- The syringe allows precise dispensing of spores into substrate jars through the inoculation port

### Equipment and Materials

Required materials for spore syringe preparation:

- **Sterile syringes**: 10–12 cc capacity with needles (18–20 gauge)
- **Sterile water**: Distilled water that has been sterilized (boiled for 10 minutes or pressure-sterilized)
- **The spore print**: A clean, dry print from a mature mushroom
- **Sterile working environment**: A still-air box, glove box, or clean room is strongly recommended
- **Alcohol lamp or lighter**: For flame sterilization of tools
- **Isopropyl alcohol (70%)**: For [[challenge-microorganisms-microwave-surface-sterilization]] of the print, syringes, and workspace

### The Syringe Preparation Procedure

The PF TEK procedure for making spore syringes:

1. **Sterilize water**: Boil distilled water for 10 minutes and allow to cool in a covered container, or pressure-sterilize
2. **Sterilize the work area**: Wipe all surfaces with 70% isopropyl alcohol. Work in a still-air environment with minimal air currents.
3. **Flame-sterilize the syringe needle**: Heat the needle tip until glowing red in the alcohol flame, then allow to cool
4. **Draw sterile water**: Pull the plunger to draw 10–12 cc of sterile water into the syringe
5. **Scrape spores from the print**: Using the sterile needle tip, gently scrape spores from the print surface into a small pile
6. **Suspend spores in water**: Expel the sterile water onto the spore pile, then draw the suspension back into the syringe. Repeat this several times to ensure thorough mixing.
7. **Cap the syringe**: Replace the needle cap or attach a fresh sterile cap
8. **Label**: Clearly label the syringe with species, strain, date, and any other relevant information

### Sterility Considerations

The most critical points for maintaining sterility during syringe preparation:

- **Water must be truly sterile**: Boiling alone may not kill all bacterial endospores; [[boil-a-bag-pressure-sterilization-technique]] at 15 PSI for 15 minutes is more reliable
- **Needle must be flame-sterilized** before every contact with the print or water
- **The print surface should be sterilized** before scraping — wipe lightly with alcohol and allow to dry
- **Minimize open-air exposure**: Work quickly and in a draft-free environment
- **Do not touch the needle** to any non-sterile surface after flame sterilization

## Spore Syringe Storage and Viability

### Storage Conditions

Proper storage extends the useful life of spore syringes:

- **Short-term** (weeks to months): Room temperature storage in a dark place is adequate
- **Long-term** (months to years): Refrigeration at 2–8°C significantly extends viability
- **Freezing**: Spore syringes can be frozen for long-term storage, though some viability loss occurs with each freeze-thaw cycle
- **Avoid heat**: Temperatures above 80°F accelerate spore degradation
- **Avoid light**: Prolonged light exposure may reduce viability over time

### Viability Testing

Before using a stored syringe for a full grow, viability can be tested by:

- Inoculating a single test jar and observing for mycelial growth within 3–7 days
- Examining the syringe contents under a microscope — viable spores appear as dark, oval to lemon-shaped structures
- Shaking the syringe — viable spore suspensions appear evenly cloudy; dead syringes may show clumping or clearing

## Legal Considerations

The PF TEK noted that while *Psilocybe cubensis* mushrooms contain the controlled substance psilocybin, the **spores themselves do not contain psilocybin** (spores are produced before the mushroom synthesizes the active compounds). In the United States at the time of publication, there were no federal laws prohibiting the possession or distribution of [[african-psilocybin-mushroom-guide]] spores, though several states had enacted spore-specific legislation. The PF TEK distributed spores and documentation "for scientific and entheogenic research only."

This legal distinction between spores and mature mushrooms created the commercial environment that supported the PF TEK's distribution model and enabled the broader internet-based spore trade that developed in subsequent decades.
