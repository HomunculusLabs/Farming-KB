## Mushroom Reproduction and the Role of Spores

### Spore Biology

Mushrooms reproduce sexually through the production and dispersal of **spores**:

- A single mature mushroom cap can produce **millions to billions** of spores
- Spores are microscopic (typically 10–20 micrometers) and are dispersed by air currents
- Each spore is a single cell containing half the genetic material of the parent (haploid)
- When a spore germinates, it produces a monokaryotic mycelium with a single nucleus per cell
- Two compatible monokaryotic mycelia must fuse to form a dikaryotic mycelium capable of producing mushrooms
- This is why multispore inoculation produces [[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]*)

### The Spore Print Method

The PF TEK spore print procedure:

1. **Harvest the mushroom**: Cut or twist the stem from the cake, leaving the cap intact
2. **Prepare a clean surface**: Use a sheet of clean white paper, aluminum foil, or glass for the print surface. Some cultivators pre-sterilize the surface with alcohol.
3. **Place the cap**: Set the cap gill-side-down on the printing surface. If the cap has a long stem, trim it close to the gills.
4. **Cover**: Place a clean glass or bowl over the cap to reduce airflow and contamination while allowing minimal gas exchange
5. **Wait**: Leave undisturbed for 6–24 hours. During this time, the cap will drop spores onto the surface in a pattern that mirrors the gill structure.
6. **Remove the cap**: Carefully lift the cap away. A visible deposit of spores (dark purple-brown) should remain on the printing surface.
7. **Dry and store**: Allow the print to dry completely (several hours to overnight), then fold or roll the paper/foil and store in a sealed bag with desiccant.

### Print Quality Assessment

A good spore print should show:

- **Even, dense deposit**: Spores covering the full area beneath the gills
- **Deep color**: Dark purple-brown to black in *Psilocybe* species
- **Clean appearance**: No visible mold growth, discoloration, or contamination on the print
- **Dry texture**: Fully dried prints store better and are less prone to contamination
