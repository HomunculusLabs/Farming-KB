## Permaculture Hardware Manufacturing and Communications Systems

A significant portion of the [[permaculture-design]] course addresses the practical infrastructure needed to support a self-reliant community: the physical tools, manufacturing systems, and communications networks that transform [[permaculture]] from theory into a functioning alternative economy.

## Distributed Manufacturing Philosophy

The central manufacturing principle is local production within range of users. Mollison explicitly rejects export-oriented manufacturing: "In every case, we make it our policy to manufacture within range of the users." This means:

- A plow should be on hire for a district rather than owned by every farm, since a person only needs it for half an hour to two hours at a time
- Well-off clients should be encouraged to install processing facilities that serve their entire district
- There is no point in having 5,000 husking systems operating in parallel when one shared facility could serve all

## The Yeoman's Plow Model

[[geoff-wallace]]'s plow demonstrates the distributed manufacturing approach. Production is split across multiple small operators:

1. **Component manufacturers**: One person makes the shoe, another makes the shank
2. **Frame production**: An unemployed group produces frames, ten at a time
3. **Assembly**: A primary manufacturer bolts components together
4. **Distribution**: Permaculture designers serve as distributors, carrying only brochures
5. **Pre-order financing**: Clients pre-order, financing the production run from retail payments

The system can be started with no capital. If manufacturing rights are available from the original designer, production begins immediately. If not, a similar tool can be developed independently.

## Small-Scale Hardware Categories

Mollison identifies two categories of hardware needed for permaculture self-sufficiency:

### Retrofit Materials
- Glass (for greenhouses and solar applications)
- Ducting and ventilation components
- Squirrel cage fans
- Automatic opening vents
- Sealants and insulation

The approach to acquiring these is straightforward: review solar catalogs, identify critical items, then approach manufacturers for distribution, retail, or ideally manufacture rights.

### Processing Hardware (The Critical Gap)
Mollison identifies processing hardware as "the big missing category." While production tools (chippers, plows, seeders, baggers) are available, processing equipment remains scarce. Needed items include:

- **Pressers**: For oil extraction, fruit pressing
- **Oil seed extractors**: For producing cooking oils and fuels from plant seeds
- **Juicers**: For food processing
- **Stills**: Both bench stills (made from ordinary or pitted glass) and field stills portable enough to carry into fields for crude distillation of up to a ton of material (e.g., mint oils)
- **Dryers**: For food and herb preservation
- **Husker-grinders**: Mollison considers the Ripple Flow the only truly good one he has seen; others are slow, heat-generating, or energy-intensive

The grain cleaner and seed cleaner—both for threshing and cleaning—are items "someone may need to reinvent." If permaculture can obtain manufacturing rights to the husker-grinder, it becomes available to the entire network immediately through local production.

## Shallow Precast Concrete Tanks

Tanks are identified as a critically lacking hardware item in America. The most useful specifications are:

- **Depth**: 15 to 18 inches
- **Diameter**: 4 to 6 feet (reachable to center)
- **Material**: Precast concrete (best for this purpose)
- **Drain**: Not needed—once full, tanks cannot be moved
- **Weight when full**: Approximately 1,000 pounds
- **Production**: Easily made from molds, suitable for small group manufacturing

**Applications:**
- Bulb growing: Lay one inch of water over saturated soil for ideal bulb conditions
- Pond creation: With 6 to 12 inches of soil depth
- Stock watering: Can be ramped for small stock access
- Poultry watering
- Aquaculture nurseries: A single nursery might use 40 to 50 tanks, supporting catfish, tadpoles, and small fish

## Vehicle Strategy

The Tagari community's vehicle approach demonstrates institutional purchasing power:

1. **Fleet registration**: 51 consultants' 17 Toyotas registered as a fleet
2. **Manufacturer negotiation**: Toyota offered 15% off list price for the fleet arrangement
3. **Tax exemption**: The Institute's public trust status eliminates sales tax
4. **Custom modifications**: The community's design engineering group develops fuel efficiency improvements and alcohol-gas conversion kits for Toyota vehicles
5. **Tractor adaptation**: Small 8 to 20 horsepower tractors are recommended for clients transitioning from grassland to forest, useful for creating tree-planting mounds and [[building-soil]]

## Nursery Design Standards

Permaculture nurseries should be functional, with plants arranged in functional groups rather than taxonomic order. Key considerations:

- **Aquaculture nurseries**: Use stock ponds rather than throwing all species into a single pond, which results in one dominant species
- **Plant labeling**: All plants sold should indicate whether they are rampant and how to manage their growth
- **Standard designs**: A standard nursery design can be distributed through the institute, adapted locally for each region
- **Functional grouping**: Plants in the permaculture section are organized by their role in systems (bee forages, poultry forages, windbreak species, etc.)

## The "Hand-Off" Manufacturing Concept

Small, simple items can be manufactured by sheltered workshops or community groups and "handed off" once production is established. The twigger—a piece of bent wire used in gardening—is an example: invented by the community, given to a sheltered workshop group in Tasmania who were perfectly capable of cutting wire and bending it. Once production is running smoothly, the item is listed in the seed catalog for distribution.

## Ham Radio Computer Network

A $300 package for Apple computers, coupled with ham radio licenses, creates a global communications network:

- **Stored queries**: Species lists, design data, and other information stored centrally
- **Instant retrieval**: A request for a bee plant list for specific conditions returns results within two seconds
- **Automatic accounting**: The computer records the requester's name and charges their account
- **No paperwork**: All transactions are electronic, no lists are mailed
- **Message relay**: Important national messages can be broadcast to all stations simultaneously
- **Asynchronous operation**: If a station operator is not present, messages are stored and delivered when they return

### The Fast Tape Copying Service

The Down to Earth Association provides rapid tape duplication of important messages, which are then distributed to media contacts on both government and private sides. These contacts incorporate the messages into their programs, reaching broad audiences through public access broadcasting.

## Local Radio Programs

Weekly half-hour permaculture radio programs in Australia reach 6 to 12 million listeners. These serve multiple functions:

- Public education about [[permaculture-principles]] and practices
- Mobilization of political action on environmental issues
- Building the movement's public profile and credibility
- Creating feedback loops where listeners form action groups independently

## The National Telephone Network

Six key telephone points across Australia link on a weekly schedule through a person who spends one evening per week coordinating. The system works as follows:

1. A person with a message contacts their local key point
2. The key point contacts central, which broadcasts to all six points
3. Each key point has local dissemination groups that distribute to their areas
4. The entire national network can be activated within hours

This system was used successfully to jam the Tasmanian Parliament switchboard for a week, forcing emergency legislation to stop aerial spraying.

## Integration of Manufacturing and Communications

The hardware and communications systems are deeply integrated. Manufacturing decisions (what to produce, where, by whom) are coordinated through the communications network. Market research happens through stored database queries. Pre-order financing for manufacturing runs flows through the accounting system. Political action to protect seed sovereignty, prevent spraying, or challenge legislation uses the same broadcast infrastructure that serves commercial and educational purposes.

The entire system operates on the principle that small, distributed, networked operations outperform large centralized ones—not because of efficiency alone, but because they build community capacity, maintain local control, and create resilience through redundancy.
