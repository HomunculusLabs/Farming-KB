# Global Distribution of Psilocybin Mushrooms

## Overview

Based on the comprehensive survey by Guzman, Allen, and Gartz (2000), 216 species of neurotropic fungi are known worldwide. These are divided into four groups: (1) species with psilocybin and related indoles (the vast majority), (2) species with ibotenic acid ([[amanita-muscaria-guide]] and allies), (3) ergot fungi, and (4) species used as sacred fungi without confirmed chemical analysis. The psilocybin-containing group alone encompasses ~190 species across 13 genera.

## Distribution by Region

### Mexico -- The Richest Psilocybin Mycoflora

Mexico has the highest diversity of neurotropic fungi in the world:

- **76 total species**, of which **44 are Psilocybe** (39% of the world total)
- Indigenous use of sacred mushrooms is well documented among Mazatec, Mixtec, Zapotec, Nahuatl, Chinantec, and Chatino peoples
- Common names include "teonanacatl" (divine mushroom), "san isidros," "pajaritos" (little birds), and "derrumbes" (landslides)
- Species include P. mexicana, P. caerulescens, P. zapotecorum, P. cubensis, P. muliercula, and many others
- Use dates back at least to pre-Columbian times; "mushroom stones" from Guatemala suggest use as early as 1000 BCE

### Central and South America

- **Brazil:** Rich diversity including Psilocybe cubensis, P. caerulipes, and numerous Amazonian species
- **Colombia/Ecuador/Peru:** Psilocybe species in highland and lowland habitats
- **Colombia** and surrounding countries have significant diversity in tropical and subtropical forests
- Psilocybe cubensis is pantropical and found throughout the region

### North America (United States and Canada)

- **Pacific Northwest (Oregon, Washington, British Columbia):** Particularly rich; includes the extremely potent P. azurescens, P. cyanescens, P. stuntzii, and many Gymnopilus species
- **Gulf Coast and Southeast:** P. cubensis widespread in cow pastures from Texas to Florida
- **Northeast:** P. semilanceata (liberty cap) in pastures and meadows
- **Southwest:** P. cubensis, P. caerulescens along waterways
- Species found across the southern US from California to Florida

### Europe

- **British Isles:** P. semilanceata is widespread and common in pastures and grasslands; P. cyanescens found on wood chips
- **Continental Europe:** P. semilanceata widespread; P. cyanescens increasingly common in urban areas on mulched gardens; P. bohemica in central Europe
- **Iberian Peninsula:** P. hispanica, P. gallaeciae, and other species
- **Scandinavia:** P. semilanceata, P. crobula

### Asia and Oceania

- **Southeast Asia:** High diversity; P. cubensis widespread; numerous tropical species
- **Japan:** Several species documented
- **India and Nepal:** Himalayan species including P. kashmiriensis
- **Australia and New Zealand:** P. cubensis, P. subaeruginosa, P. cyanescens; Australian species are notably potent
- **Papua New Guinea:** Indigenous use of Psilocybe species among native peoples is well documented; sacred mushroom use parallels that of Mexico

### Africa

- **North Africa:** One documented species from Algeria (P. mairei)
- **Sub-Saharan Africa:** Limited documentation; likely under-collected
- Psilocybe cubensis occurs widely in tropical Africa

## Ecological Distribution Patterns

Key findings from Guzman et al. (2000):

- The majority of Psilocybe species are found in or near the **Austral hemisphere**, mainly in **subtropical humid forests**
- These forests also contain the best-documented indigenous groups using neurotropic fungi (Mexico, New Guinea)
- Psilocybin mushrooms are cosmopolitan but greatly outnumbered by non-psychoactive species in any given habitat
- In some parts of the world, psilocybin mushrooms had not been reported until recently (e.g., a new species discovered in Madagascar in the late 1990s, on the verge of extinction)
- Psilocybe cubensis has expanded its range through human activity (cattle ranching, spore dispersal)
- Stamets hypothesizes that psilocybin production evolved as a competitive evolutionary strategy -- reducing insect predation through the production of a neurotropic compound

## Habitat Types

Six classic habitat types for psilocybin mushrooms (per Stamets):

1. **Dung** -- Psilocybe cubensis, Panaeolus species (tropical and subtropical pastures)
2. **Grassland and meadow** -- Psilocybe semilanceata, P. mexicana (temperate and subtropical)
3. **Enriched soils** -- Psilocybe tampanensis, P. caerulescens (along waterways, disturbed ground)
4. **Wood chips and mulch** -- Psilocybe cyanescens, P. allenii (urban/landscaped areas, temperate)
5. **Decaying wood** -- Gymnopilus, Pluteus, some Psilocybe species (forests worldwide)
6. **Tropical forest floor** -- Numerous species in undisturbed subtropical/tropical forests
