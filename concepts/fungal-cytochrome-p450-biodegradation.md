## Overview

Cytochrome P450 enzymes constitute a superfamily of monoxygenases that play a central role in the fungal biodegradation of recalcitrant organic compounds. These intracellular enzymes catalyze the initial oxidation of diverse xenobiotics — including aliphatic hydrocarbons, aromatic hydrocarbons, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), and phenolic compounds — making them critical entry points in [[fungal-enzyme-systems-in-bioremediation]] pathways. Unlike the extracellular ligninolytic enzymes of white-rot fungi, P450 systems operate inside fungal cells and are found across a broad taxonomic range of fungi.

## Enzyme System Architecture

The fungal cytochrome P450 monoxygenase system consists of two key components:

1. **Cytochrome P450 (terminal oxidase)**: A heme-containing enzyme that binds molecular oxygen and the substrate, catalyzing the insertion of one oxygen atom into the substrate molecule.
2. **NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase (CPR)**: An electron transfer protein that shuttles electrons from NADPH to the P450 enzyme, enabling its catalytic cycle.

This electron transfer chain is localized in the endoplasmic reticulum (microsomal fraction) of fungal cells. Both microsomal and cytosolic P450 isoforms have been identified in biodegrading fungi.

## Aliphatic Hydrocarbon Oxidation

### Monoterminal Oxidation Pathway

The most common pathway for n-alkane metabolism (C10–C20) involves cytochrome P450 as the terminal oxidase:

1. **P450 + NADPH-CPR** catalyzes terminal hydroxylation of the alkane to a primary alcohol
2. **Alcohol dehydrogenase** oxidizes the alcohol to an aldehyde
3. **Aldehyde dehydrogenase** oxidizes the aldehyde to a fatty acid
4. **Acyl-CoA synthetase** activates the fatty acid to acyl-CoA
5. **β-oxidation** cleaves the acyl-CoA to acetyl-CoA units

The acetyl-CoA then enters the TCA cycle for complete mineralization to CO2, or serves as an anabolic precursor for growth.

### Alkane-Inducible P450 Gene Families

In the alkane-assimilating yeast *Candida maltosa*, the P450alk gene family (ALK1–5, comprising 8 genes) encodes alkane-inducible cytochrome P450 enzymes (Ohkuma et al., 1998; Scheller et al., 1996). Isozyme function was revealed through sequential gene disruption. Similarly, *Yarrowia lipolytica* possesses the Y1ALK1 gene encoding an n-alkane-inducible P450, with peroxisomal acyl-CoA oxidase (ACO) isozymes supporting alkane catabolism (Wang et al., 1998, 1999b). Peroxisome proliferators activate cytochrome P450 genes in alkane-assimilating yeasts (Ohtomo et al., 1996).

## Aromatic Hydrocarbon Oxidation

The oxidation of aromatic hydrocarbons is initiated by cytochrome P450-dependent monoxygenases that catalyze epoxidation to arene oxides. This is the fungal equivalent of the well-characterized mammalian aromatic hydroxylation pathway (Smith and Rosazza, 1974). The arene oxide intermediate can then undergo multiple fates including rearrangement to phenols, hydration to trans-dihydrodiols via epoxide hydrolase, or conjugation reactions.

## PAH Degradation via P450

### Non-Ligninolytic Pathway

In non-white-rot fungi, cytochrome P450 monoxygenase catalyzes the initial attack on PAHs:

1. **P450 monoxygenase** oxidizes the PAH to an arene oxide
2. **Epoxide hydrolase** converts the arene oxide to a trans-dihydrodiol
3. **Phase II conjugation**: The dihydrodiol or phenolic intermediates are conjugated to glucuronides, glucosides, xylosides, or sulfates, forming water-soluble, non-mutagenic products
4. Phase I oxidative products (quinones, phenols) may also be formed and are typically bioactive and toxic

### Key Fungal Species and Studies

- ***Cunninghamella elegans***: The CPR gene was isolated from a genomic library (Yadav and Loper, 2000). The 2420-bp coding region contains two introns (149 bp and 138 bp). The CPR is transcriptionally induced by n-tetradecane and is more closely related to animal CPR than to yeast and plant homologs.
- ***Pleurotus ostreatus***: Oxidizes phenanthrene to trans-9,10-dihydroxy-9,10-dihydrophenanthrene. One atom of ¹⁸O₂ is incorporated, confirming monoxygenase involvement. P450 inhibitors reduce this reaction (Bezalel et al., 1996b,c).
- ***Phanerochaete chrysosporium***: Metabolizes phenanthrene to phenols and trans-dihydrodiols via P450-mediated reactions (Sutherland et al., 1991). A unique dual system exists — both intracellular P450 and extracellular ligninolytic enzymes (LiP, MnP, laccase) contribute to PAH degradation. The CYP63-1A gene was identified by RT-PCR as the first member of a new P450 family, expressed under both nitrogen-deficient and nitrogen-rich conditions (Kullman and Matsumura, 1997).
- ***Penicillium janthinellum***: Identified from petroleum-contaminated soils as a potent P450-mediated pyrene biotransformer (Launen et al., 1995).
- ***Crinipellis stipitaria***: Among the most potent pyrene-degrading basidiomycetes, using P450-initiated pathways to produce pyrene-1,2-oxide → 1-hydroxypyrene → sulfates, dihydroxypyrenes, and quinones.

### Kinetic Parameters

In *P. chrysosporium*, the Km values for benzo[a]pyrene hydroxylation were 89 μM for microsomal P450 and 400 μM for cytosolic P450 (Masaphy et al., 1996). Hydroxylation was NADPH-dependent and inhibited by carbon dioxide. Both P450 fractions produced a type I binding spectrum upon benzo[a]pyrene addition.

## Comparison with Extracellular Enzyme Systems

The [[enzymatic-degradation-in-mycoremediation]] landscape includes both intracellular P450 systems and extracellular ligninolytic enzymes:

| Feature | P450 Monoxygenase | Ligninolytic Enzymes |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Location | Intracellular (microsomal/cytosolic) | Extracellular |
| Substrate range | Broad (alkanes, aromatics, PAHs) | Phenolic/aromatic compounds |
| Mechanism | Monoxygenation (epoxidation, hydroxylation) | One-electron oxidation (radical formation) |
| Molecular weight specificity | Less constrained | Favored by higher MW PAHs |
| Oxygen requirement | Molecular O₂ + NADPH | H₂O₂ (peroxidases) or O₂ (laccase) |

Notably, some fungi like *P. chrysosporium* and *P. ostreatus* possess both systems, providing complementary pathways for comprehensive pollutant degradation. P450 handles initial activation, while ligninolytic enzymes can further oxidize metabolites and catalyze humification reactions that bind PAHs to soil organic matter.

## Significance for Bioremediation

The P450 pathway is ecologically significant because fungal polar and reactive metabolites can be mineralized or detoxified to innocuous compounds by indigenous soil bacteria. Unlike bacteria, which cannot efficiently degrade PAHs with more than four aromatic rings, fungi can oxidize PAHs with five and six rings through P450-mediated pathways. Lower molecular weight PAHs show 70–100% degradation rates, while higher molecular weight PAHs show 6–50% rates.
