## Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor): PSK and PSP in Cancer Treatment

## Overview

Trametes versicolor (syn. [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] demonstrated efficacy in cancers of the

## PSP (Polysaccharide-Peptide)

PSP was first isolated in 1983 from cultured deep-layer mycelium of the
COU-1 strain of T. versicolor (Yang, 1999). It contains at least four
discrete molecules, all true proteoglycans. Key differences from PSK: PSP
lacks fucose and contains arabinose and rhamnose. The polysaccharide chains
are true beta-glucans (mainly 1-4, 1-2, and 1-3 glucose linkages) plus
small amounts of galactose, mannose, and arabinose linkages. Molecular weight
is approximately 100,000 daltons, orally deliverable.

Clinical profile of PSP:

  - Powerful immunostimulant and anti-cancer agent
  - Extensively studied by Chinese scientists and oncologists
  - Phase II and III trials showed enhanced immune status in 70-97% of
    patients with stomach, oesophageal, lung, ovarian, and cervical cancers
