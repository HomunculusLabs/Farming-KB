# Fungal Ecosystem Services Framework

Fungi provide a wide range of ecosystem services that underpin the
functioning of terrestrial, freshwater, and [[gadd-fungal-carbon-sequestration]]. The
first law of thermodynamics indicates that matter can neither be created
nor destroyed; fungi transform elements between organic and inorganic
pools, driving the nutrient cycles that sustain all life.

## Soil Formation Services

further weather minerals. Fungal hyphae physically bind soil particles
into aggregates, establishing the soil structure necessary for plant
establishment and water retention.

## Soil Fertility Maintenance

Decomposition of organic residues by saprotrophic fungi (including
ericoid and ectomycorrhizal species) and the consequent nutrient
mineralization are perhaps the most fundamental fungal ecosystem
service. Fungi produce [[glomeromycota-arbuscular-mycorrhizae]]
and hyphal enmeshment of soil particles.

## Primary Production Support

Fungi support primary production through direct production by lichens
[[fungal-lichen-stress-tolerance-and-poikilohydry]], and herbivore resistance.

## Secondary Production as Food Source

Fungi serve as a food source for diverse animal groups. As secondary
production, fruit bodies and mycelia are consumed by vertebrates and
invertebrates. The fungal biomass in soil supports populations of
collembola, mites, nematodes, and other microarthropods. In aquatic
ecosystems, fungal-colonized leaf litter supports shredder
invertebrate populations. Population and biomass regulation by
pathogenic fungi affects the dynamics of plant and animal
populations across all ecosystem types.
