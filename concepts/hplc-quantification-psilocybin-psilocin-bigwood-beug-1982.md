# HPLC Quantification of Psilocybin and Psilocin

## Overview

Bigwood and Beug's 1982 study used reversed-phase high performance liquid
chromatography (HPLC) to quantify psilocybin and psilocin levels in
*[[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]].

## Extraction Procedure

The study followed the extraction procedure described in their previous
paper (Beug and Bigwood, 1982), which analyzed psilocybin and psilocin
levels across twenty species from seven genera of wild mushrooms in the
[[aminopropyl-silica-gel-chromatography-indole-alkaloid-purification]] fraction for chromatographic
analysis.

## Sample Preparation

Mushrooms were harvested as soon as sporocarps were mature, then:

1. **Freeze-dried** immediately to preserve alkaloid content
2. **Sealed in plastic** to prevent moisture absorption
3. **Stored at −5°C** until analysis to prevent degradation
4. **Voucher specimens** were prepared and deposited in the University
   of Washington Herbarium (WTU) for taxonomic verification

The use of freeze-drying rather than air-drying or oven-drying was
critical — heat can degrade psilocin more rapidly than psilocybin,
potentially skewing results if less careful drying methods are used.

## HPLC Method

### Instrumentation

- **Reversed-phase HPLC**: The standard approach for indole alkaloid
  separation, using a non-polar stationary phase with a polar mobile
  phase
- **Detection**: UV absorption at wavelengths specific to the indole
  ring system of psilocybin and psilocin
- **Quantification**: Hewlett-Packard 8380A reporting integrator-plotter
  calibrated against standards from the National Institute on Drug
  Abuse (NIDA)

### Calibration and Linearity

The HPLC system demonstrated a linear relationship between
concentration and peak area across the range of 0.2 to 3 μg total
psilocybin or psilocin, with approximately **10% repeatability**. This
means:

- Quantitative comparisons between samples are reliable within ±10%
- The method is suitable for detecting the roughly four-fold variation
  in psilocybin levels observed across controlled cultures
- It is sensitive enough to distinguish psilocin levels that are
  near-zero in early flushes from the higher levels seen in later
  flushes

### Detection Limit

The detection limit was approximately **0.01 μg** for both psilocybin
and psilocin, allowing detection of trace alkaloid levels even in
samples where psilocin was essentially absent (such as first-flush
samples that showed 0 mg/g psilocin).

## TLC Confirmation

HPLC results were qualitatively confirmed using thin-layer
chromatography (TLC) with a butanol-acetic acid-water (12:3:5) mobile
phase. This dual-method approach provided confidence that the HPLC peaks
identified as psilocybin and psilocin were correctly assigned and not
confused with co-eluting compounds.

TLC confirmation is important because mushroom extracts contain
numerous indole compounds including baeocystin, norbaeocystin, and
tryptamine derivatives that could potentially interfere with
identification if only one analytical method were used.
