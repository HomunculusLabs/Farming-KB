# Fruit Forest Establishment

Using seedlings as rootstocks for fruit trees is Sepp Holzer's very simple, economical, and practically risk-free method of cultivating a lush fruit forest or orchard.

## Soil Preparation

Fruit trees generally prefer high-quality soil. Begin by preparing the area using soil-improving plants (green manure). On the Krameterhof, acid soil where spruce forests once grew took around two years to improve to the point where fruit trees and other demanding plants could thrive without additional support. Green manure is not a one-off measure — it must play a continuous role in cultivation because fertile and healthy soil is the key to success.

## Seed Source

The best and most economical source of seeds is pomace (the pulp left over from pressing fruit for juice or cider). The material left over from the must when distilling schnapps will also work if the seeds can be separated after heating. Leave the pomace to ferment for around four to five weeks, then distribute it over the area. During fermentation the layers that naturally delay germination are broken down — this stratification greatly increases the seeds' chance for success.

## Planting Process

1. **Loosen the soil**: Graze pigs in the area — they dig over and loosen the soil
2. **Distribute fermented pomace**: This provides the seed source
3. **Protect from browsing**: Fence the area off so it can also be used as a paddock
4. **Allow growth**: Trees grow in their intended location from the start, adapting themselves to soil and climatic conditions

## Grafting

After one to two years of growth, select the best trees for [[fruit-tree-grafting-guide|grafting]]. This yields optimal plants for the location. Trees growing too close together can be replanted. Not all seedlings need to be grafted — wild fruit seedlings can produce interesting fruit, particularly sweet fruit suited to schnapps.

## Advantages

- Very simple and economical
- Particularly well suited to "unfavourable areas" because trees have time to adjust from seedlings
- Requires very little effort and practically no risk
- Could turn a spruce forest into a fruit forest using this technique
- Wild fruit hedges serve as windbreaks and provide habitat for useful animals and insects

## Supporting Plants

When planting fruit trees on new terraces, Holzer sows various supporting plants around them (sunflowers, Jerusalem artichokes, buckwheat, oilseed rape, scorpion weed, among others) which improve growing conditions as green manure crops and also provide grazing opportunities for deer as distraction plants.
