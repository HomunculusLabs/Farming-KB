# Panaeolus Subbalteatus Profile: The Banded Mottlegill

## Overview and Taxonomy

Panaeolus subbalteatus, currently more commonly referred to as
Panaeolus cinctulus, is a widely distributed psilocybin-containing
mushroom in the family Bolbitiaceae. Commonly known as the Banded
Mottlegill or the Weed Panaeolus, this species was one of the first
psychoactive mushrooms to be identified and studied in Europe. The
species epithet "subbalteatus" refers to the sub-belted or banded
appearance of the cap margin, while "cinctulus" means "belted,"
reflecting the same characteristic feature.

The taxonomic history of this species has been complex. Originally
described as Agaricus subbalteatus by Berkeley and Broome in 1871,
it has been transferred through several genera before settling in
Panaeolus. The synonym Panaeolus cinctulus has gained preference in
recent mycological literature, though both names remain in common
use. The [[psilocybin-mushroom-image-guide-habitat-clues]] documents specimens from both wild collection
and [[psilocybe-cubensis-cattle-dung-substrate-introduction-americas-guzman-1998]], using both names.

## Visual Characteristics

Panaeolus subbalteatus is a medium-sized mushroom with several
[[metzner-three-distinctive-features-shamanic-entheogenic-ceremonies]]. The cap (pileus) measures 1.5 to 4 centimeters
in diameter, starting hemispheric or convex and expanding to broadly
convex or nearly plane with a slight umbo. The cap surface is smooth
and hygrophanous, typically appearing dark reddish-brown to
cinnamon-brown when moist, with a distinctive lighter band or zone
near the margin that gives the species its common name. As the cap
dries, it fades to a pale buff or tan color, often with the banded
zone becoming more pronounced.

The gills are adnate to adnexed, crowded, and exhibit the
characteristic "mottled" appearance typical of the genus Panaeolus.
This mottling results from the uneven maturation of spores, with
some gill faces showing a mix of dark (mature) and light (immature)
patches. This feature is a key diagnostic for the genus. The gills
start gray and darken to black as spores mature.

The stem (stipe) is 5 to 12 centimeters long and 2 to 4
millimeters thick, cylindrical, and hollow. It is reddish-brown at
the base, becoming lighter toward the apex, and may show a faint
annular zone from the evanescent partial veil. The stem does not
typically bruise blue significantly, though some collections may
show very subtle bluing at the base. For more on bruising patterns,
see psilocybin-mushroom-image-guide-spore-color-chart for comparison.

## Geographic Distribution and Habitat

Panaeolus subbalteatus has an exceptionally wide distribution and is
found on every continent except Antarctica. In [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], it
occurs across much of the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] and Canada. In Europe, it is
particularly common and well-documented. The image guide includes
photographic specimens from Germany, specifically from the Black
Forest region, where specimens were found growing on horse dung.

This species exhibits a strong preference for nutrient-rich substrates,
particularly herbivore dung (especially horse and cow manure), composted
materials, and enriched soils. It is frequently found in pastures,
gardens, compost piles, and occasionally on lawns that have been
fertilized with manure. Unlike some dung-inhabiting species, P.
subbalteatus can also fruit on rich soil without direct dung contact.

Fruiting occurs from spring through autumn, with peak seasons varying
by region. In temperate zones, it is most commonly found from May
through October. The species can fruit prolifically after periods of
rain. For broader habitat information, see
psilocybin-mushroom-image-guide-lookalike-species and
[[psilocybin-mushroom-species-overview]].
