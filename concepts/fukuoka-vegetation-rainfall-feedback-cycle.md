## The Conventional View vs. Fukuoka's Observation

Standard climatology explains desertification as the result of atmospheric
circulation patterns, continental positioning, rain shadow effects, and global
temperature gradients. In this framework, vegetation loss is treated as a
consequence of changing rainfall, not a cause. Fukuoka reversed this causality
entirely, arguing that the relationship between vegetation and rainfall is
bidirectional and self-reinforcing: when vegetation cover is maintained, local
microclimates generate moisture through transpiration, evaporation from soil,
and the creation of atmospheric conditions favorable to condensation and
precipitation.

## The Desertification Spiral

Fukuoka described desertification as a self-reinforcing spiral. When vegetation
is removed through overgrazing, deforestation, or intensive agriculture, the
local moisture cycle is disrupted. Without plant transpiration, atmospheric
humidity drops. Soil exposed to direct sunlight heats rapidly and dries out.
The dry soil cannot retain what little moisture arrives. As the soil dries
further, remaining plants struggle and die, accelerating the loss of vegetative
cover. Each increment of vegetation loss reduces the probability of rainfall,
which in turn further stresses remaining vegetation.
