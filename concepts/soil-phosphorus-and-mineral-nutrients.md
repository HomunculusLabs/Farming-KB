## Soil Phosphorus and Mineral Nutrients

This section covers four critical soil elements — magnesium, aluminium, silicon, and phosphorus — that govern plant metabolism, soil structure, and long-term agricultural sustainability. Phosphorus in particular emerges as one of the most strategically important elements for global food security.

## Magnesium (Mg)

Magnesium is found with calcium in **dolomite** and is needed by all plants. It forms the **central atom of the chlorophyll molecule**, activates enzymes, and concentrates in seeds. Like nitrogen, it is very mobile in plant tissue.

### Soil Availability

Magnesium is likely to be deficient only in sandy soils. It is present in the clay fraction of soils and is released by soil acids and humus. Sources include:

- Dolomite application on farmlands
- Dolomite tablets for animals
- Mixed into animal feedstuff

### Balancing with Calcium

Magnesium carbonate from dolomite buffers plant and soil acids, as does lime, but it can cause plant poisoning if not balanced by high calcium (lime) levels. **Do not add too much dolomite if clay soils already contain adequate magnesium reserves.**

### Human Health

Magnesium is essential for chlorophyll production and for the mitochondria of human cells, hence energy metabolism. It is present in all green plants. Deficiencies do not normally occur in healthy people, but when clinically required it is administered as intravenous fluid at 10 millimoles per kilogram bodyweight.

## Aluminium (Al)

Aluminium is described as a **"Jekyll and Hyde" element** — it forms a large part of soils and red clays (with iron) or as poorer bauxitic dryland deposits, yet becomes poisonous under certain conditions.

### Toxicity Pathways

Aluminium sulphate forms in acid rain or sulphur-polluted soils (common in industrial areas). It is also released when cooking onions or other acidic foods in aluminium cookware. Heavy aluminium-silica uptake in humans may assist development of **senile dementia**; adequate calcium in the soil and as dietary supplements provides partial defence.

### Soil Tolerance

- **0.1–1.0 ppm**: Tolerable soluble aluminium
- **Above 1.0 ppm**: Susceptible plants start to die
- **15 ppm or higher**: Many plants die

Some areas are naturally high in acid-aluminium soils, requiring calcium amendments in gardens and even in rainwater tanks (as marble or limestone chips, whole sea shells, or lime gravels).

### Practical Advice

**Avoid aluminium utensils** for cooking salty, acidic, or sulphurous substances (vinegar, onions). Use iron, enamel, stone, or steel cookware instead.
