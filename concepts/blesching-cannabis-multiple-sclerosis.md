The 26 studies on MS represent one of the largest bodies of evidence for any single condition in the [[cbd-therapeutic-effects]]. The scientific community began to build on the data accumulated. The therapeutic frame of cannabis in the context of MS became more clear and defined.

### Key Therapeutic Benefits

A reduction in spasticity was recognized as only the first of many benefits. Cannabis use also reduced:

- Pain and neuropathic pains
- Depression and anxiety
- Paresthesia
- Sleep disturbances
- Urge incontinence
- Nocturnal polyuria (frequent nighttime urination)

### Neuroprotective Properties

Even the U.S. federal government issued itself a patent on the neuroprotective properties of cannabinoids employed in the treatment of several diseases involving inflammation and the nervous system, such as MS.

### Overall Assessment

While the 26 studies differ in approach, scope, and methodology, the results share a common denominator. For patients suffering from MS, cannabis may be a potent ally in alleviating the disease's symptoms. Furthermore, cannabinoids may slow the progression of the illness itself and thus provide an improvement to long-term survival and quality of life.

## Selected Key Studies

### Sativex (THC:CBD) -- Walton Centre, UK (2007)

66 patients with MS received THC/CBD via oromucosal spray. THC/CBD was effective in reducing pains with no evidence of tolerance for the 2 years tested.

### Sativex -- Royal Berkshire, UK (2007)

189 patients with MS showed a reduction in spasms with daily THC:CBD administration.

### Cannabis Extract, THC, or Placebo -- Derriford Hospital, UK (2006)

630 MS patients were studied. Both cannabis extract and THC showed significant reductions in urge incontinence.

### Meta-Analysis -- Hannover, Germany (2005)

A meta-analysis of human, animal, and laboratory studies concluded: "there is reasonable evidence for the therapeutic employment of cannabinoids in the treatment of MS-related symptoms."

### Sativex Meta-Analysis (2005)

In some trials, THC:CBD spray significantly reduced neuropathic pain, spasticity, muscle spasms, and sleep disturbances in 368 human patients with various neurological disorders including MS.

### Endocannabinoid Levels in MS Patients -- Perugia, Italy (2008)

50 patients with MS and 20 control subjects were studied. Significantly reduced levels of all tested endocannabinoids were found in the cerebrospinal fluid of patients with MS compared to control subjects, suggesting endocannabinoid deficiency in MS.
