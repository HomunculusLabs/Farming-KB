# Psilocybin Biosynthetic Metabolic Pathway

The biosynthesis of psilocybin in mushrooms follows a multi-step
metabolic pathway beginning with primary metabolic precursors and
proceeding through the Shikimate pathway to produce the indole
alkaloids psilocybin and psilocin. Understanding this pathway is
essential for scientific investigation of psilocybin production,
including efforts to optimize cultivation conditions for potency
and to understand the evolutionary origins of these compounds in
fungal metabolism.

## Primary Precursor Pathways

Both starting materials for psilocybin biosynthesis are ultimately
derived from glucose. Glucose is phosphorylated by ATP (converting
to ADP) to form Glucose-6-phosphate, which then feeds into two
parallel metabolic pathways:

**Glycolysis:** Produces phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) as an
intermediate byproduct. PEP serves as one of the two starting
substrates for the Shikimate pathway.

**Pentose Phosphate Pathway:** Produces erythrose-4-phosphate
(E4P) as a byproduct via a transketolase enzyme. E4P serves as
the second substrate for the Shikimate pathway.

These two pathways link basic carbohydrate metabolism to the
production of aromatic amino acids and, ultimately, psychedelic
compounds. The Shikimate pathway is present in plants, fungi, and
bacteria but is notably absent in animals, which is why these
organisms must obtain aromatic amino acids through their diet.

## The Shikimate Pathway

The Shikimate pathway occurs in the cytosol of fungal cells and
proceeds through the following enzymatic steps:

1. PEP + E4P, catalyzed by DAHP Synthase, produces
   7-phospho-2-dehydro-3-deoxy-D-arabino-heptonate.
2. 3-Dehydroquinate synthase produces dehydroquinate.
3. 3-Dehydroquinate dehydratase produces dehydroshikimate.
4. Shikimate 5-dehydrogenase produces shikimate.
5. Shikimate kinase produces shikimate-3-phosphate.
6. EPSP synthase produces 5-O-(1-carboxyvinyl)-3-phosphoshikimate.
7. Chorismate synthase produces chorismate.

Chorismate is a critical metabolic branch point, feeding into
five different pathways including the synthesis of folate,
ubiquinone, phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan.

## Tryptophan Biosynthesis Branch

From chorismate, the pathway proceeds toward tryptophan:

1. 2-Deoxyisochorismate synthase acts on chorismate.
2. Anthranilate synthase produces 2-amino-2-deoxychorismate,
   then anthranilate.
3. Anthranylate-phosphoribosyl transferase produces
   N-(5'-phosphoribosyl) anthranilate.
4. Isomerase and indole-3-glycerolphosphate synthase steps
   produce indole-3-glycerolphosphate.
5. Tryptophan synthase (alpha subunit) produces indole.
6. Tryptophan synthase (beta subunit) produces tryptophan.

## Conversion to Tryptamine and Beyond

Tryptophan is decarboxylated by tryptophan decarboxylase to form
tryptamine, the immediate precursor to psilocybin and psilocin.
This is the last well-characterized step in the pathway and is
subject to self-feedback inhibition. When too much tryptamine or
related compounds accumulate, the enzyme slows or stops converting
tryptophan.

Known inhibitors of tryptophan decarboxylase include N,N-dimethyl-
tryptamine (65% inhibition), indole-3-acetic acid (60%), tryptamine
itself (62%), 5-hydroxytryptamine (45%), and indole-3-acetaldehyde
(50%). Non-inhibitors include 5-methoxy-DMT and 5-methoxytryptamine,
suggesting specific substrate binding requirements.

## Tryptamine to Psilocybin: The Unresolved Steps

The conversion of tryptamine to psilocybin and psilocin remains
partially characterized. The pathway involves hydroxylation and
methylation steps, with phosphorylated intermediates baeocystin
and norbaeocystin produced alongside psilocybin. Psilocybin is
likely produced from psilocin via a phosphorylase enzyme. The
experiments by Gartz (1989) on tryptamine-supplemented substrates
showed increased psilocin relative to psilocybin, suggesting
downregulation of the phosphorylase step under conditions of
excess tryptamine availability.

Recent molecular biology work has identified four key enzymes in
the psilocybin biosynthesis pathway: PsiD (a decarboxylase), PsiH
(a monooxygenase), PsiK (a methyltransferase), and PsiM (a
hydroxylase). The order of enzymatic steps has been determined:
tryptophan is first decarboxylated to tryptamine by PsiD, then
hydroxylated at the 4-position by PsiH to form 4-hydroxytryptamine,
then phosphorylated by PsiK to form baeocystin, and finally
N-methylated by PsiM to produce psilocybin. This pathway was
confirmed through heterologous expression in E. coli and
Aspergillus nidulans by researchers at the University of Tuebingen.

## Enzyme Regulation and Feedback

The biosynthetic pathway is subject to multiple levels of
regulation. Tryptophan decarboxylase exhibits product inhibition,
slowing conversion when tryptamine accumulates. The hydroxylation
and methylation steps appear to have broad substrate specificity,
as demonstrated by Gartz's experiments showing conversion of
exogenous tryptamine. The phosphorylation step may be limited
by phosphate availability, which could explain the shift from
psilocybin to psilocin dominance under conditions of excess
tryptamine supplementation.

Understanding these regulatory mechanisms is important for
both natural cultivation optimization and potential biotechnological
applications such as heterologous production in microbial systems.
