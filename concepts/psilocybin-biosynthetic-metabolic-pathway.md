# Psilocybin Biosynthetic Metabolic Pathway

The biosynthesis of psilocybin in mushrooms follows a multi-step metabolic pathway beginning with primary metabolic precursors and proceeding through the Shikimate pathway to produce the indole alkaloids psilocybin and psilocin. Understanding this pathway is essential for scientific investigation of psilocybin production, including efforts to optimize cultivation conditions for potency and to understand the evolutionary origins of these compounds in fungal metabolism.

## Primary Precursor Pathways

Both starting materials for psilocybin biosynthesis are ultimately derived from glucose. Glucose is phosphorylated by ATP (converting to ADP) to form Glucose-6-phosphate, which then feeds into two parallel metabolic pathways:

**Glycolysis:** Produces phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) as an intermediate byproduct. PEP serves as one of the two starting substrates for the Shikimate pathway.

**Pentose Phosphate Pathway:** Produces erythrose-4-phosphate (E4P) as a byproduct via a transketolase enzyme. E4P serves as the second substrate for the Shikimate pathway.

These two pathways link basic carbohydrate metabolism to the production of aromatic amino acids and, ultimately, psychedelic compounds. The Shikimate pathway is present in plants, fungi, and bacteria but is notably absent in animals, which is why these organisms must obtain aromatic amino acids through their diet.

## The Shikimate Pathway

The Shikimate pathway occurs in the cytosol of fungal cells and proceeds through the following enzymatic steps:

1. PEP + E4P, catalyzed by DAHP Synthase, produces 7-phospho-2-dehydro-3-deoxy-D-arabino-heptonate.
2. 3-Dehydroquinate synthase produces dehydroquinate.
3. 3-Dehydroquinate dehydratase produces dehydroshikimate.
4. Shikimate 5-dehydrogenase produces shikimate.
5. Shikimate kinase produces shikimate-3-phosphate.
6. EPSP synthase produces 5-O-(1-carboxyvinyl)-3-phosphoshikimate.
7. Chorismate synthase produces chorismate.

Chorismate is a critical branch-point metabolite that feeds into multiple downstream pathways. In psilocybin biosynthesis, chorismate is converted to prephenate, which then serves as the precursor for the aromatic amino acid L-tryptophan. ### Step 3: N,N-Dimethylation

4-hydroxytryptamine undergoes two successive methyl transfer reactions catalyzed by the enzyme **methyltransferase** (PsiM). Using S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) as the methyl donor, the enzyme first transfers one methyl group to the amine nitrogen, producing 4-hydroxy-N-methyltryptamine, and then transfers a second methyl group to produce **4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine** (4-HO-DMT, also known as **baeocystin** as the monomethyl intermediate and **psilocin** as the final dimethylated product).

### Step 4: Phosphorylation

Psilocin is phosphorylated at the 4-hydroxy group by the enzyme **4-hydroxytryptamine kinase** (PsiK), using ATP as the phosphate donor, to produce **psilocybin** (4-PO-DMT, or 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine). This final step converts the active compound psilocin into its more stable, phosphorylated prodrug form. The phosphate group makes psilocybin significantly more water-soluble and stable during storage within the fungal tissue.

## Enzyme Discovery and Genetic Characterization

The complete enzymatic pathway was elucidated in 2017 by a research team led by Janis Fricke, Felix Blei, and Dirk Hoffmeister at the Hans Knöll Institute in Jena, Germany. Using genome mining, heterologous expression in E. coli and Aspergillus nidulans, and enzymatic assays, the team identified the four core enzymes: PsiD, PsiH, PsiM, and PsiK.

This discovery had several important implications:

- It confirmed that the entire pathway can operate with just four enzymes, making it relatively compact compared to many other fungal secondary metabolic pathways.
- It enabled the first successful biotechnological production of psilocybin in engineered microorganisms, opening the door to sustainable pharmaceutical production without cultivation of the mushroom itself.
- It provided genetic markers that could be used to identify psilocybin-producing species and to study the distribution of the pathway across the [[light-basidiocarp-initiation-psilocybe-cubensis-research]] has demonstrated that light not only triggers fruiting body formation but may also modulate secondary metabolism.

- **Developmental stage**: Psilocybin concentration varies throughout the mushroom's life cycle. Peak concentrations are typically reached in mature fruiting bodies just before or at the point of spore release, after which degradation begins.

- **Strain genetics**: Different strains and species carry different alleles of the pathway enzymes, resulting in significant variation in psilocybin and psilocin production capacity. The [[psilocybin-mushroom-chemistry]] that determines its pharmacological properties.
