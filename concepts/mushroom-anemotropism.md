     9|     9|# Mushroom Anemotropism
    10|    10|
    11|    11|Anemotropism is the growth response of organisms to wind or air flow.
    12|    12|
    13|    13|In mushrooms, anemotropism directs stipe (stem) growth toward moving air.
    14|    14|
    15|    15|This behavior was first systematically documented in *[[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]* by Badham (1982).
    16|    16|
    17|    17|Controlled wind tunnel experiments demonstrated that stipes grow into the air flow during early development.
    18|    18|
    19|    19|Anemotropism operates alongside phototropism and geotropism to orient the fruiting body.
    20|    20|
    21|    21|## Discovery and Evidence
    22|    22|
    23|    23|Badham's wind tunnel study at Lehman College (CUNY) provided the primary evidence.
    24|    24|
    25|    25|*P. cubensis* stipes were observed growing directly into air flow at approximately 9 ± 3 cm/sec.
    26|    26|
    27|    27|Key observations from approximately 100 developing basidiocarps included the following findings.
    28|    28|
    29|    29|Stipes grow toward the wind source at wind speeds of 6–12 cm/sec.
    30|    30|
    31|    31|Growth into the wind occurs in both light (12h photoperiod) and darkness.
    32|    32|
    33|    33|Both fertile and sterile ([[sporeless-mutant-geotropism-link-psilocybe-cubensis]]) basidiocarps exhibit anemotropism.
    34|    34|
    35|    35|When cultures are rotated perpendicular to the wind at 3 rpm, stipes grow upright instead.
    36|    36|
    37|    37|Mushrooms placed with stipes directed away from the wind reorient toward it.
    38|    38|
    39|    39|## Developmental Stage Specificity
    40|    40|
    41|    41|Anemotropism is most pronounced during early stipe elongation (Stages 1–2).
    42|    42|
    43|    43|The response is specific to this growth phase.
    44|    44|
    45|    45|Stage 1–2: Strong anemotropic response; stipes curve decisively into the wind.
    46|    46|
    47|    47|Stage 2+: Anemotropic response is masked by emerging [[coprophilous-habitat-negative-geotropism-spore-dispersal-adaptation]].
    48|    48|
    49|    49|Stage 3–4: Negative geotropism dominates; pileus turns upward regardless of wind.
    50|    50|
    51|    51|The transition coincides with the onset of spore production.
    52|    52|
    53|    53|This suggests a hormonal shift linked to reproductive maturity.
    54|    54|
    55|    55|## Proposed Mechanisms
    56|    56|
    57|    57|### [[windward-leeward-boundary-layer-redistribution-stipe-curvature-badham-1982]] Hypothesis
    58|    58|
    59|    59|Badham proposed that anemotropism results from differential evaporative demand.
    60|    60|
    61|    61|Wind creates a thinner boundary layer on the windward side and a thicker one on the leeward side.
    62|    62|
    63|    63|This asymmetry affects water vapor and potentially other gas concentrations at the stipe surface.
    64|    64|
    65|    65|The windward side experiences greater evaporative demand (drying effect).
    66|    66|
    67|    67|The leeward side retains a thicker moisture boundary layer.
    68|    68|
    69|    69|Differential cell turgor or growth rates result from this gradient.
    70|    70|
    71|    71|The stipe curves as cells on one side elongate faster than the other.
    72|    72|
    73|    73|This mechanism is analogous to the avoidance response in *Phycomyces*.
    74|    74|
    75|    75|Cohen et al. (1975) demonstrated that wind and water vapor are both necessary for that response.
    76|    76|
    77|    77|### Mechanical Force Hypothesis
    78|    78|
    79|    79|An alternative explanation is that the mushroom responds to the mechanical force of wind.
    80|    80|
    81|    81|Dennison (1961) showed that *Phycomyces blakesleeanus* growth responds to gravity and centrifugal force.
    82|    82|
    83|    83|This suggests fungi can sense mechanical stimuli directly.
    84|    84|
    85|    85|However, Badham favored the boundary layer hypothesis.
    86|    86|
    87|    87|The response operates at very low wind speeds (6–12 cm/sec) where mechanical force is minimal.
    88|    88|
    89|    89|The response is also observed in darkness where no phototropic enhancement occurs.
