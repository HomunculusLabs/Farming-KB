# Preserving Wood and Timber Framing

Timber framing is the traditional method of building structures using heavy timbers joined with mortise and tenon connections rather than dimensional lumber and nails. On a homestead, timber framing offers durability, aesthetic beauty, and the ability to work with locally harvested timber. This page covers joinery fundamentals, wood preservation treatments, and air drying methods.

## Timber Framing Joinery

Timber framing relies on precise woodworking joints that transfer loads through the frame without fasteners. The joinery is what gives timber frames their extraordinary strength and longevity — some frames have stood for 800+ years.

### Essential Joints

**Mortise and Tenon:**
The foundational joint of timber framing. A rectangular hole (mortise) is cut into one timber, and a protruding tongue (tenon) is shaped on the end of the mating timber. Variations include:
- Through tenon: Tenon passes completely through the mortise, visible on the other side
- Blind tenon: Tenon stops inside the mortise, not visible
- Wedged tenon: A wedge is driven through the tenon after insertion, locking it permanently

**Dovetail Joint:**
Used where two timbers meet at right angles and need resistance to pulling forces. Commonly used for floor joists to sill connections and collar ties to rafters.

**Scarf Joint:**
Used to join two timbers end-to-end to create a longer beam. Critical for using shorter timbers to span large distances. Types include:
- Simple splice with through bolts
- Bridle scarf
- Stop-splayed scarf with table and wedges

**Lap Joint (Half-Lap):**
Two timbers are notched halfway through and overlapped. Used where timbers cross at the same level, such as girt connections.

**Birdsmouth:**
A notch cut into the bottom of a rafter where it sits on the top plate or sill. The horizontal seat and vertical heel provide bearing and prevent lateral movement.

### Connections and Fasteners

Traditional timber frames use wooden pegs (trunnels) driven through the joints to hold them tight. White oak is the standard peg material due to its strength and resistance to shearing. Pegs are typically 1 inch in diameter, snug-fitted into 7/8-inch holes.

Modern timber framing sometimes incorporates steel plates or concealed bolts at high-stress connections, but the primary structural integrity always comes from the wooden joinery.

## Wood Preservation

Untreated timber exposed to weather and soil contact will deteriorate rapidly. Preservation extends the life of timbers from years to decades or centuries.

### Linseed Oil (Boiled)

Raw linseed oil is a natural drying oil extracted from flax seeds. "Boiled" linseed oil has metallic driers added to speed curing.

**Application:**
- Apply to exterior wood surfaces with a brush or rag
- Two to three coats, allowing 24-48 hours between coats
- Wipe off excess after 30 minutes to prevent sticky residue
- Reapply every 2-3 years on exposed surfaces
- Penetrates 1-2 mm into the wood surface

**Pros:** All-natural, easy to apply, enhances wood grain appearance, food-safe when cured
**Cons:** Slow drying, attracts dust during curing, can support mold growth in damp environments if not cured properly, provides no insect resistance on its own

### Borate Treatments

Borates (disodium octaborate tetrahydrate) are water-soluble salts that provide excellent protection against wood-destroying insects (termites, carpenter ants, powderpost beetles) and wood-rotting fungi.

**Products:** Tim-bor, Bora-Care, Disodium Octaborate Tetrahydrate (DOT)

**Application methods:**
- Dip treatment: Submerge wood in a 10-15% borate solution for 5-10 minutes
- Spray treatment: Apply with a pump sprayer, saturating the wood surface
- Diffusion: Apply to green wood — borate diffuses into the wood as it dries
- Pressure treatment: Professional treatment forcing borate deep into wood cells

**Key considerations:**
- Borate is water-soluble — it will leach out of wood exposed to rain and ground contact
- Best for interior timber framing, enclosed structures, or wood protected by a roof overhang
- For ground-contact applications, borate must be sealed with a water-resistant topcoat
- Non-toxic to humans and mammals at treatment concentrations
- Effective against most wood-boring insects and decay fungi

**Mixing:** Typical ratio is 1 pound of borate powder per gallon of warm water for a 10% solution. Apply at a rate of approximately 1 gallon per 50 square feet of wood surface.

### Other Natural Preservatives

**Tung Oil:** Derived from the tung tree nut. More water-resistant than linseed oil, cures faster, and provides better protection against moisture. More expensive.

**Pine Tar:** Traditional Scandinavian treatment for exterior wood. Provides water repellency and mild antifungal properties. Dark color and strong odor. Mixed with boiled linseed oil and turpentine in traditional formulations.

**Raw Milk Paint:** Casein-based paint that is breathable and allows wood to release moisture while providing UV protection. Traditional on timber frames in northern Europe.

**Charring (Shou Sugi Ban):** Japanese technique of lightly charring the wood surface with a propane torch, then brushing and oiling. The charred layer is resistant to insects, rot, and UV damage. Dramatic visual effect. Best on softer woods like cedar, cypress, and pine.

## Air Drying Timber

Proper drying is essential before working timber. Green wood moves significantly as it dries, which will open joints and distort frames.

### Drying Guidelines

- Air dry timber for a minimum of 1 year per inch of thickness
- A 8x8 timber should dry for 6-8 years minimum
- Stack with stickers (1x1 spacers) between every layer for airflow
- Weight the top of the stack to prevent warping
- Orient the stack to maximize cross-ventilation
- Keep the stack covered on top but open on the sides
- End-seal all timber cuts with wax or specialized end-grain sealer to prevent checking

### Target Moisture Content

For interior timber framing, target 12-15% moisture content. Measure with a pin-type moisture meter. Building with wood above 19% moisture will result in significant shrinkage and joint loosening as the building dries.

### Species Selection

Best timber framing species by region:
- Northeast: Eastern white pine, white oak, hemlock, eastern spruce
- Southeast: Southern yellow pine, cypress, white oak
- Midwest: White oak, red oak, white pine, hickory
- Pacific Northwest: [[douglas-fir]], western red cedar, Sitka spruce
