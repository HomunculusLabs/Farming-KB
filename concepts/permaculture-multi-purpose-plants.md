# Permaculture Multi-Purpose Plants

Multi-purpose plants are species that perform many different functions in a permaculture system. The permaculture principle of "each element performing multiple functions" is best embodied by these plants. Rather than growing many single-purpose species, permaculture designers select plants that stack functions -- producing food, building soil, providing habitat, yielding materials, and offering medicine all from the same plant. Hemenway calls comfrey "the winner in the multifunctional sweepstakes."

## The Principle of Function Stacking

Every plant in a permaculture design should ideally serve at least three functions. This reduces the number of species needed, simplifies management, and creates denser, more interconnected ecosystems. Multi-purpose plants are the workhorses of any permaculture system.

## Comfrey (Symphytum spp.)

The most celebrated multi-purpose plant in permaculture:

1. **Mulch production**: Cut 4-5 times per summer; leaves decompose into rich compost
2. **Mineral accumulation**: Deep taproot mines potassium, calcium, and trace minerals from subsoil
3. **Medicine**: Wound healing, bone repair, anti-inflammatory (allantoin compound)
4. **Bee forage**: Flowers attract pollinators over a long season
5. **Liquid fertilizer**: Comfrey tea provides concentrated potassium and minerals for garden plants
6. **Weed suppression**: Dense growth shades out competitors (fortress plant)
7. **Soil building**: Deep roots break compacted soil and add organic matter at depth
8. **Pest confusion**: Complex aromatic profile may deter some pests

## Bamboo

A remarkably versatile multi-purpose plant:

1. **Construction material**: Poles for trellises, fences, scaffolding, and building; [[bamboo-as-building-material|bamboo construction]]
2. **Food**: Edible shoots of many species; nutritious and productive
3. **Erosion control**: Dense rhizome networks hold soil on steep slopes
4. **Windbreak**: Dense clumps provide effective shelter
5. **Craft material**: Basketry, furniture, tools, musical instruments
6. **Water filtration**: Bamboo charcoal absorbs impurities
7. **Animal fodder**: Leaves provide browse for livestock
8. **Ornamental**: Beautiful and creates useful microclimates
9. **Carbon sequestration**: Some of the fastest-growing plants on earth
10. **Soil improvement**: Leaf drop adds organic matter; shade-tolerant understorey plants benefit

Caution: Running bamboos can be invasive; clumping varieties are preferred in most designs. See [[bamboo-species-guide]] and [[bamboo-growing-guide]].

## Moringa (Moringa oleifera)

Called the "miracle tree" or "horseradish tree" for its extraordinary range of uses:

1. **Food -- leaves**: Contain 7-10% protein; eaten in salads and cooked. Nutritional profile rivals many vegetables for vitamins and minerals.
2. **Food -- pods**: Long bean-like fruits used in soups and curries
3. **Food -- seeds**: Immature seeds eaten like peas; mature seeds roasted or fried like peanuts
4. **Food -- roots**: Used like horseradish (hence the common name)
5. **Oil**: Seed produces ben/behen oil that never goes rancid; used for cooking, salads, and as watchmakers' oil for precision machinery
6. **Water purification**: Crushed seeds clarify turbid water (a traditional use now validated by research)
7. **Medicine**: Leaves used traditionally for malnutrition, inflammation, and infection
8. **Growth rate**: Fast-growing deciduous tree to 8m; produces within months of planting
9. **Drought tolerance**: Native to India; adapted to dry conditions
10. **Forage**: Leaves provide high-protein animal fodder

Limitation: Frost tender; suitable only for frost-free climates or as a summer annual in cooler regions.

## Other Notable Multi-Purpose Plants

### Tagasaste (Chamaecytisus palmensis)
- Nitrogen fixation, stock feed (17-31% protein), bee forage, nurse crop, firewood, poultry forage, timber
- See [[permaculture-nitrogen-fixing-trees]]

### Black Locust (Robinia pseudoacacia)
- Nitrogen fixation, timber, honey, firewood, erosion control, stock feed, wildlife habitat
- See [[permaculture-nitrogen-fixing-trees]]

### Carob (Ceratonia siliqua)
- Food (pods), nitrogen fixation, drought tolerance, fire resistance, shade, stock feed, alcohol fuel
- See [[permaculture-nitrogen-fixing-trees]]

### Jerusalem Artichoke (Helianthus tuberosus)
- Food (tubers), biomass/mulch production, fortress plant (weed suppression), windbreak, bee forage, livestock fodder

### Daikon Radish (Raphanus sativus)
- Food (roots and leaves), soil breaking (deep taproot penetrates compacted soil), nutrient accumulation, cover crop, biomass production

### Chicory (Cichorium intybus)
- Food (leaves, roots), mineral accumulation, stock feed, bee forage, drought tolerance, deep soil penetration

### Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)
- Medicine, insectary plant, mineral accumulation, ground cover, drought tolerance, ornamental

## Selecting Multi-Purpose Plants

When choosing plants for a design, evaluate each candidate against the following criteria:

1. How many functions does it serve?
2. How important are those functions to the overall design?
3. How well adapted is it to the local climate and soil?
4. Is it perennial (preferred) or annual?
5. Does it require excessive management?
6. Does it have any negative characteristics (invasiveness, allelopathy, thorns)?
7. Does it support or hinder other elements in the system?

## Design Integration

This concept integrates effectively within broader permaculture design frameworks, contributing to system resilience and multiple ecosystem functions. When implemented as part of a holistic design, it supports the core permaculture ethics of earth care, people care, and fair share. Consider zone placement, relative location, and energy cycling principles when incorporating into landscape designs.

## Economic Considerations

Cost-benefit analysis should account for both immediate yields and long-term system productivity. Initial investment in infrastructure and establishment is typically offset by reduced maintenance costs and increasing outputs over time. Multiple revenue streams can be developed through value-added products, educational opportunities, and ecosystem services.
