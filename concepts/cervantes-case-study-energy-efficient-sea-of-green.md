# Case Study: Energy-Efficient Organic Sea of Green

This case study from Jorge Cervantes' *Marijuana Horticulture* (Chapter 8)
follows two growers, Nigel and Terry, through three progressive indoor
harvests using an organic sea of green (SOG) method. The study
demonstrates how professional advice and incremental improvements can
dramatically increase yield per watt and per square meter.

## Growing Statistics

### First Crop
- **Yield:** 8.4 pounds (3.8 kg) in ten weeks
- **Space:** 16'5" x 7'10" (5 x 2.4 m), half the full room
- **Wattage:** 6,000 watts (ten 600W HPS)
- **Plants:** 100 clones (Power Plant strain), USD $3.15 each
- **Total cost:** USD $5,647
- **Cost per pound:** USD $672

### Second Crop
- **Yield:** 27.6 pounds (12.5 kg) in nine weeks
- **Space:** 33' x 7'10" (10 x 2.4 m), full room
- **Wattage:** 8,400 watts (fourteen 600W HPS)
- **Plants:** 400 clones (Power Plant strain)
- **Total cost:** USD $8,220
- **Cost per pound:** USD $298

### Third Crop
- **Yield:** 30.2 pounds (13.7 kg) in nine weeks
- **Total cost:** USD $1,882
- **Cost per pound:** USD $62

## First Crop: Lessons Learned

Nigel and Terry converted a farm shed storage area into a grow room.
They built two benches (16'6" x 4' each), lined walls with white
laminated wood paneling, and used 100 five-liter [[query-fabric-pots-vs-plastic-pots-vs-air-pots-for-cannabis]] with
premixed organic potting soil. Equipment included ten 600W HPS kits,
a multi-output electrical control board with timers, a 3200 cu/hr
inline fan, a [[cervantes-ventilation-fan-carbon-filter-setup]], and two pedestal fans.

The first crop revealed several key problems:

- **Heat management:** Temperatures exceeded 86F (30C) and on a couple
  occasions reached 91F (33C), causing plants to stop growing for 3-4
  days. Bud development suffered noticeably.
- **Carbon filter airflow:** Connecting the carbon filter around week 6
  reduced airflow, exacerbating heat problems.
- **Uneven lighting:** Plants directly under lamps produced big but less
  solid and less resinous buds compared to plants further from the center.
- **Yellowing:** The largest plants started yellowing and dropping
  leaves by week 8.

Despite these issues, the smaller plants produced better quality buds
and the overall yield was encouraging.

## Second Crop: Professional Redesign

A local grow shop owner ("Bob") helped redesign the expanded room with
six key improvements:

1. **Doubled bench length** and lined the new area with white
   laminated wood
2. **Automatic watering system** with large reservoirs, reducing daily
   labor by approximately two hours
3. **Doubled plant density** to 400 clones, reducing vegetative time
   from 14 to 7 days
4. **Upgraded ventilation:** 5,000 cu/hr extraction fan + 3,200 cu/hr
   intake fan with under-bench ductwork pushing cool air up through
   the canopy
5. **Better lighting:** Fourteen 600W lamps with adjustable
   double-parabolic reflectors (Adjust-a-Wings) and Super Spreaders,
   down from twenty lamps, covering 4'8" x 3'11" per lamp
6. **Switched to cocopeat slabs** in Danish plastic trays (2 x 1 m
  each) with [[comparison-drip-irrigation-vs-passive-irrigation]], replacing individual pots

### Results of the Redesign

- Temperature maintained at 80-82F (27-28C) even with dense canopy
- Even growth across the entire room, resembling a true "sea of green"
- [[cervantes-spider-mites-identification-control]] from purchased clones were dealt with organically using
  Ecolizer "Bugs Away" foliar feed containing [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]]
- Final yield: 12.5 kg of A+ quality bud

## Third Crop: Reaping the Rewards

By the third crop, all equipment was paid for and operational
knowledge was established. The cost dropped to just USD $1,882 with
a yield of 30.2 pounds, bringing the cost per pound down to USD $62.

## Key Takeaways

- **Professional consultation** can pay for itself in a single crop
- **Proper ventilation design** is critical for dense plantings
- **Double-parabolic reflectors** with spreaders provide more even
  light distribution than standard horizontal hoods
- **Automated irrigation** dramatically reduces labor and improves
  consistency
- **Plant density optimization** (doubling plants per area) can reduce
  required vegetative time by half
- **Three 600W lamps** are more efficient than two 1000W lamps:
  more point sources of light, lower operating costs, and lamps can
  be placed closer to plants
