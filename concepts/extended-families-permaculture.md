# Extended Families in Permaculture

The concept of the extended family in [[permaculture]] goes far beyond the traditional biological family unit. It describes a network of 1,000 or fewer members who share common ethical values and a cooperative interdependence that supplants competition. Such families can ally with like groups to create a tribe, and 20 to 40 such tribes can form a nation. Unlike many societies, families have child care and the welfare of their members at heart.

## The Problem with Nuclear Families

Nuclear family households now represent only 13 to 18% of all households in modern societies. Many people find themselves trapped in nuclear family structures or traditional extended families of blood relatives who may share no ethical or interest base. Traditional extended families can sometimes exploit younger women as household serfs, or be exploited by indolent members. Blood relationship is no guarantee of freedom of choice or fair dealing.

As people grow and age, they develop differing needs for space and relationships. Many have been locked into unsatisfactory work or personal relationships, or found themselves too much alone in the context of nuclear family "ideals," which in real societies apply to the few.

## Structure of Permaculture Extended Families

In practice, any person within an extended family network has:
- **3 to 5 close friends** (who change slowly over time)
- **A support group of 30 or more acquaintances**
- **Resource access to the whole system**

Such families already exist in Europe, with small groups living in scattered households across many existing national boundaries. Some have existed for 18 or more years, and members report individual satisfaction with a larger support group.

## Functions of Extended Families

A familial system of shared ethics can:

- **Keep in touch** via a registrar and news service
- **Co-invest** in family property development and joint enterprises
- **Rationalise resources** for most efficient sharing of space and equipment
- **Develop social contacts** through visits between households
- **Care for children** as one group, setting up a fund for their needs
- **Provide cultural and support base** for individual households

## Financial Structures

Extended families can establish several funds to ensure economic resilience:

### Common Enterprise Fund
The family fund, held in two to three places and convertible to a variety of currencies, managed by a few individuals as a full-time job. All savings and contributions are accounted to individuals and available as loans across all currencies and regions, similar to any credit union or revolving fund.

### Annual Membership Fund
Invested with interest used to service the registry, newsletter, and pay part or full-time wages to a collator.

### Children's Permanent Fund
All adults (age 17 or over) contribute to this fund, with additional gifts encouraged. Managed with the Common Enterprise Fund for essential child-oriented ventures or education. This is a non-returnable fund. Until age 12, parents can apply for loans; thereafter (until age 17) children can apply themselves, after which they are recognized as adults.

### Special Venture Funds
Raised by proposal, open to groups or individuals, and handled by the Common Enterprise Fund administrators for joint ventures such as shared houses, boats, overseas programmes, or businesses.

## Core Groups and Meta-Networking

Groups of about 30 people can gather in core regions with some outlier households, making local travel easier. Meta-networking (tribe to tribe) enables higher-level organizations such as travel and accommodation networks to be set up on a global basis, cash to be transferred to areas of need, and larger joint enterprises developed.

## Children and Population

Children need a wider alliance and support group than just one or two parents. People can find "aunts and uncles" to take part of the responsibility for children in any extended family. If children have a common fund (like their own credit union) for basic needs, their care at a basic level is assured. They also have more than one household to relate to, visit, or dwell in when educational needs change.

Regarding population control, taking group responsibility for a very few children sometimes cures the urge to breed. Those who want more than two children are far outnumbered by those who do not. Where children are not seen as "future insurance" — as in very poor rural families — the population can soon achieve a steady state.

## Individual Rights Within the Family

Membership in a family of shared ethical values does not conflict with any other membership or duties. Each adult must be defined as an individual with a right to:
- Their own space (bed and work space)
- A garden
- Their own occupation

Households based on friendship, work affinity, or designed for students, single people, or elderly people are needed as alternatives to the nuclear family model.

## Global Vision

The permaculture vision for extended families creates a web of multi-racial, multi-cultural, and multi-occupational families and global nations that obliterates defended national territories. This suits peaceful lifestyles while preserving cultural and environmental diversity — preventing the world from becoming one vast monoculture.
