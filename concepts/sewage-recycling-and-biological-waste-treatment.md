# Sewage Recycling and Biological Waste Treatment

Biological treatment of sewage and organic wastes converts pollutants into productive resources through integrated aquatic and terrestrial systems. Mollison argues that sewage treatment is "the province of the biologist" rather than the technologist, and that all organic wastes can be productively redirected to food production.

## The Core Philosophy

> "Scruple not to enrich the dried up soil with dung, and scatter filthy ashes on fields that are exhausted." — Virgil

"It is in the production of dung and ashes that soil nutrients are lost, consequently they are sacred to agriculture in the philosophy of the Chinese."

Mollison notes the ironic imbalance: "Most health inspectors I know are awfully concerned with germs, but not at all with sprays and industrial residues."

## The Biological Treatment Path

All sensible town sewage treatment from flush toilets must follow this path:

1. **Primary mechanical breakdown**: Removal of solid wastes
2. **Methane production**: Biogas from [[anaerobic-digestion-biogas-production]]
3. **Trickle filter**: Aerobic biological filtration
4. **Lagoons**: Biological oxidation and [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]]
5. **Soil absorption**: Deep-chiselled fields as absorption filters
6. **Stream return**: After all taint and excess nitrates removed

## Maryborough Model (Victoria, Australia)

Mollison cooperated with P.A. Yeomans in designing "wildlife and biologically-oriented [[werribee-sewage-lagoons-mollison]]" for a town of ~8,000 people (flow ~1,300,000 l/day).

### Design Features
- Lagoons feed hundreds of wildfowl
- Discharge to keylined fields
- Deep-chiselled as absorption filters
- Removes taint of black-water residues and excess nitrates from run-off

## The Canberra Cautionary Tale

"Canberra is learning that lesson at great cost: in trying to sterilize sewage by using complex technology they end up with an expensive and dangerous product—chemicalized water."

This illustrates the futility of technological approaches to what is fundamentally a biological problem.

## Desert Sewage Integration

At Ernabella Aboriginal settlement:
- **Lagoon/pond for soiled water**: Ideal site for dates, plums, and peaches on banks and around pond
- **Water seeps underground to creek**: Safe conversion of waste water to vitamins
- **Septic tank sludge**: Directed to pre-dug planting holes; dates, mulberry, or fig planted
- **Even the most paranoid health inspector would approve this safe conversion of waste water to vitamins**

## Raked Plant Material

Raked or mown plant material pit-mulched and covered near planting holes. Raking under bamboo or tamarisk serves two purposes:
1. To provide seed-free mulch
2. To protect the mother plant from fire

## House-Garden Sewage Integration

### Shower and Washing Water
- Permanent houses: shower water led to slotted pipe drains under shadehouse or garden
- Wiltjas: pebble mounds with showers overhead provide water treatment and garden moisture
- "Many showers, so placed, make [[vegetable-garden-watering-systems-spacing]] an automatic process"

### All Household Wastewater
- Washing-up, shower, bath, washing water containing soap led under mulch is "a benefit, not a nuisance"
- "It is a matter of integrating the garden with the waste water from washing processes"

## Waste-to-Resource Conversions

### Kitchen Wastes
- Tuck all food wastes from kitchen under mulch
- "Worms are so active that the leaves and peelings disappear overnight"
- The action of soil fungi, termites, and bacteria in desert heat quickly reduces potentially noxious wastes to soil, hence to a food resource

### Camp Wastes
- Ashes, bones, and litter around camps converted into rich garden soil by mulching
- Aided by water from showers, washing, and kitchen preparation
- Result: less rubbish for flies (fewer eye problems), less old clothes carrying scabies and attracting pests

### Dry Toilet Systems
Can be installed at outstations, or areas reserved for burial of faeces in tree-crop sites.

## Aquatic Treatment Systems

### Natural Cleansing Processes
Various biological filter systems recycle water from still ponds or cleanse water from polluted outlets using plants, animals, and microorganisms.

### Sewage Ponds as Breeding Sites
"Sewage water also vastly increases insect life, and so the productivity of wildfowl and fish." Sewage ponds are ideal sites for breeding stock destined for transfer to clearwater ponds before harvest.

### Mosquito Control
- Small fish in standing water (goldfish, grass carp)
- Native snails provide grit for ducks who appreciate mosquito pests
- Periwinkles and Salinator shellfish in estuarine ponds

## The Complete Cycle

In a fully integrated system:
1. **Human waste** → methane → biogas for cooking/heating
2. **Treated effluent** → lagoons → wildfowl habitat → field irrigation
3. **Organic kitchen waste** → mulch → worm activity → soil building
4. **Grey water** (showers, washing) → [[dryland-garden-irrigation-systems]] → plant production
5. **Animal manure** → pond fertilization → fish/duck production
6. **Sludge** → tree planting holes → fruit/nut production

## The Urban Scale

In suburbs, all food and cellulose wastes could generate fuel via amylase columns, ending the "humbug of 'waste disposal' costs." The same principle applies to sewage: rather than an expensive disposal problem, biological treatment turns waste into productive assets.

## Key Principle

"There is no sane technological solution to sewage waste; it is the province of the biologist." The most productive approach integrates waste treatment with food production, creating closed nutrient cycles that benefit both people and environment.
