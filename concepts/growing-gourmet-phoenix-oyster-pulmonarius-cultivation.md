# Phoenix Oyster Mushroom ([[pleurotus-species-and-varieties]] and differs largely in
its habitat preference for conifer woods. In the Western United States,
P. pulmonarius is usually found at higher altitudes than P. ostreatus
which prefers lowland river valleys. P. pulmonarius is primarily a
spring mushroom while P. ostreatus grows most prevalently summer to
fall. The North American collections show a wider range in color than
European collections. P. pulmonarius hosts a large complex of varieties,
offering cultivators a rich resource for new strains.

Cap convex at first, expanding to broadly convex, eventually flat or
upturned and often wavy in age. 5-20 cm or more in diameter. Grayish
white to beige to lilac grey to grey-brown, sometimes with pinkish or
orangish tones. At high temperatures the cap is lighter in color; under
cold conditions the cap becomes very dark gray to grayish black. Cap
margin smooth to undulating like an Oyster shell. Color varies according
to strain, lighting, and temperature. Stems typically eccentrically
attached. Flesh generally thin. Strains rarely form clusters of more
than 5 or 6 mushrooms.

## Common Names

The Indian Oyster, The Phoenix Mushroom, Dhingri (northern India).

## The sajor-caju Taxonomic Confusion

Many cultivators have mistakenly called a variety of this mushroom
"Pleurotus sajor-caju." The true P. sajor-caju (Fr.) Singer has been
returned to the Genus Lentinus by Pegler (1975) and is now called
Lentinus sajor-caju (Fr.) Fries. The true P. sajor-caju has a distinct
veil, a persistent ring on the stem, and trimitic or dimitic hyphae
composing the flesh, whereas P. pulmonarius is monomitic.

The name "P. sajor-caju" has been mis-applied so frequently that
confusion will likely reign for a considerable time. Many scientific
papers published on extraordinary yields of "P. sajor-caju" on straw,
cotton wastes, and coffee residues are undoubtedly referring to a
strain of P. pulmonarius. Until naming a new variety (P. pulmonarius
var. sajor-caju) gains acceptance, cultivators should refrain from
calling this commercially cultivated Oyster mushroom "Pleurotus
sajor-caju" as it is incorrect.

## Distribution and Natural Habitat

Widely reported from North America and Europe. In the eastern United
States, primarily decomposes hardwoods while in western regions it can
be found at middle elevations (1200-3000 meters) on conifers (Abies and
Picea). Common in the spring and summer. This mushroom is exceedingly
aggressive on alder, cottonwood, poplar, oak, maple, elm, aspen and
some conifers.

## Microscopic Features

Spores white to yellowish to lavender grey when dense, more or less
cylindrical, 7.5-11 x 3-4 micrometers. [[spawn-grain-to-grain-transfer]] sown directly into pasteurized straw or
sterilized sawdust. More economically grown on pasteurized substrates,
especially cereal straws, than on wood-based substrates.

### Agar Media
[[spawn-grain-to-grain-transfer]]
Grain spawn throughout.

### Fruiting Substrates
Broadly adaptive, producing mushrooms on a great array of organic
debris. The cereal (wheat, rice) straws, hardwood sawdusts, corn stalks,
sugar cane bagasse, coffee waste, pulp mill sludge, cotton waste and
numerous other agricultural and forest waste by-products. Royse and
Bahier (1988) found that 20% alfalfa hay added to wheat straw
substantially increased yields. Yields peaked with wheat straw, alfalfa,
and delayed release nutrients combined.

### Containers
Perforated plastic bags, columns, bags, trays, vertical racks, and
bottles.

## Growth Parameters

### Spawn Run
- Temperature: 75-85 degrees F (24-29 degrees C)
- Relative Humidity: 90-100%
- Duration: 8-14 days
- CO2: greater than 5000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 1 per hour
- Light: not applicable

### Primordia Formation
- Temperature: 50-75 degrees F (up to 80 degrees F)
- Relative Humidity: 95-100%
- Duration: 3-5 days
- CO2: 400-800 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 5-7 per hour
- Light: 1000-1500 lux (up to 2000)

### Fruitbody Development
- Temperature: 60-80 degrees F (18-27 degrees C)
- Relative Humidity: 85-90% (up to 95%)
- Duration: 3-5 days
- CO2: 400-800 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 5-7 per hour
- Light: 1000-1500 lux (up to 2000)

### Cropping Cycle
Every 7-10 days for 3 flushes.

## Yield and Harvest
