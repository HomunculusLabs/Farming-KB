## Mineral Deficiency Remedies for Gardens

## General Principles

Before applying specific remedies, keep a **fertiliser diary** for your garden and leave it for the next person. Document what has been done for the soil. This record-keeping is essential for long-term soil management and helps future gardeners understand the history of amendments.

## Site-Specific Base Treatments

### Leached (Washed-Out) Sands

1. Dig up garden beds and place a **plastic sheet liner** below
2. Add a bucket of **clay** and a handful of **[[compost]] and a complete fertiliser like **blood and bone**
5. Mulch thickly and replant
6. Return to the diagnostic key if symptoms recur

### Peats or Heavy Compost Soils

1. Add **urea** or **blood and bone** for nitrogen
2. Raise beds to improve drainage
3. Lime the area to adjust pH

### Calcareous Soils (Coral Sands, Desert Coasts with Calcrete)

1. Spray weak **zinc** and **copper sulphates** on plants
2. Apply **iron sulphates** in very dilute solutions (12 g per 10 square metres with lots of water), or add to liquid manure
3. Make pits of compost and grow on the edges
4. Use **sulphur** at about a handful per square metre, or add trace elements and sulphur to compost pits
5. Alternatively, lay a sheet of plastic on the ground, build up logs around it to 25 cm high, fill with humus (compost plus 50% sand), mulch heavily, and add blood and bone

**On atolls specifically**: Dig down to near water table, then mulch thickly. Make a large grow pit 3-6 m wide by 10-12 m long by 3 m deep. Use any mulch, especially Casuarina, palm, and household wastes.

## Specific Nutrient Remedies

### Sulphur {1}

Add plain sulphur (not medical quality) at one handful per square metre. Near cities, atmospheric fallout may supply sufficient sulphur.

### Copper {2}

Add as fine-crushed ore, or apply copper sulphate in water at 7 kg/hectare (1 g per square metre) every 5-7 years.

### Iron {3}

1. Try sulphur first (to acidify and improve availability)
2. If necessary, add **iron sulphate** or spray foliage with very dilute iron solution
3. Bury old iron in humus pits near trees — pieces of galvanised iron, old wire, or scrap metal parts work well

### Manganese {4}

1. Try sulphur first
2. Then apply very dilute foliar spray of **manganese sulphate**

### Zinc {5}

- In **acid** areas: add **zinc oxide**
- In **alkaline** areas: add **zinc sulphate**, plus sulphur
- Rate: 7 kg/hectare (equivalent) every 7-10 years

### Boron {6}

**Caution**: Boron is poisonous in large quantities. Do not over-apply.

1. First, lime acid areas and peats, and add sulphur to alkaline areas
2. If this doesn"t resolve the deficiency, add **borax** (sodium borate) at 1 gram per square metre
3. Test with cabbages to assess plant reaction
4. Avoid detergents containing "borates" — they can poison soil

**Boron excess** (poisoning) can occur on sea sediments and is common in reclaimed marine areas (e.g., Holland). Treatment: raise garden beds, lime, and flush out with fresh water.

### Calcium {7}

- Use **lime as limestone** in areas where manganese is plentiful
- Use **dolomite** where manganese is not abundant
- Use **cement powder** in deep red hot tropical soils, then continue mulching and use lime only if deficiencies occur
- Use **gypsum** in alkaline salty soils, flush with fresh tank water, and continue using lime
- Natural calcium sources: bone, bamboo mulch, buckwheat straw

### Nitrogen {8}

1. Ensure soil is well drained to 0.5 m for vegetables, 1-2 m for trees
2. Check cobalt levels for deficiency
3. If using legumes, ensure they are inoculated and manganese levels are not too high
4. If conditions are satisfactory, apply **dilute urine** (20 parts water to 1 part urine) or **ammonium sulphate** in alkaline areas
5. Use legume mulches or interplant (about 48 small acacia or tagasaste trees per quarter acre)
6. Use compost as surface mulch
7. Build up worms and [[rock-phosphate]]** free of cadmium or uranium
3. Stop deep digging and start mulching with minimal soil disturbance (build up narrow beds)
4. Encourage soil life, add mulch on top
5. Water with **comfrey "tea"** and dilute bird manure on plant leaves
6. If beds lack clay, add some — phosphorus binds to clay particles
7. In high-iron clays, large amounts of bone meal are needed initially but will slowly release
8. If desperate, use a few handfuls of **superphosphate** per square metre, then continue with organic sources
9. Feed a patch of comfrey with bird manure and make comfrey tea in a drum of cold water
10. All animal manures (including human) contain some phosphorus
11. **Calcined (roasted) rock phosphate** is effective on acid soils in high rainfall

### Chlorine Poisoning {13}

If tap water is the source of chlorine, let it stand with a handful of lime in it for a day before using on the garden.
