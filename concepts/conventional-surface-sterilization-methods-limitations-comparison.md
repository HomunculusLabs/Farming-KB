# Conventional Surface Sterilization Methods: Limitations and Comparison

## Overview

Surface sterilization is critical in microbiology, food safety, medical device
manufacturing, and aerospace. Multiple technologies exist for eliminating
microorganisms from surfaces, each with distinct advantages and limitations.
The NASA [[microwave-surface-sterilization-technology]] (MSC-22484) was developed
to address shortcomings of [[surface-sterilization-methods-comparison-complex-geometries-thermal-chemical-residue]] in closed systems.

## Autoclaving (Steam Sterilization)

### Mechanism

Autoclaving uses saturated steam under pressure (typically 121 degrees C at
15 psi) to denature proteins and destroy microorganisms through [[agar-culture-media]], and biohazardous waste. It is the primary
method used in mycology for sterilizing grain substrates and [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]]. Doses of 25-40
kGy are commonly used for sterilization.

### Limitations

- **Radiation damage to materials**: Gamma rays degrade polymers, discolor
  materials, and cause undesirable chemical changes. Repeated exposure causes
  cumulative material degradation
- **Cannot target specific surfaces**: Gamma radiation penetrates everything,
  making it impossible to irradiate mating surfaces without also exposing
  adjacent sensitive materials
- **Requires radioactive source**: Cobalt-60 and cesium-137 demand specialized
  shielding, licensing, and security, making gamma irradiation non-portable
- **No real-time control**: Once the source is exposed, the full dose is
  delivered with no ability to modulate intensity during the cycle

### Applications

Gamma irradiation is widely used for sterilizing pre-packaged medical devices,
pharmaceuticals, and cosmetics. It also decontaminates spices and fresh
produce.

## Ultraviolet (UV) Irradiation

### Mechanism

UV sterilization uses UV-C light at 254 nm to damage microbial DNA by forming
pyrimidine dimers that prevent replication. UV-C has limited penetration depth
and acts primarily on directly-illuminated surfaces.

### Limitations

- **Line-of-sight requirement**: UV light only sterilizes directly illuminated
  surfaces. Shadowed areas, crevices, and interior surfaces receive no dose
- **Limited penetration**: UV-C penetrates only micrometers into materials and
  cannot reach surfaces beneath even thin opaque layers
- **Material degradation**: Prolonged UV exposure degrades plastics, rubbers,
  and elastomers through photo-oxidation, limiting compatibility with seals
- **Dose uniformity challenges**: Achieving uniform UV dose across complex
  geometries is extremely difficult. Corners and recessed features receive
  lower doses than flat surfaces
- **Poor sporicidal activity**: Bacterial spores are highly UV-resistant and
  require impractically high doses for reliable kill
