## Batch Stirred-Tank Reactors

Batch reactors were among the earliest systems used for evaluating
metal-biosorbent effectiveness. A plug-flow biosorption reactor with
limited retention time for copper removal has been described (Butter et
al., 1998). Metal-containing influent and biomass suspension are pumped
to an in-line mixer. Polyelectrolyte solution is added from a second
mixer, causing flocculation of biomass, and the solution is discharged
into a settling tank. This continuous process achieves 87% copper
reduction at a flow rate of 1.5 L/min. Batch stirred-tank reactors in

## Fixed Packed-Bed Reactors

Pycnosporus sanguineus adsorbs heavy metals using fixed-bed column
systems (Zulfadhly et al., 2001). The biosorption process utilizes a
Perspex column with 5 cm internal diameter and 1 m length. P.
sanguineus biomass removes more than 90% of Pb(II), Cu(II), and Cd(II).
Lead uptake increases with bed height from 4 to 15 cm. Process
efficiency remains constant for the first two cycles, then slightly
decreases in subsequent cycles using 0.1 M HCl as eluant.

## Immobilized Cell Reactors

Immobilized cell systems offer advantages including easy modeling,
biomass reuse, and phase separation after metal uptake. Dead fungal
biomass can be immobilized in polyacrylamide, alginate, polysulfone,
textile fibers, and inorganic compounds. Three immobilization techniques
are used: entrapment, encapsulation, and bonding. Biomass-polysulfone
beads for [[aspergillus-oryzae]] in a chemostat achieved greater than 99% Cu(II)
removal with 5 x 10(-3) M HClO4 elution (Huang and Huang, 1996).
Aspergillus niger immobilized in polysulfone matrix removed over 50% of
Pb(II), Cd(II), Cu(II), and Ni(II) with 0.05 N HNO3 regeneration.
[[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]] live mycelia achieved Cd(II) uptake of 102 mg/g
with 10 mM HCl elution (Arica et al., 2001). Heat-killed mycelia showed
120 mg/g uptake, and carboxymethyl cellulose-immobilized biomass
demonstrated enhanced performance. Live and heat-inactivated T. versicolor
on CMC beads showed Cu(II) uptake of 1.51 and 1.84 mM, Pb(II) of 0.85
and 1.11 mM, and Zn(II) of 1.33 and 1.67 mM, respectively (Bayramoglu
et al., 2003).
