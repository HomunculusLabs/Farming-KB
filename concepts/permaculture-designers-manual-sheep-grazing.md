# Sheep Grazing
**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]], sheep are managed as part of multi-species
grazing systems, integrated with orchards, [[comparison-rotational-grazing-vs-continuous-grazing]].
## Multi-Species Grazing Sequences
Informed graziers can make much better use of browse by scheduling
rotation. The typical sequence is:
- **Goats** browse first (shrubs, woody weeds, thorny plants)
- **Cattle** follow (tall grasses, legumes)
- **Horses** follow cattle (medium grasses)
- **Sheep** follow horses (short grasses, clovers)
Each species prepares the forage for the next in sequence, dynamically
balancing levels by species succession.
## Sheep in Arid Rangelands
In arid areas (Section 11.12), sheep form part of highly selected meat
flocks alongside goats, cattle, donkeys, and camels. They thrive when:
- Herded or penned in 15 or more rotated runs
- Each run receives 2-8 years for recovery and re-seeding
- Small, high-value herds are run on copious range
Mollison states: "It is infinitely preferable to run small, high-value
herds on copious range than to risk the inevitable collapse of range and
flocks by stressing the vegetation."
### Drought Management
Sheep graziers who allow long rest periods never suffer drought-related
animal stress. Provisions for drought feeding must be made, or farmers
may lose basic stock or sell cheaply for slaughter.
