# Sheep Grazing

**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison (1988)
**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Sheep are efficient converters of grasses and forbs to meat, wool, and milk. In permaculture design, sheep are managed as part of multi-species grazing systems, integrated with orchards, farm forestry, and rangeland rehabilitation. Mollison emphasises careful rotational grazing to prevent land degradation.

## Multi-Species Grazing Sequences

Informed graziers, knowing the preferences of different species, can make much better use of the basic browse resource by scheduling rotation. The typical sequence is:

- **Goats** browse first (shrubs, woody weeds, thorny plants)
- **Cattle** follow (tall grasses, legumes)
- **Horses** follow cattle (medium grasses)
- **Sheep** follow horses (short grasses, clovers)

This is not to keep one level of browse constant, but to dynamically balance levels by species succession. Each species prepares the forage for the next in sequence.

## Sheep in Arid Rangelands

In arid areas (Section 11.12), sheep form part of highly selected and controlled meat flocks, alongside goats, cattle, donkeys, and camels. They thrive when:
- Herded or penned in 15 or more rotated runs
- Each run receives 2-8 years for recovery and re-seeding
- Some successful Australian sheep graziers allow 7-9 years rest per run
- Small, high-value herds are run on copious range

Mollison states: "It is infinitely preferable to run small, high-value herds on copious range than to risk the inevitable collapse of range and flocks by stressing the vegetation."

### Drought Management
Sheep graziers who allow long rest periods never suffer the animal stress brought on by drought. In all arid and semi-arid areas, large livestock are at risk in drought; provisions for drought feeding must be made, or farmers may lose basic stock or sell cheaply for slaughter.

## Sheep in Orchards

In the orchard animal integration model (Section 12.9), sheep are introduced into orchards at a specific stage of tree maturity:
- Years 0-3: Only bantams and small poultry on range
- Years 3-7: Foraging pigs introduced as fruit matures
- Years 7-20: First sheep, then controlled cattle grazing permitted
- 10+ years: Trees far less susceptible to grass competition

Sheep are considered "soft" animals suitable for early supervised grazing in agroforestry systems, as they cause less damage to young trees than cattle or pigs.

## Sheep for Lawn and Ground Cover Management

Sheep have historically served as "lawn mowers" on rural estates. A USDA Yearbook photo from 1897 shows a flock of sheep grazing in Central Park, New York, described as "the lawn mowers or turf makers of the park." This function translates to permaculture systems where sheep can:
- Maintain short swards in orchard understories
- Control grass competition around young trees (with supervision)
- Convert grass covers to manure for tree crops

## Range Diet and Browse Equivalents

Range values are expressed in "cow browse days" -- the number of days a cow can forage on a unit area. Mollison provides equivalents:

One European cow browse year equals:
- 266 black-tailed jackrabbits
- 164 antelope jackrabbits
- 18 kangaroo
- 6 sheep
- 385 ground squirrels

Kangaroo and jackrabbits, if well-managed and harvested, are far more productive than cattle from a protein production standpoint alone, as kangaroo has 58% digestible protein versus cattle at 35%. A judicious balance of species always exceeds a monoculture of one species.

## Soil and Range Health

### Trampling Effects
After only 9 years of stocking, soil pore space was reduced:
- 44% in the top two inches of soil
- 60% in the lower 24-inch layer
- Infiltration correspondingly reduced, runoff increased

### Vegetative Cover and Infiltration
In the first 30 minutes of rain:
- 5% vegetative cover: 1.2% rainfall soaks in
- 15% cover: 1.3% soaks in
- 25% cover: 1.7% soaks in
- 35% cover: 2.4% soaks in

Total plant cover of 65-70% is needed to totally prevent erosion.

### Parasite Problems
Icelandic sheep on lowland or fertilised pasture suffer severe parasite infestation that reduces mineral content of meat. This illustrates the need for diverse grazing and long rest periods between rotations.

## Blackberry Control with Sheep

Sheep follow pigs in the systematic control of rampant blackberry over areas of 0.1-2.0 hectares:
1. Fence area into 0.4 hectare lots with electric fencing
2. Release 20 pigs per lot to eat roots
3. Follow with 12 goats on rotation to consume regrowth
4. Sheep (or even geese) maintain the cleared area long-term

## Design Principles

1. **Long rotations:** Allow 2-9 years rest between grazing cycles
2. **Small flocks:** Prefer small, high-quality flocks on extensive range
3. **Multi-species:** Always combine sheep with other livestock for complete utilisation
4. **Rest periods:** Never exceed 35% vegetative cover removal; 65-70% cover prevents erosion
5. **Shelter provision:** Trees and windbreaks reduce animal stress and increase carcass weight by 16-30%
6. **Water placement:** Cattle range only 3-7 km from water; place water to distribute grazing pressure
