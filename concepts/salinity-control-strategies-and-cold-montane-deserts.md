## Cold and Montane Deserts

### Climate Characteristics

Cold and montane deserts occupy arid plateau interiors and high valley floors at elevation. They are defined by **extreme temperature variations**: very hot days followed by icy nights. The diurnal range can exceed 40°C. Growing seasons are extremely short — typically **40 to 70 days** — and frosts may occur during most of the year. In these environments, **cold hardiness is far more important than drought hardiness** for plant survival.

### Snow Hydrology and Water Management

At high altitudes, snow may sublimate directly to vapour rather than melting into usable runoff. It is therefore essential to **[[cold-montane-deserts]] requires heavy thermal mass and wind protection:

- **Trombe walls** for passive solar heating
- **Double entries** (airlocks) to reduce heat loss
- **Glazing over courtyards** to create sun traps and extend the growing season
- **Earth-sheltering** (berming) for insulation against extreme cold
- **Massive fire-flue walls** for radiant heat storage and distribution throughout living spaces

## Integrated Design Synthesis

Salinity control and cold montane design share core [[water-management]], appropriate species selection, and earth-integrated structures are universal strategies that translate across these seemingly different environments.

### Key Takeaways

- Prioritise **biological solutions** (tree planting) over mechanical interventions (pumps, drains)
- Design for the **full spectrum of extreme conditions** — heat, cold, salinity, and aridity
- Use **stone, mulch, and earth-sheltering** as low-cost thermal moderators
- Secure **independent fresh water** supplies during long salinity recovery periods
- Select species for **functional roles** first — water uptake, frost tolerance, forage value
