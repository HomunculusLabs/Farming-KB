## Woodlots, Hedgerows, and Plant Barriers

Woodlots and hedgerows serve multiple functions on the permaculture farm: fuel, structural timber, forage, shelter, wildlife habitat, erosion control, and pest exclusion. Mollison provides detailed species lists and design guidance for these essential landscape elements.

## On-Farm Woodlot Functions

Farm woodlots serve needs that are often overlooked in favor of commercial timber markets:

1. **Fuel**: From high sugar crops and firewood species
2. **Structural**: Fences and buildings
3. **Forage**: Winter and summer feed for livestock
4. **Shelter**: Wind protection and microclimate modification

Some valuable trees like black walnut produce young trees for structural use, can be sold as rootstock for grafting, and at maturity provide timber of great value—potentially enough for the farmer to retire on.

## Timber Species by Durability

### High Durability (70+ years in ground)
- **Strawberry Jam Acacia** ([[acacia-acuminata]])*
- **Black Locust** ([[robinia-pseudoacacia]])*
- **Catalpa** ([[catalpa-speciosa]])*
- Almost all cedars (Cedrus spp.)
- Juniper ([[juniperus-communis]])
- River Red Gum (E. camaldulensis)
- Huon Pine (Athrotaxis franklinii)

### Medium Durability (30–70 years in ground)

- **Osage Orange** (Maclura pomifera)*
- Bald Cypress (Taxodium distichum)
- Redwood (Sequoia sempervirens)

## Plant Barriers: Five Functions

1. **Contain or exclude livestock**
2. **Shelter gardens and houses from wind**
3. **Increase efficiency of wind and sun**
4. **Prevent re-invasion by unwanted plants**
5. **Screen unwanted views and sounds**

## Livestock Exclusion Barriers

### The Ultimate Barrier: African Boxthorn (Lycium ferocissimum)
"Almost the only tree I know which stops everything, but needs no pruning." It will:
- Stop bulls, lions, and weed invasion
- Resist salt spray and gales
- Feed poultry (which also disappear into it without recourse)
- In coastal sandy areas, crown spreads to ~7 m wide
- Seeds may appear every ~50 years (small cost compared to frequent attention for other hedges)
- In pastures and heavy soils, no tendency to spread as seedlings

Lycium compounds or "bomas" of dead branches protect plantings of more useful trees. Clipped branches deter rabbits when strewn around seedlings.

### Other Formidable Barriers
- **Jujube** and **Rosa multiflora/Rosa rugosa**: Equally formidable, need hard stopping/pruning in first few years
- **Osage Orange** (Maclura pomifera): Highly recommended by Logsdon
- **Red Cedar**: Never browsed
- **Honey Locust** and **Shingle Oak** (Quercus imbricaria)
- **Cacti**: Effective in dry areas
- **Unpalatable or thorny local species**

### Traditional European Hedgerow
**Hawthorn** (Crataegus) live-set at 60–90 cm and later layered or interwoven with dead trimmings. Needs cutting to shape every 4–6 years. Made more efficient by:
- A few strands of barbed wire strained through in infancy
- Ditch and bank approaches
- Permanent electric wire protection

### Woven Fences
Beautiful middle-European woven fences have been duplicated by Tagari using local wattle, ti-tree (Melaleuca), or "whipstick" regrowth. Bamboo serves equally well. In the Caspian Sea area, living papyrus uprights interwoven with dead stems create basket-tight enclosures.

### Small Livestock
Inside impenetrable hedges, only woven or netted palisades repel small livestock.

## Windbreak Systems

Windbreaks must break the force of wind while performing as many additional functions as possible:

### Design Principles
- Shape should be that of a "sun-trap" (trellis on walls, earth banks, dam spoil create other local suntraps)
- Combination weave and thorn quickset hedges are fairly quickly established
- For the non-grazier, less fierce hedgerow is needed

### Multi-Function Windbreak Species
- Act as firebreaks where crucial
- Store emergency fodder for various animals
- Make cultivation easier with implements
- Provide bee forage and insectivorous bird cover/nesting sites
- Yield cull wood for construction timbers
- Contain species for emergency farm product diversification
- Conserve soil, clean run-off water, prevent erosion

### Productive Wind Shelter (Cattle Excluded)
Mixed hedgerow of:
- Prunus
- Crataegus
- Coprosma
- Malus (crab apple)
- Hazel
- Bamboo
- Fuchsia
- Vines

These create wonderful wildlife and forage habitat.

### Winter Shelter
Enclosures of:
- Bamboo
- Pampas grass
- Paspalum
- Sudax
- Ti-tree
- Other thickset species

Essential for newly-shorn sheep, ewes in lamb, and emergency snow retreats for wildlife.

### Dry Area Windbreaks
Tall tamarisk, casuarina, mulga (Acacia aneura), eucalyptus, and drought-proof trees protect soil from desiccating winds and reduce evaporation in open ponds.

### Salt Wind Defense
- Coprosma
- Euonymus japonicas
- Sea buckthorn (Hippophae rhamnoides)
- "Fedges" of scramblers such as Tetragonia implexa
- Tall stands of pines

### Temporary Summer Windbreaks
- Sunflower
- Jerusalem artichoke
- Belts (not rows) of pole beans and corn
- Autumn-winter clumps of broad beans
- In very severe winds, perennials are necessary

### Small Garden Protection
- Tiny hedges of clipped rosemary protect small herb gardens
