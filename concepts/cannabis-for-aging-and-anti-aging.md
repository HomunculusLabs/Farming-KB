## Key Anti-Aging Research

### Caloric Restriction and Longevity

Animal studies demonstrate longevity through modest caloric restriction. A proposed mechanism involves reduced cellular glucose triggering increased free radical production, which stimulates enhanced catalase enzyme response. This suggests repetitive, low-stress free radical exposure may have anti-aging effects, potentially making high-dose antioxidant supplementation counterproductive.

### Telomeres and Telomerase

The 2009 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded for discovery that telomere degradation limits cellular aging, while telomerase abundance sustains telomere length and arrests cellular aging. Cancer cells exploit high telomerase activity for immortality. Drug companies are developing agents to reduce telomerase in cancer while increasing it in healthy tissue.

### Senescent Cell Elimination

A Mayo Clinic study found that typical aging signs were not observed in mice whose senescent cells were eliminated, confirming the role of senescent cell accumulation in aging phenotypes.

## Cannabis and Aging

A study from Columbus University, Ohio, discovered that the synthetic cannabinoid WIN55,212-2 can enhance cognition and produce anti-inflammatory effects in older rats. This effect has not yet been confirmed in humans.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Aging

Psychosomatic research has identified specific factors affecting longevity:
- "Positive affect seems to protect individuals against physical declines in old age"
- "Longevity was associated with being conscientious, emotionally stable, and active"
- "Positive emotional content reported in early-life autobiographies was strongly associated with longevity six decades later"

A 2011 UC Davis study demonstrated that intensive meditation training significantly increased telomerase activity, a known predictor of cellular longevity achieved through telomere repair.
