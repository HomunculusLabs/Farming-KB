## Fungal Organic Acid Production for Metal Leaching

Fungi produce a variety of organic acids that play crucial roles in
metal solubilization, leaching from contaminated matrices, and
biogeochemical cycling of elements. Citric acid, oxalic acid, and
gluconic acid are the principal organic acids involved in [[penicillium-simplicissimum]]
being the most commonly used species for biohydrometallurgical
applications.

## Principal Organic Acids in Fungal Metal Leaching

### Citric Acid

Citric acid is produced from the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and
is one of the most important fungal metabolites for metal solubilization.
Commercial citric acid production by [[aspergillus-niger]] represents a
billion-dollar industry, demonstrating the feasibility of large-scale
fungal organic acid production. Citric acid forms stable, water-soluble
complexes with many metal ions, enhancing their mobility and
bioavailability.

In the presence of fly ash, A. niger produces mainly gluconic acid,
whereas in its absence citric acid is formed. Low pH favors citric acid
formation while higher pH stimulates oxalic and gluconic acid
production. The type of organic acid produced depends on the fungal
species, carbon source, and environmental conditions.

### Oxalic Acid

Oxalic acid serves a dual function in fungal-metal interactions. Unlike
most metal citrates which are highly mobile, oxalic acid produced by
fungi can immobilize soluble metal ions as insoluble oxalates. This
precipitation mechanism provides a means of detoxification, as most
metal oxalates are insoluble with the exception of sodium, potassium,
lithium, and iron oxalates.

Aspergillus niger forms oxalate crystals when grown on media amended
with insoluble phosphates of calcium, cadmium, cobalt, copper,
manganese, strontium, and zinc, as well as powdered metal-bearing
minerals. Copper oxalate (moolooite) has been observed around hyphae
growing on copper-treated wood, providing a non-toxic form of copper
detoxification through insoluble precipitation.

Oxalic acid reaches concentrations of 10 to the minus 2 to 10 to the
minus 5 moles per liter in soil environments. [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]].

## Process Types for Fungal Bioleaching

### One-Step (Batch) Process

In the one-step approach, microorganisms are grown in the presence of
solid substrates for periods ranging from days to months. Mobilized
metals are determined in the supernatant. Growth can be reduced or
inhibited by toxic elements, which reduces leaching efficiencies.
Separation of biomass from the leached mineral phase can also present
practical difficulties.

### Two-Step Process

The two-step process separates [[brassinosteroid-fungal-growth-promotion]] from the leaching step.
Fungi are first cultivated to produce organic acids, which are then
applied to the metal-containing substrate. This approach avoids toxicity
problems during fungal growth and can achieve higher leaching
efficiencies. Two-step processes have been demonstrated for leaching
nickel from lateritic ores, copper from oxidized ores, and metals from
fly ash and electronic waste.
