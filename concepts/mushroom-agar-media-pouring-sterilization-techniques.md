## Mushroom Agar Media Pouring and Sterilization Techniques

## Overview

Proper sterilization and pouring of [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities|mushroom cultivation]]. Even the finest agar formulation will fail if contaminants are introduced during preparation, sterilization, or pouring. This page covers the complete workflow from media preparation through pouring sterile petri dishes, with emphasis on [[cultivator-contaminants-of-mushroom-culture]] laboratories.

### Prevention Strategy

**Alcohol filter method:** Saturate a paper towel with alcohol (70% isopropanol or ethanol) and drape it over the point where outside air is being drawn in during cool-down. The cloth acts as a filter, lessening the chance of contaminants entering.

### Safe Handling Timeline

- **Within 20 minutes** after the heat source has been turned off, most media vessels can be handled without hot gloves
- **Before that time:** Media can be poured but some form of hand protection is needed
- **If the cooker forms a vacuum:** Media can be safely stored inside until ready to pour

## Pouring Agar Media

### Yield

One liter of [[malt-extract-agar]] medium will pour approximately **20 to 40 petri dishes** (100 × 15 mm), depending upon the depth of the pour. Shallower pours yield more dishes but may dry out faster during incubation.

### Pre-Pour Preparation

Before pouring agar medium, the table top should be thoroughly wiped clean with an **80% concentration of isopropanol** (isopropyl alcohol). This concentration is more effective than 70% for rapid surface disinfection in mycological work.

### Petri Dish Options

**Plastic petri dishes:**
- Usually come pre-sterilized and ready to use
- Disposable, reducing cross-contamination risk
- Most commonly used by home and commercial cultivators

**Glass petri dishes:**
- Should be washed and sterilized in a petri dish-holding rack simultaneously to the agar medium sterilization in an autoclavable flask
