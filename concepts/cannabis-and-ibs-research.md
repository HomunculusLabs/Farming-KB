# Cannabis and Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD/IBS)

**Source:** Uwe Blesching, The Cannabis Health Index
**Last Updated:** 2026-04-19

## Overview

Inflammatory Bowel Disease encompasses both IBS (syndrome) and IBD (disease including Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis). The endocannabinoid system is widely distributed throughout the gut with regional variation and organ-specific actions, regulating food intake, nausea, gastric secretion, GI motility, ion transport, visceral sensation, intestinal inflammation, and cell proliferation.

**Total Number of Studies Reviewed: 3**

## IBS Classifications

- **IBS-D**: diarrhea-predominant

- **IBS-C**: constipation-predominant

- **IBS-A**: alternating diarrhea/constipation

- **IBS-PI**: post-infectious

- **Ulcerative colitis**: form of IBD affecting colon with ulcers

- **Crohn's disease**: autoimmune disorder affecting the GI tract

## Key Research Findings

1. **CBD reduces hypermotility** (2008, University of Naples, Italy): CBD reduced hypermotility in mice, suggesting CBD normalizes motility in inflammatory bowel disease. CHI=2

2. **FAAH blocker reduces colitis** (2008, University of Calgary, Canada): EC membrane transport inhibitor VDM11 enhanced endocannabinoid system action; cannabinoids reduced colitis. Scientists concluded drugs targeting EC degradation offer therapeutic potential for IBD. CHI=3

3. **Cannabinoids reduce inflammation** (2006, Northeastern University, Boston): Novel molecules acting through the ECS showed therapeutic potential on inflammatory diseases including IBD. CHI=3

## Mechanisms of Action

The [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] plays a central role in gastrointestinal health through several receptor-mediated pathways:

- **CB1 receptors** are located on enteric neurons throughout the GI tract and regulate motility, secretion, and visceral pain sensation. In IBS with diarrhea (IBS-D), CB1 activation reduces excessive gut motility and hypersecretion.

- **CB2 receptors** are found on immune cells within the gut mucosa and modulate inflammatory responses. In IBD, CB2 activation reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine release, helping to control chronic intestinal inflammation.

- **FAAH inhibition** represents a promising therapeutic approach. By blocking the enzyme that degrades anandamide, FAAH inhibitors increase endogenous cannabinoid levels, enhancing the body's natural protective mechanisms without introducing exogenous cannabinoids.

- **Gut barrier function:** Endocannabinoid signaling helps maintain intestinal epithelial barrier integrity. Increased intestinal permeability is a feature of IBD that allows bacterial translocation and perpetuates inflammation.

- **Visceral hypersensitivity:** Both CB1 and TRPV1 receptors modulate visceral pain signaling from the gut. Cannabinoids may reduce the abdominal pain that is a hallmark symptom of both IBS and IBD.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Both CB1 and CB2 are found in the GI tract. Crohn's disease patients report **indica strains** work especially well for pain, nausea, vomiting, depression, low energy, and sleep. This may relate to CBD's demonstrated ability to reduce gut hypermotility. Indicas tend to have lower THC:CBD ratios, resulting in relatively greater CB2 activation.

Strains rich in [[cannabis-minor-cannabinoids]] like CBG may provide additional benefits, as CBG has shown significant anti-inflammatory effects in gut tissue models. The [[cannabis-terpene-profile]] of selected strains can enhance therapeutic outcomes, with beta-caryophyllene (a natural CB2 agonist) being particularly relevant for intestinal inflammation.

## Dosage and Administration

For IBS/IBD patients considering cannabis therapy, several factors are relevant:

- Oral preparations deliver cannabinoids directly to the GI tract, making them particularly suitable for intestinal conditions
- [[cannabis-edibles-guide]] recommendations should be followed, noting the delayed onset of oral products (30–120 minutes)
- Vaporization may provide rapid relief for acute symptom flares
- [[cannabis-dosing-guidelines]] recommend starting with low doses and titrating gradually
- Patients should be aware of potential [[cannabis-drug-interactions-and-contraindications]] with medications commonly prescribed for IBS/IBD, including immunosuppressants and antispasmodics

## Conventional Management

Meta-analysis (2008) of RCTs on Medline, Embase, and Cochrane register found **fiber, antispasmodics, and peppermint oil** showed greater effectiveness than placebo for IBS.

Additional conventional approaches include low-FODMAP diets, cognitive behavioral therapy, and prescription medications such as antidiarrheals (loperamide), laxatives (for IBS-C), tricyclic antidepressants, and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors.

## Mind-Body Connections

- Psychiatric disorders occur in **up to 94% of IBS patients** (major depression, anxiety, somatoform disorders)

- IBS-C associated with "holding on without change" — grim determination despite unsolvable problems

- IBS-D associated with wanting to "get rid of something or somebody" — desire to be done with a situation

- Nausea/vomiting linked to preoccupation with past mistakes and wishing to "undo" something

The gut-brain axis is increasingly recognized as central to IBS pathophysiology. Stress activates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis, which directly affects gut function through neural, hormonal, and immune pathways. Cannabis may modulate the gut-brain axis through both central nervous system effects and peripheral receptor activation.

## Supporting Foods

- **Acacia**: improves stool consistency, reduces fecal incontinence

- **Turmeric/curcumin**: meta-study shows promise for adenomatous polyposis, IBD, and ulcerative colitis; double-blind RCT showed curcumin safe for maintaining remission from UC

- **(E)-beta-Caryophyllene**: FDA-approved dietary cannabinoid activating CB2 with potent anti-inflammatory action; found in black pepper, basil, cinnamon, rosemary, oregano, clove

- **Peppermint oil**: Natural antispasmodic with evidence for IBS symptom relief

- **Ginger**: Anti-nausea and prokinetic properties beneficial for functional GI symptoms
