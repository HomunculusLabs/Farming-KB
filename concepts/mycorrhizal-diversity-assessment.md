# Mycorrhizal Diversity [[soil-food-web-assessment-methods]]

Assessing the diversity of [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] in ecosystems requires a combination of field sampling, morphological identification, and molecular techniques. The choice of methods depends on the mycorrhizal type (AMF vs. EMF), the research questions, and the available resources. Modern approaches increasingly rely on DNA-based methods to complement or replace traditional morphological identification.

## Challenges in Mycorrhizal Diversity Assessment

Several factors complicate diversity assessment:

- Most [[endobacteria-arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] cannot be grown axenically (especially AMF)
- Morphological features of AMF spores overlap between species
- EMF identification often requires fruiting body collection, which is seasonal and incomplete
- Belowground diversity often exceeds aboveground (fruiting body) diversity
- Single roots may be colonized by multiple fungal species simultaneously
- Sampling design must account for spatial heterogeneity of fungal distributions

## Field Sampling Methods

### Soil Sampling

- **Coring**: Soil cores (2-5 cm diameter) collected at regular intervals or along transects
- **Depth stratification**: Most mycorrhizal activity occurs in the top 20-30 cm; deeper sampling may reveal distinct communities
- **Spatial scale**: Studies range from cm-scale (rhizosphere) to km-scale (landscape)
- **Temporal replication**: Seasonal sampling captures temporal dynamics of sporulation and colonization

### Root Sampling

- **Root excavation**: Careful excavation preserves root-fungus connections
- **In-growth cores**: Mesh bags filled with sterile substrate allow selective sampling of active mycorrhizal fungi
- **Root window (minirhizotron)**: Transparent tubes for repeated non-destructive observation of root colonization
- **Correspondence between root and soil sampling**: Roots reveal active colonization; soil reveals propagule bank

### Sporocarp (Fruiting Body) Surveys

For EMF diversity assessment, fruiting body surveys remain important:

- Permanent plot surveys with regular visits throughout fruiting season
- Species-area relationships determine sampling completeness
- Fruiting body surveys underestimate diversity (many EMF species rarely fruit)
- Must be combined with belowground methods for comprehensive assessment

## Morphological Identification

### AMF Spore Identification

AMF diversity is traditionally assessed through spore morphology:

- **Wet sieving and decanting** (Gerdemann and Nicolson 1963): Soil is suspended in water, sieved through nested sieves (250-500 micrometer), and spores collected from the finest sieve
- **Sucrose density gradient centrifugation**: Spores are separated from organic debris by flotation in 20-60% sucrose
- **Spore mounting**: Spores mounted in PVLG (polyvinyl-lacto-glycerol) with or without Melzer's reagent for microscopic examination

Key morphological features for AMF spore identification:

- Spore size, color, and shape
- Wall structure (number of layers, thickness, ornamentation)
- Germination shield morphology (in Gigasporaceae)
- Subtending hyphae characteristics (width, attachment, septation)
- Reaction with Melzer's reagent (Melzer's positive = amyloid)

### EMF Root Tip Identification

Ectomycorrhizal diversity is assessed through morphotyping of root tips:

- **Mantle characteristics**: Color, texture, thickness, branching pattern
- **Rhizomorphs**: Presence, color, morphology
- **Cystidia and other surface features**: Shape, arrangement, abundance
- **Anatomical sections**: Mantle structure (echinata, angularis, plectenchymatous)
- **Color changes**: Reactions to KOH, FeSO4, and other reagents

### Specimen Preservation

For voucher specimens and reference collections:

- **Kew mixture**: A fixative mixture used for preserving fungal structures
- **Copenhagen solution**: Another standard fixative for mycorrhizal specimens
- **FAA (Formalin-Aceto-Alcohol)**: General-purpose fixative for plant and fungal tissues
- **Herbarium vouchers**: Dried sporocarps deposited in herbaria with accession numbers

## Molecular Identification Methods

### DNA Extraction

From environmental samples:

- **Spore DNA**: Individual spores picked under microscope; crushed for DNA extraction
- **Root tip DNA**: Individual mycorrhizal root tips extracted for PCR
- **Soil DNA**: Direct extraction from soil using commercial kits (MoBio PowerSoil, etc.)
- **Hyphal DNA**: Hyphae extracted from soil by sieving and filtration

### PCR Amplification

Target regions for mycorrhizal identification:

**For AMF**:
- SSU rRNA gene (small subunit): Universal eukaryotic primers; moderate resolution
- ITS region (Internal Transcribed Spacer): Most commonly used fungal barcode; AMF1/AMF2 primers
- LSU rRNA gene (large subunit): Useful for higher-level phylogeny
- 28S rDNA: Alternative to ITS for some AMF groups

**For EMF**:
- ITS1 and ITS2: Standard fungal barcode; high species-level resolution
- LSU: For phylogenetic placement and identification of unknown sequences
- beta-tubulin, EF1-alpha: Protein-coding genes for finer resolution

### Cloning and Sequencing

- **RFLP analysis**: PCR products digested with restriction enzymes to generate operational taxonomic units (OTUs)
- **Clone libraries**: PCR products cloned into plasmid vectors for Sanger sequencing
- **Next-generation sequencing**: High-throughput approaches (454, Illumina) that can sequence thousands of samples simultaneously
- **Metabarcoding**: Amplicon sequencing of environmental samples without culturing

### Sequence Analysis

- BLAST searches against curated databases (UNITE for all fungi, MaarjAM for AMF)
- Phylogenetic analysis placing unknown sequences among reference taxa
- OTU clustering at defined sequence similarity thresholds (97-99% for species-level)
- Rarefaction curves to assess sampling completeness

## Quantification Methods

### Root Colonization Assessment

- **Gridline intersect method**: Microscopic examination of cleared and stained roots; quantifies percentage of root length colonized
- **Trypan blue staining**: Standard method for visualizing AMF structures in roots
- **Magnified intersections method**: More precise quantification of arbuscules, vesicles, and hyphae separately

### Propagule Density

- **Most Probable Number (MPN)**: Serial dilution bioassay using bait plants to estimate infective propagule density
- **Direct spore counts**: Spores extracted from known soil volumes by wet sieving

### Biomass Estimation

- **Ergosterol**: Fungal cell membrane sterol used as a biomarker for fungal biomass
- **Phospholipid fatty acids (PLFA)**: Signature fatty acids (18:2omega6,9) indicate fungal biomass
- **[[glomalin-and-soil-carbon]]**: AMF-specific soil protein correlated with hyphal biomass

## Emerging Methods

- **Metatranscriptomics**: Active gene expression profiling of entire fungal communities
