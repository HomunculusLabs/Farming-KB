# Medically Important Fungi — Pathogens and Opportunists

More than 300 species of fungi are known to be potential pathogens of humans and other homeothermic vertebrates. These range from virulent systemic pathogens to opportunistic organisms that cause disease primarily in immunocompromised hosts.

## Categories of Fungal Pathogens

### Virulent Systemic Pathogens
True pathogens capable of causing disease in healthy individuals:
- **Histoplasma capsulatum:** Confined to temperate habitats; found in bat guano and bird droppings
- **Blastomyces dermatitidis:** Temperate habitats; associated with decaying wood and soil
- **Coccidioides immitis:** Desert soils of southwestern US; causes "valley fever"
- **Paracoccidioides brasiliensis:** Tropical/subtropical Latin America
- **Cryptococcus neoformans:** Associated with pigeon droppings; two varieties with different distributions
- **Penicillium marneffei:** Southeast Asia; increasingly important in AIDS patients

### Sporothrix schenckii
Causes lymphocutaneous ("rose gardener's disease") infection. Worldwide distribution, associated with finely divided, cellulosic plant debris (peat moss, grass stalks). Some parts of Central and South America are hyperendemic. Converts to a budding yeast form within host tissue.

### Opportunistic Pathogens
A vast group causing disease primarily in immunocompromised hosts:

**Thermotolerant compost fungi:**
- Aspergillus fumigatus, A. flavus, A. terreus
- Pseudallescheria boydii
- Rhizopus species, Rhizomucor pusillus, Absidia corymbifera

**Yeasts:**
- Candida species (normally commensal; most common cause of opportunistic infection)
- Trichosporon species
- Malassezia species (basidiomycetous)

**Black yeasts (Herpotrichiellaceae):**
- Exophiala species
- Phialophora species

**Thermotolerant phytopathogens:**
- Bipolaris, Curvularia, Exserohilum (Cochliobolus anamorphs)
- Alternaria alternata
- Fusarium species (Liseola, Elegans, Martiella sections)
- Acremonium subgenus Acremonium

### Dermatophytes
Cause superficial infections of skin, hair, and nails:
- **Geophilic:** Normally soil-inhabiting; transmitted via soil contact
- **Zoophilic:** Associated with animal hosts
- **Anthropophilic:** Adapted to human hosts

### Subcutaneous Pathogens
Cause chromoblastomycosis, mycetoma, and related deep infections:
- Fonsecaea pedrosoi
- Cladophialophora carrionii
- Exophiala jeanselmei
- Madurella species
- Particularly diverse in tropical and subtropical regions

## Geographic and Ecological Distribution

At a 50,000-hectare tropical site, expected fungal pathogens include:
- 1-4 virulent systemic pathogens
- 10-20 agents of subcutaneous mycosis
- 3-10 agents of cutaneous/superficial infection
- 15-20 commensal yeasts
- 150-200 noncommensal potential opportunists
- 10-30 potential corneal invaders

Temperate sites have fewer subcutaneous pathogens but similar numbers of opportunists.

## Environmental Sources

Most fungal pathogens have environmental reservoirs:
- **Soil:** Histoplasma, Coccidioides, geophilic dermatophytes, many opportunists
- **Bird droppings:** Cryptococcus neoformans, Histoplasma
- **Compost/silage:** Aspergillus species, Mucorales, thermotolerant fungi
- **Plant debris:** Sporothrix, Fusarium, Bipolaris
- **Water:** Pseudallescheria (warm standing water)
- **Wood:** Trichosporon, some yeasts

## Veterinary Importance

Opportunistic fungi cause significant disease in domestic animals:
- **Mastitis:** Candida, Aspergillus, Cryptococcus
- **Abortion:** Aspergillus, Mucorales, Mortierella wolfii
- **Avian aspergillosis:** Particularly devastating in penguins; also turkeys, chickens
- **Heavy environmental exposure** to fungal spores in artificial shelters predisposes to infection

## Challenges in Medical Mycology

- Distinguishing true infection from contamination
- Many opportunists are also common environmental isolates
- Histopathological demonstration of tissue invasion often needed for definitive diagnosis
- Increasing numbers of immunocompromised patients expanding the spectrum of disease
- Antifungal drug resistance emerging in some species
