# Fungal Chitosan Biotechnology Applications

## Overview

Chitosan is a linear polysaccharide composed of randomly distributed β-(1→4)-linked D-glucosamine and N-acetyl-D-glucosamine units, produced commercially by the deacetylation of chitin — the second most abundant natural polymer after cellulose. While chitosan is most commonly derived from crustacean shell waste (shrimp, crab, krill), fungal chitosan offers several advantages including allergen-free production, consistent quality, and the ability to produce chitosan from specific fungal species cultivated on defined media. The biotechnology of fungal chitosan encompasses production, modification, and diverse applications across agriculture, medicine, environmental science, and industry.

## Fungal Sources of Chitin and Chitosan

### Zygomycetes (Mucoromycota)

Members of the order Mucorales are the richest fungal sources of chitosan, with cell wall chitosan content reaching 20–30% of dry weight:

- **Mucor rouxii (Rhizomucor miehei)**: The most studied fungal chitosan source, producing chitosan with 85–95% deacetylation directly in the cell wall, without requiring additional chemical deacetylation

### Seed Coating and Germination Enhancement

Chitosan seed coatings improve germination rates and seedling vigor:

- Enhances water uptake through semi-permeable film formation
- Provides antimicrobial protection during vulnerable germination period
- Stimulates root development through auxin-like activity
- Typical [[ingham-compost-tea-application-rates-and-timing]]: 0.1–1.0% chitosan solution as seed soak or coating

### Soil Amendment

When applied to soil, chitosan:

- Modifies soil microbial communities, generally increasing beneficial bacteria (Pseudomonas, Bacillus) and reducing pathogen populations
- Chelates micronutrients (Fe, Zn, Mn, Cu), improving their availability to plants
- Improves soil aggregate stability through polysaccharide-mediated particle binding
- Stimulates [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]], potentially through cell wall signaling mimicry

## Biomedical Applications

### Wound Healing

Fungal chitosan's wound healing properties are among its most clinically advanced applications:

- **Hemostatic activity**: Chitosan's positive charge attracts negatively charged red blood cell membranes, promoting rapid clot formation. Chitosan-based hemostatic dressings (e.g., Celox, HemCon) are used in military and civilian trauma care
- **Antimicrobial barrier**: Prevents wound infection by direct antimicrobial activity and by absorbing wound exudate
- **Fibroblast activation**: Stimulates fibroblast proliferation and collagen deposition, accelerating wound closure
- **Anti-inflammatory**: Modulates macrophage polarization toward the M2 (pro-healing) phenotype

### Drug Delivery

Chitosan's mucoadhesive properties make it an excellent drug delivery vehicle:

- **Nanoparticles**: Chitosan nanoparticles (50–500 nm) encapsulate drugs, protecting them from degradation and enabling targeted delivery. Prepared by ionic gelation with tripolyphosphate (TPP) or by emulsion crosslinking
- **Transmucosal delivery**: Chitosan transiently opens tight junctions between epithelial cells, enhancing paracellular transport of drugs across nasal, buccal, and intestinal mucosa
- **pH-responsive release**: Chitosan swells and releases encapsulated drugs in the acidic tumor microenvironment, enabling targeted cancer therapy

### Tissue Engineering

Chitosan scaffolds support cell attachment and proliferation for tissue regeneration:
- Chitosan-collagen composites for skin regeneration
- Chitosan-hydroxyapatite composites for bone tissue engineering

## Environmental Applications

### Water Treatment

Fungal chitosan is an effective biosorbent for fungal-wastewater-treatment
- **Flocculation**: Chitosan acts as a natural coagulant for turbidity removal, offering a biodegradable alternative to synthetic polyacrylamide

### Food Industry Applications

- **Preservative**: Antimicrobial films and coatings extend [[psilocybin-shelf-life-stability]] of fruits, vegetables, meat, and seafood
- **Edible films**: Chitosan-based edible films provide moisture barriers and antimicrobial protection
