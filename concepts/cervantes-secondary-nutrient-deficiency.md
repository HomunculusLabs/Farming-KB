### Deficiency
Very common indoors, especially in acidic soils (pH below 7). Lower
leaves develop yellow patches between dark green veins. Rusty-brown spots
appear on leaf margins, tips, and between veins. Brownish leaf tips curl
upward before dying. The entire plant could discolor in a few weeks.

Most often, magnesium is in the soil but unavailable because the root
environment is too wet and cold or acidic and cold. Also bound in soil
by excess potassium, ammonia (nitrogen), or calcium (carbonate). A high
EC diminishes magnesium availability.

**Progression:**
- No deficiency symptoms visible during first 3 to 4 weeks
- In the 4th to 6th week, first signs appear: interveinal yellowing and
  irregular rust-brown spots on older and middle-aged leaves
- Younger leaves remain healthy
- Leaf tips turn brown and curl upward as deficiency progresses
- Rust-brown spots multiply and interveinal yellowing increases
- Spots and yellowing progress from bottom to top of entire plant
- Younger leaves develop rust-colored spots and interveinal yellowing
- Leaves dry and die in extreme cases

**Treatment:** Water with two teaspoons of Epsom salts (magnesium sulfate)
per gallon of water. For fast results, spray foliage with a 2 percent
solution of Epsom salts. If deficiency progresses to the top, it will
turn green there first, then move down the plant over four to six days.
Continue regular watering with Epsom salts until symptoms totally
disappear. Adding fine [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]].

### Deficiency
Somewhat uncommon indoors but not uncommon in fiber hemp. Plants can
process more calcium than is available. Symptoms start with weak stems,
very dark green foliage, and slow growth. Young leaves affected first.
Severe deficiency causes new growing shoots to develop yellowish to
purple hues, disfigure, shrivel, and die. Bud development is inhibited,
plants stunted, harvest diminished.

Growing tips could show deficiency signs if humidity is maxed out. At 100
percent humidity, stomata close, stopping transpiration. Since calcium is
transported by transpiration, it becomes immobile.

**Progression:**
- Slow growth and young leaves turn very dark green
- New growing shoots discolor
- New shoots contort, shrivel, and die
- Bud development slows dramatically
