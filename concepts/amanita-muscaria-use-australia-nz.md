# Amanita muscaria Use in Australia and New Zealand

## Overview

Amanita muscaria (L. ex Fr.) Hooker, the iconic red-and-white spotted "fly
agaric," is a psychoactive mushroom distinct from the psilocybine-containing
species commonly referred to as "magic mushrooms." While psilocybian species
contain psilocybine and psilocine as their primary active compounds,
[[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] contains ibotenic acid and muscimol -- two entirely
different psychoactive substances with markedly different effects and risks.

This species is common throughout Australia. In New Zealand, it is largely
confined to stands of pine trees, having been introduced along with its
mycorrhizal tree associates.

## Traditional and Cultural Context

[[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] has been used traditionally by several aboriginal tribal
groups in Siberia, as well as by some members of the Ojibway clan in Northern
Michigan (United States) and Ontario, Canada. It has also been proposed as the
"Soma" drug-plant of the Rig Veda (the ancient Hindu scriptures) -- a theory
discussed in [[soma-controversy-fly-agaric-as-divine-mushroom]] and
[[mckenna-vedic-soma-indo-european-mysteries]].

Dr. Malcolm C. Hall, formerly of the Commonwealth Police Force of Canberra,
referred to a few instances of [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] use by members of the
drug subculture in New Zealand. The related species Amanita pantherina has
also been used as a recreational drug source in various parts of the world.

## Psychoactive Compounds

The two primary psychoactive substances in [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] are:

- **Ibotenic acid** -- a potent neurotoxin that causes neuronal death via a
  mechanism known as excitotoxicity. It is a glutamate receptor agonist that
  overstimulates neurons, leading to cell death. This compound is water-soluble
  and can be largely removed through proper preparation.

- **Muscimol** -- the decarboxylated form of ibotenic acid and the primary
  psychoactive agent responsible for the mushroom's effects. Muscimol acts as
  a GABA-A receptor agonist, producing sedative, dissociative, and
  hallucinogenic effects.

The pharmacology of these compounds is further discussed in
[[amanita-muscaria-pharmacology]].

## Effects and Limitations

The major limitations upon the use of [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]], particularly in
Australia and New Zealand, are the severe nausea and violent vomiting which
follow ingestion. These effects are so pronounced that the species is ingested
"only by the poorly informed," despite being quite widespread in its
distribution.

In New Zealand, attempts have been made to reduce the unpleasant effects by:

1. Peeling the red "skin" from the cap
2. Drying the mushrooms in the sun for several days

These preparation methods have not proven to be successful. The consequences
of ingestion are so unpleasant that one is led to speculate that the
[[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] employed by Siberian tribesmen contains a different
balance of chemicals than that found in New Zealand. Almost no users in New
Zealand are prepared to consume it more than once.

## The Ibotenic Acid Danger

Dr. Karl L. R. Jansen of the University of Auckland noted that "ibotenic acid
is a potent neurotoxin, causing neuronal death via a mechanism known as
excitotoxicity." This distinguishes [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] significantly from
psilocybian mushrooms, where the primary danger is psychological rather than
physical. The neurotoxic potential of ibotenic acid represents a genuine
health risk that does not exist with psilocybine-containing species.

## Distinction from Psilocybian Mushrooms

It is important to distinguish [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] from psilocybian species
in several key respects:

- Different active compounds (ibotenic acid/muscimol vs. psilocybine/psilocine)
- Different mechanism of action (GABAergic vs. serotonergic)
- More severe physical side effects (nausea, vomiting, potential neurotoxicity)
- Different duration and character of effects
- Greater potential for genuine physical harm

While psilocybian mushrooms are sometimes colloquially grouped with
[[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] under the umbrella term "magic mushrooms," they are
pharmacologically and experientially quite distinct. The
[[psychedelic-mushroom-set-and-setting]] considerations for Amanita use would
differ substantially from those for psilocybian species.

## Ecological Distribution

In Australia, [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] is widely distributed and common. In New
Zealand, its distribution is largely confined to stands of introduced pine
trees (particularly Pinus radiata), with which it forms mycorrhizal
associations. The species was almost certainly introduced to both countries
along with pine tree plantings, making it an exotic species in the Australasian
context, unlike some of the native psilocybian species such as
[[psilocybe-subaeruginosa-australia]].

## Siberian Comparison

The striking difference between the New Zealand experience of
[[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] and the Siberian tribal use raises interesting questions
about chemical variation within the species. Siberian tribesmen have used the
mushroom for centuries with apparent efficacy, often preparing it through
processes that may convert ibotenic acid to muscimol. The possibility that
different populations of A. muscaria contain different ratios of ibotenic
acid to muscimol is supported by the fact that New Zealand specimens are
almost universally rejected by users after a single experience.
