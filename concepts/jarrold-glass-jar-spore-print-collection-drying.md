# Jarrold Glass Jar Spore Print Collection, Sterilization, and Drying Technique

## Overview

The [[spore-syringe-preparation-jarrold-method]] uses small glass jars for spore [[sterile-spore-print-collection-glass-jar-technique]] print collection, offering a
practical and reusable approach to harvesting and storing [[query-how-to-store-mushroom-spores-long-term]] for future
cultivation. Unlike the traditional paper or foil spore print methods, the glass jar
technique produces spore prints that are immediately stored in sterile, sealable
containers — reducing contamination risk and simplifying long-term storage. This
method is designed for growers using the boil-a-bag cultivation system who need to
maintain a self-sustaining spore supply.

## Jar Sterilization Procedure

### Oven Sterilization Method
Before collecting spore prints, glass jars with metal screw lids must be thoroughly
sterilized to prevent contamination of the valuable spore harvest. Jarrold describes
the following procedure:
1. Obtain small, clean glass jars with metal screw lids.
2. Remove the lids and place them alongside the jars on a metal baking tray.
3. Place the tray under the grill (broiler) of an oven.
4. Sterilize at half the maximum oven heat for 30 minutes.
5. Allow the jars to cool inside the oven or in a clean environment.

### Cooling and Lid Handling
After sterilization, the cooling and lid handling procedure is critical for
maintaining sterility:
1. Once cooled, carefully remove jars from the grill.
2. Place a lid loosely on each jar — do not tighten.
3. Remove the tray from the grill area.
4. Tighten the lids firmly onto each jar for transport.

The lids are left loose during the initial cooling phase so that any residual moisture
inside the jars can evaporate, preventing condensation that could promote bacterial or
fungal growth during storage. The lids are tightened only once the jars are cool and
dry, creating a sealed sterile environment.

## Equipment Required

The following equipment is needed at the growing tank for spore print collection:
- Sterilized glass jars with lids
- Metal tweezers
- Metal scissors
- Methylated spirit burner (for [[flammability-hazard-alcohol-lysol-flame-sterilization-mycology]])
- Breathing mask or clean handkerchief (to prevent the grower's breath from
  contaminating the spore solution)

The breathing mask is specifically noted by Jarrold as being "not for your safety"
but rather to prevent the grower's exhaled breath — which carries bacteria and fungal
spores — from directly contaminating the spore print or the interior of the jar.

## Spore Print Collection Procedure

### Selecting Suitable Mushroom Caps
Not all mushroom caps are equally suitable for spore printing. Ideal candidates are:
- Mature mushrooms with fully opened caps and exposed gills
- Specimens at or near peak sporulation (not over-mature and decomposing)
- Healthy specimens free from contamination or disease
- Caps with intact gill structures that have not been damaged

### Step-by-Step Collection Process
1. Bring sterilized jars, tweezers, scissors, and spirit burner to the mushroom
   growing tank area.
2. Put on the breathing mask to prevent breath contamination.
3. Select a suitable mushroom cap and unscrew the lid of a sterile jar (leave it
   on but loosened).
4. Flame sterilize the tweezers and scissors using the methylated spirit burner.
5. Gently insert the flame-sterilized tweezers into the top of a mature,
   spore-laden cap.
6. Cut the cap from the stem using the flame-sterilized scissors.
7. Carefully place the cap gills-down into the sterile jar.
8. Immediately replace the lid — loosely, not tightly — so that any moisture on
   the cap or spores can evaporate naturally.

### Critical Hygiene Notes
- Flame sterilize tweezers and scissors between each cap to prevent cross-
  contamination between specimens.
- Work quickly to minimize the time the jar is open and exposed.
- Do not touch the inside of the jar or the gill surface of the cap with bare hands.
- The breathing mask must be worn throughout the entire procedure.
