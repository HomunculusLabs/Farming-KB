### Composition and Preparation

The dung/grain substrate combined:

- **Horse dung**: Providing a natural substrate that *P. cubensis* has evolved to colonize. Horse dung is rich in partially digested plant material, providing complex carbohydrates, nitrogen sources, and micronutrients. Its fiber content also provides structure and aeration to the substrate.
- **Rice grain**: Serving as a carbohydrate-rich supplement that boosts the nutritional value of the substrate. Rice grain provides readily metabolizable starch and some protein, supporting vigorous mycelial colonization and fruiting.
- **Water**: To achieve appropriate moisture content for mycelial growth.

### Naturalistic Relevance

The dung/grain substrate more closely approximates the natural substrate conditions that *P. cubensis* encounters in the wild:

- **Coprophilous ecology**: *P. cubensis* is a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species that naturally colonizes the dung of herbivorous animals, particularly cattle and horses, in tropical and subtropical regions.
- **Complex substrate**: The mixture of dung (complex, slowly metabolized) and rice grain (simple, rapidly metabolized) provides both immediate and sustained nutritional sources.
- **Bulk substrate**: The larger volume of substrate supports the development of multiple flushes of fruiting bodies over several weeks.

### Suitability for Fruiting Studies

The dung/grain substrate is specifically advantageous for fruiting experiments:

- **Supports multiple flushes**: Gartz et al. observed five flushes of fruiting bodies during the experimental period, demonstrating the substrate's capacity for sustained production.
- **Natural fruiting triggers**: The dung-based substrate provides the complex environmental cues (nutrient gradients, microbial activity) that can trigger fruiting initiation.
- **Realistic morphology**: Fruiting bodies produced on dung/grain substrates tend to exhibit more natural morphology than those produced on artificial substrates.
