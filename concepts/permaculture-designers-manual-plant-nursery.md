# Plant Nursery
**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison (1988)
## Overview
The plant nursery is a critical early-stage component of any permaculture
development. Mollison places nursery establishment among the first priorities
in site development, essential for providing the thousands of plants needed
to establish tree crops, windbreaks, ground covers, and garden polycultures.
## The Nursery in Site Development
The planning stage of any [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]] is critical. As plans are
drawn up, components needed early in development are placed first:
- Access ways
- Shelter
- Plant nursery
- Water supply
- Perhaps an energy source
Mollison emphasises: "Because impulsive sidetracks are usually expensive,
it is best to fully plan the site and its development."
When establishing a broadscale tree crop system, so many species and
individuals of each species are needed that it is usually necessary to
first create a small plant nursery to supply the 4,000-8,000 plants that
can be placed on a hectare. While these grow in their pots, the designer
can fence and prepare soil, then plant out to a carefully-designed
long-term plan.
## Nursery Design
### Flow Analysis
For designing any special work place, from a kitchen to a plant nursery,
Mollison recommends the "flow chart" method -- imagining how the process
flows through the space:
- Propagation (seed sowing, cuttings, division)
- Potting up
- Growing on (hardening off)
- Despatch or planting out
- Composting and recycling of media
### Critical Design Factors
- Plants do not survive [[query-how-to-prevent-transplant-shock-when-moving-cannabis-plants]] when soil temperatures exceed
  36 degrees C (97 degrees F), whether in deserts or in compost piles
- Many plants are frost-affected at or below 0 degrees C (32 degrees F)
- Shade houses and cold frames [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]] season
- Irrigation must be reliable -- drip systems are ideal
- Propagation benches should be at comfortable working height
- [[solomon-potting-mix-and-seedling-soil-recipe]] storage, tools, and labels within easy reach
### Structures and Infrastructure
A well-designed nursery typically includes:
- **Propagation area:** Under cover with heat mats and misting for
  cuttings and seed trays
- **Shade house:** 30-50% shade cloth for hardening off young plants
- **Cold frame:** Low glass or plastic-covered frame for [[holzer-frost-protection-techniques]]
  and early spring starts
- **Open growing area:** Full-sun space for established plants in pots
- **Compost bay:** For recycling spent potting media and organic waste
- **Water supply:** Reliable irrigation with header tank for gravity feed
## Propagation Methods
### Seed Propagation
- Direct sow large seeds into individual tubes or pots
- Surface sow fine seeds on seed-raising mix; do not bury deeply
- Stratify cold-climate seeds by refrigeration for 4-12 weeks
- Scarify hard-coated legume seeds by soaking in hot water or nicking
- Maintain consistent moisture with misting or humidity dome
### Cuttings
- Semi-hardwood cuttings for many tree species in late summer/autumn
- Softwood cuttings for herbaceous perennials in spring
- Use rooting hormone for difficult species
- Bottom heat (20-25 C) improves strike rates significantly
- Maintain high humidity until roots develop (2-6 weeks typical)
### Division and Offsets
- Divide clumping perennials in early spring or autumn
- Separate offsets from bulbs, corms, and tubers during dormancy
- Many grasses and ground covers spread readily by division
## Tropical Village Nurseries
In the humid tropics village complex checklist (Section 10), a plant
nursery is listed among essential village facilities, alongside housing,
processing, storage, wells, and home gardens. The nursery supplies:
- Food trees (citrus, nut trees, oil palm)
- Tree legumes (Acacia, Albizia, Leucaena)
- Ground covers (sweet potato, cassava, arrowroot, comfrey)
- Herbaceous crops (beans, buckwheat, Umbelliferae, chilies, pigeon pea)
## Farm Forestry Nurseries
In the farm forestry section (12.10), Mollison lists several nursery
enterprises essential to regional self-reliance:
- **Bamboo nursery:** Together with canegrasses and large clump grasses
- **Aquatic and edge plant nursery:** Fish forages, insectary species,
  marshland perennials
- **Berry fruit and vine nursery:** Many species grow in mesothermal
  climates
- **Conservation and reforestation trees:** Pioneer and high-value species
- **Speciality nurseries:** Herbs, salt-affected areas, medicinals
## Bioregional Plant Resources
In the bioregional organisation framework (Section 14.5), Category A1
(Plant Resources) includes nurseries, botanical gardens, research
 institutes, seed sources, exchanges, native reserves, and demonstration
 farms. A bioregion should have comprehensive
plant resource inventories and nurseries capable of supplying all needed
species for local development.
## Key Principles
1. **Establish early:** The nursery is among the first infrastructure to
   create on any site
2. **Species diversity:** Grow 4,000-8,000 plants per hectare; diversity
   is essential
3. **Flow efficiency:** Design for efficient movement through propagation
   stages
4. **Climate protection:** Provide shade and frost protection
5. **Regional role:** Nurseries are a key bioregional resource
6. **Integration:** Connect nursery to composting, water, and seed-saving
7. **Species collections:** Maintain useful species for community access
