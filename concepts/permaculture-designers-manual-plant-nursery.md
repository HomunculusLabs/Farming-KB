# Plant Nursery

**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison (1988)
**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

The plant nursery is a critical early-stage component of any permaculture development. Mollison places nursery establishment among the first priorities in site development, essential for providing the thousands of plants needed to establish tree crops, windbreaks, ground covers, and garden polycultures.

## The Nursery in Site Development

The planning stage of any permaculture design is critical. As plans are drawn up, components needed early in development are placed first:
- Access ways
- Shelter
- Plant nursery
- Water supply
- Perhaps an energy source

Mollison emphasises: "Because impulsive sidetracks are usually expensive, it is best to fully plan the site and its development."

When establishing a broadscale tree crop system, so many species and individuals of each species are needed that it is usually necessary to first create a small plant nursery to supply the 4,000-8,000 plants that can be placed on a hectare. While these grow in their pots, the designer can fence and prepare soil, then plant out to a carefully-designed long-term plan.

## Nursery Design

### Flow Analysis
For designing any special work place, from a kitchen to a plant nursery, Mollison recommends the "flow chart" method -- imagining how the process flows:
- Propagation (seed sowing, cuttings, division)
- Potting up
- Growing on (hardening off)
- Despatch or planting out
- Composting and recycling of media

### Critical Design Factors
- Plants do not survive transplant shock from nursery stock when soil temperatures exceed 36 degrees C (97 degrees F), whether in deserts or in compost piles
- Many plants are frost-affected at or below 0 degrees C (32 degrees F)
- Shade houses and cold frames extend the growing season
- Irrigation must be reliable -- drip systems are ideal

## Tropical Village Nurseries

In the humid tropics village complex checklist (Section 10), a plant nursery is listed among essential village facilities, alongside:
- Housing around the compound
- Processing, storage, threshing, and meeting areas
- Well, piped water, tanks, and dam
- Cooperative store and fuel depots
- 0.25-1.0 hectare home garden per house

The nursery supplies planting materials for:
- Food trees (citrus, nut trees, oil palm)
- Tree legumes (Acacia, Albizia, Leucaena)
- Ground covers (sweet potato, cassava, arrowroot, comfrey)
- Herbaceous crops (beans, buckwheat, Umbelliferae, chilies, pigeon pea)

## Farm Forestry Nurseries

In the farm forestry section (12.10), Mollison lists several nursery enterprises essential to regional self-reliance:

### Bamboo Nursery
Together with canegrasses and large clump grasses. Bamboos are a currently neglected forestry resource.

### Aquatic and Edge Plant Nursery
Including fish forages, insectary species, and marshland perennials for bee fodders, duck forage, and wildlife refuges.

### Berry Fruit and Vine Nursery
A great many species grow in mesothermal climates.

### Conservation and Reforestation Trees
For extending natural forests; includes pioneer and selected high-value tree crop species.

### Speciality Nurseries
For herbs, salt-affected areas, smog tolerance, medicinals, food dyes, and stabilisers.

## Bioregional Plant Resources

In the bioregional organisation framework (Section 14.5), Category A1 (Plant Resources) includes:
- Nurseries
- Botanical gardens
- Research institutes
- Seed sources and exchanges
- Native reserves
- Demonstration farms
- Skilled people
- Publications
- Contractors
- Produce sources
- Species checklists

A bioregion should have comprehensive plant resource inventories and nurseries capable of supplying all needed species for local development.

## Combined Nursery Operations

Mollison advocates combined plant nurseries and new species collections that provide:
- Seed for direct sowing
- Bud and scion materials for grafting
- Vegetative propagules (cuttings, tubers, divisions)
- Grafted, layered, or tissue-cultured plants

Pioneer or innovative farmers often become providers of plant materials to others requiring sufficient stock to commence commercial operations. If a bioregion is integrated and cooperative, such enterprises can cope with very different plant/animal groups.

## Key Principles

1. **Establish early:** The nursery is among the first infrastructure to create on any site
2. **Species diversity:** Grow 4,000-8,000 plants per hectare of target system -- diversity is essential
3. **Flow efficiency:** Design the nursery for efficient movement through propagation stages
4. **Climate protection:** Provide shade, frost protection, and irrigation as needed
5. **Regional role:** Nurseries are a key bioregional resource -- plan for regional supply
6. **Integration:** Connect nursery to composting, water, and seed-saving systems
7. **Species collections:** Maintain collections of useful species for community access
