# Micro Hydro Power

Micro hydro power generates electricity from flowing water on a small scale (typically under 100 kW). For homesteads with access to a stream or creek, micro hydro is often the most cost-effective and reliable renewable energy source. Unlike solar and wind, which fluctuate with weather and time of day, a properly designed micro hydro system produces power 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. (see [[off-grid-electricity-basics]]).

## Why Micro Hydro Is Special

- **Continuous power**: Produces electricity 24/7, unlike solar (daylight only) or wind (variable)
- **Predictable output**: Flow rates are more consistent than sun or wind
- **High capacity factor**: 50-90% (vs. 15-25% for solar, 20-35% for wind)
- **Low cost per kWh**: Often the cheapest renewable energy source over its lifetime
- **Long lifespan**: Equipment lasts 25-50 years with minimal maintenance
- **Small footprint**: Minimal land use compared to solar arrays or wind towers

## Assessing Your Resource

### The Two Key Measurements

**Head (Vertical Drop)**
The vertical distance the water falls from the intake to the turbine. Measured in feet or meters. (see [[comparison-fermented-vegetables-vs-canning]]).

- Low head: 2-20 feet
- Medium head: 20-100 feet
- High head: 100+ feet

**Flow Rate**
The volume of water flowing past a point per unit time. Measured in gallons per minute (GPM) or liters per second (L/s).

- Small flow: 10-50 GPM
- Medium flow: 50-200 GPM
- Large flow: 200+ GPM

### Measuring Head

1. **Surveyor's level or transit**: Most accurate
2. **GPS altitudes**: Measure elevation at intake and turbine — subtract for head
3. **Pressure gauge**: Attach to a pipe at the bottom of the fall; every 2.31 psi = 1 foot of head
4. **Water level method**: Run a hose filled with water from intake to turbine; measure the height difference
5. **Topographic maps**: Approximate for initial assessment

### Measuring Flow

1. **Bucket method**: Time how long to fill a bucket of known volume at the intended intake point
2. **Weir method**: Build a temporary weir (notch) across the stream; measure water depth flowing through
3. **Float method**: Measure stream width, depth, and surface velocity with a floating object
4. **USGS gauges**: Check online gauge data for your stream if available

### Calculating Power Output

The theoretical power available from a stream:

Power (watts) = Head (feet) x Flow (GPM) / 10

Real-world efficiency factor: multiply by 0.50-0.60 (typical system efficiency)

Example: 50 feet of head x 100 GPM / 10 x 0.55 = 275 watts continuous

Annual energy: 275 watts x 24 hours x 365 days = 2,409,000 Wh = 2,409 kWh/year

## Turbine Types by Head

### High Head (100+ feet): Pelton Wheel

- Impulse turbine — water jets hit cup-shaped buckets on a wheel
- Best for high head, low flow situations
- Very efficient (80-90%)
- Can handle debris in water
- Simple, reliable, long-lasting
- Runner diameter: 4-24 inches depending on flow and head

### Medium Head (20-100 feet): Francis Turbine

- Reaction turbine — water flows through enclosed runner blades
- Best for medium head, medium flow
- Efficient (80-90%)
- More complex than Pelton
- Common in commercial micro hydro installations

### Low Head (2-20 feet): Propeller or Crossflow

**Propeller (Kaplan) Turbine:**
- Reaction turbine with propeller-like blades
- Best for low head, high flow
- 70-85% efficient
- Requires precise flow matching for best efficiency

**Crossflow (Banki) Turbine:**
- Impulse turbine — water passes through a drum-shaped runner twice
- Handles a wide range of heads and flows
- 70-80% efficient
- Simple construction, can be DIY-built
- Good for variable flow conditions

### Ultra-Low Head (2-10 feet): Archimedes Screw

- Water flows through a rotating screw
- Can handle debris (including fish — fish-friendly design)
- 60-75% efficient
- Simple, robust, low maintenance
- Growing in popularity for very low head sites

## System Components

### Intake and Penstock

**Intake Structure:**
- Screens to exclude debris and fish
- Desander to remove abrasive particles
- Overflow/spillway for flood conditions
- Must not block the entire stream (environmental regulations)

**Penstock (Pipe):**
- Carries water from intake to turbine
- Material: PVC (low head), HDPE (medium head), steel (high head)
- Size: Large enough to minimize friction losses (usually 4-12 inch diameter)
- Longest-lived component — design for 50+ years
- Bury below frost line for freeze protection

### Turbine and Generator

- Turbine converts water energy to mechanical rotation
- Generator converts mechanical rotation to electricity
- Direct-drive (generator on turbine shaft) or belt-drive (gears up speed)
- Permanent magnet alternator (PMA): Most common for micro hydro — no external field excitation needed

### Power Conditioning

- **Controller**: Regulates voltage and frequency
- **Dump load**: Diverts excess power when batteries are full (water heating element)
- **Inverter**: Converts DC to AC for household use (if battery-based system)
- **Battery bank**: Stores energy for peak demand periods

### Tailrace

- Channel that returns water to the stream below the turbine
- Must meet environmental regulations (fish passage, water temperature)

## Permitting and Regulations

- **Water rights**: Many states require water rights or permits to divert stream flow
- **Fish and wildlife**: Federal and state agencies protect aquatic habitat
- **Dam safety**: Even small diversions may require permits
