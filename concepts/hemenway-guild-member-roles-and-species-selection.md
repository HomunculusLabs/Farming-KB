# Guild Member Roles and Species Selection

Toby Hemenway defines seven key roles that should be filled in
any well-designed gaias-garden-nitrogen-fixers-guild-design to
guilds keeps nutrients cycling and reduces fertilizer needs.
Since nitrogen is freely available from the air, lugging bags
of it into the garden seems wasteful. Perennial options include
Dutch or New Zealand [[fukuoka-white-clover-cover-crop-system]], alfalfa, and lupine. Annual
or re-seeding options include vetch, cowpeas, beans, and peas.

Cowpeas and fava beans are edible, stacking another product into
the guild. Hemenway typically pokes one or two dozen fava seeds
into the soil beneath each [[comparison-berry-growing-vs-fruit-tree-pruning]] in late summer, harvesting
the pods in spring and mulching the stalks in place.

### 5. Insectary Plants

Flowers that attract pollinators and predatory insects are
essential for fruit set and [[comparison-pheromone-based-pest-control-vs-beneficial-insect-biocontrol]]. See gaias-garden-predatory-insects-attracting-beneficials for species lists. Borage and comfrey are among
the most popular insectary plants for fruit tree guilds.

### 6. Pest Repellents

Certain plants exude substances that repel pests. Nasturtiums
and some marigolds are the best-documented examples. However,
pest-repellent plants are the least understood guild members and
should be used cautiously, as they may repel [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]]
as well as pests.

### 7. Habitat Nooks

Piling up stones, logs, or brush near the guild, and creating
small ponds and puddles, attracts lizards, frogs, snakes, and
birds. Garter snakes and lizards nest under rocks and are
valuable predators of slugs, leaf-eating insects, and harmful
larvae. Predators are important for preserving balance: if any
prey species begins to reproduce exuberantly, a waiting
population of predators will cull their numbers efficiently.

## Multifunctional Members

Many guild members fill more than one role. Clover and alfalfa
are nitrogen fixers that also attract bees. Plantain and yarrow
sequester nutrients and are medicinal. Artichokes produce both
mulch and food. Dandelions and chicory accumulate minerals and
are edible. Comfrey yields mulch, medicine, [[hemenway-insectary-plants-and-beneficial-insects]],
potassium-rich leaves for fertilizer tea, and more.

Clever guild designers will choose as many multifunctional
members as possible. This begets a guild that swells with
connections, making the plant community flexible, responsive,
and robust. Plants acting in community can survive weather
extremes, soil problems, pest invasions, and other onslaughts
far better than isolated species.

## Sizing the Guild

The mature size of the central tree guides plant quantities. A
dwarf or semi-dwarf tree supports fewer guild members than a
large standard tree. As a rule of thumb: one central tree, one
or two large perennials like artichokes, several comfrey
plants, a dozen insectary plants, dozens of bulbs, and
hundreds of clovers. Avoid aggressive climbers like vetch
under dwarf trees.
