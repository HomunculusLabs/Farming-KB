# Oyster Mushroom Lifecycle and Reproduction

The oyster mushroom (*Pleurotus* spp.) follows the typical basidiomycete lifecycle, cycling between vegetative and reproductive growth phases. Understanding this lifecycle is essential for successful cultivation and [[mushroom-cultivation-basics]].

## Spore Production and Germination

Four basidiospores form at the tip of each basidium on the gill of a mature fruitbody. Each spore contains a single haploid nucleus. Under suitable conditions, spores germinate into primary mycelium (monokaryotic phase). Optimal temperature for spore germination is 24-28°C.

## Mycelial Growth Phases

- **Primary mycelium (monokaryotic):** Germinated hyphae containing a single nucleus per cell. These cannot produce fruitbodies on their own.
- **Secondary mycelium (dikaryotic):** Formed through plasmogamy — the fusion of two compatible primary mycelia. There is a 25% chance that any two primary mycelia will be sexually compatible. Secondary mycelia are distinguished by clamp connections, with each cell containing two nuclei. Only secondary mycelia can produce fruitbodies.

## Intersterility Groups

Mating compatibility studies have identified **eleven discrete intersterility groups** within the genus *Pleurotus* (Groups I–XI). These groups define biological species boundaries:

| Intersterility Group | Species |
|---|---|
| I | *P. ostreatus*, *P. columbinus*, *P. florida*, *P. salignus* |
| II | *P. pulmonarius*, *P. sajor-caju*, *P. sapidus* |
| III | *P. populinus* |
| IV | *P. cornucopiae*, *P. citrinopileatus* |
| V | *P. djamor*, *P. flabellatus*, *P. ostreatoroseus* |
| VI | *P. eryngii*, *P. ferulae*, *P. nebrodensis* |
| VII | *P. cystidiosus*, *P. abalonus* |
| VIII | *P. calyptratus* |
| IX | *P. dryinus* |
| X | *P. purpureo-olivaceus* |
| XI | *P. tuber-regium* |

Some reports indicate partial compatibility between groups, suggesting the possibility of hybrid species creation.

## Spawn Generation System

Commercial spawn production follows a three-generation system:

1. **Mother Culture (1st generation):** Isolated secondary mycelium from tissue culture or spore germination. Tissue culture is preferred because it preserves the genetic characteristics of the parent mushroom. Spore culture introduces genetic variation through recombination.
2. **Mother Spawn (2nd generation):** Mother culture inoculated onto grain substrate and incubated. This is a grain-to-grain expansion step.
3. **Mushroom Spawn (3rd generation):** Mother spawn inoculated onto fresh grain substrate. This is what most growers purchase.

### Spawn Vitality and Transfer Limits

Repeated subculturing progressively lowers spawn vitality and increases the risk of mutation. Spawn should **not be transferred more than 5–7 times** from the original mother culture. Most commercial growers utilize third-generation spawn for optimal vigor.

## Primordia Formation and Fruiting

When environmental conditions shift, the mycelium transitions from vegetative to reproductive growth:

1. **Primordia (pin) formation** is triggered by environmental stressors:
   - Temperature drop of ~10°C from incubation temperature
   - High relative humidity (80–95%)
   - Light exposure (200–500 lux for most species)
   - Fresh air exchange to reduce CO₂ below 800 ppm
2. **Fruitbody development** proceeds from pins to mature mushrooms, with stipe elongation and cap expansion.
3. In the basidia of mature fruitbodies, two nuclei fuse (karyogamy), undergo meiosis, and produce four haploid nuclei that become new basidiospores — completing the cycle.

## Temperature Optima by Stage

| Stage | Temperature Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spore germination | 24–28°C | Requires moisture |
| Mycelial growth | 20–30°C (species-dependent) | P. ostreatus opt. 25°C; thermophilic strains up to 35°C |
| Primordia formation | 10–20°C | Cold shock most effective for P. ostreatus and P. eryngii |
| Fruiting body development | Species-dependent | P. ostreatus: 10–17°C; P. eryngii: 13–18°C; P. florida: 15–25°C |

## Environmental Triggers Summary

The transition from vegetative to reproductive growth is induced by "worsening" conditions — the mycelium can no longer sustain vegetative growth and shifts to reproduction. Key triggers:

- **Temperature drop:** Most effective trigger; 10°C below incubation optimum
- **High humidity:** 80–95% relative humidity
- **Light:** 50–500 lux (species-dependent); some strains require light for primordia
- **Fresh air exchange:** Reduces CO₂, provides oxygen for fruiting metabolism
