High Intensity Discharge (HID) lighting is the standard for serious indoor [[cervantes-high-pressure-sodium-lighting]] (HPS) lamps from major manufacturers.

## Key Metrics

- **Lumens**: Total light output (higher = brighter)

- **PAR Watts**: Photosynthetically Active Radiation — the portion of light plants actually use for photosynthesis

- **CCT**: Correlated Color Temperature in Kelvin — ~3K-4K is blue-white (MH, vegetative), ~2K is orange-red (HPS, flowering)

## General Guidelines

- **[[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]]**: High Pressure Sodium (HPS) — orange-red spectrum promotes bud development

- **PAR watts matter more than lumens** — PAR measures the [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]. A 600W lamp covers 5x5 feet (flowering) or 6x6 feet (vegetative). A 1000W lamp covers 6x6 feet (flowering) or 8x8 feet (vegetative). Light intensity decreases exponentially with distance — the inverse square law means doubling the distance from bulb to canopy reduces light to one-quarter.

## MH vs HPS Quick Comparison

| Feature | MH | HPS |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Spectrum | Blue-white (~3K-5K) | Orange-red (~2K) |
| Best for | Vegetative growth | Flowering |
| Lumens per watt | Lower | Higher |
| Plant height | Compact, bushy | Stretchier |
| Bud density | Less | More |

## Equipment Recommendations

Digital (electronic) ballasts are preferred over magnetic ballasts — they run cooler, quieter, and more efficiently. They also allow dimming (running a 1000W bulb at 600W or 400W). Air-cooled reflector hoods are essential for managing heat in enclosed spaces. A light meter ($20-50) helps verify proper coverage. Light rails or movers can increase effective coverage area by 20-30%.

## Common Mistakes

Placing lights too close causes leaf burn and heat stress; too far reduces intensity and causes stretching. Using expired bulbs significantly reduces yield — most growers underestimate how quickly output degrades. Mixing MH and HPS in the same space without separate switching can waste energy. Failing to account for heat output when sizing ventilation leads to temperature problems. Using the wrong ballast type for a given bulb can cause premature failure or fire hazard.
