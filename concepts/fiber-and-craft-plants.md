# Fiber and Craft Plants for Permaculture

Permaculture systems can produce a remarkable range of fibre, craft, and construction materials alongside food. Many [[phragmites-australis]]** (common reed): Used for bags, baskets, matting, sandals, thatching, arrows, spears, and paper
- **[[broussonetia-papyrifera]]** (paper mulberry): Australian Aborigines made string from bark fibres; bark yields fibre suitable for the [[permaculture-castor-oil-plant]] from seeds (35-55% oil content), used in industry, pharmaceuticals, leather dressing, soap, candles, cosmetics, and textiles.

### Other Paper Species
- **Morus spp** (mulberry): Bark used in China and Europe for making paper
- **Broussonetia papyrifera** (paper mulberry): Bark yields fibre for the textile industry
- **Typha spp**: Used for paper making -- the paper is strong but difficult to bleach, useful as packaging paper and cardboard

## Craft Timber Species

### Acacia melanoxylon (Blackwood)
Highly valued craft timber, one of the world's best cabinet woods. Fine, even grain, excellent turning and finishing qualities. Used for fine furniture, veneer, and craft work.

### Casuarina spp
Hard, dense wood suited to the fine crafts industry. Casuarinas are easy to propagate, both by seed and cutting.

### Various Acacia Species
- **A. cambagei** (gingerbread wattle): Wood has potential for hard, light tool handles and craft uses
- **A. aneura** (mulga): Wood used for craft timber, fence posts, and mine timbers
- **A. excelsa** (ironwood): Striking colour, used as craft wood, turns well and takes high polish
- **A. harpophylla** (brigalow): Wood used for mine timbers, fence posts, hardboard, and fine wood crafts

### Oak Species (Quercus spp)
- **Q. alba** (white oak): Wood used for barrels, staves, flooring, cabinet making, boat building
- **Q. suber** (cork oak): Source of cork; also produces craft timber
- **Q. michauxii** (basket oak): Named for basket-making tradition
- **Q. robur** (English oak): Valued for cabinet making, carving, and construction

### Eucalyptus spp
Many eucalypts produce excellent craft and furniture timbers. Notable species include E. marginata (jarrah), E. regnans (mountain ash), and E. delegatensis (alpine ash) for their hard, durable woods.

### Populus spp (Poplars)
First quality poplar wood is used in furniture manufacture for interiors of cupboards, sideboards, and shelving. Also used for matches, fruit boxes, flooring, kitchen utensils, wooden toys, drawing boards, and tables. The grey poplar (*P. x canescens*) produces a smooth, non-splintering, non-warping, lightweight wood.

### Dalbergia sissoo (Sissoo)
Hard, elastic, close-grained wood valued for furniture, veneer, and boat building. Good firewood (specific gravity 0.64-0.7). Can be managed in coppice rotation. Excellent carving and bending wood.

### Neem (Azadirachta indica)
Wood similar to Cuban mahogany, resists decay and insects, tougher than teak. Excellent for construction and furniture making. Termite attack is rare.

## Textile Fibre Plants

### Agave spp
Leaves are a source of sisal fibre, one of the main fibre crops of the world. Also yields juice used for tequila production. One of the main food sources of California desert Indians.
