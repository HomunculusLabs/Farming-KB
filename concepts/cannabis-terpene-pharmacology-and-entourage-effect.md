## Cannabis Terpene Pharmacology and the Entourage Effect

## Introduction

Cannabis produces over 200 terpenes — volatile aromatic compounds responsible for the characteristic fragrance of different cannabis varieties. Beyond their olfactory contribution, terpenes have significant pharmacological activities of their own and, through the "entourage effect," modulate the effects of cannabinoids like THC and CBD. The interplay between terpenes, cannabinoids, and other cannabis constituents produces therapeutic outcomes that cannot be predicted from any single compound in isolation.

## Major Cannabis Terpenes

### Myrcene

Myrcene (β-myrcene) is typically the most abundant terpene in cannabis, comprising 20–50% of the total [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]].
- Anti-inflammatory: Reduces TNF-α, IL-1β, and NF-κB signaling.
- Gastroprotective: Protects against gastric ulcers through antioxidant and anti-inflammatory mechanisms.
- Chemopreventive: Induces phase I and phase II liver enzymes that detoxify carcinogens.
- Promotes absorption of other terpenes and cannabinoids through topical application.

### β-Caryophyllene

β-Caryophyllene (BCP) is a sesquiterpene with a spicy, peppery aroma found in [[blesching-cannabis-multiple-sclerosis]] and Parkinson's disease.

### Linalool

Linalool is a monoterpene alcohol with a floral, lavender-like aroma. It is the primary aromatic compound in lavender and is found in many cannabis cultivars, particularly those with "indica" character.

**Pharmacology**:
- Anxiolytic: Potent anti-anxiety effects via modulation of GABA_A receptors and NMDA receptors.
- Sedative-hypnotic: Enhances sleep quality and duration; synergizes with the sedative effects of myrcene and THC.
- Analgesic: Modulates pain perception through both peripheral and central mechanisms.
- Anti-convulsant: Reduces seizure severity in animal models; potential application in treatment-resistant epilepsy.
- Anti-inflammatory: Reduces edema and cytokine production.

### α-Pinene and β-Pinene

The pinenes are bicyclic monoterpenes with a characteristic pine aroma. α-Pinene is the most common terpene in the natural world and is found in pine needles, rosemary, and many cannabis varieties.

**Pharmacology**:
- **Memory enhancement**: α-Pinene inhibits acetylcholinesterase (AChE), increasing acetylcholine availability in the brain. This counteracts the short-term memory impairment caused by THC, which acts through CB1 receptors in the hippocampus.
- Bronchodilator: Opens airways, potentially beneficial for asthma when inhaled.
- Anti-inflammatory: Reduces TNF-α and IL-6 production.
- Antibiotic: Significant antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive bacteria.

### Terpinolene

Terpinolene has a complex, piney, herbal, and slightly floral aroma. It is present at lower concentrations in most cannabis cultivars but is characteristic of certain "sativa" varieties.

**Pharmacology**:
- Sedative: Moderate central nervous system depressant effects.
- Antioxidant: Potent free radical scavenger, comparable in potency to α-tocopherol.
- Anticancer: Induces apoptosis and cell cycle arrest in certain [[staphylococcus-aureus]]*.
