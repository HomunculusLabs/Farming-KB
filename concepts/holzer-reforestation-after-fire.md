# Holzer Reforestation After Forest Fires

Forest fires devastate enormous areas globally each year. Holzer argues that
most forest fires are not natural disasters but man-made catastrophes resulting
from monoculture forestry, destroyed hydrological balance, and wrong land
management. However, fire also creates opportunity: the misfortune holds the
seed of hope. Burnt biomass is excellent natural fertiliser, and action taken
quickly can transform devastated land into productive permaculture landscapes.

## Why Forests Burn

A healthy, mixed forest in full sap does not burn. Conifer monocultures of
spruce and pine grow fast but burn easily. Eucalyptus monocultures covering
hundreds of kilometres in Portugal are essentially fire traps. Birch, as a
pioneer plant, burns even when still green. Oak or beech need to season first
and are almost impossible to burn when healthy. The root cause is always
disturbed hydrological balance: when the land cannot hold water, vegetation
dries out and becomes fuel.

## The Trench Method for Burnt Areas

After a fire near Lisbon, Holzer proposed a technique for recultivating
desert-like, burnt sandy ground:

1. Dig big trenches in a north-south direction, against prevailing winds,
   using a digger.
2. Fill trenches with all remaining wood and biomass -- even burnt remnants --
   to a depth of 1-2m.
3. Pile up the leftover sand and soil as walls on either side, about 1m
   high, creating windbreaks. The result looks like waves in the ocean.
4. Scatter mixed tree seeds by walking behind the digger: nuts, chestnuts,
   cherries, oaks, apple trees, cedars, beeches and more.

### Why It Works

- Wood in the ground slowly decays and attracts moisture like a sponge.
- Rainwater seeps in and is stored, released slowly and steadily.
- Decomposing biomass generates warmth that rises and helps seeds germinate.
- Windbreaks protect seedlings from desiccating winds.
- A protected microclimate develops between the trench walls.
- Young plants grow quickly with warmth, moisture, and [[cervantes-wind-protection-outdoor-cannabis]].

## Seeding from the Air

For vast burnt areas, Holzer recommends seeding by plane or helicopter before
the next snowmelt. The fire breaks open the ground surface, so seeds need no
preparation. Winter moisture carries nutrient-rich ash into the ground with
the seedlings. If this opportunity is missed, wind carries grass seeds back,
and the land becomes covered in dry grass -- tinder for the next fire.

## Soil Recovery After Fire

Forest fires profoundly alter soil chemistry and biology. While ash adds a
pulse of minerals including potassium, calcium, magnesium, and phosphorus to
the soil surface, the combustion of [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] destroys the humus layer
that normally retains these nutrients. Without organic matter to act as a
buffer, minerals leach rapidly with the first rains. This is why Holzer's
trench method is so effective: the buried wood provides a replacement carbon
sponge that captures and holds these mineral nutrients before they wash away.

Soil microbial communities are devastated by fire, with temperatures above
60°C killing most beneficial bacteria and fungi. [[soil-foodweb]].
