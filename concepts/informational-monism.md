# Informational Monism

Informational monism is the philosophical position that reality consists of one fundamental substance only: information.

This framework, derived from analysis of entheogenic (psychedelic) experiences, proposes that both mind and matter are particular forms of information, resolving the centuries-old mind/[[weil-the-natural-mind-altered-states-normalcy]] of awareness lie in the realm of the intangible mind. Substances like psilocybin, which have a close molecular resemblance to the brain's neurotransmitters, elicit predictable and characteristic changes in consciousness.

Psilocybin therefore bridges the conceptual gap between the two seemingly incompatible worlds of mind and matter. The more we understand how psilocybin works, the closer we get to understanding the bridge linking mind to matter.

## The Core Propositions

The informational monism framework rests on several interconnected propositions:

**First**, consciousness is a form of information substantiated within the brain's neuronal firing activity. Chemically induced changes in global states of neuronal firing are equatable with changes in consciousness, and since global states of neuronal firing must be global states of information, we can conclude that consciousness is a form of information.

**Second**, this cerebral information has a natural tendency to organize and integrate itself. Evidence comes from examining entheogenic phenomenology and dreaming, both processes highlighting the way in which psychological information organizes itself without deliberate effort on our part.

**Third**, physical matter can also be described in terms of information. If both mind and matter are informational in nature, the entire reality process can be understood as an evolutionary flow of self-organizing information, with human consciousness occupying a significant functional role.

## What Information Means Here

The concept of information in this framework is broad. Books carry information, as do CDs, apple seeds, bank statements, fossils, fiber-optic cables, hormones, food wrappers, and the human genome. So too do vast banks of firing neurons carry information, whether infused by psilocybin or not.

The question of whether atoms themselves are units of information is the more challenging claim. However, if both consciousness and matter can be described in informational terms, then the mind and body can be seen as consisting of essentially the same kind of substance.

Of course the concept of what information itself is, or what information actually means, is a decidedly muddy issue. As to the notion of atoms being units of information also, the case is less clear. However, should we succeed in accurately defining the [[tryptamine-hallucinogens-and-nature-of-consciousness]] and matter in informational terms, then we should also be able to explain more clearly why psilocybin is able to generate both Other-derived visions and an altered perception of reality.

## Entheogenic Evidence

Entheogenic phenomenology provides the most compelling evidence for informational monism. When psilocybin enters the brain, it alters the global state of neuronal firing.

Since states of neuronal firing are states of information (what else could they be?), and these altered states correspond to profoundly different states of consciousness, we can observe a direct mapping between information patterns and conscious experience.
