# Green Manure Cover Crop Species

[[green-manure-cover-crops]] are specific plant species grown primarily to
improve soil fertility and structure rather than for harvest. In the
JADAM system, cover crops serve as the primary source of base fertilizer,
reducing or eliminating the need for purchased organic inputs. Different
cover crop species offer distinct advantages for soil building, nitrogen
fixation, organic matter addition, and pest disruption.

## Role in the JADAM System

Cover crops are the foundation of the [[ultra-low-jadam-fertilizer-program]]. The goal
is to grow enough cover crop biomass to supply 100 percent of base
fertilizer needs without any external inputs. When combined with
[[ultra-low-jadam-liquid-fertilizer-making]] made from cover crop
residues, the system approaches a closed nutrient loop where very little
is imported from outside the farm.

The JADAM philosophy emphasizes that the best fertilizer for any crop is
material that is the same as or similar to that crop. Cover crops, being
plants themselves, provide nutrient profiles similar to cash crops. When
cover crops are turned into the soil or decompose on the surface, they
release nutrients in a balanced form that supports healthy crop growth.

## Hairy Vetch (Vicia villosa)

Hairy vetch is one of the most important leguminous cover crops in the
JADAM system. As a nitrogen-fixing legume, it can capture atmospheric
nitrogen and convert it into plant-available form, reducing or
eliminating the need for nitrogen fertilizer.

Hairy vetch produces abundant biomass and has a sprawling growth habit
that provides excellent soil coverage. Its vigorous root system
penetrates compacted soil layers, improving soil structure. When used as
a living mulch or killed and left as surface residue, it suppresses
weed growth and conserves soil moisture. The nitrogen-rich residues
decompose quickly, providing a steady nutrient release to subsequent
crops.

## Cereal Rye (Secale cereale)

Cereal rye is a hardy winter annual that provides massive amounts of
organic matter. It grows well in poor soils and cold conditions, making
it an excellent choice for fall planting. Rye produces an extensive
fibrous root system that prevents erosion and improves soil structure.

Rye's allelopathic properties suppress weed germination, providing
natural weed control for subsequent crops. Its high carbon-to-nitrogen
ratio means it decomposes more slowly than legumes, providing longer-
lasting organic matter in the soil. Rye is often planted in combination
with hairy vetch for a balanced carbon and nitrogen contribution.

## Milk Vetch (Astragalus sinicus)

Milk vetch is another nitrogen-fixing legume commonly used in rice
paddy systems. It grows during the winter dormant period and is
incorporated into the soil before rice transplanting. Milk vetch
fixes significant amounts of nitrogen, often providing all the nitrogen
needed for the subsequent rice crop. It also improves soil structure
and water-holding capacity in paddy soils.

## Rape Seed (Brassica napus)

Rape seed is sown in early October at approximately 0.5 kg per 0.1
hectare. Optimal germination temperature is 20 to 25 degrees C, with
tolerance down to 0 to 2 degrees C. The crop is harvested when seeds
turn black. Rape seed contains 38 to 45 percent oil, making it a dual-
purpose crop that can provide both soil improvement and oil production.

Rape seed residues make excellent [[ultra-low-jadam-liquid-fertilizer-making]].
The high oil content provides energy for soil microorganisms, and the
residues contain a balanced nutrient profile. Rape seed is a
non-leguminous cover crop, so it does not fix nitrogen, but it excels
at scavenging residual soil nutrients and preventing leaching.

## Sudan Grass (Sorghum bicolor)

Sudan grass is valued as a fast-growing cover crop, particularly useful
in greenhouses where rapid biomass production is needed. It grows
quickly in warm conditions, producing large amounts of organic matter in
a short time. Sudan grass is also known for its allelopathic effects on
certain soil pests and nematodes, providing natural pest suppression.

One practical application described in the JADAM system is using rice
straw and sudan grass as base fertilizer for organic strawberry
production. Combined with strawberry residue JLF as additional
fertilizer, this system produces high-yielding strawberries with
excellent taste and aroma.

## Combining Cover Crops

For maximum soil improvement, JADAM recommends combining multiple cover
crop species. A common combination is cereal rye and hairy vetch,
planted together in fall. The rye provides carbon-rich organic matter
and weed suppression, while the vetch fixes nitrogen. Together they
produce a balanced fertilizer that supports the subsequent cash crop.

## Integration with JMS

Cover crop effectiveness is enhanced when combined with [[jadam-jms-pesticide]].
Applying JMS to cover crop fields accelerates decomposition when cover
crops are terminated, speeds nutrient release, and boosts the
microbial population that processes the cover crop biomass. The
combination of cover crops plus JMS is described as the best solution
to [[ultra-low-soil-compaction-and-plow-pan-solutions]].

## Cost Advantages

Cover crop seeds cost very little compared to purchased organic
fertilizers. A single planting can produce tons of biomass per hectare,
providing the equivalent of hundreds of dollars worth of commercial
fertilizer. When combined with JMS and JLF made from cover crop
residues, the total fertility program costs approach zero. This is
the essence of ultra-low-cost agriculture.
