## Growing Tomatoes, Peppers, and Eggplant

Tomatoes, peppers, and eggplant are close relatives in the nightshade family ([[solanaceae]]). Many varieties can be perennial where there is no frost. All are aggressive growers in suitable weather, responding to fertilization by expanding to the limit of their moisture supply and rooting room. Tomatoes are the most demanding to grow well; once mastered, peppers and eggplants seem easier by comparison.

## Tomatoes

### Climate Requirements

- Not frost-hardy
- Most varieties need 100-120 growing days from emerging to first ripe fruit
- Where there are fewer than 150 frost-free days, use transplants for a 50-day head start
- In warm climates with 150+ frost-free days: grow or buy only 2-3 early-maturing bush determinate transplants for early table supply, then directly seed indeterminate varieties

### Planting

- Sow seeds on at least 4-foot (120 cm) centers
- Make a small depression in loose soil about half an inch (1.25 cm) deep
- Set 3-4 seeds per station, press down to restore capillarity
- If hot and sunny, cover with a light mulch to keep soil cool and moist for about a week
- Progressively thin to a single plant

### Determinate vs. Indeterminate

| Type | Vine Length | Fruit Pattern | Best Use |
|------|------------|---------------|----------|
| Determinate | Short | Heavy yield for shorter time | Containers, small gardens |
| Indeterminate | Long, lanky | Produces until frost | Main crop, trellising |

**The root zone principle**: The more root room a tomato has, the more [[cotter-growing-space-design-fruiting-rooms]] you should give it. Tomatoes don"t only match their compact above-ground growth — they need much more underground space than their visible size suggests.

### Pruning Indeterminates

Side shoots emerge from each leaf notch in groups of three. The pattern:
1. Two weak side branches appear
2. Third one is a stronger side branch
3. Pattern repeats

**Method**: During the first few months, remove all weak side branches by pinching off with your thumbnail. Allow the strong ones to grow. Continue until the plant has formed enough branches to fill the support structure you've built. After that, remove all new branches as they appear.

### Support Methods

- Staking (for indeterminates)
- Trellising (guide vines up hanging strings and wires)
- Tall wire cages
- Old-fashioned method for determinates: spread dry brush under vines and let them grow over it

### Pests and Diseases

- If roots have room to expand, weather is favorable, and soil is reasonably fertile, vines usually won't become sick
- **Fruit worms**: Same as corn earworm — control with Bt
- **Hornworms**: Handpick — they're large and obvious
- **Blossom-end rot**: [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]]; if your subsoil offers sufficient calcium, a few years of light lime applications will cure it
- **Early blight**: The inevitable late-summer disease; have faith and accept that harvest will fade

### Varieties

- **Hybrids**: Resistant to more diseases at once, important for commercial growers
- **Heirlooms**: Superior flavor but may not be well-adapted to cool or dry climates, rarely carry disease resistance
- **Suggestion**: Experiment with one new heirloom yearly, but mainly grow proven local varieties
- **Beefsteak**: Best flavor but need warm, humid nights — usually fail in maritime climates
- **Cherry (indeterminate)**: Most aggressively growing, best suited to [[dry-gardening-unirrigated-vegetable-production-solomon]]
- **Paste/sauce**: Less moisture, cook down in half the time
- **Longkeeper**: Extremely slow-ripening, develops tough skin that holds in moisture

### Harvest

- Pick scarce first fruits when light orange (to prevent slug/woodlouse damage)
- Let trellised indeterminates ripen fully on the vine
- At season's end, bring full-sized green tomatoes indoors to ripen in cool, airy baskets

### Yield Enhancement

- **8 weeks before first frost**: Pinch off about half the flower clusters as they appear
- **4 weeks before first frost**: Pinch off over three-quarters of all flower clusters
- This lightens fruit load and enhances size and flavor of remaining fruit

### Saving Seed

Tomatoes are self-pollinating — you can save seed from individual fruits. The fermentation method:
1. Cut tomato in half crosswise
2. Scoop out seedy pulp into a jar with double the water volume
3. Let ferment 3-5 days in a warm room (white mold is fine)
4. Swill daily; good seeds settle, solids float
5. Rinse and repeat until water is clear
6. Dry on newspaper, store in paper envelopes

## Peppers (Capsicum/Chilli)

### Climate Needs

- Need warm conditions — maritime climates are challenging
- In chilly areas without a greenhouse, hybrid peppers may be essential
- Black [[jadam-plastic-mulch-problems]] warms soil a few degrees and raises nighttime air temperature

### Black Plastic Mulch Technique

1. Make the bed perfectly flat
2. Lay and anchor the mulch
3. Set transplants through small "X" slits
4. Poke small holes in any puddles that form to let water flow through
5. Not necessary to use [[drip-irrigation]] beneath the mulch

### Harvest

- Pick first fruits when light orange to prevent damage
- Let later fruits fully vine-ripen
- Peppers may produce for 100+ days where climate permits

### Saving Seed

- Isolate hot peppers from sweet ones — let the fruit get dead-ripe first
- Saving procedure is identical to tomatoes

## Eggplant (Aubergine)

### Climate Needs

- Need at least 180 frost-free days for [[clay-seed-pellets-direct-seeding]]
- In shorter seasons, use transplants
- Hybrid eggplant may be advantageous in chilly climates

### Growing

- Sometimes direct-seeded where [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]] is long enough
- Requires warm soil and consistent moisture
- Responds well to extra fertility

## The Black Plastic Mulch Advantage

For all three nightshades in cooler climates, a wide sheet of black plastic mulch:
- Warms the soil several degrees
- Increases nighttime air temperature near the plants
- Suppresses weeds
- Can make the difference between a crop and no crop

## Spacing Recommendations

| Crop | Spacing |
|------|---------|
| Tomato (indeterminate) | 4 ft (120 cm) centers minimum |
| Tomato (determinate) | 3 ft (90 cm) centers |
| Pepper | 18-24 inches (45-60 cm) in rows 3 ft (90 cm) apart |
| Eggplant | 24-30 inches (60-75 cm) in rows 3 ft (90 cm) apart |
