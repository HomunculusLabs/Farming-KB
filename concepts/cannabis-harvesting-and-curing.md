# Cannabis Harvesting and Curing

Proper harvest timing, drying, and curing are critical to maximizing potency, flavor, and smoothness of the final product. Harvesting too early sacrifices yield and full cannabinoid development; harvesting too late degrades THC into CBN and produces a flatter, more sedative effect.

## Determining Harvest Readiness

### Trichome Method (Most Reliable)
Use a jeweler's loupe or digital microscope (60-100x) to examine trichomes on bud surfaces:

| Trichome Appearance | Effect Profile | Harvest Action |
|---|---|---|
| Mostly clear, few cloudy | Low potency, less developed — premature | Wait |
| 50% clear, 50% milky/amber | Balanced cerebral + body | Good harvest window opens |
| Mostly milky/amber | Peak THC, cerebral high with body | Optimal harvest for most strains |
| Mostly amber, some degraded | High CBN, sedative, couch-lock | Harvest for maximum body effect |
| Opaque/white, deteriorating | Over-mature, declining potency | Past ideal — harvest immediately |

### Pistil Method (Supplementary)
- 50-70% of pistils (white hairs) have darkened and curled inward = entering harvest window
- 70-90% darkened = optimal harvest for most strains
- This method is less precise than trichomes but provides a quick visual check

### Calyx Swelling
- Calyxes should be swollen and plump — a sign the plant is pouring energy into resin production
- Heavy resin coating visible on calyxes and nearby leaves indicates peak resin production

## Harvest Timing and Effect Profile

The timing of harvest directly influences the psychoactive effect:

- **Early harvest** (mostly clear trichomes): Lighter, more cerebral high. Lower total cannabinoids. Less complex flavor.
- **Peak harvest** (milky trichomes): Maximum THC, clear-headed high with full flavor and terpene development.
- **Late harvest** (amber trichomes): More body effect, couch-lock. Higher CBN from THC degradation. Richer, deeper flavor but some degradation of terpenes.

## Cannabinoid Biosynthesis at Harvest (Clarke)

During the flowering cycle, CBD acid is continuously being converted to THC acid by the plant's enzymes, while THC acid is simultaneously degrading to CBN acid through heat and light. The goal is to harvest when THC acid production exceeds its degradation:

- **Peak floral stage**: THC acid at maximum, CBN still low, CBD being rapidly converted to THC. Clear, cerebral effect.
- **Late floral stage**: THC production slowing, more CBN accumulating, CBD may build up as conversion energy declines. More somatic, sedative effect.
- **Senescence**: Resin production stops, cannabinoids degrade. Inferior marijuana with lowered THC.

## Pre-Harvest Preparation

### 1-2 Weeks Before Harvest
- Stop all nitrogen-rich inputs
- Reduce watering gradually (do NOT let soil dry completely — the plant still needs water for biosynthesis)
- Some growers flush with plain water — in living soil this is less critical as the biology continues breaking down organic matter
- Optional: 48-72 hours of darkness before harvest — some growers report increased trichome production

### Harvest Day
- Harvest in the morning, before lights come on — terpene and resin levels peak in pre-dawn hours
- Prepare clean, sanitized scissors and a clean workspace
- Have drying area prepped (see below)

## Harvesting Technique

### Wet Trim (Preferred for Indoor)
1. Cut branches at main stems
2. Immediately remove large fan leaves
3. Trim sugar leaves close to buds while still fresh and pliable
4. Hang trimmed branches or place on drying racks

### Dry Trim (Better for Slow Drying)
1. Cut branches at main stems
2. Remove only large fan leaves
3. Hang whole branches
4. Trim sugar leaves after drying when they're crisp

## Drying

### Environment
- Temperature: 60-70F (15-21C) — cooler = slower, better cure
- Humidity: 50-60%
- Dark or very low light — light degrades THC
- Good air circulation (gentle fan, not blowing directly on buds)
- No direct wind or heat

### Method
- Hang branches upside down from string, hangers, or lines
- Space branches so they don't touch
- Dry for 7-14 days depending on bud size and conditions
- Buds are dry enough when the small stems snap (not bend) when bent
- Large stems should still have some flex — not bone dry

### Signs of Proper Drying
- Stems snap when bent
- Buds feel dry on the outside but slightly springy inside
- Outer surface is not crispy or brittle
- Aroma shifts from "green" to the strain's characteristic scent

## Curing

Curing is the slow breakdown of remaining chlorophyll and sugars, converting acidic cannabinoids (THCA, CBDA) to their active forms (THC, CBD) while developing complex flavors.

### Method
1. Place dried buds in airtight glass jars (quart mason jars work well)
2. Fill jars 2/3 to 3/4 full — don't pack tightly
3. Store jars in a cool, dark place (60-70F)
4. Open jars once daily for 15-30 minutes (burping) for the first 2 weeks
5. After 2 weeks, burp every 2-3 days
6. After 4 weeks, burp once weekly
7. Cure for minimum 4 weeks; 6-8 weeks for best results

### Monitoring During Cure
- If buds feel wet/spongy: leave jar open longer (1-2 hours) to release moisture
- If buds feel brittle/dry: add a small humidity pack (62% Boveda) to prevent over-drying
- Check for mold daily during first 2 weeks — any fuzzy white/gray patches mean buds must be discarded
- Proper humidity inside jars: 58-62% RH

### Why Curing Matters
- Converts remaining THCA to THC (decarboxylation)
- Breaks down chlorophyll — removes "hay" or "grass" taste
- Allows terpenes to develop their full complexity
- Smooths the smoke — properly cured bud is less harsh
- Clarke notes: "many strains, if harvested in the peak floral stage, lack the completely developed aroma, taste and psychoactive level that appear after curing"

## Long-Term Storage

- Store cured buds in airtight glass jars in a cool, dark place
- Ideal storage temperature: 55-65F
- Ideal humidity: 58-62% (use Boveda or similar humidity packs)
- Avoid plastic bags — they can impart flavors and don't provide an airtight seal
- Properly stored cannabis maintains potency for 6-12+ months
- For long-term storage (1+ years), vacuum seal and freeze
