## Phosphorylation Methods Evaluated

### Phosphoryl Iodide Method

The phosphoryl iodide method, described by Stowell and Widlanski (1995),
uses tribenzyl phosphite, iodine, and 4-dimethylaminopyridine (DMAP) as
a catalyst. While this method was reported to be effective for
phosphorylation of phenols, it was one of the methods that did not
effectively consume psilocin in the Shirota study. This was attributed
to the low nucleophilicity of the 4-hydroxy group in the psilocin
framework.

### Pyrophosphate Method (Selected)

The pyrophosphate method using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-butyllithium
was found to be the most promising approach. This method was selected for
large-scale [[gottlieb-psilocybin-production-extraction-methods]] for two practical reasons: easy
handling of reagents and reagent stability. Tetrabenzylpyrophosphate can
be prepared in nearly quantitative yield from dibenzyl phosphate using a
literature procedure (Almeida et al., 1999) with minor modifications.

## Experimental Procedure

### Preparation of Tetrabenzylpyrophosphate

Tetrabenzylpyrophosphate ([(BnO)2PO]2O) is prepared from dibenzyl
phosphate using the procedure of Almeida et al. (1999) with some
modification. The compound is obtained in almost 100% yield, making it
an economical reagent for large-scale work. The reagent is stable and
can be stored, which is important for practical laboratory operations.

### Phosphorylation Reaction

The phosphorylation is carried out as follows:

1. Psilocin (5.4 g, 26.4 mmol) is dissolved in anhydrous THF (265 mL)
   and cooled to -78 degrees C under inert atmosphere.
2. n-Butyllithium (2.6 M in n-hexane, 11.5 mL, 29.9 mmol) is added
   to deprotonate the phenolic hydroxyl, generating the more
   nucleophilic phenoxide anion.
3. Tetrabenzylpyrophosphate (18.0 g, 33.4 mmol) is added all at once.
4. The reaction is stirred for 1 hour while warming from -78 degrees C
   to 0 degrees C.
