# Lichenized Fungi Diversity
## Overview
Lichenized fungi (lichens) are symbiotic organisms consisting of a
fungal partner (mycobiont) and a photosynthetic partner (photobiont:
green algae or cyanobacteria). With over 17,000 described species,
lichens represent approximately one-fifth of all described Ascomycota
and constitute one of the most successful fungal lifestyles on Earth.
They colonize substrates ranging from arctic tundra to tropical canopy,
from bare rock to living leaves.
## Taxonomic Diversity
### Dominant Groups
The overwhelming majority of lichen-forming fungi belong to the
Ascomycota, with relatively few Basidiomycota forming lichen-like
associations. Major orders include:
- **Lecanorales:** 7,150 species; the majority of all lichen-forming
  fungi; most diverse and widespread lichen groups.
- **Arthoniales:** ~1,000 species; bark-dwelling crustose lichens.
- **Dothideales:** ~120 species of lichenized fungi.
- **Gyalectales:** ~100 species; typically small, inconspicuous.
### Uncharted Diversity
Lichen taxonomy remains dynamic, with many species complexes awaiting
revision. Molecular phylogenetic studies increasingly reveal cryptic
species within morphologically uniform taxa. True diversity likely
substantially exceeds currently described numbers.
## Ecology and Distribution
### Habitat Range
Lichens colonize an extraordinary range of habitats:
- **Rock surfaces** (saxicolous): Coastal splash zones to alpine
  summits.
- **Tree bark** (corticolous): Trunks and branches in forests
  worldwide.
- **Soil** (terricolous): Arctic tundra to tropical savannas.
- **Leaves** (foliicolous): Especially diverse in humid tropics.
- **Wood** (lignicolous): Decaying [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] is
widely used as a bioindicator. Pollution-sensitive species decline in
contaminated areas, while tolerant species may persist.
## Growth Forms
Lichen growth forms reflect both taxonomy and environment:
- **Crustose:** Tightly attached; most diverse but least conspicuous.
- **Foliose:** Leaf-like, loosely attached; many well-known species.
- **Fruticose:** Shrubby or hair-like; includes Cladonia and Usnea.
- **Squamulose:** Small, scale-like; intermediate form.
## Photobiont Diversity
Lichen photobionts include green algae (primarily Trebouxia,
Trentepohlia, and Coccomyxa) and cyanobacteria (primarily Nostoc).
Some lichens contain both. Photobiont identity influences ecology, as
cyanobacterial lichens fix atmospheric nitrogen while green algal
lichens cannot.
## Lichens and Nitrogen Fixation
Cyanolichens (8-10% of species) are ecologically significant nitrogen
sources. Cyanobacterial partners fix atmospheric nitrogen through the
nitrogenase enzyme, converting N2 to ammonium available to the
surrounding ecosystem. In [[lobaria-oregana]] contribute an estimated 2-5 kg
N/ha/year, a significant input in nitrogen-limited ecosystems.
## Reproductive Biology
Lichen reproduction involves complex coordination between partners:
- **[[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] ("lichen acids"),
almost exclusively from the fungal partner:
- **Anti-herbivory:** Bitter or toxic compounds deter grazers.
  Reindeer avoid lichens with high usnic acid in summer.
- **Antimicrobial:** Inhibit bacteria and competing fungi, protecting
  the slow-growing thallus.
- **UV protection:** Melanin and pigments screen ultraviolet radiation
  at high altitude or latitude.
- **Metal chelation:** Some compounds bind heavy metals, enabling
  Cladonia and Usnea species to serve as biomonitors for pollution.
## Biomonitoring
Lichens are among the most widely used [[biological-indicators-of-soil]]:
- **Air quality indices:** The IAP (Index of Atmospheric Purity)
