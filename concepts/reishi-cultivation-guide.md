# Reishi Cultivation Guide

Reishi ([[stamets-reishi-ganoderma-lucidum-cultivation]]) is one of the most revered mushrooms in traditional Chinese medicine, where it has been used for over 2,000 years as a tonic for longevity, vitality, and immune support. Known as "Lingzhi" in China and "Mannentake" in Japan, Reishi is now widely cultivated for both medicinal supplements and ornamental purposes.

## Overview

Reishi is a wood-decomposing polypore that produces large, kidney- or fan-shaped brackets with a distinctive lacquered, varnished appearance. Unlike most [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]], Reishi is primarily grown for its medicinal compounds rather than as food — it is extremely woody, bitter, and generally not considered palatable. It is, however, one of the most researched [[comparison-chaga-vs-turkey-tail-medicinal-mushrooms-comprehensive]] in the world.

## Species and Varieties

Several Ganoderma species are cultivated:

- G. lucidum: the classic "red Reishi" of Asian medicine, most studied
- G. tsugae: hemlock Reishi, native to [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America
- G. lingzhi: taxonomically separated from G. lucidum; the primary Lingzhi of Chinese commerce
- G. oregonense: a large Pacific Northwest species used by some growers
- G. sessile: a North American species increasingly cultivated for supplements

The red Reishi (G. lucidum and G. lingzhi) contains the highest concentrations of medicinal compounds and is the standard for commercial cultivation.

## Medicinal Compounds

### Triterpenes

Triterpenes (ganoderic acids) are the primary [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] responsible for Reishi's bitter taste and many of its therapeutic effects:

- Over 150 triterpenes identified
- Anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, and anti-tumor properties
- Ganoderic acid A: reduces cholesterol and blood pressure
- Ganoderic acid B: anti-inflammatory
- Ganoderic acid T: cytotoxic to cancer cells in vitro

Triterpene concentration is highest in the spores and the fruiting body, particularly the red outer skin (cutis).

### Beta-Glucans (Polysaccharides)

Beta-glucans are the primary immune-modulating compounds:

- Enhance innate immune response via macrophage and NK cell activation
- Anti-tumor activity through immune stimulation (not direct cytotoxicity)
- Reduce fatigue and improve quality of life in cancer patients
- Anti-oxidant and anti-radiation properties

### Other Compounds

- Ganoderans: blood sugar regulating polysaccharides
- Adenosine: anti-platelet, anti-inflammatory
- Lingzhi-8 protein: immunomodulatory
- Ergosterol: vitamin D2 precursor

## Cultivation Methods

### Log Cultivation (Traditional)

Traditional outdoor log cultivation produces high-quality Reishi with dense woody tissue and high triterpene content.

- Wood: oak, maple, or plum; 6-12 inches diameter
- Inoculation: same methods as [[shiitake-cultivation-guide]] (drill and plug spawn)
- Spawn run: 12-18 months
- Burial: partially bury logs in shady, moist soil after spawn run
- Fruiting: emerges from soil as antler-like formations, then develops into conks
- Growing season: late spring through early fall

Log-grown Reishi is considered the highest quality due to slow growth and natural environmental stressors that stimulate triterpene production.

### Bag Cultivation (Sawdust Blocks)

Indoor sawdust block cultivation is faster and more productive:

#### Substrate Formula

- Hardwood sawdust: 75-80%
- Wheat bran or rice bran: 15-20%
- Wheat straw (chopped): 5-10%
- Gypsum: 1-2%
- Moisture: 60-65%

#### Process

1. Mix and pack substrate into polypropylene bags with filter patches
2. Sterilize at 15 PSI for 2-3 hours
3. Inoculate with grain spawn (8-10% rate)
4. Incubate at 75-80 F in darkness for 25-40 days
5. Initiate fruiting when fully colonized

### Fruiting Conditions

Reishi has unique fruiting requirements:

- Temperature: 72-82 F (22-28 C) — warmer than most cultivated mushrooms
- Humidity: 85-95% for antler formation; 70-80% for conk development
- Light: 500-2000 lux — essential for cap/conk formation
- CO2: high CO2 (2000+ ppm) produces antler growth; lower CO2 (under 1000 ppm) triggers conk formation
- Fresh air exchange: moderate; Reishi tolerates higher CO2 than most species

### Two-Stage Growth Pattern

Commercial growers often exploit Reishi's morphology response:

1. Antler stage: grow in high CO2 for 2-4 weeks to produce long, antler-like growth
2. Conk stage: lower CO2 by opening bags or increasing ventilation to develop the classic bracket shape

## Harvest Timing

Harvest timing significantly affects medicinal compound concentrations:

- Early harvest (when still soft and white at edges): higher beta-glucan content, lower triterpenes
- Mature harvest (fully developed, spore-producing): highest triterpene concentration, maximum bitterness
- Pre-sporulation harvest: optimal balance of compounds

For maximum triterpene content, wait until the mushroom stops growing, develops a hard, lacquered surface, and begins dropping brown spores (visible as a dust on the growing surface and nearby surfaces).

## Processing and Storage

- Fresh Reishi: slice thin and dehydrate at 115-135 F (higher temp than most mushrooms to ensure thorough drying of the woody tissue)
- Drying time: 3-7 days depending on thickness
- Fully dried Reishi is extremely hard and brittle
- Store dried in airtight containers, protected from light
- Powder for teas, decoctions, tinctures, or capsules

Spore collection: Reishi spores have a separate market. Some growers place paper or trays beneath fruiting bodies to collect the spore dust. Spore oil (cracked spores in oil) is a high-value product.

## Yield Expectations

- Log cultivation: 50-100g dry weight per log per year; slower but higher quality
- Bag cultivation: 100-200g fresh per bag; [[stamets-biological-efficiency-yield]] 40-70%
- Bag cultivation is 2-3x faster but produces lower triterpene concentrations

## Common Problems

- Green mold contamination: ensure thorough sterilization; Reishi is slow to colonize and can be outcompeted
- Antlers only (no conks): CO2 too high, increase ventilation
- Soft, watery growth: too humid; reduce humidity during conk stage
