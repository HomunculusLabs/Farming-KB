# Winter Harvest Tools and Equipment

The [[winter-harvest-system]] requires a specific toolkit optimized for intensive planting, efficient weed control, precise seeding, and careful harvesting in cold conditions. [[eliot-coleman]] has spent decades testing, designing, and refining tools for the year-round market garden, and his recommendations reflect both historical precedent and practical innovation.

## Seeding Tools

### Pinpoint Seeders

The [[pinpoint-seeder]] (also called a four-row seeder in some configurations) is the primary tool for direct sowing in the winter harvest system. It offers several advantages over broadcast sowing or hand planting:

- **Precision spacing** — Seeds are placed at consistent intervals within rows, reducing the need for thinning
- **Multiple rows per pass** — Can sow 4–6 rows simultaneously, dramatically increasing planting speed
- **Depth control** — Adjustable planting depth ensures optimal germination conditions for different seed sizes
- **Time efficiency** — A task that takes hours by hand can be completed in minutes

The seeder is particularly valuable for the dense succession planting required by the winter harvest schedule, where crops like arugula, radishes, and turnips are sown every few days throughout the season.

### Six-Row Seeders

For larger-scale production, six-row seeders expand on the pinpoint seeder concept:

- Sow six rows per pass
- Compatible with various seed plate configurations
- Best suited for round or oblong seeds (radishes, turnips, beets)

### Seed Germination Considerations

Seed quality directly affects tool effectiveness. Coleman emphasizes:

- Testing germination rates before planting, especially for older seed lots
- Adjusting seeding rates based on germination test results
- Ensuring good seed-to-soil contact, which can be improved by using rollers after seeding
- Accounting for reduced germination in cold soils by slightly increasing seeding rates

## Bed Preparation Tools

### Broadforks

The broadfork is the primary tool for deep soil aeration:

- **Function** — Lifts and loosens compacted soil without inverting the profile
- **Benefits** — Preserves soil structure, maintains microbial communities, improves drainage
- **Technique** — Push the broadfork into the soil with foot pressure, then pull back to fracture compacted layers
- **Use case** — Used when soil shows signs of compaction, typically once per year per bed

### The Tilther

The Tilther is a specialized surface-cultivation tool that Coleman helped design:

- Prepares a fine seedbed in the top 2 inches of soil
- Does not disturb deeper soil layers or microbial communities
- Much lighter and less destructive than a rototiller
- Ideal for preparing beds for succession plantings between crops

### Rakes

Standard garden rakes serve multiple purposes:

- Final bed leveling and smoothing after tilling or forking
- Incorporating surface-applied compost and amendments
- Creating furrows for hand-seeded crops

## Weed Control Tools

### Flame Weeders

Flame weeding is a cornerstone of organic weed management in the winter harvest system:

- **Principle** — A propane flame passes over weed seedlings just after germination but before crop emergence, killing the weeds without disturbing the soil
- **Timing** — Critical — must be done after weeds have germinated but before the crop has emerged. This requires careful attention to germination rates.
- **Crops suited** — Carrots, onions, beets, and other slow-germinating direct-sown crops benefit most
- **Advantages** — No soil disturbance, no resistance development, works even in wet conditions
- **Safety** — Requires attention to fire safety, especially near mulches, row covers, and dry plant material

### Collinear Hoes

Collinear hoes (also called stirrup hoes or oscillating hoes) are the primary weeding tool for established crops:

- The blade oscillates as it moves through the soil, cutting weeds on both forward and backward strokes
- Works close to crop plants without damaging roots
- Effective for small weeds in the "white thread" stage
- Different blade widths suit different row spacings

### Wire Weeders

Wire weeders are used for very early weed control:

- Thin wire tines disturb the soil surface just enough to uproot tiny weed seedlings
- Can be used very close to crop plants
- Effective for weed prevention in intensively planted beds

### Scythes

Scythes serve both weed control and crop management functions:

- Cutting down mature crop residues between plantings
- Managing weed growth in pathways and margins
- Harvesting grain cover crops

## Harvesting Tools

### Knives

Harvesting knives are essential for the [[cut-and-come-again]] system:

- **Victorinox knives** — Coleman specifically recommends these for their durability and sharpness
- Used for harvesting salad mix, braising greens, and individual heads
- Must be kept razor-sharp to make clean cuts that minimize plant stress and reduce disease entry points

### The Greens Harvester

For larger-scale salad mix production, mechanical harvesting becomes important:

- The Greens Harvester is a motorized cutting tool that speeds up salad mix harvest
- Allows one person to harvest quantities that would otherwise require multiple workers
- Essential for the commercial-scale salad mix operation described in the book

## Transplanting Tools

### Dibbles

Dibbles are used for making planting holes for transplants:

- Create consistent, properly sized holes
- Reduce transplant shock by minimizing root disturbance
- Particularly useful for leeks, onions, and other alliums transplanted into the winter garden
- Simple wooden or metal tools that are quick to use

## Protected Structure Components

### Wiggle Wire Channels

Wiggle wire channels (also called poly-lock or spring wire channels) secure plastic coverings to greenhouse structures:

- Aluminum channels mounted on the greenhouse frame
- Flexible wire (usually galvanized spring steel) is "wiggled" into the channel, gripping the plastic film tightly
- Allow easy installation and removal of covers for ventilation
- More secure and durable than wood lath or staples
- Essential for both mobile greenhouses and quick hoops
