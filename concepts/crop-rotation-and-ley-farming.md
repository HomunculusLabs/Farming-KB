# Crop Rotation and Ley Farming

## Overview

Crop rotation and ley farming are foundational practices for maintaining
soil health and preventing the buildup of diseases and pests in

The root cause is the buildup of soil-borne diseases and pests that

clover pasture. In his Tasmanian garden, roughly half the enclosed area
is always in grass ley while the other half grows vegetables.

Every four to five years, the vegetable beds are converted to grass and
clover, while the oldest grass ley is broken and returned to vegetable
production. The first two years after breaking sod typically produce the
best vegetable growth, with declining performance by the third or fourth
year signaling that it is time to rotate again.

## Benefits of Grass Leys

The grass and clover ley period provides several important benefits.
The deep root systems of grasses and clovers rebuild soil structure by
creating channels for water infiltration and root penetration. Clover
fixes atmospheric nitrogen, adding significant fertility to the soil
without imported inputs. The grass biomass, mowed and left in place,

## Basic Crop Rotation Principles

For gardeners who cannot implement a full ley system, basic crop rotation
between vegetable families still provides significant benefits. The
principle is to avoid planting members of the same family in the same
location in successive years.
