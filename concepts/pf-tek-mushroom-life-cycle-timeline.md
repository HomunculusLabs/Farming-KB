# PF TEK Mushroom Life Cycle Timeline: Spore to Harvest

## Overview

The [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] life cycle that progresses through discrete stages, each with specific timelines, environmental requirements, and biological processes. Understanding the full temporal arc from spore inoculation to harvest is essential for successful cultivation. The timeline described here reflects the PF TEK methodology specifically, which uses half-pint canning jars with a brown rice flour and vermiculite substrate.

## Stage One: Spore Germination and Colonization

### Substrate Preparation (Day 0)

The cultivation cycle begins with substrate preparation. The PF TEK substrate consists of:

- Organic brown rice flour
- Horticultural vermiculite (medium or fine grade)
- Distilled or filtered drinking water

The substrate is loaded into shoulderless half-pint Kerr or Ball canning jars, filling to within approximately half an inch of the rim. A dry vermiculite layer is placed on top to serve as a contaminant barrier. The jars are sealed with lids fitted with inoculation holes covered by tape.

### Sterilization (Day 0)

Jars are sterilized using a [[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]] is more reliable. After sterilization, jars must cool completely to room temperature before inoculation to prevent killing the spores with residual heat.

### Inoculation (Day 1)

Spore solution from a prepared syringe is injected through the inoculation holes in the jar lid. Each jar receives approximately 1 cc of spore solution, distributed across multiple inoculation points. The needle is flame-sterilized between jars to prevent cross-contamination. The inoculation holes are re-covered with tape after injection.

### Germination and Mycelial Growth (Days 2–14)

After inoculation, spores germinate and begin forming [[gottlieb-psilocybin-cultivation-fruiting-chamber-design]] — the PF TEK uses a 10-gallon dual-chambered terrarium with a transparent partition dividing the growing space from the humidification area.

### Initiation of Fruiting

Fruiting is triggered by the environmental shift from jar conditions to the open terrarium:

- **Fresh air exchange**: Moving from the near-sealed jar to the open terrarium introduces increased oxygen and reduced carbon dioxide
- **Light exposure**: Ambient or indirect light initiates the fruiting response
- **Humidity**: The terrarium maintains high humidity through evaporative humidification
- **Temperature drop**: A slight temperature reduction can help initiate pinning

## Stage Three: Pinning and Mushroom Growth

### Pin Formation (Days 28–35)

"Pinning" refers to the initial formation of primordia — tiny mushroom knots that appear on the surface of the cake. Pins typically first appear as small white dots that gradually differentiate into recognizable miniature mushroom caps and stems. The pinning phase usually lasts 3–7 days.

### Fruit Body Development (Days 35–42)

Once pins have formed, they develop rapidly into mature mushroom fruit bodies:

- **Growth rate**: Mushrooms can double in size daily under optimal conditions
- **Cap expansion**: The cap opens from its initial rounded "button" form
- **Veil integrity**: The partial veil connecting the cap to the stem stretches and eventually tears
- **Timeline from pin to mature mushroom**: Typically 5–10 days
