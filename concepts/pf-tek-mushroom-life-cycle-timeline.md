# PF TEK Mushroom Life Cycle Timeline: Spore to Harvest

## Overview

The [[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] TEK (PF TEK) defines a complete [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] life cycle that progresses through discrete stages, each with specific timelines, environmental requirements, and biological processes. Understanding the full temporal arc from spore inoculation to harvest is essential for successful cultivation. The timeline described here reflects the PF TEK methodology specifically, which uses half-pint canning jars with a brown rice flour and vermiculite substrate.

## Stage One: Spore Germination and Colonization

### Substrate Preparation (Day 0)

The cultivation cycle begins with substrate preparation. The PF TEK substrate consists of:

- Organic brown rice flour
- Horticultural vermiculite (medium or fine grade)
- Distilled or filtered drinking water

The substrate is loaded into shoulderless half-pint Kerr or Ball canning jars, filling to within approximately half an inch of the rim. A dry vermiculite layer is placed on top to serve as a contaminant barrier. The jars are sealed with lids fitted with inoculation holes covered by tape.

### Sterilization (Day 0)

Jars are sterilized using a [[pf-tek-pressure-canner-sterilization]] at **12 PSI for 20 minutes**. Alternatively, boiling water steaming can be used as a lower-tech sterilization method, though [[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]] is more reliable. After sterilization, jars must cool completely to room temperature before inoculation to prevent killing the spores with residual heat.

### Inoculation (Day 1)

Spore solution from a prepared syringe is injected through the inoculation holes in the jar lid. Each jar receives approximately 1 cc of spore solution, distributed across multiple inoculation points. The needle is flame-sterilized between jars to prevent cross-contamination. The inoculation holes are re-covered with tape after injection.

### Germination and Mycelial Growth (Days 2–14)

After inoculation, spores germinate and begin forming [[fungal-mycelial-networks-nutrient-translocation]] throughout the substrate. Key parameters during this phase:

- **Temperature**: Room temperature, ideally 70–75°F (21–24°C)
- **Light**: Incubation in darkness or indirect light; direct sunlight is avoided
- **Air exchange**: Minimal; the tape-covered holes allow limited gas exchange
- **Timeline**: Visible white mycelial colonization typically appears within 3–7 days
- **Full colonization**: Usually achieved within **10–21 days** depending on spore viability, temperature, and inoculation volume

Fresh spores germinate "massively and quickly," while older spores show progressively reduced germination rates. Spores begin degrading a few months after being collected, and germination is "greatly reduced or won't occur at all" after approximately 1.5 years.

## Stage Two: Consolidation and Fruiting Initiation

### Consolidation (Days 14–21)

Once the substrate is fully colonized — recognizable by a solid white mycelial mass visible through the glass — the cakes benefit from an additional consolidation period of approximately 3–7 days. During this phase, the mycelium continues to strengthen its hold on the substrate, consuming remaining nutrients and thickening its network. While not strictly required, consolidation improves subsequent fruiting performance.

### Birthing the Cake (Day 21–28)

The colonized "cake" is removed from the jar. This process, called "birthing" in PF TEK terminology, involves:

1. Removing the tape and lid from the jar
2. Gently tapping the jar to dislodge the substrate cake
3. Carefully extracting the intact cake without breaking it

The cake is then placed in the [[gottlieb-psilocybin-cultivation-fruiting-chamber-design]] — the PF TEK uses a 10-gallon dual-chambered terrarium with a transparent partition dividing the growing space from the humidification area.

### Initiation of Fruiting

Fruiting is triggered by the environmental shift from jar conditions to the open terrarium:

- **Fresh air exchange**: Moving from the near-sealed jar to the open terrarium introduces increased oxygen and reduced carbon dioxide
- **Light exposure**: Ambient or indirect light initiates the fruiting response
- **Humidity**: The terrarium maintains high humidity through evaporative humidification
- **Temperature drop**: A slight temperature reduction can help initiate pinning

## Stage Three: Pinning and Mushroom Growth

### Pin Formation (Days 28–35)

"Pinning" refers to the initial formation of primordia — tiny mushroom knots that appear on the surface of the cake. Pins typically first appear as small white dots that gradually differentiate into recognizable miniature mushroom caps and stems. The pinning phase usually lasts 3–7 days.

### Fruit Body Development (Days 35–42)

Once pins have formed, they develop rapidly into mature mushroom fruit bodies:

- **Growth rate**: Mushrooms can double in size daily under optimal conditions
- **Cap expansion**: The cap opens from its initial rounded "button" form
- **Veil integrity**: The partial veil connecting the cap to the stem stretches and eventually tears
- **Timeline from pin to mature mushroom**: Typically 5–10 days

## Stage Four: Harvesting

### When to Harvest

The optimal harvest time is determined by the state of the **partial veil** — the thin membrane connecting the cap margin to the stem:

- **Before veil tearing**: Potency is at maximum but yield is lower
- **As the veil tears**: The PF TEK recommends harvesting at or just after veil separation
- **After full cap expansion and spore drop**: Potency has declined; spores are being released

### Harvest Technique

Mushrooms are harvested by gently grasping the base of the stem and twisting while pulling. Cutting with scissors is possible but leaves a stump that can become a vector for contamination. After harvest, the cake is often misted and returned to the terrarium for subsequent flushes.

### Drying

Harvested mushrooms are dried for preservation. Options include air drying, desiccant drying, or food dehydrators. Properly dried mushrooms should be "rock hard" with no remaining moisture. Dried mushrooms retain psychoactive properties for extended periods when stored in a cool, dark environment.

## Stage Five: Subsequent Flushes

After the first harvest, the cake will often produce additional flushes of mushrooms:

- **Second flush**: Typically 7–14 days after the first harvest
- **Third flush**: Possible but yields are typically diminished
- **Fourth and subsequent flushes**: Increasingly marginal returns
- **Total productive lifespan**: A single cake may produce 2–4 flushes before exhaustion

The PF TEK perlite humidification technique is specifically designed to support multi-flush production. Perlite provides weeks of sustained humidification through gradual evaporation, eliminating the need for active humidification equipment between flushes.

## Spore Collection: Completing the Cycle

After harvesting mature mushrooms, spore prints can be taken to complete the reproductive cycle. A mushroom cap is placed gill-side down on a clean surface (the PF TEK uses a jar) and left for several hours to overnight. The cap is then removed, leaving a deposit of millions of spores. These spores can be used to create spore syringes for the next cultivation cycle.

The PF TEK warns that spore viability degrades over time: spores begin degrading "a few months after they are taken," with germination "greatly reduced or won't occur at all" after approximately 1.5 years. A properly prepared spore syringe will remain viable for "several months and even up to a year or more" when stored at cool temperatures in darkness.
