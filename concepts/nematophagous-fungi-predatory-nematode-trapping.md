# Nematophagous Fungi — Predatory Fungi That Trap and Kill Nematodes

## Overview

[[nematophagous-fungi]] are a diverse group of fungi that capture, kill, and digest nematodes. They have evolved remarkable morphological adaptations for trapping their prey, including adhesive structures, mechanical traps, and spore-based infection strategies. These fungi are ecologically important as natural regulators of nematode populations and have attracted interest as biocontrol agents against plant-parasitic nematodes. Phylogenetic analyses based on 18S rDNA sequences show that nematode-trapping fungi form a **monophyletic clade** among apothecial ascomycetes.

## Trap Morphology and Types

Nematophagous fungi employ several distinct trap mechanisms:

### 1. Adhesive Knobs

- Specialized swollen cells at the tips of short hyphal branches.
- A layer of **adhesive polymers** covers the knob surface.
- The adhesive layer is absent on the supporting stalk.
- Upon nematode contact, the adhesive bonds the nematode cuticle to the knob.

### 2. Adhesive Networks

- Three-dimensional nets of adhesive hyphae.
- Nematodes become entangled and stuck to the adhesive hyphal surfaces.
- One of the most common and visually distinctive trap types.

### 3. Constricting Rings

- Mechanical traps consisting of three specialized cells forming a ring.
- When a nematode enters the ring, the cells **rapidly inflate** (within ~0.1 seconds), constricting and trapping the nematode.
- One of the most dramatic examples of rapid fungal movement.

### 4. Non-Constricting Rings

- Similar morphology to constricting rings but lack the rapid inflation response.
- Nematodes are captured by adhesion rather than mechanical constriction.

### 5. Endoparasitic Spores

- Some nematophagous fungi produce spores that are ingested by nematodes.
- Once inside the nematode, the spores germinate and the fungus digests the host from within.

## The Infection Sequence

Regardless of trap type, infection follows a conserved sequence of events:

1. **Attachment**: Trap cells adhere to the nematode surface (cuticle).
2. **Penetration**: The fungus penetrates the nematode cuticle, typically via an infection peg.
3. **Digestion**: Extracellular enzymes break down nematode tissues.
4. **Assimilation**: The fungus absorbs nutrients from the killed nematode.

## Phylogenetic Relationships

### Monophyletic Origin

- Based on 18S rDNA sequences, nematode-trapping fungi form a **monophyletic clade** among an unresolved cluster of apothecial ascomycetes (Ahren et al., 1998).
- All branches have bootstrap support values above 50%.
- The phylogenetic pattern **within the clade is concordant with trap morphology** — suggesting that trap types have evolved in a phylogenetically ordered manner.

### Relationship to Other Ascomycetes

The nematode-trapping fungal clade is related to, but distinct from, other groups of apothecial ascomycetes including:
- *Sphaerophorus globosus*
- *Ascobolus lineolatus*
- *Leotia lubrica*
- *Choiromyces venosus*
- *Rhizina undulata*

Notably, common laboratory fungi like *Aspergillus niger* and *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* are outside this clade, as is *Neolecta vitellina*.

## Ecological Significance

### Natural Nematode Regulation

- Nematophagous fungi are important natural regulators of nematode populations in soil ecosystems.
- They contribute to the "suppressive soil" phenomenon, where certain soils naturally limit nematode damage to crops.
- Their activity is influenced by soil moisture, temperature, organic matter, and agricultural practices.

### Biocontrol Potential

- Interest in nematophagous fungi as **biocontrol agents** against plant-parasitic nematodes.
- Advantages: environmentally friendly, self-sustaining, compatible with [[integrated-pest-management]].
- Challenges: inconsistent field performance, sensitivity to soil conditions, competition with native microorganisms.

## Molecular Studies — DNA Microarrays

DNA microarray technology has been applied to study gene expression in nematophagous fungi, enabling:

- Identification of trap-specific genes.
- Analysis of gene expression during the infection process.
- Comparison of gene expression between different trap morphologies.
- Discovery of genes involved in adhesive polymer production, cuticle degradation, and nutrient assimilation.
