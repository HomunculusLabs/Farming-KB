## Within-Site Sampling Protocols

A within-site sampling protocol designates the size of a site,
whether and how to subsample, and what information to record.
Protocols can range from time-constrained qualitative surveys of
the whole site to intensive, quantitative surveys of subsamples.

Sites typically correspond to habitat or macrovegetation classes
in the 0.1-5 hectare range. One hectare probably represents a
practical upper limit for a circumscribed search plot. Compact
shapes (squares, circles) are faster to lay out and more likely
to be homogeneous within, whereas extended shapes (rectangles)
better capture habitat heterogeneity.

## Substratum Stratification

Lichen species often have strong substratum and habitat
preferences, but relatively few are completely substratum-specific.
Major differences in species composition relate to:
- Calcareous composition of rock or soil substrata
- Bark pH and surface texture on trees
- Light regime and moisture status
- Substratum age and chemistry

Recommended subplot numbers for temperate-zone lichens:
- Tree trunks: 10-25 trees per tree species
- Tree branches: 25-60 branches for abundance, 100+ for species
  capture
- Ground and rock surfaces: 40-60 subplots

## Biodiversity Indicators

The use of individual species as indicators for the condition of
a whole guild or community presupposes detailed knowledge of what
the species indicate and how reliably. Epiphytic macrolichens
(squamulose, foliose, and fruticose growth forms) have been shown
to be more common and more diverse in older, less disturbed forests
and grasslands and in areas with cleaner air.

"Pin-lichens" (former order Caliciales) have been recommended as
indicators of long, continuous occupation by mature ("old-growth")
forest in northern temperate and boreal regions. Selva (1994)
developed a group of forest continuity indicator species for
northeastern North America.

Ground lichens have been used as indicators of dighton mycorrhizal diversity
for grassland and steppe habitats. Gelatinous ground lichens
including Collema, Leptogium, and Polychidium species all fix
nitrogen and protect the soil surface.

## Estimating Abundance

Abundance can be estimated by:
- Qualitative field notes on apparent abundance
- Abundance classes (e.g., density combined with cover)
- Frequency across subplots as an estimate of abundance
- Percent cover of morphological groups in fixed-area subplots
- Cover classes for macrolichen species

Biomass of canopy macrolichens has been estimated efficiently by
analysis of litterfall and lichens on lower canopy branches in
conifer forests. Permanent photoplots can be used to monitor
trends in abundance over time.
