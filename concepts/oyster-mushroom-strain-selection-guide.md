# Oyster [[mushroom-strain-selection]] Guide

Selecting the right oyster mushroom strain is one of the most important decisions a cultivator makes. The genus Pleurotus contains approximately 70 recorded species with eleven discrete intersterility groups, each having distinct growing requirements, fruiting characteristics, and market qualities.

## Taxonomy and Species Classification

Oyster mushrooms belong to the family Pleurotaceae. The type species is [[pleurotus-ostreatus]]. Most members have a monomitic hyphal system with clamp connections. Determination of species is complicated by morphological similarities and environmental effects, but mating compatibility studies have identified eleven intersterility groups:

|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| P. ostreatus | P. columbinus, P. florida, P. salignus | I |
| P. pulmonarius | P. sajor-caju, P. sapidus | II |

| P. cornucopiae | P. citrinopileatus | IV |

## Key Commercial Species

### Pleurotus ostreatus (The Tree Oyster)

The most important and widely cultivated oyster mushroom worldwide, second only to button mushroom in total global production. Widespread in temperate zones on all continents.

- **Spawn run temperature**: 25 degrees C
- **Primordia formation**: 10-15 degrees C (requires "cold shock")
- **Fruiting temperature**: 10-17 degrees C
- **CO2 tolerance during spawn run**: Up to 28% (280,000 ppm)
- **CO2 during fruiting**: Below 1,000 ppm (above this produces elongated stipes)
- **Optimum season**: Autumn
- **Cultivation methods**: Log, shelf, box, bottle, bag
- **Notes**: 66 commercial strains available in Korea as of 2003. Strains vary in heat/cold tolerance. Some strains are less affected by unfavorable temperatures at later flush stages. Cap color darkens with lower growing temperatures.

### Pleurotus florida

Widespread in temperate, subtropical, and tropical zones. Often considered a subspecies of P. ostreatus but with significantly different morphology and temperature requirements.

- **Spawn run temperature**: 25 degrees C
- **Primordia formation**: 10-25 degrees C (does not require cold shock)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 15-25 degrees C
- **CO2 tolerance during spawn run**: Up to 22%
- **Optimum season**: Spring and summer
- **Cultivation methods**: Shelf, box
- **Notes**: Cap color is light brown at low temperatures, turning pale with increasing temperature. Does not require cold shock for fruiting. Shows the highest yield among Pleurotus species.

### [[pleurotus-eryngii]] (King Oyster)

The "King Oyster" is increasingly popular due to its unique, superior flavor. Wild specimens are collected in southern Europe, North Africa, and central Asia.

- **Spawn run temperature**: 25 degrees C
- **Primordia formation**: 10-15 degrees C (requires cold shock)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 13-18 degrees C
- **CO2 during fruiting**: Below 2,000 ppm (most tolerant of the commercial species)
- **Optimum season**: Autumn
- **Cultivation methods**: Bottle, bag (sawdust-based)
- **Notes**: More prone to diseases and sensitive to growing conditions than P. ostreatus. Grows slower. Cap is cream to grey-brown, stipe is whitish and 10-14 cm long. Reigns supreme for flavor.

### [[pleurotus-pulmonarius]] / sajor-caju

Found in subtropical and tropical regions including India. Note that P. sajor-caju was once mistakenly classified separately but is now considered synonymous with P. pulmonarius.

- **Spawn run temperature**: 25 degrees C
- **Primordia formation**: 10-25 degrees C
- **Fruiting temperature**: 18-25 degrees C
- **CO2 during fruiting**: 400-800 ppm (most sensitive)
- **Optimum season**: Spring and summer
- **Cultivation methods**: Shelf, box
- **Notes**: Suitable for subtropical and tropical cultivation. Biological efficiency on uncrumpled rice straw: 85%.

### [[pleurotus-citrinopileatus]] (Golden Oyster)

One of the most spectacular oyster mushrooms with brilliant yellow coloration.

- **Spawn run temperature**: 75-85 degrees F (24-29 degrees C)
- **Primordia formation**: 70-80 degrees F (21-27 degrees C)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 70-85 degrees F (21-29 degrees C)
- **Cropping cycle**: Two crops, 10-14 days apart
- **Biological efficiency**: 25-75% on wheat straw (lower than other Pleurotus species)
- **Notes**: Extremely fragile post-harvest. Golden color fades with continued cultivation. Spicy and bitter when lightly cooked; develops appealing cashew-like flavor when cooked 15-20 minutes at high heat. Requires higher spawning rates (15-20%). Forms attractive clustered bouquets ("golden gourmet bouquets") that ease harvesting. Primordia will not form below 60-65 degrees F.

### [[pleurotus-cystidiosus]] (Abalone Mushroom)

Unique dimorphic species with both sexual and asexual life cycles. Its asexual stage produces white coremia with black-headed arthroconidia (conidia), which look like contamination but do not affect growth or fruiting.

- **Spawn run temperature**: 25-35 degrees C
- **Primordia formation**: 20-25 degrees C
- **Fruiting temperature**: 25-30 degrees C
- **Optimum season**: Summer
- **Cultivation methods**: Bottle, bag
- **Notes**: Widely distributed in subtropical and tropical regions. Productivity is relatively low.

### [[pleurotus-djamor]] (Pink Oyster)

A brilliantly colored species, the Pink Oyster is one of the most visually striking cultivated mushrooms.

- **Notes**: Tropical and subtropical distribution. Grows on a wide range of substrates. Color fades after harvest.

## Temperature During Spawn Run

During mycelial mass incubation, the room must be maintained 3-5 degrees C below the species optimum temperature to account for respiration heat generated by the colonizing mycelium. This is critical: without this adjustment, the actual substrate temperature can exceed optimal range, slowing or killing the mycelium.

## Breeding Considerations

Commercial strain development uses several techniques:

- **Introduction and selection**: Collecting wild specimens and selecting superior performers
- **Mating compatibility**: Crossing compatible monokaryons to combine desirable traits
- **Protoplast fusion**: Fusing protoplasts from different strains
- **Mutagenesis**: Inducing mutations to create novel variations

Factors to consider in strain selection and breeding:

- Morphological characteristics (cap color, size, shape, cluster formation)
- Physiological characteristics (temperature range, CO2 tolerance, colonization speed)
- Cultivation method compatibility
- Yield potential and biological efficiency
- Disease resistance
- Consumer and processor demands (appearance, shelf life, culinary quality)
- Storage life and post-harvest durability
