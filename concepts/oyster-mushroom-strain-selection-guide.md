    12|# Oyster [[mushroom-strain-selection]] Guide
    13|
    14|Selecting the right oyster mushroom strain is one of the most important decisions a cultivator makes. The genus Pleurotus contains approximately 70 recorded species with eleven discrete intersterility groups, each having distinct growing requirements, fruiting characteristics, and market qualities.
    15|
    16|## Taxonomy and Species Classification
    17|
    18|Oyster mushrooms belong to the family Pleurotaceae. The type species is [[pleurotus-ostreatus]]. Most members have a monomitic hyphal system with clamp connections. Determination of species is complicated by morphological similarities and environmental effects, but mating compatibility studies have identified eleven intersterility groups:

    21||---------|----------------|---------------------|
    22|| P. ostreatus | P. columbinus, P. florida, P. salignus | I |
    23|| P. pulmonarius | P. sajor-caju, P. sapidus | II |
    24|| P. populinus | -- | III |
    25|| P. cornucopiae | P. citrinopileatus | IV |

    34|## Key Commercial Species
    35|
    36|### Pleurotus ostreatus (The Tree Oyster)
    37|
    38|The most important and widely cultivated oyster mushroom worldwide, second only to button mushroom in total global production. Widespread in temperate zones on all continents.
    39|
    40|- **Spawn run temperature**: 25 degrees C
    41|- **Primordia formation**: 10-15 degrees C (requires "cold shock")
    42|- **Fruiting temperature**: 10-17 degrees C
    43|- **CO2 tolerance during spawn run**: Up to 28% (280,000 ppm)
    44|- **CO2 during fruiting**: Below 1,000 ppm (above this produces elongated stipes)
    45|- **Optimum season**: Autumn
    46|- **Cultivation methods**: Log, shelf, box, bottle, bag
    47|- **Notes**: 66 commercial strains available in Korea as of 2003. Strains vary in heat/cold tolerance. Some strains are less affected by unfavorable temperatures at later flush stages. Cap color darkens with lower growing temperatures.
    48|
    49|### Pleurotus florida
    50|
    51|Widespread in temperate, subtropical, and tropical zones. Often considered a subspecies of P. ostreatus but with significantly different morphology and temperature requirements.
    52|
    53|- **Spawn run temperature**: 25 degrees C
    54|- **Primordia formation**: 10-25 degrees C (does not require cold shock)
    55|- **Fruiting temperature**: 15-25 degrees C
    56|- **CO2 tolerance during spawn run**: Up to 22%
    57|- **Optimum season**: Spring and summer
    58|- **Cultivation methods**: Shelf, box
    59|- **Notes**: Cap color is light brown at low temperatures, turning pale with increasing temperature. Does not require cold shock for fruiting. Shows the highest yield among Pleurotus species.
    60|
    61|### [[pleurotus-eryngii]] (King Oyster)
    62|
    63|The "King Oyster" is increasingly popular due to its unique, superior flavor. Wild specimens are collected in southern Europe, North Africa, and central Asia.
    64|
    65|- **Spawn run temperature**: 25 degrees C
    66|- **Primordia formation**: 10-15 degrees C (requires cold shock)
    67|- **Fruiting temperature**: 13-18 degrees C
    68|- **CO2 during fruiting**: Below 2,000 ppm (most tolerant of the commercial species)
    69|- **Optimum season**: Autumn
    70|- **Cultivation methods**: Bottle, bag (sawdust-based)
    71|- **Notes**: More prone to diseases and sensitive to growing conditions than P. ostreatus. Grows slower. Cap is cream to grey-brown, stipe is whitish and 10-14 cm long. Reigns supreme for flavor.
    72|
    73|### [[pleurotus-pulmonarius]] / sajor-caju
    74|
    75|Found in subtropical and tropical regions including India. Note that P. sajor-caju was once mistakenly classified separately but is now considered synonymous with P. pulmonarius.
    76|
    77|- **Spawn run temperature**: 25 degrees C
    78|- **Primordia formation**: 10-25 degrees C
    79|- **Fruiting temperature**: 18-25 degrees C
    80|- **CO2 during fruiting**: 400-800 ppm (most sensitive)
    81|- **Optimum season**: Spring and summer
    82|- **Cultivation methods**: Shelf, box
    83|- **Notes**: Suitable for subtropical and tropical cultivation. Biological efficiency on uncrumpled rice straw: 85%.
    84|
    85|### [[pleurotus-citrinopileatus]] (Golden Oyster)
    86|
    87|One of the most spectacular oyster mushrooms with brilliant yellow coloration.
    88|
    89|- **Spawn run temperature**: 75-85 degrees F (24-29 degrees C)
    90|- **Primordia formation**: 70-80 degrees F (21-27 degrees C)
    91|- **Fruiting temperature**: 70-85 degrees F (21-29 degrees C)
    92|- **Cropping cycle**: Two crops, 10-14 days apart
    93|- **Biological efficiency**: 25-75% on wheat straw (lower than other Pleurotus species)
    94|- **Notes**: Extremely fragile post-harvest. Golden color fades with continued cultivation. Spicy and bitter when lightly cooked; develops appealing cashew-like flavor when cooked 15-20 minutes at high heat. Requires higher spawning rates (15-20%). Forms attractive clustered bouquets ("golden gourmet bouquets") that ease harvesting. Primordia will not form below 60-65 degrees F.
    95|
    96|### [[pleurotus-cystidiosus]] (Abalone Mushroom)
    97|
    98|Unique dimorphic species with both sexual and asexual life cycles. Its asexual stage produces white coremia with black-headed arthroconidia (conidia), which look like contamination but do not affect growth or fruiting.
    99|
   100|- **Spawn run temperature**: 25-35 degrees C
   101|- **Primordia formation**: 20-25 degrees C
   102|- **Fruiting temperature**: 25-30 degrees C
   103|- **Optimum season**: Summer
   104|- **Cultivation methods**: Bottle, bag
   105|- **Notes**: Widely distributed in subtropical and tropical regions. Productivity is relatively low.
   106|
   107|### [[pleurotus-djamor]] (Pink Oyster)
   108|
   109|A brilliantly colored species, the Pink Oyster is one of the most visually striking cultivated mushrooms.
   110|
   111|- **Notes**: Tropical and subtropical distribution. Grows on a wide range of substrates. Color fades after harvest.
   112|
   113|## Temperature During Spawn Run
   114|
   115|During mycelial mass incubation, the room must be maintained 3-5 degrees C below the species optimum temperature to account for respiration heat generated by the colonizing mycelium. This is critical: without this adjustment, the actual substrate temperature can exceed optimal range, slowing or killing the mycelium.
   116|
   117|## Breeding Considerations
   118|
   119|Commercial strain development uses several techniques:
   120|
   121|- **Introduction and selection**: Collecting wild specimens and selecting superior performers
   122|- **Mating compatibility**: Crossing compatible monokaryons to combine desirable traits
   123|- **Protoplast fusion**: Fusing protoplasts from different strains
   124|- **Mutagenesis**: Inducing mutations to create novel variations
   125|
   126|Factors to consider in strain selection and breeding:
   127|
   128|- Morphological characteristics (cap color, size, shape, cluster formation)
   129|- Physiological characteristics (temperature range, CO2 tolerance, colonization speed)
   130|- Cultivation method compatibility
   131|- Yield potential and biological efficiency
   132|- Disease resistance
   133|- Consumer and processor demands (appearance, shelf life, culinary quality)
   134|- Storage life and post-harvest durability

   136|## Matching Strain to Cultivation Method
   137|
   138|| Cultivation Method | Best Suited Species |
   139||-------------------|-------------------|
   140|| Log culture | P. ostreatus, P. eryngii |
   141|| Shelf culture | P. florida, P. sajor-caju, P. cornucopiae |
   142|| Box culture | P. florida, P. sajor-caju, P. cornucopiae |
   143|| Bag culture | P. ostreatus, P. cystidiosus, P. eryngii |
   144|| Bottle culture | P. eryngii, P. cystidiosus |
   145|
   146|The right strain depends on local climate, available substrate materials, market preferences, and the grower's experience level.
