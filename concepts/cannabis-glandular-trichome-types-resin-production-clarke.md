## Cannabis Glandular Trichome Types and Resin Production

## Overview

Glandular trichomes are the microscopic resin-producing structures on the surface of Cannabis plants. They are the primary site of cannabinoid and [[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]], and predicting cannabinoid content.

## Trichome Types

Three types of glandular trichomes are present on the epidermal surfaces of Cannabis, divided into:

### Bulbous Trichomes
- The smallest glandular trichomes
- Consist of a few cells forming a small bulbous head
- Present on all above-ground parts of the plant
- Their contribution to total resin production is minimal
- Less studied than the capitate types

### Capitate-Sessile Trichomes
- Intermediate in size between bulbous and capitate-stalked
- Sit directly on the epidermis without a stalk
- Consist of a head of secretory cells resting on a short pedestal
- More numerous than capitate-stalked trichomes
- Contribute to resin production, especially during [[african-landrace-sativa-genetic-contributions-modern-breeding-oner]] could develop strains with high resin production that release their resin heads with only moderate shaking (rather than customary flailing that also breaks up plant material)
- Strains bred for use as marijuana benefit from extremely tenacious resin heads that do not fall off during packaging and shipment

## Resin and Drying

- Resin has a role in preventing desiccation; high resin content might retard drying
- However, it is a misconception that resin is secreted to coat and seal the surface of calyxes and leaves
- Resin is secreted by glandular trichomes but is trapped under a cuticle layer surrounding the head cells
- This holds the resin away from the surface of the leaves, where it would rarely have a chance to seal the epidermal layer or prevent transpiration
- Some strains dry much more rapidly and completely than others under identical conditions
