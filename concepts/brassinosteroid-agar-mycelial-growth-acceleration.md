# Brassinosteroid Acceleration of Mycelial Growth on Agar Media

The Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt (1990) study demonstrated that 22S,23S-homobrassinolide at a concentration of 10^-2 ppm caused mycelial growth of [[psilocybe-cubensis]] to proceed two to three times faster than untreated controls on 6% malt agar. This finding represented the first documented instance of a brassinosteroid affecting fungal vegetative growth and remains one of the most dramatic growth rate enhancements reported for any hormonal treatment in mycological literature.

## Experimental Conditions on Agar

The agar phase of the experiment used 6% malt agar supplemented with 5 g of KH2PO4 per liter as the basal medium. Ten replicate runs were conducted for both the brassinosteroid-treated and control conditions, providing adequate statistical power to detect the magnitude of growth acceleration observed. The 6% malt extract concentration represents a relatively rich medium, supplying abundant carbohydrates, amino acids, and B vitamins that support vigorous mycelial extension.

The use of solid agar media rather than liquid culture was deliberate: the flat, transparent surface of agar plates allows straightforward measurement of colony diameter over time, making it ideal for quantifying radial mycelial growth rates. This method is standard in mycological laboratories and provides reproducible results across experiments.

## Magnitude of the Growth Enhancement

A two to three fold increase in mycelial growth rate is exceptionally large for a hormonal treatment applied at nanomolar concentrations. For context, most growth-promoting treatments in fungal cultivation produce improvements in the range of 10-30%, and even the most effective nutritional supplements rarely achieve more than a 50-100% increase in growth rate.

The 200-300% increase observed with brassinosteroid treatment suggests that the compound is not merely supplementing nutrition but is fundamentally altering the regulatory machinery that controls hyphal extension. In plant systems, brassinosteroids are known to upregulate cell wall loosening enzymes called expansins, promote the G1/S transition in the cell cycle, and enhance proton pump activity. Analogous mechanisms may operate in fungal hyphae, where tip growth depends on precisely coordinated vesicle trafficking, cell wall synthesis, and membrane expansion.

## Mechanistic Considerations for Hyphal Tip Growth

Fungal hyphal tip growth is a highly polarized process driven by the continuous delivery of cell wall synthases, membrane material, and cytoskeletal components to the growing apex. The Spitzenkorper (apical body) serves as a vesicle supply center that concentrates secretory vesicles at the hyphal tip. Any factor that increases the rate of vesicle delivery, cell wall synthase activity, or membrane expansion at the tip would increase the rate of hyphal extension.

Brassinosteroids could accelerate hyphal tip growth through several mechanisms. As steroidal compounds, they may incorporate into the plasma membrane and alter membrane fluidity, potentially enhancing the mobility and fusion of secretory vesicles. They may also interact with fungal membrane receptors that trigger intracellular signaling cascades leading to upregulation of cell wall biosynthetic enzymes. The structural similarity between brassinosteroids and fungal sterols such as ergosterol makes receptor cross-reactivity a plausible mechanism.

## Comparison with Other Growth Accelerants

Various compounds have been tested for their ability to accelerate mycelial growth on agar media. Auxins such as indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) can produce modest growth rate increases of 10-20% in some fungal species. Cytokinins have shown variable effects, with some studies reporting growth stimulation and others reporting inhibition depending on concentration and species. Gibberellins, originally discovered as fungal metabolites from Gibberella fujikuroi, can affect fungal growth but typically at much higher concentrations than those effective in plants.

The brassinosteroid effect stands out for its magnitude, its activity at extremely low concentrations, and its consistency across ten replicate runs. No other plant hormone class has been reported to produce a two to three fold increase in fungal mycelial growth rate at nanomolar concentrations.

## Implications for Inoculum Production

The practical significance of accelerated mycelial growth on agar extends to inoculum production for mushroom cultivation. In standard cultivation workflows, mycelium is first grown on agar media to generate clean inoculum for grain spawn production. A two to three fold reduction in the time required to produce fully colonized agar plates would significantly accelerate the entire cultivation cycle.

For research laboratories, faster agar growth means quicker strain isolation, purification, and maintenance. For commercial operations, it could reduce the lead time between strain selection and production inoculum availability. The ten-run consistency demonstrated in the Gartz study suggests that the effect is reliable and reproducible, important qualities for any treatment intended for routine use.

## Concentration Dependency and Optimal Dosing

The study used a single concentration of 10^-2 ppm (approximately 17 nanomolar for 22S,23S-homobrassinolide). While this concentration produced dramatic effects, the dose-response relationship for brassinosteroid effects on fungal growth has not been systematically characterized. In plant systems, brassinosteroids typically show a biphasic dose-response curve: low concentrations stimulate growth, while higher concentrations can be inhibitory.

Determining the optimal concentration for fungal growth acceleration would require testing a range of brassinosteroid concentrations on the same substrate and fungal species. It is possible that concentrations lower than 10^-2 ppm could produce similar or even greater growth acceleration, as nanomolar concentrations are sufficient for full biological activity in plant systems. Conversely, higher concentrations might lead to diminishing returns or growth inhibition.
