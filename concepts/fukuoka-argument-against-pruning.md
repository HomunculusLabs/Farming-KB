# Fukuoka's Argument Against Pruning — Natural Tree Form

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

One of Masanobu Fukuoka's most detailed and technical arguments concerns the pruning of [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-establishment-bare-hillside]], Fukuoka concluded that pruning is not only unnecessary but actively harmful -- that it destroys the natural form of trees and creates a self-perpetuating cycle of intervention. His argument is grounded in careful observation of phyllotaxy (the arrangement of leaves and branches on plants) and the growth patterns of natural versus cultivated trees.

## The Problem: Pruning Creates the Need for More Pruning

Fukuoka's central insight: "Once the pruning shears have been taken to the tree and branches emerge in complicated shapes, the tree can no longer be left alone. Unless it is carefully tended each year and each branch meticulously trained and pruned, the branches entangle, causing some to weaken and die."

### The Chain Reaction

1. The first pruning cut (even nipping one bud on a sapling) alters the tree's [[fukuoka-mandarin-orange-citrus]], orange, and cherry tree branches is always located directly above the first leaf
- The eleventh leaf is always directly above the sixth
- When the distance between consecutive buds is one inch, the distance from one leaf to the next leaf directly above it is always five inches
- Two leaves will not overlap, or two branches emerge, within any five-inch length along the branch

### The Consequence

Because of phyllotaxy, "the branches and leaves of natural plants all receive equal ventilation and sunlight" without any human intervention. Every leaf and branch grows in an orderly and regular arrangement. No part of the plant is in disarray or confusion.

## Why Conventional Trees Appear to Need Pruning

Fukuoka identifies two critical reasons why conventional fruit trees seem to need pruning:

### 1. The Transplant Wound

When a fruit tree sapling is planted conventionally:
- The roots are trimmed during digging
- The stem is cut back to 1-2 feet
- This first pruning alone robs the tree of its natural form
- The sapling begins putting out buds and suckers in a complex, confused manner

"Like the transplanted mountain pine to the top of which the gardener takes his shears, once pruned, a tree cannot be left untended."

### 2. The Convenience of Operations

Pruning is done not for the tree's benefit but for the convenience of human operations:

- Tillage and weeding require clear space beneath trees (but if you eliminate tillage and weeding, this reason disappears)
- Pesticide spraying requires accessible tree surfaces (but if you eliminate pesticides, this reason disappears)
