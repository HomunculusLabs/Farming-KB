## Psychrotolerant and Psychrophilic Fungi

### Definition and Ecological Context

Psychrotolerant fungi can grow at low temperatures but have optima in the mesophilic range. True psychrophiles have growth optima at or below 15°C and are unable to grow above ~20°C. Both groups are important in cold soils, permafrost, polar habitats, cold-stored food, and high-altitude or high-latitude ecosystems.

### Why These Fungi Are Underreported

Many psychrotolerant and psychrophilic species may appear "rare" in surveys simply because **standard incubation temperatures (20–25°C) exclude them**. They are consistently overlooked when plates are not maintained at low temperature.

### Isolation Methods

#### Direct Plating (Carreiro and Koske 1992)

- Forest soil, dung, and plant remains are plated directly at low temperatures.
- Soil/litter samples are plated onto **chilled MYP (Malt-Yeast-Peptone) agar**, or pour-plates are prepared with agar **cooled to 43°C** before pouring.
- Plates are **incubated at 0°C** and examined **twice weekly for up to 4 weeks**.

#### Extended Incubation (Petrini et al. 1992b)

- Primary plates incubated at **4°C for 10 weeks**.
- Cultures then transferred to **2% malt-extract agar slants** and returned to **4°C for an additional 3–10 weeks**.

This extended protocol recognizes that cold-adapted fungi are typically slow-growing and may require prolonged incubation to produce visible colonies.

#### Coexistence with Mesophiles

[[psychrophilic-fungi]] are expected to **coexist with mesophiles** in many habitats — their spores survive warm periods and germinate when temperatures drop (Petrini et al. 1992b). Standard room-temperature incubation of plates from cold habitats therefore recovers only the mesophilic component of the community.

#### Distinguishing Psychrophilic from Psychrotolerant

After initial isolation at low temperature, **subsequent incubation above 20°C** distinguishes:

- **Psychrotolerant** taxa — continue to grow.
- **Psychrophilic** taxa — fail to grow or die.
