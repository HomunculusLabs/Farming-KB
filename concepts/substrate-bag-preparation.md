## Substrate Bag Preparation

Complete step-by-step guide to producing substrate bags for mushroom cultivation. Based on FAO training manual for tropical mushroom cultivation in Thailand. See [[mushroom-substrates]] for substrate science and [[mushroom-pasteurization]] for pasteurization methods.

## Materials Needed

- Plastic bags (polypropylene, 7" x 12.5")
- Plastic necks (~4 cm diameter)
- Cotton plugs
- Cover filters (square pieces of filter paper)
- Rubber bands
- Sawdust (cover pile with rice sacks to maintain humidity)
- Rice bran
- Calcium sulfate
- Calcium carbonate
- Magnesium sulfate
- Sugar (optional)

## Sawdust Substrate Recipe

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sawdust | 100 kg |
| Rice bran | 5 kg |
| Calcium sulfate | 2 kg |
| Calcium carbonate | 1 kg |
| Magnesium sulfate | 0.2 kg |
| Sugar | 0-1 kg |
| Water (for moisture) | to reach 65-75% |

### Recipe Notes
- Recipe serves as reference; can be modified with rice flour, sticky rice flour, corn flour, cassava peels, cotton waste, soybean residue, and other agricultural waste
- In cool climates: additives up to 20% possible
- In hot climates: do NOT exceed 7.5% additives (contamination risk)

## Rice Straw Substrate (Alternative)

Rice straw generates higher yield and better quality mushrooms (texture and taste) than sawdust, but requires more preparation:

### Fermentation Process (9-12 days total)
1. Grind straw to reduce size
2. Soak 100 kg paddy straw with water + 1-2% urea by weight; ferment 3 days
3. Turn pile, mix with 2% lime; ferment 3 more days
4. Turn pile again, mix with 0.2% magnesium sulfate; ferment 3 more days
5. Check: no urea smell + moisture 65-75% = ready for packing
6. If urea smell persists, ferment further until gone
7. Pack in 8" x 12" PP bags

## Step-by-Step Bag Production

### 1. Weigh Components
Use a scale for accurate measurement of all ingredients.

### 2. Mix Ingredients
Mix well in a mechanical mixer or manually using shovels/paddles. Ensure no lumps.

### 3. Adjust Moisture
Add water gradually to reach 65-75% moisture content. Squeeze test: a handful should hold shape but release a few drops when squeezed firmly.

### 4. Fill Bags
Sit on floor, fill plastic bags with substrate using small shovel. Fill to consistent height.

### 5. Compact
Hit bags with empty bottle or by hand to compact substrate evenly. Alternatively, use a compacting machine.

### 6. Apply Plastic Neck
- Place plastic ring on bag opening
- Pull top of bag through plastic neck
- Fold down over plastic ring
- Secure with rubber band

### 7. Create Center Hole
Introduce a pointed stick through the plastic neck to make a hole almost to the bottom of the bag. DO NOT touch or pierce the bottom of the bag.

### 8. Check Weight
Bags should weigh 800-1000 grams each.

### 9. Close with Cotton Plug Cap
- Prepare plastic caps filled with cotton
- Close bags with cotton-filled caps
- The cotton plug allows gas exchange while filtering contaminants

### 10. Transfer to Pasteurization
- Load iron racks (9 bags per rack for commercial chambers)
- Transfer to pasteurization chamber
- See [[mushroom-pasteurization]] for pasteurization procedures

## Yield Expectations

- One substrate bag produces 250-500 grams of mushrooms
- Rice straw substrate generally produces higher yields than sawdust
- Total yield depends on species, spawn quality, and environmental conditions

## Making Substrate Bags with One Hand

The FAO manual notes that bag-making generally requires two hands but each step can be adapted. For people with limited hand function:
- Use clamps or fixtures to hold bags open during filling
- Adapted compacting tools can substitute for hand/bottle compaction
- Pre-formed plastic necks reduce manipulation needed
- Work surface height adjustments improve access
