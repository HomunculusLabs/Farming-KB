# Cannabis Seedling Care and Early Growth

The seedling stage is a critical period in cannabis
cultivation that lasts from three to six weeks after
germination. During this stage, the root system grows
rapidly while aboveground growth is slow. Proper care
during this vulnerable period establishes the foundation
for healthy, vigorous plants throughout the entire life
cycle.

See also: [[cervantes-vegetative-phase]]

## Seedling Development

When a seed sprouts, the white taproot emerges first.
Soon afterward, the cotyledon (also known as the seed
leaf or seedling leaf) appears. The cotyledons spread
out as the stem elongates, providing initial energy
through photosynthesis. Within a few days, the first
true leaves appear, and the plant is officially a
seedling.

Key developmental milestones:

- **Cotyledons emerge** and unfold to begin
  photosynthesis
- **First true leaves** appear within a few days of
  cotyledon emergence
- **Root system develops rapidly** underground while
  aboveground growth remains slow
- **Transitional phase** begins as the plant develops
  3 to 5 sets of true leaves
- **Rapid foliage growth** signals the end of the
  seedling stage and beginning of [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]

The seedling stage is over when rapid foliage growth
starts. At this point, plants need more room to grow
and should be transplanted into larger containers.

## Selecting and Thinning Seedlings

As seedlings mature, some will grow faster, stronger,
and appear healthier in general. Others will sprout
slowly and be weak and leggy. Cervantes advises culling
sickly, weak plants and focusing attention on the
remaining strong survivors.

Seedlings should be big enough to thin out by the third
to fifth week of growth. Thinning is difficult for
growers who pay premium prices for seeds, but selecting
only the strongest plants ensures better results.

## Sex Expression and Environment

[[duggar-mushroom-environmental-factors-temperature-moisture-light]] start influencing sex the moment
the seedling has three pairs of true leaves (not
counting cotyledons). Factors that increase female
ratio from seed include:

- Higher nitrogen levels in the first two weeks
- Lower potassium levels in the first two weeks
- Low temperatures
- High humidity
- Low growing medium moisture
- More blue [[cannabis-light-spectrum-and-color-bands]]
- Fewer hours of daylight (around 14 hours)
- Minimizing environmental stress

Conversely, warm temperatures, stress, high potassium,
and long daylight hours tend to produce more male
plants.
