# Natural Sewage Treatment Systems

Natural sewage treatment systems use biological processes, specific plant species, and
designed landscape features to purify wastewater, recover nutrients, and produce useful
outputs such as biogas, fodder, and compost. These systems replace or supplement
conventional sewage infrastructure with ecologically integrated alternatives that turn
waste into a productive resource.

## Principles

In permaculture design, sewage is not viewed as waste but as a nutrient stream. The goal
is to safely process human and animal waste through biological filters, plant uptake, and
microbial activity while capturing energy and returning fertility to the landscape.
Subsoil discharge alone is insufficient — nitrogen compounds persist unless actively
treated by productive pond algae or plant systems.

## Water Purification Species

A core strategy is channelling contaminated water through planted gravel beds, lagoons,
or ditches where specific species absorb and break down pollutants:

| Species | Role |
|---|---|
| *Alisma plantago-aquatica* | General water purification |
| *Mentha aquatica* (water mint) | General water purification |
| *Juncus effusus* (soft rush) | Chlorinated hydrocarbons, cyanide, thiocyanates, phenols |
| *Schoenoplectus lacustris* (bulrush) | Chlorinated hydrocarbons (large aerenchyma pith cells) |
| *Spartina* spp. (cordgrass) | General water purification, saline environments |
| *Iris pseudacorus* (yellow flag) | General water purification |

### Targeted Contaminant Treatment

- **Chlorinated hydrocarbons**: Treated by rush types with large pith cells
  (*aerenchyma*) — specifically *Juncus* spp. and *Schoenoplectus* spp.
- **Cyanide compounds, thiocyanates, phenols**: Treated effectively with *Juncus*
  when water has 7+ hours of flow time through the planted system.

## Comfrey Beds for Faecal Waste

Comfrey (*Symphytum* spp.) beds are highly effective at absorbing faecal products from
animal wash-water. Key characteristics:

- Can handle heavy inputs of raw faeces without damage
- The resulting crop can be used as fodder or applied as "instant compost"
- Ideal for farm-scale systems where animal effluent is a significant waste stream

## Sewage Lagoons and Energy Production

Sewage lagoons represent a proven, large-scale natural treatment approach:

- **Werribee case study**: Sewage lagoons were safely used for beef cattle grazing for
  over 35 years, demonstrating long-term viability and safety
- **Coppiced poplar plots**: Poplars grown on sewage-irrigated land and coppiced can
  produce approximately 60% of a town's energy needs
- **Flowthrough systems**: Can be configured for methane production alongside irrigation

## Salinity Management

Water passing through towns typically increases in salinity by 300–400 ppm. Natural
treatment systems address this through:

- Algae production in treatment ponds, which absorbs surplus salts
- Plant uptake across lagoon and reed-bed systems
- Productive pond algae that simultaneously treat nitrogen compounds

## Domestic Sewage Separation

At the domestic level, separating sewage into solids and liquids enables more targeted
treatment:

- **Urine applications**: Diluted 5%, urine controls moulds on cucurbits; 2% urea
  sprayed on rice foliage can increase grain protein to 40%
- **Solids**: Directed to composting or biogas systems
- **Liquids**: Treated through greywater systems, plant filters, or leach fields

## Leach Field Design

For septic tank effluent disposal, underground leach fields integrated with tree planting
offer an effective solution:

### Trench Design

- Base slope ratio of 1:12
- 18 cm half-pipe laid in the trench
- Coarse gravels surrounding the pipe for drainage and root access
- Trees planted 1–2 metres off the sides to access nutrients without clogging the pipe

### Pit Design

- Square or round pits, approximately 25 m square
- Layered construction: graded stone at base, cardboard layer, then straw layer on top
- Provides distributed nutrient release to surrounding soil

## Biogas Systems

Biogas conversion turns sewage solids into methane for cooking, heating, or electricity:

- **Tank dimensions**: 1.5–2 m deep, 3–4 m diameter
- **Loading**: Via a chute for adding organic material
- **Gas collection**: Perforated pipe connected to a gas compressor
- **Integration**: Effluent from biogas digesters can be directed to leach fields or
  irrigation systems, closing the nutrient loop

## Natural Swimming Pools

[[natural-swimming-pools]] demonstrate the principles of biological water treatment applied
to recreational use:

1. Water filters down through a pebble bed beneath the pool
2. Filtered water cycles through a reed-bed purification zone
3. Water returns via a cascade, re-oxygenating before re-entering the swimming area
4. No chemicals required — the planted filter system maintains water quality

## Designer's Checklist for Water on Property

When designing water and sewage systems for a permaculture property:

- [ ] Map all water sources, flows, and existing drainage patterns
- [ ] Identify opportunities for gravity-fed systems to minimise energy inputs
- [ ] Design for separation of waste streams at source (solids vs liquids, grey vs black)
- [ ] Select appropriate purification species based on known contaminants
- [ ] Ensure minimum flow times for targeted pollutant breakdown (e.g., 7+ hours for
      cyanide/phenol treatment via *Juncus*)
- [ ] Integrate nutrient-rich effluent into productive systems (fodder crops, orchards,
      energy crops)
- [ ] Plan for salinity monitoring where town water pass-through is a factor
- [ ] Size biogas systems to match waste input and energy demand
- [ ] Design leach fields with proper slope, drainage material, and tree placement
- [ ] Consider coppice systems on sewage-irrigated land for energy production
- [ ] Ensure all systems have fail-safes and overflow paths to prevent contamination
- [ ] Monitor nitrogen levels — subsoil discharge alone will not remove nitrogen compounds
