# Additional [[david-holmgren]]'s 12 principles form the core of permaculture
design philosophy, several additional principles are widely recognized
and practiced throughout the permaculture community. These principles
expand upon Holmgren's framework and provide additional guidance for
designers working to create sustainable systems.

## Stacking of Functions

While not one of Holmgren's original 12, the principle of Stacking
of Functions is one of the most oft-recited permaculture principles.
This principle ties in strongly with "obtain a yield" and expands upon
it. Instead of producing just one yield, stacking of functions implies
that you try to produce as many yields as possible from any one design
element.

Synonyms include "multi-use" and "multifunctional." By stacking
functions and maximizing the potential functions of any one thing, you
achieve much greater efficiency in your system. With this greater
efficiency you can either use fewer resources to achieve the same level
of productivity, or accomplish many more things with the resources you
use.

The more productive each individual element of your personal system,
the more functions it stacks, the fewer resources the group as a whole
needs to use. For example, a chicken provides eggs, meat, pest
control, fertilizer, soil cultivation, and entertainment all from one
design element.

## The Problem is the Solution

There are many ways to deal with problems. In permaculture, a "problem"
means something that is decreasing ecological activity in your system.
A problem may have been designed or may be pre-existing. Either way, a
problem represents a significant amount of embodied energy in the
system. The permaculture principles tell us to catch and store energy,
produce no waste, and use the edges. A "problem" is only something
that has been devalued and marginalized.

This principle requires imagination and adaptability. The first instinct
with a problem is to remove it. Instead, drop the judgment of a thing
being a "problem" and see it for its potential. Look at what it CAN
do, not what it does not do.

### Applying Beyond Physical Design

This principle applies to more than physical design elements. It can
be applied to your whole life:

- Have a job you dislike? Use the money to fund learning something
  new
- Is your life too hectic? Turn the rush into a jogging schedule
- Have an invasive species problem? Research whether it has useful
  properties

The point is not the specific suggestions but the representation of
how this principle can be applied to all aspects of design and life.
[[use-and-value-renewable-resources]]

The key word is "use" as an imperative. Go out and use renewable
resources; their value is in their usefulness. It is not ecologically
unethical to use renewable resources. What is unethical is to use
nonrenewable resources and throw them away, or to over-harvest in a
way that is nonrenewable.

By actively seeking out and using renewable services and resources,
you lead by example. We vote with our dollars as well as our ballots.
By frequenting renewable resources we help support and accelerate the
economic shift needed to make permaculture financially viable.

## Synthesis

These additional principles work in concert with Holmgren's 12 to
create a comprehensive design philosophy. The stacking of functions
maximizes efficiency, the problem is the solution transforms obstacles
into opportunities, and everything is an ecology expands the scope of
permaculture thinking to all systems. Together they form a complete
toolkit for creating regenerative designs.
