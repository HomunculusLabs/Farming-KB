## Morels (Morchella)

Morels, sometimes called "sponge mushrooms," are among the most prized of all wild fungi. They rank next to truffles in European esteem. Morels are Ascomycetes, characterized by their distinctive honeycomb-like caps composed of pits and ridges. They typically have stalks 1 to 3 inches in length and are found in spring, often in association with dead or dying trees, old apple orchards, and burned areas.

All related genera of Morchella are considered edible when thoroughly cooked. Raw morels contain small amounts of hydrazine compounds that are destroyed by cooking. They cannot be peeled -- the entire mushroom, cap and stem, is edible. Morels have never been successfully cultivated on a commercial scale, making them exclusively a wild-harvested crop.
