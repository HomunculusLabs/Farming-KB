# Psilocybe Mushrooms of South America

South America harbors a diverse array of psilocybin-containing mushrooms,
spanning the genera Psilocybe, Panaeolus, Gymnopilus, Pluteus, and others.
Species are documented from Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Brazil,
Venezuela, Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay. Many species are shared with
Central America and Mexico, reflecting the connected subtropical ecosystem.

## Psilocybe Species of South America

### Psilocybe cubensis

The most widespread psilocybin mushroom in South America, found throughout the
northern subtropical regions. Distributed across Colombia, Bolivia, Ecuador,
Honduras, El Salvador, Venezuela, and Brazil. See [[stamets-mushrooms-world-cubensis]].

### Psilocybe zapotecorum

A potent species found in swampy or muddy soils, in humus rich with leaves
and wood debris, in marshy deciduous forests, and in coffee plantations.
Frequently found on the surfaces of ravines with exposed soils. Collected in
southern Mexico and subtropical South America (Colombia, Peru, Brazil,
Argentina). From Brazilian specimens: up to .30% psilocybin and 1% psilocin
(Stijve and de Meijer 1993). This mushroom is sometimes found inside the mud
houses of native Zapotec peoples and is held in high esteem by Mazatecs and
Zapotecs.

### Psilocybe yungensis Singer and Smith

Common name: divinatory mushroom, genius mushroom. Most frequently found in
clusters on rotting wood, sometimes at bases of stumps, in coffee plantations
or subtropical forests at 1000-2000 meters. Reported from Colombia and Ecuador,
extending north to Mexico, in June and July. Also reported from Bolivia in
January. Moderately active; distinct for its penchant for growing in great
numbers on decomposing stumps or wood debris, its orangish color, and caps
that remain conic at maturity.

### [[psilocybe-caerulescens-entity]]

Distributed throughout subtropical Mexico, Colombia, Bolivia, Ecuador,
Honduras, El Salvador, Venezuela, and Australia. Probably more widely
distributed than currently reported.

### Psilocybe hoogshagenii

Also reported from Brazil and Colombia. Specimens from Brazil yielded up to
.30% psilocybin and .30% psilocin (Stijve and de Meijer 1993). A variety
from the state of Puebla, Mexico (Psilocybe hoogshagenii var. convexa), is
commonly seen by coffee growers who report massive flushes appearing and then
disappearing.

### [[psilocybe-brasiliensis]]

Known only from Brazil, near Sao Paulo. This species belongs to the South
American psilocybin mycoflora that remains poorly documented compared to
the better-studied Mexican species.

### Pluteus glaucus

A wood-decomposing species from Brazil with up to .28% psilocybin and .12%
psilocin, a range comparable to Pluteus salicinus (Stijve and de Meijer 1993).

## Panaeolus Species in South America

### Panaeolus cyanescens (= Copelandia cyanescens)

Found gregariously in grassy areas across the North and South American
continents. Reported from Brazil, Bolivia, the Philippines, eastern Australia,
and many tropical regions. A potent Copelandian species.

### [[panaeolus-subbalteatus]]

Widely distributed. Reported from North America, South America, Europe,
middle Siberia, Africa, and the Hawaiian archipelago. From Brazilian
specimens, Stijve and Meijer (1993) found .08% psilocybin and no detectable
psilocin. Gartz (1993) found maxima of 0.7% psilocybin from Brazilian
specimens with nearly tenfold variation in potency.

## Ecological Notes

The warm, humid tropical and subtropical climates of South America provide
ideal conditions for psilocybin mushrooms. Coffee plantations in particular
serve as rich habitats, with species like P. zapotecorum and P. yungensis
frequently found in the soils of these agricultural systems. The presence of
abundant cattle in many regions supports dung-dwelling species like P. cubensis
and Panaeolus cyanescens.

Many South American psilocybin species remain poorly studied and potentially
underreported. As mycological surveys continue, new species and range
extensions are expected, particularly in the vast Amazon basin and Andean
regions that remain undersampled.
