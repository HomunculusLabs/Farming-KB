### Psilocybe zapotecorum

A potent species found in swampy or muddy soils, in humus rich with leaves
and wood debris, in marshy deciduous forests, and in coffee plantations.
Frequently found on the surfaces of ravines with exposed soils. Collected in
southern Mexico and subtropical South America (Colombia, Peru, Brazil,
Argentina). From Brazilian specimens: up to .30% psilocybin and 1% psilocin
(Stijve and de Meijer 1993). This mushroom is sometimes found inside the mud
houses of native Zapotec peoples and is held in high esteem by Mazatecs and
Zapotecs.

### Psilocybe yungensis Singer and Smith

Common name: divinatory mushroom, genius mushroom. Most frequently found in
clusters on rotting wood, sometimes at bases of stumps, in coffee plantations
or subtropical forests at 1000-2000 meters. Reported from Colombia and Ecuador,
extending north to Mexico, in June and July. Also reported from Bolivia in
January. Moderately active; distinct for its penchant for growing in great
numbers on decomposing stumps or wood debris, its orangish color, and caps
that remain conic at maturity.

### [[psilocybe-brasiliensis]]

Known only from Brazil, near Sao Paulo. This species belongs to the South
American psilocybin mycoflora that remains poorly documented compared to
the better-studied Mexican species.

### Pluteus glaucus

A wood-decomposing species from Brazil with up to .28% psilocybin and .12%
psilocin, a range comparable to Pluteus salicinus (Stijve and de Meijer 1993).

## Panaeolus Species in South America

### Panaeolus cyanescens (= Copelandia cyanescens)

Found gregariously in grassy areas across the North and South American
continents. Reported from Brazil, Bolivia, the Philippines, eastern Australia,
and many tropical regions. A potent Copelandian species.
