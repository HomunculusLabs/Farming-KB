## Mineral Transformations and Biogeochemical Cycles by Fungi

## Overview

Chapter 5 of *Fungi in the Environment*, by Gadd, Burford, Fomina, and Melville, presents a geomycological perspective on how fungi transform minerals and drive biogeochemical cycles. Fungi are not merely decomposers of organic matter — they are active geological agents that weather rocks, solubilize and precipitate minerals, and mediate the cycling of essential elements including carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, iron, and manganese.

## Geomycology Defined

### What Is Geomycology?

Geomycology is the study of fungal interactions with minerals and geological substrates. It encompasses:

- **Bioweathering**: The breakdown of rocks and minerals by fungal activity
- **Biomineralization**: The formation of minerals by fungal metabolic processes
- **Bioaccumulation**: The concentration of metals and minerals within fungal biomass
- **Biotransformation**: The chemical alteration of mineral species by fungi

Fungi are uniquely suited to geomycological roles because their filamentous growth habit allows them to penetrate rock crevices, their exudates include powerful chelating agents and organic acids, and their biomass provides vast surface area for mineral interactions.

### Fungi as Dominant Soil Biomass

In many soils, fungi comprise the largest pool of living biomass, exceeding bacteria, other microorganisms, and invertebrates. Their filamentous growth habit and exopolymer production contribute to soil structure maintenance. This biomass dominance means fungal metabolic activities have outsized effects on mineral cycling relative to their proportion of total soil mass.
