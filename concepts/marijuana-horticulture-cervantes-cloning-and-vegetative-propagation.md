# Cannabis Cloning and Vegetative Propagation

## Overview

Cloning is the most common method of replicating cannabis plants in cultivation. A clone is a
genetically identical copy of the mother plant, produced by cutting a branch tip and inducing it to
form roots. Cloning ensures that all plants in a garden are female and share the exact same genetic
characteristics, including potency, growth rate, flowering time, disease resistance, and cannabinoid
profile. This consistency is essential for commercial and medical growers who require predictable
results from each harvest cycle.

## Mother Plants

Mother plants are the foundation of any cloning program. Growers select strong, healthy, potent
female plants and maintain them under an 18-24 hour daily photoperiod to keep them in the vegetative
growth stage indefinitely. Several strong, healthy mother plants ensure a consistent supply of
all-female clones.

### Selecting Mother Plants

- Choose plants with vigorous growth, strong stems, and desirable traits
- Plants should be grown for 6-9 months to establish robust root systems
- Maintain an 18-24 hour photoperiod to prevent flowering
- Repot periodically to prevent root binding
- Growing mother plants hydroponically can improve clone quality

### Mother Plant Maintenance

Mothers require consistent care including proper nutrition, adequate light, and regular pruning to
maintain healthy growth. Branches should be allowed to mature enough to provide viable cuttings
without depleting the mother's energy reserves.

## The Cloning Process

### Taking Cuttings

Branch tips are cut from the mother plant to form clones. The cut end is typically treated with a
rooting hormone before being placed in a growing medium. Bottom leaves are trimmed from the cutting
to reduce moisture loss and direct the plant's energy toward root development.

### Root Development Timeline

The speed of root development depends on the age and position of the cutting on the mother plant:

- Cuttings from young growth: cell division starts Day 4, root nubs form Day 6, roots grow Day 7,
  transplant-ready Day 14
- Cuttings from older growth: cell division starts Day 6, root nubs form Day 10, roots grow Day 20,
  transplant-ready Day 28

Younger cuttings root approximately twice as fast as those taken from older growth, making them
preferable for efficient cloning programs.

### Rooting Conditions

Clones require specific environmental conditions to successfully form roots:

- pH of the rooting medium should be between 5.0 and 6.0
- Aeroponic systems perform best at pH 5.0-5.5
- [[cervantes-compact-fluorescent-lighting]] is preferred during rooting (10-21 days)
- Temperature should be maintained in the 72-80°F range
- High humidity (70-90%) reduces transpiration stress
- Minimal nitrogen; increased phosphorus promotes root growth

## Preparing Mother Plants for Cloning

### Carbohydrate Building

Clones develop dense root systems faster when stems have a high carbohydrate and low nitrogen
concentration. Growers build carbohydrate levels by:

- Leaching the growing medium with copious quantities of water to flush out nutrients
- Reverse [[cannabis-foliar-feeding]]: misting mother plants with clean water every morning for 3-4 days to
  leach nitrogen from leaves
- Older leaves may turn light green as nitrogen depletes and carbohydrates build

### Hormone Considerations

Root growth hormones are concentrated differently throughout the plant:

- Lower, older branches near the base of the main stem contain the highest concentration of root
  hormones
- These lower branches produce the fastest-rooting clones
- The top of the plant contains older hormones; cuttings from this area root slowly

A rigid branch that folds over quickly when bent is a good indicator of high carbohydrate content,
which is favorable for cloning success.

## Cutting Size and Selection

### Optimal Cutting Characteristics

- Small clones with few leaves root faster than large cuttings with many leaves
- A small amount of leaf surface is necessary for photosynthesis to supply energy for root growth
- Thin-stemmed clones have fewer starch reserves but need less energy to initiate roots
- Large cuttings with large stems packed with starch actually grow roots more slowly
- Excess starch in moist substrate can attract diseases

### Strain Differences in Cloneability

Most Skunk and Indica strains are relatively easy to clone with high success rates. Ruderalis
Indica and Ruderalis Skunk are generally unsuitable as mother plants due to their auto-flowering
tendency. Some outdoor strains with early flowering tendencies (e.g., Early Girl, Early Skunk) can
pre-sex under an 18-hour photoperiod, but this does not necessarily exclude them as mother plants.

## Common Cloning Problems

### Embolism

An embolism is an air bubble trapped in the hollow stem of a cutting. This occurs when large
cuttings are taken and left on a surface before being placed in water or growing medium. When an
embolism forms, fluid flow stops and the clone dies. [[jadam-crop-disease-prevention-methods]] include:

- Immediately dipping cuttings in water or growing medium after cutting
- Taking cuttings underwater to eliminate air contact

### Environmental Stress

Cloning is the most traumatic event a cannabis plant can experience. The entire plant chemistry
changes as the stem that once grew leaves must now produce roots. During this vulnerable period:

- Avoid all sprays [[solomon-liquid-fertilizers-and-foliar-feeding]] during rooting
- Maintain consistent [[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]]
- Provide gentle air circulation without strong drafts
- Use sterile tools [[hamilton-potting-compost-recipes-and-growing-media]] to prevent infection

## Post-Rooting Care

Once clones have developed a strong root system, they are given 18-24 hours of light to remain in
the [[knf-vegetative-growth-stage]]. After the root system is established, clones are transplanted into
larger containers for 1-4 weeks of vegetative growth before being induced to flower under a 12/12
photoperiod.

With good technique and experience, most growers achieve a consistent 100% clone survival rate.
Growers are advised to take five to ten practice clones before committing to a serious cloning
operation, especially when working with unfamiliar strains.
