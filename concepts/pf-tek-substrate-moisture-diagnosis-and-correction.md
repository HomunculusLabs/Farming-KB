## Dried Substrate Symptoms

When substrate is too dry, a characteristic pattern emerges that serves as a
reliable diagnostic signature. [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]]. This pattern of normal
germination followed by arrested growth with no visible contamination is the
hallmark of dried substrate.

It is important to recognize because it can be confused with other problems
such as weak spore genetics or contamination that has not yet become visible.
The key distinguishing feature is the clean, white appearance of the halted
mycelium with no colored growths or foul odors.

The PF TEK describes this as an important concept that enables diagnosis and
correction of problems experienced with drying. Generally, any halted growth
with no contamination is a sign of dried substrate. This diagnostic rule
simplifies troubleshooting considerably.

## Causes of Dried Substrate

The most common cause of dried substrate is overheating during the steam
sterilization process. When jars are boiled too vigorously, excessive steam
penetrates the jars and evaporates moisture from the substrate. Gas stoves
are noted as being the easiest to control for maintaining a gentle boil.

The PF TEK specifically warns against overheating and provides detailed
guidance on maintaining the lowest possible boil. The [[pf-substrate-formula]]. The PF TEK advises cultivators to keep detailed
notes on formulas for replicating the substrate formula that fruits the best.

If a batch shows signs of dried substrate, the water quantity should be
incrementally increased in subsequent batches until the mycelium colonizes
completely without halting. The standard formula calls for 40 to 45 cc of
water per half-pint jar, equivalent to a little less than 1/4 cup.

Adjustments should be made incrementally, adding small amounts of additional
water until colonization proceeds to completion without stalling. It is better
to approach the correct moisture level gradually from the dry side than to
risk over-saturating the substrate.

## Excessively Wet Substrate Symptoms

When substrate is too wet, it takes on a sloppy or soggy consistency. The
mixture does not hold its structure when loaded into the jar and may settle
excessively. During colonization, the mycelium may grow slowly or become
matted and dense rather than forming the desired airy structure.

In severe cases, water collects in the bottom of the jar during incubation.
