# Mushroom Cultivation Pests and Contamination

Contamination by competitor organisms and pests is the most common cause of failure in mushroom cultivation. The Mushroom Cultivator provides the most comprehensive identification guide, cataloging dozens of contaminants by genus. This page summarizes the major threats and their management.

## Bacterial Contaminants

### Bacillus (Wet Spot)
- Most common bacterial contaminant in grain spawn
- Causes sour, foul odor detectable through the dry vermiculite layer within 2 days of inoculation
- Grain appears wet, slimy, or matted
- Caused by insufficient [[sterilization]] or overly wet grain
- Prevention: extend sterilization time; ensure proper grain moisture

### Pseudomonas (Bacterial Blotch and Pit)
- Causes dark brown, sunken lesions on mushroom caps
- Thrives in wet conditions with poor air circulation
- Prevention: improve FAE; reduce surface moisture; do not water mushrooms directly

## Mold Contaminants (Fungal Competitors)

### Trichoderma (Forest Green Mold)
- The most aggressive and common contaminant in mushroom cultivation
- Rapidly spreads as a thick green carpet, overwhelming mushroom mycelium
- Spores are ubiquitous in soil and air
- Prevention: rigorous [[mushroom-sterile-technique]]; pasteurize substrates properly

### Aspergillus (Green Mold)
- Common airborne contaminant
- Green to yellow-green powdery colonies
- Some species produce [[mycotoxins]]
- Appears as colored growths in pastels to greens

### Penicillium (Bluish Green Mold)
- Very common contaminant on agar and grain
- Distinctive blue-green color with white edges
- Produces abundant spores that spread easily
- Once sporulating, do not open the culture in a flow hood (spreads spores)

### Neurospora (Pink Mold)
- Bright orange-pink, very fast growing
- Often appears on grain substrates
- Can spread rapidly through a growing area
- Immediate disposal required

### Mucor and Rhizopus (Black Pin Mold)
- Rapid growers with coarse, cottony mycelium
- Produce dark sporangia on stalks ("pin mold" appearance)
- Common on bread and grain

### Dactylium (Cobweb Mold)
- Fine, cobweb-like growth over mushroom surfaces
- Can overgrow fruiting bodies
- Often confused with mushroom mycelium but grows faster and more wispy

## Non-Contaminant Organisms

### Actinomyces and Streptomyces (Firefang)
- These are actually beneficial in compost -- they produce the characteristic "sweet earthy" smell of good compost
- Appear as white, gray, or tan powdery growth in compost
- Indicator of proper [[composting]], not a problem

## Insect Pests (from Stamets)

### Mushroom Flies
- **Sciarid flies**: Small dark flies whose larvae feed on mycelium and damage crops
- **Phorid flies**: Hump-backed flies; larvae damage mushroom tissue
- **Cecid flies**: Gall midges

**Control measures**: Screen vents and air intakes; yellow sticky traps; maintain clean growing areas; remove spent substrate promptly.

### Mites
- Microscopic arthropods that feed on mycelium
- Can vector mold spores and bacteria between cultures
- Often introduced via contaminated substrate or tools

### Nematodes (Eelworms)
- Microscopic worms that damage mycelium
- More common in compost-based systems
- Indicator of poor hygiene or contaminated water

## Virus (Die-Back Disease)
- Causes progressive loss of vigor and yield over successive flushes
- No visible signs on mycelium or mushrooms
- Spread via contaminated tools or infected spawn
- Prevention: use only clean cultures; sterilize tools between uses

## Contamination Prevention Summary

1. **Sterilize properly**: Follow recommended pressure cooking times; never cut corners
2. **Use control jars**: Test sterilization by leaving jars uninoculated
3. **Work clean**: Laminar flow hood, clean surfaces, washed hands
4. **Flame sterilize**: Between every tool use
5. **Monitor closely**: Check cultures daily for early signs of contamination
6. **Discard promptly**: Heavily contaminated cultures should be disposed of immediately
7. **Isolate if possible**: Light contamination on agar can sometimes be isolated away from
8. **Manage environment**: Proper FAE, humidity, and temperature reduce susceptibility
9. **Clean growing areas**: Remove spent substrates; sanitize chambers between grows
10. **Outdoor disposal**: Contaminated grain spawn can sometimes be salvaged by discarding in a garden (may produce mushrooms outdoors)

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Reference |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Colored growths in jar (pastels to black) | Mold contamination | Sterilize longer; check technique |
| Sour foul odor within 2 days of inoculation | Bacterial contamination (Bacillus) | Dry grain better; extend sterilization |
| No growth, no contamination | Dead spores/mycelium | Cool jars before inoculation; fresh cultures |
| Growth stops at certain point | Dried substrate | Increase water in formula |
| Fuzzy white growth on caps | Low humidity (not contamination) | Increase humidity |
