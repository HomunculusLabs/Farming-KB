# [[substrate-preparation]]

The substrate for each miniculture consisted of **rye grain** prepared in
standard half-pint mason jars. The specific formulation was:

- **10 grams of rye grain** per jar
- **15 mL of water** per jar

This 2:3 grain-to-water ratio (by volume) provided adequate moisture for
[[fruiting-initiation]]

Once fully colonized, the grain substrate was **cased** to initiate fruiting.
The casing layer consisted of a mixture of:

- **Peat moss**: Provides moisture retention and a non-nutritive surface for
  pin formation.
- **Calcium carbonate**: Buffers the pH of the casing layer to an appropriate
  range for fruiting (slightly acidic to neutral).
- **Perlite**: Improves aeration and drainage within the casing layer,
  preventing anaerobic conditions that could inhibit fruiting.

The casing layer is non-nutritive, meaning it does not serve as a food source
for the mycelium. Instead, it creates a microclimate of high humidity at the
substrate surface that triggers the formation of **primordia** (tiny pin-like
structures that develop into mature fruiting bodies).

## Maintenance and Watering

During the fruiting phase, the minicultures required regular maintenance to
sustain appropriate moisture levels:

- **Watering every 2 days** with **1 mL of sterile water** applied to each
  culture.
- The use of sterile water was essential to prevent bacterial or fungal
  contamination that could compromise the culture or the analytical results.
- This modest watering regimen maintained the high-humidity microclimate
  necessary for fruiting without oversaturating the substrate.

## Production Timeline and Yield

The miniculture method proved to be productive and sustained over extended
periods:

- **First flush** appeared at **4–5 weeks** post-inoculation, reflecting the
  28-day colonization period plus additional time for pin formation and
  maturation.
- **Total production period**: Cultures continued producing flushes for
  **20 or more weeks**, yielding up to six flushes per miniculture.
- **Average yield**: Approximately **2.7 grams dry weight** of mushroom tissue
  per miniculture across the entire production period.

While the total yield per miniculture was modest, it was sufficient for the
HPLC-based analytical methodology employed by Bigwood & Beug, which required
only small amounts of tissue for reliable quantification of both psilocybin
and psilocin.

## Strain Information

The primary strain used in the study was the **Amazon strain**, sourced from
Pucallpa, Peru. Bigwood & Beug also analyzed the **M.R. strain** and the
**Equadorian strain** in complementary experiments examining alkaloid
distribution between caps and stems. All strains were maintained on agar
culture media prior to inoculation of the rye grain substrate.

## Methodological Strengths and Limitations

**Strengths:**

- Multiple independent minicultures provided biological replication.
- The controlled environment reduced uncontrolled variables.
- Extended production allowed study of multiple flushes from the same culture.
- Small scale made the study practical for a laboratory setting.

**Limitations:**

- Only one strain (Amazon) was used for the flush variation study, so
  generalization to other strains requires caution.
- The rye grain substrate differs from natural substrates used in the wild.
- Small culture size may not fully represent larger-scale cultivation systems.
