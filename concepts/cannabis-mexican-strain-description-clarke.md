# Mexican Cannabis Strain Description (Clarke)

## Geographic Origin

Mexican Cannabis originates from 15 to 27 degrees north latitude, spanning Pacific coastal states from Sinaloa in the north (27 degrees) through Nayarit, Jalisco, Michoacan, Guerrero, and Oaxaca to Chiapas in the south (15 degrees). All states stretch from the coast into the mountains where Cannabis is grown.

## Historical Context

Mexico was long the major source of marijuana smoked in America until recent years. The U.S. government (with monetary backing) began intensive eradication through aerial spraying of herbicides such as Paraquat. High-quality Mexican Cannabis is now rarely available. Ironically, NIMH uses domestic Mexican [[sativa-landrace-strains-and-geographic-origins]]

## Morphological Characteristics

### Growth Form
- Tall, upright plants of moderate to large size
- Relatively early maturation compared to Colombian or Thai strains

### Leaves
- Light to dark green, large leaves
- Long, medium-width, moderately serrated leaflets
- Arranged in a circular array

### Regional Variations
- **Michoacan strains**: Very slender leaves, very high calyx-to-leaf ratio
- **Guerreran strains**: High calyx-to-leaf ratio, often short with long upright lower limbs
- **Oaxacan strains**: Broader-leafed, often leafier floral clusters; generally the largest and most vigorously growing
- **Michoacan strains**: Smaller and more delicate plants

### Seeds
- Fairly large, ovoid, and slightly flattened
- Light colored grey or brown, unmottled perianth (in traditional varieties)
- Smaller, darker, more mottled seeds have appeared recently, possibly indicating hybridization with Colombian seed

## Maturation Characteristics

- Mexican strains mature relatively early compared to strains from Colombia or Thailand
- Produce many long floral clusters with a high calyx-to-leaf ratio
- Highly cerebral psychoactivity
- Early maturation is extremely advantageous for growers in areas of late spring and early fall freezes

## Hybridization and Genetic Changes

- Many of the hybrid strains grown domestically today originated in the mountains of Mexico
- No commercial seeded Cannabis crops are free from hybridization
- Large amounts of hybrid domestic seed have been introduced into Mexico
- It is not uncommon to find Thai and Afghani phenotypes in recent shipments of Cannabis from Mexico
- Hybridization may be taking place in Mexico with introduced seed from Colombia (the world's largest seed source)

## Psychoactive Profile

- Highly cerebral psychoactivity
- The combination of early maturation and high psychoactivity makes Mexican genetics valuable for breeding
- The cerebral quality distinguishes Mexican strains from more sedative varieties
