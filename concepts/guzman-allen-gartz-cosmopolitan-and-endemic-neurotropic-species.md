## Cosmopolitan and Endemic Neurotropic Species

The [[guzman-panaeolina-foenisecii-psilocybin-controversy-conflicting-chemical-analyses]] and almost all Panaeolus species are cosmopolitan.
P. foenisecii is common worldwide but poorly documented in some regions; it
is not recorded from Central America and the Caribbean, and in Japan was
reported only by Hongo (1986) despite being very common there. Claviceps
purpurea and allies are also broadly distributed in temperate regions.
The cosmopolitan distribution of these coprophilous and parasitic fungi
reflects the global distribution of their hosts (herbivorous mammals and
grasses, respectively).

## Arctic and Alpine Species

Psilocybe aztecorum var. aztecorum is exclusively subalpine and alpine,
growing in the high mountains of Mexico at elevations up to 4,000 m. It is
related to P. baeocystis from the Pacific Northwest and P. quebecensis from
Quebec, Canada, all belonging to Section Aztecorum. Panaeolus moellerianus
and P. olivaceus from the Faroe Islands represent northern species adapted
to cold, maritime environments.
