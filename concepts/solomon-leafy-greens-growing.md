## Kale and Brussels Sprouts

Kale is among the hardiest of leafy greens, surviving well below freezing (though not frozen soil). Frost improves flavor -- this applies to kale, Brussels sprout sprouts, and many other leafy crops when they experience chilling without being killed.

Brussels sprouts are a high-demand crop requiring rich soil. They produce small cabbage-like heads along a tall stem. Harvest from the bottom up after the first few frosts (frost sweetens the sprouts). The plants can stand in the garden through much of winter; in mild climates, harvest continues for months.

## Chicories: Endive, Escarole, Radicchio

Remarkably cold-hardy [[query-how-do-i-grow-figs-in-cold-climates]], chicories survive if the soil does not freeze solid; in mild-winter climates, they are cool-season crops that grow through autumn and winter.

Chicories may be considered low-demand vegetables that grow faster and bigger with more fertility, but eating quality remains similar regardless. If growing on poorer soil, sow a bit earlier.

Growing details:
- Sow so row starts at ~1 seedling per inch
- Thin progressively
- Root systems resemble other biennials (carrot, beetroot), penetrating subsoil for moisture
- Capable of surviving drought and producing greens afterward
- Varieties: productive, uniformly heading radicchio hybrids have appeared; some hybrid endive types are also available

Harvest: chicory roots can be dug before hard frost, stored in [[stamets-cultivator-agaricus-bitorquis-warm-weather-cultivation]], a sowing remains in prime condition only a few days
- Plant several varieties of differing maturities at the same time for extended harvest

### Mesclun

Growing densely spaced salad greens and cutting repeatedly (like mowing a lawn) is popular but Solomon believes it produces second-rate salads after the first cutting. He prefers growing individual heads to full size.

### Seed Saving

Lettuce is a self-pollinated annual. Seeds ripen irregularly and shatter easily. Cut branches as they mature and dry. Mild crosses occur occasionally (about 1 in 1,000 plants) -- these may produce interesting new varieties. In mild winters, sow seed crop in mid-autumn; in harsh winters, start earliest possible in spring.

## Arugula

A near-wild leafy annual in the cabbage family. Peppers up salads; delicious as a standalone salad when young. In hot weather, leaves go from mild to bitter in under two weeks. Sow every 3 weeks alongside lettuce. Direct seed by sprinkling thinly into a furrow; thin progressively.
