## Bioregional Resource Index — Food Systems

The Bioregional Resource Index is a comprehensive inventory system developed by Bill Mollison and Maxine Cole for the Northern Rivers Bioregional Association of New South Wales, Australia. It provides a structured framework for cataloguing all practical resources essential to the functioning of a small region, organised to assist conservation of resources, regional cash flow, settlement survival, employment, and community security.

## Overview of the Resource Index

The index is organised into primary categories covering the full spectrum of regional needs:

- **A**: Food and food support systems
- **B**: Shelter and buildings
- **C**: Livelihoods and support services
- **D**: Information, media, communication, and research
- **E**: Community and security
- **F**: Social life
- **G**: Health services
- **H**: Future trends and potential
- **I**: Transport services
- **J**: Appendices (maps, publications of the bioregion)

Each section can contain case histories of successful strategies. The system is designed to be built topic by topic, sold initially by the page, and finally assembled as a loose-leaf notebook. Volunteers enter local resource centres and addresses under each category, with the system best suited to computer retrieval.

## Category A: Food and Food Support Systems

**Criteria**: Native and economic species, organic and biocide-free products of good nutritional value.

### A1: Plant Resources

The plant resources subcategory inventories the full chain from propagation to production:

- **Nurseries and propagation centres**: tissue culture, sources of inoculants, mycorrhiza
- **Plant collections and botanical gardens**: economic plant assemblies, aquatics
- **Research institutes**: horticultural and pastoral agencies
- **Seed sources and seed exchanges**: critical for regional food sovereignty
- **Native species reserves and nurseries**: preserving local germplasm
- **Demonstration farms and gardens**: teaching centres and workshop conveners
- **Government departments**: resources and regulations affecting plant systems
- **Voluntary agencies**: involved in plant protection, planting, and propagation
- **Skilled people**: botanists and horticulturists available in the region
- **Publications and information**: leaflets, reference books, libraries, posters
- **Contractors and consultants**: implementation of plant systems, farm designs
- **Produce**: products and producers in the region, local growers
- **Checklists**: vegetables, fruits, and nuts growable in the region, plus non-food species

### A2: Animal Resources

The animal resources subcategory mirrors the plant section for livestock and aquatic systems:

- **Breeders and propagation centres**: stud services, artificial insemination, hatcheries
- **Species collections**: including worms and other invertebrates
- **Fish breeders and aquatic species**: aquaculture resources
- **Useful native species**: collections and reserves with cultivation potential
- **Demonstration farms**: free range systems, bee culture, teaching centres
- **Government departments**: resources and regulations for animal systems
- **Voluntary agencies**: animal protection societies
- **Skilled people**: farriers, veterinarians, natural historians
- **Contractors**: shearers, consultants, farm designers
- **Publications**: posters, libraries for the region
- **Produce**: species and suppliers in the region

### A3: Integrated Pest Management

A dedicated subcategory for biological and safe pest control:

- **Insectaries and predator breeders**: suppliers of biological controls
- **Safe control chemicals**: traps and non-toxic interventions
- **[[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-internet-information-sources]]**: [[integrated-pest-management]] (IPM) resources
- **Stored grain management**: [[query-what-is-integrated-pest-management-for-organic-gardens]] stored foods
- **References and libraries**: IPM documentation
- **Checklists**: common pests and predators, safe pest control procedures

### A4: Food Processing

Processing adds value and extends the utility of regional food production:

- **Processing equipment**: suppliers of local-scale food processing tools
- **Food Processing Centres (FPCs)**: shared community processing facilities
- **Information sources**: food processing and preservation knowledge
- **Fermentation materials**: sources of yeasts, bacterial and algal ferment cultures
- **Processed-product producers**: value-added goods made in the region

### A5: Markets and Outlets

Connecting producers to consumers through diverse channels:

- **Local markets**: farmers' markets and community markets
- **Delivery services**: bringing food from farm to consumer
- **Export markets and wholesalers**: regional products reaching broader markets
- **Urban-rural co-op systems**: direct marketing arrangements
- **Retail outlets**: shops carrying local produce
- **Market advisory**: skills, groups, contract and legal expertise
- **Roadside and self-pick sales**: farm-gate commerce
- **Packaging**: market packaging, package suppliers, ethical packaging systems and designs
- **Annual barter fair**: community exchange events

### A6: Support Functions

The infrastructure supporting food production systems:

- **Residue testing**: biocide residue testing, nutrient and vitamin content analysis (food [[blesching-cannabis-contaminants-testing-quality-control]])
- **Soil, water, and leaf analysis**: micronutrient and soil additive testing, water analysis, pH levels
- **Hydrological services**: dams, domestic water supply, design and implementation
- **Fencing and trellis**: suppliers and services, cattle grids and gates
- **Natural fertilisers**: mulch materials, trace elements, soil amendments
- **Farm machinery**: garden and domestic tool suppliers, appropriate and tested equipment, fabricators and designers, repair services, hire and contract services
- **Land planning services**: professional design for agricultural systems
- **Protected growing**: glasshouses, shadehouses, food dryers, suppliers, appropriate materials
- **Geological resources**: lime quarries, stone dusts, local trace [[mineral-sources-for-soils]]

## Significance for Permaculture Design

The resource index serves as a practical tool for bioregional self-reliance assessment. By cataloguing existing resources across all food system categories, a bioregional group can identify gaps, prevent resource leakage, and develop strategies for local production of necessities. The emphasis on organic, biocide-free production, native species preservation, [[orchard-plant-guilds-and-integrated-pest-management]] management reflects permaculture's core ethics of earth care and ecological health.

The index format — topic-by-topic assembly into a loose-leaf notebook with computer-retrieval suitability — makes it a living document that evolves with the bioregion's capacity and needs.
