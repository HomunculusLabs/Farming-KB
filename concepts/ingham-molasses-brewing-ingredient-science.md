**Source:** The Field Guide I for [[ingham-blackstrap-molasses-compost-tea-concentration-osmotic-effects]] (the standard recommendation for AACT) contains:
- **Sugars** (primarily sucrose, glucose, fructose): 40-55% — the primary
  microbial food source
- **Minerals**: significant calcium, magnesium, iron, potassium, and
  [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]]
- Widely available at feed stores and health food stores
- The standard choice for most AACT recipes

### Fancy or Light Molasses (Not Recommended)
First or second extraction with higher sugar but lower mineral content. The
high simple sugar without buffering minerals can cause overly rapid bacterial
growth that depletes oxygen, potentially shifting the brew anaerobic.

### Sulfured vs. Unsulfured
Always use unsulfured molasses. Sulfur dioxide is added to some molasses
as a preservative and color stabilizer, but it acts as an antimicrobial
agent that inhibits the beneficial organisms the tea is trying to grow.
Sulfured molasses produces teas with lower organism diversity and biomass.

## Application Rates

### Standard Bacterial-Dominant Tea
- 1 tablespoon (15 ml) unsulfured blackstrap molasses per 5 gallons
  (19 liters) of tea
- Added at the beginning of the brew
- Provides sufficient food for 24-36 hours of aerobic microbial growth

### Fungal-Dominant Tea
- Same molasses rate (1 Tbsp per 5 gallons)
- Combined with additional fungal foods: kelp, fish hydrolysate, and humic
  acids (1 Tbsp each)
- Diverse food sources favor fungal growth while maintaining bacteria

### Higher Rates
Some recipes use up to 2-3 tablespoons per 5 gallons for heavily inoculated
teas. However, excessive molasses can cause problems: oxygen depletion as
bacteria rapidly consume sugars, foam production, and selection for
opportunistic bacteria rather than a balanced community.

## Timing of Addition

### Single Addition (Standard)
Add molasses at the beginning of the brew. This is the simplest and most
common approach. The organisms have continuous access to food throughout the
brewing period, producing steady growth.

### Split Addition (Advanced)
Some brewers add half the molasses at the start and half at the midpoint
(12-18 hours). This can maintain more consistent [[aact-compost-quality-standards-ingham]] (adequate starting organisms?)
- Ensure aeration is functioning properly

### Clumping or Settling
Molasses can be viscous and may clump if added to cold water. Dissolve
molasses in a small amount of warm water before adding to the brew tank.
Stir or agitate briefly after addition to ensure even distribution.

## Storage

- Store in a cool, dry place with the container tightly sealed
- Properly stored, blackstrap molasses lasts indefinitely
- Crystallization is normal — warm gently to re-dissolve
