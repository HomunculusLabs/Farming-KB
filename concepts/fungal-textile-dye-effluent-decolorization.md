## Fungal Degradation of Textile Dyes and Industrial Effluents

[[ligninolytic-enzyme-systems-white-rot-fungi]] offer a promising biological approach for
dye decolorization and [[textile-dye-bioremediation]] as part of the broader field of [[constructed-wetland-design]]
- In mammals, azo dyes are reduced to carcinogenic aromatics, then
  oxidized to N-hydroxy derivatives that form DNA-damaging electrophiles
- Dyes are used in textile dyeing, paper printing, colour photography,
  and petroleum products

## Phanerochaete chrysosporium Dye Degradation

*P. chrysosporium* has been extensively studied for dye decolorization:

- **Azo dyes degraded**: Congo Red, Orange II, Tropaeolin, Disperse
  Yellow 3 (DY3), Disperse Orange 3, Solvent Yellow 14,
  4-phenylazophenol, and others
- Crude LiP decolorized all dyes tested except Congo Red, suggesting
  other enzymes are involved
- DY3 (carcinogenic azo dye) oxidized by LiPs and MnPs to yield
  4-methyl-1,2-benzoquinone, acetanilide, and a dimer
- Ring carbons were extensively degraded under nitrogen-limiting
  conditions (radiolabelled studies)
- Some dyes degraded under nitrogen-sufficient, non-ligninolytic
  conditions, indicating non-LDS enzymes

## Pleurotus ostreatus Dye Decolorization

- Solid-state fermentation on [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]]
- MnP from *B. adusta* oxidized dyes in an Mn(II)-independent manner
  (unusual)
- Reactive Violet 5, Reactive Black 5, Reactive Orange 96, Reactive
  Red 198, Reactive Blue 38, and Reactive Blue 15 all decolorized

## Marine Fungi for Dye Bioremediation

*Flavodon flavus*, a basidiomycete isolated from coastal marine
environment:

- Produces laccases, MnPs, and LiPs
- Efficiently degrades poly R, poly B, azure B, and RBBR
- Better degradation in the presence of salts (simulating marine
  environment)
- Suggests potential for bioremediation in marine environments

## Pulp and Paper Effluent Decolorization

Bleach plant effluents (BPEs) from pulp and paper industry contain
chlorophenols, chlorolignols, and other pollutants:

- **P. chrysosporium**: MnPs play the primary role in BPE
