# Fungi Associated with Aquatic Animals

## Overview

Fungi associated with aquatic animals include a diverse assemblage that ranges from primary pathogens to opportunistic secondary invaders of host animals under stress. They affect fish, crustaceans, mollusks, and aquatic invertebrates across marine, freshwater, and estuarine environments. As the aquaculture industry expands to include new and exotic species and moves into new geographic areas, concerns about aquatic animal mycoses and their causative species are increasing.

## Major Pathogen Groups

### Significant Pathogen Genera

Key genera causing disease in aquatic animals include:

- **Aspergillus**: Common in marine fish; causes systemic infections
- **Candida**: Yeast infections in various aquatic hosts
- **Fusarium**: Important pathogen of crustaceans and fish
- **Exophiala**: Dematiaceous fungi causing chromomycosis
- **Paecilomyces**: Opportunistic infections in immunocompromised hosts
- **Phoma**: Various aquatic animal infections
- **Ochroconis**: Causes phaeohyphomycosis in fish

### Zoosporic Pathogens

Major groups with swimming zoospores that actively seek hosts:

- **Aphanomyces**: Causes crayfish plague (A. astaci) and other diseases
- **Saprolegnia**: Common water mold infecting fish eggs and adult fish
- **Lagenidium**: Parasites of crustaceans and other invertebrates
- **Coelomomyces**: Mosquito-parasitic fungi with complex life cycles
- **Ichthyophonus**: Significant pathogen of marine fish; exhibits secular (18-30 year) patterns of occurrence

### Additional Pathogen Groups

- **Entomophthorales**: Pathogens of aquatic insects
- **Mucorales**: Various aquatic animal infections
- **Labyrinthomorpha**: Important molluscan parasites; Labyrinthuloides haliotidis causes significant mortality of juvenile abalone
- **Meiotic Ascomycetes**: Including Trichomaris invadens, implicated in massive dieoffs of Alaskan tanner crabs

## Disease Patterns

### Primary vs. Opportunistic Infections

- **Primary pathogens**: Cause infections in otherwise healthy hosts
- **Opportunistic pathogens**: Secondary invaders of hosts stressed by other infections, poor water quality, or environmental conditions

### Geographic Distribution

- Well-studied mycopathogens (Aphanomyces, Coelomomyces, Lagenidium, Ichthyophonus, Saprolegnia) are cosmopolitan
- Despite wide host ranges, epidemiological data indicate restricted and patchy distributions within host ranges
- Patchy infection patterns may reflect gene-based differences in host susceptibility
- Patterns may also result from climatic and biotic factors differing among locations

### Temporal Patterns

- Seasonal variation in number of infected hosts (most zoosporic pathogens)
- Recruitment of Ichthyophonus hoferi synchronized seasonally, possibly related to spring water temperature warming
- Collection timing should be carefully planned to maximize probability of recovering infected animals

## Sampling and Collection

### Sample Requirements

- Minimum sample should provide 95% confidence that infected specimens are included
- Tables available for minimum sample sizes to detect pathogens at prevalence of 2%, 5%, or 10%
- Samples should be randomly selected from randomly selected sites
- Aquatic insects/microinvertebrates (100 specimens/week/site) should be reared in Petri plates for 1-6 weeks to allow latent infections to appear

### Collection Challenges

- Disease surveys hampered by limitations and selectivity of sampling gear
- Use of variety of sampling techniques or consistent single method recommended
- Most infected specimens collected as moribund or clinically diseased individuals
- Often presented as case reports rather than systematic surveys
- Collection of infected animals often prompted by need for accurate diagnosis

### Specimen Handling

- Specimens should be alive when collected and processed as soon as possible
- Process before death or as soon after death as possible to avoid:
  - Degenerative postmortem tissue changes
  - Artifact formation
  - Contamination from air- and water-borne microorganisms
  - Overgrowth by normal commensal biota
- If not examined within 12-24 hours: refrigerate or keep on ice
- If not chilled within 24 hours: preserve in 10% buffered formalin
- Freezing is least preferred (limits subsequent study options)
- Diseased tissue samples (1-5 mm3) for light/electron microscopy
- Transport under aseptic conditions; use sterilized plastic bags

### Diagnostic Examination

Essential case record data:
1. Common and scientific names of specimen
2. Collection date
3. Collection site with geographic coordinates and elevation
4. Collector's name and collection number
5. Collection methods used
6. Specimen length, width, weight, sex

Diagnostic steps:
- Record external disease signs; photograph with color film
- Prepare smears and squashes of lesion material
- Inspect internal structures using aseptic techniques
- Collect tissue samples for light and electron microscopy
- Calculate condition indices (CI = weight/length^3 x 100)

## Understudied Areas

Many regions and host groups remain poorly studied:

- **Coral reef animals**: Remarkably little studied
- **Tropical rainforest aquatic animals**: Fungal associations hardly known
- **Trichomycetes**: Fungi in guts of aquatic arthropods are hardly known
- **Annelids, nematodes, branchiurans, tunicates, ascidians**: Mycology of these aquatic invertebrates remains in its infancy

## Concerns

- Expansion of aquaculture into new geographic areas and with new species increases risk
- Many new species likely to be uncovered as mycotic investigations intensify
- Need for standardized survey protocols across regions
- Climate change may alter pathogen distributions and host susceptibility
