## Nitrogen Fixation and Soil Building

The nitrogen-fixing ability of alders rivals that of many leguminous
trees. Wide-spaced planting of Alnus spp as timber has been known to
increase fodder production several fold through nitrogen fixation and
shelter. Inoculation of new soils will be necessary to ensure nitrogen
fixation, as the specific Frankia bacteria may not be present in all
locations. Once established, alders act as living fertiliser factories,
continuously enriching surrounding soil.

## Timber Uses

Alder timber, classified as softwood, is moderately soft, moderately
light, and moderately dense with a fine, even texture. It is easy to
work, does not tend to warp much during seasoning, and glues easily.
Specific uses include:

- Bridge construction and pilings
- Furniture and panelling
- Coffins, crates, and plywood
- Pulp for paper manufacture (provides smoothness and softness to
  tissue, bond, envelope, and book papers)
- Clogs, turnery, chair making, and cart building
- Carving, bowls, spoons, and wooden heels

### Alnus cordata (Italian Alder)
A large tree to 27 metres with a conical shape. Grows rapidly on most
sites including chalk soils and dry sites. Can reach 15 metres in 20
years. An excellent choice for |windbreaks
on [[tagasaste-nurse-crop-fodder-systems]]. The tree
coppices well and makes good charcoal.
