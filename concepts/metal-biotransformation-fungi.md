# Metal Biotransformation by Fungi

Updated: 2026-04-18 Fungi possess remarkable abilities to transform metals and
metalloids through diverse mechanisms of mobilization, immobilization,
accumulation, and precipitation. These activities have profound implications for
[[gadd-mycogenic-mineral-precipitation-secondary-minerals]]
- Reduction and oxidation of metal species

Fungi can be highly efficient bioaccumulators of soluble and particulate forms of
metals (Ni, Zn, Ag, Cu, Cd, Pb), especially from dilute external concentrations
(Gadd, 1993, 2000). Metal binding can be an important passive process in both
living and dead fungal biomass.

## Mechanisms of Metal Solubilization

Three synergistic mechanisms operate for mineral dissolution:

1. **Acidolysis**: Fungi acidify their microenvironment through proton
   excretion, organic acid excretion, and formation of carbonic acid from
   respiratory CO2. Acidolysis was the main mechanism of fungal dissolution of
   toxic metal phosphates when ammonium was the nitrogen source (Fomina et al.,
   2004)

2. **Complexolysis**: Metal-complexing metabolites include carboxylic acids
   (oxalic, citric, malic, succinic, formic acids), amino acids, siderophores,
   and [[fungal-role-in-carbon-cycling]], and landscape evolution. Their filamentous
  growth habit, ability to translocate nutrients within
  fungal-mycelial-foraging-resource-heterogeneity, and production of aggressive
  biochemical metabolites make them particularly effective at transforming
  geological substrates.

## Overview

Fungi have been components of microbial communities of terrestrial environments
including deserts, metal-rich and hypersaline habitats, since the Ordovician
period (480-460 MYBP) (Heckman et al., 2001; Burford et al., 2003). The fungal
explorative growth habit and high surface-area-to-mass ratio, together with
[[dighton-fungal-nutrient-translocation-element-redistribution]] ability and
hydrated mucilaginous sheaths, ensure that fungal processes are an integral
component of biogeochemical change (Gadd, 2004, 2006).

Fungal bioweathering of basaltic outcrops in sub-polar areas is believed to be
chronologically the first process of weathering, followed by cryogenic processes
(Etienne and Dupont, 2002). Fungi can dissolve minerals and mobilize metals at
higher pH values and over a wider redox range more efficiently than bacteria
(Gu et al., 1998; Castro et al., 2000; Burford et al., 2003).

## Rock-Dwelling Fungal Communities

Rock-dwelling microorganisms are classified as epilithic (on surfaces),
hypolithic (associated with pebbles), endolithic (inhabiting rock sub-surface),
chasmolithic (growing in cracks), cryptoendolithic (in cavities among crystal
grains), and euendolithic (actively boring into rock) (May, 2003). Diverse
communities have been found from Antarctic dry valleys to hot deserts and rock
substrates kilometers below the Earth's surface. Fungi colonize many rock types:
limestone, marble, granite, sandstone, basalt, gneiss, and quartz. Alkaline
rocks are more susceptible to fungal attack than acidic rocks (Eckhardt, 1985).
