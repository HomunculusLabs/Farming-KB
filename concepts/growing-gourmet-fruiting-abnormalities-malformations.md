## Common Abnormalities and Their Causes

### Coral Formation in Oyster Mushrooms

One of the most distinctive malformations is "coral" formation in
Oyster mushrooms ([[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]] period fails to
exceed 2400 lux-hours per day (equivalent to 100 lux of continuous
lighting), P. ostreatus is triggered into producing abnormal, coral-
like structures instead of normal mushrooms. This is caused by
insufficient light and can be remedied by increasing illumination to
appropriate levels.

### Long Stems and Small Caps (Etiolation)

Perhaps the most universal fruiting abnormality, elongated stems with
underdeveloped caps occur from two primary causes:

1. **High carbon dioxide levels:** When CO2 concentrations remain
   elevated during fruiting, mushrooms respond by producing long
   stems, attempting to elevate the cap above ground level where
   spores can be dispersed. This response is identical whether
   caused by high CO2 or insufficient light.
