# Psilocybin to Psilocin Ratio Dynamics Across Flushes

## Overview

One of the most striking findings from Bigwood and Beug's 1982
cultivation study of *[[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]* was the dramatic shift in the
psilocybin-to-psilocin ratio across [[psilocin-biosynthetic-delay-hypothesis-successive-flushes]]. In early
flushes, psilocin was nearly absent; by [[psilocin-emergence-in-later-flushes]], psilocin
content rose significantly while psilocybin fluctuated. This ratio
dynamic has implications for understanding biosynthesis, enzymatic
conversion, and dosing accuracy in both clinical and recreational
contexts.

## The Ratio Pattern

### First and Second Flushes

In the controlled miniculture experiments, first-flush mushrooms
showed:

- **High psilocybin content**: Ranging from approximately 3–8 mg/g dry
  weight depending on culture
- **Near-zero psilocin**: First-flush specimens contained 0 mg/g or
  trace amounts of psilocin
- **Ratio**: Effectively infinite or undefined (psilocybin-dominant)

The second flush maintained this pattern with still-low psilocin
levels, though some specimens began showing detectable psilocin
concentrations.

### Third and Fourth Flushes

By the third and fourth flushes, a clear shift occurred:

- **Psilocybin decreased** in some cultures, though it remained the
  dominant alkaloid
- **Psilocin increased substantially**, reaching concentrations of
  1–2 mg/g in some specimens
- **Ratio shifted** toward a more balanced psilocybin:psilocin profile

The fourth flush consistently showed the highest psilocin levels,
suggesting a progressive enzymatic conversion or accumulation pattern.

## Biosynthetic Interpretation

### Psilocybin as the Precursor

Psilocin is the dephosphorylated, active form of psilocybin. The
phosphorylation of psilocin to produce psilocybin is believed to be a
storage or transport mechanism within the fungal organism:

- **Phosphorylation** (psilocin → psilocybin): May stabilize the
  molecule for storage within [[hplc-analysis-psilocybin-psilocin-mushroom-tissue]]
- **Dephosphorylation** (psilocybin → psilocin): Catalyzed by alkaline
  phosphatase enzymes, particularly upon drying, heating, or exposure
  to basic conditions

The flush-dependent ratio shift suggests that younger mushrooms invest
more in phosphorylated (storage) form, while older flushes accumulate
more dephosphorylated (active) form.

### Enzymatic Activity Hypothesis

The progressive increase in psilocin across flushes could reflect:

- **Accumulating [[mycorrhizal-phosphatase-activity-elf97-detection]]** in the mycelial substrate as
  cultures age, progressively dephosphorylating stored psilocybin
- **Shift in metabolic priorities**: Early flushes prioritize rapid
  alkaloid synthesis for defense; later flushes may have different
  [[mycelial-foraging-resource-allocation]]
- **Substrate depletion**: As [[miniculture-rye-grain-cultivation-method-for-psilocybe-cubensis]] nutrients are consumed, the
  biochemical environment may shift toward conditions favoring
  dephosphorylation

## Street Sample Ratios

The five street samples analyzed by Bigwood and Beug showed highly
variable ratios:

- Some samples showed **high psilocybin, moderate psilocin** —
  consistent with later-flush harvested material
- Other samples contained **very low psilocin** relative to psilocybin
  — possibly first-flush material or samples dried at low
  temperatures
