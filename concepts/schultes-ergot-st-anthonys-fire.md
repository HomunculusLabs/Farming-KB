# Ergot and St. Anthony's Fire

Ergot (*[[eleusinian-mysteries]] Connection

It has been convincingly argued that ergot played a role in the
[[mckenna-food-of-the-gods-kykeon-and-eleusinian-mysteries]] of ancient Greece, the most important religious
initiation ceremony of the ancient Hellenic world. The mysteries at
Eleusis, conducted for nearly two thousand years, may have involved
the controlled use of ergot-containing preparations to induce visionary
experiences among initiates. The goddess Demeter and her daughter
Persephone were central to these rites, which promised revelations about
the afterlife to those who underwent the secret ceremonies.

## Chemical Constituents and the Discovery of LSD

The pharmacologically active constituents of ergot are ergoline
alkaloids, mainly derivatives of [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]]. These alkaloids form
the basis of important medicines used today in obstetrics, internal
medicine, and psychiatry. The most potent hallucinogen known, lysergic
acid diethylamide (LSD), is a semisynthetic derivative of ergot.

gordon-wasson first synthesized LSD in 1938 while studying ergot
alkaloids at Sandoz Laboratories in Switzerland. He accidentally
discovered its extraordinary psychoactive properties in 1943. This
discovery, born from research on a humble rye parasite, would transform
modern understanding of consciousness, brain chemistry, and the nature
of perception itself.

## Significance in Ethnobotany

Ergot occupies a unique place in ethnobotany as a fungus rather than a
plant. Its accidental psychoactive effects shaped European history,
inspired religious speculation about ancient mysteries, and ultimately
led to the synthesis of LSD. The ergot alkaloids demonstrate how natural
substances, even those never intentionally used by human cultures, can
open new frontiers in pharmacology and the study of [[weil-the-natural-mind-altered-states-normalcy]].

The story of ergot also illustrates the thin line between poison and
medicine, between accidental intoxication and deliberate visionary
practice. While no culture is known to have purposefully cultivated
ergot for its psychoactive properties, its impact on human history is
unquestionable, from medieval plague to modern psychopharmacology.

## Dosage and Toxicity

Dosage is extremely difficult to determine and can be dangerous. Ergot
alkaloids in uncontrolled quantities are highly toxic, and ingestion of
ergot-contaminated grain has historically caused death. The line between
a psychoactive dose and a lethal one is very narrow, which explains why
no culture appears to have developed a safe, intentional use of ergot
as a hallucinogen despite its potent psychoactive properties.

## Modern Applications

Ergot derivatives continue to be important in modern medicine. Ergot
alkaloids and their synthetic derivatives are used in the treatment of
migraine headaches, postpartum hemorrhage, and Parkinson's disease.
The discovery that LSD could model psychotic states in otherwise
healthy individuals revolutionized psychiatric research and contributed
to the development of serotonin receptor theory in neuroscience.
