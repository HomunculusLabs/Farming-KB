# Cannabis for Skin Conditions

The skin is richly endowed with components of the endocannabinoid system. Both CB1 and CB2 receptors are found throughout the skin, in epidermal keratinocytes, hair follicles, and sebaceous glands. This widespread distribution provides a strong anatomical basis for cannabis-based dermatological treatments, particularly via topical administration.

## Endocannabinoid System in Skin

The skin's endocannabinoid system plays crucial roles in:
- **Cell growth and differentiation**: Regulating skin cell turnover
- **Immune function**: Modulating inflammatory responses in skin
- **Sebaceous gland activity**: Controlling [[blesching-cannabis-hirsutism-unwanted-hair-growth]] patterns

## Psoriasis

Psoriasis is an autoimmune condition characterized by rapid skin cell proliferation and chronic inflammation. The endocannabinoid system's role in regulating skin cell growth makes cannabinoids a logical therapeutic approach.

### Mechanism
- CB1 and CB2 receptors in epidermal keratinocytes modulate cell proliferation
- Cannabinoids may slow the excessive skin cell turnover that causes psoriatic plaques
- Anti-inflammatory properties address the inflammatory component

### Topical Application
Topical cannabis preparations are particularly well-suited for psoriasis because:
- Direct delivery to affected skin areas
- Minimal systemic absorption and psychoactive effects
- Combination of cannabinoids and terpenes provides anti-inflammatory and anti-proliferative effects
- Can be applied as creams, salves, oils, or balms

## Dermatitis and Eczema

Inflammatory skin conditions like dermatitis and eczema involve immune-mediated inflammation in the skin. Cannabinoids' anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties through CB2 receptors in skin tissue may help reduce inflammation, itching, and skin barrier dysfunction.

### CBD for Skin Inflammation
CBD has demonstrated particular promise for skin conditions due to:
- Strong anti-inflammatory properties without psychoactive effects
- Ability to reduce cytokine production in skin cells
- Sebum-regulating properties (relevant for acne-associated dermatitis)
- Good [[blesching-cannabis-apoptosis-and-cancer-cell-death]]) in abnormal skin cells
- May help manage symptoms of skin cancer treatments (pain, inflammation)

This remains pre-clinical research and cannabis should not replace conventional skin cancer treatment.

## Painful Skin Conditions

Conditions involving skin pain (post-herpetic neuralgia, diabetic neuropathy affecting skin, complex regional pain syndrome) may benefit from topical cannabinoids:
- CB1 receptors mediate analgesic effects
- Local application minimizes systemic side effects
- Can be combined with other analgesic agents

## Wound Healing

Emerging research suggests cannabinoids may influence wound healing:
- Anti-inflammatory properties reduce excessive scar tissue formation
- Potential to modulate collagen production
- May reduce pain during healing process

## Topical Cannabis Products

### Types of Topical Preparations
- **Balms and salves**: Oil-based, good for localized pain and inflammation
- **Creams and lotions**: Water-based, easier to spread over large areas
- **Oils and tinctures**: Can be applied directly to skin or added to baths
- **Patches**: Transdermal delivery for sustained release
- **[[kava-traditional-preparation-ceremonial-context]] using fresh plant material

### Key Considerations for Topical Use
- Topicals generally do not produce psychoactive effects because cannabinoids do not enter the bloodstream in significant quantities through the skin
- The addition of terpenes may enhance penetration and provide additional therapeutic benefits
