# Mushroom Pinning and Primordia Formation Strategies

Chapter IX of The Mushroom Cultivator provides a systematic framework for
triggering [[stamets-casing-layer-design]] application, the
casing is conditioned for even mycelial growth. Key requirements:

1.  The casing layer is at optimum moisture capacity.
2.  The casing surface is rough and porous — minute mountains and
    valleys provide more surface area for pinhead formation.
3.  Relative humidity of the growing room is 95%.
4.  The substrate is incubated in total darkness.

Water moves by capillary action to the casing surface where it evaporates.
This constant movement slowly depletes moisture, so high room humidity
(95%) is essential. Lower humidities must be accompanied by light but
regular waterings.

## Stage II: Environmental Transition (1-2 Days)

Begin when mycelium reaches the valleys of the casing surface. Within
this critical period:

1.  **Lower temperature** from spawn running optimum to the fruiting
    range — accomplished by ventilation with large volumes of cool fresh
    air. This temperature "shock" effectively slows vegetative growth and
    signals the mycelium to fruit.
2.  **Maintain 95% humidity.**
3.  **Reduce CO2** by introducing fresh air. For *Agaricus
    brunnescens*, CO2 must fall below 2000 ppm. Higher levels cause
    overlay — a dense mycelial mat that makes the casing impervious to
    water.
4.  **Introduce light** on a 12-hour on/off cycle.

Timing varies by strain: some cultivators initiate when 20% of valleys
show mycelium, others wait until 90%. Normally within 12-48 hours of
first visible mycelium in valleys, initiation begins.

## Stage III: Primordia Formation — Knotting

Once temperatures are lowered and CO2 reduced, primordia begin to form.
Maintain:

1.  Constant fresh air supply — CO2 below 1000 ppm.
2.  Constant temperature within the fruiting range.
3.  Relative humidity of 95%.
4.  12-hour on/off light cycle.

Pinhead initials form in the humid valleys as small knots of mycelium.
Within five days these enlarge into buttons that differentiate into
mushrooms. Fresh air supply can be moderated to minimum levels since
CO2 evolution drops with slowed mycelial growth. Oversupply of fresh air
leads to excessive drying. Humidity must never fall below 90%.

## Stage IV: Pinhead Development

After pinheads reach pea size (3-5 mm):

1.  Hold air temperature constant within fruiting range.
2.  **Lower relative humidity to 85-92%** — increasing evaporation rate,
    essential for pinhead maturation.
3.  Fresh air with CO2 below 2000 ppm.
4.  12-hour on/off light cycle.

If humidity remains too high, development is retarded. The easiest way
to reduce humidity is to raise air temperature 1-2°F or increase air
movement. A slightly higher CO2 level (1500-2000 ppm) is now desirable.

Research by Lambert (1938) showed that a 2mm *Agaricus brunnescens*
pinhead matures in: 22 days at 50°F, 10 days at 60°F, or 6 days at
70°F. Although faster at 70°F, overall yields diminish. Optimum
cropping temperature for Agaricus is 62-64°F.

## Relationship Between Primordia and Yield

1.  Pinheads for the **first and second flush** are generated during the
    primordia formation period. Since 60-75% of total yield comes from
    these two flushes, the few days of pinhead initiation are the most
    critical in mushroom growing.
2.  More pins set = higher yield (if nutrients suffice), but individual
    mushrooms will be smaller.
3.  The substrate supports only a certain number of primordia per flush;
    excess pins may abort.
4.  Early-forming "volunteer" pins delay neighbors — removing them
    before development is advantageous.

## The Influence of Light

Photosensitive species (e.g., *Psilocybe cubensis*, *Pleurotus
ostreatus*) respond to specific wavelengths for pinhead initiation:

-   **Blue and near-UV light** — peaks at 370, 440, and 460 nanometers.
-   As little as **half a millisecond per day** of stimulation at these
    wavelengths causes primordia formation (Badham, 1979).
-   **Red, infrared, and green light** (>510 nm) are ineffective.

Red light is safe for checking cultures without triggering premature
pinning.
