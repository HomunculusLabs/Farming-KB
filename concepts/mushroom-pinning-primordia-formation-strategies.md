## Stage III: Primordia Formation — Knotting

Once temperatures are lowered and CO2 reduced, primordia begin to
form. Maintain constant fresh air supply with CO2 below 1000 ppm,
constant temperature within fruiting range, 95% relative humidity, and
12-hour on/off light cycle. Pinhead initials form in the humid valleys
as small knots of mycelium. Within five days these enlarge into buttons
that differentiate into mushrooms.

Fresh air supply can be moderated to minimum levels since CO2
evolution drops with slowed mycelial growth. Oversupply of fresh air
leads to excessive drying. Humidity must never fall below 90%.

## Stage IV: Pinhead Development

After pinheads reach pea size (3-5 mm):

1. Hold air temperature constant within fruiting range.
2. **Lower relative humidity to 85-92%**, increasing evaporation rate,
   essential for pinhead maturation.
3. Fresh air with CO2 below 2000 ppm.
4. 12-hour on/off light cycle.

Research by Lambert (1938) showed that a 2mm Agaricus brunnescens
pinhead matures in 22 days at 50 F, 10 days at 60 F, or 6 days at 70
F. Although faster at 70 F, overall yields diminish. Optimum cropping
temperature for Agaricus is 62-64 F.
