# Soil Microorganisms in Natural Farming

## Overview

Soil microorganisms are the invisible foundation [[cannabis-chemical-fertilizers]], Masanobu Fukuoka understood
soil as a **living ecosystem** whose biological health determines its
fertility. His entire farming system was designed not to feed plants directly
but to maintain the conditions under which soil microorganisms can sustain
plant growth naturally. He estimated that one gram of soil on his farm
contained about **100 million nitrogen-fixing bacteria** and other
soil-enriching microbes — a population he considered the true source of
agricultural productivity.

## The Living Soil

Soil is arguably the most complex biological system on Earth. A single
teaspoon of healthy soil contains:

- **Bacteria** (1–10 billion per gram): Including nitrogen fixers,
  decomposers, and pathogens. Dominant groups include Proteobacteria,
  Acidobacteria, and Actinobacteria.
- **Fungi** (100–1,000 meters of hyphae per gram): [[biological-nitrogen-fixation-agriculture]] is the primary natural pathway through which
atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) becomes available to plants. Fukuoka's farming
system relies heavily on two mechanisms of [[jadam-natural-nitrogen-fertilizer]]. The
nitrogen from rhizobial fixation is released gradually through
decomposition, matching the pace of crop uptake far better than the
pulse of soluble nitrogen from chemical application. It also builds soil
[[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] rather than depleting it.

### Free-Living Nitrogen Fixers

Fukuoka's estimate of 100 million nitrogen-fixing bacteria per gram of
soil refers primarily to **free-living (asymbiotic) nitrogen fixers**
such as *Azotobacter*, *Clostridium*, *Klebsiella*, and
cyanobacteria. These organisms fix nitrogen independently of any plant
host, contributing a continuous background flux of nitrogen to the soil.
While their per-organism contribution is smaller than that of rhizobia in
nodules, their sheer abundance means their collective contribution is
significant.

The conditions that favor free-living nitrogen fixers — adequate soil
moisture, organic carbon sources, near-neutral pH, and minimal
disturbance — are precisely the conditions maintained by natural farming
practices: permanent soil cover (straw mulch), living roots (continuous
crop rotation), no tillage, and no chemical inputs.
