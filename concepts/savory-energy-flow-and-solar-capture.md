# Energy Flow and Solar Capture

Energy flow is the fourth [[savory-ecosystem-foundation-blocks]] block in Holistic
[[savory-holistic-resource-management-animal-impact]]. It addresses the question of fuel that keeps all
[[fungal-ecosystem-processes-overview]] and life functioning. The natural living world runs
on [[holmgren-trees-solar-power-plants-detailed]], and management decisions can drastically affect how
much solar energy is captured and put to productive use. Understanding
energy flow is essential because it underpins the productivity of all
other [[fungi-in-ecosystem-processes-dighton]].

## Solar Energy as the Foundation

All living things, apart from a few rare organisms near thermal springs,
depend for food on the ability of green plants to capture the energy of
sunlight and convert it to usable form. Fossil fuels, though they
represent solar energy stored by green plants, are nonrenewable and
inedible. Photovoltaic, hydroelectric, wind, and tidal power convert
energy but not directly into edible forms.

America's current way of life is not sustainable because it demands an
energy flow that cannot be sustained. [[fukuoka-critique-modern-agriculture]] consumes about
9.8 calories of petrochemical energy to produce one calorie of sunlight
energy in the form of food. While machinery and petrochemicals may push
yields higher, they simultaneously distort successional communities,
mineral cycles, and water cycles so that costs rise with increasing
unsustainability.

## The Energy Pyramid

The flow of sunlight is traditionally represented as an energy pyramid.
Green plants form the base, converting a small portion of incoming
sunlight into food for their own growth and that of other organisms.
Above them, each level of consumers is smaller because energy is
expended in the living processes of the feeders.

At level two, animals that eat plants store less energy than the plants
they consumed. Level three predators that eat the level two animals
store less still. By level five, only scavengers and decay organisms
remain to extract the last useful energy from complicated organic
molecules assembled by the original green plants.

Humanity's position in the pyramid covers three possible levels. One
person can dine on all three in a single meal: potatoes represent level
one, grain-fed meat represents level two, and fish represents level
three.

## The Energy Tetrahedron

The discovery of the [[savory-four-missing-keys]] enabled Savory to see that the
old two-dimensional pyramid does not reveal the full possibilities for
managing energy flow. He reconceived the energy pyramid as two
tetrahedrons joined at their bases, one aboveground and one belowground.

This multidimensional view reveals that the crucial bottom level has
three dimensions: time, volume, and area. The right management can
increase the volume of energy stored at level one not only by raising
the volume of standing crop on a unit of ground, but also by lengthening
the time during which that crop can grow and by expanding the leaf area
of individual plants to capture more energy.

The more any of the three sides of the base can be extended, the more
energy humans can harvest at levels two, three, and four.

## Belowground Energy Flow

The energy pyramid also extends belowground where energy flow greatly
affects the health of the mineral cycle, water cycle, and succession.
All three require a biologically active soil community, which in turn
requires solar energy to be conveyed underground mainly by plant roots.
