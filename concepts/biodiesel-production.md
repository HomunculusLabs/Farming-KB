## Overview

Biodiesel is a renewable diesel fuel substitute made through transesterification of vegetable oil or animal fat. On a homestead scale, biodiesel can be produced from waste vegetable oil (WVO) collected from restaurants, providing fuel for diesel engines, generators, tractors, and heating equipment at a fraction of petroleum diesel cost.

## The Chemistry of Transesterification

Biodiesel production converts triglycerides (the main component of vegetable oils) into fatty acid methyl esters (FAME) — biodiesel — and glycerin as a byproduct.

The reaction:
- Vegetable oil (triglyceride) + [[methanol]] + Catalyst (sodium hydroxide or potassium hydroxide) = Biodiesel (FAME) + Glycerin

For every liter of oil, you need approximately:
- 200ml methanol (20% by volume)
- 3.5g sodium hydroxide (NaOH) or 5g potassium hydroxide (KOH) — adjusted by titration
- The reaction produces approximately 970ml biodiesel and 100ml glycerin per liter of oil

## Waste Vegetable Oil Collection

### Sourcing

- Restaurants are the primary source — most are happy to give away used oil
- Establish relationships with 2-3 restaurants for reliable supply
- Chinese, Japanese, and fried chicken restaurants produce the most oil
- Avoid establishments that mix oils or use hydrogenated oils (solid at room temperature)
- Average collection: 5-15 gallons per restaurant per week

### Collection Equipment

- Clean 5-gallon jugs or 55-gallon drums with tight lids
- Funnel with a fine mesh screen
- Hand truck or cart for transport
- Label containers with collection date and source

### Pre-Processing

Before making biodiesel, WVO must be prepared:

1. **Dewatering** — Heat oil to 140F and hold for 15 minutes, or let settle for 2+ weeks and draw oil from above the water line
2. **Filtering** — Pass through progressively finer filters: 100 micron, then 10 micron. A hydraulic filter press or bag filters work well
3. **Testing for water** — A simple test: fry a small piece of bread in the oil. If it sizzles aggressively, water is present. If it gently sizzles, it is dry enough

## Titration

Titration determines how much catalyst is needed for your specific batch of oil. Fresh oil needs less catalyst; degraded oil needs more.

### Titration Procedure (NaOH method)

1. Dissolve 1 gram of NaOH in 1 liter of distilled water ([[isopropyl-alcohol]] solution)
2. In a separate container, mix 1ml of oil with 10ml of isopropyl alcohol
3. Add the NaOH solution drop by drop to the oil-alcohol mixture while stirring
4. Count the drops until the solution turns pink and stays pink for 20 seconds
5. The number of ml of NaOH solution used = the "titration number"

### Catalyst Calculation

Base catalyst for NaOH: 3.5 grams per liter of oil
Plus: titration number x 3.5 additional grams per liter

Example: If titration = 4ml
Total NaOH = 3.5 + (4 x 3.5) = 17.5 grams per liter of oil

If the titration number is above 6-7, the oil is heavily degraded and may produce lower quality biodiesel or excessive soap formation.

## The Biodiesel Production Process

### Equipment Needed

- Processor tank (stainless steel or HDPE plastic, 20-40 gallon capacity)
- Heating element (immersion heater capable of 130-140F)
- Pump for mixing and transfer
- Methanol-safe containers and plumbing (no aluminum or zinc)
- Thermometer
- Scale accurate to 0.1 gram
- Safety equipment: goggles, gloves, respirator (for methanol), fire extinguisher

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Heat the oil** to 130-135F in the processor tank
2. **Mix the methoxide** — In a separate sealed container, dissolve the measured NaOH in the methanol. Stir until completely dissolved. This produces sodium methoxide, which is highly caustic. Always add NaOH to methanol, never the reverse
3. **Add methoxide to oil** — With the oil at temperature, slowly add the methoxide while the pump circulates the oil. Vigorous mixing is critical for the reaction
4. **Mix for 1-2 hours** — Maintain temperature at 130F throughout. The mixture will darken and become translucent
5. **Settle for 8-24 hours** — Turn off heat and pump. The biodiesel (lighter, amber colored) will rise to the top, and glycerin (darker, denser) will settle to the bottom
6. **Drain the glycerin** — Open the bottom valve and drain the glycerin layer. Glycerin is roughly 20% of total volume
7. **Water wash** — This is the most critical quality step

## Washing Biodiesel

Washing removes residual methanol, catalyst, and soap from the biodiesel:

### Mist Wash (Gentlest Method)

1. Transfer biodiesel to a wash tank
2. Add water equal to 1/3 the volume of biodiesel
3. Spray water as a fine mist on top of the biodiesel (do not agitate — mist rises and falls through the biodiesel)
4. As water droplets fall through the biodiesel, they absorb impurities and settle to the bottom
5. Drain the cloudy water from the bottom
6. Repeat with fresh water until the drained water runs clear (typically 3-5 washes)

### Bubble Wash

1. Add water to the bottom of the wash tank
2. Use an aquarium air stone to create fine bubbles that rise through the biodiesel
3. Bubbles carry water droplets upward through the biodiesel, absorbing impurities
4. Water collects at the bottom as it becomes heavier
5. Drain and repeat until water is clear

### Final Drying

After washing, biodiesel contains suspended water droplets:

1. Let biodiesel settle for 24 hours — water will separate to the bottom
2. Heat biodiesel to 120F to evaporate remaining water
3. Or pass through a fuel filter with water-absorbing media
4. The biodiesel should be crystal clear and amber-yellow when dry

## Quality Testing

### Visual Test

- Clear, amber liquid = good
- Cloudy = water present (needs more drying)
- Dark brown or black = incomplete reaction or contaminated oil

### 3/27 Test (Simple Conversion Test)

1. Mix 3ml biodiesel with 27ml methanol in a clear jar
2. Shake vigorously and let settle for 5 minutes
3. If the mixture separates cleanly into a clear top layer (unreacted oil) and a darker bottom layer (biodiesel dissolved in methanol), conversion is complete
4. If it stays mixed or separates poorly, the reaction was incomplete and the biodiesel should be reprocessed

### Other Tests

- **pH test** — Biodiesel should be neutral (pH 7). High pH indicates residual catalyst
- **Cloud point** — The temperature at which biodiesel begins to gel. Important for cold climate use
- **Water content** — Professional testing kits available; should be below 500ppm

## Glycerin Byproduct

Glycerin makes up about 20% of the reaction volume and has several uses:

- **Soap making** — Glycerin-rich soap from biodiesel production is excellent quality. See [[soap-making-guide]]
