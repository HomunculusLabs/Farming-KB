# Hydroponics Systems and the Bubbler
Chapter 9 of [[hydroponic-cannabis]] cultivation, covering the basic
principles, common system types, [[jadam-nutrient-management-wetting-agent-and-calcium]], and detailed
instructions for building a simple but effective "bubbler" system.

## What Is Hydroponics

Hydroponics is the technique of growing plants without soil, using beds
of sand, gravel, or artificial mediums flooded with a [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]].
The word comes from Greek: "hydro" (water) and "ponos" (labor).
A basic hydroponics system consists of:
- A pot or growing container
- A [[dighton-fungal-biomass-nutrient-reservoir-ecosystems]]
- A grow medium
- A water pump
- Growth nutrients

The system has a top layer (grow medium) and a lower layer (water with
nutrients). Roots grow through the medium into the nutrient solution.
Water and nutrients are pumped in at timed intervals. Plants drink the
solution down, exposing roots to air. This allows plants to devote more
energy to upper growth rather than root searching.

## Advantages of Hydroponics

- Can produce a flowering plant in 3/4 the time of a soil grow.
- Produces massive root clusters (a 2-gallon bucket of roots from a
  single plant is common).
- Bigger and better buds compared to soil grows of the same strain.
- Over 100 different DIY systems exist, with about 15 proven useful
  for cannabis.
The biggest risk: if a hydroponics system is not well maintained, the
whole unit may fail and kill plants very quickly.

## Hydroponics Growing Mediums

### Rockwool

The most popular hydroponic medium, available in slabs or cubes (1 to 6
inches). Slabs can be cut to fit any pot or container. Rockwool should
be pre-soaked for 24 hours in water with a pH of 5.6 before use.

### Other Mediums

Growers also use clay pebbles, coconut coir, perlite, and various
artificial mediums designed to hold minerals, provide drainage, and
allow air to reach the roots.

## Hydroponics Systems

### NFT ([[ebb-and-flow]])

Recognized by its depth. The grow medium sits above the reservoir, which
pumps nutrients to the roots at set intervals. During the day, plants
go through spells of dryness as the solution drains back to the
reservoir. The whole unit recycles nutrient solution at timed intervals.

### Drip Irrigation System

Feeds plants individually through small drippers. Plants are in separate
chambers, and the solution drains through much like an Ebb and Flow
system.

### Aeroponics

Roots are suspended in air and misted with nutrient solution. This
method provides maximum oxygen to roots but requires precise timing and
maintenance.

### Automatic and Manual Hydroponic Pots

Various commercial pots that automate or simplify the hydroponic process
for home growers.
