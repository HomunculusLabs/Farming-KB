## Light and Basidiocarp Initiation in Psilocybe Cubensis

E.R. Badham's 1980 paper in Mycologia (Vol. 72, No. 1, pp. 136-142) provides
the first systematic determination of an action spectrum for basidiocarp
(fruitbody) initiation in Psilocybe cubensis. This research resolved a
significant contradiction in the literature and established the specific light
wavelengths, intensities, and durations required to trigger [[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]] and related species.

## Research Background

The requirement of light for basidiocarp initiation in fungi was well
documented across multiple species by 1980. Earlier work by Heim and Wasson
(1958) had suggested that P. cubensis might form fruitbodies in complete
darkness at elevated temperatures of 27 degrees Celsius. However, Jackson and
Alexopoulos (1976) stated that the species requires light for initiation at
22 to 25 degrees Celsius. Badham's study was designed to resolve this
discrepancy by systematically characterizing the [[psilocybe-cubensis-flush-variation]]
detailed findings.

## Materials and Methods

The isolate used was obtained in 1975 from a basidiocarp depositing
basidiospores onto agar, deposited with ATCC number 36359. Cultures were
grown on Brodie (1975) agar medium containing relatively low sugar levels,
asparagine, peptone, [[marmite-yeast-extract-straw-substrate]], and salts. Slant tubes were grown in
the dark for one month before treatment. Light sources included cool-white
fluorescent lamps with broad-band filters, a xenon-arc lamp for flash
experiments, and a 300-watt quartz-iodine projector lamp with a 0.25m
Jarrell-Ash monochromator for spectral sensitivity experiments. All
experiments were conducted at 21 degrees Celsius with 85% relative humidity.

## Cultivation Observations

Several important observations were made beyond the primary experimental
results. Initiation did not occur in non-ventilated cultures, confirming the
importance of gas exchange. Light had no effect on initiation before
vegetative maturity was completed. The agar surface must be fully colonized
by mycelium before the photomorphogenic response is activated. Abnormal
formations were noted including chestnut-colored spheres up to 5mm diameter
containing spores, grown at 10 degrees Celsius. Under low light levels, a
blue zone subtended the pileus corresponding to the phototropic zone
described by Plunkett (1961).
