# Amanita Muscaria Resurrection Experiments

The resurrection of [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]] refers to the mushroom's ability to regrow mycelium from dried, extracted tissue when provided with nutrient-rich liquid and proper conditions. This phenomenon, independently discovered by the author of "Amanita Muscaria: Herb of Immortality," may explain numerous ancient religious concepts including the three-day resurrection, the Holy Grail, and the "bread of life" -- and provides empirical evidence for the mushroom's ancient reputation as the immortal herb.

## Background and Discovery

The discovery emerged from the author's attempt to understand the New Testament's "resurrection in three days" references in the context of his theory that Amanita muscaria was the original Christian Eucharist. The investigation proceeded through several logical steps:

### The Three-Day Problem

Simple rehydration of dried Amanita muscaria with water can take place in minutes to hours -- it does not fit the three-day references found in religious texts. Something more was needed.

### The Rig Veda Clue

Re-reading the Rig Veda, the author discovered that the water used for rehydration had grain (barley) boiled in it before being used on the Somas. This would result in a very nutritious food source for the fungus. A puzzling statement referring to "these ancient Somas and these new ones" was noted. The new Somas were obviously freshly picked and dried mushrooms -- but what were the ancient Somas?

### The Siberian Clue

In Wasson's "Soma: Divine Mushroom of Immortality," the Siberian tribes using Amanita muscaria as an intoxicant preferred older specimens that had dried in the ground. A fuzzy feel to the stem and a dark color distinguished these mushrooms.

### Field Observation

During summer 2000, while gathering specimens, the author came upon several old specimens that had turned black. Each was covered in silvery-gray long hairy mycelium, some strands more than 6 inches long. Closer inspection with a hand lens revealed that the fuzz was erupting from the body of the mushroom and growing back into the ground, eating needles and leaf litter wherever it touched the forest floor.

The author recognized this "hairy mold" from previous observations -- it had appeared on specimens that had been water-extracted and left in a jar for a couple of days. The question arose: had he been resurrecting the herb of life without knowing it?

## Experimental Method

### Nutrient Liquids Tested

Three nutrient liquids were chosen based on their availability to ancient peoples:

1. **Grape juice** -- Readily available in the ancient Middle East and Mediterranean Basin
2. **Boiled barley water** -- Mentioned in the Rig Veda; barley was one of the earliest cultivated cereal grains
3. **Honey water** -- Honey diluted in water; available to humans long before agriculture, the most widely available sweetener in Eurasia

### Extraction and Resurrection Protocol

1. Dry Amanita muscaria specimens were extracted with one of the nutrient liquids (8-12 hours)
2. The liquid was poured off for consumption as ambrosia or sacred wine
3. The extracted pieces were left in the jar, covered with a cloth or paper towel (NOT airtight)
4. The jar was maintained at 65-70 F (room temperature)
5. Within three days, the pieces erupted in silvery, bluish-gray mycelium
6. Cultures were allowed to grow for up to three weeks
7. The resurrected mycelium was dried and re-extracted
8. The cycle was repeated

### Crucial: Non-Sterile Conditions

All experiments were deliberately carried out in a non-sterile environment to replicate ancient conditions. This was considered essential to testing whether the process could have been performed by ancient peoples without modern laboratory equipment.

## Results

### Consistent Resurrection

In all cases (grape juice, barley water, honey water), the extracted pieces erupted in silvery, bluish-gray mycelium within three days at normal room temperature. The cultures were allowed to grow for up to three weeks, then dried and re-extracted. In all cases, the liquid extract was again psychoactive.

The cycle was repeated three times with no real change in psychoactivity. The author concluded that as long as the herb of life is adequately fed, the cycle can be repeated indefinitely.

### Aroma Profiles

One important finding: the resurrected mycelium does not produce offensive odors.

- **Grape juice:** Slight vinegar smell that transforms into one of the most delightful flowery fragrances the author had ever encountered
- **Barley water:** Much heavier smell, strong but not offensive
- **Honey water:** Flowery, mild, and pleasant

This is significant because it means the resurrected Amanita muscaria would not have offended ancient users, removing a practical barrier to consumption.

### Antibiotic Evidence

Despite massive exposure to spores and bacteria from the non-sterile environment, not one culture appeared contaminated with any other life form. The author even carried out the entire process -- from extraction with grape juice to resurrection to greatest growth -- outdoors during late summer with only a cloth covering the jar. Not only could the ancients have carried out the resurrection, the author believes anyone can.

The resurrected cultures were dried to convert ibotenic acid to muscimol. These seemed to possess the same gram-for-gram potency they originally had.

## Detailed Observations

### Mycelium Appearance

- In the dark, the mycelium is pure snow white
- On exposure to light, it quickly changes to silvery, bluish gray
- Occasionally a brownish tint appears
- The pieces of the original specimen invariably turn dark (dark brown to almost black)
- The pieces also grow back together into one piece
- The mycelium is very distinctive in appearance once observed a few times

### Growth Requirements

- **Oxygen:** Essential. Sealing in an airtight jar results in slow or no growth, with production of very smelly chemicals
- **Temperature:** 60-80 F suitable; 65-70 F for three-day resurrection
- **Light sensitivity:** Avoid direct sunlight (can be burned)
- **Handling:** Do not shake or handle too roughly while growing

### Hardiness

Amanita muscaria proved extremely hardy in these experiments:

- The author has resurrected specimens that had been powdered before extraction
- Even specimens actually crushed while full of grape juice (reduced to slime) still resurrected, though they took slightly longer
- Once resurrected, subsequent resurrections easily happen within three days
- For re-extracted resurrected cultures, extraction time should be halved (12 hours maximum) to prevent the extract from becoming charged with living chemicals

### Drying Resurrected Cultures

Drying is necessary to convert ibotenic acid to muscimol. The resurrected mycelium can be dried in the jar or bottle it grew in: simply remove the paper or cloth cover and place the jar in a warm oven at 120 F until dry. This reduces handling damage to the mycelium considerably.

## Natural Resurrection in the Forest

The author's field observations suggest that resurrection occurs naturally:

- Old black specimens with erupting mycelium were found on the forest floor
- The mycelium eats needles and leaf litter, creating bird-nest-like depressions
- Fresh deer feces found near fruiting Amanita muscaria sometimes show what appears to be Amanita muscaria mycelium sprouting several inches long
- The mushroom is acting like fruiting plants that give animals food in return for dispersing seeds over a wide area

### Spore Production by Exposed Mycelium

When Amanita muscaria mycelium is exposed to the environment, it develops spores similar to those produced by simpler fungi. Under the microscope, small balls appear along the length of the exposed mycelium, which turn black when mature. Each ball contains several hundred clear spores, and there can be thousands of these balls in even a tiny culture.

## Implications for Ancient Religion

The author argues that the resurrection ability is the last major piece of evidence identifying Amanita muscaria as the Christ in the New Testament gospels:

1. **The resurrection** is the most dramatic and distinctive property of this mushroom -- no other life form on the planet does this
2. **Three-day timing** matches the Gospel accounts exactly
3. **The New Testament is ancient scientific (empirical) knowledge embedded in a story** -- the major points (virgin birth, water to wine, healing, prophecy, resurrection) fit Amanita muscaria precisely
4. **Two methods of preserving complex knowledge without writing:** make it poetry/song (Rig Veda) or make it part of a story (New Testament)
5. **All major religions are descended from shamanism** -- a shaman's job is to make contact with the divine; an immortal plant would be the ultimate vehicle for this contact
