# Shamanism and Plant Hallucinogens

Shamanism is the archaic practice of entering [[weil-the-natural-mind-altered-states-normalcy]] of consciousness to
interact with a hidden world of spirits, allies, and nonhuman intelligences for
purposes of healing, divination, and communal guidance. Mircea Eliade defined
shamanism as "the archaic techniques of ecstasy." Preeminent among the shaman's
techniques is the use of shamanism and plant hallucinogens, repositories of what Terence
McKenna called "living vegetable gnosis" that lie nearly forgotten in humanity's
ancient past. The relationship between shamans and psychoactive plants represents
the oldest known form of human religion.

## The Shamanic Worldview

The shaman operates from the premise that the visible, material world is
embedded within a larger reality that is ordinarily hidden from ordinary
perception. In this other dimension exist powers both helpful and malevolent,
governed by rules more akin to those of myth and dream than of everyday life.
Shamanic healers insist on the existence of an intelligent Other somewhere in a
dimension nearby — an ecology of souls or disincarnate intelligence that has
long been a part of the terrestrial ecology, present but unseen.

The shaman is one who has attained a vision of the beginnings and endings of
all things and who can communicate that vision. This is achieved through what
McKenna called "a feat of linguistic prowess" — the ability to navigate, map,
and bring back knowledge from nonordinary states of consciousness.

## Techniques of Ecstasy

Shamanic ecstasy is not mere intoxication but a disciplined encounter with
transcendence. The experience involves:

- **Journeying**: The shaman's consciousness travels to other worlds or
  dimensions, often described as upper, middle, and lower worlds.
- **Communication with spirits**: Direct dialogue with nonhuman intelligences,
  ancestors, animal allies, or plant teachers.
- **Healing**: Diagnosis and treatment of illness, often understood as soul
  loss or spiritual intrusion.
- **Divination**: Gaining knowledge about the future, the location of game
  animals, or the causes of communal misfortune.
- **Shape-shifting**: The experiential transformation into animal form, either
  through identification or through the feeling of actually becoming the animal.

## Plant Hallucinogens as Shamanic Tools

Psychoactive plants are the primary technology of the shaman in most indigenous
cultures. The specific plants vary by region:

- **[[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]]** (genus [[psilocybe-mairei]]): Used throughout Mesoamerica,
  particularly by Mazatec and other Oaxacan peoples. The mushrooms are
  referred to as "the flesh of the gods" or "los niños" (the little ones).
- **Ayahuasca** ([[banisteriopsis-caapi]] with [[psychotria-viridis]] or
  [[diplopterys-cabreraeana]]): A visionary brew used throughout the Amazon
  basin. The Banisteriopsis vine provides MAO inhibitors while the admixture
  plants supply DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine), creating a potent oral
  psychedelic preparation.
- **Peyote** ([[lophophora-williamsii]]): A cactus containing mescaline, used
  by Huichol and other indigenous peoples of northern Mexico and the American
  Southwest.
- **Iboga** ([[tabernanthe-iboga]]): Used by the Fang people of Gabon in the
  [[mckenna-iboga-tabernanthe-ibogaine-fang-bwiti-initiation-cult]] ceremony. Contains ibogaine, a potent oneirophrenic
  (dream-inducing) alkaloid.
- **[[amanita-muscaria-fly-agaric-history-and-pharmacology]]** ([[amanita-muscaria]]): The iconic red-and-white mushroom,
  used in Siberian shamanism and possibly the identity of the Vedic [[soma-hypothesis]].

## The Icaro and Healing Songs

In [[mckenna-food-gods-amazonian-shamanism-ayahuasca]], the healing ceremony centers on the icaro — a magical
curing song that, in the ecstatic state of both healer and patient, becomes
more than a vocal performance. The icaro is experienced as a visible
manifestation of power, enfolding the participants and making them secure. The
shaman sings to direct the visionary experience, call protective spirits, and
extract illness from the patient. The icaro tradition represents a sophisticated
system of sound-based healing developed over thousands of years.

## Shamanism as Social Catalyst

In hunter-gatherer societies, shamanic ceremonies served as social glue. At
regular intervals (often lunar), ordinary activities were suspended. The whole
clan gathered at night — night being the time of magical projection and
hallucinations. Elders, usually women but often men, distributed each person's
dose of the psychoactive plant. Bone flutes and drums wove within chanting.
Line dances with heavy foot stamping channeled the energy of the first wave of
visions. When the elders signaled silence, each mind followed its own trail of
sparks into darkness.

the strength of the group. They felt relief mingled with wonder at the beauty
of the visionary expanse. An individual felt no distance between themselves and
