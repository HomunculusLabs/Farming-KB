# Tropical Soils and Fertility

> Source: *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*, Section 10.3 — Bill Mollison

## Overview

Tropical soils present unique challenges for agriculture and [[bee-forage-systems-permaculture-design]].
Unlike [[cool-temperate-soils-and-water-conservation-mollison]], periodically renewed by glaciation and physical mixing of
mineral substrates, tropical soils are among the oldest on Earth. Millions of
years of heavy rainfall and high temperatures have produced soils that are
deeply leached, chemically depleted, and structurally fragile.

## The Nature of Tropical Soils

### Age and Leaching

Tropical soils have not been renewed by glaciation for geological epochs. Under
continuous warm temperatures and intense rainfall, soluble minerals have been
progressively washed out. This deep leaching removes bases (calcium, magnesium,
potassium, sodium) and silica, leaving behind only the most resistant minerals.
The two elements most critically depleted are **silica** (essential for cell
wall strength) and **calcium** (vital for soil structure and pH regulation).

### Kaolinitic Clay Soils

In many tropical areas the dominant clay mineral is **kaolinite** — the
end-product of extreme weathering. Only kaolin clays and iron-aluminium oxides
remain. These residual minerals have very low nutrient-holding capacity, so
applied nutrients are quickly lost to leaching.

### Fresh Volcanic Areas

**Fresh volcanic areas** possess sufficiently rich soils due to recent mineral
deposition. These young soils retain higher base levels and better structure,
making them significantly more productive than ancient weathered soils.
