# Terrace Construction and Earthworks

Terraces are among the most important earth-shaping strategies in permaculture, transforming slopes into productive, stable agricultural land. They control erosion, enable irrigation, and create accessible growing surfaces on otherwise unusable terrain.

## Common Mistakes in Terracing

Several factors can undermine terrace systems:

- **Failing to assess soil type** — deep loams over permeable subsoils drain well and are suited to terraces, while shallow soils over impermeable clay require careful drainage design
- **Over-terracing the landscape** — covering too large a proportion of the landscape reduces leaf and tree nutrient input to crops
- **Creating excessively large terrace series in high-rainfall areas** — this concentrates run-off beyond safe capacity

## Principles of Effective Terrace Design

With careful assessment of site factors, long-term production can be achieved through:

- **Short series of polycultural terraces** combining wet and dry crops
- **Stable bunds** constructed either as rock walls or at a 1:3 slope ratio
- **Tree integration** — trees on bunds and between, above, and below terrace series should form 40-60% of the total landscape plan
- **Controlled irrigation** — soils and installed water inlets/outlets must permit safe, controllable water flow

## Benefits of Terraces

Terraces provide several critical advantages for sloped land:

1. **Easy crop access** — level surfaces on slopes make planting, maintenance, and harvesting far more practical
2. **Controlled irrigation** — water can be directed precisely across level surfaces, reducing waste and increasing effectiveness
3. **Minimal soil loss** — overland water flow and slope cultivation erosion are dramatically reduced
4. **Nutrient gain** — silts and nutrients from irrigation or run-off waters accumulate, supplemented by leaf fall from trees on bunds

## Slope Considerations

Terracing is most effective on moderate slopes. At approximately 30° slope (though preferably 10-18°), terracing becomes worthwhile. As slopes steepen, the ratio of earth moved to area of cropland developed becomes impractical or inefficient.

### Steep Slope Strategy (20°+)

On very steep sites, especially in high-rainfall areas, broad terracing should be abandoned in favor of:

- **4-6 narrow production terraces** per series
- Careful drainage to spill excess rain down permanently vegetated slopes
- **Ridged terrace tips** to contain water
- **Mulched paths** to reduce erosion
- **Staggered path spills** in short series

This approach captures the advantages of terracing on steep slopes without risking erosion and soil loss. Machines are inappropriate for such construction — steep slope terracing of this nature is always hand-cut.

## Construction Sequence

Terrace construction follows a specific bottom-up sequence:

1. **Begin on the lowest terrace level** — remove and stockpile all topsoil over the entire area of the lower terraces
2. **Proceed uphill** — as each terrace is completed, the topsoil of the next highest level is cleared onto the preceding lower terrace
3. **Finish at the top** — stockpiled soil from the lowest terrace is finally carted or lifted to the last terrace uphill

Every terrace system should ideally be designed to accommodate **perennial bund and terrace wall plants** specifically for soil stability and [[irrigation-systems]]
- **Aquaculture** integration in wet terrace zones adds protein production
- **Tree crop systems** on bunds maintain long-term soil fertility and provide additional yields
- **Drainage systems** must be designed to safely convey excess water without causing erosion below the terrace series
