## LSA Chemistry and Relation to LSD

LSA (lysergic acid amide) is an ergoline alkaloid with a molecular
structure closely related to LSD. The key difference is that LSA lacks
the diethylamide group that characterizes LSD, accounting for its much
lower potency. LSD is psychoactive at the microgram level, while LSA
requires milligram doses. LSA intoxication reportedly has both
quantitative and qualitative differences from LSD, generally described
as less intense, more sedating, and with more prominent physical side
effects including nausea and lethargy.

## Primary Plant Sources

### Argyreia nervosa (Hawaiian Baby Woodrose)

Argyreia nervosa is a perennial climbing vine native to the Indian
subcontinent but widely cultivated as an ornamental. Its seeds contain
the highest concentrations of LSA among commonly available sources, at
0.14% LSA by dry weight. Five to ten seeds yield an average dose of
LSA for hallucinogenic effects.

### Ipomoea violacea (Morning Glory)

Ipomoea violacea is a popular annual vine grown for its abundant
flowers. Seeds contain LSA at 0.02% by dry weight, substantially
lower than Argyreia nervosa. A much larger quantity is needed: 150 to
200 seeds (approximately 3 to 6 grams) yield an average dose. Ipomoea
violacea and related Ipomoea corymbosa seeds were used historically in
[[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]] for shamanic and ceremonial purposes.

### Stipa robusta (Sleepygrass)

The highest concentrations of LSA have been found in Stipa robusta
(Achnatherum robustum), growing in the southwestern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]].
However, there is no history of shamanic tradition with this plant and
no evidence that it is commonly ingested for intoxication.

## Dosage and Effects

### Dosage

Hallucinogenic activity of LSA occurs at doses of 2 to 5 mg, providing
a 4 to 8 hour intoxication. Required seed quantities:

- **Argyreia nervosa**: 5 to 10 seeds
- **Ipomoea violacea**: 150 to 200 seeds (3 to 6 grams)

### Effects Profile

Effects include visual and auditory hallucinations, altered perception
of color and form, mild to moderate euphoria, sedation and lethargy,
nausea and gastrointestinal discomfort, pupil dilation, and altered
thought patterns. The experience is generally less visual and more
sedating compared to LSD or psilocybin.

## Preparation Methods

Several methods are used to prepare LSA-containing seeds:

- **Whole seed consumption**: Seeds eaten directly
- **Crushed seeds**: Ground and consumed
- **Germination**: Seeds germinated before consumption, which some
  users report reduces nausea
- **Water extraction**: Seeds soaked in water; liquid extract consumed
  after filtering out solids
