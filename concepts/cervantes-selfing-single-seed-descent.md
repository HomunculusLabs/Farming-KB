# Selfing and Single Seed Descent

Selfing is the process of creating seed by fertilizing a plant with
pollen obtained from itself. The result of a self-cross is a population
of plants that derive from a single individual. Selfing is the most
intensive form of inbreeding and is the fastest route to achieving
homozygosity within a breeding population.

## The Selfing Process

The first generation population derived from selfing an individual is
called the S1 population. If an individual is chosen from the S1 and
again selfed, the resulting population is denoted the S2 generation.
Subsequent generations derived in the same manner are denoted S3, S4,
etc.

## Genetic Effects of Selfing

Traits for which the plant is homozygous remain homozygous upon
selfing, whereas heterozygous loci segregate and may demonstrate novel
expressions of these characters.

Each selfed generation leads to an increase in homozygosity by 50% for
each heterozygous locus. Each subsequent generation derived from selfing
an S1 individual is 50% more homozygous than the parent from which it
was derived. This predictable increase makes selfing a powerful tool
for breeders seeking to fix desired traits.

## Achieving Homozygosity Through Selfing

After six generations of selfing without selection, 98.44% of the genes
of an individual are homozygous. This refers to genes, not the number
of plants that are homozygous. Repeated selfing, or single seed
descent, is the fastest way to achieve homozygosity within a group or
family.

The more plants grown from a selfed population, the better probability
a breeder has of finding selfed progeny that show all of the desired
traits. This is because each generation of selfing produces a range of
genetic combinations as heterozygous loci segregate.

## Single Seed Descent Method

Single seed descent is a specific application of selfing:

1. A plant is self-fertilized and the resulting seed collected
2. One of these seeds is selected and grown
3. The plant is again self-fertilized, and seed produced
4. All progeny and future generations have descended from a single
   ancestor, as long as no pollen from an external family is introduced
5. Each generation is the result of selfing one individual from the
   previous generation

This method is space-efficient because only one plant per generation
needs to be maintained, but it requires many generations to reach high
homozygosity. Breeders typically run multiple single seed descent lines
in parallel to preserve different genetic combinations.

## Selfing vs. Sib-Mating

Selfing is the most severe form of inbreeding because all genetic
material comes from a single individual. Sib-mating (crossing siblings
within a generation) is less intense because it draws from at least two
parents. Both methods increase homozygosity, but selfing does so faster.

In cross-pollinated species like cannabis, selfing can reveal
deleterious recessive alleles that remain hidden in out-crossed
populations. This makes selfing useful for identifying and eliminating
unwanted recessive traits, but it also means inbreeding depression can
appear rapidly.

## Practical Considerations

Selfing cannabis presents practical challenges:

- Cannabis is naturally dioecious (separate male and female plants), so
  selfing requires inducing a female plant to produce male flowers, as
  described in [[cervantes-feminized-seed-production]]
- Each generation of selfing may reveal deleterious recessive traits
  that reduce plant vigor or health
- Population sizes must be large enough to find individuals with all
  desired traits in later generations
- The rapid approach to homozygosity means genetic diversity is quickly
  lost from the breeding population

## Recurrent Selection

As a complement or alternative to selfing, recurrent selection is any
breeding program designed to concentrate favorable genes scattered among
a number of individuals by repeated cycles of selection:

1. Identify superior genotypes for the trait under selection
2. Inter-mate the superior genotypes and select improved progeny
3. Repeat steps 1 and 2 over a series of generations

Recurrent selection maintains more genetic diversity than selfing while
still concentrating favorable alleles, making it suitable for traits
controlled by multiple genes.

## Pedigree Selection

Pedigree selection is a system of breeding in which individual plants
are selected in the segregating generations from a cross on the basis of
their desirability, judged individually, and on the basis of a pedigree
record. This method combines elements of selfing with careful record-
keeping to track the inheritance of specific traits through generations.
