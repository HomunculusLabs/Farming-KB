# African Neurotropic Mushroom Species

## Overview

Africa remains one of the most poorly explored continents for neurotropic
fungi. The existing records are scattered, with most documentation from
North Africa (especially Morocco and Algeria) and southern Africa. The
central and western African regions are particularly understudied, despite
having suitable tropical and subtropical climates for [[psilocybin-potency-dosage-guide]]
mushrooms.

## Widely Distributed African Species

Several neurotropic species are reported across broad African regions:
- **Amanita muscaria**: Documented from northern Africa and South Africa
- **A. pantherina**: Found in northern and southern Africa
- **Claviceps paspali**: Widespread in tropical and subtropical Africa
- **C. purpurea**: Reported from northern Africa, Ethiopia, and other
  regions
- **Copelandia tropicalis**: Distributed widely in Africa (Ola'h, 1969;
  Weeks et al., 1979)
- **[[gymnopilus-spectabilis]]**: Reported from northern Africa
- **Panaeolina foenisecii**: Widespread in Africa
- **Panaeolus africanus**: Distinctly African species, found in Chad,
  Central African Republic, and Sudan
- **P. fimicola**: Widespread in northern Africa
- **P. microsporus**: Found in Central African Republic
- **P. papilionaceus**: Widespread across Africa
- **P. sphinctrinus**: Widespread in northern Africa
- **P. subbalteatus**: Reported from multiple African countries
- **[[psilocybe-cyanescens]]**: Reported from Africa (Gartz, 1996)
- **P. goniospora**: Found in Kenya, Uganda, and Sri Lanka
- **P. mairei**: Found in Algeria and Morocco

## North Africa

North Africa has the best-documented neurotropic fungal records on the
continent, largely due to the work of Malencon and Bertault (1970) in
Morocco.

### Morocco
Morocco has the richest documented neurotropic mycoflora in Africa:
- **Psilocybe mairei**: Originally described as Hypholoma cyanescens by
  Maire; confirmed from Morocco by Malencon and Bertault (1970); also
  found in Algeria and southwestern Africa
- **Copelandia bispora**: Reported from Morocco
- **[[inocybe-corydalina]]** and **I. calamistrata**: Present in Morocco
- **Pluteus cyanopus**, **P. atricapillus**, **P. salicinus**, **P.
  villosus**: All present in Morocco; P. villosus confirmed to contain
  psilocybin (Stijve and Kuyper, 1985)
- **Amanita muscaria** and **A. pantherina**: Both present
- Diverse Panaeolus species: P. fimicola, P. papilionaceus

### Algeria
- **P. mairei**: Type locality region; originally collected by Maire
- **[[claviceps-purpurea]]**: Present in Algeria

## West and Central Africa

The tropical regions of Africa are severely underexplored for neurotropic
fungi.

### Ivory Coast
- Conocybe sp. (unconfirmed): Reported by Samorini (1995)
- Psilocybe sp. (unconfirmed): Reported by Samorini (1995)

### Central African Republic
- **Panaeolus africanus**: Found by Ola'h (1968, 1969)
- **P. microsporus**: Found by Ola'h (1969, 1970)
- **Pluteus atricapillus**: Reported by Horak (1978)

### Chad and Sudan
- **P. africanus**: Found in Chad (Ola'h, 1968) and Sudan

## East Africa

### Kenya
- **Panaeolus aquamarina**: Described by Pegler (1977) from Kenya
- **P. cubensis**: Questionably reported; identified as Stropharia sp.
  cf. cubensis by Vedcourt and Trump (1969)
- Psilocybe sp.: Identified as Stropharia sp. by Charters (1957, 1958)

### Ethiopia
- **Claviceps purpurea**: Reported from Ethiopia

### Tanzania
- **Amanita muscaria**: Documented by Harkonen (1995)
- **Copelandia tropicalis**: Reported from Tanzania
- **Pluteus atricapillus**: Reported from Tanzania

### Uganda
- **Panaeolus papilionaceus**: Present in Uganda
- **Psilocybe goniospora**: Found in Uganda (Pegler, 1977)

## Southern Africa

### South Africa
South Africa has one of the best-documented neurotropic mycofloras south
of the Sahara:
- **[[psilocybe-natalensis]]**: First described from South Africa by Gartz,
  Reid, Eicker, and Smith (1995); described as "the first indigenous
  bluing member of the Agaricales of South Africa"; confirmed to contain
  psilocybin and psilocin
- **P. semilanceata**: Questionably reported from South Africa
- **Amanita muscaria** and **A. pantherina**: Both present; documented
  by Watt and Breyer-Brandwijk (1962)
- **Panaeolina foenisecii**: Widespread in South Africa
- **Panaeolus** species: P. papilionaceus, P. retirugis, P. subbalteatus
- **[[pluteus-salicinus]]**: Present in South Africa

### Madagascar and Indian Ocean
- **Copelandia cyanescens**: Documented from Madagascar (Heim et al.,
  1967); also in Zaire
- **Dictyophora indusiata**: Reported from Madagascar
- **Panaeolina foenisecii**: Present in Zaire

## Biogeographic Notes

The African neurotropic mycoflora shows affinities with both European
(northern Africa) and Asian (eastern Africa) fungal communities. The
presence of P. goniospora in Kenya, Uganda, India, and Sri Lanka suggests
paleotropical distribution patterns. The discovery of P. natalensis in
South Africa demonstrates that indigenous bluing Psilocybe species exist
in the southern hemisphere, supporting the hypothesis of a southern origin
for the genus.

## Research Gaps

Africa represents one of the largest gaps in neurotropic fungal
knowledge. The tropical forests of West and Central Africa, the Congo
Basin, and the montane regions of East Africa are virtually unexplored.
Given the climatic similarity to well-documented regions of Southeast
Asia and South America, Africa likely harbors many undocumented species
of neurotropic fungi.
