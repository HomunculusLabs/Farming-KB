# Edaphic Factors and Microbial Community Structure

Edaphic factors — the physical, chemical, and biological properties of soil — are the
primary determinants of microbial [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] across all sample types in the
plant-root-soil continuum. In studies of the Cannabis microbiome (Winston et al., 2014),
soil type was consistently the strongest predictor of microbial community variation,
explaining more variation than either plant cultivar or sample type (endorhiza,
rhizosphere, or bulk soil).

## Measured Edaphic Variables

The Cannabis microbiome study characterized five key soil physicochemical properties:

| Variable | Description | Typical Range (Study) |
|---|---|---|
| pH | Soil acidity/alkalinity | 6.63 – 6.94 |
| Salinity | Electrical conductivity (dS/m) | 1.73 – 7.44 |
| Total Nitrogen | Percent N by weight | 0.26% – 1.51% |
| Total Organic Carbon | Percent C by weight | 3.02% – 20.0% |
| Water Content | Gravimetric water content | 0.101 – 0.371 |

All soils in the study were classified as sandy loam, with physical compositions of
approximately 62-66% sand, 16-18% silt, and 18-21% clay. Despite this textural
similarity, significant chemical differences between soil types drove microbial
community divergence.

## Ranked Importance of Edaphic Factors

Both weighted and unweighted analyses identified the same hierarchy of edaphic
factor importance for structuring microbial communities:

### Weighted UniFrac (abundance-sensitive)

1. **Nitrogen** (r = 0.465, p = 0.001) — Strongest single predictor
2. **Salinity** (r = 0.437, p = 0.001)
3. **Total Organic Carbon** (r = 0.330, p = 0.001)
4. **Water Content** (r = 0.281, p = 0.001)
5. **pH** (r = 0.221, p = 0.001)

### Unweighted UniFrac (presence/absence-sensitive)

1. **Nitrogen** (r = 0.630, p = 0.001)
2. **Salinity** (r = 0.620, p = 0.001)
3. **Total Organic Carbon** (r = 0.512, p = 0.001)
4. **Water Content** (r = 0.466, p = 0.001)
5. **pH** (r = 0.292, p = 0.001)

The ranking is identical between analyses, but effect sizes are larger in the
unweighted analysis, indicating that edaphic factors influence both which taxa are
present and how abundant they are, with a particularly strong effect on presence/
absence patterns.

## Nitrogen as the Dominant Factor

Nitrogen's position as the strongest edaphic predictor is consistent with ecological
theory. Nitrogen is typically the primary limiting nutrient for microbial growth in
temperate soils. Its effects on community structure operate through several mechanisms:

- **Direct nutrient limitation:** Microbial taxa differ in their nitrogen acquisition
  strategies (ammonium vs. nitrate preference, nitrogen fixation capability, organic
  nitrogen scavenging), and soil nitrogen levels favor different functional groups
- **Carbon-to-nitrogen ratio:** The C:N ratio determines whether net mineralization
  or immobilization occurs, affecting [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]] for the entire community
- **pH co-variation:** Nitrogen cycling processes (nitrification, ammonification)
  directly modify soil pH, creating feedback loops between nitrogen availability,
  pH, and community composition

## Salinity's Strong Influence

Salinity ranked second in importance, which is notable given that the study soils
varied from low (1.73 dS/m) to moderate (7.44 dS/m) salinity. Salinity affects
microbial communities through:

- **Osmotic stress:** High salt concentrations create [[fungal-fruiting-body-mechanics-osmotic-pressure]] that many soil
  microbes cannot tolerate, selecting for halotolerant taxa
- **Ion toxicity:** Specific ions (sodium, chloride) can be directly toxic to
  sensitive organisms
- **Water potential effects:** Saline soils have lower water potential, reducing
  the effective availability of water to microorganisms
- **Clay dispersion:** High sodium can disperse clay particles, altering soil
  structure and pore connectivity

## Soil Carbon and Microbial Communities

Total organic carbon influences microbial communities primarily as an energy source:

- Higher organic carbon generally supports larger and more diverse microbial populations
- The nature of organic matter (labile vs. recalcitrant) selects for different
  functional guilds
- Carbon quality interacts with nitrogen availability through the C:N ratio
- The study found a particularly high carbon soil (OC.2: 20.0% TOC) that supported
  distinct microbial communities from the lower-carbon soils

## pH: The Universal Soil Biodiversity Driver

Despite ranking fifth in this specific study, pH is widely recognized as one of the
most important global drivers of soil microbial diversity. Its relatively modest
effect in the Cannabis study may be due to the narrow pH range examined (6.63-6.94),
all within the slightly acidic range. Across broader pH gradients (4.0-8.0), pH
typically shows the strongest correlation with microbial community structure of any
single soil parameter.

## BEST Analysis

A Best Subset of Environmental Variables with Maximum (Rank) Correlation (BEST)
analysis was used to identify the combination of edaphic factors that best explained
community variation. This multivariate approach confirmed that the combined effects
of all five measured variables provided the best explanation of microbial community
structure, suggesting that the factors operate largely independently rather than
through a single dominant pathway.

## Soil Type as a Composite Variable

In the Cannabis study, soil type (categorical: MB.1, MB.2, OC) explained significant
community variation even after accounting for individual edaphic factors. This
indicates that unmeasured soil properties — such as specific mineral composition,
micronutrient availability, clay mineralogy, or organic matter quality — also
contribute to microbial community differentiation. Soil type thus serves as a
composite variable capturing both measured and unmeasured edaphic influences.

## Implications for Cultivation

Understanding edaphic drivers of microbial communities has direct applications:

- **Soil [[soil-mineral-management-and-amendment-strategies]]:** Targeted amendments can shift the soil microbial
  community toward beneficial compositions. Nitrogen management has the largest
  leverage point.
- **Site selection:** Pre-planting soil testing can predict the microbial community
  that will colonize crop roots, enabling informed cultivar-soil matching
- **Crop rotation effects:** Different crops leave different soil legacies through
  root exudate deposition, creating edaphic changes that persist and influence
  subsequent crops
