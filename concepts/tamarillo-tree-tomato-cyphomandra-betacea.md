## Tamarillo — Tree Tomato (*Cyphomandra betacea*)

## Overview

The tamarillo, or tree tomato (*Cyphomandra betacea*, family Solanaceae), is a fast-growing evergreen shrub native to the temperate Andes of [[query-how-to-grow-rare-tropical-fruits-in-marginal-and-subtropical-climates]], it can also grow in cooler areas if given a sunny, sheltered position. The name "tamarillo" was adopted in New Zealand in the 1960s as a marketing term; the fruit is still widely known as "tree tomato" in many regions.

## Botanical Characteristics

The tamarillo is an erect, shrubby, fast-growing evergreen reaching 4 meters in height. It is easy to grow but fairly short-lived, typically producing for 3–5 years before declining. The plant is shallow-rooted, making heavy mulching essential. If roots become too dry or damaged, fruits will not mature and will drop prematurely.

## Environmental Requirements

- **Soil**: Requires rich, moist, well-drained soil. Intolerant of both waterlogging and drought
- **Light**: Full sun preferred; partial shade tolerated
- **Frost**: Slightly frost tolerant to approximately -2°C
- **Wind**: Sheltered position recommended due to brittle branches and shallow roots

## Fruit Characteristics

The egg-shaped fruits are approximately 5 cm long. Three main color varieties exist:
- **Yellow**: Sweetest, mildest flavor
- **Red**: Harsher, more acidic flavor
- **Dark red**: Intermediate flavor, deep pigmentation

The skin is easily removed by dipping the fruit in hot water. Fruits are best eaten fresh but can also be made into jams, jellies, and preserves. The seeds contain concentrated nutrients — phosphorus content is notably higher in the seeds than the flesh.

## Propagation

Tamarillos can be propagated from seed or cuttings. Plants from cuttings tend to be lower and bushier, which is advantageous for small gardens and easier harvesting. Seed-grown plants show more genetic variability.
