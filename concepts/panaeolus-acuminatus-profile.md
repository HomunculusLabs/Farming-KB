# Panaeolus acuminatus Profile

## Taxonomy and Synonyms

Panaeolus acuminatus (Schaeffer) Quelet sensu Ricken is a
medium-sized mushroom in the genus [[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]]. It is also known
by the synonym Panaeolus rickenii Hora, which most authorities
consider conspecific. Moser (1983) described two forms: one as
Panaeolus acuminatus ss. Ricken, which is campanulate when young,
and another as Panaeolus acuminatus (Schaeffer ex Secr.) Quelet,
which has a dark band around the cap margin. The species epithet
"acuminatus" means pointed or acuminate, referring to the conic
cap shape characteristic of this mushroom.

## Macroscopic Features

### Cap

The cap measures 1.5 to 2.5 cm broad, sometimes reaching 4 cm.
It is conic to conic-campanulate when young, becoming more
campanulate to convex, and in extreme age expanding to plane.
The margin is shortly striate along the edge, then opaque, and
incurved when young, soon straightening. When moist, the cap is
chestnut or deep reddish brown, hygrophanous, becoming more
tawny in fading from the apex while remaining darker along the
margin. The surface is smooth, viscid to lubricous when wet, and
soon dries. The cap color and striations are distinctive features
of this species.

### Gills

Gill attachment is adnate to adnexed to sinuate, close to crowded,
broad, and even. Several tiers of intermediate gills are present.
At maturity, the gills become very dark purplish gray-black and
mottled, a characteristic pattern typical of the genus
[[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]]. The mottled appearance results from the uneven
ripening of spores on the gill faces, which is a hallmark of
Panaeolus species in general and a useful field identification
feature.

### Stem

The stem measures 45 to 105 mm long, occasionally reaching 150 mm,
by 2.5 to 6.5 mm thick. It is equal to slightly enlarged at the
base or at the apex, tubular, and more or less brittle
(cartilaginous). The color is very dark reddish brown or nearly
concolorous with the cap. The surface is pruinose and often has
small water droplets adhering near the apex of the stem, an
unusual and notable field characteristic that can aid
identification.

## Microscopic Features

Spores are blackish in deposit, smooth, and lemon-shaped, measuring
11 to 16 by 7.5 to 11 micrometers. The basidia are 4-spored.
Cheilocystidia are irregular in form. Pleurocystidia are absent.
These spore characteristics, particularly the lemon shape, are
consistent with the genus [[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]] as a whole and help
distinguish Panaeoli from other dark-spored agarics.

## Habitat and Distribution

Panaeolus acuminatus grows scattered to gregariously on manured
grounds or on dung in the spring. It is distributed throughout
North America and much of temperate Europe. In the Pacific
Northwest, this species is reported to be as common as
Panaeolus Papilionaceus. It is frequently found along the
field-forest interface, a habitat preference it shares with several
other Panaeoli. See also Panaeolus Foenisecii and
Panaeolus Castaneifolius for closely related species with
overlapping ranges.

## Chemical Constituents

Panaeolus acuminatus is not known to produce psilocybin or psilocin.
Chemical analysis by Stijve (1987) found no psilocybin and no
psilocin. However, the study did detect 0.016% serotonin, 0.066%
5-OH-tryptophan, and tryptophan. Gurevich (1993) also did not
detect any psilocybin or psilocin in a mushroom identified as
Panaeolus rickenii, which most authors consider conspecific with
P. acuminatus. The presence of serotonin and related indole
compounds is biochemically interesting but does not confer
psychoactive properties at the concentrations found.

## Field Identification Notes

The cap color and striations along the cap margin are distinctive
features for field identification. Stamets notes that beads of dew
often collect on the upper regions of the stem, dusted with black
spores. This observation, while subtle, can help distinguish P.
acuminatus from other grassland mushrooms in the field.

Despite belonging to a genus that includes many psychoactive
species, P. acuminatus itself is considered non-active and is
included in taxonomic treatments primarily for its close
relationship to active Panaeoli. Collectors searching for active
species should not rely on this mushroom for psychoactive effects.
For identification of active Panaeoli, the
[[psilocybin-mushroom-bluing-reaction-chemistry]] remains the
most reliable co-indicator of psilocin presence. The absence of
any bluing reaction in P. acuminatus further confirms its
non-active status.

## Ecological Role

As a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species, Panaeolus acuminatus
plays an important ecological role in nutrient cycling. Like other
Panaeoli, it helps break down herbivore dung and return nutrients
to the soil. Its presence in grassland and pasture ecosystems is
an indicator of healthy nutrient cycling processes. The species'
frequent occurrence at field-forest interfaces suggests it
benefits from the edge effects where grassland and woodland meet,
combining the open conditions of pastures with the moisture
retention of forest margins.

## Similar Species

Panaeolus acuminatus can be confused with several other Panaeoli
in the field. Panaeolus Foenisecii, the haymaker's mushroom,
is similar in stature but typically has a different cap coloration
and habitat preference. Panaeolus Castaneifolius has darker
mature gills and spores. Among active species,
[[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]] is generally larger and bruises
conspicuously bluish, a feature entirely absent in P. acuminatus.
Careful attention to spore color, bluing reaction, and
microscopic features is essential for accurate identification.
