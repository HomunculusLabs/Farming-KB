# Integrated Tropical Homestead Garden Layout

The integrated tropical homestead garden is a comprehensive permaculture design pattern from Bill Mollison's *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* that describes how every element of a tropical smallholding — house rooms, garden zones, animal systems, water flows, and tree crops — can be woven into a single productive and self-maintaining system. The layout is organized around a set of labelled elements (H, A–K, T, W, Y) that interconnect through water, nutrient, mulch, and forage pathways.

## Design Philosophy

This layout embodies core permaculture principles: every output of one element becomes an input for another; placement follows zone analysis (most-used elements closest to the house); and biological resources (forage hedges, legume trees, poultry) replace external inputs. The system is designed for humid tropical climates with distinct wet and dry seasons.

## House Elements and Their Roles

### Living Spaces (H2, H3, H4, H10, H11)

The house is oriented around a **kitchen (H10) / living area (H2) axis**. Bedrooms (H3, H4) and a study or library (H11) open onto this central corridor, keeping the household's daily activity hub connected to the garden. This layout ensures easy visual monitoring of garden zones and quick access to outdoor cooking and processing areas.

### Bathroom and Greywater System (H5)

The bathroom and toilet (H5) serve as a **greywater distribution node**:
- **Shower water** is directed to the wet garden (A) or used as mulch forage.
- **Toilet and sink water** always flows to the dry forage patch (C).
- **Handbasin water** is routed to banana or papaya circles (B).

This triple-path greywater design ensures that nutrient-rich waste water is productively reused without contaminating food crops — only clean water reaches edible plants in the wet patch, while more contaminated flows go to non-food forage species.

### Shadehouse (H6)

A shadehouse (H6) extends around the sides of the house, incorporating existing trees as structural supports. It provides resting spots for residents and a sheltered microclimate for shade-tolerant crops grown beneath the canopy.

### Outdoor Kitchen and Cooking (H7, H8)

- **H7**: A part-paved summer kitchen for cooking during hot weather.
- **H8**: An outdoor cooking stove and bulk-cooking stove. Ashes from both are directed to the dry forage patch (C) and garden beds, returning potassium and trace minerals to the soil.

A **spiral herb bed** is placed beside H8 for immediate access to culinary herbs during cooking.

### Mudroom and Pantry (H9)

The mudroom/pantry (H9) opens off the kitchen and includes an **outside basin for vegetable processing**. Table scraps follow one of two pathways: fed to livestock or placed below mulch in garden boxes. This closes the nutrient loop between kitchen waste and garden fertility.

### Water Catchment (T, t)

- **T**: A main tank catching roof water.
- **t**: A small tank on the animal shed.
- **Overflow** from tanks is directed to the wet garden bed (A), ensuring rainwater surplus feeds productive water-loving crops.

## Garden Elements

### A — Wet Food Patch

A shallow, earth-banked wetland area receiving **clean water** only (roof overflow, shower water). It supports:
- Aquatic food plants: kangkong, watercress, Chinese water chestnut.
- Bank plantings: yam, sweet potato, cassava — crops that thrive in the moist riparian zone.

### B — Banana and Papaya Circles

Banana circles, papaya circles, or mixed polycultures receive greywater from the **hand basin and kitchen sink**. They are mulched from the forage hedge (E). Banana circles are one of the most productive tropical garden features, combining high yield with passive greywater treatment.

### C — Dry Forage Patch

A critical nutrient-cycling element that receives **no direct food plants** — only vigorous, damp-tolerant, manure-tolerant green forage species:
- Comfrey, banna grass, lemongrass, legumes.

These are regularly cut and carried to animal pens (Y1, Y2) or used as mulch on garden beds. The patch accepts toilet/sink water and wood ashes, concentrating nutrients in fast-growing biomass that is safely cycled through animals and mulch before reaching food crops.

### D and D* — Avenue Crop Systems

Avenue crops of maize/corn are planted in **spiral or zigzag layouts** that maximize edge and diversity. These are **self-mulched intercrop systems**:
- Maize/beans, yam/beans, cassava/beans, melon/beans.

Each avenue is bordered by a productive **legume hedge** of Leucaena, Gliricidia, or Sesbania, which fixes nitrogen, provides mulch, and yields fodder.

### E — Forage-Mulch Hedge

A low, raggedly-trimmed hedge serving multiple functions:
- **Privacy screen** and **windbreak**.
- **Food** production (edible leaves, fruits).
- **Forage and mulch** source for banana circles (B) and garden beds (G).

Outside the hedge is a **dense weed barrier (W)**. Inside it, two **chicken pens (Y1 and Y2)** are fenced, allowing poultry to forage on hedge droppings and insects. **Bamboo clumps** within the hedge provide food, mulch, and material for trellis repairs.

### F — Front Yard

The front yard combines aesthetic and functional plantings:
- Traditional flowers, medicinals, scented plants, fibre plants.
- Ornamental food plants — blurring the line between ornamental and productive landscaping.

### G — Keyhole Garden Beds

Keyhole beds for preferred vegetables, especially leafy greens, are placed near the house for frequent harvesting. Features include:
- **Culinary herbs** in the nearest bed for kitchen access.
- **Spiral herb bed** beside the outdoor stove (H8).
- **Wet-area herbs** in pots under trellis in the pond area (A).
- **Fruit trees** near the house: lemon, lime, allspice, caper.
- **Seasonal vegetable crops** mulched from the hedge (E) and forage pit (C).

### H — Fukuoka-Style Grain Plot

A no-till grain plot following Masanobu Fukuoka's natural farming principles, producing grain for both people and poultry. This element reduces dependence on purchased grain while building soil health through natural succession and minimal disturbance.

### K — Palm Circle

A circle of **6–12 palms** of 1–4 species, deeply mulched with chopped fronds. In the wet season, yam and climbing vines grow through the mulch. This stacking of tree crops with ground vines maximizes vertical space.

## Tree Crop Systems

House trees are carefully selected for **light crowns** (to avoid shading gardens) and/or **productive fruits**. Specific recommendations include:
- **Avocado**: planted on mounds 1m high and 5m across in wet areas, improving drainage.
- **Citrus**, **nut trees**, **oil palm**.
- **Soft groundcover** beneath all trees to suppress weeds and retain moisture.

## Vertical Growing Systems

### House Trellis

Vine crops (chayote, kiwifruit, beans, cucurbits, grapes) grow on a trellis extending **1–15m above the roof**. Non-bearing vines are allowed over the roof for passive cooling — a practical application of vertical space for both food production and microclimate regulation.

### Bamboo Trellis and High Vines

- **Bamboo trellis** divides keyhole beds (G).
- **High vines** (granadilla, grape, kiwifruit) climb on palms and leguminous trees.
- **Shade-tolerant plants** (coffee, taro) grow beneath the trellis, completing the vertical stack from canopy to groundcover.

## Animal Integration

- **Y1 and Y2**: Fenced animal runs inside the forage hedge (E), rotated and rested to prevent overgrazing.
- **Rabbit hutches, chicken roosts, pigeon lofts**: positioned as hatched areas that catch rainwater, combining animal housing with water collection.
- Table scraps from H9 feed livestock; forage from C and E supplements their diet; manure returns to gardens.

## Key Connections Summary

| From | To | Flow |
|------|----|------|
| Roof (T) | Wet garden (A) | Clean rainwater overflow |
| Handbasin (H5) | Banana circles (B) | Greywater |
| Kitchen sink (H5) | Banana circles (B) | Greywater |
| Toilet/sink (H5) | Dry forage (C) | Contaminated greywater |
| Stove ashes (H8) | Dry forage (C), garden beds | Potassium, minerals |
| Table scraps (H9) | Livestock (Y1, Y2) or mulch boxes | Nutrients |
| Forage hedge (E) | Banana circles (B), garden beds (G) | Mulch |
| Forage patch (C) | Animal pens (Y1, Y2), garden beds | Cut forage/mulch |
| Legume hedge (D border) | Avenue crops (D) | Nitrogen fixation, mulch |
