## Spatial and Temporal Resource Partitioning

Companion plants that occupy different spatial niches can coexist with
reduced competition. Deep-rooted species such as comfrey and daikon
radish access water and nutrients from soil layers unavailable to
shallow-rooted crops like lettuce and onions. Tall plants provide shade
for shade-tolerant species. Vining crops use vertical space above
groundcover species.

Temporal partitioning involves staggering growth cycles so that plants
make their peak resource demands at different times. Fast-maturing crops
can be harvested before slow-growing perennials reach full size. Cover
crops occupy the soil during fallow periods, preventing erosion and
maintaining biological activity. Successional planting ensures continuous
production while maintaining soil cover throughout the year.
