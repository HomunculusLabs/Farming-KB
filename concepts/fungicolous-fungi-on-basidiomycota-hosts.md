# Fungicolous Fungi on Basidiomycota Hosts

Fungicolous fungi — fungi that grow on other fungi — represent a vast and poorly documented dimension of fungal biodiversity. Basidiomycota serve as hosts for an extraordinary range of fungicolous species, including ascomycetes, heterobasidiomycetes, and anamorphic fungi. These relationships span parasitism, commensalism, and antagonism, and many remain undescribed or poorly understood. This page surveys the major groups of fungicolous fungi recorded on basidiomycete hosts across several morphological guilds: aphyllophorales, clavariaceous fungi, jelly fungi, polypores, and agarics.

## Fungi on Aphyllophorales (Corticioid and Shelf-like Fungi)

Aphyllophorales — including corticioid (crust-like) fungi, stereoid fungi, and other non-gilled, non-poroid basidiomycetes — host numerous fungicolous species. Many of these associations are highly host-specific.

### Key Genera on Aphyllophorales

- **Hymenochaete** and **Hypocreopsis lichenoides** are notable fungicolous taxa recorded on aphyllophoralean hosts.
- **Cladobotryum stereicola** colonizes **Hyphodontia** and **Drostereum** species, producing characteristic conidiophores on the host hymenium.
- **Hypomyces** species are common parasites on corticioid fungi, often transforming the host morphology dramatically.
- **Denticularia limoniformis** is strictly host-specific on **Hyphodontia breviseta**, representing one of the most specialized fungicolous relationships known.
- **Arthrobotrys** species, better known as nematode-trapping fungi, have also been recorded on corticiaceous fungal hosts, suggesting a broader ecological repertoire than traditionally recognized.
- **Stilbella** species occur on various aphyllophorales, forming synnemata (erect conidiophore bundles) on host surfaces.
- **Zakatoshia hirschiopori** parasitizes **Trichaptum**, while **Zakatoshia erikssonii** is associated with **Sistotrema** species.
- **Nodulisporium cecidiogenes** induces gall-like structures on **Coniophora puteana**, a common wet-rot fungus.

## Intrahymenial Heterobasidiomycetes

A remarkable group of fungicolous fungi consists of heterobasidiomycetes (jelly fungi and their relatives) that grow **inside** the hymenia of corticioid host fungi. These intrahymenial parasites are often overlooked because they are microscopic or nearly so, embedded within the host tissue.

- **Colacogloea** species parasitize **Peniophora**, developing their own basidia within the host hymenial layer.
- **Tremella encephala** is a conspicuous brain-like parasite of **Stereum sanguinolentum**, one of the best-known fungicolous heterobasidiomycetes.
- **Tulasnella** and **Exidiopsis** species have been found living inside corticioid fungi, often detectable only through microscopic examination.
- **Platygloea** and members of **Syzygospora**/**Tremella** colonize hymenia of various aphyllophorales, producing distinctive basidial structures intermixed with host basidia.
- **Tremella mycophaga** and **Tremella simplex** are specialist parasites of **Aleurodiscus** species.

## Clavariaceous Fungi as Hosts

Clavarioid (coral) fungi are colonized by a smaller but distinct set of fungicolous species:

- **Mycogone calospora** — recorded colonizing clavariaceous basidiocarps.
- **Helminthosphaeria clavariarum** — a perithecial ascomycete specifically associated with clavariaceous fungi.

## Jelly Fungi (Auriculariales, Tremellales, Dacrymycetales) as Hosts

Jelly fungi serve as hosts for a diverse assemblage of fungicolous fungi spanning multiple phyla:

### On Tremellaceous Hosts

- **Acremonium**, **Verticillium**, and **Chalara** species are common anamorphic colonizers of **Tremella**.
- **Dactylaria** species have been recorded on **Pseudohydnum** gelatinosum.
- **Hypomyces mycophilus** parasitizes **Hirneola** (ear fungi).

### On Exidia and Related Genera

- **Hypocrea sulphurea** colonizes **Exidia** species, forming bright orange perithecia on the dark, gelatinous host.

### On Dacrymycetaceae

- **Rhizophlyctis**, a chytrid fungus, has been found on **Dacrymyces**, representing a rare cross-phylum fungicolous association (Chytridiomycota on Basidiomycota).
- **Tremella**, **Sebacina**, and **Itersonilia** species colonize **Dacrymyces**.
- **Platygloea** and **Tremella** species are recorded on the broader Dacrymycetaceae family.
- Lichenicolous fungi have even been found parasitizing lichenicolous heterobasidiomycetes, creating a three-tier parasitic chain (fungus on fungus on lichen).

## Polypores (Bracket Fungi) as Hosts

Polypores represent the single most important basidiomycete host group for fungicolous fungi, owing to their long-lived, perennial carpophores and large surface area. Table 17.5 in the source text provides a comprehensive listing.

### Hypomyces Species on Polypores

The genus **Hypomyces** dominates polypore mycoparasitism, with multiple species and their **Cladobotryum** anamorphs:

- **H. aurantius** — a widespread orange parasite producing vivid color changes on host polypores.
- **H. rosellus** — common on various bracket fungi.
- **H. semitranslucens** — named for its translucent perithecial walls.
- **H. orthosporus**, **H. odoratus**, **H. polyporinus**, **H. subiculosus**, and **H. mycophilus** round out the genus's representation on polypore hosts.

### Ascomycete Genera on Polypores

- **Cosmospora vilior**, **C. coccinea**, and **C. purtonii** — brightly colored Nectriaceae common on decaying polypore surfaces.
- **Hydropisphaera peziza** — distinctive cup-shaped perithecia on polypore substrates.
- **Pseudonectria tilachlidii** — a specialized nectriaceous species.
- **Sphaerostilbella broomeana** — forms striking synnematous sporulating structures on polypores.
- **Hypocrea pulvinata** and **H. pallida** — cushion-like stromatic fungi on bracket fungi.
- **Trichoderma polysporum** — a common Trichoderma species on polypore surfaces.
- **Sporophagomyces chrysostomus** — a specialized fungicolous ascomycete.
- **Ophiostoma polyporicola** — an ophiostomatoid fungus specifically named for its polypore association.

### Other Ascomycetes on Polypores

- **Melanospora lagenaria** — a dark-spored pyrenomycete.
- **Albertiniella polyporicola** — specifically associated with **Ganoderma** species.
- **Orbilia inflatula**, **Cistella hymeniophila**, and **Bisporella citrina** — discomycetes (cup fungi) fruiting on polypore surfaces or in hymenial tissue.
- **Eleutheromyces subulatus** — a distinctive cyphelloid fungus on polypores.
- **Endomyces polyporicola** — an ascomycete yeast-like fungus.
- **Rhinotrichella globulifera** — a hyphomycete with globose conidia on polypore surfaces.

### Mycelia Inside Perennial Carpophores

The living context (inner tissue) of perennial polypores harbors diverse mycelial fungi, often detectable only by culturing:

- **Acremonium**, **Monocillium**, **Fusarium**, **Gliocladium**, and other anamorphic genera commonly inhabit polypore trama.
- These endophytic-like fungi may influence host longevity, decay rates, and competitive interactions.

### Antagonists of Heterobasidion annosum

Biological control of the devastating forest pathogen **Heterobasidion annosum** involves fungicolous competitors:

- **Phlebiopsis gigantea** — applied commercially as a stump treatment to prevent Heterobasidion colonization.
- **Scytalidium** species — additional antagonists that compete for stump resources.

### Tremella and Exidiopsis on Polypores

- **Tremella polyporina** and **Exidiopsis opalea** are heterobasidiomycete parasites growing directly on polypore hymenophores.

### Wood-Rotting Successor Fungi

Some fungicolous relationships involve sequential colonization where one wood-rotting fungus succeeds another:

- **Antrodiella citrinella** succeeds **Fomitopsis** species on conifer wood.
- **Antrodiella hoehnelii** is associated with **Inonotus** species.
- **Buchwaldoboletus lignicola** fruits on decaying **Phaeolus schweinitzii**, representing a bolete that has become a successor of another polypore.

## Hypomyces on Agarics

The genus **Hypomyces** is also the principal fungicolous fungus on gilled mushrooms (agarics), with several highly host-specific species that dramatically transform the appearance of their hosts:

- **H. lactifluorum** — the best-known agaricolous Hypomyces, transforms **Lactarius** species (especially L. deliciosus group) into orange, lobster-colored, hard masses prized in some culinary traditions.
- **H. transformans** — parasitizes **Russula** species, converting them into distorted, colored masses.
- **H. macrosporus** and **H. banningiae** — additional species on **Lactarius** hosts.
- **H. hyalinus** — recorded on **Amanita** species.
- **H. succineus** — parasitizes **Pholiota** species.
- **H. porphyreus** — associated with **Entoloma** hosts.
- **H. tremellicola** — found on **Crepidotus** species.

## Significance and Knowledge Gaps

Fungicolous fungi on basidiomycota represent one of the most underexplored areas of mycology. Many associations are known from single collections, and host ranges remain poorly characterized. The functional ecology of most fungicolous species — whether parasitic, commensal, or saprotrophic on dying host tissue — is unresolved. Systematic surveys using both morphological and molecular methods would likely reveal hundreds of undescribed species, particularly among intrahymenial heterobasidiomycetes and endophytic mycelia within perennial carpophores. The biological control applications (e.g., *Phlebiopsis gigantea* against *Heterobasidion*) demonstrate the practical importance of understanding these fungal–fungal interactions.
