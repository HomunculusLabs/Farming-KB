## Fundamental Natural Patterns

Mollison identifies and describes several fundamental patterns found
repeatedly in nature and applicable to design:

### Branching (Dendritic) Patterns

Branching patterns appear in tree structure, river systems, blood vessels,
leaf veins, root systems, lightning, and neural networks. The branching
pattern is the solution to the universal problem of distributing a resource
(flow of water, nutrients, energy, information) from a central source to a
broad area with minimum total path length and maximum access.

In design, branching patterns inform:
- Road and path networks (main roads branch into smaller tracks)
- Water distribution systems (mains branching to laterals to drippers)
- Organizational structures (central administration branching to local units)
- Plant root [[savory-dung-behavior-and-nutrient-distribution]]

### Spiral Patterns

Spirals appear in nautilus shells, [[fukuoka-ideal-rice-plant-morphology-compact-growth-high-yield]] with maximum exposure and
access.

Design applications include:
- Herb spirals for creating multiple microclimates in small spaces
- Spiral garden layouts for efficient access to diverse plantings
- Spiral water flow patterns in ponds and treatment systems
- Spiral access paths that maximize edge and minimize walking distance

### Wave and Sine Patterns

Waves appear in ocean surfaces, sound propagation, light, seasonal cycles,
[[seasonal-planning]] using wave-like cycles of planting, growth, harvest,
  and rest
- Zoning as concentric waves of intensity from the center outward
- Succession as waves of species replacing each other over time

### Net and Mesh Patterns

Nets appear in spider webs, [[savory-community-organization-and-shared-grazing]], water
distribution, and information networks.
