# Rattan and Cane
# Permaculture Designers Manual - Climbing Palms for Craft and Construction

updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

Rattan (from the Malay "rotan") refers to approximately 600 species of climbing palms in the subfamily Calamoideae, native to [[permaculture-design-course-methodology]], rattan and other climbing palms are integrated into:
- Mixed-species [[food-forest]] understories
- Riparian buffer zones
- Community [[mollison-pdc-forest-management-products]] areas

## Species and Growth Habits

### Rattan Palms (Calamus and Daemonorops spp.)

Rattan palms are climbing palms with the following characteristics:
- Long, slender, flexible stems (canes) that climb using whip-like flagella
- Stems range from a few millimetres to over 10cm in diameter
- Can grow to lengths of over 100 metres in mature forest
- Typically found in lowland and montane [[fungal-biodiversity-tropical-forests]]
- Require support trees for climbing (forest trees, plantation trees)

### Growth Cycle

Rattan has a relatively fast [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] compared to timber trees:
- Commercial-sized canes (2-5cm diameter) can be harvested in 7-15 years
- Some species produce harvestable cane in as little as 5-7 years
- after harvest, new shoots emerge from the root system
- Sustainable management allows continuous production from established clumps

## Uses of Rattan

### Furniture and Craft

Rattan is primarily valued for its flexibility, strength, and light weight:
- Furniture frames (chairs, tables, sofas)
- Woven seating and backs
- Basketry and containers
- Walking sticks and umbrella handles
- Fishing rods and traps

### Construction

- Binding and tying in traditional construction
- Ladder rungs and handles
- Temporary structures and scaffolding
- Roofing supports in traditional architecture

### Household Items

- Mats and floor coverings
- Baskets of all sizes
- Hat and hat frames
- Kitchen implements
- Garden ties and supports

## Cane Species

### Bamboo vs. Rattan

The manual references bamboo extensively alongside other cane species. While bamboo is a grass (Poaceae) and rattan is a palm (Arecaceae), both provide similar construction and craft materials:

**Bamboo Advantages**
- Faster growth (harvestable in 3-5 years)
- Larger diameter stems
- More versatile construction material
- Easier propagation
- Wider climate range

**Rattan Advantages**
- Greater flexibility
- More suitable for weaving and bending
- Lighter weight for same strength
- Unique aesthetic qualities
- Traditional craft skills and markets
