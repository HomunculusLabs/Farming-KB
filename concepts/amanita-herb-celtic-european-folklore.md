# Amanita Muscaria in Celtic and European Folklore

In "Amanita Muscaria: Herb of Immortality," Donald E. Teeter argues that the Holy Grail legends originated not in medieval Christianity but in far older Celtic mythology, and that the archaeological record of the Beaker people provides physical evidence for a widespread Indo-European sacramental drink cult based on [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]].

## Celtic Origins of the Grail

The Grail stories are commonly assumed to be Christian in origin, with the Grail being either the cup used at the Last Supper or the cup that held Christ's blood. Teeter argues instead that these stories predate Christianity entirely and are old Celtic myths that were later adopted by the Church. The word "Grail" comes from the Celtic "Greal," meaning a brew of inspiration.

## The Beaker People

The Iron Age Celts (Irish, Welsh, Scots, Bretons) are descended from a much older group of Stone to Bronze Age cultures known collectively as the Corded Ware or Beaker People. This name derives from the earthenware drinking beakers and amphorae found in male burials. These beakers are notably large (1.1 liter), impressed with cords before firing, and were placed either in the hand of the deceased or close to it in the grave.

Other consistent burial finds include flint and copper knives, stone arrowheads, wrist guards, and perforated stone mace heads, hammerheads, and battle-axe heads -- leading some scholars to call them the "Battle-axe people."

The Beaker culture extends back to approximately 4000 BC. These people had horses, cattle, sheep, and goats, practiced agriculture, and possessed the wheel, plow, and early metallurgy. By 2000 BC they occupied all the territory the Celts would be found in 1,500 years later. Teeter believes the Beaker people are the source of the Grail myth and the ancestors of the Celtic and other European peoples.

## The Indo-European Sacred Drink

The Celtic languages belong to the Indo-European family. Teeter argues that one cultural trait common to all early Indo-European peoples is the legend of a Divine Plant of Immortality processed into a Sacred Drink of Inspiration. This drink and the plant that produced it are known as Ambrose (ambrosia), Nectar, and Soma -- words found in both Greek and early Sanskrit.

The knowledge of the Soma ceremony comes from the [[amanita-muscaria-herb-of-immortality-soma-hypothesis]], a book of hymns composed by Seers and Sages, one of the largest and oldest texts in any Indo-European language. Book 9 of the Rig Veda is entirely about Soma, and Soma is mentioned in almost every hymn in the other books. The hymns were sung as the Soma was being prepared and were an integral part of the ceremony.

## Archaeological Parallels: Beaker Burials and Soma Cups

After the Soma was prepared, it was poured into wooden cups for consumption. These cups were owned by individuals who carried them to their graves. A man's wooden Soma cup was placed in his burial within easy reach. When burial was by cremation, the cup was placed on the funeral pyre. Large wooden and earthenware bowls used in processing the Soma would also contain the living Immortal Plant.

Teeter identifies a striking parallel: the beakers in Beaker burials correspond to individual Soma cups, while the amphorae correspond to the large Soma processing bowls. Both are representations of the Magic Cauldrons, Pots, and Bowls found throughout Indo-European mythology.

## The Universal Ancient Wine

Teeter argues that there was nothing more sacred in the ancient world than their wine, which was "the one and only god worshipped by the entire ancient world; consumed by Greek, Roman, Jewish, Armenian, Celtic, Persian, Babylonian, etc." This universal sacred wine, he contends, was not alcoholic fermentation but rather water-based extracts of [[amanita-muscaria-ancient-wine-question]].

## The Grail: Ancient Prehistory

The Grail has had hundreds of books written about it, yet not one book, until Teeter's, could prove what the Grail really is, what it does, or show how to make one. The Grail is described as "a most precious, highly desired object of Knightly Quests, the Holy chalice that held Christ's Blood, and became magical as a result, a miraculous, mysterious, magical container of the Divine." Legends and stories of the Grail "echo down through the Ages, whispering to us, of Power and Wisdom, living in a Cup! Stories of Wondrous Cauldrons and Magic Bowls whose drink of Healing, Poetry, and Inspiration never runs out!"

The secret of the Grail is ancient -- "older than King Arthur, older than Christianity, far older than civilization itself." Teeter argues it is "probably one of the oldest magic tricks known to man, but was absolutely baffling to early man when he performed it. This unexplainable Natural Magic astounded even the Wisest of Magicians." The creation of a Grail involved "the practice of the highest Magic known to early humanity": a sacred, immortal, hallucinogenic plant was crushed and consumed as a beverage; since the plant is immortal, it comes back to life and lives in a suitable container, perhaps forever.

## The Celtic Cauldrons and Magic Cups in Detail

The people known as the modern Celts -- Irish, Welsh, Scots, and Bretons -- before the rise of the Roman Empire occupied a large part of Northern Europe, from Hungary through Germany and France to Ireland, Spain, and Italy. The Iron Age Celts descended from the Corded Ware or Beaker People, named for the earthenware drinking beakers and amphorae found in male burials. These beakers are notably large (1.1 liter), impressed with cords before firing, and were placed either in the hand of the deceased or close to it.

Other consistent burial finds included flint and copper knives, stone arrowheads, wrist guards, and perforated stone mace heads, hammerheads, and battle-axe heads -- leading some scholars to call them the "Battle-axe people." The Beaker culture extends back to approximately 4000 BC. They had horses, cattle, sheep, goats, the wheel, plow, and early metallurgy. By 2000 BC they occupied all the territory the Celts would be found in 1,500 years later.

We will never know what language the Beaker people spoke -- they are simply too far away in time and left no known writings. But the Celtic languages are Indo-European, and Teeter argues that if certain cultural traits and legends are common among descendants of the earliest Indo-European people, those traits originated with the original Proto-Indo-European speakers.

## The Magic Cauldrons of Indo-European Myth

The amphorae in the Beaker burials correspond to the large Soma processing bowls used in the Vedic ceremony, while the beakers correspond to individual Soma cups. Both are "representations of the Magic Cauldrons, Pots and Bowls, found in all Indo-European Myth." After the Soma was prepared and poured into wooden cups, these cups were owned by individuals who carried them to their graves. A man's wooden Soma cup was placed in his burial within easy reach -- "strikingly similar to the Beaker burials." When burial was by cremation, the cup was placed on the funeral pyre to accompany him into the afterlife. Large wooden and earthenware bowls used in processing the Soma would also contain the living Immortal Plant.

## Conclusions: The Grail as Natural Magic

The Corded Ware, Beaker culture and the Grail religion lasted more than 2500 years, eventually spreading from east of Moscow to Scandinavia, Scotland, Ireland, England, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Portugal, and North Africa -- "an ancestral culture of all of Europe and beyond." The theme of the Beaker burials is echoed in the Grail myths: the combination of warriors and magic vessels are the two main elements common to both.

There are two ancient claims for the Grail of special interest: first, the Grail could heal any non-fatal wound and cure any illness; second, those who possessed the ancient Grail had extremely long life spans. These attributes match Amanita muscaria in the ancient legends and its demonstrated antibiotic properties.

Teeter concludes: "The Amanita Muscaria as the Ambrose, Amarta, Amrita, the Nek-tar of the Gods is an Indo-European heritage encoded in our languages, religions, myths, and folklore. This immortal plant and its accompanying Magic vessel are the source of ancient legend and Myth." The Grail when recreated with Amanita muscaria "matches the Grail of ancient legends perfectly" -- from being a never-ending source of wisdom and inspiration to having the spicy smell described in Grail legend. "It is very unlikely if there is any other explanation for these ancient legends except Amanita Muscaria and its natural ability to do the seemingly impossible."

## The Magic Beaker in Burial Context

The Beaker burials exist across Europe from Moscow to North Africa, all sharing common elements: weapons (copper daggers, stone arrowheads), male burial orientation, and the cord-impressed "Magic Beaker." Typical Beaker burials from England dating to 2000 BC contain a male skeleton with weapons placed alongside the cord-impressed drinking vessel -- a pattern consistent with the Vedic practice of burying a man with his Soma cup. This continent-wide pattern of warrior burials with sacred drinking vessels represents the oldest archaeological evidence for the sacramental drink cult that Teeter traces from the Indo-European homeland to the Grail legends of medieval Europe.

## The Living Vessel: Natural Magic of the Grail

When Amanita muscaria is used in the Soma ceremony, the mushrooms are "hydrated and pressed or pounded three times" and "shattered into fragments." Though the mushrooms are "basically gone," they have "released billions of spores and countless microscopic fragments" into the fluid -- "every drop of this fluid is alive with Amanita Muscaria." When this fluid penetrates the porous walls of a wooden or terra cotta vessel, it carries spores and fragments with it. With the addition of food (barley gruel, milk, honey), the fungus receives energy to grow: "in approximately three days the mycelium will be clearly visible to the human eye, and very much resembles white or gray sheep's wool or an old man's beard."

Once established, the vessel becomes "in effect a living vessel, the Herb of Immortality now has a new home and a new symbiotic relationship; one with the man whose cup it now lives in." The Amanita muscaria fungus receives food and water, and in return the human "receives a dose of effective antibiotics and a very unique, profound spiritual experience." Teeter concludes: "It is this symbiosis that makes the Holy Grail truly 'Holy,' a Living Vessel that transforms the simple shared food and water into a Communion meal with a Living God."

## The Grail's Never-Ending Supply

One of the most celebrated attributes of the Grail in legend is its inexhaustible supply of food and drink. Teeter explains this through the living vessel technology: the ancient technology of the living vessel "can be used to make the Living Bread of the Bible." By "simply changing the shape of a living vessel from the Grail's vat, pitcher or cup to a shallow bowl, changing the food to water ratio from the Grail's mostly water, to a mostly food ratio, and adding gentle heat," cooked grain placed in a shallow living vessel becomes colonized by the Amanita muscaria mycelium. This grain, dried at low heat, can be used "as if it were the dried Amanita Muscaria mushroom." The living vessel "can then be refilled with new boiled grain as soon as the batch is removed" and can "at the same time create even more magic living vessels, which can produce still more living bread."

From a single living vessel, Teeter reports producing "several pounds of dried living bread and at least a dozen small living vessels" -- the technological basis for the Grail legend's claim of never-ending sustenance.

## The Names of the Sacred Plant

The sacred plant that created the Grail was known by many names across Indo-European cultures. Soma means "the pressed one," from the crushing of the plant in preparation. Other ancient names include Ambrose (not-mortal) and Nek-tar (death-overcomer), both "obviously based on the plant's resurrection and immortality." One name deserves special mention: Maga (the Great Gift), which is "the root of the modern words magic, magi, and magician." The Celtic word "Greal" (a brew of inspiration) from which "Grail" derives belongs to this same family of names -- all referring to the same immortal sacramental plant and its transformative effects.
