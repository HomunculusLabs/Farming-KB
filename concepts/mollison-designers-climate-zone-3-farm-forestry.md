# Zone 3 - Farm Forestry and Broadacre Systems

Zone 3 in the [[permaculture-design]] framework is the broadacre production
zone. Visited weekly to monthly, it contains main field crops, pastures,
orchards, firewood lots, large livestock (sheep, cattle), dams, and windbreaks.
Mollison designed Zone 3 for commercial-scale production while maintaining
ecological integrity. The zone operates at a scale requiring machinery or
larger animal power but still follows permaculture principles of stacking
functions and closed nutrient loops.

## Design Philosophy

Zone 3 balances productivity with low maintenance. Mollison argues that
conventional monoculture farming is the least efficient use of land. Instead,
Zone 3 integrates multiple yields from the same area: crops, livestock,
timber, water, and wildlife habitat. The design relies on establishing
permanent or semi-permanent systems (pastures, tree crops) rather than annual
monocultures requiring constant tillage and input. See [[holmgren-food-forests-agroforestry]] for
the broader context.

## Pasture and Grazing Systems

Mollison advocates managed rotational grazing as the primary pasture system.
Key principles include:
- High stock density for short periods, followed by long recovery periods
- Moving stock frequently (daily to weekly) using electric fencing
- Rest periods long enough for grass recovery (30-90 days depending on
  season)
- Diverse pasture species including legumes, deep-rooted herbs, and grasses

Pasture species mixes include clovers (white, red, subterranean), lucerne,
chicory, plantain, cocksfoot, and native grasses adapted to local conditions.
Diversity in pasture composition improves animal nutrition, extends the
growing season, and builds soil carbon. This connects to holistic grazing
and soil building practices.

## Livestock Integration

**Sheep** are Mollison's preferred Zone 3 livestock for many climates. They
are hardy, reproduce quickly, graze close to the ground, and can be managed
with minimal infrastructure. Sheep integrate well with tree crops -- they
graze under established orchards, controlling understory growth while
fertilizing trees.

**Cattle** require more infrastructure (fencing, water, yards) but produce
significant manure and can utilize coarser forage. Mollison recommends
smaller breeds (Dexter, Jersey) for smaller properties. Cattle in rotational
systems build soil rapidly through dung deposition and trampling of residual
vegetation.

**Pigs** can be integrated for pasture renovation -- their rooting behavior
prepares land for planting. Mollison recommends confining pigs to specific
areas using electric fencing and moving them regularly to prevent over-
disturbance.

## Main Crop Systems

Zone 3 crops are typically staple foods and animal feeds: grains, legumes,
root crops, and oilseeds. Mollison recommends:
- Alley cropping: rows of trees (nut, timber, or fodder) with crops in
  between
- Pasture cropping: direct-drilling annual crops into living perennial
  pasture
- Strip cropping: alternating strips of different crops to reduce pest and
  disease spread
- Cover cropping and green manuring between cash crops

Perennial staple crops like chestnuts, acorns, and edible bamboos reduce the
need for annual tillage. See perennial agriculture for expanded
discussion.

## Orchard Systems in Zone 3

Zone 3 orchards are larger-scale than Zone 2 food forests. They may include:
- Commercial nut orchards (walnut, chestnut, pecan, hazelnut, macadamia)
- Dried fruit orchards (fig, persimmon, prune, date)
- Olive groves for oil production
- Citrus in suitable climates

These orchards are typically planted in wide rows with inter-row grazing or
cover crops. Windbreak protection is essential -- see [[mollison-windbreak-design]].
Nitrogen-fixing understory trees (tagasaste, alder, carob) between crop trees
reduce fertilizer needs.

## Firewood and Timber Production

Mollison insists that every property should produce its own firewood. Zone 3
firewood plantings use coppicing or pollarding systems: fast-growing species
harvested on 5-15 year rotations that regrow from the cut stump. Suitable
species include willow, poplar, eucalyptus, black locust, and oak. Coppice
blocks are divided into equal sections harvested in rotation, ensuring a
constant supply of seasoned wood.

Timber plantings for construction, fencing, and craft wood are long-term
investments (30-100 year rotations). Mollison recommends mixed-species timber
belts rather than monoculture plantations for resilience against pests and
disease. This connects to [[farm-forestry-design-mollison]] principles.

## Dams and Water Storage

Zone 3 is the primary location for large-scale water storage. Dams are sited
in valleys using [[keyline-design]] principles -- placed at the highest
practical point in the landscape to enable gravity-fed distribution to lower
zones. Mollison outlines design criteria:
- Catchment area must be sufficient to fill the dam
- Dam walls must be properly compacted with clay cores
- Spillways must handle extreme rainfall events safely
- Dam depth should exceed surface area to reduce evaporation

Dams serve multiple functions: irrigation, stock water, fire fighting,
aquaculture, wildlife habitat, and recreation. Their placement integrates with
[[permaculture-slope-analysis-and-reading]] and [[zone-and-sector-analysis]] considerations.

## Windbreaks and Shelterbelts

Zone 3 windbreaks protect crops, livestock, and buildings from wind damage
and desiccation. Mollison recommends multi-row shelterbelts with:
- Windward row: dense, low-growing conifers or dense broadleaves
- Center rows: tall timber species for maximum height
- Leeward row: medium-height deciduous species allowing winter sun through

Windbreak effectiveness extends 10-15 times the height of the tallest trees.
Species selection depends on climate -- see [[mollison-windbreak-design]] for regional
recommendations.

## Fencing, Infrastructure and Nutrient Cycling

Zone 3 fencing must be stock-proof, durable, with permanent boundary fencing
and electric internal subdivision for rotational grazing. Laneways connect
paddocks to handling facilities, water points, and the Zone 2/3 boundary.
Zone 3 aims for closed nutrient cycles: livestock manure returned to pasture,
crop residues composted, legumes fixing nitrogen, deep-rooted plants mining
subsoil, and water carrying nutrients from upper to lower zones. External
inputs are minimized through careful biological design.
