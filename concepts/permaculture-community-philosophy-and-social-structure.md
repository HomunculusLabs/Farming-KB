## Permaculture Community Philosophy and Social Structure

Mollison concludes Permaculture Two with a vision for community-scale social transformation through [[permaculture-design]], [[appropriate-technology]], and a philosophy of individual responsibility for collective survival.

## The Problem Statement

"The applied theories of politics, economics and industry have made a sick society; it is time for new approaches."

Mollison identifies systemic failures:
- **Obesity in the west** balanced by **famine in the third world**
- **Petrol is running out**, yet freeways are still being built
- **A society which spends as much on the arms race in one hour as it spends on famine relief in a year must inevitably perish from war and famine**
- "The whole world is disenfranchised, with satellites spying on us from above, and multiple Idi Amins at ground level"

## The Central Thesis

"I see no other solution (political, economic) to the problems of man than the formation of small responsible communities involved in permaculture and appropriate technology, for both individual and competitive enterprise and 'free' energy have failed us."

### Energy Integration
"Permaculture both conserves and generates the fuel energies of transport systems, and would enable any community to exist comfortably on very restricted land areas." Supplemented with methane, alcohol fuels, dry distillation, and wind/wave/water/solar energies, "it would provide the basis of a sustainable and regionalized society."

### The Self-Sufficient Dwelling
"Every dwelling needs a tank, dry toilet, a small glasshouse, an insulated space and a garden." Nobody needs the flush toilet and a monopoly on political and economic power.

## The Philosophy of Individual Responsibility

"Against such universal insanity the only response is to gather together a few friends and commence to build the alternative, on a philosophy of individual responsibility for community survival."

### Key Principles
1. **World citizenship**: Membership of Spaceship Earth
2. **Global communication and education**
3. **Aid to others**: To establish self-reliance, not create dependence
4. **Self-reliance** in ourselves and our group (village, tribe, community)
5. **Care of the earth**
6. **No waste products**: Hence no "unemployed"
7. **Most sophisticated environmental principles**
8. **Moratorium on freeways, arms, centralized power, and export of energy sources** not used in accordance with these principles
9. **Gradual removal of all tariffs, passports, visas and impediments to travel**
10. **Open media** devoted to spreading these principles

## Intentional Community Design

### Size
"All groups/neighbourhoods limited in size from 300–3,000 (no more or less)."

### Structure
- **At least 5 locations for each group**, one to be an "overseas" centre
- Groups never to have a fixed boundary
- **Community ownership of land and public resources** with life leases on homes and gardens
- "The cruel myth of 'ownership' of resources and people is where we have lost touch with reality"

### Economic Model
- Regional groups choose specialized 'trade' manufactures suited to local resources, skills, inclinations, and markets
- Programme to make every house and town self-sufficient
- Teams from each stabilized area advance into disaster areas and the third world
- Global federations of specialized groups in travel and trade, exchange of skills

## The Gift Relationship

"Perhaps the greatest truth is that we can only own the resources we give to others." Mollison cites Titmuss (The Gift Relationship, 1970): "A world totally governed by private market principles ultimately deprives man of the 'freedom to give', the right to behave altruistically."

## The Organic Farming Movement

"The organic farming movement is part of the new revolution in self-sufficiency in country and town, as the Down to Earth Movement started by Jim Cairns and Juni Morosi is the new synthesis of alternatives in lifestyles."

## The Post-Industrial Opportunity

"The global village community is in the throes of its formative years, and should produce over the next decade, the most remarkable revolution in thought, values, and technology that has yet been evolved."

"I believe that the days of centralized power are numbered, and that a re-tribalization of society is an inevitable, if sometimes painful process."

### The Information Advantage
"We live in the post-industrial world, and have an immense amount of sophisticated information and technology which enables us to exchange information while living in a village situation. Permaculture is a basic technique for such an evolution, and like all biological, wholistic systems, is within the reach of everyman."

## Definition of "Enough"

"'Enough' is a warm place, good nutrition (hence, health), plenty of information, many friends, a meaningful task or two and reliance on the group, hence absolute security."

"Who needs insurance, spy satellites, or any of those expensive waste products of insecurity? Multinationals, like national pride, are a result of greed and the need to hold what you have."

## The Call to Action

"Not all of us are, can, or need to be, farmers and gardeners. However everyone has skills and strengths to offer and may form or join ecology parties or local action groups to change the politics of our local and state governments, to demand the use of public lands on behalf of landless people, and to join internationally to divert resources from waste and destruction to conservation and construction."

## Permaculture Institute (Tagari)

Mollison established the Permaculture Institute at Stanley, Tasmania:
- **78 acres** of coastal swamp and dryland
- Devoted to demonstrating techniques of planting and design
- Still-pond culture as a feature
- Species of sub-tropical to cool temperate range (no frosts)
- Research and resource for species for southern Australia
- Partly self-supporting through admission fees, schools, seed/plant sales
- Commercial crops: nuts, some fruits, water plants, aquatic birds/poultry

### Species Index
Preparing a looseleaf species index of useful plants and animals. First 1,000 species targeted. Species described according to standard sheets, covering all climates and niches. Special attention to design potential, placement, use, and processing.

### Standard Designs
Supplied as looseleaf sheets, updated regularly. Categories include:
- **Landscape** (Series L): Rock dome planting, tidal flat ponds, flatland dam
- **Pests and Problems** (Series P): [[backswimmer-mosquito-control-mollison]], [[berry-production-and-blackberry-control]], fox prevention, rabbit presence
- **Annual Garden** (Series A): Tomato/asparagus polyculture, herb spiral, potato production
- **Marketing and Management** (Series M): Farm-link, wayside marketing, self-pick sales
- **Techniques** (Series T): Pruning, sewage disposal, broadscale planting, tyre uses, fuel from plants
- **Structures** (Series S): Trellis, rock dome seepage, shade house, attached glasshouse
- **Species Lists** (Series D): [[free-range-poultry-forage-design-mollison]], hedgerow species, cattle forage, pig forage, seacoast species
- **Urban Strategies** (Series U): Contract cropping, dispersed tree crops, dispersed livestock, public allotments

### Permaculture Quarterly
Journal listing requests, supplies, exchange news and views, covering permaculture, appropriate technology, and community.
