# Walnut Species for Permaculture

## Overview

The genus *Juglans* (family Juglandaceae) comprises approximately 15 species
distributed widely across Europe, China, Japan, and the Americas. Walnuts are
among the most valuable multipurpose trees [[ginseng-cultivation-and-medicinal-properties]], and ecological
functions. Several species combine rapid growth with premium wood and edible
crops, making them strong candidates for food-forest integration, alley
cropping, and broadacre agroforestry.

## Species Descriptions

### *Juglans regia* — Persian / English Walnut

Slow-growing, broad-crowned tree to 24 m. The most widely cultivated species,
with recorded yields of 150–200 kg of nuts per tree. Eaten raw and in every
conceivable culinary form — jams from unripe fruits, vinegar/syrup preserves,
brandy, and pressed cooking oil. Leaves brewed as a stomach tonic tea. Timber
is the most sought-after of all walnuts: decorative grain, highly durable,
takes a high polish, resistant to splitting. Preferred for prestige car trim
and prized for gun butts due to resilience.

### *Juglans nigra* — Eastern Black Walnut
