## psychedelic crisis response protocol

## Overview

A psychedelic crisis (commonly called a "[[cervantes-grow-room-security-law-enforcement]] response in many jurisdictions)
- Send someone to meet the ambulance; lock away all substances before responders arrive
- Be honest with medical staff about what was taken, how much, and when

## What NOT to Do During a Crisis

- **Do not restrain** unless they are an immediate danger. Restraint dramatically escalates panic.
- **Do not argue with their reality** even if perceptions seem irrational. The experience is real to them.
- **Do not force actions** -- eat, move, stop crying, open eyes. Offer choices instead.
- **Do not leave them alone** unless absolutely necessary and only briefly.
- **Do not give additional substances** to "calm them down" -- no alcohol, benzos, or antipsychotics unless prescribed for this purpose.
- **Do not film or photograph** without clear, prior consent.
- **Do not shame or judge** them for having a difficult experience.

## Trip Sitting Principles

A trip sitter is a sober person who stays present to provide safety and support:

- **Stay sober**: No alcohol or substances. You need full cognitive function.
- **Be prepared**: Know the substance, dose, expected duration, and common effects.
- **Be present but not intrusive**: Sit nearby, check in periodically, but do not direct the experience.
- **Hold space**: Be a calm, steady anchor, not a guide or controller.
- **Know your limits**: If the situation exceeds your ability, call for support.

For preparation, see [[psychedelic-integration]], especially if trauma surfaced.
