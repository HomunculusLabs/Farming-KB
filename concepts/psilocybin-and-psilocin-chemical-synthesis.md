# Psilocybin and Psilocin Chemical Synthesis

## Overview

Psilocin and [[tryptamine-chemistry-psilocin-and-psilocybin-synthesis]] have been regulated by the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law in Japan. The popularity of magic mushrooms among young people prompted this regulation.

Identification through morphological and microscopic analysis is difficult without expertise, making chromatographic methods (TLC, GC, HPLC) essential. These methods require pure reference standards, creating demand for reliable synthetic routes.

## Synthetic Route to Psilocin

The Shirota synthesis starts from commercially available 4-hydroxyindole and proceeds through four steps without requiring chromatographic purification at any stage.

### Step 1: Protection of the Hydroxyl Group

4-Hydroxyindole is protected by acetylation with acetic anhydride in pyridine and dichloromethane at 0 degrees Celsius warming to room temperature.

After aqueous workup and crystallisation, 4-acetylindole is obtained as ivory white crystals. [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-formation-oxalyl-chloride-indole]] in anhydrous diethyl ether at 0 degrees Celsius, followed by addition of n-hexane and storage at minus 20 degrees Celsius overnight.

The oxalyl chloride adduct precipitates as yellow crystals, which are collected by filtration. This installs the glyoxyl group at the reactive 3-position of the indole ring.

### Step 3: Amidation to Form the Glyoxalylamide

The oxalyl adduct is dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran in an ice bath and reacted with dimethylamine solution and pyridine. The product is collected by filtration as an ivory white crystalline powder in over 80% yield.

### Step 4: Reduction to Psilocin

Lithium aluminum hydride reduction in tetrahydrofuran under argon atmosphere, followed by reflux for 2 hours, produces psilocin in over 85% yield as white crystals.

Melting point: 169 to 174 degrees Celsius with decomposition, matching literature values. The overall yield from 4-hydroxyindole exceeds 59% without chromatography.

### The Phosphorylation Challenge

Converting psilocin to its 4-O-phosphate ester proved considerably more difficult than anticipated. Most standard phosphorylation methods failed to consume psilocin, reflecting the low nucleophilicity of the phenolic oxygen.

Two methods showed promise: the phosphoryl iodide method (using tribenzyl phosphite, iodine, and DMAP) and the pyrophosphate method (using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-butyllithium). The pyrophosphate method was selected for easy handling and reagent stability.

### Key Discovery: Zwitterionic Intermediate

The phosphorylation produced a dibenzyl-protected [[shirota-catalytic-hydrogenolysis-debenzylation-psilocybin]] using 10% palladium on carbon under hydrogen atmosphere in methanol yielded [[psilocybin]] as white needle crystals.

Melting point: 190 to 198 degrees Celsius. Isolated yield from psilocin exceeded 72% on gram scale, a dramatic improvement over previously reported yields of 20% and 47% that required anion-exchange chromatography.
