# Climate and Microclimate Analysis

Understanding climate is perhaps the most important thing for [[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]]. All deserts in the world fall within a latitude range that puts them near the equator.
- **Land mass** determines wind patterns and humidity.
- **Altitude** affects [[soil-temperature-and-oxygen]] concentrations. All mountain tops tend to have similar climates because of their altitude.

Areas with similar factors will have similar climates. For example, areas at similar latitudes with similar land masses will share temperature and rainfall patterns.

## Basic Climate Types

Every biome has a unique climate, but climatologists have identified basic climate types:

- **Arid** - dry and hot
- **Mediterranean** - mild winters and dry hot summers
- **Polar** - cold and dry all year round
- **Temperate** - cold winters and mild summers with varying rainy seasons
- **Tropical** - hot and wet all year round
- **Tundra** - very cold all year

## Biomes

Within a climatic zone there will be distinct biomes. A biome is a large area with a unique ecology, defined for the most part by their plant and animal types. A savannah is dominated by grasses with thin forests; an oak savannah is grasslands with oak trees spread among them. Rain forests are another biome type.

Biomes are created by climate, soil composition, ground water, migration histories and many other factors. They are not necessarily static -- they can change and evolve over time, though it may take decades, centuries or millennia. Human interventions can increase this evolution to an unhealthy rate.

Anthropogenic biomes are biomes created by humans -- wheat fields, suburbs, and cities could all be considered anthropogenic biomes.

## Ecotones and the Edge Effect

An ecotone is a "clash" zone where two or more biomes come together and interact. Ecotones will have species from both biomes, plus possibly unique plants and animals adaptable to both. Ecotones increase the edge effect -- the edge is the physical line where two biomes (or any two different conditions) meet. The edge effect always produces greater variety of bio-diversity and ecological activity. The greater the edge, the greater the bio-diversity.

Permaculture prefers curvy edges as it increases the surface area for bio-diversity. In garden or pond design it is good to increase the edge effect, but in designing a home increasing the edge effect of walls would be bad because the home would bleed more heat.

## Microclimates

Micro-climates are smaller climatic niches in your local area. If you live in a snowy climate, have you ever noticed a patch of snow that persists for a month or so after the rest has melted? That is a micro-climate with a lower temperature. There is no minimum or maximum size that makes something a micro-climate. Valleys have micro-climates, mountains and hills cast micro-climates, a rock can generate a micro-climate, a ditch can have a micro-climate.

All houses and buildings create micro-climates. If a house is oriented right there may be a patch of land that never gets direct sun because the house always shades it, and the house may block winds too. There is nothing inherently good or bad about micro-climates -- they just are. However a good Permaculture Designer will design innovative and productive ways to take advantage of all the available microclimates, and even design micro-climates for specific purposes.

Perhaps there is a type of plant you want to put in your design that would not survive in your local climate. A Permaculture Designer could design structures and landscaping to create a micro-climate that supports that particular, otherwise inappropriate, plant.

## Riparian Zones

Riparian zones are a specific type of ecology formed along rivers and streams. Because of the presence of water, riparian habitats are robust ecologies with much greater concentrations of trees, plants and wildlife than the surrounding ecology. They form intense microclimates usually quite distinct from their surrounding environments, providing niches for unique plants and animals. Riparian habitats can exist in any type of climate -- they only require the presence of a surface river or stream.
