# 5-MeO-DIPT Compound Profile

5-MeO-DIPT (N,N-diisopropyl-5-methoxytryptamine) is a synthetic tryptamine first synthesized by Alexander Shulgin. In TIHKAL it is compound #37. Street names include "Foxy" and "Foxy Methoxy." It was briefly sold legally in the US before being emergency-scheduled in 2003. The compound was co-developed with Michael Carter in England, with whom Shulgin had previously published on [[2c-b-compound-profile]] in 1981.

## Basic Information
- **Full name**: 5-Methoxy-N,N-diisopropyltryptamine
- **CAS**: Unavailable
- **Molecular weight**: 274.4 g/mol
- **Category**: Tryptamine, 5-MeO-substituted
- **Legal status**: Schedule I in US (since 2004), Class A in UK, controlled in many countries

## Dosage
- **Threshold:** 2-4 mg
- **Light:** 5-8 mg
- **Common:** 8-12 mg (Shulgin's tested range: 6-12 mg)
- **Strong:** 12-18 mg
- **Heavy:** 18+ mg
- **Duration:** 4-8 hours
- **Route:** Oral (most common)

## Effects

### Physical
- Nausea (especially at onset; one 12 mg report noted dramatic increase in peristalsis)
- Pupil dilation
- Increased heart rate and sense of hypertension at higher doses
- Body temperature changes
- Muscle tension, gluteal spasms reported at 12 mg
- Reduced appetite

### Mental
- Mild visual effects (less than DMT or psilocybin; "colors on the edges of the wiggles of the eye")
- Enhanced colors and pattern recognition
- Mood elevation, euphoria
- Enhanced tactile sensitivity
- Musical enhancement — qualitatively different from 5-MeO-DIPT Compound Profile. With DIPT, there was physical harmonic distortion of sounds. With 5-MeO-DIPT, the distortions involved musical character and interpretation rather than harmonic structure
- Erotic enhancement noted at lower doses; one 7 mg report called it a "short term aphrodisiac" that "leaves 2C-B in the dust"
- Some reports of confusion, paranoia, or "unusable" altered states at higher doses
- Sense of vibrations and stillness reported at lower doses

### Duration Timeline
- **Onset**: 20-60 minutes
- **Come up**: 30-60 minutes (one 12 mg report shot up to +++ "in a very LSD-like manner, without the visuals")
- **Peak**: 1-3 hours
- **Plateau**: 2-4 hours
- **Come down**: 1-2 hours (rapid drop-off noted)
- **After effects**: Clean residual feeling, excellent sleep reported

## Qualitative Reports (from TIHKAL)

At 6 mg: "Each weed had special significance... a car drove by with sitar music playing on its radio! Why not." Effects present in 20 minutes, back to baseline in 3-4 hours.

At 7 mg: "In one hour I was in a marvelous, sexy place. Everything was shaded with eroticism. Sex was explosive."

At 10 mg: "A definite sense-distorter. I am not completely sure I like it." Faces appeared "quite serious, and not completely friendly."

At 12 mg: "Erotic world was fantastic, explosive, almost scary. Rapid drop-off, and by the fourth hour I am clear of any effects." Another 12 mg report described the experience as "awful" with significant body load and muscle spasms.

## Synthesis

TIHKAL describes synthesis from 5-methoxytryptamine in sulfolane (tetramethylenesulfone) with diisopropylethylamine and 2-iodopropane, heated on steam bath for 3 hours then stirred 16 hours. Product distilled at KugelRohr (140-150°C at 0.01 mm/Hg), yielding a viscous white oil. Hydrochloride salt crystallized from IPA, mp 181-182°C (17% yield). IR: 731, 809, 826, 931, 1035, 1064, NH at 3165 cm⁻¹. See [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]].

## Safety Notes
- Nausea is common at onset, especially with food in stomach
- Body load and muscle spasms reported at higher doses (12+ mg)
- Some users report anxiety, paranoia, or "unusable" altered states at higher doses
- Combining with MAOIs may dangerously increase potency
- Long-term effects unknown
- Variable experience quality: some found it "outstanding" while others found it "awful" at the same dose
- Fast-acting with rapid drop-off — shorter than most psychedelics

## Comparison with Related Compounds
- Less visual than [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]]
- More body-focused than psilocybin
- Longer duration than [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]]
- Some similarities to MDMA in warmth and tactile enhancement
- Musical distortion differs from 5-MeO-DIPT Compound Profile (interpretation vs. harmonic)
- The diisopropyl substitution pattern (two isopropyl groups on the basic nitrogen) appears to be the key structural feature shared with DIPT that produces both the erotic enhancement and the auditory distortions

## Historical Context

The compound was first published in 1981 by Shulgin and Michael Carter. After initial correspondence and sample exchange, Carter became unreachable — letters returned as undeliverable with no forwarding address. Shulgin eventually tracked down 20 "Michael F. Carter" listings through the London telephone company but received no response. The paper was published using Shulgin's address for both authors. Carter's fate remains unknown; Shulgin included a note in TIHKAL hoping Carter might read it.
