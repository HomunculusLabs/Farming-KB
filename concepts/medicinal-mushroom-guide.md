# Medicinal Mushroom Guide

Comprehensive guide to medicinal mushrooms: active compounds, health benefits, preparations, dosing, and cultivation. Draws primarily from peer-reviewed cancer immunotherapy literature.

Related: [[mushroom-species-guide]], [[herbal-medicine]], [[fungal-ecology]]

## Key Active Compounds

- **Beta-glucans (BG)**: (1,3)-(1,6)-linked polysaccharides. Primary immunomodulators. Found in all medicinal mushrooms. Differ by molecular weight and branching, which determines bioactivity.
- **Lentinan**: Beta-glucan specific to shiitake (Lentinula edodes). FDA-approved as adjunct cancer therapy in Japan.
- **PSK (Polysaccharide-K)**: Protein-bound polysaccharide from turkey tail (Trametes versicolor). Approved anticancer drug in Japan since 1977. Brand name Krestin.
- **PSP (Polysaccharide-Peptide)**: From Trametes versicolor. Similar to PSK but with different protein binding. Approved in China.
- **Ganoderic acids**: Triterpenoids from [[reishi-cultivation|reishi]] (Ganoderma lucidum). Anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, anticancer via apoptosis induction.
- **Hericenones and erinacines**: Compounds from lion's mane (Hericium erinaceus). Stimulate NGF (nerve growth factor) synthesis. Erinacines in mycelium; hericenones in fruiting body.
- **Cordycepin (3'-deoxyadenosine)**: Nucleoside analog from Cordyceps militaris. Anti-proliferative, immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory.
- **Ergothioneine**: Amino acid antioxidant found in all mushrooms. Protects against oxidative stress. High concentrations in shiitake, oyster, porcini.

## Species Profiles

### Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)

- **Active compounds**: Ganoderic acids (triterpenes), beta-glucans, ganodermanontriol, lucidenic acids
- **Health benefits**: Potent immunomodulation; anti-tumor via apoptosis induction and anti-angiogenesis; anti-inflammatory; hepatoprotective; cardioprotective (lowers blood pressure and cholesterol); adaptogenic (reduces fatigue, improves sleep); neuroprotective
- **Preparations**: Dual extraction essential -- hot water for beta-glucans, ethanol for triterpenes. Decoction (tea): simmer dried slices 30-60 min. Tincture: 1:2 dual extract, 1-3 mL/day. Powder: encapsulated or mixed in food
- **Dosage**: 1.5-9 g dried mushroom/day (divided doses); 1-3 mL tincture 2-3x/day; 500-1500 mg extract 2x/day
- **Cultivation**: Moderate difficulty. Prefers hardwood logs or supplemented sawdust. Long growth cycle (4-6 months to fruiting). Requires specific environmental triggers for conk formation.
- **Sourcing**: Dried slices, dual extract tinctures, powders. Reishi spore oil is a premium product (high triterpene content).
- **Cautions**: May have anticoagulant effects. Avoid with blood thinners. Possible GI upset at high doses.

### Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor)

- **Active compounds**: PSK, PSP, beta-glucans, krestin (Japan), Yun Zhi (China)
- **Health benefits**: Most studied mushroom for cancer support. PSK extends survival in gastric, colorectal, breast, and lung cancers when used as adjuvant with chemotherapy. Immune restoration post-chemotherapy. Antiviral activity (HPV, HIV, hepatitis). Gut microbiome support (prebiotic)
- **Preparations**: Hot water extraction required (beta-glucans water-soluble, chitin-bound). Decoction: simmer 1-2 hr. Capsules of hot water extract. Myceliated grain products widely available but quality varies significantly.
- **Dosage**: 2-6 g dried mushroom/day; 3-6 g/day standardized PSK (Krestin equivalent); 1-3 g/day PSP
- **Cultivation**: Easy to moderate. Grows on hardwood logs and stumps. Very common in wild. Does not produce a traditional soft mushroom body -- hard, leathery brackets.
- **Sourcing**: Widely available as supplements. Look for beta-glucan content (15-45%) and PSK/PSP standardization. "Host Defense" and "Fungi Perfecti" are well-known brands (Stamets).
- **Cautions**: Generally very safe. May cause darkened stools. The fungal-immune interaction is complex -- consult oncologist before combining with immunotherapy.

### Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)

- **Active compounds**: Hericenones (fruiting body), erinacines (mycelium), beta-glucans, phenolic compounds
- **Health benefits**: Neuroprotective -- stimulates NGF and BDNF synthesis. Improves cognitive function and memory (human RCTs show improvement in mild cognitive impairment). Reduces anxiety and depression. Promotes nerve regeneration (peripheral nerve injury models). Anti-inflammatory. Potential for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease prevention
- **Preparations**: Fresh: cook like seafood (crab/lobster flavor). Dried powder in capsules. Dual extract tincture (mycelium for erinacines, fruiting body for hericenones). Tea from dried slices
- **Dosage**: 500-3000 mg extract/day; 2-4 g dried mushroom/day; fresh: 100-250 g cooked
- **Cultivation**: Moderate. Prefers hardwood supplemented sawdust. Fruiting in 2-4 weeks after primordia. Can be grown on logs but less reliable than indoor cultivation.
- **Sourcing**: Fresh at specialty [[mushroom-market-and-economics|market]]s. Supplements widely available. Both mycelium and fruiting body products sold -- ideally use both for full spectrum of compounds.
- **Cautions**: Rare cases of contact dermatitis from fresh mushrooms. Generally very safe.

### Cordyceps (Cordyceps militaris / C. sinensis)

- **Active compounds**: Cordycepin, adenosine, ergosterol, beta-glucans, cordycepic acid (mannitol)
- **Health benefits**: Increases ATP production and oxygen utilization -- ergogenic aid for endurance. Improves VO2 max in human studies. Anti-fatigue. Supports kidney function (traditional use). Immunomodulatory. Anti-inflammatory. Potential anti-cancer via cordycepin-induced apoptosis
- **Preparations**: C. militaris (cultivated): tea from dried fruiting bodies, tincture, capsules. C. sinensis (wild): extremely expensive ($10,000-50,000/lb); traditionally prepared in chicken soup. Mycelial fermentation products available (CS-4).
- **Dosage**: 1-3 g dried C. militaris/day; 3-6 g/day for athletic performance; CS-4 extract: 1-3 g/day
- **Cultivation**: C. militaris moderate difficulty (requires specific grain substrates, temperature cycling). C. sinensis cannot be commercially cultivated -- wild harvested only from Himalayan caterpillars.
- **Sourcing**: C. militaris fruiting bodies increasingly available. CS-4 mycelial products common. Wild C. sinensis rare and expensive -- often adulterated.
- **Cautions**: May lower blood sugar -- caution with diabetes medications. Potential immunostimulatory effects -- caution with autoimmune conditions.

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)

- **Active compounds**: Lentinan (beta-glucan), eritadenine, ergothioneine, lentinula polysaccharides
- **Health benefits**: Lentinan enhances immune response (NK cell activation, macrophage stimulation). Anti-tumor activity demonstrated in clinical trials. Antiviral (HIV, hepatitis). Lowers cholesterol via eritadenine. Antioxidant (high ergothioneine). Anti-inflammatory.
- **Preparations**: Excellent culinary mushroom -- regular consumption provides health benefits. Dried shiitake has higher ergothioneine than fresh. Medicinal: hot water extract, tincture, capsules. Medicinal dose requires concentrated preparations beyond dietary intake.
- **Dosage**: Culinary: 5-10 g fresh/day. Medicinal: 5-15 g dried/day as extract; lentinan therapy (Japan): 1-2 mg/kg IV weekly (medical setting only)
- **Cultivation**: Easy to moderate. Classic log cultivation on oak or similar hardwoods. Also grows on supplemented sawdust blocks. Requires cold shock for fruiting. 6-12 month cycle on logs; 2-3 months on blocks.
- **Sourcing**: Widely available fresh and dried at grocery stores. Supplements less common than reishi/turkey tail.
- **Cautions**: "Shiitake dermatitis" (flagellate erythema) in rare cases from raw or undercooked mushrooms. Always cook thoroughly. Rare allergic reactions.

### [[maitake-cultivation|Maitake]] / Hen of the Woods (Grifola frondosa)

- **Active compounds**: Maitake D-fraction (MD-fraction, specific beta-glucan), grifolan, beta-glucans, ergosterol
- **Health benefits**: MD-fraction activates macrophages, NK cells, and T-cells. Anti-tumor activity in animal models and early clinical trials. Insulin sensitization -- may help manage type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome. Hypotensive. Hepatoprotective.
- **Preparations**: Culinary: excellent eating mushroom with rich, earthy flavor. Medicinal: hot water extract of fruiting body or MD-fraction concentrate. Capsules of extract.
- **Dosage**: Culinary: as desired. Medicinal: 3-5 g dried/day; MD-fraction: 20-35 mg/kg/day (animal studies); 500-1000 mg standardized extract 2-3x/day
- **Cultivation**: Moderate to difficult. Prefers oak stumps or buried supplemented blocks. Requires specific temperature and humidity cycling. Less domesticated than shiitake or oyster mushrooms.
- **Sourcing**: Fresh at specialty markets and farmers markets in fall. Supplements available but less common.
- **Cautions**: May lower blood sugar and blood pressure. Caution with antidiabetic and antihypertensive medications.

### Chaga (Inonotus obliquus)

- **Active compounds**: Betulin/betulinic acid (from birch bark substrate), inotodiol, melanin, beta-glucans, polyphenols, superoxide dismutase (SOD)
- **Health benefits**: Extremely high antioxidant capacity (ORAC among highest of any food). Anti-inflammatory. Anti-tumor via betulinic acid-induced apoptosis. Immunomodulatory. Traditional use for GI issues, fatigue, and skin conditions. Antiviral activity.
- **Preparations**: Hot water decoction (30-60 min) -- traditional preparation. Dual extraction for full spectrum (water + ethanol). Powder in capsules or smoothies. Chaga tea widely available.
- **Dosage**: 2-4 g dried chaga/day in tea; 500-1500 mg dual extract/day
- **Cultivation**: Cannot be reliably cultivated for fruiting body. Mycelial cultivation possible but traditional chaga is a sterile conk (sclerotium) that grows on living birch trees for 10-20 years. Most commercial products are wild-harvested.
- **Sourcing**: Wild harvested from birch forests (Siberia, Canada, northern US, Scandinavia). Quality varies widely. Look for betulin content and third-party testing for heavy metals. Overharvesting is a conservation concern.
- **Cautions**: High oxalate content -- may contribute to kidney stones with heavy use. Contains compounds that may interact with blood thinners. Avoid in pregnancy (traditional禁忌). Potential for contamination with heavy metals from polluted environments.

### [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-agaricus-blazei|Agaricus blazei]] (Agaricus subrufescens)

- **Active compounds**: Beta-glucans (high concentration), ergosterol, agaritine, blazein, linoleic acid derivatives
- **Health benefits**: Originally discovered in Brazil where local populations had very low cancer rates. Immunostimulatory (NK cell activation). Anti-tumor activity in animal models and some clinical trials. Anti-inflammatory. Anti-diabetic. Antioxidant.
- **Preparations**: Hot water extract (tea, capsules). Culinary -- edible with almond-like flavor.
- **Dosage**: 3-6 g dried/day as extract; 500-1500 mg standardized extract 2x/day
- **Cultivation**: Moderate. Requires specific compost substrate and casing. More demanding than shiitake or oyster mushrooms.
- **Sourcing**: Supplements available. Fresh less common outside specialty markets.
- **Cautions**: Higher agaritine content than most mushrooms -- a hydrazine derivative that is a suspected carcinogen when raw. Always cook thoroughly or use processed extracts.

## Cancer Support Overview

Medicinal mushrooms work primarily through immunomodulation rather than direct cytotoxicity:

### Mechanisms of Action
- Activate macrophages, NK cells, dendritic cells; stimulate cytokine production (IL-2, IFN-gamma, TNF-alpha)
- Promote Th1 immune response; anti-angiogenesis
- Induce apoptosis in cancer cells via caspase activation and mitochondrial pathways
- Protect bone marrow during chemotherapy; reduce treatment side effects
- Multi-cytokine inducers able to induce gene expression of immunomodulatory cytokines and receptors
- Glucan-protein complexes show more potent anti-tumor activity than glucans alone

### Clinically Validated Products (Phase I-III Trials)

| Product | Source | Status | Primary Cancer Application |
|---------|--------|--------|--------------------------|
| **Lentinan** | Lentinula edodes (shiitake) fruiting body | Approved in Japan (1985) | Gastric, colorectal cancer adjuvant |
| **Schizophyllan (SPG)** | Schizophyllum commune mycelial broth | Approved in Japan | Cervical, gastric cancer |
| **PSK (Polysaccharide-K/Krestin)** | Trametes versicolor mycelial culture | Approved in Japan (1977), China | Gastric, colorectal, breast, lung cancer |
| **PSP** | Trametes versicolor mycelial culture | Approved in China | Gastric, esophageal, lung cancer |
| **Grifron-D (MD-fraction)** | Grifola frondosa (maitake) fruiting body | Phase I-III (Japan, US) | Breast, lung cancer |

### PSK (Turkey Tail) — Most Evidence Base
- Extends survival in gastric cancer: 5-year survival +11% when combined with chemotherapy
- Effective in colorectal cancer, breast cancer, and non-small cell lung cancer
- Used as standard adjuvant in Japanese cancer treatment since 1977
- Mechanism: Activates cytotoxic T-cells and NK cells; induces cytokine production; enhances dendritic cell maturation
- Dose: 3 g/day standardized PSK (oral)
- Also shows antiviral activity against HPV, HIV, and hepatitis

### Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)
- Ganoderic acids induce apoptosis and inhibit metastasis in vitro and in animal models
- Inhibits tumor-induced angiogenesis via suppression of VEGF
- Clinical evidence emerging but less robust than PSK
- Used extensively in traditional Chinese medicine for "fu zheng" (restoring normal body function)
- Ganodermanontriol and lucidenic acids show direct cytotoxic effects on cancer cell lines

### Safety Profile
- Remarkably few adverse reactions in Phase I clinical trials
- No reports of short-term or long-term toxicity from purified mushroom polysaccharides used clinically
- However, immunostimulatory effects could theoretically interfere with certain immunotherapies — consult oncologist

### Key Insight from Cancer Research Commission
"The ability of mushroom-derived polysaccharides when taken prior to and during radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy to significantly reduce the side-effects of these treatments" is perhaps the most encouraging observation. Mushrooms improve quality of life and survival while reducing the debilitating effects of conventional treatment.

## Preparation Methods Summary

| Method | Best For | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Hot water decoction | Beta-glucans, water-soluble polysaccharides | Simmer 30-120 min. Standard for PSK/PSP |
| Ethanol tincture | Triterpenes, sterols, terpenoids | Essential for reishi triterpenes |
| Dual extraction | Full-spectrum products | Water + ethanol, combined |
| Capsules (powder) | General wellness | Limited bioavailability without extraction |
| Food/culinary | Daily maintenance | Shiitake, maitake, lion's mane are excellent food |
| Fermented products | Gut health synergy | Kombucha, tempeh-style products |

## Sourcing Quality

- Look for: beta-glucan content on label, third-party testing (heavy metals, pesticides), fruiting body vs mycelium specification, extraction method
- Fruiting body extracts generally preferred for most species (higher beta-glucan concentration in mature fruiting bodies)
- Mycelium-on-grain products: ensure starch content is subtracted from total polysaccharide claims
- Organic certification preferred -- mushrooms bioaccumulate environmental contaminants
- Avoid products with fillers or unlisted ingredients
