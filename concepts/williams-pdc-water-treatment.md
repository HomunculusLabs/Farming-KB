# Water Treatment in Permaculture Design Water treatment in permaculture harnesses the ecological forces of the hydro-cycle to clean and recycle water for continuous reuse on site. Since water is used and not consumed, it can be cleaned and recycled nigh indefinitely. As Dr. Paolo Soleri stated, the plan is to use the same water in three successive steps: domestically, aesthetically, and agriculturally. There is no such thing as waste water, only water that is wasted. ## The Principle of No Waste Water Before discussing how to design water treatment systems, the first lesson is critical: there is no such thing as waste water. Water can be cleaned and recycled indefinitely, therefore there is no waste water, only water that is wasted. As permaculture principle number eight tells us, produce no waste. If there is any waste water from your design then it is not a permaculture design. The technical phrase for water that comes out of a system after it has been used is effluent. There are two main types of effluent: grey water and black water. ## Grey Water and Black Water Grey water is effluent used for domestic purposes, usually bathing, laundry, and dishes. Black water is effluent suffused with dangerous bacteria, most commonly from toilets where fecal matter infests the water with coliforms. In some municipalities the kitchen sink is also considered black water. The focus here is on grey water, as black water is dangerous and more appropriate for advanced designers.
