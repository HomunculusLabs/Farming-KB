## Social Functions

Metzner identified several important social functions served by these
syncretic churches:

- **Community cohesion**: They provide members with a sense of belonging and
  participation within a supportive community framework.

- **Addiction reduction**: Participation tends to reduce the incidence of
  alcoholism and drug addiction, a finding documented across multiple studies.

- **Cultural protection**: They provide a protective shield of traditional lore
  against the encroachments of Christian missionaries and Western consumer
  culture, a function noted by anthropologists studying similar movements.

- **Spiritual accessibility**: They have made profoundly transformative
  entheogenic experiences accessible to large numbers of people from all walks
  of life, representing a significant departure from the exclusive,
  apprenticeship-based model of traditional shamanism.

## The Transition from Shamanism to Religion

Metzner viewed these churches as representing a transition from shamanic
healing rituals — where only a very limited number of people came into contact
with entheogenic substances — to a more broadly accessible religious format.
This transition has brought entheogenic practice out of the forest and into
urban centers, making it available to people who would never have had the
opportunity to sit with an indigenous ayahuasquero.

Metzner suggested that we may be seeing "the beginnings of a broader cultural
transformation movement with significant impact" — a movement in which the
entheogenic experience is integrated into mainstream religious and therapeutic
practice rather than being confined to indigenous or countercultural contexts.
