---# Enzymatic Oxidation of Petroleum Hydrocarbons by Fungi

Enzymatic oxidation of petroleum hydrocarbons by fungi is an emerging
field driven by the advantages of regio- and stereoselectivity and mild
physiological reaction conditions. Enzymatic oxidation can succeed where
chemical catalysts fail, providing a valuable tool for the detoxification
of complex hydrocarbon mixtures in contaminated environments.

## Laccase-Mediated Oxidation

Laccase from the white-rot fungus Trametes hirsuta oxidizes alkenes
through mediated oxidation (Niku-Paavola and Viikari, 2000). This
two-step process involves: (1) the enzyme catalyzing oxidation of the
primary substrate (the mediator); and (2) the oxidized mediator
oxidizing the secondary substrate (the alkene). All alkenes are
oxidized to varying extents depending on the alkene structure and
mediator employed. Hydroxybenzotriazole (HBT) serves as the most
effective mediator.

## Oxidation of Terpenes and Alcohols

Treatment with laccase-HBT at 20 degrees C for 20 hours achieves 45-50%
oxidation of alpha-pinene, cis-2- and cis-3-hexenols, and 90-100%
oxidation of linalool, geraniol, nerol, and cinnamyl alcohol. Other
alkenes such as allyl ether, cis-2-heptene, and cyclohexene are
oxidized less than 25% even with all mediators. The main reaction
products of alkenes are aldehydes and ketones, though additional
products are also identified.

## Chloroperoxidase Applications

Chloroperoxidase (CPO) from Caldariomyces fumago has been employed for
enantioselective epoxidation of alkenes and olefins (Dexter et al.,
1995). CPO catalyzed chlorination of different phenolic compounds has
been demonstrated (Wannstedt et al., 1990). The enzyme provides a route
for both oxidation and halogenation of petroleum-derived compounds,
expanding the toolkit for enzymatic hydrocarbon transformation.
