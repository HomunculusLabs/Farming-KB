Leucaena leucocephala is one of the most versatile multipurpose trees in tropical permaculture. Native to [[comparison-rotational-grazing-vs-continuous-grazing]]. ## Food Uses Young leaves, shoots, and immature green beans are edible. In Mexico, the Zapotec people eat the immature green bean as a vegetable. They are stripped of their outer layer and boiled. Mature but not dried seeds are eaten raw or cooked, wrapped in banana leaves. Various parts of the tree have traditional food uses across its native range. ## Timber and Wood Products Leucaena wood from two-year-old trees produces excellent fence posts and building fittings. It is one of the best tropical hardwoods for paper pulp production, with good fibre quality. The wood serves as girders, floor joists, and rafters in construction. Charcoal from leucaena has a heating value of about 7,000 kcal per kilogram, making it a high-quality fuel. Its rapid growth and coppicing ability ensure continuous fuelwood supplies from managed plantations. ## Growth and Adaptability Leucaena grows rapidly in tropical and subtropical conditions, with plantations covering 160,000 hectares in Indonesia and over 125,000 hectares in Brazil. It is cold-tolerant, often losing leaves in severe cold but recovering. ## Design Integration Summary

Leucaena and Inga species represent the backbone of tropical
permaculture fodder systems. When integrated with grass strips,
fruit trees, and animal rotation, they create self-fertilising
productive landscapes. The designer should plan for multiple access
rows, regular pruning schedules, and diverse companion plantings.
Starting with a small trial area of alley cropping allows observation
of local performance before scaling up. Once established, these
legume tree systems reduce or eliminate purchased fertiliser, feed,
and fuel costs while building soil carbon and supporting biodiversity.
