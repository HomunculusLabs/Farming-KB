# Language as Self-Replicating Information System and the Noosphere

## Overview

In "[[mckenna-tryptamine-hallucinogens-and-consciousness]] and Consciousness" (1983), [[terence-mckenna]]
advanced the provocative thesis that language is not merely a human
communication tool but a self-replicating information system that has
achieved a degree of autonomy from its biological hosts. He described
language as moving through human beings and expanding into the
"noosphere" — a planetary sphere of thought analogous to the
biosphere.

## Language as an Autonomous System

McKenna proposed that language functions as an independent
informational entity with properties analogous to biological life:

- **Self-replication**: Language reproduces itself through human
  communication, generation after generation
- **Mutation and evolution**: Languages change, diversify, and
  evolve over time, developing new forms and capabilities
- **Coding and templating**: Language encodes information in symbolic
  form, analogous to DNA's encoding of biological information
- **Testing and re-testing**: Ideas encoded in language are
  continuously tested against reality through human action and
  experience
- **Learning and recording**: Language accumulates knowledge across
  generations, building on previous encoding

McKenna explicitly drew the parallel: "What is loose on this planet
is language, self-replicating information systems that reflect
functions of DNA: learning, coding, templating, recording, testing,
re-testing, re-coding against DNA functions."

## The DNA-Language Parallel

The comparison between genetic and linguistic information systems
Both systems:
- Use discrete combinatorial units (nucleotides / phonemes) to
  build larger structures (genes / sentences)
- Operate through codes mapping symbols to meaning (codon → amino
  acid / word → concept)
- Accumulate information over time through selection pressures
- Show evidence of functional convergence across independent lineages

McKenna acknowledged the alternative: "Language may be a quality
of an entirely different order." The DNA analogy may be incomplete,
and language may represent something genuinely novel in the history
of life — a new replicator that transcends biological substrates.

## The Noosphere

The concept of the noosphere (from Greek *nous*, "mind") was
originally developed by Vladimir Vernadsky and Pierre Teilhard de
Chardin. McKenna adopted it to describe the emerging planetary
network of thought, information, and communication:

"We are just beginning to push the buttons on the mind. Once we
take a serious engineering approach to this, we are going to
discover the plasticity, the mutability, the eternal nature of
the mind."

The noosphere represents the idea that human thought, mediated by
language, is creating a new kind of planetary environment — an
informational sphere surrounding the Earth, analogous to how the
biosphere is the planetary environment of life.

## Language Moving Through Us

A striking aspect of McKenna's thesis is the reversal of the usual
assumption about language. Rather than humans possessing language,
McKenna suggested that language possesses humans:

"Whatever language is, it is in us monkeys now and moving through
us and moving out of our hands and into the noosphere with which
we have surrounded ourselves."

This framing casts language as a symbiotic organism that has
colonized the human species, using our brains as hardware for its
own replication and evolution. The implication is that human
culture and technology are not purely human achievements but the
extended phenotype of a linguistic replicator.
