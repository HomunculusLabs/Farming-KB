## Amanita Muscaria vs Psilocybe Mushrooms

Amanita muscaria (fly agaric) and psilocybin-containing mushrooms are
often conflated by the public, but they differ fundamentally in taxonomy,
chemistry, effects, and risk profile. Understanding these distinctions
is critical because some closely related Amanita species are lethal.

## Taxonomic Classification

### Amanita muscaria
- **Family**: Agaricaceae (Agaric family)
- **Genus**: Amanita
- **Common names**: Fly agaric, fly Amanita
- **Notable relatives**: A. pantherina (panther cap), A. phalloides
  (death cap — fatally toxic), A. virosa ([[1960s-counterculture-and-magic-mushrooms]], liberty caps, wavy caps

## Active Compounds

### Amanita muscaria
The active chemicals are **completely different** from psilocybin:

- **Ibotenic acid**: A glutamate receptor agonist acting primarily on
  NMDA and metabotropic glutamate receptors. Neuroexcitatory; causes
  confusion, agitation, and ataxia.
- **Muscimol**: The decarboxylation product of ibotenic acid. A potent
  GABA_A receptor agonist producing sedative-hypnotic, dissociative, and
  hallucinatory effects. This is the primary psychoactive compound
  after drying or heating converts ibotenic acid.
- **Muscarine**: Present in trace amounts; historically associated with
  the name but not the primary psychoactive agent.

### Psilocybe mushrooms
- **Psilocybin** (4-PO-DMT): Prodrug converted to psilocin
- **Psilocin** (4-HO-DMT): Active 5-HT2A [[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]]

See [[amanita-genus-morphology]]
- Closely related species (A. phalloides, A. virosa) contain amatoxins
  that cause fatal liver failure
- Misidentification with deadly Amanita species is a genuine and
  documented risk
- Ibotenic acid poisoning causes CNS excitation: seizures, agitation,
  hallucinations, and potentially coma
- Muscimol overdose produces deep sedation, respiratory depression,
  and prolonged unconsciousness
