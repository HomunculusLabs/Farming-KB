# Rainwater Harvesting and Storage

Every 1,000 square feet (92 square meters) of roof surface area will gather
600 gallons (2,270 liters) of water per inch (2.5 cm) of rain. Every roof
on your property can be used to collect rainwater. The best roofing material
for this purpose is metal because it is relatively clean. You will also need
to install gutters with a leaf screen. Rainwater harvesting is a cornerstone
of and gordon-wasson
design.

## Collection System Components

### Gutters and Roof Washers
If the rainwater is being used for human consumption, it should be directed
into a roof washer that automatically diverts 1 gallon (4 liters) per 100
square feet (9 square meters) into a separate tank or to the garden. This
helps keep dirt out of the stored water. The entrance of the stored water
should be covered with a mosquito screen, and the tank should also have an
overflow valve directed into the garden.

### Pipes and Pumps
From the tank, water goes through a series of pipes to where you want it.
You need check valves to prevent any backflow. Unless your tank is far enough
up a slope to provide gravity pressure, you will also need a water pump.
From the water pump, there should be another check valve and then a pressure
tank. There are other options, such as a water-sensitive pump, which will
create water pressure and pump your water in one device.

### Purification
Once in the house, the water goes to a water purification system. If you
are in the city and still want to be connected to the municipal water
supply, you usually need an approved backflow prevention device that ensures
no rainwater gets into the city water supply. This device must be examined
and maintained properly.

## Water Storage Tanks

Water for human consumption is stored in tanks. A single person should store
at least 1,000 gallons (3,700 liters), and a family needs a minimum of 2,500
gallons (9,500 liters). Tanks can be made from concrete, compacted clay,
metal, or even plastered dirt. The water to fill tanks comes from rainwater
running off roofs or other surfaces, or is pumped from a dam.

## Tank Material Options

### Earthbag Tanks
Earthbag tanks are cheap and durable but take up a huge amount of space.
Even an earthbag tank is lined with polyethylene, so it may not be any
cleaner than plastic options.

### Polyethylene Tanks
Polyethylene is cheap and durable and comes in any size and shape, but
people concerned about chemicals leaching from the plastic will want to
avoid them.

### Galvanized Steel
Galvanized steel is considered safe to drink from, although it has a zinc
coating that does leach into the water. Modern versions no longer contain
lead, which manufacturers at one time used.

### Concrete Tanks
Concrete tanks are less expensive but have more metal in them in the form
of rebar than galvanized steel tanks, and their lifespan is shorter. If the
right concrete mix is used, they may be the safest to drink from.
