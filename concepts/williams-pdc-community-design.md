# Community Design in [[permaculture-design]] is about creating architecture and
infrastructure, community is one of the fundamental human needs that
sustainability must address. Permaculture systems reach a much higher
potential when plugged into the invisible structure of a community.

## Why Community Matters in Permaculture

Permaculture is modeled after nature, and nature has no one caretaker.
Natural systems are maintained by communities of animals and plants
working together in harmonious symbiosis. Nature benefits from and is
dependent on the network effect: the more individuals involved in the
network, the more value the network has for each individual.

While a permaculture system may be in your backyard, it can and should
still benefit the Earth, other people, and be shared. With a community
around your permaculture system you can swap seeds with others, trade
and forage for supplies, coordinate food strategies with other
gardeners, pull together work parties, and draw upon pools of talent
and knowledge greater than your own. Some systems may require steps of
succession over a period of time that may outstretch your involvement,
and the community can see those steps through. Just as birds and bees
help natural systems propagate and spread, humans can do the same for
permaculture systems.

## Setting Intention

At the heart of an intentional community is the intent of the
community. Do not assume that because it has the label of intentional
that the intent can be assumed. It benefits the community to have an
organized and agreed-upon intention.

To set the intention, the group should create a Mission and Vision
Statement. Mission statements are goal-oriented, articulating what you
want to accomplish and why. The Vision Statement articulates what this
mission will look like when manifest, often covering the how of the
mission statement.

The recommended process is to get everybody together, have each
person put one sentence on the board encapsulating what they think the
mission should be, reduce any redundant ones, then synthesize what
remains into a mission statement about three sentences long and a
vision of three or four sentences.

Remember that in permaculture you should endeavor to use and value
diversity and [[permaculture-guilds]] where community members
  coordinate seed swaps, work parties, and coordinated food
  strategies

## Practical Considerations

Successful implementation of Community Design in Permaculture requires attention to
several practical factors including environmental conditions,
resource availability, and timing. Careful monitoring and
adaptive management help optimize outcomes across varying
conditions. Integration with other system elements enhances
overall effectiveness and creates beneficial synergies that
improve resilience and productivity over time.
