The PDC references Allan Savory's work with [[comparison-rotational-grazing-vs-continuous-grazing]] systems
and the dramatic restoration of degraded grasslands through proper
grazing management. The principle: "The land needs the animals, but the
animals need to be managed."

## Grassland Ecology Principles

Understanding grassland dynamics is essential for range management:

- Grass plants need periodic defoliation to stimulate root dieback and
  [[acacia]] pods, tagasaste (tree lucerne) produces protein-rich fodder year-round
- Cactus (Opuntia) provides emergency fodder and water in deserts

## Integration of Livestock Types

Different livestock species utilize different plants and plant parts:

- Cattle graze tall grasses, leaving short species and forbs
- Sheep prefer short grasses and forbs, grazing close to the ground
- Goats browse shrubs and woody plants that cattle and sheep ignore
- Pigs root for tubers and grubs, tilling soil and controlling pests
- Poultry follow larger animals, cleaning up insect larvae in manure
- Running multiple species together maximizes forage utilization and
  reduces parasite loads through "self-medication" behavior

## Fencing for Range Systems

Permaculture fencing strategies minimize materials while achieving
effective animal control:

- Electric fencing enables rapid paddock subdivision and rotation
- [[comparison-living-fences-vs-hedgerows]] (hedges, woven branches) provide shelter and corridors
- Natural barriers (waterways, slopes, thorny thickets) reduce fencing
- Permanent fencing should follow contours and waterways, not straight

## Water Points in Range Design

Strategic water placement controls grazing distribution:

- Water should never be in the lowest corner of a paddock -- animals
  congregate there causing severe overgrazing and erosion
- Multiple water points distribute grazing pressure across the paddock
- Water points should be moved periodically in extensive systems
- Shade should be available near water to reduce [[query-how-do-i-manage-heat-stress-in-my-cannabis-garden]]
- Water reticulation (pipes, troughs) is cheaper than fencing in many
  situations and provides better animal distribution

## Restoration of Degraded Range

The PDC provides a systematic approach to range restoration:

1. **Reduce stocking rate** immediately below carrying capacity
2. **Implement rotational grazing** with adequate rest periods
3. **Address soil surface** -- keyline plowing, compost teas, seed
   inoculation
4. **Re-seed with diverse species** including deep-rooted perennials
5. **Introduce tree systems** for shade, shelter, and fodder
6. **Monitor and adapt** -- adjust stocking rates and rotation timing
   based on observed plant response

Mollison emphasizes that degraded land can recover remarkably quickly
when the right management is applied, sometimes within three to five
years of implementing time-controlled grazing.

## Wildlife Corridors and Habitat

Range design should incorporate wildlife needs:

- Connect isolated habitat patches with vegetated corridors
- Maintain riparian zones as wildlife refuges and water sources
- Leave standing dead trees for cavity-nesting birds and mammals
- Create pond and wetland areas for waterfowl and amphibians
- Avoid monoculture pastures -- diverse plantings support more wildlife
  species
