# Cytokinin: Plant Growth and Development Hormone

Cytokinins are a class of plant hormones (phytohormones) that promote cell division (cytokinesis), regulate shoot development, delay leaf senescence, and mediate nutrient signaling. They work in concert with auxin, the other major hormone controlling plant development, with the cytokinin-to-auxin ratio being a fundamental determinant of organogenesis in plant tissue culture.

## Discovery and Chemical Nature

Cytokinins were first discovered in the 1950s through independent research by Folke Skoog and Carlos Miller at the University of Wisconsin. They identified a substance in herring sperm DNA that promoted cell division in tobacco pith tissue culture. This compound, named kinetin (6-furfurylaminopurine), was the first cytokinin described, though it was later found to be an artifact of DNA autoclaving and does not occur naturally in plants.

Natural cytokinins are adenine derivatives with an isoprenoid or aromatic side chain at the N⁶ position. The major classes include:

- **Isoprenoid cytokinins**: The most common in plants. Trans-zeatin is the most biologically active natural cytokinin. Others include cis-zeatin, dihydrozeatin, and isopentenyladenine (iP).
- **Aromatic cytokinins**: Less common, with benzyladenine (BAP) being the most studied. These are primarily synthetic but some occur naturally.
- **Phenylurea cytokinins**: Synthetic compounds like thidiazuron (TDZ) and diphenylurea (DPU) that have strong cytokinin-like activity, widely used in tissue culture.

The side chain structure determines bioactivity. Trans-zeatin is the most active form, while cis-zeatin has much lower activity in most bioassays. The hydroxyl group at the terminal position of the isoprenoid side chain (present in zeatin but absent in iP) contributes significantly to biological activity.

## Cytokinin Biosynthesis

Cytokinin biosynthesis occurs primarily through the **isopentenyltransferase (IPT) pathway**. The key steps are:

1. **Dimethylallyl diphosphate (DMAPP)** is transferred to AMP, ADP, or ATP by IPT enzymes, forming iP-nucleotides (iPRMP, iPRDP, or iPRTP)
2. **iP-nucleotides are hydroxylated** by cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (CYP735A family) to form trans-zeatin nucleotides
3. **Nucleotides are converted to nucleosides and free bases** by phosphoribohydrolases and nucleosidases for biological activity

There are two sources of cytokinin biosynthesis:
- **tRNA-derived pathway**: Cytokinins are released as degradation products of tRNA containing cis-zeatin. This pathway contributes mainly to cis-zeatin, which has low biological activity in most contexts.
- **De novo pathway**: The IPT pathway described above is the primary source of bioactive cytokinins.

Cytokinin biosynthesis is localized primarily in root tips, young leaves, developing seeds, and cambial tissue. Roots are the major site of cytokinin production, and the hormone is transported acropetally (upward) through the xylem to influence shoot development.

## Cytokinin Signaling

The cytokinin signaling pathway follows a bacterial two-component system (TCS), inherited from the cyanobacterial ancestor of chloroplasts. The core pathway involves three component families:

1. **Histidine Kinase Receptors (AHK2, AHK3, AHK4/CRE1/WOL)**: These membrane-localized receptors bind cytokinin and autophosphorylate on a conserved histidine residue. AHK4/CRE1 is primarily expressed in roots and is the major cytokinin receptor for root-to-shoot signaling. AHK2 and AHK3 are more important in shoots.

2. **Histidine Phosphotransfer Proteins (AHPs)**: Cytoplasmic proteins that shuttle the phosphoryl signal from the receptor to the nucleus. Six AHPs (AHP1-6) have been identified in Arabidopsis.

3. **Type-B Response Regulators (ARRs)**: Nuclear transcription factors that are activated by phosphorylation. They bind to DNA at ARR-binding motifs and activate transcription of cytokinin-responsive genes, including type-A ARRs (negative feedback regulators).

The signaling cascade:
- Cytokinin binds AHK receptor → receptor autophosphorylation → phosphotransfer to AHP → AHP translocates to nucleus → phosphorylates type-B ARR → type-B ARR activates gene transcription → type-A ARRs are induced and competitively inhibit signaling (negative feedback)

## Roles in Plant Development

### Cell Division and Shoot Apical Meristem

Cytokinins maintain the shoot apical meristem (SAM) by promoting cell division in the central zone. Key functions include:

- Upregulation of cyclin genes (particularly CYCD3) to drive G2/M transition
- Promotion of WUSCHEL (WUS) expression, which maintains stem cell identity in the SAM
- Interacting with CLAVATA signaling to balance stem cell proliferation and differentiation
- Overproduction of cytokinin leads to enlarged SAMs with excess stem cells

### Regulation of Apical Dominance

Cytokinins are key regulators of apical dominance, working antagonistically with auxin:

- Auxin produced by the shoot apex suppresses axillary bud outgrowth indirectly by maintaining low cytokinin levels in axillary buds
- Direct application of cytokinin to axillary buds can override apical dominance and release buds from dormancy
- The auxin-cytokinin balance determines whether a bud remains dormant or grows out
- Decapitation (removing the shoot apex) increases cytokinin delivery to axillary buds, promoting branching

### Shoot vs. Root Development

The classic Skoog-Miller experiment established that the ratio of cytokinin to auxin determines the fate of callus tissue:

- **High cytokinin : low auxin** → shoot formation
- **Low cytokinin : high auxin** → root formation
- **Intermediate ratios** → undifferentiated callus

This principle underlies virtually all plant tissue culture and micropropagation protocols.

### Leaf Development and Chloroplast Biogenesis

Cytokinins are essential for normal leaf development:

- They promote leaf cell expansion and lamina growth
- Cytokinin-deficient plants produce smaller leaves with reduced chlorophyll content
- Cytokinins stimulate chloroplast development and chlorophyll biosynthesis
- They upregulate genes for photosynthetic proteins in developing chloroplasts
- Cytokinin treatment can delay the senescence-induced degradation of chlorophyll and Rubisco

### Delay of Leaf Senescence

One of the most dramatic effects of cytokinins is their ability to delay leaf senescence:

- Cytokinin levels naturally decline during leaf aging
- Exogenous cytokinin application can dramatically extend leaf lifespan
- Cytokinin receptor mutants show premature leaf senescence
- The mechanism involves suppression of senescence-associated genes (SAGs) and maintenance of photosynthetic capacity
- Autoregulation of cytokinin biosynthesis through cytokinin response factors (CRFs) plays a role in senescence timing

## Cytokinin in Root Development

While cytokinin promotes shoot growth, its effects on roots are generally inhibitory:

- Cytokinin negatively regulates primary root elongation
- It suppresses lateral root initiation when applied exogenously
- Cytokinin regulates the transition from root meristem cell proliferation to differentiation
- Low cytokinin levels in the root tip are maintained by PIN-mediated auxin transport that suppresses IPT expression
- In the root vascular tissue, cytokinin promotes protoxylem differentiation

## Nutrient Signaling

Cytokinins serve as long-distance signals communicating nitrogen status from roots to shoots:

- Nitrate availability rapidly upregulates IPT gene expression in roots
- Increased cytokinin production under high nitrate conditions promotes shoot growth, adjusting the root-to-shoot ratio
- Cytokinin levels in xylem sap correlate with soil nitrogen availability
- Under low nitrogen conditions, reduced cytokinin signaling promotes root foraging and inhibits shoot growth
- Cytokinins also mediate signaling for other nutrients including phosphate and sulfate

## Cytokinin in Agriculture and Horticulture

Cytokinins have several practical applications:

- **Tissue culture**: BAP and kinetin are standard components of plant tissue culture media for shoot proliferation
- **Fruit thinning**: Synthetic cytokinins like CPPU (forchlorfenuron) are used to thin excess fruit in apple and pear orchards
- **Fruit enlargement**: CPPU can increase fruit size in kiwifruit, grapes, and other crops
- **Delay of senescence**: Cytokinin sprays can extend shelf life of cut flowers and leafy vegetables
- **Lawn care**: Cytokinins are sometimes included in turf management products to maintain green color
- **Yield improvement**: Transgenic plants with enhanced cytokinin biosynthesis in roots show improved drought tolerance and yield under stress

## Cross-Talk with Other Hormones

Cytokinin signaling interacts extensively with other hormone pathways:

- **Cytokinin × Auxin**: The fundamental developmental partnership; their ratio determines organogenesis and branching patterns
- **Cytokinin × Ethylene**: Cytokinins can promote ethylene biosynthesis, and ethylene mediates some cytokinin effects on root development
- **Cytokinin × ABA**: Generally antagonistic; cytokinins counteract ABA-mediated stomatal closure and stress responses
- **Cytokinin × Gibberellin**: Synergistic in shoot elongation; both promote internode elongation
- **Cytokinin × Brassinosteroid**: Interactive regulation of cell division and elongation in growing tissues
