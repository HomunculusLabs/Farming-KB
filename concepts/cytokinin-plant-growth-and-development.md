# Cytokinin: Plant Growth and Development Hormone

Cytokinins are a class of plant hormones (phytohormones) that promote cell division (cytokinesis), regulate shoot development, delay [[cervantes-tissue-culture-micropropagation]].

## Discovery and Chemical Nature

Cytokinins were first discovered in the 1950s through independent research by Folke Skoog and Carlos Miller at the University of Wisconsin. They identified a substance in herring sperm DNA that promoted cell division in tobacco pith tissue culture. This compound, named kinetin (6-furfurylaminopurine), was the first cytokinin described, though it was later found to be an artifact of DNA autoclaving and does not occur naturally in plants.

Natural cytokinins are adenine derivatives with an isoprenoid or aromatic [[ascomycota-phylogeny-major-classes-lineages]] include:

- **Isoprenoid cytokinins**: The most common in plants. Trans-zeatin is the most biologically active natural cytokinin. Others include cis-zeatin, dihydrozeatin, and isopentenyladenine (iP).
- **Aromatic cytokinins**: Less common, with benzyladenine (BAP) being the most studied. These are primarily synthetic but some occur naturally.
- **Phenylurea cytokinins**: Synthetic compounds like thidiazuron (TDZ) and diphenylurea (DPU) that have strong cytokinin-like activity, widely used in tissue culture.

The side chain structure determines bioactivity. Trans-zeatin is the most active form, while cis-zeatin has much lower activity in most bioassays. The hydroxyl group at the terminal position of the isoprenoid side chain (present in zeatin but absent in iP) contributes significantly to biological activity.

## Cytokinin Biosynthesis

Cytokinin biosynthesis occurs primarily through the **isopentenyltransferase (IPT) pathway**. The key steps are:

1. **Dimethylallyl diphosphate (DMAPP)** is transferred to AMP, ADP, or ATP by IPT enzymes, forming iP-nucleotides (iPRMP, iPRDP, or iPRTP)
2. **iP-nucleotides are hydroxylated** by cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (CYP735A family) to form trans-zeatin nucleotides
3. **Nucleotides are converted to nucleosides and free bases** by phosphoribohydrolases and nucleosidases for biological activity

There are two sources of cytokinin biosynthesis:
- **tRNA-derived pathway**: Cytokinins are released as degradation products of tRNA containing cis-zeatin. This pathway contributes mainly to cis-zeatin, which has low biological activity in most contexts.
- **De novo pathway**: The IPT pathway described above is the primary source of bioactive cytokinins.

Cytokinin biosynthesis is localized primarily in root tips, young leaves, developing seeds, and cambial tissue. Roots are the major site of cytokinin production, and the hormone is transported acropetally (upward) through the xylem to influence shoot development.

### Leaf Development and Chloroplast Biogenesis

Cytokinins are essential for normal leaf development:

- They promote leaf cell expansion and lamina growth
- Cytokinin-deficient plants produce smaller leaves with reduced chlorophyll content
- Cytokinins stimulate chloroplast development and chlorophyll biosynthesis
- They upregulate genes for photosynthetic proteins in developing chloroplasts
- Cytokinin treatment can delay the senescence-induced degradation of chlorophyll and Rubisco
