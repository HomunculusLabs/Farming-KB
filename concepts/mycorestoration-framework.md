# Mycorestoration Overview

[[paul-stamets]]' umbrella concept for using fungi to heal the environment. Coined in *Mycelium Running* (2005), mycorestoration encompasses four branches: mycoremediation, mycofiltration, mycoforestry, and mycopesticides.

## The Four Branches

### 1. Mycoremediation
Using fungi to degrade or sequester environmental toxins:
- Petroleum hydrocarbons (crude oil, diesel, BTEX)
- PCBs, PAHs, dioxins
- Heavy metals (arsenic, cadmium, lead, mercury)
- Radioactive elements (cesium-134, cesium-137)
- Chemical warfare agents (VX, sarin, soman surrogates)
- Pesticides and herbicides
- See: [[mycoremediation-basics]], [[mycoremediation-petroleum-and-oil-spills]], [[mycoremediation-heavy-metals-radioactive]]

### 2. Mycofiltration
Using mycelial mats to filter water and protect watersheds:
- E. coli and coliform bacteria removal
- Sediment capture from construction runoff
- Agricultural runoff filtration
- Heavy metal biosorption from water
- See: [[mycofiltration-overview]], [[mycofiltration-watershed-protection]]

### 3. Mycoforestry
Using fungi to restore and protect forests:
- Chip-and-leave road decommissioning
- Species barriers against parasitic fungi (Armillaria)
- Mycorrhizal inoculation at planting
- Erosion control
- Fire prevention
- Carbon banking
- See: [[mycoforestry]], [[mycoforestry-forest-restoration]]

### 4. Mycopesticides
Using entomopathogenic fungi to control insect pests:
- Termite colony elimination (Metarhizium anisopliae)
- Carpenter ant control
- Fire ant control (Solenopsis invicta)
- Agricultural pest management
- See: [[mycopesticides]], [[fungal-pest-control]]

## Core Philosophy

### Mycelium as Nature's Internet

Stamets proposes that mycelium is the Earth's biological internet — a sentient, information-sharing membrane:

- **The mycelial archetype**: Branching network patterns seen in mycelium mirror neurons, the Internet, dark matter filaments, and galactic structures
- **A digestive cellular membrane**: Mycelium is "a fusion between a stomach and a brain, a nutritional and informational sharing network"
- **An archetype of matter and life**: "Our universe is based upon these networking structures"
- **Information sharing**: Mycelial networks sense and respond to environmental conditions, share nutrients between organisms, and coordinate biological activity across landscapes

### The 6th Mass Extinction

Stamets frames mycorestoration as urgently needed:
- Current extinction rates: 15-37% of species could die out by 2050 (Nature, De Siqueira et al. 2004)
- Fungi hover between 1 and 2 million species (Rossman 1994), most undescribed
- Insects: 4-6 million species, ~5% are economic pests
- Human activity — deforestation, fossil fuels, pesticides, heavy metals — is driving the crisis
- Mycorestoration offers biological solutions that work with nature rather than against it

### Move It or Lose It

Stamets' motto for cultivators: "Mycelium consumes its preferred habitat resources and then strategizes for transporting itself to new niches. Unless the mycelium is recharged with basic nutrients, it will move on as it transforms debris fields into soil."

A mycelium grower is "a mycelial herdsman" — success is temporary, requiring constant attention to keep the mycelium nourished and expanding.

## Key Scientific Insights

### Mushrooms Predestine Biological Communities

Research by Tornberg, Baatli, and Olsson (2003) supports Stamets' hypothesis that:
- Most saprophytic fungi predestine and steer subsequent biological communities
- Mushrooms select for specific bacterial communities through their antibiotic secretions
- These bacterial communities influence all other organisms in the food web, particularly plants
- Mushrooms have a vested interest in developing plant communities that fuel their future life cycles

### The Mycelial Tsunami

When mycelium is introduced to a new habitat:
- "A sudden influx of fungal cells inalterably changes the microbial landscape"
- Creates a "mycelial tsunami" that sets a new path in habitat evolution
- The consequences for both micro and macro organisms are dramatic

### Native vs. Imported Strains

Native mushrooms have natural advantages for mycorestoration:
- History of exposure to endemic hostile and beneficial organisms
- Acclimation to local weather patterns
- Existing niche within the ecosystem
- However, the most powerful mycoremediators (oyster, turkey tail) are ubiquitous worldwide

## Cost Advantages

Mycorestoration vs. conventional remediation:
- Spent mushroom compost is far cheaper than pure culture spawn
- Mushroom farms generate tons of spent substrate annually with no market
- A single 4x4-foot patch can expand to 3+ acres in 3 years
- In-situ treatment avoids excavation and disposal costs
- Biological solutions are self-sustaining and self-replicating
- After treatment, sites become productive rather than barren

## Practical Barriers

- The skill of a mushroom cultivator is critical — many scientific mycoremediation trials fail because scientists lack cultivation skills (Sasek 2003)
- Patent landscape is complex — multiple overlapping patents can hinder practitioners
- In situ trials have been less successful than laboratory studies
- Regulatory frameworks don't yet accommodate biological remediation approaches
- Long-term monitoring data is still being collected
