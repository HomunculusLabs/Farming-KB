## Metal-Specific Trends

### Uranium (Consistently Highest)
Uranium (as uranyl ion UO₂²⁺) is consistently biosorbed to the highest levels across all biomass types:

- Typical uptake: >0.8 mmol/g dry weight (>200 mg/g)
- Explained by the linear O=U=O structure enabling coordination to multiple binding sites
- R. arrhizus demonstrated uptake exceeding commercial ion-exchange resins for U and Th (Tsezos & Volesky, 1981)

### Lead (Consistently High)
Lead is repeatedly found to be biosorbed to high levels across diverse genera, reflecting its high electronegativity and affinity for cell wall functional groups.

### Divalent Cations (Moderate)
Cd²⁺, Cu²⁺, Zn²⁺, and other divalent cations typically show uptake in the 0.1–0.5 mmol/g range.

### Monovalent Cations (Low/Negligible)
Alkali metals are generally not biosorbed, though Ag⁺ is appreciably taken up (up to 0.5 mmol/g).

## Cell Wall Chemistry and Binding Sites

### Functional Groups
Fungal cell walls contain multiple potential binding sites:

- **Chitin/chitosan**: Key binding sites identified in early studies; amino groups coordinate metals
- **Carboxyl groups**: Account for up to 55% of Zn binding by P. chrysogenum and 70% by [[trichoderma-reesei]]; preferentially bound at low concentrations due to higher affinity
- **Phosphoryl groups**: Account for up to 95% of Pb binding by P. chrysogenum (Sarrat et al., 1999)
- **Sulfhydryl groups**: Present in some species; contribute to heavy metal binding

### Binding Mechanisms
The binding process involves:

1. **Initial step**: Ion exchange (evidenced by ion release) or coordination (evidenced by proton release)
2. **Secondary step**: Possible crystallization/precipitation on cell surfaces

Factors affecting the initial binding include ionic radius and chemical nature of ions relative to cell wall functional groups (Pearson's "hard and soft" acid-base theory).

## Environmental Factors

### pH
Optimum biosorption occurs at pH 4–7:
- Below pH 4: Increasing H⁺ competition for binding sites reduces cation uptake
- Above pH 7: Metal hydrolysis/precipitation increases apparent removal but is not biosorption

### Temperature
Little effect within normal ranges (5–30°C), consistent with a non-metabolic process.

### Competing Ions
Cation competition typically reduces individual ion uptake, though total biosorption may remain unchanged. No recent studies address simultaneous cation and anion biosorption.

## Multi-Metal Systems

### Binary Isotherms
The Langmuir model extends to binary systems:

```
q₁ = (q₊₁ · a₁C₁) / (1 + a₁C₁ + a₂C₂)
q₂ = (q₊₂ · a₂C₂) / (1 + a₁C₁ + a₂C₂)
```

Adding a second concentration axis creates three-dimensional isotherm surfaces (Kratochvil & Volesky, 1998) that predict co-ion effects on uptake levels.

### Speciation Modeling
Software packages (REDEQL, MINEQL) have been adapted to include biosorption. Langmuir parameters are incorporated as additional binding equilibria, allowing prediction of multi-component systems.

### Modified Competition Models
Jain & Snoeyink (1973) competition modifications adapted to multicomponent biosorption show significant improvement in correlation with empirical data, though applied mainly to algal systems.

## Anion Biosorption

Toxic metal anions (CrO₄²⁻, AsO₄³⁻, MoO₄²⁻, VO₄³⁻) show opposite pH dependence to cations:

- Optimum Cr(VI) removal at pH 1–2
- Negligible biosorption at neutral pH
- At low pH, protonated functional groups attract anions electrostatically
- Cr(VI) reduction to Cr(III) may also occur at low pH

## Practical Applications and Limitations

Despite extensive research since the 1980s, actual industrial application of fungal biosorption remains unproven. Less than 20% of biosorption publications from 1998–2000 focused on fungi. Key limitations:

- Case-specific uptake values that vary widely with experimental conditions
- Difficulty comparing results across studies
