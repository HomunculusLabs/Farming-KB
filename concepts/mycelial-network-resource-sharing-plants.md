## Ecological Implications

CMNs influence plant community composition, succession
dynamics, and ecosystem productivity. By facilitating seedling
establishment near mature plants (nursing effect), CMNs
promote biodiversity and canopy gap filling. They may also
mediate competitive hierarchies—dominant trees sometimes
suppress competitors through network connections while
supporting their own offspring, a phenomenon called "kin
recognition" in some mycorrhizal systems.

However, the extent and ecological significance of resource
sharing remain debated. Some researchers argue that most
observed transfer is incidental fungal leakage rather than
adaptive plant-to-plant sharing, and that experimental
conditions (pots, mesocosms) may overestimate natural
transfer rates. Field-scale confirmation remains technically
challenging.

## Methods

Key techniques include stable isotope probing (13C, 15N, 2H),
radioactive tracers (14C), DNA-based fungal identification to
confirm network connectivity, microdialysis for in situ soil
chemistry, and increasingly, molecular tools like transcriptomics
