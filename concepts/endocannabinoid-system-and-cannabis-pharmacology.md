## Endocannabinoid System and Cannabis Pharmacology

The endocannabinoid system (ECS) is a widespread neuromodulatory
system in the human body that plays critical roles in regulating
pain, mood, appetite, sleep, immune function, and many other
physiological processes. Cannabis exerts its therapeutic and
psychoactive effects primarily through interactions with this system.
[[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]]* provides a comprehensive
overview of the ECS as the foundation for understanding medical
cannabis.

## Discovery and Significance

The endocannabinoid system was discovered through research into how
cannabis produces its effects. The identification of cannabinoid
receptors in the late 1980s and early 1990s, followed by the
discovery of endogenous cannabinoid ligands (endocannabinoids) in
the early 1990s, revealed that the human body produces its own
cannabis-like molecules. This discovery transformed understanding of
cannabis from a recreational drug of unknown mechanism to a plant
that interacts with a fundamental biological regulatory system.

The ECS is now recognized as one of the most important physiological
systems involved in maintaining homeostasis—the body's tendency to
maintain internal stability despite changes in the external
environment. Dysregulation of the ECS has been implicated in
numerous disease states, which helps explain the remarkably broad
[[cannabis-autoimmune-conditions]].
