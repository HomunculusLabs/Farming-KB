# Endocannabinoid System and Cannabis Pharmacology

The endocannabinoid system (ECS) is a widespread neuromodulatory
system in the human body that plays critical roles in regulating
pain, mood, appetite, sleep, immune function, and many other
physiological processes. Cannabis exerts its therapeutic and
psychoactive effects primarily through interactions with this system.
Uwe Blesching's *[[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]]* provides a comprehensive
overview of the ECS as the foundation for understanding medical
cannabis.

## Discovery and Significance

The endocannabinoid system was discovered through research into how
cannabis produces its effects. The identification of cannabinoid
receptors in the late 1980s and early 1990s, followed by the
discovery of endogenous cannabinoid ligands (endocannabinoids) in
the early 1990s, revealed that the human body produces its own
cannabis-like molecules. This discovery transformed understanding of
cannabis from a recreational drug of unknown mechanism to a plant
that interacts with a fundamental biological regulatory system.

The ECS is now recognized as one of the most important physiological
systems involved in maintaining homeostasis—the body's tendency to
maintain internal stability despite changes in the external
environment. Dysregulation of the ECS has been implicated in
numerous disease states, which helps explain the remarkably broad
therapeutic potential of cannabis.

## Cannabinoid Receptors

### CB1 Receptors

CB1 receptors are primarily found in the central nervous system,
particularly in areas of the brain involved in motor control,
cognition, emotional responses, motivated behavior, and pain
perception. They are among the most abundant G-protein coupled
receptors in the brain. CB1 receptor activation by THC produces the
characteristic psychoactive effects of cannabis: euphoria,
altered perception of time, enhanced sensory experience, impaired
short-term memory, and increased appetite.

### CB2 Receptors

CB2 receptors are predominantly found in the immune system,
particularly in the spleen, tonsils, and immune cells. They are
also present in the brain but at much lower concentrations than
CB1 receptors. CB2 receptor activation modulates immune function
and inflammation without producing psychoactive effects. This makes
CB2 an attractive therapeutic target for inflammatory and

### Other Receptors

Beyond CB1 and CB2, cannabis compounds interact with additional
receptors. The terpene (E)-β-caryophyllene is a selective CB2
agonist. THC also acts at TRPV1 (vanilloid) receptors involved in
pain signaling, and CBD interacts with 5-HT1A serotonin receptors,
GPR55 (an orphan receptor), and PPARγ nuclear receptors. This
polypharmacology contributes to the complex and multifaceted
effects of whole-plant cannabis preparations.

## Four Prime Cannabinoids

### Anandamide (AEA)

Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamine) is the first discovered
endocannabinoid, named from the Sanskrit word "ananda" meaning
bliss. It is a partial agonist at CB1 receptors, produced on demand
in response to physiological needs, and rapidly degraded by the
enzyme fatty [[lysergic-acid-amide-containing-plants-halpern]] hydrolase (FAAH). Anandamide plays roles
in mood regulation, pain modulation, and the generation of feelings
of well-being and happiness. Blesching describes it with the
shorthand "I am at ease."

### Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC)

Δ9-Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is the primary psychoactive component
of cannabis and a partial agonist at both CB1 and CB2 receptors. It
mimics the action of anandamide at CB1 receptors but is more stable
and longer-lasting due to its resistance to enzymatic degradation.
THC is responsible for the characteristic cannabis "high" and
contributes to therapeutic effects including pain relief, appetite
stimulation, anti-nausea, and muscle relaxation. Its psychoactive
effects can also produce anxiety, paranoia, and cognitive impairment
at higher doses.

### Cannabidiol (CBD)

Cannabidiol is the second most abundant cannabinoid in cannabis and
has no significant psychoactivity at normal doses. CBD modulates the
effects of THC by competing for receptor access and by inhibiting the
enzymes that metabolize THC. It has demonstrated anti-inflammatory,
anxiolytic, antipsychotic, anticonvulsant, and neuroprotective
properties in preclinical and clinical studies. CBD's broad
therapeutic profile and lack of intoxicating effects make it
attractive for medical applications.

### (E)-β-Caryophyllene ((E)-BCP)

(E)-β-Caryophyllene is a sesquiterpene found in cannabis and many
other plants including [[comparison-black-pepper-vs-cardamom-tropical-spice-cultivation]], cloves, and rosemary. It is
notable as a selective CB2 receptor agonist—the first dietary
cannabinoid identified. Its CB2 activity provides anti-inflammatory
and analgesic effects without psychoactivity. (E)-BCP represents the
growing recognition that non-cannabinoid compounds in cannabis
contribute meaningfully to the plant's overall therapeutic effects.

## Sativa vs Indica

The traditional classification of cannabis into sativa and indica
varieties has practical significance for therapeutic use. Blesching
presents this as a spectrum rather than a strict binary, noting
that sativa-dominant varieties tend to produce more energizing,
cerebral, and uplifting effects, while indica-dominant varieties
tend toward sedating, body-focused, and relaxing effects. This
distinction, while not perfectly correlated with chemical profile,
provides a useful framework for patients selecting strains for
specific therapeutic purposes.
