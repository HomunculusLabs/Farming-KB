# Light Spectrum and Photomorphogenesis

Marijuana needs light with the proper spectrum and intensity to ensure rapid growth. Light is comprised of separate bands of colors, each sending the plant a separate signal and promoting different types of growth. Understanding how cannabis responds to different wavelengths and photoperiods is essential for optimizing indoor cultivation.

## Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR)

Plants need and use only certain portions of the light spectrum. The most important colors for maximum chlorophyll production and photosynthetic response are in the blue and red range. The main portion of light used by plants falls between 400 and 700 nanometers (nm) -- this region is called the Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) zone.

PAR watts measure the actual amount of specific photons a plant needs to grow. Photons are a measure of light energy; photosynthesis is activated by the assimilation of photons. Blue photons are worth more PAR watts than red photons, though scientists have difficulty measuring the exact difference. Each color of light activates different plant functions -- positive tropism (the plant's ability to orient leaves toward light) is controlled by specific portions of the spectrum.

Some scientists define the PAR zone as 350-750 nanometers, which yields slightly higher PAR watt calculations. The nanometer is one billionth of a meter; light wavelengths are measured in nanometers.

## Light Measurement

Light is most commonly measured in foot-candles, lux, or lumens -- all of which measure light visible to the human eye, not to plants. The human eye is most sensitive to light between 525-625 nanometers, while plants use the broader PAR spectrum. Foot-candle and lux meters underestimate the light available to plants because they miss the important blue and red portions of the spectrum.

## Kelvin Temperature and Color Spectrum

Light color is measured in Kelvin temperature, which expresses the exact color a bulb emits. Bulbs with a Kelvin temperature from 3000 to 6500 are best for growing marijuana. The Color Corrected Temperature (CCT) is the peak Kelvin temperature at which colors in a bulb are stable. The Color Rendering Index (CRI) indicates bulb quality for growing -- the higher the CRI, the better.

Metal halide lamps typically range from 3200K to 6500K (more blue spectrum), while high pressure sodium lamps range from 2000K to 2700K (more red/yellow spectrum). Adding a touch more blue light helps prevent most plants from stretching. Spectrum-enhanced HPS lamps incorporate more blue into their output to provide a more complete spectrum.

## Blue Light Effects

More blue light in the spectrum increases the number of branches and overall plant compactness. Metal halide lamps, with their stronger blue spectrum, produce shorter, bushier plants with tighter internodal spacing. Blue-dominant light during vegetative growth promotes strong stem development and lateral branching. "Daylight spectrum" fluorescent lamps (5000-6500K) provide sufficient blue light for cloning and early vegetative growth.

## Red Light Effects

Red spectrum light (600-700nm) promotes flowering response and stem elongation. HPS lamps, rich in red/yellow light, are preferred for flowering because they stimulate bud production. However, pure HPS spectrum can cause excessive stretching if used alone during vegetative growth.

## Photoperiod and Flowering

Cannabis is photoperiodic-reactive; flowering is induced by changing the light cycle. The standard method is shifting from 18 hours light/6 hours dark to 12 hours light/12 hours dark. The 12-hour dark period must be total and uninterrupted -- even brief light leaks can prevent flowering or cause hermaphroditism.

Less than 12 hours of light will not induce flowering any faster and actually reduces flower formation and yield. More than 12 hours of light often prolongs flowering. Some growers achieve higher yields by inducing flowering via 12-hour photoperiod, then changing to 13-14 hours after 2-4 weeks (about 10% yield increase but prolonged flowering).

### Strain-Specific Responses

Sativa-dominant plants from the tropics respond to long days better than indica-dominant plants. On the equator, days and nights are nearly equal year-round. Tropical sativas like 'Haze' flower slowly for three months or longer even under a 12-hour photoperiod. Indica-dominant varieties from northern latitudes tend to flower sooner and respond more quickly; many indica varieties will flower under a 14/10 or 13/11 photoperiod.

### Jump-Starting Flowering

Give plants 36 hours of total darkness just before inducing flowering with the 12/12 photoperiod. Three contiguous 12-hour nights sends plants an unmistakable signal to flower sooner. Growers report pistil formation within one week rather than the typical two weeks.

### Working in the Dark Period

A green light bulb can be used to work in the indoor garden during the dark period. Green light does not affect the photoperiod of flowering plants because cannabis does not respond to the green portion of the light spectrum.

## LED Spectrum Tuning

The advent of LED grow lighting has given cultivators unprecedented control over the light spectrum delivered to cannabis plants. Unlike HID lamps, which produce a broad, fixed spectrum determined by the gas mixture and arc tube chemistry, LED fixtures can be engineered to emit specific wavelength combinations tailored to each growth stage. This capability has sparked ongoing debate about the optimal spectrum for cannabis cultivation and led to the development of two broadly contrasting approaches: full-spectrum white LEDs and tuned red/blue (narrowband) LEDs.

### Full-Spectrum White LEDs

Full-spectrum white LED fixtures use phosphor-coated blue LED chips to produce a broad, continuous spectrum that closely mimics natural daylight. The phosphor layer absorbs some of the blue light from the LED chip and re-emits it across a wider range of wavelengths, producing light with a color temperature typically between 3000K and 5000K. High-CRI (Color Rendering Index) full-spectrum LEDs approach the spectral quality of sunlight more closely than any HID lamp.

Advantages of full-spectrum white LEDs for cannabis include:

- **Broad spectral coverage**: Full-spectrum LEDs provide energy across the entire PAR range (400-700nm), including green and yellow wavelengths that are often underrepresented in tuned red/blue fixtures. While green light was historically considered unimportant for photosynthesis, research has shown that green light penetrates deeper into the canopy and drives photosynthesis in lower leaves that are shaded from blue and red wavelengths. This "green wave" effect can improve overall canopy photosynthesis.
- **Reduced stretching**: The inclusion of blue wavelengths (typically peaking at 450nm) in full-spectrum white LEDs promotes compact, bushy growth with tight internodal spacing, similar to the effect of metal halide lamps. This natural morphology is often considered more robust and easier to manage than the extreme compactness sometimes produced by heavy blue-dominant narrowband lighting.
- **Improved visual inspection**: Full-spectrum white light allows growers to accurately assess plant health, nutrient status, and pest problems. Under narrowband red/blue light, plants appear purple, making it difficult to detect yellowing, browning, or other color changes that indicate problems. Many growers run full-spectrum white LEDs alongside or alternating with narrowband fixtures specifically for this reason.
- **Single-fixture convenience**: A quality full-spectrum white LED can be used for both vegetative and flowering stages, eliminating the need to swap fixtures between rooms or adjust spectrum settings when transitioning plants. This simplicity is particularly valuable for perpetual grow systems.

### Tuned Red/Blue (Narrowband) LED Spectrums

Tuned red/blue LED fixtures concentrate their output in the two wavelength bands most efficiently absorbed by chlorophyll: blue (430-470nm) and red (630-670nm). This approach is grounded in the McCree curve and the original PAR research showing that these two bands drive the majority of photosynthetic activity. Some advanced narrowband fixtures also include far-red (730nm) LEDs to influence phytochrome-mediated responses.

Advantages of tuned red/blue spectrums include:

- **Higher energy efficiency for photosynthesis**: By concentrating photons in the wavelengths that chlorophyll absorbs most efficiently, tuned red/blue fixtures can achieve higher photosynthetic photon efficacy (micromoles per joule) than full-spectrum white fixtures. This means more usable light per watt of electricity consumed.
- **Targeted morphogenic control**: The ratio of red to blue light can be adjusted to influence plant morphology. A higher blue ratio (e.g., 30% blue, 70% red) produces shorter, more compact plants with thicker leaves -- ideal for vegetative growth. A higher red ratio (e.g., 10% blue, 90% red) promotes stem elongation and bud development -- preferred for flowering. Some modern fixtures allow the grower to adjust the red:blue ratio via a controller or app.
- **Far-red integration**: Adding far-red light (730nm) at the end of the daily photoperiod can accelerate flowering induction by rapidly converting the active phytochrome (Pfr) to the inactive form (Pr), simulating the onset of darkness. Research has shown that end-of-day far-red treatments can reduce flowering time by 5-10 days in some cannabis cultivars. Far-red is also involved in shade avoidance responses and can be used strategically to control plant height.

The primary disadvantages of narrowband red/blue fixtures are the unnatural purple appearance of the light (which complicates visual plant inspection), the potential for abnormal morphology if the spectrum is too extreme (excessively compact growth under heavy blue, or excessive stretching under heavy red), and the risk of missing beneficial wavelengths outside the chlorophyll absorption peaks.

### Hybrid and Adjustable Spectrum Fixtures

Many modern LED grow lights combine elements of both approaches, using a mix of full-spectrum white LED strips and supplemental narrowband red, blue, and far-red LED chips. These hybrid fixtures aim to provide the broad spectral coverage of white light with the targeted efficiency of narrowband LEDs. Some advanced models offer programmable spectrum control, allowing the grower to shift the spectral output throughout the day (simulating dawn-to-dusk spectral changes) or adjust it for different growth stages with a single fixture.

## Light Intensity and Canopy Penetration

While spectrum quality determines the type of growth responses cannabis exhibits, light intensity determines the magnitude of those responses and the overall rate of photosynthesis. Understanding how to measure, deliver, and optimize light intensity throughout the canopy is essential for maximizing yield and quality.

### PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)

PPFD is the standard metric for measuring light intensity in horticulture. It quantifies the number of photosynthetically active photons (400-700nm) that arrive at a given surface area per second, expressed in micromoles per square meter per second (µmol/m²/s). PPFD is measured with a quantum sensor (PAR meter), which counts photons rather than measuring lumens or foot-candles.

Different growth stages and lighting technologies have different optimal PPFD ranges:

- **Clones and seedlings**: 100-300 µmol/m²/s. Young plants are easily stressed by excessive light and require gentle illumination until they develop sufficient leaf area and root systems.
- **Vegetative growth**: 300-600 µmol/m²/s. Most cannabis cultivars thrive in this range during vegetative growth, with compact indica-dominant strains tolerating the higher end and stretch-prone sativas preferring the lower end.
- **Flowering**: 600-1,500 µmol/m²/s. The flowering stage benefits from the highest light intensity the plants can tolerate without stress. Many commercial growers target 800-1,000 µmol/m²/s as a practical balance between yield and energy cost. Some elite cultivars grown under CO2 enrichment can utilize intensities up to 1,500 µmol/m²/s, but this requires careful management of temperature, humidity, and nutrients.
- **Light stress threshold**: Above approximately 1,500-2,000 µmol/m²/s, most cannabis cultivars exhibit signs of light stress, including leaf curling (canoeing), chlorosis (bleaching of upper canopy leaves), and reduced photosynthetic efficiency due to photoinhibition.

### DLI (Daily Light Integral)

PPFD measures instantaneous light intensity, but the total amount of light a plant receives over the course of a day is equally important. The Daily Light Integral (DLI) quantifies this total, expressed in moles of photons per square meter per day (mol/m²/d). DLI is calculated by multiplying the average PPFD by the number of seconds in the photoperiod and dividing by one million:

DLI = (PPFD × photoperiod in hours × 3600) / 1,000,000

For example, a flowering room delivering 900 µmol/m²/s for 12 hours produces a DLI of 38.9 mol/m²/d. Recommended DLI targets for cannabis are:

- **Clones/seedlings**: 6-12 mol/m²/d
- **Vegetative**: 12-20 mol/m²/d (under 18 hours, 200-300 µmol/m²/s achieves this range)
- **Flowering**: 25-45 mol/m²/d (under 12 hours, 600-1,000 µmol/m²/s achieves this range)

DLI provides a useful framework for comparing lighting systems regardless of spectrum or technology. A grower can use DLI targets to set lighting intensity and duration, ensuring that plants receive a consistent daily light "dose" regardless of the specific fixture being used.

### Strategies for Even Canopy Illumination

Achieving uniform light distribution across the entire canopy is one of the most important and often overlooked aspects of grow room lighting. Light intensity decreases rapidly with distance from the source (following the inverse square law), and the canopy of a mature cannabis plant is inherently uneven due to branching patterns, plant height variation, and bud development.

- **Canopy management**: Training techniques such as low-stress training (LST), screen of green (ScrOG), and defoliation are used to create a flat, even canopy that maximizes light interception. A uniform canopy height ensures that all bud sites receive similar light intensity, rather than the upper canopy receiving too much light (causing stress) and the lower canopy receiving too little (causing underdeveloped buds).
- **Light height adjustment**: The distance between the light source and the canopy should be set so that the PPFD at the canopy edge is at least 70-80% of the PPFD directly below the fixture. With HID lamps, this typically means positioning the lamp 18-36 inches above the canopy. With LED fixtures (which have lower heat output and can be positioned closer), 12-24 inches is typical. Using a PAR meter to map PPFD at multiple points across the canopy is the most reliable way to optimize light height.
- **Light movers**: As described in advanced grow room design, motorized light movers distribute light more evenly by continuously changing the angle and distance of illumination. This reduces hot spots directly under the fixture and improves light penetration to the outer edges of the growing area.
- **Overlapping coverage**: When using multiple fixtures, positioning them so that their coverage areas overlap ensures more uniform intensity at the boundaries between fixtures. A rule of thumb is to space fixtures so that the PPFD at the point equidistant between two fixtures is at least 75% of the PPFD directly under either fixture.
- **Reflective walls**: Flat white paint, Mylar, or Foylon on the grow room walls reflects light back into the canopy from the sides, increasing total light interception and reducing the intensity gradient from center to edge. This is particularly important for single-fixture setups where the walls are the only source of side lighting.
- **Supplemental side lighting**: In large rooms or with tall plants, vertical or side-mounted LED strips can provide supplemental light to the lower canopy. This is especially valuable during late flowering when dense bud sites on lower branches may receive inadequate light from overhead fixtures alone.
