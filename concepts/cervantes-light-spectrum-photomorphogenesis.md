## Light Intensity and Canopy Penetration

While spectrum quality determines the type of growth responses cannabis exhibits, light intensity determines the magnitude of those responses and the overall rate of photosynthesis. Understanding how to measure, deliver, and optimize light intensity throughout the canopy is essential for maximizing yield and quality.

### PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)

PPFD is the standard metric for measuring light intensity in horticulture. It quantifies the number of photosynthetically active photons (400-700nm) that arrive at a given surface area per second, expressed in micromoles per square meter per second (µmol/m²/s). PPFD is measured with a quantum sensor (PAR meter), which counts photons rather than measuring lumens or foot-candles.

Different growth stages and lighting technologies have different optimal PPFD ranges:

- **Clones and seedlings**: 100-300 µmol/m²/s. Young plants are easily stressed by excessive light and require gentle illumination until they develop sufficient leaf area and root systems.
- **[[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]**: 300-600 µmol/m²/s. Most cannabis cultivars thrive in this range during vegetative growth, with compact indica-dominant strains tolerating the higher end and stretch-prone sativas preferring the lower end.
- **Flowering**: 600-1,500 µmol/m²/s. The [[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]] benefits from the highest light intensity the plants can tolerate without stress. Many commercial growers target 800-1,000 µmol/m²/s as a practical balance between yield and energy cost. Some elite cultivars grown under CO2 enrichment can utilize intensities up to 1,500 µmol/m²/s, but this requires careful management of temperature, humidity, and nutrients.
- **Light stress threshold**: Above approximately 1,500-2,000 µmol/m²/s, most cannabis cultivars exhibit signs of light stress, including leaf curling (canoeing), chlorosis (bleaching of upper canopy leaves), and reduced photosynthetic efficiency due to photoinhibition.

### DLI (Daily Light Integral)

PPFD measures instantaneous light intensity, but the total amount of light a plant receives over the course of a day is equally important. The Daily Light Integral (DLI) quantifies this total, expressed in moles of photons per square meter per day (mol/m²/d). DLI is calculated by multiplying the average PPFD by the number of seconds in the photoperiod and dividing by one million:

DLI = (PPFD × photoperiod in hours × 3600) / 1,000,000

For example, a flowering room delivering 900 µmol/m²/s for 12 hours produces a DLI of 38.9 mol/m²/d. Recommended DLI targets for cannabis are:

- **Clones/seedlings**: 6-12 mol/m²/d
- **Vegetative**: 12-20 mol/m²/d (under 18 hours, 200-300 µmol/m²/s achieves this range)
- **Flowering**: 25-45 mol/m²/d (under 12 hours, 600-1,000 µmol/m²/s achieves this range)

DLI provides a useful framework for comparing lighting systems regardless of spectrum or technology. A grower can use DLI targets to set lighting intensity and duration, ensuring that plants receive a consistent daily light "dose" regardless of the specific fixture being used.

### Strategies for Even Canopy Illumination

Achieving uniform light distribution across the entire canopy is one of the most important and often overlooked aspects of [[cannabis-grow-room-setup]] lighting. Light intensity decreases rapidly with distancescreen-of-greene (following the inverse square law), and the canopy of a mature cannabis plant is inherently uneven due to branching patterns, plant height variation, and bud development.

- **Canopy management**: [[mushroom-grow-room-design]], motorized light movers distribute light more evenly by continuously changing the angle and distance of illumination. This reduces hot spots directly under the fixture and improves light penetration to the outer edges of the growing area.
- **Overlapping coverage**: When using multiple fixtures, positioning them so that their coverage areas overlap ensures more uniform intensity at the boundaries between fixtures. A rule of thumb is to space fixtures so that the PPFD at the point equidistant between two fixtures is at least 75% of the PPFD directly under either fixture.
- **Reflective walls**: Flat white paint, Mylar, or Foylon on the grow room walls reflects light back into the canopy from the sides, increasing total light interception and reducing the intensity gradient from center to edge. This is particularly important for single-fixture setups where the walls are the only source of side lighting.
- **Supplemental side lighting**: In large rooms or with tall plants, vertical or side-mounted LED strips can provide supplemental light to the lower canopy. This is especially valuable during late flowering when dense bud sites on lower branches may receive inadequate light from overhead fixtures alone.
