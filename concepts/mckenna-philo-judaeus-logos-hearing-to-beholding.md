## Philo Judaeus and the Logos: From Things Heard to Things Beheld

## The Philonic Logos and Tryptamine Synesthesia

In his 1983 Esalen lecture on [[terence-mckenna]] invoked the first-century Hellenistic Jewish philosopher Philo Judaeus to describe a pivotal transition within the [[psilocybin-and-tryptamine-hallucinogens]].

## Application to Tryptamine Phenomenology

McKenna's deployment of this Philonic concept was not merely decorative or analogical.

The tryptamine experience, particularly the DMT experience, involves a precise phenomenological transition that mirrors Philo's description with remarkable specificity.

The experience unfolds in distinguishable phases that correspond to Philo's epistemological stages.

### The Auditory Phase

The initial moments of the DMT experience are dominated by sound.

McKenna described the characteristic "[[mckenna-radio-entelechy-dmt-onset-acoustic-phenomena]] ripping out of the [[mckenna-cognitive-dissonance-translinguistic-object]] that McKenna identified as characteristic of this phase—the "wow" or "flutter" at the edge of comprehension—corresponds to the epistemological tension between two modes of knowing that have not yet been fully integrated.

### The Visual Phase

In the full-blown tryptamine state, meaning is beheld directly.

The [[mckenna-translinguistic-objects-hyperdimensional-language]] that McKenna described—"feeling-toned, meaning-toned, three-dimensional rotating complexes of transforming light and color"—are not symbols or representations of meaning but meaning itself presented in visual form.

The experiencer does not interpret these objects through a hermeneutic process; they apprehend them directly, as one apprehends a physical object in ordinary perception.

The Logos has become visible.

McKenna was emphatic that this is not hallucination in the ordinary sense. These objects have an ontological status that distinguishes them from the products of ordinary imagination.
