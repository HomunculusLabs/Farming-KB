# Soil Compaction Solutions

Soil compaction is the compression of soil particles, reducing pore space and restricting root growth, water infiltration, air exchange, and biological activity. It is one of the most common and damaging soil problems in gardens and farms.

## Causes

### Mechanical
- Heavy machinery (tractors, mowers) on wet soil
- Foot traffic in garden beds (especially when wet)
- Tilling creates a hardpan (plow pan) at the tiller depth
- Raindrop impact on bare soil

### Natural
- Clay soils naturally compact when wet
- Single-grain structure (sandy soils with no [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]])
- Sodium-affected soils (sodium disperses clay particles)

## Symptoms
- Water pools on the surface or runs off instead of infiltrating
- Plants are stunted with shallow, stubby root systems
- Roots grow horizontally instead of downward
- Soil is hard to dig (needs a pickaxe instead of a trowel)
- Few earthworms or other soil organisms
- Crop yields decline progressively over years

## Solutions (from least to most intensive)

### 1. Prevent Compaction
- **Designated paths**: Never walk on garden beds; use permanent paths
- **Raised beds**: Concentrate foot traffic in pathways between beds
- **Avoid working wet soil**: Wait until soil crumbles (not smears) when squeezed
- **Cover bare soil**: Mulch, cover crops, or living [[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos]] protect from rain impact
- **Broad tires/low pressure**: If using machinery, use the widest tires possible at lowest pressure

### 2. Biological Decompaction
- **Earthworms**: Nature's aerators. Encourage with organic matter, no tillage, and mulch. [[earthworm-breeding]] for active encouragement
- **Root channels**: Deep-rooted plants (daikon radish, chicory, comfrey) punch through compacted layers
- **Cover crops with taproots**: Daikon radish (tillage radish) creates 3-4 ft channels that decompose into macropores
- **[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]]**: Hyphae penetrate compacted soil and create micro-channels
- **Apply AAct**: Actively [[actively-aerated-compost-tea-aact-brewing]] tea restores biological communities that improve soil structure

### 3. Physical Remediation
- **Broadfork**: The best tool for relieving compaction without inverting the soil. Push the broadfork into the soil and rock back — it lifts and fractures the compaction layer without mixing soil horizons
- **Aerating with a fork**: Spike the soil at regular intervals with a digging fork, rocking slightly to create air channels
- **Subsoiling**: For severe cases, a tractor-drawn shank rips through the hardpan at 12-18 inch depth (one-time fix, follow with biological restoration)

### 4. Soil Building
- **Add organic matter**: Compost, aged manure, leaf mold — improves aggregation and creates stable pores
- **Avoid excessive tillage**: Tilling destroys soil aggregates and organic matter. Switch to no-till or minimal-till methods
- **Green manure crops**: Deep-rooted cover crops break compaction and add organic matter when chopped and dropped
- **Gypsum on clay soils**: Calcium displaces sodium, improving structure (use only on sodic soils with a soil test)

### 5. Design Solutions
- **Keyline plowing**: On sloping land, plow parallel to the keyline to redistribute water and break compaction
- **Swales**: Capture and infiltrate water along contour, reducing runoff pressure on compacted areas
- [[keyline-design]] for details on keyline methods
- [[swale-construction-guide]] for swale building

## Priority Approach
1. **Stop causing it** — paths, raised beds, no wet-soil working
2. **Add biology** — compost, AAct, [[pgpr-mycorrhizal-inoculants-microplant-establishment]]
3. **Plant roots through it** — daikon radish, chicory, comfrey
4. **Broadfork** if physical intervention is needed
5. **Add organic matter** — compost, mulch, cover crops
6. **Avoid re-compacting** — permanent beds, no-till going forward
