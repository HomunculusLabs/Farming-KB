# Photoreception in Plants — What a Plant Sees

## Overview

Plants perceive light through specialized photoreceptor proteins, most notably **phytochromes** and **cryptochromes**, which enable them to detect not only the presence of light but its colour, intensity, duration, and direction. This sensory capability underlies critical processes including phototropism (growth toward light), photoperiodism (measurement of day length for flowering), shade avoidance, germination, and circadian rhythms. [[daniel-chamovitz]]'s "What a Plant Knows" explores these sensory capabilities, drawing parallels between plant and animal photoreception.

## Human vs. Plant Light Perception

### Human Vision

Humans perceive light through photoreceptor cells in the retina:

- **Rods**: Contain rhodopsin; responsible for low-light vision.
- **Cones**: Contain photopsins (red, blue, green); responsible for colour vision.
- Different wavelengths are absorbed by different photopigments.
- The brain processes signals from millions of photoreceptors into a coherent image.

### Plant Light Sensing

Plants lack eyes and a brain but possess:

- **Photoreceptor proteins** that absorb specific wavelengths.
- **Signal transduction pathways** that convert light absorption into biochemical responses.
- No central processing — responses are direct biochemical cascades.

## Photoperiodism — Measuring Day Length

### The Maryland Mammoth Discovery

- The **Maryland Mammoth** variety of tobacco was discovered to flower only under short days.
- This led to the concept of **photoperiodism** — plants measuring day/night length to trigger flowering.
- Discovery meant farmers could manipulate flowering by controlling light exposure.

### Photoperiodic Categories

| Category | Requirement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Short-day plants | Flower when days are short | Chrysanthemums, soybeans, Maryland Mammoth tobacco |
| Long-day plants | Flower when days are long | Irises, barley |
| Day-neutral plants | Flower regardless of day length | Tomato, rice |

### Day vs. Night Measurement

Key question in photoperiodism research: **Do plants measure the length of the day or the night?**

- Experiments demonstrated that for short-day plants, it is the **length of the uninterrupted dark period** that matters — not the day length.
- A brief flash of light during the dark period can prevent flowering in short-day plants.
- This suggested that plants accumulate a "dark-time signal" that is reset by light.

## Photoreceptor Proteins

### Phytochromes

- Detect **red and far-red light** (600–750 nm).
- Exist in two interconvertible forms:
  - **Pr** (red-light absorbing form): Active in darkness.
  - **Pfr** (far-red light absorbing form): Biologically active form.
- Red light converts Pr → Pfr; far-red light converts Pfr → Pr.
- This system allows plants to detect the ratio of red to far-red light, which indicates:
  - Whether the plant is in direct sunlight or shade.
  - Whether neighbouring plants are competing for light.

### Cryptochromes

- Detect **blue and UV-A light** (320–500 nm).
- Related to DNA photolyase enzymes.
- Involved in:
  - Circadian rhythm regulation.
  - Phototropism (in combination with phototropins).
  - Inhibition of stem elongation under blue light.

### Phototropins

- Detect **blue light** for phototropic responses (growth toward light).
- First identified in Darwin's experiments with oat coleoptiles.

## Darwin's Contributions to Plant Photobiology

Charles Darwin, alongside his son Francis, conducted pioneering experiments on plant phototropism:

- Demonstrated that the **tip of the coleoptile** (the protective sheath covering emerging grass shoots) was necessary for phototropic response.
- If the tip was covered or removed, the plant did not bend toward light.
- If the tip was covered with an **opaque cap**, no bending occurred.
- If covered with a **transparent cap**, bending still occurred.
- This showed that a signal was transmitted from the tip to the lower tissues, causing differential growth.

## Ecological Significance

### Shade Avoidance

- When a plant detects a low red:far-red ratio (indicating shade from other plants), it triggers:
  - Stem elongation (reaching for light).
  - Reduced branching.
  - Accelerated flowering.
- This "[[shade-avoidance-syndrome]]" is a major competitive strategy in plant communities.

### Circadian Rhythms

- Plants maintain internal 24-hour clocks synchronized with light/dark cycles.
- Cryptochromes and phytochromes provide the light input to these clocks.
- Circadian rhythms regulate:
  - Leaf movement.
  - Stomatal opening/closing.
  - Photosynthetic gene expression.
  - Flower opening/closing.
