# Mushroom Cultivation Cost and Profit Analysis

The FAO training manual provides detailed cost calculations for mushroom bag production,
startup investment estimates, and profitability projections at various scales. These figures,
based on conditions in Ubon Ratchathani province, Thailand (as of February 2000), demonstrate
that mushroom cultivation can be a viable enterprise for people with disabilities when
managed properly.

## Cost Per Mushroom Bag

### Raw Material Costs

| Item | Cost per Bag (Baht) |
|------|---------------------|
| Sawdust | 0.76 |
| PP Bags (plastic) | 0.24 |
| Rice bran | 0.23 |
| Gypsum | 0.06 |
| Limestone | 0.03 |
| Magnesium sulfate | 0.02 |
| Spawn | 0.16 |
| Plastic neck | 0.14 |
| Fuel (corncob and wood) | 0.26 |
| Cotton, rubber band, alcohol | 0.07 |
| Total materials | 1.97 |

### Full Cost Structure

| Category | Cost per Bag (Baht) |
|----------|---------------------|
| Materials only | 1.97 |
| Materials plus labor | 2.52 |
| Including contamination (5%), unforeseen | 2.88 |
| Plus 20% overhead | 3.46 |
| Sales price (plus 17% profit) | 3.95 |

Labor costs include making bags (0.30), pasteurization (0.07),
[[mushroom-training-inoculation-techniques]] (0.05), farm transport (0.03), and
incubation monitoring (0.10). Water and electricity are generally free in remote areas.

## Key Cost Justifications

- Sawdust: 982.39 Baht/ton; 100 kg produces 130 bags = 0.76 Baht/bag
- Plastic bags: 47 Baht/kg; 200 bags per kg = 0.24 Baht/bag
- Rice bran: 5 Baht/kg; 6 kg per 100 kg sawdust = 0.23 Baht/bag
- Spawn: 5 Baht per bottle; one bottle inoculates 30 bags = 0.16 Baht/bag
- Fuel: corn cob (900 Baht/ton) and wood (2,000 Baht/ton) = 0.26 Baht/bag

## Startup Investment

### Minimum (Buying Inoculated Bags)

| Item | Cost (Baht) |
|------|-------------|
| Mushroom house 3m x 4m (2,000 bags, 2 years) | 500 to 2,000 |
| 1,000 inoculated bags (3 months production) | 3,500 |
| Total minimum investment | 4,000 to 5,500 |

### Making Own Bags (Additional Equipment)

| Item | Cost (Baht) |
|------|-------------|
| 200-liter oil drums for pasteurization | 400 to 700 |
| Gas tank and burner (optional) | 2,000 to 3,000 |
| Other tools (basket, sprayer, etc.) | 1,000 |
| Cement floor (optional) | 4,000 |
| Compacted earth floor | Free |

## Profitability Projections

Based on 250 grams yield per bag and 25 Baht/kg sales price:

| Bags | Total Income (Baht) | Monthly Income | Net Profit (Buying Bags) | Net Profit (Making Bags) |
|------|---------------------:|---------------:|-------------------------:|-------------------------:|
| 1,000 | 6,250 | 2,083 | 750 | 1,333 |
| 2,000 | 12,500 | 4,167 | 1,500 | 2,667 |
| 3,000 | 18,750 | 6,250 | 2,250 | 4,000 |
| 4,000 | 25,000 | 8,333 | 3,000 | 5,333 |
| 6,000 | 37,500 | 12,500 | 4,500 | 8,000 |

Each bag produces for approximately 3 months. Monthly income = total income divided by 3.
No labor costs included since the farmer's own labor generates income. Further income can
come from selling spawn bags.

## Additional Income Streams

- Selling inoculated [[mushroom-training-substrate-preparation]] to other
  farmers
- Selling spent substrate as compost or organic fertilizer
- Processing mushrooms into value-added [[mushroom-training-shiitake-cultivation]]
- Producing and selling spawn
- Growing [[mushroom-training-straw-mushroom-cultivation]] from recycled
  substrate

## Financial Management Records

The manual recommends seven essential records for
[[mushroom-training-marketing-business]]:
1. Monthly cash flow statement (daily: date, expenses, income, balance, comments)
2. Inventory book (raw materials, prices, dates, suppliers)
3. Mushroom sales record (all daily sales)
4. Raw materials sales record
5. Credit control system (with payment due dates)
6. Petty cash voucher system
7. Cash book (bank inflows and outflows)

## Checking Profitability

To verify sustainability, compare total income from mushroom sales against total costs,
including contamination losses (target less than 5%). If net monthly profit exceeds the cost
of buying new bags for the next production cycle, the farm is financially sustainable.
Trainers should encourage clear record keeping and help identify causes when the farm is not
profitable.
