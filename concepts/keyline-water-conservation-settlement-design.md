## Keyline Water Conservation and Settlement Design in Humid Landscapes

The humid landscape presents unique opportunities for [[water-conservation]], landform management, and settlement design as a unified system.

## Keyline System of Farm Water Management

The classic "S" profile of the humid landscape is where P.A. Yeomans developed his **Keyline approach** to farm water management. This system integrates several elements into a whole-system design:

- **Open water storage** with dams and ponds
- **Soil reconditioning** through cultivation techniques
- **Soil water storage** via swales and contour management
- **Fencing** aligned to water flow patterns
- **Farm forestry** integrated with water distribution

The classic Keyline system of gravity-flow irrigation applies primarily to gentle foothill country in the headwater regions of first and second order streams. However, skilled designers working over a very wide range of soils and landscapes can achieve the essentials of drought-proofing through:

- Gravity-flow irrigation from high-point storage
- Soil conditioning prior to forest or pasture establishment
- **Fire control by downslope flooding** — a dual-purpose water use
- Integrated forest-farm systems where water serves multiple functions

## The Design Sequence for Humid Landscapes

The overriding design input into humid-area landscape planning is **water management**, followed by access planning (roads and tracks), and then plant and animal system planning. In water planning, the sequence follows elevation:

1. Start from the **highest accessible slopes**
2. Lead water flow via the **longest routes downhill** (working on or near contour)
3. Create small dams, swales, rip-lines along the way
4. Integrate energy systems to take advantage of abundant rainfall

## Functions of Water in the Landscape

Water serves multiple simultaneous duties in the designed landscape:

- **Irrigation**: As soil and pond storage to irrigate crops and forests
- **Domestic use**: In homes for cooking, drinking, cleaning, and toilet uses
- **Energy**: As a source of energy via turbines or hydraulic pressure
- **Aquaculture**: In growing fish and aquatic plants as a growing medium in itself
- **Nutrient transport**: As a carrier for nutrients — nutrients added to upstream or uphill areas are distributed downstream to crops
- **Recreation and aesthetics**: Recreational and aesthetic uses for human wellbeing

## Settlement Design Principles

The greatest cost in house maintenance in temperate cold areas is space and water heating; together these may constitute **80% of all domestic energy costs**. Both settlement design overall and house design in detail are critical conservation factors — more so than in tropical areas or hot deserts. Not only housing, but barns, outbuildings, and livestock shelters must be solid, well-designed, and carefully assessed for thermal efficiency.

### Site Selection

Settlement site choice and surrounding landscape plantings are so critical as to provide for **70% of heat energy conservation** in cool climates; structural techniques cope with the remaining 30%. Key factors in sustainable settlement creation include:
