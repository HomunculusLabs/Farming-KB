# Aphids on Cannabis

Aphids (also called plant lice) are small sap-sucking insects that attack cannabis plants. They are easy to spot with the naked eye and can rapidly multiply to devastating numbers due to their extraordinary reproductive rate. Aphids are also the most significant disease vector among [[plant-disease-management-solomon]] as well.

## Identification

- Size: about the size of a pinhead — easily visible without magnification
- Colors: normally grayish to black, but can be green to pink — in any color, aphids attack plants
- Most aphids have no wings, but winged aphids are about four times the size of their bodies
- Use a 10X magnifying glass for positive identification
- Found in all climates, but most common indoors when plentiful outdoors
- Look for them under leaves, huddled around branch nodes, and growing tips
- Some species prefer succulent new growth; others prefer older foliage or flower buds
- When affixed to foliage and sucking fluid, aphids are unable to move — making them easy to crush
- Aphids are often found in dense colonies on the most tender plant tissue
- Cast skins (white flecks from molting) may be visible among aphid colonies

## Life Cycle and Reproduction

Aphids have an extraordinary reproductive capacity that makes them one of the fastest-reproducing pests:

- Give birth to mainly live female larvae without mating (parthenogenesis) — no male required
- Can produce 3-100 hungry larvae every day depending on species and conditions
- Each female reproduces 40-100 offspring over her lifetime
- Offspring start reproducing soon after birth — multiple generations overlap
- Winged aphids are often the first to enter the garden, flying in from outdoor populations
- Install yellow [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]] produces eggs that overwinter, starting the cycle again the following spring
- Warmer temperatures accelerate reproduction dramatically — populations can explode in warm conditions
- A single aphid colony can expand from a few individuals to thousands within weeks

## The Ant-Aphid Relationship

As they feed, aphids exude sticky honeydew that attracts ants. Ants like honeydew so much that they farm aphids — moving them to uninfected plants to ensure a continued honeydew supply. Look for columns of ants marching around plants as an indicator of aphid presence. Any aphid control program must also control ants if they are present.

Control ants by mixing borax with hand soap or borax powder with powdered sugar. Ants eat the sweet mix, and the borax kills them. They excrete the sweet borax mix in the nest where other ants eat it and die. This approach controls the entire ant colony, not just individual foragers.

## Damage Symptoms

- **Sap extraction**: Aphids suck life-giving sap from foliage, causing leaves to wilt and yellow
- **Honeydew**: Sticky excretion that attracts ants and encourages sooty mold growth on leaf surfaces
- **Disease vectors**: Aphids transport (vector) bacteria, fungi, and viruses — they vector more viruses than any other source
- **Plant preference**: They prefer to attack weak, stressed plants, making overall plant health important for prevention
- **Sooty mold**: Destructive black sooty mold grows on honeydew deposits, blocking photosynthesis and further weakening the plant
- **Curling and distortion**: Heavily infested leaves may curl and become distorted from feeding damage
- **Stunted growth**: Continuous sap extraction reduces plant vigor and slows development
- **Reduced yield**: Heavy infestations during flowering can significantly impact bud development
- **Transmission of viruses**: Aphids can spread [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]] or [[knf-soap-water-and-hot-pepper]], and soap-based sprays work well
- **Insecticidal soap**: Effective against soft-bodied aphids; apply at regular intervals for ongoing control
- **Neem oil**: Effective as both contact spray and systemic antifeedant; disrupts aphid feeding and reproduction
- **Horticultural oil**: Smothers aphids on contact; particularly effective against eggs

## Chemical Controls

- **Pyrethrum** (aerosol): Apply 2-3 times at 5-10 day intervals for knockdown of adults
- **Insecticidal soap**: Effective against all life stages; apply at regular intervals
- **Imidacloprid**: Systemic insecticide effective against aphids, but generally not recommended for consumable crops
- Rotate chemical controls to prevent resistance development
- Always observe pre-harvest intervals when using any chemical control
