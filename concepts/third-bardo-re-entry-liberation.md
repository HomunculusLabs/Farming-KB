# Third Bardo Re-Entry and Liberation

The Third Bardo, as described in *The [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]* (based on the *Tibetan Book of the Dead*), is the stage of [[psychedelic-consciousness-expansion]] where the voyager struggles to return to ego-based reality after the dissolution experienced in the earlier bardos. This phase is characterized by confusion, disorientation, and a series of visions that test the voyager's capacity for recognition and liberation. The instructions in this phase are among the most critical in the manual, as they address the final moments before re-entry into ordinary consciousness.

## Signs of the Third Bardo

The voyager entering the Third Bardo may experience several characteristic phenomena:

- **Mental restlessness**: The mind, lacking its habitual ego-structure as a resting place or focus, races uncontrollably like a feather tossed by the wind or a rider on a runaway horse. The voyager calls out for their old ego in despair.
- **Confusion and bewilderment**: Questions about sanity arise. Familiar people, places, and relatives appear "as in a dream" or "through a glass darkly."
- **Feelings of oppression**: Sensations of being squeezed between rocks or imprisoned in a cage may arise, signaling attempts to force a return to the ego.
- **Dull gray light**: A murky, gray suffusion of all objects is another hallmark sign.
- **Fish-out-of-water misery**: An intense sense of having been cast out of one's element, akin to a fish on red-hot embers.

The key instruction throughout these experiences is to refrain from holding onto thoughts, to allow the mind to rest in its unmodified state, and to meditate on the oneness of all energy. Struggling to explain or analyze the experience is explicitly counseled against.

## Re-Entry Visions

When the voyager cannot locate their past personality, they may begin to fear that they will return permanently changed. This triggers self-pity and a desperate search for the ego to regain control. During this wandering, different images of the future self appear, with the most probable trajectory showing most clearly.

The prescribed technique at this crucial moment is a profound meditative alternation:

1. **Meditate on the protective ideal** (a chosen figure of reverence) for as long as possible.
2. **Visualize the figure as a magician's illusion** — then let it melt away starting from the extremities until nothing remains.
3. **Enter the state of Clearness and Voidness** and abide there.
4. **Return to meditation on the protective ideal**, then again on the Clear Light.
5. **Allow one's own mind to melt away** gradually.

The text states: "Wherever the air pervades, consciousness pervades. Wherever consciousness pervades, serene ecstasy pervades." By abiding tranquilly in this uncreated state of serenity, paranoid rebirth is prevented and perfect enlightenment is gained.

## The All-Determining Influence of Thought

In the Third Bardo, the voyager's mind has no focus or integrating force. It is light and continuously moving. Because of this state, any thought that occurs — positive or negative — wields enormous power. The voyager is described as "extremely suggestible."

This section introduces the critical teaching that mood swings of great intensity will occur, "like the stretching and relaxing of a catapult," all determined by karma. The instructions are unequivocal:

- **Do not become attached to joys** or displeased by sorrows.
- **Prevent angry thoughts from arising**, no matter what companions do.
- **Meditate on love** for all beings.
- **Recognize that each companion is Buddha within.**

The text emphasizes that even at this late stage, the voyager is "only one second away from a life-changing joyous discovery." The power of thought in this state is so great that selfish thinking or anger can immediately produce hellish experiences.

## Judgment Visions

When the voyager experiences visions of judgment and guilt, the manual provides a radical reframing: no one is doing anything to them. The suffering is entirely the result of their own mental set — their karma. The mind itself is creating the problem.

The instructions for judgment visions include:

- **Float into meditation** rather than engaging with the content of the visions.
- **Remember former beliefs, teachings, and the friendly presence of companions.**
- **Concentrate on a single object or sensation** if formal meditation is not possible.
- **Recognize the illusory nature of existence and phenomena.**

The manual stresses that this moment is of great importance: "If you are distracted now it will take you a long time to get out of the quagmire of misery." Even if previous bardo experiences were not recognized, liberation is still possible here.

## Sexual Visions

Visions of mating couples and orgies may appear, triggering desire and anticipation. The instructions are clear:

- **Withhold from action or attachment.**
- **Float with the stream** and trust the process.
- **Reject both attraction and repulsion** to the sexual hallucinations.
- **Meditate on unity** with fellow voyagers.
- **Abandon jealousy.**

The text warns that attempting to enter into the old personality through sexual attraction or repulsion leads to rebirth on an "animal level" — a state of possessive desire, jealousy, stupidity, and misery.

## Four Methods of Preventing Re-Entry

### First Method: Meditation on the Buddha

The voyager is instructed to tranquilly meditate on their protective figure, understanding that this figure is "like the reflecting of the moon in water — apparent yet non-existent." After meditation, the visualized form is allowed to melt away from the extremities, and the voyager meditates without thought-formation upon the Void Clear Light. This is described as "a very profound art" that postpones rebirth and ensures a more illuminated future.

### Second Method: Meditation on Good Games

In this method, the voyager must form a single, firm resolve. Looking into a mirror, they will not see their usual self — a sign of being in the Third Bardo. The teaching emphasizes that "whatever you desire will come to pass" and warns against indecision: "If you give way to indecision for even a second, you will have to suffer misery for a long, long time." The instruction is to persevere with "good games" — one's highest aspirations and spiritual purposes.

### Third Method: Meditation on Illusion

All phenomena experienced in the Third Bardo — sexual activities, manipulation machinery, mocking laughter, dashing sounds, terrifying apparitions — are recognized as illusions. The text provides a series of metaphors: "like dreams and apparitions, non-permanent, non-fixed," "like echoes," "like cities of clouds," "like mirages," "like mirrored forms," "like phantasmagoria," "the moon seen in water." By holding one-pointedly to the understanding that these experiences are unreal, liberation is attained.

### Fourth Method: Meditation on the Void

The final method employs the formula: "All substances are part of my own consciousness. This consciousness is vacuous, unborn, and unceasing." The mind is allowed to rest in its uncreated state, "like the pouring of water into water," in its natural, unmodified condition — clear and vibrant. By maintaining this relaxed state, rebirth into routine game-reality is prevented.

## Choosing the Post-Session Personality

The final instructions address the imminent return to ordinary consciousness. The voyager is told that the signs and characteristics of the level of existence to come will appear as premonitory visions. The guidance is:

- **Follow pleasant, delightful visions** and avoid dark, unpleasant ones.
- **Return in radiance** rather than panic, as the mental state at return determines the subsequent state of being.
- **Choose impartially**, without attraction or repulsion.
- **Enter into game-existence voluntarily and freely**, with good grace and calm.

The text encapsulates the entire Third Bardo teaching in the sequence: hearing the words → recollection → recognition → liberation. This progression represents the ideal arc of the psychedelic experience, where the voyager moves from confusion through remembrance of instructions to the direct recognition of the nature of their experience, culminating in liberation from habitual patterns of mind.

## The Nature of the Third Bardo Experience

The Third Bardo differs fundamentally from the First and Second Bardos. Where the First Bardo presents the luminous Clear Light of reality and the Second Bardo presents the [[leary-peaceful-and-wrathful-visions]] deities as archetypal projections, the Third Bardo is characterized by a more fragmented, confused, and ego-oriented experience. The voyager is no longer in the expansive realms of pure consciousness but has descended into a state where habitual mental patterns reassert themselves with tremendous force.

This is why the instructions for the Third Bardo are so numerous and specific. The voyager needs concrete techniques to navigate the pull of old habits, the power of amplified suggestibility, and the vivid hallucinations that can easily be mistaken for reality. Each set of instructions addresses a different class of experience that typically arises.

The progression through the Third Bardo follows a recognizable pattern: confusion and disorientation, followed by the emergence of various classes of visions (re-entry, judgment, sexual), and finally the approach of re-entry itself. At each stage, the instructions provide both a framing (what is happening and why) and a technique (what to do about it).

## Relationship to the Original Tibetan Buddhist Context

Leary's *The Psychedelic Experience* adapts the *Tibetan Book of the Dead* (Bardo Thodol) for use in guided [[bardo-instruction-texts-psychedelic-sessions]]. In the original Buddhist context, the Third Bardo (Sidpa Bardo) refers to the period between [[shamanism-death-and-rebirth]], during which the consciousness wanders through various realms driven by karma before being drawn toward rebirth. Leary and his collaborators — Ralph Metzner and Richard Alpert — translated this framework into a model for understanding the stages of a psychedelic experience, with "re-entry" replacing "rebirth."

The instructions preserve the essential structure of the Tibetan original while adapting the language and context for [[shamanic-vs-western-psychedelic-healing]] voyagers. The four methods of preventing re-entry, for instance, correspond directly to traditional Tibetan Buddhist meditation practices for achieving liberation from the cycle of rebirth.

## Key Principles

- The Third Bardo is characterized by extreme suggestibility — every thought carries amplified power.
- Non-struggle is the primary orientation: do not hold on, do not push away.
- Liberation is available at every moment, even late in the session.
- The voyager's mental state at re-entry directly shapes their subsequent experience.
- All visions are understood as projections of the voyager's own mind — no external agent is acting upon them.
- The sequence of hearing → recollection → recognition → liberation represents the ideal arc through the Third Bardo.
- The voyager's karma (conditioned mental patterns) determines the nature and intensity of Third Bardo experiences.
