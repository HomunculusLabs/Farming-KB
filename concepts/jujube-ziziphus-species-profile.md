# Jujube (Ziziphus spp)

## Overview

The jujube ([[ziziphus-jujuba]]), also known as the Chinese red date, is a deciduous fruit tree of extraordinary versatility and resilience. Cultivated for so long that its exact origin is obscured—candidates include China, India, and the Mediterranean region (possibly Syria)—the jujube has been a staple food, medicine, and cultural symbol across Asia and the Middle East for millennia. Its combination of [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]], salt tolerance, nutritional value, and multiple product streams makes it one of the most underutilized fruit [[query-what-are-the-best-nitrogen-fixing-trees-for-permaculture]] systems in arid and semi-arid regions.

## Botanical Profile

- **Family**: Rhamnaceae
- **Primary species**: Ziziphus jujuba (syn. Z. vulgaris), Z. mauritiana (Indian jujube), Z. nummularia, Z. spina-christi (Christ thorn)
- **Growth habit**: Deciduous tree with upright habit; branches droop under fruit weight
- **Height**: Up to 9 meters
- **Hardiness**: Varies by species (see individual species profiles below)

## Species Profiles

### Ziziphus jujuba (Chinese Jujube)

The primary cultivated species, known as the Chinese red date. It prefers full sun and is well suited to deep, alkaline, and saline soils. It thrives under drought conditions but does not favor waterlogging or heavy clay soils. Damaged roots can cause the tree to send out suckers, which should be managed in cultivated settings.

**Fruit characteristics**: Light green to reddish brown, rich in vitamin C. When eaten fresh, the fruit has a crisp texture with a flavor between apple and date. It dries exceptionally well, making it a saleable dried product. The fruit is also boiled with millet and rice, stewed, baked, pickled, or used as a coffee substitute. It features in puddings, cakes, breads, jellies, soups, and sweetmeats. In Korea, jujube flour is made into a miso-like fermented hot pepper and soybean paste. The dried fruit is used in fish dishes and soups. The original "jube" candy was made from jujube fruit.

### Ziziphus mauritiana (Indian Jujube)

A more tropical species that withstands severe heat, drought, and frost. Rainfall requirements are as low as 300-500mm, though it occurs in areas up to 2000mm annually. It produces smaller fruit than Z. jujuba. The fruits are eaten fresh, dried, powdered into meal, boiled with rice, or used in preserves, jellies, sauces, and beverages. It is made into a liqueur (crema de ponsigne) and dried fruits are mixed with salt and tamarind pulp as a condiment.

**Additional uses**:
- **Silkworm host**: Leaves feed the tasar silkworm.
- **Lac insect host**: Used for shellac production.
- **Fodder**: Foliage is considered good fodder for cattle, camels, and goats.
- **Tanning**: Bark used for tanning leather.
- **Timber**: Fine-grained, hard, strong, reddish wood that takes a good polish.
- **Firewood**: Specific gravity of 0.93 with heat content of nearly 4,900 kcal/kg.
- **Young leaves**: Cooked and eaten as a vegetable in Indonesia.

### Ziziphus nummularia

A salt-tolerant species native to India and Pakistan with potential as a rootstock for more productive Ziziphus jujuba in saline conditions. It serves as both a good browse plant for livestock and a quality firewood, with heartwood calorific value of 4,400 kcal/kg.

### Ziziphus spina-christi (Christ Thorn)

Known as the Christ thorn because it is believed to be the tree used for the crown of thorns. It is a spiny bush or tree that strongly resists both heat and cold, making it adaptable to [[fungal-biodiversity-in-extreme-environments]]. Its thorny habit makes it useful as a living fence or barrier planting.

## Medicinal Uses

The fruits are soothing to the throat and the juice is used in decoctions for treating colds and sore throats. In traditional Chinese medicine, jujubes are used to:
- Sweeten bitter medicinal preparations
- Calm the mind and reduce anxiety
- Strengthen the spleen and stomach
- Nourish the blood

The fruits are also used in pharmacy to mask the taste of medicines, a traditional practice that continues in modern herbal preparations.

## Ecological Functions

- **Drought tolerance**: Among the most drought-resistant fruit trees, making it ideal for arid permaculture.
- **Salt tolerance**: Suitable for saline soils where few other fruit trees will grow.
- **Wind tolerance**: Upright habit and flexible branches withstand wind.
- **Soil building**: Leaf litter contributes organic matter to poor soils.
- **[[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]]**: Fruits feed birds and small mammals; thorny species provide nesting cover.
- **Living fence**: Spiny species (especially Z. spina-christi) serve as effective livestock barriers.
