### Ziziphus mauritiana (Indian Jujube)

A more tropical species that withstands severe heat, drought, and frost. Rainfall requirements are as low as 300-500mm, though it occurs in areas up to 2000mm annually. It produces smaller fruit than Z. jujuba. The fruits are eaten fresh, dried, powdered into meal, boiled with rice, or used in preserves, jellies, sauces, and beverages. It is made into a liqueur (crema de ponsigne) and dried fruits are mixed with salt and tamarind pulp as a condiment.

**Additional uses**:
- **Silkworm host**: Leaves feed the tasar silkworm.
- **Lac insect host**: Used for shellac production.
- **Fodder**: Foliage is considered good fodder for cattle, camels, and goats.
- **Tanning**: Bark used for tanning leather.
- **Timber**: Fine-grained, hard, strong, reddish wood that takes a good polish.
- **Firewood**: Specific gravity of 0.93 with heat content of nearly 4,900 kcal/kg.
- **Young leaves**: Cooked and eaten as a vegetable in Indonesia.

### Ziziphus nummularia

A salt-tolerant species native to India and Pakistan with potential as a rootstock for more productive Ziziphus jujuba in saline conditions. It serves as both a good browse plant for livestock and a quality firewood, with heartwood calorific value of 4,400 kcal/kg.

### Ziziphus spina-christi (Christ Thorn)

Known as the Christ thorn because it is believed to be the tree used for the crown of thorns. It is a spiny bush or tree that strongly resists both heat and cold, making it adaptable to [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]]**: Fruits feed birds and small mammals; thorny species provide nesting cover.
- **Living fence**: Spiny species (especially Z. spina-christi) serve as effective livestock barriers.
