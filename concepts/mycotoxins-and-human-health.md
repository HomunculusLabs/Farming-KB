## Mycotoxins and Human Health

Mycotoxins are poisonous compounds produced by fungi that contaminate food, indoor environments, and agricultural products. While most people are familiar with poisonous mushrooms, the more pervasive threats come from mold-produced toxins that operate invisibly in food chains and buildings. This page draws extensively from [[nicholas-money]]'s Mr. Bloomfield's Orchard.

## Major Mycotoxin Categories

### Aflatoxins

Produced by **Aspergillus flavus** and **Aspergillus parasiticus** on corn, peanuts, peanut butter, milk, eggs, and meat.

- Among the most potent naturally occurring mutagens and carcinogens
- Ring structure perfectly suited to bind DNA, creating adducts that cause genetic damage
- Colonize crops at all development stages, especially plants damaged by insects or environmental stress
- In heavily infested crops: up to 0.1 gram fungus per kilogram of plant material
- Contamination of dairy/meat occurs when livestock eat aflatoxin-tainted feed
- Traces found in human milk

**Historical incident — Turkey X Disease (1960, England):**
100,000 turkeys died from liver failure after eating aflatoxin-contaminated peanut meal. This disaster launched modern mycotoxin research.

**Cancer risk:** If rats are fed maximum anticipated human exposure levels, 1 in 10,000 develops liver cancer. Human incidence appears 10x lower than rodent models predict.

**Likely evolutionary purpose:** Mycotoxins probably target competing microorganisms (bacteria, other fungi) in the immediate vicinity of growing hyphae, not animals. Animals are collateral damage.

### Trichothecenes

Produced by **Stachybotrys chartarum** (black mold) and Fusarium species:

- Macrocyclic trichothecenes from Stachybotrys caused pulmonary hemorrhage in infants (Cleveland, Ohio, 1993-1994)
- **Act immediately** upon contact — unlike aflatoxins which take months/years
- Effects: burning/blistering skin, severe coughing, breathing difficulty, nausea, bloody diarrhea
- A few milligrams of T-2 toxin or diacetoxyscirpenol are lethal
- No specific antidotes exist
- Soap and water can remove from skin, but only if used immediately

**Biological warfare potential:**
- Iraqi scientists experimented with concentrated aflatoxins before the Gulf War
- Trichothecenes may have been used as "yellow rain" in Laos/Cambodia (1970s) and Afghanistan (1980s)
- Production techniques well within capability of anyone with a microbiology PhD

### Ergot Alkaloids

Produced by **Claviceps purpurea** (ergot fungus) on cereal grains:

- Historical "St. Anthony's Fire" epidemics caused mass hallucinations, gangrene, and death
- Led to the Salem witch trials and other episodes of mass hysteria
- Modern ergotism is rare due to grain cleaning procedures
- See [[entheogen-culture]] for the connection to the Eleusinian Mysteries

## Indoor Mold and Health

### Stachybotrys chartarum (Black Mold)

- Proclaimed as a "toxin-producing killer" in media following the Cleveland infant deaths
- Associated with water-damaged homes — burst pipes, flooded basements, wet drywall
- **Not all strains produce toxins** — only some strains produce trichothecenes
- Several other black molds (Cladosporium, Penicillium, Aspergillus, Alternaria) are far more prevalent in water-damaged homes but do NOT generate toxins
- Susan Kaminskyj's advice: "If this mold didn't kill you yesterday, it's unlikely that you'll die today"
- Treat isolated patches with diluted bleach, but address the moisture source

### Modern Housing and Mold Problems

- Polymer-wrapped, hermetically sealed modern homes create extremely stale air
- Spaces between wall layers can become humid with no vapor escape
- Air conditioning exacerbates moisture problems (prevalent in Houston, etc.)
- Mold-damaged homes can have thousands of spores per cubic meter of air
- Indoor air quality standard suggests >50 spores/m³ is a health risk

### Allergic Effects

- During summer, 500+ spores per cubic meter is common even in "clean" indoor air
- Broken hyphal fragments are just as allergenic as intact spores
- Culture plate methods can underrate total fungal particles by a factor of 100
- We inhale fungal particles from our first breath to our last

### Lycoperdonosis

- Rare respiratory illness from inhaling puffball spores
- Case: Wisconsin teenagers snorted puffball spores seeking hallucinogenic effects
- Millions of spiny spores entered lungs, causing fever, breathing difficulty, muscle pain
- Spores too small for macrophages to digest — they carry them as "hunchbacked immune cells"
- Recovery through natural mucociliary clearance and steroid therapy

## Fungal Antibiotics as Ecological Weapons

Fungi secrete antibiotics to:
- Remove competing microorganisms from their territory
- Obtain nitrogen from dead bacteria (plant tissues are sugar-rich but protein-poor)
- Species that form ink-caps, puffballs, and bird's nest fungi are adept bacterial predators
- See [[fungal-symbiosis-types]] and [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]] for ecological context

## Nematode-Trapping Fungi

Some fungi are active predators of nematode worms:
- A single gram of soil may contain 1,900 nematodes and 1,000+ meters of hyphae
- **Adhesive traps:** Short hyphal branches exude glue that cements passing worms
- **Explosive rings:** Inflate when disturbed by a worm, gripping with up to 18 atmospheres pressure
- **Chemical lures:** "Siren songs" attract nematodes to traps
