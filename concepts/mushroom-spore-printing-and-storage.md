# Mushroom Spore Printing and Storage

Spore collection is the foundation of [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] — every grow begins with spores. Techniques range from simple field prints for identification to sterile lab methods for long-term cultivation stock. See mushroom-identification for spore print colors as an ID feature, and [[fungal-biology-fundamentals]] for the role of spores in the fungal life cycle.

## Spore Print Technique

### Basic Field Print (for identification)
1. Select a mature mushroom with intact gills/pores
2. Cut stem close to cap with a clean knife
3. Place cap gills-down on clean white paper (half on white, half on dark paper for light spores)
4. Cover with a glass or bowl to reduce air currents and moisture loss
5. Leave 2–12 hours in a draft-free location
6. Lift cap to reveal the spore deposit pattern

### Sterile Print (for cultivation — Stamets method)
1. Sever cap from stem of a fresh, well-cleaned mushroom
2. Place gills-down on a clean glass surface (microscope slide) or sterile petri dish
3. If partially dried, add a drop of water to the cap surface to aid spore release
4. Cover with a cup or glass to reduce air currents
5. After a few hours, spores fall in the radiating symmetry of the gills
6. For paper prints: cut out, fold in half, seal in an airtight container, label with date/species/collection number
7. For glass slides: sandwich between two pieces of glass, tape edges to prevent contaminant entry

### PF Tek Jar Method (for cultivation)
1. Pre-sterilize a wide-mouth 1/2 pint jar and metal lid in a toaster oven at ~300°F for 30 minutes
2. Flame-sterilize cuticle scissors, snip off the mushroom cap as high into the cap as possible
3. Place cap gills-down on jar bottom using a straight pin to handle it
4. Place lid on loosely; leave in draft-free area for a couple of days away from sunlight
5. Remove cap, let jar dehumidify for a few days with loose lid
6. Seal jar with tape; store at room temperature in a dark place (not refrigerator)

### Veil Method for Clean Spores (Stamets)
Species with a partial veil (e.g., *[[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]*, *Agaricus brunnescens*) offer a natural advantage: the veil seals the gills from the outside, creating a semi-sterile chamber. By choosing a healthy young specimen with the veil intact and carefully removing veil tissue under aseptic conditions, a nearly pure spore print is obtained — the ideal way to start a multispore culture.

## Making a Spore Syringe

### PF Tek Method
**Materials**: Spore print in jar, sterile syringes, sterile water, small Pyrex glass stirring rod, alcohol, tequila shot glass, eye dropper, lid with two holes (one for rod, one for syringe needle).

**[[mushroom-spore-syringe-preparation-and-storage]]**: Boil syringes in water for 1 hour, wrapped in foil. Cool before use.

1. Inject sterile water into the spore print jar through the needle hole
2. Flame-sterilize the glass stirring rod, cool, insert through center hole
3. Scrape spores into the water with the rod
4. Insert sterile syringe needle through the edge hole, tilt jar until water reaches needle tip
5. Draw spore water into the syringe
6. Store at cool temperatures in the dark — viable for several months to over a year

### Stamets Hydration Method
Dehydrated spores germinate poorly. Soaking improves viability:
1. Sterilize an eye dropper/syringe and water-filled flask at 15 psi for 30 minutes
2. Touch spores onto a sterile scalpel, insert into sterile water
3. Seal tightly and let stand 6–12 hours
4. Draw up spore solution and inoculate several agar plates with 1–2 drops each
5. Inoculate at least 3 plates to improve germination success

## Agar Inoculation from Spores

1. Sterilize inoculating loop or scalpel over alcohol lamp flame until red hot (5–10 seconds)
2. Cool tip by inserting into sterile agar media
3. Scrape some spores off the print and streak across the agar surface
4. Alternatively, scrape spores above an opened petri dish and let them free-fall onto medium
5. Incubate at species-appropriate temperature; germination visible in 3–7 days as grayish thread-like strands becoming whitish (Stamets)

See [[mushroom-sterile-technique-detailed]] for full agar work procedures.

## Long-Term Spore Storage

### Viability Timeline
- **Fresh spores**: Highest germination rate, massive and quick (PF Tek)
- **1–6 months**: Good viability, gradual decline
- **6–18 months**: Reduced germination; *P. cubensis* spores begin degrading after a few months
- **1.5+ years**: Greatly reduced or no germination (PF Tek)

### Storage Methods
| Method | Temperature | Expected Viability | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Room temp, dark, sealed | ~70°F | 6–18 months | PF Tek recommends this over refrigeration |
| Refrigerator (38°F) | Cold | 1–3 years | Keep sealed with desiccant |
| Freezer (0°F) | Frozen | 2–5+ years | Risk of ice crystal damage; use silica gel |
| Silica gel in sealed container | Any | Extends all methods | Absorbs residual moisture, prevents mold |
| Vacuum-sealed with desiccant | Any | 2–5+ years | Best long-term option |

### Key Storage Principles
- **Dry spores last longer** — dehumidify before sealing (PF Tek method: leave jar with loose lid for a few days)
- **Darkness is essential** — UV light degrades spores
- **Label everything** — date, species, collection number, source
- **Avoid repeated temperature cycling** — condensation kills viability
- **Airtight seal** — prevents contamination and moisture exchange
- **Silica gel packets** — include in all storage containers to absorb residual moisture
