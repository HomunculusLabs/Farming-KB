# Fungal Bioremediation Formulation and Delivery

## Overview

Formulation refers to methods for preparing and delivering microorganisms
for application in environmental, agricultural, or industrial settings.
Encapsulation and entrapment describe techniques where the microbial
inoculum is embedded in a specific carrier substance. Despite the
demonstrated bioremediation potential of fungi, most successful
applications remain laboratory experiments. Field trials are plagued by
suboptimal results due to physical parameters (aeration, moisture,
nutrient level, pH, temperature, toxic contaminant concentration) and
biological parameters (predation, competition from resident microbial
populations). Effective formulation addresses these challenges by
providing a protective habitat and growth advantage for introduced
fungi.

## Biostimulation versus Bioaugmentation

Two major biological strategies enhance microbial bioremediation.
Biostimulation stimulates indigenous populations through delivery of
limiting nutrients, most commonly nitrogen and phosphorus. Composting
is another biostimulation form, mixing straw, manure, and agricultural
wastes with contaminated soil to stimulate uncharacterized microbial
consortia. Bioaugmentation is the controlled addition of specific
microorganisms that augment but do not replace resident populations.
Organisms efficient in laboratory conditions often fare poorly in
the field due to nutritional limitations, variable weather, competition
with established communities, and predators. When biostimulation and
bioaugmentation are used simultaneously, added nutrients may favor
indigenous populations that overgrow the introduced species.
