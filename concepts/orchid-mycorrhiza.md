# Orchid Mycorrhiza

## Definition and Overview

Orchidaceous mycorrhizae represent a specialized form of endomycorrhizal
symbiosis between orchids (family Orchidaceae) and fungi. The fungal
association involves penetration of the cell wall and invagination of the
plasmalemma, with the fungus forming distinctive hyphal coils (pelotons)
within cortical cells of the orchid root. This type of mycorrhiza was one
of the first recognized, described by A.B. Frank in 1887 alongside ericoid
mycorrhizas when he distinguished between ectotrophic and endotrophic
mycorrhizae.

## Obligate Dependency on Fungi

Unlike most other [[teaming-with-microbes-fungi-and-mycorrhizal-relationships]] often involve an
obligate dependency. Orchid seeds are extremely small and contain minimal
nutrient reserves. They germinate only in the presence of a suitable fungus,
which provides carbohydrates and other nutrients to the developing embryo.

This dependency is most pronounced in the early stages of the orchid life
cycle. In some species, the fungal partner continues to provide significant
nutritional support even in adult plants, particularly in species that lack
chlorophyll (mycoheterotrophic orchids). These achlorophyllous orchids
depend entirely on their fungal partners for carbon throughout their lives.

This tripartite relationship (tree-ECM fungus-orchid) means that some

mycorrhizal fungal partner. This ecological strategy allows orchids to
establish in deeply shaded forest understories where light for
photosynthesis is limited.

## Siderophore Production

Orchidaceous mycorrhizal fungi produce a novel class of siderophores. A
novel hydroxamate siderophore named **basidiochrome** (molecular weight
787) was isolated from culture filtrates [[query-what-are-the-different-types-of-mycorrhizal-fungi]] of orchids,
specifically from *Ceratobasidium* and *Rhizoctonia* spp. (Haselwandter et
