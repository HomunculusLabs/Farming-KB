## Irrigation Systems

Comprehensive guide to irrigation methods for a regenerative homestead. Covers drip, sprinklers, flood/furrow, olla pots, wicking beds, automation, rainwater integration, winterization, and troubleshooting. See [[homestead-water-systems]] for sourcing and [[water-management]] for earthworks.

## Drip Irrigation (Primary Homestead System)

### Components
| Component | Purpose | Cost Range |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Backflow preventer | Protects water supply from contamination | $10-30 |
| Filter (155-mesh screen) | Prevents emitter clogging — always install | $15-40 |
| Pressure regulator | Reduces to 20-30 PSI (drip standard) | $10-25 |
| Main line (1/2" poly) | Distribution backbone | $0.10-0.20/ft |
| Lateral line (1/4" tubing) | Offshoots to individual plants | $0.05-0.10/ft |
| Emitters | Delivers water to root zone | $0.25-1.00 each |
| End caps / flush valves | System drainage | $2-5 |
| Timer/controller | Automation | $15-200 |

### Emitter Types
| Type | Flow Rate | Best For | Spacing |
|------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Drip emitter (inline) | 0.5-2 GPH | Individual trees, shrubs | 1-2 per plant |
| Drip emitter (button) | 1-4 GPH | Large containers, trees | 2-4 per tree |
| Drip tape | 0.5-1.0 GPM/100 ft | Row crops, garden beds | 6-18" spacing built-in |
| Dripperline (1/2") | 0.5-1.0 GPH/18" spacing | Permanent perennial beds | Every 12-18" |
| Micro-sprinkler | 5-20 GPH | Ground cover areas, orchards | 4-8 ft radius |
| Bubbler | 5-20 GPH | Large trees, basins | 1 per tree |

### Layout Design
- **Zone by water need**: Vegetables (daily), berries (every 2-3 days), fruit trees (2x/week), established perennials (weekly)
- **Run length limit**: 1/2" main line max 200 ft per zone; 1/4" lateral max 30 ft
- **Pressure**: 20-30 PSI for most drip; 25-50 PSI for micro-sprinklers
- **Sizing rule**: 1 zone = 100-200 GPH maximum flow (match to your water source GPM)

### Timer Options
| Type | Cost | Features | Best For |
|------|------|----------|----------|
| Battery hose timer | $15-30 | 1-2 zones, basic schedule | Simple gardens |
| WiFi hose timer (Rachio, Orbit B-hyve) | $50-150 | 4-8 zones, app control, weather | Smart homestead |
| AC controller | $40-100 | Multiple zones, rain sensor | Greenhouse, large setup |
| Smart controller (Rain Bird, Hunter) | $100-300 | Evapotranspiration, soil sensors | Full homestead system |

## Sprinkler Systems

| Type | Coverage | Efficiency | Best Use |
|------|----------|-----------|----------|
| Impact sprinkler | 20-80 ft radius | 50-70% | Pasture, large lawn areas, [[pasture-management]] |
| Oscillating sprinkler | 10-30 ft x 15-45 ft | 60-75% | Garden beds, seed germination |
| Rotary nozzle | 15-30 ft radius | 70-80% | Lawn, ground cover |
| Micro-sprinkler | 4-8 ft radius | 80-90% | Food forest understory, berry bushes |

Sprinklers waste 30-50% to evaporation and wind compared to drip. Reserve for pasture and cover crop areas where precision is less critical.

## Flood/Furrow Irrigation
- **Basin irrigation**: Build soil berms around fruit trees, flood basins weekly. Simplest method. Excellent for establishing young trees.
- **Furrow irrigation**: Channels between crop rows. Works with gravity flow from rainwater tanks. Good for [[grain-and-staple-crops]] and [[cover-cropping-guide]].
- **Swale irrigation**: Contour swales ([[water-management]]) fill during rain, slowly release to downslope plantings. Zero energy input.

## Olla Pots
- **How they work**: Unglazed clay pots buried in soil, filled with water. Water seeps through clay walls by capillary action directly to root zone.
- **Flow rate**: Self-regulating — faster when soil is dry, stops when saturated.
- **Coverage**: 1-3 ft radius per olla depending on size.
- **Sizes**: 1-5 gallon. 2-gallon standard for raised beds.
- **Refill**: Every 2-5 days depending on weather and plant demand.
- **Cost**: $15-30 each (DIY with terracotta pots + silicone: $5-8).
- **Best for**: Raised beds, containers, intensive garden zones ([[greenhouse-design]]).

## Wicking Beds
- **How they work**: Water reservoir (gravel/pipe) below soil, separated by geotextile. Soil wicks water upward by capillary action.
- **Water savings**: 50-80% less than surface watering.
- **Reservoir depth**: 6-8 inches typical. Fill every 1-2 weeks.
- **Best for**: Annual vegetables, greens, shallow-rooted crops in [[raised-beds-vs-in-ground-vs-containers]].
- **Build**: Frame (wood/corrugated metal), pond liner, gravel layer, perforated PVC inlet/outlet, geotextile, soil on top.

## Connecting to Rainwater Catchment

See [[homestead-water-systems]] for rainwater sizing. Key integration points:

- **Gravity feed**: Place tanks uphill from gardens. 1 ft elevation = 0.43 PSI. 10 ft head = 4.3 PSI — enough for drip with low-pressure emitters.
- **Pump feed**: Use solar DC pump from cistern to drip system. Pressure tank smooths flow.
- **First flush diverter**: Discards first 0.5-1 mm of rain (dust, debris) before water enters tank.
- **Filtration**: 155-mesh screen filter at tank outlet + drip filter at zone header.
- **Sizing**: 1 inch rain on 1,000 sq ft roof = 623 gallons. A 1,500 sq ft roof yields ~935 gallons per inch of rain.

## Automatic vs Manual

| Factor | Automatic | Manual |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Labor | Minimal after setup | 30-60 min/day during peak season |
| Water use | 30-50% less (consistent, scheduled) | Often over- or under-watering |
| Cost | $50-300 initial | $0 (hose + timer optional) |
| Reliability | Needs power/battery backup | Only fails if you forget |
| Best for | Vacation, busy periods, precision | Small gardens, mindful connection to plants |

Recommendation: Automate Zone 1-2 (daily/weekly visits). Manual is fine for Zone 3-4 established perennials.

## Winterization (Zone 7-8)
- **Burial depth**: Main lines 36+ inches below frost line (Zone 7). Above-ground lines must be drained.
- **Blowout method**: Air compressor at 50 PSI max (PVC) or 80 PSI max (poly). Open all end caps, flush each zone for 2-3 minutes until no water exits.
- **Drain valves**: Install automatic drain valves at low points in every zone.
- **Disconnect**: Remove backflow preventer, filter, and pressure regulator — store indoors.
- **Insulation**: Foam pipe sleeves ($0.50-1/ft) for any exposed above-ground sections.
- **Heat tape**: Thermostatically controlled ($1-2/ft installed) on main supply line only.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Uneven flow / dry spots | Clogged emitters | Flush lines, remove and clean emitters in vinegar soak |
| Low pressure throughout | Filter clogged, line too long, insufficient source GPM | Clean/replace filter, shorten zone runs, check source flow rate |
| Leaks at fittings | Loose connections, UV degradation, rodent damage | Push fittings fully, replace damaged sections, use mouse guards |
| Blown emitters | Pressure too high | Verify pressure regulator (20-30 PSI), install if missing |
| Green slime in lines | Algae growth (light exposure) | Bury lines, use opaque tubing, flush with hydrogen peroxide |
| Mineral buildup | Hard water | Vinegar soak emitters monthly, consider acid injection system |
| Roots in emitters | Emitters too deep, aggressive roots | Install emitters at soil surface, use root barrier discs |
