# Ayahuasca Sorcery Types

Amazonian vegetalismo encompasses a sophisticated taxonomy of
sorcery, with distinct methods of causing harm classified by the
weapons and techniques employed. [[pablo-amaringo]]'s visions enumerate
three primary types of sorcerers, each using different means of
attack, along with numerous secondary methods. A
[[mariri-phantom-sorcery]] (magical phlegm). When he wishes to
cause harm, he takes a chonta from his mariri and blows towards his
victim. If the victim's body has been previously treated by a good
vegetalista, he immediately feels the attack and seeks remedy. If
untreated, the victim may suffer great pain or die.

Virotes are also made from specific palm trees:
- **Inchahui palm** (Syagrus tessmannii)
- **Pona** (Iriartea exorrhiza), the palm with long roots
- **Inayuga** (Maximiliana stenocarpa), the thicker palm
- **Huasai palm** (Euterpe precatoria)

## Marupa Sorcery

Marupa sorcery (hechiceria marupa) involves the manipulation of
living animals -- insects, snakes, scorpions, bats, rays, toads --
kept within the sorcerer's mariri. The Cocama sorcerer sends these
animals through his visions to attack victims. The victim feels an
urge to go to a certain place, where the sorcerer's animal waits to
attack. The mariri also contains the Sachamama (forest serpent
mother) used in marupa sorcery. Animals are used both by sorcerers
and healers to convey messages through this technique.

## Huani Sorcery

The huani sorcerer uses a steel bow with a crystal arrow, kept under
his left armpit. Inside his mariri he carries a living piranha. When
attacking, he puts the bow in his mouth and blows the crystal arrow,
which pierces the victim's body and returns to his armpit. The victim
feels very tired, goes to sleep, and upon waking begins to vomit
blood until death. No vegetalista can cure this kind of sorcery --
only God can save the victim. The virote huani consists of glass
arrows and a cumaceba (Swartzia sp.) bow.

## Diagnostic Signs of Sorcery

A curandero distinguishes sorcery types by the lights surrounding
the patient during an [[ayahuasca-vision-art-and-shipibo-tradition]]:

- **Zigzagging lights**: The patient has been hit by a chontero
  (chonta/virote sorcery)
- **Small waves crossed by dark lines**: The patient has been hit by
  huani (crystal arrow sorcery)
- **Larger waves**: Marupa sorcery was used

## Sorcery Plants

Several plants are specifically associated with sorcery:

- **Marannara**: Used by sorcerers (unidentified)
- **Tungara**: Its icaros are used to cause harm (unidentified)
- **Cocona-sacha**: A thorny plant used in sorcery (unidentified)
- **Siuca-huito** (Solanum kioniotrichum): Used for harmful purposes
- **Alambre-casha**: Used by sorcerers (unidentified)
- **Ishanga** (Laportea aestuans): Used to cause harm
- **Casha-sacha**: A thorny plant growing on rocks, its spirit
  guards victims of bewitchment
- **Puka-gramalote**: Used by sorcerers to inflict nausea and
  headaches
- **Ajosquiro**: The tree from which huaira-kuros (worms of the
  air) sorcery is learned

## Sorcery Animals and Creatures

Sorcerers work with dangerous creatures including snakes (yana-machaco,
nacanaca, shushupi, jergon), the supay-masho (diabolic bat) that
produces sleepy weakness, the huaira-kuros (worms of the air) that
enter through the nose and ears, and the huasi-ukullucos (house
lizards) that enter the anus during defecation causing death.
