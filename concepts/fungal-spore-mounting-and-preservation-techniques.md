# Fungal Spore Mounting and Preservation Techniques

Updated: 2026-04-18

Proper mounting and preservation of fungal spores and microscopic structures is essential for taxonomic identification, voucher documentation, and long-term reference. Techniques vary depending on the fungal group, the structure being mounted, and the intended duration of preservation.

## Mounting Media

### Permanent Mountants

**PVLG (Polyvinyl-Lacto-Glycerol):**
- Most widely used mountant for [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] spores and soil fungi
- Semi-permanent; preserves spore wall layers and structure for months to years
- Composition: polyvinyl alcohol, lactic acid, glycerol, water
- Spores retain shape and wall layer differentiation
- Can be combined with Melzer's reagent for simultaneous mounting and staining (PVLG + Melzer's)

**PVA (Polyvinyl Alcohol):**
- Similar to PVLG; various formulations with different viscosities
- Good for preserving hyphal morphology and spore characters

**Amann's Lactophenol:**
- Classic mycological mountant
- Composition: lactic acid, phenol, glycerol, distilled water (1:1:1:1)
- Provides good clarity for most fungal structures
- Phenol component provides some preservation against microbial degradation
- Phenol is toxic; handle with gloves in fume hood

### Temporary Mountants

**Lactic Acid:**
- Simple, quick-mounting medium
- Good for clearing and observing spore wall layers
- May cause some swelling of delicate structures

**Glycerol:**
- Useful for temporary observation of living or fresh material
- Non-toxic; compatible with most stains
- Structures may shift during observation

**Water:**
- Simplest mountant for quick examination
- No preservation value; structures degrade rapidly
- Useful for motile spore observation (chytrid zoospores)

## Stains for Spore and Tissue Examination

### Melzer's Reagent
- **Composition:** Iodine 1.5g, potassium iodide 5g, distilled water 20ml, chloral hydrate 22g
- **Primary use:** Amyloid reaction (blue-black) and dextrinoid reaction (reddish-brown) of spore walls, hyphal septa, and other structures
- **Critical for:** AMF spore identification, Russula and Lactarius spore reactions, ascomycete ascus tip reactions (j-)
- **Limitation:** Chloral hydrate is a controlled substance in many jurisdictions

### Lactophenol Cotton Blue (LPCB)
- **Composition:** Lactophenol + cotton blue (aniline blue) 0.05-0.1%
- **Primary use:** General fungal staining; highlights septa, walls, and cytoplasm
- **Effective for:** Hyphomycete identification, dematiaceous fungi, spore ornamentation
- **Note:** Cotton blue solution degrades over time; prepare fresh monthly

### Trypan Blue
- **Composition:** Trypan blue 0.05% in lactoglycerol
- **Primary use:** Staining endophytic fungi in cleared plant tissue
- **Procedure:** Clear tissue in 10% KOH, rinse, stain in trypan blue/lactoglycerol overnight
- **Effective for:** Visualizing hyphae within needles, leaves, and stems

### Acid Fuchsin-Malachite Green
- **Primary use:** Epidermal cell staining for endophyte visualization
- **Procedure:** Clear tissue in lactophenol-ethanol (1:2 v/v) by boiling, then stain
- **Used for:** Juncus and other herbaceous plant endophytes

### Diazonium Blue B (DBB) Test
- **Primary use:** Discriminating ascomycetes from basidiomycetes
- **Reaction:** Ascomycete cell walls turn deep purple/red; basidiomycetes remain negative
- **CRITICAL SAFETY:** DBB is extremely carcinogenic and sensitive to oxidation above 0C. Must be stored desiccated at -20C. Handle with extreme care using appropriate PPE.
- **Application:** Single drop of 0.1% DBB in 2M sodium acetate buffer (pH 6.7) on colony; observe within minutes

## Fixatives and Preservatives

### FAA (Formalin-Aceto-Alcohol)
- **Composition:** Formalin 5ml, glacial acetic acid 5ml, 70% ethanol 90ml
- **Primary use:** Fixation of fungal structures and plant material for microscopic examination
- **Advantage:** Excellent preservation of cytoplasmic detail
- **Note:** Some ascomycete structures (e.g., asci) may collapse or become distorted

### Ethanol (70-95%)
- Standard preservative for fungal specimens
- Voucher specimens of macrofungi preserved in 70% ethanol for DNA extraction
- 95% ethanol preferred for molecular work (better DNA preservation)

### Glutaraldehyde (2-3%)
- **Primary use:** Fixation for electron microscopy (TEM/SEM)
- **Procedure:** Fix in 2-3% glutaraldehyde in buffer (phosphate or cacodylate), rinse, post-fix in osmium tetroxide
- **Essential for:** Ultrastructural studies of zoospores, haustoria, septal pore organization

## Permanent Slide Preparation

### Procedure

1. Select mature, representative spores or structures
2. Clean material of debris (sequential washing for soil spores)
3. Place material in drop of mountant on clean microscope slide
4. Gently lower coverslip; avoid trapping air bubbles
5. For permanent mounts, seal coverslip edges with nail polish or mounting medium
6. Label slide with: species/collectors number, date, mountant, stain used
7. Store flat in slide boxes at room temperature away from light

### Soil Spore Extraction for AMF

For arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi from soil samples:
1. Wet-sieve soil through nested sieves (decreasing mesh: 710, 250, 53 um)
2. Decant supernatant; collect material from each sieve
3. Transfer to centrifuge tubes; sucrose gradient centrifugation (1:1 soil:sucrose, 3000 rpm, 5 min)
4. Collect floating spores from sucrose surface
5. Rinse spores with distilled water on 53-um sieve
6. Mount spores individually or in groups on slides

## Documentation

- Photograph spores at multiple magnifications before mounting (structures may shift)
- Record spore dimensions from multiple specimens (minimum 20 for species descriptions)
- Note color changes during mounting or staining
- Cross-reference with voucher specimen collection data
- For DNA work, split spores: mount some, extract DNA from others
