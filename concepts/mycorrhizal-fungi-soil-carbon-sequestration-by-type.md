### Arbuscular Mycorrhiza (AM)

AM fungi associate with most herbaceous plants, grasses, and many tropical trees, and dominate in agricultural and temperate grassland ecosystems:

## The Nutrient Competition Mechanism

A key mechanism by which mycorrhizal fungi (especially EM and ERM) promote carbon sequestration is through competition with saprotrophic decomposer fungi:

1. Both mycorrhizal and saprotrophic fungi require mineral nitrogen and phosphorus
2. In nitrogen-limited boreal and heathland soils, these nutrients are the primary factor limiting decomposition
3. Mycorrhizal fungi receive a continuous supply of carbohydrates from host plants, giving them a massive competitive advantage over saprotrophs in acquiring limiting nutrients
4. When mycorrhizal fungi extract nitrogen from decomposing litter, they leave behind organic residues with even higher C:N ratios
5. These increasingly nutrient-poor residues become increasingly difficult for saprotrophs to decompose
6. The result is a positive feedback loop: more mycorrhizal activity leads to slower decomposition, which leads to more carbon accumulation, which supports more mycorrhizal biomass

This mechanism effectively means that mycorrhizal fungi can "lock up" carbon by removing the nutrients that decomposer organisms need to access it.
