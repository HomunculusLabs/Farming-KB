## Aquatic Polyculture and Pond Construction

Aquatic polyculture integrates fish, crustaceans, molluscs, waterfowl, and plants in multi-species pond systems that far exceed the protein yields of most land-based animal production. Mollison provides detailed construction designs and species strategies for farm-scale aquaculture.

## The Polyculture Approach

"All authors concur that less disease and greater total productivity are a feature of polyculture, and that monoculture in ponds, as on land, is less stable and more energy-consuming."

### Essential Elements (selected for local climate)
1. Mussels in pond bottom mud
2. Eels (if naturally present) in tyre refuges
3. A browsing fish for algae
4. A predator fish (screened off)
5. Crustaceans (shrimp, crabs, marron) in brush pile refuges
6. Insect attractants: flowering verge plants (Buddleia, ti-tree, herbs), lights over pond at night, meat baits over water for flies
7. Small local fish (pygmy perch, galaxids, minnows) for [[backswimmer-mosquito-control-mollison]]
8. Shallow edge plants (tall rush, wild rice) as frog and bird refuges
9. Lawn edge for grazers (geese, swan)
10. Islands for breeding waterfowl and protection from foxes
11. Nestboxes for wildfowl
12. Raft culture of plants
13. Floating aquatics (water chestnut, Trapa)
14. Edible root species (water lilies, lotus, water chestnut) as underwater tyre plantings
15. Bank stabilization: stepped log, tyre, or hand-cut planted ledges with bamboo, pampas
16. Seepage planting of fruit or nut trees (cherry, walnut)
17. Local plantings of watercress, mint
18. Trials of spawning "bottom" materials

## Yield Potential

John Wood estimated yields from 1 ha of pond:
- **3,000 kg of fish**
- **1,000 kg of crayfish** (yabbies)
- **400 kg of mussels**
- **Eggs and progeny of 200 ducks**

Mussel and duck manure provide sufficient nutrients. "These yields (where achieved) are orders greater than most land yields of animal protein."

## Pond Nomenclature

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Barrage ponds** | Across stream courses, filled directly by stream or valley run-off |
| **Diversion ponds** | Filled by diversion channel from stream or run-off area |
| **Ring Dams** ("Turkey nests") | Flatland storages above grade; water pumped in |
| **Storage ponds** | Steady level ponds from upstream barrages leading to contour channels |
| **Contour dams** | Along contour with wing banks running upslope |

### Biological Terms
- **Brood ponds**: For adult breeders
- **Spawning ponds**: For reproduction
- **Nursery ponds**: For young fish
- **Solar ponds**: Specifically to produce heat
- **Dew ponds**: Constructed to catch night moisture
- **Perched ponds**: Small settling, filter, or frog ponds perched above larger storages
- **Subsurface ponds**: Walls under water that preserve shallow estuarine water in draw-down

### Key Size Principles
- Many small ponds of 100–500 m² are better for fish culture than large storages of 400 m² or more
- Graded bottoms of 75 cm to 2 m depth suit many fish
- Storage ponds for water need 3–6 m depth for large acreages
- In deserts, even tiny rockholes are critical for survival of quail and desert animals

## Dam Modifications for Polyculture

Standard Keyline dams gain these features:
1. **Island refuges** for breeding waterfowl
2. **Shallow shelves** for waterfowl forage plants at rear and edges
3. **Deep sump refuges** for fish in dams less than 10 feet deep with hot summers
4. **Peninsula structures** (with or without moat) for house fire-protection
5. **South banks** to site plants, houses, glasshouses (reflected heat benefits in winter)

## Gley Sealing for Leaking Ponds

For granitic, sandy, shaly sites where water won"t lie:
1. Clear pond bottom of all debris and rocks
2. Lay fresh cow or pig manure, green lawn or lucerne clippings at 75–100 mm deep
3. Cover completely with paper, cardboard, plastic, old carpet, hay, leaves, grass
4. Put weight layer of sand, soil, clay over
5. Wait 2–3 weeks for ferment in base layers
6. Fill pond

Traditional alternatives: "bumping" walls with gelignite plugs, lime and [[gypsum]] to seal cracks, feeding cattle in dry pond with hay bales.

## Pond Preparation and Liming

Chakroff recommends liming before filling:
| Material | Rate | Notes |
|----------|------|-------|
| Ground limestone | 1,140 kg/ha | Let stand 2 weeks |
| Agricultural limestone | 2,270 kg/ha | Then fill pond slowly |
| Hydrated limestone | 114 kg/ha | |
| Quicklime | 200 kg/ha | |

Best pH for ponds: **6.5–9.0** (vs. 5.5–6.0 for gardens). Fish ponds are best if soupy green (Secchi disc disappears at 20–40 cm), achieved by manuring with sewage or heavily stocking with ducks.

### Water Quality Enhancement
- Green plants, [[compost]], loam
- Ploughing in a green crop
- Feeding grain mash to fish
- Livestock pens (pig or cow yards) near fish ponds help with manurial input
- Old ponds with deep mulch on floor are best

## Conditions for Effective Pond Culture

Progressing towards more [[mollison-designers-market-garden-intensive-production-design]]:
- Small impoundments of 500–1,000 m²
- Depth of 2–5 m (7–16 feet)
- Floor graded as slope to outlet
- Mixture of fish, crayfish, plants, molluscs, waterfowl, edge plants, land animals penned nearby
- Insect foods attracted by plants, lights, baits, colours, scents
- Adequate refuges: shallows, pipes, brush piles, logs, deeps
- [[fukuoka-natural-predator-protection]]: nets or fences
- Liming and manuring as needed to pH 6.5+
- Manure or waste foods added as available
- Water paddled, sprayed, stirred, or filtered as [[aquaculture-pond-yields-and-stocking-rates]] increase
- Special invertebrate food ponds nearby (worms, insect larvae, frogs)
- Special crops grown for fish food (cereal or tree crop)
- Enclosure of pond in glasshouses as final intensive ploy

## Water as Multi-Dimensional System

"Water is a three-dimensional system, or four if flow (duration) is taken into account. By using all dimensions, aquaculture can benefit."

### The Land-Pond Interchange
- Drained ponds yield rich mud fertilizer for gardens
- Freshwater mussels and quick-growing plants fix nitrogen in ponds
- Edge and surface animals (duck, wild rice) add to pond yield as hedgerow adds to field yield
- Dry-land cropping as part of fish crop cycle increases total yield and prevents disease transmission

### Runoff Example
Runoff from a modest barn roof (20 × 8 m) in a 1 m rainfall area yields **1,600,000 litres per annum**. Waste heat or manure production can be used to great advantage in ponds.

## Sewage Treatment Through Aquatic Systems

Maryborough (Vic.) uses water and soil to deal with sewage outfall of ~8,000 people. P.A. Yeomans and Mollison designed wildlife and biologically-oriented [[werribee-sewage-lagoons-mollison]] feeding hundreds of wildfowl, then discharging to keylined fields as absorption filters.

The path: primary mechanical breakdown → methane → trickle filter → lagoons → soil absorption → stream.

"There is no sane technological solution to sewage waste; it is the province of the biologist."

## Natural Water Cleansing

Various biological filter systems recycle water from still ponds or cleanse water from polluted outlets. The system uses plants, animals, and microorganisms rather than chemicals.
