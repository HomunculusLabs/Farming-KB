# Maitake (Grifola Frondosa) Cancer Clinical Research

Grifola frondosa (Maitake, Hen of the Woods) produces beta-D-glucan and glycoprotein complexes with strong anti-tumour activity. Its D-fraction and Grifron-D preparations have progressed through clinical trials in the US and Japan.

## Active Compounds

- Grifron-D (GD): Highly purified beta-1,6 glucan branched with beta-1,3 linkage
- D-fraction: Beta-glucan glycoprotein complex
- Crude Maitake powder: Whole [[lentinan-pre-clinical-antitumour-models]].

## Chinese Pilot Studies

Early pilot studies from China (published in abstract) involving 63 cancer patients reported:
- 95% response rate (partial and complete) against solid tumours
- 90% response rate for leukaemia (type not specified) (Jones, 1998)

## FDA IND Approval

The US FDA has approved Grifron-D for trial under an Investigational New Drug Application (IND) for patients with advanced cancer. Key milestone: due to absence of adverse reactions in previous trials and no significant pre-clinical toxicity, the FDA exempted this polysaccharide from Phase I study.

## Ongoing Clinical Trials

Clinical trials are underway in the US and Japan for breast, prostate, lung, liver, and gastric cancers. Most are at early clinical stage (Phase I/II).

## Suitability as Nutriceutical

The apparent success of crude Maitake powder by oral administration in cancer therapy and immune stimulation supports its suitability as a nutriceutical -- a functional food with medicinal properties.

## Mechanisms of Action

The anticancer properties of these mushroom-derived compounds operate through multiple pathways:

1. **[[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]]
2. **Direct antitumor effects**: Induction of apoptosis in cancer cells via mitochondrial pathways
3. **Anti-angiogenesis**: Inhibition of tumor blood vessel formation
4. **Anti-metastatic activity**: Prevention of cancer cell migration and invasion
5. **Synergistic effects**: Enhancement of conventional chemotherapy and radiation therapy while reducing side effects

## Clinical Evidence

Clinical trials and preclinical studies have demonstrated promising results across multiple cancer types. The research spans in vitro studies, animal models, and human clinical trials, with varying levels of evidence quality. While many studies show significant antitumor activity, larger randomized controlled trials are still needed to establish definitive therapeutic protocols.

## Safety and Dosage Considerations

Mushroom-derived preparations are generally well-tolerated with minimal side effects. Common preparations include hot water extracts, alcohol extracts, and refined polysaccharide fractions. Dosages in clinical studies have varied widely depending on the specific preparation and the condition being treated.
