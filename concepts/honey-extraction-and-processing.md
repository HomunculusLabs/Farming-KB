# Honey Extraction and Processing

Extracting honey is the culmination of the beekeeping season. Proper
technique preserves honey quality, ensures food safety, and maximizes
yield. This guide covers the full process from uncapping to bottling,
including beeswax processing. (see [[homestead-dairy-equipment]]).

## When to Harvest

### Readiness Indicators
- At least 80% of cells in the super are capped (sealed with wax)
- Capped honey has moisture content below 18.2% — safe from fermentation
- Uncapped honey can be harvested if it passes the shake test: hold the
  frame horizontal and give a sharp shake — if no nectar flies out, it is
  ripe enough
- Use a refractometer to confirm moisture content below 18.6%

### Timing
- Harvest after the main nectar flow ends (varies by region)
- Harvest in the morning before bees begin flying and foraging
- Avoid harvesting during rainy or very humid conditions (moisture
  absorption)

## Removing Supers from the Hive

### Bee Escape Board
Place a bee escape (one-way maze) between the super and the brood chamber
the night before harvest. Bees pass down through the escape but cannot
return. Gentle but slow — requires 12-24 hours. (see [[comparison-fermented-vegetables-vs-canning]]).

### Bee Brush
Brush bees off each frame manually into the hive. Fast for a few frames but
tedious for large harvests. Causes some bee agitation.

### Leaf Blower (Compressed Air)
Blow bees off frames with a leaf blower or shop vac on reverse. Fast and
efficient but can damage comb if too aggressive. Best for warm days when
bees are not tightly clustered.

### Fume Board and Bee-Quick
A fume board with a non-toxic repellent (Bee-Quick, Bee-Go) drives bees
downward. Fast and effective but must not contaminate honey. Use only
approved products.

## Uncapping

The wax capping must be removed before honey can be extracted.

### Tools
- **Uncapping knife** — heated electric knife or manual serrated knife
  dipped in hot water; slice across the cappings
- **Uncapping fork** — scratches open cappings; good for thick or
  irregularly capped areas
- **Roller uncapper** — pierces cappings with needles; faster for large
  operations

### Technique
1. Support the frame over the uncapping tank (a container with a screen
   to separate wax from honey)
2. Slice horizontally through the cappings with a smooth, steady stroke
3. Alternate sides — uncapping from both faces of the frame
4. Let cappings fall into the tank; honey drains through the screen into
   the collection bucket

## Extraction

### Radial Extractor
Frames sit with the top bar pointing outward. Centrifugal force flings
honey out of both sides simultaneously. Most efficient for medium to large
operations. Requires even frame loading.

### Tangential Extractor
Honey is extracted from one side at a time; frames must be flipped halfway
through. Less expensive but slower. Suitable for small-scale operations.

### Extraction Process
1. Load uncapped frames into the extractor, evenly spaced
2. Spin slowly at first (hand-crank or electric), then increase speed
3. Extract about half the honey, flip frames (tangential only), extract
   the other half
4. When honey stops flowing from the frames, remove them

## Filtering

Raw honey contains wax particles, propolis, and bee parts. Filtering
improves clarity without removing beneficial enzymes and pollen.

### Two-Stage Filtering
1. **Coarse filter** — nylon or stainless steel mesh (200-400 micron)
   removes wax cappings and large debris
2. **Fine filter** — nylon mesh (600 micron) or cheesecloth removes
   finer particles

### Raw Honey Standard
To label as "raw," honey should not be heated above 115F (46C). Filter
through mesh rather than fine paper to preserve pollen and enzymes. Let
honey settle for 24-48 hours in a settling tank; wax and air bubbles rise
to the top and can be skimmed.

## Bottling

### Containers
- Glass jars are preferred for quality presentation
- Food-grade plastic (PET) for cost-effective packaging
- Mason jars for homestead use

### Process
1. Ensure honey is at room temperature for easy pouring
2. Fill jars to within 0.5 inches of the top
3. Wipe rims clean
4. Apply lids and labels
5. Store at 70-80F in a dark, dry place

### Labeling Requirements (US)
- Net weight in ounces and grams
- "Honey" as the product name
- Your name and address
- Origin state or "Product of USA"

## Beeswax Processing

Cappings from extraction are roughly 20% honey by weight and yield
high-quality beeswax.

### Cleaning Wax
1. Rinse cappings in warm water to remove residual honey
2. Melt cappings in a double boiler or solar wax melter (never direct heat)
3. Pour melted wax through a fine filter (cheesecloth or nylon) into molds
4. Discard the slumgum (dark residue of propolis and debris) at the bottom

### Solar Wax Melter
A simple insulated box with a glass lid. Sun melts wax, which flows into
a collection container. Slumgum stays behind on the screen. Excellent
off-grid method — see [[solar-oven-cooking]] for related solar energy
principles.

### Uses for Beeswax
- Candles, lip balm, salves, furniture polish
- Waxing frames (foundation coating with a wax dip tank)
- Cheese waxing and food preservation
- See [[bee-product-medicinal-uses]] for medicinal applications
