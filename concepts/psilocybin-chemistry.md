# Psilocybin Chemistry in Fungi

The psychoactive properties of neurotropic fungi derive from several distinct classes of chemical compounds. The most significant are the **tryptamine-based indole alkaloids** ([[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-species-classification-and-chemistry]], and the **isoxazole derivatives** (ibotenic acid and muscimol), found exclusively in *Amanita* species. Understanding the chemistry of these compounds is essential for both pharmacological research and the identification of neurotropic fungal species.

## Tryptamine Alkaloids: The Psilocybin Family

### Psilocybin

**Psilocybin** (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the primary psychoactive compound in the majority of neurotropic fungi. It was first isolated and identified by **Albert Hofmann** in 1958 from *Psilocybe mexicana* specimens collected by R. Gordon Wasson in Mexico. Psilocybin itself is pharmacologically inactive — it serves as a **prodrug** that is dephosphorylated in the body to its active metabolite, psilocin.

Key properties of psilocybin:
- Molecular formula: C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P
- Molecular weight: 284.25 g/mol
- Solubility: soluble in water and methanol
- Stability: relatively stable compound; the phosphate group protects against oxidative degradation
- Onset: 20-60 minutes after ingestion (delayed by dephosphorylation)
- Duration: 4-6 hours total experience

### Psilocin

**Psilocin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, or 4-HO-DMT) is the pharmacologically active metabolite of psilocybin and the compound responsible for the actual psychoactive effects. It is also found naturally in smaller quantities in many psilocybin-containing mushrooms, where it forms through endogenous dephosphorylation during drying or storage.

Key properties of psilocin:
- Molecular formula: C₁₂H₁₆N₂O
- Molecular weight: 204.27 g/mol
- Solubility: soluble in ethanol, slightly soluble in water
- Stability: less stable than psilocybin; oxidizes readily upon exposure to air, which causes the characteristic blue bruising of many *Psilocybe* species
- Mechanism: acts primarily as a 5-HT₂A serotonin receptor agonist

### Minor Tryptamine Alkaloids

Several additional tryptamine derivatives occur in neurotropic fungi, typically in lower concentrations:

- **Baeocystin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-methyltryptamine): A demethylated analog of psilocin, found in *P. baeocystis* and other species. Its psychoactive contribution remains debated.
- **Norbaeocystin** (4-hydroxytryptamine): Further demethylated, with unclear pharmacological significance
- **Aeruginascin** (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N,N-trimethyltryptamine): Found in *[[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]]* and allies) produce **ergoline alkaloids**, a chemically distinct class of neuroactive compounds:

- **Ergotamine**: A vasoconstrictor used medicinally for migraine treatment
- **Ergometrine** (ergonovine): Used to induce uterine contractions
- **Lysergic acid**: The core structure from which LSD ([[gottlieb-peyote-extraction-and-preparation-methods]] alter the chemical profile
- **Geographic origin**: Regional populations of the same species can differ substantially

Gartz (1986) demonstrated significant intraspecific variation in psilocybin and psilocin content, with some collections of the same species varying by orders of magnitude in total alkaloid concentration.

## Analytical Methods

Modern identification and quantification of fungal alkaloids employs:

- **High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)** with UV detection for routine analysis
- **Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS)** for definitive identification
- **Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS)** for volatile and derivatized compounds
- **Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR)** spectroscopy for structural elucidation of novel compounds

These techniques have resolved many of the taxonomic controversies that plagued earlier mycochemical work, where species identification was based solely on morphological characteristics and subjective bioassay.
