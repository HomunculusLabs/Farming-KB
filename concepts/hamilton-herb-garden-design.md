## Planning the Herb Garden

The range of herbs you grow will depend on personal priorities: culinary
value, decorative qualities, or a combination. However, you must also
consider practicalities. A young fennel plant seems attractive but will
grow into a bushy plant about 5 ft tall. Sensitive species like bay must
be planted in pots and brought into a frost-free greenhouse during
winter.

Before buying seeds or young plants, ensure you can provide the space and
conditions needed to grow them successfully.

## Design and Layout

### Formal Design
Traditional herb gardens use formal patterns with box or lavender hedges
creating geometric beds. This looks attractive but requires regular
maintenance to keep the hedging trimmed.

### Informal Design
A more natural approach mixes herbs with ornamental plants in borders.
Many herbs are attractive in their own right and work well in mixed
plantings.

### Key Design Principles
- Locate in the sunniest part of the garden (most herbs originate from
  the Mediterranean)
- Ensure good access for harvesting and maintenance
- Plan paths wide enough for comfortable access
- Consider height: place tall herbs (fennel, lovage) at the back
- Group herbs with similar water requirements together

## Specific Herbs and Their Cultivation

### Annual Herbs

**Basil**: Grown as an annual [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]]. Two types: sweet
(taller, better flavor) and bush. Plant in a sunny, sheltered spot in
the best possible soil. Sow inside in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]], harden off, and
plant out after frost danger passes. Keep well-watered. Pinch off
flower buds to maintain growth. Leaves can be frozen in ice cubes.

**Borage**: Hardy annual that freely self-seeds. Prefers sunny, open
conditions, tolerant of wide soil range. Sow in mid-spring, thin to
15 inches. Flowers attract bees. Young leaves can be frozen; flowers
can be crystallized.

**Chervil**: Self-seeding hardy annual with spicy, aniseed flavor.
Must have shade and moist soil or it will bolt. Sow in early to mid-
spring, 9 inches apart. Allow some plants to seed, then resow in late
summer. Protect late sowings with cloches.

**Dill**: Hardy annual with feathery leaves and yellow flowers.
Well-drained soil, sunny position. Sow 12 inches apart in mid-spring.
Do not sow near fennel as they may cross-pollinate. Leaves and seeds
can be dried.

**Summer Savory**: Hardy annual, 6 inches apart in mid to late spring.
Sow plenty as yield per plant is low. Both summer and winter savory
attract bees.

### Perennial Herbs

**Chives**: Hardy perennial making attractive border edging with
globular flowers. Prefer moist soil. Divide clumps every three years
in early fall. Cut with scissors leaving half an inch. Can be frozen
in ice cubes but do not dry well.
