## Sheep Shearing

Shearing is the first step in the wool processing chain. Mollison
notes that permaculture systems may include small flocks of 5-50 sheep
managed primarily for landscape management (grazing, fertilization)
with wool as a secondary product. Shearing is typically performed
annually in spring, before lambing and the onset of hot weather. Key
considerations:

  - Shearing skill: Proper technique avoids cuts to the animal and
    preserves fleece integrity (second cuts reduce staple length and
    spinning quality)
  - Fleece handling: Skirting removes contaminated belly, leg, and
    hindquarters wool, retaining the clean blanket fleece
  - Fleece classification: Sorting by fiber diameter (micron count),
    staple length, and cleanliness for appropriate end uses
  - Storage: Raw fleece should be stored in breathable bags (not
    plastic) in a cool, dry place to prevent moth damage and
    felting before processing

## Scouring

Scouring removes lanolin (wool wax), sweat, and dirt from raw fleece.
For small-scale permaculture processing, Mollison describes gentle
methods that preserve fiber quality:

  - Soap scouring: Washing fleece in warm water (not exceeding 50C)
    with a mild soap or detergent, with minimal agitation to prevent
    felting. Multiple washes may be needed for very greasy fleeces
  - Bio scouring: Fermentative methods using controlled bacterial
    action to [[query-how-do-white-rot-fungi-break-down-lignin]] lanolin (ammonia in stale urine saponifies the wax)

After scouring, wool is rinsed thoroughly in water of the same
temperature to prevent shock-induced felting, then dried on racks
in a shaded, well-ventilated area.

## Carding

Carding aligns and separates wool fibers to prepare them for spinning.
Hand carders (flat paddles with fine wire teeth) are used for small
quantities, while drum carders process larger batches more efficiently.
The wool is loaded onto the carder, passed between the teeth, and
removed as a continuous roll of aligned fiber called a rolag or batt.
Carding blends different fiber types, evens out staple lengths, and
removes remaining vegetable matter.

## Spinning

Spinning draws and twists carded fiber into yarn. Mollison describes
spinning as a skilled craft accessible through practice with drop
spindles or spinning wheels. Key variables in yarn production include:

  - Twist direction: Z-twist (clockwise) or S-twist (counter-
    clockwise), affecting yarn strength and ply compatibility
  - Twist amount: Fine yarns require more twist; bulky yarns less
  - Ply: Single-ply yarns are simpler but less durable; 2-ply or
    3-ply yarns are stronger and more balanced
  - Yarn weight: Lace, fingering, worsted, and bulky weights serve
    different knitting, weaving, and crochet applications
