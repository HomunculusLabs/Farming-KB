# Gartz 1990: Agar vs Dung-Grain Substrate Comparison Under Brassinosteroid Treatment

## Overview

The 1990 study by Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt (Naturwissenschaften 77, 388–389) was notable for evaluating the growth-promoting effects of 22S,23S-homobrassinolide across two fundamentally different cultivation systems for [[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]: a semi-solid agar medium and a complex grain-manure bulk substrate. This dual-system approach provided valuable comparative data on how [[ultra-low-dose-brassinosteroid-response-psilocybe-cubensis]] varies with substrate complexity.

## The Two Cultivation Systems

### Malt Agar System

- **Composition:** 6% malt extract agar
- **Purpose:** Pure mycelial growth assessment in a defined medium
- **Advantage:** Simple, reproducible, allows [[fukuoka-direct-observation-method]] of radial mycelial expansion
- **Limitation:** Does not support fruiting body formation

The agar system served as a controlled baseline. With no complex nutrients or competing microflora, any growth differences could be attributed primarily to the brassinosteroid's direct effect on [[singh-fungal-metabolism-pahs]] rather than indirect nutritional effects.

### Dung-Grain Substrate System

- **Composition:** Horse dung mixed with rice grain, supplemented with 5 g KH₂PO₄ per liter
- **Purpose:** Full life-cycle cultivation including fruiting
- **Advantage:** Represents a realistic cultivation substrate mimicking natural coprophilous growth conditions
- **Supplementation:** KH₂PO₄ provided additional phosphorus and potassium

This substrate model was chosen because P. cubensis is a coprophilous species naturally adapted to herbivore dung. The addition of rice grain increased carbohydrate availability, while KH₂PO₄ buffered pH and supplied essential minerals.

## Experimental Design

Both systems received the same treatment: 10⁻² ppm (0.01 ppm) of synthetic 22S,23S-homobrassinolide dissolved in the substrate. Ten replicate runs were conducted for each condition (treated and control). The brassinosteroid was synthesized from stigmasterol using established methods.

### Dose Selection

The 0.01 ppm concentration was chosen based on prior plant studies showing biological activity in the nanomolar to low micromolar range. For fungi, this represented a conservative starting dose that could be scaled in subsequent experiments.

## Comparative Results

### Mycelial Growth Rate (Agar)

On malt agar, treated cultures showed mycelial growth occurring two to three times faster than untreated controls. This was a striking result because:

1. **Rapid radial expansion** was easily quantified on the flat agar surface
2. The semi-defined nature of malt agar meant the effect was not confounded by nutritional variables
3. The magnitude of the effect (2–3×) was unusually large for a single phytohormone application

### Fruiting Parameters (Dung-Grain)

| Parameter | Control | Brassinosteroid-Treated |
|---|---|---|
| First flush appearance | 4–5 weeks | 3–3.5 weeks |
| First flush mushroom count | 1–3 | 4–7 |
| Total dry mass per 10g substrate | 2.3–2.9 g | 3.4–3.9 g |
| Incomplete fruit body formation | Common | Almost completely suppressed |

## Interpretation of System Differences

The agar system demonstrated that brassinosteroids directly accelerate mycelial growth independent of complex substrate interactions. The dung-grain system revealed additional effects on the fungal life cycle including:

1. **Earlier initiation of fruiting** — suggesting brassinosteroids may influence the developmental switch from vegetative to [[knf-reproductive-growth-stage]]
2. **Increased first-flush yield** — indicating improved [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]] or survival
3. **Higher overall dry mass** — reflecting more efficient substrate colonization and nutrient conversion
4. **Reduced abnormal fruiting** — suggesting brassinosteroids improve developmental regulation

## Significance of the Dual-System Approach

Using two complementary cultivation systems strengthened the study's conclusions in several ways:

- **Agar results** confirmed a direct growth-promoting effect on fungal cells, ruling out the possibility that enhanced fruiting was merely due to improved substrate conditioning
- **Dung-grain results** demonstrated practical relevance for cultivation, showing that the effect translates from simple to complex substrates
- The combination suggested that brassinosteroids influence multiple stages of the fungal life cycle, not just one isolated process

## Limitations

- The study used only a single species (P. cubensis) and a single strain
- Only one brassinosteroid concentration was tested
- The agar system could not assess fruiting, and the dung-grain system could not easily separate mycelial growth from fruiting effects
- No mechanism of action was proposed

## Relevance to Modern Cultivation

The comparison remains relevant because many modern cultivators still use grain jars (analogous to the dung-grain approach) for spawn production and agar for isolation work. The finding that brassinosteroids benefit both systems suggests they could be applied at multiple stages of a cultivation workflow, though practical brassinosteroid application in amateur mycology remains unexplored.
