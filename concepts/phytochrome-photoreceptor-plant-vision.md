## The Pr/Pfr Switching Mechanism

Phytochrome functions as a remarkable biological switch through its ability to
interconvert between two stable forms. The inactive form, known as Pr (for
P-red), absorbs red light at approximately 660 nm. Upon absorbing red light,
Pr is converted to the active form, Pfr (for P-far-red), which absorbs far-red
light at approximately 730 nm. When Pfr absorbs far-red light, it converts
back to Pr. In natural sunlight, which contains more red than far-red light,
the equilibrium shifts toward Pfr, signaling to the plant that it is in full
sunlight. In shade, where far-red light is relatively more abundant (due to
absorption of red light by chlorophyll in the canopy above), the equilibrium
shifts toward Pr, allowing the plant to detect that it is being shaded by
neighbors. This mechanism is the basis of the "shade [[shade-avoidance-syndrome]]," where
plants grown in shade elongate their stems to reach for light. The reversibility
