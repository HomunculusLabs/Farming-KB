# Mycorrhizal Symbiosis Mechanisms

Mycorrhizal symbiosis is the mutually beneficial association between fungal
hyphae and plant roots, found in over 90% of terrestrial plant species. This
ancient partnership, dating back over 400 million years, was likely critical to
the colonization of land by plants. The fungus provides soil-derived minerals
and water to the plant, while receiving photosynthetically fixed carbon in
return. Beyond this simple exchange, the symbiosis involves complex molecular
signaling, nutritional trading, and defense coordination.

## Types of Mycorrhizal Associations

### Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF)

The most widespread form, involving Glomeromycotina fungi that form arbuscules
(tree-like branched structures) inside root cortical cells. AMF are obligate
biotrophs, entirely dependent on their plant host for carbon. They associate
with the majority of herbaceous plants, many crops, and some woody species. Key
genera include *Rhizophagus*, *Funneliformis*, and *Gigaspora*. AMF produce
vesicles for lipid storage and extensive extraradical hyphal networks extending
far beyond the root zone.
