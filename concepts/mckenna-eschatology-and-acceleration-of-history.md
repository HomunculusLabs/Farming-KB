# Tryptamine Eschatology and the Acceleration of History

Terence McKenna proposed that [[mckenna-dmt-experience-and-hyperspace|DMT]], [[mckenna-ayahuasca-and-amazonian-shamanism|ayahuasca]], McKenna developed the idea that history is not the gradual, linear
unfolding that physicists describe but rather a "shockwave of eschatology," a process
of accelerating transformation drawing all human becoming toward a culminating point.

## History as Eschatological Shockwave

McKenna's central eschatological claim is that "something is at the end of time and
is casting an enormous shadow over human history, drawing all human becoming toward
it." In this view, all of human activity, the wars, philosophies, migrations, cities,
and civilizations, occupies what is essentially a microsecond of geological and
galactic time. History is not an open-ended process extending billions of years into
the future; it is a finite event with a discernible direction and an approaching
culmination.

This eschatological framework challenges the dualistic conception promoted by
physicists, who describe the universe as having lasted billions of years with an
equally vast future projected ahead. McKenna regarded this as an inductive projection
that is fundamentally unsophisticated when applied to the nature of consciousness and
language. The tryptamine experience provides direct experiential evidence that
consciousness has the capacity to "collapse the state vector," in the language of
quantum mechanics, and thereby participate in what Alfred North Whitehead called
"the formality of actually occurring."

## The Pattern of Acceleration

McKenna identified a clear pattern of acceleration visible in all processes around
humanity. Fire was discovered several million years ago. Language emerged perhaps
thirty-five thousand years ago. Measurement developed five thousand years ago.
Galileo and modern science appeared four hundred years ago. Watson and Crick
discovered the structure of DNA within living memory.

"What is obviously happening," McKenna argued, "is that everything is being drawn
together." The rate of significant novelty entering human experience is increasing,
and the intervals between major transformative events are shrinking. This
acceleration is not merely technological; it encompasses consciousness itself, as the
tryptamine experience demonstrates by collapsing temporal boundaries and revealing
the interconnectedness of historical epochs in a single apprehension.

## The Tryptamine State as Transtemporal Experience

McKenna described the tryptamine state as transtemporal, an anticipation of the
future. He invoked Plato's metaphor that "time is the moving image of eternity,"
suggesting that the [[mckenna-tryptamine-ecstasy-and-platonic-eternity]] represents a stepping out of the moving image
and into eternity itself, the "standing now" or nunc stans of Thomas Aquinas. In
this state, all of human history is seen to lead toward a culminating moment.

The experience suggests that the distinctions we normally draw between past, present,
and future are artifacts of ordinary consciousness rather than features of reality
itself. In the tryptamine state, the observer can perceive the entire arc of human
becoming as a single, coherent process with a discernible direction. This perception
is not speculative or metaphorical but is experienced as directly apprehended fact.

## The Centrality of the Perceiving Self

McKenna argued for a radical recentralization of the human being in cosmology.
Western societies, he noted, have spent five hundred years on a "decentralizing
bender," concluding that Earth is not the center of the universe and that humans are
not the beloved of God. We have moved ourselves out toward the edge of the galaxy,
when the fact is that "the most richly organized material in the universe is the
human cerebral cortex, and the densest and richest experience in the universe is the
experience you are having right now."

Everything should be constellated outward from the perceiving self, because the
perceiving self is the [[decentralizing-bender-perceiving-self-primary-datum-mckenna]]. This recentralization does not imply crude
anthropocentrism. Rather, it acknowledges that consciousness, wherever it occurs,
is the most complex and significant phenomenon known to exist. The tryptamine
experience, by revealing dimensions of consciousness that dwarf ordinary awareness,
paradoxically reinforces the centrality of the conscious observer by showing how
much more the observer can become.

## The Exteriorization of the Soul

McKenna's ultimate eschatological vision involved what he called the "exteriorization
of the soul and internalization of the body." He envisioned a future in which the
exterior soul would exist as a "superconducting lens of [[mckenna-translinguistic-matter-and-exteriorized-soul]]
generated out of the body of each of us at a critical juncture." From that point
onward, humans would exist eternally within the solid-state matrix of the
translinguistic lens they had become. The body image would exist as a holographic
wave transform while consciousness lived in what he metaphorically called "the fields
of the Lord."

This vision draws on the tryptamine experience of language as a visible,
three-dimensional phenomenon. In the DMT state, meaning can be beheld in spatial
terms, suggesting that a human language is possible in which the intent of meaning
is actually beheld in three-dimensional space. Given cultural engagement with such a
talent over millennia, he speculated, it could become as much a cultural convenience
as mathematics or ordinary language.

## Language and Self-Replicating Information

McKenna connected his eschatological framework to the nature of language itself.
What is "loose on this planet," he argued, is language: self-replicating information
systems that reflect functions of DNA, including learning, coding, templating,
recording, testing, re-testing, and re-coding against DNA functions. Language may be
a quality of an entirely different order than biology, but whatever it is, it is
embedded in human beings and moving through them, moving out through our hands and
into the noosphere we have built around ourselves.

The tryptamine experience reveals language as something more fundamental than a
communication tool. In the psychedelic state, language is experienced as having an
ontological dimension: words and meanings are not merely transmitted but are beheld,
felt, and inhabited. This suggests that the acceleration of history is driven by
language's own evolutionary trajectory, which is carrying consciousness toward a
point of radical transformation.

## Consciousness as the Collapse of the State Vector

McKenna drew on quantum mechanics to explain the relationship between consciousness
and reality. "Consciousness is somehow able to collapse the state vector," he wrote,
"and thereby cause the stuff of being to undergo the formality of actually
occurring." This places consciousness at the center of reality, not as an
epiphenomenon of matter but as a fundamental creative force.
