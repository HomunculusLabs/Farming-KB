# Ampelomyces quisqualis as Biocontrol Agent in AACT

## Overview

Ampelomyces quisqualis is a naturally occurring parasitic fungus that specifically attacks powdery mildew fungi. In Elaine Ingham's AACT field guide, Ampelomyces is discussed as a notable biocontrol organism that can be enhanced and delivered through compost tea applications. This hyperparasite represents one of the most targeted biological control strategies available for managing powdery mildew, one of the most widespread and persistent foliar diseases affecting agricultural and horticultural crops.

## Biological Characteristics

### Taxonomy and Classification

Ampelomyces quisqualis belongs to the phylum Ascomycota and is classified as a mycoparasite, meaning it is a fungus that parasitizes other fungi. Unlike generalist biocontrol agents such as Trichoderma species, Ampelomyces has a strong host specificity for powdery mildews (Erysiphales), making it a precision tool in the biological disease management toolkit.

### Life Cycle and Parasitic Mechanism

The lifecycle of Ampelomyces involves direct penetration of powdery mildew hyphae and conidiophores. The parasite produces specialized infection structures that breach the cell walls of its host. Once inside the powdery mildew tissue, Ampelomyces absorbs nutrients from the host cells, ultimately causing their death and collapse.

This parasitic relationship is remarkably specific. Ampelomyces does not attack plant tissue, beneficial fungi, or other non-target organisms. Its host range is essentially limited to the Erysiphales (powdery mildew order), which includes genera such as Erysiphe, Podosphaera, Sphaerotheca, Uncinula, and Blumeria.

### Environmental Requirements

Ampelomyces thrives under conditions that are also favorable for powdery mildew development, which is a key advantage for biocontrol timing. It requires moderate temperatures (20-28°C), adequate humidity, and the presence of its host. However, unlike powdery mildew itself, Ampelomyces does not require the same leaf surface dryness and can function under a broader range of humidity conditions.

## Role in AACT Systems

### Enhancing Ampelomyces Through Compost Tea

Ingham's field guide identifies several pathways through which AACT can support Ampelomyces populations:

1. **Direct inoculation**: Compost containing Ampelomyces propagules, when brewed into tea, can distribute these organisms across crop foliage. The aeration and nutrient addition during brewing encourages multiplication of Ampelomyces along with other beneficial fungi.

2. **Indirect support**: AACT applications that establish diverse fungal communities on leaf surfaces create a competitive environment where Ampelomyces can thrive. The leaf surface microbiome (phyllosphere) supported by regular compost tea applications provides the ecological context in which Ampelomyces functions most effectively.

3. **Nutrient facilitation**: The humic acids, amino acids, and other compounds present in well-brewed compost tea provide nutritional support for Ampelomyces colonization and sporulation on leaf surfaces.

### Integration with Existing AACT Protocols

Ampelomyces is not something that needs to be added as a separate product when using AACT systems. If the compost used for brewing is derived from a healthy, biologically diverse source, particularly one that has had contact with powdery mildew-affected plant material during the composting process, Ampelomyces is likely already present.

The thermal composting process does not necessarily eliminate Ampelomyces. While thermophilic composting reaches temperatures that kill many organisms, Ampelomyces can survive in the cooler outer layers of the compost pile or recolonize from surrounding soil. Vermicompost (worm compost) is an even better source, as the mesophilic temperatures in vermicomposting systems are well within Ampelomyces' survival range.

## Target Diseases

### Powdery Mildew on Various Crops

Powdery mildew affects an enormous range of crops, and Ampelomyces has demonstrated efficacy against the disease on many hosts:

**Vegetables**: Cucurbits (cucumber, melon, squash), tomatoes, peppers, lettuce, and peas are all susceptible to various powdery mildew species. AACT applications enriched with Ampelomyces activity can significantly reduce disease severity on these crops.

**Grapes**: Uncinula necator causes powdery mildew on grapevines, one of the most economically important diseases in viticulture. Ampelomyces has been extensively studied and commercialized for grape powdery mildew control. Ingham references vineyard success stories where AACT programs incorporating Ampelomyces activity reduced or eliminated the need for fungicidal sprays.

**Strawberries**: Podosphaera aphanis causes powdery mildew on strawberries, affecting both fruit quality and yield. Foliar applications of compost tea that support Ampelomyces can suppress this disease while also improving overall plant health through the broader benefits of the tea's microbial community.

**Ornamentals**: Roses, phlox, lilacs, and many other ornamental plants suffer from powdery mildew. AACT applications provide both direct biocontrol through Ampelomyces and indirect protection through competitive exclusion and plant immune system stimulation.

## Application Strategies

### Timing of Applications

For Ampelomyces to be effective as a biocontrol agent through AACT, applications should begin before or at the very early stages of powdery mildew infection. The parasite needs to be established on leaf surfaces before the powdery mildew mycelium becomes too dense. Once powdery mildew has extensively colonized a leaf, the effectiveness of Ampelomyces is reduced because the host fungus has already damaged plant tissue.

A proactive application schedule, beginning when environmental conditions become favorable for powdery mildew (typically warm days with cool nights and moderate humidity), provides the best results. Regular applications every 7-14 days during the vulnerable period maintain Ampelomyces populations on leaf surfaces.

### Foliar Application Technique

AACT containing Ampelomyces should be applied as a fine foliar spray that thoroughly covers both the upper and lower leaf surfaces. Powdery mildew often begins colonization on the undersides of leaves and in sheltered areas of the plant canopy, so coverage must be complete.

Application should be made in the early morning or late evening to avoid the desiccating effects of midday sun on the applied organisms. The higher humidity during these periods also favors Ampelomyces establishment. Ingham recommends using a spray nozzle that produces a mist rather than large droplets, as finer coverage improves organism distribution and adhesion to leaf surfaces.

### Additives and Adjuncts

The efficacy of Ampelomyces through AACT can be enhanced with appropriate brewing adjuncts:

- **Humic acids** support fungal growth and improve the leaf surface environment for Ampelomyces colonization
- **Kelp extracts** provide micronutrients and natural growth regulators that support both the biocontrol agent and the host plant's defense responses
- **Fish hydrolysate** supplies amino acids that serve as a nitrogen source for fungal growth on the phyllosphere
- **Molasses** in modest amounts supports general microbial activity but should be used carefully as excessive sugars on leaf surfaces can favor non-target organisms

### Environmental Management

AACT applications work best when combined with cultural practices that reduce powdery mildew pressure:

- Adequate plant spacing to improve air circulation and reduce humidity within the canopy
- Proper pruning to open plant structure and reduce shaded areas where powdery mildew thrives
- Avoiding excess nitrogen fertilization, which produces succulent growth that is more susceptible to infection
- Choosing resistant or tolerant varieties when available

## Monitoring and Assessment

### Microscopic Verification

The presence and activity of Ampelomyces in compost tea can be verified through direct microscopy. The organism produces distinctive pycnidia (fruiting bodies) within the powdery mildew mycelium, and these can be observed at 200-400x magnification. Ingham's testing protocols for AACT include examination for the presence of mycoparasitic fungi as part of quality assessment.

### Field Assessment

Field monitoring for powdery mildew should track both the incidence (percentage of plants affected) and severity (percentage of leaf area covered) of the disease. AACT programs that successfully support Ampelomyces activity should show reduced disease progression compared to untreated controls.

## Compatibility with Other Biocontrol Organisms

One of the strengths of the AACT approach to delivering Ampelomyces is that the tea simultaneously introduces and supports a diverse community of beneficial organisms. These work synergistically with Ampelomyces rather than competing with it:

- **Bacillus species** produce antibiotics that suppress powdery mildew spore germination, complementing Ampelomyces' parasitic action
- **Trichoderma species** compete for leaf surface space and nutrients, reducing the substrate available for powdery mildew establishment
- **Beneficial yeasts** occupy ecological niches on the phyllosphere that might otherwise be colonized by powdery mildew spores
- **Pseudomonas bacteria** induce systemic resistance in host plants, priming their natural defense mechanisms

This multi-organism approach is fundamentally different from single-organism biocontrol products and is one of the reasons Ingham advocates for AACT over commercial biological fungicides. The diversity of the compost tea community provides redundancy; if one control mechanism is less effective under current conditions, others compensate.

## Comparison with Commercial Biocontrol Products

Several commercial products containing Ampelomyces quisqualis are available as standalone biocontrol agents. These typically contain concentrated spore preparations that can be mixed with water and sprayed. While effective, these products differ from the AACT approach in important ways:

- Commercial products deliver a single organism or narrow range of organisms, while AACT delivers the full soil food web community
- Commercial products require repeated purchase, while AACT can be produced on-farm from locally sourced compost
- AACT organisms are actively growing at the time of application, while commercial spores may require time to germinate and establish
- AACT provides additional benefits beyond disease control, including nutrient cycling, plant growth promotion, and soil health improvement

Ingham does not dismiss commercial biocontrol products but positions them as complementary to rather than replacements for a well-managed AACT program. In high-value crops or severe disease situations, combining commercial Ampelomyces products with AACT applications may provide the highest level of protection.

## Limitations

Ampelomyces alone cannot completely prevent powdery mildew under severe disease pressure. It is most effective as part of an integrated approach that includes cultural practices, variety selection, and environmental management. The organism is also sensitive to certain fungicides, so chemical and biological control programs must be coordinated carefully. Additionally, the efficacy of Ampelomyces through AACT depends on the quality and consistency of the compost used for brewing; not all compost contains adequate populations of this specific mycoparasite.
