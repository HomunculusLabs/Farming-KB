## Ampelomyces quisqualis as Biocontrol Agent in AACT

## Overview

Ampelomyces quisqualis is a naturally occurring parasitic fungus that specifically attacks powdery mildew fungi. In Elaine Ingham's AACT field guide, Ampelomyces is discussed as a notable biocontrol organism that can be enhanced and delivered through compost tea applications. This hyperparasite represents one of the most targeted [[biodiversity-powdery-mildews]] (Erysiphales), making it a precision tool in the biological [[fungal-appressorium-and-infection-structures]] that breach the cell walls of its host. Once inside the powdery mildew tissue, Ampelomyces absorbs nutrients from the host cells, ultimately causing their death and collapse.

This parasitic relationship is remarkably specific. Ampelomyces does not attack plant tissue, beneficial fungi, or other non-target organisms. Its host range is essentially limited to the Erysiphales (powdery mildew order), which includes genera such as Erysiphe, Podosphaera, Sphaerotheca, Uncinula, and Blumeria.

### Environmental Requirements

Ampelomyces thrives under conditions that are also favorable for powdery mildew development, which is a key advantage for biocontrol timing. It requires moderate temperatures (20-28°C), adequate humidity, and the presence of its host. However, unlike powdery mildew itself, Ampelomyces does not require the same leaf surface dryness and can function under a broader range of humidity conditions.

## Role in AACT Systems

### Enhancing Ampelomyces Through Compost Tea

Ingham's field guide identifies several pathways through which AACT can support Ampelomyces populations:

1. **Direct inoculation**: Compost containing Ampelomyces propagules, when brewed into tea, can distribute these organisms across crop foliage. The aeration and nutrient addition during brewing encourages multiplication of Ampelomyces along with other beneficial fungi.

2. **Indirect support**: AACT applications that establish diverse fungal communities on leaf surfaces create a competitive environment where Ampelomyces can thrive. The leaf surface microbiome (phyllosphere) supported by regular compost tea applications provides the ecological context in which Ampelomyces functions most effectively.

3. **Nutrient facilitation**: The humic acids, amino acids, and other compounds present in well-brewed compost tea provide nutritional support for Ampelomyces colonization and sporulation on leaf surfaces.

### Integration with Existing AACT Protocols

Ampelomyces is not something that needs to be added as a separate product when using AACT systems. If the compost used for brewing is derived from a healthy, biologically diverse source, particularly one that has had contact with powdery mildew-affected plant material during the composting process, Ampelomyces is likely already present.

The [[uncinula-necator]] causes powdery mildew on grapevines, one of the most economically important diseases in viticulture. Ampelomyces has been extensively studied and commercialized for grape powdery mildew control. Ingham references [[cannabis-cultivar-microbial-community-effects]].

**Ornamentals**: Roses, phlox, lilacs, and many other ornamental plants suffer from powdery mildew. AACT applications provide both direct biocontrol through Ampelomyces and indirect protection through competitive exclusion and plant immune system stimulation.

## Application Strategies

### Timing of Applications

For Ampelomyces to be effective as a biocontrol agent through AACT, applications should begin before or at the very early stages of powdery mildew infection. The parasite needs to be established on leaf surfaces before the powdery mildew mycelium becomes too dense. Once powdery mildew has extensively colonized a leaf, the effectiveness of Ampelomyces is reduced because the host fungus has already damaged plant tissue.

A proactive application schedule, beginning when environmental conditions become favorable for powdery mildew (typically warm days with cool nights and moderate humidity), provides the best results. Regular applications every 7-14 days during the vulnerable period maintain Ampelomyces populations on leaf surfaces.

### Foliar Application Technique

AACT containing Ampelomyces should be applied as a fine foliar spray that thoroughly covers both the upper and lower leaf surfaces. Powdery mildew often begins colonization on the undersides of leaves and in sheltered areas of the plant canopy, so coverage must be complete.

Application should be made in the early morning or late evening to avoid the desiccating effects of midday sun on the applied organisms. The higher humidity during these periods also favors Ampelomyces establishment. Ingham recommends using a spray nozzle that produces a mist rather than large droplets, as finer coverage improves organism distribution and adhesion to leaf surfaces.
