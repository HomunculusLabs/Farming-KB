## Virola Snuff

Virola snuffs are psychoactive preparations made from the resinous
inner bark of several species of Virola, a genus of trees in the
nutmeg family (Myristicaceae). Used by numerous indigenous tribes of
the western Amazon basin, virola snuffs represent one of the most
widespread and pharmacologically significant entheogenic snuff
traditions in [[virola-calophylla]]**: Used by tribes in the upper Rio Negro region 
  of Brazil
- **[[virola-elongata]]**: Used by various tribes of the Vaupes region of 
  Colombia

Virola trees are large canopy trees of lowland [[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]], particularly concentrated
in the cambial layer just beneath the outer bark. When the bark is
cut or scraped, the resinous exudate oozes out and can be collected
for snuff preparation.

## Geographic Distribution

Virola snuffs are used by indigenous peoples throughout the western
Amazon, including parts of Brazil, Colombia, Venezuela, and the
Guianas. Among the tribes known to use virola snuffs are the Waika
(Yanomamo), the Makú, the Barasana, the Kubo, the Tukano, and
numerous other groups of the Rio Negro and Vaupes river systems. The
practice is particularly widespread among tribes of the upper Rio
Negro basin.

## Chemistry

The psychoactive constituents of Virola bark resin are tryptamine
alkaloids, the same class of compounds found in [[ayahuasca-preparation]]:

- **DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine)**: The principal active compound, fou
  nd at concentrations of 0.11-8.3% in dried bark
- **5-MeO-DMT**: Present in significant amounts in some species, addin
  g to the intensity of the experience
- **N-methyltryptamine (NMT)**: Found in smaller quantities
- **Bufotenine**: Present in some Virola species, though usually at lo
  wer concentrations than in Anadenanthera
