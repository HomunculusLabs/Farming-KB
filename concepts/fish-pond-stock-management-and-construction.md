# Fish Pond Stock Management and Construction

## Overview

Managing fish populations in ponds requires controlling breeding, minimizing losses, and optimizing stocking rates. These factors, combined with appropriate pond construction techniques, form the foundation of productive aquaculture. Mollison draws on Swingle (1966) and Chakroff (1982) to present practical guidelines for both small-scale and commercial operations.

## Breeding Control in Fish Ponds

Optimum stocking rates only make sense if the number of fish in the pond remains fairly constant. Fast-growing pond fish kept beyond 3–7 months can breed in the pond itself, leading to overstocking and stunted growth. Fish culturalists employ several methods to prevent this:

### Sterile Hybrids

Hybridization in [[tilapia]] species produces sterile males or one-sex stocks. With sterile fish, "counts in" equal "counts out" (less mortality, allowing for 30–40% fry losses).

### Crowding Suppression

[[brown-bullhead]] cease to breed above 7,500 fish per hectare. Other species are similarly inhibited, probably by crowding stress or waste accumulation.

### Timing of Stocking and Harvest

Fish added and harvested during spring-summer fast-growing conditions can be taken before or at the point of breeding, with the pond then restocked.

### Predator-Mediated Control

Predators with, or screened off from, breeding fish can reduce their numbers to an optimum level. Predator fish yields serve as a bonus.

### Substrate and Habitat Denial

Ponds lacking the right substrate or niche for breeders prevent eggs being laid or surviving. Fish will often not spawn if substrate is not provided. Fish that breed in fresh, saline, or seawater will not breed if kept in a different habitat — this applies to mullet, eels, trout, milkfish, prawns, and shrimp. Water temperature and water level changes also influence spawning; a rise or fall in water level is crucial to carp and mullet species that breed on flooded grasses or reeds.

### Hormonal Manipulation

Pituitary extract is widely used to induce spawning. Trials of other hormones are being made to inhibit spawning. *Puntius* is a fish used as a source for pituitary extract.

## Fish Losses

Losses in ponds occur from multiple causes:

- **Theft** — requires fences, locks, and proximity of the fish culturalist to the pond area
- **Predation** by numerous or large predators — requires predator control measures
- **Diseases or parasites** carried in by stream water
- **Extreme heat or cold**
- **Accidental draining** of the pond — requires automatic signalling or pumping systems
- **Sudden heavy rains** in estuarine ponds (may be unavoidable for shrimp culture)
- **Flood bypasses** are a normal precaution in freshwater pond culture

As fish density increases, the upper limits to yield may be determined by pond water quality and pathogens encouraged by crowding — a factor that may be the ultimate barrier to yields, as it is in land livestock.

## Stocking Rates

Stocking rate is a critical factor, ranging from 200–300 fish/ha for predators to 5,000–10,000 fish/ha for plankton eaters and detritus feeders like Tilapia or carp. Rates depend on the ability of fish to tolerate crowding, find food, and convert food efficiently.

Very low stocking rates produce low yields of large fish — beware of early trials showing low yield if fish were stocked alone at less than 1,000/ha. Too dense a rate results in a larger proportion of unmarketable small fish. A tolerance of 5–10% stunted fish is acceptable.

### Tilapia Stocking Data

| Rate/ha | Yield (kg) | Marketable (%) |
|---------|-----------|----------------|
| 5,000   | 3,162     | 97.5           |
| 10,000  | 903.2     | 50.4           |

Tilapia below market size took 7 months longer to reach the 200 g limit achieved by fast growers in 6 months. A clear selection factor is at work — fast growers should be selected for future brood stock.

### Buffalofish Stocking Data

| Rate/ha | Yield (kg) | Average Fish Weight |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| 300     | 152       | 636 g             |
| 600     | 2,735     | 603 g             |
| 1,080   | 6,565     | 590 g             |

From this data, the sensible stocking rate for Tilapia has been exceeded, while the rate reducing marketable size for buffalofish has not yet been reached.

### Fertilized Pond Stocking Rates

Fish stocked as fingerlings lose food unless a fast-breeding forage fish utilizes it. With supplementary food, much larger numbers can be stocked, saved, and therefore can use natural foods better:

| System                  | No. Stocked/ha | Fish Produced (kg) |
|-------------------------|----------------|--------------------|
| Tilapia alone           | 2,500          | 631.7              |
| Tilapia alone           | 5,000          | 840.1              |
| Tilapia alone           | 7,500          | 1,011.8            |
| Tilapia alone           | 15,000         | 1,387.0            |
| Tilapia with catfish    | 2,500          | 1,076.7            |
| Tilapia with catfish    | 5,000          | 1,709.7            |
| Tilapia with catfish    | 10,000         | 2,646.6            |

(Above 10,000/ha with Tilapia plus catfish, no spawning occurred in the pond.)

## Pond Construction

### Small-Scale Ponds (up to 0.2 ha)

Can be hand-tuned for drainage, levelling, and spillways. Still-ponds require a compact clay base, while tidal and diversion ponds can tolerate some seepage losses.

### New Pond Assessment

New ponds need careful survey for:
- Evaporation rates
- Seepage potential
- Water sources
- Sealing requirements
- Structural stability

Few fish ponds exceed 2–3 m in depth, so earth, clay, and stone-faced earth walls suffice. Trout ponds and fast-flow channels may be concrete-sealed in intensive systems, but by far the most productive, common, and economical ponds are clay-based.

### Clay Core and Sealing

The clay core or seal is effective in miniature in fish ponds. A build-up of organic mulch and algae in ponds assists sealing, so that even ponds in sandy loams may gradually evolve to reduce water losses. Long uncompacted canals need good clay bases to prevent water loss in transit, although seepages are not necessarily lost to production if land crops and trees can be planted to utilize seepage water.

### Evolution from Rice Paddies

Ponds can evolve from low-walled padi (rice paddies) to deeper permanent or seasonal fish systems, or have a dry cycle in summer seasons. This represents one of the most accessible pathways for rice farmers to diversify into aquaculture.

## Key Principles

1. **Control breeding** through at least one method appropriate to species — sterile hybrids, crowding, timing, predation, substrate denial, or hormonal manipulation
2. **Prevent losses** through physical security, water quality management, and flood protection
3. **Optimize stocking rates** through experimentation — both over-stocking and under-stocking reduce productive yield
4. **Use supplementary food** to enable higher stocking rates and faster growth to market size
5. **Build clay-based ponds** for the most economical and productive results, with organic mulch buildup to improve sealing over time
