# Design Charette Process

The charette is where the design process moves from analysis to synthesis. It is the brainstorming and integration session where a design team works together to create a coherent permaculture design. The charette is a critical component of [[pdc-design-project-methodology-and-case-study]] and represents the collaborative heart of the design process.

## Overview

The charette is the brainstorming session of design. A team will typically have multiple charettes before arriving at a final design. The goal of each charette is to move progressively closer to a final design, not to spin wheels on endless brainstorming. Teams should bring better and better presentation materials to each charette, refining the specifics of their design elements each time.

## Scheduling

As a team, work out a charette schedule determining how many times you will get together. The schedule should allow enough time between sessions for individual research and design work, but be frequent enough to maintain momentum.

## First Charette: Sharing Analysis

### Purpose
At the first charette, everyone shares the results from their [[pdc-landform-and-topography-reading]] and produces an agreed-upon copy of the base map data.

### Process
1. Each team member shares their analysis findings
2. The team creates a single agreed-upon base map incorporating all data
3. Each member receives a copy of the working base map and the client interview
4. The team brainstorms what the design should be like

### Client Participation
The client should be invited to each charette. However, if the client cannot attend, the team should proceed without them. The client's input at this stage helps ensure the design reflects their philosophy and needs.

## Between First and Second Charette: Individual Research

After the first charette, all members work independently (or inter-dependently) on their specific elements. This phase concentrates on research:

- Finding the right type of plants for each zone
- Researching design precedent
- Looking for structures in analogous climates
- Investigating specific technologies
- Studying [[williams-pdc-appropriate-technology]] options

When everyone has completed their research, the team reconvenes to share findings so that all members are on the same page about what elements the design will contain.

## Second Charette: Individual Element Design

### Process
1. Each team member presents their research findings
2. Team agrees on the full set of design elements
3. Each member retires to work out the specific design of their assigned area:
   - How large will structures need to be?
   - How should gardens be laid out?
   - What specific species will be used?
   - What are the technical requirements?

4. Everyone reconvenes to share individual designs

## Third Charette: Integration (The Tetris Phase)

This is the most critical charette, where all design elements must be connected together.

### The Integration Challenge
- How do the gardens interact with the structures?
- Where exactly should the greywater systems go?
- How do the zones connect?
- Does the design address all sectors?
- Are there conflicts between elements?

"Essentially you are playing Tetris, so to speak, with your design elements."

### Ego Management
Each team member MUST check their ego at the door and be prepared to make whatever changes are necessary to make the design fit together. This requires:
- Flexibility and willingness to compromise
- Respect for other team members' expertise
- Focus on the overall design quality rather than individual elements
- Trust in the collaborative process

### Design Arbiter
If needed, there should be a final design arbiter to settle any disputes. All team members MUST respect the arbiter's final decisions. The best arbiter is the client, if they can attend the charette.

### Output
When the team reaches consensus on how everything fits together, each member updates their working copy of the base map with all design decisions.

## Final Charette: Polish and Verification

One final charette ensures that:
- All designs fit together coherently
- All elements meet criteria from the [[pdc-design-exercise-method]]
- No conflicts remain between elements
- The design is ready for [[pdc-design-project-methodology-and-case-study]]

## Final Individual Work

After the final charette, each team member works on finalizing their aspect of the design so it integrates with the whole. This requires:
- Reference to recommended readings for each specific permaculture element
- Knowledge that designing a garden is different from designing a greywater system, which is different from designing a straw-bale house
- Each team member should have resources specific to their element
- The Permaculture Designer's Manual by Bill Mollison covers many design particulars
- The internet is an excellent resource for design research

## Common Pitfalls

- Endless brainstorming without convergence
- Team members becoming too attached to their individual elements
- Failure to integrate elements before finalizing individual designs
- Skipping the integration charette
- Not inviting the client to charettes
- Poor scheduling that allows too much time between sessions
