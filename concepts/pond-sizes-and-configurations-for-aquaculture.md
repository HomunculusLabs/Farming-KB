# Pond Sizes and Configurations for Aquaculture

Fish ponds range from tiny garden containers to multi-hectare impoundments, each scale suited to different purposes, management intensities, and economic contexts. Understanding the appropriate uses for each size order—and how ponds can be arranged in landscapes—allows permaculture designers to integrate aquaculture effectively at any scale from household to commercial enterprise.

## Pond Construction Fundamentals

Small-scale ponds (up to 0.2 ha) can be hand-tuned for drainage, levelling, and spillways. Larger constructions result from years of hand labour shaped to the landscape, or are built by modern survey and machine methods.

### Core Requirements

- **Still ponds** have an essential need for a compact clay base
- **Tidal and diversion ponds** can tolerate some seepage losses
- Clay cores or seals are effective in miniature, just as in full-scale dams
- Organic mulch and algae buildup in ponds assists sealing over time—even ponds in sandy loams may gradually reduce water losses
- Long uncompacted canals need good clay bases to prevent water loss in transit

As few fish ponds exceed 2–3 m in depth, earth, clay, and stone-faced earth walls suffice to hold water. Trout ponds and fast-flow channels may use concrete sealing in intensive systems, but the most productive, common, and economical ponds are clay-based. Ponds can evolve from low-walled padi to deeper permanent or seasonal fish systems, or have a dry cycle in summer seasons.

## Ponds from 1–10 Square Metres

Very small garden ponds of 2–60 cm depth can be made from old baths, stock watering tanks, plastic-lined holes with protective clay or earth covers, and pre-cast ponds in plastic, fibreglass, or concrete.

### Shallow Pond Crops

In shallow configurations, productive species include:

- **Chinese water chestnut** (*[[eleocharis-dulcis]]*)
- **Kangkong** (*Ipomoea aquatica*)
- **Watercress** (*Rorippa aquatica*)
- **Taro** (*Colocasia esculentus*)—a single square metre yields 20–30 kg of starchy food
- **Frogs**—excellent garden predators (as are lizards); Hylid (tree) frogs inhabit plant leaves, feeding on insects day and night
- **Small fish**—for forage stock for larger ponds

Deeper small ponds will grow Indian water chestnut (*Trapa natans*), lotus (*Nelumbo*), and arrowhead (*Sagittaria*). Boiled taro, cassava, or plantain can supplement fish food.

### The 10 m² Intensive Pond

At about 10 square metres and 2 m deep, clear fibreglass or Kalwall® ponds (as used at the [[new-alchemy-institute]], Cape Cod, USA) have demonstrated remarkable productivity:

- Production valued at **$4.50 to $17 per square foot** (1984)
- Costs amortized in 3–5 years
- Yield fish, shrimp, and enriched water for semi-hydroponic crops
- Provide useful heat storage for night and winter heating
- Can be used in greenhouses or outdoors for much of the year

**Design enhancements:**

- Outdoor ponds placed in front of reflective (white) walls on white gravels maximize solar input, increase yields, and lengthen growing seasons
- In glasshouses, ponds moderate temperature extremes while providing algae-rich waters for terrestrial plant beds, routing fish wastes (nitrates) to plants

There is a clear business opening for standard-sized productive pond kits for homeowners—a field with few suppliers (most having concentrated on decorative plants). Subsidiary uses include water stores for fire, tank supplies for gardens in dry periods, productive disposal systems for food wastes fed to omnivorous fish, and recreational aquaria (recommended for workaholics as a relaxing hobby).

## Ponds from 10–100 Square Metres

Ponds of this size serve multiple valuable functions:

- Rearing fish fry for sale or stocking
- Breeding forage fish
- Producing large quantities of vegetable crop
- Supplying aquaculture nurseries and aquariums with plant and animal stocks
- Creating fire-breaks, water reserves, and heat moderation

A 100 square metre intensively-managed fish pond comes close to providing a full protein and vegetable resource for a family, if carefully designed and stocked with beneficial polyculture. Aeration via photovoltaic cell and air pump may be necessary for high yields, but the modest yields of 300–2,000 kg of protein are a significant contribution to food self-reliance.

These are specialty ponds requiring very careful planning and subsequent modification. They can form part of total wastewater systems: in line with a biogas or septic tank unit, they grow a fairly constant mass of green manures for garden mulch or biogas recycling.

## Ponds from 100–500 Square Metres

Chakroff (1982) and others regard ponds of this size (alone or in series) as **ideal for fish culture**:

- Easily harvested, netted, or drained
- Capable of holding specific age, size, or species assemblies
- Give flexibility in management

A single 500 square metre pond (or five 100 square metre ponds) devoted to a special crop can provide income from baitfish, aquarium fish, special plant crops, and intensively managed fish (e.g., prawns). A nursery providing eggs, seeds, fry, or fingerlings need not exceed this size.

In 1983, incomes of about **$30,000** were achievable from open-air ponds of this size stocked with prawns (Hawaii).

## Ponds from 500 Square Metres to 5 Hectares

About 5 ha is estimated to provide a full (affluent) family income in high-value product. Ten to twenty prawn ponds (with ducks, mussels, and edge plants) represent a full-time job akin to—but more easily managed than—a market garden.

This scale permits:

- Multiple use and leasehold of other activities
- Supply of a relatively large area of land crop
- Complete fire-break for homesteads and fuel forests
- Semi-intensive management; many cell-rearing establishments operate at about this size
- Sewage ponds of this size provide safe disposal for small towns

## Ponds Greater Than 5 Hectares

Impoundments of 50–500 hectares are now not uncommon as extensive farms. A single impoundment designed for each management level can support a family or families. At 10–20 ha, harvest and occasional re-stocking become the main activities.

Companies and investors may attempt intensive management at this scale but are often limited by cost-benefit, food supply, or disease control ceilings. Innovative leaseholds, recreational fishing, and new planning scales (village or shoreline residence development, holiday homes, boating, water sales) may become feasible.

### The Nested Size Principle

Every size order can include specialty ponds of the previous order, devoted to specific functions. A large commercial pond complex might contain a 100 m² hatchery, a 10 m² isolation tank, and a 5 m² breeding cage—all serving the needs of the larger system while themselves remaining small and manageable.

## Pond Layout Principles

Ponds can be arranged in several configurations:

- **Isolated**: Separate still-water ponds for quarantine, disease management, or trials
- **Parallel**: Common inflow/outflow with individual pond management (the most common fish-culture system)
- **Series (consecutive)**: Trophic ladder arrangement where forage ponds flow into carnivore ponds
- **Annidated (nested)**: Smaller ponds within larger ponds for specialized functions

The choice of arrangement depends on landscape configuration, water supply, species requirements, and management intensity. Each configuration has distinct advantages and risks that must be matched to local conditions.

## Key Design Principles

1. **Start small**—even 1–10 m² ponds contribute significantly to household food security
2. **Clay-based construction** is the most economical and productive approach
3. **100–500 m² is the ideal size** for serious fish culture at family or small-enterprise scale
4. **5 hectares** can provide a full family income in high-value products
5. **Nest specialty ponds** within larger systems for breeding, isolation, and nursery functions
6. **Integrate with greenhouse systems** for temperature moderation and nutrient cycling
7. **Match arrangement to landscape**—don't force configurations that don't fit the terrain
