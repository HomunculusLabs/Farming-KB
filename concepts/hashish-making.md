# Hashish Making

Hashish (hash) is a cannabis concentrate made by separating and collecting the resinous trichomes from the plant material. It is one of the oldest forms of cannabis processing, with a history spanning thousands of years across Central and South Asia, the Middle East, and North Africa. Modern techniques have refined the process while traditional methods remain in practice today.

## What Is Hashish

Hashish is a concentrated form of cannabis resin consisting primarily of trichome heads — the tiny, mushroom-shaped glandular structures on cannabis flowers and leaves that produce cannabinoids and terpenes. When separated from the plant material and compressed, these trichomes form a solid or semi-solid mass that is significantly more potent by weight than raw flower.

Traditional hashish contains 20-60% THC, while modern ice water extraction can produce material exceeding 70% THC when made from high-quality starting material.

## Dry Sift (Kief Collection)

Dry sift is the simplest and oldest mechanical method of making hash. It uses screens of varying micron sizes to separate trichome heads from plant material through physical agitation.

### Process

1. **Starting material**: Use dry, cured cannabis flower and sugar leaves. Material should be dry enough to be brittle but not so dry that trichomes shatter.
2. **Screen sizes**: Multiple screens are used in sequence, typically 160μ, 120μ, 90μ, 73μ, and 45μ (microns).
3. **Agitation**: Place material on the top (largest) screen and gently agitate over a collection surface. This can be done by:
   - **Cardboard box method**: A simple box with a screen bottom, shaken or tapped.
   - **Tumbler**: A motorized drum with screen mesh that gently tumbles the material.
   - **Hand sifting**: Rubbing material over a flat screen with a card or scraper.
4. **Grading**: The collected kief is passed through progressively smaller screens to separate grades:
   - **Full melt**: Primarily intact trichome heads (73-120μ). Bubbles completely when heated — the highest quality.
   - **Half melt**: Mix of heads and some broken stalks/broken trichomes. Partially bubbles.
   - **Non-melt**: Mostly plant material contamination. Doesn't bubble; used for edibles or cooking.

5. **Pressing**: Dry sift kief is pressed into hash blocks using a pollen press or by wrapping in parchment paper and applying heat and pressure.

### Quality Factors
- Starting material quality is the biggest variable — use well-grown, trichome-dense flower.
- Cold temperatures reduce contamination — sift in a cool room or freeze material first.
- Gentle agitation preserves trichome heads; aggressive agitation breaks them and increases plant contamination.

## Ice Water Extraction (Bubble Hash)

Ice water extraction uses cold water and agitation to separate trichomes based on their density — trichome heads sink while plant material floats. This is the method that produces "bubble hash" or "ice water hash."

### Equipment

- **Bubble bags**: A set of nested mesh bags with different micron sizes (220μ, 160μ, 120μ, 90μ, 73μ, 45μ, 25μ).
- **Bucket**: 5-gallon or larger food-grade bucket.
- **Ice**: Plenty of ice — the colder the water, the better the separation.
- **Mixing tool**: A hand mixer, drill with paint mixer attachment, or a dedicated washing machine (Bubble Now machine).
- **Spoon or ladle**: For collecting from each bag layer.

### Process

1. **Prep**: Freeze your starting material (trim, small buds, or whole flower) for at least 2 hours. Frozen material is more brittle, making trichome separation easier.
2. **Setup**: Line your bucket with the bubble bags in order from smallest (25μ) to largest (220μ) on the inside.
3. **Fill**: Add frozen plant material, ice (fill the bucket roughly 1/3 with ice), and cold water to near the top. The water should be just above freezing — 33-38°F (1-3°C).
4. **Agitate**: Stir gently for 10-20 minutes. The goal is to dislodge trichomes without pulverizing plant material:
   - **Hand mixing**: Stir in one direction, occasionally reversing. Don't create a vortex.
   - **Machine washing**: Set a timer — 5-10 minutes is usually sufficient.
5. **Drain and collect**: Pull bags up one at a time, letting water drain through. Scrape the collected material from the inside of each bag into separate containers.
6. **Repeat**: You can run the same material through a second or third wash to extract remaining trichomes, though quality decreases with each run.

### Grading Ice Water Hash

Each micron bag produces a different grade:

| Grade | Micron Range | Quality | Description |
|-------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| First wash (120-90μ) | 120μ, 90μ, 73μ | Highest | Full melt — primarily intact trichome heads. Melts completely, bubbles. |
| Second wash (73-45μ) | 73μ, 45μ | Medium-High | Good quality, some contamination. Mostly melts. |
| Third wash (45-25μ) | 45μ, 25μ | Lower | Increasing plant contamination. Partial melt. |
| Mixed/lowest grades | All combined | Variable | Suitable for cooking, edibles, infusions. |

### Drying and Curing

Properly drying ice water hash is essential:

1. **Freeze-drying** (ideal): Place hash pucks in a freeze dryer for 24-48 hours. This preserves terpenes and produces the best texture.
2. **Air drying**: Spread hash thin on parchment paper or a silicone mat. Place in a cool, dark area with good airflow. Flip daily. Takes 3-14 days.
3. **Curing**: Once dry, store in glass jars at 60°F and 58-62% RH (use Boveda packs). Cure for 2-4 weeks for best flavor.

## Rosin Pressing

Rosin is a solventless extract made by applying heat and pressure to cannabis flower, kief, or bubble hash to squeeze out the resinous oil. No solvents are used — only mechanical force.

### Equipment

- **Rosin press**: Hydraulic, pneumatic, or electric press with heated plates.
- **Parchment paper**: Food-grade, folded into packets.
- **Collection tools**: Dab tools, silicone containers.

### Process

1. **Prep**: Starting material should be slightly dried (62-65% RH is ideal). Place material between folded parchment paper.
2. **Press settings** (general guidelines):
   - **Flower rosin**: 180-220°F, 750-1500 PSI, 1-4 minutes
   - **Hash rosin (ice water hash)**: 150-190°F, 300-750 PSI, 1-3 minutes
   - **Kief rosin**: 170-200°F, 500-1000 PSI, 2-4 minutes
3. **Press**: Place parchment packet between heated plates and apply pressure. The oil will squeeze out onto the parchment.
4. **Collect**: While still warm, collect the rosin with a dab tool and transfer to a silicone container or parchment for storage.

**Quality tips**: Lower temperatures preserve more terpenes but yield less. Higher temperatures yield more but degrade terpenes. Experiment to find the balance for your starting material.

## Hand-Rubbed Charas

Charas is the traditional hand-rubbed hashish of India and Nepal, made by gently rubbing live cannabis plants between the hands to collect the resin.

### Process

1. **Timing**: Performed during the flowering season when plants are at peak resin production.
2. **Technique**: Growers slowly rub their hands up and down flowering branches. The friction and gentle heat cause trichomes to adhere to the skin.
3. **Collection**: The accumulated resin on the hands is periodically scraped off and rolled into balls or logs.
4. **Curing**: Fresh charas is typically aged to develop flavor and smoothness.

Charas is a labor-intensive, artisanal product. The quality depends heavily on the cultivar, timing, and the individual technique of the charas-maker. It retains a unique terpene profile because it is made from living, un-dried plant material — conceptually similar to modern live resin.

## Solvent-Extracted Hashish (from Cannabis Alchemy)

Ben Lemon documented a method for making hashish from extracted cannabis oil (1979). The oil is mixed with finely powdered marijuana at varying ratios to produce hashish of different consistencies and potencies:

- **15% oil minimum** for proper hashish consistency (similar to powdery Moroccan or Lebanese hash)
- **50-60% oil** maximum (resembles sticky, pliable charas of Nepal and India)
- Oil at any stage of refinement may be used; isomerized and acetylated oil produces extremely strong hashish

### Preparation of the Powder Base
Clean, very dry marijuana is pulverized in a high-speed blender. A fine dust collects on the blender lid, sifted through nylon stocking or fine mesh screen. For improved taste, the oils contributing flavor and smell are removed by alcohol extraction followed by boiling water washes until the water runs clear. The cannabis dust is thoroughly dried before mixing with oil.

### Mixing and Forming
Heat the dust and oil, then work together in a mortar or knead by hand. Traditional forms include:
- Thin, flat, hand-pressed patties (Afghanistan style)
- Clumps of "fingers" or round "temple balls" (Nepal style)
- Flat sheets and blocks (pressed between heated steel plates in a vise)

### Oil Capsules for Oral Ingestion
Mix purified oil with equal amount butter (butterfat carries oil through stomach/intestinal membranes). Blend into two volumes of marijuana, parsley, lactose, or edible inert powder. Stuff into large gelatin capsules.

### Smoking Oil Directly
- **Glass oil pipe (vapor pipe)**: Oil placed in glass bowl, heated from below. Very efficient for small amounts.
- **Foil method**: Tiny dab on aluminum foil, heated from below, smoke inhaled through tube. Foil pre-heated in gas flame to burn off impurities.
