## Lobster Mushroom Hypomyces lactifluorum

The lobster mushroom is not actually a single mushroom species but
rather the result of a parasitic fungal infection. The ascomycete
fungus Hypomyces lactifluorum colonizes the fruiting bodies of
various Russula and Lactarius species, transforming their appearance
and characteristics completely. The name "lobster mushroom" derives
from the bright orange-red coloration and the seafood-like aroma
that develops after the parasitization.

## The Parasitic Fungus Hypomyces lactifluorum

Hypomyces lactifluorum is an ascomycete that infects gilled
mushrooms, primarily those in the Russulaceae family. The fungus
belongs to the order Hypocreales and is one of the most conspicuous
mycoparasites in [[russula-brevipes]] (short-stemmed Russula) and [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]] when opportunities present themselves.
