## Overview

Research studies have demonstrated that psilocybin-containing mushrooms
thrive all over the world and can be found on all continents. These mushrooms
are no different from any other mycoflora and should not be excluded from
scientific investigation because of their alkaloid content. The global
distribution of psychoactive fungi spans tropical, subtropical, and
temperate zones, with species adapted to remarkably diverse ecological niches.

## Asia and the Pacific

Psilocybin mushrooms are found throughout Southeast Asia and the South
Pacific. Species of Panaeolus, particularly Copelandia (tropical Panaeoli
that readily bruise bluish), are common in tropical and subtropical Asian
regions. Australia's mycoflora has attracted increasing attention, with
Psilocybe cubensis and other species documented across the continent.

Japanese researchers have documented Psilocybe venenata, a bluing species
found in that country. Throughout Asia, traditional knowledge of
psychoactive mushrooms varies widely, with some cultures showing mycophilic
attitudes and others mycophobic ones.
