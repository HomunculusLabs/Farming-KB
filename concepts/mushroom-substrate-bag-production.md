# Mushroom Substrate Bag Production

## Definition

Substrate is the material used to grow mushrooms — a mixture of all ingredients or "food" necessary for [[acropetal-cell-expansion-stipe-elongation-mushroom-development]]. It provides the physical structure, moisture, and nutrients that the mushroom mycelium needs to colonize and eventually fruit. Producing high-quality substrate bags is one of the most critical steps [[contamination-prevention-in-mushroom-cultivation]], as contamination or improper nutrient balance at this stage can ruin an entire crop.

## Materials

The following materials are required for producing substrate bags:

- **Plastic bags** — Polypropylene, 7" x 12.5" (for sawdust substrate); 8" x 12" (for rice straw substrate)
- **Plastic necks** — Approximately 4 cm diameter, used to form the bag opening
- **Cotton plugs** — Placed inside the plastic caps for gas exchange and contamination filtration
- **Cover filters** — Square filter paper placed over the neck opening for additional contamination protection
- **Rubber bands** — Used to secure the bag around the plastic neck/ring
- **Sawdust** — Primary base ingredient; cover the sawdust pile with rice sacks to maintain proper humidity during storage
- **Rice bran** — Provides supplemental carbohydrates and nitrogen
- **Calcium sulfate** — Source of calcium and sulfur; improves substrate structure
- **[[peat-calcium-carbonate-perlite-casing-psilocybe-cubensis-miniculture]]** — pH buffer to prevent the substrate from becoming too acidic
- **Magnesium sulfate** — Trace mineral supplement (Epsom salt) supporting enzyme function

## Rice Straw as an Alternative Substrate

Rice straw can serve as an effective substitute for sawdust as the base substrate material, and it offers several advantages and trade-offs:

### Advantages

- **Readily available** in rural Asia due to widespread rice cultivation
- **Lower cost** than sawdust, making it accessible for low-budget operations
- **Higher yields** and **better quality mushrooms** — improved texture and taste compared to sawdust-grown mushrooms

### Disadvantages

- **Requires harder work** — more labor-intensive preparation process
- **Longer preparation time** — fermentation is required for 9–12 days before the straw is ready for bagging

### Rice Straw Preparation Process

1. **Grind** the rice straw into smaller pieces for better packing and decomposition
2. **Soak** 100 kg of ground straw with water plus 1–2% urea by weight
3. **Ferment** for 3 days
4. **Turn the pile** and add 2% lime (calcium carbonate) by weight
5. **Ferment** for another 3 days
6. **Turn the pile** again and add 0.2% magnesium sulfate by weight
7. **Ferment** for 3 more days (total fermentation: 9–12 days)
8. **Check readiness** — the substrate should have no urea smell and a moisture content of 65–75%
9. **Pack** in 8" x 12" polypropylene bags

## Sawdust Substrate Recipe

The standard sawdust-based substrate recipe is:

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sawdust | 100 kg |
| Rice bran | 5 kg |
| Calcium sulfate | 2 kg |
| Calcium carbonate | 1 kg |
| Magnesium sulfate | 0.2 kg |
| Sugar | 0–1 kg (optional) |

### Recipe Modifications

The basic recipe can be modified by substituting or adding various supplementary ingredients:

- Rice flour
- Sticky rice flour
- Corn flour
- Cassava peels
- Cotton waste
- Soybean residue
- Other agricultural waste products

### Climate-Based Additive Limits

The amount of supplementary additives should be adjusted based on the local climate:

- **Cool climates:** Up to 20% additives (by weight of the base substrate)
- **Hot climates:** Maximum 7.5% additives (by weight of the base substrate)

Higher additive content in hot climates increases the risk of contamination during fermentation and incubation, as the extra nutrients accelerate the growth of competing organisms.

## Bag Production Steps (17 Steps)

The following is the complete step-by-step process for producing substrate bags:

1. **Prepare all materials** — Gather and organize all bags, necks, cotton, filters, rubber bands, and substrate ingredients
2. **Prepare substrate** — Prepare rice straw substrate (if using) following the fermentation process, or weigh out sawdust and all dry ingredients according to the recipe
3. **Mix all ingredients** — Combine dry ingredients thoroughly using a mechanical mixer or manually with shovels and paddles; ensure even distribution of all supplements
4. **Add water** — Gradually add water while mixing until the substrate reaches 65–75% moisture content; the squeeze test (a handful should hold shape without dripping excess water) is a reliable field check
5. **Ensure uniform mixing** — Break up any lumps and verify that the substrate is homogenous throughout; no dry pockets or clumps should remain
6. **Fill bags** — Sit on the floor and fill each bag with substrate using a small shovel; work efficiently to minimize exposure time
7. **Compact substrate** — Hit each bag firmly with an empty bottle or by hand to compact the substrate (a compacting machine may be used at scale); proper compaction removes air pockets and ensures uniform density
8. **Place plastic ring** — Position the plastic ring on the open end of the filled bag
9. **Pull through neck** — Pull the top of the bag through the plastic neck, drawing it upward through the ring
10. **Fold down** — Fold the excess bag material down over the outside of the plastic ring
11. **Tie with rubber band** — Secure the folded bag tightly around the ring using a rubber band
12. **Create center hole** — Insert a stick with a pointed head through the plastic neck to make a hole reaching almost to the bottom of the bag; **DO NOT TOUCH THE BOTTOM** — the hole must stop short of the bag base to prevent contamination ingress
13. **Check weight** — Weigh each finished bag; the target weight is 800–1000 grams per bag; adjust filling amounts accordingly
14. **Prepare plastic caps** — Assemble the plastic caps with cotton plugs and cover filters; ensure cotton is packed firmly enough to filter contaminants but allows gas exchange
15. **Close bags** — Place the prepared plastic caps onto each bag's plastic neck to seal them
16. **Load racks** — Fill iron racks with the completed bags; for a commercial pasteurization chamber, load 9 bags per rack
17. **Transfer to pasteurization** — Move the loaded racks into the pasteurization chamber for sterilization; this step kills competing organisms and prepares the substrate for inoculation with mushroom spawn

## Key Quality Control Points

- **Moisture content** is the single most important variable — consistently target 65–75% throughout the entire batch
- **Uniform compaction** prevents air pockets that can harbor contamination or cause uneven mycelium growth
- **Clean working area** — minimize dust and airborne contaminants during the bagging process
- **Batch consistency** — weigh ingredients accurately and mix thoroughly to ensure every bag has the same [[dighton-fungal-nutritional-composition-animal-diets]]
- **Center hole depth** — must reach nearly to the bottom but never puncture it, as this is the primary channel for spawn inoculation and mycelium spread
- **Timing** — complete the bagging process as quickly as possible once substrate is hydrated to reduce the window for contamination
- **Visual inspection** — check every bag for tears, holes, or improperly sealed necks before loading into racks; reject any defective bags

## Pasteurization and Next Steps

After the bags are loaded onto racks and transferred to the pasteurization chamber, they undergo sterilization to eliminate competing microorganisms. Once pasteurization is complete and the bags have cooled to room temperature, they are ready for inoculation with mushroom spawn. Proper handling during and after pasteurization is essential — any lapse in hygiene at this stage can undo all the careful work of [[bulk-substrate-preparation]].

## Safety Notes

- Work in a well-ventilated area when handling fine sawdust to avoid respiratory irritation
- Wear gloves when mixing additives, especially lime and mineral supplements
- Ensure pasteurization chamber is properly vented before opening to release steam safely
