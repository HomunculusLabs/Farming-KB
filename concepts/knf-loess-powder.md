# Loess Powder Preparation and Use

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Loess powder is a [[natural-farming-knf-overview]] input derived from loess (a fine-grained, wind-deposited soil composed primarily of silt-sized particles). Loess has been used for centuries in East Asian agriculture and traditional medicine for its unique mineral composition and natural healing properties. In KNF, loess powder serves as a disease preventive when mixed with seed treatment solutions and as a general soil vitality enhancer.

## What Is Loess

Loess is a type of unconsolidated sediment formed by the accumulation of wind-blown silt over geological time periods. It is characterized by:

- Extremely fine particle size (predominantly silt, 0.002-0.05 mm)
- High mineral content including silica, calcium, iron, magnesium, potassium, and numerous trace elements
- Porous structure that retains both water and air
- Natural microbial communities adapted to the mineral-rich environment
- Yellow to brownish-yellow color (the name comes from German "los" meaning loose)

In Korean farming tradition, loess has been valued as "yellow gold" for its ability to heal soil and support plant health.

## Why Use Loess Powder

The rationale for using loess powder in natural farming is based on several properties:

1. **Soil vitality foundation:** Loess powder contains a broad spectrum of the trace elements and minerals that serve as the foundation for all biological activity. It is essentially concentrated earth vitality.
2. **Uncontaminated mineral source:** Unlike agricultural limestone or other mined amendments, loess in its natural state is free from chemical contamination and industrial processing.
3. **Growth factors:** Loess contains certain growth-promoting substances that have not yet been fully identified or explained by scientific analysis. Natural farming embraces these empirically observed benefits even when the exact mechanisms are not fully understood.
4. **Natural healing power:** Loess possesses documented healing properties in traditional medicine and agriculture. In farming, it helps plants recover from stress and disease.

## Selecting Quality Loess

Not all loess is equal. The selection process is critical:

### The Water Test

The primary method for selecting loess is the water dispersion test:

1. Take a sample of loess and place it in a container of water
2. Observe how the loess behaves when submerged

**Two types of loess are found:**

- **Type A -- Submerges in a lump:** The loess clumps together and sinks as a solid mass. This type has poor dispersion and fewer bioavailable minerals. It is NOT suitable for making loess powder.

- **Type B -- Scatters in all directions:** The loess disperses and spreads throughout the water, creating a cloudy suspension. The particles separate and float freely. This is the desirable type for loess powder production. It indicates high mineral bioavailability and fine particle structure.

Always choose Type B loess that disperses readily in water.

## Preparation Method

### Materials

- Quality loess (Type B, as determined by the water test)
- Clean water
- Two containers (large and small)
- A cool, shaded area for drying

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Weigh the loess:** Place 2-3 kg of selected loess into a container
2. **Add water:** Pour 20 liters of clean water over the loess
3. **Stir thoroughly:** Stir the mixture vigorously to fully disperse the loess particles in the water. The water will become cloudy and yellowish.
4. **Allow settling:** Let the mixture stand undisturbed. Over time (30 minutes to several hours), a separation will occur:
   - Thick, heavy particles will sink to the bottom
   - Fine, light particles will remain suspended in the upper portion of the water
5. **Collect the fine particles:** Carefully pour off or scoop out the upper layer containing the fine suspended particles into a separate container. Avoid disturbing the settled heavy particles at the bottom.
6. **Allow precipitation:** Set the collected suspension aside. As time passes (several hours to overnight), the fine particles will gradually precipitate to the bottom of the second container.
7. **Drain surface water:** Once precipitation is complete, carefully drain off the clear surface water (called "Jijangsu" -- loess-filtered water) into another container. This filtered water itself has uses as a mineral supplement.
8. **Dry the loess:** Spread the precipitated fine loess in a thin layer and dry it in a cool, shaded place.

### Critical Drying Note

- **Do NOT dry loess in direct sunlight.** Sun-dried loess undergoes physical changes that prevent it from dispersing properly when added to water later. The particles become "sun-baked" and will not re-suspend.
- **Cool, shaded drying** preserves the dispersal properties of the fine loess particles
- Drying may take several days depending on temperature and humidity
- The dried loess should be a fine, powdery consistency

### The Byproduct: Jijangsu (Loess-Filtered Water)

The clear water drained from the precipitated loess (Jijangsu) is itself a valuable product:

- Contains dissolved trace minerals extracted from the loess
- Can be used as a mild mineral supplement for plants
- Can be added to livestock drinking water
- Use as a base water when preparing other KNF input dilutions

## How to Use Loess Powder

### Seed Treatment Additive

The primary use of loess powder in KNF is as an additive to the seed treatment solution:

1. **Preparation:** Add 20-30 grams of dried loess powder to 20 liters of water
2. **Mix with seed treatment liquid:** Combine the loess powder suspension with the standard [[knf-seed-treatment-and-soil-foundation]] (1:1000 dilution)
3. **Use:** Soak seeds in this combined solution following the standard seed treatment timing guidelines

The loess powder in the seed treatment solution provides several benefits:
- Minerals from the loess are absorbed by the seed coat, providing initial nutrition for the germinating seed
- Fine loess particles coat the seed, creating a physical barrier against soil-borne pathogens
- The mineral coating may stimulate early root growth and disease resistance

### Disease Prevention

- Mix loess powder with seedling treatment liquid for preventing diseases such as canker in fruit trees
- The mineral coating on treated seedlings creates an unfavorable environment for pathogenic organisms
- Can be combined with [[knf-oriental-herbal-nutrient]] for enhanced disease prevention

### General Soil Amendment

- Loess powder can be dusted lightly onto the soil surface around plants
- Can be mixed into [[knf-fermented-mixed-compost]] (FMC) as a mineral supplement
- Apply as a foliar dust in very small quantities (mixed with water as a suspension)

## Storage

- Store dried loess powder in a sealed container in a cool, dry location
- Protect from moisture to prevent clumping
- Properly dried and stored loess powder has an essentially unlimited shelf life
- Keep the Jijangsu (filtered water) in a cool, dark place and use within a few weeks
