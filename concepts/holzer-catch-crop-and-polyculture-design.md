## Slope Stabilisation Polycultures

On steep slopes and embankments, Holzer makes sure that there is a large
number of very deep-rooted plants like lupins and sweet clover in his seed
mixtures. These plants not only improve the soil but they also stabilise the
slope with their deep root systems. Their strong growth and well-established
roots also improve the capacity of the soil to retain water.

A mixture of sweet clover, lucerne, vetch, peas, lupins, sunflowers, and
different tubers like Jerusalem artichokes and turnips is well suited to
slope stabilisation. In Holzer's polycultures, the soil and plants are
"working" to a depth of metres and not centimetres.

## Leaving Green Manure Crops Standing

In autumn, Holzer usually leaves green manure crops standing. Nature does
all the work: the first heavy snow pushes down the plants and they begin to
decompose. In his opinion, this is the best and most successful way to
improve the soil. The plants rot down slowly and the biomass does not compact
so easily, which would usually be the case with material that is cut down.

Leaving green manure crops standing means saving the work of cutting the
plants down. The plants can ripen, bloom, and produce seeds. Birds eat many
of these seeds, stratify them in their stomachs, and distribute them in other
areas. This natural seed dispersal eliminates the need for constant
resowing, connecting to [[jerusalem-artichoke]]
