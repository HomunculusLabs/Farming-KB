# Mycetozoans — Slime Molds and Their Relatives

Mycetozoans (slime molds and their relatives) are a diverse assemblage of protists that share amoeboid trophic stages and fruiting body formation. They include the Myxomycetes (plasmodial slime molds), Dictyostelia (cellular slime molds), and Protostelia, classified together in the class Eumycetozoa.

## Classification and Phylogeny

### Eumycetozoa
Class containing three subclasses:
- **Protostelia:** 37 described species in 17 genera; >50 undescribed species observed
- **Dictyostelia (cellular slime molds):** Social amoebae that aggregate to form fruiting structures
- **Myxogastria (plasmodial slime molds):** Form large, multinucleate plasmodia

Molecular evidence from nuclear small subunit rRNA and elongation factor 1-alpha genes supports the monophyly of this group. Protostelia appear paraphyletic, with one lineage including the myxomycetes.

## Substrata and Microhabitats

### Occurrence Patterns

| Group | Dead plant parts | Litter | Soil | Bark | Dung | Rotting wood |
|-------|-----------------|--------|------|------|------|-------------|
| Protostelids | +++ | +++ | + | +++ | + | ++ |
| Myxomycetes | ++ | +++ | +++ | ++ | ++ | +++ |
| Dictyostelids | * | ++ | +++ | * | +++ | * |

(ADPP = Attached dead plant parts; * = very rare; + to +++ = occasional to abundant)

### Key Microhabitat Differences
- In temperate regions, many more myxomycetes in litter than on dead aerial plant parts
- Different assemblages colonize litter vs. dead aerial plant parts (microhabitat matters more than habitat type)
- In tropical rainforests, the distinction between litter and aerial microhabitats is less pronounced
- Litter assemblages from grasslands similar to those from forests
- "Canopy soil" at bases of vascular epiphytes supports both dictyostelids and myxomycetes

## Sampling Methods

### Moist Chamber Technique
The primary method for detecting mycetozoans:
1. Petri dishes fitted with sterile filter paper or paper toweling
2. Substrata (bark, litter, wood) placed in single nonoverlapping layer
3. 10-15 ml sterile distilled water added
4. Soak 10-12 hours, then remove excess water
5. Observe after ~24 hours; repeat daily for 7-10 days
6. Allow to dry completely, then rewet to stimulate further fruiting

### Cavender Method (for Soil)
Standard method for dictyostelids:
1. Collect soil from multiple sites
2. Dilute and suspend set mass of soil in set volume of sterile water
3. Spread on weak nutrient agar overlain with E. coli suspension
4. Incubate at ambient temperature for 3-4 days
5. Individual clones form plaques in bacterial lawn
6. Subculture on 1.5% nonnutrient agar with E. coli

### Modification for Myxomycetes
Feest's modification uses Saccharomyces cerevisiae instead of E. coli. Plasmodia appear after several days. The yeast prevents plates from being overrun by filamentous fungi while allowing food bacteria to grow.

### Canopy Soil
Material collecting around bases of vascular epiphytes on tree trunks and branches in rainforests. This decaying organic matter microhabitat supports diverse mycetozoan communities.

## Geographic Distribution

### Protostelids
- Probably ubiquitous; the great majority of species appear cosmopolitan
- Collected from subarctic North America, temperate and desert areas, Caribbean, Brazil, Melanesia, Micronesia, Polynesia, Japan, southeastern Asia, England, Africa
- All or most known species expected within a single 50,000-hectare tropical or temperate forest plot

### Myxomycetes
- Similar cosmopolitan distribution pattern
- Greatest diversity expected in tropical forests
- Most species from Hawaii have been found elsewhere

## Identification

Most mycetozoans can be identified from fruiting body morphology alone, but culturing is often useful for confirmation. The trophic cells of protostelids and dictyostelids are microscopic and usually not distinguishable in mixed samples.

## Ecological Roles

- Bacterial predators in soil and litter ecosystems
- Contribute to nutrient cycling
- Food source for soil microarthropods
- Some species produce compounds of pharmacological interest
- Myxomycete plasmodia occasionally feed on wood-rotting basidiomycetes

## Culture Collections
- University of Arkansas Mycetozoan Culture Collection (~25 species)
- American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) — a few species
- Cultures stored as embedded material for electron microscopy
