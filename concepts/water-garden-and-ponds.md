# Water Gardens and Ponds
Water gardens and ponds introduce aquatic ecosystems into the landscape, supporting wildlife, creating tranquil focal points, and enabling the cultivation of aquatic plants and fish. They integrate [[water-management]] with [[fungal-ecology|ecology]] and [[permaculture-design|design]] principles.
## Pond Construction
### Planning
- **Location**: Minimum 6 hours of sunlight for water lilies and most aquatic plants; partial shade reduces algae
- **Size**: Larger ponds are more stable (fewer temperature swings, easier biological balance); minimum 50 square feet for ecological balance, ideally 100+ square feet
- **Depth**: Shelves for marginal plants (6-12 inches), deeper zone for fish and overwintering (24-36 inches minimum; 48+ inches in cold climates to prevent freezing to the bottom)
- **Utilities**: Plan for electrical access (pumps, lighting, aeration) and water supply
- **Zoning**: Avoid placing under trees (leaf debris causes water quality issues) or in low spots that receive runoff (fertilizers, pesticides)
### Construction Methods
**Pre-formed Liner Ponds**
- Rigid plastic or fiberglass shells
- Easy to install, defined shape
- Limited sizes and shapes available
- Less forgiving of uneven ground — must be perfectly leveled
- Best for small, beginner ponds (50-200 gallons)
**Flexible Liner Ponds**
- EPDM rubber or PVC liner
- Any shape and size
- More durable (EPDM lasts 20+ years)
- Requires careful installation to prevent punctures
- Best for medium to large ponds and custom designs
**Concrete Ponds**
- Permanent, durable, customizable
- Highest cost and labor
- Requires professional installation for structural integrity
- Risk of cracking from ground movement or freeze-thaw cycles
- Best for koi ponds and large formal water features
### Installation Steps (Flexible Liner)
1. Mark pond outline with hose or rope
2. Excavate to planned depth with shelves for marginal plants
3. Remove all sharp objects (rocks, roots) from the excavation
4. Add 2 inches of sand or underlayment fabric for cushioning
5. Drape liner over the hole, allowing 12-18 inches overlap on all sides
6. Fill slowly with water while smoothing wrinkles
7. Secure liner edges with stone or bury in soil
8. Install pump, filter, and any water features
9. Add plants and allow system to stabilize 2-4 weeks before adding fish
## Aquatic Plants
### Planting Zones
**Submerged Plants (Oxygenators)**
- Grow entirely underwater, release oxygen, absorb nutrients (reduce algae)
- Species: Hornwort (Ceratophyllum demersum), anacharis (Egeria densa), water milfoil, cabomba
- Plant in pots on the pond bottom; 1 bunch per 2 square feet of surface area
- Often free-floating or loosely anchored
**Floating Plants**
- Roots hang in water, leaves float on surface; shade water and reduce algae
- Species: Water hyacinth (invasive — contain in small ponds), water lettuce, duckweed (can be aggressive), frogbit
- Add sparingly — they multiply rapidly
- Remove excess regularly to prevent complete surface coverage
**Surface Plants (Water Lilies and Lotuses)**
- Rooted in bottom soil, leaves and flowers float on surface
- **Hardy water lilies**: Survive winter in most zones, bloom during the day
- **Tropical water lilies**: More flowers, night-blooming varieties available, not winter-hardy
- **Lotus**: Dramatic, large leaves and flowers; needs large containers and full sun
- Plant in wide, shallow pots; place at appropriate depth on pond shelf
**Marginal Plants (Bog Plants)**
- Grow in shallow water at pond edges (0-6 inches of water over the crown)
- Species: Iris (Iris versicolor, I. pseudacorus), pickerel weed, cattail, arrowhead, cardinal flower, marsh marigold, sweet flag
- Plant in pots placed on the marginal shelf
- Soften the transition between water and land
### Planting Tips
- Use heavy loam or aquatic planting soil (no regular potting mix — it floats)
- Top pots with 1 inch of gravel to prevent fish from digging up plants
- Fertilize aquatic plants with aquatic plant tablets pushed into soil (not granular — dissolves in water column and feeds algae)
- Divide overgrown plants every 2-3 years
## Fish
### Species Selection
**Goldfish**
- Hardy, long-lived (10-20+ years), easy to care for
- Comets, shubunkins, and fantails are popular varieties
- Allow 20-50 gallons per fish minimum
- Omnivorous — will eat plants, insects, and algae
- Survive winter in ponds 24+ inches deep in most climates
**Koi**
- Larger (24-36+ inches), longer-lived (25-50+ years)
- Require larger ponds (500+ gallons minimum, ideally 1000+)
- More expensive and require better filtration
- Can be trained to hand-feed
- Destructive to plants — protect plant roots with large stones
**Mosquitofish (Gambusia)**
- Small, hardy, voracious mosquito larvae eaters
- Add to any pond for mosquito control
- Reproduce rapidly — population self-regulates
### Fish Care
- Introduce fish gradually (float bag in pond for 30 minutes to acclimate temperature)
- Feed lightly — fish can survive on pond organisms alone; overfeeding causes water quality problems
- Provide shade and hiding spots (plants, rocks)
- Monitor for herons, raccoons, and other predators — netting or motion-activated deterrents help
- Do not add fish until pond has cycled (2-4 weeks after planting)
## Filtration
### Biological Filtration
- Beneficial bacteria convert toxic ammonia (from fish waste) to nitrite, then to relatively harmless nitrate
- Biofilter media: lava rock, bio-balls, ceramic rings, foam pads
- Essential for any pond with fish
- Bacteria colonize surfaces — never clean all filter media at once
### Mechanical Filtration
- Removes physical debris (leaves, algae, fish waste)
- Skimmers at the surface catch floating debris before it sinks
- Filter pads or screens trap particles
- Clean mechanical filters regularly (weekly in summer)
### Natural Filtration
- **Plant filters**: Bog areas or separate bog filters where water flows through gravel-planted zones
- **Constructed wetlands**: The most effective natural filtration — water pumped through a gravel wetland bed planted with marginals
- **Beneficial bacteria**: Add bacterial starter cultures; maintain surface area for colonization
### UV Clarifiers
- Kill suspended algae (green water) by exposing it to ultraviolet light
- Do not harm beneficial bacteria
- Effective but not necessary if biological balance is maintained
## Wildlife Habitat
A well-designed pond becomes a magnet for wildlife:
- **Amphibians**: Frogs, toads, and salamanders will colonize naturally (no stocking needed)
- **Dragonflies**: Lay eggs in water; larvae are voracious mosquito predators
- **Birds**: Provide a sloped beach area for drinking and bathing
- **Insects**: Water striders, whirligig beetles, and other aquatic insects add to biodiversity
- **Design for wildlife**: Include shallow edges, varied depths, native marginal plants, and hiding spots (rocks, logs)
## Maintenance
- **Spring**: Clean out debris, divide plants, restart pump, check liner
- **Summer**: Monitor water level, top off as needed, control algae, feed fish lightly
- **Fall**: Remove fallen leaves with net, trim dead foliage, prepare for winter
- **Winter**: Keep a hole in ice for gas exchange (use a floating de-icer), stop feeding fish below 50F, leave hardy plants in place
