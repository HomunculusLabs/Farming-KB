# PF TEK Substrate Formula and Preparation

The PF TEK (Psylocybe Fanaticus Technique) is a simplified mushroom
[[miniculture-rye-grain-cultivation-method-for-psilocybe-cubensis]] using brown rice powder and vermiculite as substrate
in half-pint canning jars. Introduced by Psylocybe Fanaticus in 1991-1992,
it became the most widely copied amateur cultivation technique worldwide.
The key innovation is using vermiculite to create air space within the
substrate, mimicking natural growing conditions and avoiding the density
problems of grain-based substrates.

## Standard Substrate Formula (per half-pint jar)

- **1/8 cup brown rice powder** (health food stores, co-ops)
- **1/2 to 2/3 cup horticultural vermiculite** (medium grade, garden
  centers)
- **40-45 cc water** (~3 tablespoons + 1 teaspoon)

### Maximum Fruiting Formula
- 1/4 cup brown rice powder
- 1/2 cup vermiculite
- 60 cc water

The maximum fruiting formula holds more nutrients but requires careful
moisture management. Use a loose jar lid during incubation to prevent
water collection at the jar bottom.

## Jar Requirements

Half-pint (250 ml / 8 oz) jars are essential — larger jars are unreliable
and fail easily. Jars must have **tapered sides with no shoulder** so the
colonized cake can slide out. Acceptable containers:

- KERR wide mouth half-pint canning jar (most versatile)
- BALL regular mouth half-pint canning jar
- BALL half-pint jelly jar
- 1/2 pint tapered drinking glasses (use tin foil cover)

Note: KERR regular mouth half-pints are NOT tapered despite similar
appearance to BALL regular mouth half-pints.

## Vermiculite Selection

Not all vermiculite is equivalent — coarseness varies significantly
between brands:

- **Fine vermiculite** (particles ~1 mm): Recommended. Holds more water,
  critical for consistent results.
- **Coarse vermiculite** (particles 4-8 mm): Holds less water; requires
  adjusted formulas.

The finer type provides superior moisture retention. Examine particles
under a photo magnifier against a millimeter ruler to verify grade.

## Brown Rice Powder

- Freshly ground brown rice from a small canister coffee grinder is
  preferred over pre-packaged powder
- Freshness significantly impacts [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]] quality
- Grind to a fine, consistent powder — coarse particles reduce nutrient
  availability

## Substrate Mixing Procedure

### Step 1: Mix
1. Place 1/2 cup vermiculite into a mixing bowl
2. Add brown rice powder on top of the vermiculite
3. Slowly pour water directly onto the rice powder to wet it first
4. Thoroughly mix all ingredients until the mixture feels damp and
   cohesive
5. Mix each jar's substrate individually for accurate formula rendering

### Step 2: Load Jars
1. Fill the jar very loosely — do not pack or compress
2. Leave 1/2 to 3/4 inch of space at the top
3. Level the substrate surface
4. Wipe the inside jar walls clean with a tissue or fingertip
5. Fill the remaining space with **plain [[pf-tek-dry-vermiculite-contaminant-barrier]]** and level at
   the top

**The dry vermiculite layer** is the critical PF innovation. This
contaminant barrier protects the wet substrate from airborne spores and
bacteria. It also absorbs and regulates moisture transpiration and
condensation within the jar.

### Step 3: Lid Preparation
1. Prepare canning lids by punching 4 holes inside the rubber sealing
   edge periphery using a sharpened 3-penny nail and vise grips
2. Place lid with rubberized edge UP (needle hole jagged edges down)
3. Screw the lid band on loosely for now
4. Cover needle holes with professional-grade masking tape

### Step 4: Steam Sterilization
1. Use a 3-piece vegetable steamer (pot, basket insert, lid)
2. Keep jar bottoms off the pot bottom to prevent cracking
3. Heat water to a boil, place jars in the pot
4. Keep lid bands loose during steaming for steam penetration
5. Jars may sit in water but boiling water must not slosh in
6. Reduce heat to the **lowest possible gentle boil**
7. Steam for **1 hour** in a tightly covered pot
8. Gas stoves offer the easiest temperature control

**Critical**: Do not overheat — drying is evidenced by [[growing-gourmet-spore-germination-strain-isolation]]
followed by halted growth. If growth stops without contamination, the
substrate is too dry. Remedy: increase water content in future batches.

### Step 5: Cool and Store
After steaming, allow jars to cool completely. Tighten lids and store
in a cool, draft-free place until ready for inoculation. Jars must be
cool to the touch before inoculation — hot jars will kill spores.

## Contamination Control

Run **control jars**: After sterilization, leave a few jars uninoculated
for several days. Watch for colored growths or the telltale rancid odor
of bacteria. If contamination appears at this stage, lengthen the
[[pressure-cooker-sterilization-time-temperature-jar-size-mushroom-substrate]]. If contamination occurs after inoculation, the
syringe was contaminated or the dry vermiculite barrier was breached.
