# Species-Specific Flush Patterns in Psilocybe and Panaeolus Cultivation

- **Flush pattern**: More constant and distributed across multiple flushes
- **Flush count**: 2-4 productive flushes per casing (when grown for mushrooms)
- **Sclerotia production**: Continues in jars for up to 4 months after inoculation without casing
- **Dual production**: Sclerotia also form in the casing layer of cased trays, providing a secondary harvest

The more distributed flush pattern suggests that *P. tampanensis* has a different reproductive strategy, possibly adapted to more stable or prolonged favorable conditions in its natural habitat. - **Strain variation**: Some strains (e.g., Ecuador) fruit more readily and predictably than others

The guide notes that proper watering after each flush is critical for sustaining multiple flushes, as maturing mushrooms pull significant moisture from the casing soil. The Ecuador strain is specifically mentioned as an "easy fruiting strain" that does not require cold-shocking to initiate fruiting, making it particularly suitable for cultivators seeking reliable multi-flush production.

### Panaeolus subbalteatus

*Panaeolus subbalteatus* occupies an intermediate position in flush behavior:

- **Flush pattern**: More constant production across multiple flushes (similar to *P. cubensis*)
- **Flush count**: Multiple productive flushes with moderate yield per flush
- **[[species-specific-cold-shock-response-psilocybe]]**: Does not benefit from cold-shock treatment, unlike some *Psilocybe* strains
- **Casing requirement**: Responds well to the standard grass seed casing protocol

## Cold Shock and Flush Initiation

The guide differentiates species by their response to cold shock — placing casings in a refrigerator overnight before introducing them to the fruiting environment:

- **Cold shock beneficial**: Harder-to-fruit strains (some *Psilocybe* strains benefit)
- **Cold shock unnecessary**: *P. cubensis* Ecuador strain (fruits readily without treatment)
- **Cold shock not beneficial**: *P. mexicana*, *P. tampanensis*, *P. subbalteatus* (do not benefit from this treatment)

This differential response reflects the diverse ecological origins of these species. Tropical and subtropical species may not have evolved cold-triggered fruiting mechanisms, while temperate species may use temperature drops as a cue for seasonal fruiting.

## Total Yield Optimization Across Flushes

The guide recommends harvesting 3 flushes as a standard practice, but notes that properly watered casings may produce 5 or even 6 flushes. Yield optimization strategies include:

- **Consistent moisture management** — The casing must never be allowed to dry out between flushes
- **Post-flush watering increase** — Directly after picking a flush, increase watering to compensate for moisture lost to mushroom metabolism
- **Abort removal** — Dead pinheads (aborts) should be removed promptly from the casing surface to prevent mold colonization
- **Surface hygiene** — Keep the casing surface as clean as possible throughout the flush cycle
