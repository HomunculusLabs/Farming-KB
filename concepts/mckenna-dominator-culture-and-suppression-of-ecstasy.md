## The Indo-European Invasions and Cultural Overthrow

The Kurgan culture of the Eurasian steppes, identified by Gimbutas as the 
source of the Indo-European migrations, brought a fundamentally different 
social organization. Warrior graves containing weapons, horses, and sacrificed 
women testify to a culture centered on conquest, male dominance, and the 
worship of sky gods. Beginning around 4500 BCE, successive waves of Kurgan 
expansion overwhelmed the partnership cultures of Old Europe, replacing goddess 
worship with patriarchal pantheons, communal ritual with priestly hierarchy, 
and ecstatic religious practice with obedience to authority.

McKenna connected the suppression of psychoactive plant use specifically to 
this cultural overthrow. The ecstatic mysteries of the partnership cultures, 
which used psychoactive plants to facilitate direct experience of the sacred, 
were incompatible with a social order based on hierarchy and obedience. If 
anyone could directly encounter the divine through plant hallucinogens, the 
authority of priests and kings would be undermined. The suppression of 
entheogenic plants was therefore a political necessity for the dominator model.

## Alcohol as the Dominator Drug

McKenna argued that alcohol replaced psychoactive plants as the dominant 
intoxicant precisely because it reinforces dominator social patterns. Unlike 
hallucinogens, which dissolve ego boundaries and promote egalitarian bonding, 
alcohol encourages aggression, hierarchical posturing, and the reinforcement of 
existing power structures. The transition from hallucinogen-based religions to 
alcohol-based social rituals marked a fundamental shift in the relationship 
between consciousness and culture.

The historical record supports this analysis. The Indo-European invasions that 
overthrew goddess-worshipping partnership societies brought with them a culture 
centered on cattle raiding, warfare, and the ritual consumption of alcoholic 
beverages. The ecstatic mysteries of earlier periods, which had used 
psychoactive plants to facilitate direct experience of the sacred, were 
replaced by priestly hierarchies that mediated between the individual and the 
divine. Alcohol sustains the dominator order by reinforcing ego, aggression, 
and conformity rather than dissolving them.
