# Persimmon

The persimmon (Diospyros kaki), also known as Chinese persimmon and
kakee, is a deciduous, round-headed tree growing to about 5 m in the
family Ebenaceae. It has strikingly coloured autumn foliage, making it
an attractive ornamental tree as well as a fruit producer.

## Description

The fruit is orange in colour, slightly larger than a tomato, and has to
be fully ripe before being eaten. The unripe fruit is very astringent
and contains tannic acid. It can be eaten either fresh or dried, or can
be made into jams or jellies. There are different cultivars available:
some are astringent varieties, others are non-astringent. Persimmons
begin to bear fruit when about 3 years old (grafted trees or trees grown
from cuttings).

## Climate and Soil Requirements

Persimmons prefer a medium loam soil, but will grow well in most ordinary
soils as long as they are kept moist. They do not thrive in dry or acid
soils, but will withstand a certain amount of waterlogging. The mature
plant is frost hardy to -10 degrees C.

Persimmons need plenty of water during the growing season and benefit
from mulching, as this helps to keep the roots moist. Minimum spacing
between plants is 5 m.

## Pollination

They are dioecious, requiring male and female plants, although in some
cases male and female flowers are found on the same tree. The female
flowers will produce fruit if not pollinated, but these will be seedless
and will take longer to ripen. Under some conditions, fruiting will be
biennial.

## Propagation

They are propagated from seed, by grafting, or from cuttings. Seedling
trees grow tall and make good shade trees. Seedlings tend to be variable
and often do not grow true to type. Grafted trees are preferred for
reliable fruit production.

## Related Species

### Diospyros virginiana (American Persimmon)

The fruits are smaller than the Chinese persimmon, usually yellow but
can also be dark red or purple-red. Nutritional composition per 100
grams: Water 64.4%, Energy 127 calories, Carbohydrate 33.5 g, Protein
0.8 g, Calcium 27 mg, Potassium 310 mg, Iron 2.5 mg, Ascorbic acid
66 mg.

### Diospyros lotus (Date Plum)

Fruits are smaller still (1-2 cm), purple-black when ripe. Another
useful species for permaculture systems in suitable climates.

### Diospyros dignya (Black Sapote, Chocolate Pudding Fruit)

An evergreen native of Central America, having a very sweet fruit with
chocolate brown flesh. This species extends the range of edible
Diospyros into tropical permaculture systems.

## Cultivation Tips

- Plant in full sun for best fruit production
- Mulch well to retain soil moisture
- Water regularly during the growing season
- Space trees at least 5 m apart
- Protect young trees from frost
- Non-astringent varieties are easier for fresh eating
- Astringent varieties need to be fully soft before eating
- Fruit can be dried for long-term storage
- Makes an excellent dual-purpose ornamental and food tree

## Uses and Processing

Fresh persimmons can be eaten when fully ripe and soft. The astringent
varieties must be allowed to ripen completely until the flesh has a
jelly-like consistency. Non-astringent varieties can be eaten while still
firm, like an apple. Persimmons can be dried, made into jams, jellies,
and preserves, or used in baking.

The fruit stores well and can be dried for long-term preservation.
Dried persimmons have a concentrated sweet flavour and were traditionally
valued in Asian cultures as a winter food. The fruit can also be frozen
successfully for later use.
