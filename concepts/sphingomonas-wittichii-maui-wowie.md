## Sphingomonas wittichii Enrichment in Maui Wowie Cannabis Endorhiza

## Overview

Sphingomonas wittichii was identified as one of the most significant OTUs
differentiating Cannabis cultivars in the Winston et al. (2014) microbiome
study. This bacterium was found to be prevalent in the endorhiza of the
Maui Wowie cultivar, a Cannabis sativa strain associated with a high THC to
CBD ratio. The species-level identification of Sphingomonas wittichii as a
cultivar-specific marker provides a second example, alongside Methylophilus
in Bookoo Kush, of how Cannabis genotypes selectively recruit distinct
bacterial taxa to their root interiors.

## Species Identification and Metabolic Capabilities

Sphingomonas wittichii is a species within the Sphingomonadaceae family
and the Alphaproteobacteria class. The study notes that S. wittichii can
metabolize phenazine-1-carboxylic acid in some contexts and has been
implicated in increased survival in soil environments. Phenazine-1-carboxylic
acid is a bacterial [[medicinal-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties]]
produced by Pseudomonas and other genera. The ability to metabolize this
compound could give S. wittichii a competitive advantage in root
environments where phenazine-producing bacteria are present.
