## Sequestrate Fungi — Ecology, Diversity, and Sampling ## Overview Sequestrate fungi represent a diverse guild of fungi that produce enclosed, hypogeous (underground) or semi-hypogeous fruiting bodies. These fungi do not actively discharge their spores; instead, they rely entirely on animal vectors for spore dispersal — a strategy known as mycophagy. Approximately **150 genera** and **~1,200 species** have been described across **38 families** and **11 orders**, spanning the [[biodiversity-fungal-biodiversity-estimation-methods]] knowledge. These organisms are central to understanding fungal-earthworm-interactions in forest ecosystems.

## Ecology and Symbiosis

The overwhelming majority of sequestrate fungi are **ectomycorrhizal**, forming essential mutualistic partnerships with forest trees. This association links them directly to the productivity and health of forest ecosystems, particularly in temperate and subtropical regions.

A remarkable ecological observation is that **no sequestrate fungi are known to be toxic**. This almost certainly reflects the evolutionary pressure of mycophagous dispersal — toxic species would be selected against because their spores would never be dispersed. Instead, many sequestrate fungi produce **aromatic compounds** that function as attractants for dispersal agents, including mammalian pheromones.

## Animal Dispersal

Sequestrate fungi are dispersed by a wide range of animals:

- **Mammals** — rodents (e.g., flying squirrels), marsupials, ungulates
- **Birds** — ground-foraging species
- **Arthropods** — beetles and other insects
- **Gastropods** — snails and slugs

The reliance on animal dispersal creates critical food-web linkages. A classic example from the Pacific Northwest involves the chain: **trees → sequestrate fungi → flying squirrel → spotted owl**, demonstrating how sequestrate fungi support higher trophic levels.

## Evolution

Sequestrate fungi have evolved independently from epigeous (above-ground) ancestors multiple times. A well-documented evolutionary progression exists within the Boletales:

> **Suillus** → **Gastrosuillus** → **Truncocolumella** → **Rhizopogon** → **Alpova / Melanogaster**
