# Archaic Revival Philosophy

The "Archaic Revival" is Terence McKenna's overarching philosophical framework, developed in Food of the Gods and elaborated throughout his later work. It proposes that Western civilization is undergoing a spontaneous, necessary return to values and practices associated with prehistoric, pre-patriarchal, partnership-based societies, and that this revival is being catalyzed primarily by the rediscovery of psychedelic plants and the experiential knowledge they provide.

## Partnership vs. Dominator Model

McKenna's historical analysis divides human social organization into two fundamental patterns:

**Partnership societies** (Archaic/Gaian):
- Egalitarian, matrifocal or at least gender-balanced
- Centered on the Great Mother Goddess
- Ecstatic religious practices using psychoactive plants
- Embedded in nature, cooperative rather than competitive
- Values: community, harmony, boundary dissolution, shamanic ecstasy
- Examples: Minoan Crete, Catal Huyuk, prehistoric Amazonian societies, pre-Indo-European Old European cultures

**Dominator societies** (Patriarchal/Kurgan):
- Hierarchical, patrilineal, male-dominated
- Sky-god religion emphasizing law, obedience, and distance from the divine
- Alcohol as the primary intoxicant (socially sanctioned) vs. psychedelics as threatening/taboo
- Nature as a resource to be exploited
- Values: conquest, control, boundary maintenance, private property
- Examples: Indo-European pastoralists, all subsequent Western civilizations, Christianity and Islam

## The Great Transition

McKenna identifies several historical moments when [[mckenna-dominator-vs-partnership-society-model]] was displaced by dominator society:

- **Catal Huyuk** (c. 6500 BCE): Destroyed by fire, marking "the end of the age of undefended cities, the age of partnership"
- **Minoan Crete** (c. 1450 BCE): Destroyed by the Theran eruption and Mycenaean invasion
- **The Indo-European migrations**: Brought horse, chariot, male dominance, and "a social economy based on rape and plunder"
- **The rise of agriculture**: Women's discovery of agriculture led to sedentary lifestyles and the beginning of humanity's "retreat from the natural world"

## The Abandonment of the Mystery

Central to the Archaic Revival thesis is McKenna's argument that the original partnership societies were catalyzed and maintained by [[archaeology-of-psychedelic-mushroom-use]] use. The progressive loss of mushroom use (due to [[dighton-fungi-climate-change-feedback]], migration, and cultural suppression) led through four stages of abandonment:

1. Substitution with other psychoactive plants
2. Symbolic replacement with inactive materials
3. Purely symbolic religion (dogma without experience)
4. Complete abandonment and active repression (the modern condition)

Western civilization, in this analysis, represents the terminal stage of this process: "we traded our birthright as partners in the drama of the living mind of the planet for the broken pot shards of history, warfare, neurosis, and--if we do not quickly awaken to our predicament--planetary catastrophe."

## Signs of the Revival

McKenna identifies several contemporary phenomena as evidence that the Archaic Revival is already underway:

- The widespread use of cannabis and psychedelic compounds despite [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-legal-prohibition-impact-uk-netherlands]]: "Everything about cannabis that makes it inimical to contemporary bourgeois values endears it to the Archaic Revival. It diminishes the power of ego, has a mitigating effect on competitiveness, causes one to question authority, and reinforces the notion of the merely relative importance of social values."
- The counterculture of the 1960s as the most visible manifestation of the Archaic Revival impulse
- The revival of shamanic practices, nature spirituality, and goddess worship
- The environmental movement as an expression of partnership values
- The rise of holistic, integrative, and systems-thinking approaches to knowledge

## The Role of Psychedelics

Psychedelics are central to the Archaic Revival because they are the most direct route to the experiential knowledge that characterized partnership societies:

- They dissolve the boundaries of the ego, countering the dominator pattern
- They produce experiences of unity with nature, undermining the nature-as-resource orientation
- They generate the sense of the sacred that is absent from secular scientism
- They catalyze language, creativity, and cognitive flexibility

McKenna argues that the suppression of [[mckenna-suppression-psychedelic-research-lsd-therapy]] "is an obvious case" of the fourth stage of abandonment: "the rediscovery of the mystery and its interpretation as evil and threatening to social values."

## The Gaia Hypothesis Connection

The Archaic Revival connects to James Lovelock's Gaia hypothesis (the Earth as a self-regulating organism) by proposing that partnership societies were in dynamic equilibrium with Gaia while dominator societies are actively destroying her. The revival is not merely cultural but ecological: it represents the only possible path to a sustainable human future.
