## Self-Seeding Vegetable Strategies

### Legumes
- Broad beans: Resprout if large base pods left to dry and hay-mulched in late summer. Alternatively, prune back hard after harvest and the root crown will reshoot
- Peas and beans: Allow to mature and drop seed; many varieties will self-select for local conditions

### Grains
- Corn: Summer interplant between grain crops; let some cobs mature fully for next year's seed
- Wheat, rye, barley, oats: Under-sown with white clover; grain heads drop seed for volunteer plants

### Salad and Greens
- Lettuces: Allow to go to seed; seedlings scatter naturally in mulch
- Parsley and flat-seeded species: Reseed freely in mulch conditions
- Amaranthus: Encouraged as persistent annuals by soil disturbance
- Chickweed: Persistent annual encouraged by light disturbance

### Fruiting Vegetables
- Tomatoes: Axil shoots pinched out and reset as small plants all summer; last lot potted for indoor winter fruiting. Fermented whole under mulch throw up seedlings next season
- Peppers: Winter-pruned and set outside in spring; can persist as perennials above frost line
- Pumpkin, melon: Fermented whole under mulch produce volunteer seedlings

### Root Vegetables
- Potatoes: Seed potatoes under mulch sprout in spring without planting
- Carrots: Tops sprouted in dark, cool place produce divisions
- Jerusalem artichoke: Pulled as needed; flourishes under oak forest or poor soil

### Brassicas
- Cabbages: Split crosswise to produce sprouts for division
- Some varieties will self-seed if left to bolt

## The Mulch Connection

Self-seeding systems depend on continuous mulch for success:

- Mulch retains moisture for seed germination
- Mulch suppresses competitive weeds
- Mulch provides the organic matter for soil biology
- Mulch creates the conditions for volunteer seedlings to establish
- Approximately 900 kg of mulch per 1000 m2 annually

## The Grain-Legume Rotation System

A five-plot rotation integrating small grains with white clover:

- Plot rotation: rye, barley, millet, wheat, oats (one per plot per year)
- White clover as permanent leguminous intercrop
- Straw from each grain type moves to a different plot each year as mulch
- Remarkably low seeding rates: clover at 1 kg/ha, grains at 7-16 kg/ha
- The system demonstrates productive agriculture with minimal seed inputs

## Integration with Perennial Systems

The self-seeding annual system is not isolated but integrated with:

- Perennial tree crops providing shade and wind protection
- Poultry scratching and manuring the mulched beds
- Duck-rice integration in wet areas
- Perennial vegetable beds (asparagus, globe artichoke, comfrey)
- Herb spirals and permanent plantings

## Advantages of Self-Seeding Systems

1. No seed purchase required after establishment
2. Locally adapted varieties develop naturally
3. Reduced labor (no seed-starting, transplanting, bed preparation)
4. Continuous harvest rather than seasonal glut
5. Greater biodiversity as many varieties persist
6. Resilience to crop failure (multiple varieties, multiple seasons)
7. Knowledge of plant life cycles deepens through observation
