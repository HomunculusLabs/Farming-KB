# Hyperspace, the Logos, and Consciousness

Terence McKenna's framework of hyperspace and the Logos provides an ontological interpretation of the experiences produced by [[tryptamine hallucinogens]]. This framework posits that the [[dmt]] and [[psilocybin]] states grant access to a dimension of transhuman intelligence that exists at the quantum foundation of being, in the human future, and after death, all of which are "seen to be part of a single continuum."

The framework integrates elements of quantum physics, philosophy of mind, and phenomenological description into a unified account of consciousness and its relationship to a larger informational reality.

## The Logos

McKenna used the term "Logos" to refer to the transhuman, hyperdimensional intelligence encountered in the tryptamine state. Drawing on the philosophical tradition from Philo Judaeus through Heraclitus, the Logos in McKenna's usage is:

- A universe of "active intelligence that is transhuman, hyperdimensional, and extremely alien"
- Something that exists "one quanta away" from ordinary reality, always present but normally inaccessible
- Accessible through [[tryptamine hallucinogens]], which "invoke the Logos" by acting directly on the brain's language centers
- Capable of conveying translinguistic information that cannot be translated into any human language
- Consistently encountered across different tryptamine compounds and different experiencers

McKenna reported engaging the Logos in ongoing dialogue, asking: "What are you? Are you some kind of diffuse consciousness that is in the ecosystem of the Earth? Are you a god or an extraterrestrial? Show me what you know."

This interrogative stance, rather than devotional submission, characterized his approach to the numinous throughout his career.

## Hyperspace

Hyperspace in McKenna's framework is not a speculative physics concept but a phenomenological description of the space encountered in the tryptamine state. It is characterized by:

- A feeling of being underground or inside an insulated, domed space (the "merry go raum")
- Three-dimensional and fourth-dimensional deployment of information coded as light and evolving surfaces
- The presence of [[self-transforming machine elves]] and other hyperdimensional entities
- Translinguistic information complexes that resist human comprehension
- An intensity that exceeds any other form of hallucinogenic experience

McKenna stated that "magic is alive in hyperspace" and that the gnosis present there "comes from plants." He distinguished plant-derived gnosis from the products of laboratory synthesis, cautioning that while dealing with a plant offers "some certainty that one is dealing with a creature of integrity," the "creatures born in the demonic artifice of laboratories have to be dealt with very, very carefully."

## The Continuum of Being

A central claim of McKenna's framework is that several states previously considered discrete and separate are actually part of a single continuum:

- Dream
- The [[psilocybin]] trance
- The [[dmt]] state
- The quantum foundation of being
- The human future
- The state after death

In this framework, all of these "places" are coordinates within the same hyperspatial manifold, accessible through the tryptamine compounds and possibly through other means. The boundaries between them are artifacts of ordinary consciousness rather than genuine ontological divisions.

This claim, if true, would have profound implications for understanding the nature of death, the structure of reality, and the potential range of human experience.

## Consciousness and Quantum Mechanics

McKenna proposed that the major quantum mechanical phenomena experienced by humans (aside from waking consciousness) are dreams and hallucinations. These states emerge when sensory radiation is restricted and "interior images and interior processes that are psycho-physical" arise at the quantum mechanical level.

He connected this to Alfred North Whitehead's concept of consciousness causing "the stuff of being to undergo the formality of actually occurring." In this view, consciousness is not an epiphenomenon of matter but a fundamental agent capable of collapsing the state vector and bringing being into actuality.

The tryptamine hallucinogens, by revealing the quantum mechanical basis of conscious experience, demonstrate that mind and matter are dynamically linked at their most fundamental level.

## The Centrality of Human Experience

Against the scientific trend toward decentralizing humanity's place in the universe, McKenna argued for the primacy of the perceiving self:

- The most richly organized material in the universe is the human cerebral cortex
- The densest and richest experience in the universe is the experience occurring right now
- "Everything should be constellated outward from the perceiving self" as the primary datum
- Western civilization's five-hundred-year project of decentralization has placed humanity at "the edge of the galaxy" when in fact we are at the center of experiential reality

McKenna criticized the "inductive projection" by which physicists describe the universe as lasting billions of years into the past and future, calling this "a dualistic conception" that is "very unsophisticated when applied to the nature of consciousness and language."

## Transtemporality and Eschatology

McKenna described the tryptamine state as "transtemporal," invoking Plato's metaphor that "time is the moving image of eternity." The tryptamine ecstasy is "a stepping out of the moving image and into eternity, the eternity of the standing now, the *nunc stans* of Thomas Aquinas."

In this state, temporal succession is revealed as an artifact of ordinary consciousness, and all moments are seen to coexist in a timeless present.

In this state, all of human history is seen to accelerate toward a culminating moment. McKenna described "History as the shockwave of eschatology," with something at the end of time "casting an enormous shadow over human history, drawing all human becoming toward it."

He pointed to visible acceleration in human development: fire discovered millions of years ago, language perhaps thirty-five thousand years ago, measurement five thousand years ago, Galileo four hundred years ago, and the Watson-Crick DNA discovery in the twentieth century.

## The Exteriorized Soul

McKenna's vision of the human future involved what he called "exteriorizing the soul and internalizing the body." He imagined a "superconducting lens of translinguistic matter generated out of the body of each of us at a critical juncture at our psychedelic Bar Mitzvah."

From that point, the exteriorized soul would exist eternally "somewhere in the solid-state matrix of the translinguistic lens we have become," while the body image would exist as a "holographic wave transform."

This vision draws together the translinguistic information of the tryptamine state with a speculative technological future in which the boundary between mind and matter is dissolved.

## Language and the Noosphere

McKenna connected the tryptamine state to the evolution of language itself. He proposed that either language is "the shadow" of the translinguistic ability revealed by tryptamines, or that this ability will become "a further extension of language."

He speculated that a human language is possible "in which the intent of meaning is actually beheld in three-dimensional space" and that with ten thousand years of cultural involvement, this could become "a cultural convenience in the same way that mathematics or language has become."

He described language as "self-replicating information systems that reflect functions of DNA: learning, coding, templating, recording, testing, re-testing, re-coding." Language is "in us monkeys now and moving through us and moving out of our hands and into the noosphere with which we have surrounded ourselves."

## Relation to the UFO Phenomenon

McKenna suggested that the UFO or flying saucer may be "the central motif to be understood in order to get a handle on reality here and now." He proposed that humanity is so alienated that the Self "must disguise itself as an extraterrestrial in order not to alarm us with the truly bizarre dimensions that it encompasses."

The resolution, he argued, lies in learning to "love the alien," which would begin to heal the "psychic discontinuity that has plagued us since at least the sixteenth century."

## Philip K. Dick and the Hibernation of the Logos

McKenna drew on [[Philip K. Dick]]'s novel Valis for the concept of the Logos as a "creature of pure information" that was buried at Nag Hammadi along with the Chenoboskion Library circa 370 A.D.

The Logos existed as static information until 1947, when the Nag Hammadi texts were translated and read, bringing the symbiotic intelligence back to life. This parallels the rediscovery of psilocybin mushrooms by the Wassons in the 1950s, which "unleashed" a consciousness that had been driven into hiding.

## The Future Belongs to the Mind

McKenna concluded with the assertion that "the future is bound to be psychedelic, because the future belongs to the mind." He argued that humanity is "just beginning to push the buttons on the mind" and that a serious engineering approach to consciousness will reveal "the plasticity, the mutability, the eternal nature of the mind."

The ultimate trajectory of human evolution, in his view, is the release of mind from the constraints of biology through the exteriorization of the soul.
