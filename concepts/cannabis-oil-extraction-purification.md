# [[ethanol]] (Methyl Alcohol, Wood Alcohol)

- **Boiling point:** 64°C (~190°F)
- **Pros:** Commonly available; uniform evaporation temperature; does not extract many water-soluble (non-psychoactive) tars
- **Cons:** Fumes are toxic and explosive; inhalation causes sickness and body ache; continued inhalation may cause permanent damage; any traces remaining in oil are hazardous
- **Sources:** Pharmacies, industrial chemical supply companies, paint thinner (often impure)

### Isopropyl Rubbing Alcohol (70% isopropyl / 30% water)

- **Pros:** Widely available, inexpensive, less toxic and explosive than [[petroleum-ether]]

- **Boiling point:** 30–60°C
- **Pros:** Much more selective than alcohols; produces oil roughly twice as potent by weight as alcohol-extracted oil
- **Cons:** Highly explosive; best used for secondary re-extraction rather than direct extraction from plant material
- **Sources:** Chemical supply companies
- **Recommended approach:** Extract with alcohol first, then re-extract with petroleum ether — this minimizes the quantity of the dangerous solvent needed

## Soxhleting (Wash Extraction)

After refluxing, oil-bearing solvent trapped in the expended cannabis material must be recovered. A vegetable-draining colander fitted with a large coffee filter paper (12-inch papers work well) is placed above the stainless steel pot. The cannabis/solvent/oil mixture is poured through, allowing the dark oil-bearing liquid to drain free of vegetable particles.

The colander is set on top of the pot (now containing the alcohol/cannabis-oil solution) and the apparatus reassembled for refluxing. As the solution boils, pure recondensed solvent drips through the cannabis material in the colander, washing out remaining oil. Extraction is complete when drops of liquid from the colander leave no colored residue when evaporated on glass.

**Safety:** Before opening the apparatus after soxhleting, cool it sufficiently — setting the stew pot in ice water for several minutes works well. A thick blanket soaked in the ice tub serves as an excellent fire extinguisher if needed.

## Solvent Removal

A small collection pan replaces the spent cannabis in the colander. The apparatus is reassembled and returned to the water bath. As the solvent/oil solution boils, pure solvent condenses and drips into the collection pan while oil remains in the stainless steel pot. Collected solvent is essentially pure and can be saved for future extractions.

After solvent removal, the pot is kept in boiling water to eliminate all traces. For toxic solvents or those containing water: add water to the oil and evaporate in an oil bath ([[cottonseed-oil]] works) at approximately 220°F. When the water is gone, all solvent traces have been removed (all mentioned solvents evaporate below water's boiling point).

## Purification by Liquid-Liquid Extraction

Raw extracted oil is potent but contains taste, smell, and color compounds. Removing these greatly increases potency while decreasing yield proportionally.

### Process

1. Dissolve the oil in **five times its weight of cold alcohol**
2. Pour into an **equal volume of cold water** in a large glass jug with screw cap
3. Add **petroleum ether** equal to half the water volume
4. Invert the jug approximately 25 times, releasing pressure each time
5. Allow to stand for ~30 minutes — three distinct layers form:
   - **Bottom:** Water, alcohol, and tars/resins insoluble in petroleum ether
   - **Middle:** Emulsion of waxes, ether, and air bubbles
   - **Top:** Purified oil dissolved in petroleum ether

### Collection

A two-holed rubber stopper with glass tubing is fitted to the jug. One tube extends to a half-inch from the bottom of the ether/oil layer; the other serves as a pressure inlet. A siphon transfers the ether/oil solution to a collection jug. The glass tube is positioned above the emulsion layer.

Fresh petroleum ether is added and the process repeated until the ether layer remains clear after inversion — indicating complete extraction. Combined ether/oil solutions are evaporated using the water bath apparatus. The refined oil is substantially superior to the original alcohol extraction.

## Oil Composition

The final purified oil contains:

- **Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC)** — the primary psychoactive compound
- **Cannabidiol (CBD)** — non-psychoactive but convertible to THC via isomerization
- **Cannabinol (CBN)** — a related non-psychoactive compound
- **Terpenes and other compounds** — contributing taste and aroma

The quality of the THC "high" is determined by the relative positions of double bonding in the THC molecule — higher-rotating forms are more potent and produce a more psychedelic, spiritual effect. The quantity of CBD is significant because it can be converted to THC, potentially doubling or even quintupling the oil's potency.
