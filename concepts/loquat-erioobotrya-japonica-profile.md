# Loquat (Eriobotrya japonica)

## Overview

The loquat, also known as the Japanese loquat or Japanese medlar, is a small evergreen tree originally from central China that has become widely naturalized and cultivated across warm-temperate and subtropical regions worldwide. Valued for its early-spring fruit, attractive evergreen foliage, shade tolerance, and multiple landscape functions, the loquat is one of the most versatile and underappreciated fruit [[query-what-are-the-best-nitrogen-fixing-trees-for-permaculture]] systems in Mediterranean [[query-how-to-grow-rare-tropical-fruits-in-marginal-and-subtropical-climates]]. Its ability to serve simultaneously as a fruit producer, shade tree, windbreak, hedge, and [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]] plant makes it an excellent example of function stacking [[pattern-understanding-in-ecological-design]].

## Botanical Profile

- **Family**: Rosaceae
- **Genus**: Eriobotrya
- **Species**: Eriobotrya japonica
- **Common names**: Loquat, Japanese loquat, Japanese medlar, nispero (Spanish), pipa (Chinese)
- **Growth habit**: Small evergreen tree
- **Height**: 6-9 meters under favorable conditions; often 3 meters in less favorable sites
- **Native range**: Central China
- **Spread**: Broad, rounded canopy

## Climate Requirements

The loquat is remarkably adaptable but has specific climatic needs for reliable fruit production:

- **Frost hardiness**: The tree itself is fairly frost hardy, surviving temperatures to -10°C.
- **Fruit production**: Winter temperatures below -5°C will prevent fruit set, since flowers are borne at the beginning of winter. This limits reliable fruiting to regions with mild winters.
- **Heat tolerance**: Performs well in warm climates but prefers some winter chilling.
- **Rainfall**: Grows best in moist conditions but is moderately drought-tolerant once established.
- **Wind tolerance**: Moderately wind-tolerant; can serve as a windbreak.
- **Salt spray tolerance**: Tolerant of coastal salt spray, making it suitable for seaside plantings.
- **Ideal climate**: Mediterranean (warm, dry summers; mild, wet winters) or subtropical.

## Soil Requirements

- **Preferred**: Light, deep, loamy soil kept moist.
- **Tolerated**: Wide range of soil types.
- **Avoid**: Excessive lime in the soil.
- **Drainage**: Good drainage is important; the tree does not tolerate waterlogging.

## Fruit Characteristics

The loquat fruit is distinctive and appealing:
- **Shape**: Oval, approximately 5-6 cm long.
- **Color**: Yellow to orange when ripe.
- **Flavor**: Sweetly perfumed with softly acid flesh; refreshing and easily digested.
- **Seeds**: Usually one large seed per fruit, sometimes two.
- **Harvest season**: Spring to early summer (a time when few other fresh fruits are available).
- **Fresh eating**: Best eaten fresh, peeled and seeded.
- **Processing**: Can be made into jams, jellies, preserves, and wine.
- **Nutritional value**: While not highly nutritious, the fruit provides moderate calories, vitamin A (670 I.U. per 100g), and potassium (348mg per 100g).

## Cultivation

### Planting
- **Spacing**: Minimum 4 meters between trees; allow more space for larger specimens.
- **Cross-pollination**: Cross-pollination is an advantage, so planting multiple varieties or trees improves fruit set.
- **Site selection**: Full sun to partial shade; protection from cold winter winds improves fruit set.

### Propagation
- **Seed**: Loquats can be grown from seed and will sometimes self-sow around the parent tree. However, seed-grown trees may not produce fruit true to type.
- **Grafting/budding**: The preferred method for named cultivars. The rootstock is often quince ([[cydonia-oblonga]]), which both dwarfs the tree and hastens early fruiting.
- **Cuttings**: Possible but less reliable than grafting.
- **Time to fruit**: Grafted trees begin bearing at approximately 3 years old.

### Management
- **Pruning**: Minimal pruning needed. Remove dead wood and shape as desired.
- **Fruit thinning**: Thinning the fruit will result in greater individual fruit size.
- **Fertilizer**: Responds well to moderate fertilization but does not require heavy feeding.
- **Pests**: Relatively pest-free; birds may eat ripe fruit.
- **Animal forage**: The tree has some potential as animal forage, with foliage and fallen fruit browsed by livestock.
