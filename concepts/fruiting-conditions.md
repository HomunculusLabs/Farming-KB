# Fruiting Conditions

After full substrate colonization, mushrooms require a shift in environmental
conditions to trigger the transition from vegetative mycelial growth to
reproductive fruiting. This shift is callfruiting-initiationtion" and involves
changes to temperature, humidity, fresh air exchange, and light.

## The Fruiting Trigger

Mushroom formation is triggered by a combination of environmental cues that
sigmyceliummycelium that conditions are favorable for reproduction:

1. **Drop in temperature**: A 5-10°C reduction from colonization temperature
   simulates seasonal change.
2. **Introduction of fresh air**: Lower CO2 levels signal that the mycelium
   has reached the substrate surface and is exposed to open air.
3. **High humidity (85-95%)**: Prevents the developing fruiting bodies from
   drying out before maturity.
4. **Light**: Most cultivated mushrooms require at least some light to
   initiate and guide fruiting. Indirect ambient light or a 12-hour light
   cycle with 5000-6500K spectrum is typical.

## Pinning (Primordia Formation)

"Pinning" is the formation of tiny mushroom initials called primordia. These
are the first visible stage of mushroom development, appearing as small white
bumps on the substrate surface. The pinning stage is the most critical and
delicate phase of cultivation:

- Pins are extremely sensitive to drying. A drop in humidity can kill an
  entire pin set within hours.
- Temperature fluctuations during pinning can cause aborts (pins that stop
  growing and rot).
- CO2 levels must be lowered but not so drastically that humidity drops.
- The goal is a dense, even pin set across the entire substrate surface,
  which leads to uniform mushroom size at harvest.

## Key Environmental Parameters

### Fresh Air Exchange (FAE)

During colonization, high CO2 encourages mycelial growth. For fruiting, CO2
must be reduced to below 800 ppm (compared to 5000-10000 ppm during
colonization). In practice, this means providing regular air exchange:

- Passive exchange through filtered holes or gaps
- Active exchange using fans (with care not to blow directly on substrates)
- Mist and fan cyclshotgun-fruiting-chamberting Chamber) setups

Insufficient FAE causes elongated, thin-stemmed mushrooms with small caps
("leggy" growth). Excessive FAE can dry out substrates and pins.

### Humidity

Maintaining 85-95% relative humidity during fruiting is essential:

- **Humidifiers**: Ultrasonic or evaporative, often on timers or humidity
  controllers.
- **Perlite**: Expanded volcanic rock that holds water, providing passive
  humidity in fruiting chambers.
- **Misting**: Hand-misting with a spray bottle 2-4 times daily, combined
  with fanning for air exchange.
- **Martha tent**: A modified greenhouse enclosure with automated humidity
  and fresh air exchange for larger-scale cultivation.

### Temperature

Most gourmet mushrooms fruit between 15-22°C (59-72°F). Specific ranges:

| Species | Fruiting Temp |
|---------|--------------|
| Oyster (Pleurotus) | 15-25°C (59-77°F) |
| Shiitake (Lentinula) | 10-20°C (50-68°F) |
| Lion's Mane (Hericium) | 15-20°C (59-68°F) |
| Reishi (Ganoderma) | 22-28°C (72-82°F) |
| King Oyster | 12-18°C (54-64[[agar]]|

### Light

While mushrooms are not photosynthetic, light serves as a directional cue
for fruiting body development:

- Most species need 12 hours on / 12 hours off light cycle
- Indirect sunlight or 6500K fluorescent/LED lighting is ideal
- Intensity of 200-1000 lux is sufficient
- Too little light causes pale, elongated, misshapen mushrooms
- Direct sunlight should be avoided as it can overheat and dry substrates

## Fruiting Chambers

Several designs are commonly used:

- **Shotgun Fruiting Chamber (SGFC)**: A plastic tote with many drilled
  holes on all six sides, filled with damp perlite. Simple, effective for
  small-scale cultivation. Relies on passive air exchange and perlite
  humidity.
- **Monotub**: A larger plastic tote modified with polyfill-stuffed holes
  for passive FAE and humidity control. Popular for bulk grows.
- **Martha tent**: A commercial greenhouse tent with humidifier and fan
  for mid-scale cultivation.
- **Dedicated fruiting room**: Climate-controlled room for commercial
  operations, with automated systems for humidity, temperature, and FAE.

## Harvesting

Mushrooms should be harvested at the right stage for optimal quality:

- Harvest just before or as the veil under the cap begins to tear
- Twist gently at the base rather than cutting to prevent rotting stumps
- Harvest all mushrooms from a flush promptly to prepare for the next flush
- Most substrates produce 2-4 flushes with diminishing yields each time
- After the final flush, spent substrate can be composted or used outdoors

## Flush Management

After harvesting a flush, the substrate needs recovery before the next flush:

- Maintain humidity but reduce FAE slightly for 3-5 days
- Some growers soak the substrate block in cold water for 4-12 hours to
  rehydrate before the next flush
- Second flush typically yields 50-70% of the first flush
- Third and subsequent flushes continue to decline
