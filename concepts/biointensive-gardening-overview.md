# GROW BIOINTENSIVE Sustainable Mini-Farming

GROW BIOINTENSIVE (GB) is a small-scale agricultural system developed by John
Jeavons at Ecology Action in Willits, California. It is a method of organic
growing that focuses on maximizing yields in minimal space while building soil
fertility. The method has been researched and refined since 1972 and is now
practiced in over 150 countries, making it one of the most widely adopted
small-scale sustainable farming systems in the world.

## Core Principle

The GB method is based on replicating natural ecosystems. Instead of mining
soil, it builds soil faster than nature alone by growing all fertility needs
on-site. The system is designed to be closed-loop: everything the plants need
is produced within the garden itself. This closed-loop approach distinguishes
GB from many other organic methods that still rely on imported fertility inputs
such as composted manure from off-site sources, purchased organic fertilizers,
or green manure crops grown elsewhere.

The fundamental insight is that truly sustainable agriculture must be measured
not just by output per acre but by the ratio of calories produced to calories
invested. When all inputs are accounted for — including the embodied energy
in imported fertilizers and the ecological cost of depleting distant soils to
produce them — GB's closed-loop approach becomes one of the most efficient food
production systems available.

## Resource Efficiency Claims

When practiced completely, GROW BIOINTENSIVE can produce:

- **60-80% less water consumption** than conventional methods (through close
  [[biointensive-close-plant-spacing]] that shades the soil
  surface and reduces evaporation, combined with improved soil structure that
  increases water infiltration and retention)
- **50% less purchased fertilizer** (by biointensive-composting|growingyour own compost from crop residues and dedicated compost crops)
- **99% less energy use** (by eliminating tractor cultivation, minimizing
  fossil fuel inputs, and reducing transportation of materials)
- **2-6 times higher yields per square foot** than conventional farming
- **Up to 4 times more calories per unit of area** than conventional methods
  when including [[biointensive-calorie-crops]]

These efficiency gains are cumulative — the more completely the system is
implemented, the greater the returns. Partial implementation yields partial
benefits, which is why Jeavons emphasizes the importance of all eight
components working together as an integrated whole.

## The Eight Components

The GB system consists of eight interlocking principles:

1. **Deep soil preparation** via [[biointensive-bed-preparation]]
   — loosens soil to 24 inches, improving drainage, aeration, and root
   penetration. This creates a deep, loose growing bed that allows roots to
   access nutrients and water from a much larger soil volume than shallow
   cultivation permits.

2. **Close plant spacing** in [[biointensive-close-plant-spacing]] — shades soil, reduces evaporation, maximizes photosynthesis
   per unit area, and creates a living mulch that suppresses weeds. Plants
   are spaced so that their leaves just touch at maturity, creating a
   continuous living canopy.

3. **Composting** — [[biointensive-composting]] of
   all crop residues to return nutrients to the soil. Compost is the primary
   fertility source in GB, and compost crops typically occupy about 60% of
   the growing area to ensure sufficient biomass production.

4. **Carbon farming** — [[biointensive-carbon-farming]] to produce enough biomass to sustain soil fertility
   indefinitely. These crops are typically high-biomass plants like rye,
   wheat, oats, and comfrey that produce large amounts of carbon-rich material
   for the compost pile.

5. **Calorie farming** — [[biointensive-calorie-crops]] (potatoes, sweet potatoes, wheat, rye, corn, beans) to produce
   complete dietary calories in minimal space. This component ensures that the
   garden can meet a significant portion of the grower's caloric needs, not
   just supplemental vegetables.

6. **Companion planting** — interplanting compatible species to reduce pest
   pressure, improve pollination, and maximize space use. GB draws on
   traditional companion planting knowledge as well as modern research into
   plant interactions and [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]].

7. **Open-pollinated seed saving** — [[biointensive-seed-saving]]
   adapted to local conditions, reducing dependency on commercial seed
   suppliers and preserving genetic diversity. Over generations, saved seeds
   become increasingly adapted to the specific microclimate and soil
   conditions of the garden.

8. **Whole system integration** — all eight components work together
  synergistically; removing any one reduces the effectiveness of the whole.
  For example, close plant spacing requires deep soil preparation to provide
  sufficient root volume, which requires compost to maintain fertility, which
  requires carbon crops, which require calorie crops for dietary balance.

## Area Requirements

Jeavons estimates that a complete diet for one person (approximately 2,500-
3,000 calories/day) can be grown on as little as 2,800-4,000 square feet
(about 1/16 to 1/10 of an acre) using the full GB method, including space
for [[biointensive-carbon-farming]] and [[biointensive-calorie-crops]].

This compares favorably to the estimated 10,000-22,000 square feet needed
with conventional organic methods, and the 33,000-77,000 square feet needed
with conventional mechanized agriculture. The dramatically reduced land
requirement is GB's most significant implication for global food security —
if adopted widely, it could free enormous areas of land for ecological
restoration, desertification|reforestation, and biodiversity conservation.

## Soil Building Focus

The fundamental insight of GB is that soil is the limiting resource for
human civilization. While conventional agriculture depletes soil at rates
far exceeding natural rebuilding (estimated at 1-2 mm of topsoil lost per
year in many regions), GB is designed to rebuild soil. The goal is to grow
soil faster than it is consumed — building approximately 1 inch of topsoil
per year through proper biointensive-composting|compost management and
careful cultivation practices.

This soil-building focus connects GB to broader movements in [[mycelium-running-mycelium-soil-building]] and [[teaming-with-nutrients-composting-and-nutrient-cycling]]. The emphasis on closed-loop fertility also aligns with [[permaculture-principles]] of producing no waste and catching and storing energy within the
system.

## Global Application

GB has been particularly impactful in developing countries where smallholders
have limited land, scarce water resources, and limited access to purchased
inputs. Ecology Action has established demonstration and research sites
worldwide, and the method has been adapted to local conditions in diverse
climates from tropical to arid. The method's emphasis on seed saving and
locally-produced fertility makes it accessible to farmers who cannot afford
or access commercial agricultural inputs.
