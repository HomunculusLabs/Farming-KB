# GROW BIOINTENSIVE Sustainable Mini-Farming

GROW BIOINTENSIVE (GB) is a small-scale agricultural system developed by John
Jeavons at Ecology Action in Willits, California. It is a method of organic
growing that focuses on maximizing yields in minimal space while building soil
fertility. The method has been researched and refined since 1972 and is now
practiced in over 150 countries, making it one of the most widely adopted
small-scale sustainable farming systems in the world.

## Core Principle

The GB method is based on replicating natural ecosystems. Instead of mining
soil, it builds soil faster than [[comparison-wood-chip-mulch-vs-leaf-mold-vs-composted-manure-soil-building]] from off-site sources, purchased [[biointensive-calorie-crops]]

These efficiency gains are cumulative — the more completely the system is
implemented, the greater the returns. Partial implementation yields partial
benefits, which is why Jeavons emphasizes the importance of all eight
components working together as an integrated whole.

## Global Application

GB has been particularly impactful in developing countries where smallholders
have limited land, scarce water resources, and limited access to purchased
inputs. Ecology Action has established demonstration and research sites
worldwide, and the method has been adapted to local conditions in diverse
climates from tropical to arid. The method's emphasis on seed saving and
locally-produced fertility makes it accessible to farmers who cannot afford
or access commercial agricultural inputs.
