# Hemenway Keyhole Bed Design

Keyhole beds are round garden beds with a central path that provides access to all plants from a single standing point. They dramatically reduce the land sacrificed to paths compared to traditional row gardening.

## Path Efficiency

- **Single row garden**: paths consume about 50% of soil area
- **Raised bed garden**: paths consume about 30% of area
- **Keyhole bed**: needs only 6 square feet of path for a 50 sq ft planting area
- A keyhole bed containing 50 square feet of planting needs only 12% of its area for paths vs 44% for single rows

## Construction

1. Begin with a circle of soil 8-12 feet in diameter
2. Pierce on one side by a path to the center
3. Can be created by shoveling fertile topsoil into a horseshoe shape
4. Preferred method: layer up lasagna-style with leaves, organic matter, newspaper, and soil using [[hemenway-sheet-mulching-guide]]

## Dimensions

- **Plantable zone**: 3-5 feet across (same as standard raised bed)
- **Access path into bed**: narrow, about 1 foot wide
- **Central circle of path**: 18-24 inches in diameter (big enough to turn around in)

## Zone-Based Planting

Use the zone system within the bed:

### Center (most accessible)
Herbs, greens, and other daily-eaten vegetables border the central path.

### Middle ring
Plants picked regularly over the growing season: tomatoes, peppers, bush peas and beans, eggplants. Still easy to reach without stretching.

### Back row
Long-term crops and those harvested only once: potatoes, carrots, root crops, cauliflower, iceberg lettuce, cabbage. These may require stepping onto the bed to harvest -- one footprint per season will not annihilate soil porosity. Alternatively, lay down a board to stand on.

## Sun and Shadow Management

- Place crops that wilt in midsummer's full blaze to the east of taller sun-lovers
- Taller plants shade tender ones in scorching afternoons
- For trellised plants, curve a length of fencing around the bed

## Wind Protection

- Plant tall sturdy crops such as Jerusalem artichokes or stocky sunflowers on the exposed side as a windbreak

## Creative Possibilities

- Dedicate a whole circle to tomatoes with companion culinary herbs (basil, chives) at the inner margin
- Use circular geometry to balance sun and shadow
- Flowers work too -- stand in a circle of brilliant color while contemplating vase arrangements

## Margin Planting

The triangular spaces at the corners between keyhole beds are not wasted:
- Plant insect-attracting flowers
- Perennial nitrogen-fixers such as Dutch clover
- Wind and weed barrier
- Robust mulch-providers (crotolaria, comfrey)
- Small fruit tree
- Or expand the bed to fill the corners

## Mandala Garden

A further modification: 4-8 keyhole beds arranged in a circle with one more bed in the center, and a path entering from one side. Combines beauty and efficiency -- few designs fit more growing space into less area.

## Multiple Keyhole Beds

- Keyholes can extend left and right of a central walkway
- An undulating path flanked by keyhole beds can wrap around a house for an attractive Zone 1 garden
