### Hardwood vs Softwood Cuttings
Hardwood cuttings are taken during dormancy from mature wood — the
simplest method for deciduous trees like fig, grape, pomegranate, and
currant. Softwood cuttings are taken from new growth in spring and
require mist systems or high humidity. Semi-hardwood cuttings bridge the
gap, taken in late summer from partially mature wood.

## Layering
### Natural Layering
Many plants naturally layer where branches touch the ground. Blackberry
spreads by tip-layering and seed. These processes can be encouraged by
pinning branches to the ground.
### Air Layering
Air layering (marcotting) is used for difficult-to-root species. A branch
is girdled, wrapped with moist sphagnum moss and plastic, and roots form
at the wound. Works well for lychee, mango, citrus, and many tropical
fruits.
### Grafted and Layered Plants
For [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-establishment-bare-hillside]], apple, fig, quince, pear, and plum can be
started from ungrafted whips planted in protective thickets, later
selected for superior performers to graft.
## Grafting
### Multi-Graft Trees
Multi-graft trees allow several varieties on one rootstock, extending the
harvest season and providing insurance against variety-specific failures.
### Bud and Scion Wood
Scion wood must be collected from disease-free, productive parent trees
during the dormant season and stored properly until grafting time.
### Grafting Techniques
Common methods include whip-and-tongue (matched diameters), cleft graft
(topworking established trees), budding (T-bud and chip bud for summer),
and approach grafting (difficult species). Each suits different species
and timing requirements.
