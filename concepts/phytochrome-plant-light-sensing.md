## Overview

Phytochrome is a photoreceptor pigment protein that plays a central role in
how plants perceive and respond to light. Discovered through the work of
multiple research groups in the mid-twentieth century, phytochrome mediates
a wide range of developmental responses including [[fungal-environmental-sensing-signal-transduction]] to
downstream cellular responses. Phytochrome exists in two stable isomeric
forms: Pr (P660), which absorbs [[photoperiodism-plants-measure-night-length]] and determine whether the photoperiodic conditions are

## Night-Break Experiments and Photoperiodic Measurement

The role of phytochrome in photoperiodism was decisively demonstrated
through night-break experiments. If a short-day plant (which requires long
nights to flower) is given a brief flash of red light during its dark
period, flowering is inhibited. However, if the red light flash is
immediately followed by a far-red light flash, flowering is restored. This
red/far-red reversibility is the hallmark of phytochrome action and
provided the key evidence that phytochrome is the photoreceptor mediating
photoperiodic responses. The red flash converts Pr to Pfr, resetting the
plant's night-length counter. The subsequent far-red flash converts the Pfr

phytochrome to the Pfr form, which activates the germination program. This
ecologically important mechanism prevents germination when seeds are too
deep to reach the surface as seedlings, thereby serving as a depth-sensing
system that optimizes seedling establishment.
