## Why Straw?

Straw — typically wheat, oat, or rye straw — is an excellent mushroom substrate because:

- **High cellulose content** — many [[mycelial-growth-and-colonization]] prevents anaerobic conditions
- **Easy to prepare** — straw requires less processing than wood-based substrates and can be prepared with simple hot water treatment

## Materials Required

### Substrate Components

- **Straw** — chopped to 3-10 cm lengths (pre-cut straw is available from many pet stores as animal bedding)
- **Brown rice flour** — provides supplementary nutrition (approximately 3-4 handfuls per 6 bags)
- **Yeast extract (Marmite or Vegemite)** — one heaped tablespoon, dissolved in boiling water; used to supplement nutrients and potentially accelerate spore germination

### Equipment

- **Boil-a-bags** — heat-resistant plastic bags rated for pressure cooking (e.g., Lakeland Plastics, 20cm × 30cm, approximately 50 per pack)
- **Large watertight container** — for soaking the straw
- **Pressure cooker** — rated for 15 psi, large enough to hold at least two bags
- **Colander or bathtub** — for draining the soaked straw
- **Masking tape** — wide, for securing bags and creating inoculation points
- **Filter can** — a modified food can containing rock wool and vermiculite layers (see below)

## The Filter Can

The filter can is a critical component that allows gas exchange while preventing contamination. To make one:

1. Obtain a clean 3-4 inch round food can, leaving the lid partially attached (about 1 inch connected) to serve as a closable cover
2. Punch numerous small holes in the bottom of the can using a hammer and nail
3. Line the inside bottom with approximately 1 cm of rock wool (or fiberglass loft insulation)
4. Fill the can with vermiculite to within 1 cm of the top (alternatively, fill entirely with small pieces of rock wool)
5. Top with another thin layer of rock wool to hold everything in place
6. Close the lid to complete the filter

The filter can sits in the neck of each bag and allows air exchange while trapping airborne contaminant spores in the rock wool and vermiculite layers.

## Step-by-Step Substrate Preparation

### Step 1: Preparing the Straw

Chop straw to lengths of 3-10 cm if not already pre-cut. Place the required amount of straw into a large watertight container. The amount needed depends on the number of bags being prepared — approximately one handful per bag.

### Step 2: Hot Water Soak

Dissolve one heaped tablespoon of yeast extract (Marmite or Vegemite) in a jug of boiling water, then add this to approximately 6 liters of additional boiling water. Pour the hot solution over the straw in the container, and weigh the straw down with a heavy object (a water-filled bucket works well) to ensure complete submersion.

Let the straw soak for at least one hour, checking periodically to ensure the water level still covers the straw. Add more boiling water if needed and continue soaking for a second hour (minimum 2 hours total soak time).

### Step 3: Draining

Turn the soaked straw out into a bathtub or large colander and let it drain for approximately one hour. Spread the straw evenly to ensure thorough drainage. If the straw is still too wet after draining, squeeze out excess water by hand. The substrate should be at field capacity — moist but not dripping when a handful is squeezed firmly.

### Step 4: Adding Supplements

Sprinkle brown rice flour evenly over the drained straw (approximately 3-4 handfuls for 6 bags' worth of substrate). Mix thoroughly by hand to distribute the flour evenly throughout the straw. The rice flour provides additional carbohydrates and nutrients that support vigorous [[boil-a-bag-filter-can-construction]], or unsterile [[inoculation-technique-and-alcohol-flaming]].
