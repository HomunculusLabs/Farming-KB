## Clearing Winter Beds

The process of clearing winter beds involves removing the remaining plants
from the previous season's crops. For crops like spinach and lettuce that
have been harvested repeatedly through the winter, the plants may be
declining in quality anyway as day length increases and temperatures rise.

For mâche and other once-over harvest crops, the remaining plants are
simply removed when the [[azurescens-outdoor-bed-preparation-woodchips]] preparation but with the advantage of earlier access to
workable soil. The protected environment means beds can be worked and
planted weeks before outdoor soil conditions would allow.

Coleman emphasized that the greenhouse soil, like all soil at Four Season
Farm, is managed with minimal disturbance. Rather than deep tilling or
heavy cultivation, the beds are lightly worked to incorporate surface
amendments and create a suitable seedbed or transplant bed.

This minimal-disturbance approach preserves soil structure, protects the
soil microbiome, and maintains the long-term productivity of the
greenhouse beds.

## Transition Crop Opportunities

The spring transition period creates opportunities for catch crops and
intercrops. As winter crops are harvested and beds are cleared, there may
be windows of several weeks before summer crops need the space.

Quick-growing crops like radishes, baby greens, or pea shoots can fill
these windows, providing additional harvests during a period when outdoor
production is just getting started. However, these catch crops must be
planted and harvested on a strict schedule to avoid delaying the main
summer planting.

Coleman noted that the temptation to extend winter crops or insert catch
crops must be balanced against the risk of delaying summer plantings. A
delayed summer crop may have consequences that extend far beyond the lost
spring harvest window.
