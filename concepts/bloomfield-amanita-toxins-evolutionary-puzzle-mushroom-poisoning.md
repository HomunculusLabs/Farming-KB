## Amanita Toxins and the Evolutionary Puzzle of Mushroom Poisoning

## Overview

The genus Amanita contains some of the most spectacular and deadly
mushrooms, including the [[amanita-phalloides]]). These species produce amatoxins,
miniproteins absorbed from the intestine that destroy the liver. Yet the
evolutionary rationale for producing such lethal delayed-action toxins
remains a genuine biological puzzle, illuminating fundamental questions
about why organisms produce poisons at all.

## Amatoxin Poisoning: Clinical Course

The destroying angel is a ravishingly beautiful pure white mushroom with
a membranous cup (volva) at the stem base and a floppy ring (annulus)
below the cap. The same mycelium may have fruited in the same location
for decades. Pathologist Dennis Benjamin estimates that 6 to 7
milligrams of pure amatoxin kills an adult, corresponding to a single
Amanita cap.

The clinical course is insidious: symptoms begin eight hours or even
two days after consumption with abdominal cramping, vomiting, and violent
diarrhea. Then a deceptive "honeyymoon" period follows during which the
patient may appear well enough to leave the hospital. Liver damage
continues silently, coma ensues, and death is frequent. No antidote
exists; mortality is below 30 percent with treatment.

### Hypothesis 2: Ghost of Evolution
Like the Osage orange (once dispersed by extinct mammoths), amatoxins
might once have deterred a prehistoric animal. But amatoxins block gene
expression by inhibiting protein synthesis, and no animal would be
affected quickly enough. [[adaptive-anemotropism-spore-dispersal-optimization-mushroom-evolution]].
Amanita also contains phallotoxins, which bind to cellular protein
skeletons and inhibit growth. Though not absorbed from the gut (so
harmless to human consumers), phallotoxins serve as additional weapons
against submerged insect larvae. Insects and fungi have interacted for
400 million years, providing ample evolutionary time for defense and
counter-defense. If insect larvae are the real targets, human casualties
were entirely unintended by evolution.
