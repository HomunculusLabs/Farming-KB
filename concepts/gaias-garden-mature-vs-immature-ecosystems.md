## Mature vs Immature Ecosystems in [[holmgren-ecological-succession-and-pulsing]]. They are
characterized by:

- **Fast growth** — Pioneer species grow rapidly to colonize open ground
- **Simple structure** — Few plant layers, often a single canopy height
- **High species turnover** — Species replace each other frequently
- **Nutrient leakage** — Nutrients cycle loosely; much is lost to runoff
- **Low biodiversity** — Dominated by a few aggressive species
- **External inputs needed** — Without intervention, productivity declines
- **Bare soil** — Exposed ground between plants invites weeds and erosion

Most conventional gardens are immature ecosystems. Tilling creates bare soil,
monocultures invite pest outbreaks, and the gardener must constantly
intervene with watering, fertilizing, weeding, and [[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]] are naturally controlled
- **Self-maintaining** — Minimal external inputs required
- **No bare soil** — Every surface is covered with living or dead plant matter

A mature ecosystem produces more biomass per unit area than an immature one,
uses water more efficiently, and resists disturbance better. This is the
model ecological gardeners strive to emulate.
