# The Trainwreck Generation: Early Outdoor Multi-Strain Breeding

## Overview

In his introduction to [[blesching-cannabis-sativa-indica-classification]] Volume 3, The Rev provides a fascinating
firsthand account of how [[cannabis-breeding-basics]] was practiced in Northern California
before the advent of [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]]. He describes the "Trainwreck generation"
— a term referring to the first-generation seeds produced by outdoor multi-strain
pollination events that characterized cannabis breeding in the late 1970s and
early 1980s. This breeding method, born of practical necessity, produced some of
the most legendary and genetically diverse cannabis lines in history.

## Pre-Indoor Breeding Context

The Rev establishes that before indoor cannabis breeding existed, all breeding
was done outdoors. This created specific challenges for cash croppers who needed
to produce seedless sinsemilla flowers while also wanting to create new
genetic combinations:

- **Limited control**: Unlike indoor grows where pollen can be precisely
  contained and directed, outdoor grows were subject to wind, insects, and
  unpredictable pollen drift.
- **Isolation challenges**: Preventing unwanted pollination in an outdoor
  setting required careful distance management between male and female
  plants.
- **Time investment**: Each breeding cycle required a full [[cannabis-outdoor-growing]]
  season, making every pollination decision consequential.

## The "Train Wreck" Pollination Method

The Rev describes the practical breeding method used by outdoor growers:

1. **Remote male plots**: Several male plants of various varieties were
   isolated in remote patches. Prevailing winds and distance were relied
   upon to direct pollen toward the intended female plants.
2. **Multiple female varieties**: Several different female varieties were
   planted in the receiving garden, meaning multiple strains were being
   pollinated simultaneously.
3. **Multiple male varieties**: Several different male varieties contributed
   pollen to the same group of females, creating a complex web of
   genetic combinations.
4. **Uncertain parentage**: The breeder could not know which male
   pollinated which female. The Rev describes this as a "train wreck of all
   killer genetics — you couldn't be sure who pollinated who."

## Origin of the "Trainwreck" Name

According to The Rev's account, the term "Trainwreck" as applied to cannabis
genetics originated from this breeding method — the unpredictable
first-generation seeds that resulted from multi-strain outdoor pollination.
These seeds were called "Trainwreck" because the genetics were a chaotic mix
of multiple parent lines, analogous to a train wreck of different genetic
contributions.

This origin predates and differs from the more commonly known Humboldt
Trainwreck clone, which is a specific stabilized variety. The original
"Trainwreck generation" referred to a breeding methodology and its
unpredictable results, not a specific strain.

## Regional Trainwreck Variations

The Rev notes that different regional variations of Trainwreck emerged from
this breeding method:

- **Humboldt Trainwreck**: The Northern California version that became the
  most famous.
- **Purple Trainwreck**: A phenotype expressing purple coloration.
- **L.A. Trainwreck**: A Southern California variation.
- **African Trainwreck**: Crosses incorporating African sativa genetics.
- **Colombian Trainwreck**: Crosses with [[oner-colombian-landrace-sativa-strains]] sativas.
- **Mexican Trainwreck**: Crosses with Mexican landrace varieties.
- **Hawaiian Trainwreck**: Crosses with Hawaiian sativa genetics.

Each regional variation reflected the local genetic pool — the particular
strains that outdoor breeders in that area had access to and chose to include
in their breeding plots.
