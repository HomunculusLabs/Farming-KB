## GABA Biochemistry

GABA acts as an inhibitory neurotransmitter throughout the central nervous system.
When GABA and glutamate balance each other, relaxation and excitement balance
each other. Glutamate is the brain's primary excitatory neurotransmitter; GABA
is its counterweight. This balance is fundamental to neurological health.

Low levels of GABA are related to multiple conditions:

- Irritability and anxiety
- Panic attacks
- Lack of empathy and aggression
- Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)
- Antisocial behavior
- Depression
- Craving for carbohydrates
- Adrenal fatigue

A pilot study at the University of Boston demonstrated a mind-body link by
measuring a significant increase in brain GABA after subjects practiced yoga.
This finding confirmed that behavioral interventions can directly alter
neurotransmitter availability.

## Cannabinoid-GABA Interactions

The endocannabinoid system's dual capacity to simultaneously balance upper
effects (via cortical glutamate) and downer effects (via GABA) presents a novel
therapeutic approach. CB1 receptors, which are concentrated in the brain, are
the primary site of this interaction.

THC and other CB1-activating cannabinoids can enhance GABAergic transmission.
The calming, sedating effects commonly associated with indica-dominant cannabis
strains are largely mediated through this GABA pathway. Indica strains, with
their higher CBD-to-THC ratios and greater CB2 affinity, tend to produce more
pronounced GABA-mediated relaxation effects compared to [[cannabis-for-anxiety-disorders]]:** Enhanced GABA signaling reduces fear signaling in the
  amygdala, the brain's threat-detection center
- **Insomnia:** GABA promotion supports natural sleep architecture
- **Muscle spasms:** GABA inhibits excessive motor neuron firing
- **ADHD:** The dual modulation of glutamate and GABA may offer a new approach
  to attention regulation without stimulant medications
- **Epilepsy:** GABA enhancement helps prevent the neural hyperexcitability
  that triggers seizures
