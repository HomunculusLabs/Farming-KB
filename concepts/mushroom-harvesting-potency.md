# Mushroom Harvesting and Potency

Harvest timing, drying method, and storage dramatically affect the quality and potency of cultivated mushrooms. PF (Psylocybe Fanaticus) and Paul Stamets both provide detailed guidance. This page covers harvest timing for maximum potency, drying and storage methods, spore printing, and spore syringe preparation.

## When to Harvest: Pre-Sporulation

The immature specimens are the best in quality, digestibility, and potency. Harvest characteristics:

- **Very light in color** with white stems and light colored caps
- **Conical shape** -- cap has not spread out yet
- **Veil intact** -- harvest just before or right after the veil breaks
- **Gills light in color** -- sporulation has not really begun
- **No purple spore deposits** visible on the stem

### Why Pre-Sporulation Matters

As the mushroom cap expands like an umbrella:
- Size increases but **potency decreases by weight**
- The mushroom is past its prime for psychedelic use once:
  - Cap opens, veil tears away
  - Gills darken to brown
  - Purple spore deposits appear on the stem
  - Colors darken with bluish tones

PF notes: "This is probably true for all other magic mushroom species -- including wild specimens."

### Harvest Abhorts and Mutants

Pick ALL tiny primordia that stop growing (abhort) and small mutant growths (round blobs, no heads) as soon as it is clear they are not developing into normal specimens:
- Several cakes will produce "quite a pile of these little ones" (several dried grams)
- "These are the source of the potent magic"
- They are small but extremely potent per unit weight

## Potency Factors

From PF and Jochen Gartz's research, P. cubensis can achieve baseline psilocybin content of 1% (similar to P. semilanceata) when:

1. **Grown on brown rice** (PF TEK substrate)
2. **Harvested young** (pre-sporulation)
3. **Dried properly** (cool desiccation)

### Dosage Reference (from Paul Stamets)

| Dose (dried) | Effects |
|-------------|---------|
| 1-2 grams | Moderate effects for most people; 4-5 hour duration |
| 2 grams | First colorful geometric patterns, noticeable auditory changes |
| 3 grams | Visual waves; air appears liquid with tidal movements |
| 4 grams | Intense experience, 4-5 hours |
| 5 grams | Nearly overwhelming, 6 hours |
| 7+ grams | Not recommended; requires very supportive set and setting |

Note: Individual sensitivity varies enormously. One mycologist needs only 1-2 grams for a full experience; another woman felt nothing at 5 grams.

## Drying Methods

### Method 1: Cool Desiccant Drying (Best)

**Materials**: Desiccant (Drierite, silica gel, or "Damp Gone"), wire screen, plastic tub, plastic bag with tie-off.

1. Place 1-inch layer of desiccant on bottom of container
2. Put mushrooms on wire screen laid over the desiccant
3. Place container into a plastic bag; seal with wire tie
4. Clear plastic bag allows observation of drying process
5. After 24 hours: slight shriveling visible
6. After 4-5 days: mushrooms are "dried rock hard"
7. **Test**: stem should snap cleanly when bent

**For maximum alkaloid preservation**: Put the desiccant box in the refrigerator -- mushrooms dry at near-freezing temperatures.

**Desiccant sources**:
- "Drierite" -- universal lab desiccant (chemical/science supply)
- Silica gel granules (chemical/science supply)
- "Damp Gone" -- looks like kitty litter, inexpensive, works as well as any (hardware stores)

**Safety**: Desiccant may have toxicity warnings for dust/skin contact. In an airtight box under a screen it is completely safe for drying mushrooms.

**Reusing desiccant**: Heat in oven per manufacturer instructions. Pre-heat before first use (purchased desiccant is usually somewhat damp). Store in airtight container.

### Method 2: Air Pre-Drying + Desiccant

1. Place mushrooms on wire screen with air available to all sides
2. Air dry for a couple of days (if room humidity is not high)
3. Transfer shriveled mushrooms to desiccant box for quick, complete drying
4. Reduces desiccant workload

### Method 3: Frost-Free Refrigerator

- Works but is time-consuming
- Not everyone has a frost-free fridge
- Less effective than desiccant

### What NOT to Do

- **Never dry in an oven** -- heat leaches chemical constituents and reduces quality
- **Never use hot air dryers** -- same problem
- Mushrooms dried with desiccant "lose hardly any chemical constituents"

## Storage

- **Seal in plastic bags** or canning jars with tight rubber-edged lids
- **Freezer is ideal** for long-term preservation
- Ensure mushrooms are tightly sealed to protect against freezer moisture
- Properly dried and stored mushrooms "preserve them in their prime for months"
- Spore syringes: store cool and dark; good for several months to over a year

## Spore Printing

Mature specimens (with dark brown gills and purple spore deposits) are good for spore production but weaker for consumption.

### Equipment

- Kerr 1/2 pint wide mouth canning jar (or any suitable jar)
- Micro curved cuticle scissors (cosmetics/drug stores)
- Denatured alcohol, tequila shot glass, eye dropper
- Sterile syringes with water, glass stirring rod (science supply)
- Lid with two holes (one for stirring rod, one for syringe needle)

### Procedure

1. **Pre-sterilize** jar and metal lid in toaster oven at ~300F for 30 minutes; cool; tighten lid
2. **Wash hands** -- hands are "a prime source for bacteria and microspore contaminants"
3. **Sterilize work surfaces** with rubbing alcohol; minimize drafts
4. Run a HEPA air cleaner (99.97% efficiency) for a few hours in a closed room
5. **Flame sterilize scissors** with alcohol flame; snip mushroom cap as far up the stem as possible
6. **Place cap gills-down** on jar bottom using straight pin; lid on loosely
7. **Leave 2 days** in draft-free area away from direct sunlight
8. **Remove cap** with minimal air disturbance; let jar dehumidify a few days with loose lid
