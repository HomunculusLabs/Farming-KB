# Neurotropic Fungi Classification: Four Groups System

## Overview

Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (2000) organized the world's known neurotropic (psychoactive) fungi into four distinct groups based on their chemistry [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] agonism of the indole alkaloids.

### Species Included
- **Amanita muscaria** — The "fly agaric," the most widely known species in this group. Contains ibotenic acid, muscimol, and muscarine (though muscarine is not the primary psychoactive compound). Its distinctive red cap with white spots makes it one of the most recognizable mushrooms in the world.
- **Amanita pantherina** — The "panther cap," contains similar compounds to A. muscaria but is generally considered more toxic.
- **Amanita regalis** — The "royal fly agaric," a closely related species with confirmed psychoactive properties.

### Ecological and Chemical Notes
These species are mycorrhizal, forming symbiotic associations with tree roots, primarily with Pinus and Betula (birch) in the Northern Hemisphere. They are found in temperate and boreal forests, and also in pine plantations [[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]]** — The classic ergot fungus, parasitizing grasses and cereals worldwide. Contains ergotamine, ergocristine, and related alkaloids. Historically responsible for ergotism (St. Anthony's Fire) outbreaks when infested grain was consumed.
