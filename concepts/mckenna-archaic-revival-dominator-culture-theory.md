# McKenna's Archaic Revival and Dominator Culture Theory

In *Food of the Gods*, [[terence-mckenna]] presents a sweeping theory of human cultural evolution organized around a central dichotomy: the **partnership society** (Archaic, goddess-worshipping, plant-using) versus the **dominator society** (patriarchal, hierarchical, alcohol-fueled). This framework explains the historical suppression of visionary plants and the rise of what McKenna called "pathological monotheism."

## The Partnership Model: Paradise

McKenna traces the earliest human societies to a partnership model characterized by:
- **Goddess worship** — fertility figurines found from the Paleolithic through Neolithic across Europe, Anatolia, and the Near East
- **Egalitarian social structure** — minimal hierarchy, communal decision-making
- **Psychedelic plant use** — [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] and other visionary plants as central to religion and social bonding
- **Feminine values** — emphasis on nurturing, community, sexuality, and cyclical time
- **Connection to nature** — humans as participants in the natural order, not conquerors of it

### The Tassili Plateau and Round Head Civilization

McKenna points to the rock art of the Tassili Plateau in the central Sahara (dating from approximately 9,000 to 5,000 years ago) as evidence of an early hallucinogen-using culture. The "Round Head" period paintings depict:
- Human figures with mushroom-like heads and elaborately decorated bodies
- Dancing figures in states of apparent ecstatic trance
- Scenes suggesting ritual use of psychoactive plants
- A culture of abundance and celebration

The Sahara was green and fertile during this period, supporting a rich ecosystem and complex human societies. As desertification advanced, these cultures were forced to migrate — carrying their plant knowledge and religious practices into the Nile Valley, Europe, and beyond.

### Çatal Hüyük and the Neolithic Goddess Culture

The Neolithic city of Çatal Hüyük (c. 7500–5700 BCE) in Anatolia represents for McKenna the high point of the partnership model:
- Elaborate shrine rooms dominated by female figurines and bull imagery
- No defensive walls or weapons — suggesting a peaceful society
- Rich artistic tradition focused on fertility, nature, and the feminine
- Evidence of long-distance trade and cultural exchange
- No evidence of social stratification or kingship

## The Fall: Transition to Dominator Culture

McKenna identifies several factors in the collapse of the partnership model:

### 1. Climate Change and Migration
As the Sahara dried and grasslands expanded, nomadic pastoralism replaced settled agricultural life. The mobility of herding societies favored a more militaristic, hierarchical social structure.

### 2. The Discovery of Alcohol
Fermentation of grain and grapes produced alcohol, which McKenna argues became the psychoactive substance of dominator culture. Unlike psilocybin:
- Alcohol **reinforces ego boundaries** rather than dissolving them
- Alcohol promotes **aggression, dominance displays, and territoriality**
- Alcohol is **compatible with hierarchy** — it enhances the individual's sense of separateness
- Alcohol became associated with **male-dominated ritual** (the symposium, the mead hall)

### 3. The Rise of Kingship and the Male God
The transition from goddess worship to the sky-god religions (first male deities appearing alongside goddesses, then supplanting them) parallels the rise of patriarchal social structures. The Epic of Gilgamesh, written around 2000 BCE, records the transition — the hero Gilgamesh rejects the goddess Ishtar and replaces her worship with male-dominated kingship.

### 4. Abandonment of Visionary Plants
As settled agriculture replaced foraging, people lost direct contact with the psychoactive plants of the wild. The knowledge of plant use became concentrated in specialized priestly castes, and eventually the plants themselves were suppressed as dangerous or demonic.

## The Dominator Model: Paradise Lost

The dominator model, which McKenna argues has been the dominant cultural force for the last 5,000 years, is characterized by:
- **Patriarchy** — male gods, male priesthoods, male political authority
- **Hierarchy** — rigid social stratification with a warrior/ruling class
- **Territorialism** — expansion through conquest, not symbiosis
- **Linear time** — history as progress toward an end point (eschatology), replacing cyclical time
- **Ego reinforcement** — psychoactive substances (alcohol) that strengthen the individual ego
- **Nature as resource** — the natural world as something to be exploited, not participated in
- **Suppression of the feminine** — devaluation of emotion, sexuality, and nature

## Pathological Monotheism

McKenna's most provocative argument is that **monotheism** became the psychological vehicle for the dominator model:
- The "drive for unitary wholeness" within the psyche becomes pathological when the ego-dissolving rites of visionary plants are unavailable
- Monotheism replaces direct [[mystical-experience]] with mediated authority (priesthood, scripture)
- The abstract male god becomes a narcissistic mirror for the dominator ego
- Science inherits both the explanatory power and the moral bankruptcy of this model

## The Four Stages of McKenna's History

McKenna organizes human drug-using history into four epochs:

### I. Paradise (Prehistory to ~5000 BCE)
- Partnership societies, goddess worship, [[archaeology-of-psychedelic-mushroom-use]]
- Psilocybin catalyzes language, religion, social bonding
- Seasonal and lunar cycles organize mushroom availability and ritual

### II. Paradise Lost (~5000 BCE to ~1500 CE)
- Transition to dominator culture; rise of alcohol, kingship, sky-god religions
- Suppression of visionary plants and goddess worship
- Soma in Vedic India, [[eleusinian-mysteries]] in Greece, ergotism in medieval Europe — last remnants of plant-based religion

### III. Hell (~1500 CE to Present)
- Age of exploration brings sugar, coffee, tea, chocolate, opium, tobacco, cocaine, and synthetic drugs
- Global drug trade emerges as a tool of colonialism
- Television and electronic media as the "ultimate drug"
- Drug prohibition and the "war on drugs"

### IV. Paradise Regained? (Future)
- Psychedelic renaissance beginning in the 1950s
- DMT research, [[ayahuasca-tourism]], psychedelic psychotherapy
- Archaic revival — renewed interest in shamanism, plant medicines, and direct mystical experience

## The Archaic Revival

McKenna's proposed solution is the "Archaic Revival" — a conscious return to partnership values:
- Reintegration of visionary plant experiences into culture
- Recovery of the "lost feminine" — valuing emotion, nature, and community
- Rejection of dominator hierarchy in favor of egalitarian partnership
- Recognition that "the world is made of language" — that reality is co-created through our descriptions of it
- A "neo-Archaic" lifestyle that integrates modern knowledge with ancient wisdom

## Significance

McKenna's cultural framework, while speculative and controversial, provides a coherent narrative for understanding:
- Why psychoactive plants were suppressed across cultures
- The connection between patriarchy and the rise of alcohol-based cultures
- The persistent human attraction to visionary experiences despite millennia of prohibition
- The current psychedelic renaissance as a recovery of lost cultural knowledge
- The tension between dominator and partnership values in contemporary society
