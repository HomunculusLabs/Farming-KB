## Rainfall Types — Orographic, Cyclonic, and Convective

## Overview

Rainfall is the most significant form of precipitation for most [[water-conservation]] and storage become critical

### Beyond Averages

Rainfall averages are best used as broad indicators rather than definable limiting factors. Of far more practical use:

- **Distribution**: When does rain fall? Is it concentrated in a wet season or evenly distributed?
- **Extremes**: What are the recorded maximum and minimum rainfall amounts? What are the 100-year flood levels?
- **Intensity**: How hard does it rain? This determines drainage requirements and erosion risk.
- **Reliability**: How much year-to-year variation exists? How many consecutive dry months are possible?

### The Changing Climate

There is a general consensus that world climatic variation (the occurrence of extremes) is increasing, so that more floods, droughts, temperature extremes, and intense wind periods can be expected. Deforestation on a broad or local scale can also cause longer periods of increased aridity. Any strategy to generate, conserve, or store water is therefore critical to the [[permaculture-design]] approach.

## Water Strategy in Design

Given the variability and unpredictability of precipitation, permaculture designers should:

1. **Design for drought**: Choose species and develop water strategies that ensure some yield even in "drier than usual" conditions
2. **Store water**: Implement multiple storage strategies (dams, tanks, soil moisture, groundwater recharge)
3. **Conserve water**: Minimize evaporation through mulching, windbreaks, and appropriate planting
4. **Maximize infiltration**: Design earthworks (swales, contour planting) to capture rain where it falls
5. **Use multiple sources**: Combine rainfall harvesting with dew collection, fog capture, and groundwater where available
6. **Design for flood**: Account for extreme rainfall events in drainage and structural placement
7. **Buffer with diversity**: Spread risk through mixed species, mixed strategies, and multiple water sources

As Mollison notes, a fish population out of water for an hour is as dead as if a year-long drought were in effect — the timing and continuity of water availability can be more important than the total annual amount.
