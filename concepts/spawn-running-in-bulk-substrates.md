Since mycelium grows within the substrate, substrate temperature must
be monitored closely. Thermometers should be placed both in the center
of the substrate (hottest region) and in the room's atmosphere. The
optimum temperature varies by species: Agaricus brunnescens grows
fastest at 77 degrees F, [[pleurotus-ostreatus]] on straw takes 10-14 days, and Lentinus
edodes on logs requires 6-12 months. Spawning must take place under
carefully controlled hygienic conditions, with the working area and
all tools disinfected one day prior using a 10% bleach solution.

## Key Points

- Optimum moisture at spawning: compost 64-66%, straw 70-75%
- Yield depressions occur with moisture deviations greater than 2% from
  optimum
- Substrate temperature peaks on day 7-9 after spawning; keep within
  species-specific range
- Standard spawn rate: 7 liters per ton compost or 1 quart per 8 sq ft
- Commercial growers target 5-8 lbs dry substrate per sq ft
- Cropping efficiency of 80-100% is near maximum yield potential
- Humidity during spawn run must be 90-100%; CO2 at 10,000-15,000 ppm
- Mixed spawning (thorough grain distribution) gives fastest
  colonization
- Colonize as rapidly as possible: once dominant, mycelium secretes
  antibiotics inhibiting competitors
