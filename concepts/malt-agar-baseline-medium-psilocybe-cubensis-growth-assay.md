## Advantages of Malt Agar for Growth Assays

Malt agar offers several advantages as an assay medium:

- **Transparency**: Clear plates allow visual monitoring of mycelial
  growth, colony morphology, and contamination without opening the
  plate. Radial growth rate can be measured directly.

- **Reproducibility**: Commercial malt extract and agar have consistent
  composition, allowing results to be compared across laboratories and
  experiments.

- **Nutrient completeness**: Malt extract contains all major nutrient
  classes required for [[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]] on malt agar between
brassinosteroid-treated and control plates. Standard methods for
measuring growth on agar include:

- **Radial growth rate**: The increase in colony radius per unit time,
  typically measured in mm/day. This is the most common metric and is
  easily measured with a ruler.

- **Colony morphology**: Qualitative assessment of hyphal density,
  sectoring, aerial mycelium formation, and pigmentation.

- **Dry weight measurement**: For more precise quantification, mycelium
  can be scraped from the agar surface, dried, and weighed. This is
  destructive and requires replicate plates.

The study reported that mycelial growth on brassinosteroid-supplemented
