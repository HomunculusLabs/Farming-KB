## Phytophthora infestans and the Irish Famine

The historical importance of Phytophthora infestans cannot be
overstated. This organism triggered the Irish famine of the
1840s, in which a million people starved to death and a million
more emigrated across the Atlantic. The British are often blamed
for the catastrophe, and the fact that millions were maintained
in a state of near-starvation by British landowners is an
appalling episode in history. But placing blame solely on the
British ignores the complexity of the tragedy.

The introduction of the potato to Ireland had allowed the
population to swell to eight million by the 1840s, the majority
utterly dependent on bumper crops of potatoes for sustenance
and as the commodity used to meet rent payments. As the
temperature plunged late in the summer of 1845, and weeks of
fog and continuous rain ensued, Phytophthora got the better of
the crop. The pathogen's predilection for unusually wet climates
made these conditions ideal for epidemic spread.
