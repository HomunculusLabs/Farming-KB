## Environmental Parameters

Temperature is the single most important environmental factor
affecting mycelial growth rate. P. cubensis mycelium grows
optimally at 25-28 degrees C, with growth rate declining
sharply above 32 degrees C and ceasing below 18 degrees C.
Incubation temperatures above 30 degrees C promote metabolite
excretion, visible as yellowish liquid droplets, indicating
physiological stress.

Humidity during colonization should be maintained at 85-95%
to prevent substrate desiccation. For jar-based cultivation,
loose lids covered with foil allow gas exchange while retaining
moisture. For bag-based systems, filter patches or gas-permeable
materials serve the same function.

Light is not required during vegetative colonization, and
darkness may slightly accelerate growth by directing metabolic
resources toward biomass accumulation rather than phototropic
responses. However, ambient light exposure is generally not
harmful during this phase.
