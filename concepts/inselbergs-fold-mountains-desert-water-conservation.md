## Inselbergs and Fold Mountains in Desert Water Conservation

Inselbergs and fold mountains are the most extensive elevated features in many deserts. Both provide critical water catchment opportunities, though they differ significantly in complexity and the strategies required to utilise them effectively.

## Inselbergs and Rock Domes

### Characteristics

Great domes such as Uluru (Ayers Rock) in Australia and similar massifs of granitic or metamorphosed sandstones are relatively simple systems compared to scarps and wadis. They rarely develop deep caves (though some rock shelter exists), rarely have pronounced valley entries, and usually dip steeply into loose sandy soils around the base.

Despite their simplicity, inselbergs are very solid **run-off areas**, some covering many tens of hectares. Large trees and good [[wadi]]-like streams flowing to the plains can be led off to create complex freshwater dam systems

### Rock Palisades

All fold mountains of sedimentary or metamorphic rock show more or less marked palisades of harder rock series running along slopes like sub-contours. These are critically important:

- Vegetation is far **taller, greener, and denser** on the upslope side of palisades because run-off is slowed and forced to infiltrate
- Some are wide-spaced, fairly well on contour, project above the surface, and develop striking tree-lines
- While not as effective as swales, they provide natural water retention

### Headwater Stream Diversion

Fast streams from fold mountains can be bled off to dams or infiltration beds and tree lines. A shallow, fast dry-stream system usually develops out of the upper foothill regions, often only 4-15 metres wide with banks 0.5-2 metres high. The bed is usually rock-filled.

**Critical technique**: Rather than damming violent flows directly (which would fill with boulders and silt in a few rains), bleed off the torrent and divert it to a more peaceful contour flow, then to a dam. An oversized, wide, shallow contour trench causes silt to fall out before reaching the dam, and the trench itself becomes a planting site and easily-cleaned silt trap.

### Bore Water

Wherever series of palisades are a feature, a horizontal bore will often tap sweet water trapped in the rock strata. Unlike quanats, such water can be sealed off and used only as needed, and regulated to the amount taken into the aquifer.
