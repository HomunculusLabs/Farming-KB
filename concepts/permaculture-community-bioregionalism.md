# Permaculture Community and Bioregionalism

updated: 2026-04-18

Permaculture Two (1979) concluding sections articulate Mollison's vision for community-scale permaculture, bioregional organization, and the political philosophy underlying permaculture design.

## The Community Vision

"I see no other solution (political, economic) to the problems of man than the formation of small responsible communities involved in permaculture and appropriate technology, for both individual and competitive enterprise and 'free' energy have failed us."

## Why Community Scale

Society is in a mess: obesity in the west balanced by famine in the third world. Petrol is running out and yet freeways are still being built. Against such universal insanity the only response is to gather together a few friends and commence to build the alternative, on a philosophy of individual responsibility for community survival.

## The Days of Centralized Power Are Numbered

Mollison believes a re-tribalization of society is an inevitable, if sometimes painful, process. The applied theories of politics, economics, and industry have made a sick society. Permaculture is a basic technique for post-industrial evolution and is within the reach of everyman.

## The Energy System

A permaculture system integrated with human settlement provides an inexhaustible energy system, fueled by the sun and developed by the community. Supplemented with appropriate technologies:

- Methane and alcohol fuels
- Dry distillation processes
- Wind, wave, water, or solar energies

Permaculture would provide the basis of a sustainable and regionalized society.

## Principles for Action

For individuals:

- World citizenship, membership of spaceship earth
- Global communication and education
- Aid to others to establish self-reliance, not create dependence
- Self-reliance in ourselves and our group
- Care of the earth
- Absolutely no waste products, hence no "unemployed"
- Adoption of the most sophisticated environmental principles known
- A moratorium on freeways, arms, centralized power, and non-principled energy export
- Gradual removal of tariffs, passports, visas, and impediments to travel
- Open media devoted to spreading these principles

## For Intentional Communities

- Groups limited to 300-3,000 people (no more or less)
- At least 5 locations for each group, one to be an "overseas" center
- Groups never to have a fixed boundary
- Community ownership of land and public resources
- Life leases on homes and gardens (the cruel myth of "ownership" of resources and people is where we have lost touch with reality)
- Regional groups choose specialized "trade" manufactures suited to local resources, skills, inclinations, and markets
- Every house and town made self-sufficient
- Teams from stabilized areas advance into disaster areas and the third world
- Global federations of specialized groups in travel and trade, exchange of skills

## The Permaculture Institute

Tagari purchased 78 acres of coastal swamp and dryland in Stanley, Tasmania (from January 1979). Plans:

- Main land area devoted to demonstrating techniques of planting and design
- Still-pond culture as a feature
- Species of sub-tropical to cool temperate range (no frosts)
- Partly self-supporting through admission charges, schools, visitors, seed and plant sales
- Commercial crops: nuts, some fruits, water plants, aquatic birds/poultry

## Permaculture Quarterly

Edited by Terry White at 37 Goldsmith St., Maryborough, Victoria. Lists requests, supplies, exchange news and views. Covers permaculture, appropriate technology, and community. Local suppliers may obtain one free listing of useful species. Groups wanting help can call for assistance.

## Standard Designs Program

A series of documented design sheets covering:

- Landscape (rock dome planting, tidal flat ponds, flatland dam and house site)
- Pests and problems (mosquito control, blackberry control, fox predation, planting with rabbits)
- Annual garden (tomato/asparagus polyculture, herb spiral, potato production)
- Marketing (farm-link town with country, wayside marketing, self-pick sales)
- Techniques (pruning, domestic sewage, community sewage, broadscale planting, tyre uses, fuel from plants)
- Structures (trellis and sun traps, rock dome seepage water, shadehouse, attached glasshouse)
- Species lists (poultry forage, barrier hedges, cattle forage, pig forage, seacoast species)
- Urban strategies (contract cropping, dispersed tree crops, dispersed livestock, public allotments)

## The World Self-Reliance Volunteers

"Enough" is defined as: a warm place, good nutrition (hence health), plenty of information, many friends, a meaningful task or two, and reliance on the group, hence absolute security. "Perhaps the greatest truth is that we can only own the resources we give to others."

## The Lateral Thinkers

"All of those mentioned care more for health, land, and humanity than for themselves, and all are busy people in many fields; all are 'lateral thinkers' or multidisciplinary in the best sense."

Key references include Jacques Cousteau, Sir MacFarlane Burnett, the organic farming movement, and the Down to Earth Movement (Jim Cairns and Juni Morosi).
