# JADAM Natural Pesticide Institute and Research

JADAM's technical achievements emerged not from a well-funded research
facility but from an "unsophisticated and underinvested facility" powered by
collective intelligence. The JADAM Natural Pesticide Institute, combined
with its network of member farmers, has produced a remarkable body of
agricultural innovations that are shared openly with the global farming
community.

## The Institute and Its Philosophy

JADAM developed all its farming inputs in a modest facility with minimal
investment. The organization could establish its technical system quickly
because member farmers with extensive field experience actively participated
in the development process. This is the power of sharing: thousands of
farmers contributing observations, experiments, and field results from
diverse growing conditions.

JADAM's founder, [[fungal-sampling-methods]], established JADAM in 1991. Over two
decades, the organization has grown to include tens of thousands of member
farmers who interact constantly through the internet, sharing information,
guidance, and answers via smartphones and other devices. The organization's
slogan is "ultra-low-cost" (ULC), and all knowledge is freely shared
through the website en.jadam.kr, which has been running since 2003.

## Collaborative Research with RDA

JADAM's credibility is strengthened by collaboration with the Korean Rural
Development Administration (RDA), the official agricultural research body
of South Korea. This partnership enabled JADAM to present detailed
numerical analysis of its inputs, providing scientific validation of
effectiveness. JADAM thanks Dr. Hyeongjin Ji, former Organic Farming
Division Director, and Nanhee An of the RDA for their support.

The RDA has also conducted extensive research on cover crops used in the
JADAM system, including hairy vetch, milk vetch, rye, Sudan grass, and
rapeseed, providing data on nitrogen fixation, biomass production, and
soil improvement effects that support JADAM recommendations.

## Products and Methods Developed

The JADAM Natural Pesticide Institute has developed numerous products and
methods, all shared publicly:

**Natural pesticide ingredients:**
- Natural wetting agent (JWA) made without heating
- Natural sulfur (JS) made without heating
- Natural pesticide against rice bakanae disease
- Natural pesticide against canker and leaf spot
- Natural pesticide against powdery mildew and downy mildew
- Natural pesticide against aphids and mites
- Natural pesticide against tobacco moth and beet armyworm
- Natural pesticide against stinkbug and mealybug
- Natural pesticide against slug and snail
- Natural pesticide against citrus latid planthopper and leafhopper
- Natural pesticide against rice water weevil
- Naphthalene pesticide for livestock fly control
- Comprehensive pesticide programs for rice and fruit trees

**Fertilizer and soil inputs:**
- Natural microorganism solution made with potatoes
- Natural microorganism solution made with mixed grains
- Natural liquid fertilizer made without sugar or molasses
- Complete fertilizer program for crops

**Livestock applications:**
- Eliminating flies from livestock housings
- Organic pig and poultry farming methods

## The Brainstorming Method

JADAM developed a simple method of scientific experiment that farmers can
easily follow. The approach involves systematic testing of pesticide
formulations by member farmers across diverse conditions. JADAM staff
visited member households, collected data, and iteratively refined
formulations based on real-world results.

A notable example is the discovery that Jerusalem artichoke has pesticide
properties. Mr. Jeongho Gim of Gimcheon first found that Jerusalem
artichoke was not only effective for diabetes but also had pesticide
effects. JADAM interviewed him, validated the finding, and incorporated
it into the system. This discovery was entirely new; there was no prior
information anywhere mentioning Jerusalem artichoke's pesticidal
properties.

The natural pesticide workshop is held regularly and has become a major
source of both revenue and innovation. Over 7 years, the workshop has
been a "wild success," with many people supporting JADAM through
participation. Participants contribute their own field observations and
experiments, continuously expanding JADAM's knowledge base.

## Bongha Village as Proof of Concept

Bongha village in Korea serves as JADAM's large-scale demonstration. Under
the leadership of Jeongho Gim, 160 households farm 134 hectares (330 acres)
using [[jadam-methods]]. They have successfully grown rice for over 5 years
organically with pesticide costs of only 9 dollars per acre (4 applications
total). For persimmon production over the same area, annual pesticide cost
was approximately 2,300 dollars total, or 7 dollars per acre.

This scale proves that JADAM's system works not only for small-scale
market gardens but for large commercial operations. The village uses
machinery for pesticide application, demonstrating compatibility with
modern farming equipment.

## Open Knowledge and the Patent-Free Approach

JADAM's founder explicitly chose not to patent key discoveries including
JADAM sulfur and JADAM wetting agent. The belief is that agricultural
knowledge is a public asset shared by humanity. Everything JADAM has
developed is shared through books, the website, smartphone applications,
and workshops. Under patent law, knowledge that has been publicly
disclosed cannot be patented, ensuring JADAM's methods remain free for
all farmers.

JADAM plans to build a farm and research center that practices both
organic crop and livestock farming, further developing and demonstrating
the ultra-low-cost system. The knowledge base continues to grow as more
farmers join and contribute their experiences from diverse climates,
crops, and growing conditions around the world.

## Future Directions

The JADAM system is described as constantly evolving, not only about
techniques but about methodology, principles, and philosophy. New methods
and techniques are continuously incorporated based on member contributions.
The website serves as a real-time platform where farmers learn from each
other, and all methods are evaluated against the SESE criteria: simple,
easy, scientific, and effective.
