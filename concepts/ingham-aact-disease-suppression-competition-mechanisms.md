## Four Mechanisms of Disease Suppression

### 1. Competition for Food Resources

The primary control point in disease suppression is food. Pathogens and
beneficial organisms compete for the same carbon sources on leaf surfaces,
in the phyllosphere, and in the rhizosphere. When AACT is applied, it
introduces large populations of beneficial bacteria and fungi that rapidly
consume available food resources. With their food supply depleted,
pathogenic organisms cannot multiply to damaging levels.

This mechanism operates continuously. As long as beneficial populations
remain dominant, pathogens are held in check through simple resource
deprivation. The plant continuously provides new exudates through its roots
and leaf surfaces, but these are preferentially consumed by the established
beneficial community. New pathogens arriving on wind or water find the food
resources already claimed.

### 2. Competition for Space

Both above ground (on leaf surfaces and stems) and below ground (on root
surfaces), physical space is limited. Beneficial organisms introduced
through AACT colonize available surfaces, forming protective biofilms. This
physical occupation of space prevents pathogenic organisms from finding
attachment points.
