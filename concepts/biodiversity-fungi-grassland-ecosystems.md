Grassland ecosystems, including prairies, savannas, steppes, and

## Grassland Soil Characteristics and Fungal Habitats

Grassland soils are enriched by organic accretion throughout the soil
profile, especially from grass roots that are annual, finely divided,
and contribute a biomass that far exceeds above-ground biomass. This
root-dominated organic input creates a unique substrate environment for
fungal decomposers and symbionts.

In mesic or wet grasslands and in most shrublands and heathlands, the
superficial debris is underlain by an amorphous, aromatic layer of
organic material (humus) that rests on mineral soil impregnated with
humus.

grasslands, and some forest communities, the above-ground debris often
consists of relatively intact organic matter (litter) only, creating
different conditions for fungal colonization.

## Grassland Fungal Diversity

Fungal diversity in grasslands is shaped by the unique combination of
warm-season and cool-season grasses, forbs, and legumes that compose
the plant community. Saprotrophic fungi dominate in terms of biomass,
contributing approximately 78 to 90 percent of total decomposer biomass
in grassland soils.

Common saprotrophic genera include species from the Eurotiales,
Sordariales, Onygenales, and numerous mitosporic fungi. Basidiomycetes
are prominent decomposers of grass litter, although they have
historically been described as "the missing link in soil mycology"
because culture-based methods severely underrepresent them.
