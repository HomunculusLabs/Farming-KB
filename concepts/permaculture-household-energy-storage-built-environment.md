## Permaculture Household Energy Storage, Built Environment, and Cultural Capital

This page covers energy storage at the household and community scale, the built environment as energy storage, cultural and informational forms of energy storage, appropriate use of non-renewable resources, [[garden-soil-fertility-mineral-balance-solomon]] in soils, and the attitudes and values needed for [[holmgren-energy-descent-culture-scavenging-modernity-rubbish-tip]].

## Household Storages of Energy

### Traditional Storage Forms

[[permaculture]] encourages household and local economic self-reliance, rebuilding traditional energy storage in food, fuel, and other resources:

- **Seedbox** — order and diversity reflect efforts to save, exchange, and purchase potential for future abundance
- **Pantry and cellar** — captures essence of a bountiful season, insurance against a bad one
- **Firewood stack** — green wood added at one end, seasoned wood used from the other; represents abundance of nature and honest work slowly maturing

### Characteristics of Household Storages

These types of storage are by their nature:
- **Diverse** — many types and forms
- **Small** — human-scale
- **Dispersed** — spread across many households
- **Easily used** — no specialized technology needed
- **Unattractive to thieves** — not rich or portable enough to monopolize

A nation full of such distributed wealth is much more secure and stable than one dependent on centralized supermarket and electricity grid supply.

## The Built Environment as Energy Storage

### The Problem with Modern Storage

Modern society has been very active in catching and storing energy in "stuff" — buildings, infrastructure, technology. Unfortunately:
- Most stores are useless without the rest of the industrial complex
- They mostly encourage more energy consumption and waste
- Energy-consuming buildings and freeways rely on constant fossil energy flow
- Even computers may depend on continuous redundancy and replacement

### Design Criteria for Built Energy Storage

When developing tools, buildings, and infrastructure, aim to emulate natural storage characteristics:

1. **Modest in scale** — appropriate to needs, not oversized
2. **Long life and/or renewable materials** — designed for durability
3. **Simple to maintain** — not necessarily maintenance-free
4. **Multi-purpose and adaptable** — easy to retrofit for other uses

### Melliodora Examples

- Maximum use of earth and stoneworks for terraces, dams, paths
- Resisted building too many fences, outbuildings, and structures needing continuous renewal
- Buildings pack more functions and hours of use through working-from-home lifestyle
- Each building is multi-purpose and constructed for easy internal retrofitting
- Extensive use of earth and wood as locally available, renewable materials
- Minimized pumps, active devices, and technology in essential building functions

### Maintenance Engineering

The costs of maintaining existing built assets are beginning to erode economic wealth. Maintenance engineering has a bright future, but solutions will not be easy because most assets were designed assuming cheap energy.

## Energy Storage in Culture

### The Weightless Economy Illusion

Information and organization in government, economy, community, and culture are thought to be ethereal "goods" unrelated to energy. But their proliferation has been essential to our high-energy fossil-fuel society.

In nature, long food chains transform solar energy into increasingly complex forms — beehive organization, forest structure, hunting skills, biodiversity. The EMERGY (embodied energy) required to create these complex forms is very large. Human governance, economy, education, and culture follow the same energy rules.

### Financial Capital

Financial capital is the most potent form of non-material wealth. Rapid movement of capital worldwide and focus on destructive short-term investment has been recognized as the most dysfunctional element of modern capitalism. Much financial value may be illusory — so disconnected from real wealth sources.

**[[ethical-investment-and-earth-banking]]** is one of the fastest-growing financial services areas, constrained more by shortage of appropriate businesses meeting social/environmental criteria than by shortage of investors.

## Appropriate Use of Non-Renewable Resources

### The Strategic Principle

The best use for non-renewable resources should be to **establish a system**, rather than to maintain or harvest it, even if establishment is gradual over a lifetime or generations.

### Examples

**[[comparison-passive-solar-vs-earth-sheltered]] houses:** Use high-embodied energy in glass to capture solar energy while providing natural lighting and views — good use of non-renewable energy.

**Earth-moving equipment:** Bulldozers create well-designed water harvesting/distribution systems and vehicle access that increase landscape productivity. These earthworks can be maintained indefinitely by human labor if necessary.

**Soil rehabilitation:** Deep ripping of hard pans followed by well-designed plantings to maintain soil structure long-term.

### Mineral Fertilizers for Building Soil

A special case of using non-renewable resources to improve long-term soil productivity:

- Most modern fertility attempts are short-term (crop feeding, not soil feeding)
- If soil mineral balancing principles are better understood, re-mineralization can create permanent improvement
- Rock minerals are powerful medicine — easily overused or misused
- Permaculturalists who ignore mineral balance may design systems that don't provide essential livestock and human health minerals

### Soil Testing Methods at Melliodora

- Albrecht-method [[soil-testing]]
- Refractometer testing of plant sap
- Broadly based observation skills
- Current focus on refining biological approaches (including [[biodynamic-preparations]]) given reasonable basic mineral balance

## Key Mineral Balance Principles

1. **Level and balance are different measures** — both necessary for fertility understanding
2. **Ideal balanced soil** supports full range of climate-appropriate crops productively
3. **Calcium-magnesium balance** is most powerful factor in soil friability, water storage, and biological productivity
4. **Calcium-potassium balance** determines whether vegetation is lush/herbaceous or woody/fibrous
5. **High calcium** → soft, lush, palatable herbs; sweet, long-keeping fruits; rapid bacterial humus formation
6. **High potassium** → fibrous, less palatable plants; acidic, shorter-keeping fruits; woody plants do well; fungal decay dominates; forest litter accumulates as dry fuel
7. **pH of 6.5** indicates potential balance but doesn't guarantee it
8. **Fertilizing to maintain balance differs from fertilizing to establish balance** — good result from one application doesn't mean more is better

## Attitudes and Values for Energy Descent

### Contributing to Descent Culture

- Acknowledging useful contributions from outside any particular discipline
- Learning from knowledge systems outside scientific rationalism
- Remaining skeptical of official authority unless supported by local, networked credentials
- Acknowledging validity of pre-existing cultures of place
- Contributing to evolution of local culture — supporting local knowledge, food, art
- Using media and IT with skeptical caution — "the icing on the cake, not the cake itself"

### Characteristics of Future Sustainable Cultures

- **Bioregional** political and economic structures with renewed geographic diversity
- **Cross-fertilized** biogenetically, racially, culturally, intellectually — giving natural hybrid vigor
- **Accessible** and low dependence on expensive centralized technology
- **Developable incrementally** with feedback and refinement
