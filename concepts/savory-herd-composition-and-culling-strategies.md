## Calving and Breeding Season Timing

The timing of calving and breeding seasons has major implications for both
supplemental feed costs and animal performance. In conventional American
ranching, cows are made to calve very early in the year, a policy established
years ago to avoid excessive calf mortality from screw worms. Although the
screw worm is no longer the problem it was, many ranchers continue the same
policy. Cows reach peak nutritional demand at the worst time of year, when
forage quality is at its lowest.

A different approach, brought about by shifting the time of breeding
combined with biological planning across many paddocks, places cows at their
toughest with lowest nutritional needs at the worst time of year. In such
cases, annual supplementary feed costs can be drastically lowered. Such a
change requires careful planning over a few seasons as dollars are shifted
from supplementation to fencing for a higher marginal reaction in the long
run.
