Source: David Holmgren, *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* (2002)

## The Core Framework

Holmgren restates the environmentalist slogan "thinking globally, acting locally" as **"top-down thinking, bottom-up action"** -- a framework that describes both permaculture design methodology and a new mode of understanding power in complex systems.

### Permaculture Design Application
Permaculture design begins with the most concrete and mundane aspects of material existence through self-audit (Principle 4). The garden provides a laboratory for exploring change processes. Top-down thinking means understanding the big picture (global energy peak, ecological limits, system dynamics) while acting from the ground up (garden, household, community).

### As Power Analysis
There is abundant evidence that "global power elites" have already learned this new mode of understanding and action. A shift from evident, formal, managerial exercise of power to invisible, informal, collaborative modes allows elites to bypass democratic and bureaucratic controls.

When elites overcome their own egos to see they are just small players in vast human systems, they recognise that collaborative and subtle exercise of power is more effective than threats and brute force.

## Systemic Understanding vs Conspiracy Theory

### The Problem with Conspiracy Theories
Political and economic analysts generally misunderstand events through two frameworks:
1. **Random market forces**: events as "almost random aggregations of market and political forces" representing balanced democratic outcomes
2. **Conspiracy theories**: events as fully controlled outcomes by particular elite groupings using managerial methods

Both are inadequate. Conspiracy theories assume exposure of the persons at the centre will remedy adverse effects. A more sophisticated understanding is needed.

### Guilds of Power
World events tend to be driven by **loose coalitions of economic, political, and military interests** that function like guilds of species in an ecosystem. These guilds generate patterns of events meeting their interests without unity of purpose or clear plan.

When powerful players accept they are not all-powerful, they increase their effectiveness while being able to deny responsibility for adverse outcomes.

### Historical Examples
- During the Cold War, the CIA and KGB functioned as complementary and self-reinforcing institutions rather than representing respective national interests
- The CIA term "blowback" describes adverse consequences of agents of influence who turn against their sponsors
- Liberal-left analysts interpret blowback as CIA incompetence (needing more top-down managerial control); a wholistic understanding sees eruptions by drug barons, dictators, and movements like the Taliban as essential to sustain the power structure

### EMERGY Exploitation
Howard Odum's EMERGY evaluations can be used (and probably have been) by global corporations to identify free and undervalued resources for exploitation. When systemic thinking is a skill in the hands of a few, it can and will be used for good and ill.

## Post-September 11 Analysis (2002)

### The New Mode of Power
A barrage of propaganda from mainstream media denigrates any systemic understanding of political processes as "conspiracy theories." This ensures all evidence is evaluated through reductionist logic which separates causes and effects, processes and products, system and element.

September 11 was, in Holmgren's view, an outcome of shadowy coalitions linking global energy corporations, US foreign policy, the global "intelligence community," Islamic fundamentalists, arms dealers, and the illegal drug trade.

### Fundamentalism and Globalization
Dr Sherif Youssef Hetata, Egyptian activist against religious fundamentalism, pointed out that globalization as delivered by hegemonic Western power not only drives the rise of religious fundamentalism but shares many characteristics with it. Holmgren maps this onto the Ethical Principles figure as the "destructive pathway to the emergent union of our materialist and spiritual aspects."

### The Sustainable Alternatives Iceberg
During the 1990s, social and environmental movements lost ground through declining effectiveness of top-down bureaucratic control. New power excesses are exercised through proxies, lobbyists, agents provocateurs, think tanks, public relations, and identification of invisible allies in grassroots movements.

However, those same movements have had extraordinary success using limited resources through:
- Small but significant consumer and shareholder boycotts
- Culture jamming
- Empowering and celebrating sustainable alternatives
- Creating sustainable alternatives that expose "the big lie that we and our descendants are all beholden to this global hegemonic power for our very existence"

### Historical Parallel
Social democratic movements of the late 19th and early 20th centuries mastered the then-novel model of **bottom-up thinking and top-down action** to control corporatist and fascist power. Today the anti-globalisation movement is "the tip of the sustainable alternatives iceberg" with potential to stop "titanic forces precipitating global Armageddon after the oil peak."

## Implications for Permaculture Practice

### For Designers
Understanding top-down thinking and bottom-up action means:
1. Always consider the larger system context before designing details
2. Start implementation with what is immediately at hand (bottom-up)
3. Recognise that small, well-placed interventions can have large systemic effects
4. Be aware that powerful actors may co-opt or suppress grassroots alternatives
5. Focus on creating visible, viable alternatives rather than just opposing existing systems

### The Anti-Globalisation Connection
The so-called anti-globalisation movement is not simply oppositional -- it represents a network of viable alternatives to the global corporate model. Permaculture demonstration sites, local food systems, community currencies, and cooperative enterprises are all part of this "iceberg."

### Self-Reliance as Strategy
Creating household and community self-reliance is not withdrawal from the world but the most powerful form of political action within the top-down/bottom-up framework. "The creation of sustainable alternatives exposes the big lie."
