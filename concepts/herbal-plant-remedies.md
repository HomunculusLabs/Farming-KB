# Herbal Plant Remedies

Simple traditional herbal remedies that can be made by people with small gardens without great difficulty, as described by Sepp Holzer. These were once found in almost every "home pharmacy." As the potency of medicinal plants can vary from place to place, recipes should be adapted — with experience, the correct strength can easily be determined.

## Calendula Salve (Calendula officinalis)

A wound-healing and anti-inflammatory salve made from the whole calendula plant.

### Recipe

1. Cut two heaped double handfuls of calendula (whole plant including stem, leaves, and flowers) finely
2. Heat roughly half a litre of lard (available from a butcher) in a pan
3. Carefully fry the calendula in the lard, keeping it moving
4. Alternative: use olive oil or other vegetable oil instead of lard
5. Cover the mixture and leave to stand for one day
6. Lightly warm, then filter through a cloth into a container

### If using vegetable oil (not lard)

You must incorporate a thickening agent such as beeswax:
- For one litre of oil: use 200-250g of beeswax, already warmed and melted
- Mix the melted wax into the filtered calendula oil well
- Leave to cool
- More wax = stiffer salve; less wax = creamier consistency

### Uses

Calendula salve treats all kinds of injuries — it encourages wounds to heal and keeps inflammation down. One of the most versatile and gentle first-aid remedies.

## Lemon Thyme and Thyme Oil

An old remedy for sprains, rheumatism, and stroke recovery.

### Recipe

1. Pick sprigs of thyme during dry weather at midday (when the scent is most intense)
2. Place sprigs in a bottle with cold-pressed sunflower or olive oil
3. Oil should be about two fingers above the sprigs
4. Leave the bottle in a sunny place (e.g., a windowsill) for 14 days
5. Sieve out the flowers with a cloth

### For stronger oil

Repeat the process with new flowers in the same oil.

### Uses

- Rubbed in regularly to the affected area for sprains and rheumatism
- Recommended for people who have suffered strokes
- **Caution**: use on children only with caution — watch for possible reactions on sensitive skin

## Chicory Tea for Diabetics (Cichorium intybus)

A traditional tea used by farmers to help manage blood sugar levels.

### Recipe

1. Combine equal amounts of:
   - Chicory root (Cichorium intybus)
   - Dandelion root
   - Stinging nettles
   - French lilac (goat's rue)
   - Bilberry leaves
2. Use three tablespoons of the plant mixture per one litre of water
3. Steep in hot water for a short period only
4. Can be taken every day

### Uses

Chicory was traditionally used by diabetics. Farmers also made fresh juice from it — only a teaspoonful was taken to lower blood sugar levels. Note: this is a traditional remedy, not a substitute for medical treatment.

## Tormentil Powder (Potentilla erecta)

A hemostatic (bleeding-stopping) remedy from dried tormentil roots.

### Preparation

1. Dig up tormentil roots (Potentilla erecta)
2. Dry the roots thoroughly
3. Grind into a fine powder using a coffee mill — the finer the better
4. Store in a sealed jar

### Uses

Apply the powder directly to heavily bleeding wounds. The powder stops bleeding effectively, and wounds heal very well without leaving large scars.

## Bone Salve (from Sepp Holzer's childhood)

An old traditional remedy made from rendered animal bones, used for wounded livestock.

### Preparation

1. Obtain bones from a slaughterhouse
2. Place bones on a grill and smoke them
3. Set up a two-pot system: upper pot (with bone fragments on a grill) sits above a lower pot that collects the rendered salve
4. Seal the join between the two pots with wet mossy loam to protect from heat
5. Carefully regulate the fire to slowly render the bone fragments
6. A glutinous brown salve collects in the bottom pot; used-up bone remains in the top as light grey flakes

### Uses

- Treatment of wounded livestock
- Watered-down form spread on draught animals to protect from flies and horseflies at haymaking/harvest times
- Deterrent against bark stripping in forest cultures
- Protection for fruit trees from rodent gnawing — mix with linseed oil, fresh cow dung, slaked lime, and very fine quartz sand to paintable consistency

## Sage Tea (Salvia officinalis)

Mentioned by Holzer as a well-established remedy. Sage nutrient content is greatest when grown in a sunny place without fertiliser.

### Uses

- Sore mouth or throat
- Digestion problems
