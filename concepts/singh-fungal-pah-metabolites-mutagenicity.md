# Fungal Metabolites of PAHs and Their Mutagenicity

[[singh-fungal-metabolism-pahs]] of [[singh-fungal-biodegradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]] hydrocarbons (PAHs) produces a
diverse array of metabolites with varying degrees of toxicity and
mutagenicity. Understanding the metabolic pathways and the toxicological
properties of these metabolites is critical for evaluating the safety and
effectiveness of [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-pleurotus-ostreatus]], and Cyclothyrium sp. Each species
produces distinct metabolite profiles reflecting different enzyme
systems and metabolic capabilities.

### Benz[a]anthracene Metabolism

[[staycare-cunninghamella-elegans-in-bioremediation]] metabolizes benz[a]anthracene to metabolites
including trans-3,4-dihydrodiol (4%), trans-8,9-dihydrodiol (90%),
trans-10,11-dihydrodiol (6%), and tetraol derivatives. Benz[a]anthracene
tetraol was identified as a metabolite, representing the first report of
biologically produced benz[a]anthracene tetraol. This tetraol may be an
additional oxidation product of the 8,9- or 10,11-dihydrodiol pathway.

### Benzo[a]pyrene Metabolism

Benzo[a]pyrene is one of the most potent carcinogens. Of 17 strains of
filamentous fungi screened, six species eliminated more than 40% of
benzo[a]pyrene at 25 degrees C in 17 days. A rare fungus, Marasmiellus
troyanus, achieved up to 95% removal. Candida lipolytica detected 3- and
9-hydroxybenzo[a]pyrenes within 2 days at an initial concentration of
200 mg/l.

The relative degradation per unit biomass for three mitosporic fungi was:
[[fusarium-solani]] (17%), and [[fusarium-oxysporum]]
(8%). T. viride degraded 50% of benzo[a]pyrene without detectable laccase
or peroxidase activities, suggesting a different metabolic pathway. F.
solani can store benzo[a]pyrene in intracellular vesicles independently of
degradation rate, with about 5.3% incorporated into the biomass.

An alternative metabolic pathway based on free radical production,
including reactive oxygen species (ROS) and hydroxyl radicals, has been
hypothesized as agents for initiating benzo[a]pyrene oxidation.
Potassium cyanide and piperonyl butoxide (both P450 and peroxidase
inhibitors) enhanced benzo[a]pyrene degradation significantly, suggesting
that increased availability of H2O2 plays an important role.

## Cytochrome P450 Involvement

F. solani oxidizes benzo[a]pyrene in the C1, C3, and C6 positions,
producing 1,6- and 3,6-benzo[a]pyrene quinones. These metabolites
suggest the involvement of cytochrome P450 in the oxidation process. Two
metabolites identified as quinone derivatives indicate that P450-mediated
epoxidation followed by rearrangement is a key metabolic route in
filamentous fungi.

## Conjugation Reactions

Fungi produce various conjugated metabolites from PAHs:

- **Sulfate conjugates**: Water-soluble sulfates formed during pyrene
  metabolism, including pyrene-1-sulfate and pyrene-1,6-disulfate
- **Glucoside conjugates**: Glucose-conjugated metabolites detected from
  PAH metabolism by C. elegans
- **Glucuronide conjugates**: Glucuronic acid-conjugated metabolites,
  particularly from methyl-substituted PAHs by Syncephalastrum
  racemosum

These conjugation reactions generally reduce the toxicity and
bioavailability of singh fungal pah metabolites mutagenicity, facilitating their excretion or
further degradation.

## Mutagenicity of Fungal Metabolites

### Toxic Metabolites

Most fungal metabolites produced from PAH metabolism are less toxic than
the parent compounds, which is an encouraging result for the
detoxification process. However, oxidation products such as quinones or
hydroxy derivatives are often more toxic and mutagenic:

- **Benz[a]anthracene trans-3,4-dihydrodiol**: More mutagenic than the
  parent compound
- **Benzo[a]pyrene trans-7,8-dihydrodiol 9,10-epoxide**: Highly
  mutagenic intermediate
- **7,12-Dimethylbenz[a]anthracene trans-3,4-dihydrodiol**: More
  mutagenic or carcinogenic than parent compound
- **1-Hydroxy-3-methylcholanthrene trans-9,10-dihydrodiol**: More
  mutagenic than parent compound
- **1-Hydroxypyrene**: Initial pyrene metabolite showing cytotoxic and
  mutagenic properties from Crinipellis stipitaria

### Reduction of Mutagenicity

In C. elegans, the mutagenic activity of five metabolites of fluoranthene
toward Salmonella typhimurium decreased with time but was not eliminated.
Similar reductions in mutagenic activities of benz[a]anthracene,
7,12-dimethylbenz[a]anthracene, benzo[a]pyrene, and 3-methylcholanthrene
have been shown without total elimination.

The mutagenicity of 7,12-dimethylbenz[a]anthracene is reduced in
cultures of Syncephalastrum racemosum after biotransformation to
glucuronide and sulfate conjugates. This demonstrates the protective role
of conjugation reactions in reducing mutagenic potential.

### Nonmutagenic Conjugates

Sulfate, glucuronide, and glucoside conjugates produced from PAH
metabolism by fungi generally exhibit nonmutagenic properties. However,
certain microorganisms contain hydrolytic enzymes that can eliminate the
conjugative groups in the environment, potentially restoring the toxicity
of the original metabolites. This highlights the importance of complete
mineralization rather than mere transformation in bioremediation
strategies.
