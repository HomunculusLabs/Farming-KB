## Leaf Shape Genetics: Webbed vs. Compound-Pinnate

Variation in leaf shape among Cannabis strains provides one of the clearest
examples of Mendelian inheritance in the genus. Robert Connell Clarke
documented a classic breeding experiment involving two true-breeding
populations: one with webbed leaves and one with normal compound-pinnate
leaves.

In this system, the gene for compound-pinnate leaves (designated W) is
dominant over the gene for webbed leaves (designated w). When a cross is made
between a true-breeding compound-pinnate parent (WW) and a true-breeding
webbed-leaf parent (ww), all first-generation (F1) offspring exhibit
compound-pinnate leaves because the dominant W gene masks the recessive w gene.

When two F1 offspring (Ww) are crossed, the second generation (F2) shows the
classic Mendelian ratio of approximately 3:1, with three plants showing
compound-pinnate leaves for every one showing webbed leaves. This demonstrates
that leaf shape is controlled by a single gene pair with complete dominance.

If incomplete dominance were involved, the F1 generation would show
intermediate leaf forms, and the F2 ratio would shift to 1:2:1
(compound-pinnate : intermediate : webbed). The back-cross technique, crossing
an F1 plant back to one of the parental (P1) types, produces a predictable 1:1
ratio that helps breeders determine the genotype of parental plants.
