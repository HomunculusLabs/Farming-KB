# Cold House Winter Harvest System

The cold house winter harvest system, developed by Eliot Coleman at Four Season Farm in
Harborside, Maine (USDA Zone 5), demonstrates that fresh vegetables can be produced
year-round in cold climates using unheated greenhouses combined with simple, low-cost
technologies. The system is built on three foundational components that work in synergy:
cold-hardy vegetables, [[coleman-protected-cultivation]] through double-
[[coleman-double-cover-microclimate-protection]]).

## The Three Components

### Cold-Hardy Vegetables

The first component is the selection of appropriate plant species. Cold-hardy vegetables
are those that tolerate and even thrive in cold temperatures. Many of these species
naturally grow in mild winter climates without any protection at all. The cold house
system takes these naturally cold-tolerant species and extends their growing range into
much harsher climates by providing minimal but effective shelter. (see [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], or the South.

The succession approach means that greenhouses are never left empty. Within twenty-four
hours of harvesting a crop, the bed is cleared, the soil is prepared, and the next
crop is planted. Careful records are maintained to ensure varied crop rotation and to
refine sowing dates from year to year.

### Protected Cultivation (Double-Layer System)

The third and most innovative component is the double-layer protection system. The
outer layer is a standard unheated hoop house (gothic-arch, pipe-frame, plastic-
covered). Coleman's commercial houses are 30 feet wide and 96 feet long, aligned on
an east-west axis. The inner layer is floating row cover material (spunbonded
polyester) supported by flat-topped wire wickets at approximately 12 inches above the
soil surface, spaced every 4 feet along the length of 30-inch-wide growing beds.

This double-layer system creates a "twice-tempered" climate. When the outdoor
temperature drops to -15°F (-26°C), the air temperature inside the greenhouse drops
to approximately 28-32°F (-2 to 0°C), and the temperature under the inner row cover
layer drops only to 15-18°F (-10 to -8°C). This temperature modification is sufficient
to protect cold-hardy vegetables from freeze damage while keeping the soil from
freezing solid.

The inner layer also increases relative humidity, which provides additional freeze
protection and reduces moisture stress on the plants. The soil itself acts as a thermal
mass, absorbing heat during sunny days and releasing it slowly at night. No artificial
