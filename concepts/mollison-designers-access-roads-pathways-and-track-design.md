    15|# Access Roads, Pathways, and Track Design
    16|
    17|Access design is one of the first and most consequential decisions in any
    18|permaculture layout. Mollison treats roads, tracks, and pathways not merely as
    19|transportation infrastructure but as primary elements that shape water flow,
    20|determine land use patterns, create or destroy microclimates, and define the
    21|framework within which all other elements are arranged. Poorly placed roads are
    22|among the most common and costly [[mollison-type-one-errors-in-design]] in land design. Access design integrates closely with
    23|[[mollison-designers-road-water-harvesting-and-integration]],
    24|[[mollison-settlement-siting-on-slopes-and-landscape-profile]],
    25|and [[mollison-zone-planning-frequency-distance-and-placement]].
    26|
    27|## Principles of Access Design
    28|
    29|Mollison establishes several core principles that govern all access design:
    30|
    31|- **Access comes first**: Before placing houses, gardens, dams, or forests,
    32|  establish the access network. Roads are permanent, expensive to modify, and
    33|  profoundly influence the function of everything they connect. The
    34|  [[mollison-pdc-design-report-methodology]] sequence always includes
    35|  access as an early step.
    36|- **Minimum necessary**: Create the least amount of road surface necessary
    37|  to serve the site's functions. Every metre of road represents compacted,
    38|  non-productive land that sheds water and disrupts soil biology.
    39|- **Multi-functional**: Roads should serve multiple purposes: access, water
    40|  harvesting, fire breaks, property boundaries, and wind orientation. A
    41|  road that only provides access is an under-designed element.
    42|- **Follow natural contours**: Roads placed on the contour or at gentle
    43|  angles to it minimise cut-and-fill, reduce erosion risk, and can harvest
    44|  water effectively. Steep road grades (exceeding 10-15%) are expensive to
    45|  construct, dangerous to use, and cause severe erosion.
    46|- **Connect, don't divide**: Access should connect elements of the design
    47|  without dividing productive areas into fragments too small for practical
    48|  management. Consider the movement of people, animals, machinery, water,
    49|  and wildlife.
    50|
    51|## Road Types and Hierarchy
    52|
    53|Mollison defines a hierarchy of access types, each with distinct design
    54|criteria aligned with the [[mollison-zone-planning-frequency-distance-and-placement]] framework:
    55|
    56|### Primary Access Roads
    57|- **Function**: Connect the property to the public road network and provide
    58|  access for heavy vehicles, machinery, and emergency services.
    59|- **Zone**: Typically Zone 4-5, forming the backbone of the property's
    60|  infrastructure.
    61|- **Width**: 4-6 metres for two-way traffic; 3-4 metres for one-way with
    62|  passing bays.
    63|- **Design speed**: 30-50 km/h maximum for rural properties.
    64|- **Grade**: Maximum 10% (1:10); preferably 5-8%. Steeper grades require
    65|  extensive drainage and surface treatment.
    66|- **Alignment**: Follow ridgelines where possible to minimise drainage
    67|  requirements and avoid water concentration. On slopes, follow the contour
    68|  at a gentle angle (1-3% fall across the road for drainage).
    69|- **Surface**: Gravel or compacted earth with appropriate drainage. Concrete
    70|  or asphalt only where absolutely necessary for traffic volume.
    71|- **Curve radius**: Minimum 10-15 metres for vehicle turning; 20-30 metres
    72|  for machinery and trucks.
    73|
    74|### Secondary Roads and Farm Tracks
    75|- **Function**: Provide access between major zones, connect fields and
    76|  production areas to the primary road, and support [[mollison-designers-climate-zone-3-farm-forestry]] operations.
    77|- **Zone**: Zone 2-4, connecting production areas.
    78|- **Width**: 2.5-4 metres for vehicles; 1.5-2 metres for tractor and
    79|  equipment access only.
    80|- **Grade**: Maximum 15% with careful drainage; 8-10% preferred.
    81|- **Surface**: Gravel, compacted earth, or grass track depending on traffic
    82|  volume and soil type.
    83|
    84|### Garden Paths and Foot Tracks
    85|- **Function**: Provide pedestrian access for [[mollison-zone-one-garden-design]] management, harvesting, and daily movement.
    86|- **Zone**: Zone 1-2.
    87|- **Width**: 0.6-1.2 metres for single pedestrian; 1.5 metres for wheelbarrow
    88|  and cart access.
    89|- **Surface**: Stepping stones, gravel, wood chips, compacted earth, or
    90|  living paths of low-growing herbs (thyme, chamomile, pennyroyal).
    91|- **Design**: Curved paths are preferred over straight paths; they slow
    92|  movement, create visual interest, and reduce erosion velocity on slopes.
    93|  See [[mollison-designers-herb-spiral-design-construction]] for
    94|  compact spiral pathway concepts.
    95|
    96|### Animal Tracks and Laneways
    97|- **Function**: Facilitate rotational movement of livestock between paddocks,
    98|  provide access to water, shelter, and milking or handling facilities.
    99|- **Design**: 2-4 metres wide; fenced on both sides; gentle grades; surface
   100|  of compacted earth or gravel in high-traffic areas.
   101|- **Integration**: Animal laneways double as fire breaks, windbreaks (when
   102|  planted with hedgerow species), and contour water harvesting structures.
   103|  See [[mollison-designers-woven-fence-hedge-live-barrier]] design.
   104|
   105|## Water Harvesting from Roads
   106|
   107|One of the most important multi-functions of road design is water harvesting.
   108|Roads are effectively linear catchment surfaces that can be designed to direct
   109|runoff to productive use. See [[mollison-designers-road-water-harvesting-and-integration]]:
   110|
   111|- **Crown camber**: Roads should be crowned (higher in the centre) or
   112|  sloped to one side to direct rainfall to specific collection points rather
   113|  than allowing uncontrolled runoff down both sides.
   114|- **Catch drains**: Shallow drains along the uphill side of contour roads
   115|  intercept overland flow and direct it to storage or infiltration points.
   116|  These drains can connect to [[mollison-pdc-swale-design-water-harvesting]] for
   117|  distribution across slopes.
   118|- **Spreader banks**: At intervals along the road, water is released from
   119|  the roadside drain into adjoining land through level spreader banks that
   120|  distribute flow evenly across the slope rather than concentrating it.
   121|- **Pond recharge**: Road drains can be directed to fill dams and ponds,
   122|  supplementing natural catchment. Even small road surfaces on large
   123|  properties can contribute significant water volumes.
   124|- **Ford crossings**: Where tracks cross watercourses, ford designs that
   125|  incorporate rock weirs can slow water flow and create small sediment
   126|  traps while allowing vehicle passage.
   127|
   128|## Road Construction on Slopes
   129|
   130|Road construction on sloping land requires careful earthworks design to
   131|minimise environmental impact and long-term maintenance:
   132|
   133|### Cut and Fill Balance
   134|- The ideal road profile balances cut (material removed from the uphill
   135|  side) with fill (material placed on the downhill side), minimising
   136|  earthworks import or export.
   137|- Cut slopes should be stabilised immediately with grass seeding or native
   138|  plantings. See [[mollison-designers-post-earthworks-planting-and-slope-stabilisation]].
   139|- Fill material should be compacted in layers, with the steepest edge
   140|  protected by rock armouring or vegetated reinforcement.
   141|- Excess cut material from road construction is valuable topsoil and subsoil
   142|  for garden beds, dam construction, or earth-shelter building. See
   143|  [[mollison-earth-resources-from-excavation]].
   144|
   145|### Drainage Structures
   146|- **Table drains**: Open drains on both sides of the road to carry water
   147|  along the road to safe discharge points.
   148|- **Cross drains**: Culverts or angle drains at regular intervals (every
   149|  50-100 metres on grades) to prevent water accumulation and velocity
