# Bad Trip First Aid and Crisis Intervention for Psilocybin Mushrooms

## Overview

[[hallucinogenic-mushroom-acute-psychological-effects-and-bad-trips]] are acute psychological crises during psilocybin use, characterized by severe anxiety,
paranoia, disorientation, and in extreme cases, psychotic-like episodes. Unlike many drugs, psilocybin
has no pharmacological antagonist, making psychological first aid the primary intervention. Understanding
recognition, management, and aftermath is essential for [[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]] and [[hallucinogenic-mushroom-telephone-helplines-and-emergency-response]].

## Recognizing a Bad Trip

### Mild to Moderate Distress
- Restlessness and inability to remain still
- Anxiety or panic that the user cannot self-manage
- Mild paranoia or suspiciousness about surroundings
- Confusion about identity, time, or location
- Repeated questions like "am I going to be okay?"
- Desire to end the experience prematurely

### Severe Crisis
- Severe agitation with incoherent communication
- Acute psychotic episodes with frightening visual images
- Severe paranoia and total loss of reality testing
- Belief that one is dying, going insane, or trapped permanently
- Self-injurious behavior or dangerous flight responses
- Suicidal ideation during the experience
- Complete dissociation with no response to verbal communication

## Immediate Response Protocol

### Five Principles of Bad Trip Management

1. **Remain calm** — The sitter's emotional state directly affects the user. Anxiety is contagious;
   calm is equally contagious. Speak slowly, clearly, and with confidence.

2. **Change the setting** — Move to a different room, go outside if safe, change lighting, reduce
   or eliminate music. Sensory environment change can interrupt negative thought loops.

3. **Reassure, don't dismiss** — Never say "it's just a drug" or "it's all in your head." Instead:
   "You're having a difficult time, but this will pass. You are safe and I am here with you."

4. **Redirect attention** — Offer simple engagement: hold hands, listen to calming music, focus on
   breathing, look at a specific object. Grounding connects the person to present physical reality.

5. **Never leave the person alone** — Even if they request privacy, maintain visual contact. The
   risk of accidental self-harm peaks during the crisis window.

### Physical Comfort Measures
- Offer water in small sips
- Provide a blanket for shivering
- Reduce sensory input: dim lights, silence music, minimize conversation
- Ensure the person is in a safe position (sitting or lying down)
- Remove potentially dangerous objects from the immediate area

## Pharmacological Intervention

### Benzodiazepines: First-Line Treatment

Benzodiazepines are the safest and most effective medication for acute psilocybin crises. They
enhance GABA-A receptor activity, counteracting serotonergic overstimulation:

- **Diazepam (Valium)**: 5-10mg oral or intravenous
- **Lorazepam (Ativan)**: 1-2mg sublingual or intramuscular
- **Midazolam (Versed)**: 2-5mg intramuscular for acute severe agitation

Benzodiazepines are preferred because they have a wide [[cannabis-therapeutic-index-overview]], effectively reduce anxiety
without suppressing consciousness, do not interact dangerously with psilocybin, and are widely
available in emergency settings.

### What NOT to Use

- **Antipsychotics (haloperidol, olanzapine)**: May worsen the experience or cause excessive
  sedation without addressing the serotonergic mechanism
- **Physical restraints**: Absolute last resort only to prevent self-harm, as they dramatically
  increase panic and paranoia

### Self-Limiting Nature

Short-term tolerance to psilocybin develops rapidly. The pharmacological effects are self-limiting —
intensity naturally diminishes over 4-6 hours. The goal is keeping the person safe during this window.

## Risk Factors for Bad Trips

- **First-time users** with no prior [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]
- **High doses** exceeding tolerance or experience level
- **Unfavorable set**: Pre-existing anxiety, depression, trauma, or current life stress
- **Unfavorable setting**: Crowded, unfamiliar, or threatening environments
- **Polydrug use**: Alcohol significantly increases risk of adverse outcomes. The 2004 Manchester
  case involved fatal outcomes from mushrooms combined with alcohol
- **Psychiatric history**: Psilocybin can precipitate latent psychotic or personality disorders
