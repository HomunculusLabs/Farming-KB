# Agaricus blazei (Brazilian Mushroom)

## Overview

[[agaricus-blazei]] Murill (also known as [[agaricus-subrufescens]], Agaricus brasiliensis, and "the almond mushroom") is a gilled mushroom native to the coastal region of Sao Paulo state in Brazil. It was first identified by the American mycologist William A. Murrill in 1945 but gained prominence in the 1970s-80s when researchers noticed extremely low cancer rates in the Piedade region of Brazil where it was traditionally consumed.

The mushroom has a distinctive almond-like aroma when fresh, which is the origin of its common name "cogumelo do sol" (mushroom of the sun) in Brazil and "himematsutake" (princess matsutake) in Japan.

## Key Compounds

- **Beta-glucans:** The primary immunomodulatory compounds. A. blazei contains particularly high concentrations of 1,3-beta and 1,6-beta-glucans, comparable to or exceeding those of reishi and maitake.
- **Proteoglucans:** Protein-bound polysaccharides including the well-studied ABM-P (Agaricus blazei Murill polysaccharide).
- **Ergosterol:** Provitamin D2 precursor with anti-tumor properties.
- **Blazein and blazeispirols:** Triterpenes with anti-inflammatory activity.
- **Lectins:** Sugar-binding proteins with immunomodulatory effects.
- **Vitamins and minerals:** Rich in B vitamins, ergocalciferol (vitamin D2), and minerals including selenium.

## Immune System Research

Agaricus blazei has generated significant research interest for immune modulation:

### Immune Activation
- Potent activation of macrophages, NK cells, and neutrophils.
- Stimulates dendritic cell maturation and antigen presentation.
- Enhances production of interferon-gamma (IFN-gamma) and interleukin-12 (IL-12), promoting Th1 immune response.
- Increases IgG and IgA antibody production.

### Anti-Tumor Research
- ABM-P and other polysaccharides demonstrated anti-tumor activity in multiple animal models.
- Tumor inhibition rates of 40-60% reported in some studies.
- Enhances the effectiveness of chemotherapy drugs while reducing their side effects.
- Anti-angiogenic effects (inhibiting tumor blood vessel growth).
- Human clinical trials have been conducted primarily in Japan and Brazil, with mixed but generally positive results.

### Anti-Inflammatory Effects
- Reduces TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, and IL-6 production.
- Suppresses NF-kB signaling pathway.
- Demonstrated efficacy in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) models.
- May help with allergic conditions by modulating Th1/Th2 balance.

### Antioxidant Properties
- High phenolic content provides significant free radical scavenging.
- Reduces lipid peroxidation.
- Protects against oxidative DNA damage.

## Metabolic Health

- Anti-diabetic effects in animal models: reduces blood glucose, improves insulin sensitivity.
- Cholesterol-lowering activity via increased bile acid excretion.
- Hepatoprotective effects: protects liver from toxin-induced damage.
- May support weight management through effects on lipid metabolism.

## Taxonomic Confusion

Agaricus blazei has a complex taxonomic history:
- Originally described as Agaricus blazei by Murrill (1945) from Florida.
- Later associated with the Brazilian Piedade mushroom by Heinemann (1967).
- Wasser et al. (2002) proposed Agaricus brasiliensis for the Brazilian strain.
- Kerrigan (2005) determined the correct name is Agaricus subrufescens (Peck, 1893), which has priority.
- Despite this, "Agaricus blazei" remains the most commonly used name in research and commerce.

The Florida and Brazilian populations may represent different strains with slightly different chemical profiles, though both are used medicinally.

## Preparation Methods

### Culinary
- Edible with a mild, almond-like flavor.
- Can be sauteed, grilled, added to soups, or eaten raw.
- Commercially available fresh in some markets, particularly in Brazil and Japan.

### Hot Water Extract (Tea)
- Simmer dried mushroom or powder in water for 30-60 minutes.
- Effective for beta-glucan extraction.

### Dual Extraction Tincture
1. Hot water extraction for polysaccharides.
2. Alcohol maceration for triterpenes and ergosterol.
3. Combine extracts.

### Capsules and Tablets
Most common supplement form. Many products use dried whole mushroom powder or standardized extracts.

## Dosing Guidelines

- **General immune support:** 1-3g dried mushroom powder per day.
- **Enhanced immune support:** 3-6g per day, divided into 2-3 doses.
- **Cancer adjunct (under medical supervision):** 6-9g per day based on some clinical protocols.
- **Tincture:** 2-4ml dual extract, 2-3 times daily.
- **Culinary:** 100-200g fresh per week.

## Cultivation

Agaricus blazei is commercially cultivated on a large scale, primarily in Japan, China, and Brazil:

- **Substrate:** Composted substrate (similar to button mushroom compost) — horse manure, straw, and various organic materials.
- **Temperature:** 75-82°F (24-28°C) for mycelial growth; 60-68°F (16-20°C) for fruiting.
- **Humidity:** 85-95% for fruiting.
- **Spawn to harvest:** 60-90 days total.
- **Yield:** Biological efficiency of 60-100%.

## Safety Considerations

- Generally safe with a history of culinary use.
- **Important note:** Agaricus species can contain hydrazine compounds. Properly cultivated and prepared A. blazei has not shown significant hydrazine issues, but this is a consideration for Agaricus mushrooms broadly.
- May cause gastrointestinal upset at high doses.
- Immune-stimulating effects — caution with autoimmune conditions and immunosuppressant drugs.
- Possible interactions with diabetes and blood pressure medications.
- Avoid during pregnancy and breastfeeding (insufficient safety data at medicinal doses).
- Rare reports of liver enzyme elevation at very high doses — monitor if using therapeutically.

## Quality Concerns

- The market has significant quality variation. Some products contain lower species of Agaricus or adulterants.
- Look for products with verified beta-glucan content and species identification.
- DNA testing can verify species authenticity.
