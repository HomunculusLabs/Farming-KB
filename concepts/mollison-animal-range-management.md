# Mollison on Animal Integration in Zone Two and Three

**Source:** Bill Mollison, Permaculture Design Course, Pamphlet IX (Permaculture Techniques), 1981
**Last Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Mollison provides detailed guidance on integrating domestic and wild animals into permaculture zones two and three, emphasizing range management, forage design, and trophic efficiency. This extends [[mollison-animal-integration-in-permaculture]] and [[permaculture-chicken-tractors]].

## Range Capacity Enhancement

- Carob tree experiment (East Africa): range went from 1 cow per 12 acres to 12 cows per acre
- One carob tree (17 years old, not high-yielding) maintains 8 milking goats year-round
- 3 carob pods per day per goat; goats milk well on laterite cap with rough forage
- Correct components at correct time of year: sugar pods let animals utilize dry grass

## Three Range Strategies

1. **Permanent high-mineral herbs**: dandelion, chicory, comfrey throughout pastures
2. **Evergreen high-nutrition tree forage**: Tagasaste, Coprosma, honey locust, carob
3. **Winter carbohydrate sources**: acorns, large nuts, turnips, fodder beets

## Shelter Design

- 20% loss of milk/meat in unsheltered environments
- Pampas grass: ideal shelter, survival rates equal to insulated sheds
- Dense tree clumps or tall grasses required (not just hedgerows)
- Cattle are forest animals -- belong in cool forest swamplands in summer

## Animal-Propagated Ranges

- Cattle pass honey locust/carob seeds: 90-100% germination from cow patties
- Bluejays plant 50-60 acorns and forget them; squirrels accumulate in rotting logs
- "Nearly every animal is at work planting its own garden"
- Hawaiian Islands, Australia, Argentina: cattle mainly propagate their own range plants

## Trophic Efficiency and Shortcuts

- Standard trophic chain (leaf to trout): 10,000 lbs leaf = 1 lb trout (10x loss per step)
- Shortcuts via phreatocides (cold-water decomposer amphipods): 100 lbs leaf = 1 lb trout
- Grey mullet browses diatoms directly, weighs 15 lbs -- extremely efficient conversion
- Mussel role: phosphate and calcium fixation (stop nutrients before they reach sea)
- Phreatocides: major food of trout, active under ice, pH range 3.5-7+
- Key principle: "Seek out large, low-level decomposers chomping on leaf and algae"

## Specific Animal Systems

### Chickens
- Escapement design: African boxthorn (millions of spines) protects brooding hens from all predators
- Chickens slip through boxthorn fast (scaly legs); cats, dogs cannot enter
- Black raspberries: excellent cover for brooding, associated with apple trees at drip line
- Chickens take lower berries (~8 inches); people pick higher ones
- Racoons: unreasonably competent predator; require electric fence solutions

### Pigs
- Gloucester Old Spot: specific orchard forager breed
- Eliminate Jerusalem artichoke, quack grass, and grubs
- Rotate into gardens after harvest; smell only when overcrowded or fed wrong garbage
- Control slugs and poison snakes

### Fish Ponds
- Put pike in with pan fish: small fish swim through to pike, keep pond from overstocking
- Maximum size limit (not minimum): eat young, leave large breeding fish
- Mesh at 2.25-2.75 inches: anything that can't pass through is harvestable

### Specialized Animals
- Slender blue-tongue lizard: eats only slugs
- Tiliqua lizards: snail eaters (desert, subtropical, cool temperate species)
- Geckos: glasshouse pest controllers
- Coconut crab: shredding, insect control (only on a few Pacific islands)
- Gloucester Old Spot pig: bred as orchard forager
- Wallabies: short browsers, fantastic lawn systems (under 24 inches)
- Geese with walnuts: ancient combination

## Pruning Systems by Zone

- **Zone one**: Standard cut pruning for light and access
- **Zone two**: Three-section rotation (heavy/light/no prune) eliminates biennial bearing; slightly more total fruit, more predictable sizes
- **Zone three**: Stake 8 feet, clear trunk, let go at 4-5 years -- dome-shaped, zero maintenance, animals browse through
