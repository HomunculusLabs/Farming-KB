## Hot Water and Hydrated Lime Substrate Treatment

Hot water treatment and hydrated lime soaks are two of the most accessible

## Hot Water Pasteurization

Hot water pasteurization involves submerging chopped straw in water heated
to approximately 160 degrees Fahrenheit (71 degrees Celsius) for one to two
hours. The process kills most contaminant organisms while preserving
beneficial microflora that help defend against later invasions.

The simplest setup uses a metal barrel over a propane burner. Straw in a
mesh bag is submerged in heated water for the required duration, then
removed and drained thoroughly before inoculation. An insulated box can
also be used for steam pasteurization, which is less water-intensive but
requires more careful temperature monitoring.

## The Boil-a-Bag Technique

The boil-a-bag method, developed by D.H. Jarrold, adapts hot water
treatment for [[marmite-yeast-extract-straw-substrate]] (Marmite or Vegemite) is dissolved in
boiling water, poured into a bucket with six additional liters of boiling
water, and poured over chopped straw. After soaking for two hours, the
straw is drained in a bathtub and cooled. Three to four handfuls of brown
rice flour are sprinkled over the straw and mixed in. The prepared straw is
packed into boil-a-bags, sealed, and sterilized at 15 psi for sixty minutes
before inoculation.

## Semi-Selectivity of Pasteurization

A key advantage of hot water treatment over full sterilization is its
semi-selectivity. Pasteurization kills most harmful organisms but preserves
some beneficial bacteria and microflora. These surviving organisms compete
with contaminants and provide the inoculated mushroom mycelium with a brief
window of biological protection during early colonization.
