## AMF-Mediated Biocontrol of Phytophthora via Root Exudate Modification

## Overview

[[singh-trichoderma-biocontrol-mechanisms-applications]] are diverse, interrelated, and often synergistic, involving changes in root exudation patterns, induction of plant defense pathways, and modifications to the rhizosphere [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]], and volatiles
- For P. nicotianae specifically: attracted by sucrose, dextrose, fructose, rhamnose, maltose, asparagine, and glutamine

## AMF Biocontrol Mechanisms

### Local Defense Induction
- Colonization with G. mosseae induces constitutive synthesis of beta-1,3-glucanase and new isoforms of beta-1,3-glucanase and chitinase within colonized roots
- Callose accumulation around cortical cells containing arbuscules
- Cell wall thickenings rich in pectin in colonized areas

### Systemic Defense (ISR)
- In nonmycorrhizal roots of plants colonized with G. mosseae: accumulation of PR-1 proteins and cell wall modifications characteristic of [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]]

### Root Exudate Modification
The most direct biocontrol mechanism involves changes in root exudation patterns:

**In vitro findings (Lioussanne et al. 2008):**
- Exudates from mature mycorrhizal roots attracted significantly fewer P. nicotianae zoospores than water or exudates from nonmycorrhizal roots
- Exudates from actively growing young mycorrhizal roots were MORE attractive than nonmycorrhizal root exudates
- Suggests higher amounts of attractive molecules released by young mycorrhizal roots, while older mycorrhizal roots contain repulsive molecules

**Specific exudate changes:**
- Among 27 sugars, amino acids, and organic acids quantified, only proline and isocitric acid concentrations differed between mycorrhizal and nonmycorrhizal roots
- Proline concentration was higher in exudates from mature mycorrhizal roots
- Proline is involved in plant protection against water and salt stresses and accumulates in tomato leaves following P. nicotianae infection

## Glomalin and Rhizosphere Effects

- Glomalin is the only protein detected from AMF structures that favors soil aggregation
- Root colonization with AMF inhibits further mycorrhizal colonization systemically (autoregulation)
- AMF-mediated changes to root exudation also affect the broader rhizosphere microbial community

## Evidence from Compartmented Systems

Using compartmented soil systems where tomato plants were colonized with G. mosseae or G. intraradices before exposure to P. nicotianae:
- Pathogen biomass significantly reduced within precolonized roots compared to nonmycorrhizal plants
- G. intraradices (DAOM 181,602) showed similar biocontrol ability to G. mosseae
- However, biocontrol effectiveness is strain-dependent (different G. intraradices strain BEG 72 did not induce biocontrol in some studies)

## Implications for Agriculture

- AMF-mediated biocontrol offers environmentally respectful alternative to chemical pesticides and fumigants
- Understanding the specific mechanisms allows optimization of AMF management in agriculture
- The dual role of AMF -- promoting plant growth while suppressing pathogens -- makes them valuable components of integrated pest management
- Biocontrol effectiveness depends on AMF strain selection, plant species, and timing of colonization relative to pathogen exposure
