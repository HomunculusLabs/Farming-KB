# Cultivation of *Psilocybe azurescens* — The World's Most Potent Psilocybin Mushroom

## Overview

*Psilocybe azurescens* (Stamets & Gartz, 1995) is the most potent psilocybin-containing mushroom known, capable of accumulating up to **25 mg [[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]] per gram of dried material**. Originally discovered in 1979 in the Columbia River basin near Astoria, Oregon, it grows in beds of hardwood or conifer chips in half-shaded environments. Cultivation involves agar germination, wood substrate expansion, and [[azurescens-outdoor-bed-preparation-woodchips]].

## Species Description

### Taxonomy

- **Author**: Stamets & Gartz (1995).
- **Complex**: *[[growing-gourmet-caramel-capped-psilocybe-cyanescens-cultivation]]* complex — closely related to *P. cyanescens* and *P. cyanofibrillosa*.
- **Distinguishing features**: Comparatively great size, microscopic characteristics, and extraordinary [[mycelial-broth-alkaloid-production]].

### Morphology (Latin Description)

- **Pileus**: Ochre-brown, hygrophanous, viscid, separable pellicle, conic then convex to plano, 30–100 mm wide, umbonate.
- **Lamellae**: Sinuate-adnate, pale to brown.
- **Stipe**: White, strict, elongated, 90–200 mm, with bluish fibrils and strigosity at base.
- **Flesh**: Bluening on bruising.
- **Spores**: 13–13.5 × 6.5–8.0 µm.
- **Cheilocystidia**: Fusoid-ventricose, 23–28 × 6.5–8.0 µm.
- **Pleurocystidia**: 23–35 × 9–10 µm.

## Alkaloid Content

*P. azurescens* produces exceptionally high levels of tryptamine alkaloids:

| Sample | Psilocybin (%) | Psilocin (%) | Baeocystin (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.40 | 0.31 | 0.28 |
| 2 | 1.56 | 0.30 | 0.32 |
| 3 | 1.68 | 0.28 | 0.38 |
| 4 | 1.71 | 0.34 | 0.41 |

These values represent up to **~2.5% total tryptamine alkaloids** — the highest of any known psilocybin mushroom.

## Cultivation Method

### Materials

- [[malt-extract-agar]] (MEA) for germination and isolation.
- Sterilised wood substrate (jars or heat-resistant polypropylene bags).
- Fresh wood chips (preferably beech, alder, hazelnut).
- Outdoor bed location (half-shaded, under shrubs).

### Step 1: Agar Germination and Isolation

1. Prepare malt extract agar.
2. Germinate spores on agar plates.
3. Isolate pure mycelium from any contaminant growth.
4. Transfer clean mycelium to fresh plates.

### Step 2: Wood Substrate Expansion

1. Transfer mycelium to sterilised wood chips in jars.
2. Incubate until fully colonised.
3. Optionally expand using polypropylene bags (higher contamination risk).

**Critical rule**: Never use contaminated cultures to inoculate new substrate.

### Step 3: Outdoor Bed Preparation

1. Dig a **15–20 cm deep hole** in a half-shaded location.
2. Fill with fresh chopped wood chips mixed with spawn (at least **1/5 spawn by volume**).
3. Water thoroughly (but don't overwater).
4. Cover with wet cardboard and perforated plastic to maintain humidity.
5. Keep humid for first 2 weeks, then leave until September.

### Fruiting

- From **September**, water once or twice weekly.
- First mushrooms appear when temperatures drop to **7–10°C (45–50°F)**, typically late September to early October.
- Usually produces **two flushes** about a month apart.
- May fruit until January depending on temperatures.
- A tent over the bed can prolong fruiting season.

### Long-Term Maintenance

- Add a layer of fresh wood chips to the top when temperatures drop below 0°C — this provides nutrition for the following year.
- Treated properly, a mushroom bed can **last for decades**.
- Infected cardboard can be used to inoculate new beds.
- Even discarded contaminated spawn (as little as 0.3 litres) can establish patches.
