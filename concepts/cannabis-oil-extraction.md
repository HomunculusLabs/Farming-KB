# Cannabis Oil Extraction

## Overview

Cannabis oil extraction encompasses methods of producing concentrated cannabis oils for cooking, topical use, and sometimes smoking. According to Cervantes, cannabis oils range from simple butter and vegetable oil infusions to highly concentrated solvent-based extracts, with potencies that can exceed 70 percent THC. The psychoactive cannabinoids dissolve in butter, cooking oil, or alcohol, making them available for absorption by the body.

## Types of Cannabis Oil

### Cannabis Butter (Cannabutter)

[[cannabis-cannabutter-preparation]] is the most common and accessible form of cannabis oil. It is made by simmering cannabis leaf in water with butter for two hours. The butter retains about 80 percent of the cannabinoids from the original foliage. After cooling, the butter separates from the water and can be used in any recipe calling for butter.

### Cannabis Vegetable Oil

Cannabis vegetable oil substitutes one quart (1 L) of vegetable oil for butter, following the same simmering process. After cooling, the mix is placed in the freezer for 4-6 hours. The water freezes and the oil can be poured off or scraped from the ice. The oil liquefies at room temperature and keeps for about a month at room temperature, longer in the freezer.

### Hash Oil

Hash oil is a concentrate made by dissolving cannabis in hydrocarbon solvents such as ether or alcohol to extract THC. Cannabis oils are normally dark due to chlorophyll and other contaminants. Filtering through charcoal removes virtually all impurities. Hash oil was never super popular because it is inconvenient to smoke and users are concerned about solvent health risks.

### Honey Oil

Honey oil was popular in America in the early 1970s. It has a distinctive translucent golden-amber color and transforms from stiff, toffee-like consistency to runny oil when warmed. Pure honey oil represents one of the most refined forms of cannabis extraction.

### Butane Cannabis Oil (BHO)

Butane hash oil is made by passing butane gas through chopped cannabis. The butane dissolves THC and is collected, then evaporated away by warming. The residual honey-colored oil is scraped from the collection pan. This method is extremely dangerous due to explosion and toxicity risks.

## Cooking Oil Extraction Process

The book provides a detailed recipe for cannabis-infused cooking oil:

### Ingredients

- 1.6 quarts (1.5 L) of water
- 4 ounces (112 gm) of marijuana leaf
- 1 quart (1 L) of vegetable oil

### Instructions

1. Heat water in a large pan on the stove
2. Add marijuana leaf and vegetable oil, stir
3. Bring to a boil, cover, simmer on low heat for 2 hours
4. Strain through a colander, press foliage to extract all liquid
5. Pour 0.5 quart (0.5 L) of boiling water over wet leaf to wash out remaining oil
6. Press leaves to squeeze out all liquid possible
7. Let cool; oil separates and rises to the top
8. Place in freezer for 4-6 hours; water freezes solid
9. Pour off oil (or scrape from frozen water for olive/peanut oil)
10. Oil liquefies at room temperature

### Oil Selection

Olive oil and peanut oil coagulate in the freezer and cannot be poured off. Use a spatula to scrape the solidified oil from the frozen water. Other vegetable oils that remain liquid when cold can simply be poured off.

## Consumption Methods

Cannabis oil can be used in several ways:

- Added to any recipe calling for vegetable oil
- Wiped on joint papers or cigarettes for smoking
- Applied to pipe screens before smoking
- Smoked using hot knives
- Vaporized in an oil pipe (a stem with a glass bubble on one end)
- Applied as [[cannabis-topical-preparations]] for skin

A popular smoking method involves placing a little oil inside the glass bubble of an oil pipe. When heat is applied, the THC vaporizes and is inhaled through the stem.

## Tinctures as Liquid Extraction

[[cervantes-cannabis-tinctures]] represent another form of liquid cannabis extraction, using alcohol as the solvent. The process retains 60 percent or more of the THC from raw material and can be concentrated by evaporation. Tinctures offer faster onset than edibles.

## Potency Comparison

Different extraction methods produce oils of varying potency:

- Cannabis butter: approximately 80% of original cannabinoids retained
- Cannabis tincture: 60% or more of THC retained
- Well-made solvent-extracted oil: often more than 70% THC
- Hash oil concentrates: potentially the most concentrated form available

## Safety Considerations

- Cooking oil extractions should be simmered on low heat to avoid degrading cannabinoids
- Do not exceed 200F (93C) or THC will vaporize into the air
- Solvent-based extractions carry explosion, fire, and toxicity risks
- Hash and cannabis oil frequently retain residues from processing solvents
- Chemical damage may result from premature use before all solvents are extracted
- Always label cannabis-infused products clearly to prevent accidental consumption

## Storage Guidelines

- Cannabis butter: refrigerator for short term, freezer for long term
- Cannabis vegetable oil: one month at room temperature, longer in freezer
- Tinctures: cool, dark place for 1-3 months; heat and light degrade quickly
- Hash oil: cool, dark, airtight container to preserve potency
- Freezing is not recommended for flower but works well for infused oils and butters
