# Ornamental Bulbs Growing

Bulbs, including corms, rhizomes, and tubers, are food-storage organs
that enable plants to survive a dormant season and produce new growth
the following year. Bulbs are ideal for filling spaces between shrubs
in the border with color. They should not be considered merely spring-
flowering plants; with careful planning and planting, it is possible
to have a year-round display of color from bulbs in the faires-growing-and-season-extension.

## Post-Flowering Care

It is essential, after bulbs have flowered, that you allow the foliage
to remain attached so it can build up the food reserve for next year's
flower. Remove the leaves only after they have turned completely
yellow, then cut them off and compost them. If you cut the foliage off
too early or tie it in neat little knots, the plant cannot use
sunlight to make food. The bulb gets smaller and eventually disappears.

Daffodils (Narcissus) can be planted next to large-leaved perennials
so the dying foliage is quickly masked. This approach keeps the border
looking tidy while the bulb completes its lifecycle. For temporary
storage, dig bulbs after flowering and replant in a slit trench in a
sheltered corner, keeping foliage exposed to the sun. Give a couple
of liquid feeds before the foliage dies down.

## Key Points

- Buy firm bulbs with intact skins; larger bulbs produce stronger
  flowers
- Plant spring bulbs in late summer or autumn; summer bulbs in spring
- Plant at twice the bulb's height in light soil, same depth in heavy
  soil, on a gravel layer
- Do not mix varieties in the same block as they flower at different
  times
- Naturalize early-flowering bulbs in lawns by scattering and planting
  with a bulb planter
- Feed after flowering with potash; use kelp or liquid manure for
  bulbs in dedicated beds or containers
- Never cut off foliage before it yellows; the bulb needs it to build
  next year's flower
- Mulch over dormant bulbs with well-rotted manure or compost
