# Slugs and Snails - Organic Control

## The Problem

The non-indigenous Spanish slug (*Arion vulgaris*) breeds on an enormous scale in many regions. [[comparison-sepp-holzer-vs-biodynamic]] found up to 15 slugs per square metre on farms and vegetable plots in Southern Styria and Lower Austria. Cattle would no longer graze because the grass was so full of them. In town gardens, slugs crawl up house walls all the way to balconies, forcing removal of espalier trees and climbing plants.

## Sawdust Barrier Method

For smaller gardens, Holzer recommends a sawdust-and-ash barrier:

1. Take a watering can and cut the spout to half its original length for a wider pour
2. Fill with a mixture of very dry fine sawdust (from untreated natural wood, ideally from a carpenter's workshop where wood is completely dry and sawdust is finer)
3. Mix sawdust with one part wood ash to ten parts sawdust, or quicklime powder (around 1:20)
4. All ingredients must be bone dry
5. Pour a finger's-width border around the outside of the lettuce or vegetable patch
6. Free the border area of vegetation first
7. Replace after rain to keep dry

**Mechanism:** The fine dry sawdust adheres to the foot of a slug or snail. The ash and quicklime extract moisture, preventing them from entering the crop. Slugs visibly turn around when they reach this barrier.

## Egg-Trap and Solar Destruction Method

Slugs and snails lay eggs in dark, moist places. Create deliberate egg-laying habitat:

1. Make rows of freshly cut grass and leaves in the garden
2. Pile higher and compact more than normal mulch
3. Keep as moist as possible for ideal egg-laying conditions
4. Slugs and snails travel great distances to use these places
5. On a particularly sunny day, turn over the rows with a gardening fork at midday
6. Whole clusters of eggs will have adhered to the rotting grass
7. The eggs are rapidly destroyed by the heat of the sun and UV rays

This method also demonstrates how improper use of mulch (fresh material, piled too high and not loosely enough) can cause slug overpopulation.

## Biological Controls

- **Pigs:** Movable pens can place pigs exactly where needed. Mix collected snails with their normal feed. Pigs soon acquire a taste and eat all newcomers. They need a great deal of water to digest snails. Pigs also control soil insects like cockchafer larvae through rooting.
- **Ducks and toads:** Also reduce [[hamilton-slug-and-snail-control]] numbers to a harmless level
- **Hedgehogs, shrews, lizards, and [[ground-beetles-carabidae-and-biological-pest-control]]:** Excellent natural predators
- **Edible snail (*Helix pomatia*):** Helps regulate slug populations by eating their eggs -- not all snails are harmful
