# Brassinosteroid Experimental Methodology — Gartz 1990

The 1990 study by Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt on brassinosteroid
growth-promoting effects [[brassinosteroid-effects-on-fungal-morphology]] across two
cultivation systems. The first used 6% malt agar plates for
observing mycelial extension rates in isolation from fruiting
dynamics. The second used a horse dung and rice grain mixture,
a complex substrate supporting full fruiting body production.

This dual-substrate approach distinguished between effects on
vegetative growth (mycelial extension on agar) and reproductive
development (fruiting on dung/grain substrate). Using both in
parallel provided more complete information about brassinosteroid
activity than either system alone.

## Mycelial Growth — Agar System

On malt agar, brassinosteroid-treated cultures showed mycelial growth
two to three times faster than untreated controls. This dramatic
acceleration suggests brassinosteroids influence fundamental cellular
processes in fungal hyphae, consistent with their known effects on
cell elongation and division in higher plants.

Agar plates provide a clean platform for measuring vegetative growth
without confounding variables from complex substrates. However,
agar-based growth does not necessarily predict performance on grain
or compost substrates — hence the value of the dual-substrate design.

## Fruiting Assessment — Dung/Grain System

The dung/grain system was evaluated using multiple parameters:
