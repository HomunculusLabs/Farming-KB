# Contaminants of Mushroom Culture in The Mushroom Cultivator

Source: The Mushroom Cultivator (Stamets & Chilton, 1983), Chapter XIII
Updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

Chapter XIII of The Mushroom Cultivator provides the most comprehensive [[duggar-mushroom-insect-pests-and-fungal-diseases]], with detailed descriptions, conditions favoring growth, and control measures for each. Over 30 specific contaminants are catalogued.

## The Nature of Contamination

Contaminants are named solely because they are undesired -- if one were trying to culture Penicillium and Agaricus spores settled on the media, the [[duggar-mushroom-environmental-factors-temperature-moisture-light]] that favor contaminants:**
- Stagnant air pockets (breeding grounds for Trichoderma and Pseudomonas)
- Excessive humidity without adequate air movement
- Wet mushroom caps (prime sites for [[trichoderma-forest-green-mold-guide]])**: The most common contaminant in mushroom culture. Rapidly colonizes substrates, casing soils, and compost. Green spore clouds easily spread. Thrives in stale air and on over-wet substrates. Control: improve air circulation, reduce moisture, maintain hygiene.
- **Penicillium (Bluish Green Mold)**: Extremely common airborne contaminant. Often appears along petri dish periphery or near transfer points. Control: HEPA filtration, careful [[grain-sterilization-methods]] (full hour at 15 psi).
- **Cladosporium (Dark Green Mold)**: Common airborne contaminant in many environments.

### Pink Molds

- **Neurospora (Pink Mold)**: One of the fastest growing contaminants. Produces prolific pink-orange spore clouds. Can colonize a petri dish overnight. Particularly problematic in warm conditions.
- **Fusarium (Pink Mold)**: Affects mushroom fruitbodies directly. Can cause serious crop losses.
- **Trichothecium (Pink Mold)**: Less common but problematic when established.

### White/Cottony Contaminants

- **Dactylium (Cobweb Mold)**: One of the most easily recognized contaminants. Fine white cobweb-like growth over casing and mushrooms. Spreads rapidly by air. Control: reduce humidity, increase air circulation, salt treatments.
- **Mycelia Sterilia (White Mold)**: Sterile white mycelium with no visible spore structures. Can be confused with mushroom mycelium. Distinguished by different growth pattern and lack of fruiting.
- **Mycogone (Wet Bubble)**: Causes distorted [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] with wet, bubble-like lesions.
- **Verticillium (Dry Bubble)**: Causes dry, brownish lesions on mushroom caps and stems.

### Bacterial Contaminants

- **Pseudomonas (Bacterial Blotch and Pit)**: One of the most common diseases. Causes brown, sunken lesions on mushroom caps. Thrives when caps remain wet. Control: reduce humidity, increase air movement, avoid wetting caps, chlorinated water (150 ppm).
- **Bacillus (Wet Spot)**: Causes slimy, foul-smelling areas in grain spawn. Source: contaminated grain or poor sterilization. Control: proper sterilization times, quality grain selection.
- **Actinomyces / Streptomyces (Firefang)**: Thermophilic organisms active during composting. In compost they are beneficial; in casing they indicate problems.

### Yeast Contaminants

- **Cryptococcus (Cream Colored Yeast)**: Cream to pink colored colonies on agar media. Common but usually less damaging than molds.
