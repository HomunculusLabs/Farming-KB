# Cannabis Harvesting and Curing Techniques

Harvesting is the culmination of the growing cycle and the moment when
cultivators reap the rewards of their labor. However, the quality of the final
product depends as much on proper harvesting and [[cannabis-flushing-before-harvest]]
techniques as it does on the growing process itself. Greg Green's Cannabis
Grow Bible provides detailed guidance on identifying harvest readiness and
employing proper post-harvest methods to preserve potency, flavor, and aroma.

## Identifying Harvest Readiness

The flowering time guidelines provided by seed breeders offer a starting point,
but experienced growers learn to recognize visual indicators of peak maturity.
Not all of these signs appear on every strain, but collectively they provide a
reliable harvest assessment:

- 50% to 70% of the pistils (hairs) have changed color from white to brown or
  red
- The plant stops producing new [[greg-green-trichomes-and-resin-glands]]
- The plant stops producing visible resin
- Fan leaves and lower leaves have turned yellow and begun to drop off
- The aroma has reached its peak intensity
- Bud mass has not increased in the past several days

## Indica Harvest Method

Indica plants typically range from 1 to 4 feet in height at harvest. The plant
should be cut at the base and hung upside down in a cool, dark room with fresh
air circulation. Using a pair of clippers, the grower removes as many fan
leaves as possible, followed by the secondary leaves. These are separated into
different piles because they represent different quality grades of smokable
material.

The final step is gathering trim from the buds. Trim refers to the small
leaves that are covered in resin glands. This material is particularly
valuable for [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-hash-making-and-trichome-extraction]] or other extraction methods. The result is four quality grades: fan
leaf (lowest quality), middle leaves, trim, and bud (prime quality).

The plant is left hanging until a branch snaps cleanly and easily between the
fingers, indicating that the internal moisture content has dropped
sufficiently. This drying process typically takes two to three weeks.

## Sativa Harvest Method

Sativa plants can grow much larger than Indicas, sometimes reaching 12 feet
and carrying over 20 ounces of bud. Harvesting large outdoor Sativas requires
a canvas spread or similar transport medium. The plant is chopped at the base,
spread onto the canvas, and the canvas is rolled and tied for transport.

Because of the plant's size and bushiness, individual branches may need to be
cut and hung separately. The same leaf removal and trimming procedures used
for Indica harvest apply. It is critical to note that light degrades THC
quantities, so the drying room should be dark. It does not need to be as
light-proof as the grow room, but direct light exposure must be avoided.

## The Drying Phase

Proper drying is essential for preserving cannabinoid content and preventing
mold. The drying environment should be cool (around 60-70 degrees Fahrenheit)
with moderate humidity and good air circulation. Plants should never be rushed
through drying using ovens, microwaves, or other heat sources. Slow, natural
drying preserves the terpene profile and prevents the harsh taste associated
with improperly dried material.

Fan leaves and trim can be dried on flat surfaces away from direct light with
plenty of fresh air. These smaller pieces typically dry within three weeks and
can be tested for quality at that point.

## Curing: The Canning Method

Curing is the process that transforms dried bud into a smooth, flavorful, and
potent product. Once the branches are brittle enough to snap, the buds should
be clipped from the branches and placed into airtight containers such as glass
jars or metal cans. The stems and large branches are discarded as they contain
only small quantities of cannabinoids.

The containers are stored in a dark place. Every day, the lids should be
opened for several hours (approximately 6 hours is recommended) to allow
moisture exchange and prevent anaerobic conditions that could promote mold.
The buds should be shaken or gently agitated every couple of days to ensure
even curing.

The canning process should continue for 3 to 4 weeks before sampling. Bud
that is 8 weeks into the curing process is considered to be at its peak. After
this point, some of the THC cannabinoids begin to change composition and
convert to different cannabinoid compounds, including [[blesching-cannabis-cannabinoid-terpene-entourage-effect]].
Year-old bud is considered vintage but may have lost potency compared to
freshly cured material.

## Time Adjustment for Pre-Drying

If the plants have been dried for three weeks while hanging upside down before
canning, this time can be subtracted from the canning timeline. Growers can
expect acceptable bud to smoke approximately two weeks after harvest, but
four weeks or more of total processing time produces significantly better
results.

## Utilizing Trim and Leaf Material

Trim and leaf material from the harvest are valuable byproducts. They can be
smoked directly (fan leaves being the lowest quality, trim being the best),
used for making hash or other extracts, or composted. Many growers find that
trim produces excellent water hash or bubble hash, making harvest waste
minimization both economical and practical.
