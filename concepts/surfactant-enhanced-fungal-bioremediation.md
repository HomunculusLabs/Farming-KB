# Surfactant-Enhanced Fungal Bioremediation

## Overview

Surfactant-enhanced bioremediation represents a critical strategy for improving
the degradation of hydrophobic organic pollutants by fungi. Many environmental
contaminants, particularly [[dighton-fungal-extracellular-enzyme-systems-decomposition]] systems.

The application of surfactants in fungal bioremediation draws on principles from
both [[staycare-laccase-enzymes-in-bioremediation]], [[fungal-volatile-organic-compounds-communication]]. In fungal
bioremediation, surfactants serve several key functions:

1. **Increased apparent solubility**: Non-ionic surfactants like Tween 80 can
   dramatically increase water solubility of PAHs such as anthracene, pyrene,
   and benzo[a]pyrene, making them accessible to extracellular fungal enzymes.

2. **Enhanced desorption from soil matrices**: Surfactants displace sorbed
   contaminants from soil organic matter, increasing the fraction available for
   microbial degradation. This is critical for aged, sequestered contaminants.

3. **Improved mass transfer**: By reducing interfacial tension, surfactants
   facilitate transport of hydrophobic pollutants into the aqueous phase where
   fungal enzymes operate.

### Non-Ionic Surfactants and White-Rot Fungi

Research demonstrates that non-ionic surfactants, particularly Tween 80,
significantly enhance PAH degradation by white-rot fungi. Studies with
Bjerkandera sp. strain BOS55 showed two- to fivefold augmentation of degradation
of anthracene, pyrene, and benzo[a]pyrene in the presence of Tween 80 (Bohmer,
Messner & Srebotnik, 1998). The surfactant increased PAH solubility, thereby
enhancing oxidation by [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] from shear-induced inactivation in agitated cultures. Jager,
Croan & Kirk (1985) reported that detergents like Tween 80 or 20 allow high LiP
yields in agitated cultures where air-liquid interface inactivation would
otherwise limit [[ligninolytic-enzyme-production-bioremediation]].

### Bioemulsifiers and Biological Surfactants

Biological emulsifiers produced by microorganisms offer advantages over synthetic
surfactants, including biodegradability and lower toxicity. The bioemulsifier
Alasan enhanced solubilization and biodegradation of polyaromatic hydrocarbons
(Barkay et al., 1999). Some fungi produce surface-active compounds that aid in
accessing hydrophobic substrates in their natural wood-decay habitat, where
lignin and recalcitrant polymers must be solubilized for enzymatic attack.

### Interaction with Fungal Enzyme Systems

The interaction between surfactants and fungal-enzyme-systems-in-bioremediation
is complex. Surfactants influence enzyme activity through substrate presentation
by solubilizing PAHs, enzyme stabilization against denaturation at air-water
interfaces, and potential alteration of fungal cell membrane permeability for
enhanced intracellular uptake of partially oxidized metabolites.
