# Bionutrients Lab — Lactic Acid Bacteria Serum (LABS)

**Updated:** 2026-04-24

Lactic acid bacteria (LAB) serum is one of the major workhorse beneficial [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]] used in [[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]]. LAB is popularly used in composting to arrest foul odors associated with anaerobic decomposition. It thrives and feeds on the ammonia released during decomposition, converting wastes into harmless forms through natural biological processes. As described by Gil Carandang of Herbana Farms, LAB serum is the single most important microorganism in the beneficial indigenous microorganism (BIM) formulation, comprising 50% of the total mix.

Related preparations include [[bionutrients-lab-fish-aminos]], [[bionutrients-lab-fermented-plant-juice-fpj]], and [[lactic-acid-bacteria]] and [[teaming-with-microbes-compost-tea-brewing-guide]], and [[bokashi-composting]] describes how LAB is used in anaerobic fermented compost systems.

## Role in Natural Farming Systems

LAB serves multiple critical functions across plant, animal, and soil applications:

- **Odor control in livestock:** In natural piggery systems, pigs roam pens where floors (substrate) are made of compost, soil, and organic matter like sawdust, sprayed with LAB serum. No cleaning of excretions is needed — LAB converts wastes into harmless forms
- **Organic poultry:** Serum diluted and added to water and feeds of chickens improves nutrient assimilation through better breakdown of food
- **Aquaculture water quality:** Ammonia from fish excretions pollutes water and stresses fish. Regular LAB addition minimizes this ammonia problem and helps complete denitrification
- **Plant and soil health:** Spraying diluted LAB on plant leaves and soil aids decomposition, making more food available and assimilated by plants
- **Composting:** Accelerates decomposition while controlling foul odors from anaerobic conditions
- **Antibacterial properties:** LAB produces enzymes and natural antibiotics, including control of salmonella and E. coli
- **Phyllosphere support:** Application to plant leaves fortifies beneficial microorganisms on the leaf surface that help process organic matter into available nutrients

## Collection Method — From Air

The following method collects lactic acid bacteria from the air using rice wash as a growth medium:

### Stage 1: Rice Wash Fermentation

1. Pour rice wash (solution from washing rice with water) into a container with lid
2. Allow air gap of at least 50-75% of the container — the air space is critical
3. Cover loosely (not vacuum tight, allowing air to still move into the container)
4. Place in a quiet area with no direct sunlight
5. Allow to ferment for 5-7 days at 20-25 degrees C
6. Rice bran will separate and float as a thin film, smelling sour
7. Strain and collect the liquid

The temperature range of 20-25 degrees C is optimal for lactic acid bacteria to dominate over other microorganisms in this initial collection stage.

### Stage 2: Milk Saturation for Pure Culture

1. Put the strained liquid in a bigger container
2. Pour ten parts milk (fresh milk is best; skim or powdered milk can be used)
3. The original liquid has been infected with different microbes including lacto bacilli
4. Saturation of milk eliminates other microorganisms — pure lacto bacilli remain
5. In 5-7 days, carbohydrate, protein, and fat will float, leaving yellow liquid (serum)
6. Dispose the coagulated carbohydrate, protein, and fat (add to compost or feed to animals)
7. The yellow serum contains the pure lactic acid bacteria

### Stage 3: Preservation

- Store in refrigerator, OR
- Add equal amount of crude sugar (diluted with 1/3 water) or molasses
- Do NOT use refined sugar — it is chemically bleached and may affect the bacteria
- Sugar/molasses keeps LAB alive at room temperature by serving as food
- 1:1 ratio is suggested, though any amount of sugar helps maintain viability

## Application Rates

- **Dilution:** Dilute pure culture with 20 parts water (1:20 ratio)
- **Water quality:** Ensure water is not chemically treated (chlorine kills microorganisms)
- **Spray:** 2-4 tablespoons per gallon of water for foliar application
- **Animal feed:** 2-4 tablespoons of diluted serum added to animal water and feed
- **Larger animals:** Use 2-4 tablespoons of diluted serum without further water dilution
- **Application targets:** Plant leaves, soil, compost, animal drinking water and feed

## Role in BIM Formulation

The complete Beneficial Indigenous Microorganism (BIM) recipe uses:

- **50% lactic acid bacteria** (the dominant component)
- **50% other microorganisms** (forest microbes, bamboo microbes, plant-specific microbes)

For plants, the full BIM blend is used. For animals, pure lacto bacilli alone is recommended. Bamboo microorganisms are specifically used for fermenting animal feeds.

## Key Observations

- LAB is used in combination with bionutrients ([[query-how-do-i-use-fermented-plant-extracts-in-my-garden]], fermented fruit juices, ginger-garlic nutrients, brown rice vinegar, fish amino acid) to make it more effective
- The general health improvements observed include better nutrient assimilation, feed conversion, and toxin elimination in animals
- For any kind of imbalance — in soil or digestive systems — lacto bacilli can be of help
- A popular Japanese commercial inoculant contains lactic acid bacteria as its major component, along with photosynthetic bacteria, yeasts, actinomycetes, and fermenting fungi
