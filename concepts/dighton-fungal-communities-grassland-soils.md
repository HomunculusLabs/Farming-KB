## Fungal Communities in Grassland Soils

## Overview

Grassland ecosystems are dominated by [[fungal-functional-diversity-decomposition]] that collectively influence plant community structure, productivity, and ecosystem stability.

## Mycorrhizal Distribution in Grasslands

Read (1991a) placed temperate and tropical grasslands firmly within the AM-dominated zone of his global mycorrhizal distribution model. In grasslands:
- Plant litter is readily decomposed
- Soils contain a higher proportion of nutrients in inorganic form
- AM fungi are adapted for efficient inorganic nutrient uptake
- Lower enzyme capacity for organic nutrient access compared to ECM or ericoid systems

## AM Community Structure and Host Specificity

### Differential AM Communities Among Host Species

- **Johnson et al. (1992)**: AM fungal communities differed among five plant species in a grassland community
- **Eom et al. (2000)**: Different species in a tallgrass prairie had significantly different AM fungal associates after 4 months of growth (Fig. 3.25 in Dighton)

Distinct spore densities of *Glomus intraradices*, *G. mosseae*, and other *Glomus* spp. were found under *Solidago missouriensis*, *Poa pratense*, *Panicum virginiatum*, *Sporobolus heterolepis*, and *Baptisia bracteata*.

### Feedback Mechanisms

This host-specificity suggests feedback loops:
- Plants can dictate the fungal species assemblage on their roots
- Fungal assemblages can influence plant performance
- The result is a dynamic, co-evolved plant-fungal community structure

## AM Diversity and Plant Community Structure

### van der Heijden et al. (1998) -- Calcareous Grassland Mesocosms

Key findings:
- At **low AM diversity**: Plant species composition varied widely as AM species were altered; asymmetric beneficial effects on plant growth caused large shifts in community structure
- At **high AM diversity**: Nutrient acquisition increased across the host community, leading to greater biomass accumulation; changes in AM species composition caused smaller changes in plant community
- **Interpretation**: At low diversity, variability in function among AM species creates asymmetric growth benefits that reshape plant communities. At high diversity, each plant has a greater chance of associating with an efficient AM species, losing the asymmetry.

### Fungicide Effects on Grassland Diversity

Gange et al. (1993) showed that applying fungicide to grassland:
- Reduced total root colonization by AM fungi
- Reduced plant species diversity
- Likely had differential effects on different AM fungal species, reducing [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] influence the ability of clonal plants to colonize new areas via stolons
