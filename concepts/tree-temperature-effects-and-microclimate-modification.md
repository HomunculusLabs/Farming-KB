# Tree Temperature Effects and Microclimate Modification

## Fundamental Principles

Two basic thermodynamic processes govern the [[daubenmire]], 1974).

### White and Light-Coloured Leaves

- **Whitish plants** (wormwood, birch) may reflect **85%** of incoming light
- **Dark shade plant leaves** may reflect as little as **2%** of incoming light

### Design Application for Buildings

White or red-coloured **roof vines over tiles** can effectively lower summer temperatures within buildings or trellis systems. This effect can be enhanced by:

- Fitting **fine water sprays** under trellises
- Installing **damp mulch systems** beneath climbing plants
- Creating cool areas of dense air through combined evaporation and shading
- Drawing cool air from these areas via **induced cross-ventilation**

This combined strategy of shade, evaporation, and reflective foliage represents one of the most effective [[mollison-designers-solar-chimneys-and-passive-cooling-in-desert-housing]] techniques for buildings in hot climates.

## Night-Time Condensation and Warming

Moisture condenses on surfaces cooler than the dew point. Leaves provide excellent condensation surfaces because:

- Leaf surfaces are cooler than surrounding objects at evening due to daytime stomatal evaporation
- Air rising over trees creates additional vertical lift cooling
- The combination of surface cooling and lift cooling condenses atmospheric moisture on the forest

This condensation process releases latent heat, warming the surrounding air at night. The result is a **diurnal temperature moderation** — cooler days and warmer nights — that is one of the most valuable microclimate services provided by trees.

## The Forest as Thermal Buffer

Within the forest canopy, several temperature-moderating effects combine:

1. **Incoming air modification**: Hot dry air entering the forest is shaded, cooled, and humidified. Cold humid air entering is warmed, dehumidified, and slowly released via the crown.
2. **Visible evidence**: Warm humid air can often be observed as misty spirals ascending from the forest crown on cool mornings.
3. **Extreme moderation**: Trees modify extremes of heat and humidity to life-enhancing and tolerable levels for all organisms within the forest ecosystem.

## Wind-Driven Temperature Effects

As streamlines converge over trees or hills, air speed increases along with density and heat, creating fast low-pressure air. To leeward of the obstruction, streamlines diverge, producing:

- Slower airflow
- Higher pressure
- Cooler air

However, if rain has fallen due to streamline compression, the latent heat of evaporation is released, and the resulting drier air can actually be **warmer** than the air mass rising over the obstruction. These pressure differences caused by uplift and descent affect evaporation rates, experienced as wind drying or wind heating.

## Design Guidelines for Temperature Modification

### For Cooling (Hot Climates)

1. **Plant deciduous trees** to the south (northern hemisphere) or north ([[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]]) of buildings for summer shade
2. **Use light-coloured or red-leaved vines** on roof trellises for maximum radiation reflection
3. **Install water features** upwind of living spaces
4. **Combine with mulch and mist systems** under trellises for [[bloomfield-mushroom-evaporative-cooling-and-thermal-regulation]]
5. **Create cross-ventilation paths** from shaded, vegetated areas through buildings

### For Warming (Cold Climates)

1. **Plant evergreen windbreaks** to reduce convective heat loss from buildings
2. **Use dense conifers** on the windward side to slow cold winds
3. **Exploit condensation warming** by maintaining forest canopy for night-time heat release
4. **Design air flow** so that warmed forest air reaches living spaces

### For Humidity Control

1. **In arid climates**: Use trees to humidify dry air through transpiration
2. **In [[humid-tropics-design-and-climate-zones]]**: Use trees with high transpiration rates to dehumidify through absorption
3. **Near water bodies**: Combine trees and open water for maximum moderating effect

## Summary

Trees provide a remarkable suite of temperature modification services through evaporation, condensation, shading, radiation reflection, and wind modification. These effects are not merely passive byproducts of tree biology — they can be deliberately designed into landscapes to create comfortable microclimates for human habitation, reduce energy costs for heating and cooling, and extend [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]] for crops. The key principle is that **each tree species, leaf colour, canopy density, and placement position** contributes to specific thermal outcomes that can be matched to design objectives.
