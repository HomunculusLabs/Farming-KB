## Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum) Cancer Research

## Overview

Ganoderma lucidum, known as Reishi in Japan and Ling Zhi (Mushroom of Immortality) in China, has the longest historical usage of any medicinal mushroom, dating back at least four millennia. It is the most depicted [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]]:
- **Polysaccharides**: Over 100 types isolated, primarily beta-D-glucans with antitumour and immunostimulating activities
- **Triterpenoids**: More than 100 alcohol-soluble triterpenes identified, including highly oxidised lanostane-type triterpenoids (ganoderic acid, ganoderenic acid, lucidenic acid, ganolucidic acid)
- **Proteins**: Including Ling Zhi-8 (LZ-8) with immunomodulatory properties
- **Steroids, lactones, alkaloids, and organic germanium**

## Immunomodulatory Mechanisms

Reishi extracts act on multiple immune pathways:

### Beta-Glucan Receptor-Mediated Effects
- Beta-D-glucans bind to membrane complement receptor type 3 (CR3, CD11b/CD18) on immune effector cells including macrophages
- The glucan-receptor complex is internalised, activating NF-kappa-B
- Beta-glucan overrides normal resistance of iC3b-opsonised tumour cells to cytotoxic activation of phagocyte and NK cell CR3

### Cytokine Production
- Water-soluble extracts activate human T lymphocytes, inducing production of IL-1beta, INF-gamma, TNF-alpha, IL-2, IL-6, and IL-10
- Polysaccharide fraction GLB promotes IL-2 production in a dose-dependent manner and augments cytotoxic T lymphocyte toxicity by up to 100%
- LZ-8 mediates T [[medicinal-mushrooms-biological-response-modifiers]] modifying effects
- Cytokine release from activated immune cells causes anti-proliferation and apoptosis induction in HL-60 and U937 leukemic cells

## Clinical Trials

### Ganopoly Phase I/II Trial (Zhou et al., 2001)

The most rigorous clinical study to date was an open, non-randomised trial of Ganopoly (aqueous G. lucidum extract) in 143 patients with advanced cancers.

**Cancer types treated**: Lung, breast, liver, colorectum, prostate, bladder, brain, and non-Hodgkin's lymphoma. Ninety-three percent had stage IV disease. All had been previously treated with conventional chemotherapy.

**Protocol**: Oral Ganopoly 1800 mg three times daily for 12 weeks, with evaluations at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks. Standard WHO criteria for efficacy assessment and Common Toxicity Criteria for adverse events. Quality of life measured by FACT-G scale.

**Results**:
- Of 100 fully assessable patients, no objective (partial or complete) responses were observed
- 38 of 143 patients (26.6%) achieved stable disease for 12+ weeks (range 12-50 weeks)
- In the stable disease group, FACT-G scores improved by +7.6 at 6 weeks and +10.3 at 12 weeks (P < 0.05)
- Palliative effects on sweating and insomnia observed in many patients
- In stable disease patients, lymphocyte mitogenic reactivity increased 48-52% (P < 0.05) and NK cell activity increased 75% (P < 0.05)
- Only 5 grade I adverse events recorded (3 gastrointestinal: nausea and diarrhoea)

**Conclusion**: While objective responses were not observed, the results indicate Ganopoly could have an adjuvant role in advanced cancer treatment, particularly for immune restoration and quality of life improvement.
