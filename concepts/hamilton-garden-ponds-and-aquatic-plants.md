## Locating the Pond

The pond should form a feature in the garden rather than being tucked away
out of view. Choose a site that receives plenty of sunlight for at least
part of the day, as most aquatic plants prefer sunny conditions and fish
prefer warmer water. Avoid sudden temperature changes by providing some
shade during the hottest part of the day.

Site the pond as far from deciduous trees as possible. Falling leaves
turn the water sour as they rot anaerobically, and trees cast shade over
the water surface. If small children are present, ensure the pond is
visible from the house and consider postponing construction until they
are older, or fence the area securely.

## Pond Construction Methods

Several approaches to building a pond are available. The simplest is to
purchase a ready-made fibreglass shell and dig a hole to fit it. For
greater flexibility in shape, dig a hole to your own specifications and
line it with plastic sheeting, though this will eventually disintegrate. A
rubber pond liner is more expensive but far more durable and allows you
to choose any shape.

To calculate the minimum liner size, measure the maximum length and width
of the pond, then add twice the maximum depth to each measurement. Line
the hole with a thick layer of soft material, sand, or old carpet to
protect the liner from sharp rocks. Paving stones around the edge secure
the liner and make planting and maintenance easier.

## Pond Design and Depth

A well-designed pond incorporates several depth zones to accommodate
different types of aquatic plants. The marsh garden at the edge consists
of damp soil for plants that like wet ground. A shallow area about 2in
(5cm) deep suits plants with roots in shallow water. A marginal shelf,
3-5in (7-12cm) deep, holds containers of marginal plants. The deep water
area should be at least 18in (45cm) deep to accommodate most water lilies
and provide overwintering space for fish and frogs.

Include a very shallow area no more than 2in (5cm) deep where the
smallest fish can gather in warmer water. This also provides a refuge for
frog spawn, which larger fish may otherwise eat. Place rocks in the marsh
garden to provide hibernation sites for frogs.

## Types of Aquatic Plants

### Deep-Water Aquatics

These plants root at the bottom of the pond with leaves floating on the
surface. Water lilies (Nymphaea) are the most well-known and are both
decorative and useful for reducing algae growth. Other deep-water plants
include water hawthorn (Aponogeton distachyum), water violet (Hottonia
palustris), and water crowfoot ([[myriophyllum-spicatum]]), Canadian pondweed ([[potamogeton-crispus]]). Plant one bunch per 2 square yards/metres
