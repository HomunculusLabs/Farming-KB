## Supplementation at Spawning: Delayed Release Nutrients

One of the newest advances in Agaricus culture is the development of
delayed-release nutrients added to compost at spawning. These
supplements are specially formulated nutrients encapsulated in a
denatured protein coat designed to become available to the growing
mushrooms during the first three flushes, precisely when nutrient
demand is highest. The application rate is 5-7% of the dry weight of
the substrate. Yield increases of 0.5 to 1 pound per square foot are
normal with this method.

Complete and thorough mixing of the delayed-release nutrients
throughout the substrate is essential to success. Uneven distribution
creates pockets of enriched substrate that are more susceptible to
contamination while leaving other areas under-supplemented. The
denatured protein coat is the key innovation: it protects the nutrients
from rapid breakdown during spawn running, when the mycelium is
primarily in vegetative mode, and releases them gradually as the crop
enters the generative fruiting phase.

Caution is warranted with supplementation at spawning. These materials
enrich the substrate, making it more suitable to contaminants if
factors predisposing to their growth are present. A hygienic
spawning environment, proper pasteurization, and rapid colonization
are even more critical when supplements are used. If colonization
is slow, the enriched substrate becomes a prime target for
competitor molds, particularly Trichoderma. The spawn rate should
be increased when using supplements to ensure the fastest possible
colonization.

## Supplementation at Casing (S.A.C.)

Supplementation at Casing (SACing) is another method used to boost the
nutritional content of the substrate after full colonization. The
materials used are soy bean meal, cottonseed meal, and/or ground rye,
wheat, or kafir corn grains. The fully colonized substrate is
thoroughly mixed with any one of these materials at a rate of 10% of
the dry weight of the substrate. With this type of nutrient
enhancement, yield increases of 0.5 to 2 pounds per square foot are
possible, representing a substantial improvement over unsupplemented
crops.

Both the substrate and the supplements must be clean and free from
contaminants; otherwise contamination will spread and threaten the
entire culture. This is a critical requirement because the mixing
process at casing creates many opportunities for introducing
contaminants. The supplement material itself can harbor mold spores,
bacteria, or insect eggs if not properly stored and handled. High
substrate temperatures should be anticipated on the second to third
day after supplementation as the mycelium responds to the newly
available nutrients. This temperature spike must be managed through
increased ventilation and cooling.

## Super Spawning: Active Mycelium Propagation

Super spawning, also called "active mycelium spawning" or the
Hunke-Till process, is a technique where a set amount of substrate is
inoculated and colonized in the normal manner, then the fully run
substrate is used as inoculum to spawn increased amounts of a similar
substrate. One could theoretically pyramid a small quantity of
