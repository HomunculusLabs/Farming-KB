## Mollusc Control

Snails and slugs are almost totally controlled by a duck flock
on range. Several large lizards (Tiliqua spp.) also feed
primarily on snails, but ducks are far more effective at scale.
Ducks can be ranged seasonally (autumn to spring) in plant
systems, and in summer on marshlands. Ducks will eat seedlings,
so appropriate scheduling is essential to avoid damage to young
crops.

## Range Requirements

Ducks differ from chickens in their habitat needs. They require
access to shallow water for dabbling and foraging. Marsh plants,
aquatic weeds, snails, and insects form a significant portion
of their diet. When given access to ponds, wetlands, and
marginal areas, ducks convert otherwise unproductive zones into
sources of eggs, meat, and manure.

## Marsh and Aquatic Integration

Duck and waterfowl nest in claypan swamps and marsh areas. As
the swamps dry out, marsupials and rodents take refuge in the
same areas. This creates a productive succession of use. Banks
raised in marshes allow foot or vehicle access to place and
service duck nest boxes, to harvest fruit and vine, and to
attend to fish ponds. Earth mounds in ponds isolate useful but
rampant species while providing duck habitat.
