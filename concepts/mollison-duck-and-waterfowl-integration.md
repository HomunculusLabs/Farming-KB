# Duck and Waterfowl Integration in Permaculture Systems

Ducks have always been kept in the same range area as chickens
in mixed farm systems, but they need marsh plants, snails, and
shallow water for dabbling. They supply up to 200 eggs per year
each, are relatively disease-free, and are good foragers. Given
[[fukuoka-natural-predator-protection]], they thrive on the mixed farm. Mollison
places special emphasis on the unique role of waterfowl in
[[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]].

## Mollusc Control

Snails and slugs are almost totally controlled by a duck flock
on range. Several large lizards (Tiliqua spp.) also feed
primarily on snails, but ducks are far more effective at scale.
Ducks can be ranged seasonally (autumn to spring) in plant
systems, and in summer on marshlands. Ducks will eat seedlings,
so appropriate scheduling is essential to avoid damage to young
crops.

## Range Requirements

Ducks differ from chickens in their habitat needs. They require
access to shallow water for dabbling and foraging. Marsh plants,
aquatic weeds, snails, and insects form a significant portion
of their diet. When given access to ponds, wetlands, and
marginal areas, ducks convert otherwise unproductive zones into
sources of eggs, meat, and manure.

## Marsh and Aquatic Integration

Duck and waterfowl nest in claypan swamps and marsh areas. As
the swamps dry out, marsupials and rodents take refuge in the
same areas. This creates a productive succession of use. Banks
raised in marshes allow foot or vehicle access to place and
service duck nest boxes, to harvest fruit and vine, and to
attend to fish ponds. Earth mounds in ponds isolate useful but
rampant species while providing duck habitat.

## Comparison with Chickens

All that can be said about chickens applies to ducks, guineafowl,
turkey, and wildlife such as pheasant and quail on range. The
latter species differ only in food preferences, preferring more
snails, berries, and a higher proportion of insect foods. Ducks
are more disease-resistant than chickens and require less
[[savory-supplemental-feeding-and-winter-management]] when given access to natural water bodies
and marsh forage.

## Aquatic Plant Nurseries

Aquatic and edge plant nurseries, including fish forages,
insectary species, and marshland perennials for bee fodders,
duck forage, and wildlife refuges, form an essential support
enterprise for waterfowl integration. These nurseries provide
the plant materials that sustain duck populations while also
serving multiple other functions in the permaculture system.

## Geese as Orchard Grazers

Geese are the traditional small grazers of mixed orchards,
replacing lawn-mowers and tractors to control grasses and
groundcovers. In the subtropics, rampant greens such as
comfrey, Tradescantia, arrowroot (Canna), [[comparison-cassava-vs-taro-vs-sweet-potato-vs-yam]] greens,
cassava foliage, and leguminous tree leaves all supply green
crop for poultry and [[permaculture-domestic-animals]]. Many also yield surplus
[[root-crop-harvest-and-storage]] for cold periods, fed as a boiled mash.
