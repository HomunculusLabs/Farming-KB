# LSD Pharmacology

The pharmacology of lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD): mechanism of action, receptor interactions, potency, pharmacokinetics, and effects.

## Chemical Identity

**LSD** (D-lysergic acid diethylamide) is a semi-synthetic ergoline alkaloid derived from lysergic acid, a component of ergot alkaloids produced by the fungus *Claviceps purpurea*. Its molecular formula is C20H25N3O, and it has a molecular weight of 323.4 g/mol.

LSD was the twenty-fifth compound in Albert Hofmann's series of lysergic acid derivatives synthesized at Sandoz, hence the original designation LSD-25.

## Mechanism of Action

### Primary Target: Serotonin 5-HT2A Receptor

LSD acts primarily as a **serotonin receptor agonist**, with its most significant actions at the **5-HT2A receptor subtype**:

- LSD binds to 5-HT2A receptors with very high affinity, acting as a partial agonist.
- This is the same receptor that mediates the effects of psilocybin, DMT, and mescaline -- all "classical psychedelics" share this primary mechanism.
- 5-HT2A receptors are concentrated in the cerebral cortex, particularly in layer V of the prefrontal cortex, an area critical for integrating sensory information, self-awareness, and executive function.
- Receptor activation triggers increased glutamate release, which in turn activates AMPA and NMDA receptors, producing cascading effects on neural activity.

### Broad Receptor Profile

Unlike psilocybin, which is relatively selective, LSD interacts with a broad range of receptors:

- **5-HT2A**: Primary psychedelic effects (partial agonist)
- **5-HT2B, 5-HT2C**: Other serotonin receptor subtypes (agonist/partial agonist)
- **5-HT1A**: Serotonin autoreceptor (partial agonist) -- may contribute to mood effects
- **Dopamine D2/D3 receptors**: Moderate affinity (partial agonist) -- may contribute to the stimulant-like qualities
- **Adrenergic receptors**: Alpha-1 and alpha-2 receptors -- may contribute to physical effects (pupil dilation, increased heart rate)

This broad receptor profile may explain why LSD's subjective effects are longer-lasting and in some ways qualitatively different from more selective psychedelics like psilocybin or DMT.

## Extraordinary Potency

LSD is one of the most potent pharmacologically active substances known:

- An active dose ranges from approximately **20-200 micrograms** (0.02-0.2 milligrams).
- Hofmann's first deliberate dose of 250 micrograms was very high -- a typical modern full dose is 100-150 micrograms.
- For comparison, this is approximately 1/10,000th the weight of an aspirin tablet.
- A single gram of LSD crystal contains approximately 5,000-10,000 active doses.
- The reasons for this extraordinary potency include LSD's very high receptor affinity and its unique molecular conformation, which fits precisely into the 5-HT2A receptor binding pocket.

## Pharmacokinetics

### Absorption and Distribution

- LSD is well absorbed through mucous membranes (oral, sublingual, nasal). Oral absorption is reliable.
- Onset of effects: typically 30-90 minutes after oral ingestion.
- Peak effects: 2-4 hours after ingestion.
- LSD readily crosses the blood-brain barrier due to its lipophilic (fat-soluble) properties.

### Duration of Action

- Total duration: approximately **8-12 hours**.
- The comedown/aftereffects can extend several additional hours.
- LSD's long duration compared to psilocybin (4-6 hours) or DMT (15-60 minutes when smoked) is a distinguishing feature and an important factor in set and setting preparation.

### Metabolism and Elimination

- LSD is metabolized primarily in the liver.
- Major metabolic pathways include N-dealkylation, hydroxylation, and conjugation.
- The metabolites are largely inactive pharmacologically.
- LSD is not detected by standard drug tests; specific detection requires specialized and relatively expensive testing methods.
- The exact elimination half-life is approximately 3-5 hours.

### Tolerance

- LSD produces rapid **tachyphylaxis** (tolerance): repeated doses within 48-72 hours show dramatically diminished effects.
- Cross-tolerance exists between LSD and other classical psychedelics (psilocybin, mescaline, DMT).
- Tolerance dissipates rapidly, typically within 3-7 days of abstinence.

## Subjective Effects

### Psychological Effects

- Visual hallucinations: colors become more vivid, patterns appear, geometric forms, synesthesia
- Altered sense of time: time dilation, moments seeming eternal
- Ego dissolution: sense of boundary between self and world dissolving
- Enhanced emotional responsiveness: emotions become more intense
- Altered thought patterns: novel associations, philosophical insights, cosmic consciousness
- Mystical experiences: feelings of unity, sacredness, transcendence, ineffability

### Physical Effects

- Pupil dilation (mydriasis)
- Increased heart rate and blood pressure
- Elevated body temperature
- Sweating
- Loss of appetite
- Insomnia
- Tremor
- Mild ataxia (impaired coordination)

### Adverse Effects

- **Anxiety/panic reactions**: Can occur especially at higher doses or in unprepared users
- **Persistent perception disorder (HPPD)**: Rare condition involving ongoing visual disturbances
- **Psychotic reactions**: Very rare, typically in individuals with pre-existing vulnerability
- LSD is not considered physically addictive and has no significant withdrawal syndrome

## Comparison with Psilocybin

| Property | LSD | Psilocybin |
|---|---|---|
| Active dose | 20-200 mcg | 5-30 mg |
| Duration | 8-12 hours | 4-6 hours |
| Primary receptor | 5-HT2A | 5-HT2A |
| Receptor selectivity | Broad (serotonin, dopamine, adrenergic) | Relatively selective (serotonin) |
| Source | Semi-synthetic from ergot | Natural from mushrooms |
| Tolerance | Rapid; cross-tolerant with other psychedelics | Rapid; cross-tolerant |
