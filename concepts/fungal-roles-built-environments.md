### Insurance and Legal Issues
Insurance companies use "dry rot" as a blanket exclusion in homeowners policies. Victims of *Meruliporia* have successfully challenged this in court by arguing that damage from this specific fungus represents a new and unforeseen disaster. The shift toward steel framing in home construction is partly driven by fungal decay concerns.

### Contemporary Targets
Steel ships are resistant to dry rot, but wood-hulled pleasure boats and recreational vehicles (RVs) remain vulnerable. Fungi can establish in wet locations within vehicles (e.g., flooring beneath plumbing leaks) and use rhizomorphs to colonize the rest of the structure. Stationary homes remain the primary target for dry rot fungi.

## Other Building Fungi

- **Gloeophyllum trabeum**: Causes substantial damage in California homes; implicated in the recent increase in wood rot in California. Little attention has been paid relative to *Meruliporia* despite its destructive potential
- **Coniophora puteana**: Tolerant of creosote-treated timber
- **Lentinus lepideus**: The "train wrecker"; tolerant of creosote and copper-arsenate treatments
- **Stachybotrys chartarum**: Indoor mould producing mycotoxins; prefers warmer temperatures than dry rot fungi

## Building Materials as Substrates

Fungi attack diverse building materials:
- **Timber**: Primary substrate for most building decay fungi
- **Concrete and mortar**: Colonized and degraded by fungi producing biogenic sulfuric and nitric acids
- **Glass**: *Penicillium simplicissimum* and *P. expansum* identified as bio-weathering agents
- **Plaster and insulation**: Can support fungal growth under moist conditions
- **Building stone**: Fungi contribute to biodeterioration through acid production, physical penetration, and mineral transformations

## Detection and Control

Detection methods include visual inspection, moisture measurement, and molecular identification. Control focuses on moisture management, removal of affected material, and chemical treatment. Cutting rhizomorphs can kill dry rot colonies in dry homes by severing their water supply. Understanding fungal biology is essential for effective prevention and remediation strategies.

## Indoor Airborne Fungi

### Sources and Dispersal

Indoor fungal spores originate from:

- Outdoor air (the primary source for most indoor environments)
- Indoor growth on damp materials (walls, ceilings, carpets)
- HVAC systems (heating, ventilation, air conditioning)
- Houseplants and potting soil
- Pets and human activity
- Food storage and preparation areas

### Common Indoor Genera

- **Cladosporium**: The most common airborne fungus worldwide; outdoors on dead plant material; indoors on damp surfaces
- **Penicillium**: Common on damp building materials, food, and textiles; some species produce mycotoxins
- **Aspergillus**: Ubiquitous; some species (A. fumigatus) are serious human pathogens
- **Alternaria**: Common allergen; associated with damp areas and plant material
- **Stachybotrys chartarum**: "Black mold"; associated with water-damaged buildings; produces mycotoxins
- **Chaetomium**: Indicator of chronic water damage; cellulolytic species
- **Trichoderma**: Common on damp wood and building materials

### Health Impacts

**Allergic responses**: Allergic rhinitis, asthma exacerbation, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, allergic fungal sinusitis.

**Toxic effects**: Mycotoxin exposure (especially from Stachybotrys, Aspergillus, Penicillium), sick building syndrome, trichothecene mycotoxins from Stachybotrys.

**Infections**: Invasive aspergillosis (A. fumigatus in immunocompromised patients), dermatophytosis, various opportunistic infections.

**Risk factors**: Dampness and moisture problems, poor ventilation, water damage, condensation on cold surfaces, inadequate building maintenance.

## Food Spoilage Fungi

### Xerophilic Spoilage Agents

Xerophilic fungi are the primary cause of spoilage in dried and intermediate-moisture foods:

- **Aspergillus species**: Major spoilers of cereals, nuts, spices, dried fruits
- **Penicillium species**: Spoilage of bread, cheese, fruits, cured meats
- **Wallemia sebi**: Extremely xerotolerant; found in dried fish, salted foods
- **Xeromyces bisporus**: Among the most xerophilic fungi known

### Mycotoxin Producers

- **[[thermophilic-fungi]]**: Spoilage in heat-processed or stored foods
- **Mesophilic fungi**: Most common spoilage agents at ambient temperatures
