## The Nunc Stans in Aquinas

Thomas Aquinas developed the concept of the "nunc stans"—literally, the "standing now"—as part of his theological account of God"s relationship to time.

For Aquinas, God exists in the nunc stans: an eternal present in which all moments of time are simultaneously present, not sequentially ordered.

This is not a claim about God's psychological experience of time but about the ontological structure of eternity: in eternity, there is no before or after, only an eternal now.

McKenna appropriates this concept to describe the phenomenological quality of the peak tryptamine experience, in which the experiencer feels that all of time is simultaneously present.

The identification of the psychedelic state with the nunc stans is bold: it suggests that what theology attributes to God is in fact accessible to human consciousness through chemistry.

The parallel is not exact—Aquinas's nunc stans is an ontological claim about God's mode of being, while McKenna's is a phenomenological report about a specific kind of experience—but the structural similarity is striking.

## The Standing Now in Phenomenological Terms

In the tryptamine state, McKenna reports, the ordinary experience of temporal flow gives way to an experience of timeless presence—a "now" that does not pass but stands.

Events are not experienced as occurring one after another but as coexisting in a single, all-encompassing present.

The past is not remembered but is somehow directly present; the future is not anticipated but is somehow already included in the eternal now.

This phenomenological transformation of temporal experience is not unique to tryptamines—it is reported in mystical traditions worldwide, from Christian contemplation to Buddhist samadhi to the [[quantum-mechanics-fundamentals]], in which time may be an emergent property of a more fundamental, atemporal reality.

## The Eternal Return

McKenna connects the transtemporal quality of the tryptamine experience to the concept of the eternal return found in Nietzsche and in various mystical traditions.

In the nunc stans, all moments are simultaneously present, which means that the distinction between one moment and another is revealed as illusory.

This has the phenomenological consequence that the experiencer feels that they are not merely witnessing eternity but are identical with it—that their own consciousness is the eternal consciousness looking at itself.

The dissolution of the boundary between self and other, between subject and object, between now and then, is a hallmark of both the tryptamine experience and the [[mystical-experience]] in general.

McKenna suggests that this dissolution is not a loss of identity but a gain—a recognition that the individual self is a local expression of a universal consciousness that is eternal.
