# Fungal Phylogeny: Microsporidia and Slime Molds

Molecular phylogenetics has revolutionized understanding of fungal
evolutionary relationships, revealing surprising connections and
redefining the boundaries of [[spore-production-geotropic-sensitivity-coupling-basidiomycete-badham-1982]]) and animal-like (amoeboid movement)
characteristics.

### Acrasid Slime Molds

Acrasid slime molds occupy a basal position in phylogenetic analyses,
representing an early-diverging lineage. This basal position is
significant for understanding the evolution of multicellularity and
social behavior in eukaryotes.

### Phylogenetic Analyses

Baldauf et al. (2000) used multi-gene phylogenetic analyses to resolve
key relationships, including:

- The Fungi-Microsporidia connection
- The grouping of dictyostelid and plasmodial slime molds
- The basal position of acrasid slime molds

These analyses combined both morphological and molecular data to
produce robust phylogenetic hypotheses.

## Implications for Fungal Classification

The phylogenetic discoveries have forced significant reclassification:

- Microsporidia moved from Protozoa to Fungi
- Several traditional fungal groups have been reorganized based on
  molecular evidence
- The boundaries between Fungi and other eukaryotic kingdoms are
  better understood
