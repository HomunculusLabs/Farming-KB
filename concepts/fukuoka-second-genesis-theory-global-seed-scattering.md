# Second Genesis Theory

## Overview

The **Second Genesis** is Masanobu Fukuoka's most radical and controversial proposal for healing the planet's deserts and degraded landscapes. Rather than attempting to restore ecosystems to some prehuman baseline, Fukuoka advocated broadcasting the seeds of all plant species — trees, vegetables, grains, legumes, grasses, ferns, mosses, and lichens — along with fungi, bacteria, and other soil microorganisms, across vast desert areas simultaneously. By providing nature with a full palette of species and stepping back, nature would establish a new ecological balance suited to present conditions rather than past conditions.

## The Problem with Restoration to Baseline

Fukuoka argued that the idea of restoring landscapes to their original, prehuman condition is fundamentally misguided. The reasons include:

- **Soil has eroded and become drier** due to thousands of years of agriculture, overgrazing, and deforestation
- **Plant communities and microbial balances** have been altered beyond recognition by plowing and agricultural chemicals
- **Animals and plants** are becoming extinct from habitat elimination
- **The seas are becoming more acidic** and even the climate is changing
- **People travel freely everywhere**, carrying seeds and microorganisms with them, so the world's biota are already thoroughly mixed

Even if we went to the trouble of putting back the plants that were native to a certain place, there is no guarantee that they would thrive there anymore. Conditions are far different today from what they were even one hundred years ago.

## The Core Proposal

Without questioning whether seeds were native or non-native, Fukuoka proposed mixing:

- Seeds of **forest trees** and **fruit trees**
- **Perennials**, **vegetables**, **grasses**, and **legumes**
- **Ferns**, **mosses**, and **lichens**
- **Fungi**, **bacteria**, and other **soil microorganisms**
- **Black forest soil** as a rich source of microorganisms and their spores

All would be encased in [[fukuoka-clay-seed-pellets]] and broadcast over the desert from airplanes or by hand. In extreme deserts, including fertile jungle or forest soil in the pellets would be the most economical means of reintroducing microorganisms.

## Why Abolish Plant Quarantine

Fukuoka called for the complete abolition of plant quarantine regulations. His reasoning was:

- The world has become smaller and people travel everywhere, carrying seeds and microorganisms
- The reality is that plants and animals of the world have already been thoroughly mixed together
- Quarantine systems have rapidly become obsolete
- Mixing all species together and scattering them worldwide would give nature a full palette to work with

This was one of his most controversial positions, as plant quarantines are considered essential by most agricultural and ecological authorities to prevent the spread of invasive species.

## The Mechanism of Recovery

Fukuoka described a cascading recovery process:

1. Seeds germinate after rain, creating temporary green cover
2. Green cover protects against radiant heat, lowering soil temperature
3. Even if drought follows and most plants die, heat-resistant survivors persist
4. In the shade of survivors, grasses, shrubs, and trees grow
5. Green summons more green — insects, birds, and animals arrive and scatter more seeds
6. A single tree acts as a pump, bringing underground water to the surface
7. Mist transpired from leaves acts as both sprinkler and fan
8. The geometric increase of vegetation exceeds imagination

## Regional Adaptations

Fukuoka specified that seed mixes should include plants adapted to local conditions:

- **California and India**: seeds of native plants that grew before desertification
- **Africa and Thailand**: heat-resistant plants
- **Somalia**: salt-resistant plants

In India specifically, he identified more than five hundred varieties of trees bearing edible nuts and more than five hundred varieties of fruit trees, plus five hundred varieties of grains, vegetables, and green manure that could be sown on the barren Deccan Plateau and in the desert.

## Philosophical Foundation

The Second Genesis theory flows directly from Fukuoka's core philosophical positions:

- **No intrinsic value distinctions**: There is no good or bad among life-forms on earth; each has its role and is necessary
- **Nonintervention**: People should provide materials and then let nature decide
- **Rejection of human selection**: Choosing "useful" trees and calling other plants "weeds" destroys the very plants enriching and holding soil together
- **Holism**: A tree cannot grow in isolation; tall trees, midsized trees, shrubs, and understory plants must all grow together

## Resignation to Failure as Strategy

A crucial but often overlooked aspect of the Second Genesis is Fukuoka's acceptance of high failure rates:

- Seeds are sown "resigned to the fact that if a drought followed the rain, a majority of the plants might die"
- This acceptance of loss is the opposite of conventional agriculture's insistence on control and guaranteed yields
- Even if most plants die, the survivors — especially heat-resistant, drought-tolerant species — persist
- In the shade of survivors, new vegetation emerges naturally
- The geometric increase of diverse plant communities eventually exceeds all expectations

This "resignation" is not passivity but a sophisticated understanding of how natural succession actually works. Conventional revegetation projects fail because they try to guarantee success for every planted seed, which requires irrigation, fertilizers, and protection — inputs that ultimately create dependency rather than self-sustaining ecosystems.

## Seed Mix Composition

Fukuoka was specific about what to include in the seed pellets for different environments. For desert areas, the mix would include:

- Seeds of all plants: forest trees, fruit trees, perennials, vegetables, grasses, and legumes
- Ferns, mosses, and lichens for ground cover and moisture retention
- Fungi, bacteria, and other soil microorganisms to rebuild soil biology
- Black forest soil as a rich inoculant of microbial spores
- Herbs such as derris, Japanese star anise, Japanese andromeda, lacquer tree, and Chinaberry to make pellets resistant to insects
- Binding compounds of lime, bittern, and seaweed paste to harden the pellets

For specific regions, he recommended adding native plants from before desertification occurred, heat-resistant varieties for Africa, and salt-resistant plants for coastal deserts like Somalia.

## Relationship to Natural Farming

The Second Genesis applies the same principles Fukuoka used on his farm to a global scale. On his farm, he started by planting a haphazard mixture of fruit trees, vegetables, grains, and clover among acacia trees, then observed which plants thrived. He neither cleared the land nor tilled it, and used no fertilizers, herbicides, or pesticides. After forty-five years, it had become a fruit tree jungle. The Second Genesis extends this "do-nothing" approach to the entire planet's degraded landscapes.

## Criticisms and Limitations

The Second Genesis theory has been criticized on several grounds:

- Risk of introducing **invasive species** that could outcompete native plants
- **Ethical concerns** about deliberately mixing global biota
- Practical difficulties of collecting and broadcasting seeds of thousands of species
- Lack of controlled scientific studies validating the approach at scale
- Potential for unintended ecological consequences

Fukuoka acknowledged some of these concerns but maintained that the greater danger was inaction, given the accelerating rate of desertification worldwide.
