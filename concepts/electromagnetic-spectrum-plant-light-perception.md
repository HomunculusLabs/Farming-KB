## Electromagnetic Spectrum and Plant Light Perception

## Overview

The electromagnetic spectrum encompasses all forms of electromagnetic
radiation, from radio waves to gamma rays. Plants have evolved to detect
specific bands within this spectrum for photosynthesis, photomorphogenesis,
and [[daniel-chamovitz]] in *What a Plant Knows*
(2012).

## The Electromagnetic Spectrum

Electromagnetic waves span an enormous range of wavelengths and
frequencies:

- **Radio waves**: ~1 mm to > 100 km. Used for AM/FM radio,
  telecommunications. Antennas are metres tall because wavelengths are
  long
- **Microwaves**: ~1 mm to 1 m. Used for radar, cooking
- **Infrared**: ~700 nm to 1 mm. Felt as heat, not visible
- **[[blue-light-phototropism-red-light-photoperiodism-dual-plant-vision-system]] has the shortest wavelength in
the visible range, red the longest, with green, yellow, and orange in
between — which is why rainbows always display the same colour ordering.

## How Photoreceptors Absorb Light

Both human and plant photoreception work on the same fundamental principle:
specific chemicals absorb photons of specific wavelengths.

### Human Photoreception

The human retina contains two photoreceptor types:

- **Rods** (~125 million): Sensitive to all visible light, enable
  low-light (night) vision but not colour. Contain rhodopsin
- **Cones** (~6 million): Respond to specific colours via photopsins
  (red, green, blue). Enable colour vision in bright light
- Purple light activates rhodopsin plus blue and red photopsins, but
  not green — showing how overlapping sensitivities enable colour
  discrimination

The retina covers a passport-photo-sized area, giving ~130-megapixel
equivalent resolution.

### Plant Photoreception

Plants lack spatial imaging but have broader spectral coverage:

- **Photoreceptor proteins** absorb specific wavelengths, triggering
  conformational changes that initiate signaling cascades
- Multiple receptor types allow plants to detect UV, blue, red, and
  far-red simultaneously
- Unlike human eyes, plant photoreceptors are distributed throughout
  the plant body, not concentrated in a single organ
