# Goat Keeping Guide

Goats are among the most versatile and practical homestead livestock. They provide milk, meat, fiber (certain breeds), brush clearing services, and excellent companionship. Their relatively small size, hardiness, and adaptability make them ideal for small-acreage operations.

## Choosing the Right Breed

### Dairy Breeds
- **Nubian** — High butterfat milk (4-5%), gentle temperament, large size. Good for cheese and butter making (see [[silvopasture-systems]] are ideal for goats.
- **Hay** — Good quality grass or grass-legume mix. Avoid moldy hay (causes listeriosis).
- **Grain** — Dairy does need grain during lactation (1-2 lbs/day depending on production).
- **Minerals** — Free-choice loose goat minerals (NOT sheep minerals — goats need copper).
- **Water** — Clean, fresh water always available. Goats are picky drinkers and will reduce intake if water is dirty.

## Health Management

### Common Health Issues
- **Internal parasites** — The biggest health challenge for goats. Haemonchus contortus (barber pole worm) is the most deadly. Manage through pasture rotation, FAMACHA scoring, and strategic deworming.
- **Hoof trimming** — Every 6-8 weeks. Overgrown hooves cause lameness and infection.
- **CAE (Caprine Arthritis Encephalitis)** — Viral, incurable. Test before purchasing. Spread through colostrum.
- **CL (Caseous Lymphadenitis)** — Bacterial, causes abscesses. Test before purchasing.
- **Ketosis** — Energy deficiency in late pregnancy or early lactation. Prevent with proper nutrition.
- **Mastitis** — Udder infection, common in dairy does. Monitor milk quality and udder health.

### Breeding
- Does reach breeding age at 7-8 months (prefer 12+ months for better development)
- Gestation: ~150 days (5 months)
- Breeding season: August-January (most breeds are seasonal)
- Plan kidding for spring when pasture is available
- Buck-to-doe ratio: 1:20-30 for hand breeding

## Milk Production

Dairy goats are the backbone of many homestead dairy operations:

- Peak production occurs at 6-8 weeks post-kidding
- Lactation lasts 10-12 months with proper management
- Expect 1-3 gallons/day depending on breed and individual
- Milk must be filtered and cooled rapidly to prevent off-flavors
