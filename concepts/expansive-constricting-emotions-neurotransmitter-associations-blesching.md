# Expansive and Constricting Emotions - Neurotransmitter Associations

## Overview

[[uwe-blesching]]'s *The [[cannabis-health-index]]* (2015) presents a model categorizing
emotions and their associated neurochemicals into two broad groups: expansive
emotions (linked to healing, openness, and well-being) and constricting emotions
(linked to stress, defense, and contraction). This framework bridges the gap
between subjective emotional experience and the underlying molecular biology,
with particular emphasis on how the endocannabinoid system modulates both
categories of emotional states.

## Expansive Emotions and Their Neurochemistry

Expansive emotions are associated with parasympathetic nervous system activation,
relaxation, social bonding, and subjective well-being. Blesching identifies six
primary neurochemicals linked to expansive states:

### Acetylcholine — "I Remember"
Associated with learning, memory consolidation, and focused attention. In the
expansive context, acetylcholine supports the cognitive aspects of emotional
processing—the ability to reflect on experiences, integrate memories, and
maintain present-moment awareness. Adequate acetylcholine is associated with
mental clarity and the capacity for insight.

### Anandamide — "I Am at Ease"
The endogenous cannabinoid that binds to CB1 receptors. Anandamide (from the
Sanskrit *ananda*, meaning "bliss") produces a sense of inner peace, contentment,
and physical relaxation. It is rapidly degraded by FAAH (fatty acid amide
hydrolase), and its levels are modulated by exercise, stress, and dietary factors.
Blesching positions anandamide as the central molecule of the expansive emotional
spectrum.

### Endogenous Opioids — "It Feels So Good When the Pain Stops"
Endorphins and enkephalins produce analgesia and euphoria. Their release is
associated with exercise ("runner's high"), social bonding, laughter, and
positive social interactions. The opioid system interacts extensively with
the endocannabinoid system through receptor heterodimerization and shared
downstream signaling pathways.

### GABA — "I Am Melting"
Gamma-aminobutyric acid is the brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter,
counterbalancing the excitatory effects of glutamate. GABAergic transmission
produces feelings of calm, relaxation, and reduced anxiety. Many anxiolytic
drugs (benzodiazepines) work by enhancing GABA signaling. Cannabis-derived
compounds, particularly THC in low doses, can modulate GABA release.

### Oxytocin — "I Feel for You"
The "bonding hormone" released during physical touch, social bonding, childbirth,
and positive social interactions. Oxytocin promotes trust, empathy, and
prosocial behavior while reducing fear and social anxiety. It interacts with
the endocannabinoid system in mediating social reward and pair bonding.

### Serotonin — "I Am Happy"
Serotonin (5-HT) modulates mood, appetite, sleep, and social behavior. Low
serotonin is associated with depression and anxiety. The serotonergic system
interacts extensively with the endocannabinoid system—CB1 receptors are found
on serotonergic neurons, and both systems modulate mood and emotional processing.

## Constricting Emotions and Their Neurochemistry

Constricting emotions are associated with sympathetic nervous system activation,
the stress response, and defensive physiological states:

### Epinephrine — "I Am Scared"
The primary fight-or-flight hormone, released by the adrenal medulla in
response to acute threat. Epinephrine increases heart rate, blood pressure,
and blood glucose while redirecting blood flow to muscles. Chronic epinephrine
elevation contributes to cardiovascular stress and anxiety disorders.

### Dopamine — "I Am Motivated"
While dopamine is essential for motivation and reward, excessive or dysregulated
dopamine signaling is associated with addiction, compulsive behavior, and
manic states. Blesching places dopamine in the constricting category when
its pursuit becomes driven or anxious rather than naturally rewarding.

### Norepinephrine — "I Am Attentive!"
Norepinephrine mediates alertness, vigilance, and focused attention. In the
constricting context, chronic norepinephrine elevation produces hypervigilance,
anxiety, and the inability to relax. The endocannabinoid system modulates
norepinephrine release in the locus coeruleus, the brain's primary
norepinenergic center.

### Cortisol — "I Am Stressed"
The primary glucocorticoid stress hormone, released by the adrenal cortex in
response to ACTH stimulation. Chronic cortisol elevation is associated with
immune suppression, abdominal fat deposition, insulin resistance, hippocampal
atrophy, and impaired wound healing. Cortisol interacts extensively with the
endocannabinoid system—CB1 receptor activation can reduce HPA axis activity
and cortisol release.

### Glutamate — "I Am Excited"
The brain's primary excitatory neurotransmitter, essential for cognition and
neural communication. However, excessive glutamate release (excitotoxicity)
damages neurons and is implicated in stroke, neurodegenerative diseases, and
seizures. The endocannabinoid system serves as a retrograde inhibitor of
glutamate release, protecting against excitotoxic damage.

### Vasopressin — "I Am Aggressive"
Vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone) regulates water retention and blood
pressure, but in the brain it modulates social behavior, aggression, and
pair bonding (particularly in males). Elevated vasopressin is associated
with territorial aggression and social defensiveness.

## The Endocannabinoid Bridge

Blesching's central thesis is that the endocannabinoid system serves as a
regulatory bridge between expansive and constricting emotional states:

- **Downregulating constricting chemistry**: CB1 receptor activation in the
  amygdala reduces fear responses; in the HPA axis it reduces cortisol; in
  the locus coeruleus it reduces norepinephrine.
- **Facilitating expansive chemistry**: Anandamide promotes oxytocin release,
  enhances serotonin signaling, and modulates GABA and glutamate balance.
- **Homeostatic regulation**: The endocannabinoid system continuously monitors
  and adjusts emotional neurochemistry toward balance, acting as a biological
  thermostat for emotional state.

## Clinical Implications

This framework has implications for understanding how cannabis-based therapies
might address emotional dysregulation:

- **Anxiety disorders**: May involve excessive constricting neurochemistry
  (cortisol, norepinephrine, glutamate) that could be modulated by
  endocannabinoid activation.
- **Depression**: May involve deficient expansive neurochemistry (serotonin,
  anandamide, endogenous opioids) that could be supplemented.
- **PTSD**: Characterized by persistent constricting states (hypervigilance,
  fear conditioning) potentially addressable through endocannabinoid-mediated
  extinction of fear memories.
