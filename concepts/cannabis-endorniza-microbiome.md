# Cannabis Endorhiza Microbiome

## Overview

The **endorhiza** (Greek: *endo* = within, *rhiza* = root) is the microbial
community inhabiting internal root tissues. In Cannabis, it is a cultivar-
specific assemblage providing growth promotion, [[gadd-mycorrhizal-mineral-weathering-nutrient-mobilization]], disease
suppression, and modulation of plant secondary metabolism.

Winston et al. (2014) provided the first comprehensive characterization using
Illumina 16S rRNA gene sequencing of the V4 region across five cultivars.

## Endorhiza vs. Rhizosphere vs. Bulk Soil

The endorhiza is distinct from the rhizosphere (soil adjacent to roots) and
rhizoplane (root surface). Endorhiza bacteria are true **endophytes** — living
inside root tissue without causing disease. Key differences:

- Sheltered from environmental fluctuations in temperature, moisture, and pH
- Direct access to plant-derived carbon not available in the rhizosphere
- Face active plant immune surveillance requiring [[coleman-hardy-winter-vegetables-cold-tolerance-mechanisms]]
- [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] driven by host genotype, not soil type

## Sampling and DNA Extraction

Root balls were excavated, adhering soil removed (for rhizosphere samples), and
roots rinsed with alcohol and sterile water. DNA was isolated from 0.25 g root
tissue using PowerSoil DNA Isolation Kit with a 65°C 10-minute pre-heat step
for improved lysis. Triplicate extractions per plant assessed within-plant
variability.

## Core Community Members

All Cannabis plants shared a **[[core-endorhiza-microbiome-proteobacteria-enrichment-cannabis]]** of six groups:

### Pseudomonas

Most consistently abundant genus. Pseudomonas species are among the best-
characterized plant growth-promoting endophytes, contributing through:

- **[[singh-fungal-siderophore-production-iron-acquisition]]**: Iron-chelating compounds limiting pathogen growth
- **Antibiotic production**: Phenazines and pyoluteorin suppress pathogens
- **IAA synthesis**: Stimulates root growth and branching
- **Phosphate solubilization**: Converts insoluble phosphorus into bioavailable
  forms through organic acid excretion

### Cellvibrio

An aerobic cellulolytic bacterium degrading plant cell wall components. Unlike
other core members, it's not a classic endophyte — its presence may relate to
cell wall degradation during root colonization, potentially facilitating entry
of other bacteria.

### Oxalobacteraceae

Betaproteobacteria associated with nitrogen cycling and growth promotion.
Common in diverse crop rhizosphes, some species fix atmospheric nitrogen or
degrade phytotoxic oxalate compounds.

### Xanthomonadaceae

Diverse Gammaproteobacteria including both pathogens and non-pathogenic plant
associates. In Cannabis endorhiza, non-pathogenic members likely contribute to
[[soil-protozoa-nutrient-cycling-and-mineralization]] competitive pathogen exclusion.

### Actinomycetales

High-GC Gram-positive bacteria renowned for bioactive [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]]
production. Endophytic actinomycetes are prolific antibiotic and antifungal
sources, protecting host plants from microbial pathogens. *Streptomyces* are
the most studied representatives.

### Sphingobacteriales

Bacteroidetes common in soil and root environments, contributing to organic
matter decomposition and nutrient recycling within the root zone.
