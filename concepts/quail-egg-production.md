# quail egg production

Coturnix quail (Coturnix japonica) are the most productive small-scale egg producers. They reach laying age at 6-8 weeks, produce 250-320 eggs per year per hen, and require minimal space compared to chickens. quail [[mollison-designers-chicken-tractors-egg-production]] is increasingly popular on homesteads and urban farms due to low startup costs, fast return on investment, [[holzer-stone-fruit-varieties-and-growing]] market demand.

## Coturnix Quail Overview

- Mature weight: 150-200 g (hens), 170-250 g (males)
- Space requirement: 0.05 sq m (0.5 sq ft) per bird in cage systems
- Feed consumption: 20-30 g per bird per day
- Feed conversion ratio: 2.5-3.0 lb feed per lb of egg mass
- Laying age: 6-8 weeks from hatch
- Peak production: 10-20 weeks of age
- Productive lifespan: 1-2 years (replace flock annually for best production)
- Lifespan: 2-5 years

## Cage Systems

### Wire Cage (Most Common)

Standard quail cages are constructed from 1.3 cm (1/2 inch) galvanized hardware cloth:

-  **Dimensions** — Minimum 15 cm wide x 20 cm deep per bird. A standard cage is 60 cm x 60 cm x 30 cm tall, housing 12-15 birds.
-  **Floor** — Sloped at 8-10 degrees toward the front to allow eggs to roll out into a collection tray.
-  **Manure tray** — Slides under the cage for easy cleaning. Line with newspaper, cardboard, or dried leaves.
-  **Feeders** — External tube feeders reduce feed waste [[mushroom-cultivation-pests-and-contamination]]. One feeder per 6-8 birds.
-  **Waterers** — Nipple waterers (one per 4-6 birds) are cleanest. Alternatively, small cup waterers or inverted jar waterers.
-  **Ventilation** — Wire on all sides except the top (which should be solid for weather protection). Good airflow is critical — quail produce ammonia-laden manure in concentrated spaces.

### Colony Cages

Larger cages housing 20-30 birds together. More social interaction but higher risk of feather picking and cannibalism. Provide enrichment (hay bales, perches) to reduce stress.

### Floor Pens

Deep litter systems on solid floors with bedding (pine shavings, straw). Birds express more natural behaviors but egg collection is more difficult [[mollison-designers-rabbit-housing-and-manure-management]] is labor-intensive. Best for breeder flocks rather than egg production flocks.

### Stackable Cages

Multiple tiers of wire cages stacked vertically. Maximizes space efficiency in small areas. Ensure adequate light reaches lower tiers. A-frame stackable systems house 48-96 birds in a footprint of 1.2 x 0.6 m.

## Lighting

Light drives quail egg production. Coturnix quail require 14-16 hours of light per day for maximum production.

-  **Natural light** — Adequate during spring and summer at most latitudes. Production drops in fall as day length decreases.
-  **Supplemental light** — Use a timer to ensure 16 hours of total light per day year-round. A 7-15 watt LED bulb per cage unit is sufficient. Light intensity of 20-30 lux at bird level is adequate.
-  **Light placement** — Above and slightly forward of the cage. Avoid glare.
-  **Lighting schedule** — Set the timer so supplemental light comes on in the early morning (before dawn) rather than extending evening light. This matches the birds' natural circadian rhythm.
-  **Dark period** — Never provide continuous light. Quail need 8 hours of darkness for rest and reproductive hormone cycling.

## Feeding

### Commercial Feed

-  **Starter** (0-6 weeks): 24-28% protein game bird or turkey starter crumbles
-  **Layer** (6+ weeks): 20-22% protein game bird or layer feed. Quail need higher protein than chickens.
-  **Feed form** — Crumbles preferred over mash (less waste). Pellets acceptable for adult birds.
-  **Feeders** — Adjust feeder height to bird back level. Provide 2.5 cm of feeder space per bird.

### Supplementation

-  **Calcium** — Provide free-choice oyster shell or crushed eggshells once hens begin laying. Eggshell quality depends on adequate calcium (3.5-4.0% of diet).
-  **Grit** — Not required if feeding only commercial feed. Provide if birds have access to other foods.
-  **Greens** — Offer kale, spinach, clover, or grass as treats (5-10% of diet). Provides vitamins and enrichment.
-  **Protein supplements** — [[black-soldier-fly-larvae-as-feed]], mealworms, or scrambled egg boost protein intake. Feed 2-3 times per week.
-  **Water** — Clean, fresh water at all times. Quail drink 30-50 ml per bird per day. Nipple waterers reduce spillage and contamination.

### Feed Consumption and Cost

A single Coturnix hen eats approximately 7-10 kg of feed per year. At $0.50-0.80/kg for game bird feed, annual feed cost per bird is $3.50-8.00. With 250-300 eggs produced, feed cost per dozen eggs is $0.15-0.40 — significantly less than chicken eggs.

## Egg Collection

-  **Frequency** — Collect eggs 1-2 times per day. Once in the morning and once in the evening.
-  **Roll-out design** — Sloped floors allow eggs to roll into a collection tray, keeping eggs clean and reducing breakage.
-  **Cleaning** — Wipe soiled eggs with a dry cloth or fine sandpaper. Avoid washing eggs if possible — the bloom (protective coating) preserves freshness. If washing is necessary, use water warmer than the egg (35-40 C) and dry immediately.
-  **Storage** — Store unwashed eggs at 15 C (59 F) for up to 3 weeks. Refrigerated eggs last 6-8 weeks. Pointy end down in cartons for best yolk centering.
-  **Candling** — Candle eggs before selling to check for cracks and internal defects. A simple LED flashlight in a dark room works for quail eggs.

## Breeding and Flock Replacement

### Ratio

Maintain 1 male per 3-5 females for fertile eggs. Remove excess males to reduce aggression and feed waste.

### Incubation

- Coturnix eggs incubate in 17-18 days at 37.5 C (99.5 F) with 55-65% humidity
- Turn eggs 3-5 times per day for the first 14 days; stop turning on day 15
- Lockdown (last 3 days): increase humidity to 65-70%, do not open incubator
- Hatch rate for well-managed flocks: 70-85%

### Chick Care

- Brood at 35 C (95 F) for the first week, reducing 2-3 C per week until ambient temperature
- 28% protein starter feed from day 1
- Water with added vitamins and electrolytes for the first 3 days
- Quail chicks are tiny and can escape through surprisingly small gaps — use 1 cm (1/2 inch) hardware cloth or smaller
- No broody instinct in Coturnix — artificial incubation required

## Marketing Quail Eggs

### Market Channels

-  **Farmers markets** — Sell by the dozen or half-dozen. Premium pricing: $4-8 per dozen (versus $3-5 for chicken eggs). Emphasize nutritional benefits and novelty.
-  **Restaurants** — High-end restaurants and sushi bars use quail eggs for garnish, appetizers, and tasting menus. Consistent supply and quality are key.
-  **Direct sales** — Neighbors, coworkers, and online marketplaces. Quail eggs are a conversation starter that sells itself.
-  **CSA inclusion** — Add a dozen quail eggs to weekly CSA shares as a value-add.

### Value-Added Products

-  **Peeled hard-boiled quail eggs** — Ready to eat. Sell in vacuum-sealed packs.
-  **Pickled quail eggs** — Popular bar snack. Adds value and extends shelf life to 3-6 months.
-  **Quail egg cartons** — Custom-printed or plain pulp cartons. Professional presentation increases perceived value.

### Regulations

- In most US states, selling quail eggs from flocks under 3,000 birds does not require USDA inspection
- Egg candling and grading requirements vary by state — check local regulations
- Some states require a small-scale egg handler's license
- Label with: farm name, address, date packed, "keep refrigerated"

## Health Considerations

- **Coccidiosis** — Most common quail disease. Prevent with clean, dry conditions and avoid overcrowding. Medicated feed (amprolium) is available for prevention.
- **Ulcerative enteritis** — Bacterial infection causing sudden mortality. Maintain clean waterers and feeders.
- **Cannibalism** — Triggered by overcrowding, bright light, nutritional deficiency, or boredom. Reduce light intensity, provide hiding spots, and ensure adequate protein and space.
- **External parasites** — Mites and lice. Treat with [[comparison-neem-oil-vs-diatomaceous-earth]] in dust baths or permethrin spray on housing (not on birds).
- **Biosecurity** — Keep wild birds away from the quail area. Quarantine new birds for 2 weeks before introducing to the flock.

Quail production pairs well with [[chickshaw-and-poultry-tractors]] systems — quail can be housed in mobile units for limited foraging, or their manure can be composted for garden use. Processing facilities for [[rabbit-processing-guide]] can sometimes be adapted for quail meat production as well.
