## Agaricus bisporus Cultivation Biology

Agaricus bisporus (button mushroom, also called white button mushroom, cremini, or portobello depending on maturity) is the most widely cultivated mushroom species worldwide.

It belongs to the subdivision Basidiomycota, order Agaricales, and is characterized by sexual spores borne externally on structures called basidia.

The global mushroom industry is dominated by A. bisporus cultivation, making understanding its biology essential for commercial production.

## Morphology and Life Cycle

The vegetative phase of A. bisporus is primarily underground, consisting of filaments called hyphae that branch in all directions and constitute the mycelium.

The mycelium is the active growing phase that colonizes the substrate (compost), secretes extracellular enzymes for decomposition, and absorbs nutrients.

The reproductive phase produces the above-ground fruiting body (mushroom), which consists of a pileus (cap) supported by a stipe (stalk).

Sexual spores (basidiospores) are produced on gills beneath the cap.

## Environmental Control

Modern commercial cultivation requires precise environmental control throughout the growing cycle.

Temperature, humidity, CO2 concentration, and fresh air exchange rate must be carefully managed.

Computers and automated systems control these parameters in dedicated growing rooms.

Temperature fluctuations, inadequate ventilation, or improper moisture management can lead to reduced yields, disease outbreaks, or off-quality mushrooms.

## Diseases and Pests

A. bisporus cultivation is vulnerable to numerous diseases and pests.

Major fungal diseases include dry bubble (Verticillium fungicola), wet bubble (Mycogone perniciosa), and green mold (Trichoderma species).

Bacterial diseases include bacterial blotch (Pseudomonas tolaasii).

Pests include mushroom flies (Sciaridae and Phoridae), mites, and nematodes.
