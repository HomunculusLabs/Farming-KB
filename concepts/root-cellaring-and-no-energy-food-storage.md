# Root Cellaring and No-Energy Food Storage

Updated: 2026-04-18

Root cellaring is the practice of storing food without processing or energy input, maintaining a cold storage environment that preserves fresh produce for months. This method relies on proper temperature control (targeting 32 degrees F), adequate humidity (60-75%), and careful handling of stored foods. Root cellaring is one of the most sustainable food preservation methods, requiring no electricity, fuel, or processing equipment.

## Live Storage Principles

Many foods can be stored "live" -- without any processing -- in properly maintained cold storage. Suitable crops include pumpkins, potatoes, dry [[growing-root-vegetables-guide]]. Success depends on:
- Selecting species bred for preserving
- Harvesting at the right time
- Using only unbruised, unblemished specimens
- Removing tops from root vegetables
- Leaving dirt on roots (protects from decay)
- Using rodent-proof containers (plastic buckets or enamel cans)

Fruits must be stored away from vegetables because ethylene gas from apples causes vegetables to sprout. Root vegetables should be packed in damp sawdust, sand, or moss. Potatoes must be kept in complete darkness -- exposure to light causes greening and production of solanine, a poison.

## Temperature and Humidity Management

The target temperature is 32 degrees F (0 degrees C). Monitor with thermometers both inside and outside the cellar daily.
