## The Puddling Process

Puddling refers to the practice of flooding a rice field and then
stirring the saturated soil with a plow or harrow. The immediate
effect is to destroy soil structure:

- Crumbs and aggregates collapse into a uniform paste
- Macropores and air channels are eliminated
- The soil surface "sets as hard as plaster" when it dries
- A compacted hardpan forms below the cultivated layer

Fukuoka observed that this hardening effect creates a dependency:
once the soil is killed and compacted by puddling, it must be plowed
again each year to soften it before the next crop can be sown. The
farmer creates the preconditions that make the plow indispensable,
then considers the plow indispensable. "All we are doing is creating
the conditions that make a plow useful, then rejoicing at the utility
of our tool."
