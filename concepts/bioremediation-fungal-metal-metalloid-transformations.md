## Fungal Metal and Metalloid Transformations in Bioremediation

Fungi can transform metals, metalloids, and organometallic compounds
through reduction, methylation, and dealkylation reactions. These
transformations modify the mobility and toxicity of contaminants, making
them important mechanisms in [[staycare-fungal-biomethylation-of-metals-and-metalloids]] through enzymatic and non-enzymatic processes.
These transformations are significant because changing the chemical form
of a contaminant can alter its solubility, volatility, bioavailability,
and toxicity. The three principal transformation mechanisms are
reduction, methylation (biomethylation), and dealkylation.

## Metal Reduction

Reduction reactions convert metals from higher to lower oxidation states,
often resulting in precipitation and immobilization. Silver(I) reduction
to metallic silver(0) during [[alpha-1-3-glucan-fungal-pathogen-cell-walls]]. Both enzymatic and non-enzymatic copper(II)-reducing
systems have been purified from Debaryomyces hansenii cell walls.

Reductive processes are integral to high-affinity metal transport. The
reduction of copper(II) to copper(I) and iron(III) to iron(II) are
prerequisites for uptake of these essential metals. Mercury(II) can be
reduced to elemental mercury(0) by fungi, although the fungal system
is less well characterized than the bacterial mercuric reductase pathway.

## Metalloid Reduction

[[gliocladium-roseum]], Candida humicola, and
Penicillium species can convert monomethylarsenic acid to trimethylarsine.
Numerous fungi convert selenite and selenate to dimethylselenide and
dimethyldiselenide. Inorganic forms of selenium are methylated more
rapidly than organic forms such as selenoamino acids, and the mechanism
for selenium methylation appears similar to that for arsenic.

## Field Application: Selenium Removal

Selenium methylation and volatilization has been applied at field scale.
In the San Joaquin Valley and Kesterson Reservoir in California,
selenium-contaminated drainage water was managed through evaporation
pond systems. Incoming water was evaporated to dryness repeatedly until
sediment selenium concentrations approached 100 mg per kg dry weight.
The volatilization process was then optimized using carbon source,
moisture, temperature, and aeration parameters until selenium
concentrations fell to acceptable limits.

Mercury biomethylation by fungi has been reported, and dimethyltelluride
and dimethylditelluride production from tellurite and tellurate by
Penicillium species has been demonstrated. However, tellurium
volatilization by Fusarium occurred at extremely low levels, suggesting
it may not be an important detoxification mechanism for this metalloid.
