# Cannabis Topical Applications

## Overview

Cannabis topicals are cannabis-infused products applied directly to the skin for localized relief of pain, inflammation, and other conditions. Unlike inhaled or ingested cannabis, topicals generally do not produce psychoactive effects because cannabinoids absorbed through the skin do not enter the bloodstream in significant quantities to reach the brain. This makes topicals appealing for patients using [[marijuana-strain-guide-indica|indica]], [[marijuana-strain-guide-sativa|sativa]], or [[marijuana-strain-guide-hybrid|hybrid]] strains therapeutically without intoxication.

## How Cannabis Topicals Work

### The Endocannabinoid System in Skin

Human skin contains CB1 receptors (in hair follicles, sebaceous glands, nerve endings) and CB2 receptors (more abundant, in immune cells and keratinocytes). Cannabinoids interact with these local receptors for therapeutic effects without systemic absorption.

### Absorption Through the Skin

Cannabinoids are fat-soluble (lipophilic), helping them penetrate the lipid-rich skin barrier. Carrier agents (coconut oil, shea butter, beeswax) improve absorption. Standard topicals produce local effects at the application site. Transdermal patches use permeation enhancers for systemic (bloodstream) delivery.

## Types of Cannabis Topicals

### Balms and Salves

Balms and salves are oil-based preparations with a firm, semi-solid consistency.

**Composition:**
- Cannabis-infused carrier oil (coconut, olive, jojoba, hemp seed)
- Beeswax or carnauba wax (provides structure and firmness)
- Essential oils (for additional therapeutic properties and fragrance)
- Optional: butters (shea, cocoa) for additional skin benefits

**Common uses:**
- Joint and muscle pain
- Arthritis
- Inflammation
- Dry, cracked skin
- Minor skin irritations

### Creams and Lotions

Creams and lotions are water-based or emulsion-based products with a lighter consistency than balms.

**Composition:**
- Cannabis-infused oil (in oil phase of emulsion)
- Water (provides lighter texture)
- Emulsifier (binds oil and water phases)
- Preservatives (water-based products require preservation)
- Additional skin-care ingredients (vitamin E, aloe vera, chamomile)

**Advantages over balms:**
- Lighter, less greasy feel
- Absorbs more quickly
- Easier to apply over large areas
- Can include a wider range of skin-care ingredients

### Oils and Tinctures (Topical Use)

Liquid preparations for direct skin application. Faster absorption, easy precise application, can be mixed with carrier oils for different absorption rates.

### Transdermal Patches

Adhesive patches delivering cannabinoids into the bloodstream over 8-12 hours. Unlike standard topicals, these CAN produce psychoactive effects. Used for chronic pain, fibromyalgia, and neuropathic pain with consistent time-released dosing.

### Bath Products

Cannabis-infused bath soaks, bombs, and salts deliver cannabinoids to large skin surface areas. Warm water improves absorption. Epsom salt provides additional muscle relaxation. Primarily used for relaxation and localized muscle relief.

### Sprays and Roll-Ons

Convenient, portable formats. Roll-ons allow precise, mess-free application. Sprays cover larger areas. Often include cooling/warming ingredients (menthol, camphor).

## Cannabinoids in Topicals

- **THC:** Potent anti-inflammatory, effective for pain and muscle spasms. Does not produce psychoactive effects in standard topical formulations.
- **CBD:** Anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, reduces sebum production, works synergistically with THC.
- **CBG:** Antibacterial (particularly against MRSA), anti-inflammatory, promotes skin cell differentiation.
- **CBN:** Anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, may enhance other cannabinoid effects.
- **THCA:** Non-psychoactive precursor, anti-inflammatory properties.

## Terpenes in Topical Formulations

- **Linalool:** Calming, anti-inflammatory, analgesic
- **Caryophyllene:** Anti-inflammatory, CB2 receptor agonist, analgesic
- **Pinene:** Anti-inflammatory, may improve absorption
- **Limonene:** Skin penetration enhancer, anti-inflammatory
- **Myrcene:** Analgesic, anti-inflammatory, muscle relaxant
- **Menthol/Camphor:** Often added for cooling/warming sensation and analgesic effects

## Making Cannabis Topicals at Home

### Basic Cannabis-Infused Oil

Foundation ingredient for most topical preparations:

1. **Decarboxylate cannabis:** Heat dried flower at 220-240F for 30-45 minutes in an oven-safe container
2. **Combine with carrier oil:** 1:4 to 1:2 ratio of decarboxylated cannabis to carrier oil (coconut oil is most common)
3. **Infuse:** Heat in a double boiler or slow cooker on low (160-180F) for 2-6 hours, stirring occasionally
4. **Strain:** Strain through cheesecloth or fine mesh to remove plant material
5. **Store:** In a glass jar in a cool, dark place (lasts 2-3 months at room temperature, longer refrigerated)

### Basic Cannabis Balm Recipe

- 1 cup cannabis-infused coconut oil
- 1/4 cup beeswax pastilles
- 1 tablespoon shea butter (optional)
- 15-20 drops essential oils (lavender, peppermint, eucalyptus, etc.)

Process:
1. Combine infused oil and beeswax in a double boiler
2. Heat gently until beeswax is fully melted
3. Remove from heat, stir in shea butter until melted
4. Add essential oils once slightly cooled (below 140F to preserve volatile compounds)
5. Pour into containers and allow to cool and solidify

### Dosage Guidelines

- Mild strength: 1-2 grams cannabis per cup of oil
- Medium strength: 3-4 grams per cup
- Strong strength: 5-8 grams per cup
- Start with lower concentrations and increase as needed
- Consistency of application is more important than high concentration

## Medical Applications

### Pain and Inflammation (most common use)
- Arthritis and joint pain, muscle soreness, nerve pain (neuropathy)
- Fibromyalgia, chronic back pain, sports injuries
- Post-workout [[cannabis-and-exercise-recovery|muscle recovery]]

### Skin Conditions (emerging applications)
- Psoriasis (anti-inflammatory, slows cell proliferation)
- Eczema and dermatitis (anti-inflammatory, moisturizing)
- Acne (CBD reduces sebum production)
- Minor burns and wounds (antibacterial, anti-inflammatory)

### Other Applications
- Headache relief (temples and neck), menstrual cramp relief (lower abdomen)
- Foot neuropathy, hand/wrist pain (carpal tunnel, arthritis)

## Safety Considerations

- Topicals generally do not produce psychoactive effects (except transdermal patches)
- Patch test on a small area before widespread use
- Keep away from eyes and mucous membranes
- [[cannabis-testing-and-quality|Testing]] for topicals includes potency, pesticides, and microbial contamination
- Consult healthcare provider before combining with other topical medications
- Verify [[cannabis-regulations-by-state|local regulations]] regarding topical cannabis products
