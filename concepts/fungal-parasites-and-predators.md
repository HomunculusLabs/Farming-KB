# Fungal Parasites and Predators

## Overview

Not all fungi are decomposers or symbionts — many are active predators or parasites of other organisms, including other fungi, nematodes, insects, and even small animals. These predatory and parasitic fungi play crucial roles in ecosystem regulation and have significant applications in biological control of agricultural pests.

## Nematophagous Fungi

Nematophagous (nematode-eating) fungi are a diverse group that trap, kill, and digest nematodes. Plant-parasitic nematodes cause billions of dollars in crop damage annually, making these fungi important biological control agents.

### Trapping Fungi (Arthrobotrys and Allies)

Species in the genera Arthrobotrys, Dactylaria, and Monacrosporium produce specialized trapping structures:

- **Adhesive knobs**: Small sticky knobs on short branches that adhere to passing nematodes ([[arthrobotrys-oligospora]])
- **Adhesive networks**: Three-dimensional webs of sticky hyphae (Arthrobotrys conoides)
- **Constricting rings**: Hyphal rings that swell inward in under 0.1 seconds when a nematode passes through, mechanically crushing it (Arthrobotrys dactyloides)
- **Non-constricting rings**: Sticky ring structures that hold nematodes until they die

After capture, the fungus penetrates the nematode cuticle with a specialized hyphal peg, injects toxins, and digests the internal contents. The entire process from capture to digestion can take 24-72 hours.

### Endoparasitic Nematophagous Fungi

These fungi produce spores (conidia or zoospores) that are ingested by or adhere to nematodes, then germinate inside the host:
- **Catenaria anguillulae**: Produces zoospores that attach to nematode cuticle
- **Drechmeria coniospora**: Conidia adhere to nematode sensory organs, then germ tubes penetrate
- **Hirsutella rhossiliensis**: Produces adhesive conidia that stick to nematodes in soil

### Egg-Parasitic Fungi

- **Paecilomyces lilacinus**: Parasitizes nematode eggs, especially root-knot nematode (Meloidogyne) eggs. Commercially available as a biocontrol product.
- **Pochonia chlamydosporia**: Specializes in parasitizing cyst and root-knot nematode eggs

## Mycoparasites

Mycoparasitic fungi attack and kill other fungi. They are important in natural disease suppression and are used as biocontrol agents against plant pathogenic fungi.

### Trichoderma spp.

The most widely studied and commercially used mycoparasites. Trichoderma harzianum and T. viride are the most common biocontrol species.

Mechanisms of action:
1. **Mycoparasitism**: Direct hyphal contact, coiling around host hyphae, penetration, and digestion
2. **Antibiosis**: Production of secondary metabolites (trichothecenes, gliotoxin, peptaibols) that inhibit or kill other fungi
3. **Competition**: Rapid colonization of resources, outcompeting pathogens for space and nutrients
4. **Enzyme production**: Chitinases, glucanases, and proteases that degrade fungal cell walls
5. **[[endophytic-mycorrhizal-induced-systemic-resistance]]**: Priming plant immune responses

Trichoderma is used against Botrytis (gray mold), Rhizoctonia (damping off), Fusarium wilt, Pythium, and Sclerotinia. Available as seed treatments, soil drenches, and foliar sprays.

### Other Mycoparasites

- **Gliocladium virens**: Related to Trichoderma, used against Pythium and Rhizoctonia
- **[[ampelomyces-quisqualis]]**: Parasitizes powdery mildew fungi; used as a biocontrol agent in vineyards and greenhouses
- **[[pythium-oligandrum]]**: A mycoparasitic Pythium that attacks other Pythium species and some plant pathogenic fungi
- **[[sporidesmium-sclerotivorum]]**: Parasitizes Sclerotinia sclerotioria, destroying its overwintering structures

## Entomopathogenic Fungi

Fungi that infect and kill insects. See [[fungal-symbiosis-types]] for contrast with mutualistic relationships.

### Beauveria bassiana

- Broad host range: over 700 insect species including whiteflies, thrips, aphids, beetles, and termites
- Mechanism: Conidia adhere to insect cuticle, germinate, penetrate, and proliferate inside the insect. Death typically occurs in 3-7 days.
- Commercial products: BotaniGard, Naturalis, Mycotrol
- Used in glasshouses, field crops, and termite control

### Metarhizium anisopliae

- Infects soil-dwelling insects, termites, grasshoppers, and spittlebugs
- Commercially available as Met52 and other formulations
- Used for tick control, termite management, and spittlebug control in sugarcane

### Cordyceps and Ophiocordyceps

- Famous for infecting and manipulating insect behavior (e.g., [[ophiocordyceps-unilateralis]] and "zombie ants")
- Spores infect ants, fungal growth takes over the nervous system, ants climb vegetation and bite down, then the fruiting body erupts from the ant's head to release spores
- Over 400 species of Cordyceps sensu lato described, each typically specific to one insect genus

## Predatory Fungi (Non-Nematode)

### Arthrobotrys and Related Genera

In addition to nematode trapping, some species can capture small soil arthropods like tardigrades and collembola using adhesive networks.

### Zoopagales

An order of fungi that parasitize small soil animals including amoebae, rotifers, and small nematodes. They use adhesive spores or specialized haustoria to penetrate their prey.

## Applications in Biological Control

Fungal biocontrol agents offer advantages over chemical pesticides:
- Target-specific (reduced non-target effects)
- No chemical residues
- Can establish and persist in the environment
- Lower risk of resistance development

Challenges:
- Environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, UV) affect efficacy
- Slower action than chemical pesticides
- Mass production and formulation challenges
- Regulatory approval processes
- Variable field performance
