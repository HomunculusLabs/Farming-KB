# Psychedelic Plant Sources

## Overview

Psychedelic compounds occur naturally across a wide range of plant families, fungi, and even animal species. These have been used by indigenous peoples worldwide for millennia in healing, divination, and religious ceremonies. The major categories include psilocybin-containing mushrooms, [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]]-containing plants (used in [[beta-carboline-chemistry]] and snuffs), mescaline-containing cacti, and iboga-containing plants.

## Psilocybin Mushrooms

Over 200 species of fungi are known to contain psilocybin and related indole alkaloids. See [[psilocybin-mushroom-distribution-worldwide]] for detailed distribution data.

### Major Genera

- **Psilocybe** -- 116+ species; the largest genus of psychoactive fungi. Includes P. cubensis, P. semilanceata, P. mexicana, P. cyanescens, P. azurescens, P. zapotecorum
- **Panaeolus** (including Copelandia) -- 25+ species; P. cyanescens (Copelandia cyanescens) is notably potent
- **Gymnopilus** -- 14 species; G. spectabilis (big laughing gym) is the most well-known
- **Inocybe** -- 6+ psychoactive species; many are difficult to identify and some toxic species resemble them
- **Conocybe** -- 4+ species; small, fragile mushrooms, easily overlooked
- **Pluteus** -- 6+ species; wood-inhabiting
- **Hypholoma** (Naematoloma) -- 6+ species

## Ayahuasca Complex

Ayahuasca is a decoction combining two types of plants:

### DMT-Containing Admixture Plants
- **Psychotria viridis** (chacruna) -- the most common admixture
- **Diplopterys cabrerana** (chaliponga) -- contains both DMT and 5-MeO-DMT
- **Mimosa hostilis** (jurema) -- root bark used in Brazilian preparations

### MAOI-Containing Plants (Banisteriopsis)
- **Banisteriopsis caapi** -- the ayahuasca vine; contains harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine ([[beta-carboline-chemistry]] / [[beta-carboline-chemistry]] alkaloids)
- **Banisteriopsis quitensis** -- related vine used in some preparations
- The harmala alkaloids inhibit MAO enzymes, allowing DMT to be orally active

See [[beta-carboline-chemistry]] for detailed pharmacokinetic information.

## DMT Snuff Plants

- **Virola** species (V. theiodora, V. calophylla, V. elongata) -- bark resin prepared as snuff (Psychedelic Plant Sources) by indigenous peoples of the Amazon basin
- **Anadenanthera peregrina** (yopo, cohoba) -- seeds ground into snuff; contains DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, and bufotenin
- **Anadenanthera colubrina** (cebil) -- similar use in southern Amazon and Argentina

## Mescaline-Containing Cacti

- **Lophophora williamsii** (peyote) -- 1-6% mescaline; see [[mescaline-compound-profile]]
- **Echinopsis pachanoi** (San Pedro) -- 0.1-2% mescaline
- **Echinopsis peruviana** (Peruvian torch) -- 0.1-2% mescaline
- **Echinopsis lageniformis** (Bolivian torch) -- variable mescaline content

## Iboga

- **Tabernanthe iboga** -- shrub native to West Central Africa (Gabon, Congo)
- Contains ibogaine, a complex indole alkaloid with both psychedelic and anti-addictive properties
- Used in Bwiti initiation ceremonies in Gabon
- Under investigation for treating opioid addiction and other substance dependence
- Duration is extremely long: 24-48 hours or more

## Other Notable Psychedelic Plants

### Solanaceous Plants (Tropane Alkaloids)
- **Datura stramonium** (jimsonweed) and **Datura candida** (tree datura) -- contain scopolamine and atropine; deliriant, not true psychedelic; extremely dangerous
- **Atropa belladonna** (deadly nightshade) -- tropane alkaloids; historically used in European witchcraft preparations
- **Hyoscyamus niger** (henbane), **Mandragora officinarum** (mandrake) -- similarly dangerous deliriants

### Morning Glories
- **Ipomoea tricolor** (ololiuqui) and **Rivea corymbosa** (tlitliltzin) -- seeds contain lysergic acid amide (LSA), a close relative of LSD; used by Aztecs

### Other
- **Peganum harmala** (Syrian rue) -- seeds contain harmala alkaloids (MAOIs); used alone and as an ayahuasca analogue
- **Salvia divinorum** -- contains salvinorin A (kappa-opioid agonist, not a classical psychedelic)
- **Coleus blumei** and **Coleus pumilus** -- reported mild psychoactivity
