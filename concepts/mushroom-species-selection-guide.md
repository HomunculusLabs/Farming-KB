# Mushroom Species Selection Guide

Choosing the right mushroom species to cultivate depends on your
experience level, available equipment, substrate materials, climate, and
market goals. Different species have vastly different requirements for
substrate, temperature, humidity, and fruiting triggers. This guide
covers the most commonly cultivated gourmet and medicinal species with
practical cultivation parameters.

## Beginner Species

### Oyster Mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.)

The recommended starting point for new cultivators. Oysters are aggressive
colonizers, grow on a wide range of substrates, fruit readily, and
tolerate imperfect conditions. Several varieties exist:

- **Blue oyster (P. ostreatus)**: Cold-fruiting, dense flesh, robust
  flavor. Fruits at 10-18°C. Best on straw or supplemented sawdust.
- **Pink oyster (P. djamor)**: Tropical species requiring warm
  temperatures (20-30°C). Vibrant pink color fades when cooked. Very
  fast colonizer but short shelf life.
- **Golden oyster (P. citrinopileatus)**: Warm-fruiting with a nutty
  flavor and striking yellow color. Requires careful humidity management.
- **King oyster (P. eryngii)**: Larger individual mushrooms with thick
  stems and small caps. Higher CO2 tolerance during fruiting produces
  longer stems. Demands more precise temperature control (12-18°C).
- **Phoenix oyster (P. pulmonarius)**: Warm-season alternative, fruits
  at 15-25°C. Good yielder on straw.

Oysters can grow on straw, sawdust, paper waste, coffee grounds,
agricultural byproducts, and even toilet paper rolls. They are
prolific sporulators, so grow them outdoors or in well-ventilated areas
to avoid indoor allergy issues.

### Wine Cap (Stropharia rugosoannulata)

An excellent outdoor species that thrives in wood chip mulch beds.
Extremely low maintenance once established. Plant in spring by mixing
spawn into fresh hardwood chips spread 4-6 inches deep in a shaded area.
Fruits in flushes during cool, wet weather. Produces burgundy-capped
mushrooms with a mild potato-like flavor. Beds can produce for 3-5
years with occasional chip replenishment.

## Intermediate Species

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)

The most commercially important cultivated mushroom worldwide. Requires
hardwood sawdust or logs as substrate. Two main cultivation methods:

- **Log cultivation**: Inoculate freshly cut hardwood logs (oak, beech,
  maple) with shiitake plug spawn or sawdust spawn. Logs produce for
  3-7 years. Requires outdoor space and patience (6-18 month wait for
  first fruiting). Traditional and low-input.
- **Indoor sawdust blocks**: Supplemented hardwood sawdust in
  sterilized bags. Requires pressure cooker sterilization and more
  controlled fruiting conditions. Yields faster (6-10 weeks) and
  higher per substrate volume.

Shiitake demands a cold shock (reduce temperature by 10-15°C for 24-48
hours) and physical impact (banging blocks) to trigger fruiting.
Browner strains fruit at cooler temperatures (10-16°C); warmer strains
fruit at 15-22°C.

### Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)

A distinctive coral-like mushroom prized for its seafood-like flavor and
potential neuroprotective compounds. Grows on supplemented hardwood
sawdust. Prefers cooler fruiting temperatures (15-20°C) and high
humidity (90-95%). Slower colonizer than oysters but less prone to
contamination once colonized. Yields large, dense fruiting bodies when
grown on supplemented blocks with 10-20% bran. Fruits in single large
clusters rather than multiple flushes.

### Maitake / Hen of the Woods (Grifola frondosa)

A prized medicinal and culinary mushroom forming large, ruffled clusters.
More challenging than oysters but rewarding. Requires supplemented
hardwood sawdust, sterilized substrate, and cool temperatures (15-18°C)
for fruiting. Slow colonizer (30-45 days) but can produce substantial
yields on a single flush. CO2 levels affect morphology; higher CO2
produces more compact clusters.

## Advanced Species

### Reishi (Ganoderma lingzhi / G. tsugae)

Cultivated primarily for medicinal use rather than culinary value (woody,
bitter texture). Grows on supplemented hardwood sawdust. Prefers warm
temperatures (25-30°C) and very high humidity. Produces distinctive
antler-like formations when grown in high CO2, or flat conks in low CO2.
Long cultivation cycle (3-6 months). Can also be grown on hardwood logs
outdoors.

### Cordyceps (Cordyceps militaris)

An advanced species requiring sterilized substrate (typically rice-based)
and very precise environmental control. Grows at 18-22°C with high
humidity. Light is required for proper fruiting body development and
pigment production. Commercial cultivation uses liquid culture
inoculation of sterilized grain or rice in jars. The market value is
high, making it attractive despite the difficulty.

### Morel (Morchella spp.)

Among the most challenging mushrooms to cultivate reliably. No fully
reliable indoor method exists, though some commercial operations have
achieved consistent results using specific soil-based methods outdoors.
Morels associate with certain tree species (elm, ash, tulip poplar) and
require complex nutritional and environmental triggers. Not recommended
for beginners.
