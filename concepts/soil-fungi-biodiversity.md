# Soil Fungi Biodiversity

Soil is arguably the most diverse habitat for fungi on Earth, supporting an estimated 1.5 million or more fungal species. Soil fungi include decomposers, mycorrhizal symbionts, plant pathogens, predators, and parasites, forming the backbone of terrestrial nutrient cycling.

## Overview

The soil fungal community is one of the most complex biological systems known. A single gram of soil may contain hundreds of species of fungi, represented by mycelium, spores, and resting structures. Most soil fungi are microscopic and can only be detected through culturing or molecular methods.

## Major Functional Groups

### Saprotrophic Soil Fungi
- Primary decomposers of organic matter in soil
- Degrade cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, chitin, keratin
- Include Zygomycetes (Mucorales), Ascomycetes (many genera), Basidiomycetes
- Key genera: *Trichoderma*, *Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*, *Fusarium*, *Gliocladium*, *Cladosporium*, *Mucor*, *Rhizopus*

### Mycorrhizal Fungi (see [[mycorrhizal-types]])
- **Arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi**: Glomeromycota; associated with ~80% of plant species; form arbuscules in root cortical cells; major component of soil fungal biomass
- **Ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungi**: Primarily Basidiomycetes; associate with trees (Pinaceae, Fagaceae, Betulaceae); form mantle and Hartig net around roots
- Soil hyphal networks extend root foraging zone by 10-100x

### Plant Pathogenic Soil Fungi
- Cause root rots, wilts, damping-off, and other diseases
- Key genera: *Fusarium*, *Verticillium*, *Rhizoctonia*, *Pythium* (Oomycete), *Phytophthora* (Oomycete)
- Survive in soil as resting structures (chlamydospores, sclerotia, oospores)
- Often managed through crop rotation and biological control

### Predaceous Soil Fungi
- Trap and digest nematodes, amoebae, and other microfauna
- Nematode-trapping fungi: *Arthrobotrys*, *Dactylaria*, *Duddingtonia* (adhesive branches, constricting rings, adhesive knobs)
- Endoparasitic: *Catenaria*, *Nematophthora* (zoosporic; infect nematodes internally)
- Ovicidal fungi: parasitize nematode eggs

### Entomopathogenic Soil Fungi
- Parasitize soil-dwelling insects
- Key genera: *Metarhizium*, *Beauveria*, *Paecilomyces*
- Spores in soil contact insect cuticle, germinate, penetrate, kill host
- See [[fungal-pest-control]]

### Yeasts
- Common in soil, especially in association with plant surfaces and exudates
- Key genera: *Cryptococcus*, *Rhodotorula*, *Sporobolomyces*, *Candida*
- Often detected by dilution plating

## Vertical Distribution

Soil fungi show distinct vertical stratification:

- **Surface/litter layer (O horizon)**: Highest species diversity and abundance; dominated by saprotrophs decomposing fresh litter; many specialized Ascomycetes
- **Organic horizon (A horizon)**: Rich in organic matter; mycorrhizal hyphae concentrated in root zone; diverse decomposer community
- **Mineral horizon (B horizon)**: Lower diversity; some persistent saprotrophs and mycorrhizal hyphae extending downward
- **Deep soil/subsoil**: Reduced diversity; some oligotrophic fungi adapted to low organic matter

## Factors Influencing Soil Fungal Communities

### Soil Properties
- **Organic matter**: Primary driver of fungal diversity; more organic matter = more fungi
- **pH**: Strongly affects community composition; most fungi prefer slightly acidic (pH 5-6)
- **Moisture**: Fungal activity peaks at field capacity; both drought and waterlogging reduce diversity
- **Texture**: Clay soils retain more water and organic matter; sandy soils have lower fungal biomass
- **Cation exchange capacity (CEC)**: Higher CEC supports more diverse fungal communities

### Vegetation
- Plant species composition strongly influences soil fungal communities
- Mycorrhizal fungi are plant-host-specific at varying levels
- Root exudates selectively stimulate rhizosphere fungi
- Litter quality (C:N ratio, lignin content) determines decomposer community

### Land Use
- **Natural forests**: Highest fungal diversity, especially ECM basidiomycetes
- **Grasslands**: High diversity of AM fungi and saprotrophs
- **Agricultural soils**: Reduced diversity; dominated by fast-growing Ascomycetes
- **Disturbed soils**: Shift toward r-selected species (fast-growing, generalist)
- **No-till systems**: Higher fungal:bacterial ratios than tilled systems (see [[composting-and-soil-biology]])

### Seasonal and Temporal Dynamics
- Fungal biomass and community composition fluctuate seasonally
- Peaks often in autumn (leaf fall) and spring (snowmelt, root growth)
- Soil temperature and moisture are primary drivers
- Some fungi form persistent resting structures (chlamydospores, sclerotia) during unfavorable periods

## Diversity Estimates

### Culturable Diversity
- Dilution plating typically recovers 0.1-1% of estimated species
- Most commonly isolated genera: *Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*, *Trichoderma*, *Fusarium*, *Cladosporium*, *Mucor*, *Rhizopus*
- Estimates of culturable species per gram of forest soil: 100-1000 CFU

### Molecular Diversity
- Environmental DNA surveys reveal 10-100x more species than culturing
- A single soil sample may contain 500-2000 fungal OTUs
- Vast majority cannot be assigned to described species
- ITS metabarcoding is the standard approach

### Global Patterns
- Tropical soils generally more diverse than temperate (but understudied)
- Forest soils more diverse than grassland or agricultural soils
- Undisturbed soils more diverse than disturbed
- Soil fungal diversity may exceed plant diversity at the same site

## Ecological Roles

### Decomposition and Nutrient Cycling
- Primary agents of organic matter decomposition
- Release N, P, and other nutrients from organic matter
- Produce extracellular enzymes: cellulases, ligninases, proteases, phosphatases
- See [[fungal-nutrient-cycling-mineral-transformations]]

### Soil Structure
- Mycelium binds soil particles into aggregates
- AM fungi produce glomalin (see [[glomalin-and-soil-carbon]])
- Hyphae stabilize macroaggregates (>250 um)
- Improves water infiltration and retention

### Plant Health
- Mycorrhizal fungi enhance nutrient and water uptake
- Some soil fungi suppress plant pathogens (see [[mycorrhizal-plant-defense]])
- Endophytic fungi in roots can provide systemic resistance
- Disease-suppressive soils often have specific fungal communities

### Food Web Support
- Fungal biomass is high-quality food for fungivorous nematodes, microarthropods
- See [[fungi-soil-food-webs]]
- Fungal channel energy flow is more efficient than bacterial channel

## Methods for Studying Soil Fungi

### Traditional Culturing
- Soil dilution plating on selective media (DRBC, GAN, PDA + antibiotics)
- Soil washing techniques
- Warcup soil plate method
- Baiting with buried substrates
