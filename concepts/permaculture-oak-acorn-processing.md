Updated: 2026-04-18 Oaks (Quercus spp.) are massive, long-lived trees providing
timber, wildlife habitat, and a traditional food source — acorns — that predated
grain flour by thousands of years.

## Species

- Holm Oak (Quercus ilex) — warm climate, dryland tolerant
- Swamp White Oak (Quercus bicolor) — moist areas
- Burr Oak (Quercus macrocarpa) — hardiest, large sweet acorns with less tannin
- Schuette's Oak (Quercus x schuettei) — hybrid with sweeter acorns
- Cork Oak (Quercus suber) — evergreen, source of cork (bark regrows in 150-200
  years)

## Uses

- Wood and timber
- Edible acorns (processed into flour)
- Wildlife habitat

## Growing Conditions

- Full sun
- 75-100 feet (23-31 m) tall and wide
- Most soil types
- Select species appropriate to climate

## Propagation

Oaks are grown from acorns planted in the fall, directly in the ground or in
deep containers. Select acorns that sink in water; floaters are typically
infested. Plant 1-2 inches deep with the pointed end down. Cold stratification
occurs naturally in temperate climates; in milder regions, refrigerate moist
acorns for 30-60 days. Protect seedlings from rodents with wire cages.
Transplant when young, as mature oaks develop extensive taproots.
