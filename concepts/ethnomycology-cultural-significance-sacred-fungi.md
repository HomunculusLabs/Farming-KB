## Ethnomycology and Cultural Significance of Sacred Fungi

## Definition and Scope

Ethnomycology is the study of the cultural uses and significance of fungi
by human societies. The field encompasses the religious, ceremonial,
medicinal, and social roles of fungi in indigenous and traditional
cultures. The paper by Guzman, Allen, and Gartz, while primarily a

ritual involving all-night vigils where the curandero (healer) consumes
sacred fungi to diagnose illness, locate lost objects, predict the
future, and communicate with the divine. The mushrooms are considered
sacred entities with their own intelligence and agency, not simply
chemical tools. The ceremony includes specific prayers, chants, and
rituals that structure the experience within a cosmological framework.

## The Church of Our Lord of the Little Mushroom

A remarkable ethnomycological phenomenon documented in the paper is the
church of "Nuestro Senor del Honguito" (Our Lord of the Little Mushroom)
in Chignahuapan, Puebla, Mexico. This represents a syncretic religious
practice that blends indigenous mushroom veneration with Catholic
iconography, creating a unique religious institution centered on the
worship of sacred fungi. The church has an image of Christ surrounded
by mushrooms, and the faithful pray to the "Senor del Honguito" for
healing and guidance.
