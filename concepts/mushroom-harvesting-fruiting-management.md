# Mushroom Fruiting Management and Harvesting

This page covers the critical stages from opening mature spawn bags through maintaining [[fruiting-conditions]] to harvesting mushrooms at the right time. Proper execution of these steps directly impacts yield, quality, and profitability.

## Step 9: Opening Bags

Once spawn bags are fully colonized and mature, they must be transferred to the fruiting house and opened to initiate the fruiting phase. Careful handling at this stage prevents contamination and ensures strong fruiting.

### Preparing the Fruiting House

- Thoroughly clean the fruiting body house before introducing any bags
- Use a trolley to transfer mature spawn bags from the incubation area
- Place bags securely on shelves with adequate spacing between them for air circulation
- Ensure shelves are clean and dry before loading

### Opening Methods by Mushroom Type

Different mushroom species require different bag-opening techniques to trigger fruiting effectively:

| Species | Common Name | Opening Method |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| *[[auricularia-polytricha]]* | Ear mushroom | Cut four vertical lines around the bag, with four cuts per line |

**Important notes on opening:**
- Use clean, sharp tools (knives or cutters) to avoid ragged edges that could tear further
- For cut-bag methods, cut at the shoulder of the bag — the upper curved portion — not too low
- For ear mushrooms, space the four vertical lines evenly around the circumference of the bag, and make four small cuts along each line
- Open bags during cooler parts of the day to reduce stress on the [[mycelium]]

## Step 10: Maintaining and Monitoring Fruiting Conditions

Once bags are opened, the [[cho-natural-pest-control-methods]] where possible
- Avoid chemical pesticides on or near fruiting mushrooms
- Encourage natural predators and maintain a clean environment to suppress pest populations

### 7. Contaminated Bag Identification and Separation

- Inspect bags regularly for signs of contamination (green mold, slimy patches, foul odors)
- Immediately separate any contaminated bags from healthy ones
- Contamination spreads quickly — act as soon as it is noticed

### 8. Sanitation and Disposal

- Manually pick up all garbage and debris from the fruiting area daily
- Remove contaminated bags promptly from the fruiting house
- Contaminated substrate can be re-pasteurized and reused if the contamination is not severe
- Dispose of heavily contaminated material away from the cultivation area

### 9. Production Record-Keeping

- Keep clear, organized records of all production data
- Track: bag numbers, opening dates, harvest dates, weights, flush numbers, and any issues
- Records help identify patterns, improve yields, and plan future production cycles

## Step 11: Harvesting

Harvesting at the correct time and with proper technique is essential for maximizing quality, shelf life, and market value.

### When to Harvest

- Mushrooms are typically ready for harvest **2–3 days after their first appearance**
- Harvest timing is critical:
  - **Too early (too small):** Poor market price, lower weight per harvest
  - **Too late (too big):** Reduced conservation/storage period, less sweet and less tasty
- Monitor bags closely once fruiting begins and establish a regular harvest schedule

### Harvesting Technique

- Grasp the mushroom stalk gently near the base
- Pull slowly and steadily — do not twist or yank
- Avoid damaging the surrounding mycelium or nearby developing pins
- Use a clean, sharp knife if mushrooms do not pull cleanly

### Harvest Frequency and Handling

- Harvest **2–3 times per day** during peak fruiting periods
- Place harvested mushrooms in baskets — **5–8 kg per basket** to avoid crushing
- **Trim and peel** the base of each stalk for a clean, attractive presentation
- Handle mushrooms gently to minimize bruising

### Post-Harvest Processing and Yields

- Weigh all harvested mushrooms immediately and record the data
- Note the flush number (first flush, second flush, etc.) for each bag
- **250–350 grams per bag** over the full production cycle in **4–5 flushes**
- Later flushes generally yield smaller quantities than earlier ones
- Bags will noticeably lose weight as the substrate is exhausted over [[psilocin-biosynthetic-delay-hypothesis-successive-flushes]]

### Pro Tip: Timing Harvests After Watering

- Harvest mushrooms at least **2–3 hours after watering** for longer conservation
- Mushrooms harvested while still wet from recent spraying have a shorter shelf life
- Allowing time for surface moisture to evaporate before picking improves post-harvest storage

## Environmental Control Summary

Maintaining the right environment throughout fruiting is the single most important factor in successful mushroom production:

| Factor | Target Range | Key Actions |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Humidity** | 75–90% | Spray often but lightly; never spray into bags |
| **Temperature** | Comfortable for humans | Open/close doors and windows; cool at night if needed |
| **Ventilation** | Continuous fresh air | Adjust with doors and windows; avoid direct drafts |
| **Light** | Moderate to increasing | Natural light is sufficient; increase as fruiting progresses |
| **Cleanliness** | High standard | Daily inspections; immediate removal of contaminated material |
