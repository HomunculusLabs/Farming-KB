## Animal Manure Guide

## Overview

Animal manures are the very best sources of organic matter for soil, improving condition and supplying nutrients. They can be used on any soil type. Some, like poultry manure, require care due to high nitrogen content. Commercial manure may be adulterated with hormones, herbicides, and pesticides, but stacking for at least a year significantly reduces contamination risk.

## General Guidelines

- All manure is used at full strength unless otherwise specified
- Avoid putting manure on young shoots — it will scorch them
- Stack manure for at least 12 months before use to leach out impurities and prevent root scorching
- Some nutrient loss occurs during storage but can be made up in other ways
- General coverage: 20-30lb (9-15kg) per sq yard/meter (varies by type)

## Manure Types

### Cow Manure

- **Nitrogen:** 0.6% | **Phosphorus:** 0.2-0.3% | **Potassium:** 0.3-0.5% | **Trace elements:** Full range
- **Coverage:** 20-30lb (9-15kg) per sq yard/meter
- Store for 12 months before use. Very cheap and excellent as soil conditioner and nutrient source. Though lower in nutrient percentage than [[ingham-inorganic-fertilizers-soil-biology-damage]], the large volume applied makes mineral concentration less significant. Manure holds water and maintains high fertility levels.
- Source from farmers who graze cattle outside — obtain after cows are turned out for summer.

### Horse Manure

- **Nitrogen:** 0.6% | **Phosphorus:** 0.6% | **Potassium:** 0.4% | **Trace elements:** Full range
- **Coverage:** 20-30lb (9-15kg) per sq yard/meter
- Often more available near urban areas. Use only from stables where straw or peat is used as bedding — wood shavings may harbor plant disease.
- **Fresh horse manure must not be used directly** — it causes leaf/stem scorching and loses nutrient value; straw takes long to decompose.
- **Storage:** Small quantities go on the [[compost]] heap (high N helps decomposition). Large quantities: stack on concrete base, mash down (lots of air in straw), cover with plastic in winter. Ready in a couple of months, or a year if pesticide contamination is suspected.

### Pig Manure

- **Nitrogen:** 0.6% | **Phosphorus:** 0.6% | **Potassium:** 0.4% | **Trace elements:** Full range
- **Coverage:** 20-30lb (9-15kg) per sq yard/meter
- Colder and wetter than horse or cow manure but not to be discounted — very high nutrient content. Treat like horse manure but generally no need to mash down (heavier).

### Sheep Manure

- **Nitrogen:** 0.8% | **Phosphorus:** 0.5% | **Potassium:** 0.4% | **Trace elements:** Full range
- **Use:** As liquid manure (half a sackful lasts an average garden a full year)
- No straw mixture since sheep are not normally stabled. Very high in nutrients — worth collecting from fields.

### Chicken Manure

- **Fresh/wet:** N 1.5% | P 1.5% | K 0.5% | Coverage: 7-10lb (3.25-4.5kg) per sq yard/meter
- **Dry:** N 4% | P 4% | K 1.5% | Coverage: 8-12oz (20-30g) per sq yard/meter
- Extremely high nitrogen content — do not use undiluted. From deep-litter houses on straw: stack like horse manure. Commercial chicken manure can compost straw (alternate layers of straw and manure; rot for at least a year due to hormones).

### Other Manures

- **Pigeon droppings:** Even higher nitrogen than chicken manure. Contact local pigeon-racing enthusiasts.
- **Rabbit manure:** Ideal but available only in small quantities. Use like chicken manure.
- **Goat manure:** Similar to horse manure but better quality. Compost and use like horse manure.

## Chickens as Soil Improvers

Half a dozen chickens in a portable house with movable wire-mesh run can be rotated through the vegetable garden. As crops finish, move hens onto the space — they devour green matter (recycling it as high-nitrogen fertilizer), peck out old seeds, and eat soil pests near the surface.
