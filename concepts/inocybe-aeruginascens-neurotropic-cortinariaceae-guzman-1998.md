## Inocybe aeruginascens

## Overview

Inocybe aeruginascens is a neurotropic mushroom species in the family Cortinariaceae, notable for being one of the few Inocybe species confirmed to contain psilocybin and related indole alkaloids. Listed as species #191 in the Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (1998) world checklist, it was first described by Babos and has been the subject of chemical analysis by Jochen Gartz. The species is unusual within the large and predominantly toxic genus Inocybe for its psychoactive properties.

## Taxonomic Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Cortinariaceae
- **Genus:** Inocybe
- **Species:** I. aeruginascens Babos

Inocybe is one of the largest genera of agarics, containing several hundred described species worldwide. The vast majority are mycorrhizal associates of trees and many contain toxic compounds, particularly muscarine. The discovery that select Inocybe species produce psilocybin was unexpected given the genus" general reputation for toxicity rather than psychoactivity.

## Geographic Distribution

According to Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (1998), I. aeruginascens has been documented from:

- **Hungary:** Original collection locality and primary area of documentation. The species has been collected multiple times from Hungarian forests.
- **Germany:** Reported from forested areas, particularly in association with deciduous trees.
- **Other European countries:** Possible distribution in central and eastern European forests, though confirmed records are limited.

The species is considered European in distribution, fitting within the temperate zone pattern for many Inocybe species. Its range is likely broader than currently documented, as Inocybe is an under-surveyed genus that requires [[distribution-of-neurotropic-fungi]] in genera such as Psilocybe and Panaeolus, which are predominantly saprotrophic. This ecological difference has important implications for cultivation — mycorrhizal species are extremely difficult to cultivate artificially because they require a living host tree for nutrition.

## Morphological Characteristics

Inocybe aeruginascens shares the general morphological features of the genus Inocybe while exhibiting some distinctive characteristics:

- **Pileus (cap):** Small to medium (typically 2–5 cm diameter), conic to convex, often with a prominent umbo. Surface typically fibrous-silky (fibrillose) with radial fibers. Color varies from pale brown to olive-brown or yellowish-brown.
- **Gills (lamellae):** Adnate to sinuate, initially pale grayish, darkening to tobacco-brown with spore maturity. The gills are a critical identification feature in Inocybe species.
- **Stipe (stem):** Cylindrical, equal or slightly thickened at the base. Surface pruinose (frosted) near the apex. Pale to brownish.
- **Bluing reaction:** The species epithet "aeruginascens" refers to the greenish-blue (aeruginous) coloration that develops on the stipe base or bruised areas. This bluing reaction is the key indicator of psilocybin content and is unusual within the genus.
- **Odor:** Often described as spermatic or mealy, characteristic of many Inocybe species.
- **Spores:** Smooth, ellipsoid to bean-shaped (phaseoliform), with a prominent apical germ pore. Spore print dull brown.
