# Fungicolous Fungi — Parasites and Associates of Other Fungi

Fungicolous fungi are those that grow on, within, or in association with other fungi. This diverse group includes biotrophic parasites, necrotrophic pathogens, and commensals that exploit fungal sporocarps, hyphae, spores, and sclerotia as substrata.

## Types of Fungicolous Relationships

### Biotrophic Mycoparasites
Obtain nutrients from living host fungi through specialized structures (haustoria, contact cells) without killing them immediately. Often highly host-specific.

### Necrotrophic Parasites
Kill host tissue and then exploit the dead fungal biomass. Generally less host-specific than biotrophs.

### Mycosaprotrophs
Colonize dead or moribund fungal structures. A large portion of Hypocreales fungi are mycoparasitic or mycosaprotrophic — a fact often unrecognized.

## Taxonomic Groups Rich in Fungicolous Fungi

### Zygomycota
- **Piptocephalidaceae and Dimargaritaceae:** Biotrophic mycoparasites on Mucorales. Dispirales cannot utilize glucose; require glycerol, organic nitrogen, and vitamins.
- **Spinellus, Syzygites, Dicranophora:** Parasitize agarics. Spinellus requires organic nitrogen and temperatures near or below 20C.
- **Parasitella parricida:** Weak parasite of various Mucoraceae, using capturing hyphae.
- **Mortierella:** Highly chitinolytic; often appears indiscriminately on old fungal sporocarps. M. armillariicola known only from Armillaria.

### Ascomycota

#### Laboulbeniales — Pyxidiophoraceae
Heteroxenous biotrophic mycoparasites that can change hosts during life cycles. Ascospores translocated by mites; the fungus can penetrate its vector with haustoria.

#### Hypocreales
A large portion is mycoparasitic — extremely versatile in exploiting fungal substrata:
- **Trichoderma:** Polyphagous mycoparasites; includes biocontrol agents (T. harzianum) that can hardly be distinguished microscopically from aggressive competitors of cultivated mushrooms
- **Hypomyces:** Large genus of fungicolous species; H. cervinigenus on Humaria, H. stephanomatis on Helvella, H. mycogones on Geoglossaceae
- **Gliocladium (Aphanocladium album):** Common fungicolous species
- **Clonostachys (Gliocladium roseum):** Versatile mycoparasites

#### Sordariales — Ceratostomataceae
Many mycoparasites including:
- **Melanospora:** On various discomycetes, Fusarium, Botrytis, and aphyllophorales
- **Syspastospora parasitica:** On Paecilomyces, Beauveria, Hirsutella, Verticillium
- **Sphaerodes:** Specialized on truffle genera (Labyrinthomyces, Sphaerozone)

#### Leotiales
- **Orbilia:** On stromata of ascomycetes and basidiomata of aphyllophorales; anamorphs include Arthrobotrys (nematophagous) and Dactylella
- **Helicogonium:** 15 species growing on inoperculate discomycetes; asci easily mistaken for deviating host asci
- **Unguiculariopsis:** At least 24 species, all fungicolous; 10 also lichenicolous

## Fungi on Myxomycetes (Slime Molds)

A specialized group grows exclusively on sporocarps of myxomycetes:
- **Nectriopsis violacea:** Only on Fuligo species
- **Byssostilbe tomentosum:** Only on Trichiales
- **Nectriopsis exigua:** One of most ubiquitous; recorded from all major myxomycete groups
- Some are less specific colonizers, including yeasts (Dipodascus macrosporus) that can live inside plasmodia

## Biocontrol Implications

Several fungicolous fungi are important in biological control of plant diseases:
- **Trichoderma harzianum:** Biocontrol agent against plant pathogens
- **Penicillium glabrum:** Against Monilinia laxa peach twig blight
- **Epicoccum nigrum and Coniothyrium olivaceum:** Suppress canker development

## Challenges

- Many fungicolous fungi easily overlooked due to small size or similarity to host structures
- Some species are so host-specific they are rarely collected
- Anamorph-teleomorph connections often unclear
- The ecological significance of most fungicolous relationships remains poorly understood
