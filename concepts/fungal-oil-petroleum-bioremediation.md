# Mycoremediation of Oil and Petroleum Hydrocarbons

Oil spills and petroleum contamination represent some of the most
widespread environmental pollution challenges globally. While bacterial
biodegradation of petroleum hydrocarbons has been extensively studied,
fungi play important complementary roles through their ability to
degrade both aliphatic and aromatic fractions, including the most
recalcitrant high-molecular-weight components.

## Composition of Petroleum Pollutants

Petroleum-contaminated sites contain a complex mixture of
hydrocarbon classes:
- **Aliphatic hydrocarbons** -- straight-chain and branched alkanes
  (relatively easily degraded)
- **Cycloalkanes** -- cycloparaffins of varying ring sizes
- **Monocyclic [[singh-fungal-biodegradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]]** (MAHs) -- BTEX compounds
  (benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, xylenes)
- **[[staycare-fungal-degradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]]** (PAHs) -- the most persistent
  fraction (2-6 fused rings)
- **Resins and asphaltenes** -- polar, high-molecular-weight components
  (most recalcitrant)

The relative proportions affect remediation strategy. Alkanes are most
biodegradable; asphaltenes are least.

### Mechanisms of Alkane Degradation
- **Cytochrome P450 monooxygenase** -- introduces oxygen at terminal
  or subterminal positions
- **Secondary alcohol dehydrogenase** -- converts alcohols to ketones
- Further oxidation yields fatty acids that enter beta-oxidation
- Terminal oxidation yields primary alcohols, aldehydes, then fatty
  acids; subterminal oxidation yields secondary alcohols and ketones

## Oil Spill Case Studies

### Exxon Valdez (1989)
The most studied oil spill bioremediation effort provided key lessons:
- Biostimulation with fertilizer nutrients enhanced biodegradation
  of oil on shorelines
- Indigenous microbial populations (both [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-bacterial-and-fungal-dominant-soil]])
  responded to nutrient addition
- Oil composition changes over time favored more resistant aromatic
  fractions

### Deepwater Horizon (2010)
The Deepwater Horizon spill released approximately 4.9 million barrels
of crude oil. [[biodiversity-fungi-soil-fungal-communities]] in deep-sea sediments showed
significant shifts in composition following the spill.
- Cladosporium and Penicillium species dominated oiled sediment
  communities
- Fungal contribution to hydrocarbon degradation was underestimated
  in early response models
- Marine-derived fungi adapted to cold, high-pressure deep-sea
  conditions demonstrated degradation of both aliphatic and aromatic
  fractions

### General Field Experience
- Nutrient addition (biostimulation) is often more effective than
  organism addition (bioaugmentation)
- Lignocellulosic carriers (sawdust, wood chips) serve dual purposes
  as inoculum carrier and [[pressure-cooker-sterilisation-fungal-substrate]]
