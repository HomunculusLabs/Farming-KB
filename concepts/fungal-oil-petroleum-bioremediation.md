# Mycoremediation of Oil and Petroleum Hydrocarbons Oil spills and petroleum contamination represent some of the most widespread environmental pollution challenges globally. While bacterial biodegradation of petroleum hydrocarbons has been extensively studied, fungi play important complementary roles through their ability to degrade both aliphatic and aromatic fractions, including the most recalcitrant high-molecular-weight components. ## Composition of Petroleum Pollutants Petroleum-contaminated sites contain a complex mixture of hydrocarbon classes: - **Aliphatic hydrocarbons** -- straight-chain and branched alkanes (relatively easily degraded) - **Cycloalkanes** -- cycloparaffins of varying ring sizes - **Monocyclic aromatic hydrocarbons** (MAHs) -- BTEX compounds (benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, xylenes) - **Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons** (PAHs) -- the most persistent fraction (2-6 fused rings) - **Resins and asphaltenes** -- polar, high-molecular-weight components (most recalcitrant) The relative proportions affect remediation strategy. Alkanes are most biodegradable; asphaltenes are least. ## Fungal Degradation of Aliphatic Hydrocarbons ### Alkane-Degrading Fungi Numerous fungi can utilize aliphatic hydrocarbons as sole carbon sources: - **Cunninghamella elegans** -- degrades n-alkanes from C10 to C32; optimal chain length C14-C16 - **Aspergillus niger, A. fumigatus, A. terreus** -- alkane oxidation capabilities - **Cladosporium, Penicillium, Fusarium** -- common soil isolates with alkane-degrading ability - **Paecilomyces lilacinus** -- shows activity against long-chain alkanes ### Mechanisms of Alkane Degradation - **Cytochrome P450 monooxygenase** -- introduces oxygen at terminal or subterminal positions - **Secondary alcohol dehydrogenase** -- converts alcohols to ketones - Further oxidation yields fatty acids that enter beta-oxidation - Terminal oxidation yields primary alcohols, aldehydes, then fatty acids - Subterminal oxidation yields secondary alcohols and ketones ## Fungal Degradation of Aromatic Components The aromatic fraction (BTEX and PAHs) is the most environmentally concerning due to toxicity and persistence. Fungi degrade these through: - **Cytochrome P450 oxidation** -- converts BTEX and PAHs to epoxides, dihydrodiols, and phenols - **Ligninolytic enzymes** -- [[fungal-degradation-chlorophenols-btex]].

## Oil Spill Case Studies

### Exxon Valdez (1989)
The most studied oil spill bioremediation effort provided key lessons:
- Biostimulation with fertilizer nutrients enhanced biodegradation of oil on shorelines
- Indigenous microbial populations (both [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-bacterial-and-fungal-dominant-soil]]) responded to nutrient addition
- The experience established that nutrient delivery to intertidal zones was feasible
- Oil composition changes over time favored more resistant (aromatic) fractions

### General Field Experience
Field applications have demonstrated:
- Nutrient addition (biostimulation) is often more effective than organism addition (bioaugmentation)
- [[formulation-and-delivery-of-fungal-inocula]] can be added to contaminated soils
- Lignocellulosic carriers (sawdust, [[fukuoka-textdoc-sawdust-wood-chips-orchard-soil-acidification-experiment]]) serve dual purposes as inoculum carrier and fungal substrate
