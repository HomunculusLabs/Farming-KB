## Preparation Methods

### Traditional Boiling Method

The most common preparation involves slicing the cactus into disks or strips
(after removing spines and the waxy outer skin),
then boiling in water for several hours.
The liquid is reduced and strained through multiple boilings to concentrate the active material.
This produces a bitter, nausea-inducing tea.
Because of the large volume of plant material required,
emesis is even more likely with Trichocereus preparations than with peyote.

### Dried Powder Method

The cactus flesh can be sliced thin and dried, then ground into a powder.
This powder can be encapsulated or mixed with liquid for consumption.
Drying concentrates the active material by removing water weight,
making it easier to consume the required dose.

### Chemical Extraction

Chemical [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-spore-extraction-methods]] can isolate mescaline from the plant material,
producing a relatively pure crystalline substance through acid-base extraction.
