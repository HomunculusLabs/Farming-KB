Pest management is an essential skill for cannabis growers. Pests can quickly devastate a crop if not identified and treated promptly. Green emphasizes prevention first, with [[integrated-pest-management]] (IPM) as the standard approach.

## Prevention (First Line of Defense)

The most effective pest management is preventing pests from entering your grow space:

### Physical Barriers
- **Sealed grow space**: Close all gaps, cracks, and vents
- **Intake filters**: HEPA or fine mesh filters on all air intakes
- **Screened vents**: Fine window screen on exhaust and intake vents
- **Positive air pressure**: If possible, maintain slightly higher pressure inside the grow room than outside — air flows out, not in

### Hygiene
- **Clean grow area**: Remove dead plant material, debris, and standing water
- **Sterilize between grows**: Clean and sanitize all surfaces, containers, and equipment between crops
- **No outdoor clothing**: Change clothes before entering the grow area if you have been in the garden or around other plants
- **Quarantine new plants**: Isolate any new clones or plants for 1–2 weeks before introducing them to your main grow

### Monitoring
- **Yellow sticky traps**: Hang near plants to catch flying pests and monitor pest populations
- **Regular inspection**: Check plants thoroughly at least 2–3 times per week, especially the undersides of leaves
- **Magnification**: Use a jeweler's loupe or hand lens (30–60x) to inspect closely for tiny pests

## Common Cannabis Pests

### Spider Mites

**Identification**: Tiny (0.5mm) spider-like arachnids. Look for:
- Tiny dots on leaf undersides
- Fine webbing between leaves and branches (advanced infestation)
- Small yellow or white speckling (stippling) on leaves
- Leaves eventually turn yellow and die

**Treatment**:
- **Spray with water**: A strong blast of water can dislodge mites (temporary)
- **[[neem]] oil**: Diluted neem oil spray (per label directions) applied to leaf undersides
- **Insecticidal soap**: Effective against mites, less toxic than chemical sprays
- **Predatory mites**: Release predatory mites (Phytoseiulus persimilis) as biological control
- **Chemical miticides**: As a last resort — follow label directions and respect pre-harvest intervals
- **Repeat treatments**: Spider mites have a rapid life cycle (7 days) — repeat treatments every 3–5 days until eliminated

### Fungus Gnats

**Identification**: Small (2–3mm) dark flying insects around the soil surface. Larvae live in the soil and feed on roots.

**Signs**:
- Tiny flies hovering around plants and soil surface
- Small white larvae visible in the soil
- Poor plant growth from root damage
- Yellowing leaves from root stress

**Treatment**:
- **Yellow sticky traps**: Catch flying adults and monitor populations
- **Allow soil to dry**: Larvae need moist soil — letting the top inch dry between waterings reduces populations
- **Neem oil drench**: Water with diluted neem oil to kill larvae in soil
- **BTI (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis)**: Mosquito dunks or Gnatrol added to water — kills larvae
- **Sand layer**: Apply a thin layer of sand or diatomaceous earth on the soil surface to prevent egg-laying

### Thrips

**Identification**: Very small (1–2mm) slender insects. Look for:
- Silver or bronze streaking on leaves
- Tiny black fecal spots on leaves
- Small insects visible on leaves and flowers
- Deformed new growth

**Treatment**:
- Blue sticky traps (thrips are attracted to blue)
- Neem oil or insecticidal soap sprays
- Spinosad-based sprays

### Aphids

**Identification**: Small (1–3mm) soft-bodied insects, often green, black, or white. Found in clusters on new growth.

**Signs**:
- Clusters of insects on stems and new leaves
- Curled, deformed leaves
- Sticky honeydew on leaves and below
- Sooty mold growing on honeydew

**Treatment**:
- Insecticidal soap
- Neem oil

### Whiteflies

**Identification**: Tiny white flying insects that flutter up when plants are disturbed. Found on leaf undersides.

**Signs**:
- White flies rising when plants are shaken
- Yellowing leaves
- Sticky honeydew on leaves

**Treatment**:
- Yellow sticky traps
- Neem oil or insecticidal soap
- Encarsia formosa (parasitic wasp) as biological control

### Caterpillars

**Identification**: Larvae of moths/butterflies found on outdoor and greenhouse grows.

**Signs**:
- Holes chewed in leaves
- Webbing in buds
- Droppings (frass) on leaves

**Treatment**:
- Hand-picking (for small numbers)
- BTI spray (Bacillus thuringiensis)
- Spinosad spray
- Physical barriers (row covers)

## Treatment Guidelines

### General Rules
1. **Identify the pest first**: Different pests require different treatments
2. **Start with the least toxic option**: Begin with neem oil, insecticidal soap, or water — escalate only if needed
3. **Treat the undersides of leaves**: Most pests live and feed on the undersides
4. **Repeat treatments**: Most pesticides require multiple applications (follow label instructions)
5. **Isolate affected plants**: If possible, move infested plants away from healthy ones
6. **Do not spray during flowering**: Sprays on buds during flowering create residue, off-flavors, and mold risk. If pests appear during flower, use predatory insects or accept some damage rather than spraying.
7. **Respect pre-harvest intervals**: Chemical pesticides have minimum time requirements before harvest — check labels

### Organic and Natural Options

Green favors organic methods:
- **Neem oil**: Broad-spectrum insecticide and fungicide
- **Insecticidal soap**: Safe, effective against soft-bodied insects
- **Diatomaceous earth**: Physical insecticide for crawling pests
