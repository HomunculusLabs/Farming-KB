## Pond Maintenance and Troubleshooting

Operational guidance for farm ponds — water quality management, feeding strategies, fish harvest, winter care, predator control, and regenerative integration. See [[pond-and-aquaculture]] for pond design, site selection, fish species, and duck integration.

## Water Quality Management

### Key Parameters
| Parameter | Ideal Range | Danger Level |
|-----------|------------|--------------|
| **Dissolved Oxygen** | >5 mg/L | <3 mg/L (fish die) |
| **pH** | 6.5-8.5 | <5 or >10 |
| **Ammonia (NH₃)** | <0.02 mg/L | >0.1 mg/L (toxic) |
| **Nitrite (NO₂⁻)** | <0.1 mg/L | >0.5 mg/L |
| **Temperature** | 65-80°F (warm water) | >90°F (stress) |
| **Alkalinity** | 50-200 mg/L | <20 mg/L (pH crash risk) |

### Aeration
- **Diffused aeration** (bottom): Most efficient. Air compressor + weighted diffuser. 0.5-1 CFM per acre.
- **Surface aerators/fountains**: Decorative + functional. Less efficient, but add oxygen at surface.
- **Wind-powered**: No electricity needed. Good for remote ponds. Low output.
- **No aeration needed** if: stocking density low (<200 fish/acre), pond <8 ft deep, moderate duck load, and good plant coverage.

### Biofiltration
- Constructed wetland filter (gravel bed with plants) treats overflow water before returning to pond
- Beneficial bacteria (see [[effective-microorganisms-em]]) process ammonia → nitrite → nitrate
- Barley straw bales (1 bale per 1000 sq ft) reduce algae via slow-release hydrogen peroxide

## Feeding

### Natural Forage (Free)
- Phytoplankton and zooplankton (primary food web)
- Aquatic insects and larvae
- Duckweed (harvest and feed, or let fish graze directly)
- Black soldier fly larvae ([[composting-systems]] connection)
- Earthworms and detritus

### Supplemental Feeding
| Season | Feed Rate | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Spring (50-65°F) | 1-2% body weight/week | Gradual increase as water warms |
| Summer (65-80°F) | 3-4% body weight/week | Peak feeding, split into 2+ feedings |
| Fall (65-50°F) | Reduce to 1% | Stop when water drops below 50°F |
| Winter (<50°F) | **Do not feed** | Fish are dormant, food rots |

**Feed types**: Commercial catfish pellets (28-32% protein), floating pellets (can observe consumption), duckweed (free), kitchen scraps (limited — avoid bread).

## Harvest and Processing

### Methods
- **Seine net**: Best for pond harvest. 100 ft × 6 ft net with ¼" mesh. Requires 2 people.
- **Trotlines**: Passive method for catfish. Bait with liver, shad, or worms.
- **Traps**: Minnow traps for baitfish. Larger hoop traps for catfish.
- **Hook and line**: Recreational harvest, selective.

### Timing
- **Catfish**: Harvest at 1-2 lbs (12-18 months). Best in spring/fall when water is cool (better meat quality).
- **Tilapia**: Harvest before water drops below 55°F (October in zone 7-8). 6-9 months from stocking.
- **Trout**: Harvest in spring before water warms above 65°F.

### Processing
- Ice slurry immediately after harvest (better meat quality)
- Scale, gut, fillet, or skin
- Preservation: freezing (vacuum-sealed, 6 months), smoking (hot smoke 140-180°F), canning (pressure canner), pickling

## Pond as Irrigation Source

- **Gravity feed**: Pond at elevation → garden below. Zero energy. Size outlet pipe for flow rate needed.
- **Pump**: Solar DC pump or gasoline transfer pump. 1-2 GPM sufficient for drip irrigation on ½ acre garden.
- **Nutrient-rich water**: Pond water contains dissolved nutrients from fish/duck waste. Excellent for garden irrigation — reduces fertilizer needs. See [[soil-fertility-management]].
- **Fill-and-drain cycling**: Draw water in summer (lowers level for oxygen), replenish with winter rain/spring overflow.
- **Connection to [[water-management]]**: Pond can receive greywater (if biologically filtered), overflow to swales, supply livestock water.

## Winter Management (Zone 7-8)

### Critical Concerns
- **Winter kill**: Ice seals surface → no gas exchange → oxygen depletion → fish die. Most common in shallow (<6 ft) or heavily stocked ponds.
- **Prevention**: Maintain open hole (1-2 ft diameter) with aerator or de-icer. Do NOT break ice (shock waves damage fish).
- **Feeding**: Stop all feeding below 50°F. Fish metabolism is too slow to process food; uneaten food rots.
- **Depth matters**: Ponds 8+ ft deep rarely winter-kill. Shallow ponds need active management.

### Seasonal Timeline
| Month | Action |
|-------|--------|
| **Nov** | Reduce feeding. Remove floating plants (they die and rot). |
| **Dec** | Stop feeding. Install de-icer/aerator if needed. |
| **Jan-Feb** | Monitor ice. Keep aerator running. No access needed. |
| **Mar** | Check fish survival. Test water quality. Resume light feeding at 50°F+. |
| **Apr** | Restock if needed. Begin full feeding. Plant new aquatic plants. |

## Predators and Problems

### Predators
| Predator | Sign | Control |
|----------|------|---------|
| **Great Blue Heron** | Fish vanishing, stabbed fish on bank | Net over pond, motion-activated sprinkler, scare tape |
| **Raccoons** | Fish with heads eaten, tracks in mud | Electric fence wire around edge, live trap |
| **Otters/Mink** | Multiple fish killed, not eaten | Exclude with fencing, trapping (check regulations) |
| **Turtles** | Fish with bitten fins | Trap and relocate (check regulations) |

### Algae Management
- **Prevention**: Limit nutrient input (duck stocking, feed), maintain 30-50% plant coverage, use barley straw.
- **Green water (planktonic algae)**: Beneficial in moderation (feeds fish). Problem when water turns pea-soup. Add submerged plants, reduce feeding.
- **String algae (filamentous)**: Rake out manually. Add barley straw. Reduce nutrients.
- **Blue-green algae (cyanobacteria)**: **Toxic to humans and animals.** If present: do not swim, do not let animals drink, test water. Usually caused by excess nutrients + warm stagnant water.

### Mosquito Control
- Stock **mosquitofish (Gambusia)** — consume 100+ larvae/day
- Maintain [[duck-keeping]] presence — ducks are voracious mosquito predators
- Use **Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis (Bti)** dunks — biological, safe for fish/plants
- Ensure flowing water or aeration — mosquitoes breed in still, stagnant water

## Regenerative Integration

A pond is the connective tissue of a regenerative homestead:

- **Nutrient cycling**: Fish/duck waste → phytoplankton → fish food → harvest → scraps → [[composting-systems]] → garden → crop waste → duck/chicken feed → manure → pond
- **Wildlife habitat**: Amphibians (frogs, salamanders), reptiles (turtles, snakes), birds (herons, kingfishers, ducks), beneficial insects
- **Fire protection**: Pond provides emergency water source for firefighting. Position near structures and garden.
- **Microclimate**: Water moderates temperature — cooler in summer, warmer in winter within 50-100 ft radius. Extends growing season for nearby [[food-forest-design]] plantings.
- **Duck forage**: Pond supports ducks year-round with aquatic plants, insects, and snails. See [[duck-keeping]].
- **Irrigation relay**: Pond overflow → swales → [[cover-cropping-guide]] fields → [[water-management]] storage. No water leaves the property.
- **Goat/duck watering**: Gravity-fed troughs from pond reduce well dependence. See [[goat-keeping]].
- **Pest management**: Frog populations from pond provide natural pest control in gardens. Reference [[cannabis-pest-management]].
