# Cultivar Specificity in Plant Microbiomes

Cultivar specificity refers to the phenomenon where different cultivars (genetic varieties) of the same plant species maintain distinct microbial communities, particularly in their root endosphere (endorhiza). This genotype-dependent selection is a key component of the two-tier selection model, representing the second filtering step that shapes root-associated microbiomes after soil-driven community assembly.

## What Is Cultivar Specificity?

A cultivar is a plant variety that has been selected and maintained through cultivation for desirable characteristics — yield, flavor, appearance, disease resistance, or (in the case of Cannabis) cannabinoid profile. Despite belonging to the same species, different cultivars can have substantial genetic differences that influence their root exudate chemistry, immune responses, and root architecture, all of which affect which microorganisms can successfully colonize the root interior.

Cultivar specificity means that two genetically distinct plants of the same species, grown side-by-side in identical soil, will harbor significantly different endorhiza communities. This effect has been documented in numerous crops including maize, potato, barley, Arabidopsis, poplar, and Cannabis.

## Evidence from Cannabis Cultivars

The study by Winston et al. (2014) provided some of the clearest evidence for cultivar-specificity in a crop plant. Five Cannabis cultivars were examined across two experiments:

**Experiment 1** (three cultivars, one soil type):
- Burmese — balanced C. sativa × C. indica hybrid, moderate THC:CBD ratio
- BooKoo Kush — sativa-dominant hybrid, moderately high THC:CBD ratio
- Sour Diesel — C. sativa, high THC:CBD ratio

**Experiment 2** (two cultivars, two soil types):
- Maui Wowie — C. sativa, high THC:CBD ratio
- White Widow — balanced C. sativa × C. indica hybrid, moderate THC:CBD ratio

### Key Findings

1. **Endorhiza communities clustered significantly by cultivar** — when controlling for soil type, the [[edaphic-factors-microbial-community-structure]] within the endorhiza was significantly different between cultivars (weighted UniFrac, ADONIS R2 = 0.59, p = 0.004 in experiment 2)

2. **Cultivar affected abundance, not presence** — in weighted (abundance-based) analysis, 71 OTUs differed significantly between strains. In unweighted (presence/absence) analysis, zero OTUs differed between strains. This means cultivar selection modulates how abundant each microbe becomes inside the root, but not whether it can enter at all.

3. **Soil type had stronger effects on composition** — 657 OTUs differed significantly between soil types (unweighted), compared to zero between strains. Soil determines the available pool; cultivar selects from within that pool.

4. **Specific bacterial taxa drove cultivar differences:**
   - Methylophilus was highly abundant in BooKoo Kush endorhiza (13% of community) but nearly absent in Burmese (0.13%) and completely absent in Sour Diesel
   - [[sphingomonas-wittichii-cannabis-endorhiza-strain-specificity]] was prevalent in Maui Wowie but not in White Widow, and has been implicated in phenazine metabolism and enhanced soil survival

## The Cultivar-Specificity Spectrum

Cultivar effects on the microbiome follow a clear spatial pattern:

- **Bulk soil** — no cultivar effect (microbes have not yet encountered the plant)
- **Rhizosphere** — weak to moderate cultivar effect (some influence through root exudates, but soil chemistry dominates)
- **Endorhiza** — strong cultivar effect (host immune system and tissue chemistry create strong selective pressures)

This gradient was clearly demonstrated in the pooled analysis of both Cannabis experiments. In weighted UniFrac analysis, strain explained R2 = 0.301 of total variation when all samples were combined, but this effect was concentrated in the endorhiza compartment.

## Mechanisms of Cultivar-Specific Selection

Several mechanisms likely contribute to cultivar-specific microbiome assembly:

### Root Exudate Chemistry

Different cultivars produce different blends of root exudates, including sugars, amino acids, organic acids, [[plant-defense-chemistry-and-secondary-metabolites]]. These chemical profiles create distinct nutritional environments that favor different microbial taxa. In Cannabis, the production of cannabinoids and terpenes by roots may directly influence which bacteria can thrive in the endorhiza.

### Immune System Variation

Plant immune receptors (NLRs, RLKs, and RLPs) vary between cultivars, leading to different patterns of microbe recognition and immune response. A bacterial strain that evades detection by one cultivar's immune system may be recognized and excluded by another's.

### Root Architecture

Differences in root branching patterns, root hair density, cortical cell structure, and lateral root emergence frequency create different physical landscapes for microbial colonization. Cultivars with more lateral root emergence points may offer more entry routes for endophytes.

### Epigenetic Regulation

Epigenetic differences between cultivars can affect the expression of genes involved in root-microbe interactions, even when the underlying DNA sequence is similar.

## Cannabinoids and the Microbiome

An intriguing finding from the Cannabis study was the correlation between cannabinoid profiles and endorhiza community structure. Mantel tests showed significant associations between cannabinoid composition and endorhiza community similarity (unweighted r = 0.863, p = 0.001).

However, the researchers noted this correlation is difficult to disentangle from soil effects, because plants grown in Orange County soil had both higher THC levels and different soil chemistry than those grown in Mo-Bio soil. The causal direction also remains unclear: do cannabinoids shape the microbiome, or does the microbiome influence cannabinoid production?

## Cross-Species Comparisons

Cultivar-specificity is not unique to Cannabis. It has been observed in:

- **Maize (Zea mays)** — different maize lines maintain distinct rhizosphere communities, with effects stronger in older plants
- **Potato (Solanum tuberosum)** — transgenic potatoes with altered starch composition harbored different soil and [[cannabis-rhizosphere-bacterial-communities]]
- **[[arabidopsis-thaliana]]** — natural accessions showed genotype-dependent root microbiome composition, with specific loci mapped to microbiome traits
- **Poplar (Populus deltoides)** — distinct endophytic communities in roots across contrasting soil types, with host genotype effects on community structure

## Implications for Cultivation

For Cannabis cultivators, cultivar-specificity has several practical implications:

- **Inoculant selection** — a microbial inoculant that benefits one cultivar may not benefit another; testing across cultivars is essential
- **Crop rotation planning** — different cultivars may differentially deplete or enrich specific soil microbial populations
- **Breeding programs** — selecting for cultivars that recruit beneficial endophytes could improve plant fitness, disease resistance, and [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]]
- **Consistency and quality** — understanding the microbiome's role in cannabinoid production could help achieve more consistent product profiles

## Limitations of Current Knowledge

Several limitations constrain our understanding of cultivar-specificity in plant microbiomes:

- **Small sample sizes** — most studies examine only a handful of cultivars; the full range of cultivar effects across the species' genetic diversity remains unknown
- **Confounding variables** — separating cultivar effects from soil, climate, and management effects requires carefully controlled experiments
- **Temporal dynamics** — most studies provide snapshots rather than tracking how cultivar-specific communities change over the growing season
- **Functional vs taxonomic specificity** — different cultivars may harbor taxonomically similar communities with different functional capacities, or vice versa

## Research Methods for Detecting Cultivar Specificity

Standard approaches for identifying cultivar-specific microbiome patterns include:

- **16S rRNA gene sequencing** — Illumina sequencing of the V4 region provides taxonomic resolution to approximately genus level, sufficient for detecting community-level differences between cultivars
- **ADONIS/PERMANOVA** — statistical tests that partition variance in community composition between factors (soil, cultivar, sample type) to quantify the relative importance of each
- **UniFrac distance metrics** — weighted UniFrac (accounts for abundance) is more sensitive to cultivar effects than unweighted UniFrac (presence/absence only)
- **Indicator species analysis** — identifies specific bacterial taxa that are significantly associated with particular cultivars
- **Mantel tests** — correlate community dissimilarity matrices with environmental or chemical variables (e.g., cannabinoid profiles)
