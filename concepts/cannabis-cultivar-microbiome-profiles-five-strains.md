# Cannabis Cultivar Microbiome Profiles Across Five Strains

The Winston et al. (2014) study provided the first systematic comparison of bacterial
communities across five distinct Cannabis cultivars, revealing how host genotype shapes
the [[endorhiza-endophytic-bacteria]] differently in each strain. The five cultivars
examined spanned sativa, indica, and hybrid chemotypes grown under controlled organic
conditions in Southern California. The study's two-experiment design allowed researchers
to distinguish between soil-driven and genotype-driven effects on the root microbiome,
establishing foundational knowledge for [[cannabis-microbiome-research]].

## The Five Cultivars and Their Chemotype Profiles

Each cultivar was selected for its distinct chemotype, defined primarily by the
tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) to cannabidiol (CBD) ratio. **Sour Diesel**, a pure
Cannabis sativa cultivar, was associated with a high THC-to-CBD ratio typical of
stimulating daytime varieties. **Bookoo Kush**, a sativa-dominant hybrid of C. sativa
and C. indica, showed a moderately high THC-to-CBD ratio characteristic of relaxing
but not heavily sedating strains. **Burmese**, a balanced hybrid of both species,
displayed a moderate THC-to-CBD ratio representing a middle ground in the chemotype
spectrum. **Maui Wowie**, a classic C. sativa landrace from Hawaii, carried a high
THC-to-CBD ratio. **White Widow**, a balanced C. sativa × C. indica hybrid, was known
for a more moderate THC-to-CBD ratio and was the only cultivar tested across two
different soil types.

The chemotype diversity among these five cultivars is important because secondary
metabolites like THC and CBD are exuded into the rhizosphere and can influence which
microbes colonize root tissues. The [[cannabinoid-microbiome-correlation-cannabis]]
data from the second experiment confirmed that cannabinoid profiles were significantly
correlated with endorhiza community structure, though disentangling this from soil
edaphic effects proved challenging due to the co-variation of soil chemistry and
cannabinoid production in the White Widow plants.

## First Experiment: Three Strains Under Uniform Edaphic Conditions

The first experiment compared Burmese, Bookoo Kush, and Sour Diesel grown in locally
composted sandy loam soil in Vista, California. Soil physicochemical analysis revealed
minimal edaphic variation between the three cultivars' growing environments (pH range
6.63–6.94, similar sand-silt-clay ratios of approximately 63–66% sand, 16–18% silt,
and 18–21% clay). This experimental design deliberately minimized soil differences to
isolate the effect of plant genotype on microbial community structure.

Despite the uniform soil, [[endorhiza-microbiome-cannabis-cultivar-specificity]]
differed significantly between strains. Using unweighted UniFrac, endorhiza communities
clustered significantly by strain (ADONIS: R² = 0.39, p = 0.003). Weighted UniFrac
showed an even stronger effect (ADONIS: R² = 0.59, p = 0.004), indicating that strain
differences were most pronounced in the relative abundances of shared taxa rather than
in the presence or absence of unique species. This pattern is consistent with the
broader [[cannabis-microbiome-otu-abundance-vs-presence-cannabis-strains]] finding
across the pooled dataset.

The most striking strain-specific differentiator was the methylotrophic bacterium
Methylophilus, which comprised 13% of the Bookoo Kush endorhiza community but only
0.13% in Burmese and was entirely absent from Sour Diesel. This dramatic enrichment
suggests that Bookoo Kush either produces root exudates that selectively favor
methylotrophs or provides a unique niche within its root tissues for these organisms.
The significance of this Methylophilus enrichment survived false discovery rate
correction (FDR: p = 0.012), confirming it was not a statistical artifact.

## Second Experiment: Two Strains Across Two Soil Types

The second experiment tested White Widow and Maui Wowie across two geographically and
chemically distinct soil types: Mo-Bio soil (MB; pH 6.77, salinity 1.73, total N 0.53%,
organic C 20.0%, water content 0.371) and Orange County soil (OC; pH 6.63, salinity
5.12, total N 0.26%, organic C 3.02%, water content 0.113). White Widow was grown in
both soils while Maui Wowie was grown only in MB soil, providing a cross-soil comparison
for one cultivar.

Both weighted and unweighted UniFrac analyses showed that soil type was the dominant
factor structuring communities overall (unweighted ADONIS: R² = 0.32, p = 0.001;
weighted ADONIS: R² = 0.21, p = 0.001). However, within the endorhiza, strain remained
a significant factor (unweighted ADONIS: R² = 0.10, p = 0.008; weighted ADONIS:
R² = 0.27, p = 0.001). A key strain-level differentiator was the presence of
[[sphingomonas-wittichii-cannabis-endorhiza-strain-specificity|Sphingomonas wittichii]],
which was prevalent in Maui Wowie endorhiza. This bacterium can metabolize
phenazine-1-carboxylic acid and has been implicated in increased survival in soil
environments, suggesting a possible role in [[cannabis-endophytes-and-terroir]]
through biotransformation of root secondary metabolites.

## Shared Core Community Across All Strains

Despite the significant inter-strain differences, all five cultivars maintained a core
[[core-microbiome-cannabis-endorhiza]] consisting of Pseudomonas, Cellvibrio,
Oxalobacteraceae, Xanthomonadaceae, Actinomycetales, and Sphingobacteriales. With the
exception of Cellvibrio, all prevalent core members are well-known endophytic bacteria
primarily within the orders Gammaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria. This supports
observations from other plant systems and is consistent with the [[two-tier-selection-model-rhizosphere-endorhiza]]
where soil determines the available species pool while host genotype fine-tunes relative
abundances within the root interior.

## Sampling Timing and Community Preservation

An important methodological consideration across both experiments was the timing of
sampling relative to harvest. The first experiment sampled eight weeks post-harvest,
while the second sampled two weeks prior to harvest. Despite this difference, cultivar
specificity was detectable in both experiments, suggesting that strain-level microbial
signatures persist even after the plant's above-ground biomass has been removed. The
persistence of these signatures has implications for crop rotation and succession
planting in Cannabis cultivation systems.

## Implications for Cultivar-Specific Microbial Management

The finding that each Cannabis cultivar harbors a distinct [[endorhiza-bacterial-communities]]
has direct implications for commercial cultivation. Breeders selecting for specific
chemotypes may inadvertently be selecting for specific microbial partners through
root exudate profiles. Understanding these cultivar-microbe associations could enable
targeted inoculation strategies where beneficial microbes identified in one strain's
endorhiza could be introduced to related cultivars to enhance growth, disease
resistance, or secondary metabolite production. The strong strain effect on
[[proteobacteria-dominance-cannabis-endorhiza]] abundance, rather than presence/absence,
suggests that management practices need only shift the competitive balance within the
root environment to favor beneficial organisms already present in the soil reservoir.
