## The Five Cultivars and Their Chemotype Profiles

Each cultivar was selected for its distinct chemotype, defined primarily by the
tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) to cannabidiol (CBD) ratio. **Sour Diesel**, a pure
Cannabis sativa cultivar, was associated with a high THC-to-CBD ratio typical of
stimulating daytime varieties. **Bookoo Kush**, a sativa-dominant hybrid of C. sativa
and C. indica, showed a moderately high THC-to-CBD ratio characteristic of relaxing
but not heavily sedating strains. **Burmese**, a balanced hybrid of both species,
displayed a moderate THC-to-CBD ratio representing a middle ground in the chemotype
spectrum. **Maui Wowie**, a classic C. sativa landrace from Hawaii, carried a high
THC-to-CBD ratio. **White Widow**, a balanced C. sativa × C. indica hybrid, was known
for a more moderate THC-to-CBD ratio and was the only cultivar tested across two
different soil types.

The chemotype diversity among these five cultivars is important because secondary
metabolites like THC and CBD are exuded into the rhizosphere and can influence which
microbes colonize root tissues. The [[endorhiza-microbiome-cannabis-cultivar-specificity]]
differed significantly between strains. Using unweighted UniFrac, endorhiza communities
