# Endocannabinoid System

The endocannabinoid system (ECS) is a biological regulatory mechanism
present in all mammals that plays a central role in maintaining
physiological and psychological balance across the entire body. It
functions like a lock-and-key system, with cannabinoid receptors (locks)
activated by cannabinoid molecules (keys) produced by the body, sourced
from plants like [[cannabis-health-thc-tetrahydrocannabinol]], or synthesized in laboratories.

## Overview and Discovery

The scientific identification of the endocannabinoid system in 1990 is
a relatively recent development that stemmed from research into the
cannabis plant, for which it is named. Since then, approximately ten new
studies examining the ECS have been published every month, reflecting
the excitement and hope that early results have generated in the medical
research community and among physicians, patients, and caregivers.

The ECS is involved in many critical functions including:
- Initiating physiological and psychological changes needed to adapt
  to ever-changing internal and external environments
- Determining whether a fertilized egg implants in the uterine wall
- Producing nurturing responses to injuries and inflammations
- Providing protective mechanisms against numerous cancers,
  neurological diseases, and nerve damage
- Mitigating changes associated with aging

The ECS's role in stress and trauma responses is particularly relevant
to [[cannabis-health-methods-of-administration]] research.

## Cannabinoid Receptors

Large numbers of cannabinoid receptors are embedded in specific cell
membranes throughout the human body. These receptors can be activated
in three ways:
1. Release of the body's own cannabinoids (e.g., anandamide)
2. Introduction of plant-based cannabinoids such as cannabis
3. Manufactured cannabinoids such as [[dronabinol]]

The two most common types of cannabinoid receptors are CB1 and CB2.
Scientists suspect there are three more endocannabinoid receptors whose
locations and functions will be more fully understood after more
research. For the time being, these are referred to as non-CB1 and
non-CB2.

## CB1 Receptors

CB1 receptors are located primarily in the central nervous system (CNS),
consisting of the brain and spinal cord. They are found in parts of the
brain that regulate motor control, attention, emotion, thinking
(cerebellum), habits (basal ganglia and limbic system), and memory
functions (hippocampus). CB1 receptors are also found in both male and
female reproductive systems.

Notably, CB1 receptors are absent in the part of the brain that
regulates heart and respiratory function (the medulla oblongata). This
makes cannabinoid-based pain management inherently safer than opiates,
which can cause death from respiratory arrest.

## CB2 Receptors

CB2 receptors cluster in the periphery of the body, especially in
tissue involved in proper immune responses. The spleen houses a high
concentration of CB2 receptors. CB2 receptor engagement is particularly
important for treatment of chronic pain associated with autoimmune
diseases in which the body's immune system turns on itself.

## Five Endogenous Cannabinoids

Five distinct endocannabinoids produced by the human body have been
identified:
1. Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamide, AEA)
2. 2-arachidonoyl glycerol (2-AG)
3. 2-arachidonoyl-glyceryl-ether (Noladin ether)
4. O-arachidonoyl-ethanolamine (Virodhamine)
5. N-arachidonoyl-dopamine (NADA)

## Key Functions of the ECS

The ECS balances and strengthens the nervous and immune systems,
initiates pain control, and calms inflammation. It initiates neurogenesis
(the production of new nerve cells), which is essential to recovery from
brain damage and crucial to protecting nerve cells and enhancing memory
function. The ECS also increases the ability to try out new perspectives
and experiences, changing brain functions for the better in a process
called neuroplasticity. For how [[blesching-cannabinoid-pharmacology]] translates
into specific therapeutic applications, see the [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-cannabinoid-terpene-synergy]].

Evidence suggests the ECS may be involved in generating subtle but
therapeutic shifts in the ways people perceive the world, relate to
their internal landscape, think and feel about themselves, and interact
with each other. These shifts can be induced by endocannabinoids
(cannabinoids produced by the body) or by cannabinoids extracted from
plants or synthesized in a laboratory.

## Clinical Significance

A search in the U.S. National Library of Medicine using the single
keyword "cannabinoids" currently yields more than 15,000 studies. The
ECS is a bridge between the body and mind. If properly activated, it is
capable of suppressing numerous cancers and may be protective against
Alzheimer's disease. Worldwide, millions of patients rely on cannabis or
cannabinoid prescriptions to maintain health and well-being.

## Therapeutic Potential

Hundreds of medical and scientific organizations support the use of
medical marijuana including Kaiser Permanente, the California Medical
Association, and the American Nurses Association. Even the conservative
American Medical Association now supports research on cannabis for
medicinal use. This support reflects the level of frustration felt by a
large number of physicians who lack adequate and safe treatment options
for many chronic degenerative diseases.

One of cannabis's astounding capabilities is that it simultaneously
relaxes and stimulates the autonomic nervous system. Cannabinoids
induce these changes by enhancing and balancing individual cellular
function as well as that of the whole organism, enhancing both left-
brain and right-brain functioning.

## Summary

The endocannabinoid system is essential to life, health, and well-being.
Understanding the ECS is a critical task for more effectively managing
diseases, especially chronic, debilitating diseases for which there is
no orthodox cure. The body makes its own cannabinoids to regulate,
regain, or maintain a healthy body and mind, and the ECS is involved in
healing more than 100 chronic symptoms and stubborn diseases.
