# Shamanic Battles and Protective Powers in Amazonian Vegetalismo

In the tradition of Amazonian vegetalismo as revealed through the visions of Pablo Amaringo, shamanic practice is not merely herbal medicine but a vast battlefield of invisible forces. Sorcerers and healers wage war through magical darts, spirit attacks, and counter-spells, while a strict hierarchy of mastery determines a practitioner's capacity to defend, diagnose, and cure. The visions presented in Amaringo's *Ayahuasca Visions* (Visions 35, 36, 41, and 42) offer a detailed cartography of this occult ecology—mapping the weapons of sorcery, the techniques of extraction and incorporation healing, the diagnostic art of pulsation, and the graduated ranks of the vegetalista path.

## The Art of Magical Dart Extraction

Central to [[chullachaki-and-soul-recovery-in-amazonian-shamanism]], animal fats and plant-based attractants are potent magical substances that must be prepared and applied with strict protocol. Improper use invites spiritual backlash—the very forces the practitioner seeks to harness turn against them.

The dolphin, a creature associated with seduction and shape-shifting in Amazonian folklore, is particularly dangerous when its power is invoked carelessly. The youth's illness serves as a cautionary tale within the tradition: magical knowledge without discipline is not merely ineffective but actively harmful. The healer must then intervene to undo the damage caused by the patient's own reckless spiritual experimentation.

## Protective Plants and Their Powers

The Amaringo visions describe a pharmacopoeia of plants whose powers extend far beyond the biochemical into the electromagnetic and spiritual:

- **Pichana-machaco** (a type of snake-associated plant): Draws out insanity through a process described as electricity, suggesting that certain plants operate on the nervous system's energetic field, discharging pathological energy from the patient's body.
- **Punga tree** (*Bombax munguba*): Strengthens the healer's mariri, amplifying the protective resin that is the vegetalista's primary defensive tool. A stronger mariri means a stronger defense against sorcerous attack.
- **Bufro-avispa** (dolphin-wasp): Its venom brings fever, but healers learn its icaro and use the same power to cure sorcery—demonstrating the Amazonian principle that the poison and the cure are often two aspects of a single force.
- **Huiririma palm** (*Astrocaryum jauari*): Possesses an electromagnetic force so strong that electric eels eat its fruits and beat their bodies against its trunk, generating charge. This plant's bioelectric properties make it a powerful ally in energy-based healing and protection.
- **[[vegetalista-healing-techniques-and-plant-medicines]] described in Amaringo's work is *incorporation*—the healer literally entering the patient's body to combat illness from within. In Vision 36, a healer enters a patient whose eyes have been harmed by a sorcerer's magic lamp. Once inside, the healer operates on the afflicted organs directly, working in a dimension where physical and spiritual anatomy overlap.

The preparatory ritual involves giving the patient water over which an icaro has been sung and tobacco has been blown—a practice that consecrates the water and opens the patient's energy field to the healer's intervention. Tobacco smoke (*mapacho*) functions as a purifying medium and a conduit for the healer's intent, creating a bridge between the material and spiritual dimensions through which the healer can pass.

Incorporation represents the most intimate form of shamanic healing: the boundaries between healer and patient dissolve entirely, and the healer must navigate the interior landscape of the patient's body-spirit complex, identifying and neutralizing the sources of affliction. This technique is reserved for advanced practitioners and carries significant risk—if the healer is not sufficiently protected, they too may fall victim to the sorcery they are attempting to cure.

## The Anatomy of the Healer's Consciousness

Vision 36 provides a rare phenomenological map of the healer's own energetic anatomy during incorporation. Four rays of light emanate from the healer's head: the two lower rays represent consciousness (the waking, rational mind), while the two upper rays represent subconsciousness (the deep, visionary awareness accessed during trance). Small waves along the spinal cord represent the vibrations of the senses, and the brain itself is identified as the "astral nucleus"—the seat from which the healer projects consciousness into the patient's body.

This schema bridges Amazonian shamanic cosmology with a kind of occult neurology, suggesting that the healer's power depends on the integration of conscious and unconscious faculties, channeled through the body's energetic pathways. The four-ray system implies that full healing capacity requires the activation of both the known and the hidden dimensions of the mind, and that the spinal cord serves as the transmission line for vibrational energy generated in the healer's astral nucleus.

## Cosmic Helpers and Genies of Ancient Cultures

A striking feature of Vision 36 is the assembly of helpers who attend the healer during incorporation. These are described as genies of ancient cultures from around the world: Le Muel Kan, Lui Ce Fu, a Mayan priest, an African healer, a yoga master, a priest of Vishnu and Shiva, and the Incan Ayar—identified as the priest and prince of all witch doctors, healers, and sorcerers.

This pan-cultural council suggests that Amazonian vegetalismo conceives of its healing tradition as part of a universal wisdom, drawing on the accumulated spiritual knowledge of all civilizations. The healer does not work alone but is supported by an invisible parliament of ancestral masters, each contributing specialized knowledge from their own cultural and spiritual lineage. The presence of an Incan Ayar as the supreme authority among all shamanic practitioners anchors the tradition in its indigenous Andean-Amazonian roots while simultaneously embracing a global spiritual heritage.

Vision 42 extends this cosmological reach further: the Palace of Sublime Fakirs of Brahma, the mystical rose of wisdom, and giants from the star Antares who teach icaros for snake bites. The vegetalista's allies include extraterrestrial and interdimensional beings, placing Amazonian shamanism within a truly cosmic framework where the boundaries between Earth and the stars dissolve.

## The Three-Temple Initiation System

Vision 36 describes a graduated system of three temples through which a healer must pass:

1. **The First Temple**: Dedicated to initiation—the beginning of the healer's journey, where the foundations of plant knowledge, icaro repertoire, and spiritual discipline are laid. The initiate undergoes dietary restrictions and purification before being accepted.
2. **The Second Temple**: For graduation as masters—the healer has demonstrated sufficient mastery to operate independently, diagnose complex cases, and take on students of their own.
3. **The Third Temple**: For ordination as sublime masters—the highest rank, reserved for those who have achieved complete union with the plant spirits and cosmic forces. Only at this level does the healer gain access to the full council of spirit helpers and interdimensional travel.

Admiral Manuel Huaya is named as a master of plant secrets within this hierarchy, suggesting that the tradition preserves a lineage of named individuals who have attained the highest degrees and serve as living bridges between the human and spirit worlds.

## The Pulsation Diagnostic System

Vision 41 introduces Queen Pulsarium Coya, a spirit being who grants vegetalistas the power to diagnose illness by *pulsing* patients—reading the rhythmic energy signatures of the body. The pulsation reveals layers of color that correspond to the healer's rank and the nature of the illness:

- **White and pink layers**: Indicate an apprentice—a beginner who has received basic training but lacks deep mastery.
- **Blue layers**: Indicate a *muraya*—a fully qualified healer capable of independent practice.
- **Red and yellow layers**: Indicate a *banco*—a master who commands three realms: the atmospheric/sidereal, the terrestrial, and the aquatic.
- **Black and red layers**: Indicate an *evil banco*—a sorcerer who has turned their mastery toward harmful ends.

This diagnostic system allows a practitioner to immediately assess the spiritual rank and intent of another shaman, as well as to read the energetic state of a patient's illness. The pulsation technique is a non-invasive yet profoundly deep method of diagnosis, operating on a level that conventional medicine cannot access.

## The Hierarchy of Healers: From Apprentice to Sumiruna

The hierarchy of Amazonian vegetalistas, as revealed across these visions, can be summarized as follows:

- **Apprentice**: Beginning student, still learning plant preparation, icaros, and basic diagnosis. Associated with white and pink pulsation layers.
- **Muraya**: A full healer, competent in extraction, incorporation, and pulsation diagnosis. Associated with blue layers.
- **Banco**: A master of three realms (sky, earth, water), capable of the most advanced operations. Associated with red and yellow layers. Bancos may be benevolent or malevolent.
- **Sumiruna**: The sublime master, ordained in the third temple. Commands cosmic helpers, interdimensional travel, and the deepest plant knowledge.
