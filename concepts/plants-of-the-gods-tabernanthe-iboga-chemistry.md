# Tabernanthe iboga: Chemistry and Pharmacology

## Botanical Description

*Tabernanthe iboga* Baill. is a shrub 3-4.5 ft (1-1.5 m) tall, found
in the undergrowth of tropical forests of western Africa but often
cultivated in native dooryards. It belongs to the Apocynaceae
(Dogbane family). The shrub has copious white, vile-smelling latex.
The ovate leaves, usually 3.75-4 in. (9-10 cm) long, about 1.25 in.
(3 cm) wide (but occasionally up to 8.5 by 2.75 in. or 22 by 7 cm),
are yellowish green beneath. The tiny yellowish, pinkish, or white-
and pink-spotted flowers grow in groups of 5 to 12, with a
crateriform corolla with twisted lobes 0.4 in. (1 cm) long. The
ovoid, pointed yellow-orange fruits occur in pairs and become as
large as olives. One of the few members of the Apocynaceae utilized
as a hallucinogen, its yellowish root is the active part of the plant,
containing the psychoactive alkaloids.

## Admixture Plants

Other plants of reputed narcotic properties are involved in the Iboga
cults, sometimes used alone, sometimes as admixtures with *Tabernanthe
iboga* itself. [[plants-gods-iboga]] Apocynaceous Plants

Most varieties of related Apocynaceous plants contain ibogaine-like
alkaloids. *Voacanga* species of tropical Africa contain voacangine,
which is chemically related to ibogaine. *Tabernaemontana* species
(including *T. coffeoides* and *T. crassa*) have already revealed
psychoactive properties and uses. These plants expand the
pharmacological significance of the iboga alkaloid family.
