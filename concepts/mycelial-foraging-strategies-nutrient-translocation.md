## Mycelial Foraging Strategies and Nutrient Translocation

Basidiomycete mycelia develop complex networks that function as integrated
resource-distribution systems, foraging for and translocating nutrients
across considerable distances. These mycelial systems can extend from
metres to many hectares in the natural environment and display remarkable
plasticity in response to heterogeneous resource distribution. The
architecture of these networks is continuously reconfigured in response to
nutritional cues, environmental conditions, damage, and predation.

## The Mycelial Network as Foraging System

Basidiomycetes are the major agents of decomposition and [[dighton-fungi-acidifying-pollutants-forest-ecosystems]], occurring as both [[plant-nutrient-uptake-mechanisms]], storage,
and redistribution across the entire colony.

The distribution of resources in soil is extremely heterogeneous and
unpredictable in [[niche-in-space-and-time]]. Fungi have evolved species-specific
strategies to search for new resources and capitalize on resources landing
on their mycelial systems. These foraging strategies can be broadly
classified into patterns of search, capture, and redistribution that differ
among species.

## Foraging Strategies

Research on cord-forming saprotrophic basidiomycetes has identified several
distinct foraging strategies:

- **Phalanx strategy**: dense, slower-extending mycelium that exploits
  resources intensively within a limited area. This strategy is effective
  where resources are patchy but close together.
- **Guerrilla strategy**: sparse, rapidly extending mycelium that covers
  large areas but may miss small, isolated resources. This strategy is
  favoured when resources are widely dispersed.
- **Sit-and-wait strategy**: persistent networks that maintain connectivity
  across large areas, ready to capitalize on resources that arrive within
  the network's zone of influence. This is analogous to spider webs or
  ant lion pits.

Many species exhibit intermediate strategies that shift depending on
resource availability. When resources are scarce, mycelia often shift toward
more exploratory growth patterns.

## Nutrient Translocation Mechanisms

The precise mechanisms underlying nutrient translocation in fungi are not
yet fully known but are thought to include:

- **Mass flow**: bulk movement of cytoplasm through hyphae, driven by
  pressure gradients within the mycelial network.
- **Diffusion**: passive movement of solutes along concentration gradients.
- **Cytoplasmic streaming**: active movement of cytoplasm within hyphae.
- **Vesicular transport**: specific [[membrane-transport-of-nutrients-into-plant-cells]] in membrane-bound
  vesicles.
- **[[mycelial-nutrient-translocation-and-vacuolar-transport]]**: the dynamic pleiomorphic vacuolar system in
  [[gadd-hyphal-tip-growth-and-branching-mechanisms]] to a series of
larger, more spherical, adherent vacuoles interconnected with fine tubes in
mature hyphal regions. [[fluorescence-recovery-after-photobleaching]] photobleaching (FRAP)
studies have demonstrated that diffusion alone can explain observed
transport within vacuolar compartments.
