## Overview

Harvesting is the culmination of the cultivation cycle. Proper timing and technique directly affect yield, potency, quality, and shelf life. Mushrooms are living organisms that continue to metabolize after being picked — they lose potency and quality with every hour they remain undried. This page covers when to harvest, how to harvest, drying methods, and long-term storage.

## Harvest Timing

### The Veil as Indicator

The most reliable indicator of harvest readiness for P. cubensis is the partial veil — the thin membrane connecting the cap to the stem. The veil stretches as the mushroom grows and eventually tears.

- **Before veil tears**: Mushroom is still growing. Potency is increasing. Harvesting now yields smaller, more visually appealing mushrooms but lower total weight.
- **At veil tear**: The traditional harvest point. The mushroom has reached near-maximum size and potency. The cap margin is still slightly curved inward.
- **After veil tears**: The cap flattens and begins to turn upward, exposing the gills. The mushroom starts dropping spores. Potency plateaus or begins to decrease slightly as the mushroom redirects energy to spore production. Size continues to increase slightly.

### Recommended Harvest Window

For maximum total potency and quality, harvest when the veil has just torn or is actively tearing. For maximum visual appeal (dense, closed-cap mushrooms), harvest just before the veil tears. The practical difference in potency between these two points is minimal.

### Signs of Readiness

- The partial veil is tearing or has just torn.
- The cap has expanded to near its full size.
- Growth rate has slowed noticeably (the mushroom has essentially stopped getting larger).
- The stem base has thickened and the overall mushroom feels firm and dense.

### When to Harvest the Whole Flush

Mushrooms within a single flush do not all mature simultaneously. However, it is generally best to harvest all mushrooms within a 24-48 hour window:

- Leaving mature mushrooms (veil torn, dropping spores) on the substrate can trigger contamination.
- Spores dropped on the substrate surface create a dark, wet mess that promotes bacterial growth.
- Spore-covered substrates can have reduced subsequent flush yields.
- Harvest mature mushrooms first, then return for those still developing. Do not wait more than 24-48 hours between the first and last harvest of a flush.

## Harvesting Technique

### Twist and Pull (Recommended)

The standard harvesting method for [[monotub-cultivation]]:

1. Grasp the mushroom at the base of the stem, as close to the substrate as possible.
2. Gently twist the mushroom while pulling upward.
3. The mushroom should detach cleanly from the substrate, pulling up with minimal substrate attached.
4. If a small piece of substrate comes up with the mushroom, brush it off gently.

**Advantages**:
- Leaves no stump or organic material behind to rot.
- Reduces contamination risk at the harvest site.
- Clean detachment promotes healthy subsequent flushes.

**Why not cut?** Cutting mushrooms at the stem leaves a stump of dead tissue in the substrate. This stump can become a site for bacterial or fungal contamination. While some growers report success with cutting, twisting is the safer and more widely recommended approach.

### Exceptions to Twisting

- **Clusters**: When mushrooms grow in tight clusters, twisting one can disturb its neighbors. In this case, gently twist the entire cluster as a unit, or cut individual mushrooms from the cluster with a sterile scalpel.
- **Side pins**: Mushrooms growing against the tub wall may be difficult to twist. Gently pry them away from the wall, then twist.
- **Large mushrooms**: Very large or dense mushrooms may break at the stem if twisted aggressively. Support the base with your other hand while twisting.

### Handling After Harvest

- Handle mushrooms gently. Bruising (blue discoloration) occurs with physical contact. Bruising does not significantly affect potency but indicates handling damage.
- Place harvested mushrooms on a clean surface (paper towel, plate, or drying rack).
- Do not wash mushrooms before drying. Water on the surface slows drying and can promote rot.
- Trim any substrate debris from the base with clean scissors.

## Aborts

Aborts are small mushrooms that have stopped growing and begun to die. They are identifiable by:
- No growth over 24-48 hours while surrounding mushrooms continue to develop.
- Caps may darken or develop a bluish tint.
- Stems may appear shriveled or thin compared to healthy mushrooms.

**Harvest aborts along with the flush.** Aborts are fully potent — often proportionally more potent by weight than mature mushrooms because [[psilocybin]]/psilocin concentration peaks before full maturity. Leaving aborts on the substrate creates contamination risk.

## Drying

Drying is critical and must begin as soon as possible after harvest. Fresh mushrooms are 90-92% water and will rot within days at room temperature if not dried.

### Target Moisture Content

Mushrooms must be dried to "cracker dry" — so dry that they snap cleanly when bent, with no flexibility or spongy texture. The target moisture content is below 10%, ideally 5-8%. At this moisture level, psilocybin is chemically stable for long-term storage.

### Dehydrator Method (Recommended)

A food dehydrator is the fastest and most reliable drying method:

1. Arrange mushrooms on dehydrator trays without overlapping.
2. Set temperature to 95-115F (35-46C). Some cultivators use higher temperatures (up to 160F) with no significant potency loss, but lower temperatures are more conservative.
3. Run for 8-24 hours depending on mushroom size and quantity.
4. Check for cracker-dry consistency. Bend a stem — it should snap cleanly.
5. If not fully dry, continue until cracker dry. Partially dried mushrooms will rot in storage.

**Dehydrator recommendations**:
- Nesco or Excalibur models are popular.
- Avoid dehydrators without temperature control.
- A fan in the dehydrator significantly speeds drying.

### Desiccant Chamber (Low-Tech Method)

For cultivators without a dehydrator:

1. Place mushrooms on a wire rack over a bed of desiccant (silica gel, DampRid, or indicating silica gel).
2. Seal in an airtight container (Tupperware, gallon jar).
3. Replace desiccant when it changes color (for indicating gel) or becomes saturated.
4. This method takes 3-7 days and may not achieve cracker-dry consistency in humid environments.
5. Use as a supplementary step after fan-drying, not as the sole drying method.

### Fan-Drying First

For any drying method, fan-drying for 24-48 hours before using a dehydrator or desiccant chamber speeds the process significantly:

1. Place mushrooms on a wire rack or paper towel.
2. Position a fan to blow air over them (not directly on high — use low or medium speed).
3. After 24-48 hours, the mushrooms will have lost 60-80% of their water content and will feel leathery but not cracker dry.
4. Transfer to a dehydrator or desiccant chamber for final drying.

## Storage

### Short-Term Storage (Weeks to Months)

- Place cracker-dry mushrooms in a glass jar with a desiccant packet.
- Seal tightly. Store in a cool, dark place (pantry, closet).
- Check periodically for moisture. If the desiccant changes color, replace it and re-dry the mushrooms.

### Long-Term Storage (Months to Years)

- **Vacuum sealing**: Vacuum-seal cracker-dry mushrooms with a desiccant packet. This removes oxygen, which slows oxidative degradation of psilocybin.
- **Freezing**: Vacuum-sealed mushrooms can be frozen for maximum longevity. Ensure they are cracker dry before freezing — any residual moisture will cause freezer burn and tissue damage.
- **Mason jars with oxygen absorbers**: Place cracker-dry mushrooms in a mason jar with a food-grade oxygen absorber packet (200cc for a quart jar). Seal and store in a cool, dark place.

### Potency Degradation

Psilocybin degrades through oxidation, exposure to UV light, and heat:

- **Oxygen**: The primary degradation pathway. Minimize air exposure.
- **Light**: UV light accelerates degradation. Store in opaque containers or dark locations.
- **Heat**: Elevated temperatures accelerate chemical degradation. Store below 70F (21C).
- **Moisture**: Residual moisture promotes enzymatic degradation. Ensure cracker-dry before storage.
