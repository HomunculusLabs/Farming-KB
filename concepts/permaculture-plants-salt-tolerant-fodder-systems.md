The *Permaculture Plants* reference includes an extensive section on salt-tolerant
fodder species — plants capable of providing livestock forage on saline,
waterlogged, and otherwise degraded land. This approach transforms unproductive
salt-affected areas into productive pastoral systems while simultaneously
rehabilitating the soil.

## The Salt-Affected Land Problem

Salinization occurs when water tables rise, bringing dissolved salts to the
surface. This is accelerated by clearing deep-rooted native vegetation and
replacing it with shallow-rooted crops and pastures. Affected land loses
productivity, soil structure degrades, and erosion increases.

The permaculture response treats this not as a lost cause but as a design
opportunity: salt-tolerant plants can provide forage, lower water tables
through transpiration, reduce soil evaporation through shading, and stabilize
soil against erosion.

## Key Design Principles

**Mound planting**: On saline, waterlogged land, plants should be established
on mounds 200–300 mm above normal ground level. Mounds can be created with a
tractor grader blade and should follow contours where possible.

**Stock exclusion during establishment**: Salt-affected land should be fenced
from livestock until fodder species are well established, then stock can be
periodically introduced.

**Cover maintenance**: Maintaining vegetative cover on salt-affected land
provides three critical benefits: reduced evaporation (shading and wind
protection), reduced soil erosion, and increased transpiration that lowers
the water table.

## Salt-Tolerant Grasses

### Puccinellia (*Puccinellia ciliata*, *P. capillaris*)
A perennial, summer-dormant grass forming tussocks 10–70 cm high. Nutritionally
equivalent to annual rye grass. Suited to areas with 350 mm or more rainfall
and tolerant of winter waterlogging. Mature tussocks provide valuable summer
feed. This is one of the most reliable salt pasture grasses.

### Tall Wheat Grass (*Agropyron elongatum*)
Native to southern Russian and Asian minor seashore marshes. Well adapted to
poorly drained saline soils with best growth where soil dries in summer.
Direct sown from seed and can be grazed once established. Also suited to
marginally saline, well-drained soils in rainfalls above 350 mm.

### Smilo (*Oryzopsis miliacea*)
A perennial clumped grass to about 1 m from the Mediterranean. Tolerates
shallow and calcareous soils and can grow in rainfalls as low as 100–150 mm.
Extremely competitive once established. Yields may reach 12 tonnes dry matter
per hectare per year (protein 12–15%). Known to persist in sown pastures
for at least 15 years. No summer dormancy if soil moisture is available.

### Tall Fescue (*Festuca elatior* subsp. *arundinacea*)
Extremely resistant to water stress. In the Sahel under saline irrigation
(4000–5000 ppm salts), yields of 8–10 tonnes dry matter per hectare are
still achievable. Can withstand high salinity levels. Warning: may cause
lameness (fescue foot) and death in cattle if fed in large quantities for
extended periods.

### Other Notable Grasses
- **Canary grass** (*Phalaris truncata*) — withstands 350–1000 mm rainfall
- **Beardgrass** (*Poty pogon monspeliensis*) — tolerant above 450–500 mm
- **Rye grass** (*Lolium rigidum*) — 100–1200 mm rainfall range
- **Salt water couch** (*Paspalum distichum*) — tolerates water with up to
  14,000 ppm total soluble salts; valuable winter feed in summer months

## Companion Legumes

Nitrogen-fixing legumes are essential companions to grass pastures:

- **Strawberry clover** (*Trifolium fragiferum*) — creeping perennial for
  heavy, poorly drained saline soils; very salt tolerant
- **Fleshy sulla** (*Hedysarum carnosum*) — biennial from North Africa,
  tolerates irrigation water with 8000–10,000 ppm salt
- **Sulla** (*Hedysarum coronarium*) — biennial requiring deep calcareous
  soils; yields up to 20 tonnes dry matter/ha/year recorded

## Saltbushes (*Atriplex* spp)

Saltbushes are perhaps the most important group for saline land. They
concentrate salt onto their leaf surfaces and could potentially be used for
removing salt from soil. Yields of over 10 tonnes/ha/year are possible:

- **Old man saltbush** (*A. nummularia*) — shrub to 3 m; crude protein
  17.0–21.9%, digestibility 68–74%; found in arid regions of all mainland
  Australian states
- **Bladder saltbush** (*A. versicaria*) — to 60–70 cm; crude protein
  11.1–18.4%, digestibility 58%
- **Wavy leaf saltbush** (*A. undulata*) — spreading Argentine native
  providing 900 sheep grazing days per hectare at 350 mm rainfall
- **Four wing saltbush** (*A. canescens*) — North American native growing
  1–3 m on dunes; 18–20% protein with low toxicity even at high salinity

## Bluebushes (*Maireana* spp)

Australian native companions to saltbushes, generally preferring calcareous
soils:

- **Small-leafed bluebush** (*M. brevifolia*) — to 0.9 m; crude protein
  15–26%
- **Pearl bluebush** (*M. sedifolia*) — major shrub of the Nullarbor Plain;
  17.2% crude protein
- **Black bluebush** (*M. pyramidata*) — to nearly 2 m; crude protein
  15.1–22.0%

## Salt-Tolerant Trees

### Paperbarks (*Melaleuca* spp)
Many melaleucas are valuable for honey, essential oils, and saline land
rehabilitation. Key species include prickly paperbark (*M. stypheloides*,
6–18 m, tolerant of saline and wet soils), white cloud tree (*M. bracteata*,
to 7 m), and broom honey myrtle (*M. uncinata*).

### Casuarina and Acacia Species
Numerous casuarinas and wattles tolerate saline conditions. *Casuarina obesa*
and *C. glauca* (swamp sheoak) are particularly noted for saline sites.
Many [[permaculture-zone-design]] as
Zone 4 or Zone 5 elements — broader-scale land management where minimal
intervention yields gradual rehabilitation. They can be combined with
salt-tolerant windbreaks (Casuarina, Melaleuca) and integrated with
managed grazing rotations that allow recovery periods.
