## Cannabis Therapeutic Condition Guide

Detailed cannabinoid, terpene, delivery method, evidence, and dosing recommendations for specific conditions. Based on Blesching's Cannabis Health Index. For ECS overview, cannabinoid/terpene profiles, delivery method comparison, and dosing principles, see [[cannabis-health-therapeutics]].

## Chronic Pain

- **Best cannabinoids**: THC (analgesic, reduces nociception), CBD (anti-inflammatory, modulates THC psychoactivity), THCA (anti-inflammatory), CBG (analgesic)
- **Best terpenes**: Myrcene (muscle relaxant, analgesic), caryophyllene (anti-inflammatory, CB2 agonist), pinene (analgesic)
- **Delivery methods**: Vaporized flower (fast onset, dose control), sublingual tinctures (sustained), transdermal patches (localized, steady-state), edibles (long-duration, 4-8 hr)
- **Evidence**: Strong. Multiple systematic reviews support cannabis for chronic neuropathic pain. Moderate evidence for musculoskeletal pain. Weak for acute pain.
- **Dosing**: Start low, go slow. 2.5-5 mg THC initial dose. Titrate up by 2.5 mg every 2-3 days. CBD:THC ratio of 1:1 to 2:1 often effective for pain with minimal intoxication. Chronic users may require 20-40 mg THC/day divided doses.
- **Notes**: Opioid-sparing effect well documented. Cannabis may reduce opioid requirements by 30-50% in chronic pain patients.

## Inflammation

- **Best cannabinoids**: CBD (potent anti-inflammatory), THCA (raw, anti-inflammatory), CBG (anti-inflammatory), CBC
- **Best terpenes**: Caryophyllene (direct CB2 binding, strongest anti-inflammatory terpene), myrcene, limonene, pinene
- **Delivery methods**: Sublingual CBD oil, topical preparations for localized inflammation, raw juiced cannabis (THCA/CBDA rich), oral capsules for systemic inflammation
- **Evidence**: Moderate to strong for inflammatory conditions. CBD inhibits NF-kB and COX-2 pathways. Caryophyllene acts as selective CB2 agonist.
- **Dosing**: CBD: 15-50 mg/day for systemic inflammation. Higher doses (up to 1500 mg/day) studied in clinical trials. THCA: 10-30 mg/day via juice or raw preparation.
- **Conditions**: Rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease/IBD, multiple sclerosis, neuropathic inflammation, autoimmune conditions

## PTSD and Anxiety

- **Best cannabinoids**: CBD (anxiolytic without intoxication), THC (low doses only -- high doses can worsen anxiety), CBDA (5-HT1A agonist, fast-acting anxiolytic)
- **Best terpenes**: Linalool (anxiolytic, similar to lavender), limonene (mood elevation), myrcene (sedative, calming), terpinolene
- **Delivery methods**: Sublingual tinctures (dose control crucial), CBD-dominant vaporizable flower, oral CBD capsules, CBDA tinctures for acute episodes
- **Evidence**: Moderate. CBD shows promise for social anxiety, generalized anxiety, and PTSD nightmare reduction. THC evidence mixed -- dose-dependent bidirectional effects.
- **Dosing**: CBD: 25-300 mg for acute anxiety events. Chronic: 15-50 mg/day. THC: microdose only (1-2.5 mg) for PTSD -- higher doses counterproductive. 1:1 or higher CBD:THC ratios preferred.
- **Critical notes**: High-THC can trigger panic/anxiety, especially in PTSD patients. Start CBD-only, add THC cautiously. Avoid sativa-dominant strains if anxiety is primary symptom.

## Sleep

- **Best cannabinoids**: THC (sedative at moderate doses), CBN (sedative -- "sleepy cannabinoid"), CBD (anxiolytic, may improve sleep quality indirectly via anxiety reduction)
- **Best terpenes**: Myrcene (sedative, muscle relaxant), linalool (sedative), terpinolene (sedative), nerolidol (sedative)
- **Delivery methods**: Indica-dominant flower vaporized 30-60 min before bed, sublingual tincture (faster than edible), oral edible (longer duration but delayed onset -- take 2 hr before bed), CBN+CBD sublingual oil
- **Evidence**: Moderate. Cannabis improves sleep onset and duration in short term. Long-term tolerance to sedative effects develops. CBN sedative effects primarily from myrcene presence.
- **Dosing**: THC: 5-15 mg before bed. CBN: 5-20 mg combined with CBD 10-25 mg. Tolerance builds -- take breaks. "Strain hop" to prevent tolerance.
- **Notes**: THC suppresses REM sleep (reduces dreaming). May be beneficial for nightmare disorders but undesirable otherwise.

## Epilepsy

- **Best cannabinoids**: CBD (primary -- FDA-approved as Epidiolex for Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes), THCA (anticonvulsant), CBDV (anticonvulsant, under investigation)
- **Best terpenes**: Linalool (anticonvulsant), caryophyllene, pinene
- **Delivery methods**: Pharmaceutical CBD (Epidiolex): 100 mg/mL oral solution. Full-spectrum CBD oil sublingual. High-CBD strain extracts (Charlotte's Web type).
- **Evidence**: Strong for specific syndromes. Epidiolex FDA-approved 2018. 25-50% reduction in seizure frequency in Dravet syndrome.
- **Dosing**: Epidiolex: 5-20 mg/kg/day divided BID, titrated over 2 weeks. Full-spectrum CBD oil: start 5-10 mg/kg/day. Therapeutic window is narrow -- requires medical supervision.
- **Critical notes**: Drug interactions significant -- CBD inhibits CYP3A4 and CYP2C19. Liver enzyme monitoring required. High-CBD, low-THC preparations essential for pediatric use.

## Cancer (Supportive Care)

- **Best cannabinoids**: THC (antiemetic, appetite stimulant, analgesic), CBD (anti-nausea, anti-inflammatory, potential direct anti-tumor), THCA (anti-nausea, anti-inflammatory)
- **Best terpenes**: Limonene (anti-tumor, mood elevation), caryophyllene (anti-inflammatory), pinene (bronchodilator), myrcene
- **Delivery methods**: Dronabinol (synthetic THC, FDA-approved for chemo nausea). Nabilone (synthetic THC analog). Vaporized cannabis for breakthrough nausea. Edibles for sustained appetite stimulation.
- **Evidence**: Strong for antiemesis. Strong for appetite stimulation. Moderate for cancer pain. Preclinical evidence for direct anti-tumor effects -- NOT clinical standard of care.
- **Dosing**: Antiemetic: 5-10 mg THC every 2-4 hr as needed. Appetite: 2.5-5 mg THC before meals. Pain: 5-20 mg THC + 10-25 mg CBD divided throughout day.
- **Critical notes**: Cannabis does NOT replace conventional cancer treatment. Use as complementary therapy only.

## Nausea and Appetite

- **Best cannabinoids**: THC (gold standard for chemo-induced nausea), THCA (anti-nausea without intoxication -- raw), CBDA (potent anti-nausea via 5-HT1A), CBD
- **Best terpenes**: Limonene (anti-nausea, gastric acid regulation), myrcene (digestive)
- **Delivery methods**: Sublingual tincture (absorbed even if vomiting prevents oral intake), vaporized (fastest onset), edibles (sustained appetite), raw cannabis juice (THCA/CBDA)
- **Evidence**: Very strong for chemo-induced nausea (5-HT3 antagonist pathway). Strong for HIV/AIDS wasting syndrome.
- **Dosing**: Nausea: 2.5-5 mg THC + 5-10 mg CBDA sublingual, repeat q2-4h. Appetite: 2.5-5 mg THC 30 min before meals. THCA: 10-20 mg via juice.
- **Notes**: Raw cannabis juice contains THCA and CBDA (non-psychoactive) -- anti-nausea effects without intoxication.

## Multiple Sclerosis and Spasticity

- **Best cannabinoids**: THC (muscle relaxant, anti-spasticity), CBD (anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective), THCA (anti-spasticity)
- **Best terpenes**: Myrcene (muscle relaxant), linalool (antispasmodic), caryophyllene (anti-inflammatory)
- **Delivery methods**: Sativex (nabiximols, 1:1 THC:CBD oral spray). Oral THC (dronabinol). Vaporized indica-dominant strains. Topical THC/CBD balms for localized spasticity.
- **Evidence**: Strong for spasticity reduction. Sativex clinical trials show significant improvement in spasticity scores, pain, and sleep quality.
- **Dosing**: Sativex: start 1 spray/day, titrate to effect (max 12 sprays/day). THC: 2.5-10 mg divided doses. 1:1 THC:CBD ratio generally optimal.
- **Notes**: Benefits often plateau -- patients may not need to continuously escalate.
