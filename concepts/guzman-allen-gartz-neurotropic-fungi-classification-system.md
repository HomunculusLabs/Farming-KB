# Neurotropic Fungi Classification System of Guzmán, Allen and Gartz

## Overview

Gastón Guzmán, John W. Allen, and Jochen Gartz published a landmark comprehensive treatment in 2000 (based on 1998 data) cataloguing 216 species of neurotropic (hallucinogenic, psychotropic, or entheogenic) fungi from around the world. Their system divides these fungi into four major groups based on their active chemical constituents and ethnobotanical significance, providing the most authoritative taxonomic framework for understanding the global diversity of psychoactive mushrooms.

## The Four Classification Groups

### Group 1: Psilocybin and Related Indole Fungi

This is by far the largest group, containing the majority of known neurotropic species. These fungi contain psilocybin, psilocin, baeocystin, and norbaeocystin. The key genera and their species counts are:

- **Psilocybe**: 116 species, representing the overwhelming majority
- **Gymnopilus**: 14 species
- **Panaeolus**: 13 species
- **Copelandia**: 12 species
- **Hypholoma**: 6 species
- **Pluteus**: 6 species
- **Inocybe**: 6 species
- **Conocybe**: 4 species
- **Panaeolina**: 4 species
- **Gerronema**: 2 species
- **Agrocybe**: 1 species
- **Galerina**: 1 species
- **Mycena**: 1 species

The bluing reaction serves as a primary field indicator for identifying neurotropic Psilocybe species. Species belonging to the sections Aztecorum, Brunneocystidiatae, Cordisporae, Cubensies, Mexicanae, Semilanceatae, Stuntzae, Subaeruginosae, and Zapotecorum all exhibit this characteristic blue bruising.

### Group 2: Ibotenic Acid Containing Fungi

This smaller but culturally significant group contains species with ibotenic acid and muscimol as their primary active compounds. It includes only three species:

- **[[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]] primarily with Pinus and Betula in northern hemisphere forests. Their use as sacred fungi is well documented among Siberian peoples including the Chukchee and Koryak.

### Group 3: Ergot Fungi (Clavicipitales)

This group contains the well-known ergot alkaloid-producing fungi, encompassing both Ascomycotina genera:

**Claviceps species** (5 species):
- [[claviceps-paspali]] (= C. rolfesii)
- Claviceps tripsicii

**Cordyceps species** (2 species):
- Cordyceps capitata, found growing on Elaphomyces (deer truffle) hosts
- Cordyceps ophioglossoides, also an Elaphomyces parasite

The Cordyceps species hold particular [[dictyophora-indusiata]] (stinkhorn or veiled lady mushroom), used by the Chinantec Indians of Oaxaca for divination purposes. It exists in three recognized forms.

## Species Excluded from the Classification

Guzmán and colleagues carefully excluded several species that had been erroneously reported as neurotropic:

- **Psilocybe atrobrunnea** and **P. coprophila**: not true neurotropic species
- **Psilocybe pseudobullacea**: a non-bluing species with no confirmed neurotropic properties
- **Psathyrella sepulchralis**: was wrongly confused with P. zapotecorum
