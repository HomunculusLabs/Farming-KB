# Spore Formation and Geotropic Response in P. cubensis

## Overview

Badham's 1982 study revealed a critical linkage between spore formation
and [[negative-geotropism-in-mushroom-fruiting]] [[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]. The transition from
wind-directed growth to gravity-directed upward curvature occurs at the
onset of sporulation and is completely absent in sporeless mutants. This
establishes [[spore-production-geotropic-sensitivity-coupling-basidiomycete-badham-1982]] as a regulatory switch for tropic behavior.

## The Spore-Geotropism Connection

Key experimental evidence linking spore formation to geotropic response:

- **Fertile basidiocarps** at Stage 2 exhibit negative geotropism — pileus
  recurves upward with lamellae facing down, regardless of light direction.
- **Sterile (sporeless) mutants** continue growing into the wind throughout
  development without Phase 2 geotropic recurvature.
- **Darkness-grown cultures** produce few spores and show minimal geotropic
  response — light enables geotropism indirectly through spore production.
- **Early-stage cultures** (Stage 1, before spore production) show no
  geotropic bending even under light.

This pattern suggests a substance produced during spore formation in the
gills acts as the signal for the tropic transition.

## Sterile Mutant Analysis

Badham studied a sterile mutant from the same isolate as fertile cultures.
The mutant provided crucial evidence through double dissociation:

- **No spores** produced under any conditions tested.
- **No functional lamellae** — gill tissue absent or severely reduced.
- **Phase 1 intact** — stipe still grew into the wind, confirming Phase 1
  mechanism is independent of reproductive status.
- **Phase 2 absent** — no upward recurvature at any stage.

This dissociation demonstrates that Phase 1 (anemotropism) uses physical
[[evaporative-demand-gradient-stipe-elongation-mechanism-badham-1982]] gradients, while Phase 2 (geotropism) requires a
biological signal from reproductive structures.

## Timing of the Tropic Transition

The transition occurs precisely at Stage 2, coinciding with spore onset:

| Stage | Pileus | Spore status | Tropic behavior |
|-------|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Slight swelling | None | Anemotropism into wind |
| 2 | Spherical | Initiation | Transition to geotropism |
| 3 | Hemispherical | Active sporulation | Full geotropic response |
| 4 | Plane | Maximum dispersal | Maintained upward |

The transition is relatively abrupt, suggesting threshold concentration
of a gill-derived signal must be reached before Phase 2 activates.

## Light as Indirect Factor

Light's role in geotropic expression is almost certainly indirect:

- **Darkness suppresses spores:** Few spores form, no geotropic response.
- **Light direction irrelevant:** Light from below still produces upward
  recurvature — ruling out direct phototropism as the cause.
- **Photoperiod independence:** Identical results in 12h and constant
  light, as long as spore production is enabled.
- **Threshold model:** Light enables spore production, which generates
  the tropic signal. Below a minimum light threshold, insufficient
  spores form to trigger the geotropic switch.

## Cross-Species Comparisons

The spore-geotropism link appears across multiple basidiomycete genera:

- **[[agaricus-bisporus]]:** Gruen (1963) showed decapitation eliminated
  geotropic responses; gill agar diffusates partially restored them.
- **Coprinus sterquilinus:** Jeffereys and Greulach (1956) found gill
  maturation correlated with tropic sensitivity changes.
- **Polyporus brumalis:** Plunkett (1961) documented tropism shifts
  during [[mushroom-stipe-development-stages]] correlated with pileus differentiation,
  analogous to Badham's Stage 2 transition.
