# Psilocybin Mushrooms

Psilocybin mushrooms, commonly known as "magic mushrooms," are a polyphyletic
group of fungi containing the psychoactive compounds psilocybin and psilocin.
Over 200 species across multiple genera — primarily *Psilocybe*, but also
*Panaeolus*, *Gymnopilus*, *Copelandia*, *Inocybe*, and *Pluteus* — are known
to produce these alkaloids. [[terence-mckenna]] devoted significant attention to
psilocybin mushrooms in *Food of the Gods*, arguing they were central to human
cognitive evolution and the development of religion.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

### Psilocybin and Psilocin

Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is a prodrug that is
rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase in the body to form psilocin
(4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine), the pharmacologically active compound.
Both are structural analogs of serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT) and act
primarily as agonists at the 5-HT2A receptor, though they also bind to other
serotonin receptor subtypes including 5-HT1A, 5-HT2B, and 5-HT2C.

The structural similarity to DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is notable: psilocin
is simply the 4-hydroxy analog of DMT. This places psilocybin within the
tryptamine class of psychedelics alongside DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, bufotenin, and
ergoline derivatives.

### Dose and Effects

Effects are highly dose-dependent and typically last 4-6 hours. Common dose
ranges include:

- **Sub-perceptual (<0.5g dried)**: Mild mood enhancement, subtle changes in
  perception. This range is the basis of modern "microdosing" practices.
- **Low (0.5-1.5g)**: Enhanced color perception, mild visual distortions,
  emotional openness, increased sociability.
- **Moderate (1.5-3.5g)**: Significant visual hallucinations, altered sense of
  time and space, synesthesia, introspective insights, mystical-type
  experiences.
- **High (3.5-5g+)**: Complete ego dissolution, profound mystical experiences,
  encounters with apparently autonomous entities, intense emotional catharsis,
  experiences of unity or interconnectedness.

### Tolerance and Safety

Psilocybin produces rapid tolerance, with significant attenuation of effects
after consecutive days of use. Cross-tolerance exists with other serotonergic
psychedelics (LSD, DMT, mescaline) but not with non-serotonergic substances.
Tolerance typically resolves after 5-7 days of abstinence.

Psilocybin has a very high therapeutic index. The lethal dose in humans has
never been established and is estimated to be hundreds of times the active
dose. Physiological effects are generally mild: mild increases in heart rate
and blood pressure, pupil dilation, and occasional nausea. The primary risks
are psychological: anxiety, panic reactions, and the triggering of latent
psychiatric conditions in vulnerable individuals.

## Taxonomy and Distribution

The genus *Psilocybe* was first described by Paul Kummer in 1871, though the
psychoactive properties of its members were not scientifically established
until [[albert-hofmann]] isolated psilocybin from *Psilocybe mexicana* in 1958,
following the identification of psychoactive mushroom use by R. Gordon Wasson
among the Mazatec people of Mexico.

### Major Groups

**Section *Psilocybe***: The largest group of psychoactive species, found
worldwide in tropical and subtropical regions. Key species include:
- *P. cubensis*: The most commonly cultivated species, native to subtropical
  regions of the Americas and widely distributed through cattle ranching.
- *P. mexicana*: The species from which Hofmann first isolated psilocybin.
- *P. semilanceata* ("liberty cap"): Found in temperate grasslands of Europe
  and North America, one of the most potent species.
- *P. cyanescens*: Known as "wavy caps," found in wood chip mulch in temperate
  coastal regions; notably potent.
- *P. azurescens*: Among the most potent known species, native to the Pacific
  Northwest coast of North America.

**Section *Cyanescens***: Wood-inhabiting species, often found in mulched
landscapes and disturbed habitats. These include some of the most potent
species known.

### Global Distribution

Psychoactive *Psilocybe* species are found on every inhabited continent. Their
distribution has been significantly expanded by human activity, particularly
the introduction of cattle and other ungulates to new regions, which created
new substrates for coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species like *P. cubensis*.
McKenna noted this ecological relationship with interest, as it connects to
the Stoned Ape Hypothesis — the natural association of these mushrooms with
herbivore dung on the African savanna.

## Ethnobotanical History

The use of psilocybin mushrooms in indigenous Mesoamerican cultures predates
the Spanish conquest by millennia. Stone carvings from Guatemala ("mushroom
stones") dating to approximately 1000 BCE depict stylized *Psilocybe* mushrooms
and suggest ritual use. The Aztec term *teonanácatl* ("flesh of the gods")
attests to the sacred status of these mushrooms in pre-Columbian Mexico.

Spanish colonial authorities vigorously suppressed mushroom use, but the
traditions survived among indigenous communities, particularly the Mazatec,
Mixtec, Nahuatl, and Zapotec peoples of Oaxaca. Wasson's participation in a
velada (healing ceremony) with the Mazatec curandera María Sabina in 1955
brought these practices to global attention.

## Modern Research

Since the early 2000s, clinical research into psilocybin has experienced a
significant renaissance. Key findings include:

- **Treatment-resistant depression**: Studies at Imperial College London and
  Johns Hopkins have demonstrated rapid and sustained reductions in depressive
  symptoms following a single or small number of psilocybin sessions.
- **End-of-life anxiety**: Trials with terminal cancer patients have shown
  significant reductions in death-related anxiety and depression.
- **Addiction**: Preliminary studies suggest efficacy in treating alcohol and
  tobacco dependence.
- **Cluster headaches**: Some evidence of therapeutic benefit, though research
  is limited.

The FDA granted Breakthrough Therapy designation to psilocybin for treatment-
resistant depression in 2018, accelerating clinical development.

## Legal Status

Psilocybin is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance under the UN
Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971) and is illegal in most countries.
However, decriminalization and legalization efforts have advanced significantly
in the 2020s, with Oregon legalizing psilocybin therapy in 2020 and several
other jurisdictions deprioritizing enforcement.
