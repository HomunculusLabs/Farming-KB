# Psilocybin Mushroom Cultivation

Psilocybin mushroom cultivation refers to the process of growing fungi that
contain the psychoactive compounds psilocybin and psilocin. The most commonly
cultivated species belong to the genus *Psilocybe*, particularly *P. cubensis*,
which is favored for its relatively fast growth cycle and forgiving cultivation
requirements.

## Legal Status

Psilocybin mushrooms remain scheduled or prohibited in most jurisdictions
worldwide. However, a growing number of regions have decriminalized possession
or legalized therapeutic use. The legal landscape shifts rapidly; cultivators
must research local regulations thoroughly. This page covers the biology and
techniques for educational purposes.

## Species Overview

Several *Psilocybe* species are cultivated by mycological enthusiasts:

- **P. cubensis**: The most widely cultivated species. Colonizes quickly,
  fruits readily, and tolerates a range of environmental conditions. Strains
  like Golden Teacher, B+, and Ecuador are popular.
- **P. cyanescens**: Known as "wavy caps." More potent but harder to cultivate
  indoors. Prefers outdoor wood chip beds in temperate climates.
- **P. semilanceata**: Liberty caps. Rarely cultivated successfully indoors
  due to specific habitat requirements; typically foraged.
- **P. azurescens**: Among the most potent species. Extremely difficult to
  cultivate; found naturally in the Pacific Northwest of North America.
- **P. mexicana**: Produces sclerotia (truffles) in addition to fruiting
  bodies. Popular in jurisdictions where sclerotia occupy a legal gray area.

## Cultivation Methods

### PF Tek (Brown Rice Flour)

The "Psilocybe Fanaticus" technique is the classic beginner method. Brown rice
flour and vermiculite are mixed, loaded into half-pint mason jars, sterilized
in a pressure cooker, inoculated with spore syringes, and allowed to colonize.
Once fully colonized, the cakes are birthed into a fruiting chamber with high
humidity and fresh air exchange.

### Monotub Bulk Cultivation

A step up in scale, monotub cultivation uses grain spawn (typically rye, oats,
or millet) inoculated with a liquid culture or spores. Once colonized, the
grain is mixed with a bulk substrate (coconut coir and vermiculite, often with
gypsum) inside a modified plastic storage tub. Monotubs produce significantly
higher yields than PF Tek.

### Spawn Bags

Polypropylene filter patch bags filled with grain substrate can be inoculated
and sealed, providing a self-contained colonization environment. These are
useful for producing large volumes of spawn efficiently.

## Substrate Preparation

Grain substrates must be hydrated to the correct moisture content — roughly
50-60% by weight — then sterilized. Common grains include:

- **Rye berries**: Excellent water retention, widely available.
- **Whole oats**: Cheap, nutritious, easy to prepare.
- **Millet**: Small grain with many inoculation points per volume.
- **Brown rice**: Used in PF Tek; less common for grain spawn.

Bulk substrates provide the nutritional base for fruiting. Coconut coir is the
most popular base due to its resistance to contamination. It is combined with
vermiculite for water retention and gypsum for calcium and pH buffering.

## Sterile Technique

Contamination is the primary challenge in mushroom cultivation. Mold spores
and bacteria are ubiquitous. Cultivators use still air boxes (a simple
container with arm holes), laminar flow hoods, flame-sterilized tools, and
alcohol wiping to maintain sterility during inoculation and transfer steps.
Wearing gloves and a mask, and working quickly, significantly reduces
contamination rates.

## Fruiting Conditions

Once the substrate is fully colonized, fruiting is triggered by:

1. **Fresh air exchange**: Introducing ambient air lowers CO2 levels, signaling
   the mycelium to form primordia.
2. **High humidity**: 90-95% relative humidity prevents cakes from drying out.
3. **Light**: Indirect ambient light (12 hours on/off) provides direction cues.
4. **Temperature drop**: A slight reduction (5-10°F) from colonization
   temperature can stimulate pinning.

## Harvesting and Drying

Mushrooms are harvested just before or as the veil beneath the cap tears.
Harvesting at this stage maximizes potency relative to weight. Mushrooms are
dried using a food dehydrator at 95-115°F (35-46°C) until cracker-dry, then
stored in airtight containers with desiccant packets. Properly dried mushrooms
retain potency for 1-2 years.

## Common Contaminants

- **Trichoderma** (green mold): The most common contaminant. Appears as
  fast-growing green patches. Affected substrates should be discarded.
- **Cobweb mold** (Dactylium): Gray, fluffy growth that smothers mycelium.
  Often caused by poor air exchange.
- **Bacteria** (wet spot/bacterial bloom): Sour smell, slimy texture on grain.
  Usually results from insufficient sterilization.
- **Penicillium**: Blue-green mold that colonizes substrates aggressively.

## Potency Considerations

Psilocybin and psilocin content varies significantly between species, strains,
growing conditions, and flush number. Younger mushrooms tend to be more potent
by weight. UV light exposure during growth may increase potency. Potency
degrades with heat, light, and oxygen exposure during storage.

## Safety and Harm Reduction

Set and setting are critical for safe experiences. Starting with low doses
(0.5-1g dried), having a trusted trip sitter present, and avoiding
combinations with other substances are foundational harm reduction practices.
Individuals with personal or family histories of psychotic disorders should
exercise particular caution. Integration of the experience through journaling,
reflection, or therapy enhances long-term benefits.
