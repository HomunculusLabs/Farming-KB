# Psilocybin Mushroom Cultivation

Psilocybin mushroom cultivation refers to the process of growing fungi that
contain the psychoactive compounds psilocybin and psilocin. The most commonly
cultivated species belong to the genus *Psilocybe*, particularly *P. cubensis*,
which is favored for its relatively fast growth cycle and forgiving cultivation
requirements.

### Monotub Bulk Cultivation

A step up in scale, monotub cultivation uses grain spawn (typically rye, oats,
or millet) inoculated with a liquid culture or spores. Once colonized, the
grain is mixed with a bulk substrate (coconut coir and vermiculite, often with
gypsum) inside a modified plastic storage tub. Monotubs produce significantly
higher yields than PF Tek. See [[agar-culture-and-isolation]] allows
cultivators to verify clean cultures before committing to bulk grows.

## Fruiting Conditions

Once the substrate is fully colonized, fruiting is triggered by:

1. **Fresh air exchange**: Lowering CO2 levels signals mycelium to form
   primordia.
2. **High humidity**: 90-95% relative humidity prevents drying.
3. **Light**: Indirect ambient light (12 hours on/off) provides cues.
4. **Temperature drop**: A slight reduction from colonization temperature can
   stimulate pinning.

Properly designed [[fruiting-chambers-and-environmental-control]] are essential
for consistent, high-quality harvests.

## Harvesting and Drying

Mushrooms are harvested just before or as the veil beneath the cap tears.
