## Psilocybin Chemistry and Pharmacology

## Overview

Psilocybin and psilocin are the primary psychoactive compounds found in [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] profiles and subjective effect profiles:

1. **Indolealkylamines (tryptamines)**: LSD, psilocybin, psilocin, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT
2. **Phenylethylamines**: Mescaline, 2C-B, DOI
3. **Cannabinoids**: THC and related compounds

Chemically related substances within a group tend to produce similar subjective and physiological effects. This is because of their shared receptor affinity profiles. Agents that produce psychotic or delirious effects without the classic visual disturbances and insight-producing qualities of true hallucinogens are classified as pseudo-hallucinogens (e.g., diphenhydramine, atropine).

## Mechanism of Action

Psilocin acts primarily on the serotonin system as a **5-HT2A post-synaptic agonist or partial agonist**. The 5-HT2A receptor is a G-protein coupled receptor found abundantly in the cerebral cortex, particularly in layer V pyramidal neurons of the prefrontal cortex. Activation of these receptors produces a cascade of effects:

- **Perceptual alterations**: Enhanced visual acuity, geometric patterns, color intensification
- **Synesthesia-like experiences**: Blending of sensory modalities in some individuals
- **Changes in mood and thought processes**: Ranging from euphoria to anxiety depending on [[serotonin-psilocybin-structural-relationship-hallucinogenic-mechanism]] to tryptamines. These act primarily on GABA_A receptors (muscimol is a potent GABA agonist) and glutamate receptors (ibotenic acid is a glutamate agonist), rather than serotonin receptors.

The subjective effects are markedly different: Amanita intoxication is characterized by sedation, dissociation, and ataxia rather than the visual and cognitive effects of psilocybin mushrooms. Additionally, some closely related Amanita species (A. phalloides, A. virosa) contain lethal amatoxins, contributing to the lower popularity and greater danger of this genus compared to psilocybin mushrooms.

## Stability, Storage, and Potency

Psilocybin is relatively stable when stored dry and cool, but degrades with exposure to heat, light, oxygen, and moisture. Dried mushrooms retain potency significantly longer than fresh ones. The phosphate ester group of psilocybin provides greater chemical stability than the free hydroxyl group of psilocin, explaining why the mushroom biosynthesizes and stores the prodrug form.

Potency varies significantly between species, within species based on growing conditions, and between individual fruiting bodies. Factors affecting potency include substrate composition, growing temperature, harvest timing, and drying method. As a general principle, smaller, younger specimens tend to have higher concentrations of active alkaloids per unit of dry weight compared to mature specimens.

## Pharmacokinetics

After oral ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly and completely dephosphorylated to psilocin. Onset of effects typically occurs within 20-60 minutes, with peak intensity reached at approximately 2-3 hours post-ingestion. Effects generally subside over 4-6 hours, though subtle perceptual changes may persist longer.

Psilocin is metabolized primarily in the liver through oxidative deamination by monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes, producing 4-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (4-HIAA) as a major metabolite. Excretion is primarily renal. The subjective experience is strongly dose-dependent, with threshold perceptual changes appearing at doses of approximately 4-8 mg psilocybin and more profound alterations in consciousness at doses of 10-30 mg or more.

## Biosynthesis in Fungi

Psilocybin is biosynthesized in the fungal mycelium through a pathway that begins with the amino acid L-tryptophan. The pathway proceeds through several enzymatic steps:

1. **L-tryptophan → tryptamine**: Decarboxylation by aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase
2. **Tryptamine → N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT)**: Two successive N-methylations catalyzed by an indolethylamine N-methyltransferase
3. **DMT → 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (psilocin)**: Hydroxylation at the 4-position of the indole ring by a monooxygenase enzyme
4. **Psilocin → psilocybin**: Phosphorylation by a 4-hydroxytryptamine kinase

The discovery of the psilocybin biosynthetic gene cluster (PsiK, PsiM, PsiD, PsiH) has enabled researchers to understand the genetic basis of [[metzner-shamanic-healing-and-divination-paradigm]] ceremonies, referring to them as "teonanácatl" — "flesh of the gods."
