## Overview

A [[biogas-digester]] (anaerobic digester) is a sealed container where organic waste breaks down in the absence of oxygen, producing biogas — a mixture primarily of methane and carbon dioxide — and nutrient-rich digestate that serves as fertilizer. Small-scale digesters can provide cooking fuel, lighting, and heating for off-grid homesteads while simultaneously processing waste.

## Biogas Composition

Typical biogas from a well-managed digester:

| Component | Percentage | Properties |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Methane (CH4) | 50-70% | Combustible, the valuable component |
| Carbon dioxide (CO2) | 30-45% | Non-combustible, dilutes the gas |
| Nitrogen (N2) | 1-5% | Inert |
| Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) | 0.1-2% | Corrosive, toxic, smells like rotten eggs |
| Water vapor | 1-5% | Varies with temperature |
| Trace gases | <0.5% | Hydrogen, ammonia, oxygen |

The energy content of biogas is roughly 600 BTU per cubic foot (compared to 1,000 BTU for pure methane and 1,030 BTU for natural gas). A typical small digester produces 1-3 cubic meters of biogas per day, enough for 2-4 hours of cooking.

## The Anaerobic Digestion Process

Four stages of microbial [[decomposition]] occur in sequence:

### 1. Hydrolysis

Complex organic molecules ([[cellulose]], proteins, fats) are broken down into simpler soluble compounds by hydrolytic bacteria. This is often the rate-limiting step, especially for fibrous feedstocks. Temperature range: psychrophilic (55-68F), mesophilic (85-105F), or thermophilic (120-140F).

### 2. Acidogenesis

Acidogenic bacteria convert the products of hydrolysis into short-chain volatile fatty acids (acetic, propionic, butyric acid), alcohols, hydrogen, and carbon dioxide. The pH drops during this stage as acids accumulate.

### 3. Acetogenesis

Acetogenic bacteria convert the longer-chain fatty acids and alcohols into acetic acid, hydrogen, and carbon dioxide. This stage produces the primary substrate for methanogenesis.

### 4. Methanogenesis

Methanogenic archaea (strict anaerobes) convert acetic acid and hydrogen/carbon dioxide into methane and carbon dioxide. These organisms are the slowest-growing and most sensitive to environmental disturbances. Two pathways:

- Acetoclastic: CH3COOH → CH4 + CO2 (approximately 70% of methane production)
- Hydrogenotrophic: CO2 + 4H2 → CH4 + 2H2O (approximately 30% of methane production)

## Digester Types for Small-Scale Use

### Fixed-Dome Digester (Chinese Model)

- Underground masonry construction (brick or concrete)
- Gas collects in the upper portion of a fixed dome
- No moving parts, very durable (20+ year lifespan)
- Requires skilled masonry for construction
- Gas pressure varies with volume stored
- Common in rural China — millions installed

**Best for:** Permanent installations, warm climates, experienced builders

### Floating-Drum Digester (Indian Model)

- Underground or above-ground tank with a floating gas holder (drum) that rises and falls with gas production
- Constant gas pressure regardless of volume stored
- Simpler construction than fixed-dome
- Steel drum requires painting and maintenance to prevent corrosion
- Easier to measure gas production

**Best for:** Variable gas demand, moderate construction skills

### Tubular Digester (Plastic Bag)

- Long plastic tube (polyethylene or PVC) laid horizontally in a trench
- Inlet and outlet pipes at each end
- Gas is collected from the top of the bag
- Extremely low cost ($100-300 for materials)
- Shorter lifespan (3-5 years for plastic)
- Easy to build, no special skills required

**Best for:** Low-budget installations, tropical climates, temporary setups

### Artisanal Small-Batch Digester

- 55-gallon drum or IBC tote-based system
- Good for learning and experimentation
- Limited production (0.5-1 cubic meter per day)
- Not suitable for full cooking fuel supply
- Excellent for understanding the process before scaling up

## Feedstocks

### High-Yield Feedstocks

| Feedstock | Biogas Yield (L/kg) | Methane % | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------|-------|
| Cattle manure | 200-300 | 55-65% | Most common homestead feedstock |
| Poultry manure | 300-450 | 55-60% | High nitrogen, mix with carbon |
| Food waste | 400-700 | 55-65% | Excellent yield, may acidify |
| Grass/clover | 400-500 | 50-55% | Good if land is available |

### Feedstock Mixing

Successful digestion requires balancing carbon and nitrogen. Ideal C:N ratio: 20:1 to 30:1. Mix high-nitrogen feedstocks (manure, food waste) with high-carbon feedstocks (straw, leaves). Avoid: antibiotics, heavy metals, high oil concentrations, large bones, citrus in large quantities.

### Operating Parameters

- Temperature: Psychrophilic (55-68F) slow but stable; Mesophilic (85-105F) preferred for small-scale; Thermophilic (120-140F) fastest but sensitive
- pH: Optimal 6.5-8.0. Below 6.0 causes acid inhibition. Buffer with limestone or eggshells.
- Retention time: Mesophilic 20-40 days, psychrophilic 40-60 days
- Loading rate: Start small, increase over 2-3 weeks. A 2 cubic meter digester processes 5-10 kg/day once established

### Startup Procedure

1. Fill the digester with water warmed to 90-95F
2. Add seed material: fresh cattle manure or sludge from an active digester (20-30% of total volume)
3. Add initial feedstock: manure mixed with water to a slurry consistency (roughly 1:1 ratio)
4. Seal the digester completely — no oxygen can enter
5. Wait 2-3 weeks for gas production to begin
6. Initially, the gas may contain too much CO2 to burn — vent it safely and wait
7. Test flammability at a safe distance; once gas burns with a blue flame, the digester is established
8. Begin regular feeding: remove an equivalent volume of digestate before adding new feedstock

## Digestate: The Fertilizer Byproduct

The liquid effluent from a biogas digester is an excellent fertilizer:

- Nitrogen content: 2-5% (mostly ammonium, readily available to plants)
- Phosphorus: 1-3%
- Potassium: 1-4%
- Organic matter: partially stabilized, beneficial for soil
- Pathogen reduction: mesophilic digestion reduces pathogens by 90-99%

Application rate: dilute 1:3 to 1:5 with water and apply to garden beds, fruit trees, or pasture. Avoid applying to crops intended for raw consumption within 30 days of harvest.

## Gas Storage and Use

### Storage

- Low-pressure storage in a flexible gas bag (heavy-duty PVC or rubber)
- Alternatively, the digester itself acts as the storage vessel (fixed-dome or floating-drum designs)
- 1 cubic meter of biogas = roughly 2 hours of cooking on a single-burner stove
