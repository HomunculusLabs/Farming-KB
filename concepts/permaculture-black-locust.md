Black Locust (Robinia pseudoacacia) is a valuable hardwood tree prized for its rot-resistant timber, excellent firewood, and nitrogen-fixing ability. It is one of the most valuable bee forages in France and a key [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] species in temperate permaculture designs.

## Botanical Description

Black Locust is a fast-growing deciduous tree reaching 50-75 feet (15-23 m) tall with a spread of 35-50 feet (11-15 m). It has compound leaves and produces showy, fragrant white flower clusters in late spring. The bark is deeply furrowed and dark brown. Trees can live 80-100 years. It spreads by both seed and root suckering, forming dense groves over time.

## Uses

- Bare soil erosion control

- Bee forage (produces very tasty honey)

- Wood and timber (hard, durable, rot-resistant)

- Windbreak

- Dryland tolerant

- Leguminous nitrogen fixer

- Firewood (long-burning, low smoke)

- [[mulch-producing-plants]] from leaf fall and prunings

- [[urban-permaculture-fodder-trees-livestock]] — livestock browse leaves and young branches

## Growing Conditions

- Prefers well-drained soil but tolerates a wide range of conditions

- Hardy in USDA zones 4-9

- Drought tolerant once established

- Usually grows in eastern United States but widely adaptable

- Considered invasive in some areas — check local species before planting

- Prefers full sun

- Tolerates soil pH from 4.5 to 7.5

## Propagation

Black Locust is most commonly propagated from seed, which requires scarification (soaking in hot water or nicking the seed coat) followed by cold stratification for 30-60 days. Root cuttings and suckers transplant easily. Trees grow rapidly — 3-6 feet per year in good conditions. They can begin flowering at 4-6 years of age.

## Timber Qualities

The wood is hard and durable, resistant to rot, making it excellent for fence posts, outdoor construction, and furniture. It has a Janka hardness rating comparable to oak. As firewood, it grows quickly and produces long-burning logs with very little smoke, yielding about 28-29 million BTU per cord. The wood is also valued for [[urban-permaculture-fodder-trees-livestock]] where branch pruning provides livestock browse.

## Beekeeping Value

Black Locust is one of the premier honey plants in temperate regions. The fragrant white flowers bloom for 10-14 days in late spring, producing a light, mild, and very tasty honey that remains liquid for long periods. A single large tree can support multiple hives. See [[permaculture-beekeeping]] for integration strategies.

## Pollination and Flowers

The pendant clusters of white, pea-like flowers are highly fragrant and attract a wide range of pollinators including honeybees, bumblebees, and native bees. Flowering typically occurs in May or June, depending on latitude.

## Establishment

Seedlings are particularly tasty to deer in the first couple of years and need protection (tree tubes or fencing). Weed control around young trees is important during the first 2-3 years. Once established, Black Locust is extremely vigorous and self-sufficient.

## Caution

Seedpods are toxic to animals when eaten. Do not plant where livestock can access fallen pods. All parts except the flowers contain toxic proteins called robin. The tree can be invasive through root suckering.

## Pests and Diseases

Black Locust borer is the primary pest, tunneling into the trunk and weakening trees. Leaf miners can also affect foliage but rarely cause serious damage. Trees stressed by poor drainage are more susceptible to heart rot.

## Coppicing and Pollarding

Black Locust responds very well to coppicing (cutting to ground level) and pollarding (cutting at head height). This management technique encourages rapid regrowth and provides a sustainable supply of poles, fence posts, and firewood. Coppiced stools can produce useful poles every 5-8 years.

## Design Integration

In permaculture designs following [[faires-permaculture-ethics-and-design-principles]], Black Locust is typically placed in zones 3-4 as a multipurpose tree. It serves well as a windbreak component, a nitrogen-fixing nurse tree for orchards, and a source of durable timber. Its deep root system helps break up compacted subsoil layers, improving conditions for subsequent plantings.
