    12|# Casuarina Windbreak and Erosion Control
    13|
    14|Bill Mollison highlights Casuarina (she-oak) species as among the most
    15|valuable multipurpose trees for windbreaks and erosion
    16|control systems. Native to Australia and widely naturalised across the
    17|tropics and subtropics, Casuarina combines nitrogen fixation, rapid
    18|growth, wind resistance, soil stabilisation, and multiple useful products
    19|in a single tree genus.
    20|
    21|## Species Overview
    22|
    23|Key Casuarina species referenced by Mollison:
    24|
    25|- Casuarina equisetifolia: coastal she-oak; salt-tolerant, fast-growing,
    26|  reaching 15-25m; excellent for coastal windbreaks and sand stabilisation
    27|- Casuarina cunninghamiana: river she-oak; found along waterways, grows
    28|  to 20-30m; excellent for riparian planting and farm windbreaks in
    29|  inland areas
    30|- Casuarina glauca: swamp she-oak; tolerates waterlogging and saline
    31|  soils; useful for reclamation of degraded land
    32|- Allocasuarina species: smaller she-oaks (5-12m) suited to hedgerows,
    33|  wildlife habitat, and smaller properties
    34|
    35|All Casuarina species share distinctive needle-like branchlets (often
    36|mistaken for leaves) and a conifer-like growth habit that makes them
    37|exceptionally effective wind filters.
    38|
    39|## Nitrogen Fixation
    40|
    41|Casuarina forms a symbiotic relationship with Frankia bacteria in root
    42|nodules, fixing atmospheric nitrogen and converting it to forms available
    43|to plants. This nitrogen fixation capacity rivals that of leguminous trees
    44|like [[permaculture-plants-legume-trees-acacia-carob-locust]] [[acacia]], carob, and locust, and Leucaena.
    45|
    46|Mollison emphasises several advantages of Casuarina as a nitrogen fixer:
    47|
    48|- Frankia-nodulated trees fix nitrogen across a wider pH range than
    49|  Rhizobium-nodulated legumes, performing well in acidic and alkaline
    50|  soils alike
    51|- Casuarina leaf litter is nitrogen-rich and breaks down relatively
    52|  quickly, contributing significant nitrogen to the soil surface
    53|- Understory plants and adjacent crops benefit from the nitrogen
    54|  enrichment provided by Casuarina root systems and leaf fall
    55|- The nitrogen-fixing capacity allows Casuarina to thrive on
    56|  nitrogen-poor soils including degraded farmland, mine sites, and
    57|  coastal sands
    58|
    59|## Windbreak Design
    60|
    61|Casuarina is a premier windbreak species because its dense, conifer-like
    62|foliage provides year-round wind filtration. Unlike deciduous windbreak
    63|trees that lose effectiveness in winter, Casuarina maintains its
    64|protective canopy year-round.
    65|
    66|Windbreak design principles using Casuarina:
    67|
    68|- Height: plant 2-3 rows of Casuarina as the windward component of a
    69|  multi-row windbreak; the tallest row should be on the windward edge
    70|- Density: 2-4 metre spacing within rows creates a dense barrier; the
    71|  foliage is sufficiently fine-textured to filter wind without creating
    72|  severe turbulence downwind
    73|- Length: windbreaks should extend 10-15 times the height of the trees
    74|  beyond the area being protected to account for wind curling around the
    75|  ends
    76|- Distance: the protected zone extends approximately 10-15 times the
    77|  height of the windbreak downwind, so a 20m Casuarina belt protects
    78|  a zone 200-300m long
    79|- Composition: combine Casuarina with understory species in a multi-strata
    80|  windbreak; the lower layers catch wind that passes through the canopy
    81|
    82|## Erosion Control
    83|
    84|Casuarina excels at stabilising soils on slopes, riverbanks, and coastal
    85|dunes. Its erosion control mechanisms include:
    86|
    87|- Deep and extensive root systems that bind soil to depths of 3-5 metres,
    88|  anchoring slopes that would otherwise fail during heavy rain
    89|- Dense surface root mats that hold topsoil and resist sheet erosion
    90|- High tolerance for waterlogged, saline, and nutrient-poor soils,
    91|  allowing establishment on degraded sites where other trees fail
    92|- Rapid growth providing quick ground cover; Casuarina equisetifolia
    93|  reaches 3-5m within 3 years from seed
    94|- Leaf litter that forms a protective mulch layer on the soil surface,
    95|  reducing raindrop impact and promoting infiltration
    96|
    97|Mollison recommends Casuarina for stabilising dam walls, road cuttings,
    98|gully heads, and coastal foredunes where erosion threatens infrastructure
    99|or productive land.

   101|## Mulch and Fodder
   102|
   103|Casuarina provides useful mulch and limited fodder:
   104|
   105|- Mulch: the fine branchlets (needle-like cladodes) that shed naturally
   106|  form a nitrogen-rich surface mulch. Pruned branches can be chipped for
   107|  concentrated mulch application around garden plants and fruit trees.
   108|- Fodder: Casuarina branchlets have moderate protein content (8-12%)
   109|  and are browsed by cattle, sheep, and goats in times of feed shortage.
   110|  They are not a primary fodder species but provide emergency forage
   111|  during droughts when other feed is scarce.
   112|- Bedding: fallen branchlets provide clean, aromatic bedding for poultry
   113|  and livestock.
   114|
   115|## Timber and Fuel
   116|
   117|Casuarina produces dense, hard wood (specific gravity 0.8-1.0) that
   118|sinks in water. Uses include:
   119|
   120|- Firewood: extremely dense and hot-burning; one of the best firewood
   121|  species in the tropics, producing high heat with low smoke when seasoned
   122|- Fence posts: naturally durable in the ground due to high tannin content;
   123|  posts last 10-20 years without treatment
   124|- Tool handles: the hard, straight wood makes excellent handles for
   125|  tools and implements
   126|- Craft wood: the fine grain and natural finish suit turnery and carving
   127|- Charcoal: Casuarina charcoal is high-quality and was historically used
   128|  in iron smelting in Australia
   129|
   130|## Establishment
   131|
   132|Casuarina is easily established from seed or nursery seedlings:
   133|
   134|- Seed: tiny winged seeds are sown in nursery beds; germination occurs
   135|  within 2-4 weeks. Seedlings are ready for transplanting at 20-40cm
   136|  height, typically 3-6 months after sowing.
   137|- Direct seeding: successful on sandy soils where weed competition is
   138|  low; broadcast seed into prepared ground at the start of the wet season
   139|- Spacing: 2-3m within rows, 3-5m between rows for windbreaks; wider
   140|  spacing (5-10m) for scattered erosion control plantings
   141|- Care: seedlings are drought-tolerant once established but require
   142|  weed control for the first 6-12 months; mulch with organic material
   143|  and water during dry periods
