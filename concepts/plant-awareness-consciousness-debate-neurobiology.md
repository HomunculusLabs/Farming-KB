## Anthony Trewavas and Plant Intelligence

Anthony Trewavas, a plant biochemist at the University of Edinburgh, has been the most prominent advocate for plant
intelligence. His argument, articulated in a seminal 2003 paper, rests on several pillars:

- **Signal integration**: Plants continuously process information about light quality, gravity, touch, humidity,
  chemical gradients, and neighbor proximity. These signals are integrated to produce coordinated growth decisions.
- **[[plant-hormones-and-chemical-signaling-systems]], not neural information integration.
- **No centralized processing**: Animal consciousness correlates with centralized nervous systems. Plants process
  information locally and decentrally, with no equivalent of a brain or ganglion.
- **Adaptive behavior is not cognition**: A thermostat responds to temperature adaptively but not cognitively.
  Similarly, plant hormone cascades are sophisticated but mechanistic.
- **Evolutionary argument**: Consciousness likely evolved for mobile decision-making in complex environments.
  Sessile organisms face different selective pressures and have no apparent need for subjective experience.
- **Metabolic cost argument**: Neural tissue capable of consciousness is enormously expensive. Plants under
  competitive pressure would be strongly selected against unnecessary metabolic expenditure.

## The Mimosa pudica Learning Experiments

Monica Gagliano"s 2014 experiments with *Mimosa pudica* have become a focal point. Gagliano repeatedly dropped
Mimosa plants onto foam from a fixed height. The plants initially folded their leaves, but after 5–6 drops stopped —
appearing to habituate to the harmless stimulus.

Gagliano reported that the habituated response persisted for weeks and transferred to new contexts — plants
habituated to dropping did not fold when shaken, suggesting stimulus-specific learning. She interpreted this as
evidence of "[[hyphal-biology-and-turgor-pressure]] — not learning in any cognitive
sense. The debate highlights how the same data support radically different conclusions depending on theoretical
commitments.
