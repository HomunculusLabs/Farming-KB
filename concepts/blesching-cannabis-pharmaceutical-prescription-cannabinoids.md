# Pharmaceutical Prescription Cannabinoids

## Overview

Pharmaceutical cannabinoids are isolated, synthetic, or standardized cannabis-derived compounds approved by regulatory agencies such as the FDA for the treatment of specific medical conditions. In *The Cannabis Health Index*, Uwe Blesching reviews the four major pharmaceutical cannabinoids available for prescription use: **Dronabinol** (Marinol), **Nabilone** (Cesamet), **Sativex**, and the discontinued **Rimonabant**. These drugs represent the intersection of pharmaceutical science and endocannabinoid system pharmacology, offering standardized dosing but lacking the full spectrum of biologically active compounds found in whole-plant cannabis.

## Dronabinol (Marinol)

### Chemical Identity and Mechanism

Dronabinol is the generic name for synthetic delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) formulated as an oral capsule. It is pharmacologically identical to the primary psychoactive cannabinoid found in cannabis. Dronabinol activates both CB1 and CB2 receptors, though its affinity for CB1 receptors is significantly stronger, producing the characteristic psychoactive effects associated with THC.

### FDA-Approved Indications

Dronabinol is FDA-approved for two primary indications:

1. **Anorexia associated with weight loss in AIDS patients** — Dronabinol stimulates appetite through CB1 receptor activation in the hypothalamus, enhancing the hunger response and promoting caloric intake.
2. **Nausea and vomiting associated with cancer chemotherapy** — The antiemetic properties of dronabinol are mediated through CB1 receptors in the brainstem's vomiting center (area postrema).

### Clinical Evidence from the CHI Database

Multiple clinical trials reviewed in the Cannabis Health Index document dronabinol's efficacy:

- A 2007 human trial at Saint Louis University involving 117 AIDS patients demonstrated that dronabinol was safe and effective for anorexia associated with weight loss (CHI: 3).
- A 2007 trial at the Orlando Immunology Center confirmed that dronabinol produced significant appetite improvement in AIDS patients (CHI: 3).
- A 1991 study at UNIMED, Inc. found that dronabinol caused weight gain in seven of ten patients with symptomatic HIV infection and improved appetite in both HIV and cancer patients (CHI: 3).
- A landmark 1995 study at St. John's Hospital in Tulsa involving 139 participants who received 2.5 mg of dronabinol showed robust antiemetic effects for chemotherapy-induced nausea (CHI: 5).

### Limitations and Considerations

Blesching emphasizes that dronabinol, like all isolated THC pharmaceuticals, lacks the companion cannabinoids (particularly CBD), terpenes, and flavonoids present in whole-plant cannabis. This absence eliminates the potential [[blesching-cannabis-cannabinoid-terpene-entourage-effect|entourage effect]], where multiple cannabis compounds work synergistically. Patients using dronabinol may experience stronger psychoactive effects than those using balanced whole-plant preparations because CBD normally modulates THC's psychoactivity.

## Nabilone (Cesamet)

### Chemical Identity and Mechanism

Nabilone is a fully synthetic cannabinoid structurally related to THC but chemically distinct. It was designed to mimic THC's therapeutic properties while potentially offering different pharmacokinetic properties. Nabilone activates CB1 and CB2 receptors similarly to THC, with binding to the CB1 receptor being particularly significant for its antiemetic and analgesic effects.

### FDA-Approved Indications

Nabilone is FDA-approved for:

1. **Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting** in patients who have not responded adequately to conventional antiemetic treatments.
2. **Off-label uses** that have accumulated significant clinical evidence include chronic pain management, fibromyalgia, multiple sclerosis spasticity, and PTSD-related nightmares.

### Clinical Evidence from the CHI Database

- A 2006 study at the William Osler Health Center in Toronto involving 82 cancer patients receiving nabilone for 53 days demonstrated significant pain reduction alongside improvements in nausea, appetite, and mood (CHI: 3).
- For [[blesching-cannabis-fibromyalgia-tai-chi-and-trauma|fibromyalgia]], nabilone at doses of 0.5–1.0 mg showed significant improvements in sleep quality and pain reduction compared to placebo (CHI: 3).
- In hepatitis C patients, nabilone reduced nausea and vomiting while improving appetite (CHI: 2).
- For cancer-induced night sweats, nabilone at 1 mg daily demonstrated positive effects on perspiration, pain, and anorexia (CHI: 3).

### Nabilone and the Endocannabinoid System

Blesching notes that nabilone's relationship to the endocannabinoid system is instructive for understanding how synthetic cannabinoids interact with naturally occurring pathways. THC and anandamide (the endogenous cannabinoid) bind relatively equally to CB1 and CB2 receptors, and nabilone follows a similar binding profile. However, as a synthetic compound, nabilone may interact differently with metabolic enzymes, potentially affecting duration of action and elimination compared to plant-derived THC.

## Sativex

### Composition and Formulation

Sativex is unique among prescription cannabinoids because it is not a synthetic compound but rather a standardized whole-plant extract. Developed by GW Pharmaceuticals, Sativex is formulated as an oromucosal spray containing approximately equal parts THC and CBD, delivered in a 1:1 ratio. This formulation preserves the broad spectrum of cannabinoids and terpenes found in the cannabis plant, making it the pharmaceutical preparation most similar to whole-plant medicine.

Blesching specifically notes that "with the exception of Sativex, which is essentially a plant-derived tincture, pharmaceuticals that contain THC do not contain any of the other biologically active components of cannabis that may play an important therapeutic role in the human body."

### Approved Uses and Status

Sativex is approved in numerous countries (including Canada, the UK, and several European nations) for:

- **Multiple sclerosis spasticity** — The most extensively studied indication, with multiple randomized controlled trials supporting efficacy.
- **Cancer pain** — Approved as an adjunctive analgesic for moderate-to-severe cancer pain.
- **Neuropathic pain** — Various forms of chronic nerve pain.

As of the publication of the Cannabis Health Index, Sativex had not received FDA approval in the United States, though clinical trials were ongoing.

### Advantages Over Isolated Cannabinoids

The 1:1 THC:CBD ratio in Sativex is significant because CBD modulates many of THC's adverse effects:

- CBD reduces THC-induced anxiety and paranoia
- CBD counteracts THC's psychoactive intensity, allowing higher therapeutic doses
- CBD contributes its own anti-inflammatory, anticonvulsant, and anxiolytic properties
- The entourage effect from additional terpenes and minor cannabinoids may enhance overall efficacy

## Rimonabant (Discontinued)

### Mechanism of Action

Rimonabant was notable as a **CB1 receptor antagonist** — the first drug of its kind designed to block rather than activate cannabinoid receptors. By blocking CB1 receptors, rimonabant was intended to reduce appetite and promote weight loss by interfering with the endocannabinoid system's role in hunger regulation.

### Clinical Failure and Withdrawal

Despite initially showing promise for weight loss, rimonabant was withdrawn from the market due to severe psychiatric side effects, including depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation. Blesching uses rimonabant as a cautionary example of the endocannabinoid system's complexity: blocking this fundamental regulatory system produced mental health consequences that far outweighed any metabolic benefits. The rimonabant story underscores the importance of the endocannabinoid system in maintaining emotional homeostasis and mental health.

## Key Differences: Pharmaceutical vs. Whole-Plant Cannabis

### Advantages of Pharmaceutical Cannabinoids

1. **Standardized dosing** — Precise milligram-level dosing allows for consistent therapeutic windows
2. **Quality control** — Pharmaceutical manufacturing ensures purity and absence of contaminants
3. **Insurance coverage** — FDA-approved medications are typically covered by health insurance
4. **Legal accessibility** — Available by prescription throughout the United States regardless of local cannabis laws
5. **Physician familiarity** — Doctors are more comfortable prescribing familiar pharmaceutical products

### Limitations of Pharmaceutical Cannabinoids

1. **Lack of entourage effect** — Isolated THC lacks the synergistic benefits of full-spectrum cannabis
2. **Stronger psychoactive effects** — Without CBD modulation, THC alone can produce more pronounced psychoactive effects
3. **Limited cannabinoid spectrum** — Missing CBD, CBG, CBC, THCV, terpenes, and flavonoids
4. **Inflexible ratios** — Fixed formulations cannot be tailored to individual patient needs
5. **Route of administration** — Oral capsules like dronabinol have variable absorption and delayed onset compared to inhaled or sublingual routes

## Research Cannabinoids (Non-Prescription)

Beyond the prescription pharmaceuticals, the Cannabis Health Index catalogs numerous synthetic cannabinoids used primarily in research settings:

### CB1 Agonists
- **CP 55,940** — Binds equally to CB1 and CB2 receptors
- **HU 210** — Primarily CB1 selective
- **HU 239 (ajulemic acid)** — A synthetic THC analog with anti-inflammatory properties

### CB1 Antagonists
- **SR 141716A (Rimonabant)** — Formerly prescribed; now withdrawn
- **AM 251** — Research tool for blocking CB1

### CB2 Agonists
- **JWH-015, JWH-133** — Selective CB2 activation for anti-inflammatory applications
- **GW-405,833** — Primarily CB2 selective
- **WIN55,212-2** — Binds both CB1 and CB2

### CB2 Antagonists
- **SR144528** — Blocks CB2 more strongly than CB1
- **AM 630** — Research-grade CB2 antagonist

These research compounds are essential tools for understanding cannabinoid receptor pharmacology and have contributed to the development of potential therapeutic agents targeting specific aspects of the [[endocannabinoid-system|endocannabinoid system]].

## Clinical Implications

Blesching's review of pharmaceutical cannabinoids reveals a fundamental tension in cannabinoid medicine: the pharmaceutical approach offers precision and regulatory acceptance but sacrifices the complex synergistic benefits of whole-plant cannabis. The evidence compiled in the Cannabis Health Index consistently shows that while pharmaceutical cannabinoids are effective for specific conditions, they often achieve lower CHI scores than whole-plant preparations for the same conditions, potentially due to the absence of the entourage effect.
