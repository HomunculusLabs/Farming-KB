# Property Trusts and Ethical Investment

Property trusts represent one of the most powerful mechanisms for channeling investment capital into [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]] and sustainable community development. In [[permaculture-design]], the property trust model allows many small investors to collectively purchase, rehabilitate, and manage land — turning conventional real estate investment on its head by prioritizing earth repair and community benefit alongside financial returns.

## The Ethical Investment Imperative

No investment in "glamour stocks" — coal, oil, uranium, properties, paper pulp, agrochemicals, mining — is likely to yield anything but more pollution and a hastening of global collapse. The evidence on acid rain alone will convince any sane person that further "progress and development" in these sectors will cause social and environmental upheaval. The need is to turn money resources to truly rehabilitative ends: accelerated reafforestation, preservation of existing forests, sane [[permaculture-village-development]], and the rehabilitation of eroded and misused lands.

## How Property Trusts Work

A property trust purchases real estate for improvement, lease, or rental on behalf of many small investors who cannot afford to individually own or develop such properties. By improving properties so purchased, their value increases. Under present rulings in many jurisdictions, taxation is not incurred on that increase in value if the property is held for 10 or more years, nor is the trust itself taxed on its income from investors.

## The Trust Structure

The management group obtains backing from investors (via a public prospectus) to float a Property Trust on the investment market. The prime purpose is to give every person a chance to do more than object to or protest inappropriate land sales to overseas investors, land misuse, and poor planning — and to invest in saving critical or endangered national resources such as wildlife and forests, while actively rehabilitating eroded lands.

A low unit price (e.g., $100) enables even poorer people to invest. A single unit can be held by a partnership, society, or other corporate group so that even less money need be contributed per person. There need be no limit to the number of units held by any person.

## Active Investor Involvement

Unlike conventional property trusts, investors should be given every opportunity to involve themselves in their investments:

- On-site work and volunteering
- Consultancy roles
- Leasehold arrangements
- Tree nursery supply contracts
- Preference in sales of titles in villages
- Access to products or services
- Controlled recreational access to lands and buildings

The trust can inform investors of any opportunity for their involvement at any level, from volunteer or recreational use to paid consultancy, building, or leaseholds.

## Fund Allocation

Funds are used for several purposes, with strict cost controls:

- Setting up the trust deed and management company
- Paying for the work of the trustee
- Paying management a retainer for their work
- Normal running costs: office expenses, printing, bookkeeping, accounting, valuation, and travel of management to properties

The precise amount used for administration should not exceed 4 to 8% of funds. The remainder is devoted to the purchase and development of properties as outlined in the prospectus. Costs reduce as trust income grows. Any surplus or unused funds accumulating in the trust can be invested in ethical systems, including housing cooperatives, inventory for development projects, and shares in ethical businesses.

## Project Areas for Ethical Trusts

Ethical property trusts can operate across a wide range of project areas:

- **Wildlife and Forest Habitat:** Purchase of threatened habitat, usually with a specific plant or animal group in mind, managed for the aims stated in a specific prospectus
- **Land Rehabilitation:** Purchase and development of eroded, salted, deforested, or misused land for rehabilitation
- **Sustainable Villages:** Development of energy-efficient, sustainable villages on trust lands, for sale as developed or pre-sale to raise capital for development
- **Special Interest Developments:** Trout fisheries, aquaculture, or seed orchards for lease or sale
- **Bioregional Development:** Purchase of community resources in specific regions for use by residents, community groups, trust unit holders, or by residents buying trust properties in that area
- **Overseas Outposts:** Purchase of selected properties for assisting [[low-cost-mushroom-growing-house-construction-developing-countries]]
- **Clean Energy and Transport:** Bioregional clean energy systems or clean transport methods

## Development Staging and Returns

Trusts [[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]] sequentially as funds become available. The process follows a defined cycle:

1. Purchase, prospectus writing, and design
2. Sequential project initiation as funds allow
3. Management of 4 to 8% for office and retainer costs
4. Annual re-evaluation of properties by an appointed valuer
5. Investors are encouraged to assist in developments and sales
6. Possible sale or disposal of trust properties after 10 years
7. Increase in real value of units to investors

## The Investment Cap

In the first trust of this type, the aim can be to stop accepting investors at $2 to 5 million, which will develop a property or properties as listed in the prospectus. This cap ensures manageable scale and focused development, while still providing meaningful impact.

## Connecting Investors to Outcomes

The property trust model creates a direct connection between investment capital and ecological outcomes. Rather than abstract financial instruments, investors can see, visit, and participate in the tangible results of their investment — restored landscapes, thriving [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]], and sustainable communities. This transparency and involvement addresses one of the key failures of conventional investment: the disconnection between capital and its real-world effects.

## Self-Funding Through Ethical Enterprise

The combination of charitable trust and trading [[permaculture-village-development-and-trust-structures]] means that ethical property trusts can become self-funding over time. As properties appreciate and generate returns, the trust can fund its own operations and expand its portfolio without relying on continued donations or grants. This financial sustainability is essential for long-term ecological restoration work, which often operates on timescales of decades rather than years.
