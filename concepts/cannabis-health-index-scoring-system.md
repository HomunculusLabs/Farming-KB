# Cannabis Health Index Scoring System

## Overview

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) is an evidence-based compilation and review of available scientific literature relevant to more than 100 different chronic diseases and persistent symptoms. The CHI model evaluates the practical value of each study design alongside key findings to rate, on a defined scale, the degree of confidence that cannabis represents a viable treatment for a given condition.

The CHI rating system was developed to provide patients, caregivers, and physicians with a systematic, at-a-glance assessment of what current scientific evidence says about how cannabis may work for specific conditions. It bridges the gap between the rapidly growing body of cannabis research (more than 15,000 entries at the National Library of Health) and practical, informed medical decision-making.

## What Is a CHI Score?

The CHI score represents a cumulative confidence level derived from evaluating individual studies. Each study contributing to a condition's overall CHI score is assessed independently. The system takes into account both the type of research study (which determines the weight of its contribution) and the key findings of that study. The total CHI value for any given condition represents the sum of all individual study scores.

Higher CHI values indicate stronger evidence for cannabis as a therapeutic option. The system is designed to be transparent about the quality and type of evidence supporting each claim, rather than making binary yes/no determinations about efficacy.

## Importance of Study Type

Not all research studies carry equal weight in the evidence hierarchy. The CHI system recognizes this by assigning different point values based on the type and rigor of each study design. Studies conducted in the laboratory on cells or animals provide preliminary evidence but are weighted less heavily than studies involving human subjects.

The general hierarchy of study types, from preliminary to most definitive, includes:

1. **Laboratory studies** (in vitro): Experiments conducted on cells or tissues in a controlled environment. These provide mechanistic insights but cannot directly predict human outcomes.

2. **Animal studies** (in vivo): Experiments conducted on living animals. While more complex than cell studies, animal physiology differs from human physiology in important ways.

3. **Case reports and case series**: Documentation of individual patient experiences or small groups of patients. Useful for generating hypotheses but prone to bias and confounding variables.

4. **Observational studies**: Studies that observe patients without intervention, including cohort studies and case-control studies. Can identify correlations but cannot establish causation.

5. **Clinical trials** (human): Controlled studies with human subjects, ranging from open-label trials to randomized controlled trials. These carry the most weight in the CHI system.

6. **Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials**: The gold standard of clinical research. Neither patients nor researchers know who receives the active treatment versus placebo, minimizing bias.

## How to Use CHI Scores

### General Guidelines

The CHI system provides a framework for understanding the current state of evidence rather than a definitive prescription. When reviewing CHI scores, consider the following:

- Higher total CHI values indicate a stronger cumulative evidence base for the therapeutic potential of cannabis for that condition.
- The number of studies reviewed is also important; a high score from a single study carries less weight than a moderately high score from multiple independent studies.
- CHI scores represent the current state of published research and may change as new studies become available.

### Evaluating Individual Studies

When examining a specific study contributing to a CHI score, consider:

- The study design type and its corresponding weight in the system
- The number of participants (larger sample sizes generally provide more reliable results)
- The duration of the study
- Whether the study was conducted independently or with industry sponsorship
- The specific cannabinoid preparation used (whole plant, isolated THC, CBD, synthetic)
- The mode of administration (smoking, vaporization, oral, topical)
- The specific findings and whether they were statistically significant

## Evidence-Based Strain and Form Considerations

The CHI system encourages attention to the specific characteristics of the cannabis preparation used in each study:

### Strain Considerations

- **Sativa-dominant strains**: Higher THC to CBD ratio, generally more stimulating and energizing, often recommended for daytime use and depression.
- **Indica-dominant strains**: Lower THC to higher CBD ratio, generally more sedating and relaxing, often recommended for evening use and anxiety.
- The THC:CBD ratio is therapeutically significant because it determines the degree to which the whole plant's constituents are additive or counteractive.

### Form Considerations

Different modes of administration produce different pharmacokinetic profiles:

- **Inhalation** (smoking or vaporization): Near-instant onset (minutes), shorter duration of effect. Vaporization is preferred over smoking as it eliminates inhalation of carbon compounds from burned plant material.
- **Oral ingestion** (oils, tinctures, edibles): Delayed onset (30 minutes to 2 hours, especially on a full stomach), longer duration. Effects may last many hours and can be intense if too much is consumed.
- **Topical application**: Localized effects for skin conditions, pain, and inflammation without psychoactive effects.

## The Setting, Intention, and Exploration Framework

The CHI model incorporates a mind-body medicine framework alongside the evidence-based scoring system. This framework consists of three elements:

### The Setting

Create a supportive environment for healing. The physical and emotional environment in which cannabis medicine is used can significantly influence outcomes. A calm, safe, and comfortable setting allows for deeper therapeutic effects.

### The Intention

Set a conscious and caring intention before using cannabis medicine. Rather than simply seeking symptom relief, patients are encouraged to articulate a deeper healing intention. This practice draws from established traditions of using cannabis as a tool for exploring consciousness and facilitating personal growth.

### The Exploration

Use the cannabis experience as an opportunity for self-discovery and deeper understanding of the relationship between one's frame of mind and physical health. This involves being present with whatever arises during the experience, including emotions, sensations, and insights that may surface.

## Powerful Questions for Self-Guided Healing

The CHI model includes a set of "powerful questions" for each condition, designed to facilitate deeper exploration of the mind-body connection in illness:

- What emotions or mental patterns may be connected to this condition?
- What beliefs about myself or my body might be contributing to this illness?
- What unresolved experiences or relationships might be relevant?
- What would healing look like beyond the absence of symptoms?
- What strengths or resources do I already possess that support my healing?

## Integration with Mind-Body Medicine

A distinctive feature of the CHI is its integration of evidence-based research with techniques from mind-body medicine. This reflects the understanding, supported by psychosomatic research, that emotional states directly influence physiological processes including immune function, inflammation, and healing.

The model acknowledges that mental-emotional states can induce susceptibility to disease or support formidable immune defense. Negative states such as chronic fear, powerlessness, and violation of personal boundaries deplete immune reserves. Conversely, emotional support, safety, trust, love, and confidence in one's abilities contribute to robust immunity.

## Companion Resources

The CHI was published alongside a free mobile application and a companion monthly electronic magazine. The app provides the same rating system as the book, making it possible to quickly determine what the current scientific consensus says about cannabis for specific conditions. The monthly magazine explores how the endocannabinoid system works as a bridge between body and mind, examining new research and offering articles on mind-body medicine, cannabinoid-based medicine, and emotional intelligence.

## Limitations

- CHI scores reflect published research at the time of compilation and may not include the most recent studies.
- The system relies on the quality and availability of published research, which may be influenced by regulatory barriers to cannabis research.
- Individual patient responses vary significantly based on genetics, endocannabinoid tone, and other factors.
- CHI scores should be considered alongside clinical expertise and patient preferences in making treatment decisions.
