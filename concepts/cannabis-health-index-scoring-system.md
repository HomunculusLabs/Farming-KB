# The Cannabis Health Index (CHI)

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) is an evidence-based scoring system
developed by Uwe Blesching, PhD, for evaluating the scientific
support for using cannabis in the treatment of over 100 chronic
symptoms and diseases. Published in the book *The Cannabis Health
Index: Combining the Science of [[cervantes-medical-marijuana-pharmacology]] with Mindfulness
Techniques to Heal 100 Chronic Symptoms and Diseases* (North
Atlantic Books, 2015), the CHI model integrates [[hofmann-lsd-clinical-research-early-studies]]
data with mind-body medicine principles to provide a comprehensive
framework for therapeutic cannabis use.

## Purpose and Motivation

The CHI was created to address a significant gap in medical cannabis
literature. While over 15,000 scientific entries on medical cannabis
existed at the National Library of Health at the time of writing, a
systematic evidence-based model for the plant's safe and targeted
use was largely lacking. Blesching, a former mobile intensive-care
paramedic in [[shipibo-san-francisco-ceremonial-center-ucayali]], was motivated by his experience
witnessing the limitations and dangers of conventional pharmaceutical
approaches, including the Vioxx scandal where an approved pain
medication killed an estimated 55,000 to 500,000 patients.

The CHI addresses the concern that only about 15% of medical
interventions are actually based on solid scientific evidence. By
compiling and scoring the available research on cannabis for specific
conditions, the CHI aims to give patients and practitioners a
transparent, evidence-based tool for making informed decisions.

## CHI Score Structure

The CHI assigns numerical scores reflecting the quality and quantity
of scientific evidence supporting cannabis use for each condition.
The score takes into account the type of research studies available,
with higher scores indicating stronger evidence bases. The system
follows the hierarchy of evidence used in evidence-based medicine:

1. **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses**: Highest level of
   evidence, synthesizing data from multiple studies
2. **Randomized controlled trials (RCTs)**: Gold standard for
   establishing causation
3. **Controlled [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]]**: Studies with control groups but
   potentially less rigorous design than RCTs
4. **Observational studies**: Cohort and case-control studies
5. **Case reports and series**: Individual patient reports
6. **Preclinical evidence**: Animal studies and in vitro research
7. **Anecdotal evidence**: Patient testimonials and practitioner
   experience

## Coverage: 100+ Conditions

The CHI evaluates cannabis research across a comprehensive range of
conditions organized alphabetically in Chapter IV of the book. The
scope includes:

### Cancer (14 subtypes)
General cancer plus bone, brain/glioma/glioblastoma, breast,
cervical, colon, Kaposi's sarcoma, leukemia/lymphoma, liver, lung,
melanoma, pancreatic, prostate, rhabdomyosarcoma, and thyroid
cancers, as well as cancer-induced night sweats.

### Neurological Conditions
Alzheimer's disease, ALS (Lou Gehrig's disease), epilepsy/status
epilepticus, Huntington's disease, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's
disease, Tourette syndrome, plus general neuroprotection and
alcohol dependence/abuse.

### Mental Health
ADD/ADHD, anxiety, autism, depression, bipolar disorder, PTSD,
schizophrenia, and general mental disorders.

### Pain and Inflammation
General pain, chronic non-malignant pain, migraine, neuropathies,
fibromyalgia, and a wide range of inflammatory conditions including
arthritis, atherosclerosis, IBD/IBS, and periodontitis.
