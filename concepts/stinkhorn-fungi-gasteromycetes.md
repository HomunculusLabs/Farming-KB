## Stinkhorn Fungi and Gasteromycete Fruiting Bodies

## Overview

Stinkhorn fungi and related gasteromycetes represent some of the most visually
striking and biologically ingenious organisms in Kingdom Fungi. Members of the
[[bloomfield-stinkhorns-phallus-impudicus-osmotic-fruiting]]*, has been impossible to ignore
throughout human history. Pliny the Elder featured it in his thirty-seven-volume
*Natural History* in the first century A.D. John Gerard pictured it in his
seventeenth-century herbal with the description "Fungus virilis penis arecti
forma, which wee English call Pricke Mushrum, taken from his forme."

Charles Darwin's daughter Etty mounted an antifungal campaign against
stinkhorns, burning them with gloves and a pointed stick to protect her female
servants from their suggestive appearance. The mushroom's Latin name translates
literally to "shameless phallus," and its appearance has provoked reactions
ranging from scientific fascination to Victorian outrage.
