Casing is a non-nutritive layer applied on top of fully colonized
grain substrate to provide the microclimate necessary for mushroom
fruiting. Unlike the bulk substrate, casing soil does not provide
significant nutrition to the fungus — its primary functions are
moisture retention, provision of a humid microclimate at the
substrate surface, and creation of conditions that trigger the
biological switch from [[mollison-designers-microclimate-creation-frost-protection]]**: It creates a humid zone at the
  substrate surface where pinning ([[knf-reproductive-growth-stage]].

## Standard Casing Recipe

A well-tested and reliable casing recipe uses the following
proportions:

- 10 parts peat moss
- 5 parts vermiculite
- 2 parts limestone (marl)

The peat moss provides the organic matrix that holds moisture
and supports [[edaphic-factors-microbial-community-structure]] is already disrupted.

## Application

When the grain substrate is fully colonized, the casing is applied:

1. Shake the colonized grain loose from the jar into a clean
   plastic tray (1-litre disposable trays work well).
2. Break up any large lumps using the clean rim of the jar.
3. Level the grain surface evenly.
