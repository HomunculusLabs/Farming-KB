# Panaeolus cyanescens Cultivation Guide

Panaeolus cyanescens (copelandia) and P. tropicalis are tropical coprophilous mushrooms that require a dung-based substrate for cultivation. They are more demanding than [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]] in terms of fresh air requirements but can be cultivated on sterilised substrate in bags.

## Substrate Preparation

### Ingredients (for 4 standard spawn bags)
- 500g dry straw (soaked in water for 12 hours)
- 4 litres dried cow dung
- 3 litres vermiculite
- 3-4 litres water

### Mixing
1. Mix dry dung and vermiculite thoroughly
2. Add water and stir — ensure the mixture is **not too wet** (loose water leads to bacterial contamination)
3. Add soaked straw and mix thoroughly
4. Divide into 4 autoclavable spawn bags (with filter patches)
5. Fold flaps, place in pressure cooker with a lid on top to prevent blocking the steam valve

### Sterilisation
- **2 full hours** at 121°C in the pressure cooker
- Allow bags to cool in the [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]]

## Inoculation

- Note: Panaeolus species do **not colonise grain as densely** as P. cubensis
- Open bags in flow cabinet (touch only the outside)
- Each bag receives **300 ml** of [[spawn-grain-to-grain-transfer]] by free pouring
- Seal bags with impulse sealer
- Shake to distribute spawn throughout substrate

## Colonisation

- Fully colonises in **5-10 days** depending on temperature and shaking frequency
- Shake bags regularly to distribute mycelium
- White mycelium should be clearly visible after 3 days

## Casing

1. Clean a plastic bin with alcohol (bins should have holes plugged with polyfill)
2. Cut colonised bags open with scissors
3. Place substrate into bin — **2 bags per bin**
4. Level substrate by hand (wear gloves)
5. Apply a thin layer (**1-1.5 cm**) of sterilised casing soil
6. Level casing by hand
7. Cover with polyethylene wrap to prevent contamination and moisture loss
8. Place in warm location for casing colonisation (5-6 days until mycelium shows on surface)
9. Create a "mini-greenhouse" by placing an upside-down bin on top (with mesh-covered holes)

## Fruiting

- First mushrooms appear within days of mini-greenhouse setup
- Keep casing soil moist using a spray bottle
- Mushrooms mature in a couple of days
- Pick when fully mature

## Critical Cultivation Notes

### Fresh Air Exchange (Most Important Factor)

The most common reason people fail with Panaeolus species is insufficient fresh air:

- These species are **much more sensitive to high CO2 concentrations** than P. cubensis
- Insufficient air exchange causes many mushrooms to form but **few to mature** — the rest abort
- Improper air exchange produces **tall, spindly mushrooms with small caps**
- The polyfill-filtered holes in the fruiting bin are essential

### Temperature

- Will grow in a wide temperature range
- **Optimal**: around 25°C or slightly higher
- These are tropical species and prefer warm conditions

### Strain Selection

- Not all substrains from a multispore germination are equally viable
- Best practice: start with multispore culture, fruit, then **clone the best-looking mushrooms**
- Cloning ensures you work with a substrain proven to fruit under your conditions
- Tissue culture from the interior of a fruitbody works well

## Differences from Psilocybe cubensis Cultivation

| Parameter | P. cyanescens | P. cubensis |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| Grain colonisation | Less dense | Very dense |
| Substrate | Dung + straw | Grain or compost |
| Casing | Required | Optional |
| Fresh air needs | Very high | Moderate |
| Optimal temperature | ~25°C+ | 22-25°C |
| CO2 sensitivity | Very sensitive | Moderate |
