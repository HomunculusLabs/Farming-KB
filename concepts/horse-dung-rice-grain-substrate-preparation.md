## Overview

Horse dung and rice grain substrate is a traditional two-component cultivation medium used for growing *[[mycelial-colonization]] and prolific fruiting body production.

## Historical Context

The use of dung-based substrates for [[edaphic-factors-microbial-community-structure]] that partially breaks down complex plant compounds, making nutrients more accessible to fungi.
- The texture of horse dung provides good aeration and water retention properties.

Rice grain serves as a supplemental nutrient source, providing easily metabolized starches and proteins that accelerate mycelial colonization.

## Substrate Formulation

The Gartz (1990) study used a horse dung and rice grain mixture in water, supplemented with potassium dihydrogen phosphate (KH₂PO₄) at 5 g per liter. The complete formulation is:

### Base Components
- **Horse dung**: Provides bulk, structure, and complex carbon sources. Fresh or aged dung can be used; aged dung (1–2 weeks) is often preferred as ammonia levels decrease while beneficial microbial activity increases.
- **Rice grain**: Whole or cracked rice grain provides readily available starch. The grain should be clean and free of mold.
- **Water**: Used to hydrate the mixture to field capacity.

### Supplementation
- **KH₂PO₄ ([[fukuoka-straw-decomposition-microbial-succession]]. Alternatively, use commercially available pre-pasteurized [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]]/autoclave**: 121°C (15 psi) for 60–90 minutes.
- Larger volumes require longer sterilization times (2 hours for 1.5 L jars or spawn bags).
- Jar lids must remain loose during sterilization to prevent pressure buildup and jar breakage.
- After sterilization, allow jars to cool completely before inoculation. Loosened lids prevent vacuum formation during cooling.

## Nutritional Profile

The dung/grain substrate provides a balanced nutritional environment:

| Nutrient Category | Source | Role |
|------------------|--------|------|
| Complex carbohydrates (cellulose, hemicellulose) | Horse dung | Sustained energy source |
| Simple carbohydrates (starch) | Rice grain | Rapid colonization fuel |
| Nitrogen | Both components (dung higher) | Protein synthesis, enzyme production |
| Phosphorus | KH₂PO₄ supplementation | Energy metabolism, nucleic acids |
| Potassium | KH₂PO₄ supplementation | Osmoregulation, enzyme cofactor |
| Micronutrients | Horse dung | Cofactors for various enzymatic reactions |
| Beneficial microflora | Aged dung | Partial substrate pre-digestion |

## Comparison with Alternative Substrates

| Substrate | Colonization Speed | Yield | Complexity | Cost |
|-----------|-------------------|-------|------------|------|
| Dung/grain (with KH₂PO₄) | Moderate | High | High | Low |
| Rye grain alone | Fast | Moderate | Low | Low |
| Malt agar | Fast | N/A (agar only) | Low | Moderate |
| Grass seed | Moderate | Good | Low | Moderate |
| Composted straw | Slow | High | High | Very low |

The dung/grain substrate offers the best balance of yield and cost for species that naturally colonize dung. Its primary disadvantage is the preparation complexity compared to grain-only substrates.
