# Natural Pest Deterrents

Natural pest deterrents are non-synthetic substances and homemade preparations used to repel, disable, or kill insect pests, fungal pathogens, and other garden problems. They are essential tools [[hamilton-kelp-and-seaweed-in-organic-gardening]] and [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]] (IPM). While generally safer than synthetic pesticides, natural deterrents should still be used judiciously to avoid harming [[beneficial-insects-and-biological-pest-control]].

## Neem Oil

Neem oil is extracted from the seeds of the neem tree ([[azadirachta-indica]]). The active compound, azadirachtin, disrupts insect hormone systems, preventing molting, feeding, and reproduction. It also acts as a repellent and antifeedant.

### Uses

- Controls: aphids, whiteflies, thrips, spider mites, scale insects, Japanese beetles, squash bugs, cucumber beetles, leafminers
- Fungal suppression: [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]], black spot, rust (when used as a foliar spray)
- Repels: mosquitoes, flea beetles, cabbage worms

### Application

- **Concentration**: 1-2 tablespoons of neem oil per gallon of water, plus 1 teaspoon of mild liquid soap (as an emulsifier)
- **Timing**: Apply in the evening (neem can burn foliage in direct sun; also, many pests feed at night)
- **Frequency**: Every 7-14 days for prevention; every 5-7 days for active infestations
- **Coverage**: Spray thoroughly, including leaf undersides, stems, and growing tips
- **Caution**: Neem is broad-spectrum — it can harm beneficial insects if they are directly contacted. Spray only affected plants, not the entire garden. Avoid spraying flowers (protects pollinators).

### Limitations

- Degrades rapidly in sunlight (UV breaks down azadirachtin within hours)
- Less effective on mature insects and heavy infestations
- Must make direct contact with pests — it is not systemic when applied as a foliar spray
- Some neem products are refined to remove azadirachtin (clarified hydrophobic extract) — these have reduced insecticidal activity but retain fungicidal properties

## Diatomaceous Earth (DE)

Diatomaceous earth is a fine powder made from fossilized aquatic organisms (diatoms). Under a microscope, DE particles have sharp, glass-like edges that pierce insect exoskeletons, causing desiccation and death.

### Uses

- Controls: slugs, snails, aphids, thrips, flea beetles, spider mites, ants, cockroaches, earwigs, and other soft-bodied or small insects
- Effective as a barrier dust around plants or applied directly to pests
- Indoor and outdoor use

### Application

- **Dry application**: Dust a thin layer on and around affected plants. Use a duster (puff bottle) for even application. Apply to leaf undersides where pests hide.
- **Wet application**: Mix 2-4 tablespoons per gallon of water with 1 teaspoon of soap. Spray plants; DE adheres to foliage as it dries.
- **Barrier**: Create a ring of DE around individual plants or garden beds to exclude crawling pests (slugs, ants).
- **Reapply**: After rain or heavy dew, DE loses effectiveness when wet. Reapply after drying.

### Cautions

- **Use food-grade DE only**: Pool-grade DE contains crystalline silica, which is a respiratory hazard. Food-grade (amorphous) DE is safe for garden use.
- **Wear a dust mask**: DE dust is irritating to lungs. Wear an N95 mask when applying.
- **Harms beneficial insects**: DE kills all insects it contacts, including ladybugs, bees, and [[ground-beetles-carabidae-and-biological-pest-control]]. Apply selectively, not broadly.
- **Avoid flowers**: Do not apply to open flowers where pollinators visit.
- **Less effective in high humidity**: DE absorbs moisture from insects; in very humid conditions, it works more slowly.

## Insecticidal Soap

Insecticidal soap is a potassium salt of fatty acids that breaks down insect cell membranes on contact, causing dehydration and death. It is one of the safest and most effective organic pest controls.

### Uses

- Controls: aphids, whiteflies, thrips, spider mites, mealybugs, soft scale, young caterpillars, fungus gnats
- Safe for most beneficial insects (dries quickly, minimal residual toxicity)
- Can be used on edible crops up to the day of harvest

### Application

- **Concentration**: 1-2 tablespoons of liquid soap per gallon of water. Use true soap (potassium salts of fatty acids), not detergent.
- **Recommended soaps**: Dr. Bronner's liquid castile, Safer's Insecticidal Soap, or any pure castile soap
- **Do NOT use**: Dish detergents (Dawn, etc.), laundry detergents, or any product with degreasers — these damage plant foliage
- **Timing**: Spray in the early morning or evening (cooler temperatures reduce risk of leaf burn)
- **Coverage**: Must make direct contact with pests. Thoroughly cover leaf undersides, stems, and growing tips.
- **Frequency**: Every 5-7 days for 2-3 applications to break the pest life cycle

### Cautions

- Test on a small area first — some plants (fern, bleeding heart, sweet pea) are sensitive to soap
- Do not spray in direct sun or temperatures above 85°F (leaf burn risk)
- Repeated applications can damage delicate new growth
- Not effective on hard-bodied insects (beetles, mature scale)

## Garlic and Chile Spray

Garlic-chile sprays combine the repellent properties of garlic (allicin and other sulfur compounds) with the capsaicin burn of hot chiles. They repel a wide range of pests by smell and taste.

### Basic Recipe

Ingredients:
- 1 whole head of garlic (10-12 cloves), peeled
- 1-2 hot chile peppers (cayenne, jalapeno, or habanero — hotter = stronger), with seeds
- 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
- 1 teaspoon mild liquid soap
- 1 quart water

Preparation:
1. Crush garlic and chiles in a blender or food processor with 1 cup of water
2. Let steep for 1-24 hours (longer steep = stronger spray)
3. Strain through a fine mesh or cheesecloth
4. Add oil, soap, and remaining water
5. Pour into a spray bottle

### Application

- Spray affected plants thoroughly, including leaf undersides
- Apply every 5-7 days, or after rain
- Reapply more frequently during heavy pest pressure
- Use as a preventive on pest-prone crops (eggplant, brassicas, cucurbits)

### Effectiveness

- **Repels**: aphids, cabbage worms, flea beetles, spider mites, rabbits, deer
- **Moderate control**: whiteflies, thrips
- **Not effective**: hard-bodied beetles, scale insects, root pests

### Variations

- **Garlic-only spray**: Omit chiles for a milder repellent (better for tender seedlings)
- **Extra-hot spray**: Add 1 tablespoon of cayenne powder to the basic recipe
- **Garlic-chile concentrate**: Make a strong batch and dilute 1:4 with water before use. Store concentrate in the refrigerator for up to 2 weeks.

## Additional Natural Sprays

### Kaolin Clay (Surround WP)

Sprayed as a particle film that coats plant foliage, creating a physical barrier that repels insects and reduces feeding damage.

- **Controls**: Japanese beetles, cucumber beetles, leafhoppers, thrips, apple maggot, plum curculio
- **Application**: Mix 1/4-3/4 cup per gallon of water. Spray to form a complete white film on foliage. Reapply after rain.
- **Advantages**: Non-toxic, does not harm beneficial insects, safe for pollinators, reduces sunburn on fruit
- **Disadvantages**: Leaves a visible white residue on foliage and fruit (wash off before eating)

### Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis)

A soil bacterium that produces proteins toxic to specific insect groups when ingested.
