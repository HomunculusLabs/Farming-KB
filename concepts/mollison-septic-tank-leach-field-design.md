# Septic Tank and Leach Field Productive Design

## Overview

Mollison addresses two basic productive disposal systems for septic
tank effluent. The first involves underground and surface leach fields
around which trees are grown. The second uses biogas conversion followed
by a pond growing [[mollison-aquatic-plant-systems]] for
biogas feedstock, then a leach field. Both approaches transform what is
normally regarded as a waste disposal problem into a productive
resource system.

## The Leach Field as Tree Irrigation

A leach field is a trench or open gravelled soakage pit through which
sewage wastes from a septic tank flows. In clays and clay-loams, tank
water from a family home will stimulate fruit tree growth without other
irrigation for 20 metres or more. The system follows normal procedures
in that a long trench with a 1:12 ratio base slope is dug away from the
septic tank outlet pipe.

### Construction Method

1. Topsoil is put to one side
2. The trench is fitted with an 18 cm or larger half-pipe
3. Coarse gravels or stones are placed in the trench
4. A strip of plastic or tarpaper is placed over the gravel
5. The trench is back-filled
6. Trees are planted 1-2 m off both sides at 2-6 m spacing

All fruit and nut trees benefit from this nutrient-rich seepage. The
system converts waste water into [[mollison-tree-crop-systems-permaculture]] production.

## The Arbor System

Developed in South Australia, the Arbor system uses a supported
half-pipe that never clogs with tree roots. This enables trees to remove
waste water from trenches which have cross-supports every 1.2 m to
create "pools" of water along the trench length. The system is
specifically designed to prevent root intrusion while maximising water
absorption by surrounding trees.

## Disposal Pit Design

Square or round pits about 25 m square can be dug and filled with
graded stone, from coarse 6 cm at the base to 2 cm at the top. Over
this, a layer of cardboard and a thick layer of straw is spread, and
the straw sown to oats or green crop. Around the pit, trees can be
planted. In semi-arid areas, this system goes to an 8 x 8 m disposal
pit with 0.5 m of gravel, straw mulch, and trees around the pit. This
method is widely used in rural Australia.

## Integration with Biogas Systems

For [[mollison-biogas-and-methane-production-from-sewage]] applications, septic
tank effluent, weeds, and manures are loaded into a tank 2 to 1.5 m
deep and 3-4 m in diameter. A loading chute for weeds and wastes,
about 20-30 cm slant, leads to the base. Septic tank effluent also
enters at the base. The system includes:

- A pond with baffles where Pistia (water lettuce), watercress, or
  any rampant soft water weed is grown
- These plants are returned to the tank weekly as feedstock
- A perforated pipe at the tank base is worked by a small gas
  compressor to "bubble" gas back into the tank for 1-3 hours daily
- This breaks up the scum on top of the ferment
- Gas caught in an inverted tank feeds to the house cooking range,
  lights, and refrigerators
- Surplus from the pond feeds to a leach field

## Large Shallow Leach Fields in Arid Areas

Where water is sufficient, low-flush toilets are led to soakage beds
where large trees rapidly transpire the water and convert nutrients.
Large shallow leach-fields with thick top [[mollison-mulching-and-soil-health]] are officially permissible in arid Australia. These
function effectively as broad swales, absorbing surplus runoff and
infiltrating water to soil while simultaneously growing productive
trees.

## Site Considerations

Soils that do not suit septic tanks require alternative approaches.
Mollison notes that the safest disposal of sewage is in pipes or
trenches below plants. The lagoon or pond for settled water is the
ideal site for productive planting: dates, plums, and peaches on the
banks, and around the pond where the water seeps to the creek
underground. Even the most cautious health inspectors would approve
this safe conversion of waste water to vitamins.

## Connecting to Broader Waste Systems

The septic tank leach field is one element in a chain of waste
treatment that includes:

1. Initial settlement and anaerobic digestion in septic tanks
2. Leach field distribution to tree crops
3. Or biogas conversion first, then pond treatment, then leach field
4. [[mollison-greywater-domestic-water-recycling]] from the
   house routed separately to swales or irrigation

This integrated approach ensures no nutrient is wasted and every
element in the waste stream serves a productive function.
