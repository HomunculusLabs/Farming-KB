## The Leach Field as Tree Irrigation

A leach field is a trench or open gravelled soakage pit through which
sewage wastes from a septic tank flows. In clays and clay-loams, tank
water from a family home will stimulate fruit tree growth without other
irrigation for 20 metres or more. The system follows normal procedures
in that a long trench with a 1:12 ratio base slope is dug away from the
septic tank outlet pipe.

### Construction Method

1. Topsoil is put to one side
2. The trench is fitted with an 18 cm or larger half-pipe
3. Coarse gravels or stones are placed in the trench
4. A strip of plastic or tarpaper is placed over the gravel
5. The trench is back-filled
6. Trees are planted 1-2 m off both sides at 2-6 m spacing

All fruit and nut trees benefit from this nutrient-rich seepage. The
system converts waste water into [[mollison-biogas-and-methane-production-from-sewage]] applications, septic
tank effluent, weeds, and manures are loaded into a tank 2 to 1.5 m
deep and 3-4 m in diameter. A loading chute for weeds and wastes,
about 20-30 cm slant, leads to the base. Septic tank effluent also
enters at the base. The system includes:

- A pond with baffles where Pistia (water lettuce), watercress, or
  any rampant soft water weed is grown
- These plants are returned to the tank weekly as feedstock
- A perforated pipe at the tank base is worked by a small gas
  compressor to "bubble" gas back into the tank for 1-3 hours daily
- This breaks up the scum on top of the ferment
- Gas caught in an inverted tank feeds to the house cooking range,
  lights, and refrigerators
- Surplus from the pond feeds to a leach field
