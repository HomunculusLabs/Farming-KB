At the core of Dr. Cho Han Kyu's Natural Farming system lies a
philosophy of non-interference with natural processes. Cho
believed that the best way to achieve top quality yield is to
respect the nature of life rather than forcing crops to produce
more than they naturally can. This philosophy distinguishes
Natural Farming from both conventional agriculture and standard
[[coleman-deep-organic-farming-philosophy]].

Cho's approach recognizes the abundance of nature and utilizes
indigenous resources for production. The basic philosophy is to
maximize the inborn potential of every life form and its harmony
with the environment by not interfering with growth and
development. Natural Farmers believe that respect for natural
cycles produces superior results compared to forced intervention.

## Maximizing Inborn Potential

Every living organism possesses inherent potential that is often
suppressed by human agricultural practices. Cho taught that
plants and animals have an innate capacity for healthy growth,
disease resistance, and high-quality production. This capacity
is diminished when humans impose artificial conditions, whether
through [[cervantes-chemical-fertilizers-guide]], genetic modification, or
controlled environments that eliminate natural selection
pressures.

The practical implication is that farmers should focus on
creating conditions where natural potential can express itself
rather than trying to manufacture outcomes through external
inputs. In crop production, this means building healthy soil
ecosystems, using locally adapted microbial inoculants, and
timing inputs to match the plant's natural growth cycle rather
than overriding it with [[query-organic-vs-synthetic-nutrients-cannabis]].

## Interdependence Over Control

Natural Farming applies the principle of interdependence: people
should work with nature rather than attempting to surmount or
undermine it. Cho envisioned a farming system where humans,
crops, livestock, and microorganisms exist in mutually beneficial
relationships rather than hierarchical control structures where
humans dominate every aspect of production.

This principle manifests in the integration of livestock and crop
systems. Poultry provides food and manure for soil conditioning,
agricultural by-products become chicken feed, and the cycle
continues without external inputs. Microorganisms in the soil
process organic matter, suppress pathogens, and make nutrients
available to plants. Earthworms replace mechanical tillage.

## Respect for Life as Agricultural Practice

Cho gave utmost respect and care to crops and animals, following
the natural cycle of life in all farming operations. He argued
that better productivity and harvest result from nursing and
paying attention to the environment rather than from applying
force. This is not merely an ethical stance but a practical one:
stressed plants and animals produce inferior quality products.

The philosophy extends to pest management. Rather than using
pesticides to kill pests, Natural Farming uses aromatic and
fluorescent insect attractants to divert pests away from crops.
This approach focuses on co-existence and co-relation with pests
rather than elimination. The recognition is that pests are part
of the ecosystem and that their complete eradication is neither
desirable nor possible without causing broader ecological damage.

## What Natural Farming Is and Is Not

Cho drew careful distinctions between Natural Farming and other
systems. While Natural Farming shares the non-chemical approach
of organic farming, it is specifically tied to the methodology
developed at the Janong Farming Institute. Natural Farming has
its own theoretical framework, the [[cho-nutritive-cycle-theory]] Theory, and
its own set of inputs made from locally available materials.
