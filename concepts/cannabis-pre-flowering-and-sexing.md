Identifying plant sex before full flowering is essential for sinsemilla production. Male plants must be removed to prevent pollination of females.

## The Six Stages of the Cannabis Life Cycle

1. **Germination** (12 hours to 3 weeks)
2. **Seedling Stage** (1–3 weeks)
3. **Vegetative Growth** (1–5 months)
4. **Pre-flowering** (1 day to 2 weeks)
5. **Flowering** (4–16 weeks)
6. **Seed Production** (if pollinated, 2–16 weeks)

## Pre-Flowering Stage

Pre-flowering begins when the plant slows vertical growth and starts producing more branches and nodes. The plant fills out and develops calyxes at the nodes where branches meet the stem. This is the window for early sex identification.

### Identifying Males

Male plants produce small ball-like structures clustered together like grapes at the nodes. These are pollen sacs that will eventually burst open. Male plants should be identified and removed as early as possible to prevent any pollen release.

Key male indicators:
- Round pollen sacs at nodes (no pistils/hairs)
- Sac clusters resemble tiny bunches of grapes
- Generally taller and less bushy than females (not always reliable alone)
- May show sex signs slightly earlier than females

### Identifying Females

Female plants produce small white or creamy pistils (hairs) emerging from teardrop-shaped calyxes at the nodes. These pistils are the plant's stigmas designed to catch pollen.

Key female indicators:
- White/cream pistils (hairs) emerging from calyxes
- Calyxes are teardrop or pear-shaped
- More branching and bushier growth pattern
- Pistils may appear in pairs at each node

## The 12/12 Light Cycle for Sexing

The standard method to trigger pre-flowering and sex expression is switching to a 12 hours light / 12 hours dark photoperiod:

- The dark period must be completely uninterrupted — even brief light exposure can cause problems
- Most plants show sex within 7–14 days of the 12/12 switch
- Some strains may show pre-flower signs during vegetative growth under 18/6 or 24/0 if given enough time

See [[cannabis-photoperiodism-and-light-cycles]] for the science behind photoperiod control.

## Hermaphrodites

Hermaphrodite plants develop both male pollen sacs and female pistils. This is usually caused by:

- **Genetics**: Some strains are prone to hermaphroditism
- **Stress**: Light leaks, temperature extremes, nutrient problems, or physical damage
- **Light interruption**: Light leaks during the dark period are the most common cause

Hermaphrodites should be removed from the grow room immediately. Even a single open pollen sac can pollinate your entire crop and ruin your sinsemilla harvest. If detected very early (before sacs open), some growers carefully pluck the male sacs with tweezers, but this is risky and the plant should be monitored closely.

## Pollen Control

Pollen travels easily through air and can be carried on:
- Clothing and skin
- Shared tools and equipment
- Insects

Best practices:
- Remove males as soon as sex is confirmed
- Wash hands and face after handling male plants
- Keep separate grow areas for breeding and sinsemilla production
- Never handle males before tending to females
- Consider changing clothes between rooms if breeding

## Early Sexing Methods

Some growers use techniques to force early sex identification:

- **Forced pre-flowering**: Switch a clone to 12/12 in a separate small space while the mother remains in veg. This reveals sex without committing the full plant to flowering.
- **Pre-flowers during veg**: With enough patience (8+ weeks of veg), some plants will naturally show pre-flowers while still under 18/6 or 24/0 light.

## Timing Considerations

- **Indica**: May show sex within 7 days of 12/12 switch
- **Sativa**: May take 10–14 days or longer
- **Hybrids**: Varies by dominant parent

Once sex is confirmed and males are removed, females can remain under 12/12 to develop full buds for harvest.
