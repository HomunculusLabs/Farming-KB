# Taphrinales: Gall-Forming Biotrophic Ascomycetes

The Taphrinales are a small but distinctive order of
biotrophic ascomycetes that cause plant diseases
characterized by hyperplasia, hypertrophy, and abnormal
development of host tissues. Members of this order induce
leaf curl, witches' broom, leaf spot, and gall formation on
a wide range of angiosperm hosts, particularly in temperate
regions. The order is monogeneric or nearly so, with
Taphrina being the primary genus of interest. These fungi
represent a deeply divergent lineage within the
[[tompkins-plant-growth-regulators]],
particularly cytokinins and auxins. This manipulation
results in characteristic symptoms including leaf curling,
thickening, chlorosis, and the formation of witches' broom
shoots.

The growing gourmet black involves both a yeast-like (saprobic) phase
and a mycelial (pathogenic) phase. In the saprobic phase,
the fungus exists as budding yeast cells on leaf surfaces.
Infection occurs when yeast cells or ascospores land on
susceptible host tissue and penetrate through natural
openings or directly through the cuticle. Once inside the
tissue, the fungus grows as dikaryotic mycelium.

Sexual reproduction produces asci that are typically
cylindrical and arise directly from the host epidermis
without a protective ascocarp (gymnothecial development).
The asci are often clustered in a hymenial layer on the
leaf surface, and ascospores are forcibly discharged.
These ascospores can bud to form yeast cells, completing
the cycle.

## Host Range and Symptom Types

Taphrina species have relatively narrow host ranges, often
restricted to a single plant genus or species. The symptoms
they induce vary with the host and fungal species:

- **Leaf curl**: Taphrina deformans on peach (Prunus
  persica) causes puckering and reddening of leaves,
  one of the most recognizable plant diseases worldwide.
- **Witches' broom**: Some species induce proliferation of
  shoots, creating dense clusters of shortened branches.
- **Peach leaf curl**: T. deformans is the type species and
  the most economically significant member of the order.
- **Plum pockets**: T. pruni causes swollen, distorted
  fruits in Prunus species.
- **Witch's broom on Alnus**: T. alni induces shoot
  proliferation on alder trees.

## Taxonomic Position

The Taphrinales occupy a unique phylogenetic position.
Molecular phylogenetic analyses have consistently placed
them as a deeply divergent lineage within the Ascomycota,
sometimes sister to the Saccharomycetales (yeasts) or
occupying an intermediate position. Their combination of
yeast-like and filamentous growth forms, their dikaryotic
mycelium, and their direct ascus formation without an
ascocarp distinguish them from other ascomycete orders.

## Ecological and Economic Significance

Most Taphrina species are of minor economic importance,
with the notable exception of T. deformans on peach and T.
pruni on plum. These diseases can cause significant crop
losses in orchards when environmental conditions favor
infection during bud break. Fungicidal sprays applied
before bud break are the primary management strategy.
In natural ecosystems, Taphrina species are widespread
but rarely cause serious damage to their hosts, suggesting
a long evolutionary history of coexistence.
