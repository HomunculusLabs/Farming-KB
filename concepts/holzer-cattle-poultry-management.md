## Wild and Old Cattle Breeds

Holzer keeps old cattle breeds that are suited to outdoor, year-round living on alpine pasture. He favours breeds that have not been specialised for maximum milk or meat production in confinement:

- **Scottish Highland cattle**: Extremely hardy, long hair, calving ease, good mothers
- **Dahomey miniature cattle**: Small, undemanding, well-suited to smallholdings
- **Old domestic cattle breeds**: Generally more disease-resistant and self-sufficient

### Keeping Principles

- Cattle live outside year-round with access to simple [[permaculture-plants-root-crops-and-tubers]]
- In winter, animals can survive on Jerusalem artichokes, kale, and turnips left in the field if snow is not too deep
- No concentrated feed required when diverse forage is available
- Shelters must be easily accessible for mucking out with a tractor (cattle are less clean than pigs)

## Roundwood Shelters

These multi-purpose structures serve as cattle housing, pig shelters, and crop storage rooms:

### Construction
1. Dig into a slope ( terrace) rather than digging down
2. Excavate a 1m deep ditch at the back and sides for the log walls
3. Place logs in the ditch using an excavator with a grappler arm
4. Cut all logs to the same height; back wall slightly higher for roof fit
5. Hold walls together with cap logs (logs split lengthwise, nailed on top)
6. Notched tree trunks form the roof, nailed to the walls
7. Cover with membrane, sheeting, and earth (not more than 0.5m deep)
8. Add buttresses against the sides for soil stability

### Specifications
- Width: up to 4m (roof load limit)
- Depth: few limits
- Height: 3m for cattle (allows tractor access for mucking out)
- Timber: larch or robinia lasts ~30 years at 30-40cm diameter
- Cost: 700-800 euros (with excavator rental at 400-500/day) if timber is available
- Build time: one day with excavator and one additional person

### Orientation
The entrance faces east so animals wake with the rising sun. The shelter provides shade at midday in summer and [[sepp-holzer-earth-cellars-and-philosophy]] maintain 80-90% humidity and 8-10C temperature using only the soil's thermal mass. Key features:

- Gravel floor for drainage
- 10m-long underground air inlet pipe (brings air to soil temperature)
- Air outlet pipe at the highest point (prevents condensation)
- Pipe diameter: at least 15cm for a 10m3 cellar
- Inlet pipe sloped slightly downward doubles as a drain during cleaning
