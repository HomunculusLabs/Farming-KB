## Cannabis Rosin Pressing

## Overview

Rosin pressing is a solventless [[cervantes-cannabis-flower-anatomy]] or hash. While the term "rosin" as specifically applied to [[greg-green-trichomes-and-resin-glands]] and warms the resin, causing volatile aromatic terpenoids to release
- Heat causes the outer layer of pressed material to oxidize and darken
- Hash can have a dark exterior and a blonde interior packed with creamy resin glands
- Relatively pure resin powder congeals faster than less-potent powders with impurities
- A little vegetable matter and debris gives hash different flavors and more body

These same physical principles govern the extraction of rosin from fresh flower, kief, or water hash.

## Heat Applications in the Source Material

### Controlled Heat for Pressing

Cervantes specifically notes: "Add a little heat to hard-to-press hash that contains impurities. The heat will help the mass congeal so bricks do not need excessive pressure to stay together." This principle directly applies to rosin pressing, where controlled heat makes resin flow from plant material.

The book also warns: "Do not heat the resin too much or it will be damaged. Do not forget that pressing itself increases heat." This cautions against excessive temperatures that degrade cannabinoids.

### Temperature Limits

From Cervantes's descriptions of cannabis processing:

- Temperatures above 80F (27C) degrade THC during storage and handling
- The oven drying method should not exceed 200F (93C) or THC will vaporize into the air
- Slow drying at 65-75F (18-24C) preserves [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]] in cannabis volatilize and release fragrance above 70F (21C)

Modern rosin pressing applies these principles with precise [[oner-cannabis-water-extraction-ice-water-hash]] pressing methods described by Cervantes are direct ancestors of rosin pressing:

- Hand pressing works dried resin powder between palms
- Potent resin powder is creamy white to gold, darkening through oxidation
- Water hash does not react like dry-sieved hash when pressed
- Water-extracted resin has been melded differently than dry powder
- Pressing wet hash traps moisture inside, preventing proper gelling

## Material Preparation

Following Cervantes's guidance for preparing material before pressing:

- Ensure material is clean and free of contaminants
- Freeze material to make resin glands brittle before processing
- Remove stems and sharp objects that could damage screens or containers
- Use the most resinous material available for best results
