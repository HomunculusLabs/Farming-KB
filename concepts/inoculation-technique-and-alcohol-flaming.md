### Step 3: Deposit the Spore Solution

Press the syringe plunger gently. As the plunger is pressed, observe the
needle tip against the inside of the glass. As soon as water appears around
the needle tip, release the plunger pressure. This visual cue prevents
over-inoculation of a single point and ensures even distribution.

Inoculate a few drops down each of the four needle holes.

### Step 4: Between Holes

In between each hole inoculation, shake the syringe a little to keep the
spores distributed in the solution. This prevents clumping and ensures each
hole receives a viable dose of spores.

### Step 5: Dosage

Use approximately 1 cc (1 milliliter) of spore solution per jar. This amount
allows a standard 10 cc syringe to inoculate 10 jars. More spore solution per
jar can be used to speed colonization, but fewer jars can be inoculated from a
single syringe.

### Needle Plugging

If the syringe needle plugs up as it is inserted into the substrate, draw the
needle back slightly and it will unplug. This can happen when the needle
encounters compacted substrate material.

## Inoculation Without Lids

For drinking glasses without a lid with punched holes, the critical precaution
is to disturb the dry top vermiculite layer as little as possible. The
underlying wet substrate must not be exposed to the air. Carefully replace any
disturbed vermiculite. Cover the glass mouth with tin foil and replace
immediately after inoculation. Gain experience with the standard lid technique
before attempting this method.
