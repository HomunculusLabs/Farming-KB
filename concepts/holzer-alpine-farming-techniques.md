## Farming at 1,100-1,500m Altitude

The Krameterhof operates at elevations where conventional agriculture has been largely abandoned. Holzer has developed specific techniques that make productive farming possible in these marginal conditions where winters reach -25C and the [[green-manure-crops]] left standing create an insulating quilt over the soil
- Water bodies moderate temperature fluctuations on adjacent land

### Altitude-Shifting Results
The same variety behaves differently at different altitudes:
- White Transparent apple at 1,100m: ripens mid-August, floury within days, barely suitable for pressing
- White Transparent apple at 1,500m: ripens September, firm and juicy, stores over a month, excellent for pressing

## Indicator Plants

Holzer uses wild plants as indicators of soil conditions and microclimate:

### Soil Indicators
- **Nettles**: Nitrogen-rich soil
- **Horsetail**: Heavy, damp soil; often indicates compaction
- **Plantain**: Compacted soil, often along paths
- **Dandelion**: Acidic soil
- **Chicory**: Deep, calcareous soil

### Alpine Plant Cultivation

Growing plants from alpine regions requires understanding their natural germination conditions:

**Yellow Gentian (*Gentiana lutea*) example**:
- Seeds ripen at ~2,000m altitude where spring weather is changeable: frosty mornings, warm dry days, rain and snow, then a hot period in June
- This specific weather pattern (freeze-thaw cycles with wet-dry alternation) is needed to break dormancy
- Conventional greenhouse propagation fails because it does not replicate these conditions
- Holzer accidentally discovered this by leaving seed trays outside in the elements for months
- Lesson: observe natural events closely and replicate them rather than following rigid instructions

## Slope Stabilisation

### Root Depth Matters
On steep slopes, Holzer has soil and plants "working" to a depth of metres rather than centimetres:
- Lupin and sweet clover roots reach metres into the soil (vs. grass roots at centimetres)
- Deep roots stabilise slopes and improve water retention capacity
- Nitrogen-fixing legumes improve nutrient content of poor soils

### Gradual Terracing
Large-scale earthworks are done gradually over years:
- Year one: terraces at top, middle, and bottom; plant immediately
- Year two: fill in between once original terraces are stable
- On steep slopes, start at the bottom and work up
- Seeds pushed into loosened humus by rain germinate best immediately after construction

## Adaptive Livestock

### Year-Round Outdoor Living
Animals on the Krameterhof live outside in all conditions:
- **Pigs**: Earth shelter pigsties stay warm enough at -25C; windfall fruit supplements feed
- **Cattle**: Scottish Highland cattle tolerate extreme cold; [[cervantes-wind-protection-outdoor-cannabis]]
- **Poultry**: Ducks and geese need open water access (steep-angled inflow prevents ice formation)
- **Natural feed**: Polyculture paddocks of cereals, legumes, and root crops; animals can survive winter in the field if snow is not too deep
