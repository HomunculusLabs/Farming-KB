# Microfungi on Wood and Plant Debris — Survey Overview

Microfungi are defined as fungi with microscopic spore-producing
structures. They comprise an unnatural but practical grouping that
cuts across classification schemes for Fungi and fungus-like
organisms. Despite their small size, microfungi are enormously
important [[fungal-ecosystem-services]] framework, and their diversity on decomposing material is catalogued in [[fungal-diversity-leaf-litter-coarse-woody-debris]] lineages:

- **Ascomycota:** Largest division, approximately 3,250 genera with
  32,250 recognised species. Most microfungi are ascomycetes,
  including the vast majority of leaf litter and wood-inhabiting
  species.
- **Anamorphic Fungi (Deuteromycetes):** Approximately 2,600 genera
  and 15,000 species, characterised by mitotic [[chamovitz-plant-scent-communication-volatile-organic-compounds]]:

- **Saprotrophy:** Using nonliving organic material from a plant the
  fungus did not kill. This is the dominant strategy among debris-
  colonising microfungi and drives the decomposition process that
  recycles nutrients in all terrestrial ecosystems.
- **Necrotrophy:** Killing living plant tissues and then using them.
  Frequently involves processes similar to saprotrophy. Necrotrophic
  fungi are important plant pathogens responsible for cankers, leaf
  spots, and root diseases.
- **Biotrophy:** Exploiting only living host cells. Much less common
  among debris-colonising microfungi but includes important
  endophytic species that switch to saprotrophy upon host death.

Many species exhibit nutritional metamorphoses: a large proportion of
[[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]].

Key [[fungal-adaptations-environmental-gradients]] include:
- Moisture availability: from permanently saturated to xeric
- Temperature: from permanently frozen to thermophilic
- pH: from strongly acidic leaf litter to alkaline wood ash
- Substrate quality: from nutrient-rich fresh litter to recalcitrant
  heavily decomposed wood

## Factors Influencing Diversity

### Site-Level Factors
- Temperature, water relations, seasonality, and physical factors
- Plant diversity and distribution across the landscape
- Degree of habitat variety (microhabitats increase diversity)
- Disturbance history and successional age of the ecosystem

### Substratum Characterization
Factors that determine which fungi colonise a given piece of debris:
- **Origin:** roots, stems, trunks, bark, branches, twigs, leaves,
  flowers, fruits, seeds
- **Condition:** age, fragmentation, decomposition degree, chemical
  composition (C:N ratio, lignin content, tannins)
- **Location:** superficial, buried, immersed, litter layer, canopy
- **Size:** fine twigs support different communities than coarse
