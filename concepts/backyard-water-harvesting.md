## Backyard Water Harvesting

In an ecologically designed garden, water is not an externally caused event -- it is designed in, naturally present, naturally abundant. The cheapest place to store water is in the soil. A well-designed garden doesn't require constant irrigation; it spontaneously cycles toward lush and vibrant growth, even when the gardener is absent.

## The Fivefold Path to Water Wisdom

The [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] can reduce irrigation needs by 75 percent compared to poor soils with less than 1 percent organic matter. Humus acts as a sponge, swelling to hold several times its weight in water. One foot of rich, moist soil blanketing a backyard holds as much water as a 3-inch-deep lake the same size. The key is building organic matter through compost, mulch, and the techniques of [[permaculture-earthworks-and-water-harvesting]] for detailed guidance.

**Swales**: A shallow trench laid dead level along the land's contours, one to several feet across and about a foot deep. Earth dug from the swale is piled on the downhill side as a berm. The cross-section is roughly S-shaped. Surface water runs in, spreads along the length, and slowly percolates into the soil. This underground water seeps downhill, forming a lens of moisture held together by hydrostatic tension that aids plant growth for tens of feet downslope.

**Basins and dishes**: Circular depressions, even gentle ones invisible to the eye, collect rainwater and let it soak in. Every tree can be planted in a small basin to capture and hold water directly at its root zone.

**Fishscale swales**: Small, overlapping basins on slopes that catch and hold water like overlapping fish scales.

**Urban swales**: In a backyard setting, swales don't need to be large or industrial-looking. Small, delicate, eye-pleasing curves softened by plant silhouettes work beautifully. Even a modest 6-inch-deep swale along a property line can significantly reduce runoff and build soil moisture. ### 5. Mulching Deeply

A thick layer of mulch slows evaporation, cools soil, adds fertility, boosts soil life, and smothers weeds. Deep mulching and rich soil work together: mulch feeds the soil organisms that create the humus that holds the water. [[graywater-systems]] and graywater-systems|graywater for design details.
