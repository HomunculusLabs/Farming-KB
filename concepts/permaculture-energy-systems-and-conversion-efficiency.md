## Permaculture Energy Systems and Conversion Efficiency

## Overview

Energy systems in permaculture villages prioritise diversity, local production, non-pollution, and long-term soil/water/air preservation. Mollison compiled comprehensive data on energy conversion efficiencies across dozens of devices and systems, establishing clear benchmarks for evaluating [[appropriate-technology]] choices.

## The Permaculture Energy Philosophy

### Core Principles

Permaculture energy systems are guided by several non-negotiable principles:

1. **Non-pollution**: The process must not degrade soil, water, or [[bloomfield-stachybotrys-indoor-mold-trichothecenes-air-quality]].
2. **Local relevance**: The technology must be appropriate to the place and climate.
3. **Social acceptability**: The technology must be socially acceptable and locally benign.
4. **Affordability**: The cost must be affordable and amortise under 10 years.
5. **Local production and maintenance**: The technology should be producible and maintainable locally.

### The Economics of Extinction

A foundational principle states that no matter how efficient a technology may be, if it lays waste to or destroys the basic quality or quantity of soil, water, or clean air, it must be rejected. This explicitly excludes:

- **Fission processes** with radioactive by-products
- **Coal** as currently used
- **Mineral petroleum** beyond initial or transition use

### The Diversity Principle

A diverse approach to energy is always recommended, drawing from multiple sources:
- Wind
- Tide
- River (micro-hydro)
- Solar (thermal and photovoltaic)
- Methane/biogas

This diversity provides resilience against any single source failing and allows each technology to be applied where it is most appropriate to local conditions.

### Amplified Efficiency Systems (Heat Pumps)

Heat pumps and compressed air systems achieve amplified efficiencies — they produce more useful energy than the fuel input would suggest:

| System | Amplification | Effective Efficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Heat pumps (extracting low-grade heat for space heat or cooling) | ×3.5 to ×7 | 350% |
| Compressed air heaters (expanding cool compressed air before engines) | ×5.5 to ×6 | 500–600% |

### Key Observations

1. **Water power is consistently efficient**: Overshot water wheels, Pelton wheels, and rotary hydraulic turbines all achieve 80–90% efficiency.
2. **Biogas outperforms direct combustion**: Cow dung converted to methane (65%) with useful fertiliser residue far outperforms burning dung directly for heat (7%).
3. **Slow combustion stoves triple open fire efficiency**: From ~15% to ~60%.
4. **Co-generation dramatically improves diesel**: From ~35% (power only) to ~90% (combined heat and power).
5. **Thermal mass heating is extremely efficient**: At ~90%, it far exceeds conventional stoves.
6. **Heat pumps represent the highest efficiency**: At 350–600%, they extract environmental heat and amplify it.

## Financing Village Energy

### The 30% Trust Model

By allotting 30% of sites to the body corporate (Trust A), developers ensure:

- All necessary public services are funded
- Residents, over a period of 20 years, have effectively paid nothing for land
- As soon as sales exceed 60% of total titles, capital or interest for public services becomes available

### Credit Union Integration

The trust capital can be transferred to a credit union's loans account, enabling residents to draw on it at low interest for local occupational development. This funds:
- Energy systems
- Dairies
- Food processing centres
- Glasshouses
- Computers
- Machinery

### The 10% Levy

A 10% levy on net profits of locally-funded cooperatives (modelled on the Mondragon system) funds banks, schools, and research facilities. This applies only to net profits of trust-funded cooperatives.

## Evaluating Energy Technology for Villages

When selecting energy technologies for a permaculture village, the evaluation hierarchy should be:

1. **First filter**: Does it preserve soil, water, and air quality? (Eliminates coal, nuclear fission, petroleum dependence)
2. **Second filter**: Can it be locally produced and maintained? (Favours water wheels, biogas, solar thermal over complex manufactured systems)
