## Datura (*Datura metel*)

### Old World Significance

Known as dhatura and dutra in India, the purple-flowered *Datura metel* has been valued since prehistory for its narcotic properties. Its cultural reach is extraordinary:

- Possibly responsible for the intoxicating smoke of the **Oracle of Delphi**
- Identified with an hallucinogen in early Chinese writings
- Mentioned by the Arabian physician **Avicenna** in the 11th century under the name *jouzmathel*
- Recorded as an aphrodisiac in the East Indies in 1578
- Held sacred in China, where people believed Buddha's preaching caused heaven to sprinkle the plant with dew

### Chemistry and Use

- **Principal alkaloid:** Scopolamine (heaviest concentrations in leaves and seeds)
- Commonly mixed with cannabis and smoked in Asia to this day
- Leaves of a white-flowered form (*D. fastuosa*, considered by some a distinct species) are smoked with cannabis or tobacco across Africa and Asia
- In many parts of Asia, seeds are mixed with food and tobacco for illicit use, especially by thieves for stupefying victims

## Syrian Rue (*Peganum harmala*)

### Distribution and Uses

Syrian rue grows from the Mediterranean to northern India, Mongolia, and Manchuria. It has many uses in folk medicine throughout its range:

- Seeds employed as a spice
- Fruits source of a red dye and an oil
- Seeds contain known hallucinogenic alkaloids, especially **harmine** and **harmaline**

### Cultural Significance

The extraordinary esteem in which the peoples of Asia hold this plant may indicate a former religious use as a hallucinogen, though purposeful use to induce visions has not been established through literature or field work. The plant belongs to the caltrop family (Zygophyllaceae), comprising about two dozen genera native to dry parts of the tropics and subtropics of both hemispheres.

### Chemistry

Syrian rue is one of the most important natural sources of **β-carboline alkaloids**, particularly harmine and harmaline.
