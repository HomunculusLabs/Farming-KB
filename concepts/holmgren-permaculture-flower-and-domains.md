# Holmgren Permaculture Flower and Domains

The Permaculture Flower is David Holmgren's conceptual model illustrating the key domains that require transformation to create a sustainable culture. It represents the evolution of the permaculture concept from permanent agriculture to permanent (sustainable) culture, showing how [[holmgren-twelve-design-principles-overview]] apply across all aspects of human life.

## From Agriculture to Culture

The word Permaculture was coined by Bill Mollison and Holmgren in the mid-1970s to describe "an integrated, evolving system of perennial or self-perpetuating plant and animal species useful to man." A more current definition reflecting expanded focus is: "Consciously designed landscapes which mimic the patterns and relationships found in nature, while yielding an abundance of food, fibre and energy for provision of local needs."

People, their buildings and the ways they organise themselves are central to permaculture. Thus the permaculture vision of permanent agriculture has evolved to one of permanent culture. This evolution reflects the recognition that sustainable land use cannot be achieved without corresponding transformation in how people organise their economies, build their homes, educate their children, and govern their communities.

## The Design System

Holmgren sees permaculture more precisely as the use of [[holmgren-wholistic-design-thinking]] that provide the organising framework for implementing the above vision. It draws together the diverse ideas, skills and ways of living which need to be rediscovered and developed in order to empower us to move from being dependent consumers to becoming responsible and productive citizens.

In this more limited but important sense, permaculture is not the landscape, or even the skills of organic gardening, sustainable farming, energy efficient building or eco-village development as such. But it can be used to design, establish, manage and improve these and all other efforts made by individuals, households and communities towards a sustainable future.

## The Seven Petals

The Permaculture Flower shows seven key domains requiring transformation, each connected to the ethical and design principles at the centre:

### Land and Nature Stewardship

The original and most developed domain of permaculture, drawing on organic and biodynamic agriculture, [[comparison-food-forest-vs-orchard]], seed saving, gleaning and wild harvesting, and bush tucker. Historically, permaculture has focused on this domain as both a source for and an application of ethical and design principles. Strategies include [[holmgren-food-forests-agroforestry]], [[holmgren-livestock-animal-systems]], and [[holmgren-mineral-fertilizers-soil-building]].

### Building

Encompassing passive solar design, earth and straw building, owner building, biotecture, and energy efficient construction. The [[holmgren-built-environment-energy-store]] connects building design to broader energy descent planning. Principles of thermal mass, orientation, and natural ventilation make buildings that work with rather than against climate.

### Tools and Technology

Including appropriate technology, renewable energy, bicycle transport, and reuse and recycling systems. The emphasis is on technologies that can be maintained and repaired with local skills and materials, rather than dependent on global supply chains. This domain becomes increasingly critical as energy descent reduces the energy available for maintaining complex technological systems.

### Education and Culture

Covering participatory arts and music, home and Steiner education, action research, reading landscape, and the spirit of place. Education is not just about transmitting information but about developing the [[holmgren-wholistic-design-thinking]] capacities needed for ecological living. The concept of spirit of place (genius loci) recognises that each location has unique qualities that should inform design and cultural expression.

### Health and Spiritual Well-being

Including holistic medicine, yoga and other body/mind/spirit disciplines, home birth and dying with dignity. This domain recognises that sustainability is not just a material project but encompasses physical, mental, and spiritual dimensions of human well-being. Non-material well-being is emphasised as a key strategy -- enjoying a sunset rather than watching a movie, spending time playing with a child rather than buying them a toy.

### Finance and Economics

Encompassing ethical investment, subscription farming, [[holmgren-lets-systems-and-alternative-economics]], and community economics. The [[holmgren-household-economy-and-self-reliance]] is the primary scale at which economic redesign can occur. As global supply chains weaken during energy descent, local and bioregional economic systems become essential for meeting basic needs.

### Land Tenure and Community Governance

Including co-ops and body corporates, [[holmgren-permaculture-education-training-and-pdc]], conflict resolution, and [[holmgren-permaculture-land-tenure-and-legal-structures]]. Community governance connects to [[holmgren-community-design-and-intentional-communities]]. The challenge of vesting control of land in collective structures, rather than accepting individual ownership as natural, remains one of the most difficult aspects of creating sustainable culture.

## The Spiral Evolutionary Path

The spiral evolutionary path beginning with ethics and principles at the centre suggests a knitting together of these domains, initially at the personal and local level and proceeding to the collective and global level. The spidery nature of that spiral suggests the uncertain and variable nature of the process of integration.

This spiral pattern means that the domains are not developed sequentially or independently. Progress in one domain creates conditions for progress in others. For example, household food production (land stewardship) provides economic savings (finance) that free time for community participation (governance). The spiral nature acknowledges that integration is iterative and non-linear.

## Network and Movement

Beyond the conceptual framework, permaculture is also a worldwide network and movement of individuals and groups working in both rich and poor countries on all continents to demonstrate and spread permaculture design solutions. Largely unsupported by government or business, these people are contributing to a more sustainable future by reorganising their lives and work around permaculture design principles.

The Permaculture Design Course has been the prime vehicle for permaculture inspiration and training worldwide for over 15 years. A curriculum was codified in 1984, but divergent evolution of both form and content has produced varied and localised experiences and understandings. This diversity is both a strength (adaptation to local conditions) and a challenge (maintaining conceptual coherence).

## Relevance to Energy Descent

The Permaculture Flower is especially relevant to [[holmgren-fossil-fuel-pulse-and-energy-descent-cycle]] because it shows how permaculture's design framework extends beyond land management into every domain of human life. As energy availability declines, all seven domains will require redesign. The principles provide the common thread for that transformation.

Insofar as permaculture is an effective response to the limitations on use of energy and natural resources, it will move from its current status as "alternative response to environmental crisis" to the social and economic mainstream of the post-industrial era. The Permaculture Flower provides the map for that transition, showing that the journey involves not just technical changes in how we grow food or build houses, but fundamental transformation in how we organise our entire civilisation.

## Ethics at the Centre

At the centre of the Permaculture Flower sit the three ethical principles: Care for the Earth, Care for People, and Set Limits to Consumption and Redistribution of Surplus. These are not abstract ideals but practical constraints on design action. See [[holmgren-permaculture-ethics-foundations]] and [[holmgren-ethics-cultural-mechanisms]].

The spiral evolutionary path suggests that ethical reflection is not a one-time exercise but a continuous process. As we develop competence in each domain, our understanding of the ethical implications deepens, which in turn reshapes our approach to design. This iterative relationship between ethics and practice is what distinguishes permaculture from purely technical approaches to sustainability.

## The Flower as Assessment Tool

The Permaculture Flower can also be used as an assessment tool. By considering each petal, practitioners can identify which domains of their life are well-developed and which need attention. Most people come to permaculture through the land stewardship petal, but the framework encourages engagement with all seven domains. This comprehensive approach is essential because the domains are interconnected: progress in one creates conditions for progress in others, while neglect of any one can undermine the others.

For [[holmgren-energy-descent-planning]], this assessment function is particularly valuable. It helps identify vulnerabilities and opportunities across all domains, ensuring that adaptation is comprehensive rather than narrowly focused on food production or energy supply.
