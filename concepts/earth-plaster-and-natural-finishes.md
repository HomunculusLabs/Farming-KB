# Earth Plaster and Natural Finishes

Natural wall finishes protect building surfaces while maintaining breathability. Unlike conventional paint and gypsum plaster, natural finishes allow moisture vapor to pass through walls, preventing trapped moisture that causes mold and decay. This is essential for earth, straw, and timber-based buildings.

## Why Natural Finishes Matter

Natural walls — cob, straw bale, light straw-clay, hempcrete, adobe — must breathe. Applying standard latex paint or cement stucco creates a vapor barrier that traps moisture inside the wall, leading to rot, mold, and structural failure. Natural finishes are the only appropriate surface treatment for these wall systems.

Additional benefits:

- Non-toxic: zero VOCs, no off-gassing, safe for chemically sensitive individuals
- Repairable: patch and blend new material into existing finishes seamlessly
- Beautiful: rich, textured, living surfaces that age gracefully
- Low cost: materials are often free or very cheap (subsoil, sand, lime, milk, pigments)

## Earthen Plaster

Earthen plaster is a mix of clay-rich subsoil, sand, and fiber (chopped straw or manure), applied to interior and protected exterior walls. It is the most common finish for cob, adobe, and straw bale buildings.

### Mix Ratios

A typical earthen plaster mix by volume:

- **Clay-rich soil:** 1 part (the binder)
- **Sand:** 2-3 parts (prevents cracking)
- **Fiber:** 0.5 part chopped straw, cattail fluff, or horse manure (tensile strength)
- **Water:** enough to achieve workable consistency (like thick cake batter)

### Application Process

1. **Prep the wall:** Mist the wall surface, fill any deep voids or cracks with a thicker mix (scratch coat)
2. **Apply scratch coat:** 3/8 to 1/2 inch thick, scored with a notched trowel to provide key for the next coat
3. **Let dry:** Wait until the scratch coat changes color (lightens) before applying the next coat
4. **Apply brown coat:** 1/4 to 3/8 inch thick, smoother than scratch coat
5. **Apply finish coat:** Thin layer (1/8 inch) with finer sand and optionally a clay slip enrichment for a smooth surface

### Testing Your Mix

Make test batches and apply to a small area. Watch for:

- **Cracking:** Add more sand or fiber
- **Dusting/flaking:** Add more clay
- **Slumping:** Add more sand; reduce water
- **Poor adhesion:** Wet the substrate more, add more clay

## Lime Wash

Lime wash is a thin, breathable coating made from hydrated lime (type S) and water. It has been used for centuries on exterior and interior masonry, earth, and lime plaster walls.

### Recipe

- **Hydrated lime (type S):** 1 part by volume
- **Water:** 3-5 parts by volume
- **Optional pigment:** natural mineral pigments (ochres, iron oxides) for color
- **Optional salt:** 1 tablespoon per gallon to improve adhesion on porous surfaces

### Application

1. Mix lime into water and let slake for at least 24 hours (longer is better)
2. Stir thoroughly before use (lime settles)
3. Apply with a large brush in thin, even coats
4. Apply multiple coats (3-5) for better coverage and durability
5. Allow each coat to carbonate (absorb CO2 from air) before recoating — at least several hours

### Characteristics

- Brightens walls naturally with a glowing, matte finish
- Naturally antimicrobial — good for animal shelters and milking rooms
- Breathable: allows moisture vapor to pass
- Requires periodic reapplication (every 2-5 years on exteriors)
- Burnishes and improves with age

## Milk Paint

Milk paint is one of the oldest known paints, made from milk protein (casein), lime, and pigments. It produces a beautiful, matte, velvety finish on wood, earth plaster, and masonry.

### Recipe

- **Quark or skim milk powder:** 2 cups (provides casein protein)
- **Hydrated lime:** 1 cup (binder)
- **Pigment:** natural earth pigments as desired (1-4 tablespoons)
- **Water:** enough to reach painting consistency

### Process

1. Dissolve milk powder in warm water; let sit until fully dissolved
2. Slowly add hydrated lime, stirring to avoid lumps
3. Add pigment and mix thoroughly
4. Let rest for 30 minutes to thicken
5. Apply with a brush in thin coats; 2-3 coats typical
6. Cures to a durable finish over 2-4 days as proteins cross-link

### Characteristics

- Completely non-toxic and food-safe when cured
- Beautiful matte finish with slight texture
- Adheres well to porous surfaces (wood, plaster, raw earth)
- Can be sealed with linseed oil or wax for water resistance
- Not washable on its own — use where abrasion is minimal

## Alis

Alis is a traditional earthen finish coat from the American Southwest, used on adobe buildings. It is essentially a refined clay slip with additives for durability and sheen.

### Recipe

- **Clay soil (fine):** 1 part
- **Fine sand:** 0.5-1 part (optional, for reducing shrinkage)
- **Water:** enough for a creamy consistency
- **Additives (choose some):** wheat paste (1/4 cup per gallon), cactus juice (prickly pear pad extract), borax (1/4 cup), linseed oil (1/4 cup), milk (1 cup), or manure (1 cup)

### Application

1. Mix clay with water to create a smooth slip — strain through a sieve
2. Add chosen additives and mix thoroughly
3. Apply with a brush, sponge, or trowel in thin coats
4. Burnish with a smooth stone, spoon, or plastic sheet while still damp for a polished look

### Characteristics

- Produces a smooth, almost leather-like finish
- The burnished surface sheds water well
- Cactus juice acts as a natural binder and waterproofing agent
- Easy to repair and reapply

## Tadelakt

Tadelakt is a waterproof lime plaster technique originating from Morocco. It produces a smooth, polished, water-resistant surface suitable for showers, sinks, and exterior walls in wet climates.

### Materials

- **Lime plaster:** hydraulic lime (type NHL 2 or NHL 3.5) with fine marble sand
- **Black soap (savon noir):** olive-oil-based soft soap for burnishing
- **Water**

### Process

1. Apply lime plaster in thin coats (1/8 inch) to a smooth substrate
2. Allow to cure for several days until firm but still slightly damp
3. Smooth with a wooden or stone trowel, then compress with a hard plastic trowel
4. Apply black soap diluted in water with a rag
