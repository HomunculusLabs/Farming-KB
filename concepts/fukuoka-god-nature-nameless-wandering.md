- Seen from the right, he may appear as the Japanese deity

- And seen from the south, perhaps he has the countenance of Gautama

- The Japanese know only the language spoken in Japan; they do not know all languages

- The vision of God seen by Christians in Europe is only a profile seen from the West

- Muslims know only the northern profile of God and Japanese only the eastern profile

## Monotheism and Polytheism Are One

Buddhism says that God dwells in all things while Christianity says that the Holy Ghost dwells in all things. The former is polytheistic and thus different from monotheistic Christianity.

- But when one stands at the summit of the absolute world, there is no "mono" or "poly"

- Whether one calls that rock over there a small pebble or a large stone makes no difference

- In the world of language, "mono" and "poly" are unlike, but in the absolute realm beyond the secular world, neither exists

- Everything is one and the same

- Even "one" and "none," too, are identical

It is true of the Buddha of which Gautama spoke and the nothingness taught by Lao Tzu; regardless of who or what, these all refer to the same thing. Only the names by which these are called and the manner in which they are described differ. One other thing that differs is the position from which one views God.

## God Dwells in All Things but Man Has Forgotten

People have a difficult time understanding what is meant by "nature is God." One could not say, for example, that the birds in nature are God.

- But Fukuoka felt it was all right to say that those birds know God or at least that God dwells in them

- God dwelled at one time in man as well, but man has forgotten God and no longer even notices this

- Man does not know God, and loses his way in search of God

- Those sparrows, that dog, and that gingko tree have no need to look up to man

- On the contrary, man himself must bow down in respect on occasion for it is these others who are superior

- They just pretend not to know

Man cuts down the gingko tree, kills the sparrows, and puts the dog on a leash and drags it around. That is why he thinks himself better and smarter. But the truth is that those others -- the gingko, the sparrows, and the dog -- are looking down on us. Yet mankind, unaware that he is the object of their gaze, persists in believing that he has caught and controls this thing called nature.

## Man's Arrogance Has Torn Him from Nature

Man's arrogance and pride have torn him from the embrace of nature. If man were to rid himself of this cloak of hubris and stand naked, if he were to abandon himself, crawling around on hands and knees with the dogs and cats, climbing trees and acting like children do, he would approach God.

- Unless one becomes a fool and renounces attachments, one cannot advance to the world of the transcendent
