## Administration Routes

Cannabis may be administered through several distinct pathways, each with unique pharmacokinetic profiles:

### Inhalation (Smoking and Vaporization)
Cannabinoids are absorbed through the alveoli of the lungs directly into the bloodstream, bypassing first-pass hepatic metabolism.

**Pharmacokinetics:**
- **Onset:** Seconds to minutes
- **Peak effect:** 15-30 minutes
- **Duration:** 2-4 hours
- **Bioavailability:** 10-35% (variable due to smoking technique, inhalation depth, and individual lung physiology)

**Effect characteristics:**
- Rapid onset allows for precise, real-time dose titration
- Effects are perceived as more "cerebral" or head-focused
- Shorter duration requires more frequent administration for sustained relief
- Vaporization eliminates combustion byproducts while maintaining similar pharmacokinetics

**Best suited for:**
- Acute symptom relief (pain flare, nausea, anxiety attack)
- Patients who need immediate feedback on dosing
- Conditions requiring rapid onset (breakthrough pain, spasms)
- Initial therapeutic window establishment

### Oral Ingestion (Edibles, Capsules, Oils)
Cannabinoids are absorbed through the gastrointestinal tract and metabolized by the liver before entering systemic circulation (first-pass metabolism).

**Pharmacokinetics:**
- **Onset:** 30-90 minutes (up to 2 hours on a full stomach)
- **Peak effect:** 2-4 hours
- **Duration:** 4-8 hours (sometimes longer)
- **Bioavailability:** 4-20% (lower than inhalation due to first-pass metabolism and variable GI absorption)

**Effect characteristics:**
- Significantly delayed onset creates risk of overconsumption
- Hepatic conversion of THC to 11-hydroxy-THC produces more potent, longer-lasting psychoactive effects
- Effects are perceived as more "body-centered" and sedative
- Longer duration provides sustained relief with fewer administrations
- Greater variability between individuals due to differences in metabolism, stomach contents, and GI transit time

**Best suited for:**
- Chronic conditions requiring sustained relief (arthritis, neuropathy, insomnia)
- Patients who cannot or prefer not to inhale
- Baseline maintenance dosing between inhaled rescue doses
- Overnight [[cannabis-cancer-symptom-management]]

### Sublingual (Tinctures, Sprays)
Cannabinoids are absorbed through the mucous membranes under the tongue directly into the bloodstream, partially bypassing the GI tract and liver.

**Pharmacokinetics:**
- **Onset:** 15-45 minutes
- **Peak effect:** 1-2 hours
- **Duration:** 3-6 hours
- **Bioavailability:** Higher than oral, lower than inhaled (estimates vary widely: 12-35%)

**Effect characteristics:**
- Intermediate onset between inhalation and oral ingestion
- Partial first-pass avoidance provides more predictable dosing than edibles
- Less psychoactive intensity than edibles (less 11-hydroxy-THC conversion)
- Dropper-based dosing allows precise measurement

**Best suited for:**
- Patients who want precise, measurable doses without inhalation
- Moderate-duration relief for ongoing symptoms
- Patients who find edibles too intense or unpredictable
- Combination with inhaled cannabis for layered symptom management

### Topical (Creams, Balms, Lotions)
Cannabinoids are applied to the skin for localized effects with minimal systemic absorption.

**Pharmacokinetics:**
- **Onset:** 15-60 minutes (variable)
- **Duration:** 2-6 hours
- **Bioavailability:** Minimal systemic absorption; effects are primarily local

**Effect characteristics:**
- No psychoactive effects at therapeutic doses
- Localized anti-inflammatory and analgesic action
- Can be applied repeatedly throughout the day without intoxication
- Combines well with other topical therapeutic agents

**Best suited for:**
- Localized pain (joint pain, muscle soreness, nerve pain)
- Skin conditions (psoriasis, eczema, dermatitis)
- Patients who cannot tolerate any psychoactive effects
- Adjunct therapy alongside systemic cannabis use

### Fresh Raw (Juicing, Salads)
Unheated cannabis provides cannabinoids in their acidic, non-psychoactive forms (THCA, CBDA). Onset 30-60 minutes, duration 2-4 hours. No psychoactive effects since THCA does not bind CB1 receptors. Provides anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective properties at high doses without intoxication. Best for patients requiring high-dose cannabinoid therapy without psychoactivity, anti-inflammatory protocols, and home growers with access to fresh material. Clinical evidence remains limited to case studies and testimonials.

## Safety Considerations

Blesching emphasizes several critical safety points across all administration methods:

- **Do not drive** or operate heavy machinery after any psychoactive form of cannabis
- **Start low, go slow** — especially with edibles where delayed onset tempts overconsumption
- **Method matters** — the same amount of cannabis produces very different effects depending on the route
- **Empty stomach vs. full stomach** significantly affects oral onset time and bioavailability
- **Individual variation** is substantial — what works for one patient may not work for another

## Matching Administration Route to Condition

Blesching's framework for selecting the appropriate route considers three factors:

1. **Desired onset speed:** Acute symptoms demand inhalation; chronic conditions benefit from oral/sublingual
2. **Required duration:** Short-acting conditions need frequent inhalation; all-day relief needs oral or topical
3. **Psychoactive tolerance:** Patients who cannot tolerate intoxication should use topicals or raw cannabis

## Key Takeaways

- Inhalation provides the fastest onset (seconds) but shortest duration (2-4 hours)
- Oral ingestion has the slowest onset (30-90 min) but longest duration (4-8 hours) and most intense effects due to 11-hydroxy-THC conversion
- Sublingual administration offers a middle ground: moderate onset, good dose control, reduced psychoactive intensity
- Topical application provides localized relief without psychoactivity
- Raw cannabis allows high-dose non-psychoactive cannabinoid therapy
- The most common dosing error is overconsumption of edibles due to delayed onset
- Patients should establish their therapeutic window using inhalation (immediate feedback) before attempting oral dosing
