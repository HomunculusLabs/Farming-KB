# Gartz Tryptamine Enrichment and Psilocybin Biosynthesis

[[jochen-gartz]]'s 1989 discovery that feeding tryptamine HCl to [[psilocybe-cubensis]] substrate causes the fungus to convert it to psilocin at levels up to 3.3% dry weight -- the highest psilocin concentrations ever reported in any mushroom. This finding demonstrated the hydroxylation and methylation capacity of P. cubensis enzyme systems and established tryptamine as a superior biosynthetic precursor to tryptophan. See also [[bigwood-beug-psilocybin-psilocin-variation-analysis]], [[psilocybin-biosynthesis-pathway]], [[psilocybin-mushroom-potency-comparison-by-species]], and [[psilocybin-mushroom-chemistry]].

## The Gartz Experiment

### Cultivation Method

- Substrate: dried cow dung/rice-grain mixture (2:1) with twice the amount of water
- This substrate produced first flush earlier than rye (with casing) or rice substrates used in previous studies
- Average yield: 3g dry mass per 10g of substrate
- Tryptamine addition: 25 mM concentration of tryptamine hydrochloride added to medium
- Strain: Psilocybe cubensis from Gartz's 1987 study
- Cultures produced fruiting bodies in 3-4 weeks, continuing through five flushes
- Mushrooms freeze-dried immediately at harvest, sealed in plastic, stored at -10 C until analysis
- Control cultivations without tryptamine addition were run for direct comparison

### Analytical Methods

- Extraction procedure and analysis described in Gartz's previous papers (1987)
- HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography) for quantification
- TLC (thin-layer chromatography) for qualitative confirmation
- Tryptamine presence/absence confirmed by TLC as described by Stijve et al.
- Methanolic extracts of both fruiting bodies and vegetative mycelia were analyzed

### Key Finding: Unprecedented Psilocin Levels

Flush | Psilocin (with tryptamine) | Psilocin (without) | Psilocybin (with) | Psilocybin (without)
 2.1%                        | 0%                 | 0.01%              | 0.55%
 3.3%                        | 0.01%              | 0.02%              | 0.48%
 2.8%                        | 0.02%              | 0.2%               | 0.51%
 3.1%                        | 0.09%              | 0.07%              | 0.46%
 2.9%                        | 0.15%              | 0.13%              | 0.61%

Psilocin values of 2.1-3.3% are the highest ever described in any mushrooms. All previously reported psilocin values are below 1% in dried specimens.

## Biosynthetic Implications

### Tryptamine as Superior Precursor

Earlier submerged culture studies established tryptamine's role in psilocybin biosynthesis:

- Agurell, Blomkvist, and Catalfomo (1966) showed radioactive tryptamine was a better precursor than tryptophan
- At least 22.4% of psilocybin formed was derived from labeled tryptamine precursor
- Psilocin level was generally zero in mycelial tissue from these submerged culture experiments
- Gartz's fruiting body experiments confirmed and dramatically amplified these results in whole mushrooms

### Enzyme Capacity

The results demonstrate that P. cubensis enzyme systems have high hydroxylation and methylation capacity:

- Tryptamine is completely consumed in all cases (always zero in harvested mushrooms)
- No tryptamine detected in methanolic extracts of vegetative mycelia from substrate either
- Conversion to psilocin is highly efficient across all five flushes
- The pathway proceeds: tryptamine -> 4-hydroxytryptamine (psilocin) -> psilocybin
- Hydroxylation at the 4-position and N,N-dimethylation are both required
- No detectable baeocystin was produced under these conditions
- Gartz (1987) had also been unable to detect baeocystin in P. cubensis

### Psilocybin Suppression

With tryptamine feeding, psilocybin levels dropped dramatically (0.01-0.2%) compared to controls (0.46-0.61%):

- Gartz proposed that reduced phosphate in the culture media may have decreased psilocybin biosynthesis from psilocin
- The phosphorylation step (psilocin -> psilocybin) requires phosphate donors (likely ATP or similar)
- When excess tryptamine is available, the enzyme system appears to saturate on psilocin production
- The hydroxylation/methylation enzymes outpace the phosphorylation enzyme under tryptamine enrichment
- This creates a bottleneck at the phosphorylation step, accumulating psilocin

### Flush Variation Pattern

With tryptamine addition, psilocin was present in ALL flushes including flush 1, unlike in control cultures where psilocin was absent or trace in early flushes. This contrasts with the Bigwood and Beug finding that psilocin is generally zero in the first flush without supplementation, suggesting that early-flush psilocin absence is due to insufficient tryptamine precursor rather than enzyme immaturity.

## Non-Specific Enzyme Systems

Repke et al. (1977) had reported traces of baeocystin in some P. cubensis strains and suggested:

- Many non-specific enzyme systems exist in fungi
- These enzymes can oxidize exogenously added compounds as well as normal obligatory intermediates
- This non-specificity explains why P. cubensis can efficiently convert fed tryptamine to psilocin
- The enzyme systems did not produce baeocystin (norbaeocystin) as a side product, suggesting specificity within the indole pathway

## Comparison with Bigwood and Beug (1982)

Gartz's control data (without tryptamine) closely matches Bigwood and Beug's findings:

- Both found consistently low psilocin in first flushes, increasing in later flushes
- Both found psilocybin generally at least twice the level of psilocin
- Both found substantial flush-to-flush variation in psilocybin levels
- Bigwood and Beug's Amazon strain on rye showed 0-2.6 mg/g psilocin across flushes
- Gartz's cow dung/rice controls showed 0-0.15% psilocin
- The substrate difference (rye vs cow dung/rice) may account for some variation in absolute values

## PF TEK Practical Application

Psylocybe Fanaticus (1997) adapted Gartz's technique for the PF TEK:

- Original formula: 0.16g tryptamine HCl in 45ml water per half-pint jar with 1/2-2/3 cup vermiculite + 1/8 cup brown rice powder
- Results: dwarfed fruiting bodies, at least 3x normal potency by bioassay
- Some jars failed to fruit entirely (substrate overload)
- Revised formula: 0.08-0.1g tryptamine HCl per jar
- Rationale: PF substrate is lighter and less nutritious than Gartz's cow dung/rice, requiring less tryptamine
- Psilocin preservation: cool desiccation technique, refrigerator drying, freezer storage with desiccant

## Safety and Regulatory Notes

- Tryptamine HCl is a controlled precursor substance in many jurisdictions
- The technique produces mushrooms with substantially higher psilocin content than normal
- Psilocin is the dephosphorylated active metabolite of psilocybin
- Psilocin is less chemically stable than psilocybin and degrades more readily
- Exact potency increase is difficult to predict due to natural variation in conversion efficiency
- The interaction between tryptamine enrichment and natural flush-to-flush variation is unexplored
