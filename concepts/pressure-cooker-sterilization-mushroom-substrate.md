# Pressure Cooker Sterilization of Mushroom Substrate

## Overview

Pressure cooker sterilization is a critical step in the boil-a-bag mushroom
cultivation method, eliminating competing microorganisms from the substrate
to give inoculated [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] a uncontested environment for
colonization. Without proper sterilization, contamination rates increase
dramatically and most or all bags may fail. The method uses standard
[[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]] principles adapted for [[oil-drum-sterilizer-mushroom-substrate-bags]].

## Equipment Required

- **Pressure cooker**: Must reach and maintain 15 PSI (approximately 121°C
  at sea level). Larger cookers can process more bags per run. The Jarrold
  method fits two bags per cooking cycle in a standard cooker.
- **Heat-resistant bags**: Polypropylene or autoclavable bags that do not
  melt at sterilization temperatures. Bags must have filter can assemblies
  already installed before loading.
- **Water**: Enough to generate steam for the full sterilization duration
  without boiling dry.
- **Trivet or rack**: To keep bags elevated above direct contact with the
  cooker bottom.

## Sterilization Protocol

### Loading

Two bags are placed into the pressure cooker per cycle (capacity depends on
cooker size). Bags should be positioned upright with filter cans facing
upward. Ensure bags are not compressed against the cooker walls, which
could cause melting or uneven heating. Leave adequate headspace above the
bags for steam circulation.

### Processing

1. Add water to the pressure cooker (amount depends on cooker size and
   duration).
2. Place bags on a trivet or rack above the water line.
3. Secure the pressure cooker lid and ensure the weight/valve is properly
   set.
4. Bring to full pressure (15 PSI) over medium-high heat.
5. Once at full pressure, reduce heat to maintain pressure without excessive
   steam venting.
6. Maintain 15 PSI for 60 minutes total. This may be done as two 30-minute
   stages, allowing the cooker to be checked between stages.
7. Remove from heat and allow pressure to drop naturally. Do not force-cool
   or release pressure manually, as rapid depressurization can disturb
   the substrate and compromise bag seals.

### Cooling

Bags must cool completely to room temperature before inoculation.
Inoculating hot substrate will kill the mushroom spores or mycelium.
Cooling typically takes 4–8 hours depending on [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]].
Bags should cool in a clean environment to minimize recontamination
during the cooling period.

## Sterilization Time Considerations

The standard 60-minute sterilization at 15 PSI is sufficient for straw
substrate in bags. However, several factors may require adjusted times:

- **Larger bag volumes**: May need 90–120 minutes for complete heat
  penetration to the center of the substrate mass.
- **Denser substrates**: Wood chips or [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]] require longer
  sterilization than loose straw.
- **Higher altitude**: At elevations above sea level, 15 PSI corresponds
  to lower actual temperatures. Additional time or higher pressure may be
  needed to achieve equivalent sterilization.
- **Supplemented substrates**: Rice flour or bran supplements increase
  contamination risk and warrant the full sterilization time.

## Contamination Sources

Understanding common [[mushroom-contamination-vectors]] helps improve success rates:

- **Endogenous contaminants**: Organisms present in the straw or supplements
  before sterilization. Proper sterilization eliminates these.
- **Airborne contaminants**: Spores that enter during cooling if bags are
  opened or filter cans are compromised. Keep bags sealed until
  inoculation.
