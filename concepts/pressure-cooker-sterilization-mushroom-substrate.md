# Pressure Cooker Sterilization of Mushroom Substrate

## Overview

Pressure cooker sterilization is a critical step in the boil-a-bag mushroom
cultivation method, eliminating competing microorganisms from the substrate
to give inoculated [[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]] principles adapted for [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]].
Bags should cool in a clean environment to minimize recontamination
during the cooling period.

## Sterilization Time Considerations

The standard 60-minute sterilization at 15 PSI is sufficient for straw
substrate in bags. However, several factors may require adjusted times:

- **Larger bag volumes**: May need 90–120 minutes for complete heat
  penetration to the center of the substrate mass.
- **Denser substrates**: Wood chips or [[mushroom-contamination-vectors]] helps improve success rates:

- **Endogenous contaminants**: Organisms present in the straw or supplements
  before sterilization. Proper sterilization eliminates these.
- **Airborne contaminants**: Spores that enter during cooling if bags are
  opened or filter cans are compromised. Keep bags sealed until
  inoculation.
