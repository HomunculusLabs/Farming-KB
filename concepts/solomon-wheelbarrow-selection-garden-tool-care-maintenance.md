# Wheelbarrow Selection and Garden Tool Care Maintenance

## Wheelbarrow Selection

### Why a Wheelbarrow Beats a Garden Cart

Garden magazines advertise expensive two-wheeled garden carts as more
labor-saving than the "old-fashioned" wheelbarrow. However, unless practicing
no-dig gardening, there is no need to haul dozens of pickup-truck loads of
[[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]]. A large wheelbarrow holds enough compost or aged manure to
adequately restore a 100-square-foot bed — plenty of hauling capacity.

Advantages of a wheelbarrow over a garden cart:
- **Maneuverability**: Navigates neatly down narrow paths between beds that a
  two-wheeler cannot manage
- **Cost**: Significantly less expensive
- **Weight**: Lighter and easier to handle for a single person

### Choosing the Right Wheelbarrow

Buy the largest barrow of the best quality you can barely afford. Key features:

- **Handles**: Strong wooden handles with proper height — arms should be only
  slightly bent at the elbows when lifting
- **Tire**: Broad pneumatic tire (with inner tube) on a steel rim, turning on
  ball bearings
- **Material**: No plastic — metal construction only
- **Fit**: You should be able to stand between the handles and walk forward
  without risking your knees
- **Source**: Visit a farm supplier or commercial hardware, not a national
  discount chain

### Avoid Concrete Barrows

Builders' supply companies sell specialist wheelbarrows designed for carrying
concrete. These have:
- Deep, rounded channels
- Thick-walled tubular metal handles instead of wood
- Super-strong but unnecessarily heavy construction for gardening

The style you want should be capacious but lightweight, since you will be
carrying organic matter, not dense materials like concrete or gravel.

## Essential Garden Sprayer

A pump-up sprayer holding five to six quarts is essential for:
- [[comfrey-tea-liquid-fertilizer]] on leaves ([[cannabis-foliar-feeding]])
- [[compost-tea-for-specific-crops]] [[cervantes-damping-off-seedling-disease-prevention]]
- [[bacillus-thuringiensis]] (Bt) for corn earworms and cabbageworms
- Home remedies like soapy water or rhubarb leaf juice

### Sprayer Quality Indicators
- **Interchangeable brass nozzle inserts** for different spray volumes and
  patterns
- **Spare parts availability**: Pump pads and o-rings
- **Metal pump**: Plastic pumps may become brittle and snap after a few years
- **UV protection**: Keep plastic sprayers out of the sun to extend life from
  2-3 years to potentially a decade

### Sprayer Maintenance

Add a surfactant ([[jadam-nutrient-management-wetting-agent-and-calcium]]) to sprayer solutions: a quarter teaspoonful
of mild liquid dishwashing soap per quart of spray. This quantity will not
burn or damage leaves. Before unscrewing the pump to fill the tank, wipe off
any grit lurking around the pump seal to prevent damage.

## Garden Knives

Two identical thin-bladed, sharp, pointed kitchen paring knives stuck into
the top of a short wooden stake in the garden are invaluable for:
- Thinning tiny seedlings by cutting (not pulling) crowded plants
- Weeding close to nearly emerged rows
- Harvesting

Keep a medium/coarse sharpening stone next to the stake. Hone the knife after
every five minutes of use. The stone can also hone a dull hoe in an emergency.
Bright red plastic handles improve visibility.
