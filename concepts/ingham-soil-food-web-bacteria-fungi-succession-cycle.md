# Soil Food Web: Bacteria-Fungi Succession Cycle

## Overview

In the soil food web model developed by Dr. Elaine Ingham, soil ecosystems
undergo a predictable succession from bacterial dominance to fungal dominance
over time. Understanding this cycle is fundamental to managing soil health,
producing effective compost teas, and matching biological amendments to
specific crop requirements. Succession reflects the changing availability of
organic materials and the evolving physical and chemical conditions of the
soil environment.

## The Succession Process

When soil is disturbed — whether by tillage, excavation, compaction, or
chemical application — the microbial community is set back to an early
successional stage dominated by bacteria. Bacteria are the first colonizers
because they reproduce rapidly (doubling every 20-30 minutes under ideal
conditions), require relatively simple carbon sources, and tolerate a wide
range of environmental conditions including reduced oxygen availability. Their
small size allows them to occupy the tiny pores in disturbed, compacted soils
where larger organisms cannot survive.

As succession progresses and soil structure improves through bacterial
activity, fungal populations begin to establish and expand. Fungi require
more stable soil conditions with good aggregation, continuous air channels,
and more complex organic matter. Their hyphal networks grow more slowly than
bacterial colonies but cover far greater distances and can access resources
