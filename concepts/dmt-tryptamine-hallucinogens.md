## DMT and Tryptamine Hallucinogens

## Overview

Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a powerful psychedelic tryptamine compound
found
naturally in many plants and animals, including the human brain. Terence
McKenna's 1983 essay "[[tryptamine-hyperdimensional-entities]] that appear dynamically
contorting, "somehow distinct from the surrounding background, which is
itself undergoing a continuous transformation." These entities
communicate
through a translinguistic modality that cannot be rendered in English, a
"language of alien meaning that is conveying alien information."

## DMT vs Other Psychedelics

McKenna emphasizes that DMT is phenomenologically distinct from other
psychedelics. While it is often grouped with LSD and mescaline, each
compound constitutes "a phenomenologically defined universe unto
itself."
DMT and psilocybin share the property of invoking the Logos — working
directly on language centers to produce an interior dialogue. This
distinguishes them from compounds that primarily produce visual effects
without the linguistic/communicative dimension.

The intensity and brevity of DMT also set it apart. McKenna describes it
as
"bastante" — enough, so much enough that it is too much. The experience
inspires genuine fear, which McKenna considers a mark of authenticity:
"One of the interesting approaches to evaluating such a compound is to
see
how eager people are to do it a second time. A touch of terror gives the
stamp of validity."

## The Logos and Translinguistic Objects

A central theme in McKenna's account is the concept of the Logos — a
realm
of pure information or communicative intelligence encountered under the
influence of tryptamines. The Logos manifests as translinguistic
objects:
"feeling-toned, meaning-toned, three-dimensional rotating complexes of
transforming light and color" that seem to convey information that
cannot
be expressed in ordinary language.

This translinguistic phenomenon manifests as glossolalia (speaking in
tongues), but unlike classical glossolalia, it is experienced as a brain
state allowing expression of "the assembly language that lies behind
language" — a primal "Ursprache" emerging from within. McKenna connects
this to [[robert-graves-british-mushroom-connection]]' concept in The [[mckenna-primal-ursprache-kabbalistic-zohar-and-white-goddess-glossolalia]] and to Kabbalistic
descriptions of a [[mckenna-glossolalia-and-primal-language-tryptamine-states]] in the Zohar.

## Endogenous DMT and Consciousness

McKenna argues that DMT's [[quantum-mechanical-correlates-hallucinogenesis-smythies-shulgin]] to hallucinogenesis — the
finding
that moving a single atom on a molecular ring can convert an inactive

## The "Black Hole Effect"

McKenna describes a "black hole effect" surrounding DMT: once one knows
about the experience, it becomes very difficult to communicate it to
others.
"The more one is able to articulate what it is, the less others are able
to understand." This epistemic barrier prevents the experience from
being
adequately studied or shared through conventional scientific and
cultural
channels, contributing to what McKenna sees as a profound gap in modern
understanding of consciousness.
