# Homestead Winter Preparations

Winter preparation is a critical annual cycle for any homestead. In cold climates, inadequate preparation means frozen pipes, hungry livestock, cold buildings, and unnecessary hardship. A systematic approach beginning in late summer ensures that buildings, animals, water systems, food stores, and energy supplies are ready for months of cold, snow, and short days.

## Timeline

### August-September: Planning and Procurement

- Order firewood (or begin cutting and splitting your own)
- Order livestock feed and bedding for winter (prices rise in winter)
- Inspect and order replacement parts for heating systems
- Test [[homestead-internet-options|internet]] and communication equipment
- Review last winter's problems and plan solutions
- Stock up on supplies before roads become difficult

### October: Major Preparation Work

- Winterize all structures (buildings, barns, coops, outbuildings)
- Install or check heating systems
- Process and store harvest
- Set up winter water systems
- Prepare livestock housing
- Stockpile firewood

### November: Final Readiness

- Complete all winterization tasks
- Confirm backup power systems work
- Stock emergency supplies (food, water, medicine, batteries)
- Test communication equipment
- Review emergency plans with household members

## Winterizing Structures

### House and Primary Residence

**Insulation:**
- Check attic insulation depth — minimum R-49 in cold climates (16-20 inches of fiberglass or cellulose)
- Insulate pipes in unheated spaces (crawl spaces, attics, exterior walls)
- Weatherstrip doors and windows — replace worn weatherstripping
- Caulk gaps around window and door frames, utility penetrations
- Install foam gaskets behind outlet and switch plates on exterior walls
- Consider insulating window film for single-pane windows

**Heating System:**
- Schedule annual inspection for furnace, boiler, or [[masonry-heater-design|masonry heater]]
- Clean or replace furnace filters
- Test backup heat sources (wood stove, space heaters)
- Stock at least 1-2 weeks of backup fuel (propane, wood, kerosene)
- Check [[radiant-floor-heating|radiant floor]] systems for proper operation
- Verify [[passive-solar-design|passive solar]] features are unobstructed (clean south-facing windows)

**Windows and Doors:**
- Install storm windows or insulating window inserts
- Hang heavy curtains on all windows (open during day for solar gain, close at night)
- Add door sweeps to exterior doors
- Close and seal unused doors and windows

### Barns and Outbuildings

- Check roof for leaks before snow loads accumulate
- Repair any structural damage
- Close ventilation gaps that are not needed (but maintain some ventilation)
- Stock bedding material (straw, wood shavings) — animals need deeper bedding in winter
- Ensure adequate lighting for chores in the dark
- Clear gutters and downspouts of debris

### Greenhouses and Season Extension

- Insulate north wall with reflective insulation
- Add thermal mass (water barrels painted black) for nighttime heat retention
- Install a backup heater for extreme cold events
- Double-layer poly film or add bubble wrap for insulation
- Ensure automatic vent openers are disabled or adjusted for winter
- See also: [[season-extension-techniques|season extension methods]]

## Winterizing Livestock

### General Principles

- Most livestock tolerate cold better than heat — focus on wind protection and dry bedding
- Access to clean, unfrozen water is the most critical winter livestock challenge
- Increase feed rations 10-20% in cold weather (animals burn more calories to stay warm)
- Provide shelter from wind and precipitation (not necessarily heated space)

### Water Access

- Heated water bowls or bucket heaters (plug-in or solar)
- Insulate water pipes to troughs
- Check and break ice multiple times per day if no heaters available
- Add warm water to troughs in extreme cold to delay freezing
- Position water sources in sheltered, sunny locations

### Specific Animals

**Chickens**
- Add a heat lamp only if temperatures drop below 0F (most cold-hardy breeds tolerate below freezing without supplemental heat)
- Add extra roosting space (birds huddle for warmth)
- Ensure coop is draft-free but ventilated (moisture buildup causes frostbite)
- Increase protein in feed during cold months
- Collect eggs frequently — they freeze and crack quickly
- Use heated water founts or check waterers 2-3 times daily

**Goats**
- Provide draft-free shelter (not necessarily heated)
- Deep bedding method: keep adding clean straw on top of soiled bedding — it composts and generates heat
- Increase hay ration — rumen fermentation generates body heat
- Trim hooves before ground freezes hard
- Protect does in late pregnancy from extreme cold

**Ducks**
- Ducks are very cold-hardy but need protection from wind
- Ensure swimming water is available (ducks need water to clear their nostrils)
- Provide a sheltered area with dry bedding
- Raised waterers prevent splashing and wet bedding

**Bees**
- Reduce hive entrance to prevent mice from entering
- Add a wind block (bale of straw or insulation board) on the windward side
- Ensure adequate honey stores (60-80 lbs per hive depending on climate)
- Do not open hives during cold weather unless absolutely necessary
- Wrap hives with insulating material in extreme cold climates (remove in spring)

## Winterizing Water Systems

### Well and Pump Systems

- Insulate wellhead and pitless adapter
- Heat tape on exposed pipes from well to house
- Ensure pump house or well house is insulated and has a heat source
- Know how to prime the pump if it loses prime
- Have a backup hand pump if possible

### Outdoor Plumbing

- Drain all outdoor hoses and store them indoors
- Shut off and drain outdoor spigots (install frost-free spigots if possible)
- Drain sprinkler systems
- Insulate any exposed pipes with foam insulation or heat tape
- Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during extreme cold
