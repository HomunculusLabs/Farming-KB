## Cannabis Rhizosphere Microbiome Zonation

The rhizosphere — the narrow zone of soil immediately influenced by root secretions and
associated soil microorganisms — is one of the most biologically active environments on
Earth. In Cannabis, this zone exhibits a distinct gradient of microbial communities
that shifts systematically from bulk soil through the rhizosphere and into the root
interior (endorhiza). Understanding this zonation is essential for optimizing Cannabis
cultivation through microbiome management.

The Orange County soil (OC.2) stood out with dramatically higher organic carbon
(20.0%) and water content (0.371) but lower salinity and nitrogen. These differences
propagated through the rhizosphere and into the endorhiza, though the strength of
edaphic influence diminished at each tier.

## Cultivar-Specific Signatures

Even within the same soil type, different Cannabis cultivars maintained distinct
endorhiza communities. A striking example was the genus **Methylophilus**, which
comprised 13% of the endorhiza community in Bookoo Kush, only 0.13% in Burmese,
and was entirely absent from Sour Diesel (FDR p = 0.012). Methylophilus species
are methylotrophic bacteria capable of utilizing single-carbon compounds like
methanol — a product of plant cell wall demethylation. The [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]] including cannabinoids.
