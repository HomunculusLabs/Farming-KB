# Herb Spiral Construction

An herb spiral is a three-dimensional, spiral-shaped garden bed that creates multiple microclimates in a compact footprint. Rising from ground level to approximately 3-6 feet at the center, a single herb spiral can support a wide variety of herbs — from moisture-loving plants at the bottom to drought-tolerant Mediterranean herbs at the top — all within a 5-6 foot diameter circle.

## The Concept

The herb spiral is a classic permaculture design pattern that maximizes edge and microclimate diversity. By building upward in a spiral, you create:

- **Moisture gradient:** The bottom of the spiral collects runoff and stays damp; the top drains quickly and stays dry
- **Sun exposure gradient:** The top receives full sun from all angles; lower portions may be shaded by the upper sections depending on orientation
- **Temperature gradient:** Stone walls absorb heat during the day and release it at night, creating a warmer microclimate — especially on the south-facing side (in the Northern Hemisphere)
- **Soil depth gradient:** Deeper soil at the bottom for deep-rooted plants, shallower soil at the top for shallow-rooted species
- **Drainage gradient:** Water flows down through the spiral, so the top is well-drained and the bottom is moisture-retentive

## Dimensions and Siting

### Size

- **Diameter:** 5-6 feet (1.5-1.8 meters) at the base
- **Height:** 3-6 feet (0.9-1.8 meters) at the center/top
- **Path width:** The spiral path between the outer wall and the inner mound should be 12-18 inches wide for comfortable planting and harvesting
- **Footprint:** Approximately 25-30 square feet — replaces a 50+ square foot linear herb garden

### Location

- **Full sun:** 6-8 hours of direct sun minimum for Mediterranean herbs
- **Near the kitchen:** Place the herb spiral close to your kitchen door for convenient harvesting while cooking
- **Level ground:** Essential for even water distribution
- **Access from all sides:** The spiral should be approachable from any direction

## Materials

### Stone (Recommended)

Natural stone is the traditional and best material for herb spiral walls:

- **Field stones:** Free if you have them on your property or can gather them legally
- **Limestone or sandstone:** Easy to stack, good thermal mass
- **Basalt or granite:** Very durable, excellent heat absorption
- **Stone size:** Mix of large (fist-size to head-size) for the lower courses and smaller stones for upper courses
- **No mortar needed:** Dry-stacked stone allows water to seep through the walls, which helps distribute moisture throughout the spiral

### Alternative Materials

- **Concrete blocks:** Quick to build, durable. Fill cores with soil for planting.
- **Bricks:** Good thermal mass, neat appearance. Can be dry-stacked or mortared.
- **Timber:** Treated or naturally rot-resistant timber (cedar, redwood). Less thermal mass than stone.
- **Earth bags:** Polypropylene bags filled with earth and stacked. Very low cost, excellent for earth-building enthusiasts.
- **Bamboo:** Creative option for tropical climates. Less thermal mass, shorter lifespan.

### Soil and Fill

- **Bulk fill (bottom):** Gravel, rubble, branches, wood chips — provides drainage and reduces the amount of soil needed
- **Growing soil (top 6-12 inches):** Well-draining, fertile garden soil mixed with compost and sand (for Mediterranean herbs that need sharp drainage)
- **Compost:** 2-3 inches of finished compost worked into the top layer

## Construction Steps

### 1. Mark the Spiral

1. Drive a stake in the center of the site
2. Tie a string to the stake, measure out 2.5-3 feet (half the diameter)
3. Walk in a circle, marking the outer edge with lime, sand, or spray paint
4. Mark the spiral path: Starting from the outer edge, draw a spiral that makes 1.5 to 3 full turns as it rises to the center
5. The spiral path should be 12-18 inches wide, with the wall on the outer edge and the mound on the inner edge

### 2. Prepare the Base

1. Remove grass or weeds from the marked area
2. Loosen the soil surface with a garden fork
3. Optionally, dig a shallow trench (6-12 inches deep) for the bottom course of stone

### 3. Build the Stone Wall

1. Lay the first (lowest) course of stone around the outer edge, following the spiral path
2. Start each subsequent course slightly inward from the one below, creating the rising spiral
3. Backfill behind each course with gravel, rubble, or branches as you build up
4. The wall should lean slightly inward (batter) for stability — about 5-10 degrees
5. Continue building upward, spiraling inward, until you reach the center height (3-6 feet)
6. Use smaller stones for the upper courses and the top of the spiral

### 4. Fill with Soil

1. Once the wall is complete, fill the growing areas with your soil mix
2. Add extra sand to the upper portion for better drainage
3. Add extra compost to the lower, moister zones
4. Create a slight slope away from the center to help guide water down the spiral

### 5. Install a Water Feature (Optional)

- Place a small pond or bowl at the base of the spiral for aesthetics and to attract beneficial insects and frogs
- The moisture from the pond helps create the humid microclimate for moisture-loving herbs at the bottom

## Planting by Zone

The microclimates of the herb spiral allow you to grow herbs with very different needs in one structure:

### Top of the Spiral (Zone 1) — Hot, Dry, Full Sun, Well-Drained

Mediterranean herbs that thrive in hot, dry conditions with sharp drainage:

- Rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis)
- Lavender (Lavandula spp.)
- Thyme (Thymus vulgaris)
- Oregano (Origanum vulgare)
- Sage (Salvia officinalis)
- Winter savory (Satureja montana)

### Upper Middle (Zone 2) — Warm, Moderately Dry

Herbs that like warmth but slightly more moisture:

- Tarragon (Artemisia dracunculus)
- Chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla)
- Calendula (Calendula officinalis)
- Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)
- Catnip (Nepeta cataria)

### Lower Middle (Zone 3) — Moderate Moisture, Some Shade

Herbs that prefer average moisture and some protection:

- Basil (Ocimum basilicum)
- Cilantro (Coriandrum sativum)
- Dill (Anethum graveolens)
- Parsley (Petroselinum crispum)
- Chives (Allium schoenoprasum)
- Mint (Mentha spp.) — plant in the lower zones as mint spreads aggressively
