# Fungal Ecosystem Roles

How fungi perform specific ecological functions: water transport, [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]], decomposition, and succession management. Part of the [[fungal-ecology]] framework from *[[woodlovers-mycelium-running-pathway-technique]]* by [[paul-stamets]].

## Water Transport

Mycelium acts as a **biological hydraulic system**:
- Rhizomorphic mycelia channel water from wet zones to dry zones
- [[paul-stamets]] observed mycelium transporting water from a creek up a 40-foot vertical bank
- [[fungal-serpula-lacrymans-dry-rot-buildings]] (dry rot fungus) transports water from meters away to moisten wood before contact
- Mycelial mats act as sponges — inoculated wood chips retain dramatically more moisture
- A cubic inch of colonized soil holds more water than non-colonized soil
- This hydraulic function is critical for [[mycofiltration-overview]] applications

## Erosion Control

- Rhizomorphs (cord-like fungal structures) can support 30,000 times their own mass
- Stamets demonstrated a rhizomorph holding a wooden dowel at 3,029 times its own weight
- Mycelial polysaccharides act as mucilaginous soil-binding agents
- Colonized wood chips on slopes reduce sediment runoff by catching and filtering silt
- Tahuya State Forest: mycofiltration mats on old logging road created a mantle of contiguous mycelia at the wood chip/soil interface, binding loose gravel

## Decomposition Chemistry

### The Wood Rot Spectrum
- **Soft rot**: Primarily bacteria and a few fungi; slow, affects damp wood
- **Brown rot**: Fungi that digest cellulose, leaving brown lignin behind (cubical cracking)
- **White rot**: Fungi that digest lignin, leaving white cellulose (critical for toxin remediation)
- **Simultaneous rot**: Some species cause both white and brown patterns

### Enzymatic Arsenal
| Enzyme | Produces | Key Function |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] | White rot fungi | Breaks lignin's aromatic rings |
| [[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]] | White rot fungi | Mineralizes wood; breaks H-C bonds |
| Laccase | White rot fungi | General oxidase; degrades phenols, dyes |
| Cellulase | Most fungi | Breaks down cellulose |
| Quinone reductase | Brown rot (Gloeophyllum) | Consumes wood preservatives |

## The Bacterial Partnership

Aged mycelium performs better than pure culture for remediation:
- **Battelle experiment**: Unsterilized chips + mycelium = 97% PAH degradation vs 65% with sterilized chips
- **Reason**: Native microbes activated by mycelial introduction produce complementary enzymes
- **Stamets' insight**: "Acclimated spawn" (aged mycelium exposed to habitat microbes) outperforms pure culture
- **Mechanism**: Mycelium declines → bacteria bloom on dying mycelium → bacteria finish toxin breakdown
- **Practical implication**: Spent [[gourmet-medicinal-mushroom-substrate-preparation]] from farms is better remediation material than fresh spawn

## Carbon Cycling

- Fungal decomposition releases CO2 gradually over years (not hours like fire)
- CO2 from decomposition is cool and stays near ground level, where plants absorb it
- Burning wood sends CO2 high into atmosphere, beyond plant reach
- Fungal decomposition is the reverse of photosynthesis: converts cellulose + lignin back to CO2 + water
- Each 12 inches of wood chips produces 1-2 inches of soil over 4 years with oyster mycelium

## Fungi and Temperature

- Decomposition generates heat (exothermic) — compost piles reach 150°F+
- Thermogenesis from fungal activity warms soil, extending growing seasons
- Cold-tolerant species (Flammulina velutipes, Hygrophorus) metabolize at near-freezing temps
- Temperate fungi produce antifreeze proteins that protect soil microcosms
- Fire prevention: moist, myceliated wood chips do not easily burn

## Fungi and Insects

Mycelium-insect interactions are crucial to ecosystem recovery:
- Many insects are "fungus-loving" (mycophilous) — attracted to spores
- Mushrooms provide food for flies, beetles, slugs, squirrels, deer, bears
- Rotting fruiting bodies become breeding grounds for fly larvae and grubs
- Larvae attract birds, which deposit seeds
- Seeds → plants → organic debris → next fungal generation
- Some mycelia emit fragrances that attract predator insects (phorid flies for fire ants)
- See [[fungal-pest-control]] for applied insect management

## Fungi and Fire

- Chipping wood debris and leaving it on the forest floor reduces fire danger
- Burning debris robs the ecosystem of nutrients and creates conditions for hotter fires
- Myceliated wood chips are moist — wood with >30% moisture content doesn't ignite easily
- Short-term fire risk may increase for 1-2 years after chipping, then drops dramatically
- Old-growth forests have low fuel load near ground (moist nurse logs) vs dense replanted monocrops
