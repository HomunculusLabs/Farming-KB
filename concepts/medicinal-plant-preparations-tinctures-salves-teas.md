# Medicinal Plant Preparations — Tinctures, Salves, Teas

Detailed preparation guides for common herbal medicine forms. For an overview
of all preparation types, equipment, and shelf life, see
[[herbal-medicine-making-basics]].

## Tinctures

Concentrated alcohol extracts that pull a wide range of plant constituents
including alkaloids, glycosides, and volatile oils.

### Folk Method

1. Fill a glass jar 1/2 to 2/3 full with chopped dried herb (packed full
   with fresh herb).
2. Cover with alcohol (all plant material submerged by 1-2 inches).
3. Seal, label (herb, date, alcohol %). Shake daily for 4-6 weeks.
4. Strain through cheesecloth into amber dropper bottles.

**Alcohol choice:** Vodka 80-100 proof (all-purpose); Everclear 190 proof
(best for resins, roots, bark); brandy for pleasant flavor.

### Weight-to-Volume Method (Standardized)

Produces consistent, repeatable results. Standard ratios:
- Dried herbs: 1:5 (1g herb to 5ml alcohol)
- Fresh herbs: 1:2 (1g fresh herb to 2ml alcohol)

Weigh herb precisely, calculate volume, combine, shake daily 4-6 weeks,
strain, and label with ratio and alcohol percentage.

### Fresh vs Dried

- **Fresh:** Better for high-water-content plants where fresh juice adds
  value (St. John's wort, milky oat tops). Use 1:2 ratio with 95% alcohol.
- **Dried:** More concentrated and predictable. Use 1:5 with 40-60% ABV.

## Infused Oils

### Solar Method (delicate herbs)

1. Fill jar 1/2 to 2/3 full with dried herb. Cover with olive oil.
2. Cap loosely (coffee filter + rubber band). Place in sunny window 4-6
   weeks. Shake occasionally.
3. Strain. Store in amber glass.
Best for: calendula, chamomile, lavender, plantain.

### Heat Method (roots, bark, seeds)

1. Combine herb and oil in double boiler or slow cooker.
2. Gently heat at 100-120F for 2-8 hours. Never fry or smoke.
3. Strain while warm. Store in amber glass.

## Salve Making

Infused oil thickened with beeswax for semi-solid topical use.

1. Measure 8 oz infused oil into double boiler. Add 1 oz beeswax.
2. Heat gently until wax melts (adjust wax: 0.75 oz softer, 1.25 oz firmer).
3. Remove from heat. Add 1 tsp vitamin E oil (preservative) and 15-30 drops
   essential oil if desired.
4. Pour into tins or jars. Cool undisturbed. Cap and label.

Common salves: calendula (skin healing), plantain (bites/stings), comfrey
(sprains, strains — external only).

## Tea Blending

- **Infusions** (leaves, flowers): boiling water, covered, 10-15 min.
  1-2 tsp dried herb per cup.
- **Decoctions** (roots, bark, seeds): simmer 20-30 min. 1 tbsp per cup.
- **Cold infusions** (marshmallow root): cold water, 4-8 hours.
- **Blending:** Base herb (60%), supportive herbs (30%), accent herb (10%).

## Glycerites

Alcohol-free extracts suitable for children.

1. Mix 3 parts vegetable glycerin with 1 part distilled water.
2. Pack jar 1/2 full with dried herb. Cover with glycerin mixture.
3. Seal, shake daily 4-6 weeks. Strain and bottle.
Shelf life: 1-2 years (refrigeration recommended). Less effective for
alkaloids and resins.

## Dosing Guidelines

- **Tinctures:** 1-3 ml (20-60 drops) 2-3x daily. Children: (weight in
  lbs / 150) x adult dose (Clark's Rule).
- **Teas:** 1 cup 2-3x daily. Nourishing infusions: 1-2 quarts daily.
- **Salves:** Apply liberally to affected area 2-4x daily.
