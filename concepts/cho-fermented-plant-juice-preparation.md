Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) is a fermented extract of a plant's sap
and chlorophyll. It is a rich enzyme solution full of microorganisms
such as [[cho-natural-farming-inputs-overview]] system.

## Selection of Plant Materials

### Criteria for Plant Selection

Choose plants that possess one or more of these characteristics:

- Strong against cold, able to grow well in spring -- passes the
  characteristic of endurance against extreme climatic changes
- Fast-growing and vigorous -- these have highly active growth
  hormones that can improve plant weaknesses and recovery
- Plants with abundant growth hormones: bamboo shoots, lateral buds
  of all kinds of plants
- Thinned-out fruits (contain gibberellins for thicker foliage and
  enhanced fruit thickness)

### Recommended Plants by Growth Stage

- **Germination to early vegetative growth**: Mugwort (Artemisia
  vulgaris) and bamboo shoot FPJs help crops resist cold and grow
  fast and strong
- **Vegetative growth**: Arrowroot, bamboo shoot, and reed FPJs help
  crops obtain nitrogen for volume increase
- **Crop-specific**: Use lateral buds and stems of the same crop
  (tomato for tomatoes, squash vines for squash, sweet potato vines
  for sweet potatoes)

### When to Collect

- Avoid days with excessive sunshine (evaporates nutrients) or
  heavy rainfall (washes away nutrients and microorganisms)
- If it rains, wait two days before collecting
- Collect just before sunrise when plants have perfect moisture level
- Do not pick near roadsides to avoid polluted plants

## Preparation Method

### Materials
- Selected plant material
- Jaggery or brown sugar (NOT molasses -- too much moisture)
- Clay jar or glass jar (brown glass preferred)
- Porous paper and rubber band
- Stone for weight

### Procedure
1. Shake off dirt from plants but do NOT wash (washing removes
   useful microorganisms). If ingredients are too big, cut to
   3-5 cm pieces to increase surface area for osmotic pressure
2. Do NOT mix different plant types in one container
3. Measure weight of plant material; brown sugar should be about
   half the weight (adjust according to plant moisture level)
4. Put ingredients and brown sugar in a large wide container; mix
   with hands. Cover with porous paper for 1-2 hours
5. Transfer to clay pot, filling only 3/4. The remaining 1/4 is
   filled with air for optimum fermentation
6. Place a stone weight on the mixture to control air content
7. Cover with paper secured by rubber band (prevents insects while
   allowing air exchange)
8. After 1-2 days, remove the weight (air has escaped); re-cover
9. Place in cool, shaded place. Do NOT open, move, or stir during
   fermentation

## When to Use FPJ

- Germination to early vegetative growth: 1:1000 dilution
- Vegetative growth: 1:800 to 1:1000 dilution
- Pest management: Mix FPJ with rice bran and sprinkle around fruit
  trees to lure pests away from fruits

## When NOT to Use FPJ

- During excessive vegetative growth or overgrowth from prolonged
  rain or cloudy weather
- FPJ made from the crop itself (promotes further overgrowth)
- During acidic or nitrogen-excessive conditions (pest-attractive)
- Conditions of too much moisture or bad ventilation (fungal growth)

## Storage

- Use polyethylene, glass, or clay jars (brown glass preferred)
- Store in cool, shaded place with no direct sunlight
- Optimum storage: 1-15 degrees C (refrigerator) for up to one year
- Room temperature: use within 30 days

## Troubleshooting

- Small bubbles or fungi appear: lacks brown sugar or unbalanced
  air-to-ingredient ratio. Add sugar, stir, filter, and preserve.
- Avoid molasses (too much moisture, insufficient osmotic pressure)
- If hot weather continues, water the plant one day before picking
- Do not wash ingredients (removes lactic acid bacteria and yeast)
- After rain, wait 2-3 days for microbes to return to leaf surfaces

## Application Notes

FPJs are normally used at dilution of 1:800 to 1:1000 in water. When
FPJ is combined with other NF inputs, more water should be added to
the solution. FPJ is also a key ingredient in making
[[cho-indigenous-microorganisms-collection-and-cultivation]].
