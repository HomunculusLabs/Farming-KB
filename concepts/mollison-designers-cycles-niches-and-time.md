# Cycles, Niches, and Time

Cycles and niches are two fundamental concepts in permaculture that together
determine the potential for diversity and yield in any system. If niches are
opportunities in space, cycles are opportunities in time.

## The Nature of Cycles

Cycles are any recurring events or phenomena. They have another implication,
which is one of diversion. A cycle is an interruption or eddy in the
straight-line progression towards entropy. It is the special provenance of
life to cycle materials. So efficiently does this happen that in a tropical
forest almost all material nutrients are in cycle in life forms. It is this
very complex cycling in the tropics which opened up so many opportunities
for yield that thousands of species evolved to take advantage.

## Cycles Create Yield

Every cyclic event increases the opportunity for yield. To increase cycling
is to increase yield. Geese eat grass, digest it, moult, produce waste
products, add parasites, digestive enzymes, acids and alkalis, and defecate.
The ground receives the rejecta, the sun shines, and rain may fall. Fungi,
bacteria, grass roots and foliage work on feathers and faeces, and
re-metabolise them into life.

Each such cycle is a unique event. Diet, choice, selection, season, weather,
digestion, decomposition, and regeneration differ each time it happens. It
is the number of such cycles, great and small, that decide the potential for
diversity.

## Cycles as Niche in Time

If niches are opportunities in space, cycles are opportunities in time (a
time-slot) and both together give harbour to many events and species. By
reorganising and encouraging such cycles, our opportunities to obtain yields
multiply. Every peasant farmer who keeps pigeons (as they still do in the
Mediterranean borders) knows this truth. Here, every thinking farmer builds
his own phosphate factory, as a pigeon loft.

## People and Cycles

People are built up molecule by molecule, cycling through themselves the
materials of their environment: its air, soils, foods, minerals, and
pathogens. Over time, people create their own local ecology. Their wastes,
exudae, and rejecta eventually create the very soils in which they garden.

We gardeners are constantly cycling ourselves, and by a generational pattern
of adjustment become "eco-compatible" with our landscape and climate. We are
not the end point of evolution but a step on the way, and part of a whole
sequence of cycles.

## Degenerative and Regenerative Cycles

It is the number of degenerative-regenerative cycles which determine the
number of opportunities in the system, and its potential to change, mutate,
diversify, and reintegrate. Every cycle is a new opportunity. Not only can
we never cross the same river twice, we can never see the same view twice,
nor know the same system twice.

It is our tolerance of the proliferation of life which permits such cycles.
Deprived systems, like those blasted by biocides, lose most or all
opportunity to transcend their prior state, and the egg of life is broken,
degrades, and assumes a lower potential.

## Cycling in Different Climates

Cycling of nutrients is continuous in the tropics, but is interrupted
wherever drought, cold, or low nutrient status reduces the base opportunity.
Such cycles are slowed or even stopped by climatic factors or by our
interference. Cycles in nature are diversion routes away from entropic ends,
giving opportunities for species to occupy time niches.

## Cycles as Time Maps

Cycles, like comets, have schedules or times to occur. Some are frequent and
obvious like day and night, others long-term like sunspot cycles. Both short
and long cycles are used in phenomenological reckoning by aborigines, who
use cycle-indicators as time maps.

## Time as a Resource

Time is a resource which can accumulate in ecosystems. It can be "lost" to
an evolving system by setback (adverse disturbance), just about the same way
as we can set back the hands of a clock. Such setbacks are termed deflection
states by ecologists.

Ecosystems are always proceeding to some other state of evolution. Left
alone, they may evolve to some unknowable endpoint once called a climax
state. However, forest climax states are temporary events in geological time.
Time can work as a rehabilitative resource, for active intervention in
successions enables us to supply key nutrients and soil treatments.

## Life-Time as a Resource

A second time concept is that of life-time, or the "quality time" that we
have to enjoy, examine, and understand our world. Tribal peoples still
manage to preserve a high quota of celebrations, discussions,
contemplations, mutual preening, and creative artwork on which many of us
"wish we had time to spend."

## The Principle of Cyclic Opportunity

Every cyclic event increases the opportunity for yield. To increase cycling
is to increase yield. This is one of the most powerful design directives in
permaculture: by designing systems with many interacting cycles, we
maximise both diversity and productivity.
