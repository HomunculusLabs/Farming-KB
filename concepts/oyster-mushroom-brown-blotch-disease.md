# Oyster Mushroom Brown Blotch Disease

Brown blotch disease causes significant crop loss in [[pseudomonas-tolaasii-entity-the-mushroom-cultivator]]*
- **Secondary reported agent:** *Pseudomonas agarici* (importance as a pathogen of brown blotch disease is questionable)
- *P. tolaasii* has been isolated from all observed symptomatic mushrooms, though mixed infections may contribute to the variety of symptoms seen

The bacterium is a Gram-negative rod that produces a characteristic lipopeptide toxin called tolaasin, which is the primary virulence factor responsible for tissue damage and lesion formation. Tolaasin disrupts cell membranes, causing the characteristic brown discoloration and tissue maceration.

### Symptoms

Bacterial brown blotch presents with various symptoms on [[mycogone-perniciosa-entity-the-mushroom-cultivator]]* (syn. *Sepedonium perniciosum*), a pathogenic fungus that produces distinct symptoms:

- Small, brownish spots on the cap surface that may develop white, powdery spore masses
- Distorted, malformed fruiting bodies — particularly "balloon-shaped" caps with thick, shortened stipes
- Internal tissue becomes spongy and brown
- A characteristic musty or earthy odor (distinct from the fishy smell of bacterial blotch)

Fungal brown blotch is less common than bacterial brown blotch in modern oyster mushroom cultivation but can cause significant damage when it occurs. It is more frequently associated with button mushroom (*[[mushroom-growing-room-environmental-control]] systems.
- Maintain **constant humidity and temperature** in growing houses; abrupt changes increase brown blotch incidence. Rapid humidity fluctuations stress mushroom tissue and create micro-wounds where bacteria can enter.
- **Avoid free water** on fruiting bodies, as it allows pathogenic bacteria to multiply rapidly. Standing water droplets on the cap surface provide the ideal microenvironment for *P. tolaasii* proliferation.
- **Ventilate after watering** to remove standing water from mushroom surfaces. Allow adequate time for evaporation before reducing airflow.
- **Do not over-water** — brown blotch incidence increases dramatically with excessive moisture. Target substrate moisture at 65-70% and avoid surface saturation.

### Environmental Management

- **Relative humidity control:** Maintain 85-90% RH during fruiting without allowing condensation on mushroom surfaces. Use fogging systems rather than misting to avoid large droplets.
- **Air circulation:** Ensure adequate horizontal air movement across the fruiting surface. Dead air zones promote bacterial accumulation and humidity pockets.
- **Temperature management:** *P. tolaasii* proliferates most rapidly at 20-25°C. Slightly cooler fruiting temperatures (15-18°C) can slow bacterial growth, though this must be balanced with the species' optimal fruiting temperature.

### Chemical and Biological Controls

For organic and conventional operations alike, chemical options for bacterial blotch are extremely limited:

- **Chlorine-based sanitizers:** Sodium hypochlorite solutions (100-200 ppm available chlorine) can be used for surface disinfection but are not approved for direct application to mushrooms in most jurisdictions.
- **[[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]]:** Dilute H₂O₂ solutions (0.5-1%) can reduce bacterial loads on surfaces and equipment.
