# Oyster Mushroom Brown Blotch Disease

Brown blotch disease causes significant crop loss in oyster mushroom cultivation and is very common
in mushroom houses. The disease manifests in two distinct forms -- **bacterial brown blotch** and
**fungal brown blotch** -- each with different pathogens, symptoms, and control measures. Yellowing
of fruiting bodies can also be caused by environmental factors, particularly rapid humidity changes
from excessive ventilation.

## Bacterial Brown Blotch

### Pathogen

- **Primary pathogen**: *Pseudomonas tolaasii*
- **Secondary reported agent**: *Pseudomonas agarici* (importance as a pathogen of brown blotch
  disease is questionable)
- *P. tolaasii* has been isolated from all observed symptomatic mushrooms, though mixed infections
  may contribute to the variety of symptoms seen

### Symptoms

Bacterial brown blotch presents with various symptoms on oyster mushroom fruiting bodies:

**Typical brown spot symptoms:**
- Brown spots appear on caps and stipes
- Spots enlarge and coalesce with other spots
- Affected areas become sunken and covered with sticky material
- A distinctive **rotten fish smell** is evident at this stage

**Severe symptoms:**
- The entire fruiting body may become discolored **reddish-brown** and appear waterlogged
- Young fruiting bodies are covered with a clear, glossy material and **stop growing** entirely

### Control Measures

**Sanitation and cultural practices:**

- Sanitation is the basic control measure -- follow strict disease and pest management protocols
- **Pasteurize substrates thoroughly** and use only healthy spawn
- **Control mushroom flies** -- they are well-known vectors of the pathogen
- Maintain **constant humidity and temperature** in growing houses; abrupt changes increase brown
  blotch incidence
- **Avoid free water** on fruiting bodies, as it allows pathogenic bacteria to multiply rapidly
- **Ventilate after watering** to remove standing water from mushroom surfaces
- **Do not over-water** -- brown blotch is favored by excessive moisture

**Chlorinated water treatment:**

Chlorinated water is effective for preventing brown blotch disease. Several chlorine sources are
available:

| Product | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) | Most commonly used |
| Calcium hypochlorite [Ca(OCl)2] | Most commonly used |
| Biospot (sodium dichloroisocyanurate) | Recently become available |

Active chlorine content varies among formulas, and chlorine vaporizes easily, so concentrations
must be monitored:

- **Routine prevention**: **5 ppm** chlorinated water (active chlorine concentration)
- **Active brown blotch observed**: increase to **20 ppm** chlorinated water

## Fungal Brown Blotch

### Pathogen

- **Suspected pathogen**: *Verticillium fungicola*
- The actual cause of the disease remains **unconfirmed** -- the pathogen is suspected but not yet
  definitively proved

### Symptoms

Fungal brown blotch produces different symptoms than the bacterial form:

- Mushroom cap is **partially or entirely discolored yellow to brown**
- Spots are **not as clearly defined** as those caused by bacterial brown blotch
- Fruiting body shapes become **abnormal**
- Mushrooms **stop growing** (stunted growth)
- **Malformation** of caps and stipes occurs

### Control Measures

Critically, fungal brown blotch does **not** respond to treatments effective against bacterial brown
blotch:

- **Bactericides and chlorine disinfectants are NOT effective**
- **Fungicides are effective**, including:
  - Sporgon (prochloraz-manganese complex)
  - Benomyl
  - Thiabendazole

The fact that fungicides rather than bactericides are effective strongly indicates that the cause
of this form of brown blotch is fungal rather than bacterial, despite the similar visual appearance.

## Distinguishing Between Bacterial and Fungal Brown Blotch

| Feature | Bacterial | Fungal |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Pathogen | *Pseudomonas tolaasii* | *Verticillium fungicola* (suspected) |
| Spot definition | Clear, well-defined spots | Less defined discoloration |
| Odor | Rotten fish smell | No distinctive odor |
| Fruit body shape | Generally normal shape | Abnormal, stunted, malformed |
| Effective treatment | Chlorinated water, sanitation | Sporgon, benomyl, thiabendazole |
| Chlorine response | Effective | Not effective |
