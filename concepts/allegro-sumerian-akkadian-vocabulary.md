# Sumerian and Akkadian Mushroom Vocabulary in Religious Texts

A central pillar of Allegro's argument in *The Sacred Mushroom and the Cross*
is his [[amanita-muscaria-cultural-history]] mushroom.

## The Sumerian Key

Allegro argued that Sumerian stands as a linguistic ancestor bridging the
Semitic languages (Hebrew, Aramaic, Arabic) and the Indo-European group
(Greek, Latin, Sanskrit). While modern linguistics does not accept Sumerian as
a common ancestor of these families, Allegro's method involved tracing
character-by-character correspondences between Sumerian ideograms and later
word forms, often through intermediate Akkadian (a Semitic language that
preserved many Sumerian loanwords).

He noted that the old Sumerian word for "physician," A-ZU or I-ZU, literally
"water-oil-expert," also stands for "prophet, seer." The name Essene, known
otherwise only in its Greek transliterated form, comes probably from the same
root. The [[amanita-muscaria-cultural-history]] is involved. Pliny
supports this by stating that "the fungi are all derived from the gum that
exudes from trees."

The name *kukru* itself links to the "swan" motif in Greek mythology through
a common Sumerian phrase meaning "pod" -- for the pine referring to kernels
like small "lice pods" (the "louse-tree"), and for the swan because, like
other fertility-birds, its name connected with the "womb-pod."

## Akkadian Tigilla Uprooting Ritual

An Akkadian magical text provides detailed instructions for extracting the
*tigilla* (bolt-plant, i.e., mushroom):

> "Go, my son, the tigilla, which springs up of its own accord in the
> desert -- when the sun enters its dwelling, cover your head with a cloth,
> and cover the tigilla, surrounding it with flour, and in the morning before
> sunrise root it out of its place, and take its root."

This ritual matches Josephus's description of the Mandrake extraction near
the Dead Sea, where the plant had to be covered and approached under darkness.
The flour encirclement offered magical protection and was "a token of
compensation to the earth for its rape." See
[[allegro-names-of-the-gods-and-their-fertility-origins]].
