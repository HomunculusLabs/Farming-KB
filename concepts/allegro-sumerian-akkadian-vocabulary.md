# Sumerian and Akkadian Mushroom Vocabulary in Religious Texts

A central pillar of Allegro's argument in *The Sacred Mushroom and the Cross*
is his [[allegro-philological-methodology]]: the claim
that the ancient Sumerian language, preserved in cuneiform texts from the
fourth millennium BC, provides the key to decoding names of gods, heroes, and
sacred plants across both the Semitic and Indo-European language families. He proposed that many apparently obscure
proper names and epithets in biblical and classical texts are actually
disguised references to the [[amanita-muscaria-cultural-history]] mushroom.

## The Sumerian Key

Allegro argued that Sumerian stands as a linguistic ancestor bridging the
Semitic languages (Hebrew, Aramaic, Arabic) and the Indo-European group
(Greek, Latin, Sanskrit). While modern linguistics does not accept Sumerian as
a common ancestor of these families, Allegro's method involved tracing
character-by-character correspondences between Sumerian ideograms and later
word forms, often through intermediate Akkadian (a Semitic language that
preserved many Sumerian loanwords).

He noted that the old Sumerian word for "physician," A-ZU or I-ZU, literally
"water-oil-expert," also stands for "prophet, seer." The name Essene, known
otherwise only in its Greek transliterated form, comes probably from the same
root. The [[allegro-dead-sea-scrolls-and-early-christian-sects]] were
thus, in etymological terms, healers -- and their healing arts were inseparable
from their use of drug plants.

## Mushroom Names in Cuneiform Texts

Among the most important Sumerian-derived mushroom names Allegro identified:

- **UKUSh-TI-GIL-LA**: A "jumbled version" giving the Greek *Glykyside* for the
  Holy Plant. An Akkadian magical text instructs in the uprooting of the
  *tigilla*, the "bolt-plant" or "phallus-plant" (mushroom).

- **MASh-TAB-BA-RI/LI**: Source of the word "tablet" via Greek *tabula*.
  Also a play on "five words" -- the number of commandments on each of the
  two stone tablets given to Moses. The two tablets represent the two halves
  of the split mushroom volva.

- **GEShPU-AN-UR**: "Strong man (holding up) the sky," which became the Greek
  *Boanerges* ("Sons of Thunder") and also the name *Pollux*, one of the
  Dioscuri twins representing the mushroom stem.

- **TAR**: A Sumerian element appearing in many mushroom-related names,
  including the Semitic root for "serpent" (*siph'oni*, "adder"), itself a
  mushroom reference in Isaiah 59:5.

## The Akkadian Kukru Incantation

One of the most significant pieces of evidence Allegro cites is an Akkadian
incantation directed at pine-resin (*kukru*): "O kukru, kukru, kukru, in the
pure, holy mountains thou hast engendered 'little-ones' by a sacred prostitute,
'seeds-of-a-Pine' by a vestal." The plea follows that whatever sorcery may
have been begotten shall be dispersed.

Allegro interprets the "little-ones" and "seeds-of-a-Pine" as clearly
substitute-words for some magic vegetation too powerful to be given its proper
name. Their manner of "engendering" by sacred prostitutes and their resinous
origin from conifers leave little doubt, in his view, that the
[[amanita-muscaria-cultural-history]] is involved. Pliny
supports this by stating that "the fungi are all derived from the gum that
exudes from trees."

The name *kukru* itself links to the "swan" motif in Greek mythology through
a common Sumerian phrase meaning "pod" -- for the pine referring to kernels
like small "lice pods" (the "louse-tree"), and for the swan because, like
other fertility-birds, its name connected with the "womb-pod."

## Akkadian Tigilla Uprooting Ritual

An Akkadian magical text provides detailed instructions for extracting the
*tigilla* (bolt-plant, i.e., mushroom):

> "Go, my son, the tigilla, which springs up of its own accord in the
> desert -- when the sun enters its dwelling, cover your head with a cloth,
> and cover the tigilla, surrounding it with flour, and in the morning before
> sunrise root it out of its place, and take its root."

This ritual matches Josephus's description of the Mandrake extraction near
the Dead Sea, where the plant had to be covered and approached under darkness.
The flour encirclement offered magical protection and was "a token of
compensation to the earth for its rape." See
[[allegro-plants-and-drugs-in-the-ancient-world]].

## Vocabulary of Semen and Life

A recurring pattern in Allegro's Sumerian etymologies is the equivalence of
semen, divine seed, intoxication, and life. In Sumerian, the words for "live"
and "intoxicate" are the same: TIN. The "tree of life," GEShTIN, is the
"vine." Similarly, Greek *amos* and Hebrew *yayin* ("wine") share a probable
Sumerian root *IA-U-NU*, meaning "semen-seed."

The name Jesus (Greek *Iesous*) is thus appropriate as a healing invocation.
Its Hebrew original *Yehoshua* comes from Sumerian *JA-U-ShUShA (Shush)*,
"semen, which saves, restores, heals." Hellenized Jews used for "Joshua" the
Greek *Iason* (*Jason*), since *iason* ("healer") and *iaomai* ("heal") come
from the same source.

## Demon and Drug: Etymological Unity

The Greek *daimon* derives through Persian *dew* from a probable Sumerian
original *DA-...-U-NA*, meaning "having power over fertility." The demon thus
had power over birth, death, and health. The Hebrew *dawah* ("be sick") and
its Arabic cognate meaning "medicine" come from the same root. "So the demon
of health and sickness and the drug are radically one and the same."

This linguistic unity of demon, disease, and drug pervades the ancient medical
and religious worldview. The physician-prophet had to know the names of
disease-demons to counteract them, and the names of healing angels (contained
in drug plants) to deploy their power. See
[[allegro-names-of-the-gods-and-their-fertility-origins]].
