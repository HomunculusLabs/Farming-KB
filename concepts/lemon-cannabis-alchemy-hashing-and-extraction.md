## Lemon Cannabis Alchemy - Hashing and Extraction

Ben Lemon's "Cannabis Alchemy" provides detailed methods for extracting, purifying, and refining cannabis oils, along with techniques for producing hashish from [[cannabis-solvent-extraction]] through increasingly refined processes including isomerization, acetylation, and charcoal filtration to produce oils of extraordinary potency.

## Overview of the Extraction Process

The extraction process follows six major steps: preparation of the cannabis material, pulverization, refluxing (solvent extraction), soxhleting (washing), solvent removal, and purification. Each step is described with equipment that can be assembled from household and hardware-store items.

### Step 1: Preparation

Marijuana seeds are removed and the remaining material is crumbled and dried thoroughly in an oven preheated to 250F, placed on a cookie sheet for fifteen-minute intervals until the leaf and flower parts crumble easily to powder between the palms. Hashish is heated in an oven or frying pan at low heat until it begins to smoke slightly, then crumbled by hand or ground in a mortar and pestle. Powdered hashish exposed to air decreases in potency, so grinding is done just prior to extraction.

### Step 2: Pulverization

Reducing material to the finest powder possible ruptures cell walls so the oil is extracted more readily and reduces the volume of [[cannabis-reflux-extraction-apparatus]] and amount of solvent required. A heavy-duty blender at slower speed is used, with the material agitated with a wooden stick when the blender is not running. Pressed hashish may be shredded with a cheese grater before blending.

### Step 3: Refluxing

### Step 4: Soxhleting

After refluxing, the oil-bearing solvent remaining in the expended cannabis material is drained and washed repeatedly with clean solvent through a coffee-filter-lined colander. The recondensed solvent washes through the cannabis material until several drops of the draining liquid leave no colored residue when evaporated on glass.

### Step 5: Solvent Removal

The solvent is distilled off by replacing the expended cannabis with a collection pan in the colander. The solvent/oil solution boils; fumes condense on the ice-cooled lid and drip into the collection pan. Collected solvent is essentially pure and can be saved for future extractions. After solvent removal, the pot is kept in boiling water to remove all traces. For toxic or water-containing solvents, water is added and evaporated in an oil bath at approximately 220F; when water is gone, all solvent traces have been removed since all solvents evaporate below water's boiling point.

## Translucent (Honey) Oil

One of the most potent and popular preparations is a thick, translucent, amber oil extracted from hashish. The consistency is achieved by dissolving purified cannabis oil (which may or may not be isomerized or acetylated) in ten times its volume of pure alcohol. Granulated activated charcoal (Norit) equal to half the weight of the oil is added, the solution is filtered through fine filter paper, and the alcohol is evaporated. The residue is a translucent amber oil with the appearance and consistency of dark honey.

## Preparation of Hashish from Extracted Oil

Hashish is prepared by mixing extracted cannabis oil with finely-powdered marijuana.

The oil may be at any stage of refinement; isomerized, acetylated, and refined oils
produce extremely strong hashish. The oil-to-powder ratio determines strength:

- A minimum of 15% oil produces Moroccan or Lebanese-style powdery hash
- 50-60% oil produces sticky, pliable Nepalese charas-like hash

The powdered marijuana is produced by pulverizing very dry material in a high-speed blender; fine dust collects on the blender top and is sifted through nylon stocking or fine mesh screen. Taste may be improved by extracting the dust with alcohol and boiling in water to remove flavor compounds. The cannabis dust is thoroughly dried before mixing. The oil and dust are heated and worked together in a mortar or kneaded by hand. Products can be formed into flat patties (Afghan style), fingers or temple balls (Nepal style), or pressed between heated steel plates in a vise.

## Dry-Sieving (Screening) for Hash

The "Super Hash" method described in the book involves dry-sieving resin glands ("pollen") from intact cannabis flowers. A 12x18-inch wooden frame is built from 2x2 lumber and a piece of nylon mesh cloth (similar to nylon stockings) is stretched over it. The frame is held over a large tray, and intact dried flowers are gently shaken on the cloth surface. The loose material and seeds rub against the mesh, and fine granules of resin glands fall through. From one pound of high-quality Mexican flowers, approximately half an ounce of pure fine "pollen" was obtained. The flowers retained their potency after screening, while the collected pollen hash was considerably stronger and tastier.

This screened pollen can then be extracted with isopropyl alcohol in an ISO-2 apparatus, refluxed for four hours, followed by isomerization with an activator for two hours. After neutralization and solvent removal, water is added and the mixture is poured through a bedsheet and squeezed to produce a ball of hashish.

## Hash Pressing

An ISO-Hash press can be constructed from a square of one-inch steel tubing about two feet long, three pieces of quarter-inch hard steel (base plate, plunger plate, and guide), and a quarter-inch diameter rod. Hash is loaded through a five-eighths-inch loading hole and packed with the plunger until sufficiently compressed. The base rods are then removed and the entire assembly pushed out from the bottom, yielding a pressed cube of hash.

## High-Volume Extraction

For large-scale operations, the book describes an apparatus built from two 55-gallon oil drums. The lower drum holds the powdered cannabis material and solvent, the upper drum serves as a condenser with copper cooling tubes. The drums are sealed with inner-tube rubber bands and [[passive-humidity-fruit-chamber-polyethylene-sheeting]] (which also serves as a safety valve). The apparatus is heated in a water tub on three sparkless electric hotplates, with ice-water coolant circulated through the condenser by an immersion pump. The process follows the same principles as the kitchen-scale method: refluxing for three hours, draining and collecting the oil/solvent mixture, distilling off the solvent, then soxhleting the remaining cannabis material. Multiple passes through the soxhlet chamber ensure complete extraction.
