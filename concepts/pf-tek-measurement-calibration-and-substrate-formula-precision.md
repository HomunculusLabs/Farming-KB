# PF Tek Measurement Calibration and Substrate Formula Precision

## Overview

[[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] emphasised that measurement accuracy is critical
to successful [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]. The PF [[pf-tek-substrate-moisture-diagnosis-and-correction]] formula uses
small volumes in half-pint jars, where even minor measurement errors
can lead to [[slimy-clump-water-logged-grass-seed-substrate-failure-modes]]. Fanaticus specifically warned that
inaccurate measuring cups "will set up certain failure or diminished
growth."

## Standard PF Substrate Formula (Per Half-Pint Jar)

### Basic Formula
- **1/8 cup** brown rice powder
- **1/2 to 2/3 cup** horticultural vermiculite (medium grade)
- **40-45 cc** (millilitres) of water — approximately 1/4 cup,
  or equivalently 1.5 ounces / 3 tablespoons + 1 teaspoon

### Maximum Fruiting Formula
- **1/4 cup** brown rice powder (double the basic amount)
- **1/2 cup** vermiculite
- **60 cc** water

The maximum fruiting formula increases the nutrient load but may
require loose jar lids during incubation to accommodate the wetter
substrate, particularly when using fine-grade vermiculite.

## Cup Calibration

Fanaticus provided specific calibration standards that are essential
for reproducing the PF Tek:

- **1 cup = 237 millilitres = 1/2 pint = 8 liquid ounces**
- **2 cups = 1 pint**
- **2 pints = 1 quart**
- **4 cups = 1 quart**

These are US customary measurements. Fanaticus warned that if measuring
cup specifications are not true to these standards, the substrate
formulas will be off. This is particularly important because:

1. Many kitchen measuring cups are designed for dry ingredients and
   may not accurately hold liquid volumes
2. The difference between 1/8 cup and a slightly overfilled 1/8 cup
   represents a proportionally large error in a half-pint jar
3. European users may encounter metric-only measuring equipment
   requiring careful conversion

## Metric Conversions for International Use

For cultivators using metric measurements:

| Imperial | Metric Equivalent |
|----------|------------------|
| 1/8 cup | ~30 ml brown rice powder |
| 1/2 cup | ~118 ml vermiculite |
| 1/4 cup | ~59 ml brown rice powder |
| 40-45 cc water | 40-45 ml water |
| 60 cc water | 60 ml water |
| 1/2 pint jar | 250 ml (8 oz) jar |

## Brown Rice Powder Preparation

### Freshness Matters

Fanaticus recommended freshly ground brown rice powder over prepackaged
commercial varieties. He noted that "freshness sometimes makes a big
difference" in colonization success and eventual fruiting performance.

### Grinding Method

Place regular brown rice in a small canister-type coffee bean grinder
and grind to fine powder. The powder should be consistently fine
with no large particles remaining, as uneven particle size affects
both water absorption and [[jar-shaking-grain-redistribution-mycelial-colonisation]] speed.

### Source

Brown rice powder is available from health food stores and food
cooperatives. The whole brown rice for grinding can be purchased at
the same locations or at any grocery store.
