# Lakhovsky's Multi-Wave Oscillator and Cellular Oscillation Theory

Georges Lakhovsky (1869-1942), a Russian-born engineer living in Paris,
proposed a revolutionary theory that the basis of life is not matter but
immaterial vibrations. His work, conducted primarily in the 1920s and
1930s, suggested that cells are microscopic oscillating circuits capable
of emitting and absorbing high-frequency waves, and that disease results
from disequilibrium in cellular oscillation.

## The Theory of Cellular Oscillation

Lakhovsky's central thesis was that "every living thing emits
radiations" and that cells, the essential organic units of all living
things, are electromagnetic radiators capable, like wireless sets, of
emitting and absorbing high-frequency waves. In electrical terms, an
oscillating circuit requires two basic elements: a capacitor (source of
stored electric charge) and a coil of wire. As current flows back and
forth, it creates a magnetic field oscillating at a certain frequency.

Lakhovsky believed that in the microscopically tiny nuclei of living
cells, the small twisted filaments function as analogs to electrical
circuits. When greatly reduced in size, very high frequencies are
obtained. In the L"Origine de la Vie (1925), Lakhovsky set forth his
theory that disease is a matter of disequilibrium in cellular
oscillation -- that the fight between healthy cells and pathogens is
a "war of radiations."

If the radiations of the microbes are stronger, cells begin to
oscillate aperiodically and become "diseased." When they cease to
oscillate, they die. If the cellular radiations gain ascendance, the
microbes are killed. To restore a diseased cell to health, Lakhovsky
argued, it should be treated with a radiation of appropriate frequency.
