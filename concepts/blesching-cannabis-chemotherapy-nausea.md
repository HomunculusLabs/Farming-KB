## Dronabinol and Nabilone: Pharmaceutical Cannabinoids

The FDA has approved two synthetic cannabinoid medications specifically
for CINV: dronabinol (Marinol, synthetic THC) and nabilone (Cesamet, a
synthetic THC analog). These pharmaceutical products provide standardized
dosing and are covered by some insurance plans. However, Blesching notes
that many patients report better symptom control with whole-plant cannabis,
likely due to the presence of additional cannabinoids and terpenes that
modulate THC's effects. The onset of action also differs: oral
pharmaceutical cannabinoids have a delayed onset (45-90 minutes), while
inhaled cannabis provides more rapid relief (minutes), which is
particularly valuable during acute nausea episodes.

## Combination Therapy Approaches

Modern oncology increasingly uses combination antiemetic regimens, and
cannabinoids can play a role in these protocols. Blesching discusses the
potential for combining cannabinoids with 5-HT3 receptor antagonists
(ondansetron) and NK1 receptor antagonists (aprepitant) for multi-target
emetic control. This approach may be especially useful for patients
receiving highly emetogenic chemotherapy regimens.
