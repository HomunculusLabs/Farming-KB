## The Potassium-Yield vs Nutrition Tradeoff

This is one of the most important — and least discussed — insights in Steve
Solomon's work.

When soil [[cannabis-potassium]] levels are pushed high (as commercial growers routinely
do), **bulk yield increases but nutritional content decreases by roughly the
same proportion**. The higher yield comes almost entirely in the form of
calories — specifically starch and fiber — not protein, vitamins, enzymes, or
minerals.

In practical terms: you get more weight per acre, but each pound of that
harvest is nutritionally dilute. The vegetable looks impressive and weighs more,
but it feeds you less.

This tradeoff exists because high potassium availability shifts the plant's
metabolism toward carbohydrate production at the expense of protein synthesis
and mineral uptake. The plant literally fills with empty bulk.

### Who Pushes Potassium and Why

Both chemical and organic commercial growers push potassium for profit. Bulk
yield determines revenue at market, and nutritional quality is invisible to the
buyer. There is no economic incentive to grow more nutritious food — only to
grow more of it. The result is a food system optimized for weight and
appearance, not for human health.

## Chemical vs Organic Nutrient Release

### Chemical Fertilizers: Fast In, Fast Out

Chemical fertilizers dissolve rapidly in soil water, delivering an immediate
surge of available nutrients. This causes a rapid growth boost followed by a
pronounced sag as the nutrient spike is consumed or leached away. The grower
must then apply more fertilizer to recover, creating a cycle of boom-and-bust
feeding.

In soils with little clay content to hold cations, dissolved nutrients from
chemical fertilizers may leach away entirely before plants can absorb them. This
wastes money, pollutes groundwater, and leaves the crop undernourished despite
heavy fertilization.
