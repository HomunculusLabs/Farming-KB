# Training Program Structure and Curriculum

The mushroom cultivation training program for people with disabilities
follows a nine-week structured curriculum that combines theory, hands-on
practice, guest lectures, field trips, and personal development. Training
is divided into two parts: theoretical understanding and practical
hands-on experience. Several specialists, teachers, and guest speakers
participate to offer a wider approach and maintain trainee interest.

## Training Approach

Training is conducted primarily as a hands-on experience with some basic
theory for better understanding of the meaning behind each activity.
Trainers must closely monitor procedures and actions, showing trainees
how to develop ease in accomplishing all required tasks. Alternative
approaches for accomplishing certain tasks are developed to help trainees
with specific disabilities. Trainees who have become trainers are in the
best position to teach others about different ways of accomplishing tasks.

## Start-Up Procedure

Before official training begins on opening ceremony day, formalities are
necessary: registration of trainees and accommodation organization,
meetings to review center regulations and training guidelines, preparation
of the opening ceremony, and the ceremony itself with municipal and
provincial officials from Public Welfare, Ministry of Agriculture, and
Ministry of Labour and Social Welfare.

## Four Training Sectors

Trainees are divided into four sub-groups of 4 to 6 persons each, with
each group rotating through four main sectors of activity:

1. **Preparation of sawdust bags** - Sieving, mixing, bagging, transfer
   of materials for inoculation, waste management including composting
   and recycling, and cultivation.
2. **Pasteurization and inoculation** - Materials preparation, cleaning,
   recording, and labeling of processed bags.
3. **Incubation and fruiting body management** - Carrying bags between
   incubation and fruiting areas, disease management, watering, and
   ventilation control.
4. **Harvesting, packaging, processing, and marketing** - Post-harvest
   handling through to final sale.

## Nine-Week Proposed Schedule

### Week 1: Orientation and Introduction

Days 1-2 cover registration, accommodation, center regulations, and the
opening ceremony. An overview of the mushroom world covers nature,
humidity needs, ventilation, light, diseases, insects, nutritional
properties, and recipes. Days 3-4 begin hands-on practice with sub-groups.

### Week 2: Deepening Practice

Guest speakers discuss the mushroom world in detail. Hands-on practice
continues with the second week of sub-group rotation through the four
sectors.

### Week 3: Microbiology and Production Management

Topics include estimating manageable production amounts (determined
individually), general microbiology and mushroom culture, how microbes
grow in nature, and scientific cultivation in aseptic conditions.
Hands-on practice continues with group rotation.

### Week 4: Cultivation Techniques and Pest Management

Guest speakers cover cultivation in plastic bags, management of mushroom
houses, temperature, humidity, and ventilation control. A separate session
addresses disease and pest identification and control using both chemical
and natural methods.

### Week 5: Motivation and Continued Practice

The second series of motivational sessions reinforces personal
development (see [[motivational-sessions-trainees]]). Hands-on practice
continues with group rotation.

### Week 6: Spawn Production and Marketing

Topics include mushroom spawn development in agar and seed, selecting
clean and healthy spawn, identifying various spawn types, and packaging
and marketing strategies for remote communities. Hands-on practice
continues with group rotation.

### Week 7: Advanced Cultivation and Processing

Guest speakers cover cultivation in logs and straw mushrooms, which use
different techniques. Waste management covers converting cultivation
waste into organic fertilizer. Processing covers transformation of
mushrooms into sweet snacks and other food products. Hands-on practice
continues with group rotation.

### Week 8: Enterprise Development

Running a feasible small-scale business in mushroom production is the
focus, covering management and basic bookkeeping. Hands-on practice
continues with the final week of group rotation.

### Week 9: Field Trips and Evaluation

Visits to small and medium-sized mushroom farms in the region provide
real-world exposure. Training evaluation verifies how well each trainee
has learned necessary tasks. A closing ceremony distributes achievement
certificates to trainees.

## Theory to Practice Mapping

The curriculum maps theoretical knowledge to practical skills across three
blocks: mushroom biology and culture (nature of mushrooms, tissue culture,
spawn production); production management (substrate bags, incubation,
fruiting body, harvesting, marketing); and enterprise operations
(equipment, waste management, farm management, processing, pest control).

## Guest Speakers

Invited guest speakers are welcomed to reduce monotony and share
experience as specialists or entrepreneurs. Preferred speakers are
successful entrepreneurs who have overcome disease and pest problems and
understand the market. Each successful entrepreneur introduces their own
personal methods, teaching trainees that multiple approaches can succeed.
