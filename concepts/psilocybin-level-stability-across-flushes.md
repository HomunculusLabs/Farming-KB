# Psilocybin Level Stability Across Flushes

## Overview

A key finding from Bigwood and Beug (1982) is that [[psilocybin-compound]] levels in [[psilocybe-cubensis]] showed no consistent upward or downward
trend as fruiting progressed through multiple flushes. While individual flushes varied unpredictably by a factor of four, the overall
psilocybin concentration at the final flush was generally comparable to the first flush. This stability contrasts sharply with [[psilocin]],
which systematically accumulates over successive flushes. Understanding this divergent behavior is essential for predicting total alkaloid
yield across a cultivation cycle and for interpreting the biosynthetic relationship between these two closely related compounds.

## Complete Data From Three Independent Minicultures

The study tracked three independent minicultures of an Amazonian strain across five to six flushes each, providing robust replication of the
stability observation. Each miniculture was grown from the same stock culture on identical rye-grain substrate, cased with the same
peat-calcium carbonate-perlite mixture, and maintained under the same environmental conditions.

### Miniculture 1 (Six Flushes Achieved)

| Flush | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| 1     | 8.3               | 0.5             |
| 2     | 6.5               | 1.5             |
| 3     | 13.3              | 1.0             |
| 4     | 4.8               | 2.6             |
| 5     | consumed          | consumed        |
| 6     | 6.8               | 0.5             |

Miniculture 1 showed the widest range of psilocybin values, from 4.8 mg/g (flush 4) to 13.3 mg/g (flush 3), a range of 2.8-fold. Flush 5 was
entirely consumed in a separate experiment and is not reported. The sixth flush returned to 6.8 mg/g, comparable to the first flush value of
8.3 mg/g, confirming the absence of a declining trend even after six production cycles from the same 10 grams of rye grain.

### Miniculture 2 (Five Flushes)

| Flush | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| 1     | 5.1               | 0               |
| 2     | 7.0               | 0               |
| 3     | 4.7               | 1.7             |
| 4     | 3.7               | 2.9             |
| 5     | 5.2               | 2.2             |

Miniculture 2 showed peak psilocybin at flush 2 (7.0 mg/g) and minimum at flush 4 (3.7 mg/g), a range of 1.9-fold. The final flush returned
to 5.2 mg/g, nearly identical to the first flush value of 5.1 mg/g. This near-perfect match between first and last flush (only 2%
difference) provides the strongest evidence for the no-trend conclusion.

### Miniculture 3 (Five Flushes)

| Flush | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| 1     | 7.6               | 0               |
| 2     | 6.2               | 0               |
| 3     | 5.3               | 0.9             |
| 4     | 3.2               | 1.8             |
| 5     | 6.7               | 1.7             |

Miniculture 3 actually showed its highest psilocybin at the first flush (7.6 mg/g) with a decline through flush 4 (3.2 mg/g) before
rebounding to 6.7 mg/g at flush 5. This rebound at the final flush is particularly notable because it demonstrates that psilocybin
biosynthesis capacity is maintained throughout the fruiting cycle even after an apparent mid-cycle decline.

## No Directional Trend Confirmed Across All Three Replicates

Across all three minicultures, the psilocybin level at the final measurable flush was within the range observed at the first flush:
- Miniculture 1: 6.8 mg/g (flush 6) versus 8.3 mg/g (flush 1), a difference of only 18%
- Miniculture 2: 5.2 mg/g (flush 5) versus 5.1 mg/g (flush 1), a difference of only 2%
- Miniculture 3: 6.7 mg/g (flush 5) versus 7.6 mg/g (flush 1), a difference of only 12%

These small differences between first and last flushes stand in stark contrast to the large flush-to-flush fluctuations within each culture.
If there were a systematic trend (either increasing or decreasing), the difference between first and last flush would be large and
consistent across all three cultures. The absence of such a pattern in any of the three independent replicates provides strong evidence that
psilocybin levels are homeostatically regulated across the fruiting cycle.

## Factor of Four Within-Culture Variability

Although no trend exists across flushes, individual flush-to-flush variation spans a factor of approximately four within a single culture.
Miniculture 1 ranged from 4.8 mg/g to 13.3 mg/g. Miniculture 2 ranged from 3.7 mg/g to 7.0 mg/g. Miniculture 3 ranged from 3.2 mg/g to 7.6
mg/g. This variability was observed even under rigorously controlled conditions with identical substrate, casing, watering, and environment.

The source of this within-culture variability remains unexplained. Potential contributing factors include microclimate differences between
jars despite identical treatment, subtle variations in moisture content of individual fruiting bodies at harvest, differences in maturation
stage or exact harvest timing, stochastic variation in gene expression affecting alkaloid biosynthesis enzymes, and possible
micro-differences in grain colonization density that affect nutrient availability.

## Contrast With Psilocin Accumulation

[[psilocin]] displays fundamentally opposite behavior across the flush cycle: it is typically absent in the first one or two flushes, rises
steadily through flushes 3 and 4, and then may begin to decline. This divergent behavior between the two compounds has significant
implications for understanding their biosynthetic relationship and for predicting the psychoactive profile of mushrooms from different flush
numbers. The fact that psilocybin remains stable while its dephosphorylated product accumulates suggests that total tryptamine alkaloid
content actually increases across the flush cycle. See [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]] for detailed analysis of the
psilocin accumulation pattern.

## Biosynthetic Interpretation

The stability of psilocybin across flushes while psilocin accumulates suggests several possibilities about
[[psilocybin-biosynthesis-pathway]]:

1. Psilocybin biosynthesis reaches a steady state early in the fruiting cycle and is maintained at relatively constant capacity by the mycelium throughout its productive life, regardless of the number of flushes already produced or the remaining nutrient reserves.

2. The accumulation of psilocin likely reflects progressive dephosphorylation of psilocybin over time, mediated by phosphatase enzymes whose activity increases as the mycelium ages or as substrate nutrients are depleted and the organism shifts metabolic priorities.

3. Alternatively, the two compounds may be independently regulated, with psilocybin synthesis maintaining homeostatic levels while psilocin synthesis ramps up independently through a different branch of the tryptamine biosynthetic pathway.

The data alone cannot distinguish between these mechanisms, but the pattern is consistent and reproducible across three independent
cultures, suggesting a robust biological phenomenon rather than a stochastic artifact.

## Total Tryptamine Content Across Flushes

When psilocybin and psilocin are summed to estimate total tryptamine alkaloid content, an interesting pattern emerges. In miniculture 2, for
example, total tryptamine rises from 5.1 mg/g (flush 1) to 6.6 mg/g (flush 3) to 7.4 mg/g (flush 4), a 45% increase from first to fourth
flush. This means that despite stable psilocybin, the overall potency of the mushrooms actually increases in later flushes due to psilocin
accumulation. This finding has practical implications for cultivators seeking to maximize yield of psychoactive compounds per gram of dried
material.

## Practical Implications for Cultivators

Since psilocybin content does not systematically decline with flush number, cultivators should not assume that later flushes are necessarily
weaker in psilocybin. The total alkaloid profile does shift however, as psilocin increases substantially in later flushes. The combined
psilocybin plus psilocin content may actually peak at flushes 3 through 4, even though psilocybin alone remains relatively stable. This has
implications for harvest timing decisions and for maximizing total tryptamine yield from a given culture. Cultivators who discard later
flushes as weak may be throwing away material with higher total alkaloid content than their first flush produced.
