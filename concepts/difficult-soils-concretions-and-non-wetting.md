## Difficult Soils — Concretions, Pans, and Non-Wetting Sands

## Overview

Many soils present physical barriers to plant growth in the form of cemented subsurface layers (concretions or pans) or hydrophobic surface conditions (non-wetting sands and clays). Understanding these difficult soil types and their remedies is essential for successful land management, especially in arid, tropical, and coastal environments.

## Types of Soil Concretions and Pans

### Plastic Liner Technique for Deep Sands

For acid or deep silica sands, a practical garden technique involves:
1. Laying plastic sheeting at 0.5 m depth in garden beds
2. Adding clay above the plastic to hold water at root level
3. Mulching on top to prevent surface evaporation

## Sodium-Rich Clays

Clays that seal on the surface in light rains are often sodium-rich and "melt" in rain. Remedies include:
- Making low banks across run-off
- Adding gypsum at 2–3 handfuls per square metre
- Flushing out sodium as sulphate with fresh water if possible
- Placing sand over the surface to 4 cm depth

For deep cracking clays and lumpy soils, add a sand layer, scatter gypsum at a handful per square metre, and mulch.
