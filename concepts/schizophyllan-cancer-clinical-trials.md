# Schizophyllan Cancer Clinical Trials

Schizophyllan (Sonifilan, Sizofiran, SPG) is a [[beta-glucan-receptor-binding]]
polysaccharide derived from schizophyllum commune (split gill fungus).
It is a beta(1,3)-D glucan with beta(1,6)-D glucan side-chains that has
been investigated as an immunotherapeutic agent in cancer treatment since
the 1970s, primarily in Japan.

## Chemical Properties

Schizophyllan consists of large molecules with a wide range of molecular
weights. As with all glucan preparations, the molecules are never
homologous in molecular weight. The large molecular size necessitates
clinical administration via intramuscular or intraperitoneal routes; oral
administration is ineffective.

## Pre-Clinical Activity

Schizophyllan demonstrated cytostatic activity in sarcoma 180 tumour
xenografts. Key observations:

- Combined pre- and post-treatment increased survival
- Pre-treatment alone did not affect survival
- No effect on Sarcoma 37, Ehrlich carcinoma, or Yoshida sarcoma ascites
- Mechanism is primarily immunostimulatory, consistent with other
  mushroom-derived [[beta-glucan-receptor-binding]] compounds

## Clinical Trials in Gastric Cancer

An early randomised controlled study evaluated Schizophyllan with tegafur
or mitomycin C and 5-fluorouracil in 367 patients with recurrent and
inoperable gastric cancer, finding a significant increase in median
survival (Furne, 1985). A subsequent study was unable to confirm this
result (Fugimoto et al., 1984), highlighting reproducibility challenges.

## Head and Neck Cancers

Schizophyllan increased overall survival in patients with head and neck
cancers (Kimura et al., 1994), expanding the therapeutic scope beyond the
more extensively studied gastrointestinal cancers.

## Cervical Cancer

In a randomised controlled study combining Schizophyllan with
radiotherapy, survival was significantly prolonged for Stage II
cervical cancer patients (Okamura et al., 1986, 1989) but not Stage III.
This differential effect may relate to immune competence preserved in
earlier-stage disease.

## Multimodal Therapy Trial

A 312-patient prospective trial (Miyazaki et al., 1995) used surgery,
radiotherapy, chemotherapy (fluorouracil), and Schizophyllan in various
combinations. Patients receiving Schizophyllan had better overall survival,
though the variety of regimens limited statistical power.

### Immune Correlates of Response

Patients with 10% or more activated CD4+ cells and more than 25% activated
CD8+ cells before treatment showed highly significant Schizophyllan-induced
survival increases. This suggests pre-existing immune competence predicts
efficacy, consistent with observations for [[psk-psp-polysaccharide-peptides]] and
lentinan.

### Intratumoral Injection

Intratumoral Schizophyllan injection into cervical cancers caused
significant infiltration of Langerhans cells and T-cells (Nakano et al.,
1996), providing direct evidence for immune cell recruitment to the tumour
microenvironment.

## Regulatory Status

Schizophyllan is approved for clinical use in Japan and produced
commercially by several Japanese pharmaceutical companies.

## Limitations

- Many Japanese clinical trials were not blinded
- Sample sizes sometimes inadequate for firm conclusions
- Biomarker-guided patient selection not yet routine
- Standardisation of treatment protocols varied across studies
- Reproducibility challenged by mixed gastric cancer results

## Future Directions

- Identification of immune biomarkers predicting response
- Optimisation of combination protocols with conventional therapies
- Development of formulations enabling oral bioavailability
