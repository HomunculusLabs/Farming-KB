## The Y2K Precedent

The vulnerability of modern systems to slight disturbance was highlighted in the late 1990s by the concern over the possible impacts of the Y2K computer bug and the enormous cost of reactive prevention strategies. This episode demonstrated how fragile systems based on continuous flow have become.

## Rediscovering Household Storage

In an emerging low-energy world, people will rediscover opportunities to harvest and store immediately available (on-site) renewable energies and wasted resources across their rural and urban landscapes, households, and local economies. This will be essential to avoid disasters from inevitable disruptions to energy and resource supply lines.

This rediscovery connects to [[holmgren-catch-and-store-energy-detailed]] to the preservation of biological and cultural heritage.

### Food Storage

Traditional methods of food preservation — drying, fermenting, pickling, root cellaring — represent household-scale energy storage. At Melliodora, simple drying of wood and crops uses solar energy directly for storage. With an evaporative cool cupboard, a seasonal diet and low-energy food preservation methods, electricity for refrigeration is more of an extra than an essential service.

### Biomass and Fuel

Wood and other biomass stored for heating and cooking represents captured solar energy. The accumulation of wood in living trees and as slowly curing firewood allows considerable flexibility in timing of harvest. At Melliodora, wood harvested by thinning from well-managed native forest is used for structural purposes and as fuel for cooking, water heating, and back-up heating.

## The Energy Circuit Model

Holmgren uses Howard Odum's energy circuit language to explain household storages: all biological and mineral resources can be considered (and measured) as embodied energy. The tools, infrastructure, and technology needed to support human society, whether simple or complex, all derive from primary energy sources. The more useful and durable forms into which we transform those sources are stores of high quality energy — or "real wealth."

The energy circuit model shows the interconnection between landscape elements: increasing power and value per unit energy and mass along the food chain, essential energy dissipation losses, and feedbacks of high quality energy to stimulate inflows.

## Low Depreciation Rate

An energy storage with a low depreciation rate will last for a long time, only slowly degrading in quality and quantity, as well as requiring minimal additional energy to maintain. If we can establish such stores when energy, information and labour are abundant, then a future low-energy society should be able to maintain them. The leaching of nutrients from well-balanced compost into the soil illustrates a store with a low depreciation rate that provides ongoing benefit.

## Depreciation and Maintenance

An important insight is that stored energy depreciates over time. Food spoils, wood rots, buildings decay. The maintenance of energy stores requires ongoing energy investment. This is why Holmgren emphasizes that depreciation rates of household and community storages matter as much as initial investment.

## Sources of Renewable Energy

Important sources of energy are currently poorly used but are commonly available for building greater personal and local self-reliance:

- Solar energy: simple drying of wood and crops, passive solar design, and devices such as dryers, water heaters and photo-electric panels

- Wind energy: pumping and power generation

- Biomass: the sustainable management of trees and forests for fuel and construction

# Holmgren Natural Capital Characteristics

Updated: 2026-04-18

David Holmgren discusses natural capital throughout *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* (2002), developing the concept from Howard Odum's systems ecology and extending it to permaculture design practice.

## Definition

Natural capital refers to the accumulated stores of energy and resources in natural systems that provide ongoing flows of goods and services. It is the ecological foundation of what Holmgren calls "real wealth" — the more useful and durable forms into which primary energy sources are transformed.

## The Four Key Storages

Holmgren identifies four key landscape storages of natural capital that permaculture strategies focus on rebuilding:

1. **Water** — stored in vegetation, soil, dams, aquifers, and stream systems
2. **Living soil** — organic matter, humus, microbial life, and mineral availability
3. **Trees** — standing biomass, root systems, and forest ecosystems
4. **Seed** — genetic resources for future production

## Erosion of Natural Capital

The capacity of terrestrial systems to hold water, mineral nutrients, and organic matter is limited and is always being eroded by gravity. Atmospheric oxygen continuously oxidizes minerals and breaks down organic matter. These forces have been consistent since life emerged from the sea; all terrestrial ecosystems can be seen as design systems to overcome or limit these effects.

## Geological Accumulation

Natural capital accumulation occurs through geological processes: mountain building and vulcanism deliver rock minerals, glacial grinding produces mineral fertilizer dust, and biological succession builds organic matter in soils. Geologically young regions tend to be much more biologically productive because of recent capital accumulation.

## Biological Enhancement

Beyond geological processes, biological systems actively enhance natural capital:

- Trees capture and store solar energy as wood
- Soil organisms cycle mineral nutrients
- Mycorrhizal networks extend nutrient reach of plant roots
