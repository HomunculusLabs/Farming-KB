## Second Bardo - Introduction to the Hallucinatory Period (Chonyid Bardo)

## Overview

The [[chonyid-bardo]], or Second Bardo, is the period of hallucinations that begins
when both the Primary and Secondary [[clear-light]] of the First Bardo have been
lost. In Leary"s adaptation, this stage is described as a realm of "karmic
illusions" and "intense hallucinatory mixtures of game reality" — a vivid,
uncontrolled flow of visionary experience in which the ego has not fully
reasserted itself but liberation from self has also slipped away.

## The Secondary Clear Light

Before the Second Bardo fully begins, there is a second chance at liberation.
Unlike the Primary Clear Light"s initial burst of content-free unity, the
Secondary Clear Light involves a distinctive flashing between two states:

- **Pure ego-less unity** — transcendence returns, but in diminished form.
- **Lucid, non-game selfhood** — conceptual clarity in which a knowing self
  hovers within transcendent terrain from which it is usually barred.

This oscillation produces an "intellectual ecstasy and understanding that
defies description." Previous philosophic reading suddenly takes on living
meaning. Both the mystic non-self and the mystic self become available within
a single session.

### The Ancient Debate: Part of the Sugar or Tasting It

This dual experience opens onto one of the oldest debates in Eastern
philosophy: *Is it better to be part of the sugar or to taste the sugar?*
Leary notes that thanks to experimental mysticism made possible by
consciousness-expanding drugs, one may actually experience the flashing back
and forth between these two states — knowing what academic monks could only
theorize about.

## Transition from First to Second Bardo

When the Secondary Clear Light fades, the ego begins to reassert itself but
never regains full control. Consciousness remains microscopically clear and
intense, yet is interrupted by fleeting attempts to rationalize. The normal
game-playing ego is not functioning effectively. Unlimited possibilities exist
for both delight and terror. The person stands on a threshold — not in
ordinary reality, but no longer liberated either.

## The Chonyid Bardo: Karmic Illusions and Hallucinatory Mixtures

The Chonyid Bardo is the Tibetan term for this intermediate period. It is
characterized by the eruption of stored mental content — memories, archetypes,
fears, desires, cultural imagery — into consciousness in vivid hallucinatory
form. Strange sounds, weird sights, and disturbed visions may occur.

### The Underlying Problem

Any and every shape — human, divine, diabolical, heroic, evil, animal, thing —
which the human brain conjures up or the past life recalls, can present itself
to consciousness: shapes and forms and sounds whirling by endlessly. The
cortex contains "file-cards for billions of images" from the history of the
person, the race, and living forms. Any of these can flood into awareness at
enormous rates. Bobbing in this brilliant sea of imagery is the remnant of the
conceptual mind, desperately trying to impose order on the ocean.

### The Underlying Solution

Recognize that your brain is producing the visions. They do not exist. Nothing
exists except as your consciousness gives it life. All deities and demons, all
heavens and hells are internal productions. Recognition of this truth gives
liberation. There is no reality behind any phenomenon of the ego-loss state
save the illusions stored in one"s own mind.

## Passive Integration: The Central Instruction

The key instruction for navigating the Second Bardo is **inaction**: passive
integration with all that occurs. The experienced person sits quietly,
controlling expanded awareness like a phantasmagoric multi-dimensional
television set. **The motto: peace, acceptance.** It is all an ever-changing
panorama. If one tries to impose will, rationalize, or seek explanations, one
gets caught in hallucinatory whirlpools.

## The Brotherhood of Breath: Physical Contact as Antidote

For those who cannot remain passive, there is one certain remedy: **physical
contact with another person**. The guide instructs the person to put their head
on the guide's lap or chest, place their face next to the guide's, and
concentrate on the movement and sound of breath — breathing deeply, feeling
the air rush in and the sighing release. Leary calls this "the oldest form of
living communication; the brotherhood of breath." The guide"s hand on the
forehead may add to relaxation. This tender huddling is natural — human beings
have gathered together during long, dark, confused nights for hundreds of
thousands of years.

## The Seven Types of Second Bardo Visions

Following the Tibetan Thodo, Leary classifies visions into seven types:

1. **The Source or Creator Vision** — eyes closed, external stimuli ignored.
2. **The Internal Flow of Archetypal Processes** — internal, conceptual.
3. **The Fire-Flow of Internal Unity** — internal, emotional.
4. **The Wave-Vibration Structure of External Forms** — external, conceptual.
5. **The Vibratory Waves of [[visions-external-unity-retinal-circus-magic-theatre]]** — external, emotional.
6. **The [[retinal-circus-and-magic-theatre]]** — term from [[henri-michaux]] (*Miserable Miracle*).
7. **The Magic Theatre** — term from [[hermann-hesse]] (*Steppenwolf*).

## Classification: Internal/External × Intellectual/Emotional

| | **Internal** (closed eyes) | **External** (open eyes) |
|---|---|---|
| **Intellectual** | Vision 2: Archetypal Processes | Vision 4: Wave-Vibration of Forms |
| **Emotional** | Vision 3: Fire-Flow of Unity | Vision 5: Vibratory Waves of Unity |

Visions 2 and 3 involve closed eyes. In Vision 2, internal imagery is primarily
conceptual (revelation to confusion); in Vision 3, primarily emotional (love
and unity to fear and isolation). Visions 4 and 5 mirror this with open eyes
and rapt attention to external stimuli.

## Connection to Tibetan Deities (Peaceful and Wrathful)

The original *[[jung-and-the-tibetan-book-of-the-dead]] the Dead* organizes the Chonyid Bardo around
fourteen visionary dramas in two groups:

- **Seven Peaceful Deities** — bisexual Father-Mother figures, each
  accompanied by consorts, attendants, and lesser deities, with
  symmetrically opposed ego traps.
- **Eight [[wrathful-deities]]** — fierce, seething figures who can be joyfully
  accepted as visionary productions or fled from in terror.

Leary's sevenfold schema parallels the mandalic arrangement of the Peaceful
Deities. The first, Bhagavan Vairochana (center of the mandala of the five
Dhyani-Buddhas), corresponds to Vision 1 (The Source). The lamas listed a
thousand images that can "boil up in the ever-changing jeweled mosaic of the
retina." Through preparatory reading, the novice learns to recognize this
kaleidoscope and, most importantly, to understand that all of it comes from
within.
