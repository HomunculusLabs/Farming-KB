# Autoflowering Breeding

Autoflowering breeding involves incorporating the automatic flowering
trait from [[cervantes-cannabis-ruderalis-autoflowering]] into drug-type cannabis varieties to
create plants that flower based on age rather than photoperiod.
Cervantes describes the origins of [[comparison-autoflowering-cannabis-vs-cannabis-photoperiod-flowering-cycle]] and the role
of C. ruderalis genetics in modern breeding programs.

## The Ruderalis Foundation

Cannabis ruderalis (= C. sativa var. spontanea) is the genetic source
of the autoflowering trait. First brought to Amsterdam from Central
Europe in the early 1980s by the Seed Bank, C. ruderalis is a short,
weedy, scrubby plant containing very little THC. However, it possesses
a unique and valuable characteristic: it begins the [[cannabis-sativa-flowering-cycle-management]]
after only a few weeks of [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]], regardless of photoperiod.

In C. ruderalis, photoperiod does not induce flowering. This automatic
trigger is genetically distinct from the photoperiod-dependent
flowering of C. sativa, C. indica, and C. afghanica, all of which
require a change in the light cycle (typically to 12 hours of darkness)
to initiate flowering.

## Ruderalis Characteristics

C. ruderalis has several distinguishing features:

- Short, weedy, scrubby growth habit, seldom exceeding two feet.
- Very low THC content -- it yields a headache rather than a high.
- Automatic flowering after a few weeks of growth.
- Originates from Central Europe, with similar plants found from
  Minnesota north through Manitoba and Saskatchewan, Canada.
- Often confused with more potent indicas due to its compact stature.

The original Seed Bank catalog from 1987 shows a C. ruderalis plant
alongside a highway in Hungary. Many breeders initially hailed this
plant as the "[[amanita-muscaria-holy-grail-theory-detailed]]" of cannabis, though pure C. ruderalis has
little value as a drug crop.

## The Breeding Challenge

The primary challenge in autoflowering breeding is combining the
automatic flowering trait of C. ruderalis with the potency, flavor,
yield, and [[cannabis-glandular-trichome-types-resin-production-clarke]] of drug-type cannabis (C. sativa, C.
indica, and C. afghanica). Early breeding efforts produced plants that
were autoflowering but had unacceptably low potency. The goal is to
introgress the autoflowering trait while preserving the desirable
characteristics of the drug parent.

This is essentially a backcrossing problem: the recurrent parent
(drug-type cannabis) contributes potency and quality, while the donor
parent (C. ruderalis) contributes the autoflowering trait. Multiple
generations of selection and backcrossing are needed to produce plants
that are both autoflowering and THC-potent.

## Early Autoflowering Varieties

Cervantes identifies 'Lowryder' as one of the few early C. ruderalis
crosses that achieved both auto-flowering capability and meaningful THC
potency. Other early ruderalis hybrids included 'Ruderalis Indica' and
'Ruderalis Skunk', which offered auto-flowering capability but varied
in potency and quality.

## Modern Autoflowering Development

Today, many breeders have incorporated the early flowering C. ruderalis
genes with other early-blooming C. sativa, C. indica, and C. afghanica
varieties. Modern autoflowering strains represent significant
improvements over early ruderalis crosses, with much higher potency,
better flavors, and improved yields. The explosive growth of seed
breeders in the Netherlands, UK, Canada, France, Switzerland, and
Spain has accelerated autoflowering variety development.

## Breeding Autoflowering Strains

The general approach to breeding autoflowering cannabis follows these
principles:

1. **Select a potent drug-type parent** with desirable
   characteristics (potency, flavor, yield, growth habit) as the
   recurrent parent.

2. **Cross with a ruderalis donor** that reliably expresses the
   autoflowering trait.

3. **Grow out F1 progeny** and select individuals that both
   autoflower and show acceptable potency.

4. **Backcross selected individuals to the drug-type parent** across
   multiple generations, selecting for both autoflowering and drug
   traits at each generation.

5. **Stabilize the resulting population** through filial inbreeding
   until the autoflowering trait and desired drug characteristics
   breed true.

## Advantages of Autoflowering Varieties

- No photoperiod manipulation required; plants flower automatically.
- Multiple harvests per season are possible outdoors.
- Compact size suits discreet growing and small spaces.
- Short life cycle from seed to harvest (often 8-10 weeks total).
- Suitable for outdoor growing at high latitudes where summers are
  short.

## Limitations

- Generally lower potency than photoperiod-dependent drug varieties,
  though this gap has narrowed with modern breeding.
- Reduced yield compared to full-season photoperiod varieties.
- Limited clone potential, as the autoflowering clock begins at
  germination and cannot be reset by taking cuttings.
- The genetic contribution of C. ruderalis may introduce unwanted
  traits that require additional generations to eliminate.
