## Permaculture Root Crops and Tubers

Root crops and tubers form an essential staple food layer in permaculture systems, providing high-calorie carbohydrate yields from relatively low-maintenance perennial and annual plants. "A Selection of Permaculture Plants" (Nugent & Boniface, 2004) covers root crops primarily within its water plants and palms sections, focusing on species suited to diverse and challenging conditions.

## Aquatic and Wetland Root Crops

The most extensively documented root crops in this source are those adapted to wetland and aquatic environments, which are particularly valuable in permaculture for their dual role in food production and water system management.

### Taro (Colocasia esculenta)
Possibly the earliest cultivated plant. Grown in paddy culture like rice or on dry land under irrigation. Both the main corm and smaller cormels are eaten. Leaves and petioles cooked like spinach (must be cooked). Requires 6-7 month frost-free period. Nutrition comparable to potato and sweet potato. Some cultivars highly [[pf-tek-ethyl-alcohol-extraction-liqueur-concentration]]. Pollen used as flour additive at up to 1:1 ratio.

### Arrowhead (Sagittaria spp)
Plants occurring at water"s edge to 0.5 m depth:

- **S. latifolia / S. fasciculata** -- Tubers called "wapatoo" by American Indians, said to be as good as potatoes. Good water fowl plant.
- **S. sagittifolia** -- Native of Europe, to 1.5 m. Walnut-sized roots are edible, harvested in autumn.
- **S. trifolia** -- Native to tropical and subtropical Asia, produces 8+ underground stems each with a corm, boiled like potatoes. High in protein (5-7%) for a root crop. Requires 6-7 month [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]].
- **P. tuberosus (Amazon yam bean)** -- Larger tubers; native to Amazon basin. Tubers to 1 m long.
- **P. ahipa (Andean yam bean)** -- Adapted to highland tropical conditions; smaller tubers.

Yam beans fix nitrogen (200+ kg/ha) while producing food, making them an excellent multi-function crop. Fast-growing, ready in 4-6 months from seed. Vines used as green manure.

### Sweet Potato (Ipomoea batatas)
While common, deserves mention for its versatility in permaculture. Vigorous ground-covering vine producing tubers in 3-5 months. Edible leaves and shoots provide continuous greens. Many cultivars suited to diverse conditions. Some varieties produce tubers in as little as 90 days. Propagated from vine cuttings (no seed needed).

### Cassava / Manihot (Manihot esculenta)
Tropical shrub producing large starchy roots. Extremely drought-tolerant once established; grows on poor soils. Roots contain cyanogenic compounds requiring proper processing (peeling, cooking, fermenting). Leaves are edible when cooked and provide good protein (up to 10% dry weight). Yields of 10-40 tons/ha possible. Multiple cultivars with varying maturity (6-24 months). Propagated from stem cuttings.

## Palms with Edible Root/Stem Starch

Several palms provide significant starch from their trunks:

- **Sugar Palm (Arenga pinnata)** -- Starch extracted from trunk yields 75 kg/tree. Sap also provides sugar and wine (36 l/tree/day). Widely cultivated in India and SE Asia.
- **Sago Palm** species -- Trunk pith processed into sago flour, a carbohydrate staple in tropical regions.

## Nut Crops as Calorie Staples

While not technically root crops, several nut-producing trees serve the same calorie-providing function in permaculture systems:

- **Chestnuts (Castanea spp)** -- Carbohydrate-rich nuts used as flour substitute
- **Acorns (Quercus spp)** -- Edible after leaching tannins; Q. lobata acorns contain 19.81% fat, 62% carbohydrate
- **Macadamia** -- 71.6% fat, extremely high energy (691 cal/100g)
- **Walnut** -- 64% fat, 14.8% protein (J. regia)

## Design Considerations

- Wetland root crops can be integrated into dam edges, [[comparison-constructed-wetlands-vs-bioswales]]
- Multiple root crop species at different water depths maximise pond edge productivity
- Rotation systems (e.g., water chestnut with rice) improve soil and yield diversity
- Root crops grown in waterlogged areas expand productive land beyond what conventional crops can use
- Perennial root crops like taro and arrowhead reduce annual soil disturbance
- Cumbungi serves triple function: food production, water filtration, and fibre source
