# Fungal Pathogens of Plants

Fungal pathogens of plants represent one of the most
economically and ecologically significant groups of fungi,
causing diseases that affect crop yields, forest health, and
[[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]], and
[[biodiversity-fungal-biodiversity-estimation-methods]] research
because understanding the diversity of pathogenic species is
fundamental to managing disease and assessing ecosystem health.

## Diversity Across Phyla

Plant pathogenic fungi are distributed across all major
fungal phyla, though they are particularly diverse within the
Ascomycota and Basidiomycota. The Taphrinales (Ascomycota)
includes approximately 100 species of dimorphic plant
pathogens that alternate between a saprobic yeast phase and
a filamentous pathogenic phase. This dimorphic life-history
trait is shared with certain basidiomycetes, such as rusts.
The Euascomycetes includes major groups of plant pathogens,
with the Pyrenomycetes clade encompassing the Diaporthales,
Hypocreales, and Xylariales, many members of which are
significant disease agents.

## Rusts and Smuts

The [[trichoderma-virens]] species, well-known
biocontrol agents of plant pathogens, are polyphagous
mycoparasites that are difficult to distinguish morphologically
from genetically distinct strains, highlighting the importance
of molecular characterization for biocontrol strain selection.
