# Rice Blast Disease and Magnaporthe Appressorium Mechanics

[[magnaporthe-grisea]], the causal agent of rice blast disease, is an ascomycete fungus that
produces one of the most extraordinary [[fungal-appressorium-and-infection-structures]] in all of biology: the
appressorium. This melanized, pressurized dome generates sufficient mechanical force to
puncture the leaves of rice plants, and can even penetrate bulletproof vest material.
Rice blast ranks among the most devastating crop diseases on Earth, threatening the food
supply of more than half the world's population.

## Rice Blast as an Agricultural Crisis

A rice field infected by Magnaporthe appears as if it has been struck by a bomb,
accounting for the common name blast. The fungus usually infects rice leaves and spreads
as an invasive mycelium, consuming plant tissues and starving the developing grain.
When the neck or panicle of the rice plant is ravaged, it becomes bleached by the sun
and withers to dry straw. Because grains normally ripen on the panicle, crop loss due
to neck blast is severe. Blast is most prevalent in areas of intensive agriculture
where soil is supplemented with nitrogen fertilizers. Crops grown by subsistence
farmers who rely upon [[fukuoka-natural-soil-fertility]] are less likely to be devastated.

## The Infection Sequence

Trouble begins when a pear-shaped conidium drifts onto the rice [[bloomfield-rust-germ-tube-tactile-sensing-leaf-surface-navigation]]. Impact
triggers the release of a sticky mucilage globule from one end of the spore that binds
the fungus to the waxy cuticle. If the leaf is wet with dew, the conidium germinates
and a germ tube travels a short distance before adhering at its tip and swelling into
the appressorium, a bulbous infection apparatus. Within a few hours the appressorium
becomes blackened with melanin, fills itself with glycerol, becomes highly pressurized,
and plunges straight through the leaf surface.

## Melanin and the Pressure Chamber

Once the appressorium adheres to the leaf by secreting an O-ring of glue, it deposits
a layer of melanin pigment against the inner surface of its cell wall. Most cell walls
are leaky structures allowing small molecules to pass freely, but a melanized wall
turns the cell into an impregnable pressure vessel. The glycerol generated inside the
cell draws water inward by osmosis, and because the melanin wall prevents glycerol
from escaping, immense hydrostatic pressure builds. Pressure inside an appressorium
can exceed 80 atmospheres, making Magnaporthe arguably the most powerful pathogen on
the planet in mechanical terms.

## Mechanical Force Compared

The pressure exerted by the Magnaporthe appressorium against the rice leaf exceeds the
pressure of a 400-pound dancer engaged in a brief pirouette, concentrated on the area
of a stiletto heel. Richard Howard of DuPont, responsible for much groundbreaking
research on appressorial function, discovered that these structures can puncture
Kevlar, the material used in bulletproof vests. This extraordinary force is generated
by a single [[gadd-fungal-cell-structure]] measuring just a few micrometers across.

## The Penetration Hypha

From the base of the pressurized appressorium, a slender penetration hypha extends
downward, pushed by the enormous [[bloomfield-turgor-pressure-and-hyphal-invasion]] into the substance of the leaf. The
penetration hypha is narrower than the appressorium itself and passes through a
narrow pore in the cell wall at the base of the dome. Once inside the leaf, the
fungus establishes itself as a feeding mycelium that spreads between plant cells,
using haustoria to extract nutrients while keeping the host cells alive.

## Disabling the Appressorium

The critical importance of melanin has been demonstrated through both chemical
inhibition and genetic mutation. If melanin formation is inhibited using a fungicide
targeting the melanin biosynthetic pathway, the appressorium never penetrates the
leaf. Similarly, transparent appressoria of albino mutants of Magnaporthe are
ineffective at plant penetration. The melanin layer is essential because it creates
the impermeable barrier needed to maintain the enormous [[fungal-fruiting-body-mechanics-osmotic-pressure]] generated
by glycerol accumulation.
