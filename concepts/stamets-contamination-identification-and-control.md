### Papulospora (Brown Plaster Mold)
- **Class**: Fungi Imperfecti, order Mycelia Sterilia
- **Common name**: Brown Plaster Mold
- **Habitat**: Overly mature compost or compost with excessive moisture; can grow on wooden trays
- **Appearance**: Dense whitish mycelium (resembling White Plaster Mold early), becoming cinnamon brown from bead-like sclerotia-like balls of dark cells
- **Spread**: Air; spent compost; untreated trays
- **Control**: Avoid over-composting; proper moisture balancing; prompt removal of old compost; steam clean trays; hygiene between crops
- **Notes**: No conidial phase known; placed in "garbage" order Mycelia Sterilia; can grow to several feet in diameter; frequently grows on wooden trays/shelves

### Sepedonium (Yellow/White Mold)
- **Class**: Fungi Imperfecti
- **Habitat**: Occasionally on agar; more common on compost; parasitic on wild mushrooms
- **Spread**: Primarily air; also spent compost

### Dactylium (Cobweb Mold)
- **Class**: Fungi Imperfecti
- **Common name**: Downy Mildew; Cobweb Mold
- **Habitat**: Casing soil and compost surfaces
- **Appearance**: Fine, grayish, cobweb-like mycelium spreading over mushroom caps and casing surface
- **Spread**: Air; insects; contaminated casing materials
- **Control**: Lowering humidity; salt applications; improving fresh air exchange; proper pasteurization of casing; hydrogen peroxide spray (3% solution diluted 1:10)
- **Notes**: Particularly damaging during fruiting; spreads rapidly in humid conditions

## General Contamination Control Strategy

### Prevention (Best Approach)
1. **Air filtration**: HEPA filters in laboratory; good ventilation in growing rooms
2. **Sterilization**: Proper autoclaving of grain and agar media
3. **Hygiene**: Clean hands, tools, surfaces; change clothes between areas
4. **Isolation**: Separate laboratory, spawn run, and fruiting areas
5. **Waste management**: Prompt removal and proper disposal of contaminated materials
6. **Casing preparation**: Proper pasteurization or sterilization of casing materials
7. **Compost preparation**: Adequate Phase I and Phase II composting eliminates most competitors

### Identification Tips
- **Green molds** (Trichoderma, Aspergillus, Penicillium): most common; differentiate by colony texture and colour
- **White molds**: Scopulariopsis (splotchy), Sepedonium (yellow tinge), Mucor (tall aerial hyphae)
- **Gray molds**: Botrytis (fluffy, rapidly spreading), Dactylium (cobweb-like, fine)
- **Black/pin molds**: Rhizopus (tall stalks, dark heads), Aspergillus niger (black)

### Response to Contamination
- Isolate contaminated cultures immediately
- Do not open contaminated containers in clean areas
- Discard heavily contaminated materials (compost or casing with green mold)
- Light contamination may be treated with salt (NaCl) on casing surface
- Identify and correct the cause (poor sterilization, air filtration failure, hygiene breach)
