# Neurotropic Fungi of the Caribbean and Central America

The Caribbean and Central America form a critical biogeographic bridge between North and South America, and this region hosts a notable diversity of neurotropic fungi. The tropical climate, combined with the introduction of cattle during the colonial period, created ideal conditions for coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) psilocybian species. The region's [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungal-diversity-patterns-distribution]] reflects both native Neotropical elements and species that may have been introduced through historical trade and livestock movements.

## Central America

Central America, stretching from Guatemala to Panama, contains several important [[mexico-center-neurotropic-fungal-biodiversity]] species, though the region has been less intensively studied than Mexico or South America.

### Guatemala

Guatemala has documented reports of several important neurotropic species:

- **[[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]]** — reported by Lowy (1972, 1974, 1977), Torres (1984), and Wasson et al. (1986); grows in mycorrhizal association with Pinus in highland areas
- **[[copelandia-cyanescens]]** — reported by Sáenz et al. (1983)
- **[[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]** — documented by Sáenz et al. (1983) and Guzmán (1995)
- **Psilocybe cf. aztecorum** — an unconfirmed record from Sáenz et al. (1983), suggesting possible highland species yet to be formally documented
- **Psilocybe cf. mexicana** — another unconfirmed record, hinting at species shared with Mexico

The paucity of records from Costa Rica is striking given the country's renowned biodiversity and extensive cloud forest systems, suggesting significant undercollection.

### El Salvador and Honduras

- **Psilocybe subcubensis** — reported from both El Salvador and Honduras by Guzmán (1983); this species is closely related to P. cubensis but is smaller and less commonly collected

### Panama

- **Psilocybe caerulescens** var. caerulescens — reported by Guzmán (1983)
- **Psilocybe dumontii** — a species described by Singer, known from Panama; unusual in its habitat preferences compared to other tropical Psilocybe species

### Belize

- **Copelandia cyanescens** — documented by Gerhardt (1996)
- **Psilocybe cordispora** — reported by Reid (1970)
- **Psilocybe cubensis** — documented by Heim (1956b) and Singer and Smith (1958)

## Caribbean Islands

The Caribbean archipelago presents a fascinating study in island biogeography for neurotropic fungi. The scattered island chains, combined with their tropical climate and livestock economies, support populations of several widespread coprophilous species.

### Cuba

Cuba is notable as the type locality for Psilocybe cubensis, which was originally described by Earle (1906) from Cuban material. The species epithet "cubensis" directly references its Cuban origin:

- **Psilocybe cubensis** — the type species was described from Cuba; additional reports by Heim (1956b, 1978) and Singer and Smith (1958)
- **Psilocybe plutonia** — a distinctive species known from Cuba, described by Berkeley and Curtis, growing on woody substrates rather than dung

### Puerto Rico

Puerto Rico is one of the better-studied Caribbean islands for neurotropic fungi, with several endemic and widespread species:

- **Psilocybe cubensis** — common and widespread (Heim, 1956b; Singer and Smith, 1958)
- **Psilocybe portoricensis** — an endemic species described by Guzmán, Tapia, and Nieves-Rivera (1997b), found only in Puerto Rico
- **[[archaeology-of-psychedelic-mushroom-use]] (Gerhardt, 1996)
- **Bermuda** — Claviceps paspali and Copelandia cyanescens
- **Guadalupe** — Psilocybe cubensis, P. plutonia, [[amanita-muscaria]]
- [[copelandia-cyanescens]]
