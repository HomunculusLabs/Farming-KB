## Mushroom Growing Substrates

A substrate is the material on which [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]]. Selecting and preparing the right
substrate is one of the most important factors in successful mushroom
cultivation. Different mushroom species have evolved to decompose different
materials, so substrate composition must match the target species.

## Grain Spawn Substrates

Grains serve as the initial nutritious medium for expanding mycelium from a
spore or culture sample. Grains are not typically fruited directly; instead,
colonized grain is used to inoculate bulk substrates. Key grain options include:

- **Rye berries**: Considered the gold standard. Excellent moisture retention,
  good nutritional profile, and individual grains separate easily for even
  distribution into bulk substrates.
- **Whole oats**: Inexpensive and readily available. Slightly more resistant to
  bacterial contamination than rye due to their hull.
- **Millet**: Tiny grains provide enormous surface area for mycelium, speeding
  colonization. Often mixed with larger grains.
- **Wild bird seed**: A cheap mix of millet, milo, and sunflower seeds.
  Effective but requires careful preparation to remove floating debris.
- **Brown rice**: Standard for PF Tek. Not ideal for grain spawn due to
  tendency to clump.

  chopped, hydrated, and pasteurized (not sterilized).
- **Hardwood sawdust**: The standard for wood-loving species like shiitake,
  lion's mane, and reishi. Oak, beech, and maple are preferred.

## Supplementation

Adding nitrogen-rich supplements increases yields but also raises contamination
risk. Supplements must be used after full colonization (delayed supplementation)
or mixed in small amounts before pasteurization.

- **Gypsum** (calcium sulfate): Added at 5-10% by volume. Improves substrate
  structure, provides calcium and sulfur, and buffers pH.
- **Bran** (wheat or rice): High in nitrogen. Dramatically increases yields.
  Typically added at 5-10%.
- **Lime** (calcium carbonate): Raises pH for species preferring alkaline
  conditions. Used in straw substrates for oyster mushrooms.

## Substrate Preparation Methods

### Sterilization

Full sterilization (15 psi, 90-120 minutes) eliminates all [[mushroom-substrate-sterilization-and-pasteurization]].

### Pasteurization

Pasteurization (140-170 degrees F for 1-2 hours) kills most contaminants while
preserving beneficial thermophilic bacteria. Used for straw, manure, and
coir-based bulk substrates. Methods include hot water baths, steam pasteurizers,
