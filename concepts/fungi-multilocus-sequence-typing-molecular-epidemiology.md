# Multilocus Sequence Typing and Molecular Epidemiology of Fungi

## Overview

Multilocus sequence typing (MLST) is a molecular technique for
characterizing genetic variation in fungal populations. Originally
developed for bacterial epidemiology (Maiden et al., 1998), MLST was
adapted in parallel by mycologists as a portable, reproducible approach
to discriminating isolates of pathogenic fungi. The technique indexes
nucleotide variation at multiple housekeeping gene loci, enabling
researchers to trace disease outbreaks, map global biogeography of
fungal species, and investigate speciation processes.

## Principles of MLST

MLST works by amplifying a 450-500 nucleotide fragment from each of
7-10 housekeeping genes using PCR for every isolate in a study cohort.
This fragment length is chosen so it can be sequenced in its entirety
for both forward and reverse strands with a single primer pair, ensuring
that all polymorphisms are scored at least twice to minimize sequencing
errors. After sequencing all loci for every isolate, each unique allele
at a particular locus receives a unique integer code. The concatenated
string of all integers -- called the sequence type (ST) -- represents
the allelic profile of an isolate. No weighting schemes are used, so a
single point mutation carries as much weight as a recombinational
exchange translocating many polymorphisms.

ST data are uploaded to online databases (http://www.mlst.org/) stored
in SQL server relational databases accessible via web interfaces. This
allows laboratories worldwide to upload and compare their isolate STs
directly, making data permanently accessible for re-analysis.

## Key Design Criteria

MLST was designed to satisfy four criteria: (i) 100% reproducibility
between laboratories; (ii) sufficient discriminatory power while
remaining stable over time; (iii) accurate portrayal of evolutionary
relationships at multiple scales; and (iv) portability and accessibility
to the wider research community. Previous molecular typing methods --
including VNTRs, MLEE, RFLPs, RAPDs, and PFGE -- were often
laboratory-specific, poorly reproducible, or lacked standardized
databases for data sharing.

## MLST Applications in Fungi

The first [[gadd-fungal-mlst-population-genetics]] scheme was developed for Coccidioides immitis by
sequencing five genes (CHS1, pyrG, tcrP, CTS2, and a serine
proteinase), revealing 1.4% average nucleotide diversity between
isolates and two strongly supported clades separated by 11-12.8 million
years of evolution. This led to naming a new sister species,
C. posadasii. The scheme further showed that North and Central American
populations were geographically separated, genetically recombining
groups where geography covaried linearly with distance, indicating low
long-distance [[aquatic-fungi-spore-dispersal-ingold]].
