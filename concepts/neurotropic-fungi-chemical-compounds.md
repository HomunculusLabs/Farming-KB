## Primary Active Compounds

### Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT)

Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the phosphate ester
of psilocin and the primary psychoactive compound found in fresh
*Psilocybe* mushrooms. It is more stable in air and water-soluble, making it
the dominant form in living fungal tissue. Upon ingestion, psilocybin is
rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes in the body to
produce psilocin, the pharmacologically active metabolite.

### Psilocin (4-HO-DMT)

Psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the pharmacologically active
compound responsible for the hallucinogenic effects. It acts on the serotonin
system, primarily as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist. Psilocin is less stable than
psilocybin and is more prone to oxidation, which is why dried mushrooms
gradually lose potency. The blue bruising reaction observed in psilocybin-

### Baeocystin and Norbaeocystin

Baeocystin (4-phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine) and norbaeocystin
(4-phosphoryloxytryptamine) are N-demethylated analogs of psilocybin found
in lower concentrations in many psilocybin-containing mushrooms. They are

## Variability in Chemical Content

The concentration of psychoactive compounds in neurotropic fungi varies
considerably depending on several factors:

- **Species**: Different species contain markedly different levels of
  psilocybin and related compounds. *P. azurescens* and *P. bohemica*
  are among the most potent, while some species contain only trace amounts.

- **Age of specimen**: Potency decreases significantly with time after
  collection. Ohenoja et al. (1987) found that [[herbarium-specimens-fungal-collections]] of
