# Neurotropic Fungi Chemical Compounds

## Overview

Neurotropic (hallucinogenic) fungi produce a range of psychoactive compounds,
the most significant of which are the indole alkaloids [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]].
These compounds are structurally related to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine,
5-HT) and act primarily as agonists or partial agonists at the 5-HT2A
postsynaptic receptor. The chemistry of these fungi is complex, with
additional compounds including baeocystin, norbaeocystin, and various
tryptamine derivatives that may contribute to the overall psychoactive profile.

## Classification of Neurotropic Fungi by Chemistry

Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz (2000) divided neurotropic fungi into four groups:

1. **Psilocybin and related indoles**: Species containing psilocybin,
   psilocin, baeocystin, norbaeocystin, or likely to contain these
   substances. This is by far the largest group, encompassing 13 genera
   and over 180 species, with *Psilocybe* (116 species) being dominant.

2. **[[amanita-muscaria-and-ibotenic-acid-containing-fungi]] group**: Three *Amanita* species — *A. muscaria*, *A.
   pantherina*, and *A. regalis* — which contain ibotenic acid and its
   decarboxylation product muscimol. These compounds act on GABA receptors
   rather than serotonin and produce markedly different effects from the
   psilocybin-containing fungi.

3. **Ergot fungi**: Five species of *Claviceps* and two of *Cordyceps*
   that produce [[claviceps-purpurea-chemistry-and-water-soluble-ergot-alkaloids]]. *[[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]]* is the classic ergot
   fungus responsible for ergotism (St. Anthony's Fire) and is the natural
   source of lysergic acid derivatives including ergotamine and, indirectly,
   LSD.

4. **Sacred fungi without confirmed chemistry**: Various boletes (two genera,
   8 species), *Russula* (6 species), and gasteromycetes (6 species in 3
   genera) used ceremonially by indigenous peoples but for which no reliable
   chemical studies confirming psychoactive constituents have been published.

## Primary Active Compounds

### Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT)

Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the phosphate ester
of psilocin and the primary psychoactive compound found in fresh
*Psilocybe* mushrooms. It is more stable in air and water-soluble, making it
the dominant form in living fungal tissue. Upon ingestion, psilocybin is
rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes in the body to
produce psilocin, the pharmacologically active metabolite.

### Psilocin (4-HO-DMT)

Psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the pharmacologically active
compound responsible for the hallucinogenic effects. It acts on the serotonin
system, primarily as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist. Psilocin is less stable than
psilocybin and is more prone to oxidation, which is why dried mushrooms
gradually lose potency. The blue bruising reaction observed in psilocybin-
[[psilocybin-containing-mushrooms-in-us]] is caused by the oxidation of psilocin and related
[[ingham-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds]].

### Baeocystin and Norbaeocystin

Baeocystin (4-phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine) and norbaeocystin
(4-phosphoryloxytryptamine) are N-demethylated analogs of psilocybin found
in lower concentrations in many psilocybin-containing mushrooms. They are
considered less potent than [[psilocybin-and-psilocin-chemical-synthesis]], though their
contribution to the overall psychoactive experience is not fully understood.
Repke et al. (1977) demonstrated that baeocystin content varies with
[[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybin-volatility-specimen-age-degradation]].

## Variability in Chemical Content

The concentration of psychoactive compounds in neurotropic fungi varies
considerably depending on several factors:

- **Species**: Different species contain markedly different levels of
  psilocybin and related compounds. *P. azurescens* and *P. bohemica*
  are among the most potent, while some species contain only trace amounts.

- **Age of specimen**: Potency decreases significantly with time after
  collection. Ohenoja et al. (1987) found that [[herbarium-specimens-fungal-collections]] of
  *P. semilanceata* showed decreasing psilocybin content: 0.014% dry
