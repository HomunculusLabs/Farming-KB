# Food Drying and Dehydration Methods

Sun drying is a no-cost, low-energy dehydration method that uses the power
of the sun. This method works well in a hot, dry climate. In temperate
regions a large reflector can be made to help focus the sun's rays, like a
big solar cooker, but it will still take a few days if successful at all.
The alternative is an electric dehydrator. There are many on the market that
use very little energy to power a heating element and fan, and there are
some that work effectively with a fan alone. Food drying is an essential
skill within systems.

## Preparation

Use only ripe fruits and vegetables. Wash everything thoroughly, peel and
slice them very thin unless you are drying peas or corn, which can just be
removed from pods and cobs. For seed pods, harvest them before they burst
and use them whole. This preparation step is the most time-consuming. Simple
devices such as apple corers and peelers can help speed the process.

Over-ripe or even fermenting fruit can still make good fruit leather. Wash,
peel, remove seeds and pits, then grind them up by mashing or blending.
The puree must be thin enough to pour but not too thin to be watery. If it
is too thick, add [[cho-fermented-fruit-juice-preparation]] or water; if too thin, add another fruit
puree.

For meat (jerky), choose lean cuts of beef, buffalo, goat, or deer. Do not
use pork, which is too fatty. Cut into trimmed strips around an inch wide
and half an inch tall, and as long as you want; cut along the grain.
Sprinkle with ground pepper and salt.

## Anti-Oxidation Treatment

Normally when dehydrating foods, soak fruits and vegetables in a solution of
vitamin C or sugar for five minutes to prevent oxidation and discoloration.
However, this extra soaking will make it difficult to dry foods in the sun.
If using an electric dehydrator, get a big bowl of ice water with sugar or
citric acid (vitamin C) and put the finished slices into the bowl until
ready to lay them on the drying trays.

## Drying Process

### Step 1: Arrange on Trays
Spread one layer on a drying tray. When making fruit leather, line the tray
with plastic wrap or parchment paper. Each type of food dries at a different
rate, so keep them all separate. Label everything so you know what it is.

### Step 2: Position for Drying
Put the trays in the hot sun or in the electric dryer. If drying meat in
the sun, the tray should be 4 feet above a slow fire. The firewood should
be non-resinous hardwood with very low flames to keep away birds and flies.
Green wood that makes smoke works well for this purpose.

### Step 3: Turn Regularly
Turn big chunks of food three times a day and small foods once or twice a
day. In a dehydrator, move the almost-dry trays to the top and the moist
food to the bottom. It should not take more than two days. Protect
everything from dew and bugs. Bring food inside before dusk and back out
in the morning.

## Testing for Doneness

Vegetables are dry when they are brittle and break when bent. Fruits are
leathery or brittle and should produce no moisture when squeezed. Fruit
leathers will be a little sticky but easily peeled from the paper or
plastic lining. Pods become dry and brittle. Meat should be hard and solid
with a uniform dark red color throughout, without excessively large wrinkles
in one place. Break a piece to make sure it is dried in the center.

## Conditioning and Storage

Put dried fruits and vegetables into a wide-mouthed bowl for a week,
stirring two to three times a day. Keep it covered with a screen or porous
cloth. This conditions the food to resist mold. Then repack tightly in an
airtight container or freezer bag and store in a dark, dry place.

To pasteurize, put the food in the oven for 30 minutes at 175 degrees F
(79 degrees C). Label with type and date, and check in the first two weeks
for moisture. If you find some, dry them more. Properly dried food stays
good for at least six months or more. If you find bugs, remove them and
roast the food at 300 degrees F (149 degrees C) for 30 minutes.

## Egg Drying

Drying is a somewhat more time-consuming method that extends the life of
eggs. Crack very fresh eggs and beat them well. Pour onto a drying surface
lined with plastic or foil, no more than 1/8 inch thick. Dry at 120
degrees F (49 degrees C) for 24 to 36 hours, then turn the eggs over,
remove the plastic or foil, break them up and dry for 12 to 24 more hours.
In the sun, it takes about five days until dry enough to break easily.
Grind the eggs into powder and use in baking, or reconstitute by adding an
equal amount of water.

## Solar vs. Electric Dehydration

Sun drying costs nothing but depends on weather and climate. Electric
dehydrators provide consistent results regardless of weather and are faster.
A fan-only dehydrator uses minimal electricity. For a self-sufficient
homestead, having both options provides flexibility. Build a simple solar
dehydrator from a wooden box with a glass lid and screened vents for air
circulation. This captures solar heat while allowing moisture to escape.
