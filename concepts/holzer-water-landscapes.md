### Site Assessment
- Survey soil conditions, topography, and existing water
- Natural wet areas or previous landslide sites are advantageous
- The most reliable model is nature itself -- study natural bodies of water

### Construction
1. Rough shape dug out with appropriate machinery
2. Coarse and fine material separated by heaping into a tall pile
3. Pond walls built from 30-50cm thick layers of fine material, tamped down
4. Coarse material used later for stabilising walls and banks
5. Islands and small biotopes created within the pond
6. **Sealing**: Water filled to 30-40cm depth; a small excavator bucket vibrates the subsoil 0.5-1m deep (similar to vibrating concrete)
7. A standpipe at the deepest point regulates water level
8. An emergency overflow pipe handles heavy rainfall

### No Pond Liners
Holzer is convinced that wildlife ponds should never use liners. Liners prevent natural pond life from developing, are easily damaged, and make future working impossible. The vibration method works on almost every subsoil type and is usually cheaper than liners. Liners also prevent the pond from interacting with the surrounding soil ecology, cutting it off from the very systems that would sustain it naturally. A properly sealed pond with the vibration method becomes part of the landscape rather than an isolated container sitting on top of it.

### Design Elements
- Stones rising from the water warm quickly in the sun, raising water temperature and reducing ice formation
- Gnarled tree trunks, stone steps, and crossings add aesthetic and habitat value
- Both deep and shallow areas provide different habitats for plants and animals
- A sharp drop from 2m to 3m depth prevents aquatic plants from overgrowing

## Stream Management

Holzer is strongly opposed to straightening streams. Natural streams meander, creating diverse habitats and slowing water flow. Flooding at headwaters prevents worse flooding downstream -- channelling water faster only moves the problem downstream. Water should be dispersed across the land rather than concentrated into fast-flowing channels.

On the Krameterhof, natural streams are left to find their own course. Where streams feed into the pond system, they bring fresh water, oxygen, and nutrients. Where they exit, they carry cooler, cleaner water to downstream ponds and eventually to lower elevations. This natural water flow is the circulatory system of the entire farm.

## Water Management Philosophy

- Never drain wetlands -- cultivate plants suited to damp conditions instead
- Keep water on the land as long as possible
- Disperse accumulated water rather than channelling it
- Every water body should serve multiple functions simultaneously
- Study natural water systems before building -- they have perfected the design over millennia
