## The Black Wattle as Pioneer Species

The seeds that fall to the ground below a single old black wattle tree
number in the hundreds of thousands, and even millions. But very often no
more than one or two saplings will grow from these. Only very few of those
millions of seeds survive — perhaps one in ten thousand, or one in a hundred
thousand. The rest simply perish: eaten by birds, stashed away by mice, or
carried off by ants. Yet what appears to us as waste is not waste in the
eyes of nature. The survival of just a few seedlings out of all those seeds
is simply part of the natural ecological process.

Acacias and silk trees grow rapidly, so their roots quickly reach ground
water. Banana and papaya saplings planted below these also grow very well.
Once kudzu vines begin climbing over shrubs and trees, the soil becomes
enriched and the transition to green land is rapid.
