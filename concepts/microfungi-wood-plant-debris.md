# Microfungi on Wood and Plant Debris

## Overview

Microfungi on wood and plant debris represent one of the most species-rich and ecologically important groups of fungi. They are responsible for the [[decomposition]] of lignocellulose -- the most abundant organic polymer on Earth -- and play critical roles in [[nutrient-cycling-in-soil]], carbon flow, and ecosystem functioning. Their diversity is enormous but poorly documented, with most studies limited to temperate regions.

## Species Diversity at Large Scales

### Known Diversity from English Sites

The most complete information on fungal species numbers for specific areas comes from three sites in southern England (Table 11.3 from source):

| Site | Area | Total Species |
|------|------|--------------|
| Mickleham Down | 4 ha | 1,380 |
| Slapton Ley NNR | 211 ha | 2,500 |
| Esher Common | 400 ha | 2,700 |
| Warwickshire County | 250,000 ha | 2,700 |

### Taxonomic Composition

Proportions of major taxa are remarkably consistent across sites:

- **Basidiomycota**: 32-46% of species
- **Ascomycota**: 28-43% of species
- **Mitosporic fungi**: 8-24% of species
- **Mycetozoa**: 3.5-6% of species
- **Oomycota + Zygomycota**: 1-6% of species

### Leaf Litter Studies

Microfungal diversity on leaf litter varies by habitat and method:

| Locality | Habitat | No. Species | Method |
|----------|---------|-------------|--------|
| Great Britain | Salt marsh | 141 | Unspecified (Ascomycota only) |
| Austria | Alpine sedge | 128 | Direct observation |
| Ivory Coast | Forest | 129-165 | Moist chamber |
| Costa Rica | Wet forest | 78-134/sample | Particle washing |
| Puerto Rico | Wet forest | 338 total | Particle washing |
| Panama | 6 tree species | 500 | Dilution plating |

The enormous difference between direct observation (24 species) and particle washing (338 species) at the Puerto Rico wet forest site illustrates the critical importance of methodology on apparent diversity.

## Factors Affecting Diversity

### Substratum Quality

- Leaf litter quality varies enormously among plant species
- Lignin content, nitrogen content, and secondary chemistry affect fungal colonization
- Mixed litter generally supports higher diversity than single-species litter
- Decomposition stage affects which fungal species are present

### Environmental Conditions

- Temperature and moisture regimes
- Altitudinal gradients
- Soil type effects appear minimal for microfungal litter diversity
- Seasonal patterns in sporulation and community composition

### Human Disturbance

- Cultivation can reduce microfungal diversity
- In some cases, abandoned cultivated plots show gradual return to natural mycobiota
- However, continuing cultivation has potential to permanently reduce diversity through climatic changes induced by cultivation

### Succession

Fungal communities on decomposing plant material change over time:

- Early colonizers: Rapidly growing species on fresh litter
- Mid-successional: More specialized decomposers
- Late successional: Slow-growing specialists on recalcitrant material
- The sequence depends on substratum chemistry and environmental conditions

## Sampling Methods

### Direct Observation

- Examine substrata directly under microscope
- Best for fungi with distinctive sporulating structures
- Significantly underestimates total diversity compared to cultural methods
- Useful for recording fungi that do not grow in culture

### Moist Chamber Incubation

- Place plant material in moist chambers (usually Petri dishes with moist filter paper)
- Incubate at ambient temperature or controlled conditions
- Allows fungi present as spores or mycelium to develop fruiting structures
- Particularly effective for detecting fungi on tropical leaf litter
- Time-course observations reveal sequential sporulation

### Particle Washing

- Wash litter particles to remove adhering microorganisms
- Plate washings on selective or general media
- More effective than direct observation for total species counts
- Bills and Polishook (1994): detected 78-134 species per sample in Costa Rica
- Rarefaction curves often show little sign of leveling off despite large culture numbers

### Dilution Plating

- Serial dilution of substratum suspensions
- Plate on agar media
- Quantitative -- allows estimation of population sizes
- Can detect very high diversity (500 species from 6 tree species in Panama)
- May favor fast-growing species over slow-growing specialists

### Baiting

- Place substratum baits in natural settings to attract specific fungal groups
- Useful for aquatic fungi, [[coprophilous-fungi]], and [[nematophagous-fungi]]
- Selective baits attract specific ecological groups

## Challenges in Diversity Assessment

### Method-Dependent Results

- Isolated species depend entirely on methods used
- Many fungi do not grow readily in culture
- Some species present only as resting spores, not active mycelium
- Cultural methods may favor rapidly growing species
- Direct observation misses non-sporulating fungi

### Species Accumulation

- Rarefaction curves from intensive studies often do not plateau
- Bills and Polishook detected average of 424 isolates per sample with no leveling
- Complete sampling of microfungal diversity may be practically impossible
- True diversity may be many times higher than observed diversity

### Taxonomic Expertise

- Many microfungi require specialist taxonomic knowledge
- Pleomorphy (multiple sporing stages with different names) complicates identification
- Molecular methods are revealing previously undetectable cryptic species
- Lack of trained taxonomists limits inventory efforts
