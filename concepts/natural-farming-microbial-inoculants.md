---# Natural Farming Microbial Inoculants

Microbial inoculants are living microorganism preparations applied to
soil or plants to enhance nutrient cycling, [[cho-han-kyu]], uses
a multi-stage IMO preparation process:

### Stage 1: Collection
IMO are collected using steamed rice placed in a covered container in
a location with characteristics most similar to the target field. The
rice attracts diverse microorganisms from the surrounding environment.
Collection can occur at any time and from multiple sites with different
orientations.

### Stage 2: Cultivation
The collected microorganisms are mixed with brown sugar in a 1:1 ratio
by weight and placed in a jar. This creates optimal fermentation
conditions. The mixture is maintained at 23 to 25 degrees Celsius. As
fermentation progresses, the temperature rises above 40 degrees and
the consistency becomes that of thick paste. This produces the
foundation stock, or IMO #2.

### Stage 3: Expansion
IMO #2 is diluted 500 to 1,000 times with water and mixed with rice
bran at 65 to 70 percent moisture content. Nutrient liquids including
OHN, FPJ, and brown rice vinegar are added to activate the
microorganisms. The mixture is stacked 35 to 40 centimeters high and
covered for further multiplication.

### Stage 4: Final Preparation
The expanded IMO is combined with field soil to create the final soil
treatment preparation. This is applied to fields as a soil drench or
incorporated into compost preparations.
