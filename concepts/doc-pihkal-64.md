# DOC

## Overview

DOC (2,5-dimethoxy-4-chloroamphetamine) is a potent psychedelic amphetamine belonging to the DOx family of 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted amphetamines. Along with [[dob-pihkal-62]] and [[doi-pihkal-67]], DOC completes the trio of halo-amphetamine derivatives that Shulgin identified as being of approximately equal potency and all very long-lasting. DOC is a [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] derivative described by Shulgin as "an archetypical psychedelic" with "everything there in spades."

DOC's effects are characterized by intense visuals, interpretive cognitive challenges, physical comfort, and a duration of 12-24 hours. Unlike the 2-carbon phenethylamines which tend toward "gentle images and gentle fantasies," DOC provides what Shulgin described as the full psychedelic experience with few subtle graces.

## Synthesis (Summary)

DOC is synthesized by direct chlorination of 2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine (2,5-DMA). The 2,5-DMA hydrochloride is basified with aqueous NaOH and extracted into dichloromethane. After solvent removal under vacuum, the free base is dissolved in glacial acetic acid and cooled to 0 degrees C with an external ice bath. Liquid chlorine (3 mL) is added via Pasteur pipette with good stirring, generating visible HCl gas. The reaction proceeds for 3 hours at 0 degrees C.

Workup involves pouring the reaction mixture into water, washing with ether, basifying with NaOH, and extracting with dichloromethane. The hydrochloride salt is precipitated from ether, yielding off-white crystalline material. Recrystallization from methanol/ether gives lustrous white crystals. An alternative synthesis route proceeds through diazotization of the amino analogue (DON, 2,5-dimethoxy-4-nitroamphetamine) with eventual hydrolysis of an acetylated precursor, producing material with a slightly higher melting point (193-194.5 degrees C from EtOH/ether vs. 187-188 degrees C from acetone/EtOH).

The Canadian synthesis procedures also identify two unexplored intermediates: reduction of DON with Pd/charcoal and hydrogen yields DOA (2,5-dimethoxy-4-aminoamphetamine dihydrochloride, mp 248-250 degrees C), and reduction of an oxime intermediate gives DOAA (2,5-dimethoxy-4-acetamidoamphetamine hydrochloride, mp 249-250 degrees C). Neither has been tested in humans.

## Qualitative Commentary (from PIHKAL)

**1.6 mg:** Slightly light head with quite real effects. Disconnected and spacey in a favorable, fun way. Beginning to drop off at 6 hours, but still significant memory of effects at 12 hours. "This is a long thing."

**2.4 mg (first report):** Described as "an archetypical psychedelic. Everything is there in spades, with few if any of the subtle graces, the gentle images and gentle fantasies of the 2-carbon phenethylamines. This is the works." Strong visuals with interpretive problems regarding spatial awareness -- "the place where nothing makes sense, and yet everything makes sense." Still significantly altered at 18 hours with a relaxed, good feeling. "Anyone who uses this had better have 24 hours at their disposal."

**2.4 mg (second report):** Full plus-three at the sixth hour. Neither anti-erotic nor anorexic. Body and mind both very comfortable. Eyes-closed imagery notable for a completely dark screen -- "lovely and seductive, subtle, and yet light must be deliberately brought in." At 24 hours, sleep was poor with tight dreams and defensive posturing ("the nervous system was too alert"), but good humor persisted. "This is excellent stuff, but start early in the day."

## Effects Summary

### Visual and Perceptual
- Intense, full-spectrum visuals -- "everything is there in spades"
- Interpretive challenges with spatial awareness
- Eyes-closed imagery described as dark, lovely, and seductive -- requiring deliberate invocation of light
- Marked contrast with the gentler visual quality of 2-carbon phenethylamines

### Cognitive and Emotional
- Classic psychedelic cognitive effects with both confusion and clarity coexisting
- "The place where nothing makes sense, and yet everything makes sense"
- Generally positive mood throughout, including residual period
- No anorexic or anti-erotic effects

### Physical
- Described as very comfortable in the body
- Extended stimulation preventing deep sleep even at 24 hours
- Nervous system remains alert during sleep, producing tight, defensive dreams
- No noted body load or physical discomfort

## Comparisons

- vs. [[dob-pihkal-62]]: Approximately equal potency. Both are long-lasting DOx psychedelics, though DOB has a slightly longer duration (18-30h vs. 12-24h for DOC). DOB has been more frequently associated with overdose risk.
- vs. [[doi-pihkal-67]]: Similar potency and duration. DOI may have slightly more variable emotional character, with some subjects reporting depression at lower doses. DOC is described as more consistently positive in mood.
- vs. 2-carbon phenethylamines: DOC provides a "full works" psychedelic experience compared to the gentler, more subtle quality of shorter-chain phenethylamines. The amphetamine backbone contributes both to intensity and duration.
- vs. [[dom-pihkal-68]]: DOC is somewhat more potent than DOM and represents the halogenated end of the DOx spectrum. DOM's methyl group produces a somewhat different character than the chloro substitution.

## Harm Reduction

- DOC shares the DOx family risks: long duration (12-24 hours) and slow onset that may tempt redosing
- Active dose is in the low milligram range -- confusion with other substances requiring much higher doses can be dangerous
- Shulgin explicitly warns: "Anyone who uses this had better have 24 hours at their disposal"
- Sleep quality is poor even 24 hours post-dose -- plan rest time accordingly
- The chlorinated variant is less explored than DOB or DOI in the scientific literature
- Consider [[psychedelic-set-and-setting]] carefully; the intensity of the experience benefits from a safe, familiar environment
- See [[psychedelic-harm-reduction]] and [[harm-reduction-guide]] for general principles

## The DOx Halogen Series

Shulgin explicitly noted that DOI, DOB, and DOC are "all pretty much of the same potency. And all of them very long lived." The halogen atom at the 4-position (iodine, bromine, or chlorine) is the primary variable among these three compounds. In the 2-carbon phenethylamine series (2C-I, 2C-B, 2C-C), Shulgin observed that potency follows the halogen series with iodine being the most potent, though the differences among the three are modest. The amphetamine (3-carbon) versions share this general pattern but are all significantly more potent and longer-lasting than their 2-carbon counterparts.

The DOx family represents one of the most pharmacologically significant groups within the substituted amphetamines. The presence of a heavy halogen atom not only increases potency but dramatically extends duration, likely through effects on metabolic stability and distribution patterns. Both DOB and DOI have been shown to accumulate first in the lungs before reaching the brain, suggesting a metabolic conversion step that contributes to the slow onset characteristic of the entire family.

## Extensions and Commentary

Shulgin noted with characteristic humor that DOC's code name "should certainly appeal to some physicians," and reflected on the unsettling implications of the unexplored intermediate DOA, which in coroner's terminology means "Dead-On-Arrival." The amyl homologue at the 4-position follows the DOM convention and carries the code name DOAM.
