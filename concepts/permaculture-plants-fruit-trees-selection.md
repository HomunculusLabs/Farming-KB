# Permaculture Fruit Trees Selection

A curated selection of fruit-producing trees and shrubs suited to [[permaculture-design]] systems. These species are chosen for their multiple functions beyond fruit production, including shade, shelter, fodder, timber, and ecosystem support.

## Selection Principles

The book "A Selection of Permaculture Plants" (Nugent & Boniface, 2004) emphasises that common fruits such as apples, apricots, and plums are deliberately avoided since readily available information exists on these species. The focus is on less common but highly productive permaculture fruits suited to diverse climates.

Key criteria for selection in permaculture design:

- Multiple yields (fruit plus timber, fodder, medicine, or bee forage)
- Adaptability to a range of soil and climate conditions
- Compatibility with companion plants and nitrogen-fixing nurse species
- Long-term productivity and low maintenance requirements
- Resistance to pests and diseases

## Major Fruit Tree Categories

### Temperate and Subtropical Fruits

- **Figs (Ficus spp)** -- Deciduous trees to 5-10 m, frost tolerant to -10 C, suited to warm temperate and subtropical regions. Over 700 varieties exist. Fruit can be eaten fresh, frozen, or dried (sugar rises from 12% to 50% on drying). Long-lived (100+ years), bearing at 2-3 years. Good poultry feed. Trees are generally fire retardant.

- **Mulberries (Morus spp)** -- Long-lived deciduous trees (300 years), heavy regular bearers (19-24 t/ha). Hardy, drought resistant, frost tolerant, shade tolerant. Fruit eaten raw, dried into flour, or used as self-harvest pig food. Good companion plant for grapes. Timber useful for tool handles and fence posts. Leaves fed to livestock including silkworm.

- **Persimmons (Diospyros kaki)** -- Deciduous trees bearing at 3 years (grafted). Fruit eaten fresh or dried; astringent and non-astringent cultivars available. Rich in vitamin A (2710 IU/100g). Dioecious, requiring male and female plants.

- **Olives (Olea europaea)** -- Hardy evergreen trees to 25-30 m, bearing after 5 years for 2000+ years. Frost hardy to -9.5 C. Drought tolerant, wind resistant, some salt resistance. Oil content 18-25% of fruit. Average yield 4.8 t/ha.

- **Loquats (Eriobotrya japonica)** -- Small evergreen trees 6-9 m, frost hardy to -10 C. Fruit matures in spring/early summer. Good shade tree, hedge, or windbreak. Salt spray tolerant.

- **Jujubes (Ziziphus jujuba)** -- Deciduous trees to 9 m, suited to alkaline and saline soils, drought conditions. Fruit rich in vitamin C (69 mg/100g fresh), crisp texture like apple-date cross. Dries well. Used in puddings, cakes, soups, and fermented pastes.

### Tropical Fruits

- **Passionfruit (Passiflora spp)** -- Over 40 edible species of woody, fruiting vines. Best known is P. edulis (purple passionfruit) native to Brazil. Most cold-tolerant is P. incarnata (maypop). Vines climb by tendrils onto trellis or trees. Fruit eaten raw, juiced, or made into sauces, wines, and jellies.

- **Cherimoyas (Annona spp)** -- Semi-evergreen Andean native, growing to 9 m. Fruit flesh described as cross between pineapple and strawberry. Best in warm areas outside the tropics. Requires hand pollination. Frost tolerant to -3 C when established.

- **Papaya (Carica papaya)** -- Fast-growing tropical, herbaceous-looking tree. Fruit rich in vitamins A and C.

- **[[permaculture-feijoa]] (Acca sellowiana, syn. Feijoa sellowiana)** -- Evergreen shrub or small tree 3-6 m, frost hardy to -12 C. Native to S. Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay, N. Argentina. Fruit eaten fresh, cooked into jams and jellies, or fermented into wine. High in vitamin C (28 mg/100g), iodine, and antioxidants. Excellent hedge plant; tolerates salt spray. Self-fertile but cross-pollination improves yield. Flowers edible, used in salads. Leaves used medicinally as antiseptic and anti-inflammatory.

- **White Sapote (Casimiroa edulis)** -- Evergreen tree to 15 m, native to Mexico and Central America. Frost tolerant to -5 C when mature. Fruit eaten fresh or used in drinks; described as having creamy, mild banana-vanilla flavour. Three main varieties: white, yellow, and green. Fruit contains sleep-inducing compounds. Prefers well-drained soils.

- **Pomegranate (Punica granatum)** -- Deciduous shrub or small tree to 6 m, extremely drought tolerant and adapted to alkaline soils. Frost hardy to -15 C. Native to Iran and N. India. Fruit high in vitamin C, potassium, and antioxidants. Rinds used for tanning leather; juice used as natural dye. Multiple functions: ornamental, fruit, boundary plant, windbreak.

- **[[carob]] (Ceratonia siliqua)** -- Evergreen Mediterranean tree to 15 m, extremely drought tolerant, lives 100+ years. Frost hardy to -6 C. Pods contain 40-50% sugar, used as cocoa substitute, sweetener, and animal feed. Often used as shade and nurse tree. Dioecious (separate male and female trees). Produces in low-rainfall areas (250 mm annual rainfall).

- **Che (Cudrania tricuspidata)** -- Deciduous tree to 9 m, native to China. Hardy to -20 C. Fruit resembles mulberry in appearance and flavour. Dioecious; females produce parthenocarpic fruit without male pollination. Pest-free, disease-resistant, tolerates poor soils.

- **Medlar (Mespilus germanica)** -- Deciduous tree to 6 m, native to SE Europe and W. Asia. Hardy to -25 C. Fruit must be bletted (softened by frost/storage) before eating. Related to hawthorns and pears. Ornamental form with twisted branches.

- **Strawberry Tree (Arbutus unedo)** -- Evergreen shrub or tree to 9 m, native to Mediterranean. Fruit edible but mealy; better used for jams, liqueurs, and distillation (sugar content 15-20%). Frost hardy to -15 C. Ornamental, bee forage, fire retardant, tolerates salt spray. Good for marginal sites.

### Berry Fruits and Small Fruits

- **Blueberries (Vaccinium spp)** -- Bee forage plant, suited to water margins and acidic soils.
- **Fuchsia (Fuchsia spp)** -- Edible berries on ornamental shrubs.
- **Lillipillies (Eugenia/Acmena/Syzigium spp)** -- Australian native fruits with fire retardant properties.
- **Ugni (Ugni molinaea)** -- Small evergreen shrub with edible berries.

## [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] for Fruit Trees

[[permaculture-tagasaste]] (Chamaecytisus palmensis) is the most frequently recommended nurse species, appearing as a companion for nearly every fruit tree. Garlic is the most commonly recommended herb companion. Other key companions:

- **Citrus**: lucerne, tagasaste, raspberries, guava, garlic
- **Apple**: tagasaste, lucerne, wallflowers, chives, nasturtiums, garlic, mulberry (avoid walnut nearby)
- **Fig**: tagasaste, lucerne, garlic
- **Grapes**: geraniums, mulberry, hyssop, basil, peas, beans, garlic

## Design Considerations

- Interplant young fruit trees with fast-growing nurse species (tagasaste, small acacias, cassias) to reduce wind damage
- Secondary crops can fill space between young trees until canopy closes
- Diversity of cultivars ensures cross-pollination and resilience to adverse weather
- Sheltered sites recommended for species prone to wind damage (e.g., macadamias)
- Avoid cultivating soil around established trees to prevent root fungus damage

## Multi-Function Fruit Trees

The most valuable permaculture fruit trees provide yields beyond fruit:

- **Mulberry**: Fruit + timber (tool handles, fence posts) + livestock fodder (leaves) + silkworm food + pig forage + bee forage
- **Fig**: Fruit (fresh, dried, frozen) + poultry feed + fire retardant + long-lived shade + bee forage
- **Olive**: Oil + fruit + windbreak + drought/soil indicator + extremely long-lived
- **Carob**: Sweet pods (cocoa substitute) + animal feed + shade/nurse tree + drought tolerance + 100+ year lifespan
- **Pomegranate**: Fruit + juice (dye) + rinds (tanning) + drought/salt tolerance + ornamental + windbreak
- **Feijoa**: Fruit + edible flowers + medicinal leaves + hedge + salt spray tolerance + bee forage
- **Strawberry Tree**: Fruit (jams/liqueur) + ornamental + bee forage + fire retardant + salt tolerance
