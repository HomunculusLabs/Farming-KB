## Biofilm Management

Biofilm develops on all surfaces in contact with the tea, including the underside of lids, 90-degree pipe bends (especially on the opposite side from the flow impact), and tank bottoms. Biofilm begins where water flow carries the most debris, not on the impact surface.

- Wash all biofilm off at the end of each run
- Flat-bottom tanks with 90-degree angles are hard to clean and aerate
- Non-removable lids complicate cleaning
- Any dark/black biofilm is a breeding ground for E. coli
- Consider pipe design: plastic tubing is easier to clean than rigid pipe assemblies with many fittings

## Water Quality

- Remove un-reacted chlorine by aerating water before use (oxygen radicals destroy organism membranes)
- Alkaline water requires acid neutralization -- consult SFI for guidance on amounts
- Reverse osmosis is appropriate for very salty or highly chlorinated water, but pure de-ionized water lacks necessary mineral salts for microbes
- EC/salt levels must be checked; high salts kill microbes through osmotic effects

## Compost Containment

Compost must be contained in a bag or mesh during brewing if the tea will be sprayed (to prevent nozzle clogging). Bag opening sizes can go down to 200 micrometers without harming biology. Nylon stockings work but tend to run; dedicated mesh bags are more durable. Filtering tea often results in fungal loss.

## Machine Size and Compost Amounts

- A 50 gallon machine typically uses 7 to 15 lbs of compost
- A 500 gallon machine typically uses 15 to 20 lbs of compost
- The increase is non-linear -- better to use two 2000L machines than one 4000L machine
- Testing each specific machine is required to determine the correct compost amount

## pH During Brewing

Aerobic fungi buffer pH between 5.5 and 7.0. Hold tea pH in this range for best results. pH is a consequence of the biology, not a driver -- it serves as a symptom indicator. If oxygen drops, [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]], then open carefully and smell:
- Bottle blown up or sucked in = problem with tea maker (likely anaerobic conditions)
- Smells bad = not enough aeration or too much food
- Smells good and dark brown color = tea is likely in good shape
