# Ayahuasca Preparation

## Overview

Ayahuasca (from Quechua: "vine of the souls" or "vine of the dead") is a psychoactive brew originating from the Amazon basin, used traditionally by Indigenous peoples of Peru, Ecuador, Colombia, Brazil, and Bolivia. The brew combines **[[banisteriopsis-caapi]]** (ayahuasca vine), which contains beta-carboline [[harmala-alkaloids]] (MAO inhibitors), with **[[psychotria-viridis]]** (chacruna) or **Diplopterys cabrerana** (chaliponga), which provide N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT). The MAO inhibition allows orally active DMT, which would otherwise be degraded by monoamine oxidase enzymes in the gut.

See [[dmt-reference]] for detailed pharmacology of DMT.

## Core Ingredients

### Banisteriopsis caapi (Ayahuasca Vine)

- Family: Malpighiaceae
- Contains: harmine, harmaline, tetrahydroharmine (THH), and related beta-carbolines
- These are reversible MAO-A inhibitors and also 5-HT2A agonists
- THH is a serotonin reuptake inhibitor, contributing to the brew's antidepressant qualities
- The vine alone produces mild psychoactive effects: calm, introspection, purgation
- Multiple varieties recognized by Indigenous users: red (cepal), yellow, black (trueno), white, each with distinct effects

### Psychotria viridis (Chacruna)

- Family: Rubiaceae
- Contains: DMT (0.1-0.6% dry weight), sometimes 5-MeO-DMT in trace amounts
- Leaves are the plant part used; occasionally fresh leaves are preferred over dried
- The primary DMT source in Peruvian and Ecuadorian ayahuasca

### Diplopterys cabrerana (Chaliponga / Chagropanga)

- Family: Malpighiaceae
- Contains: DMT (0.1-0.4%) plus 5-MeO-DMT in significant amounts
- Produces a more intense, "denser" experience than chacruna
- More commonly used in Colombian and Ecuadorian preparations

### Other Traditional Admixtures

Indigenous recipes often include additional plants with various purposes:

- **Brunfelsia grandiflora** (chiric sanango) — for bone pain, cleansing
- **Tobacco ([[nicotiana-rustica]])** — Mapacho, added for protection and strength; very strong
- **Datura brugmansia** (toé) — added in some traditions; contains tropane alkaloids, increases risk
- **[[justicia-pectoralis]]** (marmelinho) — flavoring and aromatic
- **[[ilex-guayusa]]** — stimulant admixture, rich in caffeine
- **[[theobroma-cacao]]** — occasionally added, especially in Ecuadorian ceremonies

From Pablo Amaringo's Shipibo tradition (see [[pablo-amaringo]], [[amazonian-plant-teachers-doctores]]):

- **Toe (Brugmansia sp.)** — Two leaves added to brew; contains tropane alkaloids
- **Motelo huasca (Bauhinia guianensis)** — Two pieces of ground vine; "motelo" = tortoise
- **Chicuro (Cyperus sp.)** — Combined with shillinto
- **Shillinto (Mascagnia psilophylla)** — Vine teacher admixture
- **Puka-lupuna (Cavanillesia hylogeiton, C. umbellata)** — Canopy tree spirit admixture
- **Clavohuasca (Tynanthus panurensis)** — Vine for strength and vitality

Important Shipibo principle: "It is not always good to mix in so many things. It's better to make it each time with the chacruna and, if he so wishes, he may add another plant, always one at a time."

## Traditional Preparation Method

### Materials

- B. caapi sections (vine segments, often cleaned and pounded or shredded)
- P. viridis leaves (fresh or dried)
- Large pot (aluminum, stainless steel, or clay)
- Water (soft water preferred; river water traditionally)
- Fire source (wood fire preferred traditionally)

### Process

1. **Vine preparation**: Clean B. caapi vine sections of outer bark. Pound with a mallet or shred into fibers. Some traditions toast the vine lightly first. Quantity varies: typically 50-150 g of fresh vine per person.
2. **Leaf preparation**: Wash P. viridis leaves. Tear or cut into pieces. Typically 25-75 g fresh leaves per person.
3. **First boil**: Add vine to pot with water (enough to cover generously). Bring to a boil, then simmer for 2-6 hours. Some traditions boil the vine alone first before adding leaves.
4. **Add leaves**: Add chacruna/chaliponga to the pot. Continue simmering for 1-3 hours.
5. **Reduction**: Periodically remove liquid and continue reducing to concentrate. Some traditions perform 2-3 separate reductions (espumado — skimming foam).
6. **Strain**: Pour through a strainer or cloth to remove plant material.
7. **Second/third boil**: Plant material is often re-boiled 1-2 more times with fresh water, then combined.
8. **Final reduction**: All combined liquid is reduced to a drinkable volume, typically 100-200 ml per dose. The brew is dark brown, bitter, and thick.
9. **Storage**: Can be stored in glass or plastic; refrigeration extends shelf life to several weeks. Some traditions prepare fresh for each ceremony.

### Traditional Variations

- Shipibo preparation tends to produce a thick, dark, strong brew with extended boiling
- Some Amazonian groups add the leaves only at the end to preserve DMT content
- Certain traditions fast or diet (dieta) before and after ceremony, avoiding salt, sugar, pork, alcohol, and sex
- Brew strength varies enormously between traditions and practitioners

### Shipibo Preparation (from Pablo Amaringo)

Specific details from Amaringo's documented Shipibo tradition:

1. **Pre-harvest discipline**: 3 days sexual abstinence; no contact with ill-tempered people; no salty, sweet, or bitter food; no alcohol
2. **Harvest ritual**: Rise early; offer tobacco, matches, and banana leaf paper at the foot of the ayahuasca plant; observe the chicua bird (Piaya cayana) omen — "chis, chis" = proceed, "chicua" = abort
3. **Chacruna harvesting**: Collected in the morning while fasting
4. **Pot preparation**: First put ayahuasca in pot, whistle an icaro (power song), and blow on the pot
5. **Chacruna placement**: Sun-receiving side placed downward, "spurs" pointing upward
6. **Layering**: Ground ayahuasca layered over chacruna (12-15 pieces of ground vine, two handfuls of chacruna leaves)
7. **Pot material**: Must be clay, not aluminum — essential for effective purge
8. **Tobacco**: A handful added to make the mixture boil
9. **Boiling**: 3-4 hours across 3-4 pots; juice periodically transferred; new vine pieces added to empty pots
10. **Solitude**: No one may see or speak to the preparer; preparation must be in an isolated place
11. **Spiritual requirement**: "It does not suffice to cook the vine. One must also have some spiritual contact to give one strength"

## Modern Variations

### Pharmahuasca

Pharmahuasca uses isolated or synthetic compounds rather than plant material:

- **Harmine or harmaline** (150-300 mg) + **DMT** (50-150 mg), taken orally
- Provides precise dosing control, avoiding the variability of plant material
- Can be prepared with pharmaceutical-grade compounds or extracted from plant sources
- Doses should be taken 15-30 minutes apart (MAOI first)
- Risk of hypertensive crisis with tyramine-containing foods (cheese, red wine, cured meats) is low with reversible MAOIs but not zero

### Changa

Changa is a smokable DMT blend, distinct from ayahuasca but related:

- DMT-containing plant material or extracted DMT infused onto a smoking herb blend
- Typically includes Banisteriopsis caapi leaf or extract (providing harmala alkaloids)
- The harmala alkaloids modify and extend the DMT experience when smoked
- Common base herbs: mullein, peppermint, passionflower, caapi leaf, blue lotus
- Typical DMT content: 10-30% by weight
- Ratio of DMT to harmalas: roughly 1:1 to 1:3
- Effects: onset 10-30 seconds, duration 10-20 minutes (vs. 4-6 hours for oral ayahuasca)
- Can be smoked in a pipe, bong, or vaporizer

### Ayahuasca Microdosing

- Small doses (5-15 ml of standard brew) taken daily or several times per week
- Claimed benefits: mood elevation, increased focus, emotional processing
- Less studied than psilocybin or LSD microdosing
- Should follow standard ayahuasca dietary precautions

## Dietary Restrictions (Dieta)

Traditional ayahuasca use involves dietary restrictions, partially pharmacological and partially spiritual:

- **Pharmacological basis**: MAOIs can interact dangerously with tyramine-rich foods and serotonergic drugs
- Avoid: SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol, dextromethorphan, 5-HTP, St. John's wort, meperidine
- Avoid: fermented foods, aged cheeses, cured meats, red wine (tyramine interaction)
- Avoid: alcohol, recreational drugs, excessive caffeine
