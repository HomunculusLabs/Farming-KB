# Food Drying and Dehydration Methods

Drying is one of the oldest and simplest methods of [[homesteading-food-preservation]]. It requires no refrigeration and preserves nutrients well. Three things give hardy strength: sleeping on hairy mattresses, breathing cold air, and eating dried food (Welsh proverb).

## Air Drying

The simplest method, works well for herbs and alliums:

- **Herbs**: Tie in bundles and hang in a warm, dry, well-ventilated area
- **Onions and garlic**: Braid and hang, or spread on screens
- **Peppers**: String on thread and hang
- **Fruit**: Slice thinly and spread on screens in full sun
- **Tomatoes**: Slice, salt lightly, and dry in sun or dehydrator

Key factors: warmth, air circulation, and low humidity. A solar dehydrator improves on air drying by trapping heat and directing airflow.

## Blanching Before Drying

Some vegetables benefit from blanching (brief boiling water bath) before drying to deactivate enzymes that cause quality loss during storage:

| Vegetable | Blanch Time | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| Green beans | 3 min | Cut to uniform size |
| Peas | 2 min | Shell first |
| Carrots | 3-4 min | Slice or dice |
| Corn | 3 min | Cut from cob |
| Broccoli | 3 min | Cut florets |
| Spinach | 1-2 min | Whole leaves |

## Solar Dehydrators

A solar dehydrator uses passive [[passive-solar-and-biomass-energy]] to dry food efficiently:

- Absorber plate (painted black) heats air
- Chimney effect draws warm air upward through food trays
- Vents control airflow and temperature
- Can reach 130-160°F (54-71°C) in good sun
- Protect food from insects and dust

## Electric Dehydrators

More consistent than solar but uses energy:

- Adjustable thermostat (95-155°F / 35-68°C)
- Fan for even air circulation
- Multiple trays for different foods simultaneously
- Most foods dry at 125-135°F (52-57°C)

## Drying Guidelines by Food

| Food | Preparation | Temperature | Time |
|------|------------|-------------|------|
| Apples | Slice 1/4 inch, treat with lemon | 135°F | 6-12 hrs |
| Bananas | Slice 1/4 inch | 135°F | 6-10 hrs |
| Tomatoes | Slice 1/2 inch, salt | 135°F | 8-15 hrs |
| Herbs | Whole stems | 95°F | 2-4 hrs |
| Jerky | Slice 1/4 inch, marinate | 155°F | 4-6 hrs |
| Peppers | Slice or whole | 125°F | 4-12 hrs |
| Berries | Whole | 135°F | 8-15 hrs |
| Zucchini | Slice 1/4 inch | 125°F | 5-10 hrs |

## Storage

- Cool, dark, dry location
- Moisture is the enemy — ensure food is fully dry
- Store in airtight containers (glass jars with desiccant)
- Vacuum sealing extends shelf life significantly
- Properly dried foods last 6-12 months at room temperature
- Frozen storage extends life to several years
