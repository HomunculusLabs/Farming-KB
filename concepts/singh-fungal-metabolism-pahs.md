# Fungal Metabolism of Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (Singh)

## PAHs in the Environment

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) are ubiquitous environmental
contaminants formed during incomplete combustion of organic materials.
Major sources include:

Fossil fuel combustion (vehicles, power plants) Industrial processes (steel, coke, aluminum production) Wood burning and forest fires Waste incineration Oil spills and petroleum contamination Agricultural burning

PAHs are classified by ring number:

- **2-3 rings** (naphthalene, anthracene, phenanthrene, fluorene):
  More soluble, more readily degraded
- **4 rings** (pyrene, chrysene, fluoranthene): Intermediate
  solubility and persistence
- **5-6 rings** (benzo[a]pyrene, benz[a]anthracene): Highly
  hydrophobic, persistent, and carcinogenic

## Fungal Metabolism Pathways

### Cytochrome P450 Monooxygenase Pathway

The primary fungal PAH metabolism pathway involves:

1. **Epoxidation**: Cytochrome P450 introduces an epoxide group across
   an aromatic double bond
2. **Hydration**: Epoxide hydrolase converts epoxide to trans-dihydrodiol
3. **Dehydrogenation**: Dihydrodiol dehydrogenase converts to catechol
4. **Ring cleavage**: Ortho- or meta-cleavage of the catechol ring
5. **Further metabolism**: Side-chain oxidation and entry into central
   metabolism

### Ligninolytic Enzyme Pathway

[[white-rot-fungi]] degrade PAHs through non-specific ligninolytic
enzyme systems:

- **[[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] (LiP)**: One-electron oxidation of PAH aromatic
  rings to radical cations, initiating non-enzymatic reactions leading
  to ring opening
- **[[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]] (MnP)**: Generates diffusible Mn(III) chelates
  that oxidize PAHs at distance from [[fungal-hyphae]]
- **[[ligninolytic-enzymes]]**: Oxidize PAHs with mediator compounds
  (ABTS, HBT) extending substrate range to higher-molecular-weight PAHs

## PAH Degradation in Soil

### Influence of Co-substrates and Surfactants

- **Co-substrates**: Lignocellulosic materials (straw, sawdust, wood
  chips) stimulate [[white-rot-fungi]] growth and ligninolytic enzyme
  production, enhancing PAH degradation
- **Surfactants**: Non-ionic surfactants (Triton X-100, Tween 80)
  increase PAH solubility and bioavailability. Cyclodextrins are
  biodegradable alternatives that form inclusion complexes with PAHs
- **Nutrient amendments**: Nitrogen and phosphorus
  ([[biostimulation-techniques]]) enhance overall microbial activity

### Fate of PAH Disappearance

**Mineralization**: Complete conversion to CO2 and water. Rarely
achieved for high-molecular-weight PAHs. Measured using radiolabeled
(14C) PAHs.

**[[fungal-bound-residue-formation]]**: [[singh-fungal-pah-metabolites-mutagenicity]] become covalently
incorporated into [[knf-leaf-mold-humus]] (humus). Bound residues are less
bioavailable and less toxic but may persist for years. Fungal
transformation contributes significantly to bound residue formation
through oxidative coupling reactions.
