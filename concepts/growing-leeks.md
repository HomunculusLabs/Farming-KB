# Growing Leeks

Depending on your climate, raising this slow-growing allium can be effortless or daunting. In a maritime climate, leeks grow effortlessly during mild periods and are harvested throughout winter. Where summers are short and winters severe, timing is critical.

## Climate Adaptation

| Climate | Strategy |
|---------|----------|
| Maritime (mild) | Start in spring when convenient — about when apple trees bloom |
| Short summers | Start earliest possible sowing for maximum growing time |
| Hot summers | Start after worst heat breaks — gives less time to size up |
| Southern US / warm AU | Sow after end of hot weather, push with maximum fertility and moisture |

Leeks take **150-180 days** to achieve admirable size. In all situations, the leek goes to seed shortly after the spring equinox following overwintering.

## Starting Seedlings

### Nursery Bed Preparation

1. Take a small bit of bed or row and amend with at least 2 inches (5 cm) of best [[compost]] or well-aged manure, well mixed in
2. Also add COF or [[chicken-manure]] compost or another rich source of rapidly available complete plant nutrients
3. Sow seeds rather densely — about 12 seeds to the inch (2.5 cm)
4. Cover with fine soil or fine compost about 1/4 inch (6 mm) thick
5. Pat down firmly enough to restore capillarity

A single 4-foot-long (120 cm) row of seedlings produces enough to transplant for your garden and two friends' gardens.

### Thinning and Care

- If good germination, thin to about 1 inch (2.5 cm) after a month or so
- Side-dress with extra fertilizer if they aren't growing really fast
- Do not let this little bit of row get dry
- In moderate climates, leeks take about 90 days to reach transplanting size
- Consider providing about 40% shade for the first month in hot climates
- Presprout seeds if sowing in late summer heat

## Transplanting

### Digging and Sorting

1. Just before transplanting, dig up the entire seedling row
2. Gently shake the earth from roots while doing as little damage as possible
3. **Separate by size**: large, medium, and small
4. **For seed saving**: Sort within medium-sized category and remove any with a swelling at the bottom — put those without a perfectly straight shank with the large ones
5. Medium-sized ones with straight shanks and the longest space between root and first leaf are the ones to grow for seed (at least 25 in the lot)

### The Transplanting Cut

Grasp seedlings in large handfuls, held so roots are all at the same position. With a sharp knife, cut or trim off approximately half the leafy part (not including the stem). This means they have to draw less water while roots re-establish. Also cut back the roots, leaving them about 1.5 inches (4 cm) long. This insures 100% transplant success.

### Planting

Keep bare-root seedlings out of the sun — put them in a bucket with about a quarter inch of water. Prepare their bed as loose and as fertile as you know how.

1. Make a hole with a pointed stick or dibber about 8 inches (20 cm) deep
2. Drop one seedling in each hole
3. Do not fill the hole with soil — just pour in water and let it settle the soil around the roots
4. Space about 6-9 inches (15-23 cm) apart in rows 18-24 inches (45-60 cm) apart

## Ongoing Care

- Side-dress with fertilizer along rows about a month after transplanting
- Hill up earth against the stalks as they grow — this increases the length of the white shank
- **Important**: Only hill when you can avoid getting soil into the lower leaf notches — soil in the notches will be taken into the kitchen at harvest and is nearly impossible to clean out
- Irrigation is desirable

## Pests and Diseases

Pests and diseases should not be a problem if you do not grow leeks (or other alliums) in the same soil more than once every 3-4 years.

## Varieties

### Three Types

| Type | Growth Speed | Cold Hardiness | Flavor | Best For |
|------|-------------|----------------|--------|----------|
| Autumn | Faster | Less hardy | Milder, tender | Commercial EU, hot-summer climates |
| Winter | Slower | Tougher, much hardier | More fibrous, pungent | Home gardens (most catalogs) |
| Spring | Variable | Hardy | Good | Overwintering in maritime climates |

In every type, good breeding shows as a **non-bulbous stalk that is long to the first leaf notch**.

## Harvest and Storage

- Leeks must be dug — tops won't lift them
- Remarkably cold-hardy; even in Pennsylvania (low elevations), hardiest varieties may survive winter in the earth if soil is pulled up against them
- Where soil freezes, leeks may be held over winter in a root cellar
- Leeks won't cross with ordinary onions, so saving seed is easier regarding isolation

## Saving Seed

- In mild climates, carefully chosen perfect specimens may be set in a separate area in autumn
- Plant about 1 foot (30 cm) apart in rows 3 feet (90 cm) apart
- The seedstalks grow enormously tall and usually have to be supported
- Seed takes a very long time to mature — until mid-autumn in mild climates
- When seedstalks pull from the earth with the gentlest of tugs, they're ready
- Test each one about once a week
- Cut seedballs free from stalks when crisp, bring inside to finish drying
- Rub between palms to release seeds
