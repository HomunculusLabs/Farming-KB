Understanding light spectrum is essential for choosing the right lighting and maximizing cannabis growth. Greg Green explains how different wavelengths of light affect plant development.

## Light Bands (Wavelengths)

The visible light spectrum ranges from approximately 380nm (violet) to 750nm (red). Different portions of this spectrum serve different functions in plant growth:

### Blue Light (400–500nm)
- Promotes compact, bushy vegetative growth
- Encourages strong stems and healthy leaf development
- Short internode spacing
- Ideal for the vegetative stage
- Produced strongly by metal halide (MH) bulbs

### Green Light (500–600nm)
- Least absorbed by chlorophyll — mostly reflected (which is why plants appear green)
- Still plays a role in canopy penetration (lower leaves receive green-filtered light)
- Less critical for artificial lighting

### Red Light (600–700nm)
- Promotes flowering, stretching, and bud development
- Triggers and supports the flowering response
- Longer internodes, taller growth
- Ideal for the flowering stage
- Produced strongly by high-pressure sodium (HPS) bulbs

### Far-Red / Infrared (700–750nm+)
- Beyond visible range but affects plant morphology
- Can trigger the flowering response when present in the right ratios
- The natural red-to-far-red ratio at sunset signals seasonal changes

## Light Sources and Their Spectra

### Metal Halide (MH)
- Strong blue spectrum
- Best for vegetative growth
- Produces compact, leafy plants
- Some growers use MH for the entire grow for shorter, bushier plants

### High-Pressure Sodium (HPS)
- Strong red/orange spectrum
- Best for flowering
- Promotes bud development and stretching
- Most common flowering light
- Some yellow/green light that is less useful but not harmful

### LED Grow Lights
- Can be tuned to specific spectrum combinations
- Many modern LEDs provide full-spectrum or programmable spectrums
- Energy-efficient with less heat output
- Quality varies significantly between manufacturers

### Compact Fluorescent (CFL)
- Limited spectrum compared to HID
- Good for seedlings, clones, and small grows
- Low heat output
- Best for supplemental or early-stage lighting

## Lighting Factors and Optimization

Greg Green emphasizes several key factors for getting the most from your light:

### Wattage and Coverage
- Approximate coverage per wattage:
  - 250W: ~2.5 x 2.5 feet (0.75 x 0.75m)
  - 400W: ~3 x 3 feet (0.9 x 0.9m)
  - 600W: ~3.5 x 3.5 feet (1 x 1m)
  - 1000W: ~4 x 4 feet (1.2 x 1.2m)
- Higher wattage produces deeper light penetration into the canopy

### Lumens and Plant Growth
- More lumens = more potential energy for photosynthesis
- However, diminishing returns apply — doubling lumens does not double yield
- Light must be evenly distributed across the canopy for best results

### Reflectors
- A good reflector directs light downward onto the plants that would otherwise be lost to the walls and ceiling
- Air-cooled reflectors allow heat management while maintaining light output
- Horizontal reflectors are generally more efficient than vertical for indoor grows

### Light Distance from Canopy
- **HID lights**: Must maintain proper distance to avoid burning plants
  - 250W: 12–18 inches from canopy
  - 400W: 14–24 inches
  - 600W: 18–30 inches
  - 1000W: 24–36 inches
- Use the hand test: place your hand at canopy level under the light — if it's uncomfortably hot after 30 seconds, the light is too close

### Light Movers
- Moving lights can improve light distribution across a larger area
- Eliminates hot spots directly under the fixture
- Plants receive light from multiple angles, improving lower branch development

## Spectrum Manipulation for Growth Stages

### Vegetative (18/6 or 24/0)
- Blue-dominant spectrum preferred
- Metal halide or blue-heavy LED
- Results in compact, bushy plants with short internodes

### Flowering (12/12)
- Red-dominant spectrum preferred
- HPS or red-heavy LED
- Maximizes bud production and flower development
- Some growers start flowering under HPS after vegetative growth under MH

### Dual Spectrum
- Some fixtures (like enhanced HPS or full-spectrum LEDs) provide a balanced spectrum
- Can be used for both stages, simplifying the grow

## UV Light
Some research suggests UV-B exposure (280–315nm) can increase THC and terpene production by triggering a stress response in the plant. However, UV-B is harmful to both plants and humans at high intensities and should be used cautiously.
