Understanding light spectrum is essential for choosing the right lighting and maximizing cannabis growth. Greg Green explains how different wavelengths of light affect plant development.

## Light Bands (Wavelengths)

The visible light spectrum ranges from approximately 380nm (violet) to 750nm (red). Different portions of this spectrum serve different functions in plant growth:

### Blue Light (400–500nm)
- Promotes compact, bushy [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]
- Encourages strong stems and healthy leaf development
- Short internode spacing
- Ideal for the [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]]
- Produced strongly by [[cervantes-metal-halide-lighting-systems]] (MH) bulbs

### Green Light (500–600nm)
- Least absorbed by chlorophyll — mostly reflected (which is why plants appear green)
- Still plays a role in canopy penetration (lower leaves receive green-filtered light)
- Less critical for artificial lighting

### Red Light (600–700nm)
- Promotes flowering, stretching, and bud development
- Triggers and supports the flowering response
- Longer internodes, taller growth
- Ideal for the [[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]]
- Produced strongly by high-pressure sodium (HPS) bulbs

### Far-Red / Infrared (700–750nm+)
- Beyond visible range but affects plant morphology
- Can trigger the flowering response when present in the right ratios
- The natural red-to-far-red ratio at sunset signals seasonal changes

## Light Sources and Their Spectra

### Metal Halide (MH)
- Strong blue spectrum
- Best for vegetative growth
- Produces compact, leafy plants
- Some growers use MH for the entire grow for shorter, bushier plants

### High-Pressure Sodium (HPS)
- Strong red/orange spectrum
- Best for flowering
- Promotes bud development and stretching
- Most common flowering light
- Some yellow/green light that is less useful but not harmful

### LED Grow Lights
- Can be tuned to specific spectrum combinations
- Many modern LEDs provide full-spectrum or programmable spectrums
- Energy-efficient with less heat output
- Quality varies significantly between manufacturers

### Compact Fluorescent (CFL)
- Limited spectrum compared to HID
- Good for seedlings, clones, and small grows
- Low heat output
- Best for supplemental or early-stage lighting
