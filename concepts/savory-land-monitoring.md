## Implementing Control

When monitoring indicates deviation from the plan, control actions include:

1. **Apply the same tool but differently**: Adjust grazing periods, change [[savory-fencing-economics-and-stock-density-curve]], modify timing
2. **Apply a different tool**: Switch from rest to animal impact, or from fire to grazing
3. **Apply a combination of tools**: Use grazing plus animal impact, or technology plus living organisms

If the model shows that correction requires a tool the goal itself rules out, the goal must be reexamined. Tools should never be specified in the goal (beyond general forms of production), as this prematurely constrains management options.

## Monitoring Plant Growth Rates

Where livestock run with few paddocks, daily growth rate monitoring during the growing season is essential. On bunch grass ranges:

- Mark severely grazed plants and ungrazed controls as livestock leave each paddock
- Periodically compare recovery of grazed plants to the ungrazed yardstick
- If plants have barely grown but livestock are due back soon, the move is too fast for prevailing slow growth—overgrazing will occur
- If plants are already nearly indistinguishable from ungrazed plants, the move may be too slow

For pure stands of runner-type grasses where individual plants cannot be detected, judge by general growth rate or volume in the pasture as a whole.

## Annual Landscape Monitoring

All managed lands require annual landscape monitoring—farms, ranches, forests, national parks. This annual check assesses progress toward the landscape goal and detects trends that may require management adjustment.

The monitoring notes used with this technique are constantly being improved to incorporate simpler and more practical methods for measuring change, recognizing that field-level operators usually lack the time or training for full academic studies.
