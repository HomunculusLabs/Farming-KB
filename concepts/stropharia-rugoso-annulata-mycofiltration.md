## Overview

The wine-cap mushroom (Stropharia rugoso-annulata), also known as the garden giant or [[mycelium-running-techniques]]

***

## Species Characteristics

Stropharia rugoso-annulata is a distinctive mushroom with the following traits:
- Large fruiting bodies with wine-red to burgundy caps
- Grows robustly on wood chip substrates
- Extremely aggressive [[singh-mycelial-mats-and-biological-soil-crusts]] before reaching sensitive downstream areas

### Results

The Stropharia beds proved remarkably effective at filtering:
- **Fecal coliform bacteria** — dramatically reduced levels in water passing through the beds
- **Silt and sediment** — trapped by the dense mycelial mat
- **Nutrient runoff** — excess nutrients absorbed by the [[mycelium-running-mycelium-natures-internet-fungal-network-theory]]

The effectiveness against fecal coliform was particularly significant because:
- Fecal contamination threatens shellfish farming operations
- Conventional methods (chemical treatment, pond settling) are expensive and imperfect
- Stropharia beds provide continuous, passive filtration with minimal maintenance

***

## How Stropharia Mycofiltration Works

### The Mycelial Mat

As Stropharia mycelium colonizes a wood chip bed, it forms a dense, interconnected mat that functions as a living filter:
- **Physical filtration:** The dense hyphal network physically traps particles, bacteria, and sediment
- **Biological filtration:** The mycelium secretes enzymes and antibiotics that kill or inhibit pathogens
- **Chemical filtration:** Fungal enzymes break down organic contaminants in the water
- **Biological consumption:** Bacteria and other microorganisms living in the mycelial mat contribute additional filtration

### Water Flow Dynamics

Water percolating through the mycelial bed:
1. Enters the bed carrying contaminants
2. Passes through the dense mycelial mat
3. Contaminants are trapped, killed, or broken down
4. Clean water exits the bed
5. Filtered water is cooler (the mycelial mat provides thermal insulation)
