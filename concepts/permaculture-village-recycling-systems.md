# Permaculture Village Recycling Systems

## Overview

[[permaculture]] extends its design principles to waste management through comprehensive [[community-recycling-systems]]. Mollison highlights the Devonport Borough system in Auckland, New Zealand, as a model that not only eliminates waste but returns a cash benefit to households — inverting the typical cost-of-waste-disposal paradigm.

## The Devonport Model

### Context

The borough of Devonport, in the city of Auckland, New Zealand, operates a total solid waste-recycling system. Rather than households paying rates (taxes) for waste disposal — as in non-recycling areas — the system actually returns a cash benefit to households.

### Key Feature 1: Consumer-Side Separation

The borough issues an **[[fukuoka-rice-barley-annual-calendar]], colour-coded**, and picks up only one category of waste on any given day:

- Clean glass
- Metal
- Tyres
- Paper
- Organic waste
- Oils
- Other specific categories

No mixed loads are collected. This forces recycling to begin at the point of consumption — every household must separate its waste by category before collection.

**Why it works**: By making collection category-specific on any given day, the system removes ambiguity and the temptation to throw everything into one bag. The colour-coded calendar makes compliance simple and predictable.

### Key Feature 2: Supervised Waste Disposal Site

At the waste disposal site, loads must also be in a single category. Trained supervisors direct incoming loads and sort any mixed waste. This ensures downstream processing receives clean, pre-sorted material.

### Material-Specific Processing

#### Wood Processing

Wood arriving at the site is sorted into three streams:

1. **Useful wood** — diverted to a community woodwork centre where residents can access materials for projects
2. **Firewood** — issued to elderly people, providing a social welfare benefit alongside waste reduction
3. **Chipping/mulch wood** — processed for garden mulch [[mollison-permaculture-two-arid-water-harvesting-and-erosion-control]]

#### Organic Waste

All organics are composted using a small tractor windrow system. This produces:
- Saleable [[compost]] for gardens and agriculture
- Mulch for soil conditioning
- Any surplus is used to carpet a "ziggurat"

#### Oil Recycling

All oils are collected and re-sold as lubrication oils after filtration. This:
- Prevents soil and water contamination
- Generates income from what would otherwise be a pollutant
- Reduces demand for virgin oil products

### The Ziggurat: Waste-to-Landscape Innovation

Any surplus [[using-accumulators-in-compost-and-mulch]] is used to carpet a **ziggurat** — an ascending spiral ramp constructed from:
- Broken pipe
- Brick
- Clay
- Concrete
- Clean fill

As the ziggurat ascends, community [[plant-disease-management-solomon]] follow the fill, creating:
- A terraced growing space from waste materials
- A productive landscape feature from construction debris
- A visual landmark and community gathering point
- Multiple microclimates for diverse planting

The ziggurat concept embodies core [[permaculture-principles]]: the problem (waste surplus) becomes the solution (growing space), and every element serves multiple functions.

### Site Organisation

The waste site is:
- **Supervised** — staff direct traffic and processing
- **Loads directed** — each category goes to its designated area
- **Mixed waste sorted** — non-compliant loads are separated on-site
- **Saleable/recyclable items grouped** — clearly marked areas for each material type

## Principles of Community Recycling

### Waste as a Resource

The Devonport model demonstrates that what is conventionally called "waste" is in fact a resource stream. When properly sorted, virtually everything has value:

| "Waste" Category | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Clean glass | Recycled glass products or aggregate |
| Metal | Scrap metal for smelting |
| Tyres | Rubber crumb, erosion control, retaining walls |
| Paper | Recycled paper products, mulch |
| Organic waste | Compost, mulch |
| Oils | Filtered lubrication oils |
| Wood | Timber, firewood, mulch |
| Construction debris | Ziggurat fill, terraced gardens |

### Economic Inversion

The most significant insight from the Devonport model is the **economic inversion**: from a cost centre (households pay for waste removal) to a revenue source (households receive cash benefit). This is achieved through:

1. **Reduced disposal costs** — less goes to landfill
2. **Revenue from sales** — compost, mulch, recycled oil, useful wood all generate income
3. **Reduced raw material costs** — community members access free firewood, woodwork materials
4. **Social welfare benefits** — elderly receive free firewood

### The Role of Design in Waste Elimination

[[permaculture-design]] aims to eliminate the concept of waste entirely. The recycling system is not an add-on but an integral part of village design:

- Organic waste feeds compost systems that feed gardens
- Water waste (sullage) feeds biomass conversion systems
- Methane from sewage provides energy
- Wood waste provides mulch for gardens and fuel
- Construction waste becomes [[desert-landscape-features-and-erosion-landforms]]

## Designing a Village Recycling System

### Collection System Design

1. **Colour-coded calendar** — one waste category per collection day
2. **No mixed loads accepted** — forces source separation
3. **Regular schedule** — builds habits and compliance
4. **Clear signage** — at collection points and disposal site

### Processing Infrastructure

A village recycling system needs:

- **Supervised disposal area** — staffed during operating hours
- **Sorting stations** — for each material category
- **Composting area** — windrow system with small tractor
- **Woodworking centre** — for useful wood
- **Oil filtration facility** — for lubrication oil recovery
- **Storage areas** — for saleable materials awaiting sale
- **Ziggurat site** — for construction debris and surplus compost

### Integration with Village Systems

Recycling should connect to other village systems:

- Compost → market gardens and orchards
- Mulch → food forests and erosion control
