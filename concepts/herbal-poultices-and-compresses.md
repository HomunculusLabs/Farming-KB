# Herbal Poultices and Compresses

Poultices and compresses are among the oldest and most direct forms of herbal medicine application. They involve applying plant material (fresh or dried) directly to the skin to deliver medicinal compounds locally for wounds, inflammation, infections, sprains, and other conditions. These preparations are simple, effective, require minimal equipment, and can be made from plants growing wild or in the garden.

## Poultices

A poultice is a soft, moist mass of plant material applied directly to the skin, usually covered with a cloth or bandage to hold it in place. Poultices deliver the full spectrum of the plant's medicinal compounds directly to the affected area, including volatile oils, mucilage, tannins, and other constituents that might be lost or altered in more processed preparations.

Fresh herb poultices are the simplest and most potent form. Fresh plant material is chewed, crushed, or macerated to release its juices and active compounds, then applied directly to the skin. This method preserves heat-sensitive compounds and volatile oils that might be lost in drying or heating. Fresh poultices are ideal when the medicinal plant is readily available at the site of need.

To make a fresh herb poultice, collect the fresh plant material (leaves, flowers, roots). Clean it if necessary. Crush or chew the plant material to break the cell walls and release the juices. Apply the macerated mass directly to the affected area. Cover with a clean cloth or plastic wrap to hold the poultice in place and prevent drying. Secure with a bandage or tape. Leave in place for 30 minutes to several hours, replacing when dry.

Dried herb poultices are made by reconstituting dried herbs with a small amount of hot water to form a paste. The herbs absorb the water and release their constituents into a thick, spreadable mass. This method is used when fresh plant material is not available. Use just enough hot water to create a thick paste; too much water makes the poultice runny and difficult to apply.

## Specific Compress Applications

Chamomile compresses are used for eye inflammation, skin rashes, and eczema. A strong chamomile tea cooled to room temperature is applied to closed eyes or affected skin. Chamomile's anti-inflammatory and soothing properties make it ideal for sensitive areas.

Witch hazel compresses are astringent and anti-inflammatory, used for hemorrhoids, varicose veins, bruises, and under-eye swelling. Witch hazel extract applied to a cloth and placed over the affected area provides rapid relief.

Peppermint tea compresses applied to the forehead or temples provide cooling relief for tension headaches and migraines. The menthol in peppermint produces a cooling, analgesic effect.

Yarrow tea compresses are used for bleeding wounds and nosebleeds. Yarrow's hemostatic properties help stop bleeding when applied with gentle pressure.

## Wraps and Packs

Wraps and packs are larger applications of the poultice/compress principle that cover broader areas of the body. They are used for conditions affecting joints, large muscle groups, the abdomen, or the chest.
