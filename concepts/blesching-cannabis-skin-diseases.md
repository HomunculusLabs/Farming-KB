# Cannabis and Skin Diseases (General)

## Overview

Diseases affecting the skin, hair, and nails are generally considered skin
diseases. A majority of patients who seek treatment from a dermatologist also
request cosmetic procedures such as hair removal, hair transplants, mole
removal, wrinkle reduction, or liposuction. Dermatological treatment options
include pharmaceuticals, radiation (including laser and UVB), and surgery
(cryosurgery, mole removal, hair transplants).

According to the Cannabis Health Index, the evidence for cannabis in treating
skin diseases is based on 4 studies reviewed with a total CHI value of 10,
yielding a CHI score of 2.50.

## The Endocannabinoid System in the Skin

A multi-institutional study (2009) with researchers from Germany, the United
States, England, and Hungary identified the endocannabinoid system (ECS) in
the skin for possible targeted approaches in treating various skin diseases.
The main function of the ECS in the skin is to:

- Control and balance growth of skin cells
- Regulate differentiation (the process of becoming a specific skin cell)
- Manage survival of skin cells
- Produce proper immune responses

### ECS Receptor Functions for Skin Conditions

| Condition             | CB1/CB2 Action                                           |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Skin cancer           | Up-regulation suppresses cancer growth and metastasis     |
| Psoriasis             | Up-regulation suppresses keratinocyte proliferation       |
| Hair loss             | Down-regulation of CB1 stimulates hair growth             |
| Hirsutism             | Up-regulation of CB1 suppresses hair growth               |
| Seborrhea, acne       | Down-regulation of CB2 inhibits sebum/lipid production    |
| Dry skin              | Up-regulation of CB2 increases sebum/lipid production     |
| Dermatitis (eczema)   | Up-regulation suppresses immune/inflammatory process      |
| Systemic sclerosis    | Up-regulation of CB2 suppresses inflammation/fibrosis     |
| Pain, itching         | Up-regulation reduces pain and itch, inhibits signals     |

## Cannabis and Skin Diseases: The Evidence

### ECS Targeting for Skin Conditions (2009, Multi-institutional)

The landmark multi-institutional study concluded: "Targeted manipulation of
the ECS might be beneficial in a multitude of human skin diseases." This
finding opened the door for developing cannabinoid-based dermatological
treatments tailored to specific conditions by selectively targeting CB1 or
CB2 receptors.

### Endocannabinoids in Hair Follicles (2007, University of Debrecen, Hungary)

Researchers discovered that human scalp hair follicles contain both
endocannabinoid receptors and endocannabinoids, determined to be key players
in hair growth regulation. CB1 agonists may reduce unwanted hair growth,
while CB1 antagonists might counteract hair loss. This finding has
implications for treating both hirsutism and alopecia.

### Topical Cannabinoid Cream for Itching (2006, Munster, Germany)

A cream containing N-palmitoyl ethanolamine (a fatty acid amide that
enhances anandamide action) produced an average of over 80% reduction in
itching. Researchers wrote: "Topical cannabinoid agonists represent a new,
effective, and well-tolerated therapy for refractory itching of various
origins."

### Oral THC for Cholestatic Pruritus (2002, University of Miami)

Three patients with intractable pruritus from cholestatic liver disease
received 5 mg Delta-9-THC (Marinol) at bedtime. All three reported decreased
itching, improved sleep, and eventually returned to work. Depression resolved
in two of three patients.

## The Skin as an Organ

The skin is the largest organ of the body, sensing touch, vibration, and
temperature. It is semi-permeable -- preventing fluid loss while absorbing
some oxygen and small substances. The skin functions as:

- A first line of defense
- A temperature regulation system
- A border, boundary, and barrier
- An expression of self-image, identity, and individuality

## Mind-Body Medicine and Skin Disease

The way a person feels about their skin often reveals the inner image they
hold of themselves. Feeling ugly, hideous, embarrassed, or ashamed are
common emotional themes in the self-talk of patients with skin problems.
Projecting these feelings onto others intensifies these emotional prisons:
"If I feel ugly, I look ugly."

Stress reduces the body's ability to heal itself and worsens overall skin
health by increasing hormone releases such as epinephrine and cortisol,
which over-stimulate sebaceous glands, producing skin eruptions.

### The Path to Healing

Tracing negative feelings back to the constricting pattern that comprises a
person's self-image and replacing it with one that gives rise to a more
positive sense of self can yield positive results in the emotional life and
may also balance hormone levels, increasing the effectiveness of whatever
healing regimen is engaged.

## Dietary Support for Skin Health

General skin health support includes: Bush Tea, Cacao, Cajuput, Cayenne,
Coconut, Garlic, Grains of Paradise, Oregano, Rosemary, and Turmeric.

Turmeric's active compound curcumin has demonstrated anti-inflammatory and
antioxidant properties that support skin healing. Cayenne stimulates
peripheral circulation, improving nutrient delivery to skin tissue.
Rosemary is approved by the German Commission E for supportive therapy for
rheumatic diseases and circulatory problems.
