## IMO-2: Sugar Fermentation

IMO-1 is mixed with jaggery or brown sugar in a 1:1 ratio by weight. The less processed
the sugar, the more effective — white refined sugar is not recommended because it lacks
minerals. Crude unrefined jaggery is ideal. The mixture ferments in a clay pot or glass
jar covered with porous paper: approximately 3 days in summer, 5 days in winter.

## IMO-3: Mass Cultivation on Rice Bran

IMO-2 is diluted with water at 1:1000 and mixed with rice bran or flour to achieve 65–70%
moisture. The squeeze test confirms correct consistency: the mixture forms a lump when
pressed but breaks apart when shaken. Diluted NF inputs (FPJ, FAA, OHN at standard rates)
can enhance the mixture.

The bran is stacked on soil floor (never concrete) in heaps no more than 30–40 cm high,
covered with paddy straw to prevent temperature exceeding 50°C. Straw is flipped every
2 days. Temperature management is critical: below 40°C indicates anaerobic conditions
from excess moisture; above 70°C causes thermophilic protein breakdown and nitrogen loss.
The pile is turned when it reaches 40–50°C. After 5–7 days, white spores cover the
surface, fermentation odor becomes fragrant, and moisture drops to ~40%. This is IMO-3.

## IMO-4: Soil Integration

IMO-3 is combined 1:1 with soil — 50% from the crop field and 50% from fresh sources
(mountain soil, anthill/termite mound red fine soil). This harmonizes wild IMOs with
field-adapted populations. Mixing occurs on soil floor in heaps no more than 20 cm high,
covered for two days. Moisture is maintained at 65–75% using diluted NF inputs. Seawater
is added before field application to provide trace minerals optimizing microbial activity.
