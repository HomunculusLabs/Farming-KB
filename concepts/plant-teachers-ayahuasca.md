## Plant Teachers (Doctores) in Amazonian Shamanism

In Amazonian vegetalismo, every plant is understood to possess a spirit --
a conscious, living essence that sees everything. As [[ayahuasca-preparation]]|Ayahuasca ([[chacruna-psychotria-viridis]]) is the essential companion to ayahuasca. In
visions, the ayahuasca vine appears as a black snake with yellow, orange,
and blue spots, while chacruna manifests as a bright, luminous serpent.
The chacruna serpent penetrates the ayahuasca serpent, producing the
visionary effect. Their combined action creates esoteric experiences: the
world penetrates the top of the head, stimulating a gland between the
eyebrows, and generating psychic bodies never before perceived.

## How Plant-Teachers Communicate

Plant-teachers communicate through several channels:

- **Visions during ayahuasca sessions**: The primary mode of instruction,
  where spirits of plants appear and teach directly
- **Dreams**: Plants such as renaquilla (Ficus sp.) send their spirit

## The Campa Indians as Masters of Plant Knowledge

Among all jungle peoples, the Campa (Asháninka) Indians are described as
knowing better than any other group the magical uses of plant-teachers.
They use ayahuasca-preparation|ayahuasca along with piripiri (Cyperus
sp.), toe (Brugmansia sp.), and chirik-sanango (Brunfelsia grandiflora).
Their ancestral Indian chiefs approach in vision to bestow knowledge upon
their practitioners.

## Additives to Ayahuasca as Secondary Teachers

Many plants serve as additives to the ayahuasca brew, each contributing
their own teaching and spirit. These include murero-huasca, chacruna,
clavohuasca, bobinzana (Calliandra angustifolia), ajosquiro, motelo
huasca, albahaca (Ocimum micranthum), and alcanfor (Zanthoxylum sp.).
The spirits of these plants dance while extending the ayahuasca vine,
each radiating their own power. Aromatic plants contribute to the beauty
of the visionary experience.
