Proper watering is one of the most important and commonly misunderstood aspects of cannabis cultivation. Both overwatering and underwatering cause significant problems.

## How to Determine When to Water

### The Finger Test

The simplest and most reliable method:
1. Insert your index finger 1–2 inches into the soil (past the first knuckle)
2. If the soil feels moist at that depth, do NOT water — wait
3. If the soil feels dry at that depth, it is time to water

This checks moisture below the surface, where the roots actually are, rather than the dry top layer.

### The Lift Test

Lift the container before and after watering to learn the weight difference:
- Light container = dry soil = time to water
- Heavy container = moist soil = wait

With experience, you can judge moisture by weight alone.

### Visual Cues

- **Dry top inch**: Normal and expected — the surface dries faster than the root zone
- **Wilting**: Can indicate both under and overwatering (check soil moisture to determine which)
- **Drooping leaves that recover after watering**: Underwatering
- **Drooping leaves that do NOT recover**: Likely overwatering

## Overwatering

Overwatering is the most common mistake among new growers. It does not mean "too much water at once" but rather "watering too frequently before the soil has dried out."

### Symptoms of Overwatering

- Leaves droop and feel heavy and limp (not dry and crispy)
- Leaves may curl downward like a claw
- Soil remains constantly wet and may develop algae on the surface
- Plant growth slows dramatically
- Stems may feel soft and mushy near the base
- Fungus gnats appear around the soil surface
- Root rot may develop in severe cases
- Leaves turn yellow, starting with lower foliage

### Treatment

1. **Stop watering immediately** — let the soil dry out completely
2. **Improve drainage**: Ensure containers have adequate drainage holes
3. **Add perlite**: Increase the perlite content in your soil mix (25–40% is ideal)
4. **Increase air flow**: Better ventilation around the soil surface speeds drying
5. **Check container size**: Small plants in large containers stay wet too long
6. **For severe cases**: Gently remove the plant, inspect roots for rot (brown, slimy roots), trim affected roots, and repot in fresh, well-draining soil

## Underwatering

### Symptoms of Underwatering

- Leaves droop and feel dry and crispy to the touch
- Leaf tips and edges turn brown and brittle
- Soil pulls away from the container edges
- Plant recovers quickly (within hours) after watering
- Growth slows and new leaves are small
- In severe cases, leaves dry up completely and fall off

### Treatment

1. **Water thoroughly**: Apply enough water so that runoff comes from the drainage holes
2. **Water slowly**: Pour gradually to allow the soil to absorb rather than running off
3. **Do not overcorrect**: After underwatering, return to a proper schedule — do not flood the plant
4. **Check soil retention**: If soil dries out too fast, add more organic matter or reduce perlite

## Watering Best Practices

### Water Quality
- **pH**: Maintain water pH between 6.0–7.0 for soil grows, 5.5–6.5 for hydroponic grows
- **Temperature**: Room temperature water (65–75°F / 18–24°C). Cold water shocks roots; hot water damages them.
- **Chlorine**: Let tap water sit for 24 hours in an open container to allow chlorine to dissipate. Or use filtered water.
- **Source**: Rainwater, reverse osmosis, or dechlorinated tap water are preferred

### How to Water Properly

1. **Water slowly and evenly** around the base of the plant, not just one spot
2. **Water until 10–20% runoff** comes from drainage holes — this prevents nutrient salt buildup
3. **Do not water on a fixed schedule** — let the plant tell you when it needs water using the finger test
4. **Avoid getting water on the foliage**, especially under intense lights — wet leaves promote mold and mildew
5. **Adjust for growth stage**: Larger plants need more water; seedlings need very little
6. **Adjust for environment**: Hot, dry conditions increase water needs; cool, humid conditions decrease them

### Watering Frequency Guidelines

These are approximate and will vary based on container size, environment, and plant size:
- **Seedlings in small pots**: Every 2–4 days
- **Vegetative plants in medium pots**: Every 2–5 days
- **Large vegetative plants in large pots**: Every 3–7 days
- **Flowering plants**: Every 2–5 days (generally need more than vegetative due to increased metabolism)

Always use the finger test rather than counting days.

## Hydroponic Watering

Hydroponic systems handle watering differently:
- **DWC/Bubbler**: Roots are always submerged in aerated nutrient solution
- **Ebb and flow**: Flooding cycles (typically 2–4 times per day during lights-on)
- **Drip systems**: Continuous or periodic dripping to each plant
- **NFT**: Thin film of nutrient solution constantly flowing over roots
