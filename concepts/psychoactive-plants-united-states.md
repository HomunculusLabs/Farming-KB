# Psychoactive Plants Growing Wild in the United States

Many potent hallucinogenic and dissociative agents occur naturally in plants and fungi growing wild or cultivated in gardens throughout the United States. These organisms contain a range of psychoactive compounds including tryptamines, phenethylamines, diterpenes, ergolines, anticholinergics, and ibotenic acid derivatives. This concept page catalogs the major naturally occurring psychoactive species found within the US, their active constituents, preparation methods, pharmacology, and associated risks.

## N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) Sources

DMT is an endogenous hallucinogen present in small amounts in the human brain. When smoked or injected, onset occurs within 15 seconds to 5 minutes, peaking rapidly and clearing over 20 to 30 minutes. Typical smoked doses are 40 to 50 mg. The effective intravenous dosage range is 0.1 to 0.4 mg/kg.

### Natural US Sources

- **Phalaris arundinacea, P. tuberosa, P. aquatica** (reed canary grass): Found throughout the US in fallow fields, sidewalk cracks, and lawns. DMT content is highly variable. High-percentage strains are commercially available online. DMT can be extracted through simple juice extraction.

- **Desmanthus illinoensis** (prairie bundleflower): Wide distribution across the American prairies. Root bark contains approximately 0.34% DMT by dry weight.

- **Psychotria viridis**: Not native to the US but relevant as the traditional DMT source in Amazonian ayahuasca preparations, containing 0.1% to 0.66% DMT by dry weight.

### Ayahuasca and MAOI Sources

DMT is orally active only when monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes are inhibited. Amazonian ayahuasca combines DMT from Psychotria viridis with reversible type A MAOIs (harmaline, harmine, tetrahydroharmine) from Banisteriopsis caapi. US-native MAOI sources include:

- **Peganum harmala** (Syrian rue): Introduced to the western US, now found wild. Contains 2 to 4% MAOI by dry weight. The richest natural source of beta-carboline alkaloids.
- **Passiflora incarnata** (passionflower): Grows throughout the Southeast and much of the Midwest. Contains approximately 1% MAOI. Bears edible fruit and is used in herbal preparations for sedative properties.

Nontraditional ayahuasca-analogue brews from these American plants are documented online, though their pharmacology differs from traditional preparations.

## Psilocybin Mushrooms

Psilocybe cubensis and related species contain psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) and psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine). These are Schedule I substances, though mushroom spore prints remain legal in most states (except California). Psilocybin is metabolized in the gut to psilocin, the actual intoxicating compound.

### US Distribution

In the United States, Psilocybe species are coprophagous, growing on cow and horse manure in pastures from Florida through the southern Gulf States to Texas, in pasture and grain fields, and along the Pacific Northwest coastline from California into Canada. Psilocybe cubensis typically contains 10 to 12 mg psilocybin per gram dried mushroom.

### Intoxication Profile

Onset occurs within 30 to 60 minutes, peaking within 2 hours and diminishing over 3 to 4 hours. A threshold dose is approximately 40 micrograms per kg body weight. Typical ingestion is 1 to 2 grams of dried material, yielding 4 to 20 mg psilocybin. Cross-tolerance occurs between LSD and psilocybin, but less readily than between LSD and mescaline.

### Risk of Misidentification

Misidentification is the primary health risk. Poisonous mushroom species grow in similar habitats and can cause severe organ damage or death. Home cultivation eliminates this risk.

## Mescaline-Containing Cacti

### Peyote (Lophophora williamsii)

Peyote contains over 60 alkaloids, with mescaline (beta-3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) as the principal hallucinogen at 1.5% by dry weight. It grows in a narrow strip of desert along the Texas-Mexico border. Spineless and slow growing, the edible "button" (crown) is harvested while leaving the tuberous root intact for regeneration.

Peyote induces nausea and vomiting in novices. A potent intoxication requires approximately 3.75 mg/kg body weight, peaking at 2 to 4 hours and wearing off over 4 to 6 hours. Peyote is consumed as fresh buttons, dried buttons, powder, or tea.

### San Pedro and Peruvian Torch Cacti

Trichocereus pachanoi (San Pedro) and T. peruvianus (Peruvian torch) are common ornamental plants available from garden shops nationwide. Mescaline content varies widely: T. pachanoi contains 0.33% to 2.375% mescaline by dry weight; T. peruvianus contains 0% to 0.82%. These are typically boiled into a soup for ingestion, producing more nausea than peyote due to the large volume of plant material.

## Salvia divinorum

Salvia divinorum (Lamiaceae family) contains the neoclerodane diterpene salvinorin-A, notable as the first naturally occurring nonnitrogenous kappa-opioid receptor agonist. Unlike most hallucinogens that act at the 5-HT2A serotonin receptor, salvinorin-A has no serotonergic activity.

### Intoxication

When smoked, salvinorin-A is psychoactive for approximately 15 minutes at doses of 200 to 500 micrograms. Oral absorption (chewing fresh leaves or drinking leaf juice, the traditional Mazatec method) produces less intense intoxication lasting up to 1 hour. Concentrated extracts are commercially available online and through smoke shops.

### Legal Status and Risk

The DEA had not scheduled Salvia divinorum at the time of this review but was monitoring it as an emerging drug of abuse. It lacks reinforcing properties, and harm typically results from inadequate preparation or dangerous settings rather than pharmacological toxicity. Coleus blumei and C. pumilus are related Lamiaceae sometimes used by Mazatec Indians, but only Salvia divinorum has been confirmed to contain salvinorin-A.

## Lysergic Acid Amide (LSA) Sources

LSA (ergine) is a close analogue of LSD found naturally in several plant seeds:

- **Argyreia nervosa** (Hawaiian baby woodrose): Contains 0.14% LSA by dry seed weight. Five to 10 seeds yield an average dose (2 to 5 mg LSA), producing 4 to 8 hours of intoxication.
- **Ipomoea violacea** (morning glory): Contains 0.02% LSA by dry seed weight. A dose requires 150 to 200 seeds (3 to 6 grams). Historically used in Central American shamanic ceremonies.
- **Stipa robusta** (sleepygrass): Found in the southwestern US. Contains the highest LSA concentrations of any known plant, though it has no history of shamanic use.

Commercial morning glory seeds are typically coated with emetic poisons to discourage ingestion.

## Anticholinergic Plants

Atropine and scopolamine are competitive antagonists of muscarinic cholinergic receptors and central nervous system depressants.

### US Species

- **Datura stramonium** (Jimson weed): Grows wild throughout the US except Wyoming and Alaska
- **Atropa belladonna** (deadly nightshade): Introduced as an ornamental
- **Hyoscyamus niger** (henbane): Contains scopolamine without atropine
- **Mandragora officinarum** (Mandrake): Introduced ornamental

### Intoxication Profile

Overdose induces toxic delirium with pronounced anterograde amnesia, confusion, dissociation, hallucinations, delusions, giddy affect, impaired coordination, blurry vision with pupillary dilation, and dry mucous membranes. Some symptoms (headache, pupillary dilation) may persist for days or weeks. Severe overdose can be lethal with fever, tachycardia, and arrhythmia. The frightening adverse profile generally precludes recreational use.

## Amanita Muscaria and Amanita Pantherina

### Active Constituents

These mushrooms contain ibotenic acid (an excitatory amino acid at glutamate receptors, inducing agitated toxic delirium), muscimol (a GABA receptor agonist with mild sedative effects), and muscarine (minimal in A. muscaria despite the name). Ibotenic acid apparently decarboxylates to muscimol during drying.

### Intoxication

Reactions begin within 30 minutes, peaking over 2 to 3 hours. Described as similar to alcohol intoxication but capable of inducing powerful hallucinations, slurred speech, seizures, nausea, vomiting, transient deep sleep or coma, and persistent headaches. Reactions are estimated at 6 mg for muscimol and 30 to 60 mg for ibotenic acid.

### Unique Feature

Ibotenic acid is mostly excreted unmetabolized into urine, which has been consumed traditionally to achieve intoxication. This is unique to Amanita species.

### Misidentification Risk

Amanita phalloides (death cap) grows in close proximity and is extremely lethal. This misidentification risk is the primary danger.

## Sacramental Religious Use

Several religions use these plants as sacraments within the United States:

- **Native American Church (NAC)**: Approximately 300,000 members with federally protected sacramental peyote use. Exclusive to Native Americans with recognized tribal membership.
- **Santo Daime** and **Uniao do Vegetal (UDV)**: Brazilian-origin syncretic religions using ayahuasca as sacrament. Open to all ethnic backgrounds. The UDV has achieved preliminary federal court injunctions barring DEA enforcement, creating a novel legal situation regarding religious use of Schedule I DMT.
