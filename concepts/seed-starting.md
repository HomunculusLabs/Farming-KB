# Seed Starting

Comprehensive guide to starting seeds indoors, direct sowing, soil blocking, and troubleshooting. Proper seed starting is the foundation of a productive homestead garden. See [[seed-saving]] for producing your own seed stock and [[seasonal-planning]] for planting dates by crop and zone.

## Indoor Seed Starting Setup

### Grow Lights
| Type | Cost | Lifespan | Pros | Cons |
|------|------|----------|------|------|
| **LED shop lights** (T5/T8, 4000-5000K) | $30-60 | 50,000 hrs | Low heat, efficient, inexpensive | Less intensity per fixture |
| **LED quantum boards** | $100-300 | 50,000+ hrs | High output, full spectrum, dimmable | Higher upfront cost |
| **T5 fluorescent** | $40-80 | 20,000 hrs | Proven, affordable | Less efficient, more heat, bulb replacement |
| **T8 fluorescent** | $20-40 | 15,000-30,000 hrs | Cheapest option | Lower light intensity, heat |

**Recommendation**: LED shop lights (4ft, 2-4 fixtures) are the best value. Hang on chains for adjustable height.

### Light Guidelines
- **Height**: 2-4 inches above seedlings; adjust as they grow
- **Duration**: 14-16 hours on, 8-10 hours off (use outlet timer, $5-10)
- **Intensity goal**: 2000-3000 foot-candles at leaf level for seedlings
- **If seedlings are leggy**: light is too far away — lower it

### Heat Mats
- Seeds need specific soil temps for germination (see crop table below)
- Heat mat with thermostat: $25-40
- Use a temperature probe in the soil, not on the mat surface
- Remove mat once seeds germinate (most seedlings grow cooler)
- **Exception**: peppers, eggplant benefit from continued bottom heat (70-75F)

### Trays and Cell Sizes
| Cell Size | Volume | Best For |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| **128-cell** | 0.5 oz | Small seeds: lettuce, herbs, onions |
| **72-cell** | 1.5 oz | Most vegetables: tomatoes, peppers, brassicas |
| **50-cell** | 2.5 oz | Larger seedlings: cucurbits, flowers |
| **32-cell** | 4 oz | Fast growers or plants going out quickly |
| **4-inch pots** | 16 oz | Potting up tomatoes, peppers before transplant |

**Tip**: Start in 72-cell, pot up to 4-inch if indoor time exceeds 6-8 weeks.

### Seed Starting Mix Recipe
- 1 part peat moss or coconut coir
- 1 part perlite or vermiculite
- 1 part screened compost (use finished, sieved compost)
- Optional: 1 cup worm castings per 5 gallons mix for biology
- **Do not add fertilizer** to starting mix — seedlings feed from seed reserves for first 2-3 weeks

## Direct Sowing vs Transplanting

| Crop | Method | When to Start (Zone 7-8) | Notes |
|------|--------|--------------------------|-------|
| Peas, beans, corn | Direct sow | After last frost (mid-Apr) | Beans need 60F+ soil; corn 50F+ |
| Root crops (carrot, beet, radish, turnip) | Direct sow | Mid-Mar to Aug | Transplanting damages taproots |
| Lettuce, spinach, arugula | Either | Late Feb (indoor) / Mar-Apr (direct) | Direct sow every 2 weeks for succession |
| Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant | Transplant | Indoors: mid-Feb to Mar | Need 6-8 weeks before transplant |
| Brassicas (broccoli, cabbage, kale) | Either | Indoors: Feb-Mar / Direct: Mar-Apr | Transplants get head start on pests |
| Cucurbits (cucumber, squash, melon) | Either | Indoors: Apr / Direct: May | Sensitive to root disturbance — use pots, not cells |
| Onions, leeks | Transplant | Indoors: Jan-Feb | Need 8-10 weeks; day-length sensitive |

## Hardening Off

Seedlings grown indoors must be gradually acclimated to outdoor conditions over 7-10 days:
1. **Days 1-3**: Outside in shade, 1-2 hours, sheltered from wind
2. **Days 4-6**: Outside in partial sun, 3-4 hours, light wind OK
3. **Days 7-10**: Full sun, all day, overnight if no frost
4. **Transplant**: After overnight hardening on last day

Never skip this — direct sun or wind will burn/kill tender indoor seedlings.

## Soil Blocking

Soil blocks eliminate plastic trays and air-prune roots naturally:

### Mix Recipe (Eliot Coleman method)
- 3 parts peat moss or coconut coir
- 1 part perlite
- 1 part compost (screened fine)
- 1 handful lime per bucket (if using peat)
- 1 handful organic fertilizer blend (blood meal, bone meal, greensand mix)

### Blocker Sizes
- **Mini (3/4 inch)**: For germination, then pot on to 2-inch
- **Micro (1.5 inch)**: For lettuce, herbs, onions — direct transplant
- **2-inch**: Standard size for most vegetables
- **4-inch**: Final size before transplant

### Advantages
- No plastic trays needed
- Air-pruning prevents root circling
- Faster transplanting — just pop block into hole
- Stronger root systems
- Lower cost over time ($20-40 blocker lasts years)

## Pricking Out and Potting Up

- **Pricking out**: Moving seedlings from communal flat to individual cells when first true leaves appear (not cotyledons)
- **Potting up**: Moving from small cell to larger pot when roots fill the cell or before the plant gets root-bound
- Handle by cotyledon leaves, not stem (stems bruise easily)
- Water in well after transplanting; keep out of direct sun for 24 hours

| Beans (bush) | Every 2-3 weeks | May-Aug |
| Radish | Every 2 weeks | Mar-Oct |
| Cilantro | Every 3 weeks | Mar-Jun, Sep-Oct |
| Beets, carrots | Every 3 weeks | Mar-Jul |
| Sweet corn | Every 2 weeks | May-Jul |

## Fall Seed Starting and Winter Sowing

- **Fall crops**: Start brassicas (broccoli, cabbage, kale) indoors mid-Jul to early Aug; transplant Aug
- **Winter sowing in milk jugs**: Cut jugs in half (leave hinge), fill with damp potting mix, sow seeds, tape shut, set outside in Jan-Feb. Mini-greenhouse effect. Works for: kale, collards, lettuce, spinach, poppies, calendula, parsley
- **Garlic and shallots**: Plant cloves Oct-Nov for summer harvest

## Stratification and Scarification

Some seeds need special treatment to break dormancy:
- **Cold stratification**: Store moist seeds at 35-40F for 30-90 days (milkweed, echinacea, lavender, many perennials). Refrigerator in damp paper towel in bag.
- **Warm stratification**: 70-85F for similar periods (some native seeds).
- **Scarification**: Nick seed coat with sandpaper, nail file, or soak in hot water 12-24 hours (morning glory, sweet pea, locust, okra). Breaks physical dormancy.
- **Fire treatment**: Some seeds need smoke or fire exposure (certain natives).

## Seedling Problems

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| **Damping off** (seedlings flop and die at soil line) | Fungal disease from overwatering, poor air circulation | Use sterile mix, water from below, increase airflow, thin seedlings, chamomile tea drench |
| **Leggy** (tall, weak, stretched) | Insufficient light | Lower lights to 2-3 inches, increase duration to 16h, add fan for stem strength |
| **Curling leaves** | Temperature stress, over-fertilizing, aphids | Check temp (65-75F day, 55-65F night), reduce fertilizer, inspect for pests |
| **Yellow leaves** | Overwatering, nutrient deficiency, transplant shock | Let soil dry between waterings; fertilize after true leaves appear |
| **Wilting despite wet soil** | Root rot from overwatering | Improve drainage, reduce watering, repot if severe |

## Equipment Sourcing and Cost Tips

- **Seed trays**: Amazon or Johnny's Seeds; reusable for 5+ seasons with cleaning
- **Lights**: LED shop lights from hardware store — cheapest per watt
- **Heat mat**: Amazon; get one with thermostat
- **Mix**: Make your own (see recipe above) — 5x cheaper than bagged
- **Seeds**: [[seed-saving]] eliminates cost; buy from Johnny's, Territorial, or Fedco for reliability
- **Fan**: Small USB fan improves airflow and strengthens stems ($10-15)
- **Timer**: Basic outlet timer ($5-10)
- **Total startup**: $100-150 for lights, mats, trays, mix ingredients
