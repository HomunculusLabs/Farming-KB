# Permaculture Energy Descent Planning

Energy descent planning is David Holmgren's framework for preparing for a future of declining fossil fuel availability and the resulting economic and social transitions. Developed in "Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability," it represents a fundamental rethinking of how societies should plan for the post-carbon era.

## The Energy Descent Concept

Holmgren argues that the inevitable decline in fossil fuel availability (due to peak oil, climate change imperatives, or both) will force a progressive reduction in society's energy consumption. This "energy descent" is not framed as a catastrophe but as a process that can be managed through permaculture design. The key insight is that we should proactively design for lower energy futures rather than being caught unprepared.

Holmgren contrasts three broad scenarios:

1. **Techno-explosion**: Continued growth through new energy sources (which Holmgren considers unlikely)
2. **Techno-stability**: Maintaining current energy levels through technology (also considered unlikely at scale)
3. **Energy descent**: Progressive reduction in energy availability, requiring adaptive redesign of all systems

## Planning Principles for Energy Descent

### Design for Lower Energy Input

All systems should be designed to function with decreasing external energy inputs over time. This means:

- Reducing dependence on fossil-fuel-based inputs (synthetic fertilizers, mechanized equipment, long supply chains)
- Building systems that require less energy to maintain
- Favoring biological over mechanical solutions (animals instead of tractors, hand tools instead of powered equipment)
- Designing passive systems (passive solar heating, gravity-fed water, natural ventilation)

### Increase Self-Reliance

Energy descent planning emphasizes building local capacity:

- Food production at household and community level
- Local energy generation (solar, wind, micro-hydro, biomass)
- Water harvesting and conservation
- Local manufacturing and repair skills
- Community networks for sharing resources and knowledge

### Build Redilience Through Diversity

Resilient systems have many redundant pathways and fail-safes:

- Diverse food sources (multiple crops, multiple growing methods)
- Multiple water sources and storage methods
- Varied income streams and economic exchanges
- Strong social networks and community connections
- Skills and knowledge distributed widely, not concentrated in specialists

### Use Renewable Resources Preferentially

Prioritize resources that can be sustained indefinitely:

- Timber and bamboo instead of steel and concrete
- Biological fertility instead of synthetic fertilizers
- Human and animal labor instead of fossil-fueled machines
- Local materials instead of transported goods

## The Retrofit Approach

Rather than building new systems from scratch, Holmgren emphasizes retrofitting existing settlements and infrastructure:

- Retrofitting houses for passive solar design and energy efficiency
- Converting ornamental landscapes to food production
- Adapting suburban lots for intensive food growing, water harvesting, and animal husbandry
- Reclaiming waste streams (organic waste, greywater, stormwater) as productive resources
- Strengthening local economies through community enterprise and local currencies

## The Importance of Bottom-Up Action

Holmgren stresses that energy descent planning works best at the household and community scale, where people have direct control over design decisions. National and global policy is important, but the most effective adaptation happens through:

- Individual household design and lifestyle changes
- Neighborhood-level cooperation (tool sharing, bulk buying, community gardens)
- Local government initiatives (zoning reform, farmers' markets, local energy programs)
- Bioregional organization (watershed management, regional food systems)

## Permaculture as the Design Framework

Permaculture provides the design methodology for energy descent planning because it:

- Starts with ethics (earth care, people care, fair share) that align with descent values
- Provides design principles applicable at any scale
- Emphasizes observation of existing conditions and natural patterns
- Integrates many disciplines (agriculture, building, water, energy, community)
- Has been tested in practice for decades across diverse climates and cultures

## The Stepped Descent Pathway

Holmgren suggests that energy descent will not be a sudden collapse but a stepped process of change, with periods of stability punctuated by crises that force further adaptation. Permaculture designers should plan for this reality:

- Design systems that can function at multiple energy levels
- Build in flexibility to adapt to changing conditions
- Prioritize investments that provide value regardless of energy availability
- Focus on knowledge and skills that become more valuable as energy declines
