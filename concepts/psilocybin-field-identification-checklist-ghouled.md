# Psilocybin Mushroom Field Identification Checklist

The [[field-guide-psilocybin-mushroom-hunting-methodology]] by F.C. Ghouled (1972) provides a structured identification sheet
for field mycologists. The checklist was designed for novice foragers to
systematically evaluate specimens against known psilocybin-containing species.
This paper-based system represented a practical method for distinguishing
psychoactive fungi from harmless lookalikes and toxic species.

## The Ghouled Identification System

Ghouled's checklist is divided into ten fields, each capturing a critical
dimension of mushroom morphology and ecology. The system was intended to be
reproduced by hand into a field notebook. The structured format enforces a
disciplined observation process, reducing the likelihood of misidentification
from casual or incomplete visual inspection.

### Field 1: Age and Condition

The first field requires the collector to assess the developmental stage.
Psilocybin mushrooms undergo dramatic morphological changes throughout their
brief life cycle. The [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]]
transitions from a conical cap to a broadly convex or bowl-shaped form
within 24 to 48 hours after rain. Coloration shifts from near-white with a
golden center to light brown. Recording specimen age is essential because
many diagnostic features, including gill coloration and veil presence, are
stage-dependent.

### Field 2: Bluing Reaction

The second and arguably most critical field asks whether the specimen turns
blue when damaged. The [[stamets-psi-bluing-reaction-psilocybin-indicator]] is
caused by the oxidation of psilocybin and psilocin, the primary psychoactive
alkaloids, when cell tissue is disrupted. Ghouled emphasizes that the
inside flesh of the broken stem typically yields the fastest and most
reliable bluing response. The reaction is considered a primary screening
characteristic, though it is not exclusive to psilocybin-containing species.
The bluing is best observed within seconds to minutes of bruising and
intensifies over the following hours as oxidation progresses.

### Field 3: Cap (Pileus) Characteristics

The cap examination requires four sub-observations:

**Coloration:** P. cubensis displays a wide color range from near-white
with a gold center to light brown. [[panaeolus-subbalteatus]] shows light
cinnamon that is uniform in young specimens, with a distinctive ring at the
center as it matures. [[psilocybe-caerulescens-entity]] ranges from deep green to
black fading to cinnamon or rust.

**Shape and size:** P. cubensis caps range from one-half to four inches,
conical when young, becoming flat with uplifted edges. P. subbalteatus
retains a bluntly conical shape with incurved margin. P. caerulescens
ranges from one to over three inches wide.

The margin (edge) of the cap is diagnostically important. P. subbalteatus
characteristically retains its incurved margin even at maturity. P.
caerulescens exhibits a downward-curving margin with an irregular color
boundary between center and edge.

**Surface texture:** Fresh P. cubensis has a sticky protective film
(pellicle), and young P. subbalteatus caps show white flecks. Cap
translucence when waterlogged is useful as dark gill lines become visible.

### Field 4: Gill (Lamellae) Characteristics

Gill examination provides three sub-fields:

**Coloration:** Gills start light brown, transitioning through purple to
deep black at maturity. P. caerulescens gills begin as light cinnamon and
become dark brown to black with lighter-colored edges.

**Shape and spacing:** P. subbalteatus has very closely spaced gills, while
P. cubensis gills are described as "rather closely spaced."

**Attachment:** Gills may be attached to the stem in early stages but may
separate (secede) with age, a pattern useful for estimating specimen age.

### Field 5: Stem (Stipe) Characteristics

Stem observation includes:

**Coloration:** Stems are generally white or lighter than the cap. P.
subbalteatus may show a sepia or light cinnamon tint near the base.

**Shape and size:** P. cubensis stems range from 1.5 to 6 inches tall, up
to one-half inch thick, with a sometimes-thickened base (volva). P.
subbalteatus stems are more uniform at 1.5 to 4 inches tall, never
exceeding one-half inch thick.

**Base and hollowness:** A thickened base is inconsistent in P. cubensis.
All three Ghouled species have hollow stems, a critical diagnostic for
ruling out non-psilocybin lookalikes.

### Field 6: Flesh Characteristics

Flesh examination captures coloration, texture, and special color traits
such as bluing. P. cubensis flesh is white with little odor and a taste
resembling fresh grain. P. subbalteatus flesh is white to yellowish, similar
to commercial table mushrooms. P. caerulescens flesh is off-white with
occasional light brown tints and a strong grain-like odor.

### Fields 7 through 9: Environmental Context

Ghouled uniquely includes three environmental fields alongside morphology:

**Field 7: Last rain** establishes the moisture window critical for
fruiting. P. cubensis can complete its fruiting cycle within 24 hours.

**Field 8: High and low temperatures** over the past 24 hours or week.
Ghouled specifies 65 to 85 degrees Fahrenheit as the primary ecological
filter for psilocybin mushroom distribution.

**Field 9: Substrate** records what the specimen grows on. P. cubensis and
P. subbalteatus grow on manure of grain-fed animals, while P. caerulescens
occurs on stream and river banks.

### Field 10: Species Determination

The final field is for the forager's species determination, completed only
after all preceding observations have been recorded and compared against
known descriptions. Ghouled emphasizes conservative identification,
favoring "unknown" over an incorrect positive determination.
