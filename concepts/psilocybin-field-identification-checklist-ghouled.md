    10|# Psilocybin Mushroom Field Identification Checklist
    11|
    12|The [[field-guide-psilocybin-mushroom-hunting-methodology]] by F.C. Ghouled (1972) provides a structured identification sheet
    13|for field mycologists. The checklist was designed for novice foragers to
    14|systematically evaluate specimens against known psilocybin-containing species.
    15|This paper-based system represented a practical method for distinguishing
    16|psychoactive fungi from harmless lookalikes and toxic species.
    17|
    18|## The Ghouled Identification System
    19|
    20|Ghouled's checklist is divided into ten fields, each capturing a critical
    21|dimension of mushroom morphology and ecology. The system was intended to be
    22|reproduced by hand into a field notebook. The structured format enforces a
    23|disciplined observation process, reducing the likelihood of misidentification
    24|from casual or incomplete visual inspection.
    25|
    26|### Field 1: Age and Condition
    27|
    28|The first field requires the collector to assess the developmental stage.
    29|Psilocybin mushrooms undergo dramatic morphological changes throughout their
    30|brief life cycle. The [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]]
    31|transitions from a conical cap to a broadly convex or bowl-shaped form
    32|within 24 to 48 hours after rain. Coloration shifts from near-white with a
    33|golden center to light brown. Recording specimen age is essential because
    34|many diagnostic features, including gill coloration and veil presence, are
    35|stage-dependent.
    36|
    37|### Field 2: Bluing Reaction
    38|
    39|The second and arguably most critical field asks whether the specimen turns
    40|blue when damaged. The [[stamets-psi-bluing-reaction-psilocybin-indicator]] is
    41|caused by the oxidation of psilocybin and psilocin, the primary psychoactive
    42|alkaloids, when cell tissue is disrupted. Ghouled emphasizes that the
    43|inside flesh of the broken stem typically yields the fastest and most
    44|reliable bluing response. The reaction is considered a primary screening
    45|characteristic, though it is not exclusive to psilocybin-containing species.
    46|The bluing is best observed within seconds to minutes of bruising and
    47|intensifies over the following hours as oxidation progresses.
    48|
    49|### Field 3: Cap (Pileus) Characteristics
    50|
    51|The cap examination requires four sub-observations:
    52|
    53|**Coloration:** P. cubensis displays a wide color range from near-white
    54|with a gold center to light brown. [[panaeolus-subbalteatus]] shows light
    55|cinnamon that is uniform in young specimens, with a distinctive ring at the
    56|center as it matures. [[psilocybe-caerulescens]] ranges from deep green to
    57|black fading to cinnamon or rust.
    58|
    59|**Shape and size:** P. cubensis caps range from one-half to four inches,
    60|conical when young, becoming flat with uplifted edges. P. subbalteatus
    61|retains a bluntly conical shape with incurved margin. P. caerulescens
    62|ranges from one to over three inches wide.
    63|
    64|The margin (edge) of the cap is diagnostically important. P. subbalteatus
    65|characteristically retains its incurved margin even at maturity. P.
    66|caerulescens exhibits a downward-curving margin with an irregular color
    67|boundary between center and edge.
    68|
    69|**Surface texture:** Fresh P. cubensis has a sticky protective film
    70|(pellicle), and young P. subbalteatus caps show white flecks. Cap
    71|translucence when waterlogged is useful as dark gill lines become visible.
    72|
    73|### Field 4: Gill (Lamellae) Characteristics
    74|
    75|Gill examination provides three sub-fields:
    76|
    77|**Coloration:** Gills start light brown, transitioning through purple to
    78|deep black at maturity. P. caerulescens gills begin as light cinnamon and
    79|become dark brown to black with lighter-colored edges.
    80|
    81|**Shape and spacing:** P. subbalteatus has very closely spaced gills, while
    82|P. cubensis gills are described as "rather closely spaced."
    83|
    84|**Attachment:** Gills may be attached to the stem in early stages but may
    85|separate (secede) with age, a pattern useful for estimating specimen age.
    86|
    87|### Field 5: Stem (Stipe) Characteristics
    88|
    89|Stem observation includes:
    90|
    91|**Coloration:** Stems are generally white or lighter than the cap. P.
    92|subbalteatus may show a sepia or light cinnamon tint near the base.
    93|
    94|**Shape and size:** P. cubensis stems range from 1.5 to 6 inches tall, up
    95|to one-half inch thick, with a sometimes-thickened base (volva). P.
    96|subbalteatus stems are more uniform at 1.5 to 4 inches tall, never
    97|exceeding one-half inch thick.
    98|
    99|**Base and hollowness:** A thickened base is inconsistent in P. cubensis.
   100|All three Ghouled species have hollow stems, a critical diagnostic for
   101|ruling out non-psilocybin lookalikes.
   102|
   103|### Field 6: Flesh Characteristics
   104|
   105|Flesh examination captures coloration, texture, and special color traits
   106|such as bluing. P. cubensis flesh is white with little odor and a taste
   107|resembling fresh grain. P. subbalteatus flesh is white to yellowish, similar
   108|to commercial table mushrooms. P. caerulescens flesh is off-white with
   109|occasional light brown tints and a strong grain-like odor.
   110|
   111|### Fields 7 through 9: Environmental Context
   112|
   113|Ghouled uniquely includes three environmental fields alongside morphology:
   114|
   115|**Field 7: Last rain** establishes the moisture window critical for
   116|fruiting. P. cubensis can complete its fruiting cycle within 24 hours.
   117|
   118|**Field 8: High and low temperatures** over the past 24 hours or week.
   119|Ghouled specifies 65 to 85 degrees Fahrenheit as the primary ecological
   120|filter for psilocybin mushroom distribution.
   121|
   122|**Field 9: Substrate** records what the specimen grows on. P. cubensis and
   123|P. subbalteatus grow on manure of grain-fed animals, while P. caerulescens
   124|occurs on stream and river banks.
   125|
   126|### Field 10: Species Determination
   127|
   128|The final field is for the forager's species determination, completed only
   129|after all preceding observations have been recorded and compared against
   130|known descriptions. Ghouled emphasizes conservative identification,
   131|favoring "unknown" over an incorrect positive determination.
   132|
   133|## Historical Significance
   134|
   135|The Ghouled identification checklist represents one of the earliest
   136|standardized field protocols for psilocybin [[mushroom-identification]] in
   137|North America. Its emphasis on systematic observation, environmental
   138|context, and conservative determination established principles that
   139|inform modern [[field-guide-psilocybin-mushroom-species-identification]] practices. The inclusion of environmental fields
   140|(rainfall, temperature, substrate) alongside morphology anticipated the
   141|ecological approach to mycology that became standard in later decades.
   142|
   143|## Safety Considerations
   144|
   145|Ghouled's checklist implicitly reinforces that no single characteristic,
   146|including the bluing reaction, is sufficient for positive identification.
   147|The multi-field approach forces holistic evaluation, reducing confusion
   148|with toxic lookalikes. The checklist is a field tool, not a substitute for
   149|expert verification.
