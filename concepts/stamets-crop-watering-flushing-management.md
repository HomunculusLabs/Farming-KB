## Crop Watering and Flushing Management

## Overview

Once pinheads have matured into mushrooms, the crop enters the cropping phase
where careful management of watering, temperature, humidity, and [[growing-gourmet-growing-room-ventilation-air-exchange]]
determines yield quality and total harvest. This phase encompasses multiple
flushes of mushrooms and requires the cultivator to balance competing
environmental demands throughout the cropping cycle.

High temperatures shorten cropping by promoting rapid flushes but risk heat

Commercial [[agaricus]] growers commonly lower air temperature by 2 degrees F,

then run hotter to speed the crop to completion.

## Flushing Pattern

The [[mushroom-crop-management-flush-harvesting]] grows in cycles called flushes or "breaks." Depending on the
species, these flushes normally come in seven to ten day intervals with each
successive flush bearing fewer mushrooms. The manner of flushing is determined
during the pin initiation period.

Even pinning sets up a uniform flushing pattern. Uneven flushing creates
difficult situations for watering and [[pf-tek-mushroom-growth-timeline]] to a halt, so the goal is to maximize yields
in the early flushes.

## Watering Techniques

Maturing mushrooms have water requirements that must be met for maximum yields.
Mushrooms grown on uncased substrates draw moisture from the substrate, while
cased mushrooms draw equally from both substrate and casing. Uncased substrates
require 90-95% relative humidity plus periodic misting.

### Building Casing Moisture

Other than light mistings, any substantial waterings before the button stage can
damage pins. Once mushrooms reach button size, it is time to begin building
casing moisture back to the peak reached at pre-pinning. This is accomplished
by daily light to moderate waterings with a fine misting nozzle.

Commercial growers have traditionally used rose-nozzles but many now use
nozzles with finer sprays and variable volume outputs. High water pressures and
close nozzle proximity to the casing should be avoided.

### Preventing Panning

The goal is to keep the [[abort-dead-pinhead-cleanup-casing-surface-hygiene]] open and porous throughout the cropping
cycle. Putting on too much water at once is the most common cause of panning.
By watering 2-4 times per day rather than just once, the casing can slowly
absorb water without damage to the surface.

### Between Flushes

After harvest, keep casing moist with light mistings until the next flush
reaches button stage. Later flushes require less water. Never let the casing
dry out, as mushrooms pulled from dry casing create gaps exposing substrate to
contaminants. Fill exposed holes with moist casing material.
