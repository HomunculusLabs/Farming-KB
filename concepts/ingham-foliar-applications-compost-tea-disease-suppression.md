# Foliar Application of Compost Tea — Disease Suppression and Pest Deterrence

## Overview

Foliar application of actively aerated compost tea (AACT) introduces [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]]. Modern [[cannabis-cultivar-microbial-community-effects]] helps the plant's natural stomatal function and nutrient absorption.

## Disease Suppression Mechanisms

### 1. Competitive Exclusion (Resource Competition)

Compost tea organisms consume the foods and occupy the ecological niches that disease-causing organisms would otherwise use. The tea microorganisms use the exudates, leaf surface sugars, and moisture that pathogens depend on for germination and growth. By pre-empting resource availability, beneficial organisms prevent pathogen establishment.

### 2. Physical Site Occupation

Compost tea organisms occupy all the available space on the leaves. If every infection site is already colonized by beneficial organisms, disease-causing organisms have no space to grow. This is particularly effective against:

- **Black spot** (*Diplocarpon rosae*): The causal fungus has weak enzymatic ability and cannot outcompete other organisms for leaf surface space. Initial infection appears as vague gray hyphal growth; black spores form as the colony matures.
- **Fusarium** (snow mold): Preventable with bacterial tea alone
- **Anthracnose on foliage**: Preventable with bacterial tea
- **Anthracnose on crowns/roots**: Requires full bacterial plus fungal tea
- **Rhizoctonia**: Some species prevented by bacterial tea; others require full bacterial plus fungal tea

### 3. Predation

[[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]] of leaf-surface microorganisms elevates CO₂ levels in the leaf boundary layer. Many insect pests use CO₂ gradients and specific atmospheric cues to locate host plants. Elevated CO₂ may provide the wrong signal for pest attraction.

### 3. Consumption of Stress Signaling Compounds

Stressed plants emit alcohols and other volatile organic compounds that attract insect pests. Tea organisms consume these alcohols and other stress signals, removing the chemical attractants. When the plant is no longer broadcasting stress signals, pests are less likely to find and attack it.

### 4. Plant Health Restoration

Tea organisms cause the plant to become less stressed by improving nutrient availability and uptake. A healthier plant is inherently less susceptible to insect attack. Insects preferentially target stressed plants, so restoring plant health removes the conditions that attract pests.

## Application Guidelines

### When to Apply

- Apply when leaf surfaces are likely to remain moist for at least 2-4 hours after application, allowing organisms to establish
- Early morning or late evening applications are preferred to avoid UV damage to microorganisms
- Do not apply in direct sunlight or during hot midday conditions

### Coverage Requirements

- Complete coverage of leaf surfaces is essential—the protection is only as good as the coverage
- Both upper and lower leaf surfaces should be treated
- Spray to the point of runoff for thorough coverage

### Frequency

- Typical interval: every 7-14 days during growing season
- More frequent applications may be needed during high disease pressure periods
- Stop applications if the plant does not need foliar feeding or if pest pressure is absent

### Integration with Soil Applications

- Foliar and soil applications work synergistically
- Soil biology supports plant health from below; foliar biology protects from above
- Both soil and leaf surface biology should be established and maintained simultaneously
- Stop killing soil and leaf surface biology with synthetic chemicals
