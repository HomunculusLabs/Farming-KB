# Co-Culture of Plant Cells with Piriformospora indica for Secondary Metabolite Production

Co-culture systems combining plant cell suspension cultures with the axenically cultivable endophytic fungus Piriformospora indica represent a biotechnological platform for enhanced production of plant-derived [[bloomfield-fungal-secondary-metabolites]]. P. indica, isolated from the soil of Rajasthan, India, mimics the capabilities of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi while being culturable on synthetic media, making it a practical partner for in vitro co-culture systems.

## Piriformospora indica Characteristics

Piriformospora indica (order Sebacinales) is an endophytic fungus that colonizes roots of a wide range of plant species, forming mutualistic associations that benefit both partners. Unlike arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, P. indica can be grown axenically on synthetic media including MYPG medium (malt extract 3 g/L, yeast extract 3 g/L, peptone 5 g/L, glucose 10 g/L, pH 6.2) or Hill and Kafer's medium. The fungus promotes plant growth, enhances secondary metabolite production, and increases stress tolerance in diverse medicinal and economically important plants.

The axenic culturability of P. indica distinguishes it from obligate biotrophs like arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, which cannot be grown independently of a host plant. This property makes P. indica far more practical for laboratory co-culture experiments and potential industrial scale-up. The fungus has been shown to colonize over 200 plant species across diverse taxonomic groups.

## Co-Culture Rationale

Many plant-derived secondary metabolites of pharmaceutical importance are produced in limited quantities in whole plants and cell cultures. The interaction between P. indica and plant cells can enhance secondary metabolite production through elicitation of plant defense pathways and metabolic priming. Co-culture systems offer controlled conditions for studying the molecular basis of metabolite enhancement while providing a scalable platform for commercial production.

The fungal elicitor effect is particularly valuable because many commercially important metabolites — such as podophyllotoxin, bacosides, azadirachtin, and withanolides — are produced at low levels in conventional plant cell cultures. Elicitation through co-culture offers a sustainable alternative to wild harvesting of medicinal plants, many of which are endangered or slow-growing.

## Plant Cell Culture Development

Plant cell suspension cultures are initiated by germinating surface-sterilized seeds (e.g., Linum album), excising stem portions, and inducing callus on MS medium supplemented with NAA (0.4 mg/L). Friable callus is transferred to liquid MS medium with NAA for suspension culture development on gyratory shakers at 125 rpm, 25 degrees C, with 16-hour light cycles. Cells are subcultured every 12 days to maintain exponential growth.

The quality of the callus is critical — only friable, fast-growing callus produces uniform suspension cultures suitable for co-culture. Cell viability is assessed before fungal inoculation using Evans blue staining or fluorescein diacetate methodology.

## Fungal Culture Development

P. indica is maintained on solid MYPG medium and subcultured monthly. For co-culture, fungal inoculum is prepared by transferring 5-day-old mycelial plugs to Hill and Kafer's liquid medium and incubating at 200 rpm, 30 degrees C for 5 days. The fungal biomass is collected by centrifugation at 5,000 rpm for 10 minutes and resuspended in fresh medium at the desired concentration for inoculation into plant cell cultures.

## Podophyllotoxin Production

Podophyllotoxin is a cytotoxic aryltetralin lignan used for synthesis of anticancer drugs (etoposide, teniposide). Conventionally extracted from Podophyllum species, supply is limited by the endangered status of P. hexandrum and difficulties in chemical synthesis. Cell cultures of Linum album produce podophyllotoxin with higher productivity than Podophyllum cultures. Co-culture with P. indica significantly enhances podophyllotoxin production compared to plant cell monocultures.

Enhancement ratios of 2-5 fold over control monocultures have been reported in optimized systems, making co-culture a commercially attractive approach for podophyllotoxin supply.

## Enhancement Mechanisms

P. indica enhances secondary metabolite production through multiple mechanisms. The fungus acts as a biotic elicitor, triggering plant defense responses that include activation of the phenylpropanoid pathway. The key enzyme phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL), which catalyzes the rate-limiting step of lignan biosynthesis, shows increased activity in co-cultures. The fungus may also modify the metabolic flux of carbon and nitrogen in plant cells, redirecting resources toward secondary metabolite biosynthesis.

Additional mechanisms include oxidative burst responses, reactive oxygen species signaling, and upregulation of stress-responsive transcription factors in the plant cells. These signaling cascades converge on the secondary metabolite biosynthetic pathways, amplifying production.

## Optimization Parameters

Optimization of co-culture systems requires attention to several parameters. The ratio of fungal to plant cell biomass affects the magnitude and timing of elicitation. The timing of fungal inoculation relative to plant cell growth phase influences the response; inoculation at late exponential or early stationary phase generally produces maximum metabolite accumulation. Culture duration after inoculation must be optimized for each specific metabolite. Medium composition (carbon source, nitrogen source, phosphate concentration) affects both plant cell growth and fungal elicitor production.
