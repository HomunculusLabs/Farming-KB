## Arid Zone Species Selection and Forage Systems

Selecting the right species for arid and semi-arid environments is critical to establishing self-sufficient settlements. Mollison provides comprehensive species lists based on field trials at Aboriginal settlements in central Australia, emphasizing multi-function plants that serve forage, food, shelter, and soil-building roles simultaneously.

## Established Tree Crop Species

### Mulberry (Morus spp.)
- Only red or black varieties established; white mulberry could be added
- First-class poultry forage
- High vitamin C content
- Fruit can be ground to flour
- Hardy; resists white ant and fire

### Carob (Ceratonia siliqua)
- Established but attacked by termites; needs careful site selection
- Needs 5% male trees in stands
- High nutrient value for forage and people
- Beans are 68% sugars (valuable for fuel production)

### Citrus spp.
- Oranges and lemons established
- Needed: limes, grapefruit, other citrus species
- High vitamin C value
- May need Tagetes understorey to control eel-worm

### Black Locust (Robinia pseudoacacia)
- Very durable timber
- Useful poultry seed
- Spiny leaves toxic to stock in large quantities (can be advantageous)
- Non-inflammable bark and foliage
- Hardy

### Honey Locust (Gleditsia triacanthos)
- Durable timber
- Leaves not poisonous
- Edible beans
- Useful for pole timbers

### Olive (Olea europaea)
- Useful oil crop and shade tree
- Easily propagated from cuttings
- Good [[free-range-poultry-forage-design-mollison]] crop
- Many species possible

### Fig (Ficus spp.)
- Needs more varieties for trials
- Possible hybrids with native figs
- Native fig (iii) used as fruit and graft stock

### Mango (Mangifera indica)
- Established at Mt. Isa
- Could be more widely grown in many areas

### Pomegranate
- Established as seedlings
- Should be a major summer fruit
- Pakistani species keep well into winter

### Grape Vine
- Many cultivars do well (26 varieties flourish)
- Grapes bear fruit from cuttings in 18 months in desert climate

### Stone and Nut Pines (Pinus, Araucaria)
- Several species including pinon of Mexico and other desert nut pines
- Some established; P. coulteri, P. pinaster, P. pinea, A. bidwillii

### Karreeboom (Rhus lancea)
- [[cannabis-strain-swazi-landrace-south-african-sativa-oner]] species
- Established in arid Australia
- Frost and termite resistant
- Fodder; small edible berries for poultry

### Jujube (Zizyphus jujuba)
- Established
- Needs wider trials as hedge

### Cacti
- Several selected fruiting species need wider trials
- Useful hedgerow and non-irrigated crop species available

### Other Species Needing Trials
- Pistachio (P. vera)
- [[holzer-sweet-chestnut-and-nut-trees]] (frosty areas, stream beds)
- Almond
- Plums (especially prunes and gages)
- Cashew Nut
- Paw Paw
- Cherries (near watercourses)
- Guava (true guava, feijoa, strawberry guava, ugni)
- Passionfruit (banana passionfruit stands frost, good poultry fodder)
- Stone or nut pines

## Legumes for Interplanting

| Species | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| **Centrosema pubescens** | Established; brings up moisture to topsoil |
| **Paulownia spp.** | Aids in providing surface moisture |
| **Leucaena** | For frost-free areas |
| **Tree Lucerne** (Chaemocytisus proliferus) | Fire and frost resistant; stock and poultry fodder |
| **Mulga** (Acacia aneura) | Native acacia; multi-purpose |
| **Acacia albida** (Africa) | "Wet deciduous" in monsoon; hardy; poultry fodder |
| **Mesquites** (Prosopis spp.) | Deep-rooted nutrient pumps |

## Hedgerow Species

- **[[coprosma-repens-entity]]** and other NZ species: Resist fire; poultry and stock fodder
- **Bamboo spp.**: Established; larger black and giant bamboo needed; very useful for structural needs
- **Cacti, Wormwood, Jujube**: Formidable barrier hedges

## Shade and Shelter Only

- **White Cedar** ([[melia-azedarach-entity]]): Fruits poisonous to pigs and poultry—use with care
- **Jacaranda** (J. mimosifolia)
- **Tamarind** (Tamarindus indicus): Marginal food use
- **Tamarisk** (Tamarix articulata): Also for mulch

## Useful Native Species

| Species | Uses |
|---------|------|
| **Kurrajong** (Brachychiton gregorii) | Nectar, pollen, fodder, bark fibre |
| **Bottletree** (Apophyllum anomalum) | Fodder, possible spice |
| **Ebony** (Bauhinia currnoi) | Leaves and pods: edible stock food |
| **Lime Bush** (Eremocitrus glauca) | Small preserving fruit; grafts |
| **Native Currant** (Carthanium latifolium) | Fruit and fodder; poultry |
| **Wild Oranges** (Capparis mitchelli, C. ambonata) | Fruit eaten by Aborigines |
| **Berrigan** (Eremophila longifolia) | Emu and pigeon food |
| **Emu Apple** (Owenia acidula) | Edible fruit; emu forage |

## Vegetable Categories for Arid Zones

### Perennials
Globe artichoke, asparagus, sweet potato, cassava (Manihot), comfrey, beans, herbs (chives, sage, thyme, marjoram, mints), many perennial onions not yet tried

### Biennials
Parsley, fennel, other umbelliferous species, celery

### Annuals
Sweet corn, tomato, melons, borage, lettuce, chard, spinach, sprouts, cabbage, peas, beans

### Tubers and Roots
Potato, sweet potato, turnip, carrot, onion, cassava, [[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]], oca, plus native wapiti and yala
