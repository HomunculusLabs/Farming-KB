Deep Water Culture (DWC), often called "the bubbler" in Greg Green's terminology, is a simple yet highly effective hydroponic growing method where plant roots are suspended directly in an oxygenated nutrient solution.

## How DWC Works

In a DWC system:
- Plant roots hang directly in a nutrient solution
- An air pump drives air stones that bubble oxygen through the water
- The oxygenated solution delivers nutrients directly to the root zone
- Net pots hold the plants suspended above the water line with roots growing down into the solution
- No growing medium is strictly required, though some growers use hydroton clay pebbles or rockwool cubes for support

## The Bubbler Setup (Green's Description)

Greg Green describes the basic bubbler as one of the simplest hydroponic systems:

### Components
- **Reservoir container**: A bucket, storage tote, or dedicated DWC reservoir (5 gallons minimum per plant)
- **Net pots**: Mesh pots (3–6 inch) that hold the plant while allowing roots to pass through
- **Air pump**: Aquarium-style air pump rated for the reservoir volume
- **Air stones**: Porous stones that create fine bubbles for oxygenation
- **Air tubing**: Connects pump to air stones
- **Growing medium (optional)**: Hydroton clay pebbles, rockwool cubes, or perlite to support the seedling in the net pot
- **Nutrient solution**: Hydroponic nutrients mixed to the correct EC/pH

### Assembly
1. Drill or cut holes in the reservoir lid to fit your net pots
2. Place air stones at the bottom of the reservoir
3. Connect air stones to the pump with tubing
4. Fill reservoir with nutrient solution (pH 5.5–6.0)
5. Place net pots in the lid holes
6. Start the air pump — the solution should bubble vigorously

## Advantages of DWC

- **Fast growth**: Direct access to water, oxygen, and nutrients accelerates growth rates compared to soil
- **Simplicity**: Fewer moving parts than other hydroponic systems (no drip emitters to clog, no timers for flood/drain)
- **Low maintenance**: Check and top off nutrient levels and pH periodically
- **Cost-effective**: Simple components, no expensive controllers or pumps beyond the air pump
- **Scalable**: Easy to build single-bucket systems or large multi-site setups

## Disadvantages

- **No buffer**: Problems with pH or nutrients manifest quickly — there is no soil to buffer changes
- **Power dependence**: If the air pump fails, roots can quickly suffocate. A backup power source is wise.
- **Temperature sensitivity**: Water temperature must be maintained at 65–70°F (18–21°C). Warm water holds less oxygen and promotes root rot (Pythium).
- **Cleanliness required**: Reservoirs must be kept clean to prevent algae and pathogens

## Nutrient Management

DWC requires careful attention to nutrients:

- Use hydroponic-specific nutrients (not soil nutrients)
- Start at half-strength for seedlings, gradually increase
- Monitor EC (electrical conductivity) and pH daily
- Target pH: 5.5–6.0 (lower than soil due to direct root exposure)
- Change the full reservoir every 1–2 weeks to prevent salt buildup and nutrient imbalance
- Top off with fresh water between changes to maintain volume

## Growing Stages in DWC

### Seedling/Clone
- Start plants in rockwool cubes or small net pots
- Lower water level so just the bottom of the net pot touches the solution
- Roots will grow down seeking moisture
- Use plain water or very mild nutrient solution (EC ~0.4–0.6)

### Vegetative
- Roots should be well-developed and reaching into the solution
- Increase nutrient strength gradually (EC ~1.0–1.5)
- 18/6 or 24/0 light schedule
- See [[cannabis-vegetative-stage-management]]

### Flowering
- Switch to bloom nutrients (higher phosphorus and potassium)
- EC ~1.5–2.0 depending on strain tolerance
- 12/12 light schedule
- Monitor for nutrient burn at higher concentrations

## Common Problems

### Root Rot (Pythium)
- Caused by warm water (above 72°F/22°C), poor oxygenation, or contaminated equipment
- Roots turn brown and slimy instead of white and crisp
- Treat with hydrogen peroxide (3%, 3ml per gallon), beneficial bacteria (HydroGuard), or reservoir cleaning
- Maintain water temperature below 70°F

### Algae
- Green growth on reservoir walls and net pots
- Block light from reaching the nutrient solution (use opaque reservoirs)
- Does not directly harm plants but consumes nutrients and can clog air stones

### pH Drift
- DWC pH tends to drift over time due to plant nutrient uptake
- Test and adjust pH daily
- Use pH Up (potassium hydroxide) and pH Down (phosphoric acid) products

## Recirculating vs. Bucket DWC Systems

Two main DWC configurations exist. Single-bucket (or "bubbler") systems are self-contained reservoirs where each plant has its own nutrient solution — simple to set up and isolate, but require individual pH/EC monitoring. Recirculating DWC (RDWC or "current culture") connects multiple buckets to a central reservoir and pump, circulating nutrient solution through the system. RDWC provides more uniform nutrient conditions and reduces per-plant maintenance, but a single contamination event (such as Pythium) can spread rapidly to all connected plants. RDWC systems typically require a chiller to maintain water temperature across the larger volume, and inline UV sterilizers are sometimes added to control pathogen spread between sites.
