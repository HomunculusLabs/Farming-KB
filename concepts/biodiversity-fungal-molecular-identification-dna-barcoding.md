# Fungal Molecular Identification and DNA Barcoding

Molecular methods have revolutionized the discrimination of fungal taxa,
monitoring of species, and [[monitoring-and-assessment-of-fungal-bioremediation]] diversity. These techniques
provide powerful tools for identifying fungi that are difficult or impossible
to distinguish by morphology alone, and they enable detection of fungi in
complex environmental samples such as [[biodiversity-of-fungi-endophytic-fungi]] that may be present at very low biomass within host
plant tissues.

## Ribosomal RNA/DNA Sequence Comparisons

Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and ribosomal DNA (rDNA) comparisons are the
standard approach for assessing evolutionary relationships among fungi
and yeasts. Ribosomes occur in all cellular organisms with a common
evolutionary origin; some sequences are conserved reference points for
aligning variable regions. In eukaryotes, genes for large (25S-28S),
small (18S), and 5.8S rRNAs occur as tandem repeats with 100 to 200
copies, each providing different phylogenetic resolution.

### RFLP Analysis

RFLP analysis of rDNA provides rapid species identification through
restriction endonuclease digestion of amplified regions. Patterns
distinguish species within genera such as Candida and Cryptococcus.
The hypervariable intergenic spacer is particularly useful for strain
discrimination, though RFLP-based evolutionary estimates are less
accurate than full sequence comparisons at greater distances.

### rRNA Sequencing

The small 5S rRNA (approximately 120 nucleotides) estimates broad
phylogenetic relationships. Basidiomycetous yeast 5S similarity
correlates with [[fungal-woronin-bodies-and-septal-pore-organization]] type. Complete 18S and 26S rDNA sequences
are now routine via PCR with specific primers and automated sequencing.
Partial small subunit sequences provide essentially the same phylogenies
as complete sequences, resolving long-standing taxonomic uncertainties
and revealing new species, especially among yeasts.

## Messenger RNA Transcript Analysis

In contrast to DNA, messenger RNA (mRNA) in complex substrata is rarely
analyzed because labile RNA molecules are difficult to purify. However,
magnetic capture techniques using oligo(dT) beads have overcome these
problems, enabling rapid and efficient purification of polyadenylated RNA
from soil and wood samples. When combined with competitive or real-time
PCR, quantitative assessment of transcript levels provides a measure of
[[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] and physiological activities of fungi in situ.

RT-PCR analysis is applicable to a wide array of substrata and fungal
species. The technique is particularly well suited for differentiating
genes within complex gene families such as those encoding peroxidases,
laccases, and cellobiohydrolases of white-rot fungi. Quantitative
transcript analysis using competitive RT-PCR or real-time PCR is as
sensitive and specific as conventional PCR amplification of DNA for
identifying fungi.

## Sample Collection and DNA Extraction

Tissue samples from sporocarps are collected and placed in DNA
preservation buffer (150 mM EDTA, 50 mM Tris pH 8.0, 2.0%
N-lauroylsarcosine). Samples can be stored at ambient temperatures for at
least one year without DNA degradation. For extraction, samples are
pulverized, incubated at 65 degrees C, and processed through precipitation
steps using PEG/NaCl and ammonium acetate, followed by isopropanol
precipitation and ethanol washing.

Nucleic acid extractions from fungi colonizing soil are particularly
difficult due to humic substances that interfere with Taq polymerase. The
introduction of magnetic capture techniques has enabled purification of
both DNA and RNA from soil samples, facilitating molecular identification
of [[biodiversity-fungi-plant-pathogens]] in complex samples.

## Important Considerations

Several precautions must be observed when applying molecular methods to
[[pcr-methods-fungal-identification-monitoring]]. Thermocyclers should be tested for accuracy and
reproducibility. Transcripts may not directly reflect protein levels, so
caution should be exercised in quantitative interpretations of cDNA
levels. Excessive thermocycling beyond the plateau phase should be
avoided for quantitative comparisons. Housekeeping genes such as GAPDH,
beta-actin, and HPRT can serve as internal standards for normalization.

As more recalcitrant taxa are sequenced, it will become feasible to
design amplification primers specific to particular fungal groups,
further enhancing our ability to assess [[biodiversity-fungal-species-abundance-diversity]] across diverse habitats and ecosystems.
