## Compost and Manure Teas

Solomon endorses experimentation with homebrewed compost/manure teas for
foliar feeding. The disease-suppressive qualities of well-made compost
tea are acknowledged, with a reference to Elaine Ingham's work. If you
make good compost, compost tea may serve a dual purpose -- providing
nutrients and disease protection through beneficial microbial
colonization of leaf surfaces.

However, the specific disease-suppressive properties of compost tea
depend on starting materials -- how much woody matter the compost
contains and how much green stuff -- which determines which diseases the
tea will control. Not all compost teas are equal in their effects.
