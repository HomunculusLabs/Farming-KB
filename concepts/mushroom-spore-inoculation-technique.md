# Mushroom Spore Inoculation Technique

## Overview

Spore inoculation is the process of introducing [[mycelial-colonization]] phase. The [[pf-tek]] employs a mass
spore inoculation approach where spores are delivered via syringe directly to the
[[mushroom-casing-moisture-management]].

## Pre-Inoculation Preparation

Before inoculating, several conditions must be met:

- **Jars must be cool to the touch.** Inoculating into hot jars kills the spores
  instantly. After [[growing-gourmet-atmospheric-steam-sterilization]], jars should be allowed to cool completely.
  After cooling, tighten the lids and store in a cool, draft-free place until ready
  to inoculate.

- **The [[comparison-mushroom-liquid-culture-vs-spore-syringe]] must be shaken well.** This is critical — shaking redistributes
  the spores in the water carrier solution. Without adequate shaking, spores settle
  to the bottom and the inoculum will be unevenly distributed, potentially leaving
  some holes without viable spores.

- **Tape is removed from both the syringe needle guard and the [[carbon-dioxide-retention-jar-lid-mushroom-cultivation]] holes.**
  The tape covering the needle holes on the jar lid was placed during sterilization
  to prevent contaminant entry. It must be removed before inoculation.

- **The jar lid should be tight.** Ensure the lid band is screwed on firmly before
  inserting the needle.

## The Inoculation Process

Once the jar is cool and the syringe is prepared, follow these steps:

1. Remove the needle guard from the syringe.
2. Insert the needle through one of the four punched lid holes.
3. Tilt the syringe body back towards the center of the lid so that the needle tip
   touches the inside glass wall of the jar.
4. This angle causes the spore solution to run down the side of the jar, distributing
   the inoculum along the substrate cake surface for a thorough inoculation.
5. Press the syringe plunger gently. As soon as water appears around the needle tip,
   release the plunger pressure — this prevents over-wetting any single spot.
6. Repeat for each of the four holes in the lid.

Between each hole inoculation, shake the syringe a little to keep the spores evenly
distributed in the solution. Use approximately 1 cc (1 ml) of spore solution per jar.
This standard volume allows a single 10 cc syringe to inoculate 10 jars. More spore
solution per jar can be used to speed colonization, but fewer jars can be inoculated
from the same syringe.

## Needle Placement

Proper needle depth is critical for success. The needle tip must be positioned below
the non-nutritive upper vermiculite barrier layer. If the spore solution is deposited
into the [[dry-vermiculite-contaminant-barrier-pf-tek]] layer instead of the substrate, it will be absorbed by the
non-nutritive material and the spores will not germinate because they lack access to
the nutrients in the [[brown-rice-powder]] below.

If the syringe needle plugs up as it is inserted into the substrate, draw the needle
back slightly and it will unplug. The needle should penetrate into the substrate
proper, not just the dry top layer.

## Inoculation Without Lids

For containers without punched lids (such as regular 8 oz tapered drinking glasses
covered with tin foil), the inoculation technique requires extra care:

- The dry top vermiculite layer must be disturbed as little as possible, especially
  when removing the needle after inoculation.
- The underlying wet substrate must not be exposed to the air.
- Any disturbed vermiculite should be carefully moved back into place to maintain
  the [[pf-tek-dry-vermiculite-contaminant-barrier]].
- After inoculation, the tin foil cover must be replaced immediately.

It is recommended to master inoculation with punched lids first before attempting the
lidless technique, as the lid method is nearly fail-proof by comparison.
