# Mushroom Fruiting Chamber Designs

A fruiting chamber provides the controlled environment necessary for mushrooms to form and mature. The key parameters are humidity, fresh air exchange (FAE), temperature, and light. This page covers the major chamber designs from simple to advanced.

## PF Dual Chambered Terrarium

The original fruiting chamber designed by [[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] for use with PF TEK cakes. Built from a standard 10-gallon aquarium.

### Components
- **10-gallon aquarium**: The main chamber body
- **Airtight lid**: Wooden frame with clear polyethylene film stretched across the underside, sized to hang tightly over the aquarium top. Must be sealable with no air leaks.
- **Spray shield/partition**: 1/8" thick clear acrylic (Plexiglas), approximately 15" x 18", fitted tightly inside the aquarium to create two chambers:
  - **Primary chamber**: Receives direct spray from the bottle
  - **Secondary chamber**: Houses the fruiting cakes, receives only fine mist

### Operating Principle
1. Water is sprayed into the primary chamber through the lid
2. The spray hits the shield and is broken into fine mist
3. Mist flows around the shield edges into the secondary chamber
4. The shield also acts as a drip shield, preventing condensation from falling on the cakes
5. The sealed chamber maintains near-100% humidity

### Spraying Procedure
- Pre-spray all interior surfaces before adding cakes
- Spray vigorously into the center of the shield for ~5 seconds
- Immediately withdraw sprayer and seal the lid
- Spray at least 2 times per day; more is better
- All interior surfaces should be foggy or dripping with water
- Siphon out pooled water from the bottom (prevents bacteria)

### Ventilation
Each time the terrarium is sprayed, ventilate by removing the lid and fanning with cardboard. This exchanges CO2 for fresh air.

### Lighting
- Expose to normal room light or indirect sunlight
- A small low-wattage fluorescent plant light encourages upward phototropic growth
- Can be left on continuously

### Heating
Do NOT heat the terrarium directly. No heating cables, heat pads, or blankets. Direct heating works against humidification and adds a drying influence. Mushrooms grow well at 60F and up. Optimal range: 65-85F. Too hot = spreading mycelium but no fruiting.

### Perlite Humidification (Alternative)
Wet perlite placed as a "floor" in the fruiting chamber provides excellent passive humidification:
- Perlite absorbs water but does not hold it (unlike vermiculite)
- Water evaporates gradually from perlite's porous structure over weeks
- Cakes can sit directly on the perlite for wicking action
- Eliminates the need for frequent manual spraying
- Can be combined with an aquarium air bubbler for additional FAE

## Monotub

A modern, simple design using a large plastic storage tote:
- Holes cut in the sides (typically near the substrate level and above) and stuffed with polyfill for FAE
- Substrate is colonized directly in the tub (spawn mixed with bulk substrate)
- No separate fruiting chamber needed
- Humidity maintained by the large substrate mass and limited ventilation
- Perlite or no additional humidification needed

This is the most popular method for intermediate cultivators doing grain-to-bulk cultivation.

## Martha Tent (Greenhouse Chamber)

A large greenhouse-style enclosure (originally a "Martha Stewart" brand closet greenhouse):
- Humidified by an ultrasonic humidifier connected to the enclosure
- Fresh air provided by an intake fan with HEPA filter
- Shelves hold multiple trays or blocks
- Environmental controller manages humidity and temperature
- Best for larger-scale cultivation with multiple trays

## Professional Growing Room (Stamets)

For commercial-scale cultivation, Stamets describes integrated systems:
- Ducted fresh air with HEPA filtration
- Recirculation system with damper control
- Evaporative cooling or refrigeration
- Humidification integrated into air handling
- 4-6 air changes per hour for Agaricus; 2-3 for P. cubensis
- Variable speed fan controls for different growth phases
- CO2 monitoring to determine air exchange requirements

## Panaeolus Fruiting Setup

[[panaeolus-cyanescens-bali-omelette]] and P. tropicalis require modified setups:
- Substrate spawned in autoclave bags
- After colonization, substrate is transferred to a plastic bin with polyfill-stuffed holes
- Covered with 1-1.5 cm sterilized casing soil
- Covered with polyethylene wrap for case run (5-6 days)
- Once mycelium shows on casing, a second bin is placed upside-down on top (with mesh-covered holes) to create a "mini-greenhouse"
- These species are extremely sensitive to CO2 -- insufficient FAE causes tall, spindly mushrooms with small caps and many aborts

## Outdoor Beds (Woodlovers)

See [[outdoor-wood-lover-cultivation]] for outdoor fruiting chamber alternatives. Outdoor beds are covered with perforated plastic during colonization and uncovered for fruiting in autumn.

## Key Design Principles

1. **Humidity**: Near 100% is ideal. Mushrooms "bathe in a shroud of floating water molecules." Molecularized water (evaporated) is what the fungus needs, not water droplets.
2. **FAE**: Essential to prevent CO2 buildup, which causes deformed, elongated stems and small caps
3. **No standing water**: Pooled water promotes bacterial growth
4. **Light**: Low-intensity ambient or fluorescent light is sufficient
5. **Temperature stability**: Avoid direct heating; use ambient room temperature
6. **Cleanliness**: Regularly clean and maintain chambers to prevent contamination buildup

## Symptoms of Low Humidity

- Fuzzy white mycelium growing on tops of caps ("crown of white hair") -- not contamination
- Deformed, convoluted, withering mushrooms and primordia
- Abortive primordia
- Cracking or splitting of stems and caps
