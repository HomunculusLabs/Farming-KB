# Root Maggots and Soil Pests

Root maggots are among the most frustrating garden pests because they operate underground, hidden from view. By the time damage is visible above ground — wilting, stunting, or collapse — it's often too late for effective treatment. Prevention is far more effective than cure.

## Cabbage Root Maggot (Hylemya brassicae)

### Geographic Distribution

A major problem primarily in Cascadia (Pacific Northwest) and the United Kingdom. In other parts of North America, it's a minor annoyance.

### Life Cycle and Behavior

- The fly waits until the brassica root system is extensive enough to support its brood (stem ~1/4 inch or 6 mm in diameter)
- Eggs are laid on the soil surface near the plant
- Larvae hatch and burrow down to feed on roots
- Weaker-rooting brassicas (small-framed cabbage, most cauliflower) are demolished
- Stronger-rooting types tolerate some damage without wilting

### Seasonal Pattern

| Time | Activity | Risk Level |
|------|----------|------------|
| Early spring | First generation emerges | HIGH for early-sown brassicas |
| Mid-May to July | Spring maggots pupating harmlessly | LOW — best sowing window |
| Late summer | Second generation pupae hatch | MODERATE — large plants tolerate it |
| Autumn/winter | Population increases | LOW — most crops harvested |

### Control Methods

**Sawdust Collar** (best simple organic method):
- A ring of fresh fine sawdust ~1.5 inches (4 cm) thick
- 6-8 inches (15-20 cm) in diameter
- Not touching the stem
- Prevents the fly from reaching the soil to lay eggs
- For radishes and turnips: cover seeds with a band of fine sawdust 4 inches (10 cm) wide and 1 inch (2.5 cm) deep

**Row Covers**:
- Floating row covers carefully anchored on all sides
- Especially useful for Chinese cabbage and turnips
- Must be meticulously anchored — any gap allows access

**Parasitic Nematodes**:
- Certain species effectively attack root maggots in the soil
- Seed large numbers into soil surrounding seedlings
- Can live for months, breeding and maintaining effective population levels
- Also control wireworms, onion maggots, carrot weevils, cutworms, rhododendron weevil larvae, strawberry root weevil larvae, and cucumber beetle larvae
- Cheap to culture by the billions, but not always simple to transport or store alive
- Be cautious when buying — ensure they are fresh and effective

**Hilling**:
- Careful and persistent hilling of soil around plant stems
- Increases seedling survival by burying the root system deeper

**Planting Timing**:
- Sow brassicas mid-May through July to avoid the spring population peak
- Later sowings avoid the worst predation window

### What Doesn't Work

Wood ashes or lime dustings — once widely recommended — actually **attract** cabbage flies. They may incidentally help in acidic, calcium-deficient soils by boosting brassica growth enough to compensate for the increased predation.

## Carrot Rust Fly (Psila rosae)

### Geographic Distribution

A similar Cascadian (and British) pest that gardeners in eastern North America are fortunate to rarely encounter. Even within Cascadia, it's highly localized — some gardens 40 miles apart can have vastly different infestation levels.

### Life Cycle

- Begins breeding in late summer
- Goes through a generation every month
- Becomes a plague by winter if the season is not too severe
- Population can grow a hundredfold or more each month into autumn

### Damage

Maggots riddle carrots and leave them inedible. Carrots left in the ground after summer ends become increasingly infested.

### Seasonal Strategy

Carrots started in late May come up after the spring hatch is through. These may finish growing relatively unharmed and be harvested by late summer. Carrots left in ground longer become increasingly infested.

### Control

- **Late sowing**: Start carrots after the spring hatch is through
- **Row covers**: Covering the bed with meticulously anchored spun-fiber row covers may give maggot-free carrots. When tops are 3-4 inches (7.5-10 cm) tall, thin to 150% normal spacing and eliminate every weed, then carefully cover
- **Winter storage**: Store carrots by laying plastic sheets over the beds and covering with straw or soil for insulation

### What Doesn't Work

Parasitic nematodes are useless against carrot rust fly because this maggot is most active when soil temperatures are low and nematodes are inactive.

## Colorado Potato Beetle (Leptinotarsa decemlineata)

### Range and Impact

Found almost everywhere in North America, this beetle can almost completely defoliate a commercial potato crop. Also feeds on other solanums (peppers, eggplants).

### Life Cycle

- Adults overwinter 12-18 inches (30-45 cm) below surface in or close to the potato plot
- Emerge in late spring, lay eggs on leaf undersides
- Both adults and larvae feed on foliage

### Control

- **New ground**: Growing potatoes on new ground means few beetles emerge immediately — row covers can be an effective defense
- **Straw mulch**: Using wheat or rye straw after planting greatly reduces problems by providing habitat for predator insects that feed on beetles and larvae
- **Early varieties**: Growing early-maturing varieties may help plants produce before beetles get too thick
- **Handpicking**: A few hours of handpicking adult beetles when they first emerge in spring, before they lay eggs, can be very effective
- **Propane flamers**: Lethally burn beetles and their eggs on young plants — one of the best and most affordable methods for the home gardener

## Japanese Beetle (Popillia japonica Newman)

Mostly in the northeastern United States, though it feeds on many kinds of plants. The beetle goes through only one generation per year — adults emerge in spring and are gone within a few months.

### Control

- **Traps**: Can significantly reduce population if everyone in the area traps them (adults are strong flyers)
- **Bt and biologicals**: Strains specific to this pest can be mixed in water and poured on soil
- **Rotenone**: Spray adults where they are unwanted

## General Prevention Strategies

1. **Healthy soil grows healthy plants** — the single most important factor
2. **Foliar feeding** with fish emulsion and kelp gives plants a boost when under attack
3. **Diverse plantings** spread risk — don't put all your brassicas in one spot
4. **Beneficial insect habitat** provides natural pest control
5. **Timely planting** avoids peak pest emergence windows
6. **Resistant varieties** when available — some brassica varieties show root maggot resistance
