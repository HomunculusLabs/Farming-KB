## Selection of Inoculant Strains

Selection of effective inoculant strains for microplant systems requires consideration of multiple factors. Strains should be effective under the specific substrate conditions used in micropropagation (peat, coir, rockwool, hydroponics). Temperature tolerance should match greenhouse conditions. Strains should not be inhibited by the pesticides commonly used in micropropagation facilities. For commercial viability, inoculants should offer at least a 4-fold return on investment. Some researchers advocate seeking inoculants from pathogen-suppressive soils, while others pursue universal strains with broad environmental tolerance.

## Inoculation Timing and Methods

The timing of inoculation significantly affects establishment success. AMF inoculation is most effective when applied during Stage 2 (rooting) or early Stage 3 (weaning) of micropropagation, allowing the symbiosis to establish before transplanting. PGPR can be applied as seed or cutting treatments, root dips, or substrate amendments.
