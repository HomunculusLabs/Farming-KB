## Tyre Ponds

Used car tyres can be stacked and sealed (or half-buried) downslope of
the chicken house to create small watering points. These tyre ponds serve
ducks or chickens directly while also growing duckweed, azolla, and water
hyacinth for poultry feed. They provide drinking water, dust bathing areas,
and supplementary forage all in one integrated unit.

Tyre ponds should be placed on contour so overflow feeds into the next
swale. In tropical climates, water plants in tyre ponds can double as
nutrient biofilters before water reaches food production zones.

## Windbreaks on Every 6th to 10th Swale

Mollison recommends planting windbreaks on every 6th to 10th swale
depending on wind exposure. These planted windbreaks reduce evaporation
from the swale system, protect poultry from cold winds, and create
microclimates that extend the growing season for understory crops.

Windbreak swales typically carry timber trees, casuarina, or
multi-purpose species that yield fodder, mulch, and wildlife habitat in
addition to wind protection. They are oriented perpendicular to prevailing
winds for maximum effect.
