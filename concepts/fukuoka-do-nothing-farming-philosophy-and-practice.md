# Do-Nothing Farming Philosophy and Practice

updated: 2026-04-18

In a 1975 speech outlining his experiences and approach to natural
farming, [[fukuoka-heart-sutra-and-illusion-of-life-death]]: "Form is emptiness
and emptiness is form. All is nothing." He interpreted this to mean
that all effort is in vain. Buddha is saying that we are not alive, we
are not growing, and we are not dead. Most people react to this by
thinking it nonsense, pointing to a flower on the table as proof of
life. But the Lord Buddha says that this is neither dead nor alive.

The soul itself is just something that arises from the body, and
neither exist; that the flesh and the soul are emptiness. No matter how
hard he tried to show this to others, it never got through. But he
wanted to try to demonstrate it for himself while farming in the
fields.

the opposite approach of "What about doing this? What about doing
that?" But this only makes people busier and makes things harder for
the farmer, who ends up having to do everything. Preoccupied with the
thought of having to do this and that, people are forever worrying.

When he became a farmer following the war, he searched for things that
do not have to be done. He asked whether the fields really need to be
plowed and turned, whether the rice farmer really has to transplant his
seedlings, whether it is necessary to spread fertilizer on the fields.

## The Paradoxes of Scientific Farming

Fukuoka explained that scientific truth appears to be truth, plain and
simple, but it is only scientific truth, not absolute truth. Things
appear to have value, but invariably man has set up the conditions that
give them their apparent value.
