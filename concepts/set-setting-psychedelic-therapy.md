## Set, Setting, and Session Planning in Psychedelic Therapy

## Overview

One of the most influential contributions of *The Psychedelic Experience* (Leary, Metzner, Alpert, 1964) to the broader field of psychedelic research is its systematic treatment of **[[lsd-set-and-setting-framework]]** — the foundational principle that the nature and quality of a psychedelic experience is determined not primarily by the chemical substance, but by the psychological preparation (set) and environmental conditions (setting) of the session. This principle, now widely accepted in psychedelic science, was articulated here with particular clarity and depth through the framework of the Tibetan [[bardo-thodol]].

## The Primacy of Set and Setting

The manual states explicitly: "The specific reaction has little to do with the chemical and is chiefly a function of set and setting." Psychedelic chemicals are described as *not drugs in the usual sense* — there is no specific reaction, no expected sequence of events, somatic or psychological. The better the preparation, the more ecstatic and revelatory the session. In initial sessions with unprepared persons, setting — particularly the actions of others — is most important. With persons who have prepared thoughtfully and seriously, the setting is less important.

## Two Aspects of Set

### Long-Range Set
Long-range set refers to the personal history and enduring personality. The kind of person one is — fears, desires, conflicts, guilts, secret passions — determines how one interprets and manages any situation, including a psychedelic session. Perhaps more important are the **reflex mechanisms** used when dealing with anxiety — the defenses and protective maneuvers typically employed.

**Positive characteristics** for psychedelic sessions include: flexibility, basic trust, religious faith, human openness, courage, interpersonal warmth, and creativity. These allow for fun and easy learning.

**Negative characteristics** include: rigidity, desire to control, distrust, cynicism, narrowness, cowardice, and coldness. These make any new situation threatening.

**Most important is insight** — the person who has some understanding of their own "recording machinery," who can recognize when they are not functioning as they would wish, is better able to adapt to any challenge, even the sudden collapse of their ego.

The most careful preparation includes discussion of personality characteristics and planning with the guide as to how to handle expected emotional reactions.

### Immediate Set
Immediate set refers to expectations about the session itself. People naturally impose their personal and social game perspectives on any new situation. Careful thought should precede the session to prevent narrow sets from being imposed. Several common expectation-patterns are identified:

#### Medical Expectations
Some ill-prepared subjects unconsciously impose a medical model. They look for symptoms, interpret each sensation in terms of sickness/health, place the guide in a doctor-role, and demand chemical rebirth (tranquilizers) if anxiety develops. This is particularly problematic if the guide also employs a medical model.

#### Rebellion Against Convention
The idea of doing something "far out" or vaguely naughty is identified as a naive set that can color the entire experience.

#### Intellectual Expectations
While appropriate for experienced subjects (LSD offers vast possibilities for accelerated learning and research), for initial sessions intellectual reactions can become traps. The Tibetan Manual never tires of warning about the dangers of rationalization. "Turn your mind off" is the best advice for novitiates. Control of consciousness is compared to flight instruction — after learning to move consciousness around at will, intellectual exercises can be incorporated. The last stage of the session is the best time to examine concepts.

#### Religious Expectations
As with intellectual set, the subject in early sessions is best advised to float with the stream, postpone theological interpretations until the end of the session, or to later sessions.

#### Recreational and Aesthetic Expectations
Natural and valid — the experience provides ecstatic moments, Himalayan heights of interpersonal intimacy, aesthetic delights raised to extraordinary intensities. But these reactions can become [[third-bardo]] ego games: "I am having this ecstasy. How lucky I am!" Such reactions can become tender traps preventing the subject from reaching pure ego-loss or the glories of Second Bardo creativity.

#### Planned Expectations
This manual prepares the person for a [[mystical-experience]] according to the Tibetan model. The authors acknowledge that the Bardo Thodol model is itself a human artifact, a Second Bardo hallucination, however grand its scope.

## Practical Preparation Recommendations

### Time Allocation
The subject should set aside at least **three days**: a day before, the session day, and a follow-up day. This guarantees reduction in external pressure and a more sober commitment to the voyage.

### Pre-Session Activities
- **Talking to others** who have taken the voyage — excellent preparation, though the Second Bardo hallucinatory quality of all descriptions should be recognized.
- **Observing a session** — another valuable preliminary; seeing others during and after a session shapes expectations.
- **Reading books about mystical experience** — a standard orientation procedure. Accounts by [[aldous-huxley]], Alan Watts, and Gordon Wasson are specifically recommended.
- **Meditation** — described as probably the best preparation. Those who have spent time managing the mind, eliminating thought, and reaching higher stages of concentration are the best candidates. When ego-loss occurs, they recognize the process as an end eagerly awaited rather than a strange event.

## The Setting

### Privacy and Freedom from Intrusion
The most important consideration: provide a setting removed from one"s usual social and interpersonal games, as free as possible from unforeseen distractions and intrusions. The voyager must ensure they will not be disturbed by visitors or telephone calls, since these jar one into hallucinatory activity. Trust in the surroundings and privacy are necessary.

### Time Scheduling
A period of at least three days should be set aside for the experience to run its natural course with sufficient time for reflection and meditation. A too-hasty return to game-involvements blurs the clarity of the vision and reduces the potential for learning. If the experience was with a group, staying together afterward to share and exchange experiences is very useful.

### Night vs. Day Sessions
Many people report being more comfortable in the evening and consequently having deeper, richer experiences. The person should choose the time of day that seems right according to their own temperament.

### Indoor vs. Outdoor Sessions
Natural settings — gardens, beaches, forests, open country — have specific influences one may or may not wish to incur. The essential thing is feeling as comfortable as possible, whether in one's living room or under the night sky. Familiarity with surroundings may help confidence during hallucinatory periods.

### Sensory Environment
Music, lighting, availability of food and drink should be considered beforehand. Most people report no desire for food during the height of the experience, and later prefer simple, ancient foods: bread, cheese, wine, and fresh fruit. The senses are wide open — the taste and smell of a fresh orange are unforgettable.

### Group Session Room Arrangement
- People usually will not feel like walking or moving much for a long period; beds or mattresses should be provided.
- One suggestion: place the heads of beds together in a star pattern.
- Sometimes a few beds together with one or two at a distance for anyone wishing seclusion.
- The availability of an extra room is desirable for someone who wishes privacy for a period.
- Music, paintings, or religious objects should be arranged so everyone feels comfortable.

## The Psychedelic Guide

### The Guide's Power
For initial sessions, the guide's attitude and behavior are critical factors. With the cognitive mind suspended, the subject is in a heightened state of suggestibility. The guide can move consciousness with the slightest gesture or reaction.

### The Key Requirement
The guide's ability to **turn off their own ego and social games** — in particular, to muffle their own power needs and fears. To be there: relaxed, solid, accepting, secure. The Tao wisdom of creative quietism — to sense all and do nothing except let the subject know your wise presence.

### The Airport Tower Metaphor
The guide is ground control in the airport tower: always there to receive messages and queries from high-flying aircraft, always ready to help navigate their course. An airport-tower-operator who imposes their own personality and games upon the pilot is unheard of — the pilots have their own flight plan. If the flier suspects that ground control harbors selfish motives, the bond of security and confidence crumbles.

### Experience Requirement
The guide should have had considerable experience in psychedelic sessions themselves and in guiding others. To administer psychedelics without personal experience is described as unethical and dangerous.

### The Root of Negative Reactions
From research studies, the authors concluded that **almost every negative LSD reaction has been caused by fear on the part of the guide** which augmented the transient fear of the subject. When the guide acts to protect themselves, they communicate concern to the subject.

### The Guide's Dosage
The most certain way to achieve the necessary state of alert quietism is for the guide to take a **low dose** of the psychedelic with the subject. Routine procedure: one trained person participates in the experience and one staff member serves as ground control without psychedelic aid. The knowledge that an experienced guide is "up" and keeping company provides inestimable value — intimacy, communication, cosmic companionship, the security of having a trained pilot at your wing tip.

Guides are not recommended to take large doses during sessions for new subjects, as the subject's intense Second and Third Bardo games can pull the experienced guide into the hallucinatory field.

### The Guide as Liberator
The role of the psychedelic guide is described as perhaps the most exciting and inspiring role in society — literally a liberator who provides illumination and frees people from lifelong internal bondage. The psychedelic liberation is so powerful that it far outstrips earthly game ambitions. Awe and gratitude — rather than pride — are the rewards of this profession.

## Group Composition

### Trust and Knowledge
The most important consideration: knowledge of and trust in fellow voyagers. If preparing for an experience with strangers, it is very important to share as much time and space as possible prior to the session. Participants should set collaborative goals and explore expectations, feelings, and past experiences mutually.

### Group Size
Initially, small groups are preferable. Group experiences exceeding six or seven people are demonstrably less profound and generate more paranoid hallucinations.

### Guide Ratio
For five or six people, at least two guides should be present: one taking the psychedelic substance, one serving as practical guide (changing recordings, providing food, reading from the manual). If possible, one guide should be an experienced woman who can provide an atmosphere of spiritual nurturing and comfort.

### Married Couples
It is sometimes advisable that the initial session of married couples be separate, so that exploration of their marriage game does not dominate the session. With experience in consciousness-expansion, the marriage game — like others — may be explored for increased intimacy, clearer communication, or exploration of the sexual relationship.

## Session Goals

Classic Hinduism suggests four possible goals for a psychedelic session:

1. **Personal power** — intellectual understanding, sharpened insight, accelerated learning, professional growth.
2. **Duty** — help of others, providing care, rehabilitation, rebirth for fellow humans.
3. **Fun** — sensuous enjoyment, aesthetic pleasure, interpersonal closeness, pure experience.
4. **Transcendence** — liberation from ego and space-time limits; attainment of mystical union.

This manual aims primarily at transcendence-liberation, though this emphasis guarantees attainment of the other goals because illumination requires stepping beyond game problems of personality, role, and professional status.

### Introverted vs. Extroverted Experience
The voyager may wish for either an extroverted experience (self ecstatically fused with external objects) or an introverted experience (self fused with internal life processes: lights, energy-waves, bodily events, biological forms). For the extroverted mystic experience, one brings objects or symbols to guide awareness — candles, pictures, books, incense, music. For the introverted mystic experience, all stimulation is eliminated: no light, no sound, no smell, no movement.

## Drugs and Dosages

The manual provides dosage guidelines for the most widely used psychedelic substances:

| Substance | Full Dose | Group/Lower Dose |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| LSD-25 | 200-500 micrograms | 100-200 micrograms |
| Mescaline | 600-800 mg | 300-500 mg |
| Psilocybin | 40-60 mg | 20-30 mg |
| DMT (i.m.) | 50-60 mg | — |

**Time of onset** (orally, empty stomach): 20-30 minutes for LSD and psilocybin; 1-2 hours for mescaline.
