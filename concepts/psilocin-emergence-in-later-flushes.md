# Psilocin Emergence in Later Flushes

[[psilocin]] levels in *[[psilocin-biosynthetic-delay-hypothesis-successive-flushes]].

Psilocin is generally absent or nearly absent in the first flush, increases to a maximum by the fourth flush, and then begins to decline.

This pattern suggests a developmental maturation of the enzymatic pathway responsible for psilocin production.

## Observed Pattern

Bigwood and Beug (1982) quantified psilocin levels across 5–6 flushes in three independent minicultures of an Amazonian strain:

**Miniculture 1**: 0.5, 1.5, 1.0, 2.6, —, 0.5 mg/g psilocin (flushes 1–6)

**Miniculture 2**: 0, 0, 1.7, 2.9, 2.2 mg/g psilocin (flushes 1–5)

**Miniculture 3**: 0, 0, 0.9, 1.8, 1.7 mg/g psilocin (flushes 1–5)

The pattern is remarkably consistent across all three cultures despite variation in absolute values.

### First Flush: Absence or Trace

Psilocin is at or near zero in the first flush.

Miniculture 2 and Miniculture 3 showed absolutely zero detectable psilocin.

Miniculture 1 showed a trace of 0.5 mg/g.

This near-absence in the first flush was confirmed in additional strains from outside sources.

### Second Flush: Transitional

The second flush shows an intermediate state.

Miniculture 1 showed 1.5 mg/g, Miniculture 2 showed zero, and Miniculture 3 showed zero.

This variability at flush 2 suggests the enzymatic pathway is in the process of being established.

### Third Flush: Onset of Production

By the third flush, all three minicultures showed measurable psilocin levels (0.9–1.7 mg/g).

The pathway appears to be fully operational by this point.

### Fourth Flush: Peak Production

The fourth flush consistently showed the highest psilocin levels (1.8–2.9 mg/g).

This represents the peak of enzymatic capacity for psilocin production.

### Fifth Flush and Beyond: Decline

By the fifth flush, psilocin levels begin to decline.

Miniculture 1 showed a return to 0.5 mg/g at flush 6.

Unfortunately, the researchers could not follow the decline further because most cultures stop fruiting after 5–6 flushes.

## Biosynthetic Interpretation

The delayed emergence of psilocin suggests that the enzyme responsible for its production from [[psilocybin]] (or from a common precursor) requires time to reach full expression.

Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the phosphorylated form.

Psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the dephosphorylated, pharmacologically active form.

In vivo, psilocin is generated by dephosphorylation of psilocybin by alkaline phosphatase enzymes.

## Relationship Between Psilocybin and Psilocin

Psilocybin levels are nearly always at least twice the level of psilocin.

This ratio is consistent [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]], even as both compounds vary independently.

The relatively constant ratio suggests that psilocin production is stoichiometrically linked to psilocybin availability.
