# White Rot and Brown Rot Fungi Fungi that decompose wood can be classified into two primary subgroups based on how they break down the structural components of wood: brown rot fungi and white rot fungi. The powerful enzymes secreted by these fungi to digest lignin and cellulose, the primary structural components of wood, can also break down a surprisingly wide range of toxins with chemical bonds similar to those found in wood, making them essential tools for both forest ecology and environmental remediation. ## Brown Rot Fungi Only about 7 percent of mushrooms are brown rot fungi; of those, about 70 percent are polypores. Brown rot fungi's extracellular enzymes break down the white, pulpy cellulose, leaving behind the brownish lignin (hence the name). These fungi cause checkered cubical cracking and shrinking in wood, which is commonly seen on downed conifer trees in forest ecosystems. ## Lignin and Cellulose Structure

Wood is composed primarily of three polymers: cellulose (40-50%),
hemicellulose (20-30%), and lignin (20-30%). Cellulose forms linear
chains of glucose that provide tensile strength. Hemicellulose is a
branched polymer of various sugars that cross-links cellulose
microfibrils. Lignin is a complex, irregular phenolic polymer that
fills the spaces between cellulose and hemicellulose, providing
compressive strength and resistance to microbial degradation. The
recalcitrance of lignin, formed from radical coupling of phenylpropanoid
units, makes it one of the most difficult organic molecules to break
down in nature. White rot fungi are among the few organisms capable of
depolymerizing lignin.

## Enzymatic Mechanisms of White Rot Fungi

White rot fungi deploy a unique extracellular enzymatic system for
lignin degradation. Unlike most enzymes that act through hydrolysis,
the ligninolytic enzymes of white rot fungi act through oxidative
mechanisms:
- **Lignin peroxidase (LiP)**: Oxidizes non-phenolic aromatic
  structures in lignin, which constitute approximately 90% of lignin
  bonds. Uses hydrogen peroxide as a co-substrate and veratryl alcohol
  as a redox mediator.
- **Manganese peroxidase (MnP)**: Oxidizes Mn(II) to Mn(III), which
  then diffuses into the lignin matrix to oxidize phenolic compounds.
  This enzyme is unique to white rot fungi and is considered essential
  for complete lignin mineralization.
- **Laccase**: A multicopper oxidase that oxidizes phenolic subunits
  and can work with mediators to attack non-phenolic structures. Found
  in both white rot fungi and some bacteria.

## Brown Rot Fenton Chemistry

Brown rot fungi use a fundamentally different mechanism: rather than
producing ligninolytic enzymes, they generate hydroxyl radicals through
the Fenton reaction (Fe2+ + H2O2 producing Fe3+ + OH- + OH radical).
These extremely reactive hydroxyl radicals non-enzymatically depolymerize
cellulose, creating characteristic cubical cracking in wood. Brown rot
fungi also produce oxalic acid, which lowers the pH of the wood and
helps solubilize iron for the Fenton reaction. This chemistry is
important in bioremediation because Fenton reactions can degrade a wide
range of organic pollutants including polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons
and some chlorinated compounds.
