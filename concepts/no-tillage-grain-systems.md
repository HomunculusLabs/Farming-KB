# No-Tillage Grain and Staple Crop Systems

No-tillage grain systems from Mollison"s Permaculture Two, building on Fukuoka's methods. See [[grain-and-staple-crops]] for general homestead grain growing and [[one-straw-revolution-guide]] for Fukuoka's philosophy.

## Fukuoka's Grain/Legume Rotation System

### The Five-Plot Rotation

Five plots rotate through rice + winter grain + white clover simultaneously:

| Plot | Current Crop | Previous Straw Applied |
|------|-------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Rice | Rice + clover + rye |
| 2 | Rice | Rice + clover + barley |
| 3 | Rice | Rice + clover + millet |
| 4 | Rice | Rice + clover + wheat |
| 5 | Rice | Rice + clover + oats |

### Monthly Calendar (Southern Hemisphere — add 6 months for Northern)

**April**: Broadcast rice seed, white clover, and winter grain (rye, barley, etc.) under straw mulch. Light application of chicken manure.

**May**: Harvest last year's rice. Dry on racks 2-3 weeks, thresh. Return all rice straw and husks to field. Resow unhusked rice within a month.

**June-September**: Winter grains grow. Light grazing by sheep or geese assists stooling and adds manure. When crop reaches 150mm, ~100 ducks per hectare reduce pests.

**October**: Check rice is growing; re-sow thin patches.

**November**: Harvest winter grains. Stack to dry 7-10 days. Return all straw to different plots (rotate straw types between plots).

**December**: Only rice remains. Summer weeds sprouted; weaken by flooding 7-10 days until clover yellows but doesn't die.

**January-March**: Field at 50-80% saturation under rice. Prepare other grain seed for April sowing.

### Key Principles

- **Never till**: Straw mulch is the only weed control
- **Return all straw**: Every year, cycle straw between plots to prevent disease
- **Clover as permanent base**: White clover provides nitrogen and suppresses weeds
- **No chemicals**: Fukuoka maintained this for 25 years with only chicken/duck manure
- **Seed pelleting**: Mix grain with mud, press through wire-mesh into balls, or coat with clay dust to prevent bird predation

## Dryland Adaptation

Where paddies aren't possible:
- Substitute dryland rice or other grain species
- Spray irrigation replaces summer flooding
- In monsoon areas, summer rain suffices
- For cool areas: substitute spring wheat or corn sown in September-November with oats/barley/wheat as winter crop
- **Mulch-only system**: No irrigation needed once established; soil builds organic matter rapidly

## Yield Distribution Strategy

Mollison emphasizes dispersing yield over time rather than concentrating into one period:

1. Select early, mid, and late season varieties
2. Plant same variety in early and late-ripening situations
3. Select long-yielding species
4. Increase diversity so leaf, fruit, seed, and root are all product yields
5. Use self-storing species (tubers, hard seeds, nuts, rhizomes) that can be harvested on demand
6. Preserve, dry, pit, and cool-store
7. Regional trade between communities; purchase land at different altitudes or latitudes

## Keeping Annuals "Perennial"

Techniques from Mollison for reducing annual replanting:

- **Leeks**: Leave a few to run to seed; lift and find many small bulbils around the base
- **Onion family**: Perennial chives, garlic chives, shallots, potato onions (25 per 1 planted), Welsh onions, tree onion bulbils
- **Broad beans**: Leave pods to dry and hay-mulch in late summer — resprout in autumn; or hard-cut after harvest for regrowth
- **Potatoes**: Leave seed potatoes under mulch to sprout in spring
- **Lettuce**: Let go to seed — scatter seedlings around base for replanting
- **Parsley**: Flat-seeded species reseed freely in mulch
- **Tomatoes, pumpkins, melons**: Place whole fruits under mulch at harvest — ferment and produce volunteer seedlings
