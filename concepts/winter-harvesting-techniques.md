## Winter Harvesting Techniques

> Core practices for harvesting and packing cold-season crops from unheated and
> minimally heated greenhouses during the depths of winter, as developed and
> refined by [[eliot-coleman]] at [[four-season-farm]].

## Overview

Winter harvesting is a tightly choreographed operation that balances thermal
constraints, labour efficiency, and food-safety hygiene. Everything from the
weekly calendar to the diameter of sink drain pipes is tuned so that freshly
cut greens move from greenhouse to customer within hours — even when overnight
lows plunge well below zero.

## Harvest Schedule

Crops are harvested **twice per week**, on **Mondays and Thursdays**. The
finished product is delivered the same afternoon or the following morning,
ensuring maximum freshness.

This two-day cadence is non-negotiable during the winter months. It provides a
predictable rhythm for the crew and keeps the interval between harvest and
delivery as short as possible.

## Temperature Constraints

After a night when temperatures drop **below zero**, harvesting cannot begin
until the greenhouse air temperature climbs back above **32 °F (0 °C)**. The
practical harvest window under these conditions typically spans **10 AM to 3 PM**.

This constraint means that on the coldest days the crew has roughly five hours
to complete all cutting, washing, packing, and loading. Every minute of that
window must be used efficiently.

## Daily Harvest Sequence

The workday follows a deliberate path through the farm's different growing
environments to make the best use of warming temperatures:

1. **Early morning** — Begin in the **cool greenhouse**, where the higher
   baseline temperature allows earlier access.
2. **Midday** — Move to the **cold houses**, which have warmed enough by then
   to permit safe cutting.
3. **Late afternoon** — Return to the **cool greenhouse** for radishes and
   turnips, which are harvested last.

This routing maximises the usable hours across structures with different
thermal profiles without wasting time waiting for a single house to warm.

## Inner Cover Management

**Row-cover inner blankets must be replaced immediately after harvesting** in
each section. Below freezing, exposed row cover quickly freezes to itself,
making it stiff, difficult to reposition, and prone to tearing. Prompt
replacement protects both the crop and the equipment.

## Staying Warm While Working

Harvest crews must maintain manual dexterity in cold conditions. The clothing
and gear choices reflect a balance between warmth and precision:

- **Vests** are preferred over heavy parkas — they keep the core warm without
  restricting arm movement.
- **Small, sharp Victorinox knives** (7" overall length, 3" blade) are the
  standard cutting tool; a compact knife reduces hand fatigue and improves
  cut quality.
- **Neoprene gloves** are worn on the knife hand for insulation while
  retaining grip.
- The **bare (non-knife) hand** is periodically soaked in warm water at the
  packing area to restore sensation and flexibility.

## Washing and Packing Hygiene

Food safety is treated as paramount. The wash/pack station operates under a
strict cleanliness protocol:

- The scrub area is **cleaned after every harvest day**.
- The farm's well water is **tested annually**.
- **Hand washing is mandatory** for all crew members before handling produce.
- **Hats and hairnets** are required at all times in the wash/pack area.
- Each salad ingredient is **washed separately** before combining, which
  prevents cross-contamination and makes it easier to inspect individual items.

## The "Pac Man" Salad Mixer

Mixed salads are prepared in a custom-built wooden barrel nicknamed the "Pac
Man":

| Specification       | Detail                         |
|---------------------|--------------------------------|
| Length              | 48 inches                      |
| Diameter            | 36 inches                      |
| Capacity            | 80 lbs of salad greens         |
| Orientation         | Mounted horizontally           |
| Design              | Clamshell (opens along the top)|
| Mixing action       | 10 slow revolutions in each direction |

The clamshell design allows the barrel to be loaded and unloaded from the top.
Ten slow turns clockwise followed by ten counter-clockwise produces a thorough,
gentle mix that bruises leaves far less than a tumbling action would.

## Efficiency Improvements

Small infrastructural upgrades yield outsized time savings during the short
winter harvest window:

- **Larger sink drain pipes** reduced drain-and-refill time by **75 %**, freeing
  significant minutes for actual washing.
- **Parallel workflow**: one crew member begins washing as soon as the first
  harvest buckets arrive, so washing and cutting overlap rather than running
  sequentially.

## Speed and Skill

The compressed harvest window demands both physical quickness and mental
sharpness. Key principles:

- **Minimise hand travel** — studies showed that roughly **40 % of a harvester's
  time** is spent simply moving the hand between the plant and the collection
  container. Reducing that distance through better bucket placement and cutting
  technique is one of the single largest productivity gains available.
- New employees are taught by **direct demonstration**; experienced harvesters
  model proper knife technique, body positioning, and workflow rhythm so trainees
  can absorb the mechanics visually rather than verbally.

## Key Takeaways

- Harvest is tightly scheduled (Mon/Thu) and time-limited by temperature.
- The daily route through cool → cold → cool greenhouses exploits thermal
  gradients.
- Inner covers go back on immediately to prevent freeze damage to the fabric.
- Clothing prioritises dexterity (vests, small knives, neoprene gloves).
- Hygiene standards in the wash/pack area are rigorous and non-negotiable.
- Purpose-built equipment (Pac Man mixer, oversized drains) compounds small
  efficiency gains into meaningful time savings.
- Minimising hand travel and demonstrating technique are the primary levers for
  individual harvester productivity.
