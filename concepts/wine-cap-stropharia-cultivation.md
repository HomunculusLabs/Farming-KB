# Wine Cap Stropharia Cultivation

This page provides detailed practical instructions for cultivating Wine Cap mushrooms (Stropharia rugosoannulata) in outdoor wood chip beds. See [[kings-stropharia-garden-giant]] for the species overview, ecology, and nematode trapping biology.

## Wood Chip Selection and Preparation

### Ideal Wood Species

Hardwood chips produce the best results:

- Oak (Quercus spp.): excellent, long-lasting, highest yields
- Maple (Acer spp.): very good, widely available from tree services
- Alder (Alnus spp.): good, decomposes faster than oak
- Poplar (Populus spp.): acceptable, fast decomposition, shorter bed life
- Birch (Betula spp.): good alternative
- Sweetgum: acceptable but chips tend to be stringy

### Wood to Avoid

- Cedar, redwood, juniper: aromatic oils are antifungal
- Black walnut: contains juglone toxic to many fungi
- Eucalyptus: antifungal oils
- Treated or painted wood: chemical contamination
- Plywood or particle board: glues and resins are toxic
- Very old decomposing chips: already colonized by other fungi

### Sourcing Chips

The best source of fresh chips is local tree services:

- Often free or very cheap
- Call and request a drop-off
- Specify hardwood species preference
- Chips should be from recently cut live trees (not dead wood with existing fungal colonization)
- Chips mixed with leaves and small twigs are fine — even beneficial

### Chip Preparation

1. Inspect chips for contaminants (trash, painted wood, undesirable species)
2. Remove any large branches or non-wood debris
3. Optionally soak chips in water for 24-48 hours before bed building
4. Chips should be moist but not dripping wet before installation

Fresh chips from recently felled trees are ideal. Chips that have been sitting in piles for months may already be colonized by competitor fungi. If using older chips, pasteurize by soaking in 160 F water for 1 hour. (see [[query-how-to-make-and-use-leaf-mold-in-your-garden]]).

## Spawn Selection

### Spawn Types

- Grain spawn (on rye or millet): most aggressive, fastest colonization
- Sawdust spawn: slower but more economical for large beds
- Cardboard spawn: budget option; colonize cardboard with mycelium indoors, then transplant

### Sourcing Spawn

Purchase from reputable mushroom supply companies. Wine Cap spawn is widely available in the US and Europe due to its popularity with permaculture growers. Ensure the spawn is fresh (used within 2-3 months of purchase for best results). (see [[shiitake-cultivation-guide]]).

## Bed Installation

### Timing

Best installation times:

- Spring (March-May): allows full growing season for colonization; may fruit same fall
- Fall (September-November): colonization over winter; first fruiting following spring

Fall installation is generally preferred in most climates — the winter months allow slow, thorough colonization without competition from fast-growing summer fungi.

### Step-by-Step Bed Construction

1. Choose and prepare the site (partially shaded, near a water source)
2. Clear ground vegetation; optional: lay cardboard or newspaper as weed barrier
3. Loosen the top 2-4 inches of soil with a garden fork
4. Moisten the soil surface
5. Apply first layer of chips: 2-3 inches deep, evenly spread
6. Crumble and spread spawn evenly over the chip layer
7. Apply second chip layer: 2-3 inches, covering spawn completely
8. Lightly tamp down to ensure chip-spore contact
9. Repeat steps 5-8 for additional layers (2-4 total layers)
10. Finish with a 1-2 inch layer of straw or leaves as moisture-retaining mulch
11. Water thoroughly until the bed is soaked through

### Spawn Rate

- Minimum: 1 lb grain spawn per 20 sq ft
- Recommended: 2-5 lb grain spawn per 20 sq ft
- Higher rates: faster colonization and more reliable fruiting
- Large commercial beds: sawdust spawn at 10% of substrate weight

### Bed Dimensions

- Width: 3-4 feet (comfortable to reach across for maintenance)
- Length: as desired (4-12 feet typical)
- Depth: 6-12 inches of layered substrate
- Spacing between beds: 2-3 feet for walking and airflow

## Bed Maintenance

### Watering

- Critical during the first 2 months while mycelium colonizes
- Keep the bed consistently moist but not waterlogged
- Water deeply 1-2 times per week in the absence of rain
- In hot, dry weather: daily light misting plus weekly deep soaking
- Established beds (after full colonization) are more drought-tolerant

### Mulching

Maintain a 2-3 inch layer of straw or leaf mulch on top:

- Conserves moisture
- Moderates soil temperature
- Suppresses weed germination
- Replace mulch as it decomposes (annually)

### Weeding

- Remove invasive plants that compete for moisture
- Leave beneficial companion plants (comfrey, yarrow, etc.)
- Hand-pull rather than disturb the chip layer with tools

### Monitoring Colonization

- Check under the mulch periodically
- Healthy mycelium: white, cottony threads spreading through chips
- After 2-3 months: should see extensive white mycelium throughout
- If colonization stalls: increase moisture, check for contaminants

## Harvesting

### When to Harvest

- Mushrooms appear 2-4 days after heavy rain or deep watering
- Harvest when caps are fully expanded but still firm
- Young specimens: burgundy-red cap, white gills, firm flesh
- Mature: cap faded to tan, gills purple-black, slightly softer

### How to Harvest

- Cut at the base with a knife or twist gently
- Do not pull — this damages the mycelial network
