### Procedure

1. **Cover the glass**: Use tin foil to cover the mouth of the drinking glass during sterilization and incubation
2. **Minimize disturbance**: The single most important precaution is to disturb the dry vermiculite layer "as little as possible, especially when removing the needle after the inoculation"

### Why It Works

The dry vermiculite creates a physical barrier that is inhospitable to microbial growth. Without moisture or nutrients, airborne contaminant spores that land on the surface cannot germinate or colonize. The syringe needle can pass through this layer to deliver spores to the nutritious substrate below, and the vermiculite closes around the needle track, maintaining the barrier.

## Alcohol Flaming Technique

### Purpose

Sterilizing metal tools (especially syringe needles) between inoculations or after accidental contact is essential for preventing contamination. The alcohol flaming technique provides a clean, soot-free flame using common household materials.

### Equipment

- Tequila shot glass (inverted, to hold the fuel)
- Denatured [[permaculture-on-farm-alcohol-fuel-production]] (available at hardware stores)
- An eyedropper for dispensing fuel
- A match or lighter for ignition

### Procedure

1. **Place the shot glass upside down** on a stable surface
2. **Apply fuel**: Using the eyedropper, put "a few drops of denatured alcohol fuel on the hollow bottom of the glass"
3. **Ignite**: Touch the fuel with a match or lighter
4. **Sterilize**: "The blue flame will cleanly and safely sterilize small stainless steel tools"
5. **Heat the needle**: Hold the syringe needle in the flame for "a few seconds to resterilize it"

### Why Alcohol Over Butane

PF specifically recommends alcohol flame over butane cigarette lighters because "an alcohol flame is a clean flame whereas a butane cigarette lighter leaves behind an undesirable soot residue." This soot can contaminate the needle and potentially introduce foreign particles into the substrate.

### What Happens to Spores During Flaming

A practical concern is whether flaming the needle kills spores inside the syringe. PF addressed this directly: "There might be a few "pops' of boiling water spurt out of the needle, but the spores within the syringe are safe." The brief exposure at the needle tip does not transfer enough heat through the metal to affect spores in the syringe body.

### Storage After Use

"If there is some left over spore solution, replace the needle guard and store the syringe for later use." The syringe should be stored "in a dark, cool place" and the needle "resterilized immediately before re-use."
