## MDA

## Overview

[[mda]] (3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine) is a substituted amphetamine that
occupies a unique position as both a powerful empathogen-entactogen and a
classical psychedelic. Documented in PIHKAL as compound #105, MDA is the
direct precursor and inspiration for [[metzner-psycholytic-psychedelic-therapy-models]]
research. Shulgin's exploration of MDA ultimately led to his discovery of
[[mdma]]'s unique properties, making MDA pivotal in modern empathogen history.

The dual nature of MDA, combining empathogenic and psychedelic qualities,
gives it a character distinct from both MDMA (primarily empathogenic) and
classical psychedelics like LSD (lacking pronounced emotional warmth). This
combination makes MDA simultaneously rewarding and demanding.

## Chemical Structure and Classification

MDA's IUPAC name is 1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)propan-2-amine. It consists of
an amphetamine backbone with a methylenedioxy bridge connecting positions 3
and 4 on the phenyl ring. This bridge is the defining feature of the MDxx
family (MDA, MDMA, [[mdma-serotonergic-neurotoxicity]] potential and
more pronounced psychedelic effects. MDA can also be understood as the
alpha-methylated version of the phenethylamine backbone, though the
substitution pattern differs from [[mescaline]].

## Dosage and Administration

Shulgin's recommended oral dosage is 100 to 160 milligrams, with 100 to 130
mg considered standard. Onset occurs within 30 to 60 minutes, peaking at
approximately 2 hours. Total duration ranges from 8 to 12 hours,
significantly longer than MDMA's 3 to 6 hours.

The extended duration is a key differentiator from MDMA and one reason some
users prefer MDMA for recreational settings. The comedown can be more
pronounced, with residual stimulation and emotional fatigue lasting well
beyond primary effects.

## Effects Profile

MDA's empathogenic effects include profound emotional openness, enhanced
tactile sensation, feelings of love and connection, and increased
sociability. These are similar to MDMA but often described as deeper and
more "organic."

The psychedelic component is more pronounced than with MDMA. Visual effects
include color enhancement, geometric patterning, and genuine hallucinations
at higher doses. Cognitive effects include altered thought patterns,
philosophical introspection, and enhanced creativity. The headspace is more
"trippy" and less clear-headed than MDMA, with greater potential for ego
dissolution.

Physical effects include pronounced pupil dilation, elevated heart rate,
jaw clenching, significant stimulation, and heavier body load compared to
MDMA. Nausea during onset is possible at higher doses.

## Shulgin's Commentary

Shulgin's relationship with MDA was foundational to his career. His
exploration of MDA in the 1960s led him to investigate related compounds and
ultimately synthesize MDMA in 1976. In PIHKAL, Shulgin described MDA with
both affection and respect, noting its more "psychedelic" character compared
to MDMA, with greater visual activity and a less clear-headed mental state.

He described it as capable of genuinely transformative experiences while
recognizing greater potential for challenging experiences compared to MDMA.
He also acknowledged emerging neurotoxicity concerns while maintaining that
responsible use at reasonable doses posed minimal risk.
