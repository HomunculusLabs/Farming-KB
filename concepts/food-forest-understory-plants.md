# Food Forest Understory Plants

In nature, forests demonstrate vertical stacking where different species occupy every level from canopy to forest floor. Permaculture food forests mimic this pattern with carefully planned layers of productive plants. The understory -- the layer beneath the canopy -- is critical for overall system productivity, providing food, nitrogen fixation, pest control, and microclimate regulation.

## The Stacking Principle

The interface between two ecosystems (forest and grassland, land and water) is always richer in species than either system alone. These edges are habitat to species from both ecosystems plus species specific to the edge. Permaculture uses this edge effect in designs to maximise productivity.

In a food forest, stacking includes:
- **Canopy trees**: Large nut and fruit trees (walnut, oak, chestnut)
- **Sub-canopy trees**: Smaller fruit trees (apple, plum, persimmon, citrus)
- **Shrub layer**: Berry bushes, nitrogen-fixing shrubs
- **Herbaceous layer**: Herbs, perennial vegetables, ground covers
- **Root layer**: Root crops, tubers
- **Climbing/vine layer**: Passionfruit, kiwi, grapes
- **Ground cover layer**: Suppresses weeds, retains moisture

## Understorey Trees and Shrubs

### Tagasaste (Chamaecytisus palmensis)
The premier pioneer/nurse tree for establishing food forests. Fast-growing nitrogen fixer that provides shade, shelter, mulch from leaf litter, and poultry forage from abundant seed. Acts as a nurse tree for longer-term species, which eventually crowd it out. Staggered cutting increases flowering duration for bee forage.

### Loquat (Eriobotrya japonica)
Occurs naturally as a thicket understorey in open woodland. In dryer areas may only reach 3 m. Recommended planting distance about 5 m, but can be grown as an understorey to larger trees. Edible fruit, leaves used in Chinese cooking, bark and wood are medicinal.

### Cotoneaster spp
Evergreen and deciduous shrubs providing poultry forage and honey. Many species suitable for the shrub layer.

### Dogwood (Cornus capitata)
Evergreen large shrub suitable for the understorey. Provides habitat for insectivorous birds.

### Lillipillies (Eugenia spp, Acmena spp, Syzygium spp)
Australian native shrubs and small trees well suited to the understorey. Many produce edible fruit. Range from shrubs to trees reaching 30 m depending on species.

### Fuchsia spp
Many species of this genus make excellent understorey plants in temperate food forests. Edible flowers and fruit.

### Blueberries (Vaccinium spp)
Shrubs well adapted to the understorey layer, especially in acidic soils. Valuable edible fruit.

### Ugni (Ugni molinaea)
Small evergreen shrub producing aromatic, tasty berries. Well suited to understorey conditions.

### Feijoa (Feijoa sellowiana)
Small evergreen tree/shrub to 6 m. Can function as sub-canopy or understorey. Good companion to citrus, tagasaste, raspberries, and garlic. Edible fruit with pineapple-mint flavour.

### Hackberry (Celtis australis)
Medium tree to 20 m, but can be managed smaller. Edible sweet fruit. Occurs naturally as scattered trees in open forest.

### Acacia melanoxylon (Blackwood)
Occurs in the understorey of tall, open forest dominated by eucalypts. Can form dense thickets. As a large tree it grows as understorey to Eucalyptus regnans, E. obliqua, and E. viminalis. Premier cabinet timber.

## Herbaceous Understorey Plants

### Comfrey (Symphytum spp)
Deep-rooted perennial that mines minerals from subsoil. Leaves high in protein and potassium, used as mulch and compost activator. Valuable fodder for poultry and livestock. See [[medicinal-plant-guide]] for medicinal uses.

### Chicory (Cichorium intybus)
Deep-rooted perennial providing both nectar and pollen for bees. Medicinal herb for livestock. Drought tolerant once established.

### Ribwort Plantain (Plantago lanceolata)
Common perennial herb providing good poultry forage and bee forage. Medicinal properties.

### Nasturtiums (Tropaeolum spp)
Excellent companion plant for apples, cabbages, cauliflowers, and many other crops. Edible leaves and flowers with peppery flavour. Attracts beneficial insects.

### Mint (Mentha spp)
Spreading herbs well suited to moist understorey conditions. Bee forage. Medicinal and aromatic. Grow in containers to prevent excessive spreading.

### Borage (Borago officinalis)
Excellent companion for strawberries. Long flowering season provides bee forage. Edible flowers.

### Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale)
Deep-rooted perennial. Long season bee forage. Edible leaves and flowers. Medicinal for livestock.

## Nitrogen-Fixing Understorey

### Albizia lophantha (Crested Wattle)
At home either as an understorey species or as a major species. Fast-growing nitrogen fixer, useful as a nurse tree.

### Acacia pulchella
Valued companion for avocados, Chinese chestnuts, and macadamias in the understorey.

### Lupinus arboreus (Tree Lupin)
Nitrogen-fixing pioneer shrub. See [[permaculture-plant-selection]] for details.

### Lespedeza bicolor (Miracle Plant)
Nitrogen-fixing shrub providing browse, erosion control, and wildlife habitat.

## Shade-Tolerant Food Plants

Many useful plants thrive in partial shade beneath canopy trees:

- **Sweet chestnut**: Can be grown as an understorey to larger trees (planting distance ~5 m)
- **Fuchsia**: Naturally adapted to shaded conditions
- **Blueberries**: Tolerate partial shade, especially in warmer climates
- **Mint**: Prefers moist, partly shaded conditions
- **Hostas**: Shade-tolerant edible foliage
- **Bamboo**: Many species tolerate partial shade, especially running types
- **Wasabia japonica** (wasabi): Requires shade and flowing water
- **Many ferns**: Edible fiddleheads from shade-tolerant species

## Understorey Design Principles

- Use [[zone-and-sector-analysis]] to place understorey plants where they receive appropriate light levels
- Layer nitrogen-fixing species throughout the understorey to maintain soil fertility without external inputs
- Include [[mollison-designers-insectary-plants-and-beneficial-insect-habitat]] in the understorey to support biological pest control
- Design corridors through the food forest to allow insect-eating birds access to all layers
- Use [[chop-and-drop-mulching]] with understorey prunings to build soil organic matter
- Include [[gaias-garden-living-mulches-ground-covers]] to suppress weeds and retain moisture
- Plan for succession: pioneer understorey species (tagasaste, tree lupin) will eventually be replaced by longer-term species
- Combine with [[garden-care-and-succession-planting]] principles for synergistic plant relationships
