## Beyond Cultural Boundaries

Fukuoka"s travels convinced him that the principles of natural farming were recognized in every culture. Traditional farming systems in India, Africa, Europe, and the Americas all contained elements of natural farming -- mixed cropping, composting, [[coleman-deep-organic-farming-philosophy]], sustainable agriculture, and conservation agriculture all fall into this category.

Mahayana farming is the path of sudden enlightenment. It recognizes that nature is perfect and that all human intervention is unnecessary. It seeks not to improve farming but to transcend farming entirely -- to reach a state where the farmer does nothing and nature does everything.

Fukuoka placed himself in the Mahayana tradition. His goal was not to make farming more efficient or more sustainable but to demonstrate that farming, in the conventional sense, is unnecessary.

## Genetic Engineering and the Limits of Science

Fukuoka was particularly critical of genetic engineering, which he saw as the ultimate expression of scientific arrogance. "Organisms created through genetic manipulation are imperfect creatures caught within the domain of the concepts of space and time."

Because science works within the framework of space and time, it can never create anything that transcends this framework. It can rearrange existing genetic material but cannot create life itself. The living cell, with all its complexity and self-organization, remains beyond the reach of human understanding and manipulation.
