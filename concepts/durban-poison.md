# Durban Poison

Durban Poison is one of the most important and influential pure sativa landrace strains in cannabis genetics. Originating from the city of Durban, South Africa, located approximately 30 degrees south latitude, this strain has played a pivotal role in cannabis breeding worldwide, particularly in the development of early-finishing sativa hybrids suited for northern hemisphere cultivation.



## Geographic Origin and Significance

Durban Poison originates from Durban, South Africa, a coastal city positioned at roughly 30 degrees south latitude. This geographic positioning is critical to understanding the strain's unique flowering characteristics. When grown in the northern hemisphere, Durban Poison begins flowering outdoors in late June and finishes in the second half of September, well before coastal fog or autumn rain becomes problematic. This early finishing trait proved revolutionary for outdoor growers in temperate climates, as most pure sativas typically require 14 to 16 weeks or more of flowering.

The "magic" of Durban Poison's photoperiod response lies in its equatorial adaptation. Plants indigenous to approximately 30 degrees south latitude carry dominant early-flowering genetics that persist strongly in hybrids, making Durban Poison an invaluable breeding tool for reducing flowering time in sativa-dominant crosses.



## Role in Cannabis Breeding History

Before widespread indoor cultivation became common, outdoor breeding in the late 1970s and early 1980s relied on Durban Poison to address the long flowering times of pure sativa strains. Northern California growers, facing the risk of mold and weather damage from late-finishing plants, used Durban Poison as a hybridization partner to create faster-finishing sativa-dominant varieties. The strain was introduced to these growers through pro surfers who traveled to South Africa and brought genetics back.

Durban Poison became one of the most sought-after clones in California during the early 1980s. Its genetics were used extensively to create numerous hybrid lines, and its influence can be found in countless modern cannabis strains either directly or through its progeny.



## Botanical Characteristics

Durban Poison grows in a classic Christmas tree structure typical of pure sativas, with strong vertical growth and well-defined apical dominance. The plant tends to be tall and produces elongated colas. Key morphological features include:

- **Leaf structure**: Thin, serrated sativa-type leaflets with narrow finger-like projections
- **Growth pattern**: Tall, upright Christmas tree shape with moderate lateral branching
- **Bud structure**: Medium-dense, elongated colas with generous resin production
- **Coloration**: Deep green with occasional hints of purple in cooler temperatures



## Cultivation Notes

Durban Poison is considered a relatively easy sativa to grow compared to other pure landrace varieties. Its early finishing time makes it one of the most forgiving sativas for outdoor cultivation in temperate regions. The strain performs well in both indoor and outdoor environments, though its tall stature requires management techniques such as Low Stress Training or topping for indoor grows.

The plant is generally resilient and resistant to many common cannabis pests and molds, partially owing to its landrace heritage and adaptation to diverse South African growing conditions. It responds well to organic cultivation methods and does not require heavy nutrient feeding programs.



## Notable Durban Poison Hybrids

The influence of Durban Poison extends through numerous hybrid strains:

- **Early Durban Gold #1**: A cross of Durban Poison with Early Girl, further bred with Guerilla Gold #5, creating a compact but powerful plant
- **OGRE**: An auto-flowering cross combining Durban Skunk (a Durban Poison hybrid) with Secret Citrus auto-flowering genetics
- **Peptide**: Combines Durban Poison with Heavy Duty Fruity and Sweet Tooth #3 genetics
- **Durban Skunk**: A foundational cross of Durban Poison with Skunk #1 that has been used in numerous further breeding programs



## Terpene Profile and Effects

Durban Poison is known for its sharp, sweet, and piney aroma with licorice undertones. The flavor profile is similarly complex, offering a blend of sweetness and earthiness with an anise-like quality. The effects are classically sativa: an uplifting, cerebral, and energetic high that provides mental clarity and focus. The strain's resin profile delivers what enthusiasts describe as a "rollercoaster" experience, with the high building progressively and lasting several hours.



## Cultivation Characteristics

Durban Poison is known for its relatively short flowering time for a pure sativa, typically completing its bloom cycle in 8-9 weeks when grown indoors. Outdoor plants can reach impressive heights of 2-3 meters, with long inter-nodal spacing characteristic of equatorial sativa genetics. The strain is considered moderately difficult to cultivate, requiring attention to stretching during the early flowering phase.



## Terpene and Resin Profile

The dominant terpenes in Durban Poison include high concentrations of [[myrcene]], [[ocimene]], and terpinolene, contributing to its sweet, anise-like aroma with subtle earthy undertones. The resin production is exceptionally heavy for a landrace variety, making it a popular choice for hashish and extract production.



## Breeding Influence

Durban Poison has been used as a foundational parent in numerous hybrid breeding programs. Its genetics appear in crosses with [[afghan-kush]] and various indica lines, contributing energetic sativa effects and reduced flowering times. The strain's stable, pure sativa profile makes it valuable for breeders seeking to introduce equatorial genetics into their programs.
