# Neem and Indian Beech as Permaculture Utility Trees

Neem (Azadirachta indica) and Indian beech (Derris indica, also known
as pongam or karanja) are two of the most valuable arid-zone trees for
permaculture systems. Both offer extraordinary utility spanning insect
control, medicine, timber, fuel, soil improvement, and livestock
integration. They are essential species for [[permaculture-food-forests-design-course]]
designs in tropical and subtropical drylands.

## Neem (Azadirachta indica)

### Profile

- **Family**: Meliaceae
- **Form**: Deep-rooted broad-leaved, usually evergreen
- **Origin**: Dry forests of India through Burma
- **Rainfall**: 130-1150 mm average annual (optimal 450-1150 mm)
- **Frost tolerance**: Not frost-hardy
- **Soil pH**: Optimum 6.2; tolerates pH 5.0; leaf litter brings
  soil toward neutral

### Adaptability

Neem tolerates long dry seasons and extreme heat. It grows on most
soil types including dry, stony, clay, and shallow soils, but will not
tolerate seasonally waterlogged soils (taproot rots) or deep dry sands
where the water table lies below 18m. Its roots extract nutrients from
even the most leached sandy soils. Not saline-tolerant.

### Insect Control Properties

Seeds and leaves yield azadirachtin, a powerful natural insecticide. In
India, dried leaves are placed in books, grain stores, and clothing to
repel insects. This makes neem invaluable in [[permaculture-plant-guilds]]
where integrated pest management replaces chemical inputs. Neem-based
preparations complement [[jadam-natural-pesticide-jnp]] and
[[cho-natural-pest-control-methods]] approaches.

### Medicinal Uses

Neem has an extensive pharmacopeia in traditional Indian medicine:

- **Twigs**: Used as toothbrushes (antibacterial)
- **Bark decoction**: Fevers, nausea, liver complaints, jaundice
- **Bark poultice**: Wounds and skin diseases
- **Bark oil**: Rub for rheumatism
- **Fruit**: Urinary complaints, piles, worms
- **Seed oil**: Skin disease treatment

### Timber and Fuel

Neem wood resembles Cuban mahogany, resists decay and insects, and is
tougher than teak. It coppices readily and produces straight, strong
poles for construction and furniture. Termite attack is rare. As
firewood, it has nearly the calorific value of coal, making it an
exceptional [[biochar]] feedstock. Propagation is by seed with short
viability of only 2-3 weeks.

## Indian Beech (Derris indica)

### Profile

- **Family**: Fabaceae (leguminous)
- **Form**: Medium deciduous thornless tree to 8m
- **Origin**: India, now widely distributed
- **Growth rate**: Fast, mature height in 4-5 years
- **Rainfall**: 500-2500 mm
- **Temperature range**: Below 0 degrees C to 50 degrees C
- **Altitude**: Sea level to 1200 m
- **Salt tolerance**: Highly tolerant, even roots in salt water

### Salt Tolerance and Arid Adaptation

Indian beech thrives on sandy and rocky soils, limestone, and saline
conditions where few other trees survive. Its ability to grow with
roots in salt water makes it a premier [[salt-tolerant-fodder-plants]]
species. Grasses grow well in its shade, and the foliage is likely high
in protein for livestock integration.

### Oil Production

Seeds contain 30-40% yellow to red-brown oil, not edible but useful as:

- Lubricant and kerosene lamp fuel
- Leather dressing and soap manufacturing
- Varnishes and paints
- Antiseptic for human and animal skin diseases

### Pest Control

Dried leaves repel insects, used to protect stored grain. Ploughed
into soil as green manure, they repel nematodes. Presscake after oil
extraction serves as insecticide and effective poultry feed.

### Other Products

- **Bark fibre**: Very strong rope
- **Wood**: Good firewood (4600 kcal/kg), cabinet-making, posts
- **Litter**: Abundant leaf, flower, and pod drop for mulching

### Propagation

Seeds remain viable for long periods. Tree grows readily from cuttings
of all sizes. Seedlings transplant easily. Extensive surface roots
produce suckers freely for natural regeneration and erosion control.

## Companion Planting

In India, neem is planted in sacred groves as a companion to mango and
Ficus religiosa. Both neem and Indian beech function as
[[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] species that protect adjacent crops through
insect repellency and soil improvement.
