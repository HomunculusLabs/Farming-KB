## Spagyric Cannabis Preparation

Spagyric preparation is an alchemical herbal processing method that aims to
produce a more complete and potent extract than conventional extraction. When
applied to cannabis, spagyric techniques seek to capture not only the plant's
volatile oils and resins but also its mineral salts, creating what
practitioners believe is a holistically superior preparation.

## Principles of Spagyric Processing

The word "spagyric" derives from Greek roots meaning "to separate" and "to
recombine" (spao and ageiro). This encapsulates the core methodology: separate
the plant into its three philosophical principles (sulfur, mercury, and salt),
purify each, then recombine them into a unified whole.

In practical terms, spagyric processing involves:

1. **Extraction**: Separating the plant's soluble compounds ([[comparison-steam-distillation-vs-co2-extraction-for-essential-oils]], cannabinoids, and terpenes
  representing the plant's unique identity [[cervantes-cannabis-flower-anatomy]] material is macerated in high-proof ethanol for several weeks.
The alcohol dissolves cannabinoids, terpenes, and other soluble compounds. The
maceration may be enhanced with gentle warmth or periodic agitation.

**Step 2: Filtration and Separation**
The ethanol extract is filtered from the plant material. The plant marc
(residual plant matter) is set aside for calcination. The ethanol may be
partially evaporated to concentrate the extract.

**Step 3: Calcination**
The plant marc is thoroughly dried, then burned in a fire or kiln until reduced
to white or gray ash. This step breaks down [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]].

**Step 4: Lixiviation and Purification**
The ash is dissolved in distilled water and filtered repeatedly to remove
insoluble carbon and other impurities. The resulting mineral-rich solution is
evaporated slowly, often in sunlight according to traditional practice, to
produce crystalline salt.

**Step 5: Recombination**
The purified mineral salts are dissolved in a small amount of water and added
back into the ethanol extract. The final preparation may be further aged or
potentiated according to spagyric tradition.
