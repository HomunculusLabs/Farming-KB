## Urban Guerrilla Gardening

## Overview

Urban guerrilla gardening is the practice of cultivating food, native plants, and productive ecosystems on neglected, abandoned, or unauthorized urban land. It combines [[permaculture-design-principles]] with direct action to reclaim concrete-dominated cityscapes for food production, ecological restoration, and community building. The term encompasses a spectrum from seed-bombing vacant lots to systematic reclamation of abandoned properties with full garden installations.

## Philosophy and Motivation

Core drivers from practitioner accounts:

- **Food sovereignty**: "We need to start growing food where we live and reclaiming all this knowledge for ourselves and future generations"
- **Anti-corporate resistance**: Rejecting globalized food systems where "big corporations control the most basic aspect of our lives"
- **Ecological restoration**: Bringing "the real world into the heart of the city" through native plants that survive on local rainfall
- **Intergenerational knowledge transfer**: Teaching children "there's more to life than concrete and hate and fear"
- **Cultural bridge**: "Dealing with living systems that change over time" — art as much as agriculture

## Key Techniques

### Abandoned Lot Reclamation

- Identify abandoned rubble lots, pastures, and disturbed urban land
- These sites follow natural succession patterns — pioneer species colonize first
- Remove pavement from low spots in asphalt/concrete lots and plant trees there
- The runoff from remaining pavement waters the trees passively
- "Bust up your driveway and divert the water from the street to the street trees"
- In sloping parking lots: mark contours, remove 3-foot asphalt strips along contour lines, plant native trees

### Raised Beds in Poor Soils

- Build raised vegetable beds "out of sticks and blackberries" even in "really crappy soil"
- Use salvaged materials: bathtubs, tires, milk crates, refrigerators
- [[sheet-mulching|Sheet mulching]] with newspapers (6 pickup loads), leaf mulch, and straw
- Sunk planting basins: dig into hard subsoil (caliche), fill with compost and salvaged manure
- Grade surrounding soil to drain all rainwater into garden beds

### Salvage-Based Construction

- Dumpster-dive for bathtubs, tires, milk crates, refrigerators, chain-link fencing
- Trade salvage materials (e.g., chain-link fence traded for used 2x4s)
- Salvage election signs, plywood, carport lumber for construction
- Use ocotillo canes and agave flower stalks for living fences
- Salvage cacti (saguaro, barrel, cholla) from areas slated for clearing

### Grafting Urban Fruit Trees

- Graft scion wood of good [[fruit-tree-varieties]] onto crabapples in alleyways
- Graft onto non-fruiting cherries, peaches, and plums in parks
- T-bud graft fruit trees and root semi-hardwood cuttings in tins of wet sand
- Save diverse vegetable, flower, and herb seeds
- "Come back later for the harvest" — fruit tree grafting as low-maintenance food production

### Native Plant Guerrilla Gardens

- Plant native species that survive on local rainfall alone
- Native plants provide: medicinal value, food, wildlife habitat, bee forage, nitrogen fixation
- They "bring the real world into the heart of the city"
- Create 8-10 foot wide buffer zones of native plants for noise, pollution, light, and privacy screening

## Urban Water Harvesting

### Passive Systems

- **55-gallon drums** from bakeries, linked with PVC pipe for gravity-fed irrigation
- **Ferrocement cisterns**: 1200-gallon cistern for under $20 using remesh, plastic, and carpet
- **Tub water recovery**: 1/2-inch vinyl tube through window jam, suction-cupped to tub bottom, hand-pump siphon to fruit trees
- **Gutter diversion**: Route rainwater to cisterns and planting basins
- **Thermal mass benefit**: Water tanks trap solar heat by day and radiate it at night, protecting citrus and tomatoes from frost

### Greywater Systems

- Pond-and-slope systems for greywater filtration and garden irrigation
- "The water loop is extended from the home through the ponds and to the garden"
- If all water caught in cisterns, the loop is complete

### Street Runoff Capture

- Mark contours on sloping parking lots
- Remove asphalt strips along contour lines; plant in the low spots
- Street runoff waters guerrilla-planted trees
- Swales in urban parks to infiltrate stormwater

## Urban Microclimate Strategies

- **Deciduous vine trellises**: Edible annuals (squash) shade buildings in summer, die back for solar gain in winter
- **Living fences**: Ocotillo canes + agave stalks create wildlife habitat and privacy screens
- **Noise/pollution buffers**: 8-10 ft native plantings along property edges
- **Rock walls**: Local granite retaining walls create microclimates and act as heat banks
- **Shade cloth**: Over planting beds dug in caliche for temperature moderation

## Community Building

- "Urban permaculture is intensely social — everything you do is within sight, sound, or touch of a neighbor"
- Start with visible, friendly activity — wave at passersby
- Neighbors contribute: physical help, security, advice, plants, hot meals, materials
- School gardens: permaculture gardens at middle schools build community across teachers, bus drivers, and parents
- Potlucks, workshops, and presentations for knowledge sharing
- Stream rehabilitation as community project
- "In fifty years, this coalition building will be a necessity. Now it is a choice"

## Soil Building in Urban Contexts

- Compost from restaurant and kitchen organic waste
- Worm composting: "catch the worms and let them turn the soil"
- "Composting creates rich soil from trash"
- Manure from local stables mixed with yard prunings
- Leaf mold from municipal leaf collection (6+ pickup loads)
- Green manure crops and cover cropping

## Broadscale Urban Restoration

- **Urban woodlots** around industrial zones: filter air pollution, produce oxygen, create bird/animal habitat
- **Parks as food forests**: Fruit trees and berries in public parks
- **Stream rehabilitation**: Plant native species along urban waterways; within two years vegetation grows lush, water temperature drops, aquatic life returns
- **Rooftop gardens**: Tomatoes, herbs, and vegetables on building roofs
- **City-country fingers**: Christopher Alexander's pattern — urban fingers never more than 1 mile wide, farmland fingers never less than 1 mile wide
