## Definition of Eloptic Energy

As Hieronymus continued experimenting, he grew convinced the energy
modulated by his devices had little to do with electromagnetism. This
became a certainty when he found the device itself was short-circuited
if bathed in light rays from the sun, just as electrical circuits in a
radio are shorted by being plunged into water.

He designed a special analyzer with lenses and a prism by which he
could identify many elements from the radiations they emitted. The
energy, when refracted through a prism, behaved like light except the
angles of refraction were much more acute, and the energy from various
elements came through at angles in the same order as the contents of
their nuclei.

Hieronymus coined the term "eloptic energy" -- "Energy obeying some of
the laws of electricity but not all of them, and some of the laws of
optics, but not all of them." He concluded it was independent of but
somehow affiliated with electromagnetic energy, and might be the same
as what physicists once described as "the ether put in action at
higher harmonics than so far experienced."

## Key Properties of Eloptic Energy

Unlike electromagnetic energy, eloptic energy does not attenuate
inversely as the square of the distance from its source. It radiates
only a certain distance depending on the object from which it is
emitted, the direction it takes, and even the time of day of
measurement. Something varies the amount of radiation emitted in the
same way that fog or smoke varies the intensity of light.
