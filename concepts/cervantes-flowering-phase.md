## Cannabis Flowering Phase Management

**Source:** Marijuana Horticulture: The Indoor/Outdoor Medical Grower's Bible — [[clarke-marijuana-botany-cannabinoid-biosynthesis-pathway]]* as "primordial," are the first indication of a plant's sex. The pre-flowers grow at branch internodes just behind the leaf scar or stipule about the fourth week of vegetative growth, when the plant is six to eight weeks old. This is the point of sexual maturity — the first sign a plant is preparing for flowering.

Pre-flowers can be seen with the naked eye, but a 10x to 30x magnifier makes viewing easier. You can accurately determine plant sex after eight weeks. Using this method, you can distinguish sex before inducing flowering.

**Male pre-flowers** are normally visible when plants are six to eight weeks old, emerging behind the stipule at the fourth to fifth branch internodes. They generally do not turn into full flowers but remain as indicators of sex.

**Female pre-flowers** emerge at the same time and location but have two tiny hair-like pistils that protrude from an immature calyx. These pistils are the definitive marker of a female plant.

## Photoperiod Disruption and Hermaphroditism

Inducing flowering in cannabis grown from seed with a 12/12 day/night photoperiod will cause plants to show sex. Once the sex is guaranteed, males are almost always harvested before they shed pollen, and females are coaxed into higher yields. Once the photoperiod is set, disrupting it will cause plants to suffer stress. If they suffer enough stress, hermaphrodite tendencies increase.

Tropical strains often start flowering under more light and less darkness than 12/12, reflecting their equatorial origins where day length varies less seasonally.

## Pollination and Seed Production

Upon pollination, a grain of male pollen from the staminate flower pod lands on a pistil of the pistillate flower. Female flower tops are a mass of calyxes with each calyx harboring an ovule and a protruding set of pistils. Actual fertilization takes place when the grain of male pollen slides down the pistil and unites with the female ovule deep within the female calyx. Once fertilization takes place, pistils turn brown and a seed forms within the seed bract.

During vegetative growth, plants require higher levels of nitrogen (N) to support leaf and stem development. During flowering, phosphorus (P) and potassium (K) demand increases substantially as the plant redirects energy toward bud production. A common flowering nutrient ratio is N-P-K 5-15-15 or 0-20-20, though specific formulations vary by manufacturer.

Key nutrient considerations during flowering:
- **Nitrogen**: Reduce by half or more compared to vegetative levels. Excess nitrogen during late flowering can produce harsh-tasting, poorly burning buds and delay maturation.
- **Phosphorus**: Critical for flower development and resin production. Increase when switching to 12/12 photoperiod.
- **Potassium**: Supports overall plant health, water regulation, and bud density. Important throughout flowering.
- **Calcium and Magnesium**: Often supplemented separately (Cal-Mag products) to prevent deficiencies that manifest as leaf spots or tip burn.
- **Micronutrients**: Iron, zinc, manganese, and boron remain important but are needed in trace amounts.

Flushing — giving plants plain water (no nutrients) for the final 1-2 weeks before harvest — is a common practice intended to improve flavor and smoothness by allowing the plant to use stored nutrients in the foliage.

## Trichome Development and Harvest Timing

Trichomes are the resin glands that contain cannabinoids (THC, CBD, etc.) and terpenes. They appear as tiny mushroom-shaped structures on bud surfaces and sugar leaves. Trichome appearance is the most reliable indicator of harvest readiness:

- **Clear trichomes**: THC is still developing. Harvesting now produces a more cerebral, energetic high but yields less potent buds.
- **Cloudy/milky trichomes**: Peak THC production. Most growers harvest at this stage for maximum potency.
- **Amber trichomes**: THC is degrading into CBN (cannabinol), which produces a more sedative body high. A mix of cloudy and amber trichomes is preferred by many growers for balanced effects.

A 30x to 60x jeweler's loupe or pocket microscope is essential for accurate trichome assessment. Harvesting too early sacrifices potency; harvesting too late produces a less desirable effect profile.
