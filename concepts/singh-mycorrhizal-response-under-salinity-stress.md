# Mycorrhizal Response Under Salinity Stress

Soil salinity affects approximately one-third of irrigated agricultural land
worldwide, causing billions of dollars in annual crop losses. Arbuscular
[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]] (AMF) improve plant performance under saline conditions
through a combination of nutritional, physiological, and biochemical
mechanisms that collectively enhance salt tolerance in host plants.

## Salt Tolerance Mechanisms

Salinity stress imposes two primary challenges on plants: osmotic stress from
reduced water potential, and ionic toxicity from excessive Na+ and Cl-
accumulation. [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]] addresses both challenges through
multiple complementary mechanisms:

- **Enhanced nutrient acquisition**: AMF improve phosphorus, potassium, calcium,
  and micronutrient uptake under saline conditions where these nutrients become
  less available. The improved K+/Na+ ratio in mycorrhizal plants is
  particularly important, as [[knf-potassium-deficiency-and-excess]] under salt stress contributes
  to growth inhibition.
- **Selective ion uptake**: AMF hyphae preferentially absorb K+ over Na+, and
  the fungal interface may partially exclude Na+ from the plant symplast. The
  arbuscular interface within root cortical cells regulates ion transfer,
  acting as a selective barrier.
- **Compartmentalization**: Na+ absorbed by extraradical hyphae can be
  sequestered in fungal vacuoles or bound to cell wall components, preventing
  translocation to host plant tissues. This mechanism parallels metal
  sequestration described in singh [[micro-pixe-analysis-mycorrhizal-metal-tolerant-plants]] translocation and   sequestration.

## Osmotic Adjustment

Mycorrhizal plants maintain higher tissue water content and turgor under
saline conditions through enhanced osmotic adjustment. AMF contribute to
accumulation of compatible solutes including:

- **Proline**: Mycorrhizal colonization often increases proline accumulation in
  host tissues under salt stress. Proline serves as an osmoprotectant,
  ROS scavenger, and protein stabilizer.
- **Glycine betaine**: Some AMF associations enhance glycine betaine
  accumulation, which protects photosynthetic apparatus and enzyme function
  under osmotic stress.
- **Sugars and sugar alcohols**: Soluble sugars (glucose, fructose, sucrose)
  and polyols (mannitol, trehalose) contribute to osmotic adjustment.
  Trehalose produced by the fungal partner may have dual roles as osmoprotectant
  and signaling molecule.
- **Organic acids**: Malate, citrate, and oxalate contribute to osmotic
  balance and also chelate metal cations in saline-alkaline soils.

## Ion Homeostasis

AMF modulate ion transport systems in host roots to maintain favorable ionic
ratios under saline conditions. Mycorrhizal plants typically show:

- Higher K+ concentrations and lower Na+ concentrations in shoots compared to
  non-mycorrhizal controls.
- Upregulated expression of high-affinity K+ transporters (HKT, AKT families)
  in host roots, improving K+ acquisition under competitive Na+ conditions.
- Enhanced expression and activity of plasma membrane H+-ATPases, which
  generate the electrochemical gradient driving K+ uptake and Na+ exclusion.
- Improved Ca2+ nutrition, which stabilizes cell membranes and signaling
  pathways disrupted by Na+ toxicity.

## Antioxidant Defense Enhancement

Salinity stress generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) including superoxide
radicals, [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]], and hydroxyl radicals that damage lipids, proteins,
and nucleic acids. Mycorrhizal plants exhibit enhanced antioxidant defense
systems:

- **Enzymatic antioxidants**: Higher activities of superoxide dismutase (SOD),
  catalase (CAT), ascorbate peroxidase (APX), glutathione reductase (GR), and
  guaiacol peroxidase (GPX) in mycorrhizal plants under salt stress. These
  enzymes work in concert to scavenge ROS through sequential reactions.
- **Non-enzymatic antioxidants**: Elevated levels of ascorbate, glutathione,
  alpha-tocopherol, carotenoids, and phenolic compounds in mycorrhizal tissues.
  The ascorbate-glutathione cycle is particularly important for H2O2
  detoxification.
- **Reduced lipid peroxidation**: Lower malondialdehyde (MDA) levels in
  mycorrhizal plants indicate reduced oxidative damage to membrane lipids.

## AMF Inoculation for Saline Agriculture

Field application of AMF inoculation for saline soil management involves
several considerations:

- **Strain selection**: Not all AMF species are equally effective under saline
  conditions. Glomus intraradices (now Rhizophagus irregularis), Glomus
  mosseae (Funneliformis mosseae), and Glomus etunicatum (Claroideoglomus
  etunicatum) have shown consistent salinity tolerance and plant growth
  promotion.
- **Inoculum preparation**: AMF inocula are produced using pot cultures with
  trap plants, incorporating soil containing spores, hyphae, and colonized
  root fragments. See [[singh-composted-fungal-biomass-heavy-metal-immobilization]] amendments improves
  establishment and efficacy by providing organic matter, buffering capacity,
  and additional nutrient sources.
- **Host plant compatibility**: The effectiveness of mycorrhizal salinity
  mitigation varies with host plant species and cultivar. Plants with
  obligate mycorrhizal associations typically benefit more than facultative or
  non-host species.

## Molecular Signaling in Salinity Response

Mycorrhiza-induced salt tolerance involves complex signaling networks including
calcium-dependent protein kinases (CDPKs), mitogen-activated protein kinases
(MAPKs), and hormone signaling through abscisic acid (ABA), jasmonic acid
(JA), and salicylic acid (SA). AMF colonization modulates expression of salt
stress-responsive genes (SOS pathway, NHX antiporters, LEA proteins) in host
plants, representing a form of [[singh-mycorrhizal-fungi-in-heavy-metal-phytostabilization]]
