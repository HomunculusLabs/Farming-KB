# Abscisic Acid (ABA): Plant Stress Hormone and Dormancy Regulator

Abscisic acid (ABA) is a crucial plant hormone (phytohormone) that plays a central role in plant responses to abiotic stress, particularly drought and salinity, and in regulating [[bloomfield-turgor-pressure-and-hyphal-invasion]]
- Reduced turgor causes guard cells to deflate and stomatal pores to close

This entire process can occur within minutes of ABA perception. ABA also promotes stomatal closure through a secondary pathway involving elevated cytosolic Ca²⁺ levels, which activate additional anion channels.

Long-term ABA responses to drought include:
- Upregulation of genes encoding dehydrins and other LEA (Late Embryogenesis Abundant) proteins
- Accumulation of compatible solutes (proline, glycine betaine, trehalose)
- Increased root-to-shoot ratio
- Modification of root [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]] (ROS) as secondary messengers

## Seed Dormancy and Germination

ABA is the primary hormone maintaining seed dormancy and inhibiting germination. During seed maturation, ABA levels peak and contribute to:

- Induction of dormancy in developing seeds
- Accumulation of seed storage proteins and lipids
- Acquisition of desiccation tolerance (anhydrobiosis)
- Synthesis of LEA proteins that protect cellular structures during drying

The balance between ABA and gibberellin (GA) is the key determinant of whether a seed remains dormant or germinates. ABA promotes dormancy while GA promotes germination. The transition from dormancy to germination involves:

1. After-ripening or stratification reduces seed ABA sensitivity
2. Imbibition triggers GA biosynthesis in the embryo
3. GA promotes the production of α-amylase and other hydrolytic enzymes in the aleurone layer
4. These enzymes mobilize stored reserves (starch) to fuel germination
5. ABA signaling through ABI3, ABI5, and other transcription factors must be overcome for germination to proceed

In some species, dormant seeds maintain elevated ABA levels that must decline before germination can occur. In others, ABA levels remain constant but sensitivity to ABA decreases during after-ripening.
