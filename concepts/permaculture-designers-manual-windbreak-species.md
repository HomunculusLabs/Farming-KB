# Windbreak Species
#permaculture #windbreak #trees #shelter #species-selection

Updated: 2026-04-18

> From: Bill Mollison, *Permaculture: A Designers Manual* (1988), Chapters 6, 11, 12.

## Overview

When planning windbreaks, consider trees that give multiple function. The windbreak planting itself may need initial protection and care (nutrients, water, weeding, or mulching). If the winds are very severe, look around the area to see what stands up to it, and plant it whether it provides multiple function or not. Plant more useful plants in its lee.

## Multi-Function Windbreak Species

### Mulch Production
- **Casuarina** (sheoak): excellent mulch producer, nitrogen-fixing root associates, hardy, salt-tolerant

### Bee Nectar
- **Dogwood**: provides bee nectar

### Sugar Pods for Animals
- **Carob** (Ceratonia siliqua): drought-hardy, produces sugar pods for livestock
- **Honey locust** (Gleditsia triacanthos): sugar pods for animals, hardy nitrogen-fixer

### Edible Leaves for Forage
- **Leucaena** (Leucaena leucocephala): edible high-protein leaves for livestock
- **Tagasaste** (Chamaecytisus palmensis): edible leaves, excellent poultry forage, nitrogen-fixing

### Berries for Poultry
- **Coprosma repens**: berries for poultry forage
- **Russian olive** (Elaeagnus angustifolia): berries for poultry, nitrogen-fixing, salt-tolerant

## Windbreak Functions

Windbreaks serve many functions beyond wind reduction:
- Exclude frost from protected areas
- Nullify salty or hot winds
- Provide mulch material
- Moderate temperature extremes
- Provide bee forage
- Supply livestock forage
- Create wildlife habitat
- Condense moisture from fogs and mists
- Reduce evapotranspiration in protected crops
- Increase crop yields in lee zone (often 10-30% increase)

## Windbreak Design Considerations

### Configuration
Choose a windbreak configuration effective for the particular design situation:
- In tropical and subtropical areas, a thin-crowned windbreak in crop can provide shade and mulch for vegetable crops
- Serrate or zig-zag fences resist wind-throw much better than straight barriers
- Vary edge geometry for different shelter zones (lobular, crenellated)

### Height and Density
- Even low hedgerows of 0.5-1 m provide essential cover for bee foraging
- Tall windbreaks of equal or greater height than protected crops exclude frost
- Dense evergreen windbreaks for year-round protection
- Deciduous windbreaks allow winter sun through while providing summer shade

### Spacing
- Windbreaks can occupy every sixth to tenth [[permaculture-designers-manual-swale-design-detailed]] on sites where wind is a limiting factor
- It is better to plant on the downslope side to allow mulch collection in the swale base for use elsewhere

## Protection During Establishment

Windbreak plantings may need:
- Nutrient supplements
- Water during establishment
- Weeding until established
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-mulch-systems]] to retain moisture
- Temporary fencing or earth banks for protection
- Tyre walls for extreme sites

## Tropical Windbreak Species

### Living Fence Hedgerows
- **Erythrina** ("willi-willi"): fast-growing, provides shade and mulch, easy from cuttings
- **Banna grass** (Pennisetum purpureum): fast windbreak, cut for mulch, can be removed as Erythrina outgrows it
- **Casuarina**: permanent windbreak, nitrogen-fixing
- **Leucaena**: permanent hedgerow, edible leaves
- **Grevillea**: permanent hedgerow

### Traditional Windbreaks
- **Euphorbia antiquorum** (often used in traditional systems, provides windbreak, spider habitat, bamboo support, and cattle laneway demarcation)
- **Euphorbia tirucalli** (used in Botswana; goggles needed when taking cuttings due to milky sap)

## Trees and Moisture

Trees in windbreaks dramatically increase effective precipitation:
- A large proportion of rime which collects on twigs of trees in frosts afterwards reaches the ground as water
- In climates such as the British Isles, the total amount of water deposited on twigs from fogs and drifting clouds is considerable
- Woodlands reduce the rush of water from bare hillsides, extending the period of surface flow
- Maximum floods of rivers are reduced, and lowest summer flow increased
- Woodlands are usually much more effective than minor vegetation (gorse, heather) in preventing soil from washing into reservoirs

## Condensation from Trees

On the dry island of Hierro in the Canary Islands, a giant Til tree (Ocotea foetens) condensed mountain mists and caused water to drip into two large cisterns placed beneath. The tree distilled sufficient water from sea mists to meet the needs of all the inhabitants. This principle applies broadly: windbreak trees condense moisture from passing air, increasing effective rainfall.
