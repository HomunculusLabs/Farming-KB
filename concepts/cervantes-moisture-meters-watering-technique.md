# Moisture Meters and Watering Technique

Proper watering is one of the most critical yet commonly
misunderstood aspects of cannabis cultivation. Jorge Cervantes
provides comprehensive guidance on using moisture meters, recognizing
overwatering and underwatering, and developing reliable watering
habits.

## Moisture Meters

Moisture meters take most of the guesswork out of irrigating. They
can be purchased for less than $30 and are well worth the investment.
The meter measures exactly how much water the soil contains at any
level or point. Often the soil will not hold water evenly, developing
dry pockets that are invisible on the surface.

Checking moisture with a finger provides only an educated guess and
disturbs the root system. A moisture meter gives an exact reading
without disturbing the roots. This is especially valuable for large
containers where the center may be bone-dry while the surface
appears moist.

## The Lift Test

After developing some skill at knowing when plants need water,
growers can check moisture simply by tipping containers. Once
familiar with the weight of a properly watered pot versus a dry one,
all you need to do is tip each container to assess moisture level.
This technique becomes second nature with experience.

## Proper Watering Technique

### Gradual Application

Apply about one-quarter to one-half as much water or fertilizer as
the plant is expected to need, then wait 10 to 15 minutes for it
to soak in completely. Apply more until the soil is evenly moist.
This gradual approach prevents water from running straight down
cracks between the soil and pot edge, out the drain holes, without
saturating the root zone.

### Surface Cultivation

Cultivate the soil surface to allow water to penetrate evenly and
guard against dry soil pockets. Gently break up the top half inch of
soil with fingers or a salad fork, being careful not to disturb tiny
surface roots. This also prevents water from channeling down the gap
between pot wall and soil.

### Wetting Agents

Add a few drops (one drop per pint) of biodegradable, concentrated
liquid soap such as Castile or Ivory to water. It acts as a wetting
agent by helping water penetrate soil more efficiently and guards
against dry soil pockets. Most soluble fertilizers already contain a
wetting agent.

### The Soak Method

For smaller pots, submerge the container in a larger vessel filled
with water. Fill a 5-gallon bucket with 3 gallons of water, then
submerge the smaller pot for a minute or longer until the growing
medium is completely saturated. This ensures against dry soil pockets.

## Runoff Management

Use plenty of water and allow up to 10 percent runoff during each
watering. The runoff prevents fertilizer from building up in the
soil, reducing the risk of [[cervantes-salt-buildup-leaching-management]].
Water early in the day so excess water evaporates from the soil
surface and leaves. If using trays to catch runoff, use a turkey
baster, large syringe, or sponge to remove excess water after a few
hours or overnight.

## Overwatering

Overwatering is a common problem, especially with small plants. Too
much water drowns roots by cutting off their oxygen supply. Signs of
overwatering include:

- Leaves curled downward and yellowing
- Waterlogged and soggy soil
- Fungal growth on the soil surface
- Slow growth that mimics nutrient deficiency

One of the main causes of overwatering is poor air ventilation.
Plants need to transpire water into the air. If humid air has nowhere
to go, water is locked in the grow room environment. Well-ventilated
air carries moist air away, replacing it with fresh dry air. See
[[cervantes-air-quality-ventilation]] and
[[cervantes-humidity-control-dehumidification]].

Symptoms of overwatering are often subtle, and inexperienced growers
may not see blatant symptoms for a long time. See
[[cervantes-overwatering-underwatering]] for detailed diagnosis.

## Underwatering

Underwatering is less common but occurs frequently with small (1-2
gallon) pots. Small containers dry out quickly and may require daily
watering. Once tender root hairs dry out, they die. Most growers
panic when they see prize plants wilt in bone-dry soil. Dry soil,
even in pockets, makes root hairs dry up and die. It takes
considerable time for roots to grow new root hairs and resume rapid
growth.

## Organization

Keep pots in straight lines when growing and watering. This makes it
much easier to keep track of which pots have been watered and prevents
accidental double-watering or missed plants in larger gardens.
