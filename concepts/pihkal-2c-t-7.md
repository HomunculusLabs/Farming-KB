# 2C-T-7

## Overview

2C-T-7 (2,5-dimethoxy-4-propylthiophenethylamine) is one of the most
controversial and potent members of the 2C-T family documented in PIHKAL as
compound #31. Known by street names including "Blue Mystic" and "T7," 2C-T-7
has gained notoriety due to several reported fatalities, making it one of
the most safety-concerning compounds in Shulgin's catalog.

The propylthio group at the 4-position distinguishes 2C-T-7 from 2C-T-2 and
contributes to higher potency, longer duration, and more demanding effects.
Within the 2C-T series, increasing alkyl chain length generally increases
potency and duration, and 2C-T-7 represents the point where this trend
produces effects significantly more challenging and potentially dangerous.

Despite its controversial reputation, 2C-T-7 is regarded by some experienced
users as among the most visually stunning and profoundly psychedelic 2C
compounds, with intense visuals and significant time distortion earning it a
cautious following among experienced psychonauts.

## Chemical Structure and Classification

2C-T-7's IUPAC name is 2-(4-propylsulfanyl-2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)ethan-1-
amine. It features the characteristic 2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine backbone
with a propylthio group (S-CH2-CH2-CH3) at the 4-position. Compared to
2C-T-2's ethylthio group, the additional carbon increases lipophilicity,
affecting both receptor binding and pharmacokinetics.

Within the 2C-T series, 2C-T-7 sits at a critical structure-activity
relationship point. The progression from 2C-T (methylthio) through 2C-T-2
(ethylthio) to 2C-T-7 (propylthio) shows increasing potency and duration,
but further chain extension (2C-T-8, 2C-T-9, 2C-T-13, 2C-T-21) tends to
diminish activity, suggesting an optimal chain length around the propyl
group.

## Dosage and Administration

Shulgin's recommended oral dosage is 10 to 30 milligrams, with 20 to 30 mg
considered standard. However, doses above 25 mg are strongly cautioned.
Onset occurs within 1 to 2 hours, peaking for 4 to 6 hours. Total duration
ranges from 8 to 15 hours.

Insufflation at 5 to 10 mg is particularly dangerous. Several documented
fatalities involved insufflation, which produces rapid onset and intensely
high peak plasma concentrations. Shulgin strongly cautioned against this
route of administration.

## Effects Profile

2C-T-7's visual effects are among the most intense in the 2C family: rich
layered geometric patterning, intense color enhancement, and profound visual
distortion. At higher doses, fully immersive visual hallucinations are
possible, often described as having an organic, flowing quality attributed
to the thio substituent.

Time distortion is particularly pronounced, even more than many other
psychedelics. Users report time appearing to slow dramatically or become
almost meaningless during the peak. Cognitive effects include deep
philosophical introspection, altered thought patterns, and potentially
challenging ego dissolution. Physical effects include moderate to heavy body
load with muscle tension, nausea, pronounced pupil dilation, and significant
cardiovascular stimulation.

## Shulgin's Commentary

Shulgin documented 2C-T-7 as having considerable potency and long duration,
placing it among the more demanding substances in his collection. He noted
the visual richness and pronounced time distortion that distinguish it from
other 2C compounds.

Importantly, Shulgin expressed concern about dosage sensitivity and the
potential for dangerous outcomes if not approached with appropriate caution.
His warnings about potency and insufflation dangers proved tragically
prescient given subsequent fatalities. He also commented on systematic trends
within the 2C-T series regarding chain length effects.

## Pharmacology

2C-T-7 acts as a potent agonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors with
significant 5-HT2C affinity. The propylthio group's increased lipophilicity
enhances membrane penetration and receptor interaction. The compound may
also have alpha-adrenergic receptor activity, contributing to pronounced
cardiovascular effects. The interaction between serotonergic and adrenergic
systems may underlie dangerous synergistic effects when combined with MAOIs.

Metabolism occurs through hepatic oxidative deamination and S-oxidation of
the propylthio group. Production of potentially active metabolites through
S-oxidation has been proposed as a factor in some adverse reactions.

## Safety and Harm Reduction

2C-T-7 carries more significant safety concerns than most other 2C
compounds. Multiple fatalities have been associated with its use, several
involving insufflation or combination with MAOIs. The 2C-T-7 plus MAOI
combination has been implicated in deaths and is considered extremely
dangerous.

The most critical harm reduction principle: never insufflate 2C-T-7. Oral
administration with accurate dosing is significantly safer. Keep doses at or
below 25 mg. Never combine with MAOIs, SSRIs, or other serotonergic
substances. Individuals with cardiovascular issues should avoid entirely.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

Compared to 2C-T-2, 2C-T-7 is more potent, longer-lasting, and carries
greater safety risks. 2C-T-2 is more manageable with lighter body load.
Against 2C-B, 2C-T-7 is dramatically more intense, longer-acting, and more
physically demanding. Within the 2C-T series, 2C-T-7 represents a turning
point where compounds become significantly more dangerous; 2C-T and 2C-T-2
are generally safer alternatives.

## Historical and Cultural Significance

2C-T-7 emerged in the late 1990s and early 2000s research chemical market.
Its reputation was damaged by high-profile fatalities reported in media and
medical literature. The compound was emergency-scheduled by the DEA in 2002,
among the first 2C compounds specifically targeted. It is Schedule I in the
US and controlled in most countries. The safety incidents have made 2C-T-7
a cautionary example in harm reduction discussions about research chemicals.
