# Permaculture Fruit Trees for Temperate Climates

Temperate fruit trees thrive in regions with cold winters and warm summers, typically requiring winter chilling hours for proper fruit set. This page covers the less-common temperate fruit species from the source that are particularly valuable in permaculture systems for their multi-functionality, resilience, and productivity.

## Persimmon (Diospyros kaki)

Family: EBENACEAE

Also called Chinese persimmon or kakee. A deciduous, round-headed tree growing to 5 m with strikingly coloured autumn foliage, making it attractive as both ornamental and fruit producer. Prefers medium loam soil, does not thrive in dry or acid soils but withstands some waterlogging. Frost hardy to -10C.

The fruit is orange, slightly larger than a tomato, must be fully ripe before eating (unripe fruit is very astringent due to tannic acid). Eaten fresh, dried, or made into jams/jellies. Astringent and non-astringent cultivars available. Bears fruit at about 3 years (grafted). Dioecious (male and female plants needed), though female flowers produce seedless fruit if unpollinated.

[[permplant-carob-drought-tolerant-food-tree]]. Withstands strong winds -- useful for windbreak. Some salt resistance.

Self-pollinating, bears fruit after about 5 years and can continue for more than 100 years.

## Loquat (Eriobotrya japonica)

Family: ROSACEAE

Also called Japanese loquat or Japanese medlar. Originally from central China, a small evergreen tree reaching 6-9 m (often 3 m in less favourable conditions). Fairly frost hardy to -10C, but does not fruit well in cold climates (winter temps below -5C prevent fruiting as flowers are borne in early winter). Grows in a variety of soils, prefers light deep loamy soil kept moist. Moderately drought and wind tolerant, tolerant of salt spray. Makes a good hedge or windbreak.

Fruit are oval (5-6 cm), yellow or orange, with sweetly perfumed softly acid flesh. Usually one large seed per fruit. Mature in spring and early summer. Best eaten fresh, also made into jams and wine. Easily digested, though not highly nutritious. Many fine cultivars available.

[[dighton-fungal-nutritional-composition-animal-diets]] per 100g: Water 86.5%, Energy 48 cal, Carbohydrate 12.4g, Vitamin A 670 IU, Potassium 348mg. Cross-pollination is an advantage.

## Jujube (Ziziphus jujuba)

Family: RHAMNACEAE

Also called Chinese red date. Deciduous tree, origin obscure (possibly China, India, or Syria). Upright habit to 9 m. Prefers full sun, deep alkaline and saline soils, and drought conditions. Tolerant of, but does not favour, waterlogging or clay soils. Damaged roots cause suckering.

Fruit is light green to reddish brown, rich in vitamin C (69mg/100g fresh). Crisp texture with flavour between apple and date. Dries well -- a saleable product. Also boiled with millet and rice, stewed, baked, pickled, or used as coffee substitute. Used in puddings, cakes, breads, jellies, soups, and sweetmeats. Dried jujube flour is made into a miso-like fermented paste in Korea.

Nutritional per 100g fresh: Water 70.2%, Energy 105 cal, Carbohydrate 27.6g, Protein 1.2g, Ascorbic acid 69mg.
Dried: Water 19.7%, Energy 287 cal, Carbohydrate 73.6g, Protein 3.7g, Calcium 79mg, Iron 1.8mg.

Fruits are soothing to the throat; juice used in decoctions for treating colds and sore throats. Used in pharmacy to sweeten medicines.

## Mulberries (Morus spp)

### White Mulberry (M. alba)
Named for its white fruit. Native to China where leaves were traditionally fed to silkworms. Fast-growing shade tree to 25 m, tolerates a wide range of conditions but prefers deep soil, good drainage, and full sun. Leaves very palatable to livestock and nutritious (crude protein 15.1%, fat 5.7%, fibre 13.7%). If properly managed by annual pruning, an important fodder source. Dried fruit ground with almonds as a staple in Afghanistan. Wood used for sporting goods, house building, furniture, tool handles. Bark used for paper and yields textile fibre.

### Black Mulberry (M. nigra)
Also called English or Persian mulberry (native to Persia). Small rounded tree seldom exceeding 12 m with broad rounded crown. Timber prized for turnery and carving.

### Red Mulberry (M. rubra)
Native to eastern and central [[permaculture-pawpaw]] ([[asimina-triloba]])

Family: ANNONACEAE

Called pawpaw in Australia but not a true pawpaw -- related to custard apples. Other names: custard banana, Michigan banana. Deciduous shrub or low tree native to [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]. Tolerant of snow conditions and fairly saline water. Brittle branches, very prone to wind damage.

The largest native fruit [[huckleberries-and-wild-berries-of-north-america]] (up to 300g), develops in autumn. When fully ripe the skin is dark brown or almost black. Soft creamy fruits have banana custard flavour, delicious raw. Also dried, used for preserves, pies, puddings, and desserts. Yellow fruiting varieties are superior to white.

Nutritionally: carbohydrate 16.8%, protein 5.2%, fat 0.9%, water 76.6%. The seeds contain a colourless alkaloid (asimine) acting on the brain.

## Hackberry (Celtis australis)

Family: CELTIDACEAE

Also called nettle tree. Small to medium tree native to southern Europe, North Africa, and Asia Minor. Grows to 25 m, round-headed, useful shade tree, reasonably frost hardy. Fruits are pea-sized, purple-black when ripe (sometimes orange-red), sweet flavour. Eaten raw or used for jams, jellies, wines. Good addition to chicken forage systems.

## Irish Strawberry Tree (Arbutus unedo)

Family: ERICACEAE

Attractive, easily grown evergreen tree native to Mediterranean region, Asia Minor, and Ireland. Small, rounded, densely foliaged tree or large shrub to 7 m (rarely exceeding 5 m). Good shade tree, prefers moist soil. Frost tolerant, moderately drought, shade, and salt spray tolerant. Does not tolerate tropical climates or alkaline/waterlogged soils.
