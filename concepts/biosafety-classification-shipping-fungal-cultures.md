## Biosafety Classification and Shipping of Fungal Cultures

The transport and handling of fungal cultures are governed by overlapping national and international regulatory frameworks. Mycologists shipping live cultures, dried specimens, or soil samples must navigate biosafety classifications, agricultural quarantine rules, and dangerous goods shipping regulations. Non-compliance can result in seizure of materials, fines, or loss of permits.

## USPHS Biosafety Classification System

The United States Public Health Service (USPHS) classifies infectious agents into four levels based on hazard:

- **Class I** — Agents of no or minimal hazard under ordinary conditions (e.g., most environmental saprobes and common laboratory contaminants).
- **Class II** — Agents of ordinary potential hazard (e.g., opportunistic human pathogens such as *Candida albicans*, *Aspergillus fumigatus*). Requires Biosafety Level 2 (BSL-2) practices.
- **Class III** — Agents of special hazard or agents derived from outside the United States requiring special permits (e.g., *Coccidioides immitis*, *Histoplasma capsulatum*). Requires BSL-3 containment.
- **Class IV** — Agents requiring maximum containment (BSL-4). No fungal agents are currently classified at this level.

Most fungal cultures handled by herbaria and research collections fall into Class I or II. Class III fungi require import permits from the CDC and enhanced containment.

## USDA APHIS Regulations for Plant Pathogens

The US Department of Agriculture's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) regulates the movement of plant-pathogenic fungi. Key requirements include:

- **Form PPQ-526** — "Application for Permit to Import, Export, or Re-export Plants or Plant Products" is required for shipping plant-pathogenic fungi, soil samples, and plant material into or out of the United States.
- Permits specify authorized ports of entry, containment conditions, and any post-arrival processing requirements.
- Certain high-consequence plant pathogens (e.g., *Puccinia graminis* race Ug99) may require additional containment or be subject to Select Agent regulations.
- Cultures must be shipped to approved facilities listed on the permit.

APHIS also inspects incoming international packages at designated plant inspection stations. Shipments arriving without proper documentation may be destroyed.

## Import/Export Regulations (DOC, Customs, International)

Importing or exporting fungal cultures involves coordination with multiple agencies:

- **Department of Commerce (DOC)** — Regulates export of certain organisms under the Export Administration Regulations (EAR). Most fungal cultures of non-dual-use significance qualify for general license exceptions (e.g., EAR99 or TSR), but researchers exporting to embargoed destinations need validated licenses.
- **U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)** — All international shipments of biological materials must be declared. Undeclared biological specimens may be seized.
- **International shipping** — Requires compliance with both the origin country's export rules and the destination country's import rules. Many countries require advance import permits before shipment.

## Canadian Risk Group System

Health Canada classifies biological agents into four risk groups paralleling the USPHS system:

- **Risk Group 1 (RG1)** — Low individual and community risk (e.g., most environmental fungi).
- **Risk Group 2 (RG2)** — Moderate individual risk, low community risk (e.g., *Blastomyces dermatitidis*, *Cryptococcus neoformans*).
- **Risk Group 3 (RG3)** — High individual risk, low community risk (e.g., *Coccidioides* spp., *Histoplasma capsulatum*).
- **Risk Group 4 (RG4)** — High individual and community risk. No fungal agents are currently in this group.

**Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada (AAFC)** requires separate permits for import of plant-pathogenic fungi. The Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) administers these permits, which are analogous to USDA APHIS Form PPQ-526.

## European Federation of Biotechnology (EFB) Risk Classification

The EFB classifies microorganisms into risk groups consistent with EU Directive 2000/54/EC:

- **Group 1** — Unlikely to cause human disease.
- **Group 2** — Can cause human disease, may be a hazard to workers; unlikely to spread to the community; effective prophylaxis or treatment usually available.
- **Group 3** — Can cause severe human disease, serious hazard to workers; may present a risk of spreading to the community; effective prophylaxis or treatment usually available.
- **Group 4** — Causes severe human disease, serious hazard to workers; high risk of community spread; no effective prophylaxis or treatment usually available.

Each EU member state implements these classifications into national law. Researchers shipping to or from EU countries must verify the specific national requirements.

## USPS and DOT Shipping Requirements

The U.S. Postal Service (USPS) and Department of Transportation (DOT) regulate shipment of etiological agents (infectious substances):

- **42 CFR Part 72** — Interstate shipment of etiological agents. Shippers must package materials to withstand leakage and must include appropriate labeling. Primary receptacles must not exceed 50 mL for liquids or 50 g for solids.
- **49 CFR Parts 171–180** — DOT Hazardous Materials Regulations (HMR). Classifies infectious substances as Division 6.2 hazardous materials.
- USPS Publication 52 governs mailing of biological materials. Etiological agents must be shipped as Priority Mail Express with appropriate markings.
- Category A infectious substances (UN 2814 or UN 2900) have the most stringent requirements. Most fungal cultures qualify as Category B (UN 3373, "Biological Substance, Category B").

## Packaging Requirements

Triple packaging is the standard for shipping biological materials:

1. **Primary container** — A watertight, leak-proof receptacle containing the specimen (e.g., screw-cap tube, sealed ampoule, or cryovial). Volume limit: 1 L for liquids shipped by air; 4 L by ground.
2. **Secondary container** — A durable, watertight, leak-proof container enclosing the primary container(s). Must contain sufficient absorbent material (cotton, paper towels, absorbent pads) to absorb the entire volume of liquid in case of breakage.
3. **Outer shipping container** — A rigid container (corrugated fiberboard box, insulated cooler, etc.) bearing the shipper's and recipient's addresses, appropriate hazard labels, and handling instructions.

Multiple primary containers may be placed in a single secondary container, separated by cushioning material.

## Dry Ice and Liquid Nitrogen Shipping

- **Dry ice (UN 1845, Class 9)**: Must be declared on the air waybill with net weight. Outer packaging must allow venting of CO₂. Packages must bear the Class 9 miscellaneous dangerous goods label. The "dry ice" marking and net weight must be displayed on the outer packaging.
- **Liquid nitrogen (UN 1977)**: Shipped as a cryogenic liquid under Class 2.2 (non-flammable gas). Special insulated packaging (LN₂ dry shippers) is required. These are typically classified as "not restricted" when properly sealed and vented, but documentation requirements vary by carrier.

Dry ice quantities are limited by aircraft type: typically 2.5 kg per package for passenger aircraft and unlimited for cargo-only aircraft.

## IATA/ICAO/IMO International Transport Regulations

International air transport of infectious substances follows regulations set by three bodies:

- **IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR)** — Updated annually; the primary reference for air shippers.
- **ICAO Technical Instructions** — The regulatory basis for IATA DGR.
- **IMO International Maritime Dangerous Goods (IMDG) Code** — Governs maritime transport.

Key requirements for fungal cultures:

- **UN 2814** — Infectious substance, affecting humans (Category A). Assigned to **Class 6.2**.
- **UN 2900** — Infectious substance, affecting animals only (Category A).
- **UN 3373** — Biological substance, Category B. Most fungal cultures of human or plant health significance are shipped under this designation.
- **Labels and marks**: Outer packages require the Class 6.2 diamond label (UN 2814/2900) or the UN 3373 marking, proper shipping name, UN number, and orientation arrows.
- **Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods** — Required for Category A substances and any package containing dry ice on passenger aircraft. Must be signed by a trained shipper and accompany the shipment.

Training in IATA DGR is required for anyone preparing or offering dangerous goods for air transport (initial training plus biennial recurrent training).

## Export Controls

Export of fungal cultures is subject to U.S. export control laws:

- **DOC Validated License** — Required for exports to embargoed or sanctioned countries (e.g., Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Crimea region). Also required when the organism or its end-use is subject to specific controls.
- **General License (G-DEST)** — Most fungal cultures not on the Commerce Control List (CCL) or Entity List may be exported under general license provisions, meaning no prior written authorization is needed, though record-keeping is required.
- **Shipper's Export Declaration (SED)** — Filed electronically through the Automated Export System (AES). Required for shipments valued over $2,500 per Schedule B number, or for any shipment requiring a validated license.

Dual-use biological agents (those with potential for biological weapons) may fall under additional controls from the Australia Group or be subject to Select Agent Regulations (42 CFR Part 73) even for domestic transfers.

## Summary Checklist for Shipping Fungal Cultures

1. Classify the organism (USPHS Class, Risk Group, EFB Group).
2. Determine if USDA APHIS PPQ-526 permit is needed (plant pathogens, soil).
3. Verify destination country import requirements and obtain advance permits.
4. Select proper UN number (2814, 2900, or 3373) and packaging.
5. Prepare triple-packaged shipment with absorbent material.
6. Complete required documentation (Shipper's Declaration, air waybill, customs forms).
7. Declare dry ice or liquid nitrogen if applicable.
8. File export documentation via AES/EEI if value exceeds threshold.
9. Retain copies of all permits, labels, and shipping records for a minimum of 2 years.
