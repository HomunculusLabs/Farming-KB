# Psilocybin Chemical Synthesis

## Overview

The chemical synthesis of psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine)
and its dephosphorylated active metabolite psilocin has been pursued since
Albert Hofmann first isolated and identified these compounds from *Psilocybe*
mushrooms in 1958-1959. The concise large-scale synthesis reported by
Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda in 2003 represents a significant improvement in
efficiency, achieving gram-scale production of both compounds without any
chromatographic purification — a practical necessity for producing analytical
[[shirota-forensic-standard-compounds-magic-mushroom-identification]].

## Historical Context

### Hofmann's Original Isolation (1958-1959)

Albert Hofmann, working at Sandoz Laboratories in Switzerland, first isolated
psilocybin from *[[dark-incubation-sclerotia-production-psilocybe-mexicana-tampanensis]]* mushrooms collected by R. Gordon Wasson
in Mexico. Hofmann identified psilocybin as 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-
dimethyltryptamine and psilocin as its dephosphorylated counterpart,
4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine. The structural similarity to serotonin
(5-hydroxytryptamine) immediately suggested a basis for their psychedelic
activity through interference with normal serotonin neurotransmission.

### Early Synthetic Routes (1959-2002)

Several synthetic routes to psilocin were published between 1959 and 2002,
but reports on the synthesis of psilocybin were few. The primary challenge
was the phosphorylation step — introducing the phosphate ester group at the
4-position of the indole ring while maintaining the integrity of the
N,N-dimethyltryptamine side chain. Previous approaches by Troxler, Seemann,
and Hofmann (1959) and Nichols and Frescas achieved phosphorylation but
with low yields (20% and 47% respectively) and required chromatographic
purification.

## The Shirota-Hakamata-Goda Synthesis (2003)

### Starting Material

The synthesis begins with commercially available 4-hydroxyindole, which is
first protected by acetylation to yield 4-acetylindole. This protection step
is straightforward, using acetic anhydride in pyridine and dichloromethane,
and produces the protected intermediate in good yield as ivory white crystals.

### Psilocin Synthesis Route

The psilocin synthesis proceeds through three steps from 4-acetylindole:

1. **Glyoxylation**: Treatment with [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-formation-oxalyl-chloride-indole]] in diethyl ether at 0°C
   produces 3-glyoxyloyl-4-acetylindole as yellow crystals after
   precipitation with hexane and freezer storage.

2. **Amination**: The glyoxalyl intermediate is treated with dimethylamine
   in THF with pyridine to yield 3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-acetylindole in
   over 80% yield as an ivory white crystalline powder.

3. **Reduction**: Lithium aluminum hydride (LiAlH4) in THF reduces the
   glyoxalylamide to yield psilocin in over 85% yield as white crystals.

The overall yield from 4-hydroxyindole to psilocin exceeds 60% without any
chromatographic purification steps.

### Psilocin Characterization

The synthesized psilocin is characterized by:
- **Melting point**: 169-174°C (dec.), consistent with literature values
  of 173-176°C (dec.)
- **UV spectrum** (MeOH): λmax 222.5, 268.0, 284.5, 294.0 nm
- **IR spectrum** (KBr): characteristic bands at 3285, 2959, 1620, 1588,
  1473, 1345 cm⁻¹
- **Molecular ion**: m/z 205.1 [M+H]+, consistent with C12H17N2O

### Key Innovation: The Zwitterionic Intermediate

The critical innovation in the Shirota synthesis is the discovery of the
zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (compound 8) as an
intermediate in the phosphorylation of psilocin to psilocybin. This
intermediate was completely characterized by 2D NMR analyses (HMBC and
NOESY experiments).

When psilocin is treated with tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-butyllithium
in THF, the expected O,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (compound 7) forms
initially. However, during aqueous workup, one of the O-benzyl groups
undergoes rapid hydrolytic cleavage. The resulting O-monobenzyl phosphate
then undergoes an intramolecular rearrangement in which the remaining benzyl
group migrates from the phosphate oxygen to the nitrogen of the
N,N-dimethyl group, forming a quaternary ammonium ion.

This zwitterionic compound is insoluble in dichloromethane, which makes
possible its isolation by simple filtration in over 85% yield — a dramatic
practical improvement over previous methods requiring chromatographic
purification. Excess dibenzyl phosphate reagent is removed by washing with
dichloromethane.

### Psilocybin Synthesis Route

The phosphorylation of psilocin to psilocybin proceeds in two steps:

1. **Phosphorylation**: Treatment of psilocin with tetrabenzylpyrophosphate
   and n-butyllithium in THF at -78°C to 0°C, followed by workup and
   suspension in CH2Cl2 overnight to allow zwitterion formation.

2. **Hydrogenolysis**: Catalytic hydrogenation over palladium on carbon in
   methanol removes the remaining benzyl groups, yielding psilocybin as a
   crystalline product.

The isolated yield of psilocybin from psilocin exceeds 72%, even on gram
scale, and requires no chromatographic purification — a substantial
improvement over previous methods.

### Psilocybin Characterization

The synthesized psilocybin is characterized by:
- **Melting point**: 190-198°C, consistent with literature values of
  185-195°C and 210-212°C
- **UV spectrum** (MeOH): λmax 221.0, 267.5, 278.5, 290.0 nm
- **IR spectrum** (KBr): characteristic bands at 3266, 3034, 1620, 1580,
  1505, 1439, 1352, 1298 cm⁻¹
- **31P NMR**: δ -4.48 ppm, confirming the phosphate moiety
- **Molecular ion**: m/z 285.1 [M+H]+, consistent with C12H18N2O4P

## Regulatory Context

Since June 6, 2002, fungi containing [[tryptamine-chemistry-psilocin-and-psilocybin-synthesis]] have been
regulated by the Narcotics and Psychotropic [[japan-narcotics-control-law-psilocybin-fungi-2002]] in Japan. The
identification of [[guzman-magic-mushrooms-african-species]] using morphologic and microscopic analyses
is difficult without experts, so chromatographic methods including TLC, GC,
and HPLC are typically employed. For these analyses, standard compounds are
always needed, motivating the development of efficient synthetic routes.
