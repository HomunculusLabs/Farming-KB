## How CSA Works

### The Model

1. **Farmers plan** their seasonal production
2. **Consumers purchase a share** before [[fukuoka-seasonal-eating-natural-food-calendar]]**: Encourages connection to natural growing cycles
- **Variety**: Exposure to vegetables and fruits they might not otherwise try
- **Farm connection**: Knowing where food comes from and who grows it
- **Community**: Being part of a local food system

## Starting a CSA

### Common Mistakes

The most common failure mode for startup CSAs:

- **Over-optimistic projections**: Hoping for good weather and producing much less food than expected
- **Overselling shares**: Selling to too many people, resulting in skimpy weekly boxes
- **Underwhelming first experience**: Customers who feel their up-front investment was not worthwhile will not return

### Best Practices

1. **Start with your own family**: Ensure you can produce enough food for your household first
2. **Expand to a few more families**: Add just 2–3 additional households in the first year
3. **Overflowing boxes**: The weekly box should be overflowing with food — quantity and quality both matter
4. **Include surprises**: Extra items not expected create delight and perceived value
5. **High quality**: Everything in the box should be fresh and premium
6. **Consistency**: Boxes arrive on time, every time, all season

### Growth Strategy

- **Word of mouth**: The most powerful marketing tool — satisfied customers recommend you
- **Customer service**: If a customer isn't happy, do anything it takes to make it right
- **Gradual expansion**: Add a few shares each year as your capacity and confidence grow
- **Track what grows well**: Use data from each season to improve planning

## Pricing

- **Typical range**: $300–500 per season per share
- **Season length**: Typically 20–30 weeks (depending on climate)
- **What's included**: Usually weekly vegetables and some fruits; some CSAs include value-added products like jam, honey, or eggs
- **Payment timing**: Full payment before the season starts is standard
- **Payment plans**: Some CSAs offer installment options to reduce the barrier

## Delivery Options

- **Farm pickup**: Members come to the farm — simplest, lowest cost
- **Delivery point**: Drop-off at a central location (community center, school, church)
- **Home delivery**: Most expensive option but most convenient
- **Mixed model**: Weekly pickup with occasional special deliveries

## Challenges

- **Weather dependency**: Drought, flood, frost, or pests can reduce yields
- **Gluts and gaps**: Some weeks produce too much of one crop, too little of another
- **Member expectations**: Managing what members expect vs what nature provides
- **Labor intensity**: Harvesting, packing, and distributing weekly boxes is significant work
- **Seasonal limitation**: In most climates, CSA runs only during the growing season
