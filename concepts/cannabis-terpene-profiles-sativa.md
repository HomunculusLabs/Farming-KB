# Cannabis Terpene Profiles in Sativa Strains

Terpenes are the aromatic compounds responsible for the distinctive
smells and flavors of cannabis, and they play a significant role in
the effects profile of different strains. Cannabis Sativa Volume 3
provides extensive descriptions of terpene profiles across numerous
sativa and sativa-dominant hybrid strains, revealing the remarkable
diversity of aromas within the sativa gene pool. Understanding these
terpene profiles helps growers select strains, predict effects, and
appreciate the complexity of cannabis as a botanical product.

## Common Sativa Terpenes

Several terpenes are characteristically associated with sativa strains.
Terpinolene contributes floral, herbal, and slightly fruity notes found
in many sativa-dominant varieties. Myrcene, while present in all
cannabis types, often appears in lower concentrations in sativas
compared to indicas, contributing to the less sedative effect profile.
Pinene (both alpha and beta) provides pine and fir aromas and may
counteract some of the memory impairment associated with THC.
Limonene offers citrus notes and is associated with elevated mood and
stress relief. Caryophyllene contributes peppery, spicy notes and is
unique among terpenes for its ability to directly activate cannabinoid
receptors. Humulene provides earthy, woody aromas with subtle herbal
undertones. These terpenes rarely appear in isolation; rather, it is
their synergistic combination that gives each strain its unique
sensory signature.

## Haze Family Aromas

The Haze family exemplifies the complexity of sativa terpene profiles.
Original Haze, the patriarch of the family, is described as having
a spicy, piney, and earthy aroma with floral undertones. Amnesia Haze
carries a complex mix of earthy, lemon, and citrus aromas with subtle
spicy incense notes. [[sativa-landrace-strains-and-geographic-origins]] carry distinctive terpene
signatures reflecting their evolutionary environments. Durban Poison
from South Africa is noted for its sweet, anise, licorice, and
clove-like aroma with a characteristic sharpness. Malawi Gold from
southeastern Africa develops anise and herbal aromas during flowering.
Thai strains contribute sweet, fruity, and sometimes chocolatey
terpene notes. Chocolate Thai, as the name suggests, carries distinct
chocolate and coffee aromas alongside tropical fruit notes. Cambodian
strains often present floral and herbal profiles with subtle spice.
These geographically distinct terpene signatures represent hundreds
or thousands of years of adaptation to local conditions and represent
a genetic resource that is increasingly threatened by hybridization
with modern commercial varieties.
