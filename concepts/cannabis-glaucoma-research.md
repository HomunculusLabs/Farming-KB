# Cannabis and Glaucoma Research

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Glaucoma is a group of eye diseases that can cause partial or complete vision loss through damage to the optic nerve, often associated with increased intraocular pressure (IOP). Cannabis has a long history of use for eye conditions and modern research confirms its ability to reduce intraocular pressure, though questions remain about practical long-term management.

## Condition Overview

### What Is Glaucoma?
Glaucoma damages the optic nerve leading from the eye to the brain, resulting in partial or complete blindness. Chronic or acute increased intraocular pressure is commonly present, caused by blocked aqueous humor (fluid inside the eye) drainage channels.

### Risk Factors
- Age over 40
- Family history of glaucoma or vision problems
- Diabetes
- Corticosteroid medication use
- Ethnicity: African American, Hispanic, Inuit, Irish, Japanese, Russian, Scandinavian descent at increased risk

### Symptoms
- Loss of peripheral vision
- Red sclera (white of eye)
- Loss of visual acuity
- Eye aches
- Tunnel vision
- Nausea and vomiting (in some cases)

### Orthodox Treatment
No cure exists, and lost vision cannot be restored. Treatment focuses on surgery and prescription medications to slow progression.

## Cannabis Research for Glaucoma

### Historical Context
Cannabis has been part of traditional medicine's treatment of eye disease for millennia. Most historical Materia Medicas include cannabis prescriptions for eye problems. Modern scientific interest began in 1971 when it was first noted that smoking marijuana lowered intraocular pressure.
