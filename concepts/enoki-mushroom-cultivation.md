# Enoki Mushroom Cultivation

Enoki ([[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]] in Japan, where it has been grown commercially for centuries. In the wild, it is a short-stemmed mushroom with broad caps. Through manipulation of CO2 and light levels, cultivators produce the characteristic long-stemmed, small-capped form that dominates the [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide, from sea level to tree-line

## Morphology

- Cap: 1-5 cm diameter, convex to plane, smooth, viscid when wet, bright to dull yellowish brown to orangish brown
- Gills: white to yellow, attached to stem
- Stem: usually short (1-3 inches) in the wild, yellow to yellowish brown, covered with fine brown hairs (velvety) near the base
- Spores: white, 6-8 x 3-4 microns, ellipsoid; clamp connections present
- Spore size varies geographically: Japanese varieties 5-7.5 x 3-4 microns; European collections 8-9 x 4.5-6 microns

## Natural Habitat

Primarily on hardwoods, occasionally on conifers. Commonly found growing in late fall through early winter. This mushroom can freeze, thaw, and continue growing -- a defining characteristic of the "Winter Mushroom." The definition of "Enoki" in Japanese is huckleberry, implying it grows on that shrub in Japan.

## The Classic Example of Environmental Manipulation

Enoki is the classic example of how CO2 and light control fruitbody morphology:

- Under outdoor conditions (moderate light, low CO2): short-stemmed with caps as wide as stems are long
- Under high CO2 and no light: thin stems without caps
- Under high CO2 and limited light: long yellowish stems, small white to yellowish caps
- Cap-to-stem ratio ranges from 1:1 to 1:100 depending on environment
- Stems range from 1 inch to 12 inches depending on conditions

This environmental responsiveness makes Enoki an excellent teaching species for understanding how microclimate affects mushroom development.

## Growth Parameters

| Stage | Temperature | RH | CO2 | Duration | Light |
|-------|-----------|-----|-----|----------|-------|
| Spawn Run | 70-75 F (21-24 C) | 95-100% | >5,000 ppm | 14-18 days | n/a |
| Primordia | 45-50 F (7-10 C) | 95-100% | 2,000-4,000 ppm | 3-5 days | 100-200 lux |
| Fruiting | 50-60 F (10-16 C) | 90-95% | 2,000-4,000 ppm | 5-8 days | 100-200 lux |

Cropping cycle: 2-3 crops, 10-12 days apart. Most strains require a cold shock for primordia. An aggressive warm-weather strain was isolated from high-altitude Colorado (above 10,000 ft) that produces primordia at room temperature without cold shock. Fresh air exchanges during fruiting: 2-4 per hour.

## Cultivation Method: Bottle Culture (Standard)

The Japanese bottle method is the industry standard:

1. Polypropylene bottles filled with [[comparison-grain-spawn-vs-grain-to-grain-transfer]] at 10-15% rate
3. Incubate at 70-75 F for 2-3 weeks
4. Cold shock to 45-50 F for [[mushroom-pinning-primordia-formation-strategies]]. Long-stemmed, small-capped mushrooms commonly form along inside periphery of petri dish or during [[biological-efficiency]]: up to 150% (preponderance is stem mass)
- Standard yield from 1-liter bottles: 3-5 oz per first flush
- Clusters average 5-6 oz (140-170 grams) per bottle in commercial operations
- Multiple flushes possible but first flush is dominant
