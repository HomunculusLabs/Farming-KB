## Psychedelic Session Preparation — Set and Setting

## Overview

The specific reaction to a psychedelic substance has little to do with the chemical itself and is chiefly a function of **[[lsd-set-and-setting-framework]]** — the psychological predisposition of the individual and the environment in which the session takes place. Understanding and carefully preparing these two dimensions is the single most important factor in determining the quality and safety of a [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]. Leary, Metzner, and Alpert emphasize that no amount of pharmacological knowledge can substitute for proper attention to set and setting.

## Set

"Set" refers to the total psychological makeup of the person entering the session. It is divided into two interrelated aspects: the **long-range set** and the **immediate set**.

### Long-Range Set

The long-range set encompasses the person"s enduring psychological characteristics and life history. It includes:

- **Personal history** — the accumulated experiences, traumas, and formative events that shape identity.
- **Enduring personality traits** — the stable patterns of thought, feeling, and behavior that define the individual.
- **Fears, desires, conflicts, guilts, and secret passions** — the deeper emotional currents that may surface during the session, often with amplified intensity.
- **Reflex mechanisms for dealing with anxiety** — habitual coping strategies (intellectualization, denial, withdrawal, aggression) that the person may automatically deploy when confronted with overwhelming material.
- **Character structure** — qualities such as **flexibility, basic trust, religious faith, and courage** on one end of the spectrum, versus **rigidity, desire to control, distrust, and cynicism** on the other. These characteristics profoundly shape how the individual responds to the ego-dissolving effects of the psychedelic.
- **Capacity for insight** — the willingness and ability to look honestly at oneself and integrate new perspectives. This is perhaps the single most important trait for a productive session.

The long-range set is not something that can be dramatically altered before a single session, but awareness of its contours helps both the subject and guide anticipate what may arise.

### Immediate Set

The immediate set consists of the person's **expectations about the session itself** — what they think will happen, what they want to happen, and what they fear might happen. Leary et al. identify several types of expectations that can shape the experience:

#### Medical Expectations

The medical model is described as a trap. When a subject adopts a patient-role and expects the session to be a "treatment" administered by a "doctor," the experience is constrained by that framework. The subject may demand tranquilizers at the first sign of anxiety, treating uncomfortable but potentially transformative material as symptoms to be suppressed rather than experiences to be explored.

#### Rebellion Against Convention

Some subjects approach the psychedelic experience primarily as an act of defiance against social norms. While this motivation may catalyze the initial decision to take the substance, it can become a limiting framework if rebellion itself becomes the ego-game played throughout the session.

#### Intellectual Expectations

Intellectually oriented subjects often attempt to rationalize or analyze the experience as it unfolds. For novices this is identified as a particular danger — the tendency to keep the mind running in its accustomed patterns rather than surrendering to the experience. The guidance is direct: "Turn your mind off." The psychedelic state is not amenable to ordinary intellectual processing, and attempts to force it into conceptual categories can produce anxiety and frustration.

#### Religious Expectations

Subjects with religious inclinations may be tempted to interpret every vision and sensation in theological terms. Leary et al. recommend postponing theological interpretations — the experience should be allowed to unfold on its own terms before being fitted into any doctrinal framework. Premature religious labeling can be just as limiting as premature medical labeling.

#### Recreational / Aesthetic Expectations

Some subjects seek the experience for its aesthetic or pleasurable dimensions — the visual beauty, the sense of expansion, the ecstatic moments. While these are valid and often beautiful aspects of the experience, they carry their own trap: ecstatic moments can become Third Bardo ego-games in which the subject clings to pleasurable states and resists the deeper, more challenging phases of the journey.

#### Planned Expectations (Tibetan Model Orientation)

The most deliberately structured approach involves orienting the subject using the Tibetan model — the [[bardo-thodol]] as a human artifact designed to guide consciousness through the stages of the psychedelic experience. This orientation provides a map without insisting on a specific terrain, allowing the subject to recognize phases of the journey without being rigidly bound to any single interpretation.

## Practical Preparation

The authors offer concrete recommendations for preparing a psychedelic session:

- **Set aside three days** — one day for preparation, one for the session itself, and one for integration and reflection. This three-day rhythm allows the experience to unfold without the pressure of immediate return to ordinary life.
- **Talk to experienced persons** — those who have undergone [[bardo-instruction-texts-psychedelic-sessions]] can provide invaluable practical guidance and help calibrate expectations.
- **Observe a session** — watching another person"s session (with their consent) provides a sense of the terrain before entering it oneself.
- **Read books about [[mystical-experience]]** — recommended authors include [[aldous-huxley]], Alan Watts, and R. Gordon Wasson. These works help familiarize the subject with the phenomenology of expanded states.
- **Meditation as the best preparation** — regular meditation practice develops exactly the capacities most needed in a psychedelic session: the ability to observe without grasping, to remain present with discomfort, and to let go of the ego's demands for control.

## The Setting

The physical and social environment of the session must be carefully arranged to support the experience rather than interfere with it.

### General Principles

- **Removed from usual social games** — the setting should feel distinct from the environments of ordinary life, where habitual roles and interactions are constantly reinforced.
- **Free from distractions and intrusions** — no visitors, no phone calls, no unexpected interruptions. The session requires a protected space.
- **Trust in surroundings** — the subject must feel safe and comfortable in the physical environment. Unfamiliar or threatening surroundings introduce unnecessary anxiety.
- **Three-day scheduling** — the setting should be secured for the full three-day period.

### Time and Place

- **Night vs. day sessions** — each has its qualities. Night sessions tend to encourage inward, visionary experiences; day sessions may feel more grounded and connected to the natural world. The choice should be made collaboratively.
- **Outdoor vs. indoor sessions** — natural settings can be profoundly supportive, but they also introduce variables (weather, encounters with others) that must be considered. Indoor sessions offer more control.

### Sensory Environment

- **Music** — carefully selected music can provide structure, emotional resonance, and a sense of journey. The music should be chosen in advance and should support rather than dictate the experience.
- **Lighting** — should be adjustable, allowing for both dim, introspective conditions and brighter, more engaged states.
- **Food** — simple, ancient foods are recommended: bread, cheese, wine, fresh fruit. Heavy or unfamiliar foods are to be avoided.

### Room Arrangement for Groups

When the session involves multiple participants, the room should be arranged with beds placed in a **star pattern** — radiating outward from a central point. An **extra room should be available for seclusion** when a participant needs to withdraw from the group for a period of introspection or to work through difficult material privately.

### Collaborative Decisions

Goals and setting should be decided collaboratively among all participants and guides. This shared investment in the preparation creates a sense of collective purpose and mutual responsibility.

## The Psychedelic Guide

The guide's **attitude and behavior** are identified as among the most critical factors in determining the outcome of a psychedelic session. The guide is not merely a passive supervisor but an active participant in shaping the experiential field.

### Core Qualities

- **Ability to turn off one's own ego and social games** — the guide must be able to set aside personal concerns, ego-identifications, and habitual social roles. This is the foundational requirement.
- **Creative quietism** — the guide's [[default-mode-network-and-ego-dissolution]] should be one of calm, attentive presence rather than active intervention. The guide creates a supportive atmosphere through being rather than doing.
- **[[palmer-mushroom-cooking-and-personal-experience]]** — the guide should have direct personal experience with psychedelic states. Theoretical knowledge alone is insufficient for understanding what the subject is going through.
- **Passive sensitivity and intuitive relaxation** — the guide must remain receptive to the subject's state without imposing interpretations or directions. Intuitive relaxation allows the guide to respond appropriately to each moment as it arises.

### The Airport Tower Analogy

The guide's role is compared to an airport control tower — monitoring the situation, aware of conditions, available to provide critical information or assistance, but not flying the plane. The guide sees the whole picture and can offer guidance when needed, but respects the subject's autonomy and the natural unfolding of the experience.

### The Problem of Fear

The **greatest problem** in guided psychedelic sessions is **fear** — and specifically, fear on the guide's part. When the guide becomes frightened by the subject's experience (by intense emotion, by loss of contact, by unusual behavior), that fear is transmitted to the subject and can transform a challenging but productive experience into a genuinely negative one. The guide's composure is the single most important safety factor.

### Dosing Strategy for Guides

The authors describe a model in which one trained person takes a low dose with the subject (remaining "up" and able to relate to the psychedelic state) while a second trained person remains without psychedelic — in **"ground control."** This dual-guide system provides both empathic connection and practical capability.

Two analogies illustrate this approach:

- **Wing-tip pilot** — the dosed guide flies alongside the subject, maintaining formation, able to communicate from shared experience.
- **Scuba diver** — the dosed guide is in the water with the subject, familiar with the medium, while the undosed guide remains at the surface with the equipment and the ability to manage external contingencies.

Large doses for guides during sessions with new subjects are **not recommended**, as the guide needs sufficient clarity to monitor safety and provide support.

### The Guide as Liberator

Ultimately, the guide's role is that of a **liberator** — helping the subject free themselves from limiting patterns, fears, and ego-constructions. The guide does not impose liberation but creates the conditions in which it can naturally occur. The psychedelic experience, properly supported, is understood as an opportunity for the consciousness to encounter its own nature — and the guide's task is to protect the space in which that encounter can unfold authentically.

## Summary

Successful psychedelic session preparation rests on three pillars: understanding and working with the subject's **set** (both long-range personality structure and immediate expectations), crafting a supportive and protected **setting** (physical environment, sensory conditions, temporal arrangement), and providing a skilled **guide** whose composure, experience, and ability to remain present without imposing ego-games are the ultimate safety net. The chemical is merely the catalyst; set, setting, and guidance determine the reaction.
