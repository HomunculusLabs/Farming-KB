# Soil Preparation for Winter Crops

Fall soil preparation is the foundation of successful winter crop
production. Eliot Coleman emphasizes that winter crops must establish
strong root systems and access adequate nutrition before cold
temperatures slow growth to a near standstill. The soil must be
prepared to support both immediate establishment and sustained
health through months of reduced biological activity.

## Compost Application

Compost is the cornerstone of Coleman's fertility program for winter
crops. He applies 1 to 2 inches of finished compost across all bed
surfaces before fall planting. This translates to approximately 2 to
4 cubic yards per 100 square feet of bed area. The compost is
incorporated into the top 4 to 6 inches of soil using a garden fork
or through the broadforking process described in
[[comparison-kelp-meal-vs-rock-dust]], or a broad-spectrum trace
  mineral mix to ensure micronutrient availability.
- **Calcium:** Gypsum (calcium sulfate) if calcium is deficient
  without requiring a pH adjustment.

Coleman applies mineral amendments in early fall, several weeks
before planting, to allow them to begin reacting with the soil. Rock
phosphate in particular benefits from time to become available to
plant roots.

## Bed Shaping and Final Preparation

After amendments are incorporated, beds are shaped to their final
profile. Coleman's standard bed is 30 inches wide and raised 4 to 6
inches above the walkway surface. The bed surface is raked smooth and
slightly crowned to shed excess water. For direct-seeded crops, the
surface is further refined with a fine-tined rake to create a uniform
seedbed.

- **Mulching:** A thin layer of straw or leaf mulch between rows
  protects the soil surface from rain impact and compaction.
