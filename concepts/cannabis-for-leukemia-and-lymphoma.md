# Cannabis for Leukemia and Lymphoma

Leukemia is a type of blood cancer that usually begins in the bone marrow, where production of red and white blood cells and platelets occurs. Cancerous mutations of blood cells at their point of genesis can lead to serious impairment of immune function, [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] (CHI) rates the evidence for cannabis in leukemia and lymphoma at a combined CHI value of 9, based on 7 studies reviewed. The evidence focuses primarily on the ability of cannabinoids to induce apoptosis (programmed cell death) in leukemia cells.

## CBD and Apoptosis

A 2006 Columbia, South Carolina team discovered that CBD, via CB2 pathways, produced apoptosis of leukemia cells, reduced tumor burden, and increased tumor apoptosis. The researchers suggested that CBD may be a novel and highly selective treatment for leukemia.

The selectivity of CBD for leukemia cells (through CB2 pathways) is particularly significant because CB2 receptors are predominantly found on immune cells, including leukemic white blood cells. This targeting may allow CBD to destroy cancer cells while sparing healthy tissue.

## THC and Chemotherapy Enhancement

A 2005 London study initially suggested that THC does not work synergistically with chemotherapy agents. However, a 2008 London analysis concluded that THC does in fact enhance the effectiveness of anticancer drugs to induce death in leukemia cells.

This reversal of findings highlights the complexity of cannabinoid-chemotherapy interactions and the importance of continued research. If THC can enhance chemotherapy effectiveness, it could potentially allow lower chemotherapy doses while maintaining efficacy, reducing side effects.

## CB2-Selective Targeting

The CB2 receptor selectivity of CBD for leukemia cells is particularly relevant because CB2 receptors are not found in significant numbers in the brain, meaning CBD-based leukemia treatments could avoid the psychoactive effects associated with CB1 activation while specifically targeting cancerous immune cells.

This CB2 selectivity may also reduce side effects compared to conventional chemotherapy, which typically targets all rapidly dividing cells including healthy ones.

## Combined Leukemia and Lymphoma Considerations

Both leukemia and lymphoma are cancers of the immune system. The Lymphoma Foundation of America has acknowledged the medical and therapeutic benefits of marijuana and supports its legal use for patients with serious medical conditions.

The shared immune system origin of these cancers means that cannabinoid-based treatments targeting CB2 receptors on immune cells may have applications across both conditions. The anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects demonstrated in leukemia cells may also apply to lymphoma cells.

## Risk Factors

Causative relations are found between leukemia and prior chemotherapies for cancer, ionizing radiation, and exposure to benzene, formaldehyde, and other chemical toxins. Risk factors may include genetic predisposition, smoking, viral influences, Down syndrome, and extremely low frequencies associated with high-voltage electrical power lines.

## Conventional Treatment Context

Conventional leukemia treatments include chemotherapy, bone marrow transplants, and radiation. These treatments carry significant risks and side effects. Cannabis may serve as a complementary therapy both for its direct anti-leukemia properties and for managing treatment side effects including nausea, pain, and loss of appetite.
