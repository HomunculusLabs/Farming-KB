# JADAM Soil Mineral Replenishment

JADAM's mineral replenishment strategy addresses one of the most overlooked
problems in modern agriculture: the progressive depletion of soil minerals
through continuous harvesting and leaching. The system uses two primary
inputs — seawater and phyllite rock solution — combined with microbial
diversification to restore and maintain the full spectrum of minerals that
plants require for optimal health.

## The Mineral Depletion Problem

Mineral deficiency is identified by JADAM founder Youngsang Cho as the
primary reason farming becomes more difficult with age and experience rather
than easier. Most farmers have a few mineral products they favor and use
repeatedly, but the problem persists. Modern science cannot definitively
identify which mineral is deficient or in what quantity, and cannot clearly
distinguish between insolubilized and solubilized nutrients. All soil
evaluation results are therefore extremely inaccurate.

JADAM's approach draws an analogy to human nutrition: rather than trying
to identify individual mineral deficiencies and supplement them with
pills, the system aims to recover mineral diversity and balance through
a wholesome, comprehensive approach.

## Seawater as Mineral Supplement

### Why Seawater

Seawater contains 83 identified elements — essentially the entire periodic
table of elements that make up the Earth, excluding only oxygen and
hydrogen. Not only does it contain magnesium, calcium, potassium, and
phosphorus, but also iodine, manganese, molybdenum, cobalt, selenium, and
germanium, which are all essential for crop growth.

Approximately 100 million marine microorganisms live in each milliliter of
seawater. When combined with [[ultra-low-cost-jadam-microorganism-solution-and-soil-foundation]],
which is bred from leaf mold microorganisms, seawater brings together the
microbial diversity of both land and sea — the complete biological
diversity that Earth has to offer.

### Application Protocol

-   **Standard rate**: 20 liters of seawater per 0.1 hectare (approximately
    one-quarter acre).
-   **Sea salt equivalent**: 600 grams of sea salt when seawater is not
    available (20 liters seawater equals approximately 500 grams salt).
-   **Dilution**: Mix with 30 to 100 times water, since seawater is
    approximately 3% salt while plant body fluid is approximately 1%.
-   **Frequency**: Apply 3-4 times per month, combined with every JMS
    application.
-   **Timing**: Start before transplanting or the growth period for
    maximum effectiveness.

### Benefits and Evidence

Seawater supplementation provides mineral diversity recovery (83
elements), improved sweetness/coloring/storage of produce, enhanced market
quality, and powdery mildew control when applied as foliar spray with JMS.
Fields near the sea have been showered with seawater elements for
centuries and consistently produce better quality food — even confirmed by
the Korean National Institute of Agricultural Sciences.

## Phyllite Solution

### What is Phyllite

Phyllite is a fine-grained metamorphic rock formed from the accumulation
of organic matter under pressure and heat. Rocks made from organic matter
accumulation contain materials closest to the mineral requirements of
plants. Phyllite is sold in fine powder form and is inexpensive.

### Preparation Method

1.  Add 60 kg of fine-powdered phyllite to 500 liters of water.
2.  Stir thoroughly and let settle for a few hours.
3.  Scoop out the upper water for use as "phyllite solution."
4.  Refill with water when depleted.
5.  Continue using the same phyllite for up to one year.

### Application Rates

-   **Standard**: Use phyllite solution with every watering, alongside
    JMS and seawater.
-   **Severe mineral leaching**: Directly sprinkle phyllite powder onto
    soil at approximately 1 kg per 3.3 square meters (36 sq ft).
-   **Alternative rocks**: If phyllite is not available, other rock powders
    can be used, though phyllite is preferred.

### Combined Effect

When phyllite solution is used together with seawater and JMS, the
combination addresses both the diversity and quantity of soil minerals
comprehensively, dramatically improving crop taste, aroma, and market value.

## The Diversity Principle

The core of JADAM's mineral strategy is diversity: different microorganisms
have different diets and nutrient processes, creating diverse available
nutrients. Seawater (83 elements) plus phyllite (broad-spectrum rock
minerals) ensures no mineral is missing — unlike conventional farming's
approach of "junk food diet plus vitamin pills."

## Soil Nutrient Tolerance and Humus

Regular input of JMS, seawater, and phyllite increases the soil's nutrient
tolerance — its ability to maintain optimal levels even when individual
elements are in excess. For example, acid rain causes aluminum ionization
and root rot (a problem with no conventional solution), but constant JADAM
input allows aluminum-tolerant microorganisms to absorb excess aluminum,
keeping it at optimal levels.

JADAM also recommends humus soil — leaf mold exposed to microbial activity
for thousands of years, becoming a pure organic fertilizer with abundant
minerals. It is especially effective for fruits and vegetables when
supplemented with nitrogen.

## The Core JADAM Mantra

The most important technology in JADAM is summarized as: **Apply JMS, sea
water, and phyllite solution every time you water.** This simple, repeated
practice builds soil health progressively and is the foundation upon which
the entire [[jadam-fertilizer-program-design]] rests.
