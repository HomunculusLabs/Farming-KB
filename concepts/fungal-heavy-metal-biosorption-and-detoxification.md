# Fungal Heavy Metal Biosorption and Detoxification

Fungi possess remarkable abilities to accumulate, tolerate, and detoxify heavy
metals through biosorption, bioaccumulation, intracellular sequestration, and
chemical transformation. These capabilities make fungi valuable agents for
[[mycoremediation]] of metal-contaminated environments and heavy metal
remediation strategies.

## Metal Contamination Problem

Heavy metal contamination arises from industrial activities including mining,
smelting, electroplating, battery manufacturing, pesticide application, and
fossil fuel combustion. Problem metals include lead (Pb), cadmium (Cd),
chromium (Cr), mercury (Hg), arsenic (As), copper (Cu), zinc (Zn), and
nickel (Ni). Unlike organic pollutants, metals cannot be degraded and persist
indefinitely in the environment.

## Fungal Metal Tolerance Mechanisms

Fungi employ multiple strategies to survive in metal-contaminated environments.
These mechanisms operate at the cellular level and are often deployed in
combination to provide robust protection against metal toxicity.

### Extracellular Mechanisms

- **Biosorption**: Binding of metal ions to fungal cell wall components
  (chitin, glucans, proteins)
- **Extracellular precipitation**: Metal precipitation through metabolite
  excretion (oxalate, phosphate, sulfate)
- **Extracellular complexation**: Organic acids and siderophores chelate
  metals, reducing bioavailability
- **Cell wall binding**: Chitin and glucans in the cell wall provide binding
  sites for metal cations

### Intracellular Mechanisms

- **Bioaccumulation**: Active uptake and intracellular concentration of metals
- **Sequestration**: Compartmentalization in vacuoles
- **Metallothionein production**: Small cysteine-rich proteins that bind metals
- **Phytochelatin synthesis**: Metal-binding peptides for detoxification
- **Enzymatic transformation**: Reduction, oxidation, or methylation of metal
  species
- **Efflux pumps**: Active transport of metals out of cells

## Mechanisms of Metal Tolerance

### Antioxidant Defense Systems

Metal toxicity in fungi is largely mediated through the generation of reactive
oxygen species (ROS). Fungi counteract oxidative stress through a multi-layered
antioxidant defense system:

- **Superoxide dismutase (SOD)**: Converts superoxide radicals to hydrogen
  peroxide, the first line of defense against metal-induced oxidative stress.
- **Catalase and peroxidases**: Decompose [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] to water and
  oxygen, preventing the formation of highly damaging hydroxyl radicals.
- **Glutathione**: A tripeptide thiol that directly scavenges ROS and serves
  as a precursor for phytochelatin synthesis.
- **Melanin**: The dark pigment found in many fungal cell walls acts as a
  potent antioxidant and metal chelator, providing protection against both
  oxidative stress and direct metal binding.

### Metal-Specific Detoxification Pathways

Different metals trigger distinct detoxification responses in fungi:

- **Copper tolerance**: Fungi produce metallothioneins with high copper-
  binding affinity. Some species sequester excess copper in vacuoles
  complexed with metallothioneins or phytochelatins.
- **Cadmium tolerance**: Primarily mediated through phytochelatin synthesis
  and vacuolar compartmentalization. Cadmium-phytochelatin complexes are
  transported into vacuoles by ABC transporters.
- **Mercury tolerance**: Some fungi possess mer operon-like genes enabling
  enzymatic reduction of mercuric ions to elemental mercury, which volatilizes
  from the cell. Methylation and demethylation pathways alter mercury
  speciation and toxicity.
- **Arsenic tolerance**: Fungi can reduce arsenate (As(V)) to arsenite
  (As(III)), which is then extruded from cells or complexed with thiol
  compounds. Some species also methylate arsenic to form less toxic organic
  arsenicals.
- **Chromium tolerance**: Fungal reduction of hexavalent chromium (Cr(VI)) to
  the less toxic trivalent form (Cr(III)) is a well-documented detoxification
  mechanism. Cr(III) is subsequently precipitated or bound to cell walls.

### Genetic Regulation of Metal Tolerance

Metal tolerance in fungi involves coordinated regulation of multiple genes.
Metal-responsive transcription factors activate expression of metallothioneins,
phytochelatin synthases, efflux transporters, and antioxidant enzymes when
cells are exposed to toxic metals. In [[saccharomyces-cerevisiae]], the ACE1 and
CUP1 systems regulate copper tolerance, while the Yap1 transcription factor
controls the oxidative stress response to cadmium and other metals.

## Fungal Cell Wall as Biosorbent

The fungal cell wall is the first line of defense and primary biosorption
site:

| Cell Wall Component | Metal Binding Mechanism                         |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Chitin             | Amine and hydroxyl groups coordinate metal ions |
| Glucans            | Hydroxyl groups provide binding sites           |
| Proteins           | Carboxyl, amine, and thiol groups bind metals   |
| Melanin            | Multiple functional groups; high metal affinity  |
| Phosphates         | Precipitate metals as insoluble phosphates      |

Dead fungal biomass (spent from industrial fermentation) retains biosorption
capacity and can be used as low-cost biosorbent material. This is a key
advantage over living systems, as dead biomass requires no nutrients or
maintenance and can be stored for extended periods.

## Key Fungal Species for Metal Biosorption

### White-Rot Fungi

- [[phanerochaete-chrysosporium]]: Biosorbs Cd, Cu, Pb, Zn; tolerant to
  multiple metals
- [[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]]: Effective biosorbent for Cu, Pb, Cd; laccase
  activity maintained under metal stress
- [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-pleurotus-ostreatus]]: Used in PAH-heavy metal co-contaminated soils;
  tolerant of Cd and Hg

### Yeasts

- Saccharomyces cerevisiae: Well-characterized biosorbent; effective for
  Cu, Pb, Cd, Zn
- Candida species: Effective biosorbent for various metals

### Molds

- Aspergillus niger: High biosorption capacity; citric acid production
  enhances metal leaching
- Penicillium species: Effective for Cu, Pb, Cd biosorption
- Rhizopus species: Chitin-rich cell walls provide high metal binding
  capacity
- Mucor species: Significant biosorption of Pb and Cd

### Mycorrhizal Fungi

- Ectomycorrhizal species: Accumulate metals from soil, protecting host
  plants
