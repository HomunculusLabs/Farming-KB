# Fungicolous Fungi — Taxonomic Groups and Classification

## Overview

The fungicolous habit is widespread throughout the fungi but is particularly common in certain taxonomic groups. An estimated 2,175 species of [[fungicolous-fungi]] have been documented across all major fungal lineages, distributed among five ecological categories: sporocarp-inhabiting, lichenicolous, biotrophic plant parasite mycoparasites, hyphal mycoparasites, and aquatic mycoparasites. This page provides a systematic overview of the major taxonomic groups containing fungicolous fungi, their characteristics, and notable examples.

## Oomycota

One-celled members of the Olpidiopsidales (*Olpidiopsis*) and Rozellopsidales (*Rozellopsis*, *Dictyomorpha*) form biotrophic associations with diverse Oomycota (particularly Saprolegniales and Pythiales), some Chytridiomycetes, and many algae.

### Key Features

- **Olpidiopsidales**: Sparrow (1960) listed 18 fungicolous species of *Olpidiopsis*
- **Rozellopsidales**: Karling (1981) listed 22 fungicolous species of *Rozellopsis*
- Walled endoparasites form several zoosporangia in one hypertrophied host compartment
- Wall-less endoparasites (some *Rozellopsis*) form a single thallus per host compartment, delimited by a host-induced septum

### Mycoparasitic Pythium Species

Several specialized *Pythium* species in soil are mycoparasitic on congeneric plant pathogens and other fungi. These include four species with spiny oogonia (*P. oligandrum*, *P. acanthicum*, *P. periplocum*, *P. acanthophoron*) and two with smooth oogonia (*P. nunn*, *P. mycoparasiticum*).

Distinguishing features from plant-pathogenic *Pythium*:
- Require organic nitrogen and thiamine for growth
- Noncellulolytic—function as "secondary sugar fungi," profiting from reducing sugars liberated by cellulolytic fungi
- Zoospores encyst on chitinous substrata (vs. cellulose for plant pathogens)
- Form of cyst attachment is a sensitive indicator of taxonomic affinity and determines host–parasite specificity (Deacon 1988a; Jeffries and Young 1994)

## Hyphochytridiomycota

Two genera of Hyphochytridiales include species that parasitize oospores of *[[phytophthora]]* species:
- *Hyphochytridium*: One mycoparasitic species
- *Rhizidiomyces*: Two mycoparasitic species

## Plasmodiophoromycota

A few species of *Woronina*, *Octomyxa*, and *Sorodiscus* (Plasmodiophorales) parasitize Oomycota genera (Saprolegniales and Pythium). They form intracellular naked plasmodia that later transform into cystosori and sporangiosori (Batko 1975; Held 1981).

## Chytridiomycota

One-celled members of the Chytridiomycota live in intracellular or epibiotic parasitic associations, mostly with algae, occasionally with other Chytridiomycota and Oomycota, and rarely with Zygomycota. They cause some host fungi to hypertrophy.

### Endobiotic Taxa

Endobiotic taxa form an intracellular thallus either:
- **Surrounded by a wall** ensheathed in the host's [[endoplasmic-reticulum]] (e.g., *Catenaria allomycis* in *Allomyces*; Sykes and Porter 1980; Powell 1982)
- **Naked** (e.g., *Rozella allomycis* in *Allomyces*; Karling 1942; Held 1981; *Rozella polyphagi* in *Polyphagus euglenae*; Powell 1984)

The naked endobionts consume part of the host cytoplasm by phagocytosis. Their thallus is surrounded only by the host [[plasmalemma]], which disintegrates during sporogenesis.

### Host Defense Responses

When *R. allomycis* invades an *Allomyces* hypha, the host forms lomasomes with apposed wall material, creating an internal papilla. The parasite enters through the center of the papilla with minimal cell disruption. The reaction does not occur in already-infected hyphae, preventing renewed penetration (Held 1972).

### Epibiotic Taxa

*Caulochytrium* and *Sparrowia* grow on a diversity of hosts (Karling 1977). Approximately 45 potentially mycoparasitic Chytridiomycota taxa have been described across Chytridiales and Spizellomycetales.

### Classification by Host Affinity

Held (1981) assigned 25 known mycoparasitic *Rozella* species to five groups:
1. Parasites of monocentric Chytrids
2. Parasites of *Blastocladia* and Leptomitales
3. Parasites of *Monoblepharis* and Pythiaceae
4. Parasites of *Allomyces* and Saprolegniaceae
5. Parasites of miscellaneous hosts

## Zygomycota

### Mucorales

Ten species of sporocarp-inhabiting fungi are documented from the Mucorales.

### Zoopagales

This order includes numerous obligate, biotrophic, contact parasites—both endoparasites and predators of amoebae, nematodes, and other small animals.

**[[piptocephalidaceae]]** (obligate haustorial mycoparasites):
- *[[piptocephalis]]* and *Kuzuhaea* species are purely biotrophic, forming small haustoria in host cells
- *Piptocephalis xenophila* can grow on *Penicillium* and *Chaetomium* species, but all other species grow only on Mucorales sensu lato
- Best growth on media with organic nitrogen sources and low sugar concentration
- Haustoria are enucleate, delimited from host cytoplasm by a very thin electron-transparent wall layer
- Host morphology hardly affected apart from increased branching and decreased marginal hyphal density
- *P. virginiana* can only parasitize fungi where linolenic acid concentration exceeds a certain threshold
- Trehalase activity in sporelings increases during interaction, suggesting trehalose reserves of the host are exploited (Evans et al. 1981)

**Syncephalis species** (more necrotrophic):
- Form a highly branched haustorial system in host hyphae
- Impair host growth and sporulation
- *S. californica* was successfully cultivated axenically on liver medium after transfer from monoxenic culture (Emerson 1958; Ellis 1966)

**Mating type compatibility**: Burgeff (1924) noted that mating type factors often determine compatibility between host and parasite. Plasmatic continuity between sikyotic (contact) cells of parasites (*[[parasitella-parasitica]]*, *Chaetocladium brefeldianum*) and their hosts has been observed.

### Dimargaritales

Comprises mycoparasitic genera *Dimargaris*, *Dispira*, and *Tieghemiomyces*:
- *Dispira cornuta* (Ayers 1935; Brunk and Barnett 1966) is a biotrophic parasite forming nucleate haustoria from gall-like swellings
- Found on rat dung, growing on *Mucor* species and other Mucorales

## Ascomycota

The [[ascomycota]] contain the greatest diversity of fungicolous fungi, with over 1,000 documented species spanning multiple orders.

### Major Orders

**Hypocreales** (excl. Clavicipitaceae): The largest group with ~247 species, including 185 sporocarp-inhabiting, 59 lichenicolous, and 3 biotrophic plant parasite mycoparasites. Many discoveries of anamorph–teleomorph connections have been made in this order since the 1980s.

**Sordariales**: ~95 species (58 sporocarp-inhabiting, 37 lichenicolous)

**Dothideales**: ~264 species (10 sporocarp-inhabiting, 224 lichenicolous, 30 biotrophic plant parasite mycoparasites)

**Leotiales**: ~107 species (40 sporocarp-inhabiting, 67 lichenicolous)

**Ascomycetous conidial fungi**: ~256 species (30 sporocarp-inhabiting, 198 lichenicolous, 27 biotrophic plant parasite mycoparasites, 1 hyphal parasite)

**Verrucariales**: ~191 species (predominantly lichenicolous)

**Arthoniales**: ~150 species (predominantly lichenicolous)

**Lecanorales**: ~112 species (predominantly lichenicolous)

## Basidiomycota

### Tremellales

The Tremellales are the most diverse basidiomycetous group of fungicolous fungi with ~170 species (120 sporocarp-inhabiting, 50 lichenicolous). Many tremelloid fungi exhibit features suggesting evolutionary connections between Tremellales and polypore hosts.

### Urediniomycetes

~93 species, predominantly (92) sporocarp-inhabiting. These include diverse mycoparasites, some of which are also entomogenous or nematophagous.
