## Limitations and Challenges

Several challenges complicate the study of interplant nitrogen transfer.

The addition of 15N to quantify transfer has limitations in fertile soils where soil nitrogen pools are large relative to transferred amounts.

Root depth and uptake patterns may differ between mixed and single-species situations.

It is experimentally difficult to distinguish nitrogen transfer between soil and mycorrhizal pathways.

15N that crosses an air gap through hyphae may leak into soil and be recaptured, confounding measurements of direct transfer.

## Future Research Directions

More research is needed on two-way nitrogen transfers mediated by mycorrhizal networks with different species combinations and under field conditions.

Dual-isotope approaches could enable concurrent measurement of bidirectional transfer in single experiments.

Understanding the factors that determine the direction and magnitude of nitrogen transfer (including plant demand, fungal identity, and environmental conditions) will improve predictions of nutrient cycling in mycorrhizal plant communities.
