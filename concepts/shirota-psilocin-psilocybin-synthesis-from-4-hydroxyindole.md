## Protection Strategy: Acetylation of 4-Hydroxyindole

The synthesis starts with commercially available 4-hydroxyindole. The
hydroxyl group is protected by acetylation using [[acetic-anhydride]] in
pyridine and dichloromethane at 0 degrees Celsius warming to room
temperature. After 2 hours of stirring, water is added and the mixture is
evaporated. The concentrate is dissolved in ethyl acetate, washed with
water and saturated sodium chloride, dried over sodium sulfate, and
concentrated to yield 4-acetylindole as ivory white crystals.

The authors noted that benzyl ether protection was also utilized in
previous syntheses, but this required a separate step for deprotection. The
4-O-benzyl derivative of 4-hydroxyindole was somewhat unstable and not
isolated in crystal form without careful reaction control. The acetyl
[[acetyl-protection-route-psilocin-psilocybin-synthesis]] proved simpler and more practical for scale.

## Oxalyl Chloride Acylation to Glyoxalyl Intermediate

The 4-acetylindole is dissolved in anhydrous [[diethyl-ether]] with stirring
in an ice bath. Oxalyl chloride is added. After 15 minutes, n-hexane is
added and the reaction flask is placed in a freezer overnight. The resulting
yellow crystal of the oxalyl intermediate is separated by filtration and
dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran.

This glyoxalyl intermediate represents the key electrophilic species that
will be coupled with dimethylamine in the next step. The yellow crystalline
nature of this intermediate was advantageous for purification by simple
filtration, avoiding chromatography.

## Dimethylamidation to Glyoxalylamide

To the THF solution of the oxalyl intermediate with stirring in an ice
bath, a 2M dimethylamine THF solution and pyridine are added over 15
minutes. Additional anhydrous ether is added due to solidification, and
the reaction is stirred for 15 minutes at room temperature. The product is
filtered and washed with n-hexane, ethyl acetate, and water to afford
3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-acetylindole as an ivory white crystalline powder
in over 80% yield.

The two-step acylation-amidation conversion from 4-acetylindole to the
glyoxalylamide proceeded in over 80% yield, making it highly efficient for
scale. The crystalline nature of the intermediates at each stage allowed
purification by simple filtration rather than chromatography.

## Reduction to Psilocin

The glyoxalylamide is reduced using lithium aluminum hydride in anhydrous
tetrahydrofuran under argon atmosphere. The solution is added dropwise
over 2 hours and the reaction mixture is refluxed for 2 hours. After
cooling, anhydrous sodium sulfate powder is added, followed by dropwise
addition of saturated sodium sulfate solution.
