# Home Garden Seed Saving

Saving seed from your own garden is one of the most rewarding aspects of [[synthetic-log-cultivation-methods-guide]]
- **Biodiversity**: By growing and saving seed from diverse varieties, you help preserve genetic diversity
- **Tradition**: Seed saving connects you to centuries of gardening tradition

## Which Seeds to Save

Save seed only from open-pollinated varieties. F1 hybrid seed will not breed true — the plants grown from saved hybrid seed will be variable and often inferior to the parent. Check seed packets or catalog descriptions to confirm a variety is open-pollinated.

Some vegetables are easier to save seed from than others:

### Easy

- **Beans** (French, runner, broad): Self-pollinating; simply leave pods on the plant to mature and dry
- **Peas**: Self-pollinating; leave pods to dry on the plant
- **Lettuce**: Allow a few plants to flower and produce seed
- **Tomatoes**: Seed can be extracted from ripe fruit, fermented briefly, and dried

### Moderate

- **Peppers**: Cross-pollinate readily — isolate different varieties or grow only one
- **Chicory and endive**: Biennial — need to overwinter before flowering in the second year

### Difficult

- **Brassicas** (cabbage, broccoli, etc.): Biennial, cross-pollinate freely — need isolation
- **Carrots**: Biennial, cross with wild carrot (Queen Anne's lace) — need careful isolation
- **Onions**: Biennial, need to overwinter bulbs and allow them to flower

## Seed Saving Techniques

### Selecting Parent Plants

Choose the healthiest, most vigorous plants with the best characteristics — flavor, yield, disease resistance, or whatever traits you value most. Do not save seed from weak or diseased plants.

### Harvesting Seed

1. Allow the seed pods or seed heads to mature fully on the plant
2. Harvest on a dry day when the seeds are fully dry
3. For wet seeds (tomatoes, cucumbers): scoop out the seeds, rinse, and dry thoroughly on paper towels
4. For dry seeds (beans, peas): shell the pods and spread the seeds on newspaper to dry completely

### Drying and Storing

- Seeds must be completely dry before storage — any moisture will cause mold or premature germination
- Store in paper envelopes (not plastic, which traps moisture)
- Label clearly with the variety name and year
- Keep in a cool, dry, dark place
- Many seeds remain viable for 2-3 years; some for much longer

### Viability Testing

Before planting, test saved seed by placing a few seeds on damp paper towel in a warm place. After a week or so, count how many have germinated to estimate the germination rate.

## Isolation and Cross-Pollination

Some crops cross-pollinate easily, meaning pollen from one variety can fertilize another. To keep varieties pure, you need to isolate them:

- **Self-pollinating crops** (beans, peas, lettuce): Little risk of crossing — can grow different varieties nearby
- **Insect-pollinated crops** (brassicas, carrots, onions): Need significant isolation — at least 1/4 mile for complete purity. In a small garden, growing only one variety of each is the simplest approach
- **Wind-pollinated crops** ([[fukuoka-natural-cover-grass-green-manure]] crop can also be left to produce beans for eating and seed for the next season. The rotation helps maintain healthy conditions for seed-producing plants.

## Legal Considerations

In many countries, older heritage varieties are not protected by plant breeders' rights, so saving and exchanging their seed is unrestricted. However, some modern varieties may be protected.
