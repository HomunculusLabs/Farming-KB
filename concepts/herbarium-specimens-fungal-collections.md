# Herbarium Specimens and Fungal Collections

Herbarium specimens and live [[brassinosteroids-in-fungal-cultures]] are critical resources
for systematists, ecologists, and conservation biologists. These
collections document species [[guzman-global-distribution-patterns-neurotropic-fungi]], fruiting
phenologies, host associations, and species composition data for
specific sites. Collections that originated more than 100 years ago
provide irreplaceable historical baselines against which to measure
contemporary changes in [[sequestrate-fungi]]. Proper curation and
accessibility of type specimens is essential for taxonomic
stability.

## Culture Collections

Live fungal cultures complement herbarium specimens by preserving
the living organism. Some fungi grow readily in culture, while
others cannot be maintained on artificial media. Cultures are
preserved by lyophilization, storage in liquid nitrogen, or
periodic transfer. Major fungal culture collections include the
American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and numerous
institutional collections. The Kenneth B. Raper Cellular Slime
Mold Culture Collection at Ohio University is a specialized
resource.

## Digital Resources

Electronic databases are increasingly important for managing
and disseminating collection data. Online specimen databases,
digital images, and interactive identification keys enhance
accessibility. The Mycological Resources website provides
comprehensive links to fungal databases and identification
tools. Integration of collection data with geographic
information systems enables biogeographic analyses.
