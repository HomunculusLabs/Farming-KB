# Fungal Species Abundance and Diversity Indices Quantifying [[biodiversity-fungal-biodiversity-estimation-methods]], defined as an enumeration of species in a
particular sample, area, habitat, or substratum. Three kinds of species
richness can be distinguished:

- **Numerical species richness**: species where biomass or number
  of individuals has been standardized.
- **Species density**: species where area, volume, or weight of
  the sampling unit has been standardized.
- **Total species richness**: cumulative species from a series
  of samples from a habitat or substratum.

Environmental variation effects on species density and numerical
species richness are scale-dependent. Most ecological studies of
fungi cover species densities as a consequence of [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] sample
  point from the origin of an S-dimensional hypervolume. Good
  discriminant ability but influenced by sample size.
- **Berger-Parker index (d)**: reflects proportional importance of
  the most abundant species. Poor discriminant ability.
- **Fisher's log-series alpha**: first mathematical model of species-
  abundance relationships. Good discriminant ability, low sensitivity
  to sample size, but unaffected by evenness distribution.
- **Hill's numbers (N0, N1, N2)**: effective numbers of species.
  N0 = total species; N1 = number of abundant species (e to H');
  N2 = number of very abundant species (1/D). Most interpretable.

## Evenness Indices

Each diversity index has a corresponding evenness index measuring the
degree to which a community evinces maximal diversity possible given the
observed richness: e = Diversity(observed) / Diversity(max), where
Dmax is calculated with each taxon assuming 1/S of total abundance.
The Shannon evenness index (E = H'/ln S) and Hill's evenness
(E = N2/N1) are widely used.
