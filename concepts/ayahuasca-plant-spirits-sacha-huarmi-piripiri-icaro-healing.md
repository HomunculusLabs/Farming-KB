# Amazonian Plant Spirits, Sacha Huarmi, Piripiri, and Icaro Healing

## Overview

In the [[amazonian-vegetalista-shamanism-degrees-and-hierarchy]] tradition, the plant world is not passive vegetation but a vast community of sentient beings. Each species possesses its own spirit — a conscious entity capable of communication, teaching, and healing. This animistic worldview, revealed through [[amazonian-shamanic-battles-and-defensive-powers]] medicine and its intricate system of plant-spirit relationships.

## Plant Spirit Cosmology

In ayahuasca visions, plants do not appear merely as botanical specimens. They manifest as sentient beings — as women, animals, luminous spirits, and other animate forms. The fundamental axiom of the vegetalista tradition is that every plant has a spirit that is conscious and can communicate through visions. This is not metaphor but experienced reality within the ceremony.

Among the most significant of these spirit beings are the **sacha-huarmi** — the "women of the forest" (sacha = forest, huarmi = woman in Quechua). The sacha-huarmi are plant spirits that appear in visions as seated women engaged in conversation. They are encountered in certain places in the Amazon, generally where there are large rocks — formations that serve as thresholds or dwelling places for these entities. They are very hard to find and recognize in ordinary consciousness; they are visible only within the heightened perceptual field of ayahuasca visions. The sacha-huarmi are considered guardians and teachers, holding knowledge of plant medicines and the deeper ecological intelligence of the forest.

## Specific Plant Spirits and Medicines

### Caballo Piripiri (*Cyperus* sp.)

The caballo piripiri (horse piripiri) is a [[eleutherine-bulbosa]]*), a red-rooted iris relative whose name incorporates yahuar (blood in Quechua), reflecting its crimson coloration and its association with blood-related illnesses. The icaro of the yahuar-toro cures **malaire** — an illness caused by an evil breeze or malevolent wind — and protects against malevolent beings encountered during ceremonies.

### Ajosquiro Tree

The ajosquiro tree is associated with protective plant medicines. Its name derives from ajo (garlic), suggesting its aromatic and purificatory qualities. Vegetalistas work with ajosquiro for spiritual defense, calling upon its spirit to create barriers against harmful forces.

### Renaquilla

Renaquilla is a plant spirit entity that appears in visions. Like other visionary plant beings, renaquilla represents a specific medicinal intelligence that the vegetalista can learn to invoke and work with through dietas and icaros.

## Icaros — Healing Songs

Icaros are the sacred songs and chants used by vegetalistas to invoke plant spirits and direct healing energy. They are perhaps the single most important tool in the Amazonian healer's repertoire. Each plant, animal, or spirit has its own icaro — a unique melodic and lyrical pattern that serves as its invocation and its signature.

The icaro functions as a kind of **telephone** to communicate with plant spirits during ceremonies. When the vegetalista sings an icaro, they are not merely performing a song; they are establishing a direct line of communication with the spirit being invoked, requesting its presence, its teachings, and its healing power. The icaro of the yahuar-toro, for example, cures malaire and simultaneously protects both patient and healer against malevolent beings.

Icaros are central to Amazonian shamanic healing practice. They structure the ceremony, guide the visionary experience, call in protective spirits, extract illness, and restore balance. A skilled vegetalista may know hundreds of icaros, each learned through rigorous dietas — extended periods of isolation, dietary restriction, and communion with a specific plant teacher.

## Malevolent Beings

The Amazonian spirit world is not exclusively benevolent. Ayahuasca visions may also reveal dangerous entities that the vegetalista must know how to confront and defend against:

- **Ayanahui** (aya = dead person, nahui = eye; the firefly): A malevolent being that harms people, appearing as or associated with fireflies — their flickering light a deceptive lure in the darkness.
- **Chullachaki** (one-footed): A forest spirit that can harm or mislead travelers and those who venture carelessly into the forest. It is one of the most widely feared beings in Amazonian folklore.
- **Maligno**: An evil spirit — a general term for malevolent force that must be countered through icaros, plant protections, and the accumulated defensive knowledge of the vegetalista.

The vegetalista must know how to defend against these beings using icaros and plant protections. This defensive knowledge is as essential as curative knowledge; without it, the healer cannot safely navigate the spirit world.

## Preparation Rituals and Dietas

Plant medicines require strict preparation protocols known as **dietas**. The dieta is a disciplined period of purification and communion with a plant teacher, during which the practitioner ingests the plant while adhering to rigorous restrictions.

After applying the piripiri/jagua mixture, for instance, the practitioner must avoid sun, salt, sweets, garlic, liquor, and pig fat for eight days. These are not arbitrary rules but precise requirements — each prohibited substance interferes with the plant's energetic action in specific ways. Salt, for example, is believed to ground spiritual energy and counteract the plant's visionary effects; sugar overstimulates the system; liquor corrupts the plant's teaching.

The practitioner must also abstain from sex and socializing with sexually active people. Sexual energy is considered a potent force that can disrupt the delicate relationship being established with the plant spirit. These restrictions ensure that the plant's power works correctly and that the transmission of knowledge is clean and undistorted.

The dieta is the primary method through which a vegetalista learns — not through books or instruction, but through direct visionary communion with the plant spirit itself. Each dieta builds the healer's repertoire of icaros, plant allies, and protective knowledge.

## Alchemy and Transformation

Amazonian plant medicine involves a kind of **alchemy** — transformation through plant combinations. The preparation of caballo piripiri with jagua, the combination of multiple plants in a single remedy, the layering of icaros during a ceremony — all of these reflect a sophisticated understanding of plant interactions and synergies.

This knowledge is extensive and precise, developed over millennia of empirical observation and visionary exploration. It represents an indigenous pharmacological system of remarkable depth, one that integrates botanical science, spiritual cosmology, and practical healing into a unified tradition. The combinations are never arbitrary; they follow patterns revealed by the plant spirits themselves during ayahuasca visions, where the inner properties of plants become visible and their relationships to one another can be directly perceived.

In Pablo Amaringo's paintings, this alchemical worldview is rendered visible: plants appear as luminous beings, icaros flow as streams of light and color, and the entire forest reveals itself as a living, communicating, healing intelligence — one that has been cultivated by indigenous peoples for thousands of years and continues to offer its wisdom to those who approach it with respect, discipline, and openness.
