# Nutrient Mobility and Deficiency Diagnosis

Understanding nutrient mobility is essential for
diagnosing plant health problems accurately. Jorge
Cervantes explains that each nutrient can be classified
as either mobile or immobile within the cannabis plant,
and this characteristic determines where deficiency
symptoms first appear. Mastering this concept allows
growers to quickly narrow down the cause of foliage
discoloration and other symptoms.

See also: [[cervantes-macronutrient-deficiency-toxicity]]

## Mobile Nutrients

Mobile nutrients are able to translocate, or move, from
one portion of the plant to another as needed. When a
deficiency occurs, the plant relocates these nutrients
from older tissue to support new growth. Because of this
redistribution, [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]] of mobile nutrients
appear first on the **older, lower leaves**.

The mobile nutrients are:

- **Nitrogen (N):** The most common deficiency.
  Causes yellowing between veins (interveinal
  chlorosis) on lower leaves that progresses upward.
  Stems and leaf undersides may turn reddish-purple.
  Nitrogen is very mobile and dissipates into the
  environment quickly, requiring regular replacement.

- **Phosphorus (P):** Deficiency causes dark green or
  purplish leaves, especially on older foliage.
  Stunted growth and slow development are common.
  Cannabis uses the highest levels during germination,
  seedling, cloning, and flowering.

- **Potassium (K):** Deficiency causes older leaf
  edges and tips to brown and curl. Interveinal
  chlorosis, weak stems, and [[holmgren-long-rotation-forestry-slow-growth]] follow.

- **Magnesium (Mg):** Deficiency shows as interveinal
  chlorosis on older leaves, with veins remaining
  green. As it progresses, leaves may develop rust-
  colored spots and necrotic patches.

- **Zinc (Zn):** Deficiency causes chlorosis between
  veins of new and older leaves, small leaf size,
  and shortened internodes.

## Immobile Nutrients

Immobile nutrients do not translocate to new growing
areas as needed. Once deposited in older leaves, they
remain in place. This is why [[knf-phosphoric-acid-deficiency-symptoms]] of
immobile nutrients appear first on the **upper, new
leaves** at the top of the plant.

The immobile nutrients are:

- **Calcium (Ca):** Deficiency causes distorted new
  growth with curled leaf tips and brown spots.
  Root tips may die back. Often confused with other
  deficiencies.
- **Boron (B):** Deficiency causes growing tips to die,
  brittle stems, and abnormal growth.
- **Chlorine (Cl):** Rarely deficient, but deficiency
  causes wilting, chlorosis, and stubby roots.
- **Copper (Cu):** Deficiency causes young leaves to
  wilt, darkened or bluish-green foliage.
- **Iron (Fe):** Deficiency causes interveinal
  chlorosis on new leaves, similar to magnesium but
  appearing on upper foliage.
- **Manganese (Mn):** Deficiency causes interveinal
  chlorosis on younger leaves, similar to iron
  deficiency but with less contrast.
- **Molybdenum (Mo):** Deficiency resembles nitrogen
  deficiency, causing middle-aged leaves to yellow.
