# Salad and Braising Mix Production

Baby-leaf (mesclun) salads are an ideal crop for the [[coleman-baby-leaf-salad-mix-quality-standards]]
ingredients that are too large, bitter, tough, stringy, or frost
damaged. If an ingredient does not meet standards, it is left out of
the mix until conditions improve. These evaluations have led to
several management adjustments:

- Moving cold-sensitive crops like claytonia from colder edge beds
  to warmer inner beds for midwinter cold-house harvest.
- Increasing succession plantings of minutina and narrow-stem chard
  for more young leaves rather than relying on regrowth.
- Cutting back crops like red-oak lettuce earlier because regrowth
  is hardier than initial leaves.
- Continual trial plantings of standard and potential crops to
  identify better cultivars and discover new flavors, shapes, and
  textures.

The mix is composed only of whole baby leaves, ideally no more than
three inches in length. Leaves should easily fit onto a fork and into
the mouth. Overgrown leaves (such as mizuna with seven- to
eight-inch stems) are better suited to a braising mix or sold bunched
as cooking greens. The farm does not chop mature plants into small
pieces like the cut-up endive and radicchio seen in supermarket mixes.

## Specific Ingredient Choices

For endive, the farm grows 'Indivia Bianca Riccia da Taglio' (white
curly endive for cutting), sold by Johnny's Seeds as 'Bianca Riccia',
which has nice light-colored frilly leaves at the baby-leaf stage.
For red color, 'Bull's Blood' beet leaves are preferred over radicchio
because they lack radicchio's bitter edge under poor growing
conditions. Cool winter temperatures keep arugula and watercress from
becoming too strong and biting.

The farm deliberately does not add hardy oriental greens to the salad
mix because customers prefer a milder salad without mustard tang. Many
parents report this is the only mixed salad their children will eat.

## Managing Winter Variability

Lettuce, endive, and arugula do not consistently meet quality
standards during the coldest parts of January and February in the
cold houses. These three are given protection in a [[coleman-cold-hardy-varieties]] at the baby-leaf stage but quality is still
better from the cool house. 'Sylvetta' wild arugula is perfectly
hardy but requires more study of its soil-fertility needs and planting
timing.

Despite preferring a consistent mix with the same percentage of each
leaf type, extreme weather, poor germination, or quality issues can
force modifications. The most extreme variations were mixes that were
50 percent claytonia and 50 percent baby spinach. The key is
maintaining high quality even when consistency slips temporarily.

## Braising Mix

The braising mix is made up of hardy Asian greens, cut slightly
larger than baby-leaf salad but never more than 4 inches long unless
absolutely necessary. The braising mix excludes any leaves not
normally cooked: no mache, lettuce, claytonia, or endive.

The mix includes spinach, chard, and all the Asian greens (mizuna,
mibuna, tatsoi, pak choi, tokyo bekana, etc.). Red-leaved mustards
are excluded because they lack sufficient color in winter; 'Bull's
Blood' beet leaves provide red color instead. Spinach is not
necessary if the tatsoi supply is good, but chard leaves add
tenderness. Many customers enjoy this mix as a raw salad, so it is
sold as an Asian salad/braising mix.
