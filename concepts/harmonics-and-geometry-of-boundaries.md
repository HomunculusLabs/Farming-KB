# Harmonics and Geometry of Boundaries

## Overview

The amplitude, configuration, and periodicity of edges, surfaces, and boundaries can be deliberately varied by design to achieve specific functional outcomes. The geometry of boundaries is not merely aesthetic — it directly affects flow dynamics, species distribution, microclimate, and resource accumulation. Understanding edge harmonics allows [[edge-effect]] extent**: For wheat and lucerne, the edge effect extends approximately 1 meter into each crop
2. **Calculate the double edge width**: This gives a 2-meter wide zone of higher yield
3. **Design strip widths**: Sow fields in 2-meter wide alternate strips of each crop, creating a field of nothing but edge

This approach can yield approximately the same total production from a given area as sowing twice the area to single-crop stands, because the entire field operates at edge-level productivity.

## Zone or Edge Cropping

Extending the strip-cropping principle to many varied crops on a broader scale produces the concept of **zone or [[edge-cropping]]**. This creates a matrix of hedgerows or edge-rows, each suited in width to a particular crop. Such zonal strips occur naturally on coasts and around saltpans or waterholes.

This approach has immense potential for smallholders in a single land trust, where each person tends one or more crop strips. It resembles older patterns of French-intensive agriculture and the farmed strips of modern Quebec, producing very productive crop mosaics. Polycultures can be composed of such mosaics or zonal strips.

## Compatibility at Edges

Not all edge interactions are beneficial. The possible interactions between two systems at a boundary include:

| Interaction | Effect |
|---|---|
| (0, 0) | No difference in yields, stability, or growth |
| (+, -) or (-, +) | One benefits at the expense of the other |
| (+, +) | Both benefit |
| (-, -) | Both are decreased |
| (+, 0) or (0, +) | One benefits, the other is unaffected |
| (-, 0) or (0, -) | One is decreased, the other is unaffected |

### Resolving Incompatibility
For cases of mutually negative interaction (-,-), active intervention with a component acceptable to both systems may work. The design strategy is to place an intervening, mutually-compatible component between two incompatible systems. Natural interveners often arise as hybrids between apparently antagonistic systems.

### Mosaic Design
Mosaic design — the opposite of monoculture — means creating many small areas of differences. A few mistakes will occur, but good average benefit will result. This was the traditional tribal strategy for landscape management. The golden rule of design in this context is:

> Keep it small, and keep it varied.

## Trade and Transaction at Boundaries

When a boundary separates two things which differ, there is an opportunity for trade, transactions, or translation across the border. Differences make trade — every medium seeks to gather in those things it lacks, and which occur in the other medium.

The translator is often of neither medium but a thing in itself — the "connection or path between," created from the media but with its own unique characteristics. Plants, people, and pipes are all translators. In design, the goal is to make flow function in the local system before allowing it to pass to other systems. Each function carried out by information flow builds a local resource and a yield.

## Human Affinity for Edges

People are fundamentally drawn to edges and boundaries. Even dwellings accumulate at the junction of sea and land, on estuaries, and at the edge of forest, river, marsh, or plain. Invariant ecologies may attract the simple-minded planner, but they will not attract people as inhabitants or explorers. The richness and stimulation of edges is both a biological necessity and a psychological preference.
