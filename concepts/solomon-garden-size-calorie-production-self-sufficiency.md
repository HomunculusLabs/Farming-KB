## Garden Sizing for Calorie Production and Self-Sufficiency

One of the most practical questions for anyone growing their own food is:
*how much land do I actually need?* Steve Solomon's analysis draws on
historical data, his own decades of gardening, and soil science to offer
concrete benchmarks.

## The Wartime Allotment Benchmark

During World War II, the British government allocated standard allotment plots
of roughly 2,700 square feet to civilians for food production. Solomon cites
this as a well-documented real-world trial. A typical family working a plot
this size in the cool, cloudy UK climate could produce about **half of their
daily calories** from it — provided they focused on calorie-dense crops.

This figure assumes the family obtained milk, bread, and meat from other sources. The allotment was not expected to supply complete nutrition, but it made a serious dent in the household calorie budget.

## Rough Sizing Guidelines

Solomon offers these rough gauges for calorie-oriented [[dryland-vegetable-gardening]]:

- **2,700 sq ft** — roughly half the calories for two adults in a cool, cloudy
  UK-type climate with adequate rainfall.
- **~4,500 sq ft (420 sq m)** — the same production level in drier North
  American climates east of the 98th meridian, where hotter summers and less
  reliable rainfall demand wider spacing.
- **2,000 sq ft** of active growing beds can supply significant nutrition;
  doubled to **4,000+ sq ft** when using a drought-gardening for dry-climate techniques.
- **Climate and season length** — frost-free days, latitude, and overall
  warmth determine what can be grown and how many successions are possible.
- **Vegetable varieties** — some varieties are bred for yield; others for
  flavor, appearance, or shipping tolerance.
- **Seed quality** — open-pollinated seed adapted to local conditions
  outperforms generic seed in marginal conditions.

## The Potato as Foundation Calorie Crop

Solomon strongly advocates the [[solomon-root-cellaring-food-preservation]] conditions, potatoes keep
  for many months without processing.

By contrast, growing cereal grains (wheat, oats, corn) for calories requires
roughly **5–10 times as much land per person** as potato production. For
gardeners without acreage, grains are impractical; potatoes are the rational
choice.
