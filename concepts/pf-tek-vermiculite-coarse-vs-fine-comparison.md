# PF Tek Vermiculite Coarse vs Fine Comparison

## Overview

Vermiculite is described by [[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] as the "magic
ingredient" of the PF Tek. However, not all vermiculite is the same.
The coarseness varies considerably between brands, and this variation
directly affects water holding capacity, substrate consistency, and
ultimately cultivation success. Fanaticus provided detailed guidance
on selecting and working with different vermiculite grades.

## Particle Size Classification

### Fine Vermiculite (Recommended)

- **Average particle size**: approximately 1 millimetre across
- Individual particles range from smaller to slightly larger than 1mm
- Feels similar to coarse sand or fine gravel
- **Preferred for PF Tek** due to superior water retention

### Coarse Vermiculite

- **Average particle size**: approximately 4-5 millimetres across
- Some particles up to 8 millimetres
- Feels similar to small pebbles or perlite
- Holds less water than fine grade
- Requires formula adjustments for PF Tek use

## How to Assess Vermiculite Grade

Fanaticus recommended using a photo magnifier (magnifying glass with
measurement scale) to observe vermiculite particles next to a
millimetre ruler. This allows objective comparison:

1. Place a small sample on a flat surface
2. Observe through a magnifier alongside a ruler marked in millimetres
3. Measure several representative particles to determine average size
4. Classify as fine (~1mm), medium (~2-3mm), or coarse (~4-5mm+)

## Water Retention Differences

The primary functional difference between grades is water holding
capacity:

### Fine Vermiculite
- Higher surface area to volume ratio
- More capillary spaces between particles
- Absorbs and retains more water per unit volume
- Creates a substrate with optimal moisture content using standard
  PF Tek water amounts
- Better for the basic formula and [[pf-tek-maximum-fruiting-substrate-formula]] formula

### Coarse Vermiculite
- Lower surface area to volume ratio
- Larger air gaps between particles
- Drains more freely, retains less water
- Standard PF Tek water amounts may produce a wet, sloppy substrate
- Requires reducing water in the formula to compensate

## Formula Adjustment for Coarse Vermiculite

If using coarse vermiculite, Fanaticus advised:

- **Reduce the water amount** in the [[pf-tek-measurement-calibration-and-substrate-formula-precision]]
- If the mixture is "really wet or sloppy," use less water
- Keep notes on formula variations for replicating the substrate
  that fruits best
- It may take several attempts to dial in the correct water
  amount for a specific brand of coarse vermiculite

## Brand and Regional Variability

Fanaticus noted significant variability in vermiculite products:

- **Stores usually carry one type or the other**, rarely both
- There are **regional differences** between brands
- Shopping around to compare different brands is worthwhile
- Try to obtain both fine and coarse types for comparison

The vermiculite available at any given garden centre or hardware
store may differ from what another cultivator is using, which can
explain why identical-looking substrate formulas produce different
results for different people.

## Why Fine Vermiculite Is Preferred

Fanaticus explicitly recommended the finer type over the coarser
type for several reasons:

1. **More water retention**: Critical for [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]], which
   requires consistent moisture throughout the colonisation period
2. **Better substrate consistency**: Fine vermiculite creates a
   more uniform substrate mixture with brown rice powder
3. **Standard formula compatibility**: The published PF Tek water
   amounts are calibrated for fine vermiculite
4. **Dry barrier effectiveness**: The fine grade works better as
   the [[pf-tek-dry-vermiculite-contaminant-barrier]] contaminant barrier at the top of the jar

## The Role of Vermiculite in the PF Tek

Vermiculite serves multiple critical functions:

1. **Water reservoir**: Absorbs and slowly releases water to the
   mycelium during colonisation
2. **Aeration**: Creates air spaces in the substrate, preventing
   the dense, airless mass that forms when mycelium is grown in
   grain alone
3. **Contaminant barrier**: A dry vermiculite layer at the top of
   each jar protects the wet substrate from airborne contaminants
   and regulates moisture transpiration and condensation
4. **Structure**: Provides physical structure for the substrate
   cake, supporting fruiting when the cake is removed from the jar

The last function is the key innovation Fanaticus identified:
when mycelium is grown in just grain, it turns into a mass with
little air space. But with vermiculite, "the mycelial threads
stretch across space," creating a lighter, more productive
substrate that mimics natural growing conditions.

## Vermiculite as the "Secret" of the PF Tek

Fanaticus stated that "the secret is in the vermiculite." The PF
Tek's success in enabling easy [[mushroom-home-cultivation-history]] with common kitchen
equipment depends fundamentally on vermiculite's unique properties
of water retention, aeration, and structural support. Without
vermiculite, the brown rice method produces a dense, poorly
aerated substrate that is prone to contamination and produces
inferior yields.
