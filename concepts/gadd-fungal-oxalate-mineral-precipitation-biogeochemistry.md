# Fungal Oxalate Mineral Precipitation and Biogeochemistry

## Overview

Fungi produce metal oxalates with a variety of metals and metal-bearing
minerals, playing a significant role [[mycorrhizal-fungi-in-biogeochemical-cycling]], soil formation,
and metal detoxification. [[fungal-calcium-oxalate-and-carbonate-precipitation]] is the most common fungal oxalate
in nature, occurring in several crystalline forms and associated with diverse
ecophysiological groups. These mycogenic minerals influence soil structure,
[[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]], and metal mobility.

## Calcium Oxalate: The Dominant Fungal Oxalate

### Crystalline Forms

Calcium oxalate occurs in two primary hydration states:
- **Weddellite** (CaC2O4·2H2O): Less stable dihydrate form
- **Whewellite** (CaC2O4·H2O): More stable, thermodynamically favored monohydrate

Biotic fungal calcium oxalate exhibits varied crystalline morphologies
including tetragonal, bipyramidal, plate-like, rhombohedral, and needle-like
crystals depending on physicochemical conditions.

### Ecological Functions

Calcium oxalate precipitation serves multiple ecological roles: acting as a
calcium reservoir (important in calcium-poor environments), influencing
phosphate availability through interactions with [[bionutrients-lab-calcium-phosphate-calamansi]] minerals,
contributing to formation of calcic and petrocalcic soil horizons, and linking
to carbon dioxide dynamics through oxalate degradation to carbonate.

### Carbonate Transformation

Calcium oxalate can degrade to calcium carbonate (calcite) in semi-arid
environments: organic oxalate is metabolized releasing CO2, calcium carbonate
precipitates, and this process may cement pre-existing limestones. During
fungal hypha decomposition, calcite crystals serve as nucleation sites for
further secondary calcite precipitation, with chitin in fungal cell walls
acting as a substrate for calcite nucleation.

## Metal Oxalate Production

### Range of Metals

Fungi produce oxalates with Ca (most common), Cd, Co, Cu (forming moolooite),
Mn, Sr, Zn, Ni, and Pb.

### Beauveria caledonica: Oxalate Over-Production

The oxalate over-excreting fungus *Beauveria caledonica* demonstrated
remarkable metal tolerance by transforming cadmium, copper, lead, and zinc
from toxic metal minerals into corresponding metal oxalates, suggesting oxalate
excretion serves as a general detoxification strategy.

### Factors Enhancing Oxalate Excretion

- **Nitrogen source**: NO3- enhances oxalate excretion compared to NH4+
- **Bicarbonate**: HCO3- stimulates oxalate production
- **Calcium**: Ca2+ promotes calcium oxalate precipitation
- **Toxic metals**: Cu, Al and others stimulate oxalate over-production as
  a [[fukuoka-textdoc-disease-as-natures-self-defense-mechanism]]
- **Mineral substrates**: Pyromorphite and zinc phosphate trigger enhanced
  excretion

## Bioweathering Mechanisms

### The Three Solubilization Pathways

1. **Acidolysis** (proton-promoted dissolution): Fungi acidify their
   microenvironment through proton and organic acid excretion, plus carbonic
   acid from respiratory CO2. This is the main mechanism for toxic metal
   phosphate dissolution when ammonium is the nitrogen source.

2. **Complexolysis** (ligand-promoted dissolution): Fungi excrete
   metal-complexing metabolites including carboxylic acids, amino acids,
   siderophores, and [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]. When large amounts of strong
   chelators like oxalate are excreted, the mechanism shifts to
   ligand-promoted dissolution.

3. **Redoxolysis**: Redox transformations including reduction of Ag, Se, Te
   to elemental forms and oxidation of Mn and Fe from carbonate to oxide forms.
