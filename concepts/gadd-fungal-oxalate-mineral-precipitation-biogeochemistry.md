### Carbonate Transformation

Calcium oxalate can degrade to calcium carbonate (calcite) in semi-arid
environments: organic oxalate is metabolized releasing CO2, calcium carbonate
precipitates, and this process may cement pre-existing limestones. During
fungal hypha decomposition, calcite crystals serve as nucleation sites for
further secondary calcite precipitation, with chitin in fungal cell walls
acting as a substrate for calcite nucleation.

## Metal Oxalate Production

### Range of Metals

Fungi produce oxalates with Ca (most common), Cd, Co, Cu (forming moolooite),
Mn, Sr, Zn, Ni, and Pb.

### Beauveria caledonica: Oxalate Over-Production

The oxalate over-excreting fungus *Beauveria caledonica* demonstrated
remarkable metal tolerance by transforming cadmium, copper, lead, and zinc
from toxic metal minerals into corresponding metal oxalates, suggesting oxalate
excretion serves as a general detoxification strategy.
