# Soil Pollution and Human Health

## The Scale of Agricultural Soil Pollution

Orchards, sugarcane areas, pineapple, cotton, tobacco, and banana plantations have received extensive applications of mineral additives, arsenicals, Aldrin, DDT, copper salts, and dioxins. The persistence of these contaminants is remarkable: even after 18–20 years without chemical use, apple orchards in West Australia produced unsafe levels of Aldrin and Dieldrin in the eggs of free-range chickens. Attempts to grow prawns in ponds on such lands failed due to residual Dieldrin levels in the soils.

## Persistent Chemical Contaminants

### Heavy Metals in Orchard Soils

Large quantities of lead, arsenic, copper, and persistent biocides have been applied on most apple orchards:

- **Copper and lead** stay at or near the soil surface in high concentrations
- **Arsenic** may remain at the surface in clay soils, or leach to subsoil (50–60 cm deep) in acid sands
- **Phosphate application** may dislodge arsenic to deeper levels
- **Copper in pasture plants** under orchards can reach 50 ppm—poisonous to sheep, causing toxaemic jaundice (liver poisoning) and blood in urine
- **Molybdenum, zinc, and sulphur** may buffer copper uptake in sheep
- Plant uptake of copper and lead **increases with temperature and acidity**
- **Arsenic uptake** is not related to acidity
- **Silver beet (Swiss chard)** fed with nitrogen shows high arsenic concentrations but may then be unsuitable for children due to high nitrate levels

### Lead Contamination

Worst cases occur in urban areas with older buildings, where lead from car exhausts and lead paint, pipes, and battery burning can reach 1,100 ppm. Lead is both taken up by and dusted onto vegetable surfaces. Remediation strategies include:

- Using cracked bricks or gravel as a base
- Building up raised beds to 30 cm deep with rich composted soil of over 40% organic content
- Growing vegetables and having leaf analyses performed
- Washing produce in dilute vinegar if leaded petrol is still used locally

### Persistent Biocides

DDT, Aldrin, Dieldrin, BHC, and similar compounds persist in soils for decades. If inheriting an old orchard, canefield, or plantation (banana, pineapple, cotton, tobacco), it is unlikely that any animal product (milk, eggs, meat) will be free of high biocide levels. The only option is to convert to forestry and non-food crop production until remediation methods are developed.

### Groundwater Contamination

Groundwater beneath farms can contain 80–90 biocidal substances derived from fertilisers, sprays, and fuels. Near industrial waste dumps, dioxins, radioactives, and heavy metal wastes (cadmium, chromium, mercury) compound the problem. Key recommendations:

- Do not use untested wells or bore waters for any purpose
- Drink tank water (rainwater harvest)
- Harvest surface run-off for gardens
