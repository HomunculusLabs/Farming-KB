# Oyster Mushroom Harvest Techniques

Harvest timing and technique directly impact yield, quality, and the
viability of subsequent flushes. Oyster mushrooms typically produce 2-4
flushes per substrate block, with the first flush yielding 50-70% of
total production. Proper handling during and after harvest preserves
shelf life and market value.

## Determining Harvest Readiness

Mushrooms should be harvested when caps have fully expanded but before
the margin begins to curl upward significantly. The ideal harvest window
is narrow — typically 12-24 hours for most oyster varieties.

Visual indicators of readiness:
- **Cap shape**: Convex to flat. Slight upward curling at the margin is
  acceptable but pronounced curling indicates overmaturity.
- **Spore production**: If the gill surface is dropping visible spore
  deposits, the mushroom is past optimal harvest. Spore release reduces
  shelf life and creates a messy harvest area.
- **Stem texture**: Stems should be firm but not woody. King oyster
  (*P. eryngii*) stems should be harvested before they become tough.
- **Color**: Rich, species-typical coloration. Fading or yellowing
  suggests aging.

Size-based harvesting is common in commercial operations: clusters are
harvested once the majority of individual mushrooms reach target size,
even if some are slightly under or over.

## Harvest Methods

### Cluster Harvesting

Most *Pleurotus* species grow in shelf-like clusters (bouquets) that
are harvested as a unit. This is the standard method for oyster
mushrooms:

1. Grasp the entire cluster at the base.
2. Twist gently while pulling upward and slightly sideways.
3. Avoid cutting with knives when possible — the remaining stump can
  become a vector for bacterial infection on subsequent flushes.
4. If the cluster does not detach cleanly, a single clean cut at the
  base with a sharp, sanitized knife is acceptable.

### Individual Mushroom Harvesting

Used primarily for king oyster mushrooms (*P. eryngii*), which produce
large, solitary fruiting bodies with thick stems. Each mushroom is cut
individually at the base with a sanitized knife, leaving minimal stump.

## Post-Harvest Handling

### Temperature Management

Rapid cooling after harvest is essential for shelf life. Mushrooms
continue to respire after harvest, generating heat and moisture:

- **Optimal storage temperature**: 1-4 °C.
- **Target cooling time**: Within 1-2 hours of harvest.
- **Cold chain maintenance**: Keep at 1-4 °C through distribution.
- **Shelf life at proper temperature**: 7-14 days depending on species
  and packaging.

### Packaging

Oyster mushrooms are highly perishable and benefit from modified
atmosphere packaging (MAP):

- **Paper bags**: Most sustainable option, provides good moisture
  regulation. Shorter shelf life (5-7 days).
- **Punnet trays with overwrap**: Standard retail packaging. Allows
  consumer inspection while maintaining humidity.
- **Flow-wrap with micro-perforations**: Extends shelf life to 10-14
  days. Requires specific equipment investment.

Do not seal oyster mushrooms in airtight plastic — accumulated moisture
and CO₂ accelerate decay.

### Cleaning

Avoid washing mushrooms if possible. Brush off substrate debris with a
soft brush or damp cloth. Water exposure promotes rapid bacterial
growth and significantly reduces shelf life. If washing is unavoidable,
ensure thorough air drying before packaging.

## Flush Management

After the first harvest, substrate blocks continue to produce additional
flushes. Management between flushes affects total yield:

- **Rest period**: Allow blocks to rest 7-14 days between flushes. The mycelium recovers energy reserves during this time.
- **Soaking (cold shock)**: Submerging blocks in cold water (4-10 °C)
  for 6-12 hours between flushes can stimulate additional fruiting and
  rehydrate the substrate. Not all growers use this technique.
- **Second flush yields**: Typically 30-50% of first flush.
- **Third flush yields**: 10-20% of first flush. Many operations stop
  after the second or third flush due to diminishing returns and
  increasing contamination risk.

## Yield Expectations

Typical yields per kilogram of dry substrate:
- Straw-based, unsupplemented: 30-60 kg fresh mushrooms (30-60% BE).
- Straw-based, supplemented: 60-100 kg fresh (60-100% BE).
- Sawdust-based, supplemented: 80-150 kg fresh (80-150% BE).

King oyster tends toward lower BE (50-80%) but commands higher market
prices due to thick, meaty stems. Pink and golden oysters achieve high
BE (100-150%) but have very short shelf life (3-5 days), limiting
distribution radius.

## Common Harvest Mistakes

- **Waiting too long**: Overmature mushrooms have tough texture, bitter
  flavor, reduced shelf life, and reduced subsequent flush yields.
- **Tearing the substrate**: Rough pulling can remove chunks of
  colonized substrate, creating infection sites and reducing future
  yields.
- **Leaving stumps**: Cut stumps too short or too long. Short stumps
  may expose substrate; long stumps rot and contaminate.
- **Delayed cooling**: Every hour of delay at room temperature
  measurably reduces shelf life.
