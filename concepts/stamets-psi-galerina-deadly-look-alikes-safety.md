## Additional Deadly Look-alikes

Beyond Galerina, another deadly species poses a significant risk:
Galerina venenata also contains amatoxins similar to those found in the
most deadly Amanitas. This species can be confused with small Psilocybes
or other lawn mushrooms. There are probably more amatoxin-containing
species than presently realized.

Other brown-spored genera containing poisonous species include Inocybe,
Hebeloma, and Cortinarius. Additionally, Gymnopilus species produce
rusty orange to yellow-orange spore prints and can resemble Galerinas to
the untrained eye. A mistake between Gymnopilus and Galerina could be
deadly.

## The Danger of Overlapping Habitats

Stamets describes dramatic examples of deadly and psilocybin-producing
mushrooms growing side by side. In one documented case, colonies of
deadly Galerinas and [[amanita-muscaria-legal-alternative-psilocybin-mushrooms-europe]] had overlapped to the point
where the stems of the poisonous and psilocybin varieties were actually
touching. Amateur collectors would not be knowledgeable enough to
separate the deadly species from the psilocybin one.

Similarly, deadly Galerinas have been found growing near Psilocybe
cyanescens, [[psilocybe-semilanceata]]. In some
cases, woodland Galerinas coexist in the same habitat as grassland
Psilocybes, especially in lands recently converted to pastures. This
habitat overlap is one of the most dangerous aspects of psilocybin
foraging.
