## Biological Sewage Treatment with Plants

Higher aquatic plants provide sophisticated biological treatment of sewage and polluted water, capable of removing heavy metals, human pathogens, and even breaking down halogenated hydrocarbons synthesised as herbicides and pesticides. These plant-based systems represent the most advanced stage of natural sewage treatment, following anaerobic digestion and facultative/aerobic pond processing.

## Algal Sewage Treatment Systems

### Israeli Canal Method

Israel leads in algal sewage treatment (New Scientist, 22 Feb 1979). Sewage water flows through long canalised ponds agitated by slowly-revolving paddle-wheel aerators. Ponds are 0.5 m or less deep. Under bright sunlight (or greenhouse covers), dense algal mats form and are:

1. Broken up by addition of aluminium sulphate (a pollutant trade-off)
2. Skimmed off
3. Drained and centrifuged
4. Steam-dried
5. Fed to carp or chickens

Algal protein replaces 50% of soya bean protein in poultry feed rations. Total treatment takes approximately 4 days. Water remains alkaline and somewhat anaerobic, requiring more agitation in winter.

### Dutch Reed Method

Holland runs sewage through similar canals, harvesting reeds or plants as green crop or craft supplies.

## Alternating Aerobic and Anaerobic (A.A.A.) Digestion

Research (Ecos 44, Winter 1985) found that artificial aeration of facultative ponds is most efficient when run at **intervals of two hours in six** (30% of the time). Facultative bacteria operate in two digestive modes and perform best when a rush of air follows a four-hour anaerobic period, excreting carbon dioxide and reducing sludge bulk.

### Performance Results

- Nitrogen reduced from 20 mg/L to less than 5 mg/L
- Phosphorus reduced from 8.5 mg/L to less than 1 mg/L (with ferric chloride addition)
- Significant energy cost reductions for aeration

### Combined System Benefits

Agitation of anaerobic systems by bubbling compressed methane, combined with A.A.A. digestion of facultative ponds, yields:
- Useful methane quantities
- High-protein algae from sewage
- Efficient nutrient removal
