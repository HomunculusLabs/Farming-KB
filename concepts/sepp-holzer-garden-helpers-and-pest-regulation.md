Sepp Holzer's approach to [[cannabis-pest-management-guide]] is fundamentally different from conventional agriculture. Rather than fighting pests, he designs systems where natural predators and [[fukuoka-ecological-balance-principles]] keep potential problems in check.

## Core Philosophy

"It is not a question of what can I do to fight the 'pests', but what can I do for them, so that they will not cause damage and even work to my benefit."

## Voles and Decoy Plants

Voles can cause significant damage by eating plant roots. Rather than using poison, Holzer uses decoy plants:
- [[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]] and black salsify make excellent decoy plants
- Provide enough decoy plants and voles leave [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]] alone
- The energy required to repair soil damage from poison is far greater than the supposed damage from voles eating crops
- Ensure voles always have enough decoy plants available

## Slugs and Snails

The non-indigenous Spanish slug (Arion vulgaris) breeds prolifically -- up to 15 per square metre in affected areas. Holzer's integrated approach:

### Sawdust Barrier Method
1. Mix very dry fine sawdust (from untreated natural wood, ideally from a carpenter's workshop) with wood ash (1:10 ratio) or quicklime powder (1:20)
2. Cut a watering can spout to half length for wider distribution
3. Pour a finger's width border of the dry mixture around the vegetable patch
4. Clear vegetation from the border area first
5. Keep the border as dry as possible; replace after rain
6. The fine dry sawdust adheres to the slug's foot; ash and quicklime extract moisture, preventing passage
7. Slugs visibly turn around when they reach the barrier

### Egg Collection Method
1. Create rows of freshly cut grass and leaves, piled higher and more compacted than mulch
2. Keep rows moist to provide ideal egg-laying habitat
3. Slugs travel great distances to use these sites
4. On a sunny day, turn over the rows with a gardening fork
5. Whole clusters of eggs will have adhered to the rotting grass
6. Turn rows at midday when sun is strongest -- eggs are rapidly destroyed by heat and UV rays
7. Neighbours using the same method multiply the effect

### Natural Predators
Hedgehogs, shrews, lizards, toads, and many kinds of ground beetle are excellent slug predators. The edible snail (Helix pomatia) also helps by eating slug eggs -- "so not all snails are harmful!"

## Earthworms: Nature's Ploughs

Earthworms are among the most important garden helpers. Three local varieties at the Krameterhof:
- **Brandling worm** (Eisenia foetida): dark red with yellow bands, epigeal (surface-dwelling), processes large amounts of organic material, provides the best compost
- **Red earthworm** (Lumbricus rubellus): spends youth on surface, later burrows into deeper soil layers, aerates soil
- **Common earthworm** (Lumbricus terrestris): creates burrows up to three metres deep, provides drainage system, retains moisture, prevents erosion

### Benefits
- Earthworm casts contain more nitrogen, potassium, phosphorus, and calcium than the best garden soil
- Crumbly consistency provides good soil structure
- Tunnels improve drainage, moisture retention, and erosion resistance
- Plant roots extend better through worm tunnels
- Healthier vegetation more resistant to disease

### Providing Optimal Conditions
- Permanent soil cover (mixed crops, mulch) -- earthworms are sensitive to UV rays
- Avoid harvesting large areas all at once
- Mulch attracts earthworms
- If few earthworms present, breed them yourself

### Breeding Earthworms
- Small scale: wooden box of one cubic metre
- Substrate: mixture of straw, cardboard, soil, and a little dung
- Incorporate layers of branches, leaves, and roots for aeration
- Any cooking waste as food (except onions and garlic)
- Worms particularly like used coffee filters with grounds
- Regular supply of [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]; rate matched to worm population
- Room temperature ideal
- Steady moisture balance, good oxygen supply
- Drill holes in bottom to prevent water buildup
- Observe regularly; intuition is important for optimal conditions
- Greenhouse method: breed directly in soil using a pile with a central depression for daily feeding

### Application
- Scatter bred worms with soil and worm eggs over new terraces and raised beds in the evening during wet weather
- Use nutrient-rich worm humus for especially valuable and demanding plants
