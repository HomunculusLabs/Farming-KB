## Nitrogen in Plant Nutrition — The Most Limiting Macronutrient

Nitrogen (N) is the nutrient most commonly limiting plant growth worldwide. It is a primary component of amino acids, proteins, chlorophyll, nucleic acids, and many other essential compounds. Understanding [[fertilizer-pollution-environmental-impact]])

## The Nitrogen Cycle

### Forms of Nitrogen
1. **Atmospheric N2 (78% of atmosphere):** Biologically unavailable to most organisms
2. **Ammonium (NH4+):** Plant-available; positively charged (held by CEC)
3. **Nitrate (NO3-):** Plant-available; negatively charged (leaches easily)
4. **Organic nitrogen:** In proteins, amino acids, humus; must be mineralized

### Key Processes

**[[ozier-pdc-composting-and-soil-building]]

**Nitrification:**
- NH4+ → NO2- (nitrite) → NO3- (nitrate)
- Carried out by Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter bacteria
- Produces nitrate which is mobile and easily leached
- Inhibited by low temperature, low oxygen, acidic pH
- [[dighton-fungal-greenhouse-gas-nitrogen-saturation]]

**Immobilization:**
- Inorganic N → Organic N (microbial biomass)
- Occurs when soil organisms incorporate N into their bodies
- Net immobilization when high C:N materials decompose
- Net mineralization when low C:N materials decompose

## C:N Ratio and Nitrogen Availability

The carbon-to-nitrogen ratio of organic materials determines whether nitrogen is released or tied up during decomposition:

| Material | C:N Ratio | Effect on Soil N |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Fresh manure | 5-25:1 | Releases N quickly |
| Legume residue | 15-25:1 | Releases N |
| Finished compost | 15-20:1 | Releases N slowly |
| Grass clippings | 15-25:1 | Releases N |
| Young leaves | 20-30:1 | Balanced |
| Straw | 80:1 | Ties up N |
| Sawdust | 400:1 | Strongly ties up N |
