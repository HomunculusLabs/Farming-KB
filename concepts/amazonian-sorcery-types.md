# Amazonian Sorcery Types

In the Amazonian vegetalismo tradition, the ability to cause harm through magical means is as developed as the ability to heal. [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]]'s [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]] reveal a detailed taxonomy of sorcerers, their methods, the tools they employ, and the diagnostic techniques used by curanderos to identify and counteract specific forms of magical attack. Understanding these categories is essential for any study of [[amazonian-shamanic-battles-and-defensive-powers]] warfare.

## The Three Principal Types of Sorcerers

### The Chontero (Dart Sorcerer)

The chontero is typically a Shipibo shaman who inflicts harm using chontas or virotes — magical darts crafted from the thorns of various plants and trees, the beaks of certain birds, the fangs of snakes, or the hair of the casha-cushillo (porcupine, Coendu bicolor). These darts are stored in the sorcerer's mann (magical phlegm).

When a chontero wishes to cause harm, he takes a chonta from his mann and blows toward his victim. The invisible dart strikes the body, causing pain, illness, or death. If the victim has previously been treated by a good vegetalista with protective icaros, he will immediately sense the attack and seek remedy. If unprotected, the victim may suffer in ignorance of the cause and eventually die.

**Diagnostic sign**: A curandero observing a patient under ayahuasca who sees zigzagging lights around the patient can identify chontero sorcery.

### The Cocama Marupa Sorcerer

The Cocama sorcerer practices hechicería marupa (marupa sorcery), which involves manipulating living creatures kept within his mann. These include insects, snakes, scorpions, bats, rays, and toads. The sorcerer sends these animals through visionary space to attack his victims.

In operation, the victim feels an inexplicable urge to travel to a specific location, where the sorcerer's animal lies in wait. The attack appears as a natural animal bite or sting, making this form of sorcery particularly insidious because it mimics ordinary accidents.

**Diagnostic sign**: If the curandero sees small waves crossed by dark lines surrounding the patient, marupa sorcery has been employed.

### The Huani Sorcerer

The most dangerous of all sorcerers uses the huani — a steel bow with a crystal arrow — kept under the left armpit. Inside his mann, this sorcerer carries a living piranha. When attacking, he places the bow in his mouth and blows the crystal arrow, which pierces the victim's body and returns to the sorcerer's armpit.

The victim feels extreme fatigue and falls asleep. Upon waking, they begin vomiting blood until death. According to Amaringo, no vegetalista can cure this form of sorcery — only divine intervention can save the victim.

**Diagnostic sign**: Larger waves (without dark cross-lines) surrounding the patient indicate huani sorcery.

## The Powers of the Pipes

Sacred pipes made from specific tree species serve as conduits for different classes of spirits. Each pipe releases its own beings when smoked, and the spirit of the tree from which the pipe is carved determines its function.

### Tahuari Pipe (Tabebuia sp.)

The spirit of the tahuari tree manifests as both a human and a black bull. When smoked, the yura-cukchas mailcas (old snowy-haired wise ones) are released — renowned doctors capable of performing miracle cures. Below the pipe appears the face of a giant and a talabat, a king of great stature with a facial mole extending from his upper lip to his hairline.

### Huacamayo-caspi Pipe (Coutarea hexandra / Sickingia tinctoria)

The spirit of this tree is the prince Yana-huya (black face). From the pipe rise the curikiros (golden teeth) and a forest nymph companion. The name huacamayo refers to the macaw, suggesting colorful and powerful visions.

### Shihuahuaco Pipe (Dipteryx sp.)

The spirit is the serpent with a human face known as machaco-runa (the snake people). Released from this pipe are the maya-machaco (snake ancestors) and the sylph Pichamui shipash (the maiden that cleans), who belongs to the tobacco flower.

### Cumaceba Pipe (Cesalpina equinata)

This pipe releases majestic lords called ampishcashamuy (those who come to cure) and their guardian, the serpent tamer Alli Urman. The spirit of the cumaceba tree is a giant named Maun who wears a golden crown.

## Healers in Time and Space

In one of the most cosmopolitan visions, Amaringo describes shamans from diverse traditions all practicing vegetal and spiritual medicine simultaneously. The vision is organized into three vertical sections representing different streams of healing knowledge:

### Amazonian Healers

- A mestiza woman practicing Rosicrucianism with plant brews
- A mestizo ayahuasquero practicing medicine with pure soul and heart
- A Shipibo shaman using ayahuasca with additives including piripiri (Cyperus sp.), toe (Brugmansia sp.), and chirik-sanango (Brunfelsia grandiflora)
- A Campa Indian possessing occult knowledge of diverse plants, considered the most knowledgeable jungle people regarding magical uses of plant-teachers
- A Cocama native exercising traditional medicine powers

### Eastern and African Traditions

- A guru master of extrasensory perception in Krishna temples, purifying his soul through transcendental meditation of the sixth sense
- An African woman practicing fetishism for healing
- A lama, master of healing through plants of the mystical Himalayan mountains

### Indigenous American Wisdom

- An Inca priest or Varayok, guardian of the temples of the occult sciences, who had direct contact with extraterrestrial beings from Andromeda
- An oracionista (prayer practitioner) using celestial spirits alongside images, stamps, prayer books, and perfumes

### Extraterrestrial Connections

The vision includes spaceships from Ganymede (with a magic ladder for crew disembarkation), Venus, and the Pleiades. The spirit Grinfel, Count of the Empire of the Planet Saturn, appears with powers of sensual hypnotism. A supay-tuyuyo (large bird) serves as a vehicle for the master when traveling to outer space.

## The Mesta Oracionista

The oracionista represents a syncretic healing practice combining Catholic prayer traditions with indigenous plant medicine. Surrounded by images, stamps, books of prayer, and perfumes, she practices medicine with the help of celestial spirits. This figure demonstrates how Amazonian vegetalismo has absorbed and transformed external religious traditions while maintaining its core emphasis on direct spiritual experience.

## Medicinal Plants in the Incaic Vision

The Incaic vision describes several medicinal plants found near ceremonial sites:

- **Papaflia** ([[momordica-charantia]]): Bitter melon, widely used in traditional medicine
- **Sacha-pituca** (unidentified)
- **Renaco** (Ficus sp.): A strangler fig with traditional medicinal uses
- **Patiquina negra** (Dieffenbachia sp.): Dumb cane, used with caution in traditional preparations
- **Uchusanango** (Tabernaemontana sp.): A genus known for alkaloid-containing [[amanita-muscaria-medicinal-properties-heart-anxiety-dreams]]
- **Boasacha** (unidentified)
- **Achuni-casha** (Rheedia macrophylla): A Garcinia relative with potential medicinal applications

## The Forest Spirits: Three Powers

The vision of the three powers establishes the fundamental tripartite structure of Amazonian spiritual ecology:

- **Land powers**: Represented by the Sachamama (giant earth serpent) and the various practitioners seated in different plants, each demonstrating their alignment (healer, sorcerer, or both)
- **Water powers**: Embodied by the Yakumama (water mother serpent), who sometimes manifests as a steamboat, and the various aquatic beings
- **Sky powers**: Represented by the Huairamama (wind serpent), who moves with great winds and produces thunder-like sounds without rain — a phenomenon called supay-cato (ghost bath)

## Extraterrestrial Beings in Shamanic Cosmology

Amaringo's visions consistently include extraterrestrial beings and spacecraft. Beings arrive from Antares, Andromeda, Saturn, Venus, Ganymede, and the Pleiades. These are not random additions but integral parts of the cosmology — extraterrestrials bring specialized knowledge, serve as allies or adversaries, and interact with human shamans on equal or superior terms. The Inca priest received direct contact with beings from Andromeda whose vision was "very much superior to ours" and who gave specialized knowledge to the Tahuantinsuyo shamans.
