# Indole Hallucinogen Classification

## Overview

Indole hallucinogens are a family of psychoactive compounds characterized by
their structural feature: a five-sided pyrrole group in association with a
benzene ring. These molecular rings make the indoles highly reactive
chemically and hence ideal molecules for [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]]. [[four-families-of-indole-hallucinogens]]
hallucinogens that occur in plants and have been used by humans for visionary
and spiritual purposes.

## Structural Chemistry

The defining characteristic of these hallucinogens is structural: all contain
an indole ring system, consisting of a benzene ring fused to a pyrrole ring.
This bicyclic structure is shared with the neurotransmitter serotonin
(5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT), which is the primary reason these compounds are
able to interact with serotonergic receptor systems in the brain.

Hallucinogens may be psychoactive and/or physiologically active and may
target many systems within the body. Some indoles are endogenous to the human
body, serotonin being a good example. Many more are exogenous, found in
nature and the plants we can eat. Some behave like hormones and regulate
growth or rate of sexual maturation. Others influence mood and state of
alertness.
