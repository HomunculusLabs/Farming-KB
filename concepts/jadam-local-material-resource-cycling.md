# JADAM Local Material Resource Cycling

JADAM's ultra-low-cost farming depends on using locally available, free or nearly free materials as inputs. The system eliminates dependence on purchased inputs by cycling nutrients on-farm through [[jadam-nutritional-balance-fertilizer]]. JADAM achieves this by using crop residues and wild grass -- materials with nearly identical [[jadam-nutritional-balance-philosophy]] the crop needs. JADAM does not view crop residues as a disease source but as a nutrient source. They are the perfect fertilizer -- returning to the soil what the crop extracted.

- Use as base fertilizer by leaving on the soil surface
- Convert to [[jadam-anaerobic-fermentation-principles]] in water with leaf mold
- Nitrogen content of various JLF sources: wild grass (0.01%), purslane (0.2%), tomato (0.27%), spinach (0.29%)

### Wild Grass

Wild grass is second only to crop residues because it has a nutrient composition similar to crops -- they are the same type of organism. Wild grass with vigorous growth stretches roots deep into the earth (more than 1.5x above-ground height), pulling up minerals from deep subsoil layers.

- Cut wild grass and use as JLF material
- Combine with crop residues to make "GC-JLF" (grass and crop residues JLF)
- Dilute GC-JLF approximately 100x and use throughout the season
- Rye broadcast in autumn provides approximately 20 tons of [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]] that have adapted to the local environment -- the environment most similar to your fields -- teem in leaf mold.

- Collect from adjacent mountains: remove top leaves, take soft soil mixed with decomposing debris
- Store in black plastic bags or gunnysacks in shade
- Use approximately 500 g each time as JMS starter
- If unavailable, create artificial leaf mold: cover clean soil with cut grass, keep moist, wait ~3 weeks

### Human and Animal Waste

- Human manure (feces JLF: 2.48% N; urine JLF: 3.92% N)
- Food waste JLF (2.93% N)
- Livestock manure (avoid antibiotic-contaminated sources)
- Fish waste JLF (5.08-7.44% N)

## Anaerobic Fermentation: The Traditional Method

JADAM's input-making follows anaerobic fermentation -- the method used for thousands of years in Asia. Simply fill a container with organic material and water, add leaf mold, seal, and wait. This preserves nutrients far better than aerobic fermentation (which destroys vitamins and amino acids).

### Gunnysack Fermentation Method

1. Place a pallet on the ground
2. Fill gunnysacks with wild grass, leaves, sawdust as main ingredients
3. Add food waste, livestock manure, fish waste as desired
4. Pile sacks in approximately 6 layers on the pallet
5. Apply generous JMS to each layer as you pile
6. Wrap tightly with plastic sheet
7. Use contents after 3 months

## Rejection of Purchased Inputs

JADAM strongly opposes the use of sugar, molasses, vinegar, wood vinegar, and alcohol as farming essentials:
- Sugar and molasses are 100% imported in Korea (linked to oil prices)
- Black sugar contains artificial caramel coloring (potential carcinogen)
- Sugar makes inputs and soil acidic, promoting disease outbreak
- These inputs are unnecessary -- water and leaf mold are sufficient
