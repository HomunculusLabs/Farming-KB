# Closed-Circuit Wind Tunnel Design for Mushroom Tropism Research

Edmond Badham's 1982 study of tropisms [[falconer-growing-mushrooms-in-sheds]] over
72-hour experimental periods. The closed-circuit wind tunnel he designed
and described in Mycologia represents a specialized piece of mycological
equipment that enabled the first systematic investigation of anemotropism
in a basidiomycete mushroom.

## Wind Tunnel Dimensions and Airflow

The wind tunnel had a working area measuring 43 by 30 by 20 centimeters,
providing sufficient space for multiple pie-tin cultures of developing
basidiocarps. Wind velocity within the working area was maintained at 9
plus or minus 3 centimeters per second, measured using a TSI hot wire
anemometer Model 1650. This relatively low velocity is comparable to a
gentle breeze and was chosen to represent wind conditions that mushrooms
might encounter in natural environments without causing physical damage to
developing fruiting bodies.

The airflow was generated by a Dayton 4C564 blower rated at 0.07 cubic
meters per second. Air passed through a polyester fiber filter that served
to slow the wind speed and remove particulate matter from the air stream
before it entered the working area. An air baffle with eight sides was
positioned upstream of the working area to ensure laminar, uniform airflow
across the mushroom cultures.

## Humidity Control System

Humidity was maintained at 94 plus or minus 3 percent relative humidity,
measured using wet and dry copper constantan thermocouples from Omega
Engineering. The humidity control system employed a spin-type humidifier
with an integrated heater. A two-pen strip-chart recorder from Houston
Instrument Omniscribe continuously monitored humidity through a wet and dry
thermocouple arrangement, where one pen carried a magnet and one pen a reed
switch. This reed switch activated the humidifier through a relay whenever
the humidity dropped below the setpoint. The wet bulb depression was
adjustable, providing a measure of relative humidity that could be fine-
tuned for experimental requirements.

This feedback-controlled humidity system was essential because mushroom
fruiting bodies are highly sensitive to desiccation. At lower humidities,
the evaporative demand on mushroom surfaces would dominate all other
tropic stimuli, masking the specific effects of wind direction, light
direction, and gravity that Badham was investigating. By maintaining
humidity near saturation at 94 percent, the system isolated wind-induced
boundary layer effects from general desiccation stress.

## Temperature Regulation

Temperature was maintained at 24.5 plus or minus 1.5 degrees Celsius using
a heat cable and a temperature proportioner module designated as
Thermotrack II. A thermometer attached to the temperature proportioner
provided continuous temperature monitoring. This temperature range is
within the optimal growth range [[four-stage-basidiocarp-development-psilocybe-cubensis-badham]] throughout the
72-hour experimental runs.
