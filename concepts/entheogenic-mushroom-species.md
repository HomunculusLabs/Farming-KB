Entheogenic mushrooms are fungi containing psychoactive
compounds, primarily psilocybin and psilocin, that have been
used in religious, spiritual, and healing contexts throughout
human history. The term "entheogen" (meaning "generating the
divine within") reflects the sacred role these organisms have
played in diverse cultures. These species span multiple genera
across several fungal families.

## Genus Psilocybe

The genus Psilocybe contains the largest number of entheogenic
species and is the most widely used group globally. Psilocybe
cubensis is perhaps the most well-known species, native to
tropical and subtropical regions of the Americas and widely
cultivated. It is a large, robust mushroom found on bovine
dung in pastures, making it one of the easiest species to
identify and cultivate for [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]], the [[amanita-muscaria-muscimol-ibotenic-acid-pharmacology]]
as its primary active compounds. It has been used in Siberian
shamanism for centuries and is the leading candidate for the
[[psilocybin-mushroom-global-distribution]] for detailed range maps.

## Potency Variation

Potency varies enormously between and within species. Factors
including genetics, substrate, growing conditions, age at
harvest, and storage methods all influence alkaloid content.
