Mesophytic forests — characterized by moderate moisture, mixed canopy, and
rich humus layers — provide the combination of humidity, shade, and organic

**Tropical and subtropical species**: *Psilocybe cubensis*, *P. subcubensis*,
and most *Copelandia* species. These species thrive in warm, humid
conditions. *P. cubensis* grows on cow dung in pastures across tropical and
subtropical regions worldwide.

**Subtropical and cloud forest species**: The majority of neurotropic
Psilocybe species grow in subtropical, mesophytic, cloud, or deciduous humid
forests. This category includes the forests of Mexico, the Caribbean, the
eastern United States, and Central Europe.

## The Dung Substrate Guild

Several neurotropic species are coprophilous (dung-growing). *Psilocybe
cubensis* and *P. subcubensis* grow almost exclusively on bovine dung. Their
distribution is therefore tied not to natural vegetation but to the presence
of cattle — a human-mediated ecological factor.

The introduction of cattle to the Americas by Spanish colonizers in the 16th
and 17th centuries created vast new habitats for these species. Guzmán
suggested that *P. cubensis* may have been introduced to the Americas through
the slave trade during the Spanish colonial period, as it does not grow
naturally in Europe.
