Sterilization is the single most critical step in the PF TEK [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] but also for a wide range of competing microorganisms including bacteria, yeast, and molds. Without thorough sterilization, these contaminants will outcompete the mushroom mycelium, rendering the entire batch useless. The PF TEK"s accessibility and success rate are largely attributable to its effective but low-cost approach to sterilization.

## The Contamination Challenge

### Sources of Contamination
[[bacillus-subtilis]] contamination is one of the most common causes of substrate failure, producing a distinctive "wet rot" or "sour" smell.

- **Mold spores**: Spores from common environmental molds (Aspergillus, Penicillium, Rhizopus, Trichoderma, Mucor) are ubiquitous in indoor air and on surfaces. They can colonize a substrate rapidly if given the opportunity, often outgrowing the relatively slow mushroom mycelium.

- **Yeasts**: Yeast contamination produces a slimy, wet appearance on the substrate surface and can prevent [[gourmet-medicinal-mushroom-oyster-cultivation-methods]] that use grain substrates requiring a pressure canner, the PF TEK's brown rice flour/vermiculite mixture is designed to be sterilized with relatively accessible equipment.

The vermiculite in the PF TEK substrate serves a dual purpose: it provides structure and water retention for the mycelium, and it also acts as a physical barrier that makes it more difficult for contaminants to spread through the substrate. The loose, airy structure of vermiculite means that steam can penetrate more effectively than through a dense grain substrate.

## Steam Sterilization vs. Pressure Sterilization

### The Fundamental Difference
Two methods of sterilization are used [[growing-gourmet-atmospheric-steam-sterilization]], eliminating the need for an expensive pressure canner. This design decision was central to the PF TEK's philosophy of accessibility — the technique was intended to be achievable with materials and equipment available at any discount store or hardware store.

The PF TEK achieves reliable sterilization without a pressure canner through several clever design features:
- The substrate is relatively low in density compared to grain substrates, allowing better steam penetration
- The vermiculite creates air channels that facilitate steam circulation
- The small jar size (1/2 pint) allows heat to penetrate to the center of the substrate within a reasonable time
- The substrate recipe (brown rice flour, vermiculite, water) is less prone to harboring resistant endospores than grain-based substrates

## Atmospheric Steam Sterilization Technique

### The Steaming Pot Setup
The original PF TEK uses a large cooking pot with a tight-fitting lid to create a steam sterilization chamber:

1. **Pot selection**: A large pot (approximately 16-20 quarts / 15-19 liters) with a tight-fitting lid. The pot must be tall enough to accommodate the substrate jars standing upright with the lid on.

2. **Jar placement**: The substrate jars are placed upright on a rack, trivet, or layer of jar lids at the bottom of the pot. The jars must not sit directly on the bottom of the pot, which can get too hot and crack the glass.

3. **Water level**: Water is added to the pot to a level approximately 1 inch below the bottom of the jars. The water should not touch the jars — they should be elevated above the water line, sterilized by steam rather than by direct contact with boiling water.

4. **Lid covering**: The pot lid is placed on tightly. Some practitioners place a weight on the lid to ensure a good seal, which helps maintain steam pressure inside the pot (note: this is not true pressure sterilization — the pot is not pressurized, but a tight seal helps retain steam).

### Sterilization Times
The PF TEK specifies the following steam sterilization times:

- **1/2 pint substrate jars**: 60 minutes of steaming after the water reaches a full boil. Some practitioners extend this to 90 minutes for extra safety margin, particularly in high-altitude locations where water boils at a lower temperature.

- **1 pint jars**: 90 minutes of steaming. The larger jar size requires more time for steam to fully penetrate the substrate.

- **Water bottles (for preparing spore syringes)**: 55 minutes at 12 PSI in a pressure canner (this application does require pressure sterilization due to the need to kill endospores in the water).

- **Spore syringes**: 10 minutes at 12 PSI in a pressure canner.

### Altitude Considerations
At higher altitudes, water boils at a lower temperature, which can reduce the effectiveness of steam sterilization. As a general rule, for every 1,000 feet (300 meters) of elevation above sea level, steam sterilization time should be increased by approximately 5-10 minutes. At very high altitudes (above 5,000 feet / 1,500 meters), a pressure canner may become necessary for reliable results.

### The Cooling Phase
After the steaming period is complete, the jars must be allowed to cool slowly inside the pot with the lid on. This slow cooling serves two purposes:

1. **Thermal shock prevention**: Removing hot jars from the steam environment and exposing them to cool room air can cause thermal shock, cracking the glass or pulling the substrate away from the jar walls (which creates air gaps that contaminants can enter during inoculation).

2. **[[grass-seed-substrate-for-mushroom-cultivation]] substrates, the standard pressure canner parameters are:

- **Pressure**: 12-15 PSI (approximately 0.8-1.0 bar above atmospheric pressure)
