## Definition and Overview

Radiotrophic fungi are species that appear to use melanin pigment to harvest
energy from ionizing radiation, converting it into chemical energy usable for
growth. This phenomenon was first documented at the Chernobyl Nuclear Power
Plant in the late 1990s, where darkly pigmented fungi colonized the destroyed
reactor"s sarcophagus, thriving in environments with radiation levels lethal
to most life forms.

The term "radiotrophic" remains controversial. Some researchers prefer
"radiotolerant" or "melanized fungi in radiological environments," as direct
proof of radiation-to-chemical-energy conversion comparable to photosynthesis
has not been definitively established. However, experimental evidence strongly
suggests melanin plays a functional role beyond simple shielding.

## Proposed Mechanisms

### Radiation-Enhanced Electron Transfer

The leading hypothesis from Dadachova and Casadevall: ionizing radiation
interacts with melanin to generate electrons transferred to cellular redox
systems. In cell-free experiments, irradiated melanin reduced NAD+ to NADH,
suggesting melanin feeds reducing equivalents into metabolic pathways,
particularly in nutrient-limited environments.

### Induced Radiotropism

ISS experiments with C. sphaerospermum showed colonies growing preferentially
toward radiation sources. This directional growth, analogous to plant
phototropism, suggests fungi sense and orient toward radiation through
radiation-induced changes in melanin's electrical potential.

### Combinatorial Model

Most researchers favor a combined model: melanin provides passive
radioprotection plus radiation-enhanced electron transfer, giving melanized
fungi reduced damage and a modest metabolic boost from radiation exposure.
