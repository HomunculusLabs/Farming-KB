# Mushroom Antimicrobial Properties — The Species Chart

## Overview

In *Mycelium Running*, Paul Stamets presents a comprehensive chart titled "Mushrooms
versus Microbes" documenting the antimicrobial properties of numerous mushroom species.
This chart synthesizes research from mycological and pharmacological literature to
demonstrate that many mushrooms produce potent compounds active against bacteria,
fungi, viruses, and other microorganisms. The chart serves as both a scientific
reference and a practical design tool for mycorestoration applications, particularly
mycofiltration and mycopesticide development.

## The Broad-Spectrum Species

Several mushroom species stand out in Stamets' chart for their broad-spectrum
antimicrobial activity, affecting multiple classes of microorganisms simultaneously.
These species are of particular interest for general-purpose mycofiltration
applications where the target pathogens are diverse or unknown.

**[[stamets-cultivator-coprinus-comatus-shaggy-mane-cultivation]]** (shaggy mane) demonstrates broad-spectrum activity, effective
against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria as well as some fungal
pathogens. Its wide-ranging antimicrobial profile makes it a versatile candidate
for mycofilter installations.

**Lentinula edodes** (shiitake) exhibits what Stamets identifies as the broadest
antimicrobial spectrum among commonly studied species. Shiitake produces lentinan
and other polysaccharides with well-documented immunomodulatory and antimicrobial
effects. Its activity spans bacterial, fungal, and viral targets, making it
exceptionally valuable for comprehensive pathogen control.

**Pleurotus ostreatus** (oyster mushroom) rivals shiitake in the breadth of its
antimicrobial activity. Beyond its well-known capacity to degrade organic
pollutants and even break down hydrocarbon contaminants, oyster mushroom mycelium
produces compounds active against a wide range of pathogenic microorganisms. This
dual capacity — environmental remediation and antimicrobial activity — makes
*Pleurotus* one of the most versatile species in the mycorestoration toolkit.

## Species with Targeted Antimicrobial Effects

Beyond the broad-spectrum performers, Stamets' chart catalogs numerous species with
more targeted antimicrobial profiles, each effective against specific classes of
pathogens. This specificity is crucial for designing customized mycofilter systems
tailored to particular contamination scenarios.

**Agaricus brasiliensis** (formerly *A. blazei*) shows significant antibacterial
activity, particularly against Gram-positive organisms. This Brazilian species has
attracted considerable medical research attention for its immunomodulatory
properties and its potential as a source of novel antimicrobial compounds.

**Armillaria mellea** (honey mushroom), better known as a pathogenic forest
fungus, produces antimicrobial compounds as part of its competitive ecology. The
same chemical arsenal that allows honey mushroom to combat rival fungi and bacteria
in forest soils can be harnessed for mycofiltration applications.

**Flammulina velutipes** (enoki mushroom) demonstrates measurable activity against
several bacterial species. While less extensively studied than shiitake or oyster
mushrooms for antimicrobial applications, enoki's commercial cultivation
infrastructure makes it a readily available candidate for mycofilter inoculum.

**Fomes fomentarius** (tinder conk) and **Fomitopsis officinalis** (quinine conk)
are both wood-decay polypores with documented antimicrobial properties. *F.
officinalis* has a particularly storied history in traditional medicine and was
once harvested commercially for its [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]].

## The Ganoderma Species

**Ganoderma applanatum** (artist's conk) and **[[stamets-reishi-ganoderma-lucidum-cultivation]]** (reishi)
represent two of the most extensively studied medicinal mushrooms, and both appear
in Stamets' antimicrobial chart. Reishi has been the subject of hundreds of
pharmacological studies confirming its antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral
properties. The ganoderic acids and polysaccharides produced by these species
target a wide range of microorganisms.

The inclusion of both *Ganoderma* species in the chart underscores the connection
between traditional medicinal mushroom use and modern mycorestoration practice.
Compounds valued for human medicine for millennia can be deployed in environmental
applications through the living mycelium that produces them.

## Additional Species of Note

**[[maitake-grifola-frondosa-comprehensive-profile]]** (maitake) produces antimicrobial compounds alongside its
better-known immunomodulatory polysaccharides. Maitake's activity against certain
bacterial pathogens makes it a dual-function species for health and environmental
applications.

**Hericium erinaceus** (lion's mane), primarily known for its neuroprotective
properties, also demonstrates antimicrobial effects. This finding suggests that
even species valued primarily for other bioactivities contribute to the
antimicrobial capacity of mycofilter installations.

**Hypsizygus ulmarius** (elm oyster) shows activity against selected bacterial
targets. As a species well-adapted to temperate climates and wood-chip
substrates, it is a practical choice for outdoor mycofiltration installations.

**[[growing-gourmet-polyporus-umbellatus-zhu-ling-cultivation]]** occupies a special position in Stamets' chart for its
documented anti-malarial activity. While most entries target bacteria or fungi,
*P. umbellatus* demonstrates that mushroom compounds can be effective against
protozoan pathogens as well, expanding the potential scope of mycofiltration to
address parasitic contamination.

**Stropharia rugoso-annulata** (garden giant), featured in Stamets' Skookum
Inlet experiment, appears in the chart confirming its antimicrobial credentials.
The species' ability to consume bacteria and secrete antibacterial compounds
explains its effectiveness in the field trial.

**[[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]]** (turkey tail) rounds out the chart with well-documented
antimicrobial activity, particularly its capacity to stimulate immune responses
against pathogens through polysaccharide-K (PSK) and related compounds.

## Customized Mycofilter Mosaics

Stamets introduces the concept of "mycofilter mosaics" — intentionally designed
blends of multiple fungal species, each selected for its specific antimicrobial
profile, deployed together in a single filtration installation. Rather than relying
on a single species for all pathogen control, a mosaic approach leverages the
complementary antimicrobial spectra of different species to achieve more
comprehensive and resilient pathogen reduction.

The mosaic concept is analogous to combination antibiotic therapy in medicine:
by using multiple antimicrobial agents with different targets, the system reduces
the likelihood that any single pathogen can evade treatment. In mycofiltration,
this means combining broad-spectrum species like *Lentinula edodes* with targeted
species selected for specific known contaminants in the water being treated.

## Applications in Mycofiltration Design

The antimicrobial species chart provides a rational basis for selecting fungal
inocula for specific mycofiltration applications. When the contaminant profile of
a water source is known — for example, if testing reveals dominance of particular
bacterial species — the chart allows practitioners to select mushroom species whose
antimicrobial compounds are most likely to be effective against those specific
targets. This evidence-based approach elevates mycofiltration from empirical
practice to design science.
