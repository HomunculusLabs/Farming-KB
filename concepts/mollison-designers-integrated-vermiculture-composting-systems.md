## Integrated Vermiculture and Composting Systems

Vermiculture (worm farming) is a central component of permaculture soil

multi-pathway system for converting organic waste into high-quality soil
amendments, liquid fertilizers, and animal feed.

## Worm Biology and Species Selection

Earthworms are annelid worms that feed on organic matter at or near the
soil surface, passing it through their digestive system to produce
vermicast (worm castings), one of the finest soil amendments available.
Key species for vermiculture include:

  - Eisenia fetida (Red Wiggler / Tiger Worm): The standard composting
    worm for temperate and subtropical systems. Tolerant of a wide
    range of conditions, processes organic waste rapidly, and
    reproduces prolifically. Optimal [[phytochrome-ecological-function-dusk-dawn-red-far-red-light-switch]] and are
not typically used in surface composting systems but are essential
in garden soils for aeration and subsoil [[mushroom-casing-moisture-management]]: Bedding should be maintained at 70-80%
    moisture content, approximately the feel of a wrung-out sponge
  - Surface area: Worm processing capacity is determined by surface
    area, not volume. A larger, shallower bin processes more waste
    than a deep, narrow one

Common bin designs include stackable tray systems (flow-through),
continuous flow-through reactors, and simple single-chamber boxes.
Outdoor worm beds can be constructed from concrete blocks, timber, or
excavated pits lined with shade cloth.

## Feedstock and Management

Composting worms consume a wide range of organic materials:
  - Kitchen scraps: Fruit and vegetable peelings, oyster-mushroom-coffee-grounds-cultivation,
    tea leaves, crushed eggshells
  - Garden waste: Soft green prunings, leaf mold, grass clippings
    (in moderation)
  - Paper and cardboard: Shredded newspaper, unprinted cardboard,
    paper egg cartons (excellent bedding material)
  - Animal manures: Horse, cow, and rabbit manure (avoid fresh
    poultry manure, which can be too acidic and nitrogen-rich)

Feeding rate: Worms consume approximately half their body weight per
day. A well-established bin of 1 kg of worms can process 0.5 kg of
organic waste daily. Feed should be buried under the bedding surface
to prevent fruit fly attraction and odor.
