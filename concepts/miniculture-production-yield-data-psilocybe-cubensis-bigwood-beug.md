### Colonization Phase

- **Duration:** 28 days total from inoculation to fully colonized grain
- **7 shaking events:** At days 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, and 28
- **Expected outcome:** Mycelium fully covers the grain mass, appearing as a dense white mat

The 28-day colonization period is relatively long by modern cultivation standards. Contemporary methods often achieve full colonization in 14-21 days, potentially due to:

- Use of more aggressive commercial strains selected for fast colonization

## Casing Layer Application

### Casing Composition

After 28 days of colonization, the jars were opened and the grain was cased (covered) with:

- **Mix ratio:** 2 parts peat : 1 part calcium carbonate : 2 parts perlite and/or vermiculite
- **Depth:** Approximately 2 cm deep layer over the colonized grain
- **Function:** The casing layer provides a non-nutritive, moisture-retaining surface that triggers [[fruiting-initiation|fruiting initiation]]

### Component Roles

| Component | Function | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Peat moss | Moisture retention, microflora habitat | Provides water reservoir and supports beneficial bacteria |
| Calcium carbonate | pH buffering | Raises pH to ~7.0-7.5, inhibiting competitive molds |
| Perlite/vermiculite | Aeration, drainage, moisture | Prevents compaction, maintains air exchange at substrate surface |

### Post-Casing Watering

- **Frequency:** Once every two days
- **Volume:** 1 ml of sterile water via syringe per watering
- **Method:** Direct syringe application to maintain casing moisture without oversaturation

The use of sterile water via syringe demonstrates the laboratory-grade hygiene maintained throughout the experiment. This level of precision was essential for preventing contamination over the extended production period.

## Fruiting Timeline

### Initiation

- **First flush:** 4-5 weeks after inoculation (approximately 2 weeks after casing)
- **Casing to fruit:** ~14 days from casing application to first harvestable mushrooms
- **Total inoculation to harvest:** 28-35 days
