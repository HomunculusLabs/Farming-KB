## Selfing and Regular Seed Production

Vic High's breeding text discusses selfing as one of the available inbreeding strategies for cannabis, alongside backcrossing and generational inbreeding. While the text does not provide an extended dedicated treatment of selfing mechanics, it places selfing within the broader framework of inbreeding strategies and explains its role in creating true breeding strains from special individuals.

## Selfing as an Inbreeding Strategy

Selfing refers to the process of fertilising a plant with its own pollen -- effectively crossing a plant with itself. In Vic High's overview of inbreeding strategies, selfing is presented as one of the possible starting points for a breeding project when a breeder identifies a special individual worth preserving.

Vic High describes three possible approaches when a breeder finds a unique individual:

1. **Selfing the individual**: The plant is self-pollinated to create seed. This is the simplest approach and the fastest way to expose hidden recessive genetic information in a single generation.

2. **Creating F1 offspring**: The special individual is crossed with another plant. This produces heterozygous F1 offspring from which further inbreeding can proceed via backcrossing or generational crossing.

3. **Backcrossing (cubing)**: The special individual is crossed with an unrelated male, and successive generations are crossed back to the original parent to progressively increase its genetic contribution to the gene pool.

Each approach has different trade-offs in speed, [[cervantes-dominance-recessive-traits-alleles]] a plant carries in hidden form.
- Finding homozygous recessive individuals that express valuable hidden traits.
- Quickly creating a population from which true breeding individuals can be selected.

## Regular Seed Production Context

Regular seeds (as opposed to [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]] between male and female plants. Vic High's entire breeding framework operates within the context of regular seed production.

Key aspects of regular seed production in Vic High's framework:

- Regular seeds maintain natural genetic diversity, which is essential for inbreeding and selection programmes.
- The presence of both male and female plants allows for controlled crosses, backcrosses, and generational inbreeding.
- Male selection is a central concern throughout the text because males cannot be assessed for many important traits.
- Population size matters: larger populations ensure that rare genetic combinations appear and that matings approximate theoretical randomness.

## The Male Selection Constraint

A recurring theme in Vic High's text is the practical difficulty of selecting male plants. Many desirable traits -- flavour, potency, flowering characteristics -- can only be evaluated in female plants because male plants do not produce female flowers.

This limitation forces breeders into half-sib breeding where males contribute genes randomly, and the impact is quantified throughout the text. Vic High's comparison of full-sib versus half-sib F5 results shows that the inability to select males reduces phenotypic uniformity from 96% to 87% for dominant traits -- a significant efficiency loss.

### Strategies for Addressing Male Selection Limitations

- **[[cervantes-progeny-testing-combining-ability]]**: Growing out test crosses from candidate males to determine which males pass on the most desirable genes. Vic High describes this as the most reliable method but notes it requires additional time and resources.
- **Large male numbers**: Using many males per cross to increase the probability that the pollen pool includes the desired alleles.
