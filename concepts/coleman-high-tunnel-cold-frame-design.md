# High Tunnel and [[cold-frame-construction]] Design

Eliot Coleman's approach to [[coleman-protected-cultivation]] centers on simple,
affordable structures that maximize [[passive-solar-design]] gain while withstanding
harsh winter conditions. His designs prioritize functionality over
complexity, making season extension accessible to small-scale growers.

## High Tunnel Dimensions and Layout

Coleman's standard high tunnel measures 30 feet wide by 96 feet long,
with sidewalls approximately 6 feet tall and a peak height of 10 to 12
feet. This footprint provides ample internal space for raised beds and
allows comfortable working conditions. The width accommodates four
standard 30-inch beds with adequate walkways between them.

The gothic arch design is preferred over Quonset-style hoops because the
peaked roof sheds snow more effectively and reduces the risk of
structural collapse under heavy snow loads. Ground posts are driven into
the soil at regular intervals, typically every 4 feet, with metal bows
attached to create the arched frame.

## Polyethylene Coverings

Coleman recommends a double-layer polyethylene greenhouse film inflated
by a small squirrel-cage blower. The air gap between layers provides
insulation value roughly equivalent to a single pane of glass while
costing a fraction of the price. The inflation system also keeps the
covering taut, reducing wind damage and flapping.

UV-stabilized greenhouse-grade poly should be replaced every 4 to 6
years. Coleman uses a 6-mil thickness for durability. Some growers
opt for specialized films with anti-condensate properties that prevent
water droplets from forming on the interior surface, improving light
transmission and reducing drip damage to crops below.

## End Wall Construction

End walls can be framed with wood or metal and covered with the same
polyethylene film, rigid polycarbonate panels, or even reclaimed
materials. Each end wall should include a personnel door and a large
roll-up or sliding door wide enough for a small tractor or wheelbarrow
access. The large door also serves as a critical ventilation opening.

Coleman often uses a simple frame-and-plywood design for end walls,
sheathed in poly on the interior surface to maintain the insulated air
space. Hinged doors at both ends allow cross-ventilation during warm
periods, which is essential even in winter to manage humidity and
prevent disease.

## Ventilation Systems

Adequate ventilation is critical in high tunnels year-round. Coleman
installs roll-up sidewalls that can be raised to 3 or 4 feet, allowing
hot air to escape from the bottom while drawing cooler air through the
end wall doors. This natural convection system is sufficient for most
conditions without powered exhaust fans.

In winter, ventilation is managed conservatively. Small top vents or
louvers at the peak of the arch allow moist air to escape without
creating cold drafts at plant level. Coleman monitors humidity closely,
as excessive moisture promotes fungal diseases in the dense canopy of
winter greens.

## Snow Load Considerations

In northern climates, snow load is the primary structural concern.
Gothic arch designs with a minimum 6:12 pitch shed snow effectively.
Coleman recommends ground posts anchored at least 3 feet deep in
concrete footings. Cross-bracing between hoops adds rigidity and
prevents racking under wind and snow loads.

During heavy snowfall events, Coleman monitors tunnels and may sweep
snow from the roof with a long-handled broom or padded push pole. The
inflation system helps maintain structural integrity by keeping the
cover under constant tension. Internal braces or temporary center
posts can supplement the frame during extreme storms.

## Cold Frame Design

Cold frames are the simplest protected growing structure. Coleman's
cold frames are bottomless boxes with a translucent lid, typically
3 to 4 feet wide and 6 to 8 feet long. The back wall is 12 to 18
inches tall, and the front wall is 6 to 10 inches, creating a
south-facing slope that maximizes solar capture.

Lids are traditionally made of glass sash, but Coleman also uses
twin-wall polycarbonate panels for lighter weight and reduced
breakage risk. Hinges allow the lid to be propped open for
ventilation on sunny days. Automatic vent openers using a
temperature-sensitive wax cylinder can automate this process.

## Integration with Winter Production Systems

Cold frames and high tunnels work together in Coleman's system. Cold
frames serve as nurseries for starting transplants and as
supplementary protection for the most cold-sensitive crops. See
[[coleman-moveable-greenhouse-system]] for how Coleman integrates
multiple structure types into a cohesive production plan.

The design principles extend to [[coleman-row-cover-fabric-management]]
as a secondary layer of protection within these structures. Proper
[[coleman-soil-preparation-for-winter]] ensures that the protected
environment translates directly into productive winter growing.

## Cost and Accessibility

A key philosophy in Coleman's work is that protected cultivation
should not require massive capital investment. His high tunnel designs
can be built for a fraction of the cost of commercial glasshouses,
using readily available materials and basic construction skills. This
democratization of season extension technology has enabled countless
small farms to extend their growing seasons profitably.
