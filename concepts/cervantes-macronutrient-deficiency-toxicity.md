### Deficiency
Lack of phosphorus causes stunted growth and smaller leaves; leaves turn
bluish-green with dark blotches. Stems, leaf stems (petioles), and main
veins turn reddish-purple starting on the leaf underside. Severely
affected leaves develop large purplish-black necrotic blotches, later
becoming bronzish-purple, dry, and dropping off. Flowering is delayed,
buds are smaller, and seed yield is poor. Aggravated by clay, acidic, and
soggy soils.
**Progression:**
- Stunted and very slow-growth plants
- Dark bluish-green leaves, often with dark blotches
- Plants are smaller overall
- Leaves turn bronzish-purple, contort, and drop
**Treatment:** Lower pH to 5.5-6.2 in hydroponics; 6-7 for clay soils;
5.5-6.5 for potting soils. Mix organic nutrients: bat guano, steamed
bone meal, natural phosphates, or barnyard manure into soil.
### Toxicity
Excessive phosphorus interferes with calcium, copper, iron, magnesium,
and zinc stability and uptake. Toxic symptoms manifest as deficiency of
zinc, iron, magnesium, calcium, and copper; zinc deficiency is most
common. May take several weeks to surface, especially if buffered by a
stable pH.
**Treatment:** Flush growing medium with a very mild complete fertilizer.
Flush minimum three times the volume of water for volume of medium.
## Potassium (K) -- Mobile
### Role
Helps combine sugars, starches, and carbohydrates. Essential to growth by
cell division. Increases chlorophyll in foliage and helps regulate
stomata openings. Essential in accumulation and translocation of
carbohydrates. Necessary for proteins that augment oil content and improve
flavor. Encourages strong root growth and is associated with disease
resistance and water intake.
### Deficiency
Initially plants appear healthy but are susceptible to disease. Older
leaves (first tips and margins, then whole leaves) develop spots, turn
dark yellow, and die. Stems become weak and sometimes brittle. Potassium
is usually present in soil but locked in by high salinity.
**Progression:**
- Plants appear healthy with dark green foliage
- Leaves lose their luster
- Branching may increase but branches are weak and scrawny
- Leaf margins turn grey, progress to rusty-brown, curl up and dry
- Yellowing of older leaves accompanied by rust-colored blotches
- Leaves curl up, rot sets in, older leaves drop
- Flowering retarded and greatly diminished
**Treatment:** First leach toxic salt out of soil, then apply a complete
N-P-K fertilizer. Organic growers add soluble potash (wood ashes) mixed
with water. Be careful: wood ash pH is normally above 10. Use a
pH-lowering mix to bring pH to around 6.5 before application. Foliar
feeding for potassium deficiency is not recommended.
