# Zwitterionic Phosphate Intermediate Discovery

One of the most significant findings in Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda's 2003
synthesis of [[gottlieb-psilocybin-production-extraction-methods]] by simple
filtration.

## The Phosphorylation Reaction

For the synthesis of [[psilocybin]] (2) from [[psilocin]] (1), several phosphorylation
methodologies were applied. Most did not consume the starting psilocin. The
phosphoryl iodide method (using tribenzyl phosphite, I2, and DMAP) and the
pyrophosphate method (using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-BuLi) both appeared
promising. The pyrophosphate method was selected for large-scale work due to easy
handling and reagent stability.

The phosphorylation was carried out by adding tetrabenzylpyrophosphate to a
solution of psilocin in THF at -78°C, then allowing the temperature to warm to
0°C over one hour. TLC showed consumption of psilocin and formation of a new
product (the expected dibenzyl phosphate derivative, compound 7).

## Unexpected Zwitterion Formation

After the usual aqueous workup to remove excess reagents, the 1H NMR spectrum of
the remaining substance in CDCl3 showed complicated signals. Rechecking the TLC
revealed an additional spot at the origin, and the whitish material no longer
dissolved in dichloromethane.

A similar observation had been reported by Nichols and Frescas, who concluded that
hydrolytic cleavage of one O-benzyl group had occurred, yielding a zwitterionic
O-monobenzyl phosphate as a mixture. Shirota and colleagues pursued purification
by preparative reversed-phase HPLC, which afforded a single compound (8).

## 2D NMR Structural Elucidation

The 1H and 13C NMR spectra of 8 in CD3OD showed signals for two sets of benzyl
groups and a psilocin core. Critically, the proton signal of the methylene on one
benzyl group was shifted to high field (delta_H 4.56, 2H, s) compared to the
other (delta_H 4.98, 1H, s; 4.96, 1H, s). The proton signals of the
N,N-dimethyl parts and methylene groups on the psilocin core were shifted to low
field relative to psilocin itself.

The 31P NMR spectrum confirmed the presence of the phosphate moiety. These data
suggested an intramolecular conversion in which one benzyl group migrated from the
oxygen to the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethylaminoethyl [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-side-chain-construction-psilocin-synthesis]], forming a
quaternary ammonium ion.

Confirmation was achieved using HMBC (Heteronuclear Multiple Bond Correlation)
and NOESY (Nuclear Overhauser Effect Spectroscopy) experiments. The HMBC spectrum
revealed that one benzyl group was directly linked at the nitrogen of the
N,N-dimethyl part, confirming the quaternary ammonium structure. The NOESY
spectrum provided additional support for these linkages.

## Zwitterionic Structure

The zwitterionic structure of compound 8 features a positively charged
quaternary ammonium (N-benzyl-N,N-dimethyl) and a negatively charged phosphate
group. This zwitterion is designated as {benzyl[2-(4-oxyindol-3-yl)ethyl]
dimethylammonio}-4-O-benzyl phosphate.

The conversion from the neutral O,O-dibenzyl phosphate (7) to the zwitterionic
N,O-dibenzyl phosphate (8) was found to be easily achieved simply by suspending
the worked-up reaction mixture in CH2Cl2 overnight, suggesting the migration is
thermodynamically favorable.

## Practical Significance

The zwitterionic nature of compound 8 is what makes the large-scale synthesis
practical. The zwitterion is insoluble in CH2Cl2, while excess dibenzyl phosphate
remains soluble. This allows isolation of the intermediate by simple filtration
with a CH2Cl2 wash, giving compound 8 in over 85% yield. Previous methods by
Nichols and Frescas had required preparative HPLC purification, making gram-scale
production impractical. This discovery transformed the synthesis from an
interesting academic exercise into a practical method for producing analytical
standards for forensic and regulatory applications.
