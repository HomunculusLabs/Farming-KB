# JADAM Human Manure Liquid Fertilizer

Human excrement and urine are permitted materials under organic farming regulations and represent the oldest, most nutrient-complete fertilizer known to agriculture. Most famous Korean local produce brands were historically grown with human manure. Youngsang Cho calls for rediscovering this "precious resource" that flush toilets have eliminated from farming.

## Youngsang Cho's Position

- "Our ancestors saw feces and urine as money."
- "It is foolish to flush this fertilizer away; they become nothing but environmental pollutants when in fact they can save the environment."
- "I do not understand why you would need animals when your family can produce better quality fertilizer!"
- The body is "the ultimate fertilizer producing machine."
- Bad smell indicates high nitrogen content -- ancestors could judge nitrogen levels by smell
- Smell was never an issue for thousands of years of organic farming
- Input manufacturers campaign against traditional inputs to sell commercial products

## The Phosphorus Crisis

The UK Soil Association's 2010 report "Peak Phosphorus and the Threat to Our Food Security" warns that phosphate rock (source of phosphorus fertilizer) will peak in production by 2033, after which prices will soar. In 2007-2008, phosphate rock price surged 800%. Human manure is rich in N, P, and K -- it is the last option in the coming age of chemical fertilizer depletion.

## Production Potential Per Family

A family of four produces per year:
- Human manure JLF: approximately 1 T (264 gal)
- Urine JLF: over 2 T (528 gal)
- Food by-product JLF: 2 T (528 gal)

Individual daily production:
- Feces: ~150g (meat-eaters) to ~400g (vegetarians) = ~0.2T/year
- Urine: 1-2 kg/day = ~0.5T/year; one day's urine contains ~30g urea plus amino acids and inorganic salts

## Urine JADAM Liquid Fertilizer

**Analysis (per 100L urine + leaf mold):**
- pH: 9.1 | EC: 61.15 ds/m | T-N: 2.48% | P2O5: 0.016% | K2O: 0.183%
- Over 30 times richer in nitrogen than wild grass JLF
- Excellent replacement for chemical nitrogen fertilizers

**Recipe:**
1. Collect urine continuously in a container
2. Throw in a handful of leaf mold occasionally
3. Keep lid closed to limit evaporation
4. Let ferment for more than 6 months

**Application:**
- Dilution: 50-500 times (standard x100)
- When to apply: early to mid-growth
- How to apply: soil drench or foliar spray
- Use when crop growth seems weak (nitrogen supplement)
- Combine with wild grass and fruit JLF as main additional fertilizer

## Human Feces JADAM Liquid Fertilizer

**Analysis (per 100L feces + leaf mold):**
- pH: 9.5 | EC: 12.51 ds/m | T-N: 3.92% | P2O5: 0.085% | K2O: 0.212%
- Richer in nitrogen AND phosphorus than urine JLF
- C/N ratio: 1.36 (very narrow -- excellent for plant uptake)

**Recipe:**
1. Build excrement-collecting toilets on the farm
2. Throw wood chips and rice stalks in before use (reduces splashing)
3. Add leaf mold to the collection container
4. Close lid and let ferment

**Fermentation Requirements (organic regulation):**
- Option A: Ferment in high heat (over 50C / 122F) for over 7 days
- Option B: Ferment at ambient temperature for over 6 months
- **Cannot be directly sprayed onto parts for human consumption**

**Application:**
- Dilution: 50-500 times (standard x100)
- When to apply: early to mid-growth
- How to apply: soil drench or foliar spray (NOT on edible parts directly)
- Use x100 dilution as starting point; adjust based on plant response

## Safety Protocols

1. **Fermentation is mandatory** -- raw application is not permitted under organic regulations
2. **High-heat option:** Maintain above 50C for 7+ days (achieved naturally in summer; may need insulation in winter)
3. **Ambient option:** Simply wait 6+ months with lid closed
4. **No direct contact with edible portions** -- apply to soil around plants, or as foliar only on non-edible plant parts
5. **Hygiene:** Use dedicated containers, tools, and gloves. Wash hands thoroughly after handling
6. **Toilet design:** Throw in wood chips, rice stalks, or sawdust before each use to absorb moisture and reduce odor/splashing

## Comparison to Other JLF Types

| JLF Source | T-N% | Relative N Strength |
|-----------|------|-------------------|
| Wild grass/fruit | ~0.05% | 1x (baseline) |
| Human feces | 3.92% | ~80x |
| Human urine | 2.48% | ~50x |
| Mackerel fish | 10.98% | ~220x |
| Fish meal | 4.69% | ~94x |

Human feces JLF has 3x the nitrogen of human urine JLF and ~80x that of wild grass JLF.

## Historical Context

In Korea's past, human manure was so treasured that a trade existed for buying and selling collection rights. The introduction of flush toilets in the name of hygiene "completely exterminated human manure from farming." Cho argues this was a catastrophic loss of a free, complete, and irreplaceable nutrient source.
