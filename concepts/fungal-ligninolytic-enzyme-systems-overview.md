# Fungal Ligninolytic Enzyme Systems Overview

Updated: 2026-04-18

The ligninolytic enzyme system (LDS) is a collection of extracellular enzymes produced by white rot basidiomycetes that enables them to degrade lignin, the second most abundant biopolymer on Earth after cellulose. These enzymes are unique because they attack a non-repeating, non-hydrolyzable polymer through non-specific free radical mechanisms, unlike most enzymes that target specific bonds.





## Why Lignin Requires a Special System

Lignin is a complex, three-dimensional polymer of phenylpropanoid units linked by a variety of carbon-carbon and ether bonds with no symmetrical repeating structure. This means there is no single bond type for a specific enzyme to target. Instead, white rot fungi produce enzymes that generate highly reactive, non-specific free radicals capable of attacking many bond types simultaneously. See [[lignin-degradation-mechanisms-wood-rot-fungi]] for detailed degradation pathways.





## The Three Main Ligninolytic Enzymes

### Lignin Peroxidase (LiP)
- Approximately 42 kDa haem-containing glycoprotein
- Catalyzes H2O2-dependent one-electron oxidation of aromatic substrates
- pH optimum below 3.0 (unusually acidic)
- Requires veratryl alcohol as a redox mediator to oxidize non-phenolic aromatic compounds; veratryl alcohol also protects LiP from inactivation by excess H2O2
- Directly oxidizes PAHs, chlorinated aromatics, and dyes
- Produced by Phanerochaete chrysosporium, Bjerkandera adusta, and other white rot species

### Manganese Peroxidase (MnP)
- Haem-containing glycoprotein
- Catalyzes the oxidation of Mn(II) to Mn(III) using H2O2
- Mn(III) is chelated by organic acids (oxalate, malonate, lactate) and acts as a diffusible oxidant
- The Mn(III)-organic acid complex mediates oxidation of phenolic compounds and some non-phenolic compounds
- Mn(II) is critical for activity in most species (exception: Bjerkandera adusta MnP oxidizes dyes Mn-independently)
- Plays the primary role in bleach plant effluent decolorization by P. chrysosporium

### Laccase
- Blue multi-copper oxidase (polyphenol oxidase)
- Uses molecular oxygen (O2) as the electron acceptor, producing water as the only byproduct
- Oxidizes phenolic compounds directly; oxidizes non-phenolic compounds only with mediator molecules (ABTS, HBT, 1-hydroxybenzotriazole)
- Produced by Trametes versicolor, Coriolopsis gallica, Flavodon flavus, and many other basidiomycetes
- Plays the primary role in BPE decolorization by T. versicolor
- Laccases from C. gallica oxidize benzo[a]pyrene and other PAHs





## Regulation

Ligninolytic enzyme production is typically triggered by:
- **Nitrogen limitation** -- the classic trigger for secondary metabolism in P. chrysosporium
- **Carbon limitation** -- also triggers ligninolytic activity
- **Manganese** -- Mn(II) in the medium stimulates MnP production
- **Chemical inducers** -- veratryl alcohol, 2,5-xylidine, and various aromatic compounds can induce LiP, MnP, or laccase production
