# DMT vs Psilocybin vs LSD — Pharmacokinetic and Experiential Comparison

Terence McKenna, drawing on decades of personal experimentation beginning with
DMT in 1965, drew sharp pharmacokinetic and phenomenological distinctions
between the three major classical hallucinogens: N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT),
psilocybin, and lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD). These differences, he
argued, reflect fundamentally different relationships between the compounds
and human neurochemistry.

## DMT — The Endogenous Flash

DMT is distinguished by its extraordinary pharmacokinetic profile:
fifteen-second onset when smoked, peak effects within thirty seconds, and
a total duration of approximately five minutes. McKenna regarded this
rapidity as evidence that the brain is "absolutely at home with this compound":

> The very fact that the onset of DMT is so rapid, coming on in forty-five
> seconds and lasting five minutes, means that the brain is absolutely at
> home with this compound.

This interpretation rests on [[mckenna-endogenous-dmt-brain-familiarity|DMT's status as an endogenous compound]] — it is present in small amounts in the
human brain, structurally similar to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine), and
metabolized rapidly by monoamine oxidase. The brain's enzymatic machinery
handles DMT with the efficiency of a familiar substrate, not a foreign toxin.

The experiential quality matches the pharmacokinetic intensity. The [[mckenna-dmt-onset-phenomenology-and-mode-shift-experience|DMT onset]]
involves a [[mckenna-dmt-onset-phenomenology-ripping-cellophane|characteristic acoustic phenomenon]] (described as "ripping cellophane" or "radio entelechy
ripping out of the organic matrix"), an ascending tone, and a mode shift that
deposits the experiencer into what McKenna called the "merry go raum" — a
domed, underground-feeling space of extraordinary alien presence.

## Psilocybin — The Slow Build

Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) produces qualitatively
similar phenomenology to DMT but with dramatically different kinetics. The
experience builds over approximately one hour and is sustained for several
hours. McKenna described the relationship:

> The psilocybin mushrooms do the same things that DMT does, although the
> experience builds up over an hour and is sustained for a couple of hours.

The slower onset of psilocybin reflects its pharmacokinetic pathway. Unlike
DMT, which is active when smoked and reaches the brain directly, psilocybin
is orally active and must be dephosphorylated to psilocin (4-HO-DMT) before
crossing the blood-brain barrier. This conversion adds latency to onset while
extending duration through gradual hepatic processing.

Despite the kinetic differences, McKenna emphasized that psilocybin produces
the same confrontation with [[mckenna-heraclitus-aeon-child-play-tryptamine-ecstasy|alien intelligence]] and
[[mckenna-translinguistic-matter-and-exteriorized-soul|translinguistic information complexes]] that characterize the DMT experience. The
[[mckenna-tryptamine-hallucinogens-and-consciousness|tryptamine hypercontinuum]]
is the same; only the time scale differs.

## LSD — The Prolonged Retention

LSD represents a different pharmacological class entirely. McKenna noted that
LSD is "retained in the body for some time," contrasting it with DMT's rapid
clearance. This prolonged retention has several consequences:

- The experience lasts 8–12 hours, far exceeding either DMT or psilocybin
- The extended duration creates a fundamentally different experiential
  texture — more time for integration, narrative development, and ego
  engagement
- The slower clearance means the body must metabolize an unfamiliar
  molecular structure, potentially increasing physical stress

McKenna's implicit hierarchy — favoring the endogenous familiarity of DMT
over the synthetic unfamiliarity of LSD — reflects his broader epistemological
commitment to natural compounds. He stated: "There is some certainty that one
is dealing with a creature of integrity if one deals with a plant, but the
creatures born in the demonic artifice of laboratories have to be dealt with
very, very carefully."

## The Tryptamine Family

Both DMT and psilocybin are tryptamines — derivatives of the amino acid
tryptophan that share structural similarity with serotonin. This structural
kinship is central to McKenna's argument about their significance:

- **Serotonin** (5-hydroxytryptamine) — the major neurotransmitter found
  in all life, most concentrated in humans
- **DMT** (N,N-dimethyltryptamine) — endogenous hallucinogen, present in
  the human brain in trace amounts
- **Psilocybin** (4-phosphoryloxy-DMT) — the phosphorylated prodrug found
  in psilocybin mushrooms

LSD, by contrast, is an ergoline derivative — structurally related to ergot
alkaloids rather than tryptamines. This chemical distinction may underlie
the phenomenological differences: tryptamines produce the distinctive Logos
experience (direct engagement with language centers and [[mckenna-glossolalia-and-primal-language-tryptamine-states|translinguistic objects]]), while LSD produces a qualitatively different state.

## Phenomenological Distinctions

McKenna insisted that each hallucinogen constitutes "a phenomenologically
defined universe unto itself," resisting the common practice of lumping them
together. Key differences:

**DMT:** Maximum intensity, minimum duration. Complete ego displacement while
remaining fully lucid. Terror is common and considered a validity marker.
The [[mckenna-translinguistic-objects-hyperdimensional-language|entities]]
are immediate and overwhelming.

**Psilocybin:** Same phenomenological content as DMT but with time for
narrative development. The confrontation with alien intelligence unfolds
more gradually, allowing for greater integration and understanding during
the experience itself.

**LSD:** Visual hallucinations are present but the Logos phenomenon — the
direct encounter with translinguistic intelligence — is less prominent.
The experience is more aesthetically oriented, with greater emphasis on
pattern perception, emotional processing, and philosophical contemplation.

## Implications for Consciousness Research

The pharmacokinetic differences between these compounds carry implications
for the study of consciousness. DMT's fifteen-second onset demonstrates
that radically altered states of consciousness can be accessed nearly
instantaneously, suggesting that the neural substrate for these experiences
is always present and only requires appropriate activation. Psilocybin's
slower build shows the same substrate accessible through oral ingestion.
LSD's prolonged duration demonstrates that the brain can sustain these
altered states for extended periods without neurological damage.

McKenna used these observations to argue that consciousness is far more
plastic and multidimensional than the standard model allows: "We are just
beginning to push the buttons on the mind. Once we take a serious engineering
approach to this, we are going to discover the plasticity, the mutability,
the eternal nature of the mind."
