# Shamanic Singing, Icaros, and Rhythmic Entrainment in Entheogenic Ceremonies

## Overview

Shamanic singing is a nearly universal element of entheogenic ceremonies
across cultures. The songs — known as icaros in the Amazon, peyote songs
among the [[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] Church, and ndzamba among the Bwiti — serve as
both navigational guides through [[weil-the-natural-mind-altered-states-normalcy]] and active agents in the
healing process itself.

Ralph Metzner identified the continuous use of song as one of the key
distinguishing features of [[metzner-three-distinctive-features-shamanic-entheogenic-ceremonies]] ceremonies, noting that
the rhythmic, percussive driving quality of the singing helps to sustain
the altered state and deepen the experience.

Shamans consider their songs to be received directly from plant spirits
during their own initiation visions, not composed through ordinary
creative effort.

## Icaros — The Shamanic Songs of the Amazon

Among mestizo and indigenous ayahuasceros in the Peruvian and Brazilian
Amazon, the icaros constitute the shaman's primary therapeutic tool.

Luis Eduardo Luna documented that vegetalistas claim to learn their
icaros directly from the plant spirits during their dieta periods of
isolation in the jungle. Each plant teacher imparts its own song, which
the shaman then uses to invoke that plant's specific healing power.

An experienced vegetalista may know dozens or even hundreds of icaros,
each one associated with a particular plant ally and its particular
medicinal application.

The icaros are performed during the [[ayahuasca-ceremony-pharmacology-safety]] at specific
moments: to call in protective spirits, to intensify the visions, to
cleanse participants of negative energy, and to bring the session to a
close.

They are typically sung in a high, melodic register, sometimes with a
rapid, rhythmic quality that drives the energy of the ceremony forward.

## Rhythmic Entrainment and Altered States

The rhythmic quality of shamanic singing serves a physiological function
in addition to its spiritual significance.

Continuous, rhythmic drumming and singing at approximately four to seven
beats per second has been shown to produce theta-range brainwave
entrainment, corresponding to the deep trance state characteristic of
shamanic journeying.

[[michael-harner]] observed among the Jivaro that shamans use rapid, monotonous
chanting combined with the consumption of natema (ayahuasca) to enter a
state he called the "shamanic state of consciousness" (SSC).

Metzner argued that the rhythmic, percussive driving quality of shamanic
singing acts as a kind of technology of consciousness, sustaining and
deepening the altered state produced by the entheogenic plant.

This entrainment effect may explain why music is so universally present
in entheogenic ceremonies and why the absence of music is often reported
as making the experience more difficult or fragmented.
