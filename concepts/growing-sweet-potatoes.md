# Growing Sweet Potatoes

Sweet potatoes are a warm-climate crop that demands completely different treatment than Irish potatoes. They prefer light, well-drained soils, need warm temperatures throughout their growing season, and produce their edible tubers (technically root thickenings, not true tubers) in a spreading vine habit rather than in a compact hill. Understanding their unique requirements is essential for success.

## Sweet vs. Irish Potatoes: Key Differences

| Feature | Irish Potatoes | Sweet Potatoes |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| Climate | Cool-season | Warm-season (night temp >60°F/15°C) |
| Plant part eaten | True tubers (stem swellings) | Root thickenings |
| Propagation | Seed pieces (cut tubers) | Slips (shoots) |
| Soil preference | Wide range | Light, well-drained |
| Frost tolerance | Some | None |
| Vine habit | Upright to spreading | Long trailing vines |
| Virus accumulation | Yes | Yes |
| Caloric density | High | Higher |

## Climate Requirements

- Sweet potatoes need the night temperature to stop falling below 60°F (15°C) before planting
- In most of the United States, start the nursery bed 4-6 weeks before the correct planting time
- Near the equator, you may have to bed the roots in shade to sprout at the right temperature
- In cool climates, use a soil- or sand-filled box in a bright sunny location or a germination box with bottom heat

## Propagation: Growing Slips

### Small-Scale Method (Suspension)

1. Suspend a sweet potato in a container of water (cover half of it)
2. Place in a warm location
3. Shoots will emerge from the top portion
4. Once shoots reach 10-12 inches (25-30 cm), cut off with a sharp knife 2 inches (5 cm) above the soil

### Larger-Scale Method (Sand Bed)

1. Place several presprouted (conditioned) sweet potatoes on a bed of sand
2. Cover with a 2-inch (5 cm) layer of moist soil
3. Keep soil between 70-75°F (21-24°C)
4. Shoots will develop; cut when 10-12 inches long with a stout stem

### Buying Slips

If the seedling raiser is reputable, buying may be the best option because:
- Commercially raised shoots are usually only a few generations from pure, virus-free tissue-culture clones
- Virus-free starts increase yields by as much as a third
- Sweet potatoes accumulate virus diseases like Irish potatoes do
- They also have a tendency to mutate

### Important Warning

Do not use supermarket sweet potatoes for seed because:
- They may be treated with sprout-inhibiting chemicals
- Even genuinely organic ones may carry virus load
- Laboratory-grown mini-tubers are available but not ideal

## Planting

### Transplanting Technique

Because the slips are unrooted, plant them almost horizontally, about 2 inches deep:
1. Make sure to allow two leaves to be above ground
2. Take care not to damage the shoot's terminal bud
3. Immediately water after setting them in their permanent position

### Spacing

- Rows 4 feet (120 cm) apart on center
- In-row spacing: 12-15 inches (30-40 cm)
- May also be arranged with 3 seedlings per moderately fertile hill, hills on 4-foot (120 cm) centers

## Soil Preparation

### Preferred Soils

The light soils that sweet potatoes prefer drain rapidly. Because of this:

- Keep soil moist as plants grow, but **never soaked**
- Moist but not wet encourages better root development
- Overwatering causes rotting and misshapen roots

### Bed Preparation

- Form wide raised rows 4 feet (120 cm) apart on center
- In maritime climates, raise beds about 6-10 inches (15-25 cm) above narrow footpaths for drainage after heavy rains
- Once beds are formed, plant vines 12-15 inches (30-40 cm) apart atop these ridges

### Fertilization

- Spread a quarter to half inch (6-12 mm) layer of well-rotted manure or finished compost over their entire growing area, then dig it in
- Alternatively, dig in a stand of overwintered legume green manure before planting
- Sweet potatoes do well with moderate fertility — excess nitrogen produces lush vines but small roots

## Cultivation

### Early Growth

- Keep the area well-weeded before the vines run
- The light soils dry rapidly, so monitor moisture carefully
- Shallow scraping with a hoe is the best weeding method — avoids disturbing shallow roots

### Hilling

- Gradually make a low hill around the stem of the vine as it grows
- Some university agricultural extensions say hilling reduces insect problems
- Do not hill excessively — sweet potatoes form near the soil surface

### Vine Management

- Do not cut or prune the vines — they are the plant's food factory
- Vines can reach 14+ feet (425+ cm) in length
- Each vine can produce 3-5 good-sized potatoes
- Avoid walking on the beds once vines are established

## Growth Cycle

1. **Establishment** (weeks 1-3): Slips root and begin growing
2. **Vine growth** (weeks 3-8): Rapid vegetative growth
3. **Root enlargement** (weeks 8-14): Underground roots begin to thicken
4. **Maturation** (weeks 14-20): Roots reach full size, skins toughen
5. **Pre-harvest** (week 20+): Vines may yellow; time to harvest

## Harvest and Storage

### When to Harvest

- Harvest before first frost — sweet potatoes are very frost-sensitive
- Usually 100-140 days from transplanting, depending on variety
- Vines may yellow and die back naturally

### Harvest Method

- Cut vines first, then dig carefully
- Sweet potatoes bruise easily — handle gently
- Do not wash before storage — just brush off loose soil
- Cure in a warm, humid place (80-85°F / 27-29°C, 85-90% humidity) for 10-14 days to heal cuts and toughen skins
