## Permaculture Definition, Vision, and Movement History

This page covers the definition of permaculture, its evolution from concept to global movement, the structure of the [[permaculture-design]] Course, and the various ways permaculture has been perceived and received by different communities.

## Defining Permaculture

### Original Definition (1978)

The word Permaculture was coined by [[bill-mollison]] and [[david-holmgren]] in the mid-1970s to describe an "integrated, evolving system of perennial or self-perpetuating plant and animal species useful to man."

### Current Definition

A more current definition reflecting expanded focus: **"Consciously designed landscapes which mimic the patterns and relationships found in nature, while yielding an abundance of food, fibre and energy for provision of local needs."**

### As a Design System

More precisely, Holmgren sees permaculture as **the use of systems thinking and design principles** that provide the organizing framework for implementing the vision. It draws together diverse ideas, skills, and ways of living needed to move from being dependent consumers to becoming responsible and productive citizens.

Permaculture is not the landscape itself, or even the skills of organic gardening, sustainable farming, or energy-efficient building. Rather, it is the design system used to design, establish, manage, and improve all efforts toward sustainability.

### As a Network

Permaculture is also a **worldwide network and movement** of individuals and groups working in rich and poor countries on all continents. After 20 years, permaculture may rank as one of Australia"s most significant "intellectual exports."

## Foundational Assumptions

Permaculture was originally based on these core assumptions:

1. The environmental crisis is real and will transform modern global industrial society beyond recognition, directly threatening human survival
2. Ongoing impacts of industrial society on biodiversity are far greater than the massive changes of the last few hundred years
3. Humans are subject to the same scientific (energy) laws that govern the material universe
4. Fossil fuel exploitation was the primary cause of the explosion in human numbers and technology
5. The inevitable depletion of fossil fuels will see a return to general patterns observable in nature and pre-industrial societies

## Key Intellectual Influences

### Howard T. Odum

The most significant influence on Holmgren"s thinking. Odum's systems ecology provided:

- Energy hierarchy concepts
- Maximum power principle
- EMERGY evaluation
- Understanding of the energy basis of civilization
- *Environment, Power & Society* (1971) was the first reference in Permaculture One

### Other Influences

- **[[stewart-brand]]** — Whole Earth Catalogue, Spaceship Earth concept
- **Edward De Bono** — Lateral thinking and design thinking
- **Norbert Wiener** — Cybernetics
- **Rudolf Steiner** — Spiritual science, biodynamics, Waldorf education
- **Carl Jung** — Jungian psychology
- **Wendell Berry** — Agrarian philosophy, critique of planetary stewardship

## The Permaculture Design Course

The Permaculture Design Course (PDC) has been the prime vehicle for permaculture inspiration and training worldwide for over 15 years.

- **Curriculum codified:** 1984
- **Format:** Typically a two-week residential course
- **Evolution:** Divergent evolution of form and content by different teachers has produced varied experiences
- **Effectiveness:** Particularly effective in galvanizing fundamental change and new focus in participants' lives

### Debate About Content

Bill Mollison and others have asserted that failure to adhere to the curriculum, inclusion of religious beliefs outside "design science," and lack of attention to principles dilutes permaculture education. Holmgren balances this concern against the value of diversity, even when "like weeds, it comes in forms we don"t particularly like."

## Popular Conceptions of Permaculture

### Permaculture as Gardening

Permaculture has been accepted by many in Australian society as a benign activity — a system of gardening or commonsense, environmentally aware living. Television programs, DIY books, school projects, community gardens, and LETSystems have all contributed.

**Strength:** Complex, revolutionary ideas can exercise influence through positive grassroots processes without alienating people.

### Permaculture as Counterculture

The perception of permaculture as countercultural lifestyle has provided a wholistic framework for reorganizing lives and values for those ready for fundamental change. Particularly effective for young people disillusioned with consumer culture.

**Strength:** Facilitated experimentation and pioneering of lifestyle models directed by the ecological imperative.

## Academic and Professional Reception

### Positive Reactions

- "Permaculture provides a valuable [[holmgren-permaculture-flower-conceptual-framework]] for future thought on sane, sustainable societies" — Earle Barnhart, [[new-alchemy-institute]]
- University courses incorporating permaculture, including a postgraduate sustainable agriculture unit at UNE Orange (1992)
- Academic study from sociological, political, educational, ecological, and agricultural perspectives

### Negative Reactions

- "The professional community was outraged because we were combining architecture with biology, agriculture with forestry" — Bill Mollison
- Perceived as theoretical, utopian, and impractical for large-scale application
- Lack of intellectual rigor and populist image inhibited serious academic consideration
- Bill Mollison"s confrontational style alienated scientific communities

### Stuart Hill's Contribution

Stuart Hill's "Deep Sustainability" perspective reinforces personal and bottom-up strategies for change. His analysis is primarily psychosocial rather than just political — requiring recognition of [[permaculture-self-regulation-energy-hierarchy-personal-responsibility]] and self-change before pointing fingers at others.

## Permaculture vs. Green Tech Optimism

Permaculture differs from technological optimists (like Amory Lovins" natural capitalism) in key ways:

- **Priority on natural capital:** Using existing wealth to rebuild forests and trees as proven wealth storage
- **Bottom-up redesign:** Starting with individual and household as drivers for change
- **Designed for collapse:** Predicated on likelihood of some degree of breakdown in technology, economics, and society
- **Pre-industrial models:** Sees traditional sustainable societies as reflecting design principles observable in nature

## Third Wave Environmentalism

Environmental awareness emerges in waves of intense activity followed by consolidation phases:

- **First wave (1970s):** Club of Rome report (1972), oil shocks (1973, 1975) — permaculture emerged here
- **Second wave (late 1980s):** Greenhouse effect awareness — accelerated permaculture interest
- **1990s:** Consolidation phase as technology and global economy diverted attention
- **Third wave (1999+):** Signs of new environmentalism with mainstreaming of second-wave innovations

Each new phase throws up insights challenging previous assumptions.

## Over-Promotion Risks

Robert Gilman described communities being "inoculated against good ideas" by too-early and strong promotion before ideas are well tested. Permaculture was catapulted into popular domain so quickly that it may have muddied further intellectual development — comparable to how Sustainable Development was discredited by rapid projection into intergovernmental policy.

## Key Takeaways

1. Permaculture is simultaneously a vision, a design system, and a global network
2. The foundational assumption of energy descent distinguishes permaculture from technological optimism
3. The PDC has been the primary vehicle for spreading permaculture worldwide
4. Both "gardening" and "counterculture" perceptions have strengths and limitations
5. Permaculture's academic reception has been mixed — more valued by practitioners than scholars
6. Howard Odum's systems ecology is the most significant theoretical influence
7. Each wave of environmentalism has both mainstreamed previous innovations and generated new ones
