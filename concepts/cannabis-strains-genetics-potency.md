# Cannabis Strains — Genetics, Types, and Potency

## Overview

There are many strains (varieties) of Cannabis alive today, most resulting from human intervention through selective breeding. Breeders aim to produce strains with desirable characteristics including taste, aroma, potency, growth characteristics, and specific psychoactive effects. Understanding strain genetics is fundamental to successful cultivation and product selection.

## Cannabis Plant Anatomy

The cannabis plant can be divided into six main sections:

1. **Bud** — The flower, the most potent part of the plant.
2. **Stem** — [[comparison-silicon-vs-calcium-in-plant-defense-and-structural-support]]; low potency.
3. **Branches** — Support the buds; moderate potency in sugar leaves.
4. **Nodes** — Junction points where branches meet the stem.
5. **Leaves** — Photosynthesis; lower potency than buds.
6. **Main Cola** — The largest top bud, typically the most potent.

## Strain Development

### Breeding Goals

Breeders select for:
- **Taste and aroma** ([[cannabis-terpene-profiles]]).
- **Potency** (THC, CBD, and other cannabinoid levels).
- **Growth characteristics** (height, yield, flowering time, resistance to pests/diseases).
- **Effect type** (indica body high, sativa cerebral high, hybrid balance).

### Selecting Seeds

- **Best source**: A friend who has grown a strain you enjoyed — free, and you know the effect.
- **Internet seed banks**: Wide selection but risks include rip-offs, shipping limitations, misrepresentation, and high cost ($80–300 for 10–16 seeds).
- When selecting, consider: strain genetics, grower reviews, flowering time, expected yield, and potency ratings.

## Hashish and Potency Grading

### What is Hashish?

[[hashish]] is a concentrated cannabis product made by separating and compressing the resin glands (trichomes) from the plant material. It can improve or sometimes degrade overall potency depending on the source material and technique.

### Hashish Grading System

| Grade | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | Zero Zero | Purest form; high THC + excellent technique |
| 0 | Zero | High quality |
| 1 | One | Good quality |
| 2 | Two | Moderate quality |
| 3 | Three | Lower quality |

"Zero Zero" is the highest grade, requiring both high THC levels in the source plant and excellent [[psilocybin-methyl-alcohol-extraction-technique]]. Note that good technique with low-THC plant material may still produce only grade 2 or 3.

### Factors Affecting Potency

- **Genetics**: The strain's inherent cannabinoid profile.
- **Growing conditions**: Light, nutrients, temperature, and stress levels.
- **Harvest timing**: Trichome maturity at harvest.
- **Curing and storage**: Proper post-harvest handling preserves potency.

## Plant Height Considerations

- When planning a grow, consider the strain's **maximum genetic height**.
- Plants can always be shortened through pruning, but rarely doubled in height beyond their genetic potential.
- Most indoor strains reach 2–4 feet; some sativas can exceed 6 feet.
- Plan grow space height accordingly, accounting for lighting distance.

## Security Fundamentals

The single most important security principle for [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-cannabis-cultivation]]:

> **NEVER TELL ANYONE THAT YOU ARE GROWING CANNABIS.**

- Loose lips cause 99.9% of security breaches.
- Only people who live with you should know about the grow.
- If sharing product, attribute it to a purchase.
- Consider guerrilla outdoor growing if household members cannot maintain discretion.
- Growing alone is the safest approach.
