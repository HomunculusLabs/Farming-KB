# Edaphic Factors in Microbiome Structuring

## Overview

Edaphic factors — the physical, chemical, and biological properties of soil — are the primary determinants of microbial community composition across all root-associated compartments. The [[teaming-with-microbes-bacteria-and-archaea-in-soil]], denitrifiers, nitrogen fixers, and generalist heterotrophs each occupy distinct ecological niches defined by nitrogen availability.

### 2. Salinity

Salinity was the second strongest predictor of community structure:

- Weighted analysis: r-stat = 0.437, p = 0.001
- Unweighted analysis: r-stat = 0.620, p = 0.001

Soil salinity creates osmotic stress that differentially affects microbial taxa based on their halotolerance. Halophilic and halotolerant organisms are favored in saline conditions, while sensitive taxa are suppressed. The two soils in the Cannabis study showed dramatic salinity differences (7.15 dS/m vs 1.73 dS/m), contributing to strong community divergence between locations.

### 3. Total Organic Carbon

- Weighted analysis: r-stat = 0.330, p = 0.001
- Unweighted analysis: r-stat = 0.512, p = 0.001

Organic carbon serves as the primary energy source for heterotrophic soil microorganisms. Carbon quality and quantity influence which taxa can thrive — copiotrophs (fast-growing, nutrient-rich adapted organisms) dominate in high-carbon environments while oligotrophs (slow-growing, nutrient-efficient organisms) prevail in carbon-limited conditions. The Cannabis study found organic carbon varied from 3.02% to 20.0% across soil types.

### 4. Water Content

- Weighted analysis: r-stat = 0.281, p = 0.001
- Unweighted analysis: r-stat = 0.466, p = 0.001

Soil water content governs oxygen availability, solute diffusion, and microbial mobility. Water-filled pore space directly determines whether aerobic, microaerophilic, or anaerobic metabolism predominates. In the study, water content ranged from 0.101 to 0.371, reflecting significant differences in soil moisture regimes between growing locations.

### 5. pH (Weakest Factor)

- Weighted analysis: r-stat = 0.221, p = 0.001
- Unweighted analysis: r-stat = 0.292, p = 0.001

Although pH was the weakest of the five tested factors, it remained highly significant. This is noteworthy because in many broader-scale soil microbiome studies, pH is often the strongest predictor of bacterial community composition. The relatively narrow pH range in the Cannabis study (6.63 to 6.94 — all mildly acidic) likely compressed its explanatory power compared to studies spanning wider pH gradients.

## Soil Types Compared

The study analyzed five soil types across two experiments:

| Soil ID | Texture | pH | Salinity | Total N (%) | Organic C (%) | Water Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MB.1.B | Sandy loam | 6.94 | 7.15 | 1.41 | 5.00 | 0.164 |
| MB.1.SD | Sandy loam | 6.80 | 7.10 | 1.51 | 4.32 | 0.178 |
| MB.1.BK | Sandy loam | 6.82 | 7.44 | 1.30 | 3.31 | 0.101 |
| MB.2 | Sandy loam | 6.63 | 5.12 | 0.26 | 3.02 | 0.113 |
| OC.2 | Sandy loam | 6.77 | 1.73 | 0.53 | 20.0 | 0.371 |

All soils were classified as sandy loam, with sand content ranging from 62-66% and clay from 17.8-20.7%. Despite the similar texture, the dramatic differences in salinity (1.73 to 7.44), organic carbon (3.02 to 20.0%), and nitrogen (0.26 to 1.51%) produced strongly differentiated microbial communities.

## Edaphic Effects Across Compartments

Edaphic factors influenced community structure across all three root-associated compartments:

- **Bulk soil**: strongest direct edaphic influence (expected, as communities are in direct contact with soil matrix)
- **Rhizosphere**: significant edaphic influence (r² = 0.21 for soil type alone, weighted UniFrac)
- **Endorhiza**: edaphic factors remained significant but were partially overridden by host genotype selection

The progressive dilution of edaphic influence from bulk soil to endorhiza is a key prediction of the two-tier selection model. Even within the endorhiza, however, soil type still explained a significant portion of community variation, confirming that the tier 1 selection filter continues to exert influence even after tier 2 genotype-based filtering.

## Interaction with Cultivar Effects

A critical finding was the interaction between soil type and cultivar in the second experiment:

- White Widow plants grown in two different soils showed endorhiza communities that were more similar to other plants in the same soil than to the same cultivar in a different soil
- This means that soil type can override cultivar effects when edaphic differences are large enough
- The implication for growers is that even the best cultivar genetics cannot fully compensate for poor soil conditions — the microbial partners available for selection are fundamentally shaped by soil properties

## Practical Implications for Soil Management

### Nitrogen Management

As the strongest community structuring factor, nitrogen management should be a primary focus:

- Excessive synthetic nitrogen can shift communities toward fast-growing copiotrophs at the expense of beneficial oligotrophs
- Organic nitrogen sources (compost, cover crops, amendments) provide slow-release nitrogen that supports more diverse communities
- Balanced C:N ratios in soil amendments help maintain microbial community stability

### Salinity Control

- Irrigation water quality directly impacts soil salinity and thus microbial community structure
