# Amazonian Boa Spirits and Water Beings in Shamanic Practice

Boa constrictors (anacondas and other large snakes) are among the most powerful and frequently depicted beings in Pablo Amaringo's ayahuasca visions. They serve as protectors, healers, owners of sacred lakes, and -- when employed by sorcerers -- terrifying weapons of destruction. The boa is the quintessential Amazonian spirit being, bridging the worlds of water, earth, and sky.

## Anacondas as Owners of Sacred Lakes

The twisting shapes of anacondas appear as the owners of mama cochas -- enormous lakes hidden in the forest. These lakes are sacred places inhabited by powerful spirits. The anacondas guard these waters and the beings within them.

### Atun-Ailpa-Machaco (Large Snake of the Earth)
A massive boa that surrounds the house where ayahuasca sessions take place. Its presence indicates that the ceremony is under the protection of powerful earth spirits.

## Types of Boa Spirits

### Yana-Yakumama (Black Boa, Mother of the Waters)
One of the most dangerous beings in the Amazonian spirit world. The yana-yakumama:
- Has a white stripe on its forehead, distinguishing it from other black boas
- Lives in the depths of black-water lakes and in lakes closed by the aquatic plant piripiri
- Comes up for sun-baths at midday when the sun is fullest, absorbing solar energy to fill itself with electromagnetism
- Used by witch sumirunas as a battleship to attack victims
- When deployed, a big electrified tornado rises from it, releasing flying animals (sarara, rompe-mortaja, tibemama, supay-masho, etc.)
- Behind it come the yana-puma-runa (black tiger-like people) with red eyes, symbolizing sorcerers

### Sachamama Boa
The forest mother appears in boa form in some visions, connecting the earth realm to the serpent archetype.

### Boa-Sacha
An unidentified plant whose spirit is a boa. It appears near the huiririma palm in visions of the golden city Korillacta.

### Boas with Hypnotic Powers
- **Mantona mama**: A large snake with hypnotic magnetism, used as a defensive power by healers
- **Gigantic black boa**: Protects healing groups with its hypnotic powers
- **Golden snake-sphinxes**: With horns and human faces, they grind enemies with their bodies

## Boas in Healing

### Diagnostic Boas
In Vision 42 (Lucero Ayahuasca), the twisting shapes of anacondas surround the ceremony space. The angash-sarara bird flies above the great snake. Together they establish the visionary field within which diagnosis and healing occur.

### Hypnotic Healing
The boa's hypnotic gaze is used therapeutically:
- The mantona mama's hypnotic magnetism can calm or immobilize
- Anacondas with multicolored horns radiate energy from their eyes and ears, using their tails to hypnotize
- The healer sits on a living stone tiger that floats on water, surrounded by his boa-powered genies

### Extraction of Illness
Some boa spirits are invoked to extract illness from patients. Their powerful electromagnetism can draw out harmful substances implanted by sorcerers.

# Amazonian Mythological Beings in Pablo Amaringo's Visionary World

Pablo Amaringo (1938–2009) was a Peruvian **vegetalista** and painter whose
visionary art documents the spirit beings encountered during **ayahuasca**
ceremonies. His paintings, collected in *Ayahuasca Visions* (Luna & Amaringo,
1991), reveal a complex cosmology of Amazonian mythological entities that
inhabit rivers, forests, mountains, and atmospheric realms — experienced as
living presences with agency, power, and moral significance.

## Sachamama — Mother of the Forest

**Vision 15**

The Sachamama ("mother of the forest") is a serpent of colossal proportions,
so thoroughly integrated with the landscape that trees and weeds grow on her
body. She may remain stationary for hundreds of years in a single location,
becoming indistinguishable from a small hill or earthen mound. A small pond
lies nearby, from which she drinks.
