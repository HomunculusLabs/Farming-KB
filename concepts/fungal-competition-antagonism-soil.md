## Morphological Responses to Competition

Fungi exhibit significant morphological changes during competitive encounters. Studies on
interactions between Stropharia caerulea and other cord-forming saprotrophic
basidiomycetes in soil trays demonstrated that fractal dimension and rate of biomass
increase varied depending on the interaction combination (Donnelly and Boddy, 2001).
These changes include:

Production of dense localized fans at confrontation zones, which serve as defensive
structures that concentrate hyphal biomass at the interface with competitors.

Altered fractal dimensions reflecting changes in mycelial branching patterns and
space-filling efficiency during combat.

Localized changes in mycelial morphology, including defensive ridges, pigment production,
and modifications to cord structure.

Ingress into the opponent's territory through specialized exploratory hyphae that breach
defensive barriers.

## Competition Between Mycorrhizal and Saprotrophic Fungi

A particularly important category of fungal competition occurs between mycorrhizal and
saprotrophic fungi for limiting nutrient sources. Ectomycorrhizal (EM) mycelia can
sometimes territorially exclude wood-decay saprotrophs, which are the organisms best
adapted for decomposition of lignocellulose, the most abundant and recalcitrant carbon
source entering the soil (Leake et al., 2002).
