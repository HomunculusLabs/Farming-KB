## Ancient Parallels

The Living Bread preparations are not unique to one tradition. Across widely separated cultures,
the same fundamental practice appears in different guises:

| Tradition | Sacred Substance | Preparation |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Vedic** | Soma | Pressed from the plant and consumed in ceremony |
| **Greek** | Ambrosia | Food/drink of the gods conferring immortality |
| **Christian** | Eucharist | Bread and wine as body and blood of Christ |

All three traditions may represent **the same practice**: the ritual consumption of *Amanita
muscaria* in various preparations. The remarkable versatility of the dried mushroom — it can
be eaten whole, cracked into cereal, ground into flour, steeped in water, or mixed with fruit
juice — explains how a single sacred substance could take so many different cultural forms
depending on local ingredients, climate, and culinary tradition.

What appears as many different myths and sacraments may in fact be one practice viewed through
the lens of many cultures, each adapting the preparation to their own tastes and calling it by
their own name.

## Practical Notes

### Storage and Shelf Life

Dried *Amanita muscaria* keeps extremely well. The drying process — traditionally over an
open fire or in the sun — preserves the mushroom for extended periods, allowing it to be
stored, transported, and traded across vast distances. This durability is essential for a
substance that served as the basis of religious practice across continents and millennia.

### Grinding and Extraction

Grinding the dried mushroom into flour significantly increases its surface area, which in
turn **accelerates the extraction of active compounds** into water or juice. Coarse pieces
extract more slowly; fine flour extracts quickly. The grind size can be adjusted to control
both the texture of the preparation and the speed of extraction.
