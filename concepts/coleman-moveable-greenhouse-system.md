## Moveable Greenhouse System

One of Eliot Coleman's most innovative contributions to small-scale
farming is the moveable greenhouse system. Rather than keeping a
greenhouse permanently fixed over one plot of ground, Coleman designed
structures that can be relocated along tracks, allowing multiple plots
to benefit from protected growing conditions throughout the year.

## Concept and Rationale

The fundamental insight behind moveable greenhouses is that the
greenhouse itself is a tool for [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] for starting transplants, winter for cold-hardy greens,
and late fall for [[coleman-winter-greens-nutritional-quality-cold-sweetening]]. The original site, now exposed to rain and open air,
can be amended and cover-cropped to rebuild soil health.

## Track and Rail Systems

Coleman's moveable greenhouses run on parallel steel pipes or wooden
rails set into the ground. The greenhouse base includes pipe runners
or skids that rest on the rails, allowing the entire structure to be
pushed or pulled by hand along the track. A typical setup uses two
or three positions along a single set of tracks.

The tracks are level and firmly anchored to prevent shifting. Pipe
tracks made from 2-inch galvanized steel are common because they are
smooth, durable, and inexpensive. The distance between positions is
determined by the length of the greenhouse plus an access gap for
maintenance and crop transition.

## Multi-Location Growing

A typical moveable greenhouse system serves two or three growing
locations in rotation. In a two-position system, one site houses
summer crops while the other grows winter greens. In a three-position
system, an intermediate site can be used for spring or fall
transitions, extending the utility of the protected space.

Coleman's four-season farm in Maine uses moveable tunnels extensively.
During summer, the tunnels cover heat-loving crops like tomatoes,
peppers, and eggplant. In early fall, these crops are cleared, winter
greens are transplanted into the second position, and the tunnel
slides over to provide winter protection. This rotation eliminates
the need for separate structures for each season.

## Soil Building Beneath Greenhouses

One of the most significant benefits of the moveable system is
improved soil health. Permanent greenhouses create a rain shadow that
prevents natural rainfall from reaching the soil, leading to salt
accumulation, nutrient imbalances, and soil structure degradation over
time. Irrigation alone cannot fully replicate the benefits of natural
precipitation.

When a greenhouse is moved, the soil beneath it is exposed to rain,
snow, and cover crops for months at a time. This exposure leaches
accumulated salts, allows beneficial soil organisms to recolonize,
and provides an opportunity for [[coleman-high-tunnel-cold-frame-design]] for construction
details applicable to moveable structures.

## Practical Considerations

Moving a greenhouse requires attention to weather, crop timing, and
physical logistics. The structure should be moved on a calm day to
avoid wind damage during transit. Coleman recommends scheduling moves
well in advance and preparing both the departure and destination
sites before moving.
