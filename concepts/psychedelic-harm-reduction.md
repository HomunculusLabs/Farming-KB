# Psychedelic Harm Reduction

Evidence-based safety practices for psychedelic use, including substance testing, dosage guidelines, contraindications, SSRI interactions, and trip sitting principles.

## Principles

Psychedelic harm reduction operates on the principle that people will use these substances regardless of legal status, and that providing accurate safety information reduces harm. This approach does not encourage use but acknowledges its reality and seeks to minimize negative outcomes.

## Substance Testing

### Why Test?

- Natural mushrooms can be confused with toxic lookalikes (see [[psilocybin-mushroom-identification]]).
- Synthetic substances sold as psychedelics may be adulterated or misidentified.
- Testing services such as **Erowid's DrugsData**, **DanceSafe**, **Energy Control**, and **MAPS** provide analytical testing of submitted samples.

### Testing Methods

- **Reagent testing**: Chemical reagent kits (Ehrlich, Marquis, Hofmann, Hofmann Freebase) can provide preliminary identification of compounds. Ehrlich reagent turns purple in the presence of indoles (psilocybin, LSD, DMT) but cannot distinguish between them or detect adulterants.
- **Laboratory testing**: GC/MS or HPLC analysis provides definitive identification of compounds and their concentrations. This is the gold standard but requires sending samples to a lab.
- **Spore identification**: For mushrooms, microscopic examination of spores can confirm genus and species.

## Dosage Guidelines

### Psilocybin Mushrooms

Doses vary enormously depending on species, age, preparation, and individual sensitivity. The following are approximate guidelines for dried *Psilocybe cubensis* (the most commonly available species):

| Level | Dose (dried *P. cubensis*) | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Microdose | 0.1-0.3 g | Sub-perceptual; subtle mood/cognitive enhancement |
| Light | 0.5-1.0 g | Mild visual changes, enhanced colors, light mood shift |
| Moderate | 1.0-2.5 g | Clear visual hallucinations, altered thought patterns, emotional intensity |
| Strong | 2.5-5.0 g | Intense visuals, ego dissolution possible, profound experiences |
| Heroic | 5.0+ g | Complete ego dissolution, overwhelming, for experienced users only |

**Important caveats**:
- Potency varies 2-10x between species and even within a single batch. *P. azurescens* and *P. cyanescens* are significantly more potent than *P. cubensis*.
- Start low, go slow. You can always take more, but you cannot take less.
- Empty stomach enhances absorption; full stomach delays onset and may reduce intensity.
- Lemon tek (soaking powdered mushrooms in lemon juice) accelerates onset and may increase perceived intensity.

### LSD

| Level | Dose | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | 15-25 mcg | Subtle mood/visual changes |
| Light | 25-75 mcg | Mild visuals, enhanced perception |
| Common | 75-150 mcg | Full psychedelic effects, strong visuals |
| Strong | 150-300 mcg | Intense experience, ego dissolution |
| Heavy | 300+ mcg | Overwhelming, for experienced users only |

## Critical Contraindications

### Pharmacological Contraindications

- **SSRIs and SNRIs**: Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (fluoxetine/Prozac, sertraline/Zoloft, escitalopram/Lexapro, paroxetine/Paxil, venlafaxine/Effexor, etc.) significantly **reduce or block** the effects of classical psychedelics. The SSRI "ceiling effect" means doses that would be overwhelming without the medication may produce only mild effects.
- **MAOIs**: Monoamine oxidase inhibitors combined with psychedelics can cause **serotonin syndrome** -- a potentially life-threatening condition characterized by agitation, rapid heart rate, high blood pressure, fever, seizures, and in severe cases, death. This is especially relevant for ayahuasca, which contains natural MAOIs.
- **Tricyclic antidepressants**: Can also interact dangerously with psychedelics.
- **Lithium**: Lithium combined with psychedelics has been associated with seizures and other dangerous reactions. This is a well-documented and serious contraindication.
- **Tramadol**: Lowers the seizure threshold; combining with psychedelics increases seizure risk.

### Psychological Contraindications

- **Personal or family history of schizophrenia or psychotic disorders**: Psychedelics can trigger psychotic episodes in vulnerable individuals.
- **Bipolar disorder (especially Type I)**: Psychedelics may trigger manic episodes.
- **Borderline personality disorder**: While not an absolute contraindication, individuals with BPD may be more susceptible to difficult experiences and should work with experienced guides.
- **Active suicidal ideation**: A psychedelic experience is not appropriate during acute mental health crises.
- **Recent trauma**: Processing fresh trauma in a psychedelic context without professional support can be re-traumatizing.

## Trip Sitting Principles

A trip sitter is a sober, trusted person who remains present throughout a psychedelic experience to ensure safety and provide support.

### Core Responsibilities

1. **Be sober**: No alcohol or other drugs. Full presence and judgment are essential.
2. **Create a safe environment**: Remove hazards, ensure comfortable temperature, provide water and light food.
3. **Stay calm**: The trip sitter's emotional state significantly influences the experience. Project calm confidence.
4. **Reassure without dismissing**: If the person is having a difficult experience, acknowledge their feelings while reassuring them that the experience is temporary and they are safe. "You are safe. This will pass. I am here with you."
5. **Change the setting**: If the experience becomes difficult, changing the music, lighting, or moving to a different room can shift the trajectory.
6. **Do not physically restrain** unless there is immediate physical danger.
7. **Call for medical help if needed**: Be prepared to call emergency services. Be honest with medical professionals about what substances were taken.
8. **Know the substances**: Understand what was taken, the dose, and the expected timeline.

### The Five Rs of Trip Sitting

- **Remain** present
- **Reassure** the person of their safety
- **Redirect** attention to comfortable stimuli (music, objects, breathing)
- **Relocate** if the environment is contributing to difficulty
- **Remember** that the experience is temporary

## Set and Setting

The foundational framework for harm reduction. See [[psychedelic-mushroom-set-and-setting]] for full discussion.

- **Set**: The person's mindset, expectations, intentions, emotional state, and preparation.
- **Setting**: The physical environment, social context, and the presence and demeanor of others.
- Both must be carefully considered and optimized before any psychedelic experience.

## Post-Experience Care

- **Rest**: Psychedelics can be physically and emotionally exhausting. Allow time for recovery.
- **Hydration and nutrition**: Eat lightly and stay hydrated.
- **Avoid other substances**: Do not consume alcohol or other drugs for at least 24 hours afterward.
- **Integration**: Process the experience through journaling, meditation, therapy, or conversation. See [[psychedelic-integration-practices]].
- **Grounding**: Physical activity, time in nature, and connection with trusted friends help integrate expanded states back into ordinary awareness.

## Legal Note

Psilocybin and LSD are Schedule I substances in many jurisdictions. Possession, cultivation, and distribution carry serious legal consequences. Harm reduction information is provided for educational purposes and does not constitute legal advice.
