# Mushroom Cultivation Environmental Parameters

Oyster mushroom cultivation requires careful management of environmental
parameters at each stage of the growing cycle. The two primary phases,
colonization and fruiting, have dramatically different requirements.

## Colonization Phase

During colonization, mycelium grows through the substrate digesting
nutrients and establishing the network that will support fruiting.

### Temperature

Most oyster species colonize optimally at 22-28 degrees Celsius:

- P. ostreatus: 20-25 degrees C
- P. pulmonarius: 25-30 degrees C
- P. eryngii: 18-24 degrees C
- P. djamor: 28-35 degrees C

Temperatures above 30 degrees C increase contamination risk. Below 15
degrees C, colonization slows dramatically. Consistency matters more than
peak temperature.

### Humidity

Relative humidity should be moderate (60-70%) during colonization. High
humidity encourages bacterial growth and mold on substrate surfaces. The
substrate itself provides all moisture the mycelium needs.

### Gas Exchange

Mycelium requires oxygen and produces CO2. Filter bags with 0.2-0.5 micron
filters allow gas exchange while excluding contaminants. CO2 levels should
remain below 5000 ppm. Higher concentrations slow growth and cause abnormal
morphology.

### Light

Colonization is optimal in complete or near-complete darkness. Light is not
needed and may stimulate premature pinning.

## Fruiting Phase

The transition from colonization to fruiting is triggered by environmental
changes known as fruiting triggers: fresh air exchange, high humidity,
light, and a temperature drop.

### Temperature Drop

A drop of 5-10 degrees C from colonization temperature signals favorable
conditions for reproduction. For P. ostreatus, moving from 25 degrees to
15-18 degrees C is a strong trigger.

### Fresh Air Exchange (FAE)

The most important fruiting trigger. Reducing CO2 from below 5000 ppm
(colonization) to 400-1000 ppm signals that the mycelium has reached the
substrate surface. CO2 levels above 1000 ppm cause abnormal development:

- Elongated, thick stems
- Small or absent caps
- Coral-like growth patterns

Active air exchange using fans and intake/exhaust ports is standard for
indoor grow rooms. Passive exchange through bag holes works for simpler
setups.

### Humidity

Oyster mushrooms require 85-95% relative humidity during fruiting. Below
80%, pin development is inhibited. Above 95%, condensation promotes
bacterial blotch. Humidification methods include ultrasonic foggers,
misting systems, and evaporative cool pads. Fine droplet size and even
distribution are key.

### Light

Oyster mushrooms require light for fruiting body development:

- **Intensity**: 500-1000 lux (indirect ambient daylight equivalent)
- **Duration**: 12 hours on, 12 hours off
- **Spectrum**: Blue light (450-500 nm) most effective; white light works
- **Directionality**: Even, diffuse lighting prevents crooked stems

Insufficient light causes pale, elongated stems with small caps. Excessive
direct light causes drying and heat stress.

## Pinning and Fruiting Stages

### Primordia Formation

After fruiting triggers are applied, small white knots appear and develop
into primordia over 3-7 days. This is the most sensitive stage; maintain
stable temperature, humidity above 90%, and gentle air movement. Avoid
direct misting on pins.

### Fruiting Body Development

Primordia grow into mature mushrooms over 5-14 days. Management
considerations include: harvest before spore drop, maintain consistent
humidity, ensure adequate FAE, and monitor for pests and diseases.

### Harvest Timing

Optimal harvest when the cap margin begins to flatten but before full
expansion and spore release. For P. ostreatus, harvest at 70-80% cap
expansion. Proper timing maximizes quality and subsequent flush potential.

## Grow Room Design

Effective environmental control requires proper facility design:

- **Insulation**: Minimizes temperature fluctuations, reduces energy costs
- **Airflow Design**: Even air distribution prevents high-CO2 dead zones
- **Drainage**: Substrate blocks generate condensation; floors must drain
- **Sanitary Surfaces**: Smooth, cleanable surfaces reduce contamination
- **Monitoring**: Sensors for temperature, humidity, and CO2 with data
  logging enable pattern recognition and optimization
