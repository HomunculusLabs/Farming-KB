# Entomopathogenic Fungi

Entomopathogenic fungi are those that attack and kill insects
and other arthropods. They include necrotrophic parasites that
kill their hosts rapidly and biotrophic parasites that maintain
long-term relationships with living hosts. This diverse group

conidiophores, or mummified cadavers. Insects in the
litter layer and upper soil are particularly productive
sources. Infected hosts should be collected with their
associated substrata when possible.

## Trichomycetes as Insect Associates

Trichomycetes (gut fungi) are obligate inhabitants of the
digestive tracts of arthropods. Currently about 225 species
and 55 genera are known. They are shed at each molting event
along with gut linings, requiring rapid reinfection. Their
geographic and habitat distributions depend on those of their
hosts. All species are associated with mandibulate arthropods
that are detritivores, algivores, or omnivores, but not with
predators or herbivores consuming living vascular plants.
