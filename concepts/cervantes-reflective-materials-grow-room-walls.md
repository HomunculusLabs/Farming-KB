# Reflective Materials for Grow Room Walls

Covering grow room surfaces with reflective materials is one of the
most cost-effective ways to increase light available to cannabis
plants. Jorge Cervantes evaluates common reflective materials and
explains how proper wall treatment can increase effective HID lamp
coverage by 10 to 20 percent.

## Why Reflective Walls Matter

The more reflection in a grow room, the more light energy available
to plants. Good reflective surfaces allow effective coverage of an
HID lamp to increase from 10 to 20 percent, just by applying a few
dollars worth of paint or material to the walls. This improvement
comes without any increase in electrical consumption, making it one
of the best returns on investment in any
[[cervantes-indoor-grow-room-setup]].

When setting up a grow room, Cervantes advises covering walls,
ceiling, and floor with a highly reflective material. Every surface
matters because stray light that hits a dark wall is wasted energy
that could have been redirected to the canopy.

## Flat White Paint

Flat white contains little or no light-absorbing pigment, so it
absorbs almost no light and reflects almost all light. The matte
texture provides more reflective surface area than gloss finishes.

Key points:
- Do not use glossy white paint; it contains varnish that inhibits
  reflective light
- A primer coat may be needed to prevent bleed-through of dark colors
  or stains, especially on rough or unpainted walls
- Painting is labor-intensive and messy but worth the trouble
- Install vent fans before painting; fumes are unpleasant and can
  cause health problems
- Flat white is the most economical option for large spaces

## Reflective Mylar

Reflective Mylar provides one of the most reflective surfaces
possible. It looks like a very thin mirror and reflects almost all
light. Installation is simple: tape or tack it to the wall. To
prevent rips or tears, place a piece of tape over the spot where the
staple, nail, or tack will be inserted.

Cervantes notes several important considerations:
- Position Mylar flat against the wall; when loosely affixed, light
  is poorly reflected in scattered directions
- Keep Mylar clean for maximum effectiveness
- Although more expensive than paint, Mylar is preferred by many
  growers for its superior reflectivity
- The trick is achieving a smooth, taut installation

## Foylon

Foylon is a reflective material that reflects light and heat in an
evenly dispersed pattern. It is durable and reflects about 95 percent
of the light that hits it. The material is plied with ripstop fiber
and is thick enough to act as an insulator. It is also heat and flame
resistant, making it a safer choice than some thinner materials.

Foylon's durability and dual reflective/insulating properties make
it well suited for [[cervantes-grow-room-design-advanced]] where
both light efficiency and thermal management are priorities.

## Reflective White Visqueen Plastic

Reflective white Visqueen plastic is inexpensive and protects walls
and floors while providing good light reflection. It is waterproof
and easy to clean, making it practical for grow rooms where
spills and splashes from irrigation are common. This is an excellent
budget option for growers who want more reflectivity than paint but
cannot justify the cost of Mylar or Foylon.

## Aluminum Foil: Avoid It

Aluminum foil is one of the worst possible reflective surfaces for
grow rooms. The foil always crinkles up and reflects light in the
wrong directions, actually wasting light. It also reflects more
ultraviolet rays than other surfaces, which are harmful to
chloroplasts in leaves. Despite being commonly suggested by
beginners, Cervantes strongly advises against using aluminum foil.

## Anti-Detection Barrier Foil

Specialized reflective/anti-detection barrier foil serves a dual
purpose: reflecting light inward while preventing heat signatures
from escaping. Products from suppliers like Hysupply are airtight
and infrared-proof, keeping the grow room's thermal signature from
being detected externally. This is particularly relevant for growers
concerned about [[cervantes-grow-room-security-law-enforcement]].

## Reflective Hoods and Reflectors

Beyond wall materials, the reflector hood around each lamp
dramatically affects light distribution. Horizontal reflectors
deliver many more lumens to the canopy than vertical setups. See
[[cervantes-lighting-systems]] for detailed reflector comparisons.
Adjustable parabolic reflectors (such as Adjust-a-Wings) offer
flexible "wings" that can be tuned for different growth phases,
narrowing during [[cervantes-vegetative-phase]] and widening during
[[cervantes-flowering-phase]] for final maturation.

## Application Tips

- Cover every surface: walls, ceiling, and floor
- Ensure no light is visible from outside the grow space
- Insulate windows and walls to prevent heat signature escape
- Combine reflective walls with [[cervantes-air-quality-ventilation]]
  to manage the heat reflected by some materials
- Replacing or cleaning reflective materials periodically maintains
  peak performance
