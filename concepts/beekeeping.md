# Beekeeping

Beekeeping provides pollination, honey, beeswax, and propolis for the homestead while supporting [[food-forest-design]] and garden productivity.

## Bee Species for Homesteads (Zone 7-8)

| Trait | Italian | Carniolan | Russian | Buckfast |
|-------|---------|-----------|---------|----------|
| Temperament | Gentle | Very gentle | Moderate | Gentle |
| Honey Production | High | Moderate | Moderate | High |
| Overwintering | Fair | Excellent | Excellent | Good |
| Varroa Resistance | Low | Moderate | High | Moderate |
| Spring Buildup | Fast | Very fast | Slow | Fast |
| Swarming Tendency | Moderate | High | Low | Moderate |
| Best For | Beginners, honey focus | Cold climates, small colonies | Low-maintenance, Varroa issues | Balanced production |

**Recommendation:** Carniolans for Zone 7-8 overwintering; Russians if Varroa pressure is high.

## Apiary Site Selection

- **Sun:** Southeast exposure for early morning flight; afternoon shade reduces summer stress
- **Wind protection:** Hedge, fence, or building on north/west sides
- **Water source:** Required within 0.25 miles — provide a shallow water feature with landing stones to prevent drowning
- **Forage:** Assess 2-mile radius; aim for 3+ major nectar sources per season
- **Setback:** 25+ feet from property lines; 50+ feet from neighbor high-traffic areas
- **Hive spacing:** Minimum 3 feet between hives, 4-6 feet optimal for bee flight paths

## Obtaining Bees

| Method | Timing | Cost | Pros | Cons |
|--------|--------|------|------|------|
| Package bees | April-May | $120-180 | Cheap, disease-free | Slow buildup, queen acceptance risk |
| Nuc colony | April-June | $175-250 | Established brood, faster growth | More expensive, limited availability |
| Captured swarm | April-June | Free | Local genetics | Queen quality unknown, disease risk |
| Established hive | Any | $300-500+ | Instant production | Expensive, potential issues hidden |

**Package installation:** Shake bees into brood box, suspend queen cage between frames, feed 1:1 sugar syrup continuously until drawn comb.

## Seasonal Management (Zone 7-8)

| Month | Tasks |
|-------|-------|
| Mar | First inspection (weather permitting); check stores, queen status; begin feeding if light |
| Apr | Feed heavily; reverse boxes if Langstroth; monitor swarm signs; add first super |
| May | Weekly swarm checks; split if needed; super as needed; Varroa monitoring begins |
| Jun | Peak flow management; continue supering; implement Varroa treatment if threshold exceeded |
| Jul | Harvest early flows; mid-summer dearth feeding; SHB monitoring; second Varroa treatment |
| Aug | Fall flow prep; final Varroa treatment critical; assess queen performance |
| Sep | Remove supers; feed 2:1 syrup for winter stores; reduce entrance |
| Oct | Winterize — wrap hives, windbreak check; ensure 60-80 lbs stores |
| Nov | Minimal disturbance; mouse guards on; order bees/supplies for spring |

## Honey Harvest

- **Indicators:** 80%+ capped honey in super; bees reluctant to leave during inspection
- **Extraction:** Uncap frames, spin extractor, filter through mesh; crush-and-strain for top bar
- **Yields:** First year: 0-30 lbs (often none); Established: 40-80 lbs per hive
- **Ripening:** Let honey settle 24-48 hours; bottle at 70°F+ to prevent crystallization

## Diseases and Pests (IPM Approach)

| Threat | Symptoms | Monitoring | Treatment | Prevention |
|--------|----------|------------|-----------|------------|
| Varroa destructor | Mites on bees, deformed wings | Sugar shake, alcohol wash (threshold: 3%) | Oxalic acid vapor, formic acid, Apivar | Screened bottom board, genetic selection |
| Small Hive Beetle | Larvae in comb, fermented smell | Beetle traps | Oil traps, coumaphos strips | Strong colonies, hygienic genetics |
| Wax Moths | Webbing, tunneling in weak hives | Visual inspection | Freeze frames; destroy severe cases | Maintain colony strength |
| American Foulbrood | Peppery smell, sunken cappings, ropey test | Visual, lab test | Burn infected hive (mandatory) | Sterile equipment, avoid robbing |
| European Foulbrood | Mottled brood, sour smell | Visual inspection | Antibiotics (oxytetracycline); requeen | Strong nutrition, hygienic stock |
| Nosema | Dysentery spots, reduced population | Microscopy, fecal counts | Fumagillin (if severe) | Good ventilation, winter nutrition |
| Chalkbrood | Mummified larvae at entrance | Visual inspection | Improve ventilation, requeen | Dry hive conditions |
| Bears | Destroyed hives | Tracks, damage | Electric fence | Fence installation before keeping bees |

## Homestead Integration

- **Pollination:** 2-3 hives adequately serve a 1-acre [[food-forest-design]] and [[orchard-management]]
- **Beeswax:** Render for candles, salves, beeswax wraps ([[food-preservation]])
- **Propolis:** Tincture for immune support (see [[herbal-medicine]])
- **Honey medicine:** Wound dressing (antimicrobial), cough suppression, local allergy desensitization
- **Forage plants:** Clover, buckwheat, aster, goldenrod, basswood, fruit tree bloom; integrate with [[cover-cropping]]
- **Livestock proximity:** Keep hives 20+ feet from [[duck-keeping]] and [[goat-keeping]] areas; poultry can disturb hives but don't generally predate bees
- **Regenerative role:** Hive products feed [[regenerative-agriculture]] cycles; wax and propolis are valuable homestead outputs

## Regulations

- **Registration:** Most states require apiary registration ($5-25/year); check state apiary inspector program
- **Selling honey:** Cottage food laws typically allow raw honey sales without commercial kitchen; labeling requirements vary by state
- **Neighbor notification:** Not universally required but recommended; offer honey to build goodwill
- **Local ordinances:** Check zoning for beekeeping allowances; common requirements include setback distances, water provision, hive limits, and flyway barriers

Coordinate beekeeping activities with [[seasonal-planning]] and [[homesteading-infrastructure]] development for integrated property management.
