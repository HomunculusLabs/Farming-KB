# Pest Management (Organic)

Source: Steve Solomon, "Gardening When It Counts" (2005)

## Solomon's Pest Philosophy: Questioning the Doctrine

The [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] movement has long promoted the idea that properly balanced, organically grown plants are resistant to insects and disease. Solomon tested this claim through years of observation at Territorial Seed Company and in his own gardens. His conclusion: it is largely a myth.

Well-fertilized plants with balanced minerals do grow more vigorously and can tolerate some pest damage, but they are not immune. [[solomon-insects-and-diseases-integrated-approach]] will attack any plant they can reach. The "resistant through organic methods" doctrine is dangerous because it causes gardeners to be unprepared, losing crops when simple, timely interventions would have saved them.

Exceptions exist: some insect damage is purely cosmetic and does not affect yield. But much pest damage is genuinely destructive.

## The Hierarchy of Pest Responses

Solomon advocates a pragmatic, escalating approach:

1. **Prevention through cultural practices**: rotation, variety selection, proper spacing, clean cultivation
2. **Mechanical controls**: handpicking, row covers, trap crops
3. **Biological controls**: Bt, [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]], nematodes
4. **Least-toxic sprays**: insecticidal soap, pyrethrum/rotenone, neem
5. **Stronger measures**: only when absolutely necessary

## Row Covers (Spun Fabric)

One of the most effective organic pest controls. Floating row covers of spun-bonded polypropylene exclude flying insects while allowing light, air, and water through. Particularly effective for:

- **Cucumber beetles**: cover hills until plants begin blooming, then remove for pollinators
- **Root maggots**: cover brassica seedlings from sowing until stems thicken
- **Flea beetles**: protect vulnerable seedlings in early spring
- **Colorado potato beetles**: on new ground, covers can exclude emerging adults

Key: covers must be carefully anchored at all edges with soil. Any gap allows entry.

## Key Pests and Controls

### Aphids

Usually worst in early spring before predator populations build. Control:
- Hose spray to knock them off leaves
- Insecticidal soap (Safer's brand is effective, test on spinach first)
- Pyrethrum/rotenone combination (kills beneficials too, use sparingly)
- Neem spray (long-lasting natural remedy)
- Avoid excessive nitrogen that produces soft, susceptible growth

### Cabbageworms and Cabbage Loopers

Two species: large green larvae of white butterflies (day-flying) and smaller larvae of brown moths (night-flying). Both can defoliate brassicas rapidly.

- **Handpicking**: effective in small gardens
- **Bt ([[cervantes-bacillus-thuringiensis-bt-control]])**: the most effective control. Marketed as Dipel. Harmless to mammals, bees, and most beneficials. Lethal only to caterpillars.
- Spray Bt every few days during peak season. Add 1/4 teaspoon dishwashing liquid per quart as a spreader-sticker.
- Spray undersides of leaves as much or more than tops.
- Bt remains active on leaves about one week; even a single spray maintains infection in the garden population.

### Colorado Potato Beetle

Devastating in [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America. Adults overwinter 12-18 inches deep near potato plots and emerge in late spring.

- **New ground**: fewer beetles emerge; row covers effective
- **Straw mulch** (wheat or rye): provides habitat for ground predators that eat beetle eggs and larvae (but hilling for potato production conflicts with mulching)
- **Handpicking adults** when they first emerge, before egg-laying
- **Rotenone/pyrethrum**: effective for 1-2 days, can be repeated
- **Bt 'San Diego' strain**: specific to Colorado potato beetle larvae
- **Flamers**: propane torches that burn beetles and eggs on contact

### Cucumber Beetles (Striped and Spotted)

Attack seedlings of all cucurbits; adults transmit a virus wilt that kills seedlings.

- **Delayed planting**: sow 2-4 weeks later than usual; overwintering beetles have less impact
- **Overplant and thin**: sow 4 seeds per hill, thin to 2 after true leaves appear
- **Row covers** until flowering (remove for bee pollination)
- **Reseeding**: if seedlings die from predation or virus, sow replacements
- Avoid excessive soil nutrients that make seedlings more succulent
- [[ingham-beneficial-nematodes-soil-food-web]] can control soil-dwelling larvae

### Corn Earworm

Moth lays eggs on corn silks; larvae follow silks into the ear. Control:
- **Bt on silks**: after pollination finishes, daub double-strength Bt (with spreader-sticker) on silks of each ear using a soft brush
- Alternatively, blend of dormant oil + water + Bt applied to silks

### Flea Beetles

Tiny black hopping insects that chew pinholes in brassica leaves. Most problematic in early spring when plants grow slowly and garden is bare.

- **Direct sow heavily** (5 seeds per station) and thin only as competition begins
- **Husky transplants** outgrow damage
- **Delay sowing**: avoid the earliest possible moment
- **Foliar feeding** with fish emulsion if damage exceeds 10% leaf area
- **Rotenone or pyrethrum** if damage exceeds 20%
- Problem usually resolves when weather warms and plants grow faster

### Root Maggots (Cabbage and Carrot Flies)

Major problem in Cascadia and the UK. Cabbage fly waits until brassica stems reach ~6mm diameter before laying eggs. Larvae burrow down and destroy root systems.

- **Row covers**: the most reliable defense for direct-seeded brassicas
- **Timing**: avoid spring sowing; mid-May through July avoids peak spring populations in North America
- **Varietal resistance**: some brassicas tolerate maggots better than others; rutabaga's thick skin resists damage
- **Chinese cabbage**: highly susceptible; row covers essential

## Disease Prevention

Solomon emphasizes cultural practices over sprays for disease:
- **Rotation**: 3-4 year rotation for all crops; essential for potatoes (soil-borne diseases)
- **Air circulation**: wider spacing reduces humidity around plants
- **Clean up**: remove all crop debris after harvest; compost or burn diseased material
- **Variety selection**: choose disease-resistant varieties; avoid heirlooms that have lost resistance
- **Avoid overhead watering** late in the day; wet foliage overnight promotes fungal disease

## Beneficial Insects and Habitat

Solomon acknowledges the value of beneficial insects but is pragmatic. Creating habitat around the vegetable garden helps maintain predator populations. However, relying solely on biological control without intervention often means accepting significant crop losses.
