# Methylophilus — Methylotrophic Bacteria in Cannabis Endorhiza

## Overview

*Methylophilus* emerged as one of the most striking findings in the Winston et al. (2014) [[winston-cannabis-microbiome-study-design]]. This genus of methylotrophic bacteria showed extreme cultivar-specific enrichment in the endorhiza of Cannabis, comprising 13% of the entire endorhiza microbial community in Bookoo Kush while being virtually absent from Burmese (0.13%) and completely undetectable in Sour Diesel. This dramatic strain-level difference was the single most statistically significant OTU abundance difference between Cannabis cultivars (FDR-corrected p = 0.012).

## Taxonomy and Biology of Methylophilus

### Classification

- **Phylum:** Proteobacteria
- **Class:** Betaproteobacteria
- **Order:** Methylophilales
- **Family:** Methylophilaceae

*Methylophilus* belongs to a specialized group of bacteria known as methylotrophs — organisms capable of utilizing one-carbon (C1) compounds such as methanol as their sole carbon and energy source. This metabolic capability distinguishes them from the vast majority of environmental bacteria that require multi-carbon substrates.

### Metabolic Capabilities

Methylotrophs like *Methylophilus* oxidize methanol to formaldehyde using methanol dehydrogenase enzymes, then assimilate formaldehyde carbon via the ribulose monophosphate (RuMP) or serine pathways. Methanol in soil environments primarily originates from:

- **Pectin demethylation** during plant cell wall turnover
- **Methane oxidation** by methanotrophs in the rhizosphere
- **Lignin degradation** products from organic matter decomposition
- **Root exudates** containing methylated compounds

### Ecological Niche

*Methylophilus* species are commonly found in:

- Freshwater and soil environments with active organic matter cycling
- The rhizosphere of plants producing methanol-rich exudates
- [[constructed-wetland-design]] systems with high C1 compound loads
- Phyllosphere (leaf surface) communities exposed to atmospheric methanol

## Strain-Specific Enrichment in Cannabis

### Distribution Across Cultivars

| Cultivar | Relative Abundance | Presence |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| Bookoo Kush | 13.0% | Dominant member |
| Burmese | 0.13% | Rare |
| Sour Diesel | 0% | Absent |
| White Widow | Not dominant | Present at low levels |
| Maui Wowie | Not dominant | Present at low levels |

This 100-fold difference between Bookoo Kush and Burmese, and complete absence in Sour Diesel, represents one of the most dramatic examples of cultivar-specific bacterial enrichment documented in any plant microbiome study at the time.

### Statistical Significance

The *Methylophilus* OTU was identified as the single most significant contributor to cultivar-level beta-diversity differences in the endorhiza [[methylophilus-cannabis-endorhiza]] communities of the first experiment (Winston et al., 2014). The FDR-corrected p-value of 0.012 survived the stringent multiple-comparison corrections applied across hundreds of OTUs.

### Compartment Specificity

The *Methylophilus* enrichment was specific to the endorhiza compartment — it was not observed as a dominant taxon in the rhizosphere or bulk soil samples from any cultivar. This compartment specificity is consistent with the two-tier selection model where host genotype-dependent filtering primarily acts on communities within plant tissues rather than in the surrounding soil.

## Possible Mechanisms of Cultivar-Specific Enrichment

### Root Exudate Hypothesis

Different Cannabis cultivars produce distinct profiles of root exudates, which serve as the primary carbon source for rhizosphere and [[endorhiza-endophytic-bacteria]]. If Bookoo Kush roots exude higher concentrations of methylated compounds (methanol, methylamines, or methylated phenolics), this could selectively favor *Methylophilus* colonization. Cannabis is known to produce numerous methylated [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]], and cultivar-specific terpenoid and cannabinoid profiles could extend to root exudate chemistry.

### Niche Availability Hypothesis

*Methylophilus* may occupy a specific metabolic niche in the Bookoo Kush endorhiza that is already filled by other taxa in Burmese and Sour Diesel. The 13% community share in Bookoo Kush suggests either abundant substrate availability or a competitive advantage in that particular root environment. In other cultivars, [[functional-redundancy-fungal-communities]] may mean other bacteria fulfill the same ecological role.

### Host Immune Selectivity Hypothesis

Plant immune responses, which differ between cultivars, could differentially tolerate or reject *Methylophilus* colonization. If Bookoo Kush has a less aggressive immune response against this genus (or actively recruits it), this could explain the enrichment pattern.

## Relationship to the Two-Tier Selection Model

The *Methylophilus* finding provides strong support for the second tier of the two-tier selection model:

1. **First tier (soil filtering):** *Methylophilus* was present at low levels in the bulk soil — it passed the initial soil-derived filter into the rhizosphere
2. **Second tier (host genotype selection):** Only within the Bookoo Kush endorhiza did *Methylophilus* undergo dramatic population expansion, suggesting that host-specific factors in Bookoo Kush created conditions favorable for its proliferation

This pattern — presence in soil but cultivar-specific amplification in the endorhiza — exemplifies how the two-tier model predicts [[biodiversity-fungal-community-assembly]] in plant roots.
