# Screen of Green (SCROG)

Screen of Green (SCROG) is a plant training technique that uses a horizontal screen, trellis, or net to create an even canopy of bud sites. ## Bending to Fill the Screen

Cervantes provides detailed guidance on bending, which is central to SCROG training. Bending alters the flow of growth hormones in the plant. When a growing tip is bent horizontally, the growth-inhibiting hormone (auxin) is partially neutralized, encouraging lateral bud development. Bending is much easier on plants than pruning -- "it is easy to prune too much, but it is hard to over bend."

To bend a branch, lean it in the desired direction and tie it in place. Branches can take significant bending before they fold. Even if a branch folds, it can be tied in place and will often heal. Young, supple branches take bending much better than old, stiff ones. Bending branches horizontally encourages buds to grow vertically toward the light.

## Harvesting from SCROG

SCROG plants are typically harvested all at once, unlike the staggered harvest of SOG. However, some growers with multiple screens or screen sections can stagger flowering for more frequent harvests. The even canopy makes harvesting straightforward -- all colas are at a similar height and maturity.

## Screen Height Considerations

The optimal screen height depends on several factors:

- Available vertical space between the growing medium and the lamp
- Expected stretch during flowering (indicas stretch less than sativas)
- Container height (taller containers mean less room for screen-to-lamp distance)
- Lamp heat output (needs more clearance than cooler LEDs)

A common guideline is to set the screen at about one-third of the total available height. If there are 36 inches between the container top and the lamp, the screen should be about 12 inches above the container. This leaves room for vertical bud growth above the screen while maintaining adequate lamp clearance.

## Vegetative Time Requirements

SCROG requires more vegetative time than SOG because branches must grow enough to fill the screen before flowering. Typical timelines:

- 1-4 plants in a 3x3 foot area: 4-6 weeks vegetative
- 4-8 plants in a 4x4 foot area: 3-5 weeks vegetative
- 8+ plants in larger areas: 2-4 weeks vegetative

The screen should be 70-80 percent filled before switching to flowering. The remaining 20-30 percent will fill in during the early stretching phase of flowering.
