## Overview

All flows in nature pulse \u2014 whether blood, wind, water, lava, or traffic. This fundamental pulsation is organized by **pulsers**, which are regulatory mechanisms that start, run for a preset time, and stop. ## What Limits Size and Growth?

Every living event carries its characteristic time-shape memories encoded in its cells. Rivers, volcanoes, and the sun itself exhibit similar patterned behavior \u2014 the sun "pulses" every 11 years, affecting ozone and climatic factors such as rainfall. Our own bodies have characteristic or normal resting rates for pulse, peristalsis, and visceral movements.

All mammals have an allotted number of heartbeats in relation to their body size. When these are exhausted, the organism dies. This principle extends beyond heartbeat \u2014 the timing mechanisms that govern growth, reproduction, and senescence are built into every organism.

## Discharge Phenomena

Not only expansion, but **discharge phenomena** such as lightning (or sudden shock in people) can:

- Disrupt or trigger initiatory reactions in life rhythms
- Introduce irregularities in cycles or pulsers
- Cause expansion or shock that introduces irregularity in fixed forms

This raises the question of whether disturbances produced by shock or sudden stimulus are responsible for expansions, cyclic changes, or shape deformations on a more general scale. For designers, this means that sudden changes \u2014 whether planned interventions or environmental disturbances \u2014 can reset or disrupt the timing systems of organisms and ecosystems.

## Shapers and Timers

Species and individual organisms need both **shapers** (DNA) and **timers** (biological clocks) to achieve specific size and shape. The two must work synchronously:

- **Shapers** encode the spatial pattern \u2014 branching angles, placement of main branches, room for sideshoots and forks, but not for interweaves or cross-points that damage function.
- **Timers** control when growth starts, continues, and stops \u2014 determining that a toe will stop at being a toe and not grow into a monstrous appendage.
- **Synchronization** between shapers and timers achieves correct proportions of parts such as fingers and toes.

### Branching Patterns

Branching patterns in bodies must have already-encoded correct angles and placements for main branches, leaving room for sideshoots and forks but not for interweaves or cross-points which damage the function of the organism. To generate the surface or boundary of a person and their reticulation systems, patterns of incredible complexity and strict limits must be "known" by the cells or cell organizers.

### Multi-Species Organisms

Humans are part of a **guild of species** that lie within and without our bodies. Aboriginal peoples and the Ayurvedic practitioners of ancient India have names for such guilds \u2014 beings made up (as we are) of two or more species forming one organism. Most of nature is composed of groups of species working interdependently, and this complexity too must have its synchronistic regulators.
