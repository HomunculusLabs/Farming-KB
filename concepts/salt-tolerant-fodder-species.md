# Salt Tolerant Fodder Species

Saline and waterlogged land presents significant challenges for
agriculture, but many useful fodder species can be established on
affected areas. By maintaining a cover of salt-tolerant plants, farmers
can reduce evaporation, decrease soil erosion, and lower water tables
through increased transpiration.
## Establishment

Salt-affected land should be fenced from stock during establishment.
Plant into mounds 200-300 mm above ground level (created by grader
blade), following contours where possible. As fodder species establish,
stock can be periodically turned onto these areas.

## Benefits of Saltland Pasture

Maintaining plant cover on salt-affected land provides three key
benefits: reduced evaporation (shading from sun and wind), reduced
erosion (cover prevents soil loss), and lowered water tables through
increased transpiration.

## Grasses for Salt Fodder

### Puccinellia ciliata / P. capillaris (Puccinellia)

A perennial, summer-dormant grass forming tussocks 10-70 cm high.
Nutritionally equivalent to annual rye grass. Suited to areas with 350
mm or more rainfall and tolerant of winter waterlogging.

### Paspalum distichum (Salt Water Couch)

Suited to summer-damp bogs, gullies, and seepage areas. Tolerant of
water containing up to 14,000 ppm total soluble salts. Produces
valuable winter feed in summer months.

### Oryzopsis miliacea (Smilo / Millet Grass)

A perennial, clumped grass to about 1 m, native to the Mediterranean.
Tolerates shallow and calcareous soils, grows in rainfalls as low as
100-150 mm. Yields may reach 12 tonnes dry matter/ha/year (protein
12-15%, digestibility 65%). Sown pastures observed to last 15+ years.

### Festuca elatior (Tall Fescue)

A summer-dormant, tufted perennial extremely resistant to water stress.
Requires minimum 350-400 mm rainfall, withstands high salinity. Under
irrigation with 4000-5000 ppm salts, yields 8-10 tonnes dry matter/ha.
Warning: can cause lameness (fescue foot) in cattle if over-fed.

### Agropyron elongatum (Tall Wheat Grass)

A perennial native to southern Russia and Asia Minor, growing in
seashore marshes. Well adapted to poorly drained saline soils. Best
growth where soil dries out in summer. Direct sown from seed.

### Arundo donax (Giant Reed)

Often wrongly called a bamboo, this vigorous perennial has thick
underground stems. Native to the Mediterranean, survives in inundated,
saline water courses. Has colonised many damp areas in Australia.

## Companion Legumes

No pasture treatment is complete without accompanying legumes to fix
nitrogen into the soil.

### Trifolium fragiferum (Strawberry Clover)

A creeping perennial adapted to heavy, poorly drained, often saline
and alkaline soils. Summer dormant, withstands long drought periods.
Very salt tolerant, native to north Africa, requires 300+ mm rainfall.

### Hedysarum spp (Sulla)

- **H. carnosum** (Fleshy Sulla): Biennial from Libya/Tunisia/Algeria,
  100-350 mm rainfall, tolerates irrigation with 8000-10000 ppm salt
- **H. coronarium** (Sulla): Biennial from north Africa and southern
  Italy, yields over 20 tonnes dry matter/ha/year recorded, potential
  as [[acacia]] spp

Many wattle species tolerate saline conditions including A. ampliceps,
A. auriculiformis, A. cyclops, A. ligulata, A. pendula, A. saligna,
and A. salicina. See mycofiltration-silt-erosion-control and
  furniture timber
- **Colophospermum mopane** (Mopane): Leguminous tree to 23 m, native
  to Africa, best firewood on the continent, coppices vigorously
