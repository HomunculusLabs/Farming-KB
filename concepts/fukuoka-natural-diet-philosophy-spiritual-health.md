## Fukuoka Natural Diet Philosophy and Spiritual Health

## Overview

In the final chapters of The Natural Way of Farming, Masanobu Fukuoka extended his philosophy beyond agriculture into the realm of human diet and health. He argued that [[cho-brown-rice-vinegar-preparation]] proponents** claim its merits while scientists proclaim the benefits of white rice.
- **Cooking enthusiasts** argue that cooking enriches the human diet, while opponents say it creates sick people.
- **Water debates**: some claim fresh water is beneficial, others harmful.
- **Salt controversies**: some acclaim salt as invaluable while others attribute a range of diseases to excessive intake.
- **Fruit positions**: one camp sees fruits as yin food fitting for monkeys but not humans, while another contends fruit and vegetables are the best foods for health and longevity.

Fukuoka argued that given the right circumstances, any one of these views is correct. Nature is a fluid entity that changes from moment to moment. People become perplexed when bound by theories that try to freeze a fluid nature. One misses the mark by relying on something that is unreliable. Right and left do not exist in nature; there is no happy medium, no good and evil, no yin or yang.

## The Centrifugal and Centripetal Diet

Fukuoka applied his spatial model of agriculture to diet. He distinguished between two dietary directions:

- **Centrifugal diet**: Eating outwardly, expanding the range of foods consumed, pursuing flavor and novelty. This is the diet of the city dweller who eats foods from distant lands, follows fashion, and uses the intellect to choose foods.
- **Centripetal diet**: Eating inwardly, contracting the range of foods consumed, returning to local and seasonal foods. This is the diet of the farmer who eats what grows nearby, follows the seasons, and receives food with gratitude.

Fukuoka drew food mandate diagrams, akin to compasses, that could guide a person toward either direction depending on their circumstances and degree of sickness or health. But he insisted that once these mandates have been examined, they should be discarded. People should not eat on the basis of human intellect and action, but should merely receive with gratitude the food that grows in nature.

## The Diet of the Sick

Fukuoka proposed a provocative thesis: illness begins when man moves away from nature, and the severity of affliction is proportional to the degree of estrangement. This means that if a sick person returns to nature, he is cured. As mankind distances itself from nature, the number of sick people rises rapidly.

However, he distinguished between true healing and superficial dietary fixes:

- **Natural food without a [[fukuoka-natural-foods-movement-america]]**: The materials for a natural diet do not exist in the modern food system.
- **Complex natural diets defeat their purpose**: The very complexity of eating a natural diet in an urban environment would drive people further from nature rather than closer to it.
- **There is no universal standardized natural diet**: To push upon people living in different environments and of different races and temperaments a rigid, standardized natural diet is an impossibility.
