## Aeroponics for Cannabis

Aeroponics is the most advanced and highest-performing [[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]]. Aeroponic systems use no growing medium at all, instead suspending plant roots in a dark growth chamber where they are misted with oxygen-rich [[query-what-is-light-deprivation-cannabis-growing-and-how-does-it-work]] Mediums and suspended from the top of the system. The net pots anchor the plants while allowing roots to extend into the misting chamber below.

## Root Environment

The humidity in the growth chamber remains at or near 100 percent, 24 hours a day. Roots have the maximum potential to absorb nutrients in the presence of abundant air. This combination of constant moisture and high oxygen availability is what gives aeroponics its superior performance over other hydroponic-cannabis|hydroponic methods.

Roots develop exceptionally quickly in aeroponic systems. Long, dense root systems form rapidly on cuttings and seedlings, translating to vigorous above-ground growth.

## Component Quality

Because aeroponic systems are sensitive and unforgiving, it is important to purchase quality components or a ready-made system from a qualified supplier. Cheap pumps, nozzles, and timers are more likely to fail and can result in total crop loss.

## Nutrient Solution Management

Aeroponic nutrient solutions must be carefully maintained:
- **pH**: Maintain between 5.5 and 6.0. Fluctuations affect plants faster than in media-based systems.
- **EC/TDS**: Monitor carefully and adjust based on growth stage.
- **Filtration**: Use inline filters to prevent nozzle clogging.
- **Solution changes**: Change nutrient solution every one to two weeks.

## Maintenance Requirements

- Check nozzle operation daily to ensure proper misting.
- Inspect the root chamber for signs of drying or disease.
- Clean and maintain the pump and filtration system regularly.
- Monitor pH, EC, and reservoir levels at least daily.
- Keep the root chamber completely light-proof to prevent algae growth.
- Flush and clean the entire system between crops.
- Replace spray nozzles at the first sign of reduced output or uneven spray patterns.
- Maintain a log of pH, EC, and maintenance activities for troubleshooting.

## Troubleshooting

- **Dry roots**: Misting system failure. Check pump, nozzles, and connections immediately.
- **Root discoloration**: May indicate nutrient imbalance or pathogen. Check pH, EC, and solution clarity.
- **Reduced growth**: Often caused by clogged nozzles reducing misting coverage. Clean or replace nozzles.
- **Algae in chamber**: Light leak. Seal all openings and check for cracks or gaps in the chamber.
