# Cola Development and Bud Architecture

The cola is the large central bud cluster at the top of a cannabis plant,
and understanding how colas develop and how the plant's bud architecture
is organized is essential for maximizing yield. [[greg-green]]'s "The
Cannabis Grow Bible" describes the cola as the prime target of advanced
growing methods like SOG and ScrOG, which are designed specifically to
produce seriously large top colas. Bud architecture determines how
growers should manage each section of the plant for optimal results.

## The Three Plant Sections

From its vegetative cycle through flowering, the cannabis plant develops
in three most noticeable sections:

### Top Cola (Main Cola)

The top cola develops a large amount of bud and is generally the most
flower-dense part of the plant. It receives the most direct light and
produces the largest single mass of bud. The top cola is the primary
target of [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-sog-scrog-advanced-methods]] methods, which concentrate on making this part grow really big.

### Mid-Section

The mid-section contains buds and stems along with old and new leaves.
Buds grow in between the nodes near the stem. Although these mid-section
buds are generally smaller than the main cola, they should be just as
potent. This section benefits greatly from
[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-plant-training-pruning-and-yield]] techniques that open up the canopy to more light.

### Base Section (Bottom Section)

The bottom section contains the large fan leaves and a small amount of
bud. Towards the bottom of the plant, very small leaves that are about
to fall away may appear yellow. This section receives the least light
and contributes the least to overall yield in most indoor setups.

## Fan Leaf Management

Fan leaves are critical to bud development and should be left alone
whenever possible. Greg Green emphasizes several important points:

- Fan leaves produce sugar that is used in bud production.
- Cutting away fan leaves may cause bud growth to stunt, resulting in
  a smaller harvest.
- The only times a fan leaf should be removed are when it is dying,
  badly burnt, or covering a large bud mass.

### When to Remove Fan Leaves

In a [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-sog-scrog-advanced-methods]]
setup, a fan leaf may be removed if it is covering the bud mass of
another plant. Light is probably the most important factor in bud
development, and if a fan leaf is preventing light from reaching the
bud, removal may be justified.

Dead leaves from the bottom of the plant should always be removed and
discarded. They should never be left on the soil as fertilizer because
they attract unwanted [[green-cannabis-pests-prevention]].

## Cola Formation Through Flowering

During flowering, the cannabis plant takes on a Christmas tree shape as
it fills out. The process unfolds as follows:

1. White hairs (pistils) appear at the internodes and top cola.
2. Pistils curl slightly and grow longer and thicker.
3. The top cola carries the most pistils, which become sticky with
   resin.
4. During peak flowering, the female pistils on the flower tips swell
   up.
5. Pistils change color from white through orange/red to brown,
   signaling harvest readiness.

The Christmas tree shape means that the top cola receives the most
direct light, the mid-section gets partial light, and the bottom section
gets the least. This natural light gradient is the reason why advanced
growing methods focus on creating even canopies where all bud sites
receive similar light intensity.

## Advanced Methods for Cola Production

### SOG ([[sea-of-green]])

SOG is designed to maximize top cola production by growing many small
plants close together, each producing a single large cola. The method
eliminates the lower, less productive sections of the plant and
concentrates all energy into the top bud.

### ScrOG ([[screen-of-green]])

ScrOG uses a screen to train plants into an even canopy, producing very
even colas across the entire grow area. The top colas and side branches
are all trained to grow through the screen at the same height, ensuring
uniform light distribution.

### Cabinet Growing

Cabinet growing adapts these principles to confined spaces, using
training and pruning to keep plants short while maximizing cola
production within the limited vertical space.

## Single Cola vs Multiple Colas

A natural, untrained plant typically produces one dominant main cola at
the top. Through [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-plant-training-pruning-and-yield]], growers can create plants with multiple top colas:

- Two colas: Achieved by a single topping cut.
- Four colas: Achieved by topping both secondary stems.
- Six or more colas: Possible with repeated topping on responsive
  strains.

However, Greg Green notes that the total bud volume is constrained by
the strain's genetic threshold. Multiple colas share the same total bud
volume that a single cola would produce on some strains, while on other
strains the total can genuinely increase.

## Bud Quality Across Sections

While the main cola produces the largest bud mass, potency is not
limited to the top. Greg Green states that mid-section buds "should be
just as potent" as the main cola, even though they are smaller in size.
This is important for growers using whole-plant harvesting methods --
the smaller buds from the middle and lower sections still deliver
quality medicine.
