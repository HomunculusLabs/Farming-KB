# Cold and Montane Deserts

Cold and montane deserts occupy the arid plateau and high valley interiors of continents. They present unique challenges for human habitation and agriculture due to their extreme temperature fluctuations, intense [[albedo-and-solar-radiation]], and very short [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]]. Despite the harsh conditions, these environments support specialised plant and animal communities and offer opportunities for [[water-management]] strategy:

- **[[swale]] for snow-melt** — swales positioned to capture and infiltrate snowmelt water
- **Grow trees as snow traps** — trees planted in or near swales catch and hold snow, slowing sublimation and allowing meltwater to infiltrate
- **Fences** help retain snow for meltwater in streams, serving as low-cost snow fences

#### General Water Principles

- Damp stream areas grow good tree crops
- Water storage in protected locations is essential
- Rock and stone can help moderate soil temperatures and retain moisture

### Shelter and Housing

Very solid houses are essential for cold desert living:

- **Trombe walls** — massive thermal walls that absorb heat during the day and release it at night
- **Well-sealed double entries** — airlock-style entries prevent heat loss
- **Glazing over massive internal courtyards** — creates protected growing spaces
- **Earth-sheltering** — bermed or underground construction provides [[comparison-windbreak-vs-thermal-mass]] and [[cervantes-wind-protection-outdoor-cannabis]]
- **Massive fire-flue walls** — masonry chimney systems that store and radiate heat
- **Efficient stick-wood cookers** — for fuel economy in regions where all plants grow slowly

### Cultivation Strategies

#### Appropriate Plant Species

- **Trees**: Willows, birch, poplars, and junipers — fast-growing or hardy species suited to cold
- **Summer crops**: Tuberous summer plants and fast-growing annuals
- **Hardy grains**: Can be grown on small fields
- **Root crops**: For winter storage, providing food security through the cold season

#### Soil and Site Management

- **Rocky soils** are good growing sites — rocks help preserve ground warmth and moisture
- **Stone piles** and **mulch** preserve a moderate air layer over plant roots, insulating against extreme temperature swings
- Small, protected field sites are more productive than large exposed areas

### Clothing and Personal Protection

Cold desert conditions demand specific protective clothing:

- **Dry, airy, absorbent linings** — for [[mushroom-bed-moisture-management]] during activity
- **Wind-excluding waterproof exteriors** — furs worn inside out for optimal wind protection
- **Radiation burn protection** — visors or broad hats for sun protection
- **Eye protection** — dark glasses or snow goggles essential for the intense reflected light

## Fuel Considerations

Fuel is a special problem in cold montane deserts because **all plants grow relatively slowly**. This constraint affects every aspect of design:

- Efficient heating systems (Trombe walls, thermal mass) reduce fuel demand
- Fast-growing tree species (willows, poplars) should be prioritised for firewood plantations
- Stick-wood cookers and efficient combustion systems stretch limited fuel supplies
- Earth-sheltered construction reduces heating requirements significantly

## Comparison with Hot Deserts

| Feature | Hot Deserts | Cold Montane Deserts |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|
| Succulents | Common | Very rare (cold ruptures cells) |
| Deciduous pattern | Dry-deciduous | Winter-deciduous |
| Evergreens | Acacias | Conifers |
| Growing season | Months | Days (40–70) |
| Primary hardiness | Drought | Cold (equal or greater than drought) |
| Bird dispersal | Seed-eating | Berry-eating |
| Animal migration | Horizontal (to rain) | Vertical (altitudinal) |
| Sugar storage | In pods | In saps |
| Snow sublimation | Not a factor | Major water loss pathway |

## Key Design Principles

1. **Retain snow** — this is the primary water management challenge
2. **Insulate** — both plants and animals need protection from extreme diurnal temperature swings
3. **Think small** — small protected fields, small animal herds, efficient housing
