## Project MKULTRA and the CIA

The Central Intelligence Agency's interest in psychedelic compounds was codified in Project MKULTRA, a vast and largely illegal program of mind control research that operated from 1953 until it was officially halted in 1973.

MKULTRA encompassed over 150 subprojects involving universities, research institutions, hospitals, and prisons across the United States and Canada.

The program was motivated by fears that the Soviet Union and China had made advances in mind control and brainwashing techniques.

LSD was one of the primary compounds investigated under MKULTRA, along with mescaline, heroin, barbiturates, and various other substances.

CIA operatives conducted experiments on unwitting subjects, including government employees, military personnel, and members of the general public.

## Ethical Violations and Accountability

The full scope of the ethical violations committed under these programs did not become public until the 1975 Church Committee hearings in the U.S. Senate.

The hearings revealed that the CIA had conducted experiments on unwitting subjects, had destroyed most of its records in 1973, and had obstructed investigations into its activities.

The destruction of records meant that the full scope of the program may never be known.

Several victims of MKULTRA experiments later sued the government and received financial settlements, though many others were never identified or compensated.

The scandal led to significant reforms in the ethics of human subjects research, including strengthened requirements for informed consent.
