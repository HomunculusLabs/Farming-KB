# Psilocybin and the Mystical Experience

The mystical experience induced by psilocybin is the central phenomenon in Powell's *The
[[powell-psilocybin-solution-ecological-cognition-theory]]*. He treats it not as a curiosity or aberration but as the primary evidence
for his thesis that consciousness is mutable, that transcendental knowledge is accessible, and
that [[entheogenic-fungi-history]] are key to understanding both mind and reality.

## The Good Friday Experiment

The legendary Good Friday experiment of 1962 (the "miracle at [[good-friday-experiment-marsh-chapel]]") is Powell's
flagship case study. [[walter-pahnke]], working for his PhD with Leary and the [[leary-harvard-psilocybin-project]]
Project, arranged for twenty theology students to receive either psilocybin or a placebo in a
double-blind study within Boston University chapel.

Leary recalls that the ten psilocybin subjects began to act unconventionally: some wandered
around murmuring prayer, one lay on the floor, some lounged on benches, and another played
strange music on the chapel organ. Analysis of 147-item psychological questionnaires completed
by subjects was then rated by naive markers who compared psilocybin phenomenology with mystical
phenomenology from religious scriptures, without knowing which was which.

Incredibly, the results showed that the psilocybin group had mystical religious experiences
indistinguishable from those reported in religious literature. A naturally occurring substance
had been shown capable of generating a full-blown mystical experience within religiously
receptive minds. Traditionally cherished beliefs about mystical enlightenment and the religious
impulse were being threatened by a drug.

## The 25-Year Follow-Up

Powell documents Rick Doblin's follow-up study conducted in the late 1980s, by which time many
subjects were practising reverends. The psilocybin group's characterisation of their mystical
experiences had actually strengthened over 25 years. While control subjects who received placebo
could barely remember the day, psilocybin subjects retained clear memories.

Reverend K.B. remembered: "It left me with a completely unquestioned certainty that there is an
environment bigger than I am conscious of. I have my own interpretation of what that is, but it
went from a theoretical proposition to an experiential one."

Reverend Y.M. recalled: "I closed my eyes and the visuals were back.....it was as if I was in
an ocean of bands, streams of colour, streaming past me. The colours were brilliant and I could
swim down any one of these colours."

Doblin concluded that "all of the psilocybin subjects still considered that their original
experiences contained genuinely mystical elements and that psilocybin had made a uniquely
valuable contribution to their spiritual wellbeing."

## The Visionary State

Powell describes the psilocybin visionary state with precision. With eyes closed, one is
confronted with "a powerful communicatory flow of organised symbolic information that compels
one to infer an intelligent presence of some kind as the issuer of the information." Unlike
ordinary dreams, during psilocybin visions one remains "more conscious and attentive than
normal," so that the visionary scenes are not forgotten, or at least their overall message,
impact and urgency are not forgotten.

He describes these as "animated superdreamscapes, charged with almost blinding metaphorical
imagery," akin to vivid dreams but experienced with full waking consciousness. The experience
carries a sense of intentional communication "leaving no room for doubt." Powell notes that
even the most dogged sceptic must concede that psilocybin carries epistemological value.

## The Transcendental Other

Powell introduces the concept of the "transcendental Other" — the felt presence of an
intelligent, communicating entity encountered during the visionary state. He proposes that this
Other can be understood as "a dynamic information-integration process given life within the
entheogen-infused brain," a higher, more integrated manifestation of the human psyche so full
of "Otherness" and purposeful import that it can be considered fully autonomous and dissociated
from the individual self/ego.

The Other communicates through a meta-language of symbols and images and their creative
juxtaposition. The Reverend's description of the visionary experience as "a kind of movie
issuing from somewhere apart from his sense of self" is identified as one of psilocybin's
hallmark effects. Powell argues that symbols are "the concentrated expression of information"
and that the formation of mythical symbols represents the only way vast amounts of related
psychological information can be captured and expressed.
