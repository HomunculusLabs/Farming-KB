# Leek Cultivation and Deep Planting Techniques

Leeks are a nearly year-round crop in the diversified winter-harvest system, prized for their edible white blanched stems. The key to efficient, intensive leek production is growing custom transplants and planting them using the **dibble-and-drop method**, which produces long blanched shanks without labor-intensive hilling. This approach allows tight spacing and high yields per bed.

## Year-Round Leek Supply

With proper [[coleman-winter-greens-variety-selection]] and succession planning, leeks can be available for ten or more months of the year:

| Season | Source | Variety Type |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| May | Cold house (extra-early transplants) | Early variety |
| June–September | Outdoor field | Summer variety |
| October–November | Outdoor field (plastic-covered late) | Fall variety |
| December–March | [[movable-greenhouse]] + [[inner-layer-row-cover-management]] | Winter variety |

Winter leeks typically sell out by early March due to strong demand. Each season uses a different variety selected for its performance under those specific conditions.

## The Dibble-and-Drop Method

The dibble-and-drop technique is the central innovation that makes intensive leek production practical. Rather than the traditional approach of sowing seeds in the field and hilling soil around the growing stems to blanch them, transplants are dropped into deep pre-made holes.

### Why It Matters

Traditional hilling requires multiple passes with a hoe or cultivator as the leeks grow, gradually mounding soil higher and higher against the stems. This is labor-intensive, disturbs the root zone, and limits how closely leeks can be spaced because the hills need room.

The dibble method solves all three problems:
- **No hilling needed**: The deep hole itself creates the blanching zone
- **Zero root disturbance**: Transplants are dropped in once and left alone
- **Intensive spacing possible**: Three rows on a 30-inch bed at 4-inch in-row spacing

### The Dibble Tool

The standard dibble is a **36-inch-long, 1-inch-diameter hardwood dowel** with the lower end tapered to a spatulate (flat, blade-like) tip. Optional enhancements:

- A shovel handle attached to the upper end for comfortable grip
- A 6-inch-diameter plate positioned 9 inches above the tip, which serves as a depth stop

### Technique

1. **Prepare the soil**: Ensure the bed is moist. Dry soil will collapse back into the hole when the dibble is withdrawn. Water the surface before transplanting if conditions are dry
2. **Create the hole**: Push the dibble vertically into the soil to the full 9-inch depth, then twist the handle so the spatulate tip flattens out the bottom of a tubular hole
3. **Spacing**: Make a hole every 4 inches along the row, with three rows per 30-inch bed
4. **Drop the transplant**: Simply drop a leek transplant vertically into each hole—about 1 inch of green top should remain above the soil surface
5. **Irrigate**: Water the field thoroughly after planting
6. **Let nature fill the holes**: Soil slowly migrates into the holes with each irrigation and cultivation cycle. The leeks grow beautifully, each with a guaranteed 9 inches of blanched shank

## Growing Transplants

Leek transplants are not grown in soil blocks (unlike most other crops in the system). Instead:

1. **Seedbeds**: Construct 30-inch-wide × 8-foot-long × 3-inch-deep wooden-sided frames on the floor of the plant-starting greenhouse, filled with potting soil
2. **Sowing**: Use a six-row seeder to sow leek seeds directly into these beds
3. **Growing**: Allow seedlings to grow until at least 10 inches tall
4. **Preparation for transplanting**: Trim roots to 1 inch long and trim tops to 10 inches long before dibbling

Seeds for the first winter crop are sown on **February 15** in the plant-starting greenhouse, with transplanting to a cold house following.

## Baby Leeks for Restaurants

Restaurant customers particularly value **early baby leeks**. These are grown at tighter spacing than standard leeks:

- **Five rows** per 30-inch bed (vs. three for standard)
- **3-inch spacing** in the row (vs. 4-inch for standard)
- At this density they can be cultivated with the long-handled wire weeder

The dibble-and-drop method is essential at this spacing—there is no way to hill individual baby leeks at 3-inch intervals.

## Adaptable to Constrained Spaces

The dibble method's minimal soil disturbance makes it adaptable to spaces where other [[desert-garden-bed-design-and-planting-methods]] would fail. Leeks have been successfully grown in single rows between the edge bed and the wall of a greenhouse—space that would otherwise go unused.

## Maximizing Blanched Shank Length

The edible portion of a leek is the white blanched stem. More blanched length means a better product and higher market value. The dibble method guarantees a consistent 9-inch blanched shank for every plant, which is:

- Longer and more consistent than hilling typically achieves
- More uniform across the crop
- Achieved with less total labor

## Winter Protection

Winter leeks are covered with a movable greenhouse starting in early December. An inner layer is added from mid-December onward until all leeks are sold. The combination of cold-hardy winter varieties and double-layer protection keeps leeks in harvestable condition through the coldest months.

## Key Takeaways

- Deep planting via dibble eliminates hilling labor while producing superior blanched stems
- Growing your own transplants in seedbeds allows precise scheduling [[seed-saving-and-variety-selection]]
- Intensive spacing (3 rows × 4 inches = 270 plants per 10-foot bed section) maximizes yield per square foot
- Year-round supply requires multiple varieties matched to each season's conditions
- Baby leeks at tighter spacing offer a high-value restaurant crop
