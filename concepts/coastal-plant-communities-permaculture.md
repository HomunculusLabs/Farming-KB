# Coastal Plant Communities in Permaculture

Coastal environments present unique challenges for plant establishment: salt spray, sandy soils with low nutrient and water retention, strong winds, and occasional inundation by seawater. Permaculture design in [[coastal-ground-covers-hedges-permaculture]] covers, hedges, shrubs, and trees — each contributing to a resilient coastal system.

## Understanding Coastal Conditions

Coastal sites vary widely, from exposed headlands and sand dunes to sheltered estuaries and mangrove margins. Key stress factors include:

- **Salt spray**: Carried by wind, salt coats foliage and soil, causing dehydration and ion toxicity in non-adapted plants.
- **Sandy soils**: Free-draining, low in organic matter, with poor water and nutrient retention.
- **Wind**: Constant exposure desiccates plants and causes physical damage.
- **Tidal inundation**: Estuarine and mangrove zones experience periodic flooding with saline water.
- **Frost**: Some coastal climates experience frost, requiring cold-hardy selections.

## Mangrove and Estuarine Trees

### Avicennia marina (Grey Mangrove)

The common mangrove tree of Australian estuaries, river banks, and sheltered coasts. Grey mangroves obtain oxygen through specialised **pneumatophore roots** that thrust upward from the mud. The soil is typically soft, moist, saline, and lacks profile development. After three separate boilings, the seed looks and tastes similar to avocado. The golden-yellow flowers yield nectar that produces a light, amber-coloured, pleasant honey from mid-autumn to early winter. Leaves are palatable to stock. The wood is extremely tough and hard, with conspicuous brown growth rings and a density of about 880 kg/m³, used for knees and elbows in boat building.

Companion species include **Casuarina glauca** (swamp oak) on less inundated margins in southern regions and **Ceriops tagal** (small stilted mangrove) in northern areas. The species tolerates some frost.

### Betula populifolia (Grey Birch)

A tree to 9 meters that thrives on sandy or swampy coastal soil. Extremely frost hardy, it is best planted in groves where individual trees provide mutual protection.

### Chrysobalanus icaco (Cocoa Plum, Icaco Palm)

An extremely hardy shrub or tree to 12 meters with branches that creep in all directions. Native to south Florida and the Caribbean, it grows in dense clumps on the seashore and in both fresh and saline swamps, extending well inland into mountain regions. The fruit was an important food of the Seminole Indians — eaten raw, stewed with sugar, dried like prunes, or made into jellies. Seed kernels are roasted and eaten. The tree may prove frost tolerant given its inland mountain range.

## Large Coastal Trees

### Elaeagnus angustifolia (Russian Olive, Bohemian Olive)

A deciduous small tree to 12 meters, native from the Mediterranean through Southwest Asia. Extremely tough as a seaside plant, hardy to -34°C, and capable of growing right to the sea in France. It forms a symbiotic relationship with *Frankia* spp nitrogen-fixing soil microbes, improving fertility on nutrient-poor coastal sands. Prefers dry, well-drained, alkaline soils in full sun. Yellow, sweet berries are eaten fresh or made into pies, sauces, jams, jellies, sherbets, and wines. An excellent windbreak and hedge species providing bird habitat [[nyssa-aquatica]]* and *Nyssa ogeche* are also worthy of coastal trial.

### Persea borbonica (Red Bay, Bull Bay)

An evergreen, frost-hardy tree occurring in low areas near the sea along the eastern coast of the USA. A relative of avocado, its leaves are used as flavouring similar to bay leaves and can also be made into tea.

### Populus tremula (European Aspen)

A small, suckering, open-headed tree native to Europe that withstands full exposure to the sea. Hardy to frost and extremely resistant to salt.

### Swietenia mahagoni (West Indian Mahogany)

An evergreen tree to 24 meters native to southern USA and the Caribbean. Common in low-lying limestone swamps close to the sea, it produces wood of exceptional quality and survives just above saline water. Considered frost tender.

### Taxodium distichum (Bald Cypress)

A swamp tree of the Florida Everglades that has been found to be quite frost hardy, suggesting potential for wider coastal planting.
