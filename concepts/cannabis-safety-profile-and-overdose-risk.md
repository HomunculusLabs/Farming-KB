## Impact on Special Populations

A Duke University study (approximately 38 years of observational data)
concluded that while adult cannabis use has no effect on intelligence,
"cannabis dependency" in adolescents may contribute to reduced IQ test
scores. The study has limitations: subjective data, small sample size
(153 people), and only some IQ-altering factors were considered. Until
more is known, a possible correlation should be assumed.

## Lung and Heart Health

The largest population-based case-controlled study of cannabis-only use
(2,252 people in Los Angeles) found smoking only cannabis was mildly
lung-protective and not associated with increased [[psk-breast-lung-cancer-clinical-trials]] risk.
[[cervantes-hash-cannabis-oil-production]] has shown therapeutic effects in patients with
[[cannabis-for-rheumatoid-arthritis]] and asthma. Vaporizers can eliminate
inhalation of burned carbon compounds.

Endocannabinoid receptors are present in the heart. THC can increase
heart rate but not dangerously. THC, CBD, and CBN have demonstrated
potent cardio-protective properties in numerous studies.

## Cancer and Schizophrenia Considerations

Cannabis constituents produce apoptosis ([[blesching-cannabis-apoptosis-and-cancer-cell-death]] death) across many
cancer types. Cannabis is not considered a carcinogen in typical medical
use. Observational studies suggest adolescent cannabis use may increase
schizophrenia risk as a co-factor, though cannabis is not itself causal.
Adolescents with family history of psychosis should exercise caution.

## Contaminants and Gateway Theory

Pesticides on cannabis may increase toxic load; this risk is eliminated
by purchasing organic cannabis. Aspergillus mold poses risk to
immunocompromised patients. Proper drying (water activity less than 0.81
at 25 +/- 2 degrees C) prevents [[brassinosteroid-fungal-growth-promotion]].

A study of more than 4,000 cannabis smokers concluded that cannabis use
leads to a decrease in the use of alcohol, tobacco, and hard drugs,
contradicting the gateway theory.

## Bottom Line

Extensive evidence indicates that cannabis is neither dangerous nor
harmless. Consideration of its medicinal use should include a risk
versus benefit analysis focused on the specific therapeutic needs and
health challenges of the individual patient.
