# Mushroom Food Value From time to time there is discussion in popular articles of the food value of mushrooms. The cultivated mushroom is properly an accessory food or luxury, deserving particular attention more as a relish or condimentary dish. Understanding its nutritional composition requires consideration of both its chemical makeup and its role in the diet. ## Nature as an Accessory Food In reference to food value, the cultivated mushroom is properly classified as an accessory food, deserving particular attention more as a relish or condimentary dish. As such it has a value which cannot adequately be expressed physiologically, much less measured by the figures which express the chemist's data. Food values are of little consideration, as a rule, with products which are essentially a matter of taste. Truffles may bring twenty or thirty francs per kilo in Paris; the common mushroom may sell at two francs; and green beans at less than a franc. Comparing equal weights, beans have greater actual food value, but flavor commands the special price. ## Chemical Composition Both cultivated mushrooms and wild edible species possess a food value equal to that of many vegetables, better than some, poorer than others.
