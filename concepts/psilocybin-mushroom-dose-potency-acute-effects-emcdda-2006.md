## Active Compounds and Mechanism of Action

The primary psychoactive compounds in hallucinogenic mushrooms are:

- **Psilocybin** (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine; 4-PO-DMT): The phosphate ester of psilocin; more stable in air and water-soluble
- **Psilocin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine; 4-HO-DMT): The pharmacologically active compound; psilocybin is converted to psilocin in vivo
- **Baeocystin** and **Norbaeocystin**: Additional tryptamines that may be present but are considered less active

[[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] are classified as indolealkylamines (tryptamines), structurally similar to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT). Psilocin acts on the serotonin system as a 5-HT2A post-synaptic agonist or partial agonist. Chemically, they belong to the same group as LSD. The three major chemical groups of hallucinogens are: (1) indolealkylamines (tryptamines) — LSD, psilocin, psilocybin; (2) phenylethylamines — mescaline; (3) cannabinoids.

The EMCDDA notes that "hallucinogens" is a misleading term — these drugs rarely cause true hallucinations (perceptions without external stimuli). Effects are more accurately described as perceptual distortions, extending beyond perception to include changes in thought, mood, and personality integration (self-awareness). Many other agents classified as "pseudo-hallucinogens" produce psychotic and delirious effects without classic visual disturbances.

## Potency Variation by Species

Mushroom potency depends on species or variety, geographic origin, growing conditions, and specimen age (Hasler et al. 2004):

| Species | Approximate Maximum Psilocybin Content |
|---|---|
| *[[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]]* (liberty caps) | Up to 1% psilocybin |
| *[[psilocybe-azurescens]]* | Among the highest reported |
| *[[psilocybe-bohemica]]* | Among the highest reported |
| *Psilocybe cubensis* varieties | Up to 0.6% psilocybin + psilocin |

These values serve only as initial orientation. Actual concentration may vary significantly between individual mushrooms, making precise dosing difficult.

The primary field identification characteristic of psilocybin-containing mushrooms is **bluing** when handled, caused by oxidative degradation of psilocin. Nearly all [[cap-versus-stem-alkaloid-distribution-in-psilocybin-mushrooms]] are small, brown, or tan, and can be mistaken for non-psychoactive, inedible, or poisonous species — a significant safety concern for wild foraging.

## Recreational Dosing

The psychoactive dose of psilocybin is estimated at **45–315 µg/kg body weight** (Hasler et al. 2004).

- **Dried mushrooms**: 1–3.5 to 5 grams (threshold/light to full psychedelic effects)
- **Fresh mushrooms**: Approximately 10× dried weight = 10–50 grams fresh

### Dosing Caveats

These ranges should be interpreted cautiously:
- Converting psilocybin concentrations into mushroom weight is imprecise
- Multiple active substances are typically present simultaneously
- **Short-term tolerance** develops rapidly, requiring dose escalation for repeated use
- Individual sensitivity varies considerably based on body weight, metabolism, prior experience

## Routes of Administration and Onset

### Preparation Methods

1. **Raw consumption**: Chewing fresh mushrooms and holding in mouth before swallowing
2. **Dried consumption**: Eating dried mushrooms directly
3. **Tea preparation**: Brewing or stewing chopped mushrooms in hot water — preferred for reduced nausea
4. **Food mixing**: Combining with food may cause nausea and vomiting (Lifeline Publications, 2005)

### Onset and Duration

- **Onset**: 20–60 minutes after ingestion; tea preparations may produce faster onset
- **Peak effects**: Approximately 2–3 hours post-ingestion
- **Total duration**: 4–6 hours
- **Comedown**: Gradual, with residual perceptual effects potentially persisting several additional hours

## Acute Psychological Effects

### Primary Effects

- **Visual distortions**: Enhanced colors, pattern recognition, geometric hallucinations, trailing effects
- **Altered time perception**: Time may appear to slow or speed up
- **Synesthesia**: Blending of sensory modalities
- **Altered thought patterns**: Philosophical insights, creative thinking
- **Mood changes**: Euphoria to anxiety depending on set and setting
- **Changed self-awareness**: Altered body image, ego boundary dissolution

### Negative Psychological Effects

- **Nausea**: Common during onset, especially with raw consumption
- **Panic attacks**: Can occur at higher doses or in anxious users
- **Paranoia**: May develop in unfamiliar or threatening settings
- **Confusion**: Temporary inability to process information normally
- **Flashbacks**: Brief recurrence of effects after the experience (relatively rare)

Effects vary greatly between individuals and between episodes. The EMCDDA emphasizes that user accounts suggest mushrooms may not be viewed favorably enough for regular repeat use — unpredictable potency, nausea, panic attacks, and lack of entactogenic (sociable, mood-enhancing) effects all limit appeal.
