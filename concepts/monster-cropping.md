# Monster-Cropping

Monster-cropping is a cannabis cultivation technique that involves taking clones from a flowering female plant and reverting them to vegetative growth. The re-vegged clones exhibit extreme branching and bushy growth patterns, producing unusually large, multi-cola plants. The technique is sometimes called "monster cropping" because the resulting plants grow into massive, heavily branched specimens. While the specific term "monster-cropping" does not appear in Cervantes' text, he provides detailed coverage of all the underlying principles and techniques that comprise this method.

## Core Concept

When a cannabis plant is flowering, its growth pattern has shifted from vegetative (symmetrical branching) to reproductive (asymmetrical branching and flower production). Taking a cutting from this flowering plant and forcing it back into vegetative growth creates a genetic "memory" of the flowering state. When the clone re-vegetates, it produces an abnormal number of new growth points and branches, resulting in a bushy, multi-cola plant far more complex than a clone taken during vegetative growth.

Cervantes confirms this phenomenon when he discusses [[re-vegging]]: rejuvenated plants "will stop flowering and rejuvenate and revert back to vegetative growth stage," and during this re-vegetation, "leaves will continue to grow more and more fingers." The unusual leaf morphology and excessive branching are hallmarks of monster-cropped plants.

## The Monster-Cropping Process

### Step 1: Select a Flowering Mother

Choose a healthy flowering female plant, ideally in weeks 2-3 of flowering. Cervantes notes that "the earlier in the flowering stage cuttings are taken, the more rapid the rooting and the re-vegetation rate." Plants deep into flowering (near senescence) have depleted growth hormones and may not survive the process.

### Step 2: Take Cuttings

Cervantes provides specific instructions:

- "Cut clones from the lower green branch tips"
- "Cut a one to two-inch-long (3-5 cm) stem"
- "Trim off flowers and lower leaves"
- "Keep two or three green leaves" -- survival depends on maintaining some green foliage
- "If leaves have yellowed, survival chances diminish exponentially"

### Step 3: Root the Cuttings

- "You can clone a favorite flowering plant, but it is difficult"
- "Clones take longer to root, and results are not always the best"
- "Powerful flowering hormones must be reversed, and rooting hormone signals must be sent"
- "Give plants 24 hours of light to signal them to grow"
- Use rooting hormone and a humidity dome to improve success rates
- Expect rooting to take 2-4 weeks, significantly longer than vegetative clones

### Step 4: Re-Vegetate

- Once rooted, place clones under 18/6 or 24/0 light
- Apply high-nitrogen nutrients to promote vegetative growth
- New growth will emerge in 4-6 weeks with unusual branching patterns
- Leaves may display abnormal morphology -- single-blade or "whorled" leaves initially
- Normal leaf patterns return after 2-3 weeks of new growth

### Step 5: Flower

- Allow the re-vegged plant to grow to desired size (typically 4-8 weeks of vegetative growth)
- Switch to 12/12 flowering
- The plant will produce an exceptional number of bud sites across its bushy structure

## Why Monster-Cropped Plants Are Different

### Excessive Branching

Normal cannabis clones taken during vegetative growth maintain the parent's branching pattern. Monster-cropped clones produce significantly more branches because the plant's hormonal system has been disrupted by the flowering-to-vegetative transition. The re-vegging process activates dormant bud sites throughout the plant.

### Abnormal Leaf Morphology

During re-vegetation, new leaves often emerge with unusual shapes: single blades, three-fingered leaves, or whorled patterns. This is a visual indicator that the plant is in a transitional state. Cervantes describes a plant that received accidental 12/12 light for three days and then reverted: "The light stress also caused leaves to grow in circles!" This abnormal growth pattern is typical of re-vegged plants.

### Multiple Main Colas

The excessive branching naturally creates many main-stem-quality colas without the need for topping or training. Each branch develops with the vigor of a main stem, producing dense, high-quality buds.

## Advantages

- Massive yield potential: the bushy structure produces many large colas
- No need for extensive training: the plant naturally develops multiple main colas
- Preserves genetics from flowering: useful when no vegetative mother was kept
- Unique branching structure: creates a wide, even canopy with minimal training
- Can produce exceptionally heavy yields from a single plant

## Disadvantages and Challenges

Cervantes outlines several significant challenges:

- "Clones take longer to root" -- significantly longer than vegetative clones
- "Results are not always the best" -- success rate is lower than normal cloning
- "Powerful flowering hormones must be reversed" -- the hormonal shift is stressful
- "Taking clones from rejuvenated plants also diffuses hormones and severely stresses plants"
- Longer timeline: 4-6 weeks for re-vegetation plus normal flowering time
- Slower initial growth during the re-vegetation period
- Unpredictable results: not all clones survive, and growth patterns vary
- Lower success rate with clones taken late in flowering

## Stress Considerations

Cervantes emphasizes the role of stress throughout his text:

- "Stress is a growth inhibitor"
- "Stressed plants with wounded stems and vegetation grow slower and invite pests and diseases"
- The flowering-to-vegetative transition is inherently stressful
- Plants should be given optimal conditions (nutrients, light, humidity) during recovery

## Integration with Other Techniques

Monster-cropped plants can be further enhanced with additional training:

- [[low-stress-training]]: bend and position the many branches for an even canopy
- [[screen-of-green]]: the abundant branches fill a SCROG screen quickly
- [[lollipopping]]: remove lower growth to focus energy on the many top colas
- [[defoliation-cannabis]]: thin the dense canopy to improve airflow
- [[sea-of-green]]: not typically combined, as monster-cropped plants are large

## Nutritional Requirements

During re-vegetation, Cervantes recommends "an increased dose of high-nitrogen fertilizer to promote green, leafy growth." The plant needs abundant nitrogen to support the rapid vegetative regrowth. Once flowering begins, transition back to a bloom nutrient formula with higher phosphorus and potassium.

## IBA and Hormone Treatments

Cervantes notes that indole-3-butyric acid (IBA) "can be used to encourage regeneration of flowers" and helps "generate roots, build a larger root mass, and improve plant growth and yield." Using IBA during the cloning phase may improve success rates for monster-cropped cuttings.
