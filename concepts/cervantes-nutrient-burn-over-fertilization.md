# Nutrient Burn and Over-fertilization in Cannabis

Nutrient burn, also called fertilizer burn, is one of the most common
problems in [[jorge-cervantes]] identifies it as a
frequent mistake that can impair growth, reduce yield, and compromise
bud quality. Over-fertilization occurs when the concentration of
dissolved mineral salts in the [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]]
exceeds what the plant can absorb, leading to toxic accumulation in

## Soluble Salts and EC Thresholds

Cervantes provides guidelines for electrical conductivity (EC) and
total dissolved solids (TDS) in nutrient solutions. The desirable
range is 0.75 to 2.0 mS (500-1300 ppm TDS). A permissible range
with potential for concern is 2.0 to 3.0 mS (1300-2000 ppm).
Probable salt damage occurs at 3.0 mS and above (2000+ ppm TDS).
One mS is approximately equivalent to 650 ppm total dissolved solids.

## Treating Nutrient Burn

The primary treatment for over-fertilization is flushing the growing
medium. Cervantes recommends flushing with a minimum of three times
the volume of water per volume of growing medium. For hydroponic
systems, drain and replace the entire reservoir with fresh, mild
nutrient solution. Key steps include:

1. Stop all fertilization immediately
2. Flush with plain pH-balanced water or very mild (quarter-strength)
   nutrient solution -- Cervantes notes that mild nutrient solution
   actually removes more excess fertilizer than plain water
3. Allow at least ten percent of the flush water to drain from the
   bottom of containers to carry away accumulated salts
4. Resume feeding at half the previous concentration once new growth
   appears healthy
5. Monitor EC of runoff solution to confirm salt levels are declining
