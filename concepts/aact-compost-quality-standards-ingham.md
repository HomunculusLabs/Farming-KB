### Worm Compost (Vermicompost)
- Superior for beneficial nematodes and protozoa
- Provides the habitat conditions that support diverse beneficial communities
- Worms modify bacterial, fungal, and protozoan communities
- Requires feeding worms fungal foods to get fungal-feeding nematodes
- Moisture maintenance is the biggest challenge

### Recommended Mix
A combination of good aerobic thermal compost with good aerobic worm castings, approximately 50-50 or 25-75 ratio.

## Evaluating Compost Quality

### Visual Indicators
- Dark brown to black color
- Crumbly texture
- Earthy smell (no offensive odors)
- Visible fungal hyphae (white threads)
- No recognizable original materials

### Biological Testing
The only way to KNOW your compost has the right biology is to test it:

- **Total and active bacteria**: Adequate numbers and diversity
- **Total and active fungi**: Especially for [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-bacterial-and-fungal-dominant-soil]] species

### Warning Signs
- Offensive odor = anaerobic conditions = don't use
- Recognizable original materials = not finished = don't use
- Cedar Grove products specifically mentioned as NOT making good compost
- Swollen containers of fish products indicate anaerobic conditions before purchase
