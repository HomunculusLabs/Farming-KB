# Cannabis Health Index CHI Score - Evidence-Based Interpretation Guide

## Overview

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) is an evidence-based scoring system developed by
Uwe Blesching that rates the available scientific literature supporting cannabis
use for specific medical conditions. The CHI synthesizes data from over 15,000
entries at the National Library of Health into a single numerical score for each
condition, enabling patients and practitioners to quickly assess the strength of
evidence for cannabis as a therapeutic option.

## The Evidence Problem in Medical Cannabis

Blesching's introduction highlights a fundamental problem in modern medicine:
only about 15% of medical interventions are based on solid scientific evidence.
The Vioxx scandal is cited as an example—the drug killed an estimated 55,000 to
500,000 patients through heart attacks and strokes before being withdrawn, yet
the company still profited by approximately ten billion dollars. This context
underscores the importance of evidence-based approaches to all medical
interventions, including cannabis.

The CHI was developed to address the lack of a systematic evidence-based model
for cannabis use, which despite the growth of scientific literature to over
15,000 entries, had no standardized framework for evaluating and comparing the
quality of evidence across different conditions.

## CHI Score Structure

The CHI score rates each condition based on the type and quality of available
research studies. The hierarchy of evidence (from strongest to weakest) generally
follows:

1. **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses**: Comprehensive analyses of multiple
   studies, providing the highest level of evidence. These synthesize data
   across many individual studies to reach robust conclusions.
2. **Randomized controlled trials (RCTs)**: Gold-standard individual studies
   where patients are randomly assigned to treatment or control groups.
3. **Controlled clinical trials**: Studies with control groups but without
   randomization.
4. **Observational studies**: Cohort studies, case-control studies, and
   cross-sectional studies that observe outcomes without intervention.
