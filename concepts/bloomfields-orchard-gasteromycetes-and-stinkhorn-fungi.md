## Cage Fungi

### Clathrus

Clathrus forms a spherical cage with spores on the inner
bar surfaces. The cage expands from an egg through osmotic
pressurization into an open polyhedral structure. Flies
are usual dispersal vectors, though ants and stingless
bees have been observed on some species.

### Anthurus

Anthurus archeri separates into four or more bright orange
arms curling back over the egg to create a star shape. Arms
are smeared with spores on their inner surface.
Time-lapse video of hatching is shocking: the livid arms
simulate the agonized contortions of a horribly injured
lobster. There is nothing comparable in nature.

### Ileodictyon

The intestinal net fungus grows in New Zealand and
Australia. The Maori accorded it nine names and barbecued
its eggs. The mature white cage detaches from its papery
skin and is blown about by wind, dispersing spores from
its smeared interior.
The Maori rejected the mature fruiting bodies as the
feces of ghosts or of the stars. The polyhedral structure
shares geometry with Buckminster Fuller's geodesic domes
and buckminsterfullerene carbon molecules, reflecting the
strength of lightweight polyhedral forms.
