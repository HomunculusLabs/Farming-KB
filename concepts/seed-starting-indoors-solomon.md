# Seed Starting Indoors (Solomon)

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: Gardening When It Counts, Steve Solomon, Chapters 3-5

Solomon takes a minimalist approach to indoor seed starting. His core advice: grow seedlings only for crops that genuinely benefit from a head start, and use the simplest possible methods.

## When Indoor Starting Is Worthwhile

Very few vegetable crops actually need to be started indoors. Solomon recommends starting seedlings only for:

- **Tomatoes, peppers, and eggplants** (solanum family): These heat-loving crops benefit enormously from every extra frost-free day
- **Melons and winter squash** (in short-season climates only): In areas with fewer than 120 frost-free days, starting a few cucurbit transplants may be justified
- **Cabbage family** (for very [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] harvests): If you want the earliest possible spring cabbage, starting indoors helps

Most other vegetables should be direct-seeded. [[fukuoka-direct-seeding-rice-barley-cultivation]] is simpler, avoids [[query-how-to-prevent-transplant-shock-when-moving-cannabis-plants]], and produces stronger plants.

## The Germination Cabinet

A germination cabinet (germ box) is the only specialized equipment Solomon recommends. It is simply a insulated box large enough to hold seedling pots, heated by a lightbulb to maintain 75-80°F (24-27°C).

Construction:
- A wooden or cardboard box roughly the size of a carry-on suitcase
- A 25-40 watt lightbulb on the bottom for heat
- A sliding glass top (window glass in tracks) to retain heat and humidity
- The glass top allows you to adjust temperature by changing bulb wattage or propping the top open

Temperature control: 75-80°F is ideal for most vegetables. Above 80°F may reduce germination in some species. Monitor with a thermometer.

## Seedling Soil Mix

Solomon's recipe is simple and avoids expensive commercial potting mixes:

For non-clay gardens:
- Half-fill a 5-gallon bucket with ordinary garden soil
- Blend in about 1.5 gallons of well-rotted manure or well-ripened compost
- If no compost is available, use sphagnum moss
- Add exactly one cup (250ml) of [[seed-starting-indoors]], [[seed-starting-reference]], [[solomon-potting-mix-and-seedling-soil-recipe]]
