## Cannabis for PTSD and Trauma

## Overview

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is characterized by persistent re-experiencing of traumatic events, hyperarousal, avoidance behaviors, and negative mood changes. The [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] for detailed coverage of anandamide's role in fear processing.

## Anandamide and Fear Processing

Anandamide acts as a key regulator of the fear response. When released in the amygdala, it dampens the fear signal by activating CB1 receptors, essentially providing an "emotional brake." Its emotional keyword -- "I am at ease" -- reflects its role in bliss, relaxation, and social bonding. In PTSD, this system is compromised: FAAH (the enzyme that breaks down anandamide) is often overactive, leading to anandamide deficiency. CBD inhibits FAAH, effectively raising anandamide levels and restoring the brain's capacity to modulate fear responses.

Anandamide levels are boosted naturally through exercise (the "runner's high" phenomenon), meaningful social interactions, and meditation practices. The presence of corticosteroids stimulates the body's production of anandamide, which may account for the antidepressant effects of physical exercise. For PTSD patients, these natural anandamide-boosting activities may complement cannabinoid therapy. This mechanism connects PTSD directly to the broader [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] with CBD alone and THC:CBD combinations show promise for sleep and anxiety outcomes. Blood levels of anandamide may serve as a biomarker for PTSD severity and treatment response, though this remains an area of active research.

### Integration with Therapy

Cannabis should not replace trauma-focused psychotherapy but can complement it. When used as an adjunct to EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing) or CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy), cannabis may lower the emotional barrier to processing traumatic memories. Low doses of CBD before therapy sessions may facilitate exposure-based work by reducing amygdala reactivity without causing intoxication. ## Case Study: Repressed Emotions and Somatic Consequences

The Blesching source documents a case from Cornell Medical Center of a 43-year-old woman whose severe, treatment-resistant hypertension was linked to repressed memories of sexual assault. After decades of suppression, nightmares triggered by a family encounter led to the emergence of traumatic memories. Through counseling that enabled emotional processing -- moving from numbness to feeling powerlessness, betrayal, and rage, and ultimately to identifying as a survivor -- her blood pressure normalized over 18 months with gradual reduction of pharmaceuticals.

This case illustrates the deep psychosomatic connections between unresolved trauma and physical disease. ## Strain and Cannabinoid Selection

- CBD-dominant strains preferred for daytime use (anxiolytic without intoxication)
- THC-containing strains may be beneficial before sleep (reduces nightmares, improves sleep continuity)
- Balanced 1:1 THC:CBD strains offer synergistic effects for many patients
- Myrcene-rich strains enhance sedative effects for nighttime use
- Pinene-rich strains may help with daytime cognitive clarity and counteract THC-induced brain fog
- Raw cannabis juice provides non-psychoactive CBD-acid for patients who cannot tolerate psychoactive effects while still obtaining anxiolytic benefits

## Cautions

- High-THC strains may exacerbate anxiety in some patients, particularly early in treatment
- Cannabis should not replace trauma-focused psychotherapy (EMDR, CPT) but can complement it
- Withdrawal from cannabis can temporarily worsen PTSD symptoms
- Adolescent use is contraindicated due to effects on brain development
- VA healthcare systems are increasingly recognizing cannabis as a treatment option for veterans with PTSD, though policies vary by region
- Cannabis may be most effective when combined with evidence-based therapies that address the root causes of trauma rather than just managing symptoms
- Blood levels of anandamide may serve as a biomarker for PTSD severity and treatment response, though this remains an area of active research
