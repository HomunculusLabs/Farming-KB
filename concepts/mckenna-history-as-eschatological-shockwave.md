# McKenna on History as Eschatological Shockwave

## Overview

In his 1983 Esalen lecture, [[terence-mckenna]] proposed a striking interpretation
of human history: that it is "the [[mckenna-history-shockwave-of-eschatology]]" — a ripple
propagating backward through time from some culminating event at the end of
time. This framework, which he developed more fully in later works including
*The [[tryptamine-hypercontinuum-and-invisible-landscape]]* and *Food of the Gods*, presents history not as
random contingency but as a process with an inherent directional tendency
drawing all human becoming toward a transcendental conclusion.

## The Shadow from the End of Time

McKenna's formulation begins with a simple but radical claim: "Something is at
the end of time and is casting an enormous shadow over human history, drawing
all human becoming toward it." The metaphor of the shadow implies that the
culminating event, while not yet fully manifest, is already exerting a causal
influence on the present. Just as a physical object casts a shadow that extends
spatially behind it, the eschatological object casts a shadow that extends
temporally backward through history.

All of human activity — the wars, philosophies, migrations, cities, and
civilizations — occupies what McKenna called "a microsecond of geological,
planetary, and galactic time." From this perspective, the entire span of human
history is not a gradual unfolding but a rapid acceleration toward a point of
convergence.

## The Acceleration Principle

McKenna identified acceleration as the defining characteristic of historical
processes. He provided a timeline of increasingly rapid developments:

- Fire was discovered several million years ago
- Language emerged perhaps thirty-five thousand years ago
- Measurement systems arose five thousand years ago
- Galileo's scientific revolution occurred four hundred years ago
- Watson and Crick's discovery of DNA structure occurred within living memory

"What is obviously happening," McKenna concluded, "is that everything is being
drawn together." The rate of significant novelty entering human experience is
itself accelerating, producing a concavity in the graph of historical change
that approaches an asymptote.

This acceleration is not merely technological but encompasses all domains of
human activity: art, philosophy, religion, social organization, and
consciousness itself. Each breakthrough creates the conditions for further,
more rapid breakthroughs, producing a self-reinforcing spiral of novelty.

## The Monkey-Symbiote Framework

McKenna embedded the eschatological framework within a broader narrative about
the relationship between humanity and what he called "the symbiote" — a form
of intelligence present in the environment, particularly associated with
psychoactive plants, that feeds information to humanity about "the larger
picture." The symbiote operates through the tryptamine-containing plants
([[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]], DMT-containing vines) to accelerate the development
of human consciousness and culture.

In this reading, history is not just the story of human achievement but the
story of humanity's reaction to the information being transmitted by the
symbiote. The acceleration of novelty is driven in part by this ongoing
information exchange, which periodically disrupts existing [[harner-cross-cultural-patterns-shamanic-plant-use]]
and propels humanity toward greater understanding.

## Critique of Scientific Cosmology

McKenna contrasted his eschatological view with the prevailing scientific
cosmology, which describes a universe that has existed for billions of years
and will continue for billions more. He characterized this as "a dualistic
conception, an inductive projection that is very unsophisticated when applied
to the nature of [[mckenna-catalyzing-consciousness-and-language]]."

The scientific view treats time as a uniform, linear dimension — a container
within which events occur. McKenna's view treats time as having a qualitative
dimension: it is not merely duration but direction, not just passage but
purpose. The eschatological shockwave model implies that time itself is
heterogeneous, with different periods possessing different qualities of
experience and different densities of novelty.
