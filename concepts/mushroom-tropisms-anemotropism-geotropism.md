# Mushroom Tropisms — Anemotropism and Geotropism

## Overview

Tropisms are directional growth responses of organisms to environmental stimuli. In mushrooms, tropisms govern the orientation and development of the basidiocarp (fruit body) and are critical for successful [[adaptive-anemotropism-spore-dispersal-optimization-mushroom-evolution]]. The foundational study by Edmond R. Badham (*Mycologia*, 1982) investigated tropisms in *[[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]* using a controlled wind tunnel, revealing a complex interplay between anemotropism (wind response), geotropism (gravity response), and phototropism (light response) that changes as the mushroom matures.

## Study Context

Badham's research studied approximately 100 developing basidiocarps of *Psilocybe cubensis* in a closed-circuit wind tunnel with controlled parameters:

- **Wind velocity**: 9 ± 3 cm/sec
- **Humidity**: 94 ± 3% RH
- **Temperature**: 24.5 ± 1.5°C
- **Light**: Cool white fluorescent at 2685 lux, 12h photoperiod

A sterile mutant strain was also studied to isolate the role of [[spore-production-geotropic-sensitivity-coupling-basidiomycete-badham-1982]] in tropic responses.

## Developmental Stages

Badham defined four stages of *P. cubensis* development:

| Stage | Pileus Shape | Significance |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| Stage 1 | Slight swelling | Early primordium, pre-stipe elongation |
| Stage 2 | Nearly spherical | Active stipe elongation phase |
| Stage 3 | Open hemispherical | Maturity / sporulation begins |
| Stage 4 | More or less plane | Late maturity |

The tropic response changes fundamentally between Stage 2 and Stage 3, coinciding with the onset of spore production.

## Anemotropism (Wind Response)

### Stages 1–2: Growth Into the Wind

When primordia between Stage 1 and Stage 2 are placed in the wind tunnel, the stipe grows **directly into the airflow**. This anemotropic response was observed to:

- Occur in both 12-hour photoperiod and complete darkness
- Occur in both fertile and sterile basidiocarps
- Occur regardless of the initial orientation of the stipe relative to wind
- Be most pronounced when cultures are placed in the chamber before Stage 2

If cultures are rotated perpendicular to the wind (3 rpm), stipes grow **upward** instead of bending into the flow, confirming that the response is directional rather than a general growth acceleration.

### Proposed Mechanism: Evaporative Demand Gradient

Badham proposed that the anemotropic curvature results from differential evaporative demand across the stipe:

1. Wind creates a thinner [[windward-leeward-boundary-layer-redistribution-stipe-curvature-badham-1982]] on the **windward side** of the stipe
2. The thinner boundary layer increases evaporative demand (water loss)
3. The **leeward side** has a thicker boundary layer with less evaporation
4. Cells on opposite sides of the stipe grow at different rates due to this moisture differential
5. The result is bending toward the windward side (into the wind)

This mechanism is analogous to the avoidance response in *Phycomyces*, where wind and water vapor are known to influence [[gadd-spitzenkorper-vesicle-supply-centre-hyphal-tip-growth-direction]]. Evaporative demand is a function of four factors: wind, humidity, light, and temperature.

### Alternative Explanations

Two alternative mechanisms were considered:

1. **Force response**: The mushroom may respond directly to the physical force of wind, similar to how *Phycomyces* sporangiophores respond to gravity and centrifugal force
2. **Gas redistribution**: Wind may redistribute gases other than water vapor. Ethylene has been suggested as a potential signaling molecule in *Phycomyces* avoidance responses

## Geotropism (Gravity Response)

### Stage 2–3: Negative Geotropic Recurvation

At Stage 2, a dramatic shift in tropic behavior occurs. Fertile basidiocarps exhibit **[[coprophilous-habitat-negative-geotropism-spore-dispersal-adaptation]]** — the pileus turns upward from the direction of the wind, opening with lamellae facing downward. This recurvation:

- Coincides with spore production (Stage 3)
- Occurs regardless of whether light comes from above or below
- **Does not occur** in sterile (sporeless) mutants
- **Does not occur** in the absence of light (which also prevents spore formation)

### The Spore-Geotropism Connection

The critical observation is that sporeless mutants lack the negative [[spore-formation-geotropic-response-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]] entirely. This establishes a strong link between spore production and geotropic sensitivity, suggesting that:

1. A factor associated with spore formation (possibly a hormone linked to the lamellae) triggers geotropic sensitivity
2. Light is required not directly for geotropism, but because it is necessary for spore production
