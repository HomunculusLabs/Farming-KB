# Seed Germination Methods

Greg Green's Cannabis Grow Bible Chapter 3 covers propagation in detail,
including multiple methods for germinating seeds, transplanting
seedlings, and the logistics of planning a grow from seed to harvest.

## Ways to Germinate Your Seeds

Seeds can be germinated in several ways. Green recommends the rockwool
SBS propagation tray method for the highest success rate.

### Seed Soil Propagation

Seeds are placed in moist soil about 3mm (or roughly the length of the
seed) below the surface. The soil is kept moist, not soaking wet, by
sprinkling water over it once a day. This method has a moderate success
rate. Out of 10 seeds, typically 7 to 8 will germinate.

### Seed Towel Propagation

Seeds are placed on a damp towel, cotton wool, or cheesecloth and
covered with more damp material. The material must be kept moist at all
times. If it dries out, seeds may be damaged. Check daily for root
emergence. When roots appear, immediately transfer seedlings to a grow
medium using tweezers. Do not touch the root. This method has a moderate
to high success rate (8 to 9 out of 10), but transplant shock can kill
some seedlings.

### Propagation Kits (Rockwool SBS Tray)

The recommended method uses a seed or clone propagator, a miniature
greenhouse with small grow cubes (rockwool) placed in slots over a water
reservoir. Seeds are placed into tiny holes in the cubes and covered.
This has a very high success rate where all seeds can germinate.

The cost is modest: approximately $10 for the tray, $5 for rockwool
cubes, and $5 for grow fertilizers. The same tray can later be used for
rooting clones, making it a versatile investment.

## Dangers When Propagating Seeds

Several hazards can reduce germination success:

- Drafts are a killer and will stunt germination. Keep seeds away from
  open windows and fans.
- Cold rooms inhibit germination. Maintain warmth.
- High doses of fertilizers will burn seedlings. Water alone is usually
  sufficient for seedlings. If using germination solutions, keep
  concentrations very low.
- Do not disturb the soil to check on seeds. This can break or damage
  the seed and root (the term "digging up your seeds" refers to this).
- Some strains produce seedlings with weak stems that lean. Support
  them with a small stick and thread. Never tie thread above a growing
  shoot.
- Never bring a stick from outdoors indoors for support, as it may
  carry pests like spider mites in their incubation nests inside the
  wood.

If a plant has a weak stem during vegetative growth, give the base a
gentle shake each morning and evening. This simulates wind and helps
the plant develop a stronger, more solid stem.

## Seed Viability Requirements

Seeds must be viable to germinate successfully:

- Never use white seeds (they are immature).
- Choose seeds with white and grey markings or other colors.
- Crushed seeds will not germinate.
- Old seeds may struggle to germinate.
- Always use the best seeds you can find.

## Transplanting

Transplanting is done as early as possible during the transition from
germination to vegetative growth. Two main approaches exist:

### Rockwool Cube Transplant

When seedlings are ready in the rockwool SBS tray, lift the seedling
with its cube and place it into another grow medium such as soil. Dig a
small hole, place the cube in, and cover with soil. The cube will not
affect plant growth and adds structural support.

### Soil-to-Soil Transplant

Two methods for moving a plant from a smaller pot to a larger one:

1. Cut away the base of the small pot and place it into the bigger pot.
   Roots will grow down through the bottom hole into the new soil.

2. Let the soil dry out for a couple of days. Use a clean knife to cut
   around the inside of the pot. Push fingers down the side and lift
   the plant and soil out together. Quickly place into the larger pot
   and fill with soil. Water to help the plant take to new soil.

Critical rule: Never lift a plant by the stem. Always maintain a firm
grip on the soil during transplants.

## Transplant Shock

Plants may go into shock even after a clean transplant. Shock is caused
by disturbance of the roots. If roots are cut or fall away, the plant
responds poorly. To minimize shock:

- Always keep a firm hold of the soil when transplanting.
- Refrain from feeding for 1 week after transplanting.
- Keep the plant well maintained during the recovery period.

## Propagation Logistics

Green recommends staggered germination for beginners:

- New growers: Germinate 3 seeds first, then 3 more the next week,
  then the remaining 4 two weeks later.
- Experienced growers: Germinate 5, then 5 more the next week.
- Expert growers: All 10 at once.

This reduces the risk of losing all seeds to a failed germination
attempt. The approach depends on your grow space and budget.

## Scuffing Seeds

Some growers lightly scuff or sand the seed coat before germination to
help water penetrate. This technique can improve germination rates for
older or particularly hard-shelled seeds. However, care must be taken
not to damage the seed interior.
