## Selecting Grass Seed

Rye grass seed (*Lolium perenne*) is the most commonly used variety,
but mixtures of different grass species also work well. The critical
requirement is that the seed must **not** be treated with fungicides.
Treated seed is usually labelled as such. Inexpensive untreated seed
can often be sourced from animal feed stores, sold as bird feed.

## Substrate Preparation

The standard formula per 720 ml jar is:

- 110 grams grass seed
- 180 ml water

### Procedure

1. Measure grass seed and water into each 720 ml jar.
2. Place lids on the jars but **do not screw them tight**. The lids
   must remain loose to allow pressure equalisation during
   sterilisation.
3. Cover each jar with a double layer of aluminium foil, crumpled
   over the lid and upper portion of the jar.
4. The jars are now ready for sterilisation.

### Water Absorption Variability

Different varieties and even batches of the same variety can differ
greatly in water absorption. Too much water produces a slimy, unshakeable
clump; too little leaves the substrate dry with poor yields. Some
experimentation with water volumes is expected. As an alternative, the
seed can be soaked overnight in water before filling jars, producing a
more homogenous substrate with consistent moisture distribution.

## Sterilisation

A pressure cooker or autoclave is required. A normal boiling pot will
not achieve the temperatures needed to eliminate contaminants.

1. Place a layer of water in the bottom of the pressure cooker.
2. Set the jars inside, ensuring lids remain loose.
3. Sterilise at pressure for **1 hour** following the manufacturer's
   directions.
4. For larger containers (1.5 litre jars or spawn bags), extend
   sterilisation to **2 hours**.
5. Once depressurised, remove the jars and **shake** them to mix wet
   and dry kernels into a uniform substrate.
6. Allow jars to cool in a clean place. Check for cracks before
   shaking. Keep lids loose during cooling to prevent vacuum sealing.

## Inoculation

Once jars have cooled to room temperature, they are ready for
inoculation. Inoculating while jars are still hot can kill spores or
mycelium, so patience is essential.

### Inoculant Options

- **Spore syringe**: The most common approach for beginners.
- **Mycelium syringe**: Liquid culture with live mycelium; typically
  faster to colonise than spores.
- **Agar squares**: Small pieces of colonised agar transferred with a
  flame-sterilised scalpel.

### Clean Technique

Working clean is the single most important factor in avoiding
contamination. A simple cardboard box hood is sufficient to prevent
drafts. Flame the needle (syringes) or scalpel (agar) before each jar.
Open jar lids only a crack and work swiftly. After introducing the
inoculant, close the lid, shake to distribute, then loosen the lid
again so the mycelium can breathe. **Warning**: Alcohol and spray Lysol
are highly flammable. Keep away from open flames.

## Colonisation

Place inoculated jars in a clean, draft-free location at approximately
20 degrees C or slightly higher. When mycelium begins growing in only
a few spots, shake the jars to redistribute colonised kernels. This
dramatically accelerates colonisation. Depending on temperature, species,
and inoculation method, full colonisation takes 5 to 20 days. If jars
are incubated too long or at too high a temperature, the mycelium
excretes yellowish metabolite fluid as a stress signal; affected jars
should be cased or processed as soon as possible.

## Transition to Fruiting or Sclerotia

Once fully colonised, the substrate branches into two pathways.

### Casing for Mushroom Production

The colonised grass seed is shaken loose, poured into 1-litre plastic
trays, and covered with a thin (1.5 to 2.0 cm) layer of casing soil.
The casing recipe from this method is **10 parts peat, 5 parts
vermiculite, and 2 parts limestone (marl)**, mixed dry with water
added to field capacity, then sterilised for one hour. See
[[fruiting-chamber-setup]] for the simple plastic bin fruiting setup
with polyethylene sheeting described in the source material.

### In-Jar Sclerotia Production

For *P. tampanensis* and *P. mexicana*, sclerotia form within the
colonised substrate without casing or fruiting triggers. Jars are
stored in a clean, preferably dark location. Sclerotia continue to
enlarge for up to four months after inoculation. They may also form in
the casing layer of trays cased for mushroom production. See
[[grass-seed-substrate-species-compatibility]]
