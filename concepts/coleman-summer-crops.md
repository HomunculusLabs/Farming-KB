Coleman considers three crops most economically significant for
[[soil-moisture-management-and-root-growth]]. Seeds germinate in mini-blocks on heat
pads at 70 degrees Fahrenheit, then move to two-inch blocks after
seven to eight days. These remain on heating pads for ten days
before being potted into five-inch square pots, the only crop for
which Coleman uses pots rather than soil blocks. The extra soil
volume guarantees sturdy, well-rooted plants. Plants are started
only six weeks before greenhouse transplanting to keep them young
and manageable.

Tomatoes are grown in 30-inch-wide beds with 12-inch paths, the
same system used for all other crops. Eight beds fit in a 30-foot-
wide house, with the middle six planted to tomatoes. Edge beds,
which lack sufficient headspace for staked tomatoes, grow early
celery and Tuscan kale as companion crops. Plants are set 24 inches
apart down the center of the bed, with monthly topdressing of
compost to sustain long-season production through late fall.

## Vertical Growing and Trellising

Tomatoes are supported by plastic twine unrolling from small spools
attached to wire frames hanging from horizontal wires running the
greenhouse length. Plants are pruned to a single stem by removing
all suckers between the leaf branch and the stem. Commercial
tomato trellis clips secure the stem to the twine every 12 inches.
Fruit clusters are limited to four fruits on beefsteak varieties and
five on medium-sized types. Cherry tomato clusters are pinched at
the tip once the first fruit begins ripening.

When growing tips reach the support wire (about 8 feet high), the
lowest fruit has been harvested and lower branches are removed. The
twine is unrolled a turn or two, lowering the plant top about 12
inches and moving it horizontally along the support wire. At row
ends, plants curve around corners to partner rows moving in the
opposite direction, resembling circular trolleys in dry-cleaning
establishments. This lowering and shifting is repeated each time
tops reach the wire, keeping the top 8 feet of each vine producing
continuously from [[mollison-designers-passive-solar-heating-building-design]] of the
soil, then runs a propane heater for a few days before the April 7
transplant date to keep nights above 60 degrees Fahrenheit.
