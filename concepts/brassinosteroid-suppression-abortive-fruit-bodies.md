# Brassinosteroid Suppression of Abortive and Incomplete Fruit Bodies in Mushroom Cultivation

The Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt (1990) study reported that the formation of incomplete fruit bodies, described as "normally present in mushroom cultivation," was "almost completely suppressed" by treatment with 10^-2 ppm 22S,23S-homobrassinolide. This qualitative improvement in fruiting body development represents a distinct benefit beyond the quantitative increases in growth rate and total yield, addressing one of the persistent quality challenges in commercial mushroom production.





## The Problem of Abortive Fruit Bodies

Incomplete or abortive fruit bodies are a widespread phenomenon in mushroom cultivation affecting nearly all cultivated species. These structures begin development as primordia but fail to mature into harvestable mushrooms, instead arresting at various stages of development. Abortive fruit bodies represent a significant source of yield loss in commercial operations, consuming substrate nutrients and biological resources without producing a usable crop.

The prevalence of abortive fruiting varies considerably depending on species, strain, substrate quality, environmental conditions, and cultivation methodology. In some operations, abortive rates of 20 to 40% of initiated primordia are common, representing a substantial waste of the biological potential of the substrate. Factors contributing to fruit body abortion include inadequate humidity, temperature fluctuations, insufficient fresh air exchange, genetic instability of the strain, and competition between simultaneously developing primordia for limited resources.





## Mechanisms of Brassinosteroid-Mediated Suppression

Several mechanisms may explain the near-complete suppression of abortive fruit bodies by brassinosteroid treatment:

**Enhanced Resource Allocation:** Brassinosteroids may improve the efficiency of nutrient transport from the colonized substrate to developing primordia, ensuring that initiated fruit bodies receive sufficient resources to complete development. In plant systems, brassinosteroids are known to enhance phloem loading and sugar transport; analogous effects on fungal nutrient translocation could reduce resource competition between primordia.

**Strengthened Developmental Commitment:** The steroid may reinforce the developmental commitment of primordia once fruiting has been initiated, making the transition to mature fruit body more robust and less susceptible to disruption by environmental fluctuations. Brassinosteroids in plants upregulate genes involved in cell division and differentiation, which could similarly stabilize fungal developmental programs.

**Improved Synchrony of Development:** Brassinosteroids may promote more coordinated development of primordia, reducing the destructive competition that occurs when many primordia at different developmental stages compete simultaneously for limited resources. The observed increase in first flush fruit count (4 to 7 versus 1 to 3 in controls) suggests that treated cultures develop more primordia in a shorter time window, potentially allowing more efficient resource distribution.

**Stress Resistance Enhancement:** Brassinosteroids are well-documented stress mitigants in plants, improving tolerance to drought, temperature extremes, and pathogen attack. If brassinosteroids confer similar stress resistance to developing fungal fruit bodies, this could protect primordia from environmental perturbations that would otherwise cause abortion.





## Economic Significance of Abort Reduction

The economic impact of reducing abortive fruit body formation is twofold. First, more initiated primordia reaching maturity means higher harvestable yield from the same substrate, directly increasing the biological efficiency of the cultivation system. Second, the labor cost of harvesting is reduced when a higher proportion of developed structures are harvestable, as cultivators spend less time identifying and removing abortive material.

In commercial mushroom operations, the cost of substrate preparation, sterilization, inoculation, and incubation represents a fixed investment regardless of how many primordia ultimately mature. Maximizing the proportion of initiated primordia that develop into harvestable mushrooms therefore directly improves the return on this fixed investment. The "almost complete" suppression reported by Gartz and colleagues would represent a near-ideal outcome from an economic perspective.





## Relationship to Fruiting Body Quality

The suppression of abortive fruit bodies was accompanied by other quality improvements in the Gartz study, including increased dry mass per fruit body and more consistent fruit body development. This pattern suggests that brassinosteroids do not simply push more primordia past the abortion threshold but fundamentally improve the quality and consistency of the entire fruiting process.

The distinction between abort suppression and yield increase is important. Yield can be increased by producing more total primordia even if the abort rate remains the same, but this approach has diminishing returns as resource competition between primordia increases. Brassinosteroids appear to work through both mechanisms: increasing total primordia initiation and improving the maturation rate of those primordia, a combination that produces the dramatic yield increases observed.





## Implications for Cultivation Protocol Design

The abort-suppressing effect of brassinosteroids has implications for cultivation protocol design. If brassinosteroids can reliably reduce or eliminate abortive fruiting, cultivators may be able to simplify their environmental management protocols, reducing the need for precise humidity and temperature control during the critical primordia development phase. This would be particularly valuable for small-scale or low-tech cultivation operations where maintaining optimal environmental conditions is challenging.

However, the concentration and timing of brassinosteroid application would need to be optimized for each species and cultivation system. The single concentration tested in the Gartz study (10^-2 ppm) may not be optimal for all applications, and the most effective application timing (at inoculation, at fruiting initiation, or during primordia development) remains to be determined through systematic dose-response and timing studies.





## Comparison with Abort Reduction by Other Methods

Various approaches have been used to reduce abortive fruiting in mushroom cultivation. Environmental optimization (precise control of humidity, temperature, and CO2) is the most common approach and can reduce abort rates but requires significant infrastructure investment. Genetic improvement through strain selection can produce strains with lower abort rates but is a long-term strategy. Nutritional supplementation can improve fruit body development but may also increase the number of initiated primordia, potentially exacerbating resource competition.

Brassinosteroid treatment appears to offer a unique combination of benefits: effective abort reduction at extremely low concentrations, no apparent trade-off with growth rate or fruit body quality, and compatibility with standard cultivation methods. The hormonal mechanism of action suggests effects on fundamental developmental processes rather than environmental or nutritional optimization, potentially providing a more robust and broadly applicable solution.
