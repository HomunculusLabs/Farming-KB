# Peyote Cultivation from Seed

## Overview

Any cactus can be grown from either seed or cutting. Seed-grown plants can take many
years to develop to a usable size, but should ultimately provide strong, healthy stock
from which cuttings may be taken. Plants have to grow through the lengthy seedling
stage before reaching maturity.

## Seed Growth Rates for San Pedro

Gottlieb provides specific growth benchmarks for [[trichocereus-pachanoi]] (San Pedro)
started from seed, which illustrate the slow pace of cactus seedling development:

- **First year**: May be no more than 1/2 inch high, diameter 1/8-1/4 inch
- **Second year**: Perhaps 1 inch high, still 1/8-1/4 inch diameter
- By comparison, a cutting of San Pedro may be 2 feet high by 4 inches diameter
  when planted

## Growth from Cuttings

Cuttings provide a much faster path to usable plants compared to seed. A San Pedro
cutting planted in soil can show remarkable growth:

- After 6 months: may gain 4-6 inches in height
- Will send forth one or two branches 6-8 inches long by 2 inches diameter
- Will sprout several branch buds that will do the same within the next six months
- Offshoots of 6 inches or more may be broken off and planted
- Or allowed another 6 months growth to deepen from pale to dark-green and
  accumulate alkaloids before consumption

## Selecting Peyote Cuttings for Planting

Freshly harvested peyote cuttings are frequently available on the underground market
for 50 cents to one dollar per button. When selecting peyote cuttings for planting:

- Choose ones which are firm and unbruised
- Ensure at least 1/2 inch of taproot below the top
- If the bottom of the taproot is still delicate where it has been cut, place the
  button bottoms up in partial shade for a day or two
- Wait until the severed area has a dry corky texture before planting
- If this is not done, the plant will be prone to rot

## Soil Preparation

The [[query-what-is-the-best-soil-mix-for-growing-cannabis]] can be prepared from:

- 3 parts coarse sand
- 1 part loam
- 1 part [[comparison-leaf-mold-composting-vs-hot-composting-method]]

Bake this mixture in an oven at 400 degrees F for an hour to kill fungus, bacteria,
weed seeds and insect eggs. After the soil mix has cooled it is ready to use.

## Rooting and Planting

- The taproot of the plant may be dipped in a rooting mixture such as ROOTONE before
  planting
- This enhances root development and hinders decay
- Place the bottom just deep enough so that the soil does not quite touch the green
  part of the plant
- The soil should be kept slightly moist and evenly so
- For tall cacti like San Pedro, the cutting should be placed deeply enough in the
  soil for sufficient support to stand
- San Pedro type cacti can also be laid upon the ground and will send down roots
  from their sides while the buds grow upwards
- San Pedro can grow well in almost any soil as long as there is decent drainage

## Watering Schedule

Cacti tend to follow a seasonal growth pattern:

- Grow mostly during spring and autumn
- Send down roots in the summer
- Rest through the winter

Watering recommendations:

- Although cactus cuttings may be planted anytime of the year, they stand the best
  chance if planted in late spring
