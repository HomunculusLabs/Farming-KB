## Mushroom Mites (Tyroglyphus lintneri)

Minute arthropods found invariably in manure. They feed upon both mushrooms and, more
critically, upon mushroom spawn. In large numbers they may entirely prevent mushroom
production.

**Biology**: Multiply rapidly when [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]], known in France for several
generations. Extensively studied by Costantin and Dufour in France and Veihmeyer in the
United States. Infection causes characteristic deformities, discoloration, and wet rotting
of fruiting bodies, spreading rapidly under warm, humid conditions.

**Prevention and control**:
- Remove all old compost, soil, and casing material between crops
- White-wash boards and walls with stone lime after drying
- Spray with strong copper sulphate solution as an alternative
- Fumigate with hydrocyanic acid or sulphur fumes
- Never reuse old compost or casing material in subsequent crops — this is a source of
  great danger, increasing disease and insect injury many times over

## Post-Crop Cleanup Protocol

Duggar outlined a comprehensive protocol: dump beds and remove all old material (excellent
for gardens but never for mushroom reuse); remove all wooden supports and boards; clean
walls and floors thoroughly; when dry, white-wash or spray with copper sulphate; optionally
fumigate. Mid-winter bearing beds usually cease production with warm May/June weather.
