## Mushroom Growing Reference

Complete practical reference synthesizing all cultivation sources into one actionable page. See [[mushroom-cultivation-methods]] for method selection and [[mushroom-contamination-identification]] for problem-solving.

## Spawn Types

| Type | Use Case | Shelf Life | Notes |
|------|----------|-----------|-------|
| **Grain spawn** (rye, millet, WBS) | Inoculating bulk substrates, grain-to-grain transfer | 2-4 months (refrigerated) | Most versatile. See [[mushroom-substrates]]. |
| **Sawdust spawn** | Log inoculation, outdoor beds | 4-6 months | Preferred for outdoor and log methods |
| **Plug spawn** (wooden dowels) | Log inoculation | 6-12 months | Easiest for beginners doing log cultivation |
| **Spore syringe** | PF TEK inoculation, agar germination | 6-12 months (refrigerated) | Genetic variability; clone via agar for consistency |
| **Liquid culture** | Grain inoculation (fast), agar transfer | 1-3 months (refrigerated) | Faster colonization than spores; must be clean |

## Inoculation Techniques

**Spore syringe (PF TEK)**: 1cc per 1/2 pint jar. Insert needle through inoculation holes, tilt syringe so solution runs down inner jar wall. 4 holes per jar lid. Flame-sterilize needle between jars. Wipe needle with alcohol-soaked cloth after flaming, wait 15-20 seconds to cool.

**Grain-to-grain (G2G)**: In still air box, shake colonized grain jar to loosen kernels. Open donor jar, pour 1/4-1/3 cup grain into each recipient jar. Flame sterilize jar rims. G2G is exponentially faster than spore germination (colonizes in 5-10 days vs 3-4 weeks).

**Agar wedge transfer**: Cut 3-5mm wedge from clean growth front on agar plate. Place face-down on fresh plate or into grain jar. Best for isolating clean genetics away from contamination.

## Colonization Conditions

| Parameter | Range | Optimal | Notes |
|-----------|-------|---------|-------|
| **Temperature** | 65-85F | 75-80F | Most species; reishi prefers 80-85F |
| **Humidity** | 60-80% | 70%+ | Jars retain moisture; don't over-saturate |
| **Gas exchange** | Passive | Via filter | CO2 buildup slow; polyfill or filter disc lids |
| **Light** | Dark preferred | Ambient OK | Darkness during colonization; light not harmful |
| **Duration** | 1-8 weeks | Varies by species/method | PF TEK: 2-3 wk; grain: 2-4 wk; sawdust: 3-6 wk |

**Key rules**: Never open jars during colonization. Shake grain jars at 20-30% colonization to redistribute (except PF TEK — do not shake). Incubate in a clean area away from drafts. Elevated CO2 from mycelial respiration is beneficial during colonization.

## Fruiting Triggers

Mushrooms fruit when environmental cues signal favorable conditions. Master these four triggers:

### 1. Fresh Air Exchange (FAE)
Most important trigger. Introducing fresh air drops CO2 levels from thousands of ppm to under 1000 ppm. Causes a shift from vegetative mycelial growth to reproductive fruiting. Oss & Oeric identified this as the primary fruiting trigger. Provide passive FAE via holes in fruiting chamber, or active via small fan pointed away from cultures.

### 2. Light
Phototropic response directs mushroom growth. Badham (1980) confirmed light is a fruiting initiator for P. cubensis. 12-hour on/off cycle at 6500K (daylight). Indirect light is fine — direct sun is too intense. Light quality matters: blue spectrum (370-420nm) most effective for primordia formation.

### 3. Temperature Drop
A 5-10F drop signals seasonal change. Cold shocking (refrigerate for 24-72 hours) initiates fruiting for shiitake on logs. Most indoor methods achieve this naturally by moving from warm incubation (80F) to room temperature fruiting (70-75F).

### 4. Humidity
High relative humidity (85-95%) is required for primordia formation and fruit development. Without adequate humidity, pins abort and caps crack. SGFC with perlite, monotub with polyfill, or Martha tent with humidifier achieve this.

## Fruiting Chamber Parameters

| Chamber | Humidity | FAE | Cost | Best For |
|---------|---------|-----|------|----------|
| SGFC (shotgun) | 85-95% (perlite) | Passive (drilled holes) | $10-20 | PF TEK cakes |
| Monotub | 85-95% | Passive (polyfill holes) | $15-30 | Bulk substrate |
| Martha tent | 85-95% (humidifier) | Active (fan + timer) | $60-150 | Multiple species |

## Harvesting

**When to harvest**: Pick just before or as the veil tears (for caps that have veils). Harvest when caps are fully expanded for species without veils. Timing affects potency and texture — younger fruits are firmer.

**Harvest technique**: Twist gently at base or cut with sterilized scissors. Do not pull — can damage substrate. Harvest all mushrooms from a flush at once to allow next flush to begin.

**Drying**: Dehydrate at 95-115F (food dehydrator) until cracker-dry (snap when bent). Store in airtight jars with silica gel desiccant. Dark, cool location. Properly dried mushrooms last 1-2 years.

## Species-Specific Quick Tips

### Oyster (Pleurotus spp.)
Easiest species. Tolerates imperfect sterile technique. Grows on straw, sawdust, paper, coffee grounds. No casing needed. Requires high FAE — poor formation under high CO2. Multiple varieties: blue, golden, pink, king. Biological efficiency 50-100% on straw.

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)
Best on oak logs (traditional) or supplemented sawdust blocks. Requires cold shock for log fruiting (submerge 24-72 hours in cold water). Two main strain types: cold-weather (fruit at 50-65F) and warm-weather (fruit at 65-80F). Log production lasts 3-5 years. Always cook before eating.

### Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)
Requires supplemented sawdust (oak, maple, beech) with 15-20% bran. Very sensitive to CO2 — needs excellent FAE. Poor air produces elongated, coral-like formations. Prized culinary (lobster flavor) and medicinal (NGF stimulation).

### Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)
Prefers warmer temperatures (75-85F). High CO2 produces antler form; normal CO2 produces conk form. Slow grower (8-12 weeks to fruit). Harvest when white margin disappears. Best for extraction (tea, tincture) rather than fresh eating.

### Wine Cap / King Stropharia (S. rugoso-annulata)
Outdoor specialist. No sterilization needed. Straw or wood chips 4-6 inches deep. Pasteurize straw at 160F for 20-30 min. Casing with peat/humus (1:1) at pH 5.7-6.0. Fruits spring and fall. 22% protein (dry weight). Excellent for garden pathways and mycofiltration.

### Psilocybe cubensis
PF TEK or monotub. BRF cakes or grain spawn to bulk. Colonizes at 75-80F. Fruits at 70-80F. Light initiates primordia (Badham 1980). High humidity essential. Veil-tear harvest timing critical. Spore syringes are standard inoculum. Dry and store cracker-dry.

### Panaeolus cyanescens and P. tropicalis
Coprophilous species requiring pasteurized dung-based substrate. Substrate: dried cow dung + vermiculite + soaked straw (1:3:0.75 ratio dry). Sterilize 2 hours at 121C. Inoculate with rye grain spawn. Case with 1-1.5cm sterilized soil after full colonization. Very sensitive to CO2 — tall spindly mushrooms with small caps if air exchange insufficient. Best at 25C+. Clone best performers from multispore for reliable results.

### Outdoor Wood-Lover Species (P. azurescens, P. cyanescens, P. ovoideocystidiata)
Outdoor beds in shaded locations. Wood chip spawn preferred over direct grain inoculation (grain attracts pests). Bed depth 15-20cm, spawn ratio 1:3 to 1:5. Cover with wet cardboard + plastic during summer. Remove plastic in September, increase watering. Fruit at 7-15C in late autumn. See [[outdoor-wood-lover-cultivation]] for detailed guide.

## Troubleshooting Quick Reference

| Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| No growth after 2 weeks | Dead spores / wrong temp | Try new syringe; verify 70-80F |
| Slow colonization | Too cold / too wet / old spawn | Raise temp; check moisture; use fresh spawn |
| Green mold (Trichoderma) | Contamination | See [[mushroom-contamination-identification]] |
| Fluffy mycelium, no pins | Too high CO2 / too high temp | Increase FAE; lower temp 5-10F |
| Pins aborting | Low humidity / touching / CO2 | Raise humidity; don't handle; increase FAE |
| Small mushrooms | Insufficient substrate / genetics | Use more substrate; clone good performers |
| Fuzzy stems, small caps | High CO2 | Increase FAE significantly |
| Splitting caps | Low humidity / rapid growth | Raise humidity gradually |
