## Cannabis Polyhybrid Crosses and F1 Stability

[[vic-high]]'s breeding text addresses the genetics underlying hybrid crosses and the challenges of maintaining stability when traits are controlled by multiple genes. While the term "polyhybrid" is not used explicitly, the principles governing complex multi-gene inheritance are central to the text's practical breeding advice and directly explain why complex crosses are difficult to stabilise.

## F1 Hybrids: Uniform but Not True Breeding

An F1 cross is the first-generation offspring of two distinct parents. In Vic High's framework, when a special individual (P1 mom) is crossed with an unrelated male, the F1 generation receives 50% of its genes from each parent.

If one parent is homozygous for a recessive trait (pp) and the other is homozygous dominant (PP), all F1 offspring are identical heterozygotes (Pp). The F1 is phenotypically uniform -- all individuals look the same -- but genetically heterozygous and not true breeding.

For a recessive trait, none of the F1 offspring will express it, even though the gene frequency is 50%. The trait is present but hidden in every individual.

## The Clone Preservation Dilemma

Vic High frames a key question that every breeder must answer before starting: what exactly do you want from the seeds you are going to create?

1. **Reproducing individuals like the clone**: Simple backcrossing (cubing) will accomplish this. The seeds will produce plants that resemble the original clone with high probability.

2. **Creating seeds that produce more seeds like the clone** (a true [[cannabis-genetic-stability]]. Only generational inbreeding with selection can produce a self-sustaining true breeding strain.

Vic High emphasises that a special clone is likely heterozygous for many of the traits she phenotypically expresses. She contains genetic information for two opposing traits, but only the dominant one is visible. Her seeds will only get one or the other of each gene, so her offspring will express all the genetic information she has, including [[what-a-plant-knows-photoperiodism-flowering-time-measurement]] -- are controlled by multiple gene pairs (polygenic), not single genes. Vic High demonstrates how this complexity compounds using concrete probability calculations.

### Single Recessive Gene

- 87.5% of cubed offspring express the desired trait and are true breeding.
- Gene frequency: approximately 94%.

### Two Recessive Genes

- Each gene individually fixes at 87.5%.
- Combined probability: 87.5% x 87.5% = 76.6%.
- Nearly 10 percentage points lost by adding just one more gene.

### Two Recessive Genes Plus One Heterozygous Dominant Gene

- Combined: 87.5% x 87.5% x 71.9% = 55%.
- Going from one gene to three genes drops the [[maitake-fruiting-body-success-rate-management]] from 87.5% to 55%.

### The Pattern

Each additional gene pair multiplies the probabilities together. This is why Vic High states that cubing is "a good technique where you want to increase the frequency of a few genes" but becomes increasingly ineffective as trait complexity grows. The breeder must account for this multiplication when planning projects.

## Choosing Between Cubing and Generational Inbreeding

For complex polygenic traits, Vic High's models consistently show that generational inbreeding with full-sib selection outperforms cubing.

### Single Recessive Gene Comparison

- Cubing: 88% true breeding after 3 backcrosses.
- Full-sib generational inbreeding: 100% true breeding by F3 (fewer generations).

### The Heterozygous Source Problem

When the parent being preserved carries heterozygous dominant traits (common with complex polygenic traits), neither strategy achieves full stabilization through brute force alone:

- Cubing: gene frequency caps at 50% for each heterozygous dominant gene, regardless of how many backcrosses are performed. This means 25% true breeding maximum.
- Generational inbreeding: requires more generations but benefits enormously from full-sib selection, reaching 96% phenotypic uniformity by F5.

Vic High states bluntly: "as long as the cubed source is heterozygous, it doesn't matter how many backcrosses you do, you will never achieve a true breeding strain." This makes generational inbreeding with selection the only viable path for complex trait stabilization.
