# Persimmon as Ornamental Fruit Tree in Permaculture

The persimmon (Diospyros kaki), also known as Chinese persimmon or
kakee, is a deciduous, round-headed tree growing to about 5 metres.
Belonging to the family Ebenaceae, it has strikingly coloured autumn
foliage that makes it an attractive ornamental tree as well as a
productive fruit producer. Persimmons are valued in [[mollison-town-permaculture-urban-food-systems]] for their dual role as landscape beautification and food
production.

## Growing Conditions

Persimmons prefer a medium loam soil but will grow well in most
ordinary soils as long as they are kept moist. They do not thrive in dry
or acid soils but will withstand a certain amount of waterlogging. The
mature plant is frost hardy to -10 degrees C. Persimmons need plenty of
water during the growing season and benefit from |mulching
to keep roots moist. Minimum spacing between plants is 5 metres.

## Fruit Characteristics

The fruit is orange in colour, slightly larger than a tomato, and must
be fully ripe before being eaten. The unripe fruit is very astringent
and contains tannic acid. It can be eaten fresh, dried, or made into
jams or jellies. There are different cultivars available, some being
astringent varieties and others being non-astringent. Persimmons begin
to bear fruit when about 3 years old from grafted trees or trees grown
from cuttings.

## Nutritional Composition

Chinese persimmon per 100 grams:
- Water: 78.6%, Energy: 77 calories
- Carbohydrate: 19.7 g, Fat: 0.4 g, Protein: 0.7 g
- Calcium: 6 mg, Potassium: 174 mg, Phosphorus: 26 mg
- Vitamin A: 2710 I.U., Vitamin C: 11 mg
- Digestion time: 3.75 hours

American persimmon (D. virginiana) per 100 grams:
- Water: 64.4%, Energy: 127 calories
- Carbohydrate: 33.5 g, Fat: 0.4 g, Protein: 0.8 g
- Calcium: 27 mg, Potassium: 310 mg
- Iron: 2.5 mg, Vitamin C: 66 mg
- Digestion time: 3.75 hours

The American persimmon is notably higher in calories, carbohydrates,
calcium, potassium, and vitamin C than the Chinese persimmon.

## Pollination

Persimmons are dioecious, requiring male and female plants, although in
some cases male and female flowers are found on the same tree. Female
flowers will produce fruit without pollination, but these will be
seedless and take longer to ripen. Under some conditions, fruiting will
be biennial, a tendency that can be managed through pruning and thinning.

## Propagation

They are propagated from seed, by grafting, or from cuttings. Seedling
trees grow tall and make good shade trees. Seedlings tend to be variable
and often do not grow true to type, so grafting named cultivars onto
seedling rootstock is the preferred method for consistent fruit quality.

## Species and Varieties

### Diospyros kaki (Chinese/Japanese Persimmon)
The primary cultivated species worldwide. Produces the largest fruit
with the widest range of cultivars for both astringent and
non-astringent types.

### Diospyros virginiana (American Persimmon)
Fruits are smaller than the Chinese persimmon, usually yellow but can
be dark red or purple-red. Much higher vitamin C content (66 mg vs 11
mg per 100 g). Native to eastern and central North America.

### Diospyros lotus (Date Plum)
Fruits are smaller still (1-2 cm) and purple-black when ripe. A more
cold-hardy species suitable for marginal climates.

### Diospyros digyna (Black Sapote / Chocolate Pudding Fruit)
An evergreen native of Central America with very sweet fruit and
chocolate brown flesh. The flavour and texture resemble chocolate
pudding, making it a unique and desirable fruit for subtropical
systems.

## Ornamental Value

The striking autumn foliage colours make persimmons excellent
ornamental plantings. In [[comparison-food-forest-design-vs-backyard-orchard-design]], they serve the dual function of providing seasonal visual
interest and productive fruit harvest. Seedling trees, which grow
taller than grafted trees, make particularly good shade trees for
outdoor living areas.

## [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]]

Persimmons integrate well in mixed plantings with other fruit trees.
Their relatively small mature size (5 metres) allows them to fit into
the mid-canopy layer of a food forest. Their moisture requirements
mean they pair well with ground covers that help retain soil moisture.
They can be combined with |figs,
olives, and other Mediterranean-adapted species in warm temperate
designs.

## Design Considerations

- Space requirement: minimum 5 metres between plants
- Water needs: regular moisture during growing season
- Soil preference: medium loam, avoid dry or acidic soils
- Cold tolerance: mature plants to -10 degrees C
- Bear fruit at 3 years from grafted trees
- Long productive life once established
- Dual function: ornamental beauty plus food production
- Excellent for adding autumn colour to permaculture landscapes
