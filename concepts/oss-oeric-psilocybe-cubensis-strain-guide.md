## Oss & Oeric: Psilocybe Cubensis Strain Guide

The 1976 guide focused exclusively on [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. Key identifying features include:

**Cap:** 1.5-8 cm broad, conic to bell-shaped when young, becoming
convex and eventually flat with upturned edges in age. Surface is viscid
and hairless, whitish to pale yellow, light brownish in age. The flesh
is firm and white, bruising blue when damaged. The cap stains bluish
with age, a characteristic enzymatic oxidation of indole substrates.

**Gills:** Adnate to adnexed (attached to notched), close spacing,
grey becoming violet-grey with white edges in maturity.

**Stem (Stipe):** 4-15 cm long, 4-14 mm thick, slightly enlarging
toward the base. Surface is dry and hairless, white, staining blue
when bruised. A persistent membranous annulus (veil remnant) is present
on the upper stem.

**Spores:** 10-17 x 7-10 micrometers, elliptical to oval in side view,
thick-walled with a large pore at the apex. Spore print is purple-brown.
Cystidia on the gill edge are club-shaped with rounded heads.

**Chemical Tests:** The blue-staining reaction can be confirmed with
metol (p-methyl aminophenol), a photographic darkroom compound that
turns deep purple within 1-30 minutes when applied to crushed stem
tissue. This test distinguishes psilocybin-containing species from
non-psychoactive blue-staining genera like Boletus and Russula. See
[[psilocybe-cubensis-biogeography-columbian-exchange-hypothesis]]
for the biogeographical debate.

## Genetic Complexity

The guide provides a thorough explanation of the species mating system.
Members of the family Strophariaceae are heterothallic and tetrapolar,
meaning [[dikaryon-formation-mushroom-strain-genetics]] for the
genetics of dikaryotic mycelium formation.

The dikaryotic mycelium, containing two nuclei per cell, is the most
prolonged stage of the life cycle and serves as the main assimilative
stage. It can propagate vegetatively indefinitely without sexual
reproduction, but given appropriate conditions, it differentiates into
the spore-bearing basidiocarp (mushroom).

## Related Genera and Species

The guide notes that [[guzman-magic-mushrooms-african-species]] include members of the genera
Psilocybe, Stropharia, Conocybe, Panaeolus, and Copelandia, all
containing psilocybin and psilocin as active agents. These compounds
share the basic indole structure characteristic of natural hallucinogens.
