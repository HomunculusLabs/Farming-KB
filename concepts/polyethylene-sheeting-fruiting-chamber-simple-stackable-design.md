### Hole Placement and Sizing

The melted holes in the bin sides provide passive air exchange driven by:
- **Concentration gradients**: CO₂ diffuses out through the holes while fresh air diffuses in.
- **Temperature gradients**: Slight temperature differences between the chamber interior and exterior create gentle convection currents that move air through the holes.
- **Evaporative currents**: Water evaporating from the casing surface creates local air movement that helps distribute gases within the chamber.

The holes should be positioned near the top of the bin because CO₂ is heavier than air and will tend to accumulate at the bottom. Upper holes encourage CO₂ to escape while drawing fresh air in from the bottom edges where the polyethylene sheeting does not seal perfectly.

## Stackability and Space Efficiency

### Vertical Stacking

The use of stackable bins is a significant advantage for cultivators with limited space. The guide emphasizes that these bins are "stackable and thus very space efficient." Practical considerations for stacking include:

- **Weight limits**: Each bin with five trays of cased substrate can be heavy. Ensure the bottom bin can support the weight of bins above it.
- **Access**: Stacked bins require the cultivator to lift upper bins to access lower ones. Arrange the stack so that the most mature casings are on top and the most recently cased trays are on the bottom.
- **Air exchange**: Stacking may partially block the side holes. Ensure that holes are not blocked by the bin above. Alternating hole positions between bins (e.g., holes on the left for even-numbered bins, right for odd) can help maintain air exchange in stacked configurations.

### Production Capacity

With five trays per bin and stackable design, a cultivator can scale production linearly with available vertical space. A typical shelf or corner could accommodate 4–6 stacked bins, representing 20–30 cased trays and the potential for continuous harvest across multiple flush cycles.

## Maintenance and Monitoring

### Daily Tasks
- **Mist casing surfaces**: Light misting to maintain moisture without overwatering.
- **Visual inspection**: Check for contamination, abnormal growth patterns, or insect activity through the clear bin walls.
- **Remove aborts**: Dead pinheads that have stopped growing should be removed promptly to prevent mold colonization.
- **Harvest mature mushrooms**: Pick mushrooms at the appropriate maturity stage (before veil tearing for most species).
