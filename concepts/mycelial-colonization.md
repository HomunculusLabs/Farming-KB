# Mycelial Colonization

Mycelial colonization is the phase in [[mushroom-spawn-production]] and precedes [[substrate-contamination]].
- **Ropy, thick growth** — vigorous mycelium forms thick strands that bind
  the substrate tightly. Thin, wispy, or cottony growth may indicate
  suboptimal genetics or environmental stress.
- **Mushroom or earthy aroma** — a clean, pleasant smell confirms healthy
  metabolism. Sour, sweet, or putrid odors signal bacterial contamination.
- **Even colonization front** — uniform advancement across the substrate
  surface. Patchy or stalled areas may indicate compaction, dry zones, or
  localized contamination.

## Troubleshooting Stalled Colonization

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| No growth after 5 days | Dead spawn or wrong temp | Re-inoculate; check temp |
| Growth stops at 50% | Contamination block | Discard; improve pasteurization |
| Slow, thin growth | Low nutrition or low temp | Supplement; raise temp |
| Premature pinning | Light or FAE exposure | Darken; seal container |
| Wet spots, slime | Bacterial contamination | Discard; reduce moisture |

## Speed Optimization

Commercial growers optimize colonization speed because faster turnaround means
more production cycles per year. Key levers:

- Use liquid culture instead of grain spawn for faster, more uniform
  inoculation.
- Maintain substrate at the upper end of the optimal temperature range.
- Use high inoculation rates (10-15%) for fast-growing species.
- Ensure substrate particle size is uniform — fine sawdust (2-5mm) colonizes
  faster than coarse chips.
- Pre-wet substrate 12-24 hours before pasteurization to ensure even
  moisture distribution.
