# First Flush Acceleration by Brassinosteroid Treatment

## Overview

First flush acceleration refers to the significant reduction in time to first fruiting observed when the brassinosteroid 22S,23S-homobrassinolide is added to fungal cultivation substrates. In the original Gartz (1990) study on *[[gadd-mycelial-network-dynamics]] to colonize the substrate more rapidly and reach full maturity sooner. On malt agar, the study observed mycelial growth occurring "two to three times faster" than in controls.
- **Metabolic priming**: The steroid appears to shift the mycelium into a reproductive state more quickly, possibly by modulating gene expression related to fruiting body initiation. The earlier transition from vegetative to reproductive growth is the direct cause of the accelerated first flush.
- **Substrate utilization**: Faster colonization may also reflect improved enzymatic activity for substrate degradation, making nutrients available sooner for fruiting. The KH₂PO₄ supplementation in the dung/grain substrate provides additional phosphate for ATP-driven metabolic processes.
- **Stress reduction**: Brassinosteroids are known to have anti-stress effects in plants. If similar protective effects occur in fungi, the treated mycelium may experience less metabolic stress during colonization, allowing it to reach reproductive maturity more efficiently.

## Quantitative Results from Gartz (1990)

The study used dung/grain substrate inoculated with *Psilocybe cubensis* mycelium and compared brassinosteroid-treated cultures against untreated controls. Each condition was run for ten replicates to ensure statistical reliability:

| Parameter | Control | Brassinosteroid-Treated |
|-----------|---------|------------------------|
| Time to first flush | 4–5 weeks | 3–3.5 weeks |
| First flush yield (mushrooms) | 1–3 per flush | 4–7 per flush |
| Total dry mass | 2.3–2.9 g / 10 g substrate | 3.4–3.9 g / 10 g substrate |
| Incomplete fruiting bodies | Normally present | Almost completely suppressed |
| Annulus (ring) on stems | Present | Absent |

The brassinosteroid concentration used was 10⁻² ppm (0.01 ppm), applied at [[annulus-loss-morphological-effect-brassinosteroid-treatment]], while not directly relevant to cultivation yield, provides additional evidence that brassinosteroids interact with fundamental developmental pathways in fungi.
