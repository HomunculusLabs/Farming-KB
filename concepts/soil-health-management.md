## Soil Health Management

A healthy, fertile soil is the basis of the organic approach
to gardening. Soil management can be divided into four
phases: testing the soil; general conditioning with organic
matter; applying general fertilizer; and using specific
fertilizers for particular plant needs.

## Soil Types and Their Management

### Clay Soil
Clay is badly drained, cold, and heavy because the spaces
between particles are too small for free passage of water
and air. When wet it becomes a soggy mess; when dry it sets
like concrete. However, well-managed clay can grow far
better crops than sandy soil ever could.

**Digging:** Dig in the fall, leaving the surface rough to
expose maximum soil to winter weather. Never walk on wet
clay; lay boards down first if you must cross it.

**Drainage:** Add sufficient lime to encourage flocculation,
binding tiny clay particles into larger crumbs. Dig in one
to two bucketfuls of coarse sand per square yard/meter.

**[[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] holds clay
particles apart. After a few years, every plant you grow
improves the soil for the [[knf-leaf-mold-humus]]:** Add substantial amounts of bulky organic
material each year. Maintain a vegetation cover, especially
in winter when leaching is most rapid. Use mulch between
plants to lower evaporation and improve structure.

### Chalk Soil
Chalk is thin, dry, hungry, and very alkaline. Water drains
rapidly, taking nutrients with it. Few plants tolerate the
high pH.

**Digging:** Generally dry enough to work even in winter.
Dig in spring before sowing. Keep digging shallow since
topsoil is usually thin.

**[[humus]]:** Keep the soil surface covered year-round.
Grow [[green-manure]] crops in winter. Use acid materials like
peat, grass cuttings, or compost as mulch to counteract
alkalinity.

### Silt Soil
Silt has the smallest particles of any soil formed by
grinding, leading to poor drainage similar to clay.

**Digging:** Cultivate only when dry enough. Dig in fall to
take advantage of winter weather breaking the soil down.

**Organic matter:** Liberal quantities of compost or manure
hold particles apart for better drainage. Keep soil covered
with a green-manure crop in winter.

### Peat Soil
Peat is dark, spongy, and rich in decomposed organic
matter. It is potentially very fertile and usually easy to
work but tends to be acidic and may need drainage.

## The Four Phases of Soil Management

### Phase 1: Soil Analysis
Have soil tested professionally, especially for new gardens.
Test pH annually. Soils uncultivated for years may be
grossly deficient in essential elements.
