# Mushroom Cultivator Species Growing Parameters Overview

## The Parameter Framework

Chapter XI of *The Mushroom Cultivator* presents comprehensive growing parameters
for the major cultivated mushroom species. Each species profile follows a standard
format covering every stage from spawn run through cropping, allowing cultivators to
precisely dial in environmental conditions. The parameters were established through
countless trials by home and commercial cultivators and represent optimal ranges
for maximizing fruitbody production.

Mushrooms fruit in response to unique sets of conditions involving nutrition
(substrate), temperature, pH, relative humidity, light, and carbon dioxide. By
adhering to the optima for each stage in a species' life cycle, cultivators can
maximize yields in a precise and deliberate fashion.

## Stages of the Growing Cycle

Each species profile covers the following stages, each with specific environmental
parameters:

### Spawn Run
- **Relative humidity:** 90–100%
- **Substrate temperature:** species-specific range
- **CO2:** typically 5,000–10,000 ppm
- **Fresh air exchanges:** 0 per hour
- **Duration:** 1–4 weeks depending on species

### Post-Casing / Pre-Pinning
- **Relative humidity:** 90–100%
- **Bed temperature:** species-specific (often lower than spawn run)
- **Duration:** 10–14 days
- **CO2:** 5,000–10,000 ppm

### Primordia Formation (Pinning)
- **Relative humidity:** 95–100%
- **Temperature:** specific drop or shift triggers fruiting
- **Light:** species-specific (none for some, ambient for others)
- **CO2:** below 2,000 ppm (dramatic reduction from spawn run)
- **Fresh air exchanges:** 2–4 per hour

### Cropping
- **Relative humidity:** 85–92%
- **Air temperature:** species-specific
- **CO2:** below 3,000 ppm
- **Fresh air exchanges:** 2–4 per hour
- **Flush interval:** species-specific (7–10 days typical)

## Key Species and Their Distinguishing Parameters

### Agaricus bitorquis (Rodman's Agaricus)
A warm-weather species offering commercial growers flexibility in warmer climates.
Resistant to virus disease that attacks *A. brunnescens* and tolerant of high CO2.
Spawn run at 84–86°F; primordia at 77–80°F with no light required. Thermal death
at 93°F prolonged. Double annulus distinguishes it from *A. campestris*.

### Agaricus brunnescens (White Button Mushroom)
The standard commercial species. Prefers cooler temperatures than *A. bitorquis*.
Requires compost-based substrates balanced to 71–74% moisture. More sensitive to
virus but produces the familiar button mushroom valued in commercial markets.

### Pleurotus ostreatus (Oyster Mushroom)
A primary decomposer tolerant of a wide range of substrates including straw and
wood. One of the easiest species for home cultivation. Requires light for
primordia formation and benefits from fresh air exchange during all stages. Can
fruit on a wide range of temperatures depending on strain.

### Lentinus edodes (Shiitake)
A wood-decomposing species traditionally grown on supplemented sawdust or logs.
Requires a longer spawn run than most species and a distinct cold shock for
fruiting initiation. The browning of the mycelium before fruiting is a critical
developmental signal.

### Psilocybe cubensis
A subtropical species requiring warm temperatures throughout. Primordia formation
triggered by lowering temperature and increasing fresh air exchange. Light
required for normal fruitbody development and directional orientation.

### Stropharia rugoso-annulata (Wine Cap)
A robust garden mushroom thriving in outdoor wood chip beds. Tolerant of a wide
range of environmental conditions and competitive against weeds and other fungi.
Well-suited to permaculture applications.

## Environmental Control Parameters

### Temperature
The single most critical parameter for each species. Most mushrooms have distinct
optimal temperature ranges for mycelial growth vs. fruiting, and the transition
between these ranges (the "temperature shock") often triggers primordia formation.
Thermal death limits define the upper boundary — exceeding these kills the
mycelium.

### Carbon Dioxide (CO2)
CO2 levels serve as a primary fruiting trigger. During spawn run, high CO2
(5,000–10,000 ppm) encourages mycelial colonization. For pinning, CO2 must drop
below 2,000 ppm. During cropping, levels are maintained below 3,000 ppm. This
dramatic reduction in CO2, combined with increased fresh air exchange, is the
primary environmental signal that initiates the transition from vegetative to
reproductive growth.

### Relative Humidity
Near-saturation humidity (90–100%) is required during spawn run, casing
colonization, and primordia formation. During cropping, humidity is lowered to
85–92% to prevent bacterial blotch and other moisture-related diseases while
still providing sufficient moisture for fruitbody expansion.

### Light
Requirements vary dramatically by species. *Agaricus* species require no light
for fruiting. *Pleurotus* and *Psilocybe* species require ambient light for
normal primordia formation and cap development. Light intensity, spectrum, and
photoperiod all influence fruitbody morphology.

## The p-Value Strain Aging System

Stamets introduced the p-value system for tracking strain age and vigor. As
mycelium is successively transferred from one medium to another (subcultured),
it ages and gradually loses vigor. The p-value quantifies this generational
distance from the original isolation, helping cultivators plan for strain
replacement before yield declines become significant.

## Practical Application

The parameter tables serve as both a planning tool and a diagnostic reference.
When cultivation problems arise, comparing actual growing conditions to the
published optima often reveals the source of difficulty. Deviations of even a
few degrees in temperature or a few hundred ppm in CO2 can dramatically affect
yields, flush timing, and fruitbody quality.
