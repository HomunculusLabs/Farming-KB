# DMT (N,N-Dimethyltryptamine) Pharmacology and Effects

## Chemical Identity

DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is also known as: N,N-dimethyltryptamine, 3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]indole, desoxybufotenine, and nigerine. It is a simple tryptamine with two methyl groups on the terminal nitrogen.

## Dosage and Administration

| Route | Dosage |
|-------|--------|
| Oral | >350 mg (essentially inactive) |
| Intramuscular | 60-100 mg |
| Subcutaneous | 60-100 mg |
| Smoking | 60-100 mg |
| Intravenous | 4-30 mg |
| Duration | Up to 1 hour |

DMT is notable for being **inactive orally** at doses up to 350 mg. It requires parenteral administration (injection or smoking) to produce psychedelic effects. The smoked route produces effects within seconds; intravenous administration produces a characteristic "freight train" rush well before a 45-second infusion is complete.

## Pharmacokinetics and Metabolism

The principal reason DMT must be administered parenterally is its rapid and efficient metabolism:

- **Oxidation to the N-oxide**
- **Cyclization to beta-carbolines** (with and without N-methyl groups)
- **N-dealkylation** to N-methyltryptamine (NMT) and simple tryptamine
- **Oxidative destruction by monoamine oxidase (MAO)** to inactive indoleacetic acid (the [[psychedelic-dose-response]]): Nearly all subjects had visual hallucinations; auditory changes were rare
- **30 mg**: Effects overwhelming in both speed and intensity

Repeated administrations of 16 mg i.v. at half-hour intervals showed **no development of tolerance**.

## Endogenous Occurrence

DMT has been reported in:
- Urine of schizophrenic patients and normal subjects (no differences found between groups)
- A methylating enzyme in blood capable of forming DMT in plasma, present in both normal subjects and schizophrenics

As an endogenous psychotogen candidate, DMT does not produce tolerance upon repeated administration -- a property consistent with its proposed role as a natural neurotransmitter.

## Natural Sources

DMT is extraordinarily widespread in nature:
- **Piptadenia peregrina** and **P. macrocarpa**: Beans and pods ([[entheogen-jurema-mimosa-hostilis-ethnobotany]]**: Roots (jumera/vinho de jurema drink)
- **Phalaris** species (reed canarygrass): Pasture grasses
- **Acacia** species: Africa and Australia
- **Virola** species: Amazon basin snuffs (yakee, yato, parica, epena, nyakwana)
- **Sonora Desert Toad** ([[5-meo-dmt-and-bufo-alvarius]]): Venom contains DMT and 5-MeO-DMT

## Serotonin Receptor Interaction

DMT has identical serotonin binding capacity to its 1-methyl homologue. It has been proposed as both an endogenous psychotogen and a natural neurotransmitter. The serotonin antagonist UML-491 (methysergide) dramatically intensifies DMT effects when pre-administered.

## Physical Properties

- Melting point (base): 44-68 degrees C (literature range, likely due to purity and polymorphism)
- Hydrochloride salt: Difficult to crystallize (only one valid claim exists)
- Picrate salt: Well-defined, mp 167 degrees C
- Fumarate salt: FDA-approved form for human injection studies
- MS (EI): C3H8N+ m/z 58 (100%), indolemethylene+ m/z 130 (10%), parent ion m/z 188 (4%)

## Representative Trip Reports

### Intramuscular Administration

**20 mg i.m.**: "I began to see patterns on the wall that were continuously moving. They were transparent, and were not colored. After a short period these patterns became the heads of animals, a fox, a snake, a dragon. Then kaleidoscopic images appeared to me in my inner eye, fantastically beautiful and colored."

**75 mg i.m.**: "The third or fourth minute after the injection vegetative symptoms appeared, such as tingling sensation, trembling, slight nausea, mydriasis, elevation of the blood pressure and increase of the pulse rate. At the same time, eidetic phenomena, optical illusions, pseudohallucinations, and later real hallucinations, appeared. The hallucinations consisted of moving, brilliantly colored oriental motifs, and later I saw wonderful scenes altering very rapidly. The faces of people seemed to be masks."
