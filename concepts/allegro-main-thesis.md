# Allegro's Main Thesis: Christianity and Amanita muscaria

John Marco Allegro (1923-1988) was a British philologist and one of the original
one of the original international team entrusted with editing and publishing the [[dead-sea-scrolls-entheogenic-reading]].
His 1970 book *The Sacred Mushroom and the Cross* presented a revolutionary and
highly controversial thesis: that [[allegro-christianity-as-a-fertility-cult]] originated as a fertility cult
centred on the worship and ritual consumption of the [[amanita-muscaria-cultural-history]] (Fly-Agaric) mushroom, and that the New Testament was a
deliberately coded document designed to transmit the secrets of this cult to
initiates while misleading the authorities.

## Core Argument

Allegro argued that many of the most secret names of the sacred mushroom trace
back to ancient [[allegro-sumerian-akkadian-vocabulary]], the oldest written language known,
witnessed by cuneiform texts dating from the fourth millennium BC. He proposed
that Sumerian provides a philological bridge between the Indo-European languages
(Greek, Latin) and the Semitic group (Hebrew, Aramaic), making it possible to
decipher the names of gods, mythological characters, and plant names across the
entire ancient Near East.

The fungus recognized today as the [[amanita-muscaria-cultural-history]] had been known from the beginning of history. Beneath the skin of its
characteristic red-and-white-spotted cap there is concealed a powerful
hallucinatory poison. Its religious use among Siberian peoples has been
documented, and its effects include stimulation of perceptive faculties so that
the subject sees objects much greater or much smaller than they really are,
colours and sounds are enhanced, and there is a general sense of power both
physical and mental quite outside normal human experience.

## The Mushroom as God

Allegro contended that the mushroom was conceived as "God himself, manifest on
earth." Every aspect of its existence was fraught with sexual allusions. Born
from a volva or "egg," it appeared like a small penis, raising itself like the
human organ sexually aroused, and when it spread wide its canopy, the old
botanists saw it as a phallus bearing the "burden" of a woman's groin. It was
the "son of God," its drug was a purer form of the god's own spermatozoa than
that discoverable in any other form of living matter. To the mystic it was the
divinely given means of entering heaven.

This theological framework connects directly to [[allegro-fertility-philosophy-of-the-ancient-near-east]]: since rain makes crops grow, the ancients reasoned it must
contain the seed of life -- spermatozoa. Rain was thus heavenly semen, the
all-powerful creator God. The sacred mushroom was the most concentrated form of
this divine substance on earth.

## Christianity as Cryptogram

Allegro's most provocative claim was that the entire New Testament is a
cryptogram -- a coded document in which mushroom epithets and holy invocations
were woven into stories about a man called Jesus and his companions. He
identified three levels of meaning:

1. **Surface level**: Greek words in their plain meaning, presenting the story
   of Jesus and his adventures.
2. **Semitic level**: Word-plays on Aramaic words underlying the Greek text,
   revealing secret mushroom names.
3. **Cultic level**: The fundamental conceptions of the mushroom cult, the
   real philosophy behind the mystery religion.

The names of Jesus himself, Peter, John, James, and other New Testament
characters were, according to Allegro, all plays on mushroom epithets. "Jesus"
(Hebrew *Yehoshua*) derives from Sumerian *IA-U-ShUShA*, meaning "semen which
saves, restores, heals." "Peter" is a play on the Semitic *pitrā'*, meaning
"mushroom." See [[allegro-jesus-name-as-mushroom-epithet]] and
[[allegro-key-of-the-kingdom-and-peter-as-mushroom]].

## The Cultic Cover Story

Allegro argued that the Christians used plant mythology, known for thousands of
years across the ancient world, as their "cover." Mushroom stories abounded in
the [[allegro-biblical-references-to-mushrooms-and-fungi]]. The
Christians believed, like their Essene brethren, that they were the true
spiritual heirs to ancient Israel. They conveyed reminders of their most sacred
doctrines and incantatory names concealed within a story of a "second Moses,"
named after the patriarch's successor Joshua (Greek *Iesous*, "Jesus").

This, according to Allegro, was how the Gospel myth of the New Testament was
born. The Roman records speak with loathing of the Christians and they were
hounded with extreme ferocity reserved for political troublemakers. Those most
deceived, Allegro claimed, were the later Church who took the name "Christian"
and who formed the basis of institutional Christianity, having lost the original
cultic meaning entirely.

## Key Implications

If correct, Allegro's thesis implies:
- There may never have been a historical Jesus connected with the Christian sect.
- The New Testament was a "hoax" -- but a deadly serious attempt to transmit
  secrets to the scattered faithful.
- Christianity was only a latter-day manifestation of a religious movement
  thousands of years old.
- The "surface" details of the Gospel story -- names, places, doctrinal
  teachings -- must be considered as potentially false as the pseudo-renderings
  of the secret names.
- The moral teachings of the Gospels must stand or fall on their own merit,
  independent of any supposed divine authority.

## Known Limitations

The source document notes that Allegro did not understand the growth cycle,
effects, and "bitterness" of the [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]]. He overlooked some references to *A. pantherina* and *P.
cubensis*, and wrongfully classified Rue as an "abortifacient," not recognizing
Acacia and Rue as an entheogen-culture|Ayahuasca analogue. He also
wrongfully assumed that the mushrooms themselves, and not religion and
government, were the cause of persecution.

Despite these errors, Allegro was a pioneer in entheogen-culture|ethnomycology and founded many ideas that researchers in the field still use today,
though most deny the connection.
