# Organic Fertilizer Sources by Nutrient

updated: 2026-04-18

Natural fertilizers are derived from plant and animal by-products as well as rock powders. Unlike synthetic alternatives, they feed the [[lowenfels-earthworms-soil-food-web]] while providing [[teaming-with-nutrients-essential-plant-nutrients]]. All N-P-K values listed represent the nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium content by weight. See also [[organic-fertilizer-sources-guide]].

## Nitrogen Sources

**Alfalfa meal (2-1-3):** Good all-purpose nitrogen with trace elements and natural growth hormones. Feeds bacteria and fungi; lasts 1-4 months. Warning: can contain weed seeds (which will attract endomycorrhizal fungi); contains natural hormones so avoid overuse late in season.

**Bat guano (10-3-1):** Highly soluble, readily available nitrogen. Excellent for first-time gardens or quick start. Lasts 4-6 months. Two types exist: nitrogen-rich (migratory bat) and phosphorus-rich (fruit-eating bat) — read labels. Ensure sustainable harvesting.

**Blood meal (12-0-0):** Fast-acting nitrogen from dried livestock blood. Available immediately; lasts 1-4 months. Warning: can burn plants at high rates, has unpleasant odor, attracts dogs/cats, repels deer and moose.

**Cottonseed meal (6-0.4-1.5):** Slow-release, slightly acidic. Contains zinc, copper, manganese, molybdenum. Good for acid-loving plants. Lasts ~4 months. Warning: may contain pesticide residues; often from GMO cotton.

**Corn gluten meal (9-0-0):** By-product of corn syrup manufacturing. Lasts 1-4 months. Warning: prevents root hair development on seedlings — use only on established lawns and perennials, not at seeding time.

**Feather meal (7-12-0-0):** Poultry industry by-product rich in keratin protein. Very slow release (6+ months) due to complex protein structure requiring extensive microbial digestion.

**Fish emulsion (5-2-2):** Heat- and acid-processed fish; soluble and concentrated. Quick acting; full of micronutrients. Lasts 1-4 months. Dilute before use. Warning: strong odor for several days; attracts flies.

**Fish meal (10-6-2):** Ground, heated, dried fish parts. Good phosphorus source. Lasts 1-4 months. Less soluble than emulsion; heat processing loses some enzymes and vitamins.

**Human hair (18-0-0):** Extremely high nitrogen. Very slow release (1-2 years) because hair is not readily soluble. Does not harm soil food web at this concentration. Available free from salons.

**Human urine (15-2-2):** Contains urea (pure: 46-0-0). Sterile when fresh; soil bacteria convert urea to ammonium. Dilute 8:1 with water. Warning: possible heavy metals and antibiotics; do not use on food crops. Excellent undiluted for [[compost-building-guide]] piles.

**Soybean meal (7-2-1):** All-purpose favorite. Contains lots of carbon as well as nitrogen, odorless, stores well. Attracts both fungi and bacteria. Lasts 3-4 months. Warning: major GMO crop.

## Phosphorus Sources

**Animal bone meal (3-15-0):** Steam-processed ground bones. Readily available; lasts 1-4 months. Works best below pH 7. May attract animals.

**Bat guano, phosphorus type (3-10-1):** For phosphorus needs. Lasts 1-4 months. mycorrhiza|Mycorrhizal fungi are the best releasers — but excess phosphorus limits mycorrhizal presence, so apply sparingly.

**Colloidal rock phosphate (0-2.5-0):** Clay particles surrounded by phosphate. Extremely long lasting (3-5 years); some gardeners report better results in second and third years. Placement where roots will intercept is key.

**Crab shell meal (2-3-0):** Contains calcium, trace elements, and chitin (great mycorrhiza|fungal food). Chitin also helps control nematode populations.

## Potassium Sources

**Greensand (0-0-7):** Ancient sea bed mineral (glauconite) containing 30+ elements. Slow release; lasts 2-3 years.

**Wood ashes (0-1-3):** Free and fast-acting. Warning: alkaline — raises pH; do not use in already alkaline soils. Use only untreated wood. Lasts 1-2 months.

**Sulfate of potash (0-0-2):** Mineral salt (Sul-Po-Mag) containing 23% sulfur, 22% potash, 11% magnesium. Very soluble and quick-acting. Lasts ~2 months.

## Micronutrient Sources

**Shrimp shell meal (5-8-15):** Ground shrimp heads/shells. Contains calcium, chitin, and broad trace elements. Slow release; lasts 3-6 months.

**Kelp meal (0-0-0 to 1-0-4):** Seaweed containing up to 60 elements including all trace minerals. Contains natural plant growth hormones. Available ~1-2 months after application; decays over 4-5 months. Ensure sustainable harvesting.

**Liquid kelp:** Enzymatically digested kelp preserving growth hormones. Instantly available; lasts 1-3 weeks.

**Azomite (0-0-0.1):** Volcanic ash mineral deposit containing 67 trace minerals. Very slow release; lasts 2-4 years. Best used as a long-term soil mineral supplement rather than a quick correction.

**Compost:** The most balanced and forgiving of all [[jeavons-fertilization-organic-soil-amendments]]. Finished compost contains N-P-K in roughly 1-1-1 ratios along with calcium, magnesium, sulfur, and all micronutrients. Its greatest value lies not in nutrient quantity but in feeding the [[composting-and-soil-biology]] that makes all other nutrients available.

## Calcium and Sulfur Sources

**Agricultural lime (CaCO3):** Primary calcium source and pH amendment. Raises soil pH; apply only when soil test indicates acidity. Contains 30-40% calcium.

**Gypsum (CaSO4):** Calcium sulfate. Provides calcium and sulfur without affecting pH — useful when calcium is needed but soil pH is already adequate. Also improves soil structure in clay soils by promoting flocculation.

**Epsom salt (MgSO4):** Magnesium sulfate. Fast-acting source of both magnesium and sulfur. Effective as a foliar spray for quick correction of magnesium deficiency.

**Elemental sulfur:** Used to lower soil pH in alkaline soils. Oxidized by soil bacteria to sulfate over weeks to months. The bacterial conversion rate depends on soil temperature and moisture.

## Choosing Between Organic Sources

The choice among organic fertilizer sources depends on several factors:

- **Speed of need:** Liquid kelp and fish emulsion for immediate results; rock minerals and bone meal for slow, sustained release
- **Soil biology health:** Biologically active soils can access slow-release forms more effectively; depleted soils may need faster sources initially
- **Existing nutrient levels:** Soil test results should guide which nutrients are actually needed rather than applying broad-spectrum amendments indiscriminately
- **Budget and availability:** Many effective organic fertilizers (compost, [[comfrey-multifunctional-guild-plant]], wood ash) are free or low-cost byproducts of other activities
- **Soil type:** Sandy soils need more frequent, lighter applications; clay soils benefit from fewer, heavier applications with longer-lasting sources

## Application Timing

Nutrient availability from organic sources follows a decomposition curve — initially slow as microbes begin processing, then accelerating as microbial populations build, and finally tapering as the material is consumed. This means:

- Apply slow-release amendments (rock phosphate, greensand) in autumn so they begin releasing by spring
- Apply fast-release amendments (fish emulsion, liquid kelp) at planting or during peak growth
- Side-dress heavy feeders (tomatoes, corn, squash) with compost or balanced amendments during the growing season
- Avoid applying nitrogen in late summer to prevent tender new growth before frost
