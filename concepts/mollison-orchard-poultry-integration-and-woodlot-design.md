### Establishing the System
1. Prepare the whole site by soil conditioning
2. Set out leguminous species (small species like white clover, lab-lab and lucerne, larger acacias, albizias and locusts, plus leguminous shrubs)
3. Interplant the selected orchard trees
4. Allow small animals to forage below the system as pest control and manurial elements

### Suitable Livestock by Season
- **Autumn:** Pigs (for fallen fruit, grubbing)
- **Winter:** Geese (for grass control)
- **All year:** Ducks and poultry

### High-Value Fodder Species
All fruits are useful fodder, but particularly valuable are elderberry, mulberry, and Crataegus (hawthorn) species. Tree lucerne (for bees) can be top-trimmed as goose forage in winter, while adding nitrogen and providing bee fodder and ground pasture control.

### Benefits of Poultry Under Orchards
Trees under which poultry or small livestock (wallaby, sheep) are allowed to range are more vigorous and healthier, showing less lichen, dead branches, and very little insect attack on fruits. Conversely, trees or orchards where cattle or horses are allowed to browse show severe damage and disease.

Litter under poultry-run trees is more plentiful; natural mulches are thicker. Leaf mould is constantly being turned over by hens, suckers from trees are fewer, and water absorption of the soil is better, while grass mats are rare and many persistent weeds are absent.

### Remedial Measures for Existing Monocultural Orchards
Add 3-4 hens, a pig, and 5-6 large wattles per 1,000 m2 (1/4 acre), with many smaller legumes. As decoration and variety:
- Fuchsias, banksias, and Kniphofia for insectivorous birds
- Borage and white clover for bees
- Judicious mowing and adding more species as the system evolves

### Trial Species for Understorey
Black currants, red currants, white currants, gooseberries, lucerne, tree lucerne, clover, narcissus, perennial dahlia, Jerusalem and globe artichoke, ugni. Replace diseased deciduous trees with evergreen (feijoa, citrus, loquat, olive) and long-term interplants of chestnut, walnut, almond, and plum.

Planned variety gives a good display at wayside stalls and enables direct marketing of varied products, from flowers to fruit and nuts. Savings in pest-infestation and fertilizer use more than compensate for dispersing the system, while secondary yields increase total income and free the producer from commodity market fluctuations.

## Pruning: Necessity or Habit?

Most fruit trees from nurseries are shaped so that later pruning is essential. Conventional reasons for pruning include ease of spraying and harvesting, maximum fruit size, even ripening, removal of diseased parts, and small tree size for density.
