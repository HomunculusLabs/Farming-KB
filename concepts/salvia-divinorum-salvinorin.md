# Salvia divinorum and Salvinorin-A

## Overview

[[salvia-divinorum]] is a psychoactive plant from the mint family
(Lamiaceae) that contains the neoclerodane diterpene salvinorin-A.
It is traditionally consumed by Mazatec Indians of Oaxaca, Mexico,
but is now grown and sold in the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] and worldwide.
Salvinorin-A is unique among naturally occurring hallucinogens as
the first identified non-nitrogenous kappa-opioid receptor agonist.

## Unique Pharmacology

### Kappa-Opioid Receptor Mechanism

Most hallucinogens are psychoactive through action at the 5-HT2A
[[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] subtype (including LSD, psilocin, DMT, and
mescaline). Salvinorin-A has no activity at [[psilocybin-pharmacology-and-serotonin-receptors]]
and instead is the first known naturally occurring non-nitrogenous
kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) agonist (Roth et al., 2002).

This unique mechanism makes salvinorin-A pharmacologically
distinct from all other classical and atypical hallucinogens.
KOR agonism produces dissociative effects rather than the
classical [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]].

### No Nitrogen
Unlike virtually all other psychoactive alkaloids, salvinorin-A
is a non-nitrogenous compound (a diterpene rather than an
alkaloid). This makes it structurally unique among psychoactive
natural products.

## Traditional Use

The [[mazatec-people]] of Oaxaca, Mexico traditionally consume Salvia
divinorum by chewing fresh leaves or drinking the juice of crushed
leaves. The salvinorin-A is absorbed through the oral mucosa
during chewing (Valdes et al., 1983). Traditional use is
integrated into Mazatec [[metzner-shamanic-healing-and-divination-paradigm]] practices, where
the plant is treated with reverence and used under controlled
ceremonial conditions.

## Modern Use Patterns

### Smoking
When smoked, salvinorin-A is psychoactive for approximately
15 minutes at doses of 200-500 micrograms (Siebert, 1994). The
smoked experience is typically more intense and disorienting
than the traditional chewed method. Modern users often purchase
concentrated extracts (5x, 10x, 20x standardized extracts)
through Internet-based companies and smoke shops.

### Oral Absorption
Oral absorption from chewing fresh leaves produces a less
intense intoxication lasting up to 1 hour. This method more
closely approximates traditional Mazatec practice.

### Extract Products
Commercially available concentrated extracts represent a
significant departure from traditional use patterns. These
products deliver much higher doses than fresh leaves,
increasing the risk of overwhelming experiences.

## Legal Status

As of the Halpern (2004) review, the DEA had not scheduled
Salvia divinorum but was closely monitoring it as a possibly
emerging drug of abuse. Since then, many US states have
independently scheduled salvinorin-A or the plant itself,
though it remains unscheduled at the federal level as of the
early 2020s.

## Risk Profile

### Reinforcing Properties
Salvinorin-A has no reinforcing (addictive) properties. The
intense dissociative experience is not typically sought
repeatedly in the way that drugs with dopaminergic reward
mechanisms are.
