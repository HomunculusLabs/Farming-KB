## Critique of Modern Medicine

Fukuoka was sharply critical of both Western and Japanese modern medicine, seeing in both the same hubristic desire to control nature that characterizes industrial agriculture. He argued that:

- Modern medicine treats symptoms rather than causes, just as conventional farming treats symptoms (weeds, pests) rather than causes (unnatural practices)

- The medical industry, like the agricultural industry, creates dependency through its interventions

- Overmedication is analogous to over-fertilization -- both overwhelm natural systems

- Diagnostic categories create the illusion of understanding while obscuring the fundamental unity of health and environment

He titled one section "[[fukuoka-eastern-western-medicine-healing-world-crisis]], though he felt even Oriental medicine had become too interventionist. He valued its recognition of:

- The body as an integrated system rather than a collection of parts

- Food as the primary medicine

- The importance of prevention over treatment

- The role of environment and season in health

He noted that techniques such as shiatsu (finger-pressure therapy), acupuncture, and moxibustion were "gradually being unified and simplified" and moving "in the direction of doing nothing." He cited a Dr. Hashimoto who practiced a remarkably simple therapy: "People ought to live pleasantly and die pleasantly."
