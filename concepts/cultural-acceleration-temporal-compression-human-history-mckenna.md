# Cultural Acceleration and Temporal Compression in Human History

## Overview

[[terence-mckenna]], drawing on observations from both ordinary historical analysis and
tryptamine-informed visionary experience, proposed that human [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]] is
characterized by an accelerating trajectory — a compression of time in which major
innovations occur with increasing frequency as history progresses toward a
culminating point. This pattern, visible in the archaeological and historical record,
mirrors the phenomenology of the tryptamine state, which McKenna described as
"transtemporal" — an experience of stepping outside the "moving image of eternity"
into the eternal present.

## The Acceleration Pattern

The historical data points McKenna cited form a striking sequence of compression:

| Development | Approximate Time Ago |
|---|---|
| Fire discovery | Several million years |
| [[psychedelic-plants-language-emergence-mckenna-synesthesia-hypothesis]] | ~35,000 years |
| Agriculture | ~10,000 years |
| Writing / measurement | ~5,000 years |
| Scientific revolution (Galileo) | ~400 years |
| [[mckenna-caffeine-coffee-and-industrial-revolution]] | ~200 years |
| Digital computers | ~70 years |
| Watson-Crick DNA structure | ~70 years |
| Genomic engineering | ~30 years |
| Internet / global communication | ~30 years |

Each major development takes significantly less time to emerge than the previous
one. McKenna described this as "everything being drawn together," with the rate
of innovation increasing as human cultural complexity builds upon itself.

## The Eschatological Shadow

McKenna interpreted this acceleration through an eschatological lens: "Something is
at the end of time and is casting an enormous shadow over human history, drawing
all human becoming toward it." In this model, the acceleration is not merely
technological but teleological — it has a direction and a destination.

The wars, migrations, cities, civilizations, and philosophies of human history
are, in McKenna's framing, "occupying a microsecond of geological, planetary, and
galactic time as the monkeys react to the symbiote, which is in the environment
and which is feeding information to humanity about the larger picture."

## Plato's Moving Image of Eternity

McKenna grounded his temporal philosophy in Plato's Timaeus, where time is described
as "the moving image of eternity." In Platonic cosmology, eternity (the realm of
the Forms) is the true reality, and time is its imperfect, changing reflection.
The [[dmt-black-hole-effect-and-ineffability-of-tryptamine-experience]], McKenna suggested, is "a stepping out of the moving
image and into eternity, the eternity of the standing now, the [[mckenna-tryptamine-transtemporal-eternity-and-nunc-stans]] of
Thomas Aquinas."

The concept of the nunc stans (the "standing now") from medieval Scholastic
philosophy provides a Western philosophical correlate to the Buddhist concept of
suchness or the [[mystical-experience]] of the eternal present. In both frameworks,
ordinary temporal consciousness is seen as a secondary phenomenon — a veil
obscuring a more fundamental atemporal reality.

## The Shockwave of History

McKenna's metaphor of history as "the [[mckenna-history-shockwave-of-eschatology]]" inverts the
common assumption that novelty and innovation are randomly distributed across
time. Instead, novelty concentrates at the leading edge of the temporal wave,
with each successive innovation occurring more quickly than the last.

This pattern has been independently observed by:
- **Ray Kurzweil** in his "law of accelerating returns," which tracks the
exponential growth of computational power
- **Carl Sagan** in his cosmic calendar, which compresses the entire history
of the universe into a single year
- **Ilya Prigogine** in his work on far-from-equilibrium systems, where
complexity increases nonlinearly as systems move further from thermodynamic
equilibrium

## The Noosphere and Teilhard de Chardin

McKenna's acceleration thesis parallels Pierre Teilhard de Chardin's concept of
the noosphere — the sphere of human thought and cultural information that
surrounds the Earth like a new layer of the planetary system. Teilhard proposed
that human cultural evolution is driving toward an "[[omega-point-and-psychedelic-eschatology]]" — a
culminating state of maximum complexity and consciousness.

McKenna described this process in more concrete terms: "Language, self-replicating
information systems that reflect functions of DNA: learning, coding, templating,
recording, testing, re-testing, re-coding against DNA functions" are "moving out
of our hands and into the noosphere with which we have surrounded ourselves."
