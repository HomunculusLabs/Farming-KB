# Purdue Four-Step Garden Fertilization Program

A systematic approach to [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]].

## Step 1: Establish Basic Fertility Level

The foundation of the fertilization program is establishing the right nutrient balance in your garden soil. This is done by applying the correct kind and amount of fertilizer based on a professional soil test.

### Getting a Soil Test

- Test soil in autumn to allow ample time to receive results and prepare materials for spring planting
- Contact your local county Extension office for soil testing details and instructions
- The test report will specify exactly what nutrients your soil needs and in what quantities

### Understanding Fertilizer Labels

Fertilizer bags are labeled with three numbers indicating the analysis or composition of nutrients:

- **First number:** Percent by weight of nitrogen (N)
- **Middle number:** Percent of the oxide of phosphorus (P2O5), which is 44% P by weight
- **Last number:** Percent of the oxide of potassium (K2O), which is 83% K by weight

For example, a bag of 8-24-8 fertilizer contains per 100 pounds: 8 pounds of N, 24 pounds of P2O5, and 8 pounds of K2O.

### Application Method for Basic Fertility

1. Apply **half** of the recommended fertilizer amount
2. Follow with deep plowing or spading to incorporate it
3. Apply the remaining half of the fertilizer
4. Disc or rake it in lightly

This two-stage method distributes the fertilizer throughout the upper 7 inches of soil for thorough incorporation.

- If your soil test recommends "no basic application," your fertility level is already adequate — skip this step
- If the report shows excess of a particular element, leave it out entirely
