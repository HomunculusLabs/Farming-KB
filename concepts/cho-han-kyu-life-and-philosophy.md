## Cho Han-Kyu: Life, Philosophy, and the Foundation of Korean Natural Farming

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Dr. Cho Han-Kyu (1936-2015) was the founder and developer of Korean Natural Farming (KNF), also known as Cho"s Global Natural Farming (CGNF). A Korean agricultural scientist turned farmer-innovator, Cho developed a comprehensive system of natural inputs and management practices that enables farmers to grow crops and raise livestock without reliance on [[knf-vs-chemical-farming-comparison]] paradigm. He noticed that despite increasing chemical inputs, soil fertility was declining, pest problems were worsening, and farmers were becoming trapped in cycles of debt to purchase ever-more expensive agricultural chemicals.

## The Turning Point

The pivotal moment in Cho's thinking came when he recognized a fundamental principle: nature already possesses everything needed for abundant food production. The problem was not that nature was insufficient, but that human intervention -- through chemicals, tillage, and monoculture -- was destroying nature's inherent productivity.

This realization led Cho to develop the core philosophy of Korean Natural Farming: "Life with Nature" or "Life in accordance with Nature." Rather than trying to control nature through external inputs, the farmer's role is to support and enhance the natural processes that already exist.

## Core Philosophy

### Life with Nature

Cho's philosophy rests on several interconnected principles:

1. **Nature is complete and self-sufficient.** The [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]] are superior.** The microorganisms native to any specific environment are already adapted to local conditions. They are more effective than any commercially produced biological input. IMO technology enables farmers to capture and multiply these native microbes.

3. **Plants know what they need.** By observing plant growth stages and providing appropriate inputs at the right time, the farmer supports the plant's natural development rather than forcing it with excessive nutrients.

4. **The nutritive cycle theory.** Plants progress through defined growth stages (germination, vegetative growth, reproductive growth, harvest), each requiring different nutritional support. Providing the wrong input at the wrong time is as harmful as providing no input at all.

5. **Input self-sufficiency.** Farmers should be able to produce all necessary farming inputs from locally available, inexpensive materials. This eliminates dependence on purchased chemicals and reduces farming costs to near zero.

## Cho's Teaching Methods

Cho was not only a farmer and inventor but also a dedicated teacher. His approach to spreading KNF was characterized by:

- **Hands-on demonstration:** Cho believed in showing rather than telling. He conducted practical workshops where farmers learned by making inputs and observing results.
- **Simplicity:** He insisted that KNF methods must be simple enough for any farmer to practice, regardless of education level. The inputs use common materials and straightforward techniques.
- **Self-reliance:** He taught farmers to produce their own inputs, breaking dependence on external suppliers.
- **Integration:** He taught farming as a whole system -- crops, livestock, soil, water, and human health are interconnected and cannot be managed in isolation.
