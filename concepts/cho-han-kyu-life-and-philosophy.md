# Cho Han-Kyu: Life, Philosophy, and the Foundation of Korean Natural Farming

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Dr. Cho Han-Kyu (1936-2015) was the founder and developer of Korean Natural Farming (KNF), also known as Cho's Global Natural Farming (CGNF). A Korean agricultural scientist turned farmer-innovator, Cho developed a comprehensive system of natural inputs and management practices that enables farmers to grow crops and raise livestock without reliance on chemical fertilizers, pesticides, or imported biological products. His life story is one of perseverance against institutional opposition and eventual global recognition.

## Early Life and Education

Cho Han-Kyu was born in 1936 in South Korea. He studied agricultural science and initially worked within the conventional agricultural establishment. His early career exposed him to the standard practices of chemical agriculture that were being promoted as the path to modernization and food security.

However, through his observations of farming communities and the declining health of both soil and farmers under chemical agriculture, Cho began to question the fundamental assumptions of the chemical farming paradigm. He noticed that despite increasing chemical inputs, soil fertility was declining, pest problems were worsening, and farmers were becoming trapped in cycles of debt to purchase ever-more expensive agricultural chemicals.

## The Turning Point

The pivotal moment in Cho's thinking came when he recognized a fundamental principle: nature already possesses everything needed for abundant food production. The problem was not that nature was insufficient, but that human intervention -- through chemicals, tillage, and monoculture -- was destroying nature's inherent productivity.

This realization led Cho to develop the core philosophy of Korean Natural Farming: "Life with Nature" or "Life in accordance with Nature." Rather than trying to control nature through external inputs, the farmer's role is to support and enhance the natural processes that already exist.

## Core Philosophy

### Life with Nature

Cho's philosophy rests on several interconnected principles:

1. **Nature is complete and self-sufficient.** The natural ecosystem already contains all the microorganisms, nutrients, and processes needed for plant growth. The farmer's job is to nurture these natural systems, not replace them.

2. **Indigenous microorganisms are superior.** The microorganisms native to any specific environment are already adapted to local conditions. They are more effective than any commercially produced biological input. IMO technology enables farmers to capture and multiply these native microbes.

3. **Plants know what they need.** By observing plant growth stages and providing appropriate inputs at the right time, the farmer supports the plant's natural development rather than forcing it with excessive nutrients.

4. **The nutritive cycle theory.** Plants progress through defined growth stages (germination, vegetative growth, reproductive growth, harvest), each requiring different nutritional support. Providing the wrong input at the wrong time is as harmful as providing no input at all.

5. **Input self-sufficiency.** Farmers should be able to produce all necessary farming inputs from locally available, inexpensive materials. This eliminates dependence on purchased chemicals and reduces farming costs to near zero.

6. **Livestock integration.** Animals are an integral part of the farming system, not a separate enterprise. Natural farming methods for livestock produce healthy animals without odor, without antibiotics, and without environmental pollution.

### Rejection of Chemical Farming

Cho was an outspoken critic of chemical agriculture on multiple grounds:

- **Soil destruction:** Chemical fertilizers kill soil microorganisms and degrade soil structure over time
- **Escalating inputs:** Farmers require ever-increasing chemical inputs as soil health declines, creating dependency
- **Health damage:** Chemical residues in food damage human health
- **Economic trap:** Farmers spend increasing amounts on inputs while receiving decreasing prices for their products
- **Environmental pollution:** Chemical runoff contaminates waterways and ecosystems

## Persecution and Resistance

Cho's advocacy of natural farming methods brought him into direct conflict with powerful interests:

- He was **jailed multiple times** in South Korea at the instigation of the chemical fertilizer and pesticide lobby
- His methods were dismissed as unscientific by the agricultural establishment
- He faced official obstruction when trying to teach and promote his techniques
- Chemical companies viewed his system as a direct threat to their business model

Despite this persecution, Cho persisted in developing and teaching his methods. His persistence was eventually vindicated as the limitations and harms of chemical agriculture became increasingly apparent.

## Global Spread and Recognition

Over time, Cho's Korean Natural Farming system gained international recognition:

- **Adoption across Asia:** KNF spread to Japan, China, the Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam, and other Asian countries
- **Pacific Islands:** KNF was adopted in Hawaii, Guam, and other Pacific island communities
- **Africa:** KNF methods were introduced to several African nations as a sustainable alternative to chemical-dependent agriculture
- **The Americas:** KNF practitioners and teaching centers were established in the United States, Central America, and South America
- **Institutional recognition:** Agricultural institutions in several countries began studying and validating KNF methods

## Cho's Teaching Methods

Cho was not only a farmer and inventor but also a dedicated teacher. His approach to spreading KNF was characterized by:

- **Hands-on demonstration:** Cho believed in showing rather than telling. He conducted practical workshops where farmers learned by making inputs and observing results.
- **Simplicity:** He insisted that KNF methods must be simple enough for any farmer to practice, regardless of education level. The inputs use common materials and straightforward techniques.
- **Self-reliance:** He taught farmers to produce their own inputs, breaking dependence on external suppliers.
- **Integration:** He taught farming as a whole system -- crops, livestock, soil, water, and human health are interconnected and cannot be managed in isolation.

## Legacy

Cho Han-Kyu passed away in 2015, but his system continues to spread globally. The Cho Global Natural Farming organization continues to teach and promote his methods. His greatest legacy is the demonstration that farming can be productive, profitable, and environmentally regenerative when it works with nature rather than against it.
