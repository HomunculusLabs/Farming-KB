# Soil Micronutrients — Sulphur, Potassium, Calcium, and Trace Elements

## Overview

Beyond the major elements (N, P, K, C), soils contain a range of micronutrients and trace elements that are essential for plant, animal, and human health. Sulphur, potassium, and calcium are secondary macronutrients required in significant quantities, while elements like iron, manganese, zinc, copper, cobalt, and molybdenum are needed only in trace amounts but are equally critical. This section covers their roles, deficiency symptoms, toxicity concerns, and [[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos|Permaculture]] management strategies.

## Sulphur (S)

Sulphur is fixed by many anaerobic bacteria (*thiobacilli*), which is why anaerobic ferment of plant and animal materials is rich in sulphur-based amino acids and of high nutritional value.

### Environmental Functions

- Sulphur oxidised by anaerobic bacilli removes most heavy metals as insoluble sulphates from flow-through systems such as sewage digesters
- Some *thiobacilli* occur in all warm wet soils and many can operate down to pH 1.0
- With ammonium sulphate, soils tend to become acidic, reducing plant yields
- **Sulphur in drylands** reduces pH and makes iron, zinc, and trace elements available
- **Clover**, in particular, may show sulphur deficiency in the sub-tropics (wet summers)

### Human Nutrition

- Sulphur is part of all proteins, present in the body as the amino acids **methionine** and **cysteine**
- The vitamins **thiamin** and **biotin** contain sulphur
- Food intake is from amino acids; deficiencies do not occur if meat protein is sufficient or if anaerobic ferment of leaf materials is part of food preparation
- Many vegetarians get their amino acids from yeasts or bacteria rather than from fresh plant material

## Chlorine (Cl)

Chlorine was used as a war gas and today to "sterilise" waters. It is a dangerous gas to inhale but used by plants in minute trace amounts, normally available as salt.

### Key Facts

- A trace element used only in minute amounts
- Concentrates in crops: 350 ppm in soil can give 1000 ppm in crop
- In water and in contact with organic materials, chlorine releases **chloroform**, a carcinogenic gas
- **Recommendation**: Avoid chlorinated water if possible

## Potassium (K)

Potassium is used in large quantities by plants and is usually plentiful in arid areas.

### Deficiency Conditions

- Deficient on sandy, free-draining coastal soils
- Not much is removed by livestock
- Potatoes, beans, flax, and export of hay may remove soil reserves below plant needs
- Readily absorbed on [[colloids]]; usually plentiful in clays, especially illites (not kaolin)

### Sources and Application

- **Gardeners**: Add ashes, bone, natural urines and manures, or green crops for heavily cropped ground
- **Earthworm castings**: Concentrate potash at **11 times** soil levels — an excellent reason to maintain worm populations

### Caution — Potassium-Sodium Interaction

Excess potash fertiliser can greatly increase soil sodium and block calcium uptake. This is especially dangerous in alkaline or dryland soils where sodium is already problematic.

## Calcium (Ca)

Calcium is needed in all soils and is removed by sodium in drylands. Even where calcium exists in alkaline areas, sodium may suppress its uptake by plants.

### Soil Amendment

- **Gypsum** is sometimes applied at 30 tonnes/ha; excess sodium is then flushed out as sodium sulphate
- Plants need large amounts of calcium — it is essential for cell walls, enzymes, and chromosome structure

### The Ideal Ion Ratio

The proportion of the four major ions in ideal agricultural soils should be approximately:

| Ion | Proportion |
|-----|-----------|
| Calcium | 50 |
| Magnesium | 35 |
| Potassium | 6 |
| Sodium | 5 |

**Key interactions to remember:**
- Adding potassium can increase sodium
- Sodium is antagonistic to (displaces) calcium
- Magnesium ions should always be less than calcium ions
- Do not add too much dolomite if soils already contain adequate magnesium reserves

### Human Health

- Many peoples have lactose intolerance and cannot get calcium from milk; lime or dolomite on gardens or as tablets may be needed
- Calcium is excreted during stress and needs replacement after prolonged stress
- **Low calcium areas** produce predominantly male farm animals and humans (as a primary sex ratio)
- Lack of calcium produces skeletal and metabolic malfunction
- [[calcium-phosphate]] is the chief mineral constituent of bones
- Older women suffer bone fractures from calcium loss in the ageing process
- Gross calcium and vitamin D deficiency results in **rickets**

## Trace Elements

### Titanium (Ti)

- Not a nutrient, but in sands and in the presence of sunlight, it acts as a catalyst to produce ammonia for plants, often combined with iron (TiFe)
- Ammonia is provided at 50–100 kg/ha/year
- Rain and moist soils make this available to plants — a useful desert strategy

### Chromium (Cr)

- A poison to plants and animals, occurring in serpentine rock
- Used in timber preservation; must be guarded against from electrolytic and leather works
- Easily removed or recycled at source
- In fact a trace element related to glucose tolerance in humans, necessitating insulin if absent or very low
- Very little firm knowledge of chromium's metabolic function is available

### Manganese (Mn)

- Readily available on acid soils, except in sands
- May cause manganese poisoning below pH 6.5
- **Deficient on alkaline soils** or when pH exceeds 7.5 in grain crops or vegetables
- Seed soakage, [[seed-pelleting]], or foliar sprays supply this nutrient
- Even periodic flooding mobilises manganese
- A typical deficiency situation occurs on poor sands heavily dressed with lime

### Manganese Dynamics

- Bacteria fix insoluble manganese and can create problems in pipes and concrete water raceways even at 2–3 ppm
- Aluminium sulphate (from acid rain on soil) may mobilise manganese, mercury, and cadmium to lethal levels
- Manganese leaches out of acid soils, deposits in alkaline horizons as manganese-iron concretions, and on sea floors as larger nodules

## Iron (Fe)

Iron is normally plentiful in soils and is a critical element for plants and animals.

### Plant Deficiency

- Iron deficiency symptoms appear in alkaline drylands or on heavily limed garden soils
- **Remedies**: Sulphur in gardens, iron chelates, or small amounts of ferric sulphate
- Deficiency causes **interveinal leaf yellowing** in plants

### Human Health

- Low blood iron causes **anaemia**, a common problem in deserts or after blood losses in menses or dysentery
- Blood iron is as important in energy transfers as copper is in invertebrates
- Deficiencies must be watched in women of reproductive age
- **Dietary sources**: Veal, liver, fish eaten with soya beans, lettuce, parsley, maize
- Meat and ascorbic acid (vitamin C) increase iron absorption from food
- Iron in bone marrow is needed for new blood cells
- Clinically, ferrous salts can be used in cases of excessive blood loss
- People brewing beer or cooking cereals in iron pots often ingest excess iron
- Alcoholism and diabetes exacerbate iron excess
