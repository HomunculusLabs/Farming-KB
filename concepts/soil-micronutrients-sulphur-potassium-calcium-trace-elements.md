# Soil Micronutrients — Sulphur, Potassium, Calcium, and Trace Elements

## Overview

Beyond the major elements (N, P, K, C), soils contain a range of micronutrients and trace elements that are essential for plant, animal, and human health. Sulphur, potassium, and calcium are secondary macronutrients required in significant quantities, while elements like iron, manganese, zinc, copper, cobalt, and molybdenum are needed only in trace amounts but are equally critical. This section covers their roles, deficiency symptoms, toxicity concerns, and [[colloids]]; usually plentiful in clays, especially illites (not kaolin)

### Sources and Application

- **Gardeners**: Add ashes, bone, natural urines and manures, or green crops for heavily cropped ground
- **Earthworm castings**: Concentrate potash at **11 times** soil levels — an excellent reason to maintain worm populations

### Caution — Potassium-Sodium Interaction

Excess potash fertiliser can greatly increase soil sodium and block calcium uptake. This is especially dangerous in alkaline or dryland soils where sodium is already problematic.

## Calcium (Ca)

Calcium is needed in all soils and is removed by sodium in drylands. Even where calcium exists in alkaline areas, sodium may suppress its uptake by plants.

### Soil Amendment

- **Gypsum** is sometimes applied at 30 tonnes/ha; excess sodium is then flushed out as sodium sulphate
- Plants need large amounts of calcium — it is essential for cell walls, enzymes, and chromosome structure

### The Ideal Ion Ratio

The proportion of the four major ions in ideal agricultural soils should be approximately:

| Ion | Proportion |
|-----|-----------|
| Calcium | 50 |
| Magnesium | 35 |
| Potassium | 6 |
| Sodium | 5 |

**Key interactions to remember:**
- Adding potassium can increase sodium
- Sodium is antagonistic to (displaces) calcium
- Magnesium ions should always be less than calcium ions
- Do not add too much dolomite if soils already contain adequate magnesium reserves

### Human Health

- Many peoples have lactose intolerance and cannot get calcium from milk; lime or dolomite on gardens or as tablets may be needed
- Calcium is excreted during stress and needs replacement after prolonged stress
- **Low calcium areas** produce predominantly male farm animals and humans (as a primary sex ratio)
- Lack of calcium produces skeletal and metabolic malfunction
- [[seed-pelleting]], or foliar sprays supply this nutrient
- Even periodic flooding mobilises manganese
- A typical deficiency situation occurs on poor sands heavily dressed with lime

### Manganese Dynamics

- Bacteria fix insoluble manganese and can create problems in pipes and concrete water raceways even at 2–3 ppm
- Aluminium sulphate (from acid rain on soil) may mobilise manganese, mercury, and cadmium to lethal levels
- Manganese leaches out of acid soils, deposits in alkaline horizons as manganese-iron concretions, and on sea floors as larger nodules

## Iron (Fe)

Iron is normally plentiful in soils and is a critical element for plants and animals.

### Plant Deficiency

- Iron deficiency symptoms appear in alkaline drylands or on heavily limed garden soils
- **Remedies**: Sulphur in gardens, iron chelates, or small amounts of ferric sulphate
- Deficiency causes **interveinal leaf yellowing** in plants

### Human Health

- Low blood iron causes **anaemia**, a common problem in deserts or after blood losses in menses or dysentery
- Blood iron is as important in energy transfers as copper is in invertebrates
- Deficiencies must be watched in women of reproductive age
- **Dietary sources**: Veal, liver, fish eaten with soya beans, lettuce, parsley, maize
- Meat and ascorbic acid (vitamin C) increase iron absorption from food
- Iron in bone marrow is needed for new blood cells
- Clinically, ferrous salts can be used in cases of excessive blood loss
- People brewing beer or cooking cereals in iron pots often ingest excess iron
- Alcoholism and diabetes exacerbate iron excess
