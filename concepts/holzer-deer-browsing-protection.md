Sepp Holzer's approach to deer browsing protection combines ecological understanding with practical deterrents. Rather than fighting deer, his system provides them with alternative food sources while protecting valuable trees.

## Core Philosophy

"The need for browsing drops when wild animals find enough food." Holzer always sows and plants in large enough quantities so that deer, birds, hares, and mice all have something to eat. Nature is fertile enough to provide for everyone. When humans become too miserly and want everything for themselves, a battle against fellow creatures begins.

## Method 1: Side Shoots as Natural Protection

When fruit trees are grafted, the side shoots and branches between the graft union and the ground serve as natural browsing protection:
- The lowest twigs are eaten by hares
- The middle shoots are eaten by roe deer
- The highest shoots are eaten by red deer
- Each animal species finds food at its preferred height
- The trunk itself remains undamaged

This is the simplest and most natural protection method. Keep the lower branches intact rather than pruning them clean to the trunk.

## Method 2: Distraction Plants

Plant species that deer prefer over your valuable crops:

### Excellent Distraction Plants
- [[permaculture-jerusalem-artichoke]] -- top choice
- Various kinds of clover
- Buckwheat
- Various types of fruit bushes

### Dual-Purpose Plants
Many distraction plants also serve as [[mollison-designers-cover-crops-and-green-manure-species]], making them doubly valuable. Jerusalem artichokes, clover, and buckwheat all build soil while diverting deer from fruit trees.

### Willow Trees for Strip Protection
- Plant extra willow trees in the area and especially in front of slopes
- Deer much prefer to strip willow bark over fruit tree bark
- Willow bark is softer and more elastic, making it their preferred food
- Strategic willow placement creates a "sacrificial zone" that protects valuable trees behind it

## Method 3: Bone Salve Repellent

For high-danger situations or small cultivation areas, Holzer uses a homemade repellent salve.

### Ingredients
- Bone salve (see Gardens chapter)
- Linseed oil (made from flax seeds, available in wholefood shops)
- Slaked lime
- Fine quartz sand
- Fresh cow dung

### Mixing
- Mix all ingredients to a spreadable consistency
- For sprinkling: add more linseed oil, use less sand
- For painting: standard consistency with more sand

### Application Methods
- Paint directly onto tree trunks and branches with a brush or broom
- Sprinkle over plants "like holy water in a church"

### How Each Ingredient Works
- **Bone salve**: intense, long-lasting odour that repels deer; smell gets into bark and lasts for years
- **Linseed oil**: binds ingredients together and ensures adhesion to bark
- **Slaked lime**: emits heat (beneficial to tree), helps combine ingredients
- **Quartz sand**: causes unpleasant sensation between teeth if animal bites through the odour barrier
- **Cow dung**: bulks out the salve, absorbs other ingredients, adds additional repellent effect

### Alternative to Bone Salve
Mineral naphtha or beechwood tar can substitute for bone salve, but they have less intense odour. Mix in singed hair (pig bristles or cattle hair) to compensate:
1. Put hair in a metal container
2. Light a fire beneath it
3. The heat singes the hair into a floury mass
4. Mix this into the salve
5. The smell of singed hair is only noticeable to humans briefly but continues repelling wild animals

### Proven Effectiveness
Holzer observed a deer and her fawn trying to eat young fruit trees treated with the salve. After a few bites with no reaction, the deer encountered the salve and reacted dramatically -- gagging, throwing its head wildly, and trying to wipe the taste from its mouth on the grass. The fawn reacted the same way. The salve has never failed since.

## Method 4: Protective Barrier Plants

Plant thorny or prickly species around fruit trees:
- Wild roses
- Barberries
- Blackthorn
- Other similar thorny plants

The young shoots of these plants are grazed heavily, causing them to become bushy and dense. This creates a living barrier that protects fruit trees behind them.

## General Principles

1. Plant in abundance -- provide enough for everyone
2. Use multiple methods together for best protection
3. Keep lower branches on grafted trees as natural sacrifice zones
4. Observe which plants deer prefer in your area and use those as distraction plants
5. Fencing is never a bad idea when the cultivated area is small
6. Trees grown this way are better protected from browsing damage because they develop within a diverse plant community
