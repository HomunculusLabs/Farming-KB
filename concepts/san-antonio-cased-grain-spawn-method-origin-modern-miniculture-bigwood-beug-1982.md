## San Antonio"s Original Protocol (1971)

San Antonio published a laboratory method in *Mycologia* (Vol. 63, pp. 16-21)
designed to reliably produce fruiting bodies from grain spawn without requiring
bulk substrate. The key innovation was the use of a **casing layer** applied
directly over colonized grain, which triggered the environmental cues necessary
for fruiting body initiation in *[[agaricus-bisporus-cultivation-biology]]*.

The significance of this method was that it dramatically reduced the scale of
cultivation from traditional compost-based systems to something manageable in a
laboratory setting. Prior methods required large trays of prepared compost, while
San Antonio's approach could be executed in simple jars or small containers.

## The Oss and Oeric Refinement (1976)

Oss and Oeric (pseudonyms for K. McKenna and D. McKenna) adapted the grain
spawn methodology in their *Psilocybin Magic Mushroom Grower's Guide* (And/Or
Press, Berkeley, 1976). Their contribution was to:

1. Specify **rye grain** as the preferred spawn medium, establishing the
   rye-to-casing workflow that became standard
2. Detail the sterilization, inoculation, and colonization parameters
3. Adapt the technique specifically for psilocybin-producing species,
   particularly *Psilocybe cubensis*
4. Provide accessible instructions for non-specialist cultivators

The McKenna brothers' guide effectively democratized the San Antonio method and
introduced it to a much broader audience beyond professional mycologists.

## Bigwood and Beug's Miniculture Adaptation (1982)

Bigwood and Beug scaled the technique down further for their analytical
chemistry research, creating a standardized "miniculture" system with the
following specifications:

### Substrate Preparation
- **Container**: Wide-mouth half-pint jar (~250 ml)
- **Grain charge**: 10 grams of rye grain per jar
- **Water**: 15 ml deionized water per jar
- **Sterilization**: Autoclaving

### Inoculation and Colonization
- Inoculation performed under sterile conditions with agar-cultured mycelium
- **Shaking schedule**: Every 4 days for a period of 28 days
- Purpose of shaking: distribute growing mycelium evenly across the grain
- Total colonization time: approximately 28 days

### Casing and Fruiting
- **Casing composition**: 2 parts peat : 1 part [[peat-calcium-carbonate-perlite-casing-psilocybe-cubensis-miniculture]] perlite casing [[psilocybe-cubensis-miniculture-method]] : 2 parts
  perlite and/or vermiculite
- **Casing depth**: approximately 2 cm
- **Watering**: 1 ml sterile water via syringe every two days
- **Time to first flush**: approximately 2 weeks after casing (4-5 weeks total
  from inoculation)
- **Production longevity**: cultures continued producing for at least 20 weeks
  if uncontaminated
- **Average yield**: 2.7 g dry weight per miniculture

### Harvest and Storage
- Mushrooms harvested as soon as sporocarps were mature (pileus fully expanded)
- Immediately freeze-dried after harvest
- Sealed in plastic and stored at -5°C until analysis
- Voucher specimens deposited at the University of Washington Herbarium (WTU)

## The 28-Day Colonization Period

The 28-day colonization timeline with periodic shaking is notable. In modern
cultivation, rye grain jars typically colonize in 14-21 days without regular
shaking. The longer timeline may reflect:

- Lower incubation temperatures or less optimized conditions in the 1982 study
- The use of half-pint jars with relatively small grain charges
- Conservative timing to ensure complete colonization before casing
- The shaking interval potentially disrupting mycelial networks, requiring
  recovery time between distributions

## Significance for Analytical Chemistry

The miniculture system was specifically designed for reproducibility in
analytical chemistry research. By standardizing substrate composition, jar size,
water content, colonization protocol, and casing material, Bigwood and Beug
created a system where the primary variable was flush number, allowing them to
isolate and study the temporal dynamics of [[mycelial-broth-alkaloid-production]].

This methodological rigor is what made their finding — that psilocin is absent
in the first flushes and peaks by the fourth flush — a reliable observation
rather than an artifact of cultivation variability.

## Legacy and Modern Descendants

The San Antonio → Oss/Oeric → Bigwood/Beug lineage of grain spawn cultivation
directly influenced modern techniques including:

- **PF Tek** ([[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus]] Technique): [[brown-rice-flour-substrate]] in
  half-pint jars, essentially a simplified variant of the same approach
- **Spawn bag culture**: Grain-based colonization in filter-patch bags
- **Monotub cultivation**: Bulk casing over grain spawn at larger scale
- **Laboratory miniculture**: Small-jar systems for potency research that
  continue to use the same fundamental grain-casing paradigm
