# Time, Relativity, and the Tessellated Worldview

## Overview

The permaculture understanding of time and pattern leads to a radical worldview in which all phenomena are seen as interconnected events in a single, enfolded reality. Drawing on insights from physics (David Bohm), biology (Lovelock's Gaia), ecology (Birch and Cobb), and indigenous wisdom traditions, this perspective sees the present moment as the origin of both past and future, all events as projections of a single totality, and pattern understanding as the key to sustainable design. This philosophical framework underpins all [[permaculture-design]] practice.

## Time and the Origin Point

### The Seed as Time-Focus

Just as a seed is the origin of a tree, we can broaden our view to see the tree as the current time-focus of its own genealogy. Before it in time lie its ancestors; after it, its progeny. The tree lies on the plane between past and future and, like the seed, determines by its expression the forms of both—and is in turn determined by them.

Just as the stem of a tree encapsulates its history as smaller and smaller growth rings, universal time encapsulates the tree in its own evolutionary history. This multi-dimensional relationship is the basis of the Buddhist belief that **all time is enfolded or implicate in the present**, and that current events are part of a total sequence, all of which are enfolded in the present tree as ancestors or siblings.

### The Present as Origin

As we read this, we stand in the plane of the present. We are the sum of all our ancestors and the origin of all our descendants. In terms of the permaculture model, we are at an ever-changing origin, located on the boundary of past and future. We are also spinning with the earth, spiralling with the galaxy, and expanding or contracting with the universe—all movements with characteristic pulse rates.

Our bodies contain the potential for future generations, awaiting the events of pairing to create their own future events. Like a seed origin buried in the tree stem, we are buried in the stem of our siblings in a genealogy whose branches thrive, die, and put forth new shoots and roots over time.

### All Origins in the Matrix

All origins can be located in this temporal matrix, regardless of their age. If we know how to reconstruct the tree, we can find the place of the seed—and vice versa. All rivers, erosion cells, and glaciers originated at the central stem of their courses and built their pattern both ways along the kinetic gradient of their flow. In terms of the time dimension, **the present is the origin of both the past and the future**, located at the centre of the pattern.

### Designers as Time-Surfers

Designers can move "sideways in the waves of time," transporting seed from continent to continent, permitting natural or induced hybrid palms and legumes to weave an alternative future. Mankind is described as an "active translator of life, and, of course, of death."

## The World as a Tessellation of Events

### A Personal Landscape Portrait

Mollison describes his home landscape as a unity of patterns: living in the crater of an ancient volcano, partly eroded by the sea, with trees rising from soils, birds nesting in them, great wind spirals sweeping from the west bearing fractal cloud forms, hills stepped by successive sea-level changes recording pulses of long-term cycles, and the fundamental rhythm of day following night, life following death following life.

"All of these phenomena are a unity of patterns long repeated and based on one master pattern, each one preparing for new evolutions and dissolutions. It is the number and complexity of such cycles that give us life opportunities, and **life is the only integrative force in this part of the universe**."

### Pattern as Design Framework

An understanding of the underlying patterns that link all phenomena creates a powerful abstract tool for designers. At any point in the design process, appropriate patterning can assist the achievement of a sustainable yield from flows, growth forms, or information flux. Patterns imposed on constructs in domestic or village assemblies can result in:

- **Energy savings**
- **Satisfying aesthetics and function**
- **Sustained habitat** for organisms within the designed system

Patterning is the way we frame our designs, the template into which we fit the information, entities, and objects assembled from observation, map overlays, the analytic divination of connections, and the selection of specific materials and technologies. **The pattern is design, and design is the subject of permaculture.**

## Bohm's Implicate Order

David Bohm (1980) urges us to go beyond regarding ourselves as interactive with each other and the environment, and to see all things as **"projections of a single totality."** As we experience this totality, incorporate new information, and develop our consciousness, we ourselves are fundamentally changed.

> "To fail to take this into account must inevitably lead one to a serious and sustained confusion in all that one does."

The word "implicate" comes from the Latin "enfolded." When we separate individuals, effects, or disciplines from this enfolded order, we must recognize only that we have part of the unknowable totality, not the truth itself. **There are no opposites, just phases of the one phenomenon.**

## Pattern and Self-Knowledge

For the pattern student, the contemplation of the forms of life and flow enables a conscious experience of the unity of all things and the enfolded nature of Nature. In genealogy, we can become conscious of ourselves in the time and pattern stream—startlingly realizing that as origin, we "determine" (or rather define and are defined by) our ancestry as much as we define and are defined by our descent.

Patterns tell us that all is streams, all particles, all waves. Each defines the other. All is one plan. Although we find it difficult to see pattern in all of it, it is there. "We are the universe attempting to define its processes." A Kalahari bushman would say, "We are the dreams of a dreamer."

What we can never define is substance—except as process; this may be all it is. We can only know a few local patterns and thus have some weak predictive capacity. It is the pattern that our local patterns cannot know that will surprise us—the strike of cosmic lightning from an unguessed source or stress.

## Converging Worldviews

### Lovelock's Gaia Hypothesis

James Lovelock (1979) expressed the combination of scientific insights and older tribal beliefs asserting the interdependence of animate and inanimate events. The universe and this earth behave as **self-regulating and self-generated constructs**, very much akin to a single organism or a thought process. The conditions that make life possible are balanced about such fine tolerances that many processes seem to exist specifically to preserve this equilibrium.

### Birch and Cobb's Liberation of Life

Birch and Cobb (1984) deny the validity of the existence of individual organisms or separate events. All exist in a field of events or as an expression of one life force. Organisms such as ourselves exist only as an inseparable part of our event environments, in continual process of exchange with the animate and inanimate entities that surround us. **We are acted upon and acting, created and creating, shaped and shaping.**

Warwick Fox, reviewing this work, asserts: "We must view the cosmos as an infinite complex of interrelated events"; all things "are in actuality enduring societies of events."

### Capra and Theoretical Physics

Fritjof Capra (1976) and other theoretical physicists contribute to these worldviews, all of which are in conflict with current ethics governing political, educational, and economic systems. Yet all contribute to an increasing effort to unify and cooperate in a **common ethic of earth-care**, without which we have no meaning to the universe.

## Pattern Application: From Philosophy to Practice

### Perceiving vs. Imposing Pattern

There are two aspects to patterning:

1. **Perception**: Recognizing the patterns that already exist and understanding how they function
2. **Imposition**: Applying pattern to sites to achieve specific design ends

Both are skills of sophisticated design that may result in specific strategies, harmonious resolution of problems, or the production of local resources.

### Rectilinear vs. Natural Patterns

A bird's-eye view of centralized and disempowered societies reveals a strictly rectilinear network of streets, farms, and property boundaries—as though the earth were patterned to suit survey instruments rather than human or environmental needs. These straight-line patterns prevent sensible landscape planning and result in neither aesthetically nor functionally satisfactory landscapes. Once established and entered into law, such patterning is stubbornly defended—but it was created by people and can be dismantled by people.

### Hawaiian Land Division: A Model

Hawaiian villagers developed a far more sensible approach, taking natural ridgelines as boundaries. As each area was contained in one water catchment, they achieved very stable and resource-rich landscapes reaching from dense cloud forests to outer reefs. The conic and radial volcanic landscape with its radial water lines suited this method perfectly. A whole valley of people could:

- Maintain a clean catchment
- Store and divert mid-slope water resources
- Catch lost nutrients in shallow ocean enclosures (converting first to algae, then to crabs and fish)
- Preserve the offshore reef area and marine environment

Zulus and American Indians independently adopted circular or zonal modes in their plains settlements. Such models can be studied and adopted by future bioregional societies.
