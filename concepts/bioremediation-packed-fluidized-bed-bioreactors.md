## Fluidized Bed Bioreactors

### Design and Operation

Fluidized bed bioreactors contain small particles of [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] and the contaminated stream.
Free mycelial pellets can be used directly in fluidized bed
configurations.

Zhang et al. demonstrated continuous decolorization of cotton bleaching
effluent in a fluidized bed bioreactor using an unidentified white rot
fungus strain 7. When concentrated effluent (95% v/v) was treated, the
continuous fluidized bed process gave 70-80% color removal, performing
better with more dilute effluents.

### Advantages Over Packed Beds

Fluidized bed reactors offer several advantages over packed bed designs.
The continuous mixing prevents clogging and channeling that can occur
in packed beds. Better mass transfer rates improve contact between
pollutants and fungal enzymes. The system is more easily scaled and
maintained, with simpler biomass replacement procedures.

## Batch Versus Continuous Operation

Both batch and continuous methods have advantages and disadvantages.
On theoretical grounds, batch processes are more likely to achieve
complete treatment and may have kinetic advantages. If the effluent is
non-toxic, decolorization may approximate Michaelis-Menten kinetics.
More rapid reaction at high substrate concentrations would favor batch
treatment.

For toxic effluents where toxic components are degraded by the fungus,
continuous treatment may be advantageous because it maintains a lower
steady-state concentration of toxic compounds. Fed-batch approaches
represent a compromise between these two extremes.
