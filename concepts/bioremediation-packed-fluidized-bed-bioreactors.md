# Packed Bed and Fluidized Bed Bioreactors for [[fungal-bioremediation]]

Packed bed and fluidized bed bioreactors are among the most widely
studied reactor configurations for applying white rot fungi and their
enzymes to the treatment of industrial effluents. These designs offer
distinct advantages for continuous processing of colored and
contaminated wastewaters from pulp and paper, textile, and chemical
manufacturing industries.

## Packed Bed Bioreactors

### Design and Operation

Packed bed bioreactors consist of a column packed with immobilized
fungal biomass, typically in the form of mycelial pellets, or with a
support matrix colonized by the fungus. The contaminated effluent flows
through the bed, where contact with the fungal mycelium and its
extracellular enzymes brings about pollutant degradation and
decolorization. These reactors can operate in either continuous or
batch mode.

Large laboratory-scale packed bed reactors have been successfully
demonstrated. A notable example is the 200-liter packed bed reactor
used by Schliephake and Lonergan for treating Remazol Brilliant Blue R
using Pycnoporus cinnabarinus. In this system, laccase variation within
the reactor was monitored to optimize treatment performance. The packed
bed configuration was shown to be the reactor of choice for high-
concentration effluents.

### Applications in Effluent Treatment

Packed bed bioreactors have been applied to diverse effluent types.
Schliephake et al. demonstrated at least 90% color removal from a
pigment manufacturing effluent in a packed bed configuration. Palma et
al. used a packed bed bioreactor with [[phanerochaete-chrysosporium]] to
decolorize polymeric anthraquinone dyes. P. cinnabarinus was also used
in a packed bed bioreactor for the treatment of pigment plant effluent,
with effective color removal achieved.

For chlorophenol degradation, immobilized fungus in packed bed reactors
has been used to treat 2,4,6-trichlorophenol and 2,4,5-trichlorophenol
containing wastewaters. The packed bed design allows for high biomass
retention and good contact between the effluent and the fungal
degradative enzymes.

## Fluidized Bed Bioreactors

### Design and Operation

Fluidized bed bioreactors contain small particles of immobilized fungal
biomass that are suspended and kept in motion by the upward flow of
effluent or air. This design provides excellent mixing, mass transfer,
and contact between the fungal biomass and the contaminated stream.
Free mycelial pellets can be used directly in fluidized bed
configurations.

Zhang et al. demonstrated continuous decolorization of cotton bleaching
effluent in a fluidized bed bioreactor using an unidentified white rot
fungus strain 7. When concentrated effluent (95% v/v) was treated, the
continuous fluidized bed process gave 70-80% color removal, performing
better with more dilute effluents.

### Advantages Over Packed Beds

Fluidized bed reactors offer several advantages over packed bed designs.
The continuous mixing prevents clogging and channeling that can occur
in packed beds. Better mass transfer rates improve contact between
pollutants and fungal enzymes. The system is more easily scaled and
maintained, with simpler biomass replacement procedures.

## Comparison with Other Bioreactor Types

### Rotating Biological Contactors

Rotating biological contactors (RBCs) have been used to immobilize
fungal mycelium on rotating discs for effluent treatment. Cultures of
Phanerochaete chrysosporium were immobilized on RBC discs to treat TNT-
contaminated "pink water" waste streams, effectively removing both TNT
and RDX to allowable limits.

### Trickling Filters

Trickling filter processes represent another approach where effluent
is distributed over a bed of fungal biomass supported on inert media.
This design provides good aeration and is well-suited for aerobic
treatment processes requiring oxygen for enzyme function.

### Silicone Membrane Biofilm Reactors

A silicone membrane biofilm reactor has been used to study fungal
lignin peroxidase production and activity. The membrane configuration
allows precise control of gas exchange, particularly oxygen supply,
which is critical for peroxidase function.

## Batch Versus Continuous Operation

Both batch and continuous methods have advantages and disadvantages.
On theoretical grounds, batch processes are more likely to achieve
complete treatment and may have kinetic advantages. If the effluent is
non-toxic, decolorization may approximate Michaelis-Menten kinetics.
More rapid reaction at high substrate concentrations would favor batch
treatment.

For toxic effluents where toxic components are degraded by the fungus,
continuous treatment may be advantageous because it maintains a lower
steady-state concentration of toxic compounds. Fed-batch approaches
represent a compromise between these two extremes.

## Biomass Longevity and Reuse

One of the most important practical considerations for bioreactor
operation is the longevity and reusability of the fungal biomass.
Several studies have reported the repeated use of white rot mycelia
over many cycles of treatment, covering periods of several weeks to
months. Mycelia are rugged enough to withstand repeated use and can
be stored at 4 degrees Celsius for several months while retaining full
decolorization activity.

## Contamination Considerations

Most studies have employed aseptic conditions and axenic cultures of
white rot fungi. Some studies have found that effective treatment can
occur under non-aseptic conditions, particularly for wood pulping
effluents where high temperature, alkaline pH, and the presence of
sodium sulfite inhibit competing microorganisms. Contamination often
leads to treatment failure in other systems.
