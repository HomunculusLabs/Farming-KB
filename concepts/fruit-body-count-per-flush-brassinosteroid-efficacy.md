# Fruit Body Count per Flush as a Measure of Brassinosteroid Efficacy

## Overview

The 1990 Gartz study documented a striking difference in fruit body
counts between brassinosteroid-treated and control cultures of
Psilocybe cubensis. In the first flush on dung/grain substrate, treated
cultures produced 4–7 mushrooms compared to 1–3 in the control. This
metric — individual fruit body count per flush — provides a distinct
measure of cultivation efficacy that complements total dry mass yield.

## The Data: First Flush Comparison

| Metric | Control | Brassinosteroid-treated |
|--------|---------|------------------------|
| First flush fruit body count | 1–3 | 4–7 |
| Time to first flush | 4–5 weeks | 3–3.5 weeks |
| Dry mass per 10 g substrate | 2.3–2.9 g | 3.4–3.9 g |

The fruit body count increase (roughly 2–3×) is notable because it
exceeds the dry mass increase (roughly 1.3–1.5×). This means that while
total yield increased, the individual mushrooms in treated cultures
were slightly smaller on average than in the control. The brassinosteroid
promoted more numerous primordia reaching maturity rather than larger
individual mushrooms.

## Biological Interpretation: Pin Set vs. Pin Development

The fruit body count per flush reflects two sequential processes:

1. **Pin initiation** ([[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]]): The number of mushroom
   primordia that form on the casing or substrate surface.

2. **Pin development** (abortion vs. maturation): The proportion of
   primordia that develop into harvestable fruit bodies rather than
   aborting.

The brassinosteroid could influence either or both processes. The data
suggests a primary effect on pin initiation (more primordia formed) with
a possible secondary effect on reducing pin abortion (more primordia
reaching maturity). The study's observation that incomplete fruit body
formation was "almost completely suppressed" supports the latter
interpretation.

## Relationship Between Count and Mass

In [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], fruit body count and total mass often trade
off. When many mushrooms share a fixed nutrient supply, each individual
tends to be smaller. This is the pattern seen in the Gartz data: treated
cultures produced more mushrooms with a moderate increase in total mass.

For cultivators, the optimal balance depends on the intended use:
- **Many small mushrooms**: Higher surface-area-to-volume ratio, which
  affects drying efficiency and dosing consistency.
- **Fewer large mushrooms**: Easier to handle, harvest, and process.

The brassinosteroid shifts the balance toward more numerous fruit bodies
without sacrificing total yield, which may be advantageous when
consistent sizing is desired for research or commercial purposes.

## Flush-to-Flush Variation

The study notes "a variation of the number of formed fruit bodies in the
five flushes during fructification." This flush-to-flush variation is a
well-known phenomenon in mushroom cultivation:

- **First flush**: Typically the largest or second-largest in terms of
  both count and mass. The substrate's nutrient reserves are at their
  peak.
- **Second flush**: Often the largest flush as the [[gadd-mycelial-network-dynamics]]
  fully adapts to the fruiting environment.
- **Third through fifth flushes**: Progressive decline as substrate
  nutrients are depleted and the mycelial network ages.

The brassinosteroid's effect was most dramatically visible in the first
flush, where the treated cultures' 4–7 mushrooms contrasted sharply with
the control's 1–3. This timing advantage — earlier and more numerous
fruiting — represents a significant practical benefit for cultivation
efficiency.

## Quantification Methodology

The Gartz study counted individual fruit bodies per flush across five
flushes, providing discrete count data rather than only aggregate mass.
This methodological choice reveals biological detail that mass-only
measurements would miss. For example, a culture producing two large
mushrooms and one producing six small ones might have identical dry mass
but very different biological characteristics.

Modern cultivation research increasingly recognizes the value of
multi-parameter yield assessment, including fruit body count, average
individual mass, size distribution, and morphological quality alongside
total dry mass.

## Implications for Commercial Cultivation

If [[annulus-loss-morphological-effect-brassinosteroid-treatment]] were applied commercially, the increased
fruit body count per flush would have several practical implications:

- **Harvest efficiency**: More mushrooms per flush means fewer total
  flushes needed to achieve a target yield, reducing labor and time.
- **Earlier harvest**: The 1–2 week acceleration in first flush timing
  improves crop rotation efficiency.
- **Reduced abort waste**: The [[brassinosteroid-suppression-of-incomplete-fruit-body-formation]] fruit body
  formation means less substrate is wasted on non-productive growth.
