# AACT Compost Extract, Leachate, and Tea Distinctions

## Overview

One of the most important contributions of Elaine Ingham's field guide is the rigorous differentiation between liquid products derived from compost. These are frequently confused, yet they have fundamentally different biological properties, [[hashish-production-methods]], and applications. Ingham insists that only [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] Compost Tea (AACT) meets the definition of true compost tea.

## Compost Tea (AACT) — The Standard

True compost tea is a **water extract of compost that is brewed**. The brewing process is what distinguishes it from extracts and leachates.

### Definition and Characteristics

- Compost is placed in water in a mesh bag or basket
- Nutrients are added to enhance beneficial microbial growth
- Active aeration is maintained throughout brewing (typically 18-24 hours)
- Organisms extracted from compost wake up and multiply
- Result contains active, diverse communities of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes
- Must contain all major groups of beneficial organisms from the source compost
- Aerobic conditions prevent growth of anaerobic pathogens

### What Disqualifies a Product

A product cannot be called compost tea if:
- It lacks beneficial fungi, protozoa, and nematodes
- Conditions were anaerobic, killing aerobic organisms
- It contains only soluble nutrients without the biological component
- No brewing period allowed organisms to activate and multiply

## Compost Extract

Compost extracts lack a brewing period, making them fundamentally different.

### Definition and Properties

- Compost is placed in water and agitated to physically rip organisms off particles
- No nutrients added, no brewing period allowed
- Organisms have not had time to wake from dormancy
- [[fungi-convention-on-biological-diversity]] limited to what was on the compost surface
- No multiplication of organisms occurs during extraction

### Use Cases

- Quick application when brewing time isn't available
- Soil drench where dormant organisms can wake in the soil environment
- Emergency applications where full tea-making is impractical

## Compost Leachate

The most passive and least biologically active product.

### Definition and Contents

- Water passes through compost passively (gravity, rainfall, irrigation)
- No active extraction, no nutrients added, no brewing or aeration
- Primarily soluble nutrients, enzymes, hormones, humic and fulvic acids
- Very limited biological content; may contain [[ingham-anaerobic-organisms-role-soil]]
- Growth responses come from soluble nutrients, not biological activity

### Limitations

- Cannot provide [[aact-banana-cultivation-tropical-crop-disease-suppression-ingham]] benefits of AACT
- Pathogen risk may be higher if compost had anaerobic zones
- Useful only as liquid fertilizer, not biological inoculant

## Plant Tea and Manure Tea

### Plant Tea
- Water extract of plant materials (no compost involved)
- Different organism profile than compost tea

### Manure Tea
- Made from animal manure (not composted material)
- If material hasn't been completely composted, it isn't compost
- Partly composted material may contain human pathogens

## Sun Tea and Fermentative Tea

### Sun Tea
- Compost in water in the sun without active aeration
- Typically goes anaerobic; not considered proper compost tea
- UV damage is minimal but desiccation and temperature are concerns
