## Overview

## Compost Tea (AACT) — The Standard

True compost tea is a **water extract of compost that is brewed**. The brewing process is what distinguishes it from extracts and leachates.

### Definition and Characteristics

- Compost is placed in water in a mesh bag or basket
- Nutrients are added to enhance beneficial microbial growth
- Active aeration is maintained throughout brewing (typically 18-24 hours)
- Organisms extracted from compost wake up and multiply
- Result contains active, diverse communities of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes
- Must contain all major groups of beneficial organisms from the source compost
- Aerobic conditions prevent growth of anaerobic pathogens

### What Disqualifies a Product

A product cannot be called compost tea if:
- It lacks beneficial fungi, protozoa, and nematodes
- Conditions were anaerobic, killing aerobic organisms
- It contains only soluble nutrients without the biological component
- No brewing period allowed organisms to activate and multiply

## Compost Extract

Compost extracts lack a brewing period, making them fundamentally different.

### Definition and Properties

- Compost is placed in water and agitated to physically rip organisms off particles
- No nutrients added, no brewing period allowed
- Organisms have not had time to wake from dormancy
- [[ingham-anaerobic-organisms-role-soil]]
- Growth responses come from soluble nutrients, not biological activity
