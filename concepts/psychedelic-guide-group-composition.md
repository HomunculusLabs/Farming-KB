## Psychedelic Guide, Group Composition, Set and Setting

> The three pillars of a well-conducted [[psychedelic-session-instructions]], as articulated in *The [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]* by [[timothy-leary]], [[ralph-metzner]], and Richard Alpert — adapted from [[jung-and-the-tibetan-book-of-the-dead]] of the Dead.

## Overview

Leary's manual identifies **[[set-and-setting]]** as the two primary variables determining the quality and direction of a psychedelic experience, with the **[[psychedelic-guide]]** and **group composition** serving as critical extensions of the setting. When the ego and cognitive mind are suspended under psychedelics, the external environment and the people within it gain enormous influence over the voyage.

## Set and Setting Framework

### Long-Range Set

The long-range set encompasses the voyager's entire personality history and psychological constitution. Key dimensions include:

- **Fears and desires** — unresolved emotional material that may surface
- **Defenses** — psychological rigidities that can create resistance and suffering
- **Flexibility vs. rigidity** — the capacity to let go and flow with the experience
- **Capacity for insight** — openness to new perspectives and self-understanding

This deep psychological backdrop is largely fixed for a given session; preparation works primarily on the immediate set.

### Immediate Set

The immediate set refers to the expectations and mindset the voyager brings into the session. Leary catalogues several common expectancy patterns:

- **Medical expectations** — scanning for symptoms, adopting a "patient" role
- **Rebellion against convention** — using the drug as an act of defiance
- **Intellectual expectations** — seeking to "figure out" the experience rather than surrender to it; the manual advises novitiates to "turn your mind off"
- **Religious expectations** — the most constructive orientation; "float with the stream"
- **Recreational/aesthetic expectations** — seeking pleasure and beauty, which can become "tender traps" that prevent pure ego-loss
- **Planned expectations per the manual** — the recommended approach, where the voyager understands the ego-death trajectory and prepares to let go

### Practical Preparation

Leary recommends a minimum of **three days**: the day before the session, the session day itself, and a follow-up day. Additional preparation includes:

- Talking with experienced voyagers about their sessions
- Observing a session before undergoing one
- Reading mystical and philosophical accounts (Huxley, Watts, Wasson)
- **Meditation** — described as the single best preparation for the psychedelic experience

## The Setting

The physical and social environment must be deliberately arranged to support the inner journey.

### Physical Environment

- **Removed from social games** — the setting should be insulated from ordinary social pressures and obligations
- **Privacy is essential** — no unexpected visitors, no intrusions
- **The three-day period** — a dedicated block of time free from external demands
- **Night vs. day sessions** — each offers different qualities; night sessions encourage inwardness
- **Outdoor vs. indoor** — natural settings (gardens, beaches, forests) can be profoundly supportive
- **Comfort and familiarity** — the space should feel safe and welcoming

### Sensory Elements

- **Music** — carefully selected to support the journey's phases
- **Lighting** — adjustable, soft, non-intrusive
- **Food** — simple, ancient foods: bread, cheese, wine, fresh fruit

### Room Arrangement

- **Star pattern beds** — individual sleeping areas arranged radially around the room
- **An extra room** — available for seclusion when a voyager needs solitude
- **Collaborative decisions** — all participants share in preparing and arranging the space

## The Psychedelic Guide

The guide's attitude and behavior are among the most critical factors in a session's outcome. With the voyager's cognitive mind suspended, the guide possesses **enormous power to shape the experience** — for better or worse.

### Core Requirements

- **Ability to turn off one's own ego and social games** — the guide must not impose their personality on the experience
- **Muffle power needs and fears** — the guide should be free from the need to control or dominate
- **"Creative quietism"** — a Taoist wisdom of non-interference; providing stability without direction
- **Considerable [[palmer-mushroom-cooking-and-personal-experience]]** — the guide must have navigated psychedelic territory extensively

### The Ground Control Analogy

Leary likens the guide to an airport control tower operator. Just as an air traffic controller would never dream of imposing their own personality on the pilots they guide, the psychedelic guide provides calm, competent assistance without making the journey about themselves.

### The Problem of Fear

Leary identifies **fear** as the greatest danger in guided sessions. He states that almost every negative LSD reaction is caused not by the drug itself but by the **guide's fear augmenting the subject's transient fear**. A guide who becomes anxious or controlling can trigger cascading panic.

### Guide Routine

- **One trained person takes a low dose** — maintaining empathic connection while retaining functional awareness
- **One person stays completely sober** — available for practical needs and emergencies
- **Guides taking large doses are not recommended** for sessions with new or inexperienced voyagers
- **"The guide is literally a liberator"** — freeing the voyager from ego-constriction and social conditioning

### Built-in Protection Against Professionalism

Leary notes that the nature of the psychedelic experience itself provides protection against the guide becoming a detached professional. The work demands genuine personal involvement and ego-transcendence that resists routinization.

## Group Composition

### Trust as Foundation

Trust among all participants is the non-negotiable foundation. Strangers should **share time and space together before the session** to establish comfort and rapport.

### Collaborative Goals

All members should share aligned intentions for the session. Mixed or conflicting agendas create turbulence.

### Group Size

- **Maximum 6–7 voyagers** — beyond this, experiences tend to become less profound and more paranoid
- **5–6 voyagers require 2 guides** — adequate supervision and support
- Larger groups dilute the intensity and create social dynamics that interfere with inner exploration

### The Role of the Experienced Woman Guide

Leary specifically recommends an **experienced woman guide** for her capacity for spiritual nurturing — providing a maternal, accepting presence that supports vulnerability and ego-transcendence.

### Married Couples

Married couples should have their **initial [[bardo-instruction-texts-psychedelic-sessions]] separately**. The emotional intensity of a first ego-loss experience, when shared simultaneously with a partner, can create confusion between psychedelic content and relationship dynamics.

## Synthesis

Leary's framework presents the psychedelic session as a carefully orchestrated sacred space where set, setting, guide, and group form an interconnected system. The guide's highest function is **invisible service** — creating conditions so supportive that the voyager can surrender completely to the experience without awareness of external influence. Success is measured not by what the guide does, but by the freedom the voyager feels to explore their own consciousness.
