# Alpha-1,3-Glucan and Aspergillus fumigatus Virulence

The [[gadd-fungal-cell-structure]] wall is a dynamic structure continuously chemically
modified during morphogenesis, playing essential roles in sensing
environments and providing adaptive responses to osmotic pressures and
host defence reactions. In Aspergillus fumigatus and other human
[[bloomfield-fungal-pathogens-and-parasites]], alpha-1,3-glucan is a major cell wall polysaccharide
with critical implications for virulence.

## Cell wall composition overview

The fungal cell wall accounts for over 90% polysaccharide mass,
consisting of three basic components: glucans, mannan, and chitin. The
fibrillar skeleton comprises alkali-insoluble beta-1,3/beta-1,6 glucans
linked to chitin via beta(1-4) linkages. This core is present in all
ascomycetes and basidiomycetes. The amorphous alkali-soluble fraction
varies between species: mannans and branched beta-glucans in Candida
and Saccharomyces, but alpha-1,3-glucan and galactomannan in
Aspergillus species and Schizosaccharomyces pombe.

## Distribution of alpha-1,3-glucan among pathogens

Alpha-1,3-glucan has been identified in multiple human pathogenic
fungi including [[blastomyces-dermatitidis]], [[histoplasma-capsulatum]],
[[paracoccidioides-brasiliensis]], and [[bloomfield-cryptococcus-neoformans-capsule-melanin-brain]]. In many of
these pathogens, alpha-1,3-glucan is present in high concentrations
and [[hofmann-lsd-clinical-research-early-studies]] showed it plays a role in fungal virulence. It can
account for up to 50% of total cell-wall carbohydrates.

## Structural features

Alpha-1,3-glucan is alkali-soluble and can be purified by periodic
oxidation and mild acid hydrolysis. In S. pombe, 28% of alpha-1,3-
glucans are in the cell wall as either fibrillar alkali-insoluble or
amorphous alkali-soluble material. They consist mainly of 1,3-linked
alpha-glucose with approximately 7% 1,4-glycosidic linkages located at
the reducing end and centre, interconnecting chains of approximately
120 glucose residues. In A. fumigatus, preliminary studies suggest a
branched structure.

## Compensatory cell wall biosynthesis

A 50% reduction of chitin content in a double chitin synthase
(chsE-chsG) mutant of A. fumigatus is compensated by increased
alpha-1,3-glucan content. This was the first demonstration that an
amorphous component can compensate for cell-wall defects. Previous
studies showed only chitin increases compensating for defects in
mannan or beta-1,3-glucan, suggesting alpha-1,3-glucan plays a more
important structural role than originally thought.

## AGS genes: alpha-1,3-glucan synthases

One to five AGS genes encoding putative alpha-1,3-glucan synthases
exist in fungal species. C. neoformans and H. capsulatum have one AGS
gene, while A. fumigatus has three and S. pombe has five. AGS genes are
the largest (approximately 8 kb) involved in cell wall synthesis,
encoding predicted proteins of approximately 2400 amino acids with
molecular masses of approximately 272 kDa. Amino acid sequences are
39-48% identical between species.

## Domain structure of Ags proteins

The Ags proteins contain five structural domains: (1) a hydrophobic
N-terminal signal peptide; (2) approximately 1000 residues with three
transmembrane domains and 23-32% identity to glycosylhydrolases; (3) a
connecting transmembrane segment; (4) approximately 1000 residues with
25-30% identity to glycogen synthases; (5) a C-terminal region with 12
membrane-spanning domains. Consensus UDP-glucose binding sequences were
found in the glycoside hydrolase and glycogen synthase domains.
