## Overview

The PF TEK specifies half-pint wide-mouth canning jars (8 oz / 237 ml) as the sole acceptable jar size. [[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] emphasized this requirement repeatedly throughout the PF TEK documentation, stating that the reason for using small half-pint jars is to keep the substrate mass small enough for steam sterilization to work. The wide-mouth feature is equally critical, enabling both proper substrate loading and easy removal of the colonized cake for fruiting.

## The Physics of Jar Size and Sterilization

### Heat Penetration and Substrate Mass

The fundamental relationship between jar size and sterilization effectiveness is one of thermal dynamics:

- A half-pint jar contains approximately 237 ml of substrate
- The small mass allows heat from steam to penetrate to the center of the jar
- Internal temperatures reach near-boiling throughout the entire substrate volume
- Larger jars create a thermal gradient — the center remains cooler than the exterior
- Cooler center regions may not reach temperatures sufficient to kill all contaminants

PF stated this principle explicitly: "The reason the PF TEK uses small half pint canning jars is to keep the substrate mass small enough so that the internal temperature of the jar will reach near boiling temperatures and sterilize the substrate."

### What Happens with Larger Jars

When growers deviate from half-pint jars and use larger sizes:
- Quart jars (approximately 946 ml) contain roughly 4 times the substrate mass
- Steam at atmospheric pressure cannot penetrate this mass adequately
- The center of the substrate may remain 10-20°C below the surface temperature
- Endospores and contaminant organisms survive in the cooler center
- Contamination appears weeks later when surviving bacteria or mold sporulate

This is why larger jars require [[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]] — the elevated temperature (121°C vs 100°C) provides enough thermal energy to penetrate the larger mass even with the reduced efficiency of steam at atmospheric pressure.

## Wide-Mouth vs Regular-Mouth Jars

### The Wide-Mouth Requirement

PF TEK requires wide-mouth canning jars specifically, not regular-mouth jars. The distinction matters for two critical operations:

**Substrate loading:** Wide-mouth jars have an opening approximately 7 cm (2.75 inches) in diameter, while regular-mouth jars have an opening of approximately 6 cm (2.375 inches). The wider opening makes it significantly easier to fill the jar with the loose vermiculite and brown rice flour substrate mixture without spillage and to level the substrate surface and add the dry vermiculite barrier layer on top.

**Cake removal:** This is the more critical factor. After colonization, the substrate must be removed from the jar intact for fruiting. The wide-mouth opening allows the fully colonized cake to slide out with minimal resistance. Regular-mouth jars create a bottleneck that can damage or contaminate the cake during removal.

### The Sliding-Out Process

Removing a colonized cake from a wide-mouth jar involves:
1. Loosening the substrate from the jar walls by gently tapping and shaking
2. Inverting the jar over a clean surface
3. The cake slides out through the wide opening in one piece
4. The cake is then placed in the fruiting terrarium

With regular-mouth jars, the narrower opening causes:
- Resistance as the cake passes through the shoulder of the jar
- Risk of the cake breaking apart during removal
- Contamination from handling required to extract a stuck cake
- Potential damage to the [[gadd-mycelial-network-dynamics]] at the cake surface

## Half-Pint vs Other Sizes

### Standard Half-Pint (8 oz / 237 ml) — Recommended

- Optimal substrate mass for steam sterilization
- Fast colonization (typically 2-4 weeks)
- Good fruiting surface area relative to substrate volume
- Easy handling and terrarium placement
- Readily available at grocery and hardware stores

### 3/4 Pint Jars — Not Recommended

- Slightly larger substrate mass may compromise steam sterilization
- Longer colonization times
- May still work with extended sterilization times but results are inconsistent

### Pint Jars (16 oz / 473 ml) — Not Recommended

- Double the substrate mass of half-pint jars
- Steam sterilization unreliable for this mass
- Significantly longer colonization times
- Higher contamination rates without [[boil-a-bag-pressure-sterilization-technique]]
- Cakes are heavier and more prone to damage during handling

### Quart Jars (32 oz / 946 ml) — Incompatible

- Far too large for steam sterilization at atmospheric pressure
- Requires pressure canning
- Not part of the PF TEK methodology
- Suitable for grain spawn preparation (a different technique entirely)

## Jar Lid Configuration for Half-Pint Jars

The half-pint jar lid in the PF TEK is modified for both gas exchange and contamination protection:

1. **Inoculation holes:** Four holes are punched through the center of each jar lid using a nail or similar pointed instrument. These holes allow spore solution to be injected into the substrate and provide gas exchange during colonization.

2. **Tape covering:** The holes are covered with tape (masking tape, first aid tape, or similar). PF recommended using a single layer of tape over the inoculation holes to prevent airborne contaminants from entering while allowing minimal gas exchange. The tape is applied after the holes are punched and before sterilization.

3. **Removal during fruiting:** When cakes are placed in the terrarium for fruiting, the tape is removed from the jar lids (if jars are used as fruiting chambers) or the cakes are removed from jars entirely.

## Practical Considerations

### Jar Acquisition

Half-pint wide-mouth canning jars are widely available:
- Grocery stores (canning supply section)
- Hardware stores
- Online retailers
- Seasonal availability peaks in late summer and fall (canning season)
- Ball and Kerr brands are the most common in [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]

### Jar Reuse

Canning jars are designed for repeated use:
- Clean thoroughly after each use
- Inspect for chips or cracks before reuse
- Replace lids if rusted or damaged (the rubber sealing ring degrades over time)
- Sterilize before each use as part of the PF TEK process

### Cost Economics

Half-pint jars are inexpensive relative to other cultivation supplies:
- A case of 12 jars typically costs $10-15 USD
- Jars last through dozens of cultivation cycles
- The cost per use is negligible compared to spore syringes or [[growing-gourmet-substrate-materials-wood-types]]
- No ongoing jar expenses once initial set is purchased

## Why the Jar Size Matters for Fruiting

Beyond sterilization, half-pint jars produce optimal fruiting cakes:

- **Surface area to volume ratio:** Smaller cakes have more surface area relative to their volume, which means more area for pin formation (primordia development)
- **Moisture management:** Smaller cakes maintain appropriate moisture levels more easily than larger ones
- **Handling:** Lighter cakes are easier to handle without damage during transfer to fruiting chambers
- **Air circulation:** Smaller cakes allow better air circulation in the terrarium around each cake
- **Contaminant exposure:** If one cake contaminates, it can be quickly removed without affecting nearby cakes
