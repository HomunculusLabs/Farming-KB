**Food dehydrator drying** uses controlled heat and airflow to accelerate
drying. Temperatures in the range of 95-115°F (35-46°C) are generally
considered safe for psilocybin mushrooms. Higher temperatures can accelerate
degradation. The reduced drying time (12-24 hours) limits oxidative exposure.

**Desiccant drying**, as recommended in the PF Tek, uses chemical desiccants
to absorb moisture from the mushrooms without applying heat. This method
operates at room temperature or below, minimizing thermal degradation, while
the sealed container environment limits oxygen exposure. Drying time is
typically 2-7 days depending on the desiccant capacity and mushroom size.

**Oven drying** is generally discouraged because the temperatures involved
(140°F+) can cause significant degradation of psilocybin and psilocin. The
rapid water removal also concentrates the active compounds before they are
fully stabilized, potentially accelerating enzymatic reactions.

## Desiccant Chemistry and Selection

The PF Tek recommended using silica gel or similar desiccants for mushroom
drying. Several desiccant options are available, each with different properties:

**Silica gel** is the most common and readily available desiccant. It is a
form of silicon dioxide with an enormous internal surface area that adsorbs
water molecules through surface interactions. Indicating silica gel contains
cobalt chloride or other color-changing compounds that signal when the gel is
saturated and needs regeneration.

**Drierite (anhydrous calcium sulfate)** is a more aggressive desiccant that
can achieve lower humidity levels than silica gel. It is commonly used in
laboratory settings and is available from scientific supply companies.

**Calcium chloride** is inexpensive and highly effective but can become
liquid as it absorbs water (deliquescence), requiring containment in a
breathable packet rather than loose placement.

For mushroom drying, silica gel is the most practical choice due to its
availability, reusability, and non-deliquescent nature.

## PF Tek Desiccant Drying Procedure

The basic procedure involves placing dried mushrooms in a sealed container
with desiccant material. A common setup uses a plastic food storage container
with a layer of desiccant at the bottom, separated from the mushrooms by a
permeable barrier such as paper towel or wire screen.

The container should be sealed to prevent ambient humidity from entering.
Mushrooms should not touch the desiccant directly, as some desiccants can
cause chemical contamination or physical damage to the dried material.

Regular monitoring is important. As the desiccant absorbs moisture, its
capacity decreases. Fresh or regenerated desiccant should be added if drying
progress stalls before the mushrooms are completely crisp.

## Long-Term Storage After Drying

Once mushrooms are fully dried (crisp and brittle, with no flexibility in
the stems), they should be stored in a manner that continues to protect
against the three degradation factors: heat, moisture, and oxygen.

Vacuum sealing in plastic bags is effective for moisture and oxygen exclusion.
Adding a small packet of desiccant to the sealed bag provides additional
moisture protection. Storage in a cool, dark location (refrigerator or
freezer for longest-term storage) minimizes thermal degradation.
