# Psilocybe Natalensis: Africa's First Documented Psychoactive Psilocybe

In January 1994, Jochen Gartz and M. Smith discovered a bluing Psilocybe species in Natal Province, South Africa -- the first psychotropic Psilocybe species ever found in the area. The discovery was published as Psilocybe natalensis Gartz, Reid, Ecker & Smith, adding a significant new species to the African psychoactive mycoflora and providing support for the hypothesis of an ancient [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]]

### Psilocybe Mairei

P. mairei (originally Hypholoma cyanescens, collected by Maire in Algeria starting in 1912) is a bluing African Psilocybe that grows on raw compost under cedar trees. ### Biogeographic Implications

The presence of psychoactive Psilocybe species across Africa (North Africa, East Africa, and South Africa) suggests that the continent's psychoactive mycoflora is more diverse and widespread than previously assumed. The fact that Africa -- the cradle of humanity -- harbors these species supports the hypothesis that human use of psychoactive mushrooms may be as old as the human species itself.
