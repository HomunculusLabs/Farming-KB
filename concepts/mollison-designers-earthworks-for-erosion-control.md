## Contour Banks

Contour banks are earth embankments constructed along the contour of a slope
to intercept surface runoff and prevent concentrated water flow from gaining
destructive velocity. Unlike swales, which are designed primarily for
infiltration, contour banks are designed for safe water disposal, directing
collected runoff to stable discharge points at non-erosive velocities.

Contour banks typically have a broad, gently sloping channel on the upslope
side and a compacted berm on the downslope side. Spacing depends on slope
gradient, soil type, and rainfall intensity. On gentle slopes, banks may be
spaced 50 to 100 meters apart; on steeper slopes, spacing narrows to 10 to
30 meters. Banks must have properly designed grassed waterways for safe
discharge, or they will concentrate flow and create worse erosion.

## Diversion Drains

Diversion drains intercept water flowing from upslope areas and redirect it
around or away from vulnerable sites. They are essential above buildings,
roads, and cultivated areas where concentrated runoff would cause damage.
Mollison recommends positioning diversion drains well above the area to be
protected, with sufficient capacity to handle peak storm flows.

Diversion drains should have a minimum grade of 1 to 2 percent to prevent
ponding and sediment accumulation. The drain cross-section must be sized for
the expected peak flow from the contributing catchment. Outfall points must
be stabilized with rock, vegetation, or energy dissipators to [[query-how-do-i-garden-on-a-slope-and-prevent-erosion]]
at the discharge location.

## Check Dams

Check dams are small barriers constructed across drainage channels or
watercourses to reduce flow velocity, trap sediment, and allow water to
infiltrate. They are particularly effective in gully rehabilitation, where
they step the water flow down a gradient rather than allowing it to cut
deeper into the channel.

Check dams can be constructed from a wide range of materials: stone, timber,
brush, sandbags, or gabions. The key [[design-principle-integration]] is that the dam height
should not exceed the channel depth at the construction point, preventing
flow from cutting around the sides. A series of check dams placed at intervals
up a gully can progressively fill the channel with sediment and eventually
stabilize the entire gully system.

## Gabion Walls and Structures

Gabions are wire cages filled with rock or stone, used for retaining walls,
check dams, bank stabilization, and energy dissipation. They are flexible,
permeable, and strong, making them ideal for erosion control in situations
where rigid concrete structures would crack or fail. Gabions allow water to
flow through while trapping sediment, and vegetation can establish in the
gaps between rocks, further stabilizing the structure over time.

Gabion retaining walls stabilize cut-and-fill slopes along roads, building
sites, and drainage channels. Their permeability prevents water buildup behind
the wall that could cause structural failure. Mollison recommends gabions as
the preferred construction material for many erosion control applications
because they are affordable, durable, and can be built with unskilled labor
using locally available stone.
