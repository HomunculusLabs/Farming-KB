## Pesticide Classes and Environmental Impact

Major pesticide classes subject to fungal degradation include:

- Organochlorines: Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane (DDT), lindane
  (gamma-hexachlorocyclohexane), endosulfan, dieldrin, and aldrin.
  These compounds are highly persistent due to their chlorine
  substitutions and resistance to biological attack. They bioaccumulate
  in food chains and many are classified as persistent organic
  pollutants (POPs) under the Stockholm Convention.

- Organophosphates: Chlorpyrifos, malathion, parathion, and diazinon.
  These compounds inhibit acetylcholinesterase and are acutely toxic.
  They are generally more biodegradable than organochlorines but still
  persist in soil for weeks to months.

- Triazine herbicides: Atrazine, simazine, and propazine. Atrazine is
  among the most widely used herbicides globally and is a common
  groundwater contaminant. Its s-triazine ring is relatively resistant
  to biological degradation.

- Carbamates: Carbofuran, carbaryl, and methomyl. Like organophosphates,
  they inhibit acetylcholinesterase and are moderately persistent.

- Pyrethroids: Synthetic analogs of natural pyrethrins; used in
  agriculture and household [[trametes-versicolor-entity]] oxidizes the phenolic moiety of
  chlorophenoxy herbicides, leading to dechlorination and ring
  cleavage.

- MnP from [[bloomfield-boletus-satanus-edible-mushroom-hazards]] with demonstrated atrazine,
  endosulfan, and chlorpyrifos degradation.

- [[staycare-cunninghamella-elegans-in-bioremediation]]: Zygomycetes that
  transform organochlorine and organophosphate pesticides through
  cytochrome P450-mediated oxidation.

## Atrazine Degradation

Atrazine receives particular attention in the Singh source as a
model recalcitrant herbicide. Fungal atrazine degradation involves:

- Initial dealkylation of the ethyl and isopropyl side chains
  by cytochrome P450, producing deisopropylatrazine and
  deethylatrazine.

- Subsequent dechlorination and ring cleavage by laccase or
  peroxidase systems.

- Complete mineralization to CO2 and NH3 has been demonstrated
  for some white-rot fungal strains.
