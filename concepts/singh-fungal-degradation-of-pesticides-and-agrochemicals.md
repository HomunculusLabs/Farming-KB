# [[fungal-degradation-of-pesticides]] and Agrochemicals

## Overview

Pesticides and agrochemicals represent a diverse group of synthetic
organic compounds applied to agricultural systems worldwide. Their
persistence in soil and water poses significant ecological and human
health risks. The Singh source documents the substantial capacity of
fungi, particularly ligninolytic species, to transform and mineralize
a wide range of pesticide classes through both enzymatic and
non-enzymatic mechanisms. The [[biochemistry-of-ligninolytic-fungi-bioremediation]]
underlying these processes involves powerful extracellular enzyme systems.

## Pesticide Classes and Environmental Impact

Major pesticide classes subject to fungal degradation include:

- Organochlorines: Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane (DDT), lindane
  (gamma-hexachlorocyclohexane), endosulfan, dieldrin, and aldrin.
  These compounds are highly persistent due to their chlorine
  substitutions and resistance to biological attack. They bioaccumulate
  in food chains and many are classified as persistent organic
  pollutants (POPs) under the Stockholm Convention.

- Organophosphates: Chlorpyrifos, malathion, parathion, and diazinon.
  These compounds inhibit acetylcholinesterase and are acutely toxic.
  They are generally more biodegradable than organochlorines but still
  persist in soil for weeks to months.

- Triazine herbicides: Atrazine, simazine, and propazine. Atrazine is
  among the most widely used herbicides globally and is a common
  groundwater contaminant. Its s-triazine ring is relatively resistant
  to biological degradation.

- Carbamates: Carbofuran, carbaryl, and methomyl. Like organophosphates,
  they inhibit acetylcholinesterase and are moderately persistent.

- Pyrethroids: Synthetic analogs of natural pyrethrins; used in
  agriculture and household insect control. Moderately persistent
  in soil.

- Chlorophenoxy herbicides: 2,4-D, 2,4,5-T, and MCPA. These mimic
  auxin plant hormones and are moderately biodegradable.

## Fungal Metabolism Pathways

The Singh source describes several fungal metabolic pathways for
pesticide degradation:

### Oxidative Degradation

Lignin-modifying enzymes (laccase, MnP, LiP) oxidize pesticide
molecules through radical-mediated mechanisms. The role of [[brown-rot-and-white-rot-fungi-in-mycoremediation]] is especially important here:

- Laccase from [[trametes-versicolor]] oxidizes the phenolic moiety of
  chlorophenoxy herbicides, leading to dechlorination and ring
  cleavage.

- MnP from [[phanerochaete-chrysosporium]] degrades organophosphate
  insecticides through Mn(III)-mediated oxidation.

- LiP catalyzes oxidative dechlorination of DDT, converting it to
  DDD and DDE, and subsequently to more polar, degradable metabolites.

### Hydrolytic Degradation

Fungal esterases, amidases, and phosphotriesterases catalyze the
hydrolysis of ester, amide, and phosphoester bonds in pesticides:

- Carbofuran is hydrolyzed to carbofuran phenol by fungal carboxylesterases.

- Pyrethroids undergo ester bond hydrolysis, producing acid and
  alcohol fragments that are further metabolized.

### Reductive Dechlorination

Some fungi mediate reductive dechlorination of organochlorine
pesticides under low-oxygen conditions:

- Endosulfan is converted to endosulfan diol through reductive
  pathways.

- Lindane undergoes sequential dechlorination to less chlorinated
  cyclohexane derivatives.

### Conjugation Reactions

Fungi conjugate pesticide metabolites with endogenous compounds
(glucose, sulfate, glutathione, amino acids), reducing toxicity
and increasing water solubility:

- Glucosyl conjugation of atrazine metabolites.

- Glutathione conjugation of chlorophenoxy herbicide intermediates.

## Key Degrader Species

- Trametes versicolor: Broad-spectrum pesticide degrader; laccase
  system targets chlorophenoxy herbicides, triazines, and
  organophosphates.

- Phanerochaete chrysosporium: LiP and MnP systems degrade DDT,
  lindane, and atrazine.

- [[pleurotus-ostreatus]]: Edible mushroom with demonstrated atrazine,
  endosulfan, and chlorpyrifos degradation.

- [[ganoderma-lucidum]]: Degrades DDT and lindane through ligninolytic
  and cytochrome P450 pathways.

- Mucor circinelloides and Cunninghamella elegans: Zygomycetes that
  transform organochlorine and organophosphate pesticides through
  cytochrome P450-mediated oxidation.

## Atrazine Degradation

Atrazine receives particular attention in the Singh source as a
model recalcitrant herbicide. Fungal atrazine degradation involves:

- Initial dealkylation of the ethyl and isopropyl side chains
  by cytochrome P450, producing deisopropylatrazine and
  deethylatrazine.

- Subsequent dechlorination and ring cleavage by laccase or
  peroxidase systems.

- Complete mineralization to CO2 and NH3 has been demonstrated
  for some white-rot fungal strains.
