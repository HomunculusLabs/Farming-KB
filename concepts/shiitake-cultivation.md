# Shiitake Cultivation

Shiitake (Lentinula edodes) is the second most cultivated mushroom globally and
one of the most valuable gourmet species. Native to East Asia, shiitake has been
cultivated for over a thousand years. It is prized for its rich umami flavor,
meaty texture, and well-documented medicinal properties.

## Two Cultivation Methods

### Log Cultivation (Traditional)
Shiitake is naturally a wood-decomposing fungus, and log cultivation mimics its
natural lifecycle. This low-tech method produces premium quality mushrooms but
requires patience and outdoor space.

### Sawdust Block Cultivation (Modern)
Indoor cultivation on supplemented sawdust blocks allows year-round production
with faster cycles and higher yields, though some argue the flavor is slightly
less complex than log-grown shiitake.

## Log Cultivation

### Wood Selection
Hardwood logs of 4-8 inches diameter and 3-4 feet length are ideal. Oak is the
traditional and best-performing species. Other suitable hardwoods include maple,
beech, ironwood, sweetgum, and hickory. Avoid fruit trees (they contain
fungicidal compounds), walnut (juglone is toxic to fungi), and all softwoods.

Logs should be cut from healthy, living trees during dormancy (late fall through
early spring). Trees cut during this period have higher sugar content, promoting
vigorous mycelium growth. Logs should be inoculated within 6 weeks of cutting
before the natural anti-fungal defenses of the wood activate.

### Inoculation
Holes are drilled in a diamond or checkerboard pattern, spaced 6 inches apart
along the log and 2 inches between rows around the circumference. Holes are
drilled 1 inch deep using a 5/16 inch drill bit for standard plug spawn.

Plug spawn (wooden dowels colonized with shiitake mycelium) is hammered into
the holes and sealed with food-grade wax (cheese wax or soy wax) to prevent
desiccation and contamination. Inoculation rate is typically 50-100 plugs per
log.

### Incubation (The "Lay" Phase)
Inoculated logs are placed in a shaded, moist location — under trees, in a
woodland area, or in a purpose-built laying yard. Logs are stacked in cribs or
leaned against a rack. The goal is to maintain moisture content above 35% while
allowing mycelium to colonize the wood interior.

Incubation takes 6-18 months depending on log diameter, species, and climate.
Logs should be watered during dry periods (1-2 inches per week). Avoid direct
sunlight and contact with soil (use bricks or gravel as a base).

### Fruiting
When logs show signs of full colonization (white mycelium visible at cut ends,
bark beginning to crack), they are ready to fruit. Fruit logs by soaking them
in cold water for 12-24 hours. This simulated rain event triggers the mushroom
to form. After soaking, stand logs upright in a fruiting area.

Optimal fruiting temperature is 15-20°C. Shiitake strains are categorized by
temperature preference:
- **Cold weather strains** (10-18°C): Produce in spring/fall, large caps
- **Warm weather strains** (18-25°C): Summer production, faster but smaller
- **Wide-range strains** (10-25°C): Versatile year-round fruiting

Logs produce fruit for 3-6 years with decreasing yields. Each log yields
approximately 1-2 pounds of fresh mushrooms per year at peak production.

## Sawdust Block Cultivation

### Substrate
The standard shiitake block formula is:
- Hardwood sawdust (oak or similar): 75%
- Wheat bran: 20%
- Gypsum: 5%
- Water: 60-65% moisture

Blocks are filled into autoclave bags, sterilized at 121°C for 90-120 minutes,
and inoculated with grain or sawdust spawn at 5% by weight.

### Incubation
Blocks incubate at 20-24°C for 60-90 days. Shiitake mycelium turns the substrate
brown as it matures — this browning phase is essential for good fruiting.
Incompletely browned blocks produce poorly. Some growers wrap blocks loosely
during incubation to slow evaporation.

### Fruiting Initiation
Shiitake requires more specific triggers than oyster mushrooms:
1. **Soak**: Submerge blocks in cold water for 12-24 hours
2. **Cold shock**: Move to 12-15°C environment for 3-5 days
3. **High humidity**: Maintain 90-95% RH
4. **Light**: 500-1000 lux, 12h on/off

Blocks typically produce 4-5 flushes over a 2-3 month cropping period. Yield is
typically 0.5-1.0 kg fresh per block.

### Donko vs. Koshin
Shiitake morphology depends on environmental conditions during fruiting:
- **Donko** (winter/flower pattern): Thick, cracked caps, slow growth in cold
  conditions. Premium grade, higher medicinal compound content.
- **Koshin** (thick-cracked): Intermediate quality, partially cracked caps.
- **Koko**: Thin caps, lighter color, warmer/faster growth. Lower market value.

## Nutritional and Medicinal Value
Shiitake contains lentinan (beta-glucan with immunomodulatory properties),
ergothioneine (potent antioxidant), and eritadenine (cholesterol-lowering
compound). Regular consumption is associated with immune support and
cardiovascular benefits.
