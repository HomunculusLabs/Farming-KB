# Food Fermentation Methods

Lacto-fermentation preserves the nutritional properties of food while creating friendly bacteria. The fermentation process produces lactic acid, which kills botulism and other harmful bacteria. This can make fermented foods safer than canning or even eating raw vegetables.

## Safety Notes

- Use jars with rubber-sealed lids (release gases, prevent explosion)
- Grow food yourself or source from reputable farmers
- Sterilize with boiling water or soap and water
- Use unchlorinated water (chlorine kills beneficial bacteria)
- If mold appears on top, scrape it off -- the food underneath is still safe

## Kimchi

Ingredients per cabbage: Napa cabbage (2-inch cubes), 1/4 cup sea salt, 1/2 bulb garlic, 1 shredded radish, 1 onion, chili powder, 1/2 tsp sugar, 2 tbsp unchlorinated water, ginger.

Process:
1. Salt cabbage cubes, let stand 2+ hours
2. Blend garlic, ginger, onion, water with chili/cayenne
3. Pack tightly into clean jars with rubber-sealed lids
4. Keep in dark cool pantry 2-3 days, periodically smashing down to keep cabbage submerged
5. When bubbling starts, refrigerate -- eat raw for 3 weeks, cooked after that

## Sauerkraut

Ingredients: 1+ cabbage (can mix types), 1/4 cup sea salt, 2 tbsp unchlorinated water. Optional additions: turnips, beets, greens, Brussels sprouts, apple, herbs, spices.

Process:
1. Chop cabbage, pack tightly into jars adding salt as you go
2. Smash to force liquid out; top with salt and hot water
3. Seal with rubber lids, stand in kitchen 4-5 days
4. Smash down daily to keep cabbage submerged
5. Move to cold storage for 4 weeks before eating

## Pickles (Fermented)

Ingredients: 1 lb sliced cucumbers, 1 cup sea salt (for salting), peppercorns, 1.5 tbsp sea salt (for brine), 1 cup unchlorinated water, mustard seeds, fresh dill, 2 cloves garlic.

Process:
1. Layer cucumbers with salt in bowl, cover with water (1 inch above), soak 24 hours
2. Pack tightly into jars with mustard seeds, peppercorns, dill, garlic
3. Close tightly, keep in kitchen until bubbles form (1-2 days)
4. Move to fridge/cellar for 6 weeks before eating

## Yogurt

Not fermented the same way as vegetables, but technically fermented milk where bacteria convert lactose to lactic acid.

Process:
1. Heat 1 quart milk + 3 tbsp plain yogurt (with active cultures) to exactly 100F
2. Turn off heat, keep warm 8-12 hours (thermos, oven, woodstove, crockpot, sun)
3. Done when tilting doesn't cause it to run
4. Always save some plain starter in fridge for next batch

## Sourdough

Wild yeast captured from air to ferment bread.

**Creating starter**:
1. Day 1: Mix 1/2 cup flour + 1-2 tbsp unchlorinated water into dough, cover with damp cloth
2. Day 3: Pull crust off, add 1 cup flour + water, cover 24 hours
3. Day 4: Refresh again with flour and water
4. Day 5: Dough rises and hole doesn't spring back -- starter is ready
5. Store in fridge; never use all of it; feed to maintain

**Baking**: Mix starter + 2 cups flour + 2 cups water, let sit covered all day. Evening: add 2 cups flour + 1 tbsp salt + 1 cup warm water + oil, knead, rise overnight. Morning: divide, form loaves, rise 3-6 hours, bake at 450F for ~30 minutes until hollow-sounding. Can also bake in crockpot (low, 4 hours with water in bottom).
