## Gathering Mushrooms

### When to Pick

- For market: plump and fresh, just before the frill connecting cap to
  stem breaks apart
- For soups: button state (marble-sized), before gills burst -- they
  retain white appearance and don't discolor soup
- For home cooking (baking, stewing, broiling): full size, frills burst
  but before cap opens flat or gills turn brown
- Never let mushrooms blow open before gathering
- Never let them stay ungathered until tomorrow to grow larger -- this
  gave an unfavored, unequal lot

### Pulling vs. Cutting

Falconer strongly advocated pulling mushrooms out by the root rather
than cutting:
1. **Benefited the bed**: Cut stumps decayed, producing fluffy/spongy
   substance that poisoned surrounding mycelium and destroyed nearby
   small mushrooms. Pulled roots left clean holes.
2. **Kept fresh longer**: Pulled mushrooms always kept fresher than cut.
3. **Increased weight and price**: Mushrooms were sold by weight. Retaining
   full stems increased both weight and price. "If part of the stems
   had been cut off the weight would have been reduced, and, in like
   proportion, the price."
In cases where pulling would disturb neighboring small mushrooms, cutting
was acceptable. A practiced hand could extract full-grown mushrooms from
thick bunches with "a twitch and a pull" without disturbing the multitude
of small buttons around them.

## Marketing Methods

### Direct Sales

Most market growers around New York City sold direct, delivering to
hotels, restaurants, and fancy fruiterers.

### Commission Merchants

Growers at considerable distance from the city sold through commission
merchants in New York, who dealt out in quantities to suit customers.
