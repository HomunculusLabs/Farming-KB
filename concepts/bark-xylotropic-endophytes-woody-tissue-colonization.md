# Bark and Xylotropic Endophytes

Bark and xylotropic endophytes are fungi that inconspicuously
colonize living bark, twigs, and xylem tissue of woody plants.
These endophytes represent a distinct guild of microfungi that
bridge the ecological categories of latent pathogen, saprobe,
and mutualist. Their study has revealed remarkably complex life-
history strategies involving prolonged quiescence within healthy
host tissue followed by rapid colonization at the onset of host
stress or senescence.

## Bark Endophytes

Many species of fungi inconspicuously colonize living bark on
twigs and small branches of both coniferous and broad-leaved
trees. The resinous young bark of conifers such as [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America, Arthonia impolita, another nonlichenized
member of a normally [[verticillium-lecanii]], and Paecilomyces farinosus have
been isolated from living bark and are not uncommon as endophytes
of foliage. The endophytic occurrence of insect parasites suggests
that bark may provide an interim substratum for saprobic growth.

## Xylotropic Endophytes

Xylotropic endophytes are a distinct guild of xylem-colonizing
species encompassing a wide range of taxa. The group is composed
mainly of xylariaceous-endophytes|xylariaceous species, such
as those of the genus Hypoxylon and related genera; Diaporthales
(e.g., Phragmoporthe, Amphiporthe, Phomopsis); Hypocreales
(Nectria species); a few Basidiomycetes (e.g., Coniophora); and
a few species more typical of the periderm mycobiota.

Species diversity and abundance are generally low in this group
compared to bark, shoot, and foliar endophytes. Some species are
host-specific: Hypoxylon species, for example, have specialized
mechanisms for recognizing and attaching to a host. In H.
fragiforme, germination is triggered only by host-specific
monolignol glucosides.
