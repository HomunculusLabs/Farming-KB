# Psilocybe Mexicana Sclerotia Cultivation

*Psilocybe mexicana* is a [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]]. The species is native to Mexico and was one of the
first psilocybin-containing mushrooms identified by Western science.

## What Are Sclerotia

Sclerotia are compacted, hardened masses of mycelial tissue that form as a
survival strategy when environmental conditions become unfavorable for mushroom
production. They can survive extended periods of drought, temperature extremes,
and nutrient deprivation. When conditions improve, sclerotia can germinate to
produce new mycelial growth and ultimately fruiting bodies.

In cultivation, sclerotia form within the substrate without any need for
casing or fruiting conditions. This makes them significantly easier to produce
than mushrooms, as they require only colonized substrate in a jar. No humidity
control, light cycles, or air exchange management is needed. The tradeoff is
that [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]].

## Grass Seed Substrate

Grass seed is the preferred substrate for *P. mexicana* sclerotia production.
The standard preparation uses 110 grams of grass seed and 180 ml of water per
720 ml jar. The small kernel size of grass seed creates numerous interstitial
spaces where sclerotia can form and grow to harvestable size.

The seed must be free of fungicide treatment, which can be confirmed by
checking packaging labels when purchasing from animal feed stores. Different
batches of seed vary in water absorption, so the hydration ratio may need
adjustment. Overnight soaking of the seed before loading jars produces more
homogenous hydration and can improve sclerotia yields.

## Colonization Phase

After inoculation with a spore syringe, mycelium syringe, or agar squares,
jars are incubated at room temperature (approximately 20°C) in a clean,
draft-free location. The lids are kept loose to allow gas exchange. When
mycelium begins growing in only a few spots, the jars are shaken to
redistribute the colonized kernels throughout the substrate.

Full colonization of grass seed typically takes 5-20 days depending on
temperature, the specific strain, and the inoculation method used. Agar
wedge inoculation generally produces faster colonization than spore syringes
because the mycelium is already established and does not need to germinate
from spores.

## Sclerotia Formation

Once the substrate is fully colonized, sclerotia begin to form without any
change in environmental conditions. No casing layer, light exposure, or
fresh air exchange is required. The mycelium simply continues growing,
consolidating into hardened masses within the jar.

The jars should be placed in a clean location, preferably in darkness, for
the sclerotia production phase. Light is not necessary and may actually slow
sclerotia development, as the organism interprets light as a signal to
produce mushrooms rather than storage structures.

## Growth Timeline

Sclerotia of *P. mexicana* continue to enlarge over a period of approximately
four months after inoculation. The growth rate is not linear -- production
accelerates after full substrate colonization and then gradually slows as the
available nutrients are consumed. Maximum yields are typically achieved by
allowing the full four-month maturation period.

Harvesting too early results in smaller sclerotia and lower total yield.
However, waiting significantly beyond four months provides diminishing returns
and increases the risk of contamination entering through the loose jar lid.
The four-month timeline represents the practical balance between yield
optimization and contamination risk.

## Harvesting and Storage

Sclerotia are harvested by breaking open the colonized jar and separating the
hardened masses from the [[grass-seed-substrate]]. The sclerotia are typically
firm, with a texture similar to nuts, and range in color from tan to brown.
They should be rinsed clean of substrate particles and any uncolonized seed.

Fresh sclerotia can be stored in the refrigerator for several weeks. For
longer-term preservation, they can be dried using a food dehydrator at low
temperature (35-40°C) to preserve psychoactive compounds. Dried sclerotia
should be stored in an airtight container with a desiccant packet in a cool,
dark location.

## Sclerotia in Cased Trays

The source document notes that sclerotia of *P. mexicana* also form in the
casing layer of cased trays. When the species is cultivated for mushrooms
rather than sclerotia, the casing soil itself can become infiltrated with
sclerotia. These can be recovered during post-harvest substrate cleanup.

This dual production capacity means that cultivators targeting mushroom
production from *P. mexicana* often receive a bonus harvest of sclerotia
from the casing layer. However, the sclerotia yield from casing is generally
lower than from dedicated jar cultivation.
