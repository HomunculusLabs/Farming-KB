# Catalytic Hydrogenolysis Debenzylation to Crystalline Psilocybin

## Overview

The final step in the Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) synthesis of
psilocybin is the catalytic hydrogenolysis of the zwitterionic N,O-
dibenzyl phosphate intermediate (8) to produce psilocybin (2) as a
crystalline product. This step removes both benzyl protecting groups
through palladium-catalyzed hydrogenation and yields psilocybin without
any chromatographic purification, completing the first gram-scale,
chromatography-free synthesis of psilocybin.

## The Debenzylation Reaction

### Reaction Conditions

- **Starting material**: Zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate (8),
  the intermediate from the phosphorylation step
- **Catalyst**: Palladium on carbon (Pd/C)
- **Hydrogen source**: H₂ gas at atmospheric pressure
- **Solvent**: Methanol (MeOH)
- **Temperature**: Room temperature
- **Atmosphere**: Hydrogen (balloon or atmospheric pressure)

### Reaction Scheme

The catalytic hydrogenolysis removes both benzyl groups from the
zwitterionic intermediate (8):

1. **O-benzyl removal**: Hydrogenolysis of the O-benzyl group
  regenerates the free phosphate, producing the O-phosphate of
  psilocybin
2. **N-benzyl removal**: Simultaneous hydrogenolysis of the N-benzyl
  group regenerates the tertiary amine, restoring the N,N-dimethyl
  functionality of psilocybin
3. **Product formation**: The free phosphate and free amine combine
  to give psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine)

The reaction is monitored by TLC, watching for the disappearance of
starting material (8) and its mono-debenzyl derivative, and the
appearance of psilocybin (2).

### Workup Procedure

After completion, the reaction solution is filtered through a Celite
pad by suction to remove the palladium catalyst. The filtrate volume
is reduced by evaporation, causing psilocybin to crystallize from
solution. The crystalline product is collected by filtration and
washed with ethanol to afford pure psilocybin.

## Product Characterization: Psilocybin (2)

### Physical Properties
- **Yield**: 5.6 g, 87.5% from the zwitterionic intermediate (8)
- **Appearance**: White needle crystalline powder
- **Melting point**: 190-198°C (decomposition)
  - Literature values: 185-195°C and 210-212°C
  - The range in reported melting points reflects the compound's
    tendency to decompose rather than melt cleanly

### UV Spectroscopy (MeOH)
- λmax (log ε): 221.0 (4.44), 267.5 (3.66), 278.5 (3.57),
  290.0 (3.42) nm
- The UV spectrum shows the characteristic indole chromophore with
  absorption bands in the near-UV region

### IR Spectroscopy (KBr)
- νmax: 3266, 3034, 2731, 2369, 1620, 1580, 1505, 1439, 1352,
  1298, 1244, 1154, 1103, 1061, 926, 858, 804 cm⁻¹
- Key features include the broad O-H stretch at 3266 cm⁻¹ (phosphate
  OH) and the aromatic C=C stretches at 1620 and 1580 cm⁻¹

### ¹H NMR (D₂O, 400 MHz)
- δ 7.22 (1H, d, J = 7.6 Hz, H-7): Aromatic proton at the
  7-position
- δ 7.18 (1H, s, H-2): The proton at the 2-position of the indole
  ring, appearing as a singlet
- δ 7.13 (1H, t, J = 7.6 Hz, H-6): Aromatic proton
- δ 6.98 (1H, d, J = 7.6 Hz, H-5): Aromatic proton
- δ 3.44 (2H, t, J = 7.2 Hz, H₂-2'): The methylene adjacent to
  the amine nitrogen
- δ 3.28 (2H, t, J = 7.2 Hz, H₂-1'): The methylene adjacent to
  the indole ring
- δ 2.86 (6H, s, NMe₂): The two N-methyl groups appearing as a
  single singlet due to rapid rotation

### ¹³C NMR (D₂O + MeOH, 100 MHz)
- δ 146.4 (C, split, C-4): The carbon bearing the phosphate group,
  split due to phosphorus coupling
- δ 139.4 (C, C-7a): Bridgehead carbon
- δ 124.8, 123.3: Aromatic carbons
- δ 59.7 (CH₂, C-2'): Methylene adjacent to the amine
- δ 43.4 (CH₃ × 2, NMe₂): N-methyl carbons
- δ 22.4 (CH₂, C-1'): Methylene adjacent to the indole ring

### ³¹P NMR (CD₃OD, 162 MHz)
- δ -4.48 (P, OPO₃H₂): The phosphorus signal, appearing in the
  characteristic region for phosphate monoesters

### Mass Spectrometry
- ESI-MS m/z 307.1 [M + Na]⁺, 285.1 [M + H]⁺
- The [M + H]⁺ peak at 285.1 is the base peak
- Additional fragments: m/z 240.0 [M - NMe₂]⁺, 205.1 [M - H₂O₃P + H]⁺,
  160.1 [M - H₂O₃P - NMe₂]⁺
- HRMS: m/z 285.0991 [M + H]⁺ (calcd for C₁₂H₁₈N₂O₄P, 285.1004)

## Comparison with Previous Syntheses

The Shirota synthesis achieved significantly higher yields than
previously reported methods for the final conversion to psilocybin:

- **Troxler et al. (1959)**: 20% yield from psilocin to psilocybin
- **Hofmann et al. (1959)**: 20% yield
- **Nichols and Frescas**: 47% yield, using anion-exchange resin
  purification
- **Shirota et al. (2003)**: 72% overall yield from psilocin to
  psilocybin, with the final hydrogenolysis step giving 87.5%

## Advantages of the Chromatography-Free Approach

The avoidance of chromatographic purification throughout the entire
synthesis is the key innovation of the Shirota route:

1. **Scalability**: Column chromatography is impractical at large
  scales; crystallization and filtration are easily scaled
2. **Cost reduction**: Eliminating silica gel and large volumes of
   organic solvents reduces cost significantly
3. **Purity**: Crystalline products are typically of higher purity
   than chromatographically purified materials
4. **Reproducibility**: Crystallization conditions are more
   reproducible across laboratories than chromatographic methods
