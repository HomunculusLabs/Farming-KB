# Yeast Diversity in Beetle Gut Microbiomes

## Overview

The digestive tracts of beetles harbour extraordinary yeast diversity that has been largely overlooked by traditional mycological surveys. Studies focusing on beetles associated with basidiomata ([[advanced-mushroom-fruiting-techniques]] bodies) have revealed that the beetle gut is a major reservoir of novel yeast species, with 650 yeasts isolated from beetle digestive tracts representing more than 200 undescribed taxa. This discovery has profound implications for estimates of global [[yeast-biodiversity-and-inventory-protocols]] our understanding of insect–fungus symbioses.

## Key Findings

### Scale of Discovery

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| Yeasts isolated | 650 from digestive tracts of beetles |
| Beetle colonization rate | >90% of all dissected beetles harboured yeasts |
| Undescribed taxa | 290 yeast genotypes representing >200 undescribed taxa |
| [[basidiomycetous-yeasts-diversity-phylogenetic-distribution]] | Distributed in clusters throughout the yeast phylogenetic tree (SSU and LSU rDNA analysis) |

### Novel Clades

Several previously unknown, entirely **insect-associated yeast clades** have been discovered:

- **Candida tanzawaensis clade**: Contained **30% of all gut yeasts collected** — the single largest group.
- Multiple additional clades with no known free-living representatives.

### Dominance Patterns

- **One dominant yeast** is usually present in a beetle gut (based on cloning methods).
- This suggests competitive exclusion or priority effects within the gut environment.

## Yeast–Beetle Specificity

### Host Specificity

- **Yeast–beetle specificity** has been observed between certain yeasts and beetles across:
  - **Broad geographical ranges**
  - **Multiple [[psilocybe-cubensis-developmental-stages]]** of the beetle
- This indicates co-evolutionary relationships rather than opportunistic colonization.

### Ecological Context

- Beetles were isolated primarily from the **basidioma habitat** (mushroom fruiting bodies).
- The basidioma provides both food (spores, tissue) and a substrate for yeast growth.
- Beetles may vector yeasts between basidiomata, facilitating dispersal.

## Phylogenetic Distribution

Gut yeasts from beetles are **not phylogenetically clustered** — they are distributed throughout the yeast phylogenetic tree based on SSU and LSU rDNA analysis. This means:

- The association between yeasts and beetles has evolved **multiple times independently**.
- The beetle gut environment can support diverse yeast lineages.
- Convergent evolution of gut adaptation has occurred across distant yeast lineages.

## Implications for Biodiversity Estimates

### The "Undescribed Majority"

- >200 undescribed yeast taxa from a single study of beetle guts suggests that current estimates of global yeast diversity are **severe underestimates**.
- The beetle gut represents a **largely unexplored [[ecological-niche]]** for yeast diversity.
- Similar studies of other insect groups would likely reveal additional novel taxa.

### Comparison with Free-Living Surveys

- Traditional yeast surveys (soil, plant surfaces, water) capture only a fraction of total yeast diversity.
- Insect-associated yeasts may represent a substantial proportion of total yeast species richness.
- The basidioma–beetle–yeast triad represents a complex ecological network.

## Methodological Approaches

- **Culturing**: Direct isolation from dissected beetle digestive tracts.
- **Cloning**: PCR amplification and cloning of gut contents to assess dominant species.
- **Phylogenetic analysis**: SSU and LSU rDNA sequencing for species identification and phylogenetic placement.
- **Specificity testing**: Cross-inoculation experiments and geographic surveys to test host specificity.

## Ecological Roles

### For the Yeast

- The beetle gut provides a **stable, nutrient-rich environment**.
- Protection from environmental extremes.
- Dispersal to new substrates (basidiomata).
- Potential for vertical transmission (parent to offspring).

### For the Beetle

- Yeasts may aid in **digestion** of fungal tissue.
- May provide **nutritional supplements** (vitamins, amino acids).
- Could contribute to **chemical defence** against predators or pathogens.
