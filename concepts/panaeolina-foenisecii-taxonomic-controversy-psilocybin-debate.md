## Panaeolina foenisecii: Taxonomic Controversy and Psilocybin Content Debate

## Overview

Panaeolina foenisecii (Pers.: Fr.) Maire, commonly known as the haymaker's
mushroom or the brown mottlegill, is one of the most contentious species in the
neurotropic fungi literature. This small, brown dung-inhabiting mushroom has been
the subject of contradictory chemical analyses spanning several decades, with
some researchers reporting the presence of psilocybin and others finding no
trace of indole alkaloids. The species remains a textbook case of the
difficulties inherent in mycochemical research and the perils of
misidentification in the study of psychoactive fungi.

## Broader Pattern in Panaeoloideous Fungi

The controversy surrounding P. foenisecii is representative of a broader
pattern in the Panaeoloideous fungi. The taxonomic boundaries between
Panaeolus, Copelandia, and Panaeolina have been debated extensively. Some
species formerly placed in Panaeolus have been transferred to Copelandia on
the basis of their bluing reaction and confirmed psilocybin content. The
absence of a bluing reaction in P. foenisecii further argues against its
classification as a genuinely neurotropic species.
