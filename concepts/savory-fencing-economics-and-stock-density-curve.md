# Fencing Economics and Stock Density Curve

Fencing is one of the most significant capital investments in grazing
operations, yet decisions about how much to install are often made
without adequate analysis. Savory's framework provides principles for
evaluating fencing investments using stock density vs stocking rate,
the diminishing marginal reaction curve, and the relationship between
paddock numbers and grazing effectiveness.

## Paddocks and Stock Density

As paddock numbers increase and paddock size decreases, stock density
(animals per acre) increases. Effects include more uniform grazing
pressure, longer recovery periods without reducing total area, higher
[[savory-animal-impact-and-herd-effect]] and herd effect, and better diet quality from frequent
moves to fresh ground. With new fence, time can change everywhere but
stock density only changes where paddock size changes.

## Stock Density vs. Stocking Rate

The distinction is central to stock density vs stocking rate:

- **Stocking rate**: Total animals on the entire area over a year
  (animals/acre/year).
- **Stock density**: Animals on the area being grazed at any moment
  (animals/acre at a moment).

The same stocking rate produces vastly different stock densities
depending on paddock numbers. A ranch running 100 cattle on 2,000 acres
year-round has a stocking rate and density of one per 20 acres. Divided
into 20 paddocks, stock density becomes one per acre during each
grazing period.

## How Movement Affects Nutrition

The number of paddocks directly influences diet quality:

- **Few paddocks**: Animals take four selections -- increasingly less
  desirable forage as they remain on the same ground.
- **More paddocks**: Two selections per paddock -- more palatable
  material available at each move.
- **Many paddocks**: Single selection per paddock -- fresh, high-
  quality forage at every move.
