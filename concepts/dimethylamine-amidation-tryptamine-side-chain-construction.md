# Dimethylamine Amidation in Tryptamine Side Chain Construction

The conversion of an [[glyoxalylamide-intermediate-in-tryptamine-synthesis]]
using dimethylamine is a critical step in the construction of the
N,N-dimethyltryptamine side chain during the synthesis of [[tryptamine]] molecule.

## Reaction Overview

The [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-formation-oxalyl-chloride-indole]] proceeds in a single step from the oxalyl
chloride adduct. The yellow crystals of compound 5, which were isolated by
n-hexane anti-solvent crystallization from the previous step, are dissolved in
anhydrous tetrahydrofuran and treated with dimethylamine and pyridine.

The reaction installs the N,N-dimethylamino group that will ultimately become
part of the [[indole]] ring system and the ethylamine side chain are
joined through the glyoxalyl linker.

Prior to this step, the molecule consists of a protected [[tryptamine]] is assembled, and only
the reduction of the glyoxalyl carbonyl groups remains.

## Detailed Experimental Procedure

The procedure as reported in the Shirota synthesis proceeds as follows:

1. The [[shirota-oxalyl-chloride-glyoxalylamide-crystallization-psilocin-route]] adduct (yellow crystals of compound 5) is dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran (100 mL per approximately 100 mmol scale)
2. The solution is stirred in an ice bath at zero degrees Celsius
3. A 2 M dimethylamine solution in tetrahydrofuran (60 mL, 120 mmol, 1.2 equivalents) is added over fifteen minutes
4. Pyridine (10 mL, 123 mmol) is added simultaneously over the same period
5. Additional anhydrous [[diethyl-ether]] is added because the mixture begins to solidify
6. The reaction is stirred for fifteen minutes at room temperature
7. The product is collected by filtration and washed with n-hexane, ethyl acetate, and water

The entire sequence takes approximately one hour, making this one of the
fastest steps in the synthesis. The sequential washing with three different
solvents removes the full range of impurities: non-polar byproducts with
n-hexane, moderately polar impurities with ethyl acetate, and water-soluble
materials including pyridinium hydrochloride salt and excess dimethylamine.

## Role of Each Reagent

**Dimethylamine** serves as the nucleophile that attacks the carbonyl carbon of the oxalyl chloride adduct, displacing chloride and forming the amide bond. The use of a 2 M solution in THF ensures controlled addition and prevents local excess that could lead to side reactions such as double amidation or decomposition.

The 1.2 equivalent stoichiometry provides a slight excess to drive the
reaction to completion while minimizing waste. The dimethylamine source is
commercially available as a THF solution, which simplifies handling compared
to gaseous dimethylamine.

**Pyridine** functions as a base to scavenge the hydrochloric acid generated during the substitution. Without pyridine, the accumulating HCl would protonate dimethylamine, reducing its nucleophilicity and potentially halting the reaction.

Pyridine is sufficiently non-nucleophilic to avoid competing with
dimethylamine for the electrophilic carbonyl carbon, yet basic enough to
neutralize the acid as it forms. Pyridine also improves the solubility of
ionic intermediates in the THF reaction medium.

**Anhydrous diethyl ether** is added as a co-solvent when the reaction mixture begins to solidify. The solidification occurs because the glyoxalylamide product has lower solubility in THF than the [[psilocin]] molecule minus the two oxygen atoms that will be removed by
lithium aluminum hydride reduction.

Specifically, the glyoxalylamide has:

- The complete [[indole]] ring with the 4-acetyl protecting group
- The two-carbon unit from the oxalyl chloride (future ethylamine side chain)
- The N,N-dimethylamino group from the dimethylamine reagent

## Spectroscopic Confirmation

The proton NMR spectrum of compound 6 in CDCl3 at 400 MHz shows key diagnostic
signals:

- 10.40 ppm (1H, br s): N-H proton of the indole ring, shifted downfield due to the electron-withdrawing glyoxalylamide group
- 7.52 ppm (1H, d, J = 3.2 Hz): H-2, showing characteristic small meta-coupling
- 7.15 ppm (1H, t, J = 8.0 Hz): H-6 aromatic proton
- 7.06 ppm (1H, d, J = 8.0 Hz): H-7 aromatic proton
- 6.91 ppm (1H, d, J = 8.0 Hz): H-5 aromatic proton
- 3.02 ppm (3H, s) and 2.92 ppm (3H, s): two distinct N-methyl singlets
- 2.50 ppm (3H, s): acetyl methyl protecting group

The appearance of two separate N-methyl signals at 3.02 and 2.92 ppm is
noteworthy. The 0.10 ppm chemical shift difference indicates magnetically non-
equivalent environments, consistent with restricted rotation around the amide
bond creating diastereotopic methyl groups.

The carbon-13 NMR shows characteristic signals at 185.4 ppm (ketone carbonyl),
170.9 ppm (ester carbonyl of acetyl group), and 168.4 ppm (amide carbonyl).
The two N-methyl carbons appear at 37.4 and 34.2 ppm, reflecting the same non-
equivalence.

Mass spectrometry shows ESIMS m/z 275.1 [M + H]+ and a fragment at m/z 233.1
[M minus Ac + H]+ confirming the acetyl group is still present and labile
under ionization conditions.

## Solidification Phenomenon

The tendency of the reaction mixture to solidify upon addition of the
dimethylamine reagent is noteworthy. This solidification reflects the dramatic
change in physical properties that occurs when the highly electrophilic oxalyl
chloride adduct is converted to the more polar glyoxalylamide.

The addition of anhydrous diethyl ether to maintain a liquid phase is an
important practical detail for scaling the reaction, as complete
solidification could make stirring impossible and lead to incomplete reaction.
