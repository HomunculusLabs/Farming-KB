Detection and monitoring of degradative fungi in contaminated environments is critical for assessing mycoremediation-techniques|bioremediation progress, verifying treatment efficacy, and making regulatory decisions. Two primary categories of assays are used: immunological and molecular methods.

## Immunological Assays

Immunological methods detect specific fungal antigens or proteins using antibody-based technologies. These approaches are valuable for identifying and quantifying target fungi in complex environmental samples.

### Principles
Immunological assays exploit the specificity of antibody-antigen interactions:
- **Polyclonal antibodies**: Raised against whole-cell preparations or crude extracts; recognize multiple epitopes
- **Monoclonal antibodies**: Highly specific to single epitopes; more reproducible
- **Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)**: Quantitative detection using enzyme-conjugated antibodies
- **Immunofluorescence**: Microscopic detection using fluorescently labeled antibodies

### Applications in Mycoremediation
- Monitoring survival and spread of inoculated [[white-rot-fungi-effluent-decolorization]] in field applications

### Advantages
- Relatively rapid turnaround time
- Can be designed for field-portable formats
- Quantitative capabilities with proper calibration
- Cost-effective for routine monitoring

### Limitations
- Cross-reactivity with non-target organisms possible
- Antibody production requires purified antigens
- May not distinguish between viable and non-viable cells
- Environmental matrix interference (soil humic acids, etc.)

## Molecular Assays

Molecular methods detect fungal DNA or RNA, providing highly specific identification and quantification capabilities.

### PCR-Based Methods
- **Species-specific PCR**: Uses primers targeting unique DNA sequences for identification
- **Quantitative PCR (qPCR)**: Real-time amplification enabling quantification of fungal biomass
- **Nested PCR**: Two-stage amplification for increased sensitivity in low-biomass samples
- **Multiplex PCR**: Simultaneous detection of multiple fungal species
- **RT-PCR**: Detects RNA (expressed genes), indicating metabolic activity

### DNA-Based Community Analysis
- **Denaturing gradient gel electrophoresis (DGGE)**: Community fingerprinting
- **Terminal restriction fragment length polymorphism (TRFLP)**: Community structure analysis
- **Clone libraries**: Identification of community members through sequencing
- **Metagenomics**: Comprehensive community characterization

### Genetic Markers for Fungal Detection
- **Internal transcribed spacer (ITS)** regions: Primary barcode for fungal identification
- **18S rRNA genes**: Broad phylogenetic marker
- **Functional genes**: Laccase, MnP, LiP genes indicate degradative potential
- **Species-specific markers**: Designed for individual target organisms

### Applications in Mycoremediation
- Verifying establishment of inoculated fungi at treatment sites
- Monitoring changes in fungal community structure during remediation
- Quantifying expression of [[fungal-biomonitoring]] approaches using indicator species can complement chemical analyses for [[brown-rot-fungi-biodegradation]] or peroxidase electrodes for phenol detection
- Enzyme electrodes for monitoring pollutant degradation

### Whole-Cell Biosensors
- Living fungal cells coupled to electrochemical or optical transducers
- Detect toxicity or specific pollutant concentrations

### Affinity-Based Biosensors (Immunoassays)
- Antibody-coated surfaces for specific fungal antigen detection
- Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) for label-free detection
