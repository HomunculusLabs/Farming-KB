## Protozoa

Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotic organisms (5 to 500 micrometers) that
are almost always heterotrophic. Over 60,000 kinds are known, and a majority
live in soil. All require moisture to lead an active life. A teaspoon of good
soil contains several thousand protozoa. If a single bacterium were the size
of a pea, a paramecium would be as large as a watermelon -- this size
difference allows bacteria to hide from [[protozoa-in-soil]] pores too small for
them to enter.

Three basic types exist:

- **Amoebae (pseudopods):** The largest protozoa. They move by pouring
  cytoplasm into false appendages called pseudopodia, surrounding and
  engulfing bacteria in gas bubbles with digestive enzymes. Some have shell-
  like exoskeletons with predefined holes; others lack shells entirely.
- **Ciliates:** Covered with rows of beating hairs that propel them and
  create currents bringing bacteria to their mouth region. The familiar
  paramecium is a ciliate. Under ideal lighting, paramecia are visible to
  the human eye in water.
- **Flagellates:** The smallest type, using one or two whip-like flagella
  to move. Most are heterotrophic; a few (like Euglena) are autotrophic
  via photosynthesis. They can access small soil spaces where larger
  protozoa cannot reach.

## Protozoan Population Dynamics

Protozoa arrive in areas with good bacterial supplies in a consistent
progression. Flagellates come first, able to access small soil pores. Ciliates
arrive next, followed by amoebae. Combined [[query-how-to-fix-nitrogen-toxicity-in-cannabis]] from
the atmosphere -- a rare triple symbiotic relationship. Some ciliates "farm"
bacteria colonies in sand. Methane-generating bacteria inside ciliates
contribute to the methane gas from anaerobic respiration.

## Nematodes

Nematodes are nonsegmented, blind roundworms (Greek: nema = "thread") and the
second most dominant animal form after arthropods. Over 20,000 species are
identified with perhaps 1 million total. A teaspoon of good soil contains
40-50 nematodes: roughly 20 bacteria-eaters, 20 fungal feeders, and a few
predatory and root-eating types. Their numbers directly reflect food
availability. Despite smaller individual size than many arthropods, nematode
and protozoan biomass exceeds that of arthropods.

Classification by feeding habit:

- **Herbivores (root-eaters):** Needle-like stylets puncture plant cell
  walls, creating lesions, cysts, and root knots. Both ectoparasitic (root
  surface) and endoparasitic (inside root) forms exist.
- **Bacterivores:** Hollow-tube mouthparts allow them to consume multitudes
  of bacteria per hour.
- **Fungivores:** Stylets for puncturing chitin [[fungal-hyphae]].
- **Predators:** Feed on protozoa, algae, grubs, weevils, slugs, and other
  nematodes -- preventing overgrazing of bacteria and fungi and keeping
  destructive herbivore nematodes in check.
