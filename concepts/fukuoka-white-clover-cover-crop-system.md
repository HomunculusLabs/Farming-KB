# White Clover Cover Crop System

updated: 2026-04-18

White clover (ladino clover) serves as the backbone of Fukuoka's [[fukuoka-ground-cover-strategy]] in both the rice paddy and the orchard. It is the key element in what he called the "clover revolution" — a complete inversion of modern rice and barley farming that eliminates the need for plowing, chemical fertilizers, and [[fukuoka-no-weeding-no-pesticides]].

## Role in the Rice-Barley Rotation

In the [[fukuoka-rice-and-barley-natural-farming]] system, clover plays multiple simultaneous roles:

- **Nitrogen fixation**: As a legume, clover fixes atmospheric nitrogen, reducing or eliminating the need for nitrogenous [[fukuoka-five-principles-natural-farming]]
- **Weed suppression**: A thick cover of clover prevents winter weed germination when broadcast over standing rice in October
- **Soil protection**: Prevents erosion and maintains soil moisture
- **Organic matter**: When trampled or cut, clover adds organic matter to the soil
- **Living mulch**: Provides a dense mat that rice seedlings can push through after flooding weakens the clover

## The Annual Cycle

The clover cycle in the rice-barley system works as follows:

1. **Early-mid October**: Clover seed is broadcast over standing heads of rice
2. **Fall-winter**: Clover establishes itself among the rice stubble and young barley shoots
3. **Spring**: A thick layer of clover grows at the foot of maturing barley; beneath the clover, rice seedlings begin emerging from [[fukuoka-seed-ball-one-seed-technique]]
4. **Late May**: Clover is cut together with the barley during harvest — this does not interfere with harvesting work
5. **Early June**: When clover appears about to choke out young rice seedlings, the field is flooded for 4-7 days to weaken the clover
6. **Summer**: Clover recovers after drainage; its nitrogen benefits the growing rice

## Seeding Rates and Timing

Per quarter-acre:
- **Clover**: 1 lb, sown September-October (and optionally March-April)
- **Barley**: 6.5-22 lbs, sown end of October to mid-November
- **Rice**: 6.5-22 lbs, enclosed in clay pellets, sown mid-November to December

For high yields, sparse and even seeding is ideal, starting with 22 pounds each of rice and barley initially, then reducing over time.

## Clover in the Orchard

For the [[fukuoka-orchard-soil-building-without-fertilizers]], clover has distinct advantages:

1. Eliminates weeds — annual weeds displaced in one year, biennials in two; after 2-3 years almost all garden weeds vanish
2. Improves soil to a depth of 16-18 inches
3. Seed does not need resowing for 6-8 years
4. Does not compete strongly with fruit trees for moisture
5. Grows back easily after cutting, remaining hardy even when trampled
6. Does not hinder farming operations

## Seeding Clover in Orchards

The seed should be drilled the first autumn; delayed seeding invites insect damage. Do not cover seeds with soil as this hampers germination — merely firm the soil after drilling. When broadcast in late autumn among dying weeds and grasses on levees and roadsides, clover growth gradually thickens. If sown in spring, cut it back a year later to stimulate growth.

Clover vine may additionally be planted in spring like sweet potato vine to ensure full cover by summertime.

## Long-Term Management

Clover does not choke out other vegetation but gradually becomes dominant by growing so thickly as to prevent germination and establishment of other weeds. When trampled and cut, most weeds weaken but clover grows more vigorously.

However, failure to properly control the clover leads to failure. After 6-7 years growth gradually slows. By about ten years, weeds begin reemerging — primarily vines (bindweed, kudzu) and perennials (sorrel, dandelion). Areas with perennial weeds should be cut more frequently, with wood ashes or coal ashes scattered.

## Clover Disadvantages

- Susceptible to summer-killing and sclerotium disease during hot, dry weather
- Growth is retarded in shade and under trees
- Requires meticulous care similar to a lawn to maintain long-term

## Alfalfa and Bur Clover Companions

In warm regions where clover loses summer vigor, and in cold dry areas, mixed seeding with alfalfa is desirable. Alfalfa sends roots down six feet or more, improving deeper soil strata. Bur clover suppresses spring weeds, withers in summer but regrows in fall to suppress winter weeds.

## The "Clover Revolution"

Fukuoka referred to his complete system of [[fukuoka-rice-and-barley-natural-farming]] under a clover cover as the "clover revolution" — a direct challenge to modern scientific farming with its reliance on chemicals and large machinery. The system enabled farmers for the first time to grow rice and barley without any pesticides or chemical fertilizers.

The six simultaneous roles that clover plays in the rice-barley rotation — nitrogen fixation, weed suppression, soil protection, organic matter contribution, living mulch, and moisture retention — illustrate Fukuoka's principle of designing elements that serve multiple functions. A single species performing six roles replaces an array of separate management interventions: fertilizer application, herbicide spraying, irrigation, compost spreading, and mulching.

The long-term management challenges of clover — decline after 6-7 years, re-emergence of perennial weeds, and summer dieback in hot dry conditions — demonstrate that even the most successful natural farming techniques require ongoing observation and adaptation. Fukuoka addressed these challenges not with chemical inputs but with companion planting (alfalfa, bur clover) and mechanical management (frequent cutting, ash application).
