Fungal spores are the primary units of dispersal, survival, and reproduction
for most fungal species. Conidia, the asexual spores produced by mitosporic

ascospores through the ascus operculum, achieving launch velocities far
exceeding passive release. Basidiomycetes discharge basidiospores through the
ballistospore mechanism, a remarkable water-drop process propelling spores
several millimeters into the air.

Insect and animal dispersal is important for fungi associated with dung, wood,
or specific plant hosts. Some fungi produce spores in mucilage that adheres to
insect bodies, while others produce arthroconidia (fragmented hyphal segments)
readily transported on animal fur. Certain fungi have evolved spore structures
mimicking pollen grains to attract insect dispersers.
