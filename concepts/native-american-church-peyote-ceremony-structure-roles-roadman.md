## Ceremonial Setting and Structure

NAC ceremonies take place inside a tipi, where participants sit on the
ground in a circle around a blazing central fire. The ceremony lasts
all night, typically from evening until dawn, and is conducted by a
"roadman" with the assistance of several supporting roles. The circular
arrangement of participants around the central fire creates a powerful
sense of community and shared sacred space that is central to the
ceremonial experience.

The all-night duration is significant. It allows participants to move
through different phases of the peyote experience, from initial onset
through peak effects to the gradual return to ordinary consciousness.
The progression from darkness through to dawn mirrors psychological and
spiritual themes of [[brazilian-ayahuasca-churches]], where formal organizational

### The Firekeeper

The firekeeper is responsible for maintaining the central fire
throughout the all-night ceremony. This role requires constant
attention and practical skill, but it also carries deep symbolic
significance. The fire represents the central axis of the ceremonial
world, the point of connection between the earthly circle of
participants and the spiritual realms accessed through the peyote
experience.
