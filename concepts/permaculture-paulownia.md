## Culinary Uses

While not commonly consumed by humans, paulownia leaves are valued as high-protein animal forage, containing 18-22% crude protein. Leaves can be fed fresh to livestock or dried for winter feed. Rabbits, goats, and cattle readily consume paulownia foliage. In some regions, young leaves are occasionally used in salads or cooked as greens, though this practice is not widespread. The flowers are edible and can be used in teas or as a garnish.

## Practical Tips for Growers

Site selection is critical — paulownia needs well-drained soil and full sun. Avoid planting in frost pockets or waterlogged areas. Young trees benefit from weed control within a 3-foot radius for the first two growing seasons. Fertilizer requirements are moderate to high, as the tree's rapid growth is nutrient-demanding. Staking young trees prevents wind damage during the first year. For timber production, prune lower branches early to encourage straight, clear trunk development.
