Paulownia (Paulownia fortunei) is one of the fastest-growing hardwood
trees in the world, capable of producing harvestable timber in as
little as 5 years. It is promoted as a sustainable alternative to
tropical hardwoods.

## Uses

- Wood and timber (harvestable in 5 years)
- Edible leaves (high-protein animal forage)
- Bare [[honey-locust-pod-fodder-timber-production]].

## Propagation

Paulownia is most reliably propagated from root cuttings or tissue
culture. [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] before bud break. Trees
can also be propagated by stump sprouts after harvesting.

## Growth Management

If the tree does not reach 10 feet in the first year, cut it down in
early spring and allow regrowth from the stump. The second growth will
be significantly faster due to the established root system. This
practice, called "coppicing," is common in commercial plantations.
After coppicing, paulownia can grow 10-15 feet per year.

## Timber Properties

Paulownia wood is lightweight yet remarkably strong for its weight:
- Density approximately 17-23 lb/cu ft (0.27-0.37 g/cm3), about half
  the weight of pine
- Low shrinkage and warping during drying
- High strength-to-weight ratio
- Natural resistance to rot and insect damage due to tannin content
- Fine, straight grain with a smooth, even texture

These properties make paulownia valuable for furniture, cabinetry,
surfboards, boat building, musical instruments, and aircraft
components. The wood is increasingly used as a sustainable substitute
for balsa in wind turbine blades and lightweight composite panels.

## Carbon Sequestration Potential

Paulownia's rapid growth translates into exceptional carbon
sequestration rates. Individual trees can absorb 10-20 tonnes of CO2
per hectare per year during peak growth, significantly exceeding most
conventional forestry species. After harvest, the carbon stored in
timber remains locked away in wood products. The rapid growth cycle
means repeated harvests can continuously capture carbon over time,
making paulownia attractive for climate mitigation projects.

## Intercropping and Agroforestry

Paulownia is well-suited to intercropping systems where trees are
spaced widely enough to allow light penetration for understory crops.
Common combinations include paulownia with wheat, corn, or vegetables.
