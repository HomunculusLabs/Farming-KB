# Lichen Desiccation Tolerance — Symplastic Cavitation and Ice Nucleation

## Overview

Lichen-forming fungi and their photobionts survive extreme desiccation and temperature stress through remarkable cellular mechanisms. When thalli dry below ~10% water content (per unit dry mass), fungal cells undergo **symplastic cavitation** — a reversible implosion where the protoplast separates from the cell wall. Conversely, when fully hydrated thalli experience rapid freezing, mycobiont-derived **ice-nucleating proteins** drive extracellular ice crystal formation, protecting cells from lethal intracellular ice. These mechanisms allow [[lichens]] to dominate ecosystems where other organisms cannot survive.

## Symplastic Cavitation During Desiccation

### Mechanism

As thalli lose water, two distinct cellular responses occur depending on wall properties:

1. **Cytorrhysis** (collapse): Cells with thin, hyaline walls shrivel and collapse. This applies to green algal and cyanobacterial photobiont cells and to thin-walled fungal cells.

2. **Cavitation** (implosion): Cells with thick, rigid walls cannot deform sufficiently and instead cavitate — the living protoplast implodes, creating a gas-filled space within the cell. This is a **regularly occurring feature** in lichen-forming fungi under drought stress.

### Desiccated Thallus Properties

- Shrinkage without wilting; different consistency and colour compared with hydrated state.
- Cortex: opaque and brittle (usually greyish) vs. elastic and glassy-translucent when hydrated.
- Crustose and squamulose thalli (tight substrate contact) shrink mainly in vertical axis; free-standing thalli shrink in all dimensions.
- Ascomal shrinkage during desiccation generates pressure on ascus flanks required for **ascospore ejection** — a functionally important element.

### Survival of Anhydrobiosis and Cavitation

Desiccated thalli contain <20% water per unit dry mass. Key questions:

- **Where does the cavitation bubble come from?** Possible origins: vaporization of water under negative pressure; gassing out of dissolved molecules (e.g., CO₂); air seeding through plasma membrane rupture.
- **How do cells survive without irreversible damage?** The exact mechanism remains unknown.
- **What happens during rehydration?** The cavitation bubble is compressed by the swelling cytoplasm and cell organelles, achieving irregular outlines. It has a smooth lining but is **not membrane-bound**.

### Rehydration Dynamics

- Top cortical cells rehydrate within <30 seconds of water contact.
- Full cortex rehydration takes >1 minute.
- A gradient from complete desiccation to full hydration exists among cortical cells during rehydration.
- One usually eccentrically located cavitation bubble per desiccated fungal cell.

## Concentric Bodies

### Description

Cells of lichen-forming and numerous desiccation-tolerant non-lichenized ascomycetes collected in the wild contain clusters of **concentric bodies** — round cell organelles (~0.3 µm diameter) of unknown origin and function:

- Usually found near the cell periphery.
- Not membrane-bound.
- Have a gas-filled centre surrounded by proteinaceous material.
- Composition and origin of the gas filling are unknown.

### Hypotheses

1. **Viral hypothesis**: Postulated to be viruses (Ahmadjian, 1993), but they lack features characteristic of viral particles.
2. **Cavitation remnant hypothesis**: Hypothesized to be the remains of cytoplasmic cavitation events — denatured proteins formerly lining the cavitation bubble.

### Key Observation

**No concentric bodies have been found in axenic cultures** of lichen-forming or non-lichenized ascomycetes kept on agarized, continuously moist media. This supports their association with the desiccation/rehydration cycle rather than being constitutive organelles.

## Ice Nucleation

### The Problem

When fully hydrated lichen thalli experience rapid temperature decline (as regularly occurs in polar and alpine ecosystems), intracellular ice crystal formation would be lethal. Lichens have evolved a remarkable solution.

### Mechanism

At cooling rates of 1 K min⁻¹ down to −20°C:

1. Cellular water **diffuses out** of fully hydrated green algal and fungal cells.
2. Water crystallizes on hyphal surfaces in the normally gas-filled intercellular spaces of the medullary and algal layers.
3. Due to substantial water loss, algal cells collapse and fungal cells cavitate.
4. Upon warming, the **extracellular water reservoir is re-mobilized** and absorbed by cells, which regain turgescence and metabolic activity.

### Mycobiont-Derived Ice Nucleation Activity

- Mycobiont-derived, **protein-based** ice nucleation activity at −5°C or warmer has been found in both symbiotic and aposymbiotic, axenically cultured phenotypes of lichen-forming ascomycetes.
- **Not found** in cultured photobionts — this is a fungal innovation.
- Very powerful ice-nucleating sites at hyphal surfaces facilitate avoidance of damaging intracellular ice crystal formation.

### Comparison with Plant Xylem Cavitation

- In plants, drought-stress-induced cavitation affects **dead** xylem cells with lignified walls. Irreversibly cavitated vessels lose function.
- In fungi, cavitation affects **living** cells — a distinctly more dramatic event.
- Yet fungal cells survive and recover fully, suggesting fundamentally different repair mechanisms.

## Molecular Basis of Stress Tolerance (Current Knowledge)

The molecular basis of desiccation and stress tolerance in lichens remains **poorly investigated**:

- **Acyclic polyols**: From fungal and green algal partners; centrally important mobile carbohydrates in the symbiotic interaction; assumed to act as compatible solutes under drought stress.
- **Glutathione-based antioxidant system**: Found activated in thalli collected in the wild, but **not** in sterile cultured isolates on agar media.
- **Major challenge**: Simulating rapidly changing, multifactorial environmental parameters under sterile culturing conditions.

## Ecophysiology — Photosynthesis in Extreme Environments

### Desert Lichens

- Photosynthesis restricted to narrow time windows: partly hydrated + available solar radiation + suitable temperatures.
- In the Negev and Namib Deserts, photobionts photosynthesize during a short period at sunrise (hydrated from fog/dew).
- *Ramalina maciformis* in the Negev: positive gas exchange balance on 218 days/year; negative on 88 days; zero (dormancy) on 49 days.
- Radial growth of crustose *Caloplaca aurantia* in the Negev: ~0.4 mm yr⁻¹.
- **Fog comb lichens**: Band-shaped to finely branched species with high surface-to-volume ratios dominate in coastal fog oases.

### Polar and Alpine Lichens

- Frost is the main water source for high Andean Paramos lichens.
- *Trebouxia* photobionts of polar lichens photosynthetically active at **sub-zero temperatures**, low photon flux, and partly hydrated state.
- Under snow cover with ice windows above darkly pigmented thalli, lichens live "as in a greenhouse."
- Very slow growth in continental Antarctic; surprisingly "fast" growth in maritime Antarctic.
