# Rules of Necessitous and Conservative Use

## Overview

Within the ethical framework of [[bill-mollison]] establishes two complementary rules of behavior toward natural systems: the **Rule of Necessitous Use** and the **Rules of Conservative Use**. Together, these rules form a practical ethical code that guides how [[permaculture]] designers interact with and harvest from natural ecosystems.

## The Evolution of Ethical Behavior

Mollison argues that humans, as thinking beings with long memories and the ability to investigate the distant past through techniques from dendrochronology to archaeology, are capable of learning from experience. Behaviors that once seemed appropriate (such as the use of biocidal pest controls) later prove damaging to society in the long term through effects on soils, water, and ecosystem health.

This capacity for reflection leads humans to moderate, abandon, or forbid certain behaviors and substances that threaten long-term survival. Mollison describes this as "a rational and sensible process, responsible for many taboos in tribal societies." The rules of necessitous and conservative use emerge from this evolutionary ethical process.

## The Rule of Necessitous Use

> We leave any natural system alone until we are, of strict necessity, forced to use it.

This is the first and primary rule — a principle of restraint and minimum intervention. It establishes that natural systems should be preserved in their existing state unless there is a genuine, unavoidable need to interact with them. The rule reverses the default assumption of industrial civilization, which treats all natural resources as available for exploitation by default.

The concept of "strict necessity" is key. Necessitous use is not driven by convenience, profit, or preference but by genuine need. This raises the ethical bar for any intervention in natural systems and requires designers to clearly justify their actions.

In practice, this rule means:

- Designing systems that require minimal extraction from wild natural systems
- Meeting human needs primarily through cultivated, designed systems rather than harvesting from wild ecosystems
- Preserving wilderness and natural areas as reserves of genetic diversity and ecological function
- Questioning whether a proposed use is truly necessary or merely convenient

## The Rules of Conservative Use

Once necessitous use has been established — once it has been determined that interaction with a natural system is genuinely unavoidable — the Rules of Conservative Use apply. These rules govern how that use should be conducted to minimize harm and ensure long-term sustainability:

### Reduce Waste, Hence Pollution
Every use of a natural resource generates some waste. Conservative use demands that waste be minimized through efficient design, complete utilization of harvested materials, and recycling of byproducts. In permaculture systems, the waste of one element becomes the food or resource for another, closing loops and eliminating pollution at source.

### Thoroughly Replace Lost Minerals
When minerals are extracted from a system (through harvesting crops, grazing animals, or other means), they must be returned. This principle underlies the permaculture emphasis on nutrient cycling, [[permaculture-ethics]]:

- **Care of the Earth** is served by the Rule of Necessitous Use, which minimizes intervention in natural systems
- **Care of People** is served by the Rules of Conservative Use, which ensure resources remain available for future generations
- **Setting Limits to Consumption** is the direct outcome of energy accounting and biosocial assessment

Together, these rules provide a decision-making framework that permaculture designers can apply in any context to determine whether and how to use natural resources.
