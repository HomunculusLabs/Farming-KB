# Odor Deodorizers and Comparison

Controlling cannabis odor is one of the most important challenges
for indoor growers, particularly those in residential areas or
jurisdictions where cultivation may attract unwanted attention.
Jorge Cervantes outlines a hierarchy of odor control methods in
Chapter Thirteen of the Marijuana Horticulture guide, ranging from
simple ventilation to sophisticated chemical and electronic
solutions. The fundamental principle is that a good exhaust fan
vented outdoors is the first and easiest step in odor control.

## The Odor Control Hierarchy

Cervantes recommends a layered approach to odor management. The
first line of defense is always adequate ventilation: a properly
sized exhaust fan that exchanges the total volume of the grow room
air in less than five minutes. When ventilation alone is
insufficient, growers can layer additional technologies including
deodorizing agents, negative ion generators, ozone generators, and
activated carbon filters. Many successful growers combine two or
more of these methods for comprehensive coverage.

## Deodorizing Products

Deodorizers work by changing the molecular structure of odors at
the atomic level. Products such as Odor Killer, Ona, VaporTek,
and Ozium are formulated from essential oils that create a neutral
atmosphere. These products are available in gel, liquid, and spray
forms. Growers typically use gels for long-term placement and
reserve sprays for emergency situations, such as unexpected visitors
during harvest.

Deodorizing pucks can be mounted on walls or placed near doorways.
Some models are designed to attach directly to ventilation ductwork.
A clever placement strategy involves mounting a deodorizing puck
just inside the front door, below the mail slot, to maintain fresh
air at the primary entry point. Timer-controlled aerosol dispensers
are also available for periodic metered releases.

## Negative Ion Generators

Negative ion generators are compact, relatively efficient devices
that control odors, smoke, airborne pollen, mold spores, dust,
and static electricity. They work by emitting negative ions into
the atmosphere. These negative ions are attracted to positively
charged odor molecules and other airborne pollutants. When the
ions attach, the odor becomes neutralized and particles fall to the
floor as a fine dust covering on surfaces.

These devices work best for small grow rooms with minimal odor
problems. They consume very little electricity and plug into
standard 115-volt outlets. The filter should be visually inspected
every few days and kept clean. The main limitation is that negative
ion generators create dust that must be cleaned from surfaces
regularly, and their effectiveness diminishes in larger spaces or
rooms with heavy odor loads.

## Ozone Generators

Ozone generators are among the most powerful odor control tools
available to growers. They work by converting oxygen (O2) into
ozone (O3) through exposure to ultraviolet (UV) light. The extra
oxygen molecule is a positively charged ion that attaches to
negatively charged odor cations, neutralizing them. Once the extra
molecule is shed, O3 converts back to O2. This chemistry takes
approximately one minute or longer, so treated air must be held in
a mixing chamber for effective conversion.

Ozone generators are rated by the cubic feet (or cubic meters)
they can treat. Growers calculate room volume by multiplying
length times width times height. Legal human exposure limit is
approximately 0.1 ppm for a maximum of eight hours. Most grow room
ozone generators produce about 0.05 ppm at timed intervals.

### Safety Considerations

Ozone generators require careful handling. UV light is extremely
dangerous and can permanently damage retinas and burn skin. The
lamps produce nitric acid as a byproduct when UV light encounters
moisture in the air. This white, powdery acid is corrosive and can
cause severe burns to skin and eyes. Quality generators include
safety features such as deadman switches that prevent direct eye
contact with UV tubes.

Ozone damages plants if they are exposed to excessive levels.
Symptoms include chlorotic spots on leaves that increase in size
and turn dark. Foliage near the generator is affected first, and
overall plant growth slows significantly. For this reason, many
growers install ozone generators in a separate closet or in
ductwork, routing air through the treatment chamber before
exhausting it outdoors.

## Activated Carbon Filters

Activated carbon (charcoal) filters are described by Cervantes as
fantastic odor control tools that consistently deliver results. The
charcoal is activated with oxygen, which opens millions of
microscopic pores. These pores absorb odor molecules and other
airborne pollutants through a process of physical adsorption.

Three critical factors affect carbon filter performance. First,
humidity must remain below 55 percent. At 65 to 70 percent relative
humidity, the charcoal absorbs moisture and clogs. At 80 percent
humidity, it stops removing odors entirely. Second, air must move
slowly through the filter to allow sufficient dwell time for odor
molecules to be absorbed. Professional units are designed with
fans that move just enough air for effective contact time. Third, a
pre-filter should be used to catch dust and large particles before
they reach the carbon. Pre-filters should be changed every 60 days
or more frequently in dusty environments.

Carbon filters typically last about one year before needing
replacement. Cervantes recommends activated carbon made from
coconut shell over crushed carbon, which is less efficient. An
intake screen should be installed to filter large particles and
prolong filter life. A more powerful exhaust fan may be needed to
draw adequate air volume through the carbon filter.

## Combining Methods

The most effective odor control strategies combine multiple
technologies. A common arrangement uses an exhaust fan with an
inline carbon filter for primary odor removal, supplemented by an
ozone generator in the ductwork for additional treatment. Some
growers add deodorizing gels near entry points and use negative
ion generators in the grow room itself. The layered approach
ensures that no single point of failure can compromise odor
security.
