# Structures in Permaculture Design
 In permaculture, a structure is any human-built piece of architecture or infrastructure, from a home to a barn, an oven, an awning, or a mailbox.
Permaculture structures are not defined by a particular style or material list but by a method of thinking that maximizes the ecological potential of every
built element, ensuring that structures fit into their environment in terms of morphology, materials, functions, and ecological impact.

## The Ecological Potential of Any Structure
 Permaculture structures do not have to follow any preconceived notions of what structures should be like. Like [[williams-pdc-ecological-design-principles]], any permaculture
structure should, as much as possible, be a product of and maintain material
and industrial loops.

Undoubtedly each structure will have a primary function: a house for people,
an oven for cooking, a coop for chickens, a dam for holding water. However,
a good permaculture designer maximizes the full ecological potential of any
structure. In addition to their primary function, any and all permaculture
structures can harness the power of the Sun, create healthy micro-climates,
create healthy soil, clean water, improve air quality, produce food, and
provide natural habitat for diverse species.

This concept follows William McDonough's building as a tree metaphor. Any
structure, from an office building to a tool shed to a bus stop, can follow
this concept. The goal is not to imitate the form of a tree but to match its
functional output: powered by the Sun, creating healthy micro-climates,
creating healthy soil, cleaning water, improving air quality, producing food,
and providing natural habitat.

## Designing for Wind and Sun

Be aware of prevailing wind patterns that will affect the structure. Understand
door and window openings and their relations to prevailing winds, making sure
they interact as intended. Be aware of how wind forces will affect roof
overhangs, awnings, and other parts of the structure.

Be aware of solar angles, both altitude and azimuth. Understand how the Sun
will penetrate and interact with both the exterior and interior of the
structure. The designer must make sure that these interactions are the ones
that are desired for specific purposes. This knowledge of sun and wind
interaction is the foundation of [[williams-pdc-sector-analysis]] provides the directional data needed to
optimize structure orientation relative to sun paths and wind patterns.
Together, [[comparison-passive-solar-vs-earth-sheltered]] design principles allow the
designer to place and orient structures for maximum comfort and minimum
energy consumption.

## Water Management Around Structures

A good permaculture designer should always calculate the amount of rainwater a
structure will collect. Once you know this, you can fashion the best strategy
for what to do with that water. It is highly recommended to put berms and
swales around the base of the structure. The main goal is to make sure
surface water runoff is under control and going where it would serve best.
