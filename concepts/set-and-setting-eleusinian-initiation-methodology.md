# Set and Setting — Eleusinian Initiation Methodology

**Source:** The Road to Eleusis, Chapters I, III — Wasson, Ruck

## Overview

The Eleusinian Mysteries exemplified what modern psychedelic researchers call "set and setting" — the deliberate preparation of psychological mindset and physical environment to maximize the therapeutic and spiritual potential of the psychedelic experience. The [[eleusinian-hierophantic-families-eumolpidae-kerykes]] developed a sophisticated methodology over centuries of practice.

## The Concept

The phrase "set and setting" was coined in the 20th century to describe the two critical variables in psychedelic experience: "set" (the subject's expectations, personality, mood, and preparation) and "setting" (the physical environment, social context, and guide's behavior). The [[lesser-and-greater-eleusinian-mysteries]] systematically optimized both variables.

## Set Preparation at Eleusis

The preparatory period for the Mysteries extended over months:

1. **The Lesser Mysteries**: Held in spring (Anthesterion), these were a mandatory preliminary that introduced candidates to the basic ritual framework

2. **Fasting and dietary restrictions**: Candidates abstained from certain foods (notably eggs) and fasted before the culminating night — see [[pelanos-barley-cake-and-fasting]]

3. **The [[sacred-road-pilgrimage-to-eleusis]]**: The 20-kilometer walk from Athens to Eleusis created a liminal state, physically and psychologically separating initiates from ordinary life

4. **Sacred bathing**: Ritual purification at the sea upon arrival

5. **Secrecy and anticipation**: The oath of silence created powerful expectations and curiosity

## Setting Design

The [[eleusinian-telesterion-architecture-and-ritual]] was itself a tool of set and setting:

- **Sensory deprivation**: The windowless hall eliminated external stimuli

- **Communal containment**: Hundreds of initiates shared the experience simultaneously

- **Acoustic amplification**: The enclosed space magnified the hierophant's voice and any sounds

- **Darkness-to-light sequence**: The sudden illumination created a dramatic revelatory moment

- **The anaktoron**: The innermost sanctuary from which the hierophant emerged — a staged revelation

## The Hierophant's Technique

Wasson noted that the hierophants of Eleusis saw "a new crop of initiates every year and there were many initiates." The limitations of this scale meant that individual consultation was impossible — unlike the [[eleusinian-oracle-vs-mexican-mushroom-oracle]], which served individual supplicants.

Nevertheless, the hierophants developed a group methodology that worked reliably for over a millennium. The key elements were:

- Careful preparation (fasting, procession, purification)

- A precisely controlled physical environment (the Telesterion)

- The dramatic timing of the revelation

- The communal nature of the experience (shared bonding)

- The secrecy that preserved the experience's uniqueness

## Hofmann's Note

Hofmann noted that his [[hofmann-ergonovine-self-experiment-detail]] with ergonovine "was performed without attention to 'set and setting'" — a significant methodological difference from the carefully prepared Eleusinian ritual. Despite this, he still experienced clear psychotropic effects, suggesting the potency of the substance itself.

## Fasting as Pharmacological Preparation

The [[pelanos-barley-cake-and-fasting]] at Eleusis served a dual purpose: religious purification and pharmacological optimization. Modern [[psychedelic-experience-preparation]] similarly recommend fasting before a session, as an empty stomach accelerates absorption and reduces nausea.

The Eleusinian initiates consumed the [[wasson-hofmann-ruck-collaboration]] after an extended fast, meaning the psychoactive compounds would have been absorbed rapidly and completely. This pharmacological insight — that fasting enhances psychedelic effects — was likely discovered empirically by the hierophants over generations of ritual practice.

## The Oath of Silence as Set Preparation

The requirement that initiates swear never to reveal the contents of the Mysteries functioned as a powerful set-preparation technique. By creating an aura of profound secrecy, the oath amplified expectations and created a psychological state of heightened receptivity. Initiates approached the Telesterion knowing that something extraordinary awaited them — a belief that itself contributed to the transformative power of the experience.

Modern research on [[lsd-set-and-setting-framework]] has confirmed that expectations profoundly influence psychedelic experiences. The Eleusinian oath of silence was, in effect, a sophisticated expectancy-management technique developed centuries before the concept was formalized by modern psychology.

## The Procession as Liminal Transition

The [[sacred-road-pilgrimage-to-eleusis]] from Athens to Eleusis served as an extended liminal experience — a deliberate transition between the ordinary world and the sacred realm. Walking for hours alongside fellow initiates, crossing symbolic boundaries (the narrow bridge, the obscene insults), and arriving at a landscape saturated with mythological meaning prepared the initiates psychologically for the visionary experience to come.

This use of physical journey as psychological preparation parallels the [[entheogenic-ceremony-preparation]] found in indigenous entheogenic traditions worldwide, where the journey to the ceremonial site is understood as an integral part of the transformative process.
