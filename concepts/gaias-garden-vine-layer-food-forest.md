## The Role of Vines in the Ecosystem

The vine layer fills the vertical gaps between the canopy, shrub, and
herb layers. By growing upward on existing structures, vines capture
light that passes between the leaves of taller plants and convert it
into food, flowers, and habitat. This is a prime example of nature
filling every available niche, a principle that ecological gardeners
emulate in their designs.

## Food-Producing Vines

Hemenway lists several food-producing vines for the forest garden:

- **Grapes**: Perhaps the most familiar food vine. Hemenway describes
  combining a grape arbor with a water tank and deck, where the grapevine
  shades the deck while letting in light in winter, cools the house,
  provides food, mulch, and propagation stock. Grapes are wind-pollinated
  and can be grown against light-colored walls for improved fruit set.

- **Hardy kiwi**: A smaller, more cold-tolerant relative of the
  fuzzy kiwifruit. Hemenway highlights Kevin Burkhart's example of
  training a hardy kiwi vine onto wires over the southern half of his
  roof. The seasonal shade significantly lowered summer temperatures
  while the bare vines allowed winter light through, and the harvest
  of delicious fruit was enormous. Hardy kiwi appears on winter-fruiting
  plant lists, with fruits clinging to branches into winter.

- **Hops**: Useful both as a brewing ingredient and as an ornamental
  vine. Hops grow rapidly and can provide dense shade.

- **Passionflower**: Produces striking flowers and edible fruit in
  warmer climates. The vines can be vigorous and may need management
  to prevent them from overwhelming other plants.

- **Vining berries**: Certain blackberry and other berry species have
  climbing or trailing habits that suit the vine layer.

- **Annual vines**: Squash, cucumbers, and melons can fill the vine
  layer temporarily while perennial vines become established.

## Wildlife-Supporting Vines

feed birds through winter. Hardy kiwi also serves wildlife, appearing

## Vines in Microclimate Modification

Vines contribute significantly to [[gaias-garden-super-guild-design]] layouts where they connect
different guild elements. A grape or kiwi vine growing through a fruit
tree guild adds another layer of production without requiring
additional ground space. However, Hemenway cautions that with dwarf
fruit trees, vining plants might shroud the tree and should be used
with discretion.
