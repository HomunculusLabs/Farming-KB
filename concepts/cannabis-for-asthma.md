## Cannabis for Asthma

Asthma is typically a chronic medical problem, ranging from mild breathing difficulties to severe and life-threatening emergencies requiring rapid intervention. Asthma does not discriminate by demographics, though children and senior citizens are the most vulnerable. Some children outgrow their asthma while others do not.

## Bronchodilator Properties

Since 1974, studies have been conducted to document the effects of cannabinoids on human patients with asthma. In the first trial (1974), researchers enrolled ten patients with stable asthma to test and compare the efficacy of smoked cannabis, ingested THC, placebo, and isoproterenol (a pharmaceutical bronchodilator). Results showed that while the initial effect of isoproterenol was more pronounced, the effects of smoked cannabis and oral THC lasted longer.

The study concluded that both smoked marijuana and oral THC caused significant bronchodilation of at least two hours duration in asthmatic subjects.

## Exercise-Induced Bronchospasm

By 1975, eight stable asthmatics were enrolled in another study to determine the effects of cannabis on both chemically and exercise-induced asthma. Results showed that in both cases, smoked cannabis (THC 2 percent) produced a prompt correction of bronchospasms and associated hyperinflation, similar to isoproterenol.

This finding is significant because exercise-induced bronchospasm is a common trigger for asthma attacks, and having an effective agent that works for both chemical and exercise-induced bronchospasm would be valuable for asthma management.

## THC Versus Salbutamol Comparison

A 1976 double-blind placebo-controlled study enlisted ten asthma patients to compare THC with Salbutamol (a standard bronchodilator). Results showed both drugs significantly improved ventilatory function. Maximal bronchodilation was achieved more rapidly with Salbutamol, but at one hour both drugs were equally effective.

The conducting scientist concluded that the mode of action of THC differs from sympathomimetic drugs and that THC or a derivative may make a suitable adjuvant in the treatment of selected asthmatics. This suggests THC could complement rather than replace standard bronchodilators.

## Aerosolized THC Studies

In 1977 and 1978, studies evaluated aerosolized THC compared to placebo and isoproterenol. Results showed aerosolized THC was less pronounced than isoproterenol in producing bronchodilation in the short term (5 minutes) but significantly better in longer time ranges (1 to 3 hours).

However, researchers noted that aerosolized THC caused moderate to severe bronchoconstriction associated with cough and chest discomfort in some patients, indicating that local irritation of the airways may make raw aerosolized THC unsuitable for therapeutic use.
