# Cannabis Root Decay Detection: Cellvibrio as a Post-Harvest Endorhiza Biomarker

## Overview

An unexpected but analytically valuable finding from the Winston et al. (2014)
[[cultivar-cannabis-microbiome-two-tier-selection-model]] study was the dramatic difference in endorhiza community
composition between the first experiment (sampled 8 weeks post-harvest) and
the second experiment (sampled from actively growing plants). The cellulolytic
bacterium *Cellvibrio* emerged as a clear biomarker of root tissue decay, with
its abundance differing by two orders of magnitude between the two sampling
timepoints. This finding has implications for understanding post-harvest soil
microbiology and for interpreting microbiome studies that sample at different
growth stages.

## The Cellvibrio Signal

### First Experiment (Post-Harvest)

In the first experiment, endorhiza samples were collected eight weeks after
harvesting the flowering buds and foliage. During this interval, root systems
remained in the ground undergoing natural senescence and [[cover-crop]]
planting schedules, as active decomposition may create nitrogen competition
for newly planted crops.

## Limitations

- Only two timepoints were compared; a full time-series would better
  characterize the succession dynamics
- The first experiment had only 9 plants vs 18 in the second, reducing
  statistical power for direct comparisons
- Other decomposer taxa beyond Cellvibrio were not individually analyzed for
  their post-harvest dynamics
- The study could not distinguish between colonization of decaying root tissue
  and mere presence of decomposer DNA from surrounding soil
