# Fungal DNA Extraction Methods

Reliable extraction of high-quality DNA from fungal tissues and environmental samples is fundamental to all molecular approaches in fungal biodiversity assessment. Fungi present unique challenges for DNA extraction due to their tough cell walls, high polysaccharide content, and the diversity of substrata they inhabit.

## Challenges in Fungal DNA Extraction

Fungal DNA extraction is complicated by several factors:
- **Rigid cell walls**: containing chitin and glucans that resist standard lysis protocols
- **High polysaccharide content**: polysaccharides co-purify with DNA and inhibit PCR amplification
- **Melanized structures**: dark pigments in many fungi can interfere with downstream applications
- **Environmental contaminants**: soil humic acids, phenolics, and other PCR inhibitors
- **Variable cell wall composition**: different fungal groups require different extraction approaches
- **Low biomass**: many environmental samples contain small amounts of fungal tissue

## CTAB-Based Extraction

CTAB (cetyltrimethylammonium bromide) extraction is widely used for fungal DNA, particularly from pure cultures and fruiting bodies:
1. Tissue is ground in liquid nitrogen or with extraction buffer
2. CTAB buffer with proteinase K is added for cell lysis
3. Chloroform-isoamyl alcohol extraction removes proteins
4. DNA is precipitated with isopropanol or ethanol
5. The pellet is washed and resuspended in TE buffer

CTAB extraction effectively removes polysaccharides, which are a major PCR inhibitor in fungal DNA preparations. The high level of polysaccharides in fungal DNA interferes with PCR; alternative methods using proteinase K digestion and CTAB have been developed to address this problem.

## Mechanical Disruption Methods

Mechanical disruption is essential for breaking tough fungal cell walls:
- **Bead beating**: using glass beads or zirconia/silica beads in a vortex or specialized homogenizer
- **Mortar and pestle grinding**: tissue frozen in liquid nitrogen is ground to a fine powder
- **Sonication**: ultrasonic disruption for liquid cultures or spore suspensions

The choice of method depends on the sample type. For environmental samples such as soil, bead beating is typically combined with chemical lysis for maximum cell disruption.

## DNA Extraction from Sporocarps

DNA extraction from fungal fruiting bodies (sporocarps) follows standard protocols but may require modifications:
- Fresh specimens provide the highest quality DNA
- Dried herbarium specimens yield DNA but may require additional purification steps
- Old or degraded specimens may yield fragmented DNA suitable only for short PCR amplicons (e.g., ITS region)
- CTAB extraction with proteinase K digestion (1-2 hours at 65 degrees C) is effective for most sporocarp tissues
- For small or delicate specimens, minimal tissue should be used to preserve voucher material

## DNA Extraction from Environmental Samples

Soil and other environmental samples require specialized approaches:
- **Direct extraction**: DNA is extracted directly from soil without prior separation of fungal cells
- **Indirect extraction**: fungal cells are first separated from soil particles before DNA extraction
- **Commercial kits**: many soil DNA extraction kits are available (e.g., MoBio PowerSoil, Qiagen DNeasy)
- **Humic acid removal**: activated charcoal columns, PVPP, or Sephadex columns to remove inhibitors

For soil samples, bead-beating with 0.5 mm glass or zirconia beads, combined with SDS-based or CTAB-based lysis buffer, provides effective cell disruption and DNA recovery.

## Quality Assessment

DNA quality should be assessed before PCR:
- **Spectrophotometry**: A260/A280 ratio of 1.8-2.0 indicates pure DNA; lower ratios suggest protein contamination; higher ratios suggest RNA contamination
- **Gel electrophoresis**: checks for DNA integrity (high molecular weight band) and degradation (smearing)
- **PCR amplification**: the ultimate test; amplification of a standard fungal marker (ITS) with universal primers

## Specialized Extraction for Difficult Samples

Some fungal groups and sample types require specialized approaches:
- **Melanized fungi**: additional purification steps may be needed to remove melanin
- **Zygomycetes**: large cells with tough walls may require extended bead-beating
- **Ectomycorrhizal root tips**: selective digestion of plant tissue while preserving fungal DNA
- **Herbarium specimens**: ancient DNA protocols with strict contamination controls
- **Low-biomass samples**: whole-genome amplification may be necessary after extraction

## Preservation of Extracted DNA

Extracted DNA should be stored at -20 degrees C for short-term or -80 degrees C for long-term preservation. DNA is stable for years when properly stored, but repeated freeze-thaw cycles should be avoided. For environmental studies, extracted DNA from baseline samples provides a reference for future comparison.
