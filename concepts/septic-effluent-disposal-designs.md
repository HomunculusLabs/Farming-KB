# Septic Effluent Disposal Designs

[[permaculture-design]] offers two basic productive disposal systems for septic tank effluent, transforming what is conventionally treated as waste into a resource for irrigation, tree growth, and energy production.

## System Types

### 1. Leach Field Systems

A leach field is a trench or open gravelled soakage pit through which sewage wastes from a septic tank flow. The nutrient-rich water then supports productive plantings.

**In clays and clay-loams**, tank water from a family home will stimulate fruit tree growth (without other irrigation) for 20 metres or more from the trench.

#### Trench Construction

The system follows standard procedures with these key specifications:

- A long trench with a **1:12 ratio base slope** is dug away from the septic tank outlet pipe
- Topsoil is set aside, and the trench is fitted with an **18 cm or larger half-pipe** (the South Australian "Arbor" system)
- Coarse gravels or stones are placed in the trench
- A strip of plastic or tarpaper is placed over the gravel
- The trench is back-filled
- Trees are planted **1-2 m off both sides** at **2-6 m spacing**
- All fruit and nut trees benefit from the nutrient loading

The Arbor system (developed in South Australia) uses a supported half-pipe that never clogs with tree roots. Cross-supports are placed every 1.2 m to create "pools" within the trench, allowing even water distribution and root access.

#### Disposal Pits

For semi-arid areas, square or round pits approximately **25 m²** can be constructed:

1. Dig out the pit
2. Fill with graded stone (coarse 6 cm at base to 2 cm at top)
3. Layer cardboard over the stone
4. Spread a thick layer of straw
5. Sow oats or green crop over the straw
6. Plant trees around the pit perimeter

This system is widely used in rural Australia and is effective for disposing of septic effluent while producing timber and fodder.

### 2. Biogas Conversion Systems

For maximum resource recovery, septic tank effluent can be fed into a biogas digester before final disposal:

#### Digester Construction

- Tank dimensions: **2 to 1.5 m deep**, **3-4 m in diameter**
- A loading chute for weeds and wastes (approximately 20 cm wide, slanted 30 cm to the base)
- Septic tank effluent enters at the base
- A perforated pipe at the tank base is worked by a small gas compressor to "bubble" gas back into the tank for **1-3 hours daily** on a timer, breaking up scum on top of the ferment
- Gas is caught in an inverted tank and fed to the house for cooking, lighting, and refrigeration

#### Effluent Cascade

After the digester, overflow proceeds through a multi-stage treatment chain:

1. **Baffled pond** — Pista, watercress, or any rampant soft water weed is grown
2. **Return loop** — Aquatic weeds are harvested weekly and returned to the digester as feedstock
3. **Leach field** — Surplus from the pond is fed to a conventional leach field for final soil absorption and tree irrigation

This cascading approach extracts maximum energy value from the waste stream before final soil disposal, while the aquatic plant stage removes residual nutrients and produces additional biomass.

## Design Considerations

- **Soil type matters**: Clay soils retain effluent near the root zone of adjacent trees; sandy soils may allow deeper percolation requiring different management
- **Tree selection**: All fruit and nut trees benefit from the nutrient loading; avoid planting root vegetables directly over leach fields
- **Slope**: Use gravity flow wherever possible; the 1:12 trench slope ensures even distribution
- **Safety**: Maintain adequate distance from water wells and bores; test groundwater periodically
- **Climate adaptation**: Disposal pits with straw mulch are preferred in semi-arid areas to minimise evaporation

## Integration with Broader Systems

These disposal systems integrate with:

- **[[natural-swimming-pools]]** as a final polishing stage
- **Greywater systems** for combined household water management
- **Orchard and food forest plantings** for nutrient cycling
- **Methane production** for household energy independence
- **Comfrey beds** for additional nutrient uptake and fodder production
