## 1. No Cultivation

Plowing is considered the most essential farming activity, yet Fukuoka
challenges every assumption behind tillage.

**Plowing compacts soil rather than loosening it.** The effect of turning
soil is the same as kneading bread: particles acquire an increasingly
regular arrangement with smaller interstitial spaces. The result is
harder, denser soil.

**Intertillage was a weeding method, not soil improvement.** Paddy
fields are typically tilled five to seven times per season. When
herbicides became available after World War II and farmers reduced
plowing, their yields actually improved, demonstrating that intertillage
had been effective only for [[comparison-seed-starting-indoors-vs-direct-seeding-techniques]].

**Fertilizers provide only temporary, local effects.** They speed growth
short-term but do not build lasting soil fertility. The soil becomes
dependent on continued applications, creating an endless cycle of
dependency.

**Fertilizers weaken crops.** Plants grown with fertilizers have lowered
resistance to diseases and pests, creating the very problems pesticides
are then deployed to solve. This establishes a vicious cycle: fertilizer
creates weak plants, weak plants attract pests, pesticides kill beneficial
organisms, the degraded ecosystem requires more fertilizer.

**Trace component deficiencies arise.** Applying major nutrients
(nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) without the full spectrum of trace
minerals creates imbalances.
