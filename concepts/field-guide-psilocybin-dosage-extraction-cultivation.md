# Field Guide to Psilocybin Dosage, Extraction, and Cultivation

This page covers the practical aspects of [[emcdda-psilocybin-non-addictive-profile-tolerance-dynamics]]
consumption, chemical extraction, and [[mushroom-home-cultivation-history]] as described

## Active Alkaloids

All [[decomposition]] generates heat up to 180 degrees Fahrenheit,
killing competing spores, insects, larvae, and molds. The compost is
ready when the straw breaks into short pieces, the manure crumbles,
and the pile takes on the smell of autumn woods. It should ball when
squeezed with no excess water visible, and remain loose rather than
compact since the straw aerates the mixture.

## Cultivation: Spawn Inoculation

Prepare a spawn medium by composting [[falconer-horse-manure-varieties]] enriched with
malt-extract sugar. Shred, compost, and pack loosely into wide-mouth
jars. Inoculate with pieces of a fresh cap of the desired strain.
The mycelium will colonize the medium visibly. Once fully permeated,
remove the colonized bulk from the jar and dry it to create **spawn**.
Insert small chunks of spawn at regular intervals into the prepared
[[mushroom-compost-bed-systems-duggar]], where they will grow to dominate the bed over several
weeks.

## Cultivation: Casing and Maintenance

Cover the inoculated bed with a 1-inch layer of sterilized soil
(casing). This layer holds moisture and prevents water from sinking
into the compost. Water lightly and regularly but never flood the bed,
as this kills the mycelium. Beds should be approximately 12 inches
deep in manageable containers. Mushrooms grow through the casing
layer, gaining physical support from it. Traditional compost lasts
4 to 6 months, yielding two or more pounds per square foot.

## Storage and Drying Methods

Proper preservation maintains potency indefinitely:

- **Air drying** at room temperature is the simplest method.
  Mushrooms dry in a few days and can be stored for decades with
  no loss of potency.
- **Critical warning:** never store fresh mushrooms in an airtight
  container. Their high water content (over 90%) causes them to rot
  in their own residual moisture.
- **Drying rack method:** The author's preferred technique involves
  stringing thread taut across the face of a box. The lower stem is
  sawed lightly back and forth against the thread until it cuts
  halfway through, then the mushroom is pulled slightly downward and
  left to dry for several days.
