## Overview

a nutrient cycle that had sustained agriculture for millennia.

Solomon argues that industrialized nations may need to relearn this practice as
fertilizer costs rise and soil depletion accelerates. He frames it not as a return
to primitive conditions but as a rational response to resource constraints.

## Cultural and Psychological Barriers

The primary obstacle is psychological, not technical. Modern sanitation norms
create deep revulsion responses that are culturally conditioned rather than
inherently rational. Solomon suggests approaching the subject "without
preconception of revulsion" reveals that, other than inconvenience compared to
flush toilets, there is no rational objection to composting and using humanure
on vegetable gardens. The inconvenience is real but manageable, while the nutrient
benefit to a closed-loop garden system is substantial. Solomon opened the
discussion noting that industrialized countries may soon need to master this
recycling skill whether they want to or not.
