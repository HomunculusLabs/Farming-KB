# Pond Edge Configuration and Yield Maximisation

A pond can act as a mirror, a heat store, a run-off area, a cleanser of
pollutants, a transport system, a fire barrier, a recreation asset, an
[[holmgren-built-environment-household-energy-storage]], and an irrigation accessory. All this, and it is
intrinsically productive as well. To design for greatest energy
efficiency, we need to look at the whole pond landscape and
configuration to aid aeration, heating, nutrient flow, and the
numerous accessory benefits we can get from hydraulic technologies.

## The Importance of Edge

Fish can be stocked as a fixed weight per surface area for planktonic
feeders, or per metre length of shoreline for omnivores; for the
latter group it pays to maximise edge. When designing ponds for
maximum productivity, increasing the shoreline length relative to
surface area dramatically increases the productive edge zone. A
crenellated or scalloped edge provides more habitat and feeding area
than a smooth circular edge.

A pond presents a host of opportunities for aesthetic and functional
placements of trees and plants; buildings and pond furniture such as
jetties, rafts, and boats; and habitat for birds and wildlife, for
beavers, water-rats and turtles amongst fringing vegetation, logs,
stones, and hummocks. Thus water (unless treated as a monoculture) has
great potential for beneficial design.

## Crenellated Pond Edge

Increasing pond margins as an [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-edge-effect-and-ecotone-design]], or planted along the pond
edges for fodder, can be achieved by creating an irregular or
scalloped shoreline. The edge zone is where the greatest biological
activity occurs: shallow water supports emergent and marginal plants,
provides spawning habitat, concentrates invertebrates, and creates
the interface between aquatic and terrestrial systems. The more edge
per unit area, the greater the total yield potential.

## Stocking Rates and Carrying Capacity

Ponds are said to have a specific "carrying capacity." If we
overstock or crowd water, fish cease to grow (or some may die) and the
water is then said to be fully stocked. [[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]] of fish follow
predictable curves in well-stocked ponds, showing the relationship
between weight gains and reduction in numbers over time.

As a good guide, a modest yield of 200 kg of fish per hectare per
year can be achieved. This may be grown as 200 one-kilogram fish, 400
half-kilogram fish, 800 quarter-kilogram fish, or 1000 fish of 125
grams each. About 300 grams is generally accepted as minimum pan-fish
size. Below this size fish can be utilised by being dried or made into
paste.

## Lifting Carrying Capacity

Several strategies lift the carrying capacity of ponds:

- Supplying more oxygen by stirring in air or using water movement.
- Supplying more nutrient by adding manures or compost.
- Increasing edge through crenellation of pond margins.
- Providing varied depths for different species niches.
- Adding pond furniture: logs, stones, and hummocks for habitat.

The highest fish production recorded was achieved in a rapid canal
flow of mainly sewage, demonstrating that nutrient flow is a critical
factor. The least productive pond would be a circular, warm, clear,
concreted basin in a quiet valley.
