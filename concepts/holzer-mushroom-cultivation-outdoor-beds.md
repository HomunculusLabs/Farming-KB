## Growing Mushrooms on Wood

### Substrate Requirements

- Only fresh, healthy, uncontaminated wood should be used
- Trees near busy roads, motorways, or industrial areas contain [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]]
- Cut a slice from each end of logs before inoculation to reduce contamination risk
- Wood stored more than half a year is probably unsuitable (except tree stumps, inoculated once they stop sprouting)
- Healthy wood: no dark or rotten areas, cut surfaces light-coloured and firm, adequate moisture content

### Hardwood vs Softwood

- **Softwood** (poplar, willow, alder, birch): Mycelium colonises quickly, yielding in 6--12 months, but logs decompose faster so yield is shorter-lived
- **Hardwood** (beech, oak): Takes twice as long to colonise but yield lasts substantially longer -- Holzer has hardwood cultures at 1,500m cropping for over ten years
- Expected total yield: 20--30 percent of wood weight
- Stone fruit wood is least suitable for mushroom cultivation
- Logs 0.5--1m long with at least 20cm diameter are easiest to manage

### Mushroom Species for Wood

All listed species grow on hardwood at the Krameterhof. Oyster varieties can also grow on straw.

- oyster-mushroom-bag-cultivation (Pleurotus ostreatus) -- very easy to cultivate; grows especially well on beech, maple, and elm
- King Oyster (P. eryngii) -- excellent flavour, thick fleshy stems, very versatile
- [[growing-gourmet-black-poplar-agrocybe-cultivation]] Mushroom (Agrocybe aegerita) -- very aromatic, requires higher temperatures (from ~15C), prefers softwood (poplar, willow)
- Judas' Ear (Auricularia auricula-judae) -- popular in Asian cuisine and as medicinal fungus; prefers elder but grows on other woods

### Inoculation Methods

**Spawn plugs**: Drill holes slightly larger than plugs, insert evenly around log, seal with fresh branch pieces cut flush. Spawn plugs are less susceptible to mould -- suitable for beginners.

**Notched inoculation**: Use a chainsaw to make notches over half the log's diameter in one or two places, fill with grain or substrate spawn, cover with plastic sheeting or adhesive tape to prevent drying, contamination, and predation by slugs, snails, birds, and mice.

### Culture Maintenance

- Start cultures in spring so mycelium colonises through summer before frost
- Mycelium grows at low temperatures but quick colonisation occurs best around 20C
- Store inoculated logs closely together in shaded areas
- Protect from slugs, snails, birds, and mice (e.g., with a line of fresh grass cuttings kept wet to attract and expose slug eggs to UV light)
- Ensure steady temperature and moisture levels
- Cultivate different kinds for yield over an extended period

### Propagating Spawn Yourself

Soak and boil wooden dowels, place in clean plastic bags with inoculated spawn plugs, turn bag so opening is at bottom for air exchange. After about one month, plugs are fully colonised. Alternatively, wrap boiled dowels with spawn in cotton cloth in a flowerpot on soil.
