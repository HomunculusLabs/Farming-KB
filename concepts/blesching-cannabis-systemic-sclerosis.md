# Cannabis and Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)

## Overview

Systemic sclerosis (scleroderma) is a skin disease of unknown cause in the
allopathic tradition. Symptoms may include tough, tight, and hard skin,
discolorations, and/or tight facial tone. Possible culprits include irritants,
microbes, or an autoimmune response where the body considers certain skin
cells foreign objects and attempts to eliminate them. For many patients, no
allopathic cure is available. Managing treatments include topical and
systemic pharmaceuticals.

According to the Cannabis Health Index, the evidence for cannabis in treating
systemic sclerosis is based on 1 study reviewed, with a CHI value rated as
not applicable (N/A) due to the primarily theoretical and preclinical nature
of the evidence. However, the endocannabinoid system research provides a
strong theoretical foundation for potential therapeutic applications.

## The Endocannabinoid System and Systemic Sclerosis

Research has identified that up-regulation of CB2 receptors would induce a
suppression of the immune/inflammatory process and fibrosis -- two key
pathological mechanisms in systemic sclerosis.

This finding comes from the same multi-institutional 2009 study that mapped
the endocannabinoid system across numerous skin diseases. The research team,
with scientists from Germany, the United States, England, and Hungary,
identified the ECS in the skin for targeted approaches to treating various
skin conditions.

### Key ECS Finding for Systemic Sclerosis

| Condition            | CB1 | CB2                                              |
|----------------------|-----|--------------------------------------------------|
| Systemic sclerosis   |     | Up-regulation induces suppression of immune/      |
| (scleroderma)        |     | inflammatory process and fibrosis                |

The dual action on both inflammation and fibrosis is particularly relevant
for systemic sclerosis, where:

- **Inflammation** drives the initial autoimmune attack on skin tissue
- **Fibrosis** (excessive collagen deposition) causes the hardening and
  tightening of skin that gives the disease its name

CB2 receptor activation could theoretically address both of these core
pathological processes simultaneously.

## Understanding Systemic Sclerosis

### Types of Scleroderma

- **Localized scleroderma**: Affects mainly the skin, with patches of
  thickened skin (morphea) or linear bands of thickening
- **Systemic sclerosis**: Affects the skin and internal organs including the
  esophagus, intestines, lungs, heart, and kidneys

### Disease Process

The word "sclero-" in "sclerosis" or "scleroderma" is derived from the Greek
word for "hard." The disease involves:

1. **Vascular damage**: Small blood vessels are damaged, reducing blood flow
2. **Immune activation**: The immune system attacks healthy tissue
3. **Fibrosis**: Excessive collagen production leads to skin and organ
   hardening

### Conventional Treatment Challenges

- No cure exists for systemic sclerosis
- Treatments focus on managing symptoms and slowing progression
- Immunosuppressive drugs carry significant side effect risks
- Organ involvement may require specialized interventions

## The CB2 Receptor Advantage for Autoimmune Skin Conditions

The focus on CB2 rather than CB1 for systemic sclerosis is clinically
significant. CB2-targeted therapies can potentially provide:

- Anti-inflammatory benefits without psychoactive effects
- Suppression of fibrotic processes
- Immune modulation without widespread immunosuppression

Indica and indica-dominant strains with their lower THC:CBD ratio favor
CB2 activation, making them theoretically preferable for systemic sclerosis.
CBD-dominant preparations may also be relevant given CBD's greater affinity
for CB2 receptors.

## Connection to Broader Autoimmune Research

Systemic sclerosis shares immunological features with other autoimmune
conditions covered in Blesching's Cannabis Health Index. The research on
rheumatoid arthritis, for example, demonstrates that cannabinoids can
effectively reduce autoimmune-mediated inflammation. The immune system
modulation properties of cannabinoids, documented across multiple conditions,
suggest a broad potential application in autoimmune diseases.

Boston scientists have noted that "regulation of the endocannabinoid
circuitry can impact almost every major function associated with the immune
system," providing a theoretical basis for cannabinoid therapy in autoimmune
conditions like systemic sclerosis.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Systemic Sclerosis

Tough, thick skin and stone-faced or leathery facial expressions may reflect
a belief system in which life is perceived as constantly dangerous and thus
requiring a thick skin. Emotional vulnerability displayed in facial
expressions must be hidden behind a mask because it is seen as a liability.

The skin is the body's boundary -- determining where one begins and ends. It
is associated with self-image, identity, and individuality. When someone
builds increasingly thick armor (thickened skin), they may simultaneously be
cutting themselves off from genuine connection with others and with their own
emotional experience.
