## Shipibo Icaros — Healing Songs of the Amazon

## Overview

Icaros are sacred healing songs used by Amazonian vegetalistas (plant healers) during [[metzner-amazonian-ayahuasca-ceremonies-icaros-and-healing-methods]]. They are received directly from plant spirits during dieta (plant dieting) and serve multiple functions: calling protective spirits, directing the visionary experience, extracting illness, and defending against hostile entities. [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]]'s paintings frequently depict icaros as visible, luminous energies — ribbons, waves, or geometric patterns that radiate from the mouth of the singing vegetalista.

## Nature and Origin of Icaros

### How Icaros Are Received

- Icaros are not composed — they are received from plant spirits during the dieta
- Each plant teacher has its own characteristic icaro(s)
- The vegetalista hears the song in visions and memorizes it
- Some icaros are whistled rather than sung
- The icaros of ayahuasca itself are described as "sad and melancholic, as if played on harps and quenas [Incaic flutes]" — reflecting the origin myth of a defeated king whose spirit inhabited the vine

### Categories of Icaros

**Protective/Defensive Icaros (Arkanas)**
- Create energetic shields around the ceremony space
- Used to ward off evil spirits, sorcerers (brujos), and hostile entities
- Depicted in Amaringo's paintings as colorful geometric arcs, nets, or barriers
- Some form visible "arkana" defenses — luminous walls of light

**[[mckenna-ayahuasca-healing-icaros-sound-suggestion]]**
- Used to extract illness from a patient's body
- Can be directed at specific organs or spiritual blockages
- The vegetalista sings while blowing tobacco smoke or sucking at the affected area
- Some icaros restore the patient's protective spirit

**Summoning Icaros**
- Call specific plant spirits, animal allies, or spirit helpers
- Used to invoke the mothers (madres) of plants for assistance
- Can summon spirit doctors, water spirits, and celestial beings

**Directional Icaros**
- Guide the visionary journey of ceremony participants
- Can intensify, calm, or redirect the ayahuasca experience
- Help navigate difficult or frightening visions

## The Role of Icaros in Ceremony

1. **Opening**: The vegetalista begins with protective icaros to establish a safe ceremonial space
2. **During visions**: Icaros are sung continuously to guide and protect participants
3. **Healing work**: Specific icaros are used when treating individual patients
4. **Closing**: Final icaros help integrate the experience and close the ceremonial space

The singing is described as producing visible effects — Amaringo painted icaros as streams of color, sound-waves made visible, that shape the visionary landscape. The icaro itself becomes a tool as real as any physical instrument.

## Whistled Icaros

Some icaros are whistled rather than sung. Whistled icaros are described as particularly powerful for:
- Calling specific spirits
- Directing energy with precision
- Working with the wind and air elements
- Blowing on the ayahuasca pot during preparation

The act of whistling an icaro and blowing on the cooking pot is part of the sacred preparation protocol.

## Relationship to Plant Spirits

Each plant teacher has a unique musical signature:
- Ayahuasca icaros are melancholic, harp-like, connected to the dead
- Different plants teach different melodic patterns
- The icaro is the "voice" of the plant spirit in the human world
- Learning an icaro means establishing a permanent relationship with that plant

## Icaros and the Visual Arts

Amaringo's paintings represent a visual translation of icaros — the patterns, colors, and forms he painted correspond to what he "saw" while singing or hearing icaros during ceremonies. This suggests a synesthetic dimension to the icaro experience where sound becomes visible form:
- Geometric patterns (especially Shipibo kené designs)
- Flowing ribbons of colored light
- Radiating waves from the singer's mouth
- Arc-shaped defensive barriers

## Modern Context

Icaros have become one of the most studied aspects of [[chullachaki-and-soul-recovery-in-amazonian-shamanism]]. They represent:
- An [[allegro-religion-prehistory-oral-tradition-etymology]] of immense complexity and specificity
- A pharmacological component (sound/vibration affecting consciousness states)
- A cultural bridge between Indigenous Amazonian knowledge and the modern world
- A living tradition that continues to evolve
