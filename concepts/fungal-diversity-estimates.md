## Fungal Diversity Estimates

Fungal diversity remains one of the largest unknowns in biology. Only a
fraction of the estimated total species have been formally described.
Current estimates range from 2.2 million to 3.8 million species
worldwide, yet fewer than 150,000 have been cataloged, meaning over 90
percent of fungal diversity may be undocumented.

## Historical Estimates

Early estimates in the 1980s proposed approximately 70,000 fungal
species. Hawksworth's influential 1991 paper used a ratio of known
fungi to plant species in the United Kingdom and extrapolated globally
to propose 1.5 million species. Subsequent approaches using
environmental DNA and macroecological models have pushed estimates
higher. A 2017 analysis suggested 2.2 to 3.8 million species, with
tropical and subtropical regions harboring the greatest proportion of
undescribed taxa.

## Current Knowledge

As of the mid-2020s, approximately 150,000 fungal species have been
formally described. These are heavily biased toward temperate regions
and conspicuous macrofungi. The Ascomycota and Basidiomycota account
for most described species, while early-diverging lineages remain
significantly undercollected. For every newly described species,
molecular surveys suggest dozens to hundreds of undescribed relatives
remain undocumented.

## Functional Diversity

Fungal diversity encompasses not only species richness but functional
diversity. Fungi serve as decomposers, plant mutualists, pathogens,
parasites, and endophytes. Each functional group harbors substantial
