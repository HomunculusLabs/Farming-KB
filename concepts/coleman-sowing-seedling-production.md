# Sowing and Seedling Production

[[eliot-coleman]]'s approach to sowing and seedling production centers
on precision seeding equipment and careful transplanting techniques
that maximize yields per square foot of valuable greenhouse space.
For economically viable greenhouse production, plantings must be
much more intensive than field plantings, making precision
multi-row seeders essential tools.

## The Need for Precision Seeding

Coleman credits precision seeders with practically doubling yields
per square foot over the single-row and homemade multi-row seeders
used previously. These tools compare very favorably with larger
(and ten to fifteen times more expensive) multi-row greenhouse
seeders used in Europe. All sowing at Four Season Farm is done with
one of two precision seeders: the original four-row "pinpoint
seeder" from Germany and a newer six-row model developed
collaboratively.

Row spacing of 2.25 inches is standard, common in European
greenhouses not only for baby-leaf salad crops but also for radishes
(at 2 square inches per plant) and carrots (at 4 square inches).
Three passes with the four-row model plant up to twelve rows on a
30-inch-wide bed. The six-row model accomplishes the same in two
passes. Crops needing more space (spinach, claytonia, turnips at
4.5-inch row spacing) are planted by filling only every other hopper.

## The Pinpoint Seeder

The pinpoint seeder uses two seed-selection mechanisms. First, four
seed-hole sizes on a movable axle (labeled A through D, smallest to
largest) are positioned so the desired size is centered under the
hoppers. Second, a small adjustable brush in each hopper removes
extra seeds. Each brush has a thumbscrew for tighter or looser
settings.

Precision seeders require finely prepared seedbeds. Rocks, chunks
of undecomposed compost, stringy weeds, or root residues cause
problems. The extra production per square foot from precision
seeding justifies the additional care in seedbed preparation.

The turning seeder wheels drive the axle. In loose soil the wheels
can sink and the axle will not turn. Coleman prevents this by
lightly rolling newly prepared beds with a 6-foot-wide mesh-surface
roller that does not glaze the soil like a smooth-surface roller.

## Determining Seed Settings

Before sowing any seed, Coleman determines the ideal hole and brush
setting. A letter code identifies settings: A, B, C, D for hole size
and T (tight), M (medium), L (loose) for brush setting. Testing
involves filling one hopper, turning the wheel one full turn multiple
times, and counting seeds dropped. The wheel circumference is about
9 inches, with nine holes per rotation for sizes A, B, and C
(approximately one seed per inch). Size D has seven holes per
rotation.

Once the correct hole and brush setting are determined, the code is
written on the seed packet. Coleman prefixes codes with the numeral
4 to identify the four-row seeder (for example, 4BT for most
baby-leaf lettuces in the greenhouse, 4BM outdoors, 4AM for arugula
in low winter light, and 4BT in summer).

For even more flexibility, Coleman had a machine shop deepen the
seed holes on an extra axle (D by three-eighths inch and A, B, C by
one-sixteenth inch). The deeper D hole now successfully plants beet
and chard seeds that were unsuccessful with the standard axle.

## Guiding the Seeder

When sowing, Coleman walks backwards down the path next to the bed
to watch the seeder in operation. If wheels stop turning or seeds
stop dropping, problems are caught immediately. Sowing depth is
adjusted by changing the handle angle: raising the handle sows more
shallowly, lowering it sows more deeply.

The furrow openers are designed so soil falls back quickly after
seed drop. After sowing, the bed can be tamped lightly with a rake
or the lightweight mesh roller, which also leaves a smooth surface
facilitating close cutting of mesclun salad crops at harvest.

The tooth marks left by seeder wheels serve as guides for lining
up the next row. When using the four-row model for multiple sets
of four rows, the wheel teeth run directly over previously sown
rows, which has never proven problematic.

For very small seeds (claytonia, minutina, sylvetta), even the
smallest hole drops multiple seeds. Coleman sets the brushes
tightly for these crops and finds the extra seeds unproblematic
since these crops tolerate close growing conditions. The seed
hoppers were sealed with liquid solder to prevent tiny seeds
from falling through seams.

The seeder should be stored dry to prevent corrosion and seed
sticking. In cold and moist conditions, the seeder is warmed
slightly in front of a heater before use. For moist greenhouse
conditions where condensation may wet seeds in hoppers, a
form-fitting Plexiglas cover for the seeder top is recommended.

## The Six-Row Seeder

The six-row seeder incorporates all proven features of the pinpoint
seeder with several professional improvements:

- Small mesh rollers mounted in front (for leveling) and behind
  (for driving the seed axle and pressing seeds into soil) the
  seed drop area, eliminating separate rolling passes
- Three different drive pulleys with a drive belt for delivery at
  one seed per inch, one every two inches, or one every four inches
- Six rows instead of four, allowing a 30-inch-wide bed to be
  planted in two passes (one down and one back)
- Seed-depth adjustment built into the front roller design via a
  screw knob

The six-row seeder is pushed rather than pulled. It is important to
maintain pressure on the rear roller to ensure the seed axle turns.
With no separate rolling passes and only two passes per bed instead
of three, the six-row seeder greatly speeds up the sowing process.

## Transplanting Techniques

Coleman uses several transplanting approaches depending on the crop:

- Soil blocks for most greenhouse transplants, with graduated
  sizes (mini-blocks, 2-inch blocks, 5-inch pots for tomatoes
  and peppers)
