# Fungal Role in Phosphorus Cycling

Phosphorus is often the most limiting nutrient in terrestrial ecosystems. Fungi play a central role in making phosphorus available to plants through mineral weathering, organic matter decomposition, and mycorrhizal symbiosis. Without fungi, most soil phosphorus would remain locked in unavailable forms.

## Phosphorus in Soil

### Forms
- **Organic P**: 30-80% of total soil P. Found in DNA, RNA, phospholipids, phytate
- **Inorganic P**: 20-70% of total soil P. Found as phosphate minerals (apatite, iron/aluminum phosphates)
- **Available P**: Only 0.1-1% of total P is in plant-available form (orthophosphate, H2PO4- and HPO4 2-)
- **P fixation**: In acidic soils, P binds to iron and aluminum oxides. In alkaline soils, P precipitates as calcium phosphate

### The Problem
- Plants can only absorb free orthophosphate ions from soil solution
- Most soil P is locked in unavailable forms (organic P, mineral P, fixed P)
- Phosphorus does not move freely in soil — roots must intercept it
- Without fungal assistance, plants access only a fraction of soil P

## How Fungi Make Phosphorus Available

### Mycorrhizal Fungi (Primary Mechanism)
- Hyphal network extends far beyond the root zone (centimeters to meters)
- Hyphae are thinner than roots (2-10 micrometers vs 100+ micrometers) — access smaller soil pores
- Explore 1000x more soil volume than roots alone
- Absorb P from soil solution and transfer it to the host plant
- In exchange: receive 10-30% of plant photosynthate (carbon)
- Essential for 80-90% of plant species (especially in P-poor soils)

### Mineral Weathering
- Fungal organic acids (oxalic, citric) dissolve phosphate minerals (apatite, rock phosphate)
- Proton extrusion lowers pH, solubilizing fixed P
- [[fungal-mineral-weathering]] for detailed mechanisms

### Organic P Mineralization
- Fungal phosphatase enzymes (acid phosphatase, alkaline phosphatase) cleave P from organic molecules
- Phytate (the main organic P form in soil) is particularly resistant — fungal phytases break it down
- Saprotrophic fungi decompose organic matter and release bound P

### P Immobilization and Turnover
- Fungal biomass contains significant P (C:N:P ratio ~100:10:1)
- Fungi immobilize P from soil into their biomass during growth
- When fungi die and decompose, P is mineralized back to available form
- This fungal P pool turns over slowly — provides a slow-release P reservoir

## Mycorrhizal Types and P Uptake

### Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF)
- Associated with most crops and vegetables
- Especially important in grasslands and agricultural soils
- Most efficient at P uptake from soil solution
- See [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]]

### Ectomycorrhizal Fungi (ECM)
- Associated with trees (especially Pinaceae, Fagaceae)
- Important in forests
- Can access P from organic matter (proteolytic enzymes)
- See [[ectomycorrhizal-ecology]]

### Ericoid Mycorrhizal Fungi
- Associated with Ericaceae (blueberry, heath, cranberry)
- Particularly important in acidic, low-P soils
- Can access P from organic matter very efficiently

## Practical Implications

### Agriculture
- Conventional P fertilizer bypasses the fungal pathway — creates dependency
- Mycorrhizal crops need less P fertilizer (30-50% reduction possible)
- Tillage disrupts mycorrhizal networks — reducing tillage improves P access
- Phosphorus is a non-renewable resource — peak phosphorus is a real concern

### Permaculture and Organic Farming
- Building soil biology (especially mycorrhizae) is the long-term P management strategy
- Compost provides P in organic forms that fungi can access
- Rock phosphate is only effective when combined with mycorrhizal fungi
- Cover crops maintain mycorrhizal networks between cash crops
