# Animal Integration in Permaculture Design

Animal integration is a fundamental principle of Mollison's
permaculture design. Rather than treating animals as separate from
the plant system, Permaculture Two weaves them into the landscape as
essential components that perform multiple functions: forage
harvesting, fertilisation, pest control, [[fukuoka-weed-management-and-green-manure]], and
product yield.

## The Interaction of Plants and Animals

Mollison emphasises that good gardening books treat each species as
though it lived alone, while in reality plants and animals exist in
complex webs of interaction. Any system which pretends to be designed
must account for the synergistic nature of plants and animals, placing
them in the correct array to obtain a third benefit or interaction
beyond what either provides alone.

Three categories of plant-animal interaction are identified from
Atsatt and O'Dowd's research on plant defense guilds:

1. Plants that breed predators attacking pests on other plants
   (insectaries such as Phacelia in orchards reducing Prospatella
   pest; sorghum or lucerne interplanted with cotton; umbelliferae
   with brassicas; strawberries near peach orchards)

2. Plants that repel browsers or pests through physical spines or
   chemical phenols (Ranunculus protecting grasses from cattle;
   Trifolium fragiferum protecting white clover from hares)

3. Attractant or decoy plants that draw pests away from crops
   (Solanum nigrum with potatoes attracting Colorado beetle egg
   masses as a lethal decoy)

## Livestock in Forage Forest Systems

Mollison advocates self-feeding forage forest systems where animals
range freely among tree crops rather than being fed cut fodder.
Traditional slash-feeding -- manually cutting tree foliage during
drought -- is far less sensible than allowing herds to self-feed
within managed forest strips.

Animals can range into forest when extremes of heat and cold affect
them, occupying pastures during tolerable spring and autumn periods.
An estimated 15% of beef yield is lost due to lack of shelter alone.
Tree crops provide this shelter as a secondary yield alongside
primary forage production.

## Poultry Integration and Forage Systems

Permaculture Two provides one of the most extensive listings of
[[free-range-poultry-forage-design-mollison]] species in permaculture literature. The goal is to
reduce or eliminate purchased feed by designing landscapes that
produce poultry forage year-round.

**Tree species for poultry forage:** Tagasaste, carob, honey locust,
mulberry, fig, oak, chestnut, black walnut, banana passionfruit
(frost-resistant, trellised on eucalypt), pokeweed (for pigeons),
Celtis spp., wild rye (moderate salinity tolerance)

**Vine species:** Kudzu (Thunbergia), chayote, gabizura
(Actinostemma lobatum)

**Root crops:** Nut-grass (Eleocharis), sour-grass (Oxalis), oca,
[[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]] under oak forest or on poor soil

**Herb layer:** Clovers, medics, lucerne, chicory, asparagus,
plantain, fennel, shepherd's purse, cleavers, partridge berry,
checkerberry

**Broadcast sowing:** Sunflower, millets, corn, buckwheat, wheat,
rye, barley, oats, teffi, chenopodium, wood millet, lupin

**Pulses:** Chick pea, lentils, field peas, lab-lab, centrosema

**Aquatic forage:** Wild rice (Zizania aquatica) in ponds for
ducks, Lotus spp., strapweed (Triglochin), algal pond weeds

## Special Herbs for Poultry

Several special herbs are highlighted for their specific benefits:

- **Shepherd's purse (Capsella bursa-pastoris):** Stimulates egg
  production; poultry control nuisance
- **Cleavers (Galium aparine):** Provides iron and iodine for
  yarded birds; protects from free-range excess
- **Plantain:** Absorbs chicken manure; ducks actively seek it out
- **Chard:** First choice green for poultry; succession sowings in
  throwover areas

## Aquaculture and Animal Integration

Poultry and livestock pens should be sited near ponds for manurial
input. The intensive pond culture system integrates fish, crayfish,
plants, molluscs, waterfowl, edge plants, and penned land animals.
Livestock pens near ponds provide continuous nutrient input, while
ducks on ponds (approximately eight per [[fukuoka-agriculture-tomorrow-quarter-acre-vision]]) contribute
manure, consume pests, and provide eggs and meat as additional
yields.

The land-pond interchange, drawing on Asian polycultural management,
treats the pond as a three- or four-dimensional system (including
flow and duration over time). Drained ponds yield rich mud fertiliser
for land crops, completing the nutrient cycle.

## Wild Rice for Ducks

Wild rice (Zizania aquatica) is specifically recommended for duck
ponds. Seed is planted in mud balls and thrown into shallow water
(18 inches to 3 feet depth). The rice grows in the [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]]
above duck height, providing a four-to-five-day harvest window before
the remaining crop drops as superb duck food. This creates a
self-maintaining forage system requiring minimal human intervention.

## Pest Management Through Design

Rather than chemical pest control, Mollison advocates design-based
approaches: dispersing plants among varied species leads to far less
pest infestation in all cases studied. Alternate or interplanting of
non-resistant and resistant plants prevents pest build-up more
effectively than the endless chase for resistant varieties.

The landscape itself responds against damaging practices. Cattle
disfavour plants they like, producing a system of plants they do
not like, which closes the landscape off to cattle. Some of those
plants are poisonous to cattle, creating a self-regulating system.
