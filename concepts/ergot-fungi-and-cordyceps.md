## Ergot Alkaloids

Ergot fungi produce a complex mixture of alkaloid compounds including:

- **Ergotamine** — a vasoconstrictor used medicinally to treat migraine
- **Ergometrine (ergonovine)** — used to control postpartum hemorrhage
- **Ergocristine, ergocryptine, ergocornine** — the ergotoxine group
- **[[ergot-fungi-and-ergotism-claviceps-purpurea]] urea* sclerotia.
The condition manifested in two forms:

**Convulsive ergotism:** Characterized by violent muscle spasms,
hallucinations, delirium, and psychotic episodes. Some historians have
suggested that ergotism may have contributed to the Salem witch trials
(1692) and other episodes of mass hysteria.

**Gangrenous ergotism:** Characterized by vasoconstriction leading to
restricted blood flow, tissue death, and gangrene of the extremities.
Victims would experience a burning sensation in their limbs, hence the
name "St. Anthony"s Fire."

Major ergotism epidemics occurred throughout European history, with
documented outbreaks in France (994, 1129), Germany (857, 1581), and
Scandinavia. The causative connection between ergot-contaminated grain
and the disease was not established until the 17th century.

## Cordyceps Species

Two species of *Cordyceps* are considered neurotropic:

- ***Cordyceps capitata*** — parasitizes *Elaphomyces* truffles
- ***Cordyceps ophioglossoides*** — also parasitizes truffle species

These species are closely related taxonomically to *[[psilocybe-muliercula]]* or alone in special nocturnal ceremonies.

## Mexican Use of Cordyceps

The indigenous peoples of the Nevado de Toluca region use *Cordyceps*
species together with their *Elaphomyces* hosts. During ceremonies, an
*Elaphomyces* specimen (such as *E. granulatus*, *E. muricatus*, or *E.
reticulatus*) is placed at the center of the room as a "director" of
the ceremony. The *Cordyceps* species are eaten either with *Psilocybe
muliercula* or alone.

However, Hofmann (in Heim and Wasson, 1958) did not find indoles in
*Cordyceps capitata* and *C. ophioglossoides* specimens that had been
gathered in a popular market in Mexico and preserved for a long time.
