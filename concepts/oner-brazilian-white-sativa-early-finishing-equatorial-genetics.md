## Brazilian White Sativa: Equatorial Early-Finishing Genetics

## Overview

The Brazilian White Sativa is a historically significant landrace variety that
The Rev describes in his introduction to [[cannabis-sativa]] Volume 3. Originating
from approximately 30 degrees south latitude in [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]].

## Geographic Origin and Photoperiod Adaptation

The Brazilian White Sativa hails from approximately 30 degrees south latitude
in South America, a region that shares a key photoperiodic characteristic with
Durban, South Africa (also approximately 30 degrees south latitude). At this
latitude, the seasonal variation in day length is less extreme than at higher
latitudes, which has produced genetic adaptations that are highly valuable to
breeders in the northern hemisphere.

### The Latitude Effect on Flowering

The Rev explains the significance of the 30-degree-south origin: when these
equatorial and sub-equatorial sativas are grown in the northern hemisphere,
they respond to the photoperiod in ways that differ from native northern
strains. Specifically, the Brazilian White:

- Begins flowering outdoors in the northern hemisphere relatively early in
  the season, similar to [[durban-poison]].
- Finishes well before the onset of cold, wet autumn weather that can
  devastate long-flowering sativa crops.
- Maintains the early-finishing trait dominantly in hybrids, passing it
  to offspring even when crossed with later-finishing varieties.

## Comparison with Durban Poison

The Rev draws a direct parallel between Brazilian White Sativa and Durban
Poison, noting that both share the photoperiod adaptation of their
approximately 30-degree-south origin. He describes Durban Poison"s "magic" as
where it is indigenous: in the northern hemisphere it starts flowering outdoors
in late June and finishes in the second half of September — well before any
coastal fog or rain. The Brazilian White shares this early-finishing behavior.

This parallel is significant because Durban Poison has become one of the most
widely used breeding tools for incorporating early finishing into sativa-dominant
hybrids. The Brazilian White represents an alternative genetic source for the
same trait, potentially offering different complementary characteristics.

## Potency and Cultural Significance

The Rev describes the Brazilian White clone from the early 1980s California
scene as having potency "close to the [[panama-red]], not quite the 6-hour ride
of Panama Red, but damn outstanding, for sure." This comparison places the
Brazilian White among the most potent sativas of its era:

- **High duration**: While shorter than Panama Red"s legendary six hours,
  the experience was still exceptionally long-lasting for a cannabis
  variety of any type.
- **Quality**: The Rev's assessment as "damn outstanding" from someone who
  had access to the finest landrace sativas of the era speaks to the
  exceptional quality of the experience.
- **Clone value**: The Brazilian White was maintained as a prized clone in
  Northern California during the early 1980s, indicating its desirability
  among knowledgeable growers.
