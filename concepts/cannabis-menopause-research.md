## Sexual Health and Libido

Menopausal decline in estrogen leads to vaginal atrophy, decreased natural lubrication, and reduced libido — collectively termed genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM).
CB1 and CB2 receptors are present in vaginal and uterine tissue, and the endocannabinoid system modulates sexual arousal and response.
Anandamide levels in reproductive tissues fluctuate with estrogen, and the decline in estrogen during menopause may disrupt endocannabinoid-mediated sexual function.
Topical cannabinoid preparations designed for intimate application have emerged as a potential non-hormonal approach to GSM.
Some patients report that small doses of cannabis before sexual activity reduce performance anxiety and enhance sensory experience, likely through modulation of the sympathetic nervous system and reduction of catastrophizing thoughts.
Research remains preliminary but promising for addressing this often-overlooked dimension of menopausal quality of life.

## Weight Management and Metabolic Changes

Metabolic rate decreases during menopause, and fat redistribution from peripheral to central (visceral) adiposity increases cardiovascular and metabolic disease risk.
The endocannabinoid system is a well-established regulator of appetite, metabolism, and energy balance.
CB1 antagonists (notably rimonabant) were investigated as anti-obesity drugs before being withdrawn due to psychiatric side effects.
CBD, however, has been shown to promote "browning" of white adipose tissue — converting energy-storing fat cells into thermogenically active beige fat cells — in preclinical studies.
CBD may also support insulin sensitivity and reduce markers of systemic inflammation.
For menopausal women navigating metabolic changes, CBD-dominant preparations may offer metabolic support without the appetite stimulation associated with THC.
Combined with dietary modifications and exercise, cannabinoid therapy represents one component of a comprehensive metabolic management strategy during menopause.
