## Overview

Mushrooms are being actively explored by virologists as new sources of antiviral medicines. Stamets identified at least 13 species with direct antiviral activity against HIV, herpes, influenza, and other viruses. Mushrooms offer protection from infectious diseases through three mechanisms: direct antimicrobial agents, host-mediated immune response enhancement, and mycofiltration to reduce environmental pathogen loads. See also [[medicinal-mushroom-extraction-preparation]], [[ganoderma-lucidum-reishi-comprehensive-profile]], and [[turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor]].

## Key Research Findings

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)
- Suzuki et al. (1990) discovered an antiviral water-soluble lignin from mycelium grown on rice bran and sugarcane bagasse
- LEM (Lentinula edodes mycelium extract) has antiviral properties
- Mycelial extracts have 70 times the in vitro antiviral activity of LEM from fruitbodies (Ghoneum 1995, 1998)
- Dried mushroom products from the cut stem base yield antiviral effects

### Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor)
- Collins and Ng (1997) identified a polysaccharopeptide inhibiting HIV type 1 infection
- Sarkar et al. (1993) identified antiviral substance from shiitake
- PSK (krestin) derived from turkey tail is a widely used immunomodulator

### Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)
- Antiviral activity in methanol-soluble fractions selectively inhibiting herpes simplex 1 and 2, and vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV)
- Gano-derenic acid A prevents death of HIV-infected lymphocytes and inhibits viral replication (Kim et al. 1994)

### Gypsy Mushroom (Cortinarius caperatus = Rozites caperata)
- Piraino and Brandt (1999) discovered derivatives that inhibit replication and spread of varicella zoster (shingles), influenza A, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)

### Birch Polypore (Piptoporus betulinus)
- Betulinic acid shows anticancer and antiviral properties
- Among the most antibacterially active species tested against E. coli and S. aureus

### Oyster Mushroom (Pleurotus ostreatus)
- Wang and Ng (2000) isolated a novel ubiquitin-like glycoprotein inhibiting HIV
- 15g/day for 30 days reduced LDL cholesterol by up to 30% (Cunde-Cimerman and Plemenitas 2002)
- NIH-funded clinical study for anti-hyperlipidemic effects in HIV/AIDS CAM therapy (Abrams 2004)

## Mushrooms Against AIDS

Several species — oyster, shiitake, maitake, turkey tails, and other polypores — have shown anti-HIV activity:
- Oyster mushrooms contain natural lovastatin isomer (cholesterol-lowering) that may combat protease inhibitor side effects
- Protease inhibitors cause LDL accumulation; oyster mushrooms reduce LDL while fighting AIDS
- Mushroom farms in developing countries address multiple needs: hunger, soil building, job creation, and HIV treatment

## Bioterrorism and Pandemic Preparedness

- Factory farms and feed lots may serve as launch platforms for pandemics
- A readily available, inexpensive, broad-spectrum antiviral from mushrooms would serve public health
- Combinations of mushrooms offer the most comprehensive protection
- Match the mushroom with the pathogen

## Antibacterial Synergy

- Mushrooms with both antiviral and antibacterial properties prevent opportunistic infections
- Immune-compromised patients with respiratory viruses can develop secondary bacterial infections (e.g., Streptococcus pneumonia)
- Mushrooms influence populations of bacteriophages — viruses that use bacteria as vectors
