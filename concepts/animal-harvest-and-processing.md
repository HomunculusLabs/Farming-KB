## Animal Harvest and Processing

Ethical harvesting, butchering, and processing of ducks and goats for the homestead. Covers dispatching, processing, cuts, food safety, curing, sausage, fat rendering, and offal use. See [[duck-keeping]] and [[goat-keeping]] for raising animals to harvest weight.

## Ethical Harvesting Philosophy

Following Joel Salatin's principle: honor the animal's life by minimizing stress, using every part, and performing the kill swiftly and skillfully. Key principles:
- Raise animals on pasture with natural behaviors respected
- Fast, single-action dispatch — no suffering
- Process in a calm, quiet environment
- Use every part: meat, fat, bones, organs, skin, feathers
- Thank the animal for its life
- Know your legal limits (see [[homesteading-legal-considerations]])

## Equipment Checklist

**Essential**: Sharp knives (8" butcher, 6" boning, skinning), steel/hone, killing cones, scalding pot (30+ gal, thermometer), plucking station or mechanical plucker, evisceration tub, cutting boards, cooler with ice, meat thermometer, freezer paper/vacuum sealer, apron, rubber boots, gloves.

**Goat additions**: Hoisting gambrel + pulley, meat saw (bone-in cuts), butcher block.

## Duck Processing

### Dispatching
1. Place duck head-down in killing cone (calms bird, restricts movement)
2. Extend neck; locate base of skull
3. Swift, deep cut across jugular veins and carotid arteries on both sides
4. Allow full bleed-out (2-3 minutes) into bucket

### Scalding
1. Heat water to 145-150F (too hot = cooks skin; too cool = feathers won't release)
2. Submerge duck fully for 30-60 seconds, agitate gently
3. Test a wing feather — if it pulls easily, scalding is complete

### Plucking
1. Pluck against the grain of feather growth in steady pulls
2. Start with wing feathers, then body, then neck
3. Mechanical pluckers work well for ducks (5-10 seconds in drum plucker)
4. Pin feathers and down: dip in wax (170F), cool to harden, peel off
5. Singe any remaining hairs over open flame briefly

### Evisceration
1. Remove head at base of skull
2. Remove feet at hock joint
3. Make vent cut around anus (2" circle), careful not to puncture intestines
4. Insert hand, gently pull out organs as a connected mass
5. Separate heart, liver, gizzard (save for cooking)
6. Cut away crop and windpipe from neck
7. Check for any remaining organs or blood clots
8. Rinse cavity thoroughly with cold water

### Chilling and Aging
1. Submerge in ice water bath (32-35F) for 1-2 hours until core temp reaches 40F
2. Age in refrigerator (35-38F) for 24-48 hours in breathable wrap
3. Aging tenderizes meat; duck benefits from 2-3 days
4. Freeze if not using within 3-4 days

## Goat Processing

### Dispatching
1. Restrain calmly with halter
2. .22 LR to brain (crown, aim forward) or captive bolt
3. Cut throat immediately — sever carotids and jugulars
4. Full bleed-out (5-10 minutes)

### Skinning (vs. Scalding)
Goats are typically skinned, not scalded:
1. Hang carcass by hind legs using gambrel
2. Ring cut around each hind leg below hock
3. Cut down inside of each leg to crotch
4. Pull skin down like a sock, using knife sparingly
5. Work skin around front legs and neck
6. Pull skin off head (or cut off at base if saving hide)

### Evisceration
1. Open abdomen from pelvis to sternum (be careful not to puncture paunch/stomach)
2. Let organs fall out into clean container
3. Tie off bung (rectum) before cutting free
4. Remove liver (cut away gall bladder carefully), heart, kidneys (save)
5. Inspect organs for disease (parasites, abscesses, spots)
6. Split sternum with saw for access to chest cavity
7. Remove lungs and windpipe
8. Rinse cavity thoroughly

### Butchering — Primal Cuts

| Cut | Location | Best Use |
|-----|----------|----------|
| Shoulder | Front quarter | Stew, curry, ground, sausage |
| Neck | Upper front | Soup bones, stew, ground |
| Rib rack | Along spine, 8-13 ribs | Rack of goat, crown roast |
| Loin | Along spine behind ribs | Chops (most tender) |
| Leg/Hindquarter | Rear quarter | Roast, stew, jerky |
| Shank | Lower legs | Osso buco, soup stock |
| Belly | Underside | Ground, sausage |

**Yield**: Live to hanging weight ~50%. Hanging to boneless ~65-70%. A 100lb goat = ~35-40 lbs boneless.

## Food Safety

- Chill meat below 40F within 2 hours of kill
- Keep raw meat separate from other foods
- Sanitize all surfaces with 10% bleach solution between animals
- Cook poultry to 165F internal; goat to 145F (medium) or 160F (well)
- Label all frozen meat with date, cut, animal
- Frozen meat quality: 6 months (duck), 8-12 months (goat)
- If any off-odor or sliminess — discard
- Check for parasites in goat organs (liver flukes, lungworms) — discard affected organs

## Curing and Smoking

See [[food-preservation-canning]] for detailed methods. [[cover-crop-quick-reference]]:
- **Dry cure**: Salt + sugar + spices rub; 7-10 days per inch thickness
- **Brine cure**: Submerge in salt/sugar/water 1-2 weeks
- **Smoking**: Cold smoke (80-100F) 4-8 hours after curing; hardwood (hickory, apple, cherry)
- **Bacon from duck/goat belly**: Cure 7 days, cold smoke 4 hours, cook to 150F
- **Jerky**: Slice 1/4" strips, marinate, dehydrate at 160F until brittle
