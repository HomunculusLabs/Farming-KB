# DOB (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Bromoamphetamine)

## Overview

DOB is an extremely potent synthetic psychedelic amphetamine, compound #62 in PiHKAL. First synthesized in 1967 and published in 1971, DOB is one of the most potent orally-active psychedelics known, with active doses in the low milligram range and effects lasting 18-30 hours. The presence of a bromine atom makes it particularly significant as a research tool — radiolabelled DOB has been used extensively to study 5-HT2 receptor distribution in the brain.

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical name**: 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-bromoamphetamine
- **Molecular formula**: C11H16BrNO2
- **Molecular weight**: 274.16 g/mol
- **Structure**: Amphetamine (3-carbon chain) with bromine at the 4-position and methoxy groups at the 2 and 5 positions
- **Appearance**: Fine white crystalline hydrochloride salt
- **Melting point**: 207-208°C (hydrochloride, recrystallized from IPA)
- **NMR confirmation**: Only two unsplit aromatic hydrogen atoms present (6.97 and 7.20 ppm), confirming unique 4-position substitution

## Dosage and Duration

- **Dosage range**: 1.0-3.0 mg orally
- **Threshold**: 0.5-1.0 mg
- **Common dose**: 1.5-2.5 mg
- **Strong dose**: 2.5-3.0 mg
- **Duration**: 18-30 hours
- **Onset**: 2-3 hours (relatively slow onset due to pulmonary first-pass metabolism)

DOB's extreme potency means that dosage errors are potentially dangerous. The difference between a threshold and overwhelming experience is only 1-2 mg. Users frequently report residual effects lasting well into the second day.

## Qualitative Effects

### Perceptual Effects
- Clearly delineated visual patterns, pictures, and colors with eyes closed
- Enhanced visual perception even at sub-milligram doses
- Long-lasting after-images following exposure to points of light
- Prismatic halos around light sources (especially the moon)
- Described as "a clear, clean psychedelic" by Shulgin

### Physical and Cognitive Effects
- Significant body load at higher doses, including tremor
- Depersonalization at doses above 2.5 mg
- Moebius-strip-like representations of reality at intellectual level
- Ability to enter and exit personal problem-solving states at will (at moderate doses)
- Cat-naps possible at 12+ hours despite ongoing effects
- Prolonged body awareness and enhanced tactile sensitivity

## Safety and Toxicity

### Animal Studies

Shulgin conducted limited toxicity studies in mice prior to human exploration:

- **50 mg/kg (ip)**: Considerable twitching and irritability
- **100 mg/kg (ip)**: Persistent shaking and hyperactivity lasting several hours
- **125 mg/kg (ip)**: Loss of righting reflex, convulsions at 50 minutes, death at 80 minutes
- **150 mg/kg (ip)**: Spontaneous convulsions within 10 minutes, death at 22 minutes

LD50 estimated between 100-125 mg/kg in mice. With a human effective dose of ~25 μg/kg, the [[mdma]], leading to massive overdoses. One reported fatality involved a young woman who snorted over 9 mg thinking it was MDA.
- **Arterial vascular spasm**: Tragic overdose cases have involved arterial vascular spasm, potentially leading to amputation or death.
- **Duration risk**: The 18-30 hour duration means overdoses cannot be "waited out" comfortably.
- **Misidentification**: In many overdose cases attributed to DOB, the actual drug identity was never established.
