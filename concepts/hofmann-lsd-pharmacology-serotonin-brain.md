# Hofmann on LSD Pharmacology, Serotonin, and the Brain

## Absorption and Distribution

[[lsd-compound-profile]] is absorbed easily and completely through
the gastrointestinal tract. It is therefore unnecessary to inject LSD,
except for special purposes. Experiments on mice with radioactively
labeled LSD established that intravenously injected LSD disappeared down
to a small vestige, very rapidly from the bloodstream and was distributed
throughout the organism.

### The Brain Concentration Paradox

Unexpectedly, the lowest concentration of LSD is found in the brain. It
is concentrated in certain centers of the midbrain that play a role in
the regulation of emotion. Such findings gave indications as to the
localization of certain psychic functions in the brain.

### Elimination

The concentration of LSD in various organs attains maximum values 10 to
15 minutes after injection, then falls off swiftly. The small intestine
is an exception, where concentration attains maximum within two hours.
The elimination of LSD is conducted for the most part (up to 80 percent)
through the intestine via liver and bile. Only 1 to 10 percent of the
elimination product exists as unaltered LSD; the remainder is made up of
various transformation products.

## The Triggering Mechanism

As the psychic effects of LSD persist even after it can no longer be
detected in the organism, researchers must assume that LSD is not active
as such, but rather triggers certain biochemical, neurophysiological, and
psychic mechanisms that provoke the inebriated condition and continue in
the absence of the active principle. This triggering mechanism remains
the central unsolved puzzle of psychedelic pharmacology.

## Sympathetic Nervous System Stimulation

LSD stimulates centers of the sympathetic nervous system in the
midbrain, which leads to pupillary dilatation, increase in body
temperature, and rise in the blood-sugar level. The uterine-constricting
activity of LSD, shared with other ergot alkaloids, has also been
documented.

## The Serotonin Connection

An especially interesting pharmacological property of LSD, discovered by
J.H. Gaddum in England, is its serotonin-blocking effect. Serotonin is a
hormone-like substance, occurring naturally in various organs of
warm-blooded animals. Concentrated in the midbrain, it plays an important
role in the propagation of impulses in certain nerves and therefore in
the biochemistry of psychic functions.

### Insufficient as Explanation

The disruption of natural functioning of serotonin by LSD was for some
time regarded as an explanation of its psychic effects. However, it was
soon shown that even certain derivatives of LSD (compounds in which the
chemical structure is slightly modified) that exhibit no hallucinogenic
properties, inhibit the effects of serotonin just as strongly, or yet
more strongly, than unaltered LSD. The serotonin-blocking effect of LSD
thus does not suffice to explain its hallucinogenic properties.

## Dopamine Interaction

LSD also influences neurophysiological functions connected with dopamine,
which is, like serotonin, a naturally occurring hormone-like substance.
Most of the brain centers receptive to dopamine become activated by LSD,
while the others are depressed. This dual action on dopaminergic systems
suggests a complex interaction with multiple neurotransmitter pathways.

## The Unsolved Biochemical Mechanism

As of Hofmann's writing, the biochemical mechanisms through which LSD
exerts its psychic effects remained unknown. Investigations of the
interactions of LSD with brain factors like serotonin and dopamine,
however, served as examples of how LSD could function as a tool in brain
research, in the study of the biochemical processes that underlie psychic
functions.

## Toxicity: Extremely Low

The toxicity of LSD has been determined in various animal species. A
standard for the toxicity of a substance is the LD50, the dose at which
50 percent of the experimental animals die. The LD50 of LSD in mice is
50 to 60 mg/kg intravenously. For an average-weight human, this would
correspond to 50 to 60 thousandths of a gram of LSD per kilogram of body
weight.

### A Remarkable Therapeutic Index

LSD is a very nontoxic substance compared to its extraordinarily high
psychic activity. The danger of LSD lies not in its toxicity, but rather
in the unpredictability of its psychic effects. This extraordinary
therapeutic index, the ratio between effective and lethal doses, is
unmatched by almost any other psychoactive substance.

## Chromosome and Teratogenic Concerns

Reports appeared alleging that damage to chromosomes or genetic material
had been caused by LSD. These effects were observed in only a few
individual cases. Subsequent comprehensive investigations of a large,
statistically significant number of cases showed no connection between
chromosome anomalies and LSD medication.

The same applies to reports about fetal deformities allegedly produced
by LSD. In animal experiments, extremely high doses of LSD, well above
those used in human beings, can induce fetal deformities, but under
these conditions even harmless substances produce such damage. Examination
of reported individual cases of human fetal deformities revealed no
connection with LSD use. If there had been any such connection, it would
long since have attracted attention, as several million people had taken
LSD.

## LSD as a Research Tool

The unique pharmacological properties of LSD, particularly its specificity
for the human psyche and its interactions with serotonin and dopamine
systems, made it an invaluable tool for neuroscientific research. The
[[hofmann-lsd-sandoz-research-program]]
distributed LSD free of charge to qualified investigators worldwide,
generating thousands of publications that advanced understanding of brain
chemistry and psychiatric disorders.
