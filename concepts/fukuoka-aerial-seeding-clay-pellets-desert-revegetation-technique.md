## Aerial Seeding Clay Pellets for Desert Revegetation

## Overview

The clay seed pellet technique for aerial seeding is Masanobu Fukuoka's method for revegetating desert areas by broadcasting seed-encased clay pellets from airplanes. Developed as Appendix B of *Sowing Seeds in the Desert*, this technique scales Fukuoka's earlier clay pellet work for rice and [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]]. Pellets protect seeds from predators and extreme conditions while providing a germination microenvironment.

## Pellet Composition

### Standard Formula

**Inner Layer — Seeds and Fungi:**
- Seeds of 100+ varieties: trees, fruit trees, shrubs, vegetables, grains, fungi — **10%**

**Middle Layer — Clay Encasement:**
- Fine powdered clay (brick or porcelain quality) — **50%**
- Applied with water mist while mixer rotates

**Outer Layer — Protection and Nutrition:**
- Bittern (concentrated seawater extract) — **10–15%**
- Seaweed paste (binder) — **5%**
- Slaked lime — **10%**
- [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]]
- Slaked lime adjusts local soil pH for germinating seed

## Ecological Rationale

### Interdependence Principle

Plants exist in intimate connection with other plants, animals, and microorganisms — none can develop alone. Desert regions especially need microorganisms and diverse plant species. The pellet technique addresses this by including 100+ plant species for ecological diversity, beneficial fungi for soil [[biological-network-optimization]] introduction.
