# Mushroom Training: Shiitake Cultivation

## Introduction to Shiitake Mushrooms

Shiitake mushrooms ([[staycare-lentinula-edodes-in-bioremediation]]), known in Thai as "Hed Hom," are one of the most valued [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]] worldwide. In the FAO [[mushroom-disabilities-training-program-design]] for people with disabilities, shiitake is included among the species that can be cultivated in substrate bags. While oyster mushrooms are the primary focus of the training, shiitake cultivation follows the same general substrate bag methodology with some important adaptations.

## Botanical Characteristics

Shiitake mushrooms are saprophytic fungi, meaning they feed on dead organic matter — specifically hardwood in nature. The scientific name Lentinula edodes refers to the species' preference for dead wood substrates. In the training program, shiitake is cultivated using sawdust-based substrate bags rather than the traditional log method, making it more accessible for small-scale producers and people with disabilities.

## Cultivation Requirements

**[[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]]:** Shiitake requires a narrower temperature range of 20-30°C for fruiting, making it best suited for cultivation during the rainy and cold seasons in Thailand (approximately August through January). This is slightly cooler than the requirements for most oyster mushroom varieties.

**Cultivation season:** Mid-rainy season through early winter (August-January), making it complementary to the summer oyster mushroom production cycle.

**Substrate:** Like other bag-cultivated species, shiitake grows on a sawdust-based substrate supplemented with rice bran and minerals. The substrate recipe is the same as for oyster mushrooms: 100 kg sawdust, 5 kg rice bran, 2 kg calcium sulfate, 1 kg calcium carbonate, 0.2 kg magnesium sulfate.

## Health and Nutritional Properties

Shiitake mushrooms are recognized for their significant health benefits:

- **Cartilage development**: Particularly noted as "good for baby's cartilage" in traditional Thai medicine
- **Immune system support**: Shiitake contains lentinan, a polysaccharide with well-documented immune-modulating properties
- **Nutritional value**: Like other cultivated mushrooms, shiitake provides high protein, essential amino acids, low fat, and high fiber
- **Medicinal applications**: Shiitake has been extensively studied for anti-cancer, cholesterol-lowering, and antiviral properties

The training program emphasizes that eating two to three types of mushroom per day provides proper amounts of essential amino acids required by the body.

## Substrate Bag Cultivation Method

Shiitake cultivation follows the same substrate bag procedure as other species in the training program:

**Step 5 — Substrate bag production:**
The standard substrate recipe applies. Note that in hot climates, additives should not exceed 7.5% of the total substrate weight to prevent excessive microbial competition and overheating during fermentation.

**Step 6 — Pasteurization:**
Bags are pasteurized at 90-100°C for 3-4 hours using either country-style drum steamers or commercial pasteurization chambers. [[mollison-designers-passive-solar-heating-building-design]] pasteurization is available as an optional alternative.

**Step 7 — Inoculation:**
Sorghum seed spawn of Lentinula edodes is inoculated into pasteurized substrate bags under sterile conditions near an alcohol lamp flame. Strict hygiene is critical to prevent contamination.

**Step 8 — Incubation:**
Incubation conditions for shiitake follow the general protocol:
- Initially minimal ventilation and light
- After 10 days, increase ventilation to regulate temperature
- After 20-25 days, provide full ventilation and light for monitoring
- Incubation period is approximately 4-5 weeks
- Daily visual checks for abnormal mycelium (contaminant colors: green, black, brown, orange, red)

**Step 9 — Opening bags:**
For shiitake, the bag is cut at the shoulder (similar to Lentinus species rather than oyster mushrooms which only require plug removal). This exposes more substrate surface area for fruiting.

**Step 10 — Maintaining and monitoring:**
- Maintain humidity at 75-90% through frequent light spraying
- Control temperature through ventilation management
- Monitor for pests (especially mites) at least twice weekly
- Keep bags clean at all times
- Separate contaminated bags immediately

**Step 11 — Harvesting:**
Mushrooms are ready to harvest 2-3 days after first appearance. Harvest by gently twisting at the base. Multiple flushes occur over the production cycle.

## Log-Based Cultivation (Traditional Method)

While the FAO training program focuses on substrate bag cultivation for accessibility, shiitake is traditionally grown on hardwood logs. The log method involves:
- Selecting fresh hardwood logs (oak, beech, or similar species)
- Drilling holes and inoculating with shiitake spawn
- Sealing holes with wax
- Incubating logs in a shaded, moist location for 6-12 months
- Shocking logs (soaking in cold water) to trigger fruiting

The log method produces higher quality shiitake with better texture and flavor but requires more space, time, and physical labor. For people with mobility limitations, the substrate bag method is more practical.

## Comparison with Oyster Mushroom Cultivation

| Feature | Shiitake (Lentinula edodes) | Oyster (Pleurotus ostreatus) |
|---------|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| Temperature range | 20-30°C | 20-35°C |
| Season (Thailand) | Aug-Jan | Jun-Feb |
| Incubation time | 4-5 weeks | 4 weeks |

## Notes for Trainees with Disabilities

Shiitake is recommended as a second-tier crop after mastering oyster mushrooms. The longer incubation, tighter temperature requirements, and lower tolerance for errors make it more challenging. However, its higher market price can significantly increase income once cultivation skills are established. Harvest twice daily for young, high-quality mushrooms.

## Processing and Marketing

Shiitake mushrooms can be dried for long-term preservation and value addition. Dried shiitake has an intensified umami flavor and commands premium prices. Sun drying or low-temperature artificial drying preserves the medicinal compounds. The training program includes record-keeping skills so trainees can track production costs and profit margins.
