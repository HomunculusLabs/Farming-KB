# Acidobacteria Decline in the Rhizosphere to Endorhiza Transition

## Overview

One of the most consistent and significant findings in [[two-tier-selection-model-plant-microbiome]] of root colonization and has been observed across multiple plant systems, though the Cannabis microbiome study by Winston et al. ## The Acidobacteria Phylum

Acidobacteria is a widespread and abundant bacterial phylum in soils worldwide. They are typically oligotrophic, meaning they thrive in low-nutrient environments where competition from fast-growing organisms is reduced. In most soils, Acidobacteria represent one of the most dominant phyla, often comprising 20-30% or more of the total [[soil-edaphic-factors-microbial-communities]] including pH, nitrogen content, carbon levels, salinity, and water content.

In the BEST analysis of the second experiment, the variance in community data was optimally explained by three edaphic factors: nitrogen, carbon, and water content (rho = 0.632). These same factors that structure the overall soil community also maintain the high relative abundance of Acidobacteria in bulk soil.

### Rhizosphere

The rhizosphere represents the zone of soil immediately influenced by root secretions (rhizodeposits), including sugars, amino acids, organic acids, and mucilage. Here, Acidobacteria begin to decline in relative abundance. The introduction of root exudates, which are rich in simple carbohydrates and other readily metabolizable compounds, creates a nutrient-rich environment that favors copiotrophic organisms over oligotrophs.

The correlation between bulk soil and rhizosphere OTU abundances was very high (Pearson's rho: 0.92), indicating that while shifts occur, the rhizosphere community is still largely derived from and structured by the surrounding soil community. This strong correlation reflects the first tier of the two-tier selection model, where edaphic factors remain the dominant organizing force.

### Endorhiza

The most dramatic decline occurs within the endorhiza, the interior root tissue where bacteria have colonized plant cells. Here, Acidobacteria from order iii1-15 showed the most significant decrease among all 51 OTUs that differentiated between sample types (Bonferroni-corrected ANOVA: p = 1.12e-7). This represents one of the most statistically robust findings in the entire study.

The correlation between rhizosphere and endorhiza communities dropped substantially (Pearson's rho: 0.63), and the correlation between bulk soil and endorhiza was even lower (Pearson's rho: 0.42), reflecting the increasingly selective nature of the colonization process. These declining correlations mirror the declining abundance of Acidobacteria at each step.
