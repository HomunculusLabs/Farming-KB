## Gourmet Mushroom Cultivation Gourmet mushroom cultivation is the practice of growing edible fungi for culinary markets. The most commonly cultivated species include **shiitake** (*Lentinula edodes*), **oyster mushrooms** (*Pleurotus* spp.), **king oyster** (*Pleurotus eryngii*), **enoki** (*[[cultivator-sterile-technique-and-agar-culture]]** — working in a laminar flow hood or still-air box to
  prevent contamination by bacteria, mold spores, or competing fungi
- **Grain preparation** — soaking, boiling, and sterilizing grain in jars or
  bags at 15 PSI for 90-120 minutes; proper grain moisture (50-55%) is
  critical — grains should be firm but not dry when sterilized
- **Inoculation** — transferring mycelium from agar plates or liquid culture
  into sterilized grain under sterile conditions; each transfer point multiplies
  the mycelium exponentially (one plate can inoculate 10 jars, each jar can
  inoculate 10 bags)
- **Incubation** — holding inoculated grain at species-appropriate temperature
  (typically 20-25°C) until fully colonized (usually 2-4 weeks); shaking jars
  at 30% colonization distributes mycelium and speeds the process

Spawn quality directly determines crop success. Contaminated spawn will fail,
and weak or slow-growing spawn produces suboptimal yields. Commercial spawn
labs test for purity and vigor before distribution.

## Substrate Preparation

The substrate is the material on which mushrooms fruit. Different species prefer
different substrates based on their natural ecological role as decomposers:

- **Shiitake**: hardwood sawdust (oak, beech) supplemented with wheat bran at
  10-20%, or hardwood logs via plug spawn inoculation; natural log cultivation
  produces premium-quality mushrooms with longer shelf life
- **Oyster mushrooms**: straw, coffee grounds, hardwood sawdust, or
  agricultural waste; highly versatile and aggressive decomposers that can
  fruit on dozens of substrate types; the easiest mushroom for beginners
- **King oyster**: enriched hardwood sawdust with higher supplementation (20-25%
  bran) for larger fruiting bodies; demands careful [[gourmet-mushroom-enriched-sawdust-cultivation]]; requires cold shocking for
  proper morphology; commercial enoki is grown in high-CO₂ conditions to
  produce long, white stems

Substrate must be **pasteurized** (60-80°C for 60-90 minutes for straw/coffee
grounds) or **sterilized** (121°C at 15 PSI for 90-120 minutes for sawdust
blocks) before inoculation. Supplementation with nitrogen-rich additives (bran,
soy hulls) increases yields significantly — often doubling or tripling
[[bloomfield-mushroom-evaporative-cooling-fungal-frigidity]] from growing mushrooms contributes to local humidity
- **Temperature** — varies by species; oysters fruit at 15-25°C, shiitake at
  10-20°C, enoki at 5-15°C; most species benefit from a 5-10°C temperature
  drop to initiate fruiting
- **Light** — indirect ambient light or 12-hour photoperiod triggers fruiting;
  blue spectrum (450-495 nm) is most effective; direct sunlight is harmful
- **Cold shock** — some species (shiitake, enoki) benefit from a temperature
  drop to initiate pinning; this mimics seasonal change in nature

## Flush Cycles

Most cultivated mushrooms produce **multiple flushes** (crops) from a single
substrate block:

- **First flush** — typically the largest yield (50-60% of total); pins form
  over 3-7 days, then grow to maturity in another 5-10 days
- **Second flush** — often 20-30% of total yield; requires a rest period of
  1-2 weeks with reduced humidity to allow substrate recovery
- **Third flush and beyond** — diminishing returns; most commercial growers
  harvest 2-3 flushes before discarding blocks
