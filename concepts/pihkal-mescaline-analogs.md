# Mescaline Analogs — Phenethylamine Psychedelics from PIHKAL

Mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) is the prototype
[[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]] psychedelic and the compound that inspired
[[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]]'s lifelong exploration of substituted
phenethylamines. Extracted from peyote cactus (Lophophora
williamsii) and used for millennia by indigenous peoples of the
Americas, mescaline provided the structural template from which Shulgin
systematically explored hundreds of analogs documented in PIHKAL.

## Mescaline: The Prototype

**Dosage:** 200-400 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 10-12 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** +++

Mescaline served as Shulgin's benchmark. He described it as producing
a richly visual, contemplative experience with a character distinct
from LSD or psilocybin. Effects unfold slowly, peaking 2-4 hours after
ingestion. Mescaline is known for vivid, colorful geometric patterns and
organic imagery with a warm, emotionally grounded quality. Shulgin
considered it the "gentle grandfather" of synthetic phenethylamines, noting its
well-tolerated body load and predictable dose-response curve.

The trimethoxy substitution pattern (3,4,5-trimethoxy) on the benzene
ring is the defining structural feature. Shulgin systematically varied
this pattern to discover how each modification affected the psychedelic
experience. His work revealed that the 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted
pattern consistently produced the most potent and interesting compounds.

## TMA (3,4,5-Trimethoxyamphetamine)

**Dosage:** 100-250 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 12-16 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** ++ to +++

TMA is the alpha-methylated analog of mescaline, adding a single carbon
to the amine side chain. This modification follows the same pattern
seen in the DOx family: extended duration and somewhat increased potency.
Shulgin described TMA as a longer, more stimulant-heavy version of
mescaline with similar visual character but less warmth and more
cognitive focus. The increased duration can make TMA more challenging.

## TMA-2 (2,4,5-Trimethoxyamphetamine)

**Dosage:** 20-40 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 8-12 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** +++

TMA-2 represents one of Shulgin's most important discoveries. By
rearranging methoxy groups from 3,4,5 to 2,4,5, he created a compound
approximately 10 times more potent than mescaline. This was
revolutionary: the 2,4,5-substitution pattern proved key to high
potency. TMA-2 produces strong visuals and powerful cognitive effects at
doses far below mescaline's range, with a more intense, focused quality.

## TMA-6 (2,4,6-Trimethoxyamphetamine)

**Dosage:** 20-50 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 8-12 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** ++

TMA-6 moves the methoxy groups to the 2,4,6 positions, producing a
compound with distinctly different character from TMA-2. Shulgin noted
less visual and more somatic effects, with a dreamy, contemplative
quality. Even within high-potency substitution patterns, the specific
arrangement profoundly affects qualitative nature.

## MMDA (3-Methoxy-4,5-Methylenedioxyamphetamine)

**Dosage:** 100-250 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 8-12 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** ++ to +++

MMDA combines structural elements of mescaline and MDA. The
methylenedioxy bridge at the 4,5-position creates a compound with both
psychedelic and mild empathogenic properties. Shulgin described MMDA as
a "gentle" compound with visual effects reminiscent of mescaline and
emotional warmth approaching but not equaling MDA. It occupies a unique
middle ground in the phenethylamine spectrum.

## MMDA-3a (2,3-Methylenedioxy-4-Methoxyamphetamine)

**Dosage:** 150-250 mg (oral)
**Duration:** 8-10 hours
**Shulgin Rating:** ++

MMDA-3a repositions the methylenedioxy group to the 2,3-position,
producing a compound with reduced potency compared to MMDA. Shulgin
described its effects as gentle and primarily sensory, with mild visual
enhancement and a relaxed physical state. This compound illustrates how
the position of the methylenedioxy bridge significantly modulates both
potency and experiential character.

## Structure-Activity Relationships

Shulgin's systematic exploration of mescaline analogs revealed several
key structure-activity principles that remain foundational to
psychedelic pharmacology:

1. **The 2,5-dimethoxy pattern** is essential for high potency.
  Compounds with 2,5-dimethoxy substitution are consistently 10-100x
  more potent than mescaline.

2. **The 4-position substituent** determines the character and duration
  of effects. Lipophilic groups (bromo, iodo, ethylthio) tend to
  increase both potency and duration.

3. **Alpha-methylation** consistently extends duration and often
  increases potency by reducing metabolic degradation through
  monoamine oxidase pathways.

4. **Ring size modifications** (methylenedioxy vs. dimethoxy) shift
  the experiential character between purely psychedelic and
  empathogenic-psychedelic blend.

## Relationship to Other Families

The mescaline analogs serve as the structural ancestors of the 2C
family ([[pihkal-2c-b-family]]) and the DOx compounds
([[pihkal-doa-family]]). Removing one methoxy group from the
trimethoxy pattern and adding a 4-position substituent yields the 2C
series. Adding an alpha-methyl group to a 2C compound yields the
corresponding DOx compound. Understanding this structural genealogy is
essential for grasping the logic of Shulgin's systematic exploration.
