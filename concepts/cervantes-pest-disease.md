## Common Pests

### Spider Mites
- The most common and destructive indoor pest
- Appear as tiny dots on leaf undersides
- Cause stippling (tiny yellow dots) on leaves
- Fine webbing between leaves and branches
- Reproduce rapidly in warm, dry conditions
- Fast-growing clones can outpace mite reproduction

### Fungus Gnats
- Small dark flies around soil surface
- Larvae feed on roots in moist soil
- More common in overwatered gardens
- Yellow [[bloomfield-amphibian-decline-fungal-disease-climate-interactions]]
- Thrives in cool, humid conditions (humidity above 80%)
- Attacks dense buds from inside out
- Brown, mushy spots on buds
- Prevent by maintaining proper humidity and air circulation
- Do not wash buds if botrytis is a threat

### Powdery Mildew
- White powdery coating on leaf surfaces
- Favors moderate temperatures and high humidity
- Spreads rapidly
- Can be treated with sulfur-based fungicides

### Damping Off
- Affects seedlings and young clones
- Caused by several soil-borne fungi
- Stem at soil line becomes water-soaked and collapses
- Prevent by using sterile medium, proper drainage, and not overwatering

## Pruning and Disease Risk

After pruning, open wounds invite diseases and pests. Wash hands and tools before and after pruning. Make cuts at a 45-degree angle to discourage moisture from sitting on wounds. Pruned plants can seal themselves, but problems can still arise when there is an appealing opening for pests.
