## Medicinally Important Mushroom Species Overview

At least 270 species of mushroom are known to have various therapeutic properties. This concept provides a cross-indexed overview of the major medicinally important Basidiomycete mushrooms, their historical usage, and their documented pharmacological activities.

## Historical Foundations

### Ancient Chinese Medicine
"An old Chinese proverb states that "medicine and food have a common origin.'"

### Key Texts
1. **Shen Nong"s Herbal** (Shen Nong Pen Ts'ao Jing, 100-200 AD): Earliest book on medicinal materials in China; recorded medicinal effects of [[ganoderma-lucidum]], [[poria-cocos]], [[tremella-fuciformis]]
2. **Pen Ts'ao Kang Mu** (Compendium of Materia Medica, 1575): Compiled by Li Shi-Zhen of the Ming Dynasty; documented more than 20 mushroom species plus [[cordyceps-sinensis]]

### Modern Validation
"As a result of large numbers of scientific studies on [[medicinal-mushrooms]] especially in Japan, China and Korea, over the past three decades, many of the traditional uses have been confirmed and new applications developed."

Medicinal mushrooms offer properties beyond their immunological and anti-cancer activities:
- **Antioxidants**
- **Anti-hypertensive**
- **Cholesterol-lowering**
- **Liver protection** (hepatoprotective)
- **Anti-fibrotic**
- **Anti-inflammatory**
- **Anti-diabetic**
- **Anti-viral** (including anti-HIV)
- **Anti-microbial**
- **Analgesic**
- **Anti-allergic**
- **Bronchitis prevention**
- **Kidney tonic**
- **Nerve tonic**
- **Sexual potentiator**

"Many polysaccharide-bound proteins produced by Basidiomycete fungi have been classified as anti-tumour chemicals by the US National Cancer Institute."

## Ganoderma lucidum (Reishi / Ling Zhi)

### Historical Primacy
"G. lucidum and related species have the longest historical usage for medicinal purposes, dating back at least four millennia." In Japan: **Reishi** or **Mannetake** (10,000 year mushroom). In China/Korea: **Ling Zhi** (Mushroom of Immortality).

### Cultural Significance
Most depicted mushroom in ancient Japanese, Korean and Chinese art. Extensively depicted in Chinese royal tapestries. Used as a talisman against evil.

### Bioactive Compounds
- **Steroids, lactones, alkaloids, polysaccharides, triterpenes**
- At least 100 different alcohol-soluble triterpenes identified (ganoderic, ganoderenic, lucidenic, ganolucidic acids)
- β-glucans and hetero-β-glucans
- Ling Zhi-8 protein

### Pharmacological Effects (Table 2 from Report)
- Analgesic
- Anti-allergic activity
- Bronchitis-preventative (regeneration of bronchial epithelium)
- Anti-inflammatory
- Antibacterial (against Staph, Strep, B. pneumoniae)
- Antioxidant (eliminating hydroxyl free radicals)
- Antitumour activity
- Antiviral (inducing interferon production)
- Lowers blood pressure
- Enhances bone marrow nucleated cell proliferation
- Cardiotonic (lowers serum cholesterol, enhances myocardial metabolism)
- Central depressant and peripheral anticholinergic
- Enhanced NK cell activity
- Expectorant and antitussive
- General immunopotentiation
- Anti-HIV activity (in vitro and in vivo)
- Improved adrenocortical function
- Increased IL-1 and IL-2 production

### Commercial Value
"Estimates place the annual value of G. lucidum products worldwide at more than US $1.6 billion."

### Non-Edible
Not edible due to bitter taste and indigestible structure (similar to varnished wood). Available worldwide in tablet and liquid products as hot water extracts.

## Cordyceps sinensis (Caterpillar Fungus)

### Unique Biology
Parasitic fungus growing in larvae of Lepidoptera. Gradually takes over the entire larval body; diseased larvae bury in soil and die; fungal stroma grows out of the pupa.

### Traditional Use
"Tochukaso" has been highly regarded in Chinese medicine for centuries. Not a true mushroom—the fruiting structure cannot be cultivated.

### Modern Cultivation
Pure mycelium can now be easily cultivated in fermentors. "Such methods use selected media under aseptic conditions, providing better quality and homogeneity through process control."

### Key Activities
- Oxygen-free-radical scavenging
- Anti-cancer polysaccharides isolated
- Hypoglycaemic activity
- Agent to treat fatigue and improve motor function

### Key Constituents
- Galactomannans (antitumour, immunostimulating)
- Cordycepin
- Sterols

## Schizophyllum commune

Small, whitish, stalkless fungus growing on dead trees worldwide. Produces **Schizophyllan** (β-glucan) with considerable anti-cancer activity in xenograft and clinical practice.

## Agaricus blazei (Himematsutake)

First discovered in USA (1940s); main commercial cultivation in Japan and Brazil. One of the most expensive medicinal mushrooms. Novel polysaccharide-protein complex highly active against various xenografts.

### Key Constituents
- β(1,3)-D-glucan, β(1,4)-D-glucan, β(1,6)-D-glucan
- Proteoglucans

## Lentinus edodes (Shiitake)

Indigenous to Japan, China, and temperate Asian countries. Found in the wild on fallen deciduous trees.

### Key Constituent
- Acidic polysaccharides (glucuronoxylomannan): Antitumour, immunostimulatory, antidiabetic, skin enhancing

## Quality and Safety Considerations

"A major concern with herbal medicine using Cordyceps collected from nature is quality and safety." This concern extends to all wild-harvested medicinal mushrooms—proper identification, contamination, and batch consistency are critical issues that commercial cultivation and fermentation address.
