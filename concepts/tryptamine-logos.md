# Tryptamine Logos

## Overview

The Tryptamine Logos is Terence McKenna's term for the translinguistic
intelligence and information field encountered in tryptamine-induced altered
states, particularly through DMT and psilocybin. Drawing on the ancient Greek
concept of the Logos — the divine reason, word, or principle that pervades and
orders the cosmos — McKenna proposed that [[mckenna-philo-judaeus-logos-hearing-to-beholding]]** (c. 20 BCE-50 CE): Described the Logos as passing
  "without any quantized moment of distinction" from things heard to things
  beheld — a description McKenna found remarkably apt for the DMT experience.
- **Christian theology**: The Logos as the Word of God, divine reason made
  flesh in Christ.
- **Stoicism**: The Logos as the animating principle pervading all of nature.

McKenna's innovation was to identify the tryptamine experience as a direct,
phenomenological encounter with this Logos principle — not as metaphor or
theological abstraction, but as lived experience.

### Cognitive Dissonance and Wonder

When a human mind encounters this translinguistic object, "a kind of cognitive
dissonance is set up in our hindbrain." The brain attempts to pour ordinary
language over the experience, and "it sheds it like water off a duck's back."
This repeated failure produces the characteristic state of wonder, astonishment,
and awe at the brink of terror that defines the tryptamine experience.

### Glossolalia

DMT can produce a form of glossolalia distinct from the classical speaking in
tongues studied in religious contexts. Unlike classical glossolaliasts who
often ask afterward "Did I do it? Did I speak in tongues?", the DMT-induced
phenomenon is described as "simply a brain state that allows the expression of
the assembly language that lies behind language."

### Object-Manifesting Language

McKenna proposed that in the tryptamine state, language may become
object-manifesting: "Perhaps a human language is possible in which the intent
of meaning is actually beheld in three-dimensional space." If this capacity
exists under DMT, it means that under some circumstances it is accessible to
human beings. With cultural investment over millennia, McKenna speculated,
such an ability could become "a cultural convenience in the same way that
mathematics or language has become a cultural convenience."
