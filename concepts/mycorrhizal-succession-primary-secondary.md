## Primary Succession on Glacial Forefronts

Studies on the Lyman Glacier forefront have provided detailed insights into mycorrhizal
succession during primary succession. Jumpponen et al. (1999, 2002) identified 68
ectomycorrhizal species belonging to 25 genera across three successional sites, with no
single ectomycorrhizal species occurring on all three sites. Ectomycorrhizal species
diversity increased to a maximum where tree canopies started to overlap, corresponding to
the transition from open to closed canopy conditions.

The existence of successions of ectomycorrhizal species during primary succession is
well-supported by these findings. The changes in mycorrhizal community composition likely
reflect both changes in host plant species composition and changes in soil conditions,
particularly the shift from bacterial-dominated to fungal-dominated microbial communities.

## The Role of Animal Vectors in Colonization

Trappe and Maser (1976) showed that spores of the arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus Glomus
macrocarpus and the hypogeous ectomycorrhizal fungus Hymenogaster were dispersed by small
mammals such as the Oregon vole and the chickaree. A proportion of the spores survived
passage through the animal gut and assisted in the colonization of bare ground by
providing mycorrhizal inoculum (Trappe, 1988).

The appearance of mycorrhizal spores in the feces of pika, voles, chipmunks, marmots,
mountain goat, and mule deer on the forefront of the Lyman Glacier forms an inoculum
source for colonizing newly developing soils. The deposition of spore-laden feces in
concave surfaces of coarse rocky particles that serve as safe sites for tree seed
germination creates favorable conditions for both plant establishment and mycorrhizal
colonization.

## Dark-Septate Mycorrhizae in Early Succession

Jumpponen et al. (1998) demonstrated that the dark-septate mycorrhizal fungus
Phialocephala fortinii significantly enhanced growth of lodgepole pine (Pinus contorta),
an early colonizer of the glacier forefront, but only in the presence of added nitrogen.
Total plant phosphorus was significantly enhanced in the presence of the mycorrhiza with
no added nitrogen, indicating that this fungus primarily assists with phosphorus
acquisition in nutrient-poor pioneer soils.

## Allen and Allen Succession Model

plant competition during seral succession. In this model, mycorrhizae play critical roles
in early succession by facilitating plant establishment on nutrient-poor substrates, and
