# Resources Classification and Yields

Understanding resources and yields is fundamental to [[permaculture-design]]. The
energies coming into a system (sun, wind, rain) are translated by living
components and technologies into useful reserves called resources. The surplus
over system needs is the yield.

## Defining Resources

Resources are the useful storages created when energies are caught and stored
in a landscape or building. Some resources must be used by the system for its
own purposes (stocks of fish must be maintained to produce more fish). An
ideal technology should at the very least fuel itself.

Yield is any useful resource surplus to the needs of the local system and
thus available for use, export, or trade. The way to obtain yield is to be
conservative in resource use, for energy, like money, is much more easily
saved than generated.

## Two Categories of Resources

1. **Common heritage resources:** Forests, soils, air, water, sunlight, and
   seeds are resources that we all regard as part of a common heritage.

2. **Developed resources:** Those fabricated, ordered, or otherwise developed
   by people through their work. What we create is always made from the
   common resource.

## Five Resource Categories by Use-Effect

A more useful classification looks at what happens when resources are used:

### 1. Resources That Increase by Modest Use

Green browse is an example: if deer do not browse shrubs, the latter may
become woody and unpalatable. A browsed biennial may tiller out and become
perennial. Information is another resource that increases with use. It
withers or is outdated if not used. Too little impoverishes a system, but
when freely used and exchanged, it flourishes and increases.

### 2. Resources Unaffected by Use

A view or a good climate is unaffected by use. The diversion of part of a
river to hydroelectric generation (water returned after use) is also
unaffected, as is a stone pile used as mulch, heat store, or water
run-off collector. A well-managed ecosystem is an example.

### 3. Resources That Disappear or Degrade if Not Used

An unharvested annual crop, grass that could be stored for winter,
irruptions of oceanic fish, swarms of bees, ripe fruit, and water run-off
during rains. These resources are lost if not captured or used in time.

### 4. Resources Reduced by Use

A fish or game stock unwisely used, clay deposits, mature forests, and coal
and oil. These require careful management to prevent exhaustion.

### 5. Resources That Pollute or Destroy Other Resources

Residual poisons in an ecosystem, radioactives, super-highways, large areas
of concrete, and sewers running pollutants to the sea. These are inherently
destructive and should be banned from use.

## Categories 1 to 3: The Sustainable Basis

Categories 1 to 3 are those most commonly produced in natural systems and
rural living situations, and are the only sustainable basis of society.
Categories 4 and 5 result from urban and industrial development. A sane
society manages categories 1 to 4 wisely, bans category 5, and regulates all
uses to produce sustainable yield.

## The Principle of Enough

Any system or organism can accept only that quantity of a resource which can
be used productively. Any resource input beyond that point throws the system
or organism into disorder. Oversupply of a resource is a form of chronic
pollution.

Both over- and undersupply have much the same effect, except that oversupply
has more grotesque results. Undersupply can be coped with by reduced growth
and wider spacing, but oversupply causes inflated growth, crowding, and
sociopathy. Ethical resource management balances out famine and obesity.

## Types of Yield

Yields can be classified as:

1. **Product yield:** The sum of primary and derived products available from,
   or surplus to, the system (both intrinsic and those created by design).

2. **Energy yield:** The sum of conserved, stored, and generated energy
   surplus to the system.

3. **Impalpable yields:** Those related to health, aesthetic pleasure,
   security, and spiritual fulfilment. These are harder to measure but
   critically important to the overall health of the system and its
   inhabitants.

## Policy of Resource Management

A responsible human society bans the use of resources which permanently
reduce yields of sustainable resources: pollutants, persistent poisons,
radioactives, large areas of concrete and highways, sewers from city to sea.
Failure to do this will cause the society itself to fail. Today's luxuries
are tomorrow's disasters.
