# Anandamide (AEA)

## Discovery

Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamine, AEA) was discovered in the 1990s
and represents a major scientific breakthrough in understanding the
endocannabinoid system. Named from the Sanskrit word "ananda" meaning
"bliss [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2]]).

## Chemical Nature

Anandamide is a lipid-based signaling molecule — an endogenous
cannabinoid produced naturally within the human body. Like all
cannabinoids, it functions as a "key" molecule that binds to receptor
"locks" to initiate physiological signaling cascades. (see [[blesching-endocannabinoid-autonomic-nervous-system-adrenal-glands]] engagement**: Affects both parasympathetic
  (rest and digest) and sympathetic (fight or flight) divisions

## Physiological Effects

Once anandamide binds to its receptors, numerous changes occur
simultaneously:

- **Pleasure enhancement**: Activates reward pathways in the brain
- **Pain modulation**: Contributes to natural pain management
- **Mood regulation**: Influences emotional states and well-being
- **Cancer cell destruction**: Has demonstrated oncolytic properties
  against numerous cancer types
- **Cough suppression**: Soothes cough reflexes through central pathways
- **Schizophrenia mitigation**: May be involved in mitigating episodes of
  acute schizophrenia
- **Neuroprotection**: Supports brain cell health and survival

## Synthesis and Degradation

Anandamide is synthesized on-demand in neuron cell membranes from
N-arachidonoyl phosphatidylethanolamine. Unlike classical
neurotransmitters stored in vesicles, anandamide is produced and released
as needed, acting as a local mediator rather than a circulating hormone.

The enzyme **fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH)** is primarily responsible
for breaking down anandamide in the body. This degradation process limits
the duration and intensity of anandamide signaling.
