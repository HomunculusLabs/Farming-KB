# Cohoba Snuff

Cohoba is the Taíno name for a psychoactive snuff prepared from the
seeds of [[anadenanthera-colubrina]], provides the seeds for the South
American snuffs known as cebil, villca, and huilca. See
[[yopo-snuff]] for the [[chiric-caspi-brunfelsia-south-american-hallucinogen]] counterpart of cohoba.

## Preparation

The [[kava-traditional-preparation-ceremonial-context]] of cohoba snuff involves a careful
multi-step process that reflects an empirical understanding of the
chemistry involved:

1. Harvesting the ripe seed pods from the Anadenanthera tree
2. Toasting or roasting the seeds over a fire to develop the psychoac
  tive properties
3. Grinding the toasted seeds to a fine powder using a mortar and pes
  tle
4. Mixing the powder with calcined lime or shell ash (a source of cal
  cium hydroxide)
5. The resulting snuff is stored in ornate containers, often carved f
  rom wood, bone, or ceramic

The addition of lime serves a critical chemical purpose: it converts
the free-base tryptamine alkaloids into a form more readily absorbed
through the nasal mucosa. This technique parallels the preparation of
other South American snuffs like [[yopo-snuff]] for the continuing South American tradition.

## Archaeological Evidence

Archaeological excavations throughout the Caribbean have uncovered
elaborate snuff paraphernalia, including carved snuff tubes, snuff
tablets, and ornamental containers made from wood, bone, ceramic, and
shell. These artifacts provide evidence of the antiquity and cultural
importance of the cohoba practice. The iconography of the Taino
people frequently depicts the cohoba ritual, with figures shown
inhaling through Y-shaped tubes while in trance states.
