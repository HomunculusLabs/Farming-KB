## The Kykeon Question

Central to the Mysteries was the ritual consumption of *kykeon*, a barley-based
drink described in the Homeric Hymn. The composition of kykeon has been the
subject of intense scholarly debate, as it may have contained psychoactive
ingredients that produced the transformative visionary experience reported by
initiates.

In 1978, chemist Albert Hofmann (discoverer of LSD) and classical scholar Carl
Ruck proposed that kykeon was prepared from ergotized barley contaminated with
*[[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] amide, also known as LSA) while
leaving behind the toxic ergopeptine alkaloids responsible for ergotism
(St. Anthony's Fire).

## Evidence and Arguments

### Supporting Evidence

- **Consistent descriptions**: Ancient accounts of the Eleusinian experience
  consistently describe it as a vision of profound beauty and emotional
  intensity — language more suggestive of a genuine psychoactive experience
  than mere suggestion or theatrical effect.
- **Secrecy**: The extraordinary secrecy maintained for two millennia suggests
  the experience was repeatable and reliably induced, consistent with a
  pharmacological mechanism rather than purely symbolic ritual.
- **Barley as substrate**: Barley was a primary ingredient of kykeon and is a
  known host for *Claviceps purpurea*.
- **Hofmann's expertise**: Hofmann's intimate knowledge of ergot chemistry
  lent significant credibility to the feasibility argument.
