# Cannabis Harvest Techniques

Harvesting cannabis at the right time and processing it correctly is critical to achieving the best possible potency, flavor, and smoothness. Improper harvesting, drying, or curing can degrade months of careful cultivation. This page covers trichome assessment, flushing, trimming methods, drying, and curing.

## Determining Harvest Readiness

The single most reliable indicator of harvest readiness is trichome appearance. While flowering time estimates from the breeder provide a general window, environmental conditions and phenotype variation mean you should always verify visually.

**Trichome stages** (use a jeweler's loupe or USB microscope at 60-100x magnification):

| Stage | Appearance | Effect Profile | When to Harvest |
|-------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Clear | Translucent, glass-like | Low potency, more racy/uplifting | Too early — wait |
| Cloudy/Milky | Opaque white, swollen heads | Peak THC, balanced head/body | Peak potency harvest |
| Amber | Golden-brown, some degradation | More sedating, body-heavy, couch-lock | Late harvest for relaxation |
| Mixed cloudy/amber | Combination | Balanced potency with body notes | Most growers' preferred window |

Most growers harvest when 50-70% of trichomes have turned cloudy with 10-30% amber. Harvesting earlier produces a more cerebral effect; harvesting later produces more sedative qualities.

**Secondary indicators**:

- Pistil color: 70-90% of pistils have darkened and curled inward
- Fan leaves: Some yellowing and dropping (natural senescence)
- Bud density: Buds feel firm and heavy, resin production is strong
- Aroma: Pungent, complex scent at peak expression

## Flushing

Flushing is the practice of ceasing nutrient feeding and watering with plain water (or water with a flushing agent) for the final 1-2 weeks before harvest. The goal is to allow the plant to use up residual nutrients stored in its tissues.

**Arguments for flushing**:
- Improves smoothness of the final smoke or vapor
- Reduces harsh, chemical taste
- Allows terpenes to fully develop without nutrient interference

**Arguments against flushing**:
- Some studies suggest plants don't store excess nutrients in flower tissue the way flushing advocates claim
- Premature nutrient cessation may reduce yield and terpene production
- Living soil growers typically never flush — the soil biology regulates nutrient availability

**Living soil approach**: If growing in a properly built living soil system, flushing is generally unnecessary. The biological system naturally meters nutrient delivery. Simply reduce watering slightly in the final week.

## Harvesting

**Cut the whole plant or individual branches**. For small grows, cutting the entire plant at the base is simplest. For larger operations, harvest branch by branch.

- Use clean, sharp scissors or a pruning saw for larger branches.
- Hang immediately or process within hours.
- Work in a clean, temperature-controlled environment (65-75°F, 45-55% RH).

## Wet Trimming vs Dry Trimming

### Wet Trimming
Trim leaves immediately after cutting while the plant is still fresh and turgid.

**Pros**: Easier to handle — leaves are stiff and easy to cut. Faster overall process. Less mold risk during drying since moist leaves are removed. Better for humid climates.

**Cons**: Trichomes are more fragile and may be knocked off during handling. Can slow initial drying since exposed buds dry faster than the interior.

### Dry Trimming
Hang the whole plant or branches to dry for 5-10 days, then trim after they've dried.

**Pros**: Trichomes are more stable and hardened, reducing loss during handling. Slower drying can preserve more terpenes. Better for dry climates.

**Cons**: Leaves curl around buds as they dry, making trimming more tedious. Higher mold risk if humidity isn't controlled. Requires more hanging space.

## Drying

Proper drying is one of the most important steps. Drying too fast destroys terpenes and creates harsh, brittle buds. Drying too slow invites mold.

**Ideal drying conditions**:
- Temperature: 60-70°F (15-21°C)
- Relative humidity: 45-55%
- Airflow: Gentle, indirect — a small oscillating fan in the room (not pointed directly at plants)
- Darkness or very low light (light degrades cannabinoids and terpenes)
- Duration: 7-14 days

**Methods**:

- **Hang drying**: Hang branches from string, hangers, or a drying rack. Space branches so they don't touch. This is the most common method.
- **Drying racks / mesh nets**: Lay trimmed buds on mesh screens. Good for smaller amounts. Turn buds daily.
- **Cardboard box drying**: Place trimmed buds in a cardboard box lined with paper. Simple and effective for small harvests.

**When is drying complete?**: Small stems snap cleanly when bent rather than folding. Buds feel dry on the outside but still slightly springy — not brittle or crumbling. The interior should still have a small amount of moisture.

## Curing

Curing is the slow, controlled degradation of remaining chlorophyll and sugars in the bud, which dramatically improves flavor, smoothness, and potency. This is done in airtight containers (glass Mason jars are standard).

**Curing process**:

1. **Jarring**: Place dried buds loosely in glass jars — fill about 3/4 full. Don't pack tightly.
2. **Burping**: Open jars once or twice daily for 5-15 minutes to release excess humidity and replenish oxygen. This prevents anaerobic conditions that cause mold and off-flavors.
3. **Monitoring**: Use a hygrometer inside one jar to track internal humidity. Target 58-62% RH (using the 62% standard for ideal curing).
4. **Duration**: Minimum 2 weeks for noticeable improvement. 4-8 weeks for significant curing. 6+ months for peak smoothness and flavor complexity.

**If buds feel too moist after jarring** (above 65% RH): Leave jar lids off for a few hours, then recheck. Repeat as needed.

**If buds feel too dry** (below 55% RH): Add a small humidity pack (Boveda 62%) to the jar.

**Long-term storage**: After curing, store in a cool, dark place. Maintain 55-62% RH with humidity packs. Vacuum sealing with humidity control works well for extended storage. Avoid freezing — trichomes become brittle and can break off.
