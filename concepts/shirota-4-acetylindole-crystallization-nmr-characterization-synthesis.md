# Shirota 4-Acetylindole: Crystallization and NMR Characterization

## Overview

In Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda's concise large-scale synthesis of
[[4-hydroxyindole]]. This crystallization step
is critical to the overall synthesis because it establishes a
crystalline intermediate that can be produced on multigram scale with
high purity, avoiding the need for chromatographic purification.

## Acetylation Reaction

The synthesis begins with commercially available 4-hydroxyindole
(compound 3). Acetylation is achieved by treating a solution of
4-hydroxyindole in anhydrous dichloromethane with pyridine and acetic
anhydride in an ice bath. After stirring for 2 hours at room
temperature, the reaction mixture is worked up with water, evaporated,
and the resulting concentrate is crystallized from ethyl acetate.

The choice of acetylation over benzyl ether protection is significant.
The Shirota paper notes that while benzyl ether protection was also
utilized by previous researchers including Nichols and Frescas, a
separate step was needed for its deprotection. The acetyl group, by
contrast, can be removed in situ during the subsequent reduction step
with lithium aluminum hydride, streamlining the overall synthesis.

## Crystallization and Isolation

The crystallization procedure yields 4-acetylindole as ivory white
crystals in constant yield (quantitative based on the starting 4-
hydroxyindole input of 210 mmol, yielding 34 g of product). The
crystalline nature of this intermediate is a key practical advantage,
as it allows easy isolation by filtration and washing, without
requiring column chromatography.

After formation, the crystals are collected by filtration and washed
successively with water and ethyl acetate. This simple workup procedure
is scalable and reproducible, which is essential for the authors' goal
of achieving gram-scale production of [[japan-narcotics-control-law-psilocybin-fungi-2002]] had recently
regulated fungi containing psilocin and psilocybin (since June 6, 2002),
creating demand for pure reference standards. The crystallization
approach directly addresses this need by providing a reliable, scalable
synthetic route to these compounds without chromatographic purification.

## 1H NMR Characterization

The 1H NMR spectrum of 4-acetylindole in CDCl3 at 400 MHz shows
several diagnostic signals that confirm the acetylation and the
indole ring structure:

- A broad singlet at delta 8.27 (1H) corresponding to H-1, the
  indole NH proton. The broadness and downfield shift are
  characteristic of the hydrogen-bonded NH of the indole ring.

- A doublet at delta 7.22 (1H, J = 8.0 Hz) for H-7, showing the
  expected ortho coupling to H-6 on the benzene ring.

- A triplet at delta 7.15 (1H, J = 8.0 Hz) for H-6, coupled to
  both H-5 and H-7 with similar coupling constants.

- A triplet at delta 7.11 (1H, J = 2.8 Hz) for H-2, the proton
  on the pyrrole ring adjacent to the nitrogen.

- A doublet of doublets at delta 6.85 (1H, J = 0.5, 8.0 Hz) for
  H-5, showing both meta and ortho coupling.

- A multiplet at delta 6.41 (1H) for H-3, the proton on the
  pyrrole ring.

- A sharp singlet at delta 2.39 (3H) for the acetyl methyl group,
  confirming successful acetylation of the phenolic hydroxyl.
