# Clavicipitaceous Grass Endophytes — Epichloe, Neotyphodium, and Balansia

Two genera of Balansieae (Clavicipitaceae, Ascomycetes) contain endophytes: Epichloe and Balansia. These fungi form some of the most intimate and ecologically significant symbioses known between fungi and plants. The relationship ranges from mutualistic to pathogenic depending on environmental conditions and the specific fungus-host combination.

- **Lolines:** Pyrrolizidine alkaloids that deter insect herbivores

- **Peramine:** A pyrrolopyrazine alkaloid with potent insect-feeding deterrent activity

- **Loitrems:** Indole diterpene alkaloids that cause tremors in grazing livestock

## Ecological Significance

Grass endophytes provide their hosts with significant benefits:

- Protection against herbivory (deterrent alkaloids)

- Protection against pathogens

- Increased [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]]

- Enhanced competitive ability

- Increased fitness

The relationship is so intimate that host and fungus function essentially as a single organism. Sporulation on the host is completely suppressed, and transmission is vertical through seeds.

## Agricultural Implications

While endophyte-infected grasses show superior vigor and persistence in pastures, the same alkaloids that deter insect herbivores can cause serious toxicosis in livestock. Fescue toxicosis and ryegrass staggers are economically significant conditions affecting cattle, sheep, and horses grazing on endophyte-infected forage. This has led to the development of novel endophyte strains that retain insect-deterrent properties while producing reduced levels of mammal-toxic alkaloids.
