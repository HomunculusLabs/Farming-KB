## Nutritional Functions

### Phosphorus Supply

Phosphorus is an essential macronutrient for fungal growth. It is a
component of:
- **ATP and other nucleotide triphosphates**: The primary energy currency
  of cellular metabolism
- **Nucleic acids** (DNA and RNA): Required for cell division and protein
  synthesis
- **Phospholipids**: Major components of cell membranes
- **Phosphorylated sugars**: Intermediates in carbohydrate metabolism and
  cell wall biosynthesis

Grain substrates contain moderate amounts of phosphorus, but supplementation
with KH2PO4 ensures that phosphorus is not growth-limiting, particularly
during the rapid colonization phase when demand is highest.

### Potassium Supply

Potassium is the most abundant cation in fungal cells and is involved in:
- **Osmoregulation**: Potassium ions are the primary inorganic osmolyte
  used by fungi to maintain [[fungal-environmental-sensing-signal-transduction-read]]

Horse dung and rice grain substrate provides some potassium, but additional
KH2PO4 supplementation ensures adequate supply for maximum growth rates.

## Role in the Gartz et al. (1990) Study

In the brassinosteroid growth promotion study, KH2PO4 was included in the
dung-grain substrate at 5 g/L for both control and brassinosteroid-treated
cultures. Its presence as a consistent component across all treatments means
it did not contribute to the differential growth effects observed between
groups. Instead, it served as a standardized nutritional and buffering
foundation that allowed the brassinosteroid effect to be measured against
