# Biological Element Transport and [[soil-ph]] is a master variable governing nutrient availability, microbial activity, and plant health. It is measured on a logarithmic scale where each whole point represents a tenfold change in hydrogen ion concentration.

### The pH Scale

- pH 8 is **10,000× less acidic** than pH 4 (four points × 10× each)
- pH 7 is neutral; below 7 is acidic, above 7 is alkaline
- Most crops prefer pH 6.0–7.0, though many useful species tolerate wider ranges

### Rock Types and pH

Parent rock chemistry fundamentally determines soil pH potential:

| Category       | Silica (SiO₂) Content | Character         |
|----------------|-----------------------|-------------------|
| Acid rocks     | > 64%                 | Low pH, leached   |
| Intermediate   | 50–64%                | Moderate pH       |
| Basic rocks    | 40–50%                | Higher pH, fertile|
| Ultrabasic     | < 40%                 | High pH, mineral-rich|

### Acid and Alkaline Waters and Soils

- **"Hard" (alkaline) water** contains Ca²⁺ or Mg²⁺ ions; soaps lather poorly
- **"Soft" (acid) water** contains H⁺ ions; soaps lather easily
- Soaps (sodium/potassium + fats) are alkaline; detergents (phosphates/sulphur) handle hard water
- Arabic for deserts: *al Khali* ("the salt")—reflecting the alkaline-salt character of arid landscapes

### Sources of Acidity and Alkalinity

Acids and alkalis arise from the solution of oxides, hydroxides, sulphates, and carbonates:

- **Metals** (Na, K, Mg, Ca; plus Fe, Zn, Al, Cu) tend to release positive ions, raising pH
- **Non-metals** (Si, S, P, B, F, Cl) tend to release negative ions, lowering pH
- Carbon in organic soils adds acidity as it decomposes

### pH Raising Materials

| Material     | Composition                | Application Notes              |
|--------------|---------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Chalk        | Calcium carbonate          | General purpose, slow-release  |
| Limestone    | Calcium carbonate          | Finer grades act faster        |
| [[dolomite]]     | Calcium magnesium carbonate| Balances Ca and Mg simultaneously |

### Nutrient Availability (kg/ha)

| Nutrient   | Removed by Crop | Available in Soil | Insoluble Reserve    |
|------------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Nitrogen   | 100             | 20–200            | 1,000–10,000         |
| Potassium  | 100             | 40–200            | 5,000–50,000         |
| Phosphorus | 20              | 20–100            | 1,000–10,000         |
| Sulphur    | 30              | 50–100            | 100–10,000           |
| Calcium    | 40              | 100–5,000         | 10,000–100,000       |
| Magnesium  | 20              | 100–1,000         | 2,000–100,000        |
| Trace elements | Minute amounts | Variable        | Variable             |

### Ideal N:P:K Ratios

- **Sandy loams:** 5:8:4 with 5 parts lime
- **Clays:** 3:6:6

### Fertiliser Application

Foliar sprays of manure teas combined with seaweed concentrate are rapidly absorbed (24–56 hours in humid weather), making them efficient for addressing acute deficiencies without relying on soil chemistry corrections alone.

### Soil Amelioration Guidelines

| Soil Type | Humid Conditions | Desert Conditions |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Sands** | Add organic matter, clay, and micronutrients; watch for rapid leaching | Add organic matter and clay; gypsum for structure; mulch heavily |
| **Loams** | Maintain with compost and green manures; best baseline fertility | Add organic matter; monitor pH drift toward alkalinity |
| **Clays** | Add gypsum for structure; organic matter to improve aeration; avoid compaction | Add organic matter and sand; manage salt accumulation; improve drainage |
| **Gravels** | Concentrate organic matter in planting pockets; deep-rooted pioneer species | Build soil in swales and planting pits; shade to reduce evaporation |

### Key Design Principles

1. **Use biology, not bags** — foster organisms that concentrate and cycle elements rather than importing fertilisers
2. **Tree canopies are nutrient pumps** — integrate trees to transpire, deposit, and cycle elements to the soil surface
3. **pH is manageable** — use appropriate amendments (lime, gypsum, dolomite) matched to parent material and soil type
4. **Animals are mineral vectors** — strategic animal integration moves concentrated nutrients across the landscape
