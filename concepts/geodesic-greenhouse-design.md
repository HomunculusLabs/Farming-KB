# Geodesic Greenhouse Design

A geodesic dome greenhouse is a spherical structure made from a network of interlocking triangles. Popularized by [[buckminster-fuller]] in the mid-20th century, the geodesic dome is one of the most efficient structural forms known. As a greenhouse, it offers superior strength, light distribution, energy efficiency, and interior growing space compared to conventional rectangular greenhouses.

## Advantages of the Dome Shape

### Structural Strength

The triangulated framework distributes loads across the entire structure. Geodesic domes are:

- **The strongest structural form per unit of weight:** The triangle is the only inherently rigid polygon. A network of triangles creates a structure that gets stronger as it gets larger.
- **Wind resistant:** The aerodynamic shape allows wind to flow around it with minimal resistance. Domes have survived hurricane-force winds that destroy conventional buildings.
- **Snow load capacity:** The curved shape sheds snow naturally. Snow loads are distributed across the entire structure rather than concentrating on flat roof planes.
- **Earthquake resistant:** The lightweight, triangulated frame flexes without collapsing under seismic loads.

### Energy Efficiency

- **30-40% less surface area** than a rectangular building of the same floor area, meaning less heat loss through the envelope
- **Improved air circulation:** No corners where hot or cold air stagnates. Warm air rises to the peak and circulates evenly.
- **Superior light capture:** The curved surface receives sunlight from a wider range of angles throughout the day. Interior light levels are more even — no dark corners.
- **Passive solar advantage:** A south-facing portion of the dome absorbs and stores heat. The thermal mass of the soil and any water storage moderates temperature swings.

### Space Efficiency

- **No interior support columns:** The dome is self-supporting. Every square foot of floor space is usable.
- **More volume per footprint:** A dome encloses roughly 30% more volume than a rectangular building with the same floor area.
- **Versatile layout:** Circular space accommodates raised beds, keyhole gardens, and central features naturally (see [[keyhole-garden-design]] and [[mandala-garden-design]])

## Dome Geometry

### Frequency (V)

The "frequency" of a geodesic dome determines how many times the edges of the base icosahedron (20-sided polyhedron) are subdivided. Higher frequency = more triangles = more spherical, but more complex to build.

- **1V (frequency 1):** 25 triangles total (or 15 if a half-dome). Simple, large triangular panels. Faceted look. Good for first-time builders.
- **2V:** 65 triangles (40 for a half-dome). More spherical, moderate complexity.
- **3V:** 130 triangles (80 for a half-dome). Very spherical appearance. Most common choice for dome greenhouses.
- **4V:** 250 triangles (150 for a half-dome). Nearly perfect sphere. Very complex, many unique struts.
- **6V:** 625 triangles (375 for a half-dome). Extremely smooth sphere. Professional-level construction.

For greenhouse applications, **3V, 5/8 sphere** is the most popular choice — it provides a good balance of sphericity, usable headroom, and buildable complexity.

### Strut Lengths

In a 3V dome, there are three different strut lengths (A, B, C), each appearing in a specific quantity. The exact lengths depend on the desired dome diameter. Strut length calculators are available online — enter your desired diameter and the calculator outputs exact lengths for each strut type.

- **Strut A (longest):** Forms the hexagonal groups
- **Strut B (medium):** Connects hexagons to pentagons
- **Strut C (shortest):** Forms the pentagonal groups (there are exactly 6 pentagons in any geodesic dome — this is a mathematical certainty)

## Frame Materials

### Wood

- **Species:** Cedar, redwood, cypress, or treated lumber for outdoor durability
- **Dimensions:** 2x2 or 2x4 lumber ripped to the calculated strut widths (1.5-2 inches wide, 2-3.5 inches deep)
- **Joinery:** Steel hub connectors (bolts through pre-drilled holes) are the most common method. Alternatively, use galvanized mending plates or custom-cut plywood hubs.
- **Treatment:** Paint or seal all wood surfaces before assembly. Exterior primer and paint provide the best protection.
- **Pros:** Natural material, good insulation value, easy to work with, renewable
- **Cons:** Heavy, requires maintenance, can rot if not protected

### Steel

- **Type:** Galvanized steel conduit (EMT) or square steel tubing
- **Dimensions:** 3/4 to 1 inch EMT for small domes, 1 to 1.5 inch for larger domes
- **Joinery:** Steel hubs with bolts, or flatten the tube ends and bolt directly together
- **Pros:** Extremely strong, rot-proof, long-lasting, thinner struts possible
- **Cons:** Requires metalworking tools, heavier than PVC, thermal bridging (heat conducts through steel)

### PVC Pipe

- **Type:** Schedule 40 or 80 PVC
- **Dimensions:** 1 to 2 inch diameter
- **Joinery:** PVC hubs with screws, or heat-bend and bolt
- **Pros:** Lightweight, cheap, easy to cut and assemble, no rust or rot
- **Cons:** Not as strong as wood or steel, UV degradation (paint or cover exposed PVC), flexible (requires cross-bracing or internal support)

## Covering Materials

### Polycarbonate Panels

- **Type:** Twin-wall or triple-wall polycarbonate (4-16 mm thickness)
- **Pros:** Good insulation (R-1.5 to R-3), shatterproof, lightweight, UV-stabilized, 10+ year lifespan, diffuses light well
- **Cons:** More expensive than poly film, panels must be cut to fit each triangle (labor-intensive), can yellow over time
- **Best for:** Permanent, year-round greenhouses in cold climates

### Polyethylene Film

- **Type:** 6-mil UV-stabilized greenhouse film (single or double layer)
- **Pros:** Cheapest covering option, easy to install, excellent light transmission, replaceable
- **Cons:** Short lifespan (4-6 years), poor insulation (single layer), susceptible to wind damage
- **Double layer:** Two layers with a small inflation fan between them provides R-2+ insulation and much better wind resistance
- **Best for:** Budget-conscious builds, mild climates, temporary structures

### Solawrap (Bubble Greenhouse Film)

- A specialized product combining UV-stabilized poly with an air bubble layer
- **Pros:** R-3+ insulation, flexible, easy to apply, shatterproof, diffuses light
- **Cons:** Expensive, relatively new product with less track record
- **Best for:** Cold climates where insulation is critical

## Foundation

A proper foundation is essential for the dome's structural integrity:

- **Wooden base ring:** A pressure-treated 2x6 or 2x8 ring bolted to the bottom tier of the dome, set on gravel or a concrete pad
- **Concrete ring footing:** A continuous concrete footing poured around the base perimeter, with anchor bolts for the dome frame
- **Earth bags:** Fill bags with gravel or earth and stack in a ring — provides both foundation and thermal mass
- **Insulated slab:** For year-round growing, pour an insulated concrete slab with [[radiant-floor-heating]]

## Ventilation

Dome greenhouses can overheat rapidly due to the efficient solar capture. Ventilation design is critical:

- **Top vent (cupola):** An opening at the peak allows hot air to escape. The chimney effect draws cool air in through lower vents as hot air exits the top. This is the most important ventilation feature.
- **Lower intake vents:** Openings near the base allow cool air to enter. Use louvered or screened vents.
- **Cross-ventilation:** Open vents on opposite sides for airflow across the growing area.
- **Automatic vent openers:** Wax-cylinder openers that automatically open vents at a set temperature (65-75°F).
- **Fan ventilation:** In larger domes, an exhaust fan with a thermostat provides reliable temperature control.

## Passive Solar Features

Enhance the dome's energy efficiency with [[passive-solar-design]]:

- **Water storage:** 55-gallon drums painted black and filled with water absorb heat during the day and release it at night. Place along the north wall.
- **Thermal mass floor:** A stone, brick, or concrete floor absorbs and stores solar heat.
- **Reflective surfaces:** White or reflective surfaces on the north wall bounce light deeper into the dome.
- **Shade cloth:** 30-50% shade cloth over the dome in summer prevents overheating.
- **Underground air tubes:** Bury perforated pipes around the outside of the dome; air drawn through them is cooled by the earth before entering the growing space.

## Sizing

- **Small (10-15 foot diameter):** 80-180 sq ft growing area. Good for a family garden, seed starting, and season extension.
- **Medium (18-25 foot diameter):** 250-490 sq ft. Suitable for serious home food production.
- **Large (30-50 foot diameter):** 700-1,960 sq ft. Community-scale or small commercial production.
- **Height:** A 3V 5/8 sphere dome has a height of approximately 0.85 x diameter. A 20-foot dome is about 17 feet tall.
