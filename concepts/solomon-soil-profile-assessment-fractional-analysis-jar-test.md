## Overview

Solomon insists that every gardener should assess the soil resource beneath
their feet before committing to a garden site. The assessment involves two
steps: digging a soil profile hole to examine the layers below the surface,
and performing a simple "soil fractional analysis" test ([[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]]
- **Below topsoil:** Subsoil — lighter in color, less [[pf-tek-canning-jar-lid-configuration]] with tight-fitting lid
- Ruler or measuring tape
- Felt pen or adhesive tape for marking
- Teaspoon of ordinary dishwashing detergent (low-suds type preferred)
- Clock or timer

### Procedure
1. Remove any roots or debris from your soil sample
2. Break up the soil as finely as possible
3. Fill the jar with approximately 1 pint (500 ml) of soil
4. Mark the top of the soil level on the jar with a felt pen or tape
5. Fill the jar with water to within ~1 inch (2.5 cm) of the top
6. Add 1 teaspoon (5 ml) of dishwashing detergent
7. Seal the jar and shake vigorously for 5-10 minutes
8. The detergent breaks apart soil aggregates (clumps) so individual
   particles separate

### Reading the Results
- **Immediately after shaking (30 seconds):** All particles are suspended
- **After 2 minutes:** Mark the level — sand has settled. Sand particles are
  the largest and settle first.
- **After exactly 2 hours:** Mark the level again — silt has settled. Silt
  particles are finer than sand but much larger than clay.
- **After 12-24+ hours:** Clay settles. Clay particles are so tiny they
  require hours or even weeks to settle completely.

### Calculating Percentages
The original soil mark = 100% of your sample. Measure the depth of each
settled layer and calculate percentages:

```
% sand = (sand layer depth / total soil depth) × 100
% silt = (silt layer depth / total soil depth) × 100
% clay = (clay layer depth / total soil depth) × 100
```

Millimeters make the math easier than inches.
