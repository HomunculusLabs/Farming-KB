## Clathrus: The Spherical Cage

The genus Clathrus produces fruiting bodies that unfold into spherical cages.
The best-known species, Clathrus ruber, forms a vivid red-orange lattice of
interlocking arms that creates a cage-like sphere when fully expanded. The
spore mass is spread on the inner surface of the bars, positioned so that
insects crawling on the structure must pass through or across the gleba.
Inside the unexpanded egg, the receptacle is packaged in prefabricated form,
compressed and folded to fit within the protective outer skin. When the egg

## Laternea: The Vaulted Lantern

Laternea triscapa represents yet another variation on the cage fungus theme.
This genus elaborates long arms similar to Anthurus but fuses them at their
tips, creating a vaulted chamber with a reeking lantern of spore slime
suspended inside. This enclosed structure may offer advantages in certain
environments — protecting the spore mass from rain or desiccation while still
allowing insect access through the opening at the base. The vaulted form also
creates a microclimate around the gleba that may help maintain the volatile
