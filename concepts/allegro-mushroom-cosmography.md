# Mushroom Cosmography — The Universe as Fungus

Allegro reveals that ancient Near Eastern cosmography — the conceptual mapping of the universe — was modeled on the mushroom's anatomy. From individual sacred sites to the geography of the entire Mediterranean, the mushroom's form (volva, stem, canopy) provided the template for understanding the world.

## The Mushroom Model of the Cosmos

To the mushroom cosmographer, the universe was a gigantic fungus. At the base was the cup-shaped volva containing the waters of creation. The central pillar, variously identified with sacred mountains, supported the heavenly canopy. In sexual terms, the phallic stem stood in the vulva cup, raising the "burden" (canopy) to heaven. The whole formed a comprehensive model of the creative process.

## Canaan: The Nuptial Couch of Heaven

The land mass at the eastern end of the Mediterranean was understood as the earth's vaginal entrance. The mushroom, imagined horizontally lying on its side, has its canopy forming the curving sweep of the Palestinian coastline — the "crutch" of the earth, with Asia Minor as the northern "leg" and Egypt/North Africa as the southern "leg." For the Mesopotamian myth-maker, the sun plunged every evening into this "vulva" of the west.

The biblical name Canaan (Hebrew Kena'an) derives from Sumerian "nuptial couch of heaven." The offshore island of Cyprus was reckoned as a "glans" poised for entry into the Canaanite womb — its name (Greek Kupros) deriving from Sumerian "head of the erect penis."

## Jerusalem: The Uterus of the World

In any population centre practicing fertility religion, the palace-temple was the seat of the god's creative activity — the "uterus" to be fertilized by his presence. The topography and nomenclature of Jerusalem/Zion must be understood in these terms.

The Temple site was placed on "the threshing floor of Araunah" (II Sam 24:15ff). The Hebrew word for threshing floor means originally "seed-container." Araunah's name means "pounder of the womb" — the phallus grinding ingredients in the uterine "mortar." The centre of the earth, the "navel," was portrayed in the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, as at Delphi in classical times.

## Zion, the Valleys, and the Birth Canal

South of the Temple lay the Jebusite stronghold of Zion proper — the mons veneris of the city. On its south-western flank was the Pool of Siloam ("place of washing"). Below Zion, the valley of the "sons of Hinnom" — from Sumerian "penis-sheath" ("vagina") — combined with the Kidron valley, running down to the Dead Sea basin, the bowels of the earth.

This gorge was the original "valley of the shadow of death" (Psalm 23:4). The real meaning of the Sumerian original is "way of birth" — "birth canal." The Psalmist's concern that his shepherd-god should guide him by "rod and staff" through the valley takes on new meaning: as a baby needs the midwife's firm hand pressed through the vagina, so the mystic needed God's guidance through the experience of re-birth.

The Arabs call the valley the Wady of Fire. It debouches onto the Dead Sea, the nearest point on the world's surface to the generative furnaces of mother earth's womb. Later theologians identified this with hell-fire, having little to do with the original fertility concept.

## Jacob's Ladder: The Phallic Pillar

The dream of Jacob's ladder reaching from earth to heaven (Gen 28:10ff) is mushroom cosmography. Jacob's name means "pillar" (Sumerian "standing-stone"), the mushroom stem. His "red-skinned" brother Esau is the scarlet canopy. As the phallic stem running with the precious "semen" of the god, Jacob pours oil on the stone-pillar (Gen 28:18) — anointing the mushroom with its own seminal essence.

## Moses and Sinai: The Brazier

Mount Sinai means "brazier" (Sumerian ZA-NE), hence its description as "wrapped in smoke... like the smoke of a kiln" (Exod 19:18). At the fiery head of this "brazier" Moses meets Yahweh and finds his face glowing (Exod 34:29). The "tablets of stone" originate from the "bun"-shape of primitive clay tablets resembling the mushroom top. The word "tablet" comes from the Sumerian "twin-cone" — the two halves of the split mushroom volva. The Ten Commandments themselves, in their number and content, are word-plays on Sumerian fungus names.

## The Phoenix, the Pillar, and the Cross

The Phoenix, the Pillar, the Cross, the Ladder — all these cosmic symbols converge in the mushroom. The upright stem supporting the canopy is the "mighty man holding up the sky" (phoinix). The pillar reaching heaven is Jacob's ladder. The cross is the yoke of the crucified phallus. In every case, the mushroom's anatomy provides the physical template for humanity's most sacred symbols.
