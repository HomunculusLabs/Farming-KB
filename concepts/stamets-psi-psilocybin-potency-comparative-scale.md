# Psilocybin Potency Comparative Scale

## Overview

The potency of psilocybin-containing mushrooms varies enormously, both
between species and within individual collections of the same species.
Stamets developed the "psilometric scale" as a systematic way to
compare the relative strength of different Psilocybe species based
on their maximum reported concentrations of psychoactive alkaloids.
Understanding potency is essential for safe and informed use of these
mushrooms.

## Key Alkaloids

The primary psychoactive compounds found in psilocybin mushrooms are:

- **Psilocybin** - The phosphorylated (inactive) precursor that is
  converted to psilocin during digestion by dephosphorylation.
  Approximately 56% of psilocybin is converted to active psilocin in
  the body.
- **Psilocin** - The dephosphorylated, active form responsible for
  the psychoactive effects.
- **Baeocystin** - A closely related indole compound found in
  lesser concentrations. May be active (Gartz 1992), though its exact
  contribution to potency remains uncertain.
- **Norbaeocystin** - Another related compound present in some
  species.
- **Aeruginascin** - Found in certain species, also related to the
  psilocin molecule.

## The Psilometric Scale

Stamets defines potency categories based on the combined maximum
percentage of psilocybin plus psilocin (dry weight):

| Category | Combined % Psilocybin + Psilocin |
|---|---|
| Not active | 0% |
| Weakly active | <0.25% |
| Moderately active | 0.25-0.75% |
| Highly active (potent) | >0.75% |

If studies prove that baeocystin is equally active to psilocybin and
psilocin, the percentage of baeocystin would become a significant
factor influencing overall potency ratings.

## Dosage Guidelines

The threshold dose for pharmacological effects is 2-4 mg of
psilocybin/psilocin. Moderate "entheogenic" effects are inspired above
6 mg for psilocin and 10 mg for psilocybin. Since psilocybin is
degraded during digestion, users effectively experience only the
effects of psilocin, the dephosphorylated form.

Using psilocybe-semilanceata as a baseline species:
- 1 gram dried: threshold effects
- 2 grams: noticeable changes in auditory and visual perception
- 4 grams: intense experience lasting 4-5 hours
- 5 grams: nearly overwhelming, spanning approximately 6 hours
- 7+ grams: not recommended even for experienced users

Stamets strongly recommends taking half the intended dose first and
waiting 90 minutes before increasing. There is a point of diminishing
returns; more is not always better.

## Factors Affecting Potency

Multiple variables affect the alkaloid content of mushrooms:

1. **Genetics:** Within any one species, there can be a tenfold or
  greater range in psilocybin and psilocin production from one
  collection to the next.
2. **Substrate nutrition:** The nutritional content of the growing
  substrate significantly affects potency. Adding tryptamine
  precursors can substantially increase psilocin levels (Gartz 1989).
3. **Age of specimen:** Younger specimens are generally more potent
  than mature ones (Gartz 1992), an observation confirmed by many
  users.
4. **UV exposure:** Ultraviolet radiation from the sun markedly lessens
  potency. Sun-struck specimens appear weak compared to those found in
  shade.
5. **Cultivation vs. wild:** Indoor-grown mushrooms seem consistently
  more potent than field-collected specimens, probably due to
  nutritional factors and UV protection.
6. **Drying and storage:** Potency decreases with bruising and
  handling, since the bluing reaction represents degradation of
  psilocin. Some species (like P. semilanceata) retain potency better
  due to lower initial psilocin content.
7. **Sugar content:** Raising malt sugars above 10% in cultivation
  substrates can completely suppress psilocybin production.

## Potentiation

Anecdotal reports suggest that Syrian rue (peganum-harmala), rich
in MAO inhibitors, potentiates the effects of otherwise weak
psilocybin-containing mushrooms. No controlled studies have been
published to confirm this effect.
