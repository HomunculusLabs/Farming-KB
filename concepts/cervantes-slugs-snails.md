# Slugs and Snails on Cannabis

Slugs and snails are soft-bodied mollusk pests that
primarily affect [[knf-pest-control-methods]] for these common pests.

See also: [[outdoor-cannabis-growing]]

## Identification

Slugs and snails are soft, slimy creatures that can be
white, dark, yellow, or occasionally striped. They
range from 0.25 to 3 inches (1 to 9 cm) in length.

- **Snails** live inside a circular protective shell.
- **Slugs** do not have a shell and have a soft,
  elongated body.

Both hide during the day and feed at night. They leave
a distinctive slimy, silvery trail of mucus wherever
they travel. They lay translucent eggs that hatch in
about a month. They reproduce prolifically, and the
young mollusks often eat relatively more than adults.

## Damage

Slugs and snails make holes in leaves, often with a
web-like or ragged appearance. They will eat almost any
vegetation, including roots. These creatures winter over
in warm, damp locations in most climates. Slugs and
snails are especially damaging to tender seedlings and
young plants.

They will migrate to adjacent gardens in quest of food.
The combination of chewing damage and slime trails on
foliage makes their presence easy to identify. In severe
infestations, they can completely defoliate young plants
and kill seedlings.

## Cultural and Physical Control

Several non-chemical methods are effective for managing
[[hamilton-slug-and-snail-control]] populations:

### Barriers

A thin layer of lime, [[comparison-neem-oil-cannabis-vs-diatomaceous-earth-cannabis]], or salty beach
sand two to six inches (6 to 15 cm) wide around
individual plants, beds, or the entire garden creates an
impassable barrier. The lime layer is not thick enough
to alter soil pH but effectively repels slugs and
snails. A clean, dry perimeter around the garden also
makes it difficult for them to travel.

### Handpicking

Spotlight and handpick slugs and snails at night when
they are actively feeding. This is time-consuming but
very effective for small gardens. To trap them during
the day, attach short one-inch (3 cm) feet on a wide
board and leave it in the garden. The pests will seek
refuge under the board. Pick up the board every day or
two and dispose of the slugs.

### Traps

- **Beer traps:** Bury a container filled with beer so
  the rim is at soil level. Slugs are attracted to the
  fermented yeast and fall in and drown. The beer
  must be deep enough to submerge the mollusks.
- **Jam traps:** A mix of fruit jam and water also
  attracts slugs effectively.
