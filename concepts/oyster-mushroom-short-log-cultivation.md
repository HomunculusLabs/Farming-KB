## Tree Species Selection

- Wild oyster mushrooms appear on dead broad-leaf hardwoods (poplars) after rain
- Most needle-leaf softwoods contain phenolic resin compounds — low productivity
- Conifer sawdust usable only after phenolic compounds are gone
- **Best species**: poplar, willow, beech, elm, alder
- Unlike shiitake, oyster mushrooms do NOT grow well on oak logs
- Select trunks with large sapwood area (mushrooms feed primarily on sapwood)
- Sapwood = lighter outer wood; heartwood = darker inner wood
- Logs with more sapwood produce mushrooms for more years

## Tree Felling

- Fell during dormant season when bark adheres tightly and trunks are full of sap and nutrients
- Later in season, nutrients consumed during bud germination
- Recently cut logs: living wood cells interfere with mycelial growth
- Too old logs: drier, less water content retards growth
- After felling, leave uncut in shade several days before inoculation
- Cut logs should NOT contact ground during storage (contamination)
- Cut into 20cm pieces a few days before inoculation for proper moisture
- Required log moisture: 38-42%
- **Coin test**: when logs show coin-thick crack in cut section, moisture is proper
- Ideal log diameter: 15-20cm

## Spawn Preparation

- Sawdust spawn is the standard for log cultivation
- Use low-temperature strains for bunch formation and high yield
- High-temperature strains: fast flushes, high yield, but lower quality
- Order spawn 1-2 months in advance from reliable dealer
- Specify desired shipping date for maximum freshness

## Inoculation

- Begin when outside temperature is close to mycelial optimal: 25-27°C
- Mycelia survive 5-35°C; best growth at 25-27°C
- **Timing**: lowest temperature 5°C, highest 20°C in spring
- Inoculum: 20-50% spawn mixed with supplement (sawdust:rice bran = 4:1 by volume, 60-65% moisture)
- Apply inoculum 5-10mm thick to log ends, more on margins
- Stack 5-6 logs like a sandwich
- Stack in cutting order to minimize contamination
- Arrange stacked logs in 3-4 rows with walking aisles
- Too much supplement increases contamination risk

## Incubation

- Cover inoculated logs with plastic in shady, moist area
- Water surrounding ground when dry
- 3-4 days for mycelia to recover and begin regrowth
- Keep temperature steady at 15-20°C; wide variation = high infestation risk
- Apply fungicides (e.g., Benlate) if green mold appears
- In summer, may need temperature controls to maintain ~20°C

## Burying

- Well-colonized logs adhere to each other by summer — ready to fruit
- Burying recommended: sand prevents drying
- Separate each log unit, bury vertically with inoculated sides up
- Leave 10-20% of length above ground
- Space logs 10-15cm apart on 1m wide ridge
- Direct free water to 50cm furrow for drainage
- Pitch tent over buried logs to prevent sun exposure and moisture loss

## Pinning Induction

- Mid-autumn when temperature drops below 20°C
- Apply sufficient water ~10 days before expected pinning
- Stimulation methods: light, cold-shock, soaking, physical impact
- Fruiting bodies appear mainly on cambium

## Fruiting and Harvesting

- Water enough to keep logs and soil moist
- Mushrooms form first on top surface at bark/wood boundary
- Remove covering sheet and increase irrigation
- Stop irrigation 1-2 days before harvest
- Fruiting takes 5-6 days
- Harvest when caps reach 5cm diameter
- Clean remains after first cycle, maintain moist conditions
- Second flush: ~15 days later
- Cover with plastic in winter; harvest again when warm
