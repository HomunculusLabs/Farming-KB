# Mycofiltration of Water and Runoff

Mycofiltration uses fungal mycelium as a biological filter medium to
remove contaminants from water, including stormwater runoff, agricultural
effluent, and industrial wastewater. Harnessing the adsorptive, absorptive,
and degradative properties of mycelial mats, mycofiltration intercepts and
treats waterborne pollutants before they reach sensitive receiving waters.

## Principles of Mycofiltration

Fungal mycelium is an exceptionally effective filtration medium:

- **High surface-area-to-volume ratio**: Dense hyphal networks (2-10 um
  diameter) provide enormous surface area for contaminant interactions
- **Charged cell walls**: Chitin, glucans, and glycoproteins with
  ionizable groups bind [[singh-fungal-biosorption-heavy-metals]]
- **Extracellular enzyme secretion**: Living mycelium produces
  [[singh-enzyme-production-for-industrial-bioremediation]] including
  [[singh-fungal-laccase-enzymes-bioremediation]] and peroxidases
- **Biofilm matrices**: [[singh-fungal-biofilms-in-contaminated-environments]] trap
  particulates and support synergistic communities
- **Mechanical filtration**: Mycelial density physically strains suspended
  solids

## Types of Mycofiltration Systems

### Mycelial Mats and Barriers

Dense mycelial mats grown on organic substrates (straw, wood chips) serve
as permeable barriers: stream bank installations filtering agricultural
runoff, swale liners treating stormwater, and check dam inserts filtering
concentrated flows.

### Mycelial Sacks and Booms

Mycelium within permeable fabric creates portable units: floating
booms across drainage channels, inlet filters at pipe outfalls, and
deployed booms for accidental spill containment.

### Subsurface and Integrated Systems

Trench fills with mycelium-inoculated material intercept groundwater;
permeable reactive barriers provide in situ treatment; and mycofiltration
integrates with [[singh-constructed-wetlands-fungal-wastewater-treatment]], bioswales,
and [[singh-fungal-bioreactor-types-configurations]] treatment trains.

## Contaminants Removed by Mycofiltration

### Sediments and Biological Contaminants

Mycelial networks physically trap particles while
[[singh-fungal-exopolysaccharides-biofilm-soil-aggregation]] enhance aggregation. Biological
contaminants removed include bacteria (*E. coli*, *Enterococcus*),
protozoan cysts (*Giardia*, *Cryptosporidium*), and viruses.

### Heavy Metals

[[singh-fungal-biosorption-heavy-metals]] removes dissolved metals through ion exchange,
complexation with cell wall groups, and precipitation with fungal
metabolites. Removal efficiencies exceed 80% for copper, lead, zinc,
cadmium, and chromium at moderate concentrations.

### Petroleum Hydrocarbons

Mycelial mats intercept and degrade
[[singh-fungal-petroleum-hydrocarbon-metabolism]] through physical entrapment,
[[singh-fungal-oxidation-petroleum-enzymes]], and
[[singh-fungal-biosurfactants-pollutant-removal]] activity.

### Nutrients and Pesticides

Nutrient removal through fungal assimilation and sorption, combined with
[[singh-mycorrhiza-mediated-plant-nutrient-uptake-pathways]].
[[singh-fungal-degradation-of-pesticides-and-agrochemicals]] treated through sorption and enzymatic
degradation by [[singh-fungal-laccase-enzymes-bioremediation]] and
[[singh-cytochrome-p450-fungal-bioremediation]] enzymes.

## Key Fungal Species for Mycofiltration

| Species | Key Strengths | Applications |
|---|---|---|
| *Pleurotus ostreatus* | Rapid growth, broad enzymes | General filtration |
| *Stropharia rugosoannulata* | Durable mats, plant associations | Agriculture |
| *Trametes versicolor* | Potent laccase production | Dye/phenol removal |
| *Ganoderma lucidum* | Metal biosorption, pH tolerance | Metal-laden water |
| *Trichoderma* spp. | Pathogen exclusion, rapid growth | Pre-treatment |

## Design Considerations

### Hydraulic Performance

Flow rate management maintains contact time while preventing overflow.
Substrate and mycelial density must balance filtration with hydraulic
conductivity. Gross solids pre-treatment extends system lifespan.

### Longevity and Maintenance

Organic substrates are gradually consumed, requiring periodic replacement.
Fungal activity declines during cold periods in temperate climates.
Regular water quality sampling assesses effectiveness. Some systems can be
regenerated through nutrient addition and resting periods.

### Substrate Selection

Effective substrates provide structural support, nutrient availability,
appropriate water retention, and decomposition resistance. Common
substrates include sawdust, straw, wood chips, coconut coir, and
[[singh-composted-biomass-as-soil-amendment-remediation]].

## Performance Assessment

Effectiveness is evaluated through contaminant removal efficiency,
hydraulic loading capacity, longevity, and [[singh-phytotoxicity-testing-bioremediation-assessment]]
of treated effluent.

## Integration with Green Infrastructure

Mycofiltration aligns with low-impact development stormwater management,
agricultural BMPs, brownfield redevelopment,
[[singh-fungal-remediation-of-mining-tailings-mine-waste]], and watershed protection.

## Challenges and Limitations

1. **Variable performance**: Depends on site-specific conditions
2. **Limited field validation**: Most data from laboratory and pilot
   studies
3. **Regulatory acceptance**: Not yet widely recognized as standalone
   treatment
4. **Scalability**: Commercial-scale production requires standardization
