## Straw Substrate Preparation with Yeast Extract Enrichment

## Overview

The Jarrold boil-a-bag method [[mycelial-colonization]]. This preparation technique is a cornerstone of the boil-a-bag approach, which was developed as an accessible indoor cultivation method requiring minimal specialized equipment beyond a pressure cooker and basic household items. The method bridges the gap between professional mushroom [[nitrogen-supplementation-for-mushroom-substrates]]**: Straw is a carbon-rich substrate with relatively low nitrogen content. Yeast extract provides bioavailable nitrogen in the form of amino acids and peptides, improving the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio for more vigorous mycelial growth.
- **Mineral content**: Yeast extract contains potassium, phosphorus, magnesium, and [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]] before packing into bags.

## Loading and Sterilization

After preparation, the straw substrate is packed firmly into boil-a-bag containers (heat-resistant polyethylene bags), leaving enough room at the top to insert a filter can. The bags are then sterilized in a pressure cooker at 15 psi for 60 minutes (or in two 30-minute stages). This sterilization step kills competing organisms and creates a clean [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] to colonize without competition.

## Safety and Hygiene Considerations

- Work in a clean environment to minimize contamination risk during the draining, supplementation, and bag-filling steps.
- Use clean hands and tools throughout the process.
- The boiling water used in soaking provides partial pasteurization of the straw surface, but full sterilization occurs during the pressure cooking step.
