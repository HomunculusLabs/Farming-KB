## Nutrient Deficiencies and Disorders in Cannabis

## Overview

Cannabis plants require 16 essential elements for healthy growth. Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen are absorbed from air and water; the remaining 13 elements (nutrients) must be supplied through the growing medium. These nutrients are categorized as macronutrients (primary and secondary) and micronutrients (trace elements). Understanding whether a nutrient is mobile or immobile within the plant is the single most important diagnostic key — it determines where [[knf-potassium-deficiency-and-excess]] causes internal foliage temperature to climb and protein cells to burn. Evaporation is highest at leaf edges — that is where burning appears. K is usually present in soil but locked out by high salinity.

**Toxicity**: Difficult to diagnose because it manifests as deficiency of magnesium, manganese, zinc, and iron.

## General Treatment Protocols

### Deficiency Treatment

1. Identify whether symptoms appear on older (mobile) or newer (immobile) leaves
2. Check pH — adjust to 5.5-6.2 (hydroponic) or 6.0-7.0 (soil)
3. Apply appropriate nutrient supplement
4. Expect visible recovery in 4-5 days for mobile nutrients
5. Organic fast sources: seabird guano, fish emulsion, blood meal (N); bat guano, bone meal (P); wood ash / soluble potash (K)

### Toxicity Treatment

1. Flush the growing medium with three times its volume of water
2. Use a very mild, complete fertilizer in the flush solution
3. Do not add the problematic nutrient for one week
4. Monitor for secondary deficiencies caused by the excess

### Foliar Feeding

Foliar feeding provides rapid nutrient uptake through leaf stomata but carries risks: stomata become clogged with residue, temporarily reducing photosynthesis. Not recommended for potassium deficiency. Avoid spraying seedlings or unrooted cuttings. Never spray within two weeks of harvest.

## pH and Nutrient Availability

The pH of the growing medium is the master variable controlling nutrient availability:

- **Hydroponic ideal**: 5.5-6.2
- **Soil ideal**: 6.0-7.0
- **Potting soil ideal**: 5.5-6.5
- **Below 6.5 in soil**: May cause [[cannabis-nutrient-lockout-and-ph]] deficiency cascades. Test pH with every watering for best results.

pH imbalance is the most common root cause of apparent nutrient deficiency. Always check and correct pH before supplementing individual nutrients.
