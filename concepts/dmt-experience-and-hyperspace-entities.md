# DMT Experience and Hyperspace Entities

The dimethyltryptamine (DMT) experience represents what [[terence-mckenna]]
called "the major quantum mechanical phenomena" accessible to human
consciousness alongside dreams. Within the [[terence-mckenna-tryptamine-hypothesis]],
McKenna's framework for understanding psychedelic compounds, the DMT
experience occupies a central position due to its extraordinary intensity,
brevity, and the uniqueness of its phenomenological content.

## The Tryptamine Family

The tryptamine hallucinogens -- DMT, psilocybin, and ayahuasca (a
combination of DMT with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor taken orally) --
are distinguished from other hallucinogens by the intensity of the
hallucinations they produce and the concentration of their activity in
the visual cortex. The interior landscapes they reveal carry an immense
vividness, with information presented three-dimensionally and deployed
fourth-dimensionally, coded as light and as evolving surfaces. Unlike
LSD or mescaline, each tryptamine is what McKenna calls "a
phenomenologically defined universe unto itself."

## Onset and Mode Shift

When DMT is smoked, onset begins in approximately fifteen seconds. The
experimenter falls immediately into a trance with eyes closed. A sound
like ripping cellophane -- someone crumpling plastic film -- is heard,
accompanied by an ascending tone. A friend of McKenna's suggested this
sound is "our radio entelechy ripping out of the organic matrix." The
normal hallucinogenic modality is also present: shifting geometric
surfaces of migrating, changing colored forms. At the synaptic site, all
available bond sites are being occupied, and the mode shift occurs over
roughly thirty seconds.

## The Merry Go Raum

After the mode shift, one arrives in a space that defies description.
McKenna borrows the term "merry go raum" from James Joyce's *Finnegans
Wake*, from the German *raum* meaning "space." The room itself seems to
go around, feeling underground or somehow insulated and domed. In this
space one feels like a child, though one has come out somewhere in
eternity. The experience always reminded McKenna of the twenty-fourth
fragment of Heraclitus: "The Aeon is a child at play with colored
balls."

## Self-Transforming Machine Elves

In the merry go raum, one not only becomes the Aeon at play with colored
balls but encounters entities as well. McKenna and his brother Dennis
described these in *The Invisible Landscape* as self-transforming machine
elves -- dynamically contorting topological modules distinct from the
surrounding background, which itself undergoes continuous transformation.
These "hyperdimensional machine-elf entities" bathe one in love that is
open-hearted but not erotic. They seem like fractal reflections of some
previously hidden and suddenly autonomous part of one's own psyche. The
elves speak, saying "Don't be alarmed. Remember, and do what we are
doing."

## Translinguistic Information and the Logos

The entities emit sounds like music, like language -- sounds that pass
without any quantized moment of distinction. As Philo Judaeus said the
Logos would when it became perfect, they move from things heard to things
beheld. One hears and beholds "a language of alien meaning that is
conveying alien information that cannot be Englished." Being humans
confronted with a translinguistic object, cognitive dissonance is set up
in what McKenna calls the "hindbrain." Language sheds off the experience
like water off a duck's back, producing wonder, astonishment, and awe at
the brink of terror.

McKenna calls this alien intelligence the Logos. Both psilocybin and DMT
invoke the Logos, working directly on the language centers so that an
important aspect of the experience is the interior dialogue. The
experimenter must decide whether to enter into this dialogue and try to
make sense of the incoming signal.

## Glossolalia and Primal Language

Under the influence of DMT, unanticipated vocal phenomena become
possible. Unlike classical glossolalia, the DMT-induced phenomenon is
simply a brain state that allows expression of the assembly language
behind language -- a primal language (*Ursprache*) of the sort Robert
Graves discussed in *The White Goddess*, or the Kabbalistic language
described in the *Zohar*. One discovers an ability to make
extra-dimensional objects: feeling-toned, meaning-toned, three-dimensional
rotating complexes of transforming light and color.

## The Black Hole Effect

McKenna describes a "black hole effect" surrounding DMT. Just as a
black hole causes a curvature of space such that no information can
leave it, DMT is metaphorically an intellectual black hole: once one
knows about it, it is very hard for others to understand what one is
talking about. "One cannot be heard. The more one is able to articulate
what it is, the less others are able to understand." McKenna suggests
this is why people who attain enlightenment are silent -- because others
cannot understand them.

## Endogenous DMT and Quantum Correlates

DMT is an endogenous hallucinogen, present in small amounts in the human
brain. Psilocybin is 4-phosphoraloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, while
serotonin -- the major neurotransmitter found in all life and most
concentrated in humans -- is 5-hydroxytryptamine. The very rapid onset of
DMT (forty-five seconds, lasting five minutes) means "the brain is
absolutely at home with this compound."

John Smythies, [[alexander-shulgin]], and others have demonstrated quantum
mechanical correlates to hallucinogenesis: moving a single atom on the
molecular ring of an inactive compound can render it highly active. To
McKenna, this is "a perfect proof of the dynamic linkage at the
formative level between quantum mechanically described matter and mind."

## Comparison with Psilocybin

The psilocybin mushrooms convey one into the same tryptamine
hypercontinuum, but the experience builds up over an hour and is
sustained for a couple of hours, versus DMT's nearly instantaneous onset
and five-minute duration. Both produce confrontation with an alien
intelligence and extremely bizarre translinguistic information complexes.
McKenna proposes that these experiences reveal a latent ability of the
human brain/body, and that a human language may be possible in which
"the intent of meaning is actually beheld in three-dimensional space."
