# Psilocybin Extraction via Alcohol [[pf-tek-alcohol-extraction-method|Method]]

## Overview

[[pf-tek-ethyl-alcohol-extraction-liqueur-concentration]] is a technique for isolating psilocybin from dried magic mushrooms using pure ethyl alcohol as a solvent. The process produces a concentrated [[psilocybin-mushroom-alcohol-extraction-pf-tek|tek]]-[[ethyl-alcohol-extraction]]-psilocybin-liqueur|psilocybin liqueur that can be dosed with precision. Because the extraction uses only mushrooms and drinkable alcohol, the resulting tincture is free from chemical contaminants. This method was popularized within the mycological community as a straightforward, low-tech approach to creating a potent and storable [[mckenna-food-gods-set-setting-psychedelic-preparation]].

## Chemistry of the Extraction

[[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]]—the primary psychoactive alkaloids [[cap-versus-stem-alkaloid-distribution-in-psilocybin-mushrooms]]—are both soluble in ethanol. When dried mushroom material is soaked in high-proof ethyl alcohol, these compounds dissolve into the liquid phase. Using 200 proof (100% anhydrous) ethyl alcohol is ideal because it contains no water, which means the final concentrated product has no residual moisture. 190 proof (95% ethanol, 5% water) is acceptable but introduces unwanted water content, especially problematic when the extract is evaporated down to high concentration.

## Materials Required

- **Psilocybin mushrooms**: Harvested before or just as caps open, then cool-dried with desiccant until rock-hard
- **200 proof ethyl alcohol**: Anhydrous ethyl alcohol, available from well-stocked liquor stores
- **Bottles with tight-fitting caps**: Whiskey bottles, pop bottles, or similar containers
- **Dust-pollen masks**: Inexpensive filters available at hardware and drug stores
- **Drinking glass, funnel, spoon, knife**
- **Small electric fan**: Desk clip-on fans work well for directing airflow
- **10cc syringe**: For accurate measurement and dispensing of the final extract

## Step-by-Step Procedure

### 1. Pulverization

Place thoroughly dried mushroom material into a strong zip-lock freezer bag. Cover the bag with a magazine for protection and pulverize the contents by pounding with the rubber heel of a large shoe. The mushrooms must be completely dry and brittle to achieve fine pulverization, which maximizes surface area for solvent contact.

### 2. Primary Soak

Transfer the pulverized material into a bottle with a tight, leak-proof cap. Add enough ethyl alcohol to cover the material and create a loose slurry. Seal the bottle, shake well, and let it sit for 24 hours. Shake the bottle periodically during this time to promote extraction.

### 3. Primary Filtration

After 24 hours, filter the alcohol from the slurry. Fashion a dust-pollen mask over the mouth of a drinking glass as a filter. Shake the slurry bottle, pour some through the filter, and squeeze to extract as much alcohol as possible. Store the filtered alcohol extract in a fresh, clean bottle.

### 4. Secondary Soak and Filtration

Retrieve the mushroom material from the filters and re-soak it with fresh alcohol. Repeat the soaking (24 hours) and filtration process. This secondary extraction captures remaining alkaloids left in the material after the first pass.

### 5. Evaporation and Concentration

Combine all alcohol extracts into a single glass container. Position a small electric fan so that airflow is directed down into the glass, creating ripples on the alcohol surface. This accelerates evaporation. Periodically wash residue from the inside walls of the glass back into the solution as the level drops. Keep all flames away—pure ethyl alcohol is highly flammable. The fumes are harmless since the alcohol is a drinkable spirit.

## Dosage and Concentration

A concentrated brew of 1 to 3 grams (or more) of dried mushroom material per 1 fluid ounce of final extract alcohol is considered potent. The more concentrated the extract, the less liquid needs to be consumed per dose. Dispensing with a 10cc syringe is strongly recommended for accuracy—an overdose calculation could send an inexperienced user far beyond the intended experience level.

## Storage

The concentrated mushroom liqueur will have a pungent, mushroomy aroma. It should be stored in small screw-cap bottles or vials in the freezer. Ethyl alcohol does not freeze solid at standard freezer temperatures, so the extract remains liquid and stable for extended periods.

## Procuring 200 Proof Ethyl Alcohol

Full-service liquor stores in the United States supply hospitals and laboratories with 200 proof anhydrous ethyl alcohol. If the local store does not carry it, staff can usually direct you to a source. Some states require a permit, obtainable from the state liquor board (typically in the state capital) for a small fee ($5–10). The permit application asks about intended use; common stated purposes include non-toxic surface sterilizing, herb extraction, preservation, tincture-making, and perfume-making.

## Safety Considerations

- **Flammability**: Pure ethyl alcohol is extremely flammable. No open flames or sparks should be near the extraction or evaporation setup.
- **Dosing accuracy**: The concentrated nature of the extract makes precise measurement essential. Even small miscalculations can produce dramatically different effects.
- **Solvent residue**: Ensure all alcohol has fully evaporated if consuming the residue directly rather than the liqueur.
- **Legal status**: Psilocybin and psilocin are [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]] in many jurisdictions. This information is presented for educational and harm-reduction purposes.

## Advantages of Alcohol Extraction

- Uses only food-grade, non-toxic solvents
- No specialized laboratory equipment required
- Produces a stable, long-lasting preparation
- Enables precise dosing through volumetric measurement
- The liqueur form avoids the taste and texture issues of eating raw or dried mushrooms

## Comparison with Other Methods

Compared to direct consumption of dried mushrooms, alcohol extraction offers superior dosing precision and storage stability. Compared to methyl alcohol or other industrial solvents mentioned in older literature, ethyl alcohol is far safer as it is non-toxic and drinkable. The primary trade-off is the time investment—multiple days of soaking and hours of evaporation—versus the simplicity of eating dried material directly.

Always use food-grade ethanol for edible preparations.
