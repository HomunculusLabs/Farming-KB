# Anaerobic Sewage Digesters

Anaerobic sewage digesters are a core component of [[biogas-digesters-and-anaerobic-digestion-systems|systems]],
transforming human and animal waste into useful biogas and nutrient-rich effluent through
controlled biological [[permaculture]]: A Designers' Manual*, these systems leverage natural microbial
processes to simultaneously treat sewage, produce energy, and remove harmful contaminants.

## Classification of Sewage Ponds

Sewage [[anaerobic-sewage-digestion-and-biogas-production|production]]. These ponds host diverse communities
  of phytoplankton, bacteria, and zooplankton.

- **Aerobic Ponds**: Oxygen-producing ponds where aerobic bacteria and algae dominate,
  completing the treatment process by oxidizing remaining [[savory-soil-organic-matter-and-decomposition-dynamics]] nutrients.

## Pond Sizing and Dimensions

Proper sizing is critical for effective sewage treatment. Key design parameters include:

- **Average depth**: 1 meter for typical sewage ponds
- **Population basis**: 1,000 townspeople require approximately 270 m² of settling pond area
- **Configuration options for a 1,000-person system**:
  - 3 m × 90 m (shallow configuration)
  - 3 m × 45 m × 2 m deep (medium depth)
  - 3 m × 30 m × 3 m deep (deep digester configuration)

Deeper configurations favour anaerobic conditions and greater methane production, while
shallower designs promote aerobic processing in later treatment stages.

## Sealed and Agitated Digester Design

For optimal methane production, digesters must be properly sealed and agitated:

- **Sealing mechanisms**: Water seals prevent gas leakage while allowing pressure relief
- **Cover materials**: Weighted covers made of plastic sheeting, metal, butyl rubber, or
  fibreglass create a gas-tight environment for biogas collection
- **Agitation method**: Pumped biogas is cycled back through the digester to stir the
  sludge, ensuring uniform contact between microorganisms and organic material

Active sludge only functions effectively when in contact with semi-liquid inputs, making
continuous agitation essential for efficient digestion.

## Digestion Process and Performance

The [[anaerobic-digestion-biogas-production]] process follows specific parameters for optimal performance:

- **Residence time**: Approximately 20 days for dissolved solids breakdown
- **[[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]]**: 25–30 °C for peak microbial activity
- **Methane yield**: 1 cubic metre of biogas removes 2.89 kg of solids and significantly
  reduces Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)
- **Pathogen reduction**: Worm egg destruction reaches 90–94% within the digester

Scum formation on the surface of digesters must be prevented, as it insulates the
digesting mass and reduces gas production efficiency.

## Self-Regulating Equilibrium

A well-designed digester achieves a self-regulating equilibrium where the rate of sewage
input naturally balances the rate of gas output. This equilibrium means the system
requires minimal external management once established, as microbial populations adjust
their activity to match the available substrate.

## Biogas Production and Uses

The biogas produced by anaerobic digesters is a valuable energy resource with multiple
applications:

- **Motive power**: Running engines and generators for electricity production
- **Cooking and heating**: Direct combustion for domestic and institutional use
- **Lighting**: Gas lamps for illumination

The gas is primarily composed of methane (60–70%) and carbon dioxide (30–40%), with trace
amounts of hydrogen sulphide.

### Mechanism of Metal Removal

Hydrogen sulphide (H₂S), produced during anaerobic digestion, combines [[singh-fungal-interactions-with-heavy-metals-in-soil]]
to form insoluble sulphides at pH levels above 7. This precipitation reaction effectively
locks metals into the sludge, preventing their release into effluent waters.

## Sulphur-Loving Bacteria and Metal Precipitation

Specialised sulphur-oxidizing bacteria play a critical role in metal removal:

- **Key genera**: *Thiosporallum*, *Chromatium*, *Rhodopseudomonas*
- **Function**: These bacteria produce elemental sulphur that binds to dissolved metals
- **Precipitation rate**: 1.8–2.0 mg/L of metals are precipitated per 1.0 mg/L of
  sulphur produced

This biological sulphur cycle creates a powerful, self-sustaining metal removal mechanism
that operates continuously within the pond system.

## Facultative Pond Ecology

Facultative ponds support rich ecological communities that contribute to further treatment:

### Phytoplankton

Algal species dominate the oxygenated upper layers, photosynthesizing and producing oxygen
that supports aerobic bacteria below. Key genera include:

- *Euglena*
- *Chlamydomonas*
- *Chlorella*
- *Scenedesmus*

### Bacterial Communities

Diverse bacterial populations process [[dighton-fungal-dissolved-organic-carbon-production]] matter:

- *Cyclotella*
- *Pinnularia*
- *Hypnodinium*
- *Rhodomonas*
