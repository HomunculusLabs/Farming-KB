# The Transcendent Other and Exopheromones

## The Problem of the Other

The central mystery of the [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]] is the encounter with the Other — an apparently independent intelligence that manifests in the altered state. This Transcendent Other appears in various forms across cultures and across different psychedelic compounds: as spirit guides, ancestors, gods, machine elves, alien entities, or simply as a vast, intelligent presence.

The question is: what is the ontological status of this Other? Is it a hallucination — a projection of the unconscious mind? Or is it something real, something independent of the individual psyche, something that exists in a dimension of reality normally inaccessible to us?

## Pheromones and Exopheromones

McKenna proposes the concept of "exopheromones" — [[query-how-do-plants-use-chemical-signals-to-defend-against-insects]] that, like pheromones, carry information between organisms, but that cross species boundaries rather than operating within a single species. If pheromones are the language of intraspecies communication, exopheromones are the language of transspecies communication.

The psychoactive compounds produced by plants — psilocybin, DMT, harmine, ibogaine, mescaline — may function as exopheromones. They are chemical messages from the vegetable mind to the human mind, carrying information about the nature of reality, the structure of consciousness, and the relationship between humans and the larger biosphere.

## The Hyperspace Entities

Under the influence of DMT, many people report encounters with what they describe as "entities" — beings that appear to possess intelligence, intentionality, and agency. These entities communicate through language, gesture, telepathy, or direct transfer of understanding. They are variously described as aliens, spirits, elves, teachers, jesters, or "self-transforming machine elves."

The consistency of these reports across individuals and cultures is striking. Terence McKenna himself reported hundreds of encounters with these entities and insisted that they were not hallucinations but genuine denizens of a dimension of reality that is normally invisible to us but becomes accessible under the influence of DMT.

## An Ecology of Souls

The idea that there exists an "ecology of souls" — a nonphysical dimension of reality inhabited by intelligent beings — is one of the oldest and most widespread beliefs in human history. It is found in shamanic traditions throughout the world, in the Platonic theory of forms, in the Neoplatonic concept of the Anima Mundi, and in the Eastern concept of the bardo realms.

McKenna suggests that this ancient belief may be literally true. The psychedelic experience, far from being a mere hallucination, may be a genuine form of perception — a way of seeing into a dimension of reality that is as real as the physical world but normally invisible to our senses.

## The Alien Nuncio

McKenna describes the DMT experience as an "audience with the alien nuncio" — a meeting with a messenger from another dimension. The message is not always clear, but it consistently involves the communication of something vast, complex, and important — something about the nature of consciousness, the structure of the cosmos, or the future of humanity.

The encounter with the Transcendent Other is not a comfortable experience. It is terrifying, awe-inspiring, and profoundly humbling. It challenges everything we think we know about ourselves and our place in the universe. It is, in the deepest sense, a religious experience — an encounter with the sacred.

## Implications for Science

If the Transcendent Other is real — if the entities encountered in the psychedelic state are genuine inhabitants of a nonphysical dimension — then the implications for science are revolutionary. It would mean that the materialist worldview is incomplete, that consciousness is not an epiphenomenon of matter but a fundamental feature of reality, and that the universe is far stranger and more wonderful than our current science can imagine.

The exploration of the psychedelic state — the systematic investigation of the Transcendent Other — may be the most important scientific frontier of the twenty-first century. But this exploration requires a new kind of science, one that is willing to take subjective experience seriously and to investigate the nonphysical dimensions of reality with the same rigor that it brings to the investigation of the physical world.

## Good Technique

The encounter with the Transcendent Other requires proper preparation and technique. As McKenna advises: "Good technique is obvious: one sits down, one shuts up, and one pays attention." The experience should be pursued in a setting of comfort, safety, and respect. The intent should be clear and humble. And the results should be received with gratitude and wonder.

## Merged: Stoned Ape Theory

# Stoned Ape Theory

The "Stoned Ape" hypothesis, proposed by Terence McKenna in Food of the Gods (1992), argues that the inclusion of psychoactive mushrooms (specifically [[mckenna-stropharia-cubensis-extraterrestrial-hypothesis-spore-dispersal]] / [[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]) in the diet of early hominids in the African grasslands was the primary catalyst for the rapid expansion of brain size, the emergence of language, and the development of human self-reflection. The theory addresses one of the central puzzles of paleoanthropology: how the human brain tripled in size in under three million years, a rate Lumsden and Wilson called "perhaps the fastest advance recorded for any complex organ in the whole history of life."

## The Evolutionary Puzzle

Between Homo habilis (brain weight ~770g, 27.5 oz) and Homo erectus (brain weight 900-1100g), and continuing through to Homo sapiens (~1400g), the human brain underwent an expansion without clear precedent in evolutionary biology. Conventional explanations (bipedalism, tool use, social organization, hunting) have all been proposed as selective pressures, but none satisfactorily accounts for the extraordinary speed of this transformation.

McKenna's core argument: mutation-causing, psychoactive chemical compounds in the early human diet directly influenced the rapid reorganization of the brain's information-processing capacities. The alkaloids in plants, specifically the hallucinogenic compounds psilocybin, DMT, and harmaline, could be the chemical factors that catalyzed the emergence of human self-reflection.

## The Three Levels of Psilocybin Use

McKenna proposes that psilocybin's unique pharmacological profile produced adaptive advantages at three distinct dose levels:

### Level 1: Low Doses (Visual Acuity Enhancement)

Citing Roland Fischer's research from the late 1960s, in which small amounts of psilocybin improved subjects' ability to detect when previously parallel lines became skewed, McKenna argues that low doses of psilocybin consumed incidentally while browsing for food would have provided enhanced visual acuity, particularly edge detection. This "chemical binoculars" effect would improve hunting success, giving individuals who regularly consumed the mushroom an adaptive advantage.
