# Fish Amino Acid (FAA)

Fish Amino Acid is a nitrogen-rich KNF input made by fermenting fish parts with brown sugar. It provides bioavailable amino acids, peptides, and nitrogen compounds that plants can absorb directly through foliage and roots. It is especially important during early vegetative growth and for recovering stressed or weakened plants.

## Why Fish?

Fish and animal proteins are high in amino acids and nitrogen. Cho emphasizes using **blue-black fish** (mackerel/pomfret family) as the preferred species because:
- High protein content
- Readily available and inexpensive
- Rapid fermentation due to high enzyme activity
- Strong nitrogen/amino acid profile

Other suitable fish: sardines, anchovies, any oily fish. Avoid large predator fish (potential bioaccumulation of heavy metals).

## FAA Recipe

### Materials
- Fresh fish (whole or parts) — blue-black fish preferred
- Brown sugar or jaggery (crude/unrefined sugar)
- IMO-3 (optional but recommended — accelerates fat dissolution)
- Glass or food-grade plastic container with lid

### Ratios
- Fish to sugar: **1:1 by weight** (standard)
- For oily fish: can use slightly less sugar (0.8:1 sugar to fish)
- Fill container 2/3 full (leave headspace for gas expansion)

### Process

**Basic Method**:
1. Clean fish but do NOT remove internal organs — organs contain digestive enzymes that aid fermentation
2. If fish is large, chop into pieces (2-3 inches) to increase surface area
3. Do NOT wash fish after cleaning — surface bacteria aid fermentation
4. Layer fish and sugar alternately in container (sugar on bottom, like FPJ)
5. The bottom 1/3 should be mostly sugar (acts as filter)
6. Cover loosely for first 1-2 days, then close lid
7. Store in cool, dark place
8. Ferment 7-10 days in summer, 10-15 days in winter
9. Strain liquid through cheesecloth — this is FAA
10. Fish residue can be added to compost (high nitrogen) or used for [[water-soluble-calcium-phosphate-wcp]]

**Enhanced Method with IMO-3** (from Cho's book):
- Add IMO-3 (see [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]]) to the fish-sugar mixture
- IMO-3 contains lipase-producing bacteria that dissolve fish fats
- Fat dissolution prevents rancidity and improves FAA quality
- Without IMO-3, fat can slow fermentation and produce off-odors

### Signs of Quality
- Dark amber to brown liquid
- Strong fermented smell (not rotting — should be pungent but not putrid)
- No visible mold (some surface yeast is normal)
- Oily layer may form on top (normal, skim off if excessive)

### Storage
- FAA separated from solids: keeps for 1-2 years
- FAA with fish residue: use within 6 months (residue continues to decompose)
- Store in glass or food-grade plastic, cool dark place
- Refrigeration extends shelf life significantly

## Application Rates

| Use Case | Dilution | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Vegetative growth | 1:1000 | Standard foliar spray |
| Stressed/weak plants | 1:500 | Higher rate for recovery |
| Soil drench | 1:500-1000 | Feed soil microbes |
| Seed treatment (SES) | 1 ml/L | Only for weak seedlings |
| Cannabis vegetative | 1:1000 | Sparingly — living soil already has N |
| Root dip for transplants | 1:100 | Quick nitrogen boost |

Apply as foliar spray early morning or late evening. Do not apply during flowering/reproductive stage — excess nitrogen causes:
- Excessive vegetative growth at expense of flowers/fruit
- Soft, weak stems
- Reduced sugar content in fruit
- Increased pest susceptibility (soft tissue = easy target)

## Using Fish Bones for Calcium Phosphate

After extracting FAA, the remaining fish bones and residue are valuable:
- Bones + [[brown-rice-vinegar-brv]] → [[water-soluble-calcium-phosphate-wcp]] (WS-CaPo)
- This extracts calcium phosphate from bones using acetic acid
- WS-CaPo is critical during the changeover period (see [[nutritive-cycle-theory]])
- Nothing is wasted in the KNF system

## Mackerel FAA for Mite Control (Cho)

From Cho's book — a specific application:
- Mackerel FAA at appropriate dilution can help control mites
- The amino acid profile of mackerel FAA creates conditions unfavorable for mite reproduction
- Combine with [[oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn]] for enhanced pest control effect
- Apply during vegetative growth when mites are most problematic

## FAA in the Nutritive Cycle

According to [[nutritive-cycle-theory]]:
- **Vegetative stage**: FAA at 1:1000 supports leaf and stem growth
- **Changeover period**: STOP FAA — nitrogen during flower transition causes problems
- **Reproductive stage**: STOP FAA — let plant focus on flowers/fruit, not leaves
- **Late reproductive**: FAA only if plants show nitrogen deficiency (yellowing older leaves)

FAA is the strongest nitrogen input in KNF. Overuse causes:
- "Over-luxurious" growth (soft, sappy tissue)
- Disease susceptibility
- Delayed flowering
- Reduced fruit quality and shelf life

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Putrid/rotten smell | Anaerobic, too warm | Add more sugar, move to cooler location |
| Too much gas/pressure | Active fermentation | Burp container daily |
| Solid residue smell bad | Fat not dissolving | Add IMO-3 or more sugar |
| Weak nitrogen effect | Too diluted | Increase concentration to 1:500 |
| Burn on leaves | Too concentrated or wrong time | Reduce rate, apply only vegetative stage |

## Pairing with Other Inputs

FAA works synergistically with:
- [[fermented-plant-extract-fpe]] — FPJ provides energy, FAA provides nitrogen
- [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]] — IMO provides microbes to break down FAA in soil
- [[lactic-acid-bacteria]] — LAB helps prevent putrefaction
- [[oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn]] — OHN prevents disease on nitrogen-rich soft tissue
- [[water-soluble-calcium-wca]] — WS-Ca balances nitrogen, strengthens cell walls
