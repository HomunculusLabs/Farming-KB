## DOC in Mangrove Ecosystems

Outwellings of water from mangrove swamps to the ocean can transfer
between 60 and 260 tonnes per year of carbon, exported mainly as
dissolved organic carbon. This DOC may be an important component of
nutrient additions to near-shore waters, a process in which fungi play
a major role. Lee (1995) demonstrated the importance of decomposition
of plant litter in coastal communities and the consequent nutrient
mineralization supplying nutrients to the ocean. Hyde and Lee (1995)

## Aquatic Hyphomycetes and DOC

In freshwater ecosystems, aquatic hyphomycetes colonize leaf litter
and produce dissolved organic carbon as they break down structural
polysaccharides. Bacteria in aquatic systems primarily access dissolved
organic matter, while fungi become important as leached organic matter
availability declines and they enter intact structural components of
leaf litter. The transition from [[wood-decomposition-by-fungi]].

## Stream Chemistry and Fungal DOC

Suberkropp and Chauvet (1995) showed that the rate of leaf
decomposition varied by a factor of 9 between streams differing in pH
and nutrient composition. Litter decomposed faster in hardwater streams
with higher nitrate, phosphorus, and temperature. Fungal biomass and
activity were more abundant in nutrient-rich streams, resulting in
more diverse fungal assemblages and greater DOC production. The
relationship between stream chemistry, fungal activity, and DOC
production suggests that anthropogenic nutrient enrichment may
significantly alter carbon fluxes through aquatic food webs.
