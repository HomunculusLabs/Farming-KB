## Darwin"s *The Power of Movement in Plants*

## Overview

*The Power of Movement in Plants* (1880) was Charles Darwin's final major botanical work, co-authored with his son Francis Darwin. Published twenty years after *On the Origin of Species* (1859), this book represented the culmination of two decades of plant experiments conducted at Down House. The work established fundamental principles of plant tropisms and [[chamovitz-phytochrome-mobile-signal-leaf-to-flowering]] — decades before either was identified at the molecular level.

### The Mobile Signal Concept

Perhaps more important than the phototropism finding itself was Darwin's inference of a mobile signaling molecule. The Darwins recognized that since the tip detects light but the stem bends, some signal must travel from tip to stem. This concept of a transmitted growth signal anticipated the discovery of auxin (indole-3-acetic acid) by Frits Went in 1926 — nearly fifty years later.

## Scope of the Book

*The Power of Movement in Plants* covered multiple categories of plant movement:

### Tropisms (Directional Responses)

- **Phototropism:** Growth toward or away from light
- **Gravitropism:** Growth oriented by gravity (roots down, stems up)
- **Thigmotropism:** Response to mechanical contact (climbing tendrils)
- **Hydrotropism:** Root growth toward water sources

- **Gas lamp at 12 feet:** Light too dim for human observation but sufficient to induce phototropism in 3 hours — demonstrating extraordinary plant light sensitivity
- **Mechanical caps and tubes:** Handmade lightproof and transparent coverings from available materials
- **Time-lapse observation:** Patient recording of gradual plant movements over hours and days using simple glass markers and graduated arcs

The gas lamp experiment is particularly noteworthy — the light was so dim that "they could not see the seedlings themselves, nor see a pencil line on paper," yet the plants responded within three hours, demonstrating the extraordinary sensitivity of plant photoreception. This finding anticipated the modern understanding that plants can detect light levels far below human visual thresholds.

## Legacy and Modern Relevance

### Auxin Discovery

Darwin"s mobile signal hypothesis was vindicated by Frits Went's 1926 discovery of auxin. Went demonstrated that an agar block placed on a decapitated coleoptile tip could absorb the growth-promoting signal and transfer it to another plant, causing bending. This directly confirmed Darwin's model of a diffusible growth substance transmitted from tip to stem. The term "auxin" (from Greek *auxein*, "to grow") was coined to describe this class of plant hormones, with indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) identified as the primary natural auxin in the 1930s.
