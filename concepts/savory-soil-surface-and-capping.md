## The Mechanism of Capping

On bare and exposed ground, the direct impact of raindrops destroys
any [[crumb-structure]] present. The damage is governed by the size and
velocity of raindrops. Evidence shows up under tall trees where large
drops form on leaves and reach terminal velocity in about twenty-two
feet of fall, creating maximum impact below the canopy.

When raindrop impact breaks down surface crumb structure, it frees
organic and lightweight material to wash away while heavier fine
particles settle and seal, or cap, the soil. The importance of crumb
structure to water penetration is easily demonstrated: pour water on a
bowl of wheat grains and it soaks in; pour it on flour and it seals
the surface and runs off.

Capping is not always obvious on sandy soils but is almost always
present. Some soils develop caps so hard that only a sharp object can
break them. Tapping a severely capped soil with the fingers can produce
a hollow drum-like sound.

## Soil Cover: Two Forms

Soil cover generally comes in two forms. Erect growing plants intercept
rainfall so drops hit the ground with less energy. Dead, prone plant
material slows the flow of water across the land. Water can flow quite
fast between plants where no litter impedes it.

In [[savory-holistic-resource-management-mineral-cycle]], like succession and the
water cycle, lies in the condition of the soil surface. An exposed,
capped surface creates a harsh microenvironment where biological
breakdown occurs slowly. The capped surface also limits [[growing-gourmet-growing-room-ventilation-air-exchange]]
between soil and atmosphere. The chain reaction is relentless:

- As aeration decreases, life decreases
- As life decreases, organic material decreases
- As organic material decreases, soil structure deteriorates
- As soil structure decreases, aeration decreases further

This feedback loop ripples through the ecosystem. Fewer plants produce
less soil cover, creating more bare, capped soil. The process is
self-accelerating and extremely difficult to reverse without
intervention.
