# Sugar Crops

updated: 2026-04-18

From: [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-pattern-understanding-in-permaculture]]



## Overview

Sugar-producing plants play multiple roles in permaculture systems, from food and energy crops to mulch and soil-building species. Mollison includes sugar-providing grasses (such as sugarcane) among the species that stimulate soil bacteria and fungi, and benefit associated trees in orchard systems.



## Principal Sugar Crops

### Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum)
Sugarcane is a tall perennial grass of the humid tropics and subtropics. It serves multiple functions:
- Sugar production for food and fermentation (ethanol, rum)
- High biomass production for mulch
- Orchard interplant that stimulates soil life
- Wind protection in shelterbelt systems (highly responsive to windbreak benefits)

Sugarcane areas have historically suffered from heavy chemical pollution, and rehabilitation of old canefields requires decades of non-food forestry before safe food production can resume.

### Sugar Beet (Beta vulgaris)
Sugar beet is a temperate climate root crop. Trials of shadecloth with 50-70% light transmission may greatly increase production of sugar beet, demonstrating the importance of tree shade and shadecloth in deserts and cleared-area tropics. Sugar beet removes significant potassium from soils.

### Sweet Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor)
Sweet sorghum produces sugar-rich stalks that can be pressed for syrup. It is more cold-tolerant than sugarcane and suited to temperate and subtropical regions.

### Maple Sugar (Acer saccharum)
Maple syrup production from sugar maples is a traditional temperate forest product. While not extensively detailed in the Designers Manual, maple trees fit within the forest farm model as high-value managed trees producing both syrup and timber.

### Palm Sugars
- **Coconut palm** (Cocos nucifera) - toddy and coconut sugar from sap
- **Sugar palm** (Arenga pinnata / Borassus flabellifer) - palm sugar production
- **Oil palm** (Elaeis guineensis) - noted as a plantation crop in humid tropics

In the integrated garden design, the ideal palm circle includes 2 coconuts, 2 oil palms, 2 dates, and a sugar palm.

### Other Sugar Sources
- **Honey** from bee forage systems (covered in [[permaculture-designers-manual-beekeeping]])
- **Stevia** (Stevia rebaudiana) - natural sweetener
- **Sugar maple and birch** sap in cold climates



## Sugar Crops in Polyculture

Sugar cane and bananas are listed among crops highly responsive to wind protection -- both plant height and yields rapidly decrease with increases in wind speed. Intensive shelterbelt systems are essential for these species. Sugar-providing grasses assist orchard species and can be slashed or trimmed to provide rich mulch below trees or between crops.



## Processing and Energy

Sugar crops can be processed into:
- Table sugar and syrups
- Fermentation substrates for ethanol production
- Animal feed (molasses, bagasse)
- Mulch and compost material
- Fuel (bagasse as biomass)



## Regional Considerations

In the humid tropics of Africa, plantation crops include sugar alongside cotton, peanuts, and sisal. Sugar cane responds to shadecloth in desert environments, showing increased production under 50-70% shade.
