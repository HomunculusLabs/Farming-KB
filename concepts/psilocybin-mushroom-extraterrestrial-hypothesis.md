## The Human Soul Alternative

McKenna offered a significant counter-interpretation: that the mushroom is not extraterrestrial at all, but that "the human soul is so alienated from us in our present culture that we treat it as an extraterrestrial."

He argued that the [[valentina-pavlovna-wasson|Valentina]] and [[philip-k-dick-valis-and-psychedelic-mysticism]]'s concept in Valis of the Logos as a "creature of pure information" that was buried at Nag Hammadi circa 370 A.D. alongside the Chenoboskion Library.

The Logos existed as static information until 1947, when the texts were translated and read, bringing the symbiotic intelligence back to life. The mushroom consciousness, like Dick's Logos, is "a thing of pure information" that comes alive when encountered by human minds.

## Critical Assessment

McKenna acknowledged the speculative nature of the hypothesis, stating "I don't necessarily believe what the mushroom tells me; rather we have a dialogue." He treated the mushroom as "a very strange person with many bizarre opinions," entertaining its claims as one would those of an eccentric friend.

The hypothesis remains untestable by current scientific methods but continues to stimulate discussion about the relationship between consciousness, biology, and cosmology. Its value may lie less in its literal truth than in its capacity to challenge assumptions about the origins and nature of mind.

## Bell's Non-Locality and Interstellar Communication

A particularly speculative element of the hypothesis involves the possible use of quantum non-locality for communication across interstellar distances. McKenna noted that he was "troubled by how freely Bell's non-locality theorem is tossed around," but acknowledged that the alien intelligence encountered in the mushroom trance "does seem to be in possession of a huge body of information drawn from the history of the galaxy."

The possibility that quantum entanglement could enable instantaneous communication across vast distances, bypassing the speed-of-light limit, remains theoretical and controversial. If such communication were possible, it would provide a mechanism by which the mushroom could serve as a real-time communication device rather than merely a repository of ancient information.

## The Problem of Recognizing Contact

McKenna argued that the main problem with searching for extraterrestrials is "to recognize them." Time is so vast and evolutionary strategies and environments so varied that "the trick is to know that contact is being made at all."

The mushroom's claim that "humanity's conceptions of organized intelligence and the dispersion of life in the galaxy are hopelessly culture-bound" challenges the assumption that extraterrestrial intelligence would be recognizable on human terms. If the galaxy has been "an organized society for billions of years," the forms that intelligence takes may be radically different from anything humans can imagine.

## Eros and the Christus

McKenna offered a mythological reading of the mushroom's history in human culture, describing the mushroom as "the body of Osiris, the condensed body of Eros." He suggested that Eros "retreated at the coming of the Christus" and that the Spanish conquistadors, having "conquered the New World and driven its people into cultural fragmentation and diaspora," unexpectedly "came upon the body of Osiris" in the mountains of Mexico.

This mythological framework places the suppression and rediscovery of the mushroom within a larger narrative of cosmic forces, suggesting that the mushroom represents a primordial consciousness that has been alternately embraced and rejected by human cultures throughout history.
