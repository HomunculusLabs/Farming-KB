# Psilocybin Mushroom Extraterrestrial Hypothesis

The extraterrestrial hypothesis for [[psilocybin]] mushrooms is Terence McKenna's proposal that [[stropharia-cubensis]] may not have evolved on Earth but instead arrived via interstellar spore dispersal. First articulated in [[Psilocybin: The Magic Mushroom Grower's Guide]], the hypothesis emerged from information McKenna reported receiving during mushroom-induced trance states.

While highly speculative, the hypothesis raises important questions about the relationship between biology, consciousness, and the possibility of interstellar life.

## The Mushroom's Self-Description

According to McKenna, the mushroom communicates that once a culture achieves complete understanding of its genetic information, it re-engineers itself for survival. The Stropharia cubensis mushroom's version of this re-engineering takes two complementary forms:

1. **Mycelial network strategy:** When in contact with planetary surfaces, the organism forms extensive mycelial networks that can process and exchange information across large areas
2. **Spore-dispersion strategy:** As a means of radiating throughout the galaxy, using extremely hardy spores capable of surviving interstellar conditions

The mushroom reportedly describes itself as a symbiote that "desires ever deeper symbiosis with the human species." It claims to have achieved early symbiosis with human society by associating with domesticated cattle and, through them, human nomads.

Wherever human genes traveled, the mushroom's genes would be carried as well. This parasitic or commensal strategy would ensure the mushroom's propagation alongside its host species.

## Spore Biology and Interstellar Travel

McKenna outlined several biological features of Stropharia cubensis spores that could theoretically support interstellar dispersal:

- **Deep purple coloration:** The color needed to absorb the deep ultraviolet end of the spectrum, providing protection against cosmic radiation in the interstellar environment
- **Extremely hard casing:** Among the hardest organic substances known, with electron density approaching that of a metal, capable of withstanding extreme conditions
- **Light weight:** Capable of percolation to the edge of a planet's atmosphere through Brownian motion, the random movement of microscopic particles suspended in fluid
- **Space survival:** Spores can theoretically survive the conditions of interstellar space, including vacuum, radiation, and extreme temperatures

Through interaction with energetic particles at the edge of a planetary atmosphere, a small number of spores could theoretically escape into space. McKenna acknowledged this is an evolutionary strategy where "only one in many billions of spores actually makes the transition between the stars."

## Galactic Time Scales

McKenna calculated the feasibility of spore-based interstellar travel using relatively straightforward physics:

- The galaxy is roughly 100,000 light-years from edge to edge
- An object moving at only one-hundredth the speed of light could cross the galaxy in approximately 100 million years
- Life on Earth is approximately 1.8 billion years old, eighteen times longer than this crossing time
- Therefore, biological spore percolation between stars is a "perfectly viable strategy for biology" on these time scales

He compared this to the principle by which plants migrate into deserts or across oceans, noting that it happens over very long periods but is nonetheless effective. The vastness of geological and galactic time makes strategies that seem implausible on human time scales perfectly feasible when viewed from a cosmic perspective.

## The Fossil Record Problem

McKenna noted a significant anomaly in the fungal fossil record: there are no fungi older than approximately forty million years.

The orthodox explanation is that fungi are soft-bodied and do not fossilize well, but McKenna pointed out that fossilized soft-bodied worms and other benthic marine invertebrates have been found in South African gunflint chert dated to over a billion years.

This gap, he suggested, is consistent with (though not proof of) a relatively recent arrival of fungal life on Earth, perhaps via the interstellar spore mechanism he described.

## The Mushroom as Communication Device

McKenna expressed uncertainty about whether the mushroom itself is the alien intelligence or whether it functions as a "technological artifact" allowing the user to hear an alien intelligence light-years away, potentially using some form of [[Bell's non-locality theorem]] for communication.

He compared this dilemma to "a child who wishes to destroy a radio to see if there are little people inside." The ambiguity between medium and message, between the mushroom as organism and the mushroom as communication channel, is a central tension in the hypothesis.

The mushroom states its own position very clearly, according to McKenna: "I require the nervous system of a mammal. Do you have one handy?"

## The Human Soul Alternative

McKenna offered a significant counter-interpretation: that the mushroom is not extraterrestrial at all, but that "the human soul is so alienated from us in our present culture that we treat it as an extraterrestrial."

He argued that the [[dmt]] trance "would remain more weird and continue to hold more promise for useful information for the human future" than any Hollywood-style alien encounter, regardless of whether the entities are genuinely extraterrestrial or manifestations of the psyche.

This interpretation reframes the entire extraterrestrial hypothesis as potentially a projection of modern humanity's alienation from its own deepest nature.

## The Mushroom's Historical Role

McKenna traced the mushroom's role in human history through several distinct phases:

- **Ancient symbiosis:** Association with cattle and nomadic human populations, ensuring propagation alongside human migration
- **Mesoamerican mushroom cults:** The mushroom was called *teonanacatl* ("flesh of the gods") and was central to indigenous religious practice, representing a form of theophagy rivaling the Christian Eucharist
- **Spanish conquest and suppression:** The Franciscans destroyed the classic mushroom cults, driving them into the mountains of Oaxaca where only fragments survived
- **Rediscovery:** [[Valentina Wasson|Valentina]] and [[R. Gordon Wasson]] found surviving mushroom ceremonies in the 1950s
- **Modern era:** The mushroom's effects were "unleashed" upon the modern world through this rediscovery

## Connection to the Logos

McKenna connected the mushroom to [[Philip K. Dick]]'s concept in Valis of the Logos as a "creature of pure information" that was buried at Nag Hammadi circa 370 A.D. alongside the Chenoboskion Library.

The Logos existed as static information until 1947, when the texts were translated and read, bringing the symbiotic intelligence back to life. The mushroom consciousness, like Dick's Logos, is "a thing of pure information" that comes alive when encountered by human minds.

## Critical Assessment

McKenna acknowledged the speculative nature of the hypothesis, stating "I don't necessarily believe what the mushroom tells me; rather we have a dialogue." He treated the mushroom as "a very strange person with many bizarre opinions," entertaining its claims as one would those of an eccentric friend.

The hypothesis remains untestable by current scientific methods but continues to stimulate discussion about the relationship between consciousness, biology, and cosmology. Its value may lie less in its literal truth than in its capacity to challenge assumptions about the origins and nature of mind.

## Bell's Non-Locality and Interstellar Communication

A particularly speculative element of the hypothesis involves the possible use of quantum non-locality for communication across interstellar distances. McKenna noted that he was "troubled by how freely Bell's non-locality theorem is tossed around," but acknowledged that the alien intelligence encountered in the mushroom trance "does seem to be in possession of a huge body of information drawn from the history of the galaxy."

The possibility that quantum entanglement could enable instantaneous communication across vast distances, bypassing the speed-of-light limit, remains theoretical and controversial. If such communication were possible, it would provide a mechanism by which the mushroom could serve as a real-time communication device rather than merely a repository of ancient information.

## The Problem of Recognizing Contact

McKenna argued that the main problem with searching for extraterrestrials is "to recognize them." Time is so vast and evolutionary strategies and environments so varied that "the trick is to know that contact is being made at all."

The mushroom's claim that "humanity's conceptions of organized intelligence and the dispersion of life in the galaxy are hopelessly culture-bound" challenges the assumption that extraterrestrial intelligence would be recognizable on human terms. If the galaxy has been "an organized society for billions of years," the forms that intelligence takes may be radically different from anything humans can imagine.

## Eros and the Christus

McKenna offered a mythological reading of the mushroom's history in human culture, describing the mushroom as "the body of Osiris, the condensed body of Eros." He suggested that Eros "retreated at the coming of the Christus" and that the Spanish conquistadors, having "conquered the New World and driven its people into cultural fragmentation and diaspora," unexpectedly "came upon the body of Osiris" in the mountains of Mexico.

This mythological framework places the suppression and rediscovery of the mushroom within a larger narrative of cosmic forces, suggesting that the mushroom represents a primordial consciousness that has been alternately embraced and rejected by human cultures throughout history.
