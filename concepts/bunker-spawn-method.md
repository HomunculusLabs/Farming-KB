# Bunker Spawn

From Chapters 9 and 11 of *Mycelium Running* by [[paul-stamets]]. Bunker spawn is one of the most scalable and versatile methods for producing large quantities of mycelium for [[mycorestoration]], [[mycoremediation-basics]], and mushroom cultivation.

## What Is Bunker Spawn?

Wood chip-filled burlap bags that have been inoculated with mushroom mycelium and allowed to fully colonize. Once colonized, these bags become powerful platforms for launching mycelium into the environment at scale.

## Why Bunker Spawn Works

- **Fast colonization**: Inoculated wood chips colonize in 6-12 months vs. 2+ years for uninoculated chips waiting for spore fall
- **Species selection**: You choose which fungi grow, customizing the mycological response to specific environmental needs
- **Antibiotic protection**: Colonized mycelium secretes antibiotics and free radical enzymes that destroy airborne competitor spores
- **Scalability**: Works with commercial filling systems, drippers, and bark blowers with simple modifications
- **Low cost**: Naturalized bunker spawn is less expensive than pure culture spawn
- **Portability**: Bags can be moved to exact locations needing remediation or restoration

## Making Bunker Spawn

### Basic Method
1. Fill burlap sacks with fresh hardwood chips (alder, maple, oak — 1-3 inch pieces)
2. Inoculate using any of these methods (or combine them):
   - Pure culture plug spawn: 100-200 plugs per sack
   - Sawdust spawn or recycled mushroom kit substrate
   - Stem butts placed into the chips
   - Myceliated cardboard sheet spawn
   - Wild mycelium transplant from a mother patch
3. Stack bags 3 high on wooden pallets, thick cardboard, or sheets of plywood
4. Keep in shade; incubate on the ground to limit temperature fluctuation
5. Periodically water; incubate 6-12 months

### Identifying Successful Bags
- **Good bags**: Fused together by thousands of interconnecting rhizomorphs; snow-white color; strong anise-like or species-specific fragrance
- **Poor bags**: Spots of dark green and black mold — remove these
- **Key indicator**: The mycelial mat forming on the burlap surface becomes a "platform of strength" — a membranous sheet of cells that helps project tendrils deep into internal wood chips

### The "Surfing the Mycelial Wave" Rule
Move spawn at the peak of its surging growth. Spot-check bags periodically:
- If growth rate is still increasing → expand
- If growth has peaked and is declining → too late, vigor is lost
- At each step of expansion, follow this rule

## Exponential Expansion

Each colonized bunker spawn bag can generate 10-100 more bags. From stem butt dowel spawn:
- Year 1: 1 stem butt → 10,000 colonized dowels
- Year 2: Each dowel × 1,000-10,000 = 10-100 million dowels (2,000-20,000 lbs)
- Year 3: Bunker spawn from these dowels could remediate many acres

## Deployment Strategies

### Direct Landscape Installation
- Lay bags side by side to create 4-6 inch deep contiguous layer of wood chips on bare ground
- Place mycelial face down (into the ground)
- Mycelium digests wood chips and burlap over time, building soil
- Once established, remove bags and let mycelium continue into native soil

### Riparian Buffer Mycofiltration
- Place bunker spawn bags along watershed peripheries
- Wood chips catch substantial bacterial colonies on day one
- Mycelium provides ongoing filtration of bacteria, viruses, protozoa, pesticides, nitrates
- Especially effective downslope of chicken, pig, and cow operations

### Spent Substrate Recycling
- Spent substrate from gourmet mushroom farms makes excellent bunker spawn base
- Extra crops of shiitake, turkey tail, or reishi can be harvested
- The primary value may be pollution-destroying enzymes at toxic waste sites

### Stump Inoculation
- Place colonized wood chips around and on top of freshly cut stumps
- Cover with additional fresh chips; mycelium leaps off into the stump
- Creates long-term mushroom production (potentially decades)

## Recommended Species for Bunker Spawn

| Species | Use | Notes |
|---------|-----|-------|
| Stropharia rugoso-annulata | Garden giant | Edible, aggressive, excellent for pathways |
| Hypholoma capnoides | Clustered woodlover | Edible, outcompetes Armillaria |
| Trametes versicolor | Turkey tail | Medicinal, mycoremediation, fast-growing |
| Sparassis crispa | Cauliflower | Edible, fights forest blights |
| Agaricus species | Woodland mushrooms | Various, site-dependent |
| Lentinula edodes | Shiitake | Dual purpose: food + spent substrate recycling |

## Combination Method for Maximum Success

If single inoculation methods fail, combine all three:
1. **Spore printing** onto the wood chips
2. **Stem butt inoculation** into the chips
3. **Mycelium transplantation** from a wild patch

"The surge of regrowth is nearly unstoppable" when all three methods are used together.
