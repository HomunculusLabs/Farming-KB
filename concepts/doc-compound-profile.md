# DOC — 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Chloroamphetamine

## Overview

DOC (PIHKAL #64) is a potent psychedelic amphetamine in the DOx family. The 4-chloro substituent gives DOC higher potency and a somewhat different character compared to [[dom-compound-profile]]. DOC has seen sporadic popularity as a designer psychedelic and has been encountered on the blotter paper market, sometimes being misrepresented as [[lsd-compound-profile]] — a dangerous substitution given DOC's far longer duration and higher typical dose. See [[pihkal-introduction-and-methodology]] for the general research context.

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical name:** 2,5-dimethoxy-4-chloroamphetamine
- **PIHKAL number:** #64
- **Class:** Substituted amphetamine (DOx family)
- **HCl melting point:** 187-188 deg C (from MeOH/Et2O); 193-194.5 deg C (via diazotization route)

## Synthesis (from PIHKAL)

The synthesis of DOC begins with 2,5-DMA HCl in water, which is basified and extracted with CH2Cl2 to obtain the free base. The free base is dissolved in glacial acetic acid and cooled to 0 deg C. Liquid chlorine is added via Pasteur pipette — HCl generation is immediately evident. The mixture stirs for 3 hours, then is poured into water, washed with ether, basified, and extracted with CH2Cl2. The resulting oil is dissolved in ether and saturated with HCl gas, yielding a heavy oily precipitate that crystallizes from MeOH/Et2O to give DOC hydrochloride with a published melting point of 187-188 deg C.

An alternative synthesis route proceeds via diazotization, producing DOC hydrochloride with a melting point of 193-194.5 deg C — a notable difference that may reflect polymorphism or purity differences between routes. See [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]] for more on halogenation methodology in this series.

## Dosage and Duration

| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| **Threshold** | ~1 mg |
| **Common dose** | 1.5-3.0 mg |
| **Strong dose** | 3-5 mg |
| **Duration** | 12-24 hours |
| **Onset** | 1-3 hours |

DOC is notably potent at 1.5-3 mg, making it one of the more powerful DOx compounds. Its long duration (12-24 hours) means users should plan for a full day and night. When sold on blotter as "LSD," the much larger amount needed (milligrams vs. micrograms) often means the blotter is unusually thick or large. See [[shulgin-dosage-guidelines]] for the general dosage framework.

## Effects

- Intense visual hallucinations and enhanced pattern recognition
- Deeply introspective and contemplative headspace
- Significant stimulant effects (amphetamine backbone)
- Strong body load with potential for vasoconstriction
- Time dilation
- Possible nausea during onset
- Neither anti-erotic nor anorexic at moderate doses
- Comfortable body feel at plus-three level

## Experience Reports (from PIHKAL)

### DOC at 1.6 mg

"I was hit with a slightly light head; the effects were quite real. Disconnected, and somehow spacey, but this was a favorable spacey which was kind of fun. Somewhere at about the sixth hour I realized I was beginning to drop off a bit, but six hours later yet, there was still a lot of memory. This is a long thing." This report establishes the characteristically extended duration of DOC even at sub-two-milligram doses.

### DOC at 2.4 mg (First Trial)

"This is what I might call an archetypical psychedelic. Everything is there in spades, with few if any of the subtle graces, the 'gentle images' and 'gentle fantasies' of the 2-carbon 2c-b-compound-profile|phenethylamines. This is the works. There are visuals, and there are interpretive problems with knowing just where you really are. The place where nothing makes sense, and yet everything makes sense...eighteen hours, and there is a lot still going on...Anyone who uses this had better have 24 hours at their disposal."

This report is notable for Shulgin's explicit contrast between DOC's full-throttle psychedelic character and the more nuanced quality of the 2-carbon phenethylamines, positioning DOC firmly in the amphetamine psychedelic tradition alongside [[dom-compound-profile]] and [[dob-compound-profile]].

### DOC at 2.4 mg (Second Trial)

"At the sixth hour, still roaring along at a full plus three. Established that this material is neither anti-erotic nor anorexic. The body is very comfortable...With my eyes closed the fantasy is a completely dark screen, lovely and seductive, subtle, and yet light must be deliberately brought in...At 24 hours, sleep was not too great. Dreams were tight, kept defending against trouble; the nervous system was too alert. In a good humor though. This is excellent stuff, but start early in the day."

This second trial adds important details: the absence of libido suppression and appetite loss distinguishes DOC from some other psychedelics, while the 24-hour persistence of alertness confirms the need for careful scheduling.

## The DOx Family Comparison

Shulgin's commentary on DOC provides a direct comparison across the halo-amphetamine derivatives: "It is clear that the three halo-amphetamine derivatives, DOI, DOB and DOC, are all pretty much of the same potency. And all of them very long lived."

| Compound | 4-Substituent | Typical Dose | Duration |
|----------|--------------|-------------|----------|
| DOC | Chlorine | 1.5-3.0 mg | 12-24 h |
| [[dob-compound-profile]] | Bromine | 1.0-3.0 mg | 18-30 h |
| [[doi-compound-profile]] | Iodine | 1.5-3.0 mg | 16-30 h |
| [[dom-compound-profile]] | Methyl | 3-10 mg | 14-20 h |

The halogen series (DOC, DOB, DOI) share remarkably similar potencies, with all active in the low single-digit milligram range — roughly an order of magnitude more potent than the parent compound DOM. The differences between them are more qualitative than quantitative: DOC is described as more "archetypical" in its psychedelic character, while individual subjective reports vary. See [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] and [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] for the pharmacological basis of these similarities.

## Untasted Analogues

Several chemical intermediates in the DOC synthesis pathway were prepared but never tasted, providing structure-activity data points:

- **DON** (2,5-dimethoxy-4-nitroamphetamine): The nitro precursor, prepared en route to the amino and acetamido derivatives. Not tasted.
- **DOA** (2,5-dimethoxy-4-aminoamphetamine, mp 248-250 deg C): The amino analogue. Not tasted. The high melting point suggests strong intermolecular interactions.
- **DOAA** (2,5-dimethoxy-4-acetamidoamphetamine, mp 249-250 deg C): The acetamido derivative. Not tasted. The near-identical melting point to DOA is noteworthy.

The fact that none of these nitrogen-functionalized analogues were tasted limits our understanding of how the 4-position pharmacophore tolerates different substituents. The halo series (Cl, Br, I) proved uniformly active, but the amino and nitro groups remain unexplored in human trials.

## Pharmacology

DOC is a potent 5-HT2A agonist with additional affinity for 5-HT2B and 5-HT2C receptors. The chloro substituent at the 4-position provides greater electronegativity compared to the methyl of DOM, contributing to increased receptor affinity and potency. See [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] and [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]].

## Harm Reduction

- Extremely potent — accurate milligram scales are essential
- Duration of 12-24 hours requires significant time commitment
- Often misrepresented as LSD on blotter — test kits cannot distinguish DOx from LSD
- Do not redose — the long onset means redosing before effects are felt is dangerous
- Start early in the day given the extended duration
- See [[dom-compound-profile]] for general DOx harm reduction principles
