## DOC — 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Chloroamphetamine

## Overview

DOC (PIHKAL #64) is a potent psychedelic amphetamine in the DOx family. The 4-chloro substituent gives DOC higher potency and a somewhat different character compared to [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]] for more on halogenation methodology in this series.

## Dosage and Duration

| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| **Threshold** | ~1 mg |
| **Common dose** | 1.5-3.0 mg |
| **Strong dose** | 3-5 mg |
| **Duration** | 12-24 hours |
| **Onset** | 1-3 hours |

DOC is notably potent at 1.5-3 mg, making it one of the more powerful DOx compounds. Its long duration (12-24 hours) means users should plan for a full day and night. When sold on blotter as "LSD," the much larger amount needed (milligrams vs. micrograms) often means the blotter is unusually thick or large. ## Untasted Analogues

Several chemical intermediates in the DOC synthesis pathway were prepared but never tasted, providing structure-activity data points:

- **DON** (2,5-dimethoxy-4-nitroamphetamine): The nitro precursor, prepared en route to the amino and acetamido derivatives. Not tasted.
- **DOA** (2,5-dimethoxy-4-aminoamphetamine, mp 248-250 deg C): The amino analogue. Not tasted. The high melting point suggests strong intermolecular interactions.
- **DOAA** (2,5-dimethoxy-4-acetamidoamphetamine, mp 249-250 deg C): The acetamido derivative. Not tasted. The near-identical melting point to DOA is noteworthy.

The fact that none of these nitrogen-functionalized analogues were tasted limits our understanding of how the 4-position pharmacophore tolerates different substituents. The halo series (Cl, Br, I) proved uniformly active, but the amino and nitro groups remain unexplored in human trials.

## Pharmacology

DOC is a potent 5-HT2A agonist with additional affinity for 5-HT2B and 5-HT2C receptors. The chloro substituent at the 4-position provides greater electronegativity compared to the methyl of DOM, contributing to increased receptor affinity and potency.
