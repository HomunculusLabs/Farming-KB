## La Quintinie and the Versailles Potager

## Origins of the French Intensive System

The intensive vegetable production system that reached its zenith in
nineteenth-century Paris had its origins two centuries earlier in the
royal potager ([[holzer-kitchen-garden-herbs-and-medicinal-plants]]) became a model of intensive cultivation.

La Quintinie was not merely a gardener but a serious student of plant
physiology. He conducted careful experiments on soil management, pruning,
and the forcing of out-of-season crops. His posthumously published work,
*Instruction pour les Jardins Fruitiers et Potagers* (1690), documented
his methods and remained an important reference for generations of
gardeners.

### Innovations at the Potager du Roi

The Versailles potager introduced several key innovations that would
become foundational to the French intensive system:

1. **Protected cultivation**: La Quintinie used early forms of cold
   frames and glass covers to protect plants from cold, enabling
   the production of fresh vegetables well beyond their normal season.

2. **Soil enrichment**: He understood the importance of deep, rich
   soil and developed methods for building and maintaining soil
   fertility through the application of manure and compost.

3. **Intensive spacing**: Plants were grown much more closely than
   was common at the time, maximizing production per unit area.
