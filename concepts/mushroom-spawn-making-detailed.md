## Overview

Spawn making is the process of expanding mushroom mycelium from a small starting culture to a large volume of colonized grain. The core technique is grain-to-grain (G2G) transfer, where colonized grain from a "master jar" is used to inoculate multiple "receiver jars" of sterile grain. This allows exponential expansion: one jar can become ten, ten can become a hundred. Proper spawn making is the key to scaling cultivation from a single monotub to a full grow room operation while maintaining genetic consistency and low contamination rates.

## Grain-to-Grain Transfer (G2G)

### Concept

G2G transfer works because each colonized grain kernel contains thousands of living mycelium cells. When you transfer colonized grains to fresh sterile grain, each kernel becomes a new inoculation point. Instead of waiting for spores to germinate or a single agar wedge to expand, the mycelium is already growing and immediately begins colonizing the new grain from dozens or hundreds of starting points simultaneously.

### The Math of Expansion

- 1 master jar (inoculated from agar or LC)
- Transfer to 10 receiver jars = 10x expansion
- Each of those 10 jars can transfer to 10 more = 100 jars total
- Third generation: 1000 jars

In practice, most cultivators limit expansion to 2-3 generations. Beyond this, senescence (genetic aging) and contamination risk increase. Each generation introduces a small amount of genetic drift and accumulates risk.

### Master Jar Preparation

The master jar is the foundation of the entire spawn run. It must be absolutely clean:

1. **Inoculate from the cleanest possible source**: Agar wedge or liquid culture. Never use a spore syringe for a master jar intended for G2G expansion — spore syringes carry higher contamination risk and introduce genetic variability.
2. **Use the best grain preparation**: Perfectly hydrated, thoroughly sterilized grain. See [[grain-spawn-preparation]].
3. **Shake at 20-30% colonization**: Redistribute colonized kernels for faster, more complete colonization.
4. **Verify 100% colonization**: Every kernel should be white with mycelium. No uncolonized grain visible.
5. **Wait an extra 3-5 days after full colonization**: This "recovery period" allows the mycelium to fully digest the grain interior, producing a more vigorous culture for transfer.
6. **Smell test**: Open the master jar briefly and smell. It should have a clean, mushroomy or neutral smell. Any sour, sweet, or rotten smell indicates bacterial contamination — do not use for G2G.

### Receiver Jar Preparation

1. Prepare grain as described in [[grain-spawn-preparation]].
2. Fill quart jars to approximately 60-70% full (leave extra headroom for shaking after G2G).
3. Sterilize at 15 PSI for 90-120 minutes.
4. Allow jars to cool completely to room temperature (overnight is safest).
5. Wipe jar lids with 70% alcohol before the transfer.

### The G2G Transfer Process

**Equipment**:
- Master jar (fully colonized)
- Receiver jars (sterilized, cooled)
- Still air box (SAB) or laminar flow hood (strongly preferred for G2G)
- 70% isopropyl alcohol
- Clean spoon or similar transfer tool (optional)
- Nitrile gloves and mask

**Inside the SAB or flow hood**:

1. Arrange all jars inside the sterile workspace. Master jar on one side, receivers on the other.
2. Wipe all jar lids with alcohol.
3. Shake the master jar vigorously to break up the colonized grain into individual kernels.
4. Open the master jar and a receiver jar simultaneously (minimize open time — a few seconds is sufficient).
5. Pour approximately 1/10 to 1/5 of the master jar contents into the receiver jar. A rough guideline is one "handful" or 2-3 tablespoons of colonized grain per receiver jar.
6. Close both jars immediately.
7. Repeat for all receiver jars.
8. Shake each receiver jar to distribute the colonized grains throughout.
9. Remove jars from the sterile workspace and store at 70-78F.

**Timing**: The entire transfer process should take 2-5 minutes for 10 jars. Speed is important to minimize contamination exposure, but accuracy matters more than speed.

### Alternative: Using a Spoon

Some cultivators use a flame-sterilized spoon to transfer grain instead of pouring. This provides more control over the transfer amount but is slower and requires flaming the spoon between each jar.

### G2G Ratios

- **1:10 (master to receiver)**: Standard ratio. One quart master jar inoculates 10 quart receiver jars.
- **1:5**: Faster colonization per receiver jar but less expansion. Good for time-sensitive grows.
- **1:20**: Maximum expansion but slower colonization and higher contamination risk per receiver.

## Liquid Culture Expansion

Liquid culture offers an alternative expansion method that does not require opening grain jars:

1. Draw 5-10 cc of colonized LC into a sterile syringe.
2. Inject into sterilized grain jars through self-healing injection ports.
3. Each LC jar (500ml) can inoculate 50-100 grain jars.

Advantages: No open-air transfer, extremely fast, minimal contamination risk.
Disadvantages: Requires a clean LC, cannot visually inspect the inoculant before use.

See [[mushroom-liquid-culture]] for full LC preparation details.

## Agar-to-Grain (A2G)

Direct inoculation of grain jars from agar plates:

1. Prepare sterilized grain jars.
2. Inside the SAB or flow hood, open the agar plate and the grain jar simultaneously.
3. Use a flame-sterilized scalpel to transfer one or two agar wedges to the grain jar.
4. Close both immediately.
5. Shake the jar to distribute the wedges (optional — some growers prefer to let the wedges sit on top for the first few days).

This is the slowest expansion method (colonization takes 2-3 weeks) but the cleanest because agar allows you to visually confirm culture health before transfer.

## Expansion Ratios and Practical Limits

### Generations

- **Generation 0**: Agar culture or liquid culture (the original genetic source).
- **Generation 1**: Grain jar inoculated directly from agar/LC. This is the "master jar."
- **Generation 2**: Grain jars inoculated via G2G from Generation 1. This is the standard spawn used for monotubs.
- **Generation 3**: Grain jars inoculated via G2G from Generation 2. Possible but with diminishing returns.
- **Generation 4+**: Not recommended. Senescence and contamination risk make this impractical.

### Senescence

Mushroom mycelium has a finite lifespan. Each cell division and growth cycle introduces small errors in DNA replication. Over many generations, the mycelium becomes less vigorous, slower-growing, and more prone to contamination. This genetic aging is called senescence.

Signs of senescence:
- Slower colonization times with each generation.
- Weaker mycelium (thinner, less rhizomorphic).
- Reduced fruiting vigor and yield.
- Increased contamination susceptibility.

To avoid senescence: return to agar regularly. Maintain master cultures on agar slants in cold storage. Start fresh from stored cultures every 6-12 months.

## Spawn Quality Indicators

Good spawn:
- Fully white, no uncolonized grain visible.
- Dense, thick mycelium covering every kernel.
- Earthy or neutral mushroom smell.
- Grains are separate (not clumped together in a wet mass).
- Colonization completed within the expected timeframe.

Bad spawn (do not use):
- Green, blue, black, or colored patches (mold).
- Wet, slimy areas (bacterial contamination).
- Sour or rotten smell.
- Uncolonized patches after the expected colonization time.
- Grains stuck together in a wet mass.
- Mycelium that appears thin, weak, or gray rather than white.

## Shake Timing Strategy

Strategic shaking dramatically speeds spawn colonization:

1. **No shake after inoculation**: Allow the inoculation point to establish (3-5 days).
2. **First shake at 20-30% colonization**: Break up the colonized grain and redistribute. This is the most impactful shake.
3. **Optional second shake at 70-80%**: Less commonly needed but can help even out the last bits of colonization.
4. **Final shake before spawning**: When the jar is 100% colonized, shake vigorously to break kernels apart. This makes the grain easier to mix evenly with bulk substrate.

## Shelf Life of Spawn

- **Room temperature (70-78F)**: Use within 2-4 weeks of full colonization. Mycelium continues to metabolize and will eventually consume available nutrients and begin to decline.
- **Refrigerated (35-45F / 2-7C)**: Viable for 2-4 months. Mycelial metabolism slows dramatically. Allow to warm to room temperature before spawning — cold spawn takes longer to recover.
- **Frozen**: Not recommended. Ice crystal formation damages mycelial cells.

Best practice: plan your grow timeline so that spawn is used within 2 weeks of full colonization for maximum vigor.

## Clean Spawn Workflow Summary

1. Start with a verified clean agar culture or liquid culture.
2. Inoculate a single master grain jar.
3. Verify master jar is 100% clean and colonized.
4. Perform G2G transfer to receiver jars in a sterile environment.
5. Verify receiver jars are clean and colonized.
6. Use receiver jars to spawn bulk substrates (monotubs, bags, etc.).
7. Return to step 1 periodically (every 3-6 months) to maintain genetic vigor.
