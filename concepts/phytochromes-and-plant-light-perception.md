## Phytochromes and Plant Light Perception

Phytochromes are a family of photoreceptor proteins that enable plants to
detect and respond to red and far-red light. They are arguably the most
important class of plant photoreceptors, mediating responses ranging from
seed germination to [[phytochrome-signaling-plant-light-perception]].

## The Red-to-Far-Red Ratio as Environmental Information

The biological significance of phytochrome's dual sensitivity lies in the
nature of sunlight and how it is modified by the environment. Full sunlight
has a relatively high red-to-far-red ratio. However, when sunlight passes
through or reflects off vegetation, chlorophyll in leaves absorbs most of
the red light while transmitting or reflecting far-red light. This shifts
the red-to-far-red ratio dramatically downward.

By sensing this ratio, phytochrome allows plants to detect the presence of
neighboring vegetation even before they are physically shaded. A low
red-to-far-red ratio signals that the plant is surrounded by competitors,
triggering shade avoidance responses.

## Physiological Responses Mediated by Phytochrome

Phytochrome controls an extraordinarily diverse array of plant processes:

- **Seed germination**: Many seeds, especially small-seeded species, require
  red light (perceived by phytochrome) to break dormancy. This ensures
  germination occurs only when light conditions are favorable and the seed
  is close enough to the soil surface.
- **De-etiolation**: When a seedling emerges from soil into light, phytochrome
  triggers the transition from etiolated (pale, elongated) growth to normal
  green development. This includes inhibition of stem elongation, expansion
  of cotyledons, and development of chloroplasts.
- **Shade avoidance**: Detection of a low red-to-far-red ratio triggers stem
  elongation, upward leaf movement (hyponasty), accelerated flowering, and
  reduced branching — all aimed at growing above competing vegetation.
- **Photoperiodic flowering**: Phytochrome measures day length (via night
  [[photoperiodism]] concept page.
- **Circadian clock entrainment**: Phytochrome signals help synchronize the
  plant's internal circadian clock with the external light-dark cycle.
