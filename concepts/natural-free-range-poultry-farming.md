# Natural Free-Range Poultry Farming

## Overview

Natural free-range poultry farming, as practiced in the [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-lacto-bacilli]] (BIM) and bionutrients to maintain health and productivity. Developed by Dr. Han Kyu Cho and demonstrated by Gil A. Carandang at Herbana Farms in the Philippines, this approach has been successfully implemented at the UCSC Farm & Garden in California.

## Housing Design

### East-West Orientation
- Chicken housing is oriented **east-west** to maximize exposure to sunlight throughout the day
- Transparent roofing sections allow direct sunlight penetration into the housing
- Sunlight provides heat, vitamin D synthesis support, and natural disease prevention

### Deep Litter System
The foundation of the natural poultry system is a deep-litter substrate:

1. **Excavation**: Dig the floor area as deep as one yard (approximately 90 cm)
2. **Substrate composition**: Mix soil, compost, and large amounts of sawdust
3. **BIM application**: Spray the substrate with beneficial indigenous microorganisms
4. **Bionutrient supplementation**: Add [[bionutrients-lab-lactic-acid-bacteria-serum]] (the primary BIM component) arrest foul odors and help neutralize toxins. Over time, the mixture of chicken dung and substrate converts into high-quality organic fertilizer — completing the nutrient cycle with zero waste.

### Ventilation Requirements
- **Mandatory exhaust ventilation** in all housing
- Loose substrate with sawdust, wood shavings, and compost allows air movement through the bedding
- Good air movement prevents respiratory disease and ammonia buildup
- Conventional operations with poor ventilation require constant antibiotic use to compensate

## Feeding and Nutrition

### Once-Daily Feeding
- Chickens in the natural system are fed only **once per day**
- This contrasts with conventional operations that provide continuous feed access
- Reduced feeding frequency encourages natural [[poultry-housing-guide]]
- **Healthy, vigorous birds** with good growth rates
- **Zero waste production** — all manure converted to fertilizer
- **Once-daily feeding** with free-range supplementation

### Disease Prevention Mechanism

The system prevents disease through multiple overlapping mechanisms:

1. **Air quality**: Exhaust ventilation and deep litter prevent ammonia buildup
2. **Sunlight exposure**: UV light provides natural sterilization and vitamin D
3. **Clean water**: Reduces pathogen load in the digestive system
4. **Microbial balance**: BIM outcompetes pathogens in the environment and digestive tract
5. **Natural antibiotics**: Ginger-garlic extract provides ongoing antimicrobial protection
6. **Low density**: Free-range access reduces stress and disease transmission
7. **Nutrient assimilation**: Better feed breakdown means stronger immune systems

### Contrast with Conventional Operations

| Factor | Natural System | Conventional System |
|---|---|---|
| Antibiotics | Not needed | Mandatory |
| Vaccines | Not needed | Required |
| Odor control | Microbial (BIM) | Chemical cleaning |
| Feeding | Once daily | Continuous |
| Housing ventilation | Exhaust + deep litter | Often minimal |
| Sunlight | Maximum exposure | Often blocked |
| Waste | Converted to fertilizer | Disposal problem |
| Stocking density | Low (free-range) | High (crowded) |

## Integration with Crop Production

The natural poultry system integrates seamlessly with vegetable production:

- **Fertilizer output**: The deep litter becomes premium organic fertilizer after the flock cycle
- **BIM crossover**: The same lactic acid bacteria cultures serve both poultry and crops
- **Bionutrient sharing**: Fermented plant extracts benefit both plants and animals
- **Kitchen garden synergy**: Chickens can forage in garden areas, providing pest control and additional fertilization

## Scalability

The system has been demonstrated at multiple scales:

- **UCSC Farm & Garden** (California): Small-scale educational farm
- **Herbana Farms** (Philippines): Commercial operation
- **Biodynamic farm in Covelo, California**: Used BIM in biotoilets, demonstrating the microorganism cultures work across applications

The core principles — deep litter, BIM application, proper ventilation, sunlight access, and clean water — scale from backyard flocks to commercial operations.
