## Coffee Rust Biology

Coffee rust is caused by Hemileia vastatrix, a basidiomycete fungus
that produces uredospores, teliospores, and basidiospores. Unlike
many other rust fungi, it lacks spermatium and aeciospore-producing
stages. In black stem rust (Puccinia graminis), spermatia and
aeciospores are produced exclusively on barberry, the alternate host.

Coffee rust has an edited life cycle because the fungus thrives on
coffee alone and has no alternative food source. This deviance from
the typical rust life cycle means that coffee rust cannot be
controlled by eradicating an alternate host, a strategy that has been
effective against black stem rust of wheat. Prevention of coffee rust
is limited to fungicide spraying and the cultivation of resistant
varieties.

## Rice Blast Disease

Magnaporthe oryzae causes rice blast, the most destructive disease of
rice worldwide. The disease can affect all above-ground parts of the
rice plant. When blast attacks a rice paddy, it destroys the field as
if it has been struck by a bomb, accounting for the common name "rice
blast." The fungus usually infects the leaves and spreads as an
invasive mycelium, consuming plant tissues and starving the
developing grain.
