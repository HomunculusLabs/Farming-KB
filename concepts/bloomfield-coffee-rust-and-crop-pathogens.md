Coffee rust, caused by the fungus Hemileia vastatrix, is one of the
most economically significant fungal diseases affecting global
agriculture. Along with other rust fungi and blast diseases, it
illustrates the devastating impact that fungal pathogens can have on
human food security and economies. Nicholas P. Money examines these
crop pathogens in detail in "Mr. Bloomfield's Orchard."

## Coffee Rust Biology

Coffee rust is caused by Hemileia vastatrix, a basidiomycete fungus
that produces uredospores, teliospores, and basidiospores. Unlike many
other rust fungi, it lacks spermatium and aeciospore-producing stages.
In black stem rust (Puccinia graminis), spermatia and aeciospores are
produced exclusively on barberry, the alternate host. Coffee rust has
an edited life cycle because the fungus thrives on coffee alone and has
no alternative food source.

This deviance from the typical rust life cycle means that coffee rust
cannot be controlled by eradicating an alternate host, a strategy that
has been effective against black stem rust of wheat. Without the luxury
of an alternate host whose removal might control the disease,
prevention of coffee rust is limited to fungicide spraying and the
cultivation of resistant crop varieties.

## Economic Importance

The importance of coffee growing to local and national economies in
South and Central America, Africa, and Asia places considerable urgency
upon the introduction of effective control measures. Coffee is one of
the most traded commodities globally, and rust outbreaks can devastate
entire harvests, causing economic hardship for millions of smallholder
farmers who depend on coffee production for their livelihoods.

## Black Stem Rust of Wheat

More wheat is cultivated than any other crop, making black stem rust
caused by Puccinia graminis a celebrity among pathogenic fungi. The
life cycle of Puccinia graminis involves two hosts: wheat and barberry.
Sexual reproduction occurs on the barberry leaf, where spermatia and
aeciospores are produced. The resulting spores infect wheat, producing
uredospores that spread the disease during the growing season.

Losses from black stem rust have been reduced by multiple strategies.
Fungicides like triadimefon, which disrupts membrane function by
inhibiting ergosterol biosynthesis, provide chemical control.
Cultivation of rust-resistant wheat cultivars is a more effective
approach that reduces the cost and environmental pollution associated
with fungicide application. Eradicating barberry bushes from
wheat-growing regions has also been effective, since the absence of the
alternate host retards the evolution of new strains or races of the
fungus.

## Prospects for [[functional-genomics-arbuscular-mycorrhiza]] rice blast looks
as if it has been struck by a bomb, which accounts for the common name
"rice blast." The fungus usually infects the leaves of the rice plant
and spreads as an invasive mycelium, consuming plant tissues and
starving the developing grain.

Sometimes the neck or panicle of the rice plant is ravaged by the
fungus, becoming bleached by the sun and withering to dry straw. Crop
loss due to neck blast is severe. Blast is most prevalent in areas of
intensive agriculture where the soil is supplemented with nitrogen
fertilizers. Crops grown by subsistence farmers who rely upon natural
soil fertility are less likely to be devastated by blast, suggesting
that agricultural intensification may increase vulnerability to this
disease.

## The Appressorium: Nature's Most Powerful Pathogen

The rice blast fungus [[fungal-appressorium-and-infection-structures]] that
is arguably the most powerful infection structure in the fungal kingdom.
Within a few hours of landing on a leaf, the appressorium becomes
blackened with melanin, fills with glycerol, and becomes highly
pressurized. Pressure inside an appressorium can exceed 80 atmospheres,
enabling it to puncture bulletproof vest material.

The mechanism of pressurization is osmotic: water diffuses into the
cytoplasm in response to rising glycerol content, and the melanin
layer traps glycerol from diffusing out. If melanin formation is
inhibited using a chemical fungicide, the appressorium will never
penetrate the leaf. Similarly, transparent appressoria of albino
mutants are ineffective at plant penetration.

## Fungal Diseases as Global Challenges

Fungal pathogens cause tens of billions of dollars in damage to crops
every year. Beyond coffee rust, stem rust, and rice blast, fungal
diseases affect virtually every crop species. Banana Panama disease,
grape powdery mildew, and corn smut are just a few examples of fungal
diseases that threaten food security. Understanding the biology of
