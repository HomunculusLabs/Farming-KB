# Inocybe aeruginascens

Inocybe aeruginascens is a psychoactive mushroom species belonging to the family
Cortinariaceae, first documented as containing psilocybin by Jochen Gartz and
collaborators in Germany. Unlike most psilocybin-containing species in the genus
Psilocybe, this Inocybe species forms mycorrhizal associations with trees rather
than growing saprophytically.

## Discovery and History

The species was first identified as containing psilocybin through analyses
performed by Gartz in Eastern Germany during the 1980s. Its discovery was
significant because it represented the first confirmed psilocybin-producing
species in the genus Inocybe within Europe. Previously, psilocybin had been found
only in members of the families Strophariaceae (Psilocybe, Panaeolus, Conocybe)
and Bolbitiaceae.

The discovery of psilocybin in Inocybe aeruginascens, along with its later
identification in [[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]] compared to species like
[[psilocybe-genus-diversity-and-taxonomy]] species with their distinctive
dark spore prints, Inocybe species have brown spore prints that can resemble
those of many non-psychoactive species.

- **Cap:** 2–5 cm broad, convex to broadly umbonate, silky-fibrillose surface,
  brown to yellowish-brown, sometimes with a greenish tint (aeruginascens =
  "becoming green")
- **Gills:** Adnexed to sinuate, close, grayish-brown becoming brown as spores
  mature, with whitish edges
- **Stem:** 3–7 cm long, 3–8 mm thick, equal or slightly bulbous at base,
  pruinose above, greenish-blue staining at base especially when handled
- **Spore print:** Brown to tobacco brown
- **Spores:** Smooth to nodulose, elliptical to amygdaliform, 8–11 × 5–6 µm
- **Odor:** Spermatic or earthy (characteristic of many Inocybe species)

The species name "aeruginascens" refers to the greenish discoloration that can
develop on the mushroom, particularly at the base of the stem, which may serve
as a preliminary field identification characteristic.

## Bluing Reaction

Inocybe aeruginascens displays only slight bluing at the stems in reaction to
pressure, and this discoloration takes a relatively long time to develop compared
to other psilocybin-containing species. The intensity of the bluing reaction is
among the weakest of all European psychoactive species, making it an unreliable
indicator of psychoactivity.

## Caution

Identification of Inocybe species in the field is extremely hazardous. Several
Inocybe species contain dangerous levels of muscarine and other toxins. The risk
of fatal misidentification is significant. Only experienced mycologists should
attempt to collect or identify any Inocybe species for any purpose. The
greenish tint and weak bluing reaction are insufficient for reliable
