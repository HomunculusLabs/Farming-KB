# Mollison on Permaculture for Low Islands

**Source:** Bill Mollison, Permaculture Design Course, Pamphlet IV (Permaculture on Low Islands), 1981
**Last Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Mollison's detailed design guidance for low coral atolls and cays addresses one of the most challenging environments for human settlement. Atolls have finite water catchment, calcareous sands that form an impermeable platen layer, and constant erosion pressure.

## The Platen Problem

- Phosphates from seabird manure combine with calcareous sands to form calcium triphosphate -- a concrete-like "platen" layer 9 inches to 3 feet below the surface
- Platen is nearly level on top but variable depth below
- Rain follows platen and runs off, preventing percolation to water table
- Only shallow-rooted agriculture possible without intervention
- New platen reforms within 12 months if organic matter is buried under sand

## Grow-Hole Technology

- Shovel away sand, break through platen to brackish groundwater
- Fill hole with humus (imported mulch, compost)
- Plant coconut or other tree; tree roots keep platen open and permeable
- Within 20 years, trees are in production
- Bring worms and compost starter to establish soil biology

## Ring (Circle) Gardens

- Break up platen, heap sand around edge of circle
- Fill with mulch, plant vegetables in mulch with central drip point
- 4-foot diameter circle contains 12 running feet of row crop
- Wick-watering from fibrous organic material extends water 2-2.5 feet laterally
- Center is a pleasant sitting area surrounded by food
- Do NOT use sprinklers -- accelerates platen formation and loses water to evaporation

## Water Management

- Fresh water sits on salt water base (3-5 feet below surface)
- Dry toilet essential; septic tanks will contaminate water table
- Gley ponds lined with papaya, grape, or banana leaves to seal
- Coconut logs line sandy banks to prevent collapse
- Roof catchment with above-ground tanks
- Peripheral settlement keeps waste away from central water lanes

## Coastal Defense

- First-line plants: Casuarina, tamarix, Monterey pine, Norfolk Island pine
- Three genera stand directly in surf: Pemphis, Tournefortia, Scaevola (up to 15 feet high)
- Second line (palms, date palms) provide some yield
- Third line (within 50 feet) gives normal crop yield
- Never remove airstrip vegetation or sea approaches -- islands can be lost to erosion
- Mangroves extend islands into lagoons; create fish trap enclosures

## Mangrove Systems

- Mangroves have the highest biomass turnover of any system
- Genera: Sonnerata, Rhizopodia, Aegiceras, Nipa (palm), Avicenna (to latitude 40)
- Aegicera is superb honey plant; responsible for most quality tropical honey
- Leaf mulch in roots creates food chain supporting shrimp fisheries
- Walkways of two planks allow access without destruction

## Key Species

- Moringa (horseradish tree): edible leaves, flowers, pods; 20-30 foot vegetable hedge
- Many island legumes available (vines, ground covers, small and tall trees)
- Giant tortoises: excellent lawn mowers, convert scraps to fertilizer
- Land crabs: compost shredders, insectivores, reduce wood beetle infestation
