# Salvinorin-A: Kappa Opioid Receptor Pharmacology

Salvinorin-A, the principal psychoactive compound in *[[golden-guide-salvia-divinorum-and-diviner-sage]]*,
represents a unique pharmacological entity among naturally occurring
hallucinogens. Unlike virtually all other known natural and synthetic
hallucinogens — which act primarily as agonists or partial agonists at
the 5-HT2A [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] subtype — salvinorin-A is the first
identified naturally occurring non-nitrogenous kappa-opioid receptor
(KOR) selective agonist.

## Chemical Classification

Salvinorin-A is a neoclerodane diterpene, structurally distinct from
the tryptamine-based hallucinogens (psilocybin, DMT, LSD), the
phenethylamine-based hallucinogens (mescaline), and the
anticholinergic deliriants (atropine, scopolamine). It contains no
nitrogen atoms — an extremely unusual characteristic for a
psychoactive compound of this potency.

This structural uniqueness is pharmacologically significant. The
absence of nitrogen means salvinorin-A does not interact with the
serotonergic system that mediates the effects of most classical
hallucinogens. Instead, its activity is mediated entirely through
the kappa-opioid receptor system.

## The Kappa-Opioid Receptor

The kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) is one of four principal opioid
receptors (mu, delta, kappa, and nociceptin/orphanin FQ). Unlike
the mu-opioid receptor, which mediates the euphoric and analgesic
effects of drugs like morphine and heroin, KOR activation produces
distinctly different effects:

- Dissociation and depersonalization
- Altered perception of reality and self
- Intense visual and spatial distortions
- Dysphoria (in contrast to mu-receptor euphoria)
- Sedation at higher doses

The KOR system is involved in the body's natural response to stress
and is thought to play a role in regulating mood, perception, and
consciousness. Endogenous KOR ligands (dynorphins) are released
during stress and produce dysphoric and dissociative effects that
mirror, in muted form, the salvinorin-A experience.

## Pharmacological Profile

When smoked, salvinorin-A is psychoactive within seconds, with a
duration of approximately 15 minutes at doses of 200-500 micrograms.
The extreme potency of salvinorin-A (active at microgram doses)
is comparable to LSD in terms of dose-response, though the
receptor target and experiential quality are entirely different.

Oral absorption through the buccal mucosa (traditional Mazatec
method of chewing fresh leaves) leads to a less intense but longer-
lasting intoxication of up to approximately 1 hour. The difference
in route of administration significantly affects both intensity and
duration, with smoking producing a much more intense, shorter
experience.

## Comparison with Classical Hallucinogens

The KOR-mediated mechanism of salvinorin-A produces an experience
qualitatively different from classical serotonergic hallucinogens:

- **Onset:** Extremely rapid when smoked (seconds) versus 20-90
  minutes for psilocybin or LSD
- **Duration:** Very short (15 minutes smoked, 1 hour oral) versus
  4-8 hours for most classical hallucinogens
- **Quality:** More dissociative and disorienting, less euphoric,
  more likely to produce feelings of unreality and loss of
  physical coordination
- **Tolerance:** Classical hallucinogens show cross-tolerance with
  each other (LSD-psilocybin-mescaline); salvinorin-A does not
  share this cross-tolerance pattern

The lack of reinforcing properties is notable. Unlike drugs that
activate the mu-opioid receptor, salvinorin-A does not produce
euphoria or compulsive drug-seeking behavior. This is consistent
with the broader KOR pharmacology, where activation tends to be
aversive rather than rewarding.

## Traditional Use and Modern Context

*[[salvia-divinorum]]* is from the mint family (Lamiaceae) and has been
used traditionally by the Mazatec Indians of Oaxaca, Mexico. The
traditional method involves chewing fresh leaves or drinking leaf
juice for buccal absorption of salvinorin-A.

The plant is now grown and sold in the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] for ingestion
by people without any historical ceremonial connection. Modern
consumers also purchase concentrated extracts through Internet-based
companies and smoke shops, which deliver far higher doses than
traditional leaf-chewing methods.

## Safety Considerations

The DEA has not scheduled *Salvia divinorum* as of the Halpern
review, but was closely monitoring it as a possibly emerging drug
of abuse. Harm from salvinorin-A most likely occurs from:

- Inadequate preparation leading to panic reactions during the
  intense, rapidly-onsetting experience
- Dangerous settings — intoxication with any rapidly-acting
  dissociative is dangerous in settings like driving, heights,
  or near water
- Use of concentrated extracts without understanding the dose-
  response curve, leading to overwhelming experiences
- Combining with other substances, particularly alcohol or other
  CNS depressants

The pharmacological literature suggests that salvinorin-A lacks
the reinforcing properties that drive addiction to other drug
classes. The dysphoric and dissociative nature of KOR activation
appears to be self-limiting, with most users not seeking repeated
experiences at high doses.

## Unique Position in Psychopharmacology

Salvinorin-A's discovery as a non-nitrogenous KOR agonist opened
new avenues in psychopharmacology. The KOR system had been
relatively understudied compared to other opioid receptor subtypes,
and salvinorin-A provided a highly selective tool compound for
investigating KOR function.

Research into KOR agonists and antagonists has implications for:
understanding dissociative states, developing new treatments for
mood disorders, investigating the neurobiology of consciousness
and perception, and exploring the [[metzner-ayahuasca-therapeutic-potential]] of KOR
modulation for conditions including substance use disorders and
treatment-resistant depression.
