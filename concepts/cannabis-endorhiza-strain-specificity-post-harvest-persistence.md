## Cannabis Endorhiza Strain-Specificity Persistence Post-Harvest

## Overview

One of the most surprising findings from the Winston et al. (2014)
study of the Cannabis microbiome was that cultivar-specificity in
endorhiza microbial communities persisted even eight weeks after
harvest, when plant tissues were undergoing active senescence and
decay. This has significant implications for understanding how
deeply host genotype shapes root-associated microbial communities
and how long those signatures endure without metabolic input from
the living plant.

## The Two-Experiment Design

The study was conducted in two experiments under different conditions:

**Experiment 1**: Samples collected 8 weeks post-harvest from three
strains (Burmese, Bookoo Kush, Sour Diesel) grown in composted soil
in Vista, California (November 2011). Triplicate endorhiza,
rhizosphere, and bulk soil samples from 9 plants yielded 27 total
samples, with 4 later discarded for insufficient coverage.

**Experiment 2**: Samples collected 2 weeks pre-harvest from two
strains (White Widow, Maui Wowie) grown in two distinct soil types
in Vista and Orange County (August 2012). This yielded 42 samples
from 6 plants with triplicate measurements.

The critical difference was sampling timing. Experiment 1 sampled
long after above-ground biomass removal with no metabolic input
from the living plant. Experiment 2 sampled actively growing,
flowering plants at peak [[weighted-unifrac-strain-clustering-cannabis-endorhiza-community-structure]] endorhiza strain clustering: ADONIS R² = 0.59,
  p = 0.004
- [[cellvibrio-aerobic-cellulytic-endorhiza-cannabis]] bacterium. Its enrichment
indicates active root decomposition during Experiment 1 sampling.
This decay artifact explains several differences: rhizosphere
effects were absent (no root exudation), alpha diversity was
dramatically lower (chao1: 916 vs 3325), and the two-step
selection model's first tier was bypassed.

## Mechanisms of Persistence

Several mechanisms may explain why cultivar-specific signatures
survive tissue decay:

**Structural substrate differences**: Cultivars produce root tissues
with distinct lignin, cellulose, hemicellulose, and suberin
compositions. These polymers decompose at different rates and
selectively favor different decomposer communities.

**Residual [[medicinal-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties]] that could
continue exerting selective pressure during decomposition.

**Pre-established endophyte biofilms**: Communities established
during active growth may resist displacement by soil decomposers,
maintaining cultivar-specific composition after host death.

**Physical root architecture**: Different branching patterns, root
diameters, cortical thickness, and vascular structures create
distinct microenvironments persisting structurally through decay.

## Agricultural Implications

### Sampling Timing

Sampling timing relative to plant life stage is critical for
microbiome diagnostics. Post-harvest sampling captures residual
endophytes, decomposers, and soil opportunists rather than the
active plant-microbiome interface.

### Legacy Effects in Crop Rotation

Cultivar-specific signatures persisting in root debris suggest
crop rotation must account for legacy microbiome effects. Root
material remaining after harvest could influence subsequent
plantings for weeks or months.

### Biomarker Applications

Methylophilus differentials demonstrate microbial profiles can
serve as cultivar biomarkers from degraded tissue, useful in
forensic or regulatory identification contexts.

## Comparison with Active Growth

Weighted UniFrac showed stronger cultivar effects during active
growth (R² = 0.27 pooled across soil types) versus post-harvest,
confirming living roots exert more powerful structuring effects.
The rhizosphere signal, absent post-harvest, was robust in
Experiment 2 (ADONIS R² = 0.13, p = 0.001), where living roots
actively shaped communities through exudation and immune modulation.
