## Salvia divinorum: Diviner's Sage

## The Mazatec Visionary Herb

Salvia divinorum is a perennial herb of the mint family (Labiatae)
known to the Mazatec Indians of Oaxaca, Mexico. It is called
Hierba de la Pastora ("herb of the shepherdess") or Hierba de la
Virgen ("herb of the Virgin"). The Mazatec cultivate it for the
leaves, which are crushed on a metate, diluted in water, and drunk
or chewed fresh for their hallucinogenic properties in divinatory
rituals.

## Botanical Description

Salvia divinorum is a perennial herb 3 feet (1 m) tall or more,
with ovate leaves up to 6 inches (15 cm) long and finely dentate
along the margin. The bluish flowers are borne in panicles up to
16 inches (41 cm) in length, approximately 0.6 inches (15 mm)
long. The plant is cultivated in plots hidden away in forests far
from homes and roads, indicating the sacred and secretive nature
of its use.

The species was described scientifically by Epling and
Jativa-M. and is known only from cultivation. It is believed to
be a cultigen — a plant that exists only in human cultivation
and is unknown in the wild. This suggests a long history of human
association and selection.

## Traditional Use

In Oaxaca, Mexico, the Mazatec Indians employ Salvia divinorum
in divinatory rituals. The fresh leaves are the primary plant part
used. They may be chewed slowly in the mouth, allowing the active
compounds to be absorbed through the oral mucosa, or crushed and
diluted in water to produce a drink. The plant is used by the
