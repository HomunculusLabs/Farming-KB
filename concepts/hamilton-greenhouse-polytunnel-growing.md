## Siting the Greenhouse

Choose a sunny spot, ideally near the house for easy access to utilities
(water and electricity). The orientation (east-west versus north-south)
does not matter greatly for general use. For small gardens, hexagonal
greenhouses are a good choice as they are attractive and efficient in
their use of space.

## Greenhouse Types

### Wooden Frame Greenhouses
Traditional and attractive. Wood provides good insulation. Requires
regular maintenance: treat with preservative and paint periodically.
Wood frames are more expensive but last well if maintained.

### Aluminum Frame Greenhouses
Lightweight, low maintenance, and generally cheaper. Less insulating
than wood but allow more light in. Available in green or natural finish.

### Polytunnels
A cost-effective alternative to glass greenhouses. Polythene sheeting
stretched over a metal framework. Excellent for large-scale vegetable
production. Less permanent and less attractive than glass, but very
practical. Replace the polythene cover every 4-5 years.

## Cold Frames

Cold frames are essential for acclimatizing plants. They provide a
halfway house between the warm greenhouse and the open garden. Use them
for hardening off seedlings before planting out, and for protecting
tender plants in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]].
