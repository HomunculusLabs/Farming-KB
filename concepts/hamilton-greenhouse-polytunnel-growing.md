# Greenhouse and Polytunnel Growing

Growing under glass extends the gardening season significantly, allowing
you to start plants earlier, grow tender crops, and protect from frost.
[[geoff-hamilton]] provides practical advice on [[hamilton-greenhouse-management-organic]] using
organic principles.

## Siting the Greenhouse

Choose a sunny spot, ideally near the house for easy access to utilities
(water and electricity). The orientation (east-west versus north-south)
does not matter greatly for general use. For small gardens, hexagonal
greenhouses are a good choice as they are attractive and efficient in
their use of space.

## Greenhouse Types

### Wooden Frame Greenhouses
Traditional and attractive. Wood provides good insulation. Requires
regular maintenance: treat with preservative and paint periodically.
Wood frames are more expensive but last well if maintained.

### Aluminum Frame Greenhouses
Lightweight, low maintenance, and generally cheaper. Less insulating
than wood but allow more light in. Available in green or natural finish.

### Polytunnels
A cost-effective alternative to glass greenhouses. Polythene sheeting
stretched over a metal framework. Excellent for large-scale vegetable
production. Less permanent and less attractive than glass, but very
practical. Replace the polythene cover every 4-5 years.

## Cold Frames

Cold frames are essential for acclimatizing plants. They provide a
halfway house between the warm greenhouse and the open garden. Use them
for hardening off seedlings before planting out, and for protecting
tender plants in early spring.

lid open on warm days for ventilation and close it at night to retain

## Soil and Growing Media

Use a mixture of good garden loam, well-rotted compost, and sharp sand
for growing in beds or borders inside the greenhouse. For container
growing, use a peat-free compost mixed with perlite or vermiculite for
drainage. Avoid garden soil alone in containers as it can become
compacted and harbor soil pests.

## Watering

Greenhouse plants need regular watering, often daily in summer.
Water in the morning so foliage is dry by nightfall, reducing fungal
disease risk. Use water at ambient temperature rather than cold water
straight from the tap. Automatic watering systems are useful for
holiday periods.

## Common Greenhouse Pests and Organic Control

### Aphids
Prevalent in the warm, sheltered environment. Introduce biological
controls: Encarsia formosa for whitefly, Aphidius for aphids. Also
use yellow sticky traps and insecticidal soap sprays.

### Red Spider Mite
Tiny pests that thrive in hot, dry conditions. Symptoms: fine webbing
and yellow-speckled leaves. Control with the predatory mite
Phytoseiulus persimilis. Increase humidity by damping down.

### Whitefly
Small white insects on leaf undersides. Particularly persistent on
cabbages and other brassicas. Use yellow sticky traps and biological
controls. Good ventilation helps reduce populations.

### Fungal Diseases
Botrytis (grey mold) and powdery mildew are common in poorly ventilated
greenhouses. Ensure good air circulation, avoid overhead watering, and
remove affected material promptly.

## What to Grow Under Glass

### Tomatoes
The classic greenhouse crop. Start seeds indoors in late winter, pot on,
and plant into greenhouse borders or grow bags in spring. Support with
canes and remove side shoots from cordon varieties. Feed with liquid
kelp or comfrey feed every two weeks once fruits form.

### Cucumbers
Grow alongside tomatoes. Train up supports. Keep well-watered and fed.
Some varieties can be grown outdoors but greenhouse cucumbers are more
reliable.

### Peppers and Eggplants
Need warmth and a long growing season. Start early under glass.
Both benefit from pot culture in a free-draining compost mix.

### Melons
Require warm conditions and careful watering. Train up supports and
net the fruits. Provide good ventilation to prevent fungal problems.

### Tender Herbs
Basil, coriander, and other tender herbs thrive under glass and can
be grown year-round for a continuous supply. Protect frost-tender
herbs like bay in the greenhouse over winter.

### Winter Salads
Use the greenhouse for winter salads: lettuce, endive, radishes, and
mustard and cress can be grown through the colder months with
protection.

## Heating

For serious year-round growing, some form of heating is needed.
Paraffin heaters are simple but can produce fumes. Electric heating
is cleaner and more controllable. Only heat to frost-free temperatures
(around 40F/4C minimum) for overwintering. Tender crops need warmer
conditions (55-60F/13-15C minimum).
