# Bionutrients Lab — Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO)

**Updated:** 2026-04-24

Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO) are beneficial microorganisms collected from local environments — primarily forests, bamboo groves, and specific plant rhizospheres — and cultured for use in agriculture. The core principle is that locally adapted microorganisms are more effective than imported or commercial cultures because they are already suited to local soil, climate, and ecological conditions. As described by Gil Carandang, IMO forms the foundation of the [[query-how-do-i-use-beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]] Microorganism (BIM) formulation alongside lactic acid bacteria.

Related preparations include [[bionutrients-lab-fermented-plant-juice-fpj]], [[bionutrients-lab-fish-aminos]], and [[bahay-kubo-imo-collection-and-preparation]] and [[soil-food-web-structure]] and [[teaming-with-microbes-soil-food-web-overview]].

## Why Indigenous Microorganisms

The philosophy behind IMO is rooted in self-reliance and ecological adaptation:

- Starter cultures of beneficial microorganisms have been available commercially, but they remain purchased inputs under someone else's control
- Local, indigenous microorganisms are already adapted to the specific environment
- Farmers can collect, culture, and multiply these microbes themselves, eliminating dependence on purchased biological inputs
- Greater microbial diversity from multiple local sources creates more resilient soil ecologies
- The goal is to "recover the spirit of farmers" and their "independent authority" over farming techniques (per Dr. Han Kyu Cho)

## Forest Beneficial Microorganisms

The primary method for collecting diverse, aged beneficial microorganisms is from local forest environments.

### Site Selection

1. Find a healthy, old, robust tree in your local forest
2. Check the humus litter around the tree
3. The tree should have accumulated deep humus, litter, compost — at least 2 feet to 1 yard deep
4. Through observation, deep humus indicates high soil fertility and microbial biodiversity

### Collection Method

1. Cook rice and place it in a flat container with lid (e.g., plastic lunch box)
2. Add about 1 inch of cooked rice, allowing air space in the container
3. A larger surface area traps more microorganisms
4. Cover container with metal netting or equivalent to protect from animals (rats, etc.)
5. Bury the container in the litter/humus of the forest around the selected tree
6. Leave for 2-10 days (time varies with temperature)
7. Dig up container — white and other color molds on the rice indicate microbial contamination (infection)
8. The cooked rice is now infected with microorganisms from the local forest

### Culturing

1. Add 1/3 amount of crude sugar or molasses to the infected cooked rice
2. After one week, the concoction will look like sticky, liquidy rice
3. Add equal amount of crude sugar or molasses for storage (arrests microbial activities)
4. Store in a cooler area

### Application

1. Dilute serum with 20 parts water
2. Strain and put in a container
3. This diluted form is the basic forest microorganism preparation

### Alternative Collection Method

- Get litter and humus from the forest floor
- Spread sparingly on top of cooked rice in the container
- Forest leaf molds can also be used
- Follow the same culturing procedure

## Bamboo Microorganisms

Bamboo attracts powerful beneficial microorganisms because bamboo roots exude sugary substances that draw in diverse microbial communities.

### Collection Method

1. Follow the same cooked rice container technique
2. Bury the container in bamboo plant litter instead of forest humus
3. Same culturing and application procedures apply

### Specific Use

- Bamboo microorganisms are specifically used for fermenting feeds fed to animals
- The unique microbial profile from bamboo environments is valued for feed fermentation

## Plant-Specific Microorganisms

Beneficial microorganisms from specific target crops can be trapped and cultured to support those same crops.

### Method

1. Select healthy, vigorous plants of the crop you want to grow
