## Cultivar-Specific Microbiome

Cultivar-specific microbiome refers to the phenomenon where different cultivars
(distinct cultivated varieties) of the same plant species maintain significantly
different microbial communities, particularly within their root interiors
(endorhiza). While growing in identical soil, different cultivars selectively
enrich for different microbial taxa from the available soil pool, resulting
in host genotype-dependent differences in [[sphingomonas-wittichii-cannabis-endorhiza-strain-specificity]] was
prevalent in Maui Wowie but not White Widow. This species metabolizes phenazine-
1-carboxylic acid and has been implicated in enhanced soil survival.

All cultivars maintained a core endorhiza community of Pseudomonas,
Oxalobacteraceae, Xanthomonadaceae, Actinomycetales, and Sphingobacteriales.
With the exception of the cellulolytic Cellvibrio, all core members are well-
known endophytic bacteria, primarily within Gammaproteobacteria and
Alphaproteobacteria.

## Mechanisms of Cultivar-Specific Selection

### Root Exudate Composition

Different cultivars produce different blends of sugars, amino acids, organic
acids, phenolics, flavonoids, [[weighted-unifrac-strain-clustering-cannabis-endorhiza-community-structure]] (Mantel
r = 0.863, p = 0.001), though disentangling cannabinoid effects from soil
edaphic effects remains challenging.

### Root Architecture

Differences in root branching density, root hair length, cortex thickness,
and cell wall polysaccharide composition create different physical niches for
microbial colonization. A cultivar with dense fine root hairs provides more
surface area for attachment than one with coarse, sparse roots.

### Plant Immune Responses

The innate immune system recognizes conserved microbial molecular patterns
through pattern recognition receptors. Differences in immune receptor
specificity or sensitivity between cultivars determine which endophytic
microbes are tolerated versus rejected during root colonization.
