# Cultivar-Specific Microbiome

Cultivar-specific microbiome refers to the phenomenon where different cultivars
(distinct cultivated varieties) of the same plant species maintain significantly
different microbial communities, particularly within their root interiors
(endorhiza). While growing in identical soil, different cultivars selectively
enrich for different microbial taxa from the available soil pool, resulting
in host genotype-dependent differences in [[edaphic-factors-microbial-community-structure]].

## The Concept of Cultivar Specificity

A "cultivar" is a plant variety produced by selective breeding. Cultivars
within a species differ in growth habit, disease resistance, secondary
metabolite profiles, root architecture, and exudate composition. These
differences extend to microbial communities: two genetically distinct cultivars
growing side by side in the same field support measurably different communities
of bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms in and around their roots.

This specificity is most pronounced in the endorhiza and is primarily a
difference in relative abundance rather than outright presence or absence.
Different cultivars host largely overlapping microbial species but maintain
them at different population levels, creating distinct community "fingerprints"
associated with each cultivar.

## Evidence from Cannabis Cultivars

The clearest evidence comes from Cannabis research, where five cultivars
(Sour Diesel, Bookoo Kush, Burmese, White Widow, Maui Wowie) were studied
using 16S rRNA gene sequencing. Soil type was the strongest overall determinant
of community composition, but cultivar identity was highly significant for
endorhiza structure. When experiments were pooled, cultivar explained 17.8%
of variance in unweighted analysis and 30.1% in weighted analysis.

Specific cultivar differences were striking: Methylophilus comprised 13% of
the endorhiza in Bookoo Kush, only 0.13% in Burmese, and was absent from Sour
Diesel. In White Widow vs. Maui Wowie comparisons, [[sphingomonas-wittichii-cannabis-endorhiza-strain-specificity]] was
prevalent in Maui Wowie but not White Widow. This species metabolizes phenazine-
1-carboxylic acid and has been implicated in enhanced soil survival.

All cultivars maintained a core endorhiza community of Pseudomonas,
Oxalobacteraceae, Xanthomonadaceae, Actinomycetales, and Sphingobacteriales.
With the exception of the cellulolytic Cellvibrio, all core members are well-
known endophytic bacteria, primarily within Gammaproteobacteria and
Alphaproteobacteria.

## Mechanisms of Cultivar-Specific Selection

### Root Exudate Composition

Different cultivars produce different blends of sugars, amino acids, organic
acids, phenolics, flavonoids, [[plant-defense-chemistry-and-secondary-metabolites]] from their roots.
These exudates serve as carbon and energy sources, and their specific
composition determines which microbes grow near the root. This is likely the
most important mechanism for cultivar-specific selection.

### Secondary Metabolite Profiles

In Cannabis, different cultivars produce dramatically different cannabinoid
and terpene profiles, some of which are exuded from roots. Cannabinoid data
showed significant correlations with [[weighted-unifrac-strain-clustering-cannabis-endorhiza-community-structure]] (Mantel
r = 0.863, p = 0.001), though disentangling cannabinoid effects from soil
edaphic effects remains challenging.

### Root Architecture

Differences in root branching density, root hair length, cortex thickness,
and cell wall polysaccharide composition create different physical niches for
microbial colonization. A cultivar with dense fine root hairs provides more
surface area for attachment than one with coarse, sparse roots.

### Plant Immune Responses

The innate immune system recognizes conserved microbial molecular patterns
through pattern recognition receptors. Differences in immune receptor
specificity or sensitivity between cultivars determine which endophytic
microbes are tolerated versus rejected during root colonization.
