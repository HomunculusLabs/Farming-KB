# Latua Pubiflora (Arbol de los Brujos)

Latua pubiflora, known in Spanish as the arbol de los brujos ("sorcerers'
tree") or latue, is one of the most potent and feared psychoactive plants
of [[entheogenic-folk-religious-ceremonies]], Latua pubiflora has no cult or ritual surrounding its
use. Instead, it is the closely guarded tool of individual medicine men
who employ it for specific, often dangerous purposes.

The absence of formalized ritual surrounding Latua use is unusual among
New [[datura-stramonium]]
(jimsonweed), [[plants-of-the-gods-atropa-belladonna-deadly-nightshade]]), and [[henbane-hyoscyamus-niger-ethnobotany]]
(henbane). The presence of these well-known deliriant compounds confirms
the pharmacological mechanism underlying Latua's dramatic effects.

Hyoscyamine and scopolamine are anticholinergic compounds that block the
action of acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors in the brain and peripheral
nervous system. This blockade produces a characteristic syndrome of effects
including dry mouth, dilated pupils, tachycardia, urinary retention,
hyperthermia, and most significantly from a psychoactive perspective, a
state of delirium characterized by realistic but bizarre hallucinations,
confusion, disorientation, and inability to distinguish fantasy from
reality. The deliriant state produced by tropane alkaloids is
fundamentally different from the hallucinogenic state produced by classical
hallucinogens like psilocybin or mescaline.
