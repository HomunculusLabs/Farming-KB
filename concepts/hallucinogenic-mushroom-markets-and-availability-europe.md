# Hallucinogenic Mushroom Markets and Availability in Europe

## Overview

The European market for hallucinogenic mushrooms has undergone significant evolution
since the late 1990s, transforming from a largely underground and foraging-based activity
into a more visible retail and online trade. The emergence of dedicated retail outlets,
the growth of internet commerce, and the increasing availability of cultivation supplies
have all contributed to broader access to psilocybin-containing mushrooms across the
continent. This page describes the key features of the European mushroom market as
documented by the EMCDDA up to 2006.

## Historical Development of the Market

### Late 1990s: Smartshops in the Netherlands

The commercial market for [[legal-responses-to-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-in-the-european-union]] Europe can be traced most directly
to the emergence of so-called "smartshops" in the Netherlands during the late 1990s.
These retail establishments, operating within the toleration framework that also
governed cannabis coffeeshops, began openly marketing fresh and dried psilocybin
mushrooms alongside other psychoactive products. The Dutch smartshop model provided a
visible template for retail distribution that would later influence market development
in other European countries. The Netherlands served as both a domestic market and an
export source for mushrooms and mushroom products.

### Early 2000s: Expansion to the United Kingdom

During the early 2000s, the market for hallucinogenic mushrooms expanded notably in the
United Kingdom. A growing number of head shops, alternative lifestyle retailers, and
specialist mushroom vendors began stocking mushroom products. The UK market developed
its own distribution networks, with shops in urban centres and increasing online sales.
By the mid-2000s, mushrooms were widely available through both physical and internet
retailers in the UK, prompting eventual legislative attention.

## Products Available in the Market

### Fresh and Dried Mushrooms

The core products in the hallucinogenic mushroom market are fresh and dried fruiting
bodies of psilocybin-containing species. Fresh mushrooms are sold by weight and are
generally more potent per gram than dried material due to their higher water content.
Dried mushrooms have a longer shelf life and are easier to transport and store, making
them the predominant form for internet and mail-order sales. Both forms are typically
consumed orally, either eaten directly or brewed into a tea.

### Growkits, Spawnbags, and Mushroom Prints

Beyond ready-to-consume mushrooms, the European market has developed a substantial
trade in cultivation supplies. Growkits — pre-inoculated substrate containers that allow
users to cultivate their own mushrooms with minimal expertise — have become widely
available. Spawnbags containing mycelium and mushroom prints (collections of spores for
home cultivation) are also sold. These products occupy a legally ambiguous space in many
jurisdictions, as they contain no controlled substances per se but are clearly intended
for the production of psychoactive material.

## Brand Names and User Terminology

Hallucinogenic mushrooms are known by a variety of names across European countries:

- **Magic mushrooms** — the most widely used English-language term across Europe
- **Paddos** — the common Dutch term, derived from the Spanish "hongos pedros"
- **Shrooms** — colloquial English term, particularly prevalent in the UK
- **Zauberpilze** — German term meaning "magic mushrooms"
- **Philosopher's stone** — a commercial name for sclerotia (truffle-like hardened
  mycelial masses) of *Psilocybe tampanensis* and related species

Sclerotia, sometimes marketed as "philosopher's stones" or "truffles," are dense masses
of mycelium containing psilocybin and psilocin that can be cultivated without producing
traditional fruiting bodies. They have been particularly associated with the Dutch
smartshop market and were sometimes positioned in a different legal category.

## Retail Context: "Herbal Highs" Market

Hallucinogenic mushrooms have commonly been sold within the broader "herbal highs" or
"legal highs" retail category. Smartshops and head shops typically stock mushrooms
alongside other psychoactive plant products, including *Salvia divinorum*, herbal ecstasy
formulations, and various novelty items. This co-marketing places mushrooms within a
commercial context that normalises their use as one of several available psychoactive
options. The "herbal highs" framing may also serve to obscure the potent pharmacological
nature of psilocybin mushrooms, potentially contributing to uninformed or careless use.

Products sold in retail settings are sometimes accompanied by user information sheets,
dosage guidelines, and harm-reduction literature, although the quality and accuracy of
this information varies considerably between retailers.

## Internet Sales and Online Communities

### Growth of Internet Commerce

Since the late 1990s, the internet has played an increasingly important role in the
European mushroom market. Online retailers offer fresh and dried mushrooms, growkits,
spores, and cultivation equipment for sale and delivery across national borders. Internet
sales have expanded the geographic reach of the market considerably, making mushrooms
available in countries where physical retail outlets do not exist. The semi-anonymous
nature of online transactions has also complicated enforcement efforts.

### Key Websites and Online Resources

Several English-language websites have served as central hubs for information exchange
and commerce related to hallucinogenic mushrooms:

- **Erowid** — a comprehensive online resource providing information on psychoactive
  substances, including detailed user experience reports and dosage guidance
- **Lycaeum** — an online library and community focused on psychoactive plants and
  compounds
- **The Shroomery** — a dedicated [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-cultivation-and-growkit-market]] identification community
  with extensive cultivation guides and discussion forums
- **Mycotopia** — an online community for mushroom cultivation enthusiasts

Beyond these major English-language hubs, country-specific websites and forums have
developed across Europe, providing localised information in national languages. These
sites facilitate the sharing of cultivation techniques, identification guidance, vendor
reviews, and user experience reports.

## Wild Collection and Cultivation

### Magic Mushroom Hunting

Information on the wild collection [[health-risks-of-hallucinogenic-mushrooms]] is widely shared through
online forums and printed field guides. Several species of *Psilocybe* and other
psilocybin-containing genera grow wild across Europe, particularly in autumn months.
However, despite the cultural visibility of mushroom hunting, the EMCDDA reports that
the majority of recreationally used mushrooms in Europe are cultivated rather than
wild-picked. Cultivation provides more consistent potency, reliable supply, and reduces
the risks associated with misidentification of toxic species.

### Predominantly Cultivated Supply

*Psilocybe cubensis* is the most commonly cultivated and marketed species in the European
recreational market. Various named "strains" or varieties are sold, often reflecting their
geographic origin or the conditions under which they were originally collected. Common
commercial varieties include Mexican, Thai, and Colombian strains, each with somewhat
different growth characteristics and reported subjective profiles. The predominance of
cultivated *P. cubensis* reflects its relatively straightforward cultivation requirements
and reliable fruiting compared to other psilocybin-containing species.
