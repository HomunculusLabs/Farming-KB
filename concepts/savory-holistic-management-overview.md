## Holistic Management Overview

Holistic Resource Management (HRM) is a decision-making framework developed by Allan Savory that treats people, their economy, and their environment as a single interconnected whole. Rather than managing land, livestock, or finances in isolation, HRM requires managing the entire system holistically—recognizing that no part can be healthy if the whole is not.

## The Four Missing Keys

Savory identified four insights that conventional resource management had overlooked:

1. **Holism**: Nature functions as interconnected wholes, not as isolated parts. Managing any part without considering the whole leads to unintended consequences.

2. **Brittle vs. Nonbrittle Environments**: Environments respond fundamentally differently to the same management tools depending on their position on a brittleness scale. Practices that heal nonbrittle environments can destroy brittle ones.

3. **The Role of Herding Animals**: In brittle environments, large herding animals concentrated by predators are essential to soil health. Their hooves break capped surfaces, trample old material into mulch, and create conditions for new seedlings.

4. **The Time Dimension**: Overgrazing is not a function of animal numbers but of the time plants are exposed to animals and the time allowed for recovery. This insight, drawn from André Voisin"s work, revolutionizes grazing management.

## Plan-Monitor-Control-Replan

Achieving any goal requires a continuous process. "Plan" becomes a twenty-four-letter word: plan-monitor-control-replan. Once a plan is made it is monitored. If deviation occurs, control is instituted. When events beyond our control demand it, we replan.

This iterative process is not merely a management technique but a fundamental shift in thinking. It acknowledges the complexity of natural systems and our incomplete understanding of them. We always assume we might be wrong, even when a tool has passed all testing guidelines.

## The Holistic Context

In later formulations, the three-part goal is referred to as the "holistic context" or "holistic context"—a broader term that better captures the idea of a shared vision encompassing all people involved in management decisions. The concept remains the same: quality of life, forms of production, and future resource base defined interdependently.

This context is not static. It evolves as understanding deepens and circumstances change. The model provides for periodic review and revision, ensuring that the goal remains relevant and that all stakeholders maintain ownership.

## Common Nongoals

Savory identifies several common errors that produce what he calls "nongoals"—statements that appear to be goals but actually lead to mismanagement:

1. **Production-only goals**: Maximizing yield without reference to ecosystem health or quality of life—the primary error of industrial agriculture
2. **Tool-as-goal errors**: Declaring "remove all livestock" or "use no chemicals" confuses tools with goals. The landscape goal should determine which tools to use
3. **Symptom-addressing goals**: Goals like "eradicate brush" or "eliminate a pest species" address symptoms. Brush encroachment is often a symptom of overrest in brittle environments. Addressing symptoms guarantees perpetual work

## Universal Application

HRM is not limited to ranching or agriculture. It applies to national parks, forestry, watershed management, urban planning, and policy development. The model can be used in diagnostic mode (analyzing what went wrong), policy analysis mode (predicting outcomes of proposed actions), and research orientation mode (identifying where knowledge is most needed).
