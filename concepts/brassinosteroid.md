# Brassinosteroid

Brassinosteroids are a class of polyhydroxylated steroidal plant growth
regulators. First isolated from *Brassica napus* (rapeseed) pollen in 1979 by
Grove and coworkers, they are now recognized as the sixth major class of plant
hormones alongside auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, ethylene, and abscisic
acid. Approximately 30 naturally occurring brassinosteroids have been
identified across a wide range of higher plants including Angiospermae,
Gymnospermae, and even a green alga. They play crucial roles in cell
elongation, cell division, seed germination, vascular differentiation,
reproductive development, and stress responses.

## Chemical Structure

Brassinosteroids are structurally characterized by a steroidal skeleton with
multiple hydroxyl groups. The most biologically active form is brassinolide, a
C28 steroid with hydroxyl groups at positions 2, 3, 22, and 23, along with a
lactone bridge between C-22 and C-23.

The compound studied by Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt was 22S,23S-homobrassinolide,
a synthetic brassinosteroid with an additional methylene group (C29) in the
side chain compared to brassinolide. It was prepared from stigmasterol, a
common plant sterol found in soybeans and other legumes. The stereochemistry at
positions 22 and 23 (both S) was confirmed as essential for biological
activity.

## Physiological Effects in Plants

Brassinosteroids promote growth through complex physiological mechanisms.
Their primary effects include:

- **Cell elongation**: By regulating gene expression related to cell wall
  loosening and expansion, particularly through activation of xyloglucan
  endotransglucosylase/hydrolase (XTH) enzymes and expansins
- **Cell division**: Stimulating the cell cycle in meristematic tissues
  through upregulation of cyclin-dependent kinases
- **Vascular differentiation**: Promoting xylem differentiation and phloem
  development, essential for water and nutrient transport
- **Stress tolerance**: Enhancing resistance to heat, cold, drought,
  salinity, and pathogen attack through modulation of antioxidant systems
  and stress-responsive gene expression
- **Reproductive development**: Influencing pollen tube growth, fruit set,
  and seed yield, making them attractive for agricultural application

Promising results have been reported in the application of brassinosteroids to
improve crop yields across diverse species including rice, wheat, maize, and
various vegetables. Their anti-stress properties make them particularly
valuable for agriculture under suboptimal conditions, where environmental
stress factors limit productivity.

## Biosynthesis and Signaling in Plants

Brassinosteroids are synthesized from campesterol through a series of
oxidation and reduction steps involving cytochrome P450 enzymes. The pathway
was elucidated largely through the study of brassinosteroid-deficient mutants
in Arabidopsis, such as det2 (de-etiolated 2) and dwf4 (dwarf 4), which
exhibit characteristic dwarf phenotypes that can be rescued by brassinosteroid
application.

The brassinosteroid signaling pathway involves a membrane-localized receptor
kinase (BRI1) that perceives brassinosteroids at the cell surface, triggering
a phosphorylation cascade that ultimately regulates gene expression through
transcription factors of the BES1/BZR1 family. Mutations in BRI1 also produce
dwarf phenotypes, confirming its central role in brassinosteroid perception.

## Effects on Fungi: The Psilocybe cubensis Study

A notable 1990 study by Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt (published in
*Naturwissenschaften*) was the first to investigate brassinosteroid activity
on fungi. Prior to this work, no studies on the occurrence or activity of
brassinosteroids in fungi had been published.

The researchers tested 22S,23S-homobrassinolide at a concentration of 0.01 ppm
on the subtropical fungus *Psilocybe cubensis* (Earle) Singer. Two cultivation
systems were used: 6% malt agar, and a horse dung and rice grain mixture in
water.

### Mycelial Growth on Agar

On malt agar supplemented with 5 g/L KH2PO4, the brassinosteroid-treated
cultures exhibited mycelial growth two to three times faster than untreated
controls across ten experimental runs. This dramatic acceleration suggests
that brassinosteroids can directly stimulate fungal metabolism, possibly
through interactions with membrane sterol receptors or signal transduction
pathways conserved between plants and fungi.

The consistency of the effect across all ten runs indicates a robust biological
response rather than stochastic variation.

### Fruiting Enhancement on Dung and Grain Substrate

On the dung and grain substrate, brassinosteroid treatment produced several
marked improvements in fruiting body production:

- **Earlier fruiting**: First flush appeared at 3 to 3.5 weeks after
  inoculation, compared to 4 to 5 weeks in controls, representing a 25 to
  30% reduction in time to first harvest
- **Increased first-flush yield**: First flush produced 4 to 7 mushrooms
  versus 1 to 3 in controls, a two- to three-fold increase
- **Higher overall dry mass**: Treated cultures yielded 3.4 to 3.9 g dry
  weight per 10 g substrate, compared to 2.3 to 2.9 g in controls,
  representing a 30 to 50% increase in biological efficiency
- **Reduced abnormalities**: Formation of incomplete fruit bodies, which are
  normally present in mushroom cultivation and represent wasted metabolic
  effort, was almost completely suppressed
