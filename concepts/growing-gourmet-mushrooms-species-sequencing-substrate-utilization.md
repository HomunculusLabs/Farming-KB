# Species Sequencing and Substrate Utilization

Species sequencing is the practice of introducing multiple mushroom species in
succession on the same substrate to maximize total biological value extraction.
Developed and championed by Paul Stamets, this technique recognizes that
different fungi produce different enzyme suites and therefore decompose
different components of organic material. By staging species in sequence, the
cultivator can extract far more nutritional and ecological value from a given
volume of raw material than any single species could achieve alone.

## Secondary Decomposers: Extracting Residual Value

After the primary species has completed fruiting — typically after 2-3 flushes
over 4-8 weeks — the spent substrate becomes the growing medium for a
secondary decomposer. Stropharia rugoso-annulata (wine cap or King Stropharia)
is Stamets' preferred secondary decomposer. This robust species thrives on
partially decomposed straw and wood chips, producing large, burgundy-capped
mushrooms that are both edible and excellent for soil building.

The transition from primary to secondary decomposer requires some preparation.
The spent substrate should be broken up and mixed with fresh woody material
(wood chips or sawdust) to provide additional carbon and improve structure.
This mixture is then inoculated with Stropharia spawn and installed in an
outdoor bed. Stropharia is notably tolerant of soil contact and can be grown
directly in garden beds, pathways, or erosion-prone areas.
