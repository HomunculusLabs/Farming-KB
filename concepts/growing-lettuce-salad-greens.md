# Growing Lettuce and Salad Greens

Updated: 2026-04-18

Steve Solomon provides detailed guidance on lettuce cultivation, emphasizing that competition is the enemy of quality lettuce and that the difference between excellent and mediocre lettuce lies primarily in soil conditions and spacing.

## The Importance of Rapid Growth

To achieve the best eating quality, lettuce must grow rapidly. The root stores water, but when lettuce has to survive using its moisture reserve, new growth stops and the leaves become bitter. Any leafy plant needs nitrates to grow; one that should grow as rapidly as lettuce demands high levels of available nitrogen. Preparing its growing bed, consider lettuce a high-demand crop.

## Growing Details

1. **Fertile soil** is the most important requirement besides proper thinning.
2. **Competition slows growth** -- don't let it happen.
3. **Thin progressively, carefully, and relentlessly.**
4. Most varieties don't tolerate much heat -- some won't even germinate in hot soil, much less grow.

## Nutrition and Quality

- Dark green looseleaf varieties grown on properly balanced, mineral-rich soils have been tested at nearly 20% protein, almost as much as some forms of flesh food.
- Unlike meat, raw vegetable protein is more digestible than cooked flesh.
- Iceberg and other ball-heading types with blanched cores, lacking high-protein chlorophyll, are much less healthful.

## Planting Strategy

Solomon's approach to continuous lettuce supply:

- Grow single lettuce heads to full size so their small inner leaves expand, then cut the entire head at the stem.
- One looseleaf head a day feeds a family. Three weeks' worth of lettuce can be harvested in a single day.
- Plant several varieties of differing maturities sown at the same time.
- Also make repeated sowings about three weeks apart.
- Three weeks is about the longest a sowing of assorted varieties remains in prime eating condition in [[stamets-cultivator-agaricus-bitorquis-warm-weather-cultivation]].

## Mesclun Critique

Growing mesclun (densely spaced salad greens mixture cut repeatedly like mowing a lawn) has become popular. Catalogs suggest harvesting from a single sowing for months. Solomon believes that after the first cutting, mesclun usually results in second-rate salads. If you spread a strongly seasoned fatty dressing on greens, you won't notice how bitter and dry they have become.

## Succession Planting

- When the entire garden grows quickly (spring), make small plantings every three weeks.
- When the garden grows much more slowly (late summer), make larger plantings.
- In warm weather, each lettuce sowing lasts only about three weeks in prime condition.

## Pests and Diseases

- [[query-how-to-get-rid-of-slugs-and-snails-organically]] are the main pests in cool, moist conditions.
- Aphids can colonize in warm weather.
- Rapid growth is the best defense -- stressed, slow-growing lettuce is most vulnerable.

## Freezing Tolerance

- No variety will withstand short-term freezing the way kale does.
- Spinach is much more cold-hardy than lettuce.

## Seed Saving

Lettuce is a self-pollinated annual that grows vegetatively for a time, then puts up a flower stalk (called "bolting"). Each flower capsule forms only a few seeds.

- Success depends on warmth and summer length. In mild-winter climates, sow seed crop in mid-autumn and overwinter outdoors.
- In harsher locations, [[query-how-do-i-create-a-seed-bank-and-start-seed-saving]] as early as possible and set out the earliest possible date.
- Heads must be fully developed and probably bolting before summer heat arrives.
- After about 6-8 weeks past the summer solstice, plants forming seed switch from ripening seed to growing vegetatively again. If the seed crop hasn't matured by then, it won't.
- Seeds ripen irregularly and tend to shatter. Cut branches as soon as you see mature seeds and let them finish drying.
- Winnow to clean. Try to let at least two plants of each variety make seed every year.
