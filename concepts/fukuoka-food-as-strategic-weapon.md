## Fukuoka Food as Strategic Weapon and the Seed War

In "The Road Back to Nature," Fukuoka delivers a sharp critique of how food has become a tool of geopolitical power, particularly focusing on America"s use of food exports as leverage over other nations and the emerging "seed war" in rice genetics.

## America"s Food Strategy

Fukuoka observed that America, while appearing powerful and affluent, is actually a country in great danger. Depending on how it uses the food it produces, such a large food-producing nation is capable of either saving the world or throwing it into chaos.

The core problem: American food is produced with oil. Transported elsewhere and sold for profit, this food serves as a pillar of the national economy. Food today is used as a strategic weapon.

## The Seed War

A particularly prescient section concerns the race to develop high-yielding rice varieties. Fukuoka observed:

- Both the U.S. and Japan were developing lines of superhigh-yielding rice
- These varieties could dramatically increase starch production
- The real motivation was not feeding people but geopolitical leverage
- Control of seed technology meant control of food supply
- The "seed war" was a new form of the same food-as-weapon strategy
