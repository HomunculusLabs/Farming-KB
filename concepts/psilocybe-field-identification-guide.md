### *Psilocybe caerulescens*

Unlike the other two species, *P. caerulescens* grows on stream and river banks rather than dung. Found throughout the entire southern United States.

**Cap**: 1 to over 3 inches wide. Cone-shaped when young, gradually expanding to the traditional convex-to-flat mature shape with a downward-curving margin. The surface is smooth and sticky, particularly when young. Translucent when moist, with dark gill lines visible through the cap. In mature specimens, the margin appears lighter or darker than the center, forming a ring with an irregularly shaped separation. Young specimens are deep green to black, fading to cinnamon to rust with age — often retaining a faded olive-green color.

**Gills**: Closely spaced, wide, light-cinnamon to light brown when young, becoming dark brown to black with age. Gill edges are lighter in color.

**Stem**: 1.5 to 4 inches tall, up to 1/2 inch thick depending on specimen size. Usually very even, hollow, and smooth at the top with thick fibrous hairs covering the rest of the way to the even base. The veil falls away very early in the mushroom's life. The stem is fibrous and tough.

**Flesh**: Off-white to yellowish, occasionally with light brown tints in the cap. Has a strong grain-like odor and turns blue readily, particularly after handling.

## Cultivation Basics

Mushroom cultivation requires specially prepared compost, most commonly made from horse manure, wheat straw, and added chemicals. The composting process gene[[ingham-field-guide-compost-tea-application-rates|rates]] internal heat up to 180°F, which kills competing spores, insects, larvae, and molds. Proper compost should ball when squeezed but show no excess water, with straw providing aeration and added nutrients.

**Key cultivation steps**:

1. **Compost preparation**: Materials are piled, soaked, and allowed to decompose until the mixture takes on an autumnal woodland odor
2. **Spawn preparation**: Horse manure enriched with malt-extract sugar is shredded, composted, packed loosely into wide-mouth jars, and inoculated with fresh cap tissue
3. **Mycelial colonization**: Mycelium grows throughout the inoculated medium over several weeks
4. **Bed establishment**: Spawn chunks are inserted into prepared compost beds at regular intervals
5. **Casing**: A 1-inch layer of sterilized soil is applied and lightly watered (never flooded — excess water kills mycelium)
6. **Fruiting**: Mushrooms grow through the casing layer, gaining structural support from the top layer

Beds should be approximately 12 inches deep in easily manageable containers. Traditional compost lasts 4–6 months, yielding two or more pounds of mushrooms per square foot.

## Alkaloid Extraction

Psilocybin can be extracted by drying specimens, grinding to powder, and soaking in methyl alcohol for several days. The alcohol is strained and evaporated in a shallow dish, and the residue is scraped and stored cool. The process can be repeated with pentane for further refinement. All solvent must be completely evaporated before ingestion, and potency should be carefully assessed.
