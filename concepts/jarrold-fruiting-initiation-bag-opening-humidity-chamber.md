# Mushroom Fruiting Initiation: Bag Opening Timing and Humidity Chamber Transfer (Jarrold Method)

## Overview

The transition from [[mushroom-fruiting-chamber-setup]] body production is the most
environmentally sensitive phase of the Jarrold boil-a-bag cultivation method. After
the mycelium has fully colonized the substrate, the grower must create the specific
environmental conditions that trigger the mushroom's reproductive cycle — primarily
high humidity, fresh air exchange, and indirect light. The Jarrold method uses a
simple fish-tank-based humidity chamber and describes clear visual cues for when to
open the bags and transfer them to the [[substrate-consistency-water-absorption-grass-seed]], and no visible uncolonized straw. Jarrold states the
criterion simply: "The Mycelium has totally colonized the bag."

### Filter Can Removal
The first step in preparing for fruiting is removing the filter can from the top of
the bag. Throughout colonization, the filter can (containing vermiculite or rockwool)
has provided gas exchange while filtering out airborne contaminants. Once the
mycelium is fully established, this barrier is no longer needed and would actually
impede the increased air exchange required for fruiting.

## The Humidity Chamber Setup

### Fish Tank Chamber
Jarrold's humidity chamber is built from a standard 4-foot by 2-foot fish tank.
The key components are:
- **Hydro pellets (hydroton/LECA)**: A layer of soaked clay pellets at the bottom
  of the tank provides passive humidification through surface evaporation.
- **Plastic sheeting**: A layer of plastic placed over the top of the tank creates
  a seal that retains humidity while preventing flies and other contaminants from
  entering.
- **Air exchange**: The plastic seal is not airtight — the grower fans fresh air
  into the tank twice daily by lifting the plastic covering briefly.

### Self-Humidifying Chamber Option
Jarrold also describes a more advanced self-humidifying design using:
- A small aquarium air pump
- Air tubing and an air stone
- A water container at the base of the chamber
- Angled internal drip lids made from 2mm plastic sheet

This automated system maintains humidity without manual intervention, though the basic
fish tank with hydro pellets and manual fanning is sufficient for beginners.

## Signs of Mushroom Formation (Pinning)

### First Pins
After bags are placed in the humidity chamber, the grower monitors for the first
signs of mushroom formation. Jarrold describes this stage as "the first signs of
mushrooms starting to form." Pinning typically manifests as:
- Small white knots or bumps appearing on the substrate surface
- These knots develop into tiny mushroom primordia (pins) with distinguishable
  caps and stems
- Pins may appear across the substrate surface or concentrated in specific areas

### When to Cut the Bags
The critical decision point is when pins appear. Jarrold instructs: "It is time to
cut the bag down the sides and remove as much of the bag as needed." This is done
for several reasons:
- **Gas exchange**: Removing the bag allows direct air contact with the substrate
  surface, providing the oxygen that developing mushrooms need.
- **Space for growth**: Mushrooms need physical space to expand upward and outward.
  The plastic bag would constrain growth and deform the mushrooms.
- **Humidity access**: Open substrate surface has direct access to the humid chamber
  atmosphere, promoting proper fruit body development.

### Bag Cutting Technique
Jarrold recommends leaving at least 2 cm of the bottom of the bag intact. This
serves as a base that allows the bag to stand upright anywhere in the humidity
chamber without the substrate sitting directly on the wet hydro pellets. The
partial bag also provides some [[fruiting-initiation]]
