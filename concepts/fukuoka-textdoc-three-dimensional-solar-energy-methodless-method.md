## Centripetal Convergence and the Path to Natural Form

One look at the diagram depicting the centripetal convergence of research on rice cultivation will immediately make clear what was aimed at from the very beginning and where the efforts have brought us. The concept of centripetal convergence describes the way disparate lines of inquiry and experimentation, when pursued with sincerity and without attachment to preconceived outcomes, spiral inward toward a single, unified truth about how rice wants to grow.

In Fukuoka's framework, this convergence is not the result of accumulating more data but of stripping away false assumptions. Each refinement of the method — reducing tillage, eliminating fertilizer, ceasing pruning — moves the practice closer to the center, which is the form the rice plant would take if left entirely to its own devices.
