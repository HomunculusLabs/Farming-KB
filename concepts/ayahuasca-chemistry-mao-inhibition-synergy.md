## The Pharmacological Problem

DMT is orally inactive when taken alone. This is because monoamine oxidase
(MAO) enzymes in the gastrointestinal tract and liver rapidly oxidatively
deaminate DMT before it can reach systemic circulation. MAO-A, the primary
isoform in the gut, converts DMT to indoleacetic acid (IAA) — an inactive
metabolite — with an elimination half-life of less than 10 minutes. This
means that drinking a DMT-containing tea produces no psychedelic effects
whatsoever.

The indigenous peoples of the Amazon solved this problem by combining
DMT-containing plants with plants containing MAO-inhibiting β-carbolines,
creating a synergistic brew where the inhibitor protects the psychedelic
from enzymatic destruction.

## The Two Plants

### DMT Source Plants

Several plants serve as the DMT component:

- **[[chacruna-psychotria-viridis]]** (chacruna) — the most common DMT source, containing
  0.1–0.6% DMT in dried leaves
- **Diplopterys cabrerana** (chaliponga, chalipongá) — contains DMT plus
  5-MeO-DMT; considered more potent than chacruna
- **[[mimosa-tenuiflora]]** (jurema preta) — used in Brazilian preparations;
  bark contains 0.57% DMT

### MAOI Source Plants (Banisteriopsis caapi)

The ayahuasca vine (*[[banisteriopsis-caapi]]*) provides the MAO-inhibiting
β-carboline alkaloids:

- **Harmine** (7-methoxy-1-methyl-9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indole) — the primary
  MAO-A inhibitor; 0.18–1.36% in dried bark
- **Harmaline** (3,4-dihydroharmine) — MAO-A inhibitor with additional
  CNS activity; tremorogenic at high doses
- **[[tetrahydroharmine]]** (THH) — weak MAO inhibitor but potent serotonin
  reuptake inhibitor (SRI); may contribute to the brew's visionary quality

## Biochemical Mechanism

### MAO-A Inhibition

Harmine and harmaline are competitive, reversible MAO-A inhibitors with
IC₅₀ values in the low micromolar range (harmine: ~0.5 µM; harmaline: ~5 µM).
Co-administered with DMT, they occupy the MAO-A active site in intestinal
mucosa and liver, preventing DMT's oxidative deamination and allowing it to
reach systemic circulation and cross the blood-brain barrier.

### The Synergistic Window

The ayahuasca synergy depends on precise pharmacokinetic timing:
1. β-carbolines must achieve sufficient MAO-A inhibition before DMT reaches
   the gut — typically 20–30 minutes after ingestion
2. MAO-A inhibition must persist long enough for DMT absorption — the
   brew is consumed as a single dose, with both components present together
3. Inhibition must not be so complete or prolonged as to cause toxicity
   (the tyramine cheese effect) — the reversible, competitive nature of
   harmine/harmaline provides a safety margin

### Additional Pharmacological Contributions

The β-carbolines are not merely inert MAO inhibitors — they contribute their
own pharmacological effects:

- **Harmine** has mild psychedelic properties at high doses and may
  enhance the visual component of the DMT experience
- **Harmaline** produces tremors and purgative effects at higher
  concentrations, contributing to the physical intensity of the experience
- **THH** acts as a serotonin reuptake inhibitor, potentially prolonging
  serotonin-mediated effects and contributing to the experience's duration

## Ethnopharmacological Discovery

The discovery of this pharmacological synergy is considered one of the most
remarkable achievements in ethnopharmacology. Several features make it
extraordinary:

1. **Pharmacological sophistication** — combining two plants with complementary
   activities requires pharmacokinetic understanding unlikely from simple
   trial-and-error
2. **Statistical improbability** — the Amazon has ~80,000 plant species;
   finding this specific combination by chance alone is improbable
3. **Dosage calibration** — indigenous practitioners have precise preparation
   methods controlling ratios, brewing times, and doses
4. **Cultural transmission** — knowledge maintained for centuries, possibly
   millennia, through oral tradition and apprenticeship

## The Experience: Oral vs. Smoked DMT

McKenna noted that ayahuasca and smoked DMT, while both [[tryptamine]]
experiences, differ significantly in character:

| Feature | Smoked DMT | Ayahuasca |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Onset | 15–30 seconds | 30–60 minutes |
| Peak | 2–5 minutes | 1–3 hours |
| Duration | 5–20 minutes | 4–6 hours |
| Intensity | Extreme, overwhelming | Gradual, manageable |
| Entity contact | Immediate | Can emerge gradually |
| Purgative | None | Often present |
| Vomiting | Rare | Common (la purga) |

The purgative aspect — vomiting, diarrhea, sometimes sweating — is
therapeutically significant in indigenous practice. "La purga" is viewed
as physical and spiritual cleansing, not merely a side effect.
