# PSK and PSP Clinical Trials and Cancer Treatment Outcomes

PSK and PSP, both derived from [[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]], represent the most clinically validated mushroom-derived cancer therapeutics. PSK has been used for decades in Japan while PSP has been developed primarily in China. This concept reviews their clinical trial evidence across multiple cancer types.

## PSK Clinical Trials

### Gastric Cancer
A meta-analysis by the "Study Group of Immunochemotherapy with PSK for Gastric Cancer of Japan" showed that combining PSK with conventional chemotherapy **significantly improved disease-free and overall survival** (Nakazato et al., 1994).

### Oesophageal Cancer
- Non-controlled retrospective analysis (133 patients): Combined radiation, chemotherapy, and PSK immunotherapy showed improvements in **one-year and two-year survival** (Okudaira et al., 1982)
- PSK improved overall survival in patients with pre-operative high α1-anti-chymotrypsin or sialic acid levels (Ogoshi et al., 1995)

### Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma
Small-scale trial in Taiwan: PSK adjunct therapy had a "small but significant impact on five-year survival" (Go and Chung, 1989).

### Lung Cancer (Non-Small Cell)
Study of 185 patients with epidermoid carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, or large-cell carcinoma (≤IIIb) given PSK following radiotherapy:
- **Almost four times more patients** had significant improvements in disease-free survival
- PSK was clinically more significant with **Stage III** disease than Stage I and II
- Greater activity for **older patients (>70 years)** and patients with **small primary tumours** (Hayakawa et al., 1993)

### Breast Cancer
- [[hofmann-lsd-clinical-research-early-studies]] implied long-term PSK immunotherapy with chemotherapy could be beneficial (Suginachi et al., 1984)
- Larger trial (914 patients): PSK "significantly extended survival in ER-negative, Stage IIA patients without lymph node involvement" (Toi et al., 1992)
- Contradictory: Further large trial found "no statistical evidence of any benefit from PSK" (Morimoto et al., 1996)
- Yokoe et al. (1997) may have clarified contradictory results through patient subgroup analysis

### Colorectal Cancer
Multiple studies in Japan demonstrated improved survival with PSK as adjunct to surgery and chemotherapy. PSK is most commonly used in post-surgical adjuvant therapy.

## PSP Clinical Trials (China)

### Regulatory Approval
"The corpus of laboratory and clinical evidence that PSP offers considerable benefits to patients suffering from cancers of the stomach, oesophagus and lung have led to the Chinese Ministry of Public Health granting it a regulatory license."

### Oesophageal Cancer
"An open-label, randomised trial in oesophageal cancer has shown that PSP did significantly improve **one-year and three-year survival**" (Yao, 1999).

### Bone Marrow Transplant
"Liu (1999) has commented on the favourable action of PSP in patients receiving bone autologous marrow transplants."

### Safety Record
"In the clinical setting tens of thousands of patients have been treated with PSP. Many patients have been successfully taking PSP for over 10 years with no serious [[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]]" (Yang, 1999).

## AHCC Clinical Trial

### Maitake (Grifola frondosa) Phase II Trial Design
A Phase II trial was designed to enrol 15 assessable patients for each of 8 tumour types (120 patients total):

#### Design Features
- Further accrual halted if no complete/partial response in each tumour type
- Planned accrual: 90% likelihood of rejecting treatment with true response rate ≤5%
- 90% probability of accepting treatment with true response rate ≥20%
- Quality of life measured by FACT-G scale

#### Results
- **143 patients** enrolled (83 men, 60 women); median age 61 years
- **93%** had Stage IV disease
- 100 fully assessable patients
- **No objective responses** (partial or complete) observed
- **38 patients (26.6%)** had stable disease for 12+ weeks (range 12–50 weeks)
- **No significant changes** in FACT-G scores overall
- **Palliative effects** on cancer-related symptoms (sweating, insomnia) observed
- Patients with stable disease showed improved quality of life

## Phellinus linteus (Meshima)

### Traditional Use
Used in TCM as hot water extracts ('song gen' in Chinese, 'mishimakobu' in Japanese) for digestive system cancers.

### Korean Development
Major national project involving industry, government, and academic laboratories:
- Fermenter-cultivated [[mycelium]] from several P. linteus strains
- Major polysaccharide product **approved as a medicine** in Korea (1997)
- Manufactured by Korean New Pharmaceutical Co.
- Available in Japan as a **functional food** (immunity activation substance)

### Clinical Evidence
"Although there have been only a few Phase II trials there have been reported tumour responses to the combination of Meshima with conventional chemotherapy." Oral formulation; further trials ongoing.

## Safety Data

### Pre-Clinical Safety of Lentinan
- Toxicity tested in multiple species: 0.0001–30 mg/kg for 5–6 weeks by IV administration
- Some swellings/proliferation of reticuloendothelial cells at dosages >25 mg/kg
- Gastrointestinal or urinary bladder haemorrhages at ≥2 mg/kg
- **All lesions in high dose groups; tended to regress after discontinuing**
- Fertility not affected at 0.1–1.0 mg/kg
- No abnormalities during fetal organogenesis at 5.0–10 mg/kg
- No foetal penetration or maternal milk excretion
- **No anaphylactic reactions, no mutagenicity, no haemolysis**

### Clinical Safety
- "Tens of thousands of patients" treated with PSP over 10+ years with no serious adverse effects
- "Large Phase III trials using Lentinan found no adverse reactions or evidence of drug-drug interactions"
- **Grifron-D FDA IND approved**; FDA exempted it from Phase I study due to absence of adverse reactions

## Knowledge Gaps

"Despite the use of PSK and PSP in humans for many years, bioavailability and the pharmacokinetics has received little detailed study. More work in this area, as well as blind RCTs, are required."

### Critical Research Needs
1. **Blinded randomized controlled trials** in Western settings
2. **Bioavailability and pharmacokinetic studies**
3. **Patient selection biomarkers** (e.g., immune competence markers)
4. **Optimal dosing and scheduling** for [[lentinan-gastric-cancer-combination-therapy]]
5. **Long-term safety monitoring**
