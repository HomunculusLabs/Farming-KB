## Specific F:B Ratios

Lowenfels and Lewis provide specific fungal-to-bacterial biomass ratios for
various garden plants. Vegetables like carrots, lettuce, and broccoli prefer an
F:B of 0.3:1 to 0.8:1. Tomatoes, corn, and wheat prefer an F:B of 0.8:1 to 1:1.
Lawns prefer an F:B of 0.5:1 to 1:1. In contrast, conifers require the most
fungally dominated soils with an F:B of 50:1 to 1000:1. Maples, oaks, and
poplars need an F:B of 10:1 to 100:1. Orchard trees do best at 10:1 to 50:1.

## Testing and Monitoring

Gardeners can assess their soil's fungal-to-bacterial ratio through several
methods. Biological soil tests from agricultural laboratories provide direct
measurements of fungal and bacterial biomass. Soil pH in the rhizosphere is an
indirect indicator: an alkaline reading (above 7) suggests bacterial dominance,
while an acidic reading (below 7) suggests fungal dominance. The presence of
visible fungal hyphae, mushrooms, or white thread-like networks in the soil also
suggests fungal activity.  Over time, gardeners learn to read these signs and
adjust their management practices accordingly, moving the F:B ratio in the
direction their plants prefer.

## Summary

The fungal-to-bacterial biomass ratio is a critical factor in soil health and
plant performance. Bacterially dominated soils favor annuals, vegetables, and
lawns by providing nitrate nitrogen, while fungally dominated soils favor trees,
shrubs, and perennials by providing ammonium nitrogen. Gardeners can manipulate
F:B ratios through their choice of compost materials, mulches, and compost tea
amendments. Testing soil microbiology is the best way to determine current
ratios and guide management decisions.
