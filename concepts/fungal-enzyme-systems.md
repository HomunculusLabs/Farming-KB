## Enzyme Competence and Fungal Succession

Different fungal species possess different enzymatic capabilities, which dictates their appearance during decomposition succession. Initial colonizers ("sugar fungi") produce enzymes for simple carbohydrate utilization. As decomposition proceeds and recalcitrant chemicals accumulate, species capable of producing lignin-degrading enzymes (primarily basidiomycetes) dominate. This shift in enzymatic competence drives the observed patterns of fungal succession on decomposing resources.

## Mycorrhizal Enzyme Systems

Mycorrhizal fungi also produce extracellular enzymes, particularly in nutrient-poor environments:
- **Ericoid mycorrhizae** produce proteases and phosphatases enabling direct access to organic N and P
- **Ectomycorrhizae** produce enzymes for accessing organic nitrogen and phosphorus from [[mycorrhizal-phosphatase-activity-elf97-detection]] increase with temperature up to species-specific optima
- **pH**: Different enzymes have different pH optima; lignin peroxidases work best under acidic conditions
- **Substrate availability**: Enzyme production is often induced by the presence of target substrates (e.g., cellulose induces cellulase)
- **Nutrient status**: Nitrogen and phosphorus limitation upregulates enzymes for acquiring those nutrients
- **Moisture**: Adequate water films are required for enzyme diffusion and substrate contact

## Enzyme Pools in Soil

The total enzyme activity in soil represents contributions from:
- Active fungal hyphae (growing and metabolically active)
- Free enzymes adsorbed to soil particles (stabilized and persisting for weeks to months)
- Enzymes associated with dead fungal biomass (contributing to the "legacy" enzyme pool)
- Intracellular enzymes released by cell lysis
