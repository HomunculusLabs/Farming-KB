# Earth Cellar Construction

Earth cellars use the natural temperature-balancing properties of soil to create ideal storage conditions for fruit and vegetables without any additional energy input. (see [[comparison-root-cellaring-vs-food-dehydration]]).

## Temperature and Humidity

The high humidity of around 80 to 90 percent in earth cellars and their fairly steady temperature between 8 and 10°C is ideal for storing the majority of crops. Modern houses with heated cellars and concrete floors are disastrous for storing produce — the low humidity causes apples to wither and wrinkle in the shortest amount of time. (see [[comparison-slip-form-stone-vs-timber-frame]]).

## Construction Method

Roundwood shelters and earth cellars are built using notched tree trunks:

1. **Foundation**: Rough timber logs are placed at least one metre deep and cut to the same height.
2. **Walls**: Notched tree trunks are used to construct the roof and walls. For stability, the back wall should fit closely with the top edge of the roof.
3. **Roof layers**: The roof is built up in layers — membrane, sheeting, fine soil, another membrane, then soil and vegetation on top.
4. **Storage**: The interior serves as a storage room with the natural insulation of earth maintaining stable conditions.

## Advantages

Building roundwood shelters and earth cellars is possible and practical all over the world. By making use of the balancing effect of the soil, a pleasant cool retreat can be created in hot places and a warm one in cold places. The key is understanding how to make use of nature's resources properly. Regular inspection and prompt repair of any damage is necessary to guarantee safety.
