# Scarp and Wadi Water Harvesting Systems

Scarps and wadis are among the most productive landscape features for water harvesting in desert environments. These fault-formed cliff-and-valley systems concentrate rainfall run-off into predictable channels, offering the [[ferricrete]], or [[water-management]] achievable in wadi landscapes:

- Hold 80-90% of run-off behind dykes in wadis, spreading water to lower dykes on the plains.
- Highly coordinated flood-and-soak regimes deliver water to orchards.
- A ratio of 20 hectares of run-off catchment for every 1 irrigated hectare.
- Around Petra, reliable winter rains occur every year, making the system dependable.
- From the moment rain begins until fields are soaked, the systems require close attention.

Thousands of hectares of Nabatean fields remain abandoned today, representing an enormous restoration opportunity.

## Settlement Design

Wadi settlements benefit from several natural advantages:

- **Natural security**: Box canyons bounded by steep cliffs with a single entry point are easily defended and protected from feral animals or predators.
- **Shade and shelter**: Cliff-base caves provide cool, shaded living spaces protected from flood and sand blast.
- **Minimal fencing**: The natural containment of box canyons reduces fencing needs dramatically.
- **Construction ease**: Most peneplain crusts form over softer rock, making channel and gutter cutting straightforward. Plentiful rock supply exists for field walls on the pediment slope.

## Maintenance Considerations

All wells, pools, and dams gather silt and require periodic cleaning. Dams can be cleaned by leaving base pipes open during alternative flows. Cliff pools self-clean through turbulence. This regular maintenance cycle is a fundamental part of wadi system management and must be factored into any settlement plan.

## Modern Potential

With modern machines, fencing, windmills, and solar panels or photovoltaics, many wadi systems currently grazed or neglected could be creatively occupied on a modest scale and planted with desert forestry. Large trees already grow in most wadis, shaded and protected by cliffs. By restricting hoofed animals, many more trees would establish naturally—overgrazing is identified as the most obvious plague of arid lands.
