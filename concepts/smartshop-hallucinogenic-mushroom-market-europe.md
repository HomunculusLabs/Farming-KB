### Product Range

Smartshops offered a diverse range of mushroom-related products, including:

- **Fresh mushrooms**: The primary product, sold in pre-weighed portions with species identification and basic dosage guidance
- **Dried mushrooms**: Preserved through dehydration, offering longer shelf life and more concentrated psychoactive content per gram
- **Grow kits**: Complete cultivation packages enabling consumers to grow their own mushrooms at home
- **Spawn bags**: Pre-inoculated substrate bags for more experienced cultivators
- **Spore prints and syringes**: For microscopy and cultivation purposes

### Brand Names and Marketing

Smartshops developed branded product lines and marketing strategies that normalized the purchase and consumption of psychoactive mushrooms. Species were sold under both scientific names and appealing common names, with packaging that included dosage recommendations, effects descriptions, and safety warnings. This professionalized retail presentation contrasted sharply with the underground market characteristics of most other recreational drugs.

## Distribution Channels

### Physical Retail

The primary distribution channel for hallucinogenic mushrooms was physical smartshop retail. In addition to dedicated smartshops, mushrooms were also available through:

- **Head shops**: Stores primarily focused on cannabis paraphernalia that diversified into mushroom sales
- **Market stalls**: Particularly in the Netherlands, mushrooms were sold at open-air markets
- **Festival vendors**: Temporary retail at music festivals and alternative culture events

### Internet Commerce

The internet emerged as a significant secondary distribution channel, serving both informational and commercial functions:

- **Online shops**: Web-based retailers shipped mushroom products to countries where legal restrictions were less stringent
- **Information sites**: Websites provided cultivation guidance, species identification, and dosage information
- **Forums and communities**: Online discussion platforms facilitated peer-to-peer information exchange
- **Mail order**: Direct shipping of dried mushrooms and grow kits from Netherlands-based retailers to consumers across Europe

The internet played a dual role in the mushroom market, simultaneously serving as a tool for harm reduction information dissemination and as a commercial channel that extended the reach of suppliers beyond national borders.

## Mushroom Hunting Culture

Beyond commercial channels, a significant component of European mushroom use involved foraging for wild specimens. The tradition of mushroom hunting is well-established in many European countries, particularly in Eastern and Central Europe, where collecting wild fungi for food is a widespread cultural practice.

### Foraging for Psychoactive Species

The existence of indigenous psilocybin-containing species in several European countries, most notably Psilocybe semilanceata (liberty cap), meant that determined individuals could obtain psychoactive mushrooms without any commercial transaction. Field guides, online identification resources, and community knowledge sharing facilitated the identification of psychoactive species in the wild.

### Risks of Foraging

Mushroom foraging for psychoactive species carries significant risks, primarily the danger of misidentification. Several psilocybin-containing species have toxic lookalikes, and errors in identification can have serious or fatal consequences. The informal transmission of foraging knowledge through online forums and word of mouth, while valuable, cannot substitute for expert botanical training.

### Differences in Effects and Risk

The promotion of Amanita muscaria as a substitute for psilocybin mushrooms was problematic from a pharmacological perspective. The active compounds in Amanita (muscimol and ibotenic acid) produce effects that are qualitatively different from those of psilocybin, and the risk profile, particularly regarding gastrointestinal effects, is more concerning. The conflation of these distinct substance classes in the marketplace created potential for consumer harm.
