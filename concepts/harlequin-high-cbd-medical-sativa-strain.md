# Harlequin — High-CBD Medical Sativa Strain

## Overview

Harlequin is a sativa-dominant hybrid cannabis strain distinguished by its unusually high cannabidiol (CBD) content relative to tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). With tested ratios of approximately 4% CBD to 2% THC, Harlequin represents one of the earliest and most influential high-CBD strains to gain recognition in the medical cannabis community. Bred by Green Haven Genetics in the San Francisco Bay Area, the strain was specifically developed to produce high-grade hash and oils for medical patients who require the therapeutic benefits of cannabis without the intense psychoactivity associated with high-THC varieties. The strain is currently available in clone form only, limiting its availability primarily to Bay Area residents and those with connections to the breeder.

## Genetic Lineage

Harlequin's genetic background is a remarkable assemblage of four distinct landrace and heirloom varieties, making it one of the most genetically diverse commercial strains:

- **Colombian Gold (1970s cut)**: A legendary South American sativa landrace that was widely available in the United States during the 1970s. Colombian Gold contributed golden-hued buds, sweet citrus and earthy flavors, and the foundational sativa structure. The specific 1970s cut used in Harlequin is considered a prized heirloom that predates much of the hybridization that occurred in subsequent decades.
- **Thai**: Southeast Asian landrace sativa genetics that contribute potency, the characteristic cerebral energy, and complex spicy, tropical terpene notes. Thai genetics have been foundational to many celebrated strains including Haze and Trainwreck.
- **Swiss Sativa**: European-bred sativa genetics that add structural hardiness and adaptability to varied growing conditions. The Swiss component may contribute to the strain's surprisingly robust growth for a sativa-dominant plant.
- **Nepalese**: Himalayan sativa landrace genetics that provide resin production, hash-making potential, and an earthy, incense-like quality to the aroma profile. Nepalese varieties have long been valued in hash-producing regions for their generous trichome coverage.

The combination of these four distinct sativa lineages from four different continents creates a genetic profile of extraordinary breadth. Each parent contributes unique terpene and cannabinoid characteristics while maintaining the sativa-dominant growth pattern and effect profile.

## The CBD-to-THC Ratio

Harlequin's defining characteristic is its CBD-to-THC ratio of approximately 2:1 (4% CBD to 2% THC). This is significant for several reasons:

- **Non-intoxicating therapeutic window**: At 2% THC, most users do not experience significant psychoactive effects, allowing them to maintain mental clarity while benefiting from cannabis's therapeutic properties. This makes Harlequin suitable for daytime medical use.
- **CBD modulation of THC**: Research has demonstrated that CBD can modulate the psychoactive effects of THC, potentially reducing anxiety, paranoia, and cognitive impairment that some users experience with THC-dominant strains.
- **Entourage effect**: The presence of both cannabinoids alongside the diverse terpene profile from four landrace parents creates a broad entourage effect — the synergistic interaction between multiple cannabis compounds that is increasingly recognized as important for therapeutic outcomes.

## Growing Characteristics

### Plant Structure and Hardiness

Despite its predominantly sativa genetics, Harlequin displays an unexpected degree of structural hardiness. The influence of the Swiss and Nepalese parentage is evident in the plant's robust stems and resistance to environmental stress. While taller than typical indica varieties, Harlequin can be easily managed at heights suitable for most indoor grow environments. The plant responds well to training techniques including topping, LST, and scrog methods.

### Vegetative Growth

Harlequin benefits from a relatively short vegetative period. The breeder notes that the plant will "soon be ready to be flipped into flower" after a brief vegetative phase. This is advantageous for medical growers who need efficient turnaround times and for indoor cultivators working with limited vertical space. The rapid onset of flowering also suggests that the plant does not require extensive vegetative preparation to produce satisfactory yields.

### Flowering and Bud Development

One of Harlequin's most remarkable characteristics is the speed and density of its flower development. The breeder reports seeing flowers "as long as your finger" just two weeks after the flip to 12/12 light cycle — an exceptionally rapid flowering response for a sativa-dominant strain. As flowering progresses, the buds become heavily encrusted with resin, to the point where the underlying bud structure becomes barely visible beneath a thick coating of crystalline trichomes.

This extreme resin production is a key attribute for the strain's intended purpose — hash and oil production. The breeder specifically developed Harlequin to yield high-grade extracts for medical patients, and the generous trichome coverage directly serves this goal.

### Support Requirements

The weight of the resin-laden flowers can become substantial enough to cause branches to bend or break under their own weight. Staking or trellising should be implemented early in the flowering phase, before the buds become too heavy to safely reposition. The breeder emphasizes doing this "sooner rather than later" to avoid damaging the plant during the critical resin production period.

### Flowering Duration

Harlequin requires approximately 70 days of flowering to reach full maturity. This places it in the moderate-to-long category for flowering time, though the heavy resin production during the final weeks justifies the extended wait. The full 70-day period should be observed for maximum CBD and terpene development.

### Drying and Curing

The density of the resin-heavy buds means that Harlequin requires an extended drying period — approximately three weeks for proper moisture reduction. Rushing the drying process will result in harsh-tasting material and reduced terpene expression. The extended cure also allows the complex flavors from the four-parent lineage to fully integrate.

## Aroma and Flavor Profile

Harlequin's flavor is shaped by its extraordinary genetic diversity:

- **Primary aromas**: Earthy and musky, with a distinct mango and tropical fruit sweetness from the Colombian Gold and Thai parentage. The scent is complex and layered, revealing different notes at different stages of consumption.
- **Secondary aromas**: Spicy, peppery notes from the Thai genetics, combined with an incense-like quality from the Nepalese parent. Some phenotypes express a subtle citrus brightness.
- **Flavor on combustion**: The smoke is smooth and carries through the tropical sweetness with added depth from the earthy undertones. The complex flavor profile is one of Harlequin's most appreciated qualities among medical users.

## Medical Applications

Harlequin was bred specifically for medical use, and its effect profile reflects this purpose:

- **Pain management**: The combination of CBD and THC at this ratio has been shown to provide effective pain relief without significant intoxication, making it suitable for patients who need to maintain cognitive function during treatment.
- **Anxiety and PTSD**: The anxiolytic properties of CBD, combined with the mood-stabilizing effects of low-dose THC, make Harlequin a candidate for anxiety disorder management.
- **Inflammation**: CBD is a recognized anti-inflammatory compound, and Harlequin's high CBD content makes it potentially useful for inflammatory conditions including arthritis and autoimmune disorders.
- **Seizure disorders**: While clinical evidence is still developing, high-CBD strains like Harlequin have shown promise in reducing seizure frequency in some forms of treatment-resistant epilepsy.
- **Nausea and appetite**: The mild THC content stimulates appetite while the CBD helps manage nausea, making it suitable for patients undergoing chemotherapy or dealing with appetite-suppressing conditions.
- **Daytime functionality**: Perhaps most importantly, the low THC content allows patients to use Harlequin during the day without impairment, making it one of the most versatile medical strains available.

## Hash and Oil Production

Harlequin was specifically bred for extract production, and it excels in this regard. The extraordinary resin density — where the breeder notes it looks like "someone just threw a bag of sugar at a tree" — means that even small quantities of plant material can yield significant amounts of hash or oil. The high-CBD content of the resulting extracts is particularly valuable for medical patients who prefer concentrate forms of cannabis medicine. Ice water hash and CO2 extraction methods are recommended to preserve the full spectrum of cannabinoids and terpenes.

## Significance in Cannabis History

Harlequin holds an important place in the evolution of medical cannabis. At a time when the industry was overwhelmingly focused on maximizing THC content, Green Haven Genetics recognized the therapeutic potential of CBD-rich varieties and invested in developing a strain that prioritized the CBD-to-THC ratio over raw potency. This approach was ahead of its time — the medical benefits of CBD would not gain mainstream scientific recognition for several more years. Harlequin helped demonstrate that cannabis breeding could be guided by specific therapeutic goals rather than simply pursuing higher THC numbers.

## Cultivation Best Practices

For growers fortunate enough to acquire Harlequin clones, the following practices will maximize both yield and quality:

1. **Mother plant maintenance**: Keep mother plants under 18/6 light in a dedicated space. Harlequin mothers are relatively easy to maintain and produce vigorous cuttings that root quickly.
2. **Vegetative phase**: A 2–3 week vegetative period is sufficient for most indoor setups. The plant's rapid flowering response means that extended vegetative growth is unnecessary and will only increase height management challenges.
3. **Nutrient management**: Moderate feeding is recommended during vegetation, with increased phosphorus and potassium during flowering to support the heavy resin and bud production. Avoid excessive nitrogen during late flowering as it can diminish terpene quality.
4. **Environmental conditions**: Harlequin performs well in standard indoor conditions (70–80°F, 45–55% humidity during flowering). The Swiss genetics provide good environmental resilience, but maintain vigilance for mold given the dense bud structure.
5. **Harvest timing**: Allow the full 70 days. The CBD content continues to develop during the final weeks of flowering, and early harvest will result in suboptimal cannabinoid ratios.
6. **Post-harvest**: The three-week drying period is non-negotiable for this strain. The dense, resin-heavy buds require extended time to release internal moisture. Rushing this process will compromise both flavor and the therapeutic quality of the final product.
7. **Curing**: A minimum 4-week cure in glass jars is recommended for medical-grade results. The extended cure allows the full terpene and cannabinoid profile to stabilize, producing a smoother, more effective medicine.

## Finger Hash Bonus

A notable perk reported by the breeder is the exceptional quantity and quality of finger hash produced during the manicure process. Given the extreme resin density of Harlequin's flowers, the act of trimming alone yields enough finger hash to be a meaningful harvest in itself. This sticky, potent byproduct can be pressed into small balls of charas-quality hash and provides an immediate reward while the main harvest cures. For medical patients who process their own medicine, this represents a significant practical advantage — effective medicine is available almost immediately, without waiting for the full cure to complete.
