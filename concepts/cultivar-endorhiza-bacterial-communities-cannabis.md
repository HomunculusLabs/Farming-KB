# Endorhiza Bacterial Communities and Cannabis Cultivar Specificity

## The Endorhiza Defined

The endorhiza is the internal root tissue colonized by bacteria -- more
specifically, bacteria that have crossed the rhizoplane and entered root
tissue. Endorhiza bacteria support plant growth and suppress disease by
providing phytohormones, low molecular weight compounds, or enzymes involved
in regulating growth and metabolism. They also assist host plants in tolerating
phytotoxic effects of environmental toxicants.

Endorhiza communities tend to be more plant-specific than rhizosphere
communities and are often shaped by compounds or proteins produced by their
host. Both endophytes and epiphytes may play a role in localized terroir for
crop plants, as has been demonstrated for wines.

## Proteobacteria Dominance in Endorhiza

The shift from bulk soil to endorhiza is characterized by:
- Dramatic decrease in Acidobacteria (acidophilic, oligotrophic bacteria)
- Increase in Proteobacteria (fast-growing, copiotrophic bacteria)
- Increase in Actinobacteria

Of 51 OTUs significantly differentiating between sample types, 17 increased
in the endorhiza. These were predominantly Proteobacteria from the
Rhizobiales order. This pattern is consistent with the general understanding
that root environments favor fast-growing, nutrient-responsive bacteria over
the slow-growing oligotrophs that dominate bulk soils.
