### COX-2 Inhibition

COX-2 is an enzyme responsible for inflammation and pain in general. Cannabis constituents have demonstrated the ability to modulate COX-2 activity, positioning cannabinoids as potential alternatives or adjuncts to NSAID medications that target this pathway. Researchers have found that cannabinoids can work synergistically with NSAIDs for enhanced anti-inflammatory effect.

### Full-Spectrum Anti-Inflammatory Action

A 2009 meta-analysis from the University of Massachusetts Medical School concluded that all classes of cannabinoids show anti-inflammatory action, including:

- Endogenous cannabinoids (anandamide)
- Related compounds (elmiric acids, EMAs)
- Non-cannabinoid components (200-250 constituents) of cannabis

All types of cannabinoids as well as non-cannabinoid parts of the plant were found effective in reducing pain from inflammation in conditions including post-surgery recovery, rheumatism, [[cannabis-and-ulcerative-colitis]], and related disorders.

### Uveitis (Eye Inflammation)

Research from the University of Nebraska and Medical College of Georgia confirmed a potent ability of CB2 activation on inflammation in a model of endotoxin-induced uveitis (EIU), involving reduction of NF-kappa-B, AP-1, and other inflammatory mediators. CBD was found to exert anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects in the eye.

### Encephalitis (Brain Inflammation)

Studies from the University of Manitoba demonstrated that cannabinoids can mitigate central nervous system (CNS) inflammation during viral encephalitis by limiting viral infection and controlling inflammatory damage, suggesting mitigation of inflammation via CB2 receptor pathways.
