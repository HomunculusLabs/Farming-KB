## Perlite Humidification
The PF Tek employs perlite as the primary humidification medium. Perlite, when
saturated with water and placed in the bottom of the terrarium, creates a large
surface area for evaporation. This passive humidification system maintains
humidity levels in the 90 to 95 percent range required for fruiting without
the need for electric humidifiers or ultrasonic foggers.
The perlite layer should be several inches deep and kept consistently moist but
not submerged. Water at the bottom of the outer container gradually wicks up
through the perlite, providing sustained evaporation over extended periods. The
system requires periodic replenishment of water but minimal daily maintenance.
The expanded volcanic glass structure of perlite provides enormous surface area
relative to its volume, making it an ideal evaporative medium.
## Temperature Requirements
Fruiting requires slightly lower temperatures than the colonization phase. PF
Tek specifies a minimum temperature of 60 degrees Fahrenheit (approximately
15.5 degrees Celsius) for successful fruiting. Optimal fruiting occurs at room
temperature, typically between 70 and 75 degrees Fahrenheit (21 to 24 degrees
Celsius). Lower temperatures than the incubation range (approximately 70 to 78
degrees Fahrenheit) help trigger the fruiting response and produce denser, more
robust mushrooms.
Temperature regulation does not require heating mats or other active systems.
Ambient room temperature is generally sufficient, provided it stays above the
60-degree minimum. The terrarium's insulation properties help buffer
temperature fluctuations. Maintaining a stable temperature prevents stress on
the developing mushrooms and promotes even growth.
## Ventilation and Gas Exchange
Adequate gas exchange is critical during fruiting. Mushrooms produce carbon
dioxide during respiration, and elevated CO2 levels inhibit proper fruiting and
can cause elongated, unhealthy stems with small or absent caps. The PF Tek
terrarium design incorporates ventilation through gaps or drilled holes in the
containers. Daily fanning — opening the terrarium and waving fresh air across
the cakes — provides additional gas exchange and helps maintain healthy
atmospheric conditions.
The balance between humidity retention and air exchange is one of the key
challenges in terrarium design. Too much ventilation dries out the cakes and
perlite; too little causes CO2 buildup and poor fruiting. The dual chamber
design inherently moderates this balance because the outer reservoir buffers
humidity loss while the inner chamber allows controlled gas exchange.
## Light as a Fruiting Trigger
While mushrooms are not photosynthetic, light serves as an important
environmental signal that triggers and directs fruiting. The PF Tek terrarium
relies on ambient room light or indirect lighting to provide the necessary light
cue. Mushrooms grown in complete darkness may form poorly or not at all. Light
direction also influences mushroom growth patterns, causing caps to orient
toward the light source, which can be used to encourage upward growth.
Direct sunlight should be avoided as it can overheat the terrarium and damage
the mycelium. Indirect ambient light from a window or room lighting on a normal
day/night cycle provides sufficient light for proper fruiting. The clear
plastic containers allow light to reach the cakes from all angles, promoting
even pin formation across the substrate surface.
## The Birthing Process
Before entering the terrarium, fully colonized substrate cakes undergo
"birthing." This involves removing the cakes from their jars, often rinsing
them to remove loose uncolonized substrate material (vermiculite layer), and
placing them on the elevated platform in the inner terrarium chamber. The shock
of the new open-air environment, combined with the temperature drop from
incubation to fruiting temperatures, helps initiate pinning.
Some practitioners perform a cold shock by refrigerating the cakes briefly
before birthing, though PF Tek itself relies primarily on the natural
temperature differential and exposure to fresh air to trigger fruiting. The
cakes should be handled with clean hands or gloves to minimize contamination
risk during transfer.
## Maintenance Routine
The standard PF Tek fruiting maintenance involves several daily and periodic
tasks:
- Daily fanning of the terrarium for gas exchange (30 seconds to 1 minute)
- Periodic misting of the cakes and perlite surface to maintain humidity
- Monitoring temperature to ensure it stays within the fruiting range
- Observing mushroom development for signs of contamination or poor fruiting
- Replenishing water in the perlite as it evaporates
