    18|# PF Tek Dual Chamber Terrarium Design and Fruiting Conditions
    19|
    20|The Psilocybe Fanaticus (PF) Tek dual chamber terrarium represents one of the
    21|most influential innovations in home mushroom cultivation. This design provides
    22|the controlled high-humidity environment necessary for fruiting Psilocybe
    23|cubensis while remaining accessible and affordable for novice cultivators using
    24|commonly available materials.
    25|
    26|## The Dual Chamber Concept
    27|
    28|The PF Tek terrarium consists of two clear plastic containers, one nested inside
    29|the other. The inner chamber houses the birthing cakes, while the outer chamber
    30|serves as a water reservoir. This design creates a self-contained humidification
    31|system that maintains optimal moisture levels for mushroom fruiting without
    32|requiring automated equipment or constant manual intervention. The separation
    33|of chambers prevents the substrate cakes from sitting directly in water, which
    34|would cause bacterial contamination and waterlogging.
    35|
    36|## Container Specifications
    37|
    38|The design uses widely available clear plastic storage containers of appropriate
    39|dimensions to hold multiple cakes. The inner container holds the colonized
    40|substrate cakes on an elevated platform above the water level. The outer
    41|container provides the reservoir that supplies moisture to the inner chamber
    42|through evaporation. Clear plastic is essential to allow light penetration,
    43|which serves as a key fruiting trigger for the mushrooms. Container size should
    44|provide adequate headroom for mushroom growth, as mature Psilocybe cubensis can
    45|reach several inches in height.
    46|
    47|## Perlite Humidification
    48|
    49|The PF Tek employs perlite as the primary humidification medium. Perlite, when
    50|saturated with water and placed in the bottom of the terrarium, creates a large
    51|surface area for evaporation. This passive humidification system maintains
    52|humidity levels in the 90 to 95 percent range required for fruiting without
    53|the need for electric humidifiers or ultrasonic foggers.
    54|
    55|The perlite layer should be several inches deep and kept consistently moist but
    56|not submerged. Water at the bottom of the outer container gradually wicks up
    57|through the perlite, providing sustained evaporation over extended periods. The
    58|system requires periodic replenishment of water but minimal daily maintenance.
    59|The expanded volcanic glass structure of perlite provides enormous surface area
    60|relative to its volume, making it an ideal evaporative medium.
    61|
    62|## Temperature Requirements
    63|
    64|Fruiting requires slightly lower temperatures than the colonization phase. PF
    65|Tek specifies a minimum temperature of 60 degrees Fahrenheit (approximately
    66|15.5 degrees Celsius) for successful fruiting. Optimal fruiting occurs at room
    67|temperature, typically between 70 and 75 degrees Fahrenheit (21 to 24 degrees
    68|Celsius). Lower temperatures than the incubation range (approximately 70 to 78
    69|degrees Fahrenheit) help trigger the fruiting response and produce denser, more
    70|robust mushrooms.
    71|
    72|Temperature regulation does not require heating mats or other active systems.
    73|Ambient room temperature is generally sufficient, provided it stays above the
    74|60-degree minimum. The terrarium's insulation properties help buffer
    75|temperature fluctuations. Maintaining a stable temperature prevents stress on
    76|the developing mushrooms and promotes even growth.
    77|
    78|## Ventilation and Gas Exchange
    79|
    80|Adequate gas exchange is critical during fruiting. Mushrooms produce carbon
    81|dioxide during respiration, and elevated CO2 levels inhibit proper fruiting and
    82|can cause elongated, unhealthy stems with small or absent caps. The PF Tek
    83|terrarium design incorporates ventilation through gaps or drilled holes in the
    84|containers. Daily fanning — opening the terrarium and waving fresh air across
    85|the cakes — provides additional gas exchange and helps maintain healthy
    86|atmospheric conditions.
    87|
    88|The balance between humidity retention and air exchange is one of the key
    89|challenges in terrarium design. Too much ventilation dries out the cakes and
    90|perlite; too little causes CO2 buildup and poor fruiting. The dual chamber
    91|design inherently moderates this balance because the outer reservoir buffers
    92|humidity loss while the inner chamber allows controlled gas exchange.
    93|
    94|## Light as a Fruiting Trigger
    95|
    96|While mushrooms are not photosynthetic, light serves as an important
    97|environmental signal that triggers and directs fruiting. The PF Tek terrarium
    98|relies on ambient room light or indirect lighting to provide the necessary light
    99|cue. Mushrooms grown in complete darkness may form poorly or not at all. Light
   100|direction also influences mushroom growth patterns, causing caps to orient
   101|toward the light source, which can be used to encourage upward growth.
   102|
   103|Direct sunlight should be avoided as it can overheat the terrarium and damage
   104|the mycelium. Indirect ambient light from a window or room lighting on a normal
   105|day/night cycle provides sufficient light for proper fruiting. The clear
   106|plastic containers allow light to reach the cakes from all angles, promoting
   107|even pin formation across the substrate surface.
   108|
   109|## The Birthing Process
   110|
   111|Before entering the terrarium, fully colonized substrate cakes undergo
   112|"birthing." This involves removing the cakes from their jars, often rinsing
   113|them to remove loose uncolonized substrate material (vermiculite layer), and
   114|placing them on the elevated platform in the inner terrarium chamber. The shock
   115|of the new open-air environment, combined with the temperature drop from
   116|incubation to fruiting temperatures, helps initiate pinning.
   117|
   118|Some practitioners perform a cold shock by refrigerating the cakes briefly
   119|before birthing, though PF Tek itself relies primarily on the natural
   120|temperature differential and exposure to fresh air to trigger fruiting. The
   121|cakes should be handled with clean hands or gloves to minimize contamination
   122|risk during transfer.
   123|
   124|## Maintenance Routine
   125|
   126|The standard PF Tek fruiting maintenance involves several daily and periodic
   127|tasks:
   128|
   129|- Daily fanning of the terrarium for gas exchange (30 seconds to 1 minute)
   130|- Periodic misting of the cakes and perlite surface to maintain humidity
   131|- Monitoring temperature to ensure it stays within the fruiting range
   132|- Observing mushroom development for signs of contamination or poor fruiting
   133|- Replenishing water in the perlite as it evaporates
   134|
   135|The low-maintenance nature of the dual chamber design was a key factor in its
   136|widespread adoption. Unlike more complex automated setups, the PF Tek requires
   137|no timers, pumps, or electronic controls. A few minutes of attention per day
   138|is sufficient for successful cultivation.
   139|
   140|## Fruiting Timeline
   141|
   142|After birthing, pin formation (primordia development) typically begins within 5
   143|to 14 days. This waiting period requires patience and consistent maintenance of
   144|humidity and temperature. Once pins appear, mushroom growth proceeds rapidly,
   145|with mature mushrooms ready for harvest within 5 to 7 days of pin formation.
   146|The mushrooms are harvested by gently twisting and pulling at the base of the
   147|stem just before or as the veil beneath the cap begins to tear.
