## Pyrethrum and Derris as Natural Insecticides

As an additional precaution, Fukuoka recommends planting pyrethrum and
derris root at the edge of the garden. Tests were conducted on different
varieties of derris root at the Kochi testing center before the war, and
those varieties that were cold-hardy, suited to outdoor cultivation, and
had a high content of the active ingredient were selected for use.

Pyrethrum flowers and derris root may be dried and stored as powders.
Pyrethrum is effective against aphids and caterpillars, while derris root
works well against cabbage sawflies and leaf beetles. However, both agents
may be used against all insect pests, including melon flies, by dissolving
the agent in water and sprinkling the solution onto the vegetable plants
with a watering can. Both are harmless to man and [[query-how-can-ducks-help-with-pest-control-and-fertility-in-a-garden]] sist in soil and water for years, these natural
compounds have been used safely for centuries.

## Fowl as Living Pest Control

While working in Kochi Prefecture, Fukuoka observed local chickens, black
as ravens, strutting through a vegetable patch in a farmyard and deftly
picking at insects without scratching the earth or harming the vegetables.
This observation led him to advocate letting fowl loose in a vegetable
patch as an effective and natural method of insect control. Chickens,
ducks, and other poultry consume large quantities of insects, particularly
in the early morning and evening when pests are most active near the soil
surface.

The key to successful integration of poultry into vegetable gardens is
the selection of appropriate bird varieties and careful timing. The local
black chickens that Fukuoka observed had been bred over generations to
forage without damaging crops, unlike commercial breeds that may scratch
