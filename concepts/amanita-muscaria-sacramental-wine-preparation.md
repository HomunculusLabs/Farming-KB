# Amanita Muscaria Sacramental Wine Preparation

The sacramental wine preparation is one of the most sophisticated [[amanita-muscaria-drying-and-preparation]] culture techniques described in the Herb of Immortality source. Unlike the simpler [[amanita-muscaria-ambrosia-preparation]] (cold water extraction), the sacramental wine method uses concentrated fruit juices as a nutrient medium, combined with modern sterile technique (pasteurization) to produce a potent, alcohol-free, sulfite-free "living wine" through the [[amanita-muscaria-resurrection-experiments]] of dried mushroom material.

## Historical Background

### Ancient Fermented Preparations

In "Soma: Divine Mushroom of Immortality," [[gordon-wasson-mushroom-discovery]] cites reports of Siberian tribes who fermented Amanita muscaria with:

- Various berry or fruit juices
- Diluted honey
- Juice from a certain type of sweet grass

Many tribes used these fermented juices for curing upset stomachs and other medicinal applications, along with their use as an intoxicant.

### Ancient Greek and Roman Wine Mystery

The wines of the ancient Greeks and Romans had properties fundamentally different from modern alcoholic wine:

- Had to be diluted with large quantities of water before consumption
- Ratios ranged from one part wine to 2-50 parts water
- Consuming large quantities of undiluted ancient wine could cause death
- Undiluted wine was used on battle wounds as an antiseptic (per Homer's Iliad, 800 BC)
- Modern wine is never used this way
- Produced from very ancient times into the late Roman Era (~500 AD), then production seems to cease

This wine dilution puzzle is analyzed in detail in the [[amanita-muscaria-ancient-wine-question]] page.

### Biblical Connection

The Gospel of John 15:1 states: "I am the true vine and my father is the husbandman." The author interprets this as referring to the Amanita muscaria -- the "true vine" that produces the sacramental wine.

## The Science: Pasteurization and Pure Culture

### Louis Pasteur's Contribution

Louis Pasteur (1822-1895) discovered that air is full of microorganisms and their spores, which cause food spoilage and disease. His experiments with plugged glass bottles of beef broth demonstrated:

1. Bottles cooked with a cotton plug remained sterile for months
2. The cotton plug filtered air but did not seal it (unlike canning)
3. Removing and reinserting the plug for even a few seconds caused contamination
4. The cotton plug, sterilized by outgoing steam, became a micro-pore filter

This technique is the basis of all modern pure culture methods for growing fungi, bacteria, yeasts, plant cells, and animal cells in sterile environments.

### Application to Amanita Wine

The author applies Pasteur's technique to Amanita muscaria wine production:

- Fruit juice is pasteurized in glass bottles plugged with polyfill
- After cooling, dried mushroom powder is added as inoculant
- The polyfill allows oxygen exchange while filtering contaminants
- Amanita mycelium grows in the sterile juice medium
- The result is a pure culture of Amanita in fruit juice -- "living wine"

## Equipment and Materials

### 1. Dried Amanita Muscaria Caps

- Low-temperature dried (120 F or less) caps preferred
- Higher grades worth the extra money if purchasing
- Inspect carefully for dirt, pine needles, and debris
- Remove all foreign material before grinding
- Grind to fine powder (like flour) so it floats on juice surface
- Small coffee grinders work well; dedicate one to Amanita only

### 2. Glass Bottles or Jugs

- 1 pint to 1 gallon capacity
- Juice bottles, beer bottles, wine bottles all work
- Bottles with tapered necks better for polyfill plugging
- Clear glass preferred (easier to monitor culture)
- 1-gallon clear glass jugs ideal (hold up to 3 quarts juice)
- Clean new bottles with detergent and hot water
- Recycled bottles: soak in bleach solution (1 oz bleach per quart water) for several days
- Inspect for cracks and chips before use

### 3. Polyfill (Polyester Fiber)

- Superior modern substitute for Pasteur's waterproof cotton
- Available from craft and fabric stores as pillow stuffing
- Waterproof, not biodegradable, easily sterilized
- Roll into ball 3-4x the bottle mouth size
- Push in with twisting motion; plug should extend into bottle
- Practice on empty dry bottles first

### 4. Cooking Pots

- Small stainless steel pot (~1.5 gallon) for reacting finished wine
- Large pot with metal rack for pasteurization
- Hot water canner with basket works well
- NEVER use aluminum for grape juice or wine (acid dissolves aluminum)
- Use only stainless steel or tempered glass for finished wine contact

### 5. Thermometer

- Accurate, waterproof
- Range 150-195 F
- Glass or stainless steel
- Rig with wire to hold at proper depth in water bath

### 6. Growing Area

- Room temperature (65-72 F)
- Some air movement
- No direct sunlight
- Clean, mold-free environment

## The Process

### Step 1: Pasteurization

1. Fill bottles with concentrated fruit juice (grape, apple, or mix)
2. Plug bottles with polyfill
3. Place in large pot with water bath
4. Heat water to 160-170 F
5. Maintain temperature for 30-40 minutes
6. Remove and allow to cool to room temperature

### Step 2: Inoculation

1. Once bottles are completely cool, add ground mushroom powder
2. Powder should float on the juice surface
3. Use small amount -- the author emphasizes the tiniest amount can start a culture

### Step 3: Incubation

1. Place inoculated bottles in growing area
2. Maintain 65-72 F with air movement
3. Mycelium will begin growing within 24-48 hours
4. Full colonization of the juice medium occurs in 3-5 days
5. The wine is ready when mycelium is well-established

### Step 4: Harvesting

1. Pour off the wine through the polyfill (which filters out mycelium)
2. Or use a clean strainer
3. The wine can be consumed immediately
4. Remaining mycelium can be used to inoculate fresh juice
