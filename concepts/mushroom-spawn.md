# Mushroom Spawn

Mushroom spawn is the vegetative propagating material from
which growing gourmet cloning grow. In Victorian cultivation,
understanding the nature, formation, and management of spawn
was essential for any successful grower.

## What Is Spawn?

Cuthill described spawn as a "tender, subtle article"
requiring careful handling. It functions like a running
underground root, needing time to ramify and strengthen
before producing mushrooms. When properly matured, spawn
grows towards the surface, and upon reaching air, quickly
forms into a mushroom head.

## Natural Formation of Spawn

The elements producing spawn originate in the horse's
stomach, where neither excessive heat nor moisture
interferes with its formation. Horse droppings, properly
attended to, produce splendid spawn in two or three months.
When dried in the sun during summer rather than heated, the
grower is assured of numerous crops.

### Summer Droppings as Spawn

Droppings collected during summer, deposited in a heap and
trod firm, generate plenty of spawn in a short time. By
mid-September they develop a grey, mouldy, thready
appearance with a mushroom-like smell, indicating vigorous
natural spawn ready for bed surfaces.

### Saving Spawn from Old Beds

When destroying an old bed, the edges and driest parts of
the dung contain one mass of superior spawn. This should
be kept carefully in a very dry place and mixed with summer
droppings for the next bed, ensuring continuous crops without
purchasing artificial spawn. This was particularly recommended
for cottagers watching expenses.

## Artificial Spawn

### Brick Spawn

Brick spawn was the standard commercial form. A London brick
measured seven inches square and two inches thick, like a
small paving tile. Cuthill protested against measuring spawn
by the bushel, preferring per-brick pricing at 4d. to 6d.
each. A bushel covered about one hundred square feet of bed.

### Why Use Artificial Spawn?

Though Cuthill produced fine beds without it, artificial
spawn served as insurance against mistakes in [[mushroom-bed-preparation]], making "doubly sure" of a crop and obtaining
an earlier, longer yield. Spawn-makers were very particular
about their materials, and with pure manure and pure spawn,
no spurious fungi could exist on the bed.

## Mill Track Spawn

Mill Track spawn was considered the best of all varieties.
It required no artificial heating beyond summer sun, and
needed only sandy mould and pure droppings, naturally mixed
by the horses' feet on mill tracks and roads. The natural
treading created ideal compaction, road-sand improved
drainage and nutrient retention soil, and gradual summer heating
produced robust spawn without overheating risk.

## Quick Spawn Production

Cuthill described a rapid method: lay a foundation of horse
droppings one square yard, four inches deep. Crumble
artificial spawn into dust and scatter a handful over the
layer, treading hard. Add another layer of droppings and
spawn, repeating until the heap is two feet high. All must
be well trodden to prevent exceeding 80 degrees. Cover with
straw, and in five to six weeks, a fine heap of pure spawn
results. Kept dry, it lasts six months or more.

## Over-Spawning Technique

As a "last guard against failure," Cuthill recommended
chopping a brick or two of spawn into dust and scattering
it over the bed surface before [[mushroom-soil-mould]] is
applied. He had never found over-spawning to prevent good
crops.

## Spawn Depth

Spawn must be planted near the surface. If too deep, it
never thrives. For artificial spawn, merely insert into the
surface and cover lightly with droppings, one foot apart
each way. Natural spawn in summer droppings is spread across
the bed surface and lightly incorporated.

## Enemies of Spawn

The two greatest enemies of spawn were too much heat and too
much water. A bed shut from sun and air cannot evaporate
moisture. The finest crops were produced in dry summers, not
wet ones. Cuthill advised against [[mushroom-watering]] until
the sixth week after spawning, and never before two months
since bed formation. Too much heat and too much water were
the greatest enemies to young spawn at every stage.

## Spawn and Mushroom Quality

Spawn-makers were careful to ensure that poisonous toadstool
spawn never entered their composition. Cuthill noted that the
true mushroom keeps itself distinct above all other fungi,
more so than any other vegetable known. In thirty years of
mushroom growing, he never saw any but the true species on
beds made with proper materials and pure spawn.
