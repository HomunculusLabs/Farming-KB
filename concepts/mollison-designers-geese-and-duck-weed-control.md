# Geese and Duck Weed Control

Mollison highlights geese and ducks as specialised weed control agents that
perform functions difficult or impossible to achieve with machinery or
herbicides. Their different feeding behaviours make them complementary rather
than competing species in integrated permaculture systems.

## Geese as Weeders

Geese are primarily grazers, preferring grasses and broadleaf weeds over
most crops. This makes them ideal for weed control in specific contexts:

**Orchard integration:**
- Geese graze grass competition from beneath fruit trees without damaging
  tree bark (unlike sheep or goats which will strip bark from young trees)
- They prefer grasses over most orchard floor weeds, selectively reducing
  grass competition while leaving beneficial broadleaf plants
- Their droppings fertilise the orchard floor, cycling nutrients
- Mollison recommends 10-15 geese per hectare of mature orchard, reduced
  to 5-8 for young plantings where the grass sward needs protection

**Berry crops:**
- Geese effectively weed strawberry, raspberry, and blueberry plantations
- Their preference for grasses means they leave the berry crop untouched
  while cleaning the inter-row spaces
- Introduce geese after berry plants are well-established (15-20cm tall)
  to prevent accidental damage to young shoots

**Grain crop weeding:**
- A traditional technique in Asia and Europe uses geese to weed rice,
  cotton, and corn fields
- Geese consume competing grasses while leaving the crop undamaged
- Timing is critical -- introduce geese after crop seedlings are tall enough
  to resist trampling but before weeds outcompete the crop

**Species selection:**
- Chinese geese are the most active grazers and best weeders
- Embden geese are larger and hardier in cool climates but less active
- Toulouse geese are docile but less effective as weeders
- Mollison recommends Chinese geese for orchard work and Embden crosses
  for general farm duty

## Ducks as Aquatic Weed Controllers

Ducks serve a complementary role managing aquatic vegetation in ponds,
waterways, and wetland areas:

**Aquatic weed management:**
- Ducks consume duckweed (Lemna spp.), algae, water hyacinth, and other
  aquatic vegetation that can choke ponds and waterways
- A small flock of 6-10 ducks can keep a 0.1 hectare pond clear of
  excessive weed growth through the growing season
- Muscovy ducks are particularly effective, consuming large quantities of
  aquatic vegetation and insects

**Pond integration:**
- Ducks in [[mollison-designers-dam-construction-and-water-storage]]
  provide multiple functions: weed control, insect control, manure for
  pond fertility (feeding phytoplankton and fish), and egg production
- Their dabbling action oxygenates surface water and disturbs sediment,
  releasing nutrients into the water column
- Duck manure increases pond productivity for fish and aquatic plants
  without the eutrophication risk of chemical fertilisers

**Rice paddy integration:**
- Ducks have been used in Asian rice paddies for millennia as weeders,
  insect controllers, and fertilisers
- The traditional aigamo system (Japan) uses duck-rice integration to
  eliminate the need for herbicides and pesticides
- Mollison advocates adapting these techniques for small-scale rice and
  taro production in permaculture systems

**Species selection:**
- Muscovy ducks (Cairina moschata): Best foragers, quiet, excellent
  insect control, adaptable to diverse conditions
- Khaki Campbell: Prolific egg layers, good foragers, hardy in cool
  climates
- Indian Runner: Active foragers, good at controlling slugs and snails
  in garden areas

## Housing and Management

- Geese require minimal housing -- simple three-sided shelters suffice
  in most climates
- Ducks need access to water for foraging, mating, and feather maintenance
- Both species can be herded between work areas using temporary fencing
- Night housing protects against predators (foxes, owls, feral dogs)
- Pair geese or ducks with [[mollison-designers-poultry-forage-design-pastured-systems]]
  for comprehensive vegetation management across the farm

## Integration with Farm Systems

Geese and ducks fill niches that chickens cannot. Geese manage terrestrial
grass competition; ducks manage aquatic vegetation. Together with chickens
(who manage insects and weed seeds), they provide complete vegetation and
pest management for the integrated farm.
