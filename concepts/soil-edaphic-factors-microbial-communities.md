# Soil Edaphic Factors and Microbial Community Structure

## Overview

Edaphic factors are the physical, chemical, and biological
properties of soil that influence microbial [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]].
In the [[cannabis-microbiome-agricultural-implications-and-future-directions]] (Winston et al., 2014), edaphic factors
were the dominant determinant of microbial community structure
across all root-associated compartments, outweighing both sample
type and plant [[cannabis-cultivar-effects-soil-microbiome]].

## Ranking of Edaphic Factors

Analysis using weighted and [[weighted-vs-unweighted-unifrac-cannabis-strain-microbiome]] distance matrices
revealed a consistent hierarchy. For weighted analysis:

1. **Nitrogen** (r-stat: 0.465, p = 0.001)
2. **Salinity** (r-stat: 0.437, p = 0.001)
3. **Total [[dighton-fungal-dissolved-organic-carbon-production]]** (r-stat: 0.330, p = 0.001)
4. **Water Content** (r-stat: 0.281, p = 0.001)
5. **pH** (r-stat: 0.221, p = 0.001)

[[unifrac-weighted-unweighted-analysis-cannabis-microbiome]] showed the same ranking but higher
correlations, particularly for nitrogen (r-stat: 0.630)
and salinity (r-stat: 0.620).

## Nitrogen

[[nitrogen-availability-in-legumes]] is the most powerful edaphic predictor,
reflecting its importance as a limiting nutrient. Different
taxa employ varying nitrogen strategies:

- **Nitrogen fixers** (e.g., Rhizobiales) convert atmospheric
  N2 to bioavailable forms, advantaged in low-N soils.
- **Decomposers** access organic nitrogen from soil organic
  matter, thriving in high-carbon environments.
- **Nitrifiers/denitrifiers** participate [[fungal-role-in-nitrogen-cycling]]
  transformations shaping available N forms.

Cannabis study values ranged from 0.26% to 1.51% total nitrogen.
A significant challenge was that THC composition correlated with
soil edaphic variables, making it difficult to disassociate
microbiome-THC associations from soil-THC relationships.

## Salinity

Salinity was the second strongest predictor (r-stat: 0.437).
High salinity creates osmotic stress eliminating salt-sensitive
taxa while promoting halotolerant organisms. Study salinity
values ranged from 1.73 to 7.44.

Effects are mediated through:
- **Osmotic stress**: Reduces water availability to
  non-adapted microorganisms
- **Ion toxicity**: Na+ and Cl- disrupt cellular processes
- **Structure changes**: High salinity alters aggregate
  stability and pore distribution

## Total Organic Carbon

Carbon reflects the organic matter pool available as substrate
for heterotrophic microorganisms. Higher carbon supports
greater biomass and diversity. Values ranged from 3.02% to
20.0%, with Orange County soil being exceptionally carbon-rich.

Both quantity and quality matter: labile carbon from root
exudates fuels rapid rhizosphere growth, while recalcitrant
carbon supports specialized decomposer communities.

## Water Content

Soil water affects microbial activity through:
- Nutrient diffusion rates in soil solution
- Oxygen availability (waterlogging creates anaerobic zones)
- Microbial motility and substrate access
- Root exudate [[guzman-global-distribution-patterns-neurotropic-fungi]]

Values ranged from 0.101 to 0.371, with Orange County soil
retaining substantially more moisture.
