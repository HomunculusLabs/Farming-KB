# Soil Edaphic Factors and Microbial Communities

## Overview

Edaphic factors (soil physical and chemical properties) are the primary
determinants of microbial community composition in the Cannabis root zone.
Winston et al. (2014) tested five edaphic variables across multiple soil
types and found that all were significantly correlated with community
beta-diversity. Nitrogen was the strongest driver, followed by salinity,
carbon, water content, and pH. A BEST analysis identified the optimal
combination of three factors: Nitrogen, Carbon, and Water (rho = 0.632).

## Edaphic Factor Ranking by Importance

### Weighted UniFrac (Abundance-Sensitive)

All edaphic factors were significantly correlated (p = 0.001) with community
beta-diversity, ranked as follows:

1. **Nitrogen**: r-stat = 0.465, p = 0.001 (strongest effect)
2. **Salinity**: r-stat = 0.437, p = 0.001
3. **Carbon**: r-stat = 0.330, p = 0.001
4. **Water content**: r-stat = 0.281, p = 0.001
5. **pH**: r-stat = 0.221, p = 0.001 (weakest effect)

### Unweighted UniFrac (Presence/Absence)

The relative importance remained the same for composition-based analysis:

1. **Nitrogen**: r-stat = 0.630, p = 0.001
2. **Salinity**: r-stat = 0.620, p = 0.001
3. **Carbon**: r-stat = 0.512, p = 0.001
4. **Water content**: r-stat = 0.466, p = 0.001
5. **pH**: r-stat = 0.292, p = 0.001

## Nitrogen as the Dominant Edaphic Factor

Nitrogen content had the strongest correlation with microbial community
structure in both weighted and unweighted analyses. This finding has
significant implications for Cannabis cultivation, as nitrogen management
directly shapes the soil and rhizosphere microbiome. The study found
substantial variation in total nitrogen between soils:

- MB.1.SD (Sour Diesel soil): 1.51% total nitrogen
- MB.1.B (Burmese soil): 1.41% total nitrogen
- MB.1.BK (Bookoo Kush soil): 1.30% total nitrogen
- OC.2 (Orange County soil): 0.53% total nitrogen
- MB.2 (Mo-Bio experiment 2 soil): 0.26% total nitrogen

The nearly 6-fold difference in nitrogen between the highest and lowest
soils underscores its potential to dramatically reshape microbial
communities.

## Carbon and Organic Matter

Total organic carbon showed strong correlation with community structure and
varied substantially between soil types:

- OC.2 (Orange County soil): 20.0% total organic carbon
- MB.1.B (Burmese soil): 5.00% total organic carbon
- MB.1.SD (Sour Diesel soil): 4.32% total organic carbon
- MB.1.BK (Bookoo Kush soil): 3.31% total organic carbon
- MB.2 (Mo-Bio experiment 2 soil): 3.02% total organic carbon

The Orange County soil had approximately 6.6 times more organic carbon than
the lowest soil, which aligns with its higher water content (0.371 vs 0.101-
0.178 in other soils).

## Salinity

Salinity was the second strongest edaphic factor in both analyses. The study
soils showed substantial salinity variation:

- MB.1.BK (Bookoo Kush soil): 7.44 (highest salinity)
- MB.1.B (Burmese soil): 7.15
- MB.1.SD (Sour Diesel soil): 7.10
- MB.2 (Mo-Bio experiment 2 soil): 5.12
- OC.2 (Orange County soil): 1.73 (lowest salinity)

The low salinity of the Orange County soil, combined with its high carbon and
water content, created a distinctly different microbial environment compared
to the other soils.

## pH

Soil pH was the weakest but still significant edaphic factor. All soils were
slightly acidic to near-neutral, with a relatively narrow range:

- MB.1.B (Burmese soil): pH 6.94
- MB.1.SD (Sour Diesel soil): pH 6.80
- MB.1.BK (Bookoo Kush soil): pH 6.82
- OC.2 (Orange County soil): pH 6.77
- MB.2 (Mo-Bio experiment 2 soil): pH 6.63

The limited pH variation (range of only 0.31 units) likely explains why pH
had the weakest correlation despite being well-established in other studies as
a major driver of soil microbial communities.

## Water Content

Water content varied significantly between soils and was the fourth strongest
edaphic factor:

- OC.2 (Orange County soil): 0.371
- MB.1.SD (Sour Diesel soil): 0.178
- MB.1.B (Burmese soil): 0.164
- MB.2 (Mo-Bio experiment 2 soil): 0.113
- MB.1.BK (Bookoo Kush soil): 0.101

## Soil Texture

All soils were classified as sandy loam with broadly similar physical
composition:

- Sand content: 62.0 to 66.0%
- Silt content: 16.0 to 17.7%
- Clay content: 17.7 to 20.7%

Despite similar textures, significant differences in clay and other edaphic
factors existed between the two soil types in the second experiment,
contributing to the strong soil-type effect on microbial communities.

## BEST Analysis: Optimal Factor Combination

A BEST (Best Subset of Environmental Variables with Maximum Rank Correlation)
analysis determined that the variance in community data is optimally explained
by a combination of three edaphic factors: Nitrogen, Carbon, and Water
(rho = 0.632). This suggests these three factors capture the majority of
edaphic influence on the Cannabis microbiome, while salinity and pH provide
additional but partially redundant information.
