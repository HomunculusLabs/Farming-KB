## Salinity

Salinity was the second strongest predictor (r-stat: 0.437).
High salinity creates osmotic stress eliminating salt-sensitive
taxa while promoting halotolerant organisms. Study salinity
values ranged from 1.73 to 7.44.

Effects are mediated through:
- **Osmotic stress**: Reduces water availability to
  non-adapted microorganisms
- **Ion toxicity**: Na+ and Cl- disrupt cellular processes
- **Structure changes**: High salinity alters aggregate
  stability and pore distribution

## Total Organic Carbon

Carbon reflects the organic matter pool available as substrate
for heterotrophic microorganisms. Higher carbon supports
greater biomass and diversity. Values ranged from 3.02% to
20.0%, with Orange County soil being exceptionally carbon-rich.

Both quantity and quality matter: labile carbon from root
exudates fuels rapid rhizosphere growth, while recalcitrant
carbon supports specialized decomposer communities.

## Water Content

Soil water affects microbial activity through:
- Nutrient diffusion rates in soil solution
- Oxygen availability (waterlogging creates anaerobic zones)
- Microbial motility and substrate access
- Root exudate [[guzman-global-distribution-patterns-neurotropic-fungi]]

Values ranged from 0.101 to 0.371, with Orange County soil
retaining substantially more moisture.
