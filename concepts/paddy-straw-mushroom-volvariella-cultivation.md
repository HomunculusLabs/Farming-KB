# Paddy Straw Mushroom (Volvariella volvacea) Cultivation

Paddy straw mushroom is the most popular mushroom in rural areas of the
Philippines, commonly known as kabuteng dayami or kabuteng saging, naturally
growing on paddy straw and decomposing banana leaves and pseudostems. Commercial
production in China, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Thailand is well-developed, but in
the Philippines it remains primarily a backyard undertaking with approximately
500 growers nationwide. The mushroom has also gained popularity in Thailand where
rice farmers cultivate it after harvest, and in Swaziland and other African
countries as a poverty alleviation crop.

## Species Characteristics

V. volvacea is a high-temperature mushroom with optimal growth at 30-37C. It is
a secondary saprophyte requiring partially decomposed organic matter, similar to
button mushroom. The production cycle is very short at just 3 weeks from spawning
to harvest, making it attractive for quick returns. The mushroom is rich in
protein and immunobiological activities have been reported. Fresh mushrooms sell
for PHP150-180/kg (USD3-3.5) in the Philippines.

## Outdoor Cultivation

The traditional outdoor method uses the bed-type approach with agricultural wastes
including paddy straw, rice stubbles, water lily, and banana leaves.

### Site Selection and Preparation
Choose a pest-free area, preferably under wide-canopy trees. Spread rice hulls
and burn to ash to eliminate ants, termites, rodents, and soil-borne pathogens.

### Bed Preparation
Sun-dry collected bedding materials, trim and bundle into 12-inch lengths with
2-inch diameter. Soak bundles for 12 hours and wash with clean water. Drain to
65% moisture content. Pile bundles into beds with spawn sprinkled thinly on each
layer. Ideal beds have six layers and 3-meter length.

### Incubation and Fruiting
Cover the entire bed with a plastic sheet to maintain 30-35C for mycelial
ramification and 28-30C for fruiting. First flush of marketable button-stage
mushrooms appears from the bed edge within 10-14 days. Harvest by pulling
clusters out by hand.

## Indoor Cultivation

Indoor cultivation involves three salient features: composting, pasteurization,
and growing house cultivation. This method achieves much higher yields of 50-60%
compared to 20% for traditional outdoor methods.

### Soaking and Composting
Soak rice straw 12 hours in clean water to loosen substrates. Pile and sprinkle
with 1% molasses and 0.5% complete fertilizer. Cover with plastic and compost
for 14 days. Turn on day 7, add 1% agricultural lime, and continue composting.

### Crating and Steaming
Place composted substrate in 12x24x18 inch wooden crates (open on all sides) at
65% moisture. Stack crates in steaming room. Steam at 60-80C for 4-6 hours.

### Spawning
Next morning, check substrate temperature is 30C before inoculating.

### Incubation and Fruiting
Seal the mushroom house. Maintain 30-35C with no ventilation or light for 7-10
days for mycelial proliferation. When fruiting initials appear, lower temperature
to 28C by sprinkling water on the floor. First harvest occurs 3-5 days after
fruiting initials appear.

## Thai Cultivation Methods

Thailand has cultivated straw mushrooms since the 1940s. A simple method uses
inoculated straw bales left in bags. Traditional rice field cultivation uses
rows of mushroom mounds made from rice straw and other agro wastes with wooden
frames, producing from December to April at THB20/kg with 20% yield.

Indoor Thai cultivation achieves 50-60% yield. Compost is placed on shelves and
steamed at 60C for 4-6 hours. When room temperature drops to 35C, spawn is laid
2cm deep into or on top of compost. Mushrooms sell fresh at THB30/kg or to
canneries at THB12-16/kg. Net profit averages THB5,000 (USD129) per month per
grower. Spent inky cap substrate can be reused for straw mushroom production.

## Spawn Production

Multiple locally available substrates serve as spawning material:
- Tobacco midrib (waste from cigar industry): soaked 3 days, washed, air-dried,
  mixed with sawdust, sterilized by autoclaving
- Rice hull (waste from rice milling): moistened with 10% corn meal or rice bran
  in polypropylene bags or plastic trays
- Leguminous tree leaves (Gliricidia, Leucaena): soaked 3 days, air-dried,
  mixed 7:3:1 with sawdust and rice bran
- Coffee hulls: used in coffee-growing areas
- All preparations use 65% moisture content

## Feasibility and Economics

The Philippines' tropical climate (30-35C max, 55-225mm rainfall) is favorable
year-round across Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao. Abundant substrates include
paddy straw, water lilies, and banana leaves. Market supply remains erratic,
making it a luxury food item with strong demand. The short production cycle
(3 weeks) allows multiple crops per year and fast return on investment.

## Zero Waste Technology

Spent substrate from V. volvacea cultivation can be efficiently utilized as
substrate for [[oyster-mushroom-bag-cultivation]], [[ganoderma-lucidum-cultivation]], and Collybia species. It also serves as fishpond fertilizer for
tilapia and feed for broiler chickens, creating an integrated farming system.
