## House Layout and Zone Connection

- Efficient layout: mud room --> pantry --> kitchen --> living room --> bedrooms (upstairs)
- Most intensive garden section is around garden entries
- You "can't depart very much from it" -- function follows zone
- Pathways emerge naturally: from parking, to barn, to wherever you go

## Herb Spiral and Key Herbs

- Place a little herb spiral near the entry, plus a great mass of parsley
- Critical herbs: chives (Chinese, ordinary purple-top, fine-leaf blue), garlic, tarragon, thyme, rosemary, sage
- Parsley: start a bed, let go to seed, pepper heads throughout garden, use as mulch; "once you get parsley thick, you never have to worry about it again"
- Mint: grow in pots (invasive); black mint for menthol distillation
- Dill: grows well throughout garden; if only seed is gathered, doesn't need to be close

## Vegetable Zoning by Distance

**Near door:** lettuce, cabbage, plucking herbs (celery, minor tomatoes, NZ spinach, broccoli, zucchini)
**Mid-distance:** peppers, tomatoes, radishes (catch crop everywhere)
**Further out:** root crops (beets for greens, winter squashes, corn, carrots, main crop onions, parsnips)
**Main crop:** winter-keeping squashes, corn, parsnips, long-term cabbages
- Nasturtium: leaves tasty in salad, scattered here and there
- Jerusalem artichoke: forms quick barrier hedge by summer

## Specific Crop Techniques

**Potatoes (straw box):**
- Board up 8'x5' area; throw in straw or seaweed; set potatoes 9"x9"; scatter ashes; fill with straw
- No soil, no bottom; poles for frame; bark rots off adding nutrient
- Can grow on concrete; works for 12+ years in same box
- Comfrey leaf under each potato = potash and nutrients; mint nearby for cooking

**Tomatoes:**
- Never rotate; grow better on same spot; set up permanent tomato bed
- South-facing shelter needed (wind-sensitive); Jerusalem artichoke windbreak
- Companions: basil (hot side), parsley (cool side), nasturtium (white fly control), marigolds
- Gooseberries: control specific tomato pests
- Never re-buy seed; mulch tomatoes in autumn --> 200 volunteer seedlings per spot
- Pinch out axil shoots for succession planting
- Marigolds (Tagetes minuta) for eelworm/nematode control

**Corn system:**
- Band of corn ~4 feet across, hollow in middle
- Manure pile in center with cucurbits (runner pumpkins, melons, watermelons)
- Climbing beans around the edge
- Harvest corn and beans; when finished, pumpkins have dropped everywhere
- Keeps seed (beans, corn) for next year

**Horseradish:**
- Four broken earthenware pots sunk in ground, open at top
- Refill with good earth each year; easy to break straight root, easy to lift

## Sheet Mulching vs Composting

- Composting decreases nutrients to ~1/12th of original; most goes into air
- Sheet mulching: spread materials, let process occur slowly on soil surface
- Leach loss goes into soil; activity spreads across whole area
- Mulch permeated with 70% nitrogen; "you will never see nitrogen deficiency"
- Worm castings: highest tonnage per acre, best manure
- pH stratification: can go from pH 6 to pH 3 in layers; "Everything" pH levels

## Frog Ponds and Mosquito Control

- Small ponds (4' across, 18" deep) produce 200-300 frogs twice per summer
- Build pond slightly higher than surrounding soil, paved with stones
- Sweet alyssum and thyme between stones for frog exit ramps
- Stone pile in pond for frog refuge
- **Mosquito control:** garlic bulbs squeezed into pond water (~100% larval kill); garlic doesn't kill tadpoles
- Backswimmers (not assasssin bugs) as secondary control

## Trellising

- Deciduous trellis around kitchen windows: hops, grapes, runner beans
- Hops: "noble vine," excellent for light pillows; child-safe
- Evergreen climbers for wind control: honeysuckle, jasmine, lilac
- Horizontal trellis for summer heat control; summer green + winter deciduous crops
- Stone walls: 40% penetrability, flat stones; soft plants above wall to reduce turbulence
- Ivy on north-facing brick walls: 40% efficient against heat escape, preserves brickwork

## Shade Fruits

- Raspberry, strawberry, black currant, gooseberry (especially green group) all do well in tree shade patterns
- Everbearing strawberries; fuchsias outside bedroom window
- Dwarf fruit trees: peaches and citrus (only area for dwarfs recommended)
- Cordon system for apples/pears: 4-foot cordons, each a different variety
- California example: 150 apple varieties on tiny quarter-acre, "bushels and bushels"
