## Psychedelic Psychotherapy

Psychedelic psychotherapy refers to therapeutic approaches that use
psychedelic substances within a structured clinical setting to treat
psychological conditions including depression, anxiety, PTSD, and
addiction. The modern renaissance builds upon decades of earlier
clinical work that was largely halted by regulatory restrictions in
the 1970s.

## Historical Context: LSD Discovery

Albert Hofmann discovered LSD"s psychoactive properties in 1943,
launching a wave of psychiatric research. By the mid-1950s, over
1,000 clinical papers had been published on LSD-assisted therapy.
Stanislav Grof made a landmark discovery during this era. As patients
moved through successive low-dose sessions, he observed that deeper
layers of the unconscious opened beyond Freudian dynamics, revealing
birth and pre-birth memories previously unknown to clinical psychology.

## European Psycholytic Therapy

The European psycholytic model, developed primarily in the UK and
continental Europe, used low to moderate doses of LSD in a series of
sessions (often 30 or more) combined with psychoanalytic talk therapy.
The term "psycholytic" means "mind-dissolving," reflecting the goal
of loosening psychological defenses to access unconscious material.
This approach was pioneered by Ronald Sandison in the UK and Hanscarl
Leuner in Germany.

## Anglo-American Psychedelic Therapy

The Anglo-American model used high doses in fewer sessions, aiming
for a single overwhelming [[dosage-unpredictability-psilocybin-subjective-experience]] of ego
dissolution and allows novel patterns of neural communication.
Psychedelics also promote structural neuroplasticity, increasing
dendritic spine formation and synaptogenesis through activation of
the 5-HT2A receptor and downstream BDNF [[plant-signaling-pathways]].

## Safety and Contraindications

Psychedelic therapy is not appropriate for all individuals.
