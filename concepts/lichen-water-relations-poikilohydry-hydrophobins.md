# Lichen Water Relations — Poikilohydry and Hydrophobin-Mediated Water Management

## Overview

Lichens dominate approximately 10% of terrestrial ecosystems — primarily polar, alpine, and desert regions where higher plants are at their physiological limits. Their remarkable environmental stress tolerance derives from **poikilohydric water relations** (equilibrating body water content with atmospheric humidity) and sophisticated structural adaptations that manage water movement within the thallus. The fungal partner (mycobiont), which typically constitutes >80% of thallus biomass, constructs elaborate water-channeling structures using **hydrophobins** and polyphenolic [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]].

## Lichen Thallus Architecture

### Heteromerous (Internally Stratified) Thalli

Most morphologically complex lichen thalli are built from two fundamental tissue types:

1. **Conglutinate pseudoparenchyma**: Tissue-like, densely packed hyphae forming cortical layers and internal strands. These are **hydrophilic** and passively absorb water and dissolved nutrients. They provide mechanical stability.

2. **Plectenchyma**: Loosely interwoven, gas-filled zones built by aerial hyphae with **hydrophobic surfaces**. Found in the medullary and algal layers.

The [[fungal-hyphae]] can change their growth patterns and wall surface properties over distances of just a few micrometres, creating precisely regulated hydrophilic/hydrophobic interfaces.

### The Symbiotic Chamber

Morphologically complex lichen thalli function as **sophisticated culturing chambers** for a population of minute photobiont cells. The fungal partner:

- Positions photobiont cells at optimal locations for photosynthesis and gas exchange (within the algal layer).
- Controls water and nutrient access via passive apoplastic fluxes.
- Protects photobionts from UV radiation and desiccation.

## Hydrophobin-Derived Wall Surface Hydrophobicity

### The Hydrophobic Rodlet Layer

A semicrystalline **rodlet layer** on wall surfaces of medullary hyphae provides the primary water-repellent barrier. This layer is formed by **class 1 hydrophobins** — small, cysteine-rich fungal proteins that self-assemble at hydrophilic/hydrophobic interfaces.

**Evidence:**
- Freeze-etch preparations resolved rodlet layers on medullary hyphae of *Xanthoria* and *Peltigera* spp.
- Similar rodlet layers found on aerial hyphae in the photobiont stratum of the lichenized basidiocarps of *Dictyonema glabratum*.
- Characterized in *Xanthoria* spp. (one hydrophobin per species, e.g., XPH1) and *Dictyonema glabratum* (three different hydrophobins).

### Developmental Regulation

Hydrophobin gene expression is **developmentally regulated** in lichenized asco- and basidiomycetes, as in many non-lichenized fungi. Different hydrophobins are expressed in different thallus regions at different [[psilocybe-cubensis-developmental-stages]].

### Enhancement by Secondary Metabolites

Mycobiont-derived polyphenolic secondary metabolites crystallize on and within the water-repellent proteinaceous rodlet layer, **enhancing hydrophobicity**:

- Species with medullary lichen compounds (e.g., *[[hypogymnia-physodes]]*) have both protein-based hydrophobicity and crystalline secondary metabolite enhancement.
- Even species without medullary compounds (e.g., *Xanthoria*, *Peltigera*) maintain hydrophobicity through hydrophobins alone.

### Water Channeling Mechanism

The hydrophobic surface layer overlays highly hydrophilic, often very thick glucan layers of hyphal walls (including the (1→3),(1→4)-β-glucan **lichenin**). During wetting and drying cycles:

- Water-repellent wall surfaces **channel the flow of solutes** from the thallus surface to the interior and vice versa.
- The hydrophobic lining prevents the algal and medullary layers from becoming **waterlogged** at high hydration levels.
- Passive fluxes of solutes occur underneath this hydrophobic barrier.
- Photobiont cells access water and dissolved nutrients **only via passive apoplastic fluxes** within the fungal exhabitant.

## Poikilohydry and Desiccation Tolerance

### Poikilohydric Water Relations

Lichen-forming fungi and their photobionts are **poikilohydric** — their water content equilibrates with atmospheric conditions rather than being actively regulated. This contrasts with homoiohydric (homeostatic) higher plants.

### Desiccation Tolerance

- Desiccated thalli of *[[xanthoria-parietina]]* grew normally after cryoimmobilization in sub-cooled liquid nitrogen (c. −200°C), freeze-fracturing, and examination under high vacuum.
- **Hydrated thalli did not survive** this treatment — desiccation is essential for stress tolerance.
- Desiccated cells survive temperature extremes in a state of dormancy.
- Most lichen-forming fungi die under **continuously moist conditions** or when prevented from drying at elevated temperatures.

### Consequences for Distribution

- Abundant epiphytic lichen growth in boreal to [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]].
- **Bryophytes** dominate epiphytic communities in temperate rainforests.
- **Tracheophytes** (ferns, bromeliads, orchids) dominate in tropical rainforests.
- Biological soil crust communities in arid ecosystems are ecologically important stabilizers, reducing wind erosion.

## Photobiont Types and Habitat

| Photobiont Type | % of Lichen-Forming Ascomycetes | Habitat Preference | Rehydration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green algae (chlorolichens) | ~85% | Arid, hot, or cold ecosystems | Can rehydrate with water vapour alone |
| Cyanobacteria (cyanolichens) | ~10% | Moist habitats | Require liquid water for full recovery |
| Both (cephalodiate) | ~4% | Variable | Mixed strategies |

### Trebouxia-Dominated Chlorolichens

*Trebouxia* (Trebouxiophyceae) is the photobiont for >50% of lichen-forming ascomycetes. Drought-stressed chlorolichens with Trebouxia can **rehydrate and re-establish partial [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]] with water vapour** alone, giving them a competitive advantage in arid ecosystems.

### Cyanolichens

- Internally stratified cyanolichens (*Peltigera*, *Sticta*, *Nephroma*) are abundant in moist habitats.
- Gelatinous cyanolichens with non-stratified thalli (*Collema*) tolerate intermediate conditions.
- Require **liquid water** for full metabolic recovery.

### Cephalodiate Lichens (Triple Symbiosis)

~4% of lichens have both a green algal partner (fixed carbon) and a diazotrophic cyanobacterial partner (fixed nitrogen). The fungal partner creates **microaerobic conditions** around the cyanobacterial colony, resulting in increased heterocyst frequencies and enhanced [[biological-nitrogen-fixation-agriculture]] compared with the free-living state.

## Polyphenolic Secondary Metabolites

Lichen-forming ascomycetes produce a wide range of polyphenolic secondary metabolites that crystallize at hyphal surfaces in the medullary layer and/or within the peripheral cortex:

- Give thalli their **characteristic coloration**.
- Most cortical compounds **absorb UV light** and transmit longer wavelengths, protecting both fungal and photobiont cells from radiation damage.
- Enhance the hydrophobicity of the medullary and algal layers.
