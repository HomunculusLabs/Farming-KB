# Nutrient Solution and Reservoir Management

Proper nutrient solution and reservoir management are the backbone of
successful [[cervantes-passive-wick-hydroponic-systems]] cannabis cultivation.
Jorge Cervantes provides detailed guidance on maintaining solution quality,
temperature control, irrigation scheduling, and troubleshooting nutrient
disorders in recirculating systems.

## Reservoir Temperature Control

The temperature of the nutrient solution should stay between 60 to 75 degrees
F (15 to 24 degrees C). However, the solution holds much more dissolved
oxygen at 60 degrees F (15 degrees C) than at 75 degrees F (24 degrees C).

### Critical Temperature Thresholds

- **Never exceed 85 degrees F (29 degrees C)**: Above this point, the
  solution holds very little dissolved oxygen
- **Heat-damaged roots** are extremely susceptible to [[cannabis-root-rot-prevention-and-treatment]],
  wilts, and fungus gnat attacks
- **Colder is generally better**: Lower temperatures promote nutrient uptake
  and help control humidity through reduced transpiration

### Heating the Solution

In cold environments, heat the nutrient solution rather than the room air to
save energy. Methods include:
- **Submersible aquarium heaters** - Most common; can take a day or longer to
  warm a large volume of solution
- **Grounded propagation heating cables** - Alternative heating method

Important safety notes:
- Never leave heaters in an empty reservoir; they will overheat and burn out
- Avoid submersible heaters that release harmful residues into the solution
- Many aquarium heaters lack ground wires, though electrocution incidents are
  rare

### Cooling the Solution

Maintaining solution temperature around 60 degrees F (15 degrees C) helps
control both transpiration and ambient humidity. An air pump submerged in the
reservoir not only aerates the solution but helps level out temperature
differentials between ambient air and the reservoir.

## Nutrient Solution Preparation

### Two-Part Solutions

A two-part nutrient solution is mixed before application. Each reservoir holds
one part of the solution, which are combined at the time of use. This
prevents premature chemical reactions between concentrated nutrient
components that can cause precipitation and nutrient lockout.

### EC and pH Monitoring

Regularly monitor the electrical conductivity (EC) and pH of the nutrient
solution:
- **pH range for hydroponics**: 5.5 to 6.5
- **EC**: Varies by growth stage and nutrient brand; follow manufacturer
  guidelines
- Check pH and EC at least once a week
- pH and EC gradually change between irrigation cycles as plants absorb
  nutrients at different rates

## Irrigation Scheduling

Irrigation cycles depend on plant size, climate conditions, and the type of
[[cervantes-growing-medium]] used:

### By Medium Type

- **Fast-draining substrates** (expanded clay, perlite): Irrigate four to
  twelve times daily in five to thirty-minute cycles; often run 24 hours a day
- **Coco coir**: Drip irrigation four to five times daily
- **Fibrous mediums** (vermiculite): Drain slowly; may need only once-daily
  watering
- **Flood and drain**: Five to ten cycles daily

### Top-Feed Systems

Top-feed systems cycle for about five minutes or longer and should be
irrigated at least three times daily. Overhead irrigation in fast-draining
mediums can be continuous.

## Nutrient Disorders in Hydroponics

Hydroponic gardens have no soil to buffer nutrient uptake, so disorders
manifest rapidly as discolored foliage, slow growth, or spotting.

### Diagnosis Checklist

When nutrient problems affect multiple plants:
1. Check irrigation fittings to ensure all plants receive full nutrient dose
2. Inspect substrate to ensure solution penetrates the entire medium
3. Check root zone - ensure roots have not plugged drainage or are standing
  in stagnant solution
4. Verify pH is within the 5.5 to 6.5 range before adding new nutrients
5. Change the nutrient solution completely if problems persist
6. Consider switching to a different fertilizer brand

### Treating Deficiencies and Excesses

- **Deficiencies**: Add 10 to 20 percent more of the deficient nutrient in
  chelated form until the disorder disappears; foliar feeding provides a fast
  fix
- **Excess/over-fertilization**: Drain the solution, flush the system at
  least twice with fresh dilute (5 to 10 percent) nutrient solution to remove
  sediment and salt buildup, then replace with properly mixed solution

### Common Pitfalls

- Do not confuse wind burn, lack of light, temperature stress, or pest damage
  with nutrient deficiencies
- Different cannabis varieties often react differently to the same nutrient
  solution
- When two or more elements are deficient or in excess simultaneously,
  symptoms may not point directly at the cause - changing the entire nutrient
  solution often resolves complex, hard-to-diagnose problems

## Solution Change Frequency

Change the nutrient solution periodically even when plants appear healthy.
Between irrigations, EC and pH gradually shift as plants selectively absorb
nutrients. If enough time passes, nutrient concentration can change so much
that plants cannot draw it in effectively. A complete solution change every
one to two weeks is standard practice.

## Reservoir Setup Tips

- Set reservoirs high enough so spent solution can be siphoned or
  gravity-flow into a drain or outdoor garden
- Use large reservoirs to reduce the frequency of manual topping off
- Cover reservoirs to limit evaporation and prevent algae growth
- Sterilize reservoirs between crops to prevent disease carryover
