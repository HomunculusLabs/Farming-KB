# Garden Hand Tools

## Overview

The basic techniques of practical [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] draw from traditional methods. The spade has been with us since Roman times and remains the best tool for digging; the hoe has controlled weeds since cultivation began. Organic techniques should combine the best of modern technology with tried-and-tested traditional methods. Buy the best quality you can afford -- cheap tools do not last and make work harder. Buy slowly, one tool at a time if budget is tight. With spades and forks especially, do not buy tools that are too large for you.

## Essential Large Tools

### Digging Fork

Second only to the spade in usefulness. Invaluable for loosening soil without inverting it. If you have only one tool, a fork can substitute for the spade in most jobs (and doubles as a rake). Buy forged steel, never pressed steel (which bends under pressure). Fitted with wooden or metal handle.

### Spade

The most-used tool in the garden. Stainless steel is best -- soil falls off the polished surface regardless of stickiness. If you cannot afford stainless steel, buy strong forged steel and always keep it clean. Once "broken in" (blade sharp, corners rounded), digging becomes much easier.

### Rakes

Forged steel rakes are best. Those with more than 12 teeth are difficult to use unless you are very strong. A spring-tine (lawn) rake is useful for removing dead thatch from lawns and raking in [[disposable-tray-casing-method-grass-seed-substrate]].

### Hoes

Own two hoes minimum:
- **Dutch (push) hoe:** Push backward and forward while walking backward to avoid treading weeds back in. Keep blade no more than 1/2in below surface
- **Swan-necked (draw) hoe:** For weeds too large for the Dutch hoe, earthing-up vegetables, and making seed rows
- **Onion hoe:** Small, ideal for working between closely planted items on [[acid-and-alkaline-soil-management]] or water retention.

### Shovel

Not simply a large spade -- the angled blade makes shoveling quicker. Main use for mixing [[holzer-grafting-fruit-tree-propagation]] work.

### Edging Knife

Half-moon-shaped tool for cutting lawn edges. Some gardeners manage fine with just a spade.

### Secateurs

Two types: anvil and parrot-bill. Both work well for most [[coleman-drip-irrigation-and-water-management]] can be left permanently under plastic mulches.

### Sprinkler

Choose one with a fine spray pattern on a tall stand. A fine-spray sprinkler works for both seedbeds and lawns.

### Sprayer

Choose one that breaks up solution into small droplets for even coverage. This means fewer chemicals are needed for [[deep-bed-cultivation]], make a 4ft board with 6in divisions.

### Dibber

Made from a broken spade or fork handle, cut to 12in and shaved to a point. Used for transplanting seedlings.

### Garden Line

Two pegs and a length of thick nylon string for marking seed rows and digging areas. Make at least two.

### Tool Cleaner

Wooden spade-shaped scraper carried in pocket. Use regularly while digging to remove soil from blades. Clean all tools before putting away and rub with oily cloth to prevent rust.

### Firming Board

1/2in thick wood cut slightly smaller than seed trays with a handle. For leveling and firming compost in trays before sowing.

## Power Equipment

### Rotary Cultivator

Invaluable for large gardens: creates fine seedbeds quickly and incorporates [[green-manure-crops]], compost, and [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] into soil. However, hand dig at least part of the garden annually -- constant rotary cultivating can create an impermeable "glazed" layer. Most cultivate to 6-9in depth.

### String Trimmer

Nylon line cuts grass right up to walls and trees without damage. Available as gas, electric, or battery-powered.

### Shredder

Electrically or gas-driven; shreds woody prunings finely. The resulting material is excellent for mulching-guide|mulching -- a cheap alternative to bark chips.

### Hedge Trimmer

Useful for large hedges. Available as battery, electric, or gas models. Does almost as well as hand shears but not quite.

### Lawn Mower

Cylinder mowers give the best finish. Rotary mowers perform better on tall grass. Electric mowers are cheap and safe but the cord is a disadvantage in large gardens.
