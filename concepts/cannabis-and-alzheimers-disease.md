## Overview

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by beta-amyloid plaque accumulation, neurofibrillary tangles, and cognitive decline. Cannabis research suggests neuroprotective properties through cannabinoid receptor mechanisms. See also cannabinoid-receptors, [[cannabis-for-parkinsons-disease]], and [[cannabis-and-epilepsy]].



## Clinical Evidence

### CHI Score: 10 (Moderate Evidence)

Four studies support the therapeutic potential of cannabinoids in Alzheimer's disease.

### Key Findings

**Beta-Amyloid Clearance**
- CB2 receptor agonist JWH-015 has been shown to effectively remove beta-amyloid from affected brain tissue
- The endocannabinoid system plays a role in regulating neuroinflammation and neuronal survival

**Neuroprotection**
- Cannabinoids demonstrate neuroprotective properties that may slow disease progression
- The US Department of Health and Human Services holds a patent (US6630507, filed 2003) on cannabinoids as neuroprotectants — specifically citing their antioxidant properties and ability to prevent neurodegeneration

**Mechanism of Action**
- CB1 receptors are densely expressed in brain regions most affected by Alzheimer's: hippocampus, cerebral cortex, and basal ganglia
- Activation of cannabinoid receptors may reduce neuroinflammation and excitotoxicity
- Potential to protect neurons from oxidative stress and beta-amyloid toxicity



## Therapeutic Considerations

### Strain Selection
- High-CBD strains may be preferable for neuroprotection without psychoactive effects
- CB2-targeted compounds (like JWH-015 studied in research) avoid psychoactivity while addressing neuroinflammation
- Whole-plant preparations may provide entourage benefits through multiple cannabinoid and terpene interactions

### Administration
- Oral preparations or tinctures for consistent dosing
- Starting with low doses and titrating gradually
- Long-term use may be necessary for neuroprotective effects

### Integrative Approaches
- Combination with conventional AD treatments under medical supervision
- Cognitive stimulation, exercise, and anti-inflammatory diet as complementary strategies
- Monitoring by neurologist recommended



## Safety Profile

- Low risk of adverse effects at therapeutic doses
- CBD-dominant preparations minimize psychoactive side effects important in elderly patients
- Potential drug interactions with cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) — monitor closely



## Limitations and Research Needs

- Most evidence comes from in vitro and animal studies
- Human clinical trials are limited in number and scope
- Long-term effects of cannabinoid use in AD patients not well characterized
- Optimal dosing protocols not yet established
