## Overview

Hongo (1978) proposed a classification of nine distribution types for Japanese fungi that has proven broadly applicable to fungal biogeography worldwide. Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (1998) demonstrated that these nine distribution patterns can be clearly observed in neurotropic fungi across all continents, providing a useful framework for understanding the global distribution of psychoactive mushrooms.

## The Nine Distribution Types

### Type 1: Cosmopolitan Species

Species with worldwide distribution found across multiple continents and climate zones.

**Neurotropic fungi examples:**
- **Panaeolus spp.** — several species found on every continent
- **[[guzman-panaeolina-foenisecii-psilocybin-controversy-conflicting-chemical-analyses]]** — the haymaker's mushroom, reported from grasslands worldwide

These species benefit from coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) habits that allow dispersal through livestock movement. Their spores can survive passage through animal digestive systems, enabling global distribution wherever suitable herbivores exist.

### Type 2: Northern Hemisphere Species

Species distributed primarily across the northern hemisphere, typically in temperate and boreal regions.

**Neurotropic fungi examples:**
- **Amanita muscaria** — circumboreal distribution across Eurasia and North America
- **Cordyceps spp.** — several species in northern temperate regions
- **Psilocybe pelliculosa** — [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] of North America
- **Psilocybe silvatica** — coniferous forests of North America and Eurasia

These species are adapted to temperate and boreal forest ecosystems and are limited by their inability to tolerate tropical conditions.

### Type 3: Eurasian Species

Species restricted to the Eurasian landmass.

**Neurotropic fungi examples:**
- **Amanita muscaria** (core distribution) — Eurasian
- **Psilocybe serbica** — Central Europe, later considered synonymous with P. cyanescens
- **Psilocybe bohemica** — Czech Republic, also placed in P. cyanescens synonymy

### Type 4: North American and Eastern Asiatic Species

Disjunct distributions between eastern North America and eastern Asia, reflecting ancient boreotropical connections. Not well documented in strictly neurotropic fungi, though [[psilocybe-aztecorum-section-aztecorum-alpine-neurotropic-fungi-guzman-1998]] connections between Mexican alpine and Pacific Northwest species may reflect remnants of this pattern.

### Type 5: Far Eastern Species

Species primarily in eastern Asia (Japan, Korea, China). Clear examples are limited from neurotropic fungi, partly because [[guzman-allen-gartz-mycological-exploration-bias-and-underexplored-regions]] in eastern Asia has focused less on psychoactive species.

### Type 6: Southeastern Asiatic Species

Species of tropical and subtropical Southeast Asia.

**Neurotropic fungi examples:**
- **Psilocybe subaeruginascens** var. **subaeruginascens** — known from Japan and Java
- **Psilocybe subaeruginascens** var. **septentrionalis** — restricted to Japan
- **Gerronema fibula** — reported from Malaysia, New Guinea, Solomon Islands, and South America

The Southeastern Asiatic distribution overlaps significantly with the tropical/subtropical type, reflecting the continuous tropical belt from Southeast Asia through the [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]].

### Type 7: Tropical and Subtropical Species

Species of low-latitude regions worldwide.

**Neurotropic fungi examples:**
- **Psilocybe cubensis** — pantropical, the most widely cultivated psychoactive mushroom
- **Psilocybe subcubensis** — tropical and subtropical Americas
- **[[copelandia-cyanescens]]** — primarily tropical, with some temperate exceptions
- **[[copelandia-tropicalis]]** — tropical regions
- **Gerronema fibula** — Southeast Asia, New Guinea, Solomon Islands, and South America

This is the dominant distribution type for neurotropic fungi, consistent with the observation that the majority of Psilocybe species occur in or near the [[biogeography-austral-hemisphere-fungi]] in subtropical humid forests.

### Type 8: Arctic and Alpine Species

Species restricted to high-latitude or high-elevation habitats.

**Neurotropic fungi examples:**
- **Psilocybe aztecorum** var. **aztecorum** — subalpine and alpine habitats in Mexican high mountains (up to 4,000 m elevation), with strong relationships to P. baeocystis (Pacific Northwest) and P. quebecensis (Quebec)
- **Panaeolus moellerianus** — northern species, Faroe Islands
- **Panaeolus olivaceus** — Faroe Islands

The alpine Psilocybe species demonstrate that psychoactive compounds can evolve independently [[fungal-biodiversity-in-extreme-environments]]. The connection between Mexican alpine species and North American temperate species within Section Aztecorum suggests historical migration corridors through high-elevation habitats.
