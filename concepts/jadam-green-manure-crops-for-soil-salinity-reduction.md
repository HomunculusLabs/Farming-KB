## Rye for Salt Elimination

Rye is among the most effective green manure crops for addressing soil
salinity in open fields. Its strong nutrient absorption capacity means it
actively pulls excess salts from the soil along with nutrients. The vigorous
root growth of rye is particularly valuable for improving the physical
characteristics of compacted soil, directly attacking the root cause of salt
accumulation.

Rye can survive temperatures below minus 25 degrees C, making it suitable
for winter [[fukuoka-clover-cover-cropping-soil-ecology]] in most climates. Its total [[dighton-fungal-biomass-production-aquatic-ecosystems]] of
20 tons (44,092 lb) per 0.1 ha provides enormous [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] that
further improves soil structure and water infiltration. As soil structure
improves, water can drain deeper instead of stagnating and evaporating.

Farmers who have practiced tree-rye symbiosis for over 20 years report that
orchard production increases every year, demonstrating the long-term
effectiveness of rye for soil health improvement. The concern that rye's
low nitrogen content might cause nitrogen immobilization during
decomposition has been consistently dismissed by JADAM field experience.

## Sudan Grass for Greenhouse Salt Removal

Sudan grass is specifically recommended for salt removal in protected
cultivations (greenhouses). Greenhouses are particularly prone to salinity
because there is no rainwater to flush the soil. All irrigation water
eventually evaporates, leaving minerals and salts behind. Over time, this
creates serious [[jadam-salt-accumulation-in-soil]] that reduces crop yields and quality.

Sudan grass is tolerant of high temperatures and drought, making it well-
suited to greenhouse conditions during warm periods. To use Sudan grass for
salt removal, grow it for over 60 days to allow the plants to absorb
accumulated salt from the soil profile. Then remove the entire plants from
the greenhouse, taking the absorbed salt with them. This physical removal of
salt-laden plant material is an effective remediation strategy.

Sudan grass also helps reduce nematode damage, which is often exacerbated
by saline conditions. Its fast growth rate allows four to five cuttings per
year, providing multiple opportunities for salt absorption. The plant can
also serve as feed crop, making it a versatile choice.

## The Cover Crop Advantage Over Other Methods

JADAM contrasts the green manure approach with conventional salinity
management. Common recommendations include excessive irrigation to flush
salts, application of gypsum, or use of chemical amendments. These methods
address symptoms rather than causes and often require purchased inputs.
Green manure crops, by contrast, address both the symptom (excess salt)
and the cause (compaction and poor drainage) simultaneously.

The physical root action of cover crops breaks through compacted layers,
improving water infiltration and drainage. This prevents future salt
accumulation by allowing rainwater and irrigation to drain properly rather
than stagnating and evaporating. The organic matter produced by cover crops
improves soil aggregation, further enhancing drainage and reducing
evaporation from the soil surface.

## Integration with JADAM Microorganism Solution

JADAM recommends applying JMS (JADAM microorganism solution) intensively
before planting cover crops and after turning them into the soil. The
microbial activity accelerates the decomposition of green manure and
enhances the salt-absorption process. Active soil microorganisms also raise
soil temperature, which helps with chill and freezing damage and promotes
earlier harvests.

JMS applied before transplanting or before cover crop sowing should be
applied abundantly so that microorganisms reach deep into the soil. For
open fields, apply JMS before rain so microbes reach deep parts of the soil
with rainwater. This combination of cover crops and microbial activity
creates a synergistic effect for soil restoration.

## Long-Term Salinity Management

JADAM's approach to salinity is not a one-time fix but a long-term soil
management strategy. By maintaining continuous cover crop cultivation,
avoiding soil compaction, and supporting microbial activity, farmers can
prevent salt accumulation from recurring. The key is to maintain the open,
permeable soil structure that nature maintains, where water can move freely
through the soil profile without stagnating and evaporating.

## Key Principles for Salinity Reduction

- Eliminate the compacted soil layer that traps water and causes
  evaporation-based salt accumulation
- Use rye as winter [[query-how-do-i-choose-the-right-cover-crop-for-my-garden]] open field salt absorption
- Use Sudan grass in greenhouses, growing for 60+ days then removing
- Apply JMS to accelerate decomposition and enhance soil biology
- Maintain continuous cover to prevent future compaction and salinity
- Avoid practices that destroy soil structure: heavy machinery, excessive
  tillage, and removal of crop residues
