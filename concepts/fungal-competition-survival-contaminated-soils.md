## Pleurotus as a Superior Competitor

Research by Lang, Eller and Zadrazil (1997) found that Pleurotus species are better competitors in soil than several other mycoremediation-field-applications-case-studies. This competitive advantage is likely related to:

- Faster growth rates compared to Phanerochaete species, allowing quicker colonization of contaminated substrates

- Ability to colonize a wider range of substrates, including agricultural residues and compost

- Production of antimicrobial compounds that suppress competitors

- Tolerance of a broader range of soil conditions, including wider pH and temperature ranges

The practical implication is that species selection for field bioremediation should consider competitive ability alongside degradation capability. A fungus that degrades pollutants efficiently in sterile conditions but cannot compete in soil is of limited practical value.

## Soil Amendment and Inoculation Techniques

To aid colonization of white rot fungi in soil, various strategies have been developed:

- **Wood chips and corn cobs** -- fungi grown on these substrates and mixed into soil enable good colonization. A ground corn cob to soil ratio of 4:1 has been used successfully.

- **Straw amendment** -- straw increases hyphal length and generally gives the greatest initial rate of pollutant breakdown. Straw also provides a selective carbon source that favors ligninolytic fungi.

- **Specially prepared pellets** -- Lestan and Lamar (1996) developed pelleted inocula that enabled good soil colonization by several fungal species, with higher biological potential leading to more efficient degradation of contaminated soil.

- **Periodic tilling** -- mixing fungal biomass through contaminated soil breaks up competing bacterial colonies and redistributes the inoculum.

- **Surfactant addition** -- surfactants like Tween 80 increase fungal-bioremediation can be reliably deployed at scale.
