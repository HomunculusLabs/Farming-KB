- **Low islands** are residual islands composed of coral and sand with
  accumulations of biomass from sea birds and migratory animals. These
  are calcareous islands of calcium from ground shell, coral, or both.
- **High islands** are volcanic or granitic islands from recent volcanic
  activity or folding of the ocean bed.

Low islands present a special arid condition due to very limited water
catchment and finite water storage. Mollison states that "the demand for
designers in the atoll marine islands is very, very high. We can"t begin
to fill that demand."

## The Fresh Water Lens

On low islands, fresh water sits on top of a saltwater base. The top
level is often only three to five feet below the surface, with the
bottom only four to five feet below that. The available fresh water can
be calculated from the island area. Critical management rules:

- Never pollute the water table -- [[greywater-management-and-dry-toilets]] are a necessity
- Even two or three [[septic-tanks-and-natural-swimming-pools]] can contaminate the entire water supply
- When the fresh water is drawn down, salt water fills the table and
  plants die
- Surface water storage must supplement the limited lens

## The Platen Problem

The combination of phosphates (from seabird manure) and calcareous
sands creates a soil with a layer of "concrete" about 18 inches down.
This calcium triphosphate layer, called "platen," varies from nine
inches to two or three feet thick and is often level on top.

The platen creates a fundamental agricultural constraint: rain follows
the platen layer out and runs off, and is lost to storage. "Thus, only
a very shallow-rooted agriculture is possible." Yet large trees like
coconut are found on these islands -- almost exclusively through human
intervention, as documented in both ancient and modern Polynesian sagas.

## Breaking the Platen

To establish productive trees, the platen must be broken:

1. Shovel away the layer of sand and break up the platen to reach
   brackish groundwater
2. Put everything available in the way of humus into the hole
3. Plant a tree, preferably a coconut -- "The tree keeps the platen
   open"
4. The tree cracks the platen around its trunk circumference, making it
   permeable to rain
5. The tree establishes its own groundwater system
6. Repeat the process to establish more trees; within 20 years, trees
   are in production

It is useless to remove all top sand, replace platen with mulch, and
cover with sand -- a new platen reforms within twelve months.

## Circle Gardening

The "magical [[ring-garden-atoll-mollison]]" is the ideal atoll growing system:

- Break up the platen and heap sand around the edge of a circle
- Fill the center with mulch and plant vegetable crops
- Install a single drip system at the center
- Drip water extends laterally about two to two and a half feet, giving
  a maximum effective diameter of four to five feet
- If the hole is deep enough to touch the water table, a "wick watering"
  system supplements the drip

The result is a highly successful island garden. The ring shape
concentrates energy and water, and the raised sand edge protects the
growing area.
