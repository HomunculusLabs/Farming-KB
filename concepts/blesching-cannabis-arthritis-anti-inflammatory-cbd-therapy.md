# CBD Anti-Inflammatory Therapy

Cannabidiol (CBD) has emerged as one of the most promising non-psychoactive
cannabinoids for treating chronic inflammatory conditions. Sourced from
Cannabis sativa, CBD demonstrates therapeutic effects across multiple
inflammatory disease states, including arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease
(IBD), atherosclerosis, and periodontitis. Its anti-inflammatory action operates
through multiple pathways distinct from those of THC, making it suitable for
patients who cannot tolerate psychoactive effects.

## Molecular Mechanisms of CBD Anti-Inflammatory Action

CBD exerts its anti-inflammatory effects through several interconnected
mechanisms:

- **CB2 receptor affinity**: CBD has a greater affinity for CB2 receptors than
  CB1 receptors. CB2 receptors are concentrated in immune cells throughout the
  body, including macrophages, T-cells, and B-cells. Activation of CB2 receptors
  modulates immune cell migration and suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine
  production, including tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha), interleukin-1
  beta (IL-1B), and interleukin-6 (IL-6).

- **FAAH enzyme inhibition**: CBD suppresses fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH),
  the enzyme responsible for breaking down anandamide. By inhibiting FAAH, CBD
  keeps the "Bliss Molecule" active at higher concentrations and for longer
  durations. Since anandamide itself has anti-inflammatory properties via CB2
  receptor activation, this FAAH inhibition creates a compounding
  anti-inflammatory effect.
