# Dune Forms and Uses of Sand in Desert Landscapes

Sand dunes are dynamic landforms shaped by wind and water interactions in arid environments. Understanding dune morphology, formation processes, and the practical uses of sand is essential for [[rammed-earth]] construction
- **Thermal mass**: Sand's heat storage capacity moderates temperature in and around structures
- **Drainage layers**: Sand layers beneath and around foundations prevent water damage

### Agriculture
- **Root zone amendment**: Sand improves drainage in heavy soils
- **Seed beds**: Sandy soils warm quickly in spring, benefiting early planting
- **Hydroponic media**: Clean sand serves as growing medium
- **Moisture retention at depth**: Deep sand layers hold water accessible to desert-adapted trees

### Water Management
- **Infiltration basins**: Direct stormwater into sand areas for groundwater recharge
- **Sand filters**: Remove sediment and contaminants from water
- **Evaporation reduction**: Subsurface water storage in sand avoids surface evaporation

### Erosion Control and Stabilisation
- **Vegetated nebkas**: Living dune stabilisation using adapted grasses and shrubs
- **Sand fences**: Slatted fences trap windblown sand and build protective berms
- **Mulch substitute**: Coarse sand as surface mulch to reduce soil evaporation (particularly in the Canary Islands, where 10–15 cm of gravel or volcanic cinder is used)

## Dune Systems in the Broader Landscape

Dune fields serve important ecological functions:

- **Windbreaks**: Large dune complexes reduce wind speed over extensive areas
- **Habitat creation**: Dune slopes with different aspects (windward vs leeward) support different plant communities
- **Water storage**: Deep sand deposits over clay hold millions of litres of freshwater from hill runoff
- **Microclimate modification**: Dune shadows create cooler, moister conditions for establishment of more demanding species
- **Sand transport**: Dunes naturally move sand from areas of erosion to areas of deposition, redistributing resources

## Designing with Dunes

In permaculture design for desert regions, dunes should be viewed as **assets to be worked with** rather than problems to be eliminated:

1. **Map existing dune forms** and understand their movement patterns
2. **Identify stabilisation opportunities** using adapted vegetation
3. **Design water harvesting** to utilise sand's infiltration capacity
4. **Place structures** on the leeward side of dunes for shelter
5. **Use dune sand** for construction and drainage where appropriate
6. **Create nebkas deliberately** as windbreaks and moisture traps
