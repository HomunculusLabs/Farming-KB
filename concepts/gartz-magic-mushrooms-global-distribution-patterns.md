# Global Distribution Patterns of Magic Mushrooms

[[jochen-gartz]]'s "Magic Mushrooms Around the World" documents the worldwide
distribution of psilocybin-containing mushrooms, establishing that psychoactive
Psilocybe and related species thrive on every continent. The book's Chapter 7
provides a comprehensive regional survey, while earlier chapters detail
individual species and their ecological niches.

## Cosmopolitan and Widespread Species

Two species dominate global distribution: [[psilocybe-cubensis-profile]] and Panaeolus
cyanescens. Both are dung-inhabiting tropical and subtropical species found
across the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Oceania. Gartz notes that P. cubensis
can be found on pastures up to 30 degrees north and south of the equator. These
species likely spread through human activity -- cattle ranching, agricultural
trade, and settlement patterns.

Psilocybe semilanceata ("Liberty Cap") represents the temperate-zone
counterpart, common across Europe and also found throughout the Pacific
Northwest of North America. It is a nitrophilic grassland species that fruits
in autumn, particularly after heavy rainfall.

## Climatic Corridors

Gartz identifies several climatic corridors supporting psychoactive mycoflora:

- **Tropical/subtropical belt**: Home to P. cubensis, P. cyanescens, and
  numerous Panaeolus species. Found in Central America, northern South America,
  sub-Saharan Africa, South and Southeast Asia, and northern Australia.

- **Temperate oceanic zones**: Support P. semilanceata, P. cyanescens (the
  European wood-debris species), and [[inocybe-aeruginascens-profile]]. Found in western
  Europe, the Pacific Northwest of North America, and southeastern Australia
  (P. subaeruginosa).

- **Mediterranean and montane**: Isolated populations of bluing Psilocybe
  species exist in North Africa (Algeria, Morocco) and mountain regions.

## Regional Centers of Diversity

- **North America (Pacific Northwest)**: Exceptionally rich mycoflora including
  P. semilanceata, P. cyanescens, P. stuntzii, P. baeocystis, P. pelliculosa,
  P. azurescens, and Panaeolus subbalteatus. The region is considered a major
  center of psychoactive mushroom biodiversity.

- **Mexico**: The original epicenter of documented psychoactive mushroom use,
  home to numerous endemic Psilocybe species including P. mexicana, P.
  aztecorum, and P. caerulescens.

- **Southeast Asia**: Thailand is identified as the country with the largest
  current consumption of psychoactive mushrooms. Species include P. cubensis,
  P. subcubensis, P. samuiensis, and [[panaeolus-cyanescens-cultivation-guide]].

- **Europe**: Despite its mycophobic cultural traditions, Europe harbors a
  flourishing psychotropic mycoflora including P. semilanceata, P.
  cyanescens, Inocybe aeruginascens, [[conocybe-cyanopus-profile]], and Pluteus
  salicinus.

## Human-Mediated Dispersal

Gartz documents numerous cases of species spreading through human activity:
bark mulch distribution creating Psilocybe stuntzii habitats at the University
of Washington; feed grain imports from Argentina introducing Gymnopilus
purpuratus to Europe; and cattle introduction bringing P. cubensis to
Australia. The book hypothesizes that psychoactive species have been
continuously present and used by humans across continents for thousands of
years, with the oldest evidence -- rock art depicting mushroom-using rituals --
dating to 7,000-10,000 B.C. in the Sahara.

## Key Taxonomic Finding

Gartz emphasizes that taxonomy and distribution remain inadequate for most
regions. Africa, Asia, and large parts of South America are barely studied.
The hypothesis that psychoactive Psilocybe and related species thrive on all
continents has been "fully confirmed," but many species await discovery and
formal description.
