## Key Fungal Species for PAH Degradation

Extensive research has identified numerous fungal PAH degraders:

### White Rot Basidiomycetes
- **Phanerochaete chrysosporium** -- degrades anthracene, phenanthrene, pyrene, and benzo[a]pyrene; mineralization linked to ligninolytic conditions
- **Phanerochaete sordida** -- used in field trials for PAH-contaminated soil remediation
- **Pleurotus ostreatus** -- degrades PAHs in soil; competitive in non-sterile conditions
- **Trametes versicolor** -- degrades PAHs and PAH-contaminated creosote; effective in bioreactor systems
- **Bjerkandera adusta** -- produces MnP under nitrogen-sufficient conditions; effective PAH degrader
- **Keuhneromyces mutabilis and Agrocybe aegerita** -- demonstrated PAH transformation

### Non-White Rot Fungi
- **Cunninghamella spp.** -- P450-mediated PAH oxidation; well-studied pathway elucidation
- **Penicillium spp.** and **Aspergillus niger** -- capable of transforming PAHs
- **Various micromycetes** -- diverse taxonomic groups show PAH-degrading ability

## Laccase-Mediated PAH Oxidation

Laccase alone has limited ability to oxidize high-redox-potential PAHs, but mediator systems expand its range:
- **1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HBT)** -- mediates laccase oxidation of PAHs by Pycnoporus cinnabarinus
- **Natural mediators** -- simple aromatics like phenol, aniline, and 4-hydroxybenzoic acid (at concentrations as low as 0.1 mmol/L) stimulate T. versicolor laccase to oxidize PAHs
- **Mn(III) chelates** -- generated by MnP, act as diffusible oxidants that can attack PAHs

## Environmental Factors Affecting PAH Degradation

- **PAH solubility** -- low solubility limits bioavailability; surfactants may help
- **Soil organic matter** -- sorption reduces availability for enzymatic attack
- **Oxygen availability** -- ligninolytic enzymes are aerobic; PAHs persist in anoxic environments
- **Nutrient status** -- nitrogen limitation promotes ligninolytic enzyme production in P. chrysosporium (but not all species)
- **Temperature** -- most fungi are mesophiles; P. chrysosporium tolerates higher temperatures

## Toxicological Considerations

A critical concern is that fungal PAH metabolism can produce reactive intermediates (dihydrodiol epoxides, quinones) similar to those formed by mammalian liver enzymes. The metabolic profiles of fungi with PAHs must be characterized to determine whether potentially toxic intermediates are formed during bioremediation of contaminated sites.
