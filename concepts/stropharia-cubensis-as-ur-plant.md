## Stropharia Cubensis as the Ur Plant

The search for the original hallucinogenic plant that catalyzed the
emergence of human self-reflection requires a careful process of
elimination. [[terence-mckenna]] argued that [[stropharia-cubensis]], also
known as [[mckenna-catalyzing-consciousness-and-language]] in evolving hominids:

- **African origin**: The evidence is overwhelming that modern humans
  emerged in Africa, so the plant must be native to that continent.
- **Grassland habitat**: Newly omnivorous ancestors adapted to
  grasslands, refining bipedalism and signaling methods there.
- **No preparation required**: The plant must be active in its natural
  state. Mixtures and extracts belong to later cultural stages.
- **Continuous availability**: It must be accessible to nomadic
  populations, easily noticed, and plentiful.
- **Immediate tangible benefits**: Individuals eating it must gain
  advantages that establish it as a permanent dietary element.

These requirements dramatically reduce the number of contenders. Africa
has a notable scarcity of hallucinogenic plants compared to the tropical
New World, a pattern that has never been satisfactorily explained.

## The Tryptamine Mushrooms

By process of elimination, the tryptamine-type hallucinogens, namely
psilocybin, psilocin, and DMT, remain as the primary candidates. In a
grassland environment, these compounds might occur in dung-loving
coprophilic mushrooms containing psilocybin or in grasses containing
DMT. Unless DMT were extracted and concentrated, which was beyond the
technical reach of early humans, these grasses could never supply
sufficient amounts for effective hallucinogenic experience.

Several grassland mushrooms contain psilocybin, including Panaeolus
species and Stropharia cubensis. Of these, only Stropharia cubensis
contains psilocybin in concentrated amounts and is free of
nausea-producing compounds. It alone is pandemic, occurring throughout
the tropical regions wherever cattle of the zebu type graze.
