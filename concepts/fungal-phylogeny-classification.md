# Fungal Phylogeny and Classification

## Overview

Fungal classification has undergone revolutionary changes since the advent of molecular phylogenetics. Historically classified alongside plants in the study of botany, fungi are now recognized as a distinct kingdom more closely related to animals than to plants. Contemporary phylogenetic analyses based on DNA sequences have revealed that many traditional groupings were polyphyletic, driven by convergent evolution rather than shared ancestry. The Kingdom Fungi, as currently defined, comprises a monophyletic group of "true fungi" exclusive of slime molds and oomycetes.

## Historical Classification

Traditional fungal taxonomy was based on morphological characters, particularly reproductive structures:

- **Four traditional phyla**: Chytridiomycota, Zygomycota, Ascomycota, and Basidiomycota, defined primarily by spore production mechanisms.
- **Slime molds** (Myxomycota, Dictyosteliomycota, Acrasiomycota) were long included among fungi due to their amoeboid and spore-producing stages.
- **Oomycetes** (water molds) were classified with fungi based on their filamentous growth and absorptive nutrition.
- This morphology-based system persisted from the 19th century through most of the 20th century.

The fundamental problem with morphological classification is that filamentous growth, spore production, and absorptive nutrition evolved independently multiple times — they are examples of **convergent evolution** rather than indicators of shared ancestry.

## The Molecular Revolution

The application of molecular phylogenetics, particularly ribosomal DNA (rDNA) sequencing, transformed fungal systematics:

- **Bruns et al. (1991)**: Developed early phylogenetic trees of fungi based on small subunit rRNA gene sequences, establishing the framework for molecular fungal systematics.
- **Baldauf et al. (2000)**: Used multiple protein-coding genes (α-tubulin, β-tubulin, actin, and elongation factor 1-alpha) plus rDNA to produce a well-resolved phylogeny of eukaryotes including fungi and fungus-like organisms.
- **James et al. (2000)**: Provided additional resolution of fungal deep-branching relationships using SSU rDNA.

These studies demonstrated that fewer than 800 fungal or fungus-like taxa had been included in any single phylogenetic reconstruction, representing less than 1% of the 80,000 currently listed species.

## Kingdom Fungi: Monophyly and Relationships

Modern phylogenetics has established the following key relationships:

1. **Kingdom Fungi is monophyletic**: The traditional phyla Ascomycota, Basidiomycota, Zygomycota, and Chytridiomycota form a single evolutionary lineage when slime molds and oomycetes are excluded.

2. **Fungi are more closely related to animals than to plants**: Molecular evidence supports a common ancestor of Fungi and Metazoa (animals), possibly through a choanoflagellate-like ancestor. This was proposed based on multiple lines of evidence including ribosomal RNA, protein sequences, mitochondrial cristae structure, and flagellum characteristics.

3. **Microsporidia are fungi**: These obligate intracellular parasites, once classified as protozoa, are now recognized as highly derived fungi that have lost many ancestral fungal features through reductive evolution.

4. **Oomycetes belong to Straminipila**: Water molds and their relatives (including diatoms and brown algae) form a separate evolutionary lineage with heterokont flagellation, distinct from true fungi.

## Major Fungal Phyla

### Chytridiomycota (Zoosporic Fungi)

- Defined by the presence of flagellated cells (zoospores) with a single posteriorly inserted smooth flagellum — an ancestral trait.
- Some chytrid lineages occupy the most basal branch of Kingdom Fungi, consistent with a flagellated ancestor.
- Include both free-living saprotrophs and important parasites (e.g., *[[batrachochytrium-dendrobatidis]]*, the chytrid fungus causing amphibian declines worldwide).
- The phylum is not monophyletic in modern analyses; some groups intergrade with Zygomycota.

### Zygomycota

- Traditionally defined by the production of zygospores through sexual reproduction.
- Not monophyletic in molecular analyses; the group is paraphyletic with respect to other fungal lineages.
- Includes Mucorales (bread molds), Entomophthorales (insect pathogens), and Trichomycetes (gut symbionts of arthropods).
- The Glomales ([[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]]) were formerly placed here but are now recognized as a separate ancient lineage.

### Ascomycota

- The largest phylum of fungi, containing over 64,000 described species.
- Defined by the production of spores within a microscopic sac called an ascus (plural: asci).
- Includes yeasts (e.g., *[[saccharomyces-cerevisiae]]*), molds (e.g., *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*), morels, truffles, and many plant pathogens.
- Many Ascomycota have no known sexual stage and were classified in a separate "Deuteromycota" (Fungi Imperfecti) — this artificial group has been largely abandoned with [[biodiversity-fungal-molecular-identification-dna-barcoding]].

### Basidiomycota

- The second largest phylum, containing approximately 32,000 described species.
- Defined by the production of spores on specialized cells called basidia, typically on large fleshy fruiting bodies (mushrooms, brackets, puffballs, coral fungi).
- Include most familiar mushroom-forming fungi, as well as important plant pathogens (rusts and smuts).
- Divided into three major classes: Ustilaginiomycetes (smuts), Urediniomycetes (rusts), and Hymenomycetes (typical mushroom-forming fungi).

## Fungus-Like Organisms

Several groups once classified as fungi are now placed in separate kingdoms:

- **Oomycota** (water molds, downy mildews): Kingdom Straminipila; include the potato blight pathogen *Phytophthora infestans*.
- **Hyphochytriomycetes**: Also Kingdom Straminipila; small group of flagellated parasites.
- **Labyrinthulales and Thraustochytriales**: Marine organisms with both fungal and algal characteristics.
- **Myxomycetes** (plasmodial slime molds): Produce large creeping plasmodia; form spore-bearing fruiting bodies resembling fungal sporocarps.
- **Dictyosteliomycetes** (cellular slime molds): Social amoebae that aggregate to form multicellular fruiting structures.
- **Plasmodiophorales**: Endoparasitic slime molds of plants; now placed near alveolates.
