# Septobasidiales Scale Insect Mutualism
Septobasidiales are fungi specialized on scale insects.
They are unusual because the association is not simply parasitic.
A fungal colony may cover an entire scale-insect colony.
The insects gain partial protection from predators.
The fungus gains access to nutrients from selected insects.
Only some individuals in the scale colony are invaded.
Those infected insects are penetrated by helical haustoria.
They do not necessarily die from the invasion.
The source notes that infected individuals may even outlive neighbors.
Their main cost is reproductive sterility.
This makes the system parasitic at the individual level.
It can still be protective at the colony level.
## Organisms Involved
The chunk names Ordonia and Septobasidium as visible colony formers.
Janetia and Johncouchia occur as associated anamorphs.
The host insects are scale insects, especially Coccidae.
The colonies occur on living plant parts.
They may be found on leaves, stems, and branches.
They occur on gymnosperms, monocots, and dicots.
A useful collection therefore records three organisms.
It records the fungus.
It records the scale insect.
It records the plant substrate.
Without all three, identification becomes weaker.
## Colony Appearance
Septobasidium colonies are usually brown to black.
Some are more brightly colored.
Most are resupinate and felty.
The surface may be smooth, warty, or spiny.
Inexperienced collectors may mistake them for corticioid fungi.
They may also be mistaken for lichens.
One clue is that they occur on living rather than dead plant parts.
Another clue is position.
Because basidia project downward, colonies often occur below branches.
They are usually away from extreme branch tips.
Coccidiodictyon inconspicuum is harder to see in the field.
It may be found by examining scale-insect collections microscopically.
## Collecting Strategy
Collection resembles lichen or aphyllophoralean collecting.
The colony is removed with a knife or ax.
A generous piece of plant substrate should be included.
The associated scale insects should also be included.
A plant voucher is important.
Leaves, flowers, fruits, and other diagnostic plant structures should be saved.
Paper bags or aerated containers are preferred.
Plastic bags can trap moisture and encourage contaminants.
Healthy scale insects should be preserved for identification.
Adult female scale insects are the most reliable diagnostic specimens.
## Seasonality and Storage
Septobasidiales are perennial fungi.
Growth occurs during the wet season.
Growth slows or stops during dry or cold periods.
Probasidia are produced near the end of growth or during dormancy.
They germinate at the onset of the next growing season.
Timing matters because basidia are taxonomically important.
The best material is often collected near dormancy's end.
Colonies tolerate periodic drying.
Probasidia function as dormant perennating structures.
Extreme heat should be avoided because it can kill probasidia.
Air drying with moving air is preferable.
Desiccants or low heat may be needed in humid climates.
The goal is to dry specimens without cooking them.
## Preparing Specimens
Identification may require haustoria, probasidia, basidia, and basidiospores.
Whole infected insects can often be mounted on slides.
Water mounts can show internal structures in transparent insects.
Dry insects may be mounted in potassium hydroxide.
Very old dry insects may even be boiled in potassium hydroxide.
Sometimes the exoskeleton must be crushed or dissected away.
The port of fungal entry or exit should be noted.
Haustoria are taxonomically important.
Couch recognized six haustorial types.
Live specimens can be induced to produce basidia.
The colony is soaked until wet.
It is placed in a covered humid container.
Probasidia often develop within twenty-four to forty-eight hours.
The resulting basidia and basidiospores can be mounted for study.
## Culture and Voucher Logic
Cultures can be attempted from probasidia-bearing colonies.
A fertile basidiocarp fragment is suspended over low-nutrient agar.
Water agar can also be used.
The desired inoculum is discharged basidiospores.
Crumbs from the specimen should not fall onto the agar.
They may carry faster-growing contaminants.
Malt-yeast-peptone media may support some species.
The source notes that few species are available in culture collections.
A proper voucher should include fungal colony, insect, and plant substrate.
Labels should include names when known, date, geography, and habitat.
The Farlow Herbarium is noted as holding substantial Septobasidium material.
## Why This Concept Matters
Septobasidiales show that symbiosis can be multilevel.
One insect may be parasitized.
The surrounding insect colony may be protected.
The fungus receives nutrients and a stable host platform.
The plant supplies the surface on which the insect colony lives.
This makes the system a three-way biological association.
For biodiversity studies, the lesson is practical.
Specialized fungi cannot be sampled independently of their hosts.
Collectors must preserve ecological context as well as morphology.
