# Fungal Biosorption Isotherms and Equilibrium Models

Biosorption is a pseudo-ion-exchange process in which metal ions are
exchanged for counterions in the [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] or resin. Equilibrium data,
known as adsorption isotherms, are basic requirements for the design of
biosorption systems. The evaluation of sorption performance is complicated
by various methodologies and criteria used in the process, and by the
difficulty of evaluating systems when more than one metal is present.

## Langmuir Isotherm Model

The Langmuir model is applicable to monolayer sorption onto a surface with
a finite number of identical sites. It is the most widely used model for
describing biosorption equilibrium data for [[dighton-fungal-heavy-metal-accumulation-bioremediation]] removal by fungal
biomass. The model assumes uniform energies of adsorption onto the surface
and no transmigration of the adsorbate in the plane of the surface. The
well-known Langmuir equation is expressed as:

    q = qmax * b * Cf / (1 + b * Cf)

where q (mg/g) is the uptake of the metal, qmax is the maximum uptake
capacity, Cf (mg/g) is the equilibrium final concentration of metal in the
solution, and b is a constant related to the affinity of the binding sites.

The Langmuir model has been applied successfully to describe the
biosorption of various [[fungal-biosorption-of-heavy-metals-by-filamentous-fungi]] by fungal species, including lead,
cadmium, copper, and chromium by [[aspergillus-niger]], [[rhizopus-arrhizus]], and
Mucor species. The model provides a useful indication of the maximum
biosorption capacity of a given [[dighton-fungal-biomass-nutrient-reservoir-ecosystems]] for a particular metal ion.

## Freundlich Isotherm Model

The empirical Freundlich model is based on sorption onto a heterogeneous
surface and does not assume monolayer capacity. It is given by:

    q = K * Cf^(1/n)

where K and n are the Freundlich constants, indicators of adsorption
capacity and adsorption intensity, respectively. The equation can be
linearized in logarithmic form, and the Freundlich constants can be
determined from the slope and intercept of the linearized plot.

The Freundlich model has been used to correlate biosorption data
satisfactorily for various metal-fungal biomass systems. It is particularly
useful for describing multilayer adsorption and systems where the binding
sites have different affinities. Biosorption of cyanide complex anions by
Rhizopus arrhizus has been evaluated using the Freundlich model along
with the Langmuir and Redlich-Peterson models.

## Additional Equilibrium Models

Several other biosorption isotherms are available in the literature,
although they may not always represent the physicochemical principles of
the biosorption process. These include:

- **Scatchard-Langmuir model**: A modified form of the Langmuir equation
  that provides a linear relationship between q/Cf and q, useful for
  determining binding site homogeneity.

- **Redlich-Peterson model**: A three-parameter isotherm that combines
  features of both the Langmuir and Freundlich equations and can be
  applied to both homogeneous and heterogeneous systems.

- **Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) model**: Originally developed for
  multilayer gas adsorption, it has been adapted for biosorption systems
  with the equation involving the saturation constant Cs and a constant B
  related to energy of interaction with the surface.

- **Dubinin-Radushkevich (DR) model**: Based on the Polanyi potential
  theory, it involves a constant B related to sorption energy and the
  Polanyi potential E = RT ln(1 + 1/Cf). This model can be used to
  distinguish between physical and chemical adsorption.
