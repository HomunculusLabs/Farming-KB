## Psilocybe Cubensis Tropism Mechanisms

## Overview

[[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]] exhibits a **dual-phase tropism system** governing stipe orientation and
cap positioning during [[fruiting-body-development]]. This two-stage mechanism combines responses
to evaporative demand and gravity to ensure efficient [[aquatic-fungi-spore-dispersal-ingold]], synthesized from
Badham (1982) with comparative data from basidiomycete and zygomycete studies.

## Phase 1: Evaporative Demand–Driven Elongation

During primary stipe elongation, growth is oriented toward **greatest evaporative demand** —
the dominant tropic response during early and mid-stage fruiting, before the pileus fully
expands and gill tissues mature.

### The Evaporative Demand Model

Evaporative demand at the stipe surface is a composite function of four environmental
variables: **wind speed**, **humidity**, **light**, and **temperature**. These create a
spatially heterogeneous evaporative environment; the stipe grows preferentially toward
regions of higher evaporative flux. Cell division is largely complete by the time primordia
reach a few centimeters, so subsequent elongation proceeds through **acropetal (base-to-tip)
cell expansion**, with tropic curvature achieved through differential expansion of
already-divided cells, enabling rapid reorientation.

### Boundary Layer Hypothesis

The **boundary layer hypothesis** provides the physical mechanism: when wind contacts the
stipe, the boundary layer of still air is redistributed — **thinner on the windward side**
and **thicker on the leeward side**. This asymmetry produces differential evaporative demand:

- Windward side: thinner boundary layer → greater evaporation → higher cell expansion rate
- Leeward side: thicker boundary layer → reduced evaporation → lower cell expansion rate

The resulting differential growth rates produce **curvature** toward windward. Individual
stipe cells may respond independently, suggesting a decentralized sensory mechanism rather
than a single apical sensing zone.

## Phase 2: Negative Geotropism

As the fruiting body matures and [[spore]] production commences, **negative geotropism**
supersedes the evaporative demand mechanism, reorienting the pileus horizontally. The
Phase 1→2 transition is mediated by a **substance produced in the gills (lamellae)**.
Decapitation experiments confirmed mushroom hormones are linked to lamellar tissue: **agar
diffusates** from gills **partially replace** pileus influence on stipe growth, and the
gill-derived substance **overrides** the evaporative demand response, shifting dominant
tropic sensitivity to gravity. This ensures horizontal pileus orientation at spore maturity.

### Known Hormonal Compounds

Plant-like hormones found in mushroom tissues include **IAA** in *[[agaricus-bisporus]]*
(Konishi & Hagimoto 1961), **gibberellin-like substances** in basidiomycete sporophores
(Pegg 1973), and **ethylene** implicated in *Phycomyces* autochemotropism (Russo et al.
1977). Their direct regulatory role in *P. cubensis* tropism remains unproven; the
identity of the gill-derived tropism-modifying substance is an open question.

## Light Interactions with Tropism

Light influences tropism through physical (heating the stipe surface, increasing
evaporation) and biochemical (triggering hormonic developmental transitions) pathways.
Light is required for **basidiocarp initiation** (Badham 1980), **spore formation**, and
**[[spore-production-geotropic-sensitivity-coupling-basidiomycete-badham-1982]]**. Conversely, light **inhibits stipe elongation**, potentially as
a braking mechanism. Eger-Hummel (1980) described **four distinct light responses** in
mushrooms; *P. cubensis* is broadly similar to *Coprinus congregatus*.

## Comparison with Phycomyces

*Phycomyces blakesleeanus* provides an important comparative model for fungal tropism:

| Feature | *P. cubensis* | *Phycomyces* |
|---|---|---|
| Anemotropism | Evaporative demand model | Direct + avoidance response |
| Avoidance response | Not established | Johnson & Gamow (1971) |
| Ethylene involvement | Unknown | Russo et al. (1977) |
| Wind sensing | [[windward-leeward-boundary-layer-redistribution-stipe-curvature-badham-1982]] | Wind + water vapor |
| Force response | Possible (analogous) | Gravity + centrifugal (Dennison 1961) |

**Johnson & Gamow (1971)** showed *Phycomyces* avoidance requires both wind and water
vapor, paralleling the *P. cubensis* model. **Dennison (1961)** demonstrated force-based
responses, suggesting *P. cubensis* may also sense wind force directly as a complement to
the boundary layer mechanism. **Russo et al. (1977)** proposed ethylene in *Phycomyces*
autochemotropism, hinting at hormonal parallels in basidiomycetes.

## Adaptive Significance

The dual-phase tropism is closely tied to *P. cubensis* ecology as a **coprophagous
(dung-inhabiting) species**:

1. **Phase 1 value**: Growing toward maximum evaporative demand orients the stipe toward
   open air, elevating spores into favorable dispersal currents early in development.
2. **Phase 2 value**: Negative geotropism ensures a **horizontal pileus** at spore maturity.
   As Plunkett (1961) argued, unstable substrates make vertical orientation unreliable;
   gravity provides a constant reference regardless of substrate angle.
3. **Veil function**: The partial veil may protect the upper stipe from wind and light
   during Phase 1, preventing premature evaporative stress before the final geotropic
   readjustment of the cap.
4. **Habitat coupling**: The evaporative demand mechanism suits the open, exposed
   microhabitats (pastures, grasslands) where *P. cubensis* typically fruits.

## Summary Model

```
Primordium formation → Cell division completes (~few cm)
        │
        ▼
Phase 1: Stipe elongation toward maximum evaporative demand
        │  (wind + humidity + light + temperature)
        │  Mechanism: boundary layer redistribution → differential growth
        │
        ▼
Gill maturation → gill-derived hormone produced
        │
        ▼
Phase 2: Negative geotropism overrides evaporative response
        │  Pileus achieves horizontal orientation
        │
        ▼
Spore release → optimal ballistic dispersal from horizontal gills
```
