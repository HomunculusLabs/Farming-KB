# Mushroom Storage and Processing Methods

Mushrooms continue to respire after harvest with a relatively high respiration
rate compared to other fresh produce. The respiration rate of [[cotter-oyster-mushroom-cultivation]] is
three times greater than most fruits. Mushrooms are 85-95% water with no barriers
to water loss from their surface. These characteristics make post-harvest
management critical for maintaining quality and marketability.

## Spoilage Factors

Several factors cause deterioration after harvest:
- Bacteria and fungi within mushrooms continue to increase during cold storage
- Enzymes react with oxygen forming brown pigments, decreasing quality
- Water loss causes wilting and shriveling, lowering fresh mushroom quality
- Metabolic processes generate heat if mushrooms are not rapidly cooled
- Mushrooms at harvest temperature (15-18C) will self-digest if not cooled
  quickly when packed together

## Short Term Storage: Refrigeration

[[psilocybin-shelf-life-stability]] of fresh mushrooms may be extended by refrigeration at 1-4C. Cooling
reduces rates of all physiological processes within mushrooms. During initial
cooling there is a high cooling load; once pre-cooled, cooling load is much
reduced. Shelf life varies from 1 day to 2 weeks depending on conditions.

### Critical Cooling Protocol
Mushrooms should be cooled to storage temperature of 0-2C within five hours of
picking to stop metabolic processes rapidly. If not cooled quickly but packed in
boxes or PVC film, temperature increases due to metabolic processes, and spoilage
can be caused by internal bacteria and fungi.

### PVC Film Storage
The best method for fresh oyster mushroom storage is 8-10C in a packed container
wrapped in microporous or perforated plastic film. This reduces moisture loss and
preserves quality. CO2 levels increase and O2 levels decrease inside wrapped
containers due to mushroom respiration. The gas composition is self-modifying
through respiration, creating a modified atmosphere effect.

## Long Term Storage: Drying

Drying removes enough water to inactivate enzymes and microorganisms. Fresh
mushrooms have 70-95% moisture; dried mushrooms reach approximately 10%. Drying
is commonly used for shiitake and [[growing-gourmet-wood-ear-auricularia-cultivation]] mushrooms, less often for button or
[[query-how-to-grow-oyster-mushrooms-at-home]], though oyster mushrooms can also be marketed in dried form.

### Sun Drying
Mushrooms are spread on shelves with gills facing upward, directly exposed to
sunlight. Drying time varies with weather. Quality is lower than thermal or
hot-air methods, and moisture content remains higher, increasing susceptibility
to molds and pests. Simple and requires no equipment.

### Thermal Power Drying
Begin at low temperature: 35C for mushrooms dried on sunny days, 30C for damp
days. After 5-7 hours initial heat, raise temperature gradually to 40-60C and
maintain for 12-18 hours. Drying enhances flavor and appearance. Firewood stoves
can dry mushrooms at 60-80C for 6 hours.

### Hot-Air Drying
Hot air is blown into a drying chamber with mushrooms on shelves. Temperature
and humidity are controlled via heaters and recirculation vents. Products have
better quality, higher hygienic conditions, and brighter color than sun-dried.
Pre-heat chamber to 40-50C before loading. Sort by size before drying for uniform
results. Place mushrooms stem-downward with 15cm shelf clearance.

### Storage of Dried Products
Dried mushrooms readily absorb moisture. At 20% moisture content, infestation by
insects and molds occurs. Store in sealed polyethylene bags in a dry, cool, dark
place. For prolonged storage, pack in cartons or wooden boxes at 2-5C.

## Canning and Bottling

### Canning
The most common industrial preservation method. Seven basic operations:
cleaning, blanching, canning, sterilization, cooling, labeling, and packing.
Trim immediately after harvest to reduce browning. Refrigerate if not canned
immediately. Incorporate sodium metabisulphite or ascorbate for color retention.
Rinse and blanch 2 minutes. Place in cans with 2.5% NaCl and 0.24-0.5% citric
acid. Seal and sterilize at 120-130C for 1 hour. Rapid cool in wash sink.

### Bottling
Same principle as canning but requires less instrumentation, suitable for
small-scale growers. Process immediately after harvest. Sort by size and quality.
Blanch in water with 0.1% succinic acid and 1% salt for 4-6 minutes (expect
35-40% weight loss). Fill bottles with brine and blanched mushrooms. Close cap
halfway to allow air escape. Boil 30+ minutes. Tighten caps and cool.

## Pickling

Mushrooms are sorted, washed, and optionally sliced. Blanch in 3% salt water for
3-4 minutes in boiling water. Transfer to cold water. Place in jars with brine
(22% salt), add vinegar, sugar, and spices (vitamin C or citric acid for color).
Loosely close jars and steam 1 hour. Tighten lids when cooled. Chill before
eating. This method produces favorable products with the right formula.

## Value-Added Products

In India, value-added products like oyster mushroom powder for soups and
mushroom pizza are recommended for market development. In Korea, Phellinus
baumii instant noodles incorporate mushroom extract. In China, extensive canning
operations produce canned mushrooms for export. Thai producers make mushroom
pickles and wines. Research in Korea is testing incorporation of P. tuberregium
sclerotium powder into bread to increase protein content.
