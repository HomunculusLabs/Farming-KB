# Mushroom Storage and Processing Methods

Mushrooms continue to respire after harvest with a relatively high respiration
rate compared to other fresh produce. The respiration rate of [[root-cellaring]]
- Enzymes react with oxygen forming brown pigments, decreasing quality
- Water loss causes wilting and shriveling, lowering fresh mushroom quality
- Metabolic processes generate heat if mushrooms are not rapidly cooled
- Mushrooms at harvest temperature (15-18C) will self-digest if not cooled
  quickly when packed together

## Short Term Storage: Refrigeration

[[ingham-aact-holding-time-shelf-life-decay]] varies from 1 day to 2 weeks depending on conditions.

### Critical Cooling Protocol
Mushrooms should be cooled to storage temperature of 0-2C within five hours of
picking to stop metabolic processes rapidly. If not cooled quickly but packed in
boxes or PVC film, temperature increases due to metabolic processes, and spoilage
can be caused by internal bacteria and fungi.

### Sun Drying
Mushrooms are spread on shelves with gills facing upward, directly exposed to
sunlight. Drying time varies with weather. Quality is lower than thermal or
hot-air methods, and moisture content remains higher, increasing susceptibility
to molds and pests. Simple and requires no equipment.

### Thermal Power Drying
Begin at low temperature: 35C for mushrooms dried on sunny days, 30C for damp
days. After 5-7 hours initial heat, raise temperature gradually to 40-60C and
maintain for 12-18 hours. Drying enhances flavor and appearance. Firewood stoves
can dry mushrooms at 60-80C for 6 hours.

### Hot-Air Drying
Hot air is blown into a drying chamber with mushrooms on shelves. Temperature
and humidity are controlled via heaters and recirculation vents. Products have
better quality, higher hygienic conditions, and brighter color than sun-dried.
Pre-heat chamber to 40-50C before loading. Sort by size before drying for uniform
results. Place mushrooms stem-downward with 15cm shelf clearance.

### Storage of Dried Products
Dried mushrooms readily absorb moisture. At 20% moisture content, infestation by
insects and molds occurs. Store in sealed polyethylene bags in a dry, cool, dark
place. For prolonged storage, pack in cartons or wooden boxes at 2-5C.

### Canning
The most common industrial preservation method. Seven basic operations:
cleaning, blanching, canning, sterilization, cooling, labeling, and packing.
