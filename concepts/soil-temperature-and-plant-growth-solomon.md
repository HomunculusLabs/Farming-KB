## Overview
A critical but often overlooked aspect of [[plant-sensory-biology-smell-touch-hearing]] growth. Most gardeners focus on
above-ground conditions — sunlight, air temperature, rainfall — while
underestimating the importance of what happens below the soil surface. Plants are
fundamentally dependent on the below-ground environment for their growth,
development, and survival.

## Plant Growth Happens at Night
Here is something most gardeners don"t know: the business of construction, of
growth, is mainly done at night. During the daytime, plants store up energy by
converting sunshine, water, and air (CO₂) into sugar through photosynthesis. They
burn that sugar as energy to grow during the night. This has important implications
for [[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]] germination:

### Temperature Categories
- **Cool-season crops** (lettuce, spinach, peas, radishes): germinate best at
  40-65°F (4-18°C) soil temperature.
- **Warm-season crops** (tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, beans): germinate best at
  65-85°F (18-29°C) soil temperature.
- **Hot-season crops** (okra, melons, [[growing-sweet-potatoes]]): need soil temperatures
  above 70°F (21°C) for reliable germination.

### Watering Lowers Soil Temperature
This is a counterintuitive but critical fact: watering lowers the soil temperature.
Many gardeners instinctively water newly planted seeds to "help them germinate,"
but this can actually delay or prevent germination by cooling the soil below the
crop"s minimum threshold. Once a seed is planted and watered initially, additional
watering should be minimal until seedlings emerge.

## Oxygen Requirements
A sprouting seedling needs significantly more oxygen than it did in dormancy. The
embryo will die if there is no oxygen in the soil — which is exactly what happens
when soil is waterlogged for more than a few hours. The relationship between water,
air, and soil particles determines whether roots (and [[solomon-sprouting-seeds-and-early-season-nutrition]]) can breathe.

### The Soil Air-Water Balance
Healthy soil is approximately 50% mineral particles, 25% water, and 25% air by
volume. When soil is waterlogged, water displaces air in the pore spaces, creating
[[aact-ph-and-anaerobic-conditions]]. Without oxygen:
- Seeds cannot germinate (embryo suffocates)
- Roots cannot respire (no energy for [[aact-microbial-foliar-nutrient-uptake-co2-stomata-ingham]])
- Anaerobic bacteria produce toxic compounds (hydrogen sulfide, methane)
- Beneficial aerobic microorganisms die, disrupting soil ecology

## The Seed Germination Process
Understanding germination helps explain why temperature and oxygen matter so much:

### Stage 1: Activation
When a seed is moistened, the embryo (which is alive but dormant) starts producing
complex chemical substances that convert the dried food surrounding it into
water-soluble sugars and amino acids. This process is essentially digestion.

### Stage 2: Root Emergence
Because drying out is one of the biggest dangers to the new plant, the first thing
a clever embryo does is send down a root. Once the root system is working, there
is less chance that the seed will die from lack of water before it can emerge.

### Stage 3: Shoot Emergence
Only after the root is descending does the embryo form the "shoot" — the part that
emerges into the light. The shoot is capable of only limited mechanical force; it
can wiggle its way between larger soil particles, but it cannot push aside compacted
soil, much less break through a crust.

## Crust Formation and Its Prevention
### How Crusts Form
Crust formation occurs through the same process used to finish a concrete slab. In
concrete, gravel, coarse sand, and cement are mixed; while still plastic, the
finisher rubs the surface, causing gravel to sink and the finest particles to rise
to the surface, forming a smooth skin. In soil, pounding rain or sprinkler droplets
act like the trowel — separating clay and silt from sand, with the fine particles
drying into a tough, impenetrable skin.

### Puddling
Puddling happens when a gardener waters after planting, and the soil slumps into a
solid, nearly airless mass. The surface seals, preventing both water infiltration
and [[mushroom-cultivation-jar-lid-management-loose-fit-vacuum-gas-exchange]].
