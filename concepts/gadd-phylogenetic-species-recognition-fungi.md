### Phylogenetic Species Recognition (PSR)

PSR defines species as the smallest aggregation of populations with a
shared parent-child pattern of ancestry and descent. In practice, this
means identifying groups of individuals that are diagnosably distinct from
other such groups based on DNA sequence characters. The approach gained
prominence in mycology through the work of researchers studying medically
important fungi.

The first major application of PSR in mycology involved *Coccidioides
immitis*, the causative agent of Valley Fever. Comparison of five gene
sequences from 17 individuals revealed two phylogenetic species where only
one morphological species had been recognized. This landmark finding
demonstrated that morphological species concepts could substantially
underestimate fungal diversity.

### Multilocus Sequence Typing (MLST)

Multilocus Sequence Typing has emerged as a powerful standard for PSR:
it sequences multiple gene regions (typically 6-8 housekeeping genes) from
each isolate, providing robust phylogenetic signal.

Key advantages of MLST:

- **Portability**: DNA sequences are "portable" — results from different
  laboratories can be directly combined and compared, unlike fingerprinting
  methods (RAPD, AFLP) where inter-laboratory comparison is difficult
- **No ascertainment bias**: Sequencing entire gene regions avoids the
  bias inherent in methods that discover polymorphic markers in one
  population that may be fixed and uninformative in another
- **Global collaboration**: Web-based MLST databases enable researchers
  worldwide to contribute and access sequence data for specific fungal
  species, facilitating truly global studies of [[analytical-methods-psilocybin-psilocin-quantification]] improve
