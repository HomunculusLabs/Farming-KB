## Overview

In the [[legal-responses-to-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-in-the-european-union]], seed sold commercially is
subject to germination standards enforced by law. These regulations exist to
protect buyers from purchasing seed that is effectively dead. However, the
minimum legal thresholds are far lower than what a serious gardener should
accept, and the [[solomon-seed-sprouting-conditions-laboratory-germination-technique]] test conducted within a specified time window.

- In the US, Canada, and EU, any package over **1 pound (454 g)** must carry a
  label showing a **certified lab germination test** performed **within the last
  6 months**.
- The test result shown must meet or exceed the USDA Minimum Standard
  Germination rate for that species.
- Packages at or below the 1-pound threshold are often **exempt from labeling
  requirements**, which is the critical loophole exploited by many seed sellers
  targeting home gardeners.

This means a small packet of seeds from a rack at a hardware store may never
have been tested at all, or may have been tested years before you buy it. The
seed inside could have germination rates far below the legal minimum, and you
would have no way to know.

## The USDA Minimum Standard Germination Table

The USDA publishes minimum germination percentages for each vegetable species.
These are the legal floor — the lowest acceptable rate at the time of testing:

| Vegetable              | USDA Min | Vegetable       | USDA Min |
|-----------------------|----------|-----------------|----------|
| Beans (snap, dry)     | 70%      | Melons          | 65%      |
| Beets                 | 65%      | Onions          | 70%      |
| Broccoli              | 75%      | Parsnips        | 60%      |
| Cabbage               | 75%      | Peas            | 80%      |
| Carrots               | 55%      | Peppers         | 55%      |
| Cauliflower           | 75%      | Pumpkins        | 70%      |
| Corn (sweet)          | 75%      | Radishes        | 75%      |
| Cucumbers             | 80%      | Spinach         | 60%      |
| Eggplant              | 60%      | Squash (summer) | 75%      |
| Lettuce               | 80%      | Tomatoes        | 75%      |
| Turnips               | 80%      |                 |          |

These numbers may look reasonable, but consider what they mean: a USDA minimum
of 55% for carrots means nearly half the seeds in a legally compliant packet
could be dead at the time of testing — and germination only declines from there.

## The Loophole and How It Is Exploited

The fundamental problem is the gap between **minimum legal standard** and
**actual performance in the garden**:

1. **The minimum is a low bar.** A seed lot that barely meets the USDA minimum
   may have been produced from marginal parent stock, harvested poorly, or
   stored in suboptimal conditions before testing.

2. **Germination declines over time.** Even properly stored seed loses vigor.
   A lot that tested at 70% six months before you plant it may only achieve
   50% or less in your garden.

3. **Small packets are exempt.** Most home gardeners buy seed in packets well
   under 1 pound. These may carry a germination date, but there is no legal
   requirement for testing, and no enforcement ensures accuracy.

4. **Seed may sit on shelves for years.** A packet printed "Packed for 2023"
   may contain seed harvested years earlier and only repackaged when demand
   warranted. The original test result bears little resemblance to actual
   performance.
