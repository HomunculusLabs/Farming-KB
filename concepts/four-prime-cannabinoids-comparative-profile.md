# Four Prime Cannabinoids: A Comparative Profile

Uwe Blesching's [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] identifies four "prime cannabinoids" that
are central to therapeutic cannabis medicine: anandamide (an endogenous
cannabinoid), THC and CBD (plant-derived cannabinoids from *Cannabis sativa* L.),
and beta-caryophyllene (a [[beta-caryophyllene-dietary-cannabinoid]] found in common spices). Each
interacts with the endocannabinoid system through distinct receptor mechanisms,
producing overlapping yet differentiated [[cervantes-terpenoid-pharmacology-therapeutic-effects]]. Understanding
these differences is essential for evidence-based [[cultivator-strain-selection-and-stock-culture]]
personalized cannabis therapy.

## Anandamide (AEA): The Body's Own Cannabinoid

Anandamide—named from the Sanskrit word *ananda*, meaning "bliss"—is an
endogenous cannabinoid and neurotransmitter produced in human cell membranes.
It binds at both CB1 and CB2 receptor sites, producing effects very similar to
THC at the proper therapeutic dose. Anandamide serves as the body's natural
modulator of mood, pain, and social behavior.

**Receptor affinity**: Binds CB1 and CB2 (partial agonist).

**Key therapeutic actions**: Anandamide may reduce hypertension, depression,
fear, and anxiety while promoting social play behavior. It reduces pain through
a novel mechanism distinct from opioid pathways. Research shows it inhibits
cancer cell movement and may prevent metastasis without compromising immune
function. The molecule is also relevant to treating psychiatric disorders
characterized by impaired social behavior, including autism.

**Modulation**: Anandamide is produced on demand and rapidly degraded by the
enzyme FAAH (fatty acid amide hydrolase). Exercise—particularly high-intensity
endurance running—significantly increases anandamide levels, contributing to the
"runner's high." Meaningful social interactions, meditation, and the presence
of corticosteroids (released during exercise) also boost anandamide production.
Anandamide inhibits norepinephrine, contributing to its calming effects.

**Unique feature**: Unlike plant cannabinoids that must be ingested or inhaled,
anandamide is continuously produced and degraded by the body as part of normal
endocannabinoid tone. FAAH inhibitors are being investigated as therapeutic
agents to extend anandamide's natural effects.

## Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC): The Primary Psychoactive Cannabinoid

Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is the principal psychoactive molecule in
cannabis. It mimics anandamide by binding at the same CB1 and CB2 receptor sites,
but with greater potency and longer duration of action since it is not rapidly
degraded by FAAH.

**Receptor affinity**: Binds CB1 (partial agonist, primary site of
psychoactivity) and CB2. In its raw acid form (THC-acid/THCA), it is non-
psychoactive and has unique anti-inflammatory properties including TNF-α
inhibition.

**Key therapeutic actions**: THC provides pain relief (partly by inducing
release of [[endogenous-opioids-pain-modulation-endocannabinoid-interaction]] dynorphin A and B), anti-nausea and anti-emetic
effects, [[cannabis-for-cachexia-and-appetite-stimulation]], muscle relaxation, bronchodilation, and anti-
inflammatory action. At low to medium doses, it can increase acetylcholine in the
brain, supporting learning and memory. THC has demonstrated cardio-protective
properties and the ability to produce apoptosis (programmed cell death) in
cancer cells.

**Safety considerations**: THC can increase heart rate and, in excess, may
heighten anxiety rather than relieve it. The psychoactive properties limit the
maximum potential dose and require patients to establish a subjective
therapeutic window. THC exists as THC-acid in fresh, raw cannabis—this form is
non-psychoactive and can be consumed at doses up to 60 times higher than heated
forms.

**Strain profile**: Sativa and sativa-dominant strains typically present higher
THC-to-CBD ratios, favoring CB1 activation. This makes them more appropriate for
neurological and mental/emotional conditions requiring CB1 engagement.

## Cannabidiol (CBD): The Non-Psychoactive Powerhouse

Cannabidiol (CBD) is the second most abundant cannabinoid in cannabis and has
garnered significant attention for its broad therapeutic profile without
psychoactive effects. Unlike THC, CBD does not directly activate CB1 or CB2
receptors but modulates them indirectly, producing what researchers call an
"entourage effect" when combined with other cannabinoids.

**Receptor affinity**: Does not directly bind CB1 or CB2 as an agonist. Instead,
CBD acts as a negative allosteric modulator at CB1 (reducing THC's psychoactive
effects), a weak agonist at CB2, and an agonist at TRPV1 (vanilloid), 5-HT1A
(serotonin), and PPAR-gamma receptors.

**Key therapeutic actions**: CBD is celebrated for its anti-inflammatory,
anti-anxiety, anti-psychotic, anti-convulsant, and neuroprotective properties.
It modulates the serotoninergic system, contributing to stress and anxiety
reduction. CBD has shown efficacy in treating epilepsy (particularly
Dravet syndrome), reducing psychotic symptoms in schizophrenia, and protecting
against neurodegeneration. As a CB2 modulator, CBD supports immune function and
reduces chronic inflammation.

**Unique feature**: CBD counteracts THC's psychoactive effects, including
anxiety and paranoia, making it valuable for patients who are sensitive to THC.
This antagonistic relationship is one reason indica strains (higher CBD ratios)
are often preferred for relaxation and immune conditions.

**Strain profile**: Indica and indica-dominant strains typically have lower THC-
to-CBD ratios, favoring CB2 activation. Fresh raw cannabis leaf is an especially
rich source of CBD in its non-psychoactive acid form (CBD-acid/CBDA).

## Beta-Caryophyllene (BCP): The Dietary Cannabinoid

(E)-beta-caryophyllene is a sesquiterpene found in black pepper, cloves,
cinnamon, oregano, basil, rosemary, hops, caraway, and copaiba oil. It is
unique among dietary compounds in being a selective CB2 agonist fully accepted
by the FDA as a food additive.

**Receptor affinity**: Selective CB2 agonist. Unlike THC, BCP does not bind CB1
and produces no psychoactive effects.

**Key therapeutic actions**: BCP protects against microbes, inflammation,
oxidative stress, pain, and cancer through CB2 activation. Its anti-inflammatory
action makes it relevant to a wide range of chronic conditions involving immune
dysfunction. Used in conjunction with cannabis, BCP creates a synergy of
beneficial CB2-mediated effects.

**Unique feature**: BCP bridges the gap between cannabis medicine and everyday
nutrition. Patients can access CB2 activation through common kitchen spices
without any psychoactive effect, making it an accessible therapeutic option for
populations who cannot or prefer not to use cannabis directly.

## Comparative Summary

| Property | Anandamide | THC | CBD | BCP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source | Endogenous | Cannabis plant | Cannabis plant | Spices/foods |
| CB1 activity | Partial agonist | Partial agonist | Negative modulator | None |
| CB2 activity | Partial agonist | Partial agonist | Weak agonist | Selective agonist |
| Psychoactive | Mild | Yes | No | No |
| Primary use | Mood, pain, social | Pain, nausea, appetite | Inflammation, seizures | Inflammation, immunity |
| Dose limitation | FAAH degradation | Therapeutic window | Very well tolerated | Food-grade safety |
