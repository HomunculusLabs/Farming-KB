# Cannabis for Atherosclerosis

Atherosclerosis was historically defined as accumulation of plaque or bad cholesterol (LDL) within arterial walls. It is now understood to be more than simple plaque build-up. The obstruction is actually a physical response to injuries in the arterial wall lining. Causes include high blood pressure, infectious microbes, and excessive homocysteine. Inflammatory molecules stimulate events leading to atherosclerotic lesion development.

## Disease Characteristics

Atherosclerosis develops slowly over many years. If present in the limbs (peripheral artery disease), it may present as pain during exercise. In torso arteries, it may produce signs similar to heart disease or strokes including chest pain, shortness of breath, or loss of sensation and function.

In mild cases, diminished oxygen supply to tissue occurs. In acute cases, thickened plaque can break loose, causing severe strokes or heart attacks. Orthodox treatment includes surgery and numerous pharmacological interventions.

## Evidence Base

The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] (CHI) rates the evidence for cannabis in atherosclerosis at a CHI value of 6, based on 3 studies reviewed. The evidence comes primarily from laboratory and animal studies but provides a compelling mechanistic rationale.

## Immune System Modulation

A 2006 study from Northeastern University acknowledged important benefits of cannabis use for the human immune system. The researchers noted that habitual cannabis use positively affects the immune system and that advances in endocannabinoid research provide a basis for understanding these immunomodulatory effects.

Cell-based experiments and animal testing suggest that regulation of the endocannabinoid circuitry can impact almost every major function associated with the immune system. The researchers tested numerous molecules exerting effects through the endocannabinoid system and suggested therapeutic potential for inflammatory diseases such as atherosclerosis.

## CBD and 15-LOX Inhibition

Researchers observed that the enzyme 15-LOX can oxygenate LDL, leading to production of oxidized LDL, which plays a factor in developing atherosclerosis. In 2009, researchers from Kanazawa, Japan, examined the impact of cannabinoids on 15-LOX activity.

Results indicated that CBD and its derivatives CBD-2'-monomethyl ether and CBD-2',6'-dimethyl ether (CBDD) inhibit 15-LOX enzyme activity to varying degrees. CBDD, which had the strongest enzyme inhibitory effects, was suggested as a potentially useful prototype for producing medicines for atherosclerosis.

## Direct Lesion Reduction

A group of scientists from Shaanxi, China (2010), examined the impact of the synthetic cannabinoid WIN55,212-2 on atherosclerosis development in a mouse model. Results revealed a significant and direct reduction in the size of aortic atherosclerotic lesions.

This finding is particularly significant because it demonstrates not merely a slowing of progression but an actual reduction in existing atherosclerotic lesion size. If translatable to humans, this could represent a disease-modifying effect.

## Anti-Inflammatory Mechanism

Atherosclerosis is increasingly recognized as an inflammatory disease. The endocannabinoid system, particularly through CB2 receptors found on immune cells, modulates inflammatory responses that drive atherosclerotic plaque formation and progression.

CB2 receptor activation may reduce the recruitment of inflammatory cells to arterial walls, decrease the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and stabilize existing plaques, potentially reducing the risk of plaque rupture and acute cardiovascular events.

## Oxidized LDL Prevention

The ability of CBD derivatives to inhibit 15-LOX addresses a specific pathway in atherosclerosis development. Oxidized LDL is a key driver of foam cell formation in arterial walls, which is a critical early step in plaque development. By inhibiting the oxidation of LDL, cannabinoids may prevent this process from occurring.

## Strain Considerations

The research primarily involved CBD and its derivatives, as well as the synthetic cannabinoid WIN55,212-2. CBD has greater affinity for CB2 receptors. Indica or indica-dominant strains with lower THC:CBD ratios may favor CB2 expression and could be more appropriate for addressing the inflammatory component of atherosclerosis.

CBD-dominant strains or isolated CBD preparations may be most relevant given the 15-LOX inhibition findings.

## Safety Considerations

Atherosclerosis patients are often on multiple cardiovascular medications including statins, blood pressure drugs, and blood thinners. Cannabis may interact with these medications. See [[cannabis-cardiovascular-health]] for detailed safety information.

Patients with existing cardiovascular disease should consult their cardiologist before using cannabis. The cardiovascular effects of THC (including potential tachycardia and blood pressure changes) warrant particular caution in this population.
