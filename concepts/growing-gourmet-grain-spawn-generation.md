Of the many cereal grains used for creating spawn, rye grain is the most
popular. Wheat, milo, sorghum, corn, and millet are also utilized.

## Two Methods of Preparing Grain

### Method 1: Boiling and Steeping

Grain is submerged in a cauldron of boiling water. After an hour of
boiling (or steeping), the saturated grain is drained of water and
scooped into awaiting spawn containers. Fitted with a lid having a 1/3
to 1/2 inch hole lined with a microporous filter disc, the grain-filled
jars are sterilized in a pressure cooker.

This method is widely used and recommended by many because even moisture
absorption and consistency is assured. A case can be made that the
boiling method leaves the starting material cleaner.

### Method 2: Dry Grain in Jars with Added Water

Dry grain is placed into glass spawn jars, the recommended amount of
water (preferably hot) is added, and the jars are allowed to sit
overnight (12-24 hours). The soaking period allows heat-resistant
endospores of bacteria to germinate and become sensitive to heat
[[growing-gourmet-nutrified-agar-media]]. Each
culture incubating in a 100 x 15 mm petri dish can inoculate 10 quarts
of grain spawn. If the mycelium is chopped in a high-speed stirrer and
diluted, one petri dish culture can effectively inoculate 40-100 quarts
of sterilized grain.

## Expansion and Transfer

Once grown out, each jar can inoculate 10 (range: 5-20) times its
original mass for a total of three generations of expansions. Grain
spawn is inoculated into sawdust, straw, etc. at a rate between 3-15%
(wet mass of spawn to dry mass of substrate). Cultivators who generate
their own spawn frequently use 8-15%, while commercial spawn
recommendations are typically 3-7%.

## Matching Spawn to Substrate

Once spawn has been created, the cultivator arrives at a critical
crossroad. For the rapid, high-yield methods of growing Shiitake,
Enokitake, Maitake, and others indoors on sterilized substrates, the
recommended path is: grain spawn to sterilized sawdust to enriched
sawdust. Each transfer step results in an expansion of mycelial mass,
usually by a factor of 5-10, and takes one to two weeks to fully
colonize.

For [[growing-gourmet-phoenix-oyster-pulmonarius-cultivation]], the
recommended path is to inoculate straw with grain spawn directly. Grain
spawn boosts the nutritional base of straw, radically improving yields
compared to using sawdust spawn.

However, for species like Shiitake, grain spawn should not be used
directly on enriched sawdust. A marked increase in temperature occurs
soon after inoculation (thermogenesis). By enriching the substrate with
grain spawn, biochemical reactions are accelerated, producing heat and
carbon dioxide. Should internal temperatures exceed 100 degrees F (38
degrees C), thermophilic contaminants spring forth.
