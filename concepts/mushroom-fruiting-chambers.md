# Mushroom Fruiting Chambers

A fruiting chamber is an enclosed environment designed to maintain the
specific conditions of high humidity, fresh air exchange, and light that
mushrooms need to form and develop fruit bodies. While many commercial and
hobby setups use elaborate systems with humidifiers, cool-mist devices, and
automated controllers, effective fruiting can be achieved with remarkably
simple setups. The key parameters are humidity, fresh air exchange (FAE),
temperature, and light exposure.

## Key Environmental Parameters

### Humidity
Mushrooms require high relative humidity (85-95%) for proper pin formation
and fruit body development. If humidity drops too low, pins abort and the
casing surface dries out. If humidity is too high (approaching 100% with
no air exchange), conditions become anaerobic and bacterial contamination
or cobweb mold can develop.

### Fresh Air Exchange (FAE)
Mushrooms produce CO₂ during respiration. High CO₂ concentrations inhibit
normal fruit body development — causing tall, thin, elongated stems with
small or absent caps. Regular air exchange is necessary to keep CO₂ levels
low enough for proper [[passive-humidity-fruit-chamber-polyethylene-sheeting]] cover**: The bins are covered with polyethylene

  while maintaining high humidity
- **Ventilation holes**: Small holes melted in the sides of the bins provide
  passive air exchange. These can be covered with mesh to prevent entry of
  fungus gnats and other flying contaminants

evaporation from the five casing surfaces provides sufficient moisture to
maintain proper humidity inside the bin without any mechanical humidification.
