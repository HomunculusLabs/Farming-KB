Environmental control, particularly temperature and humidity management,
is fundamental to successful cannabis cultivation. [[greg-green]]'s Cannabis
Grow Bible provides clear guidance on maintaining optimal growing
conditions, noting that cannabis plants are sensitive to environmental
extremes and that poor climate control can stunt growth, promote disease,
and reduce yields. See also
[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-indoor-grow-room-setup]] and
[[green-cannabis-ph-management-cannabis-growing]].

Green identifies environmental management as one of the most important
factors for achieving good results, listing air circulation alongside
light, genetics, soil, and proper fertilization as the key pillars of
successful cultivation. A grower who masters climate control will see
healthier plants, faster growth, and better yields than one who neglects
this fundamental aspect.

## Optimal Temperature Range

Temperature is controlled by any heating unit or natural sunlight that the
room receives. Normal households have suitable temperatures for growing
cannabis. Green states that 75 degrees Fahrenheit is what growers should
aim for as a target temperature. Slightly warmer rooms do help plants to
grow a bit quicker.

The best meter for temperature control, according to Green, is the human
body. If a grower finds it too cold in the grow room, then so will the
plants. However, using a thermometer to analyze the room's temperature
provides more precise readings. If too cold, turn on heating. If too hot,
open a window and maybe even use a fan.

During the dark periods of the flowering phase, the temperature is
allowed to drop. Green indicates that temperatures can fall as low as
55 degrees Fahrenheit during the night cycle without harming the plants.
This natural temperature drop mimics outdoor conditions and may actually
improve terpene production and bud quality.

Cannabis leaves will burn if placed too close to light sources, which is
a temperature related issue. The heat generated by HID lights can raise
leaf surface temperatures well above ambient room temperature, so proper
light distance management is an important part of temperature control.

## Humidity Management

Cannabis plants work best in the 40 to 80 percent relative humidity area.
rH (relative humidity) is the amount of water in the air. The ideal
target is around 60 percent relative humidity. Fresh air is always the
best way to control humidity levels in the grow space.

If a grower has an rH measuring kit, they can judge how much fresh air
is needed before hitting the optimum 60% level. There are expensive
devices called dehumidifiers that can control humidity in the room, but
Green notes this is not recommended unless the grower has a very large
grow area underway. For most grows, simply using fresh air exchange is
sufficient.

High humidity can promote mold and fungal problems, especially during
flowering when dense buds can trap moisture. Low humidity can cause plants
to dry out too quickly and stress the leaves. Maintaining a balance
through proper ventilation is the preferred approach.

Seedlings and young clones prefer higher humidity, typically 70 to 80
percent, as their root systems are not yet developed enough to meet water
demands through uptake alone. As plants mature, humidity should gradually
be reduced to the 40 to 60 percent range to prevent mold issues during
the dense flowering stage.

## Air Circulation and Ventilation

Fresh air is critical for cannabis plants. During dry periods between
watering, the roots need to breathe, and fresh air circulation around
the plant canopy supports transpiration and nutrient uptake. If the grow
is enclosed, Green recommends using fans to extract old air and another
fan as an intake to refresh the system.

During winter, growers may want to reduce the time windows are left open
as cold may stunt growth. Simply refreshing the air in the room for 15 to
20 minutes during winter and closing it again is sufficient.

Good air circulation can build up in spaces that do not have good
ventilation. A rise in temperatures can cause a plant to stunt. Keeping
fresh air moving around the plants at all times is essential. Fans also
help strengthen stems by simulating wind, which encourages sturdier
plant development.

Fans can be placed quite close to mature marijuana plants. The gentle
movement of air prevents hot spots under lights, distributes CO2 evenly,
and helps maintain uniform temperature and humidity throughout the grow
space.

## CO2 Supplementation

Plants enjoy CO2 and release O2 during photosynthesis, but more O2 is
released than CO2 is absorbed. Since plants would like more CO2, the
grower can use a CO2 generator to help the plant's growth. CO2 also
helps grow bigger buds. Since room CO2 levels in general are low, the
plants can benefit from supplementation.

CO2 systems can be purchased from some grow shops. When using CO2
enrichment, it is important to maintain proper temperature and
ventilation. Plants can utilize higher levels of CO2 more effectively
when temperatures are slightly elevated. However, CO2 supplementation
requires careful monitoring and should not be attempted by beginners
until basic environmental control is mastered.

## Summary

Maintaining proper temperature around 75 degrees Fahrenheit and humidity
around 60% relative humidity creates the ideal growing environment for
cannabis. Fresh air exchange through ventilation fans is the primary tool
for managing both parameters. CO2 supplementation can further enhance
growth but requires experience and careful environmental management.
Preventing problems through consistent climate control is always easier
than solving them after they arise.
