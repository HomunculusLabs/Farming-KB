# Permaculture Multipurpose Trees

In [[permaculture-design-course-methodology]], the most valuable trees serve multiple functions simultaneously -- timber, fodder, soil improvement, food, firewood, and habitat. The principle is: every element should serve many functions. See [[permaculture-multi-purpose-plants]] and [[permaculture-tree-crop-systems]] for broader coverage.

## Bamboo (Bambusa/Phyllostachys spp)
Family: BAMBUSACEAE. Among the most versatile plants on Earth. Two main types: clumping (non-invasive, tropical/subtropical) and running (invasive, temperate). See [[bamboo-permaculture-design]] for design applications.

### Clumping Bamboos
- **Bambusa balcoa** -- to 22 m, 15 cm diameter. Best bamboo for building in India.
- **Bambusa oldhamii** (giant timber bamboo) -- to 17 m, 10 cm diameter. Tolerates -9C. Used as ornamental windbreak or hedge around orchards. Buds eaten in China.
- **Dendrocalamus strictus** (male bamboo) -- most useful bamboo in India. Dense clumps to 18 m, often solid culms. Tolerates -1C. Source of edible manna, young shoots and seeds are eaten. Dry bamboo used for torches, charcoal for smith's work. Leaves for fodder, mats, baskets, masts, musical instruments, paper, poles, water pipes.

### Running Bamboos (control with stock grazing)
- **Phyllostachys bambusoides** (Madake) -- to 23 m, 15 cm diameter. Most useful bamboo in Japan for scaffolding and construction. Tolerates -7C and wide pH range. Edible shoots.
- **Phyllostachys nigra** (black bamboo) -- to 9 m, culms turn black with age. Hardy to -7C. Ornamental and edible shoots.
- **Phyllostachys henonsis** -- to 17 m, hardy to -21C. Third most useful bamboo in Japan. Very strong despite thin culm walls.
- **Sasa palmata** -- small (2-4 m), leaves to 35 cm long. Useful as stock feed, shelter, and paper pulp. Highly invasive -- control with grazing animals.

## Willow (Salix spp)
Family: SALICACEAE. Over 300 species with the widest natural distribution of any tree genus. Extremely good for erosion control and salinity management. Root systems reach 50 m laterally.

### Key Functions
- **Erosion control**: Sheet, gully, and slump erosion; salinity control
- **Fodder**: Weeping willow foliage -- 15.9% crude protein, superior nutrition. NZ hybrid trials: 30+ t dry matter/ha vs. 10-15 t for grass/lucerne. See [[fodder-growing-guide]].
- **Bee forage**: Provide nectar and pollen when few plants flowering (see [[bee-forage-plant-system]])
- **Medicinal**: Bark contains salicin (precursor to aspirin); traditional remedy for fever and rheumatism
- **Rooting hormone**: Willow cuttings in water produce natural rooting hormone surpassing commercial powders
- **Craft**: Basket willow (S. viminalis) harvested annually for traditional basket weaving trade
- **Timber**: S. alba 'Coerulea' (cricket bat willow) -- unique industry, ready at 12-15 years

### Propagation
Strike readily from cuttings at any size. Plant so roots are above winter groundwater table. Spacing: 2 m for cut systems, 5 m for full trees.

## Poplar (Populus spp)
Family: SALICACEAE. Fast-growing, soft light timber. Similar water use and erosion control properties to willows. Deciduous -- allows winter sun to understorey.

### Key Features
- **Fodder**: Foliage nutritionally superior to lucerne (higher minerals, fats, nitrates; lower cellulose)
- **Productivity case study**: Hunter River farm (NSW) -- 40 ha planted to poplars + grass, cattle numbers increased 20% while trees grew to timber harvest size in just 15 years
- **Timber**: Furniture, construction, plywood, fibreboard, matches, fruit boxes. Pulp for paper. See [[timber-frame-basics]].
- **Sewage recycling**: Dungong (NSW) and Darville (SA) irrigate poplar plantations with sewage effluent -- sold to match companies
- **Firewood/charcoal**: Good fuelwood quality

### Propagation
From cuttings like willows, even from 10 m high cuttings if water accessible. Autumn most successful time. Phosphorus improves root development.

## Moringa (Moringa oleifera)
Family: MORINGACEAE. Small deciduous tree to 8 m, native to India. Drought tolerant, frost tender. Called the "miracle tree" for its extraordinary range of uses:

- **Food**: Long bean-like fruit in soups/curries; young leaves and twigs with mustard flavour in salads; immature seeds like peas; mature seeds roasted like peanuts; root used like horseradish. Leaves contain 7-10% protein.
- **Oil**: Seeds yield behen/ben oil that never goes rancid -- used for salads, cooking, and as precision machinery lubricant (watchmakers oil).
- **Water purification**: Seed powder used traditionally to clarify turbid water.
- **Fodder**: Leaves used as supplementary livestock feed in [[forage-systems-animals-permaculture]].

## Willow and Poplar Companions

Being deciduous, willows and poplars are not effective winter windbreaks but shelter from hot summer winds. Being deciduous allows understorey growth of bana grass, elephant grass, pampas grass, and bamboo over winter. Good companions: [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] like Acacia saligna (fodder) and Acacia melanoxylon (furniture timber). Both species integrate well into silvopasture-systems|silvopasture designs.

## Mulberry (Morus spp)
Family: MORACEAE. Small to medium deciduous trees, long-lived (300 years). Hardy, drought resistant, frost tolerant, shade tolerant. Heavy bearers (19-24 t/ha). Fruit eaten raw or dried and powdered into flour. Self-harvest pig food (2-3 pigs per tree at 86 trees/ha for 60-day fruiting season). Excellent poultry forage. Coppices readily for tool handles and fence posts. Leaves very palatable to livestock and nutritious (crude protein 15.1%, crude fat 5.7%, crude fibre 13.7%, calcium 1.95%). Considered a good companion to grapes and can serve as living trellis. Good companion to garlic, tagasaste, lucerne, and Albizia.

- **M. alba** (white mulberry) -- to 25 m, fast growing, leaves traditionally fed to silkworms. One of the best trees for cultivation on well-drained soils.
- **M. nigra** (black mulberry) -- to 12 m, English/Persian mulberry. Timber prized for turnery and carving.
- **M. rubra** (red mulberry) -- native to eastern/central USA. Young shoots and leaves edible raw or boiled.

## Persimmon (Diospyros kaki)
Family: EBENACEAE. Deciduous round-headed tree to 5 m with striking autumn foliage. Fruit orange, slightly larger than tomato, must be fully ripe (unripe is very astringent). Bears at 3 years (grafted). Nutritional per 100g: 77 cal, 19.7g carbohydrate, 174mg potassium, 2710 IU vitamin A. Dioecious (needs male + female). Propagation by seed, grafting, or cuttings. Minimum spacing 5m. Benefits from mulching-guide|mulching. A valuable addition to [[permaculture-fruit-tree-guide]] canopy layers.

## Eucalyptus as Multipurpose Timber
Eucalyptus species provide fast-growing timber, firewood, nectar for [[mollison-designers-bee-forage-tree-planting]], and erosion control. In Western Australia, species like York gum and salmon gum serve as permaculture-windbreak|windbreaks while producing fence posts and firewood.

Some species produce high-quality furniture timber. Intercrop with [[permaculture-designers-manual-nitrogen-fixing-trees]] to prevent nutrient depletion. See [[windbreak-and-shelterbelt-design]].

## Key Design Principle: Every Element Serves Many Functions

The central permaculture principle applied to trees: a single mulberry provides fruit, livestock fodder, timber, silkworm food, grape trellis support, and [[bee-forage-plant-system]] simultaneously. Design tree systems so that each species fills at least 3-4 functions to maximize yields per unit area. This connects to [[permaculture-food-forest-design-principles]], [[silvopasture-systems]], [[permaculture-companion-planting]], [[forage-systems-animals-permaculture]], [[fodder-growing-guide]], and [[permaculture-grazing-and-pasture]]. Support with [[comparison-sheet-mulching-vs-chop-and-drop]].

This principle applies at every scale -- from a single homestead tree to broadacre silvopasture-systems|silvopasture plantings. Always ask: what else can this tree do?
