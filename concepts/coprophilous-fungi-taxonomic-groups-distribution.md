Zygomycetes generally are observed early in succession on dung (within
5-14 days). They are often isolated preferentially from fresh dung,
which is generally a poorer substratum for most Ascomycetes and
Basidiomycetes but preferable for the isolation of Zygomycetes.

### Ascomycetes

Ascomycetes are the most diverse group of coprophilous fungi. Cain
(1934) recorded 112 taxa of coprophilous Sphaeriales from Ontario,
Canada. In East Africa and Venezuela, comparable numbers of taxa are
118 and 122, respectively. If one includes all coprophilous
pyrenomycetes for these regions, the numbers increase to 151 and 159.
Cailleux (1971) recorded 91 taxa from the Central African Republic.
Richardson (2001) found 153 taxa in 50 samples from the zone 0-30
degrees north and south of the equator.

Ascomycetes characteristically dominate the middle stages of
coprophilous succession. The Sordariaceae, Sporormiaceae, and
Chaetomiaceae are particularly well-represented families. The genus
Sordaria, Podospora, Ascobolus, and Thelebolus are among the most
commonly encountered genera.

### Basidiomycetes

Basidiomycetes typically appear later in coprophilous succession,
often overlapping with Ascomycetes rather than following them
strictly. Some species that use lignin may sporulate together with
Ascomycetes. Basidiomycetes on dung are less diverse than
Ascomycetes but include distinctive species in several orders.

### Regional Differences

Lundqvist (1972) pointed out that groups of north-temperate fungi and
tropical-subtropical fungi appear to be distinct and are likely
distinct from a corresponding group of south-temperate fungi. Within
each group, the distribution of taxa is fairly cosmopolitan.
