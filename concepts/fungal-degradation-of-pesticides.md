## Fungal Degradation of Pesticides and Herbicides

Pesticides and herbicides constitute a major class of environmental pollutants.
Fungi degrade these compounds through enzymatic transformation, mineralization,
and co-metabolism. White-rot fungi are particularly effective due to their non-
specific [[phanerochaete-chrysosporium]]* degrades DDT and lindane, though complete
mineralization is limited.

**Organophosphorus insecticides:** Malathion, parathion, chlorpyrifos, and
diazinon are hydrolyzed by fungal phosphatases, esterases, and
carboxylesterases. *Trichoderma viride* effectively degrades chlorpyrifos. The
hydrolysis products are generally less toxic than parent compounds.

**Miscellaneous insecticides:** Carbamate insecticides (carbaryl, carbofuran)
are hydrolyzed by fungal esterases and amidases. Pyrethroids are degraded
through oxidative and hydrolytic pathways.

## Herbicide Degradation

**Phenoxyalkanoates (2,4-D, MCPA, 2,4,5-T):** These widely used herbicides
are degraded by fungi through side-chain cleavage, ring hydroxylation, and
dechlorination. White-rot fungi mineralize phenoxy herbicides via ligninolytic
enzymes.

**Phenylamides:** *Acylanilides* (alachlor, metolachlor) are degraded by
N-dealkylation, ring hydroxylation, and dechlorination. *Phenylureas* (diuron,
linuron, monuron) are degraded through N-demethylation and dechlorination.
*Phenylcarbamates* (propham, chlorpropham) are hydrolyzed by fungal esterases.

**s-Triazine herbicides (atrazine, simazine):** Degraded through
N-dealkylation, hydroxylation, and dechlorination. Complete mineralization by
fungi alone is limited; combined fungal-bacterial systems are more effective.

## Fungicide Degradation

Fungi can degrade compounds designed to kill them, demonstrating remarkable
metabolic versatility. **Organomercurial fungicides** are converted from
organomercury to mercury(II) by organomercury lyase, then reduced to elemental
mercury(0) by mercuric reductase — paralleling bacterial mercury resistance.
**Organosulfur fungicides** are degraded through C-S bond cleavage and further
oxidation of sulfur-containing metabolites.

## White-Rot Fungi as Model Degraders

White-rot basidiomycetes, particularly *Phanerochaete chrysosporium*, *Trametes
versicolor*, and *Bjerkandera adusta*, have received the most attention. Their
[[oyster-substrate-fermentation]]** (fungal inoculum on
lignocellulosic substrates mixed into contaminated soil), **bioaugmentation**
(concentrated inoculum introduced into soil), **biopile systems** (soil in piles
with nutrient amendments, aeration, moisture control), and **constructed
wetlands** for treating agricultural runoff. Key challenges include maintaining
fungal viability, ensuring contact between fungal biomass and sorbed residues,
and managing intermediate metabolites.
