## Fukuoka on Food Culture and the Japanese Diet

Fukuoka was deeply critical of Japan's postwar dietary transformation. The traditional Japanese diet, based on rice, barley, vegetables, miso, pickles, and small amounts of fish, represented for him a perfect integration of food production, human nutrition, and ecological wisdom. The postwar shift toward Western-style eating habits, including increased consumption of meat, dairy, refined sugar, and processed foods, was in his view both a symptom and a cause of Japan's cultural decline. He argued that the Japanese had abandoned their food self-sufficiency without understanding the consequences: dependence on imported food made Japan vulnerable to global market fluctuations and tied the nation to an agricultural system based on chemical inputs and environmental destruction.
