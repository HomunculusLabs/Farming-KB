## Regional Seed Selection and Climate Zones

It would be profitable if one variety of each vegetable could be sold to gardeners everywhere, but that is not possible. A variety that excels in a short-season northern climate may fail utterly in a hot, humid southern garden, and vice versa. Understanding your climate zone and selecting varieties adapted to it is one of the most important decisions a gardener can make.

## Why Regionality Matters

When a seedrack jobber operates across a continent and seeks to cut costs, one of the first things sacrificed is any attempt to offer regionally appropriate varieties. The compromise is to offer an assortment that appears to work everywhere — which cannot be done. This is one of the fundamental flaws of garden center seed racks and mass-market seed companies.

The consequence: gardeners in all regions get mediocre results because the varieties were selected for average conditions that don"t match any specific location.

## The Four Climate Zones

### Short-Season Climates

**Geographic extent**: The northern tier of US states and the part of southern Canada within a few hundred miles of the US border (where over 90% of Canadian citizens live).

**Characteristics**:
- Short frost-free growing season (often less than 100 days)
- Cool summers, especially at night
- Late spring frosts, early autumn frosts
- Long summer days (high latitude)
- Soil may freeze deeply in winter

**Key challenges**: Getting crops to mature before frost. Choosing varieties with the shortest possible days-to-maturity. Protecting from late spring and early autumn frosts.

**Best suppliers**: Stokes Seeds, Johnny's Selected Seeds, Veseys Seeds, [[william-dam-seeds]].

### Moderate Climates

**Geographic extent**: The middle American states (roughly from the northern border of Pennsylvania to the southern border of North Carolina, extending to the Rockies), the east coast of Australia south of Sydney, and the North Island of New Zealand.

**Characteristics**:
- Frost-free growing season of more than 120 days
- Summers get hot and steamy
- Winter is severe enough to freeze soil solid at least 12 inches (30 cm) deep
- Distinct four-season pattern

**Key challenges**: Managing heat and humidity in summer. Dealing with a wide range of conditions through the season. Both early and late plantings are possible.

**Best suppliers**: Johnny's Selected Seeds (straddles the short-season/middle-states line), Harris Seeds (notable for the Sweet Meat squash).

### Warm Climates

**Geographic extent**: The southern American states, Australia from Sydney north to about Bundaberg, and similar subtropical regions.

**Characteristics**:
- Soil never freezes solid
- Summers are long and hot
- Climate may be humid (American South) or dry (inland Australia)
- Comparatively brief winters may occasionally be frosty
- Winter gardening is possible without protection in many areas

**Key challenges**: Extreme summer heat. Choosing heat-tolerant varieties. Managing pests and diseases that thrive in warm, humid conditions. Avoiding bolting in warm-weather crops. Day-length sensitivity for onions (short-day vs. long-day varieties).

**Best suppliers**: Park Seed Company (semi-tropical trials and rigorous standards). Southern Exposure Seed Exchange.

### Maritime Climates

**Geographic extent**: The Pacific Northwest coast of North America (west of the Cascade Mountains), southern coastal Victoria and Tasmania (Australia), the South Island of New Zealand, and parts of the UK.

**Characteristics**:
- Relatively cool summers
- Mild, wet winters
- Rarely does the soil freeze solid except at higher elevations
- Periods of sub-freezing weather do happen but don't last long
- Winter gardening ranges from difficult to easy and productive
- Ocean's moderating influence reduces temperature extremes

**Key challenges**: Short, cool growing season for heat-loving crops. Excess moisture and fungal diseases. Choosing varieties suited to cool summers. Taking advantage of the mild winter for year-round growing.

**Best suppliers**: Territorial Seed Company (Oregon), West Coast Seeds (British Columbia), Chase (UK), Diggers Seeds (Australia).

## How Regionality Affects Seed Trials

### The Trials Ground Location Test

When you purchase seeds, you have a far higher likelihood of a successful result if the supplier's trials grounds are located in roughly the same climatic zone as your garden. A variety that performs brilliantly in maritime Oregon may fail completely in humid South Carolina, even though both are in the US.

### What Trials Reveal

Proper variety trials conducted in a climate similar to yours will show:

- Whether the variety will mature in your growing season
- How it handles your temperature extremes
- Disease resistance relevant to your local disease pressure
- Flavor characteristics that develop under your growing conditions
- Storage potential under your harvest conditions

### The Danger of Universal Recommendations

Books, websites, and seed catalogs that recommend varieties without specifying climate zone are doing gardeners a disservice. A "great" tomato variety for coastal Oregon may produce almost nothing in the same summer in humid Georgia. The only reliable recommendation is one from someone growing in conditions similar to yours.

## Day-Length Sensitivity

Some crops are highly sensitive to day length, which adds another layer of regional complexity:

- **Onions**: Must be matched to your latitude — short-day varieties for southern latitudes (form bulbs when days are 10-12 hours), long-day varieties for northern latitudes (form bulbs when days are 14-16 hours). Planting the wrong type produces green onions but no bulbs.
- **Spinach**: Long-day varieties bolt (go to seed) rapidly as days lengthen in spring. Short-day or bolt-resistant varieties are needed for spring planting in most climates.
- **Potatoes**: Tuber formation is triggered by day length — some varieties need long days, others are day-neutral.

## Special Regional Considerations

### Dryland Gardening

In areas with low or unreliable rainfall, variety selection must account for drought tolerance. Some varieties produce acceptably with minimal irrigation while others will fail without regular watering.

### High Elevation

Gardens at high elevation combine short-season challenges with intense sun, cold nights, and rapid temperature swings. Special variety selection is essential.

### Urban Microclimates

Cities create heat islands that may shift your effective climate zone by one or more categories. A downtown garden may support varieties that would fail 20 miles away in the countryside.
