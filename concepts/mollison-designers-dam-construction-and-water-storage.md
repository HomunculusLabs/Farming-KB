## Spillways

The spillway is the most critical safety feature. Mollison is emphatic:
every dam must have an adequately sized spillway. Failure of the spillway
causes dam failure and potential downstream disaster.

- Size the spillway for the maximum probable flood (the 1-in-100-year storm
  minimum, preferably 1-in-500-year)
- Line spillways with rock or grass to prevent erosion
- Never use the dam wall as the spillway -- erosion of the wall causes
  catastrophic failure
- Provide a trash rack at the spillway entrance to prevent blockage by
  debris

## Compaction and Sealing

Proper compaction is the difference between a durable dam and a leaky one:

- Excavate the foundation to impervious material or rock
- Build the wall in 150-200mm layers, each compacted with a sheepsfoot
  roller or by tracked machinery
- Maintain moisture content at optimal level during compaction (soil should
  hold shape when squeezed but not release water)
- Key the wall into the foundation trench to prevent seepage under the wall
