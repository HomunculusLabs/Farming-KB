# Qualitative Comments Methodology in PiHKAL

**Source:** PiHKAL (Part 2), various compound entries

**Updated:** 2026-04-19

## Overview

The QUALITATIVE COMMENTS sections in PiHKAL Part 2 are first-person subjective reports from self-experimentation with each compound. These reports — written by Shulgin, his research group, and sometimes external contributors — are the primary phenomenological data in the PiHKAL system, providing rich descriptions of the experiential character of each substance at specific dosages.

The qualitative comments methodology represents a unique contribution to psychopharmacology. Rather than reducing drug effects to numerical scores or behavioral checklists, Shulgin preserved the full texture of subjective experience, creating a phenomenological database that remains unmatched in its scope and detail.

## Report Format

Each qualitative comment follows a consistent format:

- **Dosage**: The specific milligram amount taken, in parentheses

- **First-person narrative**: A detailed description of the experience, including onset, progression, peak effects, and comedown

- **Temporal markers**: References to specific timepoints (e.g., "at the one hour point," "for the first hour")

- **Sensory modality descriptions**: Visual, auditory, tactile, emotional, cognitive effects

This format allows readers to reconstruct the temporal arc of each experience and compare effects across different dosages and compounds. The [[psychedelic-dosage-titration-methodology]] provides the quantitative anchor for the qualitative descriptions.

## The Shulgin Voice

Shulgin's own reports are characterized by:

- Precise, clinical language mixed with poetic and evocative description

- Honest acknowledgment of negative effects alongside positive ones

- References to specific activities (museum visits, meals, conversations) that anchor the experience in concrete settings

- Frequent comparisons to other compounds in the PiHKAL family

Example from 2C-B at 16 mg: "A day at the Stanford museum. Things were visually rich, yet I felt that I was reasonably inconspicuous. The Rodin sculptures were very personal and not terribly subtle."

Shulgin's literary quality sets the PiHKAL reports apart from most pharmacological literature. His background in both chemistry and the humanities gave him an unusual ability to bridge scientific observation and experiential description, a quality that connects his work to the broader field of [[mckenna-consciousness-expansion-and-drug-war]].

## Multiple Perspectives

Many compound entries include reports from different individuals at different dosages, creating a composite portrait of the compound's effects. The reports are anonymized, identified only by dosage. This multiplicity of perspectives helps distinguish compound-specific effects from individual variation.

Example from 2C-B: four reports spanning 16 mg to 24 mg show a range from "museum level" visual enhancement to "totally into my body" erotic intensity.

This multi-reporter approach anticipates modern clinical trial methodology, where aggregated subjective reports are used to characterize drug effects across populations. The difference is that PiHKAL's reporters were typically experienced with multiple psychoactive compounds, providing a more nuanced comparative framework than naive subjects could offer.

## The Overdose Reports

Shulgin included overdose reports as a deliberate safety measure. For 2C-B, he described a 64 mg experience ("propelled into something not of my choosing") and a 100 mg accidental overdose ("I had simply picked up the wrong vial"). These reports demonstrate the compounds' safety margin — even at 4-8x the normal dose, the subjects recovered without lasting harm.

These overdose reports serve multiple functions within the [[shulgin-safety-principles-self-experimentation]]:

- They document the compounds' therapeutic index (the ratio of toxic dose to active dose)

- They provide calibration points for the [[tihkal-shulgin-rating-scale]] at extreme intensities

- They serve as cautionary examples discouraging within-session dose escalation

- They contribute to the [[psychedelic-safety-and-harm-reduction]] knowledge base

## Relationship to the Rating Scale

The [[tihkal-shulgin-rating-scale]] (+/- through ++++) provides a quantitative framework that the qualitative comments illuminate with experiential detail. Comments often implicitly reference the scale: "I was a distinct +1 in 35 minutes and a +2 by the end of the hour."

The interplay between the scale and the comments creates a two-dimensional description of each experience: the scale captures intensity, while the comments capture quality. Together, they provide a more complete picture than either could alone.

## Ann Shulgin's Contributions

Ann Shulgin made significant contributions to the qualitative comments sections. Her reports are often noted for their emphasis on emotional and relational dimensions of the psychedelic experience, complementing Sasha's more cognitively-oriented descriptions. Her perspective was particularly valuable for characterizing compounds with empathogenic qualities, as documented in discussions of [[mdma-in-pihkal-context]].

## The EXTENSIONS AND COMMENTARY

Following the qualitative comments, each entry includes an EXTENSIONS AND COMMENTARY section where Shulgin provides:

- Historical context for the compound's discovery

- Comparisons with related compounds

- Discussion of structure-activity relationships

- Hypotheses about unexplored chemical variants

- Safety observations and cautions

This section bridges the phenomenological data of the comments with the chemical knowledge of the synthesis, creating an integrated picture of each compound's place in the broader pharmacological landscape. These commentaries also informed the [[shulgin-designer-drugs-analysis]] that Shulgin contributed to the field.

## Legacy and Influence

The qualitative comments methodology has influenced several areas of contemporary practice:

- Clinical researchers use first-person report frameworks inspired by PiHKAL's approach

- The [[psychedelic-harm-reduction]] relies on subjective effect descriptions for education

- Psychopharmacology researchers have called for more systematic phenomenological methods

- The [[pihkal-qualitative-effects-themes]] of PiHKAL reports has revealed cross-compound patterns in psychedelic experience

## Dosage as an Independent Variable

A distinctive feature of PiHKAL's methodology is the systematic variation of dosage across reports for the same compound. By presenting multiple reports at ascending doses — for example, 2C-B at 12 mg, 16 mg, 20 mg, and 24 mg — Shulgin created dose-response curves from subjective experience rather than from animal models or receptor binding assays. This approach maps the nonlinear relationship between dose and experiential intensity that characterizes most psychedelics: small dose increases can produce disproportionately large changes in effect at certain thresholds. The [[psychedelic-dosage-titration-methodology]] that emerges from these reports has become a foundational principle in psychedelic harm reduction, where users are advised to "start low and go slow" precisely because PiHKAL's data demonstrates that the dose-response curve is not always predictable.
