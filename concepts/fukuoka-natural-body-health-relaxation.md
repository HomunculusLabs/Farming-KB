## People Ought to Live Pleasantly and Die Pleasantly

Dr. Keizo Hashimoto, a chiropractor from Sendai, made a strong impression on Fukuoka. What Hashimoto does when a patient comes in is very simple: he has the patient lie down on a cot and just tugs on his legs and arms a bit. What he is after is simply to make the patient comfortable.

- "If the right arm hurts when the patient raises it, then I have him shake the left arm in the other direction"

- "I just try to make him feel good. If he can get rid of the pain, that is fine"

- In other words, it is enough to relax

- Hashimoto says that "people ought to live pleasantly and die pleasantly"

Fukuoka found these to be wise words indeed, and they aligned perfectly with his own philosophy of [[fukuoka-do-nothing-health-philosophy]] as a path to health.

## The Natural Body Moves Like Jello

A physical education professor at Nihon University said that all one needs to do during exercise is to make the body perfectly limp and let the limbs dangle.

- Fukuoka touched the professor's body and was astonished to find that although he was just standing there doing nothing at all, inside, his body was rippling

- His inner organs were exercising by themselves

- "The human body acts as if it were made of Jello. It is like a leather bag filled with water"

- "If you shake it, it even sloshes about inside"

- "Just let the arms and legs and everything else dangle. No one has to put himself through a hard workout"

- He told the audience to build bodies as flexible and strong as a whip

## Oriental Medicine Moving Toward Non-Intervention

The way Fukuoka saw it, techniques in Oriental medicine such as shiatsu (finger-pressure therapy), acupuncture, and moxibustion are gradually being unified and simplified.

- In spite of what everyone says, Fukuoka felt that things are moving in the direction of doing nothing

- He had long maintained that sports today which insist on rigorous training to toughen the body are a mistake

- Malady always arises from what is unnatural

- Mental disharmony with nature hardens the mind, while unnatural physical care stiffens the body

- By continuing to endure such conditions and failing to exercise moderation, one eventually meets up with serious illness

The connection to fukuoka natural body health relaxation is direct: health is not achieved through strenuous effort but through the abandonment of effort.

## Recovery Through Return to the Natural State

To recover from illness, one must lighten the heart and live easily -- without strain.

- If you do nothing and simply return to the natural body of an infant, then your health will improve

- If you have become neurotic from thinking too much, then returning to the state of an infant who thinks of nothing will cure the neurosis

- However, when one is told to become an infant, the mind does not return

- When one is enjoined to become a fool, one is not bright enough to do so

- Should this happen, the only choice is to allow that person to amuse himself with total abandon or to cast him into the mountains and leave him there to himself

- In this way, willing or not, he becomes a fool. He will resign himself.

## Therapy at the Mountain Farm

For the first day or two, the folks who come to stay at Fukuoka's yard put up with things, but after a week has passed they begin to lose track of the time and come asking what day of the week it is. What was to have been a week turns into two weeks, three weeks.

- After two weeks, they feel as if they could stay on for two or three months

- When three months have passed, staying for a year or two becomes quite natural

- This certainly is one fashion in which therapy could be conducted

Fukuoka noted that Westerners who come to stay at his farm seem to know nature best of all and would appear to have the least trouble returning to it. The mountain farm environment, with its simple huts and hearthside living, naturally induces the relaxation and mental emptiness that modern people desperately need.

## The Dogo Hot Springs as Zen

Fukuoka often urged foreigners who visit his farm to go to the Dogo Hot Springs nearby.

- "Since you have come all this way, you ought to stop by the hot springs"

- "One can also attain perfect serenity by stretching out at a spa"

- Dogo is Japan's oldest hot-springs spa, created according to legend by Okuninushi no Mikoto

- It has been visited by many distinguished people, from Prince Shotoku to Emperor Hirohito

- Poets such as Issa, Basho, and Shiki have come here

- Modern writers including Natsume Soseki have stopped by

What Fukuoka liked most of all at Dogo was just stretching out on the granite floor of the baths. The back warms up and makes you feel good. The sensation when you lie down with your arms and legs stretched out is indescribable. The whole body unwinds. As the muscles relax, you become comfortable and free. The heart too loosens up and relaxes. You become free and uninhibited.

- This loosening of the body is the road to oneness with the Buddha

- It is a shortcut to the Buddha -- which is why Fukuoka was always going over to Dogo

Meditation that involves mental concentration, Fukuoka considered a form of brain-washing that is a dangerous road. See [[fukuoka-natural-body-and-health]].

## Moving the Body Frees the Mind

Fukuoka had no objection to those who earnestly practice zazen on a tatami mat, but he wondered if one can be at rest by joining one's hands, crossing one's legs, and staying perfectly still. He would think that in moving one's arms and legs, one's attention would be diverted to this, emptying the mind and achieving the same result as if one were to sit quietly.
