## People Ought to Live Pleasantly and Die Pleasantly

Dr. Keizo Hashimoto, a chiropractor from Sendai, made a strong impression on Fukuoka. What Hashimoto does when a patient comes in is very simple: he has the patient lie down on a cot and just tugs on his legs and arms a bit. What he is after is simply to make the patient comfortable.

- "If the right arm hurts when the patient raises it, then I have him shake the left arm in the other direction"

- "I just try to make him feel good. If he can [[query-how-to-get-rid-of-slugs-and-snails-organically]] of the pain, that is fine"

- In other words, it is enough to relax

- Hashimoto says that "people ought to live pleasantly and die pleasantly"

Fukuoka found these to be wise words indeed, and they aligned perfectly with his own philosophy of [[fukuoka-natural-body-and-health]].

## Moving the Body Frees the Mind

Fukuoka had no objection to those who earnestly practice zazen on a tatami mat, but he wondered if one can be at rest by joining one's hands, crossing one's legs, and staying perfectly still. He would think that in moving one's arms and legs, one's attention would be diverted to this, emptying the mind and achieving the same result as if one were to sit quietly.
