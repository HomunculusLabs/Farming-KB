# Actively Aerated Compost Tea Basics

## Overview

Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) is the third essential tool in the soil food
web gardener's toolkit, alongside compost and mulch. Its primary purpose is to
put microbiology back into soils and onto plant surfaces, restoring the living
ecosystems that healthy plants depend on. While compost and mulch are invaluable,
AACT offers unique advantages in speed, coverage, and application flexibility.

## Why Compost Tea? The Need for a Third Tool

Compost and mulch are foundational practices, but they have inherent limitations
that AACT was developed to overcome:

- **Slow delivery to the rhizosphere:** Compost and mulch applied to the soil
  surface take considerable time for their microbial populations to migrate down
  to the root zone (rhizosphere) where they benefit plants most. AACT can be
  applied directly as a soil drench, delivering living microbes straight to the
  root zone immediately.

- **No foliar adhesion:** Neither compost nor mulch will stick to plant leaves.
  The phyllosphere (leaf surface) is an important habitat for beneficial
  microorganisms that can protect against pathogens, improve nutrient absorption,
  and support overall plant health. AACT can be sprayed directly onto foliage,
  establishing a protective microbial coating that compost and mulch simply
  cannot provide.

AACT bridges these gaps by creating a liquid suspension of living organisms that
can be applied wherever the gardener needs them — soil, roots, or leaves.

## Distinguishing AACT from Other Compost Liquids

A common source of confusion is the variety of liquid products derived from
compost or organic materials. Not all of them are created equal, and understanding
the differences is critical for soil food web gardening.

### Compost Leachate

Compost leachate is the liquid that naturally oozes or drains out of compost
piles, often collected from the bottom of bins or windrows. While it contains
some dissolved nutrients and organic compounds, it does **not** impart meaningful
microbial life. Leachate is not aerated and should not be confused with compost
tea. It is essentially compost-flavored water — beneficial in a limited way but
fundamentally different from AACT.

### Compost Extracts

Compost extracts are made by steeping compost in water without aeration. Because
there is no active oxygenation, the process is essentially anaerobic. The
consensus among soil food web experts is that compost extracts are not worth the
effort compared to AACT — they do not support the explosive microbial growth
that active aeration provides and may harbor undesirable anaerobic organisms.

### Manure Teas

Manure teas are made by steeping animal manure in water. Like compost extracts,
they are typically anaerobic. More concerning is the **pathogenic E. coli risk**
associated with manure teas. Without active aeration, pathogenic bacteria can
thrive in the oxygen-deprived environment. Manure teas should be avoided in
favor of properly made AACT, which uses finished, aerobic compost as its
microbial source.

## How AACT Works: The Science of Aerobic Brewing

Modern compost teas are aerobic mixtures designed to multiply beneficial
microorganisms in a controlled, oxygen-rich environment. The process is
remarkably effective: during a typical brew cycle, the **bacterial population
grows from approximately 1 billion to 4 billion per teaspoon** — a fourfold
increase in living organisms.

### The Basic Process

1. **Dechlorinated water** is used as the base. Municipal tap water contains
   chlorine or chloramine that would kill the very microbes the tea aims to
   cultivate. Water can be dechlorinated by letting it sit for 24 hours,
   aerating it before adding compost, or using a chlorine filter.

2. **High-quality compost** is added, along with **nutrient sources** (such as
   molasses, kelp meal, or fish hydrolysate) that feed the growing microbial
   populations.

3. The mixture is **actively aerated** for 1 to 2 days. Continuous aeration
   maintains dissolved oxygen levels, keeping the brew aerobic and safe.
   Aerobic conditions favor beneficial bacteria and fungi while suppressing
   pathogens.

4. The finished tea is applied immediately — as a soil drench, root dip, or
   foliar spray — while the microbial populations are at their peak.

### Why Active Aeration Matters

Active aeration is the defining feature that separates AACT from inferior
compost liquids. Aerobic conditions ensure that beneficial organisms thrive and
pathogens are outcompeted. Without continuous aeration, dissolved oxygen is
rapidly consumed by the multiplying microbes, the environment shifts to
anaerobic, and the tea can become a breeding ground for harmful organisms.

## The Physics of Brewing: Extracting Microbes from Compost

An often-overlooked aspect of compost tea production is the energy required to
separate microorganisms from the compost particles they inhabit. Microbes in
compost are firmly attached to organic matter surfaces — much like dental plaque
adheres to teeth. They do not simply wash off on their own.

The air pump and diffuser in a compost tea brewer serve a dual purpose:

- **Providing oxygen** to sustain aerobic microbial growth
- **Creating physical agitation** that shears microbes loose from compost
  particles and suspends them in the liquid

The brewer must be powerful enough to tease organisms out of the compost, but
not so violent that it kills them. Finding this balance is one of the key design
considerations for any compost tea system.

## Brewer Types and Sizing

Compost tea brewers come in a wide range of sizes to suit different scales of
operation:

| Scale | Volume | Use Case |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Small | 5–20 gallons | Home gardeners, small raised beds |
| Medium | 50–100 gallons | Market gardens, small farms |
| Large/Commercial | 1,000+ gallons | Large-scale agriculture, turf management |

Regardless of size, the core principles remain the same: active aeration,
quality compost, nutrient food sources, and proper brewing duration.

## Building a DIY Compost Tea Brewer

A functional AACT brewer can be assembled from inexpensive, readily available
components. A basic 5-gallon DIY setup requires:

- **5-gallon bucket** — food-grade plastic, cleaned thoroughly
- **Aquarium air pump** — provides continuous airflow; select one rated for
  the volume of your container
- **Air stone** — diffuses air into fine bubbles for maximum oxygen transfer
- **Plastic tubing** — connects the air pump to the air stone
- **Porous bag ("compost sock")** — holds the compost during brewing; pantyhose
  in size Q works well, allowing water and microbes to pass through while
  containing the solid compost material

### Alternative Aeration Methods

A **soaker hose** can be used as an alternative to a traditional air stone. The
permeable hose material allows air to escape along its entire length, providing
distributed aeration throughout the bucket rather than concentrated at a single
point. This can improve oxygen distribution and microbial extraction.

## Critical Brewing Factors

### Temperature

Temperature plays a significant role in the success of a compost tea brew. Room
temperature (approximately 65–75°F / 18–24°C) is ideal. Extreme temperatures
can slow microbial growth or kill sensitive organisms entirely. Avoid brewing
outdoors in direct sun or freezing conditions.

### Light Exposure

**Ultraviolet (UV) light kills microbes.** Compost tea should always be brewed
and stored in opaque containers, never in clear glass or plastic that allows
light penetration. Finished tea should be applied within hours of brewing and
never left sitting in sunlight.

### Cleaning and Maintenance

Bioslime (a biofilm of residual microorganisms) builds up quickly on brewer
surfaces, tubing, and air stones after each batch. This must be cleaned
immediately to prevent contamination of future brews and to maintain system
performance. Two effective cleaning agents are:

- **Hydrogen peroxide** — kills residual organisms and breaks down biofilm
- **Baking soda** — a gentler abrasive option for scrubbing surfaces

Regular cleaning is non-negotiable for consistent, high-quality compost tea
production. Neglecting maintenance will compromise every subsequent batch.

## Summary

Actively Aerated Compost Tea is a powerful, science-based tool for restoring and
amplifying soil microbiology. It overcomes the delivery and coverage limitations
of compost and mulch, enables foliar application of beneficial organisms, and
produces dramatically higher microbial concentrations than any passive method.
With proper equipment, quality ingredients, and attention to aeration,
temperature, and cleanliness, AACT can transform soil health and plant vigor in
any garden or agricultural setting.
