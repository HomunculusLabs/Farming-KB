## Understanding Lung Cancer

Lung cancer was historically rare until the industrial revolution, the introduction of cigarettes, increasing air pollution (exhaust, asbestos, coal dust, soot), and cumulative ionizing radiation exposure. Lung cancer is now the number-one cancer and leading fatal cancer worldwide. Some types are highly aggressive and resistant to conventional treatments.

### Types of Lung Cancer
- **Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)**: Accounts for ~85% of cases; includes adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and large cell carcinoma
- **Small cell lung cancer (SCLC)**: ~15% of cases; strongly associated with smoking; highly aggressive

### Risk Factors
- Tobacco smoking (primary cause)
- Second-hand smoke exposure
- Asbestos, radon, arsenic exposure
- Air pollution
- Family history
- Prior radiation therapy

## Cannabis and Lung Cancer

### THC-Induced Apoptosis

A Harvard University study (2007) found that THC induces apoptosis (programmed cell death) in lung cancer cells. The study demonstrated that THC could trigger cancer cell death through cannabinoid receptor-mediated pathways.

### CBD Anti-Invasive and Anti-Metastatic Effects

A 2014 Italian study from the University of Naples investigated CBD effects on lung cancer. CBD demonstrated:
- Anti-invasive properties
- Anti-metastatic activity
- Inhibition of lung cancer cell migration and invasion

### Anti-Angiogenic Effects

Research has demonstrated that cannabinoids can inhibit tumor angiogenesis (formation of new blood vessels that feed tumors), effectively starving cancer cells of their blood supply. This mechanism applies broadly to cancers including lung cancer.

### WIN55,212-2 Anti-Tumor Activity

Additional studies using synthetic cannabinoid WIN55,212-2 have shown anti-tumor activity against lung cancer cell lines, supporting the broader finding that cannabinoid receptor activation produces anti-cancer effects.

## The Smoking Paradox

A significant paradox exists: cannabis smoke contains many of the same carcinogenic compounds as tobacco smoke, yet epidemiological studies have NOT consistently linked cannabis smoking to increased lung cancer risk. Possible explanations:
- THC's anti-cancer properties may counteract carcinogenic effects of smoke
- Cannabis smokers typically consume far less plant material than tobacco smokers
- Different inhalation patterns (deeper but fewer puffs)
- Different combustion temperatures

However, vaporization or non-smoked delivery methods are recommended to avoid any potential respiratory risks while maintaining therapeutic benefits.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Both THC (apoptosis induction, CB1/CB2 activation) and CBD (anti-invasive, anti-metastatic, CB2-preferring) show anti-lung cancer activity. A balanced approach using strains with both cannabinoids may provide the broadest anti-cancer coverage:
- **Sativa strains**: Higher THC for CB1-mediated apoptosis
- **Indica strains**: Higher CBD for anti-invasive/anti-metastatic effects via CB2
- **Hybrid strains**: Balanced THC:CBD for combined mechanisms

## Administration Considerations

- **Vaporization**: Preferred delivery method; avoids combustion byproducts while delivering cannabinoids
- **Oral preparations**: Longer duration; avoid respiratory exposure entirely
- **Sublingual sprays**: Rapid absorption without pulmonary exposure
- **Avoid smoking**: Despite unclear epidemiological link, prudence dictates non-combustion methods for lung cancer patients

## Merged: Cannabis for Cervical Cancer
Cervical cancer develops from cancerous cells at the narrow neck of the uterus (cervix uteri). The uterus, together with the fallopian tubes and ovaries, comprises the female reproductive system. A 2004 Geneva study demonstrated that anandamide, the body's own cannabinoid, can protect healthy cervical cells from developing cancer via both [[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]] and CB2 receptor sites. By 2008, the Rostock experiment confirmed anandamide's abilities, as well as that of [[cannabis-thc-therapeutic-profile]], to significantly decrease cervical tumor cell invasiveness, even at very low dosages. The [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] plays a dual protective role in cervical cancer. ## Merged: Cannabis for Pancreatic Cancer
Pancreatic cancer is one of the most malignant forms of cancer, usually with very poor outcomes. Onset symptoms may include gastrointestinal difficulties such as abdominal pain (often radiating to the back), lack of appetite, nausea and vomiting, diarrhea, weight loss, jaundice, and diabetes. That same year, scientists in Pisa, Italy, discovered a novel endocannabinoid mechanism not regulated via typical [[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]] or CB2 receptors that was able to destroy pancreatic cancer cell lines in the laboratory. The [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] attacks pancreatic cancer through at least two distinct mechanisms: First, CB2 receptor-mediated apoptosis: cannabinoid activation of CB2 receptors on pancreatic cancer cells triggers programmed cell death. This CB2-predominant mechanism means that [[cannabis-cbd-therapeutic-profile]]-rich preparations may be particularly effective.
