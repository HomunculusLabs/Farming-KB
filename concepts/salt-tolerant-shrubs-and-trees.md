# Salt-Tolerant Shrubs and Trees

Salt-tolerant plants are essential components of permaculture systems in arid, semi-arid, and coastal regions where soil salinity is a significant challenge. These plants have evolved mechanisms to survive and even thrive in saline conditions, making them invaluable for land rehabilitation, forage production, [[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]], and [[amphibian-habitat-creation]] on marginal lands.

## Salt-Tolerant Shrubs

### Maireana Species (Bluebushes)

**Maireana aphylia** (cotton bush) is a much-branched perennial shrub reaching approximately 1 m in both height and width. It prefers clay and clay loam soils, making it well-suited to the heavy textured soils commonly found in arid Australian landscapes.

**Maireana pyramidata** (black bluebush, shrubby bluebush) grows to nearly 2 m in height and is confined to calcareous red and red-brown soils. It is a significant forage species, with crude protein content ranging from 15.1% to 22.0% and a digestibility of 58%. These nutritional values make it an important browse plant for livestock in areas where few other forage options exist.

### Samphires (Halosarcia spp)

Samphires are native to the waterlogged saltlands of Western Australia. These succulent, salt-adapted plants are critical components of salt marsh ecosystems. Their ability to extract and tolerate high levels of soil salinity makes them useful for understanding and managing saline landscapes [[mollison-lawn-reform-and-alternatives-in-permaculture-design]].

### Hop Bush (Dodonea viscosa)

Hop bush is remarkably drought-adapted, capable of growing in areas receiving less than 250 mm of annual rainfall. This makes it one of the most resilient shrub species for extremely arid permaculture zones. Its ability to persist where almost no other woody plants survive gives it significant value as a pioneer species and windbreak component.

## Paperbarks (Melaleuca spp) — Family: Myrtaceae

The Melaleuca genus contains many species valuable for honey production, essential oils, and land rehabilitation in saline environments. Several species demonstrate notable salt tolerance:

### Melaleuca stypheloides (Prickly Paperbark)

A fast-growing tree reaching 6–18 m in height, found in swampy coastal sites across eastern Australia. It tolerates saline, sandy, wet, and heavy soils while being both drought and frost tolerant. This broad environmental adaptability makes it an excellent multi-purpose tree for difficult sites, providing timber, honey, and habitat.

### Melaleuca thyroides

An evergreen shrub reaching 2 m tall and 2 m wide, native to Western Australia. It is frost and [[drought-tolerant-planting-design]] with good tolerance to waterlogging — a valuable combination of traits for seasonally waterlogged saline soils.

### Melaleuca bracteata (White Cloud Tree)

A small tree (to 7 m) with a spreading habit (to 5 m), native to all mainland Australian states except Victoria. It is drought and frost tolerant and prefers heavy to medium soils in open sunny positions. Its ornamental white flowers and dense canopy make it useful as a screen or windbreak.

### Other Notable Melaleuca Species

- **Melaleuca glomerata, M. nervosa, M. pauperiflora, M. subtrigona** — Western Australian species adapted to arid conditions
- **Melaleuca uncinata** (broom honey myrtle) — valued for honey production
- **Melaleuca acuminata, M. brevifolia** (mallee honey myrtle), **M. hamulosa, M. preissiana** — various Western and South Australian species
- **Melaleuca lanceolata** (moonah, Rottnest Island pine) — produces light amber honey in summer

## Wattles (Acacia spp) for Saline Soils

Acacia species are among the most important nitrogen-fixing trees for saline and alkaline environments. Many Australian wattles have evolved to thrive in harsh conditions:

- **Acacia ampliceps** — North Western Australia
- **Acacia auriculiformis** — Tropical and coastal Australia
- **Acacia cyclops** — South west coastal Australia
- **Acacia floribunda** — Central to south eastern coastal Australia
- **Acacia harpophylla** (brigalow, spearwood) — South eastern NSW to northern Queensland
- **Acacia ligulata** — All mainland states, north and south
- **Acacia pendula** — Central to south east Australia (notably salt-tolerant)
- **Acacia pycnantha** (Australian golden wattle) — South east Australia; 40% tannin in bark
- **Acacia redolens** — Western south coastal Australia
- **Acacia retinodes** — South eastern Australia
- **Acacia saligna** (golden wreath wattle) — Widely planted for saltland rehabilitation
- **Acacia salicina** (cooba) — Important fodder tree for arid saline areas
- **Acacia stenophylla** (river cooba) — From tropics to central Victoria
- **Acacia transluscens, A. xiphophylla** — North Western Australia

## Other Salt-Tolerant Trees

### Casuarina Species

Casuarinas are nitrogen-fixing trees well-adapted to saline conditions:

- **Casuarina obesa** (swamp sheoak) — Highly salt-tolerant
- **Casuarina glauca** (swamp sheoak) — Tolerates saline waterlogging
- **Casuarina cristata** (black sheoak) — Adapts to alkaline soils

### Dalbergia sissoo (Sissoo)

A fast-growing leguminous tree reaching up to 30 m, Dalbergia sissoo adapts well to semi-arid conditions (though it may only reach 10–15 m without irrigation). Key characteristics:

- Temperature range from below 0°C to nearly 50°C; considered frost hardy
- Rainfall range: 500–2,000 mm; tolerates arid conditions
- Grows from sea level to 1,500 m altitude
- Occurs naturally on alluvial soils along river banks and stream beds
- Does not grow well on heavy clay soils
- Produces long superficial roots that send up suckers when damaged — excellent [[mollison-designers-earthworks-for-erosion-control]]

**Timber qualities:** The wood is hard, elastic, and close-grained. White sapwood is durable and valued for furniture, carving, bending, veneer, handles, boat building, ploughs, and structural work. It has a specific gravity of 0.64–0.7, with energy content of 5,200 kcal/kg in heartwood.

**Forage:** Young branches and leaves are browsed by deer and cattle.

### Colophospermum mopane (Mopane)

A moderately tall leguminous tree (to 23 m) native to central and southern Africa, mopane is remarkable for its ability to grow on poor, badly-drained soils where even useful grasses fail. Key attributes:

- Withstands high summer temperatures but only mild frosts; heavy frosts cause crown dieback
- Rainfall range: 125–800 mm
- Grows in shallow compacted clay, alkaline, and badly drained soils; best growth on rich deep alluvial soils
- Young plants produce root suckers; seedlings may be suppressed by grasses
- Leaves are readily eaten by stock and retain nutrient values even when fallen

**Timber:** Extremely durable wood used for bridge pylons, mine timbers, railroad ties, fence posts, parquet flooring, and carving. So hard and heavy it requires heavy saws and should be cut while green. Specific gravity greater than 1.0.

**Firewood:** Considered the best firewood in Africa — dark wood burns slowly and quietly while producing great heat. Trees coppice vigorously after fire, frost, or felling.

### Athel Tamarisk (Tamarix articulata, syn. T. aphylia)

A vigorous, bushy, pine-like tree tolerating most soils in frost-free districts with a minimum of 350 mm rainfall. **Important warning:** most tamarisks cause salinisation of upper soil. Their extensive roots extract all soluble salts, excrete them on leaves, and return them to topsoil via litter fall. They reduce crop yields for up to 50 m around each tree. This makes them problematic in agricultural permaculture designs despite their apparent salt tolerance.

## Permaculture Design Applications

Salt-tolerant shrubs and trees serve multiple functions in permaculture systems:

1. **Pioneer species** — Establishing vegetation cover on degraded saline land
2. **Windbreaks** — Providing shelter in exposed coastal and arid environments
3. **Forage** — Supplying protein-rich browse for livestock (Maireana, Acacia, Dalbergia)
4. **[[biological-nitrogen-fixation-agriculture]]** — Acacia, Casuarina, Colophospermum, and Dalbergia fix atmospheric nitrogen
5. **Erosion control** — Deep and sucker-producing root systems stabilize soils
6. **Honey production** — Melaleuca and several Acacia species are excellent bee forage
7. **Timber and fuel** — Dalbergia sissoo and mopane provide high-quality timber and firewood
8. **Habitat** — Providing shelter and food for birds and wildlife in harsh landscapes

When designing with salt-tolerant species, it is important to match plant selection to specific soil conditions (saline vs. alkaline vs. waterlogged), rainfall zones, and intended functions. The tamarisk warning illustrates the importance of understanding the full ecological impact of any introduced species.
