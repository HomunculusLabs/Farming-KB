# Microbial Terroir and Crop-Specificity

Microbial terroir refers to the concept that the specific microbial communities
associated with a crop plant contribute to its localized characteristics in
ways analogous to the contribution of soil, climate, and topography to the
flavor and quality of wine. Just as the French concept of terroir holds that
the environmental conditions of a particular vineyard impart unique qualities
to its grapes, microbial terroir holds that the microbial ecosystems
colonizing a crop plant, particularly within the root zone and plant tissues,
shape its chemical profile and biological properties in cultivar-specific
ways. Research on the Cannabis microbiome has provided some of the strongest
evidence for this concept in a non-grape crop.

## Origins of the Terroir Concept in Microbiology

The application of the terroir concept to microbial communities originated
in wine microbiology. Studies demonstrated that the microbial communities
associated with wine grapes are conditioned by cultivar, vintage, and
climate, and that these microbial communities in turn influence the chemical
composition and sensory qualities of the finished wine. Volatile metabolites
produced by grape-associated microorganisms were shown to contribute
measurably to the aroma and flavor profiles of different wines, providing a
biological mechanism for effects that had previously been attributed solely
to soil chemistry and climate.

Both endophytes, which colonize plant tissues, and epiphytes, which colonize
plant surfaces, can contribute to localized terroir. The research on Cannabis
suggested that endophytes within the endorhiza may play a particularly
important role because their community composition is more strongly shaped by
host cultivar than by soil type, meaning that the microbial contribution to
terroir may be partially independent of the edaphic contribution.

environment and the genetic identity of the plant, analogous to the way
wine reflects both the vineyard site and the grape variety.
