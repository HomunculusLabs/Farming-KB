## Third Bardo — The Period of Re-Entry (Sidpa Bardo)

## Overview

The Third Bardo, called the *[[sidpa-bardo]]* in the Tibetan tradition, is the final phase of the psychedelic experience as described in *The Tibetan Book of the Dead* (as interpreted by Leary, Metzner, and Alpert). It begins when the voyager, having failed to maintain recognition of the peaceful and wrathful visions as projections of their own mind, descends into the struggle to regain routine reality and ego-functioning. The Tibetans call it the Bardo of "seeking rebirth" — the transitional period in which consciousness makes the journey from transcendent reality back to the reality of ordinary waking life.

## Entry into the Third Bardo

If, during the Second Bardo, the voyager becomes attracted to or frightened by any of the archetypal visions — the peaceful deities, the knowledge-holding figures, or the wrathful nightmares — they will lose recognition that these are self-generated thought-forms. This failure propels them into the Third Bardo. The key transition is the collapse of the understanding that *all visions are one"s own mind*, replaced by the conviction that the hallucinated content is external, real, and threatening.

The Tibetans estimate that approximately 50% of the entire psychedelic experience is spent in the Third Bardo by most normal people. In some cases, an unprepared or frightened person may skip the first two Bardos entirely and move straight to re-entry, spending the entire session struggling with ego-restoration.

## Key Instructions

The two cardinal instructions for navigating the Third Bardo are:

1. **Do nothing** — stay calm, passive, and relaxed, no matter what happens.
2. **Recognize where you are** — identify the experience as Third Bardo re-entry rather than reality.

Fear-driven premature re-entry produces unfavorable results. Only through recognition can the voyager maintain the calm, passive concentration necessary for a favorable return. Multiple recognition points are provided throughout the Bardo teachings, so that failure at one point does not preclude liberation at another.

## Seven Recognizing Features of the Third Bardo

The manual identifies seven characteristic signs that mark entry into the Third Bardo:

### 1. Feelings of Supernormal Power
The voyager may feel they possess supernormal powers of perception and movement — the ability to perform miracles, extraordinary feats of bodily control, clairvoyance, telepathy, or ESP. The Tibetan book attributes paranormal faculties to Bardo consciousness, explaining this as the result of consciousness encompassing both past and future elements. The manual warns: do not be fascinated by these heightened powers, and do not attempt to exercise them. Advanced yogic teachers consistently instruct disciples not to pursue psychic powers for their own sake, as they become a serious impediment to higher spiritual development until the selfish, game-involved nature of the person is fully mastered.

### 2. Experiences of Panic, Torture, and Persecution
These are distinguished from the wrathful visions of the Second Bardo in that they definitively involve the person's own "skin-encapsulated ego." Mind-controlling manipulative figures, demons, and hideous entities may be hallucinated. Where Tibetans saw demons and beasts of prey, Westerners may experience impersonal machinery grinding, depersonalizing control devices, world-destruction scenarios, dying in space-fiction modes, or engulfment by destructive powers. Sounds of mind-controlling apparatus, gears moving puppet-show scenery, angry overflowing seas, roaring fire, fierce winds, and mocking laughter may all arise.

The first impulse is to flee in panic. The voyager may plead for antidotes or tranquilizers. These visions symbolize the "evil passions" that enslave mankind to game-networks: anger, lust, stupidity, pride, jealousy, and control-power.

### 3. Restless, Unhappy Wandering
The person feels driven by "winds of karma" or shunted around mechanically. Like a traveller alone at night on a highway, attention is arrested by prominent landmarks — isolated trees, houses, bridgeheads, temples, hot-dog stands. There may be a desperate desire to phone family, a doctor, or friends and appeal to them to "pull you out." This desire should be resisted. The guide and fellow voyagers are the best source of assistance. One should not try to involve outsiders in one"s hallucinatory world.

### 4. Feelings of Stupidity and Inadequacy
The voyager may feel stupid and full of incoherent thoughts, while everyone else seems perfectly knowing and wise. This leads to feelings of guilt and inadequacy, and in extreme form, to the [[judgment-vision]]. This feeling is merely the natural result of the limited perspective under which consciousness operates in the Third Bardo. Calm, relaxed acceptance and trust enable liberation at this point.

### 5. The Feeling of Being Dead
The voyager may suddenly awake from a trance-like swoon and experience themselves and others as lifeless robots performing wooden, meaningless gestures. They may feel they will never come back and lament their miserable state. Such fantasies are recognized as the ego's attempts to regain control — in the true state of ego-death (First or Second Bardo), such complaints are never uttered.

### 6. Feelings of Oppression and Confinement
One may feel oppressed, crushed, or squeezed into cracks and crevices amid rocks and boulders. A metallic net or cage may seem to encompass the person. This symbolizes the attempt to prematurely enter an ego-robot that is unfitting or unequipped to deal with expanded consciousness. The solution is to relax the panicky desire to regain an ego.

### 7. Grey Twilight Light
A grey, twilight-like light may suffuse everything, in marked contrast to the brilliantly radiating lights and colors of the earlier stages. Objects are dully colored, shabby, and angular rather than shining, glowing, and vibrating.

## The Descent Process

Normally the person descends step by step into lower (more constricted) states of consciousness. Each step downward may be preceded by a swooning into unconsciousness. Occasionally the descent is sudden, jarring the person back to a reality that seems dull, static, hard, angular, ugly, and puppet-like by contrast with preceding phases. These changes can induce fear and horror, leading to desperate struggles to regain familiar reality. The person may get trapped into irrational or bestial perspectives that dominate their entire consciousness.

These narrow, primitive elements stem from aspects of personal history that are usually repressed. The enlightened consciousness of the first two Bardos and the civilized elements of ordinary waking life are shelved in favor of powerful, obsessive primitive impulses — which are in fact merely faded and incoherent instinctual parts of the voyager's total personality. The extreme suggestibility of Bardo consciousness makes them seem all-powerful and overwhelming.

## The Three Purposes of Third Bardo Teachings

For average persons who undertake a psychedelic voyage, return to game reality is inevitable. The Third Bardo teachings serve three purposes:

1. **Free the voyager from Third Bardo traps** — the various paranoid, guilty, or desperate states that can capture consciousness.
2. **Prolong the session** — maximizing the degree of illumination before returning.
3. **Select a favorable re-entry** — returning to a wiser and more peaceful post-session personality.

## The Importance of Re-Entry Quality

The type of re-entry profoundly colors the person's subsequent attitudes and feelings about themselves and the world, for weeks or even months afterwards. A session that has been predominantly negative and fearful can still be turned to great advantage, provided the re-entry is positive and highly conscious. Conversely, a happy and revelatory experience can be made valueless by a fearful or negative re-entry.

## Relationship to Tibetan Buddhist Cosmology

In the original [[bardo-thodol]], the aim of the teachings is "liberation" — release from the cycle of birth and death. Interpreted esoterically, this means remaining at the stage of perfect illumination without returning to social game reality. Only persons of extremely advanced spiritual development can accomplish this through the Transference Principle at the moment of ego-death.

The Third Bardo corresponds to the *Sidpa Bardo*, the Bardo of seeking rebirth, where the consciousness-stream is drawn toward a new embodiment. The manual's reinterpretation maps this onto the psychedelic voyager's struggle to reconstruct their ego and re-enter consensus reality after the dissolution of selfhood experienced in the earlier Bardos.

## The Role of the Guide

At this stage in a psychedelic session, the voyager is usually capable of telling the guide verbally what they are experiencing, so that appropriate instruction texts can be read. A wise guide can often sense the precise nature of the ego's struggle without words. The voyager will typically not experience all of the described Third Bardo states, but only one or some of them — or the return to reality may take completely new and unusual turns, in which case the general Third Bardo instructions should be emphasized.
