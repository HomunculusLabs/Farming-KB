## Chemical Structure and Tryptophan Derivation

All tryptamine hallucinogens share a common structural lineage built upon an indole
ring — a fused bicyclic system of benzene joined to pyrrole — with an ethylamine
side chain. This indole-ethylamine skeleton is directly descended from tryptophan.
The fact that these powerful consciousness-altering compounds share their core
structure with a molecule essential to human metabolism is key to understanding
their profound affinity for the brain. Serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) is itself a
tryptamine derivative and a primary neurotransmitter in the central nervous system.
The structural similarity allows tryptamine hallucinogens to interact with serotonin
receptors, particularly 5-HT2A, strongly implicated in hallucinogenic effects.
McKenna noted that psilocybin is 4-phosphoraloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, differing
from serotonin by the placement of a single substituent group on the indole ring.

## Endogenous Presence

Perhaps the most philosophically significant property of DMT is its status as an
endogenous hallucinogen. DMT has been detected in the human brain, spinal cord, and
other tissues. McKenna regarded this as evidence that the psychedelic state is not
an aberration imposed by external chemistry but a latent capacity inherent in the
human nervous system. The brain manufactures this compound, suggesting that visionary
states have a legitimate biological function yet to be fully characterized.

If the human brain synthesizes its own psychedelic compound, then the visionary
states produced by exogenous DMT may represent the activation of a normally dormant
neurological system — a system whose function has been largely occluded in ordinary
waking consciousness. McKenna suggested that understanding endogenous DMT could be
central to understanding consciousness itself, raising the possibility that
consciousness involves dimensions of experience accessible only through specific
neurochemical states that the body is equipped to produce.

## Pharmacokinetics and Routes of Administration

DMT can be administered through several routes, each producing distinct profiles.
When smoked or vaporized, it bypasses first-pass hepatic metabolism and reaches the
brain within seconds. When taken orally, DMT is normally rendered inactive by
monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes — circumvented in the Amazonian brew ayahuasca by
co-administration of beta-carboline alkaloids functioning as MAO inhibitors.
