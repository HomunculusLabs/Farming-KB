## The First Form: Extraction of Toxins

### Sorcerous Implants
The first form of shamanic healing involves the extraction of a toxin that may
have been implanted by means of sorcery. In many indigenous shamanic traditions,
illness is understood not merely as a biological malfunction but as the result
of a spiritual intrusion—a foreign object or energy that has been introduced
into the patient"s body or energy field by a sorcerer, by malevolent spirits,
or through exposure to polluted or dangerous places. The shaman, using
entheogenic plants or other methods of entering [[metzner-metaphysical-realms-nonordinary-reality-shamanic-cosmology]], perceives
the location and nature of the intrusion and performs an extraction to remove
it.

### Shamanic Sucking Methods
In traditional Amazonian ceremonies with ayahuasca, shamans sometimes employ
"sucking" methods of extracting toxic psychic residues or sorcerous implants.
The shaman physically sucks at the site of the intrusion, drawing out the
offending object or energy. This practice is documented across numerous
Amazonian indigenous and mestizo traditions and is considered one of the most
direct and dramatic forms of shamanic healing. The shaman may then show the
extracted object to the patient and community, providing tangible evidence of
the healing that has taken place.

### Parallels in Other Traditions
Similar extraction practices are found in shamanic traditions worldwide. In
[[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] traditions, healers may remove
"spirit arrows" or other intrusive objects. The universality of this healing
form suggests that it addresses a fundamental dimension of human experience of
illness that is not captured by purely biomedical models.

## The Second Form: Soul Retrieval

### The Concept of Soul Loss
The second form of shamanic healing involves the retrieval of a split-off
psychic fragment or "soul." In shamanic cosmology, the soul is not a unitary,
indivisible entity but can be fragmented through trauma, fright, grief, or
other overwhelming experiences. When a piece of the soul becomes detached, the
person may experience a range of symptoms including depression, dissociation,
chronic illness, addiction, or a sense of emptiness and meaninglessness. The
concept of "soul loss" as a cause of illness is found in virtually all shamanic
traditions worldwide.

### The Retrieval Journey
To perform a soul retrieval, the shaman enters a trance state—often using
entheogenic plants, drumming, or other methods—and journeys to the place where
the soul fragment is believed to reside. This may be in the "lower world" (a
realm accessed through a hole in the earth), the "upper world" (reached by
climbing a cosmic tree or mountain), or some other nonordinary landscape. The
shaman negotiates with the beings who hold the soul fragment and brings it back
to the patient, reintegrating it into the patient"s being.

### Modern Applications
Soul retrieval has been adapted into various contemporary [[metzner-psychedelic-psychotherapy-clinical-history]] tradition, called "perinatal
matrices"—experiences of ego [[shamanism-death-and-rebirth]] that occur when the psyche
opens to levels deeper than biographical material. In shamanic contexts, the
dismemberment experience is often described in vivid physical terms: the
shaman's body is torn apart by animals, boiled in a cauldron, ground to powder,
or otherwise destroyed before being reassembled.
