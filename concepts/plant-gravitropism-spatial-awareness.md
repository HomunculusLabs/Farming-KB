## Plant Gravitropism and Spatial Awareness

## Overview

Plants possess a remarkable ability to sense gravity and use it as a
reference frame for orienting their growth. This capability, known as
gravitropism, ensures that roots grow downward into the soil (positive
gravitropism) while shoots grow upward toward light (negative gravitropism).
[[phytochrome-photoreceptor-plant-vision]]
for how light sensing works alongside gravity sensing.

## Statoliths and the Root Cap Columella

The primary gravity-sensing organelles in plants are statoliths — dense,
starch-filled organelles called amyloplasts that sediment to the bottom of
specialized cells under gravity. In roots, these gravity-sensing cells are
located in the columella of the root cap, a tissue at the very tip of the
root. The columella contains several layers of cells called statocytes, each
containing numerous amyloplasts. When the root is oriented vertically, the
amyloplasts rest on the lower cell wall. When the root is tilted, gravity
causes the amyloplasts to sediment to the new "bottom" of the cell within
minutes. This repositioning is detected by the cell through pressure on
sensitive membranes (such as the endoplasmic reticulum), tethering to the
cytoskeleton, or tension gradients. The result is a directional signal that
tells the plant which way is down.

## Auxin Redistribution and the Cholodny-Went Theory

The connection between gravity sensing and growth response is mediated
by the plant hormone auxin. The Cholodny-Went theory (proposed by
Cholodny in 1927 and Went in 1928) posits that gravistimulation causes
asymmetric redistribution of auxin. In a horizontally placed root, auxin
accumulates on the lower side. In roots, high auxin inhibits cell
elongation, so the lower side elongates less, bending the root downward.
In shoots, high auxin promotes elongation, so the lower side elongates
more, bending the shoot upward. Modern molecular biology has confirmed
this model, identifying specific auxin [[photoperiodism-flowering-plants]] for other roles of auxin.
