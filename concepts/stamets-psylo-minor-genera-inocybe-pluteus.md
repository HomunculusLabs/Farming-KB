### Inocybe aeruginascens Babos

Cap 1-5 cm, conic to convex to plane with obtuse umbo. Sordid buff to
sordid ochraceous brown, often with greenish tinges. Gills adnate to
nearly free, crowded, pale grayish brown with greenish tones, bruising
greenish where injured. Stem 22-50 mm by 3-7 mm, equal to swelling at
base. Whitish to pallid, becoming bluish green from the base upwards.
Odor disagreeable, soapy smelling. Spores clay brown, smooth, ellipsoid,
7.5-10 by 4-5 um.

**Habitat**: Sandy soils including dunes, underneath Populus and Salix.
June through October. **Distribution**: Widely distributed across
temperate regions. Central Europe and western North America. **Potency**:
Weakly to moderately active. Maxima of 0.40% psilocybin, 0.52%
baeocystin, and 0.35% aeruginascin. No reports of inactive collections.

### Inocybe coelestium Kuyper

Cap 1.5-3.2 cm, conic to convex, ochraceous brown with greenish gray
tints. Gills broadly adnate, crowded. Stem 23-52 mm by 3-5 mm, solid.
Odor of Peruvian balsam. Spores brown, almond shaped, 7-9.5 by 5-6.5 um.

**Habitat**: Under beech and spruce in calcareous soils. August-October.
**Distribution**: Austria, Germany. **Potency**: Weakly active. Up to
0.035% psilocybin, 0.025% baeocystin.

### Inocybe corydalina Quelet

Cap 3.8-5.2 cm, brown to buff brown with greenish gray tones. Surface
with appressed fibrillose squamules. Gills narrowly adnate, crowded,
pale brown. Stem 24-95 mm by 5-15 mm, solid. Aromatic Peruvian balsam
scent. Spores brown, lemon to almond shaped, 7-10 by 5-6 um.

**Habitat**: Under deciduous and conifer trees in woodland soils.
August-October. **Distribution**: Europe, British Isles, North America.
**Potency**: Weakly active. Var. corydalina: up to 0.032% psilocybin.
Var. erinaceomorpha: 0.10% psilocybin. Note: one collection of var.
corydalina tested negative for psilocybin but positive for muscarine.

### Inocybe haemacta (Berkeley and Cooke) Saccardo

Cap 1.4-6.5 cm, greenish gray to dark grayish green over pale pinkish
brown. Dry surface with radially arranged darker fibrils. Gills narrowly
adnate to almost free, staining reddish. Stem 17-85 mm by 3-10 mm,
dingy white to bright pink near apex, grayish green towards base.
Strong unpleasant odor reminiscent of urine. Spores dull clay brown,
conico-cylindrical, 8-11.5 by 5-6.5 um.

**Habitat**: Clay soils, under oaks and beeches. **Distribution**:
Throughout Europe, rare in Netherlands and British Isles. **Potency**:
Weakly active. Up to 0.17% psilocybin, 0.034% baeocystin. Named for
its distinct pinkish undertones and bruising reaction.

## The Genus Pluteus

Pluteus features primarily wood-decomposing members with caps convex to
plane, gills pink and free at maturity, and a ringless stem. Most
species are small to midsized with pinkish to flesh-colored spore
deposits. Only one active species is detailed by Stamets: Pluteus
salicinus. Three others have shown activity: P. villosus, P. cyanopus,
and P. glaucus (from Brazil, with up to 0.28% psilocybin and 0.12%
psilocin).
