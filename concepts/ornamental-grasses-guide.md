# Ornamental Grasses Guide

Ornamental grasses bring movement, texture, sound, and seasonal interest to the landscape. They are low-maintenance, drought-tolerant once established, and pair well with [[perennial-vegetables]] and other garden plantings in [[food-forest-design|food forest]] edges.

## Popular Ornamental Grasses

### Cool-Season Grasses

These green up early and may go semi-dormant in summer heat:

- **Blue fescue (Festuca glauca)**: Compact, blue-gray foliage, 8-12 inches, excellent edging plant
- **Tufted hair grass (Deschampsia cespitosa)**: Fine texture, airy flower panicles, part shade tolerant, 2-3 feet
- **Feather reed grass (Calamagrostis x acutiflora 'Karl Foerster')**: Upright habit, early bloom, clay tolerant, 4-5 feet

### Warm-Season Grasses

These emerge later in spring but thrive in summer heat:

- **Miscanthus sinensis**: Large specimen grass, 5-8 feet, plumes in late summer/fall, many cultivars
- **Switchgrass (Panicum virgatum)**: Native prairie grass, 3-6 feet, strong vertical habit, excellent for [[windbreak-and-shelterbelt-design]]
- **Little bluestem (Schizachyrium scoparium)**: Native, blue-green summer foliage turns copper-red in fall, 2-4 feet
- **Indian grass (Sorghastrum nutans)**: Native, golden plumes, 4-6 feet, deep root system
- **Muhly grass (Muhlenbergia capillaris)**: Pink cloud-like blooms in fall, 2-3 feet, well-drained soil

## Planting

- **Timing**: Plant in spring after frost danger or early fall (6+ weeks before ground freezes)
- **Spacing**: Follow species recommendations — generally 12-36 inches apart depending on mature size
- **Soil preparation**: Most grasses tolerate poor soil; amend heavy clay with compost for drainage
- **Planting depth**: Set crown at soil level, not buried
- **Container vs bare root**: Container plants establish faster; bare root is more economical for mass plantings

## Dividing

Most ornamental grasses benefit from division every 3-5 years to maintain vigor:

- **When**: Early spring (before new growth) or early fall
- **Method**: Dig up entire clump, cut into sections with a sharp spade or saw, each section with roots and several growing points
- **Replant** divisions immediately and water well
- **Signs it is time**: Dead center (doughnut shape), reduced flowering, declining vigor

Some large grasses (Miscanthus, pampas grass) are difficult to divide due to dense root masses — consider hiring help or using a reciprocating saw.

## Seasonal Interest

Ornamental grasses provide four-season value:

- **Spring**: Fresh green shoots emerge, adding vertical interest
- **Summer**: Full foliage, texture contrast with broadleaf perennials
- **Fall**: Many turn gold, red, or copper; plumes and seed heads appear
- **Winter**: Dried foliage and seed heads persist, catching frost and providing structure

Leave standing through winter for wildlife habitat and visual interest. Cut back in late winter before new growth begins (typically late February to early March, depending on zone).

## Design Uses

### Mass Planting

Large sweeps of a single grass species create dramatic, unified landscapes. Effective species: switchgrass, little bluestem, Indian grass. Space 12-18 inches apart for dense coverage within 2-3 years.

### Specimen and Accent

Large grasses like Miscanthus or pampas grass serve as focal points. Place at curve apexes, path ends, or against walls.

### Mixed Borders

Combine grasses with perennials for contrast:

- Fine-textured grasses with bold-foliage plants (hostas, elephant ears)
- Upright grasses with mounding perennials (coneflowers, sedums)
- Blue grasses with warm-colored flowers (black-eyed Susan, orange coneflower)

### Erosion Control

Deep-rooted native grasses (switchgrass, big bluestem, Indiangrass) stabilize slopes and banks. See [[xeriscaping-guide]] for drought-tolerant applications.

### Screens and Hedges

Tall grasses can create informal screens. Miscanthus 'Giganteus' reaches 8-12 feet and provides seasonal privacy.

## Care and Maintenance

- **Watering**: Drought tolerant once established; water deeply weekly during first season
- **Fertilizer**: Generally unnecessary; excess nitrogen causes floppy growth
- **Cutting back**: Cut to 3-6 inches above ground in late winter before new growth
- **Weeding**: Hand-pull weeds in the first two years; established grasses suppress most weeds
- **Pests**: Rarely troubled by pests or diseases; watch for rust in humid conditions
