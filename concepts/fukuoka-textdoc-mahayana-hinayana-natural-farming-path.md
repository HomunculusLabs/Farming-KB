# Mahayana and Hinayana Natural Farming Paths

[[masanobu-fukuoka]] divided natural farming into two distinct paths, borrowing
terminology from Buddhist philosophy: the Mahayana (Great Vehicle) and
Hinayana (Lesser Vehicle) ways. These are not competing schools but
represent an ideal and a practical approach to the same destination —
farming in harmony with nature. The distinction is fundamental to
understanding Fukuoka's philosophy of Mu and the epistemological
framework underlying [[natural-farming-principles]].

## Mahayana Natural Farming: The Ideal

Mahayana natural farming represents the absolute ideal — the state in
which the human spirit and human life blend completely with the natural
order. In this state, man devotes himself entirely to the service of
nature and lives freely as an integral part of the natural world,
subsisting on its bounty without having to resort to purposeful effort.

This type of farming is realized when man becomes one with nature, for it
[[fukuoka-natural-farming-transcends-time-space]] and space and reaches the zenith of understanding and
enlightenment. Fukuoka likened the relationship between man and nature in
Mahayana farming to an ideal marriage in which the partners together
realize a perfect life without asking for, giving, or receiving anything
of each other. Mahayana farming is the very embodiment of life in
accordance with nature. Those who live such a life are, in Fukuoka's
words, hermits and wise men.

Philosophically, Mahayana natural farming exists in what Fukuoka called
the "absolute world" — a realm beyond comparison, measurement, and
[[fukuoka-microbes-soil-health-discriminating-knowledge]]. It operates in the world of Mu, or nothingness,
where all laws emanate from one source and return to Mu. It cannot be
properly depicted as either large or small in terms of yields; it lies in
the world of nothingness, shown as the innermost circle at the center of
all possible farming systems.

## Hinayana Natural Farming: The Practical Path

Hinayana natural farming arises when man earnestly seeks entry to the
realm of Mahayana farming. Desirous of the true blessings and bounty of
nature, he prepares himself to receive it. This is the road leading
directly to complete enlightenment, but falls short of that perfect state.
The relationship between man and nature here is like that of a lover who
yearns after his beloved and asks for her hand, but has not yet realized
full union.

Hinayana natural farming belongs to the same relative world as scientific
farming, and so the two may be directly compared. Both are derived from
nature as verified through [[fukuoka-nature-dissection-subjectivity-non-discriminating-knowledge]]. But Hinayana farming
attempts to cast off human knowledge and action and devote itself to
making the greatest possible use of the pure forces of nature, whereas
[[fukuoka-illusions-of-scientific-farming]] adds human knowledge and action in an effort to
establish a superior way of farming. The two differ fundamentally and are
diametrically opposed in their perceptions, thinking, and the direction
of their research.

Borrowing an analogy from the art of sword fighting, Hinayana natural
farming may be likened to the one-sword school directed toward the center,
and [[fukuoka-critique-scientific-farming-energy-efficiency]] to the two-sword school directed outward. But
Mahayana natural farming is the unmoving no-sword school — comparison with
which is impossible.

## The Path to Mahayana Through Abandoned Nature

Fukuoka acknowledged that pure Mahayana natural farming is virtually
unattainable. Crops and [[permaculture-domestic-animals]] have been altered by human
intervention over millennia and are no longer things of nature. But at
least humanity can strive for Hinayana natural farming, which approaches
closest to nature.

The ultimate goal of this way of farming is to know the true spirit and
form of nature. To do this, one can start by closely examining and
learning from a laissez-faire situation — by observing nature that has
been abandoned by man. Through this observation, we can make out the true
form of nature that lies behind human modifications.
