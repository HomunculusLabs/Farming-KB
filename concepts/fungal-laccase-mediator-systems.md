# Fungal Laccase-Mediator Systems

Laccase-mediator systems (LMS) represent one of the most significant advances in fungal enzymatic bioremediation, extending the substrate range of [[fungal-pah-degradation-mechanisms]], dye decolorization, and degradation of other recalcitrant pollutants.

## Mediator Compounds

Several mediator compounds have been identified and characterized for use with fungal laccases:

### ABTS (2,2'-Azinobis-(3-ethylbenzthiazoline-6-sulfonate))

ABTS is the most widely studied laccase mediator. It acts as a cooxidant that can interact with laccase to accomplish electron transfer. ABTS is chemically oxidized in two steps via ABTS radical cation (ABTS+) and ABTS dication (ABTS2+) with respective redox potentials of 0.68 and 1.09 V.

Veratryl alcohol (VA), anisyl alcohol, and benzyl alcohol can be better oxidized by ABTS2+ than by ABTS+. The catalytic formation of ABTS2+ by laccase is not fully understood at present.

ABTS acts as a mediator in thermodynamically unfavorable reactions. Addition of ABTS stimulates the oxidation of anthracene by purified laccase isozymes of Trametes versicolor, and ABTS is essential for the oxidation of benzo[a]pyrene by these laccases.

In the presence of 2 mM ABTS at acidic pH, laccase supports the cleavage of diketonitrile. ABTS also enhances oxidation of anthracene, with degradation rates significantly increased compared to laccase alone.

### HBT (1-Hydroxybenzotriazole)

HBT is another important mediator compound. Laccase of Trametes versicolor in the presence of HBT achieved 99% degradation of anthracene, 91% degradation of benzo[a]pyrene, 92% degradation of fluorene, and 96% degradation of perylene, all within 72 hours.

HBT at 1 mM concentration reduced methoxychlor by 23%, producing identifiable metabolites. Laccase-HBT treatment achieved 28% methoxychlor reduction without the formation of metabolites. The combined MnP-Tween 80 and laccase-HBT treatment approach using white-rot fungi has shown enhanced degradation of chlorinated pesticides.

Pleurotus eryngii degrades benzo[a]pyrene only in the presence of mediators such as HBT, achieving approximately 70% degradation within 24 hours. This demonstrates that mediators can be essential for enabling laccase-mediated degradation of certain high-molecular-weight PAHs.

### Other Mediators

The reactive cosubstrates guaiacol and 2,6-dimethoxyphenol can also influence the oxidation of PAHs by laccase. Various natural and synthetic phenolic compounds can serve as mediators, though their effectiveness varies considerably.

Mediator selection depends on the target pollutant, the laccase source, and the reaction conditions. Optimal mediator concentration must be determined empirically for each system to balance enhancement of oxidation with potential mediator toxicity or cost.

## PAH Degradation by Laccase-Mediator Systems

Laccase-mediator systems have been applied to the degradation of numerous polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, a key topic in [[fungal-pah-degradation-mechanisms]]:

### Trametes versicolor LMS

Laccase of T. versicolor oxidized most of 14 PAHs in vitro when mediators were present. With HBT as mediator, the system achieved near-complete degradation of acenaphthene, acenaphthylene, anthracene, and perylene. Benzo[a]pyrene was degraded to 91% with formation of benzo[a]pyrene 1,6-, 3,6-, and 6,12-diones.

With ABTS as mediator, laccase isozyme I achieved 84% degradation of anthracene and 100% degradation of benzo[a]pyrene. Laccase isozyme II achieved 70% anthracene degradation and 90% benzo[a]pyrene degradation.

Purified laccase isozymes without mediators showed limited oxidation capacity, highlighting the essential role of mediators in extending the substrate range of laccases.

### Other Fungal LMS

Pycnoporus cinnabarinus laccase with ABTS achieved 95% degradation of benzo[a]pyrene in a bench-scale reactor within 24 hours, producing benzo[a]pyrene 1,6-, 3,6-, and 6,12-quinones in a 2:1:1 ratio.

Coriolopsis gallica laccase with ABTS achieved 100% anthracene degradation within 10 minutes in reaction tubes. With HBT alone, degradation was 80% within 9 hours. The combination of ABTS and HBT mediators showed potential for synergistic effects.

## Challenges and Future Directions

Key challenges for laccase-mediator systems include the cost of mediators, potential toxicity of mediator oxidation products, mediator stability under reaction conditions, and the need for mediator recycling or regeneration.

Research is ongoing to identify cheaper, more effective, and environmentally benign mediators. Natural mediator compounds derived from fungal metabolism or plant materials are being explored as alternatives to synthetic mediators.
