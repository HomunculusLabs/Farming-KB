# Fungal Laccase-Mediator Systems
Laccase-mediator systems (LMS) represent one of the most significant
advances in fungal enzymatic bioremediation, extending the substrate
range of [[laccase]] to include high-redox-potential pollutants such as
[[singh-fungal-biodegradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]] hydrocarbons (PAHs), dye decolorization, and
degradation of other recalcitrant pollutants.

## Mediator Compounds

Several mediator compounds have been identified and characterized for
use with fungal laccases:

### ABTS (2,2'-Azinobis-(3-ethylbenzthiazoline-6-sulfonate))

ABTS is the most widely studied [[bioremediation-laccase-mediator-systems]]. It acts as a
cooxidant that can interact with laccase to accomplish electron
transfer. ABTS is chemically oxidized in two steps via ABTS radical
cation (ABTS+) and ABTS dication (ABTS2+) with respective redox
potentials of 0.68 and 1.09 V.
Veratryl alcohol (VA), anisyl alcohol, and benzyl alcohol can be better
oxidized by ABTS2+ than by ABTS+. The catalytic formation of ABTS2+ by
laccase is not fully understood at present. ABTS acts as a mediator in
thermodynamically unfavorable reactions.
Addition of ABTS stimulates the oxidation of anthracene by purified
laccase isozymes of [[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]], and ABTS is essential for the
oxidation of benzo[a]pyrene by these laccases. In the presence of 2 mM
ABTS at acidic pH, laccase supports the cleavage of diketonitrile.
ABTS also enhances oxidation of anthracene, with degradation rates
significantly increased compared to laccase alone.

### HBT (1-Hydroxybenzotriazole)

HBT is another important mediator compound. Laccase of Trametes
versicolor in the presence of HBT achieved 99% degradation of
anthracene, 91% degradation of benzo[a]pyrene, 92% degradation of
fluorene, and 96% degradation of perylene, all within 72 hours.
HBT at 1 mM concentration reduced methoxychlor by 23%, producing
identifiable metabolites. Laccase-HBT treatment achieved 28%
methoxychlor reduction without the formation of metabolites. The
combined MnP-Tween 80 and laccase-HBT treatment approach using
[[white-rot-fungi]] has shown enhanced [[singh-fungal-degradation-of-chlorinated-compounds]]
pesticides.
Pleurotus eryngii degrades benzo[a]pyrene only in the presence of
mediators such as HBT, achieving approximately 70% degradation within
24 hours. This demonstrates that mediators can be essential for enabling
laccase-mediated degradation of certain high-molecular-weight PAHs.

## PAH Degradation by Laccase-Mediator Systems

Laccase-mediator systems have been applied to the degradation of
numerous polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons:

### Trametes versicolor LMS

Laccase of T. versicolor oxidized most of 14 PAHs in vitro when
mediators were present. With HBT as mediator, the system achieved
near-complete degradation of acenaphthene, acenaphthylene, anthracene,
and perylene. Benzo[a]pyrene was degraded to 91% with formation of
benzo[a]pyrene 1,6-, 3,6-, and 6,12-diones.
With ABTS as mediator, laccase isozyme I achieved 84% degradation of
anthracene and 100% degradation of benzo[a]pyrene. Laccase isozyme II
achieved 70% anthracene degradation and 90% benzo[a]pyrene degradation.
Purified laccase isozymes without mediators showed limited oxidation
capacity, highlighting the essential role of mediators in extending the
substrate range of laccases.

### Other Fungal LMS

[[pycnoporus-cinnabarinus]] laccase with ABTS achieved 95% degradation of
benzo[a]pyrene in a bench-scale reactor within 24 hours, producing
benzo[a]pyrene 1,6-, 3,6-, and 6,12-quinones in a 2:1:1 ratio.
[[coriolopsis-gallica]] laccase with ABTS achieved 100% anthracene
degradation within 10 minutes in reaction tubes. With HBT alone,
degradation was 80% within 9 hours. The combination of ABTS and HBT
mediators showed potential for synergistic effects.

## Laccase Bioreactor Applications

Laccase treatment of benzo[a]pyrene has been conducted in bench-scale
reactors. Immobilised laccase systems offer advantages in terms of
enzyme reuse and process stability. Membrane reactors and packed-bed
reactors with immobilised laccase on various supports (silica,
chitosan, alginate beads) have been tested for continuous pollutant
removal. See [[fungal-enzyme-systems]] for broader context on enzyme-
based remediation technologies.

## Mechanistic Basis of Mediator Action

Mediators function as electron shuttles that bridge the gap between the
laccase's redox potential and the higher redox potential required to
oxidise recalcitrant substrates. The mechanism involves three key
steps:

1. Laccase oxidises the mediator to a high-energy intermediate
2. The oxidised mediator diffuses away from the enzyme's active site
3. The oxidised mediator reacts with the target substrate, which
   laccase alone cannot oxidise, then returns to its reduced state
This shuttle mechanism is why mediators dramatically expand the
effective substrate range of laccases. Without mediators, laccase is
limited to phenolic compounds and other low-redox-potential substrates
with oxidation potentials below approximately 0.8 V. With effective
mediators like ABTS2+ (1.09 V), the accessible redox range extends
significantly, enabling oxidation of non-phenolic aromatic compounds
including many PAHs.

## Comparison of Mediator Performance

The choice of mediator significantly impacts system performance:
| Mediator | Advantages                    | Limitations                   |
|----------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| ABTS     | High redox potential (1.09 V) | Expensive, may be unstable    |
| HBT      | Broad substrate range         | Can undergo oxidative coupling |
| HBT      | Effective for PAHs            | Moderate stability            |
| Guaiacol | Natural compound, low cost    | Low redox potential           |
| Natural  | Environmentally benign       | Variable effectiveness        |
ABTS is the most effective mediator for high-redox-potential substrates
but its synthetic origin and cost limit large-scale application. HBT
offers a good balance of effectiveness and cost for PAH degradation.
Natural mediators derived from lignin degradation or fungal metabolism
represent the most promising direction for environmentally sustainable
LMS applications.

## Natural Mediators and Sustainable Alternatives

A major research focus is identifying natural compounds that can
replace synthetic mediators. Natural mediators include:

- **Syringaldehyde:** A lignin degradation product that mediates dye
  decolorization and PAH oxidation
