## Overview

The potency of [[neurotropic-fungi]] is highly dependent on specimen age and [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]], are known to degrade over time through volatility and chemical decomposition. Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (1998) documented extensive evidence for this degradation, including personal observations, [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybin-volatility-specimen-age-degradation]] comes from Guzmán's own field experience in Oaxaca, Mexico in 1958. While staying in a small closed-door room of an Indian home filled with a large collection of fresh or semi-fermented neurotropic specimens (a mixture of [[psilocybe-caerulescens]], P. cordispora, P. cubensis, P. mexicana, and P. zapotecorum), Guzmán experienced colored hallucinations despite not having consumed any fungi:

- The mushrooms were kept in sacks, collected for commercial purposes
- The air of the room was heavy with a strong, acrid aroma from the fungi
- Prolonged exposure to this atmosphere produced visual hallucinations
- Upon leaving the room to breathe fresh air, the hallucinations immediately stopped
- This demonstrates that psilocybin and/or psilocin can volatilize into the surrounding air at sufficient concentrations to produce psychoactive effects through inhalation

### Indigenous Knowledge

The Mazatec and other indigenous peoples of Mexico have long recognized the relationship between specimen age and potency. Their traditional practice is to discard dried specimens kept for more than one year, as they are considered no longer effective. This knowledge, passed down through generations, aligns precisely with modern chemical findings:

- Old dried specimens are thrown out after approximately one year
- Fresh specimens are preferred for ceremonial use
- The Indians wisely advise that aged specimens "are not good to use"

## Chemical Evidence for Degradation

### Age-Related Baeocystin Variation

Repke et al. (1977a) demonstrated that the presence of baeocystin varies significantly according to specimen age. In their studies of Psilocybe baeocystis and P. cyanescens:

- Specimens analyzed 20-60 days after collection showed no trace of indole compounds
- This dramatic loss occurred even within the relatively short timeframe of 2-3 months
- The degradation pathway likely involves oxidation and hydrolysis of the indole alkaloids

### Herbarium Specimen Analysis

Ohenoja et al. (1987) conducted a landmark study measuring psilocybin content in herbarium specimens of [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] across different collection dates. Their findings demonstrated a clear inverse relationship between specimen age and psilocybin content:

| Collection Year | Age at Analysis | Psilocybin (% dry weight) |
|---|---|---|
| 1869 | ~115 years | 0.014% |
| 1954 | ~30 years | 0.67% |
| 1976 | ~8 years | 0.84% |

This represents a roughly 60-fold decrease in psilocybin content over approximately 100 years of storage, with the most dramatic losses occurring in the earliest decades. The degradation follows an approximately exponential decay pattern, with the steepest decline in the first few decades.

### Experimental Confirmation

Guzmán conducted personal experiments confirming the loss of potency with age:
- Normal doses of hallucinogenic fungi (P. mexicana and P. caerulescens) were only slightly neurotropic or entirely inactive
- The fungi had been kept dried for almost a year
- This confirmed both the indigenous knowledge and the chemical analyses

### The Cordyceps Example

The age-related degradation also explains a historical puzzle. Hofmann (in Heim and Wasson, 1958) was unable to find any indole compounds in specimens of Cordyceps capitata and C. ophioglossoides that had been gathered by Wasson in a popular market in Mexico as sacred fungi. These specimens had been preserved for a long time before analysis, and their apparent lack of active compounds was likely due to degradation rather than an actual absence of psychoactive substances. These species remain important to the Indians of the Nevado de Toluca region of Mexico.

## Factors Affecting Degradation Rate

### Temperature

Higher temperatures accelerate the decomposition of psilocybin and psilocin:
- Storage at room temperature leads to gradual but steady loss
- Refrigerated storage slows degradation but does not prevent it entirely
- Frozen storage provides the best preservation, though some degradation still occurs over years
- Heat during drying can cause immediate significant losses if excessive

### Light Exposure

Exposure to light, particularly UV radiation, accelerates the breakdown of indole alkaloids:
- Specimens stored in dark containers retain potency longer
- Sun-drying may reduce potency compared to dark desiccation
- Herbarium specimens exposed to light in collections degrade faster

### Moisture Content

- Completely dry specimens degrade more slowly than partially moist ones
- The balance between drying and preserving fragile compounds is critical

### Oxygen Exposure

- Storage in airtight containers significantly extends potency
