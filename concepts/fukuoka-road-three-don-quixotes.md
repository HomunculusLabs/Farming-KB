## The Donkey Sketch

At the end of a panel discussion the following day, Fukuoka
drew a picture of Don Quixote's donkey. Riding on the back
of the donkey was Morrison, who he depicted as blind. A
deaf Jackson was riding backwards, while Fukuoka was hanging
onto the donkey's tail, swinging back and forth.

He asked the audience: "Here are three Don Quixotes trying
to stop that donkey from plunging wildly over the cliff by
returning to nature, but their efforts are all in vain. What
would you do?"

Then he drew President Reagan standing on the donkey's neck
and dangling a carrot in front of its nose. "What is this
carrot?" he asked. One person replied: "Money." The host
laughed and said that this would be a shame if it were to
sum up the results of the day's meeting.

## The Second Conference: UC Santa Cruz

A week later, the second conference was held at the
agricultural department of the University of California at
Santa Cruz. The campus was built on a very large site that
had once been a ranch, with a broad meadow stretching as far
as one can see. The university buildings were located in a
large virgin forest of hemlock and redwoods more than a
hundred feet high, preserved in their original state.

The hall was packed with 800 people -- half backers of the
"nature movement" gathered from many countries, and half
local people connected with the school. Fukuoka was told to
expect both supporters and opponents of scientific
agriculture in the audience.
