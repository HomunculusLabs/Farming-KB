# Cannabis Reefers and Tincture Preparation

A "reefer" is a marijuana cigarette that has been impregnated with cannabis extract. There are several methods for preparing reefers, each producing a different result in terms of potency, flavor, and burning characteristics.

## Methods of Preparation

### Method 1: Working Oil Into Material
Work the cannabis oil directly into the marijuana or tobacco to be rolled. This is the simplest method and distributes the extract throughout the material.

### Method 2: Painting the Paper
Paint the rolling paper with cannabis oil before rolling. This concentrates the extract on the outside of the joint, creating an intense initial experience.

### Method 3: Dipping in Tincture
Dip the rolled joint in tincture of cannabis and let it dry. The tincture penetrates the paper and material, providing even distribution.

### Method 4: Injection
Inject the rolled joint with cannabis tincture using a syringe and let it dry. This allows precise control over the amount and placement of the extract.

## Historical Background of Cannabis Tinctures

Cannabis tinctures have a long pharmacological history stretching back to the 19th century. Before prohibition, cannabis tinctures were a standard item in Western pharmacopeias. The Irish physician William O'Shaughnessy introduced cannabis to Western medicine in the 1830s after observing its use in India. By the 1850s, cannabis tinctures were widely prescribed for conditions including pain, muscle spasms, and insomnia. The U.S. Pharmacopeia listed cannabis tinctures from 1851 until 1942, when they were removed during the early years of prohibition.

Early pharmaceutical tinctures were typically prepared using high-proof ethanol as a solvent, extracting both the lipophilic cannabinoids and some of the water-soluble compounds. The standard pharmaceutical preparation used approximately 10 grams of cannabis tops per 100 mL of alcohol, though formulations varied between manufacturers.

## Tincture Preparation: Standard Method

Cannabis tincture is an alcohol-based extract:
1. Pulverize cannabis material — flower, trim, or both
2. Soak in alcohol (ethanol or high-proof spirits) in a sealed container
3. Shake daily for 2-4 weeks
4. Strain through filter paper or cheesecloth
5. The resulting green liquid is the tincture
6. Can be used for dipping/injecting reefers, sublingual use, or as a base for edibles

## Green Dragon Variations

The "Green Dragon" is a colloquial term for cannabis tincture. Several preparation variations exist:

- **Room temperature extraction:** The traditional method described above. Produces a full-spectrum extract but requires 2-4 weeks of patience.
- **Heat-assisted extraction:** The sealed jar of cannabis and alcohol is placed in a warm water bath (not exceeding 77°C/170°F) or a slow cooker for several hours. This dramatically reduces extraction time to as little as 1-2 hours but may degrade some terpenes and heat-sensitive cannabinoids. Some practitioners perform multiple short heat cycles of 20-30 minutes each, with cooling periods in between.
- **Decarboxylated Green Dragon:** The cannabis is first decarboxylated by heating at 105-115°C for 30-45 minutes before adding alcohol. This converts THCA to THC, producing a more potent tincture. Without decarboxylation, the tincture will contain primarily non-psychoactive THCA, which provides medical benefits but minimal intoxication.
- **Quick wash method:** A rapid 1-3 minute agitation of cannabis in cold ethanol, then immediate straining. This selectively extracts cannabinoids while leaving behind chlorophyll and waxes, producing a cleaner-tasting product. The tradeoff is somewhat lower total cannabinoid yield.

## Ethanol vs. Glycerin Extraction

The choice of solvent significantly affects the final tincture's properties:

- **Ethanol extraction:** The most efficient and traditional solvent. Ethanol dissolves a broad range of cannabinoids, terpenes, and other cannabis compounds. High-proof grain alcohol (190 proof / 95% ABV) is preferred because water-soluble impurities like chlorophyll are less soluble in pure ethanol. Ethanol tinctures are potent, fast-acting, and shelf-stable for years. The primary drawback is the alcohol content, which some users find unpleasant for sublingual administration. The alcohol can be evaporated partially after extraction to concentrate the tincture.
- **Glycerin extraction:** Vegetable glycerin produces a sweet-tasting, alcohol-free tincture that is easier on the tongue for sublingual use. However, glycerin is a much less efficient solvent — it extracts approximately 30-50% of the cannabinoids that ethanol would recover from the same material. Glycerin tinctures also have a shorter shelf life (6-12 months compared to years for ethanol). The extraction process is slower and often requires gentle heat (60-70°C) to improve solvent penetration. Glycerin tinctures are preferred for medicinal users who cannot consume alcohol, pediatric patients, and those who find ethanol too harsh.
- **MCT oil extraction:** Medium-chain triglyceride coconut oil is increasingly popular as a tincture base. MCT oil efficiently dissolves cannabinoids and terpenes, has a neutral taste, and is shelf-stable. It does not extract water-soluble compounds, producing a cleaner product. MCT oil is well-absorbed sublingually and can also be used for [[cannabis-oil-capsules-preparation]].

## Dosage Guidelines

Because tincture potency varies enormously depending on starting material and extraction efficiency, establishing correct dosage requires calibration:

- **Standard tincture strength:** A typical ethanol tincture made from 1 gram of 15-20% THC flower per 10 mL of alcohol yields approximately 15-20 mg THC per mL. This is considered a medium-strength preparation.
- **Sublingual dosage:** Begin with 0.25-0.5 mL under the tongue, held for 60-90 seconds before swallowing. Wait 30-45 minutes before redosing. The sublingual route bypasses first-pass metabolism in the liver, producing faster onset (15-30 minutes) compared to edibles.
- **Edible dosage:** When tincture is added to food or beverages, the same dose produces stronger and longer-lasting effects due to first-pass hepatic conversion of THC to 11-hydroxy-THC. Reduce sublingual doses by approximately 50% when converting to oral use, and allow 1-2 hours before redosing.
- **Titration method:** Start with a very small dose (1-2 drops) and increase gradually every 45 minutes until the desired effect is achieved. Record the effective dose for future reference.

## Sublingual vs. Topical Use

Cannabis tinctures can be administered through multiple routes:

- **Sublingual (under the tongue):** The most efficient oral method. Mucous membranes under the tongue absorb cannabinoids directly into the bloodstream. This route produces effects within 15-30 minutes and avoids the variability of gastrointestinal absorption. Peak blood levels are reached in about 1-2 hours. The bioavailability of sublingual THC is estimated at 10-35%, compared to 4-12% for oral ingestion.
- **Oral ingestion:** Adding tincture to food, tea, or capsules. Slower onset (45-120 minutes) but longer duration (4-8 hours). More consistent dosing for chronic conditions requiring sustained relief.
- **Topical application:** Tinctures applied directly to the skin can provide localized relief for pain, inflammation, and muscle soreness without producing psychoactive effects. The cannabinoids bind to CB2 receptors in the skin and peripheral tissues. This application is particularly useful for [[cannabis-for-inflammatory-conditions]], arthritis, and neuropathic pain.

## Modern Applications

In legal cannabis markets, tinctures have experienced a renaissance as a delivery method:

- **Precision dosing:** Commercial tinctures typically include a measured dropper marked in milliliters, allowing precise dose control. This is particularly valuable for medical patients managing chronic conditions.
- **Ratio formulations:** Products are formulated with specific THC:CBD ratios (1:1, 2:1, 20:1 CBD-dominant) to target different therapeutic outcomes. High-CBD, low-THC tinctures provide anti-inflammatory and anti-anxiety benefits without significant intoxication.
- **Full-spectrum vs. isolate:** Full-spectrum tinctures retain the complete range of cannabis compounds, including minor cannabinoids and terpenes, which produce an [[blesching-cannabis-cannabinoid-terpene-entourage-effect]]. Isolate-based tinctures contain only purified cannabinoids, offering consistent dosing but without the synergistic benefits of the full plant profile.
- **Microdosing:** The precise dosing capability of tinctures has made them the preferred method for cannabis microdosing — consuming sub-perceptual doses (1-2.5 mg THC) to gain therapeutic benefits while maintaining normal cognitive function throughout the day.

## Considerations

- Dipped reefers need thorough drying before smoking (alcohol fumes are harsh)
- Oil-impregnated material may burn unevenly or too quickly
- Start with small amounts of extract — potency increases dramatically
- The quality of the extract determines the final product quality
- Tincture can be made from any stage of refined oil dissolved in alcohol

## Safety Considerations

All cannabis processing should be conducted with attention to safety protocols.
This includes proper ventilation when working with solvents, appropriate
temperature control during extraction and refinement, and careful handling
of concentrated products. Documentation of each step in the process ensures
reproducibility and helps identify any issues that may arise. Working in a
clean, organized environment reduces contamination risks and improves results.

## Quality Assessment

Evaluating the quality of cannabis products involves assessing multiple factors
including cannabinoid profile, terpene content, appearance, aroma, and
consistency. Laboratory testing provides the most accurate analysis, but
sensory evaluation also plays an important role. Understanding the relationship
between starting material quality, processing methods, and final product
characteristics helps practitioners refine their techniques over time.

## Regulatory Context

The legal status of cannabis varies significantly by jurisdiction. Practitioners
must be aware of and comply with all applicable local, state, and national
regulations regarding cultivation, processing, and distribution. This includes
licensing requirements, testing mandates, labeling standards, and packaging
regulations. Staying informed about regulatory changes is essential for anyone
working with cannabis products professionally.
