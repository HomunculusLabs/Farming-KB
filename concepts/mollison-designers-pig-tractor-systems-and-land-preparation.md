## Broader Context

Mollison notes that these intensive animal tractor systems can be a
phase followed by tree crop, an accessory to tree crop, a permanent
feature of the mixed farm, or used seasonally to remove crop wastes
and fallen fruits. Chickens, he feels, should be a permanent forage
system in all mixed orchards. The pig tractor thus represents one end
of a spectrum of animal-powered land preparation that ranges from
guinea pigs in small gardens to cattle rotations on broadacre farms.

## Design Considerations

When planning a pig tractor operation, several factors need
consideration. The ground should be ripped and limed before introducing
pigs, then sown down to good grass-legume mix with comfrey, sunroot,
and arrowroot pieces pushed into the rip lines. Trees can be planted
just outside fences and in corners protected by electric fencing.

Any fruit trees are useful, and pigs are beneficial in mature orchards.
A selection of plants for both tropics and cooler areas includes
mulberry, fig, Inga edulis, various palms, oaks, chestnuts, cattails,
arrowroot, Jerusalem artichoke, comfrey, yams, chicory, lucerne, white
clover, pigeon pea, pumpkin, and broad beans.

## Pig Shelter and Health

Various small or one large shelter sheds can be used on rearing
ranges, or dense thickets provided in the tropics. Sheds are best
thatched or insulated against cold. Strong diagonal posts wrapped in
hessian make ideal rubbing places, and the hessian can be kept
dampened with sump oil or vegetable oils with some neem (Melia
azedarach, white cedar) or pyrethrum oil added to keep the pigs free
of lice.

Restricted wallows and dust baths can be arranged by sheltering or
dampening sites, adding to pig comfort. Dust baths (a roof over a soft
dry area) kill lice and can be laced with dry neem tree leaves or
pyrethrum flowers. Rested mud wallows planted to cattails (Typha spp.)
provide excellent root and shoot foods at any time of year.

## Free-Range Benefits

Pigs on range are healthier, cheaper to feed, and have less saturated
fats than pigs kept in sheds. Good range pasture of legumes, comfrey,
chicory, and young grasses halves the necessity for protein meal and
grains. A sow in milk needs 3.5 kg of dry rations when piglets are
new, and 5.5 kg when they are 2 to 3 weeks old.
