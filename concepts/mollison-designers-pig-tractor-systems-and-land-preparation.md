# Pig Tractor Systems and Land Preparation

The pig tractor is a permaculture technique that uses confined pigs to
clear land, root out stubborn weeds, and prepare soil for planting.
Mollison presents the pig tractor as a powerful alternative to
mechanical land clearing, especially on properties from 1 to 40
hectares. It complements the [[mollison-chicken-tractor-and-animal-tractor-systems]] approach and integrates with
[[mollison-free-range-pig-systems]].

## How the Pig Tractor Works

The pig tractor follows the same basic technique as the chicken
tractor but is more suited to larger properties. Larger shrub-weeds
such as lantana, gorse, and blackberry, or deep-rooted weeds such as
convolvulus, rhizomatous grasses, and comfrey, call for a pig tractor
rather than lighter poultry systems.

The density of pigs per pen should be at the proportion of 50 per
hectare for full clearance of weeds. In practice, 0.5 to 2 hectare
plots are fenced, most economically using permanent electric fencing,
which is much cheaper than chicken mesh fencing.

## Timeline and Process

Once each pen is bare (6 to 10 weeks) and rough-ploughed by rooting
pigs, it is easy to plant lucerne, comfrey sets, sunroot (Jerusalem
artichoke), sweet potato, Inga trees, papaya, banana, and similar
crops for pig forages, and to keep up this rotation until the pigs
return to the pen.

On a large scale (20 to 40 hectares), the pig tractor system can
pioneer high-quality milk-cow pasture of chicory, dandelion, comfrey,
dock, grasses, and clover. Cows follow along 2 to 3 months behind the
pig tractor. A continuous rotation is set up, and excess milk product
(whey, skim milk) is fed to the pigs as accessory food.

## Integration with Cattle Systems

One hundred pigs in 2 hectare pens will plough 40 hectares in 18
months. They will remove gorse, blackberries, and small shrubs. They
can be followed by sowing, then cattle, then pigs again in a repeating
cycle.

Piglets ranging over such pasture rarely show iron anaemia deficiency,
parasite cycles are broken, and the soil constantly improves in humus.
Such large animals as pigs and cows need fenced tree strips, tree
guards, and border hedgerow to supply tree forage crop.

## The Hawaii Case Study

On Moloka'i, Hawaii, electric fencing restrains pigs which root out
and eat kikuyu and grasses, leaving Prosopis trees unaffected. This
allows planting to proceed and the pigs to be moved on. One hundred
pigs in 2 hectare pens will tractor 40 hectares in 2 months in the
Hawaiian Homelands area.

On Maui, a sea of kikuyu lawn was held at bay by a low barrier of
canna, lemon grass, and comfrey. Part of the lawn was converted to a
no-dig garden by covering it with mulch over newspaper and cardboard,
which obliterated the grass directly. The reverse side of the barrier
planting became an "instant garden" of mulch over newspaper laid in
the kikuyu lawn.

## Stocking Density Guidelines

When considering stocking densities, 20 pigs per 4,000 square metres
(1 acre) will plough (by scratching and rooting) the area for planting
comfrey, sunroot, lucerne, chicory, and clover. The area then needs
to rest before pigs return.

Count on at least 1 hectare per breeding sow, laid out with areas for
tree planting outside and in the corners to start with. Later, pens
can be planted out in blocks using electric fencing to keep pigs away
from young trees.

## Planning the Pig Tractor System

The whole site plan can be handled fairly casually for a few pigs on
a large range, or a clean pen will hold a family of pigs for using
surplus crop. It takes 3 to 5 years to develop a full complement of
foods on range, and even some of this must be thrown over the fence to
the pigs, as is the case for bananas and papayas.

Intensive animal tractor systems can be a phase followed by tree crop,
an accessory to tree crop, a permanent feature of the mixed farm, or
used seasonally to remove crop wastes and fallen fruits. These systems
relate to [[mollison-mixed-livestock-rotation-systems]] and [[mollison-pig-forage-species-and-range-planting-design]].

## Broader Context

Mollison notes that these intensive animal tractor systems can be a
phase followed by tree crop, an accessory to tree crop, a permanent
feature of the mixed farm, or used seasonally to remove crop wastes
and fallen fruits. Chickens, he feels, should be a permanent forage
system in all mixed orchards. The pig tractor thus represents one end
of a spectrum of animal-powered land preparation that ranges from
guinea pigs in small gardens to cattle rotations on broadacre farms.

## Design Considerations

When planning a pig tractor operation, several factors need
consideration. The ground should be ripped and limed before introducing
pigs, then sown down to good grass-legume mix with comfrey, sunroot,
and arrowroot pieces pushed into the rip lines. Trees can be planted
just outside fences and in corners protected by electric fencing.

Any fruit trees are useful, and pigs are beneficial in mature orchards.
A selection of plants for both tropics and cooler areas includes
mulberry, fig, Inga edulis, various palms, oaks, chestnuts, cattails,
arrowroot, Jerusalem artichoke, comfrey, yams, chicory, lucerne, white
clover, pigeon pea, pumpkin, and broad beans.

## Pig Shelter and Health

Various small or one large shelter sheds can be used on rearing
ranges, or dense thickets provided in the tropics. Sheds are best
thatched or insulated against cold. Strong diagonal posts wrapped in
hessian make ideal rubbing places, and the hessian can be kept
dampened with sump oil or vegetable oils with some neem (Melia
azedarach, white cedar) or pyrethrum oil added to keep the pigs free
of lice.

Restricted wallows and dust baths can be arranged by sheltering or
dampening sites, adding to pig comfort. Dust baths (a roof over a soft
dry area) kill lice and can be laced with dry neem tree leaves or
pyrethrum flowers. Rested mud wallows planted to cattails (Typha spp.)
provide excellent root and shoot foods at any time of year.

## Free-Range Benefits

Pigs on range are healthier, cheaper to feed, and have less saturated
fats than pigs kept in sheds. Good range pasture of legumes, comfrey,
chicory, and young grasses halves the necessity for protein meal and
grains. A sow in milk needs 3.5 kg of dry rations when piglets are
new, and 5.5 kg when they are 2 to 3 weeks old.
