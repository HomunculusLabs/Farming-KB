# Third Bardo: Re-Entry Visions

The Third Bardo is the period of re-entry in the psychedelic experience — the phase during which consciousness attempts to regain its ego-structure after the ego-death and visionary expansion of the First and Second Bardos. Drawing on *The Tibetan Book of the Dead* ([[bardo-thodol]]), Leary, Metzner, and Alpert map this phase as the moment when the voyager confronts the possibility of returning to ordinary existence, potentially at different levels of awareness. The quality and character of this re-entry determines the psychological and spiritual outcome of the session.

## Seven Recognizing Features of the Third Bardo

The manual identifies seven characteristic signs that one has entered the Third Bardo:

1. **Feelings of supernormal perception and power.** The voyager may experience clairvoyance, telepathy, or extraordinary feats of bodily control. The Bardo-consciousness encompasses future elements as well as past, which the text suggests may account for heightened perceptiveness. The manual warns against fascination with these powers, noting that in yogic practice they are considered impediments to higher spiritual development until the selfish game-involved nature is mastered.

2. **Panic, torture, and persecution.** Unlike the wrathful visions of the Second Bardo, these involve the person's own "skin-encapsulated ego." Mind-controlling figures, demons, or (for Westerners) impersonal grinding machinery and depersonalizing devices may be hallucinated. Visions of world destruction, engulfing destructive powers, and sounds of roaring fire, fierce winds, and mocking laughter may arise. The impulse is to flee in panic — but recognition is all that is necessary.

3. **Restless, unhappy wandering.** The person feels driven by "winds of karma" or shunted around mechanically, with brief respites at familiar landmarks. A desperate desire may arise to contact family, doctors, or friends to "pull out" of the state. This desire should be resisted; the guide and fellow voyagers provide the best assistance.

4. **Feeling stupid and full of incoherent thoughts.** Everyone else seems perfectly knowing and wise, leading to feelings of guilt and inadequacy. In extreme form this leads to the [[judgment-vision]]. This stupidity is the natural result of the limited perspective under which consciousness operates in this Bardo. Calm, relaxed acceptance enables liberation.

5. **Feeling dead, cut off from life.** The person may jolt awake from a trance-like swoon and experience self and others as lifeless robots performing meaningless gestures. Such fantasies are the ego's attempts to regain control — in true ego-death, such complaints are never uttered.

6. **Feeling oppressed or crushed.** One may feel squeezed into cracks and crevices amidst rocks, or encompassed by a metallic net or cage. This symbolizes the attempt to prematurely enter an ego-robot unfitting for expanded consciousness.

7. **Grey twilight-like light.** Everything is suffused with a dull grey light, in marked contrast to the brilliantly radiating colors of earlier stages. Objects are dully colored, shabby, and angular rather than shining and vibrating.

## The Six Worlds of Game Existence (Sangsara)

The Tibetan manual conceives of re-entry as returning to one of six levels of game existence. Two are higher than the normal human, three are lower:

### The Deva World — Saints and Sages
The highest level: enlightened beings walking the earth — Gautama Buddha, Lao Tse, Christ. Concomitant with an ever-recurring round of pleasure and free game ecstasy.

### The Asura World — Titans and Heroes
The second level: people with more-than-human power and vision, concomitant with incessant heroic warfare.

### The Human World
The third level: most normal human beings, struggling through game-networks, occasionally breaking free. Characteristic impediments include inertia, smug ignorance, and physical or psychological handicaps.

### The Animal World — Primitive Incarnations
The fourth level, characterized by helplessness and slavery. Specific animal identities include:
- **Dog and cock** — hyper-sexuality concomitant with jealousy
- **Pig** — lustful stupidity and uncleanliness
- **Ant** — industrious hoarding
- **Snake** — flashing anger
- **Ape** — rampaging primitive power
- **Wolf** — snarling isolation
- **Bird** — soaring freedom

### The Preta World — Frustrated Spirits
The fifth level: neurotics and frustrated, lifeless spirits forever pursuing unsatisfied desires, tormented by unsatisfied needs and wants.

### The Hell World — Psychosis
The sixth and lowest level: self-created hell or psychosis.

**Statistical note:** Less than one percent of ego-transcendent experiences end in sainthood or psychosis. Most persons return to the normal human level. The level one is destined for is determined by karma, and premonitory signs of the destination appear most clearly during the Third Bardo.

## The All-Determining Influence of Thought

The text emphasizes that thought itself determines the level of re-entry:

- **Attachment to material possessions** or old games will drag one down to the world of unsatisfied spirits (neurosis), even if destined for a higher level.
- **Resentment toward companions** — perceiving others as careless, inattentive, or having broken the session agreement — breeds disbelief, depression, and re-entry at an unpleasant level.
- **Love and faith** lift one upward. Even if destined for a lower level, sincere love and humble faith toward companions can raise the voyager to a higher, happier level.

The instruction is to exercise pure affection and impartial trust, recognizing that whatever others do can become a lesson. Abandon weakness, craving, and jealousy; abide in non-attachment.

## Judgment Visions

The judgment vision — the "Third Bardo blame game" — may manifest as a formal judgment scene. The Tibetan version features white pebbles (good deeds) and black pebbles (evil deeds) counted by a good genius and an evil genius. Westerners typically see the Christian version.

### Psychological Interpretation
The text offers a radical psychological reading:
- The **Lord of Death** symbolizes conscience itself — impartial and loving righteousness.
- The **Mirror of Karma** (the Christian Judgment Book) is **memory** itself.
- Different parts of the ego come forward: some offer excuses, others ascribe baser motives, still others seek pardon.
- The mirror reflects clearly; lying and subterfuge are of no avail.

Tormenting figures — the "Executive Furies of the Robot Lord of Death" — may be internal or projected onto people present. These are one's own guilt machine, one's own hallucinations. The key teaching: **personality is a collection of thought-patterns and void.** It cannot be harmed or injured:

> "Swords cannot pierce it, fire cannot burn it."

## The Tri-Kaya Doctrine

The judgment vision instructions lead into the fundamental wisdom teachings of the Bardo Thodol — the Tri-Kaya, or Three Bodies of Buddha:

| Body | Sanskrit | Nature | Manifestation |
|------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **Dharma-Kaya** | Divine Body of Truth | Primordial, formless Essential Wisdom; true experience freed from all error | Uncreated, impersonal, self-existing, indestructible; includes both Nirvana and Sangsara as polar states |
| **Sambhoga-Kaya** | Divine Body of Perfect Endowment | Reflected or Modified Wisdom | The five Dhyani Buddhas; unbearable intensity of Voidness and Brightness inseparable |
| **Nirmana-Kaya** | Divine Body of Incarnation | Practical or Incarnate Wisdom | Human Buddhas; enlightened beings reborn with full consciousness to work for the betterment of others |

Reality is "nothing but a voidness (Dharma-Kaya)" — but not the voidness of nothingness. It is a voidness before which consciousness shines more clearly and lucidly. The Tri-Kaya corresponds esoterically to one's own divinity, this manual, and one's companions.

## Sexual Visions

Sexual visions are **extremely frequent** during the Third Bardo. The voyager may see males and females copulating — internal or involving people present. Multi-person orgies may be hallucinated, provoking both desire and shame, attraction and disgust.

### Connection to the Oedipus Complex
The text notes a remarkable convergence of Eastern and Western psychology. Jung observed that Freud's theory investigates "from below, from the animal sphere of instinct" the same psychic territory that Tantric Lamaism maps as the Sidpa Bardo. The vision of parents in sexual intercourse corresponds to the psychoanalytic "primal scene," and the dynamics map directly onto the Oedipus Complex:
- Consciousness of "maleness" brings hatred of the father with jealousy/attraction toward the mother.
- Consciousness of "femaleness" brings hatred of the mother with attraction toward the father.

### The Critical Instruction
**Withhold from action or attachment.** Have faith and float gently with the stream. Attempting to join or escape the hallucinated orgy causes re-entry on an animal or neurotic level. This self-centered sexuality has little in common with the sexuality of transpersonal experiences, where physical union can express cosmic union.

### Conception and Birth Visions
Sexual visions may be followed by visions of conception (sperm uniting with ovum), intra-uterine life, and birth through the womb. Some report re-living their own physical birth, with occasional confirming evidence. Birth visions may also be symbolic — emergence from a cocoon, breaking out of a shell. The voyager should try to recognize the signs indicating the type of personality being reborn.

## Key Principles for Navigation

The overarching instruction for the Third Bardo is **neither to flee nor to pursue** any vision. Recognition of each phenomenon as a Third Bardo manifestation is itself the first step toward liberation. The voyager should:
- Allow the re-entry process to take its natural course without effort or struggle.
- Meditate calmly on the knowledge that all levels exist in the Buddha also.
- Use the instructions (passages read by the guide) to regain First Bardo transcendence if an inappropriate re-entry seems imminent.
- Trust in the unity of life and in one's companions throughout.

The suggestibility of Bardo consciousness makes primitive impulses seem all-powerful and overwhelming, but they are in fact merely faded and incoherent instinctual parts of the voyager's total personality. The more enlightened consciousness of the first two Bardos and the civilized elements of ordinary waking life are temporarily shelved in favor of these powerful, obsessive elements — which stem from aspects of personal history that are usually repressed.
