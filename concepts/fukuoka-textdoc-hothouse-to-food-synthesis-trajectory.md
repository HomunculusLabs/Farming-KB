## The Degradation Sequence

The destruction of agriculture can be traced through a series of stages,
each one further divorcing food from its natural origins. Fukuoka observed
this transition firsthand as farmers moved from open cultivation of
vegetables to hothouse horticulture.

The first stage involved growing melons and tomatoes in soil within hot
beds or vinyl houses arranged in neat rows. This was still recognizably
agriculture — plants grew in soil, under sunlight filtered through plastic.

The second stage replaced soil with sand culture and gravel culture, using
these materials because they have fewer bacteria and are thus considered
"cleaner." A fundamental shift in thinking accompanied this change: the
notion of forming rich soil was replaced with that of administering
nutrients. The growing medium became merely a physical support structure
for the plant, not a living ecosystem.

The third stage abandoned solid media entirely. Plastic or polymer netting
and containers were developed in which seeds are "planted." As these
germinate and grow, roots extend within the synthetic netting. Stems and
leaves are artificially supported within tightly sealed, completely sterile
chambers. Temperature is mechanically controlled, light exposure raised
with artificial lighting, carbon dioxide is sprayed, and oxygen is pumped
in — making plant growth several times faster than in [[natural-farming-principles|scientific farming's fundamental error]].
