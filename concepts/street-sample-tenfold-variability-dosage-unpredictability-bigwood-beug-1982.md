## Street Sample Tenfold Variability and Dosage Unpredictability

## Overview

Bigwood and Beug (1982) analyzed five street samples of dried *Psilocybe cubensis*
and found psilocybin levels varying by over a factor of ten (0.7–6.2 mg/g dry
weight), with psilocin consistently low or absent. This tenfold variability in
potency among commercially available samples represents a significant safety
concern, as users have no reliable way to predict the dosage they are consuming.
The study also found a fourfold variability among samples grown under rigidly
controlled laboratory conditions, indicating that genetic and physiological
factors contribute substantially to potency differences.

## Street Sample Analysis Results

The five street samples analyzed by Bigwood and Beug were all cultivated on
rye-grain substrate, air-dried, packaged by the ounce in sealed plastic bags,
and kept frozen until analysis. Results:

| Sample | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | 5.6 | 0 |
| 2 | 6.2 | 0 |
| 3 | 0.7 | 0.3 |
| 4 | 0.7 | 0.3 |
| 5 | 1.3 | 0.3 |

Key observations:

- Psilocybin ranged from 0.7 to 6.2 mg/g, a nearly tenfold difference.
- The two highest-potency samples (5.6 and 6.2 mg/g) contained no detectable
  psilocin, consistent with early-flush harvesting.
- The three lower-potency samples contained small amounts of psilocin (0.3 mg/g).
- No information was available about flush number or growing conditions.

## Controlled Culture Comparison

In contrast to the street samples, three minicultures grown under identical
controlled conditions showed a fourfold variation in psilocybin levels (3.2–13.3
mg/g) across flushes. This demonstrates that even with standardized genetics,
substrate, and environmental conditions, significant potency variation occurs.

The tenfold street sample variability likely reflects additional sources of
variation not present in controlled cultures:

- **Different strains**: Street samples may come from genetically distinct
  strains with different inherent potency profiles.
- **Unknown flush numbers**: Early flushes tend to have higher psilocybin
  relative to psilocin; later flushes show the opposite pattern.
- **Drying and storage**: Improper drying (heat degradation) and storage
  conditions can degrade both [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]].
- **Substrate differences**: Different substrates (compost vs. rye grain)
  produce different potency profiles.
- **Harvest timing**: Mushrooms harvested at different maturity stages
  contain different alkaloid levels.

## Dosage Implications

The practical implications of tenfold [[emcdda-psilocybin-mushroom-fresh-dried-product-forms-and-potency-variability-2006]] are significant:

- A user consuming 1 gram of Sample 1 (5.6 mg/g) ingests approximately 5.6 mg
  of psilocybin, while the same weight of Sample 3 (0.7 mg/g) delivers only
  0.7 mg—an eightfold difference in active compound consumed.
- A 10-gram fresh dose (approximately 1 gram dry) could range from a sub-threshold
  experience to a strong [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]] depending on the sample.
- This variability compounds the effects of [[set-and-setting]], making subjective
  experiences unpredictable even for experienced users.
- Users attempting to calibrate their dosage across different sources face
  essentially impossible task without analytical testing.

## Psilocin Consistency Pattern

Across both street samples and controlled cultures, psilocin levels showed a
consistent pattern:

- **Street samples**: Psilocin was either absent (early-flush samples) or
  low (0.3 mg/g). The absence of psilocin in the highest-potency samples
  suggests they came from first or second flushes.
- **Controlled cultures**: Psilocin was absent in the first one or two flushes,
  reached maximum by the fourth flush, and appeared to decline thereafter.
- **Ratio**: Psilocybin was nearly always at least twice the level of psilocin
  when both were present, consistent with psilocin being the dephosphorylated
  metabolite of psilocybin.

## Analytical Methodology

Samples were analyzed using reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography
(HPLC) with UV detection, calibrated against standards from the National Institute
on Drug Abuse (NIDA). The method provided:

- Linear relationship between concentration and peak area from 0.2 to 3 µg total
  psilocybin or psilocin.
- 10% repeatability.
- Detection limit of approximately 0.01 µg psilocybin or psilocin.
- Qualitative confirmation by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) using butanol-
  acetic acid-water (12:3:5) mobile phase.

## Broader Context

The finding of tenfold variability has been confirmed by subsequent studies using
more sophisticated analytical methods (LC-MS/MS). Modern analyses of street
samples continue to show wide variation, compounded by the presence of additional
[[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]] (baeocystin, norbaeocystin) and the increasing prevalence
 of cultivated species other than *P. cubensis*.

The unpredictability of dosage from natural mushroom material contrasts sharply
with synthetic [[psychedelic-compounds-reference]], where dosage precision is limited only by
the accuracy of weighing and the purity of the material.
