## Manganese Peroxidase (MnP)

Manganese peroxidase (EC 1.11.1.13) oxidizes Mn(II) to Mn(III) in an
H2O2-dependent reaction. The generated Mn(III) is chelated by organic
acids (oxalate, malonate, lactate) present in the fungal microenvironment:

- Chelated Mn(III) is a low-molecular-weight, diffusible oxidant that
  can penetrate soil and wood matrices beyond the hyphal surface.

- MnP degrades phenolic lignin structures and, through Mn(III)-mediated
  lipid peroxidation, can attack non-phenolic substrates indirectly.

- MnP is more widely distributed among WRF than LiP and is often the
  dominant peroxidase in environmental isolates.

- The Singh source highlights MnP as the principal enzyme responsible
  for the degradation of colored effluents and phenolic pollutants.

## Laccase

Laccase (EC 1.10.3.2) is a multicopper oxidase that uses molecular
oxygen as the electron acceptor, reducing it to water. Laccases are
the most broadly distributed lignin-modifying enzymes, found not only
in WRF but also in ascomycetes, plants, bacteria, and insects:

- Directly oxidizes phenolic substrates; oxidation of non-phenolic
  substrates requires [[fungal-laccase-and-textile-dye-decolorization]]) and
  depolymerization of aromatic pollutants.
