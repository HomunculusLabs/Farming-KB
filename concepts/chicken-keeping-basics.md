# Chicken Keeping Basics

Backyard chickens are one of the most accessible livestock for homesteaders,
providing eggs, manure, [[comparison-pheromone-based-pest-control-vs-beneficial-insect-biocontrol]], and entertainment.

## Choosing a Breed

### Egg Layers
Bred for maximum production, lighter bodied.
- **Leghorn:** 280-320 white eggs/yr, flighty. Industry standard.
- **Rhode Island Red:** 250-300 brown, hardy, can be assertive.
- **Sussex:** 250-275 brown, calm and friendly.
- **Australorp:** 250-300 brown, docile, heat and cold tolerant.

### Dual-Purpose (Meat and Eggs)
Heavier, suitable for both eggs and table.
- **Orpington:** 200-280 brown, very docile, good mothers.
- **Wyandotte:** 200-240 brown, [[coleman-cold-hardy-varieties]], beautiful plumage.
- **Brahma:** 150-200 brown, very large (10-12 lb), gentle giants.

### Bantams
Miniatures (~1/4 standard size) for small spaces. Silkie (docile, broody,
100-120 tiny eggs/yr), Cochin bantam (feathered feet, gentle).

## Coop Design

### Space Requirements
- **Inside:** Minimum 3-4 sq ft per bird
- **Outside run:** 8-10 sq ft per bird; ideally free-range access
- **Nesting boxes:** 1 per 3-4 hens, 12x12x12 in, dark quiet corner
- **Roosts:** 8-10 in per bird, 2-4 ft high, 2 in rounded bars

### Ventilation
Critical for respiratory health. Provide openings near the roofline above
roost height. Cross-ventilation is ideal. Never seal completely in winter —
moisture control matters more than heat.

### Predator Protection
- **Aerial** (hawks, owls): Cover run with hardware cloth/netting
- **Digging** (raccoons, foxes, coyotes): Bury hardware cloth 12-18 in
  around perimeter, or lay flat wire apron extending 2 ft out
- **Climbing** (raccoons, mink): Use hardware cloth (not chicken wire),
  secure latches with carabiners
- **Lock in coop at dusk every night**

## Feed and Water

- **Starter (20-24% protein):** Chicks 0-8 weeks
- **Grower (15-18% protein):** Pullets 8-18 weeks
- **Layer (16% protein):** Laying hens 18+ weeks
- **Calcium:** Free-choice oyster shells once laying begins
- **Grit:** Required for gizzard function; free-range birds find their own
- **Scratch grains:** Treat only, limit to 10% of diet
- **Water:** 1-2 cups daily per bird; clean and fresh at all times. Add ACV
  (1 tbsp/gal) weekly for gut health.

## Health Basics

- **Biosecurity:** Quarantine new birds 2 weeks; keep wild birds from feed/water
- **Parasites:** Check for mites/lice (vent, under wings); treat with DE or
  permethrin
- **Respiratory:** Good ventilation prevents most issues
- **Coccidiosis:** Wet poop, lethargy — treat with corrid or amprolium
- **Egg issues:** Soft shells (calcium), egg eating (boredom), prolapse (stress)

## Egg Production

- Hens begin laying at 18-22 weeks. Peak production first 2 years.
- Production declines ~15-20% per year after peak.
- 14-16 hours light maintains year-round laying; supplement in winter.
- Collect eggs daily. Egg color does not affect nutrition or taste.

## Permaculture Integration

### Chicken Tractors
Mobile floorless coops moved to fresh ground regularly. Each area gets 1-2
weeks of activity (manure, [[hamilton-beneficial-insects-and-biological-pest-control]], scratching). Ideal for pre-planting
beds. Size: 10 sq ft per bird minimum. Move daily for intensive, every 2-3
days for lighter systems.
