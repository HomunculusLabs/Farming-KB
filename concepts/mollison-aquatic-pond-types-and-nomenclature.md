## Diversion Ponds

Diversion ponds are filled by a diversion channel, which leads water from a
stream or run-off area such as a bare, rocky slope. Water is diverted from its
normal course into the pond, allowing the designer to place the pond in a
location that may be more useful than the natural stream course would
allow.
This type of pond is particularly valuable where the main watercourse is
located far from the point of use, or where the terrain between the water
source and the desired storage location makes a direct barrage impractical.
Diversion channels can also serve multiple purposes, acting as

where consistent water depth is important for species

## Contour Dams

Contour dams are built along a contour, with wing banks running upslope to
impound water. The contour may be concave or convex on the downhill side.
