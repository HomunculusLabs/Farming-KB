# Snow, Ice, and Winter Design Strategies in Cool Climates

## Snow as a Reflector and Heat Concentrator

Snow reflects incoming light very effectively. Fresh dry snow has
an albedo close to 100%, making it most effective at the low sun
angles of dawn, evening, and winter. This reflective property can
be harnessed deliberately in design:

- Earth structures can be sculpted so that snow covers them to
  give winter heat concentration
- Houses built in earth-formed reflectors, or that are themselves
  reflectors, can use snow for heating
- Low sun angles "bounced" off snow surfaces can add 60-70% more
  heat to walls of houses
- In summer, these same surfaces can be covered with white gravel
  or sand or clay, but in winter snow is the superior reflector

Reflected and concentrated light, even for short periods, gives
intense heat which can be stored as hot water, molten metal,
steam-heated earth, or heated salt solutions.

## Frost Heave and Spring Seeding

Frost heave on bare, part-eroded or exposed soils leaves ideal
seedbed conditions for clover and other covering seed during the
thaw period. See [[white-clover-ground-cover]] for clover uses. Only bare soils show this phenomenon, and these are
most in need of seed to stabilise soil erosion potential. Frosted
and late snow areas present an ideal surface to spread manures in
late winter, so that meltdown deposits seed and manure on the
surface of the ground for spring growth. See [[solomon-manure-types-and-nutrient-values]] for manure guidance. Sawdust in the mixture
gives a clear picture of seed or manure spread on snow.

## Snow Melt Indicators for Garden Placement

The pattern of snow-melt indicates insulated, absorbent,
reflective, and heat-generating environments or surfaces. Black
objects, soot, and leaves sink into the snow. Snow melts rapidly
in front of the bare-leaf and white-stemmed stands of birch for
about 4 m or so from their stems. Rock walls backed by birch give
an early warm site to plant out vegetables. The Chinese use slanted
bamboo and straw lean-tos to achieve this early growth of
vegetables and to extend their [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]]. See bamboo-permaculture-design for bamboo applications.

Stone walls, when embayed, form very warm early sites, as do
semicircles of tyres facing into the low sun. Such embayment can
be plastic or glass-covered to assist heat retention, or piles of
tyres can be topped with glass as miniature grow-holes, especially
if the tyres are earth-filled or half-filled with water to retain
day heat. See [[underground-greenhouse-walipini]] for
earth-sheltered growing structures.

## Ice as a Resource

Ice is, like glass and water, a transparent refracting medium
suitable for lens construction, so very cold areas can construct
very cheap ice lenses for [[fukuoka-textdoc-three-dimensional-solar-energy-methodless-method]] concentrators. Ice lenses
poured as water in moulds can be turned out cheaply, and focused
to direct heat to storages or machines during daylight hours.

Ice, frozen inside insulated boxes in winter and stored insulated
from the ground, will last for a summer in very shaded places,
forming an icebox store for summer use. Thick straw or sawdust lids
preserve underground ice for summer. Uninsulated ice pits, into
which winter snow was packed and then earthed over, were the basis
of chilled drinks in cold climates before 1830 and the advent of
mechanical or evaporative refrigerators.
