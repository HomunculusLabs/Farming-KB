## Photon-Counting Scintillation Imaging

A novel non-invasive technique, photon-counting scintillation imaging
(PCSI), has been developed to track movement of carbon-14 labelled
nitrogen compounds in foraging mycelial networks in real time. This
approach uses the non-metabolized amino-acid analogue alpha-amino-
isobutyrate (14C-AIB) to map [[mollison-designers-legume-tree-inoculation-and-nitrogen-distribution]] and transport patterns.
PCSI enables continuous imaging for extended periods exceeding six weeks,
revealing complex shifts in nitrogen distribution and transport priority
throughout the developing network.

Two complementary protocols have been developed: one correlating tracer
distribution with growth patterns, and another mapping the pulsatile
component of transport superimposed on net translocation. Automated
colony area segmentation from PCSI images using contrast-limited adaptive
histogram equalization and grey-scale thresholding has been validated
against bright-field imaging, allowing quantitative characterization of
asymmetric growth responses.
