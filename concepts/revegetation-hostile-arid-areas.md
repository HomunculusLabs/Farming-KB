# Revegetation of Hostile Arid Areas

Revegetating dry, salted, deflected, and pest-invaded areas is a fundamental challenge in [[mallee-fowl]]). These birds construct large [[brush-turkey]]** (*Alectura lathami*) compacts rain-wet vegetation in an excavated pit, maintaining mound temperature at 35°C (95°F) by adding material or opening cooling vents
- The **scrub turkey** (*Megapodius freycinet*) builds mounds up to 12 x 5 metres
- The **mallee fowl** (*Leipoa ocellata*) creates nests of 5-6 x 1-2 metres, deeply buried in sand
- These mounds function as large underground compost piles, steady at about 30°C

### The Central York Peninsula Rainforest

In the dry savannahs of Central York Peninsula, Australia, a **rainforest clump about a mile across** exists with a carbon-dated megapode nest at its centre, approximately **5,000 years old**. The rainforest is oldest near the nest and progressively younger toward the periphery, suggesting the nest provided soil nutrients for rainforest pioneers dropped by fruit pigeons during migration.

A parallel observation comes from abandoned sheep stockyards near Ernabella in Central Australia: a leaking windmill provided water to a 1-metre-deep pile of manure, resulting in a dense, tall wet sclerophyll forest structure in the desert.

## Practical Dryland Reforestation Protocol

Based on these observations, the following step-by-step protocol can re-establish self-perpetuating forests in drylands:

### Step 1: Earthworks
- Grade **wide hollows (5-10 m)** on contour from outwash (wadi) runnels, sloping into the hill
- Leave **30-100 m bare desert strips** between each swale

### Step 2: Organic Matter Placement
- At intervals of 10-20 m in the swales, deposit a **truckload of mixed chips, logs, manure, and straw or crop wastes**
- Cover with **1.0-1.5 m of sand**
- Wait for rain

### Step 3: Nursery Preparation
- Raise several thousand **pioneer legume and nurse crop trees** in large pots and containers
- Use **rich rainforest soil mixtures** or inoculants

### Step 4: Planting
- After rain, when swales are just drying out, plant pioneers into the buried manure-chip areas or around their periphery

### Step 5: Succession
- When pioneers establish, plant **second-stage forest or productive tree crop** among them (again after rain)
- If successful, **slash the pioneers** to allow rainforest to evolve

### Water Budget
The inter-swale desert run-off (assuming 8-20 cm of run-off) should supply each swale with an effective **50-300 cm of water per year**. Deep mulch holds water at tree root level, and forest soil provides the mycelial web for root systems.

## Net and Pan Systems

On steeper slopes of fold mountains where small runnels feed second and third-order streams, two modest tree-establishment systems work well:

### Net and Pan (for sheet run-off)
- **Runnels cut at gentle slope of 1:500**
- Crest: hardy needle-leaf and narrow-leaf trees (stone pine, olive, Casuarina, Callitris, Acacia, quandong)
- Midslope: deeper soils suited to citrus, fig, Acacia, pistachio
- Base: deep soils with humus suited to chestnut, mulberry, raintree, citrus

### Runnel Traps (for bare, hard soils)
- Block small runnels using logs, large stones, and bundled straw or spinifex
- Grasses trap silt; leaves and sand build small deltas of detritus
- Hardy Acacia or shrub species establish in these deltas
- Works successfully on mined-out land and lateritic soils

### Boomerang Patterns
- For larger rills, "boomerang" structures disperse and absorb flow
- Used on small, awkward, steeper, or restricted sites to catch and hold silt and water

## Recruitment: Enabling New Generations

**Recruitment** — the evolution of a new generation of plants to adult status — depends on the interaction between:

1. **Adequate rainy seasons**
2. **Fire regime** (light fires in spring can generate new trees; cutting, browsing, or fire in early winter kills young shoots)
3. **Browsing pressure** from feral or domestic animals (rabbits, hares, sheep, goats, cattle)
4. **Seed source availability**

Seeds may germinate after rains but often need a **second period of rain about 6 weeks later** to establish seedlings. To reforest desert areas effectively:

- Remove or greatly reduce browsing pressure
- Provide seed where none exists
- Reduce autumn grazing and fire (burn lightly in spring if necessary)
- Provide key nutrients (usually phosphates)
- Hope for a good rainy season or succession of rains

These conditions may only coincide every **9-20 years**, so keeping at least one-fourth of the area free of stock at any time is essential for regeneration.

## Key Principles

- **Start small**: Nuclei of established vegetation expand outward
- **Work with succession**: Pioneers prepare conditions for climax species
- **Harvest water**: Swales and earthworks maximise effective rainfall
- **Organic matter is critical**: Buried compost and mulch create the soil biology that sustains forests
- **Patience is essential**: Natural processes operate on decadal timescales
- **Protect the young**: Browsing control and fire management are non-negotiable
