# Dynamic Nutrient Accumulators

Dynamic nutrient accumulators are plants that actively draw up minerals and
nutrients from deep soil layers and concentrate them in their leaf tissue,
making those nutrients available to other plants when the accumulator biomass
is decomposed or used as mulch. This concept is central to permaculture
design and sustainable agriculture.

## Mechanism of Action

These plants develop deep or extensive root systems that access mineral
reserves beyond the reach of most common crops. Through [[mollison-designers-tropical-soils-and-cation-exchange-management]] at
root surfaces and selective uptake via [[plant-cell-membrane-transport-proteins-channels-carriers-and-pumps]] proteins, they
concentrate specific elements far above average soil concentrations. The
process is energy-intensive and varies by species and soil conditions.

Accumulators typically concentrate nitrogen, potassium, phosphorus, calcium,
magnesium, and trace minerals. The accumulation ratio — the factor by which
leaf tissue concentration exceeds surrounding soil concentration — can range
from 2x to over 100x depending on the element and species.
