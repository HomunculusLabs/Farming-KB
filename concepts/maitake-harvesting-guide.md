## Maitake Harvesting Guide

Harvesting *Grifola frondosa* (maitake) at the correct time is critical for quality, shelf life, and market value. Maitake fruiting bodies are large, fragile, and have a narrow harvest window between optimal maturity and spoilage. This guide covers the visual indicators for harvest readiness, proper harvesting technique, post-harvest handling, and expected yields.

## Visual Indicators for Timely Harvest

### Cluster Formation
- The mushroom cluster has been fully formed and increases in size.
- Fan-shaped or semicircular, irregularly shaped petals (caps + lateral stems) extend outward like a cluster flower in bloom.
- The cluster has reached approximately **80% unfolding** — petals are still growing longer and thicker with thinner pileal margins curling slightly inward.

### Color Changes
- The color of the fruiting body transitions from **dark gray-brown** (when young) to **lighter gray, grayish white, or light yellow brownish** as it matures.
- This progressive lightening during morphogenesis is a reliable maturity indicator.
- The specific final color varies by strain.

### Margin Differentiation
- Pilei (caps) **no longer have whitish or grayish-white margins** of undifferentiated new growth appearing as a band or spots.
- Once the growing edge stops producing new tissue, the cap is mature.

### Hymenial Tube Development
- At the onset of differentiation of **hymenial tubes** (fertile tubes where basidiospores are formed), minute pores appear at the **underside (back)** of the petals.
- Important distinction: pores appear on the underside of the caps but **not at the margin** of the caps (approximately 1 cm from the edge), and **not at the base of the cluster stem**.
- The appearance of pores signals that the mushroom is approaching full maturity.

### Aroma
- The mushroom cluster gives off a **clearly detectable pleasant mushroom aroma**.
- This is a practical field indicator — if you cannot smell the mushroom, it may not be fully mature.

## Negative Indicators — Harvest Before These Occur

### Petal Drooping
- **Harvest before petals droop or curve downward.** Drooping indicates the fruiting body is past its prime and beginning to deteriorate.

### Spore Release
- **Harvest before white basidiospores are released.** Spore release means the mushroom is overripe and has begun the senescence process.

### Softening and Off-Odors
- If a ripe fruiting cluster is left unattended, the fruiting body may become softened and give off a **foul fishy smell**.
- This is a clear sign of spoilage — such mushrooms should not be harvested for consumption or sale.

## Pre-Harvest Preparation

### Stop Misting
- **Stop misting with water one day before harvest** to prevent bacterial intrusion.
- Excess surface moisture during harvest creates entry points for bacterial contamination.

### Tools Needed
- A small sharp knife for cutting
- Rice paper or similar breathable wrapping material
- Refrigeration storage at 12°C

## Harvesting Technique

Maitake fruiting bodies are large but fragile, requiring careful handling:

1. **Support the cluster:** With one hand, hold the base of the cluster and support the weight of the entire fruiting body.
2. **Cut the base:** With the other hand, use a small sharp knife to cut off the thick base at the point where it meets the substrate.
3. **For soil casing cultivation:** Rotate the cluster gently and pull upward carefully to free it from the casing layer before cutting.
4. **Trim:** Cut off the chunky base and trim as necessary to remove debris (Stamets, 1993).

### Caution
- These are large but **fragile** fruiting bodies — rough handling causes breakage of the delicate caps and petals.
- The branched structure makes the cluster unwieldy; always support from below.
- Work deliberately and carefully to maintain cluster integrity for market presentation.

## Post-Harvest Handling

### Wrapping
- Wrap harvested mushrooms in **rice paper** for breathable protection.
- Rice paper allows gas exchange while preventing desiccation and physical damage.

### Storage
- Store at **12°C** in refrigeration.
- Expected shelf life: **approximately 2 weeks** under proper storage conditions (Stamets, 1993).

### Market
- Virtually all maitake produced by synthetic-log cultivation are sold at **fresh markets**.
- The delicate nature and short shelf life make fresh market the primary sales channel.

## Yield Expectations

### Typical Yields
- **Biological efficiency:** Generally low for *G. frondosa*
- **Yield range:** 1.1–2.2 kg per bag (standard production)
- **Maximum reported:** Up to 4.4 kg per bag under optimal conditions
- Yield is highly strain-dependent and influenced by substrate composition, environmental control, and grower experience.

### Factors Affecting Yield
- Strain genetics (fruiting efficiency varies enormously between strains)
- Substrate quality and formula
- Spawn quality and inoculation rate
- Environmental parameter control precision
- Timing of bag opening and harvest

## Harvest Checklist

| Indicator | Ready to Harvest | Not Ready / Overripe |
|---|---|---|
| Cluster unfolding | ~80% | <60% (too early) / drooping (too late) |
| Cap margins | No new growth band | Whitish margins present |
| Pore development | Minute pores on cap underside | No pores (immature) / open pores (overripe) |
| Color | Light gray / grayish white | Dark (immature) / yellowing (overripe) |
| Aroma | Pleasant mushroom smell | None (immature) / fishy (spoiled) |
| Spores | None released | White spores on surfaces |
| Petals | Firm, extending outward | Drooping or curling down |
