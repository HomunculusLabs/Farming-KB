# Light and UV Effects on Compost Tea Organisms

## Sun Tea Misconceptions

Sun tea is made by placing compost in water out in the sun. This
approach is sometimes recommended as a simple method for making
compost tea, but it has significant limitations. The critical issue
is not actually UV damage to the organisms but rather the lack of
active aeration and the potential for anaerobic conditions to
develop. Understanding the actual effects of light and UV on
microorganisms helps clarify what is and is not a concern.

## UV Penetration into Water

UV radiation does not penetrate water or plastic much more than a
few micrometers. In laboratory experiments testing the ability of UV
to mutate bacteria by harming DNA structure, the mutating effects
could be stopped entirely by placing a simple plastic lid over the
dish of organisms, no matter how intense the UV source. This means
that even a thin layer of water provides significant protection
against UV damage to microorganisms.

## Any Covering Protects from UV

It appears that any kind of covering protects organisms from UV
radiation. A plastic lid, a thin layer of water, or even a light
fabric cover is sufficient to block the UV wavelengths that damage
microbial DNA. However, covering does not protect from desiccation,
which is a separate and equally important concern. Organisms that
dry out on a leaf surface or in a container are just as dead as
those killed by UV, but the killing mechanism is different.

## Droplet Size and UV Protection

When compost tea is applied as a foliar spray, the droplet size
becomes critical for UV protection. If the drop size is large enough,
UV does not penetrate the drop and kill the organisms. One millimeter
appears to be the threshold drop size, although research on this
specific topic has been minimal. Drops bigger than one millimeter
have enough water mass to shield the organisms inside from UV damage
while still spreading adequately on the leaf surface.
