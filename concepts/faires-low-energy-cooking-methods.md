# Low-Energy Cooking Methods

The most ecological stove is an electric or gas modern appliance, but for self-reliance you need locally produced fuels: wood, methane, or sun. Choose based on practicality, not aesthetics.

## Cooking Method Comparison

| Method | Best Climate | Fuel Source | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-------------|-------------|------|------|
| Wood cookstove | Cold | Firewood | Renewable, local | Inefficient antiques, smoke, summer unusable |
| Gas stove | Hot | Methane | Clean, controllable | Need methane digester |
| Solar cooker | Sunny/Hot | Sunlight | Free fuel | Weather-dependent |
| Mud oven | Any | Wood | Outdoor kitchen, excellent bread | Labor to build |
| Thermal cooker | Any | Any (10 min) | Continues cooking 6+ hrs without energy | Limited capacity |

## Wood Cookstove

One end has vents and dials regulating airflow (more air = more heat). A slide unit at the back of the firebox directs smoke around the oven box.

### Operation
1. Direct slide mechanism toward chimney to create draft
2. Start fire with quick-burning wood, keep feeding until oven reaches 350°F (176°C) (~15 minutes), then add slower-burning wood
3. The whole cooktop surface is hot — warmest over firebox, coolest at front opposite
4. Must babysit the stove — temperatures fluctuate quickly

### Safety
- Keep fire extinguisher in the room
- Keep flammable materials away
- Don't burn treated lumber (plywood, railroad ties)
- Don't use liquid fire starters
- Don't throw frozen logs into hot fire (may explode)

## Solar Cooker

Any cardboard or wood box covered with shiny aluminum foil. A large reflective shield focuses light to the center where food sits. Easy and free to make. Works well in sunny areas.

## Thermal Cooker

Two pots — one for food, one creates a vacuum seal for insulation. Some have two small pots for cooking two things at once.

### Use
1. Bring food to boil/cooking temperature for ~10 minutes in the small pot
2. Place entire pot into the outer vacuum pot, shut lid
3. Continues cooking for 6+ hours without additional energy
4. Excellent paired with solar cooker (bring to temp with solar, then thermal-cook if clouds roll in)

## Mud Oven (Cob Oven)

Proportions used for centuries: door = 63% of oven height; height = 60-75% of dome diameter.

### Standard Dimensions
- Base: ~27 inches (68.6 cm) wide
- Interior height: ~17-18 inches (43-45.7 cm)
- Door height: 63% of 18 = ~11.25 inches (28.5 cm)
- Door width: ~half the inner diameter

### Construction
1. Build a stand/foundation at waist height (slip form rock foundation recommended)
2. Lay 5 inches of sand, smooth and level
3. Lay bricks on sand (no cement on them), snug and flat
4. Create sand dome mold: 18 inches tall, as wide as room allows. Cover with wet newspaper
5. Mix cob: 1 part clay + 3 parts sand on a tarp with feet. Sprinkle water. Test: drop a hard ball — should change shape but not crack
6. Sculpt cob around sand form, 4 inches thick, without pressing into sand
7. Wait 4+ hours to dry, cut door out with the 63% rule
8. Remove sand and newspaper, test with small fire — adjust door size if smoke doesn't pull up inside dome
9. Optional: brick archway with leftover mud mortar
10. Make a wooden door (used only when fire burns to coals)
11. Plaster outside with leftover mud/water mix for smoothness — don't cover with anything waterproof (oven must breathe)

### Use
1. Build fire inside, leave door off for smoke escape
2. After 2-3 hours, black soot burns off the dome interior
3. Rake out ashes and coals
4. Slide in bread, put door on
5. Test temperature: throw flour inside — should turn brown in ~15 seconds
6. Interior reaches 700°F (371°C); bakes through conduction and convection
7. Can roast, broil, steam, braise — vegetables, meats, pizza, pie, stew, casseroles, cake, cookies, beans
8. As it cools: make yogurt, dry herbs or fruit
