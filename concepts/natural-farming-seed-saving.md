# Natural Farming Seed Saving

Saving your own seeds preserves genetic diversity, adapts varieties to your local conditions, and eliminates dependence on commercial seed suppliers. After 3-5 years of selection, saved seeds become locally adapted to your climate and soil.

## Seed Types

### Self-Pollinating (easiest to save)
These rarely cross-pollinate, so you can grow multiple varieties nearby:
- Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant
- Lettuce, peas, beans
- Wheat, oats, barley

### Outcrossing (require isolation)
These cross-pollinate and need isolation distances to stay true:
- Squash, pumpkins (1/2 mile isolation)
- Corn (1/4 mile minimum, more for purity)
- Beets, chard (1/4 mile)
- Brassicas (1/4 mile)
- Carrots, onions (1/4 mile)

## Dry Seed Processing (beans, peas, lettuce, grains)
1. Allow pods/seed heads to dry fully on the plant
2. Harvest before rain or when stems are brown and brittle
3. Thresh by hand (crush pods) or by flailing in a bag
4. Winnow: pour seed from one container to another in a breeze; chaff blows away
5. Dry seeds further on a screen or paper for 1-2 weeks
6. Store in paper envelopes in a cool, dark, dry place

## Wet Seed Processing (tomatoes, cucumbers, squash)
1. Scoop seeds and gel into a jar with water
2. Ferment at room temperature for 2-4 days (tomatoes need this to break gel coating)
3. When a layer of mold forms and seeds sink, add water and stir
4. Viable seeds sink; debris and bad seeds float — pour off
5. Rinse seeds in a fine strainer
6. Spread on a plate or screen to dry (not paper — they stick)
7. Dry for 2-3 weeks, stirring daily

## Hybrid vs Open-Pollinated
- **Open-pollinated (OP)**: Seeds breed true; save these
- **F1 Hybrids**: Seeds don't breed true; offspring will segregate unpredictably
- Hybrids CAN be stabilized over generations by selecting for desired traits over 5-8 seasons
- Always save from open-pollinated varieties for reliable results

## Storage
- Seeds store best cool (40-50°F), dark, and dry (below 8% moisture)
- Glass jars with desiccant packets in the refrigerator
- Paper envelopes in a cool closet (2-5 year viability for most vegetables)
- Parsley, onion, and parsnip seeds lose viability quickly (1-2 years)
- Tomato and lettuce seeds can last 5-10 years under ideal conditions

## Germination Testing
Before planting saved seed, test viability:
1. Place 10 seeds on a damp paper towel
2. Fold and place in a plastic bag in a warm spot
3. Check in 5-10 days
4. If 7-8 sprout = 70-80% germination (plant thicker)
5. If fewer than 50% sprout = buy fresh seed or plant very thickly
