# Hanging Baskets Organic

Hanging baskets brighten up walls considerably in summer and winter,
adding vertical interest to any garden. They require a commitment to
regular watering and feeding, plus removal of dead flowers to extend
the flowering season. The secret of success is to plant them so they
create a complete ball of flowers using deep baskets that can be
planted on top and through the sides.

## Choosing and Preparing Baskets

The best baskets are made of coated wire or plastic mesh. Shallow
plastic types will not hold enough plants and cannot be planted through
the sides. Hang the basket on a special bracket long enough for it to
hang clear of the wall. Brackets must be fixed firmly, since a planted
basket can be very heavy and dangerous if it should fall.

Wire or mesh baskets must be lined before filling. Traditionally this
was done with sphagnum moss, but it is now endangered and should not
be used. Alternatives include coir mats, wool waste liners, or liners
made from a variety of other materials. Whatever you choose will
eventually be covered by the plants. Support the basket on a flower
pot while planting. Cover the base with plastic and extend it part
way up the sides to retain moisture.

## Filling and Planting Wire Baskets

Half-fill the basket with peat-free compost. Position a central bushy
plant, such as a geranium or fuchsia, and surround it with several
smaller plants like petunias. Fill with more compost, then plant
smaller plants near the edges. Allow a few trailing plants to hang
over the edge. Take trailing plants such as lobelia (Lobelia erinus
"Pendula"), bidens, and ground ivy ([[salvia-officinalis]]), parsley ([[thymus-vulgaris-entity]]) work well and are fairly temporary.

## Seasonal Planting Schemes

Spring and summer baskets use flowering plants from the ornamental
garden. In fall, when baskets start to look tired, discard their
contents onto the compost heap. Throw away old soil and lining, reline
the basket with fresh material, and replant for a winter show. In all
but the warmest areas, use frost-hardy plants for winter baskets.

For winter, and slow solutions shrubs such as variegated pieris (Pieris
japonica "Variegata") or deep pink-flowered P. j. "Christmas Cheer"
as the central plant. Surround with winter-flowering or foliage heathers
(Erica carnea). Use ivies (Hedera sp.) or evergreen honeysuckle
([[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] color. Pansies (Viola wittrockiana)
and forget-me-nots (Myosotis alpestris) or polyanthus (Primula
polyantha) and primroses (Primula vulgaris) provide additional
early color.

## Making a Wooden Hanging Basket

You can make your own wooden baskets from pieces of packing case or
similar wood. You will need 18 lengths of 1.5 x 1in (3.5 x 2.5cm)
wood 15in (40cm) long, with two 0.25in (5mm) holes in 16 of them,
1.5in (3.5cm) from either end. Also gather four 1yd (1m) lengths
of nylon string (tie a knot in one end, heat-seal the other), and
four short nails. Lay two struts parallel on the work surface, nail
two struts without holes across them at right angles to form the
base, and thread strings through holes to build up the sides.
