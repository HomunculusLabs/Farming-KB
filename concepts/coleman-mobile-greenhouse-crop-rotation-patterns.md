# Mobile Greenhouse [[jeavons-crop-rotation-system]] Patterns

Mobile greenhouses enable multi-crop and multi-season rotations that
would be impossible with static structures. By moving a greenhouse
from plot to plot, a single structure can protect successive crops
with progressively different hardiness requirements throughout the
year. This dramatically increases the productivity and versatility of
a small farm.

## General Principles

The key insight behind mobile greenhouse rotations is the progressive
hardiness of different crops. Tender summer crops like tomatoes need
greenhouse protection all season. Hardy winter crops like leeks can
survive outdoors until midwinter before needing cover. Spinach sown in
fall can be harvested until early spring without protection. By
matching greenhouse placement to each crop's vulnerability period, one
structure serves many crops across many seasons.

The same crop does not return to the same plot more than once in four
years, providing excellent rotation benefits for soil health and
disease prevention.

## Two Plots, One Move

This basic scenario involves a pair of greenhouses, each moving once
during the year from one plot to an adjoining plot. The two pairs of
plots are adjacent, and the greenhouses are diagonally offset to avoid
shading each other. Moves happen in fall, as late in the season as
possible, to extend summer crops while protecting winter crops before
cold damage.

### Example Rotation

**Summer position:**
- Plot A1: Tomatoes sheltered by greenhouse
- Plot A2: Summer onions (uncovered), followed by spinach
- Plot B1: Peas (uncovered), followed by winter carrots
- Plot B2: Greenhouse with arugula, pak choi, parsley, flowers

**Winter position (after fall move):**
- Greenhouses moved to cover A2 (spinach) and B1 (carrots)
- A1 tomatoes finished; B2 greens harvested

**Following spring:**
- A2: Prepare soil, plant tomatoes
- B1: After carrots dug, plant arugula and companions
- Continue rotating: tomatoes and spinach in B plots, carrots and
  arugula in A plots

## Three Plots, Three Moves

This rotation uses one greenhouse moving across three plots through
the year, based on the progressive hardiness of tomatoes, carrots, and
leeks.

### Sequence

1. **Mid-October:** Move greenhouse from summer tomatoes (plot 1) to
   cover fall spinach (plot 2) for late November harvest.

2. **End of November:** Move to plot 3 to protect leeks for
   midwinter harvesting. Leeks are hardy enough for outdoor
   temperatures until then.

3. **February 1:** As leeks are sold, empty beds are re-prepared and
   sown to carrots.

4. **Mid-March:** Last leeks gone, entire house planted in early
   carrots. By May 1 carrots no longer need protection.

5. **May 1:** Move house back to plot 2 for tomatoes. Soil was
   prepared in mid-April and covered with clear plastic to warm.
   Tomatoes set out around May 5.

6. **Mid-May (plot 1):** Set out new leek transplants where tomatoes
   grew the previous year.

7. **After carrots from plot 3:** Plant summer lettuce, then sow
   spinach in early September to restart the cycle.

## Four Plots, Five Moves

The most intensive rotation uses one greenhouse moving across four
plots for maximum annual production from a single 1,000-square-foot
structure.

### Detailed Timeline

| Period | Plot 1 | Plot 2 | Plot 3 | Plot 4 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Nov 30 to Mar 25 | Overwinter onions | | | |
| Mar 25 to Apr 25 | | Early carrots & beets | | |
| Apr 25 to May 25 | | | Early beans & zucchini | |
| May 25 to Jul 15 | | | | Summer tomatoes |
| Jul 15 to Nov 30 | | Fall broccoli | Cucumbers | Tomatoes |

**Full year production from one house:**
- 1,000 sq ft of onions and scallions
- 1,000 sq ft of early carrots and beets
- 1,000 sq ft of early zucchini and beans
- Earlier tomatoes
- Late-fall cucumbers
- Late lettuce

Crops are harvestable five weeks earlier and/or five weeks later than
unprotected crops, extending the sales season by two and a half months.

### Additional details

By mid-July, early carrots and beets in plot 2 are harvested and the
house returns to cover cucumber transplants for fall harvest.
Vertically trained cucumbers stay productive into October, then are
replaced by four-week-old lettuce transplants. Meanwhile, new
overwintering onions are planted in plot 3 in late August after early
zucchini removal. Onions remain uncovered until Thanksgiving, when
the house moves from plot 2 to cover them for winter. Plot 1 is
composted and fertilized in late fall, ready for spring carrots and
beets on April 1.

## Benefits Summary

Mobile greenhouse rotations are a system for generalist producers.
The spatial, temporal, and biological relationships between crops are
exploited through greenhouse mobility. Even specialist growers
producing four or five crops benefit significantly. The slight
increase in capital investment for a movable house compared to a
static house is repaid many times over through extended seasons and
increased total production.
