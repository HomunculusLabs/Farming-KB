# Harvesting and Curing Cannabis

## The Harvest Window

Growth stops at harvest and THC content cannot increase — it stays the same or decreases. Proper handling is the key to retaining potency. Prolonged light exposure, temperatures above 80°F (27°C), friction from handling, and damp humid conditions all degrade THC. The art of harvesting is timing the cut to maximize mature trichome content while minimizing degradation.

Sinsemilla flowers mature 6-12 weeks after the photoperiod changes to 12 hours. Pure indica varieties finish in 6-10 weeks. Indica/sativa crosses like Skunk #1 may take up to 10 weeks. Pure [[landrace-sativa-varieties]] (especially Thai and Asian [[cannabis-landrace-strains]]) can require four months of flowering. Commercial growers often pick immature six-week-old buds to squeeze in extra harvests per year, sacrificing peak potency.

## Reading Trichomes

Trichomes are the definitive harvest indicator. Pistil color (white to brown/red) indicates ripening but is not the best gauge across all strains. The color of resin glands (trichomes) is the most reliable metric.

Trichomes develop through three stages:

1. **Clear** — early formation, THC still developing
2. **Translucent milky white** — peak ripeness, maximum THC
3. **Amber** — degradation beginning, THC converting to CBN

**[[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]] by trichome color**:

- **50% milky** — absolute peak potency; high is both cerebral and physical
- **Majority amber** — body stone; ideal for pure indica/afghani strains
- **Clear to milky** — best for pure sativa and sativa-dominant varieties

Trichomes are stalked glandular structures: a clear column with a resin-filled ball on top. The highest concentration of THC is at the base of the resin head. Senescing glands turn brown, get smaller, and decompose. Check buds daily starting the sixth week of flowering, sampling several buds from different plants.

Cystolith trichomes are hair-like protective structures with pointed tips that exude insecticidal substances. They contain no THC and should not be confused with the stalked glandular trichomes that produce cannabinoids.

## Pre-Harvest Protocol

### Step 1: Nutrient Flush (7-10 days before)

Stop fertilization one to two weeks before harvest. Latent nutrient accumulation in foliage imparts a harsh, fertilizer-like taste. Leach the growing medium with plain water to remove residual salts. Some growers use flushing products (e.g., Final Flush) to accelerate the process and continue fertilizing until three days before harvest.

### Step 2: Residue Wash (if needed)

If sprays were applied during the final week (not recommended), mist plants heavily to wash residues from foliage. Gently jiggle buds to shake off standing water. Perform early in the day to allow drying before nightfall. If bud mold (botrytis) is a threat, do NOT wash buds.

### Step 3: Dark Period (optional)

Many growers give plants 24 hours of total darkness before harvest, reporting slightly more resinous buds afterward.

### Step 4: Morning Harvest

Harvest in the morning when THC content peaks. Cut entire plants or individual branches near the base with pruners. Do not jerk the root ball — all THC is in the foliage, not the roots.

### Step 5: Large Leaf Removal

Remove fan leaves one to two days before cutting plants, or immediately after. Removing petioles at the main stem prevents mold (dead petiole tissue attracts moisture). Early removal speeds drying and makes manicuring easier.

## Manicuring

Manicuring removes excess leaf material to expose the THC-potent bud. It is easiest when leaves are soft and supple immediately after harvest. One pound (454 g) takes 4-6 hours by hand with scissors, or 1-2 hours with an automatic trimmer.

**Technique**: Cut leaves where they attach to the stem. Snip off smaller leaves around buds that show little resin. Leave petioles attached to the main stem — leftover stubs attract moisture and mold.

**Tools**: Ergonomic scissors with small blades for reaching tight spaces. Manicure over a silkscreen or glass table to collect fallen resin glands for hash. Wear rubber gloves to collect "finger hash" — scrape with isopropyl alcohol or freeze and peel.

**Resin collection**: Scrape accumulated resin from scissors with a small knife. Ball up scraped bits by rubbing between fingers. The ball grows throughout the manicuring session.

## Drying

Drying converts THC from its non-psychoactive acidic form (THCA) to its psychoactive neutral form (THC). Fresh green marijuana is minimally potent. The process also removes 75%+ of the harvested plant mass as water vapor.

### Ideal Drying Conditions

| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|------------|
| Temperature | 65-75°F (18-24°C) |
| Humidity | 45-55% |
| Light | None (complete darkness) |
| Handling | Minimum |
| Duration | 5-7 days (longer for dense buds) |

### Critical Drying Principles

- **Slow is the key**: Slow, even drying allows pigments to break down, yielding sweet-tasting, smooth-smoking buds. Fast drying traps chlorophyll, starch, and nitrates, producing a "green" taste and harsh smoke.
- **Do not train fans directly on plants**: This causes uneven drying.
- **Do not dry in the grow room**: Different climates are required for growing and drying. Fungus and mites can migrate from dead to live plants.
- **Hanging intact plants** slows drying and protects trichomes but makes manicuring harder (dry leaves are tedious to trim).
- **Stem snap test**: When stems break rather than fold, buds are ready to cure.

### Drying Methods

- **Hanging on lines**: Use clothespins, paper clips, or hooked branch ends
- **Drying racks**: Window screen or plastic netting on wooden frames with 3-6 inch spacers between levels
- **Cardboard boxes**: Poke holes for air exchange; turn buds daily
- **Paper bags**: Good for leaf drying; fold top to close, stir twice daily

## Curing

Curing removes residual internal moisture and converts virtually all THCA to psychoactive THC. The first week of curing is when potency is most affected. Well-cured buds have an even glow when burned and smoke smooth.

### Curing Process

1. Place buds in airtight glass containers (canning jars work well)
2. Pack gently without forcing or damaging
3. Store in a cool, dry, dark place
4. Check in 2-4 hours for "sweating" — buds feel moister as internal moisture redistributes
5. If buds feel moist and stems fold: remove to paper bag, check 2-3 times daily, return to jar when dry
6. If buds feel dry and stems snap: open jar 5-10 minutes several times daily to release excess moisture
7. Continue for a few days to two weeks until buds are evenly dry
8. Seal in airtight container for long-term storage

### Storage

Store dried marijuana in dark, airtight glass containers in the refrigerator. Glass does not impart odors. An orange or lemon peel adds citrus aroma. Light (UV), heat, and friction are marijuana's biggest enemies after harvest.
