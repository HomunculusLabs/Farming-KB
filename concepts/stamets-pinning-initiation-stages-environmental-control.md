## Basic Pinning Strategy

Mushrooms fruit indoors in response to much the same conditions that trigger
fruiting in the wild. Several [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]] fruit at lower
temperatures than the optimum for mycelial growth. Usually a drop in temperature
is accompanied by an increase in humidity. Mushrooms form only when there is a
coincidence of all required factors.

The key environmental factors for pinning are water, humidity, temperature,
fresh air, CO2 levels, and light. The modification of any one beyond fruiting

# Pinhead Initiation and Fruiting Triggers

Pinhead initiation (primordia formation) is the critical transition from vegetative mycelial growth to reproductive fruitbody development in psychedelic-mushroom-set-and-setting: Less dramatic temperature shift needed

### High Humidity
- 95-100% relative humidity is required for primordia formation
- Evaporation from the casing surface creates a cool microclimate essential for pinhead initiation
- The balance between humidity and evaporation is critical — too much moisture prevents evaporation; too little desiccates the mycelium

### Fresh Air Exchange (CO2 Reduction)
- High CO2 levels during spawn running suppress fruiting
- Reducing CO2 through fresh air introduction triggers the shift to reproductive mode
- CO2 levels must be reduced to species-specific thresholds
- Physical mushroom development serves as a visual CO2 indicator: elongated stems with small caps indicate excessive CO2

### Light
- Many species require light for pinhead initiation, though requirements vary
