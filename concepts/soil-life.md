# Soil Life

Soil is not simply a substrate for growing plants. It is a living
ecosystem, teeming with billions of organisms per teaspoon. The health
of every plant depends on the health of this underground community. In
fact, soil organisms are the first recyclers, responsible for breaking
down dead plant and animal matter into forms that living plants can
absorb. Without soil life, there would be no topsoil, no fertile land,
no food.

## The Soil Food Web

The soil food web describes the community of organisms living in soil.
At the base are decomposers: bacteria and fungi that break down
organic matter. These are fed upon by larger organisms: protozoa,
nematodes, microarthropods (mites and springtails), and earthworms.
Above these are predators: centipedes, ground beetles, spiders, and
other insects. Each level feeds the next, cycling nutrients through
the soil in an intricate web of relationships.

Bacteria-dominated soils are characteristic of disturbed or
agricultural soils and favor annual plants and grasses. Fungi-dominated
soils are found in forests and mature ecosystems and favor perennials,
shrubs, and trees. Ecological gardeners aim to shift their soil toward
fungal dominance by minimizing tilling and adding woody organic matter.

## Key Soil Organisms

### Earthworms
Earthworms are perhaps the most visible and appreciated soil organisms.
They aerate soil as they burrow, create channels for water infiltration,
and their castings (vermicompost) are rich in plant-available nutrients.
Earthworms need organic matter to thrive; a bare, chemical-fed soil
supports few worms. Charles Darwin spent 39 years studying earthworms
and called them the most important creatures on earth. Darwin's final book was
devoted entirely to their role in soil formation.

### Mycorrhizal Fungi
Mycorrhizal fungi form symbiotic relationships with plant roots. The
fungal threads extend far beyond the root zone, acting as extensions
of the root system to gather water and nutrients (especially
phosphorus) that the plant could not reach alone. In exchange, the
plant feeds the fungus sugars from photosynthesis. Most forest trees
and many garden plants depend on mycorrhizae. Tilling destroys these
delicate fungal networks.

### Bacteria
Soil bacteria are the most numerous organisms in soil, with billions in
a single teaspoon. They decompose simple [[cultivar-endorhiza-bacterial-communities-cannabis]].
