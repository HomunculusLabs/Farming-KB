## Growing Kale and Cole Crops

Kale is the most vigorous and most cold-hardy of all garden brassicas — it will produce when other coles fail. Along with collards and giant kohlrabi, kale belongs to the "easy to grow" tier of brassicas. Understanding the spectrum of brassica refinement — from tough, wild kale to delicate cauliflower — is key to choosing the right cole crops for your garden and skill level.

## The Two Kale Species

### Brassica oleracea Kale

Forms an unbranched tall central stalk with leaves growing from nodes. The most vigorous garden kale. Several types exist:
- **Curly kale**: The most common type
- **Dinosaur (Lacinato) kale**: Long, narrow, blistered leaves
- **Red Russian**: Flat leaves with purple stems

### Brassica napa Kale (Siberian Kale)

Forms a rosette pattern — all leaves come out of a central point close to the ground, similar to a lettuce or spinach plant. Siberian kale crosses only with rutabaga (swede) and is essentially a rutabaga bred for tasty leaves instead of a bulbous root.

**Flavor note**: Some prefer Siberian's flavor when kale is used raw in salads.

## The Refinement Spectrum

All brassicas were bred from the same wild ancestor, *[[brassica-oleracea]]*. Understanding this spectrum helps you choose appropriate varieties:

| Crop | Refinement Level | Vigor | Cold Hardiness | Soil Needs | Difficulty |
|------|-----------------|-------|----------------|------------|------------|
| Kale | Lowest (closest to wild) | Highest | Highest | Lowest | Easiest |
| Collards | Low | High | High | Low | Easy |
| Giant kohlrabi | Low-Medium | High | Good | Low | Easy |
| Cabbage | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | Moderate |
| Brussels sprouts | Medium-High | Medium-Low | Medium | High | Harder |
| Broccoli | High | Medium-Low | Low | High | Harder |
| Cauliflower | Highest | Low | Low | Highest | Hardest |

**Key insight**: As you move up the scale of refinement from kale to cauliflower, each requires more fertile soil, more open (looser) soil, and even more moisture than the previous one.

### The Problem with Small-Framed Cabbage

To create the small cabbage heads currently popular with supermarkets, cabbage has been highly inbred and weakened. These small-framed varieties are "touchy little critters" with delicate root systems intolerant of dry or compacted soils. They need to be coddled.

**New gardeners will do better to grow bigger cabbage varieties.**

## Growing Kale

### Soil Requirements

All coles need more calcium than most other vegetables. If you're not using COF and gardening on rain-leached land, broadcast 5 lb (~1 quart or 2.25 kg) of finely ground agricultural lime per 100 sq ft (10 sq m) before sowing. Do not spread more without a soil test.

### Planting

**Main crop** (for large plants):
- Start about 3 months before the first frost date
- Space 24 × 30 inches (60 × 75 cm) apart
- Each plant will reach 4+ feet (120+ cm) in diameter and 4.5 feet (140 cm) tall

**Fill-in crop** (smaller plants):
- Sown 6 weeks before first frost
- In drills about 12 inches (30 cm) apart
- Thinned progressively
- Uses leftover fertility from previous crop

### Cold Hardiness

Kale has been observed resuming growth after thawing from an overnight low of 21°F (-6°C). As long as the soil has not frozen, kale will keep going — along with endive, spinach, and a few other salad veggies.

### Flavor Improvement

Kale's flavor gets sweeter after frost, as the chicories do. Collards don't need chilling for sweetness, which is probably why they are more popular in the American South.

### Pests

Cabbage worms show only slight interest in kale — they prefer more refined brassicas. If you can grow a large, vigorous kale plant, the pests will be diverted to more interesting targets. This suggests a strategy of growing kale as a trap crop alongside refined brassicas.

### Saving Seed

- All *B. oleracea* cross-pollinate via bees — crosses are unlikely to produce desirable plants
- Isolate different sorts by half a mile (800 m) for low-quality seed
- These brassicas are **biennial** — they must pass through cold weather before flowering
- Need at least 6 plants involved in sharing pollen to avoid inbreeding depression
- For refined brassicas, 200+ plants are better, but with kale, 6-10 is okay because kale retains most of its wild vigor

### The Traditional Seed-Saving Process

1. Overwinter large kale plants with thick woody stalks
2. In cold climates, dig them up carefully in late autumn, plant in damp soil in a root cellar
3. In spring, transplant back outside — the plant makes huge floral sprays
4. Each flower makes a small pointed pod holding a few round black seeds
5. Harvest when pods turn dark brown/black
6. Dry on a tarp in shade, then in sun
7. March on the straw to release seed, then winnow

## Growing Collards

Collards are a non-heading cabbage only slightly less vigorous than kale and may be grown just like kale. More popular in the American South, where they don't need frost for sweetening.

## Growing Giant Kohlrabi

Giant kohlrabi is basically a low-demand fodder crop whose tasty globe can grow to the size of a volleyball.

### Planting

- Start just after the solstice for autumn/winter harvest
- Grown as if it were a medium-sized cabbage
- Will produce at fertility levels suitable for ordinary vegetables

### Response to Better Conditions

Giant kohlrabi will definitely respond to better soil and abundant moisture by becoming:
- Larger
- More mildly flavored
- More tender

### Harvest

Kohlrabi awaits harvest until its base freezes, then stores well in the root cellar. In mild climates, it may overwinter in the garden with only a bit of soil mounded over the roots.

## Culinary Uses

### Colcannon (Scottish Recipe)

1. Fill a large pot with finely chopped kale
2. Add 1/4 inch (6 mm) of water
3. Set heat at low
4. When leaves collapse, add an inch-thick (2.5 cm) layer of roughly cut unpeeled potatoes
5. Steam until potatoes are soft enough to mash
6. Mash everything together — do NOT pour off the water (retain the minerals)
7. Add your usual mashed-potato seasonings

**The nutritional insight**: Steamed kale by itself is intense. Potatoes alone are low in protein and minerals. Combined they make near-perfect nutrition with complementary flavors.

### Other Uses

- Finely shredded Siberian kale blends well into green salads
- Kohlrabi: tasty when coarsely grated into slaw-type salads as though it were cabbage
- Raw kohlrabi chunks make good dipping vegetables
