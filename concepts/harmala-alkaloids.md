# Harmala Alkaloids

## Overview

Harmala alkaloids are a group of beta-carboline compounds found in several plant species, most notably Banisteriopsis caapi (ayahuasca vine) and Peganum harmala (Syrian rue). These compounds are primarily known for their function as reversible inhibitors of monoamine oxidase (MAOI), which is the key pharmacological mechanism that enables the oral activity of DMT in ayahuasca.

The three primary harmala alkaloids are harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine (THH). Each has distinct pharmacological properties, and their combined action is what makes ayahuasca uniquely effective.

## The Three Primary Alkaloids

### Harmine
- Chemical name: 7-methoxy-1-methyl-9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indole
- Molecular weight: 168.19 g/mol
- Primary MAOI activity: potent reversible inhibitor of MAO-A
- Also has affinity for 5-HT2A receptors (psychedelic effects at high doses)
- Found in B. caapi (0.3–0.7% of dry weight) and P. harmala seeds (0.3–1.5%)
- Contributes to the visual and cognitive effects of ayahuasca
- Historically called "telepathine" when first isolated from B. caapi

### Harmaline
- Chemical name: 3,4-Dihydroharmine
- Molecular weight: 170.20 g/mol
- Primary MAOI activity: reversible inhibitor of MAO-A (similar potency to harmine)
- More sedating than harmine — contributes to the "mareación" (the physical, purgative, often difficult body sensations in ayahuasca)
- Found in similar concentrations as harmine
- One of the earliest isolated harmala alkaloids (1847)

### Tetrahydroharmine (THH)
- Chemical name: 1-Methyl-1,2,3,4-tetrahydro-beta-carboline
- Molecular weight: 188.23 g/mol
- Weaker MAOI activity than harmine or harmaline
- Potent serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SRI) — similar mechanism to SSRIs
- May contribute significantly to the antidepressant effects reported with ayahuasca
- Often the most abundant harmala alkaloid in B. caapi
- Less visual, more euphoric and antidepressant

## Source Plants

### Banisteriopsis caapi (Ayahuasca Vine)
- Native to the Amazon basin
- Contains all three primary harmala alkaloids
- The traditional source in ayahuasca preparations
- Different "varieties" or "strains" have different alkaloid profiles:
  - **Red caapi**: Higher in harmine, more visual
  - **Yellow caapi**: Balanced profile, most commonly used
  - **Black/celestial caapi**: Higher in THH, more sedating, considered the strongest medicine
- Alkaloid content varies: 0.3–2.0% total harmala alkaloids by dry weight

### Peganum harmala (Syrian Rue)
- Native to the Mediterranean, Middle East, and Central Asia
- Seeds contain harmine and harmaline (lower THH)
- Seed alkaloid content: 2–6% total (much higher than B. caapi)
- More accessible and affordable than B. caapi
- Used as an ayahuasca analog (sometimes called "ayahuasca pharmahuasca" or "huasca")
- Has its own traditional use in Middle Eastern folk medicine (as an abortifacient and emmenagogue)
- Seeds are harmala-rich but lack the complex alkaloid profile of B. caapi

### Other Sources
- **Passiflora incarnata** (passionflower) — contains trace amounts of harmala alkaloids, mild MAOI activity
- **Banisteriopsis quitensis** — related to B. caapi, used in some Andean preparations

## MAOI Mechanism

### What is MAO?
Monoamine oxidase is an enzyme in the gut wall and liver that breaks down monoamine neurotransmitters including serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine, and importantly, tryptamines like DMT.

### How Harmala Alkaloids Work
- Harmala alkaloids are reversible inhibitors of MAO-A (RIMAs)
- They temporarily block the enzyme that would normally destroy orally ingested DMT
- This allows DMT to survive first-pass metabolism and reach the bloodstream
- The effect lasts 4–8 hours, roughly corresponding to the duration of ayahuasca effects

### Why This Matters
Without MAO inhibition, oral DMT is completely inactive — the body destroys it before it can reach the brain. This is why smoked or vaporized DMT is active (it bypasses the gut/liver) but oral DMT alone is not. The harmala alkaloids in ayahuasca make the oral route possible.

## Extraction and Preparation

### Simple Water Extraction from B. caapi
1. Shred 50–100 g of B. caapi vine
2. Simmer in acidified water (pH ~4 with lemon juice) for 2–3 hours
3. Strain and repeat 2–3 times
4. Combine and reduce to a drinkable volume
5. This is the standard ayahuasca vine preparation

### Syrian Rue Seed Preparation
1. Grind P. harmala seeds to a powder (5–10 g is a typical dose)
2. Simmer in water for 30–60 minutes
3. Strain and drink 30 minutes before consuming DMT source
4. Alternatively, encapsulate the powder and swallow (less efficient)

### Harmala Alkaloid Extraction (Overview)
1. Powder seeds or vine material
2. Extract with acidified water or ethanol
3. Basify the extract with sodium hydroxide (pH 9–10)
4. Harmala alkaloids precipitate as freebase (bright orange/red resin)
5. Collect the precipitate by filtration
6. Can be further purified or converted to HCl salt

## Dosage

### B. caapi Vine
- Light: 25–50 g dried
- Moderate: 50–100 g dried
- Strong: 100–150+ g dried

### P. harmala Seeds
- Light MAOI effect: 2–3 g
- Moderate: 3–5 g
- Strong: 5–7 g (risk of adverse effects increases significantly above 5 g)

### Isolated Harmala Alkaloids (Freebase)
- Harmine: 50–200 mg
- Harmaline: 50–150 mg
- Combined: 100–300 mg total

## Effects of Harmala Alkaloids Alone

At ayahuasca-appropriate doses, harmala alkaloids produce their own effects:

- Mild sedation and drowsiness
- Nausea and gastrointestinal discomfort
- Slight visual distortion (at higher doses)
- Emotional sensitivity and introspection
- Antidepressant effects (anecdotal, some research support)
- Purgative effects (vomiting, diarrhea — considered cleansing in traditional context)

At high doses (overdose range), harmala alkaloids can produce:
- Severe nausea and vomiting
- Hypotension (dangerously low blood pressure)
- Tremors and convulsions (harmaline is a tremorogenic agent)
- Serotonin syndrome (when combined with serotonergic drugs)

## Safety Cautions and Interactions

### Serotonin Syndrome Risk
Harmala alkaloids must NEVER be combined with:
- SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram, etc.) — stop 2+ weeks before
- MAOIs (pharmaceutical MAOIs like phenelzine, tranylcypromine)
- SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine)
- Tricyclic antidepressants
- Tramadol, dextromethorphan
- 5-HTP or tryptophan supplements
- Other serotonergic psychedelics at high doses

This is the most dangerous interaction — serotonin syndrome can be fatal.

### Tyramine Interaction
RIMAs are less dangerous with tyramine than irreversible MAOIs, but caution is still warranted. Avoid:
- Aged cheeses
- Cured meats (salami, pepperoni)
- Fermented foods (sauerkraut, soy sauce, miso)
- Red wine (especially aged)
- Yeast extracts

### Other Cautions
- Not safe during pregnancy — P. harmala has traditional use as an abortifacient
- Cardiovascular conditions require medical evaluation
- Start with low doses — individual sensitivity varies greatly
