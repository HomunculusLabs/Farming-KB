# Dutch Smart Shop and Paddos Trade History

## Origins: Dutch Drug Policy Pragmatism

The Dutch are famous for their pragmatism, and this was never more evident
than in their approach to the matter of drugs. Recognising that a drug-free
society, though perhaps a desirable ideal, was unobtainable, the Dutch
government acted to separate "drugs with acceptable risks" from "drugs with
unacceptable risks," both in law and in the marketplace. Putting cannabis in
the first category led most famously to the opening of the Dutch coffee
shops, where small amounts could be consumed openly without fear of
prosecution.

Their hands tied by the UN Convention on Narcotic Drugs (1961), the Dutch
were unable to legalise cannabis, but the half-measure of decriminalisation
freed them to maximise their efforts in preventing unacceptable drug use of
heroin, cocaine, LSD, and later Ecstasy. It was within this liberal climate
that the magic mushroom trade was born.

## Opening of the First Smart Shop

A young man named Dirk van den Huerk opened Holland's very first smart shop,
called Dreams, in Amsterdam in 1993. A disillusioned IT worker, drugs
charity worker, and Rave enthusiast, van den Huerk envisioned a shop that
would sell a mixture of vitamins and minerals along with legal synthetic
"designer" drugs. Dutch smart shops quickly proliferated, selling everything
from natural plant stimulants such as guarana and ephedra, to psychoactive
botanicals like Salvia divinorum and the San Pedro cactus, alongside arcane
synthetic compounds with names like 2C-B and 2C-T-2.

A cat-and-mouse game ensued, in which new drugs would come onto the market,
only to be banned a few months later by the ever-vigilant authorities. The
hunt was always on to find legal loopholes to exploit.
