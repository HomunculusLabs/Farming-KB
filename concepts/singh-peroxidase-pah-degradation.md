# Peroxidase-Catalyzed PAH Degradation by Fungi

Enzyme treatment provides a powerful strategy for the removal of
[[singh-fungal-biodegradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]] hydrocarbons (PAHs) in wastewaters and soils.
White-rot Basidiomycetes produce a complex inventory of peroxidases,
including lignin peroxidase (LiP) and [[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]] (MnP), which
play central roles in PAH oxidation through one-electron transfer
mechanisms producing reactive cation radicals.

## Lignin Peroxidase (LiP) in PAH Oxidation

The extracellular peroxidase LiP exhibits a higher redox potential than
other peroxidases, making it a superior oxidant of PAHs. LiP oxidizes
PAHs with an ionization potential (IP) of less than about 8 eV in the
presence of H2O2 or H2O2-generating enzyme systems. Compounds I and II
of the LiP catalytic cycle differ in their ability to oxidize PAHs;
compound I oxidizes substrates of higher redox potential than compound
II. Addition of veratryl alcohol (VA) enhances the rate and extent of
PAH degradation by mediating LiP to form cation radicals.

## Veratryl Alcohol Mediation

Veratryl alcohol, a common [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]] produced by white-rot
fungi along with LiP, serves as an ideal reducing substrate for
compounds I and II of the LiP catalytic cycle. The oxidized VA cation
may react with H2O2 to produce a highly reactive dioxygen anion, which
in turn reacts with the PAH substrate. VA mediation extends the
oxidizing power of the active site to distant targets, making PAH
removal more efficient. P. chrysosporium LiP oxidized benzo[a]pyrene
to benzo[a]pyrene 1,6-, 3,6-, and 6,12-quinones (Haemmerli et al., 1986).

## Manganese Peroxidase (MnP) Mechanisms

MnP is unique among peroxidases because it prefers its reducing substrate
to be complexed Mn(II). MnP oxidizes Mn(II) to the powerful oxidizing
agent Mn(III). A variety of PAHs with IP less than 7.8 eV are oxidized
to acetoxy-PAH and PAH quinones by Mn(III) in acetate-water mixtures.
High yields of PAH quinones are recovered when incubated with
extracellular fluids of white-rot fungi containing high MnP levels
(Field et al., 1992). One-electron oxidations of several PAHs by Mn(III)
have been established.

## MnP from Phanerochaete laevis

Small amounts of quinone products from anthracene and benzo[a]pyrene
are produced during MnP/Mn(II) reactions by Phanerochaete laevis
(Bogan and Lamar, 1996). MnP-based lipid peroxidation reactions
revealed transformation of all four PAHs tested. Highly polar
metabolites similar to diphenic acid were found during reactions of
phenanthrene, anthracene, benzo[a]pyrene, and benz[a]anthracene.
MnP from P. laevis achieved 6-9% degradation of anthracene and
benzo[a]pyrene in 30 hours.

## Nematoloma frowardii Peroxidase

MnP from Nematoloma frowardii (1.96 U/ml) achieved greater than 99%
degradation of anthracene with 3.7% mineralization as CO2 in 168 hours
(Sack et al., 1997c). Pyrene degradation reached 24% with 3.1% CO2.
Fluoranthene was degraded 13%, and benzo[a]pyrene showed 54%
degradation with 0.4% mineralization. Addition of 5 mM glutathione
(GSH) enhanced benzo[a]pyrene mineralization to 4.0% CO2 while
maintaining greater than 99% degradation.

## [[trametes-versicolor-entity]] MnP

[[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]] MnP degraded 75% of phenanthrene (10 mg/l) in 11
days and 100% of fluorene in 7 days (Collins and Dobson, 1996).
P. chrysosporium MnP achieved greater than 99% degradation of fluorene
(20 uM) through MnP-based lipid peroxidation in 7 days (Bogan et al.,
1996a). These results demonstrate the broad substrate range of MnP
across different PAH structures.
