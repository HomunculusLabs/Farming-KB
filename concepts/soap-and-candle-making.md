# Soap and Candle Making for the Homestead

Processing animal fats and beeswax into soap and candles closes a critical nutrient loop on the homestead. Duck fat, goat tallow, and beeswax from [[beekeeping]] operations become high-value products — for household use or sale via [[selling-farm-products-and-business]].

## Rendering Fats

### Tallow (Goat/Beef)

- **Suet** (kidney fat) renders into clean, white tallow ideal for soap and candles
- **Yield:** ~50-60% rendered fat from raw suet weight
- **Method:** Chop into 1-inch cubes, simmer with 1-2 inches water 2-4 hours, strain through cheesecloth, chill to solidify; repeat for purity
- **Storage:** Freezer paper, 1 year frozen / 6 months refrigerated

### Duck Fat and Lard

- **Duck fat:** Soft; excellent for soap (conditioning) but too soft for candles — blend max 30% with beeswax or tallow
- **Lard (goat/pig):** Classic soap ingredient; creamy lather, moisturizing
- **Yield:** ~40-50% from raw fat trimmings

### Fat Properties

| Fat | SAP Value | Soap Quality | Candle Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goat/beef tallow | 0.140 | Hard, long-lasting, cleansing | Good, bright flame |
| Duck fat | 0.138 | Conditioning, soft bar | Poor alone — blend ≤30% |
| Lard | 0.138 | Creamy lather, moisturizing | Fair, smoky |

> **SAP Value** = mg KOH per g fat. Always use a lye calculator (soapcalc.net or Bramble Berry) — never estimate.

## Cold Process Soap Making

### Safety

- **Lye (NaOH)** is extremely caustic — goggles, nitrile gloves, long sleeves, closed-toe shoes
- **Always add lye to water** — never reverse (causes violent exothermic reaction)
- **Lye solution reaches 180-200°F** — work in ventilated area; fumes are irritating
- **Neutralize spills** with vinegar spray bottle
- **Dedicated equipment:** Stainless steel pot (never aluminum), silicone spatula, immersion blender, digital scale (0.1 oz accuracy), infrared thermometer

### Basic Tallow Soap (100% Homestead Fat)

| Ingredient | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Goat/beef tallow | 32 oz | 100% of oils |
| Distilled water | 12.2 oz | — |
| NaOH (lye) | 4.5 oz | 5% superfat |

**Process:**
1. **Prepare:** Gear up, line molds, weigh all ingredients
2. **Lye:** Add lye slowly to water, stir until dissolved. Cool to 100-110°F
3. **Melt:** Melt tallow to 100-110°F
4. **Combine:** Pour lye into fats. Stick-blend to **trace** (pudding consistency, drizzled lines hold — 5-15 min)
5. **Additives:** At light trace, add essential oils (0.5-1 oz/lb), herbs, or exfoliants (see table below)
6. **Mold:** Pour, tap to release bubbles
7. **Insulate:** Wrap in towels 24-48 hours
8. **Unmold and cut:** Pull from molds, cut bars
9. **Cure:** On a rack, well-ventilated, **4-6 weeks minimum** — soap hardens, milder over time
10. **Test:** Zap test (tongue to soap — no zap = done) or pH strips (target 8-10)

### Recipe Formulation

- **Hard oils** (tallow, coconut, palm) = hard bar, stable lather, cleansing
- **Soft oils** (olive, duck fat) = conditioning, creamy lather
- **Starter blend:** 70% tallow / 20% coconut / 10% olive — hard bar with fluffy lather
- **Superfat:** 5% = balanced; 0% = harsh laundry soap; 7-8% = very moisturizing but softer

### Goat Milk Soap

Replace water with frozen goat milk from [[goat-keeping]] — freeze in ice cube trays, add lye slowly to frozen milk (prevents scorching). Full-fat milk is best. Expect: lighter color, creamier lather, lower pH. Add 1 tbsp honey/lb oils to boost lather.

### Homestead Additives

| Additive | Rate | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Dried herbs (lavender, calendula) | 1-2 tsp/lb | Color, scent, skin soothing |
| Oatmeal (ground) | 1 tbsp/lb | Gentle exfoliant |
| Honey | 1 tbsp/lb | Lather booster, humectant |
| Beeswax | 0.5-1 oz/lb | Hardness boost |
| Coffee grounds | 2 tbsp/lb | Exfoliant, odor neutralizer |

### Wood Ash Lye (Traditional)

- Only hardwood ashes (oak, maple, hickory); leach through straw-lined bucket with rainwater
- Test: float raw egg — penny-size area exposed = ~10-12% KOH
- Produces KOH (potassium hydroxide), not NaOH — makes soft/liquid soap
- Use purchased NaOH for predictable results; wood ash for heritage/educational purposes

### Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soap seizes | FOs with vanillin, cold lye, over-blending | Pour immediately; use essential oils |
| Soda ash (white layer) | CO₂ reacting with surface | Spray with rubbing alcohol after pouring |
| Lye pockets | Incomplete blending | Blend longer; 3-5 min minimum |
| DOS (orange spots) | Rancid oils, high superfat | Use fresh fats; superfat ≤7%; store cool/dry |
| Soft bar | Too much soft oil, humid cure | Increase hard fat %; longer cure |

## Candle Making

### Beeswax Candles

Beeswax from [[beekeeping]] — clean burn, natural honey scent, 50% longer burn time than paraffin, no soot. Never microwave; always double-boiler.

**Melted/poured (molded):**
1. Melt beeswax to 160°F in double boiler
2. Prime wick (dip in wax, hang to cool), thread through mold, secure with wick tab
3. Pour at 155-160°F for smooth finish
4. Cool undisturbed 4-6 hours, unmold, trim wick to 1/4 inch

**Dipped tapers:**
1. Melt wax to 160°F in tall narrow container
2. Dip wick pair in/out smoothly (1 sec in, pause, 1 sec out)
3. Hang 2-3 min between dips; repeat 20-30× for desired diameter
4. Roll on smooth surface, hang to cure 24 hours

**Wick sizing:** 1-1.5" diameter = #1 cotton/square braid; 1.5-2.5" = #2; 2.5-3.5" = #3; tapers = small square braid.

### Tallow Candles

Historical primary light source. Add 10-20% beeswax for harder candles, less dripping, less smoke. Same method as molded beeswax; pour at 145-150°F. Optional: dip finished candles in cold saltwater to harden surface.

### Candle Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Tunneling | Wick too small | Increase wick; burn 1 hr/inch first time |
| Sooting | Wick too large, draft | Downsize wick; relocate |
| Cracking | Cooled too fast | Cool at room temperature |
| Poor scent | Too little oil, wrong temp | 1 oz/lb wax at 185°F; stir 2 min |

## Selling Soap and Candles

- **Cottage food laws** vary — soap isn't food, but **cosmetic claims** trigger FDA requirements
- **"Soap" exemption:** Labeled "soap" with no cosmetic claims + made of alkali salts of fats = less regulation
- **Labeling (if cosmetic):** INCI ingredients, net weight, business name/address
- **Insurance:** Product liability recommended ($1-2M, ~$300-500/year)
- **Pricing:** Cost × 4-5 = wholesale; × 8-10 = retail
- See [[selling-farm-products-and-business]] for market channels
