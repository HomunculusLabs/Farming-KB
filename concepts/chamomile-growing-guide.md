
# Chamomile Growing Guide

## Overview

Chamomile is one of the most ancient and widely used medicinal herbs, prized
for its calming, anti-inflammatory, and digestive properties. Two species are
primarily cultivated: German chamomile ([[matricaria-chamomilla]], syn. M.
recutita) and Roman chamomile (Chamaemelum nobile). While they share many
medicinal qualities, they differ significantly in growth habit, cultivation
requirements, and ideal uses.

## German vs Roman Chamomile Comparison

| Feature | German Chamomile | Roman Chamomile |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Scientific name | Matricaria chamomilla | Chamaemelum nobile |
| Lifecycle | Annual (tender perennial in zones 8+) | Perennial |
| Height | 2-3 feet | 3-12 inches (low, creeping) |
| Growth habit | Upright, branching | Low, spreading ground cover |
| Flower size | Larger, cone-shaped center | Smaller, flatter |
| Essential oil | Rich in chamazulene (blue oil) | Lower chamazulene content |
| Best use | Tea production, large-scale cultivation | Lawns, pathways, ornamental |
| Self-seeding | Yes, prolifically | Moderate |
| Hardiness | Grown as annual in most zones | Zones 4-9 |
| Medicinal potency | Generally considered stronger | Milder, but still effective |

## Medicinal Uses

Both species contain similar active compounds: chamazulene (anti-inflammatory),
apigenin (sedative, anxiolytic), bisabolol (anti-inflammatory, anti-microbial),
and flavonoids.

Primary applications:
- Relaxation and sleep: Chamomile tea is one of the most widely used gentle
  sedatives. Apigenin binds to GABA receptors in the brain, promoting
  calmness and sleep.
- Digestive support: Relieves gas, bloating, indigestion, and mild GI spasms.
  Effective as a tea or tincture after meals.
- Anti-inflammatory: External use as a compress or in baths for skin
  inflammation, eczema, minor wounds, and sunburn.
- Teething and colic: Mild chamomile tea is traditionally used for infants
  with colic (consult a pediatrician first).
- Menstrual discomfort: Anti-spasmodic action eases menstrual cramps.
- Immune support: Mild antimicrobial properties support respiratory health.

Safety: Chamomile is generally very safe. Individuals with allergies to
ragweed, chrysanthemums, or other Asteraceae family members should use
caution. Rare but possible interactions with blood-thinning medications.

## Tea Production

German chamomile is the preferred species for tea production due to its
higher essential oil content and larger flower yield.

To make chamomile tea:
1. Harvest flowers at full bloom on a dry morning
2. Use fresh or dried flowers (approximately 1-2 teaspoons dried per cup)
3. Pour 8 oz of freshly boiled (not actively boiling) water over flowers
4. Cover and steep 5-10 minutes
5. Strain and enjoy

For larger-scale tea production:
- Dry flowers on screens at 95-100 degrees F until completely crisp
- Store in airtight containers away from light and moisture
- Dried flowers maintain quality for 6-12 months
- A 10-foot row of German chamomile can yield several ounces of dried flowers

## Cultivation of German Chamomile

### Site Selection
Full sun to light shade. Prefers light, sandy, well-drained soil but adapts
to a range of soil types. Tolerates poor soil; overly rich soil produces
lush foliage with fewer flowers. pH 5.6-7.5.

### Propagation
- Direct sow in early spring after last frost (preferred method)
- Surface sow or barely cover seed; light aids germination
- Germination: 7-14 days at 60-70 degrees F
- Can also start indoors 4-6 weeks before last frost; transplant carefully
  as seedlings dislike root disturbance
- Self-seeds prolifically once established -- expect volunteers

### Spacing
- Thin or transplant seedlings to 6-12 inches apart in rows 12-18 inches apart
- Dense planting helps support the tall, sometimes floppy stems

### Care
- Water seedlings regularly; established plants are moderately drought tolerant
- Pinch back young plants to encourage bushier growth
- No fertilizer needed; excess nitrogen reduces flower production
- Generally pest and disease free

## Cultivation of Roman Chamomile

### Site Selection
Full sun to partial shade. Prefers well-drained, slightly acidic to neutral
soil. Good drought tolerance once established. Works well as a ground cover,
between stepping stones, or as a lawn alternative.

### Propagation
- Seed: Slow and uneven germination; stratify 1-2 weeks
- Division: Most reliable method. Divide established clumps in spring or
  early fall, ensuring each division has roots and shoots.
- Cuttings: Softwood cuttings in summer root readily under mist.
- The double-flowered cultivar 'Flore Pleno' is sterile and must be
  propagated vegetatively.

### Spacing
- Space plants 6-12 inches apart for ground cover
- Closer spacing (4-6 inches) for faster fill-in

### Care
- Water regularly until established
- Mow or shear after flowering to maintain compact growth
- Tolerates light foot traffic (single-flowered varieties)
- Divide every 2-3 years to maintain vigor

## Harvesting

For both species:
- Harvest flowers when fully open, on a dry morning after dew has dried
- Pinch or snip the flower head off the stem
- Harvest every 2-3 days during peak bloom for maximum yield
- German chamomile blooms over a longer period (4-8 weeks)
- For the highest essential oil content, harvest on warm, sunny days

## Common Issues

- Damping off: Common in seedlings if soil is too wet. Use well-draining
  media and avoid overwatering.
- Aphids: Occasionally appear on flower buds; spray with water or insecticidal
  soap.
- [[powdery-mildew]]: Improve air circulation around plants.
- Leggy growth: Indicates too much shade or excessive nitrogen.

## Companion Planting

Chamomile is an excellent companion plant:
- Said to improve the flavor and growth of nearby herbs and vegetables
- Attracts beneficial insects including hoverflies and wasps
- Its anti-fungal properties may help prevent damping off in nearby seedlings
- Compatible with most garden plants; traditionally paired with onions,
  cabbage, and cucumbers
