## Overview

Cannabis nutrient recipes provide specific formulations for feeding plants throughout their life cycle. Approaches range from synthetic salt-based hydroponic solutions to organic living soil amendments and compost teas. The right recipe depends on the growing medium, stage of growth, and cultivation philosophy.

## Synthetic Nutrient Lines

### General Hydroponics Flora Series (3-Part)

The Flora series is one of the most widely used synthetic nutrient systems:

- **FloraMicro**: Provides micronutrients and some nitrogen. Must be added to water first.
- **FloraGro**: Nitrogen-focused for vegetative growth.
- **FloraBloom**: Phosphorus and potassium for flowering.

#### Vegetative Recipe (per gallon)

- FloraMicro: 5 ml
- FloraGro: 10 ml
- FloraBloom: 5 ml
- Target EC: 1.2-1.5 (600-750 PPM at 500 scale)

#### Flowering Recipe (per gallon)

- FloraMicro: 5 ml
- FloraGro: 0-3 ml (reduce as flower progresses)
- FloraBloom: 10-15 ml (increase through mid-flower)
- Target EC: 1.5-2.0 (750-1000 PPM at 500 scale)

Always add FloraMicro to water first, stir, then add FloraGro, stir, then FloraBloom. This prevents nutrient lockout from precipitation.

### Lucas Formula

A simplified approach using only FloraMicro and FloraBloom (FloraGro is omitted entirely):

- **Vegetative**: 8 ml FloraMicro + 16 ml FloraBloom per gallon
- **Flowering**: 8 ml FloraMicro + 16 ml FloraBloom per gallon (same ratio throughout)

The Lucas formula is popular for its simplicity — one ratio for the entire grow. The reduced nitrogen from omitting FloraGro is compensated by the FloraMicro's nitrogen content. Works best in coco coir or hydroponic systems.

## Cal-Mag Supplementation

Calcium and magnesium are frequently deficient in:

- Reverse osmosis (RO) or distilled water
- Coco coir (coco naturally binds calcium)
- Peat-based mediums

### Cal-Mag Recipe

- 1 ml per gallon of Cal-Mag supplement (General Hydroponics Botanicare Cal-Mag Plus, or similar)
- Add before base nutrients
- Target calcium: 150-200 ppm; magnesium: 50-75 ppm
- Some tap water already contains sufficient Ca and Mg — test before supplementing

## Organic Approaches

### Living Soil Amendment Schedule

In a [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] system, nutrients are provided through pre-amended soil rather than liquid feeding. The soil food web breaks down organic matter and makes nutrients bioavailable on demand.

#### Base Soil Mix (per cubic foot)

- 1 part high-quality compost (worm castings or thermal compost)
- 1 part aeration (pumice, perlite, or rice hulls)
- 1 part peat moss or coco coir
- 1-2 cups worm castings per gallon of soil
- 2-4 cups alfalfa meal (nitrogen source)
- 1-2 cups bone meal (phosphorus and calcium)
- 1-2 cups kelp meal (micronutrients and growth hormones)
- 0.5-1 cup oyster shell flour (calcium, pH buffer)
- 0.5 cup dolomite lime (calcium and magnesium)
- 2 tablespoons mycorrhizal inoculant

#### Top-Dressing Schedule

- **Pre-plant**: Mix amendments into soil
- **Week 3 veg**: Light top-dress of worm castings and kelp meal
- **Week 1 flower**: Top-dress with bone meal and langbeinite (sulfate of potash magnesia)
- **Week 4 flower**: Top-dress with high-P bat guano or bone meal
- **Week 6 flower**: Light kelp meal top-dress for terpene support

Water top-dressings in with plain water or compost tea.

### Compost Tea Recipes

#### Active Aerated Compost Tea (AACT)

Brewed with an air pump and air stone to maintain aerobic conditions:

- 5 gallons dechlorinated water
- 2 cups high-quality compost or vermicompost
- 1 tablespoon unsulfured blackstrap molasses
- 2 tablespoons kelp meal
- 1 tablespoon fish hydrolysate (optional)
- Brew for 24-36 hours at 65-75 F with continuous aeration
- Apply as soil drench or foliar spray within 4 hours of brewing

The molasses feeds beneficial bacteria, kelp provides micronutrients, and fish hydrolysate adds nitrogen and amino acids.

#### Simple Fermented Plant Juice Tea

- 1 cup [[food-fermentation-guide]] plant extract (comfrey, nettle, or kelp FPJ)
- 1 gallon dechlorinated water
- 1 tablespoon molasses
- Dilute and apply as soil drench during vegetative growth

## pH Management

Nutrient availability is pH-dependent. The target ranges vary by medium:

| Medium | Target pH |
|---|---|
| Soil / Living soil | 6.0-7.0 |
| Coco coir | 5.8-6.2 |
| Hydroponics (Rockwool, clay pebbles) | 5.5-6.0 |

### Adjusting pH

- **pH Up**: Potassium hydroxide (KOH) or potassium silicate
- **pH Down**: Phosphoric acid or citric acid (organic)

Always adjust pH after adding all nutrients to the solution, as nutrients change the water's pH. Check pH every feeding.

## Common Nutrient Deficiencies

- **Nitrogen (N)**: Lower leaves turn pale yellow, progressing upward. Most common in flowering.
- **Phosphorus (P)**: Dark green or purplish leaves, especially on undersides. Stunted growth.
- **Potassium (K)**: Brown leaf edges and tips, weak stems. Common in flowering.
- **Calcium (Ca)**: Leaf tip curl, brown spots on new growth. Common in coco.
- **Magnesium (Mg)**: Interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between veins) on lower leaves.
- **Iron (Fe)**: Interveinal chlorosis on new growth (upper leaves). Often pH-related.

## Feeding Frequency

- **Soil / Living soil**: Water when dry; feed with compost tea every 1-2 weeks
- **Coco coir**: Feed every watering at reduced strength (60-70% of recommended)
- **Hydroponics**: Maintain nutrient reservoir; change every 1-2 weeks

For transition between vegetative and flowering feeding, see [[cannabis-vegetative-stage]] and [[cannabis-flowering-stage]] for stage-specific guidance. Environmental factors in [[cannabis-environment-control]] also affect nutrient uptake rates.
