## Pigs as Landscape Workers

Holzer considers pigs his most valuable helpers on the farm. Rather than confining them to concrete pens, pigs on the Krameterhof live outdoors year-round and perform essential land management tasks:

- **Soil preparation**: Pigs dig over and loosen soil in preparation for planting [[fukuoka-fruit-processing-and-natural-food-marketing]]**: Windfall fruit provides high-quality feed; pigs convert it to manure
- **Seed dispersal**: Pigs distribute seeds through their manure as they move across the landscape

The integration of pigs into the farming system means the farmer does less work while the animals improve the land. This is a core principle: animals are not a separate enterprise but an integral part of the whole system.

## Old Pig Breeds

Holzer strongly favours old, robust breeds over modern commercial breeds:

- **Mangalitza**: Woolly pig, extremely hardy, excellent for outdoor keeping, produces high-quality fat
- **Swabian-Hall Swine**: Robust old German breed, good mothering instincts
- **Duroc**: Hardy breed that does well in outdoor systems
- **Turopolje**: Rare Croatian breed, very undemanding

Old breeds are better suited to outdoor life because they have not been bred for rapid growth in confinement. They are more disease-resistant, require less concentrated feed, and can withstand harsh alpine winters without heated housing. Modern breeds, by contrast, have been selected for maximum weight gain in controlled environments and suffer outdoors.

## Paddock System

Pigs are kept in a paddock system with regular rotation:

1. Sow paddocks with a mixture of cereals, legumes, and root crops
2. Pigs are moved through paddocks sequentially
3. They harvest the crops themselves, rooting up tubers and grazing
4. As they move on, the disturbed soil is ready for sowing or planting
5. Fruit trees can be established in paddocks that pigs have prepared

The paddock system means the farmer does not need to sow, harvest, or transport feed separately. The pigs do the harvesting themselves, and their manure fertilises the next crop. This circular system eliminates the need for external inputs and creates a self-sustaining cycle of fertility.

## Earth Shelter Pigsties

Holzer builds simple, economical shelters that use the soil's insulating properties:

1. Dig a 2-3m wide, 1-2m deep ditch at the foot of a slope (ensure it stays dry)
2. Lay tree trunks across the ditch as a roof, gently sloped for drainage
3. Insulate with sheeting or bitumen shingles, then cover with soil/brushwood
4. Logs should have at least 1m excess length (pigs dig around them)
5. Only one side is open for the pigs to enter/exit
6. Throw in straw or hay -- pigs distribute it themselves

### Why Earth Shelters Work
- Soil insulation maintains stable temperature year-round (critical at -25C winters)
- Draught-free environment
- Very low construction cost
- Pigs are naturally clean -- they go outside to relieve themselves
- No cleaning required; pigs keep their sleeping area spotless

The key insight is that pigs do not need heated buildings. Their natural behaviour -- huddling together and burrowing into bedding -- combined with earth insulation provides all the warmth they need even in extreme cold.
