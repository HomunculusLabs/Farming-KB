## The Global Water Context
- 75% of all fresh water is unavailable (ice caps, deep aquifers)
- Less than 1% of water moves (rivers, atmospheric)
- World mean average rainfall: 33.8 inches
- 77% of atmospheric water falls on oceans; 23% on land
- Of land rainfall, 16 parts transpire/evaporate, 7 parts run off
- Land also gets 7 parts from horizontal advection (where forests intervene)

## Core Design Principles

### Surface Storage at Height
"Most of the world needs much more surface storage at greater heights than usual, not just in the low valleys." Swales, ponds, and soil storage are the primary tools.

### Swales and the Wallace Plow
- Swales store water in soils treated with the Wallace plow
- Throughout urban areas, swales are "very appropriate"
- Construct swales before planting anything else
- They reduce runoff and increase infiltration

### Seaweed for Water Storage
- Seaweed and seaweed concentrates work as gel in soils
- In very dry soils, use dried seaweed powders and concentrates
- They greatly assist water storage and plant resistance to wilt
- Work on the surface film of the soil

## Pond Design

### Structure
- Sloping or stepped floor that can be fully drained
- Better if drainable into another pond for a dryland cycle
- Lime the whole base when making the pond (pH 6-8)
- After 3-4 years as a pond, it can carry dryland crops without further manuring
- The mud surface fixes nutrients from water via diatoms and freshwater mussels

### Mussels
- Mussels pump nitrogen and phosphorus into the mud
- Filter ~200 gallons of water per day per mussel
- "Of all plants, animals, seeds, anything, the mussels are the superior phosphorus fixer"
- Harvest modestly for chicken grit only

### The Dry Cycle
- Intensively cultivated ponds benefit from going through a dry stage
- Grow heavy feeders in first year, taper off, finish with modest crop
- Drain into another pond, transfer critical species
- Roll it down and re-flood; gley perpetuates itself in the soil

### The Secchi Disk Test
- White painted disc disappears at ~2.5 feet = well-manured pond
- Blacked out at ~1 foot = over-manured (dilute)
- Visible at 5 feet = under-manured (add chicken manure)

## Aquatic Plant Systems

### Edge Plants (0-20 feet from water, never in water)
- Bamboo, swamp trees (bald cypress), willows, poplars
- Many species must live within 20 feet of water's edge

### Marginal Plants (soaked soils)
- Venus fly traps, pitcher plants, sundews
- Arrowheads, duck potatoes (Sagittaria, 80+ species)
- Wild rice (Zizania) -- throw seed in mud balls, 18 inches to 3 feet depth
- Chinese water chestnuts, lotus (popcorn seeds)

### Reed/Cattail Zone
- Papyrus, rushes, reeds for mats, cattails
- "Real heavy crops" from this zone
- Cattails in small annual garden ponds -- cut tops to prevent seeding
- Cattail tops can be popped like popcorn

### Floating Plants
- Water lilies (plant in tire with manure bag)
- Duckweed (Lemna)
- Water hyacinth (use in restricted locations to clear pollution)

### Submerged Plants
- Continue to ~12 feet depth
- Algae production ceases at ~12 feet
- Only reason for 12-15 foot depth: fish escape from low oxygen/temperature

## Polyculture Pond Design

The cascade system: design single-culture ponds that flow one to another:
1. **Duck marsh** with mussels and wild rice (18 inches deep)
2. **Forage ponds** at different pH's for small aquatic animals
3. **Rice/paddy pond**
4. **Shrimp ponds** (no trout)
5. **Trout pond** (receives trickle flow of forage animals)

"Polyculture, but not in the sense that we have it all together." Different species need different pH's and conditions.

### Duck Integration
- 8 ducks per quarter acre
- Ducks add manure, assist energy turnover
- Wild rice grows above duck height, provides food
- Pond must be fenced/top-netted against foxes
- Duck shed behind fence, opens to water

### Insect Attraction
- UV lamp with small fan over pond
- Insects attracted to light, blown into water by downdraft
- Can eliminate need for manure input entirely
## Tidal and Marine Systems

### Salt Marshes
- Spartina (cord grass): heavy seed producer, goose forage, base food for cool water fish
- Sea lavender: honey plant
- Samphire: pickles
- "No Spartina, no bluefish" -- it is the nurse ground

### Tide Traps
- Ancient stone walls ~6 inches below high tide surface
- Fish swim over at high tide, trapped when tide drops
- Work best at night

### Inland Tidal Ponds
- Channel through salt reserve, 3:1 side slope
- Boards regulate tide range (3 inches to 1 foot twice daily)
- "Cheapest swimming pool you can build anybody, and self-flushing"
- Oysters at 60% air exposure produce most meat
- Octopus will occupy clay pots if provided

### Seaweed Systems
- Irish stone fields in the sea for seaweed cultivation
- Smaller fields produce more than larger (warmer)
- Kelp: pull on tide, load donkeys at low tide
- Kelp stems fired with peat for potash; fronds as mulch
- Sea grass composted = best insulation material

### Phosphate Collection
- Sea bird roosts concentrate phosphate
- One man built football-field-sized platform off West Africa
- "He's annually a millionaire" from gathered phosphate
