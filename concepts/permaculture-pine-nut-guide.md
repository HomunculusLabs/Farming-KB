# Pine Nut Tree Guide

## Overview

There are more than 20 species of pine which bear edible pine nuts. Pines are conifers and evergreen. Pines possess distinctive cones, which are either male or female. The edible nuts have excellent food value. Many of the pinon pines are slow growing and only produce heavy crops every few years.

## Key Edible Pine Species

### Pinus pinea (Stone Pine, Italian Stone Pine, Umbrella Pine)

Native to the Mediterranean, the stone pine is a handsome tree, reaching 12-25 m in height, with a large, spreading, crown. It has a slow to moderate growth rate, but is long lived (60-100 years). Thinning the trees interior will increase the growth rate. It grows well in most soils, apart from alkaline soils and impermeable clays, in areas receiving at least 400 mm mean annual rainfall. It will tolerate both hot summers and light frost.

Although it provides a good windbreak when young, older trees tend to lose their lower needles as a result of shading. Because of its umbrella shaped crown, it is also an excellent shade tree.

It produces the edible pignolia nut, the growing of which is an important commercial industry in Europe. The seeds are eaten raw or roasted and salted. They are used in sweetmeats, cakes, puddings, biscuits, soups, etc. In Romania the young cones are used whole to flavour game sauces.

### Pinus cembroides (Mexican Pinon)

A small, rustic tree with a short trunk, found in the higher rocky foothills of Mexico and Arizona at altitudes up to 2000 m. It is a slow grower but very wind, cold and drought hardy. The oily seeds are eaten raw or roasted. They are ground to flour for breads and cakes or made into a nut butter.

### Pinus monophylla (Single-leaf Pinon Pine)

A small, hardy, drought-resistant native to the higher rocky foothills from Idaho to Mexico. Seeds are eaten raw or roasted. They are also ground and made into pancakes. The cones take three separate growing seasons or a time span of 26 months to mature and are susceptible to adverse weather conditions. There also tend to be cycles of good and poor years.

### Other Species

- **Pinus albicaulis** (whitebark pine) -- Medium sized tree (to 15 m). Frost resistant, adaptable. Cones are 7.5 cm long with tightly closed scales. Claimed to have "unsurpassed delicacy of flavour."
- **Pinus armandii** (Chinese white pine) -- Bears decorative cones containing seeds esteemed as a great delicacy.
- **Pinus cembra** (Swiss stone pine) -- Seed used in pastries and dairy food; contains edible oil. Cones do not release the seed.
- **Pinus maximartinezii** (Martinez pinon) -- Recent discovery from Mexico. Cones up to 30 cm long, containing very large (up to 2 cm) edible seeds said to be very tasty.
- **Pinus gerardiana** (Chilgoza pine) -- Native to the western Himalayas. One of the most important commercial pine nuts of India and Afghanistan.
- **Pinus koraiensis** (Korean pine) -- Important commercial source of pine nuts in Asia. Large seeds with high oil content.
- **Pinus sibirica** (Siberian pine) -- Native to Siberia. Seeds are harvested commercially and are an important food source.

## Araucaria Pine Nuts

### Araucaria araucana (Monkey Puzzle Tree)

Native evergreen of Chile and Argentina growing from 15-35 m. Prefers cool, moist, loamy soils and sheltered positions. Hot, dry conditions will often cause the tree to die. The nuts, called pinones, are twice the size of an almond and rich in starch. They are roasted, boiled, eaten fresh and also made into an alcoholic drink. Eighteen good sized trees are reported to be able to yield enough to sustain an adult for a year. Propagation is by seed and trees require [[holzer-frost-protection-techniques]] for the first couple of years.
