# Holzer Old Varieties and Seed Saving

[[comparison-sepp-holzer-vs-biodynamic]]'s approach to preserving and breeding old plant varieties, and his philosophy on seed sovereignty. From his Practical Guide to Small-Scale Integrative Farming and Gardening.

## Why Old Varieties Are Best

Holzer's experience shows that old varieties of cereal, vegetable, potato, and all cultivated plants make the best crops:

- **Better flavour** than modern overbred varieties
- **Less demanding** — deal better with local conditions
- **More disease resistant** — adapted to local conditions over generations
- **Higher nutritional value** — especially when grown at altitude

### Regional Adaptation

In earlier times, every country and region had their own cereal varieties, vegetables, potatoes, and herbs. There were numerous local varieties grown and propagated on individual farms. Through generational selection, these were bred to become the plants best suited to local conditions. This is why it is important to ask around locally to find out what varieties exist and then propagate them.

## Against Hybrid Seeds and Genetic Modification

### Hybrid Seeds (F1 Varieties)

- Bred so most can no longer reproduce — must always be bought new
- Do not breed true (cannot pass on characteristics consistently)
- Come from homozygous inbred lines; frequently sterile
- Not suited to varying local conditions
- Give good yields but are more susceptible to disease
- Combined with monoculture, lead to pesticide dependence
- Much to the satisfaction of [[spanish-cannabis-seed-companies]]

### Genetically Modified Seeds

Holzer calls genetic modification "a crime against nature." The results and repercussions cannot yet be foreseen and can barely be regulated. As a result of specialisation and modernisation, we have almost entirely lost the plant diversity we once had.

### EU Seed Regulations

In the EU, the propagation and marketing of seeds has been taken away from farmers and left to agricultural companies. Holzer considers this particularly questionable.

## The Krameterhof as Living Gene Bank

Once aware of seed sovereignty issues, Holzer made a great effort to propagate the seeds of every conceivable cultivated plant suited to the Krameterhof. The plant diversity provides a kind of living gene bank. Visitors are allowed to harvest seeds in small amounts for their own use during farm tours.

## Holzer's Selection Criteria

### Select from the Worst Conditions

Holzer's controversial but experience-based selection principle: **The seeds of the strongest plants, which grow on the worst soils and under the most extreme conditions, are the most suitable for propagation.**

This contradicts academic advice (which prefers seeds from the largest plants on the best soils). Holzer's reasoning:

- Seeds from the strongest plants on the poorest soils provide plants that can also deal with difficult conditions
- They are undemanding and still give satisfactory yields
- Plants bred from good-soil seeds deteriorate over time
- Seeds from extreme conditions have "positive energy" and establish themselves well

### Taste as Quality Indicator

- Nutritious, high-quality food develops an intense flavour
- Contains many valuable substances
- Works almost like a medicine, protecting people and animals from sickness
- Anyone with a sense of taste (partially protected from ready meals and fast food) can determine [[solomon-seed-quality-and-germination-testing]]

### Breeding Goals

- Hardier and more robust plants that can grow and thrive on their own
- Without constant support of fertilisers and water
- Always pay attention to taste when selecting

## Preventing Cross-Pollination

### Spatial Isolation

The numerous habitats and microclimates at the Krameterhof are useful for producing seeds by isolating related varieties spatially from each other.

### Physical Barriers

- Hedges and particularly tall [[comparison-raised-beds-vs-hugelkultur]] between varieties prevent cross-pollination
- Especially effective for wind-pollinated varieties
