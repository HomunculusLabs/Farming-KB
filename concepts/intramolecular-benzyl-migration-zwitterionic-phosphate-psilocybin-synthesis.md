## Context: The Phosphorylation Step

The phosphorylation of psilocin (1) with tetrabenzylpyrophosphate in THF at −78 °C to 0 °C in the presence of n-BuLi was intended to produce the O,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (7). Several phosphorylation methodologies were evaluated before selecting the pyrophosphate route:

- **Phosphoryl iodide method** (tribenzyl phosphite, I₂, DMAP): Consumed psilocin but produced complex mixtures requiring extensive purification.
- **Dibenzyl phosphorochloridate:** Low conversion and problematic side reactions.
- **Tetrabenzylpyrophosphate (selected):** High conversion, stable reagent, and the unexpected zwitterion formation that dramatically simplified purification.

Previous psilocybin syntheses had reported yields of just 20% (Hofmann et al., 1959) and 47% (Nichols & Frescas), both requiring either column chromatography or anion-exchange resin for the final purification step. ## Structural Elucidation by NMR

### ¹H NMR Evidence in CD₃OD

The ¹H NMR spectrum of 8 showed signals for two distinct benzyl groups with clearly differentiated chemical environments:

- **N-benzyl group:** Methylene protons at δ_H 4.56 (2H, s), shifted to high field due to attachment to the positively charged nitrogen center.
- **O-benzyl group:** Methylene protons as a characteristic AB quartet at δ_H 4.98 (1H, s) and 4.96 (1H, s), consistent with diastereotopic protons on an O-benzyl group.
- **Aromatic region:** Two sets of benzyl aromatic signals: δ_H 7.56–7.45 (5H, m, NCH₂C₆H₅) and δ_H 7.31–7.20 (5H, m, OCH₂C₆H₅).
- **[[indole]]:** Appeared as a doublet due to phosphorus coupling (split signal at δ_C 147.7), consistent with direct attachment of the phosphate group to the 4-oxygen.
- **N-benzyl ipso carbon:** δ_C 139.3 (Cs/OCH₂C₆H₅) for the O-benzyl, with a separate ipso carbon for the N-benzyl group at the quaternary ammonium.
- **Quaternary ammonium carbons:** The N-methyl carbons appeared at δ_C ~45–50 ppm, consistent with attachment to a positively charged nitrogen.

### ³¹P NMR

The ³¹P NMR spectrum confirmed the presence of a phosphate monoester moiety. The chemical shift was consistent with a monoester (one O-benzyl, one O⁻), ruling out alternative structures such as a pyrophosphate or phosphonate. This is critical because it confirms that the migration is a benzyl group transfer, not a phosphate transfer.

### 2D NMR: HMBC and NOESY

The definitive structural assignment relied on two-dimensional NMR experiments:

**HMBC (Heteronuclear Multiple Bond Correlation):** This experiment revealed long-range ¹H–¹³C correlations that established which benzyl group was attached to which atom. The key observation was a correlation from the high-field benzyl methylene protons (δ_H 4.56) to the N-methyl carbons of the dimethylaminoethyl side chain, confirming N-benzyl attachment. Conversely, the AB-quartet benzyl protons (δ_H 4.98, 4.96) showed HMBC correlations to C-4 of the indole ring, confirming O-benzyl attachment at the phosphate.

**NOESY (Nuclear Overhauser Effect Spectroscopy):** The NOESY spectrum provided spatial proximity information supporting the HMBC assignments. Through-space correlations between the N-benzyl protons and the N-methyl groups confirmed their close proximity on the quaternary ammonium nitrogen, while correlations between the O-benzyl protons and H-5/H-7 of the indole confirmed the 4-O-phosphate linkage.

### Choice of NMR Solvent

The initial NMR analysis was performed in CDCl₃, which proved problematic because the zwitterionic compound has very limited solubility in this solvent. The spectrum showed complicated, poorly resolved signals. Switching to CD₃OD dramatically improved spectral quality because the polar protic solvent dissolves the zwitterion. This solvent choice was critical for obtaining clean ¹H, ¹³C, and ³¹P spectra and enabling the 2D experiments (HMBC, NOESY) that established the structure. For the final psilocybin product, D₂O was used with a drop of MeOH as the ¹³C reference, reflecting the high polarity of the phosphorylated product.

## Proposed Mechanism of the Migration

The intramolecular benzyl migration likely proceeds through an S_N2-type process. After the initial phosphorylation places the dibenzyl phosphate on the 4-hydroxyl, one benzyl group on the phosphate is activated for migration. The nucleophilic tertiary amine of the N,N-dimethylaminoethyl side chain attacks the benzylic carbon, displacing the phosphate oxygen and forming the quaternary ammonium ion.

This migration is facilitated by three factors:

1. **Proximity:** The N,N-dimethylaminoethyl side chain is conformationally close to the 4-position of the indole ring, bringing the amine nitrogen near the benzyl groups on the phosphate.
2. **Leaving group ability:** The phosphate anion is a good leaving group for S_N2 displacement from the benzylic carbon.
3. **Thermodynamic stability:** The zwitterionic product is stabilized by internal charge separation and is poorly soluble in organic solvents, which drives precipitation and prevents further reaction.

The conversion can be achieved simply by suspending the worked-up reaction mixture in CH₂Cl₂ overnight, during which time the migration proceeds to completion and the zwitterionic product precipitates. This mild, spontaneous conversion is one of the practical advantages of the method.

## Practical Significance: Enabling Chromatography-Free Purification

The zwitterionic nature of compound 8 was the critical breakthrough that made gram-scale psilocybin synthesis practical without chromatographic purification. Because 8 is insoluble in CH₂Cl₂ while excess dibenzyl phosphate remains soluble, simple filtration followed by CH₂Cl₂ washing provides the pure intermediate in over 85% yield. This eliminates the need for preparative HPLC, column chromatography, or anion-exchange resin purification.
