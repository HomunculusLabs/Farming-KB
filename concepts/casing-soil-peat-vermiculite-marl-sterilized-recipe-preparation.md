# Casing Soil Preparation: Peat-Vermiculite-Marl Sterilized Recipe

## Overview

The GUILD cultivation guide specifies a particular casing soil recipe for
grass seed-based [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]. Notably, the authors advocate for
full sterilization of casing soil rather than the more common pasteurization
approach, directly contradicting authors who recommend pasteurization to
preserve [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]]. This page documents the recipe, preparation
method, and the authors' rationale for the sterilization approach.

## Recipe

The casing soil formulation uses three ingredients in dry form:

| Component | Parts | Role |
|-----------|-------|------|
| Peat | 10 | Primary organic matrix, moisture retention, pH buffer |
| Vermiculite | 5 | Aeration, water retention, structure |
| Limestone (Marl) | 2 | pH adjustment, calcium/magnesium source |

The 10:5:2 ratio produces a casing with good water-holding capacity,
adequate aeration, and appropriate pH for [[aquarium-humidity-chamber-mushroom-fruiting]]. Marl, a
calcareous mudstone containing clay and calcium carbonate, serves as both
a pH buffer and a source of essential minerals.

## Preparation Method

### Dry Mixing

All three ingredients are combined in dry form and thoroughly mixed. This
ensures even distribution of the limestone throughout the peat-vermiculite
matrix, preventing localized pH hotspots that could inhibit mycelial
colonization or promote contaminant growth.

### Hydration

While stirring, water is gradually added. The guide notes that the amount
of water depends on the moisture content of the peat, which varies
considerably between sources and batches. The target moisture level is
described as "as much water as possible without turning it into mud." This
field-expert description translates to approximately 60-70% water-holding
capacity — the casing should hold its shape when squeezed but release only
a few drops of water.

If the casing becomes too wet, additional dry ingredients are added to
correct the moisture level. This iterative approach requires some
experience to judge correctly.

### Packaging for Sterilization

The moistened casing soil is filled into:
- **Oven bags** (nylon)
- **Autoclave bags** (polypropylene, PP)
- **Glass jars**

Any of these containers works for the [[jarrold-pressure-cooker-sterilization-boil-bag-mushroom-cultivation]] step.

### Sterilization

The casing soil is sterilized for one hour in a pressure cooker at 15 psi
(121°C). After cooling to room temperature, it is ready for use.

## The Sterilization vs. Pasteurization Debate

### The GUILD Position: Full Sterilization

The guide authors explicitly reject pasteurization of casing soil:

> "We know that some authors advise against sterilisation of casing soil
> because it would kill all the beneficial organisms. We however have had
> only bad experiences with untreated or pasteurised casing soils."

Their position is purely empirical — they tried both approaches and found
that sterilized casing produced consistently better results in their
specific context of grass seed [[oyster-mushroom-non-sterilized-substrate-cultivation]] in disposable trays.
