
# Fungal Vaccines

## Overview

Despite the significant global burden of fungal infections — estimated at over 1.5 million deaths annually — no licensed fungal vaccines exist for human use as of 2026. This represents a critical gap in infectious disease medicine. Fungal vaccine development faces unique scientific and commercial challenges, but recent advances in immunology, genomics, and adjuvant technology have generated renewed optimism. This page reviews the current state of fungal vaccine research, challenges, and promising candidates.

## The Burden of Fungal Disease

Invasive fungal infections (IFIs) cause substantial morbidity and mortality worldwide:

- **Invasive candidiasis**: ~250,000 cases annually worldwide, mortality 30-60%
- **Invasive aspergillosis**: ~300,000 cases annually, mortality 40-80% in immunocompromised patients
- **Cryptococcal meningitis**: ~220,000 cases annually (primarily in HIV/AIDS patients), mortality near 100% without treatment
- **Pneumocystis pneumonia**: ~500,000 cases annually, primarily in immunocompromised hosts
- **Mucormycosis**: Rising incidence, mortality 40-80%

The populations at highest risk — immunocompromised patients (HIV/AIDS, transplant recipients, chemotherapy patients), the elderly, and those in intensive care — are often the least able to mount effective vaccine responses.

## Unique Challenges of Fungal Vaccine Development

### Biological Challenges

- **Fungi are eukaryotes**: Unlike bacteria and viruses, fungi share fundamental cellular biology with human cells. Many potential antigens have human homologs, raising concerns about autoimmune cross-reactivity
- **Diverse pathogenic mechanisms**: Fungal pathogens use varied strategies — tissue invasion, dimorphism, biofilm formation, immune evasion — making a universal approach difficult
- **Intracellular vs. extracellular phases**: Some fungi (Histoplasma, Cryptococcus) have intracellular phases that require cell-mediated immunity, while others (Candida, [[aspergillus]]) are primarily extracellular
- **Immune evasion**: Fungi have sophisticated mechanisms to evade host immunity, including capsule formation (Cryptococcus), biofilm formation (Candida), melanin production, and morphological switching

### Practical and Economic Challenges

- **Limited commercial incentive**: Pharmaceutical companies perceive fungal vaccines as low-profit compared to chronic disease therapeutics. The primary target populations (immunocompromised) are relatively small
- **Regulatory uncertainty**: No established regulatory pathway for fungal vaccines; no precedents for approval
- **Clinical trial complexity**: Endpoint definitions are challenging; patient populations are heterogeneous and often critically ill
- **Funding**: Historical underfunding of fungal disease research relative to bacterial and viral diseases

## Candida Vaccine Candidates

Candida species (especially C. albicans, C. glabrata, C. parapsilosis, C. tropicalis, and the emerging C. auris) are the most common causes of invasive fungal infection worldwide.

### NDV-3A (Vaxinnate/Novateur)

The most clinically advanced Candida vaccine candidate:

- **Antigen**: Recombinant N-terminus of Als3 protein (agglutinin-like sequence 3) from C. albicans
- **Platform**: Recombinant protein with alum adjuvant
- **Als3 function**: Cell surface adhesin and invasin involved in biofilm formation, epithelial adhesion, and iron acquisition
- **Clinical progress**: Completed Phase II trials showing immunogenicity and reduced recurrence of vulvovaginal candidiasis. Phase III planning for recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis
- **Spectrum**: Also shown protective activity against C. glabrata, C. tropicalis, and [[staphylococcus-aureus]] (due to structural similarity between Als3 and S. aureus adhesins)

### Other Candida Candidates

- **Sap2 vaccine**: Targeting secreted aspartyl protease 2, important for tissue invasion. Shown protective in animal models. Phase I completed in Italy
- **Beta-glucan conjugate vaccines**: Conjugating fungal beta-glucan cell wall components to carrier proteins. Beta-glucan is a pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP) that stimulates innate immunity through Dectin-1 receptor. Preclinical studies show broad protection against multiple Candida species
- **Mannan-based vaccines**: Mannan is a major component of the Candida cell wall. Mannan-tetanus toxoid conjugates have shown efficacy in animal models

## Aspergillus Vaccine Candidates

Invasive aspergillosis primarily affects immunocompromised patients, especially those with neutropenia or on immunosuppressive therapy. Prevention through vaccination would be transformative.

### ASP3976 (Astellas/GlaxoSmithKline)

- **Antigen**: Recombinant Asp f3 protein from [[aspergillus-fumigatus]]
- **Mechanism**: Asp f3 is a thiol peroxidase involved in oxidative stress defense
- **Status**: Completed Phase I trials demonstrating safety and immunogenicity in healthy adults
- **Target population**: Patients undergoing chemotherapy, stem cell transplantation, or lung transplantation

### Other Aspergillus Candidates

- **Crf1 vaccine**: Cytochrome c reductase iron-sulfur subunit from A. fumigatus. Protective in murine models
- **Pep1 vaccine**: A dipeptidyl peptidase antigen. Shown to reduce fungal burden in animal models
- **Live-attenuated strains**: Genetically attenuated A. fumigatus strains lacking virulence genes (e.g., laeA, pksP/melA) have shown promise in preclinical studies but raise safety concerns for clinical use
- **Beta-glucan conjugate vaccines**: Similar to Candida approaches, targeting the common fungal cell wall component

## Cryptococcus Vaccine Candidates

Cryptococcal meningitis kills approximately 180,000 people annually, primarily in sub-Saharan Africa among people living with advanced HIV/AIDS.

### Capsule-Based Approaches

The polysaccharide capsule is the primary virulence factor of [[cryptococcus-neoformans]]:

- **GXM conjugate vaccine**: Glucuronoxylomannan (the major capsular polysaccharide) conjugated to tetanus toxoid. Shown protective in animal models. Phase I trials demonstrated safety and immunogenicity
- **GXM-tetanus toxoid**: Generates antibodies that enhance opsonophagocytosis of Cryptococcus by macrophages
- **MP98 protein**: A mannoprotein antigen that stimulates T-cell responses. Shown protective in murine models

### Protein-Based Approaches

- **Cda1/Cda2 chitin deacetylases**: Surface proteins involved in capsule integrity. DNA vaccines encoding Cda1 have shown protection in mice
- **Eno1 (enolase)**: A glycolytic enzyme expressed on the Cryptococcus surface. Antibodies against Eno1 are protective in animal models
- **Hsp70 and Hsp90**: Heat shock proteins that are immunodominant antigens. Vaccine candidates using these proteins are in preclinical development

### Challenges Specific to Cryptococcus

- The target population (advanced HIV/AIDS) is severely immunocompromised and may not respond to vaccines
- T-cell responses are critical for protection but difficult to induce with protein subunit vaccines
- The polysaccharide capsule is poorly immunogenic and may even suppress immune responses

## Pneumocystis Vaccine Candidates

Pneumocystis jirovecii causes pneumonia in immunocompromised hosts:

- **Kex1 antigen**: A cell surface protease. Recombinant Kex1 has shown protective immunity in animal models
- **Msg (major surface glycoprotein)**: The most abundant surface antigen. Highly variable, making vaccine design challenging. Conserved regions are being targeted
- **Prt1 protease**: A surface protease that shows promise as a vaccine antigen

## Novel Vaccine Platforms

### mRNA Technology

The success of mRNA vaccines against SARS-CoV-2 has renewed interest in mRNA platforms for fungal vaccines:

- Rapid design and production cycles
- Strong induction of both humoral and cell-mediated immunity
- Can encode multiple antigens in a single formulation
- Preclinical studies with mRNA encoding Candida and Aspergillus antigens are underway

### Nanoparticle Vaccines

- Protein antigens displayed on self-assembling nanoparticle scaffolds
- Enhanced immunogenicity through multivalent antigen display
- Can incorporate adjuvants for targeted immune activation

### Live Attenuated Vaccines

- Genetically modified strains with deleted virulence genes
- Potentially more immunogenic than subunit approaches
- Safety concerns limit clinical applicability

## Future Directions

- **Universal fungal vaccines**: Targeting common antigens shared across multiple fungal pathogens (e.g., beta-glucan, heat shock proteins, chitin)
- **Combination vaccines**: Incorporating antigens from multiple fungi into a single formulation
- **Therapeutic vaccines**: Designed to boost immune responses in patients already infected
- **Improved adjuvants**: TLR agonists and other pattern recognition receptor agonists to enhance fungal-specific immune responses
- **Personalized approaches**: Tailoring vaccine strategies to individual patient risk profiles and immune status
