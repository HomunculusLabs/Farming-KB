## Overview

Honey bees (Apis mellifera) produce a range of valuable products beyond honey, including beeswax, propolis, royal jelly, pollen, and bee venom. Each product has distinct properties, uses, and market value. For homesteaders with hives, harvesting and processing these products adds diversity to farm income and provides materials for health, craft, and household use.

## Beeswax

### Properties and Harvesting

Beeswax is secreted by worker bees to build honeycomb. Melting point 144-149F, density lighter than water, naturally aromatic. Collected from honeycomb cappings (highest quality, lightest color) and old comb. Render by melting in a double boiler at 160-180F, straining through cheesecloth into molds. Yield: 1-2 lbs per 10 frames of honey.

### Uses

**Candles:** Burn cleaner, longer, brighter than paraffin. 1 lb makes 6-8 tapers. Market value: $8-15/lb finished candles.
**Kitchen:** Beeswax wraps (reusable alternative to plastic wrap), cheese waxing, cutting board conditioner.
**Cosmetics:** Lip balm, salves, lotion bars, deodorant base.
**Woodworking:** Furniture polish (beeswax + mineral oil), leather waterproofing, thread waxing.
**Agriculture:** Foundation for new frames, grafting wax.

Store in cool, dark place; shelf life is essentially indefinite.

## Propolis

Propolis is a resinous substance bees collect from tree buds to seal hive gaps and maintain hygiene. Composition: 50% resin, 30% wax, 10% essential oils, 5% pollen. Its antimicrobial properties make it valuable for health applications.

### Harvesting and Processing

- Use propolis traps (screens with gaps that bees fill) placed in the hive
- Freeze traps, then pop off brittle propolis
- Soak in 70-95% ethanol for 2-4 weeks, shaking daily, then strain for tincture
- Yield: 50-200g per hive per year

### Uses

- Immune support: 5-15 drops of tincture in water during cold season
- Wound healing and skin conditions: antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory
- Sore throat spray and oral health products
- Safety: possible allergic reactions (patch test first); not recommended during pregnancy

## Royal Jelly

A protein-rich secretion from worker bee glands, royal jelly is the sole food of queen larvae and causes genetically identical larvae to develop into queens.

### Composition

60-70% water, 12-15% protein (including royalactin), 10-16% sugars, 3-6% lipids, plus B vitamins, minerals, and acetylcholine.

### Harvesting and Storage

Requires grafting larvae into queen cell cups, then extracting jelly after 3 days. Skilled and invasive; most homesteaders purchase rather than harvest. Yield: 200-500g per hive per season.

Highly perishable: refrigerate (35-40F) for 6 months, freeze for 2 years, or freeze-dry for 3 years at room temperature. Used as dietary supplement (capsules, fresh frozen) and in skin creams for anti-inflammatory effects.

## Bee Pollen

Pollen grains collected by bees from flowers, mixed with nectar, and packed into pellets on their hind legs. Primary protein source for the colony.

### Nutritional Profile

20-30% protein (all essential amino acids), 30-55% carbohydrates, 1-10% lipids, plus B vitamins, vitamin C and E, minerals, enzymes, and antioxidants.

### Harvesting

- Pollen traps at hive entrance knock pellets into a collection tray
- Harvest during spring-summer pollen abundance; take no more than 30% of incoming pollen
- Yield: 1-5 pounds per hive per season
- Dry at 95-110F to below 8% moisture. Store in sealed jars in freezer for 1-2 years
- Uses: 1-2 tsp/day as nutritional supplement, in smoothies or baking, or as animal feed supplement
- Safety: start with very small dose if pollen allergies suspected; possible anticoagulant interactions

## Bee Venom Therapy

### What It Is

Bee venom therapy (apitherapy) is the therapeutic use of bee venom for health purposes. It has been practiced for thousands of years in traditional medicine systems, particularly in Eastern Europe, China, and Korea.

### Active Components

Bee venom contains approximately 18 pharmacologically active compounds:

- **Melittin** (50% of dry weight) — Anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, stimulates cortisol production
- **Apamin** — Neurotoxin that stimulates the central nervous system
- **Adolapin** — Anti-inflammatory and analgesic
- **Hyaluronidase** — Spreads the venom through tissue
- **Phospholipase A2** — Anti-inflammatory at low doses, pro-inflammatory at high doses
- **Mast cell degranulating peptide** — Modulates immune response

### Methods of Administration

1. Live bee sting — traditional method, bee held with tweezers at specific points
2. Bee venom injection — purified venom by medical professionals only
3. Bee venom acupuncture — injection at acupuncture points (Korea, China)
4. Topical preparations — creams and ointments for localized application
5. Electro-venom apparatus — collects venom without killing the bee

### Reported Applications

- Arthritis and joint pain — most widely studied; melittin shows anti-inflammatory effects
- Multiple sclerosis — mixed clinical evidence
- Chronic pain — melittin and adolapin have analgesic properties
- Immune modulation and skin conditions — ongoing research

### Safety and Risks

- Anaphylaxis risk: 1-5% of population is allergic; can be fatal
- Always have an epinephrine auto-injector available when using live bee stings
- Medical screening (skin test or RAST) is essential before beginning therapy
- Not a DIY therapy — requires trained practitioner supervision
- Contraindications: pregnancy, heart disease, kidney disease, tuberculosis

## Economic Considerations

| Product | Value per Pound | Annual Yield per Hive |
|---------|----------------|----------------------|
| Honey | $5-15 | 30-100 lbs |
| Beeswax | $8-15 (raw) | 1-2 lbs |
| Propolis | $20-40 (tincture) | 0.1-0.4 lbs |
| Pollen | $10-25 | 1-5 lbs |
| Royal jelly | $100-300 | 0.2-0.5 lbs |

Value-added processing multiplies raw product value: beeswax candles (3-5x), propolis tincture (2-3x), pollen health products (2-4x).
