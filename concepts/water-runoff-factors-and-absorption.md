# Water Runoff Factors and Absorption

## Overview

Water runoff is the movement of water over the land surface after rainfall, and understanding its causes, rates, and consequences is fundamental to [[water-conservation]].

### Seasonal Timing

Runoff is most destructive and erosive during specific seasonal windows:

- After long dry summers, when soils are cracked and hydrophobic.
- Following broadscale fires that remove vegetative cover.
- After extensive autumn or spring cultivation.
- Any period of landscape stress that reduces vegetative cover.

These are the periods when thunderstorms create gullies, flood thin soils, and cause soil collapse that can lead to salted soils. Designing for these critical periods is essential.

### Artificially Sealed Surfaces

Roads, parking areas, house roofs, bare rock, concreted open drains, and compacted areas of settlements all produce flash floods in nearby streams. The area of these total runoff systems within a catchment must be calculated if effective flood retardation systems are to be planned. In urban and peri-urban [[cover-crops]] as rich side-dressing.
- Place restricted water exits opening into much wider drains (silt traps), which later lead into broad reed beds.
- Lead fairly clear water from reed beds to dams or swales sited well away from the stream line.
- Never site dams above valley settlement areas.
- Swales can be a few kilometres long, reaching from one fast stream to the next, growing extensive forest lines with only occasional silt clearing.
