# Double Cover Air-Inflated Plastic Trial: Temperature Data

## Overview

[[eliot-coleman]] conducted a controlled experiment comparing single-layer and double-layer air-inflated plastic coverings on unheated greenhouses (cold houses) at [[coleman-double-layer-protection-greenhouse-row-cover]] layers over the crops.

## Key Temperature Findings

The data showed that nighttime low temperatures averaged **4°F (2.2°C) warmer** in the air-inflated house than in the single-layer house.

### Specific Cold Night Comparison

On a night when the [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]]**: Light becomes increasingly important as days lengthen and growth accelerates

## The Operational Decision

Based on this trial, Coleman established a clear protocol:

> "We began double-covering the cold houses where we would be sowing new crops from December 15 to February 15. With the rest of the cold houses, such as one that protects leeks for midwinter harvest, we continue with our inclination in favor of simplicity and better light input and use only a single sheet of plastic to cover the house."

This was not an all-or-nothing decision but a strategic allocation of resources based on the specific needs of different crops at different times.

## The 4-Inch Air Space Advantage

Coleman noted that the 4-inch dead-air space between the two layers of plastic provides significant insulation:

> "According to greenhouse research, that 4-inch dead-air space between the layers of plastic can lower our fuel consumption by up to 40 percent."

While this statistic specifically applies to heated greenhouses, it illustrates why the air-inflated double cover is so effective even without heat — the trapped air layer is an excellent insulator.

## Anti-Drip Coatings

Coleman discussed the importance of plastic cover selection, particularly anti-drip coatings:

> "Plastic covers are available with an anti-drip coating that causes condensed moisture to form a thin film instead of droplets."

Benefits of anti-drip coatings:
- Let in more light (droplets scatter light; films transmit it)
- The thin moisture film reflects back heat waves radiating from the soil at night
- Helps keep the air inside the house warmer through additional heat retention

This creates a dual benefit: more light during the day and more heat retention at night.
