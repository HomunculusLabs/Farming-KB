# Woronin Bodies and Hyphal Septation

Woronin bodies are specialized peroxisome-derived organelles unique to
the Euascomycetes (Pezizomycotina) that maintain cellular integrity of
[[environmental-sensing-filamentous-fungi-read]]. They function as emergency plugs for septal pores,
sealing off damaged hyphal compartments to prevent catastrophic loss of
cytoplasm. Their discovery and functional analysis represent a
fascinating chapter in [[chitin-chitosan-fungal-cell-wall]] biology.

## The Multicellular Fungal Hypha

Fungi are one of three [[basidiomycota-classification-major-clades]] of eukaryotic life that independently
evolved multicellular organization, radiating into terrestrial and aquatic
niches from symbiotic to saprobic to pathogenic. Hyphae grow through
polarized tip-extension of a tubular cell, partitioned by cross-walls
called septa.

Phylogenetic analysis reveals four major groups: early-diverging
Chytridiomycota and Zygomycota, and the Ascomycota and Basidiomycota,
sister clades containing the majority of species. Vegetative hyphae in
Ascomycota produce perforate septa at regular intervals, a trait also
found in Basidiomycota, suggesting presence in their common ancestor.
As hyphae grow, branch, and fuse, they form a multicellular network. This
hyphal syncytium, or [[penicillium-chrysogenum]], shows that Woronin
bodies rapidly plugged 90% of septal pores near induced damage; only 5%
were plugged in undamaged hyphae.

## Structure and Properties

Woronin bodies are generally oval, centred on an electron-dense protein
matrix circumscribed by a single membrane. Diameter varies between 100 nm
and greater than 1 micrometre, generally exceeding septal pore diameter.
In most Euascomycetes they are oval, but some species appear hexagonal.

Woronin bodies are physically tethered to the septal pore by filamentous
material visible by electron microscopy. When drawn away by laser-induced
optical traps, they return to their original position, demonstrating a
tethering mechanism.

## Septal Pore Ultrastructure

The septal pore regulates cytoplasmic continuity between adjacent
compartments. It is surrounded by a specialized cell wall swelling
(septal pore rim) providing the anchor for [[hex1-protein-woronin-body-crystal-structure-euascomycetes]] tethering. In
many species, the rim contains additional proteinaceous structures
including retention apparatus and membrane-bound caps.

Pore diameter typically ranges from 50 to 500 nm depending on species and
hyphal age, allowing passage of cytoplasm, organelles, and nuclei while
remaining small enough for Woronin body sealing. The pore-associated
endoplasmic reticulum may play a role in lipid metabolism and Woronin
body biogenesis. Basidiomycetes lack Woronin bodies entirely, instead
possessing dolipore septa with septal pore caps (parenthesomes),
representing an independent evolutionary solution.

## HEX-1: The Core Structural Protein

Woronin bodies have been purified from
[[magnaporthe-grisea]], where the mutant is additionally defective in
appressorium morphogenesis and
[[fungal-hyphae-invasive-growth-mechanisms]] within the plant host, and
displays hyphal death in response to nitrogen starvation. Nitrogen
starvation and growth in planta regulate HEX-1 splice-variants, showing
the Woronin body provides Magnaporthe with an important defence system,
potentially regulated through alternative splicing.

## Role in Fungal Pathogenesis

Woronin bodies contribute significantly to virulence. In Magnaporthe
oryzae (rice blast), hex1 deletion mutants show reduced pathogenicity
because damaged appressoria cannot maintain [[bloomfield-turgor-pressure-and-hyphal-invasion]] for host
penetration. Loss of cytoplasmic integrity at the infection site directly
compromises the mechanical force driving the penetration peg through the
plant cuticle.
