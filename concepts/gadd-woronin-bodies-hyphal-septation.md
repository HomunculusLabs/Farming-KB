# Woronin Bodies and Hyphal Septation Woronin bodies are specialized peroxisome-derived organelles unique to the Euascomycetes (Pezizomycotina) that play a critical role in maintaining cellular integrity of filamentous fungi. These remarkable structures function as emergency plugs for septal pores, sealing off damaged hyphal compartments to prevent the catastrophic loss of cytoplasm. ## Structure and Properties

Woronin bodies are generally oval in shape and centred on an electron-dense
matrix circumscribed by a single membrane. The matrix is composed of protein.
Woronin body diameter varies between 100 nm and greater than 1 micrometre
and is generally greater than septal pore diameter. In most Euascomycetes
the Woronin body is oval, but in some species it presents a hexagonal
appearance.

Woronin bodies are physically tethered to the septal pore by filamentous
material visible by electron microscopy. When drawn away from the septum by
laser-induced optical traps, they return to their original position upon
release, demonstrating the existence of a tethering mechanism.
