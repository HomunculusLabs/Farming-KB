# Immobilized Fungal Bioreactors for Wastewater Treatment

Immobilization of fungal cells or enzymes in solid supports is a key
strategy for enhancing the efficiency and stability of bioremediation
processes. Immobilized bioreactors offer advantages including higher cell
densities, easier biomass-liquid separation, protection of cells from
toxic shocks, and the potential for repeated use over multiple treatment
cycles. The use of immobilized fungal cells or enzymes in beads or
carriers has achieved good success in the treatment of various industrial
wastewaters.

## Immobilization Methods

Several immobilization techniques are employed for [[fungal-mycelium-hyphae-and-the-feeding-phase]] can attach to the surface of carrier materials or
penetrate porous carriers, forming stable biofilms. Common carrier
materials include Celite, ceramic honeycomb supports, jute rope, and
plastic surfaces. An internal airlift loop reactor with a ceramic
honeycomb support for biofilm (IAL-CHS) using Candida tropicalis has
demonstrated effective treatment of high-carbohydrate wastewater,
accumulating dense biomass and producing significant amounts of
single-cell protein.

### Covalent Binding

Enzymes can be covalently coupled to solid supports for enhanced
stability. Oxirane-immobilized [[lentinula-edodes]] laccase removed about
70% of total phenols and 88% of ortho-phenols from olive mill wastewater
in 2 hours of operation. A significant regain (60%) of the immobilized
activity was restored by washing the column with buffer, suggesting the
possibility of further use of the immobilized catalyst.

## Applications in Olive Mill Wastewater Treatment

Olive mill wastewater (OMW) has been extensively treated using
immobilized fungal bioreactors. Key findings include:

- Immobilized P. chrysosporium on polyurethane foam achieved repeated
  batch treatment over three cycles without sporulation or lysis.
- Laccase of L. edodes immobilized on chitosan operated in six
  consecutive batches with significant removal of color, total phenols,
  and ortho-phenols.
- Oxirane-immobilized L. edodes laccase in a fluidized-bed bioreactor
  showed reduced efficiency in successive batches, but activity was
  restored using acetate buffer.
