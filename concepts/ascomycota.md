# Ascomycota

The Ascomycota is the largest phylum of the [[fungal-taxonomy-and-kingdom-fungi]], with approximately
32,000 described species. It is characterized by the production of meiospores
(ascospores) within sac-shaped cells called asci (singular: ascus). The
phylum includes an extraordinary range of organisms, from single-celled yeasts
to complex [[bloomfield-truffle-evolution-cup-fungi-hypogeous]] and truffles, from plant pathogens to the organisms that
give us penicillin and brewer's yeast. Some of the most economically and
[[biodiversity-medically-important-fungi-isolation]] on Earth belong to this phylum.

## Defining Characteristics

The defining feature of the Ascomycota is the ascus — a specialized cell in
which [[basidiomycete-mating-systems-sexual-reproduction]] occurs through the formation of ascospores. Typically,
each ascus contains eight ascospores (though this number varies), produced
through a process involving meiosis followed by a mitotic division. The asci
are often contained within [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]] called ascomata (formerly
ascocarps), which take diverse forms including cups (apothecia), flasks
(perithecia), and closed spheres (cleistothecia).

Many ascomycetes also reproduce asexually through the production of conidia
(mitospores), which are dispersed by wind, water, or animals. In some species,
the asexual (anamorphic) stage was described and named separately from the
sexual (teleomorphic) stage before their connection was recognized, leading
to a dual nomenclature system that is still being resolved.

## Three Major Classes

The Ascomycota is divided into three major classes based on molecular
phylogenetic analyses of rDNA and RNA polymerase II sequences:

### Class Archiascomycetes

The Archiascomycetes is a paraphyletic assemblage of basal taxa. It was
recognized based on phylogenetic analyses of rDNA sequence data and includes
yeastlike, filamentous, and possibly sporocarp-producing species.

The largest order is the **Taphrinales**, which includes approximately 100
species of plant-pathogenic fungi. Taphrinales lack both sporocarps and
ascogenous hyphae. Species in the order are dimorphic, having a saprobic
yeast phase and a filamentous pathogenic phase. This life-history trait is
shared with certain basidiomycetes (such as rusts), leading some mycologists
to hypothesize that the order represents a primitive lineage.

Another notable member is *Pneumocystis jiroveci*, an organism once
classified as a protozoan but now recognized as an ascomycete fungus. It is
an agent of pneumonia, particularly in immunocompromised patients.

### Class Saccharomycetes (True Yeasts)

The Saccharomycetes comprises the true yeasts — fungi that reproduce
primarily by budding rather than by forming hyphae. This class is of
enormous economic and medical importance:

- ***[[saccharomyces-cerevisiae]]***: Brewer's and baker's yeast, one of the
  most studied organisms in all of biology and a cornerstone of the
  biotechnology industry
- ***[[bloomfield-candida-albicans-yeast-hyphal-dimorphism]]***: A facultative human pathogen causing candidiasis
  (thrush and yeast infections), particularly in immunocompromised individuals
- Numerous species of endosymbionts of arthropods, associating with insects
  and other animals as mutualists

The Saccharomycetes forms a monophyletic clade that shares a most recent
common ancestor with the Euascomycetes. No true yeast produces a sporocarp,
and the class generally lacks ascogenous hyphae, though some species display
limited filamentous growth. Members share distinctive ultrastructural features
in nuclear division and ascospore delimitation.

### Class Euascomycetes

The Euascomycetes is the largest and most diverse class of Ascomycota. It
includes the major lineages of filamentous, sporocarp-producing taxa and
their equally diverse asexual (anamorphic) relatives. It is arguably the most
successful group of fungi, including:

- **Parasites and pathogens** of plants, algae, and animals
- **Mutualists** forming beneficial associations with other organisms
- **Saprobes** capable of decomposing virtually all known organic substrata

Major orders within the Euascomycetes include:

- **Pezizales**: Cup fungi and truffles, including many edible species
