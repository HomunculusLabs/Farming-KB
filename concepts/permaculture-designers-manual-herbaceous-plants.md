GLOBE ARTICHOKE (Cynara scolymus): A deep-rooting perennial that provides crop, mulch, and opens soil for tree root access. Listed as a standby crop in desert gardens alongside asparagus. Its tall flower heads attract pollinators.

CHICORY (Cichorium intybus): A deep-rooted perennial that spikes the soil, listed among the "spike roots" that open compacted soil and encourage earthworms. Used in earthwork site preparation mixes.

DANDELION (Taraxacum officinale): Another spike-root species that covers ground and encourages worms, yielding mulch and crop (edible leaves and flowers). Soil below its foliage is soft, free-draining, open to roots feeding near the surface, and cool.

NASTURSIUM (Tropaeolum majus): A soft ground cover that prevents soil drying and gives mulch. Nasturtium roots provide root chemicals to tomatoes or gooseberries which deter whitefly. Planted to define garden edges.

LEMONGRASS (Cymbopogon citratus): In tropical systems, lemongrass clumps provide erosion control on contour and bund stabilisation. It spreads by low hedges along with comfrey, and provides mulch material.

## Ground Covers

Herbal ground covers replace grass in orchard and food forest systems. The manual recommends maximising the floristic or flowering components of interplants:

- Spring bulbs (Narcissus, hyacinth, Allium triquetrum): flower and die back, creating grass-free zones
- Spike roots (comfrey, dandelion, globe artichoke): cover ground, encourage worms
- Insectary plants (Umbelliferae, Compositae): attract predatory insects
- Nitrogen-fixers (clovers, tagasaste, Acacia)
- Soft ground covers (Nasturtium): prevent soil drying

Ground covers are especially critical in the early years of orchard establishment when tree canopies are small and sunlight reaches the soil surface. Without ground cover, weed invasion and soil erosion are significant problems. A well-designed herbal understory eliminates the need for mowing, mulch import, and weeding in the mature system.

## Insectary Herbs

Small-flowered plants attract predatory wasps, robber flies, ladybirds, and pollinator bees. Key families:

- Umbelliferae: fennel, dill, Queen Anne's lace, carrot, coriander, parsnip flowers
- Compositae: tansy, marigold, small daisy varieties
- Lamiaceae: catnip
- Other: Phyla (Lippia) nodosa, Echium fastuosum, Salvia spp., Kniphofia spp., Fuchsia spp.

Culinary herb associates: tomatoes with parsley and basil; potatoes with mint; dill with cucumbers and apples. These arrangements reduce work by enabling grouped harvesting for cooking, salads, or processing. ## Tropical Herbaceous Plants

In the tropical home garden: forage and ground legumes provide green mulch and help suppress grasses, as do comfrey and lemongrass. Chilies, peppers, and a range of tropical vegetables are preferred to temperate species. Amaranth is a good green and grain crop. Herbs include ginger, turmeric, galangal, and other tropical aromatics.
