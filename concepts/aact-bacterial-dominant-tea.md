# AACT Bacterial Dominant Tea

Updated: 2026-04-18

Bacterial [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] tea (AACT) formulation that promotes high bacterial biomass relative to [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] is needed for fast-growing annual plants
- Soil is currently bacterial-dominated and needs reinforcement

Bacterial teas are NOT sufficient for controlling mildew, which requires adequate fungal biomass.

## Core Recipe

The basic bacterial dominant AACT recipe follows these proportions:

Compost base: Use a mix of thermal (hot) compost and worm compost. The compost must be E. coli-free, meaning it was properly composted (reached temperature for required time). Use approximately 1 pound of compost per 5 gallons of water.

Foods for bacteria:
- Unsulfured blackstrap molasses: 1-4 tablespoons per 5 gallons (the primary bacterial food)
- Fish hydrolysate: 1-2 ounces per 5 gallons (provides amino acids and bacterial nutrition; Organic Gem and Neptune's Harvest are recommended brands)
- Kelp meal or liquid kelp: 1 tablespoon per 5 gallons (provides micronutrients and bacterial stimulants)

Do NOT use humic acid or fish oil if you want to keep the tea bacterial-dominant, as these favor fungal growth.

## Brewing Process

1. Start with chlorine-free water (dechlorinate by aerating or letting sit for 24 hours)
2. Add the compost to the tea brewer basket
3. Add bacterial foods to the water
4. Brew for 18-24 hours with continuous aeration
5. Maintain [[aact-brewing-ingredients-ingham]]
