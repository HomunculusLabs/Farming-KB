# Enoki Cultivation Guide

Enoki (Flammulina velutipes), also known as winter mushroom or enokitake, is a popular edible mushroom prized for its long, slender stems and small caps. The wild form looks entirely different from the cultivated supermarket version — commercial enoki is grown in high-CO2, low-light conditions that produce the characteristic white, elongated morphology.

## Overview

Enoki is one of the most commercially cultivated mushrooms in the world, particularly in Japan, China, and Korea. It is a cold-loving species that naturally fruits in late autumn through winter on dead hardwood. Its ability to fruit at near-freezing temperatures makes it unique among cultivated mushrooms and ideal for winter production.

## Species Biology

- Scientific name: Flammulina velutipes
- Synonym: Collybia velutipes
- Wild habitat: dead or dying hardwood trees (elm, ash, poplar, persimmon)
- Natural fruiting temperature: 32-55 F (0-13 C)
- Wild appearance: amber to brown cap, short thick stem, velvet-textured stipe base

The dramatic difference between wild and cultivated forms is entirely environmental. High CO2 (5000-10000 ppm) and near-dark conditions suppress cap development and stretch the stems, while cold temperatures slow growth for a denser, crispier texture.

## Substrate Preparation

### Formula

Standard commercial enoki substrate:

- Hardwood sawdust: 70-75%
- Wheat bran or rice bran: 20-25%
- Corn cob meal (optional): 5%
- Gypsum: 1-2%
- Moisture content: 63-68%

Enoki is less tolerant of high supplementation than oyster mushrooms. Keep bran below 25% to avoid contamination and soft, stringy fruiting bodies. Sawdust from broadleaf hardwoods (beech, oak, maple) works best.

### Sterilization

Enoki is sensitive to bacterial contamination. Sterilize at 15 PSI for 2-3 hours. Some commercial operations use 18 PSI for 1.5 hours. Ensure complete sterilization — bacterial contamination is the most common failure point.

### Container Options

- Polypropylene bottles with narrow necks (standard in Japanese commercial production)
- Cylindrical poly bags with restricted openings
- Wide-mouth jars (home cultivation)

Bottles produce the most uniform, market-quality mushrooms. The narrow neck creates a micro-environment of high CO2 around the growing mushrooms.

## Inoculation and Spawn Run

Inoculate sterilized substrate in clean conditions:

- Spawn rate: 5-10% grain spawn by weight
- Incubation temperature: 68-75 F (20-24 C)
- Incubation time: 20-30 days
- Conditions: dark, still air

The mycelium is white to cream-colored and colonizes aggressively. Full colonization is indicated by a firm, white block that has pulled slightly away from the container walls.

## Pinning Induction

Once fully colonized, enoki requires specific environmental triggers:

1. Cold shock: reduce temperature to 37-46 F (3-8 C)
2. Remove or loosen lids to allow slight air exchange
3. Maintain near-total darkness (less than 50 lux)
4. Relative humidity: 90-95%

A 7-10 day cold shock period is standard. Some growers transfer blocks to a separate cold room for pinning before returning to the growing room.

## Fruiting Phase

### Environmental Parameters

- Temperature: 37-46 F (3-8 C) — warmer than this produces shorter stems
- Humidity: 85-95% — too low causes drying; too high promotes bacterial rot
- CO2: 5000-10000 ppm — critical for long stem development
- Light: near darkness (less than 50 lux) — light causes browning and cap expansion
- Air velocity: minimal — drafts cause irregular growth

### The CO2 Effect

Maintaining elevated CO2 is the key technique in enoki cultivation. In a sealed or barely-vented bottle, the mushrooms' own respiration creates a CO2-rich environment that:

- Dramatically elongates the stem (up to 5-6 inches)
- Keeps caps small (1/4 to 1/2 inch diameter)
- Produces the white color (absence of light prevents pigmentation)
- Creates a tight, dense cluster at the bottle mouth

Home growers can achieve this by covering the bottle mouth with a loosely placed cap or plastic wrap with small holes.

### Growth Period

From pin formation to harvest is typically 15-20 days at optimal temperatures. Slower growth at lower temperatures produces denser, crisper mushrooms with better shelf life.

## Harvesting

Harvest the entire cluster by grasping at the base and gently pulling. Do not cut individual stems — the cluster comes out as a single unit. Commercial enoki is often packaged with the root mat (base) intact for presentation.

- Yield: typically 150-250 grams per 1 kg dry substrate (15-25% BE)
- First flush accounts for 70-80% of total yield
- Second flushes are possible but lower quality and higher contamination risk

## Post-Harvest

Fresh enoki has a shelf life of 2-3 weeks refrigerated — one of the longest among cultivated mushrooms. Store at 35-38 F in a sealed container. The cold-growing physiology makes it naturally resistant to cold damage.

Enoki can be dried but this is uncommon as the texture is the primary culinary attribute. It is excellent in soups, hot pots, stir-fries, and salads (after blanching).

## Common Problems

- Bacterial contamination: most common issue; improve sterilization
- Brown discoloration: too much light or temperature too high
- Short stems: CO2 too low or temperature too high
- Stringy, tough texture: temperature fluctuation or high supplementation
- Mite infestation: maintain clean growing area

## Nutritional Value

Enoki is low in calories and rich in:

- Dietary fiber (beta-glucans)
- B vitamins (thiamin, riboflavin, niacin)
- Ergothioneine (antioxidant)
- Protein (approximately 2.8g per 100g fresh weight)
