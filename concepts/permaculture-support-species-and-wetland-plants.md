# Permaculture Support Species and Wetland Plants

Support species and wetland plants form the backbone of functional permaculture systems. Bill Mollison's *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual* catalogues dozens of these species across common names S through Y and the species index (A–), illustrating their roles in nitrogen fixation, water management, erosion control, food production, and guild design.

## Nitrogen-Fixing Support Species

Nitrogen fixers are among the most important support species in permaculture. They convert atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available forms through symbiotic relationships with rhizobial bacteria (in legumes) or actinomycetes (in non-legumes like *Casuarina* and *Alnus*). Mollison emphasises using them as canopy nurses, understory companions, and green-manure crops.

### Tree and Shrub Nitrogen Fixers

- **Acacia spp. (Wattle)** — Pioneering nitrogen-fixing trees widespread across tropical, subtropical, and temperate zones. Wattles fix nitrogen, provide mulch, shade, and windbreaks, and many produce edible seeds or useful timber. *Acacia aneura* (mulga) and *A. mearnsii* (black wattle) are especially noted.
- **Siberian pea shrub (*[[caragana-arborescens]]*)** — A hardy, cold-climate deciduous shrub that fixes nitrogen and produces edible protein-rich seeds. Excellent as a windbreak and poultry forage in zones 2–4.
- **Tagasaste (*[[chamaecytisus-palmensis]]*)** — Also known as tree lucerne, this fast-growing leguminous shrub thrives in Mediterranean climates. It provides high-protein fodder for livestock, abundant nectar for bees, and nitrogen enrichment for adjacent crops.
- **Sesbania** — Rapid-growing, often short-lived leguminous trees or shrubs used as green manure, shade for young crops, and in alley-cropping systems. *Sesbania grandiflora* produces edible flowers and leaves.
- **Tipuana tipu** — A large South American leguminous tree valued for shade, nitrogen fixation, and timber. Common in subtropical street plantings and larger permaculture landscapes.
- **Sissoo (*Dalbergia sissoo*)** — A nitrogen-fixing timber tree of the Indian subcontinent, excellent for agroforestry and reforestation on degraded land.

### Herbaceous and Groundcover Nitrogen Fixers

- **Silverleaf Desmodium (*Desmodium uncinatum*)** — A tropical legume used widely as a cover crop and intercrop. Its strong allelopathic effect suppresses striga weed in maize systems, and its creeping habit provides excellent soil cover.
- **Siratro (*[[macroptilium-atropurpureum]]*)** — A vigorous tropical/vining legume used for pasture improvement, erosion control, and green manure. Deep-rooted and drought-tolerant once established.
- **Stylo (*Stylosanthes*)** — Persistent tropical pasture legume that thrives on poor, acidic soils. Valuable for grazing systems and soil stabilisation.
- **White clover (*Trifolium repens*)** — The temperate-zone groundcover nitrogen fixer par excellence. Ideal under fruit trees in food forests, between garden rows, and as living mulch.
- **Vetch (*Vicia* spp.)** — Cool-season climbing legume used as green manure, cover crop, and fodder. Common vetch and hairy vetch are widely sown in temperate systems.
- **Sissoo (*Dalbergia sissoo*)** and other leguminous trees round out the woody nitrogen-fixing canopy in tropical guilds.

## Guild Design with Support Species

Mollison's guild concept places nitrogen fixers as essential structural elements. A typical fruit-tree guild might pair a **walnut** or **service berry** canopy with **caragana** as a nurse shrub, **white clover** as living mulch, and **comfrey** or **vetch** as dynamic accumulators. In tropical systems, **acacia** or **sesbania** nurse young fruit trees through establishment, then are coppiced for mulch. **Tagasaste** provides a cut-and-carry fodder bank while fixing nitrogen for adjacent pasture.

The key principle is that support species perform multiple functions simultaneously — nitrogen fixation, biomass production, insectary habitat, wind protection, and microclimate moderation — reducing the need for external inputs.

## Wetland and Aquatic Plants

Water-edge and aquatic plants serve critical functions in permaculture: water purification, nutrient cycling, food production, habitat creation, and erosion control. Mollison treats [[constructed-wetlands-wastewater-treatment]] aquaculture as integral system components.

### Food-Producing Aquatic Plants

- **Water chestnut (*Eleocharis dulcis*)** — Grown in shallow ponds or paddy margins; the corms are a staple crop across East and Southeast Asia.
- **Taro (*Colocasia esculenta*)** — A wetland staple producing edible corms and leaves. Thrives in swampy areas and constructed wetlands.
- **Watercress (*Nasturtium officinale*)** — Nutrient-dense leafy green growing in flowing water; excellent in spring-fed channels or greywater treatment systems.
- **Water lily (*Nymphaea* spp.)** — Edible rhizomes and seeds in some species; provides shade and habitat in ponds.
- **Wild rice (*Zizania* spp.)** — Native North American aquatic grass harvested as a high-protein grain from shallow lakes and constructed wetlands.
- **Water hyacinth (*[[eichhornia-crassipes]]*)** — Extremely productive aquatic plant; used for biogas production, animal fodder, and heavy-metal bioaccumulation. Must be managed carefully as it can become invasive.
- **Water lettuce (*Pistia stratiotes*)** — Floating plant useful for nutrient stripping in greywater systems and as duck fodder.

### Erosion Control and Water Purification

- **Willow (*Salix* spp.)** — Riparian powerhouse for bank stabilisation, water table management, and fodder. Willows coppice readily and their root systems bind stream banks effectively.
- **Tamarisk (*Tamarix* spp.)** — Salt-tolerant, deep-rooted shrub/small tree used in arid-zone riparian restoration and windbreaks. Note: invasive in some North American watersheds and should be selected with local ecological awareness.
- **Sedge (*Scirpus, Cyperus*)** — Important wetland grasses that filter nutrients, trap sediments, and stabilise pond edges.
- **Sea grasses (*Posidonia, Zostera*)** — Submerged marine angiosperms critical for coastal stabilisation, nursery habitat, and carbon sequestration.

## Medicinal and Culinary Herbs

Mollison's appendices include numerous culinary and medicinal herbs that serve dual roles as nectar plants, pest deterrents, and ground covers:

- **Thyme (*Thymus* spp.)** — Low-growing aromatic groundcover; attracts beneficial insects and has antiseptic properties.
- **Wormwood (*Artemisia absinthium*)** — Bitter aromatic herb used as a companion plant for pest deterrence and in traditional medicine.
- **Verbena** — Attracts pollinators; some species used medicinally and as tea herbs.
- **Violet (*Viola* spp.)** — Edible flowers and leaves; shade-tolerant groundcover for forest gardens.
- **Tea (*Camellia sinensis*)** — Evergreen shrub suitable for humid subtropical zones; integrates into agroforestry as a cash crop.
- **Turmeric (*Curcuma longa*)** — Tropical rhizome spice with powerful anti-inflammatory properties; grown as an understory crop in food forests.
- **Sorrel (*Rumex* spp.)** — Perennial leafy green with a tart flavour; useful as a permanent garden bed plant.

## Zone Integration

Mollison's zone model provides the spatial framework for placing support and wetland species:

- **Zone 1** (home garden): Spinach, Swiss chard, tomato, snow pea, strawberry, sweet potato, herbs (thyme, basil, turmeric in warm climates). White clover as pathways mulch.
- **Zone 2** (food forest edge): Service berry, Siberian pea shrub, garlic, tagasaste (as hedge), vetch as winter groundcover, strawberry as living mulch.
- **Zone 3** (orchard/farm): Acacia nurse trees, tagasaste fodder belts, caragana windbreaks, desmodium cover crops between tree rows, sisal for fibre on marginal slopes.
- **Zone 4** (forage/wild): Silky oak, tamarisk, wattles, willow along waterways, sesbania for rapid soil building.
- **Zone 5** (wilderness): Sea grape on coasts, tamarack in boreal wetlands, yucca in arid zones, native sedges and sea grasses.

## Timber, Fibre, and Multi-Use Species

Several species in this section serve industrial or structural functions within permaculture systems:

- **Teak (*Tectona grandis*)** — Premium tropical timber; suitable for long-rotation agroforestry.
- **Silky oak (*Grevillea robusta*)** — Fast-growing timber and shade tree; nitrogen-fixing root associations benefit understory crops.
- **Sisal (*Agave sisalana*)** — Fibre crop for marginal, arid lands; provides rope, twine, and erosion control on slopes.
- **Yucca** — Multi-use arid-zone plant providing fibre, food (flowers, fruit), and soap from saponins.
- **Waxberry** — Produces edible fruit and useful wax coatings.
- **Tung nuts (*Vernicia fordii*)** — Source of tung oil for wood finishing; integrates into subtropical agroforestry.

## Key Food Crops

The S–Y section also encompasses many staple food crops that anchor permaculture food production:

- **Sugar cane** — High-biomass tropical grass; provides sugar, ethanol, mulch material, and windbreaks.
- **Sunflower** — Multi-purpose annual: edible seeds, oil, pollinator attraction, phytoremediation, and biomass.
- **Watermelon** — Productive summer groundcover that suppresses weeds while producing food.
- **Yam (*Dioscorea* spp.)** — Important tropical root crop; climbing vine that can be trained on support trees.
- **Walnut (*Juglans* spp.)** — High-value nut tree; note its allelopathic juglone effect when designing guilds — avoid planting sensitive species beneath walnut canopies.
- **Wheat** — Staple cereal; in permaculture, often integrated with legume rotations (vetch, clover) for soil fertility.
- **Tomato** — Summer annual; benefits from [[companion-planting-with-fungi]] basil and marigolds.

## Species Index Context

The beginning of Mollison's species index (Abelmoschus esculentus through Amelanchier) cross-references common names to their botanical Latin, reinforcing the importance of precise botanical identification in permaculture design. Notable early index entries include **Okra** (*Abelmoschus esculentus*), **Firs** (*Abies*), **Acacia spp.**, **Kiwi** (*Actinidia*), **Agave**, **Amaranthus**, and **Aloe** — all plants with significant permaculture applications ranging from food and fibre to medicinal use and soil stabilisation.

## Design Principles

When selecting support species and wetland plants, Mollison emphasises several principles:

1. **Multiple functions** — Every species should serve at least three roles (e.g., tagasaste: nitrogen fixation, fodder, windbreak).
2. **Native and adapted** — Choose species suited to local climate, soil, and ecology; use natives for restoration zones.
3. **Stacking** — Layer nitrogen fixers at canopy, shrub, herbaceous, and groundcover levels.
4. **Water cycling** — Position wetland plants to slow, spread, and sink water across the landscape.
5. **Succession** — Use fast-growing pioneers (sesbania, acacia) to prepare sites for slower climax species.
6. **Edge effect** — Maximise productive edges between water, land, and different vegetation types.
