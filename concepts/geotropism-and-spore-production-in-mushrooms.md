## Role of the Veil

Badham speculated that the partial veil (the tissue connecting the cap
margin to the stipe before the cap opens) may serve to protect the upper
portion of the stipe from wind and light during the early anemotropic
growth phase. By shielding the stipe from these stimuli, the veil
prevents premature geotropic correction before the cap is ready to open
and spores are ready for release.

When the veil ruptures at maturity, the upper stipe is exposed to the
full environmental stimulus, and the geotropic correction occurs in
coordination with cap expansion and spore production. This timing
mechanism ensures that the mushroom orients for spore dispersal only
when spores are actually available to be dispersed.

## Adaptive Significance of Geotropism

Plunkett (1961) suggested that negative geotropism is particularly
important for lignicolous (wood-dwelling) and coprophagous (dung-
dwelling) mushroom species. These species grow on substrates that are
often oriented at various angles or in unstable positions. The ability
to correct cap orientation regardless of the emergence angle ensures
effective spore dispersal even from vertically oriented or inverted
substrates.
