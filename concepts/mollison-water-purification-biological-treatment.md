# Mollison: Water Purification and Biological Treatment

Source: Bill Mollison, Permaculture: A Designers' Manual (Chapter 7)

## The Principle of Biological Water Treatment

Mollison stresses that the only long-term insurance of good water supply to a settlement is rigorous control of a forested catchment, including a total ban on biocides and metallic processing.

Since few clean catchments remain, house roof tanks must suffice for the foreseeable future.

The fundamental principle is that the only water safe for people is also safe for other living things.

Biological treatment of common contaminants is emphasised over technological fixes, as pollution treated by further pollution represents an endpoint of technological dependency.

## Categories of Water Contaminants

Mollison identifies six primary categories of contaminants affecting drinking water:

### Turbidity

Silt and fine particles suspended in water reduce clarity and light penetration.

Turbidity is addressed by settling, filtration, and biological uptake.

### Bacterial and Organic Pollution

Sewage-derived contaminants include E. coli, disease organisms, viral and protozoan pathogens, and parasitic worm eggs.

These are addressed through biological treatment stages including anaerobic, facultative, and aerobic pond systems.

### Metallic Pollutants

Chromium, cadmium, lead, and mercury are primary concerns.

Acid rain dissolves poisonous forms of aluminium, mercury, lead, cadmium, and selenium from rocks and soils.

Organisms may convert inorganic mercury to organic forms (as occurred in Minamata, Japan) which are readily absorbed by the body.

Sewage in water aids conversion to biologically active metals.

### Biocides

Aldrin, Dieldrin, 2,4-D, 2,4,5-T, dioxin, PCB, organophosphates, and halogenated hydrocarbons persist in water systems and accumulate in food chains.

Aldrin prevents DDT from being excreted; the combination is deadly and both are available as commercial mixes.

### Excessive Fertiliser

Nitrogenous compounds, phosphates, sodium, and potassium salts cause eutrophication of water bodies.

Excessive artificial fertiliser increases aquatic biological activity, resulting in further uptake of metals in acidic waters.

### Acids

Acid-forming compounds with pH less than 5.5 increase metallic pollution and dissolve toxic metals from geological substrates.

## Water Treatment Methods

### Aeration

Aeration is achieved by wind, mechanical means, increasing turbulence in flow, trickle columns, vegetation, phytoplankton, or injected air.

Intermittent aeration at two hours in six (30% of the time) is most efficient for facultative ponds, following an A.A.A. (alternating aerobic and anaerobic) digestion pattern.

### Sand Filtration

Classical sand filters use 1.2 metres of sand (top half fine, bottom half coarse) and are effective even in temporary rural camps.

For cities, fixed sand beds with brick bases are used, with the top 1 cm periodically swept, removed, and dried or roasted to remove organic particles.

Trickle filters through sand and gravel columns feed resident bacteria that remove surplus nutrients.

### Activated Charcoal

Activated charcoal from bones, willow, or coconut husks is used as a fine filter for water purity.

A final treatment stage trickles water through a column of 10% active carbon and 90% silicon dioxide (burnt rice, oat, or wheat husks).

### Biological Removal

Bacteria, phytoplankton, and higher plants remove nutrients and metals from water.

Freshwater mussels filter up to 100 litres per day individually, digesting bacteria and depositing wastes in mud.

Mussels are sensitive monitors: they die at pH 5.5 and are very sensitive to biocides such as Dieldrin, making their living presence a constant pollution indicator.

### Coagulation and Flocculation

Lime, salt, and ferric sulphate are added to cause fine particles to settle.

Crushed marble or limestone as a layer in a tank, or cast burnt lime over a pond, flocculates particles causing them to settle.

Shells and crushed shells in water tanks and ponds achieve the same effect.

### pH Adjustment

Adding calcium (as lime) or sulphur compounds adjusts pH to optimal ranges for biological treatment.

## The Multi-Stage Filtration System

Mollison outlines a complete gravity-fed treatment sequence:

Trickle filters of loose pebbles create an active bacterial surface to absorb nutrients.

A sand filter absorbs bacterial pollution.

Water rising through a sand column emerges fairly clear.

A bed of watercress removes dyes and nitrates.

A final trickle through active carbon and silicon dioxide produces clear, sparkling, safe drinking water.

No machinery is involved if the system is laid out downslope to permit gravity flow.
