# Hurricane Preparedness Guide

Hurricanes are among the most powerful and destructive natural events, bringing sustained winds of 74+ mph, storm surge, torrential rainfall, tornadoes, and prolonged power outages. Homesteads in coastal regions from Texas to Maine face direct hurricane risk, while inland areas can receive devastating rainfall and flooding from hurricane remnants. This guide covers storm preparation, protection, evacuation, and recovery.

## Understanding Hurricane Categories

The Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale:

| Category | Wind Speed | Damage Potential |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 74-95 mph | Some damage — roof, siding, trees, power lines |
| 2 | 96-110 mph | Extensive damage — major roof/siding damage, trees blocked roads, prolonged power loss |
| 3 | 111-129 mph | Devastating — structural damage, trees snapped, multi-day power outage |
| 4 | 130-156 mph | Catastrophic — severe structural damage, area uninhabitable for weeks |
| 5 | 157+ mph | Total destruction — most structures destroyed |

Note: Categories measure wind speed only. Storm surge and rainfall flooding often cause more damage than wind.

## Storm Shutters and Window Protection

Windows are the most vulnerable point on a building during a hurricane. Once a window fails, wind enters the structure, creating internal pressurization that can blow off the roof.

### Permanent Shutters

**Accordion shutters:** Permanently mounted, fold out from side tracks. Fast to deploy (minutes). Durable. Moderate cost. Good for frequent storm areas.

**Roll-down shutters:** Electric or manual roll-down aluminum or steel. Most convenient (push-button deployment). Highest cost. Best protection. Requires professional installation.

**Colonial shutters:** Decorative louvered shutters that close over windows. Aesthetically pleasing. Manual deployment with bolts. Moderate cost.

**Bahama shutters:** Propped-open awning-style shutters that drop down and latch. Provide shade and storm protection. Manual deployment. Moderate cost.

### Temporary Protection

**Plywood panels:** The budget option. Use 5/8-inch exterior-grade plywood (CDX minimum). Cut panels to fit each window with a 4-inch overlap on all sides. Pre-install anchors (Hilti bolts or WingIts) so panels can be mounted quickly. Mark each panel for its window. Store panels in a dry, accessible location.

**Storm panels:** Corrugated aluminum or steel panels that bolt into pre-installed tracks. Lighter than plywood, reusable, and very strong. Lower cost than permanent shutters. Requires pre-installed mounting hardware.

**Polycarbonate panels:** Clear panels (Lexan) that allow light through while protecting windows. More expensive than plywood but lighter and reusable.

### Installation Priority

1. Windows facing the most likely wind direction (typically east and south for Atlantic hurricanes)
2. Large windows and glass doors (sliding glass doors are extremely vulnerable)
3. Gable-end windows (these receive the most wind pressure)
4. All remaining windows
5. Garage doors (brace with a vertical brace kit — garage door failure causes major structural damage)

## Before Hurricane Season (June-November)

- Trim trees and remove dead branches — especially limbs near structures and power lines
- Clear gutters and downspouts
- Review insurance coverage (hurricane deductible is often separate from standard deductible — usually 2-5% of home value)
- Photograph and inventory all property, structures, and contents
- Stock emergency supplies (see below)
- Test generator and ensure adequate fuel supply
- Install or prepare storm shutters
- Reinforce garage door with a hurricane-rated brace
- Secure or store outdoor items that could become projectiles (furniture, tools, trash cans, trampolines)
- Review and practice evacuation plan
- Ensure all family members know the plan and rally points
- Check emergency communication equipment

## Emergency Supplies

### Water and Food (Minimum 7-Day Supply)
- Water: 1 gallon per person per day (drinking, cooking, hygiene) — 7 gallons minimum per person
- Non-perishable food that requires no cooking: canned goods, granola bars, dried fruit, nuts, crackers
- Manual can opener
- Camp stove with fuel (for outdoor cooking only — never indoors)
- Water purification: bleach (unscented, 8 drops per gallon), purification tablets, or filter
- Cooler and ice for perishables

### Power and Light
- Generator with adequate fuel (minimum 20 gallons stored in approved containers)
- Extension cords (heavy-duty, outdoor-rated)
- Flashlights and headlamps (one per person)
- Lanterns (battery-powered or propane)
- Batteries (store multiple sizes)
- Solar phone charger or portable battery bank
- Candles and matches (use with extreme caution — fire risk)

### Communication
- Battery-powered or hand-crank NOAA weather radio
- Charged cell phone with car charger
- Whistle for signaling
- Ham radio or GMRS radio (see [[homestead-communication]])
- Written emergency contact list

### First Aid and Health
- Complete first aid kit (see [[homestead-first-aid-guide]])
- Prescription medications (30-day supply minimum)
- Mosquito repellent
- Sunscreen
- Hand sanitizer
- Toilet paper and sanitation supplies (bucket toilet, heavy-duty bags, kitty litter)
- Trash bags (heavy-duty, 55-gallon)

### Tools and Materials
- Tarpaulins (multiple, 10x20 feet minimum) for roof damage
- Duct tape (multiple rolls)
- Rope and paracord
- Basic tool kit (hammer, nails, screws, saw, pry bar, wrenches)
- Chainsaw with fuel and chain oil (for clearing debris)
- Axe and bow saw

## Evacuation Planning

### When to Evacuate
- If local authorities issue an evacuation order — obey it immediately
- If you live in a mobile home or temporary structure — always evacuate for hurricanes
- If you live in a storm surge zone (Category 1-5 zones)
- If your home is not structurally sound or is not adequately protected
- If you have medical needs that require electricity or refrigeration

### Evacuation Route Planning
- Identify primary and alternate routes to inland shelter
- Leave early — roads become gridlocked as storms approach
- Fill vehicle fuel tank before evacuating
- Know the location of pet-friendly shelters
- Notify family and friends of your plan and destination
- Take your go bag, important documents, and irreplaceable items

### If You Shelter in Place
- Shelter in an interior room away from windows (closet, bathroom, hallway)
- Stay away from windows, doors, and exterior walls during the storm
- Do not go outside during the eye of the storm — the second half may be worse
- Monitor weather radio for storm updates
- Keep your emergency supplies in your shelter room

## Protecting Livestock and Animals

- Evacuate livestock early — trailers cannot be safely towed in hurricane winds
- Move animals to the most sturdy, well-built shelter available
- Ensure access to feed and water for 7+ days (water bowls may blow away — secure them)
- Do not leave animals in pastures that may flood from storm surge or rainfall
- Tag or mark all animals with identification
- Have veterinary first aid supplies ready (see [[homestead-first-aid-guide]])
- After the storm, check animals for injuries and provide clean water immediately
