# Tropical Grassland Rehabilitation

Tropical grasslands represent one of the most challenging rehabilitation problems in the
wet-dry tropics. These degraded landscapes result from burning and over-grazing sequences
that have replaced native forests with rank grasses, creating conditions of soil erosion,
low-nutrition forage, and fire-prone vegetation. Once deforested, the pastures are exposed
to summer winds, and the critical nutrient cycle of trees, grass, and browsing animals
is broken. Fire only accelerates this destructive process. Although very few trees can
survive in established tropical grasslands, the re-establishment of tree legumes is
essential for any meaningful rehabilitation.

## The Problem of Destructive Grazing

Heavy cattle browsing is identified as a major cause of pasture deterioration and soil
loss. Extensive grazing on the agribusiness "cowboy/pyromaniac" model is described as
the most common destructive use of tropical lands. The first essential step in
rehabilitation is relieving the land of excessive cattle pressure. Mollison argues that
no nation can support destructive grazing agriculture in the long term, as such systems
prove uneconomic by any measure.

## Grass-Defeating Strategies

[[coleman-cover-crop-rotation-and-soil-health]] legumes like Desmodium and Suratro reduce grass competition and lay down mulch.
Short-stemmed Desmodium can defeat grasses under trees but competes with young trees for
dry-season moisture, requiring supplemental irrigation. Under the shelter of banana grass
or elephant grass (Pennisetum), fast-growing leguminous trees establish and provide
green mulch once they grow tall enough.

## Legume Tree Species for Nitrogen Fixation

The key [[permaculture-plants-legume-trees-acacia-carob-locust]] identified for tropical rehabilitation include:

- **Albizzia** (Albizia): Capable of fixing substantial nitrogen even in poor sandy
  soils, with estimated contributions as high as 250-500 kg N/ha/year for efficient
  legumes.
- **Acacia** (A. mearnsii, A. melanoxylon, A. auriculiformis, A. mangium): Important
  pioneer species for exhausted savannah soils. Acacia auriculiformis can defeat blady
  grass (Imperata cylindrica), restore fertility, provide firewood, and serve as a nurse
  crop. A. mangium is straight-stemmed and better suited to forestry operations.
- **Inga** ([[leucaena-leucocephala]]): A versatile legume for forage, green mulch, and
  nitrogen fixation. The low-mimosine varieties from Australia are preferred for fodder.
- **Gliricidia**: A coppicing species useful in mixed pioneer clump plantings.
- **[[calliandra-calothyrsus]]**: A stick-wood coppicing species that defeats grasses and
  provides abundant firewood while repairing exhausted soils.

## Nitrogen Fixation and Soil Building

Most legumes, along with genera like alder and Casuarina, have mycelial root associates
that fix atmospheric nitrogen. As organisms and roots undergo constant death and
replacement, nitrogen is released for other species. The amount fixed has been
underestimated at 75-100 kg N/ha/year. Efficient legumes like lucerne may provide
250-500 kg N/ha/year, and tree legumes such as Albizzia achieve similar rates even in
poor sandy soils.

Cut green material from these trees (green mulch) supplies much of the nitrogen needed
for crops. Trees must be properly inoculated with correct root associates. Nitrogen
distributed around root zones continues to diffuse for up to 6 years after a tree is cut.
This slow release is critical in tropical conditions where artificial nitrogen quickly
leaches in warm rains.

## Elephant Grass Nurse Crops

Elephant grass (Pennisetum purpureum) serves as a nurse crop for establishing legume
trees. Cliff Adam at Grand Anse, Seychelles, demonstrated a productive system using
Pennisetum atropurpureum (7 parts) plus Leucaena leucocephala (1 part), supporting
seven milk cows per acre with cut-and-carry feeding. All manure was returned to the
irrigated field, reducing imported artificial fertilizers to one-tenth.

## The Nucleus Pioneer Coppice Method

The core strategy for tropical grassland rehabilitation is the steady establishment of
clumped pioneer trees in open grassland — a process that duplicates [[fukuoka-natural-seeding-volunteer-rice-discovery]] by
fruit pigeons and frugivorous birds. Dense nucleus plantings (1-2 meter spacing) of mixed
legumes, palms, shrubs, [[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos]], and bulbs, combined with stone or stick mulch,
quickly shade out grasses and produce closed canopy systems.
