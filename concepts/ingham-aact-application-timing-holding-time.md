# AACT Application Timing and Holding Time Guidelines

**Source:** The Field Guide I for [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] Tea — Elaine Ingham (2008)

## Core Concept

The effectiveness of actively aerated compost tea (AACT) depends critically
on when it is applied relative to brewing, and how long the organisms
survive after application. AACT is a living product — the beneficial
microorganisms it contains have finite lifespans and specific environmental
requirements. Applying tea at the right time, in the right conditions, and
understanding the holding time window are essential for achieving the
intended benefits.

## Maximum Holding Time After Brewing

AACT must be used within a specific window after brewing to ensure the
organisms remain viable and aerobic:

### The 4-Hour Rule
Ingham's primary guideline is that compost tea should be applied within 4
hours of the end of the brewing cycle. During this window, the organisms
are at peak biomass and [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]]. The tea should remain aerobic
(dissolved oxygen above 5.5 ppm) throughout this period.

### Extended Holding Under Active Aeration
If the tea remains on active aeration, the window extends to 8-12 hours.
However, after food sources are consumed, organism diversity begins to
shift. After 12 hours, the community may no longer match the intended use.

### Holding Without Aeration
If aeration stops, the tea must be applied immediately. Without oxygen
input, dissolved oxygen drops rapidly as organisms respire. Anaerobic
conditions can develop within 1-2 hours, depending on temperature and
organism density. Tea that has gone anaerobic (indicated by sour or
putrid odor) should not be applied — it may contain harmful organisms
and metabolites that can damage plants.

## Environmental Conditions for Application

### Temperature
Apply tea when temperatures are moderate:
- **Soil drench**: 50-80°F (10-27°C) soil temperature
- **Foliar spray**: Apply in early morning or late evening when air
  temperatures are below 80°F (27°C) and rising dew provides natural
  moisture on leaf surfaces
- Avoid application during hot afternoon sun — UV radiation and heat kill
  exposed organisms within minutes

### Moisture
- **Foliar application**: Apply when dew is present or during light rain.
  Organisms need a thin film of moisture on the leaf surface to survive and
  colonize. Spraying during dry, sunny conditions wastes tea — organisms
  desiccate before establishing.
- **Soil drench**: Apply to moist soil. Dry soil desiccates organisms
  before they can establish. If soil is very dry, water lightly before
  applying tea.

### Light Exposure
- Organisms are sensitive to UV radiation. Foliar applications should avoid
  direct sun — early morning, late evening, or overcast days.
- Soil drenches are less affected. Mulch or light irrigation after
  application protects surface organisms.

## Application Timing by Crop Stage

### Pre-Planting
- Apply AACT to seed beds 1-2 weeks before planting to establish beneficial
  communities in the root zone
- Particularly effective for disease-prone crops — beneficial organisms
  occupy infection sites before pathogens arrive

### At Planting / Transplanting
- Drench transplant holes with AACT to inoculate the root zone immediately
- Reduces transplant shock by establishing a protective [[cannabis-cultivar-microbial-community-effects]]
  around stressed roots
- Use 1-2 cups of tea per transplant hole for standard vegetables

### Vegetative Growth
- Monthly foliar sprays during active growth maintain phyllosphere
  (leaf surface) [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]]
- Soil drenches every 4-6 weeks maintain rhizosphere populations
- Time applications to coincide with rapid growth periods when plants are
  most demanding of nutrients

### Pre-Flowering
- Apply 2-3 weeks before expected flowering to establish protective
  communities on flowers and fruit surfaces
- Helps prevent botrytis and other flower/fruit pathogens

### Post-Harvest
- Apply to crop residues to accelerate decomposition and suppress
  pathogen carryover
- Helps break down remaining [[savory-soil-organic-matter-and-decomposition-dynamics]] recycle nutrients

## Application Rates

### Foliar Spray
- Standard dilution: undiluted tea applied at 20-30 gallons per acre
- Home garden: 2-5 gallons of undiluted tea per 1,000 sq ft
- Coverage should be thorough — all leaf surfaces (top and bottom) should
  receive a fine mist, not large droplets

### Soil Drench
- Standard rate: 10-20 gallons per acre, applied to the root zone
- Home garden: 1-2 gallons per 100 sq ft
- Apply slowly to allow infiltration rather than runoff
- For [[aact-banana-cultivation-tropical-crop-disease-suppression-ingham]], increase to 30+ gallons per acre for initial
  treatment, then reduce to maintenance rates

### Seed Treatment
- Soak seeds in undiluted tea for 15-30 minutes before planting
- Coats seeds with beneficial organisms that protect against soil-borne
  pathogens during germination

## Survival After Application

### Foliar Organisms
Organisms applied to leaf surfaces face harsh conditions: UV radiation,
desiccation, temperature extremes, and limited nutrients. Survival time
ranges from hours to several days depending on conditions. To maximize
establishment:
- Apply with a sticker-spreader to improve adhesion to waxy leaf surfaces
- Apply during humid conditions or when dew is present
- Avoid washing off with overhead irrigation for at least 24 hours

### Soil Organisms
Organisms applied to soil survive longer — typically days to weeks. Soil
provides physical protection, moisture retention, and organic matter for
food. However, survival depends on:
- Existing soil biology (competition from resident organisms)
- Soil moisture (dry soil kills organisms quickly)
- Soil temperature (extreme heat or cold reduces survival)
- Food availability (organisms need organic matter to sustain populations)

### Establishing Permanent Populations
AACT applications do not establish permanent populations with a single
application. Repeated applications at appropriate intervals are necessary
to maintain beneficial organism levels. Over time, if soil conditions are
favorable (adequate organic matter, minimal disturbance, proper moisture),
introduced organisms can establish self-sustaining populations that reduce
the need for ongoing applications.
