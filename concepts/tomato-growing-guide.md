# Tomato Growing Guide

Tomatoes ([[solanum-lycopersicum]]) are the most popular home garden crop. This guide covers the key distinctions between varieties, [[pruning-techniques-organic]], support systems, [[clubroot-disease-management]], and harvesting for maximum productivity.

## Determinate vs. Indeterminate

| Feature | Determinate | Indeterminate |
|---------|------------|---------------|
| Growth habit | Bush, stops at fixed height | Vine, keeps growing until frost |
| Height | 2-4 feet | 6-12+ feet |
| Fruit set | All at once, short window | Continuous throughout season |
| Pruning | Minimal or none | Regular sucker removal recommended |
| Staking | Cages or short stakes | Tall stakes, string trellis, or tall cages |
| Best for | Canning, sauce, containers | Fresh eating, continuous harvest |
| Varieties | Roma, San Marzano, Patio, Celebrity | Brandywine, Cherokee Purple, Sungold, Sweet Million |

## Starting Tomatoes

### Seed Starting
- Start indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost.
- Germination: 70-80°F soil temp, 5-10 days.
- Transplant to larger pots when seedlings have 2-3 sets of true leaves.
- Harden off for 7-10 days before planting outside.

### Planting
- Set transplants in the ground after all frost danger has passed and soil is at least 60°F.
- Nighttime temperatures should be consistently above 50°F.
- Plant deep: bury stem up to the first set of true leaves — tomatoes root from the stem. Up to 2/3 of the plant can be buried.
- Space determinates 18-24 inches apart; indeterminates 24-36 inches apart (closer with trellising).
- Add a handful of bone meal or rock phosphate to each planting hole.
- Water in well with diluted compost tea or fish emulsion.
- Install supports (stakes or cages) at planting time — root damage from later installation can set plants back.

## Pruning Suckers (Indeterminate Only)

Suckers are the small shoots that grow in the "V" between the main stem and a branch.

### How to Prune
- Pinch or cut suckers when they are 2-4 inches long (smaller = less stress on plant).
- Remove all suckers below the first flower cluster.
- For a single-stem plant: remove ALL suckers. Requires strong support (string trellis).
- For a double-stem plant: allow one sucker below the first flower cluster to grow as a second leader.
- Stop pruning 30 days before expected first frost (allow plant to finish ripening existing fruit).

### Why Prune
- Improves air circulation (reduces disease).
- Redirects energy to fruit production rather than excess foliage.
- Larger, earlier fruit on pruned plants.
- Easier to manage and harvest.

### When NOT to Prune
- Determinate varieties — pruning reduces yield.
- In extremely hot climates — foliage protects fruit from sunscald.
- If disease pressure is low and plants are healthy.

## Staking and Caging

### Staking
- 6-8 foot wooden or metal stakes, driven 12-18 inches into ground.
- Tie main stem to stake with soft ties (twine, cloth strips, tomato clips).
- Best for: indeterminate varieties trained to 1-2 stems.
- Pros: Clean fruit, good airflow, space-efficient.
- Cons: Labor-intensive, needs regular tying.

### Caging
- Wire tomato cages (conical or rectangular), 48-60 inches tall for indeterminates.
- Concrete reinforcing wire cages (5-6 ft tall, 18-24 in diameter) are the gold standard — sturdy, reusable, and large enough for full plants.
- Pros: Minimal maintenance, no tying needed.
- Cons: Takes up more space, fruit can be hidden inside.

### String Trellis (Florida Weave)
- Stout posts every 8-10 feet, with string woven between posts in a figure-8 pattern around plants.
- Common in commercial production; works well for intensive home gardens.
- Allows plants to be spaced 12-18 inches apart.

## Nutrient Needs

- **Nitrogen**: Important early for vegetative growth, but excess N after flowering produces lush foliage with few fruits.
- **Phosphorus**: Critical for flowering and fruit set — add bone meal at planting.
- **Potassium**: Essential for fruit quality and disease resistance — kelp meal or wood ash.
- **Calcium**: Prevents blossom end rot — gypsum or crushed eggshells.
- Side-dress with compost or balanced [[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof]] when first fruits set.
- Avoid high-nitrogen liquid feeds after flowering begins.

## Watering

- Consistent, deep watering is critical — fluctuations cause blossom end rot and fruit cracking.
- Target 1-2 inches per week from rain and irrigation combined.
- Water at the base, not overhead — wet foliage promotes blight and other diseases.
- Drip irrigation is ideal.
- Mulch heavily (3-4 inches) to maintain consistent soil moisture.

## Common Diseases

### Early Blight (Alternaria solani)
- Brown spots with concentric rings on lower leaves; progresses upward.
- Caused by soil splash onto leaves.
- Prevention: mulch heavily, remove lower leaves, avoid overhead watering.
- Treatment: copper-based fungicide or biological sprays (Serenade/Beauveria).

### Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
- Rapidly spreading brown/black lesions on leaves and fruit; can destroy plants in days.
- Favors cool, wet conditions.
- FAMOUS for causing the Irish Potato Famine.
- Prevention: good airflow, avoid overhead watering, resistant varieties.
- If detected: remove and destroy infected plants immediately. Do NOT compost.

### Fusarium and Verticillium Wilt
- Soil-borne fungi; yellowing and wilting starting on one side of the plant.
- No cure once infected.
- Prevention: choose VFN-resistant varieties, rotate crops 3-4 years.

### Blossom End Rot
- Black sunken spot on blossom end of fruit.
- Caused by [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]] or inconsistent watering (calcium cannot move through the plant).
- Prevention: consistent watering, adequate calcium (gypsum, eggshells), mulch.

## Harvesting

- Pick when fruit is fully colored but still firm.
- Tomatoes ripen best on the vine, but can ripen indoors if picked at the "breaker" stage (first blush of color).
- Store at room temperature — do NOT refrigerate (chilling kills flavor and texture).
- Green tomatoes at end of season will ripen indoors in a paper bag (check daily).
- Determinate varieties: harvest window of 2-3 weeks.
- Indeterminate varieties: continuous harvest from mid-summer to frost.
