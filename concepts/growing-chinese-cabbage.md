## Growing Chinese Cabbage

Chinese cabbage is a close relative of mustard and has an almost irresistible tendency to go to seed before forming a head. Growing it successfully requires top-quality seed, highly fertile soil that is always moist, and precise knowledge about when to plant.

## The Bolting Problem

Breeders have not yet fully overcome the bolting trait. A variety that pretends to this ability: any variety claiming to be suitable for early summer harvest is extremely questionable. Harvest midsummer is nearly as dubious.

**Advice**: Do not sow this crop until after the summer solstice. In mild-winter climates, wait two months. Where the crop may be grown over winter, sow it in time to be harvested before the worst cold. If sown too late, growth will slow and it may put up a seedstalk, or quality may deteriorate.

## Growing Requirements

### The Three Essentials

1. **Top-quality seed** — use a quality hybrid if possible
2. **Highly fertile soil that is always moist** — speed of growth is everything
3. **Correct planting time** — after summer solstice in most climates

### Soil and Spacing

- Make the soil as rich as you know how and keep it moist
- Don't crowd the crop or competition will slow growth
- Each plant needs about 4 square feet (3,700 sq cm)
- Put 4 seeds in the bottom of a 1-foot (30 cm) diameter mound atop an especially well-fertilized spot
- Thin to a single plant at emergence — do not try to transplant because it forms a taproot and never grows as well without one
- Keep this in mind if considering purchase of garden center seedlings

### Fertility

Consider Chinese cabbage a high-demand crop. COF ([[complete-organic-fertilizer]]) worked into the bed before sowing, plus side-dressing as plants grow, will support rapid head formation.

## Varieties

### OP vs. Hybrid

- **OP varieties**: Be prepared in advance to have over half your plants fail to head before bolting
- **[[cervantes-hybrid-varieties-types]]**: Nearly every plant will make a perfect head
- **Santoh**: A non-heading variety that compares to Chinese cabbage as collards do to European cabbage. Much easier to grow.

### Recommended Approach

If you want a continuous supply, sow two heads every two weeks over a few months rather than one large sowing.

## Pests and Diseases

- All the usual brassica pests may pester it
- In Cascadia, the cabbage maggot has an especially nasty way of feeding on Chinese cabbage: the maggots tunnel across the bases of the leaves, collapsing them and rotting the plant
- When growing in maggot-prone areas, consider growing under a spun fabric (row cover) tent to exclude the fly
- All the usual brassica pest remedies apply

## Harvest

If when harvesting, you do not cut off the entire base, but instead allow a few of the larger outer leaves to remain attached to one side of the plant, the plant will continue putting up small leaves and then seedstalks. Both the small leaves and the upper portion of seedstalks are good in salads or stir-fries.

Thus a non-heading plant or early bolting is not a total loss if you remember that the seedstalk portions are edible if cut before the flowers open.

## Saving Seed

Chinese cabbage is a biennial brassica, bee-pollinated. It will cross with other brassicas in the same species. Overwinter plants and allow them to go to seed the following spring. Isolate from other flowering brassicas of the same species.

## Growing Conditions
Success depends on matching varieties to local climate and
season length. Soil temperature, day length, and frost dates
all influence [[fall-winter-planting-schedules]]. Start with proven varieties
for your region before experimenting with unusual types.
Maintain consistent soil moisture during establishment.

## Common Challenges
Pest pressure, disease, and [[ectomycorrhizal-community-environmental-stress]] are the most
frequent challenges. Use row covers for insect protection and
crop rotation to break [[hamilton-pest-and-disease-control-organic]] cycles. Proper spacing
improves [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] and reduces [[aact-foliar-disease-control]]. Early
detection and intervention prevent minor issues from escalating.
