### The compost sock

A porous bag made from large pantyhose (size Q) eliminates the need to strain tea before spraying. The waist stretches around the bucket top; legs fall into the water holding the compost. Or tie the legs in a knot and fill the bag with compost to sit in the water.

## Site and temperature

Room temperature is ideal. Too cold slows microbial activity; too hot cooks or dormants microbes. Keep the brewer away from direct sunlight — UV rays kill microbes. Track water temperature for lab reference. An aquarium heater with automatic thermostat or ice may be needed to maintain proper range.

## Ingredients

### Water (Rule #12)

Chlorine-free water is essential. Chlorine and preservatives kill microbes. If your water supply uses chlorine, run air bubbles through the water for 1-2 hours to evaporate it. Carbon filters and reverse osmosis systems also remove chlorine and chloramines. As a general rule, one cubic foot of carbon filters four gallons per minute.

### Compost

Use good, finished compost — give it the sniff test. If it does not smell good, it is not good compost. Avoid "almost compost" that has not finished the process, anaerobic compost, or overheated compost that killed beneficial microbes. If compost has low microbial diversity, the tea will too.

Vermicastings are an excellent substitute, especially when fresh — they are full of beneficial microbes and tend to be very bacterial. Use approximately 4 cups per 5-gallon brew.

### Bacterial foods

Nonsulfured molasses (powdered or liquid), cane syrup, maple syrup, and fruit juices feed bacteria. Two tablespoons in 4-5 gallons of water helps bacteria multiply and establish dominance. More complex sugars and fish emulsion also support bacteria with some fungal growth.

### Fungal foods

To encourage fungal growth, add kelp (Ascophyllum nodosum, a cold-water kelp), [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]] live off root exudates, the tea must reach plant roots quickly.

Brew for 24-36 hours. Good tea develops a healthy, sweet, earthy smell — especially when molasses is used. Use within 4 hours of manufacture. Tea lasts 3-5 days if refrigerated or if air continues bubbling through it. If it smells bad, it has gone anaerobic — discard it, do not put it on plants.

## Application

You can never apply too much compost tea — no ill effects from unlimited applications. It does not burn roots or leaves. The microbiology adjusts to available nutrients. Apply to lawns, vegetables, trees, shrubs, annuals, and perennials. Use as both soil drench and foliar spray. Repeated applications increase microbial diversity.
