### Kenya

In 1945, E.R. Cullinan and D. Henry described 22 cases of intoxication in Nairobi, where a yellow Stropharia species was mistaken for a culinary mushroom. The symptoms started one hour after ingestion, peaked within three hours, and persisted for 24-48 hours. Symptoms included:

- Emotional imbalance
- Fits of mirthful and irresponsible laughter alternating with depressive moods
- Nightmarish closed-eye imagery
- Patients remained conscious with rational speech throughout

### South Africa

In January 1994, Gartz and M. Smith discovered a bluing Psilocybe species in Natal Province -- the first psychotropic Psilocybe species ever found in the area. The species was generally whitish, had no annular ring, was comparable in size to Psilocybe cubensis, but did not grow directly on dung. It was published as Psilocybe natalensis.

## The Significance

The Tassili rock art represents the earliest known visual evidence for the ritual use of psychoactive mushrooms. The sophistication of the imagery -- showing specific mushroom shapes, depicting the "flow of energy" from mushroom to head, and portraying communal ecstatic dance -- suggests a well-developed cultic practice rather than casual or accidental use.

This finding supports the hypothesis that psychoactive mushroom use may be as old as human consciousness itself, originating in Africa alongside the emergence of modern humans and spreading with human migration across all continents.
