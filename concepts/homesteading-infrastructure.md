# Homesteading Infrastructure

A well-designed homestead integrates housing, animal systems, gardens, water, energy, and waste into a closed-loop system. The goal is to minimize external inputs and maximize the connections between elements so that the output of one system becomes the input of another.

## Core Infrastructure Systems

### 1. Water System
- **Rainwater catchment**: Tanks or barrels at every downspout. See [[water-management]] for sizing
- **Well or spring**: Backup water source for irrigation and animals
- **Greywater system**: Route sink/shower/laundry water to garden irrigation or constructed wetland
- **Pond**: Multi-purpose water storage, irrigation, microclimate moderation, aquaculture, wildlife habitat
- **Irrigation**: Gravity-fed drip lines from tanks, or simple hose bibs near garden zones

### 2. Animal Housing
**Duck housing**:
- Secure night shelter (predator-proof: raccoons, foxes, owls)
- Automatic waterer (ducks need water deep enough to dunk their heads)
- Access to pasture or garden rotation areas during the day
- Duck pond or kiddie pool (ducks need water for mating, grooming, and foraging)
- Bedding area with straw or wood chips (harvest for [[composting-systems]])
- Eggs collected daily; manure-rich bedding goes to compost

**Goat housing**:
- Three-sided shelter minimum; enclosed barn in cold climates
- Fenced pasture with rotational grazing system (move every 1-4 weeks)
- Hay feeder and mineral station
- Milking stanchion if keeping dairy goats
- Strong fencing (goats are escape artists) -- woven wire + electric offset
- Shelter from rain (goats hate rain and are susceptible to pneumonia)

**Chicken housing**:
- Coop with roosts, nesting boxes, ventilation
- Secure run or chicken tractor for garden integration
- Automatic waterer and feeder
- Predator protection (buried wire, hardware cloth, covered run)

### 3. Garden Infrastructure
- **Raised beds**: Sepp Holzer style -- 3-5 feet wide, steep sides (45-70 degrees), 3-5 feet tall. See [[sepp-holzer-methods]]
- **Composting area**: Minimum 3-bin system (fresh, active, finished). Close to kitchen and garden
- **Tool storage**: Near garden for convenience
- **Greenhouse/cold frame**: Season extension, seed starting
- **Fencing**: Deer fence around main garden areas; smaller fences for animal exclusion
- **Mulch storage**: Keep straw, leaves, woodchips on hand
- **Potting area**: For seed starting and transplanting

### 4. Soil and Compost Systems
- **Compost bins**: 3-bin system (add to bin 1, turn to bin 2, cure in bin 3)
- **Vermicompost bin**: For kitchen scraps; can be indoors in winter
- **Bokashi system**: Fermented kitchen waste for fast composting
- **Leaf mold pile**: Autumn leaves decompose into excellent soil amendment
- **Manure composting**: Duck/goat/chicken manure + bedding = high-quality compost
- **Cover crop seed storage**: Keep a variety of [[cover-cropping-guide]] seeds ready

### 5. Food Processing and Storage
- **Root cellar or cool storage**: For potatoes, carrots, onions, winter squash, apples
- **Canning/preserving station**: Kitchen area with pressure canner, water bath, dehydrator
- **Fermentation area**: Cool space for sauerkraut, kimchi, kombucha
- **Freezer**: For meat, frozen vegetables, fruit
- **Drying racks**: For herbs, fruits, jerky
- **Smokehouse**: Optional for meat and fish

### 6. Energy Systems
- **Passive solar design**: South-facing windows, thermal mass, deciduous trees for summer shade
- **Wood heating**: If available, woodlot in Zone 4 for firewood
- **Solar panels**: Even a small array reduces grid dependence
- **Solar water heater**: Simple batch heater or thermosyphon system
- **Rocket stove**: Efficient wood combustion for cooking or heating

### 7. Waste Cycling
- **Kitchen scraps** -> vermicompost or compost
- **Animal manure** -> compost -> garden
- **Greywater** -> garden irrigation or constructed wetland
- **Plant residues** -> mulch or compost (never burn or waste)
- **Feathers, bones, hides** -> see [[animal-byproducts]] for utilization

## Layout Principles

Using [[permaculture-design]] zone analysis:

```
Zone 0: House (kitchen, living, indoor grow)
   |
Zone 1: Herb garden, salad greens, compost, duck coop, greenhouse
   |
Zone 2: Fruit trees, berry bushes, chicken coop, goat barn, main vegetable beds
   |
Zone 3: Orchard, food forest, pasture, field crops, cover cropping
   |
Zone 4: Woodlot, forage, wild harvest
   |
Zone 5: Wildlife area, observation, reference ecosystem
```

**Key layout rules**:
- Place elements you visit daily closest to the house
- Route water flow downhill through the system
- Connect animal housing to compost to garden in a line
- Put the compost between kitchen and garden
- Place tool storage centrally among work areas
- Design paths for efficiency (no unnecessary walking)

## Animal Integration Patterns

**Ducks + garden**: Ducks patrol for slugs and insects. During the day, let them into garden areas after plants are established (not when seedlings are small). Duck pond water is nutrient-rich -- use for irrigation. Duck manure is cold (does not burn plants) and can be applied directly.

**Goats + pasture**: Rotational grazing improves pasture health. Move goats every 1-4 weeks. Follow goats with chickens to spread manure and control parasites. Goat manure + bedding = excellent compost.

**Chickens + compost**: Chickens in a chicken tractor over compost piles turn and accelerate decomposition. They eat weed seeds and insect larvae. Follow with cover crop planting.

**Rabbits + worms**: Rabbit hutch over worm bin. Manure drops directly into worm bed. Worms convert manure to castings. No odor, no waste.

## Startup Priorities

1. Water system (roof catchment, storage, distribution)
2. Composting system (bins, tools, animal manure management)
3. Animal housing and fencing
4. Garden beds and [[no-till-gardening-method]] setup
5. [[food-forest-design]] planting
6. Food preservation infrastructure
7. Energy systems (as budget allows)
