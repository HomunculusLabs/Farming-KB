# Growing Vegetables with Less Fertilizer

[[steve-solomon]] advocates for a balanced approach to soil fertility that maximizes nutrition and minimizes purchased inputs. The key insight: it's not about how much fertilizer you add, but having the right balance of minerals in the soil.

## Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF)

### Recipe (for 100 sq ft)
- Seed meal (cottonseed, canola, or soybean meal): 4 parts (4 quarts)
- Agricultural lime or gypsum: 1 part (1 quart)
- Bone meal or phosphate rock: 1/2 part (1/2 quart)
- Kelp meal: 1/2 part (1/2 quart)

### Application Rate
- 4-6 quarts per 100 sq ft, worked into top 4-6 inches of soil
- Apply at planting time, or split: half at planting, half as side-dress
- One application per crop cycle

### Why COF Works
- **Seed meal**: Slow-release nitrogen source (6-7% N). Feeds soil biology, which feeds plants
- **Lime**: Raises pH, provides [[dynamic-accumulator-plants-calcium-and-magnesium]]. Use dolomite lime if soil is Mg-deficient; agricultural lime if Mg is adequate; gypsum on alkaline soils (adds Ca without raising pH)
- **Bone meal**: Phosphorus source. Phosphate rock is slower but cheaper for large areas
- **Kelp meal**: [[dynamic-accumulator-plants-trace-minerals]] (micronutrients) and [[gibberellins-plant-growth-hormones]]. The "secret ingredient"

## The Ley System (Long-Term Fertility)

### Concept
- Rotate between [[fukuoka-dutch-priest-vegetable-garden-spiritual-renewal]] and grass/clover pasture (ley)
- 3-5 years of ley restores soil fertility naturally
- Grass sod builds soil structure; clover fixes nitrogen
- No imported fertilizer needed during ley phase

### Implementation
- Year 1-4: Grass and clover ley (mow regularly, leave clippings)
- Year 5: Till in the sod (massive [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] addition)
- Year 5-8: Intensive [[dry-gardening-unirrigated-vegetable-production-solomon]] using soil reserves
- Year 9: Return to ley

### Benefits
- Eliminates need for compost or imported fertilizer
- Rebuilds soil structure better than any amendment
- Breaks [[hamilton-pest-and-disease-control-organic]] cycles
- Provides grazing or hay during ley years

## Balanced Mineral Nutrition

### Albrecht's Insight
- Plants need balanced minerals, not just NPK
- Excess of one mineral can block uptake of another (antagonism)
- Calcium:magnesium ratio is critical for soil structure
- Ideal base saturation: Ca 65-75%, Mg 10-20%, K 2-5%

### Deficiency Symptoms by Nutrient

| Nutrient | Deficiency Symptom | Mobile in Plant? |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen | Yellowing of older leaves, stunted growth | Yes |
| Phosphorus | Purplish leaves, poor root development | No |
| Potassium | Brown leaf edges, weak stems | Yes |
| Calcium | Blossom end rot, distorted new growth | No |
| Magnesium | Interveinal chlorosis (yellow between veins) | Yes |
| Iron | Interveinal chlorosis on NEW leaves | No |
| Zinc | Small leaves, short internodes | No |
| Boron | Hollow stems, cracked roots, poor fruit set | No |

## Compost as Fertility

### What Compost Provides
- Slow-release nutrients [[soil-conditioners-guide]] (1-1-1 to 3-2-1 depending on ingredients)
- Organic matter for soil structure
- Microbial inoculation
- Micronutrients (if diverse feedstocks used)

### What Compost Doesn't Provide
- Enough phosphorus for heavy feeders (tomatoes, brassicas)
- Enough calcium (need lime separately)
- Sufficient potassium in many soils

### Best Use
- Compost for soil structure and biology
- COF or [[cervantes-organic-mineral-amendments]] for specific nutrient needs
- Combination gives best results
