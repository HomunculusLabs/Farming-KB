## Peat-Free Gardening and Alternatives

There is increasing concern about the use of peat as a [[fukuoka-textdoc-sawdust-wood-chips-orchard-soil-acidification-experiment]], with some even incorporating material produced from green
recycling centres. These products work well in practice, with coir being
the preferred choice for propagation while the others are more suited for
growing established plants.

### Coir

Coir, derived from coconut husk fibre, has become one of the most popular
peat-free alternatives. It is widely regarded as the best choice for seed
sowing and propagation because of its excellent water-holding capacity and
fine texture. Coir is lightweight, easy to handle, and rewets easily if it
dries out. It contains little or no nutrients, so fertilizers must be
added when using it as a growing medium.

### Bark-Based Composts

Composted bark is a well-established alternative, especially for
ornamental beds and [[query-how-do-cover-crops-suppress-weeds]]. Bark composts
[[comparison-ramial-wood-chip-mulch-vs-composted-wood-chips]] and material from green recycling centres offer another avenue
for peat-free growing. These materials are increasingly available as
municipal recycling programmes expand. Green compost made from collected
garden waste provides a good general-purpose growing medium, though
quality can vary depending on the source material and processing method.

## Making Your Own Peat-Free Potting Composts

As an alternative to commercial products, it is entirely possible to make
your own peat-free potting composts at home. A combination of pelleted
chicken manure mixed into the compost, supplemented with liquid kelp feed
applied when required, does an admirable job of providing nutrients.

A successful peat-free seed compost can be made by mixing [[teaming-with-nutrients-micronutrients-and-trace-elements]] and major nutrients. Moisten the compost
prior to sowing with a half-strength liquid feed solution, and use it at
full strength after the seedlings are established.

## Practical Considerations

Peat-free composts behave differently from peat-based ones. They tend to
dry out more quickly, so attention to watering is especially important.
They may also have different nutrient profiles and drainage characteristics.
