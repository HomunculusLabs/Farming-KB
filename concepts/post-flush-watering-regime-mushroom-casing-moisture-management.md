## The Daily Misting Protocol

The baseline moisture management approach is straightforward: casings are
misted each day, and the casing is never allowed to dry out. This daily
attention serves two functions — replenishing evaporative losses and
maintaining the surface microclimate that developing primordia require. The
guide emphasizes that the [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]].

Misting should be done with a fine spray bottle that produces a mist rather
than a stream. Direct streams of water can damage developing primordia and
cause [[falconer-growing-mushrooms-in-sheds]] draw water primarily from the casing
layer, not directly from the substrate below. A mature mushroom is 85-92%
water by weight, and this water comes from the casing soil. A heavy flush can
significantly deplete the casing moisture reserves, leaving the casing drier
than optimal for the next flush cycle. If this depletion is not addressed, the
next flush will be delayed, reduced in size, or may fail to initiate entirely.

The post-flush watering increase should be substantial but graduated. Rather
than flooding the casing with a large volume of water at once, the approach
should be to mist more heavily and more frequently for the first 2-3 days
after harvest, allowing the casing to absorb water between applications. This
gradual approach prevents the casing from becoming waterlogged while still
replenishing the depleted moisture reserves.

## Reading Casing Moisture

Since explicit watering directions are impossible, cultivators must learn to
read the casing moisture through visual and tactile cues:

**Surface appearance.** A properly moist casing appears uniformly dark (for
peat-based casings) with a slight sheen from surface moisture. Dry casing
appears lighter in color and may develop a dusty or cracked appearance.
Over-wet casing appears glossy and may have standing water on the surface.

**Touch test.** Lightly pressing a fingertip into the casing surface should
yield a cool, damp sensation without any water being squeezed out. If the
casing feels dry and crumbly, it needs water immediately. If water is
expressed from the casing when pressed, it is over-saturated and should be
allowed to dry before further misting.

**Weight.** Experienced cultivators often track tray weight as a proxy for
moisture content. A tray that is becoming progressively lighter between
misting sessions is losing water faster than it is being replaced. The
Badham (1982) study used weight monitoring as the primary method for
determining when cultures needed watering.

**Mushroom condition.** The developing mushrooms themselves provide moisture
cues. Mushrooms growing in under-watered casing tend to have cracked caps,
slow growth rates, and may show premature veil tearing. Over-watered casing
can cause Mushrooms to develop waterlogged, soft tissue and increased
susceptibility to [[growing-gourmet-bacterial-blotch-disease-management]].
