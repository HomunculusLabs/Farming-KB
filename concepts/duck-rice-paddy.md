# Duck-Rice Paddy Integration

## Overview

Fukuoka recommends releasing ducklings onto rice paddies as a natural alternative to chemical fertilisers and manual weeding. From the standpoint of natural farming, releasing ten ducklings per quarter-acre onto the field when the rice seedlings have become established is preferable and much easier than applying manure. (see [[mollison-designers-rice-paddy-integrated-duck-fish]]).

## What Ducks Do

- **Weed:** Ducks consume weeds in the paddy
- **Pick off insects:** They eat insect pests on the rice plants
- **Turn the soil:** Their activity aerates and mixes the paddy soil
- **Fertilise:** Duck manure provides nutrients to the rice
- **Produce protein:** The ducks themselves become a source of high-quality food

## Protection

Ducks must be protected from stray dogs and hawks when released into the paddy. (see [[cash-crop-guide]]).

## Companion Approach

Fukuoka also suggests releasing young carp into the paddy alongside the ducks. By making full, three-dimensional use of the field in this way -- ducks on the surface, fish in the water, rice growing above -- one can simultaneously produce good protein foods alongside the grain crop.

## Fukuoka's Vision of Integrated Livestock

In Fukuoka's idealised vision of livestock farming, great numbers of ducks and mallards play in the rice paddy, semi-wild chickens and rabbits frolic with dogs in fields of growing wheat, and black pigs and boars grow fat on worms and crayfish at the foot of the hills. This represents an organic partnership between man, animal, and nature. An environment comfortable for small animals is also an ideal setting for man.

## Comparison with Manure Application

As an alternative to the duck method, Fukuoka describes spreading 450-900 pounds of dried chicken manure before or after returning barley straw to the field. Fresh manure should not be used as it can harm rice seedlings. A small amount (200-450 pounds) may be added early during the heading stage. However, the duck approach is simpler and integrates multiple functions simultaneously.
