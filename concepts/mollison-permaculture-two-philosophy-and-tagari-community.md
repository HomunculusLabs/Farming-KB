2. No chemical fertilizer or prepared compost -- let the plants and animals that make the soil go to work
3. No weeding by tillage or herbicide -- use the weeds; control them by natural means, or occasional cutting

### Paths to Permanence
Three historical approaches to permanence are identified:

1. **Feudal permanence** -- The peasant approach, as in old China, where man hauled nutrients from canals, cesspits, pathways and forests to an annual grain culture. Man bound to landscape by unremitting toil and in service to a landlord. This leads eventually to famine and revolution.

2. **Baronial permanence** -- The permanent pasture approach, where large holdings and few people create vast grazing leases, usually for a single species. Pasture is the least productive use of land. Such systems, once mechanized, destroy whole landscapes and soil complexes.

3. **Communal permanence** -- Forest agriculture, needing generations of care and knowledge, and hence a tribal or communal reverence found only in stable communities. To be able to plant a pecan or citrus when old, and to know it will not be cut down by our successors, is the permanence many seek.

### Polyculture vs Monoculture
The productive, more natural polyculture of the dry tropics represents an older wisdom. Around dooryard gardens, fields are "dotted here and there with volunteer guavas and guamuchiele trees, whose fruit was carefully gathered." Were they orchards or pastures? The order described is a semi-natural order of plants in their right relationship to each other, but not separated into various artificial groupings. The house and fence form essential trellis for the garden, so that it is no longer clear where orchard, field, house and garden have their boundaries.

## The Tagari Community

Tagari is a small group of about 30 (but growing) parents, single people and children devoted to the evolution of the meta-industrial village, and to experiments in alternative systems of agriculture and industry. It is a non-profit association, where surplus goes to furthering these aims.

### Structure and Membership
- Membership is open to all easygoing workers, preferably with some capital to house themselves
- A trust holds all property in common
- A part-time income from permaculture publications, consultancy, and farming supports the community
- Subsistence comes from their own gardening, publishing, and consulting in permaculture design

### Design Consultancy
A design team was active (Ted Lamont, Bill Mollison, Andrew Jeeves, Simon Fell, Peter Moore), with others being trained in field work. Associates included specialists in educational architecture, reactive housing, agricultural science, keyline planning, horticulture and aquaculture. Specialties included planning for unemployed and disadvantaged groups.

## The Permaculture Institute

Tagari purchased 78 acres of coastal swamp and dryland in Stanley, Tasmania. The main land area is devoted to demonstrating techniques of planting and design, with still-pond culture as a featured element. Species range from sub-tropical to cool temperate (no frosts at the site).

The institute aims to research and become a resource for species for southern Australia -- a sort of permaculture arboretum. Commercial crops on site include nuts, some fruits, water plants, and aquatic birds/poultry.

## Standard Designs

Tagari produced a series of standard design sheets (looseleaf, updated regularly):

**Landscape (Series L):** Rock dome planting, tidal flat ponds, flatland dam and house site

**Pests and Problems (Series P):** Mosquito control, blackberry control, fox predation prevention, planting in presence of rabbits

**Annual Garden (Series A):** Tomato/asparagus polyculture, culinary herb spiral, home production of potatoes

**Marketing and Management (Series M):** Farm-Link involving town with country, wayside marketing, self-pick sales

**Techniques (Series T):** Pruning in permaculture, sewage disposal (domestic and community), planting on broadscale, uses for tyres, fuel production from plants

**Structures (Series S):** Trellis structures and planting, sun traps; collecting water from rock dome seepage; shade house; attached greenhouse

**Species Lists (Series D):** Complete list of poultry forage species, hedgerow species, cattle and pig forage species, seacoast species for salt and wind resistance

**Urban Strategies (Series U):** Contract cropping in neighbourhoods, dispersed tree crop with contract sales, dispersed livestock with contract sales, types of public allotments
