# Mushroom Spore Syringe Inoculation

Spore syringe inoculation is the process of introducing fungal spores into a sterilized
substrate to initiate [[mycelial-colonization]]. In the PF TEK methodology developed by
[[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus]], this step transforms a jar of inert substrate into a living fungal
culture. The technique emphasizes mass spore inoculation directly onto [[pf-tek-maximum-fruiting-substrate-formula]],
which preserves complete genetic diversity and avoids the senescence problems associated
with tissue cloning.

## The Spore Syringe

A spore syringe is a suspension of fungal spores in sterile water, housed in a standard
laboratory syringe (typically 10-12 mL capacity). The spores are microscopic reproductive
units — each syringe contains millions of individual spores that carry the complete
genetic repertoire of the parent mushroom. In the PF TEK system, one syringe inoculates
approximately 10 half-pint jars at 1 cc per jar.

Spore syringes must be stored in a cool, dark location to maintain viability. Before use,
the syringe should be shaken vigorously to redistribute spores that may have settled to
the bottom during storage.

## Pre-Inoculation Requirements

Before any inoculation begins, several conditions must be met:

1. **Substrate jars must be cool to the touch** — hot jars will kill spores on contact.
   After steam sterilization, allow jars to cool for several hours or overnight.
2. **Jar lids must be tight** — ensure the canning ring is firmly screwed down.
3. **Tape covering needle holes must be removed** — these coverings protected the holes
   during sterilization but must be removed for inoculation.
4. **Work area should be as clean as possible** — while the PF TEK's dry vermiculite
   barrier provides significant protection, working in a still-air environment improves
   success rates.

## Inoculation Procedure

### Standard Lid Inoculation

1. Shake the spore syringe well to redistribute spores evenly in the suspension
2. Remove the needle guard and discard the tape covering the lid's needle holes
3. Insert the needle through one of the four punched holes in the canning lid
4. Tilt the syringe body back toward the center of the lid, with the needle tip touching
   the inside glass surface of the jar
5. This angling distributes the spore solution down the side of the jar rather than
   injecting it deep into the substrate, providing better surface coverage
6. Press the plunger slowly, watching the needle tip against the glass — as soon as
   liquid appears around the tip, stop pressing. This delivers just a few drops per hole
7. Repeat for each of the four holes, shaking the syringe slightly between holes
8. Use approximately 1 cc total per jar (10 jars per standard syringe)

### Needle Plugging

If the needle becomes plugged with substrate as it is inserted through the vermiculite
barrier layer, slowly draw the needle back a small distance. This usually clears the
blockage without requiring removal and reinsertion.

### Open-Glass Inoculation

For jars using drinking glasses without punched lids, inoculation is performed without
a lid entirely. This technique is more advanced and should only be attempted after
mastering the standard lid method. The critical precaution is to disturb the dry
vermiculite contaminant barrier as little as possible while introducing the needle.

## Alcohol Flaming Technique

Whenever the syringe needle is touched or potentially contaminated, it must be
re-sterilized before reinsertion. The PF TEK uses an alcohol flame rather than a
butane lighter because:

- Alcohol burns cleanly with no soot residue (butane leaves carbon deposits)
- The flame is short and controlled, suitable for small tools

### Creating an Alcohol Flame

1. Place a tequila shot glass upside down
2. Using an eyedropper, place a few drops of denatured alcohol on the hollow bottom
3. Touch the alcohol with a match or lighter to ignite
4. A short blue flame results — this is the sterilizing flame

### Flaming the Needle

1. Pass the needle tip through the blue alcohol flame for several seconds
2. Small "pops" of boiling water may spurt from the needle — this is normal and
   does not harm the spores inside the syringe body
3. Allow the needle to cool for a few seconds before inserting into the next jar
   (a hot needle can damage both the substrate and the spores)

## Post-Inoculation Care

After inoculation, replace the tape over the needle holes and place jars in the
incubation environment. The ideal incubation temperature for **[[brassinosteroid-growth-promotion-psilocybe-cubensis]]** is
approximately 75-80°F (24-27°C). Incubation in complete darkness is acceptable, though
ambient room light is not harmful.

### Colonization Timeline

- **Days 3-7:** First visible signs of white mycelial growth at inoculation points
- **Days 7-14:** Mycelium spreads across the substrate surface
- **Days 14-21:** Full colonization — the entire substrate appears white with mycelium
- **Days 21-28:** Consolidation period where mycelium strengthens throughout the cake

### The Lid: Loose or Tight

A critical decision during incubation is whether to keep jar lids tight or slightly
loose:

- **Standard formula:** Keep lids tight. The dry vermiculite barrier provides
  sufficient gas exchange.
- **Maximum fruiting formula (higher water content):** Loosen lids slightly. The
  increased [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]] from the richer substrate generates more CO2 that
  needs to escape. If lids remain tight with the enriched formula, CO2 buildup
  can stall colonization.

## Advantages of Mass Spore Inoculation

The PF TEK deliberately uses multi-spore inoculation rather than tissue cloning.
This design choice has important biological advantages:

1. **Complete genotype preserved:** Spores carry the full genetic diversity of the
   parent organism, enabling natural selection and robust fruiting
2. **No senescence:** Cloned mycelium eventually mutates and ceases fruiting after
   repeated subculturing (senescence). Spore-based inoculation resets this clock
   with every generation
3. **Never-ending succession:** Each mushroom produces millions of spores, ensuring
   an indefinite supply of fresh genetic material for future cultivation cycles
