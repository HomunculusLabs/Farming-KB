## Ergoline Alkaloids in Morning Glories

## Overview

The discovery that the sacred Mexican morning glories *Rivea corymbosa* (ololiuqui) and *[[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]]*), a parasite of rye grains in Europe. Their presence in a morning glory—a member of the Convolvulaceae family previously thought devoid of intoxicating principles—challenged prevailing assumptions about the distribution of psychoactive compounds in the plant kingdom.

## Historical Context

The identification of ololiuqui and tlitliltzin as morning glories took four centuries. Spanish chroniclers had described these Aztec divinatory hallucinogens shortly after the conquest of Mexico, but efforts to eradicate their use drove the practices into remote highland villages. Crude drawings in colonial chronicles suggested a morning glory, and Mexican botanists identified ololiuqui as such as early as 1854. However, doubts persisted because no member of the Convolvulaceae had ever been observed in use as a hallucinogen. Some authorities suggested ololiuqui was actually a *Datura* species, a known hallucinogen still used in Mexico.

It was not until 1939 that actual specimens of *Rivea corymbosa* used in Mazatec divinatory rituals were collected in Oaxaca and definitively identified as the ololiuqui of the ancient Aztecs. *Ipomoea violacea* was found twenty years later in ceremonial use among the Zapotecs of the same region and identified as tlitliltzin.

## Experimental Discovery

Experimental studies of the narcotic morning glories began in 1955, when a psychiatrist published notes on self-experimentation with *Rivea* seeds, demonstrating that they produced intoxication accompanied by hallucinations. This prompted chemists to examine the plant, but no active principle could be isolated until the 1960s. At that time, Albert Hofmann—the Swiss chemist who had discovered LSD—analyzed the plant material and found several alkaloids closely related to his famously potent synthetic compound.

His discovery met with widespread disbelief. Ergoline alkaloids had hitherto been known in nature only from ergot, a fungus that had caused devastating epidemics throughout European history. When ergot-contaminated rye flour was consumed in bread, it poisoned entire towns, causing terrible intoxication frequently leading to widespread insanity and death. In the Middle Ages, before causes were understood and preventative measures taken, these mysterious mass attacks were called St. Anthony's Fire and attributed to divine wrath.

## Chemical Constituents

Half a dozen ergoline alkaloids have been identified in seeds of *Rivea corymbosa* and *Ipomoea violacea*. The principal hallucinogenic constituents of both species are:

- **Ergine** (d-lysergic acid amide, LSA): The primary psychoactive compound, closely related to LSD but approximately 10-50 times less potent. Ergine produces effects including visual hallucinations, altered perception of time, and mild euphoria.
- **Isoergine** (l-lysergic acid amide): An isomer of ergine, present in significant quantities and contributing to the overall psychoactive profile.

Minor alkaloids occurring in smaller amounts include:

- **Chanoclavine**: An ergoline alkaloid that serves as a biosynthetic intermediate in the ergot alkaloid pathway.
- **Elymoclavine**: Another clavine-type alkaloid found in the seeds.
- **Lysergol**: A reduced form of lysergic acid, present in trace quantities.

## Comparative Alkaloid Content

The total alkaloid content of *Ipomoea violacea* is approximately five times that of *Rivea corymbosa*. This difference explains why indigenous practitioners use fewer *Ipomoea* seeds in ritual preparations. Where a typical dose of *Rivea* might require several hundred seeds, a dose of *Ipomoea violacea* requires only a fraction of that number to achieve similar psychoactive effects.

## Relationship to LSD

The ergoline alkaloids found in the morning glories are chemically related to [[serotonin-psilocin-structural-similarity-hallucinogenic-mechanism]] between the morning glory alkaloids and LSD lies in the shared ergoline (tetracyclic) ring system. The key difference is that LSD carries a diethylamide substitution at position 8, while ergine (the major morning glory constituent) carries only a simple amide group. This relatively small structural change accounts for the dramatic difference in potency between the two compounds.

## Horticultural Varieties

Many horticultural varieties of *Ipomoea violacea* contain hallucinogenic constituents, including the popular ornamental cultivars:

- **Heavenly Blue**: The most widely available and well-known variety, frequently cited as having significant alkaloid content.
- **Pearly Gates**: A white-flowered cultivar with psychoactive properties.
- **Flying Saucers**: A variegated variety containing ergoline alkaloids.
- **Blue Stars**: Another commonly available cultivar with documented psychoactivity.
- **Wedding Bells**: A commercially available variety found to contain the characteristic alkaloids.
