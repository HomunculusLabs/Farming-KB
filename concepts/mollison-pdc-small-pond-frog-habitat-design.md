# Mollison PDC Small Pond and Frog Habitat Design

## Overview

In Pamphlet IX of the [[bill-mollison]] describes
small pond systems as essential components of zone one and zone two
designs. Beyond their obvious role in water storage and irrigation, small
ponds serve as critical habitat for frogs and other aquatic organisms
that provide [[phytochrome-ecological-function-dusk-dawn-red-far-red-light-switch]] and its convenience
for the gardener.

## The Pond as Pest Control Infrastructure

Mollison's primary argument for including ponds [[duggar-mushroom-insect-pests-and-fungal-diseases]]. Mollison states that a
well-placed pond can attract enough frogs to provide complete biological
pest control for the surrounding garden, eliminating or drastically
reducing the need for any chemical [[query-how-to-control-pests-without-chemicals]].

## Mosquito Control Paradox

A common objection to garden ponds is that they breed mosquitoes. Mollison
addresses this directly, arguing that a properly designed pond with a
healthy frog population actually reduces mosquito numbers in the garden.
Frog tadpoles are voracious consumers of mosquito larvae, and adult frogs
eat adult mosquitoes. The pond becomes a trap for mosquitoes rather than
a breeding ground.

To ensure the pond controls rather than produces mosquitoes, Mollison
recommends several design features. The pond should have sloping sides
that allow frogs easy access in and out. It should include emergent
vegetation like reeds and water iris that provide frog habitat and
perching sites. It should not have deep still water where mosquito larvae
can develop without predation. And it should include fish or other
aquatic predators in larger ponds.

## Zone One Pond Placement

Mollison recommends placing small ponds in zone one, close to the house.
The rationale is both practical and ecological. Practically, a zone one
pond is convenient for harvesting water for seedlings, for washing
vegetables, and for observing wildlife. Ecologically, frogs that live in
a zone one pond patrol the nearby vegetable beds, providing pest control
precisely where it is most needed.

The pond should be positioned where it receives some shade during the
hottest part of the day to prevent excessive evaporation and algal
growth. Mollison suggests placing it on the north side of a structure or
tree in the northern hemisphere, where it receives morning sun but is
shaded in the afternoon.

## Pond Design Specifications

Mollison describes several simple pond designs suitable for home gardens.
The simplest is a shallow depression lined with clay or a plastic liner,
filled with water, and planted with aquatic vegetation. A more elaborate
design includes multiple depths: shallow shelves for emergent plants, a
deeper center for overwintering frogs and fish, and a gently sloping
beach area that allows wildlife easy access.

For a basic frog habitat, Mollison suggests a pond as small as four feet
in diameter and eighteen inches deep. This is sufficient to support a
breeding population of frogs. The pond should be filled with rainwater
rather than tap water, which may contain chlorine harmful to amphibians.
