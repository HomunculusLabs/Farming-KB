# Garden Disease Identification

Common vegetable plant diseases as described in Steve Solomon"s *Gardening When It Counts*. Solomon notes that the Sanitary System (belief that vegetables must be cosmetically perfect) gives gardeners the wrong idea — many disease problems can be tolerated or avoided through proper cultural practices rather than chemical intervention.

## Solomon's Disease Management Philosophy

- **Grow a diverse garden**: With both heat-loving and cold-hardy crops, something will produce in any year
- **Plant twice what you need**: So pests and diseases can't wipe out your food supply
- **Don't sow too early**: Cold-damp conditions create ideal disease environments
- **Fertilize struggling plants**: Often the best cure is fertilizer, not fungicide
- **Rotate crops**: Avoid growing the same family in the same spot year after year
- **Destroy infected material**: Burn or hot-compost diseased plant debris

## Major Diseases by Crop

### Cabbage Family (Brassicas)

**Black rot**: Can destroy entire cabbage plantings. Bacterial disease spread by water splash. Favored by warm, wet weather.
- **Control**: Crop rotation, sanitation, avoid overhead watering.

**Club root**: Soil-borne disease causing swollen, deformed roots. Stunts plants severely.
- **Control**: Raise soil pH with lime. Long rotation (7+ years). Resistant varieties.

### Cucurbits (Squash, Cucumber, Melon)

**Powdery mildew**: White powdery coating on leaves. Common in late season.
- **Identification**: White-gray powdery patches on leaf surfaces, starting on older leaves.
- **Impact**: Reduces yield but rarely kills mature plants. Worse in humid weather with cool nights and warm days.
- **Control**: Resistant varieties. Good air circulation. Baking soda sprays (1 tablespoon per gallon with a drop of dish soap). Sulfur dust.

**Downy mildew**: Yellow patches on upper leaf surface with gray-purple fuzz on underside.
- **Control**: Similar to powdery mildew. Avoid wetting foliage.

**Virus wilt**: Transmitted by cucumber beetles. Causes rapid wilting and death of seedlings.
- **Control**: Control cucumber beetles (see [[garden-insect-identification]]).

### Tomatoes

**Early blight** (*Alternaria solani*): Brown spots with concentric rings on lower leaves, progressing upward.
- **Control**: Remove infected leaves. Mulch to prevent soil splash. Copper fungicide.

**Late blight** (*Phytophthora infestans*): Rapid browning and killing of all foliage. The disease that caused the Irish Potato Famine.
- **Identification**: Water-soaked dark spots on leaves, white fuzzy growth on undersides in humid conditions. Spreads rapidly in cool, wet weather.
- **Control**: Destroy infected plants immediately. Copper fungicide as preventive. Avoid overhead watering.

**Verticillium and Fusarium wilts**: Soil-borne fungi that block water-conducting vessels.
- **Identification**: Yellowing and wilting starting from one side of the plant. Cutting stems shows brown vascular tissue.
- **Control**: Resistant varieties (labeled V, F, or VF). Long rotation. Destroy infected plants.

### Potatoes

**Late blight**: Same organism as tomato late blight. Devastating to potatoes.
- **Control**: Destroy infected tubers. Plant certified seed potatoes. Copper sprays.

### Beans

**Anthracnose**: Dark sunken lesions on pods and stems.
- **Control**: Use clean seed. Crop rotation. Avoid working with wet plants.

**Rust**: Orange-brown pustules on leaf undersides.
- **Control**: Resistant varieties. Remove infected leaves.

### Onions

**Downy mildew**: One of the most serious onion diseases.
- **Control**: Good drainage, air circulation, avoid overcrowding.

**Onion smut**: Soil-borne disease affecting seedlings.
- **Control**: Crop rotation, hot-water seed treatment.

## General Prevention Strategies

Solomon emphasizes prevention over cure:

1. **Start with healthy seed**: Disease-free seed prevents many problems from the start
2. **Proper spacing**: Good air circulation reduces fungal diseases
3. **Avoid overhead watering**: Wet foliage promotes fungal growth
4. **Crop rotation**: Prevents soil-borne disease buildup
5. **Sanitation**: Remove and destroy diseased plant material; don't compost it
6. **Resistant varieties**: Many diseases have resistant cultivars available
7. **Timely planting**: Avoid conditions that favor disease (too early, too wet, too crowded)
8. **Soil fertility**: Well-nourished plants resist disease better than nutrient-stressed ones

## The "Eat Imperfection" Rule

Solomon argues that gardeners should accept cosmetically imperfect produce. Minor blemishes don't affect edibility or nutritional value. The expectation of supermarket-perfect vegetables leads to unnecessary pesticide use and frustration.
