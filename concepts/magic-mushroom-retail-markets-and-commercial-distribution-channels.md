- **Product reviews**: User-submitted reviews and trip reports on forums served as informal quality assurance

### Impact of Legal Changes on Internet Sales

The EMCDDA noted that the UK's decision to criminalize psilocybin mushrooms in 2005 appeared to have a measurable impact:

- Reports suggested a reduction in the **availability of mushrooms** following the ban
- **Overall volume of internet sales** declined, though some vendors shifted to selling spores and grow kits (which remained legal in some jurisdictions)
- The UK experience suggested that criminalization, while imperfect, could reduce open commercial availability

## Market Stalls and Festival Sales

### Informal Retail Channels

Beyond fixed retail locations, mushrooms were sold through more informal channels:

- **Music festivals**: Vendors sold mushrooms at dance music festivals, psychedelic trance events, and alternative culture gatherings across Europe
- **Street markets**: In cities with established alternative scenes (Amsterdam, Berlin, London), mushrooms were available at market stalls alongside other psychoactive substances
- **Person-to-person networks**: Word-of-mouth distribution through social networks remained significant, particularly in countries without legal retail access

### Product Forms at Markets

Market vendors typically sold:

- Pre-weighed bags of dried mushrooms in small portions
- Chocolate or honey products infused with ground mushroom material
- Fresh mushrooms in season
- Informal dosage advice and species identification
