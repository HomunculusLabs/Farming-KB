# DOET

## Overview

DOET (2,5-dimethoxy-4-ethylamphetamine), also known by its code name HECATE, is a substituted amphetamine and a member of the DOx family. Unlike the halo-amphetamines (DOB, DOC, DOI), DOET carries an ethyl group at the 4-position rather than a halogen atom. This difference gives DOET a somewhat different character -- at lower doses it functions more as a mood energizer and cognitive enhancer than as a classic psychedelic, while higher doses reveal rich, serene, and potentially creative psychedelic effects.

DOET is described by Shulgin as "one of the Classic Ladies, namely HECATE." At modest doses, it has a reputation as a cognitive enhancer largely free of sensory distortions. At higher doses (6-7 mg), it reveals deeply erotic-to-divine qualities with extremely rich eyes-closed imagery and no suggestion of body depletion.

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical name:** 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-ethylamphetamine
- **Alternate name:** HECATE
- **[[ayahuasca-tourism-legal-status-by-country]]:** Controlled Substance Analogue (US, under 1986 Act) / Status varies by jurisdiction

The original code DOE was abandoned because DOE was already a classic abbreviation for desoxyephedrine (methamphetamine). The ethyl suffix was added to become DOET, analogous to DOAM for the amyl homologue. DOM was already too firmly established in popular usage to rename to DOME.

## Synthesis (Summary)

DOET synthesis begins with 2,5-dimethoxy-4-ethylbenzaldehyde (prepared as described under the 2C-E entry). This aldehyde undergoes a nitropropene condensation with nitroethane and ammonium acetate in glacial acetic acid, heated on a [[maitake-fruiting-body-success-rate-management]].

**2.5 mg:** Significant visual movement with both eyes open and closed, to the point of causing near sea-sickness from movement on a concrete floor. Difficulty finding a centering reference point in reality. Twitching legs, crawling body sensations, and general physical discomfort. Still at ++ the following morning but clearly repairing. One subject described this as "definitely not my thing."

**4.0 mg:** Slow, subtle onset with effects forgotten about until noticed at the one-hour mark. At two hours, effects are real but subtle. Described as not a "psychotomimetic or STP-like thing" but rather a "mood energizer" at ++ or more. Allows the user to "bring into play whatever one wants to." The subject worked at cleaning their office until 11 PM and slept well. "None of the LSD or STP seriousness."

**6.0 mg:** Slow, subtle onset building to full effects in about three hours. All smells became vivid, as were colors and shapes -- "clean, beautiful, serenely self-contained." No visual movement. Eyes-closed fantasy images "extremely rich" and tending to "take off on their own." No dark corners to the experience. Potential for creativity noted. No body depletion or body load.

**7.0 mg:** "Unbelievably lovely erotic-to-divine, deep loving, open, not much visual." Eyes-closed imagery described as form-image-symbol. Sleep attempts very shallow with slight "thinness" and anticipation of darts. Intellect and feeling-emotion intact and functioning at all times. Still at plus-one the next morning. Described as "incredible material" with the assessment that 6-7 mg is perhaps optimal due to body load at higher doses.

## Effects Summary

### Dose-Dependent Character
DOET displays an unusual dose-dependent character shift:
- **Low doses (1-4 mg):** Gentle mood enhancement, cognitive energizing, potential cramp relief. Minimal psychedelic effects.
- **Moderate doses (4-6 mg):** Serene, clean, beautiful [[dob-pihkal-62]]: DOB (bromo) is more potent per milligram and more classically psychedelic. DOET requires higher doses and has a more variable character.

## The HECATE Naming

DOET bears the individual name HECATE in Shulgin's "Classic Ladies" naming system. Hecate is the Greek goddess of crossroads, boundaries, and the liminal space between worlds -- an apt name for a compound that exists at the boundary between psychedelic and non-psychedelic experience, depending on dose. The name reflects DOET's transitional quality: at low doses it is a gentle enhancer, at moderate doses a serene psychedelic, and at higher doses a deeply moving experience bridging the erotic and the divine.

## Historical Notes

The original publications on DOET deliberately used only low doses in the clinical literature, reporting it as a cognitive enhancer free of hallucinogenic effects. Shulgin noted this was partly strategic -- DOM had already been scheduled as a controlled substance due to its documented psychedelic properties and "sizeable measure of potential abuse." By presenting DOET as a mild cognitive enhancer, researchers avoided regulatory attention, though Shulgin acknowledged that the Controlled Substance Analogue Enforcement Act of 1986 might render this moot.

The nomenclature history of the DOx series is itself interesting. The naming convention uses "DO" (desoxy, removal of oxygen from the methoxy group's naming scheme) followed by a letter indicating the 4-position substituent. DOM (methyl) was established first. DOE (ethyl) was abandoned due to conflict with "desoxyephedrine" (methamphetamine). The pattern was then revised: DOAM for amyl, DOET for ethyl, while DOM remained unchanged since "DOME really looked strange."
