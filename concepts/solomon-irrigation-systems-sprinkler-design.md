## Optimal Nozzle Size for Vegetables

Systems applying less than half an inch (1.25 cm) per hour are best for
vegetable gardens. Solomon identifies the 7/100 inch (2.75 mm) bore as
the sweet spot -- covering the largest area with the smallest gallons
per minute. Smaller bores cannot spray far enough and require too many
sprinklers. Larger bores produce droplets too massive for vegetables;
agricultural sprinklers with 3/16+ inch nozzles draw over 5 GPM each,
beyond most home water supplies.

## Sprinkler Design Types

No single sprinkler uniformly covers a circle or square. With a circular
pattern, a sprinkler puts nearly ten times as much water on the
perimeter as the center. Every point between receives a different amount.

- **Oscillating sprinklers**: Among the worst for uniformity. The cam
  mechanism has loose fit, causing the arm to pause at turnarounds,
  putting too much water at pattern ends and too little above the
  sprinkler.
- **Impact sprinklers**: The rocker arm puts too much water close to the
  sprinkler while too little reaches the edge. Diffuser pads make this
  worse. Agricultural impulse sprinklers use scientifically designed
  bores that break up the stream properly without diffusers.
- **All types**: Even the best agricultural sprinklers put about twice
  as much water near the center as on the outer half.

## Overlapping Patterns

Farmers solve uniformity by operating several correctly spaced sprinklers
to create overlapping patterns. Optimal spacing is 60 to 70 percent of
throw radius, arranged in triangular patterns, permitting reasonably
uniform rectangular coverage.

## When and How Much to Water

Water after losing about half an inch (1.25 cm) to reduce leaching.
Clayey soils with two-foot-deep root systems can accept 1 to 1.5 inches
without leaching. More frequent, lighter irrigations suit germinating
seeds, transplants, and moisture-loving species.

Soil moisture is assessed by the squeeze test from five to six inches
below the surface. If damp and sticks together, moisture exceeds 70
percent. If it won't form a ball, moisture is below 50 percent and
plants are stressed.
