## Legal Alternative Products Market for Hallucinogenic Mushrooms in Europe

## Overview

Following the prohibition of psilocybin-containing mushrooms across multiple European countries between 2001 and 2006, a market for legal alternative products emerged as retailers sought to maintain revenue streams. The EMCDDA 2006 thematic paper documented this shift, noting that retailers in the UK and other countries began substituting prohibited psilocybin mushrooms with legal psychoactive products that could occupy the same market niche. This phenomenon represents a classic displacement effect in drug policy, where prohibition of one substance stimulates demand for legally available alternatives.

## The "Herbal Highs" Marketing Category

### Historical Context

The EMCDDA report contextualizes the legal alternative mushroom market within the broader "herbal highs" trend of the late 1990s. Hallucinogenic mushrooms were themselves initially sold under the general "Herbal Highs" label by many retailers, reflecting the consumer preference for organic and herbal products that characterized the era. This marketing category became a vehicle for introducing new psychoactive products to the market.
