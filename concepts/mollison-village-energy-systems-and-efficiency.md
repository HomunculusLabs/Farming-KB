# Mollison: Village Energy Systems and Efficiency

Source: Bill Mollison, Permaculture: A Designers' Manual (Chapter 14, Section 14.9)

## Domestic Energy First

Every house built today can be close to 100 percent efficient by design alone, at no extra cost in construction. Solar hot water systems are now routine installations, and photovoltaics almost cost-neutral. Energy needs are solved mainly in the home by a combination of good design and hardware. Some American towns have enacted ordinances to force buildings to comply with a 60 percent self-sufficient space heating requirement.

## Village-Scale Energy

Coupled with domestic energy conservation, modest power units can supply small villages or regions with their energy. A diverse approach is recommended, with energy from wind, tide, river, solar, and methane used where appropriate.

Like any other enterprise, the criteria for energy systems are:
- The process is non-polluting, or relevant to the place.
- The technology is socially acceptable and locally benign.
- The cost is affordable and amortises under 10 years.
- The technology can be locally produced and maintained.

No matter how efficient a technology may be, if it lays waste to or destroys the basic quality or quantity of soil, water, or clean air, it must be rejected as this is the "economics of extinction." For this reason, Mollison does not include fission processes with radioactive by-products, coal as now used, or mineral petroleum beyond initial or transition use.

## Integrated Energy and Transport

The very modern "urban planning" where city or town sectors are designated as industrial, commercial, residential, or recreational is the antithesis of good planning for transport energy conservation. Wherever possible, life, work, and recreation should be integrated in a dwelling. Not only are households better informed, children less alienated, and adults less isolated, but the need for complex transport systems is eliminated.

We have much to learn from older cities that evolved in an energy-conservative environment -- cities such as Florence, Vienna, older parts of Berlin, and almost every village that functioned before 1930. In all such settlements, the cultural, crafts, trades, commercial, and domestic functions were integrated.

## Village Energy Enterprises

For a 50-house (100 adults) village:
- 2 to 5 families can earn a living from house design and retrofit, with some contract and extension work outside the village.
- Hardware and appliance sales to the village and district.
- Installing, repairing, and tending a village energy system.
- Growing, distilling, or fermenting fuels for engines (2 to 4 livings).
- Fleet lease and insurance, special vehicle lease, tractor lease.

Most energy-efficient systems amortise in 3 to 7 years from initial capital investment.
