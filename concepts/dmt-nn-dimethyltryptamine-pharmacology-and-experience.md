# DMT (N,N-Dimethyltryptamine) Pharmacology and Experience

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a potent psychedelic tryptamine found naturally
in numerous plant species, animal tissues, and the human body. Terence McKenna's
1983 Esalen lecture "[[schultes-ayahuasca-preparations]]
- **[[anadenanthera-peregrina]]** (yopo) — seeds snuffed in South American rituals

DMT is also endogenous to mammalian biology, detected in human cerebrospinal
fluid, blood, urine, and brain tissue. The function of endogenous DMT remains
debated, though hypotheses include a role as a neurotransmitter, neuroprotective
agent, or regulator of near-death experiences.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Activity

DMT acts primarily as a **5-HT₂A receptor agonist**, the same receptor
responsible for the psychedelic effects of LSD and psilocybin. However, DMT
also interacts with several other receptor systems:

- **5-HT₂A** — primary psychedelic effects (high affinity)
- **5-HT₂C** — modulates mood and anxiety
- **5-HT₁A** — may contribute to anxiolytic aspects
- **Sigma-1 receptor** — neuroprotection, modulation of calcium signaling
- **TAAR (trace amine-associated receptors)** — poorly understood
- **ION channel modulation** — McKenna notes that moving a single atom on
  the molecular ring transforms an inactive compound into a highly active
  one, demonstrating "[[mushroom-taste-route-of-administration-and-consumption-barriers]]** — DMT is not orally active alone because it
  is rapidly deactivated by monoamine oxidase (MAO) in the gut and liver.
  Smoking/vaporization bypasses first-pass metabolism, producing effects
  in 15–30 seconds. The ayahuasca brew combines DMT with MAO-inhibiting
  β-carbolines (harmine, harmaline, tetrahydroharmine) to enable oral
  activity with a longer duration (4–6 hours).

- **Duration** — smoked: 5–20 minutes; IV: 10–30 minutes; oral (ayahuasca):
  4–6 hours. The brevity of smoked DMT is remarkable — the intensity
  of the experience is inversely proportional to its duration.

- **Metabolism** — primarily deaminated by MAO-A to indoleacetic acid;
  also N-oxide formation and N-demethylation pathways.

## The DMT Experience: Phenomenology

McKenna's account, delivered from personal experience beginning in 1965,
describes a remarkably consistent experiential topology:

### Onset (0–30 seconds)

After smoking, onset begins in ~15 seconds. A characteristic "ripping
cellophane" sound is heard — McKenna suggests this is "our radio entelechy
ripping out of the organic matrix." An ascending tone accompanies a shifting
geometric surface of migrating colored forms. At the synaptic level, all
available bond sites are occupied during a ~30-second mode shift.

### Peak ("Over There")

The experiencer arrives in a space described as "underground, or somehow
insulated and domed" — McKenna borrows the term "merry go raum" (going
space) from Finnegans Wake. One feels like a child in eternity. The ego
remains intact; fear reflexes remain active; there is no cognitive fuzz.

### Entity Contact

McKenna describes encountering "self-transforming machine elves" —
"hyperdimensional machine-elf entities" that bathe the experiencer in
non-erotic but open-hearted love. These entities are "fractal reflections
of some previously hidden and suddenly autonomous part of one's own psyche."
They communicate in a translinguistic modality — sounds like music and
language that "pass without any quantized moment of distinction" and convey
"alien information that cannot be Englished."

### Cognitive Dissonance

McKenna describes the attempt to process translinguistic information as
producing cognitive dissonance — "a 'wow' or 'flutter' that is building
off this object causes wonder, astonishment, and awe at the brink of
terror." The entities advise: "Don't get a loop of wonder going that
quenches your ability to understand. Try not to be so amazed."

## The Logos Hypothesis

McKenna proposes that DMT and psilocybin "invoke the Logos" — they work
directly on language centers, making interior dialogue a central feature
of the experience. The experiencer must decide whether to enter into this
dialogue and attempt to decode the incoming signal. McKenna frames this as
an encounter with a "universe of active intelligence that is transhuman,
hyperdimensional, and extremely alien" existing "one quanta away" from
ordinary consciousness.

## Authenticity and the Role of Fear

McKenna argues that a touch of terror validates the DMT experience:
"This is real." If one is not terrified, one may be out of contact with
the full dynamics. He distinguishes this from hedonistic drug use: the
experience must "move one's heart" by dealing with issues of life and
death. He advocates an I/Thou relationship with the encountered entities:
"You're long on talk, but what can you show me?"
