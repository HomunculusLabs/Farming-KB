## Old Varieties vs. Modern Hybrids

Holzer's experience shows that old varieties of all cultivated plants make the best crops:

- **Better flavour**: Intense taste indicates high [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]]
- **True-to-type seed**: Can be saved and replanted year after year

### Against Hybrid Seeds (F1)
- F1 varieties cannot reproduce true-to-type; seed must be bought every year
- Come from homozygous inbred lines, frequently sterile
- Give good yields but are more susceptible to disease
- Not suited to varying local conditions
- Benefit [[comparison-raised-beds-vs-hugelkultur]] and hedges as barriers
- **Insect-pollinated varieties**: Grow at sufficient distance from each other
- **Best method**: Select varieties that flower at different times to completely rule out unwanted pollination
- **Multi-layered habitats**: Hedges and tall [[cervantes-wind-protection-outdoor-cannabis]]

### The Krameterhof as Living Gene Bank

Holzer has made a great effort to propagate seeds of every conceivable cultivated plant suited to the farm. The plant diversity provides a living gene bank. Visitors are allowed to harvest seeds in small amounts during tours.

## Ancient Grains

Holzer cultivates several ancient cereal varieties:

### Einkorn (*Triticum monococcum*) and Emmer (*Triticum dicoccum*)
- Low yields per plant but far richer in protein and minerals than modern wheat
- Extremely nutritious, nourishing, and easy to digest
- Cope well with poor soil conditions
- Black emmer is even resistant to UV radiation

### Spelt (*Triticum spelta*)
- Thrives on poor soil, good flavour, rich in protein
- Popularly eaten before it is ripe; can substitute for coffee
- Used as pillow filling to cure headaches and sleeplessness

### Ancient Siberian Grain
- Originally from Siberia; Holzer has cultivated it from 1kg of seed since 1957
- Perennial; grows on the poorest soils
- At 1,400m, sown in June, ripens the following September
- Up to 20 ears per stalk
- Adaptability demonstrated by ripening in 5 months at sea level in Colombia (45C in the shade)
- Must NOT be fertilised -- excess nitrogen causes lodging and rotting

### Other Old Varieties
- Wild rye (*Secale multicaule*), black oats, naked oats, barley
- Fichtelgebirgshafer -- old local oat variety that does well at high altitudes
