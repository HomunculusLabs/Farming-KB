## Making Seeds Come Up

Successful seed germination requires proper conditions for each species:

- Moisture: Seeds must absorb water to initiate germination. The soil
surface should be kept consistently moist but not waterlogged until
seedlings emerge, often requiring daily attention during dry periods.

- Temperature: Each species has optimal germination temperatures. Most
common vegetables germinate best between 15 and 25 degrees Celsius, with
warm-season crops like tomatoes and peppers preferring warmer
conditions.

- Soil contact: Seeds need firm contact with fine soil particles for
moisture absorption. Pressing soil firmly after sowing improves results
significantly.

- Depth: Most vegetable seeds should be sown at a depth of approximately
two to three times their diameter. Too deep and they exhaust energy
reserves before reaching the surface.

## Saving on Seed Purchases

Several strategies can substantially reduce seed costs for the home
gardener:

- Buy larger packets from mail-order houses rather than expensive
picture packets from garden centers, where per-seed costs are much
lower.

- Store unused seeds properly in cool, dry, dark conditions for use in
subsequent seasons, as many vegetable seeds remain viable for three to
five years.

- Share seed orders with other gardeners to access bulk pricing
unavailable to individual buyers.

- Focus on productive, reliable varieties rather than experimenting with
many different types each season.
