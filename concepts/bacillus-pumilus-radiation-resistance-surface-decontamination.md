## Role in Sterilization Validation

*Bacillus pumilus* is widely used as a biological indicator (BI) organism for validating sterilization processes, alongside other resistant organisms like *Geobacillus stearothermophilus* (for steam) and *Bacillus atrophaeus* (for ethylene oxide and dry heat). Its selection in the NASA microwave study was deliberate — if a method can reliably kill *B. pumilus* spores on surfaces, it can reasonably be expected to kill less resistant organisms.

In the MSC-22484 study, *B. pumilus* was included in a mixed challenge population alongside *Escherichia coli* (a relatively sensitive Gram-negative) and *Pseudomonas cepacia* (an intermediate-resistance Gram-negative). This tiered approach validated the method across a range of microbial resistances.

## Inactivation by Microwave Irradiation

The NASA study demonstrated that *B. pumilus* spores on damp surfaces could be completely inactivated by microwave irradiation at 2.45 GHz. Key findings:

- At 3.6 W/cm² exposure rate, populations were reduced by several orders of magnitude with increasing exposure
- The spore form showed greater resistance than the vegetative forms of *E. coli* and *P. cepacia*, as expected
- Complete kill was achieved at the 13.1 W-hr cumulative exposure level

### Environmental Persistence

*Bacillus pumilus* spores can persist in the environment for years under favorable conditions:

- They survive in soil, dust, and on surfaces for extended periods
- Spores are resistant to desiccation and UV exposure in the environment
- They are commonly found in indoor air and on surfaces in homes, laboratories, and commercial facilities
- In mycology labs, they are among the most frequently recovered contaminants from failed cultures

This environmental persistence is why thorough [[comparison-of-surface-sterilization-methods]] is essential before any sterile procedure. The NASA study's finding that microwave energy effectively kills *B. pumilus* spores on damp surfaces suggests potential for rapid turnaround surface decontamination in laboratory settings between culture work sessions.
