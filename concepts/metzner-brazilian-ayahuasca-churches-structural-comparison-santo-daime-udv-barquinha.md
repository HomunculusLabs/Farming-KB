## Historical Origins

### Amazonian Rubber Tappers as Founders
All three churches were founded by rubber tappers (seringueiros) working in
the Amazon basin during the mid-twentieth century. The rubber boom brought
people from diverse backgrounds into remote Amazonian regions, where they
encountered indigenous and mestizo [[cashinahua-ayahuasca-practices]]. The founders—Mestre
Raimundo Irineu Serra (Santo Daime), Jose Gabriel da Costa (UDV), and Daniel
Pereira de Mattos (Barquinha)—each underwent transformative visionary
experiences with ayahuasca that led them to establish organized religious
communities around the sacramental use of the brew.

### Visionary Foundations
Each founder reported receiving specific visionary instructions to create a
church. These visions typically involved encounters with spiritual beings—
often understood as divine or celestial figures—who commanded the establishment
of an organized religious practice centered on ayahuasca. The visionary nature
of the founding experiences gave each church a sense of divine authorization
and spiritual legitimacy that continues to shape their self-understanding.

## Santo Daime

### Ceremonial Structure
The Santo Daime, founded by a Brazilian of African descent, involves the
singing of hymns (hinos) by the entire congregation, led by a small group of
women singers. The hymns are understood as received from the spiritual world
and form the core of the liturgical practice. Some Santo Daime ceremonies also
involve dancing, in simple rhythmic, swaying steps—the whole somewhat
reminiscent of African-American gospel services. This emphasis on congregational
singing and movement creates a strongly participatory and communal ceremonial
atmosphere.

### Afro-Brazilian Influences
The Santo Daime incorporates significant Afro-Brazilian influences, reflecting
the African heritage of its founder and many of its members. The musical
tradition, with its emphasis on call-and-response patterns, polyrhythmic
structure, and congregational participation, reflects the musical and
religious traditions of African-descended communities in Brazil. This
distinguishes Santo Daime from the more formally structured UDV and the more
esoterically oriented Barquinha.

### Testimonial Practice
Sometimes testimonials of life-transformations are offered by longer-term
members, reminiscent of AA confessions. These testimonials serve both as a
form of community sharing and as a pedagogical tool, demonstrating the
practical benefits of participation in the UDV tradition. The emphasis on

### Size and Legal Status
The UDV is the largest of the three [[brazilian-ayahuasca-churches]], with
several thousand members in Brazil. It has also achieved significant legal
recognition, including a landmark 2006 U.S. Supreme Court decision
(Gonzales v. O Centro Espirita Beneficente Uniao do Vegetal) affirming its
right to use ayahuasca as a sacrament under the Religious Freedom Restoration
Act.
