## Agricultural Production as Net Energy Deduction

## Overview

[[fukuoka-puddling-summer-flooding-soil-death-rice-cultivation]]:

### United States
- **Fifty years ago**: Each calorie of energy invested yielded about two calories of grain
- **Thirty to forty years ago**: Energy invested and grain produced became equal (1:1 ratio)
- **Present**: The investment of two calories of energy produces only one calorie of grain (0.5:1 ratio)

### Japan
- **Traditional agriculture**: Maintained harvests of more than two calories of rice for each calorie of energy invested
- **After mechanization**: As increased harvests became the overriding goal, efficiency declined sharply
- **Present**: As in the United States, energy produced is only half that invested

## The Hidden Energy Costs

The energy invested in farming is not limited to what happens in the field. It includes the entire industrial chain:

- **Factories** to create tractors and chemicals
- **Mining and drilling** to produce raw materials and fossil fuels
- **Processing and distribution** of agricultural products
- **Irrigation infrastructure** including pumps and waterways
- **Transportation** of inputs to farms and products to markets

The apparent increase in food production is subsidized by rapid depletion of the soil's [[teaming-with-nutrients-natural-vs-synthetic-fertilizers]] (natural gas)
- Producing pesticides and herbicides (petrochemicals)
- Pumping irrigation water
- Transporting food across vast distances

When petroleum becomes even slightly scarce, food production will take a sharp and immediate drop. At the point where obtaining one unit of food energy requires three to four times as much energy input, maintaining the food supply becomes impossible.
