### Psilocybe caerulescens

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Season | Summer, rainy season |
| Habitat | Shady places on soil, sugar cane mulch, turned earth, stream banks |
| Range | Alabama, northern Florida, Mexico |
| Optimal MEA growth | 81°F (27°C) |
| Thermal death | 95°F (35°C) |
| Fruiting difficulty | Very difficult on sterilized rye; possible on vegetable compost in dim light |
| Incubation | 55-85 days |

The Mexican variety *P. caerulescens* var. *mazatecorum* is known locally as "Durrumbe" (landslides), found near corn and coffee plantations. Its extremely long incubation period (55-85 days) and resistance to fruiting on standard media make it challenging for cultivation. Resistant to white mold but susceptible to other contaminants during the extended incubation.

### Psilocybe cubensis var. cyanescens

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Season | February to November |
| Habitat | Compact groups in clearings, on cow or horse dung, rich pasture soil, straw, sawdust/dung mixtures |
| Range | Mexico, Cuba, Florida, and other southern states |
| Optimal MEA growth | 86°F (30°C) |
| Thermal death | 104°F (40°C) |
| Fruiting | 4-8 weeks on vegetable compost with silica sand/limestone casing |
| Light requirement | Does poorly in darkness |
| Potency | Potent and very resistant to contaminants |

The most commonly cultivated psilocybian species and the standard recommended for beginners. Its rapid growth, contaminant resistance, and reliable fruiting make it ideal for first-time cultivators. See [[comparison-ramial-wood-chip-mulch-vs-composted-wood-chips]] mulch. Its autumn fruiting pattern and wood-based habitat distinguish it from the dung-loving species.

### Psilocybe mexicana

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Season | May to October |
| Habitat | Isolated or sparse, at 4500-5500 feet altitude |
| Substrate | Mosses, herbs, roadsides, humid meadows, cornfields, near pine forests |
| Range | Mexico, especially limestone regions |

The species used by R. Gordon Wasson in his famous 1955 ceremony with [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] to Western science. See [[wasson-hofmann-psilocybin-connection]] for historical context.

### Psilocybe pelliculosa

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Season | September to December |
| Habitat | Scattered, grouped, or clustered on humus and debris |
| Range | Near conifer forests, northwestern USA to Marin County, California |
| Potency | Small but potent |

A cold-weather species fruiting well into December. Despite its small size, it is considered potent. Its affinity for conifer forest debris makes it one of the characteristic Pacific Northwest psilocybian species.
