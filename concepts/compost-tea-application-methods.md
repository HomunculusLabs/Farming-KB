# Compost Tea Application Methods

Applying compost tea effectively requires understanding the target (soil or foliage), the timing, the rate, and the delivery method. Dr. Elaine Ingham's field guide provides detailed application recommendations developed through years of field experience across diverse agricultural and horticultural settings.

## Application Targets

### Soil Drench

Soil drenching applies compost tea directly to the root zone, introducing beneficial biology into the soil. This is the primary method for building soil foodweb populations and improving soil health.

Soil drenches are most beneficial when:

- Soil biology has been depleted by tillage, compaction, or chemical inputs
- Transitioning from conventional to biological management
- Correcting specific biological deficiencies identified by soil foodweb testing
- Establishing new plantings (transplants, seed beds, turf)

The tea should be applied so that it reaches the root zone. In lighter soils, surface application followed by irrigation is effective. In heavy or compacted soils, deeper incorporation may be needed.

### Foliar Spray

Foliar application coats leaf surfaces with beneficial organisms that compete with pathogens, occupy infection sites, and produce antimicrobial compounds. Foliar teas can also be absorbed through leaf stomata, though the biological effects are the primary mechanism.

Foliar application is most beneficial when:

- Preventing or suppressing foliar diseases (powdery mildew, downy mildew, botrytis, etc.)
- Supporting plant immune responses through [[endophytic-mycorrhizal-induced-systemic-resistance]] resistance
- Providing nutrient supplementation (compost tea contains soluble nutrients)
- Treating stressed plants with biological support

### Seed Treatment

Compost tea can be used as a seed soak or seed coating before planting. This inoculates the seed with beneficial organisms that colonize the emerging root system and provide early protection against soil-borne pathogens.

## Application Rates

### Field-Scale Rates (Per Acre)

Ingham provides general field-scale application rates:

| Application | Rate (gallons/acre) | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Soil drench — row crops | 15–25 | Banded or broadcast |
| Soil drench — orchards/vines | 20–40 | Under drip or broadcast |
| Soil drench — pasture/turf | 20–30 | Broadcast |
| Foliar — standard | 15–30 | Full canopy coverage |
| Foliar — disease pressure | 25–50 | Full canopy, thorough coverage |
| Seed treatment | 1–2 gallons/100 lb seed | Soak 1–4 hours |
| Compost pile | 5–10 gallons/ton | Surface application |

### Home Garden Rates

For home gardeners without spray equipment, Ingham suggests approximate conversions:

- **Soil drench:** 1 pint per 10 square feet, or enough to wet the root zone thoroughly
- **Foliar spray:** 1–2 quarts per 1,000 square feet, applied as a fine mist
- **Seed soak:** Enough tea to cover seeds, soak for 1–4 hours before planting

### Frequency

Application frequency depends on the objective and the growing conditions:

- **Maintenance:** Every 2–4 weeks during the growing season
- **[[cervantes-pest-disease-prevention-overview]]:** Every 7–14 days during periods of disease pressure
- **Active [[aact-disease-suppression-mechanisms-ingham]]:** Every 3–7 days until symptoms subside
- **Soil restoration:** Multiple applications (3–5) during the first growing season, then maintenance frequency
- **[[query-how-to-transition-from-conventional-to-no-till-farming]]:** Begin with intensive applications (weekly for 4–6 weeks) then reduce to maintenance

## Application Timing

### Time of Day

Foliar applications should be made in the early morning (before 9 AM) or late afternoon (after 4 PM). This timing minimizes UV radiation damage to microorganisms and allows adequate time for organisms to colonize leaf surfaces before the next sunny period. Avoid application during the heat of the day, when rapid drying and UV exposure reduce organism survival.

Soil drenches are less sensitive to timing but should ideally be applied when soil moisture is adequate — tea applied to dry soil provides less benefit because organisms cannot survive and reproduce without water.

### Growth Stage

Plants benefit from compost tea at different growth stages for different reasons:

- **Pre-planting:** Soil drench to establish biology before planting
- **Seedling stage:** Gentle foliar sprays and soil drenches to support establishment
