### Seed Treatment

Compost tea can be used as a seed soak or seed coating before planting. This inoculates the seed with beneficial organisms that colonize the emerging root system and provide early protection against soil-borne pathogens.

## Application Rates

### Field-Scale Rates (Per Acre)

Ingham provides general field-scale application rates:

| Application | Rate (gallons/acre) | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Soil drench — row crops | 15–25 | Banded or broadcast |
| Soil drench — orchards/vines | 20–40 | Under drip or broadcast |
| Soil drench — pasture/turf | 20–30 | Broadcast |
| Foliar — standard | 15–30 | Full canopy coverage |
| Foliar — disease pressure | 25–50 | Full canopy, thorough coverage |
| Seed treatment | 1–2 gallons/100 lb seed | Soak 1–4 hours |
| Compost pile | 5–10 gallons/ton | Surface application |

### Home Garden Rates

For home gardeners without spray equipment, Ingham suggests approximate conversions:

- **Soil drench:** 1 pint per 10 square feet, or enough to wet the root zone thoroughly
- **Foliar spray:** 1–2 quarts per 1,000 square feet, applied as a fine mist
- **Seed soak:** Enough tea to cover seeds, soak for 1–4 hours before planting

### Frequency

Application frequency depends on the objective and the growing conditions:

- **Maintenance:** Every 2–4 weeks during the growing season
- **[[aact-disease-suppression-mechanisms-ingham]]:** Every 3–7 days until symptoms subside
- **Soil restoration:** Multiple applications (3–5) during the first growing season, then maintenance frequency
