# Mycoremediation of Oil Spills

Mycoremediation of oil spills uses fungi, particularly oyster mushrooms (*Pleurotus* spp.), to break down petroleum hydrocarbons into smaller, more biodegradable molecules. Fungi accomplish this through **extracellular enzymes** that cleave hydrocarbon chains — a process that works because hydrocarbons share structural similarity with lignin, the woody component of wood that white-rot fungi naturally decompose. This biological approach offers a low-cost, environmentally friendly alternative to chemical dispersants and physical removal methods.

## Why Fungi Can Degrade Oil

Hydrocarbons are chemically analogous to lignin in molecular architecture. White-rot fungi have evolved enzyme systems — primarily lignin peroxidases, manganese peroxidases, and laccases — to break apart lignin's complex aromatic polymer structure. These same enzymes can attack petroleum hydrocarbons, fragmenting them into smaller units that are more readily consumed by bacteria and other microorganisms in a sequential degradation process. Fungi typically adapt to oil as a food source within just a few weeks after their primary substrate is exhausted.

## Classroom Oil Spill Simulation

A mock oil spill can be demonstrated using vegetable oil (motor oil requires hazardous waste permits):

1. Fill a small aquarium half full with water and pour a measured volume of vegetable oil onto the surface
2. Add shredded dried grass or straw, which floats and absorbs the oil
3. Stir with a large metal spoon until the plant matter has taken up all the oil
4. Remove the oiled plant matter and layer it between sheets of cardboard colonized with oyster mushroom mycelium (prepared two weeks in advance)
5. Pack tightly into small bins with lidded holes for gas exchange
6. Inspect weekly by gently peeling back layers to observe mycelial activity

## Observing the Remediation Process

After several weeks, **yellow metabolite droplets** appear on the myceliated surface. These are extracellular enzymes the mycelium exudes to break down the oil. The progression can be documented through photography and written observations. A bioassay using red wiggler worms added at weeks four, six, eight, ten, and twelve provides a living indicator of toxicity reduction — worm survival indicates the substrate is becoming less toxic over time.

## Real-World Applications

Oil contamination ranges from catastrophic oceanic spills to routine small-scale leaks during vehicle maintenance. All petroleum that enters the environment interacts with plants, animals, waterways, and geological structures. Mycoremediation using spent mushroom substrate offers a practical approach for:

- Soil contaminated by leaking underground storage tanks
- Shoreline cleanup after marine spills
- Industrial site remediation where petroleum products were used or stored
- Agricultural land affected by oil-based chemical applications

## The Sequential Degradation Model

Oil degradation by fungi is not a single-organism process. Fungi perform the initial enzymatic breakdown of complex hydrocarbons into simpler compounds. Bacteria and other microorganisms then consume these intermediate products in a cascading sequence. This is why mycoremediation is most effective using **spent mushroom substrate** — the mycelial biomass has already exhausted its primary food source and is primed to switch metabolic pathways to attack hydrocarbons. The pre-colonized mycelial network provides both the enzyme-producing organisms and the physical matrix for microbial succession.

## Measuring Success

Effectiveness can be assessed through:

- **Visual observation** of mycelial colonization and metabolite production
- **Worm bioassays** (survival rates at timed intervals)
- **Chemical testing** of water runoff for hydrocarbon content
- **Before/after comparisons** of contaminant levels using test kits
- **Silt and erosion measurements** by dry weight when combined with soil remediation

## Experimental Timeline

Full remediation experiments can span up to three months. Early planning is essential — mycelium preparation requires two weeks of lead time, and meaningful results require patience. Multiple bins allow for staggered worm bioassays and provide replication for data reliability.
