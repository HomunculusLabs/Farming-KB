# Scientific Farming Versus Natural Farming

## Overview

[[emcdda-return-to-nature-megatrend-mushroom-appeal]]; it is centrifugal and convergent.
Scientific farming breaks away from nature with the expansion of human wants
and desires; it is centripetal and divergent. Because this outward expansion
cannot be stopped, scientific farming is doomed to ever-increasing complexity,
expense, and labor. Natural farming, by contrast, is simple, economical, and
labor-saving.

This is not merely a practical difference but a structural one. Scientific
farming generates problems that require further scientific solutions, creating
an endless cycle of technological dependency. Each new intervention addresses
the symptoms of previous interventions while introducing new complications.
Natural farming avoids this cycle by not creating the problems in the first
place.

## The Buddhist Foundation

Fukuoka's [[fukuoka-critique-of-scientific-agriculture]]:

1. An intervention creates a problem (e.g., tillage compacts soil)
2. Science develops a solution (e.g., deeper tillage)
3. The solution creates new problems (e.g., loss of soil structure)
4. Science develops new solutions (e.g., soil amendments)
5. Each cycle increases complexity, cost, and dependency

Natural farming breaks this cycle by refusing to start it.
