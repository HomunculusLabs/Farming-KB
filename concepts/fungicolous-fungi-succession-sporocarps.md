## Overview

Fungi growing on the fruiting bodies (sporocarps) of other fungi — sporocarp-inhabiting fungi (SCIF) — exhibit distinct successional patterns as their host ages and decomposes. This temporal sequence from fresh fruiting through complete decay involves primary biotrophic parasites, necrotrophic colonizers, and finally generalist saprobes completing [[calcarisporium-arbuscula]]**: A secondary colonizer that appears after Hypomyces anamorphs
- **Acremonium species**: Mid-succession colonizers with moderate host specificity
- **Squamanita species**: Specialized agaricicolous fungi with erratic occurrence

These species show intermediate host specificity — they require fungal tissue but accept a range of hosts.

### Stage 3: Late Succession (Decomposing Sporocarps)

On nearly decayed sporocarps, the specialized SCIF are superseded by generalist fungi that complete the decomposition:

- **Mucor**, **Mortierella**, **Penicillium**, **[[mycorrhiza]] in Picea abies.

### Podostroma solmsii on Phallus

Podostroma solmsii is a host-specialized fungus that grows on **unexpanded** [[phallus-impudicus]] sporocarps, a pre-emptive colonization strategy.

### Hypocrea latizonata on Cyathus

The stromata of Hypocrea latizonata form bands on Cyathus striatus (bird's nest fungi) cuplike sporocarps, showing even small, tough sporocarps host fungicolous fungi.

## Geographic and Seasonal Distribution

### Geographic Patterns

Distributions of SCIF are determined largely by their host substrata:

- **Cosmopolitan**: H. aurantius, H. rosellus, H. semitranslucens
- **North American endemics**: H. lactifluorum, H. banningiae, H. macrosporus
- **Tropical**: H. subiculosus, several Cladobotryum species
- **Temperate center**: Higher Hypomyces diversity; polyporicolous species may be more numerous in tropics

### Seasonal Patterns

SCIF occurrence is even less regular than that of their hosts:

- **Autumn peak**: Highest richness in temperate zones, matching host abundance
- **Triggers**: Host abundance, dew deposition, aerial inoculum
- **Moisture**: Constant humidity promotes colonization; high proportions of myxomycete sporocarps are colonized during wet monsoons

## Quantification Challenges

Only qualitative inventories with rough quantitative estimates have been carried out. A proper quantitative census would require permanent plot observation — not attempted due to the extreme irregularity of both host and parasite. However, with H. chrysospermus, host infestation extent can be assessed at a glance.
