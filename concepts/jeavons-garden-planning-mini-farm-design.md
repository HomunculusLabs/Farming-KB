# Garden Planning and Mini-Farm Design

Making the garden plan is where theory meets practice in the
[[jeavons-grow-biointensive-method-overview]] system. No book can make gardening
foolproof—if growing plants did not involve real learning and
experimentation, it would not be nearly so satisfying. The goal
is to maximize quality and quantity of crops through good
planning and timing while maintaining [[jeavons-sustainable-soil-closing-loop]].

## Gathering Local Information

Before starting, gather local knowledge from gardening neighbors,
county agricultural agents, or local nurseries. Key questions:
- Which vegetables grow well in your area?
- When does the main planting season start?
- When are your first and last frosts?
- When are your periods with and without rains?
- What are your soil's special requirements?
- Are there special climatic conditions (heavy winds, hot dry
  spells, excessive rains)?

## The Garden Year Cycle

The GROW BIOINTENSIVE garden follows a seasonal rhythm:

**Winter**: Plan the garden; order open-pollinated, untreated
seeds (allow 2 months for mail delivery); make flats, trellises,
[[jeavons-shade-netting-mini-greenhouses]], and shade-netting units.

**Spring**: Plant flats so seedlings mature while soil is being
prepared; start compost piles with weeds and grass clippings;
harvest compost crops, dig beds, spread cured compost; plant
cool-weather crops early and warm-weather crops late.

**Summer**: Plant summer crops; keep the garden watered and
[[jeavons-weed-management]]; harvest and enjoy. In mild-winter areas, plant
fall gardens of cool-weather crops at summer's end.

**Fall**: Start additional compost piles with plentiful leaves
and garden waste; harvest summer crops; plant fall/winter
compost crops.

## Site Selection

Choose a site with optimal sunlight: 11+ hours is ideal, 7
hours may allow acceptable growth, and 4 hours may work for
cool-season crops. Experimentation will be needed for sites with
less sun. The [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]] length directly affects
planning, as a 4-6 month season requires different strategies
than an 8-12 month season.

## Progressive Garden Development

Jeavons recommends a 3-4 year progression for new gardeners:

**Year 1**: Start with approximately 100 sq ft (one person) or
300 sq ft (family of four). Plant the easiest crops based on
expected yields for a good gardener. Focus on learning
[[jeavons-double-digging-bed-preparation]] and [[jeavons-composting-methods]] skills.

**Year 2**: Double the square footage. Add more challenging
crops as skills improve. After 3-4 years with improved skills,
vegetable crops can be condensed from 200 to 100 sq ft, freeing
100 sq ft of improved soil for protein crops (wheat, rye,
peanuts, soybeans, rice), fiber crops, or special interest crops.

## Planning Considerations

Consider [[jeavons-biointensive-spacing]] and maturation times carefully. Plant
both leaf lettuce (matures sooner) and head lettuce for
continuous harvest. Include both early (65-day) and later tomato
varieties. Save space by staking tomatoes. Place pumpkins at the
garden edge to sprawl over uncultivated areas. Corn requires a
minimum block of 4x4 plants (16 total) for adequate wind
pollination; in small plantings, consider hand-pollination.

## The Sustainability Imperative

To improve soil fertility and increase garden sustainability,
additionally grow 200-300 square feet of [[jeavons-carbon-and-calorie-crop-integration]]
alongside your vegetable garden. This follows the 60/30/10 model
from [[jeavons-sustainability-philosophy-gbiointensive]]: 60% carbon-and-calorie
crops, 30% high-calorie root crops, 10% vegetable crops for
vitamins and minerals.

## Crop Rotation in Planning

Integrate [[jeavons-crop-rotation-detail]] into your plan from the start. A
four-year rotation helps prevent soil depletion and breaks pest
and disease cycles. Track which crop families occupied each bed
each year, and plan accordingly. The [[jeavons-garden-planning-four-year-rotation]] system
provides detailed guidance for sequencing crop families.

## Seed Procurement

Buying seeds for a backyard garden easily runs $10-$20+. Buy
seeds in bulk from co-ops or seed suppliers and measure with
teaspoons and tablespoons. You can easily buy seeds for 6 months
of vegetables for less than $2 through bulk purchasing. Always
choose open-pollinated, untreated varieties to support [[jeavons-seed-saving-and-propagation]]
for future seasons.

## Space Efficiency

A 200-square-foot area can yield over 300 pounds of vegetables
and soft fruits in a 4-6 month growing season at intermediate
GROW BIOINTENSIVE yields—enough for one person year-round. With
an 8-12 month growing season, only 100 square feet may suffice.
The [[jeavons-master-planning-mini-farms]] process helps determine exact areas needed
for each crop based on your household's consumption patterns.

## The Planning Mindset

As Alan Chadwick observed: "Begin with one bed and tend it well!
Then expand your growing area." An Indian gardener demonstrated
this principle by concentrating her unit of water, fertilizers,
and seed on one area, achieving double the production of others
who spread resources over 7-15 times the area. Quality attention
to a smaller area yields better results than thin spreading
across a larger one.
