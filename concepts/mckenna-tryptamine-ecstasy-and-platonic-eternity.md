## Plato's Metaphor: Time as Moving Image of Eternity

McKenna's central philosophical claim rests on a specific reading of
Plato's Timaeus (37d), where Plato defines time as "the moving image
of eternity." In this framework:

- Eternity is the stable, unchanging reality—the "standing now"
- Time is a secondary, derived phenomenon—a reflection or image of
  the eternal that unfolds in sequential becoming
- Ordinary consciousness is embedded in time, experiencing reality
  as a flow of past-present-future
- The tryptamine state, according to McKenna, represents a stepping
  out of this "moving image" and into eternity itself

## Temporal Acceleration and the Shockwave of Eschatology

McKenna connects the experience of eternity to his broader theory of
historical acceleration. He describes history as "the shockwave of
eschatology"—the ripple effect of something at the end of time that
is "casting an enormous shadow over human history, drawing all human
becoming toward it."

Evidence of acceleration is visible in the rate of major developments:
- Fire discovered several million years ago
- Language emerged perhaps 35,000 years ago
- Measurement systems appeared 5,000 years ago
- Galileo's revolution occurred 400 years ago
- Watson and Crick's DNA discovery occurred within living memory

This acceleration pattern is, for McKenna, the temporal shadow cast by
the eschatological endpoint that the tryptamine state reveals directly.

## The Tryptamine State as Transtemporal

McKenna describes the tryptamine state as "in one sense transtemporal;
it is an anticipation of the future." This is a paradoxical claim: the
experience is simultaneously an encounter with eternity (outside time)
and an anticipation of the future (directed toward an endpoint). The
resolution of this paradox lies in McKenna's view that the end of time
is identical with eternity—the temporal process culminates in the
eternal present that was always already there.
