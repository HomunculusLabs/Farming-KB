## Pollination

Most hardy kiwifruit are dioecious, meaning separate male and female plants are required for fruit production. One male plant can pollinate up to 8 female plants. Some self-fertile varieties exist (like 'Issai') but produce better with a male pollinator. Bees are the primary pollinators.

## Key Differences from Commercial Kiwi

- Much smaller fruit (grape-sized)

- Smooth, edible skin (no fuzz)

- Eat whole like grapes

- Far more cold hardy

- Sweet and flavorful

- Higher sugar content than commercial kiwi

## Harvesting and Yields

Harvest in late summer to early autumn when fruit is soft to the touch. Fruit can be eaten fresh, dried, or made into jam and wine. A mature vine (5+ years) can produce 50-100 pounds of fruit per year. Yields increase significantly as vines mature. Fruit stores well in cool conditions for 4-6 weeks.

## Trellising and Support

A strong, permanent trellis system is essential. Vines become extremely heavy with age and fruit load. A T-bar or pergola system with wires at 6 feet height works well. Space plants 8-10 feet apart. Prune annually in winter to maintain shape and encourage fruiting wood.

## Pests and Diseases

Hardy kiwifruit are relatively pest-free. Japanese beetles may defoliate leaves in summer. Cats are attracted to the vine bark and can damage young plants — protect with wire mesh. Root rot can affect plants in poorly drained soil. Good drainage and air circulation prevent most problems. See [[permaculture-vine-crops]]
