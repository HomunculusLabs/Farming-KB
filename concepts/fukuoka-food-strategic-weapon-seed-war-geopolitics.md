## The Hybrid Rice Drama

Fukuoka was particularly critical of the international drama surrounding
hybrid rice. He described how hybrid rice varieties were promoted as the
solution to world hunger, but were in fact designed so that farmers could
not save their own seed. Hybrid rice does not breed true — the offspring of
hybrid plants revert to the parent types, losing the hybrid vigor that made
them productive. This means that farmers who adopt hybrid rice must purchase
new seed every year from the company or institution that controls the hybrid.

For Fukuoka, this represented the ultimate perversion of agriculture. Seeds
had always been the farmer's most precious possession — the one input that
nature provided freely and that the farmer could save and replant year after
year. By developing varieties that could not be saved, the seed industry
transformed seeds from a free gift of nature into a proprietary product
that must be purchased annually. This transferred power and wealth from
farmers to seed companies, and from rural communities to corporate
headquarters.

## Food Scarcity as Political Tool

Fukuoka argued that food scarcity was not primarily a problem of production
but of distribution and control. The world produced more than enough food to
feed its population, but food was distributed according to political and
economic power rather than need. Nations with food surpluses used those
surpluses as bargaining chips in international negotiations, withholding
food aid from nations that did not comply with their political demands. The
specter of food scarcity, Fukuoka suggested, was maintained deliberately
as a justification for continued agricultural industrialization and as a
tool for maintaining geopolitical influence.
