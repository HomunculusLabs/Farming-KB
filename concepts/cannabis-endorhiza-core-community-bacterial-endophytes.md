# Cannabis Endorhiza Core Bacterial Community and Endophytic Proteobacteria

## Overview

Despite significant variation in microbial composition [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] driven by soil type and Cannabis
cultivar, all endorhiza samples in the Winston et al. (2014) study shared a consistent
core bacterial community. This core community was dominated by well-known endophytic
bacteria, primarily within the Gammaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria classes. The
existence of a cultivar-independent core microbiome suggests that Cannabis selectively
recruits or maintains a specific set of bacterial taxa regardless of genotype, while
cultivar-specific effects primarily modulate relative abundances rather than presence or
absence of these core members.

## Core Endorhiza Taxa

The six bacterial groups consistently found across all Cannabis endorhiza samples were:

### 1. Pseudomonas (Gammaproteobacteria)
Pseudomonas was one of the most prevalent and abundant genera in the Cannabis endorhiza.
Members of this genus are among the most well-studied plant endophytes and are known to:
- Produce siderophores that chelate iron and make it available to the plant
- Secrete phytohormones including indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) that stimulate root growth
- Synthesize antimicrobial compounds that suppress plant pathogens
- Fix atmospheric nitrogen in some strains
- Induce systemic resistance in host plants against foliar pathogens

The consistent presence of Pseudomonas across all Cannabis cultivars and soil types
suggests a fundamental and likely mutualistic relationship between this genus and
Cannabis roots.

### 2. Cellvibrio (Gammaproteobacteria)
Cellvibrio was a notable member of the core community, though its role requires careful
interpretation. This aerobic cellulolytic bacterium was present in the endorhiza of all
samples but showed dramatically different abundances between experiments: 16.9% mean
relative abundance in the first experiment (post-harvest samples) versus only 0.095% in
the second experiment (actively growing plants). This stark difference strongly suggests
that Cellvibrio in the first experiment was primarily associated with root tissue decay
rather than true endophytic colonization. In living plants, Cellvibrio likely plays a
minor role as a cellulose-degrading specialist that may assist in cell wall turnover or
organic matter recycling at the root-soil interface.

### 3. Oxalobacteraceae (Betaproteobacteria)
The family Oxalobacteraceae was consistently present in Cannabis endorhiza samples. This
family includes genera such as Herbaspirillum, Oxalobacter, and Duganella, many of which
are known endophytes or rhizosphere colonizers. Members of this family are frequently
associated with:
- Nitrogen fixation in association with grasses and other plants
- Production of plant growth-promoting substances
- Degradation of oxalic acid, which can be phytotoxic at high concentrations
- Resistance to heavy metals and environmental stressors

### 4. Xanthomonadaceae (Gammaproteobacteria)
Xanthomonadaceae, including genera like Stenotrophomonas and Xanthomonas, were part of
the core Cannabis endorhiza community. While some Xanthomonas species are plant pathogens,
many Stenotrophomonas strains are beneficial endophytes that contribute to plant health
through:
- Antibiotic production against fungal and bacterial pathogens
- Enzyme production including proteases and chitinases
- ACC deaminase activity that reduces ethylene stress in plants
- Phosphate solubilization that improves nutrient availability

### 5. Actinomycetales (Actinobacteria)
Actinomycetales, particularly within the genus Streptomyces, are well-documented
endophytes found across numerous plant species. In the Cannabis endorhiza, they likely
contribute to:
- Production of antibiotics and antifungal compounds that protect root tissue
- Decomposition of complex organic matter in the root zone
- Production of volatile organic compounds that influence plant-microbe communication
- Suppression of soil-borne pathogens through competitive exclusion

### 6. Sphingobacteriales (Bacteroidetes)
Sphingobacteriales was a consistent member of the Cannabis endorhiza community. This
order includes genera such as Sphingobacterium and Pedobacter, which are known for:
- Degradation of complex polysaccharides and plant cell wall components
- Production of sphingolipids that may modulate plant immune responses
- Association with the rhizosphere and root interior of numerous crop species

## Phylum-Level Distribution in the Endorhiza

The transition from bulk soil to endorhiza involved a dramatic restructuring of phylum-
level abundances. Under the two-tier selection model predictions:
- **Acidobacteria** showed the most significant decrease, particularly the order iii1-15
  (Bonferroni-corrected ANOVA: p = 1.12e-7). These oligotrophic organisms are poorly
  adapted to the carbon-rich environment inside roots.
- **Proteobacteria** increased substantially, becoming the dominant phylum in the
  endorhiza. Of the 17 OTUs that increased in abundance within the Cannabis endorhiza
  relative to the rhizosphere, most were Proteobacteria, including several from the
  Rhizobiales order.
- **Actinobacteria** also increased in relative abundance within the endorhiza,
  consistent with their known role as endophytic colonizers.

## Cultivar-Specific Modulation of the Core Community

While the core community remained present across all cultivars, strain-level differences
primarily affected the relative abundance of community members rather than their presence
or absence. Key cultivar-specific findings included:
- **Methylophilus** was highly enriched in Bookoo Kush (13% of the endorhiza community)
  but nearly absent in Burmese (0.13%) and completely absent in Sour Diesel.
- **Sphingomonas wittichii** was prevalent in Maui Wowie but not in White Widow. This
  species can metabolize phenazine-1-carboxylic acid and has been implicated in increased
  survival in soil environments.

These findings demonstrate that while Cannabis maintains a conserved core endophytic
community, cultivar-specific factors (likely related to root exudate chemistry, immune
responses, or secondary metabolite production) fine-tune the abundances of specific
taxa within this core.

## Functional Significance of the Core Community

The dominance of Proteobacteria and Actinobacteria in the Cannabis endorhiza aligns with
observations from other plant systems, including Arabidopsis, Populus, and various crop
species. This convergent community structure suggests that these bacterial groups possess
shared traits that make them particularly suited to endophytic life, including:
- Ability to colonize root tissue while evading or suppressing plant immune responses
- Metabolic versatility that allows utilization of diverse carbon sources in root exudates
- Production of compounds that benefit host plant fitness (growth promotion, disease
  suppression, stress tolerance)
