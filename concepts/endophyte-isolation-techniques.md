# Endophyte Isolation Techniques

Endophytic fungi inhabit the internal tissues of living plants without causing apparent disease symptoms. Their isolation requires specialized methods to separate internal fungal colonists from surface contaminants and epiphytic organisms.

## Overview of Endophyte Biology

Endophytic fungi are found in virtually all plant species examined, from tropical trees to arctic grasses. They represent one of the most diverse and poorly known groups of fungi, with estimates suggesting millions of undescribed endophyte species. Major groups include foliar endophytes, bark endophytes, xylotropic (xylem-colonizing) endophytes, root endophytes, and endophytes of nonvascular plants (mosses, liverworts, pteridophytes).

### Lichen Endophytes
Cryptic endophytic microfungi in lichen thalli require specialized methods for maximum recovery. Petrini and colleagues (1990) isolated 506 fungal taxa from 17 fruticose lichen samples, with the majority (306) isolated only once.

### Xylotropic Endophytes
These xylem-colonizing species are primarily xylariaceous (Hypoxylon and related genera), Diaporthales, and Hypocreales. They are ecologically similar to foliar endophytes, colonizing healthy tissue early, remaining quiescent, and then engaging in saprobic exploitation at the onset of host stress or senescence.
