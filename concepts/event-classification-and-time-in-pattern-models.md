## Event Classification and Time in Pattern Models

All phenomena in nature can be classified, understood, and related through a unified system of categories. Combined with an [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]] ers a comprehensive framework for analyzing any event, element, or process they encounter — and for understanding their own role within the larger patterns of nature.

## Classification of Events

All events are susceptible to classification across four dimensions: Nature, Stage, Dimension, and Location. By extending and applying these categories, any event can be given a short annotation that captures its essential characteristics.

### A. Nature — What Kind of Event?

**A1. Explosive/Disintegrative**: Events characterized by release, breakdown, erosion, impact, or percussion. Examples: a landslide, an explosion, [[mollison-designers-herb-spiral-design-construction]], accumulation, integration, or translation. Examples: a growing sapling, soil formation, the building of a reef.

**A3. Conceptual/Creative**: Events of the mind — ideas, creative thought, insight. These are the origins of all designed change.

### B. Stage — Where in the Life Cycle?

**B1. Potential only**: The event exists only as possibility — an ungerminated seed, an unexplored idea, an unexploded bomb. The pattern is present but not yet expressed.

**B2. In process of evolution**: The event is actively developing. Growth is occurring, change is happening. The sapling is growing, the idea is being developed, the storm is building.

**B3. Completed**: Growth and expansion have ceased. The event is fully articulated and expressed. A mature forest, a completed building, a fully developed idea.

**B4. Decaying**: The event is disintegrative, being replaced or invaded by new events. It is becoming disarticulated. A dying tree, an abandoned building, a forgotten idea.

### C. Dimension — How Many Dimensions?

**C1. One dimension**: Linear phenomena, curves. Examples: a coastline edge, a fault line, a path.

**C2. Two dimensions**: Surface phenomena, tessellae, dendrites. Examples: a lichen on a rock, a drainage pattern, a mosaic of tiles.

**C3. [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-animal-systems-in-permaculture]] within the atmosphere, currents within the ocean, nutrient flows within soil.

**D3. Through surfaces at 90° or so**: Events penetrating boundaries. Examples: trees growing upward through the soil surface, roots penetrating downward, rain falling through the atmosphere.

**D4. Englobements**: Events that envelop or contain. Examples: some explosions, organisms, atmospheric systems that surround a region.

**D5. Beyond normal dimensions**: An idea located out of normal dimensions of space-time. Examples: mathematical concepts, philosophical principles, abstract design frameworks.

### Example Annotations

Using this system, any event can be concisely annotated:

- **A sapling**: A2, B2, C3, D3 (Growth, evolving, 3D solid, penetrating through surface)
- **A falling bomb**: A1, B1, C4, D2 (Explosive, potential, 4D moving, within medium)
- **A mature forest**: A2, B3, C3, D3 (Growth, completed, 3D solid, through surface)
- **An idea for a design**: A3, B1, C5, D5 (Conceptual, potential, multi-dimensional, beyond normal space)

## Time and Relativity in the Model

### The Seed as Origin

The seed is the origin of the tree. But we can broaden this view: the tree itself is the current time-focus of its own genealogy. Before it in time lie its ancestors, and after it its progeny. It lies on the plane between past and future, and determines by its expression the forms of both, while being determined by them in turn.

### Encapsulated History

Just as the stem of a tree encapsulates its history as smaller and smaller growth rings, universal time encapsulates the tree in its own evolutionary history. This has more dimensions than can be illustrated on a page.

### The Buddhist Connection

This is the basis of the Buddhist belief that all time is enfolded or implicate in the present — that current events are part of a total sequence, all of which are enfolded in the present tree as ancestors or siblings. David Bohm's concept of the "implicate order" expresses this same idea in physics.

### The Present as Origin

In terms of the time dimension, the present is the **origin of both the past and the future**, located at the center of the pattern. As we read this, we stand in the plane of the present. We are:
- The sum of all our ancestors
- The origin of all our descendants
- At an ever-changing origin on the boundary of past and future
- Spinning with the earth, spiralling with the galaxy, expanding or contracting with the universe

As origins, we are on the move in time and space, and all these movements have a characteristic pulse rate.

### Designers as Time Travelers

Designers can move sideways in the waves of time — as a surfer on a wave-front. They can:
- Transport seed from continent to continent
- Permit natural or induced hybridization to weave an alternative future
- Act as active translators of both life and death

Mankind is an active translator of life and, of course, of death. This is both a privilege and a responsibility.

## The World as a Tessellation of Events

### A Unified Pattern

All phenomena — from volcanic craters to wind spirals, from lightning to nesting birds, from seed to tree — are a unity of patterns long repeated and based on one master pattern. Each one prepares for new evolutions and dissolutions.

The number and complexity of such cycles give us life opportunities, and **life is the only integrative force** in this part of the universe.

### The Design Imperative

An understanding (even a partial one) of the underlying patterns that link all phenomena creates a powerful abstract tool for designers. At any point in the design process, appropriate patterning can:
- Assist the achievement of sustainable yield from flows
- Optimize growth forms
- Enhance information flux

Patterns imposed on constructs in domestic or village assemblies can result in:
- **Energy savings**
- **Satisfactory aesthetics and function**
- **Sustained habitat** for organisms inhabiting the designed environment
