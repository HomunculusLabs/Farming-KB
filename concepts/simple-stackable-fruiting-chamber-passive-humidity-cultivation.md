## Simple Stackable Fruiting Chamber Design for Small-Scale Cultivation

## Overview

The GUILD cultivation guide describes a minimalist [[fruiting-chamber]] system
that achieves reliable mushroom production without humidifiers, cool-mist
devices, or other powered [[passive-humidity-fruit-chamber-polyethylene-sheeting]], with [[gartz-panaeolus-subbalteatus-taxonomy-and-intoxication-history]]*.

## Chamber Construction

### Materials

- **Clear plastic bins**: Transparent to allow light penetration and visual
  monitoring. Stackable design maximizes vertical space efficiency.
- **Polyethylene sheeting**: Used to cover the bins, creating a semi-sealed
  environment that traps moisture from the casing surface.
- **Melted holes**: Small holes melted in the sides of the bins provide air
  exchange. These can be covered with mesh screening to exclude flies and
  other pests.
- **Disposable plastic trays**: 1-litre trays hold the cased substrate.
  Five cased trays fit per bin.

### Passive Humidity System

The key insight of this design is that evaporation from the casing soil
surface is sufficient to maintain proper humidity inside the bin. The
polyethylene sheeting cover traps this moisture, creating a high-humidity
microclimate without any active humidification. The melted side holes
provide limited fresh air exchange (FAE) while preventing excessive moisture
loss.

This passive approach works because the casing soil itself acts as both the
humidity reservoir and the humidity source. As long as the casing is kept
properly moist through daily misting, the enclosed bin environment maintains
adequate humidity for fruiting.

## Watering and Misting Protocol

The guide describes a responsive (rather than scheduled) misting approach:

- **Daily misting**: Casing surfaces are misted each day to prevent drying
- **Post-flush increase**: Watering is increased directly after picking a
  flush, because maturing mushrooms pull significant moisture from the
  casing soil
- **Feel-based adjustment**: Explicit watering schedules are deliberately
  not provided. Cultivators must develop a "feeling" for moisture needs by
  observing the casing surface and mushroom development
- **Never allow drying**: The casing must never be allowed to dry out, as
  this halts fruiting and can damage the mycelial network

## Cold Shock Protocol

A species-specific cold shock treatment is recommended:

- **Harder-to-fruit strains**: Placed in a refrigerator overnight before
  going into the fruiting bins
- **Easy fruiting strains** (e.g., Ecuador *P. cubensis*): No cold shock
  needed
- **Psilocybe mexicana**: Does not benefit from cold shock
- **[[psilocybe-tampanensis-entity]]**: Does not benefit from cold shock
- **[[panaeolus-subbalteatus]]**: Does not benefit from cold shock

This selective application contrasts with some guides that recommend cold
shocking all species indiscriminately. The GUILD approach reserves cold shock
for strains that show improved fruiting response to the treatment.

## Flush Patterns by Species

The chamber system supports multiple flushes per tray:

- **Psilocybe cubensis**: Can produce 3-6 flushes when properly watered.
  The guide targets 3 flushes as standard but notes 5-6 are achievable with
  careful moisture management.
- **Psilocybe mexicana**: Typically produces one large flush followed by a
  smaller second flush. This two-flush pattern is characteristic of the
  species.
