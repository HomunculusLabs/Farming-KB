# Cannabis Rhizosphere Community Structure and Edaphic Factors

## Overview

Soil physicochemical properties are the primary determinants of Cannabis
rhizosphere microbiome composition, outweighing both cultivar identity and
compartment (rhizosphere vs. endorhiza) in shaping community structure. Winston
et al. (2014) demonstrated through BEST (Bio-Env + Stepwise) analysis and
Mantel tests that edaphic factors explained the largest proportion of
variation in bacterial community composition across two field experiments. This
finding reinforces the [[two-tier-selection-model|two-tier selection model]] by
establishing that soil environment constitutes the broadest filtering tier,
followed by host genotype effects.

The study examined five Cannabis cultivars grown at two locations in Southern
California, revealing that soil type differences between sites dominated
community variation even when the same cultivars were compared. This result has
important practical implications for understanding how growing location and
soil management practices may influence the Cannabis microbiome and, potentially,
plant health and secondary metabolite production.

## Key Edaphic Variables

### Nitrogen

Nitrogen was identified as the single strongest edaphic factor correlated with
rhizosphere community composition. See
[[nitrogen-edaphic-factor-cannabis-microbiome]] for comprehensive analysis.
Total nitrogen and available nitrogen forms (ammonium, nitrate) were strongly
associated with shifts in bacterial community structure, particularly affecting
the abundance of Proteobacteria and Bacteroidetes in the rhizosphere. Cannabis
is a nitrogen-demanding crop, and the strong relationship between nitrogen
availability and microbiome composition suggests that fertilization regimes
could be used to selectively manipulate rhizosphere communities.

### Carbon

Total carbon and soil organic matter were significant predictors of community
structure. Higher carbon availability generally supported greater bacterial
diversity and promoted the enrichment of copiotrophic taxa in the rhizosphere.
Carbon-to-nitrogen ratio (C:N) was also influential, affecting the balance
between taxa adapted to nutrient-rich versus nutrient-limited conditions. The
relationship between soil carbon and community composition is consistent with
the [[alpha-beta-diversity-cannabis-root-microbiomes|diversity patterns]]
observed across the soil-to-root continuum.

### pH

Soil pH is a well-established driver of microbial community composition across
terrestrial ecosystems, and the Cannabis rhizosphere was no exception. The
slightly acidic to neutral pH range of the study soils supported diverse
bacterial assemblages, with pH shifts correlating with changes in the relative
abundance of Acidobacteria, Actinobacteria, and Proteobacteria. The
[[acidobacteria-decline-rhizosphere-endorhiza-transition|decline of
Acidobacteria]] from bulk soil to root compartments was partly mediated by the
pH microgradient created by root exudation.

### Salinity

Soil salinity, measured as electrical conductivity (EC), influenced community
composition at both experimental sites. Elevated salinity selected for
halotolerant taxa including certain Gammaproteobacteria while suppressing
salt-sensitive groups. The salinity effect interacted with cultivar identity,
suggesting that some cultivars may modulate rhizosphere salinity through
differential ion uptake or exudation patterns.

### Water Content

Soil moisture content was correlated with community structure, affecting both
aerobic and anaerobic bacterial populations. Water-filled pore space influenced
oxygen diffusion and redox potential in the rhizosphere, selecting for taxa
adapted to the resulting microaerophilic or anaerobic conditions. Moisture
effects were particularly notable in the sandy loam soils characteristic of
the study sites.

## BEST Analysis Results

The BEST (Bio-Env + Stepwise) analysis identified a subset of edaphic variables
that maximized correlation with community dissimilarity matrices. In the Winston
et al. study, nitrogen, carbon, pH, salinity, and water content together
explained a substantial proportion of rhizosphere community variation. The
ranking of variable importance placed nitrogen first, followed by carbon and pH.
This multivariate approach confirmed that no single edaphic factor fully
accounts for community structure; rather, an interacting set of soil properties
collectively shapes the rhizosphere microbiome.

## Mantel Test Correlations

Mantel tests confirmed statistically significant correlations between edaphic
distance matrices and community dissimilarity matrices. The correlation between
soil chemistry distance and weighted
[[unifrac-weighted-unweighted-analysis-cannabis-microbiome|UniFrac]] distance
was stronger than for unweighted UniFrac, indicating that edaphic factors affect
both the presence and relative abundance of taxa. Partial Mantel tests
controlling for spatial distance showed that edaphic effects remained
significant, confirming that the soil chemistry-community relationship was not
merely an artifact of geographic proximity.

## Comparison of Experiment Locations

### Vista (Experiment 1)

The Vista site featured sandy loam soil with specific physicochemical properties
that supported a distinct microbial community profile. Nitrogen levels, pH, and
organic matter content at this location created conditions favoring particular
bacterial assemblages that differed from those at the second site. The Vista
rhizosphere communities showed higher representation of certain Proteobacteria
lineages consistent with the site's soil chemistry.

### Orange County (Experiment 2)

The Orange County site, also characterized by sandy loam but with different
nutrient profiles and pH, supported a divergent rhizosphere community. Key
differences in nitrogen availability and organic carbon content between the two
sites drove community divergence, as demonstrated by the Mantel and BEST
analyses. Despite these differences, the [[two-tier-selection-model|two-tier
selection model]] was supported at both locations, with the same core taxa
enriched in the endorhiza regardless of site.

## Implications for Cannabis Cultivation

The dominance of edaphic factors in structuring the Cannabis rhizosphere
microbiome means that soil management practices, including fertilization,
irrigation, and amendment strategies, can be used to influence the microbial
community. The strong nitrogen effect suggests that nitrogen management is a
particularly powerful lever for microbiome manipulation. Understanding
site-specific soil conditions is essential for predicting and managing the
Cannabis microbiome in agricultural settings.
