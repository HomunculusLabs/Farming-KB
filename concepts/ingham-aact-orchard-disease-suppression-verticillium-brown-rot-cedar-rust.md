# AACT Orchard Disease Suppression — Verticillium, Brown Rot, Cedar Apple Rust, and Late Blight

## Overview

Dr. [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] tea (AACT) to suppress major orchard and vine diseases including Verticillium wilt, brown rot (cherries), cedar apple rust, and late blight. These woody perennial and tree crop diseases require a comprehensive spray program that addresses both above-ground and below-ground infection pathways. The common thread across all these diseases is the need for **fungal-dominant teas** applied with precise timing relative to the plant's growth cycle and the pathogen's infection window.

## Verticillium Wilt

Verticillium wilt is a vascular disease that affects a wide range of fruit trees including apples, cherries, potatoes, and many ornamentals. Ingham's approach focuses on preventing the pathogen from accessing food, space, and leaf surfaces.

### The 70% Coverage Protocol

Ingham specifies a clear target for Verticillium suppression:

- Cover at least **70% of leaf surfaces, blossoms, bark, and dead leaves on the orchard floor** with both bacteria and fungi
- **Fungi must comprise at least 5% of that 70% coverage** — this minimum fungal threshold is critical
- Begin spraying **two weeks before bud break** to establish protective biology before the pathogen becomes active
- Continue applications through [[ingham-foliar-applications-compost-tea-disease-suppression]] every 10-14 days
4. **Post-harvest** — soil drench to protect roots during dormancy

### Why Verticillium Requires Fungi

Verticillium produces microsclerotia — hardy survival structures persisting in soil for years. Bacteria alone cannot effectively compete. Fungal competitors physically entrap microsclerotia in hyphal networks, produce degradative enzymes, and outcompete Verticillium for [[bloomfield-potato-blight-phytophthora-infestans]]*), though less with early blight. Late blight is an oomycete (water mold) rather than a true fungus, but the AACT suppression approach follows similar principles.
