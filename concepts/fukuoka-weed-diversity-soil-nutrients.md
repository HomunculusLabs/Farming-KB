## Specific Weed Functions in Nutrient Cycling

Deep-rooted biennial and perennial weeds such as dandelion, dock, and chicory
tap into subsoil mineral layers that annual crops cannot reach. Their roots
function as biological mining operations, drawing up calcium, magnesium, iron,
and trace minerals from depths of one to two metres. When these plants die
back in autumn, the minerals concentrated in their leaf tissue are deposited
on the soil surface as leaf litter, effectively transporting subsoil fertility
into the topsoil zone where crop roots can access it.
