## Global Adoption of GROW BIOINTENSIVE Sustainable Mini-Farming

## Overview

GROW BIOINTENSIVE sustainable mini-farming has spread far beyond its origins
at Ecology Action"s research garden in California. The method has been adopted
across diverse cultures, climates, and socioeconomic contexts, demonstrating
its universal applicability for small-scale food production.

## Kenya — Manor House Agricultural Centre

The Manor House Agricultural Centre in Kenya has been one of the most
successful large-scale biointensive training programs in the world.

**Key achievements:**
- Directly and indirectly responsible for training well over 40,000
  mini-farmers in just a 16-year period
- Provides 2-year apprentice training to individuals sent by their villages
- Graduates return to teach biointensive techniques to their entire village
- Offers shorter training periods for broader community access
- Maintains a local outreach program sending teachers to surrounding areas
- Has opened training programs to international students

**Training model:**
The village-sent-apprentice model creates a multiplier effect. Each trained
individual becomes a teacher for their community, allowing knowledge to spread
exponentially through existing social structures rather than requiring external
experts at every site.

## Philippines — National Education Program

In the Philippines, biointensive practices were integrated at a national level:

- Biointensive publications, conferences, and workshops were organized by the
  International Institute for Rural Reconstruction
- These efforts resulted in the initiation of a **mandated national
  biointensive educational program** for all grade school and high school
  students
- This represents one of the most comprehensive national adoptions of
  [[phosphate-accumulators-sustainable-agriculture]] education in the world

## United States — Emerging Network

A GROW BIOINTENSIVE network is developing in the United States through
Ecology Action's educational workshops and grassroots enthusiasm.

**Workshop program statistics:**
- 1,163 people trained from 45 states, the District of Columbia, and
  20 countries
- 3-day introductory workshops held across the country
- Locations include Seattle, Stanford University, San Diego, Willits, and
  other sites nationwide

**Community extensions:**
- Workshop graduates have gone on to teach GROW BIOINTENSIVE techniques to
  others in their communities
- One participant's correspondence with Habitat for Humanity led to Gardens
  for Humanity, which incorporates biointensive practices
- A Seattle group developed a rural [[community-supported-agriculture-csa]] Agriculture (CSA)
  project using biointensive methods to sustainably grow food for urban
  populations

## The Nutrition vs. Calorie Challenge

Despite widespread adoption, a critical challenge remains. Many people are
successfully using biointensive techniques to grow food for **nutrition
intervention** (vegetables during [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]]), but few are attempting
to grow all their **calorie food needs** on a basis that also feeds the soil
adequately.

**Key distinction:**
- When people say they are "growing all of their own food," they generally
  mean they are growing only **5% to 10% of their diet** — the vegetables
  available during the growing season
- The remaining 90% of calorie needs (grains, legumes, [[permaculture-plants-root-crops-and-tubers]]) requires
  dedicated calorie-growing areas

**The next step** is calorie and sustainable soil fertility mini-farming —
growing complete diets while maintaining soil health. Once the additional
calorie-growing area has been established, it only takes an average of about
15 minutes or less each day per bed to maintain the garden.

## Philosophy of Small-Scale Impact

Despite its worldwide impact, Ecology Action has remained a small
organization, believing that small is effective and human. The organization
considers itself a catalyst: its function is to empower people with the skills
and knowledge necessary to improve their lives and thus transform the world.

**Core message:** Live richly in a simple manner — in a way everyone can enjoy.

## The Nutrient Cycle and Human Connection

Being disconnected from our food base has separated us from the soil and the
life of the Earth. Producing food from a small area is a strengthening,
slowing-down process — a way of tuning in to the Earth's needs as we meet
our own.

Humans are part of the Earth's nutrient cycle, just as plants and animals
are. The Earth creates what we need — trees absorb [[carbon-dioxide-retention-jar-lid-mushroom-cultivation]] and give
us oxygen. As we become more aware of our place in this cycle, it becomes
natural to plant at least 60% of our growing area in carbon-producing crops,
which also produce calories.

## Practical Action

The book recommends finding 5 friends and getting them involved in GROW
BIOINTENSIVE sustainable mini-farming or other sustainable food-raising
practices. Together, significant change can be made in the world, one small
area at a time.

A healthy soil grows healthy crops, which grow healthy people, who can grow
a healthy planet and ecosystem.
