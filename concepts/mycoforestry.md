# Mycoforestry

Using fungi to restore, protect, and enhance forests. One of the four branches of [[mycorestoration-overview]]. From Chapter 6 of *Mycelium Running* by [[paul-stamets]].

## The Problem

- 40-year tree crop rotations thin soil faster than it can rebuild
- Burning or hauling logging debris removes nutrients from the carbon cycle
- Dense replanted monocultures create extreme fire danger (dead side branches = fuel load)
- Old-growth forests have low ground-level fuel; replanted forests are the opposite
- Soil depletion means each successive tree crop produces diminishing returns
- Logging roads are primary vectors of watershed siltation and erosion

## The Solution: Fungi in Forestry

### 1. Chip and Leave

Instead of burning brush:
1. Chip logging debris in place using mobile wood chippers
2. Leave chips on the forest floor (4-6 inches deep around replanted trees)
3. Inoculate with mycorrhizal and saprophytic species
4. Fungi decompose chips over 2-5 years into rich soil
5. Benefits: moisture retention, nutrient cycling, fire prevention, erosion control

### 2. Mycorrhizal Inoculation at Planting

Cortes Island field trial (Stamets):
- ~700 Douglas fir and western red cedar seedlings tagged and measured
- 4 treatment groups: ± mycorrhizae, ± wood chip collar
- Results after 10 months: **5% increase in height, 7% increase in girth** with both treatments
- Experiment designed to run 100+ years
- Mycorrhizal species used: Rhizopogon parksii (cedar), Pisolithus tinctorius (Douglas fir)

### 3. Road Decommissioning with Mycofiltration

See [[mycofiltration-overview]] for detailed methods. Key points:
- Cost: ~50% of conventional decommissioning
- Myceliated wood chips on road surface → living erosion barrier
- Spores added to chainsaw oil colonize wood as it's cut
- Roads transform into nature trails within 2-3 years

## Species Selection for Mycoforestry

### For Stump Inoculation (Combating Armillaria Blight)
Armillaria (honey mushroom) causes root rot in conifers. Stumps inoculated with these species resist Armillaria:
- **Sparassis crispa** (cauliflower mushroom) — parasitic on conifer roots, outcompetes Armillaria
- **Hypholoma capnoides** (woodlover) — grows on conifer stumps, blocks Armillaria
- **Trametes versicolor** (turkey tail) — aggressive white rot, general stump competitor
- **Ganoderma applanatum** (artist conk) — large, persistent fruiting on stumps

### For Mycorrhizal Partnership
- **Rhizopogon parksii** — ectomycorrhizal with cedar
- **Pisolithus tinctorius** — ectomycorrhizal with many conifers; drought-tolerant
- **Amanita muscaria** — iconic mycorrhizal partner with birch, pine, spruce
- **Suillus** species — mycorrhizal with pines
- **Laccaria** species — generalist ectomycorrhizae, good for nursery inoculation

### For Fuel Load Reduction
- **Pleurotus ostreatus** (oyster) — aggressive, colonizes wood chips rapidly
- **Stropharia rugoso-annulata** (garden giant) — thrives on wood chips, edible

## Fire Prevention Logic

1. Chipping debris eliminates aerial fuel load (the dangerous stuff)
2. Wood chips on ground absorb and retain moisture
3. Mycelium acts as a biological sponge, further increasing moisture
4. As chips decompose (2-5 years), they become soil, not fuel
5. Short-term risk increase (1-2 years) is followed by dramatic long-term reduction
6. The CO2 from fungal decomposition is cool and stays near ground level (unlike fire)

## The Carbon Bank Concept

- Forest soil is a "bank" of carbon, built by fungal decomposition over millennia
- Logging withdraws from this bank (removing trees)
- Burning brush is a "second sudden massive withdrawal"
- Chipping and leaving debris is reinvestment — returning carbon to the soil
- Fungi are the bankers that process the deposits into usable currency (soil nutrients)

Stamets' key argument: "You cannot harvest 3 generations of trees from the same land within 100 years, burn the brush each time, and not thin the soils."

## Integrating Mushrooms into Working Forests

- Wood chip paths between trees provide both fire breaks and mushroom production
- Stump inoculation with gourmet species (shiitake, maitake) creates additional revenue
- Mycofiltration buffers at stream edges protect fisheries while producing mushrooms
- Mycorrhizal inoculation at planting increases timber growth rates
- The forest becomes a multi-product system: timber + mushrooms + clean water + wildlife habitat
