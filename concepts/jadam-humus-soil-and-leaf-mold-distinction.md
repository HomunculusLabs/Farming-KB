# JADAM Humus Soil and Leaf Mold Soil Distinction

JADAM organic farming draws a clear distinction between leaf mold soil,
humus soil, and humic acid, treating them as different stages in the
decomposition of organic matter. Understanding these distinctions is
essential for the [[jadam-leaf-mold-soil-method]] and the broader
[[jadam-soil-management-principles]] that underpin ultra-low-cost
agriculture. This classification helps farmers select the appropriate
soil amendment for specific conditions.

## The Three Stages of Organic Matter Decomposition

### Leaf Mold Soil

Leaf mold soil is the most familiar form: the dark, crumbly soil found
directly under a pile of decomposing leaves in forests and mountains.
It represents the early-to-mid stage of organic matter breakdown where
the original leaf structure is no longer visible but decomposition is
still actively proceeding through microbial and fungal activity.

Leaf mold soil retains significant microbial activity and generates
heat when incorporated into garden soil. This active biological
component makes it valuable for [[natural-farming-soil-building]] but
also means it requires careful management to avoid nitrogen
immobilisation during active decomposition phases.

### Humus Soil

When leaf mold is exposed to microbial activity for tens of thousands
of years, it transforms into humus soil. The key distinction is that
microorganism activity is almost finished in humus soil, which means:

- It will not generate heat when added to soil
- Nutrients are fully stabilised and plant-available
- No risk of nitrogen immobilisation from ongoing decomposition
- Minerals and organic nutrients are at peak availability

Humus soil is mined from ancient bogs in Korea and similar geological
deposits worldwide. The price is comparable to livestock manure, making
it an economically viable input for the [[jadam-ultra-low-cost-agriculture]]
approach.

### Humic Acid

If humus soil is further disintegrated through continued biological and
chemical processes, it breaks down into various humic acids. These are
much more expensive than humus soil and represent the most refined
organic soil amendment. While effective, JADAM recommends humus soil as
the more practical and cost-effective choice for most farming
applications.

## Humus Soil as Optimal Organic Fertilizer

JADAM considers humus soil the optimum pure organic fertilizer because
it contains abundant minerals and organic nutrients in a stable,
plant-available form. Unlike raw compost or leaf mold, it does not
create the nitrogen demand spikes associated with active decomposition.
For fruits and vegetables, effectiveness is increased when some
nitrogen is added alongside humus soil.

## Integration with JADAM Soil Foundation

Humus soil works synergistically with the core JADAM soil management
approach. The complete soil foundation programme combines:

1. [[ultra-low-cost-jadam-microorganism-solution-and-soil-foundation]]
   (JMS) for microbial inoculation
2. Sea water/salt for mineral diversity (83 different minerals)
3. [[jadam-phyllite-mineral-solution]] for rock-derived mineral
   replenishment
4. Humus soil for stable organic matter and nutrients

This combination addresses all soil health dimensions: biological
activity, mineral availability, organic matter, and nutrient balance.
The [[jadam-soil-foundation-work]] recommends applying these together
before transplanting or at the start of the growth period.

## The Phyllite Connection

Rocks formed from accumulated organic matter contain materials closest
to the mineral requirements of plants. Phyllites, a type of
metamorphic rock, are particularly recommended by JADAM for mineral
replenishment. When combined with sea water, phyllite solution provides
a broad spectrum of plant-available minerals that complement the
organic nutrient profile of humus soil.

Together, humus soil, phyllite solution, and sea water create a
complete nutritional programme. Your soil will never be richer in
minerals, and crops will recover from mineral deficiency without the
need to identify which specific minerals are lacking.

## The Complete Nutrient-Tolerance Effect

JADAM's integrated approach to soil management produces increased
nutrient-tolerance in crops. All nutrients have optimum levels;
deficiency is harmful but excess is equally problematic. Acid rain and
soil acidification cause problems such as aluminium ionisation leading
to root rot. Constant input of JMS, seawater, and phyllite solution
reduces root rot because microorganisms that favour aluminium absorb
the excess, maintaining soil aluminium at optimum levels.

## Compost-Free Philosophy

The emphasis on humus soil over compost production reflects JADAM's
[[jadam-compost-free-approach]]. Rather than investing labour in
composting, farmers can use naturally-occurring humus soil deposits
combined with microbial inoculation to achieve superior results. This
aligns with the [[jadam-sese-philosophy]] of keeping farming simple,
easy, scientific, and effective.

## Comparison Summary

| Property | Leaf Mold | Humus Soil | Humic Acid |
|----------|-----------|------------|------------|
| Decomposition | Active | Nearly complete | Fully complete |
| Heat generation | Yes | No | No |
| Microbial activity | High | Very low | Negligible |
| Cost | Free (self-made) | Low | High |
| N immobilisation | Possible | No | No |
| JADAM preference | Secondary | Primary | Specialty use |
