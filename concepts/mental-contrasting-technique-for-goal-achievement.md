## Overview

Mental contrasting is a motivational technique developed through
collaborative research at the University of Hamburg and the University
of New York. It involves imagining a desired future while
simultaneously focusing on current reality and potential obstacles.

Unlike pure positive thinking or visualization, mental contrasting
produces motivational energy that is aligned with a person's
expectations of successfully attaining the desired outcome. The
technique addresses a critical limitation of untempered positivity.

Research has shown that positive thinking without action, or imagining
a dream as already realized, may actually rob a person of the
motivation needed to achieve their goals.

## The Problem with Untempered Positivity

While positive emotions broadly support health, studies have revealed
an important caveat: positive thinking alone, when not paired with a
realistic assessment of obstacles, can be counterproductive.

Psychologist Barbara Fredrickson established in 2003 that positive
feelings broaden minds and serve as valuable resources during hardship.
However, the mechanism by which they support physical health and
healing was only later investigated.
