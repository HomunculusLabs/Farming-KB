# Filter Can Spawn Breathing Apparatus (Jarrold Method)

## Overview

The filter can is a simple but essential component of the boil-a-bag mushroom

port that allows [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] to
escape from the sealed [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]: how to maintain
sterility while providing the aerobic conditions that [[cervantes-green-algae-growing-medium]]. Vermiculite, a naturally occurring expanded
mica mineral, provides additional filtration and moisture buffering. All
materials should be clean and, ideally, sterilized before assembly to
minimize the introduction of contaminants into the cultivation system.

## Step-by-Step Assembly

Assembly of the filter can begins with preparing the metal can. Using a
hammer and nail, multiple holes are punched through the bottom of the can.
These holes serve as the gas exchange pathway, allowing air and CO2 to move
through the filtration layers. The size and number of holes are not
precisely critical — enough holes should be created to allow adequate gas
exchange without compromising the structural integrity of the can. Next,
the interior of the can is lined with a layer of rockwool fiber pressed
against the perforated bottom. This rockwool layer acts as the primary
filtration barrier, trapping particles including mold spores as gas passes
through the dense fiber matrix. The can is then filled with vermiculite,
which provides a secondary filtration layer and helps distribute gas flow
evenly across the entire cross-section of the can.

## Final Layering and Integration with the Bag

The top of the filter can is sealed with a final layer of rockwool fiber,
creating a sandwich structure: rockwool at the bottom, vermiculite in the
middle, and rockwool at the top. This multi-layer design provides redundant
filtration — even if a small gap develops in one layer, the remaining layers
continue to block contaminants. The completed filter can is then inserted
into the open neck of the polypropylene substrate bag before the bag is
heat-sealed. The can should fit snugly within the [[pf-tek-spore-syringe-boiling-sterilization-storage-lifecycle]] step of the boil-a-bag method. When the sealed bag
containing the substrate and filter can is submerged in boiling water for
two or more hours, the heat penetrates through the bag and into the filter
can, killing any contaminant organisms present in the rockwool, vermiculite,
or can itself. After sterilization and cooling, the filter can provides a
sterile barrier between the colonizing substrate and the outside environment.
It is important that the filter can not be handled or exposed to
contaminated air after sterilization until inoculation is complete. Some
practitioners cover the filter can with a piece of aluminum foil during
cooling and inoculation to provide an additional layer of protection against
airborne contamination during these critical handling phases.
