# Ketamine Realms and Dissociative Consciousness

## Introduction

D.M. Turner considered ketamine "the most intense, bizarre, and
enjoyable psychedelic" he had ever tried. Marketed as Ketalar by Parke
Davis and distributed by several pharmaceutical companies, ketamine is
a general anesthetic whose psychedelic properties were discovered after
recipients reported "reemergence" experiences while coming out of
anesthesia. Further experiments showed that doses much smaller than the
anesthetic dose produce [[psilocybin-vs-other-psychedelics]] in its capacity to dissolve the
boundaries of consciousness.

## Material and Forms

Ketamine comes in multi-use sealed vials generally containing 500 mg
of liquid solution. It is also used as a veterinary anesthetic and is
occasionally sold in powder form on the underground market. For
recreational use, the liquid must be evaporated. The liquid is poured
onto a glass tray and set in an oven at 200 degrees F until it has
completely evaporated. The remaining powder can then be scraped off and
snorted or prepared for injection.

## Dosage and Administration

Ketamine can be administered via intramuscular injection or by
snorting. Key dosage thresholds as described by Turner:

- **10-20 mg (snorted)**: A non-psychedelic dissociative altered
  state.
- **50 mg**: Little psychedelic effect; puts one in a semi-conscious
  halfway zone.
- **75-125 mg (IM)**: The highly desirable ketamine trip. A 100 mg
  dose is considered the standard psychedelic dose.

Ketamine is normally taken intramuscularly rather than intravenously.
The psychedelic self-experimenter John Lilly injected ketamine in
himself 24 times a day for several consecutive months without
significant difficulties, though this extreme approach is not
recommended. The psychedelic dose is far below the clinical
anesthetic dose, making overdose risk very low.

## The Ketamine High: Dissolution into Hyperspace

The ketamine high comes on about two minutes after injecting. One
should already be lying down or reclining, as they will soon be
unconscious of their body. There is a break in the continuity of
consciousness, after which one finds oneself in a swirling psychedelic
universe with no concept of being high on a drug or coming down.

Frequently there is no recollection of ever having been born, had a
personality or body, or even known of planet Earth. The experience is
described as "total orgasm with the universe," simultaneously connected
to all things. Billions of images and perceptions flow through one's
circuits. One is not bound into three dimensions; in the fourth
dimension of time, one experiences backwards and forwards with the
current moment as the center of intensity.

Turner described feeling like a single atom or point of consciousness
adrift in a swirling vortex of energies, like a [[fungal-single-cell-protein-production]] within a
being of galactic proportions. Despite the dissolution of all supports
of reality, identity, and stability, he experienced no fear, "as if
the one who would experience fear at losing these things is not a part
of me."

## The Apex: Existence and Non-Existence

Some 30 minutes to an hour into the experience, Turner reached an apex
where he felt his will determined whether or not he existed, and
whether or not the universe existed. He could toggle between existence
and non-existence many times within a second. He had the impression
that he could cause the fluid universe to crystallize in whatever
format he desired.

## The Return: Alien Rebirth

The return to regular consciousness begins with one perception out of
each million seeming to be within one's familiar perceptual structure.
These "personal perceptions" increase in frequency. Unlike the return
from other psychedelics, Turner never found this moment disappointing.
The return portion is often experienced as an alien rebirth, with part
of the mind still running circles around the cosmos while another part
reintegrates with identity. Upon returning to the body, visuals
continue with eyes opened, bearing resemblance to DMT visuals.

## Recovery Period

45 minutes to an hour after injecting, one is back though strange
bodily sensations persist: feeling light (anti-gravity), slightly
dizzy, poor motor coordination, and mild nausea if moving. For a
couple of hours after the experience, it is best to relax, lie in bed,
and listen to music.

## The Memory Problem

A significant challenge with ketamine is that the experience is
difficult to bring back and reintegrate with routine reality. Within
hours after coming back, 99% of the experience is inaccessible to the
conscious mind. The experience is so bizarre and otherworldly that the
normal mind cannot conceive of experiencing in this manner. It feels
as though some part of the mind protectively closes off access to the
dimensions experienced on ketamine. Turner found that taking ketamine
while already high on 2C-B provided a "bridge" between ego and the
ego-less state, improving integration.

## Personality Correlation with Experience Type

Dr. Igor Kungurtsev, a Russian psychiatrist who treated alcoholics
with ketamine, found a strong correlation between personality type and
experience quality. People who are very controlled and have
difficulties letting go, or who have relationship problems, often have
negative experiences. For them the dissolving of the individual self
is horrible. For patients who are more relaxed and able to surrender,
with a deep capacity to love, the experience is usually blissful, even
ecstatic.

## Addiction Risk and Safety

Ketamine carries a very high potential for psychological addiction. A
fairly large percentage of those who try it will consume it non-stop
until their supply is exhausted. Turner himself developed an addiction
after about two years of once-per-week use, and noted that very few
people he knew could use ketamine in a balanced manner. The tryptamine
drugs psilocybin and N,N-DMT proved helpful in breaking ketamine
addiction by providing insights into its negative effects.
