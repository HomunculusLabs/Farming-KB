# Oyster Mushroom Supplemented Sawdust Blocks

Supplemented sawdust block cultivation is the standard method for commercial indoor oyster mushroom production. By adding nitrogen-rich supplements to hardwood sawdust, growers achieve higher yields, more consistent production, and better quality fruitbodies than with straw alone. However, this method requires [[sterilization]] (not just pasteurization) and more specialized equipment.

## Why Supplementation Works

Oyster mushrooms need substrates rich in cellulose and hemicellulose as their primary nutrition, supplemented with nitrogen sources for protein synthesis. Plain sawdust alone is a relatively poor [[mushroom-substrates]] because it lacks sufficient nitrogen. Adding bran (rice, wheat, or oat) at 20-40% of the dry substrate weight dramatically increases nutritional availability, resulting in faster colonization, larger fruitbodies, and higher biological efficiency.

The nutritional composition of key substrate materials:

| Material | Nitrogen (%) | Hemicellulose (%) | Cellulose (%) | Lignin (%) |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|---------------|------------|
| Wheat straw | 0.36 | 30.0 | 41.0 | 15.0 |
| Barley straw | 0.52 | 31.3 | 44.4 | 5.8 |
| Hardwood chips | 0.57 | 15.4 | 16.7 | 26.0 |
| Corn cobs | 0.49 | 38.0 | 28.0 | 11.0 |

Hardwood chips have the highest lignin content, which oyster mushrooms utilize less efficiently than cellulose and hemicellulose. Substrates high in cellulose and hemicellulose with moderate lignin produce the best results.

## Sawdust Selection

The choice of sawdust source significantly affects cultivation outcomes:

### Preferred Species

- **Oak**: Widely available, produces excellent results. The most common sawdust source in Korea and many temperate regions.
- **Poplar**: Fast-decomposing, ideal for supplemented formulas.
- **Alder**: Another fast-decomposing hardwood preferred by Stamets.
- **Maple, birch, wild cherry, elm**: All suitable hardwood alternatives.
- **Rubber tree sawdust**: The primary substrate in Thailand's commercial operations.

### Species to Avoid

- **Pine**: Contains resins that inhibit mycelial growth.
- **Cedar**: Also resistant to mycelial colonization.
- **Redwood**: Contains inhibitory compounds.

Sawdust particle size should be 1-5 mm for optimal colonization speed and structure. A mix of fine sawdust and wood chips (1/2 to 4 inches) provides both nutrition and aeration.

## Substrate Formulas

### Stamets Standard Formula

| Component | Amount | Proportion |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Hardwood sawdust | 100 lbs (45.5 kg) | 53% |
| Wood chips (1/2-4 inches) | 50 lbs (22.7 kg) | 26% |
| Rice/wheat/oat bran | 40 lbs (18.1 kg) | 21% |
| Water | As needed | 65-75% moisture |

This formula achieves biological efficiencies of 100-200% for compatible species.

### Regional Variations

- **Korea**: Oak or poplar sawdust supplemented with rice bran at 10-20%
- **Bangladesh**: Mango sawdust mixed with wheat bran at 4:1 ratio
- **Thailand**: Rubber tree sawdust supplemented with rice bran (approximately 20%)
- **America**: Oak, poplar, or alder sawdust supplemented with rice/wheat bran, corn meal, or cottonseed meal at 4:1 ratio, with added sugar, molasses, gypsum, and limestone; moisture adjusted to 60-65%
- **Korea (cottonseed alternative)**: 150 kg cottonseed pellets, 30 kg beet pulp, 15 kg cottonseed residue, 2 kg charcoal, 350 kg water

## Supplementation Guidelines

- **Supplement range**: 2-10% of total substrate mass; maximum 5% recommended to avoid thermogenesis
- **Effect on mycelial growth**: Supplements accelerate colonization by increasing substrate temperature through faster metabolic activity
- **Effect on yield**: While some studies show modest yield increases, the primary benefit is faster and more complete colonization
- **Risk**: Excessive supplementation causes temperature spikes during incubation that can kill [[mycelium-composites-materials]]. Higher supplementation rates require better cooling capacity.

## Moisture Management

Target moisture content for sawdust blocks is 60-65% (slightly lower than straw at 75%). The palm test provides a reliable field check: squeeze a fistful of substrate tightly. If just a few drops of water appear, the moisture is correct.

Over the course of cultivation, the substrate's physio-chemical properties change significantly:

| Property | After Wetting | After Heat Treatment | After 2nd Flush |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Nitrogen | 0.80% | 0.72% | 0.79% |
| Hemicellulose | 24.3% | 20.1% | 14.6% |
| Cellulose | 33.1% | 33.5% | 22.8% |
| Lignin | 5.8% | 7.0% | 6.5% |
| pH | 6.6 | 7.5 | 4.8 |
| Water | 73.8% | 72.4% | 71.3% |

Oyster mushrooms primarily consume cellulose and hemicellulose. Lignin is rarely used, so fermentation processes that accumulate lignin should be avoided. A falling pH indicates productive mycelial activity; when pH drops below 4.0, the harvest cycle is ending.

## Bag Preparation and Filling

### Container Selection

Polypropylene bags with microporous filter patches are the standard container. Polypropylene withstands autoclave temperatures; polyethylene does not. Bag sizes vary by region:

- **Bangladesh**: 25 x 20 cm bags holding approximately 500 g substrate
- **Vietnam**: 20 x 30 cm or 18 x 25 cm bags, filled to 5-7 cm below mouth
- **South Africa**: Large 2-meter column bags (50 cm diameter) for approximately 50 kg substrate
- **Standard Stamets**: Gusseted polypropylene bags loaded to 6 lbs gross weight

### Filling Procedure

1. Mix all dry components thoroughly (sawdust, chips, bran, gypsum)
2. Add water gradually, mixing continuously until uniform moisture is achieved
3. Fill bags, compressing firmly to eliminate air pockets
4. Create a 2-3 cm diameter center hole for deeper inoculation and better oxygen supply
5. Seal with breathable plug (cotton ball or micro-filter) to allow gas exchange while filtering contaminants
6. Alternative: leave top open with a cotton plug, wrapped with newspaper

## Heat Treatment (Sterilization)

Unlike pasteurized straw, supplemented sawdust requires sterilization because the added nitrogen makes it highly susceptible to contamination:

### Normal Pressure Sterilization

- Temperature: 90-95 degrees C for 5-8 hours, or 100 degrees C for 4 hours
- Measured from when the internal temperature reaches the target

### High Pressure Sterilization (Autoclave)

- Temperature: 121 degrees C at 15 psi
- Duration: 1.5-2 hours depending on bag size

Bags should be cooled to below 30 degrees C before inoculation.

## Spawn Run

- **Temperature**: 3-5 degrees C below species optimum (e.g., 20-22 degrees C for species with 25 degrees C optimum) to account for respiration heat
- **Duration**: Typically 14-21 days for full colonization
- **CO2**: Can reach 20-40% within sealed bags; oyster mycelium is stimulated by high CO2
- **Light**: Not required during spawn run

## Species Suitability for Sawdust Blocks

Not all oyster species perform equally on supplemented sawdust:

- **Best performers**: P. ostreatus, P. eryngii (King Oyster), P. cystidiosus
- **Good performers**: P. florida, P. pulmonarius
- **Note**: P. eryngii is primarily cultivated on sawdust blocks due to its sensitivity to growing conditions and slower growth; bottle and bag cultivation on supplemented sawdust is the standard method
