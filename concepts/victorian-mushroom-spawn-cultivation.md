## Understanding Spawn

The reproductive body of the mushroom is the spore, analogous to seeds in higher
plants. But between the spore and the visible mushroom lies the intermediate
structure called **spawn**—a mass of white, thread-like cellular material that
permeates the substrate like fine roots.

Palmer observed spawn directly during construction work opposite the Brunswick
Hotel in Boston. As workmen broke up the soil, "a white substance ran through
every piece. Starting with large branches, it divided and sub-divided like the
veins on the back of the hand. The smell was very strong, quickly noticed on the
opposite side of the way." This subterranean network was the spawn of *Coprinus
comatus* (Shaggy Mane), which Palmer compared to "a white cellular vine."

The spawn corresponds to the vegetative body of the fungus; the mushroom itself
is merely the fruit. Just as a cutting of grape-vine will continue to grow and
bear fruit when transplanted, a piece of mushroom spawn transplanted to favorable
conditions "will continue to ramify, and in time show the result in the form of
mushrooms."
