# Mushroom Spore Syringe Making

## Overview

A spore syringe is a suspension of mushroom spores in sterile water, used to inoculate [[fungal-culture-media]] or [[mushroom-cultivation-grain-spawn]]. Making spore syringes from [[fungal-spore-print-guide]] is a fundamental skill in mushroom cultivation, serving as the starting point for tissue culture, strain isolation, and genetic preservation.

## Materials and Equipment

### Essential Items

- **Spore print:** Fresh print on foil, glass, or paper (see [[mushroom-spore-printing-and-storage]])
- **Sterile syringes:** 10-12 mL luer-lock syringes (typically 2-4 per print)
- **Sterile syringe needles:** 18-20 gauge, 1.5 inch
- **Sterile water:** Distilled water, sterilized by pressure cooking (15 psi, 20 min)
- **Sterile working environment:** [[laminar-flow-hood-guide]] or still-air box (glove box)
- **Alcohol lamp or lighter:** For flame sterilization
- **Isopropyl alcohol (70%):** For surface sterilization
- **Small glass jar or vial:** For water reservoir (pre-sterilized)

### Optional but Recommended

- Scalpel or inoculation loop
- Parafilm or tape
- Magnetic stir bar and stir plate (for even spore distribution)

## Preparation Phase

### Sterilize Water

1. Fill a small glass jar with distilled water, leaving 1 inch of headspace
2. Cap loosely and sterilize in a pressure cooker at 15 psi for 20 minutes
3. Allow to cool completely inside the sealed cooker (this prevents contamination during cooling)
4. The water is now sterile and ready for use

### Prepare Syringes

1. Draw boiling water into syringes, expel, and repeat 2-3 times to pre-clean
2. For final sterilization: draw distilled water into syringes, wrap in foil, and pressure cook at 15 psi for 20 minutes
3. Alternatively, autoclave empty syringes (without needles) and sterile water separately

### Set Up Work Area

- Clean all surfaces with 70% isopropyl alcohol
- Turn on laminar flow hood 15-30 minutes before use
- Arrange all materials within easy reach inside the sterile workspace
- Have the spore print ready, keeping it covered until the moment of use

## Procedure

### Step-by-Step Method

1. **Flame sterilize tools:** Pass the scalpel or inoculation loop through the flame of an alcohol lamp until red hot, then allow to cool briefly (10-15 seconds)

2. **Scrape spores:** Open the spore print and gently scrape a small section of spore deposit with the sterilized tool. A visible amount (roughly the size of a match head) is sufficient for multiple syringes -- mushroom spores are microscopic and a single print contains millions

3. **Transfer to water:** Drop the scraped spores into the jar of sterile water. Cap and swirl gently to disperse

4. **Load syringe:** Draw the spore suspension into a sterile syringe through its needle. If using a luer-lock syringe, attach the sterile needle securely

5. **Distribute spores:** Some spores will settle; gently tap or swirl the syringe to redistribute before use. For more even distribution, draw the suspension in and out of the syringe several times

6. **Cap and label:** Cap the needle with its protective cover, label the syringe with species, date, and any collection information

### Hygiene Notes

- Work quickly but deliberately; minimize the time the spore print and sterile water are exposed
- Never touch needle tips or the inside of syringe barrels
- If any contamination is suspected (visible particles in water, off-odors), discard and start over
- Keep hands inside the laminar flow hood or glove box at all times

## Usage

### Inoculating Agar Plates

Spore syringes are commonly used to inoculate [[pda-and-tissue-culture]]:

1. Flame sterilize the needle until red hot, then cool by expressing a small drop of liquid
2. Lift the lid of the agar plate slightly and deposit 1-2 drops of spore suspension onto the agar surface
3. Spread by tilting the plate gently, or use the needle tip to streak in a pattern
4. Incubate at appropriate temperature for the species

### Inoculating Grain Spawn

Spore syringes can inoculate grain jars/bags directly:

1. Flame sterilize the needle
2. Inject 1-2 mL of spore suspension into each grain jar through the inoculation port or tape
3. Shake the jar to distribute spores
4. Incubate at appropriate temperature

Note: Spore inoculation of grain is slower and less reliable than using [[mushroom-cultivation-cloning-isolation]], because spores must germinate (a process that can take days to weeks) and the resulting mycelium is genetically diverse rather than cloned.

## Spore Syringe Storage

- **Short term:** Refrigerate at 35-40°F (2-4°C) for up to 6 months
- **Long term:** Spore syringes can remain viable for 1-2 years when refrigerated, but germination rates decline with age
- **Freezing:** Not recommended; ice crystal formation damages spores
- **Dark storage:** Store away from light to preserve viability

## Contamination Prevention

Common contamination sources in spore syringe preparation:

- **Non-sterile water:** Always pressure-cook distilled water
- **Contaminated spore prints:** Only use prints made in clean conditions from healthy specimens
- **Airborne contaminants:** Use a laminar flow hood or still-air box
- **Dirty tools:** Flame sterilize all tools between uses
- **Improper technique:** Minimize exposure time of sterile components
