## Phytochrome Red and Far-Red Light Switch in Photoperiodism

## Overview

Phytochrome is the photoreceptor protein responsible for plants measuring the length of the night, which in turn controls flowering and many other developmental processes. Discovered through the interaction of red and far-red light on plant responses, phytochrome acts as a molecular switch that can be toggled back and forth, allowing plants to "remember" the last color of light they perceived. This mechanism is one of the most elegant examples of biological information processing.

## Historical Discovery

### Maryland Mammoth Tobacco

The story begins with the Maryland Mammoth tobacco strain, discovered by farmers in southern Maryland around 1906. This remarkable plant grew continuously to four and a half metres in height with nearly a hundred leaves, flowering only when frosts set in. While this seemed like a boon, it rarely flowered, meaning farmers could not harvest seed for the next year.
