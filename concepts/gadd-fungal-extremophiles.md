# Fungi in Extreme Environments (Extremophiles)

## Overview

Fungi inhabit virtually every environment on Earth, including habitats that are considered extreme by human standards. From the frozen soils of Antarctica to the scorching temperatures of compost heaps, from highly acidic mine drainage to alkaline soda lakes, from hypersaline environments to water-stressed deserts, fungi have evolved remarkable physiological adaptations that allow them to survive and grow under conditions that challenge or exclude most other organisms. The study of extremotolerant fungi provides insights into the fundamental limits of eukaryotic life, reveals novel metabolic capabilities, and has practical applications in biotechnology, bioremediation, and food science.

## Thermotolerant and Thermophilic Fungi

Thermophilic fungi are defined as those with maximum growth temperatures at or above 50 degrees C and minimum growth temperatures at or above 20 degrees C. [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]**: Decomposing organic materials can reach 60-70 degrees C through microbial metabolic heat production. Thermophilic fungi are key agents in the thermophilic phase of composting.
- **Soils**: Especially warm soils in tropical and subtropical regions, and sun-heated surface soils in temperate zones.
- **Desert soils**: Where daytime surface temperatures can exceed 60 degrees C.
- **Stored grain and animal feed**: Where spontaneous heating can create thermophilic conditions.

Important thermophilic species include Myriococcum thermophilum, Malbranchea cinnamomea, Rhizomucor miehei, R. pusillus, R. microsporus, Scytalidium thermophilum, Thermoascus crustaceus, T. aurantiacus, Thermomyces lanuginosus, T. stellatus, and Thielavia terrestris. Thermophilic fungi produce thermostable enzymes (cellulases, xylanases, proteases, amylases) that have significant industrial applications.

## Psychrotolerant and Psychrophilic Fungi

Psychrotolerant fungi can grow at low temperatures (near or below 0 degrees C) but have optimum growth temperatures above 15 degrees C. True psychrophiles have optimum growth temperatures below 15 degrees C and maximum temperatures below 20 degrees C. Cold-adapted fungi are found in:

- **Polar and alpine soils**: Antarctica, Arctic tundra, and high mountain environments harbor diverse cold-adapted [[diffusion-osmosis-and-active-transport-in-plants]] of metal ions out of the cell.
- **Sequestration**: Intracellular binding of metals by metallothioneins, phytochelatins, and glutathione.
- **Cell wall binding**: Chitin and other cell wall components can adsorb and immobilize metal ions.
- **Extracellular complexation**: Production of organic acids and other chelating agents that bind metals outside the cell.

Heavy-metal-tolerant fungi can be isolated from most metal-contaminated sites. Notably, copper-tolerant fungi have also been isolated from locations without high copper concentrations, suggesting that metal tolerance may be a constitutive trait in some species rather than purely adaptive. Heavy metal contamination generally causes species richness to decline and community structure to change.

## Osmotolerant and Osmophilic Fungi

Water activity (aw) is a critical factor determining where fungi can grow. Most microorganisms are restricted to substrata with aw values of 0.95 and higher. Osmophilic fungi can grow on substrata whose low water activities result from high solute (sugar or salt) concentrations. Important osmophilic genera include Aspergillus, Penicillium, Wallemia, and Xeromyces, as well as the osmophilic yeasts Debaryomyces hansenii and Saccharomyces rouxii.

Foods with low water activities subject to fungal spoilage include salted fish, jams, jellies, and candy. Desert soils harbor surprisingly diverse [[plant-cell-membrane-transport-proteins-channels-carriers-and-pumps]] systems and enzyme activities to function at low pH.

### Alkalophilic Fungi

Alkaline soda lakes (pH above 10), alkaline soils (pH around 9), and concrete surfaces (pH up to 11) harbor alkaline-tolerant and alkalophilic fungi. Species of Acremonium and Fusarium are most frequently isolated from slightly alkaline soils and limestone caves. Exophiala alcalophila was found growing in soil at pH 10.4. Aspergillus oryzae strains from Japan can grow well at pH 9-9.5. Alkaline conditions also arise from industrial processes (cement manufacturing, electroplating, paper manufacturing) and from animal excreta deposition.

## Halotolerant and Halophilic Fungi

Halophilic fungi are categorized by their NaCl requirements: extreme halophiles grow best in 2.5-5.2 M NaCl; moderate halophiles in 1.5-4.0 M NaCl; and halotolerant species can tolerate salt to saturation. No extreme halophiles have been found among eukaryotes.

Halotolerant fungi (Alternaria, Aspergillus, Penicillium, Myrothecium, Stachybotrys, Trichoderma) are commonly isolated from arid soils and salted foods. Species such as Basipetospora halophila, Hortaea werneckii, and Aspergillus penicilloides are specifically adapted to high-salt environments. Seawater (0.5 M NaCl, aw 0.981) supports diverse fungal communities in marine and estuarine habitats, though most isolates appear to be terrestrial soil fungi whose propagules have accumulated as an inactive spore bank in marine sediments.

## Oligotrophic and Rock-Inhabiting Fungi

Oligotrophic fungi are adapted to grow in extremely nutrient-poor environments. Rock-inhabiting fungi (also called lithophytic or cryptoendolithic fungi) colonize rock surfaces and subsurface pore spaces, where they derive nutrients from atmospheric deposition, dust, and slow weathering of rock minerals. These fungi are important agents of rock weathering and soil formation in arid and polar environments. They typically grow very slowly, produce heavily melanized cell walls for protection against UV radiation and desiccation, and can survive extreme temperature fluctuations and prolonged desiccation.
