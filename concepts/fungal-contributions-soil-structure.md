## Fungal Contributions to Soil Structure

## Overview

Fungi are among the most important biological agents in soil aggregate
formation and stabilization. Through their physical growth patterns,
biochemical exudates, and decomposition activities, fungi create and
maintain soil structure that influences water retention, aeration,
erosion resistance, and habitat for other soil organisms.

## Physical Mechanisms

### Hyphal Entrapment

Fungal hyphae physically enmesh soil particles, binding them into
aggregates. As hyphae grow through soil, they:

- Wrap around mineral particles and [[fungal-hyphae-and-mycelium]] their extensive branching
patterns make them more effective than bacterial filaments at soil
stabilization. A single fungal mycelium can extend centimeters to

- Anchor soil at the surface to deeper layers
- Resist water erosion by creating physical barriers
- Connect organic matter patches across soil surfaces
- Maintain structural integrity during wetting-drying cycles

- **Concentration:** Can reach 100 mg/g in some soils
- **Persistence:** Extremely recalcitrant, with turnover times of

- **Correlation with stability:** Wright and Upadhyaya (1998) found
  strong correlations (r = 0.84) between immunoreactive easily
  extractable glomalin and water-stable aggregate stability
- **Mechanism:** Glomalin coats soil particles with a hydrophobic,
  sticky layer that resists slaking

Glomalin-related soil protein (GRSP) contributes significantly to
soil carbon pools. Estimates suggest glomalin accounts for 27% of
total soil carbon in some ecosystems, representing a major long-term

### Polysaccharide Exudates

Fungal hyphae secrete extracellular polysaccharides that:

- Increase soil cohesion by forming gel-like matrices between particles
- Attract and retain water, facilitating aggregate stability
- Provide food sources for bacteria that further stabilize aggregates
- Enhance water-stability of existing aggregates through coating

These polysaccharides degrade more rapidly than glomalin but play an
important role in short-term aggregate dynamics.
