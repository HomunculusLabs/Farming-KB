# [[cover-crop-species-comparison]]ping and Symbiotic Weed Management

JADAM advocates transforming the relationship with wild grass (weeds) from one
of warfare to symbiosis. If we do not succeed in making this relationship
symbiotic, farming will be forever a pain. Recent studies show that when there
is wild grass on soil, it will be much moister, more fertile and have less
disease. These benefits emerge over four to five years of co-existence.

## Problems With Bare Soil

Many farms use black plastic to suppress weeds. However, temperature
measurements inside black plastic mulch at 2pm in early spring show extreme
heat: 46 degrees C (114.8 F) at 1 cm depth, 45.5 C (113.9 F) at 5 cm. After
sunset, temperature drops to below 10 degrees C. The soil experiences rapid
rises and falls that stress plants and soil organisms.

Without grass cover, fallen tree leaves, the essence of nutrition sucked up
from deep inside the soil by trees for one year, will be blown away. This is a
tremendous loss of nutrients. Most fallen leaves fixed by grass will be broken
down by soil macro and micro fauna and microorganisms in one year and return
to the soil.

## Preventing Early Flowering

Fruit trees across many regions suffer from early flowering because temperature
in spring rises faster than in the past. Roots think summer is coming and flower
in a hurry, resulting in poor quality pollen and poor pollination. This can be
solved simply by growing [[cover-crops-soil-health]]: Cover crops soften up the compacted soil layer, reduce
   salinity and boost microbial activity which suppresses diseases and nematodes.

## The Nutrient Balance Problem

JADAM criticizes the common practice of using rice bran, perilla press cake,
and oil cake as primary organic fertilizers. These are leftovers (peel) after
the grain, nut, or fruit has been extracted of its nutritional essences. They
are deficient of the comprehensive nutrients that whole plants have. Fertilizer
made from plant skin does not possess the comprehensive nutrients required by
plants; it simply increases soil nutrient imbalance.

Creating a nutrient imbalance is in itself a form of soil contamination. When
you use skin fertilizers, use them in less than 1/10 of the total amount.

## Cover Crop Species Selection

JADAM suggests sowing seeds in autumn that don't die in winter, letting them
grow and cutting them down in spring. The fallen grass becomes excellent base
fertilizer. Effective species include:

- **Rye:** Broadcast before harvesting persimmons. Seeds sprout before leaves
  fall, catching nutrients between plants.
- **Hairy vetch:** Excellent nitrogen fixer, three to one ratio with wheat or
  barley recommended.
- **Rapes:** Planted in rice paddies, grow tall, and help suppress weeds.
- **Wheat and barley:** Effective with shorter fruit trees like grapes.

## The Forced Leaching Problem

Beyond natural leaching of minerals through harvesting, there is forced leaching
caused by removing crop residues. Current practice tells farmers to eliminate
all residues for disease prevention, but this removes nutrients from soil,
requiring more market-bought fertilizers and pesticides in a vicious cycle.

If you want to grow rice well, use rice straw as fertilizer instead of selling
it and buying something else. Farming that treats crop residue as waste is
waste. It is an exploitation of nature.

## Zero-Input Critique

JADAM strictly rejects zero-input agriculture. Nature's process is not zero-
input; it is complete recycling where everything is re-input. In farming, humans
take away all the fruits, breaking the nutrient cycle. What is taken out needs
to be put back in. Organic nutrients come from sun, air, and water, which are
infinite. However, inorganic nutrients (minerals) are finite resources.

## Detailed Cover Crop Specifications

JADAM provides specific data for major cover crop species based on Korean
Rural Development Administration research:

- **Rye:** Provides 15 kg nitrogen per 0.1 ha with 20 tons total biomass.
  Tolerates temperatures below -25 C. Strong nutrient absorption helps remove
  salt from soil. Vigorous root growth improves soil physical characteristics.
  Sprouts in just four days even at 4-5 C soil temperature. Sow 15 kg seed
  per 0.1 ha between late September and late October.

- **Hairy vetch:** Provides 20 kg nitrogen per 0.1 ha with 17 tons biomass.
  Low C/N ratio of approximately 10 means fast decomposition. Excellent weed
  suppression. Sow 3-5 kg seed per 0.1 ha by early October.

- **Sudan grass:** Summer annual that tolerates heat and drought. Can be cut
  four to five times per year. Reduces nematode damage and cures problems
  from repeated monoculture. To remove salt from protected cultivation soil,
  grow for over 60 days then remove the plants.

## The Herbivore Manure Advantage

If growing cover crop is difficult, JADAM recommends using manure from grass-
eating animals. Manure from herbivorous animals contains optimum nutritional
balance for crops because the animal has already processed whole plants. This
provides a balanced input unlike nutritionally narrow skin-type fertilizers
such as rice bran or perilla pulp. Nutritional balance is the key to high
yield and quality.

## The Disease Prevention Paradigm

JADAM challenges the conventional wisdom that crop residues cause disease.
The recommendation to eliminate all residues ("field sanitation") has not
reduced pesticide use over time. In fact, each year farmers are more deeply
entangled in the trap of pesticides. If soil and plants are healthy, they
will not easily fall prey to disease-bearing elements. Health is neither
accidental nor destined; it is the result of effort and proper environment.
