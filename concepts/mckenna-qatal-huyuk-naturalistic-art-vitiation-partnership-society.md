# Qatal Huyuk: Naturalistic Art, the Vitiation of Vision, and the Fall of Partnership Society

## The Site and Its Significance

Çatalhöyük (McKenna uses the older spelling "Qatal Huyuk") is a large Neolithic settlement in central 
Anatolia, modern-day Turkey, occupied approximately from 7100 BCE to 5700 BCE. Excavated principally by 
James Mellaart beginning in 1961, the site revealed a remarkably advanced proto-urban community of 
several thousand inhabitants living in densely packed mud-brick houses entered from the roof. The walls 
of these houses were richly decorated with elaborate murals, reliefs, and sculptural elements depicting 
animals, human figures, and abstract geometric patterns. For McKenna, Qatal Huyuk held special 
significance as the most complete archaeological record of what he called "partnership society"—a mode of 
social organization based on equality, [[metzner-three-distinctive-features-shamanic-entheogenic-ceremonies]].

## The Suppression of the Qatal Huyuk Legacy

McKenna argued that the legacy of Qatal Huyuk has been systematically suppressed in Western scholarship 
because of its association with the Mother Goddess and the partnership model of social organization. The 
prominence of female figurines, goddess imagery, and apparently female-centered ritual practices at Qatal 
Huyuk challenged the androcentric assumptions that had long dominated archaeological interpretation, and 
McKenna suggested that this challenge had been met with a combination of minimization, reinterpretation, 
and neglect. He credited the work of Marija Gimbutas and Riane Eisler with beginning the process of 
recovering the partnership society model from scholarly obscurity. Gimbutas's documentation of the 
goddess-worshipping cultures of Old Europe and Eisler's analysis of the partnership-dominator dichotomy 
in *The Chalice and the Blade* (1987) provided the [[holmgren-permaculture-flower-conceptual-framework]] necessary for understanding Qatal 
Huyuk as something more than an anomalous early farming village—a mature expression of a social order 
based on equality, ecological harmony, and the celebration of natural cycles. As long as the historical 
reality of partnership society remained buried, the assumption that hierarchy, patriarchy, and ecological 
exploitation are the natural and inevitable condition of humanity would remain unchallenged.
