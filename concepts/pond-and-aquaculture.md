## Pond and Aquaculture

A farm pond is one of the highest-leverage infrastructure investments on a regenerative homestead — providing fish protein, irrigation water, duck habitat, wildlife refuge, fire protection, and nutrient cycling. In [[regenerative-agriculture-history]], ponds serve as the keystone water element that connects [[duck-keeping]], gardens, and pasture systems. For water quality management, feeding, harvest, winter care, and troubleshooting, see [[pond-maintenance-and-troubleshooting]].

## Pond Design and Site Selection

### Site Criteria
| Factor | Ideal | Avoid |
|--------|-------|-------|
| **Soil** | 20-30% clay content (holds water) | Sandy, gravelly, fractured rock |
| **Topography** | Natural depression or swale between slopes | Flat ground requiring all excavation |
| **Sunlight** | 6+ hours partial sun | Full shade (limits productivity) |
| **Trees** | 30+ ft setback from edge | Overhanging trees (leaf drop, roots) |
| **Drainage** | 10:1 watershed-to-surface ratio | No watershed (needs well/replenishment) |
| **Depth** | 8-12 ft at deepest (zone 7-8) | <6 ft (summer heat kills fish) |

### Construction Options
- **Dug pond**: Excavated below grade. Best for flat terrain with clay soil. Most common homestead type.
- **Embankment pond**: Dam across a swale/valley. Uses existing topography, less excavation.
- **Lined pond**: EPDM rubber or bentonite clay for sandy soils. Adds $1-4/sq ft cost.
- **Spring-fed**: Natural spring inflow. Ideal but rare. Most reliable water source.

**Zone 7-8 sizing**: ¼ acre (10,890 sq ft) minimum for meaningful fish production. ½ acre supports ducks + fish + irrigation.

### Essential Features
- **Overflow/spillway**: Critical. Pipe or leveled earth spillway at target water level. Route to garden or [[water-management]] system.
- **Anti-seep collar**: Clay core or compacted zone around outlet pipe.
- **Shallow shelves (0-3 ft)**: 20-30% of perimeter for emergent plants, duck access, thermal refuge.
- **Deep zone (8-12 ft)**: 60-70% of area. Overwintering, thermal stratification in summer.

## Fish Species for Zone 7-8 Ponds

| Species | Temp Range (°F) | Stocking/Acre | Growth | Feed | Notes |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|--------|------|-------|
| **Channel Catfish** | 65-85 | 500-1000 | Fast (1-2 lb/yr) | Omnivore | Hardiest, tolerates poor water |
| **Bluegill** | 65-85 | 500-1000 | Moderate | Insectivore | Forage base for bass |
| **Largemouth Bass** | 65-80 | 50-100 | Slow | Predator | Controls bluegill population |
| **Tilapia** | 70-90+ | 1000-3000 | Very fast | Herbivore | **Dies below 50°F** — seasonal only |
| **Rainbow Trout** | 50-65 | 200-500 | Fast (spring/fall) | Carnivore | Needs cold water, spring-fed |
| **Fathead Minnow** | 50-75 | 1000-2000 | Fast | Forage | Feed for bass/trout |
| **Koi/Goldfish** | 35-85 | 100-500 | Slow | Omnivore | Ornamental + algae control |

### Stocking Strategy
- **Simplest**: Catfish only (500/acre). Supplement feed, harvest in 1-2 years.
- **Balanced**: Bluegill (500) + bass (50) + fathead minnows (500). Self-sustaining with minimal feed.
- **Polyculture**: Catfish + bluegill + bass. Each occupies different ecological niche.
- **Seasonal**: Tilapia stocked late May, harvested before October frost. Highest yield per season.

## Duck Integration

Ducks and ponds are synergistic — see [[duck-keeping]] for duck-specific care.

### Benefits
- Duck manure fertilizes pond → feeds phytoplankton → feeds fish
- Ducks eat aquatic insects, snails (some parasites), duckweed (prevents overgrowth)
- Ducks control mosquito larvae naturally
- Provides natural aeration through swimming activity

### Management Rules
- **Stocking**: 10-20 ducks per acre of water surface. More degrades water quality.
- **Shallow access**: Ensure shelves for dabbling ducks (mallard-derived breeds).
- **Plant protection**: Cage or fence emergent plants until established — ducks uproot young plantings.
- **Manure loading**: 10 ducks produce ~150 lbs manure/year. Excess causes ammonia spikes. Monitor water quality.

## Aquatic Plants

### Edible Species
| Plant | Edible Part | Depth | Notes |
|-------|------------|-------|-------|
| **Watercress** | Leaves/stems | 0-2 in flowing | Cold water, high in nutrients |
| **Lotus** | Tubers, seeds, young leaves | 2-4 ft | Ornamental + edible, perennial |
| **Arrowhead (Wapato)** | Tubers | 6-12 in | Native, traditionally harvested |
| **Water Chestnut** | Corms | 1-2 ft | Annual, needs warm temps |
| **Pickerelweed** | Seeds | 1-3 ft | Edible seeds, good habitat |

### Habitat & Filtration Plants
- **Cattails**: Edge plant, nutrient uptake, wildlife cover. Can be invasive — contain with root barrier.
- **Pickerelweed**: Purple flowers, fish habitat, edges.
- **Water iris**: Beautiful, edges, nutrient uptake.
- **Eelgrass/pondweed**: Submerged oxygenators.
- **Water lily**: Shade surface, reduce algae, habitat.

### Duckweed (Lemna spp.)
- Fastest-growing plant on Earth — doubles in 2-3 days
- 35-40% protein — excellent fish and duck feed
- Harvest 50% of surface weekly to prevent complete coverage (blocks light/air)
- Can be dried and stored as winter feed
- **Caution**: Treat as crop, not weed. Manage aggressively or it blankets the pond.
