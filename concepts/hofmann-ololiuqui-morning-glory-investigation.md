after which "thousands of fantastic images and demons then appeared to

## Contradictory Early Reports

When Hofmann decided in 1959 to investigate ololiuhqui, only a single
chemical study existed: the work of pharmacologist C. G. Santesson of
Stockholm from 1937, who had not succeeded in isolating an active
substance in pure form. Contradictory findings had been published about
the seeds' activity. Psychiatrist H. Osmond conducted a self-experiment
with 60 to 100 seeds in 1955, experiencing apathy and emptiness
accompanied by enhanced visual sensitivity, followed by relaxation.
However, V. J. Kinross-Wright's 1958 study found that eight subjects
taking up to 125 seeds perceived no effects at all.

## Seeds from Wasson

Through R. Gordon Wasson's mediation, Hofmann obtained two samples of
ololiuhqui seeds. The round, light brown seeds from Huautla were
identified as Rivea (Turbina) corymbosa, while the black, angular seeds
from San Bartolo Yautepec were identified as Ipomoea violacea.
