# AACT Applications Guide (Ingham)

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: The Field Guide I for Actively [[actively-aerated-compost-tea-aact-brewing]] Tea, Dr. Elaine Ingham, Soil Foodweb Inc.

Ingham provides detailed guidance on how and when to apply AACT, including [[compost-tea-application-rates]], timing, and strategies for different situations.

## Application Goals (Priority Order)

1. **First goal**: [[aact-disease-suppression-mechanisms-ingham]] — establish beneficial organisms that out-compete pathogens on leaf surfaces and in soil
2. **Second goal**: Improve soil tilth, decrease water use, improve aeration — build soil structure through biological activity
3. **Third goal**: Improve plant nutrition — cycle nutrients through the foodweb

## Foliar Application

### Coverage Targets
- High diversity bacteria: 60-70% of leaf surface
- Fungi: 2-5% of leaf surface
- If disease is rampant, fungal microorganisms become critical — mildew cannot be controlled without adequate [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] in the tea

### Application Rates
- Standard: 5 gallons of tea per acre
- Mildew alert: 10 gallons per acre
- Soil drench at planting: 15-20 gallons per acre
- Foliar during season: 3 applications at 5 gallons per acre

### Timing
- Start applications two weeks before bud break
- Usually see overnight improvement in plant color and [[cervantes-pest-disease-prevention-overview]]
- If plants are already healthy, dramatic changes are less apparent

## Application Schedule

### First Year (Establishment Phase)
- Apply every 7-10 days during the first four months
- Then once per month for the rest of the season
- After the first year, three applications per [[extending-growing-season]] is maintenance level

### Ongoing Maintenance
- If any type of disturbance: every 7-10 days if major, can cut back for minimal disturbance
- Disturbances include: air pollution, pesticide drift, plowing, floods, freezes, drought, excessive heat, heavy traffic

### After Chemical Applications
- Apply AACT after any chemical application
- [[ingham-manure-antibiotics-chemical-residues-composting]] can harm microorganisms
- Even sulfur (allowed by organic standards as a pesticide) can cause problems
- If inorganic fertilizer applied above 100 lbs/acre, apply water first to move fertilizer into soil, then apply tea

## Seasonal Timing

### Fall Applications
- Apply to plant residues after harvest
- Ensure organisms are present to balance the foodweb
- Organisms have all winter to improve the foodweb for next summer's growth
- When plants have lots of leaves, establish beneficials on bark with solution in fall, then do soils

### Winter Applications
- Apply if soil temperatures are high enough for organisms to get into soil and establish before freezing
- Cold-loving soil fungi wake up when it's cold (2-3 weeks to activate)
- Once awake, they have no competitors — most rapid decomposition occurs in winter
- When soil freezes, fungi shut down until thaw

### Spring
- Two weeks BEFORE budbreak: begin [[ingham-foliar-applications-compost-tea-disease-suppression]]
- Soil drench in spring before planting

## Soil Drench Considerations

- Wait until soil is warm enough for organisms to grow
- If tea is added straight to sand with no [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]], organisms don't survive (no food resources)
- VAM (vesicular [[comparison-soil-food-web-vs-arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]]) inoculation: mix tea and VAM into pancake-batter consistency, dip roots before planting
- Do NOT use gels for root dip — they smother roots and prevent air passage

## Foliar Feeding Mechanism

Plants control what nutrients they take up through leaves:
- If a plant doesn't need nutrients, it doesn't pump out sugars to feed the organisms in the tea — fewer nutrients get taken into the plant
- Plant controls what it needs
