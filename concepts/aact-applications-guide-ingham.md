# AACT Applications Guide (Ingham)

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: The Field Guide I for Actively [[actively-aerated-compost-tea-aact-brewing]] Tea, Dr. Elaine Ingham, Soil Foodweb Inc.

Ingham provides detailed guidance on how and when to apply AACT, including application rates, timing, and strategies for different situations.

## Application Goals (Priority Order)

1. **First goal**: [[aact-disease-suppression-mechanisms-ingham]] — establish beneficial organisms that out-compete pathogens on leaf surfaces and in soil
2. **Second goal**: Improve soil tilth, decrease water use, improve aeration — build soil structure through biological activity
3. **Third goal**: Improve plant nutrition — cycle nutrients through the foodweb

## Foliar Application

### Coverage Targets
- High diversity bacteria: 60-70% of leaf surface
- Fungi: 2-5% of leaf surface
- If disease is rampant, fungal microorganisms become critical — mildew cannot be controlled without adequate fungal biomass in the tea

### Application Rates
- Standard: 5 gallons of tea per acre
- Mildew alert: 10 gallons per acre
- Soil drench at planting: 15-20 gallons per acre
- Foliar during season: 3 applications at 5 gallons per acre

### Timing
- Start applications two weeks before bud break
- Usually see overnight improvement in plant color and [[cervantes-pest-disease-prevention-overview]]
- If plants are already healthy, dramatic changes are less apparent

## Application Schedule

### First Year (Establishment Phase)
- Apply every 7-10 days during the first four months
- Then once per month for the rest of the season
- After the first year, three applications per growing season is maintenance level

### Ongoing Maintenance
- If any type of disturbance: every 7-10 days if major, can cut back for minimal disturbance
- Disturbances include: air pollution, pesticide drift, plowing, floods, freezes, drought, excessive heat, heavy traffic

### After Chemical Applications
- Apply AACT after any chemical application
- Chemical residues can harm microorganisms
- Even sulfur (allowed by organic standards as a pesticide) can cause problems
- If inorganic fertilizer applied above 100 lbs/acre, apply water first to move fertilizer into soil, then apply tea

## Seasonal Timing

### Fall Applications
- Apply to plant residues after harvest
- Ensure organisms are present to balance the foodweb
- Organisms have all winter to improve the foodweb for next summer's growth
- When plants have lots of leaves, establish beneficials on bark with solution in fall, then do soils

### Winter Applications
- Apply if soil temperatures are high enough for organisms to get into soil and establish before freezing
- Cold-loving soil fungi wake up when it's cold (2-3 weeks to activate)
- Once awake, they have no competitors — most rapid decomposition occurs in winter
- When soil freezes, fungi shut down until thaw

### Spring
- Two weeks BEFORE budbreak: begin foliar applications
- Soil drench in spring before planting

## Soil Drench Considerations

- Wait until soil is warm enough for organisms to grow
- If tea is added straight to sand with no organic matter, organisms don't survive (no food resources)
- VAM (vesicular [[comparison-soil-food-web-vs-arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]]) inoculation: mix tea and VAM into pancake-batter consistency, dip roots before planting
- Do NOT use gels for root dip — they smother roots and prevent air passage

## Foliar Feeding Mechanism

Plants control what nutrients they take up through leaves:
- If a plant doesn't need nutrients, it doesn't pump out sugars to feed the organisms in the tea — fewer nutrients get taken into the plant
- Plant controls what it needs
- Temperature above freezing is sufficient for application, even if it drops to freezing at night (as long as organisms get into soil before freezing)

## Nutrient Efficiency

### Nutrients in Tea vs. Soil Application
- Adding amendments in tea means you need LESS material than soil application
- Microbes retain nutrients much better when grown with nutrients in the tea
- When added to soil directly, nutrients leach without microorganisms to hold them
- Compost tea with fish hydrolysate, protein meals, or other organic N source provides sufficient nitrogen

### The NPK Reality
- Don't fall for the NPK game — organic N is present as protein, not inorganic N
- Most N in soil is present as biology (bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, worms) or organic matter
- Getting these not-plant-available forms converted to plant-available forms is what the biology does
- Only need to replace what was removed in harvest: 25-35 lbs N per acre for gardens
- Compost alone is enough N to replace this need

### Compost Nutrient Content
- Good compost at 15:1 C:N contains about 25 lbs N per 1,000 lbs compost (at 50% moisture)
- One ton of compost per acre meets plant N needs — barely a dusting over soil
- Fish products, protein meals, feathermeal can substitute but at lower rates (C:N ~10:1)

## Combining AACT with Fertilizers

### With Inorganic Fertilizer
- If used, apply AACT immediately after
- Add microbial food to the chemical first to reduce [[ingham-antibiotics-osmotic-shock-compost-tea]]
- Inorganic chemicals are salts that take water away from organisms and plants
- Above 100 lbs/acre of inorganic fertilizer becomes problematic for microorganisms

### With Organic Fertilizer
- Combine with foods that feed microbes so they can hold nutrients in soil instead of allowing leaching
- This reduces total nutrient requirements significantly

## Disease-Specific Notes

### Powdery Mildew
- Requires adequate fungal biomass in tea (above 2 micrograms/ml)
- Without fungi in the tea, mildew cannot be controlled when it is severe
- Black spot is more easily controlled by bacterial coverage alone

### Verticillium Wilt
- Cover leaf, blossom, bark, and dead leaves on orchard floor at least 70% with bacteria AND fungi
- Fungi must be at least 5% of that 70% coverage
- Start two weeks before bud break
- Spray orchard floor and downed leaves in fall

### Peach Leaf Curl
- NOT a single-spray solution — requires ongoing applications
- Fall spray before leaves drop
- Soil drench in fall for decomposition of infected leaves
- Spring soil drench
- Foliar every 10-14 days starting two weeks before bud break
