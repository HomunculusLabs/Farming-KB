## The Seasonal Cycle

### Autumn: Broadcasting Over Ripening Rice

The cycle begins in autumn. Fukuoka simply broadcasts clover and winter grain
(barley or wheat) over the ripening heads of rice before the fall harvest. The
clover seed and grain seed fall among the standing rice plants, settling into
the moist soil surface. This timing is critical — the rice plants provide shade
and protection for the newly broadcast seeds, and the residual moisture from the
maturing rice creates ideal germination conditions. No tillage is needed because
the seeds are simply dropped onto the soil surface.

### Autumn: Rice Harvest

Later, he harvests the rice while treading on the young shoots of winter grain.
The treading actually presses the winter grain seeds more firmly into contact
with the soil, improving germination. After leaving the rice to dry for three
days, he threshs it then scatters the straw uncut over the entire field. If
chicken droppings are on hand, he scatters these over the straw. The uncut rice
straw serves as mulch, retaining moisture, suppressing weeds, and gradually
decomposing to feed the soil microorganisms.

### Winter: Sowing Rice in Clay Pellets

Next, he forms clay pellets containing seed rice and scatters the pellets over
the straw before the New Year. The clay protects the rice seeds from birds and
rodents during the winter months and prevents premature germination. With the
winter [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-mulching-and-weed-suppression]].

He then floods the field for four to five days to weaken the clover and give the
rice shoots a chance to break through the cover of clover. This brief flooding
is the only water management intervention in the entire cycle. The flooding does
not kill the clover — it merely checks its vigorous growth long enough for the
rice seedlings to establish themselves above the clover canopy.

### Summer: Minimal Irrigation

In June and July, he leaves the field unirrigated, and in August he runs water
through the drainage ditches once every week or ten days. This minimal water
management stands in stark contrast to conventional [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]]. The intermittent drainage
allows the soil to breathe and maintains the health of soil microorganisms that
would be killed by prolonged flooding.

## The Role of Clover

[[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]], a weed suppressant, a soil
builder, and a mulch crop. The clover grows throughout the fall and winter,
protecting the soil surface and adding nitrogen through its root nodules. In
spring, its luxuriant growth shades the soil and prevents weed germination. The
brief flooding in May temporarily weakens the clover without killing it, allowing
rice seedlings to emerge. After the rice is harvested in autumn, the clover
quickly recovers and the cycle begins again.
