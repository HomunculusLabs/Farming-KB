# EMCDDA Clubbing Survey Prevalence Data and Polydrug Use Patterns with Mushrooms

## Source
*EMCDDA (2006) — [[emcdda-epod-project-emerging-trend-detection-methodology]] Case Study*

## Summary
The EMCDDA's 2006 case study compiled targeted [[emcdda-mixmag-survey-magic-mushroom-prevalence-trends-2000-2005]] among dance
music and nightclub populations compared to general population surveys. These
non-probability surveys, conducted in Belgium, France, Hungary, Italy, the
Netherlands, and the UK between 1999 and 2005, demonstrated that mushroom use was
strongly associated with polydrug patterns, nightlife settings, and specific
subcultural milieus. The data revealed lifetime prevalence ranging from 9% to 55%
among clubbers — far exceeding the 1-8% typical of general population surveys.

## Survey Methodology and Limitations

The clubbing surveys were targeted (non-probability) studies conducted in dance
music settings across six European countries. Each survey used different sample
sizes, recruitment methods, and settings, making direct cross-country comparisons
problematic. However, the consistent finding across all surveys was that mushroom
prevalence among clubbers substantially exceeded general population estimates.

The surveys included: Hungary (1999, Budapest, 1,059 respondents across 33 party
settings), Italy (2000, Bologna, 2,015 respondents at a rave parade), the Czech
Republic (2003, 1,652 respondents in dance party settings), the UK (2003, 805
respondents from a club music magazine readership), France (2004, 1,500 respondents
across five urban centres in electronic music settings), and Belgium, Romania, and
Poland. The Mixmag magazine surveys provided longitudinal data from 2000-2005.

## Prevalence Range Across Countries

Lifetime prevalence of hallucinogenic mushroom use among clubbers ranged from 9%
in Hungary to 55% in France. The UK showed 24% lifetime prevalence among 760
clubbers surveyed across six south-east England nightclubs in 2000. Italy
reported 40% lifetime prevalence, the Czech Republic 33%, and Romania 30%.

These figures contrasted sharply with general population data for 15-24 year
olds, where lifetime prevalence typically ranged from 1% to 8% across European
countries. The gap between clubber and general population prevalence was
remarkably consistent: regardless of the country, clubbing populations showed
rates 3-10 times higher than the general population.

## Polydrug Clustering

A critical finding was the strong association between mushroom use and polydrug
patterns. Among the 760 UK clubbers surveyed in 2000, 24% reported lifetime
mushroom use. However, when the analysis was restricted to "current drug users"
(those who had used cannabis or any other drug in the past month and intended to
use drugs in the future), lifetime mushroom prevalence rose to 44%. This
demonstrated that mushroom use was concentrated among experienced drug users
rather than being distributed evenly across the clubbing population.

The polydrug pattern was typical of dance music culture more broadly. Mushroom
users in these settings commonly used cannabis, ecstasy (MDMA), amphetamines,
cocaine, and ketamine alongside mushrooms. The sequential or simultaneous use
of multiple substances created complex pharmacological interactions that
complicated clinical management of adverse reactions.

## The Mixmag Trend Data 2000-2005

The UK Mixmag magazine readership surveys provided the only available trend data
on mushroom use over time. Six consecutive annual surveys from 2000 to 2005
tracked both lifetime and last-month prevalence. The data showed a dramatic
increase between 2003 and 2004, with last-month prevalence jumping from 2.4% in
2002 to 18% in 2004.

However, the EMCDDA cautioned that changes in survey methodology and sample
characteristics from year to year could account for some of the apparent increase.
Non-probability samples of magazine readers are subject to self-selection bias,
and changes in magazine circulation, reader demographics, or response rates
could produce apparent trends that reflected methodological changes rather than
genuine shifts in drug use behavior.

## Average Age of First Use

The 2004 Mixmag survey reported that the average age for first use of
hallucinogenic mushrooms was between 18 and 19 years old. Among the 305
individuals who had used mushrooms, over half planned to take them again.
The most commonly used variety was "Mexican" mushrooms, referring primarily to
Psilocybe [[mdma]]
