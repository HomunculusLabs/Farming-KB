## Growing Kohlrabi, Turnips, and Rutabagas

These three crops are so similar that their cultural differences can be described together. All are medium-demand crops. All appear to be [[permaculture-plants-root-crops-and-tubers]] but kohlrabi is actually a swollen stem forming a ball-shaped vegetable. A steady supply of soil moisture is essential for all of these crops.

## General Requirements

- **Fertility**: Medium-demand — better than minimum but not the highest
- **Moisture**: Steady supply is essential — all will become woody or pithy under [[mycorrhizal-biodiversity-drought-stress-response]]
- **Soil**: Work in decent [[compost]] before sowing, side-dress as seedlings start their first true leaf
- **Daylength sensitivity**: All act like biennials — they go to seed after overwintering. Do not sow them until several weeks (or more) after the solstice or they may go to seed before sizing up

## Kohlrabi

### Why Kohlrabi Is Underrated

If you start with a top-quality (usually hybrid) variety and grow it right, **98% of your plants should make near-perfect balls** the size of grapefruit, sweet and crisp, tasting better than the giant varieties.

### Planting Technique

1. Sow hybrid kohlrabi in clusters of 3-4 seeds, 1/2 inch (1.25 cm) deep
2. Space stations at 5 by 18 inches (13 by 45 cm)
3. Thin clusters progressively so that by 3 true leaves, only one plant stands at each spot
4. First sow about 30 days before weather will begin to significantly cool down
5. By the time bulbs begin forming, the weather should suit them

### Avoiding Woodiness

- With proper genetics, the bulb will slowly enlarge in cool weather without getting woody
- **Beware**: Some commercial varieties are bred for a quick once-over harvest and don't hold long after reaching marketable size before becoming woody
- Slow enlargement in cool weather is key

### Second Sowing (Mild Winters)

In mild-winter climates, make a second sowing to size up after weather turns really chilly. This lot remains in prime eating condition until the spring warm-up begins.

### Storage

Kohlrabi is cellarable if:
- Roots are left attached
- Large leaves are cut off to facilitate [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]
- They are replanted in a bed of damp soil

### Pests

- Cabbage [[cervantes-nematodes-root-maggots-soil-pests]] are a big problem with in-ground brassica crops in some regions
- However, kohlrabi forms several inches above the soil line — the edible part is not invaded by maggots
- Their presence in the growing area may help protect other brassicas by attracting maggots to themselves

## Turnips

### Spring vs. Autumn

Turnips, like winter radishes, are spring and autumn vegetables. But if you grow them for spring harvest, you have to manage their tendency to go to seed before sizing up by encouraging extremely rapid growth.

**Recommendation**: Only sow at the end of summer. Turnips are excellent eaten raw, withstand autumn/winter frosts while sizing up, and in mild-winter climates they make excellent autumn harvests.

### Asian Hybrid Varieties

The Asian hybrid varieties are excellent — mild, tender, and reliable. They have largely replaced the old-fashioned types in [[desert-home-gardens]].

### Storage Warning

If left in their beds too long into winter, turnips become pithy and dry. Harvest before the coldest part of winter.

## Rutabagas (Swedes)

### The Unsung Hero

Rutabagas are probably the most cellarable of the lot. They have an excellent ability to stand in the garden over winter, remaining in good eating condition. They get quite a bit larger than turnips — thin to about 8 inches (20 cm) apart.

### Storage Enhancement

Rutabagas from Canadian markets were thinly coated with paraffin wax. A gardener with [[query-how-to-build-and-manage-a-root-cellar]] could melt paraffin or beeswax and dip each root, in and out, sealing their pores. This significantly extends storage life.

### Winter Quality

Rutabagas make a welcome change from potatoes in winter. They are excellent mashed, roasted, or in stews.

## Seed Saving

### General Principles

All are bee-pollinated outcrossing crops suited to home seed saving — the gardener can maintain a large enough population to avoid loss of vigor.

### Specific Requirements

| Crop | Minimum Plants | Overwintering | Spacing for Seed |
|------|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Radishes | 25-30 (spring annuals) | Not needed | 1 per sq ft |
| Winter radishes | 10-15 | Must overwinter | 2-4 sq ft each |
| Turnips | 25+ | Varies | 4 sq ft |
| Rutabagas | Dozen+ | Must overwinter | 4 sq ft |
| Kohlrabi | Dozen+ | Must overwinter | 4 sq ft |

### Kohlrabi Seed Specifics

Kohlrabi is strongly biennial — perfect specimens should be set in the seed-making area after overwintering. If they overwintered in the earth, replant them in spring. Remove at least half their leaf area (take the largest leaves) when replanting.

### Maintaining Vigor

- Working from a small gene pool (even a dozen plants per generation) has shown no deterioration over several years
- The species depends on crossing to maintain vigor — never save from fewer than about a dozen plants
