# Gottlieb Psilocybin Cultivation Contamination Prevention

Gottlieb describes contamination prevention as "the most difficult part of
psilocybin [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]" in his 1976 guide. He presents a
comprehensive sanitary protocol organized into pure [[agar-culture-technique]],
sterilization procedures, and [[mushroom-contaminant-identification-key]]. The approach is
built around the reality that bacteria and fungal spores are pervasive in
the environment -- floating in air, clinging to skin and clothing, and
issuing from the mouth with every exhalation.

## Pure Culture Technique

The "sanitary code" [[history-of-mushroom-cultivation]]. Gottlieb stresses that
extreme measures must be taken to keep contaminants out of mycelial
cultures, which they would rapidly overrun.

### Personal Preparation

- Scrub arms, hands, and nails with disinfectant soap.
- Wear freshly cleaned short-sleeve T-shirt (simple clothing, no excess
  fabric that could harbor or shed contaminants).
- Gargle with antiseptic mouthwash to reduce oral bacteria.
- Cover mouth and nose with a clean cloth or disposable surgical mask.
- Cover hair with a surgical cap or shower cap.

### Workspace Preparation

- Work in a clean, uncluttered, dust-free room.
- Wash work table and spray entire room with disinfectant immediately
  before work.
- Allow no drafts; close all doors and stuff all door jambs.
- Exclude flies, animals, and unnecessary people from the room.
- Construct a hood around the work table, or use a screen/curtain
  surrounding it.
- Keep all materials within reach; keep all equipment at least three feet
  away from the immediate work area.
- Do not lean over the work (risk of contaminant fallout from clothing or
  breath).
- Avoid swift movements that create air currents and disturb settled dust.
- Do not permit anyone to enter the room while work is in progress.

## Sterilization Procedures

### Glassware

- Boil in water for 30 minutes before use.

### Metalware (loops, blades, needles)

- Hold in a flame until glowing red.
- Allow a moment to cool before contact with any cultures or specimens.
- Re-flame-sterilize the inoculation loop after each transfer of a mycelium
  fragment.

### Medium Containers (Autoclaving)

1. Fill containers no more than half full with medium.
2. Leave lids loose enough to allow pressure escape (prevents cracking).
3. Place in canning-type [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]]; seal lid.
4. Keep stopcock open; heat on high until thick steam vents.
5. Close stopcock; bring to 15-20 psi (250 degrees F) for 30 minutes.
6. Do not exceed this time/temperature (sugar caramelization inhibits
   growth and [[gottlieb-psilocybin-production-extraction-methods]]).
7. Cool to room temperature before releasing stopcock (prevents boil-over).
8. Discard any cracked containers.

### Sterilization Verification

- Hold all autoclaved containers at room temperature for three days.
- Observe for foreign mold growth; discard any contaminated containers.
- Thoroughly clean and resterilize contaminated jars before reuse.
- Broth jars prepared for crop inoculation should be sterilized three days
  before the planned transfer date and observed during this hold period.
