# Water-Soluble Potassium (WS-K) -- Ten Functions in Plant Nutrition

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Water-Soluble Potassium (WS-K) is a [[natural-farming-knf-overview]] input that provides potassium in immediately bioavailable form. Potassium (K) is one of the three primary macronutrients (N-P-K) and plays roles in plant health that go far beyond simple nutrition. In Korean Natural Farming, WS-K is valued for at least ten distinct functions that affect every stage of plant growth and development.

## Preparation Overview

WS-K is typically prepared from potassium-rich plant materials using a brown sugar extraction method similar to [[knf-fermented-fruit-juice]] production. Common source materials include tobacco stems, banana peels, and other potassium-accumulating plants. The specific preparation from tobacco stems is detailed in [[knf-water-soluble-potassium-detailed]].

## The Ten Functions of Potassium in Plants

### 1. Starch Synthesis

Potassium is essential for the synthesis and translocation of starch within the plant. It activates the enzymes responsible for converting simple sugars into complex starches. This function is critical during the grain-filling stage of rice, wheat, barley, and other cereal crops. Without adequate potassium, grains remain shriveled and lightweight because starch cannot be properly synthesized and deposited.

In root crops like potato and sweet potato, potassium drives the conversion of photosynthetic sugars into storage starch in the tuber, directly affecting yield and quality.

### 2. Stomatal Regulation

Potassium plays a central role in the opening and closing of stomata (pores on the leaf surface):

- Potassium ions (K+) accumulate in guard cells, drawing water in by osmosis
- This causes guard cells to swell and open the stomatal pore for gas exchange
- When potassium leaves the guard cells, they deflate and the stomata close

This function is critical for:
- CO2 intake for photosynthesis
- Water regulation and drought resistance
- Temperature regulation through transpirational cooling

### 3. Enzyme Activation

Potassium activates more than 60 enzymes involved in plant metabolism, including:

- Enzymes for photosynthesis
- Enzymes for nitrogen metabolism
- Enzymes for protein synthesis
- Enzymes for carbohydrate metabolism
- Enzymes for ATP (energy) production

Without potassium, these enzymatic processes slow dramatically, reducing overall plant metabolic activity and growth.

### 4. Drought Resistance

Potassium improves plant resistance to drought through several mechanisms:

- **Improved stomatal control:** Potassium-enriched plants close their stomata more efficiently during water stress, reducing water loss through transpiration
- **Deeper root systems:** Potassium promotes root growth, enabling plants to access water from deeper soil layers
- **Cell turgor maintenance:** Potassium helps maintain cell pressure (turgor) even under water stress, keeping leaves from wilting
- **Osmotic adjustment:** Potassium acts as an osmoticum, helping cells retain water during drought conditions

### 5. Freeze and Cold Resistance

Potassium enhances cold hardiness through:

- **Sugar accumulation:** Potassium promotes the accumulation of soluble sugars in cells, which act as natural antifreeze by lowering the freezing point of cell sap
- **Cell membrane stabilization:** Potassium helps maintain the integrity of cell membranes under cold stress
- **Reduced water content:** Potassium-regulated stomatal control reduces tissue water content going into winter, making cells less susceptible to ice crystal damage

This function is particularly important for winter crops (barley, wheat), fruit trees (especially citrus), and overwintering vegetables.

### 6. Lodging Resistance

Lodging (falling over of crop stems) is a major problem in cereal crops. Potassium strengthens plant stems through:

- **Increased cellulose deposition:** Potassium promotes the synthesis of structural carbohydrates in stem tissue
- **Thicker cell walls:** Potassium-enriched plants develop stronger, thicker cell walls in supporting tissues
- **Shorter internodes:** Adequate potassium prevents excessive stem elongation, producing shorter, sturdier plants
- **Better root anchorage:** Potassium's root-promoting effects improve the plant's physical connection to the soil

Rice and barley crops with adequate potassium can withstand wind and rain without lodging.

### 7. Disease Resistance

Potassium strengthens plant defenses against disease through multiple pathways:

- **Stronger cell walls:** Physical barrier against fungal penetration
- **Thicker cuticle:** Reduced entry points for pathogens
- **Compounds production:** Potassium is involved in the synthesis of phenolic compounds and phytoalexins (natural plant defense chemicals)
- **Faster wound healing:** Potassium-accelerated cell division closes wounds more quickly
- **Reduced susceptibility:** Potassium-deficient plants are significantly more susceptible to fungal and bacterial diseases

### 8. Fruit Quality Improvement

Potassium directly affects the quality of harvested fruit:

- **Size and weight:** Potassium drives the transport of sugars into developing fruit, increasing size and weight
- **Color development:** Potassium promotes anthocyanin and carotenoid production, improving fruit color
- **Sugar content:** Potassium increases Brix (sugar content) by facilitating sugar transport and accumulation
- **Acid balance:** Potassium helps maintain the proper sugar-to-acid ratio for optimal flavor
- **Shelf life:** Potassium-enriched fruits have better post-harvest storage characteristics

### 9. Protein Synthesis

Potassium is required for protein synthesis because it:

- Activates the enzymes involved in amino acid synthesis
- Facilitates the loading of amino acids into ribosomes for protein assembly
- Maintains the pH conditions necessary for protein synthesis reactions
- Supports nitrogen metabolism that provides the raw materials for protein construction

Plants deficient in potassium cannot efficiently convert absorbed nitrogen into proteins, leading to accumulation of non-protein nitrogen compounds that can be toxic to the plant.

### 10. Nutrient Transport and Water Use Efficiency

Potassium is the primary cation involved in long-distance nutrient transport within the plant:

- **Xylem transport:** Potassium is the most abundant cation in the xylem sap, driving the movement of water and dissolved nutrients from roots to shoots
- **Phloem loading:** Potassium facilitates the loading of sugars into the phloem for distribution to developing tissues
- **Water use efficiency:** By improving stomatal regulation and root function, potassium enables plants to produce more biomass per unit of water consumed

## Deficiency Symptoms

- Yellowing or scorching of leaf margins (the most characteristic symptom)
- Weak stems and lodging tendency
- Small, shriveled fruit or grain
- Poor root development
- Increased susceptibility to disease and drought
- Slow growth and reduced overall vigor

## Excess Symptoms

While potassium excess is less common than deficiency, it can occur:

- Potassium excess can induce **magnesium deficiency** because K+ and Mg2+ compete for uptake sites on root membranes
- Symptoms of induced magnesium deficiency include interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between leaf veins while veins remain green)
- This is particularly important when WS-K is applied alongside [[knf-water-soluble-calcium]] -- balance between these cations must be maintained

## Application in the KNF System

In the [[knf-nutritive-cycle-theory]], potassium plays important roles during:

- **Late vegetative stage:** Promoting stem strength and preparing the plant for reproductive growth
- **Reproductive stage:** Supporting fruit fill and quality development
- **Pre-harvest:** Enhancing sugar content and shelf life

WS-K can be combined with [[knf-oriental-herbal-nutrient]], [[knf-water-soluble-calcium]], and other inputs in foliar spray programs.
