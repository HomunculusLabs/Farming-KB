## Clinical Presentation

### Acute Psychological Effects
The spectrum of acute psychological effects ranges widely:
- Mild: relaxation (comparable to cannabis), giddiness, uncontrollable laughter, energy, joy,
  euphoria, visual enhancement (seeing colours brighter)
- Moderate: visual disturbances (moving surfaces, waves), delusions, altered perception of
  real events, images and faces, restlessness, incoordination, impaired judgement of time or
  distance, sense of unreality or depersonalisation
- Severe: real hallucinations, panic reactions, psychosis-like states, severe anxiety, severe
  agitation, confusion, disorientation, impaired concentration and judgement, bizarre and
  frightening images, severe paranoia, total loss of reality

Severe cases may lead to accidents, self-injury, or suicide attempts. A bad trip is usually
followed by faintness, sadness, depression, and paranoid interpretations that may persist for
days, weeks, or even months.

### Acute Physiological Effects
General physiological effects are not usually significant but may include:
- Dizziness, nausea, weakness, muscle aching, shivering, abdominal pain
- Dilation of pupils (mydriasis)
- Mild-to-moderate increase in heart rate and breathing (tachycardia, tachypnea)
- Elevation of blood pressure
- Body temperature generally remains normal
- Severe presentations: pronounced stomach pain, persistent vomiting, diarrhoea

The tendency for temporarily increased blood pressure may be a risk factor for users with
cardiovascular conditions, especially untreated hypertension.

## First Aid and Clinical Management

### Primary Approach
First aid for psilocybin intoxication focuses on reassurance and prevention of harm:
- Reassure the intoxicated individual and attempt to calm them
- Prevent users from possibly harming themselves or others
- Assist them to an appropriate medical unit when necessary
- Reduce sensory stimulation (dim lights, quiet environment, minimal personnel)

### Pharmacological Intervention
Benzodiazepines are reported to be the safest medication of choice, effective for most patients
with acute psilocybin intoxication. Key considerations:
- Benzodiazepines address the anxiety, agitation, and panic components of bad trips
- They provide sedation without interacting with serotonergic mechanisms
