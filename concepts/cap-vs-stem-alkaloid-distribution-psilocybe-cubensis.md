# Cap vs Stem Alkaloid Distribution in Psilocybe cubensis

The distribution of psilocybin and psilocin between the cap (pileus) and
stem (stipe) of *Psilocybe cubensis* is not uniform. Research by Bigwood
and Beug (1982) demonstrated that caps contain significantly higher
psilocybin concentrations than stems, while psilocin shows a more complex
tissue-specific distribution pattern that varies by strain.

## Key Finding: Caps Concentrate Psilocybin

Analysis of three separate strains of *P. cubensis* grown on rye-grain
substrate consistently showed that caps contain approximately twice as
much psilocybin as stems:

| Strain | Cap Psilocybin (mg/g) | Stem Psilocybin (mg/g) | Ratio |
|--------|----------------------|------------------------|-------|
| M.R. strain | 6.7 | 4.2 | 1.6:1 |
| Equadorian | 7.6 | 4.7 | 1.6:1 |
| Amazon | 5.7 | 5.7 | 1.0:1 |

The M.R. and Equadorian strains both showed approximately 60% more
psilocybin in caps than stems. The Amazon strain was unusual in showing
equal psilocybin distribution between cap and stem.

## Psilocin Distribution: Strain-Dependent Patterns

Psilocin showed a markedly different and more strain-dependent distribution
pattern:

### M.R. Strain
- Cap: 0 mg/g psilocin
- Stem: 0.35 mg/g psilocin
- Psilocin was entirely localized in stem tissue

### Equadorian Strain
- Cap: 0 mg/g psilocin
- Stem: 0.4 mg/g psilocin
- Same pattern as M.R.—psilocin only in stems

### Amazon Strain
- Cap: 0.1 mg/g psilocin (trace amount)
- Stem: 0 mg/g psilocin
- Inverted pattern—trace psilocin only in cap

This strain-dependent variation suggests that the enzymatic pathways
responsible for psilocin production and accumulation are regulated
differently across genetic lines of *P. cubensis*.

## Biosynthetic Interpretation

Psilocybin (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the
phosphorylated precursor of psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine).
The conversion from psilocybin to psilocin occurs via dephosphorylation
by phosphatase enzymes.

The observed distribution patterns suggest several possible mechanisms:

1. **Differential phosphatase localization**: In M.R. and Equadorian
   strains, phosphatase enzymes may be concentrated in stem tissue,
   converting psilocybin to psilocin preferentially in the stem

2. **Differential transport**: Psilocin produced in cap tissue may be
   actively transported to stem tissue for storage in some strains

3. **Genetic variation**: Different strains may express different
   isoforms or levels of the enzymes involved in the biosynthetic
   pathway

4. **Compartmentalization**: The subcellular localization of biosynthetic
   enzymes may differ between cap and stem tissues

The Amazon strain's unusual pattern (trace psilocin only in cap) may
represent a different phosphatase expression profile or a fundamentally
different regulatory mechanism for the psilocybin-to-psilocin conversion.

## First Flush Observations

All three strains showed nearly complete absence of psilocin in the first
flush, regardless of tissue type. This is consistent with the broader
finding that psilocin production is developmentally regulated and
increases with successive flushes as the mycelial culture matures.

The absence of psilocin in first-flush caps was consistent across all
strains, but the stem distribution varied even at this early stage:
M.R. and Equadorian strains showed small amounts of psilocin in stems
(0.35 and 0.4 mg/g respectively), while the Amazon strain showed none
in either tissue.

## Practical Implications for Consumption

For individuals consuming *P. cubensis*, the cap-to-stem alkaloid
distribution has practical implications:

### For Maximum Potency
- Caps contain significantly more psilocybin per gram than stems in most
  strains
- Separating caps from stems allows concentration of the most potent
  material
- However, psilocin contributes to the overall psychoactive effect, and
  in some strains it is concentrated in stems

### For Consistent Dosing
- The variability in psilocin distribution between strains means that
  the cap-to-stem ratio of total psychoactive alkaloids is not constant
- A "dose" of caps from one strain may have a different effect profile
  than the same weight of caps from another strain
- Whole mushroom consumption provides a more consistent ratio of
  alkaloids than separated tissue

### For Cultivators
- Selecting for cap-heavy yields may increase overall alkaloid
  production per gram of biomass
- Strain selection affects not just total potency but also the
  qualitative character of the experience (psilocybin vs. psilocin
  ratio)

## Research Significance

The Bigwood and Beug findings were among the first to demonstrate
tissue-specific alkaloid distribution in psilocybin mushrooms. Previous
studies had analyzed whole mushrooms, masking the significant
differences between cap and stem tissue. This tissue-level analysis
provided important clues about the biosynthetic pathway and its
regulation.

The strain-dependent variation in psilocin distribution remains
incompletely understood and merits further investigation, particularly
given the pharmacological differences between psilocybin and psilocin.
Psilocin is the active metabolite that binds to serotonin receptors in
the brain, and the ratio of psilocybin to psilocin at the time of
consumption may affect the onset, duration, and character of the
psychedelic experience.
