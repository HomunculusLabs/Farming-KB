## The Hidden Problem of Bare Ground

Many people take poor water cycles for granted. A rancher who received an
award for the best-managed ranch was found, upon closer inspection, to
have 97 percent of the soil surface between plants bare, exposed, and
visibly eroding. Another so-called perfectly managed ranch showed serious
erosion on 95 percent of its area.

What appears as a sea of grass can fool the observer into thinking all
is well. Looking closely at the soil surface between grass plants often
reveals the opposite. Over 50 percent of the soil surface is commonly
exposed on western rangelands, while millions are spent annually on
research and control of so-called problem plants that actually provide
minimal cover.

## Aeration and Plant Health

Plants must have air as well as water. They grow best when soil particles
have air spaces between them. Factors reducing aeration include excessive
compaction, lack of organic material to maintain crumb structure, an
impervious layer beneath the soil, and sealed or capped soil surfaces.
Poor aeration tends to produce plant communities adapted to prevent rapid
water passage, including narrow leaves and waxy cuticles. Good aeration
favors broad-leaved grasses with rapid growth potential.
