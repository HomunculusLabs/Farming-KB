# Cannabis Pruning and Training Techniques (SOG, SCROG, LST)

**Source:** Marijuana Horticulture: The Indoor/Outdoor Medical Grower's Bible — [[gibberellins-plant-growth-hormones]] (auxins). Removing or bending a branch tip causes hormonal balances to shift. Cutting the meristem (top growth tip) diffuses auxins and causes greater concentrations in lower branch tips. Bending changes hormone concentrations less drastically than pruning.

## Pruning

Always use clean instruments. Sanitize clippers and blades between cuts by dipping in rubbing alcohol. Use indoor pruners only in the indoor garden. Make cuts at a 45-degree angle to discourage moisture from sitting on wounds.

**Key rules:**
- Avoid pruning up to a month before inducing flowering — it retards flowering and delays peak maturation by a week or longer
- Leave healthy leaves alone! Removing large fan leaves does NOT make plants more productive. It slows chlorophyll production, stresses the plant, and stunts growth
- Remove only dead leaves or leaves more than 50 percent damaged
- Removing more than 20 percent of foliage at once stresses plants too much and diminishes harvest

### Pinching Back
Pinching or pruning tops causes the two growing shoots below the cut to grow stronger and bigger, increasing the number of main buds. Pinch off tender growth with fingers to help seal the wound. Continually pinching back (as when taking clones) causes many small branches to form, transforming the plant into a hedge-like shape.

### Four-Branch Technique
Remove all but the four main branches. The meristem is removed just above the four lowest branch nodes. This concentrates floral hormones in the four remaining branches, which become stronger and bear larger quantities of dense, heavy flower tops.

### FIM Technique
Coined when a grower accidentally pinched only the bottom 10% of the bud instead of removing the entire growing shoot. The result: many different flowering tops form from a single pruning, and terminal buds put on much more weight and are denser.

### Supercropping
A form of pinching back or pruning branch tips that can incorporate FIM pruning and bending. Some versions involve breaking branches a few inches below main buds (not recommended as it mutilates plants).

## Bending

Bending efficiently neutralizes the growth-inhibiting hormone effect. Much easier on plants than pruning.

- Lean a branch in the desired direction and tie it in place
- Branches can take a lot of bending before they fold or break
- Young, supple branches bend better than old, stiff ones
- Bending branches horizontally encourages buds to grow vertically toward the light
- Use wire ties, plastic-coated electronic wire, or telephone cable wire
- If a branch folds, tie it in place; use a wooden splint if necessary
- It is easy to prune too much, but hard to over-bend

## Sea of Green (SOG)

A method of growing tight-packed short plants to maximize yield per [[darkness-low-light-facilitation-entheogenic-ceremonies]], nutrient toxicities/deficiencies, extreme temperatures, and mutilation. Stress can cause more [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] when the plant has at least four to six nodes. The plant will need a few days to recover from the shock, after which the two new branches will begin growing vigorously.

FIM (Fuck I Missed) pruning is a variation of topping where only part of the growing tip is removed rather than cutting the entire tip. This can produce three to four new branches instead of two, though results are less predictable. FIM is done by cutting approximately 75% of the growing tip, leaving a small portion intact.

Both topping and FIM slow vertical growth temporarily and redirect the plant's energy to lateral branch development. This is especially useful for controlling the height of fast-growing sativa-dominant strains in indoor environments with limited headroom.

## Super Cropping

Super cropping is a high-stress training (HST) technique that involves gently crushing or pinching the stem between the thumb and forefinger to weaken the internal structure without breaking the skin. The stem is then bent at a 90-degree angle and secured in place. Over the next few days, the plant forms a callus (a "knuckle") at the bend point, which becomes structurally stronger than the original stem.

Super cropping has several benefits:
- Reduces overall plant height by bending tall branches horizontally
- Increases light penetration to lower branches
