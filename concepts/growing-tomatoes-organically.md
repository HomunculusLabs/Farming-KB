# Growing Tomatoes Organically

Tomatoes are a popular greenhouse crop but can also be grown outside in most climates. In cold areas, choose early varieties as fruit on late-maturing plants may fail to ripen. They are one of the most important vegetable fruits, rich in Vitamin C, and need a rich, moist soil with plenty of sunshine.

## Varieties

Tomatoes come as bush or upright types. Bush types tend to produce smaller fruit.

**Bush varieties:** Red Alert (very early, small tasty fruits), Incas (disease-resistant, plum tomatoes).

**Upright varieties:** Stupice (tried and tested in cooler climates), Golden Sunrise (yellow, best flavor in warmer climates).

**Greenhouse varieties:** Herald and Shirley (early, good flavor), Gardener's Delight and Sweet Million (cherry-fruited, superb flavor but not disease-resistant).

## Soil and Site

Tomatoes need sun and a well-manured soil that retains moisture and plant nutrients. They prefer a pH of about 6.0. In a three-year crop rotation, grow tomatoes in Plot A (the plot that received manure or compost the previous fall).

## Sowing and Planting (Outdoor)

Sow seeds in trays in mid-spring, spacing about 1in (2.5cm) apart. Raise inside a greenhouse or on a windowsill. Transfer to 3in (9cm) pots at the seed-leaf stage. Harden off in the cold frame in late spring and plant at the beginning of summer. Set upright plants 2ft (60cm) square. For deep beds, plant 20in (50cm) apart.

## Training and Support

For upright (indeterminate) varieties, tie soft string to 4ft (1.2m) poles as soon as plants are planted. For greenhouse tomatoes, attach one end of a long string to the greenhouse structure above each plant. Dig out a hole, flood it with water, remove the plant from its pot, and loop the string around the root ball. As the plant grows, twist the stem around the string for support.

## Side-Shooting (Upright Varieties)

Remove the sideshoots that develop from the angle between the leaf stems and the main stem. These appear at every leaf joint and divert energy from fruit production. Remove them when they are still small — snapping them off with your fingers is easiest. Do not remove leaves above the fruit cluster that is ripening; these leaves feed the developing fruit.

## Greenhouse Pollination and Environment

When greenhouse tomatoes flower, spray them with water once a day to provide the humid conditions that favor good pollination. Hose down daily and shake the supporting wires to move pollen around. When plants reach the top of the greenhouse, pinch off the growing points. Maintain ventilation — consistent air flow prevents leaf mold and other fungal issues.

## Watering Strategy

Water tomatoes consistently to prevent blossom end rot and fruit splitting. Fluctuating moisture levels are the primary cause of these problems. In the greenhouse, water each plant about 2pts (1 liter) per day on average. The smaller cherry tomatoes (Gardener's Delight and Sweet Million) need about 3pts (1.5 liters) per week to retain their sweetness.

## Common Pests and Diseases

- **Whitefly:** Common in greenhouses. Hang yellow sticky traps, encourage Encarsia formosa parasitic wasps
- **Aphids:** Knock off with a jet of water or spray with insecticidal soap. Encourage ladybugs
- **Leaf mold:** Caused by poor ventilation and high humidity. Improve air flow, remove affected leaves
- **Red spider mite:** Favoured by hot, dry conditions. Increase humidity by damping down
- **Potato blight:** Can affect outdoor tomatoes (same pathogen). Remove and destroy affected plants immediately
- **Blossom end rot:** Black patches at the flower end of fruit, caused by [[query-how-do-i-identify-and-control-aphids-on-cannabis]] which spread viruses
