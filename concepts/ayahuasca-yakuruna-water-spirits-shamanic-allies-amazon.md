## Yakuruna: Water Spirits of the Amazon

## Overview

In the visionary cosmology of Pablo Amaringo and the Amazonian vegetalismo tradition, the yakuruna are water beings — spirits that inhabit the depths of the great rivers of the Amazon basin. They are among the most significant spiritual entities encountered in ayahuasca visions, appearing as humanoid figures with distinctly aquatic features. The yakuruna dwell beneath the water"s surface in a world that mirrors and interpenetrates the physical landscape, complete with golden trees and elaborate dwellings where they rest in hammocks woven from serpents.

These are not metaphorical beings. Within the shamanic worldview, the rivers of the Amazon are literally alive with spiritual inhabitants — a hidden ecology that ayahuasca reveals to the initiated. Understanding the yakuruna and their many varieties is considered essential knowledge for any vegetalista who works with water-related ceremonies or who must navigate the spiritual dangers of the aquatic realm.

The yakuruna occupy a central position in Amazonian visionary cosmology because water itself occupies a central position in the physical landscape. The Amazon and its tributaries define every aspect of life in the region — transportation, food, boundaries between communities, and the very rhythm of seasonal existence. It is therefore fitting that the spiritual world beneath the water should be as richly populated and as complexly organized as the human world above it. Amaringo's paintings make this hidden world visible, rendering the invisible inhabitants of the deep with extraordinary detail and specificity.

## Types of Yakuruna

The yakuruna are not a single, uniform class of beings. Amaringo's visions reveal multiple distinct types, each with their own characteristics, powers, and relationships to human shamans. This diversity reflects the complexity of the underwater spirit world, where different ecological niches — much like those in a physical ecosystem — are occupied by different species of being.

### Masha Yakuruna

The masha yakuruna is the most important ally of the murayas — the highest-ranking shamans in the vegetalista hierarchy. This yakuruna serves as a teacher and protector, instructing the muraya in the art of rescuing people who have been lost or abducted into the watery depths. The masha yakuruna is closely associated with the encanto (magical stone) and is often depicted smoking a pipe crafted from such a stone. Its willingness to ally with human practitioners makes it among the most valued spiritual contacts a shaman can establish.

The relationship between a muraya and their masha yakuruna ally is one of mutual obligation. The shaman provides offerings and maintains ritual purity; the yakuruna provides access to underwater realms and the knowledge needed to operate safely within them. This reciprocal bond is one of the foundational structures of Amazonian shamanic practice — a partnership between human and spirit that transcends the ordinary boundaries between species and worlds.

### Taksha Yakuruna (Little Yakuruna)

The taksha yakuruna, or "little yakuruna," is a smaller variety distinguished by its green hair and its habit of carrying a boa constrictor. This being emerges from the water to feed between midnight and dawn, making it visible during the most spiritually potent hours of the night. The nocturnal habits of the taksha yakuruna are significant: the hours between midnight and dawn are traditionally regarded as the time when the veil between worlds is thinnest, when spirits are most active and most accessible to human perception.

Of particular interest to vegetalistas is the fact that if a taksha yakuruna is captured by a skilled practitioner, it is compelled to remain on earth and teach the shaman the secrets of water hunting — a practical application of spiritual knowledge that blurs the line between the material and immaterial worlds. This captured knowledge includes not only the locations of fish and other aquatic resources but also the spiritual techniques needed to navigate underwater environments safely.

### Yana-Sacraruna (Black Evil-Spirit People)

Not all water spirits are benevolent. The yana-sacraruna represents the dark aspect of the yakuruna world. These beings are practitioners of black magic whose sole purpose is to kill. They take pleasure in destruction and represent a genuine spiritual threat to anyone — shaman or layperson — who ventures into their domain. The existence of the yana-sacraruna underscores why knowledge of yakuruna taxonomy is not merely academic but a matter of survival in Amazonian shamanic practice.

The stakes of this knowledge are literally life and death. ### Atun-Huarmi (Fairies)

The atun-huarmi are fairy-like beings said to come from Saturn. During ayahuasca ceremonies, they surround the shaman, contributing their otherworldly energy to the visionary field. Their extraterrestrial origin places them outside the purely Amazonian spiritual ecology, suggesting a cosmology that extends well beyond the local river system to encompass planetary and even cosmic dimensions.

The presence of Saturn-derived beings in Amazonian water cosmology is remarkable. It suggests that the spirit world, as revealed through ayahuasca, is not confined to the Amazon basin or even to Earth. The atun-huarmi imply a universe populated by spiritual beings at every scale — from the river bottom to the planets — all accessible through the visionary gateway of the ayahuasca brew. This cosmic dimension of Amazonian shamanism is frequently overlooked in popular accounts but is central to the tradition as practiced by its most experienced adherents.

### Huairamama (Great Wind Serpent)

The huairamama is the great wind serpent — a colossal spiritual being whose movements through the atmosphere produce dramatic meteorological effects. When the huairamama bathes, thunder is heard across the sky but no rain falls. This phenomenon, known in Quechua as the *supay-cato* or "ghost bath," is a signature of the wind serpent's presence. The huairamama bridges the water and air domains, connecting the yakuruna's riverine world to the broader atmospheric-spiritual ecosystem.

The supay-cato is a genuinely observable natural phenomenon — thunder without rain — that Amazonian cosmology attributes to the huairamama's bathing ritual. This is characteristic of the way Amazonian shamanism integrates natural observation with spiritual interpretation. Physical events in the sky or the water are not merely meteorological; they are the visible traces of invisible spiritual activities. The shaman who understands these traces can read the spiritual weather as readily as a sailor reads the physical weather.

### Sum Iruna

The sum iruna is a man capable of entering water effortlessly — a human being who has achieved such complete integration with the aquatic spirit world that he can pass freely between the physical and underwater realms. The sum iruna represents the ultimate aspiration of the water-oriented vegetalista: not merely to visit or communicate with the water spirits, but to dwell among them as a native inhabitant of both worlds.

The concept of the sum iruna reveals the transformative potential of long-term shamanic practice. Where most practitioners interact with the water spirit world through visions and ritual, the sum iruna has so thoroughly dissolved the boundary between worlds that he can physically enter the water and operate within the spiritual dimension it conceals. This is the highest attainment of the water-oriented path — a state of being in which the shaman has become, in effect, a bridge between the human and spirit worlds.

## Amazonian Water Cosmology

The yakuruna and their associated beings are not isolated curiosities within Amazonian shamanism. They are integral components of a comprehensive cosmological system in which water functions as a primary portal between the physical world and spiritual dimensions.

### Rivers as Living Realms

In the shamanic worldview, the rivers of the Amazon are not bodies of water inhabited by spirits — they are living realms in their own right, with their own geography, politics, ecology, and history. What appears to a casual observer as a river is, in visionary perception, a vast and complex civilization of spiritual beings operating according to their own laws and customs. The surface of the water is not a boundary but a membrane — permeable to those with the knowledge and preparation to cross it.

### Water as Portal

Water serves as one of the primary interfaces between the material and spiritual worlds in Amazonian cosmology. Ayahuasca visions consistently reveal a hidden world beneath the water's surface — a world that exists simultaneously with but independently of physical reality.
