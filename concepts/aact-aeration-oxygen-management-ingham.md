## Bubble Size Matters

Contrary to common belief, fine bubbles are NOT preferred:
- Fine bubbles are hard on fungi and seem to break them up and shatter them
- Suspended fine bubbles from over-aeration create an environment "too oxidizing"
- Coarse bubbles do a better job of extracting organisms and maintaining aeration
- Not too much, not too little — the Goldilocks principle applies to aeration too

## The Oxygen Dip Phenomenon

During brewing, oxygen may suddenly dip (to 4, 3, 2, or even 0.9 ppm) and then recover within 2-3 hours. This happens because:
- Something in the tea grew very rapidly and consumed the oxygen
- Then it ran out of food and stopped growing
- Oxygen returned because organisms were no longer consuming it

This typically occurs between 14-20 hours into the brew. If it happens within a 24-hour cycle, the resulting tea may still be usable if:
- No anaerobic odor developed
- Fungal biomass remained adequate (>2 micrograms/ml)
- Total bacteria remained high (3,000-5,000+ micrograms/ml)

Warning: People who measure oxygen only at the start, 8 hours, and end are missing this critical dip period.

## Aeration Rate and Food Balance

The key relationship:
- More food added = greater oxygen demand from growing organisms
- If food exceeds the aerator's capacity to supply oxygen, the brew goes anaerobic
