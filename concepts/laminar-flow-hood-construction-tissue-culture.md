## Laminar Flow Hood Construction for Tissue Culture

## Overview and Purpose

A [[laminar-flow-hood]] is an essential piece of equipment in any plant tissue culture laboratory. It provides a nearly sterile workspace by passing room air through a HEPA (High Efficiency Particulate Air) filter that removes 99.99% of all airborne contaminants — including dust, spores, and mycelia — and delivers filtered air into an enclosure open on one end. This sterile airflow creates a curtain of clean air across the work surface, preventing ambient contaminants from entering the work area.

The design described here was developed by G.W. Forister and D.W. Burger of the Department of Environmental Horticulture at the University of California, Davis. It was based on a conceptual design published by Meyer in 1986 (*HortScience* 21(4):1064-1065) and describes the construction of two laminar flow hoods connected to a single blower by dryer vent hose. Open petri plate contamination tests confirmed the hoods provided a reliably sterile environment for plant tissue culture work.

## System Architecture

The system consists of two major components:

1. **Fan housing**: A centralized blower unit that draws room air through pre-filters and distributes filtered air to the hoods via flexible hose
2. **Laminar flow hoods**: Two separate workstations, each with its own [[hepa-filter]], work surface, and transparent enclosure

This dual-hood design with a shared blower offers cost efficiency — one powerful motor serves two workstations — while maintaining the flexibility to position hoods in different locations within a laboratory.

## Fan Housing Construction

### Materials and Frame

The fan housing is constructed from a single 3/4" thick 4' × 8' sheet of plywood (A/B grade interior), cut into pieces as specified in the parts layout. All rabbet joints are cut 3/4" wide and 1/2" deep. Construction proceeds as follows:

1. The sides, ends, and bottom are glued together and secured with screws or nails to form a perfectly square open-top box
2. Air passages are cut into the end pieces for airflow management
3. The fan hood is constructed from #2 pine and fits tightly inside the main box

### Fan Installation

The fan hood is a pine structure with two functions: it channels air from the blower into the distribution system and provides [[comparison-silicon-vs-calcium-in-plant-defense-and-structural-support]] for filter lids. The bottom of the fan hood attaches directly to the fan outlet using #10 × 3/4" screws. When the fan and hood assembly is placed in the box with the fan openings facing the filters, the top of the fan hood aligns with the bottom of the side rabbets.

Cleats are attached on each side of the fan at the bottom of the box to center and stabilize the assembly. Importantly, the fan or fan hood need not be permanently attached to the fan housing box — it is held in place by the cleats and its own fit, allowing for removal if needed.

### Air Distribution System

The "Top Center" piece serves as the air distribution manifold. Key design features:

- Contains at least six 4" diameter holes for hose connections
- Hose attachments are made from 4" aluminum irrigation pipe, 3" long, with tabs cut, bent, and drilled for 3/4" #10 screws
- The top piece attaches to the fan hood with 2" screws — no glue is used, allowing future replacement or modification
- The design is modular: additional holes could be added since the motor has capacity beyond what six outlets demand

### Filter Lids

Pre-filters (16" × 20" × 2") protect the main HEPA filters from large particulate loading. Filter lids are designed as loose fits over the filters with handles attached — no screws secure them in place, ensuring easy access for filter replacement.

### Electrical

A rotary switch (ELECTROSWITCH part #21301A, series 21, 1 deck, 4-hole, 1-7 throw) controls the blower. Installation requires modifying the switch by reversing the plate with screw holes so the shaft extends far enough to pass through the 3/4" plywood. A face plate covers the recessed hole, and a knob attaches to the shaft. A 1" hole below the switch accommodates 14-gauge wiring, protected by a grommet made from conduit end parts and a steel conduit coupler cut to 3/4" thickness.

### Filter Retention System

Inside each end of the fan housing, an "inside end" piece attaches to a spacer with 2" screws. Fence wire is held between the spacer and the inside end piece — this wire serves as a coarse pre-filter or support grid. The inside ends fit loosely inside the box (allowing for paint thickness and ease of removal), with spacers positioned to allow a standard filter (16" × 20" × 2") to slide between them.

## Laminar Flow Hood Construction

### Work Surface

The bottom and table area is constructed from two layers of 3/4" plywood (one piece 20" × 31-3/4" and one 31-3/4" × 31-3/4") glued and screwed together, then laminated with Formica for easy cleaning and chemical resistance. The double-layer construction provides rigidity and a durable, smooth surface.

### HEPA Filter Installation

The HEPA filter (24" × 30" × 6") is positioned with its face set back from the table edge by the width of an egg crate white lighting panel. Spacer blocks attached to the base hold the filter in place. The egg crate panel — which serves both as a light diffuser and pre-filter guard — attaches to the HEPA filter frame using machine screws through 1/2" aluminum channel, passing through the plastic grid and foam insulation into the aluminum filter frame.

### Filter Enclosure

The enclosure around the HEPA filter is made from 1" × 12" pine, dadoed (grooved) to receive the filter edges for an airtight seal. This enclosure is screwed into the 3/4" base. The back of the filter enclosure is covered with 1/4" plywood for structural closure.

### Transparent Hood

A clear plexiglass hood (1/4" thick, 4' × 8' sheet) provides visibility and contamination protection. The frame is made from 1" angle aluminum (1/16" thick), formed from one continuous piece. The 90-degree bends at corners are made by cutting triangles from the top side of the angle at each corner, then bending. The frame ends fit inside 1/2" aluminum channel which secures them.

## Blower Specifications

The system uses a Dayton #5C094 direct-drive blower. This is a squirrel-cage [[centrifugal-blower-g2e140]] capable of moving sufficient air volume to supply two hoods simultaneously through the dryer vent hose distribution system.

## Key Design Principles

Several design decisions reflect practical laboratory considerations:

- **Modularity**: No glue on the top distribution piece allows future expansion
- **Accessibility**: Loose-fit filter lids and unglued inside end pieces enable easy maintenance
- **[[pf-tek-control-jar-sterility-verification]]**: The authors validated performance using open petri plate contamination tests — the gold standard for assessing laminar flow hood effectiveness
- **Scalability**: The blower motor has excess capacity, allowing additional hoods to be connected to the same fan housing
- **Cost efficiency**: The shared blower design significantly reduces per-workstation cost compared to individual self-contained units

## Applications in Mycology

While designed for plant tissue culture, laminar flow hoods of this type are widely used in mycology laboratories for [[sterile-inoculation-technique-grain-jars-mycology]] work, [[cotter-grain-spawn-production]], and culture maintenance. The HEPA filtration removes fungal spores from ambient air, creating the sterile environment necessary for working with pure mushroom cultures without contamination.
