## Understanding Arid Land Potential

Despite appearances, Fukuoka found that arid regions often contain hidden water resources. In the Somali savanna, he found stream and river beds everywhere, with at least one live spring within any field of view. Two great rivers at least a thousand kilometers long flowed through the desert. Most critically, the desert sand contained clay -- and where there is clay, there is hope.

"The earth is young; it is sleeping," he told the young people of Somalia. "The land must be awakened from its slumber with natural farming."

## The Sequence of Revegetation

Fukuoka observed a predictable succession in arid land recovery:

1. Grasses of the Gramineae family (eulalia, cogon) are the first to establish, rooting in cracks and fissures in rock
2. Vines such as Boston ivy spread and cover rock faces
3. Crucifers like shepherd's purse and daikon grow well even in harsh conditions
4. Nitrogen-fixing legumes (lespedeza, Scotch broom) thrive in sandy soils
5. When kudzu vines begin climbing shrubs and trees, soil enrichment accelerates
6. Tree species (camphor, chinquapin, oak, cryptomeria, pine) establish
7. Smaller organisms (ferns, mosses, soil microbes) increase
8. Food crops (sesames, minor cereals, gourd family) become viable
