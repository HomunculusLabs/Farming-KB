Source: Sepp Holzer, *Sepp Holzer's Permaculture* (2010)

Beyond pigs, Sepp Holzer integrates wild cattle breeds and poultry into the Krameterhof permaculture system, each fulfilling multiple functions in the landscape.

## Wild Cattle and Old Domestic Breeds

Holzer has successfully bred old domestic and wild cattle breeds for decades. At peak, he kept a mixed herd of around 50 wild cattle in a 25-hectare paddock. Breeds include:
- European bison (Bison bonasus) -- critically endangered, bred in cooperation with zoos
- American bison (Bison bison)
- Yaks (Bos Poephagus mutus) -- thick hair makes them well suited to cold
- Water buffalo (Bubalus Bubalus arnee)
- Scottish Highland cattle
- Hungarian steppe cattle
- Dahomey miniature cattle

### Keeping Bovine Species
- Always kept using paddock rotation system
- Land never overused; soil and vegetation have time to recover
- Paddock system particularly important for cattle because their weight causes soil compaction
- Animals form small herds; different species can develop bonds
- Water buffalo tend to isolate themselves and stay close to one particular pond
- Social relationships between animals require careful attention
- Natural hierarchy should include a dominant bull; rivals should be significantly weaker
- Enclosures large enough to provide space to avoid each other
- Visual barriers (wooded areas, hedges) provide places to rest and hide
- Animals should not be "put on display" -- they need escape routes and minimal human disturbance
- Two-metre-high electrified game fences have given best results (wild cattle overcome normal fences through curiosity and instinct to play)

### Feed and Self-Medication
- Additional feeding limited to winter: turnips, fodder kale, Jerusalem artichokes, hay, grain, apple and pear pomace
- Animals with diarrhoea from parasites self-select poisonous plants: lupins, monkshood, male fern, buttercups, [[palmer-poisonous-mushrooms-deadly-species]]
- Animals with lush, diverse flora including poisonous mushrooms stay healthy and no longer need worming
- Confirmed by decades of veterinary inspection
- Key principle: "Food is also medicine! It must be varied, nutritious and healthy"
- Never mix herbs or poisonous plants into feed yourself -- only the animals know what they need

### Dehorning
Holzer strongly opposes dehorning:
- Unbelievably painful for the animals
- Animals act in a completely different and disturbed way afterwards
- Animals butt each other in the stomach, potentially causing premature or stillbirths in pregnant cows
- Animals may store and dispose of harmful substances in claws and horns
- Dehorning, docking tails, and cropping ears are "nothing more than mutilation"

## Poultry

### Bird Conservation
Holzer emphasises that birds are "incredibly useful and beneficial creatures" for [[mushroom-pests-insect-control-stamets]] and plant propagation. Even small gardens can contribute:
- Well-structured gardens with diverse hedges offer habitat and food
- Chemical use must be abandoned
- Greater plant diversity leads to greater insect diversity, providing balanced bird diet
- Recommended forage plants: elderberry, Guelder rose, wayfaring tree, wild cherry, bird cherry, fly honeysuckle, barberry, bramble, dog rose, wild privet, yew, ivy, spindle tree, dogwood, snowy mespilus, whitebeam, rowan

### Keeping Poultry Humanely
- Birds kept extensively, mostly self-sufficient
- Naturally hatched birds preferred (incubator-hatched birds lack maternal instincts)
- Good breeds: mallards, Indian runner and crested ducks, Styrian chickens
- Hardy and adaptable, help keep snail population down

### Predator Protection
- Thorny hedges provide shelter and nesting sites
- Recommended wild roses: multiflora rose (strong climber, abundant flowers), dog rose (medicinal, vitamin C-rich hips), Japanese rose (large red hips, tasty)
- Mobile nesting sites: two pieces of rough timber in thorny undergrowth
- Mobile sites hardly ever attacked -- moving them repels distrustful predators
- Duck and geese breeding ponds with islands accessible only by rickety wooden planks
- Islands planted with different willow varieties for additional protection

### Winter Feeding
- Birds do not need to be fed in a diverse permaculture system
- Forage plants with varied seed sizes attract multiple species
- Best winter feeding: do not harvest everything -- "untidy" brushwood piles and thick hedges harbour insects
- Only feed when ground is completely covered with snow
- Fat and feed mixture spread onto tree bark for woodpeckers, treecreepers, and nuthatches
