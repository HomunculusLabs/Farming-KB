# Micronutrient Deficiency Symptoms

## Overview

Although plants need micronutrients (trace elements) only in small quantities, Von Liebig's Law of the Minimum applies: even the least-used nutrient, if absent, renders all others useless. Diagnosing deficiencies visually is challenging because symptoms overlap, but mobility patterns provide important clues.

## Mobility as a Diagnostic Tool

The location where deficiency symptoms appear depends on whether a nutrient is mobile or immobile within the plant:

- **Mobile nutrients** (N, P, K, Mg, Mo, Ni): Deficiency shows in **older/lower leaves** as the plant relocates them to new growth
- **Immobile nutrients** (Ca, B, S, Fe, Mn, Cu): Deficiency shows in **newer/upper leaves**, terminal buds, and root tips
- **Zinc**: Intermediate -- symptoms appear in middle-aged leaves

## Iron (Fe)

**Mobility**: Immobile in plants

**Functions**: Iron atoms serve as electron carriers for respiration and oxidation-reduction reactions. It is used to make chlorophyll (though not part of the molecule), helps chlorophyll function, and is critical to nitrogen fixation.

**Deficiency symptoms**:
- Chlorosis (yellowing) due to loss of chlorophyll
- Young leaves become bleached while older leaves and especially veins remain green
- Interveinal chlorosis on new growth
- Older leaves eventually lose color, especially at margins
- Iron is very sensitive to pH -- much more available at pH 6 than pH 7

**Availability**: Common in most soils but becomes unavailable at high pH. Compost, mulches, and manures help by supporting microbes that produce siderophores which [[chelated-nutrients|chelate]] iron and make it available.

## Zinc (Zn)

**Mobility**: Mobile in plants

**Functions**: Component of many enzymes; involved in oxidation-reduction reactions; helps produce auxins (main growth hormone); needed to synthesize chlorophyll, carbohydrates, RNA, and DNA; helps cells withstand cold.

**Deficiency symptoms**:
- Slow then no growth
- Lack of stem elongation (rosetting)
- Yellowing on middle-aged leaves
- "Little leaf" syndrome (smaller-than-normal leaves)

**Availability**: Becomes tied up at high pH and in phosphorus-rich soils.

## Copper (Cu)

**Mobility**: Immobile once assimilated

**Functions**: Key component in enzymes for oxidation, photosynthesis, respiration, and protein construction; plays a key role in lignin synthesis.

**Deficiency symptoms**:
- Chlorosis (needed for photosynthesis)
- Curling of leaves in some plants
- Excessive branching in others
- Wilting and death of leaf tips

**Availability**: Becomes unavailable at high pH and in organic soils with high organic matter.

## Manganese (Mn)

**Mobility**: Immobile in plants

**Functions**: Used to free oxygen during photosynthesis by accepting electrons from water; works with enzymes to break apart carbohydrates.

**Deficiency symptoms**:
- Interveinal chlorosis (yellow leaves with green veins)
- Similar appearance to iron deficiency but usually less severe bleaching
- Symptoms appear on new growth

**Availability**: Affected by pH and soil aeration. Poorly aerated soils can reduce manganese availability.

## Boron (B)

**Mobility**: Immobile in plants (binds readily to sugar molecules)

**Functions**: Component in cell walls connecting carbohydrate chains; necessary for pollen tube formation; balances sugar vs starch ratios; involved in translocation of sugars and potassium ions across cell membranes.

**Deficiency symptoms**:
- Damage at growing tips (death of terminal buds)
- Problems with flower and fruit formation
- Thick, brittle leaves
- Hollow stems or heart rot in some crops

**Unique property**: Boron is the only mineral nutrient that does not have to be an ion to move into plant cells -- it is taken up as uncharged boric acid (H2BO3).

## Molybdenum (Mo)

**Mobility**: Mobile in plants

**Functions**: Necessary for synthesis of organic phosphorus compounds; required by Rhizobia and Frankia bacteria to fix nitrogen in legume root nodules.

**Deficiency symptoms**:
- Chlorosis in older leaves (mobile, so relocated to new growth)
- Leaf curling ("whiptail" symptom)
- Buildup of nitrate in tissues (cannot be properly processed)

**Availability**: More available at alkaline pH, less so in acidic conditions -- the opposite of most micronutrients.

## Quick Reference Table

| Nutrient | Mobile? | Primary Symptom | Where It Shows |
|----------|---------|----------------|----------------|
| Iron (Fe) | No | Interveinal chlorosis, bleaching | Young leaves |
| Zinc (Zn) | Yes | Little leaf, stunted growth | Middle leaves |
| Copper (Cu) | No | Chlorosis, leaf curling | Young leaves/tips |
| Manganese (Mn) | No | Interveinal chlorosis | Young leaves |
| Boron (B) | No | Dead growing tips, fruit issues | Terminal buds |
| Molybdenum (Mo) | Yes | Whiptail, older leaf chlorosis | Older leaves |

## Key Points

- Visual diagnosis is unreliable on its own -- always confirm with soil testing
- pH is the single biggest factor affecting micronutrient availability
- [[chelated-nutrients|Chelation]] can improve availability of iron, zinc, manganese, and copper
- Organic matter and healthy soil biology help make micronutrients available through microbial activity
- Compost and kelp are excellent organic sources of trace minerals
