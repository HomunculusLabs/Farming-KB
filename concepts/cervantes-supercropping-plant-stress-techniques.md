# Supercropping and Plant Stress Techniques

Supercropping is a form of pinching back or pruning branch tips that
combines physical manipulation of plant tissue with strategic stress to
increase yield and control plant architecture. Cervantes describes several
variations developed by innovative growers.

## What is Supercropping?

Supercropping involves pinching, crushing, or bending branches to
intentionally stress the plant. The controlled damage triggers hormonal
responses that redirect growth energy, resulting in:

- Stronger, thicker branch structure
- More even canopy development
- Increased lateral branching
- Potential for larger overall harvest

There are several different "versions" of Supercropping invented by
innovative growers, each with slightly different techniques and
philosophies.

## Basic Supercropping Technique

The fundamental method involves pinching or crushing the stem between
fingers just enough to damage the internal tissue without breaking the
branch completely. This can be done at:

- Main growing tips to encourage lateral branching
- Branches a few inches below main buds to redistribute energy
- Any point where canopy control is needed

The crushed tissue heals with a thickened "knot" that becomes stronger
than the original stem, creating robust support for heavy flowers.

## Supercropping with Bending

Supercropping can be combined with bending for enhanced canopy control.
Some growers bend branches to:

- Expose lower bud sites to more light
- Create an even canopy height
- Increase the number of main colas
- Prevent branches from overshadowing neighbors

## Four-Branch Technique

A specific Supercropping approach removes all but the four main branches.
The meristem (central stem) is removed just above the four lowest
(main) branches. This concentrates floral hormones in the four remaining
branches.

Results of the four-branch technique:
- Fewer branches are stronger and bear larger quantities of dense, heavy
  flower tops
- More even light distribution across the canopy
- Easier management and training

Implementation: Select plants with three sets of branch nodes about six
weeks old. Pinch or prune out the last set of nodes so that two sets of
branches remain. Move plants into the flowering room when about 12 inches
tall. Robust bloomers like Skunk #1 should be set in the flowering room
when about six to eight inches tall.

## The FIM Technique

The FIM (Fuck I Missed) technique was coined by an anonymous High Times
reader from South Africa who accidentally discovered that pinching the
growing tip in a specific manner (rather than removing it entirely like
traditional topping) produces multiple new growing tips instead of just
two. FIM can be incorporated into Supercropping regimens for even more
branching.

## Important Limitations

### Yield Tradeoffs

Pinching back plants diminishes the yield of prime, dense tops, but it
may not affect the overall weight of dried smoke. The total harvest may
remain similar while individual bud size decreases and quantity increases.

### Over-Pruning Dangers

- Pruning all branches or removing more than 20 percent of foliage in a
  short time frame stresses plants too much and diminishes harvest
- Pruning too much over time may alter hormonal concentrations, causing
  spindly growth
- This is often seen in mother plants that provide too many clones
- The mother must rest and gain girth, because small, spindly branches
  root poorly

### Leaf Removal Debate

Some supercroppers practice removing healthy leaves so that "budding
sites get more light." However, leaves are the plant's solar panels and
food factories. Excessive leaf removal reduces the plant's ability to
produce energy for flower development.

## Stress and Recovery

Any kind of stress disrupts hormones and slows rapid growth. When using
Supercropping or any stress technique:

- Apply stress gradually, not all at once
- Allow adequate recovery time between sessions
- Ensure plants are otherwise healthy and well-nourished before applying
  stress
- Monitor plants for signs of excessive stress (wilting, slowed growth,
  discoloration)
