# KNF Yeast Preparation

Yeast is the microorganism responsible for the final step of fermentation — breaking sugars into alcohol and CO2. In KNF, yeast is cultured from wild sources (grape, strawberry skins) and used to resynthesize organic substances decomposed by microbes into amino acids, hormones, and vitamins beneficial to crops. Yeast also has excellent ability to decompose chemical fertilizers.

## What Yeast Does

- Completes fermentation by converting sugars to alcohol and CO2
- Enhances metabolism and expands capillary vessels in plants
- Resynthesizes decomposed organics into amino acids, hormones, vitamins
- Produces 8 essential amino acids, nucleic acids, minerals, fatty acids
- Decomposes residual chemical fertilizers in soil
- Helps plants recover from weakness, disease, and physical damage

## Cultivation Source

Wild yeast lives on fruit surfaces, especially grape skins. Other options include strawberry and other thin-skinned fruits. The yeast is specific to your local environment — an advantage of collecting your own.

## Recipe

### Materials
- Grapes or strawberries (fresh, unwashed — yeast lives on the skin)
- Brown sugar (1:1 ratio with fruit by weight)
- Clay jar or glass container
- Sterilized tools
- Porous paper or loose lid

### Process
1. **Do not wash the fruit** — yeast is on the skin
2. **Sterilize all tools** before use
3. **Crush fruit** lightly and mix with equal weight of brown sugar
4. **Fill jar 2/3** — leave headspace for fermentation gases
5. **Cover loosely** — do NOT seal (CO2 must escape)
6. **Keep separate** from other fermentations to prevent cross-contamination
7. **Shake 1-2 times daily** to protect from fungi during cultivation
8. **Optimum conditions**: 23-25C, 65-70% humidity
9. **Fermentation time**: Approximately 7 days

### Storage
- Refrigerate at 1-15C after fermentation completes
- Maximum storage: 1 month
- Recommended: use within 1 week for best results

## Application

Dilute yeast with water at 1:1000 and spray on leaves with other KNF inputs.

| Use Case | When |
|----------|------|
| Plant weakness | General recovery |
| Viral/bacterial disease | During active infection |
| Wind damage | Branches/stems heavily damaged |
| Low photosynthate | Rainy or high-humidity seasons |
| Livestock | When animals lose appetite or vigor |

## Yeast in the KNF System

Yeast is also collected during brown rice vinegar ([[brown-rice-vinegar-brv]]) production — after 2-3 days of fermentation, yeast can be collected from the fermenting rice wine (Makgeolli). This provides two paths to yeast production in KNF.
