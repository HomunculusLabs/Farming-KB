## Mushroom Growing — Success, Failure, and Economics

## Overview

The commercial cultivation of mushrooms, like any specialized agricultural enterprise, offers both opportunities for profit and risks of failure. A clear-eyed assessment of the factors that determine success or failure, combined with realistic economic planning, is essential for anyone considering entering the mushroom industry. Historical experience provides valuable lessons about the pitfalls that have ensnared unwary growers and the practices that have characterized successful operations.

## The Record of Success and Failure

### Can Success Be Counted Upon?

The honest answer is: **not at all**. There have been innumerable failures in mushroom growing, but at the same time there are conspicuous successes. Examination of failure records across agricultural and floricultural enterprises—poultry work, orange groves, carnation culture, and others requiring constant, intelligent attention—reveals that the main factors in failure are:

1. **Lack of information or incapacity**: The grower simply did not understand what was required or was unable to execute the necessary practices
2. **Deception**: Crediting irresponsible or deceptive advertisements, particularly for spawn and growing systems
3. **Extreme bad luck**: Unforeseen circumstances such as flooding, pest outbreaks, or market collapse

### The Advertisement Problem

A large number of failures in mushroom growing have been the direct result of **crediting irresponsible or deceptive advertisements**. The general rule should be: no inexperienced grower should depend upon advertisements alone to determine where to purchase spawn or obtain growing information. The false advertising problem was so pervasive that it harmed not only growers but also the reliable spawn makers whose legitimate products were lumped together with fraudulent ones.

### Remedies for the New Grower

1. **Seek authoritative information**: Appeal to the director of the experiment station of the state, or to the U.S. Department of Agriculture
2. **Get reliable spawn sources**: Through official channels, get in touch with reliable dealers or manufacturers of spawn
3. **Study thoroughly before investing**: Understand the conditions required for commercial work before committing capital
4. **Start small and experiment**: Gain experience on a small scale before expanding

### The Wrong Reasons for Entering Mushroom Growing

A recurring theme in correspondence was that many people undertook mushroom growing for the wrong reasons. The following "excuses" were frequently offered: "I am broken down in health, wealth, or temperament, and this pursuit appeals to me as an 'Aladdin's Lamp' whereby I may obtain the independence desired." Mushroom growing is an established industry requiring skill, attention, and investment—it is not a get-rich-quick scheme for the desperate.

## Qualifications for Commercial Growing

A person may be considered qualified to undertake commercial mushroom growing who meets these criteria:

1. **Genuine interest**: Undertaking the work because of interest in the pursuit, not merely because it is supposed to offer a fortune in a few years
2. **Demonstrated competence**: After successful experience on a small scale and after serious study of the conditions required for commercial work
3. **Market knowledge**: After careful observation of market demands or possibilities
4. **Financial planning**: After an equally careful computation of all costs involved

### The Role of Pure Culture Spawn

The difficulty of poor-quality spawn—the most common cause of failure—has been largely addressed by the development of pure culture spawn. One of the largest growers in the country emphasized: "If we had to depend upon foreign spawn, as in the past, mushrooms would still be scarce and high." The widespread availability of reliable American spawn of pure culture origin has removed the greatest single barrier to consistent commercial success.

## Economics and Expenses

### Capital Requirements

Relatively little capital may be required where it is unnecessary to build houses for the purpose (e.g., using existing caves or cellars). However, it must be remembered that **practically all capital invested in materials aside from houses is used up in the production of the crop**—it is recoverable only through successful yield.

### Expense Categories

In computing probable expenses, one should include:

- **Fixed costs**: Rent or depreciation on buildings and equipment
- **Growing materials**: Compost (manure), casing soil, spawn
- **Labor**: All phases from compost preparation through harvesting
- **Utilities**: Heat, water, facilities for lighting
- **Operations**: Delivery of product, spray materials, boxes, baskets
- **Incidentals**: Numerous small items that accumulate significantly

### Scale Considerations

If undertaking mushroom growing as an independent enterprise occupying one's whole time, the scale of operations should be great enough to ensure a definite (assumed) income. A realistic example:

- **3,000 square feet planted**: An average crop might yield not more than 3,000 pounds of mushrooms
- **Sold to commission men**: Might yield not more than $1,000 gross
- **Minimum recommended scale**: No person experienced in mushroom growing should undertake this work on a basis of less than **7,500 square feet**, unless merely experimental

The advice is clear: experiment by all means, but only on a small scale and with a definite end in view. Going into this work, one should not count upon more than average returns, and should at least be sure that average returns would insure sufficient profit to make the undertaking worthwhile.

### The Industry's Trajectory

Despite the risks, mushroom growing is an established industry. The figures regarding its development in recent years indicate that when freed from fake advertisers and fake growers, it will succeed throughout the United States just as it has succeeded throughout France. The transformation from an industry dependent on unreliable imported spawn and shrouded in secrecy to one built on scientific pure culture methods and open information represents one of the most remarkable success stories in American agricultural history.
