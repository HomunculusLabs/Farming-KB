## Summary

Eliot Coleman's practice of completing soil preparation and growing-bed
formation the previous autumn, so that spring planting requires only sowing
seeds or setting transplants into already-prepared ground. This strategy
enables quick starts with early outdoor plantings at Four Season Farm in
Harborside, Maine.

## Rationale

At Four Season Farm, only one-quarter acre of the 1.5-acre production area is
under greenhouses. The vast majority of summer and fall crops are grown in
open fields, and getting those crops off to a fast start in spring is
critical. Fall bed preparation is the primary technique Coleman relies on to
make that possible.

There always seems to be more time in autumn to get work done than during the
rush of spring, especially when wet spring weather makes fields inaccessible.
By front-loading the most labor-intensive soil work into the previous fall,
spring is freed for planting, cultivation, and early-season [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]].

## Connection to Greenhouse Spacing Techniques

A key insight from Coleman's system is that intensive spacing techniques
developed for greenhouse production transfer directly to outdoor fields. The
close spacing of outdoor salad crops mirrors greenhouse practice exactly:

- **Carrots:** twelve rows on a single 30-inch-wide bed
- **Turnips or spinach:** six rows on a single 30-inch-wide bed

This tight spacing, refined in the controlled greenhouse environment, produces
the same high-density results outdoors. The principle is identical whether
under glass or open to the sky: more plants per unit area, smaller individual
plants, and continuous harvesting at a young stage.
