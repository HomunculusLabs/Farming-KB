# Mushroom Drying and Storage

Proper drying and storage are essential for preserving the quality, flavor, nutritional value, and (for entheogenic species) potency of harvested mushrooms. Fresh mushrooms are composed of 80-90% water and begin degrading within hours of harvest. Drying removes moisture to levels that inhibit microbial growth and enzymatic degradation, allowing long-term preservation while concentrating active compounds.

## Why Dry Mushrooms

- Preservation: properly dried mushrooms last 1-2+ years
- Concentration: active compounds become more concentrated per gram of dry weight
- Weight reduction: 10 lbs fresh mushrooms yield approximately 1 lb dried
- Convenience: dried mushrooms are easy to store, transport, and portion
- Flavor: drying intensifies umami flavors (especially for culinary species like shiitake and porcini)
- Consistency: dry weight provides accurate dosing compared to variable fresh water content

## Dehydration Methods

### Food Dehydrator (Recommended)

The most reliable and consistent method for home-scale mushroom drying.

Temperature settings by mushroom type:

| Mushroom Type | Temperature Range | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Psilocybin mushrooms | 95-115F (35-46C) | 6-12 hours | Keep below 120F to preserve alkaloid potency |
| Shiitake | 95-110F (35-43C) | 8-12 hours | Preserves lentinan and flavor compounds |
| Porcini / Bolete | 95-115F (35-46C) | 8-14 hours | Thick stems may need longer |
| Oyster mushrooms | 95-110F (35-43C) | 4-8 hours | Thin flesh dries quickly |
| Chanterelles | 95-115F (35-46C) | 6-10 hours | Retains fruity aroma |
| Lion's mane | 95-110F (35-43C) | 6-12 hours | Maintain shape for best quality |
| Reishi | 120-140F (49-60C) | 12-24 hours | Very dense flesh, needs higher temp and longer time |

Dehydrator tips:
- Set to lowest temperature setting first (95F), increase if drying is too slow
- Arrange mushrooms on trays in a single layer without overlapping
- Slice thick mushrooms (1/4 inch or less) for more even drying
- Rotate trays every 2-3 hours if the dehydrator has uneven heat distribution
- Fan-forced models dry significantly faster than passive stackable models
- Mesh tray inserts prevent small pieces from falling through
- Target moisture content: below 10% (mushrooms should snap crisply when bent)

### Fan Drying

A budget-friendly method suitable as a precursor to further drying or for thin-fleshed mushrooms in dry climates.

Setup:
1. Place mushrooms on a mesh screen, wire rack, or paper towel (not cardboard -- absorbs moisture and molds)
2. Position a circulating fan to blow air across the mushrooms
3. Maintain room temperature (70-80F) and low humidity (below 50%)
4. Space mushrooms with at least 1 inch between them
5. Flip every 6-8 hours for even drying
6. Duration: 24-72 hours depending on mushroom thickness and ambient humidity

Limitations:
- Slow, especially in humid climates
- May not achieve sufficiently low moisture content alone
- Best used as a first step before finishing in a desiccant chamber or dehydrator
- Risk of microbial contamination during extended drying time

### Desiccant Chambers

Desiccant chambers use chemical drying agents to pull moisture from mushrooms in an enclosed space. This method produces the driest final product and is ideal for long-term storage preparation.

Common desiccants:
- Silica gel beads (rechargeable, color-indicating: blue=pink when saturated)
- DampRid (calcium chloride) -- effective but can become liquid
- Indicating silica gel packets (small scale)

DIY desiccant chamber:
1. Use an airtight container (glass jar, plastic tub with gasket lid)
2. Place a layer of desiccant at the bottom
3. Separate desiccant from mushrooms with a barrier (paper towel, mesh screen)
4. Place mushrooms on the screen above the desiccant
5. Seal container
6. Replace or recharge desiccant when it changes color (saturated)
7. Mushrooms are fully dry when they snap crisply and the desiccant stops changing color

Drying time: 24-72 hours depending on initial moisture content, mushroom size, and desiccant quantity.

For maximum dryness, use desiccant chamber as the final step after fan or dehydrator drying.

### Other Methods

**Oven drying** (not recommended for most mushrooms):
- Temperature control is imprecise in most ovens
- Minimum oven temperature (170F) is often too high for potency preservation
- Risk of cooking rather than drying
- Only suitable for culinary mushrooms where potency is not a concern

**Sun/air drying** (traditional, limited use):
- Works in hot, dry climates (below 30% humidity)
- Risk of UV degradation for light-sensitive compounds
- Slow and weather-dependent
- Still used traditionally for many culinary mushrooms (shiitake, porcini)

## Assessing Dryness

Properly dried mushrooms should:
- Snap crisply and cleanly when bent (not bend or flex)
- Sound hollow when tapped together
- Show no visible moisture or soft spots
- Weigh 8-12% of their fresh weight

If mushrooms bend, feel leathery, or have soft spots, they are not dry enough and may mold in storage.

## Storage Methods

### Glass Jars (Recommended)

The gold standard for long-term mushroom storage.

Procedure:
1. Ensure mushrooms are completely dry (snap test)
2. Place in clean, dry glass mason jars
3. Add a food-grade silica gel packet to each jar (absorbs any residual moisture)
4. Fill jars as full as possible to minimize air (oxygen) in the container
5. Seal with airtight lids (two-piece mason jar lids work well)
6. Store in a cool, dark location
7. Label with species, date, and weight

Shelf life: 1-2+ years when stored properly.

### Vacuum Sealing

Excellent for maximizing storage life by removing oxygen.

Procedure:
1. Dry mushrooms completely
2. Place in vacuum-seal bags
3. Include a silica gel packet inside the bag
4. Vacuum seal with a food saver or impulse sealer
5. Store in a cool, dark location

Shelf life: 2+ years.

### Silica Gel in Containers

For smaller quantities or storage in less-than-ideal conditions:

- Use airtight containers (glass or food-grade plastic)
- Include enough silica gel to absorb ambient moisture
- Use indicating silica gel so you know when to replace it
- Store in a cool, dark place

### Freezer Storage

For maximum long-term preservation:

- Dry mushrooms completely first
- Vacuum seal or use glass jars with silica gel
- Store at 0F (-18C) or below
- Freezing stops all degradation processes
- Shelf life: 5+ years

### Storage Conditions Summary

| Storage Method | Temperature | Light | Humidity | Shelf Life |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glass jar, room temp | 65-75F | Dark | Low (silica gel) | 1-2 years |
| Glass jar, refrigerated | 35-40F | Dark | Low | 2-3 years |
| Vacuum sealed, frozen | 0F or below | Any | Any | 5+ years |
| Paper bag (not recommended) | Room temp | Any | Variable | Weeks to months |

## Potency Preservation

For mushrooms containing psilocybin and psilocin, proper drying and storage is critical. Key principles: keep drying temps below 120F (95-115F ideal), achieve thorough dryness (<10% moisture), minimize oxygen exposure (airtight containers, vacuum sealing), store in darkness, and use food-grade undyed silica gel. Cool or frozen storage maximizes longevity.

Approximate alkaloid degradation at room temperature: 6 months <5%, 12 months 5-15%, 24 months 15-30%. Freezing effectively halts degradation.

## Reconstitution

Dried mushrooms can be reconstituted for culinary use:
- Soak in warm (not boiling) water for 15-30 minutes
- The soaking liquid becomes a flavorful broth for soups and sauces
- Reconstituted mushrooms have a chewier texture than fresh but retain flavor

For species where potency is a concern, reconstitution in a warm tea or decoction is the most common preparation method.
