# Shimeji Cultivation Guide (Hypsizygus tessulatus)

Shimeji refers to a group of cultivated mushrooms in the genus Hypsizygus, primarily H. tessulatus (also known as H. marmoreus), which includes both brown (Buna-shimeji) and white (Hakata-shimeji or Bunapi) varieties. Shimeji is a premium mushroom in Japanese cuisine and one of the most commercially important cultivated mushrooms in East Asia.

## Overview

Shimeji mushrooms are highly valued for their firm texture, nutty flavor, and excellent shelf life. They are among the top five cultivated mushrooms in Japan by production volume. The brown variety (Buna-shimeji) was traditionally wild-harvested before commercial cultivation techniques were developed in the 1970s. The white variety is a commercial cultivar selected for its appearance.

## Species and Varieties

### Hypsizygus tessulatus (syn. H. marmoreus)

The primary cultivated species, with two main commercial forms:

- Buna-shimeji (Brown Beech Mushroom): brown cap with white stem, wild-type coloration
- Bunapi-shimeji (White Beech Mushroom): pure white cap and stem, selected commercial cultivar

### Confusion with True Shimeji (Hon-shimeji)

True shimeji (Lyophyllum shimeji) is a different, mycorrhizal species that cannot be cultivated commercially. What is sold as "shimeji" in markets worldwide is always H. tessulatus. The name "shimeji" is a market term rather than a strict taxonomic designation.

## Species Profile

- Scientific name: [[hypsizygus-tessulatus]]
- Synonyms: H. marmoreus, [[pleurotus]] tessulatus
- Common names: Brown Beech Mushroom, White Beech Mushroom, Buna-shimeji, Bunapi
- Family: Tricholomataceae
- Cap: 1-3 cm, brown (Buna) or white (Bunapi), smooth to slightly wrinkled
- Stem: 3-6 cm, slender, firm, white
- Growth habit: grows in tight clusters (bouquets) from a central base
- [[spore-print]]: white
- Flavor: nutty, slightly sweet, with umami depth; intensifies with cooking
- Texture: firm, crisp, maintains integrity when cooked

## Cultivation Method: Bottle Culture

Shimeji is commercially produced almost exclusively in bottles, following the same general method used for [[enoki-cultivation]] and [[nameko-cultivation]].

### Substrate Formula

- Hardwood sawdust (primarily beech or oak): 75-80%
- Rice bran or [[wheat-bran]]: 18-22%
- Gypsum: 1-2%
- Moisture content: 63-68%

Beech sawdust is the traditional standard. Some commercial operations use a mix of hardwood sawdust and corncob (60:40 ratio) for improved aeration.

### Sterilization

- Full sterilization at 15 PSI for 2-3 hours
- Shimeji is moderately contamination-sensitive
- Ensure complete heat penetration

### Bottle Preparation

- Standard bottle size: 850-1100 ml polypropylene
- Fill with substrate to approximately 80% capacity
- Pack firmly but not compressed (moderate density)
- Sterilize, cool, and inoculate

### Inoculation and Spawn Run

- [[grain-spawn]] rate: 3-5% by weight (lower than many species)
- Incubation temperature: 68-75 F (20-24 C)
- Incubation time: 25-35 days
- Conditions: dark, still air

Mycelium is white and vigorous. Full colonization is indicated by a firm, white block with the surface slightly uneven.

## Fruiting Induction

Shimeji requires a cold shock to initiate fruiting, similar to nameko:

1. Scrape or "brush" the surface of the colonized block to remove aerial mycelium and create an even pinning surface
2. Apply cold shock: 37-45 F (3-7 C) for 3-5 days
3. Some growers add a small amount of water (5-10 ml) to the block surface before cold shock
4. Transfer to [[fruiting-conditions]] after cold shock

### Fruiting Conditions

- Temperature: 55-62 F (13-17 C)
- Humidity: 90-95%
- Light: 200-500 lux, 10-12 hours per day
- Fresh air exchange: moderate; shimeji tolerates moderate CO2
- CO2: 800-1500 ppm acceptable
- Air velocity: low

### Growth Pattern

- Pin formation: 5-10 days after cold shock
- Multiple pins form across the scraped surface
- Selective harvesting: commercial growers thin to 3-5 main clusters per bottle
- Remaining clusters grow larger and more uniform with reduced competition
- Pin to harvest: 15-20 days

### The Brown vs White Difference

- Brown shimeji: slightly more robust, more intense flavor, slightly higher yield
- White shimeji: milder flavor, more visually appealing for garnish, slightly longer shelf life
- White variety requires stricter temperature control for color maintenance
- Both have identical cultivation requirements

## Yield and Economics

- [[biological-efficiency]]: 60-100% (fresh weight per dry substrate weight)
- Yield per bottle: 150-200g fresh
- First flush: 70-80% of total yield
- 2-3 flushes possible but first flush is primary commercially
- Total crop cycle: 50-60 days from inoculation to harvest

Shimeji commands a premium price in Asian markets, making it economically attractive despite moderate yields:

- Price per kg: higher than enoki and nameko; comparable to shiitake
- Long shelf life (2-3 weeks refrigerated) improves marketability
- Premium appearance and consistent quality drive demand

## Harvesting

Harvest by cutting the entire cluster at the base with scissors or a knife:

- Do not pull individual stems from clusters
- Handle clusters gently to maintain presentation
- Trim the base (root) to a clean, even cut
- Pack clusters in ventilated containers

Grading standards:

- Premium: tight clusters, even cap size, uniform color, no broken stems
- Standard: slightly irregular clusters, minor color variation
- Processing: broken clusters, individual stems

## Post-Harvest Storage

Shimeji has one of the best shelf lives among cultivated mushrooms:

- Refrigerated (35-38 F): 14-21 days
- Store in breathable packaging
- The firm texture resists deterioration
- Brown variety: color darkens slightly but quality maintained
- White variety: may yellow if stored too warm
- Freezing: not recommended (texture degrades significantly)
