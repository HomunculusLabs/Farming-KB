# Shamanic Powers and Mariris

The acquisition and exercise of shamanic power in Amazonian vegetalismo is a complex system involving initiation rites, magical phlegm (mariris), guardian spirits, and sacred objects. As described by [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]] in his [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]], the path to becoming a vegetalista involves direct contact with plant spirits, animal allies, and cosmic beings who confer specialized abilities.

## The Mariris: Magical Phlegm

The mariri is one of the most important concepts in [[mckenna-ayahuasca-and-amazonian-shamanism]]. It is a magical phlegm that resides in the shaman's throat and chest, serving as both a repository of power and a defensive weapon. The mariri can be projected outward as a colored cord or filament to protect, attack, or heal. [[queen-inamullo]], also known as Huacra-chukcha (hair of thorns), is the master teacher of the mariris and related arts.

### Types of Mariris

There are several distinct types of mariri, each associated with a color and a specific function in the vegetalista's ciencia palística:

- **Yana-mariri** (black mann): Received by the vegetalista wearing a red shirt. This dark mariri is associated with protective and potentially aggressive functions.
- **Yura-mariri** (white mann): Taken in by the white-shirted vegetalista. Associated with healing and purification.
- **Puka-mariri** (red mann): Received by the blue-shirted shaman. Used in offensive and defensive magical operations.
- **Orange mann**: Received by the yellow-shirted vegetalista, representing a distinct role in the shamanic repertoire.

Each mariri has a specific vibration that harmonizes with different energies. Only those mariris capable of harmonizing with the vibrations of the rainbow can achieve their full potential.

## Receiving Shamanic Powers

The process of receiving shamanic powers is depicted as a coronation ceremony. An apprentice is seated upon a golden throne while queens of a great supernatural dynasty dress him in royal clothes, golden bracelets set with precious stones, and finally the emperor's crown. This coronation is overseen by Queen Sittja, who fits the crown, and Queen Baroty, who places flowers upon the coronation altar.

Queen Car Camira, a powerful lady whose task is the coronation of great wizards, psychometrics, masters of telekinesis, sorcerers, rabdomantes, murayas, bancos, and sumirunas, carries forth a diamond scepter and roses during the ceremony.

From above, the muses of song and music descend with harps, lyres, and lutes, intoning the cadence of the icaro huiksa-nanay, a specific sacred melody that accompanies the initiation.

## Guardian Spirits and Animal Allies

### The Dryads

In the background of the initiation vision, skillful dryads appear armed with hypnotic nets, spears, shields, slings, bows, and arrows. They possess supernatural powers and teach the use of plants to lengthen life. With the passage of time, they can even impart the knowledge of everlasting life.

### The Lupuna Tree Spirit

A lupuna tree (Ceiba sp.) houses its spirit — a wise old nymph that ascends a stairway leading to the tree canopy, which serves as an abode or sanctuary for forest nymphs. The lupuna is one of the most sacred trees in Amazonian shamanism, often serving as a axis mundi connecting the earthly and spiritual realms.

## The Ayahuasca Ceremony

The ayahuasca session is conducted in the house of a vegetalista, typically beginning at nine or ten at night. Participants include men, women, and young people seeking healing for conditions not treatable by [[fukuoka-eastern-western-medicine-healing-world-crisis]] — mal aire (illness from evil breezes), sorcery-induced illnesses, and spiritual afflictions.

### Preparation and Ritual

Before drinking, each person must pray to the ayahuasca: "Grandfather, please, let me see all those things I want to see." The wish may be to see a distant relative, find something lost, or discover the nature of an illness. The vegetalista indicates where on the gourd he placed his mouth when blowing and singing, because the drinker must drink from that exact spot.

### Reading the Signs

The session unfolds in complete darkness. The vegetalista blows smoke on every person's head, sometimes using perfume or camphorated water. Several signs indicate the nature of forthcoming visions:

- **Single yawn**: Beautiful visions are coming.
- **Multiple yawns**: Very strong visions, or enemy attacks are imminent.
- **Left arm contractions**: A sorcerer may be attempting harm.
- **Right arm contractions**: The person will see everything they asked of the ayahuasca spirit.

The vegetalista begins with a prayer appropriate to his faith — Catholic, Protestant, or indigenous — and then sings the first icaro to bring forth the visions.

## The Machashca Cantaro

The machashca cantaro (pitcher of vision) is a supernatural vessel containing icaros that vegetalistas sing to guard themselves against enemy visions and to strengthen their own visionary power. It represents a storehouse of concentrated shamanic knowledge.

## The Queen Inamullo's Defenses

Queen Inamullo carries with her a jar of delicate balsams for the protection of those mariris able to harmonize with rainbow vibrations. Her feathered head-dress acts as a passive defense, absorbing any curse or evil spell cast by an opponent. She is served by tutelary spirits including:

- **Sacha-supay** (the jungle demon)
- **Supayisnachi** (the maquisapa ghost)
- **Yura-huacra** (the white-horned deer)
- **Wiracocha-ailco** (the dog of the water froth)
- **Canero** (Vandelia plazai, a fish)
- **Puka-cakila** (the red-mouthed fish)
- **Yahuarachi** (Gastromyzus latior, a slime fish allied to the boa)
- **Boa-sacha** (an unidentified plant)

## Serpent Powers

Three great serpents demonstrate the raw power available to shamans:

- **Yakumama**: Blows forth its power in the form of fiery-headed people, representing the creative-destructive force of water.
- **Purahua**: Spits forth a magnetic charge like a chain of energetically armored men locking arms, demonstrating the binding power of earth magic.
- **Acoruna** (boa mama): Releases a wave of molecules taking the form of maggots, worms, snakes, leeches, fowl, and lizards — the transformative power of the serpent mother.

## Magical Plants in Shamanic Practice

### Caballo Piripiri

Whoever ingests the caballo piripiri (Cyperus sp.) acquires great strength. It is prepared in a mixture with jagua or huito ([[genipa-americana]]) in careful, exact proportions. The mixture is poured over the entire body, after which the person avoids sun, salt, sweets, garlic, liquor, pig fat, sex, and socializing with sexually active people for eight days. The skin turns black as ink, fading by the eighth day.

### Sacha-huarmi Plants

In visions, certain plants appear as seated women in conversation. These sacha-huarmi (women of the forest) are found where there are large rocks in the Amazon. They are very difficult to find and recognize, representing the elusive nature of certain plant-spirit relationships.

### Pichanamachaco

This magical plant possesses an icaro used to cure the poisonous bites of snakes and other animals. It is associated with a woman serpent-tamer who sits by a lake in the visionary landscape.

## The Enchanted City of Ankord

Beyond the mundane world lies Ankord, a mysterious city in an unknown part of the earth, surrounded by a great garden. Over the city circles a strange spaceship, suggesting connections between ancient earthly mysteries and extraterrestrial intelligence. This city appears in the visionary landscape behind a lake, accessible only through deep ayahuasca trance.
