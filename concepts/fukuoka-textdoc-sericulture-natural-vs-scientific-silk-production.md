# Sericulture: Natural vs Scientific Silk Production

## Overview

[[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]] control
- Rearing under the eaves of houses worked well, and eventually silkworms were raised outdoors and even released into nearby trees

The realization was striking: originally a creature of nature, the silkworm had thrived outdoors day and night. Only humans had feared the evening dew. Sericulture appeared to be headed decisively in the direction of [[fukuoka-critique-scientific-agriculture-civilization]]:

- **Artificial feed**: Prepared from mulberry leaf powder, soybean powder, wheat powder, starch, fats, vitamins, preservatives, and sterilized components. Eventually to be replaced by totally synthetic petrochemical-based feed.
- **Automated environment**: Fully outfitted facilities with heating, air conditioning, automatic lighting and ventilation adjustment.
- **Conveyor systems**: Feed carried in and droppings carried out on belt conveyors.
- **[[fukuoka-illusions-scientific-farming-centrifugal-centripetal-model]] — proceed in opposite directions:

- **Natural farming** proceeds centripetally inward toward "nothingness" (Mu), compressing and freezing time and space by the elimination of human labor.
- **Scientific farming** proceeds centrifugally outward toward "somethingness," expanding and developing through increasingly complex and diverse means.

Although the two may appear identical at a given point in time, they move in opposite directions — one toward zero and the other toward infinity. Once scientific farming gains momentum, it does not regress. The intertwining of the two approaches may look like a piston moving in and out, but the paths are forever parallel and never truly cross. Science periodically drinks from nature's knowledge, then forgets its debt and pushes further away.

## Broader Implications

The sericulture case study illustrates several principles applicable to all agricultural domains:

- **Scientific farming never truly regresses**: Even when conditions temporarily favor simpler methods, the underlying trajectory is always toward greater artificiality and energy input.
- **Market forces override ecological wisdom**: The return to natural sericulture was driven by labor shortages, not ecological consciousness. Its abandonment was driven by consumer demand and pricing, not agricultural reasoning.
- **Weakness is built into domestication**: Artificial breeding for specific traits (higher silk yield) systematically reduces the organism's natural resilience, creating dependency on the very human interventions that caused the weakness.
- **Factory food is the logical endpoint**: The progression from forest cocoon collection to automated factory silk production reveals the trajectory of all scientific agriculture toward complete industrialization.
