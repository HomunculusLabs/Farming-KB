# Goose Husbandry

Geese are excellent homestead birds -- they are largely self-feeding on pasture, produce large eggs, provide meat, and serve as effective weeders and alarm animals. They require less feed and housing than chickens or ducks. Drawn from [[nicole-faires]]' comprehensive manual.

## Breed Selection

| Breed | Weight (mature) | Eggs/Year | Characteristics |
|-------|----------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Embden | 20-26 lbs | 30-40 | White, large, most common meat breed |
| Toulouse | 20-26 lbs | 25-35 | Gray, heavy, good for foie gras |
| African | 18-22 lbs | 25-40 | Dark with knob on head, loud, good watchdogs |
| Pilgrim | 12-16 lbs | 40-60 | Sexable by color at hatch, medium, docile |
| Roman Tufted | 10-12 lbs | 25-35 | Small, decorative, good weeders |
| Sebastopol | 10-14 lbs | 25-40 | Curly feathers, ornamental, good layers |
| Buff | 16-18 lbs | 30-50 | Calm, good mothers, dual purpose |
| Chinese | 10-12 lbs | 40-80 | Prolific layers, excellent weeders, loud |
| Pomeranian | 12-16 lbs | 30-50 | Tuffed, hardy, good dual purpose |

### Choosing

- Meat production: Embden, Toulouse
- Egg production: Chinese, Pilgrim
- Weed/grass control: Chinese, Roman Tufted
- Small homestead: Pilgrim, Roman Tufted
- Watchdog/alert: African, Chinese
- Cold climate hardiness: Embden, Toulouse, Pilgrim

## Housing

### Shelter Requirements

- Minimum 4 sq ft per bird indoor space
- 10-15 sq ft per bird outdoor run (though they prefer free-ranging on pasture)
- Three-sided shelter is sufficient for adult geese
- Must be dry and draft-free
- Bedding: straw or wood shavings, 4-6 inches deep
- No perches needed (geese sleep on the ground)
- Nest boxes: 2x2 feet, 1 per 2-3 geese, lined with clean straw
- Water access: geese need water deep enough to submerge their heads for cleaning nares

### Fencing

- Geese are large and generally not preyed upon by small predators
- 3-4 foot fencing is sufficient to contain them (they don't fly well)
- Electric poultry netting works well for rotational grazing
- Protect from coyotes, stray dogs, and foxes -- especially goslings
- Night shelter recommended even for free-range birds

### Water Features

- Geese prefer access to a pond or pool for swimming and mating
- A child's wading pool is sufficient for small flocks
- Change water regularly to keep it clean
- Geese will foul any water source quickly

## Feeding

Geese are primarily grazers and can meet most of their nutritional needs from good pasture.

### Pasture

- Geese eat grasses, clovers, dandelions, and many broadleaf weeds
- A pair of geese can maintain 1/4 to 1/2 acre of grass
- Rotational grazing prevents overgrazing
- They prefer young, tender grass shoots

### Supplemental Feed

| Season | Feed | Amount |
|--------|------|--------|
| Spring/Summer (good pasture) | Layer pellets or scratch grain | 0.25 lbs per bird per day |
| Fall/Winter (limited pasture) | Mixed grain + pellets | 0.5 lbs per bird per day |
| Breeding season | Breeder ration (higher protein) | 0.5 lbs per bird per day |
| Goslings (0-3 weeks) | Starter crumble (20% protein) | Free choice |
| Goslings (3-8 weeks) | Grower ration (15-16% protein) | Free choice |

### Additional Feeding Notes

- Provide grit (coarse sand or small stones) for digestion
- Provide oyster shell free-choice for egg shell quality
- Geese enjoy kitchen scraps: vegetables, fruit, bread, greens
- Avoid feeding moldy grain or spoiled food
- Fresh water must be available at all times
- Green feeds: geese love lettuce, chard, grass clippings (not treated with chemicals)

## Breeding

### Breeding Behavior

- Geese form long-term pair bonds (often for life)
- Breeding season: late winter through spring (February to May, varies by climate)
- Ganders become territorial and aggressive during breeding season
- Provide 1 gander per 2-4 geese (1:1 ideal for fertility)
- Geese build their own nests if given straw

### Incubation

- Goose eggs: large, white, approximately 5-6 ounces
- Incubation period: 28-35 days (30 days average)
- Most geese are excellent setters and mothers
- If artificial incubation: 99.5F, 55-60% humidity, turn 3-5 times daily
- Stop turning at day 25, increase humidity to 65-70%
- Goslings can take 24-48 hours to hatch fully -- do not assist unless absolutely necessary

### Gosling Care

- Brood at 90-95F for first week, reduce 5F per week
- Provide non-slippery bedding (paper towels over shavings for first few days)
- Feed starter crumble (20% protein) free choice
- Provide water in a shallow dish (deep enough to dip beak, not swim)
- Goslings can go outside on grass at 2-3 weeks old (weather permitting)
- Do not let goslings swim until they have proper feathers (6-8 weeks) -- they lack waterproofing oil and can drown or chill

## Growth and Production

| Age | Weight | Notes |
|-----|--------|-------|
| Hatch | 4-6 oz | Start on starter crumble |
| 2 weeks | 1-1.5 lbs | Growing fast |
| 4 weeks | 3-5 lbs | Transition to grower feed |
| 8 weeks | 8-12 lbs | Feathering out |
| 12 weeks | 12-16 lbs | Ready for processing (young tender) |
| 16-20 weeks | 15-22 lbs | Processing weight (most breeds) |
| Maturity | 16-26 lbs | Depends on breed, start laying |

### Egg Production

- First eggs: 9-12 months of age
- Season: primarily spring (February to June)
- Quantity: 30-80 eggs per year depending on breed
- Egg weight: 5-7 ounces (very large)
- Geese lay in nests early in the morning
- Collect eggs daily; geese are not reliable setters unless broody

### Meat Production

- Processing age: 12-16 weeks for tender meat, 20+ weeks for roasting birds
- Dressing percentage: approximately 70-75% of live weight
- Goose fat is highly valued for cooking (renders easily from skin and fat deposits)
- See [[animal-harvest-and-processing]] for detailed processing

## Health

### Common Issues

| Problem | Symptoms | Prevention |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Worms | Weight loss, poor growth | Regular deworming, clean pasture |
| Bumblefoot | Swollen foot pads | Dry bedding, clean water areas |
| Respiratory | Sneezing, nasal discharge | Good ventilation, dry housing |
| Predator losses | Missing birds | Night housing, fencing |
| Angel wing | Wing tips twist outward | Avoid overfeeding, limit protein in goslings |

### Preventive Care

- Deworm twice yearly (spring and fall)
- Keep water sources clean to prevent disease spread
- Provide dry bedding -- wet conditions cause foot problems
- Monitor for predators (coyotes, dogs, foxes, owls)
- Goslings are vulnerable to cold and damp -- keep warm and dry
- Adult geese are very hardy and disease-resistant compared to chickens


## Special Uses

Geese serve multiple homestead roles: **Weeder geese** (Chinese, Roman Tufted) control grasses and weeds in crops; **guard geese** (African, Chinese) alert to intruders; **down and feathers** collected during molt provide superior insulation material.

## Integration with Homestead Systems

- Geese graze pastures that are too tough for ducks
- Manure enriches pasture (moderate nitrogen content)
- Can be rotated through garden beds after harvest for weed/grass cleanup
- Work well with goats and sheep in multi-species grazing
- Eggs and meat contribute to [[food-preservation-canning]] stores
- See [[homesteading-livestock-comparison]] for comparison with other livestock
