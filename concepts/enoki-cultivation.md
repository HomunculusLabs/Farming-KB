# Enoki Cultivation

## Overview

Enoki ([[flammulina-velutipes]]), also known as Enokitake, is one of the most commercially important [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]] worldwide. Its wild form looks entirely different from the cultivated product: in nature it produces a normal cap-and-stem mushroom with a velvety, brown stem, but commercial cultivation produces the distinctive long-stemmed, white, needle-like clusters familiar from Asian cuisine. This transformation is achieved through controlled cultivation conditions that exploit the mushroom's natural response to low light and high CO2.

## Species Description

### Wild Form

Wild Enoki grows on dead or dying hardwood trees, particularly elms, in temperate forests. The fruiting body has an amber-to-brown, velvety stem (hence the species epithet "velutipes") and a convex to plane cap 1-5 cm in diameter. It is one of the few mushrooms that fruits in cold weather, often appearing in late autumn through winter.

### Cultivated Form

Commercial Enoki is produced in clusters of long (10-15 cm), thin, white stems topped with small (1-2 cm) caps. This morphology results from growing conditions that suppress cap development and promote stem elongation -- high CO2 and near-total darkness.

## Substrate Preparation

### Primary Substrates

Enoki can be grown on various lignocellulosic materials:

- **Sawdust-based blocks:** Hardwood sawdust (beech, oak, poplar) supplemented with wheat bran or rice bran (20-30% supplementation rate, higher than most species)
- **Paddy straw:** Traditional substrate in parts of Asia
- **Corn cob or cotton waste:** Alternative substrates used in some commercial operations

### Common Formula

- 70-80 parts hardwood sawdust
- 20-30 parts wheat bran or rice bran
- Moisture content: 63-68%
- pH: 5.5-6.5 (adjust if necessary with gypsum or lime)

### Sterilization

Substrate is packed into bottles (traditional Asian method) or bags and sterilized at 15 psi for 60-90 minutes. Commercial operations often use larger autoclave systems.

## Spawn Running (Colonization)

| Parameter | Range | Optimum |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Temperature | 68-77°F (20-25°C) | 72-75°F (22-24°C) |
| Duration | 13-20 days | 15-18 days |
| CO2 | High (no exchange needed) | N/A |
| Light | Dark | Complete darkness |

Enoki colonizes aggressively. Full colonization should be achieved within 2-3 weeks. The mycelium is white to cream-colored and grows densely.

## Low-Temperature Cultivation

Enoki is unusual among cultivated mushrooms in requiring a significant cold-temperature phase for optimal fruiting. This requirement for cold temperatures is a key characteristic of the species:

### Cold Shock / Initiation

After full colonization, bottles/blocks are moved to a cold room:

- **Temperature:** 50-55°F (10-13°C) for initiation
- **Duration:** Several days to trigger [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]]
- Some cultivators recommend a more dramatic cold shock (down to 38-42°F / 3-6°C) for 24-48 hours

### Stem Elongation Phase

The distinctive long-stemmed morphology requires:

- **High CO2:** 2000-5000 ppm (significantly elevated above ambient)
- **Near darkness:** Light levels kept below 50 lux; any significant light exposure causes cap expansion and stem shortening
- **Cool temperature:** 50-59°F (10-15°C) maintained throughout the elongation phase
- **High humidity:** 90-95% RH
- **Duration:** 10-20 days for full stem development

### The Bottle Method

Traditional Japanese cultivation uses narrow-mouthed bottles. The restricted opening at the bottle neck channels the [[falconer-growing-mushrooms-in-sheds]] upward, naturally promoting the elongated form. A collar may be placed around the emerging cluster to further encourage upright growth.

## Harvest and Post-Harvest

### When to Harvest

Harvest when stems have reached the desired length (typically 10-15 cm) and caps are still small (1-2 cm). Mushrooms are harvested as intact clusters by twisting or cutting at the base.

### Yield

- [[biological-efficiency]]: 80-150% (high for a cultivated mushroom)
- One to two flushes per block/bottle
- Commercial operations typically take only the first flush for premium quality

### Storage

Refrigerate at 34-38°F (1-3°C) in breathable packaging. Enoki has a reasonable shelf life of 2-3 weeks when properly stored. Avoid freezing, which damages cellular structure.

## Nutritional and Medicinal Value

Enoki is rich in:

- **Ergothioneine:** A potent antioxidant amino acid
- **Beta-glucans:** Immune-modulating polysaccharides
- **B vitamins:** Particularly niacin (B3), thiamine (B1), and riboflavin (B2)
- **Dietary fiber:** High in chitin and other fungal cell wall components

Studies suggest potential anti-tumor, immune-enhancing, and cholesterol-lowering properties. See [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-enoki|medicinal Enoki guide]] for detailed research summaries.

## Common Problems

- **Stem shortening / cap expansion:** Too much light exposure during elongation phase
- **Browning:** Temperature too high, or bacterial contamination
- **Mold contamination:** Trichoderma and Penicillium are common competitors; strict [[cultivator-sterile-technique-and-agar-culture]] essential
- **Uneven clusters:** Improper bottle filling or uneven colonization
