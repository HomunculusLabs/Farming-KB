## Overview

Harvest and curing are the final and most patient-dependent stages of cannabis cultivation. Proper technique during these phases preserves the [[terpenes]], potency, and smoothness developed during the grow. Rushing any step — harvest timing, drying speed, or cure duration — directly reduces quality.

## Harvest Timing

Proper timing depends on monitoring trichome development throughout the [[cannabis-flowering-stage]]. The [[cannabis-environment-control]] during final weeks also affects terpene preservation at harvest. Key summary:

- **Clear [[trichomes]]**: Too early — lower potency, grassy taste
- **Milky/cloudy trichomes**: Peak potency — energetic, cerebral effect
- **Amber trichomes**: THC degrading to CBN — more sedative, body-heavy effect
- **Target**: 50-70% milky with first amber appearing

Also consider:

- **Pistil color**: 50-70% brown/dark pistils generally correlates with readiness
- **Pistil color alone is unreliable**: Some strains have dark pistils early; always confirm with trichomes
- **Strain genetics**: Indicas typically finish in 7-9 weeks; sativas 10-14 weeks

## Pre-Harvest Preparation

### Flushing

If growing in hydroponics or with synthetic nutrients, stop feeding and run plain water for 5-14 days before harvest. Living soil grows typically do not require flushing — the soil biology naturally tapers [[nutrient-availability]].

### Dark Period

Some growers give plants 24-48 hours of complete darkness before harvest. The theory is that this pushes the plant to produce final trichome development and terpenes. Evidence is anecdotal but many experienced growers swear by 24-48 hours of dark.

### Equipment Preparation

- Clean, sharp trimming scissors (2-3 pairs)
- Gloves (resin will coat hands)
- Drying rack or lines with hangers
- Hygrometer for the drying space
- Curing jars (wide-mouth Mason jars, 1 quart to 1 gallon)
- Hygrometer gauges that fit inside curing jars

## Harvest Methods

### Wet Trimming

Leaves are removed immediately after cutting down the plant, before drying.

**Advantages**:
- Faster drying (less plant material to retain moisture)
- Easier to trim — leaves are turgid and easy to handle
- Less risk of mold (fewer leaf surfaces to trap moisture)
- Better appearance — buds look cleaner after drying

**Disadvantages**:
- Trichomes are more fragile and easily knocked off during handling
- Slightly harsher initial smoke (less gradual moisture reduction)

### Dry Trimming

Plants are hung whole (or as large branches) and dried before trimming.

**Advantages**:
- Gentler on trichomes — less handling of fragile dried buds
- Slower, more gradual drying preserves terpenes
- Easier to monitor dryness by feeling branches
- Many claim smoother, better-tasting final product

**Disadvantages**:
- Leaves curl around buds as they dry, making trimming more difficult
- Longer drying time required
- Higher mold risk if humidity is not controlled

## Drying

### Ideal Drying Conditions

- Temperature: 60-70 F (15-21 C)
- Humidity: 55-65% RH
- Dark environment (light degrades cannabinoids and terpenes)
- Gentle air circulation (no direct fan on buds)
- Drying duration: 7-14 days

### Drying Setup

- Hang branches upside down from lines or hangers
- Space branches to allow airflow between them
- Position an oscillating fan to keep air moving without blowing directly on plants
- Place a hygrometer at canopy level
- Use a small exhaust fan or open window for air exchange in the drying room

### How to Tell When Drying Is Complete

Buds are dry enough for curing when:

- Small stems snap cleanly (not bend)
- Larger stems still bend slightly (they continue drying in jars)
- Buds feel dry on the outside but slightly spongy inside
- Moisture content is approximately 10-15% (55-62% RH inside the bud)

Do not rush drying. Buds dried too quickly will taste harsh, smell grassy, and have a brittle, burnt quality. Over-dried buds (crumbling to powder) are difficult to rehydrate properly.

## Curing

Curing is the slow oxidation and breakdown of chlorophyll, sugars, and residual nutrients in the bud. This process develops the full flavor profile and smoothness of the final product.

### Jar Curing Process

1. **Jarring**: Place trimmed buds loosely in wide-mouth Mason jars. Fill jars 60-75% full — do not pack tightly. Place a hygrometer inside each jar.

2. **Burping**: For the first 2 weeks, open jars once daily for 5-15 minutes. This releases accumulated moisture and gases (CO2, ammonia) and allows fresh air exchange.

3. **Monitoring**: Check jar humidity readings. Target 58-62% RH inside the jar.
   - **Below 55%**: Buds are over-dried. Add a humidity pack (Boveda 62%) to rehydrate.
   - **Above 65%**: Buds are too moist. Remove from jars, let air dry for a few hours, then re-jar.
   - **Above 70%**: Mold risk. Remove from jars immediately and extend drying time.

4. **Duration**:
   - **Minimum**: 2-3 weeks for smokeable results
   - **Good cure**: 4-8 weeks for full flavor development
   - **Premium cure**: 3-6 months for maximum smoothness and complexity
   - After 2-3 weeks, burping can be reduced to every 2-3 days
   - After 1 month, burp once per week
   - After 2 months, jars can be sealed with minimal attention

### What Happens During Curing

- Chlorophyll breaks down, removing the "grassy" or "hay-like" taste
- Sugars and starches slowly convert, smoothing the smoke
- Terpenes oxidize and develop, creating depth and complexity in the aroma
- Cannabinoids continue to decarboxylate slightly (very slowly)
- The overall experience becomes smoother, more flavorful, and less harsh

## Long-Term Storage

After curing, proper storage preserves quality for months to years.

### Best Practices

- **Airtight glass jars**: Mason jars with tight lids
- **Temperature**: 60-70 F (15-21 C) — cooler is better, but avoid refrigeration (condensation risk)
- **Darkness**: Store in a dark cabinet or closet
- **Humidity**: Maintain 58-62% RH using 2-way humidity packs (Boveda 58% or 62%)
- **Avoid**: Plastic bags (static damages trichomes), refrigerators (condensation), freezers (trichomes become brittle and break off)
- **Vacuum sealing**: Acceptable for long-term storage if buds are properly cured first
