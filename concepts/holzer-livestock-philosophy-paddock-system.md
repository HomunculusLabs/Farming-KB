# Holzer Livestock Philosophy and Paddock System

Livestock play a large role in a permaculture system. They provide high-quality produce and are industrious, pleasant workers. The key principle: always keep animals outside in family groups, meeting the needs of different species. The size of the area depends on the space naturally required by each species.

## Breeding Philosophy

### Old Breeds vs Modern Breeds
- Old, sometimes rare domestic breeds and wild animals best fulfil permaculture requirements
- Old breeds are significantly hardier, more intelligent, and more adaptable than over-bred modern cousins
- Their natural instincts are sufficient -- they do not fall over their own feet
- Produce is of far higher quality
- Can be kept under near-natural conditions (main requirement)
- Holzer has successfully bred: mouflon, chamois, ibexes, red/roe/fallow deer, yaks, water buffalo, American and European bison

### Modern Intensive Farming
- Amounts to torture of large numbers of animals
- Food produced this way is of very low nutritional value
- Growth hormones, antibiotics, and sedatives are commonly used
- Stress hormones from transport pass into meat
- Preserving diversity of old breeds is a duty and significant cultural heritage

## Holzer's Livestock Species

Poultry, pigs, horses, cattle, sheep, goats, and many other animals can all be used. The key is to select robust, hardy animals suited to the terrain.

## Duck and Goose Keeping

- Provide dry nesting sites that are well-ventilated but not draughty
- Partially dark, secluded places preferred for nesting
- At start of breeding season, sites must be closeable so birds get used to habitat slowly
- Open water access needed year round
- If pond freezes completely in winter, birds are defenceless against predators
- Pond inflow should be at steep angle -- water pressure keeps area around inflow free of ice
- Twice as many nesting sites as brooding-capable birds for good breeding results
- **Ducks**: omnivores -- young leaves, roots, aquatic plants, grain, worms, amphibians, small fish, snails
- **Geese**: live exclusively on plants, particularly grass and meadow plants; graze and fertilise small areas; best alarm system on the farm (loud honking announces visitors); used as guard animals worldwide
- Holzer has also bred quail, pheasants, and other wild poultry
- With enough sympathy and understanding, even demanding native grouse (capercaillie, hazel grouse) can be bred

## Paddock Rotation System

- Animals change paddocks regularly
- Some paddock shelters used as crop storage during winter (exactly where crops are)
- Remove straw or hay with tractor fork, pour in sand if necessary, tip crops in with tractor
- Block entrance with straw for winter insulation
- Soil insulation + earth temperature prevents freezing even in harshest winters

## Integration with Crop Growing

- Paddocks used simultaneously for crops and livestock
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-animal-housing-detailed]], crop storage, tool storage
- Design buildings for multiple uses from the start to prevent large-scale alterations later

## Animal Welfare Principles

- Every farmer should be able to say they would be happy to swap places with their livestock
- Give animals a good life -- they are the ones providing the produce
- Intensive farming of any animal creates low-quality food and stressed animals
- Animals kept outside in family groups with adequate space are naturally healthy
- Old breeds kept under near-natural conditions rarely need veterinary intervention
