# Cannabis and Brain Cancer (Glioblastoma)

## Overview

Brain cancer encompasses malignant tumors arising within the brain tissue. The
location and size of cancerous brain tumors largely determine survivability and
the various signs and symptoms likely to develop. As tumors grow, impairment
increases, potentially impacting the whole body as well as the mind. The lower
in the brain structure the tumor sits, the poorer the survival outcome, because
the brain's lower portions control vital functions such as breathing and heart
rate.

Signs and symptoms cover a relatively wide range: altered levels of
consciousness from mild confusion to epileptic seizures, odd behavior to
stroke-like handicaps, plus headache, visual impairments, and nausea with
vomiting. Orthodox medicine does not know what causes brain cancer and can
neither prevent nor cure it with standard approaches. Diagnosis relies on
patient history, physical examination, EEG, eye examination, and imaging
techniques (MRI or CT scans), with definitive diagnosis requiring needle biopsy
or brain surgery. The five-year survival rate for the most common brain tumors
is: glioblastomas 2%, astrocytomas 30%, and meningiomas 70%.

### Human Safety Studies

In 2006, scientists from Madrid enlisted nine patients with brain cancers that
had failed to respond to standard surgery or radiation. They concluded that THC
could be safely injected into human brain tumors without causing overt
psychoactive effects.

### Molecular Mechanisms

In 2008, Israeli researchers discovered that E2F1 and Cyclin A, two proteins
promoting cell cycle progression, were down-regulated by THC, enabling arrest
of glioblastoma multiforme brain cancer cells.

Two additional Spanish studies on mice and humans with recurring glioblastoma
found that TIMP-1 down-regulation and MMP-2 down-regulation may be hallmarks
of cannabinoid-induced inhibition of glioma progression.
