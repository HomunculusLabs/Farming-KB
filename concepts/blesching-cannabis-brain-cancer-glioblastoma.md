# Cannabis and Brain Cancer (Glioblastoma)

## Overview

Brain cancer encompasses malignant tumors arising within the brain tissue. The
location and size of cancerous brain tumors largely determine survivability and
the various signs and symptoms likely to develop. As tumors grow, impairment
increases, potentially impacting the whole body as well as the mind. The lower
in the brain structure the tumor sits, the poorer the survival outcome, because
the brain's lower portions control vital functions such as breathing and heart
rate.

Signs and symptoms cover a relatively wide range: altered levels of
consciousness from mild confusion to epileptic seizures, odd behavior to
stroke-like handicaps, plus headache, visual impairments, and nausea with
vomiting. Orthodox medicine does not know what causes brain cancer and can
neither prevent nor cure it with standard approaches. Diagnosis relies on
patient history, physical examination, EEG, eye examination, and imaging
techniques (MRI or CT scans), with definitive diagnosis requiring needle biopsy
or brain surgery. The five-year survival rate for the most common brain tumors
is: glioblastomas 2%, astrocytomas 30%, and meningiomas 70%.

## Research Evidence

### Cannabinoid Mechanisms Against Brain Cancer

Starting in 2001, an international research team examined cannabinoids and
brain cancer. Local injections of the synthetic cannabinoid JWH-133 (a potent
CB2 receptor agonist) into mice with brain cancer cells considerably reduced
malignant tumor size.

By 2003, Italian studies expanded understanding by showing that the
non-psychoactive CBD produced significant anti-brain tumor activity both in
vitro and in vivo. CBD selectively produced oxidative stress in brain cancer
cells, triggering apoptosis (programmed cell death) while leaving normal cells
unaffected.

A 2004 Spanish experiment revealed that cannabinoids effectively inhibited a
chemical signal needed for brain tumors to build their blood supply -- an
essential element for tumor survival and proliferation. The Spanish researchers
considered this blockade one of the most promising anti-tumor approaches
available, proposing "a novel pharmacological target for cannabinoid-based
therapies."

### Human Safety Studies

In 2006, scientists from Madrid enlisted nine patients with brain cancers that
had failed to respond to standard surgery or radiation. They concluded that THC
could be safely injected into human brain tumors without causing overt
psychoactive effects.

### Molecular Mechanisms

In 2008, Israeli researchers discovered that E2F1 and Cyclin A, two proteins
promoting cell cycle progression, were down-regulated by THC, enabling arrest
of glioblastoma multiforme brain cancer cells.

Two additional Spanish studies on mice and humans with recurring glioblastoma
found that TIMP-1 down-regulation and MMP-2 down-regulation may be hallmarks
of cannabinoid-induced inhibition of glioma progression.

A 2010 San Francisco study showed that THC and CBD acted synergistically to
inhibit brain cancer cell growth by inducing reactive oxygen species to produce
apoptosis.

## Strain and Form Considerations

Both THC and CBD are potent inhibitors of cancer cell development and produce
apoptosis independently. Greater effects may be produced synergistically when
THC and CBD are applied together. Both sativa and indica strains deliver the
full range of plant cannabinoids. Patients wishing to employ a non-psychoactive
form may use raw juice made from fresh cannabis leaves.

## Key Cannabinoids Studied

- THC -- binds CB1 and CB2; down-regulates cell cycle proteins
- CBD -- produces oxidative stress selectively in cancer cells; induces
  apoptosis in glioma cell lines
- JWH-133 -- synthetic CB2 agonist; reduces tumor size
- WIN55,212-2 -- inhibits VEGF (tumor blood supply signals)
- Anandamide -- endogenous cannabinoid; anti-tumor activity

## Mind-Body Considerations

The brain represents the physical structure associated with the mind. Brain
cancer severely disrupts free will, consciousness, and the authority to govern
actions of the body. The mind/brain interprets incoming sensory data and
delivers responses. Cancer in this realm may reflect conflicting beliefs and
internal battle against change. Rigid adherence to mental constructs fueled by
fears of change may cause rigidity in a naturally soft and ever-changing organ.
