# Trichomycetes — Gut Fungi of Arthropods

## Overview

Trichomycetes are an ecologically defined group of fungi and fungus-like organisms that inhabit the digestive tracts of arthropods. With approximately **225 described species** in **55 genera** (including the Amoebidiales), they represent a fascinating and poorly understood component of [[molecular-methods-fungal-taxonomy-and-diversity]] and [[fungal-symbiosis-types]]. These obligate gut commensals are intimately associated with their arthropod hosts and have evolved specialized morphologies and life cycles adapted to the hindgut environment.

## Taxonomic Composition

The group comprises four orders, though their phylogenetic relationships are complex:

| Order | Genera | Species | Host Group | Habitat |
|-------|--------|---------|------------|---------|
| **Harpellales** | 33 | 141+ | Dipteran larvae | Freshwater |
| **Asellariales** | 3 | 11 | Isopods, springtails | Terrestrial/aquatic |
| **Eccrinales** | 14 | 52 | Millipedes, crabs, amphipods | Aquatic/terrestrial |
| **Amoebidiales** | 2 | 12 | Insect and crustacean larvae | Aquatic |

**Important caveat**: The Amoebidiales are **NOT phylogenetically related** to the other Trichomycetes. Their inclusion in the group is based solely on ecological similarity (gut-dwelling habit), not shared evolutionary ancestry. This makes the Trichomycetes, as traditionally defined, a polyphyletic assemblage rather than a natural taxonomic group.

## Host Specificity

Trichomycetes are associated exclusively with **mandibulate arthropods** — those with chewing mouthparts — that function as:

- **Detritivores** — feeding on decaying organic matter
- **Algivores** — feeding on algae
- **Omnivores** — feeding on mixed organic materials

Crucially, Trichomycetes are **NOT found in predators or carnivores**. This host restriction is believed to reflect the longer gut residence times and more favorable physicochemical conditions in the hindguts of detritivorous and algivorous arthropods, compared to the shorter, more acidic digestive tracts of predators.

Infection rates can approach **100% of individuals** within a host population, indicating that these fungi are nearly ubiquitous in suitable hosts within their geographic range.

## Ecological Interactions

### Commensalism

The majority of Trichomycetes species are **commensal**, living in the host gut without apparent benefit or harm. Their ecological role may include:

- Nutrient processing within the gut lumen
- Stabilization of gut microbial communities
- Subtle contributions to host digestion of refractory materials

### Pathogenicity

A few species exhibit pathogenic effects:

- ***Smittium morbosum*** — Kills mosquito larvae by **inhibiting ecdysis** (molting), preventing the transition from larval to pupal stage. This species has been investigated as a potential biological control agent for mosquito populations.

### Reproductive Manipulation

Some Harpellales species grow into the **developing ovaries** of their hosts, producing a remarkable outcome:

- Infected adult females become **sterile** — unable to produce viable eggs
- Instead of eggs, females **disseminate fungal cysts**, effectively converting the host into a vehicle for fungal dispersal
- This represents an extreme form of parasitic reproductive manipulation

## Cultivation

Growing Trichomycetes in laboratory culture has proven challenging, reflecting their obligate association with arthropod hosts:

### Successful Cultures
- Some **Harpellales** species — over **190 isolates** have been maintained in axenic culture
- ***Amoebidium parasiticum*** (Amoebidiales) — the best-studied species in axenic culture

### Preferred Culture Medium
- **Dilute brain-heart infusion** (1/10 strength BHIv) supplemented with:
  - **Thiamine** (vitamin B1)
  - **Biotin** (vitamin B7)
- This medium approximates the nutritional conditions of the arthropod hindgut

## Isolation Protocol

Isolation of Trichomycetes requires careful dissection:

1. Collect and anesthetize host arthropods
2. **Dissect the hindgut** under sterile conditions
3. **Wash** the excised gut in antibiotic solution to eliminate contaminating bacteria and gut flora
4. **Transfer** the gut contents or fungal thalli to the culture medium
5. Incubate at appropriate temperature (typically room temperature for freshwater species)

Successful isolation rates vary considerably among species and host groups.

## Research Significance

Trichomycetes occupy a unique niche at the intersection of [[fungal-succession-woody-debris-ecology]], [[fungal-zoospore-biology-and-flagellate-motility]], and invertebrate–microbe interactions. Their study contributes to understanding:

- The evolution of obligate symbiosis in fungi
- The diversity of [[fungal-interactions-with-microbes]] in animal guts
- Potential biological control applications (e.g., *Smittium morbosum* against disease vectors)
- The hidden diversity of [[biodiversity-fungi-soil-fungal-communities]]

Despite their ecological importance, Trichomycetes remain understudied, and many species — particularly in tropical regions — await discovery and description.
