The connection between light, spore production, and geotropism suggests the
following signaling cascade: light triggers spore production through a
photoreceptor-mediated pathway; developing spores or associated gill tissues
produce a tropic hormone; this hormone modifies the stipe's growth response,
switching from evaporative-demand-directed to gravity-directed growth. The
identity of the tropic hormone remains unknown, but it may be a small molecule
diffusible through agar, as demonstrated by Hagimoto and Konishi in 1960.

This three-step cascade explains why geotropism requires light even though the
gravitational stimulus itself is light-independent. Light is not the proximal
stimulus for geotropic curvature but is required for the production of the
hormonal mediator that confers geotropic sensitivity.

## Phase 4: Blue Light Photomorphogenesis

Eger-Hummel described in 1980 at least four different responses to light in
mushrooms, classified under the general category of blue light photomorphogenesis.
Psilocybe cubensis shares this multi-response pattern with Coprinus congregatus.
The blue light receptors responsible for these responses are likely flavin-based
cryptochromes, which absorb maximally in the 450 nanometer range corresponding
