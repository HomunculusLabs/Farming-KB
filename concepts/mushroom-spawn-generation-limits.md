## Spawn Generation Limits and Transfer Logistics

## Overview

[[cuthill-mushroom-spawn-preparation-historical]] production follows a defined progression:

1. **Agar culture** (petri dish): Initial pure mycelium isolated from spores or [[growing-gourmet-agar-media-formulas-preparation]]
3. **G-2 (Second generation grain)**: Quart or half-gallon jars inoculated from G-1 masters
4. **G-3 (Third generation grain)**: Further expansion from G-2
5. **C-1, C-2**: Commercial designations for spawn distributed to growers

Each transfer step increases volume but also increases the risk of contamination and strain degeneration. The key principle: minimize the number of generational transfers.

## Agar-to-Grain Inoculation (G-1 Masters)

### Selection Criteria

Select a vigorously growing culture whose mycelium covers no more than three-quarters of the agar surface. Cultures that have entirely overrun the petri dish should be avoided because contaminants often enter along the margin of the dish. If the peripheral mycelium is laden with contaminant spores, any grain inoculated with it would become spoiled.

### Double Wedge Transfer Technique

Stamets recommends a "double wedge" technique for maximum efficiency:

1. Flame sterilize a scalpel until red hot
2. Cut a triangular wedge of mycelium-covered agar
3. Cut the wedge in half so both pieces can be speared simultaneously
4. Insert both pieces into an awaiting jar of sterilized grain
5. Repeat for each jar, flame sterilizing between transfers

A petri dish two-thirds covered with mycelium should amply inoculate 6-8 quart jars (maximum 10-12). The more mycelia transferred, the faster the colonization and the less chance of contamination.

### Post-Inoculation Handling

After completing transfers, tightly secure the lids and shake each jar thoroughly to evenly distribute the mycelial wedges. Each wedge travels throughout the grain media, leaving mycelial fragments adhering to kernels. If a wedge sticks to the glass, distribution is hampered and spawn running is inhibited. This usually indicates agar that was too thinly poured or has dehydrated.

## Grain-to-Grain Transfer (G-1 to G-2)

### Pre-Transfer Inspection

Before conducting transfers, carefully scrutinize each master jar for signs of contamination:

- Heavy growth or regions of sparse, inhibited growth
- Slimy or wet looking kernels (indicates bacteria)
- Exploded kernels with pallid, irregular margins
- Any unusual colorations
- Sour "rotten apple" or pungent odor
