# Mud Oven Construction

A mud oven (also called cob oven, earth oven, or clay oven) is a wood-fired baking oven built from natural materials -- clay, sand, straw, and stone. It costs very little to build, reaches high temperatures for bread and pizza, and retains heat for hours of baking or slow cooking. Drawn from [[nicole-faires]]' comprehensive manual.

## How It Works

1. Build a hot fire inside the oven for 45-90 minutes
2. The clay dome absorbs heat, reaching 600-800F (315-427C)
3. Push coals to the side or remove them
4. The hot earthen walls radiate heat evenly, like a brick oven
5. Bake bread (first: highest heat, best crust), then pizza, then casseroles, then desserts (as temperature drops)
6. After baking, the residual heat can slow-cook beans, soups, or dry herbs overnight

## Materials

| Material | Quantity | Purpose |
|----------|----------|---------|
| Clay soil | 150-200 lbs (test for clay content) | Primary building material |
| Sharp sand | 200-300 lbs | Thermal mass, prevents cracking |
| Straw | 1-2 bales | Fiber reinforcement (tensile strength) |
| Firebricks | 25-35 (for hearth floor) | Oven floor (cooking surface) |
| Gravel/crushed rock | 2-3 cubic feet | Insulation base |
| Empty glass bottles | 20-30 | Insulation layer under floor |
| Sawdust or perlite | 2-3 cubic feet | Insulation around dome |
| Newspaper | 1 stack | Mold for the oven dome |
| Water | 5-10 gallons | Mixing |

### Clay Test

Not all soil has enough clay. Test by:
1. Fill a clear jar 1/3 full with soil, add water to 2/3
2. Shake vigorously, let settle for 24 hours
3. Sand settles first, silt next, clay last (fine layer on top)
4. You want at least 20-30% clay content
5. If your soil is too sandy, dig deeper or source clay from a pottery supplier

### Cob Mix Ratio

- 1 part clay soil
- 2-3 parts sharp sand
- Enough straw to hold together when squeezed (a handful per mixing session)
- Mix by foot on a tarp: sand + clay first, then fold in straw
- Correct consistency: holds shape when squeezed in hand, doesn't crumble, doesn't stick to your hand excessively

## Construction Steps

### Step 1: Foundation and Base

1. Choose a level site protected from rain (under a roof overhang or build a shelter later)
2. Build a raised platform: concrete blocks, stone, or urbanite (reclaimed concrete chunks)
3. Platform should be 48-52 inches in diameter (for a 22-24 inch interior oven)
4. Height: 36-40 inches (comfortable working height)
5. Top the platform with a level bed of sand or gravel
6. **Total oven height rule**: the oven floor height = your knuckle height when your arm hangs at your side

### Step 2: Insulation Layer

1. Lay a bed of empty glass bottles (necks inward) on the platform
2. Fill gaps between bottles with sawdust or perlite
3. This creates an air pocket that prevents heat from escaping downward into the base
4. Cover bottles with a 2-inch layer of sand, level it

### Step 3: Hearth Floor

1. Lay firebricks tightly on the sand bed (no mortar needed)
2. The bricks must be perfectly level -- use a straight board and level
3. Sweep fine sand into any gaps between bricks
4. The hearth is the cooking surface; no cracks or unevenness

### Step 4: Build the Sand Mold (Form)

1. Moisten sand and build a dome shape on the hearth floor
2. The dome's interior dimensions determine the oven size:
   - Width: 20-24 inches (diameter at base)
   - Height: 16-20 inches (approximately 63-70% of width)
3. Cover the sand dome with wet newspaper (prevents sand from mixing with the cob)
4. This sand will be removed later through the door opening

### Step 5: First Cob Layer (Thermal Mass)

1. Build the oven dome over the sand mold using the cob mix (clay + sand + straw)
2. Start at the base, work upward in layers 2-3 inches thick
3. Each layer should overlap the previous like bricks
4. Walls should be 4 inches thick at minimum (6-8 inches is better for heat retention)
5. Smooth the surface as you go
6. Do NOT cut a door opening yet -- let the dome dry for 2-3 days

### Step 6: Cut the Door Opening

1. After the dome has dried for 2-3 days
2. Cut an arched doorway: 10-12 inches wide, 8-10 inches tall (at the top of the arch)
3. Door height: 63% of the dome interior height (this ratio ensures proper heat circulation)
4. Dig out the sand mold through the door
5. Remove all sand and newspaper

### Step 7: Insulation Layer

1. Mix a lean clay slip (clay + water, the consistency of thick cream)
2. Add sawdust or perlite until it holds together
3. Apply 4-6 inches of this insulation mix over the entire dome
4. This layer traps heat inside the oven
5. Smooth it but don't worry about aesthetics

### Step 8: Outer Protective Layer

1. Mix a final coat of clay + sand (no straw) for a smooth finish
2. Apply 1-2 inches over the insulation layer
3. Smooth with wet hands or a trowel
4. Optionally, add a lime plaster finish for weather resistance

### Step 9: Door

1. Build a wooden door from hardwood (oak, maple) to fit the opening
2. The door does NOT need to be airtight (some airflow is needed)
3. Add a handle and insulate the outside with metal or additional wood
4. Some designs use a removable door for easy ember management

### Step 10: Chimney (Optional)

- A mud oven does not strictly need a chimney (smoke exits through the door during firing)
- A small chimney (4-6 inches diameter) at the top of the dome can reduce smoke
- Build from a salvaged steel pipe embedded in the cob at the top

## Curing the Oven

This is critical -- do not skip or rush.

1. Let the oven air-dry for at least 1 week (2 weeks is better)
2. Start with a very small fire (a few twigs) for 30 minutes
3. Next day: slightly larger fire for 45 minutes
4. Increase fire size gradually over 5-7 days
5. If you see steam or hear crackling, the oven is still drying -- reduce heat
6. After a week of graduated fires, the oven is ready for full use
7. Rushing this step causes cracks

## Repairing Cracks

- Small hairline cracks are normal and seal themselves during the next firing
- Larger cracks: mix a small batch of clay slip (clay + water) and push it into the crack
- Major cracking: usually caused by insufficient curing or wrong clay-to-sand ratio
- Structural cracks: may need to rebuild the affected section

## Using the Oven

### Firing

1. Build a fire in the back of the oven using dry hardwood
2. Fire for 45-90 minutes until the dome is white-hot or blackened patches turn white
3. Push coals to the sides or rake them out
4. Sweep the hearth floor with a damp rag
5. Test temperature: throw a handful of flour on the floor; if it browns in 10 seconds, the oven is about 500F

### Baking Schedule (Temperature Drop)

| Temp Range | Timing After Fire | What to Bake |
|------------|-------------------|-------------|
| 600-700F | Immediately | Pizza (2-3 min), flatbreads |
| 500-600F | 15-30 min | Sourdough bread, rolls (20-30 min) |
| 400-500F | 45-60 min | Quick breads, cookies, roasting |
| 300-400F | 1-2 hours | Casseroles, baked beans, pies |
| 200-300F | 3-6 hours | Slow-cooked soups, stews |
| 150-200F | Overnight | Drying herbs, fruit, keeping food warm |

### Maintenance

- Cover the oven with a tarp or build a roof shelter to protect from rain
- Re-plaster the exterior annually if exposed to weather
- Clean ash from the hearth regularly
- Inspect for cracks before each firing season
