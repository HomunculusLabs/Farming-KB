### Brush Fences

For urgent stabilisation, brush fences are highly effective:

- Built from stakes 2 metres long, driven 1 metre deep, with numerous bushy weaves between.
- Constructed in parallel rows 7 metres apart across the main wind direction.
- Must have wind gaps of less than 50% to drop sand out.
- Temperature reduction of 12 degrees C or more has been measured in the shade of fences.
- Moisture loss is 1.8–3.0% less than in open areas.

These fences serve as precursors to the planting of hardy perennials adapted to dune conditions.

### Tree Planting Technique

Since lethal soil temperatures are rarely formed below 15 cm in dunes, careful placement of trees in holes this deep or deeper — stabilised from collapse by a woven basket or cardboard collar — prevents the seedling from being "cooked" until it can cast its own shade. Fertiliser should be thinly placed initially, as rapid infiltration of water in rains can carry it below root reach. Once the tree has spread a root web, additional fertiliser can be added.

### Coastal Blowout Treatment

Severe dune blowouts on coasts need intensive treatment:

1. Hand planting of quickset grasses such as *Ammophila* spp.
2. Pelleted [[moth-bean]] seed.
3. Brush fences in 7-metre squares for early stabilisation.

### Advanced Techniques

In China, rice-straw mats are used as fencing or surface stabilisers, and advanced trees in baskets are planted (basket and all) in the matting shelter. These pioneers provide shelter and mulch for later forestry and crop establishment. Sawdust and any such mulch in hollows is very beneficial; mill wastes (bark, sawdust) have helped establish *Acacia sophorae* forests in coastal dunes in Tasmania.

All plants in dunes benefit from nitrogenous fertiliser, phosphate, and trace elements, as old dunes are usually deficient in plant nutrients.

## Protection of Stabilised Dunes

Once stabilised, dunes must be protected from:

- **Recreation vehicles**: Devastation by off-road driving.
- **Heavy browsing**: Especially by goats and camels.
- **Badly aligned roads**: Which can reactivate sand movement.
- **Sand mining**: Particularly to windward of stabilised areas.
