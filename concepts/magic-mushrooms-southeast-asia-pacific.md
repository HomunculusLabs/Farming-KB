## Bali: Mushroom Omelettes

During the 1970s and 1980s, mushroom restaurants in Bali became quite famous.

Tourists could order [[psychedelic-mushroom-set-and-setting]], [[panaeolus-cyanescens-bali-omelette]].

Tourists can find menus offering mushrooms prepared as omelettes, soups, teas, pizzas, or juices.

Mushroom images and products are marketed commercially: hand-painted and mass-produced T-shirts, postcards, posters, lighters, and key rings, all featuring mushroom decorations.

During the fall of 1988, Thai authorities distributed warning leaflets at tourist centers describing a bizarre panic reaction by an Australian tourist.

Allen analyzed the incident and discovered the tourist had used excessive pharmaceuticals including highly addictive substances, not purely mushrooms.

In January 1989, this incident justified passage of a law prohibiting usage of psychoactive mushrooms ("hed keequai" in local language) with harsh penalties.

Mushroom usage continued despite prohibition, with cultivation moving to hard-to-reach wilderness locations.

It is unknown if a tourist in Thailand has ever been sentenced for usage or possession of mushrooms.

Some restaurants temporarily served dishes made from regular table mushrooms apparently spiked with a synthetic hallucinogen, causing dangerous reactions lasting up to 10 hours.

## The Soma Hypothesis

Schroeder and Guzman proposed that "soma," the substance revered as a deity by the ancient Aryan civilization, may not refer to the fly agaric mushroom as initially proposed by Wasson.

More likely, soma was the name of a psychedelic Psilocybe species, based on its spectacular psychotropic effects and geographic distribution pattern.
