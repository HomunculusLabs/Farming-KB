# Spawn Grain and Culture Medium Interaction for Lentinus Squarrosulus
# Carpophore Production

Nwanze et al. (2005) demonstrated that the interaction between spawn grain
type and culture medium composition has highly significant effects on
carpophore dry weight, stipe diameter, and pileus diameter in
Lentinus squarrosulus, an indigenous Nigerian mushroom species. This study,
published in the African Journal of Biotechnology, represents one of the
first systematic evaluations of spawn-medium interaction effects for
tropical mushroom cultivation in West Africa.

## Species and Nigerian Context

Lentinus squarrosulus (Mont.) Singer is a tropical edible mushroom commonly
found on dead logs in the Zaria environ of Kaduna State, Nigeria. It can be
cultivated using locally available lignocellulosic waste materials such as
sawdust, wood chips, and agricultural residues. The authors concluded the
species is "definitely fertile for commercial exploitation" when paired
with appropriate spawn grain and substrate combinations.

## Experimental Design

The experiment employed a split-split plot design replicated three times:
- **Main plot**: culture medium (6 types)
- **Sub-plot**: oil type and amendment rate (6 oils x 5 rates)
- **Sub-subplot**: spawn grain type (3 grains)

Cultivation used polypropylene heat-resistant bags, with 250 g of dry
substrate per bag. Bags were autoclaved at 121 degrees C for 15 min, inoculated with 10 g of
spawn (4% dry weight basis), and incubated in total darkness at 30 plus or
minus 2 degrees C for three weeks before aeration and light exposure.
Fruiting bodies from flushes 1 through 3 were harvested and measured. Data
were analysed by factorial ANOVA with Duncan's Multiple Range Test.

## Spawn Grains Tested

Three cereal grains were evaluated, all prepared by boiling, mixing with
12 g CaSO4 and 3 g CaCO3 per kg grain, and sterilising at 121 degrees C
for 20 min:

| Grain | Boil time | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Wheat | 15 min | Standard reference grain; induced widest stipe diameter in sawdust medium |
| Corn | 15 min | Produced heaviest carpophore dry weight and widest pileus diameter |
| Millet | 5 min | Smaller kernels; induced widest stipe diameter in animal bedding medium |

All spawns were incubated 2 weeks in total darkness at 37 degrees C and 70%
relative humidity before use.

## Culture Media Compositions

Six non-composted media were tested, each totalling 250 g dry weight.
Sawdust-based formulations dominate, reflecting sawmill waste abundance in the Nigerian study region:

1. **Sawdust** (Carey, 1974): 62.5 g sawdust, 62.5 g wood chips, 125 g brown rice
2. **Animal bedding and rice** (Roxon and Jong, 1977): 125 g wood chips, 125 g brown rice
3. **Lime 1** (Cangy, 1994): 195 g sawdust, 50 g rice bran, 2.5 g CaSO4, 2.5 g CaCO3
4. **Lime 2** (Oei, 1991): 235 g sawdust, 10 g rice bran, 2.5 g corn meal, 2.5 g CaCO3
5. **Lime 3** (Oei, 1991): 182.5 g sawdust, 62.5 g corn cobs, 5 g CaCO3
6. **Formulated** (Nwanze, 1996): 175 g sawdust, 70 g rice bran, 2.5 g CaCO3, 2.5 g oat meal

## Oil Amendments

All six media were supplemented with six locally available lipid sources at
five rates (0.007 to 0.028 ml/g): groundnut, coconut, palm kernel, butterfat,
palm, and cotton oils. These vegetable oils act as fruiting stimulants,
reflecting Nigerian agricultural resource availability and aligning with
prior work by Schisler and Sinden (1962).

## Key Results: Spawn Grain x Medium Interaction

### Stipe Diameter

The interaction of spawn grain and culture medium was highly significant
for stipe diameter (SE plus or minus 0.006 cm). The widest stipe diameter
(0.53 cm) was produced by the millet spawn x animal bedding and rice
combination. Corn spawn x animal bedding and rice ranked second at 0.47 cm.
Wheat spawn performed best in sawdust medium (0.36 cm) rather than animal
bedding and rice (0.32 cm). The poorest stipe diameters were consistently
associated with lime 3 and formulated media across all spawn grains.

### Carpophore Dry Weight

Dry weight showed the strongest interaction effects (SE plus or minus 0.024
g). The corn spawn x animal bedding and rice combination produced the
heaviest carpophores at 0.62 g, significantly outperforming all other
combinations. Corn spawn x sawdust was the next heaviest at 0.32 g.
Wheat spawn produced comparable dry weights in sawdust (0.25 g) and animal
bedding and rice (0.23 g), while millet spawn performed best in lime 1
(0.28 g). The lime 3 medium consistently produced the lightest carpophores
regardless of spawn grain.

### Pileus Diameter

Pileus diameter showed a similar pattern (SE plus or minus 0.069 cm). Corn
spawn x animal bedding and rice produced the widest pileus at 4.37 cm,
more than 1.4 cm wider than any other combination. Wheat spawn x animal
bedding and rice ranked second at 2.94 cm. Millet spawn showed less
variation across media, with sawdust (2.51 cm), lime 1 (2.56 cm), and
animal bedding and rice (2.68 cm) producing comparable pileus widths.

### Flush Patterns

Fruiting bodies were collected from flushes 1 through 3 across all treatment
combinations, with quantitative analysis focused on aggregate carpophore
measurements rather than flush-by-flush breakdowns.

### The Role of Brown Rice

The animal bedding and rice medium outperformed all sawdust-based media
across nearly every spawn grain x measurement combination, attributable to
the high protein, carbohydrate, fatty acid, and amino acid content of brown
rice. This is significant for Nigerian cultivators since brown rice is
widely available in the study region.

## Comparison with Psathyrella Atroumbonata

In parallel studies on P. atroumbonata, the same group found it favoured
sawdust x wheat spawn for widest stipe diameter and wheat/corn spawn x
sawdust for heaviest carpophore weight. L. squarrosulus instead favoured
animal bedding and rice x millet spawn for stipe diameter and corn spawn
for dry weight and pileus diameter. Both species produced widest pileus
diameter with corn spawn, suggesting corn spawn may have general
applicability for pileus enlargement across tropical mushroom species.

## Practical Implications

For Nigerian and West African cultivators targeting L. squarrosulus, the
optimal strategy is corn spawn inoculated into wood chips and brown rice
with appropriate oil amendment. This maximises both yield and marketable
cap size, and the simple two-component formulation is suitable for
smallholder adoption.
