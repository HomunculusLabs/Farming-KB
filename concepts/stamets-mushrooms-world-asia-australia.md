### Psilocybe subaeruginascens

A squat, collybioid, and annulate Psilocybe unique to Asia. Grows
gregariously to cespitose on soils enriched with woody debris, in wood chips,
and in wood chips mixed with horse dung. Frequently found along trails or
roadsides bordering deciduous forests. Fruiting April to July in temperate
southern Japan and subtropical Indonesia. Probably extensively distributed
between these two localities. Moderately potent; Koike et al. (1981) detected
psilocybin and psilocin.

Singer and Smith's attempted synonymy of P. subaeruginascens with
Stropharia venenata (= Psilocybe venenata) was in error (Guzman 1983).

### Psilocybe argentipes

Reported from Japan. Moderately active. Few people have actually experienced
this species. This Psilocybe may be distributed outside of Japan. Reported
analyses include psilocybin, [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]] Cleland

Cap 1.5-5 cm broad. Conic to convex, soon expanding to broadly convex with
a small umbo. Pale brown to dark brown, hygrophanous. Stem 50-125 mm long by
2-5 mm thick. Moderately to potently active based on bruising reaction. Known
only from Australia and Tasmania, found from May through August.

This species is in the center of a constellation of close relatives including
[[stamets-mushrooms-world-cyanescens]]. Chang and Mills (1992) attempted synonymy between several
Australian taxa, but their conclusions remain disputed due to questions about
specimen identification.

### Other Australian Records

Psilocybe australiana is documented from Australia (New South Wales), near
Sydney, and also known from Tasmania. An unidentified Psilocybe from
Melbourne, Australia, is noted in the epilogue of undocumented species.
