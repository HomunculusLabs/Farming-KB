# Psilocybe Mushrooms of Asia and Australia

Asia and Australia host a diverse but relatively understudied psilocybin
mycoflora. Species are documented from Japan, Thailand, Cambodia, Indonesia,
India, and throughout the Australian continent and Tasmania. Several species
are unique to these regions.

## Asian Species

### [[psilocybe-samuiensis]]

First reported by [[john-allen]] from the island of Koh Samui, Thailand. Grows in
well-manured, claylike soils in pastures, meadows, or amongst rice paddies.
First found in early August. P. samuiensis is strikingly similar to the
[[stamets-mushrooms-world-semilanceata]] and
prefers a common habitat with [[stamets-mushrooms-world-cubensis]]
and [[panaeolus-cyanescens]].

Psilocybin, psilocin, and baeocystin levels are .73%, .52%, and .05%
respectively (Gartz et al. 1994). Stijve and de Meijer (1993) reported
analysis of a species called Psilocybe thailandensis from Koh Samui with up to
.075% psilocybin and .60% psilocin. The relationship between these two taxa
remains unclear.

### Psilocybe subaeruginascens

A squat, collybioid, and annulate Psilocybe unique to Asia. Grows
gregariously to cespitose on soils enriched with woody debris, in wood chips,
and in wood chips mixed with horse dung. Frequently found along trails or
roadsides bordering deciduous forests. Fruiting April to July in temperate
southern Japan and subtropical Indonesia. Probably extensively distributed
between these two localities. Moderately potent; Koike et al. (1981) detected
psilocybin and psilocin.

Singer and Smith's attempted synonymy of P. subaeruginascens with
Stropharia venenata (= Psilocybe venenata) was in error (Guzman 1983).

### Psilocybe argentipes

Reported from Japan. Moderately active. Few people have actually experienced
this species. This Psilocybe may be distributed outside of Japan. Reported
analyses include psilocybin, [[stamets-mushrooms-world-cyanescens]], Psilocybe caerulipes, and allies.

### Japanese Gymnopilus and Other Genera

Several Gymnopilus species from Japan are noted as active. One notable
observation: a mushroom species that is inactive in Europe but psilocybin-
active in Japan, the Philippines, and Mississippi, USA, demonstrates that
chemical content can vary geographically within a species. However, a recent
study of Japanese collections by Tanaka et al. (1993) did not find
psilocybin in specimens previously thought to be active, suggesting possible
misidentification.

### Psilocybe subcaerulipes Hongo

Reported from Japan (Otsu City, Shiga-Prefecture) in soils covered with mosses
or along forest paths. Ochraceous to yellowish brown, sometimes olive toned,
hygrophanous, bruising dark bluish where injured. Moderately active.

### Panaeolus cyanescens in Asia

Originally described from Cambodia, thought to be widespread throughout the
Asian subtropics. Merlin and Allen (1993) reported this species across the
region. It is a dung-dwelling species found in pastures and fields throughout
tropical and subtropical Asia.

## Australian Species

### [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]] Cleland

Cap 1.5-5 cm broad. Conic to convex, soon expanding to broadly convex with
a small umbo. Pale brown to dark brown, hygrophanous. Stem 50-125 mm long by
2-5 mm thick. Moderately to potently active based on bruising reaction. Known
only from Australia and Tasmania, found from May through August.

This species is in the center of a constellation of close relatives including
[[psilocybe-australiana]], Psilocybe tasmaniana, and others. P. subaeruginosa
has pigmented (chocolate brown) pleurocystidia — a feature differentiating it
from related taxa which have hyaline cystidia.

### Psilocybe tasmaniana

Reported from Tasmania, New Zealand, and Australia. Active, potency unknown.
Closely related to P. subaeruginosa and [[stamets-mushrooms-world-cyanescens]]. Chang and Mills (1992) attempted synonymy between several
Australian taxa, but their conclusions remain disputed due to questions about
specimen identification.

### Other Australian Records

Psilocybe australiana is documented from Australia (New South Wales), near
Sydney, and also known from Tasmania. An unidentified Psilocybe from
Melbourne, Australia, is noted in the epilogue of undocumented species.

[[psilocybe-semilanceata]] is also reported from Australia and Tasmania (Guzman,
Bandala, and King 1993), extending its circumpolar temperate distribution.
