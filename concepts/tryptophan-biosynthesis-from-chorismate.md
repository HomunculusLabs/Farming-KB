# Tryptophan Biosynthesis from Chorismate

Tryptophan biosynthesis is the branch of the [[psilocybin-compound-profile]], shikimate-pathway-in-fungi,
into L-tryptophan through a sequence of reactions that construct
the [[indole]] ring system and attach it to an alanine-derived amino
acid [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-side-chain-construction-psilocin-synthesis]]. This pathway operates in the cytosol of fungal
cells and requires the cofactor [[pyridoxal-phosphate]] (PLP) for
the final transamination-like condensation step.

## Enzymatic Steps

### Step 1: 2-Deoxyisochorismate Formation

Chorismate is converted to 2-deoxyisochorismate by the enzyme
2-deoxyisochorismate synthase. This reaction involves the
substitution of the 2-hydroxyl group of chorismate with a hydrogen
atom, activated by magnesium ions. The reaction proceeds through
an elimination-addition mechanism that preserves the cyclohexadienyl
ring system while preparing the molecule for the subsequent
amination step.

### Step 2: Anthranilate Synthesis

2-Amino-2-deoxychorismate is formed from 2-deoxyisochorismate by
anthranilate synthase, which catalyzes an ammonia-dependent
amination at the C2 position. The ammonia is typically derived from
glutamine hydrolysis by a separate glutamine amidotransferase
domain. The resulting 2-amino-2-deoxychorismate is then
spontaneously or enzymatically converted to anthranilate
(2-aminobenzoate) through elimination of pyruvate.

### Step 3: Phosphoribosyl Transfer

Anthranilate undergoes phosphoribosyl transfer via anthranilate-
phosphoribosyl transferase, which attaches anthranilate to
5-phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate (PRPP) to form N-(5'-phosphoribosyl)
anthranilate (PRA). This reaction links the aromatic ring to a
ribose phosphate scaffold and commits carbon from the pentose
[[pentose-phosphate-pathway]] into the indole ring construction process.
PRPP serves as a key metabolite in both purine/pyrimidine
biosynthesis and [[cho-fish-amino-acid-preparation]] salvage pathways.

### Step 4: Amadori Rearrangement

N-(5'-phosphoribosyl) anthranilate isomerase catalyzes the
ribosyl-amine to ribosyl-imine rearrangement of PRA, converting
it to 1-(2-carboxyphenylamino)-1-deoxyribulose-5-phosphate
(CdRP). This Amadori rearrangement is analogous to the chemistry
observed in non-enzymatic Maillard browning reactions, but is
stereochemically controlled by the enzyme to produce a single
enantiomer essential for the subsequent ring closure.

### Step 5: Indole Ring Closure

Indole-3-glycerolphosphate synthase catalyzes the decarboxylative
cyclization of CdRP to form indole-3-glycerolphosphate (InGP).
This remarkable reaction simultaneously constructs the five-membered
pyrrole ring of the indole system through a condensation mechanism
that involves removal of the C2 carboxyl group and formation of
the C2-C3 bond of the indole ring. The enzyme requires no external
cofactors but proceeds through a tightly controlled mechanism
involving an enamine intermediate.

### Step 6: Indole Release

Tryptophan synthase alpha subunit catalyzes the reversible
aldolytic cleavage of indole-3-glycerolphosphate to produce free
indole and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate. This step is notable because
free indole is an unstable, hydrophobic intermediate that does not
readily diffuse away from the enzyme. In many organisms, the
alpha and beta subunits form a tightly coupled alpha-2-beta-2
complex that channels indole directly from the alpha to the beta
active site without release into the bulk solvent.
