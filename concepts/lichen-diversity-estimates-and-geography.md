# Lichen Diversity Estimates and Geography

Source: Biodiversity of Fungi (Hyde et al., Ch. 9)

## Species Counts

The count of lichenized fungal species accepted by Hawksworth et al. (1995) and Sipman and Aptroot (2001) is just less than 13,500, unchanged from the 1930s. The complete world inventory is expected to total about 18,000 species. Lichenized fungi are much better known than most other fungal groups, with perhaps 60-80% of species already described, compared to the generally accepted 5% for fungi overall (Hawksworth 1991).

## Geographic Distribution Patterns

Unlike vascular plants and many other fungi, the world's richest areas for lichen-forming species are NOT the humid tropics, but rather:
- **Southern temperate rainforests** (Galloway 1992a,b, 1995)
- **Northern temperate forests**
- **High-latitude zones**

### Tropical vs. Temperate

The richest known lichen biota for a single tree comes from an Asian tropical forest (Aptroot 1997). However:
- The boreal zone currently appears richer than the arctic zone
- Sweden and Norway (combined): 2,271 species vs. New Zealand: 1,378 species
- Diversity centers of Ostropales, Pyrenulales, and Arthoniales (3,100 species combined) are in the tropics
- In the high Andes, >90% of species belong to Lecanorales

### Substratum Patterns
- **Foliicolous lichens** (living on leaves): almost exclusively tropical. 10-30 species per leaf, 40-60 species per twig (Costa Rica)
- **Rocky substrata**: support much temperate/cold zone diversity, rare in lowland tropics
- A single local area (<200 ha) in old-growth tropical lowland forest may have >400 species of epiphytic lichens

### Local Area Densities
- Up to 227 species per square km on bark and wood alone in British deciduous temperate woodlands (Rose 1992)
- In a single biodiversity survey of 50,000 ha, one might find 5-20% of the species in the regional pool for a 5-10 million ha area

### Cosmopolitan Species
Several lichen species have worldwide distributions:
- Chrysothrix candelaris
- Parmelia sulcata
- Placopsis gelida
- Thamnolia vermicularis
- Xanthoria elegans

Bipolar species: Pseudephebe minuscula
Pantropical: Strigula smaragdula

## Influencing Factors

Regional distribution patterns strongly influenced by:
- Sulfur dioxide air pollution
- Habitat destruction
- Loss of ecological continuity
- Introduction of new substrata (buildings)
- Spread of exotic lichen species
