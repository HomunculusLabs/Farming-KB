# Composting and the Nutrient Cycle

Composting is the technique of controlled decomposition of
food scraps and organic waste to create fertilizer, serving
as a critical practice for maintaining the nutrient cycle
that sustains all ecological systems.

## The Nutrient Cycle

There are several key cycles that support the world's
ecology. The atmospheric cycle exchanges oxygen for carbon
dioxide. The hydro-cycle circulates and cleans water. Just
as importantly, the nutrient cycle provides the raw building
blocks that all life needs. The driving component of the
nutrient cycle is the action of plants, which use sunlight
to convert inert minerals in the ground into nutrients.

Plants incorporate nutrients into their bodies, and then
other plants and animals eat them to access those nutrients.
However, the rate of converting minerals into nutrients is
not enough to support the entire ecology. The ecology needs
to constantly recirculate nutrients through local biomes.
A bear eats berries, walks through the woods, and deposits
digested berries back as fertilizer. When the bear dies its
entire body decomposes and becomes fertilizer as well.

Plants and animals do not truly use up nutrients; they
constantly recycle them. Living things use nutrients and
then place them back into the soils to continue the process
anew. Plants use solar energy to mine inert minerals of the
earth, make nutrients out of them and put them into
circulation, basically forever.

## How Modern Infrastructure Breaks the Cycle

Modern infrastructure systems have no true nutrient
management systems. We grow food in large agri-business
plots fertilized by factory-made chemicals. The food is
harvested and shipped worldwide. People consume it. Wasted
food goes to ecologically inert landfills. The excreted
nutrients are flushed into sewer systems. Our current system
is actually designed to break down the nutrient cycle.

Nutrients are harvested from one location, transported away,
and then discarded. The ecology of food producing biomes
breaks down, requiring vast amounts of [[teaming-with-nutrients-natural-vs-synthetic-fertilizers]]
manufactured and shipped in to replace them. Our nutrient
cycle becomes dependent on fossil fuels and industry.

## What Composting Does

Composting is the technique of controlled, sanitary
decomposition of food scraps and organic waste to be used as
fertilizer. By composting we maintain the nutrient cycle.
While there is industrial composting, it is something that
can be done at the do-it-yourself scale right in the home.
When combined with home grown food systems you can maintain
your own local nutrient cycle and create an up-cycling
ecology right in your own backyard.

## Composting Techniques

Many people create compost piles in their yards, where food
scraps and plant materials are layered to encourage
controlled decomposition. The compost is occasionally turned,
tossed like a salad, which mixes the material and allows
oxygen into the process. Oxygenation is necessary for proper
and non-smelly composting. Some practitioners create compost
tea, where compost is soaked in water to dissolve nutrients
into a fertile solution for crops and soils.

## Speed and Scale

Compost can break down at different rates depending on the
system. The faster the breakdown, the more work and care
by humans the system will take. If one does not have the
proper skill and tries to create a fast compost system, one
could accidentally create quite a mess. In recent years
there has been an explosion of composting devices designed
for the layperson: barrels that rotate with a handle, or
devices like the Green Cone where you simply open a lid and
throw waste in.

## [[mycoremediation-bioreactor-design-considerations]] for Composting

The designer must design a compost system that works at the
level the client understands. Large scale or fast compost
systems require a lot of labor, skill, and time. While these
systems hold philosophical appeal, if the system takes too
much time or skill it will not function properly and will
create a mess. It is better to be conservative in designing
[[composting-systems]]. Remember the K.I.S.S. principle: Keep It
Simple Stupid! Any organic material can be composted, from
food to leaves, sticks, paper products, and clothing fibers.

## Vermiculture

Vermiculture systems are compost systems that incorporate
red worms, creating a more powerful composting system that
is not much more difficult than a normal compost system. The
worms accelerate decomposition and their castings are among
the finest fertilizers available. A vermiculture system can
be scaled to fit any context from apartment balconies to
large rural properties, making it one of the most versatile
composting strategies available to the [[permaculture-design-methodology]]er.

## Composting as Integral to Permaculture Design

Composting is an integral part of maintaining one of the
most important natural cycles necessary for a sustainable
system. Once a designer understands how composting works
there are many different ways a composting system can be
designed. One could even use prefabricated composters. An
efficient composting system should be part of any
permaculture design, matching the capacity and commitment
of the people who will manage it.
