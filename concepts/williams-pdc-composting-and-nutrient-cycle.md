# Composting and the Nutrient Cycle Composting is the technique of controlled decomposition of food scraps and organic waste to create fertilizer, serving as a critical practice for maintaining the [[teaming-with-nutrients-organic-fertilizer-types-and-sources|nutrient cycle]]
that sustains all ecological systems.

## The Nutrient Cycle

There are several key cycles that support the world's
ecology. The atmospheric cycle exchanges oxygen for carbon
dioxide. The hydro-cycle circulates and cleans water. Just
as importantly, the nutrient cycle provides the raw building
blocks that all life needs. The driving component of the
nutrient cycle is the action of plants, which use sunlight
to convert inert minerals in the ground into nutrients.

Plants incorporate nutrients into their bodies, and then
other plants and animals eat them to access those nutrients.
However, the rate of converting minerals into nutrients is
not enough to support the entire ecology. The ecology needs
to constantly recirculate nutrients through local biomes.
A bear eats berries, walks through the woods, and deposits
digested berries back as fertilizer. When the bear dies its
entire body decomposes and becomes fertilizer as well.

Plants and animals do not truly use up nutrients; they
constantly recycle them. Living things use nutrients and
then place them back into the soils to continue the process
anew. Plants use [[fukuoka-textdoc-three-dimensional-solar-energy-methodless-method]] to mine inert minerals of the
earth, make nutrients out of them and put them into
circulation, basically forever.

## How Modern Infrastructure Breaks the Cycle

Modern infrastructure systems have no true nutrient
management systems. We grow food in large agri-business
plots fertilized by factory-made chemicals. The food is
harvested and shipped worldwide. People consume it. Wasted
food goes to ecologically inert landfills. The excreted
nutrients are flushed into sewer systems. Our current system
is actually designed to [[query-can-fungi-break-down-plastic-pollution]] the nutrient cycle.

and then discarded. The ecology of food producing biomes
