# Proteobacteria Dominance in Cannabis Endorhiza

## Overview

Proteobacteria is the dominant bacterial phylum in the Cannabis endorhiza,
undergoing marked enrichment as [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]] transition from bulk
soil through the rhizosphere and into root tissue. Winston et al. (2014)
found that the [[weighted-unifrac-strain-clustering-cannabis-endorhiza-community-structure]] shift is characterized by a dramatic
decrease in Acidobacteria coupled with increases in both Proteobacteria
and Actinobacteria. Of the 17 OTUs that increased in abundance within the
Cannabis endorhiza, the majority were Proteobacteria, particularly from
the Rhizobiales order.

## The Phylum-Level Shift: Soil to Root

The transition from bulk soil to endorhiza involves predictable changes in
phylum-level composition that are consistent across plant species:

- **Acidobacteria**: Dramatically reduced in endorhiza. The most
  significant OTU decrease was Acidobacteria order iii1-15
  (Bonferroni-corrected ANOVA: p = 1.12e-7).
- **Proteobacteria**: Substantially enriched in endorhiza. Multiple OTUs
  from Alpha-, Beta-, and Gammaproteobacteria classes increased.
- **Actinobacteria**: Increased in endorhiza relative to bulk soil.

This pattern reflects the copiotroph-oligotroph continuum: Acidobacteria
are slow-growing oligotrophs adapted to nutrient-poor bulk soil, while
Proteobacteria are fast-growing copiotrophs that thrive in the carbon-rich
root environment created by rhizodeposition.

## Proteobacteria Classes in the Cannabis Core Endorhiza

The core Cannabis endorhiza community (present across all cultivars and
soil types) is dominated by Proteobacteria from multiple classes:

### Gammaproteobacteria

The most prominent class in the Cannabis endorhiza, represented by:

- **Pseudomonas**: A well-known plant growth-promoting endophyte genus
  found across diverse plant systems. Pseudomonas species produce
  phytohormones, siderophores, and [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] that support
  plant health and suppress pathogens.
- **Xanthomonadaceae**: A family within Gammaproteobacteria that includes
  both plant-associated and some plant-pathogenic species. Their presence
  as core endorhiza members suggests a primarily commensal or beneficial
  role in Cannabis roots.

### Betaproteobacteria

- **Oxalobacteraceae**: A family of root-associated bacteria, some
  species of which are known for oxalic acid degradation, a trait
  relevant to plant-microbe interactions since oxalic acid is a common
  plant metabolite that can be toxic at high concentrations.

### Alphaproteobacteria

- **Rhizobiales**: An order that includes well-known nitrogen-fixing
  symbionts and free-living root colonizers. Several Rhizobiales OTUs
  were among the 17 taxa enriched in the Cannabis endorhiza, suggesting
  an important functional role in root [[legume-nitrogen-dynamics]].

## Proteobacteria Orders Driving Cultivar Differences

The 71 OTUs significantly differentiating between Cannabis cultivars
(weighted ANOVA) were composed mostly of Proteobacteria from four orders:

1. **Pseudomonadales**: Abundance variation of Pseudomonas species between
   cultivars. Different cultivars modulate the relative abundance of
   specific Pseudomonas populations, likely through differences in root
   exudate composition.
2. **Burkholderiales**: A diverse order containing both plant-beneficial
   and some opportunistic pathogenic species. Their variable abundance
   across cultivars may reflect genotype-dependent selection for or
   against specific Burkholderiales populations.
3. **Sphingomonadales**: Including the cultivar-specific biomarker
   [[sphingomonas-wittichii-cannabis-endorhiza-strain-specificity]], prevalent in Maui Wowie. Members of this
   order are known for their metabolic versatility and ability to degrade
   aromatic compounds.
