# Proteobacteria Dominance in Cannabis Endorhiza

## Overview

Proteobacteria is the dominant bacterial phylum in the Cannabis endorhiza,
undergoing marked enrichment as microbial communities transition from bulk
soil through the rhizosphere and into root tissue. Winston et al. (2014)
found that the endorhiza community shift is characterized by a dramatic
decrease in Acidobacteria coupled with increases in both Proteobacteria
and Actinobacteria. Of the 17 OTUs that increased in abundance within the
Cannabis endorhiza, the majority were Proteobacteria, particularly from
the Rhizobiales order.

## The Phylum-Level Shift: Soil to Root

The transition from bulk soil to endorhiza involves predictable changes in
phylum-level composition that are consistent across plant species:

- **Acidobacteria**: Dramatically reduced in endorhiza. The most
  significant OTU decrease was Acidobacteria order iii1-15
  (Bonferroni-corrected ANOVA: p = 1.12e-7).
- **Proteobacteria**: Substantially enriched in endorhiza. Multiple OTUs
  from Alpha-, Beta-, and Gammaproteobacteria classes increased.
- **Actinobacteria**: Increased in endorhiza relative to bulk soil.

This pattern reflects the copiotroph-oligotroph continuum: Acidobacteria
are slow-growing oligotrophs adapted to nutrient-poor bulk soil, while
Proteobacteria are fast-growing copiotrophs that thrive in the carbon-rich
root environment created by rhizodeposition.

## Proteobacteria Classes in the Cannabis Core Endorhiza

The core Cannabis endorhiza community (present across all cultivars and
soil types) is dominated by Proteobacteria from multiple classes:

### Gammaproteobacteria

The most prominent class in the Cannabis endorhiza, represented by:

- **Pseudomonas**: A well-known plant growth-promoting endophyte genus
  found across diverse plant systems. Pseudomonas species produce
  phytohormones, siderophores, and antimicrobial compounds that support
  plant health and suppress pathogens.
- **Xanthomonadaceae**: A family within Gammaproteobacteria that includes
  both plant-associated and some plant-pathogenic species. Their presence
  as core endorhiza members suggests a primarily commensal or beneficial
  role in Cannabis roots.

### Betaproteobacteria

- **Oxalobacteraceae**: A family of root-associated bacteria, some
  species of which are known for oxalic acid degradation, a trait
  relevant to plant-microbe interactions since oxalic acid is a common
  plant metabolite that can be toxic at high concentrations.

### Alphaproteobacteria

- **Rhizobiales**: An order that includes well-known nitrogen-fixing
  symbionts and free-living root colonizers. Several Rhizobiales OTUs
  were among the 17 taxa enriched in the Cannabis endorhiza, suggesting
  an important functional role in root nitrogen dynamics.

## Proteobacteria Orders Driving Cultivar Differences

The 71 OTUs significantly differentiating between Cannabis cultivars
(weighted ANOVA) were composed mostly of Proteobacteria from four orders:

1. **Pseudomonadales**: Abundance variation of Pseudomonas species between
   cultivars. Different cultivars modulate the relative abundance of
   specific Pseudomonas populations, likely through differences in root
   exudate composition.
2. **Burkholderiales**: A diverse order containing both plant-beneficial
   and some opportunistic pathogenic species. Their variable abundance
   across cultivars may reflect genotype-dependent selection for or
   against specific Burkholderiales populations.
3. **Sphingomonadales**: Including the cultivar-specific biomarker
   Sphingomonas wittichii, prevalent in Maui Wowie. Members of this
   order are known for their metabolic versatility and ability to degrade
   aromatic compounds.
4. **Rhizobiales**: Variation in nitrogen-fixing and root-colonizing
   populations between cultivars suggests genotype-dependent modulation
   of root nitrogen symbioses.

## Functional Significance of Proteobacteria Enrichment

The dominance of Proteobacteria in the Cannabis endorhiza has several
functional implications:

- **Rapid response to root exudates**: Proteobacteria are generally
  fast-growing and respond quickly to the pulses of carbon compounds
  released by roots, giving them a competitive advantage in the
  rhizosphere and root interior.
- **Plant growth promotion**: Many Proteobacteria genera (Pseudomonas,
  Rhizobium, Burkholderia) are known to produce indole-3-acetic acid,
  gibberellins, and other phytohormones that stimulate root growth.
- **Disease suppression**: Proteobacteria produce antibiotics,
  siderophores, and lipopeptides that inhibit soil-borne pathogens.
- **Nutrient cycling**: Rhizobiales contribute to nitrogen fixation,
  while other Proteobacteria participate in phosphorus solubilization
  and sulfur cycling.
- **Stress tolerance**: Endophytic Proteobacteria can help plants
  tolerate abiotic stresses including drought, salinity, and heavy
  metals through ACC deaminase activity and other mechanisms.

## Comparison with Other Plant Systems

The Proteobacteria-dominated endorhiza of Cannabis is consistent with
patterns observed in other plant species. Studies of Arabidopsis, poplar,
and other crops have similarly found that the root endosphere is
enriched in Proteobacteria (particularly Alpha- and Gammaproteobacteria)
relative to bulk soil. This conservation across distantly related plant
species suggests fundamental convergent selection for fast-growing,
nutrient-responsive bacteria in the root interior.

[[pseudomonas]] species are among the best-characterized plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria and dominate the Cannabis endorhiza.

Winston et al. note that with the exception of Cellvibrio (see
[[cellvibrio-and-root-decay-microbiome]]), all prevalent core endorhiza
members are well-known endophytic bacteria primarily within Gammaproteo-
bacteria and Alphaproteobacteria, supporting observations from Arabidopsis
and poplar plant systems.

[[bacterial-endophytes-plant-roots]] describes the broader context of bacterial colonization within root tissues across diverse plant species. [[rhizosphere-microbiome-selection-model]] explains the two-step selection process driving these community shifts from bulk soil to root interior.

## Bacteroidetes as Secondary Contributors

Beyond Proteobacteria, Bacteroidetes orders Sphingobacteriales and
Flavobacteriales also contributed to both the core endorhiza community
and cultivar-specific differences. Sphingobacteriales were part of the
conserved core community, while both orders showed significant abundance
variation between cultivars, suggesting complementary functional roles
alongside Proteobacteria in the root microbiome.
