# Martha Tent Mushroom Growing

The Martha tent, named after the Martha [[stewart-brand]] of indoor greenhouse, is a
popular enclosed [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]. It provides an
intermediate step between simple [[fruiting-chambers-and-environmental-control]] like the [[eclss-environmental-control-life-support]], and more professional results while remaining affordable
and accessible to home cultivators.

## What Is a Martha Tent

A Martha tent is a freestanding, zippered enclosure made of clear or translucent
vinyl with a metal or plastic frame. Originally designed as an indoor greenhouse
for starting plant seedlings and growing orchids, these units are readily
adapted [[grass-seed-substrate-for-mushroom-cultivation]]. They typically measure 48-72 inches tall and
24-36 inches wide, providing several cubic feet of [[cotter-growing-space-design-fruiting-rooms]]. The clear
vinyl walls allow light transmission while the zippered door provides access.
Most models include shelves for holding [[cannabis-soil-growing-containers-flushing]] at multiple levels.

## Advantages Over Simple Chambers

The primary advantage of a Martha tent over simpler fruiting chambers is
capacity. A standard Martha tent can hold dozens of fruiting blocks or trays
across multiple shelves, whereas a shotgun FC is limited to a few substrate
cakes. This makes Martha tents practical for cultivators working with [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]] blocks, or multiple tray cultures
simultaneously.

Martha tents also provide more uniform environmental conditions across the
entire growing space. The enclosed volume, when properly equipped with
humidification and air exchange, creates a consistent microclimate that is
easier to monitor and control than the conditions in individual small chambers.

## Essential Equipment

A functional Martha tent setup requires several components beyond the tent
itself:

- **Humidifier**: Typically ultrasonic or cool mist impeller type, placed inside the tent
- **Humidistat controller**: Set to maintain 85-95% relative humidity
- **Exhaust fan**: Inline or mounted on the tent for fresh air exchange
- **Intake port**: Often HEPA-filtered opening for fresh air entry
- **Timer**: Controls the 12/12 light cycle with fluorescent or LED lighting
- **Circulating fan**: Prevents stagnant air pockets inside the tent
- **Digital thermometer/hygrometer**: For real-time [[singh-fungal-biosensors-environmental-monitoring]]

## Humidification Strategy

The humidifier is the most critical piece of equipment. Ultrasonic humidifiers
are preferred because they produce a fine, cool mist without raising the
temperature significantly. The humidifier should be connected to a humidistat
controller rather than run continuously, as over-humidification leads to
condensation problems, water pooling, and increased risk of [[mushroom-pin-formation]], the target humidity may be 95%+ RH. During
active cropping, 85-92% is typical. Some advanced setups use two-stage
humidistats or programmable controllers that adjust humidity setpoints
automatically based on the stage of the growing cycle.

## Fresh Air Exchange

Fresh air exchange in a Martha tent is typically provided by a small exhaust fan
mounted in the upper portion. As the exhaust fan removes stale, CO2-rich air,
negative pressure draws fresh air in through gaps around the zipper or through a
dedicated filtered intake port. The exhaust fan should provide 2-4 air changes
per hour depending on the species and substrate mass.

## Shelving and Layout

Wire shelving units provide the best airflow around growing containers and are
easy to clean. Solid shelves restrict air movement and create zones of high
humidity and stagnant CO2. Containers should be spaced to allow air circulation
on all sides. The humidifier sits on the floor, the exhaust fan mounts near the
top where CO2 accumulates, and lighting installs above the top shelf.

## Temperature Control

Martha tents provide a degree of thermal insulation but are not actively heated
or cooled. In most indoor environments, the ambient room temperature provides
adequate control. It is important to account for the heat generated by mushroom
metabolism during active fruiting, as multiple colonized blocks can raise the
temperature inside the tent by several degrees above ambient.
