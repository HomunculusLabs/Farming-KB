## Overview
Photon-counting scintillation imaging (PCSI) is a non-invasive technique that enables
real-time visualization of [[mycelial-cord-network-topology-graph-theory-bebber]] networks. By
using radioactively labeled amino acid analogs (such as ¹⁴C-aminoisobutyric acid,
or ¹⁴C-AIB) and a scintillation screen, researchers can observe the spatial and
temporal dynamics of [[fungal-mycelial-networks-nutrient-translocation]] colonies over extended
periods — from hours to weeks.

## Principle of Operation
The PCSI technique works by combining radioactive tracing with imaging:
1. A radiolabeled nutrient analog (¹⁴C-AIB) is applied to a specific location on
   the fungal colony (typically the inoculum or a resource site).
2. The colony is placed in contact with a scintillation screen.
3. As ¹⁴C-AIB moves through the mycelial network, the beta particles emitted by
   the decaying ¹⁴C interact with the scintillation screen, producing photons of
   visible light.
4. A sensitive camera captures the photon emissions, creating images that show
   where labeled nutrients are located within the colony at any given time.
5. Time-lapse imaging reveals the dynamics of nutrient movement over time.

## Key Advantages of PCSI
- **Non-destructive**: The fungal colony remains alive and functional throughout
  imaging. No sampling or sectioning is required.
- **Real-time**: [[fungal-destructive-sampling-herbarium-dna-extraction]] methods miss.
- **Spatial resolution**: The technique provides spatial information about where
  nutrients are within the colony, not just total amounts.
- **Extended duration**: Colonies can be imaged continuously for periods exceeding
  6 weeks in modified systems.
- **Quantitative**: Signal intensity correlates with nutrient concentration,
  enabling semi-quantitative analysis.

## Pulsatile Nutrient Transport
One of the most significant discoveries made using PCSI is that nutrient transport
in fungal [[mollison-designers-legume-tree-inoculation-and-nitrogen-distribution]] and transport priority are
  observed as the network develops over time.
- Contact with new wood resources triggers increased local branching and
  proliferation, followed by reorganization of transport routes.

## Route Switching Behavior
A particularly interesting observation is the transient nature of cord transport:
- Pre-existing cords may show no ¹⁴C-AIB movement for extended periods, then
  suddenly begin transporting at rates similar to primary cords.
