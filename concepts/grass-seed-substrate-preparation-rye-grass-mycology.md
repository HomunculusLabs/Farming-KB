# Grass Seed Substrate Preparation

## Overview

Grass seed serves as a versatile and effective substrate for the cultivation of several psilocybin-containing mushroom species. First documented in systematic cultivation guides, the grass seed method is particularly valued for producing both fruitbodies and sclerotia from species such as *Psilocybe mexicana* and *[[gartz-panaeolus-subbalteatus-taxonomy-and-intoxication-history]]** — mushrooms only

## Hydration Methods

### Direct Hydration

The standard formula for 720 ml jars:
- 110 grams grass seed
- 180 ml water

Water and seed are combined directly in the jar. The challenge is achieving the correct moisture level without creating a slimy, unshakeable mass (too wet) or a dry substrate that produces poorly (too dry).

### Overnight Soak Method (Preferred)

An alternative approach involves soaking the grass seed in water overnight before loading into jars. This produces a more homogenous substrate because all kernels absorb water evenly, reducing the risk of localized dry or oversaturated zones.

## Jar Preparation

1. Fill jars with hydrated grass seed (approximately 60% fill volume)
2. Place lids on jars but **do not screw tight** — lids must remain loose
3. Cover with a double layer of crumpled aluminum foil over the lid and upper jar portion
4. Jars are now ready for sterilization

The loose lid is critical: during cooling, a tight lid would create a vacuum that can crack the jar or pull contaminants inward. The foil layer protects the filter/lid area during sterilization.

## Sterilization Protocol

- **Equipment**: Pressure cooker or autoclave (standard boiling is insufficient)
- **Time**: 1 hour for 720 ml jars; 2 hours for 1.5 liter jars or spawn bags
- **Procedure**: Place jars in cooker with a water layer at the bottom. Ensure lids are loose throughout. After sterilization completes and pressure equalizes, remove jars and shake to redistribute wet and dry kernels.

**Safety note**: Always inspect jars for cracks before shaking hot jars.

## Moisture Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Slimy clump, cannot shake | Excess water | Reduce water volume by 10-20 ml per jar |
| Dry substrate, poor growth | Insufficient water | Increase water or use overnight soak |
| Uneven colonization | Mixed wet/dry zones | Shake more thoroughly after sterilization |
| Slow growth throughout | Slightly dry | Increase water incrementally |
