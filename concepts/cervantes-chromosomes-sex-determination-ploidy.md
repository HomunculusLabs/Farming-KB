## Haploid, Diploid, and Polyploid States

Cannabis cells may be haploid (one copy of each chromosome set) as in
gametes, or diploid (two chromosome sets per cell), which is the normal
state for somatic cells. Some researchers have investigated whether
triploid or tetraploid cannabis (cells with three or four chromosome
sets) are agronomically important.

In some species, polyploid plants grow bigger, yield more, or
outperform typical diploid members. Some early reports claimed polyploid
cannabis was more potent, but this [[vioxx-scandal-evidence-based-medicine-gaps-lessons-cannabis-research-blesching-2015]] was described as flimsy
and unscientific. No valid experiments have ever shown polyploid
cannabis to contain more THC-potent resin.

## Inducing Polyploidy

The polyploid characteristic can be induced with an application of
colchicine, which disrupts normal cell division. However, colchicine is
a poison and the technique has never led to agronomic success in
cannabis. Breeders attempting polyploidy induction have consistently
