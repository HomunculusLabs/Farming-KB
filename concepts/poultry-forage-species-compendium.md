# Poultry Forage Species Compendium

This compendium completes the annotated species list of plants known to be useful as poultry forage, organized by functional category. It represents one of the most comprehensive poultry forage references in permaculture literature.

## Summer Seed and Pod Species (Shrubs and Trees)

### Leguminous Trees
| Species | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| **Tree Lucerne** (Chaemocytisus proliferus) | Early to mid-summer seed drop; foliage also edible; hardy. False tree lucernes (Cytisus pallidus, C. pullilans) also useful |
| **Lespedeza** (L. bicolor, L. cytobotrya, L. sericea, L. striata) | As above |
| **Black Locust** ([[robinia-pseudoacacia]]) | High protein pods; leaves browsed; timber value; spiny leaves toxic to stock in large quantities |
| **Honey Locust** ([[gleditsia-triacanthos]]) | Edible pods; good timber; pole timbers |
| **Carob** ([[ceratonia-siliqua]]) | Seeds and pods stored for milling; 68% sugar beans |
| **Mesquites** (Prosopis, Strombocarpa) | As for carob; resistant to salt and drought |
| **Acacia spp.** | Many hard-seeded species eaten by man, stock, wild birds. A. giraffae (Africa): ground-up pods fed to livestock. A. albida (Africa): excellent nurse tree. Australian species: A. aneura, A. kempeana, A. holosericea, A. cowleana, A. victoriae, A. binervata, A. longifolia, A. peuce, A. oswaldia—all useful as human food in emergencies; drought, salt, and frost hardy |
| **Albizia** | Characteristics like acacia |

### Salt-Tolerant Shrubs
- **Atriplex** (salt-bushes): Wind, salt, and pest resistant
- **Pea Trees** ([[caragana-arborescens]], C. siberica): Mainly poultry fodder; seed can be eaten

## Berries and Fruits (Late Summer–Mid Winter)

| Species | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| **[[lycium-ferocissimum]], L. chinensis** | Thorny hedges with berries and seeds late summer to late winter; salt and wind tolerant; eagerly sought by poultry |
| **Coprosma spp.** (C. lucida, C. australis, C. parviflora, C. repens, C. kirkii, C. robusta) | Hardy NZ plants for coasts, swamps, understorey, shelter. Most dioecious (need ~5% male). Almost all grow from cuttings or quickset hedges. NZ Whole Earth Catalogue states poultry survive all year on 3 or so species. Stock like foliage (good manurial value). Trees prune well to hedges |
| **Mulberries** (Morus alba, M. nigra) | Important poultry food of high protein value |
| **Elderberries** (Sambucus spp.) | As for mulberry |
| **Emu Berry** (Owenia reticulata, O. acidula) | Noted food for emu and pigeons |
| **Palms** | Many species yield nuts and fruit for storage in sub-tropical and tropical areas |
| **Karreeboom** (Rhus lancea) | Hardy poultry berry bush |
| **Canthium latifolium** | Desert shrub with edible berries |
| **Berrigan** (Eremophila longifolia) | Edible berries |
| **Crataegus spp.** and **Serviceberries** | Range of fodders |
| **Chañal, Chile** (Gourliea decorticans) | Used as fodder |

## Vines for Fences and Trellis

| Species | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| **Dolichos spp.** | Annual and perennial beans; probably well-suited to pigeons. Range from temperate to tropical, evergreen perennial to annual |
| **Passiflora spp.** | Fruit of banana and black passionfruit eagerly eaten by poultry. Banana passionfruit frost-resistant; will "trellis" on most trees including eucalypt if planted at drip-line |
| **Kudzu Vine** (Thunbergia) | Free-seeding rambler recommended for poultry feed |
| **Chayote** (Choko) | Use on waste piles or low trellis |
| **Gabizura** (Actinostemma lobatum) | Scrambler with oily seeds |

## Roots

- **Nut-grass** (Eleocharis) and **Sour-grass** (Oxalis spp.): Yield shallow tubers utilized by birds (pest species in other areas)
- **Oca**: Special culture of Oxalis
- **[[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]]** (Helianthus): Pulled as needed, especially in lean periods; flourishes under oak forest or in poor soil

## Species for Broadcast Sowing in Stryards

### Grains
- Sunflower (Helianthus spp.): Heads stored in autumn
- Millets (Panicum spp.)
- Corn (Zea)
- Buckwheat (Fagopyrum)
- Wheat, rye, barley, oats, teff

### Pulses
- Chick Pea: Tolerant of fairly wide range of soils and climates
- Lentils
- Lab-Lab or small Dolichos beans as ground cover
- Field Peas: Great variety exists
- Centrosema: Useful in tropics
- Amaranthus grains of South America (~1,000 varieties cultivated)
- Eragrostis and Portulaca seeds: Used as human food in Australia
- Linseed: Judiciously used
- Chenopodium (many species): Loved by poultry

## Special Herb Species

| Species | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| **Shepherd's Purse** (Capsella bursa-pastoris) | Turner recommends as poultry forage; "stimulating effect on egg production"; nuisance control |
| **Cleavers** ([[galium-aparine]]) | "They love the seed and readily consume the whole plant when its iron and iodine content are very valuable, especially to yarded or deep-litter birds." For free-range, may need protection by brush or netted fence enclosures |
| **Plantain** (Plantago major, P. lanceolata) | Lawrence Hill recommends as crop that can absorb chicken manure waste and produce lots of green fodder; eagerly sought by ducks |
| **Chard** | First choice of all greens; free poultry access cannot be permitted. Successional sowings in throwover area ensures year-round leaves |

## Homestead Layout Principles

"It only remains for the homesteader to gather these warps of plants and wefts of animals into a logical weave."

- Plants arranged to hedge and shield buildings
- Permit observation
- Reduce losses from exposure to sun, wind, rain, and predators
- Salt marsh may suggest more geese
- Wetlands more ducks
- Deserts more quail or pigeon in the species mix
