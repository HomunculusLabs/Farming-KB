## Psilocybin Dosage and Pharmacology

Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) and its active metabolite psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) are the principal psychoactive compounds found in *[[stropharia-cubensis]]* and other psilocybin-containing mushrooms. Understanding their dosage, effects, toxicity profile, and [[lsd-discovery-aftermath-pharmacological-properties]] is essential for anyone working with these fungi — whether in research, clinical, or historical contexts.

## Chemical Structure

Both [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] belong to the **tryptamine** class of compounds and contain the characteristic **indole ring** structure found in most naturally occurring hallucinogens. The indole ring is a fused bicyclic system consisting of a benzene ring joined to a pyrrole ring. This structural motif is shared with:

- **[[lysergic-acid-diethylamide]] (LSD):** A semisynthetic lysergamide
- **N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT):** An endogenous tryptamine found in many plants and animals
- **Ibogaine:** An alkaloid from *[[tabernanthe-iboga]]*
- **Harmine and related β-carbolines:** Found in *[[banisteriopsis-caapi]]* (ayahuasca)

The notable exception among major natural hallucinogens is **mescaline** (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenylethylamine), which is a phenethylamine rather than an indole, placing it in the same chemical class as amphetamine.

### Biosynthetic Pathway

Psilocybin is produced biosynthetically within the living mycelium and fruiting body of the mushroom. The indole structure is derived from the amino acid **tryptophan**. The blue-staining reaction observed when psilocybin mushroom tissue is bruised is caused by enzymatic oxidation of indole substrates — likely tryptophan, 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin), or psilocybin itself — producing blue-colored oxidation products.

## Alkaloid Content

The dried mushrooms of *Stropharia cubensis* contain:

- **Psilocybin:** 0.2–0.4% by weight (Schultes et al., 1973)
- **Psilocin:** Present only in trace amounts in dried material
- Some strains have been reported to contain up to **0.5% psilocybin** (Wasson & Heim, 1959)

The mycelium itself also contains psilocybin, though at lower concentrations than the fruiting body. The amount in mycelium is influenced by the richness of the growth medium, suggesting that the fungus allocates more resources to alkaloid production when nutrients are abundant.

## Dosage

### Standard Doses

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **Full effective dose** | ~10–12 mg psilocybin |
| **Equivalent dried mushroom weight** | ~5 g |
| **Equivalent fresh mushroom weight** | ~50 g |
| **Threshold dose** | ~4 mg (approximately 2–3 dried mushrooms) |

These values are calibrated for a 160 lb (73 kg) adult.

### Fresh vs. Dried

Fresh mushrooms appear to be somewhat more potent per unit weight than dried ones. This may be due to:

- Partial degradation of psilocybin during the drying process
- More efficient extraction of active compounds from fresh tissue during digestion
- The approximately 90% water content of fresh mushrooms means the effective dose is distributed in a much larger mass

### Tolerance

Tolerance to psilocybin develops rapidly and easily:

- Taking psilocybin more frequently than **once per week** leads to significantly diminished effects
- Tolerance can be circumvented by increasing the dose or, preferably, by abstaining for a minimum of two weeks to allow physiological equilibrium to be restored
- Cross-tolerance with other serotonergic psychedelics (LSD, mescaline, DMT) is well documented

## Effects Profile

### Psychological Effects (Full Dose)

At a full 10–12 mg dose, the experience is characterized by:

- **Euphoria and calm lucidity** — no loss of coherence or clarity of thought
- **Visual hallucinations** — colorful, hard-edged, and highly articulated imagery with eyes closed, ranging from abstract geometric forms to visions of fantastic landscapes and architectural vistas
- **Auditory effects** — enhanced perception of sounds, occasional auditory hallucinations
- **Distortions of time and space perception** — time dilation, altered sense of scale and distance
- **Emotional detachment** — a sense of emotional distance from the immediate environment
- **Extreme hilarity** — uncontrollable laughter is commonly reported
- **Sensory enhancement** — when attention is directed outward in a natural setting, all senses appear heightened to an extraordinary degree

### Setting Dependence

The character of the experience varies significantly with setting:

- **Indoors at night in complete darkness** (the traditional Mazatec approach): Hallucinations are most intense, primarily internal/eyes-closed
- **Natural outdoor setting** with outward-directed attention: Senses become keyed to maximum receptivity — hearing, smelling, and seeing with a clarity rarely if ever experienced in ordinary consciousness

### Physical Effects

Psilocybin produces minimal physical effects at standard doses:

- Mild pupillary dilation
- Slight increase in heart rate and blood pressure
- Occasional nausea (more common with fresh mushrooms or tea preparations)
- No significant respiratory depression

## Toxicity

### LD₅₀

Psilocybin is one of the **least toxic** of all known hallucinogens:

| Compound | Minimum Effective Dose | Relative Toxicity |
|---|---|---|
| **Psilocybin** | ~10 mg | Baseline |
| **Mescaline** | ~200 mg | 2.5× more toxic than psilocybin |

The LD₅₀ (lethal dose for 50% of test subjects) of psilocybin in mice is **280 mg/kg** of body weight. For a 70 kg human, this would correspond to approximately 19,600 mg — nearly **2,000 times** the effective dose. This extraordinary safety margin means that fatal overdose from [[amanita-muscaria-legal-alternative-psilocybin-mushrooms-europe]] alone is virtually impossible; a person would need to consume their own body weight in dried mushrooms many times over.

## Pharmacokinetics

### Metabolism

- Psilocybin is a **prodrug** — it is pharmacologically inactive itself
- After ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly **dephosphorylated** by alkaline phosphatase enzymes in the liver and bloodstream, converting it to the active compound **psilocin**
- Psilocin is the molecule that actually binds to [[psilocybin-pharmacology-and-serotonin-receptors]] in the brain
- Psilocin is further metabolized by **monoamine oxidase (MAO)** and glucuronidation
- The onset of effects occurs within 20–60 minutes after ingestion
- Peak effects are reached at approximately 90–120 minutes
- The total experience duration is typically **4–7 hours**

### Mechanism of Action

Psilocin acts primarily as a **serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor agonist**. This receptor is densely concentrated in the cerebral cortex, particularly in layer V pyramidal neurons. Activation of 5-HT₂A receptors increases cortical excitability and disrupts the normal functioning of the **[[default-mode-network-and-ego-dissolution]] (DMN)** — a set of interconnected brain regions associated with self-referential thinking, introspection, and the sense of ego. The temporary dissolution of DMN coherence is believed to underlie the characteristic ego-dissolution, mystical-type experiences, and altered sense of self produced by psilocybin.

Although the 5-HT₂A receptor is the primary site of action, psilocin also binds (with lower affinity) to other serotonin receptor subtypes (5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂C, 5-HT₂B) and to dopamine receptors, which may contribute to its complex effects profile.

## Historical Chronology of Psilocybin Mushrooms

### Pre-Columbian Era

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 300–500 B.C. | "Mushroom stones" found in highland Guatemala, indicating a mushroom cult at least this old |
| ~300 A.D. | Frescoes in central Mexico depict mushroom designs, suggesting ritual use |

### Colonial Period

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1502 | Spanish conquistadores observed psilocybin mushrooms served at the coronation feast of Moctezuma |
| 1547–1569 | Fray Bernardino de Sahagún's *Historia de las Cosas de Nueva España* (Florentine Codex) describes *nanacatl* (teonanácatl = "flesh of the gods"), noting they "are harmful and intoxicate like wine" and that users "see visions, feel a faintness of heart and are provoked to lust" |
| 1651 | Dr. Francisco Hernández described three types of mushrooms worshipped by Mexican natives, reporting effects including "uncontrolled laughter," visions of wars and demons, and terrifying night-long vigils |

### Early Scientific Investigation

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1906 | *Stropharia cubensis* described by Earle in a Cuban agronomy journal |
| 1914 | A.F. Merrill (Yale) published a paper in *Science* describing hallucinogenic effects from *Panaeolus papillonaceus* in Maine — likely due to psilocybin/psilocin |
| 1915 | William E. Safford claimed teonanácatl never existed and was actually dried peyotl — this theory was accepted for three decades |
| 1919 | Dr. Blas P. Reko published that nanacatl was a [[emcdda-barriers-to-hallucinogenic-mushroom-trend-diffusion]], but earlier errors caused the report to be discounted |
