## Biodynamic Preparations

> Note: This page is compiled from general knowledge of [[biodynamic-agriculture-and-steiner]], not sourced from a specific file in the wiki's raw/ directory.

Biodynamic preparations are a set of nine specific fermented preparations used in biodynamic agriculture, developed by Rudolf Steiner in his 1924 agricultural lectures. They are intended to enliven the soil, support composting processes, and influence plant growth through subtle biological and energetic mechanisms. Biodynamic farming treats the farm as a self-sustaining organism.

## Field Sprays (BD 500 and BD 501)

### BD 500 — Horn Manure

The foundational biodynamic soil preparation.

**Preparation**:
1. Fill a cow horn with fresh cow manure (preferably from a lactating cow on biodynamic pasture)
2. Bury the horn in good topsoil about 40-60 cm deep during autumn (September/October in Northern Hemisphere)
3. Leave underground through winter — the horn protects and concentrates the manure as soil microbial activity transforms it
4. Dig up in spring (March/April) when the contents have become a dark, humus-like, sweet-smelling material

**Application**:
- Stir 60-100g of horn manure in ~15 litres of warm rainwater for one hour, creating a vortex in one direction, then reversing periodically
- Spray onto soil in late afternoon or evening in fine droplets
- Apply in spring at the start of the growing season, and in autumn after harvest
- Typical rate: 100-300g/ha per application

**Effects**: Stimulates soil biological activity, root growth, humus formation, and earthworm activity. Encourages the soil to function as a living system.

### BD 501 — Horn Silica

**Preparation**:
1. Pulverize clear quartz crystal into a fine powder
2. Mix with rainwater to form a paste
3. Pack into a cow horn
4. Bury the horn in good topsoil 40-60 cm deep during spring
5. Leave through summer — the sun's forces are meant to work through the silica
6. Dig up in autumn

**Application**:
- Stir 3-5g in ~15 litres of warm rainwater for one hour (same vortex stirring as BD 500)
- Spray as a fine mist onto plant foliage in early morning
- Apply during key growth stages: pre-flowering, during flowering, fruit development
- Typical rate: 3-5g/ha per application

**Effects**: Enhances light metabolism, photosynthesis, fruit quality, colour, flavour, and storage qualities. Balances the upward, light-seeking forces of the plant.

## Compost Preparations (BD 502-507)

These six preparations are added to compost piles to guide and enhance the decomposition process. Each uses a specific plant material prepared in a particular animal organ, buried in soil for a period, then extracted and added to the compost heap.

### BD 502 — Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)
- **Preparation**: Yarrow flowers stuffed into a deer bladder, buried in soil over winter
- **Function**: Works with sulfur and potassium processes; attracts cosmic forces; supports the compost's ability to make nutrients available

### BD 503 — Chamomile (Chamomilla matricaria)
- **Preparation**: Chamomile flowers packed into a cow intestine, buried in soil over winter
- **Function**: Works with calcium processes; stabilizes nitrogen in the compost; prevents nitrogen from being lost as gas; supports plant immunity

### BD 504 — Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica)
- **Preparation**: Whole nettle plants buried in soil, surrounded by peat, for one full year
- **Function**: Works with iron processes; distributes intelligence through the soil; supports [[humus-formation-and-soil-organic-matter]] [[stamets-compost-supplements-nitrogen-management]]; stimulates soil sensitivity

### BD 505 — Oak Bark (Quercus robur)
- **Preparation**: Oak bark packed into the skull of a domestic animal, buried in a watery/marshy place over winter
- **Function**: Works with calcium processes; provides protective forces against disease; combats excessive plant growth forces; brings balance and health

### BD 506 — Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale)
- **Preparation**: Dandelion flowers packed into a cow mesentery (peritoneal membrane), buried in soil over winter
- **Function**: Works with silica processes; mediates between silica and potassium; sensitizes the plant to its environment; supports relationship between plant and surrounding cosmos

### BD 507 — Valerian (Valeriana officinalis)
- **Preparation**: Valerian flower juice pressed and diluted, stored in a bottle protected from light
- **Function**: Works with phosphorus processes; provides warmth to the compost; stimulates phosphate activity; used as a liquid spray over the compost pile (not buried like the others)

## Additional Preparations

### BD 508 — Horsetail (Equisetum arvense) Tea

Not a buried preparation but a liquid extract:
1. Boil fresh or dried horsetail in water (ratio ~1:20 by weight) for 20-30 minutes
2. Cool, dilute to pale yellow, and spray on plants

**Uses**:
- Anti-fungal spray — particularly effective against mildew and rust diseases
- Strengthens plant cell walls through silica content
- Can be used as a foliar spray or soil drench
- Apply in early morning

## Application to Compost

The six compost preparations (BD 502-507) are inserted into the compost pile at specific points:
- BD 502-506: Bury a small amount (roughly a teaspoon-sized ball of each) at about 50 cm depth in the pile, spaced around the pile at roughly equal intervals
- BD 507: Dilute valerian extract and sprinkle as a liquid spray over the entire surface of the compost pile
- Apply when the compost pile is built, not during turning

## The Stirring Process (Dynamization)

Central to biodynamic preparation application is the process of stirring (dynamization):

1. Fill a suitable container (ideally clay, wood, or copper — avoid plastic and steel) with warm rainwater
2. Add the preparation material
3. Create a vigorous vortex by stirring in one direction for ~1 minute
4. Suddenly reverse direction, creating chaos as the vortex collapses
5. Stir in the new direction until a new vortex forms (~1 minute)
6. Continue alternating for exactly one hour
7. Apply immediately after stirring

The alternating vortex-creation and chaos is believed to create a dynamic, rhythmical pattern that imprints the water with the preparation's qualities.

## Biodynamic Calendar

Biodynamic practice also involves a planting calendar based on astronomical positions:
- **Root days**: Moon in Earth signs (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn) — good for root crops
- **Leaf days**: Moon in Water signs (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces) — good for leafy crops
- **Flower days**: Moon in Air signs (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius) — good for flowering crops
- **Fruit/seed days**: Moon in Fire signs (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius) — good for fruiting crops

## Criticism and Evidence

- Scientific evidence for biodynamic preparations is mixed. Some studies show improved soil biology, compost quality, and crop yields compared to untreated controls. Other studies find no significant difference between biodynamic and standard organic management.
- The mechanisms are not well understood by conventional science. The use of animal organs, cosmic forces, and homeopathic dilution levels place biodynamics outside mainstream agronomy.
- Practitioners consistently report improved soil structure, compost quality, and plant vitality. The emphasis on closed-system farming, biodiversity, and soil health aligns with many [[biochar-in-regenerative-agriculture]] principles.
