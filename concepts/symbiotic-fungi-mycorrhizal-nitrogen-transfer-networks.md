## Mycorrhizal Nitrogen Transfer Networks

Source: [[dighton-mycorrhizal-networks-carbon-transfer]]
examines the parallel role of [[nitrogen-transfer-through-common-mycorrhizal-networks]] CMNs is not restricted to mass flow and diffusion through
soil pathways. It can occur directly through mycorrhizal hyphae connecting plants.

Transfer patterns documented:

- **Unidirectional (one-way)**: N from N2-fixing to non-N2-fixing plants via CMN
  (most common; Newman 1988; He et al. 2003)
- **Bidirectional (two-way)**: N from non-N2-fixing to N2-fixing plants via CMN
  (Johansen and Jensen 1996; He et al. 2004, 2005; Moyer-Henry et al. 2006)

The N2-fixing plant is not always the nitrogen donor. Direction and magnitude of
transfer depend on source-sink relationships between connected plants. Limited data
show net N gains through CMNs may play beneficial roles in affecting plant fitness,
particularly in N-limited habitats.

## Quantification Methods

### 15N Enrichment (Labeling) Method

- External source of 15N-enriched compounds (up to 99.90 atom % 15N)
- Applied to plant tissues, growth media, or soils
- Follows N metabolism over short-term periods (days to weeks)
- Large enrichment over background makes measurement easy
- Can use 15NH4+ or 15NO3- as nitrogen source

### 15N Natural Abundance Method

- Analyzes naturally occurring 15N levels (delta-15N values, per mil)
- Reflects long-term periods (years to decades)
- Variations often small (around 10%) among AM or EM plant species
- Difficult to demonstrate plant-to-plant N transfer through CMNs alone
- May demonstrate magnitude of 15N fractionations during long-term N translocation

Nitrogen has two [[gadd-stable-isotopes-saprotrophic-fungi]]:

- 14N: 99.6337% of atmospheric N
- 15N: 0.3663% of atmospheric N
- Radioactive isotopes (12N, 13N, 16N, 17N) have short half-lives (0.01 s to 10 min),
  unsuitable for most plant studies
