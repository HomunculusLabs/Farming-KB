    10|# Mycorrhizal Nitrogen Transfer Networks
    11|
    12|Source: Symbiotic Fungi (Varma and Kharkwal, eds.), Chapters 4, 17
    13|
    14|## Introduction
    15|
    16|Nitrogen transfer between plants through [[common-mycorrhizal-networks]] (CMNs) is a
    17|process of fundamental importance in terrestrial ecosystems. Mycorrhizal hyphae
    18|interconnecting root systems of different plants facilitate direct nitrogen movement
    19|that bypasses soil pathways, with practical implications for agriculture, forestry,
    20|and ecological restoration. Related research on [[dighton-mycorrhizal-networks-carbon-transfer]]
    21|examines the parallel role of [[mycorrhizal-networks]] in moving carbon between plants.
    22|
    23|Nitrogen transfer is relevant to global concerns about:
    24|
    25|- Nitrogen excess in agricultural systems
    26|- Nitrogen limitation in natural ecosystems
    27|- Plant performance in N-limited habitats
    28|- Seedling establishment during forest succession
    29|- Species coexistence and community composition
    30|
    31|## Common Mycorrhizal Networks
    32|
    33|CMNs form when extraradical hyphae from [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] colonizing one plant extend
    34|and connect to roots of neighboring plants through anastomosis (hyphal fusion).
    35|
    36|Network characteristics:
    37|
    38|- Both AM and EM fungi form CMNs
    39|- AM extraradical networks spread at 738-1,067 mm per day
    40|- Hyphal extents: 10-40 mm per mm of root length
    41|- Indefinitely large numbers of fungal linkages among plants possible
    42|- Anastomosis between different plant species: 44-49% frequency
    43|- Same-species anastomosis: 62% frequency
    44|- No hyphal incompatibility between different plant species networks
    45|
    46|Anastomosis dynamics:
    47|
    48|- Hyphal tips fuse in approximately 35 minutes
    49|- Bidirectional particle flow at 1.8 micrometers per second through bridges
    50|- Particles include: vacuoles, mitochondria, nuclei, fat droplets
    51|- Complete disappearance of hyphal walls at fusion points
    52|- Protoplasmic continuity confirmed by SDH staining and DAPI nuclear detection
    53|
    54|## Mechanisms of Nitrogen Transfer
    55|
    56|Nitrogen transfer through CMNs is not restricted to mass flow and diffusion through
    57|soil pathways. It can occur directly through mycorrhizal hyphae connecting plants.
    58|
    59|Transfer patterns documented:
    60|
    61|- **Unidirectional (one-way)**: N from N2-fixing to non-N2-fixing plants via CMN
    62|  (most common; Newman 1988; He et al. 2003)
    63|- **Bidirectional (two-way)**: N from non-N2-fixing to N2-fixing plants via CMN
    64|  (Johansen and Jensen 1996; He et al. 2004, 2005; Moyer-Henry et al. 2006)
    65|
    66|The N2-fixing plant is not always the nitrogen donor. Direction and magnitude of
    67|transfer depend on source-sink relationships between connected plants. Limited data
    68|show net N gains through CMNs may play beneficial roles in affecting plant fitness,
    69|particularly in N-limited habitats.
    70|
    71|## Quantification Methods
    72|
    73|### 15N Enrichment (Labeling) Method
    74|
    75|- External source of 15N-enriched compounds (up to 99.90 atom % 15N)
    76|- Applied to plant tissues, growth media, or soils
    77|- Follows N metabolism over short-term periods (days to weeks)
    78|- Large enrichment over background makes measurement easy
    79|- Can use 15NH4+ or 15NO3- as nitrogen source
    80|
    81|### 15N Natural Abundance Method
    82|
    83|- Analyzes naturally occurring 15N levels (delta-15N values, per mil)
    84|- Reflects long-term periods (years to decades)
    85|- Variations often small (around 10%) among AM or EM plant species
    86|- Difficult to demonstrate plant-to-plant N transfer through CMNs alone
    87|- May demonstrate magnitude of 15N fractionations during long-term N translocation
    88|
    89|Nitrogen has two stable isotopes:
    90|
    91|- 14N: 99.6337% of atmospheric N
    92|- 15N: 0.3663% of atmospheric N
    93|- Radioactive isotopes (12N, 13N, 16N, 17N) have short half-lives (0.01 s to 10 min),
    94|  unsuitable for most plant studies
    95|
    96|## Experimental Design for Two-Way Transfer
    97|
    98|Four reciprocal pairings test two-way N transfer and distinguish soil vs. mycorrhizal
    99|pathways:
   100|
   101|| Pair type | N-donor | N-receiver | Mycorrhizal | Nodulated |
   102||-----------|---------|------------|-------------|-----------|
   103|| Control | N2-fixing or non-N2-fixing | Reciprocal | No | No |
   104|| Nodulated/non-M | N2-fixing or non-N2-fixing | Reciprocal | No | Yes |
   105|| Non-nodulated/M | N2-fixing or non-N2-fixing | Reciprocal | Yes | No |
   106|| Nodulated/M | N2-fixing or non-N2-fixing | Reciprocal | Yes | Yes |
   107|
   108|Physical separation:
   109|
   110|- Two perforated Perspex plates (2.5 mm thick) create a 5-mm air gap
   111|- 25-37 micrometer nylon mesh allows only hyphal penetration
   112|- High water-holding capacity crystals minimize water movement
   113|- External 15N added directly to N-donor side
   114|
   115|## Nitrogen Transfer Quantification
   116|
   117|Three metrics quantify nitrogen transfer:
   118|
   119|1. **%Ntransfer**: percentage of N transferred between donor and receiver
   120|2. **Ntransfer**: absolute amount of N transferred (mg per plant)
   121|3. **%NDFT**: percentage of receiver N derived from transfer
   122|
   123|Two-way (bidirectional) or net N transfer is calculated as the difference between
   124|N transfer from plant 1 to plant 2 and the reverse. Net transfer benefits the plant
   125|that receives more N than it donates.
   126|
   127|Limitations of the 15N enrichment method:
   128|
   129|- In fertile soils, transferred N may be much less than soil N taken up
   130|- Root depth and N uptake patterns may differ in mixed vs. single species
   131|- Based on separate reciprocal experiments (not concurrent)
   132|- Dual isotope approach would be more accurate but 13N (10 min half-life) is
   133|  unsuitable for long-term processes
   134|
   135|## Demonstrated Nitrogen Transfer
   136|
   137|### Ectomycorrhizal Systems
   138|
   139|Reciprocal N transfer demonstrated between:
   140|
   141|- Non-N2-fixing Eucalyptus maculata and N2-fixing Casuarina cunninghamiana
   142|- Linked by ectomycorrhizal fungus Pisolithus sp.
   143|- Supplied with 15NH4+ or 15NO3-
   144|- Net N transfer from Eucalyptus to Casuarina (N2-fixing plant as net receiver)
   145|- Demonstrated with both ammonium and nitrate nitrogen sources
