## Xylotropic Endophytes

Quiescent colonists of sapwood, termed xylotropic endophytes, have been demonstrated deep within sound 55- to 60-year-old Picea abies stems. These fungi colonize living wood tissue asymptomatically, remaining dormant until the tree dies or wood physiology changes, at which point they may initiate active decomposition.

This bridge between endophytic and saprotrophic lifestyles represents an important but poorly understood component of wood-associated fungal diversity.

## Wood Wasp and Fungus Interactions

Several genera of resupinate wood-decaying basidiomycetes have specific interactions with wood wasps of the family Siricidae. The wasps require the fungi for nutrition, carrying oidia in mycangia (hypopleural organs) and transmitting them to new hosts.

Amylosterium areolatum is associated with Sirex noctilio, which invades living trees and has become a serious pathogen of Pinus radiata in New Zealand and Australia. The Center for Forest Mycology maintains large culture collections of these fungi.
