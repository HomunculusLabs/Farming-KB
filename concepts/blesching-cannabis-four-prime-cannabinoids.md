# Cannabis Pharmacology: Four Prime Cannabinoids

Understanding cannabis pharmacology requires familiarity with the four prime
cannabinoids that form the foundation of therapeutic cannabis science. Uwe
Blesching's [[cannabis-health-index]] provides a detailed examination of these
molecules and their interactions with the endocannabinoid system.

## The Endocannabinoid System: Foundation

All mammals possess an endocannabinoid system (ECS), a biological regulatory
mechanism that operates like a lock-and-key system. The ECS is involved in
many important functions including physiological and psychological adaptation
to changing environments, from the moment of fertilization (where ECS
signaling determines egg implantation) throughout life.

The ECS produces nurturing responses to injuries and inflammations, provides
protective mechanisms against cancers and neurological diseases, mitigates
nerve damage, and may reduce changes associated with aging. It balances and
strengthens the nervous and immune systems, initiates pain control, calms
inflammation, and promotes neurogenesis.

## Cannabinoid Receptors

Two primary cannabinoid receptor types have been identified:

### CB1 Receptors

Concentrated primarily in the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord),
CB1 receptors mediate the psychoactive effects of cannabinoids and play key
roles in mood regulation, memory, pain perception, and motor function.

### CB2 Receptors

Found primarily in the immune system and peripheral tissues, CB2 receptors
modulate immune function, inflammation, and pain. Activation of CB2 does not
produce psychoactive effects.

Scientists suspect there are three additional endocannabinoid receptors
(referred to as non-CB1 and non-CB2) whose functions will be more fully
understood with further research.

## The Four Prime Cannabinoids

### 1. Anandamide (AEA) -- Produced by the Body

Anandamide, the "Bliss Molecule," was discovered in the 1990s. This
endogenous cannabinoid fits relatively equally into both CB1 and CB2
receptors. Once the connection is made, numerous physiological, mental, and
emotional changes take place simultaneously in the CNS (via CB1), the immune
system (via CB2), and the autonomic nervous system.

Anandamide enhances pleasure, may mitigate acute schizophrenia episodes,
destroys numerous cancer types, and soothes coughs. It may also reduce
hypertension, depression, fear, and anxiety while contributing to social play
behavior. Exercise, especially high-intensity endurance running, significantly
increases anandamide levels (contributing to "runner's high").

Five endocannabinoids produced by the human body have been identified:
anandamide (AEA), 2-arachidonoyl glycerol (2-AG), Noladin ether,
Virodhamine, and N-arachidonoyl-dopamine (NADA).

### 2. Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) -- Sourced in Cannabis

THC is the primary mind-altering constituent of marijuana, discovered in 1964
by Israeli scientists Raphael Mechoulam and Yechiel Gaoni. It binds relatively
equally to both CB1 and CB2 receptors, initiating simultaneous changes in the
CNS, immune system, and autonomic nervous system.

Key properties of THC include:
- Heart-protective effects (reduces heart attack size, protects against
  hypoxia damage)
- Neuroprotective via CB1
- Bronchodilation (enlargement of airways)
- Weight gain and increased walking distance in COPD patients
- Antiemetic and appetite-stimulating effects

Synthetic THC drugs include Sativex, [[dronabinol]], Marinol, and [[nabilone]].
Plant-based THC content varies by strain, freshness, cultivation method, and
age. Fresh cannabis contains non-psychoactive THC-carboxylic-acid, which
becomes psychoactive when decarboxylated through heat.

### 3. Cannabidiol (CBD) -- Sourced in Cannabis

CBD is a non-psychoactive cannabinoid with greater affinity for CB2 than CB1.
Much of its therapeutic influence stems from suppressing the enzyme (fatty
acid amide hydrolase or FAAH) that breaks down anandamide, keeping the "Bliss
Molecule" active at higher concentrations for longer duration.

Key therapeutic properties of CBD include:
- Treatment of pediatric epileptic seizures resistant to pharmaceuticals
- Neuroprotection against alcohol-induced nerve damage
- Antipsychotic, anxiolytic, and antidepressant effects
- Reduction of acute paranoid schizophrenia symptoms (comparable to
  Amisulpride without significant side effects)
- Anti-inflammatory effects in IBD, arthritis, periodontitis, and
  atherosclerosis
- Selective apoptosis (cancer cell death) without harming normal cells
- Calming autonomic stress responses by engaging serotonin receptors

### 4. (E)-Beta-Caryophyllene ((E)-BCP) -- Sourced in Spice Plants

(E)-BCP is a functional non-psychoactive CB2 agonist found in many common
spices. Considered by some researchers to be a food-based cannabinoid, it is
FDA-approved and readily available through dietary sources. (E)-BCP activates
CB2 receptors, providing anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects without
any psychoactive activity.

Spices containing (E)-BCP include black pepper (7-19%), Ashanti pepper
(52-58%), Indian bay-leaf (25%), basil (14%), cinnamon, rosemary, caraway,
Mexican oregano, and clove.

## Key Takeaways

- The four prime cannabinoids -- anandamide, THC, CBD, and (E)-BCP --
  represent the foundational molecules of cannabinoid pharmacology.
- THC and anandamide bind equally to CB1 and CB2; CBD favors CB2; (E)-BCP
  selectively activates CB2 without psychoactive effects.
- THC is the most studied cannabinoid with applications ranging from
  antiemetic to neuroprotective to cardioprotective.
- CBD's ability to inhibit FAAH (preserving anandamide) underlies many of
  its therapeutic effects.
- (E)-BCP provides accessible, non-psychoactive CB2 activation through
  common dietary spices.
- More than 111 cannabis-based cannabinoids have been isolated, with
  terpenoids increasingly recognized as important therapeutic co-factors.
