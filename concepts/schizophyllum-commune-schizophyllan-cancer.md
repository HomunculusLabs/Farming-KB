## Schizophyllum Commune and Schizophyllan Cancer Research

Schizophyllum commune is a small, whitish bracket fungus with [[anemotropism-in-basidiomycete-mushrooms]] globally. Despite being edible, it is primarily valued for its pharmaceutical polysaccharide production. The mushroom has been known in traditional medicine and is now cultivated commercially in Japan for Schizophyllan extraction.

## Schizophyllan Chemistry

Schizophyllan is a beta(1,3)-D glucan with beta-(1,6)-D glucan side-chains. Like all glucan preparations, molecules are never homologous in molecular weight but consist of molecules with a wide MW range. The molecules are large and require intramuscular or intraperitoneal administration in clinical settings.

Key active constituents: beta-glucans with anti-tumour and immunomodulation properties.

## Pre-Clinical Evidence

Schizophyllan has demonstrated cytostatic activity in Sarcoma 180 tumour xenografts. The survival of Sarcoma 180 xenografts was not affected by pre-treatment alone, while combined pre- and post-treatment and post-treatment alone resulted in increased survival.

However, Schizophyllan had no effect on survival of:
- Sarcoma 37
- Ehrlich carcinoma
- Yoshida sarcoma ascites tumours

This selectivity is typical of mushroom polysaccharides, which generally act through host-mediated immune mechanisms rather than direct cytotoxicity.

## Clinical Trials

### Gastric Cancer
Early clinical studies combining Schizophyllan with conventional chemotherapy (tegafur or mitomycin C plus 5-fluorouracil) in a randomised controlled study of 367 patients with recurrent and inoperable gastric cancer found a significant increase in median survival. However, a similar study was unable to confirm this success.

### Head and Neck Cancers
Schizophyllan has been shown to increase overall survival of patients with head and neck cancers.

### Cervical Cancer
In a randomised controlled study combining Schizophyllan with radiotherapy:
- Stage II cervical [[cancer-immune-modulation-mechanisms]]**: Activation of natural killer (NK) cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells through beta-glucan [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] responsible for immune activation
- Clinical evidence supports adjunctive use alongside conventional cancer treatments
