### Brittle Environments

Plant material breaks down slowly through oxidation and weathering.
Conditions do not favor abundant decomposer organisms. Dead material
accumulates above ground for years, creating a mineral cycle bottleneck.
This accumulation suppresses plant growth and can kill perennial
grasses. Large animal activity speeds breakdown and cycling without
exposing soil, unlike fire.

## The Leaching Problem

Water that carries nutrients underground can carry them beyond the root
zone. Organic matter is the primary factor impeding leaching:

- Less organic material means greater leaching tendency.
- Chemical herbicides, pesticides, and fertilizers destroy organic
  matter and soil organisms, increasing leaching.
- Farmers adding soluble nitrogen must keep applying more because the
  damaged cycle cannot retain nutrients.
- Leached minerals become pollutants as groundwater carries them to
  unintended destinations.

## The Chain Reaction of Organic Matter Loss

Loss of soil organic matter triggers a cascading chain reaction:

1. Organic matter decreases, reducing water-retaining capability.
2. Soil structure deteriorates, reducing aeration.
3. As aeration decreases, biological activity declines.
4. As life decreases, organic material production drops further.
5. Fewer plants produce less cover and more bare, capped soil.
6. [[savory-ecosystem-foundation-blocks]], is the engine of desertification in brittle
environments.
