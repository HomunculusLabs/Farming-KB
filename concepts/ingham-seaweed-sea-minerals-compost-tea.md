# Seaweed and Sea Minerals in Compost Tea Brewing

## Seaweed as a Food Source

Seaweed and kelp products are commonly added to compost tea as food
sources for the microorganisms. They provide a wide range of
micronutrients, trace minerals, growth stimulants, and complex
carbohydrates. Different species of seaweed contain different
profiles of these compounds, so the source and processing method
matter significantly for the quality of the tea produced.

## Kelp Meal vs. Liquid Kelp

Kelp meal is dried, ground seaweed that provides a slow-release food
source. Liquid kelp extracts are more immediately available to
microorganisms in the tea. The processing method used to create
liquid kelp matters: cold-processed or enzymatically-digested kelp
retains more of the beneficial compounds compared to heat-processed
or chemically-extracted products. Heat can destroy many of the
natural [[singh-fungal-plant-growth-regulators-production]] and enzymes present in raw kelp.

## Sea Water Considerations

Sea water contains the full spectrum of elements found in the ocean,
in proportions that reflect geological and biological processes over
millions of years. However, the sodium chloride concentration in sea
water is extremely high and can cause osmotic stress to freshwater
organisms in compost tea. Diluted sea water may provide trace
minerals, but the salt content must be carefully managed.

## Trace Minerals from Seaweed

One of the primary benefits of seaweed in compost tea is the
delivery of trace minerals that may be lacking in terrestrial plant
materials. Seaweed accumulates minerals from seawater in
bioavailable forms that are often more readily utilized by
microorganisms and plants than mineral supplements from rock
sources. These include iodine, selenium, zinc, copper, manganese,
and many others.

## Natural Growth Regulators

Seaweed contains naturally occurring cytokinins, auxins, gibberellins,
and other plant growth regulators. These compounds can influence
plant growth and development when tea containing seaweed is applied
as a foliar spray or soil drench. The [[edaphic-factors-microbial-community-structure]] in the tea
may also process and transform these compounds, potentially making
them more available or modifying their activity.

## Cytokinin Content

Cytokinins in seaweed are associated with cell division, delayed
senescence, and improved [[fungal-lichen-stress-tolerance-and-poikilohydry]] in plants. When seaweed is
included in compost tea and applied to plant foliage, these
compounds may contribute to the observed improvements in plant
vigor, leaf color, and disease resistance that go beyond what the
microbial community alone would provide.

## Complementing Molasses

Seaweed products work well in combination with molasses in compost
tea recipes. While molasses provides the simple sugars that fuel
rapid bacterial growth, seaweed provides the complex carbohydrates,
minerals, and growth factors that support a more diverse community
