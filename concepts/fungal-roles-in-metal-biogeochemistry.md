# Fungal Roles in Metal Biogeochemistry

## Overview

Fungi are major agents in metal cycling, transforming, mobilizing, and immobilizing metals and metalloids in the environment. Their activities span from rock weathering and soil formation to ore deposit genesis and environmental remediation. Based on Gadd et al.'s *Fungi in the Environment* and Singh's *Mycoremediation*.

## Mechanisms of Metal-Fungal Interactions

### 1. Biosorption
Metal ions bind to fungal cell wall components without metabolic energy expenditure:
- **Chitin**: The amino groups of chitin (N-acetylglucosamine polymer) bind metal cations
- **Glucans**: Beta-glucans in cell walls provide hydroxyl groups for metal coordination
- **Melanin**: Pigmented cell walls of dematiaceous fungi have high metal-binding capacity
- **Proteins**: Cell wall proteins provide carboxyl, amino, and thiol groups

Biosorption is rapid, reversible, and affected by pH, temperature, and competing ions. Dead fungal biomass retains biosorption capacity, enabling use in passive treatment systems.

### 2. Bioaccumulation (Active Uptake)
Fungi actively transport metal ions across cell membranes using:
- **Ion channels**: Passive diffusion through membrane channels
- **Transporters**: Specific and non-specific metal transporters
- **Endocytosis**: Engulfment of metal-bearing particles

Intracellular metals may be sequestered by:
- Metallothioneins (cysteine-rich metal-binding proteins)
- Phytochelatins (glutathione-derived peptides)
- Vacuolar compartmentalization
- Binding to polyphosphate granules

### 3. Extracellular Precipitation
Fungi induce metal precipitation outside cells through:
- **Oxalate production**: Many fungi secrete oxalic acid, forming metal oxalates (e.g., copper oxalate, zinc oxalate). These biominerals can be crystalline or amorphous.
- **Sulfide production**: Sulfate-reducing conditions created by fungal metabolism precipitate metals as insoluble sulfides
- **Phosphate precipitation**: Fungal phosphatase activity releases phosphate, precipitating metal phosphates
- **Carbonate precipitation**: CO2 from respiration can drive metal carbonate formation

### 4. Metal Speciation Transformation
Fungi alter the chemical form of metals:
- **Redox transformations**: Oxidation or reduction of metals (e.g., Mn(II) to Mn(IV), Cr(VI) to Cr(III))
- **Methylation**: Conversion of inorganic metals to organometallic forms (e.g., mercury methylation/demethylation)
- **Chelation**: Production of organic acids and siderophores that solubilize metals

### 5. Biomineralization
Fungi directly and indirectly form mineral deposits:
- **Direct**: Intracellular or cell-surface mineral formation
- **Indirect**: Chemical changes in the local environment drive mineral precipitation
- **Fossil record**: Fungal biominerals contribute to the geological record; some ore deposits show evidence of fungal involvement

## Rock Weathering and Soil Formation

Fungi are significant agents of rock weathering:
- **Boring**: Hyphal penetration into rocks creates physical fractures
- **Chemical dissolution**: Organic acids (oxalic, citric, gluconic) dissolve rock minerals
- **Lichen associations**: Lichen fungi are among the earliest colonizers of bare rock surfaces
- **Soil aggregate formation**: Fungal hyphae and secretions bind soil particles into stable aggregates

## Ore Formation

Evidence suggests fungi participate in the formation of certain ore deposits:
- **Iron and manganese oxides**: Fungal oxidation of Fe(II) and Mn(II) contributes to oxide deposit formation
- **Carbonate-hosted ores**: Fungal biomineralization may contribute to supergene enrichment zones
- **Uranium**: Fungi can precipitate uranium phosphates and influence uranium mobility

## Environmental Applications

### Bioremediation
- **Constructed fungal biofilters**: Passive treatment systems using fungal biomass to capture metals from water
- **Mycofiltration**: [[mycofiltration-water-treatment]] for metal removal
- **Soil remediation**: Inoculation of metal-contaminated soils with fungi to immobilize or transform metals

### Biomining
- **Bioleaching**: Fungal production of organic acids solubilizes metals from low-grade ores
- **Biooxidation**: Fungal oxidation of sulfide minerals enhances metal recovery

### Biosensing
- Metal accumulation patterns in fungal fruiting bodies reflect environmental contamination levels
- Certain fungi (e.g., Amanita) are hyperaccumulators of specific metals, useful as biomonitors
