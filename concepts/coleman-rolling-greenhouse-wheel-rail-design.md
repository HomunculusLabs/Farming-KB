## Overview

The [[rolling-greenhouse]] on wheels is Coleman's most evolved mobile
[[greenhouse-design]]. Unlike earlier sled-based designs that required a
tractor or wrecker truck for movement, this system can be repositioned
by two strong people without any machinery. The house rolls along
ground-laid pipe rails on gate-style wheels, making it practical for
growers to move greenhouses monthly across multiple sites.

The rolling design eliminates the high friction of sled-based systems
and provides directional stability during movement. It represents the
culmination of Coleman's experimentation with [[cold-frame-construction]]:**
- Hoops with crossbars and vertical supports (lighter than the truss
  design of earlier houses)
- Diagonal braces at each corner for rigidity
- Horizontal cross bracing in the upper section of both end walls to
  prevent racking during movement
- No crossbar across end walls (not needed since rails provide
  directional stability)

The absence of the traditional end-wall crossbar was a key design
breakthrough. Sled-based houses require this crossbar to prevent skids
from splaying during movement. Since pipe rails constrain the rolling
house laterally, the crossbar can be omitted, allowing a full-width
door opening at both ends.

## Wheel and Rail System

**Wheels:** Metal wheels with curved flanges are bolted to the bottom
of each greenhouse hoop leg. These are identical to the wheels used on
gates that roll on horizontal-pipe crossbars of chain-link fences. The
curved flange keeps the wheel centered on the rail during movement.

**Rails:** Two-inch-diameter round pipe laid on the ground under both
sides of the greenhouse. Twenty-foot pipe sections are connected using
shorter lengths of smaller-diameter pipe that fit inside as couplers.

**Rail length strategy:** For a house that moves to four sites covering
a 200-foot area, 200 feet of pipe rail per side is recommended. This
eliminates the need to pick up and relocate rails between moves. The
initial savings of buying less rail are outweighed by the "hassle
factor" of relocating pipes during frequent moves.

## Ground Anchoring

Since the rolling house is moved approximately once per month, it uses
a quick-release anchoring system rather than the permanent screw-in
anchors of sled-based houses:

- Metal fence T-posts driven into the ground at each corner at a slight
  angle for maximum holding power
- Each post bolted to a bracket extending from the house corner
- Posts extracted quickly with a T-post puller before each move

While fence-post anchors are not as secure as screw-in ground anchors
rated at 4,000 pounds, they are far more practical for monthly
relocation and have been adequate for the conditions experienced.

## Plastic Covering and Bottom Seal

The house uses double wiggle-wire channel (also called wire-lock,
spring-lock, or poly-lock) along the bottom sides for attaching the
plastic cover. Because the house sits on wheels, there is a gap between
the bottom of the sides and the ground that must be sealed in cold
weather.

**Sealing method:** A 2-foot flange of plastic is left below the
wiggle-wire channel on both sides during skinning. When a weather-tight
seal is needed, sandbags filled with 15 pounds of road gravel hold down
the flange. The gravel is eventually used for road repairs when bags
deteriorate. Soil dug from the field is an alternative fill material.
