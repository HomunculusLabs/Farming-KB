# Soil Pollution and Rehabilitation

## Overview

Soil pollution from agricultural chemicals, industrial waste, and urban contamination represents one of the most serious and persistent environmental challenges of the modern era. Many contaminated lands will require decades or even centuries before they can safely produce food again. Understanding the sources, behaviour, and potential remedies for soil pollution is essential for [[water-conservation]]

### Town Water Supplies

Several substances may be added to town water supplies, including chlorine, fluorine, alum (aluminium sulphate), and other metabolic poisons. Rainwater harvesting is indicated for the long-term future.

## Soil and Health

### The Soil-Plant-Animal Health Connection

Soils harbour and transfer both diseases and antibiotics. Plants take up many modern antibiotics (penicillin, sulfa drugs) from soil, and we may ingest them at concentrated levels. Animals retain residual antibiotics, producing new and resistant strains of disease organisms, and contain residual hormones and biocides.

Most environmental pollutants are concentrated by both plants and animals, though the degree varies between species. Natural levels of soil antibiotics may sustain us, and natural resistance to disease is transmitted to us via food.

### Local vs. Global Food Systems

When people lived as inhabitants of regions, they adapted to local soils, plants, and nutritional levels or died out. Today, global food reaches global markets, risking the global spread of "agricologenic" (farm-caused) diseases.

The home water tank and the house garden represent a limited and localised risk under our control, of little risk to society generally. Public water supplies and commercial foods are a different matter.

### Soil Rehabilitation Timeline

Most gardeners and farmers who have developed sustainable soil systems allow:
- **3-4 years** for building a garden from reasonable starting conditions
- **5-15 years** to restore a devastated soil landscape (physical restoration only)
- Far more lengthy processes where past chemical pollution has occurred

## Human Nutrition and Soil Health

### Four Broad Principles

1. A normally mixed diet (omnivorous) has been exhibited by most healthy human groups. Excessive dietary simplicity or reliance on too few foods has its dangers.

2. In developed countries, refined and processed foods, excess animal fats, and food additives have resulted in malnutrition and degenerative diseases. There is a general move toward lean meats, fish, and clean vegetables, fruits, and nuts.

3. In areas subject to famine, semi-starvation, or very low levels of critical vitamins (C, A, B-complex) or minerals (iron, zinc), great care is needed with human and soil health. Traditional diets, long maintained, are a guide to local food tolerances.

4. There is a complex and constant interaction between food, soil, trace elements, pH, biocides, and fertilisers. Heavy use of macronutrient fertilisers can cause deficiency of micronutrients.

### Individual Variability

We are individually different in our ability to metabolise and tolerate foods. Specific food allergies are very real and may arise from little exposure to certain food groups in our racial history. Body weight itself is a basic variable—dosages of any substance vary with body build, fat composition, and dosage per kilogram weight.

## Preventive Principles

The sane procedure in health is to:
- Maintain basic hygiene
- Grow and eat healthy plants and animals
- Avoid biocides and pollutants
- Take easy exercise
- Drink clean water or beverages
- Stay as cheerful as this world permits
