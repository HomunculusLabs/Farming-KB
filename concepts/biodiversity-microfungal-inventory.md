# Microfungal Inventory Methods

Microfungi are defined as fungi with microscopic spore-producing
structures. They comprise an unnatural group of organisms that cuts
across the classification schemes for Fungi and for fungal-like
organisms in the Protista, Chromista, and Myxomycota. The single
criterion for inclusion is the small size of the spore-producing
structures, in contrast to the large macroscopic [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]]
of the macrofungi.

## Major Groups Included

The microfungi encompass a broad taxonomic range:

- **Ascomycota**: The largest division of fungi including
  approximately 3,250 genera containing 32,250 recognized
  species. Most ascomycetes are regarded as microfungi.

- **Anamorphic Fungi**: Around 2,600 genera and 15,000 species,
  characterized by mitotic rather than meiotic production of
  spores (conidia). Most have teleomorphs in the Ascomycota.

- **Microscopic Basidiomycota**: The basidiomycetes comprise about
  1,400 genera and 22,250 species. Not all are macrofungi; some
  are only microscopically visible, such as the Tulasnellales.

- **Zygomycota**: Species in the order Mucorales coexist with
  other plant-inhabiting microfungi. About 56 genera and 300
  species are currently known from the order.

## Trophic Strategies

Three nutritional strategies, not mutually exclusive, have been
recognized for microfungi colonizing plant debris:

**Saprotrophy** is the most frequent nutritional habit, in which
a fungus uses nonliving organic material from a plant that it did
not kill. **Necrotrophy** involves killing living plant tissues
and then using them for nourishment. **Biotrophy**, in which only
living host cells are exploited, is much less common among
microfungi colonizing plant debris and largely is excluded by
definition.
