## Directing Water: Stemflow and Aerial Reservoirs

The trees often provide special receptors, conduits, and storages for
water. The random fall of rain is converted into well-directed
patterns of flow that serve the needs and growth of the forest. In
the stem bases of palms, plantains, and many epiphytes, or the
flanged roots of Terminalia trees and figs, water is held as aerial
ponds, often rich in algae and mosquitoes.

Stem mosses and epiphytes absorb many times their bulk of water, and
the tree itself directs water via insloping branches and fissured bark
to its tap roots, with spiders catching their share on webs, and
fungi soaking up what they need. Some trees trail weeping branches to
direct throughfall to their fibrous peripheral roots.
