## Historical Roots

The first recognized proponent of Appropriate Technology was Sri
Aurobindu, who called it "swadeshi," meaning self-reliance. His
teachings greatly influenced Mahatma Gandhi, who advocated smaller
technological systems appropriate to Indian villagers, such as looms
for clothes and solar distilleries for salt. By using their own skills
and local ecological resources, citizens broke systems of oppression.

AT as we envision it today was popularized in the 1970s by EF
Schumacher, author of "Small is Beautiful" and creator of "Buddhist
Economics." Schumacher proposed shifting toward local AT, which he
called [[graywater-systems]], Eco-Machines, sand filters
