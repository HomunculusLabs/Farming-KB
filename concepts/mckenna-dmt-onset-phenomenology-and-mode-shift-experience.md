## Overview

Terence McKenna's 1983 Esalen lecture provides one of the most detailed
first-person accounts of DMT's onset phenomenology—the sequence of
subjective events in the first 30-60 seconds after inhalation. His
description of the "mode shift" from ordinary waking consciousness to
the DMT state has become a foundational reference in psychedelic
literature. The onset is characterized by a distinctive auditory
phenomenon (described as "ripping cellophane"), an ascending tone,
geometric visual displays, and a rapid transition into what McKenna
calls the "merry go raum"—an insulated, domed space that feels
simultaneously underground and otherworldly.

## Pharmacokinetic Context

McKenna notes that DMT's onset profile is pharmacologically significant.
Onset begins approximately 15 seconds after smoking, with the
experience falling immediately into a trance state. The brain is
"absolutely at home with this compound"—the rapid onset (45 seconds)
and brief duration (5 minutes) suggest DMT interacts with endogenous
systems. DMT is present in small amounts in the human brain, and its
structural relatives include psilocybin (4-phosphoraloxy-N,N-dimethyl-
tryptamine) and serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine). This contrasts with
LSD, which is "retained in the body for some time" and produces a
qualitatively different experience.

## The Auditory Onset: Ripping Cellophane

The first distinctive marker of the DMT onset is auditory: a sound
"like ripping cellophane, like someone crumpling up plastic film and
throwing it away." A friend suggested this represents "our radio
entelechy ripping out of the [[weil-the-natural-mind-altered-states-normalcy]]. The voyager remains a fully
functional observer capable of judgment even while confronting realities
that challenge every assumption.

## The Heraclitus Connection

McKenna connects the experience to the twenty-fourth fragment of
Heraclitus: "The Aeon is a child at play with colored balls." In the
DMT state, one does not merely contemplate this fragment—one becomes
the Aeon at play. This is not metaphor but experiential identity: the
voyager participates directly in the play of cosmic forces. The feeling
of childhood is recurrent in DMT phenomenology—not regression but a
recovery of pre-conceptual wonder characterizing genuine encounter.
