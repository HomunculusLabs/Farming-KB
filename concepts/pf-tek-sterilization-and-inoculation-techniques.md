## PF TEK Inoculation, Incubation, and Fruiting Techniques

## Overview

The PF TEK encompasses a complete lifecycle from [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]], and harvesting mushrooms at optimal
potency.

## Inoculation Procedure

The jar must be completely cool to the touch before inoculation — warm jars
kill spores. Ensure the lid is tight. Shake the spore syringe vigorously to
redistribute spores evenly; this shaking is critical for uniform colonization.

Remove masking tape from the lid holes, remove the needle guard, and insert
the needle through a hole. Tilt the syringe body back toward the lid center
with the needle tip touching the inside glass. This angle distributes spore
solution down the jar side, inoculating the substrate along its full depth.
Inoculate a few drops per hole across all 4 holes. Watch the needle tip —
release plunger pressure as soon as water appears around it. Shake the syringe
between holes to maintain [[pf-tek-substrate-moisture-diagnosis-and-correction]]: tight
lids preserve moisture in dry substrate but may cause water pooling in wet
substrate — adjust based on observed conditions.

## Colonization Timeline

At 70°F, the complete process takes approximately 4-6 weeks:

1. **Inoculation to germination**: Within 1 week
2. **Germination to full colonization**: 2-3 weeks
3. **Surface colonization to [[fruiting-initiation]]**: Up to 2 weeks
4. **Fruiting cycle**: About 2 weeks

After full colonization, jars remain in indirect light. Tiny white pin-like
structures appear ("pinning"), followed by small round growths turning yellow,
then primordia — worm-like structures with reddish heads, the first true
mushrooms. The optimal time to remove the cake from the jar is when primordia
are visible.

## Birthing the Cake

Remove the lid and scrape away most dry top vermiculite with a clean fork.
Place an old jar lid over the mouth and invert the jar onto a magazine-cushioned
table. Lightly slam down — the cake slides onto the lid ("birthday"). Handle
carefully to avoid bruising (blue marks). Daub with tissue to absorb water
droplets, then place immediately in the fruiting terrarium.

## Dual Chambered Terrarium

The fruiting chamber is a 10-gallon aquarium with an airtight clear plastic lid
and a clear acrylic spray shield dividing it into primary and secondary chambers.
The shield breaks spray bottle droplets into fine molecular mist before reaching
the fungus cakes in the secondary chamber. Direct spraying of cakes must be
avoided — water droplets can drown or contaminate fine mycelial structures.
