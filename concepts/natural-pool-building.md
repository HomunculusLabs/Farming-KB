## Overview

[[mollison-designers-natural-swimming-pools-and-water-purification]] (also called swimming ponds or bio-pools) use biological filtration instead of chlorine or other chemicals to maintain clean, clear water. They combine a swimming zone with one or more planted filtration zones where aquatic plants and [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]] purify the water. The result is a pool that looks and functions like a natural pond while being safe for swimming.

## How Natural Pools Work

A natural pool operates as a closed-loop ecosystem:

1. **Swimming zone** — The open water area where people swim. Typically 6-8 feet deep at the center, with sloped sides.
2. **Filtration zone** — A shallower area (12-24 inches deep) filled with gravel and aquatic plants. Water circulates between zones.
3. **Circulation** — A small pump moves water from the swimming zone through the filtration zone and back. In some designs, aeration devices supplement flow.
4. **Biological filtration** — Plant roots absorb nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) from the water. Biofilm on gravel surfaces hosts bacteria that break down organic matter. Zooplankton consume algae cells.

The key principle: algae growth is limited by nutrient competition. Plants in the filtration zone take up the nutrients that would otherwise feed algae in the swimming zone.

## Pool Design Types

### Regenerative Design (Single Pool)

One continuous basin divided into zones:

- Swimming zone occupies roughly 50-60% of the surface area
- Regeneration zone (planted shelf) occupies 40-50%
- Separated by an underwater wall or barrier that allows water flow but prevents plants from entering the swimming area
- Simplest design, lowest construction cost

### Two-Basin Design

Separate swimming pool and regeneration pond connected by water flow:

- More flexibility in placement and sizing
- Regeneration pond can be larger relative to the swimming pool
- Can be designed to look like a natural pond separate from the pool
- Higher construction cost due to additional excavation and liner

### Hybrid Design

Combines biological filtration with a small mechanical component (UV sterilizer or bubble aerator):

- Useful in hot climates where algae pressure is extreme
- Allows a smaller regeneration zone
- Still chemical-free but uses electricity for the mechanical component

## Sizing Guidelines

The filtration zone must be proportional to the swimming zone:

- **[[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]]:** Regeneration zone = 50-70% of total surface area
- **Warm/hot climates:** Regeneration zone = 60-80% of total surface area
- **Shaded sites:** Can reduce regeneration zone by 10-15%
- **Sunny sites:** Increase regeneration zone by 10-15%

Minimum total surface area for effective biological filtration: approximately 30-40 square meters (320-430 sq ft) for a single-family pool.

## Construction Process

### 1. Excavation and Liner

- Mark perimeter, excavate swimming zone to 6-8 feet with sloped sides
- Create shallow shelf (12-18 inches) for marginal plants
- Install EPDM rubber liner (45 mil minimum) with underlayment fabric
- Cover entire basin including regeneration zone

### 2. Filtration Zone

- Layer washed, inert gravel: coarse (2-3 in) at bottom, medium (3-4 in), fine (2-3 in) on top — total 8-10 inches
- Install circulation pump and piping before filling with gravel
- Create planting pockets in the gravel

### 3. Circulation

- Small energy-efficient pump (100-300 watts), circulate total volume every 6-12 hours
- Water enters regeneration zone at one end, returns to swimming zone at the other
- Aeration (waterfall, bubbler) adds oxygen and visual interest; solar-powered pumps work for off-grid

### 5. Plant Selection

Plants are the primary filtration mechanism. Select for:

**Oxygenating submerged plants** (planted in gravel):
- Hornwort ([[ceratophyllum-demersum]])
- Eelgrass (Vallisneria)
- Water milfoil (Myriophyllum)

**Marginal/emergent plants** (planted on shelf):
- Pickerel weed ([[pontederia-cordata]])
- Iris (Iris versicolor, I. pseudacorus)
- Arrowhead ([[sagittaria-latifolia]])
- Cattail (Typha latifolia) — aggressive, use sparingly
- Sweet flag (Acorus calamus)
- Water mint (Mentha aquatica)
- Elephant ear (Colocasia)

**Floating plants** (for additional nutrient uptake):
- Water hyacinth (Eichhornia) — aggressive, control carefully
- Water lettuce (Pistia)

**Nutrient uptake champions:** Watercress, pickerel weed, and iris are among the most effective at removing nitrogen and phosphorus from water.

## Maintenance

### Regular Tasks

| Task | Frequency | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Remove dead plant material | Weekly in summer | Prevents nutrient recycling |
| Check pump and circulation | Weekly | Ensure proper flow |
| Skim surface debris | As needed | Leaves, pollen, insects |
| Test water clarity | Monthly | Should be able to see bottom |
| Check pH | Monthly | Target 6.5-8.0 |

### Seasonal Tasks

| Task | Season | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| Cut back dead vegetation | Autumn | Leave some for winter habitat |
| Remove excess floating plants | Autumn | Compose or discard |
| Clean pump intake filter | Autumn and spring | Remove debris |
| Divide overgrown plants | Spring | Share extras or compost |
| Top off water level | Summer | Evaporation replacement |

### Long-Term Maintenance

- Every 3-5 years: thin gravel in regeneration zone, add fresh gravel
- Monitor for invasive species and remove promptly
- Reseed beneficial zooplankton if water clarity declines (Daphnia magna can be purchased)
- Inspect liner for damage annually

## Common Problems

### Green Water (Algae Bloom)

- Cause: Excess nutrients, insufficient filtration, or warm temperatures
