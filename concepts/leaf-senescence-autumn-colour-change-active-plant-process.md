## Leaf Senescence and Autumn Colour Change as Active Plant Process

The annual spectacle of autumn foliage is not merely a passive
consequence of environmental change but an active, genetically
programmed process controlled by the plant itself.

Far from being "dying leaves," autumn colour change represents
a sophisticated resource recovery strategy.

Understanding senescence reveals how plants sense and respond
to environmental cues including [[phytochrome]]
pigments, the same photoreceptors that control flowering.

When nights exceed a species-specific threshold, the plant
begins producing hormones that initiate senescence.

Temperature also plays a role — cool nights accelerate the
process, which is why autumn colours are most vivid in
regions with cold nights and bright sunny days.

## The Chemistry of Autumn Colours

The colour changes of autumn foliage result from the

## The Anthocyanin Mystery

Unlike carotenoids, anthocyanins are not present in leaves
during the [[extending-growing-season]] — they are newly produced during
autumn.

This synthesis requires significant metabolic investment
from the plant, raising the question of why it bothers.

Several hypotheses explain anthocyanin production.

The most widely supported theory is that anthocyanins act
as sunscreens, protecting the leaf during the vulnerable
period of nutrient reabsorption.
