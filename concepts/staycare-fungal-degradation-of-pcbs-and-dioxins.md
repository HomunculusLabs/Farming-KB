## Fungal Degradation of PCBs and Dioxins

Polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) and dioxins are among the most
persistent and toxic environmental contaminants. PCBs were widely used in
dielectric fluids, heat-transfer fluids, hydraulic fluids, flame
retardants, adhesives, and textiles before their production was banned.
Dioxins, including polychlorinated dibenzo-p-dioxins (PCDD) and
polychlorinated dibenzofurans (PCDF), are released from paper mill
effluents, combustion processes, and as contaminants of chlorophenol
chemicals. [[fukuoka-textdoc-cross-breeding-fungal-strain-mutability]] employed. P. chrysosporium degrades
PCBs in decreasing order with increasing chlorination: biphenyl (23%),
2-chlorobiphenyl (16%), and 2,2-prime,4,4-prime-tetrachlorobiphenyl
(10%). Degradation of 4,4-prime-dichlorobiphenyl reached 11% while
negligible degradation occurred for 3,3-prime,4,4-prime-tetrachloro-
biphenyl, confirming that degradation depends on the chlorine
substitution pattern on the biphenyl ring.

Yadav and colleagues reported 82, 31, and 18 percent degradation of
Aroclor 1242, 1254, and 1260 respectively by P. chrysosporium. This was
the first conclusive demonstration of substantial degradation of Aroclor
1260 by a fungus in pure culture. Congeners with varying numbers of
ortho, meta, and para chlorines were extensively degraded, indicating
relative non-specificity for the position of chlorine substitutions.
Notably, PCB degradation does not require induction by biphenyl and
occurs in high-nitrogen or malt-extract media where lignin-modifying
enzymes are not produced, suggesting alternative enzymatic pathways.

## PCB Degradation by Other Fungal Species

Multiple fungal species have demonstrated PCB-degrading capabilities.
[[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] (LiP) and [[staycare-phanerochaete-sordida-in-bioremediation]] achieved up to 60 percent degradation of 2,3,7,8-
tetrachlorodibenzo-p-dioxin (TCDD), the most toxic dioxin congener.
This represents a significant finding given the extreme recalcitrance
and toxicity of TCDD, which is resistant to most biological degradation
processes.

## Chlorophenol Degradation as a Related Pathway

Pentachlorophenol (PCP) degradation by P. chrysosporium is initiated by
LiP- or MnP-catalyzed oxidative dechlorination to produce
tetrachloro-1,4-benzoquinone, which undergoes successive dechlorinations
to yield 1,2,4-trihydroxybenzene before ring cleavage. Trametes
versicolor degrades PCP through laccase-mediated pathways, with enhanced
