## Minoan Context and the Fading of the Mushroom Religion

McKenna places the Glaukos myth within the broader context of Minoan civilization as the
last refuge of partnership culture in the ancient world. After the fall of Catal Huyuk
around 6500 B.C., refugees carried the Goddess religion to Crete, where it flourished for
three millennia "after the triumph of the dominator style was everywhere else complete."

The myth of Glaukos, with its themes of death in honey, the loss of a divine child, and
the possibility of resurrection through esoteric knowledge, may encode the historical memory
of the transition from active mushroom use to a religion where the original psychoactive
sacrament had been replaced by its fermented honey vehicle, and where the old knowledge of
resurrection through plant ecstasy was preserved only in myth and ritual form.
