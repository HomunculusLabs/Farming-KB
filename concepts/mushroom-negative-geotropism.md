# Mushroom Negative Geotropism

## Overview

[[adaptive-anemotropism-spore-dispersal-optimization-mushroom-evolution]]. Badham (1982) demonstrated in
[[spore-production-geotropic-sensitivity-coupling-basidiomycete-badham-1982]], forming the second phase
of [[mushroom-anemotropism]].

## Onset and Timing

The negative geotropic response in P. cubensis is not present throughout all
developmental stages. It appears specifically at Stage 2, when:

- The pileus has become nearly spherical
- [[spore]] production is initiating in the lamellae (gills)
- The stipe has already undergone significant elongation under anemotropic
  (wind-directed) influence

### Stage-Specific Expression

| Developmental Stage | Dominant Tropism | Geotropic Response |
|--------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Stage 1 (early swelling) | Anemotropism | Not expressed |
| Stage 2 (spherical pileus) | Transition | Emerging, masks anemotropism |
| Stage 3 (hemispherical, sporulating) | Negative geotropism | Fully expressed |
| Stage 4 (plane pileus) | Negative geotropism | Fully expressed |

## Link to Spore Production

The most significant finding of Badham's study is the connection between
negative geotropism and spore formation. Three lines of evidence support this
link:

### 1. Absence in Sterile Mutants

A sporeless (sterile) mutant strain of P. cubensis was studied alongside the
fertile wild type. The sterile mutant:
- Exhibited normal anemotropic response during Stages 1–2
- Did NOT show negative geotropic recurvature at Stage 2 or later
- Continued growing in the wind direction without upward correction

The absence of the geotropic response in a strain that is genetically identical
except for spore production capacity strongly suggests that spore-related
processes drive the tropic switch.

### 2. Light Requirement

The negative geotropic response requires at least some light. However, this
light requirement may be indirect — light is necessary for spore production in
P. cubensis (Badham, 1980), so the apparent light requirement for geotropism
may simply reflect the need for spore formation to occur.

When mushrooms were kept in darkness: few spores formed, and no geotropic
recurvature occurred. This is consistent with the spore-geotropism hypothesis.

### 3. Hormonal Evidence from Prior Work

Previous research on other mushroom species supports the existence of
gill-produced hormones involved in tropic regulation:
- Gruen (1963) and Hagimoto (1963) postulated mushroom hormones involved in
  tropisms
- Decapitation experiments linked these hormones to the lamellae (gills)
- Hagimoto and Konishi (1960) showed agar diffusates from gills could partially
  replace the influence of the pileus on tropic responses

## Behavioral Characteristics

### Direction Independence

The negative geotropic response causes the pileus to turn upward regardless of:
- The direction of incident light (from above or below — the response is the
  same)
- The direction of the wind (the mushroom curves upward away from wind
  direction)
