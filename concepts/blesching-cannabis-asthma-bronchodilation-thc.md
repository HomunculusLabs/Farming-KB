## Asthma Pathophysiology

### Standard Understanding

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the airways involving:

- **Bronchoconstriction** — smooth muscle spasm in the upper airways narrows breathing passages.
- **Airway hyperinflation** — trapped air causes overexpansion of lung tissue.
- **Inflammation** — chronic immune activation produces swelling and mucus.
- **Hyperresponsiveness** — airways overreact to triggers including dust, mold, exercise, emotional stress, [[cannabis-cancer-symptom-management]].
- Children and elderly are most vulnerable and may be least tolerant of side effects.

## Clinical Evidence for THC Bronchodilation

### Study 1: Tashkin et al. (1974) — Double-Blind Crossover

The foundational study enrolled ten patients with stable asthma in a double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover design comparing:

- Smoked cannabis
- Oral THC (15 mg)
- Placebo
- Isoproterenol (standard bronchodilator)

**Results:** While isoproterenol produced a more pronounced initial effect, both smoked cannabis and oral THC caused **significant bronchodilation lasting at least 2 hours** — longer than the pharmaceutical comparator.

### Study 2: Tashkin et al. (1975) — Chemical and Exercise-Induced Bronchospasm

Eight stable asthmatics received smoked cannabis (2% THC) under two challenge conditions:

1. Chemically induced bronchospasm
2. Exercise-induced bronchospasm

**Results:** Smoked cannabis produced **prompt correction of bronchospasms and associated hyperinflation**, with efficacy similar to isoproterenol in both challenge models.

### Study 3: Williams et al. (1976) — THC vs. Salbutamol

A double-blind, placebo-controlled study of ten asthma patients compared THC with salbutamol:

**Results:** Both THC and salbutamol significantly improved ventilatory function. Salbutamol achieved maximal bronchodilation more rapidly, but **at 1 hour both drugs were equally effective**. The researcher noted that THC's mode of action **differs from sympathomimetic drugs** (which stimulate heart rate and blood pressure), suggesting THC could serve as a suitable adjuvant without cardiovascular side effects.

### Study 4: Tashkin et al. (1977) — Aerosolized THC

Eleven healthy subjects and five asthma patients received aerosolized THC in a randomized, double-blind trial:

**Results:** Aerosolized THC was less pronounced than isoproterenol at 5 minutes but **significantly better at 1–3 hours**. However, the study also identified a critical caveat — THC caused **moderate to severe bronchoconstriction with cough and chest discomfort in 2 of 5 asthmatic subjects**. The local irritating effect of THC on airway mucosa may make aerosolized delivery unsuitable for some patients.

### Study 5: Tashkin et al. (1978) — Dose-Response with Aerosolized THC

Asthmatic patients received aerosolized THC at steady-state dosages of 50–200 mcg:

**Results:** Treatment increased peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR) and forced expiratory volume, confirming bronchodilatory efficacy at controlled doses. The dose-response relationship helped establish therapeutic ranges.

## Mechanism of Action

- **Beta-2 agonists** (albuterol, salbutamol) stimulate the sympathetic nervous system, producing rapid bronchodilation but with cardiovascular side effects (increased heart rate, blood pressure).
