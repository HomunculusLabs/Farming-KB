## Design Principles

- Study natural bodies of water before building -- nature is the best model
- Combine functions: fish, crayfish, plants, bathing, and aesthetic enjoyment in one pond
- A well-structured pond with varied depths and edges fulfils more functions
- The "vibration method" for sealing ponds (no liners) allows ongoing management and modification
- Standpipes at the deepest point allow water level regulation and fish/plant harvesting
- Emergency overflow pipes handle heavy rainfall safely

## Pond Construction: The Vibration Method

Holzer's signature technique for pond construction is the "vibration method" (Vibrationsverfahren), which seals pond bottoms without plastic liners or clay. The process involves laying a foundation of suitable soil, saturating it with water, and then compacting it using a vibrating plate compactor or manual tamping. The vibration causes soil particles to settle into a dense, impermeable layer. This method requires specific soil types — loamy or clay soils work best, while sandy or gravelly soils may not seal adequately. Holzer emphasizes that pond construction must work with local geology rather than against it; sites with natural seeps or springs are preferable to dry locations. The vibration method allows ongoing modification — pond shapes and depths can be adjusted years after initial construction, which is impossible with liner-based systems.

## Breeding and Population Management

Holzer's ponds support natural breeding for most species, eliminating the need for artificial hatcheries. The key to successful natural reproduction is providing appropriate spawning habitat: shallow, vegetated areas with warm water for coarse fish like carp and tench, and clean gravel beds with flowing water for trout and char. Predatory species like pike and zander are essential for population control, preventing overpopulation of forage fish. Holzer maintains a balanced ratio between predator and prey species, noting that removing predators leads to stunted, overcrowded populations of smaller fish. The presence of crayfish provides additional population regulation through their scavenging behavior and serves as an indicator of water quality, as crayfish are sensitive to pollution and low oxygen.

## Winter Management and Ice Cover

At 1,500 meters altitude, Holzer's ponds experience significant ice cover during winter months. Deep areas (3 meters or more) ensure that fish have access to unfrozen water below the ice. The insulating effect of snow on ice actually helps maintain water temperature stability. Holzer avoids breaking ice unnecessarily, as the ice cover reduces oxygen exchange but also slows metabolic rates of fish, reducing their oxygen demand.
