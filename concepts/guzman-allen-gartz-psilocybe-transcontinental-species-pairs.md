Psilocybe that suggest ancient Gondwanan and South Pacific connections.
These disjunct distributions, where closely related species occur on widely
separated continents, provide evidence for the evolutionary history of
neurotropic fungi. The pattern was first noted by Horak (1983), who observed
relationships between South American, Mesoamerican, and Southeastern Asian
agarics and boletes.

## The Gondwanan Connection

Guzman, Allen and Gartz (2000) concluded that neurotropic [[neurotropic-fungi-in-south-america]], based
on the high diversity there, and from that region reached northern parts
([[global-psychedelic-mushroom-species-diversity]] in the Austral hemisphere
and subtropical regions.

Of 85 neurotropic Psilocybe species analyzed by Guzman (1983), the majority
occurred in the Austral hemisphere: 59 species in South America and Mexico,
versus 18 in the USA and Canada, and only 9 in Europe. This disparity holds
even though North America and Europe are more mycologically explored than
southern regions, suggesting genuine evolutionary history rather than sampling
bias.

## Section Mexicanae Species Pairs

### [[psilocybe-aucklandii]] and P. zapotecorum

Another clear transcontinental relationship involves:

- **P. aucklandii** -- Known from New Zealand (Auckland region)
- **P. zapotecorum** -- Known from Mexico and throughout South America

Both species belong to Section Zapotecorum. P. zapotecorum is one of the most
widely distributed neurotropic Psilocybe species, recorded from Mexico,
Colombia, Bolivia, Brazil, and Argentina. The New Zealand representative P.
aucklandii demonstrates the South Pacific connection between American and
Australasian mycobiotas.

## Japan-America Species Relationships

### Psilocybe argentipes and P. subcaerulipes (Japan)

These two Japanese species are very closely related to American species:

- **P. graveolens** -- From the Americas
- **P. muliercula** -- Endemic to Mexico
- **P. pintonii** -- From Colombia

These relationships fit Hongo's Type 4 distribution pattern (North American
and Eastern Asiatic species), though the connections extend further south into
Mesoamerica and South America.

## Hongo's Nine Distribution Types

Hongo (1978) proposed nine types of fungal distribution based on the Japanese
mycoflora. Guzman, Allen and Gartz (2000) applied all nine to neurotropic
fungi:

### Type 1: Cosmopolitan Species

Examples: Panaeolus spp. and [[copelandia-cyanescens]], C. tropicalis.
Also possibly Gerronema fibula from Malaysia to South America.

### Type 8: Arctic and Alpine Species

Example: P. aztecorum var. aztecorum from high Mexican mountains, related to
P. baeocystis (Pacific Northwest) and P. quebecensis (Quebec, Canada).

### Type 9: Endemic Species

Many examples: C. siliginoides, P. muliercula, P. chiapanensis (Mexico);
P. guatapensis, P. pintonii (Colombia); P. portoricensis (Puerto Rico); and
Panaeolina species described by Hongo from Japan.
