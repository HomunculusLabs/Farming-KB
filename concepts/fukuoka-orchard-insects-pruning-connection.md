## The Causal Chain

Fukuoka explained the chain of causation clearly: to the extent that
trees deviate from their natural form, pruning and insect problems
arise. When a tree is pruned, its natural shape is disrupted. The
pruned tree sends out vigorous but unnatural new growth that is weak
and susceptible to insect attack and disease. The insects that appear
are not the cause of the problem but a symptom of the tree's weakened
condition.

If a single new bud is snipped off a fruit tree with a pair of
scissors, it may bring about an unforeseen change in the direction of
the branch, altering the entire shape of the tree over time. Each
pruning cut forces compensatory growth that progressively weakens the
tree.

## The Nursery Problem

Fukuoka noted that fruit tree seedlings are already deformed in the
nursery before they are transplanted to the orchard, which makes
pruning appear necessary later. The entire system of tree propagation
is based on creating trees that will require ongoing human intervention
to remain productive.

If trees are allowed to follow their natural form from the beginning,
there is no need for pruning or sprays of pesticides. The natural form
of a tree is inherently strong and resistant.
