# Rockwool for Cannabis

Rockwool is an exceptional Cannabis Growing Mediums and a favorite of many cannabis growers worldwide. It is an inert, sterile, porous, non-degradable growing medium that provides firm root support. Rockwool is probably the most popular hydroponic growing medium in the world and is widely used across all types of [[cervantes-passive-wick-hydroponic-systems]].

## What is Rockwool

Rockwool is made from volcanic basalt rock that is melted at high temperatures and spun into fine fibers, similar to how cotton candy is made. The resulting product is a sterile, inert substrate with excellent water-holding and air-holding properties. Popular brand names include Grodan, HydroGro, and Vacrok.

## Properties and Characteristics

- **Sterile**: Rockwool is manufactured at extreme temperatures, making it free of pathogens, weed seeds, and pests.
- **Inert**: Does not react with nutrient solutions or alter their chemical composition.
- **Non-degradable**: Does not break down over time, maintaining consistent structure throughout the grow cycle.
- **Fibrous structure**: The fine fibers create a network that holds both water and air.
- **pH**: Has a naturally high pH (typically 7.0 to 8.0) and must be conditioned before use.
- **CEC**: Has zero cation-exchange capacity, meaning roots must be constantly bathed in nutrients.
- **Porosity**: Approximately 90% porosity with about 20% air space.

## Preparing Rockwool for Use

Before using rockwool, it must be properly conditioned:
1. **Soak in pH-adjusted water**: Fill a rockwool tray with water adjusted to pH 5 to 6 and soak the cubes or blocks.
2. **Pre-soak seeds**: Seeds set inside rockwool blocks often heave and should be pre-soaked before planting.
3. **pH conditioning**: Because rockwool has a high natural pH, it must be soaked in slightly acidic water (pH 5.0 to 5.5) for 24 hours before use to stabilize the pH.
4. **Moisture**: Keep rockwool evenly moist but not waterlogged after planting.

## Rockwool Products

Rockwool is available in several forms for different stages of cannabis cultivation:

- **Rockwool cubes**: Small cubes used for starting seeds and [[cannabis-clone-rooting-and-transplanting]]. Typically 1 to 2 inches in size. Hold plenty of air and nutrient solution within their fiber, and are clean and easy to use.
- **Rockwool blocks**: Larger blocks (3 to 4 inches or more) that small starter cubes are transplanted into. Used for vegetative growth and early flowering.
- **Rockwool slabs**: Long wrapped slabs used in commercial hydroponic systems. Slabs are placed on drainage tables and plants are grown in blocks set on top of the slabs.
- **Rockwool plugs**: Small pre-formed plugs used in cloning machines and propagation trays.

## Using Rockwool for Cloning

Rockwool is one of the most popular media for [[cannabis-propagation-techniques]]. To root clones in rockwool:
1. Fill rockwool tray with water at pH 5 to 6.
2. Take cuttings and apply rooting hormone.
3. Insert cuttings into pre-drilled holes in the rockwool.
4. Keep rockwool evenly moist and in a humid environment.
5. Clones typically develop strong root systems in 10 to 14 days.

## Rockwool in Hydroponic Systems

### Ebb and Flow

Rockwool blocks or cubes are placed on the flood table and flooded from the bottom. The medium absorbs nutrient solution and drains between cycles. Remember that rockwool holds a lot of moisture, so irrigation frequency may need adjustment compared to faster-draining mediums.

### Top-Feed Drip Systems

Rockwool slabs or blocks are irrigated from above via spaghetti tubes and emitters. Nutrient solution percolates through the medium and drains back to the reservoir. Rockwool cubes in this system allow individual plant management and easy replacement.

### NFT Systems

Small rockwool cubes or net pots with rockwool are placed in NFT gullies. Roots grow from the cube into the thin film of nutrient solution flowing below.

### DWC and Aeroponics

Rockwool starter cubes are used in net pots for [[deep-water-culture]] and banisteriopsis caapi systems. The rockwool supports the young plant while roots grow through the net pot into the nutrient solution or misting chamber.

## Advantages of Rockwool

- **Sterile and clean**: No pathogens, pests, or weed seeds.
- **Consistent**: Uniform structure and properties from batch to batch.
- **Excellent water-to-air ratio**: Holds both water and air effectively.
- **Widely available**: Sold at most hydroponic and garden stores.
- **Versatile**: Suitable for cloning, vegetative growth, and flowering.
- **Easy to use**: Pre-formed cubes and blocks are convenient and predictable.

## Disadvantages

- **No buffering capacity**: Rockwool has zero CEC, so roots must be constantly bathed in nutrients. Any interruption in nutrient delivery quickly affects plants.
- **High natural pH**: Must be conditioned before use.
- **Non-biodegradable**: Creates waste after use; disposal can be an issue.
- **Fibrous dust**: Can be irritating to skin, eyes, and lungs. Wear gloves and a mask when handling dry rockwool.
- **Not ideal for reuse**: Can compact and retain dead roots, making reuse problematic.

## Irrigation Management

Rockwool holds a lot of moisture, so irrigation must be carefully managed. In [[ebb-and-flow]] systems, flood when the medium is about half-full of moisture. In drip systems, a single application point per plant is common. Monitor the moisture level by lifting blocks to check their weight. Heavier blocks indicate more moisture; lighter blocks need irrigation.

During and soon after irrigation, the nutrient content of the medium and the reservoir are at the same concentration. As time passes between irrigations, the EC and pH gradually change. If enough time passes between irrigations, the nutrient concentration might change so much that the plant is unable to draw nutrients in efficiently.

## CEC and Nutrient Availability

Rockwool has zero cation-exchange capacity (CEC), meaning it holds no nutrients on its surfaces for later root uptake. This is fundamentally different from media like peat moss (CEC of 80) or Sunshine Mix (CEC of 90). Because rockwool cannot buffer nutrients, roots must be constantly bathed in nutrient solution. Any interruption in delivery quickly affects plant health.

This zero CEC makes rockwool an excellent diagnostic tool: any nutrient deficiency symptoms observed are a direct reflection of the nutrient solution composition, not the medium interacting with nutrients.

## Reusing Rockwool

Rockwool can be difficult to reuse. After a grow cycle, the medium can compact and retain dead root material. Sterilizing and reusing substrates that compact and lose structure, such as rockwool, is generally not recommended. Instead, once rockwool has been used indoors, Cervantes recommends reusing it in the outdoor garden where the issues of compaction and dead roots are less problematic.

## Maintenance Tips

- Always pre-condition rockwool before planting by soaking in pH 5.0 to 5.5 water for 24 hours.
- Keep the surface of rockwool blocks from drying out by using [[coco-coir-cannabis]] as a top dressing if needed.
- Remove the plastic wrapping from rockwool blocks before transplanting into soil or soilless mix.
- Do not squeeze or compress rockwool, as this reduces air space and water movement.
- Monitor pH closely, as rockwool has no buffering capacity.
- Flush with dilute nutrient solution if salt buildup occurs (visible as white crust).
- Wear gloves and a mask when handling dry rockwool to avoid skin and lung irritation.

## Common Problems

- **Algae growth on surface**: Caused by light exposure and excess moisture. Cover exposed rockwool surfaces with dark plastic or a top dressing.
- **pH drift**: Rockwool's high initial pH can cause ongoing pH issues. Always pre-condition and monitor regularly.
- **Root bound**: Roots can fill the block quickly. Transplant to a larger block or container before roots become constricted.
- **Salt accumulation**: Visible as white deposits on the rockwool surface. Reduce nutrient concentration and flush with plain water.
- **Fiber dust irritation**: Dry rockwool produces irritating dust. Always pre-moisten and wear protective equipment.
