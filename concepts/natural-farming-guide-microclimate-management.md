## Microclimate Management Techniques

A microclimate is a small area where the climate differs from the surrounding
region. Through deliberate design using windbreaks, water features, thermal
mass, and strategic plant placement, you can create localized conditions that
allow plants to thrive outside their normal range. The fundamental principle is
that weather is not a problem but an asset to be used and managed.

## The Principle of Climate as Asset

Most gardeners complain about too much sun, too little sun, too much wind, or
too much rain. Conditions are never perfect. Yet with planning, the real
extremes of weather and climate can be used and defended. You must set up
microclimates using trellises and water diversion, either to retain moisture
and coolness in the desert or to reflect heat and store water in a colder
climate.

You have a great deal of control over the climates of small areas through the
use of windbreaks, irrigation, and terraces. You could grow just about
anything, anywhere, if you set up a microclimate for it. Many different
microclimates are already in existence on most land, most of them related to
slopes or water.

## Managing Frost

Frost happens when the land loses heat rapidly, usually just before the sun
comes up. Understanding your region's yearly frost dates and the lowest and
highest temperatures your plants and animals will tolerate is essential.

## Managing Wind

Too much wind can harm wind-sensitive plants, blow away seeds, lower soil
temperature, dry up moisture, kill young animals, and make work intolerable.
Winds of 15 mph are strong enough to reduce production. Winds of 20 mph cause
physical damage to plants. A windbreak can either block winds or channel them
in the direction you want them to go.

### Windbreak Design

When planning sectors, note the direction of the prevailing wind by tying a
streamer or flag on a stake. Successful natural windbreaks use pioneer species
that are easy to grow, with fibrous stems and fleshy, fuzzy, or needle leaves.
Use an earth mound or tall solid fence to protect the plants as they grow.
