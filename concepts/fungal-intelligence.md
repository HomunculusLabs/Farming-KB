# Fungal Intelligence Fungal intelligence refers to the capacity of fungi — particularly their mycelial networks — to process information, make decisions, learn, and solve problems in ways that resemble cognitive behavior, despite lacking a nervous system, brain, or any conventional center of computation. This concept challenges traditional definitions of intelligence and cognition, raising fundamental questions about what it means to process information and adapt behavior in biological systems. ## Learning and Memory

Evidence suggests fungi can exhibit habituation, a basic form of learning.
When exposed to repeated non-harmful stimuli, fungal networks reduce their
response over time, indicating a form of sensory adaptation that persists.
Experiments by Fricker et al. demonstrated that mycelial networks could
remember the location of a food source and adjust their growth patterns
accordingly even after the source was removed, suggesting a form of spatial
memory encoded in [[transcendent-other-and-plant-human-information-transfer]] allows fungal colonies to transmit
experience to their descendants without any nervous system whatsoever.
