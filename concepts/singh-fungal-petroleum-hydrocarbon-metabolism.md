# Fungal Metabolism of Petroleum Hydrocarbons (Singh)

Petroleum hydrocarbons are among the most widespread environmental contaminants, resulting from oil exploration, transportation, refining, and consumption. Fungi play significant roles in the natural attenuation and engineered remediation of petroleum-contaminated environments.

## Petroleum Composition and Environmental Impact

Crude petroleum contains thousands of hydrocarbon compounds including:
- **Alkanes** (normal, branched, and cycloalkanes): 50-60% of crude oil
- **Aromatic hydrocarbons**: Benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, xylenes (BTEX); naphthalene and alkylated naphthalenes
- **Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)**: Two to six fused aromatic rings
- **Resins and asphaltenes**: High-molecular-weight polar compounds
- **Sulfur, nitrogen, and oxygen compounds**: Including thiophenes, pyridines, and carboxylic acids

## Fungal vs. Bacterial Hydrocarbon Degradation

Fungi differ from bacteria in their approach to hydrocarbon degradation:
- Fungi generally grow more slowly but tolerate higher hydrocarbon concentrations
- Fungal mycelia can penetrate soil aggregates and reach hydrocarbons in microenvironments
- Fungal enzymatic systems (especially cytochrome P450 monooxygenases) introduce oxygen into alkane chains, while bacteria often use terminal oxidation pathways
- Fungi can degrade higher-molecular-weight alkanes and PAHs that resist bacterial attack
- See [[bioremediation-fungal-vs-bacterial]] for detailed comparison

## Alkane Degradation

### Mechanisms
Fungi degrade alkanes primarily through:
1. **Cytochrome P450 monooxygenase system**: Introduces hydroxyl group at terminal or subterminal carbon position
2. **Alcohol dehydrogenase**: Converts alcohol to aldehyde
3. **Aldehyde dehydrogenase**: Converts aldehyde to fatty acid
4. **Beta-oxidation pathway**: Shortens fatty acid chain, ultimately producing acetyl-CoA

### Key Fungal Species
- **Candida species**: Especially C. tropicalis and C. maltosa, which metabolize n-alkanes through cytochrome P450 systems
- **Aspergillus species**: Degrade both aliphatic and aromatic fractions
- **Cladosporium species**: Common hydrocarbon-degrading fungi in soil environments
- **Penicillium species**: Degrade alkanes and contribute to PAH oxidation

### Factors Affecting Alkane Degradation
- **Chain length**: Medium-chain alkanes (C10-C20) are most readily degraded; very short chains (C1-C4) are volatile and very long chains are poorly soluble
- **Branching**: Branched alkanes degrade more slowly than normal alkanes
- **Oxygen availability**: Aerobic degradation requires molecular oxygen for initial monooxygenase attack
- **Nitrogen and phosphorus**: Often limiting in petroleum-contaminated soils; biostimulation-techniques|biostimulation with nutrients enhances degradation

## Aromatic Hydrocarbon Degradation

### BTEX Compounds
Benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylenes are highly soluble, mobile groundwater contaminants:
- Fungi oxidize BTEX compounds through cytochrome P450 systems
- Toluene is generally the most readily degraded of the BTEX group
- Fungal degradation of benzene is slower and less complete than bacterial pathways
- White-rot fungi can oxidize BTEX through non-specific ligninolytic enzyme systems

### Naphthalene and Alkylnaphthalenes
The simplest PAH, naphthalene, is degraded by fungi through:
- Initial epoxidation by cytochrome P450 to naphthalene 1,2-epoxide
- Conversion to trans-1,2-dihydroxy-1,2-dihydronaphthalene
- Further oxidation to 1,2-dihydroxynaphthalene
- Ring cleavage and further metabolism to TCA cycle intermediates

## Fungal-Bacterial Consortia

The most effective petroleum bioremediation strategies combine fungal and bacterial capabilities:
- **Sequential degradation**: Fungi partially oxidize recalcitrant high-molecular-weight compounds, making intermediates available for bacterial mineralization
- **Complementary pathways**: Fungal cytochrome P450 systems attack different positions on molecules than bacterial dioxygenases
- **Physical facilitation**: Fungal hyphae improve soil structure, aeration, and water infiltration, creating conditions favorable for bacterial activity
- **Mycoremediation + bioaugmentation**: Combining fungal inoculation with hydrocarbon-degrading bacterial strains

## Bioreactor Applications

### Slurry Bioreactors
- Soil or sediment slurried with water in aerated tanks
- Fungal inoculation enhances degradation of high-molecular-weight fractions
- Effective for ex-situ treatment of heavily contaminated soils

### Land Treatment (Biopiling)
- Contaminated soil excavated and arranged in piles or windows
- Aeration, moisture control, and nutrient amendment
- Fungal inoculation can accelerate degradation of recalcitrant fractions

### Composting
- Mixing contaminated soil with organic amendments (see [[staycare-fungal-agricultural-waste-conversion]])
- Thermophilic conditions enhance overall degradation rates
- White-rot fungi in composting systems degrade PAHs and other recalcitrant compounds

### In-Situ Bioremediation
- Bioventing: Air injection stimulates aerobic degradation
- Biosparging: Air injection below water table
- Nutrient injection (biostimulation-techniques|biostimulation)
- Fungal inoculation through injection or surface application

## Agricultural Land Contamination

Petroleum contamination of agricultural land is a significant concern:
- Pipeline leaks, storage tank failures, and accidental spills affect farmland
- Oil reduces soil water-holding capacity, aeration, and nutrient availability
- Petroleum residues can be taken up by crops, entering the food chain
- Fungal remediation offers a low-cost, low-impact approach for restoring contaminated agricultural land
- Integration with [[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]] enables simultaneous soil restoration and vegetation establishment

## Challenges and Future Directions

- Bioremediation effectiveness depends on pollutant bioavailability, which decreases with soil aging
- Surfactant addition can enhance bioavailability but may be toxic to fungi at high concentrations
- Genetically modified fungi with enhanced hydrocarbon-degrading capabilities are under development
- Better understanding of fungal community dynamics during petroleum bioremediation is needed
- Integration of fungal remediation with physical and chemical treatment methods offers the most practical approach for complex contamination scenarios
