# Cannabis Landrace and Heirloom Varieties

Landrace cannabis varieties are locally adapted populations that evolved in specific geographic regions over centuries of natural and human selection. They represent the genetic foundation of all modern cannabis strains and are invaluable for breeding programs seeking unique cannabinoid profiles, disease resistance, and regional adaptation.

## What Is a Landrace

### Definition
- A domesticated, locally adapted, traditional variety of cannabis
- Evolved in a specific geographic region through natural selection and traditional cultivation
- Relatively homogeneous and stable genotype adapted to local conditions
- Named after the region of origin (e.g., Thai, Afghan, Colombian)

### Characteristics
- High genetic diversity within the population (not a single clone)
- Adapted to local climate, photoperiod, pests, and diseases
- Often more resilient than modern hybrids (centuries of natural selection)
- Unique cannabinoid and [[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]] (6000+ years). Both fiber and drug types. Ruderalis from northern China

**Middle East and Central Asia**
- **Lebanon**: Famous for red Lebanese hash. Adapted to Mediterranean climate
- **Turkey**: Anatolian varieties, both fiber and drug types
- **Morocco**: Rif Mountains. Foundation of Moroccan hash. Mostly sativa but shorter than tropical types due to latitude

### Americas

**South America**
- **Colombia**: Tall, equatorial sativas. Long flowering (12-14+ weeks). Golden, red, chocolate varieties. "Colombian Gold" famous in 1970s
- **Brazil**: Amazonian varieties. Diverse — both sativa and ruderalis-influenced
- **Peru**: Andean varieties. Used for both fiber and psychoactive purposes
- **Mexico**: Acapulco Gold, Oaxacan. Sativa varieties, some indica influence from northern trade routes

## Heirloom Varieties

### Definition
- Stable, open-pollinated varieties maintained by growers over multiple generations
- Not landraces (not regionally adapted over centuries) but stable and true-breeding
- Often crosses of landraces selected and stabilized by breeders

### Examples
- **Haze**: Sativa-dominant cross of Colombian, Mexican, Thai, and Afghan. Created in 1970s California. Long flowering (12+ weeks)
- **Skunk #1**: Afghani × Acapulco Gold × Colombian Gold. Sam the Skunkman, 1970s. Foundation of countless modern strains
- **Northern Lights**: Pure Afghani indica. Stable IBL. One of the most influential breeding lines
- **White Widow**: Brazilian sativa × South Indian indica. Shantibaba, 1990s. Extremely resinous

## Landrace Conservation

### Why It Matters
- Landraces contain unique genes not found in modern hybrids
