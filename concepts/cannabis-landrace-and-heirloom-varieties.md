# Cannabis Landrace and Heirloom Varieties

Landrace cannabis varieties are locally adapted populations that evolved in specific geographic regions over centuries of natural and human selection. They represent the genetic foundation of all modern cannabis strains and are invaluable for breeding programs seeking unique cannabinoid profiles, disease resistance, and regional adaptation.

## What Is a Landrace

### Definition
- A domesticated, locally adapted, traditional variety of cannabis
- Evolved in a specific geographic region through natural selection and traditional cultivation
- Relatively homogeneous and stable genotype adapted to local conditions
- Named after the region of origin (e.g., Thai, Afghan, Colombian)

### Characteristics
- High genetic diversity within the population (not a single clone)
- Adapted to local climate, photoperiod, pests, and diseases
- Often more resilient than modern hybrids (centuries of natural selection)
- Unique cannabinoid and terpene profiles specific to the region
- Longer flowering periods than modern hybrids (adapted to natural photoperiod)

## Major Landrace Regions

### Asia

**Central Asia (Cannabis indica)**
- **Afghanistan**: Short, bushy, wide-leaf plants. High thc, resinous. Foundation of modern "indica" strains. Adapted to dry, mountainous climate
- **Pakistan (Hindu Kush)**: Similar to Afghan. Dark, hash-producing varieties. Hashish capital of the world
- **India**: Enormous diversity. Parvati Valley (Malana Cream), Kerala, Northeast states. Both drug and fiber types
- **Nepal**: Himalayan varieties. Hand-rubbed charas production. Temple Ball hash
- **Thailand**: Tall, tropical sativa. Long flowering (14-16 weeks). Cerebral, energetic high. "Thai stick"
- **Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam**: Southeast Asian sativas. Similar to Thai but with regional differences
- **China**: Oldest cannabis cultivation (6000+ years). Both fiber and drug types. Ruderalis from northern China

**Middle East and Central Asia**
- **Lebanon**: Famous for red Lebanese hash. Adapted to Mediterranean climate
- **Turkey**: Anatolian varieties, both fiber and drug types
- **Morocco**: Rif Mountains. Foundation of Moroccan hash. Mostly sativa but shorter than tropical types due to latitude

### Americas

**South America**
- **Colombia**: Tall, equatorial sativas. Long flowering (12-14+ weeks). Golden, red, chocolate varieties. "Colombian Gold" famous in 1970s
- **Brazil**: Amazonian varieties. Diverse — both sativa and ruderalis-influenced
- **Peru**: Andean varieties. Used for both fiber and psychoactive purposes
- **Mexico**: Acapulco Gold, Oaxacan. Sativa varieties, some indica influence from northern trade routes

**Central America and Caribbean**
- **Jamaica**: Caribbean sativas. Used in Rastafarian spiritual practice. "Lamb's Breath"
- **Panama**: "Panama Red" — famous 1970s import. Tall, red-haired sativa

**North America**
- Native landraces largely displaced by modern hybrids since 1970s
- Some feral populations persist (Midwest hemp, "ditch weed")

### Africa

- **South Africa**: "Durban Poison" — famous pure sativa landrace. Short flowering for a sativa (8-9 weeks)
- **Malawi**: "Malawi Gold" — equatorial sativa, long flowering, potent
- **Lesotho**: Mountainous kingdom, traditional hash production. Both sativa and indica
- **Morocco**: (See Middle East — Rif Mountains)
- **Ethiopia**: Enormous genetic diversity. Highland and lowland varieties. Ancient cultivation history

## Heirloom Varieties

### Definition
- Stable, open-pollinated varieties maintained by growers over multiple generations
- Not landraces (not regionally adapted over centuries) but stable and true-breeding
- Often crosses of landraces selected and stabilized by breeders

### Examples
- **Haze**: Sativa-dominant cross of Colombian, Mexican, Thai, and Afghan. Created in 1970s California. Long flowering (12+ weeks)
- **Skunk #1**: Afghani × Acapulco Gold × Colombian Gold. Sam the Skunkman, 1970s. Foundation of countless modern strains
- **Northern Lights**: Pure Afghani indica. Stable IBL. One of the most influential breeding lines
- **White Widow**: Brazilian sativa × South Indian indica. Shantibaba, 1990s. Extremely resinous

## Landrace Conservation

### Why It Matters
- Landraces contain unique genes not found in modern hybrids
- Climate adaptation (drought, heat, cold, disease resistance)
- Unique cannabinoid/terpene profiles (high cbd landraces, rare terpenes)
- Genetic diversity essential for future breeding programs
- Cultural heritage — many landraces are tied to traditional practices

### Threats
- Modern hybrid cultivation displacing landrace cultivation worldwide
- Government eradication programs
- Climate change disrupting traditional growing regions
- Genetic contamination from modern cultivars
- Loss of traditional cultivation knowledge

### Preservation Efforts
- Seed banks (private and institutional)
- In situ conservation (maintaining populations in their native regions)
- Breeding programs incorporating landrace genetics
- Strain hunters collecting and preserving landrace seeds
