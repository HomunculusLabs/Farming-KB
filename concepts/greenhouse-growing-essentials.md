# Greenhouse Growing Essentials A greenhouse provides the space and environment to extend gardening activities. Even in an unheated greenhouse, yields will always be considerably earlier and therefore often heavier than from plants grown outside. The greenhouse turns cultivation into a year-round activity, enabling you to sow and harvest crops months earlier than would otherwise be possible. Buy the biggest you can afford — a greenhouse of any size will always be full. ## Choosing a Greenhouse **Shapes:** Traditional rectangular "barn" shape (most practical, best space use), circular/lantern (attractive but limited working space), sunrooms/lean-tos (convenient heating and care, but reduced light from three sides). **Materials:** Aluminium frames let in more light (thinner glazing bars). Wooden houses are cheaper to heat and look better, but must be painted regularly (unless cedar). Never use creosote as preservative — fumes are toxic to plants. Glass is most common; plastic sheeting over metal hoops is cheaper but must be replaced every couple of years. **Size:** Even a tiny greenhouse can accommodate a large quantity of plants, especially used with a [[extending-growing-season]]. Ensure height to eaves is sufficient for comfortable working and won't restrict the range of plants you wish to grow.

**Ventilation:** A 6x8ft (2x2.5m) greenhouse should have at least two roof vents, proportionally more in larger houses. Plastic greenhouses need permanent mesh ventilation panels.

**Position:** Needs a sunny position, fairly close to the house for easy connection of heating, water, and electricity supplies. Provide shelter from strong winds where possible.

## Essential Equipment

- **Staging:** Waist-high benches (about 3ft/1m high, max 3ft wide) of wood or aluminium, raising plants nearer the light. Can be homemade with lumber and corrugated metal topped with sand or capillary matting.
- **Heating:** Determines the range of plants you can grow. Temperature variation between night minimum and day maximum should be no more than 50°F (10°C).
- **Thermometer:** Maximum/minimum type to record daily extremes.
- **Water supply:** Permanent tank under staging, refilled after each watering so water reaches greenhouse temperature.
- **Shading:** Roller blinds (expensive but controllable) or commercial shading paint on exterior glass. Needed in summer to prevent sun-scorch and excessive temperatures.
- **Insulation:** Sheets of insulated plastic to prevent heat loss.
- **Propagator:** Enclosed case with built-in heating element for [[cannabis-seed-germination-methods]] at 65-70°F (18-21°C). Can be bought or homemade using a wooden case lined with heavy plastic and filled with expanded styrofoam granules.
- **Capillary matting:** Automatic watering — stand pots on moist absorbent matting.

## Heating Methods

### Paraffin Heaters
Cheap to buy and produce CO2 and water vapor beneficial to plants. Require daily filling and wick trimming. The thermostat is difficult to control and heaters can emit fumes harmful to plants.

### Electric Tubular Heaters
Clean, controllable with thermostat, and cost-effective. Portable fan or convection heaters keep air circulating well. Permanent wiring must be professionally installed with waterproofing. One of the cheapest and most convenient options.

### Gas Heating
More expensive than solid fuel but very convenient. Available as portable propane/butane cylinders or piped household supply. Can emit fumes harmful to plants.

### Solid Fuel (Coal)
Cheap and efficient but requires considerable capital outlay for boiler and piping. Frequent refueling necessary.

### Hot Beds (Organic Heating)
A cheap, organic alternative to electric soil-heating cables. Constructed by stacking a 9in (23cm) layer of fresh straw [[falconer-horse-manure-varieties]], then alternating layers of 2in (5cm) soil with a dusting of lime (two more layers), covered with a final layer of soil. The lime neutralizes the acid manure. Young plants are transplanted into compost-filled holes. The decomposing manure warms the roots and gets plants off to an early start.

### Reducing Heating Costs
Locate the greenhouse in a bright place for maximum free solar heat. Insulate to prevent heat loss and heat only the minimum area required. Use a [[hamilton-animal-manure-types-and-use]] or commercial liquid kelp feed. Plants expected to grow and produce simultaneously (tomatoes, cucumbers) need feeding every time you water at peak season. Always use recommended strength; overfeeding does more damage than underfeeding.

**Watering:** Install a hose reaching inside. In [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]], fill watering can and leave in greenhouse overnight so water warms. Water thoroughly and leave plants, rather than watering "little and often." Delicate seedlings need fine mist spray. Automatic options include capillary matting and trickle irrigation systems.

**Repotting:** When roots fill the pot, repot in spring/summer (never winter). Move to only slightly larger pot. Use a "plant mold" technique: pack compost around a discarded pot to form a mold, then drop the plant in.

**Border soil:** Can be used if well-prepared with [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]. Avoid growing same crop year after year (risk of soil-borne diseases). Replace border soil periodically or use [[seed-starting-methods-comparison]]

## Seasonal Management

During spring, the greenhouse is used for raising seedlings and hardening off
young plants. In summer, ventilation becomes critical — doors and vents should
be opened early in the morning and shading applied to prevent leaf scorch.
Autumn is the time to sow winter salads, overwintering onions, and hardy
annuals. In winter, insulation is added and frost protection becomes the
priority; bubble wrap lining the interior is an effective low-cost solution.
Monitoring temperature daily with a max-min thermometer and adjusting
ventilation and heating accordingly prevents most climate-related problems.
