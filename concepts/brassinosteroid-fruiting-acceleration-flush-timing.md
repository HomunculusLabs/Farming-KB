# Brassinosteroid Acceleration of Fruiting and Flush Timing in Psilocybe Cubensis

Among the most practically significant findings of the Gartz, Adam, and Vorbrodt (1990) study was the reduction in time to first harvest of [[psilocybe-cubensis]] fruiting bodies from 4 to 5 weeks in controls to just 3 to 3.5 weeks with brassinosteroid treatment. This 25 to 30% reduction in cultivation cycle time, combined with increased first flush production of 4 to 7 mushrooms compared to 1 to 3 in controls, represents a potentially transformative improvement in cultivation efficiency.






## Time to First Flush: Control Versus Treatment

The study reported that in the horse dung and rice grain substrate, control cultures produced their first sporocarps (fruiting bodies) within 4 to 5 weeks of inoculation. When 22S,23S-homobrassinolide was added at 10^-2 ppm, the first flush appeared in 3 to 3.5 weeks. This acceleration of approximately 1 to 1.5 weeks is substantial in the context of mushroom cultivation, where the entire production cycle from inoculation to final harvest typically spans 8 to 12 weeks.

The reduced time to fruiting likely reflects faster mycelial colonization of the substrate, allowing the mycelium to reach the biomass threshold required for fruiting initiation sooner. Brassinosteroids may also directly stimulate the molecular pathways that trigger the transition from vegetative growth to reproductive development, effectively lowering the threshold biomass needed for fruiting to begin.






## First Flush Enhancement

The first flush showed the most pronounced response to brassinosteroid treatment. Control cultures produced only 1 to 3 mushrooms in the first flush, while treated cultures produced 4 to 7, representing a 2 to 3 fold increase. The first flush is typically the most productive in multi-flush cultivation systems, often accounting for 40 to 60% of total yield. Enhancing first flush production therefore has a disproportionate impact on total harvest.

The mechanisms behind enhanced first flush production may include faster and more complete substrate colonization providing more nutrients for fruiting body development, improved synchrony of primordia initiation leading to more simultaneous fruit body formation, and more efficient resource allocation from mycelium to developing fruit bodies. The brassinosteroid may also prime the mycelium for fruiting during the vegetative phase, so that when environmental conditions trigger fruiting, a larger number of primordia are ready to develop.






## Multi-Flush Production Over Five Flushes

The study monitored five flushes during the fruiting period, providing a comprehensive view of brassinosteroid effects across the entire production cycle. While specific yield data for each individual flush beyond the first were not reported in detail, the overall pattern indicated that brassinosteroid-treated cultures maintained their productivity advantage throughout the five flush period.

In standard [[psilocybe-cubensis]] cultivation, subsequent flushes typically produce fewer and smaller fruiting bodies as substrate nutrients are progressively depleted. The total dry mass of brassinosteroid-treated cultures (3.4 to 3.9 g per 10 g substrate) compared to controls (2.3 to 2.9 g) reflects cumulative production across all five flushes, confirming that the yield advantage extends beyond the first harvest.






## Implications for Cultivation Cycle Economics

The combination of faster time to harvest and increased yield per cycle has multiplicative effects on cultivation productivity. A 25 to 30% reduction in cycle time means approximately 5 to 6 production cycles per year instead of 4 to 5 from the same facility. Combined with the 35 to 48% increase in yield per cycle, the annual production capacity could potentially increase by 70 to 100%.

This productivity improvement is particularly significant for commercial mushroom cultivation, where facility utilization and turnover rate are major determinants of profitability. The ability to complete more production cycles per year from the same infrastructure would reduce the per-unit capital cost and improve the return on investment for cultivation facilities.






## Fruiting Triggers and Brassinosteroid Interaction

Psilocybe cubensis fruiting is typically initiated by environmental triggers including temperature decrease, humidity increase to 95 to 100%, CO2 reduction through fresh air exchange, and light exposure. Brassinosteroids appear to act not as a substitute for these environmental triggers but as an amplifier of the fruiting response once triggers are present.

This interpretation is consistent with the known mechanism of brassinosteroids in plants, where they function as sensitizers that prime tissues for rapid growth responses to environmental signals. In fungal systems, brassinosteroids may lower the threshold of environmental stimulus required to initiate fruiting, or may accelerate the downstream developmental program once fruiting has been triggered.






## Potential for Other Cultivated Species

The Gartz study specifically examined Psilocybe cubensis, but the authors called for further studies on commercially important species. The mechanism of brassinosteroid action, if conserved across fungal species, could benefit a wide range of cultivated mushrooms. Species such as Agaricus bisporus (button mushroom), Pleurotus ostreatus (oyster mushroom), and Lentinula edodes (shiitake) represent major commercial crops where reduced time to harvest and increased yield would have significant economic value.

The effectiveness of brassinosteroids in different species may depend on whether those species possess endogenous brassinosteroid signaling systems. The dramatic response of P. cubensis suggests that at least some fungal species have retained or evolved sensitivity to brassinosteroid compounds, even if they do not produce them endogenously.
