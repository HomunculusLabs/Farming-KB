## Seed Potato Selection and Preparation
Certified disease-free seed potatoes are essential to avoid introducing
[[solomon-root-cellaring-and-crop-storage]]). Place tubers
sprout-side up and draw soil over them, creating a slight ridge.

## Hilling (Earthing Up)
Hilling is the practice of drawing soil up around the base of growing
plants. Begin hilling when plants are 15-20cm tall, and repeat every
2-3 weeks until flowering. Hilling serves multiple purposes: it
protects developing tubers from sunlight exposure (which causes
greening and glycoalkaloid production), suppresses weeds, improves
drainage around the stem base, and increases the volume of soil
available for tuber expansion. Hill to a final ridge height of 20-30cm.
In no-dig systems, mulch (straw, leaves) can substitute for soil
hilling -- tubers form within the mulch layer.

## Irrigation
Potatoes require consistent moisture, especially during tuber
initiation (6-8 weeks after emergence) and tuber bulking. Irregular
watering causes knobby, misshapen tubers and promotes common scab.
Target 25-40mm per week, adjusting for rainfall and soil type. Drip
irrigation or soaker hoses deliver water directly to the root zone
and keep foliage dry, reducing disease pressure. Avoid overhead
watering in humid conditions where [[potato-pest-and-disease-management|late blight]] is a risk.

## Harvesting
Early potatoes can be harvested 7-10 weeks after planting when
flowers open -- simply hand-harvest from the sides of the hill. Main
crop potatoes are ready when foliage yellows and dies back naturally,
typically 90-120 days after planting. Cut foliage 2 weeks before
harvest to allow tuber skins to set (cure), reducing damage and
improving storage. Dig carefully with a fork, working from the
outside of the hill inward to avoid spearing tubers. Allow harvested
potatoes to dry on the ground for a few hours in dry weather, then
move to a dark, well-ventilated curing area at 10-15C for 10-14
days before long-term storage.

## Storage
Store potatoes in dark, cool (3-5C), humid (85-95%) conditions.
Do not refrigerate below 3C, as cold converts starch to sugars,
affecting flavour and causing darkening when fried. Exclude light
completely -- greening indicates solanine accumulation, which is
toxic. Inspect stored potatoes regularly and remove any showing rot
to prevent spread. Properly cured main crop potatoes can store for
4-8 months under these conditions.
