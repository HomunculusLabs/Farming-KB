# Aleph-2

## Overview

Aleph-2 (2,5-dimethoxy-4-(2-fluoroethylthio)phenethylamine) is a
psychedelic phenethylamine documented in PIHKAL as compound #24. It is a
member of the Aleph series, Shulgin's exploration of thioether
(sulfur-containing) substituents at the 4-position of the 2,5-
dimethoxyphenethylamine core. The Aleph series combines the sulfur chemistry
of the 2C-T series with fluorine substitution.

The Aleph compounds are essentially 2C-T series analogues with an additional
fluoroethyl extension on the sulfur atom. In Aleph-2's case, the 4-position
carries a SCH2CH2F (2-fluoroethylthio) group, combining the thioether
linkage found in the 2C-T compounds with a fluorine-terminated ethyl chain.
This dual heteroatom approach was novel and yielded compounds with unique
pharmacological properties.

## Chemical Structure and Classification

Aleph-2's full chemical name is
2,5-dimethoxy-4-(2-fluoroethylthio)phenethylamine. The core structure is
the familiar 2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine found in the 2C series, with the
4-position occupied by a thioether group to which a 2-fluoroethyl chain is
attached. The molecular weight is approximately 275.35 g/mol.

The presence of both sulfur and fluorine atoms makes this one of the more
structurally complex compounds in the phenethylamine class. The thioether
linkage is shared with the 2C-T series, while the fluorine terminus
introduces electronegativity that distinguishes the Aleph compounds. The
Aleph series explores combinations of thioether substitution with various
halogenated alkyl chains: Aleph-1 has a methylthioethyl group, Aleph-2 the
fluoroethylthio group, and other Aleph compounds explore different
chain lengths and terminal functional groups.

## Dosage and Administration

Shulgin's recommended oral dosage for Aleph-2 ranges from 5 to 10
milligrams. This places it in a moderate potency range, more potent than
the base 2C-T compound but less potent than the heavier thioalkyl
substituted compounds. A threshold dose is reported at approximately 3 to 5
mg, while doses above 10 mg produce increasingly intense and potentially
uncomfortable effects.

Onset occurs within 1 to 2 hours after oral administration. The experience
peaks at approximately 2 to 4 hours, with total duration ranging from 6 to
8 hours. This duration profile is somewhat shorter than many 2C-T
compounds, which may be related to the metabolic lability of the
fluoroethyl group.

The relatively rapid onset and moderate duration make Aleph-2 somewhat
more manageable than longer-acting thioether compounds.

## Effects Profile

Aleph-2 produces a [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]] that combines elements of both
the 2C-T series and compounds with fluorine substituents. Visual effects
include moderate geometric patterning, color enhancement, and perceptual
sharpening. The visual character has been described as "clean" and
"well-defined" without the overwhelming intensity that can characterize
high-dose 2C-T-7 or 2C-T-2 experiences.

Cognitive effects include enhanced introspection, mild euphoria, and
increased aesthetic appreciation. The headspace is generally described as
manageable and controllable, without the turbulence that can accompany
more potent phenethylamines. Some users report a quality of mental clarity
and focus that distinguishes Aleph-2 from other members of the thioether
series.

Physical effects include moderate stimulation, mydriasis, and appetite
suppression. The thioether group is associated with characteristic body
load in the 2C-T series, and Aleph-2 retains some of this quality, though
the fluorine modification appears to moderate it somewhat. Nausea is
possible during the onset phase but tends to resolve as the experience
develops.
