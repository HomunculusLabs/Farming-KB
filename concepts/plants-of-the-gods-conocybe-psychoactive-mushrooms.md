## Historical Documentation

Dr. Francisco Hernandez, personal physician to the King of Spain, wrote
that three kinds of intoxicating mushrooms were worshiped. After
describing a lethal species, he stated that "others when eaten cause
not death but madness that on occasion is lasting, of which the symptom
is a kind of uncontrolled laughter. Usually called teyuintli, these
are deep yellow, acrid and of a not displeasing freshness." He also
described mushrooms that "bring before the eyes all kinds of visions,
such as wars and the likeness of demons" and others "desired by princes
for their fiestas and banquets, of great price."

For four centuries, nothing more was known of the mushroom cult, and
it was even doubted that mushrooms were used hallucinogenically in
ceremony. In 1916, an American botanist proposed that Teonanacatl and
the Peyote were the same drug, arguing that the dried brownish crown
of Peyote resembles a dried mushroom so remarkably that it would
deceive even a mycologist. It was not until the 1930s that the role of
hallucinogenic mushrooms in Mexico began to be understood.
