## Bee Range Design

Bee range design is the deliberate planning and management of forage, shelter, and hive placement to support healthy, productive honeybee colonies. Drawing from [[permaculture]]: A Designer's Manual*, this concept integrates bee ecology with landscape design to maximise both hive products and pollination services.

## Bee Products and Services

Honeybees yield a suite of valuable products and perform an essential ecological function:

- **Honey** — the primary carbohydrate harvest, varying widely in flavour and character depending on forage species
- **Beeswax** — used in candles, cosmetics, polishes, and comb foundation
- **Pollen** — a protein-rich resource collected as a dietary supplement; hives should aim to yield the same weight in pollen as in honey
- **Propolis** — a resinous antimicrobial sealant gathered from specific tree and plant sources
- **Royal jelly** — the specialised food that determines queen development
- **Pollination services** — arguably the most economically significant output; bees pollinate food, oil, fruit, and seed crops including mustard, clovers, buckwheat, smallfruit, apples, and grain legumes

Crops within 1 mile (1.6 km) of hives that depend on bee pollination outyield bee-deficient areas by **3–10×**. Key beneficiaries include kiwifruit, bramble berries, smallfruit, mustards, Brassicas, clovers, apples, buckwheat, [[salix-discolor]]*), one of the earliest and most reliable pollen sources
- **Acacia species** — early-flowering, high-protein pollen
- **Pines** — wind-pollinated but bees readily collect pine pollen
- **Vine crops** — provide early nectar and pollen in mild climates

## Propolis Sources

Propolis is essential for hive hygiene and structural integrity. Bees gather resins from:

- **Xanthorrheas** (grass trees) — especially in Australian contexts
- **Poplars** — particularly *Populus* buds, a major source in temperate regions
- **Pines** — resin from bark and buds

Including these species within or near the bee range ensures a reliable propolis supply.

## Water Requirements

### Cooling and Thermoregulation

Clustered hives require large quantities of water for evaporative cooling. Bees regulate hive temperature as a **super organism**, ventilating in a roughly **1.2-minute rhythm** to maintain an internal temperature of approximately **21°C**. In hot summers, adequate shade and ventilation become critical.

### Water Access Design

Providing safe water access prevents drowning and predation:

- Place **soaked mats or hessian** at pond edges to give bees a landing surface
- Use **small ponds or troughs** that are free of dragonflies, which are significant bee predators
- Position water sources in full sun to encourage evaporation and warm water uptake
- Ensure water is available within the hive's foraging range — bees expend significant energy collecting water

## Hive Insulation and Climate Adaptation

### Cold Climates

Insulation is essential for overwintering success. Traditional approaches include:

- **Thatched attic hives** (Denmark) — thatch provides excellent insulation and moisture regulation
- **Insulated hive houses** — purpose-built shelters that buffer temperature extremes
- Windbreaks and sun traps to reduce heat loss

### Hot Climates

In hot summers, bees need:

- Shade from afternoon sun
- Enhanced ventilation through elevated entrances and vent boards
- Reliable water sources close to the hive
- Reflective or light-coloured hive surfaces

## Design Principles Summary

1. **Plan for the full season** — 35+ forage species ensuring continuous nectar and pollen flow from early spring through late autumn
2. **Cluster your forage** — hedgerows, fields, and clumps outperform scattered plantings
3. **Protect from wind** — low hedgerows (0.5–1 m) between hive and forage are essential
4. **Provide safe water** — soaked mats at pond edges, no dragonfly habitat
5. **Space commercial sites 5 km apart** — to limit disease transmission
6. **Match shelter to climate** — protect hives but do not create microclimates that tempt bees out in lethal weather
7. **Target equal pollen and honey yields** — pollen traps at peak periods, diverse protein sources
