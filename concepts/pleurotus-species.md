## Major Cultivated Species

### Pleurotus ostreatus (Pearl Oyster)

The classic [[saprotrophic-fungi]]. Fruit bodies are fan-shaped with white to gray
caps, short off-center stems, and white gills that run down the stem
(decurrent). See [[biological-efficiency]] often exceeding
100% on supplemented straw substrates. See [[pleurotus-pulmonarius|Pleurotus pulmonarius]] ([[growing-gourmet-phoenix-oyster-pulmonarius-cultivation]] / Indian Oyster)

Very similar to P. ostreatus but prefers warmer temperatures (20-24 degrees
Celsius). Cap color ranges from white to pale tan. More tolerant of warmer
conditions, making it popular in subtropical and tropical regions. Often
confused with P. ostreatus in the wild.

### [[pleurotus-djamor|Pleurotus djamor]] (Pink Oyster)

A tropical species producing vivid pink to salmon-colored fruit bodies. Very
fast growing at warm temperatures (22-26 degrees Celsius). The intense color
fades to pale pink or tan when cooked. Extremely aggressive colonizer but
fruits have a very short shelf life (2-3 days) compared to other species.

### [[pleurotus-cystidiosus|Pleurotus cystidiosus]] (Abalone Oyster)

Named for the abalone-like appearance and texture of its fruit bodies. Native
to subtropical Asia. Produces a unique extracellular mucilage during
colonization and has an anamorphic (asexual) stage called Antromycopsis. Prefers
warmer temperatures of 24-27 degrees Celsius.

## Less Common Species

- **P. cornucopiae** (Branching Oyster) -- forms clustered, branching caps
- **P. salmoneostramineus** -- a salmon-colored variant closely related to P.
  djamor
- **P. nebrodensis** (White [[growing-gourmet-king-oyster-eryngii-cultivation]]) -- rare Italian endemic, similar to
  P. eryngii with a pale cap
- **P. tuber-regium** (King Tuber Oyster) -- produces edible sclerotia
- **P. floridanus** -- a warm-climate species from the southeastern United
  States

## Species Selection Considerations

Choosing which species to cultivate depends on:

- **Climate** -- matching species temperature preferences to available
  conditions reduces energy costs for climate control
- **Market demand** -- P. eryngii commands premium prices; pink oysters attract
  attention at farmers markets
- **Substrate availability** -- most species grow on similar substrates but
  performance varies
- **Shelf life requirements** -- P. eryngii and P. ostreatus have the longest
  shelf life; P. djamor the shortest
- **Growth speed** -- P. djamor and P. citrinopileatus are the fastest from
  inoculation to harvest
