# Cannabis Trichome Development and Cannabinoid Biosynthesis Sites

## Introduction

[[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] — cannabinoids, terpenes, and flavonoids. Understanding trichome development, anatomy, and biosynthetic activity is fundamental to [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]] but accelerates dramatically during the [[jasmonic-acid-plant-defense-signaling]] promote trichome initiation. Ethylene and abscisic acid modulate maturation.

### Stalk Elongation and Gland Formation

After initiation, the trichome cell undergoes a series of developmental changes:

1. **Cell division**: The initial trichome cell divides to form the basal cell, stalk cells, and precursor to the glandular head.
2. **Stalk elongation**: In capitate-stalked trichomes, the stalk elongates through coordinated cell division and expansion. Stalk length varies by cultivar and environmental conditions — longer stalks expose the gland head further from the leaf surface, potentially improving terpene volatility and pollinator attraction.
3. **Head expansion**: The glandular head forms as secretory disc cells proliferate and expand. The cuticle covering the head separates from the underlying cell wall, creating a subcuticular storage space that fills with cannabinoid-terpene resin.

### Maturation

Trichome maturation is visually assessed by color change:

- **Clear/translucent**: Immature trichomes with low cannabinoid content. The secretory cells are actively synthesizing but have not yet accumulated significant resin.
- **Cloudy/milky**: Peak cannabinoid production. The resin is accumulating rapidly and the gland is at maximum biosynthetic activity. Cloudy trichomes correspond to peak THC levels in THC-dominant cultivars.
- **Amber/golden**: THC is beginning to degrade to CBN (cannabinol). In THC-dominant cultivars, amber trichomes indicate late harvest with more sedative effects. In CBD-dominant cultivars, amber coloration may indicate peak CBD maturity.

The timing of harvest relative to trichome maturity is the single most important factor determining the psychoactive profile and [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]**: Possibly involved in defense of the trichome itself.

### Subcuticular Space

The space between the secretory cell wall and the overlying cuticle is the resin storage depot. As cannabinoids and terpenes are synthesized by the secretory cells, they are transported across the cell wall and accumulate in this subcuticular space. The cuticle acts as a semi-permeable membrane, retaining the resin while allowing volatile terpenes to slowly diffuse outward (contributing to the characteristic cannabis aroma).

The cuticle can rupture under mechanical pressure, releasing the resin. This is why trichomes are easily dislodged during handling and why dry-sifting and ice-water extraction techniques are effective for collecting trichome heads (kief, hashish).

### Resin Composition

The resin stored in the subcuticular space is a complex mixture:

- **Cannabinoids** (20–35% of resin by weight in high-potency flowers): THC, CBD, CBC, CBG, THCV, CBDV, and their carboxylic acid precursors (THCA, CBDA, etc.)
- **Monoterpenes** (5–15%): Myrcene, limonene, α-pinene, β-pinene, linalool, terpinolene, ocimene
- **Sesquiterpenes** (3–10%): β-caryophyllene, α-humulene, nerolidol, bisabolol
- **Other compounds**: Flavonoids (cannaflavin A, B, C), fatty acids, sterols, alkanes
