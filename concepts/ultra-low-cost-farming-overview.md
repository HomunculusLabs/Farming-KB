# Ultra-Low-Cost Farming Overview

## Core Philosophy

Ultra-low-cost (ULC) farming, as developed by JADAM (Jayonul Damun Saramdul, meaning "people who are like nature"), is a revolutionary approach to agriculture that seeks to reduce farming costs by 10 to 100 times compared to [[jadam-pest-control-with-natural-pesticides-jhs]] from locally available herbs, oils, and minerals

## Applicability

ULC farming methods can be applied across all types of agriculture: conventional farms, hydroponics, commercial operations, large-scale plantations, and home or urban gardens. The system is designed to be universally accessible, requiring only locally available materials and simple techniques.

## Indigenous Microorganisms: The Foundation

A cornerstone of ULC farming is the use of indigenous microorganisms collected from local leaf mold. In nature, all microorganisms are beneficial because they all serve functions in decomposition and nutrient cycling. Leaf mold — the dark, crumbly soil found under piles of decomposing leaves in forests and fields — is described as "black gold" teeming with locally adapted microorganisms. These microbes are the best workers for local farms because they have already adapted to the regional environment. JADAM teaches farmers to collect this leaf mold and use it as a starter for microorganism culture, creating JADAM Indigenous [[jadam-wetting-agent-jwa]] (JWA)**: An environment-friendly surfactant made from canola oil and caustic potash. Wetting agents are essential for pesticides and foliar applications to properly coat plant surfaces.
- **JADAM Sulfur (JS)**: A powerful germicide made simply from sulfur and other permitted ingredients.

Both are unpatented and openly shared so all farmers can benefit.
