## Two Distinct Distribution Phenotypes

The data reveal two fundamentally different alkaloid distribution patterns:

1. **Cap-enriched psilocybin with stem-localized psilocin** (M.R. and Equadorian): Caps contain
roughly 1.6 times the psilocybin concentration of stems. Minimal psilocin is present and is confined
to stem tissue. This pattern was shared by both non-Amazon strains, suggesting it may be the more
common phenotype among domesticated strains.

2. **Equal distribution with cap-localized psilocin** (Amazon): Caps and stems contain identical
psilocybin levels. A trace of psilocin appears in the cap rather than the stem. This unique pattern
suggests different regulation of both psilocybin biosynthesis and its dephosphorylation to psilocin
in this tropical strain.

## Absolute Potency Comparison

Comparing the three strains on total psilocybin content (weighted average of cap and stem), the
ranking was: Equadorian approximately 6.2 mg/g (highest), Amazon approximately 5.7 mg/g, and M.R.
approximately 5.5 mg/g (lowest). The differences between strains were relatively modest (within a
factor of 1.1), suggesting that total psilocybin content is more strongly influenced by growing
conditions and flush number than by strain genetics.

## Implications for Dosage Estimation

The approximately 1.6-fold concentration difference between caps and stems in two of three strains
means that consuming whole mushrooms versus caps-only preparations would yield meaningfully
different alkaloid intakes. For the M.R. and Equadorian strains, caps-only consumption would deliver
approximately 1.3 times the total psilocybin per gram compared to whole mushroom consumption. The
strain-dependent nature of this distribution means generalized potency estimates based on one strain
cannot be reliably applied to another.

## Biosynthetic Considerations

The finding that psilocin localization between cap and stem is strain-dependent suggests that the
enzymatic machinery for psilocybin dephosphorylation to psilocin is differentially expressed across
fruiting body tissues in a strain-specific manner. The phosphatase enzyme responsible for converting
psilocybin to psilocin appears to be regulated differently in each strain. This has direct
