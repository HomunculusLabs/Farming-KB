# Stable Carbon Isotope Labelling in Signature Fatty Acids for Mycorrhizal Carbon Tracking

## Overview

A technique based on stable carbon (C) isotope labelling and compound-specific isotope ratio mass spectrometry can estimate carbon flow between different soil organism groups. The neutral lipid fatty acid 16:1 omega 5 is particularly useful for detecting and estimating carbon flow to [[singh-mycorrhizal-network-carbon-transfer-plants]] to saprophytic microorganisms.

## Conceptual Model

Carbon flow in the rhizosphere can be described using pools and fluxes:
- **Pools (boxes)**: Above-ground plant biomass, roots, AM fungal intraradical mycelium, AM fungal extraradical mycelium, spores, saprophytic microorganisms, soil organic matter
- **Fluxes (arrows)**: Carbon allocation between pools, including respiration losses
- During microbial growth, an added C substrate is used approximately 50% for energy production and 50% for biomass components (including storage)

## Signature Fatty Acids for Microbial Biomass

### AM Fungi
- **Neutral lipid fatty acid (NLFA) 16:1 omega 5**: Storage lipid in vesicles (inside roots) and spores (external mycelium)
- AM fungi do NOT contain ergosterol (unlike saprophytic fungi)
- Neutral lipids comprise approximately 20% of biomass of hyphae, vesicles, and spores
- Total 13C incorporation in AM fungal hyphae correlates with NLFA 16:1 omega 5 enrichment

### Saprophytic Fungi
- **Ergosterol**: Specific to saprophytic fungi
- **PLFA 18:2 omega 6,9**: Phospholipid fatty acid marker for saprotrophic fungi

### Bacteria
- **10 specific PLFAs**: Used to estimate bacterial biomass

### PLFAs vs. NLFAs
- The phosphate group of phospholipids is rapidly released by enzymatic action in soil
- PLFAs reflect occurrence of mainly [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]]
- Neutral lipids (storage) also degrade rapidly when fungal hyphae senesce
- The dynamics of 13C-labelled signature fatty acids can track carbon flux from plants to both intra- and extraradical AM fungal tissues

## 13C Labelling Approaches

### Timing Considerations
- Timing is critical because carbon is continually respired
- AM fungal C is largely stored in lipids in vesicles and spores, which may be stored for long periods
- Labelled respiration from AM fungal mycelium ceased within 7 days after pulse-labelling (Johnson et al. 2002)
- Similar enrichment levels in extraradical and intraradical mycelia after 7 days indicates sufficient time for C translocation
- Recently assimilated C appeared in extraradical AM fungal hyphae within a few days and remained for at least 30 days (Olsson and Johnson 2005)
- Higher 13C enrichment in intraradical mycelia lipids than in plant root lipids shows lipid metabolism is more active in fungal intraradical structures

### Monoxenic AM Cultures
- Established in 1- or multi-compartmental Petri dishes
- Supplied with sterile filtered [13C] D-glucose solution (7 mg glucose per dish, U-13C6, 99% 13C)
- Glucose applied in a ring 5 mm outside the initial inoculum plug
- Solid MM medium with only hyphae can be cut out and dissolved in 10 mM Na citrate
- Separated into intraradical (root-associated) and extraradical (medium-only) fractions

### Field Labelling
- 13CO2 pulse-labelling of plant canopies
- Allows tracking of recently fixed carbon through the plant-soil-microbe continuum
- Requires careful timing for sampling relative to pulse application
