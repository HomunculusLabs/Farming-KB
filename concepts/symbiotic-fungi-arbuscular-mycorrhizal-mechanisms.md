## Arbuscule Biochemistry

The arbuscule is the main site for nutrient exchange. Key enzymatic markers include:

- **Alkaline phosphatase (ALP)**: expressed in arbuscules; relates to phosphorus uptake
  efficiency and sugar metabolism
- **Acid phosphatase (ACP)**: also associated with AM fungal structures
- **Succinate dehydrogenase (SDH)**: indicates [[glomus-intraradices]]**: alkaline phosphatase activity normally higher than acid
  phosphatase in extraradical mycelium; acid phosphatase does not respond to external
  P content; alkaline phosphatase increases at high P content
- **[[mycelial-network-structure]] parameters:

- Hyphal density: 2.9-6.8 mm/mm2 (cotton significantly higher than lettuce or eggplant)
- Branches: 8.6-9.7 per cm of hypha
- Anastomosis frequency: 75-78% of hyphal contacts
- Viability: 100% of network viable after 7 days (confirmed by SDH staining)

## Anastomosis and Self-Recognition

Anastomosis (hyphal fusion) depends on highly regulated self-recognition mechanisms:

- Hyphal tips fuse with nearby hyphae in approximately 35 minutes
- Bidirectional particle flow (vacuoles, mitochondria, nuclei, fat droplets) at
  1.8 micrometers per second through hyphal bridges
- Successful anastomoses occur within same individual and between individuals of same
  isolate
- Hyphae from different genera, species, or geographically distinct isolates show
  rejection responses
- Interactions between Gigaspora and Scutellospora hyphae never lead to anastomosis
- Geographically different glomus mosseae isolates never produce anastomoses,
