# Pig Range Systems

**Pig range systems** are free-range management approaches for domestic pigs (*Sus scrofa domesticus*) within integrated permaculture designs. [[bill-mollison]] presents pigs as the third critical animal species — alongside bees and poultry — for domestic self-reliance, emphasising their role in land preparation, waste recycling, orchard cleanup, and high-quality meat production.

## Advantages Over Confined Systems

Free-range pigs offer several distinct advantages over shed-based or feedlot production:

- **Improved health** — exposure to sunlight, fresh air, diverse forage, and natural behaviour reduces disease incidence compared to confined systems
- **Lower feeding costs** — pigs supplement grain with roots, tubers, grubs, fallen fruit, pasture legumes, and food wastes
- **Superior meat quality** — free-range pork contains less saturated fat and a more favourable fatty-acid profile, making it healthier for human consumption than meat from confined animals fed controlled rations
- **Land management services** — rooting behaviour clears ground for future plantings, controls persistent weeds, and incorporates organic matter into the soil

## Site Selection and Design

The ideal pig range is:

- **Wooded** — trees provide shade, shelter, and supplementary forage (acorns, fallen fruit)
- **Well-drained** — mud and wet soils encourage worms and disease, and can cause problems with suckling piglets
- **Dry in wet seasons** — this may require careful attention to contour, drainage, and site elevation

Fencing represents the **greatest capital expense** in pig range establishment, though this has been substantially reduced by the adoption of electric fencing. Pigs are strong, persistent, and intelligent — fence design must account for their ability to test and defeat weak barriers.

### Winter Considerations

In very cold winter climates, **winter shedding** may be necessary. Sheltered ranges with good dry beds help mitigate cold stress. Various small or one large shelter sheds are used on rearing ranges, with thatched or insulated construction preferred. In the tropics, dense thickets provide sufficient natural shelter.

## Farrowing and Breeding Management

A **farrowing pen** is occupied by a sow and her litter for nearly **3 months**. After use, each pen should be **rested for 6 months** before the next occupancy, allowing vegetation recovery and parasite cycle interruption. With this rest period, each pen can support **two sows per year**.

## Infrastructure and Comfort Features

### Rubbing Posts

Strong diagonal posts wrapped in **hessian (burlap)** serve as rubbing places, satisfying the pigs' natural need to scratch against solid objects. The hessian can be kept dampened with:

- **Sump oil** (waste engine oil)
- **Vegetable oils** with added **neem** (*[[azadirachta-indica]]*) leaf extract
- **Melia azedarach** (white cedar) preparations
- **Pyrethrum oil**

These treatments provide natural ectoparasite control, keeping pigs free of lice and external parasites.

### Dust Baths

Dust baths consist of a roofed structure over a soft, dry area. The dry dust kills lice through desiccation and abrasion. For enhanced parasite control, dust baths can be laced with:

- Dried **neem tree leaves**
- **Pyrethrum flowers**

### Wallow Areas

Restricted wallows provide comfort during hot weather. When rested and planted with **cattails** (*Typha* spp.), wallows produce excellent root and shoot foods available at any time of year. **Duck potato** (*Sagittaria* spp.) can be planted in pond margins for summer-dry forage. Muddy, unmanaged wallows should be avoided as they promote disease and parasite burdens.

## Nutrition and Forage

### Pasture Composition

Good range pasture consists of:

- **Legumes** — clover, lucerne (alfalfa)
- **Herbaceous perennials** — comfrey, chicory
- **Young grasses** — preferably grazed by horses or cattle, or mown, as pigs will not eat or thrive on coarse, mature grasses

Pigs consume approximately **11 kg of wet-weight forage per day**, with larger appetites than confined animals. Good quality green feed halves the necessity for protein meal and purchased grains.

### Sow Nutrition During Lactation

- A sow in milk requires **3.5 kg of dry rations** when piglets are newborn
- Requirements increase to **5.5 kg of dry rations** when piglets are 2–3 weeks old

### Supplementary Feeding Sources

Pigs are most economically kept where some dairy, orchard, root crop, or meat wastes are available. They do well on:

- Restaurant and household food scraps
- Dairy waste (whey, skim milk)
- Orchard windfalls and processing residues
- Root crop waste

Pigs will not eat or thrive on coarse grasses alone, so grazing by beef cattle or horses on the same range — or mechanical mowing — may be necessary to maintain pasture quality.

## Meat Quality and Finishing

Free-range pigs are not always suitable for **bacon production**, as their softer fats may not cure properly. For bacon, grain-feeding for **2–4 weeks** before slaughter hardens (saturates) the fats. However, for **fresh or frozen pork**, the softer fats from free-range animals are entirely suitable and are considered healthier for human consumption.

Diet strongly influences fat composition. High intake of vegetable oils from sources such as avocados, oil palms, olives, or oily fish will produce softer fats. Conversely, grain-based finishing produces firmer fat suitable for curing.

## Range Establishment

Preparing a new free-range pig area involves:

1. **Rip and lime** the ground to break compaction and adjust pH
2. **Sow** a good grass-legume mix
3. **Plant** comfrey, sunroot (*Helianthus tuberosus*), and arrowroot (*Canna* spp.) pieces pushed into the rip lines for drought-resistant forage
4. **Plant trees** just outside fences and in corners protected by electric fencing, providing shade, shelter, and future forage (acorns, chestnuts, fallen fruit)

## Integration with Other Systems

Pig range systems integrate effectively with:

- **Orchards** — pigs clean up windfall fruits that would otherwise breed pests such as fruit fly and codling moth
- **Dairy operations** — whey and dairy waste provide high-quality feed
- **Poultry** — poultry following pigs in rotation access disturbed soil and uncovered invertebrates
- **Crop rotations** — pig rooting clears and aerates ground for subsequent vegetable or grain plantings
- **Aquaculture** — pond margins planted with duck potato and cattails serve pig wallows and fish habitat simultaneously
