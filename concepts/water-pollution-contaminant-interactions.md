## Water Pollution and Contaminant Interactions

The purification of polluted water is one of the most critical challenges in [[permaculture-design]] and settlement planning. The only long-term insurance of a good water supply to a settlement is rigorous control of a forested catchment, including a total ban on biocides and metallic processing. As there are few such clean areas left in the world, alternative strategies must be employed. The fundamental [[permaculture]] principle here is that the only water safe for humans is also safe for other living things. For millennia, humans existed on water supplies containing healthy plants and fish — and if we keep natural waters free of faecal and industrial contaminants, we can continue to do so.

## The Technological Fix Problem

The 30-40 additives commonly introduced into municipal water supplies are often pollutants in themselves. These additives represent the endpoint of the technological fix: pollution is "fixed" by further pollution. For the increasingly sensitive sector of society developing allergies to modern pollutants, each additional chemical treatment compounds the problem.

## Categories of Water Contaminants

### Turbidity
Silt and fine particles suspended in water. While not directly toxic, turbidity reduces light penetration, interferes with filtration, and can carry adsorbed pollutants deeper into water systems.

### Bacterial and Organic Pollution
From sewage and decay products, including *E. coli*, disease organisms, viral and protozoan pathogens, and parasitic worm eggs. These represent the most immediate health threat in contaminated water supplies.

### Metallic Pollutants
Chromium, cadmium, lead, and mercury are the primary concerns. These metals accumulate in biological tissues and become increasingly concentrated as they move up the food chain (biomagnification).

### Biocides
Aldrin, Dieldrin, 2,4-D, 2,4,5-T, dioxin, PCB, organophosphates, and halogenated hydrocarbons. These synthetic compounds persist in the environment and many resist biological breakdown. Some, like DDT, are stable residual poisons that co-distil with water, meaning even distillation will not remove them.

### Excessive Fertiliser
Nitrogenous compounds, phosphates, sodium, and potassium salts in excess promote algal blooms and eutrophication, depleting dissolved oxygen and creating dead zones in waterways.

### Acids and Acid-Forming Compounds
A pH below 5.5 increases the solubility and toxicity of metallic pollutants. Acid rain is a particularly insidious problem because it sets in motion cascading environmental damage.

## Contaminant Interactions and Synergistic Effects

Contaminants rarely act in isolation — their interactions often produce effects far worse than the sum of individual impacts:

- **Acid rain** dissolves poisonous forms of aluminium, mercury, lead, cadmium, and selenium from rocks and soil. It also leaches copper, nickel, and lead from drinking tanks, teapots, and hot water tanks.
- **Biological conversion** of inorganic mercury to organic forms (as occurred in Minamata, Japan) creates methylmercury, which is readily absorbed by the body. Sewage in water aids this conversion.
- **Pesticide synergy**: Aldrin prevents DDT from being excreted by organisms. The combination is deadly, yet both chemicals can be purchased simultaneously or applied sequentially by farmers.
- **Fertiliser-acid-metal cascade**: Excessive artificial fertiliser increases aquatic biological activity, which results in greater uptake of metals in acidic waters.
- **Protein binding during cooking**: Aluminium and other metals can bind to proteins in vegetables and meat during boiling or steaming. Cooking may increase water content of metals by a factor of 5, and metals so bound become easy for the body to assimilate.
- **Asbestos pipe threat**: Many miles of asbestos pipe in public water supply systems pose a definite threat of both stomach and bladder cancer from asbestos particles.

## Water Treatment Chemicals: Irony of the Fix

Ferric and aluminium sulphate, salt, and lime are added to water to flocculate and settle out fine particles as clay. However, as pH increases due to acid rain, aluminium goes into solution and may bind to proteins. Even adding salt to cooking water to decrease these effects may not bring levels below the 30 µg/L allowable for those with kidney problems.

Ferric sulphate may be safer to use, especially if water is initially or reasonably alkaline. The broader lesson is that any inorganic or metallic salt deserves very cautious use in water treatment.

## The Downstream Responsibility Principle

The wastewaters of upstream settlements are the drinking waters of downstream areas. This creates an ethical and practical imperative: our duty is to release from any settlement only water of sufficiently good quality to be safely usable by others. This principle transforms sewage from a "disposal problem" into a design challenge — how to treat water through biological systems that produce clean effluent while also generating useful yields.

## Prevention Over Treatment

The permaculture approach emphasises that water treatment is far less effective than pollution prevention. The priority hierarchy is:

1. **Prevent contamination** at source through forested catchments and biocide bans
2. **Separate waste streams** at the domestic level (solids from liquids, greywater from blackwater)
3. **Treat biologically** using ponds, plants, and biological filtration
4. **Monitor continuously** using living indicators (mussels, fish, plants) as pollution sentinels
