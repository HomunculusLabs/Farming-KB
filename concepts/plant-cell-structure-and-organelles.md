# Plant Cell Structure and Organelles

The plant cell is the fundamental unit of plant life and the stage upon which all nutrient uptake and utilization occurs. Understanding cell structure is essential to understanding how plants eat.

## Cell Theory

Cell Theory holds that the cell is the smallest unit of life and that all living things are made up of one or more cells. Moreover, all cells come from pre-existing cells. Most plant cells range from 10 to 100 micrometers (0.0003937 to 0.003937 inches). A 20-year-old birch tree contains approximately 15-16 trillion cells.

## The Cell Wall

Plant cells are surrounded by a cell wall, a strong, lattice-like structure averaging around 20 nanometers thick. It is mostly made of cellulose -- the most common biologically produced substance on Earth, comprising about one-third of all vegetative matter. Cellulose is a complex molecule of many glucose molecules linked into polysaccharides woven into long fibrils that overlap to create layers.

The cell wall is extremely porous, like a luffa sponge. Special protein complexes spool out cellulose fibers along the outer membrane like a knitting machine. The synthesis requires enzymes, boron, and nutrients aggregated from the soil.

Additional components include hemicelluloses and pectins that bind cellulose together. In woody plants, a thicker secondary cell wall contains lignin and suberin (cork is essentially suberin).

### The Casparian Strip

In roots, a special set of endodermis cells have walls with a strip of suberin and waxes forming the Casparian strip. This prevents water from flowing freely through cell walls and forces it through the cell membrane, regulating what enters the plant's vascular system.

### The Apoplastic Pathway

Water and gas molecules can travel through connected cell walls without entering cells themselves, via the apoplastic pathway -- until they encounter the Casparian strip.

## The Plasmalemma (Cell Membrane)

The plasmalemma is a semi-permeable, semi-fluid membrane made of two sheets of phospholipid molecules. The water-loving phosphate heads face outward/inward while hydrophobic lipid tails form the inner layer. Integral membrane proteins (IMPs) make up 50-75% of the surface and serve as entryways for nutrients.

### Plasmodesmata

Tiny tunnels (50nm diameter, up to 100nm long) connecting every cell to its neighbors, forming the symplastic pathway. A single cell can have 1,000 to 100,000 plasmodesmata, with protein sphincters regulating passage.

### Aquaporins

Special proteins covering up to 10% of the membrane surface, transporting only water molecules using electrical fields -- one at a time, at extremely high speeds.

### Transport Proteins

Three categories: channel proteins (passive, gated tunnels), pump proteins (active, using energy), and carrier/cotransporter proteins (facilitative).

## Cytoplasm

All material inside the cell membrane except the nucleus. Cytosol (a clear jelly-like fluid) holds organelles and vesicles that serve as warehouses, trash cans, and toxin storage.

## Mitochondria

The powerhouses of the cell, producing ATP from sugars via cellular respiration. They have their own double membrane, their own DNA, and divide like bacteria -- evidence they were once free-living bacteria assimilated into plant cells. They account for up to 20% of a cell's volume and also synthesize phospholipids for cellular membranes and store calcium.

## Plastids

Three groups: chloroplasts (photosynthesis, green), leucoplasts (colorless, storage of starch/lipids/proteins), and chromoplasts (red/yellow/orange pigments for attracting pollinators). Chloroplasts contain thylakoids -- stacked, flattened membrane discs with chlorophyll-filled antennae that capture light energy.

## Ribosomes

Bead-shaped organelles (25-30nm) that serve as sites of protein synthesis. They read mRNA instructions and assemble amino acids (20 types) into proteins using tRNA delivery. They can be free-floating or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum.

## Endoplasmic Reticulum

A long folded membrane creating a maze of cavities (lumen). The rough ER has ribosomes attached for protein synthesis; the smooth ER handles lipid production, protein glycosylation, and toxin neutralization.

## Golgi Apparatus

Packaging and shipping centers (5-8 per cell) that modify, sort, tag with destination codes, and load materials into vesicles for transport to their final destinations -- inside or outside the cell.

## Vacuole and Tonoplast

The vacuole can make up 90% of a mature cell's volume, filled with cell sap (water, sugars, enzymes, proteins, organic acids, pigments, wastes). The tonoplast membrane has specialized transport proteins and regulates pH by pumping hydrogen ions. When it fails, cell death occurs.

## Lysosomes and Peroxisomes

Lysosomes are recycling centers containing enzymes (requiring pH ~5) that digest large molecules. Peroxisomes digest lipids and fats, help assimilate nitrogen, and metabolize hormones.

## The Nucleus

The command center holding DNA and RNA, surrounded by a double nuclear envelope with pores (covering up to 15% of surface). Contains the nucleolus where ribosome units and RNA are assembled.

## Microtubules and Actin Filaments

2000x thinner than a human hair, these provide structural support, serve as transportation infrastructure (like railroad tracks for molecular cargo), and function as the cell's communication network.
