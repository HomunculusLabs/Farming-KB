# Fungal Sensitivity Testing and Identification Fungal sensitivity testing evaluates the growth response of fungal isolates to antifungal compounds, environmental parameters, or selective agents. In taxonomic and ecological mycology, sensitivity profiles serve as supplementary characters for distinguishing morphologically similar species and assessing functional diversity within fungal communities. ## Principles Sensitivity testing in mycology operates on the same principles as antibiotic susceptibility testing in bacteriology. A standardized inoculum of the test fungus is exposed to a gradient of the test compound on solid or liquid media, and growth is measured relative to an untreated control. Results are typically expressed as: - **Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC)**: The lowest concentration that completely prevents visible growth - **EC50**: The concentration that reduces growth by 50% relative to the control - **Growth rate ratios**: Linear extension or biomass accumulation expressed as a proportion of the control In contrast to clinical mycology (where sensitivity testing guides antifungal therapy), ecological and taxonomic sensitivity testing uses antifungal compounds as tools to probe fungal physiology, characterize species boundaries, and understand community dynamics. ## Selective Agents in Taxonomic Mycology Several antifungal compounds are used routinely in fungal identification and isolation: ### Benomyl (Methyl 1-(butylcarbamoyl)-2-benzimidazolecarbamate) Benomyl sensitivity is one of the most widely used taxonomic characters in fungal systematics.
