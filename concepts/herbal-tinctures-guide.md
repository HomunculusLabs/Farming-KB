# Herbal Tinctures Guide

Tinctures are concentrated liquid herbal extracts made by soaking plant material in alcohol (or glycerin) to extract medicinal compounds. They are the backbone of a home herbal apothecary — portable, long-lasting, fast-acting, and potent.

## What Is a Tincture?

A tincture is a solution of herbal constituents dissolved in a solvent (menstruum). The standard tincture ratio is expressed as 1:5 (1 part dried herb by weight to 5 parts solvent by volume) or 1:2 for fresh herbs. Alcohol is the primary solvent because it extracts both water-soluble and alcohol-soluble compounds effectively.

## Advantages of Tinctures

- Long shelf life: 2-5+ years (alcohol-based)
- Concentrated: small doses deliver therapeutic amounts
- Fast absorption: liquid form enters the bloodstream quickly
- Portable: small dropper bottles for travel
- No refrigeration required (alcohol-based)
- Preserves fresh plant chemistry better than drying

## Equipment

- Glass mason jars with tight-fitting lids (half-pint to quart)
- Finely chopped or powdered dried herb (or fresh herb)
- High-proof alcohol (80-190 proof)
- Kitchen scale (digital, measures grams)
- Measuring cup and funnel
- Dark amber dropper bottles (1 oz and 2 oz)
- Fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth
- Labels and [[permanent-marker]]

## Alcohol Selection

| Alcohol | Proof | Best For |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Vodka (80-100 proof) | 40-50% | Most herbs, beginners |
| Everclear (190 proof) | 95% | Roots, resins, hard-to-extract compounds |
| Brandy | 40-50% | Pleasant taste, children's formulas |
- Rum | 40-50% | Bitter herbs, tropical herbs |

Dilute 190-proof alcohol with distilled water to achieve the desired percentage. Many herbs extract best at 40-60% alcohol. Resinous herbs (myrrh, propolis) and roots need higher percentages (70-95%).

## Basic Tincture Method (Folk Method)

The folk method is simple and practical for home use:

1. Fill a glass jar 1/3 to 1/2 full with dried herb (or pack full with chopped fresh herb)
2. Pour alcohol over the herb until completely covered, plus 1-2 inches extra
3. Cap tightly and label with: herb name, alcohol %, date
4. Shake daily for the first week, then weekly
5. Store in a cool, dark place for 4-6 weeks
6. Strain through cheesecloth into a clean jar, squeezing well
7. Pour into amber dropper bottles, label

## Weight-to-Volume Method (Professional)

For consistent, reproducible results:

1. Weigh the dried herb (e.g., 100g)
2. Calculate solvent volume: 100g x 5 (for 1:5 ratio) = 500ml
3. Combine herb and solvent in jar
4. Follow steps 3-7 from folk method

### Ratio Guide by Herb Type

| Herb Type | Ratio (Dried) | Alcohol % |
|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| Leaves, flowers | 1:4 or 1:5 | 40-50% |
| Roots, bark | 1:4 or 1:5 | 50-70% |
| Seeds | 1:4 | 60-70% |
| Fresh herb | 1:2 | 50-60% (account for water in fresh plant) |
| Resins, gums | 1:5 | 90-95% |

## Fresh vs. Dried Herb Tinctures

### Fresh Herb Tinctures

Advantages: Capture volatile oils and water-soluble compounds lost in drying. Some herbs (milky oat tops, fresh valerian) are significantly more effective when tinctured fresh.

- Pack jar full with chopped fresh herb (do not wash if possible, or dry thoroughly)
- Fill with alcohol (usually 50-60% to account for water content in fresh plant)
- Use 1:2 ratio (1 part fresh herb to 2 parts solvent by weight)

### Dried Herb Tinctures

Advantages: Consistent potency, available year-round, easier to measure precisely, standard in commercial herbalism.

- Use 1:4 or 1:5 ratio
- Powdered herb extracts faster than chopped

## Dual Extraction

For mushrooms and herbs with both water-soluble and alcohol-soluble compounds:

1. Make alcohol tincture as usual
2. Take the marc (spent herb) and simmer in water for 30-60 minutes
3. Strain the water decoction
4. Combine alcohol tincture and water decoction
5. Optional: evaporate some alcohol to concentrate further

Used for: reishi, chaga, turkey tail, [[ashwagandha]], astragalus

## Glycerites (Alcohol-Free Tinctures)

Glycerites use vegetable glycerin as the solvent — suitable for children, recovering alcoholics, and those avoiding alcohol.

### Recipe

1. Fill jar 1/3 full with dried herb
2. Mix 3 parts glycerin with 1 part distilled water
3. Pour over herb to cover
4. Follow same maceration process as alcohol tinctures (shake daily, 4-6 weeks)
5. Strain into amber bottles

### Notes

- Glycerin extracts fewer compounds than alcohol — weaker tinctures
- Shelf life: 1-2 years
- Sweeter taste — good for children
- Some herbs do not extract well in glycerin (resinous herbs, some alkaloids)

## Percolation Method (Advanced)

Percolation produces a finished tincture in 24-48 hours instead of 4-6 weeks:

1. Moisten dried herb with alcohol to damp consistency, let sit 1 hour
2. Pack into a percolation cone (glass or stainless steel)
3. Add alcohol to cover herb, let sit 12-24 hours to macerate
4. Slowly drip additional alcohol through the herb at 1-2 drops per second
5. Collect the percolate until volume matches the desired ratio

Requires practice to get the drip rate right. Produces clearer, more standardized tinctures.

## Dosing Guidelines

Standard adult dose: 20-60 drops (1/4 to 1 dropperful), 2-4 times daily.

### By Body Weight (General Guideline)

- Under 150 lbs: 20-30 drops
- 150-200 lbs: 30-50 drops
