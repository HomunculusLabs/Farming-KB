## Organic Gardening Techniques (Geoff Hamilton)

Practical techniques from Geoff Hamilton's organic gardening encyclopedia. Hamilton was a BBC Gardeners' World presenter and advocate of no-chemical gardening. This page covers techniques NOT already detailed in existing wiki pages. See [[composting-systems]] for composting methods, [[seed-starting-reference]] for propagation, [[greenhouse-design]] for protected growing, and [[orchard-management]] for fruit trees.

## The No-Dig Approach

Hamilton's core philosophy aligns with [[no-till-gardening-method]] but adds specific techniques:

### Deep Bed System
- Double-dig only once at establishment — never again
- Beds 4-5 feet wide, any length, with permanent paths between
- Raised 4-6 inches above path level using soil from paths
- Annual top-dressing with compost maintains soil structure
- Never walk on beds — soil compaction is the enemy
- Roots penetrate 50% deeper in undisturbed soil vs. regularly dug soil

### Benefits Observed Over Decades
- Soil structure improves year on year without intervention
- Worm population increases dramatically (50+ per sq ft in established beds)
- Water retention doubles compared to dug beds
- Weed seeds that are buried stay dormant; surface-applied compost introduces few new weeds
- Plant health improves as mycorrhizal networks remain undisturbed

## Seasonal Calendar (UK Temperate — Adjust for Zone 7-8 by ~6 weeks)

### Spring (March-May)
- **March**: Prune gooseberries, currants; plant early potatoes, onion sets, shallots; sow radish, parsnip, beetroot under cloches; start tomatoes, peppers indoors
- **April**: Sow carrots (avoid freshly manured soil — forked roots), lettuce, spinach, peas, broad beans direct; harden off brassicas; plant asparagus crowns
- **May**: Sow French beans, sweetcorn, courgettes, marrows, pumpkins; plant out tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers after frost risk passes; successional sow lettuce, radish, carrots

### Summer (June-August)
- **June**: Plant out brassicas for autumn harvest; earth up potatoes; sow winter radish, fennel, endive; pinch out tomato side shoots
- **July**: Last sowings of beetroot, carrot, French beans, courgettes; sow spring cabbage, winter radish, chicory; take softwood cuttings of herbs
- **August**: Sow autumn/winter salads (corn salad, land cress, winter lettuce); plant out spring cabbage; harvest maincrop potatoes; begin drying onions

### Autumn (September-November)
- **September**: Plant garlic cloves, autumn onion sets; sow broad beans, hardy peas for overwintering; lift and store maincrop potatoes before frost
- **October**: Plant fruit trees and bushes (bare-root season begins); green manure empty beds with winter rye, field beans, or tares; harvest pumpkins and squashes before first frost
- **November**: Prune apple and pear trees (not stone fruit); protect tender plants; order seed catalogues; take hardwood cuttings of currants, gooseberries

### Winter (December-February)
- **December**: Plan crop rotations; clean and oil tools; check stored crops for rot; make leaf mould from fallen leaves
- **January**: Force rhubarb with a forcing jar; chit early potatoes; prune dormant fruit trees; prepare seed beds when ground isn't frozen
- **February**: Sow early peas under cloches; start onions from seed indoors; prepare greenhouse; soil test and apply lime if needed

## Vegetable Growing Specifics

### Potatoes
- **Chitting**: Stand seed potatoes rose-end up in egg cartons in cool, light position for 4-6 weeks before planting
- **Planting**: 6 inches deep, 12-15 inches apart in rows 24 inches apart
- **Earthing up**: Draw soil up around stems twice — when plants are 6 inches and 12 inches tall
- **Harvest**: First earlies when flowers open; maincrop when haulms die down
- **Storage**: Dark, cool (3-4°C), humid place. Check monthly for rot

### Carrots
- **Soil**: Fine, stone-free soil essential. Never fresh manure — causes forking
- **Sowing**: Very shallow (1/4 inch). Mix seed with dry sand for even distribution. Cover with fine soil or vermiculite, then water with a fine rose
- **Thinning**: First thin to 1 inch when seedlings have true leaves; second thin to 2-3 inches
- **Companion**: Interplant with onions or chives — onion scent deters carrot fly
- **Storage**: Leave in ground under straw mulch through winter, or lift, trim, and store in damp sand

### Brassicas (Cabbage Family)
- **Soil**: Firm, rich soil. Tread ground before planting to consolidate
- **Protection**: Net against cabbage white butterfly (mesh, not fleece — allows pollinators)
- **Club root prevention**: Raise soil pH to 7+ with lime; rotate at least 4 years
- **Collar**: Paper or cardboard collars around stems prevent cabbage root fly
- **Winter brassicas**: Need firm ground, rich soil, and reliable water through dry spells

### Onions and Garlic
- **Sets vs. seed**: Sets are easier but varieties limited; seed gives wider choice
- **Planting depth**: Just showing the tip above soil surface
- **Onion fly**: Interplant with carrots (companion planting)
- **Harvest**: When tops fall over naturally — don't bend necks prematurely
- **Curing**: Lay on dry ground or wire rack in sun for 2-3 weeks until skins rustle
- **Storage**: Cool, dry, well-ventilated. Hang in mesh bags. Check monthly

### Tomatoes
- **Indoor vs. outdoor**: Indoor varieties need heat; outdoor varieties bred for reliability
- **Side shooting**: Remove all side shoots from cordon (indeterminate) varieties
- **Feeding**: Start feeding when first truss sets fruit — high potash liquid feed (comfrey tea works well)
- **Blight prevention**: Never wet foliage when watering; good air circulation; remove lower leaves as plants grow
- **Green tomato ripening**: Wrap individually in newspaper, keep in cool dark place, or hang whole plant upside down indoors

## Organic Pest Management

### The Hamilton Approach: Prevention First

1. **Healthy soil grows healthy plants** — pests primarily attack stressed plants
2. **Diversity** — mixed planting confuses pests; monocultures attract them
3. **Encourage predators** — ladybirds, lacewings, hoverflies, frogs, birds
4. **Physical barriers** — netting, collars, grease bands before reaching for sprays
5. **Accept some damage** — 10-20% loss is normal; perfect produce isn't necessary

### Specific Organic Controls

| Pest | Organic Control |
|------|----------------|
| Aphids | Ladybird attractants (calendula, dill); soap spray (1 tbsp liquid soap per gallon); blast with hose |
| Slugs/snails | Beer traps; copper tape around pots; night patrol with torch; nematode biological control; encouraging hedgehogs |
| Cabbage white butterfly | Fine mesh netting (Enviromesh); companion plant with sage, thyme, rosemary |
| Carrot fly | Companion plant with onions; surround bed with 2-foot barrier of fine mesh; harvest by torchlight |
| Codling moth | Pheromone traps; grease bands on tree trunks; encourage tits (nest boxes) |
| Blackfly | Plant French marigolds nearby; pinch out infected growing tips; spray with diluted soap |
| Whitefly | Yellow sticky traps; companion plant with basil; Encarsia wasp biological control |
| Red spider mite | Increase humidity around plants; introduce Phytoseiulus predator mites |

### Companion Planting Combinations

See [[companion-planting-reference]] for full details. Hamilton's top combinations:
- **Tomatoes + basil**: Basil repels whitefly and improves tomato flavour
- **Carrots + onions**: Mutual pest deterrence (onion smell deters carrot fly, carrot smell deters onion fly)
- **Beans + sweetcorn + squash**: Three Sisters — beans fix nitrogen, corn provides support, squash suppresses weeds
- **Cabbage family + aromatic herbs**: Sage, thyme, rosemary, mint deter cabbage butterfly
- **Roses + garlic**: Garlic deters aphids from roses
- **Apples + nasturtiums**: Nasturtiums repel codling moth and attract predatory insects
