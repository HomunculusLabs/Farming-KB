# PF TEK Substrate Jar Preparation and Steam Sterilization Procedure

The PF TEK substrate jar preparation is the foundational procedure of the
entire PF cultivation method. The process combines a precise substrate
formula with a simple steam sterilization technique that works without a
pressure canner, making it accessible to home cultivators. A key innovation
is the dry vermiculite contamination barrier, an original discovery by
Psilocybe Fanaticus that replaces the need for complex filtration systems.

## Jar Selection

Jars used with this technique must be 1/2 pint capacity (8 ounces or 250
milliliters) with tapered sides and no shoulders, otherwise the fungus
cakes will not easily come out of the jars. Suitable options include:

- KERR wide mouth half pint canning jar
- BALL regular mouth half pint canning jar
- BALL half pint jelly jar
- 1/2 pint capacity drinking glasses with tapered sides

Jars somewhat larger than 1/2 pint are unreliable for the PF TEK and fail
easily. The low form KERR 1/2 pint canning jar is described as the most
versatile because it fits into tight spaces.

## Standard Substrate Formula (per jar)

- 1/8 cup of brown rice powder (from health food stores or co ops)
- 1/2 to 2/3 cup of horticultural vermiculite, medium grade (from garden
  centers and hardware stores)
- 40 to 45 cc (milliliters) of water, approximately 3 tablespoons plus 1
  teaspoon

## Maximum Fruiting Substrate Formula (per jar)

- 1/4 cup of brown rice powder
- 1/2 cup of vermiculite
- 60 cc of water

The maximum formula produces higher yields but requires the jar lid to be
kept loose during incubation to prevent water accumulation. It also needs
slight tamping down after loading: fill the jar loosely, level the top, then
lightly slam the bottom of the jar on the palm a couple of times to lower
the mixture level to 1/2 inch from the top.

## Vermiculite Considerations

Not all vermiculite is the same. Coarseness varies considerably among brands.
The coarser type holds less water than the finer type, altering water
holding capacity. If the formulation results in a wet or sloppy substrate,
less water should be used. The finer type of vermiculite is recommended
because it holds more water.

The finer type has particles averaging around 1 millimeter across, while
the coarser type has particles averaging 4 to 5 millimeters and up to 8
millimeters across. Store shopping and comparison between brands is
recommended.

For homemade brown rice powder, regular brown rice is placed in a small
canister type coffee bean grinder and ground to fine powder. Freshly ground
brown rice is recommended over prepackaged types as freshness can make a
significant difference.

## Canning Lid Preparation

The canning lid is prepared by placing it with the rubber sealing edge
upwards on a supporting surface. Using a sharpened 3 penny nail held with
vise grip pliers, four holes are punched inside the periphery of the rubber
sealing edge. These holes serve as inoculation points.

## Jar Preparation Step by Step

**Step 1: Mixing the substrate.** Place 1/2 cup of vermiculite into a
mixing bowl. Place the brown rice powder on top of the vermiculite. Slowly
add the water directly onto the brown rice powder, wetting it first.
Thoroughly mix the ingredients. The mixture should feel damp and cohesive.
Each jar's substrate should be mixed individually for loading to ensure
accurate formula rendering.

**Step 2: Loading the jar.** Fill the jar very loosely, leaving a 1/2 to
3/4 inch space at the top. Level the substrate. With a tissue or fingertip,
wipe the insides of the jar down to the substrate level. Fill the top of
the jar with plain dry vermiculite and level it off at the top.

This upper dry vermiculite layer serves as a contaminant barrier. It
protects the wet substrate from airborne contaminants and also absorbs and
regulates moisture transpiration and condensation. This is described as a
Psilocybe Fanaticus original discovery and is one of the key innovations
that makes the PF TEK work without sophisticated laboratory equipment.

**Step 3: Lidding and taping.** Place the lid on the jar with the
rubberized edge up (jagged edges of the needle holes facing down). Screw
the lid band on. Place pieces of professional grade masking tape (which
holds on during steaming) over the needle holes to protect them from
contaminant entry.

## Steam Sterilization

A 3 piece vegetable steamer (pot, basket insert, and lid) is used. Stainless
steel vegetable steamers that fold out and stand on the bottom of the pot
also work. The important requirement is that jar bottoms are kept off the
pot bottom where high temperature can crack the glass.

The pot of water is heated to a boil. Jars are placed into the pot with
the lid bands loose so that steam can penetrate quickly. The jars can sit
in water but boiling water must not be able to slosh into the jars. The
heat is turned down and jars are gently steamed at the lowest possible
boil for one hour in a tightly covered pot.

A good tight fitting pot lid is essential for successful steaming. Gas
stoves are noted as the easiest to control. Overheating must be avoided as
it dries the substrate. Drying is evidenced by normal spore germination
followed by halted growth at a certain point, depending on how dry the
substrate has become. Any halted growth without contamination indicates
dried substrate, and the remedy is to increase water content in the
formula.

After steaming, jars are cooled, lids are tightened, and jars are stored
in a cool draft free place until ready for inoculation. A control jar
technique is recommended: let some jars sit uninoculated for several days
after sterilization to verify that the technique is producing sterile
jars before proceeding with inoculation.

## Pressure Canner Alternative

For those with access to a pressure canner, PF substrate jars can be
sterilized at 12 psi for 20 minutes. Water bottles require 55 minutes at
12 psi, and syringes and needles require 10 minutes at 12 psi. Proper
manual consultation is essential as pressure canners can be dangerous if
used incorrectly.
