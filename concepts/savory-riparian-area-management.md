## Stream Bank Stabilization Through Animal Impact

Fish management often requires steep, vegetated banks rather than steep eroding ones.
Very high animal impact for very short periods can promote this. Where herds have firmed
up the ground somewhat, stream banks become vegetated and stable. The well-vegetated
river banks produced with cattle trampling and well-planned grazing demonstrate that
livestock, properly managed, can be a powerful tool for riparian restoration.

The key distinction is between prolonged, one-way trailing that threatens to wash out
stock trails to water or down hillsides, and the milling of excited animals for very
short periods. Although it may seem strange that damage caused by trampling can be
cured by trampling, the treatment works because of the vast difference between chronic
low-level impact and brief, intense impact followed by recovery.
