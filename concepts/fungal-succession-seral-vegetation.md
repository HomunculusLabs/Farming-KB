## Resource Quality Changes During Seral Succession

During seral succession of vegetation from herbaceous to forest ecosystems, there is a
progressive change in the diversity and complexity of resources entering the decomposer
system. The initial seral stages are characterized by high-quality resources consisting
mainly of cellulose with high C:N ratios (29-160:1) and low lignin content (3-30%).
Following forest canopy closure, woody resources and more recalcitrant leaf litters
dominate.

Angiosperm leaves in intermediate successional stages have C:N ratios of 21-71:1 and
lignin contents of 9-42%. Coniferous leaves in late successional stages have C:N ratios
of 63-327:1 and lignin contents of 20-58%. Wood represents the most recalcitrant
material, with C:N ratios of 294-327:1. These progressive changes in resource quality
drive corresponding shifts in the fungal community.

## Changes in Dominant Fungal Groups

The dominant fungal groups effecting plant litter decomposition change systematically
during vegetation succession. In early successional stages with lower plants and
herbaceous vegetation, sugar fungi, ascomycetes, and mitosporic fungi dominate. These are
replaced progressively by more enzymatically competent ascomycetes, mitosporic fungi, and
basidiomycetes as angiosperm and then coniferous leaf litter becomes the dominant input.

In the latest successional stages with wood as the dominant resource, basidiomycetes,
ascomycetes, and mitosporic fungi form the decomposer community, with basidiomycetes
playing an increasingly dominant role. The shift from sugar fungi to basidiomycetes
represents a transition from organisms exploiting labile carbon to those capable of
degrading recalcitrant lignocellulose.

## Increasing Fungi-Fauna Interactions

The degree of interaction between fungi and animals increases during succession, with
more and more intimate associations between fungi and fauna in the exploitation of more
recalcitrant plant residues. In early successional stages, enchytraeids are the primary
fauna associated with decomposition. As succession proceeds, the faunal community
diversifies to include oligochaetes, diptera, collembola, acari, and eventually insecta
and arthropods in wood decomposition.

This progressive increase in faunal complexity reflects the greater enzymatic challenge
of decomposing recalcitrant materials and the evolutionary development of more intimate
fungus-animal partnerships, including [[nematophagous-fungi]], to overcome these
challenges.

## Mycorrhizal Succession in Primary Succession

During primary succession on glacial outwash and volcanic substrates, mycorrhizal fungal
communities undergo dramatic changes. Jumpponen et al. (1999, 2002) identified 68
ectomycorrhizal species belonging to 25 genera across three successional sites on the
Lyman Glacier forefront, with no single species occurring on all three sites.
Ectomycorrhizal species diversity increased to a maximum where tree canopies started to
overlap.
