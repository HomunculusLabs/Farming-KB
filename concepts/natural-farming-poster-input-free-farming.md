# Input-Free Farming Philosophy

The Modern Natural Farming Technology (MNFT) poster promotes an approach to cultivation that minimises or eliminates purchased inputs by relying on foraged, locally available, and self-produced materials. Rooted in Master Cho Han-Kyu's Korean Natural Farming (KNF) and informed by Masanobu Fukuoka's philosophy of working with nature rather than against it, input-free farming treats the grower as part of a regenerative cycle rather than a consumer of agricultural products.

## Core Principles

### Everything You Need Is Already Available
The poster repeatedly emphasises that materials for natural farming inputs are everywhere. The key realisation is that beneficial plants, dynamic accumulators, and microbial inoculants exist in the local environment and can be harvested freely:

- Dynamic accumulator plants (Comfrey, Thistle, Nettle, Horsetail, Dandelion) grow wild in most regions
- Vegetable and fruit scraps from the garden or kitchen become fermentation substrates
- Egg shells from cooking become water-soluble calcium
- Beneficial indigenous microorganisms (IMO/BIM) live in the local soil
- Brown rice and fruits available at any grocery store become the sugar base for fermentation

### The Regenerative Cycle
The poster frames natural farming as a closed-loop system:

1. **Plants feed plants**: FPJ and FFJ extracts return plant nutrients to growing plants
2. **Microbes build soil**: BIM/IMO decompose organic matter and make nutrients bioavailable
3. **Waste becomes input**: Plant matter waste is minimised — everything that can be used, is used
4. **Mulch and compost**: All organic material returns to the soil through mulching and composting
5. **Spores and microbes spread**: Healthy plants and soils share their microbial communities with the surrounding environment

### Foraging Over Purchasing
The explicit instruction to "FORAGE!" positions the natural farmer as a gatherer rather than a buyer. This has several advantages:

- **Zero input cost**: Beyond basic fermentation supplies (sugar, jars), all active ingredients are freely available
- **Local adaptation**: Inputs made from local plants contain microorganisms and nutrients adapted to the local environment
- **Biodiversity promotion**: Foraging from diverse species promotes ecological awareness and biodiversity
- **Self-reliance**: The grower is not dependent on supply chains, shipping, or store availability

## The Spectrum of Engagement

The poster acknowledges that natural farming is not all-or-nothing. It describes a range of involvement:

### Level 1: Dabbling
- Some growers will simply experiment with FPJ and FFJ
- Even small adoption of fermented plant extracts improves soil biology and plant health
- This entry point requires minimal commitment and investment

### Level 2: Full Lifecycle Application
- Some growers will apply the full range of organic inputs across the cannabis life cycle
- This involves making and managing multiple fermented extracts simultaneously
- Requires understanding of plant growth stages and corresponding nutrient needs

### Level 3: Deep Integration
- Some will dive all the way in, making the full range of organics proposed for the cannabis life cycle
- This level involves producing FPJ, FFJ, OHN, FAA, LAB, WSC, WSCaP, WSPa, and BIM
- The grower becomes a producer of agricultural inputs, not a consumer

### Personal Preference is Valid
The poster explicitly states: "We each have our preference in growing. Grow a way that makes you feel good." This is a departure from rigid recipe-based approaches and reflects the KNF philosophy that the farmer's observation and intuition are more valuable than strict adherence to measurements.

## Connection to Korean Natural Farming

The input-free philosophy directly descends from Master Cho Han-Kyu's Korean Natural Farming system:

- Master Cho developed KNF in Korea in the 1960s, emphasising locally-sourced, self-produced inputs
- The KNF community "graciously reminded us of Natural Farming" — acknowledging the debt to Korean tradition
- Chris Trump's YouTube video series brought KNF methods to Western audiences, particularly cannabis growers
- The poster applies these time-tested Korean farming principles to the specific lifecycle of cannabis

## Connection to Fukuoka's Natural Farming

While the poster's techniques are primarily from KNF, the underlying philosophy aligns with Masanobu Fukuoka's [[fukuoka-four-principles-natural-farming-detailed]]:

| Fukuoka Principle | MNFT Application |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| No cultivation | Living soil approach — minimal tilling, mulch-heavy |
| No chemical fertiliser | All inputs are plant/microbe-derived fermented extracts |
| No weeding (or controlled weeding) | Dynamic accumulators are "weeds" that become inputs |
| No pesticides | OHN serves as natural plant health/IPM support |

## Environmental and Community Values

### Minimising Waste
- "Know that you are promoting a green movement with little to know plant matter waste"
- Every part of the plant is used — roots, stalks, leaves, pits, and fruit
- Mulch and compost close the nutrient loop

### Teaching and Sharing
- "Teach others about natural farming"
- Knowledge sharing is framed as a community obligation, not a competitive advantage
- Instagram and social media are identified as outlets for sharing techniques and inspiration

### Quality Medicine
- The goal is proving that "quality meds are more than possible using organics"
- For cannabis specifically, organic cultivation is positioned as producing cleaner, higher-quality medicine for patients

### The Bigger Picture
The poster places cannabis cultivation within a broader agricultural movement:
- Natural Vegetables and broader farming applications are mentioned
- Regenerative Nutrient Cycle principles apply beyond cannabis to all food production
- The community is "promoting such a movement for farming as a whole"

## Practical Implications

### What Input-Free Farming Actually Requires
Despite the name, input-free farming is not zero-effort. It requires:

- **Time**: Fermentation processes take 5-7 days minimum; OHN ages for weeks or months
- **Knowledge**: Understanding plant growth stages, dynamic accumulator species, and fermentation biology
- **Observation**: Reading plant health signals and adjusting inputs accordingly
- **Foraging effort**: Regular collection of plant materials from the local environment
- **Storage and management**: Multiple fermented extracts must be made, stored, and rotated

### What It Eliminates
- Purchased synthetic fertilisers (NPK products)
- Bottled nutrient lines designed for cannabis
- Chemical pesticides and fungicides
- pH adjustment products (organic systems self-regulate through microbial activity)
- Expensive growing media amendments (compost and mulch replace them)

## Criticisms and Limitations

- **Time investment**: Making all inputs from scratch is labour-intensive compared to buying bottled nutrients
- **Consistency**: Natural inputs vary in composition based on source material, season, and fermentation conditions
- **Yield**: Organic/input-free approaches may produce lower yields than optimised synthetic programs
- **Learning curve**: Understanding when and how much of each input to apply requires experience
- **Space requirements**: Foraging, fermenting, and composting all require physical space
- **Local availability**: Some dynamic accumulator species may not be available in all climates or seasons
