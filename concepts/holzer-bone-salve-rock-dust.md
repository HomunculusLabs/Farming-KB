## Bone Salve (Beinsalbe)

A traditional farm remedy made from animal bones, historically prepared by travelling "bone salve burners" (Beinsalbenbrennermandl). Holzer describes the [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] (ventilation grate keeps mice out)

4. **Set up apparatus**:
   - Two 10-litre cast iron pots
   - Upper pot: crushed bones on a wire grate (grate keeps bones in)
   - Lower pot: 0.25L water, buried flush with the ground in wet, mossy soil
   - Upper pot placed upside down on top of lower pot
   - Seal the gap between pots with clay and wet earth
5. **Fire**: Wood is laid over the covered pots and carefully regulated
   - Temperature must be precisely right -- too much heat burns the fat
   - The seal must stay intact; sparks reaching the oil could cause an explosion
6. **Result**: Glutinous brown mass (bone salve/fat) in the bottom pot; light grey flakes of spent bone in the top pot

- Treating wounded livestock (used by castrators and stockmen)
- Wound healing and anti-inflammatory

## Browsing Protection Salve

Holzer's homemade remedy for protecting young fruit trees from deer, hares, and other browsing animals:

### Ingredients
- Bone salve (intense, long-lasting odour)
- Linseed oil (binder, ensures adhesion to bark)
- Slaked lime (emits heat, beneficial to the tree, combines ingredients)
- Fine quartz sand (causes unpleasant sensation in animals' teeth)
- Fresh cow dung (bulks out the salve, aids adhesion, repels animals)

### Application
- Mix to a spreadable consistency
- For painting: thicker mixture applied with brush or broom
- For sprinkling: add more linseed oil, less sand; sprinkle like "holy water"
- The odour gets into the bark and lasts for many years
- Even when the odour fades, the quartz sand remains as a physical deterrent

### Optional Enhancement
Singed hair (pig bristles or cattle hair) can be mixed in:
- Put hair in a metal container, light a fire beneath
- Singed hair produces a floury mass
- Smell is only noticeable to humans briefly, but continues to repel wild animals long-term

### Alternative Repellents
- Mineral naphtha or beechwood tar can substitute for bone salve (less intense odour -- mix with singed hair)
- Wild roses, barberries, or blackthorn planted around trees provide physical barriers

## Rock Dust (Stonemeal)

Holzer mentions stonemeal in several contexts:

### Odour Neutralisation
- Stir stonemeal into liquid fertiliser to neutralise unpleasant odours
- Valerian can also be used for this purpose
- Particularly useful for balcony and terrace gardens where neighbours might object

### Mineral Supplementation
- Crushed rock minerals provide slow-release mineral nutrients
- Granite dust, basalt dust, and other rock powders supply trace minerals
- Complements organic fertilisation by adding mineral components that [[mollison-mulch-materials-sources-and-tropical-application]] to provide diverse nutrients
- 20cm+ of dry material (straw, hay) provides long-term soil protection
