# Permaculture Designers Manual - Energy Systems

updated: 2026-04-18

Energy systems are treated primarily in Chapter 14 (the strategies for an alternative national future) and in Sections 10.3, 12.5, and scattered references throughout the manual. Mollison emphasises diversity of energy sources, decentralisation, and use of locally available renewable resources rather than fossil fuels.

## Woodlots and Forestry Energy

### Managed Fuel Woodlots

Every settlement should plan for a dedicated fuel woodlot that provides a sustainable supply of firewood. Mollison emphasises that:
- Stove design improvements reduce wood consumption by half to two-thirds
- Well-managed coppice woodlots provide continuous harvest
- Mixed species woodlots provide additional products: forage, wildlife habitat, and building materials
- Woodlot placement should consider windbreak functions for settlements
- Leguminous trees in woodlots fix nitrogen for adjacent crops

### Firewood Species

The manual mentions species for fuel at various scales. In temperate regions, coppiced hardwoods (oak, ash, hazel) provide dense firewood on rotation of 7-15 years. In the tropics, fast-growing species such as Leucaena, Gliricidia, and Casuarina are noted for rapid fuel production.

### Pellets and Briquettes

Wood residues, crop wastes, and forest thinnings can be pelletised for efficient fuel production.

## Solar Energy

### Passive Solar

Passive solar design in buildings can approach 100% heating efficiency in well-designed structures. Key elements include orientation, glazing ratios, thermal mass floors and walls, insulation, and appropriate roof overhangs. Passive solar eliminates the need for mechanical heating systems in most climates (see [[permaculture-designers-manual-house-design-principles]]).

### Active Solar

Solar hot water systems are described as routine installations. Photovoltaic panels are approaching 30% efficiency and can be integrated into village-scale power generation.

### Solar Drying

Solar dryers for food and timber preservation are recommended as integrated components of processing facilities at village level.

## Wind Energy

Small-scale wind generators are appropriate for village and farm systems. The manual references wind as a village-scale resource alongside solar, tide, river, and methane. In cool climates, the placement of houses and windbreaks must account for prevailing wind directions.

## Biogas (Methane)

### Sources

Methane is produced from:
- Cow and animal dung
- Poultry manure
- Kitchen and food-processing waste
- Sewage and sullage
- Green crop waste

### Efficiency

Conversion of cow dung to methane achieves significant efficiency compared to direct dung burning. The fertiliser residue is superior to raw dung because nitrogen is retained. Village-scale biogas systems are integrated with housing clusters and animal shelters.

### Products

Biogas provides cooking fuel, space heating, engine fuel (for pumps, generators), and lighting. The digestate is a nitrogen-rich fertiliser.

## Water Power

### Micro-Hydro

Where terrain permits, small-scale water power from streams and rivers provides reliable energy. The manual references river power as a village-scale energy source.

### Water Wheels and Pumps

Water wheels for grain milling and water lifting are traditional technologies that the manual supports as appropriate local energy solutions.

## Village-Scale Integrated Energy

The manual advocates for integrated village energy systems where:
- Multiple energy sources are combined (solar, wind, wood, biogas, water)
- Energy generation sites are public, managed by the village trust
- Excess energy is fed into community networks
- Energy production is coupled with productive activities (methane from waste, heat from stoves used for food processing)
- Vehicle leasing systems reduce individual automobile ownership
- Community facilities (freezers, laundrettes, workshops) consolidate energy use

Mollison notes that energy should be generated close to the point of use, minimising transmission losses and creating local employment in system maintenance.

## National Energy Strategy

At national scale, the manual proposes:
- Complete transition to renewable sources
- Conservation through design as the first strategy
- Decentralised production over centralised power stations
- Regional energy planning based on local resource assessment
- Energy as a bioregional planning priority
