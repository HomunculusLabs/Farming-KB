# Sustainable Living Habits for Homesteads Updated: 2026-04-18 Sustainable living on a homestead involves reducing resource consumption, minimizing waste, and maximizing self-sufficiency through intentional daily practices. These habits apply whether living in a [[kitchen-composting-and-vermicomposting]] or worm bins
- This diverts about 30% of household waste

### Paper
- Office waste and scrap paper can be shredded and added to compost or worm bins
- Most modern inks are compostable in small amounts; receipts (BPA-coated) are the exception
- Share magazines through free racks or repurpose for art projects before composting

### Containers
- Glass jars and bottles: wash and store with lids for dry goods storage (beads, buttons, beans, etc.)
- Plastic tubs: use for leftover storage
- Aluminum cans: store fat drippings for lard, puncture for lanterns or garden-waterers, save for craft projects

## Water Conservation

- Collect rainwater for irrigation and household use
- Use [[straw-bale-construction]] and  methods when feasible
- Consider permaculture-solar-energy-inventions if site conditions allow

## Recycling and Reuse

The first step to recycling is avoiding. Before purchasing, consider:
- Can this item be reused, repaired, or borrowed instead?
- Is there a package-free or minimal-packaging alternative?
- Can the packaging be composted or recycled?
- Is there a secondhand version available?
