## Impacts on Mycorrhizal Function

Nematode feeding on mycorrhizal fungal hyphae can significantly reduce the effectiveness
of the mycorrhizal association. Brussard et al. (2001) demonstrated that nematode feeding
on mycorrhizal hyphae reduced nutrient acquisition efficiency and altered plant
competitive fitness. The degree of impact depends on nematode species, density, and the
specific mycorrhizal fungal species being consumed.

The grazing of nematodes on extraradical mycorrhizal hyphae represents a direct pathway
by which faunal activity influences plant nutrition. In agroecosystems where nematode
densities can reach high levels in the absence of predators, the effects on mycorrhizal
function and plant growth may be particularly pronounced.

## Effects on Decomposition and Nutrient Cycling

Chen and Ferris (1999) demonstrated that decomposition of alfalfa residues and cellulose
was increased by the presence of fungal-feeding nematodes. Where residues were colonized
by the preferred fungal food (Rhizoctonia solani) for nematodes, both nematode
populations and nitrogen mineralization were significantly higher than when the less
preferred fungal food (Trichoderma sp.) was available.

This suggests that nematode feeding increased either the biomass or at least the activity
of the preferred fungal food, and that this increase in fungal activity was manifested in
enhanced ecosystem function. The mechanism may involve stimulation of fungal growth
through grazing-induced compensatory responses, or the release of nutrients from nematode
excretion that stimulates fungal activity.

## Nematode-Fungi-Microbe Interactions

In microcosm experiments, Nieminen and Setala (2001) showed that the presence of
fungal-feeding nematodes and bacteria increased fungal activity in soil. Each factor,
nematodes or bacteria, had similar effects, but the two acting together were not
additive. This non-additive response suggests complex trophic interactions where nutrient
limitation and the dependence on fungi in the food web configuration can produce
unexpected outcomes.
