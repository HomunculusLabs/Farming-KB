# Hemenway Sheet Mulching Guide

Sheet mulching is one of the basics in the ecological gardener's toolkit. It is a method of eradicating weeds and building soil that eliminates herbicides and avoids tilling, both of which rupture soil ecology. It is a variation on nature's way of building soil by accumulating and breaking down organic debris from the top down.

## What Sheet Mulching Does

In its simplest form: apply a layer of weed-suppressing newspaper or cardboard, then top with about a foot of organic mulch. The soil life that develops includes mycelium networks, microfauna channels, worm populations, and beneficial decomposer organisms. An intact subterranean ecology develops, woven by silken fibers of mycelium, riddled with channels of traveling microfauna, bound into perfect tilth by gummy exudates and carbon-rich liquors of metabolism.

## Materials List (for the Perfect Sheet Mulch)

For a site of about 200 square feet near the house:

1. **Newspaper**: 2-3 foot stack (no glossy sections; black and colored inks on standard newsprint are soy-based and nontoxic), or about 300 sq ft of corrugated cardboard (no staples or plastic tape). Can also use cloth, old clothing, or wool carpet (no synthetic fabric, but these take far longer to decay).

2. **Soil amendments** (depending on soil needs): lime, rock phosphate, bonemeal, rock dust, kelp meal, blood meal.

3. **Bulk organic matter**: straw, spoiled hay, yard waste, leaves, seaweed, finely ground bark, stable sweepings, wood shavings. Overall C:N ratio of 100:1 to 30:1. Need about 4-8 cubic yards loosely piled, or 6-10 two-string bales. Grass clippings OK but only mixed with brown mulches (high nitrogen causes anaerobic decomposition).

4. **Compost**: about 1/4 to 1/2 cubic yard (6-12 cubic feet).

5. **Manure**: 1/4 to 1 cubic yard depending on concentration and bedding. About 6 cubic feet of composted cattle manure is plenty.

6. **Top layer**: 2 inches of seed-free material (straw, fine bark, wood shavings, pine needles, grain hulls, seagrass).

## Step-by-Step Construction

### Day Before
- Water the site well unless moist from rain
- Organisms need water to work; once mulch is in place, hard to moisten bottom layers

### Step 1: Slash Vegetation
- Clip, mow, scythe, or weed-whack everything down in place
- Don't pull up weeds -- leave all native organic matter including roots
- Remove stumps or big woody pieces
- All nitrogen-rich greens and roots are food for decomposers

### Step 2: Soil Amendments
- If acid soil: sprinkle lime
- If alkaline: gypsum or sulfur
- Dusting of rock phosphate or bonemeal for phosphorus
- Greensand, kelp meal, or rock dust for trace minerals
- If clayey/compacted soil: push spading fork in, rock it, pull out -- poke holes and crack open (don't turn earth)

### Step 3: Thin Nitrogen Layer
- Manure, blood meal, fresh grass clippings, or cast-off produce
- Concentrated matter (rabbit manure, blood meal): just cover the soil
- Grass clippings or bedding-rich manure: about 1 inch thick
- Attracts worms and burrowing beetles

### Step 4: The Sheet (Newspaper/Cardboard)
- Lay continuous light-blocking layer
- Overlap cardboard by 6 inches to prevent weeds between sheets
- Newspaper: 1/4 to 1/2 inch thick
- Wet thoroughly from hose as you spread (do frequently if breezy)
- Soak several times to ensure water seeps through
- Don't walk on wet paper (pulls sheets apart)
- Work from one end toward the opposite (like painting a floor)

### Step 5: Second Nitrogen Layer
- Thin layer of manure, meal, or fresh green clippings
- Entices worms up through rotting sheets
- Coax plant roots downward

### Step 6: Bulk Mulch Layer
- 8 to 12 inches of loose straw, hay, leaves, or other bulk matter
- Weed seeds not a concern (thick seed-free layer above; seeds rot rather than germinate)
- Break bales into 1-2 inch thick "books," lay about 3 thicknesses
- Pay attention to C:N ratio: sprinkle nitrogen if using high-carbon materials
- Dilute carbonaceous mulch with 1 part high-nitrogen matter per 4 parts high-carbon
- Spray water every few inches (damp but not wet -- wrung-out sponge state)

### Step 7: Compost Layer
- 1-2 inches of compost
- If short supply: compost plus whatever soil is on hand
- If planting in a few months: substitute manure or easily compostable material
- If planting within weeks: compost layer needed as seedbed

### Step 8: Final Seed-Free Layer
- 2 inches of weed- and seed-free organic matter
- Gives "finished look" and suppresses weeds
- For planting: push aside to reach compost/soil layer below

## Layer Summary (Bottom to Top)
1. Slashed vegetation on moistened soil
2. Soil amendments
3. Thin manure/nitrogen layer
4. Newspaper (1/4-1/2 inch) or cardboard
5. Thin manure/nitrogen layer
6. 8-12 inches bulk organic matter
7. 1-2 inches compost
8. 2 inches seed-free mulch

## Planting in Sheet Mulch

- Best prepared in fall; reaches prime in second season
- Fresh sheet mulch still gives plants a boost (soil life blossoms within days)
- For seeds: make tiny pockets/trenches 3 inches deep, fill with soil/compost
- For seedlings: small soil pockets about 3x root mass
- For deep-rooted plants: pull mulch aside, slit paper in X-pattern, replace mulch
- For shrubs/trees: install before mulching, or remove mulch, slit paper, peel back, dig hole
- Place plant with root crown about 1 inch above old soil level
- Don't bury trunk in mulch (rodents will tunnel in and feast on bark)

## Common Problems

- **Bindweed**: travels yards beneath paper layer -- major headache
- **Bermuda grass**: rhizomes tunnel through cardboard tubes
- **Slugs**: populations explode in early decomposition phase
  - Compensate with extra-heavy plantings of succulent greens (slugs do thinning)
  - Make slug collars from tin cans (not aluminum): remove top/bottom, cut 2-inch rings, encircle stems (galvanic shock deters slugs)

## Benefits Over Compost Pile

- Doesn't disturb soil organisms
- No need to turn or move material
- Builds soil directly where plants grow
- Can be used in Zones 2-3 for long-term soil building with less precise C:N ratios
- For heavy food production or Zone 1: strive for proper C:N ratio
