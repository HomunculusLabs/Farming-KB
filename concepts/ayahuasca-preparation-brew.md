# Ayahuasca Brew Preparation

## Overview
Ayahuasca, known as *La Purga* in the Amazonian tradition, is a sacred
psychoactive brew central to the shamanic practices documented by Pablo
Amaringo in *Ayahuasca Visions*. The preparation is far more than a
botanical extraction — it is a ritual process imbued with spiritual
discipline, dietary rigor, and deep reverence for the plant teachers
whose consciousness the brew reveals. Amaringo's paintings provide an
unparalleled window into the meticulous methods practiced by vegetalistas
throughout the Peruvian Amazon.

## Core Botanical Ingredients

### Banisteriopsis caapi — The Ayahuasca Vine

The foundational ingredient is *Banisteriopsis caapi*, a woody liana
growing throughout the Amazon basin. In Quechua, *ayahuasca* translates
to "vine of the soul," reflecting the profound spiritual journeys it
facilitates. The vine contains harmine and harmaline, beta-carboline
alkaloids that function as monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs). These
compounds are pharmacologically essential, allowing DMT from the
companion plant to become orally active. Amaringo describes the vine as
possessing a powerful mother spirit — a feminine consciousness that
guides the drinker through visionary landscapes and offers teachings
about natural and supernatural worlds.

### Psychotria viridis — Chacruna

The second essential ingredient is *chacruna* (*Psychotria viridis*), a
shrub whose leaves contain N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT). While DMT is
psychoactive when smoked, it is normally inactive orally because MAO
enzymes break it down before reaching the brain. The MAOIs from the
ayahuasca vine inhibit these enzymes, allowing DMT to cross the
blood-brain barrier. In the shamanic understanding, chacruna provides
the "light" and the "visions," while the vine provides the "strength"
and "structure" of the experience.

## Admixture Plants

Vegetalistas frequently add additional plants, each contributing
specific spiritual and therapeutic qualities based on the healer's
training and guidance from plant spirits.

- **Diplopterys cabrerana** (chaliponga): Used alongside chacruna,
  providing additional DMT and a unique spirit presence.
- **Brunfelsia grandiflora** (chiricaspi): Added for purgative and
  healing properties, cleansing the body of spiritual contamination.
- **Tobacco** (*Nicotiana rustica*): Sacred tobacco strengthens the
  brew; its smoke cleanses during ceremonies.
- **Toe** (*Brugmansia suaveolens*): May be added in minute quantities
  with extreme caution due to toxicity.

## Preparation Process

### Selection and Harvesting

Plants are gathered by someone who has undergone proper dieta with those
species. The harvester approaches with respect, offering prayers or
tobacco before cutting. Only mature, healthy specimens are selected, as
their spirit is considered fully developed and capable of providing the
strongest medicine.

### Cleaning and Preparation

The vine is cleaned and pounded with a wooden mallet to break the
fibrous bark and release the inner woody tissue. This labor-intensive
process may take hours. Chacruna leaves are cleaned and sometimes torn
to increase surface area during boiling.

### Boiling

Plant material is placed in a large clay pot filled with water. Clay
pots are essential — metal pots are believed to interfere with the
brew's spiritual properties. The mixture is boiled and then simmered
for three to four hours. The vegetalista periodically sings icaros into
the brew, infusing it with shamanic power and calling plant spirits
into the liquid. The brew is periodically strained and further reduced
until it reaches the desired concentration.

### Multiple Extractions

Plant material may be boiled two or three times, with liquids combined
and further reduced. This ensures maximum extraction of both
pharmacological and spiritual properties. The finished brew is a dark,
thick, bitter liquid with a characteristic earthy taste.

## Dietary Restrictions and Spiritual Discipline

Preparation and consumption of ayahuasca requires adherence to strict
dietary and behavioral restrictions known as *la dieta*. These are not
merely physical precautions but essential spiritual practices that
purify the body and make it receptive to the plant teachers' wisdom.

### The Dieta

Participants must abstain from salt, sugar, oils, fats, spicy foods,
fermented foods, alcohol, and sexual activity for several days before
the ceremony. More rigorous dietas may last weeks or months with
near-fasting conditions. The dieta cleanses impurities that could
interfere with the experience or make the participant vulnerable to
spiritual attack.

### Behavioral Restrictions

Beyond food, dieta protocols include avoiding cold water, excessive
social interaction, and emotionally disturbing situations. Participants
spend time in solitude and contemplation of nature, creating a container
of heightened sensitivity essential for meaningful ayahuasca work.

## Ceremony Structure

Ayahuasca ceremonies typically take place at night, in darkness, under
the guidance of an experienced vegetalista. Darkness is essential for
full development of visions, as light interferes with the perceptual
sensitivity required to perceive the spirit world. The ceremony begins
with prayers and invocations, followed by serving of the brew. The
vegetalista sings icaros throughout the night, calling protective
spirits, guiding participants through difficult experiences, and
directing healing energy. The purge — vomiting and sometimes diarrhea —
is considered necessary and beneficial, representing physical expulsion
of spiritual illness and emotional blockages.

## Amaringo's Visionary Documentation

Amaringo's paintings document the complex visionary worlds the brew
reveals. His work shows the brew itself as a living entity — a luminous
liquid containing countless spirits, serpents, and cosmic beings. His
art captures the moment when ayahuasca opens a portal between ordinary
and extraordinary worlds, revealing the vast plant intelligence
cultivated for millennia.
