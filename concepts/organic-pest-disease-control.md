## Organic Pest and Disease Control

The organic approach to pest and disease control emphasizes
prevention, natural predators, and physical methods over
chemicals. By cultivating a diverse garden, you attract
wildlife that creates a natural balance where no single
pest or disease can dominate.

## Prevention First

The very first rule is to adopt good cultivation practices.
Feeding the soil instead of the plant produces stronger
growth that is less attractive to pests and better able to
cope with attacks. Keep the garden clean and neat. Never
leave plant debris around; put waste on the [[compost]] heap
immediately. Use only clean, sterilized pots and seed trays.

## Daily Vigilance

Walk around the garden at least once a day in summer. Take
a hoe and a plastic bag with you. Remove weeds, watch for
the first signs of pest or disease attack. Pick off
caterpillars and drop them into a jar of paraffin. Rub
greenfly off stems with your fingers. The first attack of
aphids can often be removed by simply squashing them.

## Buying Healthy Plants

Ensure plants are healthy when purchased. Reject anything
showing signs of disease, pest attack, or physical damage.
Examine bulbs, corms, and tubers closely; they should be
firm with intact skins. Some diseases like [[fukuoka-pine-bark-weevil-ecology]] mulch is also effective.
Surround seedlings with cut plastic bottles for protection.

### Aphids (Greenfly and Blackfly)
These suck sap, cause distortion, excrete honeydew
encouraging sooty mold, and transmit virus diseases.
Predators include ladybugs and hoverflies. Plant French
marigolds (Tagetes) to attract hoverflies. Spray badly
infested plants with insecticidal soap.

### Wireworms
Larvae of the click beetle that make small holes in
potatoes and carrots. Grow a row of wheat between crops
to attract and trap them, then burn the wheat. Split
old cabbage stalks pushed into the ground also act as
traps.

### Leatherjackets
White, fat larvae of the crane fly that nibble roots
just below the soil surface. Usually found while digging
or hoeing; they are easy to see and squash. Ground beetles
are natural predators.

### Flea Beetles
Tiny beetles that make shot holes in seedling leaves,
especially brassicas. Control with a grease-coated board
passed along the row; the beetles jump up and stick.
