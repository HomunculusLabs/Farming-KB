## Trace Water Microwave Sterilization Mechanism at 2.45 GHz

## Overview

The water film must be thin enough to heat rapidly and uniformly but
thick enough to maintain contact with the microbial cells across the
entire surface. If the surface is completely dry, microwave energy passes
through with minimal absorption because air and most dry materials have
very low dielectric loss at 2.45 GHz. If the surface is flooded, the
water acts as a thermal mass that absorbs energy but heats slowly and
unevenly. The optimal thin film heats within seconds to temperatures
that exceed microbial thermal tolerance while the underlying substrate
remains at a safe temperature. This localised heating is the key advantage
over autoclaving, which heats the entire volume to 121°C.

## Microbial Inactivation Pathways

The microbial kill achieved by microwave irradiation in the presence of
trace water may involve both thermal and non-thermal mechanisms. The
thermal pathway is straightforward: the water film rapidly reaches
temperatures lethal to microorganisms (typically exceeding 80-100°C at
the prescribed exposure parameters). The thin film geometry means this
temperature is reached quickly, minimising the time available for
organisms to mount heat-shock responses. Non-thermal effects are more
controversial but have been proposed based on observations that microwave
irradiation sometimes achieves kills greater than predicted by thermal
modelling alone. Proposed non-thermal mechanisms include disruption of
cell membrane potential by the oscillating electromagnetic field, damage
to sensitive protein structures through dielectric stress, and selective
heating of intracellular water that may exceed the temperature of the
surrounding water film. The NASA researchers demonstrated complete kills
of all [[mixed-population-kill-kinetics-microwave-surface-sterilization-nasa]] requires
sufficient total dose regardless of the exposure rate, although higher
rates achieve the kill in less time. The NASA system parameters represent
a validated combination that reliably achieves surface sterilisation
across the tested organism spectrum.

## Frequency Selection Rationale

The 2.45 GHz frequency occupies a specific region of the electromagnetic
spectrum called the ISM (Industrial, Scientific, and Medical) band, which
is reserved internationally for applications that generate electromagnetic
radiation without causing interference with communications systems. From
a physics standpoint, 2.45 GHz is near the peak of water's dielectric
absorption spectrum, though the absolute peak occurs at approximately 22
GHz. The 2.45 GHz frequency was chosen for practical reasons: it
provides strong water absorption (sufficient for effective heating) while
penetrating several centimetres into materials, allowing the energy to
reach surfaces within enclosed structures through waveguide delivery.
Higher frequencies (closer to 22 GHz) would be absorbed too strongly,
limiting penetration depth. Lower frequencies would provide deeper
penetration but weaker absorption, requiring much higher power to achieve
the same heating effect at the surface.
