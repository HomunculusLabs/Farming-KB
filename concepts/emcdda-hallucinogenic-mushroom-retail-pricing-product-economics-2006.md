# EMCDDA Hallucinogenic Mushroom Retail Pricing and Product Economics (2006)

## Overview

In January 2006, the EMCDDA conducted a systematic snapshot of online shops selling hallucinogenic mushroom products across Europe. Using search engines Google and AltaVista with four keyword combinations ("magic mushrooms shop," "buy magic mushrooms," "psilocybe cubensis shop," "buy psilocybe cubensis"), researchers identified 39 unique online shops. The vast majority (82%) were based in the Netherlands, with the remaining 18% located in Austria, Germany, the UK, and Poland.

## Product Types and Price Ranges

The following pricing data represents the range found across identified online shops in January 2006:

### Fresh Mushrooms

| Product | Quantity | Price Range (EUR) |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| Fresh Psilocybe cubensis (Mexican) | 30 g | 8.50–45.28 |
| Fresh [[copelandia-cyanescens]] | 10 g | 13.00–17.50 |
| Psilocybe mexicana (Philosopher's stones/truffles) | 15 g | 10.00–17.50 |

Fresh Psilocybe cubensis varieties were the most commonly sold products by smartshops and online retailers. The cubensis varieties were cultivated specifically (mostly in the Netherlands) because they are easy to grow in terrariums and possess a low water content that allows them to stay fresh for 7–10 days.

### Spawn and Cultivation Products

| Product | Quantity | Price Range (EUR) |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| Spawnbag (Psilocybe azurescens) | 1 bag | 24.00–25.00 |
| Growkits | 1 kit | 25.00–100.00 |
| Spore prints | 1 print or syringe | 9.00–28.00 |

Prices for growkits and spore prints varied according to the [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-species-classification-and-chemistry]] for which they were available. Growkits typically contained a box with colonized substrate, a bag with an air filter, and paperclips.

### Free Spore Ring Europe

Users could obtain a variety of strains (e.g., Psilocybe cubensis, Panaeolus cyanescens) for a price of 1 EUR per print from a non-profit making organization called the Free Spore Ring Europe (FSRE). Instructions on how to grow hallucinogenic mushrooms using a spore print and a substrate were easily found on the internet.

## UK Market Economics (Pre-Prohibition)

Before the UK ban in July 2005, the [[hallucinogenic-mushroom-market-in-europe]] was estimated to have a turnover of approximately £1 million (€1.46 million) per annum. The UK Treasury collected sales tax on hallucinogenic mushrooms estimated to be worth up to £175,000 (€255,421) per year. It was estimated that about 300 shops and market stalls across the UK sold hallucinogenic mushrooms until the change in legal status.

According to the Mixmag 2004 readership survey, among those who had recently used hallucinogenic mushrooms, 54.6% reported purchasing their last batch from a club, shop, or stall; 16% got them from a friend; and 4.2% obtained them via the internet.

## Dutch Smartshop Economics

In the Netherlands, it was estimated that there were about 120–150 smartshops, concentrated primarily in Amsterdam. These shops sold legal and predominantly natural products, including magic mushrooms. According to the Dutch Risk Assessment Report (CAM, 2000), magic mushrooms made up approximately 50% of smartshop turnover.

A 2001 study among a representative sample of young persons aged 12 and above in the Netherlands found that 64% of young people aged 18 and older who had used magic mushrooms in the last year purchased them in smartshops.

Smartshop distribution channels included:
- **Smartshops** — primary retail outlet for magic mushrooms
- **Coffee shops** — some sold hallucinogenic mushrooms alongside cannabis
- **Headshops** — drug paraphernalia shops that sometimes stocked mushrooms
- **Grow shops** — shops for home marijuana growers that sometimes sold mushrooms
- **"Paddo cabs"** — home delivery services for [[legal-responses-to-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-in-the-european-union]] some Dutch areas

## Online Shop Geographic Distribution

Analysis of contact addresses for the 39 identified online shops revealed:

- **Netherlands**: 82% of shops (approximately 32 shops)
- **Austria**: 4 online shops selling growkits and spore prints
- **Germany**: 2 sites
- **UK**: 1 site (linked to a Dutch online shop)
- **Poland**: 1 site (primarily selling cannabis, referred to mushrooms as unavailable)

Website statistics for one Dutch online shop showed access patterns between May 2004 and February 2006:
- Netherlands: 73% of visitors
- Belgium: 14% of visitors
- Germany: 2% of visitors
- France: 2% of visitors

## International Shipping and Market Barriers

The majority of online shops offered international shipping. However, many sites did not ship to countries where sales were prohibited and/or advised customers to verify the legal status of the product before ordering. Many sites featured different language versions (mainly English, French, and German) to attract foreign customers.

After the UK ban in July 2005, internet sites in the UK stopped selling hallucinogenic mushrooms. One UK internet site (allsalvia.co.uk) claimed to be "sold out" of their kits and posted a notice that fresh hallucinogenic mushrooms, truffles, and growkits were about to become Class A controlled drugs.

## Post-Ban Market Shift

Following the UK prohibition, retailers such as online headshops EveryOneDoesIt.com and Allsalvia.co.uk began switching sales to legal alternatives:
- **Amanita muscaria** (fly agaric) and closely related **Amanita pantherina** (panther mushroom)
- **Purple Ohms capsules** — containing [[argyreia-nervosa]] (Hawaiian Baby Woodrose) with LSA

The Czech Republic saw retailers exploiting legal loopholes, with a shop in Prague selling hallucinogenic mushrooms under the label of "aromatic and decorative goods," sold with a leaflet warning customers not to ingest them.

## Price Comparison Context

The pricing data from the EMCDDA snapshot should be interpreted in the context of the broader European drug market. Hallucinogenic mushrooms occupied a unique niche as a natural product sold through legal retail channels in several EU member states during this period. The relatively low price point of fresh mushrooms (€8.50 for 30g of P. cubensis at the low end) made them accessible compared to synthetic alternatives.

The price spread within product categories was notable. For fresh P. cubensis, prices ranged from €8.50 to €45.28 for the same 30g quantity, suggesting significant variation in brand positioning, quality claims, and retailer markups. Growkits showed even wider variation (€25–100), reflecting differences in species complexity, substrate volume, and included accessories.

## Market Transparency and Consumer Information

The EMCDDA noted that online shop pricing was generally transparent, with product descriptions, quantities, and prices clearly displayed. However, information about active substance content (psilocybin/psilocin concentration), maximum shelf life, and possible side effects was typically absent from retailer listings. This information asymmetry between what retailers disclosed and what consumers needed for informed use was a recurring theme in the EMCDDA analysis.

The Free Spore Ring Europe's €1 per print pricing represented a significant disruption to commercial spore pricing (€9–28 per print from commercial retailers). This non-profit distribution model underscored the tension between commercial smartshop economics and the open-information ethic of online mushroom cultivation communities.

## Market Size Estimation Challenges

Accurate market size estimation was difficult due to:
- The largely cash-based nature of smartshop transactions
- The rapid emergence and disappearance of online shops
- Cross-border shipping complicating national market assessments
- The overlap between legitimate and gray-market distribution channels
- Lack of systematic sales data from retailers, many of whom had ties to the broader drug market and were unwilling to disclose supplier information

The Dutch National Criminal Intelligence Service (CRI) investigation found that some smartshop owners had ties with the synthetic drugs market, adding complexity to market analysis. The CRI contacted various municipalities but found no evidence of public nuisance as a result of sale or use of hallucinogenic mushrooms.
