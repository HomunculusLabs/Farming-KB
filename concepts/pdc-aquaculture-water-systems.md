# PDC Aquaculture Water Systems

Pamphlet XI of the 1981 Permaculture Design Course covers water in permaculture in extraordinary breadth -- from mountain catchments to tidal marine systems. Mollison argues that "never in the history of the world has anybody designed and implemented" comprehensive aquaculture, and that aquatic systems offer yields equal to or greater than terrestrial systems with less effort.

## Pond Design

### Structure
"The ideal structure for a pond is a sloping floor or a step floor. You should be able to fully drain it. It is even better if you are able to drain it into another pond and take it through a dryland cycle."

After several years as a pond, "it will carry dryland crops three to four years without further manuring." The gley layer (fermented organic matter sealing the pond bottom) "perpetuates itself."

### pH
"The pH in ponds is between 6 and 8, that is, it is 100 to 200 times lower in acid than most garden soils." "Most things in the pond like lime. It is quite different from land culture in that respect."

### Manuring
"Bales of hay around the edge, both to seal ponds and to get diatoms working." "Ducks will add manure to it." Figure on eight ducks to a quarter acre. A white painted disc that "blacks out at about a foot" means the pond is over-manured; visible at five feet means it needs more.

## Pond Polyculture

### The Trophic Ladder
"We want careful adjustment of maybe 20 species of plants and small animals low on the trophic ladder. That includes the shrimps, yabbies, crayfish, catfish, edge plants, pond plants, and ducks."

Small forage ponds at different pH's trickle into the main pond: "What is coming down, they get, but they can't get at the sources." This is polyculture "but not in the sense that we have it all together."

### Key Species
- Mussels: "the superior phosphorus fixer," filter 200 gallons/day each, inject nutrients into mud floor
- Catfish: "very low on the chain feeders, way down on the trophic ladder"
- Wild rice: good duck food, needs seed in mud balls, likes 18 inches to three feet depth
- Water chestnuts: hardy, form large tubers
- Water hyacinth: usable for pollution cleanup in restricted locations
- Cattails: edge crop, good insulation when composted
- Water lilies: plant in manure-filled bags inside tires for easy harvest

### Crawfish
Best habitat: "beer cans." Throw in "a clutch of beer cans tied to a cork or ping pong ball." A Scotch biologist figures "30 quarter-acre ponds in marine culture would keep a family, supplying an income of $20,000 to $30,000." Crawfish "like ponds that are about three feet deep, and they like brush piles."

## Freshwater Marsh Systems

### Sewage Treatment
"Sewage should be turned out into a marsh, not a pond." Rush (Scirpus) is "the most efficient water cleanser." In a Swiss-designed system for 8,000 people, Mollison saved "$30,000 a year in engineering work" while creating "an industrial base for the town, an employment system, through mint and bamboo."

### The Half-Pipe System
For domestic sewage disposal: a half-pipe open on one side, "buried in earth, but it is open on the bottom." Trees planted beside it grow on the nutrient flow. "Every four feet it has a two inch piece that fastens the bottom of the sections together. These act like little dams that slow the water, so that little ponds form continually behind them."

## Marine and Estuarine Systems

### Tidal Ponds
"Inland ponds" between tides need 3:1 side slopes. Adjust with boards for tide range. "The cheapest swimming pool you can build anybody, and self-flushing. It is always warm."

### Longshore Drift Traps
"Nobody had to dig those [scour holes], and nobody has to maintain them." By directing the tide into natural accumulation zones, "a deep and permanent pond forms." Octopus occupy clay pots placed there, emerging at high tide to eat shellfish.

### Irish Stone Fields
Rows of stone across hard bottom underwater grow "enormous quantities of desirable seaweeds." The Irish found that "the smaller field produces more than the larger one" because "it is warmer."

### Phosphate Islands
One man built a platform with multiple bird roosts on a desert coast. "He's gathered so much phosphate off that area that he's annually a millionaire." "Two hundred terns always roosting in the right place will entirely supply maybe an island or a village."

### Oyster Rafting
Oysters grow best at 60% air exposure. "You can actually set him up with a situation in which he can raft or support oysters inland, which is much easier than having them out in the tide land."

## Key Principles

1. Design productive systems at every water level -- from duck marsh to deep pond
2. Use trickling systems to connect ponds of different pH and species
3. Include decomposers (mussels, diatoms) in every system
4. Ponds serve multiple functions: fencing, fire control, heat storage, recreation, water cleaning
5. "The more you play with water, or walk on landscape where you can play with water, the more fascinating things you'll see that you can do."

## Large Dams

"Large dams such as the Aswan or any of those dams are mostly negative in their effect." They trap silt, reduce fertility, increase disease, change fisheries, give rise to centralized power and polluting industries. "Generally speaking, they are a disaster. So we are mainly in favor of reasonably small impoundments."
