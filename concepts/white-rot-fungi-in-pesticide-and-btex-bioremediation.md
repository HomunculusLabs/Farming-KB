# White-Rot Fungi in Pesticide and BTEX Bioremediation

White-rot fungi, particularly [[staycare-phanerochaete-chrysosporium-in-bioremediation]], degrade a remarkably wide range of persistent organic pollutants including pesticides, BTEX compounds, chlorobenzenes, and chlorinated solvents. Their ligninolytic enzyme systems (LiP, MnP, laccase) and cytochrome P450 systems enable cometabolic transformation of many recalcitrant compounds. The non-specific free radical mechanism of [[query-how-does-lignin-degradation-by-fungi-work]] provides the basis for the degradation of structurally diverse pollutants.

## Pesticide Degradation

### Organochlorine Insecticides

- **Lindane and Chlordane**: P. chrysosporium achieves up to 23% degradation to 14CO2 in 30 days in liquid culture, 60 days in soil-corn cob cultures (Kennedy et al., 1990). Degradation attributed to P450-monooxygenase, not lignin-modifying enzymes (LMEs).

- **Aldrin, Heptachlor, Mirex**: Undergo substantial biotransformation (disappearance of starting material) but not complete mineralization. P. chrysosporium, P. eryngii, Pleurotus florida, and P. sajor-caju show extensive heptachlor degradation.

- **DDT**: First [[fungal-chlorinated-aromatic-degradation]] shown to be degraded by P. chrysosporium (Bumpus et al., 1985). First metabolite is DDD, which disappears on continued incubation. Requires additional carbon source (cellulose, corn cobs). Soil-corn cob amended systems support growth and 14CO2 evolution.

### Acetanilide Herbicides

- **Alachlor**: Degraded 14% by Ceriporiopsis subvermispora, 12% by Phlebia tremellosa, and 6.3% by P. chrysosporium after 122 days (Ferrey et al., 1994)

- **Atrazine**: P. chrysosporium degrades 48% after 14 days in N-limited medium. Ethyl carbons degraded to CO2 while ring carbons remain largely intact. Metabolites: hydroxylated and N-dealkylated products.

### Chlorophenoxyacetic Acid Herbicides

- **2,4-D and 2,4,5-T**: Degradation by P. chrysosporium does not require LiP or MnP. Mixed compounds degrade faster than individually. Degradation of 2,4-D increases with nitrogen and carbon levels.

- **Dichomitus squalens**: Side chain cleavage independent of ligninolytic system (LDS); aromatic ring degradation LDS-dependent. Pathway involves chlorophenol intermediates. Methylation of 2-chloro-1,4-dihydroquinone intermediates serves as a mechanism for regenerating substrates susceptible to attack by LiP and MnP.

### Nerve Agent Degradation

- Nerve agents VX and Russian VX (RVX), and insecticide analog diisopropylamiton (containing P-S bonds), rapidly and completely oxidized by P. ostreatus in presence of ABTS mediator.

### Pentachlorophenol (PCP)

PCP is rapidly degraded by P. chrysosporium under nitrogen-limiting secondary metabolic conditions (ligninolytic conditions), while degradation is inhibited in high-nitrogen media. PCP degradation of 20-50% was reported in nitrogen-limited static cultures. Degradation is initiated by LiP- or MnP-catalyzed oxidative dechlorination to produce tetrachloro-1,4-benzoquinone (TCBQ), which undergoes successive dechlorinations to 1,4-hydroquinone and then ring cleavage.

## BTEX Degradation

BTEX compounds (benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, and o-, m-, and p-xylenes) are priority environmental pollutants listed by the US EPA. They are components of gasoline and aviation fuels and enter soil, sediments, and groundwater from leaking underground storage tanks, pipelines, accidental spills, and inadequate waste disposal practices.

### Degradation by Phanerochaete chrysosporium

Phanerochaete chrysosporium efficiently degrades all BTEX components individually or as mixtures (Yadav and Reddy, 1993b):

- **Key finding**: Greater degradation occurs in malt extract medium or high-nitrogen medium (where LiP and MnP expression is blocked) than in low-nitrogen medium

- **Mineralization**: Substantial degradation of 14C-ring-labelled benzene and toluene to 14CO2; about half of total degradation of benzene and toluene involved conversion to CO2

- **Non-ligninolytic mechanism**: Comparable levels of BTEX degradation observed with wild-type and per mutant (which lacks ability to produce LiP and MnP), confirming that extracellular peroxidases are not involved

- **Mycelial association**: Degradative activity is associated with mycelial pellets

- **Temperature**: Increased degradation at 25C compared with 37C, and under oxygenated conditions

- **p-Cresol and Phenol**: P. chrysosporium degrades high concentrations (150 mg/L p-cresol, 50 mg/L phenol), individually or in combination

All components of the BTEX mixture were simultaneously degraded and, except for toluene, degradation occurred to similar extents whether tested individually or as a mixture.

### Degradation by Other Fungi

Cladophialophora sp. strain T1 degrades alkylated benzenes such as toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylenes through a combination of assimilation and cometabolism, though it does not metabolize benzene alone. During toluene metabolism, hydroxylation to benzyl alcohol occurs prior to ring cleavage. Three white-rot fungi (P. chrysosporium, P. ostreatus, and Coriolus versicolor) degraded total petroleum hydrocarbons in contaminated soil under nitrogen-rich conditions.

## Other Pollutants

- **Chlorobenzenes**: P. chrysosporium degrades both mono- and dichlorobenzenes; simultaneous degradation of chloro- and methyl-substituted benzenes observed

- **Trichloroethylene (TCE)**: P. chrysosporium degrades TCE under nutrient-rich conditions via an alternative enzyme system not linked to LiP or MnP; most TCE converted to CO2

- **Pulp mill effluents**: MnP plays primary role in bleach plant effluent (BPE) decolorization by P. chrysosporium; laccase is primary for T. versicolor

- **Resin acids**: Mortierella isabellina hydroxylates dehydroabietic, abietic, and isopimaric acids

## Field Applications and Limitations

White rot fungi have been successfully applied to remediate contaminated field soils. P. chrysosporium depleted 2,4,5-T and pentachlorophenol from contaminated soil samples. In field studies with P. chrysosporium and P. sordida, 88-91% of PCP was depleted within 6.5 weeks even under suboptimal temperature conditions. Spent sawdust cultures of [[staycare-lentinula-edodes-in-bioremediation]] reduced PCP levels in soil by 44.4-60.5%.

However, the fungus must compete with [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]], and conditions favoring peroxidase production may enhance growth of antagonistic pseudomonads. P. chrysosporium can degrade xenobiotics in soil under non-sterile conditions, indicating ability to compete under natural conditions. Optimization of environmental parameters and selection of superior strains remain key to improving [[fungal-pesticide-and-herbicide-degradation]]
