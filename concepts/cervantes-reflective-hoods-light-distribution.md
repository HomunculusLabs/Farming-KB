# Reflective Hoods and Light Distribution

Reflective hoods are critical components of indoor cannabis lighting
systems. They ultimately dictate lamp placement, the distance between
lamps and above the plants, and how evenly light is distributed across
the garden. The proper hood can double the effective growing area. See
also [[cervantes-metal-halide-lighting-systems]],
[[cervantes-high-pressure-sodium-lighting]], and
[[cervantes-lighting-systems]].

## Importance of Even Distribution

Some reflective hoods reflect light more evenly than others. A
reflector that distributes light evenly, with no hot spots, can be
placed closer to plants without burning them. These hoods are most
efficient because the lamp is closer and the light more intense. The
farther the lamp is from the garden, the less light plants receive,
following the inverse square law.

For example, a 1000-watt reflector with a "hot spot" must be placed 36
inches (90 cm) above the garden. A 600-watt lamp with a reflector that
distributes light evenly can be placed only 18 inches above the garden.
When placed closer, the 600-watt lamp shines as much light on the
garden as the 1000-watt bulb.

Growers who use the most efficient reflective hoods harvest up to twice
as much as those who don't. The proper reflective hood over the lamp
and reflective walls can double the growing area.

## Light Requirements by Growth Stage

| Growth Stage  | Foot-candles | Lux    | Hrs of Light |
|---------------|-------------|--------|--------------|
| Seedling      | 375         | 4,000  | 16-24        |
| Clone         | 375         | 4,000  | 18-24        |
| Vegetative    | 2,500       | 27,000 | 18           |
| Flowering     | 10,000      | 107,500| 12           |

Seedlings, cuttings, and vegetative plants need less light than
flowering plants. Seedlings and clones can survive beneath fluorescent
lights. Vegetative growth requires more light, supplied by a metal
halide or compact fluorescent lamp at the rates indicated above.

## Hood Materials

Reflective hoods are made from steel sheet metal, aluminum, or even
stainless steel. The steel is either cold-rolled or pre-galvanized
before a reflective coating is applied. Pre-galvanized steel is more
rust resistant than cold-rolled steel. This metal can be polished,
textured, or painted, with white being the most common paint color.

Premium hood manufacturers apply white paint in a powder-coating
process. Flat titanium white is the most reflective color and diffuses
light most effectively. Glossy white paint is easy to clean but tends
to create hot spots of light. Sheet metal hoods are less expensive
than aluminum hoods due to reduced materials expense.

## Hood Types and Designs

### Horizontal Reflectors
The most common type. Lamps hang horizontally in an enclosed hood.
The Hydrofarm reflector is one of the best values in America. It
reflects light well and "breathes." The hammer-finish specular interior
diffuses light effectively.

### Wide Reflectors
Hortilux sets the standard with their line of reflectors, delivering
the most light. The deflector under the bulb allows it to be super
close to plants. Place a Hortilux 1000-watt fixture four feet (1.2 m)
above the garden.

### Adjust-A-Wing
One of the most popular reflectors. Wing reflectors can be adjusted
to provide more or less light spread. They are excellent for promoting
even growth and can be placed very close to plants without burning
foliage.

### Parabolic Dome Reflectors
Vertical parabolic dome reflective hoods distribute light evenly over a
large area and are perfect for vegetative growth. The Ecotechnics
Diamond reflector from Spain is very efficient.

### Air-Cooled Reflectors
Some reflectors feature air-ventilated tubes. The Easy Green reflector
has holes for forced-air ventilation and can be placed close to plants
because the heat is exhausted through the tube.

### Wall-Mounted Reflectors
Fixtures designed to be mounted next to a wall reflect light down and
away from the wall, maximizing corner space utilization.

## Inefficient Designs to Avoid

The "cone" reflector is one of the least efficient available. Much
reflected light is wasted. Vertical reflectors covered in plastic where
the bulb hangs between plants can also be less efficient unless
carefully positioned.

Cavita invented a lamp with the reflector inside the bulb, considered
one of the most efficient designs available. However, most growers use
external hoods with standard HID bulbs.

## Lamp Spacing

Nearly all stationary lamps have bright centers and dimmer edges.
Even distribution from an efficient hood mitigates this. Promote even
growth by arranging plants under lamps so the brighter centers
illuminate the outer plants and the dimmer edges fall on inner plants.

Growers prefer high-wattage lamps (400, 600, and 1000-watt) because
the light is brighter and penetrates deeper into the foliage. Smaller
250-watt bulbs are popular for small gardens up to three feet square.

## Complementary Strategies

Reflective walls increase light in the growing area. Up to 95 percent
of escaping light can be reflected back toward plants. See
[[cervantes-reflective-materials-grow-room-walls]] for wall materials.
[[cervantes-light-movers]] can also improve distribution from
stationary hoods by eliminating hot spots.
