## Darwin's Power of Movement in Plants and the Discovery of Auxin

*The Power of Movement in Plants* (1880), co-authored by Charles Darwin
and his son Francis Darwin, is one of the most influential works in the
history of plant physiology. The book documented years of meticulous
experiments on plant movement and tropisms, and its findings directly set
the stage for the discovery of auxin, the first plant hormone to be
identified. The work exemplifies the Darwinian method of careful,
extended observation followed by systematic experimentation to uncover
the mechanisms underlying biological phenomena.

## Background and Context

By the time Charles Darwin turned his attention to plant movement in the
1870s, he had already published *On the Origin of Species* (1859) and
several other major works. Darwin's interest in plants was longstanding;
his earlier work on insectivorous plants, *Insectivorous Plants* (1875),
had demonstrated his fascination with the sensitivity and responsiveness
of plant organisms. His son Francis, who became a distinguished botanist
in his own right, collaborated extensively on the movement studies,
contributing both experimental work and physiological insight.
