## Overview

Water-use efficiency (WUE) describes the ratio of carbon
fixed through
photosynthesis to water lost through transpiration. It is a
fundamental
trait linking plant productivity to water availability and a
critical
consideration in crop breeding, [[mollison-designers-climate-change-adaptation-strategies]]. WUE is typically expressed as biomass
produced per
unit of water transpired or as the ratio of net CO2
assimilation to
stomatal conductance, known as intrinsic WUE.

## Physiological Basis

At the leaf level, WUE emerges from the dual role of
stomata: they must
open to allow CO2 diffusion inward for photosynthesis, but
opening
simultaneously permits water vapor to escape through
transpiration. This
tradeoff between carbon gain and water loss is the central
constraint
governing WUE across all terrestrial plants.

Stomatal conductance responds dynamically to atmospheric
CO2, vapor
[[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]] due to limited CO2
supply.

## Definitions and Measurement

- **Intrinsic WUE (iWUE)**:is the ratio of net CO2 assimilation (A)
to stomatal conductance (gs), measured at the leaf level
with gas
exchange systems. It reflects stomatal behavior independent
of
[[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]], integrating physiological,
morphological,
and phenological adaptations.
- **Ecosystem WUE**:compares net primary productivity to
evapotranspiration, relevant for hydrological modeling and
land
management.

## Biochemical Regulation

Rubisco catalyzes both carboxylation and oxygenation of
RuBP. When
oxygenation dominates, photorespiration consumes energy and
releases CO2
without fixing carbon, reducing WUE. C4 and CAM
photosynthetic pathways
represent evolutionary solutions: C4 plants concentrate CO2
at the
Rubisco site using a carbon pump, while CAM plants
temporally separate
CO2 fixation (night) from the Calvin cycle (day).

Elevated atmospheric CO2 increases WUE by allowing partial
stomatal
closure while maintaining adequate internal CO2
concentrations. This CO2
fertilization effect has been observed across most C3 crop
species.
