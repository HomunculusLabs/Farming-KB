# Energy, Fiber, and Other Non-Food Crops

The [[jeavons-grow-biointensive-method-overview]] method envisions the mini-farm as producing not only food
but also materials for clothing, building, paper, and other needs. Energy,
fiber, and miscellaneous crops expand the self-sufficiency of the garden
beyond dietary needs.

## The Five Crop Groups

Jeavons organizes mini-farm crops into five broad groups:

1. Grains, protein sources, and vegetable oil crops

2. Compost, [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]], and fodder crops

3. Tree and cane food crops

4. Vegetable crops for additional vitamins and minerals

5. Energy, fiber, paper, and miscellaneous crops

The fifth group is the focus of this page. After three or four years of garden
development, the vegetable crop area can be condensed from 200 square feet to
100 square feet, freeing 100 square feet of improved soil for protein crops,
fibers (cotton or flax), or special interest crops.

## Fiber Crops

### Cotton

Regular cotton yields approximately 300 pounds per 1,000 square feet in the
GROW BIOINTENSIVE system. A tree cotton variety from Africa is a perennial
under research. Cotton cultivation requires attention to soil fertility, as it
can deplete soil nutrients.

### Flax

Flax is grown for both fiber and seed. It is one of the oldest cultivated
fiber crops and can be processed for linen production on a small scale. Flax
is also noted as a companion plant for carrots and potatoes, deterring potato
bugs and improving growth and flavor.

### Kenaf

Kenaf is grown for toilet paper, fiber, twine, and rope. It is a fast-growing
crop suitable for small-scale fiber production.

## Energy and Building Crops

### Bamboo

Two types of bamboo are included in the Master Charts: paper bamboo and
regular bamboo. Both are listed as under research, indicating that [[miniculture-production-yield-data-psilocybe-cubensis-bigwood-beug]]
and growing instructions are still being developed. Bamboo has enormous
potential as a renewable [[fungal-building-material-deterioration]], and many varieties are suitable
for small-scale cultivation.

### Sunflowers

Sunflower seeds are very high in fat, making them an excellent energy crop.
They are included in the garden plans at various stages. However, sunflowers
should be used in moderation to avoid excessive copper uptake. In the
one-person garden, 2 sunflower seedlings are planted in the first year; in the
family garden, up to 4 seedlings occupy 15 square feet.

### Sugar Beets

Sugar beets yield approximately 1,600 pounds per 1,000 square feet. They are
a significant calorie and energy crop that can be processed into sugar on a
small scale.

## Miscellaneous Crops

The Master Charts also include entries for crops that serve specialized
purposes:

**Guayule** is a source of natural rubber, under research for small-scale
cultivation.

**Jojoba** is grown for its oil. The notation "x 50" suggests it may be used
as a hedgerow plant with 50 plants in the spacing.
