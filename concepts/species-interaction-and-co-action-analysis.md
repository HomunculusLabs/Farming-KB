# Species Interaction and Co-Action Analysis

Species interaction analysis is a foundational methodology in [[permaculture]] categorizes all possible two-species interactions into nine outcomes based on whether each species benefits (+), is harmed (−), or remains unaffected (0):

| Interaction | Actor | Other | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mutualism | + | + | Nitrogen-fixing legumes with fruit trees |
| Synnecrosis | − | − | War between competing species |
| Amensalism | − | 0 | Wasp parasite glued to a resistant pine tree |
| Allolimy | 0 | − | Walnut roots secreting juglones that weaken nearby apple trees |
| Commensalism | + | 0 | Epiphytic vanilla growing on a sturdy coconut trunk |
| Allotrophy | 0 | + | Teacher-student relationship; charity |
| Parasitism | + | − | Pathogens and parasites weakening a host |
| Self-sacrifice | − | + | Parental care; altruism within species |
| Neutrality | 0 | 0 | Many plant species appear basically neutral |

## The Interaction Matrix

When designing cultivated systems, permaculture departs from purely observational ecology by introducing intentional value hierarchies. If the designer values only one species (e.g., a fruit tree) and wishes to suppress another (e.g., a weed like Lantana), an interaction matrix can be constructed ranking companion species by their net effect on the valued crop.

The matrix scores pairings from most beneficial to most harmful:
- **(++)**: Both species strongly benefit
- **(0+)**: Actor unaffected, other benefits
- **(+0)**: Actor benefits, other unaffected
- **(++)**: Mutual benefit
- **(00)**: Mutual neutrality
- **(−−)**: Mutual harm
- **(+−)**: Actor benefits at other's expense
- **(0−)**: Actor unaffected, other harmed
- **(+0)**: Actor benefits, other unaffected

## Field Scoring Methodology

Co-action analysis is conducted through systematic field observation:

1. **Select a target species** (e.g., apple trees) as the reference organism
2. **Define scoring criteria** — for apples: + (healthy, bearing well), 0 (fair, bearing), − (poor, sick, or dying), x (absent)
3. **Survey paired plantings** across many sites (e.g., backyard trees in a town)
4. **Record the understory** — healthy untended apple trees often grow with spring bulbs, comfrey, clover, iris, and nasturtium
5. **Build the co-action matrix** with the target species across the top and companion species down the side, tallying scores from site visits

Key empirical finding: walnut trees produce (0−) or (−−) effects on apples (juglone toxicity), while mulberries and Acacias coexist with apples in (+0) and (00) relationships respectively.

## Guild Building Through Intervention

The most powerful application of co-action analysis is the strategic use of **intervenor species** to resolve conflicts between otherwise incompatible plants:

- Apple next to walnut → (−0): harmful to apple
- Apple next to mulberry → (+0): acceptable
- Mulberry next to walnut → (00): neutral

By inserting a mulberry between apple and walnut, the net result becomes (+00) — the harmful interaction is effectively cancelled through spatial arrangement with an intervening buffer species.
