# Lichenized Fungi — Biology, Ecology, and Diversity

Lichens are composite organisms consisting of a fungal partner (mycobiont) and a photosynthetic partner (photobiont) that grow symbiotically to form a coherent structure (thallus). With >13,500 species — about 20% of all described fungi — lichenized fungi represent one of the most successful fungal lifestyles.

## The Lichen Symbiosis

### Nature of the Relationship
The fungal-photobiont relationship has been described as:
- **Mutualism:** Both partners benefit; fungus gains carbohydrates, photobiont gains desiccation protection
- **Controlled parasitism:** Fungus benefits; photobiont grows more slowly than when alone
- **Ecologically mutualistic:** The integrated lichen thallus survives in more habitats and is more abundant than either partner alone

### Partners
- **Mycobiont:** The fungal partner (names refer to the fungus)
- **Photobiont:** Alga or cyanobacterium; only ~150-200 species participate in lichen symbioses
- An individual thallus usually has one species of each
- Only the fungal partner reproduces sexually within the association
- The partners have independent phylogenies — lichen thallus is a product of coevolution

## Morphological Types

Most lichen thalli fall into five general categories:

1. **Leprose:** Mealy particles of intertwined fungal hyphae and algal cells
2. **Crustose:** Embedded on or in the surface of the substratum (most common)
3. **Squamulose:** Small flakes of thallus
4. **Foliose:** Leaflike, with distinct upper and lower surfaces
5. **Fruticose:** Shrubby or stringlike

## Ecological Characteristics

### Growth and Competition
- Slow growth; small size; long life
- Nonseasonal vegetative morphology
- Mineral nutrition mostly from airborne sources
- Greater tolerance of desiccation than most photosynthetic organisms
- Poor competitors — extreme examples of Grime's stress-tolerator lifestyle
- Compete most evenly only with bryophytes

### Distribution
- Found in every terrestrial habitat capable of supporting photosynthesis
- A few species occur in aquatic habitats
- Major controlling factors:
  1. Substratum chemistry, stability, and longevity
  2. Light availability (mediated through competition)
  3. Moisture availability

### Common Substrata
- Rock surfaces
- Woody plant bark and wood
- Soil and dead organic matter (in low productivity environments)
- Broad evergreen leaves (humid tropics)
- Most species have distinct substratum/habitat preferences

## Taxonomy

### Key Point: Not a Monophyletic Group
Lichenized fungi belong to several distantly related orders and families. The lichen habit has evolved on separate occasions in different fungal groups. Their cohesiveness relates to similar ecological/functional roles and common constraints of the lichen habit, not shared ancestry.

### Major Groups
- Primarily Ascomycota (most lichenized species)
- Some Basidiomycota (about 1% of lichenized species)
- A few former Deuteromycota

### Identification
- Scientific names refer to the fungal partner
- Thallus morphology is a product of the interaction — isolated mycobionts on agar form typical fungal colonies without lichen structures
- DNA analyses have revolutionized lichen systematics

## Biodiversity and Conservation

### Global Diversity
- >13,500 described species of lichenized fungi
- Many regions remain poorly collected, especially tropics
- Lichens are excellent bioindicators of air quality and habitat continuity

### Sampling Advantages
Because lichen thalli are:
- Physically compact and spatially circumscribed
- Distinguishable from the substratum
- Similar to plants as photosynthetic units

...lichenized fungi have been subjects of far more quantitative sampling studies than other fungi. Methods for assessing lichen diversity can be more similar to those for plants than for other fungi.

### Conservation Concerns
- Extremely sensitive to air pollution (especially sulfur dioxide and nitrogen)
- Slow growth and long life make populations slow to recover from disturbance
- Old-growth forests support distinctive lichen communities
- Many species are habitat specialists with restricted ranges
- Climate change poses significant threats

## Sampling for Biodiversity Inventory

- Quantitative surveys preferred over nonquantitative biotic surveys
- Minimum levels of quantification and repeatability required
- Data contribute to management strategies for conservation
- Monitor air quality through lichen community composition
- Both natural and artificial substrata can be used
- Crustose species require different techniques than foliose/fruticose
