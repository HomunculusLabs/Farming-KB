# Nutrient Lockout in Cannabis

Nutrient lockout occurs when nutrients present in the growing medium or solution become unavailable for plant uptake, despite being physically present. This is one of the most frustrating problems for cannabis growers because plants show deficiency symptoms even when nutrients have been properly applied.

## What Is Nutrient Lockout?

Nutrient lockout is a condition where one or more essential nutrients cannot be absorbed by plant roots, typically caused by:

- **Incorrect pH**: The most common cause — pH outside the optimal range changes the chemical form of nutrients
- **Nutrient antagonism**: Excess of one nutrient makes another unavailable
- **Salt buildup**: Toxic [[jadam-salt-accumulation-in-soil]] in the growing medium locks up nutrients
- **Chemical precipitation**: Nutrients combine into insoluble compounds

## pH-Related Lockout

Incorrect pH contributes to most serious nutrient disorders in cannabis gardens.

### Soil Gardens

- **Optimal pH**: approximately 6.5
- **Phosphorus**: Becomes unavailable when soil is too acidic and excess iron and zinc exist
- **Potassium**: Usually present in soil but locked in by high salinity
- **Magnesium**: May be present in soil but unavailable to the plant
- **Micronutrients**: Iron, manganese, zinc, and others become unavailable at high pH

### Hydroponic Gardens

- **Optimal pH**: 5.5-6.5
- pH fluctuations are somewhat less critical than in soil because nutrients remain available in solution
- Electrical conductivity (EC) is the most critical indicator of plant health and [[plant-nutrient-uptake-mechanisms]] in hydroponics
- When pH is too high, nutrients become unavailable even though they are present

## Nutrient Antagonism (Lockout by Excess)

Excessive levels of certain nutrients can lock out others:

### Phosphorus Excess
- Interferes with calcium, copper, iron, magnesium, and zinc stability and uptake
- Zinc lockout is the most common result
- Toxic symptoms manifest as deficiencies of zinc, iron, magnesium, calcium, and copper
- Treatment: Flush growing medium with a mild, complete fertilizer

### Calcium Excess
- Exacerbates deficiencies of potassium, magnesium, manganese, and iron
- These nutrients become unavailable even though they are present
- In hydroponics, excess calcium precipitates with sulfur to form insoluble gypsum (calcium sulfate)
- Can stunt growth if applied in excess early in plant life

### General Over-Fertilization
- Adding too much fertilizer can change the chemical balance of the soil
- Can supply too much of one nutrient, locking in others
- Foliage and roots burn when the condition is severe
- Can toxify soil and bind up nutrients, making them unavailable

## Salt Buildup

Containers have very little growing medium to hold nutrients, making toxic salt buildup a common problem:

- As water evaporates and plants absorb nutrients, salt concentration increases
- High salinity locks potassium and other nutrients in the soil
- Symptoms: burned leaf tips, discolored misshapen leaves, brittle leaves
- More pronounced in containers than in ground plantings

## Treatment and Recovery

### Flushing
- Flush growing medium with three times the volume of water for the volume of medium
- For severe problems, use more water
- Flush with plain water or a very mild, complete fertilizer
- Some growers flush with plain water the last two weeks of flowering

### pH Correction
- Adjust pH to the optimal range for your growing method
- Use a quality pH meter for accurate readings
- Make gradual adjustments — avoid large pH swings

### Nutrient Balance
- Use complete, balanced fertilizers
- Follow dosage instructions carefully
- Adding more fertilizer will not fix lockout and may worsen it
- Start with an EC of 1.6 and build up as needed; absolute maximum EC is 2.3

## Prevention

- Monitor pH regularly in both soil and hydroponic systems
- Use a quality pH meter for accurate readings
- Do not over-fertilize — follow manufacturer instructions
- Flush growing medium periodically to prevent salt accumulation
- Use fresh growing medium for each crop
- Monitor EC in hydroponic systems
- Use chelated micronutrients for better availability

## Diagnostic Difficulty

Once a plant shows symptoms, it has already undergone severe nutritional stress. It will take time for the plant to resume vigorous growth. [[query-how-to-set-up-an-indoor-cannabis-grow-on-a-budget]] crops are harvested so fast that plants do not always have time to recover from nutrient imbalances. One small imbalance could cost a week of growth — more than 10% of the plant's life.
