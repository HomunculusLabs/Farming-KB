# Mushroom Cooking Guide

## Overview

Cooking wild and cultivated mushrooms requires species-specific knowledge. Some mushrooms are edible raw, many require cooking, and a few contain toxins that must be neutralized through heat. This guide covers preparation by species, toxin neutralization, drying and powdering techniques, and basic recipes.

## General Preparation Principles

All wild mushrooms should be positively identified before consumption. When in doubt, throw it out. Clean mushrooms with a brush or damp cloth rather than submerging them, as they absorb water like sponges. Trim woody stems and discard any buggy or rotting portions.

## Species-Specific Preparation

### Amanitas (Amanita muscaria, A. pantherina)

Amanita muscaria is widely consumed in parts of Europe and Asia after proper preparation. It contains ibotenic acid (nauseating, psychoactive) and muscimol (the desired compound). Heat and decarboxylation convert ibotenic acid to muscimol.

Neutralization steps:
1. Peel the colored skin (cuticle) from the cap
2. Slice caps thinly
3. Parboil in abundant water for 15-20 minutes, discard water
4. Repeat parboil 2-3 times
5. Finish by sauteing or drying

Some traditions also use an air-drying or oven-drying step at low heat (50-60C) before boiling, which itself converts some ibotenic acid to muscimol. Never consume raw or inadequately prepared amanitas.

### Morels (Morchella spp.)

Morels must always be cooked. Raw morels contain small amounts of hydrazine compounds (gyromitrin-like toxins) that can cause gastrointestinal distress. Thorough cooking destroys these compounds.

Preparation:
1. Slice morels in half lengthwise to check for insects and debris
2. Soak briefly in salted water to dislodge bugs, then pat dry
3. Cook thoroughly — saute for at least 5-7 minutes or simmer in soups
4. Never eat raw morels; even small amounts can cause illness

### Boletes (Boletus spp.)

Most boletes are excellent edibles. Remove the spongy pore layer (tube layer) under the cap if it is slimy, soft, or discolored. Firm, white pores are generally fine to eat. Slice and saute, or dry for later use. Beware of boletes with red pore surfaces (some are toxic, like Boletus satanas).

### Chanterelles (Cantharellus spp.)

Chanterelles are straightforward to cook. Clean with a brush, slice, and saute in butter. They pair well with cream sauces, eggs, and poultry. Avoid overcooking — they can become rubbery.

### Honey Mushrooms (Armillaria spp.)

These must be thoroughly cooked. Boil for 10-15 minutes first, then saute. Some people are sensitive even to well-cooked honey mushrooms, so eat a small portion first.

## Drying and Powdering

### Air Drying

Thread mushrooms on a string and hang in a warm, dry, well-ventilated area. Works well for boletes, polypores, and thin-fleshed species. Takes 3-14 days depending on humidity and thickness.

### Dehydrator Drying

Set to 35-50C (95-120F). Higher temperatures can degrade flavor compounds and medicinal constituents. Slice mushrooms 3-5mm thick for even drying. Drying time: 4-12 hours depending on species and thickness.

### Oven Drying

Use the lowest setting with the door propped open. Less ideal than a dehydrator due to uneven heat, but functional.

### Powdering

Fully dried mushrooms can be ground into powder using a coffee grinder, blender, or mortar and pestle. Store powder in airtight glass jars away from light. Mushroom powder keeps for 6-12 months. Use as a seasoning, in soups, or as a nutritional supplement. See [[turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor]] for medicinal powder use.

## Recipes

### Basic Sauteed Wild Mushrooms

Ingredients: 500g mixed mushrooms, 3 tbsp butter, 2 cloves garlic, salt, pepper, fresh thyme.

1. Clean and slice mushrooms
2. Heat butter in a large pan over medium-high heat
3. Add mushrooms in a single layer — do not crowd the pan
4. Cook without stirring for 2-3 minutes to allow browning
5. Stir and cook another 3-5 minutes until liquid evaporates
6. Add minced garlic, cook 30 seconds
7. Season with salt, pepper, and thyme

### Dried Mushroom Soup

Ingredients: 30g dried porcini or mixed mushrooms, 1L broth, 1 onion, 2 carrots, celery, cream, salt, pepper.

1. Soak dried mushrooms in warm water for 20 minutes, reserve soaking liquid
2. Chop vegetables, saute in butter until soft
3. Add mushrooms, soaking liquid (strained), and broth
4. Simmer 20 minutes
5. Blend partially for texture, add cream, season

### Mushroom Powder Seasoning

Mix dried powdered mushrooms with salt, garlic powder, black pepper, and smoked paprika. Use as a rub for meats or sprinkle on eggs, pasta, and popcorn.

## Safety Notes

- Never eat a mushroom you cannot identify with 100% certainty
- Cook all wild mushrooms unless you know the species is safe raw
- When trying a new species for the first time, eat only a small amount and wait 24 hours
- Alcohol consumed with some mushrooms (especially Coprinopsis atramentaria) can cause severe reactions
- See [[morel-mushroom-guide]] for morel-specific foraging and safety information
