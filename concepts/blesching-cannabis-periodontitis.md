# Cannabis and Periodontitis

## Overview

Periodontitis is an inflammation of the tissues that support the teeth. Thought
to be caused by oral microbes and an overzealous immune response, it leads to
the reduction of tissue support and alveolar bone loss. The process of
resorption initiates this deficiency where bone cells leak substances and
become weak.

According to the [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]], the evidence for cannabis in treating
periodontitis is based on 1 study reviewed with a CHI value of 2. While the
evidence base is small, the findings are promising and the biological
mechanism involving CB2 receptors and bone metabolism is well-established.

## Symptoms and Consequences

Periodontitis symptoms may include:

- Painful swelling of the gums and tissue surrounding the teeth
- Tissue reduction
- Deep pockets around the teeth
- Bad breath (halitosis)
- Loose teeth

Periodontitis may also be implicated in the development of:

- Heart attacks
- Strokes
- Atherosclerosis

The connection between oral health and cardiovascular disease underscores the
systemic importance of periodontal health. Chronic inflammation in the mouth
can contribute to inflammatory processes throughout the body.

## Conventional Treatment

Orthodox medical treatments include:

- Deep cleaning (scaling and root planing)
- Surgical cleaning and interventions
- Alternative treatments involving [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] or other oral oxidants

These approaches primarily target the bacterial component but may not fully
address the immune-mediated bone destruction that characterizes advanced
periodontitis.

## Cannabis and Periodontitis: The Evidence

### CBD and Bone Resorption (2009, University of Uberaba, Brazil)

The first known study to examine the therapeutic effects of cannabidiol on
resorption-produced periodontitis took place in Brazil. The analysis
demonstrated that CBD-treated rats presented a reduction of alveolar bone
loss.

The authors wrote: "These results indicate that CBD may be useful to control
bone resorption during progression of experimental periodontitis in rats."

## Endocannabinoid System and Bone Health

CB1 and CB2 receptors are found in bone tissue. Anandamide (the body's
natural endocannabinoid) is produced in bone and synovial tissue. This
discovery has opened new avenues for understanding how the endocannabinoid
system regulates bone metabolism:

- **Bone formation**: CB1 stimulation appears to promote bone formation by
  osteoblasts (bone-building cells)
- **Bone resorption**: CB2 activation appears to inhibit bone resorption by
  osteoclasts (bone-breakdown cells)
- **CBD's role**: CBD has a greater affinity for CB2, making it a potential
  therapeutic agent for conditions involving excessive bone breakdown

The pre-clinical finding that CBD aided in mitigating periodontitis-related
bone loss is consistent with broader research on cannabinoids and bone
metabolism. Related research from Israel, Switzerland, and Sweden has shown
that CBD and other primarily CB2-activating cannabinoids have the capacity
to make [[blesching-cannabis-fractured-bones-cbd-healing]] stronger while they heal.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Indica and indica-dominant strains tend to contain a lower THC:[[cannabis-thc-cbd-ratio-guide-for-conditions]],
resulting in a relatively higher activation of CB2 receptors. For
periodontitis, where the therapeutic target is CB2-mediated bone protection,
CBD-rich preparations may be most appropriate.

Topical oral applications (mouthwashes, oils, or tinctures) infused with
CBD-rich cannabis preparations could potentially deliver cannabinoid therapy
directly to affected gum and bone tissue, maximizing local effects while
minimizing systemic exposure.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Periodontitis

Studies have identified possible psychological, behavioral, physiological,
and demographic risk factors implicated in the progression of simple
gingivitis to the development of periodontitis. Relationships have been
observed between ongoing stressors and periodontal disease:

- Financial difficulties
- Grief and loss
- Caring for a spouse with Alzheimer's disease or other dementias
- Depressed mood
- General perceived and ongoing stress or hassles

### Physiological Stress Response

Physiological responses to chronic stress include:

- Elevation in pro-inflammatory cytokines
- Reduction of salivary flow

Both of these factors contribute to reducing the body's ability to properly
respond to bacterial infection, which increases the risk for periodontitis.
