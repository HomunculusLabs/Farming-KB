# Winter Greenhouse Weed Control

[[fukuoka-natural-weed-management]] in winter greenhouses demands a proactive, multi-layered approach. Eliot
Coleman's system combines strict seed-bank prevention, pre-emergence flaming, and precise
cultivating tools to keep greenhouse beds nearly weed-free through the cold months.

## The Weed Seed Bank

The single most important principle of weed control is straightforward: **never let weeds
go to seed**. Every mature weed that goes unpulled deposits hundreds or thousands of seeds
into the soil, creating a persistent seed bank that can persist for years.

- Hand-weed both fields and greenhouses diligently.
- Research studies confirm that 3 to 5 years of preventing new weed seeds from entering
  the soil will dramatically reduce the weed-seed load in a bed.
- After this period, weed pressure drops to a manageable level, making ongoing maintenance
  far less labor-intensive.

This long-term investment in seed-bank depletion is the foundation upon which all other
weed-control techniques build. Skipping even one season of diligence can undo years of
progress.

## Pre-Emergence Flaming

[[flame-weeding]] is one of the most effective tools in the winter greenhouse arsenal. A
propane torch is passed over the bed to kill germinated weed seedlings **before** the crop
emerges, giving the crop a critical head start.

### How It Works

- The propane flame raises the temperature of exposed weed seedlings high enough to
  destroy their cellular structure.
- Crop seeds planted at normal sowing depths remain insulated by the surrounding soil and
  are unharmed.
- Only weeds that have already germinated and emerged above the soil surface are
  vulnerable.

### Timing the Flame

Proper timing separates a successful flaming pass from a wasted one (or worse, crop
damage):

1. **Prepare the bed up to 2 weeks before sowing** the crop. Work the soil and create a
   [[solomon-fine-seedbed-soil-tilth-clod-management-raking]].
2. **Irrigate the area** to encourage weed seeds already in the soil to germinate.
3. **Monitor weed emergence** and flame when a flush of weed seedlings appears but before
   the crop is sown or has emerged.
4. **Track crop germination timing** — keep notes on days-to-germination for each crop
   variety so you can predict when the crop will emerge. Alternatively, lay glass panes
   over a section of the sown row to warm the soil and observe germination under the
   pane, allowing you to flame the rest of the bed at the last safe moment.

### Flaming Technique

The goal is to **wilt** weeds, not burn them:

- The target temperature is **160°F (71°C)** applied for roughly **1 second**. At this
  temperature, the protein cells in the weed seedling melt and the plant collapses.
- This technique is effective only on weed seedlings **under 1 inch tall**.
- Wilting uses approximately **one-third the propane** of a full burn. Burning weeds to
  ash is wasteful and unnecessary.

### Flamer Design

A well-designed flamer improves effectiveness and fuel efficiency:

- **Properly sized nozzles** ensure a consistent, even flame [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]] the bed width.
- A **hood** or shroud contains and directs the heat downward, reducing fuel waste and
  improving control.
- A **height-adjustable roller** keeps the torch at the optimum distance from the soil
  surface, ensuring consistent heat delivery without needing to estimate by eye.

## Cultivating Tools

Once the crop has emerged, mechanical cultivation takes over. Coleman emphasizes two tools
that he personally designed for this work:

### The Collinear Hoe

A collinear hoe has its blade mounted in line with (parallel to) the handle. This
geometry allows the user to stand upright and slice weeds just below the soil surface
with a pulling motion, working between closely spaced crop rows.

### The Wire Weeder

The wire weeder is a simple but ingenious tool — essentially a thin wire or blade that
slides just below the soil surface to sever weeds at or below the crown. It excels at
removing tiny seedlings in tight spaces where a hoe blade would be too wide.

### Cultivating Technique

How you use the tools matters as much as which tools you use:

- **Think razors, not axes.** The goal is a shallow, precise cut through the weed stem
  just below the soil surface, not a hacking motion.
- **Work before weeds reach 1 inch tall.** Small weeds are easily severed; larger weeds
  develop deeper root systems and can regrow from below the cut.
- **Hold the hoe like a broom**, with a relaxed grip and a pulling stroke that keeps the
  blade just beneath the surface.
- **Never cultivate deeper than 0.5 inch.** Deep cultivation damages crop roots and
  brings dormant weed seeds to the surface where they germinate.
- **Sunny mornings are ideal.** Weeds severed in warm, dry conditions desiccate quickly
  and die. In cool, damp conditions, cut weeds may re-root.

## Chickweed: The Principal Winter Greenhouse Weed

Of all the weeds encountered in winter greenhouse production, **common chickweed**
(*[[stellaria-media]]*) is the most troublesome. Chickweed is uniquely adapted to the cool,
moist conditions inside a winter greenhouse:

- It **germinates at very low temperatures** — well below the threshold of most other
  weed species and many crops.
- It **sets seed at very low temperatures**, meaning it can complete its entire life
  cycle during the winter months inside a protected structure.
- A single chickweed plant can produce thousands of seeds, making early detection and
  removal critical.

Chickweed is the primary reason why pre-emergence flaming and vigilant hand-weeding are
non-negotiable in the winter greenhouse. If chickweed is allowed to go to seed even once,
it re-establishes a seed bank that will require years of diligence to exhaust.

## Integrated Approach

Coleman's weed-control system works best when all elements are combined:

1. **Deplete the seed bank** over 3–5 years by never allowing weeds to set seed.
2. **Flame pre-emergence** to give each new crop a weed-free start.
3. **Cultivate early and shallow** once the crop is up, using collinear hoes and wire
  weeders with razor-precision technique.
4. **Scout for chickweed aggressively**, since it thrives under the very conditions
  winter greenhouse crops need.

Consistency across all four layers produces beds that are nearly weed-free, reducing
labor and competition for [[eliot-coleman-and-the-winter-harvest-handbook]].
