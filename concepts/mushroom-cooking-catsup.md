## Nutritional Value

Cuthill held mushrooms in the highest regard as food. He
described them as a "most delicious and nutritious esculent"
and stated that mushrooms were "second only to beef and
mutton" among foods. This high estimation reflected the
Victorian view of mushrooms as a substantial, meat-like food
rather than a mere vegetable garnish.

The price of mushrooms during winter was very high, and
Cuthill noted that until people grew them more plentifully,
they would remain expensive. Mushrooms, along with sea-kale
and asparagus, were all natives of Britain, yet their high
winter prices reflected scarcity rather than any inherent
rarity in the natural world.

## Cooking Large Mushrooms

Cuthill provided specific instructions for cooking large
mushrooms that are simple enough for any household:

1. Skin the mushrooms if they are of good size
2. Place them in a dish, one on top of the other
3. Pepper and butter each layer
4. Add no salt, as salt draws out the juices
5. Put the cover on the dish
6. Place in the oven for half an hour
7. Do not remove the cover until they come to table

The prohibition of salt was notable. Cuthill explained that
salt draws out the natural juices of the mushroom, resulting
in a drier, less flavourful dish. The sealed cooking method,
with the cover left on until serving, retained all the
natural moisture and flavour within the dish.

## Cooking Button Mushrooms

For buttons or small mushrooms, a different method applied:

1. Cut off the roots
2. Wash them in water
3. Do not bother peeling them, as the skin is very tender
4. Put them into a saucepan with salt and butter
5. Rub in flour and pepper
6. Put no water in the saucepan
7. Let them stew for half an hour
8. Never allow them to boil

The distinction in preparation between large and small
mushrooms reflects practical Victorian kitchen economics.
Large mushrooms with tougher skins required peeling, while
button mushrooms had such tender skins that peeling was
unnecessary and wasteful. The stewing method for buttons,
without added water, relied on the mushrooms' own moisture
for the cooking liquid.
