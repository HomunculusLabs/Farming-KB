# Mushroom Potency and Harvest Age

The relationship between harvest timing and psychedelic potency
is one of the most debated topics in mycological cultivation.
This page examines the evidence and practical guidance from the
PF TEK and related cultivation literature.

## The Core Debate: Size vs. Potency

Two competing hypotheses exist:

1. **Constant total alkaloids**: The total amount of [[psilocin]] per mushroom is fixed early in development. As the
   mushroom grows, alkaloids are diluted by increased water and
   tissue mass. Therefore, smaller mushrooms are more potent by
   weight.

2. **Alkaloid accumulation**: Psilocybin and psilocin continue to
   be synthesized throughout the mushroom's growth cycle, so
   larger mushrooms contain more total alkaloids.

The evidence largely supports the first view — that early-harvest
specimens are more potent per gram of dried material. However,
larger mushrooms may contain more total alkaloids even if the
concentration per gram is lower.

## Aborts: The Potency Champions

"Aborts" are mushrooms that stop growing at an early stage,
remaining small and undeveloped. They are widely regarded as the
most potent material per gram:

- Aborts form when the [[agar]] work and strain isolation is an advanced technique beyond
  basic PF TEK but represents the path to potency optimization

### Flush Number
- First flush mushrooms are generally considered the most potent
- Subsequent flushes may show reduced potency as substrate
  nutrients are depleted
- However, the difference is often modest and influenced by many
  other factors

## Drying and Potency Preservation

Harvest timing is only half the equation; proper drying preserves
the potency achieved at harvest:

- Heat degrades psilocybin and especially psilocin
- Desiccant drying preserves maximum alkaloid content
- Rapid drying minimizes enzymatic degradation that occurs
  during the slow evaporation period
