## Flowform Water Aeration

## Overview

**Flowform** systems are sculptural water-treatment devices that use precisely shaped basins to induce turbulence in flowing water, dramatically increasing aeration and oxygenation. Developed at the Virbella Institute, Emerson College, Forest Row, East Sussex, UK, Flowforms represent a convergence of art, engineering, and ecology — beautiful sculptural forms that simultaneously purify water, support aquatic life, and embody natural flow patterns.

## Natural Precedent

Flowforms are modeled on naturally occurring **turbulence basins** found in streams flowing over massive sandstones or mudstones. These natural formations have been carved over millennia by the self-organizing dynamics of turbulent water flow. The ancient Romans also recognized and replicated these forms, modeling them in pozzolanic cement for hydrological applications.

The key insight is that turbulent flow in water follows specific, reproducible patterns — and that these patterns can be intentionally designed into artificial basins to achieve predictable aeration and mixing effects.

## Design and Construction

### Physical Form

Flowform basins are cast in **concrete or fibreglass** and can be configured in various arrangements:

- **Elongate** — linear series for channelized flow
- **Truncate** — shortened forms for constrained spaces
- **Symmetrical or asymmetrical** — depending on flow requirements
- **Stepped in line** — cascading vertically
- **Stacked like ladders** — for maximum vertical drop in limited space
- **Spiralled** — to conserve space while maintaining flow length

### The Three Mixing Effects

When water flows through a series of Flowform basins, three distinct mixing mechanisms operate simultaneously:

#### 1. Vertical Turbulence (Plunge Overturn)
As fluid drops from one basin to another through the outlet, it undergoes a **plunge or vertical overturn**. This entrains air from the surface, creating bubbles that dissolve oxygen into the water column. The vertical drop is the primary mechanism for air-water contact.

#### 2. Figure-8 Lateral Flow
Water courses around the basins themselves in a **figure-8 or lateral flow pattern**. This extended flow path increases contact time between water and air, while the curved surfaces create shear forces that break up bubbles into finer droplets — increasing the surface area for gas exchange.

#### 3. Rhythmic Pulsing (Vertical-Lateral Interaction)
The most fascinating effect occurs when the lateral figure-8 flow deflects the vertical drop flow, **switching it from side to side in a regular rhythm**. This pulsing action — alternating between left and right sides of each successive basin — creates additional vortices, counterflows, overfolds, and cusps at the basin edges and in the main flow stream. These secondary turbulence patterns further enhance mixing and aeration.

### Leonardo da Vinci's Observations

These turbulence patterns were clearly portrayed by Leonardo da Vinci in his drawings of flowing water (documented in Popham, *The Drawings of Leonardo da Vinci*, 1946). Modern analysis through computer models and Chappell's catastrophic theory (in *Landform Evolution in Australia*, ANU Press, 1978) for coastal uprush and backwash turbulence has confirmed the distinct vortex structures that da Vinci observed intuitively.

## Practical Applications

### Sewage Treatment
Flowforms stacked in series below sewage pipe outfalls provide **primary treatment** through aeration. The turbulence:

- Introduces dissolved oxygen that supports aerobic decomposition
- Oxidizes pollutants through enhanced gas exchange
- Reduces odor by promoting aerobic rather than anaerobic processes

### Aquaculture Pond Oxygenation
Flowforms placed above fish ponds at pipe inlets increase dissolved oxygen levels, supporting higher stocking densities and healthier aquatic species. The rhythmic pulsing action also creates gentle water movement that prevents stagnation without stressing fish.

### Organic Water Purification
For organically polluted waters (agricultural runoff, greywater systems), Flowforms provide passive aeration without chemical inputs or energy-intensive mechanical aerators. The water's own potential energy (gravity) drives the entire process.

## Advantages Over Conventional Systems

- **Energy efficient**: Uses gravity and flow momentum rather than mechanical pumps or compressors
- **Low maintenance**: No moving parts; the sculptural forms are durable concrete or fibreglass
- **Aesthetic value**: Unlike industrial aerators, Flowforms are visually pleasing sculptural installations
- **Educational function**: They demonstrate natural flow patterns and principles of fluid dynamics
- **Scalable**: Configurations can be adapted from small garden features to large-scale treatment systems
- **Self-organizing**: The turbulence patterns emerge naturally from the basin geometry — no external control needed

## The Design Evolution

Mollison traces the development of Flowform-type systems through a characteristic design evolution:

1. **Wonder** — observation of natural turbulence basins in streams
2. **Sketches** — recording and analyzing the forms
3. **Analysis** — understanding the fluid dynamics involved
4. **Observations** — studying how natural and ancient constructed systems perform
5. **Constructed hydrological basins** — building and testing prototypes
6. **Practical applications** — deploying working systems across a variety of sites

This evolutionary pathway mirrors the general pattern of design development in permaculture: observe nature, understand principles, build prototypes, refine through testing, and deploy practical solutions.
