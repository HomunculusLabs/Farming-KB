## What Is Actively Aerated Compost Tea?

AACT is made by adding compost and microbial nutrients to dechlorinated water
and aerating the mixture with pumped air for one or two days. The bubbling
action strips microbes out of the compost and into the tea, where they grow
and multiply. A teaspoon of compost contains roughly 1 billion bacteria; a
teaspoon of finished AACT contains approximately 4 billion. The tea also
contains fungi, protozoa, and nematodes.

Modern AACTs are distinguished from old-fashioned anaerobic compost teas
(steeping compost or manure in water for weeks), which went anaerobic and
fostered pathogenic organisms including E. coli. AACTs remain aerobic
throughout the brewing process, and aerobic microbes are the beneficial ones.
The air supply must be sufficient to keep the tea aerobic at all times -- if
it smells bad, it is going anaerobic.

## Advantages Over Compost

AACTs are easier to make and much easier to apply than bulk compost. They

## Tailoring the Tea

AACTs can be tailored to plant needs by choosing specific compost and nutrient
additives during brewing:

- **Bacterial dominance:** Add molasses (nonsulfured, so as not to kill
  microbes) as a bacterial food source. Bacterial teas suit annuals,
  vegetables, lawns, and grasses that prefer nitrates.
- **Fungal dominance:** Add kelp, [[teaming-with-microbes-humic-and-fulvic-acids]] acids, fish hydrolysate,
  or aloe vera extract (without preservatives) to encourage fungal growth.
