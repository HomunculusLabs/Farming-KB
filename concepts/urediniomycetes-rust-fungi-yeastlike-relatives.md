# Urediniomycetes: Rust Fungi and Yeastlike Relatives

The Urediniomycetes is a large and diverse class within the phylum
[[ustilaginomycetes-smut-fungi-classification-diversity]] ([[biodiversity-smut-fungi-diversity]]) and the
Hymenomycetes (mushrooms and shelf fungi). The Urediniomycetes is
distinguished by its dimorphic life histories, which typically
include a saprobic yeast phase and a pathogenic filamentous state,
as well as by distinctive [[fungal-woronin-bodies-and-septal-pore-organization]] structures lacking both
dolipore septa and parenthesomes.

## Major Subclasses and Orders

The Urediniomycetes encompasses several well-defined subclasses
and orders:

- **Urediniomycetidae**: Includes the orders Uredinales (rust
  fungi) and Septobasidiales. The rust fungi are among the most
  economically important plant pathogens worldwide, causing
  diseases of cereal crops, trees, and ornamental plants.
- **Microbotryomycetidae**: A heterogeneous group recognized
  mostly by molecular characters. It includes smutlike species of
  Microbotryum and yeastlike species of Rhodotorula,
  Rhodosporidium, and Sporidiobolus.
- **Agaricostilbomycetidae**: Contains [[basidiomycetous-yeasts-ballistoconidia-biocontrol]]
  with simple septate basidia.
- **Atractiellales**: A small order of dimorphic fungi with
  septate basidia.
- **Mixia**: A fern parasite that is probably the most enigmatic
  member of the Urediniomycetes. It was previously classified in
  the Taphrinales of the Ascomycota but was reclassified based on
  molecular phylogenetic analyses.

## The Rust Fungi (Uredinales)

The rust fungi display some of the most complex [[bloomfield-rust-fungus-life-cycles-puccinia-haustoria-spermatia]] known
among fungi. In the most extreme examples, these fungi are
heteroecious, requiring two distantly related host plants to
complete their [[bloomfield-heteroecious-rust-fungi-life-cycles-gymnosporangium]], and macrocyclic, possessing up to five
distinct spore-producing stages (spermogonia, aecia, uredinia,
telia, and basidia). More derived autoecious species with a
reduced number of distinct stages also exist. The economic
importance of rust fungi cannot be overstated: they cause yield
losses in wheat, barley, oats, soybeans, coffee, and numerous
other crops.

The Septobasidiales are of particular interest because of their
extreme specialization on scale insects. These fungi form
elaborate, cushionlike basidiocarps that enclose colonies of scale
insects, upon which the fungus feeds. Some species are
predatory, killing the scale insects, while others maintain a
more mutualistic relationship.

## Yeastlike Members

The basidiomycetous yeasts are a polyphyletic assemblage
distributed across multiple clades of the Urediniomycetes and
other classes. The traditional order Sporidiales, for example,
includes members of no fewer than three clades of the
Urediniomycetes, including the Erythrobasidium and
Agaricostilbum clades. Phylogenetic analyses of 18S rDNA and the
D1/D2 domain of 26S rDNA have been instrumental in resolving
relationships among these yeastlike taxa.

Species of Rhodotorula are among the most commonly encountered
yeastlike urediniomycetes. They are characterized by pink to red
colony pigmentation due to carotenoid production and are
ubiquitous in soil, water, and air. Some species are opportunistic
pathogens of immunocompromised patients. Rhodosporidium is the
teleomorphic (sexual) state of many Rhodotorula species.
