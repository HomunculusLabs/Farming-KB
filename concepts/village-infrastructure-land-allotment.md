## Village Infrastructure and Land Allotment

## Overview

[[permaculture]] village infrastructure encompasses the physical systems, tools, dwellings, and land-use planning that enable a community to function as a self-reliant settlement. Mollison emphasizes that infrastructures for energy, commerce, and land allotment are integral to village design, and that new villages must reserve land for future priority development of both structures and [[lichens-primary-production-soil-formation-dighton]] areas.

## Infrastructure Planning Sequence

The design sequence for village infrastructure follows a logical priority order:

1. **Water supply** — the most critical resource
2. **Energy systems** — both generation and conservation
3. **Access** — roads, pathways, and transportation networks
4. **Dwellings and other structures** — housing and community buildings
5. **Land use** — productive zones, forests, recreation, conservation
6. **Legal, social, and financial systems** — governance and economic frameworks

## Community Tools and Equipment

### Site-Wide Equipment

A village needs shared heavy equipment that individual households cannot economically justify:

| Equipment | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Tractor | General site work, fire control |
| Slasher | Vegetation management, fire breaks |
| Posthole borer | Fencing installation |
| Trailer | Transport of materials |
| Chisel plough / soil conditioner | Land preparation |
| Chipper | Biomass processing, mulch production, fire control |
| Tow truck | Mulch and goods transport |
| Van | Goods distribution |
| Back-pack slasher | Blackberry and brush clearing |

### Workshop Tools

Essential workshop tools can be available on **lease or time-share** through Trust B:

- Drill press
- Lathe
- Radial-arm saw
- Welder
- Planer or thicknesser
- Router

Trust B charges for replacement cost, wear and tear, fuel or power, and a service charge.

### The Leasing Model

A modest tractor-tool-truck hire service, where Trust B leases infrequently used assets to residents as needed, produces significant savings. A sensible village would investigate the advantages of a **total vehicle leasing system** including fleet purchase and insurance, local maintenance, and bulk fuel supply.

## Reserved Land Areas

The following areas should be reserved for future development and community use:

### Educational and Cultural
- School, seminar, and workshop rooms
- Computers and library facilities
- Artwork and craft areas

### Food and Commerce
- Food processing centre
- Café, coffee shop
- Home-baked goods production
- Commercial or surplus sale products

### Industrial
- Noisy/oily work areas: woodwork, metalwork, machine shop, repairs
- Bulk fuels and oils storage
- Grease, air pump, vehicle service areas

### Retail and Visitor Services
- Retail shops (including plant nursery)
- Reception area for visitors

### Agricultural
- Dairy and [[faires-dairy-processing-cheese-and-butter]] centre
- Domestic livestock housing (chickens, goats, pigs, sheep, rabbits)
- Methane generator and ferment tanks
- Sewage, sullage, biomass conversion systems
- Water clean-up systems
- Glasshouses and commercial/special crop areas

### Visitor Accommodation
- Camp area for casual visitors (with toilets and showers)
- Potential youth or student hostel

## Dwelling Types

A healthy village provides a **mix of housing types** to serve diverse needs and age groups:

| Type | Ownership | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Family homes | Individual or trust | 2–5 bedrooms |
| Singles quarters | Strata title flats | 1–2 bedrooms, some guest accommodation |
| Elderly/hospital quarters | Strata and community trust | Single and double bedrooms |
| Terrace housing | Various | Where appropriate for density |

Every village should maintain **one empty strata title** for emergencies such as family breakups, or to stage people into permanent housing.

## Energy Efficiency in Dwellings

### Building Standards

Every house built with proper design can be close to **100% [[cervantes-case-study-energy-efficient-sea-of-green]] for space heating at no extra construction cost**. Solar hot water systems are routine, and photovoltaics are becoming standard. Some American towns have enacted ordinances requiring **60% self-sufficient space heating**.

### The Integrated Village

Modern urban planning that designates sectors as industrial, commercial, residential, or recreational is identified as the **antithesis of good planning** for transport [[desert-house-water-energy-conservation]]. These zones bear little relationship to the natural zonation of function and available time around a settlement.

#### Historical Models

Older cities that evolved in energy-conservative environments demonstrate the value of integration:

- **Florence and Vienna**: Cultural, crafts, trades, commercial, and domestic functions were integrated
- **Berlin (older blocks)**: Housing over street-level shops on the sunny side, trades in accessible interior courtyards (Hofs), market or supply depot nearby
- **Istanbul and India**: Trades grouped in streets or market areas, but total neighbourhoods contain all trades
- **Davis, California**: Energy-conserving legislation encourages non-polluting home businesses and provides bikeways throughout

### Benefits of Integration

Wherever life, work, and recreation are integrated in a dwelling:

- Households are better informed
- Children are less alienated
- Adults are less isolated from social contact
- Complex transport systems are eliminated
- Complementary skills develop in the neighbourhood
- Young people gain capacity to use many materials through informal association with open workshops

## Land Allotment Categories

Land not attached to individual dwellings is allocated across these categories:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Public buildings and services | Community halls, administrative buildings |
| Recreation, trails, and walks | Parks, footpaths, recreational reserves |
| Water storages and reserves | Dams, ponds, energy sites |
| Productive land | Livestock, orchard, nursery, greenhouses |
| Forested areas | Fuel wood, [[honey-locust-pod-fodder-timber-production]] |
| Wildlife and flora reserves | Conservation areas, habitat corridors |
| Easements and wayleaves | Roads, public utility allotments |
