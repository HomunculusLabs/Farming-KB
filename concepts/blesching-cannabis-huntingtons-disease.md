# Cannabis and Huntington's Disease

## Overview

Huntington's disease (HD), also called Huntington's chorea (from the Greek for
"dance"), is a degenerative genetic disease affecting the brain and nervous
system, leading to loss of muscle control and dementia. It is caused by a
mutated form of the Huntington gene, found significantly more often in
Western Europeans. The gene can be passed to offspring: one parent with a
single-pair mutation confers a 50% risk, while dual-pair mutations in both
parents make development virtually certain.

## Disease Profile

Scientists believe the mutated gene codes for proteins that aggregate, clump
together, and corrupt portions of the brain. Symptoms begin with lack of
coordination and ataxia (unsteady gait), progressing to chorea (abnormal
twisting movements), dystonia (muscle contraction and abnormal posturing),
dementia, behavioral difficulties, and eventually death. No curative treatment
exists in orthodox medicine. Management involves physical and speech therapies,
neuroleptics, and nutritional support.

Leading causes of death in HD include pneumonia, heart disease, nutritional
deficiencies, and cerebrovascular disorders.

## Cannabis Health Index Assessment

- Total Number of Studies Reviewed: 5
- CHI Value: 10

## Key Research Findings

- A 1986 study of three HD patients unresponsive to pharmaceuticals received
  oral CBD (300 mg/day, increased to 600 mg/day). After two weeks,
  improvements of 20-40% were observed in objective and subjective tests, with
  only mild transient hypotension as an adverse effect (CHI 3)
- A 1991 University of Arizona double-blind crossover trial with 15
  neuroleptic-free HD patients found CBD no more effective than placebo (CHI 0)
- A 2007 Spanish study demonstrated CBD provides neuroprotection against
  striatal damage, relevant for HD: "This capability seems to be based
  exclusively on the antioxidant properties of CBD" (CHI 2)
- A 2011 Madrid study found Sativex (THC/CBD) protected animals from HD
  progression, providing pre-clinical evidence for its use as a neuroprotective
  agent capable of delaying disease progression (CHI 1)
- A 2011 meta-analysis from Madrid confirmed the endocannabinoid system
  behaves as an endogenous neuroprotective system in both Parkinson's and
  Huntington's diseases. Activation of CB2 receptors leads to slower
  neurodegeneration, and cannabinoids with antioxidant properties or CB2
  activation capability "may represent promising therapeutic agents in HD"
  (CHI 4)

## Mechanisms of Action

The primary mechanisms identified include:
- CB2 receptor activation slowing neurodegeneration
- Antioxidant properties of CBD protecting striatal neurons
- THC/CBD combinations (Sativex) providing neuroprotective effects against
  oxidative injury, a prominent cytotoxic mechanism in HD
- Immune response regulation and nerve cell protection via endocannabinoid
  system modulation

## Strain-Specific Considerations

The relevant cannabinoids include CBD, THC, and Sativex. CBD has greater
affinity for CB2 receptors; THC binds relatively equally to CB1 and CB2.
Sativex is a standardized cannabis extract with THC:CBD ratios similar to
natural cannabis flowers. Indica and indica-dominant strains typically contain
lower THC:CBD ratios, favoring CB2 activation compared to sativas.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Huntington's Disease

HD forces focus on living in the present moment. The archetype of youth
rebelling against predetermined destiny comes to mind. HD engages the
intensity of choice: gratitude for what exists now, or blame and hopelessness.
Another dimension involves family burden and shared destiny, as consciousness
retreats and is replaced by unconscious dance-like movement. Some patients
find meaning in consciously embracing the emerging energy.
