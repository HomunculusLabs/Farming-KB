## Nightshade Family Hexing Herbs

## Overview

Since antiquity, several members of the Nightshade family (Solanaceae)
have been associated with witchcraft in Europe. These plants — Henbane
(Hyoscyamus), Belladonna ([[mandrake-mandragora-officinarum-in-european-folklore]]) — enabled witches to perform feats of occult
wonder and prophecy, hex through hallucinogenic communication with the
supernatural, and transport themselves to far-off places. In *Plants of
the Gods*, Schultes, Hofmann, and Ratsch document their extraordinary
history from ancient oracle practices to medieval witchcraft.

## Chemistry

All four solanaceous species contain relatively high concentrations of
tropane alkaloids, primarily atropine, hyoscyamine, and scopolamine;
other bases are found in trace amounts. Scopolamine, not atropine or
hyoscyamine, produces the hallucinogenic effects. It induces an
intoxication followed by narcosis in which hallucinations occur during
the transition state between consciousness and sleep.

Belladonna contains little scopolamine, but this alkaloid is the main
component of Mandrake and especially of Henbane. The alkaloids are
found throughout the entire plant, with highest concentrations in seeds
and roots. Atropine has served chemists as a model for synthesizing
several hallucinogenic compounds.

Their effects differ from usual natural hallucinogens: they are
extremely toxic, and the user remembers nothing experienced during
intoxication, losing all sense of reality and falling into a deep sleep
like an alcoholic delirium.

## Henbane (Hyoscyamus)

Hyoscyamus has been known and feared from earliest classical periods,
when several kinds were recognized and the black variety (H. niger) was
considered most potent, capable of causing insanity. The ancient
Egyptians recorded their knowledge in the Ebers Papyrus (1500 BCE).
