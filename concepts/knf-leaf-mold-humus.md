# KNF Leaf Mold Humus

Leaf mold is the partially decomposed organic matter found on forest floors beneath deciduous trees. In KNF, leaf mold serves multiple roles: as a collection site for IMO, as a compost ingredient, as a moisture-retaining mulch, and as a source of beneficial fungi and microorganisms. Leaf mold represents the natural end-point of forest nutrient cycling.

## What Leaf Mold Does

- Primary collection site for IMO #1 (microbe collection in lunch boxes)
- Provides habitat for diverse beneficial microorganisms (fungi, bacteria, Actinobacteria)
- Retains moisture while maintaining aeration
- Slow-release source of nutrients and trace minerals
- Forms the base layer for IMO #3 and #4 cultivation piles
- Used as floor cover in FMC production areas
- Contains natural growth factors from decomposed forest vegetation

## Collection Sites

- Under deciduous broadleaf trees (bamboo groves are ideal)
- Near roots of trees whose roots contain sugar
- In mountain areas 150-200 m higher than your field elevation
- Hillside areas near the working field
- Avoid areas near roads or contaminated sites
- Look for loose, soft soil under the leaf mold layer

## Selecting Good Leaf Mold

- Should have visible white fungal threads (beneficial fungi)
- Moist but not waterlogged
- Dark brown color with recognizable leaf shapes
- Earthy, pleasant smell (no putrid or ammonia odor)
- Mix of moist leaf mold and dry fallen leaves is ideal

## Uses in KNF

### IMO Collection
- Bury lunch boxes of cooked rice in leaf mold for IMO #1 collection
- Cover with mix of moist leaf mold and dry fallen leaves
- See [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]] for full IMO process

### IMO #3 Cultivation
- Rice bran mixed with IMO #2 is covered with leaf mold and straw
- Leaf mold provides microbial diversity and moisture retention

### FMC Production Floor
- Cover concrete floors with 20 cm of loess and leaf mold
- Prevents moisture buildup that causes FMC decay
- Spread under vinyl mulch to prevent soil-film sticking

### Compost Ingredient
- Add leaf mold to [[fermented-mixed-compost-fmc]] as a carbon source
- Provides beneficial fungal inoculation
- Improves pile structure and aeration

### Mulch
- Spread several layers of rice straw or leaf mold under vinyl mulch
- Prevents soil-film adhesion and improves aeration

## Leaf Mold Tea

A simple extraction method:
1. Fill a container 1/3 with leaf mold
2. Fill with non-chlorinated water
3. Let steep 1-3 days (aerated if possible)
4. Strain and dilute 1:10 for soil drench
5. Use as microbial inoculant and mild nutrient tea
