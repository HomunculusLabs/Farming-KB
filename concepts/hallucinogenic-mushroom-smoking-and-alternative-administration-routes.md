# Hallucinogenic Mushroom Smoking and Alternative Administration Routes

## Overview

While oral ingestion (chewing raw or dried mushrooms, or brewing them into tea) is the predominant route of administration for hallucinogenic mushrooms, user reports documented on internet platforms describe alternative methods including smoking dried mushrooms and, in rare cases, intravenous injection of mushroom extracts. These alternative routes carry distinct pharmacological implications, risk profiles, and efficacy considerations that differ significantly from standard oral consumption. The existence of these alternative administration methods highlights the diversity of user practices and the importance of harm reduction messaging that addresses the full spectrum of consumption methods.

## Standard Oral Administration

The conventional method of consuming hallucinogenic mushrooms involves eating them raw or dried, or brewing them into a tea. Because mushrooms generally have an unpleasant taste, users typically chop them into smaller pieces before consumption to make the process more manageable. Eating them mixed or cooked into food is also practiced, though this may cause nausea and vomiting according to Lifeline Publications. Users report that the mushrooms can also be dried for later use, providing flexibility in timing of consumption.

When consumed orally, the onset of effects occurs within 10 to 60 minutes, depending on several factors including the modality of ingestion, the user's physical condition, food intake, and prior experience with hallucinogenic substances. The duration of a mushroom "trip" following oral administration typically lasts between 2 and 6 hours, with aftereffects (such as difficulty sleeping) persisting for an additional 2 to 6 hours.

The standard recreational dose ranges from 1 to 3.5 grams of dried mushrooms or 10 to 50 grams of fresh mushrooms, though these ranges should be interpreted with caution given the variable potency of different species and growing conditions. Fresh mushrooms contain approximately 90% water by weight, which accounts for the approximately 10:1 ratio between fresh and dried dosage recommendations. The psychoactive dose of psilocybin is believed to be between 45 micrograms per kilogram of body weight and 315 micrograms per kilogram of body weight.

## Smoking Dried Mushrooms

User reports on internet platforms including Erowid and the Shroomery document the practice of smoking dried hallucinogenic mushrooms. Proponents of this method report that smoking induces faster but milder effects compared to oral ingestion. The faster onset is consistent with the pharmacokinetics of pulmonary drug absorption, which delivers substances to the bloodstream and brain more rapidly than the gastrointestinal route by bypassing first-pass hepatic metabolism.

However, the scientific basis for the psychoactive effects of smoked psilocybin mushrooms is questionable and remains unresolved. Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) and psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) are both tryptamine compounds. The thermal stability of these molecules at the temperatures generated by smoking is uncertain. Many tryptamine compounds, including the structurally related DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine), are known to be effectively vaporized and absorbed through smoking. However, psilocybin's phosphate ester group may make it less amenable to vaporization compared to free-base tryptamines.

The reported milder effects from smoking could be attributable to several possible factors:

- **Partial thermal degradation**: Some active compound may be destroyed by the temperatures of combustion or vaporization, reducing the effective dose delivered to the lungs
- **Incomplete absorption**: Pulmonary absorption efficiency may be lower for psilocybin than for other smoked tryptamines, particularly if the compound degrades before reaching absorptive surfaces
- **Placebo effect**: Expectations of psychoactive effects combined with the mild sensory changes from smoking plant material may produce subjective experiences attributed to psilocybin that do not reflect genuine pharmacological activity
- **Rapid metabolism**: Any psilocin reaching the bloodstream through pulmonary absorption may be metabolized more quickly than orally absorbed psilocin due to the different pharmacokinetic profile
- **Low bioavailability**: The proportion of active compound that survives combustion and is absorbed through pulmonary tissue may be substantially lower than the proportion absorbed through the gastrointestinal tract during oral consumption

## Intravenous Injection of Mushroom Extracts

A more concerning alternative administration route documented in internet reports (referenced on the Shroomery) involves the intravenous injection of mushroom extracts. This method reportedly produces more severe acute effects compared to oral administration. The increased severity is consistent with the pharmacokinetics of intravenous drug delivery, which produces the highest and most rapid peak plasma concentrations of any administration route, bypassing both first-pass metabolism and the rate-limiting absorption processes of the gastrointestinal tract.

The intravenous route eliminates the gradual onset and dose-limiting effects of oral absorption. With oral administration, the body's processing of the mushrooms (including first-pass metabolism in the liver and the time required for gastrointestinal absorption) provides a degree of natural dose limitation. Intravenous injection removes these safeguards, potentially delivering a much larger proportion of the active compounds to the brain in a dramatically shorter timeframe.

The Shroomery report referencing this practice noted that it was described in the literature rather than being a commonly documented user practice, suggesting that intravenous mushroom use is extremely rare. However, even rare practices warrant documentation and harm reduction attention due to the severity of associated risks.

## Mushroom Potency and Dose Variability

Understanding the risks of alternative administration routes requires appreciation of the inherent variability in mushroom potency. Potency depends on the species or variety used, their origin, growing conditions, and age. The most potent species (such as Psilocybe semilanceata) could contain up to 1% psilocybin, with some specific species (such as Psilocybe azurescens and Psilocybe bohemica) reported to have even higher concentrations. In other species (Psilocybe cubensis), psilocybin and psilocin could be present in up to 0.6% concentration.

This variability means that any given batch of mushrooms may contain substantially more or less active compound than expected. With oral administration, this variability is partially mitigated by the body's gradual absorption processes. With intravenous administration, however, the full dose is delivered essentially instantaneously, meaning that unexpectedly potent mushroom material could produce overwhelming psychoactive effects with no opportunity for dose adjustment or discontinuation.

## Tryptamine Chemistry Context

Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) and psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) are structurally similar to the neurotransmitter serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT). Psilocybin is the phosphate ester of psilocin; it is more stable in air and water soluble, but is converted in the body into psilocin, which is the pharmacologically active compound. Psilocin acts on the serotonin system as a 5-HT2A post-synaptic agonist or partial agonist.

In addition to psilocybin and psilocin, two further tryptamines (baeocystin and norbaeocystin) may be present in hallucinogenic mushrooms but are thought to be less active. The classification of hallucinogens by chemical structure includes three major groups: indolealkylamines (tryptamines) such as LSD, psilocin, and psilocybin; phenylethylamines such as mescaline; and cannabinoids. Psilocybin and psilocin's classification as tryptamines places them in the same broad chemical family as DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine), which is well-established as an effective smoked drug due to its thermal stability at vaporization temperatures.

## Acute Psychological Effects Context

The acute psychological effects of hallucinogenic mushrooms provide important context for understanding the risks of alternative administration routes. Subjective effects described by users range from mild feelings of relaxation (comparable to cannabis), giddiness, uncontrollable laughter, energy, joy, and euphoria, through visual enhancement (seeing colours brighter) and visual disturbances (moving surfaces, waves), to delusions, altered perception of real events, images and faces, or genuine hallucinations.

The sensory distortions may be coupled with restlessness, incoordination, feelings of anxiety, impaired judgement of time or distance, a sense of unreality, or even depersonalisation. These negative effects, termed "bad trips" by users, can also involve panic reactions and psychosis-like states. Nearly a quarter of Mixmag survey respondents who had used hallucinogenic mushrooms in the last year reported experiencing a panic attack.

The faster onset and higher peak plasma concentrations associated with smoking or intravenous administration would be expected to increase the probability and intensity of these negative psychological effects, as the user has less time to psychologically adjust to the emerging altered state. The Dutch risk assessment noted that users do not find mushroom use a particularly pleasant experience as it lacks the positive mood-altering (entactogenic) effects of ecstasy, suggesting that the altered state produced by mushrooms may already be challenging for users even with the gradual onset of oral administration.

## Physiological Effects and Cardiovascular Risk

In addition to the psychological risks, the physiological effects of hallucinogenic mushrooms include dizziness, nausea, weakness, muscle aching, shivering, abdominal pain, dilation of pupils (mydriasis), mild-to-moderate increase in heart rate and breathing (tachycardia, tachypnea), and elevation of blood pressure. Generally body temperature remains normal, but pronounced physical symptoms such as severe stomach pain, persistent vomiting, and diarrhoea have been recorded.

The tendency for temporarily increased blood pressure may be a particular risk factor for users with cardiovascular conditions, especially untreated hypertension. This risk is documented in clinical research by Hasler et al (2004), who conducted a double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-effect study of psilocybin in healthy humans. The cardiovascular effects of psilocybin, combined with the rapid onset and potentially higher peak plasma concentrations associated with intravenous administration, create an elevated risk profile for individuals with pre-existing cardiovascular conditions.

## Risks of Alternative Administration Routes

### Smoking Risks

Smoking dried mushroom material introduces several risk factors beyond those associated with oral consumption:

- **Respiratory harm**: Inhaling combusted fungal material exposes the lungs to particulate matter and potentially harmful combustion byproducts that are not present in oral consumption
- **Unreliable dosing**: The amount of active compound delivered through smoking is highly variable and essentially impossible to control, as it depends on smoking technique, temperature, and the physical properties of the dried material
- **Thermal destruction**: Heating may degrade active compounds while creating potentially toxic breakdown products that would not be produced through oral consumption
- **Contaminant exposure**: Mushroom material may harbor mold spores, bacteria, or pesticide residues that become aerosolized during smoking and are delivered directly to lung tissue

### Intravenous Injection Risks

Intravenous administration of crude mushroom extracts carries extreme and potentially life-threatening risks:

- **Infection**: Non-sterile extraction methods introduce extremely high risk of bacterial contamination, potentially leading to sepsis, endocarditis, or other serious infections
- **Embolism**: Particulate matter in crude extracts can cause pulmonary or systemic emboli, which can be fatal
- **Overdose potential**: The removal of natural dose-limiting mechanisms dramatically increases the risk of overwhelming psychoactive effects that may lead to panic, self-harm, or accidents
- **Unknown excipients**: Home-prepared extracts may contain solvents, contaminants, or chemical byproducts that are dangerous when introduced directly into the bloodstream
- **Psychological severity**: The more intense and rapid onset of IV-administered psychedelics may increase the probability and severity of panic reactions, psychotic episodes, and acute psychological crises
- **Cardiovascular risk**: The tendency for temporarily increased blood pressure associated with psilocybin, combined with the rapid onset of IV delivery, may pose additional risk for users with cardiovascular conditions

## Harm Reduction Considerations

The existence of these alternative administration routes underscores the importance of accurate harm reduction information. While oral consumption of hallucinogenic mushrooms carries its own well-documented risks (including nausea, panic attacks, and acute psychotic episodes), the alternative routes introduce additional and potentially more severe hazards. The EMCDDA report noted that over a quarter of those who had used hallucinogenic mushrooms in the last year had experienced nausea or vomiting, and nearly a quarter had experienced a panic attack through standard oral use.

Harm reduction messaging should specifically address the heightened risks associated with smoking and intravenous use, as users may perceive these methods as novel or more efficient without understanding the additional dangers involved. The Dutch smartshop industry's practice of providing warning labels (advising against use by persons under 18, pregnant women, those on medication, those with mental illness, and when driving or operating machines) provides a model for risk communication that could be extended to cover alternative administration methods. A snapshot of 21 online shops found that most sites provided information on sought-after effects, yet few provided information on how to enhance sought-after effects, and the vast majority excluded information on possible negative side effects.

## Short-Term Tolerance Considerations

Short-term tolerance to both physical and psychological effects of psilocybin mushrooms develops rapidly. This tolerance may lead users to increase their dosage over successive uses, a pattern that becomes particularly dangerous when combined with alternative administration routes that deliver higher effective doses. The combination of escalating tolerance and administration routes that bypass natural dose-limiting mechanisms (particularly intravenous injection) represents a significant risk factor for adverse outcomes.

There is no evidence of psychological or physical dependency associated with hallucinogenic mushrooms, and no withdrawal symptoms occur. However, the rapid development of short-term tolerance creates a dynamic where users may feel compelled to use larger doses or more efficient administration methods to achieve desired effects, potentially driving experimentation with smoking or injection routes.
