## Standard Oral Administration

The conventional method of consuming hallucinogenic mushrooms involves eating them raw or dried, or brewing them into a tea. Because mushrooms generally have an unpleasant taste, users typically chop them into smaller pieces before consumption to make the process more manageable. Eating them mixed or cooked into food is also practiced, though this may cause nausea and vomiting according to Lifeline Publications. Users report that the mushrooms can also be dried for later use, providing flexibility in timing of consumption.

When consumed orally, the onset of effects occurs within 10 to 60 minutes, depending on several factors including the modality of ingestion, the user"s physical condition, food intake, and prior experience with hallucinogenic substances. The duration of a mushroom "trip" following oral administration typically lasts between 2 and 6 hours, with aftereffects (such as difficulty sleeping) persisting for an additional 2 to 6 hours.

The standard recreational dose ranges from 1 to 3.5 grams of dried mushrooms or 10 to 50 grams of fresh mushrooms, though these ranges should be interpreted with caution given the variable potency of different species and growing conditions. Fresh mushrooms contain approximately 90% water by weight, which accounts for the approximately 10:1 ratio between fresh and dried dosage recommendations. The psychoactive dose of psilocybin is believed to be between 45 micrograms per kilogram of body weight and 315 micrograms per kilogram of body weight.

## Smoking Dried Mushrooms

User reports on internet platforms including Erowid and the Shroomery document the practice of smoking dried hallucinogenic mushrooms. Proponents of this method report that smoking induces faster but milder effects compared to oral ingestion. The faster onset is consistent with the pharmacokinetics of pulmonary drug absorption, which delivers substances to the bloodstream and brain more rapidly than the gastrointestinal route by bypassing first-pass hepatic metabolism.

However, the scientific basis for the psychoactive effects of smoked psilocybin mushrooms is questionable and remains unresolved. Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) and psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) are both tryptamine compounds. The thermal stability of these molecules at the temperatures generated by smoking is uncertain. Many tryptamine compounds, including the structurally related DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine), are known to be effectively vaporized and absorbed through smoking. However, psilocybin's phosphate ester group may make it less amenable to vaporization compared to free-base tryptamines.

The reported milder effects from smoking could be attributable to several possible factors:

- **Partial thermal degradation**: Some active compound may be destroyed by the temperatures of combustion or vaporization, reducing the effective dose delivered to the lungs
- **Incomplete absorption**: Pulmonary absorption efficiency may be lower for psilocybin than for other smoked tryptamines, particularly if the compound degrades before reaching absorptive surfaces
- **Placebo effect**: Expectations of psychoactive effects combined with the mild sensory changes from smoking plant material may produce subjective experiences attributed to psilocybin that do not reflect genuine pharmacological activity
- **Rapid metabolism**: Any psilocin reaching the bloodstream through pulmonary absorption may be metabolized more quickly than orally absorbed psilocin due to the different pharmacokinetic profile
- **Low bioavailability**: The proportion of active compound that survives combustion and is absorbed through pulmonary tissue may be substantially lower than the proportion absorbed through the gastrointestinal tract during oral consumption

## Intravenous Injection of Mushroom Extracts

A more concerning alternative administration route documented in internet reports (referenced on the Shroomery) involves the intravenous injection of mushroom extracts. This method reportedly produces more severe acute effects compared to oral administration. The increased severity is consistent with the pharmacokinetics of intravenous drug delivery, which produces the highest and most rapid peak plasma concentrations of any administration route, bypassing both first-pass metabolism and the rate-limiting absorption processes of the gastrointestinal tract.

The intravenous route eliminates the gradual onset and dose-limiting effects of oral absorption. With oral administration, the body's processing of the mushrooms (including first-pass metabolism in the liver and the time required for gastrointestinal absorption) provides a degree of natural dose limitation. Intravenous injection removes these safeguards, potentially delivering a much larger proportion of the active compounds to the brain in a dramatically shorter timeframe.

The Shroomery report referencing this practice noted that it was described in the literature rather than being a commonly documented user practice, suggesting that intravenous mushroom use is extremely rare. However, even rare practices warrant documentation and harm reduction attention due to the severity of associated risks.
