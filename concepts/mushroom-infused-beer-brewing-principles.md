# Mushroom-Infused Beer Brewing Principles

Mushroom-infused beer represents a convergence of homebrewing and functional
mycology, creating a beverage that delivers medicinal compounds in an
enjoyable format. The concept debuted at the 2012 Telluride Mushroom
Festival, where 240 gallons of medicinal mushroom beer sold out in under
48 hours. By 2013, production quadrupled to meet demand. Tradd Cotter
documented the principles and methods for combining mushrooms with the
brewing process, emphasizing that mushrooms cannot replace malted grains
as a fermentable sugar source but can add medicinal compounds, flavor,
aroma, and bitterness.

## Prerequisites for Mushroom Brewing

Before adding mushrooms to beer, the brewer should have several successful
batches of standard beer completed. Mushroom-infused brewing introduces
additional variables that can complicate troubleshooting. Familiarity with
basic brewing chemistry, sanitation, and fermentation management is
essential. Joining local brewing clubs can accelerate the learning curve
significantly.

## Yeast Culture Management

## Stages of Addition

- **Grain and mash:** Adding mushrooms during the mash extracts flavors,
  bitterness, and water-soluble medicinal compounds during the cook.
- **Dry shrooming:** Analogous to dry hopping, mushroom powder or dried
  fruitbodies are added to the fermentation vessel. This imparts unique
  flavors, complex aromas, and varying bitterness depending on the species.
  A hops bag should be used to contain the material for easy removal.
- **Kegging or bottling:** Adding extract at this final stage preserves the
  maximum medicinal potency of the mushroom compounds.
