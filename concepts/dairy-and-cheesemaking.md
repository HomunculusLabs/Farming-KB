## Dairy and Cheese Making

Processing goat milk into cheese, butter, yogurt, and other dairy products on the homestead. Goat milk from breeds like Nigerian Dwarf (6-10% butterfat) and Nubian (4-5% butterfat) is ideal — see [[goat-keeping]] for breed selection and milking hygiene.

## Milk Handling and Safety

### Milking Hygiene
- Wash udder with warm water + teat dip before milking; strip first few squirts (highest bacterial load)
- Filter milk immediately through a stainless steel or disposable milk filter into a sanitized container
- Cool milk to 40°F (4°C) within 1 hour — rapid cooling prevents off-flavors and bacterial growth
- Store at 34-38°F (1-3°C); fresh raw goat milk keeps 7-10 days properly handled

### Pasteurization
| Method | Temp | Hold Time | Use Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vat (batch) | 145°F (63°C) | 30 min | Home kitchen, small batches |
| HTST | 161°F (72°C) | 15 sec | Larger operations |
| Ultra-pasteurized | 280°F (138°C) | 2 sec | Store-bought only — **won't work for cheese** |

- **Raw milk cheese** is legal for personal use in most US states; selling requires licensing (aged 60+ days)
- Pasteurize if herd isn't tested CAE/CL/johne's-negative (see [[goat-keeping]])
- Ultra-pasteurized milk **cannot form a proper curd** — proteins are denatured

### Cream Separation
- Goat milk is naturally homogenized — cream doesn't separate easily like cow milk
- Let milk sit 24-48 hours at 35-38°F in a wide container; skim cream from top
- Mechanical cream separator yields ~10-15% cream from Nubian milk
- Nigerian Dwarf milk separates more readily due to higher butterfat

## Cultures and Rennet

### Starter Cultures
| Culture | Type | Temp Range | Used For |
|---|---|---|---|
| MA11 / Meso | Mesophilic | 68-77°F (20-25°C) | Chevre, feta, fromage blanc, cheddar |
| TA61 / Thermo | Thermophilic | 86-106°F (30-41°C) | Yogurt, gouda, parmesan, mozzarella |
| Flora Danica | Mesophilic aromatic | 68-77°F | Brie, camembert, havarti |

- **DVI (Direct Vat Inoculation):** Freeze-dried powder, add directly — easiest for beginners
- **Mother cultures:** Propagate from DVI in sterilized milk; more economical at scale
- Source: New England Cheesemaking Supply, Cultures for Health

### Rennet
| Type | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Animal (calf) | Calf stomach | Traditional, most reliable set |
| Microbial | Fermentation-produced | Vegetarian, slightly softer curd |
| Vegetable | Fig latex, thistle, nettles | Less predictable strength |

- Dosage: ~¼-½ tsp liquid rennet per gallon (follow package); dilute in cool chlorine-free water
- Store liquid rennet in fridge; freeze for long-term (1+ year)
- **Plant rennet:** [[stinging-nettle]], cardoon thistle, fig sap (see [[herbal-medicine]])

## Fresh Dairy Products

### Kefir
- True kefir grains are a SCOBY (bacteria + yeast colony); powder is simplified starter
- Ratio: 1 tbsp grains per 1 cup milk; culture at room temp (68-75°F) for 12-24 hours
- Strain with plastic sieve; grains multiply — share extras or compost
- **Secondary fermentation:** Sealed jar 6-12 hours for carbonation

### Yogurt
**Thermophilic (firm set):** Heat milk to 180°F (82°C) 30 min → cool to 110°F → add 2 tbsp starter per quart → incubate at 105-110°F for 4-12 hours → refrigerate 4+ hours. Incubation: yogurt maker, oven with light on, cooler with hot water.

**Mesophilic (no heat):** Viili, filmjölk cultures at room temp (70-78°F) for 12-24 hours. Thinner but easier.

### Fromage Blanc
- Add mesophilic culture to milk at 72°F; set 12-18 hours; drain in cheesecloth 4-6 hours
- Season with herbs ([[herbal-medicine]]: thyme, chives, chervil), salt, pepper. Yield: ~1 lb/gallon

## Soft Cheeses

### Chevre (Fresh Goat Cheese)
1. Pasteurize milk (optional); cool to 72-75°F
2. Add 1 packet MA11 mesophilic culture + ¼ tsp diluted rennet per gallon
3. Set 12-18 hours until clean break (curd pulls from pot edge, whey is clear-yellow)
4. Ladle into cheesecloth; drain 6-12 hours at room temp
5. Salt (1 tsp/lb); add herbs, garlic, honey, or pepper as desired
- **Yield:** ~1 lb per gallon. **Shelf life:** 1-2 weeks refrigerated; freezes 3 months

### Ricotta (from whey)
- Heat saved whey to 195-200°F; add ¼ cup white vinegar per gallon; drain in cheesecloth 15-30 min
- Add 1-2 cups fresh milk to whey before heating for higher yield ("true ricotta")

### Paneer
- Heat milk to 185°F; add 3-4 tbsp lemon juice or vinegar slowly while stirring
- Drain in cheesecloth 30 min; press under weight 2-4 hours. Does not melt — ideal for frying

## Hard Cheeses

### By Difficulty
**Beginner:** Chevre, ricotta, paneer, feta, mozzarella
**Intermediate:** Gouda, colby, havarti, monterey jack
**Advanced:** Cheddar, manchego, brie, parmesan, [[blue-cheese]]

### General Process
1. **Acidify:** Add starter culture at proper temperature
2. **Ripen:** Hold 30-60 min; monitor pH if available
3. **Coagulate:** Add diluted rennet; clean break in 30-60 min
4. **Cut:** Smaller cuts = drier cheese; rest 5 min
5. **Cook:** Heat slowly to target temp while stirring (controls moisture)
6. **Drain:** Save whey for [[animal-byproducts]] or ricotta
7. **Salt:** Dry-salt curds OR brine finished cheese
8. **Press:** Gradual pressure (10 min light → 30 min medium → 4-12 hrs heavy)
9. **Air dry:** 2-5 days at 50-55°F, flipping daily, until rind is dry
10. **Age:** See conditions below

### Cheddar (Goat Milk)
- Culture: MA11 at 90°F; coagulate, cut to ¼", cook to 100°F
- **Cheddaring:** Stack and flip curd slabs at 100°F for 2 hours — develops texture
- Mill, salt (2% by weight), press at 50 lbs overnight. Age 60+ days; 6-12 months for sharp
- Goat milk cheddar is naturally white

### Gouda (Washed Curd)
- Cut curds, cook to 100°F, wash with 175°F water to reach 96°F (removes lactose → sweeter)
- Press in gouda mold; age 2-6 months. Wax or natural rind.
