# Salt Buildup and Leaching Management

Salt buildup from fertilizer residues is one of the most common and
insidious problems in container-grown cannabis. Jorge Cervantes
explains how excess salts accumulate, how to detect them, and how
leaching restores the growing medium to a healthy state.

## What is Salt Buildup?

Fertilizer salts accumulate in the growing medium over time as plants
absorb water faster than nutrients. The remaining salts concentrate in
the soil, gradually increasing electrical conductivity (EC) to toxic
levels. This toxic salt buildup limits water intake by roots, creates
nutrient lockout conditions, and can chemically bind essential
nutrients so roots cannot absorb them.

Salt buildup is accelerated by:
- Frequent fertilization without adequate runoff
- Watering with mineral-heavy tap water
- High evaporation rates in warm grow rooms
- Using [[cervantes-growing-medium]] with poor drainage
- Over-fertilization (see [[cervantes-nutrient-burn-over-fertilization]])

## Detecting Salt Buildup

### EC Comparison Test

Collect EC samples from both the reservoir (or water source) and the
growing medium. Under normal conditions, the EC in the growing medium
should be slightly higher than the nutrient solution in the reservoir.

If the EC of the solution drawn from the growing medium is
substantially higher than the one from the reservoir, there is a salt
buildup in the substrate. This simple test using an EC meter (see
[[cervantes-ec-tds-nutrient-solution-strength]]) provides definitive
diagnosis.

### Visual Symptoms

- White crusty deposits on the soil surface
- Leaf tip burn that does not respond to reduced feeding
- Nutrient deficiency symptoms despite adequate fertilization
- Stunted growth with dark, overly green foliage
- Soil that repels water rather than absorbing it evenly

## Leaching (Flushing)

### The Leaching Process

To correct salt buildup, flush the substrate thoroughly with diluted
nutrient solution and replace with fresh solution. The process involves:

1. Applying large volumes of plain or very dilute water to the
   growing medium
2. Allowing substantial runoff (at least equal to the container
   volume) to carry dissolved salts away
3. Replacing with fresh, properly balanced nutrient solution

Leaching washes away built-up salts and dead root material. Regular
monthly flushing keeps EC levels in check even before visible symptoms
appear. Cervantes recommends flushing container gardens with three
quarts of water per gallon of container volume when salt buildup is
detected.

### Pre-Harvest Flush

A special form of leaching is the pre-harvest flush, where plants are
given plain water for the final one to two weeks before harvest. This
removes residual fertilizer salts that impart a harsh, chemical taste
to the smoke. See [[cervantes-flushing-pre-harvest]] for detailed
pre-harvest flushing protocols.

## Osmotic Pressure and Salt Stress

When salt concentration becomes too high, it increases cellular
osmotic pressure to the point where roots cannot absorb water even
when the growing medium appears adequately moist. The plant
effectively dehydrates despite being surrounded by water. This
condition can be confused with [[cervantes-overwatering-underwatering]]
because the visual symptoms of wilting are similar.

If the internal osmotic systems reverse due to extreme salt
concentration, water is actually pulled out of the roots into the
surrounding medium, causing rapid wilting and potentially permanent
root damage.

## Prevention Strategies

### Runoff Management

Allow up to 10 percent runoff during each watering. The runoff
carries excess salts away before they accumulate. This single practice
prevents most salt buildup problems. See
[[cervantes-moisture-meters-watering-technique]] for proper watering
methods that promote adequate runoff.

### Regular EC Monitoring

Regularly check the EC of your water source, growing medium, and
runoff. Tracking these three values over time reveals trends before
they become problems. Calibrate EC meters regularly; corroded probes
produce inaccurate readings.

### Proper Fertilization

Avoid over-fertilizing. More fertilizer does not mean faster growth.
Excess fertilizer that plants cannot absorb becomes the salt buildup
problem of tomorrow. Follow manufacturer recommendations and adjust
based on plant response and EC measurements.

## Salt Buildup in Hydroponics

In recirculating hydroponic systems (see
[[cervantes-drain-to-waste-vs-recirculating]]), salt buildup in the
reservoir is inevitable. The solution must be periodically drained
and replaced with fresh nutrient solution. Stagnant nutrient solution
and salt buildup stifle root growth and create conditions favorable
to [[cervantes-root-rot-pythium-damping-off]].

Sediment and salt accumulate at the bottom of reservoirs over time.
Regular reservoir cleaning prevents this buildup from affecting the
nutrient solution quality.
