# Salt Buildup and Leaching Management

Salt buildup from fertilizer residues is one of the most common and
insidious problems in container-grown cannabis. Jorge Cervantes
explains how excess salts accumulate, how to detect them, and how
leaching restores the growing medium to a healthy state.

## What is Salt Buildup?

Fertilizer salts accumulate in the growing medium over time as plants
absorb water faster than nutrients. The remaining salts concentrate in
the soil, gradually increasing electrical conductivity (EC) to toxic
levels. This toxic salt buildup limits water intake by roots, creates
[[cannabis-nutrient-lockout-and-ph]] conditions, and can chemically bind essential
nutrients so roots cannot absorb them.

Salt buildup is accelerated by:
- Frequent fertilization without adequate runoff
- Watering with mineral-heavy tap water
- High evaporation rates in warm grow rooms
- Using [[cervantes-nutrient-burn-over-fertilization]])

## Detecting Salt Buildup

### EC Comparison Test

Collect EC samples from both the reservoir (or water source) and the
growing medium. Under normal conditions, the EC in the growing medium
should be slightly higher than the [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]] in the reservoir.

If the EC of the solution drawn from the growing medium is
substantially higher than the one from the reservoir, there is a salt
buildup in the substrate. This simple test using an EC meter (see
[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-sog-scrog-advanced-methods]] for detailed
pre-harvest flushing protocols.

## Osmotic Pressure and Salt Stress

When salt concentration becomes too high, it increases cellular
osmotic pressure to the point where roots cannot absorb water even
when the growing medium appears adequately moist. The plant
effectively dehydrates despite being surrounded by water. This
condition can be confused with [[cervantes-moisture-meters-watering-technique]] for proper watering
methods that promote adequate runoff.

### Regular EC Monitoring

Regularly check the EC of your water source, growing medium, and
runoff. Tracking these three values over time reveals trends before
they become problems. Calibrate EC meters regularly; corroded probes
produce inaccurate readings.

### Proper Fertilization

Avoid over-fertilizing. More fertilizer does not mean faster growth.
Excess fertilizer that plants cannot absorb becomes the salt buildup
problem of tomorrow. Follow manufacturer recommendations and adjust
based on plant response and EC measurements.

## Salt Buildup in Hydroponics

In recirculating hydroponic systems (see

Sediment and salt accumulate at the bottom of reservoirs over time.
Regular reservoir cleaning prevents this buildup from affecting the
nutrient solution quality.
