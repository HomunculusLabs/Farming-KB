# Tessellation and Annidation Patterns

## Overview

Tessellation and [[permaculture-design]], providing frameworks for understanding how complex landscapes develop from simple geometric principles and how compact, efficient spatial arrangements arise in biological and physical systems.

## Tessellation

### Definition

Tessellation refers to the tiling of a plane or surface using one or more geometric shapes without gaps or overlaps. In nature, tessellated patterns appear as matrices or grids based on approximate squares, hexagons, or intersecting sine waves. These underlying patterns are found in many natural distributions including trees in deserts, heat or convection cells, honeycomb structures, and cracks in mud.

### The Pattern Matrix

The core model can tessellate or link together to form closed surfaces, spheres, or chains. Both regular and irregular forms of the general model are capable of tessellation. The "growth lines" (T-series) of these models are essentially a series of smaller and smaller forms nested within larger boundaries, as seen in target patterns or tree cross-sections.

### Co-definitions in Models

The study of matrices reveals that T (time) lines are ogives of a tessellated model and develop from the "S" (stream) lines of the next adjoining model. This reveals the inter-dependent properties of the core model. Sets of such models and their marginal crenellations provide a complex interface in natural systems, often rich in production potential.

## Annidation

### Definition

Annidation (from the Latin *nidus*, meaning "a nest") is the process of nesting smaller forms within larger ones in a compact way. The term describes the strategy of fitting like components of the same or different size compactly together, analogous to how bowls or glasses are stored one within the other.

### Application in Nature

Annidation is observed in:
- **Target patterns**: Concentric rings of decreasing size
- **Tree cross-sections**: Growth rings nested within each other
- **Mollusc shells**: Compact spiral forms that increase absorption surface area
- **Spiral leaf arrangements**: Whorls of leaves that compactly fill space along a stem

### Design Implications

Annidation provides a strategy for compactly storing or arranging components. In [[edge-effect]] that is so productive in natural and designed ecosystems.

## Design Applications

In permaculture design, tessellation and annidation principles suggest:

1. **Compact arrangements**: Nest smaller garden plots or functional zones within larger organizing patterns
2. **Edge maximization**: Use crenellated boundaries to increase productive interface between different zones
3. **Matrix design**: Arrange multiple small areas of differing conditions rather than uniform large areas
4. **Pattern recognition**: Identify the tessellated and annidated patterns already present in a landscape and work with them rather than against them

The strategy of creating complex boundary conditions through tessellation and annidation is another primary approach for generating complex life assemblies and energy translators in designed systems.
