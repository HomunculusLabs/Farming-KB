## Flat Land Permaculture Design

## Overview

Flat land [[permaculture]] presents unique challenges compared to sloped terrain. Without the natural gravity flow of water and the thermal advantages of elevation differences, flatland designers must create their own slopes, raise their tanks, and strategically use earth banks to manage water, wind, noise, and privacy. [[bill-mollison]] emphasizes that the solutions on flat land are remarkably varied and creative, often involving excavation and earth-moving to establish the conditions that slopes provide naturally.

## Net and Pan Systems on Slopes

Before transitioning to flat lands, Mollison describes the **net and pan** technique as a bridge between steep and moderate terrain. This system involves:

- Setting up shallow guide lines for drainage across a slope
- Hand-cutting small drains at junction points
- Creating flat planting platforms at regular intervals
- Enabling nutrient stripping as water moves downslope

The key principle is using the slope to your advantage — nutrients fall downhill, mulch can be easily moved down, and water naturally flows through the system. When systems are arranged in reverse (people pushing wheelbarrows uphill), enormous unnecessary labor is created.

### Chicken Integration Above Gardens

Placing [[faires-chicken-coops-tractors-and-breeding-guide]] above garden sites provides multiple benefits:

- Chickens shred mulch as it passes through their system
- Weed seeds are removed from mulch material
- Added nutrient is received before mulch reaches the garden
- Mulch arrives pre-shredded and enriched

## Fire Control Through Placement

On slopes, fire travels uphill rapidly. Effective fire defenses include:

- **Water features**: Dams with earth banks serve dual purposes
- **High access roads**: Act as firebreaks
- **Earth walls**: Provide radiation defense
- **Stone walls**: Slow fire spread
- **Short grazing systems**: Reduce fuel loads on downhill sectors

Mollison notes that once people and elements are correctly placed on a slope, fire safety is often achieved as a byproduct of good design — more than originally planned.

## Earth Banks: The Flat Land Solution

Earth banks are the primary tool for creating conditions on flat land that would otherwise require natural topography. A 4-6 foot earth bank provides multiple functions simultaneously:

### Windbreak Function
The fastest way to establish a windbreak is to grade up earth bank and [[swale]]. Fast-growing plants like pampas grass and bamboo can be established on top, giving an immediate racing start compared to planting alone.

### Noise Reduction
Earth banks are excellent at reducing traffic and environmental noise. The rule is: from the crown of the road to the top of the earth bank, deflect in a straight line so that the roof of the house is cleared. Vegetation alone requires approximately 100 meters to significantly reduce noise, while a well-insulated house with earth bank protection can be near a noisy system and remain quiet.

### Privacy and Insulation
Achieving privacy on flat land can be difficult. Earth banks surrounding a dwelling create visual screening and a sense of enclosure without the need for dense vegetation.

### Radiation Shielding
Earth banks provide excellent radiation shielding from fire, protecting structures through their [[comparison-windbreak-vs-thermal-mass]].

### Mulch and Silt Collection
Earth banks accumulate leaf litter and silt from flowing water. This material can be transferred to gardens as high-quality mulch at very low cost.

## Case Studies in Flat Land Design

### The Underground Cave House
On an alluvial plain with a noisy roadside and eroded gully, Mollison's team designed a house built over a backhoe-excavated cave:

- The cave extended beyond the house at both ends
- One end opened to a pond at water level, glassed and trellised on top
- The cool end served as [[root-cellars-and-cold-storage]] for [[growing-root-vegetables-guide]]
- Heat could be drawn up through ducts anywhere in the structure
- The client gained an underground glass house, shower, and deck overlooking water
- The cave remained dry, about a foot above dam level

### The Peninsula House
On a flat site with poor water retention but excellent existing trees:

- A below-grade earth tank (lake) was created
- Excavated dirt formed a peninsula rising ~9 feet above grade
- The house was placed on the peninsula for mountain views
- Surrounded on three sides by water for light reflection
- Low earth banks diverted flooding rains away from the garden
- Silt and mulch deposited at the outside of the garden wall defended the dam from silting

### The Windmill Client
A park director's property with excessive water flow across the site:

- Two-story house on a high peninsula for views above the tree line
- Fire frequency site (every 5th-8th year) — client placed on peninsula for safety
- Shaded veranda on second story for mountain viewing in summer
- Low earth banks to deflect flood rains and collect silt/mulch
- Fish stocked in the pond for the fishing enthusiast client

## Key Principles for Flat Land Design

1. **Create your own slopes** — throw up roofs, tanks, and earth banks while keeping the house low
2. **Study local [[wind-patterns-and-beaufort-scale]]** — determine summer cooling winds, winter cold winds, and hot winds; any local longtime resident can tell you
3. **Use earth as your primary material** — it's already on site, free, and multi-functional
4. **Design for multiple functions** — every earth bank should serve windbreak, privacy, [[water-management]], and fire defense simultaneously
5. **Keep houses low and tanks high** — or use upper rooms only as bedrooms with service rooms below
6. **Don't be frightened of excavation** — flat lands offer enormous opportunities for creative below-grade solutions
7. **Study the flora and the client's needs** — there is no general formula for flatlands; solutions emerge from understanding both

## Water Management on Flat Lands

On a 300-acre property with only a two-foot drop:

- Diversions off roadways direct water around ponds
- Annual gardens incorporate water gardens
- Access routes come through earth banks
- Single-story houses surrounded by earth bank with tall barns and water tanks

The elegant solution to flat land water problems is making slopes, raising tanks, and keeping dwellings low — or alternatively building high with bedrooms above and service rooms below, using the roof itself as a water collector.

## Design Philosophy

Mollison emphasizes that you cannot give a general approach to flatlands. Instead, the designer must study the specific flora, understand the client's priorities (privacy, noise, water, views), and build solutions from the available earth, water, and vegetation. The three case studies presented demonstrate entirely different approaches to the same basic condition of flat topography, each tailored to the unique problems and desires of the client.
