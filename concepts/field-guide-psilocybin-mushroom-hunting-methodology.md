## Field Guide to Psilocybin Mushroom Hunting Methodology

This page covers the practical methodology for locating and harvesting
psilocybin-containing mushrooms in the wild, as described in F.C. Ghouled"s 1972
field guide. The three target species — *[[psilocybe-caerulescens]]* — are common throughout the southern
United States, from California to Louisiana and South Carolina to Florida.

## When to Hunt: Temperature and Season

The fundamental temperature window for psilocybin mushroom growth is 45 to 85
degrees Fahrenheit. However, the optimal productive range is narrower at 65 to
85 degrees Fahrenheit. The guide stresses that timing is critical:

**Late spring and early fall** are the best seasons, combining moderate
temperatures with regular rainfall. **Summer months** should generally be
avoided — even when rain occurs during a hot spell, specimens are of poor
quality with significantly reduced psilocybin content, making them "hardly worth
taking." The warm dry summer promotes mycelium growth underground, while a cool
moist fall triggers fruiting bodies to appear in plentiful numbers.

## Weather Conditions and Rainfall

Weather is the single most important factor in successful mushroom hunting.
**Monitor weather reports** for frontal systems that bring cool air and rain
simultaneously — these conditions are excellent for triggering fruiting.
**Recent rainfall is essential:** mushrooms will not fruit without adequate
moisture, and a single rainy day followed by cool temperatures can produce
abundant flushes.

**Excess water is dangerous** to the mycelium: while mature mycelium demands
large quantities of water for maximum production, prolonged saturation will
cause the mycelium to rot and die back. The mycelium can tolerate a few inches
of rain in one day and even several inches daily for a few days, but extended
wet periods are harmful. Mushrooms left growing in the field age and rot quickly
because they are over 90% water, and direct sun accelerates this deterioration.

is cool air temperatures following rain. Potency is retained indefinitely if
specimens are properly dried and stored, but fresh specimens must be picked
promptly before sun exposure degrades them.

## Field Techniques and Best Practices

Successful foraging requires attention to practical considerations beyond timing
and location. **Timing your visit:** plan to hunt 24-48 hours after significant
rainfall when temperatures are in the optimal 65-85°F range.
