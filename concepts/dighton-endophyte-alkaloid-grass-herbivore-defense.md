## Alkaloid Production and Evolution

Wilson (1993) suggests that the improved fitness conferred by alkaloid
production has led to vertical transmission of the endophyte from plant to
plant via seed. All new plants thus begin life with the endophyte and its
protective advantages. This contrasts with horizontally transmitted endophytes
(such as Discula quercina of oak, transmitted via rain splash), where a
mosaic of colonized and uncolonized plants develops depending on
environmental conditions.

Richardson (2000) postulates that alkaloid production may have additional
properties beyond herbivore defense. In other fungal-plant interactions,
secondary metabolites influence host plant physiology. The pathogenic fungus
Cercospora produces cercosporin, which kills host cells to make nutrients
available to the fungus. [[nitrogen-availability-in-legumes]] to the fungus. This nitrogen manipulation
supports the idea that mutualistic associations (mycorrhizae and endophytes)
exist along a continuum of fungal physiologies ranging from saprotrophy to
pathogenicity.
