# Button Mushroom Cultivation on Compost

Button mushrooms (Agaricus spp.) as covered in the FAO training manual. Button mushrooms are among the more technically demanding cultivated species, requiring prepared compost beds rather than substrate bags, and cooler growing conditions.

See also: [[mushroom-training-adapted-cultivation]], [[mushroom-training-oyster-straw-cultivation]], [[mushroom-training-spawn-production]]

## Cultivation Method: Plot/Bed System

Button mushrooms use a fundamentally different cultivation system from oyster mushrooms:

- **Substrate bag method**: Used for oyster, shiitake, ear mushrooms, and most other tropical species
- **Plot/bed method**: Used for button mushrooms (Agaricus), straw mushrooms (Volvariella), and some others

### Compost Preparation

Button mushrooms require composted substrate rather than raw substrate bags. The composting process:

1. Prepare base materials: straw/hay, horse or chicken manure, gypsum
2. Phase I composting (outdoor): Mix and turn compost pile over 7-14 days; aerobic decomposition generates heat (60-80C), killing pathogens and weed seeds
3. Phase II pasteurization (indoor): Compost brought into mushroom house, pasteurized at 60C to kill remaining pathogens and ammonia
4. Spawning: Compost cooled to 25C, spawn mixed in at ~1% by weight
5. Casing: Layer of pasteurized soil/peat mixture (casing layer) applied on top of spawned compost — this triggers fruiting
6. Fruiting: Maintain temperature 16-18C, humidity 85-90%, fresh air exchange

### Key Differences from Substrate Bag Cultivation

| Feature | Substrate Bags | Compost Beds |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| Substrate | Sawdust/straw + supplements | Composted manure/straw |
| Sterilization | Pasteurization at 90-100C | Two-phase composting |
| Fruiting trigger | Open bag | Casing layer application |
| Temperature | 20-37C depending on species | 16-18C optimal |
| Infrastructure | Bags on shelves | Raised beds |
| Complexity | Moderate | High |

### Plot Method in the Manual

The manual provides details on straw mushroom (Volvariella) cultivation using the plot method, which shares principles with button mushroom bed cultivation:

1. Plow and aerate soil
2. Spread fertilizer and saturate with water
3. Prepare reusable wooden frames on the ground
4. Layer substrate and spawn alternately inside frames
5. Pack firmly, remove frames to form "cakes"
6. Cover with plastic sheeting and hay for humidity/temperature control

This layered bed approach is the foundation for compost-based button mushroom cultivation as well.

## Fruiting Body Formation

Mushrooms develop from mycelium when conditions trigger the change from vegetative to reproductive growth. The "button" stage refers to the primordial mushroom before the cap expands. For button mushrooms:

- Mycelium must fully colonize the compost (2-3 weeks)
- Casing layer applied (triggers fruiting hormones)
- Temperature drop signals fruiting
- Humidity maintained at 85-90%
- Light: low levels sufficient
- CO2 levels reduced through ventilation

## Health Properties

Per the manual's traditional Thai medicine references:
- **Hed Kradum** (Agaricus sp.): increases mother's milk production
- Button mushrooms are widely consumed globally and valued for umami flavor

## Challenges for Small-Scale Farmers

Button mushroom cultivation presents several difficulties in tropical regions:

- **Temperature control**: Requires 16-18C, difficult without cooling in tropical climates (Thailand winter only: Oct-Jan)
- **Compost complexity**: Two-phase composting requires knowledge and monitoring
- **Casing layer**: Requires specific materials (peat moss, soil mix) and pasteurization
- **Infrastructure**: Needs enclosed, climate-controlled growing rooms
- **Market**: Must compete with commercially produced imported button mushrooms

## Suitability for Trainees with Disabilities

The manual recommends beginners start with oyster mushrooms due to their easier cultivation. Button mushrooms can be a later expansion once skills and infrastructure are established. The plot/bed method's physical requirements (compost turning, bed preparation) may present additional challenges that need adaptation.
