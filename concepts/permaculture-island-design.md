# Permaculture Island Design

updated: 2026-04-18

Islands present unique design challenges and opportunities for permaculture. Bill Mollison's [[permaculture-design]] course (Pamphlet IV) classifies islands into two main types, each requiring distinct strategies for food production, [[water-management]], and settlement design.

## Types of Islands

### Low Islands (Residual)
Low islands are formed from coral atolls and sand cays. Characteristics:
- Low elevation (rarely more than a few meters above sea level)
- Coral-based soils (alkaline, high in calcium, often poor in other nutrients)
- Limited freshwater (rainwater catchment and shallow freshwater lens)
- Vulnerable to sea level rise, storm surge, and saltwater intrusion
- Small land area limiting zone and sector planning

### High Islands (Volcanic/Granitic)
High islands are volcanic or tectonic in origin. Characteristics:
- Significant elevation creating multiple climate zones
- Rich volcanic soils (fertile but can be acidic)
- Watersheds generating streams and rivers
- More diverse microclimates (windward vs. leeward slopes)
- Greater land area allowing full [[permaculture-zone-planning]]

## Low Island Strategies

### Water
- Rainwater catchment is the primary freshwater source
- Desalination as backup (solar-powered where possible)
- Protect the freshwater lens from contamination (separate sewage and water)
- Use coconut water as a supplementary water source
- Minimize evaporation with mulch and shade

### Soil
- Coral soils are alkaline and poor in many nutrients
- Import organic matter (compost, seaweed, green manures)
- Use nitrogen-fixing plants extensively
- Raised beds with imported soil/compost for intensive growing
- [[green-manure-crops]] to build organic matter

### Crops
- Coconuts as the staple (food, water, oil, building material)
- Breadfruit, pandanus, taro as traditional staples
- Citrus, papaya, banana for fruit
- Sweet potato, cassava for carbohydrate crops
- Sea vegetables (seaweed) as nutrient-dense supplement
- Chickens and ducks for eggs and pest control

### Settlement
- Concentrate structures on the highest ground
- Design for storm resistance (cyclones, typhoons)
- Use prevailing winds for cooling
- Cluster buildings for mutual protection

## High Island Strategies

### Climate Zones by Elevation
High islands create their own climate zones:
- **Coastal/lowland**: Tropical, humid, sea breezes
- **Mid-slope**: Subtropical, cooler, reliable rainfall
- **High elevation**: Temperate, possible frost, cloud forest
- **Ridge/summit**: Alpine or montane

Each zone supports different crops and systems, increasing overall diversity.

### Water Management
- Watershed protection is critical (forests on upper slopes)
- Multiple water sources (streams, springs, rainfall)
- [[irrigation-systems]] from high-elevation sources
- Terracing for water retention and erosion control on slopes

### Forest Systems
- Cloud forests at high elevation capture moisture from fog
- Multi-strata food forests on slopes
- Coastal mangroves for fish breeding and storm protection
- Riverine forests along watercourses

### Agriculture
- Terraced agriculture on slopes (rice, vegetables, tree crops)
- Wetland taro and rice in valleys
- Tree crops on mid-slopes (breadfruit, citrus, avocado, coffee)
- Livestock integration (goats on steep slopes, pigs in forest, poultry everywhere)

## Coastal Systems

### Mangrove Conservation
Mangroves are critical island ecosystems:
- Fish and crustacean nursery habitat
- Coastal protection from storms and erosion
- Wood and thatch material
- Carbon sequestration

### Reef and Lagoon Management
- Traditional tabu/ra'ui systems (temporary fishing closures for recovery)
- Sustainable harvesting practices
- Coral reef health directly affects fish populations
- Sea grass beds as additional food source

### Intertidal Resources
- Shellfish collection
- Seaweed harvesting
- Crab and lobster trapping
- Fish trapping in shallow waters

## Challenges

### Sea Level Rise
Low islands face existential threats from climate change:
- Saltwater intrusion into freshwater lens
- Increased storm damage
- Loss of arable land
- Potential displacement of populations

### Isolation
- Limited import capacity
- Need for self-reliance in food, water, energy
- Limited medical and educational resources
- Waste management challenges

### Tropical Cyclones
Design must account for:
- Wind-resistant structures
- Cyclone shelters
- Emergency food and water storage
- Crop diversity to ensure some survive any given storm
- Quick-recovering species (bananas, coconuts) vs. long-term trees
