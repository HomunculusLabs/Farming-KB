# Mushroom Cultivation Requirements: Food, Water, Gas Exchange, and Light

From [[tradd-cotter]]'s *Organic Mushroom Farming and [[mycoremediation]]*, the four essential requirements for prolific mushroom production are food, water, gas exchange, and light. "If any one of these variables is missing or neglected, the mycelium and mushroom biomass suffers greatly."

## Food

### Heterotrophic Nutrition
"Fungi are heterotrophs, or consumers of food just like animals." They have adapted enzymes to break down complex organic compounds: logs, leaves, straw, manure.

### Cultivation Categories
| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Primary decomposers** | Break down fresh, undecomposed materials | [[oyster-mushrooms]] on straw |
| **Secondary decomposers** | Require pre-decomposed substrates | Button mushrooms on compost |

Sequencing strategy: Grow primary decomposers first, then add secondary species when the food source has been altered.

### Supplementation Warning
"The more you use [supplements], the higher the risk of contamination competing with the mycelium for the prime real estate, so use supplements sparingly or not at all."

## Water

### Moisture Requirements
- Mushrooms need **90-95% humidity** to sustain growth
- The "sweating" of enzymes lubricates mycelium paths, allowing it to "swim" through growing medium

### Water Distribution
"Fungi can breathe in humid air, condensing it into water internally in their cells, and redistributing the water wherever the body of mycelium needs it most."

### Hydraulic Pressure
"Once the colonized growing medium has charged its mycelium full of nutrients, it is ready and waiting for a good soaking, where the water pressure generated by some fungi can actually break through concrete and asphalt."

### Heavy Metal Warning
"Mushrooms can hyperaccumulate heavy metals in tissue" — test water for lead, mercury, and arsenic at minimum. This includes pond, well, and roof-reclaimed water sources.

## Gas Exchange

### Colonization Phase
"During colonization, they are embedded in wood, under mulch, or streaming through compost piles, with very little oxygen needed until they are ready to fruit."

The entire biomass uses the outer edges of the mycelium for oxygen transport, channeling CO₂ out. If too wet or deep, "dead zones" form.

### Fruiting Phase
"Mushrooms form where oxygen is plentiful, and will emerge from the fruiting medium wherever there is access to air." Keeping mushrooms too wet during fruiting "can drown them by coating them with a continuous film of water."

### Colonization Room CO₂
"Most commercial farms keep colonization rooms at around [elevated CO₂ levels]" to trigger natural stimuli for prolific fruiting.

## Light

### General Requirements
"Most cultivated mushrooms require light to initiate and fruit, but it does not have to be much at all."

- **Outdoor**: Shaded, indirect light ideal
- **Indoor**: Fluorescent or LED lights — supply needed color spectrums with energy savings
- **Not required** during spawn run stages
- **Essential** after pins emerge for healthy mushroom development

"You cannot overexpose mushrooms to artificial light if the temperature is cool and there is ample humidity in the fruiting room."

## Initiation and Pinning

### Temperature Triggers
- Lower temperature a few degrees after colonization simulates seasonal fluctuation
- **Cool weather species** (lion's mane, maitake, enoki): Cold shock at 40-50°F (4.5-10°C) for 1-several days
- **Blewits and morels**: Require near-freezing to freezing periods

### Humidity for Pinning
"High humidity levels (90 percent or more) are essential to initiate and sustain primordia formation." Micronized droplets (fog, dew) signal sufficient moisture for full development.

### Evaporation Effect
"Evaporation at the exposed surface of the substrate has a cooling effect, further stimulating initiation."

## Inoculation Practices

### Clean Technique
- Hands sprayed with 70% [[isopropyl-alcohol]] or half-strength bleach
- Clean rake (designated solely for [[mushroom-cultivation]]) dunked in diluted bleach
- Spread media 3-4 inches deep; break spawn into smallest fragments; broadcast evenly
- Tools don't need cleaning until after container filling if they stay in the mixing area

### Automation Warning
"Automation can also complicate your process, not only by magnifying your production volume, but also by magnifying your problems, such as low-yielding formulas, strain degeneration, and possible contamination."

## Harvesting and Storage

### Fresh Market
"Selling your beautiful mushrooms just a day or two after harvest requires nothing more than refrigeration and packing the mushrooms into slightly aerated cardboard boxes."

### Drying
- Solar-drying increases **vitamin D** content in gilled mushrooms
- Place with gills facing sun for vitamin D synthesis
- Keep whole and trim minimally during initial drying
- Store in glass containers (beetles can chew through plastic)

### Freezing
- Blanch first (boil, then plunge into cold water) to prevent mushy texture
- Freeze suspended in water/ice blocks for better color, flavor, and texture preservation

## Beginner Recommendations

"Begin with the easiest species and the most readily available substrates":
1. **Shiitakes on logs**
2. **King stropharia on wood chips**
3. **Oyster mushrooms on agricultural waste**
