# Fungal Biodiversity Patterns Across Ecosystem Types - Implications for Agriculture

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: Biodiversity of Fungi (Hyde et al., eds.), Chapters 11, 13, and related sections

## Introduction

Understanding fungal biodiversity patterns across different ecosystem types is essential for agriculture because surrounding landscapes provide inoculum sources, influence pest and pathogen pressure, and contribute to regional [[fungal-biodiversity]], [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]]
- Adaptation to periodic disturbance (grazing, fire, drought)
- Significant contribution to soil carbon storage through root-derived organic matter

See also: [[clavicipitaceous-grass-endophytes]], [[fungal-dead-wood-diversity]]

### Tropical Forests

Tropical forests are the most diverse terrestrial ecosystems, and their fungal diversity is correspondingly high. However, species diversity in tropical soils is surprisingly similar to that in temperate soils when standardized by sampling effort. The humid tropics show rapid decline in species richness with increasing depth in mineral soil.

### Ectomycorrhizal Dominance

In many temperate and [[ectomycorrhizal-fungal-ecology]], [[comparison-soil-food-web-vs-arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] fungi is generally low in agriculture compared with indigenous plant communities. Crop and rotation history also influence the abundance and diversity of AM fungi in agricultural soil.

Factors reducing fungal diversity in croplands:
- Monoculture reduces substrate diversity
- Tillage disrupts hyphal networks
- Fertilizers reduce mycorrhizal dependency
- Pesticides kill non-target fungi
- Clean fallows eliminate living root hosts

See also: fungal-roles-in-agriculture

### Edge Effects and Landscape Context

The fungal communities in agricultural fields are influenced by the surrounding landscape:
- Field margins and hedgerows serve as refugia for diverse fungi
- Adjacent forests and grasslands provide inoculum sources
- Landscape connectivity affects fungal dispersal and colonization
- Fragmented landscapes may lose fungal diversity through isolation

### Agroforestry Systems

Agroforestry combines trees with crops or livestock, creating habitat for diverse fungal communities:
- Trees provide permanent root systems that maintain [[ozier-pdc-composting-and-soil-building]] for no-till approaches.
- The degree of disturbance affects the balance between r-selected (weedy) and K-selected (competitive) fungal species

## Biogeographic Patterns

### Latitudinal Diversity Gradients

Like most organism groups, fungal diversity generally increases toward the tropics. However, this pattern is less pronounced for soil fungi than for above-ground macrofungi. Soil fungal species diversity in humid tropical soils is comparable to that in temperate forests.
