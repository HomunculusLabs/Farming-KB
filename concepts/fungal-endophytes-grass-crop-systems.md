# Fungal Endophytes Grass Crop Systems

Updated: 2026-04-18

Fungal endophytes of grasses and crop plants represent one of the most important plant-fungal interactions in both natural and agricultural ecosystems, with profound implications for pasture management, crop production, and ecological restoration.

## Two Major Types of Grass Endophytes

### Clavicipitaceous Endophytes (Systemic)

These endophytes colonize virtually all tissues of grass hosts and are found in the seed coat and near the embryo. They are:

- Systemic throughout the plant body
- Vertically transmitted through seeds
- Often mutualistic, providing herbivore deterrence through alkaloid production
- Well-studied in genera such as *Neotyphodium* and *Epichloë*
- The basis of "endophyte-enhanced" tall fescue and perennial ryegrass pastures

### P-Endophytes (Non-Systemic)

Species such as *Phialophora* and *Gliocladium* represent a different colonization strategy:

- Nonsystemic but also seed-borne (An et al. 1993)
- More restricted tissue colonization
- Less studied than clavicipitaceous endophytes
- Found in diverse grass species worldwide

## Endophytes in Non-Grass Hosts

Scattered but significant reports exist of systemic seed-borne endophytes in nongrass hosts:

- **Casuarina** — *Phomopsis casuarinae* (Bose 1947)
- **Helianthemum** — systemic colonization (Boursnell 1950)
- **Ericaceae** — systemic colonization (Rayner 1915, 1929)

In general, nongrass endophyte colonization is usually restricted to a few cells, unlike the systemic colonization seen in grasses.

## Sampling and Detection Challenges

Sampling strategies must account for colonization patterns. Key methodological issues include:

- **Surface sterilization** — must be rigorous enough to eliminate epiphytic fungi while not killing endophytes; protocols vary by host (ethanol/NaOCl treatments for different hosts)
- **Tissue selection** — different tissues harbor different endophyte communities; stem segments, leaf pieces, and seeds may yield different results
- **Seasonal variation** — endophyte colonization can vary with host phenology and environmental conditions

## Surface Sterilization Protocols

Standard protocols have been developed for various hosts:

| Host | Protocol |
|------|----------|
| Rhododendron, Vaccinium | Ethanol/NaOCl treatment |
| Crataegus, Pteridium | Modified ethanol/NaOCl |
| Conifer needles | Extended surface sterilization |
| Quercus, Pinus, Fagus | Standard protocol with modifications |
| Salix, Ulex | Host-specific adjustments |

## Agricultural Significance

Endophytic fungi affect agriculture through multiple pathways:

- **Herbivore resistance** — endophyte-produced alkaloids deter insect and mammalian herbivores
- **Drought tolerance** — some endophytes improve host water use efficiency
- **Disease resistance** — endophytes can suppress pathogenic fungi and bacteria
- **Growth promotion** — certain endophytes produce plant growth hormones

## Ecological Implications

Endophytes mediate plant-herbivore interactions, influence plant community composition, and affect ecosystem-level processes such as nutrient cycling and primary productivity. Their presence or absence can determine whether a grass species becomes dominant in a plant community.
