# San Pedro Cactus Andean Ceremony and Curandero Altar Practice

## Overview

The San Pedro cactus (*[[trichocereus-pachanoi]]*) is a columnar cactus native to the Andean regions of South America that has been used in ceremonial and healing contexts for thousands of years. In Ralph Metzner's comparative framework of [[metzner-three-distinctive-features-shamanic-entheogenic-ceremonies]] rituals, alongside peyote ceremonies [[metzner-mazatec-mushroom-veladas-darkness-singing-and-christian-syncretism]], [[metzner-five-experience-features-entheogenic-shamanic-practice]] ceremonies while maintaining distinctive features:

- **Fire ceremonies**: Some San Pedro ceremonies are conducted around a central fire at night, similar to peyote ceremonies of the [[amazonian-ayahuasca-purge-and-diet-practices]] ceremonies where the healer sings icaros throughout the night, San Pedro ceremonies may involve periods of both singing and relative silence.
- The curandero monitors participants and may work with the mesa objects during the ceremony, manipulating the altar to interact with the spiritual forces affecting the participant.

### Healing Modalities

The healing approach in San Pedro ceremonies, as in other shamanic systems described by Metzner, operates through several modalities:

1. **Divination/diagnosis**: Using the mesa and the visionary state to perceive the root causes of illness.
2. **Energetic cleansing**: The curandero performs cleansing rituals (*limpia*) using the mesa objects, plants, or other tools to remove harmful energies.
3. **Soul retrieval**: Recovering fragmented aspects of the patient's being, a practice Metzner identifies as one of three primary forms of [[native-american-church-peyote-ceremony-structure-roles-roadman]]) and ayahuasca (Santo Daime, UDV, Barquinha) have been, so it remains primarily within the shamanic healing paradigm rather than the folk religious paradigm.

### Hybrid Shamanic Therapeutic Rituals (Paradigm 4)

Modern urban San Pedro practice in Peru increasingly incorporates elements of Western psychotherapy and group processing, moving toward Metzner's hybrid paradigm. Some contemporary curanderos integrate discussion, sharing circles, and psychological framing alongside traditional mesa work.
