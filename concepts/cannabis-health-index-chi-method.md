# Cannabis Health Index (CHI) Method

## Purpose

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) is an evidence-based compilation and
review of available scientific literature relevant to more than 100
different chronic diseases and stubborn symptoms. Developed by Uwe
Blesching, PhD, the CHI model provides a systematic framework for
evaluating cannabis as a therapeutic intervention, addressing a critical
gap in evidence-based cannabis medicine. (see [[cannabis-health-index-chi-scoring-methodology-blesching]]).

## Evidence-Based Context

Only approximately 15% of medical interventions are based on solid
scientific evidence. The CHI was created to address the lack of a
systematic evidence-based model for the plant's safe and targeted use. (see [[blesching-cannabis-chi-scoring-evidence-based-medicine]]).
The demand for evidence-driven healthcare has led to the establishment of
13 evidence-based practice centers funded by the U.S. Department of
Health, including institutions like the Mayo Clinic, Duke University,
and [[johns-hopkins-psychedelic-research]].

## CHI Score

The CHI assigns a score to each condition based on the quality and
quantity of available research. The scoring considers:

- **Type of research study**: Randomized controlled trials carry more
  weight than case studies or anecdotal reports
- **Number of studies**: More independent confirmations increase
  confidence
- **Consistency of results**: Agreement across multiple studies
- **Clinical relevance**: Real-world applicability of findings

### Using CHI Scores

The CHI score helps patients and practitioners:

1. Assess the overall evidence level for cannabis treating a specific
   condition
2. Make informed decisions about [[cultivator-strain-selection-and-stock-culture]] administration
   method
3. Set realistic expectations for therapeutic outcomes
4. Track personal response relative to established evidence

## Strain-Specific Considerations

The CHI framework incorporates evidence-based strain and form
considerations:

- **Cannabinoid profile**: THC-to-CBD ratios relevant to specific
  conditions
- **CB1 vs. CB2 deficiency assessment**: Patients with neurological
  symptoms likely lack CB1 activation; those with immune conditions
  likely are CB2-deficient, guiding strain selection
- **Form of administration**: Tinctures, edibles, vaporization, topical
  applications — each with different bioavailability profiles
