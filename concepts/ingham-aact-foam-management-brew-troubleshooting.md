### Problem: Low Organism Counts
**Cause**: Multiple possible factors including poor compost quality,
contaminated water, insufficient food, or inadequate brewing time.

**Solutions**:
- Verify compost meets minimum organism standards (test by direct microscopy)
- Use dechlorinated water (aerate water for 1 hour before adding compost)
- Ensure water temperature is 65-75°F (18-24°C) — too cold slows growth
- Check brewing time — most teas need 24-36 hours minimum
- Verify molasses is unsulfured and not expired

### Problem: Thick Slime on Tank Walls or Surface
**Cause**: Excessive bacterial growth producing extracellular polymeric
substances (EPS). Often associated with too much simple sugar (molasses)
without adequate aeration.

**Solutions**:
- Reduce molasses rate
- Increase aeration
- Add diverse food sources (kelp, humic acids) to support more diverse
  [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]], or exothermic microbial
activity. High temperature reduces dissolved oxygen solubility and favors
thermophilic over mesophilic organisms.

**Solutions**:
- Brew in a cool location (basement, shade)
- Use frozen water bottles to cool the brew (pre-boil to dechlorinate)
- Reduce brewing food to slow metabolic heat production
- Switch to brewing during cooler parts of the day

## Quality Verification

After troubleshooting, verify tea quality before application using direct
microscopy:
1. Check dissolved oxygen is above 6 ppm
2. Verify pH is 5.5-7.0 (acceptable range for most applications)
3. Examine under 400x for active bacteria (bright, moving), fungal hyphae,
   and protozoa
4. Confirm absence of anaerobic indicator organisms (thick rods, spirochetes)
5. Smell test — should be earthy, pleasant, no sour or putrid notes
