# Blessing the Ground Ritual

The Blessing of the Ground is a foundational ritual from "Marijuana Magick: A
Practitioners Guide" by Magus Sativus. It is described as one of the simplest
cannabis-involved rituals and is recommended to be performed often. The ritual
serves two primary purposes: attuning the ground to a higher spiritual level
and expressing gratitude for the earth's bountiful glory. It can also be used
to promote crop growth.

## Overview and Purpose

This ritual combines the ancient practice of earth reverence with the
ceremonial use of cannabis as an offering and meditative aid. The practitioner
moves through the four cardinal directions, offering thanks and smoke to each,
while standing within a drawn pentagram. The ceremony culminates in a seated
meditation during which the practitioner visualizes receiving healing energy
from the earth.

The ritual emphasizes themes of gratitude, harmony with nature, and the
recognition that all things, including the earth itself, are fleeting yet
eternal in spirit.

## Materials Required

- Cannabis, either in a glass pipe or rolled in paper (joint)
- A small stick or the practitioner's finger for drawing
- A brush for clearing the pentagram at the ritual's conclusion

## Preparing the Sacred Space

### Drawing the Circle and Pentagram

The practitioner begins by standing within a circle approximately ten feet in
diameter, though this may be adjusted based on available space. The circle is
drawn using either a finger or a small stick. Once the circle is established,
the practitioner draws a pentagram within it and stands in the middle of the
star.

The pentagram, a five-pointed star enclosed in a circle, is a primary symbol
in pagan and wiccan traditions representing the elements and spiritual
protection. In this ritual, it serves as the focal point from which the
practitioner addresses each direction.

## The Four Directions

### South: The Earth

Facing south, the practitioner lights the cannabis and says: "Thank you for
the bounty of the ground and the earth." They then take one puff, inhaling for
five seconds or as long as comfortably possible, and exhale toward the south.

### North: The Sky and Aether

Turning to face north, the practitioner says: "Thank you for the bounty of
the sky, of space and of the aether." Another puff is taken and held for five
seconds as before.

### East: Fire and Knowledge

Facing east, the practitioner says: "I thank you for the gift of fire, of
energy, of warmth, of knowledge. Prometheus has given this to us from you and
we thank you for that." A puff is taken as before.

The reference to Prometheus invokes the Greek mythological figure who stole
fire from the gods and gave it to humanity, framing fire as sacred knowledge.

### West: Water and Life

Turning to face west, the practitioner says: "Thank you for the gift of
water, this life-sustaining liquid that has allowed us to flourish." The pipe
or joint is used once more.

## The Northern Invocation

After completing the four directions, the practitioner returns to face north
and delivers an extended invocation: "Thank you again earth, for your bounty
and your glory. Please let me flourish on this earth and you have all of your
believed plants and animals, let man and woman live together on your surface
in harmony with nature and let us live to abide by your laws and enjoy your
grace, bounty and joys."

## Seated Meditation

The practitioner then sits cross-legged or in whatever comfortable position
they are able, in the middle of the pentagram, facing south. With eyes closed,
they visualize the earth as a blue and green marble pulsating with life and
joy. Green and blue rays of energy are imagined coming through the ground's
surface to embrace the soul and furnish it with the requested energy.

This energy is then directed toward whatever endeavor the practitioner wishes
to fuel. The meditation should continue for several minutes, with optional
soft chanting. The cannabis serves as an aid to open the soul to the earth's
healing and rejuvenating energies.

## Closing Prayer

During meditation, the practitioner offers: "Thank you earth for the plants
that you give us to use, as medicine for our mind, body and soul. Thank you
for these and let us use them responsibly, to heal our body, enlighten our
mind and engage our spirit."

When ready to close, the practitioner stands and says: "Thank you earth for
your bounty, for your joy, for your gifts of pleasure and happiness."

## Erasing the Pentagram

The pentagram is gently wiped away with a brush so it can no longer be seen.
Sativus explains this act as a reminder "that the earth, like all things, is
fleeting and in time will no longer exist though the memories of it and its
spirit will continue to live on forever."

This final act carries profound philosophical significance, connecting the
ritual to the universal spiritual truth of impermanence while affirming the
eternal nature of spirit.
