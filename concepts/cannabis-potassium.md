# Cannabis Potassium (K)

## Overview

Potassium is a primary [[cannabis-magnesium-nutrient]], [[query-how-to-fix-zinc-deficiency-in-cannabis]] and [[cannabis-organic-fertilizers]] can add potassium through:

- Soluble potash (wood ashes) mixed with water
- [[comparison-kelp-meal-vs-rock-dust]] and seaweed extracts
- Greensand (glauconite)
- Banana peels (composted)
- [[comparison-ramial-wood-chip-mulch-vs-composted-wood-chips]] ash (sparingly -- pH is normally above 10)
- Granite dust (slow release)

Be careful when using wood ash -- the pH is normally above 10. Use a pH-lowering mix to bring the pH to around 6.5 before application. Avoid using alkaline wood ashes in soil with a pH above 6.5.

## Deficiency

Potassium-starved plants initially appear healthy, which makes early detection difficult. Deficient plants are susceptible to disease. Potassium is usually present in the soil but is locked in by high salinity.

### Symptoms

- Older leaves develop spots, first at tips and margins, then on whole leaves
- Leaves turn dark yellow and die
- Stems often become weak and sometimes brittle
- Internal foliage temperature climbs and protein cells burn or degrade
- Evaporation is normally highest on leaf edges, causing burning at margins
- Flowering is retarded and greatly diminished

### Deficiency Progression Timeline

1. Plants appear healthy with dark green foliage
2. The leaves lose their luster
3. Branching may increase, but branches are weak and scrawny
4. Leaf margins turn grey and progress to a rusty-brown color, then curl up and dry
5. Yellowing of the older leaves is accompanied by rust-colored blotches
6. The leaves curl up, rot sets in, and the older leaves drop
7. Flowering is retarded and greatly diminished

### Treatment

Fertilize with a complete [[cannabis-foliar-feeding]] to cure a [[knf-potassium-deficiency-and-excess]] is not recommended.

During flowering, a foliar spray of potassium can help flowering, especially if temperatures dip below 50F (10C) or above 80F (25C).

## Toxicity

Toxicity occurs occasionally and is difficult to diagnose because it is mixed with the deficiency symptoms of other nutrients.

### Symptoms

Too much potassium impairs and slows the absorption of [[cannabis-manganese-nutrient]], and sometimes [[query-how-to-identify-and-fix-cannabis-iron-deficiency]]. Look for signs of toxic potassium buildup when symptoms of magnesium, manganese, zinc, and iron deficiencies appear.

### Treatment

Flush the growing medium with a very mild, complete fertilizer. Severe problems require that more water be flushed through the growing medium. Flush a minimum of three times the volume of water for the volume of the growing medium.

## Growth Stage Considerations

Potassium is used at all stages of growth but becomes particularly important during flowering when cannabis shifts from nitrogen-dominant nutrition to potassium-dominant nutrition. Using a "super bloom" fertilizer with less nitrogen and more potassium promotes fat, heavy, dense buds. During flowering, nitrogen takes a backseat to potassium, phosphorus, and calcium intake. Cannabis needs some nitrogen during flowering, but very little -- with no nitrogen, buds do not develop to their full potential.

## Common Mistakes

- Over-applying potassium during flowering, which causes magnesium and manganese lockout
- Using wood ash without checking and adjusting pH (ash is extremely alkaline, pH above 10)
- Failing to account for sodium in irrigation water, which competes with potassium uptake
- Confusing early potassium deficiency signs with normal late-flowering yellowing
- Using [[comparison-foliar-feeding-vs-compost-tea-nutrients]] as the primary correction for potassium deficiency (it is not recommended)
- Not flushing excess potassium before it cascades into [[query-how-to-fix-magnesium-deficiency-in-cannabis]]
