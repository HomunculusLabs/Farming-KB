The default mode network (DMN) is a large-scale brain network that is active
during passive rest, mind-wandering, and self-referential thinking. In the
context of [[neurochemical-probes-brain-imaging-pihkal]] studies consistently show a marked decrease in DMN activity and
connectivity. This reduction correlates strongly with subjects' reports of ego
dissolution, the experience of losing the sense of a separate, bounded self.

Ego dissolution represents what D.M. Turner describes as the first stage of the
psychedelic experience model: the dissolution of one's identity and perceptual
framework, commonly called ego death. Following this dissolution, the individual
experiences raw, undifferentiated energies flowing through their senses from a
formless or unconditioned state.

The significance of ego dissolution extends beyond the phenomenological
experience. Research suggests that the degree of DMN disintegration during a
psychedelic session predicts long-term positive outcomes, including reductions
in depression, anxiety, and addictive behaviors. The temporary collapse of the
default mode of self-referential thinking appears to create a window of
psychological flexibility.

McKenna connected the suppression of the ego-dissolving rites of visionary
plants to the rise of what he called the [[lsd-set-and-setting-framework]] in determining whether ego
dissolution becomes a source of liberation or terror.

The relationship between ego dissolution and spiritual experience is
significant. Many of the experiences described in mystical literature across
traditions involve the transcendence of the individual self. Psychedelic-induced
ego dissolution can produce experiences that are phenomenologically
indistinguishable from those reported by advanced meditators, suggesting a
common neurobiological mechanism.

After ego dissolution, the process of reassembling a coherent sense of self is a
critical phase of the psychedelic experience. Turner describes this as the
re-assembly of a new personality based on the old personality but hopefully
improved. How this reintegration is handled significantly influences the
long-term benefits of the experience.

The concept of ego dissolution challenges the Western emphasis on maintaining a
strong, stable ego. While a coherent sense of self is important for everyday
functioning, the psychedelic experience suggests that the capacity to
temporarily dissolve ego boundaries may be equally important for psychological
flexibility, creativity, and spiritual well-being.
