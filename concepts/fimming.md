# Fimming

Fimming is a cannabis pruning technique that involves removing approximately 75-90 percent of the main growing tip while leaving a small portion intact. The name is an acronym coined by an anonymous grower who accidentally performed the technique and exclaimed "Fuck, I Missed!" when he did not remove the entire bud. Cervantes documents the technique in detail, noting that it "has become legendary" in cannabis growing communities.

## Origin of the Technique

Cervantes provides the origin story:

- The FIM Technique "was coined by an anonymous High Times reader from South Carolina in the July 2000 issue of the magazine"
- "The technique has become legendary on www.overgrow.com"
- The South Carolina grower "tried to pinch the tip of a plant and said 'Fuck, I Missed!' when he did not remove the entire bud and coined the acronym FIM"
- The grower claimed "this pruning technique could revolutionize indoor gardening"

## FIM vs Topping

FIM is often described as a variation of [[topping-cannabis]], but the results differ significantly:

- **Topping**: the entire growing tip is removed cleanly. The two branches directly below the cut grow into two main colas. The result is predictable and consistent.
- **FIM**: only 75-90 percent of the tip is removed, leaving the bottom 10-25 percent intact. This often produces 3-6 or more new colas rather than 2. The results are less predictable but potentially more productive.

Cervantes illustrates the difference clearly: "The drawing on the left shows the traditional method to top a plant. The entire growing shoot just below the bud is removed. When the entire growing shoot is removed, the two buds located directly below the cut grow faster and stronger." In contrast, "the drawing in the center and the close-up on the right show the FIM pruning technique -- the bottom ten percent of the bud remains intact."

## How FIM Works

The FIM pruning process:

1. Wait until the plant has 3-5 sets of true leaves (similar timing to topping)
2. Identify the newest growth at the apical meristem -- the tender, light-green tip
3. Instead of cutting the entire tip off, pinch or cut away approximately 75-90 percent of it
4. Leave the bottom 10-25 percent of the growing tip intact -- this is the critical step
5. The remaining tissue contains multiple growth points that will each develop into a new branch
6. Within 1-2 weeks, 3-6 or more new growing tips emerge from the cut site

The key insight from Cervantes: "This is the key to FIM pruning. Many different flowering tops form as a result of this single pruning." By leaving the base of the growing tip, multiple axillary buds that would normally remain dormant are activated simultaneously.

## Results and Effectiveness

According to Cervantes and FIM practitioners:

- "Many different flowering tops form as a result of this single pruning"
- "According to FIM aficionados, terminal buds put on much more weight" compared to topped plants
- The new colas are described as "more dense" than those from traditional topping
- Results are less predictable than topping -- some cuts produce 3 colas, others 4-6 or more
- The unpredictability is the main drawback: two identical FIM cuts can produce different results

## When to Use FIM

Fimming is best used when:

- The grower wants more colas than topping provides (3-6 vs 2)
- Working with strains that respond well to branching
- The plant is healthy and in active vegetative growth
- There is sufficient vegetative time for recovery before flowering
- The grower is willing to accept some unpredictability in results

FIM should be performed during the [[cannabis-vegetative-stage]], ideally at 3-5 weeks of age, similar to topping. Complete all FIM pruning at least 3-4 weeks before switching to 12/12 flowering.

## Integration with Supercropping

Cervantes notes that "[[supercropping]] can also incorporate FIM pruning." The combination involves performing a FIM cut and then bending or crushing the newly emerged branches to create an even more complex branch structure. This aggressive approach should only be attempted on vigorous, healthy plants with ample recovery time.

## Advantages

- More colas per cut: 3-6 new colas vs 2 from traditional topping
- Potentially heavier total yield when successful
- Single cut can replace multiple topping sessions
- Denser bud development on terminal colas
- Can be combined with other training techniques

## Disadvantages

- Unpredictable results: number and arrangement of new colas varies
- Less consistent than topping for creating an even canopy
- Recovery time similar to topping (1-2 weeks for new growth to emerge)
- Difficult to perform precisely -- the "missed" portion must be just right
- Not suitable for autoflowers with limited vegetative time
- Over-FIMing (too many cuts) can stress plants and cause spindly growth

## Cervantes' Wider Context

Fimming exists within Cervantes' broader discussion of pruning and training. He places it alongside topping, supercropping, and bending as tools for manipulating plant structure. The underlying principle is hormonal manipulation: "Removing or bending a branch or branch tip causes hormonal balances to shift. Cutting the meristem (top growth tip) of a cannabis plant will diffuse auxins and cause greater concentrations in lower branch tips."

However, Cervantes also provides the counterpoint that "most successful growers do not prune at all" and that "floral hormones are allowed to concentrate in tips of branches causing buds to grow stronger and denser" when plants are left unpruned. FIM should be viewed as one tool among many, not a mandatory technique.

## Tools and Technique

- FIM can be performed by pinching with fingers (preferred for soft new growth) or with sharp scissors
- Sterilize tools with rubbing alcohol before making cuts
- Work with the tender new growth at the very top of the plant
- A sharp, clean cut produces better results than a ragged tear
- Practice on a spare plant before attempting on your best specimens

## FIM Success Rate

The variability of FIM results is both its strength and weakness. Cervantes notes that results depend on several factors:

- The exact point and angle of the cut
- The age and vigor of the plant
- The strain's genetic tendency toward branching
- The health and nutrient status of the plant at the time of cutting

Some growers report near-100 percent success rates with specific strains after practice, while others find FIM too unpredictable. The technique rewards experimentation and careful record-keeping.

## Comparing FIM to Multiple Toppings

For growers seeking many colas, there are two main approaches:

- **FIM once**: 3-6 colas from a single cut, faster recovery
- **Top twice**: 4 colas from two sequential cuts, more predictable but slower

Each approach has trade-offs. FIM is faster and can produce more colas per cut, but with less control. Multiple toppings are more predictable and produce more uniform colas, but require more time for recovery between cuts.

## Combining FIM with Other Techniques

FIM integrates well with other training methods:

- [[low-stress-training]]: after FIM creates multiple colas, LST positions them for even light
- [[screen-of-green]]: the many colas from FIM fill a SCROG screen more quickly
- [[main-lining]]: FIM can be used at the final topping point to maximize colas per branch
- [[supercropping]]: FIM followed by stem crushing creates extremely bushy plants

## After FIM Care

- Allow 1-2 weeks for new growth to emerge from the cut site
- Provide adequate nitrogen to support new vegetative growth
- Monitor the cut site for signs of infection
- Once new shoots are 2-3 inches long, begin LST to position them
- Do not perform additional FIM or topping on the same branch until new growth is well established
