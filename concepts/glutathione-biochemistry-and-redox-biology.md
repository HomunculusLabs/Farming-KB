Glutathione (GSH) is a tripeptide thiol — γ-L-glutamyl-L-cysteinylglycine — and the most abundant non-protein thiol in mammalian cells, present at 1–10 mM in the cytosol. With a molecular weight of 307.32 g/mol, it plays a central role in redox homeostasis, detoxification, and cellular signaling across virtually all cell types. The name derives from its three constituent parts: glutamic acid, the thiol of cysteine, and the glycine residue. Discovered by Frederick Gowland Hopkins in 1921 from yeast and liver extracts, glutathione was confirmed as a tripeptide by Harington and Mead in 1935. Hopkins received the 1929 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his broader biochemical contributions, including this foundational work.

## Chemical Structure and the Gamma Peptide Bond

Glutathione is not a standard peptide. The bond between glutamate and cysteine links the γ-carboxyl group of glutamate to the amino group of cysteine, rather than the typical α-carboxyl linkage found in virtually all other peptides and proteins. This gamma (γ) peptide bond renders glutathione resistant to degradation by most cellular peptidases — a critical property for its biological stability and longevity. The cysteine residue provides the reactive sulfhydryl (–SH) group with a pKa of approximately 8.75. At physiological pH, a small fraction exists as the more nucleophilic thiolate anion (GS⁻), the species involved in enzymatic reactions.

Glutathione exists in two interconvertible forms: reduced GSH (MW 307.32 g/mol, the active thiol) and oxidized glutathione disulfide GSSG (MW 612.63 g/mol), formed by a disulfide bridge between two GSH molecules.

## Biosynthesis

Glutathione is synthesized intracellularly in two ATP-dependent steps, primarily in hepatocyte cytosol. Each step consumes one ATP, making synthesis energetically expensive.

**Step 1 — γ-Glutamylcysteine formation** (rate-limiting): L-Glutamate + L-Cysteine + ATP → γ-L-Glutamyl-L-cysteine + ADP + Pi. Catalyzed by γ-glutamylcysteine synthetase (GCL), a heterodimer of catalytic subunit GCLC (73 kDa) and regulatory subunit GCLM (31 kDa). GCL undergoes feedback inhibition by GSH.

**Step 2 — Glutathione formation**: γ-L-Glutamyl-L-cysteine + Glycine + ATP → Glutathione + ADP + Pi. Catalyzed by glutathione synthetase (GS). GS deficiency causes a rare metabolic disorder with hemolytic anemia, metabolic acidosis, and neurological damage.

### Cysteine as the Rate-Limiting Substrate

Cysteine availability is the primary bottleneck. Three supply routes: (1) trans-sulfuration from methionine via cystathionine β-synthase and cystathionine γ-lyase; (2) direct cystine uptake via the xCT (SLC7A11) transporter of system x_c⁻; (3) dietary intake from high-protein foods. N-acetylcysteine (NAC) bypasses the rate-limiting step by providing cysteine directly.

## Redox Cycling: GSH/GSSG Balance

The GSH/GSSG ratio is the primary indicator of cellular redox status, tightly regulated across compartments: ~100:1 in the cytosol (GSH 1–10 mM, GSSG 1–10 μM), ~10:1 in mitochondria, and ~1:1 to 3:1 in the endoplasmic reticulum where a more oxidizing environment facilitates disulfide bond formation.

Glutathione reductase (GR, encoded by GSR) maintains the reduced state: GSSG + NADPH + H⁺ → 2 GSH + NADP⁺. This FAD-containing dimeric flavoprotein depends on NADPH from the pentose phosphate pathway (G6PD) and secondarily from malic enzyme and NADP⁺-dependent isocitrate dehydrogenase. G6PD deficiency — the most common human enzymopathy affecting 400+ million people — impairs NADPH production and compromises the glutathione redox cycle, causing hemolytic anemia under oxidative stress.

## Enzymatic Systems

### Glutathione Peroxidases (GPx)

Eight mammalian isoforms (GPx1–GPx8), all containing selenocysteine at their active site encoded by recoded UGA codons with SECIS elements. GPx1 (cytosolic/mitochondrial) is most abundant, reducing H₂O₂ and organic hydroperoxides. GPx4 uniquely reduces lipid hydroperoxides within membranes and is essential for preventing ferroptosis. General reaction: 2 GSH + ROOH → GSSG + ROH + H₂O.

### Glutathione S-Transferases (GST)

A superfamily of Phase II detoxification enzymes with 20+ human cytosolic isoforms in classes Alpha, Mu, Pi, Theta, Omega, Zeta, and Sigma. GSTs conjugate the GSH thiolate anion (GS⁻) to electrophilic substrates including carcinogens, environmental toxins, lipid peroxidation products (4-hydroxynonenal), and chemotherapeutic drugs. GSTP1-1 is important in cancer drug resistance and modulates JNK signaling. GSTs also possess peroxidase activity toward organic hydroperoxides.

### Glutaredoxins (Grx)

Small thiol-disulfide oxidoreductases (9–15 kDa) catalyzing reversible protein de-glutathionylation. Isoforms include Grx1 (cytosolic), Grx2 (mitochondrial/nuclear), and Grx3/4/5. This cycle is a reversible redox switch protecting cysteine residues from irreversible overoxidation. Key targets: actin (cytoskeleton), caspase-3 (apoptosis inhibition), NF-κB, GAPDH, SERCA, and mitochondrial Complex I. Grx3/4/5 also participate in iron-sulfur cluster biogenesis.

## Protein S-Glutathionylation

S-glutathionylation forms mixed disulfides between GSH and protein cysteine residues (Protein-SSG), occurring spontaneously under oxidative stress or catalytically via GSTs and glutaredoxins. Functions include protecting cysteines from irreversible overoxidation, modulating enzymatic activity, and regulating apoptosis — high GSH inhibits caspase-3 via glutathionylation while depletion sensitizes cells to cytochrome c release. The GSH/GSSG balance governs mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) opening, linking redox status to cell survival.

## Detoxification: The Mercapturic Acid Pathway

GSH conjugation is the most important Phase II detoxification mechanism. The mercapturic acid pathway transforms lipophilic toxins into water-soluble derivatives in four steps: (1) GST-catalyzed conjugation of GSH to an electrophile; (2) γ-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT) removes the γ-glutamyl moiety, particularly in kidney proximal tubule and bile duct epithelium; (3) dipeptidases remove glycine; (4) N-acetyltransferase acetylates the cysteine amino group, yielding a mercapturic acid excreted in urine.

This pathway handles a vast range of substrates including aflatoxin B1, benzopyrene,
acetaminophen metabolites (NAPQI-GSH conjugate), and environmental pollutants.

GGT, the first enzyme in the processing chain, is clinically important as a serum
biomarker of liver disease and alcohol consumption. Its activity determines the rate
at which GSH conjugates are processed for excretion. GGT induction can accelerate GSH
turnover and potentially deplete cellular GSH pools under high xenobiotic exposure.

## Mitochondrial Glutathione

Mitochondrial GSH (mGSH) detoxifies H₂O₂ and lipid peroxides from the electron transport chain. Mitochondria cannot synthesize GSH; it is imported via the 2-oxoglutarate carrier (OGC) and dicarboxylate carrier (DIC) in the inner mitochondrial membrane. mGSH depletion during oxidative stress or ethanol metabolism increases susceptibility to mPTP opening and cell death. The mGSH/GPx4 axis prevents ferroptosis, with disruption implicated in neurodegeneration, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and hepatotoxicity.

## Clinical Significance

### Disease-Associated GSH Depletion
- **Neurodegeneration**: Parkinson's substantia nigra shows 40–50% GSH reduction, potentially preceding neuronal loss. Alzheimer's correlates with decreased hippocampal and cortical GSH.
- **Liver disease**: GSH depletion in alcoholic liver disease, NAFLD, and acetaminophen toxicity (NAPQI depletes >70% hepatic GSH).
- **HIV/AIDS**: Chronic CD4⁺ lymphocyte GSH depletion impairs immunity and promotes viral replication.
- **Diabetes**: Decreased GSH drives oxidative complications (neuropathy, nephropathy, retinopathy).
- **COPD**: Cigarette smoke depletes airway epithelial lining fluid GSH.
- **Aging**: Progressive GCL activity decline reduces GSH synthesis capacity, contributing to accumulated oxidative damage and age-related functional decline across organ systems.

### Glutathione in Cancer
Tumor cells maintain elevated GSH (2–10× normal), driven by upregulated GCL, increased xCT/SLC7A11 cystine uptake, and Nrf2-ARE activation. High GSH confers chemoradiotherapy resistance by scavenging ROS and inhibiting apoptosis. Buthionine sulfoximine (BSO), a GCL inhibitor, has been investigated as a chemosensitizer. GSTP1 overexpression correlates with multidrug resistance and poor prognosis.

### Therapeutic Approaches
NAC is the most widely used GSH precursor (FDA-approved for acetaminophen overdose; off-label for COPD, chronic bronchitis, contrast nephropathy). Oral bioavailability is low (4–10%) but sufficient to raise intracellular GSH. Other strategies: N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA, improved BBB penetration), α-lipoic acid, whey protein (cysteine-rich), and Nrf2 activators (sulforaphane, curcumin) upregulating GCL, GPx, and GST expression.

## Immune Function

GSH is essential for lymphocyte proliferation and T-cell activation. It modulates cytokine production (IL-2, IL-12, IFN-γ, TNF-α) and regulates Th1/Th2 balance: adequate GSH supports Th1 cell-mediated immunity while depletion favors Th2 responses linked to allergy. Macrophage phagocytic function depends on GSH to manage the respiratory burst.

## Regulation of Glutathione Synthesis

Glutathione synthesis is regulated at multiple levels. The Nrf2 (NF-E2-related factor 2) transcription factor is the master regulator of the antioxidant response, binding to antioxidant response elements (ARE) in the promoters of GCLC, GCLM, GS, GPx, GST, and GR genes. Under basal conditions, Nrf2 is sequestered in the cytoplasm by Keap1 and targeted for ubiquitin-mediated degradation. Oxidative stress or electrophilic compounds modify Keap1 cysteine residues, releasing Nrf2 to translocate to the nucleus and activate transcription.

Nrf2 activators include sulforaphane (from cruciferous vegetables), curcumin, resveratrol, and synthetic compounds like bardoxolone methyl (CDDO-Me), which has been investigated in clinical trials for chronic kidney disease and diabetic nephropathy. The Nrf2 pathway is a major therapeutic target for diseases involving oxidative stress and GSH depletion, though chronic Nrf2 activation in cancer can promote tumor survival and chemoresistance.

Glucocorticoids and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β) can also modulate GSH synthesis, generally suppressing GCL expression and promoting GSH depletion during chronic inflammation. Insulin and growth factors conversely stimulate GSH synthesis through PI3K/Akt signaling.

## Laboratory Measurement

The enzymatic recycling assay (Tietze method) is the most common high-throughput approach: DTNB (Ellman's reagent) is sequentially oxidized by GSH and reduced by GR with NADPH, measured spectrophotometrically at 412 nm. GSSG is determined separately after derivatizing GSH with 2-vinylpyridine or N-ethylmaleimide. HPLC with fluorescence detection (using monobromobimane or o-phthalaldehyde derivatization) provides separate GSH and GSSG quantification. LC-MS/MS is the gold standard for specificity and sensitivity, capable of simultaneously measuring GSH, GSSG, and GSH conjugates.

Sample handling is critical — blood must be rapidly deproteinized with perchloric acid or trichloroacetic acid and kept cold to prevent ex vivo oxidation. EDTA is used as anticoagulant. GSSG measurement requires immediate derivatization of GSH to prevent artifactual oxidation during processing. Monochlorobimane (MCB) is a cell-permeable fluorescent probe that forms a GST-catalyzed adduct with GSH, enabling flow cytometry and live-cell imaging of intracellular GSH levels.

## Glutathione in Plant Biology

While this article focuses on mammalian biochemistry, glutathione is equally important in plants. Plant GSH serves analogous roles in redox buffering, detoxification of herbicides and pollutants, and defense against pathogen-induced oxidative bursts. Plants also utilize GSH as a precursor for phytochelatins — heavy-metal-binding peptides synthesized by phytochelatin synthase that sequester cadmium, arsenic, and other toxic metals into vacuoles. The plant GSH pool is similarly maintained by GSH/GSSG cycling and is a key determinant of abiotic stress tolerance.

## Interactions with Other Antioxidant Systems

Glutathione does not operate in isolation but is tightly integrated with other
cellular antioxidant systems. The thioredoxin system (thioredoxin, thioredoxin
reductase, NADPH) provides a parallel pathway for reducing protein disulfides and
peroxides. Vitamin C (ascorbate) can regenerate GSH from its radical intermediate,
while vitamin E (α-tocopherol) is regenerated from the tocopheroxyl radical by
GSH-dependent mechanisms via the ascorbate-glutathione cycle.

Catalase, primarily located in peroxisomes, handles bulk H₂O₂ degradation in parallel with GPx. Peroxiredoxins (Prx) constitute a third major peroxide-scavenging system that uses thioredoxin as reductant. Together, these interconnected systems provide redundant and complementary protection against oxidative damage, with GSH serving as the central low-molecular-weight redox buffer that links them all.
