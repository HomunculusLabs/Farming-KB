## DMT in the Amazon

DMT has been used in the Amazon for millennia, primarily by nomadic
hunter-
gatherer cultures. McKenna notes that the prehistoric use of ecstatic
plants
"is not well understood" and that psilocybin-containing Stropharia
cubensis
is not known to be in archaic shamanic use anywhere in the world. The
Amazonian DMT traditions thus represent a distinct cultural and
pharmacological lineage from the Mesoamerican psilocybin traditions.

The secrecy surrounding these traditions has both preserved and isolated
them. McKenna describes indigenous informants who said, "Let"s take our
machetes and hike out here half a mile and get some vine and boil it up
and we will show you what we can show you" — a direct, experiential
approach contrasting sharply with theoretical or textual knowledge.

## The Cultural Suppression of Ecstatic Knowledge

McKenna traces the suppression of plant-based ecstatic knowledge through
multiple historical layers: the destruction of Hellenistic libraries,
the
Christianization of Europe, the Spanish conquest of the Americas, and
the
modern War on Drugs. He argues that this suppression represents not
merely
religious intolerance but a fundamental alienation from the "human soul"
— which he suggests "is so alienated from us in our present culture that
we treat it as an extraterrestrial."
