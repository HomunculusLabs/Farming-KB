## Foliar Epiphytic Fungi — Sooty Molds, Black Mildews, Downy Mildews
The surfaces of living plant leaves, stems, and fruits support
diverse communities of fungi that fruit on but do not necessarily
penetrate living tissues. These epiphytic and obligately biotrophic
fungi range from harmless saprobes to devastating pathogens.
Their study is essential for plant pathology, biodiversity surveys,
and understanding canopy ecosystem dynamics.
## Sooty Molds
Saprobic ascomycetes belonging to several families of Dothideales.
They form black, often extensive colonies on plant surfaces above
ground, obtaining nutrients from extracellular plant exudates or
insect excretions (honeydew from aphids, scale insects). In New
Zealand Nothofagus forests, trunks and branches are covered up to
9+ meters high. Some species produce up to three asexual forms
(pleomorphism), complicating [[mycelium]]: weft of dark hyphae, sometimes several mm thick
- Conidiogenous cells and various conidia formed within mycelium
- Metacapnodiaceae: distinctive tapering, moniliform hyphae with
  rigid smooth-walled cells
- Seuratiaceae: dark, gelatinous, lobed thalli with unique
  reproductive structures
### Growth Forms (Reynolds 1975)
- **Deciduous:** Thin hyphal layer peels away in drier or cooler
  months; lowland tropics, absent from rain forests; common on
  citrus in southeastern US
- **Permanent:** Thick cushionlike black hyphal mats on twigs and
  stems; neotropical highlands and temperate lowlands
### Collection — Colloidal Leaf Peel
1. Spread 2% collodion/parlodion solution on leaf colony
2. Solution dries to transparent film with embedded fungus
3. Peel film from [[ascomycota]]).
More than 1,580 species, mostly in genus Meliola (over 1,400).
Especially abundant in tropics; also from Chile to southern US,
South Africa to Scotland, Japan to Tasmania. Most diverse in scrub
or open parkland; rare in dense rain forest undergrowth. Greatest
infections in naturally or anthropogenically disturbed areas.
- Scattered dark superficial circular colonies on [[chitin-chitosan-fungal-cell-wall]] to haustorium
- Dark brown ascospores: usually 4-5 cells, rarely over 10 um
- No asexual states (unlike sooty molds)
- Uniform hyphae (6-10 um diameter)
- Mycelial setae in Meliola arise laterally, bend perpendicular
## Powdery Mildews (Erysiphales)
Obligate biotrophic Ascomycota producing white powdery growth on
leaf surfaces. Nourished by haustoria within epidermal cells.
Develop characteristic white dusty conidial state on host surface.
- 19-22 genera; approximately 400 species (mostly Erysiphe)
- 93% of hosts are dicotyledons; 634 of 662 monocot hosts are
  Gramineae
- No powdery mildews on gymnosperms, pteridophytes, or lower plants
- Greatest abundance in California and Israel (warm, rain-free
  summers)
- North-temperate regions have far more host species than tropical
  or arctic regions
- Herbaceous hosts (8,250) far exceed tree hosts (2,645)
- Cleistothecia ornamented with hyphal appendages for genus ID
- Host identification essential (many species are host-specific)
## Downy Mildews (Peronosporales + Sclerosporales)
More than 300 species of plant-parasitic Straminipiles (Oomycota,
NOT true fungi). Obligate parasites with intercellular hyphae and
intracellular haustoria. 54 dicot families plus 2 monocot families
serve as hosts.
- Microscopic, erect, treelike sporangiophores through stomata
- White to gray down on affected tissues
- **Sclerosporales** (monocots): Sclerospora, Peronosclerospora,
  Sclerophthora, Verrucalvus
- **Peronosporales** (dicots): Peronospora (260 species), Bremia,
  Plasmopara, Pseudoperonospora, Basidiophora
