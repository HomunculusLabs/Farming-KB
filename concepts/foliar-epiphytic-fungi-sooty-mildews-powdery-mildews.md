# Foliar Epiphytic Fungi — Sooty Molds, Black Mildews, Downy Mildews

The surfaces of living plant leaves, stems, and fruits support diverse communities of fungi that fruit on but do not necessarily penetrate living tissues. These epiphytic and obligately biotrophic fungi range from harmless saprobes to devastating pathogens.

## Sooty Molds

### Description
Saprobic ascomycetes belonging to several families of Dothideales. Form black, often extensive colonies on plant surfaces above ground.

### Ecology
- Obtain nutrients from extracellular plant exudates or insect excretions (honeydew from aphids, scale insects)
- In New Zealand Nothofagus forests, trunks and branches covered up to 9+ meters high
- Some species produce up to three different asexual forms (pleomorphism)

### Morphology
- Mycelium: weft of dark hyphae, sometimes several millimeters thick
- Conidiogenous cells and various conidia formed within the mycelium
- Metacapnodiaceae: distinctive tapering, moniliform hyphae with rigid smooth-walled cells
- Seuratiaceae: dark, gelatinous, lobed thalli with unique reproductive structures

### Growth Forms (Reynolds 1975)
- **Deciduous:** Thin hyphal layer peels away in drier/cooler months; lowland tropics, absent from rain forests; common on citrus in southeastern US
- **Permanent:** Thick, cushionlike black hyphal mats on twigs/stems; neotropical highlands and temperate lowlands

### Collection Technique — Colloidal Leaf Peel
1. Spread 2% collodion/parlodion solution (in diethyl ether or ethanol) on leaf colony
2. Solution dries to transparent film in minutes with embedded fungus
3. Peel film from leaf surface — removes intact colony
4. Place on microscope slide, dissolve collodion with acetone
5. Examine intact colony under compound microscope

### Keys and References
- Luttrell (1973): keys to genera
- Hughes (1976): comprehensive monograph
- Batista and Ciferri (1963): species descriptions
- Reynolds (1971-1985): Neotropical collections

## Black Mildews (Meliolales)

### Description
Obligate plant parasites in the order Meliolales (Ascomycota). >1,580 species, mostly in genus Meliola (>1,400 species).

### Distribution
- Especially abundant in tropics; also Chile to southern US, South Africa to Scotland, Japan to Tasmania
- Most diverse in scrub or open parkland; rare in dense rain forest undergrowth
- Abundant in upper canopy; absent from arid subtropics
- Greatest infections in naturally or anthropogenically disturbed areas
- >90% of hosts are dicotyledonous angiosperms

### Morphology
- Scattered, dark, superficial, circular colonies on leaf surface
- Characteristic branching pattern with short, two-celled lateral branches (capitate hyphopodia)
- Hyphopodia: mucronate or capitate; in opposite, alternate, or mixed arrangements
- Fine hyphal filament penetrates host cell wall to produce haustorium
- Dark brown ascospores: usually 4-5 cells, rarely >10 um long
- **No asexual states** (unlike sooty molds)
- Uniform hyphae (6-10 um diameter)
- Mycelial setae in Meliola: arise laterally, bend perpendicular; collect debris

## Powdery Mildews (Erysiphales)

Obligate biotrophic Ascomycota producing white powdery growth on leaf surfaces. Characterized by:
- White to grayish powder on leaves, stems, flowers
- Haustoria within epidermal cells
- Cleistothecia (closed fruiting bodies) as sexual stage
- Distinctive appendage types on cleistothecia (important for identification)
- Among most commonly recognized foliar fungi

## Downy Mildews (Peronosporales + Sclerosporales)

### Description
300+ species of plant-parasitic Straminipiles (Oomycota — NOT true fungi).

### Characteristics
- Microscopic, erect, treelike, determinate sporangiophores through stomata
- White to gray "down" on affected tissues
- Obligate parasites with intercellular hyphae and intracellular haustoria
- 54 dicot families + 2 monocot families as hosts

### Major Groups
- **Sclerosporales** (monocot hosts): Verrucalvus, Sclerophthora, Sclerospora, Peronosclerospora
- **Peronosporales** (dicot hosts): Peronospora (260 species), Bremia, Plasmopara, Pseudoperonospora, Basidiophora

## Fly-Speck Fungi (Microthyriaceae)

- Dark, flattened, shield-shaped (dimidiate-scutate) ascomata on leaf surfaces
- 447 species in 45 genera (Batista 1959)
- Largest genus: Micropeltis (>100 species)
- Mostly tropical and subtropical
- Mycelium entirely superficial with haustoria in epidermal cells
- Some cause necrosis and fruit on dead tissue
