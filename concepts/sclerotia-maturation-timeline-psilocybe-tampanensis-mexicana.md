# Sclerotia Maturation Timeline in *Psilocybe tampanensis* and *P. mexicana*

## Overview

Sclerotia are hardened, condensed masses of densely packed mycelial cells produced by certain fungi
as survival structures. They accumulate carbohydrates (trehalose, glycogen), lipids, and active
alkaloid compounds within thickened cell walls, often surrounded by a melanized rind protecting
against microbial degradation. This nutrient-dense composition allows persistence through drought,
temperature extremes, and nutrient depletion — some species remain viable for years before
germinating when conditions improve. In *Psilocybe*, [[steven-pollock]] in 1977 from near Tampa, Florida. One of the few
psilocybin-containing species to reliably produce sclerotia under cultivation, it is rare in the
wild and primarily maintained through cultivation; its sclerotia are commonly called "Philosopher's
Stones." *P. mexicana* was described by Roger Heim in the 1950s from Mexican specimens — among the
first psilocybin-containing species identified. Used ceremonially by indigenous Mazatec communities,
it produces sclerotia both in nature and under cultivation.

### Phase 2: Sclerotia Initiation (Weeks 3–6)

After full colonization, small hardened nodules (1–3 mm) appear throughout the seed mass as pale,
firm bodies embedded in the mycelial mat, marking the transition to storage structure formation.

### Phase 3: Active Growth and Enlargement (Weeks 6–16)

Sclerotia enlarge progressively from millimeters to several centimeters. The substrate is consumed
as nutrients translocate into developing sclerotia. This is the most critical yield-determining
period — patience directly correlates with final harvest weight.

### Phase 4: Maturation and Plateau (Months 3–4)

Per the source cultivation study, sclerotia continue to enlarge until approximately **4 months
after inoculation**, after which growth diminishes significantly. Mature sclerotia are firm and
dense — golden-brown to amber in *P. tampanensis*, lighter tan in *P. mexicana*.
