# Pig Forage Systems and Range Management

## Overview

Pigs in [[permaculture]] are managed as integrated foraging animals rather than confined factory units. They serve multiple functions: soil cultivation through rooting, pest control in orchards, waste recycling of surplus crops, and eventual meat production. A well-designed pig system combines tree forage, root crops, ground-level greens, and protective fruits into a polycultural range that reduces external inputs and builds soil fertility over time.

## Forage Species Selection

Pig forage is divided into several functional categories, each contributing different nutrients and serving different roles in the system:

### Trees Planted in Protected Areas
In tropical zones, useful species include **mulberry** (*Morus* spp.), **Bunya pine** (*Araucaria bidwillii*), **fig** (*Ficus* spp.), **Inga** (*[[inga-edulis]]*), and various palms such as **Jagua palms** (*Jessenia* spp.), **Buriti palms** (*Mauritia* spp.), **Babassu palms** (*Orbignya* spp.), **Assai palm** (*[[euterpe-oleracea]]*), **oil palm** (*[[elaeis-guineensis]]*), **date palms** (*Phoenix* spp.), and **coconut** (*Cocos nucifera*).

In cooler temperate areas, the palette shifts to **mulberry**, **olive**, **Chilean wine palm** (*Jubaea spectabilis*), and **canary date palm** (*[[phoenix-canariensis]]*).

### Trees Planted Outside Protected Fences
These are larger, more resilient species that can withstand some browsing. In the tropics: **oaks**, **chestnuts**, **chinquapins**, **breadfruit**, **jackfruit**, **Prosopis** spp., **[[chinese-tallow-tree]]**, and **Argania sideroxylon**. In cooler areas: the **white oak group**, **cork oak**, **turkey oak** pods, **honey locust**, **carob** (in dry zones), **chestnut**, **chinquapin**, **rain tree** (*Samanea saman*), and **Albizia dulcis*.

### Root Crops Planted in Rooted-up Pens
**Cattails** (*Typha* spp.), **arrowroot** (*Canna edulis*), **[[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]]**, **comfrey**, **yams**, **taro**, **sweet potato**, **potato**, **cassava**, **elephant-foot yam**, and **Maranta**.

### Greens and Seeds as Understory
**Chicory**, **lucerne** (alfalfa), **comfrey**, **white clover**, **pigeon pea** (*Cajanus cajan*), **Desmodium ovalifolium*, **pumpkin**, **chayote** (choko), **sunflower** (seed heads), and **cowpea**. In cooler climates, add **broad beans** (favas) and various **grasses**.

### Fruits (Protected 3-5 Years if Valuable)
Tropical staples include **papaya**, **mango**, **banana** spp., and **avocado**, plus **guava**, **anona**, **Inga** (in grassland settings), **sapote**, and **canistel**. In temperate zones, **apple**, **pear**, and **plum** are standard; pigs assist orchard health by cleaning fallen fruit and controlling pest larvae in the grass sward.

### Pest Control
**Neem tree oil** applied to rubbing posts controls mites and ectoparasites. **Pyrethrum daisy flowers**, dried and placed in nests or dust-bath areas, also deter mites. **Diatomaceous earth** is effective against lice.

## Stocking Densities and Land Preparation

A practical benchmark is **20 pigs per 4,000 square metres (1 are)**. At this density, pigs will plow the area through rooting and scratching, preparing the ground for planting of comfrey, sunroot (Jerusalem artichoke), lucerne, chicory, and clover. The land then needs a rest period before pigs return.

At larger scale, **100 pigs on 2 hectares** can plow approximately **40 hectares in 18 months**, removing gorse, blackberries, and small shrubs. This cleared land can then be sown, grazed by cattle, and returned to pig rotation — a classic successional use of animal traction replacing mechanical plowing.

## Pen Layout and Site Planning

Allow at least **1 hectare (2.5 acres) per breeding sow**, laid out with:

- **Tree planting areas** outside the pens and in the corners initially
- **Electric fencing** to progressively protect young trees as the system matures
- In the tropics, **banana, papaya, and similar fruits** must remain outside pens because pigs will root them out
- **Root crops** can be planted in vacated pens as pigs are rotated forward

A few pigs on a large range can be managed casually, but commercial pig-raising as an occupation demands very careful planning and siting. It takes **3-5 years** to develop a full complement of forage foods on range. Even then, some high-value fruits (bananas, papayas) must be thrown over the fence, as pigs will destroy the plants to reach them.

## The Pig Tractor Concept

Pigs serve as living plows — the "pig tractor" system eliminates the need for mechanized tractors and simultaneously improves soils. Pigs are moved through a series of pens or fenced areas at optimum densities and intervals. Their rooting breaks compacted soil, incorporates organic matter, and disturbs weed rhizomes. Following pigs with a sowing of deep-rooted forage species creates a self-renewing pasture improvement cycle.

## Rubbing Posts and Animal Welfare

A simple rubbing post can be constructed by tying **hessian (burlap)** to a post and soaking it in non-drying oils and **neem oil** insecticide. For cattle, rubbing posts are typically inverted "U" shapes. These structures allow animals to self-administer parasite control and satisfy natural grooming behaviors.

## Integrated Benefits

A well-designed pig forage system:
- **Eliminates mechanical plowing** through [[mollison-designers-willow-water-and-natural-rooting-propagation]] behavior
- **Recycles surplus crops** that would otherwise become waste
- **Controls pest species** in orchards (fallen fruit, larvae in soil)
- **Builds [[humus-and-soil-organic-matter]]** through manure deposition and root incorporation
- **Produces diverse forage** from multiple strata: canopy, understory, ground cover, and root zone
- **Reduces external feed costs** as the multi-strata forage system matures over 3-5 years
