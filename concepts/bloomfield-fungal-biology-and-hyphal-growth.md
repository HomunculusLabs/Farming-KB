## Fungal Biology and Hyphal Growth

## Overview

Fungi are eukaryotic organisms that flourish by burrowing into solid substances and transforming them into food. They penetrate the toughest leaves, woody tissues, skin, bone, and even granite. [[nicholas-money]]'s "Mr. Bloomfield's Orchard."

## Hyphal Architecture

Hyphae are eukaryotic cells with chromosomes housed in nuclei. The hyphal interior (cytoplasm) contains:

- **Ribosomes**: engines of gene expression that manufacture proteins, many studding membrane-defined pouches called endoplasmic reticulum
- **Mitochondria**: sinuous organelles functioning as furnaces for cellular respiration, capturing energy from food molecules
- **Cytoskeleton**: a web of protein strands that acts as rails for transporting new cellular materials to growing hyphal tips
- **Plasma membrane**: controls entry and release of chemicals through channel/pump proteins; transports ions that electrify the membrane, turning the hypha into a microscopic battery
- **Cell wall**: outer sheath of sugar-based polysaccharides that protects contents from abrasion, screens poisons, and allows the cell to become pressurized by restricting cytoplasmic inflation

## Mycelium Structure
