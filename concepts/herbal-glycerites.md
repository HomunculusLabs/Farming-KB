## Herbal Glycerites

Glycerites are herbal extracts made using vegetable glycerin as the primary solvent. They are alcohol-free alternatives to tinctures, making them suitable for children, individuals in recovery from alcohol addiction, those with liver conditions that contraindicate alcohol consumption, and anyone who prefers to avoid alcohol-based preparations. Glycerin is a sweet, syrupy liquid that is naturally derived from plant fats.

## Properties of Glycerin as a Solvent

Vegetable glycerin (glycerol) is a clear, viscous, sweet-tasting liquid derived from plant oils, typically coconut or palm oil. It is classified as a sugar alcohol and is used widely in food, pharmaceutical, and cosmetic products. As a solvent for herbal extraction, glycerin has both advantages and limitations compared to alcohol and water.

Glycerin effectively extracts mucilage, tannins, some alkaloids, glycosides, and many water-soluble compounds. It is particularly good at extracting and preserving the sweet-tasting, soothing properties of demulcent herbs. However, glycerin is less effective than alcohol at extracting resins, volatile oils, and some alkaloids. It is a poor solvent for lipophilic (fat-soluble) compounds.

Food-grade vegetable glycerin is typically available at 99.5 percent purity. For herbal extraction, it is diluted with distilled water to a working concentration of 60 to 75 percent glycerin. Pure glycerin is too viscous to effectively penetrate plant material. The diluted solution provides better extraction while retaining adequate preservative properties.

Glycerin has a naturally sweet taste that makes it particularly well-suited for children's preparations and for masking the bitter taste of many herbs. This palatability is one of the primary advantages of glycerites over tinctures for pediatric use. The sweetness can also be a disadvantage when extracting already-sweet herbs, as the result may be cloying.

## Preparation Methods

The basic folk method for glycerites follows the same procedure as tincture-making. Fill a [[psilocybin-shelf-life-stability]] than alcohol-based tinctures. While tinctures can last five or more years, glycerites typically maintain potency for one to three years. The lower preservative power of glycerin means that contamination is more likely if the preparation is not made carefully with clean equipment and properly dried herbs.

The viscosity of glycerin makes it more difficult to work with than alcohol. It pours slowly, is sticky, and can be difficult to measure precisely. Straining glycerites through cheesecloth takes longer than straining tinctures due to the syrupy consistency.

Some herbs simply do not extract well into glycerin. For these herbs, a blended menstruum (glycerin-alcohol-water) or an alternative preparation method should be used. Research individual herbs before choosing glycerin as the sole solvent.

## Best Herbs for Glycerites

Certain herbs are particularly well-suited to glycerin extraction due to their water-soluble active constituents and compatibility with glycerin's sweet, soothing properties.

Demulcent herbs such as marshmallow root, slippery elm bark, licorice root, and plantain leaf extract beautifully into glycerin. The mucilaginous compounds are well-preserved and the resulting glycerite is soothing and palatable.

Immune-supporting herbs including echinacea, elderberry, and astragalus work well in glycerites, especially for children. The sweet taste makes these preparations easy to administer during acute illness.
