## Hugelkultur Mounded Garden Beds

Hugelkultur is a method of burying old wood in the ground under a garden bed. It
is a very old strategy that patterns the exact processes of the forest and lends
itself well to land that has been cleared of trees for farming. Hugelkultur
returns massive amounts of organic material to the soil, retains huge quantities
of water for the plants growing in it, and over time breaks down, leaving air
pockets that provide necessary oxygen to plants.

## Wood Selection

Certain types of wood may not be suitable. Treated wood, cedar, and black locust
don"t rot very well, and woods that are naturally toxic to plants like black
walnut are probably not conducive to growing a garden. Fresh wood can be used,
but it will use nitrogen to decompose, robbing it from the soil and locking it
up. Rotten wood won't do this, and if it is very well rotted, it may actually
release nitrogen. Wood that has been laying around for a couple of years is
probably the best material, including any kind of wood material like brush, tree
stumps and roots, and general debris.

## Bed Construction

The most difficult part is moving the wood and arranging it into the desired
shape. Using a tractor makes it much easier and faster. If working with straight
pole logs, a straight bed is easier to make, but with brush, curved beds can be
created. The wood doesn't have to be very high, but over time the whole thing
will settle as air pockets fill with soil and organic material breaks down.
Because of this, the taller your bed is in the beginning the better. The wood
can be a couple of feet tall, and the soil and mulch can be a couple of feet
more. Some have made beds 6 feet tall (1.8 meters).

Hugelkultur beds start out very tall and settle over time.
