# Psychedelic Use in Ancient Greece

The [[eleusinian-mysteries-history-and-structure]] represent the most well-documented potential case, several other Greek religious and cultural practices suggest familiarity with altered states induced by plants and fungi. The [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] provides the pharmacological backdrop for this hypothesis.

Key evidence for psychedelic use at Eleusis:

- The kykeon was prepared from grain grown specifically in the sacred Rarian plain, implying controlled cultivation.
- The priestly families (Eumolpids and Kerykes) maintained hereditary knowledge of the preparation.
- The described experience -- visions, radiant light, terror followed by ecstasy, sense of divine contact -- matches psychedelic phenomenology.
- The secrecy surrounding the rites parallels secrecy around psychoactive plant knowledge in other cultures.

## Comparative Evidence from Mesoamerica

Wasson drew direct comparisons between Eleusis and the Mazatec mushroom velada:

- Both involved fasting before the ceremony.
- Both took place in darkness.
- Both centered on consuming a plant preparation.
- Both produced a long night of collective visionary experience.
- Both formed enduring bonds among participants.
- Both were guarded by hereditary specialists who transmitted preparation knowledge orally.

In both traditions, eggs were prohibited before the ceremony. In Mexico, chocolate broke the fast; at Eleusis, the pelanos (a barley-wheat cake from the Rarian plain) may have served a similar function. The [[psychedelic-plants-and-civilization]] is thus not one of ignorance versus knowledge, but of controlled versus uncontrolled use. The Greek priestly families functioned as custodians of dangerous and sacred botanical knowledge, much as curanderos and shamans do in indigenous traditions studied by modern ethnobotanists. # Mushroom Cults in Ancient Greece

The archaeological and textual evidence for entheogenic mushroom use in the ancient Greek world forms a substantial thread in McKenna's Food of the Gods, centering on the Minoan civilization of Crete, the Dionysian mysteries, and above all the Eleusinian Mysteries. McKenna argues that these traditions represent late, diluted inheritances of an archaic partnership religion centered on mushroom-induced ecstasy.

## Minoan Crete: The Last Partnership Society

Minoan Crete, flourishing from approximately 3000-1450 BCE, represents for McKenna the last fully articulated partnership society in the Western tradition. Its religion centered on the Great Mother Goddess, cattle worship, and ecstatic practices involving indole hallucinogens. The Minoan pantheon was dominated by female deities, and the civilization exhibited none of the militaristic characteristics of later Indo-European cultures.

The Minoan civilization was destroyed by the Theran eruption (~1628 BCE) and subsequent Mycenaean invasion. Refugees from Minoan Crete fled to the Greek mainland, carrying with them religious practices that would eventually crystallize as the Eleusinian Mysteries and the cult of Dionysus. McKenna notes the extensive archaeological connections between Minoan and Eleusinian cult structures, purification rituals, and fertility symbolism.

## Dionysus: The Twice-Born God

Dionysus occupies a peculiar position in the Greek pantheon. A vegetation god, a mad god, and a dying god, Dionysus was associated with orgy, androgyny, and intoxication. His myths contain elements that McKenna identifies as traces of an older, mushroom-centered religion:

- **Twice-born**: Dionysus was born from Semele, consumed by lightning before giving birth. The father (Zeus) then "assumed the role of the mother," completing gestation in his own body. This notion of the "twice-born god" anticipates the Christian resurrection in ways McKenna argues scholarship has not fully explored.

- **Androgyny**: Dionysus's androgynous qualities reflect the Gaian partnership values of the older Minoan culture.

- **Connection to Semele/Gaia**: Semele was accorded her own cult honors as a goddess despite supposedly being mortal. Kretschmer equated Semele with Ge, the Thracian form of Gaia, suggesting her true identity as the Great Mother Goddess.

- **Female-centered cult**: Dionysian rites were "comprehensible only to initiates, usually women." From the patriarchal perspective, Dionysus represented "something wild, ancient, and potentially dangerous."

McKenna notes that "only in the late phase of Greek culture was Dionysus transformed into the god of wine and drunken revelry; the older stratum of material is darker and touched with the bizarre." The Dionysian theme entered Greece from Minoan Crete, bearing "four millennia of incubation within Minoan civilization."

## The Eleusinian Mysteries

The Eleusinian Mysteries, celebrated for nearly two thousand years on the Eleusinian plain near Athens, represent the most important religious institution of classical Greek civilization. The festival occurred each September, connected to the myth of Demeter and Persephone/Kore, and was organized around two events: the Lesser Mysteries in spring and the Great Mysteries at harvest.

The Mysteries commanded universal participation. Cicero noted that people came "from all quarters" and Aristides asked: "Is there a single Greek, a single Barbarian so ignorant, so impious, as not to consider Eleusis as the common temple of the world?" Their power lay in possessing no dogma but rather "certain sacred acts that engendered religious feeling and into which each successive age could project the symbolism it desired."

The connection to Minoan practice was extensively documented: the oldest Telesteria (cult structures) are pre-Hellenic; the name Eleusis suggests pre-Hellenic Crete; cult vessels (kemoi, libation jugs) are common to both traditions; the purifications of Eleusinian cult come from Crete; and the kernel of the mysteries is a fertility cult, also central to Minoan religion.

## Robert Graves and the Mushroom Hypothesis

The first suggestion that a hallucinogenic plant was involved at Eleusis came from the English poet Robert Graves in his 1964 essay "The Two Births of Dionysus." Graves argued that Triptolemus's supposed secret (agriculture) "would have been no news" by the second millennium BCE, since grain had been cultivated at Jericho since ~7000 BCE. He proposed that "Triptolemus's secret seems therefore concerned with hallucinogenic mushrooms" and suggested that "the priesthood at Eleusis had discovered an alternative hallucinogenic mushroom easier to handle than the Amanita muscaria; one that could be baked in sacrificial cakes, shaped like pigs or phalloi, without losing its hallucinogenic powers."

## Wasson and Hofmann's Ergot Theory

The most academically developed hypothesis was proposed by R. Gordon Wasson, Albert Hofmann, and Carl Ruck in 1978. They argued that the Eleusinian kykeon (sacred beverage) was an ergotized beer brewed from barley infected with Claviceps purpurea, a fungus that produces powerful alkaloids capable of causing hallucination (as well as triggering labor onset and vasoconstriction).

The purple traditionally associated with Demeter's robe may signify the distinctive purple of ergot sclerotia. The kykeon's barley base connects directly to the Claviceps life cycle. Wasson's discussion of the scandal of 415 BCE, in which the Athenian noble Alcibiades was accused of profaning the Mysteries, provided additional circumstantial support.

## The Four Stages of Abandonment

McKenna proposes that the loss of the original psilocybin experience followed four distinct stages:

1. **Substitution**: Introduction of other psychoactive plants as substitutes (from equally profound classical hallucinogens down to stimulants like Ephedra and fermented honey)
2. **Symbolic replacement**: Substitution of inactive plants as symbols of the former power (preserving the ritual form without the pharmacological substance)
3. **Purely symbolic religion**: Elimination of plants entirely in favor of esoteric teachings, dogma, rituals, and cosmogonic diagrams (the stage of today's major world religions)
4. **Complete abandonment and repression**: Rediscovery of the mystery interpreted as evil and threatening (the stage of secular scientism and psychedelic prohibition)
