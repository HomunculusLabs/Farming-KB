## Overview

Moving [[mycoremediation-techniques]] from laboratory to field requires careful consideration of [[gadd-fungal-ecology-endophytes]], soil conditions, competition, and engineering. Despite extensive bench-scale success, commercial field applications remain limited to a handful of companies.

## Four-Phase Development Approach

Lamar and White (2001) advocate four phases for field implementation:

1. **Bench-scale treatability studies** — Determine if the target fungus can degrade the contaminant under controlled conditions
2. **On-site pilot testing** — Evaluate performance under real environmental conditions
3. **[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-inoculum-production-best-practices]]** — Generate sufficient [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] for treatment
4. **Full-scale treatment** — Deploy at the contaminated site

## Inoculum Production

A variety of substrates can be employed in inoculum production:
- Wood chips, wheat straw, peat, corncobs, sawdust
- Nutrient-fortified grain-sawdust mixtures
- Bark, rice, annual plant stems, fish oil, alfalfa
- Spent mushroom compost, sugarcane bagasse, coffee pulp
- Cyclodextrins and surfactants

Carbon-to-nitrogen ratio in substrates significantly influences degradative performance. Pelleted fungal inocula coated with alginate, gelatin, agarose, carrageenan, or chitosan (encapsulation) offer advantages over bulk substrate inocula by sustaining viability and providing nutrition for maximum degradation.

## Three Phases of Strategy

1. **Phase 1**: Inoculum preparation techniques and improvements
2. **Phase 2**: Clear technical protocols for final design and associated engineering processes
3. **Phase 3**: Remediation protocols for monitoring, adjustment, continuity, and maintenance

## Key Field Challenges

- **Soil as poor matrix**: Low substrate levels make soil antagonistic to [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]]; [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungal-diversity-patterns-distribution]] decreases with depth
- **Competition**: Native microbial populations compete with introduced fungi; no protocols exist to eliminate this variability
- **[[duggar-mushroom-environmental-factors-temperature-moisture-light]]**: Temperature, pH, aeration, water potential, nitrogen availability, and carbon quality all affect outcomes
- **Limited ecology knowledge**: Despite ~1.5 million estimated fungal species, mycoremediation ecology is known for fewer than 10 species

## Commercial Operations

A few companies employ white-rot fungi for soil bioremediation:
- EarthFax Development Corporation (Utah, USA)
- Gebruder Huber Bodenrecycling (Germany)
