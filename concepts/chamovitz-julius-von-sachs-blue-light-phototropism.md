
# Julius von Sachs and the Discovery of Blue Light Phototropism

In 1864, the German botanist Julius von Sachs made a foundational discovery
that shaped the entire field of plant photobiology: blue light is the primary
colour that induces phototropism in plants. While plants are generally
insensitive to other colours of light in terms of directional bending
responses, blue light triggers the characteristic turning of plant shoots
toward light sources. This finding established that plants do not merely
respond to light as undifferentiated illumination, but rather possess
wavelength-specific photoreceptive systems capable of colour discrimination.

## The Mechanism of Blue Light Phototropism

The phototropic response to blue light involves a complex signalling cascade.
Blue light is absorbed by photoreceptor proteins called phototropins located
primarily in the shoot tip. Upon absorption of blue light photons, these
phototropins undergo a conformational change that activates a downstream
signalling pathway. This pathway ultimately causes differential cell
elongation on the shaded versus illuminated sides of the stem. Cells on the
shaded side elongate more than those on the illuminated side, causing the
plant to bend toward the light source. This asymmetric growth response is
