## HID System Setup Step-by-Step

Setting up a High Intensity Discharge (HID) lighting system properly
is essential for safe and effective indoor [[cannabis-grow-room-setup]]
instructions from Chapter 6. Ensure the room is clean, painted with
flat white reflective paint or covered with reflective material, and
that ventilation is already in place. The room should be fully enclosed
and light-tight.

## Step Two: Position Lamp and Ballast

Both the lamp and ballast radiate significant heat. Position them
carefully:

- Keep them away from plants and flammable walls/ceilings
- If the room has a low ceiling, place a protective non-flammable
  material (metal) between the lamp and ceiling
- An exhaust fan is necessary to [[cervantes-no-reflective-hood-lighting]] causes light to be
reflected unevenly, creating "hot spots" and "cold spots" in the
garden.

## Step Three: Install a Timer

Buy and use a good quality timer to keep the photoperiod consistent. A
decent timer costs $20 to $30 and is worth its weight in buds. Timer
inconsistency causes plant stress and can disrupt flowering.

## Step Four: Find the Proper Electrical Circuit

A 1000W HID lamp draws about 9.5 amperes (amps) on standard 120V
house current. Understanding your home's electrical system is critical
for safety.

### Circuit Basics

- Each fuse or breaker switch controls a circuit in the home
- Fuses/breakers are rated for 15, 20, 25, 30, or 40-amp service
- Circuits are considered overloaded when more than 80% of the amp
  rating is being used
- A 15-amp circuit can safely support one 1000W HID (leaving 5.5 amps
  leeway for power surges)
- A 20-amp or higher circuit can support the HID plus a few low-amp
  appliances

### Finding the Right Circuit

1. Remove the fuse or turn off the breaker switch
2. Test each outlet in the home to see which ones do not work
3. All non-working outlets are on the same circuit
4. Choose a circuit with few or no lights, radios, TVs, or stereos

### Critical Safety Warning

**Never put a larger fuse in the fuse box than it is rated for.** The
fuse is the weakest link in the circuit. A 20-amp fuse in a 15-amp
circuit allows more electricity than the wiring can handle, causing
wires to burn rather than the fuse. An overloaded circuit can cause a
house fire.
