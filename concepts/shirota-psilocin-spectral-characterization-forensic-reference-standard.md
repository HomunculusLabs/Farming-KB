# Psilocin Spectral Characterization as a Forensic Reference Standard

[[psilocin]] (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) requires comprehensive
[[shirota-psilocin-psilocybin-spectroscopic-characterization-forensic-standards]] to serve as a reliable analytical reference
standard. The Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) large-scale synthesis
provided complete spectral datasets for [[psilocin]], enabling forensic
laboratories to identify the compound in seized mushroom material using TLC,
GC, and HPLC methods.

## Why Forensic Standards Are Needed

Mushrooms containing [[psilocin]] and [[psilocybin]] cannot be reliably
identified through morphological and microscopic analysis alone without expert
mycologists. Chromatographic methods including TLC, GC, and HPLC are the
primary identification tools employed by forensic laboratories. These methods
require authenticated reference compounds for comparison. Isolating
[[psilocin]] and [[psilocybin]] directly from [[hplc-analysis-psilocybin-psilocin-mushroom-tissue]] on a gram scale
is impractical because [[psilocin]] readily decomposes and [[psilocybin]] has
high polarity, making [[psilocin-chemical-synthesis]] the preferred route to analytical
standards.

The need for reference standards became urgent in Japan after the regulation
of psilocybin-containing fungi under the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control
Law on June 6, 2002. Forensic laboratories required gram quantities of
authenticated material to validate their [[analytical-methods-psilocybin-psilocin-quantification]] and to serve as
positive controls in the identification of seized specimens.

## Physical Properties

[[psilocin]] produced by the Shirota route is obtained as white crystals from
the lithium aluminum hydride reduction of the [[glyoxalylamide-intermediate-in-tryptamine-synthesis]]. The
compound melts with decomposition at 169 to 174 degrees Celsius, which is
consistent with literature values of 173 to 176 degrees Celsius (dec.)
reported by Hofmann and colleagues in 1959.

The crystalline form is stable enough for [[coleman-post-harvest-handling-and-storage]] as a reference
standard, provided it is protected from moisture, light, and elevated
temperatures that promote oxidative degradation. The decomposition on melting
rather than clean liquefaction is characteristic of many [[tryptamine]]
alkaloids and reflects the thermal lability of the indole ring system.

## Ultraviolet Spectroscopy

The UV spectrum of [[psilocin]] in methanol shows four absorption maxima:

- Lambda max at 222.5 nm (log epsilon 4.55)
- Lambda max at 268.0 nm (log epsilon 3.72)
- Lambda max at 284.5 nm (log epsilon 3.62)
- Lambda max at 294.0 nm (log epsilon 3.58)

These absorption bands are characteristic of the substituted [[indole]]
chromophore with an electron-donating hydroxyl group at the 4-position. The
longest-wavelength band near 294 nm arises from a pi to pi star transition of
the conjugated system. The pattern is consistent across multiple synthetic
preparations and matches values reported in the literature, confirming
structural integrity of the synthesized material.

The UV spectrum is particularly useful for HPLC detection because the strong
absorption at 222.5 nm (log epsilon 4.55) provides high sensitivity. Most HPLC
methods for [[tryptamine]] alkaloid analysis use UV detection at 265 to 270 nm
as a compromise between sensitivity and selectivity, avoiding the solvent
front absorption at shorter wavelengths while maintaining adequate detection
limits for forensic samples.

## Infrared Spectroscopy

The infrared spectrum (KBr pellet) of [[psilocin]] shows the following
significant absorption bands:

- 3285 cm-1: O-H and N-H stretching vibrations
- 2959 cm-1: C-H stretching (aliphatic)
- 2371 cm-1: minor overtone region
- 1620 cm-1: aromatic C=C stretching
- 1588 cm-1: aromatic ring vibration
- 1473 cm-1: aromatic ring mode
- 1345 cm-1: C-N stretching of dimethylamino group
- 1258, 1232 cm-1: C-O stretching of phenolic hydroxyl
- 1044 cm-1: C-N stretching
- 833 cm-1: aromatic C-H out-of-plane bending
- 722 cm-1: aromatic ring deformation

The phenolic O-H stretch at 3285 cm-1 and the C-O stretches at 1258 and 1232
cm-1 confirm the presence of the 4-hydroxy group. The N-H stretch is merged
with the broad O-H band, giving a combined absorption typical of [[indole]]
compounds bearing both N-H and O-H functionalities. The aromatic C=C stretch
at 1620 cm-1 and the ring vibrations at 1588 and 1473 cm-1 provide
confirmation of the [[indole]] aromatic system.

## Proton NMR Spectroscopy

The proton NMR spectrum of [[psilocin]] in CDCl3 at 400 MHz displays the
following signals:

- 7.90 ppm (1H, br s): H-1, indole N-H proton
- 7.05 ppm (1H, d, J = 8.0 Hz): H-6, aromatic proton
- 6.86 ppm (1H, dd, J = 0.8, 8.0 Hz): H-7, aromatic proton
- 6.84 ppm (1H, d, J = 2.4 Hz): H-2, aromatic proton
- 6.56 ppm (1H, dd, J = 0.8, 8.0 Hz): H-5, aromatic proton
- 2.94 ppm (2H, m): H2-1 prime, alpha-methylene of ethylamine side chain
- 2.70 ppm (2H, m): H2-2 prime, beta-methylene of ethylamine side chain
- 2.38 ppm (6H, s): N,N-dimethyl protons

The aromatic pattern confirms the 4-hydroxyindole substitution, with the
characteristic downfield shift of H-2 due to proximity to the nitrogen atom of
the [[indole]] ring. The meta-coupled doublet at 6.84 ppm (J = 2.4 Hz) for H-2
is diagnostic for the unsubstituted position adjacent to the ring nitrogen.
The ortho-coupled doublet of doublets for H-5 and H-7, combined with the
doublet for H-6, completes the ABCD spin system of the benzene portion of the
[[indole]] ring.

The N,N-dimethyl singlet at 2.38 ppm integrates for six protons, confirming
that both methyl groups are equivalent on the NMR timescale. The side chain
methylene protons at 2.94 and 2.70 ppm appear as multiplets due to coupling
with adjacent protons and the adjacent chiral center (when in a chiral
environment).

## Carbon-13 NMR Spectroscopy

The carbon-13 NMR spectrum in CDCl3 at 100 MHz confirms the expected 12-carbon
framework:

- 152.1 ppm (C-4): phenolic carbon bearing hydroxyl group
- 139.0 ppm (C-7a): indole ring junction
- 123.5 ppm (C-6): aromatic CH
- 120.8 ppm (C-2): aromatic CH, adjacent to indole nitrogen
- 117.5 ppm (C-3a): indole ring junction
- 114.6 ppm (C-3): quaternary carbon of pyrrole ring
- 106.4 ppm (C-5): aromatic CH, ortho to hydroxyl
- 102.4 ppm (C-7): aromatic CH
- 61.6 ppm (C-2 prime): beta-methylene of ethylamine side chain
- 45.3 ppm (NCH3): N,N-dimethyl carbons
- 25.1 ppm (C-1 prime): alpha-methylene of ethylamine side chain

The highly deshielded C-4 at 152.1 ppm is diagnostic for the 4-hydroxy
substitution pattern on the [[indole]] ring. This value is characteristic of a
carbon bearing a phenolic hydroxyl group in an aromatic system and
distinguishes the 4-hydroxy substitution from the 5-hydroxy pattern found in
serotonin (which shows the deshielded carbon at a different position). The
beta-methylene of the side chain (C-2 prime) at 61.6 ppm is shifted downfield
relative to typical aliphatic methylenes due to the electron-withdrawing
effect of the adjacent nitrogen atom.
