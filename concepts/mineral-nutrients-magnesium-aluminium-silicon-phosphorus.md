# Mineral Nutrients — Magnesium, Aluminium, Silicon, and Phosphorus

## Overview

These four minerals play critical but often overlooked roles in soil chemistry, plant nutrition, and broader ecosystem health. Magnesium and phosphorus are essential plant nutrients; [[permaculture]] soil design.

## Magnesium (Mg)

Magnesium is found with calcium in **[[humus]]
- **Supplied by** dolomite application on farmlands, dolomite tablets for animals, and mixed in animal foodstuffs

### pH Buffering

Magnesium carbonate from dolomite buffers plant and soil acids, similar to lime, but it can cause plant poisoning if not balanced by high lime (calcium) levels. This is why dolomite should not be over-applied on clay soils that already contain adequate magnesium reserves.

### Human Health

- Essential for chlorophyll and in the mitochondria of human cells, hence energy metabolism
- Present in all green plants
- Deficiencies do not normally occur where people are healthy and not subject to diarrhoea
- When required medically, it is given as an intravenous fluid at 10 millimoles per kg bodyweight

## Aluminium (Al)

Aluminium is a "Jekyll and Hyde" element — it forms a large part of soils and red clays (with iron) or as poorer bauxitic dryland deposits, yet becomes poisonous under certain conditions.

### Toxicity Conditions

- **Aluminium sulphate** is formed in acid rain or sulphur-polluted soils (e.g., industrial areas)
- **Cooking** onions or other acidic/sulphurous substances in aluminium cookware releases toxic aluminium
- **Health risk**: Heavy aluminium silica uptake in humans may assist development of senile dementia; calcium in soil and as tablets provides partial defence
- **Soil tolerance**: Soluble aluminium at 0.1–1.0 ppm is tolerable; above this, susceptible plants start to die. At 15 ppm or higher, many plants die

### Permaculture Recommendations

- Use iron, enamel, stone, or steel cookware — avoid aluminium utensils, especially when cooking salty, acid, or sulphurous substances (vinegar, onions)
- Maintain adequate calcium levels in all gardens, and even in rainwater tanks (as marble or limestone chips, whole sea shells, gravels of lime)
- Some areas are naturally high in acid-aluminium soils, making calcium amendment especially important
