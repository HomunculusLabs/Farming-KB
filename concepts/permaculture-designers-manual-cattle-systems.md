## Shelter Effects on Cattle
Trees and shelterbelts provide significant benefits for cattle:
- **Increased carcass weight** due to increased food intake in sheltered
  conditions
- On hot days, cattle graze all day when on shaded pasture, instead of
  sheltering from the sun
- **Increased crop production** in sheltered areas
- Lack of shelter can reduce weight gains by 16-30%
## Cattle in Orchards
Cattle are the last large animal introduced into mature orchard systems
(Section 12.9):
- Years 0-3: Only poultry on range
- Years 3-7: Pigs for windfall fruit control
- Years 7-20: First sheep, then cattle at reduced stocking rates
Cattle in mature orchards provide manure, control understory grasses, and
return income from grazing while the orchard produces its primary crop.
Stocking rates must be conservative to prevent soil compaction around tree
roots and avoid damage to lower branches of fruit trees.
## Multi-Species Grazing Systems
Mollison advocates multi-species grazing as a strategy to maximise range
utilisation while minimising environmental damage. Different livestock species
graze different plants and at different heights, creating a more uniform
utilisation of the available forage. Cattle prefer tall grasses, sheep graze
closer to the ground, and goats browse shrubs and woody plants. By combining
species in a rotational system, the total [[fukuoka-factory-livestock-critique-natural-grazing-philosophy]] patterns where herd animals graze
an area intensively then move on, allowing plants to recover fully before
being grazed again. Paddock size should allow stock to be moved in 15-30
minutes, with rest periods of 60-90 days depending on season and growth
rates. Permanent fencing uses [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]] in addition to their boundary
function. Gate placement should enable any combination of paddocks without
forcing stock through other occupied paddocks.
## Health Management in Permaculture Systems
Mollison's approach to cattle health emphasises prevention over treatment:
- Diverse pastures provide balanced nutrition, reducing metabolic disorders
- Multi-species grazing breaks parasite cycles naturally
- Access to mineral-rich soils and diverse plant species reduces mineral
  deficiencies
- Clean water from protected sources prevents waterborne disease
- Low stocking densities reduce stress and disease transmission
- Shade and shelter reduce heat and cold stress
- Fresh calving on clean pasture minimises exposure to pathogens
When treatment is necessary, Mollison prefers herbal and homeopathic
remedies that can be produced on-farm, reducing dependence on external
veterinary supplies. However, he acknowledges that conventional veterinary
care may be needed for acute conditions and emergencies.
