## Tools and Supplies for Winter Harvest Farming

Winter harvest farming on a small commercial scale requires a specific set of tools and supplies that differ significantly from conventional field agriculture. Eliot Coleman"s recommended equipment list reflects decades of refinement for the unique demands of year-round [[coleman-french-historical-winter-vegetable-production]] [[query-how-do-i-grow-figs-in-cold-climates]], including specialized seeding, harvesting, cultivation, and protection materials.

## Essential Hand Tools and Equipment

### Seeding Tools
- **Six-row seeder:** A precision seeder capable of planting six rows simultaneously, essential for efficiently establishing dense plantings of salad greens and other winter crops
- **Soil block makers:** For starting transplants without plastic pots. Soil blocks eliminate transplant shock and produce stronger root systems. Multiple sizes accommodate different crop stages

### Harvesting Tools
- **Greens harvesters:** Specialized cutting tools for efficiently harvesting salad mixes and leafy greens at scale
- **Victorinox harvest knives:** High-quality Swiss knives used for clean, precise cutting of individual heads and bunches
- **30-inch-wide Austrian grading rake:** For cleaning and preparing harvested root vegetables

### Cultivation Tools
- **Broadfork:** A deep soil-loosening tool used to aerate beds without inverting the soil profile. Essential for maintaining soil structure in permanent bed systems
- **Tilther:** A specialized shallow tilling tool that incorporates compost and amendments into the top few inches of soil without disturbing deeper soil structure
- **Collinear hoe:** An efficient weeding hoe with a blade that moves parallel to the soil surface, minimizing soil disturbance
- **Wire weeder:** A precision weeding tool that slides just below the soil surface to sever weed roots without disturbing crop roots
- **Three-tooth cultivator:** For cultivating between rows and around established plants
- **Right-angle trowel:** A versatile [[solomon-planting-and-transplanting-guide]] tool

### Pest Management
- **Propane flame weeder:** For pre-emergent weed control, particularly effective along bed edges and pathways
- **Vole traps ("The Better Mouse Trap" from Intruder Inc.):** Essential for protecting winter crops from rodent damage, which can be severe under snow cover

### Trellising and Support
- **Tomato clips and twine:** For training indeterminate tomatoes in the greenhouse during summer months
- **Wire wickets:** Made from 76-inch lengths of number 9 wire (Ken-Bar Inc.), used to support row covers and create low tunnels over crops

### Scythe
- **European-style scythe with folding blade:** For efficient management of cover crops, grass pathways, and weed borders. The folding blade allows safe storage

## Protection Materials

### Row Covers
- **Agribon row covers:** Used for both greenhouse inner layers and covering quick hoops. Available in multiple weights for different levels of frost protection
- **Covertan Pro 17 (Suntex CP, Inc.):** An alternative row cover fabric with proven performance in winter growing systems

### Ground Anchors
- **45-inch-long, 4,000-pound-rated ground anchors:** From A.M. Leonard, used to secure mobile greenhouses against wind. These heavy-duty anchors are critical for the safety and stability of moveable structures

### Packaging Materials
- **Pure cellophane box liners and bags:** From ATLAPAC. Must be ordered ahead in sufficient quantity to justify special sizes. Cellophane is preferred over plastic for environmental and food-quality reasons

## Mobile Greenhouse Systems

### Rimol Greenhouse Systems — "Rolling Thunder"
Coleman collaborated with Rimol on the design of this mobile greenhouse system:
- Heavy-duty wheels with bearings at each hoop set, attached to specially designed base posts
- Wheel/base-post combination seats on a rail system for smooth movement along growing areas
- A 30×96-foot greenhouse can be moved with an ordinary tractor; smaller houses need only two people
- Rail is laid flat according to greenhouse width and set on a solid base to prevent sinking
- First few hoops are the trickiest (wheels, base posts, and hoops erected simultaneously), but once braced, assembly proceeds quickly
- Significant internal bracing prevents wind movement; temporary end wall bracing ensures stability during construction
- Various height options available through different length base posts

### Four Season Tools
- Mobile greenhouse incorporating drop-down side technology on both sides
- Full-length roof opening for ventilation
- Available in wheeled (two options) or sled models
- Conversion kits available to make existing stationary greenhouses mobile
- Also designs efficient small farm tools

## Potting and Soil Products
- **Vermont Compost products:** Full line of potting and soil blocking mixes, formulated for organic production
- These professionally formulated mixes save time and ensure consistent transplant quality

## Predator Urine Pellets
- **Granular pellets soaked in predator urine** (from critter-repellent.com): Used as a vole deterrent around field edges and greenhouse perimeters

## Quick Hoop Conduit Bender
A quick-hoops conduit bender for bending metal conduit into hoop shapes was under development at the time of publication. Quick hoops are an affordable alternative to full greenhouses, consisting of metal conduit bent into arches and covered with row cover fabric or plastic.

## Principles Behind the Tool Selection

Coleman's tool recommendations reflect several core principles:
- **Efficiency at small scale:** Tools designed for [[intensive-planting-spacing]] on permanent beds, not large-field agriculture
- **Soil preservation:** Tools like the broadfork and tilther that maintain soil structure rather than destroying it
- **Durability:** Professional-grade tools that justify their cost through years of reliable service
- **[[appropriate-technology]]:** Human-scale tools that don't require large tractors or heavy machinery
- **Versatility:** Many tools serve multiple purposes across different seasons and crop types

The total investment in specialized tools for a winter harvest operation is modest compared to the revenue potential of year-round fresh vegetable production, making this system accessible to small-scale growers.
