# Biosafety Classification and Shipping of Fungal Cultures

## Overview

The handling, transport, and distribution of fungal cultures are governed by a complex framework of national and international regulations. These rules exist to protect human health, agriculture, and the environment from the accidental introduction of potentially hazardous organisms. Understanding biosafety classifications and shipping requirements is essential for mycologists, culture collection curators, and anyone working with live fungal material.

## Biosafety Classification Systems

### USPHS Classification (United States)

The U.S. Public Health Service (USPHS 1974) classifies infectious agents into four hazard classes, each with specific packaging and handling requirements:

- **Class I**: No recognized hazards. Special packaging is not required.
- **Class II**: Ordinary potential hazard. Distribution is restricted to bona fide laboratories, and special packaging is required.
- **Class III**: Special hazards. Both special packaging and a letter from the receiving institution acknowledging the hazard are required.
- **Class IV**: Potentially dangerous to human health. A USPHS and/or USDA permit is required for shipping.

### USDA Plant Pathogen Regulations

Plant pathogens are described under APHIS (Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service) 7 CFR 330.100 as organisms that can directly or indirectly injure, cause disease in, or damage any plant or plant part, or any processed products of plants. The USDA regulates all plant pathogens and mycotoxin-producing strains across state and international boundaries.

The permitting process involves:
1. The receiving investigator completes **Form PPQ-526** ("Application and Permit to Move Live Plant Pests")
2. The form is submitted to the plant regulatory official of the recipient's state
3. The state forwards it to the **Plant Protection and Quarantine (PPQ) Program** for approval

### Canadian Risk Groups

Canada assigns microorganisms to four risk groups:
- **Risk Group 1**: Low individual and community risk
- **Risk Group 2**: Moderate individual risk, low community risk
- **Risk Group 3**: High individual risk, low community risk
- **Risk Group 4**: High individual and community risk

Each risk group corresponds to a containment level. Containment level 1 represents a basic microbiology laboratory, while containment level 4 represents a geographically isolated, functionally independent unit. Since September 1994, all human pathogens (Risk Groups II–IV) require a Health Canada permit. Organisms that are both human and animal pathogens require permits from both Health Canada and Agriculture Canada.

### European Federation of Biotechnology (EFB) Classes

The EFB Working Party on Safety in Biotechnology classifies organisms as:
- **EFB Risk Class 1**: Harmless
- **EFB Risk Class 2**: Low risk — may cause disease and be a hazard to laboratory workers but unlikely to spread in the environment
- **EFB Risk Class 3**: Medium risk — severely threatens laboratory worker health but comparatively small risk to the population
- **EFB Risk Class 4**: High risk — causes severe illnesses

Classes 2–4 fall into special containment categories.

## Domestic Shipping (United States)

### Postal Service Requirements

Within the United States, noninfectious, perishable biological organisms can generally be shipped via the USPS or private freight carriers once required permits are obtained. Freeze-dried cultures, agar slants, and broth cultures are all acceptable.

Etiological agents shipped domestically via the USPS must be sent as:
- First-class mail
- Priority mail
- Express mail

Exceptions are agents listed in 42 CFR 72.3(f), which must be sent by registered mail or equivalent. A **24-hour emergency number** must be provided for rapid response to incidents involving hazardous materials.

### Packaging Requirements (42 CFR Part 72)

All etiological agents must be packaged according to USPHS guidelines:

1. **Primary container**: Must be securely sealed and watertight (e.g., test tube, vial)
2. **Secondary container**: Durable and watertight, enclosing the primary container(s)
3. **Absorbent material**: Placed between primary and secondary containers to absorb contents if the primary container breaks
4. **Outer shipping container**: Corrugated fiberboard, cardboard, wood, or similar material

Domestic and international shipments of etiological agents are limited to **50 ml** per package. Nonpathogenic cultures are limited to 1000 ml per primary container and 4000 ml per outer container.

### Cold Chain Shipping

- **Dry ice**: Must be placed outside the secondary container so it doesn't become loose as the ice sublimes. Outer containers must be vented to allow CO₂ escape. Dry ice cannot be sent by international mail.
- **Wet ice**: Placed between secondary and outer containers. The outer container must be designed to not collapse after ice melts, and the entire package must be leak-proof.
- **Liquid nitrogen**: Containers must withstand ultra-low temperatures and follow DOT regulations in 49 CFR 173.316.

## International Shipping

### Regulatory Bodies

Multiple international organizations govern the transport of biological materials between countries:

- **Universal Postal Union (UPU)**: Postal regulations
- **International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO)**: Air transport standards
- **International Maritime Organization (IMO)**: Sea transport standards
- **International Air Transport Association (IATA)**: Publishes air-transport procedures in agreement with ICAO
- **UN Economic and Social Council**: Provides recommendations enforced by ICAO and IMO

### U.S. Export Requirements

The Department of Commerce (DOC) regulates biological material exports through the Bureau of Export Administration. Organisms appearing under Export Control Classification Number 1C61B require a validated export license for all foreign destinations except Canada. Materials not requiring a validated license may be shipped under a **general license (G-DEST)**.

All foreign shipments of biological materials require a **Shipper's Export Declaration (DOC Form 7525-V)**, verifying either a validated export license or a general license.

### Required Labels for International Shipments

- **IATA infectious substance label**
- **Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods**
- **UN 2814 Class 6.2 shipment** designation (specifying volume, agent name, and responsible party contact)
- **PS 2976 green customs label** (for USPS air mail volumes exceeding 50 ml)
- **"Cargo Aircraft Only" label** (for volumes exceeding 50 ml)
- **IATA Miscellaneous 9 label and UN 1845** (for dry ice shipments exceeding 2.3 kg)
- **"Keep Upright" arrows** at 120° intervals
- **"Nitrogen, Refrigerated Liquid, Class 2.2 UN 1977"** (for liquid nitrogen shipments)

## Import Considerations

Foreign countries have their own import requirements:
- **Australia, New Zealand, Germany**: Require import permits and licenses
- **Canada**: Requires annual import permits for all plant and animal pathogens; extremely hazardous agents require a Canadian Department of Transport Emergency Response Assistance Plan
- Growing cultures (unacceptable for mailing to certain countries) must be sent by freight carrier
- Currently, only **Federal Express** carries packages labeled "infectious substances"
