## Straw Substrate Preparation with Yeast Extract for Mushroom Cultivation

## Overview

The Jarrold "Boil-a-Bag" technique uses chopped wheat straw supplemented with
yeast extract (Marmite/Vegemite) and brown rice flour as the primary substrate
for mushroom cultivation. This combines the structural and nutritional
properties of straw with B-vitamin enrichment from yeast extract to create an
affordable, effective growing medium for saprotrophic mushroom species. The
method is designed for home cultivators using minimal specialized equipment.

## Why Straw Works as a Substrate

Pack the prepared straw into heat-resistant polypropylene bags (e.g., Lakeland
Plastics boil-a-bags, 20 × 30 cm) with integrated filter can assemblies.
Firmly pack until almost full, leaving room for the filter can. Sterilize at
**15 psi for 60 minutes** in a pressure cooker. This can be done in two
30-minute cycles if preferred. After sterilization, bags must cool completely
(overnight is safest) before inoculation.

### Pasteurization Alternative
For larger-scale operations, straw substrates are more commonly pasteurized
(70-80°C for 1-2 hours in a hot water bath) rather than sterilized.
Pasteurization kills most competitor spores and bacteria while preserving
beneficial thermotolerant microorganisms that can help defend the substrate.

## Applicable Mushroom Species

The straw/yeast extract substrate works well for:

- **Oyster mushrooms** (*Pleurotus ostreatus* and related species): The
  classic straw-loving species; aggressive colonizer with high yield potential
- **[[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]]**: Can fruit on straw-based substrates

## Common Problems

- **Substrate too wet**: Spread and drain longer. Anaerobic zones promote
  bacterial growth and stall mycelial colonization.
- **Substrate too dry**: Mist with sterile water before packing into bags.
  Mycelium cannot grow through dry substrate.
- **Uneven colonization**: Ensure thorough mixing of yeast extract and rice
  flour into straw. Pockets of unsupplemented straw colonize much more slowly.
