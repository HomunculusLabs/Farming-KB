# DOM

## Overview

DOM (2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylamphetamine), historically known by the street name STP, is one of the most historically significant substituted amphetamines. Originally synthesized and studied by Alexander Shulgin, DOM became widely known in 1967 when it was distributed in massive doses throughout San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury district during the Summer of Love, causing numerous overdose emergencies and contributing to the scheduling of the entire class of substituted amphetamines.

DOM is a [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] and the founding member of the DOx family of 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted amphetamines. Its effects include intense visual enhancement, emotional depth, and cognitive alteration, lasting 14-20 hours at doses of 3-10 mg. The compound has been described as having "the glory and the doom sealed up in it."

## Synthesis (Summary)

DOM is synthesized from 2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylbenzaldehyde (prepared as described under the 2C-D entry). Condensation with nitroethane and ammonium acetate in glacial acetic acid (steam bath, 3 hours) yields 1-(2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylphenyl)-2-nitropropene as yellow crystals (mp 87-88 degrees C).

Reduction via LAH in anhydrous ether under inert atmosphere (Soxhlet extraction, 4 hours reflux plus overnight stirring) gives the free base, which crystallizes spontaneously (mp 60.5-61 degrees C from hexane). The hydrochloride salt forms glistening white crystals (mp 190.5-191.5 degrees C).

An alternative route proceeds through the phenylacetone intermediate: iron/acetic acid reduction of the nitrostyrene, followed by reductive amination with ammonium acetate and sodium cyanoborohydride over molecular sieves.

Optical isomers have been prepared by resolution of the racemate as the ortho-nitrotartranilic acid salt (the (+) acid preferentially provides the (+) or S-isomer), and by reductive amination of the phenylacetone with optically active alpha-methylbenzylamine followed by hydrogenolysis. Either isomer has mp 204-205 degrees C as the hydrochloride.

## Dosage and Duration

| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| **Threshold** | ~1.0 mg |
| **Light dose** | 3 - 5 mg |
| **Common dose** | 5 - 8 mg |
| **Strong dose** | 8 - 10 mg |
| **Heavy dose** | 10 - 15 mg (dangerous) |
| **Duration** | 14 - 20 hours |
| **Onset** | 0.5 - 2 hours (slow) |

**Isomer activity:** The R-isomer is the more active. The S-isomer shows no psychological effects at up to 2.6 mg, though it produces physical signs (tachycardia, blood pressure increase, teeth-rubbiness). Shulgin speculated that some of the heavier physical aspects of the racemate might be due to the S-isomer, noting that the S-isomer of amphetamine itself carries the major stimulant punch.

## Qualitative Commentary (from PIHKAL)

**1.0 mg:** Slight dryness of mouth, noticeable mydriasis, eerie feeling overall.

**2.3 mg:** Mood elevation at 2-3 hours with emotional effects becoming more pronounced. Considerable color enhancement with very little perceptual distortion. Emotional content and empathy closer to mescaline than to amphetamine. No nausea. Unable to sleep at 10 hours.

**3.0 mg:** Ability to separate components of complex things for independent evaluation. Enhanced sharpness of observation with focus at different depths. "Colors are not just brighter; there are more of them." Profound meaning inherent in anything that moves. Deeply philosophical and productive introspective experience.

**4.0 mg:** First four hours directed to the body (shuddering, tight jaw, minimal sociability). After initial physical discomfort and mild nausea, a dramatic shift: "I saw the clouds towards the west. THE CLOUDS!!! No visual experience has ever been like this. The meaning of color has just changed completely." Ranked above LSD, mescaline, cannabis, and peyote by the subject.

**5.0 mg:** Magnification of light, color, and odors. Alternation between enjoyable hilarity and overwhelming negative feeling attributed to anger (approximately 50-50). As evening progressed, became withdrawn and pensive with existential questioning including doubt about meditation practice. "I felt I knew what it was like to look across the brink to insanity."

**8.0 mg:** Quiet onset picking up speed between first and second hour. Rich curly-imaged eyes-closed show interlocking closely with music. Continuous thirst with minimal urine. Rolling +++ throughout. Sleep possible at 16 hours, real sleep at 20 hours.

**10.0 mg:** "DOM has the glory and the doom sealed up in it. All that's needed to unseal it is to surround it with a warm living human for a few hours."

**12.0 mg:** Rapid onset "more like LSD." Body tremor felt "like poisoning" during transition, clearing at the second hour. Music, erotic, and fantasy all "exceptional." Liszt's Christmas Cantata with eyes closed was "an experience without precedent."

## The STP Incident

DOM was scattered widely throughout Haight-Ashbury in mid-1967 under the name STP. Initial tablets contained 20 mg (later reduced to 10 mg), both of which Shulgin retrospectively characterized as "thoroughly whopping doses." The overdose crisis was compounded by DOM's slow onset: familiar with LSD's 15-20 minute alert, users assumed STP was weak when little was felt at 30 minutes and took additional doses. Some victims may have consumed 30 mg total. The slow onset coupled with the long duration caught many unprepared.

The name STP was said to stand for "Serenity, Tranquility, and Peace," "Super Terrific Psychedelic," or "Stop The Police." The police called it "Too Stupid to Puke." Actually, the name came from a motor oil additive with the same initials.

## Tolerance

Clinical studies documented rapid tolerance development. Five volunteers given 6 mg daily for three days all found extremely intense effects on day one (all but one found it unpleasant). By day three, responses ranged from "moderately strong" to "felt absolutely nothing." One subject slept through the experience on the third day.

## Comparisons

- vs. [[dob-pihkal-62]]: DOB is approximately 3x more potent (1-3 mg vs. 3-10 mg) and longer-acting (18-30h vs. 14-20h). DOB has been more frequently associated with overdose deaths.
- vs. [[doi-pihkal-67]]: DOI is similarly more potent and longer-lasting. Both are described as "clean" psychedelics with excellent eyes-closed imagery.
- vs. [[desert-gardening]]: DOET is the ethyl homologue, somewhat longer-acting and less overtly psychedelic at lower doses. DOET has a reputation as a cognitive enhancer at modest doses.
- vs. [[mescaline-ethnobotany]]: At 2.3 mg, DOM's emotional content and empathy were described as "closer to mescaline than to amphetamine."
- vs. [[doet-pihkal-66]]: DOM has a much slower onset and longer duration. One subject ranked DOM's visual effects above all previous experiences including LSD.

The hexadeutero-analogue of DOM (deuterium atoms on the two methoxyl groups) has been prepared as an internal standard for analytical work. A difference from mescaline is noted: with mescaline, loss of a methoxyl group is believed to be a step toward inactivation, whereas with DOM this loss may be associated with formation of an active metabolite. The questions raised by differences in both the rates and degree of demethylation between these compounds remain open.

Positional isomers of DOM have been studied at UCSF. The 2,4-dimethoxy-5-methyl isomer (F-310 or 5-DOM) is inactive at up to 20 mg in humans, making it several times less potent than DOM. The 4,5-dimethoxy-2-methyl isomer (F-320 or 2-DOM) is untested in humans. These names were derived from motor oil additive codes, continuing the STP naming tradition.

## Harm Reduction

- DOM's slow onset is a major overdose risk -- effects may not peak for 2+ hours
- Never redose based on initial lack of effects
- The STP incident demonstrated the danger of distributing high-dose tablets to users unfamiliar with the compound
- Rapid tolerance develops -- daily use becomes ineffective within 3 days
- At higher doses (5+ mg), DOM can produce deeply challenging psychological experiences including existential crisis
- Duration of 14-20 hours requires a full day of commitment
- See [[psychedelic-harm-reduction]] and [[harm-reduction-guide]] for general principles
