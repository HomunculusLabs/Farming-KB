# Natural Livestock Integration Philosophy

updated: 2026-04-18

Fukuoka's vision for livestock farming stands in stark opposition to modern concentrated animal feeding operations. He argued that truly economical livestock production requires animals that can feed and fend for themselves under the open sky.

## Energy Waste of Modern Livestock

The caloric inefficiency of modern livestock farming makes it destructive:

- **Broilers**: 50% [[fukuoka-textdoc-energy-efficiency-agricultural-labor]] (output vs input)
- **Pork**: 20% efficiency
- **Milk**: 15% efficiency
- **Beef**: 8% efficiency

Raising beef cattle cuts recoverable food energy from land tenfold. People who eat beef consume ten times as much energy as people on a diet of rice. Silver foxes require ten times their weight in rabbit meat; rabbits require ten times their weight in grass.

## The Japanese Bantam Example

The Japanese Bantam, a native breed, lays just one small egg every other day — apparently low productivity. But a breeding pair allowed to nest naturally hatches a clutch of chicks. Within a year, the original pair grows to a flock of ten or twenty birds producing many times as many eggs daily as the best White Leghorn variety. The Bantams are highly efficient because they feed themselves and "produce something from nothing."

Genetically-upgraded White Leghorns in cages require feed grain with twice the caloric value of the eggs produced. Such methods actually cut the number of food calories in half.

## Land Requirements by Diet

- **200 sq yards** per person on grains
- **600 sq yards** for potatoes
- **1,500 sq yards** for milk
- **4,000 sq yards** for pork
- **10,000 sq yards** for beef

If the entire human population depended on beef alone, humanity would have already reached its growth limits. On a grain diet, the earth could support sixty times the current population.

## Which Animals to Raise

Fukuoka recommended selecting breeds that can graze mountain pastures freely:

- **Native breeds** (Jersey cattle, black Berkshire pigs) are more feed-efficient than supposedly superior improved breeds
- **Small goats** are better than dairy cattle for countries like Japan — the "poor man's cattle" that takes care of itself
- **Deer, boars, rabbits, chickens, wildfowl**, and even edible rodents would be more economical and better protect nature than goats

Raising large numbers of genetically improved Holstein cows and beef cattle in indoor pens on concentrated feed is highly risky and yields the highest rates of energy loss.

## Fukuoka's Vision of the Natural Farm

In his idealized vision of livestock farming, Fukuoka described:

> Bees busily making the rounds of clover and vegetable blossoms thickly flowering beneath trees laden heavy with fruit; semi-wild chickens and rabbits frolicking with dogs in fields of growing wheat, and great numbers of ducks and mallards playing in the [[fukuoka-textdoc-creating-rice-paddy-natural-methods]]; at the foot of the hills and in the valleys, black pigs and boars grow fat on worms and crayfish, and from time to time goats peer out from the thickets and trees.

This scene represents an organic partnership between man, animal, and nature — an environment comfortable for small animals is also an ideal setting for man.

## Chickens in the Orchard

On Fukuoka's own farm, chickens run free through the [[fukuoka-orchard-soil-building-without-fertilizers]] using full three-dimensional space can include:
- Forage crops and semi-wild vegetables grown in abundance
- Poultry and livestock allowed grazing freely
- Bees for pollination and honey
- Ducks released onto rice paddies to weed, pick off insects, and turn soil

By making full three-dimensional use of the field, one can simultaneously produce good protein foods alongside grains and fruit.

## The True Cost of Large Animals

People once believed using horses and oxen would lighten human labor. But farmers would have been better off using pigs and goats to turn the soil. It takes over two acres of pasture to feed just one horse or cow — land that could feed fifty to one hundred people using [[natural-farming-livestock-integration]]
