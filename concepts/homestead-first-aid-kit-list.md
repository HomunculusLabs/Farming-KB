# Homestead First Aid Kit List

Homesteads are environments where injuries happen frequently — tools, machinery, animals, weather, and distance from medical facilities all increase risk. A well-stocked first aid kit is essential infrastructure. This page provides a comprehensive supply list organized by category, with veterinary additions.

## Kit Organization

Use a waterproof, impact-resistant container with clear compartments. Consider multiple kits:
- **Main kit:** Comprehensive, stays in a designated, accessible location
- **Field kit:** Compact, goes with you to pastures, woods, and remote work areas
- **Vehicle kit:** In every truck, tractor, and ATV
- **Barn kit:** Near animal handling areas

Label everything clearly and check expiration dates every 6 months.

## Wound Care

### Cleaning and Disinfection
- Chlorhexidine solution (2%) or Betadine for wound irrigation
- Irrigation syringe (60 mL) with catheter tip for pressure washing wounds
- Sterile saline solution (500 mL bottles, 2-4)
- Alcohol prep pads (50 count)
- Wound irrigation guide — pressure, volume, technique

### Dressings and Bandages
- Adhesive bandages (assorted sizes, 100 count)
- Sterile gauze pads — 2x2, 3x3, and 4x4 (50 each)
- Non-adherent pads (Telfa) — 3x4 and 4x4 (25 each)
- Rolled gauze — 2-inch and 4-inch widths (5 rolls each)
- Elastic bandage (Ace wrap) — 3-inch and 4-inch (3 each)
- Adhesive tape — 1-inch cloth tape and 2-inch silk tape
- Butterfly closures (Steri-Strips) — assorted sizes
- Tincture of benzoin for adhesive enhancement
- Hemostatic gauze (QuikClot or Celox) — 2 packs for severe bleeding
- Combat application tourniquet (CAT) — 1 per kit minimum
- Israeli bandage (emergency trauma dressing) — 1-2

### Suture and Wound Closure
- Skin closure strips and sterile sutures (for trained users only)
- Skin stapler with remover (for trained users only)
- Sterile gloves (nitrile, non-latex) — boxes of 50

## Medications

### Oral Medications
- Ibuprofen (200 mg) — anti-inflammatory, pain relief
- Acetaminophen (500 mg) — pain relief, fever reduction
- Aspirin (325 mg) — pain relief, potential cardiac emergency use
- Diphenhydramine (Benadryl, 25 mg) — allergic reactions
- Loperamide (Imodium) — diarrhea
- Oral rehydration salts (ORS packets) — for dehydration

### Topical Medications
- Triple antibiotic ointment
- Hydrocortisone cream (1%) — insect bites, rashes
- Clotrimazole cream (1%) — fungal infections
- Burn gel (Water-Jel) — for thermal burns
- Calamine lotion — poison ivy, insect bites

### Emergency Medications
- Epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) — if anyone has known severe allergies
- Prescription medications for family members (separate labeled supply)
- Naloxone (Narcan) nasal spray — opioid overdose reversal

## Tools and Equipment

### Diagnostic
- Digital thermometer, pulse oximeter, blood pressure cuff, penlight

### Instruments
- Tweezers (pointed and flat-tip), trauma shears, bandage scissors
- Safety pins (assorted), magnifying glass, cotton-tipped swabs

### Splinting and Immobilization
- SAM splint (36-inch) — 2
- Triangular bandage — 4 (for slings and bindings)
- Elastic wrap for splinting, duct tape, padding material

### Removal and Extraction
- Tick removal tool, splinter forceps, snake bite pump kit

## Burns, Eye Care, and Environmental

**Burns:** Burn dressings, Water-Jel gel, non-stick pads, instant cold packs

**Eye care:** Sterile eye wash solution, eye wash cup, eye pads, eye patch

**Environmental:** Instant cold and heat packs, emergency thermal blanket (Mylar) x2, insect repellent (DEET 30%), sunscreen (SPF 30+), poison ivy barrier cream

## Reference Materials

- First aid manual (Wilderness First Aid or equivalent)
- CPR/First Aid quick reference card
- Emergency phone numbers and contact list
- Medical history forms for each family member
- Map of nearest hospital/urgent care with route and travel time

## Veterinary Additions

Maintain a separate veterinary kit in the barn:

### Wound Care (Large Animal)
- Veterinary wound spray (AluSpray or similar)
- Povidone-iodine solution (large bottle, veterinary grade)
- Gauze roll (4-inch and 6-inch, veterinary grade)
- Self-adherent wrap (VetWrap, multiple rolls)
- Disposable examination gloves (arm-length)

### Diagnostic and Treatment
- Digital livestock thermometer with string
- Stethoscope (for large animal auscultation)
- Hoof trimmers and hoof knife
- Drench syringe, balling gun
- Syringes (assorted: 3 mL, 6 mL, 12 mL, 60 mL)
- Needles (assorted: 18G, 20G, 22G)

### Medications (Large Animal)
- Electrolyte powder for dehydration
- Probios or other probiotic paste
- Tetanus antitoxin
- Penicillin G (consult veterinarian for dosage)
- Dewormer (species-specific)

### Emergency
- Contact information for large animal veterinarian
- Livestock trailer access plan for emergency transport

## Training Recommendations

- Take a Wilderness First Aid course (NOLS, SOLO, or equivalent)
- CPR/AED certification — American Red Cross or American Heart Association
- Large animal first aid course — many veterinary extension offices offer these
- Practice with your kit regularly so you can find supplies quickly under stress
