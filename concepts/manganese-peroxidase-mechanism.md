## Degradation of Nonphenolic Compounds

Nonphenolic substructures are abundant in lignin but cannot undergo oxidation through direct attack by Mn(III)-chelates. Mediators or cooxidants generate reactive radicals that can oxidize such structures. MnP from Phlebia radiata cleaves nonphenolic beta-O-4 lignin model dimers and catalyzes the mineralization of synthetic lignin in a cell-free system.

An MnP-lipid peroxidation system is strong enough to degrade C alpha-C beta and beta-aryl ether bonds in nonphenolic lignin model dimers. Lipid peroxidation of unsaturated lipids forms lipoxyradical intermediates in the presence of Mn(II) that can oxidize nonphenolic model lignin compounds. Ceriporiopsis subvermispora produced benzyl fragments from beta-O-4 lignin structures due to MnP lipid-mediated peroxidation in the absence of LiP activity.

## Hybrid Peroxidases

Hybrid forms of MnP and LiP are known which can oxidize both phenolic and nonphenolic compounds. These enzymes have been identified in Bjerkandera sp. strain BOS55, Bjerkandera adusta, and Pleurotus eryngii.
