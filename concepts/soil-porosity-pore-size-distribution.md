## Soil Porosity and Pore Size Distribution

Soil porosity is the fraction of total soil volume occupied by pore spaces — the voids
between solid mineral particles [[mushroom-cultivation-jar-lid-management-loose-fit-vacuum-gas-exchange]], root penetration, and microbial habitat.

## Bulk Density and Particle Density

Total porosity (φ) is derived from bulk density (ρ_b) and particle density (ρ_s):
φ = 1 − (ρ_b / ρ_s). Particle density of mineral soils averages ~2.65 g/cm³, while
bulk density ranges from ~1.0 g/cm³ in organic-rich soils to ~1.8 g/cm³ in compacted
subsoils. Because bulk density is easier to measure, it serves as a practical proxy
for porosity in agronomic and engineering applications.

## Pore Size Classification

Soil scientists classify pores into three broad categories by equivalent cylindrical
diameter:

- **Macropores** (> 0.08 mm): Drain rapidly under gravity. Include cracks, worm
  channels, root channels, and inter-aggregate voids.
- **Mesopores** (0.008–0.08 mm): Hold plant-available water, draining slowly over
  hours to days. Often called "storage pores."
- **Micropores** (< 0.008 mm): Retain water tightly through capillary and adsorptive
  forces; water is largely unavailable to plants.

## Functional Roles of Pore Size Classes

**Macropores** are primary pathways for gravitational drainage and soil aeration —
diffusion of oxygen into the root zone and CO₂ out. Without adequate macroporosity
(> 10% by volume), soils become anaerobic, inhibiting root respiration and favoring
denitrification.

**Mesopores** are the principal reservoir of plant-available water, subject to matric
tensions between ~0.03 and 1.5 MPa ([[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]], nutrient dissolution, and maintaining
soil structure through capillary cohesion between particles.

## Pore Size Distribution Curves

A pore size distribution (PSD) curve plots cumulative pore volume against equivalent
pore diameter (often log-scale). PSD curves complement soil water retention curves —
where retention describes energy required to drain pores, PSD expresses the volume
fraction at each diameter class. Bimodal distributions are common in structured soils,
reflecting distinct textural (intra-aggregate) and structural (inter-aggregate) pore
populations.

## Soil Texture Effects

grains; they drain freely but retain little plant-available water. Clay soils exhibit
