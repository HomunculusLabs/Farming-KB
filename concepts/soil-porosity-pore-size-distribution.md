# Soil Porosity and Pore Size Distribution

Soil porosity is the fraction of total soil volume occupied by pore spaces — the voids
between solid mineral particles [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]. Expressed as a percentage or
dimensionless ratio (0–1), porosity is a master variable in soil physics governing
water movement, [[mushroom-cultivation-jar-lid-management-loose-fit-vacuum-gas-exchange]], root penetration, and microbial habitat.

## Bulk Density and Particle Density

Total porosity (φ) is derived from bulk density (ρ_b) and particle density (ρ_s):
φ = 1 − (ρ_b / ρ_s). Particle density of mineral soils averages ~2.65 g/cm³, while
bulk density ranges from ~1.0 g/cm³ in organic-rich soils to ~1.8 g/cm³ in compacted
subsoils. Because bulk density is easier to measure, it serves as a practical proxy
for porosity in agronomic and engineering applications.

## Pore Size Classification

Soil scientists classify pores into three broad categories by equivalent cylindrical
diameter:

- **Macropores** (> 0.08 mm): Drain rapidly under gravity. Include cracks, worm
  channels, root channels, and inter-aggregate voids.
- **Mesopores** (0.008–0.08 mm): Hold plant-available water, draining slowly over
  hours to days. Often called "storage pores."
- **Micropores** (< 0.008 mm): Retain water tightly through capillary and adsorptive
  forces; water is largely unavailable to plants.

## Functional Roles of Pore Size Classes

**Macropores** are primary pathways for gravitational drainage and soil aeration —
diffusion of oxygen into the root zone and CO₂ out. Without adequate macroporosity
(> 10% by volume), soils become anaerobic, inhibiting root respiration and favoring
denitrification.

**Mesopores** are the principal reservoir of plant-available water, subject to matric
tensions between ~0.03 and 1.5 MPa ([[bulk-substrate-field-capacity]] to wilting point). They buffer
plants against short-term drought and supply water through capillary continuity.

**Micropores** retain water below −1.5 MPa, effectively unavailable to crops, but
critical for sustaining [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]], nutrient dissolution, and maintaining
soil structure through capillary cohesion between particles.

## Pore Size Distribution Curves

A pore size distribution (PSD) curve plots cumulative pore volume against equivalent
pore diameter (often log-scale). PSD curves complement soil water retention curves —
where retention describes energy required to drain pores, PSD expresses the volume
fraction at each diameter class. Bimodal distributions are common in structured soils,
reflecting distinct textural (intra-aggregate) and structural (inter-aggregate) pore
populations.

## Soil Texture Effects

Sandy soils have lower total porosity (35–45%) dominated by macropores between sand
grains; they drain freely but retain little plant-available water. Clay soils exhibit
higher porosity (45–60%) but pores are predominantly micropores, giving high water-
[[pf-tek-vermiculite-type-comparison-water-holding-capacity]] but poor drainage and aeration. Loam soils provide an intermediate,
agriculturally optimal balance of pore sizes.

## Soil Structure and Aggregation

Soil structure — the arrangement of particles into aggregates — is dynamic and
management-responsive. Well-aggregated soils develop hierarchical pore systems:
micropores within micro-aggregates, mesopores between them, and macropores between
macro-aggregates. [[fungal-hyphae]] enmesh particles, bacterial exopolysaccharides act as
glue, and [[mycorrhiza-root-exudates]] fuel microbial production of binding agents. The resulting
diverse pore network is far more functional than in structureless soils.

## Biological Macropores

Earthworm burrows form continuous vertical channels 2–12 mm in diameter persisting for
months to years. Root channels from perennial plants create similar pathways. These
biological macropores preferentially conduct water during heavy rainfall, reducing
runoff and increasing infiltration. Fungal hyphae networks create extensive mesopore
systems enhancing water redistribution and [[fungal-pulsatile-nutrient-transport-mycelial-networks]].
