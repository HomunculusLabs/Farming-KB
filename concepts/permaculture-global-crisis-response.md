# Permaculture Global Crisis Response

Holmgren's closing chapters articulate a comprehensive framework for understanding and responding to the global crisis -- the convergence of fossil fuel depletion, ecological overshoot, climate change, and social destabilisation. Rather than presenting a single blueprint, he describes the transition from energy-abundant industrial society to energy-constrained conserver society as an ongoing process of adaptive design rooted in permaculture principles. This connects directly to [[permaculture-energy-descent-planning]], [[consumer-versus-conserver-society]], and [[low-energy-futures-in-permaculture]].

## The Energy Descent Reality

Holmgren frames the current global crisis as a natural consequence of the fossil fuel energy pulse. Industrial culture is stable only in its direction of energy use. The current cultural and economic dynamic of globalisation is one of chaotic climax and transition from growth in population and energy use to decline.

Once we accept the reality and magnitude of energy descent, we begin to ask what "sustainability" might mean. If high-energy society is energetically impossible as anything more than a pulse in the long run of human history, then it cannot be sustainable, no matter how much we shuffle the technological deckchairs. Holmgren suggests we "need to get over our naive and simplistic notions of sustainability as a likely reality for ourselves or even our grandchildren and instead accept that our task is to use our familiarity with continuous change to adapt to energy descent."

## The Mountain Peak Metaphor

Holmgren uses the metaphor of a mountain peak: humanity has climbed a spectacular but dangerous mountain through heroic effort, great sacrifice, and exhilaration. At the top, the whole world is laid out, but the weather is threatening. We must take advantage of the view to chart our way down while we have favourable weather and daylight. The descent will be more hazardous than the climb. Having been on the mountain so long, we can barely remember the home in a far-off valley that we fled as it was progressively destroyed by forces we did not understand. But we know that each step brings us closer to a sheltered valley where we can make a new home.

Permaculture is "a whole-hearted adaptation to the ecological realities of decline, which are as natural and creative as those of growth." The proverb "what goes up, must come down" reminds us that we know this to be true. "The real issue of our age is how we make a graceful and ethical descent."

## The Large-Scale Fossil Fuel Pulse

Today we have consumed the easy half of the total likely reserves of conventional oil, and opportunities for major expansion of electricity networks and consumption are contracting. At the same time the world's population has more than tripled. The rebuilding of social and cultural capital must occur within a context of declining net energy availability, and in ways that reinforce the simultaneous rebuilding of natural capital.

Grasping this energetic reality leads many to despair at the magnitude of the task. While global capitalism has been like a fire converting green forests to ashes, it has likewise released potential and information from the constraints of cultural norms that were hopelessly inappropriate for a world of declining energy. The Permaculture aphorism "the problem is the solution" is "not some naive optimism in the face of terrible prospects, or the delusion of those with all the opportunities, but a simple idea with powerful relevance to our time."

## Socio-Political Cycles as Pulsing Systems

Holmgren reads the last decades of economic history through the lens of Holling's Four-Phase Cycle of ecosystem dynamics. The current phase of laissez-faire capitalism, beginning with Thatcher and Reagan in the early 1980s, represents the consumption of public assets created over preceding decades -- physical infrastructure, institutional knowledge, shared scientific knowledge, and the goodwill of non-government organisations.

This phase was enabled by the social democratic politics of the 1930s-1950s, a time of rapidly rising net energy when the tapping of conventional oil was beginning to accelerate. The structures of public wealth that are rebuilt after the current pulse ends will of necessity be different from those swept away, because they must be built within declining net energy.

## Top-Down Thinking, Bottom-Up Action

Holmgren identifies a critical strategic principle: the need to combine top-down thinking (wholistic systemic understanding) with bottom-up action (individual, household, and community-level change). He traces this through historical stages:

- **Traditional society**: Integrated understanding with top-down action by elites, balanced by fragmented understanding and bottom-up action by the masses
- **Industrial society**: Fragmented (reductionist) understanding with top-down action by elites -- the scientific-technocratic model
- **Post-industrial society**: The most effective mode is "integrated understanding with bottom-up action" -- wholistic thinking informing collaborative, participatory change

Social democratic movements of the late 19th and early 20th centuries mastered a version of this combination to control corporate power. The contemporary challenge requires a similar synthesis to address the consequences of fossil fuel peak and ecological overshoot.

## Personal Responsibility and Leverage

The emphasis on personal responsibility arises from the situation where much of the need to design for energy descent is informed by ethical principles that have leverage primarily through individual behaviour. Because of the high-energy nature of the modern world, each person in rich countries commands roughly 100 energy slaves. During the lifetime of an American baby-boomer born in 1950 and dying in 2025, over half the conventional oil reserves of the United States will have been consumed.

It is the billion or so middle-class people around the world who are "the engine of global destruction, rather than the numerically small elite, or the relatively self-reliant but increasingly destitute majority." This empowerment of the individual provides a unique opportunity for bottom-up change. Permaculture aims to change people from dependent consumers of unsustainable products and services to responsible producers of appropriate wealth and value.

## Future Sustainable Culture

As the rate of change during energy descent slows, more enduring and diverse bioregional cultures will emerge. Holmgren expects these cultures to have the following characteristics:

- **Bioregional political and economic structures**, giving renewed geographic diversity
- **Cross-fertilised populations** -- biogenetically, racially, culturally and intellectually -- giving natural hybrid vigour
- **Accessibility and low dependence** on expensive and centralised technology
- **Incremental development** with feedback and refinement at each stage

### Attitudes Adaptive to Descent
Values contributing to a new descent culture include:
- Acknowledging useful contributions from outside any particular discipline
- Learning from knowledge systems outside scientific rationalism
- Remaining sceptical of official authority unless supported by localised, networked credentials
- Acknowledging the validity of pre-existing cultures of place
- Contributing to a culture of place by supporting and celebrating the local
- Using media and information technology with sceptical caution -- "the icing on the cake, rather than the cake itself"

## Appropriate Use of Non-Renewable Resources

The transition provides a unique strategic opportunity to make the best use of existing wealth and non-renewable resources to rebuild natural and human capital. The best use for non-renewable resources should be to establish a system, rather than to maintain or harvest it.

Examples include:
- Passive solar houses using high-embodied-energy glass to capture solar energy while providing lighting and views
- Bulldozers and earth-moving equipment to create water harvesting and distribution systems maintainable indefinitely by human labour
- Mineral fertilisers for one-time soil rehabilitation that creates permanent improvement in biological productivity

## Evolutionary Change and Ecosynthesis

Holmgren extends crisis response into the domain of evolutionary change. The same factors driving species extinction may generate new ones: mining of fossil fuels accumulated over 1000 million years, six billion highly connected people, world trade causing unprecedented species transfer, and risks from nuclear and chemical contamination.

He argues that an eruption of new species is both a positive expression of nature's abundance and a potential mortal threat to humanity. "To simultaneously hold both concepts is difficult: the focus on one tends to dilute or diffuse the power of the other."

Ecosynthesis -- the evolution of new ecosystems of native and exotic species responding to novel conditions -- provides a framework for understanding urban weedscapes and other novel ecological communities not as degraded versions of original ecosystems but as new evolving systems. This recognition is important because it means the self-organisational power of nature can be enhanced to create functional human ecosystems in relatively few generations.

## Permaculture as Strategic Framework

Holmgren positions permaculture as "ecological rationalism" -- it recognises the design rules and measures of value in energy descent, and it provides ethical and positive pathways for embracing those design rules. The [[permaculture-earthworks-guide]] and [[permaculture-ethics]] provide a coherent set of design guidelines that remain valid regardless of the energy available. They work at every scale -- from a backyard garden to a national economy -- and in every domain -- from agriculture to governance to education.

The rapid evolution of more appropriate governance, economy and culture, including art and myth, are central to adapting to energy descent. "To claim that it is possible to use Permaculture principles to design a sustainable culture and society may be too big a leap, but I believe it is possible at least to use them for evaluating the diverse cultural phenomena we find ourselves participating in."

## Practical Pathways

The transition involves multiple parallel pathways:
- **Household**: Food production, water capture, energy conservation, skill building, self-reliance
- **Community**: Shared resources, cooperative enterprises, local currencies, mutual aid networks, intentional communities
- **Bioregional**: Catchment management, regional food systems, biodiversity corridors, local governance
- **National**: Policy reform, subsidy redirection, education for resilience
- **Global**: Knowledge sharing, seed exchange, conflict resolution, communications networks

## Scepticism and Adaptive Capacity

Holmgren emphasises that scepticism and resistance to dogma are just as important in a descent culture as in a free one. "Nature is always providing us with a myriad of indicators, warning signs and questions in response to our actions driven by certainty and belief. Adaptation to energy descent depends on a dynamic balance of values and contextual knowledge rather than holy crusades of good over evil or simplistic, universal solutions."
