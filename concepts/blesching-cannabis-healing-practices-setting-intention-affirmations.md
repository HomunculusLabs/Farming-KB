# Cannabis Healing Practices — Setting, Intention, and Affirmations

## Overview

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) framework, developed by Uwe Blesching PhD,
integrates evidence-based cannabinoid science with structured mind-body
healing practices. Beyond pharmacological effects, the CHI prescribes a
deliberate protocol for engaging with cannabis as a tool for deep
psychological and emotional exploration. This protocol consists of three
core phases: **The Setting**, **The Intention**, and **The Exploration**,
supported by **Powerful Questions**, **Blessings**, and **Affirmations**.

## The Setting

The physical and psychological environment profoundly shapes therapeutic
outcomes. Blesching recommends:

- **Time**: Reserve 2 to 4 hours of uninterrupted time on a day with no
  driving or demanding tasks required afterward.
- **Physical comfort**: Sit or lie comfortably; minimize interruptions by
  turning off phones and silencing notifications.
- **Auditory environment**: Work in silence or play unfamiliar music
  selected by genre, to prevent associative memories from pulling
  attention away from internal exploration.
- **Safety**: Eliminate any need to operate machinery, drive, or make
  critical decisions during the session.

External stimuli compete with internal awareness. By removing distractions,
the patient creates conditions for the endocannabinoid system to support
deep relaxation and emotional processing without interference.

## The Intention

Intention is distinguished sharply from wishful thinking. Whereas wishful
thinking is "broad, nebulous, and abstract," intention is "focused, clear,
and direct" supported by "unwavering commitment." Blesching draws on
placebo, nocebo, and quantum research to suggest that conscious intention
can activate the body's capacity for self-healing.

Patients begin with foundational statements: "I want to heal," "I want to
heal, even if I don't know how," or "I want to discover what is blocking
my healing." Alternatively, they formulate a specific question relevant to
their symptoms using disease-specific "Powerful Questions" from the CHI
A–Z index.

The scientific basis rests on the endocannabinoid system's demonstrated
roles in neurogenesis, neuroplasticity, and frame-of-mind modulation.
Consciously directing attention through intention while the ECS is
pharmacologically activated creates conditions where new neural pathways
may form around healthier cognitive and emotional patterns.

## The Exploration

After entering a deeply relaxed state through appropriately dosed cannabis
or meditation, the patient follows a structured internal process:

1. **Focus on the symptom**: Direct attention to the tension, sensations,
   or feelings associated with the disease. These feelings are framed as
   "the needle of a compass that points to underlying issues."
2. **Follow the thread**: Allow memories or scenarios to emerge. Often
   several layers surface, revealing emotional connections and themes.
3. **Stay with discomfort**: Internal resistance manifests as desire for
   distraction, movement, or conversation. Signs of relevant material
   emerging include discomfort, distracting pains, or sleepiness. The
   instruction is to remain focused within and be curious.
4. **Self-forgiveness**: Under cannabis or gentle mindfulness, patients
   often find they can forgive themselves for any resistance present.
5. **Discharge and reframe**: Once blocked emotional energy is discharged,
   identify the beliefs, attitudes, and choices contributing to ill health
   and replace them with healthier alternatives.

## Powerful Questions — The Six-Question Framework

For conditions without specific disease-targeted questions, or as a general
healing guide, the CHI provides six foundational questions:

1. What are the symptoms keeping you from doing?
2. How do you feel about that?
3. Where do these feelings take you?
4. What scenarios or memories are associated with these feelings?
5. What is the meaning (messages, unhealthy beliefs/attitudes) you have
   given to that scenario?
6. Can the answers to these questions direct your focus to support your
   capacity for self-healing?

These questions peel back layers of emotional and cognitive architecture
surrounding illness, operating at the intersection of body and mind where
the endocannabinoid system bridges physiological and psychological processes.

## Blessings and Affirmations

**Blessings** engage the spiritual dimension for patients who wish to
connect with "something larger than ourselves, by whatever name or form."
They serve as seed intentions introducing healthier thoughts into daily
health routines. Sample blessings are provided for each condition in the
A–Z index.

**Affirmations** draw on psychoneuroimmunology — the study of how thoughts
and words affect the immune system. Blesching states directly: "We can be
imprisoned by our own words and thoughts, or we can use them to set us
free." CHI affirmations are condition-specific rather than generic,
designed to counter unhealthy beliefs, reinforce self-healing capacity,
leverage ECS-driven neuroplasticity, and integrate with the exploration
phase's emotional discharge work.

The neurochemical rationale connects to Blesching's neurotransmitter
framework identifying "expansive" molecules (acetylcholine, anandamide,
endogenous opioids, GABA, oxytocin, serotonin) versus "constricting"
molecules (epinephrine, dopamine, norepinephrine, cortisol, glutamate,
vasopressin). Blessings and affirmations deliberately shift this balance.

## Integration with Dietary Cannabinoids

The healing practices connect to the "Let Food Be Thy Medicine" protocol
using (E)-β-caryophyllene-containing spices as daily CB2 agonists. This
creates a comprehensive framework: cannabis provides primary ECS activation
for deep exploration; dietary cannabinoids offer ongoing daily CB2 support;
mind-body practices direct neuroplastic potential toward specific healing
outcomes; and mindfulness techniques maintain gains between sessions.

## Practical Considerations

- Sessions must never involve driving or operating machinery afterward
- Establish the subjective therapeutic window before deep exploration work
- The framework is complementary to, not a replacement for, conventional
  medical treatment
- "Powerful Questions" work independently of cannabis as meditation or
  journaling prompts
- Affirmations are most effective during states of deep relaxation when
  neuroplastic potential is greatest
