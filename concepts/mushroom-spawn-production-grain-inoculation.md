## Sterilization and Inoculation

Grain for spawn production is hydrated to approximately 50% moisture content
and sterilized in autoclaves at 121°C for 60–90 minutes. Complete sterilization
is essential because any surviving contaminant organism will multiply during
the incubation period and outcompete the mushroom mycelium in the grain.

Inoculation is performed in laminar flow hoods or sterile rooms using
aseptic technique. A small piece of agar culture or previously colonized grain
spawn is transferred to each container. The inoculated grain is incubated at
the species-appropriate temperature (typically 20–25°C) until fully colonized,
usually 10–21 days depending on species and grain type.

## Quality Control

Spawn quality is assessed through visual inspection, growth rate measurement,
and laboratory testing for contamination. Healthy grain spawn appears fully
colonized with dense white mycelium and has a characteristic mushroom or
earthy smell. Contaminated spawn shows discolored patches, slimy textures,
or off-odors from bacterial growth.
