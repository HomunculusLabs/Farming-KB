## Practical Holding Strategies

### Short-Term Holding (Same Day)

For same-day holding (brewed in the morning, applied in the evening), minimal intervention is needed:

- Continue aeration if the brewer allows
- If the brewer must be emptied, transfer to a container with an air stone or recirculating pump
- Do not add food
- Keep the tea in shade — direct sunlight heats the liquid and accelerates organism activity and oxygen consumption

### Extended Holding (1-3 Days)

For holding beyond the same day:

- Maintain active aeration with an air pump and diffuser stone
- Monitor [[gaias-garden-cover-crops-soil-building]], ornamental beds)
- Continue aeration
- Do not add food under any circumstances
- Accept that diversity has been significantly reduced
- The remaining organisms still provide some soil biology benefit

## Factors That Accelerate Quality Loss

Several conditions cause tea quality to degrade faster during holding:

- **High temperature**: Warm tea has faster organism metabolism, consuming oxygen and food more rapidly
- **Food residue**: Tea brewed with excessive food resources has more substrate for bacterial growth, accelerating oxygen depletion during holding
- **Large volume-to-surface-area ratio**: Tea in a deep, narrow container has less oxygen diffusion at the surface compared to a wide, shallow container
- **Lack of agitation**: Still tea develops stratification, with oxygen-depleted zones forming at the bottom

## Dissolved Oxygen Monitoring During Holding

For anyone holding tea beyond the brewing period, Ingham strongly recommends dissolved oxygen (DO) monitoring:

- **Target level**: Maintain DO above 6 mg/L at all times during holding. Below this threshold, facultative anaerobes begin to shift metabolism
- **Critical level**: If DO drops below 4 mg/L, beneficial organisms begin dying. Immediate action (increased aeration, reduced volume) is needed
