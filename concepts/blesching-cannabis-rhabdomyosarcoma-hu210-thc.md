# Cannabis and Rhabdomyosarcoma (HU210 / THC)

## Overview

[[rhabdomyosarcoma|Rhabdomyosarcoma]] is a fast-growing, highly malignant tumor
found most often in children. It affects the connective tissue and is believed to
begin in progenitor cells (similar to stem cells), which later differentiate into
muscle cells. The most common locations for rhabdomyosarcoma development are the
head and neck, followed by the genitourinary tract.

Conventional treatment is limited to chemotherapy, surgery, and radiation. The
survival rate in the late 1960s was a mere 10–15%, but by 2000 it had risen to
over 70%.

## Cannabis Research Summary

Two studies have examined the relationship between cannabis and
[[blesching-cannabis-rhabdomyosarcoma]], yielding a combined **CHI Value of 1**.

### Zurich 2009 — HU210 and THC (Laboratory and Animal Studies)

**Institution:** University Children's Hospital, Zurich, Switzerland

Swiss researchers demonstrated that blesching cannabis rhabdomyosarcoma hu210 thc and [[tetrahydrocannabinol-thc]] produced
[[mushroom-apoptosis-mechanisms|cancer cell death]] in rhabdomyosarcoma cells in laboratory tests.
The results were confirmed in vivo by xenografting rhabdomyosarcoma cancer cells
treated with the cannabinoid, which led to significant suppression of tumor
growth. This study provides a basis for considering cannabinoids as a new
treatment approach for rhabdomyosarcoma.

- **CHI Value:** 1
- **Drugs tested:** HU210, THC
- **Study type:** Laboratory and animal (xenograft) studies

### Pittsburgh 1993 — Cocaine and Marijuana (Case-Controlled Human Trial)

**Institution:** University of Pittsburgh School of Medicine, Pittsburgh, PA, USA

A survey of parents of 322 patients with [[blesching-cannabis-rhabdomyosarcoma]] suggested that
parental use of cocaine and marijuana in the year before conception may increase
the risk of rhabdomyosarcoma two- to fivefold. Cannabis use was identified as
one potential co-factor in conjunction with cocaine in the development of the
cancer. To date, no studies have explored whether use of cannabis alone
increases the risk of rhabdomyosarcoma.

- **CHI Value:** 0
- **Key limitation:** Cannabis was a co-factor with cocaine; isolated cannabis
  risk remains uninvestigated

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Two cannabinoids have been tested against [[blesching-cannabis-rhabdomyosarcoma]]:

- **blesching cannabis rhabdomyosarcoma hu210 thc:** A synthetic cannabinoid with a higher affinity for the
  [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2-tissue-distribution-blesching|cb1-receptor]]. As a research compound, it is not available through
  conventional cannabis strains.
- **[[tetrahydrocannabinol-thc]]:** Binds relatively equally to the [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2-tissue-distribution-blesching|cb1-receptor]] and
  [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2-tissue-distribution-blesching|cb2-receptor]], activating both receptor pathways.

**Practical guidance:** Sativa and sativa-heavy strains tend to present with a
higher THC:CBD ratio, thus activating [[cb1-receptor|CB1]] and
[[cb2-receptor|CB2]] in relatively equal proportions. This may be relevant when
seeking to approximate the receptor activation profile observed in the Zurich
study, where [[tetrahydrocannabinol-thc]] — which engages both receptors — demonstrated
antineoplastic activity.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Rhabdomyosarcoma

Applying mind-body medicine paradigms to infant and childhood cancers remains
a deeply challenging task. The question of what beliefs, unresolved emotions, or
unconscious patterns may contribute to disease in young children stretches the
boundaries of conventional psychosomatic frameworks. Discovery may take place in

## Complementary Approaches: Food as Medicine

### Saffron (*Crocus sativus* L.)

Saffron may possess anticancer activity, including activity against
[[blesching-cannabis-rhabdomyosarcoma]]. As a complementary food-as-medicine approach, saffron
represents an accessible adjunct that could be integrated alongside
conventional and cannabinoid-based treatment strategies.

## Therapeutic Potential

The Zurich study provides a scientific foundation for considering cannabinoids
— specifically blesching cannabis rhabdomyosarcoma hu210 thc and [[tetrahydrocannabinol-thc]] — as a new treatment approach for
[[blesching-cannabis-rhabdomyosarcoma]]. The mechanism involves [[mushroom-apoptosis-mechanisms|programmed cell death]]
in cancer cells, confirmed through both in vitro and in vivo xenograft models.

While the current evidence base is limited (CHI Value: 1, two studies), the
results are promising enough to warrant further investigation into cannabinoid
therapy as an adjunct or alternative treatment for pediatric rhabdomyosarcoma.
