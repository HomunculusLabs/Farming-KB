## Overview

Cannabis is a dioecious plant species, meaning male and female flowers
develop on separate plants, although monoecious individuals with both
sexes on one plant occur occasionally. This sexual separation has

### Life Cycle Timing Differences

Staminate plants tend to flower up to one month earlier than pistillate
plants, though pistillate plants often differentiate primordia one to
two weeks before males. After pollen dehiscence, staminate plants die
while pistillate plants may continue maturing for up to five months if
unfertilized. Compared with females, males show a more rapid increase
in height and a more rapid decrease in leaf size as flowering
progresses.

### Hermaphroditism and Environmental Stress

Under conditions of extreme stress, populations depart greatly from the
expected 1:1 staminate to pistillate ratio. Stress can trigger
self-pollination through hermaphroditism, where plants produce both male
and female flowers. Genetically, hermaphrodites produce only female and
hermaphrodite seeds, never male seeds. This mechanism represents a last
ditch reproductive effort when the plant perceives its chances of
receiving external pollen as slim. Early fertilization may help fix
the sex of pistillate plants and prevent hermaphrodism.
