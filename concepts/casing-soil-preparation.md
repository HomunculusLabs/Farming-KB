## Casing Soil Preparation for Mushroom Cultivation A casing layer is a non-nutritive material applied to the surface of fully colonized substrate to create the microclimate necessary for mushroom formation (pinning). The casing soil recipe and preparation method described here has been proven effective for psilocybe-tampanensis-cultivation, and [[mollison-designers-microclimate-creation-frost-protection]]**: Provides a gradient of moisture, CO₂
  concentration, and humidity that triggers the shift from vegetative mycelial
  growth to reproductive fruiting.
- **Physical support**: Gives developing mushrooms a stable matrix to anchor
  into as they grow upward.
- **Contamination barrier**: The non-nutritive casing is less susceptible to
  contamination than exposed grain, protecting the vulnerable mycelial
  surface during the extended fruiting period.

## The Peat-Vermiculite-Limestone Recipe

The recommended casing formulation uses three ingredients in the following
ratio by volume:

| Ingredient | Parts | Role |
|-----------|-------|------|
| **Peat** | 10 | Primary moisture-holding matrix |
| **Vermiculite** | 5 | Aeration and additional water retention |
| **Limestone (Marl)** | 2 | pH buffering to ~7.5 |

### Ingredient Notes

- **Peat**: Canadian sphagnum [[cervantes-hydrated-lime-quicklime-ph-adjustment]] — it is too caustic.

## Mixing and Hydration

The ingredients are mixed in dry form first, then water is gradually added
while stirring:

1. Combine all dry ingredients in a large container.
2. Add water gradually while mixing thoroughly.
3. The goal is maximum water retention without the mixture turning to mud.
4. Test by squeezing a handful: it should hold together in a clump but no
  water should drip out.
5. If too wet, add more dry ingredients in the correct ratio.
6. If too dry, add more water sparingly and re-mix.

The moisture content of the peat varies between sources and seasons, so the
exact water amount must be determined empirically each time.
