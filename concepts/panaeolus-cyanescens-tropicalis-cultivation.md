# Panaeolus Cyanescens and Tropicalis Cultivation

This document outlines a practical cultivation method for *Panaeolus cyanescens* and *[[panaeolus-tropicalis]]* using sterilized substrate composed of cow dung, vermiculite, and straw. The method follows a straightforward workflow: [[substrate-preparation]], sterilization, inoculation, colonization, casing, and fruiting. *Panaeolus* species differ significantly from *[[psilocybe-cubensis]]* in their cultivation requirements, particularly regarding air exchange and substrate colonization density.

## Substrate Preparation

### Ingredients (for 4 standard spawn bags)

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|-----------|----------|
| Dry straw (soaked 12 hours, submerged) | 1/2 kg |
| Dried cow dung | 4 liters |
| Vermiculite | 3 liters |
| Water | 3–4 liters |

### Mixing Procedure

1. Mix dried dung and vermiculite in dry form
2. Add water and stir thoroughly — **critical: ensure the mixture is not too wet**. Loose water will often result in bacterial contamination later
3. Add the pre-soaked straw and mix in thoroughly
4. Divide the substrate evenly over four autoclavable spawn bags (with filter patch)
5. Fold flaps and place bags in pressure cooker — place a lid on top to prevent bags from blocking the steam valve

### Sterilization

- **Sterilize for 2 full hours** in a pressure cooker/autoclave
- Allow bags to cool down in the flow cabinet before inoculation

## Spawn and Inoculation

- **Spawn type:** Rye [[grain-spawn]]
- **Important note:** *Panaeolus* species do not colonize grain as densely as *Psilocybe cubensis*
- Open bags in the flow cabinet (touch only the outside)
- Each bag receives 300 ml of spawn by free pouring
- Seal bags with an impulse sealer
- Shake bags to mix spawn thoroughly with substrate

## Colonization

- **Duration:** 5–10 days depending on temperature and shaking frequency
- Shake the bags regularly during colonization to distribute mycelium
- After 3 days, white mycelium should be visibly spreading through the substrate
- Full colonization is indicated by complete white coverage of the substrate

## Casing

### Preparation

1. Clean a plastic bin with alcohol (bin should have holes plugged with polyfill for gas exchange)
2. Cut colonized bags open with scissors
3. Use two bags per bin — empty the fully colonized substrate into the bin
4. Level the substrate by hand (wear gloves)
5. Apply a thin layer of sterilized [[casing-soil]]: **1–1.5 cm**
6. Level the casing layer by hand
7. Cover the bin with polyethylene wrap to prevent contamination and moisture loss

### Casing Colonization

- Place bins in a warm location for the casing layer to colonize
- After 5–6 days, mycelium will appear on the casing surface
- At this point, place another bin upside down on top to create a "mini-greenhouse" effect
- The top bin should have mesh-covered holes for gas exchange

## Fruiting

- Mushrooms will begin appearing several days after the mini-greenhouse is set up
- **Keep the casing soil moist** using a spray bottle
- Mushrooms mature in a couple of days from pin formation
- Harvest when fully mature

## Critical Cultivation Notes

### Air Exchange (Most Important Factor)

*Panaeolus* species are **much more sensitive to high CO₂ concentrations** than many other cultivated mushrooms. The most common reason for failure is insufficient fresh air exposure:

- With insufficient air exchange, many mushrooms will form but **only a few will mature** — the rest will abort
- Improper air exchange results in **tall, spindly mushrooms with small caps**
- Adequate fresh air exchange is the single most critical variable for successful cultivation

### Temperature

- These species grow in a wide range of temperatures
- They flourish best at approximately **25°C (77°F) or slightly higher**

### Strain Selection

- Not all substrains from a multispore germination are equally viable
- Recommended approach: start with a multispore culture, then **clone the best-looking mushrooms** for further cultivation
- This selective cloning improves consistency and yield over successive generations

## Key Differences from Psilocybe Cubensis

| Characteristic | Panaeolus spp. | Psilocybe cubensis |
|---------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Grain colonization | Less dense | Dense, robust |
| CO₂ sensitivity | Very high | Moderate |
| Air exchange needs | Critical — failure point | Important but more forgiving |
| Optimal temperature | ~25°C+ | ~25°C |
| Abort rate (poor conditions) | High | Moderate |
| Growth speed on substrate | Fast (5–10 days) | Fast |

## Summary of Critical Success Factors

1. **Substrate moisture:** Not too wet — loose water causes bacterial contamination
2. **Sterilization:** Full 2 hours — no shortcuts
3. **Air exchange:** The most important variable — ensure generous fresh air throughout fruiting
4. **Temperature:** Maintain 25°C or slightly higher for optimal growth
5. **Strain selection:** Clone from the best performers rather than relying solely on multispore
6. **Casing thickness:** Keep thin at 1–1.5 cm — too thick delays colonization
