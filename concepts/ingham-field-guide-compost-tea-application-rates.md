# Compost Tea Application Rates (Ingham Field Guide)

The Ingham Field Guide provides specific application rate recommendations for orchards, vegetable/flower row crops, and general scenarios. Rates are based on years of SFI testing and field observation.

## Standard Rates

- Foliar spray: 5 gallons of compost tea per acre for every 6 feet of plant height (canopy)
- Soil drench: 15-20 gallons of compost tea per acre
- Mildew alert or severe disease pressure: increase foliar rate to 10 gallons per acre
- Water is only a carrier -- add enough water to cover the area; backpack sprayers often apply tea straight without dilution

## Orchard Application Program

**Fall:**
- Put down 1-2 tons per acre of compost/wormcastings as top dressing on fallen leaves; let first spring rain incorporate
- For bare soil (e.g., citrus), apply directly to soil
- As soil drench, use 10-15 gallons of compost tea per acre, applied directly on top of compost
- Apply mycorrhizal spores to cores to establish VAM on root systems

**Spring/Summer:**
- Begin foliar sprays 2 weeks before bud break
- 5 gallons per acre per 6 feet of canopy height, once per month
- For citrus, continue sprays throughout winter months
- Do not spray when bees are pollinating

**Transplanting:**
- Use 30-50% compost/wormcastings mixed with site soil
- For bare root plantings, dip roots in a slurry of compost/wormcastings, nutrients, and compost tea (like pancake batter consistency)
- Do NOT use root gels -- they smother roots and prevent air passage; use yucca as a sticker if needed

**Disease and Pest Response:**
- When extension service issues disease or pest warnings, apply weekly until alert stops, then monthly until harvest
- If disease outbreak occurs, apply immediately every 3-5 days until controlled
- After any pesticide application, wait 3 days then resume compost tea program

## Vegetable/Flower Row Crop Program

**Fall:**
- Put down 1-5 tons per acre of compost/wormcastings as top dressing on crop residue

**Spring:**
- Soil drench: 15 gallons of compost tea per acre, 2 weeks before planting
- Seed treatment: roll fungicide-free seed in compost tea with VAM spores; if fungicide-treated seed, place 5 gal/ac in the planting row under seed

**Growing Season:**
- Foliar spray: 5 gallons per acre, drench foliage and stems every 10-14 days
- Start 2 weeks before bud break
- Do not spray when bees are pollinating
- Continue every 10-14 days until harvest

**Disease Response:**
- When warnings issued, apply weekly until alert stops, then every 10-14 days until harvest
- If outbreak occurs, apply every 3-5 days until stopped

## Timing Considerations

- Soil drenches: wait until soil is warm enough for organisms to grow
- Foliar sprays: organisms need 20 minutes to attach to leaf surfaces; if spraying in a downpour, use a sticker-spreader
- Soil drenches are okay anytime, even in rain
- Above-freezing temperatures are acceptable for application, as long as organisms get into soil before it freezes
- Winter applications are appropriate if soil temperatures allow establishment before freezing
- First year may require additional nutrients added in the tea while nutrient cycling gets established

## Application Methods

**Foliar Spray Equipment:**
- Nozzle opening size must be greater than 200 micrometers (300-400 micrometers preferred)
- Pressure must be low relative to distance: 1-2 feet = 20-40 psi; 6 feet = 100 psi; 100 feet = 600 psi
- Impact pressure on leaf surfaces kills organisms, not pressure in the liquid itself
- Diaphragm pumps are clearly best; some centrifugal pumps are acceptable
- Transfer pumps can reduce all organism numbers by 50% in a single pass
- Electrostatic sprayers kill organisms; avoid them
- Pivot irrigation, airplanes, and large droplet systems are acceptable (large droplets protect organisms)

**Drip Irrigation:**
- Emitter nozzle size must be at least 200 micrometers (some are as small as 5 micrometers)
- 400 micrometer emitters allow all microbes including fungal colonies to pass
- Test to confirm organisms pass through the emitters
- Clean lines with 3-10% vinegar or 3% hydrogen peroxide

## Sticker-Spreaders

For foliar applications where rain or waxy leaf surfaces are concerns:
- Unsulfured molasses at 3-5% concentration: enhances fungal growth AND sticks organisms to leaves
- Nu-Film (pine sap): dilute before adding to tea
- Saponin: works well, get preservative-free versions
- Fish hydrolysate: can act as sticker at correct concentration (too much oil smothers)
- Yucca, lignin, aloe vera (avoid the green layer right next to the leaf -- it is detrimental to life)
- Avoid dormant oils -- they smother leaf surfaces

## Coverage Guidelines

- Mid-day sun is acceptable if droplet sizes are large enough to protect organisms
- Light mists are actually ideal for tea application -- moisture helps organisms attach to leaf surfaces
- Apply tea as active organisms; put-to-sleep tea loses approximately 50% of bacterial species
- Add food to tea as it is being sprayed out (safest approach to keep organisms active)

## Water Reduction Over Time

- First year: approximately 30% decrease in water use
- Second and third years: up to 50-60% total decrease in water use
- After 4-5 years with healthy trees: reduce applications to seasonal only

## Special Considerations

- If tea is applied to straight sand with no organic matter, organisms do not survive (no food resources)
- Do not plow after tea application on low-OM fields; organisms need food to survive
- If toxic chemicals are used, reapply tea until toxins are decomposed
- Air pollution will remove organisms regardless of tea application
- If inorganic fertilizer above 100 lbs/acre is applied, it becomes problematic for microorganisms; apply water first to move fertilizer into soil, then apply tea
- Sulphur applications take out some organisms; repeated applications compound the problem
- Tea with ice-minus bacteria can provide a couple degrees of frost protection
