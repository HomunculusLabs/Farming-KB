# Compost Tea Application Rates (Ingham Field Guide)

The Ingham Field Guide provides specific application rate recommendations for orchards, vegetable/flower row crops, and general scenarios. Rates are based on years of SFI testing and field observation.

## Standard Rates

- Foliar spray: 5 gallons of compost tea per acre for every 6 feet of plant height (canopy)
- Soil drench: 15-20 gallons of compost tea per acre
- Mildew alert or severe disease pressure: increase foliar rate to 10 gallons per acre
- Water is only a carrier -- add enough water to cover the area; backpack sprayers often apply tea straight without dilution

## Orchard Application Program

**Fall:**
- Put down 1-2 tons per acre of compost/wormcastings as [[ingham-actinomycetes-soil-biology-and-compost-tea]] (like pancake batter consistency)
- Do NOT use root gels -- they smother roots and prevent air passage; use yucca as a sticker if needed

**Disease and Pest Response:**
- When extension service issues disease or pest warnings, apply weekly until alert stops, then monthly until harvest
- If disease outbreak occurs, apply immediately every 3-5 days until controlled
- After any pesticide application, wait 3 days then resume compost tea program

## Vegetable/Flower Row Crop Program

**Fall:**
- Put down 1-5 tons per acre of compost/wormcastings as top dressing on crop residue

**Spring:**
- Soil drench: 15 gallons of compost tea per acre, 2 weeks before planting
- [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]]:**
- Foliar spray: 5 gallons per acre, drench foliage and stems every 10-14 days
- Start 2 weeks before bud break
- Do not spray when bees are pollinating
- Continue every 10-14 days until harvest

**Disease Response:**
- When warnings issued, apply weekly until alert stops, then every 10-14 days until harvest
- If outbreak occurs, apply every 3-5 days until stopped

## Timing Considerations

- Soil drenches: wait until soil is warm enough for organisms to grow
- Foliar sprays: organisms need 20 minutes to attach to leaf surfaces; if spraying in a downpour, use a sticker-spreader
- Soil drenches are okay anytime, even in rain
- Above-freezing temperatures are acceptable for application, as long as organisms get into soil before it freezes
- Winter applications are appropriate if soil temperatures allow establishment before freezing
- First year may require additional nutrients added in the tea while [[aact-spray-equipment-nozzle-selection-ingham]]:**
- Nozzle opening size must be greater than 200 micrometers (300-400 micrometers preferred)
- Pressure must be low relative to distance: 1-2 feet = 20-40 psi; 6 feet = 100 psi; 100 feet = 600 psi
- Impact pressure on leaf surfaces kills organisms, not pressure in the liquid itself
- Diaphragm pumps are clearly best; some centrifugal pumps are acceptable
- Transfer pumps can reduce all organism numbers by 50% in a single pass
- Electrostatic sprayers kill organisms; avoid them
- Pivot irrigation, airplanes, and large droplet systems are acceptable (large droplets protect organisms)
