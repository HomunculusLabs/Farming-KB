## The Solution: Wider Spacing

The foundation of low-irrigation gardening is putting plants much farther apart than intensive methods recommend. This counterintuitive approach works for several key reasons:

1. **Reduced competition**: Each plant has access to a larger volume of soil moisture and nutrients, allowing it to develop a deeper and more extensive root system
2. **Larger root zones**: Well-spaced plants develop root systems that can access moisture from a much larger soil volume, making them more drought-resistant
3. **Better nutrition**: Solomon's variety trials showed that well-separated plants got much larger, tasted better, and in many cases yielded more per unit area than crowded plants
4. **Reduced disease pressure**: Better air circulation between plants reduces fungal disease incidence
5. **Lower water demand**: Fewer plants per unit area means less total water needed

## Historical Precedents

### British Wartime Allotments (1930s-1950s)

During World War II, the UK expanded allotments (community garden plots) into a national institution. Each plot was legally required to be at least 300 square yards (2,700 square feet or about 250 square meters). People spent time on their allotments after work, and Sunday afternoons became social events at the gardens. During hard times, having 2,700 square feet of vegetable garden made the difference between health and sickness, between having enough to eat and low-grade hunger.

### Cuban Urban Gardens (post-1991)

After the fall of the Soviet Union, Cuba lost its primary trade partner and source of petroleum-based agricultural inputs. The country responded by converting large cooperative sugar farms to individual holdings and freely granting garden blocks (one-third acre each) at the edges of towns and cities. Today, half of the produce consumed in Havana is grown in urban gardens, and urban gardens produce 60% of all vegetables consumed in Cuba. All of this produce is organically grown.

The Cuban experience demonstrates that a temperate climate area can produce two crops per year. Solomon's own garden in Tasmania (with a climate similar to Oregon's) uses about 2,000 square feet of growing area to feed a family of two, producing most of their daily caloric intake from the garden.

### Plant Spacing

- Space plants 2-4 times further apart than intensive recommendations
- Use the full spread of the mature plant as a guide, then add 50% more
- Root crops need particularly wide spacing for adequate moisture access

### Water Conservation Techniques

- **Mulch heavily**: Apply 4-6 inches of organic mulch to reduce evaporation
- **Water deeply and infrequently**: When you do irrigate, water deeply to encourage deep root growth rather than frequent shallow watering
- **Water at the base**: Avoid overhead watering; use drip irrigation or soaker hoses directed at the root zone
- **Time watering appropriately**: Water early morning or late evening to minimize evaporation losses
- **Choose appropriate crops**: Some vegetables are naturally more drought-tolerant than others
