# Fungal Elicitors for Enhanced Secondary Metabolite Production

## Overview

Elicitors are signalling molecules that trigger formation of secondary
metabolites in plant cell cultures by inducing plant defence, hypersensitive
response, and/or pathogenesis-related proteins. Fungal elicitors have been most
widely studied for enhancing synthesis of commercially important compounds
from plant cell cultures, opening new avenues for pharmaceutical and food
industry applications.

## Elicitor Classification

### Biotic Elicitors

Biotic elicitors originate from [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]] and include a diverse range
of molecular classes:

- **Polysaccharides**: Cell wall-derived glucans, chitosan, and pectin
  fragments from fungi and plants. Beta-glucans from fungal cell walls are
  among the most potent biotic elicitors, triggering defence responses in a
  wide range of plant cell cultures.
- **Proteins and glycoproteins**: Elicitin proteins produced by
  Phytophthora and Pythium species are well-characterized protein elicitors.
  These small (~10 kDa) extracellular proteins induce hypersensitive responses
  and phytoalexin accumulation in compatible plant species.
- **Oligosaccharides**: Specific oligosaccharide fragments derived from
  fungal or plant cell wall degradation can act as highly specific elicitors.
  The concept of "oligosaccharins" recognizes that these small sugar
  molecules function as regulatory signals at very low concentrations.
- **Lipids and fatty acids**: Certain fungal lipids, including arachidonic
  acid and eicosapentaenoic acid, can elicit defence responses and secondary
  metabolite production in plant cell cultures.
Fungal elicitors are the most extensively studied type of biotic elicitor.
They are broadly classified by their source organism: [[Cordyceps]],
[[Auxin]], and [[biodiversity-of-fungi-endophytic-fungi]] each produce distinct elicitor profiles
with different effects on plant metabolism.

### Abiotic Elicitors

Abiotic elicitors are non-biological stress factors that stimulate secondary
metabolite production:

- **Metal ions**: Copper, silver, and cadmium ions at sub-lethal
  concentrations can trigger [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]] responses that lead to
  enhanced metabolite production.
- **UV radiation**: Ultraviolet light exposure activates stress-responsive
  pathways including phenylpropanoid biosynthesis.

## Key Principles

- No elicitor has been found to have a general effect on many culture systems
- No system responds to all elicitors
- Screening is essential for each culture system and desired compound
- Three critical variables determine maximum response:
  1. **Age of culture**: Optimal induction occurs at late exponential or
     early stationary phase
  2. **Concentration of elicitor**: Varies with elicitor type and culture
     system
  3. **Incubation time**: Contact time with elicitor affects metabolite
     accumulation

## Pathogenic vs. Symbiotic Fungal Elicitors

### Pathogenic Fungi

Most studies have used pathogenic fungi as elicitor sources. These fungi
induce hypersensitive response in plant cells, activating defence pathways
and increasing phytoalexin production. Examples include various Fusarium,
Verticillium, and Phytophthora species.
