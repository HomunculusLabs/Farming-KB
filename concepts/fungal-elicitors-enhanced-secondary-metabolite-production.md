# Fungal Elicitors for Enhanced Secondary Metabolite Production

## Overview

Elicitors are signalling molecules that trigger formation of secondary metabolites in plant cell cultures by inducing plant defence, hypersensitive response, and/or pathogenesis-related proteins. Fungal elicitors have been most widely studied for enhancing synthesis of commercially important compounds from plant cell cultures, opening new avenues for pharmaceutical and food industry applications.

## Classification of Elicitors

### Biotic Elicitors (biological origin)
- Polysaccharides, proteins, glycoproteins
- Cell-wall fragments derived from fungi, bacteria, and plants
- Fungal elicitors are the most extensively studied type

### Abiotic Elicitors (non-biological origin)
- Metal ions, UV light, chemically defined compounds
- Also referred to as "abiotic stress"

## Key Principles

- No elicitor has been found to have a general effect on many culture systems
- No system responds to all elicitors
- Screening is essential for each culture system and desired compound
- Three critical variables determine maximum response:
  1. **Age of culture**: Optimal induction occurs at late exponential or early stationary phase
  2. **Concentration of elicitor**: Varies with elicitor type and culture system
  3. **Incubation time**: Contact time with elicitor affects metabolite accumulation

## Pathogenic vs. Symbiotic Fungal Elicitors

### Pathogenic Fungi
Most studies have used pathogenic fungi as elicitor sources. These fungi induce hypersensitive response in plant cells, activating defence pathways and increasing phytoalexin production. Examples include various Fusarium, Verticillium, and Phytophthora species.

### Piriformospora indica
A breakthrough came with the discovery of Piriformospora indica (Varma and Franken 1997), an endophytic axenically cultivable fungus that mimics arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal capabilities. Unlike obligate AM fungi, P. indica can grow in synthetic media, enabling:
- Study of symbiotic fungus elicitation effects
- Preparation of standardized elicitor preparations
- Combined elicitation from both pathogenic and symbiotic fungal sources

## Case Study: Podophyllotoxin Production in Linum album

### Podophyllotoxin
- Promising secondary metabolite with pronounced cytotoxic activity
- Used for synthesis of anticancer drugs: etoposide, teniposide, etopophos
- Treatment of testicular and lung cancers and certain leukemias
- Natural sources: Podophyllum hexandrum rhizomes (approximately 4% dry weight) and P. peltatum (lower amounts)
- P. hexandrum listed in CITES Appendix II (trade restrictions)
- Chemical synthesis possible but hampered by complicated stereochemical ring closure

### Linum album Cell Cultures
- Iranian flax (Linum album) tissue cultures have become attractive alternatives
- Cell cultures reported to produce lignans with highest productivity
- Elicitation with P. indica culture filtrate and cell extract enhances podophyllotoxin yield

### Elicitor Preparation from P. indica
1. Grow P. indica in defined liquid culture medium
2. Separate mycelium from culture filtrate by filtration
3. **Culture filtrate elicitor**: Sterilize filtrate through 0.22 mm filter, store at 4 degrees C
4. **Cell extract elicitor**: Homogenize mycelium, extract, sterilize, store at 4 degrees C

## Strategies for Enhanced Production

1. **Media manipulation**: Hormonal and nutrient stress
2. **Environmental conditions**: Temperature, pH, osmotic stress
3. **Precursor addition**: Supply biosynthetic pathway precursors
4. **Elicitation**: Application of biotic or abiotic elicitors
5. **Combination strategies**: Multiple approaches simultaneously
6. **Metabolic engineering**: Genetic manipulation of biosynthetic pathways

## Significance

- Plant cell culture systems are viable alternatives for producing commercially important secondary metabolites
- Elicitation provides a non-genetic method for boosting metabolite yields
- Symbiotic fungal elicitors (like P. indica) offer advantages over pathogenic sources
- Reduces pressure on wild harvesting of medicinal plants
- Enables sustainable production of pharmaceutical compounds
