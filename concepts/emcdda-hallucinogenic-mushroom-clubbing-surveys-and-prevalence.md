# EMCDDA Hallucinogenic Mushroom Clubbing Surveys and Prevalence

## Overview

Targeted surveys conducted in dance music and clubbing settings across Europe
revealed substantially higher prevalence of [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-school-surveys-adolescent-use]]. These non-probability surveys, conducted
in Belgium, France, Hungary, Italy, the Netherlands, and the UK, demonstrated
that [[health-risks-and-harm-reduction-for-hallucinogenic-mushroom-use]] was strongly associated with nightclub and
dance music cultures, with prevalence depending on country, city location,
lifestyle aspects, and ease of access to mushrooms.

## Clubbing Survey Prevalence Estimates

Surveys in various clubbing and dance music settings produced lifetime
prevalence estimates ranging from 9% to 55%:

## Polydrug Use Patterns

Within club sample populations, polydrug use tended to cluster in the same
individuals. In the UK South East England nightclub survey (2000):

- Overall lifetime prevalence for hallucinogenic mushroom use among all
  760 clubbers was 24%.
- Among current drug users in the sample (those who had used cannabis or any
  other drug during the past month), prevalence rose to 44%.
- In comparison, lifetime prevalence among 16 to 19 year olds in the
  general population in 2000 was only 11%.

This pattern indicates that hallucinogenic mushroom use was strongly
concentrated among regular polydrug users within clubbing environments.

## Mixmag Trend Data (2000 to 2005)

The only available trend data on hallucinogenic mushroom use came from six
consecutive annual readership surveys conducted by the UK clubbing magazine
Mixmag (2000 to 2005):

- Lifetime prevalence estimates increased notably between 2003 and 2004.
- Past-month prevalence increased significantly from 2.4% in 2002 to 18% in
  2004.
- The average age for first use was between 18 and 19 years according to the
  2004 Mixmag sample.
