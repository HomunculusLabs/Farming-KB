## Water Stability of Aggregates

Water-stable aggregates resist disintegration during rainfall or irrigation,
preventing surface sealing, crusting, and erosion. AMF contribution to water-
stable aggregation is measured through wet-sieving procedures that determine the
proportion of soil remaining aggregated after immersion and agitation.

Key factors mediating fungal effects on aggregate water stability include:

- **AMF species identity**: Different AMF taxa vary in their hyphal
  characteristics (diameter, branching pattern, persistence) and GRSP
  production capacity. Species with thicker, more persistent hyphae generally
  provide greater aggregate stabilization.
- **Plant host effects**: The identity and growth characteristics of host plants
  influence AMF-mediated aggregation through root system architecture, exudate
  composition, and carbon allocation to fungal partners. Grasses with dense,
  fibrous root systems typically promote greater aggregate formation than
  species with coarse roots.
- **Soil texture and mineralogy**: Clay content and mineralogy influence the
  effectiveness of fungal binding mechanisms. Expandable clay minerals (smectite
  group) form stronger organo-mineral associations with fungal products than
  kaolinite or sand-dominated soils.
- **Land management**: Tillage disrupts hyphal networks and reduces GRSP
