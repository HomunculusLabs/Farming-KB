## Overview

Cannabis extraction is the process of separating cannabinoids, terpenes, and other desirable compounds from plant material. Extracts and concentrates offer higher potency, alternative consumption methods, and purified cannabinoid profiles. Extraction methods range from simple mechanical techniques to complex chemical processes requiring specialized equipment.

## Types of Extraction

### Solvent-Based Extraction

Solvent-based methods use a chemical solvent to dissolve cannabinoids and terpenes from plant material, then evaporate the solvent to leave behind the concentrated extract.

#### Ethanol Extraction

[[ethanol]] (food-grade grain alcohol) is a versatile solvent capable of extracting a full spectrum of cannabinoids and terpenes.

**Process**:
1. Plant material is soaked or washed with cold ethanol (cryogenic extraction preserves terpenes)
2. The ethanol-cannabinoid solution is filtered to remove plant matter
3. Ethanol is evaporated under vacuum using a rotary evaporator or falling film evaporator
4. The remaining crude oil is further refined if desired

**Advantages**:
- Food-grade, generally recognized as safe (GRAS)
- Extracts a full spectrum of compounds
- Scalable from small to commercial operations
- No toxic residue concerns

**Disadvantages**:
- Also extracts chlorophyll, waxes, and lipids (requires winterization to remove)
- Lower terpene preservation than hydrocarbon methods unless cryogenic
- Highly flammable — requires proper ventilation and explosion-proof equipment

#### Butane Hash Oil (BHO) / Hydrocarbon Extraction

Butane (or propane, or a blend) is used as a solvent in a closed-loop system to extract cannabinoids and terpenes.

**Process**:
1. Cannabis is packed into an extraction column
2. Liquid butane is passed through the column, dissolving cannabinoids and terpenes
3. The butane-oil solution is collected in a chamber
4. Butane is recovered and recycled in the closed-loop system
5. Remaining oil is purged of residual solvent using a vacuum oven

**Advantages**:
- Excellent terpene preservation
- Produces high-quality shatter, wax, and live resin
- Efficient — high yields with relatively low solvent volumes
- Butane is fully recoverable in closed-loop systems

**Disadvantages**:
- Highly flammable and explosive — open-blasting is extremely dangerous and illegal in many jurisdictions
- Requires expensive closed-loop equipment for legal operation
- Residual solvent testing is required for consumer safety
- Butane quality matters — low-grade butane leaves contaminants

#### Supercritical CO2 Extraction

Carbon dioxide is pressurized and heated beyond its critical point (supercritical state), where it behaves as both a liquid and a gas, making it an effective solvent.

**Process**:
1. CO2 is pressurized to 5,000-10,000 psi and heated
2. Supercritical CO2 passes through cannabis material, extracting cannabinoids
3. The CO2-cannabinoid mixture enters a separator vessel
4. Pressure is reduced, causing CO2 to revert to gas and separate from the oil
5. CO2 is recycled back into the system

**Advantages**:
- No toxic solvent residue (CO2 evaporates completely)
- Tunable — pressure and temperature adjustments can selectively extract specific compounds
- Non-flammable
- Produces clean, consistent extracts
- Generally regarded as safe for medical-grade products

**Disadvantages**:
- Expensive equipment ($50,000-$500,000+)
- Lower terpene preservation than hydrocarbon extraction
- Requires skilled operators
- Not practical for home use

### Solventless Extraction

#### Rosin Press

Mechanical extraction using heat and pressure to squeeze resin from cannabis flower or hash.

**Process**:
1. Place flower, kief, or bubble hash between parchment paper
2. Apply heat (180-220 F for flower; 160-200 F for hash) and pressure (1-15 tons)
3. Hold for 30 seconds to 4 minutes depending on material and desired consistency
4. Collect the rosin from the parchment paper

**Advantages**:
- No solvents — safest method
- Preserves full terpene and cannabinoid profile
- Relatively affordable equipment ($100-$3,000 for home units)
- No risk of residual solvents
- Can be done at home legally in most jurisdictions

**Disadvantages**:
- Lower yields than solvent-based methods (15-30% by weight vs 20-30%+)
- Quality depends heavily on starting material
- Manual labor-intensive for larger batches

#### Water Extraction / Ice Water Hash

Uses ice and water to separate trichome heads from plant material through agitation and filtration.

**Process**:
1. Mix cannabis with ice and cold water in a bucket
2. Agitate (stir or use a washing machine) for 10-30 minutes
3. Pour the mixture through a series of mesh bubble bags (progressively smaller micron sizes)
4. Trichome heads collect in the bags; plant matter passes through
5. Collect and dry the hash from each bag separately

**Micron sizes**:
- 120-90 micron: Full melt quality, highest purity
- 73-45 micron: Good quality, common yield
- 25 micron: Fine grade, often lower quality

See [[cannabis-concentrates-types]] for details on bubble hash, dry sift, and other water-extracted products.

## Winterization

A post-extraction refinement process for ethanol extracts:

1. Dissolve crude extract in ethanol (if not already in solution)
2. Freeze at -20 C or below for 24-48 hours
3. Filter through a Buchner funnel with filter paper or centrifuge
4. Waxes, lipids, and chlorophyll precipitate out and are removed
5. Ethanol is evaporated from the filtered solution

Winterization is not typically needed for BHO or CO2 extracts that are processed correctly.

## Decarboxylation

Raw cannabis contains THCA and CBDA (acidic, non-intoxicating forms). [[decarboxylation]] converts these to active THC and CBD through heat:

- THCA → THC at approximately 220 F (105 C) over 30-60 minutes
- CBDA → CBD at similar temperatures

Decarboxylation is necessary for any extract intended for oral consumption (edibles, tinctures). See [[cannabis-edibles-guide]] and [[cannabis-tinctures-guide]].
