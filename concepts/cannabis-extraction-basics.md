## Overview
Cannabis extraction is the process of separating cannabinoids, terpenes, and other desirable compounds from plant material. Extracts and concentrates offer higher potency, alternative consumption methods, and purified cannabinoid profiles. Extraction methods range from simple mechanical techniques like dry sifting to advanced laboratory processes using solvents, supercritical fluids, or heat and pressure. The choice of extraction method significantly impacts the final product's cannabinoid and terpene profile, purity, safety, and intended use.

## Major Extraction Categories

### Solvent-Based Extraction
Solvent-based methods use chemical solvents to dissolve target compounds from plant material. Common solvents include ethanol, butane, propane, and supercritical carbon dioxide. Each solvent has different selectivity — ethanol extracts a broad spectrum of compounds including water-soluble chlorophyll, while butane preferentially extracts non-polar cannabinoids and terpenes, producing a more flavorful product. Post-extraction, the solvent must be fully purged from the final product through evaporation, vacuum purging, or winterization, making proper equipment and ventilation essential for safety. See [[cannabis-extraction-solvent-properties-and-safety]] for detailed solvent comparisons.

### Mechanical Extraction
Mechanical methods require no chemical solvents and instead use physical separation. Dry sifting (kief collection) uses screens of varying micron sizes to separate trichome heads from plant material. Ice water extraction (hashish production) agitates cannabis in ice-cold water, where the cold causes trichome heads to become brittle and detach. The resulting slurry is filtered through mesh bags of decreasing size to collect hash of different grades. Rosin pressing applies heat and pressure to squeeze resin from flower or hash, producing a solventless extract popular for its clean flavor profile.

### Hydrocarbon Extraction
Butane and propane extraction (BHO/PHO) produces high-potency extracts with excellent terpene preservation. The solvent is passed through packed cannabis material in a closed-loop system, dissolving cannabinoids and terpenes. The solution is then collected and the solvent is evaporated off using vacuum ovens. Modern closed-loop systems have largely replaced open-blasting methods, dramatically improving safety. The resulting extract can be processed further into shatter, wax, budder, or other consistencies depending on post-processing techniques.

### Supercritical CO2 Extraction
Supercritical carbon dioxide extraction uses CO2 at temperatures and pressures above its critical point (31.1°C, 1071 psi), where it exhibits properties of both a liquid and a gas. This method allows precise control over which compounds are extracted by adjusting temperature and pressure parameters. CO2 extraction is considered one of the cleanest methods since CO2 leaves no residue and is non-toxic and non-flammable. The equipment is expensive, making this method more common in commercial operations than home extraction. See [[cannabis-extraction-methods-overview]] for method comparisons.

## Key Quality Factors
The quality of a cannabis extract depends on starting material quality, extraction method, and post-processing. Fresh, properly cured flower with high trichome density produces the best extracts. Temperature control during extraction is critical — excessive heat degrades terpenes and converts THCA to THC prematurely. Winterization (dissolving crude extract in ethanol and freezing to precipitate waxes and lipids) improves purity. Final products should be tested for residual solvents, pesticides, and potency through a licensed laboratory.

## Safety Protocols
All solvent-based extraction carries inherent risks. Ethanol is highly flammable. Hydrocarbons like butane and propane are extremely volatile and can accumulate in enclosed spaces, creating explosion hazards. Proper ventilation, explosion-proof equipment, and personal protective equipment are non-negotiable. Even mechanical methods like rosin pressing involve heat and pressure that can cause burns if equipment fails. The [[cannabis-testing-and-quality]] standards for residual solvents set maximum allowable limits that all commercial products must meet before sale.
