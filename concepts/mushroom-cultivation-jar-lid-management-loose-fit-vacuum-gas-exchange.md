## Lids During Sterilization

### Loose Placement

The guide emphasizes that jar lids must be placed on top of the jars but
NOT screwed tight during sterilization in the pressure cooker. This is
essential for several reasons:

- **Pressure equalization**: During sterilization, the air and moisture
  inside the jar expand as temperature rises. If the lid is screwed tight,
  the expanding gas creates pressure inside the jar that can cause the jar
  to crack or explode under autoclave conditions
- **Steam penetration**: A loose lid allows steam to reach the substrate
  during sterilization, ensuring that all surfaces of the grass seed are
  exposed to sterilizing temperatures. A tight lid would create a barrier
  that could prevent complete sterilization
- **Moisture retention**: Although the lid is loose, the [[pf-tek-substrate-moisture-diagnosis-and-correction]]. The guide warns
to always check jars for cracks before shaking. The loose lid allows the
jar to be shaken without creating a vacuum that would make the substrate
impossible to redistribute.

### Cooling Phase

During cooling, the lids should remain loose. As the jar contents cool
from sterilization temperature (121 degrees C) to room temperature (20
degrees C), the air and steam inside the jar contract. If the lid were
tight, this contraction would pull a vacuum inside the jar. A vacuum seal
makes it extremely difficult to open the jar later and can also draw
contaminated air through any microscopic gaps in the lid seal, defeating
the purpose of sterilization.

A loose lid allows air to enter the jar as it cools, preventing vacuum
formation. This is the most important practical reason for keeping lids
loose throughout the process.

## Lids During Inoculation

### Brief Opening

When inoculating, the jar lid is opened just a crack, sufficient to
introduce the inoculant ([[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] needle, agar square, or mycelium
syringe needle). The guide emphasizes working swiftly to minimize the
time the substrate is exposed to open air. Even a simple cardboard box
hood prevents drafts and reduces the chance of airborne contamination
landing in the jar during this vulnerable moment.

### Immediate Closing After Inoculation

After the inoculant is introduced, the lid is closed immediately and the
jar is shaken to distribute the inoculant among the grass seed kernels.
Shaking after inoculation spreads the spores, mycelium, or agar piece
across multiple kernels, dramatically speeding up colonization by creating
multiple inoculation points rather than a single colony that must spread
from one location.
