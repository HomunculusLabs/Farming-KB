# Permaculture Broadscale No-Till Grain Production

## Fukuoka's Revolution in Grain Cultivation

Until Mollison read mollison-designers-thatch-and-natural-roofing-in-permaculture. The system outlined in The One-Straw Revolution (Rodale, 1975) solved the problems of no-dig grain cultivation.

The approach combines the usual rotation of legume/grain/root crop/pasture/fallow/legume into a single grain/legume mixed crop — what Mollison calls "simultaneous rotation." Any smallholder can, without tractor or machinery, produce a heavy crop of grains and legumes if this method is practised.

## Principles of the System

The core principles enabling small areas (400 m² or less) to support a family of five on grain are:

### Oats (Avena sativa)

Long-day crop, best in [[greenhouse-cool-climates-mollison]]. Neutral soils preferred. Self-pollinated. Lodges with high nitrogen, so needs less than other grains. Sow at 50–200 kg/ha. Water needed at flowering. Good yields: 3,000 kg/ha.

### Quinoa (Chenopodium quinoa)

Grown in South America at high altitudes. Tolerant of soils and salts. Spring-sown at 10–15 kg/ha. Birds are a problem. Approximately 1,000 varieties available. Good varieties yield 2,000–3,000 kg/ha.

### Teff (Eragrostis tef)

Neutral daylength. White-seeded types for summer-dry seasons, brown-seeded for summer-wet. Drought-resistant but needs shelter when flowering. Self-pollinating. Sow in spring at 10–12 kg/ha. Yields approximately 2,000 kg/ha.

### Millet (red, white, broom corn)

Treat as for maize. Plant soaked seed 10 days later than maize at 9 kg/ha. All varieties grow quickly and need little water. Good crop where other grains have failed. Can be fed directly to poultry. Seed stores well.

### Maize (Zea mays)

Short-day plant suited to 40° latitude and subtropics. Stands slight frost only. Varieties seed from 50–130 days. Cross-pollinated by wind, so needs tall windbreaks. Sow at 15–30 kg/ha. Often followed by wheat or barley, rotated with peas, peanuts, soya beans. Good interplant: climbing beans (twine on stalks) or broad beans. Melons or pumpkins at ground level. Yields: 1,200–1,500 kg/ha.

## Legume Integration

[[fukuoka-white-clover-cover-crop-system]] at 1 kg/ha serves as the primary [[comparison-living-mulch-vs-cover-crops]] and nitrogen fixer. Other legumes can be substituted: Fukuoka used 12 wattle trees (silver wattle) per hectare instead of clover for tree-crop systems.

Legumes such as lab-lab bean (Dolichos lab-lab) under Borassus palm serve as perennial systems, and a friend of Yeomans uses lab-lab with barley to great effect in annual grain culture.

## Seed Pelleting to Deter Birds

Where sparrows are a problem, grains are mixed with mud, pressed through wire-mesh, and rolled into small balls. Alternatively, dampened seed is shaken in a tray of clay dust to form mud-coated pellets. Pellets can also be formed by extruding mud and grain through a domestic mincer onto a vibrating table of dust or flour.
