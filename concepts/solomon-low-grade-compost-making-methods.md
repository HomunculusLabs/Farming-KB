## Low-Grade Compost Making Methods

## Overview

In *Gardening When It Counts*, Steve Solomon distinguishes between low-grade compost (suitable for mulching ornamentals and fruit trees) and high-grade compost (capable of effectively [[cannabis-chemical-fertilizers]] and many [[solomon-manure-quality-variable-nutrient-content-horse-steer-chicken]].

### High-Grade Compost (Brief Overview)

High-grade compost requires:

- Careful C:N ratio balancing, proper [[mushroom-casing-moisture-management]], and regular turning
- Attention to temperature (must heat sufficiently to kill pathogens and weed seeds)
- Curing time for stabilization

Solomon considers it a "highly skilled activity" that cannot be made simple, and dedicates most of his composting chapter to these techniques.

## Making Low-Grade Compost: Practical Methods

### Method 1: The Casual Pile

The simplest approach to low-grade composting:

1. **Choose a location**: Shaded area, 3-4 feet square, with decent drainage
2. **Accumulate materials**: Add grass clippings, leaves, kitchen scraps as available
3. **Mix when convenient**: Turn the pile with a fork periodically
4. **Wait**: In 6-12 months, the bottom will be dark, crumbly material
5. **Sift and use**: Separate finished compost from undecomposed material

Main pitfalls: grass mats and goes anaerobic, kitchen scraps attract pests, weed seeds survive without sufficient heat, nutrient content is low and variable.
