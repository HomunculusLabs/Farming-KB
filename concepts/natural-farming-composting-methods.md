# Natural Farming Composting Methods

Natural farming employs several advanced composting methods that go far beyond traditional composting. The centerpiece is Fermented Mixed Compost (FMC), a potent, enzyme-rich compost fortified with [[korean-natural-farming]] inputs and indigenous microorganisms. The system also includes [[bokashi]]-style designer composts and IMO-enriched composts.

## Fermented Mixed Compost (FMC)

FMC is quite similar to farmyard manure or ordinary compost, but the quality is completely different. It is very effective in promoting micronutrient absorption, enhancing plant hormone effectiveness, and providing vitamins.

### Seasonal Considerations

- **Summer** (hot, humid): Favorable for bacteria to multiply
- **Late fall to winter** (cool, dry): Favorable for fermenting microbes to multiply

FMC should ideally be made in late fall. During this season, fermenting microbes can utilize the compost without hindrance from bacteria, allowing proper sugar adaptation.

### Location

Choose a place with:
- Little wind
- Good drainage
- Water availability
- Soil floor (NOT concrete -- concrete causes moisture emission in all directions when FMC heats up, causing decay and loss of fertilizing value)
- Shade from direct sunlight (no transparent vinyl film in greenhouses)
- If floor must be covered, use 1 foot of soil

### Materials

- IMO-4 (10% of total materials)
- Organic matter (oil cake, fish waste, bone meal, bean oil cake, eggshells)
- Natural farming inputs (FPJ, FAA, OHN, etc.)
- Shovel or mixing machine
- Jute sacks for storage
- Powdered oyster/crab/shrimp/eggshells (excellent calcium addition)

Include at least one item from each category:
- **Garden**: Fallen leaves or fruits
- **Paddy**: Rice bran, straw
- **Field**: Oil cake or bean cake
- **Sea**: Seaweed, fish waste

The major ingredients should be animal materials with abundant proteins (bone meal, fish meal) and plant materials.

### Method 1: Standard FMC

1. Mix oil cake, fish waste, bone meal, bean oil cake, and eggshells
2. Spray with water and add IMO-4 (10% of total materials)
3. Mix indoors, protected from direct sunlight and rain, on a soil floor
4. Total material should be more than 500 kg (difficult to maintain fermentation heat with smaller volumes)
5. Heap to 40 cm height (hot weather) or 80 cm (cold weather)
6. Adjust moisture to 60% with diluted (1:500) FPJ, FAA, OHN, and other inputs
   - Test: slightly wet to hand, barely maintains shape when squeezed
7. Add powdered oyster/crab/shrimp/eggshells for calcium
8. Cover with rice straw mat to control moisture, temperature, and propagation
9. **Temperature management is critical**:
   - Day 2: First turning (temperature reaches ~50C)
   - Days 5-6: Second turning
   - Days 8-10: Third turning
   - If temperature reaches 70C, nutrients and liquids evaporate -- must not exceed this
10. After 20 days, FMC is complete

**Quality test**: Well-made FMC has a sweet smell. If it stinks, it is not fermented properly (deterioration). Maggots indicate poor turning or excessive moisture.

11. Store in jute/gunny bags in cool shade. Can be stored for about one year.

### Method 2: Quick IMO-Enriched Compost

1. Mix IMO-4 with compost materials (oil cake, fowl droppings, pig manure, cow manure, fish meal, commercial organic fertilizer) at 1:10 ratio
2. Ferment for 7-14 days
3. Add cow bone powder, shrimp shell, or crab shell
4. Ferment for more than a week
5. The result is IMO-5

## Hormone Production in FMC

FMC contains many hormones due to its diverse ingredients (FPJ, FAA, etc.), and additional hormones form during fermentation:

- **Auxin**: Produced by yeast and filamentous fungi
- **Gibberellins**: From red fungi
- **Cytokinins**: From germs and yeast -- promote leaf/branch growth, cell division, embryo/seed formation, flower bud formation, germination, and prevent aging

## Application

- Apply 2-3 hours before sunset (avoid hot sun) or on cloudy days
- UV rays from sun can affect microorganisms
- Cover FMC with soil right after applying, or lightly plow with 1-2 inch rotary hoe
- Partial topsoil application is better than deep burial (microorganisms survive better)
- Surface application: spread FMC under rice straw or any mulching
- **Small lump form** is better and more sustainable than smashed form

### Liquid FMC
Place FMC in cloth, immerse in water. Add FPJ, LAB (1:1000) for even better results. Spray on crops in evening or at night.

## Designer Compost (Bokashi)

A more fortified version similar to Japanese bokashi, incorporating macro/micronutrients and biodiversed beneficial microorganisms.

### Basic Philippine Recipe
- Rice bran: 10 kg
- Copra meal: 20 kg
- Coco peat: 20 kg
- Chicken manure: 30 kg
- Charcoal dust: 20 kg
- BIM (Beneficial Indigenous Microorganisms): 1 liter
- Molasses: 1 kg
- Bionutrient: 1 liter

### General Formulation
- 80% carbon source (carbohydrate)
- 17% nitrogen source (protein)
- 3% trace elements (vitamins/minerals)

### Substitutions
- Rice bran -> wheat or any grain bran
- Copra meal -> corn meal or legume meal
- Coco peat -> peat moss, sawdust, or high-carbon materials
- Chicken manure -> any animal manure (chicken preferred for higher NPK and micronutrients)

## LAB in Composting

LAB is extremely effective for composting:
- Prevents fermented mixed compost from decaying
- When used with IMO or mixed compost, fermentation occurs rapidly
- Neutralizes ammonia gas from immature compost
- Arrests foul odors associated with anaerobic decomposition

## Soil Treatment Solution (SOS)

A comprehensive soil conditioner applied per acre:
- IMO: 500 ml
- FPJ: 500 ml
- OHN: 500 ml
- BRV: 500 ml
- LAB: 300 ml
- WCaP: 300 ml
- FAA: 300 ml
- Charcoal: 50 kg
- Soil: 500 kg
- Seawater: 3 liters
