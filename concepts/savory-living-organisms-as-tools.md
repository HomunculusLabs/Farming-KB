### The Green Revolution and Its Limits

The Green Revolution of extravagantly productive hybrid crops foundered on
the assumption that technology could supplant succession. The high-yielding
strains owed their productivity to extreme uniformity and an engineered ability
to make good use of fertilizer, but they required lavish protection from
insects and disease. The combination of chemicals damaged living soil, polluted
the environment, increased insect damage, generated resistant pests, and
escalated costs so severely that thousands of American farmers left their land.

### Genetic Engineering Considerations

Modern breakthroughs in genetic engineering open tremendous possibilities and
temptations. Civilization might have spared itself grief had it gained more
wisdom about [[fungi-in-ecosystem-processes-dighton]] before acquiring this new power. Traditional
breeding has already produced species like corn that depend totally on human
cultivation. Genetic engineering merely shortens this process but new forms do
not escape the laws of succession any more than corn or sheep do. Every
organism must take its place in succession.
