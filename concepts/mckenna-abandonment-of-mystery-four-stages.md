# Abandonment of the Mystery: Four Stages of Profanation

## Overview

In *Food of the Gods*, Terence McKenna proposes a four-stage model for how humanity progressively lost its original psychosymbiotic relationship with psilocybin-containing mushrooms. He argues that the abandonment of the original catalyst for the emergence of self-reflection and language — the [[mckenna-stropharia-cubensis-extraterrestrial-hypothesis-spore-dispersal]] psilocybin-containing mushroom — was not a single event but a gradual process spanning millennia. Each stage represents a further dilution of awareness of the power and the numinous meaning resident in the mystery.

## Stage One: Substitution with Other Psychoactive Plants

The first step away from the symbiosis of the human-fungal partnership that characterized the early pastoralist societies was the introduction of other psychoactive plant substitutes for the original mushroom. This psychoactivity could range from being equal in the depths of its profundity to the Stropharia cubensis psilocybin intoxication, as in the case with the classical hallucinogens of the New World tropics, to being relatively trivial.

### The Climate Change Scenario

In the case of Stropharia cubensis in Africa, a gradual trivialization scenario is proposed. With changes in climate, frequent if not continual low levels of mushroom ingestion gradually gave way to use that was merely seasonal. Conscious ceremonial use of mushrooms was at its apex during this seasonal availability phase, which may have lasted many thousands of years.

### The Honey Connection

As mushrooms and mushroom ecologies grew more rare, efforts may have been made to preserve mushrooms by drying and by preserving them in honey. Since honey itself easily ferments into an alcoholic intoxicant, it is possible that over time a practice of mixing fewer and fewer mushrooms in more and more honey may have encouraged replacement of the mushroom cult with a cult of mead. McKenna considers this the most consequential shift possible in social values — the gradual changeover of a psilocybin cult to an alcohol cult.

### Substitutes Ranging from Powerful to Trivial

Examples of Soma substitutes include:
- **Classical New World hallucinogens** — Still profoundly psychoactive, capable of inducing boundary-dissolving ecstasy
- **Ephedra** — A stimulant, considerably less profound in its effects
- **Fermented honey (mead)** — An intoxicant with sedative and disinhibiting properties, fundamentally different from the ego-dissolving effects of psilocybin

## Stage Two: Substitution of Inactive Materials

The second step in the abandonment of the original psychosymbiotic mystery was the substitution of completely inactive materials for active ones. In this situation, the substitutes, though usually still plants, were really no more than symbols of the former power of the mystery to authentically move initiates.

### Symbolic Replacement

At this stage, the ritual form persisted but the pharmacological reality behind it was lost. Plants continued to be used ceremonially, but they no longer contained psychoactive compounds capable of inducing the original ecstatic experience. The ceremony became a memory of an experience rather than the experience itself.

### Connection to the Rig Veda

The Vedas themselves may preserve echoes of this transition. The hymns to Soma describe ecstatic experiences that the actual substances available to later Vedic priests could not reproduce. The gap between the exuberant descriptions in the Rig Veda and the relatively mild effects of the candidate Soma plants (such as Ephedra) suggests that the original substance had been lost and replaced with increasingly inadequate substitutes.

## Stage Three: Purely Symbolic Religion

In the third stage of the process, symbols are all that is left. Not only are psychoactive plants now out of the picture, but plants of any sort have disappeared, and in their place are esoteric teachings and dogma, rituals, stress on lineages, gestures, and cosmogonic diagrams.

### The World's Major Religions

Today's major world religions are typical of this third stage. The living experience of the mystery has been replaced by:
- **Esoteric teachings** — Codified systems of belief rather than direct experience
- **Dogma** — Fixed beliefs about the nature of the divine
- **Rituals** — Formalized ceremonies whose original meaning has been forgotten
- **Lineages** — Authority based on hereditary or institutional succession
- **Cosmogonic diagrams** — Maps of reality that substitute for the experience of reality itself

### Loss of Direct Gnosis

The critical loss at this stage is the elimination of direct gnosis — personal, experiential knowledge of the sacred. Religion becomes mediated through priests, texts, and institutions rather than arising from the direct experience of the individual. The mystic is marginalized or persecuted.

## Stage Four: Complete Abandonment and Active Suppression

The third stage leads into yet another stage: the complete abandonment of even the pretense of remembering the felt experience of the mystery. This last stage is typified by secular scientism as perfected in the [[coleman-louis-savier-twentieth-century-maraicher]].

### Rediscovery as Threat

McKenna posits a further aspect of this fourth stage: the rediscovery of the mystery and its interpretation as evil and threatening to social values. The current suppression of [[mckenna-suppression-psychedelic-research-lsd-therapy]] and the hysteria fanned by pharmacophobic media is an obvious case in point. The dominator culture does not merely forget the mystery — it actively fears and suppresses it.

### The Secular Worldview

Secular scientism completes the process by denying that any "mystery" ever existed. Consciousness is reduced to neurochemistry, religious experience is dismissed as hallucination or psychopathology, and the entire history of humanity's relationship with psychoactive plants is reinterpreted through the lens of pathology and addiction.

## The Pattern in Historical Context

### Minoan Crete as Transitional

Minoan civilization represents a transitional phase between stages one and two. On the island of Crete where the Goddess was still supreme, there are no signs of war. The economy prospered and the arts flourished. Even when in the fifteenth century B.C. the island finally came under Achaean dominion, the Goddess and the way of thinking and living she symbolized still appear to have held fast.

### The Eleusinian Mysteries

The Eleusinian Mysteries represent perhaps the last institutional outpost of stage one in the Western world. For nearly two thousand years, the Mysteries at Eleusis provided a genuine experience of the sacred through what was almost certainly a psychoactive potion, the kykeon. The suppression of the Mysteries in the fourth century C.E. marks the final triumph of stage three/four in the Mediterranean world.

### The Dionysian Cults

The Dionysian cults, which coexisted with the Eleusinian Mysteries, represent another manifestation of the struggle to maintain stage one against the encroachment of stages two and three. Dionysus was the god of ecstasy, of the dissolution of boundaries, and his cult was repeatedly suppressed and revived throughout antiquity.

## Implications

### The Narcissism of the Dominator Culture

McKenna argues that the loss of the mystery has made humanity ill with narcissism. Without the periodic dissolution of ego boundaries that the original mushroom sacrament provided, the ego becomes hypertrophied and the individual becomes alienated from nature, from community, and from the transcendent dimension of existence.

### The Archaic Revival

The four-stage model provides the theoretical basis for McKenna's call for an "Archaic Revival" — a recovery of the original relationship between humans and psychoactive plants. This is not a call for a literal return to Stone Age conditions, but rather for a reintegration of the experiential, ecstatic dimension of religion into a culture that has become excessively rationalistic, materialistic, and dominator-oriented.

### The Question of Reversibility

A crucial question raised by the model is whether the process is reversible. Can a culture that has passed through all four stages of abandonment recover the original mystery? McKenna believed that the rediscovery of psychedelic plants in the twentieth century represented just such a reversal — a tentative, controversial, but potentially civilization-saving recovery of the Archaic relationship with the vegetable mind.

## The Role of Climate and Ecology

The physical driver behind stage one was ecological change. The psilocybin-containing Stropharia cubensis requires warm, moist grassland conditions — precisely the conditions that prevailed in Africa during the emergence of Homo sapiens but which progressively disappeared as the Sahara dried and the Fertile Crescent desiccated. As the mushroom's habitat contracted, so too did the frequency of human-mushroom contact.

The desertification of the entire area from North Africa to the region around Delhi has distorted our conception of what occurred when ancient civilizations were in their infancy and the area received higher rainfall. The [[emcdda-psilocybin-mushroom-dose-and-effects]] religion, born at the birth of cognition in the grasslands of Africa, may actually be the [[mckenna-psilocybin-generic-religion-humanity]] of human beings.

### The Vedic Transition

When the Indo-Europeans migrated eastward from their homeland north of the Black Sea, the mild temperatures and grassland conditions required by Stropharia cubensis ceased to be available. Other mushrooms may have enjoyed use as Soma substitutes, and of these [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]] may have been preferred because of its availability in colder climes, its psychoactivity (however ambiguous), and its striking appearance.

This transition from psilocybin to Amanita represents a move from stage one toward stage two. The Amanita experience, with its deliriant and potentially toxic qualities, was fundamentally different from the clarifying, boundary-dissolving effects of psilocybin. The poetry of the Vedas still reflects the glory of the original Soma experience, but the actual substance being consumed was a pale shadow of the original.

## Contrast: Partnership vs. Dominator Values

The abandonment of the mystery tracks precisely with the transition from partnership to dominator cultural values. Partnership societies, which were Goddess-worshipping, egalitarian, and ecologically sustainable, maintained the mushroom sacrament as the central institution of their religious life. Dominator societies, which were hierarchical, patriarchal, and oriented toward conquest and control, progressively abandoned the sacrament.

### What Was Lost

The critical loss was not merely a recreational drug but an entire epistemology — a way of knowing based on direct experience of the transcendent. The mushroom taught that the world is made of language, that consciousness is primary, that the ego is a social construction that can be temporarily dissolved. These insights, which are consistent with both the deepest shamanic traditions and the most advanced contemporary physics, were lost when the mystery was abandoned.

### The Connection to Environmental Crisis

McKenna draws a direct connection between the abandonment of the mystery and the current environmental crisis. When access to shamanic ecstasy is suppressed, the refreshing waters of emotion that flow from having a deeply bonded, almost symbiotic relationship to the earth are closed off. As a consequence, maladaptive social styles that encourage overpopulation, resource mismanagement, and environmental toxification develop and maintain themselves.
