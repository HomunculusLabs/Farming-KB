## Abandonment of the Mystery: Four Stages of Profanation

## Overview

In *Food of the Gods*, [[mckenna-stropharia-cubensis-extraterrestrial-hypothesis-spore-dispersal]] psilocybin-containing mushroom — was not a single event but a gradual process spanning millennia. Each stage represents a further dilution of awareness of the power and the numinous meaning resident in the mystery.

## Stage One: Substitution with Other Psychoactive Plants

The first step away from the symbiosis of the human-fungal partnership that characterized the early pastoralist societies was the introduction of other psychoactive plant substitutes for the original mushroom. This psychoactivity could range from being equal in the depths of its profundity to the [[psilocybin-intoxication-medical-management-and-first-aid]], as in the case with the classical hallucinogens of the New World tropics, to being relatively trivial.

### The Climate Change Scenario

In the case of Stropharia cubensis in Africa, a gradual trivialization scenario is proposed. With changes in climate, frequent if not continual low levels of mushroom ingestion gradually gave way to use that was merely seasonal. Conscious ceremonial use of mushrooms was at its apex during this seasonal availability phase, which may have lasted many thousands of years.

### The Honey Connection

As mushrooms and mushroom ecologies grew more rare, efforts may have been made to preserve mushrooms by drying and by preserving them in honey. Since honey itself easily ferments into an alcoholic intoxicant, it is possible that over time a practice of mixing fewer and fewer mushrooms in more and more honey may have encouraged replacement of [[mckenna-profession-of-sacrament-psilocybin-cult-to-alcohol-cult]] an alcohol cult.

### Substitutes Ranging from Powerful to Trivial

Examples of Soma substitutes include:
- **Classical New World hallucinogens** — Still profoundly psychoactive, capable of inducing boundary-dissolving ecstasy
- **Ephedra** — A stimulant, considerably less profound in its effects
- **Fermented honey (mead)** — An intoxicant with sedative and disinhibiting properties, fundamentally different from the ego-dissolving effects of psilocybin

## Stage Two: Substitution of Inactive Materials

The second step in the abandonment of the original psychosymbiotic mystery was the substitution of completely inactive materials for active ones. In this situation, the substitutes, though usually still plants, were really no more than symbols of the former power of the mystery to authentically move initiates.

### Symbolic Replacement

At this stage, the ritual form persisted but the pharmacological reality behind it was lost. Plants continued to be used ceremonially, but they no longer contained psychoactive compounds capable of inducing the original ecstatic experience. The ceremony became a memory of an experience rather than the experience itself.

### Connection to the Rig Veda

The Vedas themselves may preserve echoes of this transition. The hymns to Soma describe ecstatic experiences that the actual substances available to later Vedic priests could not reproduce. The gap between the exuberant descriptions in the Rig Veda and the relatively mild effects of the candidate Soma plants (such as Ephedra) suggests that the original substance had been lost and replaced with increasingly inadequate substitutes.

## Stage Three: Purely Symbolic Religion

In the third stage of the process, symbols are all that is left. Not only are psychoactive plants now out of the picture, but plants of any sort have disappeared, and in their place are esoteric teachings and dogma, rituals, stress on lineages, gestures, and cosmogonic diagrams.

### The World's Major Religions

Today's major world religions are typical of this third stage. The living experience of the mystery has been replaced by:
- **Esoteric teachings** — Codified systems of belief rather than direct experience
- **Dogma** — Fixed beliefs about the nature of the divine
- **Rituals** — Formalized ceremonies whose original meaning has been forgotten
- **Lineages** — Authority based on hereditary or institutional succession
- **Cosmogonic diagrams** — Maps of reality that substitute for the experience of reality itself

### Loss of Direct Gnosis

The critical loss at this stage is the elimination of direct gnosis — personal, experiential knowledge of the sacred. Religion becomes mediated through priests, texts, and institutions rather than arising from the direct experience of the individual. The mystic is marginalized or persecuted.

## Stage Four: Complete Abandonment and Active Suppression

The third stage leads into yet another stage: the complete abandonment of even the pretense of remembering the felt experience of the mystery. This last stage is typified by secular scientism as perfected in the [[mckenna-suppression-psychedelic-research-lsd-therapy]] and the hysteria fanned by pharmacophobic media is an obvious case in point. [[mckenna-dissolution-of-the-dominator-culture]] does not merely forget the mystery — it actively fears and suppresses it.

### The Secular Worldview

Secular scientism completes the process by denying that any "mystery" ever existed. Consciousness is reduced to neurochemistry, religious experience is dismissed as hallucination or psychopathology, and the entire history of humanity's relationship with psychoactive plants is reinterpreted through the lens of pathology and addiction.

## The Pattern in Historical Context

### Minoan Crete as Transitional

Minoan civilization represents a transitional phase between stages one and two. On the island of Crete where the Goddess was still supreme, there are no signs of war. The economy prospered and the arts flourished. Even when in the fifteenth century B.C. the island finally came under Achaean dominion, the Goddess and the way of thinking and living she symbolized still appear to have held fast.

### The Eleusinian Mysteries

The Eleusinian Mysteries represent perhaps the last institutional outpost of stage one in the Western world. For nearly two thousand years, the Mysteries at Eleusis provided a genuine experience of the sacred through what was almost certainly a psychoactive potion, the kykeon. The suppression of the Mysteries in the fourth century C.E. marks the final triumph of stage three/four in the Mediterranean world.

### The Dionysian Cults

The Dionysian cults, which coexisted with the Eleusinian Mysteries, represent another manifestation of the struggle to maintain stage one against the encroachment of stages two and three. Dionysus was the god of ecstasy, of the dissolution of boundaries, and his cult was repeatedly suppressed and revived throughout antiquity.
