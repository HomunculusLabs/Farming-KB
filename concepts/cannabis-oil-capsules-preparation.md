# Cannabis Oil Capsule Preparation

Cannabis cannabis oil capsules preparation, sometimes called pot pills, represent one of the most discreet and precisely dosable methods of consuming refined cannabis extracts. The preparation of oil capsules is a straightforward process that combines purified cannabis oil with a lipid carrier and an inert filler material, creating an oral dosage form that can be stored, transported, and consumed without drawing attention.

## Rationale for Capsule Preparation

Oral consumption of cannabis produces effects that differ significantly from inhaled consumption. The cannabinoids are metabolized by the liver through first-pass metabolism, converting THC to 11-hydroxy-THC, a metabolite that is more potent and has a longer duration of action. This makes capsules particularly suitable for sustained therapeutic applications described in [[lemon-cannabis-alchemy-advanced-refinement-techniques]] uses butter as the primary carrier for cannabis oil in capsules. The purified oil is mixed with an equal amount of butter. Butterfat serves as an effective carrier because cannabinoids are highly lipophilic and readily dissolve in animal fats. The lipid carrier facilitates absorption through the membranes of the stomach and intestine, significantly increasing bioavailability compared to consuming oil alone.

The choice of butter over other lipids is based on both its effectiveness as a carrier and its availability. Other lipid carriers such as coconut oil, MCT oil, or various vegetable oils can substitute for butter. Coconut oil and MCT oil are popular alternatives because they remain liquid at room temperature, making the capsule-filling process easier and producing a more uniform product.

The butterfat carrier mimics the traditional approach of cannabis-infused butter (cannabutter) used in edible preparation. However, capsules allow much more precise dosing because each capsule contains a measured amount of oil-butter mixture, compared to the variable dosing inherent in baked goods or spreads.

## Filler Materials

The oil-butter mixture is blended with two volumes of an inert powder to create a workable consistency suitable for filling capsules. Several materials serve this purpose effectively.

Finely powdered marijuana leaf material provides a natural filler that may contribute additional minor cannabinoids and terpenes to the preparation. However, this adds plant taste and may introduce variability in cannabinoid content.

Parsley powder is an effective inert filler that adds bulk without contributing cannabinoids. Its mild flavor does not interfere with the cannabis oil.

Lactose, a milk sugar, is widely used as a capsule filler in pharmaceutical preparations. It is tasteless, readily available, and produces a smooth, uniform powder when blended with the oil-butter mixture. Individuals with lactose intolerance should avoid this option.

Other edible inert powders such as tapioca starch, arrowroot powder, or microcrystalline cellulose can serve as alternatives. The key requirements are that the filler be edible, tasteless or mildly flavored, and capable of absorbing the oil-butter mixture to form a cohesive but not wet powder.

## Capsule Selection and Filling

Large gelatin capsules (size 00, which holds approximately 0.5 grams of powder) are standard for cannabis oil capsules. The capsules are filled by hand or with a capsule-filling machine. Hand-filling involves placing the powder mixture into the body half of the capsule, lightly packing it down, and capping with the lid half.

Capsule-filling machines greatly speed the process and improve consistency between capsules. These devices allow batch filling of 24 to 100 capsules at a time and are relatively inexpensive. For larger-scale production, semi-automatic tamp-filling machines can produce hundreds of capsules per hour.

Vegetable-based capsules (hypromellose or HPMC) are available for individuals who avoid animal products. These perform identically to gelatin capsules in terms of protection, dissolution, and shelf life.
