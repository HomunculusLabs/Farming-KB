# Agar Culture Techniques

Agar culture is the foundational laboratory technique in [[mushroom-cultivation-overview]], providing the two-dimensional surface on which pure mycelial cultures can be established, maintained, and studied. paul stamets describes multiple agar media formulations and techniques in The Mushroom Cultivator.

## Purpose of Agar Culture

Agar culture serves several essential functions in the cultivation process:
- Enables isolation of pure strains from [[strain-isolation-from-wild-specimens]] or spore prints
- Allows visual monitoring for contamination on a flat, transparent surface
- Permits [[cervantes-strain-selection]] by observing sectoring and growth characteristics
- Provides a medium for rapid multiplication of mycelial mass
- Serves as the starting point for [[spawn-generation-techniques]]

## Agar Media Formulations

### PDY (Potato Dextrose Yeast Agar)
- 300g sliced potatoes (boiled, filtered)
- 10g dextrose
- 2g yeast
- 20g agar per liter of water
- A general-purpose formula suitable for most species

### MEA (Malt Extract Agar)
- 20g light tan brewer's malt
- 2g yeast
- 20g agar per liter of water
- Particularly good for [[psilocybe-bohemica-central-european]] species

### MPG (Malt Peptone Grain Agar)
- 20g malt
- 5g ground rye
- 5g peptone
- 2g yeast
- 20g agar per liter of water
- Enriched formula for demanding species

### Antibiotic Supplemented Media
For cultures prone to bacterial contamination, 0.10g of 60-80% pure gentamycin sulfate can be added per liter before sterilization.

## Preparation Protocol

1. Mix dry ingredients in water, bring to a boil
2. Stir until completely dissolved
3. Pour into media bottles or directly into petri dishes
4. Sterilize at 15 psi (250°F / 121°C) for 30 minutes
5. Allow to cool to approximately 120°F before pouring
6. Fill petri dishes one-third to one-half full
7. Allow to solidify on a level surface

## Critical Sterilization Warning

Media must NOT be exposed to temperatures above 250°F (121°C). Excessive heat:
- Caramelizes sugars
- Inhibits mycelial growth
- Promotes genetic mutations
- Reduces nutritional quality of the media

## Inoculation Techniques

### Wedge Transfer
The standard method for transferring mycelium between agar plates:
1. Flame-sterilize the agar knife or inoculating loop
2. Cool the blade by touching it to a sterile area of the agar
3. Cut a small triangular wedge from the leading edge of mycelial growth
4. Transfer the wedge to the fresh media plate
5. Seal the plate with tape or parafilm

### Double Wedge Transfer
For grain inoculation, a single wedge is cut in half, with both pieces inserted into a grain jar. This provides two inoculation points per wedge.

## Mycelial Appearance on Agar

Mycelial appearance varies by species and media:
- **Lepista nuda**: Purplish blue mycelium
- **Psilocybe tampanensis**: Multi-colored brownish mycelium
- Growth type varies from aerial (in high humidity) to appressed (in dry conditions)
- [[cervantes-strain-selection]] — rhizomorphic vs. cottony growth — is a critical diagnostic feature

## Storing Agar Cultures

Agar plates should be:
- Stored inverted (lid down) to prevent condensation from dripping onto the mycelium
- Kept at room temperature (70-75°F) for active growth
- Sealed with tape to prevent contamination and desiccation
- Transferred to fresh plates before the mycelium reaches the plate edge
- Used within 2-4 weeks for optimal vitality

## Slant Culture Storage

For long-term preservation, mycelium is grown in test tube slants:
- Fill 20mm x 100mm test tubes one-third with agar media
- Sterilize 30 minutes at 15 psi
- Cool at a 15-30 degree angle to create a slanted surface
- Inoculate with mycelial fragment
- Incubate one week at 75°F (24°C)
- Store at 35-40°F (2-4°C) for long-term preservation
- Check vitality every six months by transferring to fresh plates

## Troubleshooting Agar Cultures

- **Excessive condensation**: Cool plates more slowly; pour thinner layers
- **Contamination**: Improve [[sterile-technique-mushroom-cultivation]]; use antibiotic-supplemented media
- **Slow growth**: Check sterilization temperature (caramelization); verify media freshness
- **Sectoring**: Vary media formula; see [[strain-selection-development]]
- **Drying**: Seal plates better; increase humidity in storage area
