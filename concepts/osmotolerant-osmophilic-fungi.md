## Overview

Osmotolerant and osmophilic fungi are defined by their ability to grow on
substrata whose low water activities result from [[ingham-blackstrap-molasses-compost-tea-concentration-osmotic-effects]] -- high
concentrations of sugars, salts, or other solutes. This ecological group is
distinct from xerophilic/[[xerophilic-xerotolerant-fungi]], which tolerate low water
activity from low matrix potential rather than osmotic stress.
The water-activity value (aw) ranges from 0 to 1, with most
microorganisms restricted to aw >= 0.95. Osmophilic fungi can grow at
significantly lower aw values.

## Water Activity and Osmotic Stress

High osmotic concentrations outside a cell cause it to lose water,
potentially killing the fungus or preventing
[[growing-gourmet-spore-germination-strain-isolation]]. The ability to
preserve foods with concentrated sugar or salt solutions is based on this
principle.
Environments with naturally high salt concentrations (salt marshes, saline
soils, seawater -- average 0.5 mol/kg NaCl, aw 0.981) have restricted
[[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]]. Only a few
[[halotolerant-halophilic-fungi-saline-environments]] can grow in saturated
NaCl solution (6 mol/kg, aw 0.75).

## Mechanisms of Osmotolerance

Fungi employ several physiological strategies to survive and grow under
osmotic stress:

### Compatible Solutes

The primary mechanism of osmotolerance is intracellular accumulation of
compatible solutes -- [[fungal-volatile-organic-compounds-communication]]
that do not interfere with normal metabolic functions even at high
concentrations:

- **Glycerol:** The most common compatible solute in fungi, accumulated
  via the HOG (High Osmolarity Glycerol) MAP kinase pathway. Glycerol
  concentrations in osmophilic fungi can reach molar levels, balancing
  external [[fungal-fruiting-body-mechanics-osmotic-pressure]] without disrupting enzyme function. The HOG
  pathway senses osmotic stress through membrane receptors and activates
  transcription factors that upregulate glycerol biosynthesis genes
  (GPD1, GPD2).

- **Erythritol and arabitol:** Polyols produced by some osmophilic
  species as supplementary compatible solutes. *Zygosaccharomyces rouxii*
  accumulates significant erythritol alongside glycerol.

- **Trehalose:** A non-reducing disaccharide that stabilizes proteins and
  membranes under osmotic and thermal stress. While not a primary
  osmolyte in most species, it plays a critical role in spore
  desiccation tolerance and stress recovery.

### Ion Transport and Regulation

[[osmophilic-osmotolerant-fungi-low-water-activity]] maintain ionic
homeostasis through regulated ion transport systems. Under salt stress,
Na+/H+ antiporters in the plasma membrane actively extrude sodium ions
while the vacuolar Na+/H+ antiporter sequesters excess sodium into the
vacuole. Potassium uptake systems (Trk transporters) help maintain
favorable K+/Na+ ratios essential for enzyme function.

## Key Genera and Species

Important osmophilic fungi are confined to genera including:
*Aspergillus*, *Aureobasidium*, *Chrysosporium*, *Cladosporium*,
*Fusarium*, *Geotrichum*, *Myrothecium*, *Oidiodendron*, *Paecilomyces*,
*Penicillium*, *Rhizopus*, *Scopulariopsis*, *Trichoderma*, *Wallemia*,
and *Xeromyces* (Pitt 1981; Pitt and Hocking 1997).

### Aspergillus

Several Aspergillus species are notable osmophiles. *[[aspergillus-niger]]*
grows at aw as low as 0.77 and is a common contaminant of dried fruits,
jams, and confectionery. *Aspergillus restrictus* and *Aspergillus
