# JADAM Water Quality and Pesticide Effectiveness

## Overview

Water quality is one of the most critical yet overlooked factors in pesticide effectiveness. JADAM emphasizes that only when pesticide "wets" the target fully can it effectively control pests and leave no marks on fruits or leaves. The quality of water plays a decisive role in determining the wetting power of any pesticide, whether natural or chemical.

## Hard Water vs Soft Water

Hard water contains high levels of calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), and iron (Fe). When [[jadam-wetting-agent-jwa]] (or any soap-based surfactant) is mixed with hard water, it performs poorly: the water turns murky like milk, produces little or no foam, and has greatly reduced wetting and penetrating power.

Soft water, by contrast, keeps completely clear when mixed with JWA, produces vigorous foam when shaken, and provides excellent wetting, coating, and penetrating properties.

### Water Test with JWA
A simple test determines water suitability:
1. Put a few drops of JWA in water
2. If water stays clear and produces lots of foam when shaken, it is soft water (optimal)
3. If water turns progressively murkier with less foam, it becomes less suitable
4. Water that produces no foam at all should not be used

## Sources of Soft Water

- **Rainwater**: The typical soft water. Collect 30 minutes after rain begins (the first 30 minutes contain pollutants). Rainwater washes clothes well and skin feels smooth after washing.
- **Water softener**: If soft water is not naturally available, JADAM recommends using a water softener with ion-exchange resin that removes Ca, Mg, and Fe. This costs about 5 dollars per month to operate.

## Humidity and Spray Timing

Natural pesticides are less toxic than chemical ones and need skillful application for best results. The higher the moisture level in the air, the longer the pesticide remains on the target.

- On a clear, dry day, pesticide evaporates instantly with minimal effect
- There is a common but incorrect notion that it is bad to spray when there is morning dew -- on the contrary, natural pesticides work better with dew
- Morning dew means humidity is nearly 100%, so pesticide evaporates very slowly
- The bodies of aphids and mites do not have dew, so the pesticide coats them while the environment stays moist

### Seasonal Timing
- Spring and autumn: spray in early morning
- Summer: spray in the evening of a humid day
- If humidity is high enough (check by spraying water and observing evaporation rate), daytime spraying is acceptable
- In greenhouses: spray on a rainy day, or increase humidity artificially
- Smartphone apps provide humidity and dew point information for each region

## Nozzle Selection

Nozzles that produce fine particles are best. Finer particles mean less pesticide volume needed for high pest control. For best results, spray thoroughly so as to fully and completely wet the plant. In fruit trees with many water sprouts and leaves, pesticide cannot reach all corners -- pruning and branch shaping can help increase effectiveness by ensuring trees receive full sun and air.

## Mixing Protocol

To make 500 L (132 gal) of pesticide:
1. Fill 300 L (80 gal) of water in the container first
2. Put in ingredients (JWA, JS, JHS, etc.) one by one, stirring each time
3. Add water to make 500 L and stir once more
4. Never mix undiluted JS and JWA together directly (they clot)
5. In cold weather, ingredients dissolve more slowly -- mix more thoroughly
6. Always mix right before application; do not leave leftovers
7. If solution contains JS, leftover will easily create concentration stress

## What Does Not Mix With JWA

These substances cause clotting or reduced foam and should not be mixed with JWA pesticides:
- Liquid fertilizers made with sugar or molasses
- Vinegar
- Wood vinegar
- Seawater/sea salt (when used directly, not as part of JMS)

If these must be used, dilute to over 1,000 times. Do not mix with pesticides.

## Ten Rules for Using Natural Pesticide

1. Test water with JWA to confirm it is soft
2. Do a mixing test; do not use if it clots or reduces foam
3. Spray when humid
4. Mix ingredients one by one into water, never undiluted together
5. Do a partial concentration test before full-field application
6. When mixed with chemical pesticides, do a concentration test
7. Use up pesticide immediately; do not use containers that held chemical pesticides
8. Lock bee hives before spraying; ventilate before reopening
9. Wash before eating if recently sprayed; washing not necessary for harvesting
10. Always filter inputs (like JHS) thoroughly before mixing
