Trichomes are the tiny, crystal-like resin glands found on the surface of
cannabis flowers and leaves. According to [[delta-8-thc]], CBD, CBN, THCV, CBDV, CDC, and CBL.

Female plants produce the vast majority of trichomes, and the most potent
type is called the stalked capitate trichome. These feature a visible stalk
supporting a round, bulbous gland head that contains the highest
concentration of active cannabinoids. Bulbous glands on the leaves and
stems also produce cannabinoids but only in minor quantities compared to
the stalked capitate trichomes found on the flowers.

A frosty appearance on buds indicates heavy trichome production, which is
a visual marker that the plant is generating significant resin and potency.
Some plants may have abundant resin but lower potency, while others may have
less visible resin but be very potent. Optimal growing conditions produce
plants with both high resin quantity and high potency.

## Resin Production and Function

Resin glands are produced all over the female flowers and new leaves. They
can be seen clearly with the use of a magnifying aid such as a jeweler's
loupe or microscope. The plant produces resin oils as an evolutionary
defense mechanism, with the sticky surface designed to gather and trap
the plant continues producing resin in its effort to capture
pollen that never arrives. This means that sinsemilla grows, where no
males are present, often produce more abundant resin than pollinated crops.

 The resin production cycle follows a pattern that mirrors flowering
 development. Early in flowering, trichomes begin as small clear stalks
 with tiny round heads. As flowering progresses, the gland heads swell
 and fill with cannabinoid rich oil. In the final weeks, trichomes may
 change color from clear to cloudy to amber, which many growers use as a
 visual indicator for [[cervantes-hand-rubbed-hash-charas]]
hashish, a practice that has been used for centuries.

 However, sugar leaves, the smaller leaves that grow within and around the
 buds, are often covered in trichomes and are commonly saved for making
 extracts, edibles, or hash. These leaves are far more potent than the
 large fan leaves and represent a valuable secondary product from the
 harvest. The distinction between fan leaves and sugar leaves is important
 for any grower looking to maximize the utility of their entire harvest.

## THC Levels vs THC Quantities

Green makes an important distinction between THC levels and THC quantities.
THC levels are genetic and are not under the influence of the grower. A
strain might have a THC level of 5 out of 10 regardless of how it is grown.
THC quantities, on the other hand, are controlled by the grower through
bud mass and the amount of resin generated.

A plant with 20% THC at a level of 5 grown under poor conditions might
only produce a fraction of what the same plant produces under optimal
conditions, which could yield 70% THC at the same genetic level of 5. This
means growers can significantly influence the total THC output through
proper [[query-how-do-i-create-a-seed-bank-and-start-seed-saving]] catalogue, Green advises checking the THC levels
to understand how potent the plant is, while recognizing that some
breeders may exaggerate their claims.

## Trichome Collection for Hash

Trichomes are the raw material for making hashish. Hash is made primarily
from the collection of stalked capitate trichomes. When the collected
trichomes are compressed, they form a blocky mass referred to as hashish.
