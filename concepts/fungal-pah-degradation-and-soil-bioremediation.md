updated: 2026-04-18 Polyaromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) [[query-how-does-lignin-degradation-by-fungi-work]] is one of the most promising applications of white-rot-fungi-effluent-decolorization to break down recalcitrant environmental pollutants that resist bacterial attack. ## PAHs as Environmental Pollutants PAHs are a class of carcinogenic chemicals formed during organic combustion. Key characteristics: - Two or more fused benzene rings - Insoluble and stable in the environment - Concentrated at industrial sites, petroleum facilities, and wood-preserving operations - Creosote contains 85% PAHs by weight - Atmospheric fall-out has been steadily increasing through the 20th century ### PAH Biodegradability Hierarchy | Ring Structure | Examples | Biodegradability | |---------------|----------|-----------------| | 2-3 rings (linear) | Naphthalene, anthracene | Highly biodegradable | | 2-3 rings (angular) | Phenanthrene | Moderately biodegradable | | 4 rings | Pyrene, benz[a]anthracene | Less biodegradable | | 5-6 rings | Benzo[a]pyrene, dibenzo-p-dioxin | Least biodegradable | PAH degradation depends on the ability of microbes to introduce oxygen into the rings, which increases both solubility and chemical reactivity. ## Why Fungi Over Bacteria for PAHs Bacteria use intracellular dioxygenases and cytochrome P450 enzymes for PAH activation. PAH structures larger than 2-3 rings are too insoluble to cross the bacterial cell wall, making them inaccessible to intracellular enzymes. White-rot fungi overcome this through extracellular oxidative systems that can attack higher-molecular-weight PAHs directly. ## Key Fungal Species for PAH Degradation ### [[singh-cytochrome-p450-fungal-bioremediation]]
The model white-rot fungus, extensively studied for PAH degradation:
- Mineralizes phenanthrene, pyrene, and benzo[a]pyrene in laboratory cultures
- Degrades PAHs in soil supplemented with straw, hay, or wood
- Effective in bioslurry reactors for explosives-contaminated soils
- [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] (LiP) and [[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]] (MnP) are primary enzymes

### [[staycare-trametes-versicolor-bioremediation-applications]]
Widely used in field applications:
- Colonized soil and degraded 3,4-dichloroaniline and benzo[a]pyrene
- Grew well with alfalfa straw, bark, sawdust, or sphagnum moss amendments
- Mycelium-coated pellets removed over 80% of pentachlorophenol in 4 weeks in unsterile soil
- Produces all three major [[singh-ligninolytic-enzymes-fungal-bioremediation]] (LiP, MnP, laccase)

### [[cotter-mycoremediation-overview]]
The [[cotter-oyster-mushroom-cultivation]], superior soil colonizer:
- Effective against various PAHs in liquid culture and soil-ligninocellulose systems
- Superior to P. chrysosporium and T. versicolor in soil colonization ability
- Effective against pyrene, benzo[a]anthracene, and benzo[a]pyrene in sterile sand microcosms
- Successfully used with straw in unsterile soils contaminated with both PAHs and heavy metals

### Marasmiellus troyanus
Isolated from a toxic waste site:
- Completely degrades benzo[a]pyrene in liquid culture
- Alginate-encapsulated formulations removed ~90% of benzo[a]pyrene from soil microcosms in 6 weeks
- Does not sporulate in the laboratory, requiring mycelial encapsulation

### Lentinula edodes (Shiitake)
- Spent sawdust cultures biotransformed pentachlorophenol in soil
- Activity influenced by environmental parameters

## Soil Bioremediation Approaches

### Biostimulation
Adding nutrients (N, P) and [[cervantes-organic-amendments-chart]] (straw, wood chips) to stimulate indigenous microbial activity. Often more effective than bioaugmentation alone. Organic-rich soils show enhanced PAH degradation; organic-poor soils may show inhibition.

### Bioaugmentation
Introducing specific fungal species via formulated inocula. Key factors:
- **Substrate carrier**: Wheat straw most effective for P. ostreatus; aspen wood chips for P. chrysosporium
- **Nutrient amendments**: Carbon and nitrogen frequently limiting; alfalfa straw improved white rot growth
- **Selective biocides**: Benomyl (115 ppm) selectively benefits white rot fungi over indigenous competitors
- **Inoculum density**: Higher initial inoculum improves colonization

### Composting
Mixing contaminated soil with organic materials (straw, manure) and white-rot fungi. Composting with fungal-enzyme-systems-in-bioremediation
