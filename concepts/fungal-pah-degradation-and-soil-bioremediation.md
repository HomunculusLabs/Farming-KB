Polyaromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) degradation by fungi is one of the most promising applications of [[fungal-bioremediation]], leveraging the extracellular oxidative enzyme systems of [[white-rot-fungi-effluent-decolorization]] to break down recalcitrant environmental pollutants that resist bacterial attack.

## PAHs as Environmental Pollutants

PAHs are a class of carcinogenic chemicals formed during organic combustion. Key characteristics:
- Two or more fused benzene rings
- Insoluble and stable in the environment
- Concentrated at industrial sites, petroleum facilities, and wood-preserving operations
- Creosote contains 85% PAHs by weight
- Atmospheric fall-out has been steadily increasing through the 20th century

### PAH Biodegradability Hierarchy

| Ring Structure | Examples | Biodegradability |
|---------------|----------|-----------------|
| 2-3 rings (linear) | Naphthalene, anthracene | Highly biodegradable |
| 2-3 rings (angular) | Phenanthrene | Moderately biodegradable |
| 4 rings | Pyrene, benz[a]anthracene | Less biodegradable |
| 5-6 rings | Benzo[a]pyrene, dibenzo-p-dioxin | Least biodegradable |

PAH degradation depends on the ability of microbes to introduce oxygen into the rings, which increases both solubility and chemical reactivity.

## Why Fungi Over Bacteria for PAHs

Bacteria use intracellular dioxygenases and cytochrome P450 enzymes for PAH activation. PAH structures larger than 2-3 rings are too insoluble to cross the bacterial cell wall, making them inaccessible to intracellular enzymes. White-rot fungi overcome this through extracellular oxidative systems that can attack higher-molecular-weight PAHs directly.

## Key Fungal Species for PAH Degradation

### [[singh-cytochrome-p450-fungal-bioremediation]]
The model white-rot fungus, extensively studied for PAH degradation:
- Mineralizes phenanthrene, pyrene, and benzo[a]pyrene in laboratory cultures
- Degrades PAHs in soil supplemented with straw, hay, or wood
- Effective in bioslurry reactors for explosives-contaminated soils
- Lignin peroxidase (LiP) and manganese peroxidase (MnP) are primary enzymes

### [[staycare-trametes-versicolor-bioremediation-applications]]
Widely used in field applications:
- Colonized soil and degraded 3,4-dichloroaniline and benzo[a]pyrene
- Grew well with alfalfa straw, bark, sawdust, or sphagnum moss amendments
- Mycelium-coated pellets removed over 80% of pentachlorophenol in 4 weeks in unsterile soil
- Produces all three major ligninolytic enzymes (LiP, MnP, laccase)

### [[cotter-mycoremediation-overview]]
The oyster mushroom, superior soil colonizer:
- Effective against various PAHs in liquid culture and soil-ligninocellulose systems
- Superior to P. chrysosporium and T. versicolor in soil colonization ability
- Effective against pyrene, benzo[a]anthracene, and benzo[a]pyrene in sterile sand microcosms
- Successfully used with straw in unsterile soils contaminated with both PAHs and heavy metals

### Marasmiellus troyanus
Isolated from a toxic waste site:
- Completely degrades benzo[a]pyrene in liquid culture
- Alginate-encapsulated formulations removed ~90% of benzo[a]pyrene from soil microcosms in 6 weeks
- Does not sporulate in the laboratory, requiring mycelial encapsulation

### Lentinula edodes (Shiitake)
- Spent sawdust cultures biotransformed pentachlorophenol in soil
- Activity influenced by environmental parameters

## Soil Bioremediation Approaches

### Biostimulation
Adding nutrients (N, P) and organic amendments (straw, wood chips) to stimulate indigenous microbial activity. Often more effective than bioaugmentation alone. Organic-rich soils show enhanced PAH degradation; organic-poor soils may show inhibition.

### Bioaugmentation
Introducing specific fungal species via formulated inocula. Key factors:
- **Substrate carrier**: Wheat straw most effective for P. ostreatus; aspen wood chips for P. chrysosporium
- **Nutrient amendments**: Carbon and nitrogen frequently limiting; alfalfa straw improved white rot growth
- **Selective biocides**: Benomyl (115 ppm) selectively benefits white rot fungi over indigenous competitors
- **Inoculum density**: Higher initial inoculum improves colonization

### Composting
Mixing contaminated soil with organic materials (straw, manure) and white-rot fungi. Composting with [[singh-cytochrome-p450-fungal-bioremediation]] has been used for alachlor-impacted soil treatment.

### Bioslurry Reactors
Contaminated soil mixed with water and aerated, with fungal inoculum. Used for:
- PAH-contaminated soils
- Explosives (TNT) contamination
- Pentachlorophenol treatment

## Factors Affecting PAH Degradation

- **Bioavailability**: Sequestration in soil organic matter reduces accessibility
- **Wetting/drying cycles**: Can increase pollutant availability
- **Heavy metal co-contamination**: Cadmium and mercury inhibit ligninolytic enzymes
- **Indigenous microbial competition**: Well-adapted indigenous communities often outcompete introduced species
- **Temperature and moisture**: Weather variability profoundly affects field outcomes
- **PAH structure**: Higher molecular weight compounds are more recalcitrant

## Mixed Culture Considerations

In natural ecosystems, fungal bioremediation is a collaborative effort with bacteria. Among 10 natural isolates of P. chrysosporium, one or more bacterial species were always present (Agrobacterium radiobacter, Burkholderia sp., and rRNA superfamily IV members). Future research should emphasize mixed bacteria-fungal consortia rather than single-species inocula.
