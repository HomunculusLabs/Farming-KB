## Explosive Contaminant Treatment

Composting has shown promise for treating soils contaminated with
explosives such as TNT and RDX. The combination of thermophilic conditions
and active microbial metabolism promotes reductive and oxidative
transformation pathways. Fungi contribute through both direct enzymatic
attack on nitroaromatic compounds and through the creation of reducing
microenvironments within the compost matrix. Composting of TNT-contaminated
soil has achieved substantial contaminant removal, with bound residue
formation representing a significant fate pathway for transformed
explosive metabolites.

## Factors Affecting Composting Efficiency

Several parameters influence the effectiveness of compost-based
bioremediation. Moisture content must be maintained between 40-60% to
support microbial activity without creating anaerobic conditions. Aeration
is critical to maintain oxygen levels for aerobic decomposition. Carbon-
to-nitrogen ratio, typically adjusted to 25:1-35:1, affects microbial
growth rates and enzyme production. pH naturally trends toward neutral
during composting, which is generally favorable for fungal activity.
Particle size of the composted material influences oxygen diffusion and
surface area for microbial colonization.

## Bulking Agents and Amendments

Bulking agents such as wood chips, straw, sawdust, and municipal green
waste are commonly added to contaminated soils or wastes before composting.
These materials improve porosity and aeration while providing supplemental
carbon sources that support fungal growth. Lignocellulosic bulking agents
are particularly beneficial because they selectively favor ligninolytic
fungi. The choice of bulking agent can significantly affect the composting
temperature profile, microbial community development, and ultimate
degradation efficiency for target pollutants.
