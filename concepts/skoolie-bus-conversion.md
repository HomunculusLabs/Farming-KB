# Skoolie Bus Conversion

A skoolie is a retired school bus converted into a mobile living space. Bus conversions offer significantly more interior space than tiny houses or vans (typically 200-300 square feet in a full-size bus), strong steel construction, and the familiarity of a vehicle platform. The conversion process involves gutting the interior, adding insulation, building out living systems, and finishing with residential-quality amenities.

## Bus Selection

### Full-Size School Bus (Best for Full-Time Living)

- Length: 35-40 feet (interior space ~240-280 sq ft)
- Width: 96 inches exterior, ~90 inches interior
- Height: 78-82 inches interior (before flooring and ceiling)
- Engine: Diesel, typically 7.3L Power Stroke (Ford), DT466 (International), or T444E
- Mileage: 7-12 mpg
- Cost: $3,000-10,000 depending on age and condition

### Short Bus (Best for Solo or Couple)

- Length: 20-26 feet (interior space ~120-160 sq ft)
- Easier to drive and park
- Better fuel economy (10-15 mpg)
- Cost: $3,000-8,000

### Key Buying Criteria

- Rust: Walk away from buses with significant frame or floor rust
- Engine: Test drive; listen for knocking, smoking, or hard shifting
- Tires: Commercial tires in good condition cost $2,000-4,000 per set
- Brakes: Air brake systems need inspection — expensive to repair
- Roof: Check for leaks around emergency hatches and roof panels
- Title: Ensure clean title; some states have special requirements for converted buses

## Conversion Steps

### Phase 1: Demolition (1-2 Weeks)

Remove everything from the interior:

1. Remove all seats (bolted to the floor — use impact wrench)
2. Remove floor rubber — often glued with industrial adhesive; heat gun helps
3. Remove ceiling panels and insulation (often fiberglass — wear respirator)
4. Remove interior wall panels
5. Remove heater units beneath seats
6. Remove all windows (or selectively remove for replacement)
7. Strip the subfloor down to bare metal
8. Remove any rust with wire brush, grinder, or naval jelly
9. Treat rusted areas with rust converter and paint with rust-inhibiting primer

### Phase 2: Rust Prevention and Floor Repair (1 Week)

1. Inspect the entire floor for rust-through or thin spots
2. Cut out rusted sections and weld in replacement steel patches
3. Coat the entire floor with rust-inhibiting paint or truck bed liner
4. Fill any low spots with self-leveling compound if needed
5. Seal all floor penetrations that will not be reused

### Phase 3: Insulation (1-2 Weeks)

Insulation is critical — metal buses conduct heat and cold aggressively. Without insulation, the bus is uninhabitable in both summer and winter.

**Closed-Cell Spray Foam (Recommended)**
- Spray 2-3 inches on ceiling and walls directly to metal
- R-12 to R-18; acts as vapor barrier
- Prevents condensation on metal surfaces
- Cost: $2,000-4,000 professionally done

**Rigid Foam Board + Reflectix (Budget Option)**
- Cut XPS or polyiso boards to fit between ribs
- Tape all seams with foil tape
- Add Reflectix radiant barrier as second layer
- Cost: $500-1,000

**Havelock Wool (Natural Option)**
- Sheep's [[mollison-designers-wool-insulation-and-natural-textiles]] batts
- Naturally manages moisture, fire-resistant
- Cost: $2,000-3,000

**Floor Insulation**
- 1-2 inches of rigid foam board between floor joists
- Plywood subfloor on top (1/2 or 5/8 inch)
- Finished flooring (vinyl plank recommended — lightweight and durable)

### Phase 4: Framing (1-2 Weeks)

Build a stud wall framework inside the bus:

1. Use 1x2 or 2x2 lumber for wall framing (metal studs save weight)
2. Screw through framing into the bus structural ribs
3. Space studs 16 or 24 inches on center
4. Build ceiling framing if not using existing ribs
5. Frame rough openings for windows, doors, and cabinets
6. Build loft framing if desired (over cab or rear)

### Phase 5: Electrical System (1-2 Weeks)

**House Battery Bank**
- 200-400 Ah LiFePO4 batteries (best longevity and weight)
- Or 400-800 Ah lead-acid golf cart batteries (cheaper, heavier)
- Battery isolator charges house batteries from alternator while driving

**Solar**
- 400-1000 watts of panels on the roof
- MPPT charge controller
- Wire runs through roof with proper sealing (waterproof combiner box)

**Inverter and Distribution**
- 2000-3000W pure sine wave inverter
- AC/DC distribution panel
- 12V fuse block for DC circuits
- Shore power inlet for campground hookups

### Phase 6: Plumbing (1 Week)

**Fresh Water**
- 50-100 gallon fresh water tank (mounted low for center of gravity)
- 12V water pump with accumulator tank
- PEX piping throughout

**Hot Water**
- Propane tankless water heater (instant, on-demand)
- Or 10-gallon electric/propane RV water heater

**Greywater**
- 30-50 gallon greywater tank
- Or [[off-grid-waste-management]] disposal system with filter and direct drain

**Black Water**
- Most skoolies use [[off-grid-waste-management]] to eliminate blackwater tanks
- If using a regular toilet, install a blackwater tank with proper venting

### Phase 7: Propane System (2-3 Days)

- Mount propane tanks in a ventilated exterior compartment
- Run copper or CSST flexible gas line to appliances
- Install propane regulator, shutoff valve, and leak detector
- Appliances: cooktop, oven, heater, water heater, refrigerator

### Phase 8: Interior Finish (2-4 Weeks)

1. Install wall paneling (tongue-and-groove pine, shiplap, or painted plywood)
2. Install ceiling (thin plywood or paneling)
3. Install flooring
