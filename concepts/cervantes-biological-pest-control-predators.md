# Biological Pest Control - Predators and Parasites

Biological controls use [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]] - predators and parasites - to manage
pest populations in cannabis gardens. Jorge Cervantes documents this approach
as an integral part of [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]] management (IPM), emphasizing that
predators work best as preventative control but are slower to stop an active
infestation.

## Predators vs Parasites

### Predators

By definition, a predator must eat more than one victim before reaching
adulthood. Predators have either chewing mouthparts (like ladybugs and praying
mantises, which eat prey whole) or piercing-sucking mouthparts (like lacewing
larvae, which suck fluids from their prey). A key characteristic: predators
choose to be surrounded by prey and move on when populations diminish. They
never truly eradicate pests, making them ideal for prevention but less
effective against heavy infestations.

### Parasites (Parasitoids)

Parasites consume a single individual host before adulthood. Adult
parasitoids typically place a single egg into many hosts. The egg hatches into
larvae that eat the host insect from the inside out, saving vital organs for
last. Larvae pupate inside the host body and emerge as adults. Unlike
predators, parasites hunt until the prey is almost eliminated.

## How to Evaluate and Source Predators

When purchasing predators or parasites, Cervantes advises confirming these
details with the supplier:

1. **Latin name** of the organism to avoid confusion
2. **Specific pests attacked** by the predator
3. **Complete [[bloomfield-fungal-life-cycle-spiral-versus-circle]]** information
4. **Preferred climate** including [[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]] ranges
5. **Application rate and method** of introduction

Reliable suppliers ship predators via special delivery and provide detailed
care and release instructions. Pick up shipments immediately upon arrival -
predators left in a hot mailbox can easily reach 120 degrees F (49 degrees C)
and die.

## Key Predatory Organisms

### Ladybugs (Ladybird Beetles)

Ladybugs are among the most recognizable predators. However, they present a
challenge in indoor gardens: if released during lighted hours, they are
attracted to HID lamps. If 500 ladybugs are released on Monday, by Friday
only a few survivors remain - the rest fly into the hot lamp and perish.
Release flying predators at night to extend their survival. Release about 50
ladybugs per plant.

### Predatory Mites

Predatory mites are among the most effective controls for [[cervantes-pyrethrum-pyrethroid-insecticides]] and insecticidal
  soaps can be applied a few days before, provided residue is washed off
- Do not spray any chemicals after releasing predators
