# Biological Pest Control - Predators and Parasites

Biological controls use living organisms - predators and parasites - to manage
pest populations in cannabis gardens. Jorge Cervantes documents this approach
as an integral part of integrated pest management (IPM), emphasizing that
predators work best as preventative control but are slower to stop an active
infestation.

## Predators vs Parasites

### Predators

By definition, a predator must eat more than one victim before reaching
adulthood. Predators have either chewing mouthparts (like ladybugs and praying
mantises, which eat prey whole) or piercing-sucking mouthparts (like lacewing
larvae, which suck fluids from their prey). A key characteristic: predators
choose to be surrounded by prey and move on when populations diminish. They
never truly eradicate pests, making them ideal for prevention but less
effective against heavy infestations.

### Parasites (Parasitoids)

Parasites consume a single individual host before adulthood. Adult
parasitoids typically place a single egg into many hosts. The egg hatches into
larvae that eat the host insect from the inside out, saving vital organs for
last. Larvae pupate inside the host body and emerge as adults. Unlike
predators, parasites hunt until the prey is almost eliminated.

## How to Evaluate and Source Predators

When purchasing predators or parasites, Cervantes advises confirming these
details with the supplier:

1. **Latin name** of the organism to avoid confusion
2. **Specific pests attacked** by the predator
3. **Complete life cycle** information
4. **Preferred climate** including temperature and humidity ranges
5. **Application rate and method** of introduction

Reliable suppliers ship predators via special delivery and provide detailed
care and release instructions. Pick up shipments immediately upon arrival -
predators left in a hot mailbox can easily reach 120 degrees F (49 degrees C)
and die.

## Key Predatory Organisms

### Ladybugs (Ladybird Beetles)

Ladybugs are among the most recognizable predators. However, they present a
challenge in indoor gardens: if released during lighted hours, they are
attracted to HID lamps. If 500 ladybugs are released on Monday, by Friday
only a few survivors remain - the rest fly into the hot lamp and perish.
Release flying predators at night to extend their survival. Release about 50
ladybugs per plant.

### Predatory Mites

Predatory mites are among the most effective controls for [[spider-mites-cannabis]]. Common genera include Phytoseiulus and Mesoseiulus. They
thrive in humid conditions - extra humidity impairs pest mite lifecycles while
promoting predatory mite health. These tiny predators must be introduced to
each plant individually.

### Lacewing Larvae

Lacewing larvae are voracious predators with piercing-sucking mouthparts that
consume aphids, spider mites, thrips, and other soft-bodied pests. They are
among the most effective generalist predators available.

### Parasitic Wasps

Several species of parasitic wasps target specific cannabis pests:
- **Encarsia formosa** - Parasitizes whiteflies by laying eggs inside nymphs
- **Aphidius species** - Target aphids, available under trade names like
  Aphidend
- **Diglyphus isaea** - A chalcid wasp that attacks leaf miners
- **Thripobius species** - Parasitize thrips

## Best Practices for Biological Control

### Preparation

- Stop all toxic chemical sprays at least two weeks before introducing
  predators
- [[cervantes-pyrethrum-pyrethroid-insecticides]] and insecticidal
  soaps can be applied a few days before, provided residue is washed off
- Do not spray any chemicals after releasing predators

### Garden Conditions

- Gardens with [[cervantes-perpetual-harvest-cycle]] are
  ideal for predators since they provide continuous habitat
- Gardens that are not sterilized between crops support predator populations
  better
- Most effective indoor predators cannot fly, which keeps them in the garden
- Maintain appropriate temperature and humidity for each predator species

### Introduction Technique

- Introduce small predators to each plant individually
- Release flying predators when lights are off
- Follow supplier instructions for application rates precisely
- The rate at which predators control infestations is directly proportional to
  the number released
- Predators outbreed their victims, reproducing faster than pests can
  compensate

## Spider Mite Biological Control

Spider mites are the most common indoor cannabis pest. For biological control:
- Introduce predatory mites at the first sign of spider mite activity
- Maintain higher humidity to favor predators over pest mites
- Spray leaf undersides with cold water to knock off pest mites while
  applying water as a mist when predatory mites are present
- Remove leaves with 50 percent or more damage to reduce pest population

## Complementary Controls

Biological controls work best as part of a comprehensive IPM program that
includes [[cervantes-sticky-traps-pest-monitoring]],
proper [[cervantes-air-quality-ventilation]], and [[cervantes-companion-planting-pest-deterrent]] for pest deterrence.
