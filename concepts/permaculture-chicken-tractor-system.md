# Permaculture Chicken Tractor System

The chicken tractor is a foundational tool in permaculture design that integrates poultry into the garden and landscape in a mutually beneficial way. Toby Hemenway describes chicken tractors in Gaia"s Garden as a key example of "stacking functions" -- getting multiple useful outputs from a single element.

## What Is a Chicken Tractor?

A chicken tractor is a mobile, floorless chicken coop and run that can be moved around the garden, pasture, or yard. The birds have access to fresh ground, weeds, seeds, and insects, while simultaneously performing valuable work for the gardener: fertilizing, cultivating, and pest control.

## Functions Performed by Chicken Tractors

### Soil Fertilization

Chicken manure is rich in nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. As the tractor is moved across the landscape, it deposits a thin, even layer of manure that feeds the soil. Unlike stationary coops where manure concentrates and becomes a nuisance, the tractor distributes fertility precisely where it is needed.

### Weed and Pest Control

Chickens enthusiastically eat weeds, weed seeds, and insect pests. When placed in a garden bed after harvest, they will clean up remaining plant material, scratch up weed seeds, and consume pest larvae and eggs in the soil. This reduces pest pressure for the next planting cycle.

### Soil Cultivation

Chickens" scratching behavior lightly tills the top layer of soil, incorporating their manure and any mulch or organic material into the ground. This aeration improves soil structure without the destructive effects of mechanical tilling.

### Insect Management

In orchards and food forests, chicken tractors moved beneath trees help control codling moth, plum curculio, and other fruit tree pests by consuming fallen fruit and overwintering larvae.

### Food Production

The chickens themselves produce eggs and, eventually, meat. In a well-managed system, they can meet a significant portion of a household's protein needs while simultaneously improving the land.

## Design Considerations

### Size and Capacity

- Allow approximately 2 to 4 square feet per bird inside the sheltered area
- The run area should provide at least 8 to 10 square feet per bird
- A typical backyard tractor holds 4 to 12 birds
- Weight must be manageable for moving -- usually moved daily or every few days

### Structural Requirements

- **Floorless**: Essential -- birds must contact the ground
- **Weather protection**: A solid roof and at least one solid wall for shelter from rain and wind
- **Predator protection**: Wire mesh on all open sides, secured at ground level; consider hardware cloth rather than chicken wire for better predator resistance
- **Roosting bars**: Inside the sheltered section
- **Nest boxes**: Accessible from outside for easy egg collection
- **Feed and water stations**: Inside or accessible from the run
- **Mobility**: Wheels, skids, or handles for moving; design for the terrain

### Materials

- Lightweight lumber or PVC pipe for the frame
- Wire mesh or hardware cloth for the sides
- Corrugated metal, plywood, or recycled materials for the roof
- Wheels (bicycle wheels, lawnmower wheels) or skids for mobility

## Management Strategies

### Moving Frequency

- **Daily moves**: Best for intensive garden bed preparation; birds stay on each spot just long enough to clean up and lightly fertilize
- **Every 2-3 days**: Good for pasture management; allows birds to forage more thoroughly on each spot
- **Weekly moves**: Suitable for larger areas or when birds need more time to work the soil

### Seasonal Use

- **Spring**: Place in future garden beds to clean up and fertilize before planting
- **Summer**: Move through cover crops to terminate them and add fertility
- **Fall**: Run through harvested beds to clean up crop residue and consume pest larvae
- **Winter**: Station over compost piles (birds will scratch and turn the compost while keeping warm)

### Integration With Garden Systems

- **Before planting**: Move the tractor across beds for 1-2 weeks to clear weeds and add fertility; wait 2-3 weeks after removing birds before planting (to allow manure to mellow)
- **After harvest**: Run chickens through spent beds to clean up
- **Between crops**: Use chickens as a "living plow" to terminate cover crops without tilling
- **In orchards**: Move beneath fruit trees for pest control and fertilization

### Caution: Avoid Over-Fertilization

Chicken manure is "hot" -- very high in nitrogen. If birds stay too long in one spot, the manure concentration can burn plants or create nitrogen runoff. Move the tractor before the ground becomes bare or manure accumulates heavily.

## Chicken Tractor Variations

- **Lightweight A-frame**: Simple triangular design, easy to move manually, good for few birds
- **Wheeled cart**: Larger design with wheels, suitable for more birds, towed or pushed
- **Hoop house style**: Bent PVC or metal conduit covered with mesh, lightweight and spacious
- **Salatin-style**: Named after Joel Salatin; large, floorless shelters for pasture-based poultry
- **Chickshaw**: A wheeled chicken tractor designed for easy movement (see [[chickshaw-and-poultry-tractors]])
