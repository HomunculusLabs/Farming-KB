# Strategic Planning

**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison (1988)
**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Chapter 14 of the Designers' Manual, "Strategies for an Alternative Nation," presents Mollison's vision for strategic, bioregional, and national-scale application of permaculture principles. This goes far beyond individual site design to address the organisational, financial, and political structures needed for global transformation.

## The Alternative Nation Ethic

The minimal ethic of the alternative nation is:
- Care of earth
- Care of people
- Reduction of consumption

Mollison argues that bioregional and tribal congresses are the first move toward this alternative. No world centre is needed -- people meet as affinity groups. Centralised administrations and tax funding should be avoided.

## Bioregional Organisation (Section 14.5)

Bioregional organisation is presented as the primary strategy for scaling permaculture from individual sites to regional systems. The framework identifies 9 categories of bioregional activity:

### Category A: Food and Food Support Systems
- **A1:** Plant resources (nurseries, botanical gardens, research institutes, seed sources/exchanges, native reserves, demonstration farms, skilled people, publications, contractors, produce, species checklists)
- **A2:** Animal resources (breeders, species collections, fish breeders, native species, demonstration farms, vets, shearers)
- **A3:** Integrated pest management
- **A4-A6:** Soils, water services, fencing, trellis, fertilisers, machinery, glasshouses, geological resources

### Category B: Shelter and Buildings
Including housing, materials, construction skills, energy-efficient design

### Category C: Livelihoods and Support Services
Employment, crafts, processing, marketing, distribution

### Category D: Information, Media, Communication, Research
Publications, education, data networks, telecommunications

### Category E: Community and Security
Legal structures, conflict resolution, emergency services

### Category F: Social Life
Recreation, cultural activities, celebrations

### Category G: Health Services
Preventive health, medicinal plants, practitioners

### Category H: Future Trends
Long-range planning, environmental monitoring

### Category I: Transport Services
Road, rail, water, air access; clean transport development

### Category J: Appendices
Maps, species lists, resource inventories

## Ethical Investment Movement (Section 14.13)

The Permaculture Institute started teaching "banking on the earth" in 1983, following seminars with the E.F. Schumacher group. TOES (The Other Economic Summit) convenes annually in the UK, USA, Australia, and Canada.

### Investment Types
- **Short-term** (amortise 1-2 years): Draught-proofing, insulation, glasshouse, water tanks, irrigation, dams
- **Medium-term:** Bee/chicken/pig forage systems, aquaculture, nurseries, subdivisions, tours
- **Long-term:** reafforestation, village development, land rehabilitation, property trusts

### Disinvestment Criteria
Disinvest from companies that:
- Produce pollutants, wastes, poisons, armaments
- Have unsafe workplaces or deal in addictive substances
- Exploit people via unfair wages, bonded labour, discrimination
- Support corrupt regimes or lack public conscience

### Positive Investment Criteria
Invest in enterprises that:
- Assist conservation and reduce waste
- Grow clean food free of biocides
- Build energy-conserving houses or villages
- Produce clean transport or energy systems
- Assist people's self-reliance
- Found cooperatives and self-employed ventures

## Cooperatives: The Mondragon Model (Section 14.12)

The Mondragon Cooperatives of Spain demonstrate successful large-scale cooperative organisation:
- 96 worker-cooperatives, 17,000 worker-members in 30 years
- $5,000 investment per person (can be borrowed)
- 20% collective funds, 80% individual capital account
- Profit distribution: 10% to community, 20% reserves, 70% to workers
- Cooperative bank finances new co-ops up to 90%
- No redundancies -- workers retrained
- Pay ratio maximum 1:5
- One worker, one vote
- Full community services: schools, hospitals, university, housing

Later reduced from 3,000-5,000 to cooperatives of 300-500 to preserve individual identity. However, alliances of smaller cooperatives can manufacture any vehicle or machine if each produces a part.

## Commonwork

The commonwork model allows individuals to choose a set of duties and occupations that fit skills, choice, and age within bioregional networks. A few also develop special skills and become teachers in trades or disciplines.

## Land Access Strategies (Section 14.11)

- **Land lease system:** Oxfam model pairing land-wanters with land-havers
- **Garden/farm club:** Shares purchase farm 12 hours from city
- **City farms:** Urban agricultural production
- **Towns as farms:** Integrated urban-rural systems
- **Farm link:** Producer-consumer cooperative, 20-50 families linked to farms
- **Gleaning systems:** Redistribution of unwanted food from orchards and canneries

## Revolving Loans and Local Currency (Section 14.10)

Regional currency backed by reserves (can be living things like trees -- wealth increases). Notes dated, reissued every 4-5 years to prevent hoarding. Dollars supplied for needs, repaid in local currency.

Critical personnel: assessor-designers (permaculture team), accountants, broker-bankers, lawyer-trustees, real estate services. Can expand to travel club, credit union, food cooperative, farm club, educational/medical services.

## Key Principles

1. **Think bioregionally:** Organise at the watershed/region scale, not national
2. **Avoid centralisation:** No world centres; meet as affinity groups
3. **Ethical finance:** Direct investment toward earth rehabilitation
4. **Cooperative enterprise:** Use cooperative structures for all ventures
5. **Local currency:** Create community financial systems backed by real assets
6. **Land access:** Use trusts, leases, and community ownership models
7. **Full employment:** Design systems where everyone contributes meaningfully
