## The Mesophytic Forest Corridor

A key hypothesis for explaining the southern concentration involves
the historical distribution of mesophytic (moderately moist)
mountain forests. Of the 42 Psilocybe species recorded from Mexico,
34 (81%) are found in mesophytic forests — also known as cloud
forests or "bosque mesofilo de montaña." These forests form a
continuous ecological corridor from southern Mexico through Central
America into the Andes of South America.

This corridor is believed to represent a relatively stable
ecological niche that persisted through Pleistocene climatic
fluctuations, providing a refugium for fungal diversity. The
mesophytic forests are characterized by:

- Consistent high humidity and moderate temperatures
- Rich humus layers and abundant woody debris for substrate
- Year-round moisture availability
- Altitudinal range of 800-2500 meters

The hypothesis suggests that neurotropic fungi, being adapted to
specific humidity and temperature regimes, were able to persist and
diversify in these stable environments while being eliminated from
more climatically variable northern latitudes during glacial
periods.
