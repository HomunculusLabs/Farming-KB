# Hemenway Cover Crops Guide

Cover crops are planted specifically to build and hold soil and to smother weeds. They range from long-growing perennials to short-term green manures. Their leaves shield soil from hammering rains, carpet the earth with nutritious humus-building litter, crowd and shade out weeds, and drive roots deep to loosen earth, draw up nutrients, and place organic matter deeper than any plow.

## Why Cover Crops Matter

Roots are nature's subterranean humus builders. Root hairs -- almost-microscopic threads from root tips -- lead ephemeral existences, often living only a few hours. They absorb nutrients and channel them to main roots. Plants shed huge masses of roots constantly, not just in fall. This decaying organic matter builds humus deep in the soil. Many cover crops send roots 10-15 feet deep -- no plow will ever incorporate organic matter to that depth.

## Root Growth Cycles

Root growth is controlled by overlapping cycles: day/night, wet/dry, cold/warm, and soil organism activity. After heavy rain, ground becomes saturated and root hairs die from lack of oxygen (explains why cucumbers/squash sometimes wilt after rain). As soil dries, air flows into empty pores, and root hairs surge into growth toward nutrient pockets. Growth accelerates as oxygen rises and moisture approaches ideal range. As soil dries further, root hairs wither and whole root sections die en masse, decomposing. With next rain, new roots begin again.

This wet/dry cycle drives root-dieback, which builds deep humus -- a benefit only cover crops can provide.

## Choosing Cover Crops

- **To add nitrogen**: choose legumes (peas, beans, clovers, vetches)
- **To open heavy soil**: daikon radish, rape, mustard, oats, chicory, alfalfa
- **For drought resistance**: sweet clovers, lablab, pinto beans, sesbania
- **For weed suppression**: buckwheat, black-eyed peas, rye
- **For insectary**: phacelia, clovers (many listed attract beneficial insects)
- **For cold hardiness**: rye (-10F), Austrian winter pea (0F), vetches
- **Mix legumes with grasses**: combines nitrogen fixation with biomass production
