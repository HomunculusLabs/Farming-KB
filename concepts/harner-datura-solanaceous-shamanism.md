# Datura and Solanaceous Plants in Shamanism

## The Solanaceous Hallucinogen Family

The order **Solanaceae** (potato family) contains probably the single most important group of plants used by mankind to contact the supernatural. Hallucinogenic members include:

- **Datura** -- numerous species (Jimson weed, devil's apple, thorn apple, angel's trumpet, toloache)
- **Mandrake** (*Mandragora*)
- **Henbane** (*Hyoscyamus*)
- **Deadly nightshade / Belladonna** (*[[harner-european-witchcraft-hallucinogens]] for full treatment. In brief:
- Datura was a key ingredient in **witches' flying ointments**
- Its tropane alkaloids, absorbed through the skin, produced the visionary experiences that were interpreted as flights to Sabbats
- Lewin (1964): "The mental disorder caused by substances of this kind, for instance Datura, has even instigated some persons to accuse themselves before a tribunal."

## Cross-Cultural Pattern

Datura's use across cultures follows a consistent pattern:
1. Employed by specialists (shamans, witches, healers) for **contact with the supernatural**
2. Used in **vision quests** to gain knowledge, power, or diagnostic information
3. Its dangerous toxicity requires expert knowledge for safe use
4. The hallucinations it produces (often dark, frightening, involving transformation into animals) are interpreted culturally as encounters with spirits or demons
5. Atropine's skin absorbability enables unique methods of administration (topical ointments, eye drops)

## The Jivaro Datura Vision Quest

Beyond its use in curing, Datura plays a central role in Jivaro **vision quests**. The most typical apparitions seen during both Banisteriopsis and Datura trances are pairs of giant anacondas or jaguars fighting through the forest. These visions are interpreted as encounters with supernatural beings and are a primary means through which shamans acquire new knowledge and power.

Jaguars are frequently drawn by Jivaro shamans after Datura trances, serving as visual records of their encounters. The consistency of jaguar and anaconda visions across different hallucinogenic plants (ayahuasca and Datura) suggests that these animals occupy a privileged position in the Jivaro supernatural worldview rather than being arbitrary drug-induced imagery.

## Tropane Alkaloid Pharmacology

The tropane alkaloids share a common bicyclic structure and act primarily as **anticholinergic agents** -- they block the neurotransmitter acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors throughout the body. This produces a characteristic syndrome:

- **Antimuscarinic peripheral effects**: Dry mouth, dilated pupils (mydriasis), blurred vision, elevated heart rate, urinary retention, elevated body temperature
- **Central anticholinergic effects**: Confusion, agitation, visual and auditory hallucinations, amnesia, delirium
- The ratio of central to peripheral effects varies among the different alkaloids and different Datura species
- Atropine's ability to be absorbed through intact skin is pharmacologically unique among hallucinogenic compounds and enables the topical [[cannabis-administration-methods-comparison]] used in [[harner-european-witchcraft-hallucinogens]]

## Comparison with Ayahuasca

In contrast to ayahuasca (which is typically used in group settings and produces primarily visual hallucinations with relative mental clarity), Datura produces:
- More **deliriant** effects with genuine confusion and inability to distinguish hallucination from reality
- Greater **physical danger** due to narrow [[cannabis-therapeutic-index-overview]]
- More frequently **frightening** experiences involving transformation into animals and encounters with malevolent beings
- Primarily **individual** use by specialists rather than communal ceremonies
