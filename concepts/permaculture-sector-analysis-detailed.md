# Permaculture Sector Analysis Detailed

updated: 2026-18-04

Sector analysis maps the external energies -- sun, wind, rain, wildfire, water flow, wildlife, noise, and views -- that pass through a site from outside. It is a core permaculture design tool described in Chapter 3 of Mollison's Designers' Manual, used alongside zone planning to determine optimal placement of all elements.

## The Sector Concept

Sectors are site-specific compass directions from which we can expect energy or other factors. Some factors we invite in (sunlight for technologies and plant growth); some we exclude (unpleasant views, fire, cold winds); most we regulate to our advantage by placing zonal components to do so.

Whereas settlement or house is the ground zero for zones, it is the through point for sectors. Energies from outside can be thought of as arrows winging their way towards the home, carrying both destructive and beneficial energies. We need to erect shields, deflectors, or collectors.

## The Six Main Sectors

### Sun Sector
The sun sector determines solar access for passive heating, crop growth, and solar technologies. Design actions include:
- Opening the sector to admit maximum sunlight to houses and gardens
- Placing deciduous trees to shade summer sun while admitting winter sun
- Using reflective surfaces to direct light into shaded areas
- Placing solar collectors, greenhouses, and cold frames in optimal sun sector

### Wind Sector
Wind sectors identify directions of cold winds, hot winds, and beneficial breezes. Design actions:
- Block or deflect cold/hot winds with windbreaks, earthbanks, or buildings
- Channel cool breezes through buildings for ventilation
- Place wind-sensitive plants and animals in sheltered positions
- Use wind power generation in persistent wind sectors

### Fire Sector
The wildfire sector identifies the direction from which catastrophic fire may approach. Design actions:
- Place fire-prone species (pines, eucalypts) away from structures in Zone 4
- Create firebreaks using ponds, roads, bare ground, or fire-resistant vegetation
- Place structures away from the fire sector or behind protective elements
- Design water systems for fire fighting

### Water Sector
Water flow sectors map incoming runoff, flood paths, and groundwater movement. Design actions:
- Direct water flow to storages using swales, diversion drains, and contour banks
- Place structures above flood lines
- Use water flow for aquaculture, irrigation, and wetland creation
- Protect structures from erosion and flood damage

### Wildlife Sector
Wildlife sectors identify movement corridors, foraging areas, and habitat zones. Design actions:
- Create wildlife corridors connecting Zone 5 to other zones
- Place nest boxes, forage plantings, and habitat features
- Exclude unwanted wildlife with fencing or deterrent plantings
- Integrate beneficial wildlife (insect predators, pollinators, birds)

### Noise and View Sectors
These sectors address sensory inputs. Design actions:
- Screen unpleasant views with vegetation, earthbanks, or structures
- Block traffic noise with earth walls and dense plantings
- Frame pleasant views and admit cooling breezes
- Place noise-sensitive activities away from noise sectors

## The Placement Process

To place any component: imagine rotating the zone diagram around Zone 0. The component stops rotating when it works to govern energies in the sector diagram. For example, a pine tree goes in Zone 4 (infrequent visits) away from the fire danger sector, towards the cold wind sector, and should also bear edible nuts.

A poultry shed should border Zone 1 (frequent visits), be away from the fire sector, border the annual garden (for manure), back onto the forage system, possibly attach to a greenhouse, and form part of a windbreak system.

## Combining Zone and Sector Analysis

Zone and sector diagrams are overlaid to create a spiderweb of placements. The basic energy-conserving rules:
- Every element (plant, animal, or structure) must be placed so it serves at least two or more functions
- Every function (water collection, fire protection) is served in two or more ways

## Tools for Sector Analysis

Sector analysis is conducted using the [[permaculture-mapping-and-design-tools]] methods: base maps, overlay maps, and site observation. Sector arrows are drawn on overlays showing the direction and intensity of each energy flow.
