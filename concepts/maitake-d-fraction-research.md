# Maitake D-Fraction Research

Grifola frondosa, known as **Maitake** (Japanese for "dancing mushroom"), produces several bioactive beta-glucan and glycoprotein complexes with strong antitumour activity. The mushroom is both edible and medicinal, with growing commercial production. See also [[maitake-cultivation]] for cultivation methods.

## Key Compounds

### Grifolan
A (1-3)-beta-glucan similar to schizophyllan in primary structure. Grifolan enhances mRNA levels of IL-6, IL-1, and TNF-alpha in macrophages, functioning as a novel macrophage activator that increases cytokine production. The cytokine gene expression patterns have been demonstrated via RT-PCR, confirming that grifolan acts at the transcriptional level. Cytokine gene expression differs by cell type -- peritoneal exudate cells, splenocytes, and hepatocytes show distinct kinetics of response.

### Grifron-D (GD)
A highly purified **beta-1,6 glucan branched with beta-1,3 linkage**. GD has considerable immunomodulating and antitumour activities in animal models and is **orally bioavailable** -- a significant advantage over many other mushroom polysaccharides that require intravenous or intraperitoneal administration. This oral bioavailability places Maitake in a more accessible therapeutic category than [[lentinan-pre-clinical-antitumour-models]] or schizophyllan-research|schizophyllan, which typically require injection.

### D-Fraction
The Maitake D-fraction and crude Maitake powder have demonstrated remarkable inhibition of metastasis in immuno-competent mouse models, especially in prevention of **hepatic metastases** -- reduced by 81% (Maitake powder) to 91% (D-fraction) in experimental studies. This level of metastasis inhibition is among the highest reported for any mushroom polysaccharide preparation.

## Direct Cytotoxic Effects

Unlike many mushroom polysaccharides that act purely through immune modulation, Grifron-D has demonstrated **direct cytotoxic effects** on human prostate cancer cells (PC9 and PC-3) in vitro:

- Dose-response studies showed >95% cell death attained in 24 hours with concentrations of 480 micrograms/ml or greater
- Cell death occurs through **apoptosis** mediated by oxidative stress (elevated lipid peroxidation, oxidative membrane damage)
- **Vitamin C acts synergistically** with Grifron-D -- combinations as low as 30-60 micrograms/ml GD with 200 microM vitamin C were as effective as GD alone at 480 micrograms/ml
- **Carmustine/GD combination** showed 90% reduction in cell viability, suggesting chemosensitising potential
- GD may potentiate anticancer drugs through inactivation of **glyoxalase I**, a vital detoxifying enzyme

The dual mechanism of both immune activation AND direct cytotoxicity makes Maitake unique among medicinal mushroom preparations. Most other mushroom polysaccharides (including [[turkey-tail-mushroom-cancer-research]] and [[lentinan-pre-clinical-antitumour-models]]) operate primarily through immune modulation rather than direct tumour cell killing.

## Clinical Trials

### Japanese Non-Randomised Study (Nanba, 1997)
A study of 165 advanced cancer patients receiving oral D-fraction plus crude Maitake tablets, or D-fraction plus placebo, in addition to chemotherapy:
- **11 out of 15 advanced hepatocellular carcinomas** showed tumour regression or significant symptomatic improvement with D-fraction plus Maitake
- When D-fraction plus Maitake was combined with chemotherapy, overall response rates increased by **12-28%** across all cancer types
- Patients reportedly recovered from severe chemotherapy side-effects when D-fraction was given

### Chinese Pilot Studies
Early studies involving 63 cancer patients reported response rates (partial and complete) of:
- **95% for solid tumours**
- **90% for leukaemia** (type not specified)

These response rates are remarkably high and warrant further controlled investigation.

### FDA Approval
The US FDA has approved Grifron-D for trial under an Investigational New Drug Application (IND) for patients with advanced cancer. The FDA has also exempted Maitake polysaccharides from Phase I study because of limited side-effects. Clinical trials are underway in breast, prostate, lung, liver, and gastric cancers in the US and Japan.

## Mechanism of Action

As a [[medicinal-mushroom-immune-modulation]], Maitake compounds activate innate defences (macrophages, neutrophils, NK cells) and stimulate humoral and cell-mediated immune systems. The [[beta-glucan-receptor-binding]] mechanism involves CR3 (complement receptor 3) on immune cells, consistent with other mushroom beta-glucans. The dual mechanism of both immune activation and direct cytotoxicity makes Maitake unique among medicinal mushroom preparations.

Grifolan's cytokine induction profile (IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha) activates the immune cascade at the macrophage level, which then communicates with T-cells and NK cells. The T-cell dependence of antitumour activity has been confirmed through experiments with cyclosporin A.

## Cardiovascular and Metabolic Effects

Beyond cancer, Maitake has demonstrated beneficial effects in other areas:
- **Cholesterol reduction**: Listed among six species that reduce total cholesterol levels
- **Blood pressure**: One of three species shown to reduce arterial blood pressure
- **Blood sugar**: Active in non-insulin-dependent diabetes; can diminish glycemia, insulemia, and blood triglyceride levels
- **Blood platelet binding**: Reduced platelet aggregation (in vitro)

## Safety Profile

Grifron-D has few side effects. Anecdotal clinical reports suggest it may alleviate some side-effects of chemotherapy. The apparent success of crude Maitake powder by oral administration supports its suitability as a nutriceutical. The FDA's exemption from Phase I studies reflects the safety profile.

## Comparison with Other Mushroom Compounds

The [[shiitake-medicinal-compounds]] from shiitake and D-fraction from Maitake share [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-turkey-tail-psk-and-psp]]'s clinical validation pathway, with increasing evidence of synergy between Maitake fractions and conventional chemotherapy. Key differentiators of Maitake:
- Oral bioavailability (vs. injectable lentinan)
- Direct cytotoxic effects (vs. purely immunomodulatory PSK)
- Highest metastasis inhibition rates reported
- Synergy with vitamin C
