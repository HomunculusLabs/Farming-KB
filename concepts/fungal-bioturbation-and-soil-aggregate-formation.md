### Hyphal Reticulation
In soil, hyphae of both saprotrophic and ectomycorrhizal basidiomycetes
that ramify through soil often aggregate to form rapidly extending cords
or rhizomorphs. These multicellular structures serve as conduits for
long-distance transport of water, nutrients, and carbon, while also
contributing to the physical stabilization of soil macroaggregates.

### Ectomycorrhizal Hyphal Cords
Ectomycorrhizal fungi produce robust hydrophobic multicellular hyphal
cords that are well adapted for water transport and soil penetration.
These cords physically stabilize soil and create channels for water
movement and root growth. The external mycelium of ectomycorrhizal fungi
extends far beyond the root zone, creating an extensive network that
influences soil structure across large volumes of soil.

## Role in Biogeochemical Cycling

Fungi are ubiquitous in soil environments and can comprise the largest
pool of biomass. They play important roles in maintenance of soil
structure owing to their filamentous growth habit. Their activities in
carbon, nitrogen and other biogeochemical cycles are intimately linked
to their physical effects on soil. As nutrient recyclers, fungi transform
organic and inorganic substrates, and the products of these transformations
influence the chemical environment in which soil aggregates form and
persist.

## Interaction with Soil Fauna

The physical structures created by fungi provide habitat and food resources
for soil fauna including collembola, mites, and nematodes. These organisms
graze on fungal hyphae and contribute to soil aggregation through their
own activities. The interaction between fungal networks and soil fauna
creates complex feedback loops that influence soil structure at multiple
scales.

## Implications for Ecosystem Function

Soil aggregation influences water infiltration, erosion resistance,
nutrient retention, and carbon storage. The fungal contributions to
aggregate formation described here are therefore central to ecosystem
functioning. Loss of fungal biomass and diversity through land management
practices such as tillage, fungicide application, and monoculture can
compromise soil structure and the ecosystem services it supports.
Understanding the mechanisms of fungal soil aggregate formation is
essential for developing sustainable soil management practices.
