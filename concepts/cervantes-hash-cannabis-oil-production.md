## Hash Oil Overview

Hash oil is a concentrate made by dissolving hashish or marijuana

activated charcoal can remove virtually all of these impurities,
producing a cleaner, lighter-colored product.

Hash oil never achieved widespread popularity because it is
inconvenient to smoke and many users object to the health risks
associated with residual solvents. The oil can be applied to joint
papers, cigarettes, pipe screens, or hot knives. A dedicated oil
pipe, consisting of a stem with a glass bubble on one end, is a
common smoking method. A small amount of oil is placed inside the
glass bubble, and heat is applied to vaporize the THC for inhalation.

### Isomerization

Isomerization is a chemical extraction process that was popular in
the 1970s and early 1980s but has since fallen out of favor. The
process converts CBD to THC using harsh chemical solvents including
white gas, sulfuric acid, and hydrochloric acid. While effective at
increasing potency, the dangerous chemicals involved and the risk
of residual contamination make this method impractical for most
modern growers.

## Butane Hash Oil

Butane cannabis oil is produced by passing butane gas through
finely chopped cannabis material. The butane dissolves the THC
and carries it out through a drain into a collection container.
The captured butane-THC solution is then placed in a warm water
bath, allowing the butane to evaporate completely. The residual
honey-colored oil is scraped from the bottom of the collection pan.

Butane hash oil production is extremely dangerous. The process
involves handling large quantities of highly flammable butane in
open containers. The fumes are toxic, and even a small flame or
electrical spark can cause an explosion. Production should only
be attempted in well-ventilated outdoor locations with appropriate
fire safety measures in place. Despite these risks, butane
extraction produces some of the cleanest and most potent oils
available.
