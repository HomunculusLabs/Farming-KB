# Permaculture Ground Covers and Accumulators

[[ground-cover-plants]] serve critical functions in permaculture systems: protecting soil from erosion, suppressing weeds, retaining moisture, accumulating nutrients from deep soil layers, and providing fodder or habitat. "A Selection of Permaculture Plants" (Nugent & Boniface, 2004) addresses ground covers through utility lists, fodder grasses, and nitrogen-fixing pioneer species.

## Ground Covers with Low Fire Potential

## Nitrogen-Fixing Ground Covers

Several nitrogen-fixing species function as ground covers or low shrubs:

### Tagasaste (Chamaecytisus palmensis)
Can be maintained as low bushy hedge for stock access. Regular cutting or browsing extends life and keeps habit low. Well-fenced hedgerows can be constantly grazed. Seed (25% protein) is prolific poultry forage, dropped to ground as self-harvest feed.

### Tree Lupin (Lupinus arboreus)
Evergreen perennial shrub to about 2 m. Nitrogen-fixing, good nurse crop. Tolerant of frost. Can be cut hard to supply mulch rich in nitrogen. Bee forage flowering late winter to early summer, with second flowering in autumn.

### Lespedeza bicolor (Miracle Plant)
Leguminous shrub to 3 m. Long shallow roots spread along ground; nodules are very efficient at nitrogen fixing. High protein stock feed. Seed is good poultry feed. Planted as nurse crop to pine trees in Korea.

### Kennedia spp (Running Postman)
Australian native leguminous ground cover with low fire potential.

### Lotus bertholetii (Parrot Beak)
Leguminous ground cover with low fire potential.

## Perennial Fodder Grasses as Ground Covers

### Pampas Grass (Cortaderia selloana)
Clumping perennial to 3 m. Forms fountain of cascading leaves. Suited to cool and heat, acid and alkaline soils, waterlogged areas. Fast recovery from grazing. Good windbreak and shelter. One acre at 1.8 m spacing equals 3000 bales of hay. Deep roots bring up trace elements controlling grass tetany. May be invasive in reforestation. Companion to poplars.

### Elephant Grass (Pennisetum purpureum)
Clumping perennial to 4 m. Native to tropical Africa. Tolerant of different soil conditions, remarkably drought tolerant for a high-rainfall species. Very fast recovery from browsing. "Hungry" for nutrients but dislikes waterlogging.

### Bana Grass (P. purpureum x americanum)
Hybrid with broader leaf, less aggressive than elephant grass, produces little seed. Propagated by laying whole stems into shallow trenches.

### Reed Canary Grass (Phalaris arundinacea)
Bulky grass for animal bedding and fodder. Good quail and duck forage plant.

## Nutrient-Accumulating Plants

### Duckweed (Lemna, Wolffia, Spirodella spp)
Among the most vigorously growing plants on the planet (doubling every 3 days). Concentrate boron, aluminium, manganese, iron, titanium, copper, and cobalt from water. Spirodella oligorhiza has potential to produce feed value equivalent to 60 ha of soybeans in 1 ha (protein 37-45%).

### Azolla spp
Small, free-floating fern hosting Anabaena azolla alga in leaves, which fixes atmospheric nitrogen. Used extensively in Vietnam as companion in rice paddies. Feeds tilapia and other fish. Valuable for pigs, poultry, and waterfowl. Controls mosquitoes and weeds by covering water surface. Good nitrogen-rich mulch material with water-holding properties similar to peat moss.

### Pampas Grass
Deep roots bring up trace elements from subsoil, controlling grass tetany in livestock.

## Plants for Pasture Integration

The following plants can be introduced to pasture to increase both productivity and honey production:

- Clovers -- Up to 400 kg/ha nectar yield estimated in Russia; crimson clover particularly high
- Cowpea
- Lotus spp
- Lucerne -- Very long flowering period (78-285 kg/ha/day measured in USA); good lock-up fodder
- Lupins

## [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] with Ground Covers

Key ground-level companions for fruit and nut trees:

- **Lucerne** -- Companion to almond, apple, avocado, citrus, fig, jujube, lichee, mulberry, olive, peach, walnut
- **Garlic** -- The most universally recommended companion herb for fruit trees
- **Chives** -- Companion to apple
- **Nasturtiums** -- Companion to apple, cabbage family, cucumbers
- **Borage** -- Companion to strawberries
- **Marigold** -- Companion to lettuce, potatoes, tomatoes, roses, beans

## Design Notes

- Layer ground covers under and between trees at multiple heights
- Use nitrogen-fixing ground covers to build soil fertility between tree plantings
- Fire-resistant ground covers are essential in fire-prone regions
- Aquatic ground covers (duckweed, azolla) convert water nutrients into harvestable biomass
- Fodder grasses provide drought reserve while protecting soil
- Companion planting with herbs creates functional guilds around fruit trees
