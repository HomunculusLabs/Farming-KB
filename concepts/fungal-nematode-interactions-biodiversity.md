# Fungal Nematode Interactions Biodiversity

Updated: 2026-04-18

Interactions between fungi and nematodes represent a significant dimension of soil biodiversity, with implications for biological control, soil health assessment, and ecosystem function.

## Types of Fungal-Nematode Interactions

### Predatory Fungi

Some fungi are active predators of nematodes:

- **Adhesive networks** — species of *Arthrobotrys* and related genera produce three-dimensional adhesive networks that trap passing nematodes
- **Adhesive knobs** — specialized structures on hyphae that adhere to nematode cuticle on contact
- **Constricting rings** — *Drechslerella* species produce rings that swell rapidly when triggered by nematode touch, constricting and capturing the prey
- **Toxic droplets** — some species produce adhesive droplets containing nematotoxic compounds

### Endoparasitic Fungi

These fungi infect nematodes from within:

- Spores ingested by nematodes germinate inside the digestive tract
- Hyphae penetrate the nematode body cavity
- The fungus consumes the nematode from the inside, eventually emerging to produce spores

### Nematophagous (Nematode-Trapping) Fungi

Species that switch between saprotrophic and predatory lifestyles depending on environmental conditions. The presence of nematodes in the soil triggers the production of trapping structures — a remarkable example of phenotypic plasticity.

### Nematode-Feeding Fungi

Some fungi obtain nutrients from nematodes without killing them, forming associations analogous to mycorrhizae.

## Nematodes as Fungal Vectors

Nematodes can also serve as vectors for fungal dispersal:

- Carrying fungal spores on their body surfaces
- Transporting spores through soil profiles
- Creating entry wounds in plant roots that facilitate fungal infection

## Agricultural Significance

Plant-parasitic nematodes cause billions of dollars in crop losses annually. Fungal-nematode interactions offer natural biological control:

- Nematophagous fungi are sold commercially as biological nematicides
- Understanding these interactions helps design integrated pest management
- Soil management practices that support predatory fungi reduce nematode damage

## Soil Health Indicators

The diversity and abundance of nematophagous fungi correlate with soil health:

- High predatory fungal diversity indicates active soil food webs
- The ratio of predatory to saprotrophic fungi reflects trophic complexity
- These organisms are sensitive to soil disturbance, chemical inputs, and organic matter levels

## Research Methods

Standard methods include:

- Baermann funnel extraction for nematodes from soil samples
- Soil sprinkling technique on selective media
- Baiting with live nematodes to capture predatory fungi
- Molecular detection of nematophagous fungi from soil DNA
- Microscopic observation of trapping structures

## Ecological Context

Fungal-nematode interactions are embedded in the larger soil food web. Nematode-feeding fungi connect the bacterial/fungal decomposition channels to higher trophic levels, playing a crucial role in nutrient cycling and energy flow through soil ecosystems.
