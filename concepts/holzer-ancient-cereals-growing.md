# Holzer Ancient Cereals Growing

[[comparison-sepp-holzer-vs-biodynamic]] grows [[permaculture-quinoa-growing-guide]] varieties at his Krameterhof farm at 1,000-1,500 meters altitude in Austria. These traditional varieties are well-suited to marginal conditions and require minimal inputs.

## Ancient Grain Varieties

### Einkorn (Triticum monococcum)
- One of the oldest cultivated grains, dating back over 10,000 years
- Very hardy and undemanding; grows well on poor soils
- Lower yield than modern wheat but higher nutritional value
- Naturally disease-resistant
- Lower gluten content than modern wheat

### Emmer (Triticum dicoccum)
- Ancient hulled wheat, precursor to durum wheat
- Hardy and adaptable to various conditions
- Popular for traditional breads and soups
- Hulled -- requires special processing to remove hulls

### Rye (Secale cereale)
- Both perennial and annual varieties exist
- Extremely frost-hardy and undemanding
- Grows well at high altitudes and on poor soils
- Excellent for bread-making
- Brandroggen (fire rye) is a traditional Austrian variety
- Lungau tauernroggen: local variety still grown by Holzer
- Wild rye (Secale multicaule): good forage, good yield

### Other Cereals
- Sorghum (Sorghum dochna): annual, prefers sunny areas
- Oats and wheat also grown as [[comparison-green-manure-guide-vs-mulch-types-and-effects]] and feed

## Growing Conditions

- Ancient grains are undemanding and grow quickly
- Prefer poor, unfertilised soils over enriched garden soil
- Excess nitrogen (from fertilised soil) raises nitrogen levels beyond what plants can cope with
- Easier to grow ancient grain in a gravel pit than on fertilised garden soil
- Well-suited to high-altitude terraces
- Sow cereals on terraces already planted with [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]] for polyculture

## Green Manure Use

Cereals serve multiple purposes:
- Green [[green-manure-crops-detailed]]: sow on new plots as pioneer plants because they are undemanding and fast-growing
- Feed grain: sown in paddocks as mixture of cereals, legumes, and root crops
- Sow paddocks with cereal/legume/root crop mixture and do not harvest
- Crops provide best feed for cattle and pigs in short time with minimal energy input

## Harvesting and Processing

- Harvest with scythe and bind into sheaves
- Gather sheaves into stooks to ripen for 2-3 weeks in the field
- For larger plots, harvest second-year grain at end of August or beginning of September
- Bring sheaves into barn and arrange in troughs
- Lean sheaves in circle, ears pointing to centre, working from middle outward
- Store in troughs until November when threshing begins

## Threshing

- Use threshing machine powered by old paraffin or petrol engine
- Performed during bad weather when no outside work possible
- Laborious, dusty, exhausting work
- Ears chopped up and separated from straw
- Clean threshing machine between varieties

## Winnowing

- Hand-operated winnowing machine produces stream of air
- Light material (blind ears, straw pieces) blown away -- collected for livestock bedding
- Light material mixed with bran and hot water for feed paste
- Fine material (broken grains, sand particles) falls through sieve -- collected for chicken feed
- Bulky material (stalks, stones, deer/hare droppings) left for chickens to scratch out grains
- Clean seed and milling grain comes from main chute

## Zero-Waste Philosophy

Everything from the threshing and winnowing process is used on the farm:
- Straw: livestock bedding
