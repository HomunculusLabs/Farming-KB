## Ancient Grain Varieties

### Einkorn (Triticum monococcum)
- One of the oldest cultivated grains, dating back over 10,000 years
- Very hardy and undemanding; grows well on poor soils
- Lower yield than modern wheat but higher nutritional value
- Naturally disease-resistant
- Lower gluten content than modern wheat

### Emmer (Triticum dicoccum)
- Ancient hulled wheat, precursor to durum wheat
- Hardy and adaptable to various conditions
- Popular for traditional breads and soups
- Hulled -- requires special processing to remove hulls

### Rye (Secale cereale)
- Both perennial and annual varieties exist
- Extremely frost-hardy and undemanding
- Grows well at high altitudes and on poor soils
- Excellent for bread-making
- Brandroggen (fire rye) is a traditional Austrian variety
- Lungau tauernroggen: local variety still grown by Holzer
- Wild rye (Secale multicaule): good forage, good yield

### Other Cereals
- Sorghum (Sorghum dochna): annual, prefers sunny areas
- Oats and wheat also grown as [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]] for polyculture
