# Oyster Mushroom Substrate Preparation

Oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.) are among the easiest mushrooms to cultivate because they grow on a wide variety of agricultural waste materials. Proper substrate preparation — cleaning and partially sterilizing the growing medium — is the most critical step for success.

## Suitable Substrates

### Straw (Most Common)
- Wheat straw: most widely used, excellent results
- Oat straw: good alternative
- Rice straw: works well in tropical climates
- Barley straw: acceptable
- Straw should be fresh (not moldy), dry, and free of seed treatment chemicals

### Wood-Based Substrates
- Hardwood sawdust (oak, beech, maple — avoid cedar and redwood)
- Hardwood chips (supplemented with bran)
- Paper/cardboard (shredded, soaked)
- Cotton waste (from textile industry)
- Coffee grounds (can be used fresh without pasteurization at up to 25% of substrate)

### Other Substrates
- Sugarcane bagasse
- Corn cobs (crushed)
- Banana leaves/stems
- Cottonseed hulls
- Sorghum stalks

## Supplementation

### Why Supplement
- Raw substrates (straw, sawdust) are low in nitrogen
- Adding nitrogen-rich supplements boosts yield 50-200%
- Supplemented substrates require sterilization (not just pasteurization)

### Common Supplements
- Wheat bran: 10-20% by dry weight (most common)
- Rice bran: 10-20%
- Soybean meal: 5-10%
- Cottonseed meal: 5-10%
- Coffee grounds: up to 25% (unsupplemented — already nutrient-rich)

## Pasteurization (For Unsupplemented Substrates)

### Hot Water Method
1. Chop straw into 2-4 inch pieces
2. Submerge in water at 160-180°F (71-82°C) for 1-2 hours
3. Drain and cool to below 80°F (27°C) before inoculation
4. Moisture content target: 60-70% (squeeze test: a few drops of water escape)

### Steam Pasteurization
1. Load chopped straw into a container or bag
2. Steam at 160°F (71°C) for 8-12 hours
3. Cool and inoculate

### Lime Method (Cold Pasteurization)
1. Soak straw in water with 2% hydrated lime (calcium hydroxide) for 12-24 hours
2. The high pH (12+) kills most competitors
3. Drain, rinse if needed, and inoculate
4. Less reliable than heat methods but requires no fuel

## Sterilization (For Supplemented Substrates)

### Pressure Sterilization
- Autoclave at 15 PSI (121°C) for 90-120 minutes
- Required for supplemented substrates (bran + sawdust)
- Ensures complete elimination of competitor organisms

### Flow-Through Steam
- Steam at 212°F (100°C) for 8-12 hours
- Works for supplemented sawdust blocks

## Moisture Content
- Target: 60-70% by weight
- Test: Squeeze a handful firmly — should form a ball that holds shape, with 1-5 drops of water
- Too wet (>75%): anaerobic conditions, bacterial contamination, poor mycelium growth
- Too dry (<55%): slow colonization, reduced yield, dry mushrooms

## Container Options

### Polypropylene Bags (Most Common)
- Autoclavable bags with filter patch for gas exchange
- 5-10 lb capacity per bag
- Inoculate through top, seal, incubate

### Buckets
- Drill 2-inch holes around sides (covered with tape during colonization)
- Fill with substrate, inoculate, cover holes
- Remove tape at fruiting

### Straw Logs
- Pack pasteurized straw into mesh tubing
- Inoculate layer by layer
- Hang vertically for fruiting

## Common Problems

### Contamination
- Green mold (Trichoderma): most common competitor. Indicates insufficient pasteurization or dirty spawn
- Bacterial slime: wet spot, sour smell. Reduce moisture content
- Cobweb mold (Dactylium): fine gray web over mycelium. Increase fresh air exchange

### Slow Colonization
- Temperature too low (optimal: 70-80°F / 21-27°C)
- Moisture too high or too low
- Old or weak spawn
- Substrate too compacted (lack of air exchange)
