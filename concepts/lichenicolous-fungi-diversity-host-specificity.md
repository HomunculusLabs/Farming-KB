# Lichenicolous Fungi: Diversity and Host Specificity

[[lichenicolous-fungi]] — fungi that grow on [[lichens]] — represent a remarkably diverse and specialized group within the [[fungicolous-fungi]]. They range from biotrophic parasites to destructive necrotrophs and are found across multiple orders of Ascomycota and Basidiomycota. Their host specificity varies from extreme specialists restricted to a single host species to generalists capable of colonizing multiple lichen genera.

## Ascomycete Lichenicolous Fungi

### Arthoniales

Most species of Arthonia, Lecanographa, and Opegrapha are lichenized, but a significant proportion are lichenicolous:

- **Arthonia**: Approximately 45 species are lichenicolous (Clauzade et al. 1989; Grube et al. 1995), growing on diverse lichen hosts including [[xanthoria-elegans]] (A. molendoi)
- **Lecanographa**: Three species are lichenicolous (Egea and Torrente 1994)
- **Opegrapha**: 35 species are lichenicolous (Clauzade et al. 1989; Hafellner 1994), including O. cladoniae, which is confined to Cladonia species
- **Plectocarpon**: At least 23 species are lichenicolous (Diederich and Etayo 1994)

### Caliciales

Species of the Caliciales can be lichenized, lichenicolous, fungicolous, or saprotrophic on bark or wood. Several genera comprise a high percentage of lichenicolous species, many of which grow on other lichenized species of Caliciales:

- **Microcalicium arenarium**: Develops on saxicolous lichens Psilolechia lucida and P. clavulifera (which have a Stichococcus photobiont) or directly on saxicolous Stichococcus species
- **Chaenothecopsis, Sphinctrina**: Comprise a high percentage of lichenicolous species

### Lecanorales

- **Toninia**: Comprises numerous lichens, including several species parasitic on other lichens, and some nonlichenized lichenicolous fungi
- **Buellia, Carbonea, Catillaria, Rhizocarpon, Tephromela, Thelocarpon**: Include mostly lichenized species, some of which develop on other lichens; a few species are nonlichenized but lichenicolous
- **Phacopsis**: 14 species, exclusively lichenicolous; most species grow on members of the Parmeliaceae

### Ostropales

In the Odontotremataceae:
- **Odontotrema** (including Lethariicola): Comprises 15 lichenicolous and some nonlichenicolous species (Lumbsch and Hawksworth 1990)
- **Nanostictis**: 3 described + 2 undescribed species (J. Etayo and P. Diederich, personal communication)

### Verrucariales

Although most Verrucariales species are lichenized, several related genera include only nonlichenized, lichenicolous species:

- **Adelococcus**: Three species (Matzer and Hafellner 1990)
- **Merismatium**: Nine species (Triebel 1989)
- **Norrlinia**: Two species (Santesson 1989)
- **Stigmidium**: Approximately 70 species (Clauzade et al. 1989; Roux and Triebel 1994) — one of the largest genera of lichenicolous fungi
- **Muellerella**: 12 species, lichenicolous or hepaticolous (Triebel 1989)

### Dothideales (Pleosporales)

The Dothideales contain numerous lichenicolous taxa:

**Tubeufiaceae**: Comprise fungicolous taxa found on meliolaceous (18 species) or phyllachoraceous (9 species) leaf-inhabiting ascomycetes, as well as rusts (3 species), and 20 species on decaying wood (Rossman 1987). Associated anamorphs often have helicoid conidia.

**Dacampiaceae**: Include several genera with exclusively lichenicolous species:
- **Polycoccum**: 30 species
- **Pyrenidium**: 15 species (mostly undescribed)
- **Weddellomyces**: 8 species
- **Clypeococcum**: 6 species
- **Dacampia**: 3 species

Most of these species are specialized on a single host genus or species.

**Other Dothideales genera** with exclusively lichenicolous species include Buelliella, Cercidospora, Didymellopsis, Endococcus, Lichenostigma, Sagediopsis, Sphaerellothecium, and Zwackhiomyces.

**Lichenopeltella** (Microthyriaceae): Comprises a large number of lichenicolous species, most of which parasitize macrolichens (Aptroot et al. 1997).

### Laboulbeniales — Pyxidiophoraceae

The genus Pyxidiophora (anamorphs in Gabarnaudia or Chalara) is heteroxenous, meaning some species change hosts during their life cycles (Malloch 1995). Most species are biotrophic mycoparasites that parasitize hosts by means of noninvasive contact cells. Their ascospores generally are translocated by mites, and the fungus can penetrate its vector with haustoria (Lundqvist 1980; Malloch and Blackwell 1993a; Blackwell 1994).

### Leotiales

- **Helicogonium**: Expanded to cover 15 species growing on inoperculate discomycetes. The species lack discrete ascomata, and their asci easily are mistaken for a deviating form of host asci (Baral 1999). Classified in Leotiales.
- **Unguiculariopsis** (Helotiaceae): At least 24 species, all growing on fungi; 10 also grow on lichenized ascomycetes
- **Llimoniella**: Six species, all lichenicolous
- **Rhymbocarpus**: Nine species, all lichenicolous
- **Skyttea**: 17 species, all lichenicolous (Diederich and Etayo 2000)

### Chaetothyriales — Herpotrichiellaceae

**Capronia**: Comprises four fungicolous species commonly found on pyrenomycetes, plus at least five lichenicolous species (Aptroot et al. 1997). Anamorphs mainly belong in the genus Exophiala.

### Sordariales — Lichenicolous Genera

In several genera of Sordariales, all species are lichenicolous: Gloeosphaeria, Lasiosphaeriopsis, Reconditella, Rhagadostoma, Roselliniella, Roselliniomyces, and Roselliniopsis.

### Genera Incertae Sedis

- **Abrothallus**: At least 20 species growing on macrolichens (mainly Parmeliaceae)
- **Dactylospora**: At least 30 lichenicolous species; other members are muscicolous or saprotrophic on wood

## Anamorphic Conidial Lichenicolous Fungi

More than 100 lichenicolous conidial fungi are known (Hawksworth 1979a, 1981b). A new section, **Lichenoidea of Acremonium**, was described for nine mostly common lichenicolous species with unbranched conidiophores, some of which are anamorphs of Pronectria (Lowen 1995).

Genera containing common lichenicolous species include Acrodontium, Calcarisporium, Gabarnaudia, and Rhinotrichella, related to various ascomycete orders.

## Basidiomycete Lichenicolous Fungi

Several basidiomycete groups contain lichenicolous species:

### Within Hypocreales Relatives

- **Pronectria**: 14 species, mostly lichenicolous (one algicolous species known)
- **Paranectria**: Three species, all lichenicolous
- **Trichonectria**: Three species from nonlichenized ascomycetes, six (T. hirta and T. rubefaciens) from lichens (Samuels 1988; Sérusiaux et al. 1999)

### Platygloeomycetidae

- **Chionosphaera**: Two species grow on lichens (Diederich 1996)
- **Cystobasidium**: Two species elicit formation of conspicuous galls on lichens (Diederich 1996)

### Tremellales

- **Syzygospora bachmannii** and **S. physciacearum**: Common and widespread lichenicolous species growing on Cladonia species and Physciaceae (Diederich 1996), inducing gall-like deformations
- **Biatoropsis usnearum**: A lichenicolous species that may belong in Tremellaceae

### Lichenicolous Heterobasidiomycetes on Corticioid Fungi

Several ascomycetes that are normally lichenicolous grow preferentially on basidiomata of lichenicolous heterobasidiomycetes (Diederich and Christiansen 1994; Diederich 1996). **Phaeospora corae** and several undescribed species have been recorded from the lichenized corticiaceous Cora glabrata (Santesson 1989).

### Athelia arachnoidea

Athelia arachnoidea is the only known lichenicolous fungus whose anamorph (Fibulorhizoctonia carotae) is nonlichenicolous (Adams and Kropp 1996). It overgrows and kills epiphytic lichens and algae, capable of destroying whole lichen communities, especially in areas with significant air pollution (Arvidsson 1976, 1978; Parmasto 1998).

## Host Specificity Patterns

Lichenicolous fungi show a wide spectrum of host specificity:

- **Highly specific**: Many Polycoccum and Pyrenidium species are restricted to a single host genus or species
- **Family-level specificity**: Phacopsis species mostly grow on Parmeliaceae
- **Broad generalists**: Nectriopsis exigua colonizes all major groups of myxomycetes; some Stigmidium species have wide host ranges

The degree of host specificity often correlates with the type of interaction — biotrophic parasites tend to be more host-specific than necrotrophic colonizers.

## Ecological Significance

Lichenicolous fungi influence lichen community structure and dynamics through:

1. Direct mortality of lichen thalli
2. Reduction of reproductive output by damaging fruiting structures
3. Alteration of competitive relationships between lichen species
4. Creation of specialized microhabitats through gall formation
5. Potential bioindication of environmental conditions, particularly air quality (Athelia arachnoidea proliferates in polluted areas)

The extreme diversity of lichenicolous fungi — spanning multiple orders across two phyla — reflects the long evolutionary history and ecological importance of fungal-fungal interactions in lichen-dominated ecosystems.
