## Design Principles

Every element in a permaculture system should serve many functions, and every function should be supported by many elements. In companion planting, this means:

- Pair plants that provide mutual benefits rather than competing
- Include nitrogen fixers near heavy-feeding crops
- Use aromatic herbs to repel pests and attract [[acacia]] saligna (inter-swales/inter-rows), basil, tansy, southernwood
- **Avoid**: interplanting with vegetable crops

### Avocado
- **Good companions**: tagasaste, Acacia pulchella, garlic

### Citrus
- **Good companions**: lucerne, tagasaste, raspberries, guava, garlic

### Fig
- **Good companions**: tagasaste, lucerne, garlic

### Grapes
- **Good companions**: geraniums, mulberry, hyssop, basil, peas, beans, garlic

### Macadamia
- **Good companions**: Acacia pulchella, tagasaste, garlic

### Mulberry
- **Good companions**: Albizia lophantha, tagasaste, lucerne, garlic
- **Note**: mulberry is not a good companion to most species

### Peach / Nectarine
- **Good companions**: tagasaste, lucerne, garlic, tansy

### Walnut
- **Good companions**: Albizia lophantha, tagasaste, garlic, lucerne

### General Tree Companions
Most fruit and [[mollison-designers-bee-forage-tree-planting]], good lock-up fodder
- **Garlic**: pest deterrent

## Vegetable and Herb Companions (Good)

### Asparagus
Companions: tomatoes, parsley, basil

### Beans (climbing)
Companions: carrots, cucumbers, cabbages, lettuce, peas, parsley, cauliflower, spinach
