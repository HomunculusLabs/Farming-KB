# Psilocybin and Mystical Experience

The [[philosophical-implications-of-psychedelics]] induced by psilocybin is the central phenomenon that Powell's "The Psilocybin Solution" seeks to understand. Across cultures and throughout history, psilocybin mushrooms have been used to contact the divine, experience profound visions, and gain mystical insight into the nature of reality.

## Historical and Cross-Cultural Evidence

### Aztec and Mazatec Traditions
The Aztecs referred to psilocybin mushrooms with awe and reverence. They were not considered a common drug like alcohol but were deified and ritually worshipped. Consumption of the sacred mushroom allowed one to "contact the Gods, experience profound visions, and gain mystical knowledge." Even after the Spanish conquest destroyed Aztec culture, indigenous mushroom ceremonies continued in secret among the Mazatec and other peoples of Oaxaca, Mexico.

### The Wasson Revelation
[[wasson-hofmann-ruck-collaboration]]'s ethnomycological investigations in the 1950s brought knowledge of the sacred mushroom to the Western world. His 1957 Life magazine article "Seeking the Magic Mushroom" introduced millions to the existence of these ceremonies. Wasson's experience with the Mazatec curandera Maria Sabina was transformative -- he found himself "awed by its transcendental visionary effects."

### Ancient Traditions
Powell traces the use of visionary mushrooms (and other entheogens) across multiple ancient cultures:
- The [[eleusinian-mysteries-history-and-structure]] of ancient Greece, possibly involving ergotised barley
- The **Bwiti cult** of West Africa using iboga
- The **Rig Veda's** Soma, possibly a psychoactive mushroom
- **Amazonian** ayahuasca ceremonies

Wasson's provocative hypothesis was that the spiritual or religious impulse itself originated from our ancestors' ingestion of vision-inducing plants.

## Phenomenology of the Mystical Experience

The psilocybin-induced mystical experience is characterised by:

- **The felt presence of the transcendental Other** -- a sense of communicating with an intelligence or reality beyond the self
- **Transcendence of time and space** -- dissolution of normal temporal and spatial boundaries
- **Unity** -- a sense of oneness with all things
- **Ineffability** -- the experience resists adequate description in ordinary language
- **Noetic quality** -- a sense that genuine knowledge or insight has been gained
- **Deeply positive mood** -- often described as the most meaningful experience of one's life

Importantly, psilocybin does not cause users to "hallucinate non-existent objects" -- rather, one comes to see external reality in a new and arguably enhanced way. This is why Powell prefers the term "entheogen-culture|entheogen" (generating the divine within) or "psychedelic" (mind-manifesting) over "hallucinogen."

## Wasson's Visionary Account

Powell provides a detailed reconstruction of Wasson's first mushroom ceremony. As Wasson lay in the dark confines of the hut, visions unfolded before his eyes that "breached the ineffable realms of religious mysticism." They began as vividly coloured art motifs of an angular nature, then evolved into resplendent palaces and gardens laid over with precious stones. At one point Wasson perceived a great mythological beast drawing a regal chariot, and later felt his spirit had broken free from his body, suspended in mid-air viewing vast mountains rising to the Heavens.

Wasson confessed that the sights were "so sharp and clear as to be more real than anything that he had previously seen with his eyes, somewhat akin to archetypes and the Platonic realm of Ideas." The visions lasted only a minute or so by watch but were experienced with "aeonic duration" -- as if he had passed out of the confines of normal time.

At one point Wasson felt as though "the visions themselves were about to be transcended and dark gates reaching upward beyond sight were about to part, and we were to find ourselves in the presence of the Ultimate." Though the gates did not fully open, the experience transformed what had begun as ethnomycological research into "a personal testimony to the mystical shamanic experience" and eventually a "veritable religious treatise."

## The Roots of the Religious Impulse

The visionary effects of the mushroom, so clearly related to the experiences of religious mystics, suggested to Wasson that such fungi might be connected to the very origins of the religious impulse. He asked whether "the idea of a deity arose after our primitive ancestors first consumed psychoactive mushrooms" -- a compelling scenario for explaining the origins of religious mysticism in essentially physical terms. Wasson later helped coin the word "entheogen-culture|entheogen" to refer to these plants and fungi, meaning "generating the divine within."

## The Good Friday Experiment

Walter Pahnke's 1962 "Good Friday Experiment" at Boston University's Marsh Chapel provided a controlled scientific demonstration of psilocybin's capacity to induce mystical experience. Ten Protestant theology students received psilocybin while ten received nicotinic acid as an active placebo. The psilocybin group scored significantly higher on measures of mystical experience. In Doblin's long-term follow-up, all psilocybin subjects "still considered that their original experiences contained genuinely mystical elements and that psilocybin had made a uniquely valuable contribution to their spiritual wellbeing."

Powell notes that psilocybin "seems to force one to confront bad habits and neuroses" and that "nothing remains hidden to the mushroom," which can lead to a psychological 'shake-up' in those hitherto blind to self-knowledge. The tenet 'know thyself' is bound up with all spiritual disciplines, and psilocybin highlights this timeless truism to an extraordinary degree.

## The Neuroscience of Mysticism

From a psilocybin-neuroscience|neuroscientific perspective, the mystical experience correlates with the dismantling of the brain's normal serotonergic filtering mechanisms. When the raphe system's inhibitory function is dampened and the locus coeruleus is activated, normally suppressed informational patterns reach conscious awareness, creating the phenomenological experience of transcendence.

The visions are dreamlike but experienced with full waking consciousness and vivid memory retention — "fairly emblazoned upon the memory, like favourite movie clips," as Powell describes.

## Three Interpretations of the Transcendental Other

The felt presence of the transcendental Other during psilocybin experiences is a consistent feature reported across cultures and throughout history. Powell explores several possible interpretations:

1. An outside intelligence existing in another dimension with the capacity to move into ours (explored through Philip K. Dick's VALIS concept)
2. A highly advanced extraterrestrial intelligence (explored through Terence McKenna's alien probe scenarios)
3. An intelligence built into the fabric of reality itself, the manifestation of what Powell calls "Natural Intelligence"

Powell ultimately favors the third interpretation, arguing that the Other is "firmly entwined within the Universal Computation along with ourselves" and that "its intelligence is not far away but all around us."

## Universal Symbology in Mystical Visions

Mystical visions consistently contain universal symbols that appear across all cultures and historical periods. The serpent appears in Maya/Aztec worship (Quetzalcoatl), Egyptian pharaonic iconography, Australian aboriginal religion (Rainbow Serpent), the epic of Gilgamesh, Indian tradition (Nagas), and the Eden narrative. Always, the serpent symbolises a wise, divine, spiritual life-force or deity.

Such universality suggests that these symbols represent "stable, organised concentrations of information produced by the most holistic/integrative type of information processing achieved by the human brain." They are not culturally conditioned but reflect fundamental structures of human consciousness revealed when normal constraints are dissolved.

## Mystical Experience and the Gaian Context

Powell places mystical experience within the broader framework of Natural Intelligence. The experience of the Other during psilocybin sessions may represent the most direct access we have to perceiving the intelligence inherent in Nature. When one encounters the numinous presence, one is, in Powell's view, encountering an intelligence that is "firmly entwined within the Universal Computation along with ourselves."

## Powell's Informational Interpretation

Within Powell's framework, the mystical experience is not a hallucination but a genuine expansion of informational access. The "felt presence of the Other" represents novel informational patterns emerging from the brain that feel authentically real because they are -- they are valid informational states, just not ones normally accessible under constrained consciousness.

This provides a bridge between the subjective reality of the mystical experience and objective neuroscience: both normal and entheogenic consciousness are informational patterns embodied in neuronal firing, but the entheogenic pattern encompasses a wider informational landscape.

## The Entheogenic Path to Knowledge

Powell argues that there are two routes to transcendent understanding: traditional spiritual disciplines (fasting, yoga, meditation, perceptual isolation), which testify to the fact that "the normal human brain is somehow constrained in its mindful activity," and the more immediate route of deliberate ingestion of naturally occurring entheogenic alkaloids. Both approaches seek to overcome or bypass "the chemical system which does the constraining."

He contends that naturally occurring entheogenic plants and fungi are "the key to solving the twin mysteries of consciousness and reality." The numinous experience they induce "no matter how bizarre it might appear in the context of the mundane world and no matter what brain mechanism underlies it, is a real thing; it exists, potentially at any rate."

## Spiritual Significance

Powell argues that if the entheogenic experience reveals a transcendental reality that feels more real and meaningful than everyday life, this demands serious investigation rather than dismissal. The capacity of psilocybin to reliably induce creative insights, [[psilocybin-and-death-anxiety]], and [[psilocybin-and-addiction-treatment]] suggests that the mystical experience is not merely subjective but carries genuine informational content about the nature of consciousness and reality.
