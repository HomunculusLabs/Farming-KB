## Triptolemus, Eumolpus, and the Founding Families of Eleusis

## The Original Recipients

According to the Homeric Hymn to Demeter (lines 473-474), after making fruit spring up from the rich lands, Demeter went to "the kings who deal justice" and showed them her awful mysteries:

1. **Triptolemus**: The most famous of Demeter's initiates
2. **Diocles**: The horse-driver
3. **Eumolpus**: The ancestor of the Eumolpidae priestly family
4. **Celeus**: Leader of the people of Eleusis

Also mentioned (line 474) is **Polyxeinus**, who along with Triptolemus and Diocles received the mysteries.

## Triptolemus

### Mythological Role

Triptolemus occupies a central position in Eleusinian mythology. He is the mortal to whom Demeter entrusted the task of spreading [[plutarch-and-cicero-on-the-eleusinian-mysteries]]. His name reflects his role: he was the one who sang the sacred hymns and performed the liturgical functions of the Mysteries.

### The Eumolpidae

The Eumolpidae held the supreme priesthood of Eleusis. They provided the **Hierophant** (the one who shows the sacred things) -- the chief priest who revealed the sacred objects and presided over the climactic moments of the initiation. This was the most prestigious religious office in the Greek world.

The Eumolpidae traced their lineage to Eumolpus, son of Poseidon and Chione (or, in some versions, of Earth herself). Their authority was hereditary and unchallengeable.

## The Kerykes

The second controlling family, the Kerykes ("heralds"), provided the **Dadouchos** (torch-bearer), the second-highest priest at Eleusis. They traced their origin to Keryx, a son of Eumolpus (or of Hermes and Aglauros).

The Kerykes were responsible for:
- The Dadouchos priesthood
- Heraldic and organizational functions
- Managing the practical aspects of the initiation ceremony
- Announcing the sacred rites

## The Hierophant and the Dadouchos

Together, the Hierophant (from Eumolpidae) and the Dadouchos (from Kerykes) conducted the initiation. Their roles were complementary:

- **Hierophant**: The revealer. Showed the sacred objects, chanted the ancient words, presided over the telesterion.
- **Dadouchos**: The guide. Carried the torch, led initiates through the ritual, provided light in the darkness.

Other priesthoods included:
- **Hierophantides**: The female priestess who assisted with certain rituals
- **Priestess of [[demeter-and-persephone-in-eleusinian-mythology]]**: Maintained the sanctuary

## Celeus and the Eleusinian Royal House

Celeus was the king of Eleusis who welcomed the disguised Demeter into his home. His daughters found the goddess at the Maiden's Well and brought her to the palace. His wife Metaneira offered Demeter wine (which she refused, requesting kykeon instead). Their son Demophoon was the child Demeter tried to make immortal by placing in fire.

The royal house of Eleusis claimed descent from Celeus. While they did not control the priesthoods, their ancestral connection to Demeter gave Eleusis its sacred status.

## Demophoon and the Failed Immortality

Demeter nursed the young prince Demophoon, anointing him with ambrosia by day and hiding him in fire by night to burn away his mortal nature. She would have made him deathless and ageless -- the savior of humanity. But Metaneira's discovery and cry of terror interrupted the rite. Demeter cast the child down, declaring mortals "witless and dull to foresee your lot."

This myth encodes the Mystery's promise: immortality was possible but required conditions mortals could not sustain. The initiate at Eleusis received a foretaste of what Demophoon almost achieved.
