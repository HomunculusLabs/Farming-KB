# Oyster Mushroom Fruiting Conditions
The transition from vegetative mycelial growth to fruiting body production in
oyster mushrooms is triggered by specific environmental cues. Understanding and
controlling these parameters is essential for consistent, high-quality harvests
in both home and commercial cultivation.

## The Fruiting Trigger

Full substrate colonization is the prerequisite for fruiting. Once the mycelium
has completely colonized the substrate (typically 14-21 days for straw, 21-30
days for sawdust), it undergoes a physiological shift from vegetative growth to
generative (reproductive) growth. Environmental stressors signal the mycelium
that conditions for survival are changing, triggering the formation of primordia
(baby mushrooms).

## Temperature

### Initiation Temperature Drop
A sudden drop in temperature is the primary fruiting trigger for most oyster
mushroom species. A reduction of 5-10 degrees C from the incubation temperature
initiates [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]]. Each species has its own optimal fruiting range:

| Species | Initiation Temp | Fruiting Range |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| *P. ostreatus* | 10-15 C | 10-18 C |
| *P. pulmonarius* | 15-20 C | 15-25 C |
| *P. eryngii* | 10-15 C | 12-18 C |
| *P. sajor-caju* | 20-25 C | 20-30 C |
| *P. citrinopileatus* | 18-22 C | 20-28 C |
| *P. djamor* | 20-25 C | 22-28 C |

### Temperature Fluctuations
Daily temperature fluctuations of 5-8 degrees C can improve pinning uniformity.
In outdoor cultivation, natural day-night cycles provide this variation. Indoor
growers can achieve this with programmable environmental controllers.

## Fresh Air Exchange (FAE)

### Carbon Dioxide Levels
[[oyster-mushrooms]] require high humidity throughout the fruiting cycle:
- Primordia formation: 90-95% RH
- Early fruiting development: 85-92% RH
- Mature fruiting bodies: 80-85% RH
Humidity below 80% causes primordia to desiccate and abort. Humidity above
95% with poor air exchange promotes [[growing-gourmet-bacterial-blotch-disease-management]] and other moisture-related
diseases.

### Humidity Management Methods
- **Ultrasonic foggers**: Provide fine mist without raising temperature
- **[[bloomfield-mushroom-evaporative-cooling-fungal-frigidity]] pads**: Add humidity while cooling air
- **Misting systems**: Timer-controlled [[ingham-spray-nozzles-equipment-compost-tea]] (avoid direct spraying
  on developing mushrooms)
- **Humidistat controllers**: Maintain target RH with automatic feedback

### Water Misting Technique
When misting, use a fine mist and avoid spraying directly on mushrooms. Mist the
walls and floor of the growing area. Allow surfaces to dry between misting
cycles. Water droplets sitting on mushroom surfaces promote bacterial growth.

## Light

### Light Requirements
[[led-grow-lights]]: 5000-6500K color temperature works well. Light also
influences pigmentation in colored varieties. *P. citrinopileatus* produces more
intense yellow under higher light, while *P. djamor* develops deeper pink with
adequate exposure.

## Pinning Strategy

The pinning phase (primordia formation to small mushroom development) is the
most critical period. A systematic approach improves consistency:
1. **Cold shock**: Reduce temperature 5-10 degrees C for 24-48 hours
2. **Soak**: Submerge fully colonized blocks in cold water for 4-12 hours
   (rehydrates substrate and provides additional temperature shock)
3. **Introduce light**: Begin photoperiod
4. **Increase FAE**: Open bags or increase ventilation
5. **Maintain high humidity**: 90-95% RH for primordia development

## Substrate Preparation for Fruiting

The choice and preparation of substrate directly affects fruiting quality.
Hardwood sawdust supplemented with bran (at 10-20% dry weight) provides
excellent nutrition for dense, meaty fruiting bodies. Straw substrates are
cheaper but produce lighter, less substantial mushrooms.

[[pf-tek-substrate-moisture-diagnosis-and-correction]] content should be 60-65% before sterilization. Overly wet
substrate becomes anaerobic, encouraging bacterial contamination and stalling
mycelial growth. After sterilization or pasteurization, substrate bags must
cool completely before inoculation to avoid killing the spawn with residual heat.

Supplementation with calcium carbonate (1-2%) buffers pH and improves mushroom
quality. Gypsum (calcium sulfate) at 2-5% improves substrate structure and
provides calcium and sulfur. These amendments are particularly important for
sawdust-based substrates.

## Common Fruiting Problems

### Coral-like or Cauliflower Growth
Abnormal, dense, coralloid fruiting bodies indicate excessive CO2 levels.
Increase fresh air exchange immediately. Oyster mushrooms require more FAE than
most other cultivated species. Stems elongate and caps fail to develop properly
when CO2 exceeds 800-1000 ppm.

### Primordia Abortion
Young pins that stop growing and turn brown or black are aborting. Common causes
include: rapid humidity drops, drafts, temperature swings, or physical
disturbance. Maintain stable conditions during the critical first 48 hours after
pinning. Avoid opening grow chambers more than necessary during this period.

### Bacterial and Fungal Contamination
Pseudomonas tolaasii causes bacterial blotch, appearing as dark brown, wet
spots on mushroom caps. Reduce humidity and increase air circulation if blotch
appears. Trichoderma (green mold) on substrate surfaces indicates insufficient
sterilization or contaminated spawn. Remove affected blocks to prevent spread.

## Harvesting and Post-Harvest Handling

Harvest oyster mushrooms when the cap margin begins to flatten but before the
cap edges curl upward. Timing varies by species: *P. ostreatus* is typically
harvested 5-7 days after pinning. Cut clusters at the base with a sharp knife
to minimize substrate damage and reduce contamination risk for subsequent
flushes.

Post-harvest, oyster mushrooms are highly perishable. Store at 1-3 degrees C
in breathable packaging. Shelf life is typically 5-9 days at proper
refrigeration. Freezing is possible for culinary use but damages texture. Drying
at 30-40 degrees C preserves flavor and extends shelf life to several months.
