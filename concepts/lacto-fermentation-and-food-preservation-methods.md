## Lacto-Fermentation and Food Preservation Methods

Lacto-fermentation is one of the oldest and safest methods of food preservation. Unlike canning or freezing which try to kill all bacteria, fermentation encourages the growth of beneficial lactic [[lacto-fermentation-recipes]] potentially safer than raw vegetables (which can harbor E. coli) or improperly canned foods. Lacto-fermentation connects food preservation traditions with modern understanding of the human microbiome.

## Safety of Fermentation

The key safety principle is that lactic acid produced during fermentation creates an acidic environment (pH below 4.6) that prevents the growth of Clostridium botulinum and other pathogens. Fermented foods also retain more nutritional properties than foods preserved by heat processing. Jars with rubber-sealed lids are recommended because they release gases that build up during fermentation, preventing explosion. Traditional crocks can also be used. Clean produce from a reliable source is essential.

## Pickling

Beyond cabbage, many vegetables can be lacto-fermented as pickles: cucumbers, beets, carrots, green beans, onions, radishes, Swiss chard ribs, turnips, zucchini. Recipe: 1 pound sliced cucumbers, 1 cup sea salt for initial salting, peppercorns, 1.5 tablespoons sea salt for brine, 1 cup unchlorinated water, mustard seeds, fresh dill, 2 cloves garlic. Layer vegetables tightly with salt in a bowl, cover with water for 24 hours. Pack into clean jars with mustard seeds, peppercorns, dill, and garlic. Close tightly, keep in kitchen 2 days until bubbling starts, then refrigerate or cellar for 6 weeks before eating.

## Yogurt

Yogurt is fermented milk where bacteria convert lactose to lactic acid. Homemade yogurt requires only milk and a starter (plain yogurt with active cultures). Heat a quart of milk with 3 tablespoons of starter to precisely 100 degrees F (38 degrees C), then keep warm for 8-12 hours using a wood stove, thermos, oven, sun, or crockpot. The yogurt is done when it does not run when tilted. Always keep some plain yogurt set aside as starter for the next batch.

## Sourdough

Sourdough captures wild yeasts from the air for bread fermentation. Start with 1/2 cup wheat flour and 1-2 tablespoons unchlorinated water, kneaded into a small dough in a glass or ceramic jar (not metal), covered with damp cloth. Feed daily by removing the hard crust and adding 1 cup flour and water. After approximately 5 days, the starter will rise and hold its shape when poked. Sourdough starters can be maintained indefinitely -- some have been passed down for over 100 years. For bread: mix starter + flour + water in the morning; in the evening add more flour, salt, and water; knead well and let rise overnight; divide, form loaves, let rise 3-6 hours; bake at 450 degrees F (232 degrees C) for about 30 minutes until hollow-sounding.

## Jam Without Added Pectin

Natural pectin from apple cores and peels (tied in unbleached muslin cloth) replaces commercial pectin. The amount of sugar determines jam quality and storage life. Fruit-sugar ratios vary: apples 1 cup:1/4 cup, apricots 1:1, berries 1:3/4 cup, grapes 1:1, peaches 1:3/4 cup (with 2 tablespoons citrus juice), pears 1:3/4 cup, plums 1:3/4 cup, rosehips 1:3/4 cup. Simmer fruit with apple core bundle and sugar until consistency does not drip. Fill hot jars, close tightly, store upside down.

## Sugar Substitutions for Jam

- Honey: 3/4 cup per cup of sugar (substitute no more than half, or jam will be runny)

- Brown sugar: same amount as sugar, strong flavor, good for peaches

- Raw sugar: add 1/4 cup more per cup of sugar

## Chutney

Chutney preserves fruits or vegetables with spices for serving with cold meat, potatoes, rice, and salads. Ingredients: 4 cups chopped fruit or vegetable, 1 cup complementary vegetable, 1 cup chopped onions, 2 tablespoons brown sugar, 1/2 cup vinegar, salt, and herbs (ginger, mustard seed, cloves, cayenne, rosemary, pepper, curry). Simmer all together until very soft. Add sugar and vinegar, boil to jam consistency. Sterilize jars and pour hot chutney in. Close lids and store upside down.

## Fermented Beverages

Beyond food, lacto-fermentation principles extend to beverage production.

- **Kombucha:** Fermented sweet tea using a SCOBY (symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeast). The SCOBY converts sugar to organic acids, producing a tangy, lightly effervescent beverage. Kombucha requires 7-14 days of fermentation at room temperature, followed by optional secondary fermentation with fruit for carbonation.

- **Water kefir:** Fermented sugar water using water kefir grains (small, translucent gelatinous clusters of bacteria and yeast). The grains multiply during fermentation and can be shared indefinitely.
