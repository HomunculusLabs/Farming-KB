# JADAM Rice Bran Problems

## Overview

Despite its widespread use as an [[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof-recipe-solomon]], JADAM identifies rice bran as a fundamentally problematic input for farming. While rice bran may be suitable for growing rice in moderation, its overuse or application to non-rice crops disrupts soil nutrient balance, increases disease susceptibility, and ultimately degrades soil health. JADAM classifies rice bran as a "skin fertilizer" -- a byproduct lacking the comprehensive nutrients of whole plant material.

## What Is Wrong With Rice Bran?

Rice bran, perilla pulp, and press cakes are the leftovers after the grain, nut, or fruit has been extracted of its nutritional essences. They are just the skin of a fruit and are deficient in the comprehensive nutrients that the whole fruit contains. This makes them far from ideal substances to give to soil and crops.

### Nutritional Imbalance

Fertilizer made from plant skin does not possess the comprehensive nutrients required by plants -- it simply increases soil nutrient imbalance. Putting in nutritionally unbalanced food (skin fertilizer) certainly disrupts nutrient balance. That is why it is very beneficial to use the "whole" plant as fertilizer, as in JADAM's cover crop method.

### Crop-Specific Bias

If you put rice bran in the soil, the [[jadam-nutritional-balance-fertilizer]] in the soil changes to a condition more favorable for growing rice. Would you [[query-how-do-i-grow-tomatoes-in-a-greenhouse]] in that soil? The soil becomes adjusted to the nutritional profile of rice, potentially harming other crops.

The same applies to perilla pulp. It has higher nitrogen than rice bran, so farmers take it for granted that it is a better fertilizer. But if you use perilla pulp as base fertilizer repeatedly, you will ruin your strawberries because the soil becomes more adjusted to a nutritional balance that is good for growing perilla.

### Oil Cake Problems

Most commercial oil cakes consist of 50 percent castor pomace. If you overuse oil cakes, your soil will be suitable for castor farming. Simply putting in [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] instead of chemical fertilizer does not make you an organic farmer. You have to be extra careful; be aware that input of organic matter can seriously disrupt soil nutritional balance.

## The Escalating Spiral of Imbalance

When rice bran is applied to soil, the nutritional balance shifts. This creates conditions that may:

- Favor certain weeds or pathogens over crops
- Produce excessive [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] (water sprouts) at the expense of fruit
- Increase susceptibility to chilling and freezing damage
- Cause early lowering of fruit quality
- Reduce overall crop vigor despite abundant leaf growth

Many farmers who have gone organic report that their produce has inferior taste and quality compared to conventional produce. This is because they failed to provide the optimum nutritional balance for their crops, relying instead on readily available but nutritionally incomplete organic inputs like rice bran.

## The Philosophy of Nutritional Balance

JADAM frames the issue in terms of a fundamental principle: "Good and bad are one." Organic matter is deemed "good" but overuse it and it becomes "bad." Attachment to certain organic matter is what makes farming a failure.

When nutritional balance in the soil is broken, crops will soon be diseased. When you fail to meet the quantity and composition of nutrients that a plant needs, your crops will suffer. Farming is a precision-operation of finding and approaching the optimized nutritional balance for your crop.

## The Nature Solution

Nature does not use rice bran, perilla pulp, or press cakes in achieving optimum fertility. In forests, leaves fall in autumn -- they are the product of photosynthesis of nutrients, water, sunlight, and air. Grass and other wild vegetation wither and fall too. This colorful scene that we see every year is how nature acquires the organic fertilizer it needs.

For inorganic nutrients, microorganisms work on rock below the leaf mold layer, breaking it down into mineral nutrients that are food for plants. Nature comes to the farmer and whispers: "Stop thinking of things like rice bran and oil cakes. Just follow me!"

## The Correct Fertilizer Program

JADAM's answer to the fertilizer question is based on nutritional balance:

1. **Base fertilizer**: Use cover crop and [[jadam-crop-residue-fertilizer]] (the "whole" plant). Add manure from animals that ate a lot of grass.
2. **Additional fertilizer**: When watering, add JMS + sea water + phyllite solution + diluted JLF
3. **Skin fertilizers**: If you must use rice bran, perilla pulp, or oil cakes, use them in less than 1/10 of the total organic matter applied

The fundamental principle: give plants what is the same as (or similar to) the plants themselves. Fertilizer is to bring back the nutrients taken out of the soil from harvest. What you have emptied, you need to fill back in with something similar to what has been taken out.

## From the Soil, You Demand Fruits Yet You Give Peels

JADAM's critique is summarized in a memorable phrase: "From the soil, you demand fruits yet you give peels!" Farming does not become organic by merely putting organic matter into the soil. Organic matter comes in all qualities. The goal should be to provide the complete nutritional balance that crops need, not to simply substitute chemical inputs with organic ones.
