## The Superiority of Native Breeds

Fukuoka advocates for native breeds and varieties over genetically improved stock. Jersey
cattle, thought to be of lower productivity, actually have higher feed efficiency and do
not deplete the land. The wild boar and black Berkshire pig are more economical than the
white Yorkshire. He suggests it would be better to raise small goats than dairy cattle,
and that deer, boars, rabbits, chickens, wildfowl, and even edible rodents would be even
more economical — and better protect nature — than goats.

## The Idealized Vision of Free-Ranging Livestock

Fukuoka closes with a pastoral vision: bees making rounds of clover and vegetable blossoms
beneath fruit-laden trees; semi-wild chickens and rabbits frolicking with dogs in wheat
fields; ducks and mallards playing in rice paddies; black pigs and boars growing fat on
worms and crayfish in the valleys; goats peering from thickets. He challenges the reader:
is this a picture of "primitive, economically disadvantaged life" or "an organic
partnership between man, animal, and nature"? His answer is clear — "an environment
comfortable for small animals is also an ideal setting for man."
