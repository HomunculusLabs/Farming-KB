# [[cannabis-and-sickle-cell-disease]]

## Overview

Sickle cell disease (SCD) is a hereditary blood disorder characterized by
abnormally shaped red blood cells. Instead of the normal disc shape, sickle
cells are crescent or sickle-shaped, which impairs their ability to carry
sufficient oxygen and nutrients throughout the body. The disease causes chronic
anemia, pain crises, organ damage, and increased susceptibility to infections.

SCD primarily affects people of African, Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, and
Indian descent. Pain crises (vaso-occlusive crises) are the hallmark of the
disease, occurring when sickle-shaped cells block small blood vessels, causing
excruciating pain, tissue damage, and inflammation. Standard treatment focuses
on pain management with opioids, hydration, and blood transfusions, though
these approaches carry significant limitations and side effects.

Depression and anxiety among sickle cell patients are relatively common, and
both psychological states are associated with increased intensity and duration
of pain on both crisis and non-crisis days.

## Cannabis Research Findings

### Human Case Study: London

A London-based experiment published in 2005 demonstrated that cannabis relieved
pain, depression, and anxiety for sickle cell patients. The study provided
clinical evidence that whole-plant cannabis use could address multiple symptoms
simultaneously, which is particularly valuable given the comorbidity of pain
and psychological distress in SCD patients.

### Animal Study: University of Minnesota

A 2010 research team from Minnesota came to similar conclusions through an
animal study. They genetically altered mice to produce human sickle cell
hemoglobin while creating an internal environment similar to sickle cell crisis
pain. Researchers then studied the effects of morphine and cannabinoids during
objectively measurable experiences of pain. Results revealed that both morphine
and cannabinoids reduced sickle cell-like pain in the animals. This finding is
significant because it suggests cannabinoids may offer an alternative or
adjunct to opioid-based pain management for SCD patients.

## Strain and Form Considerations

The drugs tested against sickle cell disease in these trials included smoked
cannabis and CP 55,940. The latter is 40 times more potent than Delta-9-THC
and is extremely psychoactive. CP 55,940 is equally strong at CB1 and CB2
receptors.

THC binds relatively equally to both CB1 and CB2. Sativas or sativa-dominant
strains generally contain a higher THC:CBD profile. For SCD patients seeking
pain relief without excessive psychoactivity, balanced strains or those with
moderate THC and meaningful CBD content may be preferable.

## Mind-Body Medicine Perspective

The joy of life is connected to the flow of blood coursing through the veins.
Just as blood nurtures and sustains cellular life, joy feeds and sustains a
healthy mind. One's capacity for joy might be destroyed by holding on to
beliefs such as "You are no good; you are flawed" or "There is something wrong
with you." Sickle cells and malformed red blood cells are incapable of carrying
sufficient nutrients to the body and, thus, cause much suffering.

Blood is also related to kin (blood bonds and blood relations with family,
tribe, or group). Sickle cell disease is physically hereditary, but since the
expression of the disease varies so greatly in duration and intensity, it is
likely that non-physical signals such as the impact of deep-seated shame
(perhaps passed down through the generations) may play a role in the severity
of SCD.

Aggravating factors may include negative affect such as anxiety and depression.
Consider emotional release work, especially related to blood relatives; changing
beliefs that produce anxiety or depression; and working toward positive affect.

## Supporting Herbs and Spices

- **Rosemary**: Approved by the German Commission E for "supportive therapy
  circulatory problems."
- **Vanilla**: Researchers at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia
  discovered that vanillin, a dietary compound in vanilla beans, can bind with
  sickle-shaped hemoglobin in rodents and improve oxygen transport -- a key
  necessity for reducing symptoms in sickle cell patients.
