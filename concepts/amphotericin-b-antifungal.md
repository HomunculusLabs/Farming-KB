# Amphotericin B Antifungal

Amphotericin B is a polyene antifungal antibiotic that has been the gold standard for treating
serious systemic [[blesching-cannabis-fungal-infections-candida]] for over six decades. Despite its toxicity, it remains one
of the most potent antifungal drugs available and is essential for treating life-threatening
mycoses including [[mucormycosis-zygomycete-infections]], and invasive aspergillosis.

## Discovery

- Discovered in 1955 from a soil bacterium, [[streptomyces]] nodosus, isolated in the Orinoco
  River region of Venezuela by Gold et al.
- Named "amphotericin" (from Greek amphoteros, "both") because it has both acidic and basic
  properties
- First used clinically in the late 1950s
- Remains one of the most important antifungal drugs in medicine

## Mechanism of Action

Amphotericin B binds to [[cryptococcus-neoformans]]
- **Molds:** Aspergillus species, Mucorales (Rhizopus, Mucor, etc.), Fusarium
- **Dimorphic fungi:** Histoplasma, Blastomyces, Coccidioides
- **Some protozoa:** Leishmania species (off-label)

Notable for being effective against Mucorales, which are resistant to most other antifungals.

## Adverse Effects

### Nephrotoxicity (Kidney Damage)
- The most significant and dose-limiting toxicity
- Occurs in up to 80% of patients with conventional formulation
- Mechanism: renal vasoconstriction plus direct tubular damage
- May be partly irreversible
- Reduced with lipid formulations and aggressive hydration

### Infusion Reactions
- Fever, chills, rigors, nausea, headache
- Common during first infusions
- Pre-medication with antipyretics, antihistamines, and sometimes corticosteroids

### Electrolyte Disturbances
- Hypokalemia (low potassium) — due to renal potassium wasting
- Hypomagnesemia (low magnesium)
- Can be severe and require supplementation

### Anemia
- Normochromic, normocytic anemia due to suppression of erythropoietin production

### Other
- Thrombophlebitis at IV site (conventional formulation)
- Hepatotoxicity (rare)

## Resistance

- True clinical resistance is rare but increasing
- Some Candida species show reduced susceptibility (C. lusitaniae, C. guilliermondii)
- Mechanisms include reduced [[ergosterol]] content and altered membrane composition
- Resistance in Aspergillus is unusual but reported

## Dosing and Administration

- **Conventional:** 0.5-1.5 mg/kg/day IV
- **Liposomal:** 3-5 mg/kg/day IV (higher doses used for CNS infections and mucormycosis)
- Treatment duration: typically 2-6 weeks depending on infection and host factors
- Test dose often given before full dosing to assess tolerance

## Clinical Uses

### Cryptococcal Meningitis
- Induction therapy: liposomal amphotericin B + flucytosine for 2 weeks
- Standard of care per WHO guidelines

### Invasive Aspergillosis
- Alternative to voriconazole for initial therapy
- Used when azoles are contraindicated or resistant

### Mucormycosis
- First-line therapy (liposomal formulation preferred)
- Often combined with surgical debridement

### Visceral Leishmaniasis
- Liposomal amphotericin B is the drug of choice
- Particularly for Indian visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar)
