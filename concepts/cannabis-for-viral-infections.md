Cannabis and its cannabinoids show evidence of antiviral activity across a range of viral infections. The [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] modulates immune function through CB1 and CB2 receptors. CB2 receptors, found primarily on immune cells, play key roles in regulating inflammatory responses to viral infections. Cannabinoids may exert antiviral effects through:

- Modulation of immune cell activity and cytokine production
- Anti-inflammatory actions reducing virus-induced tissue damage
- Direct antiviral activity of specific cannabinoids
- Reduction of [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]] associated with viral infections

# Cannabis for Encephalitis

Encephalitis is an acute inflammation of the brain most commonly caused by a virus (e.g., live virus vaccination, herpes, West Nile, or equine encephalitis), although it may also be produced by bacteria (syphilis, bacterial meningitis, Lyme disease), parasites (malaria), or an over-reactive immune response to secondary infections (measles, mumps, rubella).

## Disease Overview

Symptoms may include headaches, blurred vision, fever, confusion, generalized weakness, seizures, stiff neck, and projectile vomiting. Encephalitis is a medical emergency that can cause permanent neurological damage or death if not promptly treated.

Diagnosis is done by physical examination, history, blood/urine/mucosa tests, spinal tap (cerebrospinal fluid examination), electroencephalogram (EEG), brain biopsy, and imaging studies (MRI or CT scan).

Depending on the underlying cause, treatment may include antiviral agents (acyclovir for herpes), antimicrobial pharmaceuticals, anti-inflammatory drugs to reduce swelling, analgesics, anti-emetics, and supportive care including intravenous fluids, seizure medications, and ventilation.

## Cannabis and Encephalitis

A 2013 study conducted on rodents by researchers at the University of Manitoba (Winnipeg, Canada) discovered that CB2 signaling is a mechanism of controlling central nervous system (CNS) inflammation during viral encephalitis.

The synthetic CB2 agonist HU308 (5 mg/kg intraperitoneal once daily) was shown to mitigate inflammation during viral encephalitis by limiting viral infection and by reducing microglia activation. Microglia are the resident immune cells of the CNS, and their over-activation is a key driver of neuroinflammation and associated tissue damage.

## Evidence Summary (CHI Value: 2)

A 2013 University of Manitoba and Department of Medical Microbiology (Winnipeg, Canada) animal study on rats using HU308 (5 mg/kg once daily) found CB2-mitigated inflammation during viral encephalitis by limiting viral infection and reducing microglia activation, with a CHI of 2.

While only one formal study exists, the mechanism identified — CB2-mediated control of CNS inflammation — is consistent with the broader body of research on cannabinoids as neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory agents.

## Mechanism of Action

The [[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]] pathway also reduces microglia activation, which is a primary driver of the neuroinflammatory cascade that causes brain damage during encephalitis.

The [[cannabis-thc-therapeutic-profile]]:[[cannabis-cbd-therapeutic-profile]] has a greater affinity for CB2 than CB1, making CBD-rich preparations particularly relevant for encephalitis where CB2-mediated neuroprotection is the primary mechanism.
