## Strawyard and Throwover Pen Design

updated: 2026-04-18

Permaculture Two (1979) Chapter 8 introduces the strawyard and throwover pen as key infrastructure elements in the free-range [[decomposition]] of rough residues (bracken, cornstalk, hedge clippings, straw)

### Design Principles

- Deep mulch of straw or similar material as base
- Gated to control access and stocking density
- Large enough for meaningful production but small enough to manage
- Protected from wind and rain to prevent mulch loss
- Pest-proofed against rats and sparrows for stored seed areas

### Broadcast Sowing Species

Sunflower (Helianthus spp.) -- Heads stored in autumn
Millet (Panicum spp.)
Corn (Zea)
Buckwheat (Fagopyrum)
Wheat, rye, barley, oats, teff
Chenopodium spp. -- Loved by poultry
Chick pea -- Tolerant of wide range of soils and climates
Lentils
Lab-lab (Dolichos) -- [[galium-aparine]]) -- Valuable seed plant with iron and iodine content. May need protection from free-range poultry via brush or netted fence enclosures. Poultry are useful controls when access is managed.

## Weed Control Through Stocking Management

Weed control can be achieved by regulating the time and density of stocking rates and the species permitted to range. This is a key principle: the poultry themselves become the [[fukuoka-weed-management-and-green-manure]] tool, but their access must be controlled.

## Storage Infrastructure

Stored food draws from two main sources:

1. Hard seed pods and seed heads from strawyards
2. Gathered or raked windfalls from walnut, oak, carob, and similar trees

Storage needs:
- Racks for drying
- Dry shelves
