# Insect Allies

Beneficial insects are the backbone of any ecological pest management strategy. Rather than eradicating all insects, the goal is to build balanced predator-prey populations where pests stay below economic damage thresholds. See [[cannabis-pest-management]] for crop-specific IPM protocols.

## Key Beneficial Insects

### Ladybugs (Coccinellidae)
- **Prey**: Aphids (50/day), mealybugs, scale, soft-bodied insects
- **Lifecycle**: Adult + larval stages both predatory; larvae eat more than adults
- **Note**: Most purchased adults disperse within days. Better to attract residents with habitat than release adults.

### Lacewings (Chrysopidae)
- **Prey**: Aphids, thrips, whiteflies, mealybugs, spider mite eggs, caterpillar eggs
- **Lifecycle**: Larvae ("aphid lions") are voracious; adults feed on nectar/pollen
- **Release**: Green lacewing eggs or larvae available commercially; larvae are most effective stage to release
- **Rate**: 1,000 eggs per 500 sq ft; repeat every 2 weeks for 2-3 releases

### Predatory Mites (Phytoseiulus persimilis, Amblyseius spp.)
- **Prey**: Spider mites (two-spotted), broad mites, russet mites
- **Best for**: Cannabis (spider mite pressure), indoor grows, greenhouse crops
- **Release**: P. persimilis for hot spots; A. cucumeris/A. swirskii for prevention
- **Critical**: Requires humidity >60%; doesn't tolerate dry conditions. Release at first sign of spider mites.

### Parasitic Wasps (Hymenoptera parasitica)
- **Species**: Trichogramma (moth eggs), Aphidius (aphids), Encarsia (whiteflies), Cotesia (caterpillars)
- **Mechanism**: Lay eggs inside or on host; larvae consume host from within
- **Scale**: Very small (most <2mm); released in hundreds or thousands
- **Best**: Preventive releases before pest pressure builds; each wasp species targets specific pests

### Ground Beetles (Carabidae)
- **Prey**: Slugs, cutworms, root maggots, codling moth larvae (pupae in soil)
- **Habitat**: Ground cover, mulch, stone piles, undisturbed soil
- **Note**: Nocturnal; create beetle banks with perennial grass bunches along field margins

### Hover Flies (Syrphidae)
- **Prey**: Aphids (larvae eat 400-800 aphids in their development)
- **Adults**: Important pollinators; feed on nectar/pollen from umbellifers and composites
- **Attract**: Dill, fennel, yarrow, alyssum, buckwheat — flat or shallow flowers for short mouthparts

### Bees (Apidae, Megachilidae, Andrenidae, etc.)
- **Role**: Pollination services; ~1/3 of food crops depend on insect pollinators
- **Types**: Honeybees (managed hives), bumblebees (excellent for tomatoes/peppers in greenhouses), mason bees/solitary bees (efficient early-season pollinators)
- See [[beekeeping-basics]] for hive management and [[pollinator-habitat]] for habitat design

## Attracting Beneficial Insects

### Insectary Plants
Plant specific species to provide nectar, pollen, and alternative prey:
- **Umbellifers**: dill, fennel, cilantro, caraway, Queen Anne's lace — attract parasitic wasps, hover flies
- **Composites**: yarrow, alyssum, sunflowers, chamomile — attract ladybugs, lacewings
- **Legumes**: clover, vetch — fix N, attract ground beetles, provide overwintering habitat
- **Nightshades**: borage — attracts pollinators and predatory insects; excellent companion plant

### Habitat Requirements
- **Undisturbed areas**: Leave some ground bare/mulched for ground beetles; avoid tilling entire property
- **Water**: Shallow dishes with pebbles for drinking (bees need landing pads)
- **Overwintering**: Perennial plant stubble, leaf litter, hollow stems, stone piles
- **Succession of blooms**: Ensure flowers from early spring through late fall so beneficials always have food

## Purchasing and Releasing

| Method | When to Use | Tips |
|--------|------------|------|
| Ladybug adults | Rarely recommended | Most disperse; release at dusk with water sprayed on plants |
| Lacewing eggs/larvae | Aphid/thrips pressure | Larvae are cannibalistic — distribute widely, don't clump |
| Predatory mites | Spider mite prevention/control | Match species to temperature; release immediately on arrival |
| Parasitic wasps | Specific pest targets | Timing is critical — release before pest population peaks |
| Praying mantis oothecae | General predator | Less targeted; mantids eat beneficials too. Ornamental value over practical. |

## Biological Control Products

- **Bt ([[bacillus-thuringiensis]])**: Targeted bacteria — kurstaki for caterpillars, israelensis for fungus gnat larvae. Must be ingested; no effect on adults. Apply at dusk (UV degrades it).
- **Spinosad (Spinosyn A+D)**: Broad-spectrum but organic; effective on thrips, caterpillars, leafminers. Toxic to bees when wet — apply evening after bloom.
- **Neem oil (azadirachtin)**: Antifeedant, growth disruptor, mild repellent. Effective on aphids, whiteflies, mites, soft-bodied insects. Can harm beneficials — spot-treat.
- **Diatomaceous Earth (DE)**: Mechanical desiccant; works on soft-bodied insects (slugs, aphids, ants). Non-selective — kills beneficials too. Use in dry conditions only; loses effectiveness when wet.
- **Insecticidal soap**: Potassium salts of fatty acids; works on contact against soft-bodied insects. Minimal residual; one of the safest options.

## Insect Hotels and Pollinator Corridors

- **Insect hotels**: Bundled hollow stems, drilled wood blocks, pinecones — attract solitary bees and predatory insects. Keep dry, face southeast. Avoid kits with too-deep holes or smooth plastic tunnels (disease trap).
- **Pollinator corridors**: Connected habitat strips linking garden, orchard, and wild areas. Hedgerows, native wildflower margins, unmowed buffer zones. See [[companion-planting-reference]] for integrated planting strategies.
- **Limitation**: Insect hotels alone don't solve pest problems — they're one element of a habitat strategy that includes diverse plantings, [[comparison-no-till-vs-reduced-tillage]], and no broad-spectrum pesticides.

## IPM Framework

Biological controls work within an [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]] framework:
1. **Monitor/identify** — scout regularly; know what's a pest vs beneficial
2. **Prevent** — healthy soil, resistant varieties, crop rotation, habitat for predators
3. **Intervene** — start with least-toxic options; biological controls before chemicals
4. **Evaluate** — assess control success; adjust approach

See [[cannabis-pest-management]] for cannabis-specific scouting thresholds and product rotation schedules.
