# Homesteading

Homesteading is the practice of self-sufficient living on a small plot of land, producing food, energy, and other necessities while reducing dependence on external systems. Modern homesteading integrates [[permaculture-design]], [[gardening-in-hard-times]], animal husbandry, food preservation, and appropriate technology.

## Core Elements

### Food Production
- **Vegetable gardening** — intensive and extensive approaches; see [[nutrient-dense-gardening]] and [[no-till-gardening-method]]
- **Food forests** — perennial food systems; see [[food-forest-design]]
- **Animal husbandry** — poultry, goats, rabbits for eggs, meat, dairy, manure; see [[duck-keeping]], [[goat-keeping]]
- **Seed saving** — maintain genetic diversity and self-reliance; see [[seed-saving-techniques]]

### Food Preservation
- Canning (pressure and water bath)
- Drying/dehydrating
- Fermentation (sauerkraut, kimchi, pickles); see [[food-fermentation-guide]]
- Root cellaring and cold storage
- Freezing
- See [[food-preservation-canning]] for comprehensive methods

### Water Systems
- Rainwater harvesting
- Well water management
- Greywater systems
- See [[homestead-water-systems]] and [[water-management]]

### Energy
- Solar electric and hot water
- Wood heating
- Humanure composting; see [[humanure-composting]]
- See [[homestead-energy-conservation]]

## Homestead Design

### Zoning
Adapted from [[permaculture-zones-in-practice]]:

- **Zone 0** — House (kitchen, living spaces)
- **Zone 1** — Intensive garden, herbs, daily-care animals (chickens)
- **Zone 2** — Orchard, goats, pond, intensively managed pasture
- **Zone 3** — Pasture, hay fields, main crops
- **Zone 4** — Managed forest, foraging, woodlot
- **Zone 5** — Wild area, observation, conservation

### Planning Priorities
Per [[nicole-faires]]' approach:

1. **Water first** — secure water source before anything else
2. **Access** — design paths and roads before placing structures
3. **Shelter** — house, animal housing, storage
4. **Food garden** — intensive production closest to kitchen
5. **Perennials** — orchard, berries, food forest
6. **Animals** — after fencing and shelter are ready
7. **Income systems** — value-added products, surplus sales

## Getting Started

### Land Requirements
Homesteading is possible at many scales:

| Scale | Acreage | Feasible Production |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| Urban | 0.1-0.25 acres | Intensive garden, chickens, preserved food |
| Suburban | 0.25-1 acre | Garden, orchard, small livestock |
| Small homestead | 1-5 acres | Garden, orchard, goats, pasture |
| Full homestead | 5-20 acres | All above + cows, hay, woodlot |
| Farm scale | 20+ acres | Commercial production possible |

### Skills to Develop
Priority skill progression:

1. **Gardening** — the foundation of all homesteading
2. **Cooking from scratch** — maximize what you grow
3. **Food preservation** — extend harvest through the year
4. **Basic construction** — build and repair structures
5. **Animal care** — chickens first, then larger animals
6. **Seed saving** — reduce dependency on purchased seed
7. **Fermentation** — enhance nutrition and preservation
8. **Foraging** — utilize wild food sources
9. **First aid** — see [[homestead-first-aid-guide]]
10. **Mechanical repair** — maintain tools and equipment

## Economic Considerations

- Start-up costs vary enormously by scale and ambition
- Prioritize infrastructure that produces food quickly (garden before barn)
- Consider income streams: eggs, preserves, value-added products, plant starts
- See [[selling-farm-products-and-business]] for commercial aspects
- See [[homesteading-legal-considerations]] for regulations

## Common Mistakes

1. **Over-building infrastructure** before establishing food production
2. **Starting too many projects** simultaneously
3. **Ignoring soil health** — everything starts with the soil
4. **Underestimating animal labor** — livestock requires daily commitment
5. **Isolation** — build community connections, not just independence
6. **Perfect-ism** — done is better than perfect; iterate and improve
