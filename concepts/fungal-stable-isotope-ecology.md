# Fungal Stable Isotope Ecology

Natural abundance stable isotope analysis of carbon (13C/12C) and nitrogen
(15N/14N) in fungal tissue provides powerful tools for investigating fungal
trophic strategies, substrate utilization, and ecosystem roles. Fungi cover
an extraordinary range of isotope values, making them both valuable
indicators and complicating factors in ecosystem-level isotope studies.

## The Saprotrophic-Mycorrhizal Divide

A consistent finding across multiple studies is the clear separation between
saprotrophic and ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungi in isotope space. This 'saprotrophic-
mycorrhizal divide' has been demonstrated in forests across Europe:

- [[trametes-versicolor-entity]] (a [[comparison-brown-rot-vs-white-rot-bioremediation]] fungus) showed
that the mycelium was consistently enriched in 13C compared with the wood
(enrichment factor of 3.5 per mil), even with 70% wood mass loss over 181
days. The delta-13C of respired CO2 was similar to that of the wood,
suggesting that respiration had little effect upon fungal enrichment.

## White Rot versus Brown Rot Isotope Signatures

White-rot fungi utilize all cell-wall components, while brown-rot fungi
utilize carbohydrates but degrade lignin to only a limited extent. This
fundamental biochemical difference might be expected to produce differences
in 15N and 13C values between these groups. White-rot fungi that degrade
lignin may access the 13C-depleted lignin component, potentially leading to
lower delta-13C values compared with brown-rot fungi.

## Species-Level Isotope Patterns

At present, relatively little is known about species-level isotope patterns
in saprotrophic fungi, as sample sizes have generally been too small. However,
studies of ECM fungi have suggested that species-level patterns do exist and
potentially reflect the ecophysiology of the species. Some evidence for
species-level patterns has also been found in the saprotrophic genus
Pholiota.

Taxon-specific fractionation patterns have been observed: four mould fungal
species (two zygomycetes and two ascomycetes) showed differences in
fractionation on uptake and in the 13C enrichment of many amino acids. A
discriminant analysis of isotope ratios in four amino acids (Thr, Ile, Phe,
Val) divided the fungi into their respective groups, a finding with potential
applications for investigating selective feeding on fungal species by soil
fungivores.

## Environmental Influences

The influence of site cannot be ignored, particularly for 15N values. On
sites subject to elevated levels of [[dighton-nitrogen-deposition-mycorrhizal-community-shifts]], the 15N content of
the deposition may significantly impact average delta-15N values across
ecosystem compartments, including fungi. When nitrogen deposition is high, the
range of potential delta-15N values will be narrower than on unaffected sites.
