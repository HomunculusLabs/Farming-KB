## HPS Grow Lights

development and are the standard light source for commercial indoor [[cervantes-metal-halide-lighting-systems]] (MH), and High Pressure Sodium (HPS). Among these, 600-watt HPS
lamps are the most electrically efficient in lumens-per-watt conversion.

## Efficiency and Wattage

HPS lamps outperform all other lamp types in lumens-per-watt efficiency. The 600-watt
HPS produces about seven percent more light per watt than a 1000-watt HPS. Three
600-watt HPS lamps actually deliver more total light to plants than two 1000-watt HPS
lamps. Operating costs for three 600-watt HPS units are lower than for two 1000-watt
units. Popular wattages include 150, 250, 400, 600, and 1000 watts.

The 600-watt HPS has the highest lumen-per-watt conversion (150 LPW) and can be placed
closer to the canopy than 1000 or 1100-watt bulbs. When the 600-watt bulb is closer to
plants, they receive more light. For example, a 1000-watt HPS emits 140,000 initial
lumens, while a 600-watt HPS emits 90,000 initial lumens -- seven percent more lumens
per watt.

## Light Output by Wattage

- **150-watt HPS:** illuminates up to a 2x2-foot area; hang 12-18 inches above plants
- **250-watt HPS:** illuminates up to a 3x3-foot area; hang 12-18 inches above plants
- **400-watt HPS:** illuminates up to a 4x4-foot area; hang 12-24 inches above
  plants
- **600-watt HPS:** excellent efficiency for mid-size gardens; illuminates 4x4-foot
  area; hang 18-24 inches above plants
- **1000-watt HPS:** illuminates up to a 6x6-foot area; hang 24-36 inches above
  plants; can burn foliage if closer than 24 inches

## Spectrum Characteristics

HPS lamps emit light predominantly in the yellow, orange, and red wavelengths (around
589 nanometers being a strong sodium emission line). This spectrum closely mimics the
autumn sun and is ideal for promoting flowering and fruiting. However, the spectrum is
