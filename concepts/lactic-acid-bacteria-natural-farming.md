# Lactic Acid Bacteria in Natural Farming

## Overview

Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB) are a cornerstone input in [[coleman-deep-organic-farming-philosophy]] systems. Master Han Kyu Cho
describes LAB as conditionally anaerobic microorganisms that are
resistant to high temperatures and function as strong sterilizers in
agricultural applications. Their primary roles include improving soil
ventilation, accelerating root growth during transplanting, increasing
fertilizer solubility, and neutralizing harmful gases from immature
compost. LAB are particularly effective for growing fruits and leaf
vegetables, and they help maintain plant health during rainy seasons by
adjusting internal plant fluids.

The preparation of LAB involves a two-stage fermentation: rice-washed
water captures indigenous bacteria, followed by milk as a nutrient-rich
medium for proliferation. This method obtains locally adapted pure
stocks. The resulting serum stores well and is applied at 1:1,000
dilution, making it one of the most cost-effective biological inputs
available to farmers.

In the broader context of natural farming, LAB work synergistically
with other inputs including [[cho-fermented-mixed-compost-methods]] or IMO, the fermentation process occurs rapidly and
effectively, with LAB preventing fermented compost from decaying. The
comprehensive functionality of LAB - from soil improvement to plant
health to compost management - makes them indispensable in natural
farming systems.

## Key Concepts

### Soil Ventilation and Structure

LAB are very effective for improving soil ventilation, which is critical
for root respiration and beneficial microbial activity. Fields recover
fertility and soil becomes soft and fluffy when IMO mixed with LAB is
sprayed on the field. This improved soil structure enhances water
infiltration, root penetration, and gas exchange - all essential for
healthy plant growth.

### Phosphate Solubilization

LAB solubilize phosphate at concentrations of 100 to 200 ppm. Using LAB
in phosphate-accumulated soil increases the soil's capacity to absorb
insoluble forms of phosphates and helps overcome saline disorders that
result from phosphate decomposition. This phosphorus-mobilizing function
makes LAB particularly valuable for soils that have accumulated
unavailable phosphorus from synthetic fertilizer applications.

### Root Growth Enhancement

LAB accelerate root growth during transplanting and effectively enhance
the initial growth of plants. This makes them valuable for all
transplanting operations, from vegetable seedlings to tree seedlings.
