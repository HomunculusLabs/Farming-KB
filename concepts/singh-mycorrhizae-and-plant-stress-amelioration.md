# Mycorrhizae and Plant Stress Amelioration

## Overview

[[mycorrhizal-symbiosis]] significantly enhances plant tolerance to a wide
range of abiotic and biotic stresses. Singh's treatment of
[[mycoremediation-pahs]] emphasizes that [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] not only
improve nutrient acquisition but also activate comprehensive stress
response mechanisms in host plants. This stress-mitigating function is
critical for establishing vegetation on contaminated sites where multiple
stressors typically co-occur.

## Drought Stress Tolerance

### Enhanced Water Uptake

- **Extensive hyphal networks**: Extraradical mycelium extends far
  beyond root depletion zones, accessing water from soil micropores
  unavailable to roots
- **Hyphal water transport**: Fungal hyphae transport water through bulk
  flow and cytoplasmic streaming during drought periods
- **Soil aggregation**: Mycorrhizal exudates (particularly glomalin)
  improve aggregate stability and water-holding capacity

### Osmotic Adjustment

- **Proline accumulation**: AMF-colonized plants show higher proline
  levels under drought, functioning as osmoprotectant and ROS scavenger
- **Sugar accumulation**: Increased soluble sugars (trehalose, fructose,
  sucrose) lower cellular osmotic potential
- **Glycine betaine**: Some associations increase glycine betaine levels,
  stabilizing proteins and membranes

### Aquaporin Regulation

Mycorrhizal fungi modulate plant aquaporin expression:

- Upregulation of specific aquaporin isoforms enhances root hydraulic
  conductivity
- Fungal aquaporins in extraradical hyphae facilitate water movement
- Coordinated regulation maintains water transport as soil potential
  declines

## Salinity Stress Tolerance

### Ion Homeostasis

- **Selective uptake**: Mycorrhizae help maintain favorable K+/Na+ ratios
  by enhancing K+ uptake while restricting Na+ accumulation
- **Sodium exclusion**: Some AMF species restrict Na+ transport to roots,
  sequestering it in fungal structures
- **Compartmentalization**: Sodium preferentially sequestered in vacuoles
  of both fungal and plant cells

### Antioxidant Defense Enhancement

Salinity induces oxidative stress through ROS generation. Mycorrhizal
plants show enhanced antioxidant capacity:

- **Superoxide dismutase (SOD)**: Converts superoxide to H2O2;
  elevated in mycorrhizal plants
- **Catalase (CAT)**: Decomposes H2O2; enhanced under salt stress
- **Peroxidase (POD)**: Scavenges H2O2 using various electron donors
- **Glutathione system**: Enhanced GR and APX activities maintain the
  ascorbate-glutathione cycle

## Temperature Stress Tolerance

### Heat Stress

- **Heat shock proteins**: AMF colonization upregulates HSP expression,
  protecting proteins from denaturation
- **Membrane stability**: Enhanced lipid composition and antioxidant
  protection maintain thermostability
- **Photosynthetic protection**: Reduced photoinhibition through improved
  non-photochemical quenching

### Cold Stress

- **Membrane fluidity**: Enhanced unsaturated fatty acid content
  maintains fluidity at low temperatures
- **Sugar accumulation**: Increased soluble sugars depress freezing point
- **Antifreeze proteins**: Some mycorrhizal fungi induce antifreeze
  protein expression in host plants

## Pathogen Protection

### Pathogen Protection Mechanisms

AMF provide protection through physical barriers (root colonization
impedes pathogen entry; altered architecture reduces attachment sites)
and biochemical defense: mycorrhiza-induced resistance (MIR) primes
defense responses, increased lignin and flavonoids create chemical
barriers, and pathogenesis-related proteins (chitinases, glucanases)
are upregulated. AMF also modify rhizosphere communities through
competitive exclusion and favor antagonistic bacteria.

## Signaling Pathways

### Common Symbiosis Signaling Pathway (CSSP)

The CSSP involves calcium spiking, CCaMK activation, and downstream
transcription factor activation. This pathway cross-talks with stress
signaling pathways.

### Hormonal Regulation

- **Abscisic acid (ABA)**: Elevated in mycorrhizal plants, contributing
  to stomatal regulation and drought response
- **Jasmonic acid and salicylic acid**: AMF modulate JA/SA balance,
  priming defense responses
- **Ethylene**: Altered ethylene signaling affects both stress tolerance
  and symbiosis establishment

### ROS Signaling

Mycorrhizal fungi modulate ROS levels through enhanced antioxidant
systems, preventing oxidative damage while maintaining signaling function.

## Relevance to Mycoremediation

Stress tolerance from mycorrhizae is essential for [[mycoremediation-pahs]]:

- Contaminated sites impose multiple simultaneous stresses (metal
  toxicity, nutrient deficiency, drought, pathogen pressure)
- Mycorrhizal plants survive and grow better under combined stress
- Enhanced plant vigor supports greater contaminant uptake, degradation,
  or stabilization
- Reduced need for intensive site management during remediation
