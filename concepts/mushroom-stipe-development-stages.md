# Mushroom Stipe Development Stages in Psilocybe Cubensis

The development of the *Psilocybe cubensis* basidiocarp (fruiting body) can
be divided into four distinct morphological stages, each characterized by
specific changes in the pileus (cap), stipe (stem), and tropic behavior.
This staging system, established by Badham (1982), provides a framework for
understanding how environmental stimuli differentially affect mushroom
growth at different developmental phases.

## Anatomical Background

Much of the cell division in the basidiocarp has taken place by the time
primordia are a few centimeters high (Heim and Wasson, 1958). Further
growth involves primarily **acropetal expansion** of these existing cells
— cells near the apex of the stipe and pileus expand and elongate while
cells below remain relatively static. Some limited cell division may
continue at the upper part of the stipe (Craig, 1977).

This anatomical constraint is important because it means that tropic
curvatures in the stipe are primarily the result of differential cell
expansion rather than differential cell division. The cells that have
already formed simply expand at different rates on different sides of the
stipe.

## Stage 1: Initial Pileus Swelling

### Morphology
Stage 1 is characterized by a slight swelling of the pileus. The primordium
has risen above the substrate surface and the developing cap begins to
enlarge, but the overall shape is still essentially elongated and
stipe-like.

### Tropic Behavior
- The stipe is highly responsive to wind (anemotropism dominates)
- [[gadd-spitzenkorper-vesicle-supply-centre-hyphal-tip-growth-direction]] is primarily influenced by [[evaporative-demand-hypothesis-mushroom-growth]] gradients
- The mushroom has not yet developed significant spore-producing tissue
- No [[spore-formation-geotropic-response-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]] is observed at this stage

### Duration
Stage 1 represents approximately the first 24 hours of the 72-hour
developmental window studied in the wind tunnel experiments. In natural
conditions, the duration may vary with temperature, humidity, and strain
genetics.

## Stage 2: Spherical Pileus

### Morphology
The pileus becomes nearly spherical. The cap is now clearly differentiated
from the stipe but has not yet begun to expand or open. The gill tissue is
developing within the closed pileus but spores have not yet formed in
significant numbers.

### Tropic Behavior
This is the **transitional stage** where tropic behavior begins to shift:
- Anemotropism is still active but weakening
- The negative geotropic response begins to emerge in fertile
  basidiocarps
- In sporeless mutants, the anemotropic response continues without
  transition
- The first signs of the pileus turning upward away from gravity appear

### Significance
Stage 2 represents the critical point at which the mushroom switches from
environment-driven growth (responding to wind and humidity) to
reproduction-driven growth (positioning the cap for [[mushroom-veil-function-wind-light-protection-prior-to-spore-release]]). This
switch is mediated by substances produced in the developing gill tissue.

## Stage 3: Maturity and Sporulation

### Morphology
The pileus opens into a hemispherical shape, exposing the gills. Spore
production begins. The [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] may still be intact or rupturing. The
stipe has reached its maximum length.

### Tropic Behavior
- [[negative-geotropism-in-mushroom-fruiting]] is now the dominant tropic response
- The pileus turns upward and the gills face downward
- Anemotropic bending is largely masked by the geotropic response
- Light is necessary for full expression of the geotropic response,
  likely because it is necessary for [[spore-production-geotropic-sensitivity-coupling-basidiomycete-badham-1982]]
