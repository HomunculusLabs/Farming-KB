## Vegetable Growing Guide (Solomon)

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: Gardening When It Counts, [[hamilton-seed-sowing-techniques]] depth by size:
- **Tiny seeds** (celery, basil, sorrel, most herbs): fall into tiny cracks of raked soil, barely covered with fine compost. Press earth down gently to restore capillarity. Direct-seed only in mild temperatures or shade rows temporarily.
- **Ordinary small seeds** (brassicas, carrots, parsley, fennel): about half an inch (1.25 cm) deep
- **Larger small seeds** (spinach, beet, chard, radish, oxalis): about three-quarters inch (2 cm) deep
- **Large seeds** (corn, beans, peas, cucurbits): one inch (2.5 cm) or more deep

The most common error with small seeds is sowing too deep. The emerging shoot exhausts its energy reserves before reaching light.

## Progressive Thinning Method

For row crops sown in drills:
1. Immediately after emergence, thin clusters so survivors don"t quite touch
2. When seedlings lean away from each other (competing for light), thin again
3. A week later, when bumping again, remove every other plant
4. When survivors touch at final spacing, remove every other one

Every thinning stage produces salad material — nothing is wasted.

## Fertility Categories

Solomon defines three levels of soil fertility demand:
- **Low demand**: Grows in minimally fertile soil
- **Medium demand**: Needs moderate fertility (COF or good compost)
- **High demand**: Requires high fertility for good production

All vegetables grow better with more fertility than the bare minimum.

## Kale and Cold-Hardy Brassicas

Kale is the most vigorous and cold-hardy of all garden brassicas. Two species:
- **B. oleracea kale**: Grows an unbranched tall central stalk
- **Siberian kale (B. napus)**: Forms a rosette pattern close to the ground, preferred for raw salads

Kale can resume growing after overnight freezes of 6°C. As long as soil hasn't frozen, kale keeps going. Flavor gets sweeter after frost (similar to chicories).

Collards are slightly less vigorous than kale. Giant kohlrabi is a low-demand fodder crop whose globe can reach volleyball size.

All brassicas need more calcium than most vegetables. If not using COF and gardening on rain-leached land, broadcast 5 lbs finely ground agricultural lime per 100 sq ft before sowing.

## Spacing Shorthand

Solomon uses abbreviated notation:
- **"In clusters"**: Few seeds sown in a cluster at fixed intervals, used for [[comparison-raised-beds-vs-hugelkultur]] or wide raised rows
- **"In drills"**: Seeds set in the bottom of a furrow
- **"In hills"**: Seeds sown in highly fertile mounds, sometimes with additional fertilizer
