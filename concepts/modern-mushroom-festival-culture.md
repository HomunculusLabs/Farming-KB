## Glastonbury Festival and the British Psychedelic Scene

### The 1997 Glastonbury Experience

The 1997 Glastonbury Festival marked a significant moment in British mushroom culture. That year, the area experienced abundant rainfall that produced bumper crops of liberty caps ([[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]]). Festival attendees — "festival-goers, hippies, clubbers, artists, musicians and the other usual suspects" — foraged the surrounding fields for the naturally occurring mushrooms.

The combination of a major cultural festival, abundant natural mushroom growth, and a community experienced with psychedelic use created a unique confluence of circumstances. This event exemplified how natural mushroom availability can intersect with cultural gatherings to create spontaneous, large-scale psychedelic experiences.

## The Telluride Mushroom Festival

One of the oldest and most prominent mushroom festivals in the United States, the Telluride Mushroom Festival in Colorado has been running since 1981. The festival brings together:
- Mycologists and scientists
- Mushroom foragers and culinary enthusiasts
- Artists and musicians
- Psychedelic researchers and advocates
- Environmentalists interested in fungal ecology

The festival features forays, lectures, cooking demonstrations, and art exhibitions, reflecting the broad spectrum of mushroom appreciation in contemporary culture.

## The Revival of Interest

### Scientific and Therapeutic Renaissance

Beginning in the 1990s and accelerating through the 2000s and 2010s, several factors converged to renew interest in psychedelic mushrooms:
- Growing clinical evidence for therapeutic applications (depression, anxiety, PTSD, addiction)
- Legal changes in some jurisdictions permitting therapeutic or research use
- Increased public awareness of mycology and fungal ecology
- The popularization of mycoremediation and sustainable agriculture
