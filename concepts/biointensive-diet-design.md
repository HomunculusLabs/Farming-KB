# Biointensive Diet Design

Biointensive diet design is the process of planning a complete, nutritionally adequate vegetarian diet that can be grown entirely within a [[biointensive-seed-saving]] must be included in the plan

## The GB Diet Composition

A typical one-person biointensive diet design allocates garden beds as follows:

### Calorie Crops (30-40% of area)
- Potatoes: 2-3 beds (primary calorie source in temperate climates)
- Sweet potatoes: 1-2 beds (primary calorie source in warm climates)
- Grains (wheat, corn, oats): 2-3 beds
- Legumes for protein: 1-2 beds (fava beans, garbanzos, soybeans)
- Jerusalem artichokes: 1 bed (perennial calorie backup)

### Vegetable Crops (20-30% of area)
- Alliums (onions, garlic, leeks): 1-2 beds
- [[growing-root-vegetables-guide]] (carrots, beets, turnips): 1-2 beds
- Summer vegetables (tomatoes, peppers, eggplant): 1-2 beds
- Cucurbits (squash, cucumbers): 1 bed

### Carbon/Compost Crops (40-60% of area, overlapping with above)
- Many calorie crops serve double duty ([[query-what-is-succession-planting-and-how-do-i-plan-a-succession-planting-schedule]]**: Multiple plantings of quick crops (radishes, lettuce, beans) ensure continuous harvest

## Protein Planning

A vegetarian diet from the garden requires careful protein planning:

- **Complete proteins**: Complementary grain + legume combinations (rice + beans, wheat + lentils)
- **Soybeans**: Provide complete protein alone; highest protein of any garden crop
- **Fava beans**: Hardy, nitrogen-fixing, good protein content
- **Eggs** (optional): If poultry is included in the system, they consume garden waste and produce protein
- **Nuts/seeds**: Sunflowers, sesame, peanuts for fats and protein

## Fats and Oils

Fat is the most difficult nutrient to produce in a small garden:

- Sunflower seeds: Best oilseed for small-scale pressing
- Sesame: Can be pressed for oil
- Peanuts: Good fat + protein source
- Olive trees: Too large for most mini-farms but possible in some climates
- Nuts: Walnuts, hazelnuts, almonds -- require trees but are long-term investments

## Example One-Person Annual Plan

| Season | Beds in Production |
|--------|-------------------|
| Spring | Greenhouse/transplants, early greens, peas |
| Summer | Tomatoes, peppers, squash, beans, corn, potatoes |
| Fall | Grains harvest, root vegetables, [[coleman-winter-greens-nutritional-quality-cold-sweetening]], planning/sorting seeds |

## Scaling Up

- 1 person: ~3,000-4,000 sq ft
- 2 people: ~5,000-6,000 sq ft (economies of scale; paths stay the same)
- Family of 4: ~8,000-10,000 sq ft (about 1/5 to 1/4 acre)
