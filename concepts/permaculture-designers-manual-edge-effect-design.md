# Edge Effect Design
#permaculture #edge-effect #patterns #design #biodiversity

Updated: 2026-04-18

> From: Bill Mollison, *Permaculture: A Designers Manual* (1988), Chapter 4, Sections 4.5-4.6.

## Overview

In our world of constant events, especially in the living world, more events occur at boundaries than occur elsewhere, because of special conditions or differences. It is common to find that there are more different types of living species at any such boundary or edge than there are within the adjoining system or medium. Boundaries tend to be species-rich.

## The Edge Effect

The "edge effect" is recognised by ecologists: the interface between two ecosystems represents a third, more complex system which combines both. At interfaces, species from both systems can exist, and in many cases the boundary also supports its own species.

### Production at Edges
Gross photosynthetic production is higher at interfaces. For example:
- Estuaries and coral reefs (land/ocean interface) show the highest production per unit area of any major ecosystem
- Forest/pasture interfaces show greater complexity than either system in both producers (plants) and consumers (animals)
- Fire mosaic landscapes are rich in species

The Tasmanian Aborigines burnt forest to maintain a large interface of forest/plain, since these transitional areas provided a great variety and amount of food. Such mosaics were the basis of Australian Aboriginal landscape management.

## Design Principles

### Maximising Edge
In view of the edge effect, it seems worthwhile to increase interface between particular habitats to a maximum. A landscape with a complex edge mosaic is interesting and beautiful; it can be considered the basis of the art of productive landscape design.

### Harmonics and Geometry of Boundaries
The amplitude, configuration, and periodicity of an edge, surface, or boundary may be varied by design.

## Edge Forms

### Sinuous and Lobular Edges
Notched or lobular edges, such as we achieve in plan by following hill contours, afford sheltered, wetter, drier, hotter, or more exposed micro-habitats for a variety of species. Serrate or zig-zag fences not only stand on their own, but resist wind-throw much better than straight barriers.

### Keyhole Beds
Lobular embayments, like the keyhole beds, are obviously sheltered, spacious habitats for gardens and settlements. Parallel paths take up to 50% of the area; keyhole beds less than 30% of the ground.

### Crenellated Pond Edges
Without altering the area of a field and a pond, we can double the plants on the pond-edge (e.g. blueberries) by crenellating this edge to increase the earth/water interface.

## Types of Interfaces

Edges can be created between many conditions:
- Air/water, fresh/brackish, warm/cool
- Flowing/still, grass/water, marsh/water
- Anaerobic/subsoil, soil/subsoil, stream/bank
- Brackish/salty, stream order/sub-order
- Catchment/catchment, forest/water, water/mud

## Spiral Design

Spirals are found where harmonic flow, compact form, efficient array, increased exchange, transport, or anchoring is needed. Natural spiral forms can be used in design both to create compact forms of otherwise spread-out placements and to guide water and wind flows. Species of sharks and invertebrates develop spiral gut lining to increase absorption.

### Fibonacci Series
Leaf and branch placements may progress in a regular (Fibonacci) series: 1:2 + 1:3 = 2:5; 1:3 + 2:5 = 3:8, etc., producing sequences 5:13, 8:21, 13:34 and onwards. These sequences are found in plants and in planetary orbits.

### Flow Over Landscapes
Whirlpools or vortices are shed alternately from a fixed bluff body located in flow. Beautiful and complex forms are thus generated, and these are the basis of the work at the Virbela Institute on flowforms. The sets of vortices shed downstream from fixed bodies in flow are called Von Karman trails, stable at the 1:3.6 ratio. Trees and [[permaculture-designers-manual-windbreak-species]] produce similar effects in wind.

## Accumulation at Edges

When a boundary separates two things which differ, there is an opportunity for trade, transactions, or translation across the border. Where the boundary is difficult to pass, accumulations may occur:
- Stranded shells on the beach
- Brush piled up against a fence
- People at water edges (80% live at water edges)
- Mulch and nutrients collecting on [[permaculture-designers-manual-mulch-systems]]

People are, at heart, strandlopers and beach-combers; even our dwellings pile up at the junction of sea and land, on estuaries, and at the edge of forest, river, marsh, or plain.

## Boundary Functions

Boundaries can be designed to:
- Net, stop, or sieve-through animals, plants, money, and influence
- Accumulate resources (mulch, nutrients, water)
- Create microclimates (shelter, humidity, temperature variation)
- Support unique edge-adapted species
- Translate energy and resources between systems

## Practical Applications

- Crenellate pond edges for more planting area
- Create keyhole bed patterns for efficient garden access
- Use zig-zag fences for wind resistance
- Design spiral herb gardens for compact, diverse plantings
- Create lobular forest edges for wildlife habitat diversity
- Use windbreaks with varied geometry for different shelter zones
