## Biosorption Mechanisms

Biosorption consists of several mechanisms that differ according to the
fungal species, the origin of the biomass, and its processing. These
mechanisms include ion exchange, chelation, adsorption, crystallization,
and precipitation, followed by ion entrapment in inter- and intrafibrillar
capillaries and spaces of the polysaccharide material, and diffusion
through cell walls and membranes.

Cell walls of fungi are composed of chitins, chitosans, and glucans,
and also contain proteins, lipids, and other polysaccharides. The biomass
contains a larger number and variety of functional groups than
monofunctional ion-exchange resins. These sites include carboxyl,
sulfate, phosphate, hydroxyl, amino, imino, sulfonate, imidazole,
sulfydryl, carbonyl, thioether, and other moieties.

Biosorption mechanisms are divided into two categories: metabolism
independent (employing live or dead biomass) and metabolism dependent
(transforming metals internally coupled with production of extracellular
metabolites). Electron microscopy, x-ray energy diffraction analysis, and
infrared spectroscopy are used to study binding mechanisms.
