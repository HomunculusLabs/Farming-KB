## Fungal Biofiltration Systems

### Biofilter Design and Operation

Biofilters consist of:

- **Filter bed**: Packed with organic (peat, compost, [[fukuoka-textdoc-sawdust-wood-chips-orchard-soil-acidification-experiment]]) or
  synthetic media (ceramic beads, polyurethane foam) colonized by fungi
- **Humidification chamber**: Pre-humidifies incoming gas stream
- **Gas distribution system**: Ensures even flow through filter media
- **Leachate collection**: Captures metabolic byproducts

Key operating parameters:

| Parameter | Optimal Range | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Moisture content | 40-70% (wet basis) | Critical for activity |
| Temperature | 20-35 degrees C | Mesophilic range |
| pH | 3.0-7.0 | Fungi tolerate wider range |
| EBRT | 30-120 seconds | Longer = higher removal |

### Biotrickling Filters

Biotrickling filters circulate a liquid phase through the filter bed:

- Enable better pH control and nutrient supply
- Suitable for water-soluble VOCs and chlorinated compounds
- Fungal-dominated systems show superior performance for hydrophobic VOCs

## Key Fungal Genera for VOC Biodegradation

- **Cladosporium**: C. sphaerospermum degrades toluene, benzene, styrene
- **Exophiala**: Black yeast species adapted to VOC-rich environments
- **Fusarium**: F. oxysporum degrades aromatic and aliphatic VOCs via
  cytochrome P450 systems
- **Trametes**: T. versicolor degrades VOCs through [[manganese-peroxidase-mechanism]]
- **Aspergillus**: A. niger degrades oxygenated VOCs and short-chain
  aliphatic compounds
- **Scedosporium**: S. apiospermum shows excellent biofilter performance

## Challenges and Limitations

- **Incomplete mineralization**: Some partial oxidation products may be
  more toxic than parent compounds
- **Biofilter clogging**: Excessive [[brassinosteroid-fungal-growth-promotion]] can obstruct gas flow
- **Sporulation and health concerns**: Spores may pose allergen risks
- **Process control**: Maintaining optimal conditions at large scale
  requires sophisticated monitoring
