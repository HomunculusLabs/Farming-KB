# Island and Atoll Climate Design

> Source: Bill Mollison, *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* (1988), Chapter 10

Updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

All coral sand cays and many low atolls lie within 28 degrees of the equator, as do coral reef areas and sandy alkaline coasts. Very careful design approaches are needed to avoid the known risks of hurricane erosion, water table pollution, and poor nutrition due to limited diets.

## Soils and Conditions

On atolls, expect:
- Soil pH values of 8.0-9.5 (highly alkaline)
- Sand abrasion
- Basic mineral deficiency (especially iron, zinc, molybdenum, boron) in soils and plants
- Thin coralline sand over hardpan of caliche or calcrete
- Calcrete is worsened by application of superphosphate

### Amending Soils
Elemental sulphur, iron sulphates, and humus added to garden soils and planting holes lower the pH. Humus sources include:
- Palm fronds, coconut husks, tree trunks
- Leaf litter from pioneer species: Casuarina equisetifolia, sea grape (Coccolobus unifera), coastal shrubs (Scaevola, Tornefortia, Pemphis), mangroves, and Barringtonia trees

### Phosphate Sources
Guano from seabirds (terns, gulls, boobies, frigate birds, shearwaters) provides sufficient phosphates if colonies are protected or encouraged. Failing this, domestic pigeons, quail, pheasant, geese, ducks, and chickens can be kept.

## Water Lens Management

### The Freshwater Lens
The sole natural water sources on low islands are biological storages (baobabs and coconuts provide about 12 coconut trees per person per year for drinking water) and water trapped in sand below the caliche, floating on seawater.

### Preventing Pollution
As the beach berm is 2-4 m above sea level and the inner atoll about 2-3 m, the delicacy of the water lens is obvious. For this reason:
- Settlement, toilet areas, processing, livestock, and gardens should be kept to the periphery
- Interior devoted to dense natural stands of food and native trees
- Interior kept free of pesticides, industrial pollutants, and fuels
- A polluting source at the centre of the island can affect the whole water lens, all plants, and all people

### Increasing Water Supply
1. Use runoff water to house tanks (reinforced cement-coral-sand mix construction)
2. Use deep mulches and grow coconut, banana, arrowroot, papaya, legumes for leaf and trunk materials
3. Try leaf ferment seals (gleys) of gently-sloped coral pits, where 20 cm deep mash of banana, papaya, and soft green leaf is applied to base and sides, covered with plastic until fermenting, then pumped full of water from the freshwater lens

Success follows careful trials at small scale, then scaling up to significant ponds for ducks, garden water, and animal drinking water.

## Tree Planting on Coral Cays

To plant trees:
1. Clear a patch of topsoil and break open the caliche
2. Make a pit about 40 x 40 cm and 60-80 cm deep
3. In the pit, make a humus pile of domestic and plant wastes, plus sulphur and mineral trace elements
4. Plant a coconut or other seedling tree

Tree roots spread above the caliche layer, and feeder roots go below the caliche to the water lens (usually 0.75-1.0 m down, 1.0-2.0 m deep as a saturated sand layer).

## Garden Systems

### Pit Gardens (Best Method)
Open a pit garden about 8 m x 15 m, sloped for stability to about 2 m deep (often damp or wet on the base). Sloped sides stabilised as steps with coconut logs or caliche; spoil banked around the rim. Mulch thickly applied.

Planting sequence from base to crown:
1. **Base (damp, mulch)**: watercress, parsley, chives, Brassica, taro, kangkong, salad greens
2. **First terrace (18-25 cm above base)**: tomatoes, peppers, taro, sweet corn, beans, peas
3. **Second terrace (25-60 cm above base)**: banana, papaya, sweet potato, cassava (all provide mulch)
4. **Third/highest terrace**: cassava, sweet potato, banana, dry-tolerant vegetable, mulch trees (Leucaena, Glyricidia, Tipuana tipu, Moringa oleifera), local legumes, palms for frond mulch, vines (passionfruit, four-winged bean)

### Log Box Gardens
Under light palm-legume canopies, boxes of palm logs hold thick mulch and household waste for surface gardens. Plant beds of potato, yam, sweet potato, and normal vegetable crop. The thicker the mulch, the less watering needed.

## Key Principles
- Log boxes, pits, thick mulch, and high shade canopy are the essentials for good atoll gardens
- Staples are coconut, root crop, fruits, normal salad vegetables, plus fish and shellfish from lagoons or coasts
- Protect natural vegetation and unique birds, reptiles, and lagoon or reef fauna
- Use shallow marine waters for aquaculture and pond fish
- Develop local energy resources

## Hurricane Preparedness
Hurricane erosion and damage to plantations and coasts is a primary risk. Design for wind-resistant species, secure structures, and rapid recovery systems.
