# Fruiting Conditions and Pinning

The transition from vegetative [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]] to reproductive fruiting
body formation is triggered by a combination of environmental cues.
Understanding and controlling these cues is the key to consistent
mushroom production. Different species have different requirements, but
the fundamental triggering factors are shared across most cultivated
mushrooms.

## The Fruiting Trigger

Mycelium continues growing vegetatively as long as conditions remain
favorable for colonization. To initiate fruiting, the grower must
change the environment to signal that conditions are becoming less
favorable. The primary triggers are fresh air exchange reducing CO2
levels, high humidity of 85 to 95 percent, introduction of light, a
temperature drop of 5 to 10 C, and evaporation from the substrate
surface. Not all triggers are required for every species.

## Environmental Triggers

[[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] is initiated when mycelium detects specific
environmental changes signaling less favorable conditions for continued
[[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]. These triggers vary by species but generally include
reduced carbon dioxide levels, temperature shift, increased humidity,
and light. The fungus interprets this combination as indicating
substrate depletion, prompting allocation toward [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]].
[[query-how-to-grow-oyster-mushrooms-at-home]] respond strongly to light and fresh air, while
shiitake is primarily triggered by temperature shock and physical
disturbance of the substrate surface.

## Temperature Range

Most cultivated species fruit within 12 to 25 degrees Celsius. Warmer
temperatures accelerate development but produce thinner-fleshed,
lower-quality mushrooms. Cooler temperatures produce denser, meatier
fruiting bodies with better texture and longer shelf life. A
temperature drop of 5 to 10 degrees from spawn run is a common
initiation trigger. Many commercial growers use the lower end of each
species range for premium quality product.

## Humidity Requirements

High humidity is critical throughout fruiting, particularly during
pinning. Relative humidity of 85 to 95 percent is recommended. Below
80 percent, primordia rapidly desiccate. Above 95 percent, condensation
promotes [[growing-gourmet-bacterial-blotch-disease-management]]. Consistent humidity is more important than
a specific target number. Fluctuations during pinning can trigger
widespread pin abortion.

## Fresh Air Exchange

Fresh air exchange reduces CO2 concentrations and promotes evaporation
from the substrate surface. During spawn run, CO2 levels of 5,000 to
10,000 ppm promote vegetative growth. For fruiting, levels must drop
to 400 to 800 ppm. Elevated CO2 causes elongated stems and small caps,
called leggy growth. Air should be exchanged two to four times per hour
during active fruiting.

## Light

Most cultivated species require light for normal fruiting. Light serves
as a directional cue orienting the cap upward for spore dispersal.
Indirect natural daylight or LED lighting at 5000 to 6500 Kelvin works
well. A 12-hour on and off photoperiod is standard. Direct sunlight
should be avoided. Oyster mushrooms are highly phototropic and bend
toward light. Insufficient light produces pale, spindly mushrooms with
poor cap development and reduced yields.
