# pH Management for Cannabis Growing

pH is one of the three main factors to consider when evaluating growing
medium for cannabis (along with nutrients and structure). Greg Green's
"The Cannabis Grow Bible" emphasizes that maintaining proper pH is
essential for nutrient availability and overall plant health. pH problems
can cause [[green-cannabis-rockwool-hydroponic-growing-media]]
  systems: Cannabis prefers a pH range of 5.2 to 6.3.

See [[substrate-ph-management]] for detailed pH management guidance.

It is easier to maintain a pH range rather than an exact set point.
Regular monitoring with small pH meters (available at most gardening and
grow shops) is essential for successful cultivation.

## pH in Soil Systems

### Selecting Soil by pH

When choosing soil, growers should look for a pH of 7.0 -- this should
be stated on the bag. Going above or below this mark can cause problems
during growth. The pH meter is used to verify the actual soil conditions
throughout the grow.

### Correcting Acidic Soil (pH Below 7)

When soil pH is too acidic, it needs to be brought back toward neutral.
The recommended approach:

- Use Lime (available in small containers from grow shops).
- Add small amounts of lime to the soil during the next watering.
- Check the pH the next day to study the effects.
- Repeat with incremental adjustments until the target pH is reached.

Over time, experienced growers learn exactly how much lime their
specific soil needs to correct acidity. This knowledge comes through
trial and error -- Greg Green notes that the best cannabis growers are
those who have made and learned from many mistakes.

### Correcting Alkaline Soil (pH Above 7)

When soil pH is too alkaline, organic and household ingredients can be
used to make it more acidic:

- Cottonseed meal
- Lemon peels
- Ground coffee
- Some fertilizers are naturally acidic and can bring pH down to 7

The method involves moderately applying the chosen substance and checking
pH regularly. Over time, growers learn the right amounts for their
specific pot sizes and soil volumes. Experimentation and learning are
emphasized over rigid formulas.

### pH Up and pH Down Products

Commercial pH Up and pH Down products are available in most grow shops
and provide a more precise approach to pH adjustment than organic
amendments. These chemical solutions come in two forms:

- pH Up: Raises pH (makes more alkaline).
- pH Down: Lowers pH (makes more acidic).

These products offer more predictable results than household remedies
and are increasingly popular among both soil and hydroponic growers.

## pH in Hydroponic Systems

Hydroponic pH management differs significantly from soil-based systems:

### Target Range

Cannabis plants in hydroponic systems prefer a pH of 5.2 to 6.3, which
is more acidic than the soil target of 7.0. This lower range optimizes
nutrient availability in water-based growing systems.

### pH Adjustment in Hydroponics

In hydroponics, pH Up and pH Down liquid solutions are the standard
adjustment method (rather than lime or organic amendments used in soil).
These are inexpensive and can be added directly to the nutrient
reservoir.

### Monitoring Frequency

pH can slip up and down very quickly in hydroponics systems, much more
rapidly than in soil. This means hydroponic growers must check pH more
frequently -- ideally with every reservoir change and at regular
intervals between changes.

### Rockwool Pre-Soak pH

Rockwool should be pre-soaked for 24 hours in water adjusted to the
appropriate pH level before use:

- 5.6 for hydroponic grows.
- 7.0 for soil grows (when rockwool is used as a starter medium that
  will be transplanted into soil).

## pH and Nutrient Availability

### Nutrient Lockout

pH problems are a major cause of [[teaming-with-nutrients-ph-testing-and-soil-amendment]]
for pH testing and amendment guidance. Lockout can occur when:

- pH is too high or too low for specific nutrients to be soluble.
- Chemical reactions in the medium create toxic substances that block
  root uptake.
- Reactions change the chemical properties of other nutrients.

Different nutrients become available at different pH levels. When pH
drifts outside the optimal range, certain nutrients become locked out
even if they are present in adequate quantities.
