# Beta-Glucan Pharmacology from Medicinal Mushrooms

## Overview

Beta-glucans are the primary bioactive polysaccharides responsible for the anticancer and immunomodulatory properties of medicinal mushrooms. These glucose polymers are widely distributed across fungal species and represent the most extensively studied class of mushroom-derived biological response modifiers. Water-soluble beta-D-glucans, heteropolysaccharides, and polysaccharide-protein complexes all contribute to the pharmacological activity of medicinal mushroom extracts.

## Molecular Structure and Antitumour Activity

### Primary Structure
The best-studied beta-glucans, Lentinan (from Lentinus edodes) and Schizophyllan (from Schizophyllum commune), share a common structural motif:
- Main chain of (1-3)-linked beta-D-glucopyranosyl units
- Beta-D-glucopyranosyl branch units linked (1-6) at intervals averaging three main chain units
- Degree of branching (DB) approximately 0.33
- Average molecular weights: Lentinan ~500,000 Da; Schizophyllan ~450,000 Da

### Higher-Order Structure
The antitumour activity of (1-3)-beta-glucans is critically dependent on three-dimensional conformation:
- **Triple helices**: Higher molecular weight molecules form triple helical structures stabilised by branch units
- **Single helix**: Both Lentinan and Schizophyllan may be active only in single helical conformation
- **Helical conformation**: The antitumour activity resides in the helical backbone conformation, possibly triple-stranded
- **Hydrophilic groups**: The presence of hydrophilic groups on the outside surface of the helix is perhaps even more important than helical conformation itself

### Structure-Activity Relationships
- Increased water solubility favours enhanced antitumour activity
- Location of substituent groups is important for activity
- Immune response to beta-D-glucans may be partially non-specific, determined by molecular size rather than chemical structure
- Individual species-derived beta-glucans have unique molecular structures

## Classes of Mushroom Beta-Glucans

### Homoglucans (Pure Beta-Glucans)
Water-soluble beta-D-glucans found across mushroom species:
- Auricularia auricula-judae: (1-3)-beta-glucan
- [[stamets-reishi-ganoderma-lucidum-cultivation]]: Fl-1a (beta-glucan), FIII-2b (hetero-beta-glucan), acidic beta-glucan
- [[maitake-grifola-frondosa-comprehensive-profile]]: Grifolan (beta-glucan), Fa-1a-beta (acidic beta-glucan), FIII-2c (hetero-beta-glucan)
- [[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]]: Beta-glucan, Coriolan, PSK/Krestin (beta-glucan-protein)
- Agaricus blazei: FI1-a-beta (beta-glucan), FIII2-beta (beta-glucan)

### Heteropolysaccharides
Beta-D-glucans with heterosaccharide chains of xylose, mannose, galactose, and uronic acid, extractable by salt and alkali treatments. Ganoderma lucidum contains glucuronoglucan, xyloglucan, mannanoglucan, xylomannoglucan, and other active heteroglucans and protein complexes.

### Polysaccharide-Protein Complexes (Proteoglycans)
Polypeptide chains or small proteins to which polysaccharide chains are stably attached:
- **PSK (Krestin)**: Molecular weight ~100,000 Da, rich in aspartic and glutamic amino acids, orally bioavailable
- **PSP**: Contains at least four discrete proteoglycan molecules; polysaccharide chains are true beta-glucans with 1-4, 1-2, and 1-3 glucose linkages plus galactose, mannose, and arabinose linkages. Molecular weight ~100,000 Da, orally bioavailable
- **ATOM** from Agaricus blazei: Glucomannan-protein complex
- **AB-FP** from Agaricus blazei: Mannan-protein complex
- At least 10 kinds of protein-bound polysaccharides have been isolated from Trametes versicolor, most covered by US and Japanese patents

## Stability and Degradation

### Post-Harvest Degradation
Beta-glucan concentration and activity are affected by fruitbody development stage and [[vegetable-storage-conditions-by-temperature-and-humidity]]:
- Immunomodulating activity of L. edodes extracts decreased rapidly at 20 degrees C over 7 days
- No decrease occurred at low temperature storage (1 and 5 degrees C)
- Activity decrease was related to Lentinan degradation by internal beta-glucanase activity
- Similar patterns observed with G. frondosa: activity increased during growth, then decreased at final maturation

### Pharmaceutical Implications
- Mushrooms must be harvested at optimum beta-glucan concentration
- Harvested fruitbodies require correct temperature storage before processing
- Distant sourcing with inadequate environmental conditions compromises beta-glucan content
- Fermentation processes allow harvesting at optimum production points (as practised with antibiotics)

## Administration Routes

### Intravenous/Intraperitoneal
Lentinan and Schizophyllan are large molecules effective only by i.v. or i.p. administration. This limits their use to clinical settings and reduces patient convenience.

### Oral Bioavailability
Several mushroom beta-glucans and proteoglycans demonstrate oral bioavailability:
- **PSK**: Orally bioavailable at ~100,000 Da
- **PSP**: Easily delivered by oral route
- **Maitake D-Fraction**: Orally bioavailable
- **Acid-treated Agaricus blazei beta-1,6 glucan**: Native glucan shows no oral activity, but acid treatment producing ~10 kDa fragments restores oral bioavailability -- a finding with potential significance for all large beta-glucans

### Contemporaneous Oncology Trends
Modern oncology practice increasingly favours outpatient treatment and self-administered oral formulations for improved quality of life. The development of orally bioavailable mushroom beta-glucans aligns with this trend.

## Purification Methods

Purification processes for different mushroom species vary:
- **Hot water extraction**: Primary method for most beta-glucans
- **Salt and alkali treatments**: For heteropolysaccharides
- **Deproteination**: Used to obtain D-Fraction from Maitake
- **Ethanol precipitation**: Used to obtain LAP from LEM
- **DMSO and alkali**: Used for Reishi polysaccharide purification
- **Acid treatment**: Novel method for improving oral bioavailability of large glucans

## Safety

The safety criteria for mushroom-derived beta-glucans have been exhaustively studied. In Phase I human clinical tests, beta-glucans demonstrate remarkably few side-effects. No maximum tolerated dose was reached in Japanese and Chinese Phase I studies. Similar results have been obtained across multiple beta-glucan preparations.
