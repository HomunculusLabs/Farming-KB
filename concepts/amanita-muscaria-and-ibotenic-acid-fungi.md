# Amanita Muscaria and Ibotenic Acid Fungi

The Amanita species containing amanita muscaria and ibotenic acid fungi and [[amanita-muscaria-pharmacology]] form a
chemically and culturally distinct category of neurotropic fungi, entirely
separate from the [[psilocybin]]-containing mushrooms. While psilocybin fungi
act primarily as serotonin receptor agonists producing classic psychedelic
experiences, the ibotenic acid fungi are **GABAergic** — they modulate the
GABA neurotransmitter system, producing effects more accurately described as
deliriant, sedative, or dissociative.

## Active Species

Three Amanita species are recognized as containing ibotenic acid and
muscimol:

### Amanita muscaria

[[amanita-muscaria]] — the fly agaric — is arguably the most iconic mushroom
in the world. Its bright red cap dotted with white warts is ubiquitous in
visual culture, from fairy tales to video games. It has a broad circumboreal
distribution across Europe, Asia, and North America, with introduced
populations in the Southern Hemisphere (Australia, New Zealand, South
America).

Traditionally, [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] was used as an entheogen by
several Siberian indigenous peoples, most notably the **Chukchee** and
**Koryak** of northeastern Siberia. Wasson and Wasson (1957) documented these
practices in detail. The Chukchee consumed the mushroom fresh, dried, or
in a decoction, and also practiced the unusual custom of drinking the urine
of those who had consumed the mushroom, since the active compound muscimol is
excreted largely unchanged in urine — effectively recycling and concentrating
the psychoactive constituents.

The effects of [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] are variable and depend on
preparation, dosage, and individual physiology. They may include nausea,
sweating, visual distortions, auditory changes, feelings of strength or
disassociation, and in higher doses, a delirious state with vivid dreamlike
imagery. The experience is generally considered less desirable and more
unpredictable than psilocybin-containing mushrooms.

### Amanita pantherina

[[amanita-pantherina]] — the panther cap — contains ibotenic acid and
muscimol but is generally considered more toxic than Amanita muscaria|A.
muscaria. It is found across Europe and parts of Asia. Hatfield (1979)
confirmed the presence of ibotenic acid in this species. Cases of poisoning
are reported from central Europe, where it is sometimes confused with edible
species. Its effects are more consistently unpleasant, with pronounced
nausea and gastrointestinal distress.

### Amanita regalis

[[amanita-regalis]] — the royal fly agaric — is a closely related species
found in northern Europe and Scandinavia. It resembles Amanita muscaria|A.
muscaria but has a brownish cap rather than red. It contains ibotenic acid
and muscimol and has been used traditionally in parts of its range, though
far less commonly than A. muscaria.

## Chemistry

### Ibotenic Acid

amanita muscaria and ibotenic acid fungi is a glutamate receptor agonist that acts as a
neuroexcitatory compound. It is structurally related to glutamic acid and
binds to both NMDA and metabotropic glutamate receptors. In the body,
ibotenic acid is partially **decarboxylated to muscimol**, which is the
primary psychoactive metabolite.

### Muscimol

Muscimol is a potent GABA-A receptor agonist. It is structurally
similar to GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) and produces sedative-hypnotic,
dissociative, and hallucinatory effects. Muscimol is approximately 5-10
times more potent than ibotenic acid. Its effects include altered perception,
euphoria or dysphoria, visual distortions, and at high doses, loss of
consciousness.

### Muscarine

Despite the name "muscaria," [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] contains
only trace amounts of muscarine — insufficient to produce significant
parasympathomimetic effects. The common name derives from early beliefs about
its mechanism, later disproven. [[amanita-pantherina]]
contains somewhat higher muscarine levels but still not enough to be the
primary toxic agent.

## Preparation and Detoxification

Traditional Siberian users developed methods to reduce the toxicity and
enhance the psychoactive effects of [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]]:

- **Drying** converts ibotenic acid to muscimol, reducing excitotoxic
  effects and increasing the proportion of the more desirable GABAergic
  compound
- **Boiling** in water leaches out water-soluble toxins (including excess
  ibotenic acid and muscarine), with the broth sometimes consumed
  separately
- **Urine recycling** concentrates muscimol while eliminating much of the
  remaining ibotenic acid and other byproducts

These preparation methods effectively shift the ratio of ibotenic acid to
muscimol, producing a more predictable and tolerable experience.

## Cultural Significance

The use of [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] in Siberia is among the
best-documented indigenous mushroom practices outside of Mexico. The Chukchee
and Koryak peoples considered the mushroom a portal to the spirit world, used
by shamans for divination and healing. Unlike the Mexican mushroom
ceremonies, which are relatively communal and structured, Siberian
[[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] use was often individual and less
ceremonially formalized.

Wasson proposed the controversial theory that Amanita muscaria|A.
muscaria was the identity of the ancient Vedic sacrament **Soma**, though
this hypothesis remains debated. The linguistic and ethnobotanical evidence
is suggestive but inconclusive.

## Toxicology Concerns

All three ibotenic acid Amanita species require careful identification.
While they are not lethal in the manner of the amatoxin-containing species
([[amanita-phalloides-death-cap]], Amanita virosa|A. virosa,
Amanita ocreata|A. ocreata), they can produce significant
gastrointestinal distress, confusion, ataxia, and in severe cases, seizures
or coma. Confusion with deadly amatoxin-containing species is the primary
fatal risk, as the white-gilled, veiled Amanitas share a general
morphological pattern.
