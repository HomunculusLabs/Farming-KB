# Spawn Run and Colonization

## Overview

The spawn run (also called incubation or colonization phase) is the period
between inoculation of a prepared substrate and the moment the substrate is
fully colonized by mushroom mycelium. During this phase, the fungal organism
establishes itself throughout the substrate, digesting nutrients and building
the network that will later produce fruiting bodies. This phase is purely
vegetative -- no mushrooms are formed.

## What Is Spawn?

Spawn is mushroom mycelium that has been grown on a carrier material, serving as
the inoculum for larger substrate masses. Common spawn carriers include:

- **Grain spawn** -- sterilized cereal grains (rye, millet, wheat, sorghum)
  colonized by mycelium. Most common for indoor cultivation. Each grain kernel
  becomes an independent inoculation point, enabling fast, even colonization.
- **Sawdust spawn** -- colonized hardwood sawdust, used primarily in log and
  outdoor cultivation. Slower to colonize but cheaper to produce.
- **Plug spawn** -- wooden dowels colonized with mycelium, used for inoculating
  logs and stumps.

## Inoculation Rate

Inoculation rate (the ratio of spawn to substrate) significantly impacts
colonization speed and contamination resistance:

- **Standard rate**: 5-10% by weight (wet basis)
- **High rate**: 10-20% -- faster colonization, better contamination
  resistance, higher cost
- **Low rate**: 2-5% -- slower, riskier, but reduces spawn costs

For most oyster mushroom cultivation, 10% grain spawn by weight provides a good
balance of speed and economy.

## Colonization Environment

### Temperature

Optimal temperature for the spawn run varies by [[pleurotus-species]]:

| Species | Spawn Run Range | Optimal |
|---------|----------------|---------|
| P. ostreatus | 20-30 C | 24-26 C |
| P. pulmonarius | 25-35 C | 28-30 C |
| P. eryngii | 18-25 C | 20-22 C |
| P. djamor | 25-35 C | 28-30 C |

Higher temperatures speed colonization but also increase the risk of thermophilic
contamination. A moderate temperature that balances speed and safety is
preferred over the absolute maximum.

### Darkness

Mushroom mycelium grows best in complete or near-complete darkness during the
spawn run. Light during colonization can trigger premature pinning, which wastes
energy and reduces final yield. Colonization containers should be opaque or
stored in dark conditions.

### Gas Exchange

Mycelium requires oxygen for respiration during colonization. Fully sealed bags
may stall or die. Spawn bags typically include filter patches (0.5 micron)
that allow gas exchange while preventing contaminant spores from entering.
Tubs may have small holes covered with micropore tape.

### Humidity

Substrate moisture should remain at 60-75% throughout colonization. If the
substrate dries out, mycelium growth slows or stops. Containers should be
closed enough to prevent moisture loss but vented enough for gas exchange.

## Colonization Timeline

Typical colonization times for oyster mushrooms on grain spawn:

- **Quart grain jars**: 10-21 days at optimal temperature
- **5 lb spawn bags**: 14-21 days
- **Straw logs/bags (5-10 lb)**: 14-21 days after inoculation
- **Supplemented sawdust blocks**: 21-35 days

Full colonization is indicated by a solid white mycelial mat throughout the
substrate. The substrate should feel firm and unified when gently squeezed.

## Signs of Healthy Colonization

- Bright white, cottony mycelium spreading from inoculation points
- Uniform growth pattern across the substrate surface
- Earthy, mushroom-like smell
- Gradual consolidation (substrate becoming firm and dense)
- Moderate metabolic heat production (substrate 1-3 C above ambient)

## Contamination During Spawn Run

Contamination is most likely during the spawn run because the substrate is
nutrient-rich and warm. Common contaminants include:

- **Trichoderma** (green mold) -- the most common contaminant; appears as
  green patches that rapidly expand
- **Coprinus** (ink cap mold) -- gray, fast-growing mold on straw substrates
- **Bacterial contamination** -- slimy, foul-smelling patches; often from
  insufficient pasteurization
- **Dactylium** (cobweb mold) -- fine, spider-web-like mycelium overlaying
  the mushroom mycelium

See [[substrate-contamination]] for detailed identification and prevention
strategies.

## Consolidation Period

After the substrate appears fully colonized, a consolidation period of 5-10
days is often recommended. During this time, the mycelium continues to digest
substrate, strengthening the network and improving its ability to produce a
large, even flush of mushrooms. The substrate typically becomes noticeably
firmer and whiter during consolidation.
