## Urban Guerilla Food Growing
Urban guerilla food growing is the practice of cultivating food in unauthorized,
abandoned, or underutilized urban spaces. Rooted in permaculture and DIY
ethics, it reclaims urban land for food production while building community
resilience and ecological awareness. The term combines the militant
connotations of "guerilla" with the practical, peaceful act of growing food.
## Rationale and Philosophy
The movement responds to several interconnected problems:
- Corporate control of seed supplies and the industrial food system that
  genetically alters food to depend on proprietary fertilizers and herbicides
- Monocropped agriculture transported across vast distances in fossil-fuel
  intensive logistics chains
- Suburban sprawl covering prime agricultural soil with development
- An infinite-growth economic model that ignores finite natural resources
- Urban alienation where children in apartment buildings never learn where food
  comes from

### The DIY Ethic
Skills learned in food growing never become outdated. No one gets downsized
or replaced by a machine when they know how to grow food. The practitioner's
daily routine revolves around food: sowing it, tending to it, studying it,
harvesting it, processing it, and eating it. These skills include T-bud grafting
fruit trees, rooting semi-hardwood cuttings, [[comparison-drip-irrigation-vs-passive-irrigation]], biofertilizers, and plant
propagation.
### Starting Where You Are
Urban guerilla gardening can begin at any scale. As Charlie Headington writes:
"You can begin where you are, right now, on whatever scale suits you. You can
initiate systematic or random acts of restoration and evoke the spirit of the
place." The goal is to bridge connections between people of different generations
and cultures, and teach children there is more to life than concrete and fear.
