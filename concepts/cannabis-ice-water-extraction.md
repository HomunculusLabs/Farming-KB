# Cannabis Ice Water Extraction

## Overview

Ice water extraction (also called water hash, Ice-O-Lator hash, ice hash, THC crystal, or [[cervantes-water-hash-bubble-hash]]) separates [[greg-green-trichomes-and-resin-glands]] from cannabis foliage using cold water agitation and progressively smaller mesh filters. The method was popularized by Mila of Amsterdam with her Ice-O-Lator bags, and later refined by Bubble Man who added multiple screen sizes for quality grading. When very pure, hash bubbles and releases volatile resins when exposed to a flame, hence the name "bubble hash."

## Scientific Principle

The process relies on simple physics described as "Sadu Sam's Secret" in [[hashish]]! by Robert Connell Clarke. The key principle is:

- Resin is oil-based and heavier than water
- Cannabis foliage is water-based and lighter than water
- Heavy, oil-soluble resin glands will not dissolve in water and will sink
- Water-soluble material dissolves in liquid
- Foliage is lighter than water and floats to the surface
- Small, immature resin glands also tend to float
- Mature resin glands and dense debris sink to the bottom

Cold water is essential because warm water softens the resin, causing it to stick together in unmanageable lumps. Ice-cold water makes trichomes brittle, and agitation knocks the heads off for collection through sieve screens.

## Equipment Required

- Ice-O-Lator bags with progressively smaller micron silkscreen bottoms
- 20-25 liter (5-gallon) bucket with lid
- Kitchen mixer with paddles (not a chopping blade, which cuts foliage and bruises glands)
- 4.5-11 pounds (2-5 kg) of ice cubes
- Paper towels, dinner plate, plastic card
- Large mixing spoon
- Clean working area (bathroom with shower head works well)

## Process: Ice-O-Lator Method

1. Fill bucket with ice-cold water to six inches below rim
2. Place bags in bucket in proper order, eliminating air bubbles between bags
3. Ensure screens are covered with water before introducing plant matter
4. Add up to 7 ounces (200 gm) of frozen, chopped cannabis (not stems or sharp parts)
5. Add more ice on top of plant material to ensure leaves get wet
6. Fill bucket to within two inches of top, let soak 15 minutes at 41F (5C)
7. Cut holes in bucket lid for mixer shafts, attach paddles
8. Mix on low speed for 15 minutes; move perimeter material to center with spoon
9. Let mix soak 5 more minutes, mix again; repeat until fully stirred (about 1 hour)
10. Let resin settle for 15 minutes
11. Remove first "work bag" containing raw material; rinse screen with water
12. Remove bags one by one, taking care not to drop unscreened material into next bag
13. Remove last bag with finest screen, drain water through sieve
14. Scrape wet hash from screen with plastic card or teaspoon
15. Crumble and spread on screen to dry

The cold water can be reused for up to five batches. After use, water can be poured on garden plants as nutrient-rich irrigation.

## Screen Sizes and Quality Grades

Different screen sizes produce different grades of hash quality:

- 25 micron bag: Often "full melt" quality, physically smallest bag
- 45 micron bag: Nice head hash, consistent and yellowish to white
- 73 micron bag: Full melt all the way
- 66-104 micron bags: Best quality in both quantity and purity
- 120 micron bag: Good bubble hash
- 150-160 micron bags: Best for big-headed indica resin glands
- 190-220 micron bags: Remove large debris and plant matter

Bubble Man's grading system from 25 to 220 microns separates unique sediments, some containing exceptionally pure THC that bubbles when heated.

## Multi-Bag Extraction (15-Bag Method)

Expert hash makers use up to 15 different bags in a two-step process:

1. First run: Wash and separate resin from foliage using 6 screens
2. Second run: Pass remaining hash-laden water through 8 more bags

This produces very clean hash with multiple quality grades. In a test with 100 grams of good leaf, using 15 sieves yielded 11.6 grams total across all grades, with the 104-micron bag producing the highest single yield at 3.1 grams.

## Washing Machine Method

For large-scale production, fill a washing machine with ice-cold water. Place frozen leaf in zipped Ice-O-Lator bags, two at a time for machine balance. Agitate for 10-12 minutes. Resin glands slip through mesh into the water. Drain and filter through additional bags. Every one-kilo bag of leaves yields 30-40 grams of dried resin. In 14 hours, 100 kilos of leaf can be processed into three kilos of quality resin.
