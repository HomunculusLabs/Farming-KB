## Mycorrhizal Mushrooms

Mycorrhizal mushrooms form mutually dependent, beneficial relationships with
the roots of host plants, ranging from trees to grasses. "Myco" means
mushroom while "rhizal" means roots. These mushrooms include some of the
most prized and expensive culinary fungi in the world — Matsutake, Chanterelles,
Boletes, and Truffles — yet they remain among the most difficult to cultivate
artificially. Their dependence on living host plants and complex soil
communities has frustrated [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]].

In both cases, both organisms benefit from the association. The plant
receives enhanced nutrient absorption — particularly phosphorus, nitrogen,
and essential trace elements (copper, zinc) — while the fungus receives
photosynthetically derived carbon compounds from the host.

a controversial, multi-million-dollar business. Chanterelles form
ectomycorrhizal associations with trees in both oak forests of the Midwest
and Douglas fir forests of the West.

Chanterelle cultivation is complicated by their unique interdependence on
soil yeasts. At least three organisms must be cultured simultaneously for
success: the host tree, the mushroom fungus, and specific soil yeasts. A
red soil yeast, *Rhodotorula glutinis*, is crucial in stimulating spore
germination. The timing of introduction of each organism appears critical,
and no one has yet grown Chanterelles to the fruitbody stage under
laboratory conditions.

### Truffles (*Tuber* spp.)

Truffle orchards are well established in France, Spain, and Italy, with the
renowned Périgord black truffle (*Tuber melanosporum*) fetching up to $500
per pound. Only in the past 30 years has tissue culture of Truffle mycelium
become widely practiced, allowing the development of planted truffle
orchards.

However, the success rate of truffle cultivation has been discouraging.
Several American companies have marketed truffle-inoculated trees for
commercial use, and tens of thousands of dollars have been exhausted in
the endeavor. Ten years after planting, Stamets noted knowing of only one
or two successes. Suffice it to say that the only ones to have made money
in the truffle tree industry are those who resold "inoculated" seedlings
to other would-be trufflateurs.

### Boletes

Boletes form mycorrhizal associations with both coniferous and deciduous
trees. The King Bolete (*Boletus edulis*, also known as Porcini) is one of
the most commercially important wild-harvested mushrooms worldwide. Like
other mycorrhizal species, it has defied reliable cultivation despite its
enormous market value.
