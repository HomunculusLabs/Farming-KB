# Protozoa and Nematodes in the [[teaming-with-microbes-soil-food-web-overview]] as
consumers of bacteria and fungi. They are the critical link that converts
microbe-locked nutrients into plant-available forms. Protozoa are single-celled
animals that feed on bacteria and other microbes; nematodes are tiny unsegmented
roundworms that feed on bacteria, fungi, and each other. A teaspoon of good
garden soil contains thousands of protozoa and dozens of nematodes. Both groups
play essential [[teaming-with-microbes-soil-food-web-trophic-structure-and-succession]] regulation.

## Protozoa: The Three [[teaming-with-microbes-protozoa-types-amoeba-flagellate-ciliate]]s** -- The smallest, equipped with one or more whip-like flagella
   for propulsion. They are the most numerous protozoa in soil and the most
   common predators of bacteria.

2. **Amoebae** -- Larger organisms that move by extending pseudopods (false
   feet). They engulf bacteria by surrounding and absorbing them. Amoebae are
   particularly important in soils with larger pore spaces where they can move
   more freely.

3. **Ciliates** -- The largest protozoa, covered in hair-like cilia used for
   locomotion and feeding. They are generally found in compacted or anaerobic
   soils, and their presence in high numbers often indicates soil compaction or
   poor aeration. A healthy, well-aerated soil should have relatively few
   ciliates compared to flagellates and amoebae.

## Protozoa as Bacterial Population Regulators

Protozoa are essential for maintaining bacterial populations in active growth
phase. Without predation pressure, bacteria would consume all available food and
then go dormant -- a less productive state for [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]. This [[gadd-mycelial-network-resilience-grazing-pressure]] maintains

## Nematodes: Overview

Nematodes (roundworms) are among the most numerous multicellular animals on
Earth -- a teaspoon of soil can contain dozens. They are unsegmented, typically
transparent, and most are so small they are barely visible. Despite their
simplicity, nematodes have a complete digestive system (mouth, intestine, anus)
and are classified into functional groups based on their feeding habits.

## Nematode Feeding Groups

1. **Bacterial-feeding nematodes** -- The most common group. They consume
   bacteria using a hollow, needle-like stylet to puncture cells and suck out
   contents. Like protozoa, they excrete excess nitrogen as ammonium. Their
   presence in soil indicates a healthy, functioning bacterial loop.

2. **Fungal-feeding nematodes** -- These feed on fungi by puncturing hyphae
   and drawing out cytoplasm. They play a parallel role to bacterial-feeding
   nematodes, releasing fungal nutrients as ammonium waste. A soil dominated by
   fungal-feeding nematodes indicates a fungal-dominated food web.

3. **Predatory nematodes** -- These eat other nematodes and small organisms.
   They have specialized mouthparts for grasping prey. Predatory nematodes help
   regulate root-feeding nematode populations and keep the soil food web in
   balance.
