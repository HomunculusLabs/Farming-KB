## Overview

Liquid culture (LC) is a nutrient-rich liquid medium inoculated with mushroom mycelium. The mycelium grows throughout the liquid, forming a floating mat of white growth that can be drawn into a syringe and used to inoculate grain jars. Liquid culture offers several advantages over [[query-spore-syringes-vs-liquid-culture]]: faster colonization, lower contamination rates, and the ability to expand a small amount of mycelium into a large number of inoculations. It is an essential skill for cultivators working at any scale beyond casual hobby growing.

## How Liquid Culture Works

Mushroom mycelium grows in liquid by extending hyphal threads throughout the [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]]. In an agitated environment, the mycelium forms small spherical masses called "mycelial balls" or "pellets." These balls are essentially concentrated bundles of living mycelium that, when introduced to a [[brassinosteroid-dung-grain-substrate-fruiting-enhancement]], rapidly expand and colonize the grain. Because you are inoculating with living, actively growing mycelium rather than dormant spores, colonization time is significantly reduced.

## Liquid Culture Recipes

### Honey Liquid Culture (Honey Tek)

The simplest and most accessible LC recipe.

- 500ml distilled water
- 15-20ml raw honey (approximately 1 tablespoon)
- A pinch of gypsum (optional)

Process:
1. Add honey to distilled water in a jar or flask.
2. Heat gently while stirring until honey is fully dissolved.
3. Pour into a suitable culture vessel (see below).
4. Sterilize at 15 PSI for 20-30 minutes.

The 3-4% sugar concentration is critical. Too much sugar creates a hypertonic solution that inhibits [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]]. Too little sugar provides insufficient nutrition.

### Light Malt Extract (LME) Liquid Culture

The preferred recipe for most experienced cultivators. LME provides a more complete nutrient profile than honey.

- 500ml distilled water
- 4g light malt extract (approximately 1 teaspoon)
- A pinch of gypsum (optional)

Process:
1. Dissolve LME in distilled water.
2. Pour into culture vessel.
3. Sterilize at 15 PSI for 20-30 minutes.

### Karo (Corn Syrup) Liquid Culture

An alternative to honey that works similarly.

- 500ml distilled water
- 15-20ml light Karo corn syrup (clear, not dark)

### Sugar-in-the-Raw (Turbinado) Liquid Culture

- 500ml distilled water
- 10-15g turbinado sugar

## Culture Vessels

### Mason Jars with Self-Healing Lids

The most common approach. Use quart or pint wide-mouth jars with:
- A modified lid with a self-healing injection port (silicone RTV)
- A filter (polyfill, synthetic filter disc, or micropore tape) for gas exchange
- The jar should be filled only 50-60% full to allow room for shaking and aeration

### Erlenmeyer Flasks with Air Filters

For cultivators with a [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]], Erlenmeyer flasks fitted with foam stoppers or filtered caps are excellent. The wide neck allows easy agar wedge inoculation.

### Specialty Culture Bags

Pre-sterilized LC bags with injection ports and filters are available from mycology suppliers. Convenient but more expensive than DIY.

## Sterilization

- **Time**: 20-30 minutes at 15 PSI for liquid culture. This is shorter than grain because liquid heats through much faster.
- **Caution with sugar solutions**: Sugar can caramelize at high temperatures, which inhibits mycelial growth. Do not exceed 30 minutes at pressure.
- **Loosen lids before sterilizing**: Gas expansion during heating can crack jars if lids are too tight.
- **Natural pressure release**: Allow the pressure cooker to depressurize naturally. Quick-release can boil the liquid and disrupt the medium.

## Inoculation

### From Agar Wedge

The cleanest and most reliable method:

1. Inside the SAB or flow hood, flame-sterilize a scalpel.
2. Cut a small agar wedge (5mm x 5mm) from a clean, vigorously growing plate.
3. Open the LC jar lid just enough to drop the wedge in.
4. Close immediately.

### From Spore Syringe

Less ideal because spore syringes are never perfectly clean:

1. Flame the needle.
2. Inject 0.5-1 cc of spore solution into the LC jar.
3. Spores will germinate in the liquid and begin forming mycelium.
