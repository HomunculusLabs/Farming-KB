## Drought Gardening

Growing vegetables with little or no irrigation, using wider spacing and techniques that allow plants to access deeper soil moisture. Steve Solomon mastered this approach through research and experimentation, detailed in his earlier book *Waterwise Vegetables*.

## The Core Principle

Our ancestors knew how to grow vegetables without piped water. In most temperate climates, vegetables can be grown with little or no irrigation. The key is putting plants extremely far apart.

## Why It Works

When vegetable plants are widely spaced:
- Each plant has access to a much larger volume of soil moisture
- Root systems can expand without competing with neighboring plants
- Plants develop deeper, more extensive root systems
- They become more drought-tolerant and self-sufficient

## The Moisture Availability Window

Soil holds water between field capacity (fully saturated) and the permanent wilting point (where plants can no longer extract moisture). Solomon defines important moisture thresholds:

- **70 percent of field capacity**: Ideal zone for vegetable growth
- **60 percent of field capacity**: Time to water — when the top foot has dried to this level
- **Below 50 percent**: Vegetables experience moisture stress, even if not visibly wilting

Moisture stress does not need to reach the point of wilting to cause significant damage to yield and quality.

## Watering Strategy When You Must Irrigate

When irrigation is necessary, Solomon recommends:

1. **Water infrequently but deeply**: Top up to field capacity every 2-5 days — more frequently on sandy soil, less often (but applying more) on heavier soils
2. **Replace moisture at the rate it is lost**: Without overwatering, which leaches soil fertility
3. **The basic plan**: Maintain soil moisture above 70 percent of field capacity in the top foot (30 cm) of soil

## Drought Tolerance by Spacing

Solomon's research showed that his trial plots (with well-separated plants) did not need nearly as much water as his intensive vegetable garden. The spacing system directly impacts water needs:

- Closer spacing requires more frequent irrigation
- Wider spacing allows plants to access soil moisture from a larger area
- Traditional Native American corn gardening used four-foot centers — one seed per hill with the adage "one for the worm, one for the crow, one to rot, and one to grow"

## Foliar Feeding as Water Conservation

If you are so short of water that finding a few hundred gallons a week for fertigation is not possible, foliar feeding may be the next best choice. Key points:

- Foliar feeding means spraying diluted nutrient solutions directly onto plant leaves
- Fish emulsion and kelp tea are among the best organics for this purpose
- Kelp tea contains phytamins and a full range of trace mineral nutrients
- Even a few gallons per week mixed in a sprayer can make a significant difference
- Half-strength Coca Cola has been discovered to be an effective foliar spray (contains phosphoric acid and sugars that help plants)

## Determining When to Water

Two practical methods:

1. **Soil ball test**: Dig 5-6 inches down and squeeze a handful of soil. If it's damp and sticks together solidly, moisture is above 70 percent. If it barely holds together when squeezed hard, moisture is around 60 percent (time to water). If it won't form a ball at all, the soil is below 50 percent and vegetables are stressed.

2. **Estimate moisture loss**: Regardless of soil texture, all soils lose water at about the same rate through evapotranspiration, which varies with season and vegetation cover but not with soil type.

## Sprinkler Efficiency

Solomon notes that most gardeners grossly overwater:
- Consumer-grade sprinklers spread 2-4 inches (5-10 cm) per hour
- Running one for an hour can cause massive leaching of soluble nutrients below the root zone
- Test any sprinkler with water gauges (tin cans) to determine its application rate
- Use the "sparkle test" from John Jeavons: water with a fan nozzle until the bed surface sparkles; when the shine lasts 1-10 seconds, enough water has been applied
