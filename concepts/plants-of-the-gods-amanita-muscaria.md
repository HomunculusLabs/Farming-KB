# Amanita Muscaria (Fly Agaric)

## Overview

[[amanita-muscaria]], commonly known as the Fly Agaric, is perhaps man's
oldest hallucinogen. This iconic red-capped mushroom with white spots
has been identified with Soma, the ancient Indian god-narcotic, and
plays a central role in shamanic traditions across the Northern
Hemisphere. In *Plants of the Gods*, Schultes, Hofmann, and Ratsch
provide a comprehensive account of its botany, chemistry, and cultural
significance spanning Siberia, Mesoamerica, and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]].

## Botanical Description

Amanita muscaria is a beautiful [[duggar-mushroom-growing-history-and-methods]] in thin forests,
usually under birches, firs, and young pines. It may attain a height of
8-9 inches (20-23 cm). The somewhat viscid, ovate, hemispheric cap
measures 3-8 inches (8-20 cm) when mature. Three varieties exist: one
with a blood-red cap and white warts found in the [[datura-metel-old-world-ethnobotany]] and
northwestern [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]]; a yellow or orange type with yellowish warts
common in eastern and central North America; and a white variety found
in Idaho. The cylindrical stem has a bulbous base, is white, and bears a
conspicuous cream-white ring. The gills vary from white to cream or
lemon yellow.

## Cultural Use in Siberia

The Koryak and other Siberian tribes developed elaborate ritualistic
practices around Amanita muscaria. Dried in the sun, the mushrooms were
ingested alone or as an extract in water, reindeer milk, or the juice of
sweet plants (Vaccinium or Epilobium). Women sometimes rolled the
mushroom into a moistened pellet for men to swallow.

The ceremonial use developed a unique practice of urine-drinking, as
tribesmen discovered that the psychoactive principles pass through the
body unmetabolized or as still-active metabolites — most unusual for
hallucinogenic plant compounds. An early account reported that poorer
tribesmen would wait outside huts to collect the urine of the wealthy
who could afford mushrooms, drinking it greedily for its retained
virtue.

A Koryak legend tells of the culture hero Vahiyinin (Existence) who
created wapaq (Amanita muscaria) by spitting onto the earth, creating
the mushroom as a gift directly from the divine. He pleaded: "O wapaq,
grow forever on earth," and commanded his people to learn what wapaq
could teach them.

## Intoxication and Effects

A traveler among the Koryak described the intoxication: "Fly Agaric
produces intoxication, hallucinations, and delirium. Light forms are
accompanied by animation and spontaneity of movements. Many shamans,
previous to their seances, eat Fly Agaric to get into ecstatic states."

Under strong intoxication, senses become deranged; objects appear very
large or very small (macropsia/micropsia); hallucinations, spontaneous
movements, and convulsions occur. Attacks of great animation alternate
with deep depression. The person may sit quietly, then suddenly begin
gesticulating convulsively, conversing with imagined persons, singing
and dancing. Euphoria, colored visions, and occasionally religious
fervor and deep sleep may occur.

## Chemistry

The active principle was once believed to be muscarine (isolated by
Schmiedeberg and Koppe), but this has been proven erroneous. Eugster
in Switzerland and Takemoto in Japan isolated [[amanita-muscaria-muscimol-ibotenic-acid-pharmacology]] and the
alkaloid muscimole as responsible for the psychotropic effects. The
mushroom is usually taken dried; the drying process induces the chemical
transformation of [[amanita-muscaria-and-ibotenic-acid-containing-fungi]] to muscimole, the most active
constituent. Muscazone is also present.
