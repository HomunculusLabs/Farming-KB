## Temperature Management Through Ventilation

In most indoor grows, the exhaust fan is the primary tool for temperature control. When lights turn on, heat builds rapidly. The exhaust fan removes hot air and brings in cooler fresh air, maintaining temperatures in the optimal range of 70-85 degrees Fahrenheit (21-29 degrees Celsius).

For growers using CO2 supplementation, ventilation timing becomes more complex. CO2 is only needed during lights-on periods, and ventilation must be coordinated with CO2 injection. A common approach is to run the exhaust on a timer, cycling on for several minutes to remove heat and humidity, then off for a period to allow CO2 to build up.

Air-cooled lighting systems, where air is ducted through a sealed reflector housing and exhausted separately from the grow room air, can significantly reduce the cooling burden on the main ventilation system. Water-cooled lighting systems are another option for heat management.
