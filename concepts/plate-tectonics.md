## Plate Tectonics

## Overview

Plate tectonics is the modern framework for understanding Earth's active outer shell.
It explains continents, ocean basins, earthquakes, volcanoes, mountain belts, and seafloor spreading.
The theory holds that the lithosphere is divided into moving plates.
These plates include both crust and the uppermost rigid mantle.

They move over the weaker asthenosphere on geological timescales.
Motion is usually measured in centimeters per year.
That pace is slow by human standards but powerful over millions of years.
Plate tectonics unifies many observations that once seemed unrelated.
It connects the shapes of continents with fossil distributions and rock belts.
It links deep ocean trenches with volcanic arcs and earthquake zones.

It also explains why Earth continually recycles crustal material.
The theory is central to geology, geophysics, and earth system science.
