# PF-TEK Mycelium Colonization Observation and Timing

## Overview

Mycelium colonization is the critical growth phase in the PF-TEK cultivation
method, occurring between [[mushroom-spore-inoculation-technique]] and the onset of fruiting. During
this period, fungal mycelium spreads through the brown rice flour and
[[pf-tek-brown-rice-vermiculite-substrate]], consuming nutrients and establishing the network that
will eventually produce fruiting bodies. Psilocybe Fanaticus (PF) documented
detailed observation protocols and timing expectations for this phase, providing
cultivators with benchmarks for healthy versus problematic colonization.

## Colonization Timeline

The complete process from spore inoculation to harvest follows a predictable
schedule under optimal conditions:

### Spore Germination (Days 1–7)

After inoculation, spores require approximately one week to germinate at 70°F
(21°C). During this period, the cultivator observes the substrate jars for the
first visible signs of white, threadlike mycelial growth. Germination occurs
when viable spores encounter the moist, nutritious substrate and begin
producing hyphae. The incubation temperature should remain stable; fluctuations
can delay or prevent germination entirely.

### Active Colonization (Weeks 2–3)

Once germination begins, the mycelium spreads radially from each inoculation
point. Complete colonization of the substrate cake typically requires 2 to 3
weeks. The mycelium appears as bright white, cottony growth that progressively
consumes the brown substrate. Healthy colonization is characterized by:

- Even, rhizomorphic growth patterns
- Consistent white coloration without yellowing or discoloration
- Progressive coverage from inoculation points outward
- No visible colored contaminants (green, black, pink, or pastel growths)

### Consolidation and Pre-Fruiting (Weeks 3–4)

After the substrate appears fully colonized, the mycelium benefits from an
additional consolidation period. The transition from complete colonization to
the start of the fruiting cycle typically occurs within 2 weeks. During this
phase, the mycelium strengthens its hold on the substrate and begins
accumulating the cellular resources needed for fruiting body production.

### Fruiting Cycle (Weeks 4–6)

The fruiting cycle itself lasts approximately 2 weeks. After the initial flush
of mushrooms, the mycelium cake begins to turn blue, indicating exhaustion.
Secondary fruit bodies may form if the cake is thoroughly cleaned after the
initial flush, but these are typically sparse and small.

## Total Process Duration

From spore inoculation to first harvest, the entire process takes approximately
4 to 6 weeks under optimal conditions. This timeline assumes consistent
temperatures, proper [[bulk-substrate-preparation]], viable spores, and a sterile
[[inoculation-technique-and-alcohol-flaming]]. Any deviation from optimal conditions will extend this
timeline.

## Incubation Temperature

PF recommends an incubation temperature of approximately 70°F (21°C) for
spore germination and early colonization. While the mycelium grows faster at
warmer temperatures (up to the upper 80s°F), excessive heat can promote
contaminant growth and inhibit fruiting. A stable room-temperature environment
is generally sufficient. PF observed that fruiting occurs well even at 60°F,
though growth slows considerably at lower temperatures.

## Visual Observation Protocol

Cultivators should inspect colonization jars regularly but minimize handling.

1. **First growth**: Visible white mycelium at inoculation points within 3–7
