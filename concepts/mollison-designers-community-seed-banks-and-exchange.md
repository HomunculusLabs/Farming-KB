## The Importance of Genetic Diversity

Agricultural biodiversity has declined dramatically over the past century.
The United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization estimates that 75
percent of crop genetic diversity has been lost since the early twentieth
century. This loss represents a narrowing of the genetic base upon which
food production depends, increasing vulnerability to pests, diseases, and
climate variability. Mollison saw this erosion as a direct threat to food
security and permaculture's capacity to design resilient systems.
