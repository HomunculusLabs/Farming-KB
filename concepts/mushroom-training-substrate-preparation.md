# Mushroom Substrate Preparation — Sawdust and Rice Straw Bags with Pasteurization

Complete substrate bag production workflow from the FAO training manual: preparing sawdust-based or fermented rice straw substrate, bagging at 800-1000g per bag, and pasteurization using country-style drums, commercial chambers, or solar heating. Substrate is the "food" mixture that provides all nutrients mushrooms need to develop.

## Overview

Substrate is the material used to grow mushrooms — a mixture of all ingredients or "food" necessary for mushroom development. While sawdust is the most common and easy-to-use base material, rice straw offers advantages in yield and quality where it is locally available. After bags are filled, they must be pasteurized to eliminate competing organisms before inoculation.

Making substrate bags generally requires two hands, but each step can be adapted with the help of feet for trainees with limited hand use.

## Substrate Base Materials

### Sawdust

Sawdust is the most common substrate base in the FAO training program. It is relatively easy to source and process. Cover the top of the sawdust pile with rice sacks to maintain humidity during storage.

### Rice Straw

In Asia, because of intense rice cultivation, rice straw can be used as an alternative substrate. Advantages over sawdust:
- Lower cost (readily available in most rural areas)
- Generally generates higher yield
- Better quality mushrooms — both texture and taste are improved

Disadvantage: rice straw requires more preparation work, including fermentation for 9-12 days before use.

## Sawdust Substrate Recipe

### Standard Formulation

| Ingredient | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Sawdust | 100 kg |
| Rice bran | 5 kg |
| Calcium sulfate | 2 kg |
| Calcium carbonate | 1 kg |
| Magnesium sulfate | 0.2 kg |
| Sugar | 0-1 kg |

### Recipe Notes

- This recipe serves as a reference and can be modified through research and development
- Additives can include rice flour, sticky rice flour, corn flour, cassava peels, cotton waste, soybean residue, and other nutritious agricultural waste
- In cool climates: additives or complementing materials up to 20% are possible
- In hot climates: do not use more than 7.5% additives
- If using rice straw instead of sawdust, the recipe must be modified (see rice straw fermentation below)

## Rice Straw Fermentation Method

For areas where rice straw is abundant, fermented straw substrate produces higher yields and better quality mushrooms than sawdust.

### Fermentation Procedure

1. Put straw through a grinder to reduce particle size
2. Soak 100 kg paddy straw with water and mix with 1-2% urea by weight
3. Ferment for 3 days
4. Turn over the pile; mix with 2% lime; ferment 3 more days
5. Turn over the pile again; mix with 0.2% magnesium sulfate; ferment 3 more days
6. The last turn-over makes the straw ready to use as composted substrate

### Readiness Check

- Check moisture: should be 65-75%
- Check for urea smell: if there is NO urea smell and moisture is 65-75%, substrate is ready for packing
- If there IS still a urea smell, ferment further until the smell disappears completely
- Pack fermented straw in size 8" x 12" PP (polypropylene) bags

## Bagging Procedure

### Materials

- Plastic bags (polypropylene, 7" x 12.5")
- Plastic necks (approximately 4 cm diameter)
- Cotton plugs
- Cover filters (square piece of filter paper)
- Rubber bands
- Plastic caps

### Steps

1. Weigh all substrate components using a scale
2. Mix all ingredients thoroughly — use a mechanical mixer or manually with shovels or paddles
3. Add water to maintain moisture content between 65-75%
4. Ensure all ingredients are well mixed with no lumps
5. Sit on floor; fill plastic bags with substrate using a small shovel
6. Compact substrate by hitting bags with an empty bottle or by hand — alternatively use a compacting machine
7. Place plastic ring on bag
8. Pull the top of bag through the plastic neck
9. Fold down over the plastic ring
10. Tie with rubber band
11. Introduce a stick with a pointed head through the plastic neck to make a hole almost to the bottom of the bag — DO NOT touch the bottom of the bag
12. Check weight: each bag should weigh between 800-1000 grams
13. Prepare plastic caps with cotton
14. Close bags with plastic caps
15. Place bags on iron racks (9 bags per rack for commercial chamber)
16. Transfer racks to pasteurization chamber

## Pasteurization Methods

Pasteurization kills competing organisms in the substrate without sterilizing it completely. Three methods are described in the FAO manual, ranging from low-cost country setups to commercial equipment.

### Method 1: Country-Style Drum Pasteurization

This is the lowest-cost method, using readily available materials.

#### Equipment

- Steaming pot — 200-liter drums
- Firewood
- Iron or bamboo screen — round shape, 5 inches high, to fit inside drum
- Old rice bags (double layer)
- Plastic sheeting

#### Procedure

1. Clean the steaming pot; add approximately 4 inches of water
2. Place the iron or wooden screen so it sits about 1 inch higher than the water level
3. Place bags in the pasteurization system until full (80-100 bags)
4. Cover everything with double layers of old rice bags
5. Place plastic over rice bags and close tightly
6. Light the fire; maintain temperature constant for 3-4 hours from the time temperature reaches 90-100C (the moment steam starts coming out continuously)
7. Important: temperature must remain constant and there should always be water in the drum
8. When completed, remove the firewood from the stove
9. Let cool for approximately 20 minutes; remove cover bags and let cool further
10. Transfer bags to the inoculation area

### Method 2: Commercial Chamber Pasteurization

For larger-scale operations with dedicated equipment.

#### Procedure

1. Clean the water reservoir at the bottom of the pasteurization chamber by releasing the valve; dry with cloth
2. Refill water up to the marked level (some chambers produce steam internally; others receive steam from a boiler)
3. Transfer iron racks into the chamber one by one until full
4. Close door securely; verify thermometer reading
5. Light burner and maintain at 98-100C for 3-4 hours; release all air from the chamber when starting to keep accurate time
6. For boiler-generated steam: open valve to send steam to chamber; make sure all air is released
