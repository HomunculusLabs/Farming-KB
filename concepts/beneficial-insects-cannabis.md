# Beneficial Insects for Cannabis Pest Control

Biological control using predators and parasites is a cornerstone of [[integrated-pest-management]] in cannabis cultivation. Beneficial insects provide ongoing pest suppression without chemical residues on flowers, making them especially valuable during flowering when spray applications risk affecting bud quality.

## Predators vs Parasites

Understanding the distinction between predators and parasites is essential for effective biological control:

### Predators

By definition, a predator must eat more than one victim before reaching adulthood. Predators actively hunt and consume multiple prey organisms. Types include:

- **Chewing predators**: Have chewing mouthparts and eat their prey whole. Examples include ladybugs (ladybird beetles) and praying mantises.
- **Piercing-sucking predators**: Have piercing-sucking mouthparts and suck fluids from their prey's body. Examples include lacewing larvae.

Key behavior: Predators prefer to be surrounded by abundant prey. When pest populations start to diminish, predators may move on to find a larger infestation. They never truly eradicate pests entirely. This means predators work best for **preventative control** but are slower to stop an established infestation.

### Parasites (Parasitoids)

Parasites consume a single individual host before reaching adulthood. Adult parasitoids typically place a single egg into many hosts. The egg hatches into larvae that eat the host insect from the inside out, saving the vital organs for last. Most often, the larvae pupate inside the host's body and emerge as adults.

Key behavior: Unlike predators, parasites hunt until the prey is almost eliminated. They are more thorough at reducing pest populations to near-zero levels.

### Reproductive Advantage

Both predators and parasites outbreed their victims, reproducing faster than pests are able to keep up with. The rate at which they keep infestations in check is directly proportional to the number of beneficials released -- more predators and parasites means faster control.

## Ladybugs (Hippodamia convergens)

Ladybugs are among the most recognizable and widely available biological control agents.

- **Target pests**: [[aphids-cannabis]] (primary target), mealybugs, [[spider-mites-cannabis]], soft-bodied insects
- **Application rate**: Release approximately 50 ladybugs per plant
- **Mouthparts**: Chewing -- they eat prey whole
- **Availability**: Easily obtained at many retail nurseries during summer months
- **Shipped**: Special delivery, often arriving after daily mail -- pick up promptly to avoid heat death in mailbox

### Indoor Considerations

Ladybugs have a significant drawback in indoor HID gardens: they are attracted to the bright grow lights. If 500 ladybugs are released on Monday, by Friday only a few die-hards will remain -- the rest will have flown into the hot lamp and perished. Within one to two weeks, all ladybugs will fall victim to the lamp, requiring frequent replenishment.

**Mitigation**: Release flying predators when lights are off (dark period). They will live longer and spend more time hunting rather than flying toward the light source.

## Lacewings (Chrysoperla carnea)

Lacewings, Chrysoperla species, are considered the most effective and available predators for [[aphids-cannabis]].

- **Target pests**: [[aphids-cannabis]] (primary), [[thrips-cannabis]], [[spider-mites-cannabis]], mealybugs, whitefly larvae, other soft-bodied insects
- **Application rate**: Release 1 to 20 lacewings per plant, depending on infestation severity
- **Release timing**: As soon as aphids or target pests appear; repeat releases every month
- **Mouthparts**: Piercing-sucking -- larvae suck fluids from prey
- **Life cycle**: Eggs take a few days to hatch into larvae that aggressively exterminate prey

Lacewing larvae are voracious predators often called "aphid lions" for their effectiveness. They are among the most reliable beneficial insects for cannabis pest control.

## Predatory Mites

Predatory mites are the primary biological control for [[spider-mites-cannabis]], the most common and destructive cannabis pest. Several species are commercially available:

### Phytoseiulus persimilis

One of the most common and effective spider mite predators. A specialist predator that feeds almost exclusively on spider mites.

### Neoseiulus (Amblyseius) californicus

A generalist predator that tolerates higher temperatures and lower humidity than P. persimilis. Can survive on alternative food sources when spider mite populations are low, allowing it to persist in the garden as a preventative measure.

### Mesoseiulus (Phytoseiulus) longipes

Another effective specialist predator of spider mites, commonly used alongside N. californicus.

### Other Predatory Mites

For [[thrips-cannabis]] control, several predatory mite species are effective:

- Amblyseius cucumeris
- Amblyseius barkeri
- Neoseiulus cucumeris
- Iphiseius degenerans
- Neoseiulus barkeri
- Euseius hibisci

**Application**: Release predatory mites before infestations grow out of hand. Apply water as a mist or spray when predatory mites are present -- the extra humidity impairs pest mite lifecycles while promoting predatory mite health.

## Encarsia formosa for Whiteflies

Encarsia formosa is a tiny parasitic wasp (about 0.125 inch / 3 mm long -- smaller than the whitefly itself) and the most effective biological control for [[whiteflies-cannabis]].

- **Target**: Whiteflies exclusively -- the wasps do not sting people
- **Mechanism**: The female wasp lays an egg inside the whitefly larva. The egg hatches and the parasitoid larva eats the whitefly larva alive from the inside out. Death is slow but thorough.
- **Application rate**: Release two or more parasites per plant
- **Release timing**: As soon as the first whitefly is detected
- **Repeat interval**: Every two to four weeks throughout the life of the plants
- **Note**: Because it is a parasite rather than a predator, it takes longer to control whitefly populations, but it hunts more persistently until the prey is nearly eliminated

The fungus Verticillium lecanii (trade name Mycotal) is also effective against whiteflies and can be used alongside Encarsia releases.

## Release Timing and Best Practices

### When to Release

- Introduce beneficials as early as possible -- preventative releases are more effective than responsive ones
- Predators and parasites survive best in gardens that are not sterilized between crops
- Gardens with perpetual harvests are ideal for maintaining beneficial insect populations

### Shipping and Handling

- Beneficials are shipped special delivery and may arrive separately from regular mail
- Pick up immediately -- do not let them sit in a hot mailbox (temperatures can reach 120F / 49C)
- Most predators are very small and must be introduced to each plant individually
- This takes time and patience

### Compatibility with Sprays

- Stop all toxic chemical sprays at least two weeks before introducing predators and parasites
- [[permaculture-pyrethrum-daisy]] (pyrethrum) and insecticidal soaps can be applied up to a few days before release, provided any residue is washed off with fresh water
- **Do not spray after releasing predators and parasites**
- See [[neem-oil-cannabis]] for cautions about neem oil toxicity to beneficial insects

### Environmental Requirements

Predators have very specific climatic requirements for temperature and humidity. Before purchasing, confirm with the supplier:

1. Latin name of the predator (to ensure correct identification)
2. Specific pests attacked
3. Complete life cycle information
4. Preferred climate (temperature and humidity range)
5. Application rate and mode of application

Maintain the environmental conditions that favor your beneficial insects for best results.
