### Long-Brewing Compost Tea (LBCT)
LBCT starts aerobic but passes through an anaerobic phase before potentially returning
to aerobic conditions by the end of the brewing process. The intermediate anaerobic
phase means that beneficial fungi, protozoa, and nematodes are lost, and the tea
may contain metabolites from anaerobic bacteria. LBCT occupies a middle ground but
shares many of the limitations of truly anaerobic methods.

### Not-Aerobic Compost Tea (NACT)
NACT is produced under predominantly anaerobic conditions, in which anaerobic bacteria
grow for most of the brewing period. This produces some materials that can be useful
for preventing growth of particular organisms through toxicity, but it also risks
producing plant-toxic compounds and human pathogens (Clostridium, E. coli, Salmonella).
NACT is the most problematic category and should be distinguished from true compost tea.

## Critical Distinctions from Non-Tea Products
Several related products are frequently confused with compost tea but are fundamentally
different in their composition and effects:

- **Compost Extract**: Made by extracting compost into water without a brewing period.
  The organisms have not had an opportunity to wake up and multiply, so beneficial
  organism numbers are much lower than in properly brewed tea.

- **Compost Leachate**: A passive process where water percolates through compost.
  Leachates primarily contain soluble nutrients and some enzymes and humic substances,
  but lack the diverse microbial communities of brewed tea.
