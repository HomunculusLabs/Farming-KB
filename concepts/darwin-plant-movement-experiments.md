### The Five Treatment Experiment

The key experiment tested five conditions:

| Treatment | Description | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A | Untreated control | Bent toward light |
| B | Tip pruned off | No bending |
| C | Tip covered with opaque cap | No bending |
| D | Tip covered with clear glass cap | Bent toward light |
| E | Middle covered with opaque tube | Bent toward light |

This elegantly demonstrated that:
- The shoot tip is necessary for phototropism (B)
- The tip needs light access, not just physical presence (C vs D)
- The bending region does not need to directly perceive light (E)
- A signal must travel from the light-sensing tip to the bending region

### The Transmissible Signal

Darwin wrote that when the tip is illuminated, "it transmits an influence to the
lower part, causing it to bend." He proposed that this influence moves down the
stem at a measurable speed. This was confirmed by his experiments showing that
if a cut is made between the tip and the bending region, the bending response
is blocked — proving the signal must physically traverse the intervening tissue.

The signal was later identified as the plant hormone **auxin** (indole-3-acetic
acid), which redistributes asymmetrically in response to directional light. Auxin
