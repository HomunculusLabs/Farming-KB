# Oyster Mushroom Nutritional Composition

## Overview

Oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.) are recognized as nutritionally dense foods
offering significant protein content, essential vitamins and minerals, and
bioactive compounds with medicinal properties. Their nutritional profile makes
them particularly valuable in developing countries where protein malnutrition
is prevalent and access to animal protein sources may be limited. As Quimio
(2002) notes, "mushrooms can mean cash for the poor and a new source of
nutrition" in regions where conventional protein sources are scarce or
expensive.

The nutritional value of oyster mushrooms extends beyond basic macronutrients
to include a range of bioactive compounds. Pleurotus species are good sources
of beta-1,3/1,6-glucans (called pleuran), mevinolin and related compounds,
and other molecules with immunomodulatory, cholesterol-lowering, and
antioxidant properties. These attributes position oyster mushrooms at the
intersection of food and medicine.

## Key Concepts

### Macronutrient Composition

Oyster mushrooms provide a balanced nutritional profile per fresh weight:

- **Protein**: 2.9% fresh (26-35% dry weight), comparable to corn, milk, and
  legumes, though lower than meat, fish, and eggs. The protein content is
  superior to most fruits and vegetables except beans and peas.
- **Carbohydrates**: 5.66% fresh weight, primarily as complex polysaccharides
  including dietary fiber and beta-glucans rather than simple sugars
- **Fat**: 1.79% fresh weight, with a favorable fatty acid profile that
  contributes to the low-calorie nature of mushroom-based foods

The overall C/N ratio requirements for [[oyster-mushroom-substrate-preparation]]
reflect their lower nitrogen needs compared to button mushrooms (optimal C/N
of 17 for Agaricus), making them more efficient converters of carbon-rich
agricultural waste substrates into protein-rich food.

### Amino Acid Profile

Mushrooms contain all essential amino acids as well as commonly occurring
non-essential amino acids and amides. Notably, lysine, which is deficient in
most cereal grains, is present in significant quantities in mushrooms. This
makes mushroom protein a valuable complement to cereal-based diets, which
predominate in many developing countries. The combination of cereal staples
with mushroom supplementation can significantly improve the protein quality
of meals.

### Vitamin Content

Oyster mushrooms rank high in vitamin content, with notable contributions of:

- **Vitamin C**: Present in significant amounts, contributing antioxidant
  protection
- **Riboflavin (Vitamin B2)**: At 0.22 mg per 100g fresh weight
- **Thiamin (Vitamin B1)**: At 0.20 mg per 100g fresh weight
- **Cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B12)**: Notably present in mushrooms despite
  being typically found only in animal products, making mushrooms an
  important B12 source for vegetarian diets
- **Niacin (Vitamin B3)**: At 7.72 mg per 100g fresh weight, nearly
  equivalent to levels found in pork or beef, considered among the richest
  dietary sources of this vitamin

### Mineral Composition

Oyster mushrooms are rich in several essential minerals:

- **Calcium**: 3.14 mg per 100g, supporting bone health
- **Iron**: 3.40 mg per 100g, an appreciable amount that together with
  phosphorus can provide a good proportion of recommended daily dietary needs
- **Phosphorus**: Present alongside iron for synergistic mineral nutrition
- **Potassium and sodium**: Available in good amounts, with low sodium
  content making mushrooms ideal for persons with heart and kidney ailments
- **Folic acid**: Present in quantities that help enrich the bloodstream
  and prevent deficiencies

### Bioactive Compounds

Beyond basic nutrition, oyster mushrooms contain several bioactive molecules:

- **Pleuran (beta-1,3/1,6-glucan)**: Stimulates the immune system to fight
  abnormal cells and boosts resistance against damaging effects of
  chemotherapy and radiation therapy
- **Mevinolin and related compounds**: Inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, an enzyme
  used in cholesterol biosynthesis. Consumption of oyster mushrooms can
  measurably lower cholesterol levels in the body
- **Antioxidant compounds**: Various phenolic and other antioxidant molecules
  provide cellular protection against oxidative stress

### Medicinal Applications

The medicinal properties of oyster mushrooms have been extensively studied.
Quimio (2002) reports that "for the past 20 years, interest in the medicinal
aspects of mushrooms has greatly been stimulated by the large number of
scientific studies conducted on mushrooms." Vita-Glucan tablets and elixirs
have been formulated from Pleurotus extracts, marketed for immune system
support. The broad-spectrum health benefits position oyster mushrooms as
functional foods bridging nutrition and preventive medicine.

## Applications

### Complementary Nutrition in Cereal-Based Diets

In regions where diets are dominated by rice, wheat, or maize, oyster
mushrooms provide essential amino acids, particularly lysine, that are
limiting in cereal proteins. Even landless peasants can grow mushrooms at
home, making this an accessible strategy for improving household nutrition
without requiring land ownership.

### Vegetarian and Vegan Protein Sources

The complete amino acid profile, including B12, makes oyster mushrooms
particularly valuable for vegetarian and vegan diets. Unlike soy and yeast,
which require processing before consumption, mushrooms can be eaten fresh or
cooked directly, offering a palatable whole-food protein source.

### Therapeutic and Functional Food Products

Extracts and preparations from oyster mushrooms are used in therapeutic
products including immune-modulating supplements, cholesterol management
formulations, and anti-tumor adjuvant therapies. The convergence of
nutritional and medicinal properties supports the growing functional foods
market.
