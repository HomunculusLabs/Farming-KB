# Cryptococcus Neoformans Pathogenesis

[[alpha-1-3-glucan-fungal-pathogen-cell-walls]] that causes life-threatening
meningoencephalitis, particularly in immunocompromised individuals. It is one of the most
significant fungal killers worldwide, responsible for an estimated 200,000+ deaths annually,
primarily among people living with HIV/AIDS.

## Overview

C. neoformans is an encapsulated yeast found worldwide in soil contaminated with pigeon droppings
and other bird excreta. It is a basidiomycete (related to mushrooms) and has both a yeast form
and a sexual cycle that produces basidiospores. The species complex includes two varieties:
C. neoformans var. neoformans and C. neoformans var. grubii.

## Life Cycle

The fungus has two forms:
- **Yeast form:** The pathogenic form found in infected tissues — single, round cells with a
  prominent polysaccharide capsule
- **Sexual form:** Filamentous (mating) form that produces basidiospores on basidia — the
  infectious form thought to be inhaled from the environment

The sexual cycle involves mating between two mating types (a and alpha). Remarkably, the alpha
mating type predominates in both environmental and clinical isolates, suggesting it has a
selective advantage.

## Route of Infection

1. **Inhalation:** Basidiospores or desiccated yeast cells are inhaled from the environment
2. **Pulmonary phase:** The fungus establishes in the lungs — may be asymptomatic or cause
   mild pneumonia
3. **Latency:** The fungus can remain dormant in the lungs for extended periods
4. **Dissemination:** In immunocompromised hosts, the fungus crosses the blood-brain barrier
5. **CNS infection:** Causes meningoencephalitis — inflammation of the brain and meninges

## Virulence Factors

### Polysaccharide Capsule
- The most important virulence factor — a thick coat of glucuronoxylomannan (GXM)
- Protects against phagocytosis by macrophages
- Can be shed into host tissues, causing immunosuppression
- Capsule size increases during infection

### Melanin Production
- C. neoformans produces melanin from catecholamine substrates (L-DOPA, dopamine)
- Melanin protects against oxidative damage from immune cells
- Contributes to resistance against antifungal drugs
- Melanized cells are more virulent in animal models

### Thermo-tolerance
- Can grow at 37°C (human body temperature), which many environmental fungi cannot
- This is a key prerequisite for pathogenicity

### Phospholipase and Urease
- Enzymes that facilitate tissue invasion and blood-brain barrier crossing

## Clinical Disease

### Cryptococcal Meningitis
- Most common clinical presentation
- Symptoms: headache, fever, neck stiffness, altered mental status
- Often insidious onset over weeks
- Without treatment, mortality approaches 100%
- Even with treatment, mortality is 10-30% in resource-rich settings, much higher elsewhere

### Pulmonary Cryptococcosis
- Can occur in immunocompetent individuals
- May present as pneumonia, lung nodules, or asymptomatic infection
