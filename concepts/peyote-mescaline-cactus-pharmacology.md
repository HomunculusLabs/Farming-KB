# Peyote and Mescaline Cactus Pharmacology

## Overview

[[peyote-ceremony]] (Lophophora williamsii) is a small, spineless cactus native to a narrow
strip of desert along the Texas-Mexico border, extending deeply into Mexico's
Chihuahuan Desert. It is the most culturally significant hallucinogenic plant in
[[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] history, serving as the central sacrament of the [[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] (NAC) for over a century. Peyote contains more than 60 identified
alkaloids, with [[pihkal-mescaline]] (β-3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) as its principal
psychoactive constituent at approximately 1.5% by dry weight.

Beyond peyote, mescaline is also found in [[trichocereus-pachanoi]] (San Pedro)
and [[trichocereus-peruvianus]] (Peruvian torch), widely cultivated ornamental
plants available from garden shops throughout the United States.

## Botany of Peyote

Peyote is a small, blue-green, spineless cactus growing partially embedded in
soil. Its morphology consists of two main parts:

- **Crown (button)**: The visible above-ground portion, typically 2-5 cm in
  diameter, containing the highest concentration of alkaloids
- **Tuberous root**: A large, carrot-like root extending below the soil surface

The crown is harvested by making a clean cut at the base, leaving the root
intact. The root eventually sprouts several new crowns for reharvest. The slow
growth rate — taking many years to reach maturity — has raised conservation
concerns given NAC ceremonial demands and illegal harvesting. Peyote's
restricted geographic range (southern Texas through the Chihuahuan Desert) makes
it vulnerable to habitat destruction.

## Mescaline Chemistry

Mescaline (C11H17NO3) is a phenethylamine alkaloid first isolated from peyote in
1897 by Arthur Heffter. Chemical properties:

- **Class**: Phenethylamine
- **Molecular formula**: C11H17NO3
- **Molecular weight**: 211.26 g/mol
- **Content in peyote**: ~1.5% by dry weight
- **Effective dose**: ~3.75 mg/kg body weight
- **Mechanism**: Primarily 5-HT2A serotonin receptor agonism

Mescaline is structurally related to [[pihkal-dob-doi-dom-amphetamine-series-comparison]], dopamine, and
[[blesching-cannabis-epinephrine-norepinephrine-endocannabinoid-system]]. Its three methoxy groups at positions 3, 4, and 5 on the
phenyl ring are essential for psychoactive activity. The 5-HT2A mechanism is
shared with [[lsd]], [[psilocybin]], and other classical hallucinogens, explaining
the cross-tolerance observed between these substances.

## Dosage and Intoxication Profile

A typical intoxicating dose is approximately 3.75 mg/kg body weight:

- **Onset**: 1-2 hours after ingestion
- **Peak effects**: 2-4 hours post-ingestion
- **Duration**: Effects wear off over 4-6 hours following peak
- **Total experience**: Approximately 6-10 hours

Peyote has a characteristically bitter, acrid taste inducing nausea and vomiting,
especially in first-time users. This emetic effect is sometimes considered part of
ceremonial purification. Consumption methods include eating peeled fresh buttons,
dried whole buttons, dried ground powder, or steeping into warm tea. Pickled
peyote consumption is uncommon since a botulism case was reported (Hashimoto et
al., 1998).

## Native American Church Ceremonies

The [[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] is the largest pan-tribal religious organization
among Native Americans, with approximately 300,000 members in the United States
and Canada. [[metzner-peyote-ceremonies-native-american-church-and-huichol-traditions]] represent the central sacramental practice and have
been legally protected under federal law for Native American members.
