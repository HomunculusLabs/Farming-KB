# Sepp Holzer"s Permaculture Methods

Sepp Holzer farms the Krameterhof at 1,000-1,500m altitude in Lungau, Salzburg, Austria — a climate most consider unsuitable for diverse agriculture. Over 40+ years he developed methods producing abundant food without chemicals, irrigation, or imported fertility. His approach predates the term "permaculture" but embodies its principles completely.

## Krameterhof Farm Overview

- **Location**: Southern slope of Schwarzenberg mountain, Lungau, Salzburg, Austria
- **Altitude**: 1,000-1,500m above sea level (3,300-4,900ft)
- **Size**: 45 hectares (110 acres), doubled from original 25 hectares
- **Climate**: Continental — cold winters, warm summers, high UV at altitude
- **Key features**: Network of terraces, raised beds, ponds (70+), waterways, fruit trees, livestock
- **No chemicals**: Zero synthetic fertilizer, pesticides, or herbicides
- **No irrigation**: All farming relies on natural water retention and microclimate design

## Raised Beds (Hugelkultur)

See [[hugelkultur]] for comprehensive details. Holzer"s approach:
- Incorporate whole tree trunks, branches, and shrubs into beds (not wood chips)
- Dig 1-1.5m deep, 1.5-2m wide trenches; place woody material, cover with soil
- Bulky material rots slowly (10+ years), releasing steady nutrients
- Prevents soil acidification that chipped material causes
- Creates microclimates: warm tops, moist bottoms
- Beds angled perpendicular to slopes to distribute water evenly

## Terracing and Slope Management

- **Contour terraces** slow runoff, prevent erosion, create level planting surfaces
- **Stone walls** absorb heat during day, radiate at night — extending growing season
- **Terrace width**: Adjusted to slope gradient; steeper = narrower terraces
- **Stabilization**: Plant deep-rooted species (comfrey, fruit trees) on risers
- **Humus storage ditches** between terraces catch organic matter and moisture

## Water Landscapes

Holzer considers water management the most important aspect of his system:
- **70+ ponds** of varying sizes across the farm
- **Water gardens** with aquatic plants, fish, and wildlife habitat
- **Pond placement**: Use existing wet areas; never drain wetlands — cultivate them instead
- **Functions**: Microclimate moderation (frost protection), wildlife habitat, aquaculture, irrigation reservoirs, aesthetic beauty
- **No drainage**: "Wherever water is found is where it should be made use of"
- **Stream restoration**: Opposes straightened streams; natural meandering prevents downstream flooding
- **Building**: Excavator-dug ponds with shallow edges for wetland plants, deeper centers for fish

## Animal Integration

### Pigs
- Used for tilling and fertilizing raised beds after harvest
- Rooting behavior aerates soil and incorporates organic matter
- Limited numbers, timed access — too many pigs cause damage
- Heritage breeds preferred for hardiness

### Cattle
- Kept on steep, marginal land unsuitable for crops
- Holzer"s "wild cattle" — robust breeds that thrive on mountain forage
- Manure contributes to terrace and raised bed fertility
- Moved frequently to prevent overgrazing

### Chickens
- Free-range in orchards for pest control (codling moth larvae, other insects)
- Manure adds nitrogen to orchard floor
- Mobile coops rotated through garden areas

### Fish
- Stocked in ponds for aquaculture and mosquito control
- Trout, carp, and other cold-water species at altitude
- Fed naturally from pond ecosystem, not commercial feed

## Mushroom Cultivation on Logs

- Inoculate fallen logs and stumps with edible mushroom spawn
- Shiitake, oyster, and other species cultivated on hardwood logs
- Logs placed in shady, moist locations among raised beds and terraces
- Simple method: Drill holes, insert spawn plugs, seal with wax
- Provides additional food production from "waste" wood

See [[mushroom-cultivation-methods]] for detailed techniques.

## Fruit Tree Varieties for Cold Climates

Holzer selects for hardiness at 1,100m+ altitude:
- **Stone fruits**: Sour cherries, plums (Hauszwetschke), apricots (hardy Russian varieties)
- **Pome fruits**: Apples (Bohnapfel, Krummstiel), pears (Hardy varieties)
- **Berries**: Blackberries, raspberries, currants, gooseberries, elderberries
- **Nuts**: Walnuts (thin-shelled mountain varieties), hazelnuts
- **Unusual**: Sea buckthorn, cornelian cherry, Siberian pea shrub
- Key principle: Use local heritage varieties adapted to the specific microclimate

## Anti-Grazing Methods

- **Stone walls and terraces** double as livestock barriers
- **Hedge fences** of thorny species (hawthorn, blackberry, wild rose)
- **Mixed plantings** around vulnerable crops — deer dislike aromatic herbs
- **Strategic placement** — put most valuable crops where animals cannot reach
- **Pigs as protectors** — their presence deters deer in some areas

## Landscape Reading

Holzer's approach to site assessment:
- **Observe existing vegetation** — indicator plants reveal soil conditions and moisture
- **Track sun and shade** through seasons; map microclimates
- **Study water flow** — where it collects, where it runs off, how it moves in rain
- **Feel the soil** — texture, moisture, temperature at different depths
- **Note wind patterns** — direction, speed, seasonal changes
- **Identify frost pockets** — cold air drains to low points
- **Read animal behavior** — wildlife reveals the best and worst areas

## Natural Succession Observation

- Observe how nature colonizes bare ground, then design to accelerate and guide succession
- Pioneer species (weeds, grasses) prepare soil for shrubs, then trees
- Use pioneer plants intentionally to prepare sites for food forests
- Don't fight succession — harness it
- Failed plantings indicate the wrong stage of succession, not wrong species

## No-Irrigation Farming Approach

Holzer achieves productive farming without irrigation through:
- Deep organic soils that act as water reservoirs (raised beds, humus-rich terraces)
- Ponds that raise the local water table and create humid microclimates
- Mulching and ground covers that prevent evaporation
- Windbreaks that reduce desiccation
- Selecting drought-adapted varieties and rootstocks
- Planting in the right microclimates (north slopes for moisture-loving plants)

## Root Cellars

- Built into hillside or underground for natural temperature regulation
- Stone construction maintains 2-4°C (36-39°F) year-round
- Used for storing root vegetables, apples, preserved foods
- Simple design: excavated chamber with stone walls, earth-sheltered roof, ventilation pipe
- Positioned near the house for easy access (Zone 1-2)
