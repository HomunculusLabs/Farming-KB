## Toxicity Assessment Methods

A suite of bioassays is available for monitoring ecotoxicological
changes during fungal bioremediation. These tests span multiple levels
of biological organization and provide complementary information about
the overall toxicity status of treated environments:

### Microbial Toxicity Tests

- **Microtox assay**: A standardized bioluminescence test using the
  marine bacterium Vibrio fischeri (formerly Photobacterium
  phosphoreum). Reduction in bioluminescence output indicates
  toxicity. This rapid, sensitive test provides a general measure of
  acute toxicity and is widely used for screening environmental
  samples.
- **Bacterial growth inhibition tests**: Direct measurement of
  bacterial growth rates in the presence of treated samples, using
  organisms such as [[bacillus-megaterium]] or Pseudomonas putida.
  These tests are particularly relevant for assessing whether
  [[cannabis-seed-germination-methods]] tests and root elongation
  assays using sensitive plant species (e.g., lettuce, cress, or
  locally relevant species) provide rapid assessment of phytotoxic
  effects. These tests are particularly important for evaluating
  the suitability of treated soils for revegetation.

### Genotoxicity Tests

- **Mutagenicity assays**: Tests such as the Ames Salmonella
  reverse mutation assay detect mutagenic potential of degradation
  intermediates. This is critical because some [[singh-fungal-transformation-metals-metalloids]]
  products may be genotoxic even when they are not acutely toxic.
- **Chromosomal aberration tests**: These assess the potential for
  degradation products to cause DNA damage in higher organisms,
  providing information about long-term ecological risks.

## Monitoring Strategies for Fungal Bioremediation

Effective ecotoxicological monitoring during fungal bioremediation
requires a systematic approach:

- **Baseline characterization**: Measurement of initial toxicity
  levels before treatment begins, establishing reference points
  against which treatment progress can be evaluated.
- **Time-series sampling**: Regular collection of samples throughout
  the treatment period to track toxicity changes over time. This
  is critical for detecting transient toxicity peaks that may
  occur during intermediate metabolite accumulation.
- **Multiple assay application**: Use of several complementary
  bioassays covering different trophic levels and toxicological
  endpoints to provide a comprehensive picture of ecotoxicological
  status.
- **Chemical-analytical correlation**: Combining bioassay results
  with chemical analysis (HPLC, GC-MS) of specific pollutant
  concentrations and transformation products to identify which
  compounds are responsible for observed toxicity changes.
