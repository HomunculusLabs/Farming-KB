# Salvia Divinorum and Salvinorin-A Pharmacology

## Overview

[[salvia-divinorum]] is a perennial herb in the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to
the Sierra Mazateca region of Oaxaca, Mexico. It is the only known natural source
of [[salvinorin-b]], a neoclerodane diterpene that is the first documented
naturally occurring non-nitrogenous κ-opioid receptor agonist. Unlike most
classical hallucinogens such as [[psilocybin]], [[lsd]], and [[pihkal-mescaline]], which
act primarily as 5-HT2A serotonin [[cannabinoid-receptor-agonists-antagonists-synthetic-reference-blesching]], salvinorin-A operates through
an entirely different pharmacological pathway, making it pharmacologically unique
among naturally occurring psychoactive substances.

The plant has been used for centuries by the Mazatec indigenous people in
traditional shamanic and divinatory practices. Since the late 1990s, Salvia
divinorum has become widely available through Internet commerce and smoke shops,
leading to increased recreational use outside any ceremonial context. The DEA has
monitored it as a potentially emerging drug of abuse, though it remained
unscheduled at the federal level at the time of original publication.

## Taxonomy and Botany

Salvia divinorum belongs to the genus Salvia within the Lamiaceae family.
It is a sprawling perennial herb growing over a meter in height with large
leaves as the primary site of salvinorin-A accumulation. The plant rarely
produces viable seeds and propagates almost exclusively through stem cuttings
rooting readily in water or moist soil. This clonal reproduction means virtually
all cultivated plants are genetically derived from a small number of original
collections.

The Lamiaceae family contains many popular ornamental plants, and researchers
have noted that other members may harbor undiscovered psychoactive properties.
[[coleus-blumei]] and [[coleus-blumei-and-coleus-pumilus-potential-psychoactivity]] have been reported as occasional
substitutes used by Mazatec Indians, though only Salvia divinorum has been
confirmed to contain salvinorin-A. If Salvia divinorum were scheduled, the
possibility of a substitute plant among common ornamentals could create
significant regulatory challenges.

## Salvinorin-A Chemistry

Salvinorin-A (C23H28O8) is a trans-neoclerodane diterpene with a molecular
weight of 432.47 g/mol. Its chemical structure is entirely distinct from the
tryptamine-based hallucinogens (such as [[psilocybin]] and [[dmt]]) and the
phenethylamine-based hallucinogens (such as [[pihkal-mescaline]]). Salvinorin-A contains
no nitrogen atoms, which is highly unusual for a potent psychoactive compound.

Key chemical properties of salvinorin-A:

- **Class**: Neoclerodane diterpene
- **Molecular formula**: C23H28O8
- **Active dose (smoked)**: 200-500 micrograms
- **Potency**: Among the most potent naturally occurring hallucinogens
- **Receptor selectivity**: Highly selective κ-opioid receptor agonist

The potency is remarkable. At 200-500 μg when smoked, it is active at doses
comparable to or lower than LSD, despite a completely different mechanism. This
extreme potency has raised safety concerns, particularly with concentrated
extracts containing many times the salvinorin-A content of raw leaf material.

## Pharmacological Mechanism

Salvinorin-A is a highly selective agonist at the κ-opioid receptor (KOR), a
groundbreaking discovery reported by Roth et al. (2002) as the first naturally
occurring non-nitrogenous opioid receptor agonist. The KOR is a G-protein coupled
receptor that produces dissociative effects, analgesia, and perceptual
alterations distinct from those produced by μ-opioid receptor agonists like
morphine.

The κ-opioid receptor mechanism explains several characteristic features:

- **Dissociative effects**: Users frequently report detachment from reality and
  their own body, consistent with KOR activation
- **Lack of classical psychedelic effects**: Unlike 5-HT2A agonists, salvinorin-A
  does not typically produce vivid geometric visuals or emotional amplification
- **[[savory-short-duration-grazing-and-cell-systems]]**: Smoked onset within seconds, peak effects lasting 5-15 min
- **No reinforcing properties**: KOR agonists are generally dysphoric with no
  significant abuse potential, unlike μ-opioid agonists

## Traditional Mazatec Use

The Mazatec people of Oaxaca, Mexico, have used Salvia divinorum in shamanic
and healing ceremonies for centuries. The plant is known locally as "Ska María
Pastora" (Leaves of Mary the Shepherdess). Traditional consumption methods:

1. **Fresh leaf chewing**: Curanderos chew quids of 6-26 fresh leaves, holding
   macerated material in the buccal cavity for sublingual absorption
2. **Leaf juice drinking**: Fresh leaves are crushed and the juice collected,
   sometimes mixed with water
3. **Water-based infusions**: Leaves are steeped in water (less common)

Traditional use occurs within a structured ceremonial context, often in darkness,
accompanied by prayer and chanting. The Mazatec use Salvia divinorum primarily
for divination, seeking guidance on health matters, locating lost objects, and
spiritual communication.

## Modern Recreational Use

Since the late 1990s, the Internet has been the primary vehicle for dissemination,
with dried leaves, fresh plants, and concentrated extracts sold through online
vendors and smoke shops. Modern users typically smoke the dried leaves or extracts
using pipes or water pipes.

### Smoked Administration

When smoked, salvinorin-A produces effects dramatically different from oral use:

- **Onset**: Within 30-60 seconds of inhalation
- **Peak effects**: 1-5 minutes after inhalation
- **Duration**: 5-15 minutes total, with residual effects up to 30 minutes
- **Active dose**: 200-500 μg (approximately 0.5-1 g of dried leaf)

### Concentrated Extracts

Commercial extracts range from 5x to 80x potency, concentrating salvinorin-A
and allowing much smaller amounts to produce strong effects, raising safety
concerns about inadvertent large-dose consumption.

## Dosage and Effects

| Route | Dose (salvinorin-A) | Onset | Peak | Duration |
|-------|-------------------|-------|------|----------|
| Smoked (leaf) | 200-500 μg | 30-60s | 1-5 min | 5-15 min |
| Smoked (extract) | Variable | 30-60s | 1-5 min | 5-15 min |
| Oral (chewed quid) | Variable | 5-10 min | 20-40 min | Up to 1 hr |

Subjective effects include: complete [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]], perception of alternate
realities, synesthesia, uncontrollable laughter, amnesia for the experience, and
a sense of merging with objects or environments.

At the federal level, Salvia divinorum was not scheduled by the DEA but was
listed as a "drug of concern," with multiple states enacting their own
restrictions. The plant has [[fukuoka-low-toxicity-pesticide-deception]] with no known lethal dose. Primary risks
are behavioral: accidental injury from dissociation, driving danger, and
psychological distress. The dysphoric nature of KOR agonism means there is
virtually no addiction potential.
