# Synthetic Tryptamine Methods

The synthesis of substituted tryptamines documented throughout TIHKAL follows a set of recurring methodologies that [[indole]] is reacted with [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-formation-oxalyl-chloride-indole]] in anhydrous benzene or toluene under inert atmosphere. The reaction is typically stirred at room temperature or gently heated, and the product precipitates as a yellow-orange solid. This intermediate is sensitive to moisture and must be used promptly.

**Step 2: Amine condensation to the glyoxylamide**
The glyoxyl chloride is treated with the desired secondary amine (dimethylamine, diethylamine, diisopropylamine, etc.) either as a gas bubbled through the solution or as an aqueous/alcoholic solution. The product is the substituted indol-3-ylglyoxylamide, typically crystallized and characterized before proceeding.

**Step 3: LAH reduction to the [[tryptamine]]**
The glyoxylamide is reduced with lithium aluminum hydride (LAH) in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran (THF). The reaction mixture is held at reflux, then carefully quenched with water, dilute acid, and finally made basic to liberate the free base. The product is extracted into an organic solvent, dried, and the solvent removed.

This three-step method is remarkably versatile. By varying the indole [[shirota-4-hydroxyindole-acetyl-protection-psilocin-synthesis-starting-material]] (4-hydroxyindole, 5-methoxyindole, 5-methylthioindole, etc.) and the amine (dimethylamine, diethylamine, methylisopropylamine, etc.), the entire landscape of simple N,N-disubstituted tryptamines becomes accessible through a single synthetic logic.

## Key Examples from TIHKAL

### DMT (Compound #17)
Tryptamine is itself converted to the N,N-dimethyl derivative through the glyoxylamide method. The starting material is unsubstituted indole, condensed with dimethylamine. Yield from indole through the three steps is approximately 50-60%.

### 5-MeO-DMT (Compound #38)
5-Methoxyindole serves as the starting material. The three-step sequence yields the product in approximately 78% yield in the final LAH reduction step. The overall route from 5-methoxyindole is efficient and well-documented.

### 5-MeS-DMT (Compound #46)
5-Methylthiotryptamine is methylated using methyl iodide and sodium bicarbonate in refluxing methanol (72 hours), yielding the methiodide salt. This is then dealkylated using 1,4-diazobicyclo[2.2.2]octane in refluxing DMF, followed by extraction and distillation.

### MIPT (Compound #47)
Two synthetic routes are documented: (1) From tryptamine via N-benzyloxycarbonyl protection, LAH reduction to NMT, then reductive alkylation with acetone over Pd/C under hydrogen; (2) From NIPT via methylation with methyl iodide. The first route yields 56% from the protected intermediate.

## The Reductive Alkylation Method

For compounds where one of the amine substituents is larger (isopropyl, propyl, butyl), reductive alkylation offers an alternative pathway:

1. The primary or monoalkylated tryptamine is dissolved in an alcoholic solvent
2. An aldehyde or ketone corresponding to the desired substituent is added (acetone for isopropyl, propionaldehyde for propyl)
3. The mixture is hydrogenated over a palladium catalyst (typically 10% Pd/C)
4. The product is isolated by filtration, solvent removal, and crystallization or distillation

This method is particularly valuable because it allows the synthesis of asymmetrically substituted tryptamines (different groups on each side of the amine nitrogen) that would be difficult to access through the glyoxylamide method alone.

## The N-Formamide Method

Primary tryptamines can be N-methylated through formylation followed by LAH reduction:

1. Tryptamine is treated with formic acid (or ethyl formate) to yield N-formyltryptamine
2. Reduction with LAH yields N-methyltryptamine (NMT)
3. Further alkylation of NMT yields the desired N-alkyl-N-methyl compound

# Shulgin's Tryptamine Research Philosophy

Alexander "Sasha" Shulgin's approach to the scientific investigation of psychoactive compounds, as expressed throughout TIHKAL, constitutes a distinctive research philosophy that blends rigorous chemical methodology with first-person phenomenological investigation. TIHKAL and its companion volume PIHKAL together document the most systematic exploration of the relationship between molecular structure and [[dosage-unpredictability-psilocybin-subjective-experience]] ever undertaken.

## First-Person Science

Shulgin's most controversial methodological commitment was his insistence on first-person experimentation. Every compound in TIHKAL was tested by Shulgin himself, his wife Ann, or trusted collaborators. The [[qualitative-comments-methodology-pihkal]] section of each entry records these first-person experiences in the experiential language of the subjects rather than reducing them to clinical rating scales. Shulgin argued that the primary data of psychopharmacology is subjective experience and that this data cannot be properly gathered at second hand.

This approach was explicitly at odds with the prevailing biomedical paradigm, which Shulgin characterized as so fearful of psychoactive compounds that it refused to gather the very data (subjective reports) that would make the field scientifically meaningful.
