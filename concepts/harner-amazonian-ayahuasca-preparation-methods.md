# Amazonian [[banisteriopsis-caapi]]. More recently, harmaline and d-tetrahydroharmine
have also been found in B. caapi. Additionally, Banisteriopsis rusbyana,
sometimes mixed with other Banisteriopsis species, contains significant
amounts of the fast-acting hallucinogenic alkaloid N,N-dimethyltryptamine
(DMT).

The four alkaloids known to be present in various Banisteriopsis drinks
(harmine, harmaline, d-tetrahydroharmine, and DMT) are structurally
related indole derivatives, related to mescaline (found in peyote),
psilocybin (found in Mexican mushrooms), and LSD. The beta-carboline
alkaloids (harmine and harmaline) serve as monoamine oxidase
inhibitors, preventing the breakdown of DMT in the digestive tract and
enabling its psychoactivity when taken orally.

## Regional Variation in Additive Plants

Typically, only one other plant is added to the brew, though the
identity of this additive varies by region and tribe:

- **Rio Negro region (Brazil):** A twining plant of the genus
  Haemadictyon, now called Prestonia, was reported by Richard Spruce
  (who originally made the botanical identification of Banisteriopsis
  in 1852). The Indians considered it essential to the drink's success.

- **Rio Putumayo area (eastern Colombia):** Among the Cofan, Inga, and
  Sione Indians, the second plant was simply a second species of
  Banisteriopsis. Garcia-Barriga reported that two additional plants
  were added: Altenanthera and an unidentified species.
