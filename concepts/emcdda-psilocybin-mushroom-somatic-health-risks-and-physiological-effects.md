## EMCDDA Psilocybin Mushroom Somatic Health Risks and Physiological Effects (2006)

## Overview

The EMCDDA's 2006 case study on hallucinogenic mushrooms examined both the
psychological and somatic (physical) health risks associated with their use.
While the psychological effects received greater attention in both research and
media coverage, the somatic health risks represented a distinct category of
concern with specific clinical implications for emergency medical response and
harm reduction messaging.

## Acute Physiological Effects

Psilocybin-containing mushrooms produce a range of acute physiological
effects that accompany their primary psychoactive action. These somatic
effects are generally transient and resolve without medical intervention,
but they can be distressing for users, particularly those who are
inexperienced or who consume unexpectedly potent specimens.

The case study noted that the management of acute mushroom intoxication
was primarily supportive. For psilocybin-containing mushrooms, treatment
focused on reassurance, monitoring of vital signs, and management of
agitation or anxiety with benzodiazepines when necessary. For cases of
toxic mushroom misidentification, more intensive medical intervention
including gastrointestinal decontamination and monitoring for hepatic
toxicity might be required.

## Risk Assessment Context

The EMCDDA placed the somatic health risks of psilocybin mushrooms in the
context of overall drug harm assessment. While the acute toxicity of
psilocybin was considered low compared to many other recreational drugs,
the unpredictable potency of [[cotter-mushroom-products-and-value-added-applications]] the risk of
misidentification represented genuine health concerns that justified
public health monitoring and risk reduction efforts. The case study
concluded that accurate information about product identification, dosing,
and potential somatic effects was essential for reducing harm.
