## Cottage Food Laws

Cottage food laws allow home-based production and sale of certain non-potentially hazardous foods
without commercial kitchen requirements.

### Typically Allowed
Baked goods, jams/jellies, honey, dried herbs, granola, popcorn, nuts, vinegar, dried fruit,
chocolates/candies, dry baking mixes.

### Typically Prohibited
Meat, dairy, eggs (for sale), canned low-acid foods (pressure canned), juices, sprouts, pet food.

### Common Restrictions
- Annual sales cap (ranges from $15,000 to $75,000+ depending on state)
- Must label with: ingredients, allergens, producer name/address, "Made in a home kitchen"
- Sales limited to farmers markets, direct to consumer, online within state
- No interstate sales
- Some states require food handler's card
- **Best resource**: Forrager.com — updated cottage food law comparison by state

See also [[food-preservation-canning]].

## Raw Milk Laws

Raw milk regulation varies significantly by state:

- **Legal for retail sale**: California, Connecticut, Maine, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Washington (laws change frequently — verify)
- **Herdshare/cowshare agreements**: Many states allow this workaround — buy a "share" of an animal and receive milk as a dividend
- **"Pet food" only**: Some states allow raw milk sales labeled "not for human consumption"
- **Completely prohibited**: Nevada, New Jersey, and several others ban all raw milk sales
- **Federal**: FDA prohibits interstate transport of raw milk for human consumption (21 CFR 1240.61)

See also [[dairy-and-cheesemaking]] and [[goat-keeping]].

## Egg Sales

- **FDA**: Producers with >3,000 laying hens must register under the Egg Safety Rule
- **USDA grading**: Not required for small producers selling directly, but some markets require it
- **State licensing**: Most states exempt producers with <500 birds from egg handling licenses
- **Labeling**: Varies by state — typically producer name/address, grade/size (if claimed), pack date
- **Candling**: Some states require eggs to be candled before sale

## Farmers Market Permits

- Each market has its own vendor application and fees ($25–$200/season typical)
- Many require proof of liability insurance ($1M general aggregate standard)
- Some require product liability insurance for prepared foods
- State health department may require separate permits for certain products
- Some markets require vendors to grow everything they sell (no reselling)

## Organic Certification

- **USDA NOP**: Required to label products "organic"
- **Exemption**: Farms grossing <$5,000/year from organic sales can use "organic" but not the USDA seal
- **Cost**: $500–$5,000/year depending on certifier and operation size
- **Transition period**: 3 years of compliant management before land can be certified
- **Record-keeping**: Extensive — all inputs, practices, and sales must be documented

## Business Structure and Taxes

### Choosing a Structure

| Structure | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|-----------|------|------|----------|
| **Sole proprietorship** | Simple, no filing, Schedule F | No liability protection | Small homesteads, starting out |
| **LLC** | Liability protection, pass-through tax | Filing fee ($50–$500), annual reports | Selling products, hiring, hosting |
| **S-Corporation** | Tax advantages, liability protection | Complex, payroll requirements | Profitable operations |
| **Cooperative** | Shared resources, collective marketing | Complex governance, shared liability | Groups of homesteaders |

### Farm Tax Considerations

- **Schedule F** (Form 1040): Report farm income and expenses. Available to anyone operating a farm for profit
- **Hobby farm rule**: If no profit in 3 of 5 consecutive years, IRS may reclassify (deductions limited to income)
- **Tax deductions**: Seeds, feed, equipment, fencing, soil amendments, tools, insurance, mileage, utilities (business portion)
- **Section 179**: Deduct full cost of qualifying equipment in year of purchase, up to annual limits

### Agricultural Tax Exemptions

- **Use value assessment**: Agricultural land taxed at agricultural value rather than fair market — can reduce property taxes by 50–90%. Requires proof of agricultural use
- **Farm plates/vehicle registration**: Reduced fees for farm vehicles
- **Sales tax exemptions**: Many states exempt agricultural purchases from sales tax
- **Agricultural district/zone**: Additional protections and tax benefits in some states

## Insurance

### Types to Consider

- **Farm liability** ($1–2M): Protects against visitor injuries, product liability, animal damage. **Essential if selling products.** Cost: $300–$800/year
- **Property insurance**: Dwelling, outbuildings, equipment, stored crops. Check if farm structures are covered under homeowner's or need farm policy
- **Crop insurance**: USDA RMA offers federal programs. Some subsidized. Coverage for vegetables, fruits, grains, livestock
- **Product liability**: Covers claims from food products sold. Some farm liability policies include this
- **Workers' compensation**: Required in most states with employees (even part-time). Some states exempt small farms

### Practical Tips

- Document everything: inventory with photos, receipts, farm records
- Review policy annually — homesteads evolve quickly
- Umbrella policy ($1–2M, $150–$300/year) provides additional liability coverage

## Land Access and Conservation

### Buying vs. Leasing

- **Buying**: Full control, eligible for tax benefits and conservation programs, requires capital
- **Leasing**: Lower entry cost, flexible, no equity build. Typical: $25–$100/acre/year
- **Lease essentials**: Written contract, term length, permitted uses, maintenance, insurance, right of first refusal

### Conservation Programs

- **CRP (Conservation Reserve Program)**: USDA pays to remove sensitive land from production. 10–15 year contracts, annual rental + cost-share. Competitive enrollment
- **EQIP (Environmental Quality Incentives Program)**: Financial/technical assistance for conservation practices (rotational grazing, cover crops, high tunnels, irrigation efficiency). Up to 75% cost-share. Good fit for regenerative homesteads
- **Conservation easements**: Permanent development restrictions in exchange for tax benefits. Land stays private but cannot be subdivided/developed
- **State programs**: Many states have Agricultural Preservation Restriction (APR), Agricultural Districts, Farmland Protection programs
