# Protected Cultivation — Row Covers and Low Tunnels

## The Double-Layer Principle

The technical foundation of Coleman's winter-harvest system is the combination of inner and outer protective layers. A standard unheated greenhouse provides the outer layer; floating row cover material over the crops creates a "twice-tempered climate" within.

### Temperature Performance
- When outdoor temperature drops to -15°F (-26°C), temperature under the inner layer of the cold house drops only to 15–18°F (-10°C to -8°C) on average
- Double coverage moves the covered area approximately three USDA zones to the "south"
- The double coverage also increases relative humidity, offering additional freeze protection

### Historical Origins
The concept of an inner layer inside an unheated greenhouse was pioneered in the 1950s by Dr. Emery Myers Emmert (1900–62) at the University of Kentucky. Emmert is acknowledged as the father of plastic greenhouses in America, and his work with row covers, [[jadam-plastic-mulch-problems]], and inner layers in unheated winter houses provided the models for today's high tunnels and low tunnels.

## The Inner Layer: Floating Row Covers

### Material
Any lightweight floating row cover that allows light, air, and moisture to pass through is suitable. Coleman uses standard spun-bonded polypropylene row-[[coleman-row-cover-fabric-management]].

### Support System
- Flat-topped wire wickets, 12 inches (30 cm) above the soil
- Made from 76-inch-long #9 wire
- Flat top is 30 inches wide (same as beds), each leg 23 inches long
- Wickets spaced every 4 feet (120 cm) along bed length
- Wickets don't block access paths between beds

### Installation
- Covers applied just before weather gets cold enough to freeze inside the greenhouse
- For large houses (30 × 96 ft): interior covers are 20 ft wide × 50 ft long, covering one quadrant
- For 48-foot houses: two pieces each 15 ft wide
- Fabric pulled taut and clipped to end wickets with clothespins to prevent sagging from condensation weight

### Critical Detail
Fabric must NOT touch the plants. Occasional frost damage occurs where fabric droops down and freezes to leaves. No damage occurs when fabric is properly supported above the canopy.

### Easy Handling Advantage
Since there is no wind inside the greenhouse, there is no need to bury or weigh down edges. Even large pieces can be removed and replaced easily for harvest without worrying about wind gusts.

## Row Covers vs. [[comparison-greenhouse-vs-cold-frame]]s

Coleman originally used glass cold frames as the inner layer. Trials showed:
- Row covers provide slightly less climatic protection than cold frames
- However, row covers are self-venting (no manual ventilation needed on sunny days), lighter, easier to place/remove, and much less expensive
- For commercial production, the efficiency advantages of row covers decisively outweigh the slight temperature difference

## Aluminized Night Curtains (Not Recommended)

Trials compared row covers alone vs. row covers with aluminized cloth (which reflects 100% of long-wave radiation). The aluminized cloth averaged 3°F warmer nighttime minimums but:
- No detectable difference in crop quality
- Very high material cost
- Required twice-daily spreading and removing (especially difficult when frozen to row cover beneath)
- Not worth adding to the system

The natural moisture film that condenses on floating row covers at night is nearly opaque to long-wave radiation and provides a reasonably reflective surface — effectively doing the same job as the aluminized cloth for free.

## [[coleman-quick-hoops-construction]] (Low Tunnels)

Quick hoops are an inexpensive field-scale alternative to full greenhouses:
- Cost approximately 5% of what a greenhouse would cost for the same area
- Can be disassembled and moved to cover different crops
- Have completely replaced curved wire wickets for supporting fabric covers over field crops
- Enable northern growers to explore late-fall planting of spring crops
- Recorded temperatures up to 55°F inside double-covered quick hoops on sunny February days

### Considerations for Quick Hoop Planting
- How early can fall-seeded crops be planted without triggering premature bolting in spring?
- At what size/stage are seedlings hardiest for surviving winter?
- What crops can be sown just before ground freezes to winter-over as seeds and germinate extra early in spring?

## Why Protection Works

The protected microclimate is successful principally because it:
1. Protects against wind (think wind-chill and desiccating effect of cold dry winds)
2. Protects against fluctuating wet-dry, snow-ice conditions of outdoor winter

In this microclimate, a few degrees of temperature one way or the other does not appear to be the crucial determinant of survival for most crops.
