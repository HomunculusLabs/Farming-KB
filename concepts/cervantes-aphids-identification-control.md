# Aphids on Cannabis - Identification and Control

## Overview

Aphids, also called plant lice, are about the size of a pinhead
and easy to spot with the naked eye, though a 10X magnifying
glass aids positive identification. Aphids are found in all
climates and represent one of the most common [[query-how-to-set-up-an-indoor-cannabis-grow-on-a-budget]]
pests. Their rapid reproduction and ability to vector viruses
make them a serious threat to any garden.

## Identification

Normally grayish to black, aphids can be green to pink. In any
color, aphids attack cannabis plants. Most aphids have no wings,
but winged forms are about four times the size of their bodies.
Aphids give birth mainly to live female larvae without mating
(parthenogenesis), and can produce 3 to 100 hungry larvae every
day. Each female reproduces between 40 and 100 offspring that
start reproducing soon after birth.

Aphids are most common indoors when they are plentiful outdoors.
Install yellow [[cervantes-sticky-traps-pest-monitoring]] near the base of several plants and
near the tops of others to monitor invasions of winged aphids,
which are often the first to enter the garden.

## The Ant-Aphid Relationship

As they feed, aphids exude sticky honeydew that attracts ants.
Ants like honeydew so much that they "farm" aphids, moving them
to uninfected plants to maintain their food supply. Look for
columns of ants marching around plants, and you will find aphids.
Any aphid control program must also address ants if they are
present.

## Damage Symptoms

Aphids suck life-giving sap from foliage, causing leaves to wilt
and yellow. When infestation mounts, sticky honeydew excreted by
aphids becomes visible on leaves and growing surfaces. Aphids
prefer to attack weak, stressed plants. Some species prefer
succulent new growth, while others like older foliage or even
flower buds. Look for them under leaves, huddled around branch
nodes, and growing tips.

Aphids are dangerous because they transport (vector) bacteria,
fungi, and viruses. Aphids vector more viruses than any other
insect source. Destructive cervantes sooty mold honeydew management also grows on honeydew deposits.

## Manual and Physical Control

Manual removal is easy and effective. When affixed to foliage
and sucking fluid, aphids are unable to move and can be crushed
with fingers or sponges dipped in an insecticidal solution.
Spot-spray small infestations and control ants simultaneously.

Control ants by mixing borax with hand soap or borax powder
with powdered sugar. Ants eat the sweet mix and the borax kills
them. They excrete the sweet borax mix in the nest where other
ants eat the feces and die.

## Biological Control

Several effective biological controls are available:

- Lacewings (Chrysoperla species) are the most effective and
  available predators for aphids. Release 1 to 20 lacewings
  per plant depending on infestation level, as soon as aphids
  appear. Repeat every month. Eggs take a few days to hatch
  into larvae that exterminate aphids
- Gall-midge (Aphidoletes aphidimyza), available under the
  trade name Aphidend, is a specialized aphid predator
- Parasitic wasp (Aphidius matricariae), available commercially
  as Aphidpar, lays eggs inside aphid bodies
- Ladybugs work well but are drawn to HID lamps where many
  perish. Release about 50 per plant, knowing that frequent
  replenishment will be needed
- Verticillium lecanii fungus, available as Vertalec, is very
  aphid-specific and effective

## Spray Control

Homemade and insecticidal soap sprays are very effective against
aphids. Apply two or three times at five to ten day intervals.
[[cervantes-homemade-pest-disease-sprays]]
containing garlic, hot pepper, and soap can provide effective
control without introducing toxic chemicals to the garden.
