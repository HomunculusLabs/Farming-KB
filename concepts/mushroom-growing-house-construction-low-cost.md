Source: FAO RAP Publication 2001/12, "A Training Manual on Mushroom Cultivation for People with Disabilities" by Johanne Hanko. Based on the Ubon Ratchathani mushroom house design, Thailand.

A complete mushroom growing house for 1000 substrate bags can be constructed for approximately 500 Baht (US$12) using locally available materials. See also [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] and [[mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities-training-program]].

## Standard House Specifications

- **Dimensions**: 3m wide x 6m long x 3m high at roof peak
- **Capacity**: 1000 substrate bags
- **Frame**: 8 bamboo poles (3m each) for roof supports, plus side poles
- **Walls**: Thatch or agricultural netting (sak yong) on sides
- **Floor**: Bare ground or packed earth with a 20cm drain

Total: approximately 500 Baht (US$12) for a basic 1000-bag mushroom house.

## Construction Steps

### 1. Site Selection
Choose a location that is:
- Near the trainee's home for daily management
- Close to water source
- On flat or gently sloping ground
- Shaded by trees where possible (existing shade reduces construction costs)

### 2. Ground Preparation
Clear the area of weeds and level the ground. Create a shallow drainage channel (20cm deep) around the perimeter. Lay plastic sheeting on the floor if moisture from ground is excessive.

### 3. Frame Construction
- Set 4 main bamboo poles at corners, 3m apart, buried 50cm into ground
- Set intermediate poles every 1.5m along the sides
- Build the roof frame with bamboo rafters forming an A-shape or gable roof
- Secure all connections with rope or wire

### 4. Walls and Roof
- Cover side walls with thatch, palm leaves, or agricultural netting
- Leave openings for doors at both ends for airflow
- Cover roof with thatch or palm leaves, ensuring no rain enters

### 5. Ventilation
Critical for mushroom production:
- Create openings near the top of walls for hot air escape
- Ensure cross-ventilation through door openings
- Adjustable openings allow control of humidity and fresh air

## Shelving Systems

### Wooden Shelf System
For higher-capacity houses:

- Use wood planks for shelves, 80cm wide
- Space shelves vertically at 60cm intervals
- Support with wooden posts and cross-beams
- Each shelf holds 3 rows of bags side by side
- A house of 3m x 6m x 3m can accommodate 5 levels of shelving

Shelf costs:
- Wood planks (80cm x 6m x 10 pieces): 500 Baht
- Wood posts (3m x 10 pieces): 300 Baht
- Total shelving: approximately 800 Baht

### Suspended Bag System
An alternative that saves floor space:

- Hang bags from the roof frame using rope or string
- Space bags 15-20cm apart horizontally
- Hang in rows with 60cm vertical spacing
- Ideal for people who cannot bend to floor level

This system is particularly suitable for wheelchair users as bags hang at accessible heights.

## Country-Style Mushroom House

A simpler version using existing structures:

1. **Under-house utilization** - If the house has an open ground floor, use the space beneath
2. **Between buildings** - Create a covered pathway between buildings with shade cloth
3. **In a shed** - Convert existing animal shed or storage building
4. **Under trees** - Hang bags from tree branches with minimal structure

For the country-style approach, the most important requirement is:
- Protection from direct sun
- Protection from direct rain
- Adequate ventilation
- Easy access for daily watering and harvesting

## Watering System

- Spray bottle (hand-held): 20-40 Baht
- Large watering can with fine spray: 60-100 Baht
- Automatic mister (optional): 300-500 Baht

Most disabled trainees use simple spray bottles. Water 2-4 times daily depending on humidity.

## Adaptations for Disabilities

- **Lower height**: Construct the house lower than standard (2m height) for wheelchair users
- **Suspended bags**: Hang bags at working height instead of floor shelving
- **Wider aisles**: Ensure 1m minimum aisle width for wheelchair passage
- **Rolling cart**: Use a small cart for moving bags between inoculation, incubation, and fruiting areas
- **Accessible watering**: Place water source inside or immediately adjacent to the house

## Total Investment per Farmer

For 1000 bags (Thailand 2000 costs):
- Mushroom house: 500 Baht
- Shelving: 800 Baht
- Country-style pasteurization drum: 500 Baht
- Spray bottle and tools: 200 Baht
- Substrate bags (1000 bags): 4,000 Baht
- Spawn: 2,500 Baht

**Total startup: approximately 8,500 Baht (US$210)**

Mushrooms can be produced and sold within 2-4 months, making this a rapid-return enterprise.
