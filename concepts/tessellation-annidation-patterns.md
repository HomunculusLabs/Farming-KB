## Tessellation

Tessellation refers to the tiling of a plane (or surface) using one or more geometric shapes without overlaps or gaps. In nature, tessellated patterns underlie many distributions of organisms and physical phenomena:

- **Trees in deserts** arrange themselves in roughly hexagonal patterns, maximizing distance between competitors while maintaining even coverage.
- **Convection cells** in heated fluids form hexagonal matrices (B\u00e9nard cells).
- **Cracks in mud** create approximately square, hexagonal, or sine-wave-based grid patterns.
- **Honeycomb** structures represent one of the most efficient tessellations in nature.

The general core model, in both regular and irregular form, will tessellate or link together to form closed surfaces, spheres, or chains. This means that understanding one fundamental pattern unit allows a designer to understand how many such units will combine into larger structures.

### Pattern Matrix

A pattern matrix of tessellated patterns emerges when multiple copies of the same base pattern tile together. The "growth lines" (T-series) of these models are effectively a series of smaller and smaller forms nested within larger boundaries, as seen in target patterns or tree cross-sections. The study of matrices reveals that the T (time) lines are ogives of a tessellated model and develop from the "S" (stream) lines of the next adjoining model, revealing the co-definitive, inter-dependent properties of natural systems.

## Annidation

Annidation (from Latin *nidus*, a nest) is the process of nesting like components of the same or different size in a compact way. This is the same principle used when storing bowls or glasses, one within the other, for efficient use of space.

In nature, annidation is observed in:

- **Tree cross-sections**, where annual growth rings nest within each other in progressively smaller circles.
- **Target patterns**, where concentric rings decrease in scale toward a center.
- **Spiral shells** of molluscs, where chambers nest within the overall spiral form.
- **Morphological development** of organisms, where smaller-scale structures are nested within larger body plans.
