# [[psilocybe-pelliculosa-entity]]

Psilocybe pelliculosa is a psilocybin-containing mushroom commonly
known as the "Conifer Psilocybe" due to its strong association with
coniferous forest habitats. It is a relatively small but widely
distributed species found in the Pacific Northwest of North America.

## Taxonomy

The species was first described by Singer and Smith in 1946. The
specific epithet "pelliculosa" refers to the thin, membranous pellicle
(a separable gelatinous layer) that covers the cap surface. This
characteristic is a key diagnostic feature that helps distinguish it
from similar-looking species in coniferous forest environments.

Psilocybe pelliculosa belongs to the section Semilanceatae of the
genus Psilocybe, which also includes P. semilanceata and several other
small, slender psilocybin mushrooms found in grassland and forest
habitats.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)

The cap of Psilocybe pelliculosa is relatively small, measuring 1 to
3.5 cm in diameter. When young, the cap is acutely conical or
bell-shaped (campanulate), gradually expanding to become broadly
conical or convex with age, though it typically retains a pronounced
umbo (a central bump). The surface is smooth and has a distinctive
viscid or sticky texture when moist, due to the gelatinous pellicle
from which the species takes its name.

The cap color ranges from a dark reddish-brown to amber or ochraceous
brown, often with a slightly translucent appearance when fresh. The
color fades to a lighter tan or yellowish-brown as the mushroom dries.
The margin is typically striate (marked with fine radial lines) when
moist, reflecting the gills beneath. A partial veil is present in young
specimens, often leaving a faint annular zone on the stem.

### Gills (Lamellae)

The gills are adnate to slightly decurrent in their attachment to the
stem. They are moderately close in spacing and can be relatively broad
for the size of the mushroom. Initially pale grayish, the gills darken
to deep purplish-brown as the spores mature. The gill edges are
typically lighter in color than the faces.

### Stem (Stipe)

The stem is slender and elongated, measuring 3 to 9 cm in length and
only 1 to 2 mm in thickness. It is cylindrical and equal throughout
its length, though it may be slightly thickened at the base. The
surface is smooth and silky, colored whitish to pale buff. The stem
is firm but fragile and hollow. A distinctive annulus (ring) from
the partial veil may be present, though it is often evanescent and
quickly disappears.

Blue bruising can occur on the stem when handled or damaged, though
it is typically less pronounced than in some other Psilocybe species
such as P. cyanescens or P. azurescens.

### Spore Print and Microscopy

The spore print is dark purplish-brown to nearly black. Spores are
ellipsoid to slightly amygdaliform (almond-shaped) in outline,
measuring approximately 9 to 12 micrometers in length and 5 to 7
micrometers in width. They are smooth and thick-walled, with a
distinct germ pore.

## Habitat and Distribution

Psilocybe pelliculosa is found primarily in the Pacific Northwest
region of North America, with a range extending from British Columbia
through Washington and Oregon into northern California. The Psilocybin
Mushroom Image Guide documents this species through a photograph of a
cluster of conifer psilocybes, consistent with its known habitat.

### Forest Associations

As the common name "Conifer Psilocybe" suggests, this species has a
strong ecological association with coniferous forests. It is most
commonly found growing in:

- Douglas fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii) forests
- Western hemlock (Tsuga heterophylla) forests
- Sitka spruce (Picea sitchensis) forests
- Mixed conifer-hardwood forest edges

The species typically fruits directly on forest floor debris,
including needle duff, twigs, and decaying wood chips. It may also
appear along forest trails, roadsides, and in disturbed areas within
coniferous forests where the ground has been cleared or modified.

### Fruiting Season

Psilocybe pelliculosa fruits in the autumn, typically from September
through December, with peak fruiting occurring during the wettest
months. It responds well to the cool temperatures and abundant
moisture of Pacific Northwest autumns, often appearing in large
numbers after heavy rainfall events.

## Potency and Chemistry

Psilocybe pelliculosa contains psilocybin and psilocin as its
primary psychoactive compounds. It is considered to be only
moderately potent compared to other psilocybin mushrooms, with
psilocybin and psilocin content generally lower than that of
P. cyanescens or P. azurescens. The psychoactive effects are
typically milder, which may explain why the species receives less
attention than its more potent relatives despite being relatively
common in its range.

## Similar Species

Psilocybe pelliculosa can be confused with several other small
mushrooms found in Pacific Northwest conifer forests. The most
important lookalikes include Galerina species, some of which contain
deadly amatoxins. Careful attention to spore print color (dark
purplish-brown in P. pelliculosa versus rusty brown in Galerina) and
the presence of blue bruising can help differentiate them.
Psilocybe silvatica is another similar species found in the same
habitats but typically has a larger stature.
