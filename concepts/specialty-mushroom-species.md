# Specialty Mushroom Species Guide

Additional cultivated and notable mushroom species beyond the core five (oyster, shiitake, lion's mane, reishi, wine cap). These species offer unique medicinal properties, ecological roles, or cultivation opportunities for advanced growers.

Related: [[mushroom-species-guide]], [[medicinal-mushroom-guide]], [[mushroom-cultivation-basics]]

## Agarikon — Fomitopsis officinalis

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Very difficult (near-extinct in Europe) |
| **Substrate** | Old-growth conifers (Douglas fir, hemlock, spruce) |
| **Fruiting temp** | Cool temperate |
| **Cultivation** | Outdoor logs/stumps, very slow |

Ancient medicinal polypore known since Hippocrates (460-377 B.C.). Near-extinct in Europe due to overharvesting and old-growth forest loss. Protein only 3% (lowest of cultivated species) but extraordinarily high in medicinal compounds. Strong antibacterial and antiviral properties. Among the strongest aromatase and 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors tested. See [[medicinal-mushroom-guide]].

## Enokitake / Winter Mushroom — Flammulina velutipes

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy (indoors); requires care outdoors (confusion risk) |
| **Substrate** | Hardwood sawdust (oak, alder, poplar), also some softwoods |
| **Fruiting temp** | 40-60F (late fall through winter) |
| **Humidity** | 85-95% RH |
| **Yield** | Commercial bottle culture highly productive |

Wild form: short, fuzzy-footed, brown cap. Cultivated form: long stems, tiny caps from high-CO2, low-light conditions. Freezes and thaws while continuing to grow. One of the first mushrooms with antitumor agents brought to Western scientists' attention. Ikekawa (1969, 1985) found unusually low cancer rates among enokitake growers near Nagano, Japan. Contains FVP (Flammulina velutipes polysaccharide) and proflamin (immunomodulator). **WARNING**: Wild enokitake can resemble deadly Galerina species (rusty brown spores). Always confirm spore color before consuming.

## Pioppino — Agrocybe aegerita

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy to intermediate |
| **Substrate** | Cottonwood, willow, poplar, maple stumps and logs |
| **Fruiting temp** | 60-70F (primarily fall) |
| **Flavor** | Nutty, crunchy texture |

Excellent stump recycler. Can be cultivated via log rafts — scarify logs with chainsaw and pack sawdust spawn between adjoining logs. Reported to produce tumor regressions in mice via carboxymethylated glucans. Also has hypoglycemic activity and free radical scavenging properties. One of Stamets' employees' favorite edibles.

## Shaggy Mane — Coprinus comatus

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy (outdoor) |
| **Substrate** | Manure-enriched soils, hardwood sawdust beds, newly fertilized lawns |
| **Fruiting temp** | 40-60F (spring and fall) |
| **Shelf life** | 2 days maximum (deliquesces) |

A "first responder" mushroom that proliferates after habitat disturbance — construction sites, highways, scraped ground. Contains natural antibiotic coprinine. Bioaccumulates arsenic and mercury. Shows antimicrobial activity against E. coli, S. aureus, Candida albicans, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Aspergillus niger. Historically used both as food and as ink for medieval manuscripts. Must be consumed within 2 days of picking before gills deliquesce.

## Tinder Conk / Amadou — Fomes fomentarius

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate (outdoor logs/stumps) |
| **Substrate** | Birch, aspen, willow, alder |
| **Type** | Perennial polypore (5-10 years outdoors) |
| **Fruiting** | Primarily summer |

Oldest known manipulated biological product associated with Paleolithic humans (11,600 B.C.). Found with Otzi the ice man (3,300 B.C.). Used as fire-starting tinder, felt material, and medicinally for cauterizing wounds and treating arthritis. Mycelium secretes crystalline structures that attract, stun, and consume E. coli — a predator-prey relationship. Strong antiviral properties. Antitumor polysaccharides isolated from mycelium (Ito et al. 1976). Perennial outdoors; indoors, forms tube layers bimonthly.

## Artist Conk — Ganoderma applanatum

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy (outdoor) |
| **Substrate** | Hardwoods and conifers (especially old-growth Douglas fir) |
| **Type** | Perennial polypore (up to 50 years) |
| **Size** | Up to 1 meter wide |

Third-largest mushroom in the world. Perennial conk with white pore surface that stains brown when bruised (used by artists for etching). Methanol extracts show significant antibacterial activity against E. coli and S. aureus. Often occurs alongside turkey tails, suggesting the two species could work together in habitat restoration. Prodigious spore producer — billions can be gathered from a single well-developed conk.

## Agaricus brasiliensis (Brazilian Blazei / Almond Portobello)

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Substrate** | Leached cow manure, composted substrates |
| **Fruiting temp** | 70-95F (summer mushroom) |
| **Flavor** | Strong almond (fresh); terrible when dried |

One of the most protein-rich cultivated mushrooms (33-48% dry weight). Bruises bright yellow when cut. Beta-glucans up to 14%, total polysaccharides ~27%. Promotes natural killer cells and is selectively cytotoxic to tumor cells. Absorbs cadmium, copper, lead, and mercury. Good mycofiltration candidate around dairies and feedlots. Controversial in commercial markets due to competition between growers in China, Japan, and Brazil. Samples from China found with significantly more cadmium than US-grown.

## Hypholoma capnoides (Clustered Woodlover)

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy (outdoor) |
| **Substrate** | Conifer stumps and logs (Douglas fir, pine, hemlock) |
| **Fruiting temp** | 50-60F (fall) |
| **Warning** | Easily confused with poisonous H. fasciculare (olive-green gills) and deadly Galerina |

Aggressive conifer stump decomposer and Armillaria blight fighter. Stumps inoculated with this species resist Armillaria root rot. Chapman et al. (2004) found close relative H. fasciculare reduced Armillaria by up to 67% in BC field trials. Produces long, forking rhizomorphs that penetrate deeply into wood. One of the few edible mushrooms found on aged redwoods and cedars. Nutty flavor when cooked until crispy. See [[mycelium-running-techniques]].

## Hypsizygus ulmarius (Elm Oyster)

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate (outdoor logs/beds) |
| **Substrate** | Elm, cottonwood, beech, maple, willow |
| **Fruiting temp** | 50-70F |
| **Flavor** | Superior to common oyster (Stamets' highest-rated oyster-like mushroom) |

Despite its common name, this is NOT a Pleurotus — it produces brown rot (not white). Does not spoil as quickly as Pleurotus species, suggesting antibacterial properties. Friendly to many garden vegetables (unlike P. ostreatus, which inhibits some plants). TCM practitioners recommend it for stomach/intestinal diseases. May have anti-nematode and cholesterol-lowering properties. Lower spore load than most oyster species.
