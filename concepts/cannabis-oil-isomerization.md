# Cannabis Oil Isomerization — Converting CBD to THC

The process of converting cannabidiol (CBD) to delta-9-THC through acid-catalyzed isomerization. Based on techniques from Lemon's "Cannabis Alchemy" (1990). See [[cannabis-extraction-basics]] for the complete extraction process.

## Why Isomerize?

The quantity of CBD in cannabis oil is usually at least equal to the quantity of THC. Through isomerization, the potency of the oil can be at least doubled, and in some cases increased five to six times. This is because:

- CBD is the biosynthetic precursor to THC in the cannabis plant
- Acid-catalyzed cyclization converts CBD to delta-9-THC and delta-8-THC
- The reaction also converts existing delta-9-THC to the more stable delta-8-THC
- Delta-8-THC is psychoactive (approximately 2/3 the potency of delta-9-THC)

## Chemistry

The isomerization of CBD to THC involves acid-catalyzed cyclization:

- CBD (open-ring compound) is treated with an acid catalyst
- The acid protonates the hydroxyl group, facilitating ring closure
- The product is a mixture of delta-9-THC, delta-8-THC, and minor isomers
- The ratio depends on reaction conditions (temperature, time, acid concentration)
- Delta-8-THC is more thermodynamically stable and predominates at higher temperatures

## Lemon's Method

### Equipment Required
- Pyrex pot (not stainless steel — acid is reactive)
- Refluxing apparatus (see [[cannabis-extraction-basics]])
- Sulfuric acid (concentrated), stored in safety bottle
- Sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) for neutralization
- Separatory funnel
- Water, ice baths

### Procedure
1. Dissolve the crude cannabis oil in alcohol (ethanol or methanol)
2. Add sulfuric acid carefully — the ratio of acid to oil is critical (typically 1-3 drops per gram of oil)
3. Place the Pyrex pot in the refluxing apparatus over a boiling water bath
4. Reflux for approximately 2 hours
5. Cool the apparatus in an ice-water bath
6. Pour the solution into an equal volume of water to dilute the acid
7. Neutralize with sodium bicarbonate until effervescence stops
8. Extract the oil from the aqueous solution using a non-polar solvent (petroleum ether or chloroform)
9. Wash the solvent layer with water to remove residual acid
10. Evaporate the solvent to recover the isomerized oil

### Safety Notes
- Sulfuric acid is extremely corrosive — treat burns immediately with water and bicarbonate of soda
- Use Pyrex, not metal, for the acid reaction
- Work in a well-ventilated area
- The fumes from the reaction are irritating — avoid inhalation
- Always add acid to solvent, never the reverse

## Modern Methods

### Toluene/Sulfuric Acid Method
- CBD dissolved in toluene with a small amount of sulfuric acid
- Heated under reflux for 1-2 hours
- Higher yields and cleaner product than alcohol-based methods
- Toluene must be fully evaporated (toxic solvent)

### p-Toluenesulfonic Acid (p-TSA) Method
- A milder, more controllable alternative to sulfuric acid
- Dissolved in an organic solvent (toluene, hexane)
- Produces a cleaner product with fewer side reactions
- More expensive but more precise

### Lewis Acid Catalysts
- Boron trifluoride (BF3) can catalyze the isomerization
- Used in pharmaceutical settings
- More precise control but requires specialized equipment

## Yield and Purity

- Typical conversion rates: 50-80% of CBD converted to THC isomers
- Starting oil with 5% THC and 5% CBD could yield oil with 8-15% total THC
- The product is a mixture of delta-9-THC, delta-8-THC, and unconverted CBD
- Chromatographic analysis recommended to verify results

## Legal Status

- Isomerization is considered drug manufacturing in most jurisdictions
- The process converts legal CBD into controlled THC
- Research laboratory settings may be exempt with proper licensing
- Laws vary significantly by country and region
