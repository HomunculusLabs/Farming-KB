# Food Fermentation

Fermentation is the controlled transformation of food by microorganisms — bacteria, yeasts, and molds. It preserves food, enhances nutrition (increases bioavailability of B vitamins, creates new compounds), and develops complex flavors. See [[food-preservation]] for fermentation alongside other preservation methods, and [[lactic-acid-bacteria]] for the biology of LAB fermentation.

## Lacto-Fermentation Basics

Lacto-fermentation uses lactic acid bacteria (LAB) naturally present on vegetables to convert sugars into lactic acid. The acid preserves the food and creates the characteristic sour flavor.

### Core Principles
- **Salt**: Inhibits spoilage organisms while LAB tolerate moderate salinity. Use non-iodized salt (kosher, sea, pickling).
- **Anaerobic environment**: LAB are facultative anaerobes; exposure to oxygen promotes mold and yeast. Keep food submerged below brine.
- **Temperature**: 60-72F (16-22C) is ideal. Too cold = slow fermentation; too hot = off-flavors, mushy texture.
- **Time**: Minimum 3-7 days for basic ferments; 3-6 weeks for developed flavor. Ferments continue to change slowly in cold storage.

### Salt Ratios

| Style | Salt by Weight of Vegetables | Brine (if used) |
|-------|------------------------------|-----------------|
| Light/saucerkraut style | 1.5-2.0% | N/A (vegetables release their own liquid) |
| Standard pickles | 2.0-2.5% | 3-5% brine |
| Strong/long-ferment | 2.5-3.5% | 5% brine |
| Quick pickle | 2.0% | 4% brine |

**By weight method** (preferred): Weigh vegetables, multiply by 0.02 for 2% salt. Example: 1kg cabbage = 20g salt. Mix thoroughly, pack tightly, wait for liquid to release.

### Troubleshooting Lacto-Ferments
- **Kahm yeast** (white film on surface): Harmless; skim off. Caused by oxygen exposure or low salt.
- **Mold** (fuzzy, colored): Toss the batch. Caused by vegetables above brine, contamination.
- **Soft/mushy texture**: Too warm, not enough salt, or enzyme activity (blanch or salt immediately).
- **Too salty**: Soak in water before eating, or use as seasoning ingredient.
- **No bubbles**: Fermentation may still be happening (not all ferments are visibly active). Trust your nose and taste.

## Vegetable Fermentation

### Sauerkraut
- Shred cabbage finely (mandoline or knife), salt at 1.8-2%, massage until juicy liquid releases
- Pack tightly into vessel, eliminating air pockets; ensure cabbage is fully submerged
- Ferment 3-4 weeks minimum; improves with age (6+ months for complex flavor)
- Classic kraut: cabbage + salt only. Additions: caraway, juniper, dill, garlic, chili, apple

### Kimchi
- Based on napa cabbage, Korean radish (mu), garlic, ginger, gochugaru (Korean chili), fish sauce or salted shrimp, scallions
- Brine cabbage in 3-5% salt for 2-12 hours, drain, mix with paste
- Ferment 1-2 weeks at room temp (faster in summer), then refrigerate
- Many regional styles: baechu (whole cabbage), kkakdugi (radish), oi sobagi (cucumber)

### Pickles
- Use firm vegetables: cucumbers, green beans, radishes, carrots, peppers, onions
- Whole or speared; brine at 3-5% salinity
- Add aromatics: dill, garlic, mustard seed, coriander, bay leaf, peppercorns, chili
- Ferment 1-3 weeks; check daily; refrigerate when desired sourness is reached

### Hot Sauce
- Ferment chili peppers (with or without garlic, onion, carrots for sweetness) in 2-3% brine for 2-4 weeks
- Blend fermented peppers with some brine to desired consistency
- Bottle and optionally age; add vinegar for acidity boost and shelf stability

## Dairy Fermentation

See [[dairy-and-cheesemaking]] for complete coverage of yogurt, kefir, cheese, and butter. Key dairy ferments:
- **Yogurt**: Lactobacillus bulgaricus + Streptococcus thermophilus; 110F for 4-12 hours
- **Kefir**: Mesophilic symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeasts (SCOBY); grains multiply over time
- **Cheese**: Controlled curdling with rennet + culture; wide range from fresh (chevre) to aged (cheddar)

## Beverage Fermentation

### Kombucha
- Sweet tea (black/green + sugar) fermented by SCOBY (Symbiotic Culture of Bacteria and Yeast)
- Acetobacter produces acetic acid; yeasts produce ethanol + carbonation
- Primary fermentation: 7-14 days at 70-80F; secondary (flavor/carbonation): 1-5 days in sealed bottles
- Feed SCOBY with fresh sweet tea every 1-2 weeks (continuous brew) or start new batch

### Mead
- Honey + water fermented by yeast (Saccharomyces); simplest: equal parts honey and water by volume
- Hydromel (session): 1 part honey to 3 parts water (~5-8% ABV)
- Traditional: 1:1 (~12-16% ABV); sack mead: 2:1 or more
- Primary: 2-6 weeks; secondary aging: 3-12+ months. Needs nutrients (fermaid-o, raisins, tea) beyond honey alone.

### Wine (Grape)
- Crush grapes, add yeast (wild or commercial), ferment 1-2 weeks primary, press, secondary 3-12 months
- Key parameters: Brix/sugar (22-26 for dry wine), pH (3.0-3.6), TA (0.55-0.85%), SO2 management
- Red wines ferment on skins; whites press before fermentation

### Ginger Beer
- Ginger bug: grated ginger + sugar + water; feed daily for 3-7 days until actively bubbling
- Make soda: 2-4 tbsp ginger bug per quart of sweetened ginger tea; bottle, ferment 1-3 days (burp to prevent explosions)
- Alcohol content: 0.5-2% (soda) vs 5-10% if fermented longer with more sugar

## Mold-Based Fermentation

### Koji (Aspergillus oryzae)
- Rice or barley inoculated with koji spores; incubated at 85-90F, 80-90% humidity for 36-48 hours
- Produces amylase (converts starch to sugar) and protease (converts protein to amino acids)
- Foundation of: miso, soy sauce, shio koji, amazake, sake, mirin

### Miso
- Koji-inoculated grain + cooked soybeans + salt; packed and aged
- Sweet miso (shiro): 1-3 months, higher koji ratio, less salt
- Red miso (aka): 6-12 months, more soybeans, more salt
- Dark miso (hatcho): 2-3 years, soybean-only (no grain), long aging
- All-purpose ratio: 10 parts soybeans, 10 parts koji rice, 5 parts salt (by weight)

## Fungal Fermentation

### Sourdough
- Wild yeast (Saccharomyces exiguus, Candida milleri) + LAB (Lactobacillus sanfranciscensis) in flour + water
- Maintain starter: equal weights flour and water (100% hydration), discard half and feed daily
- Bread: starter + flour + water + salt; bulk ferment 4-12 hours, shape, cold proof 12-24 hours, bake
- See [[lactic-acid-bacteria]] for sourdough microbiology

### Tempeh
- Cooked and dehulled soybeans inoculated with Rhizopus oligosporus
- Incubate at 85-90F in perforated bags for 24-48 hours until fully bound by white mycelium
- Key: beans must be dry on surface before inoculation; good airflow prevents off-spots
- Can also use: black beans, chickpeas, lentils, wheat berries, oats

### Natto
- Cooked soybeans inoculated with Bacillus subtilis natto
- Ferment at 100-105F for 24-48 hours until sticky, stringy texture develops
- Distinctive aroma (earthy, ammoniacal); rich in vitamin K2 (menaquinone-7) and nattokinase
- Can ferment in a dehydrator, yogurt maker, or warm oven with light on

## Vessels and Equipment

- **Glass mason jars**: Most accessible; weight vegetables below brine with fermentation weights or folded cabbage leaf. Burp regularly if not using airlock.
- **Crocks (ceramic):** Traditional; water-seal crocks (Harsch/Gairtopfer) create automatic airlock. Heavy and expensive but excellent for large batches.
- **Airlock lids**: Convert mason jars to anaerobic vessels; allow CO2 out, no air in. Affordable and effective.
- **Fermentation crocks with moat**: Traditional open crock with water moat seal; allow gas to bubble through water barrier.
- **Plastic buckets**: Fine for large batches (5 gal); food-grade only. No long-term storage — plastic breathes.

## Safety

- **Botulism risk**: Near-zero in properly salted, acidic lacto-ferments (pH <4.6). Risk increases with low salt, no acid, and anaerobic conditions (canning fermented products without proper acidification).
- **Mold**: Any fuzzy, colored mold (green, black, pink) — discard the batch. White Kahm yeast is safe.
- **Off-smells**: Rotten egg (H2S) or putrid smells indicate spoilage. Ferments should smell sour, tangy, or earthy — never putrid.
- **Alcoholic ferments**: Mead/wine/cider require ABV monitoring if selling; fermentation lock prevents pressure buildup.
- **Allergens**: Miso, soy sauce, tempeh, natto all contain soy. Cross-contamination risk in shared fermentation spaces.
