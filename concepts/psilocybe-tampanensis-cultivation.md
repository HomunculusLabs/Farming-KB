# Psilocybe Tampanensis Cultivation

*Psilocybe tampanensis* is a rare psychoactive mushroom species first
collected near Tampa, Florida in 1977. It is renowned among cultivators
for its ability to produce sclerotia, colloquially known as
"philosopher's stones." This species, along with [[psilocybe-mexicana-cultivation]], is one of the primary sclerotia-producing
species cultivated on [grass seed substrate. The source material provides specific cultivation data
for *P. tampanensis*, particularly regarding sclerotia formation
timelines and environmental requirements. See also
[[sclerotia-cultivation]] for a comprehensive sclerotia production
guide.

## Species Overview

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Common name | Philosopher's stones (sclerotia) |
| sclerotia production | Yes, prolific and well-developed |
| Mushroom fruiting | Possible but less commonly pursued |

*P. tampanensis* is of particular interest because the original wild
specimen from which all cultivated strains descend was collected only
once in 1977 near Tampa, Florida, making it one of the most genetically
uniform cultivated mushroom species.

## Substrate and Colonization

*P. tampanensis* is cultivated on standard
[grass seed substrate: 110 grams
seed and 180 mL water per 720 mL jar. Rye grass (*Lolium perenne*) or
mixtures work well; seed must be fungicide-free.

Colonization follows standard practice:
- Inoculate cooled jars with spore syringe, mycelium syringe, or agar
  squares using clean technique.
- Incubate at approximately 20 degrees C, draft-free.
- Shake jars when mycelium appears in only a few spots to redistribute
  colonized kernels and dramatically speed colonization.
- Full colonization takes 5 to 20 days.
- Yellowish metabolites indicate excessive incubation time or
  temperature; case or harvest promptly.

## Sclerotia Production

Sclerotia production is the primary reason most cultivators grow *P.
tampanensis*. The source material provides the most detailed timeline
data for this species, making it the reference standard for
[sclerotia cultivation.

### Formation Process

Colonized grass seed need not be cased for sclerotia to form. After
full colonization, the jar incubates undisturbed. Sclerotia begin as
small white to tan knots within the grain mass, gradually enlarging and
hardening over weeks. They become visible pressing against the glass.

### Growth Timeline

The source states that sclerotia will continue to enlarge until 4
months after inoculation. This 4-month mark represents the practical
maximum; beyond it, growth slows to negligible levels.

| Stage | Timeline | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| Colonization | Days 5 to 20 | Mycelium fully colonizes grass seed |
| Sclerotia initiation | Weeks 2 to 4 | Small knots appear within substrate |
| Active growth | Weeks 4 to 12 | Steady enlargement of sclerotia |
| Maturation | Months 3 to 4 | Growth slows; harvest recommended |
| Post-maturity | Beyond 4 months | Diminishing returns; harvest recommended |

### Environmental Conditions

Sclerotia formation requires minimal environmental management:
- **Temperature:** Room temperature (approximately 20 degrees C).
- **Light:** Dark conditions preferred. Store jars in a closet,
  drawer, or covered box.
- **Air exchange:** The loose jar lid provides sufficient gas
  exchange. No active ventilation is needed.
- **Container:** Standard canning jars with loose lids and foil
  covers, as prepared in the substrate preparation process.

Sclerotia also form in the casing layer of cased trays alongside mushroom
production. These are fully viable, representing an additional yield from
the same culture, a trait shared with [ P. mexicana.

## Casing for Mushroom Production

While sclerotia are the primary product, *P. tampanensis* can also
produce mushroom fruiting bodies when cased:

1. Transfer fully colonized grass seed to 1-litre disposable plastic
   trays, levelling the surface and breaking up lumps with the jar rim.
2. Apply 1.5 to 2.0 cm of [casing soil
   (peat/vermiculite/limestone mix) with an even but rough surface.
3. Cover with tin foil and incubate at 20 to 25 degrees C until
   mycelium grows through the casing.
4. Transfer to the [fruiting chamber when
   mycelium is visibly colonizing the casing surface.

## Cold Shock Response

*P. tampanensis* does NOT benefit from cold shock treatment. The
source material explicitly lists it alongside *P. mexicana* and
*Panaeolus subbalteatus* as species that do not benefit from
refrigeration before fruiting. Cold shocking is reserved for
harder-to-fruit strains and is unnecessary for *P. tampanensis*.

## Fruiting Chamber Requirements

When fruiting mushrooms, *P. tampanensis* casings are placed in the
simple plastic bin [fruiting chambers
described in the source. Five trays per bin with polyethylene
sheeting covers. Daily misting prevents casing drying, and dead
pinheads (aborts) should be removed promptly to prevent mold.
