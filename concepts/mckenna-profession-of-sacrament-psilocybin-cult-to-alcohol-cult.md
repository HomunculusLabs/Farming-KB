## Overview

[[terence-mckenna]] identifies the transition from psilocybin-based religion to alcohol-based
culture as one of the most consequential shifts in human history. This "profanation of the
sacrament" represents not merely a change in preferred intoxicant but a fundamental
transformation in the relationship between human consciousness, community, and the natural
world. McKenna describes this as a four-stage process of abandonment that culminates in the
complete loss of the original mystery experience.

### Stage Two: Substitution with Inactive Materials

The second stage involved replacing psychoactive plants with completely inactive materials
that served as symbols of the former power. These substitutes, though still plants, were
"really no more than symbols of the former power of the mystery to authentically move
initiates." The original experiential content of the ritual was now preserved only in the
form and gesture, not in the pharmacological substance.

### Stage Three: Complete Abstraction into Symbol and Dogma

In the third stage, even plants disappeared from the ritual. In their place arose "esoteric
teachings and dogma, rituals, stress on lineages, gestures, and cosmogonic diagrams."
McKenna identifies the major world religions as typical of this stage, where the memory of
direct ecstatic experience has been entirely replaced by institutionalized belief systems
that preserve the form of the mystery without its substance.
