# Thatch Species

From Permaculture: A Designers' Manual by Bill Mollison.

## Overview

Thatch is one of the age-old durable building materials of mankind, referenced alongside stone, mud, bamboo, and round timbers. The Designers' Manual treats thatch as both a practical roofing material and a product of productive permaculture systems, particularly in tropical and arid climates. Mollison emphasises that all such materials can be locally produced if energy from community forests and people is provided.

## Thatch in Climate-Specific Housing

### Wet Tropics
In the wet tropics, housing is usually raised, steep roofed, and thatched with permeable walls and screens. Thatched roofs are the norm for traditional housing in these regions, where rapid plant growth ensures continuous material supply. The steep pitch sheds heavy tropical rainfall effectively.

### Wet-Dry Tropics
Housing in the wet-dry tropics is often mud-pole structures that are thatched. The thatch material must withstand both intense wet-season rain and prolonged dry-season heat and desiccation.

### Arid and Desert Climates
Where thatch or tile is impractical in desert areas, a vented roof system is recommended as an alternative. Thatch may still serve for animal shelters, poultry houses, and garden structures even where it is not the primary house roofing material.

## Thatch as a Design Material

### Insulation Properties
Thatch is listed among adequate insulators alongside straw, wool, feathers, cork, woven materials, and even paper and cardboard. However, Mollison notes that few of these equal thick vines over wall and roof as active cooling systems. In desert housing, the emphasis should be on developing the arbor for living and creating compact reactive housing mainly for night occupancy.

### Building Materials Philosophy
Mollison makes the case that some of the most charming and climatically appropriate houses on earth are built without bank loans, architects, metals, concrete, or contractors. Stone, mud, bamboo, round timbers, rope, thatch, and even baked brick and tiles are the age-old durable building materials. All can be locally produced if energy from community forests and people is provided.

## Thatch Species and Sources

### Palm Thatch
Palm fronds are among the most widely used thatch materials in tropical permaculture systems:
- Coconut palm (Cocos nucifera) fronds provide thatch and are abundant in tropical designs
- Palm fronds also supply mulch, structural material, and animal feed
- Shredded palm fronds serve as crop mulch and soil amendment
- Spiny-trunked palms are recommended as seedlings in tropical hedgerows

### Grass Thatch
Arid-area grasses and forbs are selected for specific purposes including thatch production:
- Coarse tussock grasses become unpalatable if left unbrowsed but produce excellent thatch material
- A careful selection of perennial and annual grasses can provide thatch as a managed product
- Mown or managed swale fields are the most productive for grass thatch
- Pennisetum species are recommended as tall grasses in tropical hedgerows and can supply thatch
- Pampas grass (Cortaderia) clumps are listed among first plantings for earthwork sites and provide thatch material
- Banna grass is used alongside bamboo for garden structures and thatch

### Reed and Rush Thatch
Wetland species provide traditional thatch materials:
- Reeds and rushes gathered from ditches are excellent crop mulch and can serve as thatch
- Rushes are specifically noted as being used for thatch and matting
- Typha (cattail/bulrush) provides browse or reed thatch for crafts
- Typha browse is useful where acid conditions prevail in wetland areas

### Straw Thatch
- Straw is listed among adequate insulating materials for housing
- Straw lean-tos combined with bamboo are used in cool climates as season-extending shelters
- Straw and thatch are both referenced as traditional roofing materials

## Integration in Permaculture Systems

### Garden Structures
- Small waterholes benefit from being thatched to reduce evaporation
- Beekeeping sheds are best thatched or insulated against cold (Denmark bees are over-wintered in thatched attics)
- Poultry houses and animal shelters use thatch for weather protection
- Thatched shelters serve as breeding substrate "cave" habitats for fish in aquaculture systems

### Productive Hedgerows
The manual recommends a classical tropical hedgerow that combines species for multiple yields including thatch:
- Tall grasses or clump bamboo (Pennisetum) for thatch and structural material
- Quickset cuttings of Erythrina fusca or Jatropha
- Leucaena or Acacia seedlings
- Occasional palms for frond thatch

### Arid-Area Grass Management
A carefully-selected sowing of grasses in plots (pot-grown and divided) serves specific purposes including:
- Providing durable thatch and cane fences, mats, screens, or baskets
- Creating grassed waterways for downhill sheet flow
- Supplying green hay for domestic stock
- Filtering silt and nitrates in diversion drains

### Forest Garden Integration
Thatch species integrate with broader forest garden yields:
- Fuels, oils, and dry-stored food are listed alongside thatch as forest garden products
- Waterfowl forage species can also provide thatch material
- Green forage or hay from the same grass stands serves multiple purposes

## Durability and Maintenance

The manual notes that durable thatch results from selecting appropriate species. Reed thatch from wetland species and palm thatch from appropriate palm species have proven longevity. Grass thatch from well-managed tussock grasses provides durable roofing when properly applied to steep-pitched roofs.

The combination of thatch with other natural materials creates housing that is both climatically appropriate and locally self-reliant, requiring no external inputs beyond community labour and locally-sourced materials.
