# DMT Self-Transforming Machine Elves

## Overview

The self-transforming machine elves are a recurring entity encounter reported by many DMT users, most extensively described by Terence McKenna in his 1983 lecture "[[mckenna-translinguistic-objects-cognitive-dissonance-tryptamine]]: feeling-toned, meaning-toned, three-dimensional rotating complexes of transforming light and color. They emit sounds "like music, like language" that pass without any quantized moment of distinction. McKenna compared this to [[mckenna-radio-entelechy-dmt-onset-acoustic-phenomena]] ripping out of the organic matrix." An ascending tone is heard, along with the normal hallucinogenic geometric patterns of migrating and changing colored forms.

After about thirty seconds of synaptic saturation, where "all available bond sites are being occupied," one arrives at "a place that defies description, a space that has a feeling of being underground, or somehow insulated and domed." McKenna referenced Joyce's *Finnegans Wake*, calling this the "merry go raum" (from the German *raum*, meaning "space"), where "the room is actually going around, and in that space one feels like a child, though one has come out somewhere in eternity."

### Authenticity Through Terror

McKenna noted that DMT "sometimes inspires fear," and argued that this fear marks the experience as "existentially authentic." He distinguished between genuine psychedelic encounters and compounds that "paralyze the ability to be terrified." The terror indicates that "the rules of pharmacology do not really apply and that control of existence on that plane is really a matter of focus of will and good luck."

He explicitly stated: "I'm not saying that there's something intrinsically good about terror. I'm saying that, granted the situation, if one is not terrified then one must be somewhat out of contact with the full dynamics of what is happening."

### Cognitive Dissonance

When encountering these translinguistic objects, McKenna described a cognitive dissonance in the "hindbrain": "We try to pour language over it and it sheds it like water off a duck's back. We try again and fail again." This failure to categorize the experience through ordinary language produces the "wow" or "flutter" that causes "wonder, astonishment, and awe at the brink of terror."

## The Logos Hypothesis

McKenna proposed that DMT and psilocybin "invoke the Logos," meaning they "work directly on the language centers, so that an important aspect of the experience is the interior dialogue." This distinguishes tryptamines from other hallucinogens. While LSD and mescaline are phenomenologically distinct, "psilocybin and DMT invoke the Logos, although DMT is more intense and more brief in its action."

This led him to suggest that human language may be "the shadow of this ability" or that this translinguistic capacity "will be a further extension of language." He speculated about a future human language "in which the intent of meaning is actually beheld in three-dimensional space," suggesting that if such a thing is accessible under DMT, it is "at least, under some circumstances, accessible to human beings."

## Glossolalia and Primal Language

DMT-induced glossolalia differs from the classical religious speaking in tongues. Classical glossolalia leaves practitioners asking "Did I do it? Did I speak in tongues?" In contrast, the DMT phenomenon is "simply a brain state that allows the expression of the assembly language that lies behind language." McKenna connected this to several historical concepts of primal language:

- Robert Graves's concept in *The White Goddess* of a primal poetic language
- The Kabbalistic primal "Ursprache" described in the *Zohar*
- A "primal language of the sort" that "comes out of oneself"

The experiencer discovers they can "make the extra-dimensional objects" through vocalization, feeling "like a child" playing with colored balls, becoming the Aeon.

## Prevalence and Cross-Cultural Reports

McKenna noted the "total absence of the motifs of DMT" in any religious or artistic tradition familiar to him. He found this puzzling: "It is not there; it is not there in any tradition." This absence contrasts with the rich iconographic traditions surrounding other psychedelic experiences, such as the parallels between LSD experiences and Mahayana Buddhist art that initially drove McKenna's interest in psychedelics.

Subsequent research has documented consistent reports of entity encounters across thousands of DMT experiences. Rick Strassman's [[mckenna-stropharia-cubensis-extraterrestrial-hypothesis-spore-dispersal]] "is a symbiote, and it desires ever deeper symbiosis with the human species." He proposed it achieved early symbiosis through association with domesticated cattle and human nomads, so that "where human genes went these other genes would be carried." The mushroom, in this view, uses human civilization as a vehicle for its own dispersal, offering expanded consciousness in exchange for propagation.
