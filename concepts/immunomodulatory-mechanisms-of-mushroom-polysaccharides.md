# Immunomodulatory Mechanisms of Mushroom Polysaccharides

Mushroom-derived polysaccharides exert their antitumour effects primarily through modulation of the host immune system rather than direct cytotoxicity. This concept explores the detailed immunological mechanisms by which these compounds recognize, target, and destroy cancer cells.

## The Immune System and Cancer

### Host Defense Against Tumours
"Tumours may develop when transformed cells escape immunological host defence mechanisms." The increased incidence of spontaneous tumours in immunosuppressed individuals indicates that the immune system provides a significant mechanism for host resistance against cancer.

### Two Lines of Defense

#### Innate Immunity
The first line in host defence, including natural killer (NK) cells, macrophages, and cytokines such as interferons and interleukins targeted toward destroying cancer cells. "These may themselves successfully eliminate infected or transformed cells prior to the establishment of fully-fledged humoral and CMI responses."

#### Acquired (Specific) Immunity
Includes:
- **Humoral immunity**: Generates antibodies
- **Cell-mediated immunity (CMI)**: Promotes inflammatory responses and ultimately kills infected or abnormal cells

### The Role of Immunomodulation
"As a fully functional immune response is critical to the recognition and elimination of tumour cells, the identification of mushroom-derived compounds that are capable of stimulating components of innate or acquired immunities may be of potential benefit for [[clinical-trials-of-mushroom-polysaccharides-in-cancer-treatment]]."

## Why Polysaccharides Are Effective Immunomodulators

### Structural Diversity
"The ability of bioactive polysaccharides and polysaccharide-bound proteins to modulate so many important immune cells may be due to the structural diversity and variability of these macromolecules."

### Information Carrying Capacity
"Polysaccharides offer the highest capacity for carrying biological information because they have the greatest potential for structural variability."

- **Four different sugar monomers**: Can form up to **35,560 unique tetrasaccharides**
- **Four different amino acids**: Can form only **24 different permutations**

"This enormous potential variability in polysaccharide structure gives the necessary flexibility for the precise regulatory mechanisms of various cell-cell interactions in higher organisms."

## Mechanism of Action

### T-Cell Orientation
"Lentinan is in a unique class of **T-cell-oriented assistants**, in which macrophages play some part."

### Macrophage Requirement
"The effect of Lentinan was inhibited by anti-macrophage agents such as carrageenan." This demonstrates that macrophage activation is essential for Lentinan's antitumour activity.

### NK Cell Activation
"Lentinan can activate NK-cells **in vitro** in the same concentrations that are achieved in the blood plasma of patients treated clinically with Lentinan." NK cell activity is involved in tumour suppression.

### T-Helper Cell Stimulation
NK cells are **strong T-helper cell stimulants** both in vitro and in vivo. This creates a cascade of immune activation.

### Suppressor T-Cell Inhibition
"Lentinan can inhibit prostaglandin synthesis, which can slow T-cell differentiation in animals and humans, as well as inhibiting suppressor T-cell activity in vivo."

### LAK Cell Enhancement
"Using the blood of healthy donors and [[cannabis-for-cancer-patients]], Lentinan has been shown to stimulate peripheral blood lymphocytes in vitro to increase interleukin-2-mediated LAK-cell (lymphokine-activated killer cell) and NK cell activity."

### Clinical Synergy
"Lentinan has been shown to inhibit suppressor T-cell activity in vivo and to increase the ratio of activated T cells and cytotoxic T cells in the spleen when administered to [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-psk-gastric-cancer]] patients undergoing chemotherapy."

## Additional Biological Activities of Lentinan

- **Acute phase [[fungal-single-cell-protein-production]]**: Increased non-specific inflammatory response
- **Vascular dilation**: Haemorrhage-inducing factor in vivo
- **Helper and cytotoxic T cell generation and activation**
- **Enhanced phagocytosis**: Macrophage-mediated tumour cell destruction

## The Immunomodulation Cascade

The orchestrated immune response triggered by mushroom polysaccharides follows this general pattern:

1. **Recognition**: Polysaccharide binds to specific receptors on immune cells (macrophages, NK cells)
2. **Activation**: Immune cells become activated and release cytokines
3. **Amplification**: Cytokines (IL-1, IL-2, IFN, TNF) recruit and activate additional immune cells
4. **T-Cell Response**: Helper T cells coordinate the immune attack; cytotoxic T cells directly kill tumour cells
5. **NK Cell Activity**: NK cells independently recognize and destroy abnormal cells
6. **Macrophage Attack**: Activated macrophages phagocytose tumour cells and present antigens
7. **Memory**: The immune system develops memory against specific tumour antigens

## Key Immune Cells Involved

### Macrophages
- First responders; phagocytose abnormal cells
- Present tumour antigens to T cells
- Release pro-inflammatory cytokines
- Essential intermediate in Lentinan activity

### Natural Killer (NK) Cells
- Directly kill tumour cells without prior sensitization
- Activated by mushroom polysaccharides at clinically achievable concentrations
- Strong T-helper cell stimulants

### T-Helper Cells
- Coordinate overall immune response
- Release cytokines that activate other immune cells
- Enhanced by NK cell stimulation

### Cytotoxic T Cells
- Directly kill tumour cells
- Activated by T-helper cells
- Ratio increased by Lentinan in cancer patients

### Suppressor T Cells
- Normally downregulate immune responses
- Inhibited by mushroom polysaccharides
- Inhibition allows stronger anti-tumour response

## Implications for Cancer Therapy

The immunomodulatory approach offers several advantages over direct cytotoxic therapy:

1. **Selectivity**: The immune system can distinguish between normal and abnormal cells
2. **Memory**: Long-term protection against recurrence
3. **Synergy**: Works alongside conventional chemotherapy and radiotherapy
4. **Toxicity profile**: Generally lower toxicity than cytotoxic drugs
5. **Quality of life**: Immune enhancement improves overall patient wellbeing
6. **Resistance**: Less likely to develop drug resistance since multiple immune pathways are activated simultaneously
