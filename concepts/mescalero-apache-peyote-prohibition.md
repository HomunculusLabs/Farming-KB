# Mescalero Apache Peyote Prohibition

## Overview

The Mescalero Apache prohibition of peyote represents one of the most psychologically
nuanced cases of a culture abandoning a psychoactive substance within its shamanistic
tradition. Unlike many Native American groups who adopted peyote into ceremonial life,
the Mescaleros — along with the Chiricahuas and Lipans — not only abandoned peyote in
legitimate shamanistic rituals but came to classify the plant as intrinsically evil.
This prohibition is rooted in the intersection of Apache personality dynamics,
child-rearing practices, the social structure of shamanic authority, and the
specific effects of hallucinogens on repression of hostile impulses.

## Rorschach Personality Profiles: Shamans vs. Pseudoshamans

The foundation for understanding peyote prohibition lies in the psychological
differences between genuine Apache shamans and pseudoshamans, as documented through
Rorschach testing by Klopfer and analyzed within Devereux's framework.

### Shamans

Apache shamans displayed personality profiles that, when viewed heteroculturally,
more nearly approached normality than those of their culture mates. Key
characteristics included:

- **Hysterical signs** coupled with heightened awareness of peculiarities and more
  selective theoretical interest in handling data.
- **Creative characteristics** — the capacity for imaginative and original thought.
- **High degree of reality testing potential** — the ability to maintain contact with
  external reality despite engaging in altered states of consciousness.
- **Capacity to regress in the service of the ego** — the psychologically healthy
  ability to voluntarily enter regressed or dissociative states (such as trance) and
  return from them intact, using those states productively.

Shamans were able to use imposture convincingly, suggesting sophisticated social
intelligence. Cross-culturally, Boyer (1964b) concluded that shamans share these
personality configurations across diverse societies.

### Pseudoshamans

In sharp contrast, pseudoshaman personalities were described as strikingly different:

- **Non-hysterical** — lacking the productive dissociative capacity seen in genuine
  practitioners.
- **Variable degrees of reality testing potential** — inconsistent ability to
  distinguish internal experience from external reality.
- **Impoverished personalities** — lacking the creative and adaptive qualities of
  true shamans.

Pseudoshamans could not use imposture convincingly. They were generally scorned by
the community and employed only by the most suggestible individuals.

## Apache Child-Rearing and the Dynamics of Hostility

Apache child-rearing practices engendered considerable hostility in individuals.
Aggression was addressed institutionally toward designated out-group targets —
outsiders, witches, ghosts, and cultural bogies — in an attempt to produce individual
repression of hostile impulses that were originally directed toward familial and
societal members (parent and sibling surrogates).

This mechanism of redirecting aggression outward was more effective aboriginally but
was never strikingly successful. The repression of hostile impulses toward intimate
community members remained fundamentally unstable.

## The Peyote Camp Crisis

The introduction of peyote into Apache shamanistic practice created conditions that
destabilized this already fragile system of repression. Two critical factors converged:

### Simultaneous Assumption of Authority

The peyote camps introduced a foreign element into Apache shamanistic procedure:
the **simultaneous assumption of authority by more than one practitioner**. In
standard Apache shamanistic practice, authority was individually held and exercised.
At peyote meetings, multiple practitioners vied for supremacy of power and status,
creating competitive tensions absent from traditional one-on-one shaman-patient
relationships.

### Pharmacological Reduction of Repression

The physiopsychological effects of the hallucinogen reduced the efficacy of the
practitioners' repression of hostilities rooted in their socialization experiences.
Drug-induced regression caused them to release aggression in its earlier, childish
form — directly toward parent and sibling surrogates present in the camp setting.

The combination of these factors produced bloodshed and feuds. The Apache community
responded by banning peyote camps entirely.

## The Ascription of Evil to Peyote

The prohibition was reinforced through a significant conceptual act: the ascription
of intrinsic evil to peyote (power). This was a basic deviation from the general
Apache conceptualization of supernatural power, which lacked intrinsic properties
of good or evil.

### Projection of Intragroup Hostility

This classification served a psychological function: it was intended to **deny the
presence of intragroup hostility**. By attributing the aggression and conflict that
erupted at peyote camps to an external evil agent (the plant itself), the community
could avoid confronting the reality that the hostility came from within their own
members.

### Parallel with Owl Power

It is no coincidence that only peyote and owl power were considered evil in
themselves among the Mescaleros. In each instance, murderous wishes are projected
onto the power in question. After prohibition, peyote was employed by possessors of
supernatural power solely in witchcraft rituals, alongside owl power and love magic
practices.

## Two Dimensions of Fear

The Mescaleros, Chiricahuas, and Lipans articulated two stated reasons for fearing
peyote use:

### 1. Evil Power Driving the User to Evil

The first fear — that peyote has an evil power which will drive them to do evil —
represents a **projection of intrapersonal asocial tendencies** onto the substance.
Sexual transgressions arouse little overt anxiety among these Apaches except when
intergenerational incest has occurred, but they deeply fear their poorly controlled
aggressive impulses. The plant becomes a vessel for disowned destructive urges.

### 2. Hallucinations Reducing Perceptual Accuracy

The second fear — that peyote causes hallucinations and reduces the capacity to
perceive and judge external reality accurately — involves fear of visual aberrations
and strange qualities of movement. This appears to be a **displacement**: fear of
loss of control over destructive urges is transposed into fear of loss of control
over perceptual accuracy. The two fears are psychologically linked.
