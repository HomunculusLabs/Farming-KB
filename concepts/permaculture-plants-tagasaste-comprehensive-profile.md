# Tagasaste (Chamaecytisus palmensis): Comprehensive Profile

Tagasaste, also known as tree lucerne, is a nitrogen-fixing leguminous tree
native to the Canary Islands and one of the most useful pioneer species in
[[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]]. It serves multiple functions simultaneously: stock feed,
bee forage, nurse crop, [[free-range-poultry-forage-design-mollison]], firewood, and soil improvement. Its
remarkable productivity on marginal land has made it a cornerstone of
[[biochar]] in Australia, where over 50,000 hectares of mostly
marginal farmland had been planted to tagasaste by 1996. (see [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]]).

## Botanical Profile

- **Family**: Fabaceae (Papilionaceae)
- **Scientific name**: Chamaecytisus palmensis
- **Common names**: Tagasaste, tree lucerne
- **Origin**: Canary Islands
- **Height**: Up to 7 meters
- **Lifespan**: Moderately short-lived; regular cutting or browsing
  considerably extends life expectancy
- **Rainfall tolerance**: As low as 300 mm annually
- **Drought response**: Improved with readily available phosphorus

## Soil and Establishment

Tagasaste thrives on a wide range of soils, including near-useless sandy soils
capable of yielding only 700 kg of wheat per hectare. Liberal application of
rock phosphate at planting time works well. The nitrogen-fixing bacteria
generally does not need introduction with the plant, as it is usually present
in the soil from other legume species including Acacia spp, Albizia spp, and
Cassia spp. (see [[permplant-tagasaste-pioneer-fodder-tree]]).

## Nutritional Value as Stock Feed

Tagasaste foliage compares favorably with conventional stock feeds:

| Constituent | Tagasaste | Lucerne | Rye Grass | Wheat Grain | Field Pea |
|-------------|-----------|---------|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Crude Protein | 17-31% | 21% | 16% | 11% | 26% |
| Carbohydrate | 38-58% | 40% | 46% | 82% | 63% |
| Crude Fat | 2-6% | 3% | 4% | 2% | 1% |
| Crude Fibre | 14-30% | 26% | 24% | 3% | 7% |
| Calcium (Ca) | 0.48-1.62% | 2.2% | 1.0% | 0.04% | 0.24% |
| Phosphorus (P) | 0.12-0.41% | 0.33% | 0.26% | 0.24% | 0.24% |

The crude protein range of 17-31% is exceptional, exceeding lucerne at the
upper end. Low toxicity and high palatability make it readily accepted by
sheep, cattle, horses, pigs, and rabbits.

## Field Trial Results

Research at the Martindale Research Project in Western Australia demonstrated
striking results on marginal sandy soils:

- Tagasaste yielded the feed equivalent of 1,100 kg of wheat on soil that
  could produce only 700 kg of wheat directly.
- Ovulation rate and twinning rate of ewes on tagasaste were significantly
  higher than flock mates on dry pasture or lupin stubble.
- 45 hectares of tagasaste easily fed 650 ewes (in lamb) for 56 days over
  summer, increasing average live weight from 51 to 52 kg.
- Ewes on tagasaste over summer/autumn grew 30% more wool with the same
  yield and slightly broader micron.
- One farmer reported profit increasing from $6/ha to $30/ha.

## Stock Carrying Capacity

At a planting density of 1,000 trees per hectare, tagasaste's edible yield
escalates dramatically as the stand matures (tonnes dry matter per hectare):

| Year | Tagasaste Yield | Pasture Yield |
|------|----------------|---------------|
| 1 | — | 5.5 t |
| 2 | 2.7 t | 5.5 t |
| 3 | 5.4 t | 4.0 t |
| 4 | 9.4 t | 2.0 t |
| 5 | 16 t | 1.0 t |
| 6 | 19 t | 1.0 t |

Stocking rates (sheep per hectare) reach 35 on tagasaste alone by year six,
or 37 when combined with pasture—compared to just 1 sheep per hectare on
degraded pasture alone. Combined tagasaste and pasture systems provide useful
grazing from year one.
