--- Edible Agarics
The Agaricaceae constitute the majority of conspicuous fleshy fungi,
with more than five thousand species described. They are characterized
by gills on the underside of the pileus, which constitute the
spore-bearing surface or hymenium. Several genera of agarics are
important for their edible species.

## Black-Spored Agarics

### Agaricus

The genus *Agaricus* includes the most important commercially
cultivated species. *A. campestris*, the Field Mushroom, and
*A. arvensis*, the Horse Mushroom, are the two most widely cultivated.
*A. fabaceus* (*A. subrufescens*) is the almond-flavored mushroom found
in greenhouses and flower beds, requiring more completely fermented
compost and higher temperature. *A. placomyces* is a woodland species
found from early summer until late fall, with a large, flat, thin cap.
*A. silvicola*, an inhabitant of woods, is almost pure white.
The commercial [[growing-gourmet-button-mushroom-agaricus-cultivation]]) is the most
widely cultivated fungus worldwide, descended from wild Agaricus
species originally foraged in open fields and meadows.

### Coprinus (Ink Caps)

The genus *Coprinus* is characterized by the deliquescence of gills
and other parts to an inky black liquid when fully mature.
*C. comatus*, the [[bloomfield-coprine-disulfiram-alcohol-reaction-coprinus-atramentarius]]* causes severe reactions when
consumed with alcohol due to coprine, a compound that inhibits
acetaldehyde dehydrogenase. This interaction can persist for several
days after mushroom consumption.

## White-Spored Agarics

### Lepiota

The genus *Lepiota* resembles *Agaricus* but possesses white spores.
*L. procera*, the Parasol Mushroom, is perhaps the most conspicuous of
the edible agarics, with a tall stem and large cap. *L. naucinoides*
is a smaller but also edible species. Both occur in lawns, fields, and
meadows.
Caution: Some Lepiota species (particularly small ones) contain deadly
amatoxins. Only large Lepiota species with distinctive rings should be
considered for consumption, and even these should be identified by
experienced foragers.

### Tricholoma (Blewitts)

This large genus embraces species with caps typically two to five inches
in diameter, white or slightly dingy gills, no ring, and no basal cup.
Only a single species among more than 200 has been marked as suspicious.
*T. personatum*, the Masked Tricholoma, and *T. nudum*, the Naked
Tricholoma, are both edible and found on European and Asian markets.
*T. personatum* has been successfully cultivated on well-fermented
leaves.

### Amanita

The genus *Amanita* contains the most deadly poisonous species, but
also some excellent edible ones. The identification of the volva (cup
at the stem base) and annulus (ring on the stem) is essential.
