# Alley Cropping and Contour Hedgerow Systems

Alley cropping, also known as hedgerow intercropping, is an agroforestry
practice that integrates rows of perennial woody plants with annual or
perennial crops in the alleys between them. Mollison treats alley cropping as
a bridge between broadscale agriculture and permanent food forest systems,
offering a transition pathway that maintains crop production while building
soil, controlling erosion, and establishing the perennial framework of a
future [[mollison-designers-keyline-pattern-plow-technique]] methods and [[mollison-designers-swale-spacing-and-contour-interval]] design.
- **Competition management**: Tree rows and crop alleys must be managed to
  minimise competition for light, water, and nutrients. Root pruning, shoot
  pruning, and species selection all contribute to balancing tree and crop
  productivity.
- **Multiple functions**: Hedgerow species are selected for nitrogen fixation,
  mulch production, fodder, timber, or fruit production, not just soil
  conservation. Every element performs multiple functions per the core
  permaculture principle.
- **Progressive perennialisation**: Over time, tree rows can be widened or
  additional species added, gradually converting the system from annual crop
  dominance to perennial polyculture. See
  [[mollison-designers-coppicing-management-of-legume-trees]].
- **Calliandra calothyrsus**: High-altitude tropical species tolerating
  acidic soils; excellent fodder and mulch producer; fixes 100-200 kg N/ha/yr.
- **Sesbania sesban**: Fast-establishing short-lived species for the first
  years of system establishment; excellent green manure; tolerates waterlogging.
- **Flemingia macrophylla**: Shade-tolerant understory nitrogen fixer; good
  for adding to existing systems; produces persistent mulch.

### Hedgerow Species for Temperate Climates
Temperate alley cropping uses different species adapted to cold winters and
deciduous growth patterns:
- **Tagasaste (Chamaecytisus proliferus)**: Fast-growing evergreen nitrogen
  fixer; excellent poultry and livestock fodder; drought-tolerant once
  established. Requires well-drained soils and frost protection when young.
- **Black locust (Robinia pseudoacacia)**: Extremely hardy nitrogen fixer;
  produces durable timber and excellent firewood; foliage is good fodder
  (contains some anti-nutritional factors but palatable to livestock).
  See [[mollison-designers-keyline-pattern-plow-technique]] cultivation
  between hedgerows to sever shallow roots without deep soil disturbance.
- Selection of species with naturally deep root systems that minimise surface
  root competition.

## Practical Considerations

Successful implementation of Alley Cropping and Contour Hedgerow Systems requires attention to
several practical factors including environmental conditions,
resource availability, and timing. Careful monitoring and
adaptive management help optimize outcomes across varying
conditions. Integration with other system elements enhances
overall effectiveness and creates beneficial synergies that
improve resilience and productivity over time.
