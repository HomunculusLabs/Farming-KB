# Permaculture Species Selection for Urban Environments
Selecting the right plants for [[pdc-urban-permaculture-design-principles]] requires understanding which
species perform multiple functions, tolerate urban conditions, and fit into
small-space polyculture systems. The species listed here are drawn from Bill
Mollison's comprehensive tagasaste (Chamaecytisus palmensis) -- [[permplant-carob-drought-tolerant-food-tree]], protein-rich fodder,
  bee forage, excellent cut mulch, windbreak, nurse plant for frost-sensitive
  trees
- Siberian pea shrub (Caragana spp.) -- very cold and wind hardy, poultry
  forage from seeds, windbreak and hedge for cold climates
- Autumn olive (Elaeagnus umbellata) -- nitrogen-fixing, drought tolerant,
  wildlife habitat, edible berries
- Mesquite (Prosopis spp.) -- extremely drought resistant, [[halotolerant-halophilic-fungi-salt-tolerant-diversity]],
  pods high in sugar and protein, coppices for firewood
## Shrubs and Bushes

### Fruit Bushes

- Gooseberries (Ribes spp.) -- tolerate partial shade, neglect-tolerant,
  10-20 year bearing life, grow in rock crevices
- Blueberry -- ornamental, shade-tolerant, acid soil
- Elderberry (Sambucus spp.) -- hedgerow shrub, windbreak, wine and conserves
  from berries
- Hawthorn (Crataegus spp.) -- tough, thorny, long-lived (100-300 years),
  hedgerow, wildlife habitat, bee forage
- [[dynamic-accumulators]] and Soil Builders

- Comfrey (Symphytum spp.) -- deep-rooted, mines potassium, excellent bee
  forage, stock fodder, nutrient-rich "manure tea," mulch material. Any part
  of the root crown will grow. High potash content makes it valuable for
  fruiting plants
- Chicory (Cichorium intybus) -- deep taproot mines minerals, early and long
  flowering bee forage, roots roasted as coffee substitute, mineral-rich
  leaves for pasture
- Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) -- drought resistant, insectary, medicinal
  for livestock
- Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale) -- early and long flowering bee forage,
  high pollen yield, all parts edible, forage crop improves milk quality
### [[perennial-vegetables]]

- Asparagus (Asparagus officinalis) -- yields well for 20+ years, naturalises
  along watercourses, bank stabiliser
- Globe artichoke (Cynara scolymus) -- perennial, ornamental, edible buds
- Good king henry (Chenopodium bonus-henricus) -- young plant eaten like
  asparagus and spinach
- Daylily -- edible flowers for salads, ornamental
## Climbing and Vining Plants

### Edible Vines

- Grape (Vitis spp.) -- long-lived, deciduous vine, fresh fruit, dried
  (raisins), wine, juice, cooking oil from seeds. Deciduous vines block
  summer sun from houses
- Kiwifruit (Actinidia chinensis) -- deciduous climber, delicious fruit,
  shade vine for pergolas and patios
- Passionfruit (Passiflora spp.) -- vigorous evergreen perennial, edible
  fruit, poultry fodder, sun deflector to shade walls, ornamental flowers
- Scarlet runner bean (Phaseolus coccineus) -- perennial in mild climates,
  edible pods and beans, shade from bright red flowers
- Choko/chayote (Sechium edule) -- perennial vine, edible fruit and tubers,
  smothering cover for less vigorous plants
### Wildlife and Utility Vines

- Virginia creeper -- ornamental, wildlife habitat
- Wisteria -- ornamental, shade
- Hops (Humulus lupulus) -- long-lived (80-100 years), beer flavouring,
  pillow filling, mild narcotic, browsed by sheep and geese when young
## Ground Covers and Green Manures

- Clover (Trifolium spp.) -- [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]], bee forage, living mulch
- Lucerne/alfalfa (Medicago sativa) -- 10-year perennial, human food (tea,
  sprouts), major fodder, bee forage, soil improver drawing up subsoil nutrients
- Vetch (Vicia spp.) -- nitrogen fixation, [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]] on slopes
- Buckwheat -- phosphorus accumulator, suppresses weed growth, bee forage,
  quick cover crop
- [[comparison-cassava-vs-taro-vs-sweet-potato-vs-yam]] (Ipomoea batatas) -- perennial groundcover for orchards, staple
  food, livestock fodder, dies back in frost
