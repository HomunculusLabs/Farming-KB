# Egyptian Mushroom Temple Architecture and Iconography

The temples, tombs, and artistic monuments of ancient Egypt contain extensive mushroom symbolism that has been documented by ethnomycological researchers including James Arthur and others. From the distinctive shapes of temple pillars to the hieroglyphic representations of mushrooms, the architectural and artistic record of ancient Egypt reveals a complex symbolic vocabulary centered on entheogenic fungi, particularly Amanita muscaria and Psilocybe species.

This connects to the broader [[egyptian-mushroom-symbolism]] and the traditions of [[amanita-in-ancient-religion]].

## Temple Pillars as Mushroom Representations

Arthur identifies the massive pillars found at every major Egyptian temple as deliberately shaped like giant mushrooms. Some pillars resemble the Amanita muscaria with their broad, rounded capitals, while others approximate the more slender profile of Psilocybe species. Still others take the form of tree fungus and are decorated with pictures of an incredible variety of plants.

The pillar tops (capitals) at many temples, when examined closely, depict mushroom imagery. Arthur documents one pillar top that shows clear mushroom shapes with remarkable similarity to an 11th-century painting of Jesus with four mushroom-type images across the bottom. This cross-cultural consistency in mushroom depiction across a thousand years of artistic tradition suggests a persistent symbolic tradition.

The Egyptians, described by Arthur as "the ultimate Alchemists" and known as "Al Khemitians" in their own tradition, are proposed to have revered thousands of plants in their temple artwork, with the mushroom holding a central position.

## The Djed Pillar

The Djed pillar, one of the most important symbols in Egyptian religion, is associated with the Cedars of Lebanon and with Osiris, god of the underworld, the moon, plants, and vegetation. While some Egyptologists interpret the Djed as a representation of a sheaf of wheat or a spinal column, Arthur identifies it as a mushroom symbol.

The mythology surrounding the Djed supports this identification. After Set murdered Osiris and cut his body into thirteen pieces, Isis recovered and reassembled all the parts except the penis. Using magic, she fashioned a replacement from a living tree (or part of a tree) and used it to impregnate herself on December 21st (winter solstice). Osiris lay dead for three days as the sun stopped its north-south motion, and on December 25th Isis gave birth to Horus.

In an alternative version of the myth, Set placed Osiris in a coffin and floated it down the Nile. Isis found him grown into (or encased within) a Cedar tree, which became the Djed. The Djed was used in fertility rites and rites of passage for young females as a practice phallus. This mythological complex connects the Djed to mushroom symbolism through its associations with fertility, death and rebirth, the number thirteen (the pieces of Osiris), and the December 25th birth date that parallels numerous other mushroom-associated solar deities.

## The Ankh as Mushroom Symbol

The Ankh, the Egyptian symbol of life, is identified by Arthur as representing the mushroom and its extracted juices. The Ankh means "waters and life" or "the waters of life," which is interpreted as the juice extracted from the mushroom for sacramental use.

The Ankh is frequently depicted being poured out of vessels in streams, traditionally interpreted as water. However, Arthur argues that it is not ordinary water but water with magical properties of eternal life, the psychoactive juice of the entheogenic mushroom. In temple reliefs, the Ankh is often shown being pushed into the mouth of a recipient (such as Horus) by a deity (such as Amon), representing the administration of the sacramental substance.

A composite symbol combining the Ankh and the Djed provides a key to their shared meaning: both symbols represent the same thing, or possibly two different types of mushrooms. The Djed that grows under the Cedar tree and the Ankh representing the mushroom and the cross both point toward the entheogenic sacrament.

## Mushroom Hieroglyphs

Arthur documents specific hieroglyphs that appear to directly represent mushrooms. One temple wall, buried for thousands of years and still retaining paint remnants, clearly shows two mushroom shapes, one red and one blue, which Arthur identifies as Amanita muscaria and a Psilocybe species respectively.

A single mushroom hieroglyph is documented at the exit of an underground tunnel used in death and rebirth rites of passage. After passing through the tunnel, which symbolizes the womb, the initiate would emerge having completed a profound initiation. The presence of a mushroom symbol at this liminal point reinforces its role as the central sacrament of the Egyptian death-and-rebirth mysteries.

## The Celestial Food (Tchefau)

The Egyptian Book of the Dead contains extensive references to a "celestial food" or "divine food" that the gods consume and share with the initiated. The deceased pharaoh, in his journey through the afterlife, searches for this food continuously, repeating the question "what is it?" at every step.

E.A. Wallace Budge identifies "tchefau" as the name of the food on which the gods lived, comparing it to the Ambrosia and Nectar of Olympus. The gods Hu and Saa, associated with this food, were created from drops of blood that fell to the ground when Ra cut off his phallus, a creation myth that parallels numerous mushroom origin stories involving blood, spores, and emergence from the earth.

Arthur connects the Egyptian tchefau to the Sumerian "shem-an-na," the Greek ambrosia, and the Vedic soma as all being names for the same entheogenic mushroom substance. The pharaoh who eats of the "tree of life" and wears the white linen of the gods is described as achieving immortality through this celestial food, a direct parallel to the effects attributed to entheogenic mushrooms in shamanic traditions worldwide.

## Amon and the Initiation of Horus

A specific temple relief depicts Amon (with a feather crown) holding the arm of Horus while pushing the Ankh into his mouth. When viewed head-on, Horus appears to have his mouth closed. When the viewer steps around the pillar to the left, an optical effect makes it appear that Horus opens his mouth wide to receive the sacrament. His consort Amaunet accompanies Amon, and the lion-headed goddess Sekhmet accompanies Horus, ready for his journey into Amenta (the underworld).

This relief encodes the moment of sacramental administration, with the mushroom (Ankh) being placed in the mouth of the initiate (Horus) by the presiding deity (Amon), with protective deities present for the visionary journey that follows.

## Osiris and the December 25th Tradition

The Osiris mythology contains elements that directly parallel the Christmas mushroom traditions. Osiris circles the sky in the Big Dipper constellation (his chariot), the same constellation identified as the chariot of Odin, Thor, Wotan, and King Arthur. His traditional birth date is December 25th, the same date celebrated for the birth of Horus, Jesus, Mithra, and numerous other solar deities associated with mushroom symbolism.

The celebration of Osiris's birth involved placing presents around the Cedar tree, a tradition at least five thousand years old that directly prefigures both the Christmas tree tradition and the mushroom-gathering practices of Siberian shamanism.

## The Al Khemitian Tradition

Arthur consistently refers to the ancient Egyptians as "Al Khemitians," using what he presents as their own name for their civilization. The Al Khemitian tradition, as Arthur describes it, was one of advanced spiritual and technological knowledge that revered thousands of plants in its temple artwork. The mushroom held a central position in this pharmacopoeia, as evidenced by its prominent depiction in temple architecture, hieroglyphic writing, and funerary art.

The Al Khemitians are described as "the ultimate Alchemists," a characterization supported by the sophisticated chemical knowledge evident in their embalming practices, metallurgy, and pharmaceutical preparations. The identification of the prima materia and Philosopher's Stone of alchemy with the Amanita muscaria mushroom suggests that the alchemical tradition, which traces its origins to Egypt (Hermes Trismegistus), preserves the remnants of an Egyptian entheogenic knowledge tradition.

The destruction or suppression of this knowledge, whether through conquest, cultural collapse, or deliberate erasure, created the conditions for the same mushroom symbolism to persist in encoded form while the explicit knowledge of its meaning was lost. The temples, with their mushroom-shaped pillars and mushroom hieroglyphs, stand as monuments to a tradition that was deliberately hidden rather than completely destroyed.

## Comparative Connections

The Egyptian mushroom temple tradition connects to similar traditions in other cultures through shared symbolic elements. The Djed pillar connects to the Hindu lingam and the Christian cross through the common mushroom/phallus symbolism. The Ankh connects to the Holy Grail through the shared cup/chalice shape representing the mushroom cap. The celestial food (tchefau) connects to the Vedic Soma, the Greek ambrosia, and the Christian Manna through the shared identity as entheogenic mushroom preparations.

These cross-cultural connections support the theory of a common source tradition, an ancient entheogenic knowledge system that was encoded in symbolic form across multiple civilizations as it was transmitted through time. The Egyptian temple architecture, with its extraordinary preservation, provides some of the most direct evidence for this tradition's existence and nature.

## Merged: Sacred Mushroom and Secret Societies

# Sacred Mushroom and Secret Societies

James Arthur's "Mushrooms and Mankind" presents a provocative thesis that entheogenic knowledge — particularly regarding the [[sacred-mushroom-names-cross-cultural-synonyms]] — has been deliberately suppressed and hoarded by secret societies throughout history. Arthur argues that the true sacramental nature of many world religions traces back to mushroom use, and that this knowledge has been one of the most closely guarded secrets of initiatory orders from antiquity to the present day.

## The Egyptian Mystery Schools as Origin Point

Arthur identifies the Egyptian mystery schools as the primary source from which later secret societies descended. These schools gave birth to other secret initiatory orders such as the Rosicrucians, Freemasons, Templars, and more. These modern societies elaborate upon, and use as a model, "the tiers of learning" which the Egyptian mystery schools incorporated.

Later mystic societies perpetuated the systems, although in what Arthur describes as a degraded state. The actual pyramid was no longer used in the highest initiatory levels; a room replaced the pyramid and a simple coffin replaced the sarcophagus. Arthur suggests that those who still recognise the value of these original artifacts would not settle for the symbolic replacements.

## The Highest Initiation Rite

According to Arthur's interpretation, the highest rite of initiation in the Egyptian system was to be accomplished with the assistance of what he calls the "multi-dimensional effects of the pyramid" in conjunction with the "multi-dimensional effects of the [[amanita-muscaria-in-eastern-religions]] unique form of the death and rebirth experience."

The sarcophagus within the central chamber of the Great Pyramid was a key element. Arthur notes that the word "sarcophagus" literally translates as "flesh-eating," and since the mushroom is referred to as flesh in most ancient traditions, this linguistic connection is significant. The initiate would lie down in the sarcophagus, consume the sacred flesh, and drink of the waters of life.

## The Three-Day Death and Resurrection Ordeal

Arthur describes the pyramid ritual as a three-day ordeal. The initiate was placed in a death-like trance induced by the Amanita muscaria. During this state, the initiate descended into Hades to do the work of lifting up lost souls through ministering in charity and good deeds.

After three days of this death and resurrection ordeal, the initiate was taken at the close of the third night to the entrance of a gallery. At sunrise, the beams of the rising sun struck the face of the entranced initiate, who was given "passage to the heavens," initiated by the god Osiris, and became "as a god himself."

Arthur cites Manley P. Hall and Peter Tompkins, who described how the candidate "passed through the mystic passageways and chambers of the great pyramid, entering its portals as men and coming forth as Gods." Having personally experienced the Amanita muscaria's effects, Arthur claims he can "see plainly the truth behind this initiatory rite."

## Freemasonry and the Hoarding of Knowledge

Arthur is particularly critical of Freemasonry and similar secret societies. He argues that revealing the secrets of entheogens — one of the most closely guarded secrets of Freemasonry and many other societies — can only be done by a layman (non-member), because if one has reached that level of initiation, they could not discuss it further due to oaths of secrecy.

Arthur describes personal encounters with High Priests and Bishops who, upon learning that Arthur knew about the entheogenic secrets, "were so blown away that it scared the hell out of them." He notes that these initiates invariably felt compelled to tell him that they did know what he was talking about — itself a possible violation of oath — but could not discuss it further.

## The Problem of Secrecy

Arthur's central critique is that secrecy itself is a problem of high proportional magnitude. He argues that the oaths and secret combinations are the basis for "that which is fanatical, control-oriented and destructive." Supposed truth, he contends, should be given to all for open debate; it is in this way that evolution is enhanced instead of inhibited.

The secret societies, in Arthur's view, strive to acquire information and technology, then hoard it for themselves. They systematically destroy all public remnants of this knowledge to keep it exclusive. This process is compared to Christians burning the libraries of the East — "after pillaging what they wanted" — in an attempt to erase knowledge from the public domain forever.

Arthur argues that this secrecy is an abomination that makes the rest of the world desolate. Just as revealing the truth would relinquish churches of their power over people, so too would governments and secret societies lose their power were the truth revealed.

## The Tiers of Learning and Bloodline Exclusion

Arthur describes the ancient temple order initiations as a gradual process of learning accompanied by levels of accomplishment intertwined with secret oaths, rewarded through the revealing of hidden knowledge. The process was typically a lifetime endeavour, with twenty-two years as the base period according to Masonic tradition.

This base period encompassed the study of sciences including mathematics, geometry, astronomy, and astrology — or more appropriately "astro-theology," concerned with astronomical cycles, the esoteric principles of man's evolution, and the mythology of the gods. However, Arthur claims that the Egyptian mystery schools were supremely exclusionist, and that the highest culmination ritual was more likely conferred upon those with the proper bloodline rather than those who rose through the ranks. The initiate pools, he suggests, were more likely servile knowledge gatherers than prospective candidates for the highest ceremony.
