# Hyphal Growth and Invasive Biology

## Overview

Hyphae are the fundamental cellular units of most fungi:
microscopic tubes that extend at their tips, branch
repeatedly, and form networks called mycelia.
[[fungal-hyphae-invasive-growth-mechanisms]] by filamentous hyphae is a uniquely
fungal capability that allows these organisms to
penetrate the toughest plant tissues, animal skin,
bone, and even granite bedrock.
A fungus that normally infects rice leaves will punch
holes through bulletproof vest material in its futile
search for nourishment. This invasive power makes
[[bloomfield-fungal-pathogens-and-parasites]] among the most destructive organisms
in agriculture and medicine.

## The Mycelium as Cooperative Organism

Individual mycelia can be enormous. An Armillaria specimen
in Oregon's Blue Mountains may cover more than 2,200 acres
and is estimated to be between 2,400 and 7,200 years old.
A Michigan specimen spreads over 15 hectares and equals
the mass of a blue whale. Armillaria holds the title of
World's [[armillaria-largest-organism]], outclassing quaking aspen
colonies in terms of living mass.
When food is plentiful, mycelia adopt a torus shape,
spreading outward and leaving dead cells at their core,
producing [[bloomfield-fairy-rings-armillaria-giant-fungi]]. Within the mycelium, nutrient
absorption in some regions stimulates growth toward
food-rich areas.
The fungus transports water and nutrients between
distant parts of the colony. The plasma membrane
functions as a continuous film throughout the mycelium,
creating a pipeline vulnerable to catastrophic injury
when membranes rupture.

## Invasive Growth and Enzymatic Degradation

### The Enzymatic Arsenal

Fungi secrete an astonishing array of enzymes. Cellulases
employ water molecules to break bonds between sugars in
cellulose. Proteases break proteins into amino acids.
Cutinases dissolve leaf cuticles. Lipases degrade fats.
Fungi can etch camera lens coatings, eat plastic fuel
injector linings, pit machine tool surfaces, and liquefy
wallpaper. A black mold works on a shampoo bottle in the
shower, ironically attacking the contents that are
supposed to possess antifungal properties.
Diana Davis found that a fungus killing grass on golf
courses and a related species feeding on marine algae
release massive quantities of proteases when fed animal
proteins. Although they operate as vegetarians in the
wild, they retain innate capability to consume animal
tissues.
