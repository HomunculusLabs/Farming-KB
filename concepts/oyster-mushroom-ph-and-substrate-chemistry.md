# Oyster Mushroom Substrate pH and Chemistry

Understanding substrate chemistry — particularly pH dynamics and nutritional composition — is critical for optimizing [[bulk-substrate-preparation]] and maximizing yields. This page covers pH requirements, nutritional profiles of common substrates, and the physio-chemical changes that occur during the cultivation cycle.

## pH Requirements

### Mycelial Growth Phase

The optimal substrate pH for mycelial growth is **5.0–6.5**, though mycelium can survive in a range of **pH 4.2–7.5**. Key observations:

- Growth slows progressively as pH drops below 5.0
- Growth stops entirely at **pH 4.0**
- Above-optimal pH accelerates growth but produces **abnormal mycelial structure**

### Fruiting Phase

Optimal pH for primordial induction and fruiting is **5.0–5.5**, though fruiting is possible in the wider range of **pH 5.5–7.8**. The more acidic range produces better quality fruitbodies.

### pH Adjustment

Substrate pH can be adjusted using:
- **Gypsum (CaSO₄):** Provides calcium without significantly raising pH; acts as a buffer
- **Lime (CaCO₃):** Raises pH; useful when substrate is too acidic
- Typical additive rates: 2% lime powder, 3–4% gypsum, 4–6% fodder chalk

### pH as a Harvest Indicator

The substrate pH drops throughout the cultivation cycle (see Table 2 below). When pH falls to **below 4.0**, this indicates it is time to **end the harvest**. This acidic threshold signals that the substrate is exhausted.

## Nutritional Composition of Staple Substrates

The following table shows the nutritional substance contents of common staple substrate materials used for oyster mushroom cultivation.

**Table 1. Nutritious substance contents of staple substrates**

| Material | Nitrogen (%) | | Hemicellulose (%) | Cellulose (%) | Lignin (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Total | Assimilable | | | |
| Wheat straw | 0.36 | 0.07 | 30.0 | 41.0 | 15.0 |
| Barley straw | 0.52 | 0.10 | 31.3 | 44.4 | 5.8 |
| Hardwood chips (poplar, beech, ash) | 0.57 | 0.04 | 15.4 | 16.7 | 26.0 |
| Corncobs | 0.49 | 0.06 | 38.0 | 28.0 | 11.0 |

### Key Observations from Nutritional Data

- **Barley straw** has the highest hemicellulose (31.3%) and cellulose (44.4%) with low lignin (5.8%), making it an excellent substrate for oyster mushrooms
- **Hardwood chips** have the highest lignin content (26.0%) and lowest assimilable nitrogen (0.04%), which is less favorable since oyster mushrooms poorly utilize lignin
- **Wheat straw** offers a balanced profile with moderate lignin (15.0%) and good cellulose content (41.0%)
- **Corncobs** have the highest hemicellulose (38.0%) with moderate cellulose (28.0%)

## Physio-Chemical Changes During Cultivation

**Table 2. Physio-chemical trend of substrate through the growing cycle**

| Substance | After Soakage | After Thermal Disinfection | After Second Flush |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen (%) | 0.80 | 0.72 | 0.79 |
| Hemicellulose (%) | 24.3 | 20.1 | 14.6 |
| Cellulose (%) | 33.1 | 33.5 | 22.8 |
| Lignin (%) | 5.8 | 7.0 | 6.5 |
| Phosphorus (total %) | 0.06 | 0.05 | 0.04 |
| Calcium (total %) | 6.64 | 7.40 | 8.95 |
| Potassium (total %) | 0.46 | 0.49 | 0.27 |
| pH | 6.6 | 7.5 | 4.8 |
| Water (fresh material %) | 73.8 | 72.4 | 71.3 |

## Key Insights from Physio-Chemical Trends

### Primary Nutrient Consumption

Oyster mushrooms primarily consume **cellulose and hemicellulose** as their main nourishment sources:

- **Hemicellulose** decreases significantly from 24.3% to 14.6% — the largest relative decline of any component
- **Cellulose** decreases substantially from 33.1% to 22.8% — representing the bulk of energy consumed
- **Lignin** is **rarely used** — it barely changes (5.8 → 6.5%), and actually increases slightly after thermal disinfection (7.0%) before decreasing marginally

### Critical Cultivation Implications

1. **Avoid fermentation that accumulates lignin.** Since oyster mushrooms cannot efficiently metabolize lignin, any substrate preparation method (particularly prolonged fermentation) that increases the proportion of lignin relative to cellulose/hemicellulose will reduce yield.
2. **Nitrogen is consumed only during incubation** (drops from 0.80% to 0.72%), not during fruiting. This aligns with the book's note that amino nitrogen is used during spawn run but is not suitable for fruiting.
3. **Calcium acts as a catalytic buffer**, increasing from 6.64% to 8.95% through the cycle. It helps maintain pH stability and supports enzyme function.
4. **pH drops from 6.6 to 4.8** after the second flush, reflecting the accumulation of organic acids from metabolic activity.
5. **Potassium decreases** from 0.46% to 0.27%, indicating it is consumed during fruiting.

## Substrate Preparation pH Targets

For optimal results, target the following pH at each stage:

| Stage | Target pH |
|---|---|
| Initial substrate (after mixing) | 6.0–7.0 |
| After pasteurization | 6.5–7.5 |
| During incubation | 5.0–6.5 |
| At fruiting induction | 5.0–5.5 |
| End of harvest (stop threshold) | < 4.0 |
