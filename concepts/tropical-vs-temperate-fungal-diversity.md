Distinct guilds of fungi are associated with dead bryophytes, algae, grasses, and other plant groups, suggesting that the diversity of fungi will be strongly positively correlated with the numbers of these plant groups.

### Endophyte Diversity

Tropical endophyte assemblages are consistently more diverse than temperate ones. The Xylariaceae dominate tropical endophyte communities, with many species restricted to tropical distributions. Tropical leaves often harbor dozens of endophyte species per leaf, compared to far fewer in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]]. The diversity of leaf epiphytes and inhabitants of living leaves is significantly greater in tropical than temperate areas (Dennis 1970).

### Macrofungal Diversity

Tropical forests produce macrofungal fruitings throughout the year (rather than concentrated seasonal flushes in temperate zones), providing more opportunities for documentation. The diversity of tropical macrofungi is believed to be substantially higher than in temperate forests, though systematic comparative data remain limited. Whether the species richness of wood-inhabiting fungi follows a latitudinal gradient, as is found in many other taxa, remains an open question. Studies in Puerto Rico have indicated that diameter class and microclimate are among the most important factors influencing wood-inhabiting macrofungal community structure.
