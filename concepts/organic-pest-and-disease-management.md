# Organic Pest and Disease Management

Organic pest management relies on prevention first, then biological controls, physical barriers, and only as a last resort, approved organic sprays. The goal is to manage pest populations, not eradicate them — some pest presence supports beneficial insect populations.

## Prevention: The First Line of Defense

### Healthy Soil, Healthy Plants
- Well-nourished plants resist [[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]] naturally
- Over-fed plants (excess nitrogen) produce soft, sappy growth that attracts aphids
- Balanced nutrition with adequate potassium and silica strengthens cell walls

### Crop Rotation
- Don't grow the same crop family in the same spot year after year
- Minimum 3-year rotation for most crops
- Prevents buildup of soil-borne diseases (clubroot, onion white rot)
- Breaks pest life cycles (carrot fly, cabbage root fly)

### Plant Diversity
- Mixed plantings confuse pests ( harder to find host plants)
- Interplant flowers to attract [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]]
- Avoid large monoculture blocks

### Good Hygiene
- Remove diseased plant material promptly (don't compost it)
- Clean tools between uses (wipe with alcohol)
- Remove crop debris at end of season
- Control weeds (they harbor pests and diseases)

## Companion Planting for Pest Control

### Proven Combinations
- **Marigolds (Tagetes)** near tomatoes: deter nematodes (root-knot)
- **French marigolds**: suppress eelworm in soil
- **Nasturtiums**: trap crop for aphids, deter cabbage white butterfly
- **Alliums (garlic, onions)** near carrots: deter carrot fly
- **Basil** near tomatoes: deter whitefly and aphids, improves flavor
- **Borage** near tomatoes/squash: attracts pollinators, deters tomato hornworm
- **Mint** near cabbage: deter cabbage moth
- **Rosemary** near carrots: deter carrot fly
- **Thyme** near brassicas: deter cabbage worm

## Biological Controls

### Predators and Parasitoids
- **Ladybugs/ladybirds**: Both adults and larvae eat aphids (50-60 per day)
- **Lacewings**: Larvae eat aphids, mealybugs, thrips, whitefly
- **Hoverfly larvae**: Consume aphids voraciously
- **Parasitic wasps (Encarsia formosa)**: Control whitefly
- **[[comparison-predatory-mites-vs-ladybugs-biocontrol]] (Phytoseiulus)**: Control red spider mite
- **Nematodes (Phasmarhabditis)**: Control slugs

### Encouraging Beneficial Insects
- Plant umbellifers (dill, fennel, parsley, yarrow) — adult food for parasitic wasps
- Provide water sources (shallow dishes with pebbles)
- Leave some wild areas for overwintering habitat
- Avoid broad-spectrum sprays (even organic ones)

## Physical Barriers

### Netting
- **Enviromesh/netting**: 0.5mm mesh keeps out carrot fly, cabbage root fly, onion fly, aphids, leaf miners, butterflies
- **Bird netting**: Protects fruit from birds
- **Flea beetle netting**: Fine mesh for brassicas

### Other Barriers
- **Collars** around brassica stems: prevent cabbage root fly egg-laying
- **Grease bands** on fruit tree trunks: trap winter moth, ants
- **Fleece/row covers**: Protect seedlings from cold and pests
- **Fruit cages**: Complete protection for soft fruit

## Organic Approved Sprays (Last Resort)

### Insect Control
- **Insecticidal soap**: Contact insecticide for soft-bodied insects (aphids, whitefly, mealybugs). No residue
- **Pyrethrum**: Natural insecticide from chrysanthemum flowers. Broad-spectrum, breaks down quickly. Toxic to bees — spray evening
- **Neem oil**: Insecticide, fungicide, miticide. Disrupts insect hormone systems. Low bee toxicity
- **[[query-how-to-use-diatomaceous-earth-in-the-garden]]**: Physical desiccant for slugs, ants, fleas. Wear mask when applying
- **Spinosad**: Derived from soil bacterium. Effective against caterpillars, thrips. Toxic to bees — spray evening

### Disease Control
- **Copper fungicide** (Bordeaux mix): Prevents blight, canker, leaf spot. Some soil accumulation — use sparingly
- **Sulfur**: Controls [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]], rust. Can't use within 2 weeks of oil sprays
