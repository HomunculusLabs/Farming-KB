# HEX-1 Protein and Woronin Body Crystal Structure in Euascomycetes

## Overview

Woronin bodies are specialized peroxisome-derived organelles unique to the
Euascomycetes (a major subgroup of the Ascomycota fungi). They function
as emergency plugs that rapidly seal septal pores when hyphal damage
occurs, preventing catastrophic loss of cytoplasm. Chapter 2 of *Fungi in
the Environment*, by Gregory Jedd, details the discovery that the HEX-1
protein is the key structural determinant of the Woronin body core, and
that this protein spontaneously crystallizes to form a dense matrix that
gives the organelle its plug-like properties.

## The Septal Pore Problem

Filamentous fungi grow as networks of tube-like cells (hyphae) partitioned
by cross-walls called septa. Each septum contains a central pore that
allows cytoplasmic streaming — the movement of organelles, nutrients, and
nuclei between cells. This intercellular continuity is essential for
coordinated colony function, but creates a vulnerability: if a hypha is
severed, cytoplasm can drain through septal pores from adjacent living
cells into the dead compartment.

Buller (1933) first posed this problem experimentally: when he broke
hyphae with a drawn needle, septal pores adjacent to the damage became
plugged within minutes, preventing protoplasmic loss. However, Buller
mistakenly attributed the plug to coagulated protoplasm rather than to
the Woronin bodies first observed by Woronin in 1886.

## Woronin Body Structure

Electron microscopy reveals the Woronin body as:

- Generally **oval** in shape, though **hexagonal forms** occur in some
  species (notably *[[fusarium-oxysporum]]*)
- Centered on an **electron-dense protein matrix** circumscribed by a
  single unit membrane
- **Diameter**: 100 nm to >1 μm, generally larger than the septal pore
  it must plug
- **Tethered** to the septal pore vicinity by filamentous material of
  unknown composition (demonstrated by laser-trap experiments where
  Woronin bodies returned to their position after being physically
  displaced)
- Associated with **peroxisomes** (microbodies), confirming their
  peroxisomal origin

Early hexagonal forms in *Fusarium* appear to mature into oval forms,
suggesting both shapes share a common structural core — later confirmed
by the discovery of HEX-1.

## Discovery of HEX-1

Jedd and Chua (2000) and Tenney et al. (2000) independently purified
Woronin bodies from *Neurospora crassa* and identified HEX-1 as the key
structural protein. Key findings:

- **Antibodies to HEX-1** decorated the Woronin body matrix in *N.
  crassa*, *Aspergillus nidulans*, and *Magnaporthe grisea*
- **HEX-1 exists as an extremely large, stable protein complex**
  (confirmed in *Magnaporthe*)
- The **hex-1 gene** encodes a consensus peroxisome-targeting signal
  (PTS-1), targeting the protein to peroxisomes where Woronin bodies
  form
- **Expression in yeast** generates intraperoxisomal protein
  assemblies morphologically similar to native Woronin bodies
- **Recombinant HEX-1 spontaneously crystallizes in vitro**, indicating
  it is a self-assembling structural protein

## The hex-1 Gene: Phylogenetic Distribution

The hex-1 gene is **specific to the Euascomycetes** — tblastn searches of
fungal genome and EST databases confirm its presence in all examined
Euascomycete genomes and its absence in all other known fungal genomes.
This single evolutionary origin makes hex-1 a diagnostic marker for the
group.

Notably, hex-1 is present in numerous **plant and human pathogenic**
Euascomycetes, including *[[coccidioides-immitis]]* (Valley fever), *[[botrytis-cinerea]]* (gray mold), and
model organisms *A. nidulans* and *A. oryzae*. This pathogen prevalence
suggests Woronin bodies may be attractive targets for novel fungicides.
