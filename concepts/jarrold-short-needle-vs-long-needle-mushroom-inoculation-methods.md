## Short Needle Method

The short needle method involves injecting spore solution through the plastic bag wall, below the level of the fungicide-treated-seed-contamination-risk-mushroom-substrate.

### Preparation

Before inoculation, two inoculation points must be prepared on each bag:

1. **Tape placement**: Small pieces of adhesive tape are positioned at two points on the bag wall, below the filter level. Jarrold describes a **loop-back tape design** where the tape is folded back on itself, leaving a non-adhesive tab. This allows the operator to quickly cover the needle puncture site immediately after withdrawing the needle, without fumbling.

2. **Sterilisation of inoculation points**: Each inoculation site is wiped with a isopropyl-alcohol ([[isopropyl-alcohol]]) to surface-sterilise the bag wall before needle insertion.

### Inoculation Procedure

The standard syringe needle is heated until **red-hot** using a flame. This serves a dual purpose: it sterilises the needle tip and softens the plastic bag wall, making penetration significantly easier. The needle is then inserted through the pre-cleaned bag wall at the prepared tape point, and **5 cc of spore solution** is injected per inoculation point. After injection, the needle is withdrawn and the pre-positioned loop-back tape is immediately pressed over the puncture to seal it.

### Limitations

The short needle method carries a higher contamination risk because the needle creates a direct pathway through the bag wall into the substrate. Although the tape covers the hole, the puncture site remains a potential entry point for airborne contaminants. The tape also serves to prevent the hole from enlarging upon needle removal, which would create an even larger contamination vector.

Additionally, the two-point inoculation approach doubles the number of puncture sites per bag, each representing a potential failure point. The need for precise tape placement, sterilisation of each site, and careful timing of tape application makes the short needle method more labour-intensive and technique-dependent. Beginners may find the coordination of flaming, injecting, withdrawing, and taping challenging to perform consistently.
