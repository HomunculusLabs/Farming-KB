Spore print color is the single most important macroscopic
identification feature for distinguishing psilocybin-containing
mushrooms from potentially dangerous lookalikes. The Psilocybin
Mushroom Image Guide documents species across several genera, each
with characteristic spore colors.

## Spore Print Colors by Genus

### Psilocybe Species

The core genus for psilocybin-containing mushrooms. All [[panaeolus-acuminatus]] and other Panaeolus genus documented in the guide includes:
P. campanulatus and P. subbalteatus (=cinctulus).

- Spore print color: black
- Gills are typically mottled due to uneven spore maturation,
  with dark mature spores alongside lighter immature ones
- The black spore print is distinct from the rusty brown of
  dangerous lookalikes like Panaeolina foenisecii

### Copelandia Species

Three Copelandia species are documented: C. bispora,
C. cambodgeniensis, and C. tropicalis.

- Spore print color: black
- Copelandia is sometimes treated as a section within Panaeolus
  due to shared spore characteristics
- C. cambodgeniensis is noted as the most potent of the
  Copelandia species

### Gymnopilus Species

[[gymnopilus-genus-neurotropic-species]] are the Gymnopilus
species in the guide.

- Spore print color: rusty orange to reddish-brown
- This is notably different from the dark purple-brown to black
  spore prints of Psilocybe and Panaeolus
- The orange-brown spore print can help distinguish Gymnopilus
  from galerinas (which have rusty brown prints that may appear
  similar)
- Microscopic examination is more definitive for Gymnopilus
  identification

## How to Take a Spore Print

While the image guide is photographic, spore print analysis is a
standard companion technique. For [[gymnopilus-genus-neurotropic-species]] and other genera have distinct
spore colors that help discriminate species. The spore print
immediately eliminates many dangerous
possibilities:

- Dark purple-brown to black (Psilocybe, Panaeolus, Copelandia):
  consistent with psilocybin-containing species
- Rusty brown (Galerina): indicates potentially lethal amatoxin-
  containing species -- a critical warning signal
- White or cream (many other genera): not consistent with any
  psilocybin mushroom image guide spore color chart genus in the guide
- Rusty orange (Gymnopilus): requires additional identification
  beyond spore color alone

## [[mushroom-identification]] keys. For psilocybin mushroom hunters,
the decision tree from the guide's species works as follows:

1. Is the spore print dark (purple-brown to black)?
   - Yes: Proceed to Psilocybe, Panaeolus, or Copelandia
   - No, rusty brown: Could be Gymnopilus (check other features)
   - No, rusty brown with white ring: Warning -- could be
     Galerina (potentially lethal)
   - No, white or cream: Not a psilocybin mushroom from this
