# Faires [[faires-chutney-and-condiment-making]] Low-Energy Cooking and Food Preservation

Practical guide to off-grid cooking methods and traditional food preservation techniques.

## Cooking Methods Comparison

| Method | Best For | Drawbacks |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Electric/gas stove | Most convenient | Depends on grid/gas supply |
| Wood cookstove | Cold climates with plentiful firewood | Smoke, summer heat, maintenance |
| Methane gas | Hot climates with biogas digester | Requires digester infrastructure |
| Solar cooker | Summer, sunny days | Weather-dependent |

## Wood Cookstove Use
- One end has vents/dials regulating airflow (more air = more heat)
- Slide unit directs smoke around oven box before chimney
- Takes ~15 minutes to reach 350 degrees F (176 degrees C)
- Whole cooktop surface is hot; warmest over firebox, coolest opposite end
- Must babysit stove — temperatures fluctuate quickly
- Safety: fire extinguisher nearby, no treated lumber, no liquid fuels, no frozen logs

## Thermal Cookers
- Two pots: one for food, one creating vacuum seal for insulation
- Bring to boil for ~10 minutes, then place in outer pot
- Continues cooking 6+ hours with no additional energy
- Can combine with solar cooker (solar heats, thermal maintains if cloudy)

## Mud Oven

### Design Proportions (centuries-old)
- Door height = 63% of oven height
- Oven height = 60-75% of dome diameter
- Standard: 27 inches wide base, ~18 inches tall inside, ~11 inches high door
- Door width = half inner diameter

### Construction (10 steps)
1. Foundation at waist height (slip form rock, concrete, or recycled fireproof materials)
2. 5-inch sand layer, leveled
3. Bricks on sand (no cement), snug and level
4. Wet sand/topsoil dome mold, 18 inches tall, covered with wet newspaper
5. Mix cob: 1 part clay to 3 parts sand, moist but not sticky (test: ball dropped should change shape but not crack)
6. Sculpt 4-inch-thick walls around sand form
7. Wait 4+ hours to dry, cut door using 63% rule
8. Remove sand/newspaper, test with small fire (smoke should pull up inside dome)
9. Add brick archway and wooden door (used only after fire burns to coals)
10. Plaster outside with leftover mud (don't waterproof — oven must breathe)

### Use
- Build fire 2-3 hours ahead, let burn to coals, rake out ashes
- Test heat: throw flour inside, should brown in ~15 seconds
- Interior reaches 700 degrees F (371 degrees C)
- Can bake bread, roast, broil, steam, braise; as it cools: make yogurt, dry herbs/fruit
- Holds heat for several hours

## Food Preservation

### Food Safety (Botulism Prevention)
- Cook home-preserved food at least 20 minutes
- Wash hands frequently, keep utensils extremely clean
- Use separate utensils/containers for each food type, especially meat
- Botulism is invisible, tasteless, odorless

### Salting Meat
1. Cut meat into strips, rub in spices then salt until meat won't accept more
2. Coat with salt layer, hang at 59 degrees F (15 degrees C) for 3+ weeks
3. Salt dissolves into meat water, preventing bacteria if >3.5% salt-to-water (ideally >10%)
4. Wash off salt before cooking; may need soaking

### Clarified Fat (Lard)
- Save fats from meat, store in freezer
- Simmer on low for hours until liquid, pour through cheesecloth
- Can add vegetables/herbs/spices for bouillon cubes
- Lasts years; use in soups, stews, frying

### Clarified Butter (Ghee)
- Melt butter at very low temperature, don't stir
- Skim solids from surface
- When golden and completely clear, pour into container
- Sits at room temperature for months; use in cream sauces and frying

### Egg Storage Methods
| Method | Duration | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Fridge carton | 6 weeks | Standard |
| Fridge plastic bag | 2 months | |
| Cold storage (sawdust/oatmeal) | 3 months | 30-40 degrees F, high humidity |
| Pickled | 4-6 months | Hard-boil, brine, then spiced vinegar |
| Frozen (cracked) | 8 months | Add 1 tbsp sugar OR 1/2 tsp salt per cup |
| Dried (powdered) | 3-4 months | 120 degrees F, 24-36 hours |
| Lard-coated (in salt) | 6-12 months | Dip in melted lard, pack in salt |

### Freezing Vegetables
- Must blanch first (slows/stops enzyme breakdown)
- Turn freezer to -10 degrees F (-23 degrees C) day before
- Boiling: 1 gallon water per pound vegetables; submerge, then ice bath for same duration
- Steaming: 2 inches boiling water, steam basket, time from when steam pushes lid
- Onions, peppers, herbs: no blanching needed
- Squash, pumpkins, sweet potatoes, beets: fully cook before freezing
- Frozen veg/fruit: ~1 year; baked goods: ~6 months; meat: 3-6 months

### Air Drying [[faires-food-drying-and-no-energy-storage]]
- Works for herbs, onions, garlic
- Hang upside down in cool, dry, dark, airy room
- Takes ~2 weeks
- Herbs hold flavor longer when left hanging
