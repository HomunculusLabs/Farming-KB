## Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) Pharmacology

## Overview

Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is the primary mind-altering constituent of
marijuana, responsible for generating complex changes that occur physically
as well as mentally and emotionally. Under a microscope, THC appears as a
sticky liquid crystal when warm and a glass-like solid when cool. The chemical
structure of THC was discovered in 1964 by two Israeli scientists, Raphael
Mechoulam and Yechiel Gaoni. It remains the most studied cannabis constituent,
yet this unique molecule continues to surprise scientists as new information
emerges about its immense influence on human physiology and psychology.

## THC-Acid and Decarboxylation

Fresh cannabis contains THC in the form of THC-carboxylic-acid (THC-A), which
is considered only minutely psychoactive. Once dried, the chemical composition
of THC-acid changes through decarboxylation as it dries or when burned. Once
devoid of its carboxyl group through heat exposure, THC becomes psychoactive.
This conversion explains why cannabis is typically heated through smoking,
vaporizing, or cooking into edibles before consumption for psychoactive effect.

## Factors Affecting THC Content

Plant-based THC content varies depending on several key factors:

- **Cannabis strain**: Some strains may be especially potent in THC while
  others may contain only trace amounts, reflecting genetic variation.
- **Freshness**: THC content decreases over time and is affected by UV light,
  heat, and exposure to moisture during storage.
- **Growing conditions**: Indoor cultivation follows a three-month cycle,
  while outdoor cultivation follows an annual or biannual cycle.
- **Environment**: Indoor crops tend to contain a markedly higher THC content
  than outdoor crops due to controlled lighting and environmental optimization.
