## THC Acetate and Isomerization

Chemical modification techniques for cannabis extracts, documented in Ben Lemon's "[[cannabis-alchemy-book]]" (1979). These processes convert non-psychoactive cannabidiol (CBD) to psychoactive THC (isomerization) and further modify THC to produce THC acetate -- a compound approximately twice as potent with distinct psychoactive properties.

Related: [[cannabis-extraction-basics]], [[cannabis-hashish-guide]]

## The Chemistry

### Cannabidiol to THC (Isomerization)

Cannabidiol (CBD) is a non-psychoactive cannabinoid that typically occurs in quantities at least equal to THC in most [[cannabis-microbiome-otu-abundance-vs-presence-cannabis-strains]]. Through acid-catalyzed cyclization, CBD can be converted to THC, potentially doubling or more the potency of cannabis oil.

**Mechanism**: CBD contains an open ring structure. Under acidic conditions, a third ring closes, converting it to THC. Simultaneously, the THC can be converted from its lower-rotating to higher-rotating isomeric form, which produces a more psychedelic and spiritual effect.

**Rotation and potency**: The quality of the psychoactive effect is determined by the specific rotation of polarized light passing through the THC molecule. Higher-rotating forms are more potent and produce more psychedelic effects. Methods exist to convert THC from lower to higher rotation.

### THC to THC Acetate (Acetylation)

THC acetate is produced by reacting THC with [[acetic-anhydride]], which adds an acetyl group (CH3CO) to the molecule.

**Properties**:
- Adams scale potency: THC = 7.3, THC acetate = 14.6 (approximately double)
- Additional 25% increase in molecular weight after acetylation
- Effect is more spiritual and psychedelic than ordinary THC
- 30-minute onset delay before effects are felt (vs nearly immediate for smoked THC)

## Isomerization Process

### Starting Material
Begin with cannabis oil that has been extracted and purified by [[petroleum-ether]] re-extraction (see [[cannabis-extraction-basics]]). The oil should be dissolved in absolute ethanol or pure methanol at a ratio of 1 gram oil to 10 grams solvent.

### Acid Catalysis
Add 1 drop of 100% sulfuric acid per gram of oil, slowly with continuous stirring. Safety requirements: safety glasses, long rubber gloves, clothing covering as much body surface as possible. Sulfuric acid burns are treated by immediate washing with water and bicarbonate of soda. Store in a safety bottle (glass bottle fitted into a styrofoam-lined metal can).

### Reflux
Place the solution in a Pyrex pot (not stainless steel -- sulfuric acid is reactive) in the refluxing apparatus. Reflux for 2 hours in a boiling water bath.

### Workup
1. Cool the solution, pour into equal volume of water
2. Extract with petroleum ether (same technique as initial purification)
3. Wash ether solution with 4 volumes of water (invert 25 times, release pressure each time)
4. Separate layers, siphon off ether/oil layer
5. Wash with 5% bicarbonate of soda solution
6. Repeat water wash twice
7. Evaporate ether, collect for reuse
8. Result: oil with much higher THC percentage, all in the high-rotating isomeric form

## THC Acetate Synthesis

### Safety Requirements
Acetic anhydride fumes are very flammable and poisonous. The entire operation must be performed in a safety box (glove box) with:
- Sparkless electric motor exhaust fan
- Anhydrous nitrogen atmosphere (oxygen removed -- test by attempting to strike a match)
- Fire extinguisher mounted inside
- Thick reinforced front board protecting the operator
- Hinged side panels that blow out in case of explosion
- Long sealed gloves for manipulation

### Equipment
- 500ml Pyrex round-bottom boiling flask with ground glass fitting
- Tubular condenser with matching ground glass fitting
- Metal pot (2000ml) as hot oil bath
- Thermometer for oil bath monitoring
- Sparkless electric hotplate with external rheostat control
- Ring stand and clamps
- Cottonseed oil for bath
- Immersible water pump, bucket, and hoses for condenser cooling

### Process
1. Place measured cannabis oil in the boiling flask
2. In nitrogen atmosphere, add 3 volumes acetic anhydride per volume of cannabis oil
3. Assemble apparatus, circulate icewater through condenser
4. Heat oil bath slowly; note temperature where fuming begins
5. Raise temperature until solution boils; stabilize bath temperature
6. Reflux for 3 hours, supplying ice as needed to coolant
7. Cool to room temperature (maintain condenser circulation)
8. Distill off acetic anhydride using a separate distillation flask
9. Collect acetic anhydride for reuse
10. Dissolve resulting THC acetate in pure alcohol, pour into 5 volumes water
11. Extract with petroleum ether, evaporate ether
12. Repeat alcohol-water-ether extraction once more for purity
13. Result: THC acetate oil

## Advanced Refinement

### Fractional Distillation
The oil can be further refined by fractional distillation under vacuum. This requires heating the oil to high temperature under reduced pressure (2-5 mm Hg), causing THC and related cannabinoids to vaporize at different temperatures while impurities remain behind. Roger Adams (1940) documented this process in the Journal of the American Chemical Society (Vol. 62, p. 198).

### Chromatographic Isolation
Pure cannabidiol can be isolated from hashish extract using Florisil column chromatography, then converted to CBD-bis-3,5-dinitrobenzoate (which crystallizes readily), and finally converted back to pure crystalline cannabidiol prisms. These prisms can then be isomerized to pure THC. This process, documented in the Lloydia Journal of Natural Products (Vol. 33, No. 4, p. 456), used 13 kg of confiscated hashish from the National Institute of Mental Health.

### Honey Oil (Translucent Oil)
Activated charcoal filtering removes colored impurities from petroleum-ether-purified oil. Dissolve purified oil in 10 volumes pure alcohol, add granulated activated charcoal (Norit) equal to half the oil weight, filter through fine filter paper, evaporate alcohol. Result: thick translucent amber oil with the appearance of dark honey.

## Historical Significance

These techniques, documented in 1979, prefigured the modern cannabis concentrate industry by decades. The isomerization concept is particularly relevant given modern high-CBD cannabis varieties -- the same chemistry could dramatically increase the psychoactive potential of CBD-rich material. THC acetate has re-emerged in modern discourse as "THC-O-acetate" (THC-O), a semi-synthetic cannabinoid available in some markets.

## Safety Notes

- All solvent operations should use indirect heating (water bath) to prevent ignition
- Methanol fumes are toxic and explosive -- use adequate ventilation
- Sulfuric acid causes severe burns -- always have bicarbonate of soda available
- Acetic anhydride requires nitrogen atmosphere and professional safety box
- All solvents must be completely evaporated before consumption
- Water wash after solvent removal ensures all traces are eliminated (all described solvents evaporate below water's boiling point)
