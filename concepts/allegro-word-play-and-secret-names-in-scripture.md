## The Technique of Multilingual Punning

The core of Allegro's linguistic argument was that a single Greek word in
the New Testament could simultaneously evoke multiple unrelated meanings in
different languages. A Greek place-name like "Bethlehem," for instance,
could be read as a Hebrew phrase meaning "house of bread" while also
recalling Sumerian words describing the mushroom's appearance or effects.

This technique of multilingual punning was, Allegro argued, a deliberate
compositional strategy. The cult's writers chose their words precisely
because they functioned on all three levels at once. The surface Greek
narrative told one story; the Semitic reading revealed another; and the
Sumerian roots disclosed the true, hidden meaning. Only those who had been
initiated into the cult and taught the Sumerian etymologies could fully
appreciate the deepest level.

## The BALAG Root: Crown, Glans, and Church

One of the most productive Sumerian roots in Allegro's analysis was BALAG,
meaning "crown of the penis" or "glans." This root generated an enormous
family of related words across multiple languages, all of which, in Allegro's
reading, ultimately referred to the mushroom's cap or to the act of its
erection (growth). From BALAG came the Greek Phalangx (a roller, log, or
rank of soldiers), the Latin Pallacana (a name for the onion), and the
Hebrew word for the mushroom itself.

The creative potential of this root for the New Testament myth-makers was
considerable. BALAG in full could evoke Semitic roots meaning "make" or
"build," providing the pun behind Peter's declaration as the "rock" upon
which the church would be built. Shortened and combined with other mushroom
name elements, it could produce roots meaning "bless," "praise," "release,"
and "forgive" -- all concepts central to the Gospel message.
