# Faires Orchard Design and Intercropping

Zone 2 is the orchard — located at the edge of Zone 1 gardens. The boundary between zones should be curved or zigzagged. This is where the forest garden model really plays out with intercropping between and under fruit trees.

## Choosing Trees

When mature, consider:
- **Shape:** Umbrella (shade) vs. open (intercropping possible underneath)
- **Shade tolerance:** For growing smaller trees beneath
- **Height:** Tall trees shade out undergrowth unless heavily pruned
- **Water needs:** Group trees with similar water requirements together
- **Compatibility:** Some trees inhibit neighbors
- **Pollination:** Plant male and female species together

**Cold climate species:** Apple, pear, quince, cherry, peach, plum, apricot, filbert, chestnut, walnut, hickory, olive, loquat, pineapple guava. Choose heritage/disease-resistant varieties.

## Intercropping Under Trees

Species that resist disease, don't compete for water/nutrients, function as windbreaks, and serve multiple purposes:

**Grass suppression (first few years, when young trees compete):**
- Bulbs: daffodils, onion species (spring, die off by summer)
- Deep roots: dandelions, comfrey (cover ground)
- Insect attractors: fennel, dill, tansy, carrot, Queen Anne's lace, catnip, daisy
- Nitrogen fixers: clover, leguminous plants

**Additional understory:** Green manure, forage crops for chickens/sheep/pigs, flowers/herbs for bees, vegetables until trees get too big.

## Desert Orchards

**Species selection:** Trees that don't need much water or withstand drought.

**Spacing:** Farther apart than temperate zones (prevent water competition). Plant during rainy season.

**Mulching:** Essential. Rocks protect roots from heat and act as thermal mass (warm at night). Palm leaves or brush protect saplings from sun.

**Water management:**
- Drip irrigation (underground pipe system)
- Roof water and storm drains leading to swales
- Trees planted on swale edges to catch water
- Sandy soil: line planting holes with mud clay to retain water

**Slope strategy:** Zigzag planting with logs/ditches between trees. Hardy trees at hilltop, progressively less tolerant species toward bottom (deepest soil, most water).

**Flexibility:** In deserts, zones are flexible. Plant trees along water pathways or next to house for graywater access.

## Animals in the Orchard

- **Years 0-3:** No animals (they destroy young saplings)
- **Years 3-7:** Small chicken breeds (~100 chickens/acre) — eat insects, fallen fruit, fertilize
- **Years 7+:** Pigs — same function at larger scale
- **Years 15+:** Sheep and cattle

## Pruning Methods

**Pyramid:** Cuts tree smaller than other methods. Prune after April (not winter) to prevent silver leaf disease.
1. Year 1: Cut sapling to 2 ft (60 cm)
2. Year 2: Cut 18 in (45 cm) from top, trim branches to last healthy bud
3. Year 3: Cut 18 in (45 cm) from top, trim top branches ~10 in (25 cm)

**Bush (open-centered):** Stem 2.5 ft (0.8m), open center. Early spring.
1. Pinch buds on bottom trunk, pull suckers
2. Remove crossing, vertical, weak, or diseased stems
3. Thin further in July if needed

**Fan:** Against wall/fence 6 ft high, facing south/southwest. Dwarf varieties best.
1. Plant 6-9 in (15-22 cm) from wall, angled toward it
2. Year 1: Cut main stem to 15 in (38 cm) with 3+ strong buds
3. Summer: Put stakes at 45°, tie two side branches
4. Year 2: Two more inside branches at lesser angle
5. Pinch shoots growing toward wall, others back to one leaf

**Silver leaf disease prevention:** Use sharp shears, prune on warm sunny spring day, apply wound dressing same day, never use homemade paint.
