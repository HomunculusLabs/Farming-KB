## The Telluride Mushroom Festival and Modern Mycological Culture

The Telluride Mushroom Festival, held annually for twenty-five years
in the small ski town of Telluride, high in the Rocky Mountains of
Colorado, represents a distinctive intersection of scientific
mycology, countercultural spirituality, and popular enthusiasm for
fungi. On the surface it resembles any other gathering of amateur
mycologists, but it also serves as a meeting point where the
disenchanted modern world encounters the ancient human fascination
with mushrooms.

## Festival Structure and Activities

Every day there are forays, a chance to wander off into the
surrounding forests and collect mushroom specimens, with an expert on
hand to help with identification. The day's pickings are diligently
wrapped in wax paper and carried back to the town centre, where they
are formally identified, labelled and laid out in a marquee for all to

## The Disenchantment of Modern Life

The festival can be understood as one response to what the sociologist
Max Weber called the "disenchantment of the world." By this he meant
that there was no longer any need for mysterious forces to explain the
phenomena of the world; one could, in principle, master all things by
calculation. The scientific tide had steadily scoured away belief in
fairies, djinns, God even, and with it any notion that our existence
was ordained or had meaning.

Yet a palpable thirst for enchantment remains. Children's literature
and fantasy fiction have never been more enthusiastically consumed by
adults. The burgeoning smorgasbord of alternative spiritualities, New
Age, Pagan and neo-Shamanic, represents genuine attempts to answer the
spiritual anxieties of our times. For those caught between science on
the one hand and religion on the other, mushrooms offer a different
path to re-enchantment.
