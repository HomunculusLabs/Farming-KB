## Understanding Prostate Cancer

The prostate is a male reproductive gland that produces seminal fluid. Prostate cancer is one of the most common cancers in men, particularly those over 50. The gland surrounds the urethra, so enlargement or tumors typically produce symptoms related to urination difficulty.

Risk factors include age, family history, African American heritage, and diet. Diagnosis involves PSA testing, biopsies, and imaging. Treatments include surgery, radiation, hormone therapy, chemotherapy, and watchful waiting.

## Cannabis and Prostate Cancer

### Cannabinoid Anti-Cancer Mechanisms

Research has demonstrated several mechanisms by which cannabinoids may affect prostate cancer:
- **CB1 receptor activation**: May inhibit tumor growth and proliferation
- **Anti-angiogenic effects**: Cannabinoids may prevent formation of new blood vessels feeding tumors
- **Apoptosis induction**: Programmed cell death in cancer cells
- **Anti-proliferative effects**: Slowing or stopping cancer cell multiplication

### Key Studies

Multiple studies have examined cannabinoids in the context of prostate cancer. Cannabinoid receptor ligands have been shown to have promise in treating bone diseases associated with accelerated osteoclastic bone resorption, including bone metastasis from prostate cancer.

### CB1/CB2 Expression in Prostate Tissue

Cannabinoid receptors are expressed in prostate tissue, and their activation modulates cellular processes relevant to cancer progression. The anti-inflammatory properties of cannabinoids through CB2 receptor activation may be particularly relevant given the role of chronic inflammation in prostate cancer development.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

For prostate cancer applications, balanced THC:CBD ratios may provide optimal benefit through combined CB1 and CB2 activation. Both receptors are relevant: CB1 for direct anti-proliferative effects and CB2 for anti-inflammatory and immune-modulatory actions. Sativa strains with higher THC content may be more appropriate when CB1-mediated effects are desired.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Prostate Cancer

### Psychological Stress and Prostate Function

A study of 83 men with benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) found that stress and hostility influence prostate volume and residual urine volume, with effects mediated via the sympathetic nervous system and hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis.

A large-scale study of 19,730 adults over nine years confirmed that anger control and negative affect have a role in prostate cancer risk.

### Metaphorical Dimensions

The prostate (from Greek "protector" or "guardian") represents survival, safety, and security. An enlarged gland applying pressure on the urethra may reflect:
- Financial pressure: "Have I made enough money? Am I a good enough provider?"
- Sexual performance pressure related to male energy, image, and virility
- Self-judgments about unreached goals
- Negative self-image: "old is useless, weak, and decrepit"
- Suppressed anger about aging and loss of male potency

Aggravating factors: chronic stress, hostility, negative affect, male pressure to be anti-emotional, performance pressure, suppressed male anger, negative older male self-image.

Supporting factors: stress reduction, defining and releasing feelings about male image and age, transforming negative affect by re-evaluating emotionally limiting beliefs.

## Supporting Therapies

### Cayenne
Scientists studying cayenne and prostate cancer concluded that capsaicin "is a promising anti-tumor agent in hormone-refractory prostate cancer, which shows resistance to many chemotherapeutic agents."

### Garlic
Hong Kong scientists reported significant success inhibiting primary prostate tumor formation and reducing secondary tumor formation in rodents. Garlic possesses potent antimetastatic properties that may apply to other cancer types.

### (E)-beta-Caryophyllene
FDA-approved dietary cannabinoid activating CB2 receptors for anti-inflammatory action. Found in black pepper, rosemary, basil, clove, cinnamon, and oregano.
