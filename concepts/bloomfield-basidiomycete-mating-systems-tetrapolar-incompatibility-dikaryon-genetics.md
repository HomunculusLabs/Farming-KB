## The Mating-Type Locus

In basidiomycetes, sexual compatibility is determined by genes at one or two mating-type loci:

- **Bipolar species** (unifactorial incompatibility): A single mating-type locus with multiple alleles controls compatibility. If two mycelia share the same allele, they are incompatible. This is analogous to a simple "match/mismatch" system. Approximately 25% of basidiomycete species use this system. Many rust fungi and smuts are bipolar.
- **Tetrapolar species** (bifactorial incompatibility): Two unlinked loci, designated A and B, both determine compatibility. Both loci must carry different alleles between partners for a successful mating. The A locus controls nuclear pairing and clamp [[stamets-cultivator-coprinus-comatus-shaggy-mane-cultivation]]* and related species as model organisms. The dikaryon can persist for months or years, colonizing vast substrates, before environmental cues (temperature, moisture, nutrient depletion) trigger the formation of fruiting bodies. Inside each basidium on the gills, the paired nuclei finally fuse, undergo meiosis, and produce four haploid basidiospores—each potentially carrying a novel combination of parental alleles. This brief diploid window is the only point in the basidiomycete life cycle where two genomes physically merge.

## Evolutionary Significance

The tetrapolar mating system offers several evolutionary advantages:

1. **Disease resistance**: High genetic diversity means some individuals will resist any given pathogen or environmental stress
2. **Adaptation**: Novel gene combinations arise with every mating, accelerating adaptation to changing environments
3. **Population stability**: Even small populations maintain high outcrossing potential due to the multiplicity of mating types
4. **Genomic exploration**: The dikaryotic state allows both parental genomes to be expressed simultaneously, creating hybrid phenotypes
5. **Territorial flexibility**: Somatic incompatibility prevents takeover by any single genotype, maintaining community-level diversity

## Vegetative Compatibility and Non-Sexual Fusion

Beyond mating-type recognition, basidiomycetes also possess **vegetative compatibility** (or somatic compatibility) systems that govern whether hyphae of the same species can merge their cytoplasm without forming a sexual dikaryon. These systems involve multiple **vic (vegetative incompatibility) genes** that trigger programmed cell death when incompatible hyphae fuse. This is distinct from mating-type incompatibility:
