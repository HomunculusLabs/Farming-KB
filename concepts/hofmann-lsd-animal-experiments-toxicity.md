# LSD Animal Experiments and Toxicity

## Reintroduction After Discovery of Psychic Effects

After the discovery of its extraordinary psychic effects, the substance
LSD-25, which five years earlier had been excluded from further
investigation after the first trials on animals, was again admitted into
the series of experimental preparations. Most of the fundamental studies
on animals were carried out by Dr. Aurelio Cerletti in the Sandoz
pharmacological department, headed by Professor Rothlin.

## The Challenge of Animal Testing

Before a new active substance can be investigated in systematic clinical
trials with human subjects, extensive data on its effects and side
effects must be determined in pharmacological tests on animals. These
experiments must assay the assimilation and elimination of the substance
in organisms, and above all its tolerance and relative toxicity.

Animal experiments reveal little about the mental alterations caused by
LSD because psychic effects are scarcely determinable in lower animals.
LSD produces its effects above all in the sphere of the higher and
highest psychic and intellectual functions. Subtle psychic changes
cannot be established in animals because, even if they should be
occurring, the animal could not give them expression. Only relatively
heavy psychic disturbances expressing themselves in altered behavior
become discernible. Quantities substantially higher than the effective
dose in human beings are therefore necessary, even in higher animals.

## Behavioral Effects Across Species

While the mouse under LSD shows only motor disturbances and alterations
in licking behavior, in the cat we see, besides vegetative symptoms like
piloerection (bristling of the hair) and salivation, indications that
point to the existence of hallucinations. The animals stare anxiously in
the air, and instead of attacking the mouse, the cat leaves it alone or
will even stand in fear before it.

The behavior of dogs under the influence of LSD also suggests
hallucinations. A caged community of chimpanzees reacts very sensitively
if a single member of the tribe has received LSD. Even though no changes
appear in this individual animal, the whole cage gets in an uproar
because the LSD chimpanzee no longer observes the laws of its finely
coordinated hierarchic tribal order.

## Unusual Animal Models

Of the remaining animal species tested, aquarium fish and spiders were
particularly noteworthy. In the fish, unusual swimming postures were
observed. In spiders, alterations in web building were apparently
produced by LSD. At very low optimum doses the webs were even better
proportioned and more exactly built than normally. However, with higher
doses, the webs were badly and rudimentarily made.

## Toxicity Data: LD50 Values

The toxicity of LSD has been determined in various animal species. The
LD50 (median lethal dose, where 50 percent of treated animals die)
fluctuates broadly according to the animal species. In mice the LD50 is
50-60 mg/kg intravenously. In rats it drops to 16.5 mg/kg, and in
rabbits to 0.3 mg/kg.

One elephant given 0.297 g of LSD died after a few minutes. The weight
of this animal was 5,000 kg, corresponding to a lethal dose of 0.06
mg/kg. While this single case cannot be generalized, it shows that the
largest land animal reacts proportionally very sensitively to LSD, since
the lethal dose in elephants is roughly 1,000 times lower than in mice.
Most animals die from a lethal dose of LSD by respiratory arrest.

## Extraordinarily Low Toxicity in Humans

The minute doses causing death in animals may give the impression that
LSD is very toxic. However, comparing the lethal dose in animals with
the effective dose in human beings (0.0003-0.001 mg/kg) shows an
extraordinarily low toxicity. A 300- to 600-fold overdose of LSD,
compared to the lethal dose in rabbits, or fully a 50,000- to
100,000-fold overdose compared to the toxicity in the mouse, would have
fatal results in human beings.

To Hofmann's knowledge, no casualties had occurred as a direct
consequence of LSD poisoning. Numerous episodes of fatal consequences
attributed to LSD ingestion had been recorded, but these were accidents
or suicides attributable to the mentally disoriented condition of LSD
intoxication. "The danger of LSD lies not in its toxicity, but rather in
the unpredictability of its psychic effects."

## Chromosome and Teratogenicity Concerns

Reports alleging chromosome damage or genetic injury from LSD appeared in
scientific and lay literature. These effects were observed in only a few
individual cases. Subsequent comprehensive investigations of large,
statistically significant numbers showed no connection between chromosome
anomalies and LSD medication. The same applied to reports about fetal
deformities. While fetal deformities can be induced in animal
experiments through extremely high doses well above those used in
humans, even harmless substances produce such damage under those
conditions. Examination of reported individual human cases revealed no
connection between LSD use and injury. "If there had been any such
connection, it would long since have attracted attention, for several
million people by now have taken LSD."

## Significance for Brain Research

Despite the limitations of animal models for studying psychic effects,
the animal research with LSD contributed significantly to neuroscience.
The distribution patterns, the identification of brain centers
concentrating LSD in the midbrain, and the interactions with serotonin
and dopamine all emerged from this animal work. LSD served as a tool in
brain research, illuminating the biochemical processes underlying psychic
functions. The pharmacological data from animal experiments also provided
essential safety information that guided human clinical research.
