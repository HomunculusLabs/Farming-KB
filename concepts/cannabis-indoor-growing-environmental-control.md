## Cannabis Indoor Growing — Environmental Control Fundamentals

## Overview

Indoor cannabis cultivation requires precise control of environmental variables including soil conditions, pH, nutrients, air quality, humidity, and temperature. [[greg-green]]"s "The Cannabis Grow Bible" provides comprehensive guidance on managing these parameters across the vegetative and flowering stages. This concept page summarizes the core environmental control principles for indoor cannabis cultivation.

## Soil Control

### Soil Composition

A well-balanced growing medium provides:
- Adequate drainage and aeration.
- Water retention capacity.
- Nutrient availability.
- pH buffering capacity.

### Soil Flushing

- Periodic flushing of soil with plain water removes accumulated nutrient salts.
- Prevents [[nutrient-lockout]] and toxicity.
- Typically performed before harvest to improve final product quality.

## pH Management

### Importance of pH

- [[soil-ph]] directly affects **nutrient availability** to plant roots.
- Most cannabis nutrients are optimally available in the range of **pH 6.0–7.0** for soil and **pH 5.5–6.5** for hydroponic systems.
- Outside these ranges, essential nutrients become locked out (unavailable) even if present in the medium.

### pH Monitoring

- Regular pH testing of runoff water indicates the actual root-zone pH.
- pH-adjusted water should be used for irrigation.
- Commercial pH up/down products or natural amendments (lime for up, sulfur for down) can adjust pH.

## Nutrient Control

### Macronutrients

| Nutrient | Role | [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]] |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | Vegetative growth, chlorophyll | Yellowing of lower leaves |
| Phosphorus (P) | Root development, flowering | Dark/green-purple leaves, slow growth |
| Potassium (K) | Overall plant health, water regulation | Brown leaf tips, weak stems |

### Micronutrients

Calcium, magnesium, sulfur, iron, manganese, zinc, copper, boron, and molybdenum are required in smaller quantities but deficiencies can cause significant problems.

### Feeding Schedule

- Vegetative stage: Higher nitrogen, moderate phosphorus and potassium.
- Flowering stage: Reduced nitrogen, increased phosphorus and potassium.
- Over-fertilization is a common beginner mistake — causes nutrient burn (leaf tip necrosis).

## Air Quality

### Ventilation

- Fresh air exchange is essential for:
  - **CO₂ replenishment**: Plants consume CO₂ during photosynthesis.
  - **[[blesching-cannabis-fever-temperature-regulation]]**: Removing excess heat from lighting.
  - **Humidity control**: Preventing mould and mildew.
  - **Strengthening stems**: Air movement causes plants to develop stronger structural tissues.

### CO₂ Enrichment

- Elevated CO₂ levels (1000–1500 ppm vs. ambient ~400 ppm) can significantly increase growth rates and yields.
- Requires sealed grow space and supplemental lighting to be effective.
- CO₂ enrichment without adequate light shows diminishing returns.

## Humidity

### Ideal Ranges

| Stage | Ideal Humidity |
|---|---|
| Seedling/Cloning | 70–80% |
| Vegetative | 50–70% |
| Flowering | 40–50% |

### Management

- High humidity promotes mould and fungal diseases, especially during flowering.
- Low humidity can cause excessive transpiration and nutrient stress.
- Dehumidifiers and humidifiers are used for control.

## Temperature

### Ideal Ranges

| Stage | Day Temperature | Night Temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetative | 70–85°F (21–29°C) | 60–70°F (15–21°C) |
| Flowering | 65–80°F (18–27°C) | 55–65°F (13–18°C) |

### Temperature Effects

- Higher temperatures increase metabolic rate and water consumption.
- Excessive heat (>90°F/32°C) causes heat stress, reduced growth, and hermaphroditism.
- The temperature differential between day and night (10–15°F) is important for healthy development.

## Fans and Air Circulation

- **Oscillating fans**: Provide gentle air movement within the canopy.
- **Exhaust fans**: Remove stale air and heat from the grow space.
- **Intake fans**: Bring in fresh air (can be passive or active).
- Proper air circulation prevents hot spots, ensures even CO₂ distribution, and strengthens stems.

## The 12/12 Light Cycle for Flowering

### Principle

- Cannabis is a **short-day plant** — it flowers when the dark period exceeds a critical length.
- The standard flowering light cycle is **12 hours light / 12 hours dark** (12/12).
- This mimics the natural shortening of days in autumn.

### Implementation

- Switch from vegetative light cycle (e.g., 18/6 or 24/0) to 12/12 to initiate flowering.
- **Light leaks** during the dark period can delay flowering or cause hermaphroditism.
- Complete darkness is essential during the 12-hour dark period.

### Pre-Flowering and Early Sexing

- The first 1–2 weeks after switching to 12/12, plants show pre-flowers.
- Female plants develop pistils (white hairs); male plants develop pollen sacs.
- Early identification allows removal of males to prevent pollination.

## Hermaphroditism

- Stress (light leaks, temperature extremes, nutrient problems) can cause female plants to develop male sex organs.
- Hermaphrodite plants can self-pollinate, producing seeded buds.
- Preventing stress is the primary strategy for avoiding hermaphroditism.
