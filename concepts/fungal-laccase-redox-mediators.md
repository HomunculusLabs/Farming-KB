## Fungal Laccase Redox Mediator Systems

## Overview

Laccase (benzenediol : oxygen oxidoreductase, EC 1.10.3.2) is a
multicopper blue oxidase produced [[fungal-biodegradation-chlorinated-aromatics-and-btex]],
and [[biochemistry-of-ligninolytic-fungi-bioremediation]] are secreted glycoproteins belonging to
the blue multicopper oxidase family. They contain four or more copper
atoms and catalyze the four-electron reduction of molecular oxygen to
water. Laccase possesses a highly specific binding pocket for oxygen,
but the binding pocket for reducing substrates is relatively shallow
and non-stereospecific. Whether a compound will be oxidized depends
largely on the redox potential differences between the substrate and
type I copper in the active site. Most fungal laccases oxidize compounds
with redox potentials up to approximately 1.0 V/NHE.

## The Redox Potential Limitation

A fundamental limitation of laccase is its inability to directly oxidize
non-phenolic aromatic compounds with high ionization potentials. These
compounds, which include the majority of structural units in lignin and
many environmental pollutants, have redox potentials exceeding the
oxidizing capacity of the enzyme. For bioremediation purposes, this is
a significant constraint because many priority pollutants, such as
polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) and high-molecular-weight PAHs, are
non-phenolic. Redox mediators overcome this limitation by acting as
electron shuttles between the enzyme and the target substrate.

## ABTS as a Mediator

2,2'-azinobis(3-ethylbenzthiazoline-6-sulfonate) (ABTS) was one of
the first mediators identified for laccase. The laccases of Trametes
versicolor can oxidize non-phenolic substrates and depolymerize Kraft
lignin in vitro when ABTS is provided as a co-substrate. Notably, the
laccase-ABTS couple operates through hydrogen atom abstraction rather
than the single-electron oxidation typical of laccase alone. Veratryl
alcohol (VA), with a redox potential of 1.36 V/NHE placing it beyond
the range of laccase alone, can be oxidized to veratraldehyde when
both laccase and ABTS are present. The laccase-ABTS system has been
demonstrated to oxidize anthracene to anthraquinone and to mediate the
oxidation of benzo[a]pyrene, expanding laccase's applicability to
high-molecular-weight PAH remediation.

## 1-Hydroxybenzotriazole (HBT) Mediator

1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HBT) is another widely studied laccase
mediator. Laccase delignifies Kraft pulps effectively when HBT is
provided as a co-substrate. The HBT mediator system operates through
a different mechanism than ABTS, involving the formation of a nitroxyl
radical that can abstract hydrogen atoms from non-phenolic substrates.
