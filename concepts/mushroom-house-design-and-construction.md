# Mushroom House Design and Construction

## Overview

The design and construction of [[mycology]], engineering, and practical agriculture. Whether built as dedicated mushroom houses, adapted from existing cellars and caves, or constructed as simple temporary shelters, the growing facility must provide precise control over temperature, moisture, ventilation, and light — the environmental factors that determine the success or failure of a mushroom crop.

## Environmental Requirements

The fundamental principle underlying all [[agaricus-campestris]]* and *A. arvensis* are found in parts of California during winter months. The mean monthly temperature of San Francisco is normally from 50° to 60°F, and the daily range is not pronounced. A mean temperature rather below than above the requirements is desirable, say 48° to 56°F.

Inquiries about spawning pastures and lawns to establish natural cultures have not yielded reliable results. No permanent success was achieved, and the failures were under a variety of conditions. English correspondents give a similar version. It is therefore not to be depended upon, though spreading spawn about lawns or pastures involves so little labor that it may be worth the experiment when spawn is available.
