## Renewable versus Non-Renewable Resources

A renewable resource is one that can be replenished within a human time frame
through natural processes. Trees, soil fertility, water, and solar energy are
renewable resources. Non-renewable resources such as fossil fuels, phosphate
rock, and many mineral deposits are finite and cannot be replaced once
consumed. The principle does not demand absolute avoidance of non-renewable
resources but calls for their strategic use and gradual replacement with
renewable alternatives.

The concept extends beyond physical resources to include environmental
services: the purification of air and water, pollination of crops, climate
regulation, and biological pest control that nature provides freely. These
services are often undervalued because they are not captured in economic
accounting systems.

## Total Process Evaluation

Evaluating the appropriateness of any resource use requires considering the
total process from extraction through use to disposal. This includes:
- The energy cost of obtaining and processing the resource
- The environmental impact of extraction and processing
- The longevity and efficiency of the resource in use
- The consequences of disposal or recycling

Simple metrics like EMERGY analysis, developed by Howard Odum, can help compare
the true energetic cost of different resource pathways. Without such total
process evaluation, apparently "green" choices may prove to have hidden costs
that outweigh their benefits.

## Investment of Non-Renewable Energy in Agriculture

A key application of this principle is evaluating the use of non-renewable
energy in agricultural systems. Industrial agriculture depends heavily on fossil
fuel inputs for fertiliser production, mechanisation, irrigation pumping, and
transport. When these inputs become scarce or expensive, agricultural systems
that have been designed around them will face serious challenges.

[[permaculture-design]] prioritises systems that maximise
the service component of resource use, as services are typically renewable and
self-maintaining while products require harvesting and processing.
