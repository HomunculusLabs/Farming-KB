## What Is a Swale

A swale is a shallow, level trench dug on contour, with the excavated soil
placed on the downhill side to form a mound or berm. Unlike drainage ditches,
which channel water off-site, swales are designed to spread water and allow it
to infiltrate. When rainfall runs downslope and encounters a swale, the water
pools in the trench, slowly seeping into the soil rather than continuing as
destructive surface flow. The berm below provides a raised planting zone that
benefits from the moisture stored in the soil above.

Swales work on slopes from approximately 1 percent to about 20 percent. Below
1 percent slope, water does not flow effectively enough to fill swales; above
20 percent, the volume and velocity of water can overtop swales and cause
catastrophic failure. On very steep slopes, terraces or other specialized
structures are more appropriate.

## Spacing Calculations

Swale spacing depends on three interrelated factors: slope gradient, soil
infiltration rate, and expected rainfall intensity. Mollison provides a
general guideline: on gentle slopes (3 to 5 percent), swales may be spaced
15 to 30 meters apart. On steeper slopes (10 to 15 percent), spacing narrows
to 5 to 15 meters. The key principle is that each swale should be able to
handle the maximum expected runoff from the catchment area above it without
overtopping.

A practical calculation method involves determining the maximum rainfall
intensity for the site, the catchment area contributing runoff to each swale,
and the infiltration rate of the soil. The swale volume must accommodate the
runoff volume long enough for infiltration to occur. Clay soils with slow
infiltration require wider swales and closer spacing, while sandy soils with
rapid infiltration can use narrower, more widely spaced swales.

## Swale Failures and Common Mistakes

Mollison warns against several common swale design errors. The most dangerous
is failing to provide level-sill spillways. Without overflow capacity, water
will find the lowest point of the berm and carve an erosion channel, potentially
undermining the entire structure. This error has caused landslides and
significant property damage.

Other common failures include:

- **Off-contour construction**: Even a 1 to 2 percent grade in the swale
  bottom causes water to concentrate at one end, leading to erosion.
