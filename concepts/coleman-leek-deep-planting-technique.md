# Leek Deep Planting Technique

Leeks are nearly a year-round crop at the [[coleman-four-season-farm]] Farm, available
from May through early March. The key to growing leeks efficiently and
intensively is the dibble-and-drop deep planting technique, which
produces nine-inch blanched shanks without the labor of hilling soil
up against stems as the plants grow.

## Seasonal Leek Production

The farm uses a different leek variety for each season to maintain
continuous supply:

- **First crop (May):** Extra-early seedlings transplanted to a cold
  house. Seeds are sown February 15 in the plant-starting greenhouse.
- **Summer leeks (through September):** Harvested directly from the
  field.
- **Fall leeks (through November):** Harvested from the field with a
  sheet of plastic over the last of the fall leeks during the second
  half of November.
- **Winter leeks (early December to early March):** Protected by a
  movable greenhouse starting in early December, with an inner layer
  added from mid-December until sold out.

## The Dibble Tool

The dibble is a 36-inch-long, 1-inch-diameter wooden dowel with the
end tapered to a spatulate tip. Sometimes a shovel handle is attached
to the upper end, and a 6-inch-diameter plate is added 9 inches above
the tip to serve as a depth stop and to help flatten the bottom of
the hole.

## Growing Transplants in Floor Seedbeds

Unlike most other crops on the farm, leek transplants are not grown
in soil blocks. Instead, they are grown on the floor of the
plant-starting greenhouse in seedbeds measuring 30 inches wide by
8 feet long by 3 inches deep, constructed with wooden sides and
filled with potting soil.

Leek seeds are sown directly into these beds using a six-row seeder.
The seedlings grow in the beds until they are at least 10 inches
tall. At transplanting time, seedlings are dug out by loosening the
potting soil underneath with a trowel.

## Preparing Transplants for Planting

Before transplanting, the young leek plants are trimmed:
- Roots are trimmed to 1 inch long
- The top (leaf) portion is trimmed to 10 inches long

This trimming reduces transplant shock and makes handling easier
during the dibble-and-drop process.

## The Dibble-and-Drop Method

1. **Moisten the soil** if conditions are dry. Dry soil tends to
   collapse back into the hole when the dibble is removed.

2. **Push the dibble** into the soil to the full 9-inch depth.

3. **Twist the handle** so the spatulate tip flattens out the bottom
   of a 9-inch-deep tubular hole.

4. **Make a hole** every 4 inches along the row.

5. **Drop a leek transplant vertically** into each hole. Only about
   1 inch of green top sticks up above the soil.

6. **Irrigate the field** after planting. The holes are not
   intentionally refilled. Soil slowly migrates in with each
   irrigation or cultivation.

7. **Let the leeks grow.** Each plant develops a guaranteed nine
   inches of blanched shank.

## Intensive Spacing

Because the dibble method eliminates the need for hilling, leeks can
be planted more intensively than with traditional methods. The farm
plants three rows on a 30-inch bed with leeks spaced 4 inches apart
in the row. This close spacing would be impractical if hilling were
required.

The dibble-and-drop method is so space-efficient that single rows of
leeks can be planted between the edge bed and the wall of the
greenhouse -- space that would otherwise go unused.

## Baby Leeks

Restaurant customers love early baby leeks. These are grown in a cold
greenhouse at five rows to a 30-inch bed, set 3 inches apart in the
row. At that spacing, they can be cultivated with the long-handled
wire weeder. The same dibble-and-drop method is used for baby leeks,
producing smaller but perfectly blanched specimens prized by chefs.

## Why Deep Planting Works

The edible part of a leek is the white blanched stem. The more
length a blanched leek stem has, the better the product. Traditional
blanching is accomplished by hilling soil up against the stems as the
leeks grow, which requires significant labor and wider spacing to
allow access for the hilling operation.

The dibble-and-drop technique achieves the same blanching effect with
a single operation at planting time. By placing the transplant at the
bottom of a 9-inch hole, the entire depth of the hole becomes
blanched stem as the plant grows and soil gradually fills in. The
method seems counterintuitive -- dropping a small plant into a deep
hole with almost no top showing -- but it works reliably.

## Winter Protection

Winter leeks are protected by a movable greenhouse starting in early
December. An inner layer of row cover is added from mid-December
onward. The combination of the 'Tadorna' extra-hardy variety and
greenhouse protection ensures a supply of fresh leeks through the
coldest months. Winter leeks typically sell out by early March due to
high demand.
