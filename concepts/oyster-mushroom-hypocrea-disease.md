### Hypocrea Disease (teleomorph)

- Does NOT turn the infected area green (no conidiospores produced)

- White stroma formed by Hypocrea spp. is very similar to primordia of oyster mushroom

- Brown stroma may also form

- By the time stroma is visible, the pathogen has already occupied the substrate deeply and widely

- Much harder to identify early than typical green mold

## Why Hypocrea Is Particularly Dangerous

1. **No green color** — visual inspection misses it during early stages

2. **White stroma mimics mushroom primordia** — growers may mistake it for normal pinning

3. **Deep penetration** — by the time stroma appears, infestation is advanced

4. **Lack of asexual stage** in some new strains means no conidiospore production for standard detection

5. **Resistant strains** of pathogens are increasingly common

## Control Measures

- Sanitation and hygiene are the most important controls — follow basic disease management practices strictly

- Severe infestations found in poorly pasteurized substrates with uneven moisture content

- Do NOT use green mold-contaminated spawn — any green patch in/around spawn bottles is a major spore source

- Observe substrates carefully during hyphal growth; remove or treat spots with dense white mycelial growth

- Spray or drench with 500 ppm Sporgon (prochloraz-manganese complex 50%)

- Spraying affected parts with 250-500 ppm Sporgon before pasteurization prevents green mold AND Hypocrea disease

- Benomyl and thiabendazole are known control agents but resistant strains are increasingly common

- Prochloraz-manganese complex is by far the most effective in Korea where resistant strains prevail

Proper mushroom-pasteurization|pasteurization is the first line of defense — uneven heating creates refuges where Hypocrea survives. See [[oyster-mushroom-substrate-preparation]].
