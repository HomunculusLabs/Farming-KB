# Orchard Management

Establishing and maintaining a productive orchard is a cornerstone of [[regenerative-agriculture]] and [[food-forest-design]]. This guide covers site selection through harvest for homestead-scale orchards in zones 7-8. For detailed variety recommendations, rootstock selection, and chill hours, see [[fruit-tree-varieties]].

## Site Selection

### Soil Requirements
- **Ideal**: Loam soils with 2-5% organic matter, pH 6.0-7.0 (apples/pears prefer 6.0-6.5, stone fruits 6.5-7.0)
- **Minimum**: 18-24 inches of well-drained soil before reaching hardpan or water table
- **Heavy clay**: Amend with compost, consider raised beds or mounds 12-18" high, 6-8' diameter
- **Sandy soil**: Add organic matter, mulch heavily
- Avoid sites with known replant disease history (plant cover crops for 2+ years before planting fruit trees)

### Drainage
- **Critical factor**: Fruit trees die from wet feet faster than drought
- Test by digging 18" hole, filling with water, timing drainage:
  - <2 hours: Excellent
  - 2-4 hours: Acceptable
  - >4 hours: Plant on mounds or choose different site
- Tile drainage can remediate marginal sites
- Avoid depressions where water pools

### Sun Exposure
- Minimum 6-8 hours direct sun during growing season
- Morning sun is critical: dries dew, reducing disease pressure
- Full sun = better fruit set, sugar development, disease resistance
- Observe winter sun angle — don't assume summer pattern applies year-round

### Frost Pockets
Cold air flows downhill and pools in low spots. Avoid:
- Bottoms of valleys
- Natural depressions
- Areas behind solid fences that block cold air drainage
- Frost pockets can be 5-10°F colder than surrounding slopes

**Mitigation**: If no alternative, plant later-blooming varieties or use overhead irrigation for frost protection.

### Wind Protection
- Sustained winds >15 mph damage trees, reduce pollinator activity, increase water loss
- Plant windbreaks on prevailing wind side (typically west/northwest):
  - Deciduous shrubs (elderberry, hazelnut) allow some airflow, don't shade
  - Evergreen hedge (eastern redcedar, juniper) for year-round protection
  - Distance: 10-15' height of windbreak = protected zone
- Avoid solid fences — create turbulence

### Slope and Orientation
- **South-facing**: Warmer, earlier bloom (frost risk), faster drying
- **North-facing**: Cooler, delayed bloom (frost protection), slower drying
- **East-facing**: Good compromise — morning sun, moderate warmth
- **West-facing**: Afternoon heat stress possible in zone 8 summers
- **Ideal slope**: 2-8% grade for air drainage without erosion
- Steep slopes (>15%): Terracing needed, difficult maintenance
- Contour planting on slopes to minimize erosion

## Planting

### Timing
**Bareroot trees**: Late winter/early spring when soil is workable but before bud break. Zone 7: Late February–March. Zone 8: January–February.

**Potted trees**: Spring through early fall. Avoid mid-summer heat.

### Hole Preparation
1. Dig wide, not deep: 2-3x root spread width, only as deep as root system
2. Loosen sides to prevent root circling
3. No amendments in hole — top-dress only with compost
4. Soil test first: Address pH and major deficiencies before planting

### Planting Process
1. Soak bareroot trees 2-4 hours before planting
2. Inspect roots — trim damaged, circling, or excessively long roots
3. Find graft union — must remain 2-4" above final soil level
4. Mound soil in bottom of hole, spread roots naturally
5. Backfill halfway, water to settle, finish backfilling
6. Create watering basin 3-4' diameter, 3-4" high berm

### Spacing
|| Tree Type | Standard | Semi-Dwarf | Dwarf |
||-----------|----------|------------|-------|
|| Apple | 20-25' | 12-15' | 8-10' |
|| Pear | 20-25' | 12-15' | 8-10' |
|| Peach | 15-18' | 10-12' | 6-8' |
|| Cherry (sweet) | 25-30' | 15-18' | 10-12' |
|| Fig | 12-15' | — | — |

### Initial Care
- Water: 5-10 gallons per week first season, deep and infrequent
- No fertilizer at planting — wait until leaves emerge
- Trunk protection: Tree guards against rodents/rabbits
- Weed control: Keep 3-4' circle weed-free for first 3 years

## Pruning

### Training Systems

**Central Leader** (Apples, Pears, Sweet Cherries): Single main trunk with scaffold branches in tiers. First tier at 24-30", subsequent tiers every 18-24". Good for tighter spacing.

**Open Center/Vase** (Peaches, Plums, Sour Cherries): 3-5 scaffolds radiating from trunk at 18-24" height. Essential for peaches (fruit on 1-year wood).

**Espalier**: Trees trained flat against wall, fence, or wire. Good for small spaces. See [[permaculture-design]].

### When to Prune
- **Dormant pruning** (Late Winter): Best for structural work. Apples/pears: Feb–Mar. Stone fruits: Mar–Apr.
- **Summer pruning** (Jun–Aug): Slows vigor, improves light penetration. Don't remove >25% of canopy.

### Renewal Pruning
- **Apples/Pears**: Fruit on spurs. Remove oldest, least productive wood.
- **Peaches**: Fruit on 1-year wood only. Remove 1/3 of older wood each year.
- **Plums**: Japanese on 1-2 year wood; European on spurs. Adjust accordingly.
- **Cherries**: Sweet on spurs; pie on 1-year wood.

## Pest Management

Orchard IPM targets the most common pests and diseases: codling moth, cedar apple rust, apple maggot, plum curculio, brown rot, fire blight, and peach leaf curl. Key strategies include resistant varieties, sanitation, canopy management, and targeted organic sprays. See [[orchard-pest-management]] for detailed identification, treatment, and scouting guidance.

## Thinning

### Why Thin
- Prevents alternate bearing (heavy/light year cycle)
- Increases fruit size
- Reduces limb breakage
- Improves air circulation and disease resistance

### When and How
- **Apples/Pears**: When fruit are dime- to quarter-sized. Thin to 6-8" apart.
- **Peaches**: When fruit are 1/2-3/4" diameter. Thin to 6-8" apart.
- **Plums**: 3-4" between fruit
- **Cherries**: Generally not thinned
- Aggressive thinning pays off — better to over-thin than under-thin

## Harvest

### Signs of Ripeness
- **Apples**: Background color changes green to yellow-cream; fruit separates easily with upward twist
- **Pears**: Pick when still firm — pears ripen off the tree
- **Peaches**: Ground color green to yellow; fruit softens; aromatic
- **Cherries**: Color fully developed; firm but not hard; stems pull easily
- **Figs**: Fruit droops; skin fully colored; soft; figs don't ripen further after picking

### Storage
- Short-term: Refrigerate 32-38°F, high humidity. Stone fruits 1-2 weeks max.
- Long-term (apples): 32-35°F, 90-95% humidity. Best keepers: GoldRush, Arkansas Black. 3-6 months.
- See [[food-preservation]] for canning, drying, freezing methods

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| No fruit set | Poor pollination, frost, insufficient chill | Check bloom overlap, protect from frost, verify chill hours |
| Sparse, yellow leaves | Nitrogen deficiency, water stress | Soil test, check irrigation |
| Leaves curl (peach) | Peach leaf curl | Copper spray next dormant season |
| Cankers on branches | Fire blight, winter injury | Prune out, improve air circulation |
