# [[cannabis-and-adhd]] / Attention Deficit Disorder

[[cannabis-health-index]] (CHI) score: **15** (out of 100)
Based on **7 clinical studies** reviewed by Blesching.

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is one of the most common
neurodevelopmental conditions, characterized by inattention, hyperactivity,
and impulsivity. While stimulant medications (methylphenidate, amphetamines)
remain the first-line treatment, a subset of patients seek cannabis as an
alternative or complementary approach, particularly those who experience
intolerable side effects from prescription stimulants.

## The Endocannabinoid System and ADHD

Research suggests that individuals with ADHD may have dysregulation in the
endocannabinoid system (ECS). Specifically, lower levels of anandamide and
altered CB1 receptor density have been observed in ADHD populations. The ECS
plays a modulatory role in dopamine signaling, which is centrally implicated
in attention and executive function -- the very domains impaired in ADHD.

CB1 receptors are concentrated in the prefrontal cortex, basal ganglia, and
cerebellum, all regions involved in attention regulation and motor control.
THC's partial agonism at CB1 receptors may help modulate dopaminergic tone,
potentially explaining why some patients report improved focus.

## Evidence Base (7 Studies)

The evidence for cannabis in ADHD is rated as preliminary but promising. The
studies include self-report surveys, small clinical observations, and a
limited number of controlled trials. Key findings include:

- Patients commonly report subjective improvement in concentration and
  task completion when using specific cannabis strains
- Some individuals use cannabis as a substitute for stimulant medications,
  reporting fewer side effects such as appetite suppression and insomnia
- A notable study found that adults with ADHD who used cannabis showed
  improved performance on cognitive tasks compared to non-users
- However, some studies also noted that heavy cannabis use could worsen
  inattention and working memory in certain individuals

## Strain Recommendations

Blesching distinguishes between strains useful for focus-enhancement versus
those better suited for managing hyperactivity:

### For Focus and Concentration

- **Sativa-dominant strains** with moderate THC and pinene or limonene
  terpenes are often preferred for daytime cognitive enhancement
- Pinene is thought to promote alertness and counteract THC-induced
  cognitive fog through acetylcholinesterase inhibition
- Recommended profiles: Jack Herer, Green Crack, Super Lemon Haze
- Low-to-moderate THC doses (5-15 mg) are recommended to avoid cognitive
  impairment while still benefiting from dopaminergic modulation

### For Hyperactivity and Restlessness

- **Indica-dominant strains** with myrcene and linalool terpenes may help
  calm nervous system overactivity and reduce physical restlessness
- These strains are more appropriate for evening use when hyperactivity
  interferes with sleep
- Recommended profiles: Granddaddy Purple, Northern Lights, Blue Dream
- CBD-dominant preparations may also help reduce anxiety associated with
  hyperactivity without impairing cognition

## Dosing Considerations

- Start with very low doses (2.5-5 mg THC) and titrate slowly
- Microdosing regimens may provide the best cognitive balance
- Avoid high-THC products which can paradoxically worsen attention
- CBD:THC ratios of 1:1 or 2:1 may offer therapeutic benefit with
  reduced psychoactive side effects
- Vaporization provides better dose control than combustion

## Risks and Precautions

- Cannabis is not a substitute for evidence-based ADHD treatments without
  medical supervision
- Adolescent use is particularly concerning due to potential impacts on
  brain development
- High-dose or chronic heavy use may impair working memory and executive
  function
- Patients should be monitored for cannabis use disorder, especially those
  with impulsive personality traits
