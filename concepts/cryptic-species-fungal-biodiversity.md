# Cryptic Species in Fungal Biodiversity

Cryptic species are two or more species that are morphologically
similar or identical but reproductively isolated and genetically
distinct. In mycology, cryptic species represent one of the most
significant challenges to accurate [[fungal-biodiversity-and-ecosystem-function]], the presence of multiple cryptic species may provide
functional redundancy or, conversely, represent irreplaceable
functional roles.

## Impact on Biogeographic Studies

Cryptic species complicate biogeographic interpretations. What
appears to be a single widely distributed cosmopolitan species
may in reality be a complex of regionally endemic species. The
hypothesis that the great majority of mycetozoan species are
cosmopolitan has been supported by observation, but this may
reflect the difficulty of detecting cryptic differences rather
than genuine range breadth. Molecular methods are essential for
testing biogeographic hypotheses involving putatively
cosmopolitan taxa.

## Conservation Implications

Cryptic species may be rare and threatened without being
recognized. Conservation assessments based solely on morphological
identification may miss endangered cryptic taxa. The inability
of an expert to name a species may indicate a high degree of
taxonomic uniqueness or endemism. [[dna-barcoding-fungal-identification]]
