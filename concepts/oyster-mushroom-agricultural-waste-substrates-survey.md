tags: [[cotter-oyster-mushroom-cultivation]]. Oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus spp.) possess the broadest substrate range of any cultivated mushroom due to their multilateral enzyme system capable of biodegrading nearly all types of available lignocellulosic wastes.

## Global Waste Potential

- Yearly world waste in agriculture: ~500 billion kg dry weight
- Yearly world waste in forestry: ~100 billion kg dry weight
- Total available: 600 billion kg could theoretically produce 360 billion kg fresh mushrooms annually (60 kg per capita)
- Using only 25% of burned cereal straw worldwide could yield 317 million metric tons of fresh mushrooms per year
- Current world mushroom production: ~6 billion kg total (1 kg per capita per year)
- Most surveyed wastes have C/N ratio between 32-600 and pH between 5.0-7.5

## Cereal Straw Substrates

- **Wheat straw** (Triticum aestivum): 40% cellulose, 20% lignin, 0.5% N, C/N 104; basic component for nearly all cultivated mushrooms; USD 0.1-0.2/kg; pasteurized (not fermented) for Pleurotus
- **Rice straw** (Oryza sativa): 41% cellulose, 13% lignin, 0.8% N, C/N 58-70; intensively used for Volvariella and Pleurotus; crushed form also used in Asia
- **Barley straw** (Hordeum vulgare): 43% cellulose, 14% lignin, C/N 72; biological efficiency 96% for Pleurotus
- **Oat straw** (Avena sativa): 40% cellulose, 17% lignin, 2% protein
- **Maize straw**: biological efficiency 52% for P. sajor-caju
- **Ragi straw** (Eleucena coracana): enriched with cottonseed meal for P. flabellatus
- **Buckwheat straw** (Polygonum fagopyrum)
- **Sorghum stover**: substrate for P. sajor-caju in Africa, alone or with cotton waste

## Legume Straws and Pods

- **Bean pods**: substrate component or bulk for Pleurotus
- **Bean straw**: basic substrate or component for Pleurotus
- **Pea straw** (Pisum sp.): 43% cellulose, 15% lignin, 0.9% N, C/N 45
- **Pea haulms**, **French bean haulms**, **Melilotus haulms**
- **Soybean stems**: best substrate for P. sajor-caju at 77% BE; soybean husks/straw good for P. ostreatus
- **Legume straws**: generally rich in N, suitable as Pleurotus substrates

## Grasses and Herbs

- **Alang-alang grass** (Imperata cylindrica): abundant in Asia, especially Indonesia
- **Elephant grass** (Pennisetum purpureum): tested in Cameroon and Zambia with satisfactory results
- **Wild grasses**: ~3% protein, ~12% lignin, ~23% hemicellulose, ~18% cellulose; must be dried for hay
- **Lemon grass leaves** (Cymbopogon citratus): BE 113% for Pleurotus
- **Reed** (Phragmites communis): 20% lignin, C/N 50
- **Fern** (high, low, kukot): semi-satisfactory results in Asia
- **Tea leaves**: partial or integral substrate
- **Mentha stalks**: after oil extraction, useful in combination with cereal straw
- **Cardamon pulp** (Elettaria cardamomum): BE 113%
- **Cinnamon leaves** (Cinnamon zeylanicum): BE 82%
- **Pepper leaves** (Piper nigrum): BE 57%

## Cotton and Textile Wastes

- **Cottonseed hulls** (Gossypium hirsitum): 1% N; best substrate without thermic treatment; BE 56-86%
- **Cotton wastes** (mill droppings, ball locules, husks, gin waste): BE 56-86%; total N 0.25-1.45%
- **Cotton straw silage**: chopped into 3cm particles, stored in silos of 450 tons in Israel
- **Textile industry waste**: card sweeping, card drops, blow gutter, chimney waste for Pleurotus

## Corn (Maize) Wastes

- **Corncobs**: 40% cellulose, 15% lignin, 0.4% N, C/N 129; tested first in Hungary 1956
- **Corn stalks**: 48% cellulose, 16% lignin, 0.8% N, C/N 63
- **Corn stover**: 65% polysaccharides + 30% lignin
- **Corn fiber**: when added to sawdust + rice bran, markedly increases yield for multiple species
- **Corn waste** (post-shelling dust, cleaning fiber, broken pith): satisfactory yields

## Sawdust and Wood Products

- **General sawdust**: single substrate for Pleurotus, Auricularia, Flammulina, Tremella, Pholiota, Hericium (mostly sterilized)
- **Beech/oak sawdust**: 44% cellulose, 26% lignin, C/N 244
- **Gum-wood sawdust**: first used 1960 for P. ostreatus
- **Coffee sawdust**: efficient when mixed with ipil-ipil powder
- **Wood shavings**: 0.3% N
- **Wood logs**: productive for P. quebeca; 75+ hardwood species suitable for Pleurotus
- **Tree bark**: alone or combined with wheat straw, corncobs, feather meal
- **Wood wastes**: ~140 tree species listed as useful (Stamets, 1993)
- **Populus wood logs**: slightly lower production than Salix logs
- **Salix wood logs** (willow): slightly higher P. ostreatus production than poplar

## Coffee and Tropical Wastes

- **Coffee parchment**: suitable with or without pasteurization
- **Coffee pulp**: sundried, stored, rehydrated for Pleurotus; good in Mexico for Auricularia with sugarcane pulp and corncobs
- **Banana leaves**: 1.45% N, very productive for Pleurotus
- **Banana pseudostems**: better results than sawdust or rice straw (Jandaik et al., 1976)
- **Tequila bagasse** (Agave tequilana): BE 60%
- **Cactus, Agave, Yucca**: useful as substrate components

## Sugarcane Wastes

- **Sugarcane bagasse** (Saccharum officinarum): 0.7% N; good production for Pleurotus; pure bagasse BE 15%
- **Sunflower husks**: very useful without pasteurization; moderate Stropharia production in open field
- **Sunflower stipes and heads**: suitable for Pleurotus and synthetic Agaricus compost

## Aquatic Weeds

- **Water hyacinth** (Eichhornia crassipes): abundant in Philippines, Indonesia, Africa, Bangladesh; must be dried; BE 50% for P. sajor-caju
- **Azolla**: fast-growing fern near tropical rivers; used for Agaricus, Pleurotus, Collybia
- **Water spinach** (Ipomoea aquatica): used in India for P. sajor-caju
- **Papyrus plants**: aquatic, abundant, dried for Pleurotus

## Other Notable Substrates

- **Groundnut shells**: 10% water hyacinth additive increased production by 22%
- **Coconut fiber pith/coir**: composted then used for Pleurotus or Volvariella
- **Coconut husks**: used for P. cystidiosus and Volvariella in India
- **Sesame stems**: BE 60% for P. sajor-caju
- **Citrus fruit peels**: reasonable Pleurotus production
- **Newspapers** (shredded): combined with rice bran or sawdust
- **Paper pulp by-product**: substrate component in South Africa
- **Paper waste**: for Pleurotus and Stropharia
- **Quinoa plant** (dried): used in Bolivia
- **Mustard straw**: useful for Pleurotus
- **Vegetable biomass**: bitter gourd, chili, cowpea, French beans, winged bean, pumpkin, tomato, okra — all gave good results
- **Manioc stipes and leaves** (Cassava): chopped for Pleurotus
- **Potato foliage**: useful for Stropharia and Pleurotus
- **Euphorbia rayleana**: successfully used for P. florida in India
- **Brassica haulms**: straw of rape, 22.7% lignin, C/N 70
- **Subtropical forest dead leaves** (Platanus spp.): BE 35%
- **Scrubs in India**: dried and pasteurized for P. sajor-caju

## Spent Substrates Reuse

- **Spent Pleurotus substrate**: suggested for King Stropharia
- **Spent Volvariella compost**: dried and re-used for P. sajor-caju at BE 80%
- **Spent Agaricus compost** + cotton waste: satisfactory Volvariella cultivation
- **Pleurotus substrate**: mixing half spent Volvariella + 20% rice bran

## Key Conclusions

About 90 distinct waste types are proven useful, but cereal straw, sawdust, and wood logs each subdivide into 100+ individual types — yielding approximately 200 different usable wastes total. Every grower can select the best substrate from locally available materials.
