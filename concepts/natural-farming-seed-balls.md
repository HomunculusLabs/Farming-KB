# Natural Farming Seed Balls

Seed balls (also called clay pellets or "earth dumplings") are Fukuoka's primary technique for direct seeding without cultivation. Seeds are encased in clay to protect them from birds, rodents, and drying until conditions are right for germination. See [[natural-farming-fukuoka]] for the full method context.

## Why Seed Balls?

Three problems seed balls solve:
1. **Bird predation** -- Seeds scattered on bare soil are eaten immediately. Clay shell hides them.
2. **Rodent damage** -- Overwintering seeds (especially rice) are vulnerable to mice. The hard pellet deters gnawing.
3. **Premature germination** -- Seeds sprout with the first rain, then die if conditions dry. The clay delays germination until consistent moisture arrives.
4. **Erosion** -- On slopes and bare ground, seeds wash away. Clay balls hold position.

## Fukuoka's Basic Recipe

### Bulk Pellets (Multiple Seeds Per Ball)

1. Mix seeds in at least a **5:1 to 10:1 ratio of well-crushed clay or red earth** to seed by volume
2. Add water and **knead until hard** by treading with feet
3. Pass kneaded mixture through a **half-inch screen**
4. Dry for a **half-day** in shade
5. Shape into **half-inch pellets** by rolling with hands or in a mixer
6. Aim for 4-5 seeds per pellet initially; with practice, approach 1 seed per pellet

### One-Seed Pellets (Precision)

1. Place individual seeds moistened with water in a **bamboo basket or mixer**
2. Sprinkle with **clay powder** while spraying water mist with an atomizer
3. Move the basket in a **swirling motion** so seeds tumble and coat evenly
4. Seeds grow to **quarter- to half-inch pellets**
5. For large batches, use a **concrete mixer**

### Clay Selection

- Use subsoil clay or red earth, not topsoil. Topsoil-containing clay crumbles too early in spring, exposing seeds to pests.
- The clay must be hard when dry but dissolve with sustained moisture.
- Avoid sandy soils; they won't hold shape. Pure clay works best.

## Optional Additions

Fukuoka's basic recipe is clay + seed only, but practitioners add:

- **Compost or composted manure** (1 part to 5 parts clay): provides initial nutrients for germination
- **Microbes**: coating with beneficial organisms ([[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]]) gives seedlings a head start
- **Red clay with a thin outer layer of topsoil**: nutrients on the outside, protection on the inside
- **Cayenne pepper**: deters ants from carrying away balls before rain

Do NOT add nitrogen fertilizer -- it can burn seedlings in the confined space of a ball.

## When to Apply

| Season | Application | Notes |
|--------|------------|-------|
| **Fall (Oct-Dec)** | Overwintering rice, clover, winter grains | Balls protect seed through freezing; germinate in spring |
| **Spring (Mar-Apr)** | Vegetables, cover crops, legumes | Apply just before or during rainy season |
| **Early summer** | Warm-season crops, greens | Must have reliable moisture |
| **Before rainy season** | Any seeds | Ideal timing -- rain triggers germination |

Fukuoka scattered rice seed balls in mid-November or later over standing straw and clover. Germination occurred naturally in spring with warming temperatures and spring rains.

## What Works Well in Seed Balls

- **Grains**: rice, barley, wheat, rye, oats -- Fukuoka's primary use
- **Legumes**: clover, cowpea, mung bean, adzuki bean (birds eat exposed legume seed; balls protect them)
- **Root vegetables**: daikon, turnip, radish -- Fukuoka noted these grow surprisingly large
- **Brassicas**: mustard, Chinese cabbage -- vigorous germinators
- **Green manure**: alfalfa, bur clover, black wattle
- **Wildflowers**: for [[cover-cropping]] and habitat creation

## What Doesn't Work Well

- **Very large seeds** (beans over 1/2 inch): too bulky for practical ball sizes
- **Seeds needing light to germinate**: clay blocks light; transplant instead
- **Extremely tiny seeds** (poppy, lettuce): difficult to handle individually; mix with sand and scatter directly
- **Perennials with slow germination**: may not establish before weeds overwhelm them

## Guerrilla Gardening Applications

Seed balls are the primary tool for [[urban-permaculture-design]]:

- Fukuoka proposed scattering seed balls over deserts from airplanes, containing green manure trees (black wattle), clover, alfalfa, and grain seeds
- He tested this concept in Somalia, distributing seed balls to farmers and refugees
- Urban guerrilla gardeners toss seed balls into vacant lots, road margins, and abandoned land
- Balls can be made months in advance and stored dry -- perfect for group events and community action
- Use native species mixes for ecological restoration; food crops for community food security

## Large-Scale vs Small-Scale

| Scale | Method | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| **Garden (under 1/4 acre)** | Hand rolling in bamboo basket | 1-2 hours for 1,000+ balls |
| **Farm (1-10 acres)** | Concrete mixer, drying screens | Fukuoka prepared enough for his fields by treading with feet |
| **Restoration (10+ acres)** | Mechanical pelleting equipment | Rent a commercial seed coater; add organic binder |
| **Guerrilla (any)** | Hand-made, distributed by walking | Portable, no tools needed beyond clay and seeds |

## Storage

- Dry thoroughly before storing
- Keep in breathable bags (burlap, paper) -- NOT sealed plastic (moisture causes premature sprouting)
- Store in cool, dark, dry place
- Viability: most seeds last 1-2 years in balls; use within the season for best results
