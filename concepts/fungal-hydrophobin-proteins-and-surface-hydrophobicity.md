## Class 1 Hydrophobin Rodlet Layers

The functionally important wall surface hydrophobicity in lichen thalli
has been assumed to be generated by mycobiont-derived secondary
metabolites, which crystallize on hyphal surfaces within the medullary
layer. However, water-repellency is also a characteristic feature of
species with no medullary lichen compounds such as Xanthoria and
Peltigera spp.

In freeze-etch preparations a semicrystalline rodlet layer was resolved
on wall surfaces of medullary hyphae of vegetative thalli of Xanthoria
and Peltigera spp. and on aerial hyphae in the photobiont stratum of the
lichenized basidiocarps of Dictyonema glabratum, a tropical
basidiomycete. These rodlet layers are formed by class 1 hydrophobins.

### Species-Specific Hydrophobins

In various Xanthoria spp. (haploid mycelia), one hydrophobin per species
has been characterized. In Dictyonema glabratum (dikaryotic hyphae),
three different hydrophobins have been identified. As in many
non-lichenized fungi, hydrophobin gene expression in lichenized asco- and
basidiomycetes is developmentally regulated.

## Enhancement by Secondary Metabolites

Mycobiont-derived medullary secondary compounds of lichen-forming
ascomycetes crystallize on and within the water-repellent, proteinaceous
rodlet layer that spreads over the photobiont cells, thus enhancing the
hydrophobicity of the medullary and algal layers. Water-repellent wall
surface layers overlie highly hydrophilic, often very thick glucan layers
of hyphal walls and channel the flow of solutes from the thallus surface
to the interior and vice versa during the wetting and drying cycles.

### Aerial Hyphae and Reproductive Structures
Hydrophobins coat aerial hyphae and conidiophores, making them
hydrophobic and enabling them to break through the water-air interface.
This is essential for spore dispersal in many fungal species.

### Attachment to Surfaces
Hydrophobins mediate fungal attachment to hydrophobic surfaces such as
plant cuticles, insect exoskeletons, and synthetic polymers. This
property is important for both pathogenic and saprotrophic fungi.

### Spore Hydrophobicity
Hydrophobin coatings on spores reduce wettability, influence dispersal
patterns, and protect spores from environmental stresses. The class 1
hydrophobin rodlet layer on spore surfaces is a common feature across
many fungal species and contributes to the remarkable environmental
resilience of fungal spores.

## Biotechnological Applications

The unique self-assembly properties of hydrophobins have attracted
significant interest for biotechnological applications, including surface
coating, emulsion stabilization, and as fusion tags for protein
purification. Their ability to reversibly switch between hydrophilic and
hydrophobic states makes them versatile biomolecular tools.
