## Grow Room Design Fundamentals

The most productive setups utilize two rooms: a vegetative room (about one-quarter the size of the flowering room) for mothers, clones, and [[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]] range: 72-76°F (22-24°C). At night, temperature can drop 5-10°F with little effect. Daytime temperatures above 85°F (29°C) or below 60°F (15°C) slow growth.

When CO2 is enriched to 1200-1500 ppm, a temperature of 85°F (29°C) promotes more rapid gas exchange and faster growth.

A thermostat can control vent fans. Single-stage maintains same temp day/night. Two-stage can maintain different daytime and nighttime temperatures, saving on heating costs.

## CO2 Enrichment

Carbon dioxide (CO2) enrichment can increase growth rates and yields by 20-100% when all other growing conditions are optimized. Cannabis plants use CO2 during photosynthesis, and the ambient level of approximately 400 ppm can be raised to 1200-1500 ppm in an enclosed grow room. Above 1500 ppm, diminishing returns set in and above 2000 ppm, CO2 can become toxic to plants.

CO2 enrichment is most effective during the light period when plants are photosynthetically active. It should be turned off during the dark period since plants respire (release CO2) at night. For CO2 enrichment to work effectively, temperature should be raised to 80-85°F (27-29°C) and light intensity must be high — at least 5000 lumens per square foot. Without adequate light, plants cannot use the extra CO2.

CO2 can be supplied from:
- **Compressed CO2 tanks**: Regulated with a flow meter and timer — most precise method
- **CO2 generators**: Burn natural gas or propane to produce CO2 — produce heat as a byproduct
- **Fermentation**: Simple but produces minimal CO2 — impractical for most grows

## Odor Control

Cannabis produces strong odors during flowering, particularly in the last weeks. An activated carbon filter connected to the exhaust system is the most common and effective odor control method.
