# Cannabis Forms, Preparations, and Resin Products

## Source
- **Book:** The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]]: Combining the Science of [[cervantes-medical-marijuana-pharmacology]] with Mindfulness Techniques to Heal 100 Chronic Symptoms and Diseases
- **Author:** Uwe Blesching, PhD (2015)

## Overview

Blesching catalogs the diverse forms in which cannabis is used medicinally, ranging from dried flower buds to concentrated extracts and pharmaceutical preparations. Each form has distinct pharmacokinetic properties affecting onset time, duration of effect, bioavailability, and [[psilocybin-therapeutic-applications]]. This page details the major categories of cannabis preparations, their [[hashish-production-methods]], advantages, and risks.

## Dried Cannabis Flower

The most traditional and widely used form. Dried buds, flowers, and leaves of the female cannabis plant are consumed by smoking, vaporizing, or as a precursor to other preparations.

### Key Characteristics
- Contains the full spectrum of cannabinoids, terpenoids, and flavonoids produced by the plant
- Potency varies significantly by strain, growing conditions, harvest timing, and storage
- Cannabinoid content degrades over time with exposure to light, heat, and oxygen
- Must be stored in airtight, light-proof containers for optimal preservation

### Advantages
- Full [[blesching-cannabis-cannabinoid-terpene-entourage-effect]] from whole-plant chemistry
- Immediate onset when smoked or vaporized (seconds to minutes)
- Easy dose titration with inhalation (one puff at a time)
- Wide availability through dispensaries

### Disadvantages
- Smoking introduces combustion byproducts (carbon monoxide, tar, particulate matter)
- Dried material is bulky to store and transport
- Potency can vary between batches, making precise dosing difficult

## Vaporization

Vaporizing heats cannabis to a precise temperature that evaporates cannabinoids and terpenoids below the combustion point of plant matter (approximately 180-210°C / 356-410°F).

### Advantages Over Smoking
- Eliminates inhalation of carbon compounds from burned plant material and paper
- Produces a cleaner vapor with fewer respiratory irritants
- Preserves more of the volatile terpenoid profile
- More efficient extraction of cannabinoids from the same amount of material
- Preferred delivery method for patients with lung conditions

### Disadvantages
- Quality vaporizers represent a significant upfront investment ($100-500+)
- Battery-powered portable units require regular charging
- Learning curve for optimal temperature settings and draw technique
- Some patients find vapor less satisfying than smoke initially

## Edible Preparations

Cannabis can be infused into a wide variety of foods. The active cannabinoids are fat-soluble, so they are typically incorporated through:

- **Cannabutter:** Butter infused with cannabis, used as a direct substitute in recipes
- **Infused oils:** Coconut oil, olive oil, or hemp seed oil infused with cannabis
- **Baked goods:** Cookies, brownies, and other treats
- **Savory dishes:** Soups, sauces, and main courses

### Pharmacokinetics of Edibles
- **Onset:** 45 minutes to 1.5 hours (longer on a full stomach)
- **Duration:** 4-8 hours, significantly longer than inhaled forms
- **Metabolism:** THC is converted to 11-hydroxy-THC in the liver, which is more psychoactive and crosses the blood-brain barrier more readily
- **Effect character:** Edible effects are often described as more body-centered and sedative compared to the head-focused effects of inhalation

### Critical Dosing Considerations
- Effects are delayed — patients must resist the urge to consume more before the first dose takes effect
- Overconsumption produces effects lasting many hours that can be intense and unpleasant
- Precise measurement using standardized infused products or a kitchen scale is essential
- Starting dose should be 20% below the previously established effective dose

## Fresh Raw Cannabis

Fresh, unheated cannabis leaf and flower contain cannabinoids in their acid forms — THCA and CBDA — which are not psychoactive. This makes raw cannabis suitable for patients who need the therapeutic benefits of cannabinoids without the intoxicating effects.

### Consumption Methods
- **Raw salads:** Fresh leaves mixed with other greens
- **Juicing:** Leaves juiced and diluted with other vegetable juices to mask bitterness
- **Smoothies:** Blended with fruits and vegetables

### Therapeutic Rationale
- THCA and CBDA have documented anti-inflammatory, anti-nausea, and neuroprotective properties
- High doses can be consumed without psychoactive effects
- Raw cannabis provides a concentrated source of acidic cannabinoids that are largely lost during drying and heating
- Patient testimonials and physician case studies suggest benefits, though controlled [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-schizophyllan-spg-clinical-trials]] are limited

### Limitations
- Requires access to fresh plant material (best for home growers)
- Bitter taste requires masking with other ingredients
- Acidic cannabinoids are less well-studied than their decarboxylated forms
- Shelf life is very short compared to dried preparations
