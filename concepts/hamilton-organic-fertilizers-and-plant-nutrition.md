## Major Elements Required for Plant Growth

Nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) are the major elements needed in the largest quantities. Also important are magnesium, calcium, and sulfur. Too much of one element can inactivate another. For example, too much potassium can inactivate magnesium.

### Nitrogen

One of the most important plant foods, this is a component of chlorophyll and a vital part of the structure of plant protein. It is the element in the soil responsible for the vegetative growth of shoots and leaves. Deficiency is not unusual because nitrogen is easily lost by leaching in open soils and can be depleted by digging in unrotted material. Plant leaves become yellowed, particularly the older ones, and plants are stunted.

Treatment: Apply a high-nitrogen fertilizer such as dried blood (12 to 14 percent nitrogen).

### Phosphorus

Mainly responsible for good root growth, so a deficiency causes slight stunting of the plant. It can be diagnosed by a distinct blue color which affects the older leaves first. The root system is likely to be underdeveloped.

Treatment: Use an application of bone meal fertilizer (22 percent phosphorus).

### Potassium (Potash)

Required in about the same quantities as nitrogen. It affects the size and quality of flowers and fruit, and is essential for the synthesis of protein and carbohydrates. Deficiency results in small, inferior flowers and fruit, stunted plants, and yellowing around the edge of older leaves followed by brown scorching.

Treatment: Use an application of rock potash (10.5 percent potassium). Rock potash is insoluble and remains in the soil for long periods, enabling plants to take it up as required.

### Magnesium

Needed in much larger quantities than many gardeners realize. Magnesium should be present in about the same quantities as phosphorus. It is also a component of chlorophyll, so a deficiency causes yellowing between the veins of the leaves, generally affecting older leaves first.
