## Permaculture Community Legal Structure

The legal and financial architecture of a [[permaculture]] community is as important as its physical design. [[bill-mollison]] presents a specific organizational model—developed through the Tagari community in Australia—that uses a layered trust structure to achieve tax efficiency, risk insulation, land accumulation, and international cooperation while remaining consistent with [[permaculture-ethics]] [[permaculture-ethics-of-earth-care-and-people-care]], people care, and non-accumulation of wealth beyond need.

## The Three-Layer Structure

### 1. The Community

The foundation is a group of people who agree to a common set of principles and ethics. This community may be designers living in one house, or individuals scattered across the globe. No legal incorporation is required at this level—the community exists through shared values and mutual agreement. The three core ethics are:

- **Earth care**: Stewardship of natural systems
- **People care**: Responsibility to human communities
- **Non-profiteering**: Not accumulating wealth beyond one’s needs

### 2. The Institute (Public Trust)

The community forms a company—called the [[permaculture-institute]] in the Australian model. This company serves solely as a trustee; it does not trade, nor does it receive or disburse cash flow. It issues shares of one dollar to each member, and all members can serve as directors.

The Institute operates under a publicly oriented trust drawn according to the laws of the host country, with language such as “for the good of all Australians” (adapted to the relevant jurisdiction). Its mandate covers three broad areas: health, education, and agriculture.

**Key advantages of the Institute structure:**

- **Tax immunity**: Generally exempt from land taxes, rates, and local government charges—shared by schools, churches, and other public institutions
- **Research links**: Automatic membership in networks like the World Free [[permaculture-designers-manual-seed-exchange]] (connecting 4,600 institutes and 680 botanical gardens globally), enabling free seed acquisition from anywhere in the world
- **Joint research capacity**: Can undertake collaborative research with any similar institute, with options for joint funding, staff arrangements, and shared facilities
- **Insulation from seizure**: Extremely rare for governments to raid public trusts, as doing so would threaten all schools, churches, and public institutions with similar status
- **Wealthy precedents**: Mirrors the structure used by major foundations like the Ford Foundation for their own money flow systems

### 3. The Trading Trust (Non-Profit Trading Corporation)

The critical innovation is a second trust, completely separate from the Institute Trust though under the same company trusteeship. This Trading Trust is a non-profit trading corporation with distinctive characteristics:

- **Owns no buildings**: Occupies structures loaned by the Institute at no cost
- **Employs no staff**: Staffed by community members at no charge
- **Owns no chattels**: Borrows movable fixtures, furnishings, vehicles, and equipment from a political party or small external society
- **Incurs no risk**: The trust itself holds nothing that can be lost

This structure closely parallels that of some merchant banks worldwide, where the operational entity holds no assets while facilitating significant financial flows.

## Trading Trust Operations

The Trading Trust registers and operates businesses that generate income for the community. Suitable businesses include:

- **Travel agencies**: Community members need to travel for consultancy and teaching; agents may receive 15% discounts, free tickets, or free flights
- **Publishing companies**: Information dissemination is core to permaculture’s mission
- **Consultancies**: Transmitting permaculture knowledge globally through paid professional services
- **Real estate operations**: Managing property transactions on behalf of the community
- **[[spanish-cannabis-seed-companies]]**: Central to the agricultural mission and community self-sufficiency

The Trading Trust can also enter into business-sharing arrangements: holding shares in consumer cooperatives, workers’ cooperatives, and other collaborative enterprises; holding residential shares in seed companies; and participating in international trading cooperation.

## Profit Distribution

As a non-profit corporation, the Trading Trust must distribute all profits. The distribution follows four channels:

1. **Institute funding (largest portion)**: This creates a double tax exemption—the distribution itself is not taxable, and it constitutes a tax-deductible donation to a public trust
2. **Public charity**: Donations to charitable causes (such as aboriginal ethnic groups), providing another tax deduction
3. **Political party donations**: An amount exactly equal to the cost of equipment (typewriters, desks, vehicles, tractors) that the party then purchases and loans back to the Trading Trust
4. **Community worker support (smallest portion)**: For the Tagari community, this was $21 per week for every person—regardless of age or gender

## Government Business Aids

The Australian legal framework provides several automatic business aids that the Trading Trust can access:

- **Export travel remission**: Businesses engaged in exporting receive reimbursement of up to 90% of overseas travel fares, plus full remission of accommodation costs for agents
- **Agent network benefits**: Appointed agents in any country, flying on behalf of the exporter, receive the same fare remissions plus accommodation remission
- **Invention development refunds**: Full government refund of development costs for any salable invention
- **These aids are automatic**: No applications, no bureaucrats, no forms—simply normal channels of capital flow from government to business

The export travel system works particularly well when combined with a community travel agency. A consultant flying to Japan or America on permaculture business can have 75% of fares returned plus all accommodation costs. If the travel agency also receives airline discounts or fare remissions, the consultant may actually profit from the travel itself.

## Land Management

Land handling follows a strict hierarchy designed to insulate the Institute from risk:

### Acquisition Pathway
All land donations flow to the Trading Trust, never directly to the Institute. The Trading Trust pays all transfer costs. Only fully owned properties—often with financing arrangements attached—pass from the Trading Trust to the Institute as gifts.

### Property Types
Real property includes buildings, land, equipment, and rights. Properties may include warehouses, office buildings, inner urban spaces, suburban lots, rural acreage, or preservation land.

### Tenancy Arrangements
Land going out to communities, cooperatives, or individuals can be offered through:
- Limited tenancy
- Life tenancy (inheritable or non-inheritable)
- Transferable and inheritable tenancy
- Public land (open to all)

Houses not fully paid off remain in the Trading Trust and are rented to community members. Rent is remitted for those working within the Trading Trust; those who leave incur rent even if they originally donated the house.

### Land Accumulation
Land can come from public donations, government grants, local government surplus land, cooperative shares (sometimes exchanged for design work), and international offers. Mollison states that there is “absolutely no problem” in acquiring several million dollars worth of land within twelve months, provided a group is organized to handle the logistics.

## Worker Compensation and Community Economics

The Tagari community operated with all members receiving identical compensation ($21 per week), regardless of age, gender, or role. Members lived below minimum income levels, avoiding income tax. Clothing came from charitable organizations. The Trading Trust provided transport, seeds, and formerly bulk food.

Mollison describes this community as “the most heavily self-taxed group in all of Australia” because virtually all money flows to the public. He characterizes this as freedom: “We enjoy the best of foods, great company. We do right well, but only because people like us.”

## International Structure and Bioregional Delegation

The legal model is designed for international scalability. Urban groups in other regions can join without going through the legal setup process—they simply become partners, traders, or Institute directors for their local area. This enables:

- **Bioregional delegation**: Responsibility distributed to local areas with local knowledge
- **International cooperation**: Closely following merchant banking systems that operate globally
- **Individual agent networks**: Even isolated individuals can be appointed as agents, benefiting both themselves and the community

## Design Principles Behind the Structure

1. **Risk insulation**: The Institute (public trust) takes no risks. All risk resides in the Trading Trust, which holds nothing of value.
2. **Tax efficiency**: Double exemptions through donation pathways between trusts
3. **Non-accumulation**: No individual profits; all surplus flows to public purposes
4. **Functional separation**: Research/education (Institute) is separated from commerce (Trading Trust), yet both serve the community
5. **Scalability**: The structure works for a single household or an international network
6. **Power distribution**: No individual has authority over another; the community exists only through mutual agreement
