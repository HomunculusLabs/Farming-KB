## The Three Universal Marks

Palmer identified three characteristics that, appearing together, denote the
sub-genus Amanita. These marks are visible without magnification and require no
chemical tests:

**1. The Volva (Wrapper)**

The base of the stem, close to the soil, has a white wrapper around it, "from
which the plant seems to grow." This volva encloses the young mushroom before it
emerges from the ground, like an egg shell. In mature specimens, the volva may
be reduced to a cup-like structure or a series of concentric rings at the stem
base. It is the single most important diagnostic feature of the genus.

**2. The Annulus (Ring)**

About two-thirds the length of the stem from the ground is "a similar membrane
in the form of a veil or ragged ring, encircling the stem." This ring is the

to include at least one specimen. One mushroom "the size of a silver dollar
mixed with a wholesome dish" is enough to make five or six persons dangerously
sick. The genus demands respect and attention from every forager.
