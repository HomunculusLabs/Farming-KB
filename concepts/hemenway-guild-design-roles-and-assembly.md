# Hemenway Guild Design Roles and Assembly

A guild is a harmoniously interwoven group of plants and animals, often centered around one major species, that benefits humans while creating habitat. Guilds bridge the gap between conventional vegetable gardens and wildlife gardening by creating plant communities that act and feel like natural landscapes while including humans in their webwork.

## The Three Sisters Guild

The Native American triad of corn, beans, and squash is the classic guild example:
- **Corn**: forms a trellis for bean vines; roots secrete special sugars that feed the nitrogen-fixing bacteria on bean roots
- **Beans**: draw nitrogen from air via symbiotic bacteria, converting it to plant-available form for all three sisters
- **Squash**: broad leaves form a living parasol, inhibiting weeds and keeping soil cool and moist

Together, the Three Sisters produce about 20% more calories than corn grown alone in an equal-sized plot, with less water and fertilizer.

### The Fourth Sister
In the Southwest, Rocky Mountain bee plant (Cleome serrulata) adds a fourth dimension:
- Powerful attractant for beneficial insects that pollinate beans and squash
- Young leaves, flowers, and seedpods are edible
- Accumulates iron
- Source of deep-hued paint for Anasazi pottery
- Mentioned in Tewa people's songs alongside corn, beans, and squash

By adding bee plant, the guild extends into three kingdoms: animal, plant, and bacterial.

## Guild Design Roles

Every well-designed guild needs elements to fill these roles:

### 1. Central Element
- Usually a food-producing plant (fruit tree, nut tree, or other staple)
- Could also be a wildlife-attracting tree, nitrogen-fixing tree, or timber-producer
- Size determines how many associated plants can be supported

### 2. Grass-Suppressing Bulbs
- Shallow roots keep grasses from competing with tree surface roots
- Should be planted in a circle at the drip line of the tree
- Must be spring-flowering and summer-dormant (won't rob water in heat)
- Examples: daffodils (toxic to animals -- repels deer and gophers), camas (edible), garlic chives, ramps, Egyptian onions
- Plant two circles: one around the trunk, one at the drip line

### 3. Insect- and Bird-Attracting Plants
- Lure pollinators for boosting fruit set
- Attract predatory wasps that feast on pest larvae (borers, codling moths)
- Examples: dill, fennel, coriander, bee balm
- Flowering shrubs (butterfly bush, fuchsia) nearby encourage insectivorous birds
- Commercial tip: select flowers blooming just before and after (not during) apple blossom time

### 4. Mulch Plants
- Build soil in-place, eliminating need to wheelbarrow compost
- Examples: comfrey (hack down 4-5 times per summer), artichokes, cardoon, rhubarb, clovers, nasturtiums
- Slash and leave to compost in place
- Nutrient-rich greenery delivers minerals and organic matter
- Resulting soil supports diverse worms, fungi, bacteria that suppress disease through competition

### 5. Nutrient Accumulators
- Deep taproots dredge up potassium, magnesium, calcium, sulfur from mineral soil
- Examples: chicory, dandelion, yarrow, plantain
- As guild matures, nutrients recycle internally -- accumulators naturally decline
- Can be pulled and replaced when they become redundant

### 6. Nitrogen Fixers
- Keep nutrients cycling within the plant community
- Perennials preferred: Dutch or New Zealand white clover, alfalfa, lupine
- Self-seeding annuals: vetch, cowpeas, fava beans
- Fava beans add edible harvest on top of nitrogen fixation

### 7. Soil Fumigants and Pest Repellents
- Exude substances that repel pests
- Examples: nasturtiums, certain marigolds
- Least understood role -- use with caution (may repel beneficials too)

### 8. Habitat Nooks
- Piled stones, logs, brush near the guild
- Small ponds and puddles
- Attract lizards, frogs, snakes, birds
- Predators cull pest populations (caterpillars, aphids, slugs) with chilling efficiency

## Design Principles

### Start Simple, Add Connections
Begin with something known and basic (Three Sisters), gradually add connections.

### Multifunctional Members
Choose plants with multiple functions:
- **Comfrey**: mulch, medicine, insect attractor, potassium accumulator, fertilizing tea
- **Clover/alfalfa**: nitrogen fixation AND bee attraction
- **Plantain/yarrow**: nutrient accumulation AND medicinal
- **Artichokes**: mulch AND food
- **Dandelion/chicory**: nutrient accumulation AND edible

### Sizing the Guild
- One apple tree (central)
- One or two artichokes
- Several comfrey plants
- A dozen insectary plants
- Dozens of bulbs
- Hundreds of clovers

With dwarf trees, avoid climbers like vetch and oversized plants like artichokes.

### Harvest Consideration
By the time mid- to late-fruiting varieties are ripe, much undergrowth has died down. Summer apples require care with ladder placement but the reduced maintenance balances any inconvenience.

## The Guild Advantage

A guild restores nature's role as the gardener's partner. A solitary apple tree in a clean-swept, chemically fertilized orchard floor requires: spraying for disease, watering, fertilizing, importing bee hives. The only fungi able to thrive are pests. A guild shifts the workload onto nature's broad back, creating a connected, self-maintaining community.
