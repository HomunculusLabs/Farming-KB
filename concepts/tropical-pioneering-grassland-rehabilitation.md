# Tropical Pioneering and Grassland Rehabilitation

Tropical pioneering is the deliberate, ethically grounded process of rehabilitating degraded tropical landscapes back toward productive, stable, and diverse ecosystems. It relies heavily on leguminous plants — both ground covers and trees — to rebuild soil, fix nitrogen, and outcompete invasive grasses and weeds.

## Ethical Conditions for Pioneering

Tropical pioneering is justified only when land has already been degraded by human activity. Mollison identifies three conditions under which rehabilitation should proceed:

1. **Grasslands from burning and over-grazing** — original forest cover destroyed by repeated fire and livestock pressure, leaving degraded pasture with minimal tree cover.
2. **Semi-forested clearings and old monoculture plantations** — partial tree cover or plantation remnants where ecological function is diminished but some structure remains.
3. **Logged and burnt forest with reject logs, stumps, and weedy regrowth** — the most severely disturbed category, where primary forest has been cleared and left to regenerate chaotically.

These conditions ensure pioneering restores what has already been lost, not displaces existing functional ecosystems.

## Envisaged End Results

The goal is productive, managed landscapes performing multiple ecological functions:

- **Terrace culture and water absorption** — earthworks capturing and infiltrating rainfall.
- **Extensive aquaculture or substantial dams** — integrated water bodies for food and habitat.
- **Polycultural forests** — diverse, multi-strata tree systems producing food, fuel, and timber.
- **Managed forestry for perpetual yields** — sustained-yield harvest without degradation.

These outcomes are not mutually exclusive; resilient designs combine several or all of them.

## Problems of Tropical Grassland Management

Tropical grasslands are the main problem of the wet-dry tropics. Once forest cover is removed, a cascade of degradation follows:

- **Soil erosion** accelerates as bare soil faces heavy rains and dry-season winds.
- **Rank grasses** dominate in wet seasons, suppressing other vegetation and creating fire hazards.
- **Inflammable, low-nutrition feed** prevails in drought; fires further degrade soil and eliminate seedlings.
- **Broken nutrient cycles** are fundamental — the tree/grass/browsing animal loop is destroyed by deforestation.

Fire accelerates every aspect. Very few trees survive in established tropical grasslands, making it essential to actively re-establish tree legumes.

## Re-establishing Tree Legumes in Grasslands

The primary strategy is introducing legumes at both ground and canopy levels — they fix nitrogen and outcompete rank grasses.
