# Cannabis Reflux Extraction Apparatus

Reflux extraction is the foundational technique in [[lemon-cannabis-alchemy-advanced-refinement-techniques]], providing a safe and efficient method for dissolving the [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]] of cannabis into a solvent. The reflux apparatus, as described in Ben Lemon's Cannabis Alchemy, is designed to be constructed from common household and hardware store items, making cannabis oil extraction accessible without specialized laboratory equipment. The design prioritizes safety while enabling the extraction of cannabinoids from large quantities of plant material.

## Principles of Reflux Extraction

Reflux extraction works by boiling a solvent in contact with cannabis material and continuously returning the condensed solvent vapors to the extraction vessel. As the solvent boils, cannabinoid-rich vapors rise, contact a cooled surface, recondense into liquid, and drip back down through the plant material. This continuous cycling ensures thorough extraction while preventing the loss of solvent through evaporation.

The key safety advantage of reflux is that the solvent is contained within a closed system. Unlike open-vessel evaporation, reflux prevents the release of flammable solvent fumes into the surrounding environment. The entire extraction occurs within the apparatus, with the only safety concern being the potential for pressure buildup, which is managed through proper apparatus design.

## Basic Apparatus Design

The classical reflux apparatus for [[comparison-cannabis-extraction-basics-vs-extraction-methods]] consists of two nested vessels within a water bath. The inner vessel is a stainless-steel pot containing the cannabis material and solvent. The outer vessel is a large stew pot filled with boiling water that serves as an indirect heat source.

The top of the stew pot is covered with a lid that has been modified to hold ice. As solvent fumes rise from the inner pot, they contact the ice-cooled lid, condense back to liquid, and drip down into the extraction pot. This simple but effective condensation system eliminates the need for laboratory-grade condensers.

A sheet of stainless-steel screen is placed in the inner pot to support the cannabis material above the bottom. This allows the solvent to drain freely for collection after extraction is complete. A collection pan can replace the cannabis material when it is time to distill off the solvent from the oil.

## Construction Details

The inner pot should not be over two-thirds full when the cannabis material is covered with half again its volume of solvent. This headspace is necessary to accommodate boiling and prevent solvent from bubbling over into the water bath.

The stew pot lid must fit snugly to contain the solvent fumes. Ice is placed on top of the lid, and the [[snow-mold-fungi-cold-temperature-plant-pathogens]] causes rapid condensation of the solvent vapors. The ice must be replenished as it melts to maintain condensation efficiency.

The stainless-steel pot containing the cannabis and solvent sits in the stew pot, elevated above the bottom by the screen. The boiling water in the outer pot heats the inner pot indirectly, preventing the solvent from reaching its flash point through direct contact with the heat source.

## Solvent Options for Reflux

Several solvents are suitable for cannabis reflux extraction, each with different properties and tradeoffs.

[[psilocybin-methyl-alcohol-extraction-technique]] (methanol, wood alcohol, boiling point 64 degrees Celsius) is inexpensive and effective but highly toxic. Ingestion of even small amounts can cause permanent damage, and any traces remaining in the final product pose health risks. Methanol also extracts some non-psychoactive tars that require additional purification steps.

Ethyl alcohol (ethanol, grain alcohol, boiling point 78 degrees Celsius) is the preferred solvent for safety. It is significantly less toxic than methanol and produces a cleaner initial extract. Denatured ethanol is commonly used for cost reasons, provided the denaturants can be removed during processing. Ethanol's higher boiling point means slower extraction but also reduces fire risk.

Isopropyl alcohol (boiling point 82 degrees Celsius) has extraction properties very similar to ethanol but is not recommended for internal use. It produces clean extracts and is readily available as rubbing alcohol, though the 70 percent concentration commonly sold contains water that must be accounted for.

Petroleum ether (boiling point 30-60 degrees Celsius) is a light, non-polar solvent that selectively extracts cannabinoids while leaving many impurities behind. However, it is extremely flammable, presents significant explosion risk, and is usually available only through chemical supply houses. It is more commonly used for secondary purification rather than primary extraction.

## Extraction Procedure

Cannabis material is ground to a fine powder to maximize surface area for solvent contact. The grinding should be done just prior to extraction to minimize terpene loss through evaporation from the increased surface area. Finer grinding reduces the extraction time and the amount of solvent required.

The powdered cannabis is placed in the stainless-steel pot on the screen, and solvent is added to cover the material. The pot is placed in the water bath, the apparatus is sealed, and ice is placed on the lid. The water bath is heated to boiling, which causes the solvent to boil within the inner pot.

Refluxing continues for three to four hours, during which most of the essential oils of the cannabis material dissolve in the solvent. The apparatus should be monitored and the ice replenished as needed.

## Solvent Removal

After extraction, the solvent-cannabis solution is drained from the plant material. The plant material is washed with additional clean solvent to recover remaining oil. The combined solvent-oil solution is then subjected to distillation to remove the solvent.

For distillation, the collection pan replaces the expended cannabis material in the inner pot. The solvent-oil solution is placed in the inner pot and the apparatus reassembled. As the solution boils, the pure solvent vaporizes, condenses on the ice-cooled lid, and drips into the collection pan. The collected solvent is essentially pure and can be saved for future extractions.

After the solvent is removed, the inner pot is kept in boiling water to drive off any remaining traces of solvent. The temperature of boiling water (100 degrees Celsius) exceeds the boiling point of all commonly used extraction solvents, ensuring complete removal.

## Safety Considerations

Boiling flammable solvents is inherently dangerous. The indirect heating through a water bath is a critical safety feature that prevents direct contact between the heat source and the solvent. The closed reflux system prevents accumulation of explosive fumes in the workspace.

A water-soaked towel kept nearby is recommended as an emergency fire suppression measure. If a fire occurs, smothering it with the wet towel is the appropriate response, as water should never be poured on a solvent fire.

All operations should be conducted in a well-ventilated area away from open flames, sparks, and pilot lights. No smoking should be permitted in the vicinity. The operator should wear safety glasses and have access to a fire extinguisher rated for chemical fires.

## Cycling Extraction Technique

An enhancement to the basic reflux method involves multiple soaking cycles before soxhleting. The cannabis is soaked in fresh solvent for 24 hours, then the solvent is poured off and replaced with fresh solvent for another 24-hour soak. After several such cycles, the soxhleting time is dramatically reduced, often to less than two hours for complete extraction. This approach trades time (extended soaking over several days) for reduced active processing time.
