## Metol Testing and Bluing Reaction in Mushrooms The science and mythology of the bluing reaction in psilocybin-containing mushrooms, and the role of metol testing as an identification tool, including why popular assumptions about both are often incorrect. ## The Bluing Phenomenon The bluing reaction is the most well-known macroscopic indicator of psilocybin-containing mushrooms. When the flesh of a psilocybin mushroom is bruised or injured, it develops a blue or blue-green discoloration. This reaction is caused by the oxidation of psilocin, not psilocybin. The bluing reaction is obvious in the more potent species, especially those high in psilocin. In general, the less psilocin there is in a species, the more subtle the bluing reaction. ## The Chemistry of Bluing Psilocybin is a prodrug that is dephosphorylated into psilocin in the body. Psilocin is more susceptible to oxidation than psilocybin. When mushroom tissue is damaged, enzymes are released that convert psilocybin to psilocin, which then oxidizes to form blue-colored compounds. The specific oxidized products responsible for the blue color have been studied but the complete pathway remains complex. The intensity of bluing does not directly correlate with total psilocybin content, as it primarily reflects psilocin levels. ## Species Variation in Bluing Not all psilocybin-containing species blue with equal intensity. [[conocybe-cyanopus-profile]] shows bluish-green discoloration primarily at the stem base.
