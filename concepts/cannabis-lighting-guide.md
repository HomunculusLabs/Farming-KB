# Cannabis Lighting Guide

Light is the single most important factor in indoor cannabis cultivation. It drives photosynthesis, regulates the plant's life cycle through photoperiod, and directly influences yield, potency, and morphology. This guide covers the science of grow lighting, fixture types, and practical setup guidance.

## Light Science Fundamentals

### Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR)
PAR refers to the spectrum of light (400-700nm wavelength) that plants use for photosynthesis. When evaluating grow lights, PAR output is the relevant metric — not lumens or lux, which measure brightness for human eyes.

### PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)
PPFD measures the number of PAR photons hitting a given area per second, expressed in micromoles per square meter per second (μmol/m²/s). This is the most useful metric for growers.

**Target PPFD ranges for cannabis**:

| Growth Stage | Target PPFD | DLI (Daily Light Integral) |
|-------------|------------|--------------------------|
| Seedling/Clone | 100-300 μmol/m²/s | 8-12 mol/m²/d |
| Vegetative | 300-600 μmol/m²/s | 15-25 mol/m²/d |
| Early Flowering | 600-900 μmol/m²/s | 30-40 mol/m²/d |
| Late Flowering | 800-1200 μmol/m²/s | 35-45 mol/m²/d |

### Light Spectrum
Different wavelengths serve different functions:

- **Blue light (400-500nm)**: Promotes compact, leafy vegetative growth. Essential for strong stems and healthy leaf development.
- **Green light (500-600nm)**: Less efficiently absorbed but penetrates deeper into the canopy.
- **Red light (600-700nm)**: Drives flowering, stem elongation, and biomass production. Most efficient for photosynthesis.
- **Far-red light (700-750nm)**: Influences the phytochrome system, affecting flowering triggers and plant stretching. Some growers use far-red at the end of the light period to accelerate flowering onset (Emerson effect).

## Light Fixture Types

### LED (Light Emitting Diode)
LED technology has become the standard for modern cannabis cultivation due to efficiency, spectrum control, and longevity.

**Advantages**:
- Energy efficiency: 2-5 μmol/J (significantly better than HPS)
- Full-spectrum or tunable spectrum options
- Low heat output — can be placed closer to canopy
- Long lifespan (50,000+ hours)
- No bulb replacement needed

**Disadvantages**:
- Higher upfront cost
- Quality varies enormously between manufacturers
- Some budget LEDs have poor spectrum or underperform claimed specs

**Types**:
- **Quantum boards**: Samsung LM301B/H diodes on a board. The gold standard for efficiency and value.
- **COB (Chip on Board)**: Single powerful LED chip. Good light intensity, but more concentrated beam pattern.
- **Bar-style**: Multiple LED bars in a fixture. Excellent even coverage for wide canopy areas.

### HPS (High Pressure Sodium)
The traditional industry standard for flowering cannabis.

**Advantages**:
- Proven, reliable technology with decades of track record
- Excellent raw photon output per dollar
- Strong in the red/yellow spectrum — promotes flowering
- Lower initial cost than quality LED

**Disadvantages**:
- High heat output — requires more ventilation and distance from canopy
- Less energy efficient than modern LED
- Limited spectrum (heavy red/yellow, weak blue)
- Bulbs degrade and need replacement every 6-12 months
- Higher electricity costs over time

**Common wattages**: 250W, 400W, 600W (sweet spot for efficiency), 1000W

### CMH / LEC (Ceramic Metal Halide)
Ceramic metal halide (marketed as LEC by some brands) offers a broad spectrum closer to natural sunlight.

**Advantages**:
- Broad, full-spectrum light (excellent CRI)
- Good balance of blue and red — can be used for both veg and flower
- Lower heat than HPS
- Superior UV output (may enhance terpene production)

**Disadvantages**:
- Less efficient than top-tier LED
- Still produces significant heat
- Bulb replacement needed periodically
- Less intense than HPS for flowering-only applications

**Common wattages**: 315W (most popular), 630W

### Fluorescent (T5, CFL)
Best for seedlings, clones, and very early vegetative growth. Not sufficient for flowering.

**Advantages**:
- Very low heat
- Inexpensive
- Good spectrum for young plants
- T5 fixtures provide even, gentle light

**Disadvantages**:
- Low intensity — insufficient for flowering
- Bulbs degrade quickly
- Not energy efficient for larger grows

## Light Schedules (Photoperiod)

### Vegetative Stage: 18/6 or 24/0
- **18 hours on / 6 hours off**: Most common. Gives plants a dark rest period. Saves electricity.
- **24 hours on**: Some growers use this for maximum vegetative growth. Most research suggests dark periods are beneficial for root development and metabolic recovery.

### Flowering Stage: 12/12
- 12 hours light / 12 hours dark triggers the flowering response in photoperiod cannabis strains.
- **Critical**: The dark period must be completely uninterrupted. Even brief light leaks during the dark cycle can cause stress, hermaphroditism, or delayed flowering.
- Use light-proof timers and seal any light leaks in the grow space.

### Autoflowering Strains
Autoflowers flower based on age rather than photoperiod. Common schedules:
- **18/6 throughout**: Standard recommendation for autoflowers
- **20/4**: Some growers use this for maximum growth
- **12/1**: 12 hours light, 1 hour dark — an advanced schedule claimed to reduce total flowering time

## Light Height and Distance

Proper distance between the light source and the canopy is critical:

- **Too close**: Light burn, bleaching (white, toasted tops), leaf curling
- **Too far**: Stretchy, leggy growth, reduced yield

**General distance guidelines** (adjust based on plant response):

| Fixture Type | Vegetative Distance | Flowering Distance |
|-------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| LED (quality, 200-300W) | 18-24 inches | 12-18 inches |
| HPS 600W | 24-36 inches | 18-24 inches |
| CMH 315W | 24-30 inches | 18-24 inches |
| T5 fluorescent | 2-4 inches | N/A (not for flower) |

Use a PAR meter if available to measure PPFD at the canopy level. If you don't have one, observe your plants: leaves reaching upward and praying (flat, wide) indicate good intensity. Leaves curling down or bleaching means too close.

## Canopy Management and Light Distribution

- **Even coverage**: Position lights so PPFD varies by no more than 30% across the canopy.
- **Reflective walls**: Use mylar (95% reflectivity) or flat white paint to maximize light utilization.
- **Light movers**: Rail systems that move lights back and forth can improve coverage in larger areas.
- **Defoliation and training**: See [[cannabis-topping-training]] for techniques to create an even canopy that maximizes light absorption.

## Electricity and Safety

- Calculate your power needs: total wattage of lights + 20% buffer for ballasts and cooling.
- Use dedicated circuits for grow lighting — don't share with other equipment.
- Install GFCI (ground fault circuit interrupter) outlets in grow spaces.
- Ensure proper ventilation for any lights that generate significant heat.
- Keep all electrical connections away from water sources.
