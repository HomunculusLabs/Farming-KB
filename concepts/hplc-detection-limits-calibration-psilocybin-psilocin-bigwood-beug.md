# HPLC Detection Limits and Calibration — Psilocybin & Psilocin Quantification

## Overview

Bigwood and Beug (1982) employed reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) for the quantitative analysis of [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] levels in *[[psilocybe-cubensis]]* [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]]. Their analytical methodology included careful calibration against certified reference standards and defined the detection limits achievable with their instrumentation. These parameters established the quantitative framework for their flush-variation study.

## Analytical Instrumentation

### HPLC System Configuration

- **Method:** Reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC)
- **Detection:** UV-Vis absorbance (wavelength not specified in the paper)
- **Quantification:** Hewlett-Packard 8380A reporting integrator-plotter
- **Calibration standards:** National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) certified reference materials

The use of NIDA-certified standards is significant because it provides traceability to an authoritative reference source, ensuring that reported concentrations are comparable to other studies using the same standards. NIDA maintains certified reference materials for scheduled substances to support forensic and research laboratories.

### Qualitative Confirmation

HPLC results were qualitatively confirmed by thin-layer chromatography (TLC):

- **Mobile phase:** Butanol : acetic acid : water (12:3:5 by volume)
- **Purpose:** Independent verification that HPLC peaks corresponded to the expected compounds
- **Methodology:** Two orthogonal analytical techniques applied to the same extracts

The dual-method approach (HPLC for quantification, TLC for confirmation) was standard practice in the early 1980s and remains a prudent quality assurance measure. TLC provides a rapid, low-cost check for gross errors such as co-eluting compounds or misidentified peaks.

## Calibration Characteristics

### Linear Dynamic Range

[[bigwood-beug-miniculture-rye-grain-flush-variation-method]] established a linear relationship between analyte concentration and detector response (peak area):

- **Linear range:** 0.2 to 3 μg total psilocybin or psilocin per injection
- **Repeatability:** 10% relative standard deviation across the linear range
- **Relationship:** Linear (proportional) between concentration and peak area

### Practical Implications of the Linear Range

| Parameter | Value | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Lower limit | 0.2 μg per injection | Minimum reliable quantification |
| Upper limit | 3.0 μg per injection | Maximum before non-linear response |
| Dynamic range | 15:1 | Moderate range by modern standards |
| Repeatability | ±10% | Adequate for biological variability studies |

The 15:1 dynamic range is relatively narrow compared to modern HPLC systems, which typically achieve 3-4 orders of magnitude (1000:1 to 10000:1). This meant that samples needed to be:

- **Prepared at appropriate concentrations** to fall within the 0.2-3.0 μg range
- **Potentially diluted or concentrated** depending on expected potency
- **Injected multiple times** if concentrations exceeded the linear range

### 10% Repeatability

The ±10% repeatability (relative standard deviation) is adequate for the study's purpose of comparing flush-to-flush variation, which showed differences ranging from 0 to 2.9 mg/g for psilocin and 3.2 to 13.3 mg/g for psilocybin — far exceeding the analytical uncertainty. However, the 10% RSD means:

- A sample measuring 5.0 mg/g could actually range from 4.5 to 5.5 mg/g
- Small differences between adjacent flushes may not be statistically significant
- The large inter-flush variability (factor of 4 for psilocybin) dwarfs analytical error

## Detection Limits

### Limit of Detection (LOD)

- **Approximate LOD:** ~0.01 μg psilocybin or psilocin per injection

This detection limit corresponds to roughly:

- **0.001% (10 ppm)** in a 1 mg sample injection
- **0.01 mg/g (10 μg/g)** in dried [[hplc-analysis-psilocybin-psilocin-mushroom-tissue]]
- Approximately 0.0001% of dry weight

### Significance for Psilocin Measurement

The detection limit is particularly relevant to the psilocin findings:

- **First flush psilocin:** Often reported as 0 mg/g (below detection limit)
- **"Generally zero" in first flushes:** The analytical method could not reliably distinguish between true [[fukuoka-textdoc-absence-no-fertilizer-tests]] and trace levels below 0.01 μg
