## Cannabis and Alcoholism Research

**Source:** [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] (2017)

## Condition Overview

Alcohol abuse: neurological and mental illness causing domestic violence, child/spousal abuse, homicide, suicide, accidents, fatal overdose, poverty. No antidote for acute alcohol overdose exists. Orthodox medicine: no cure; patients remain alcoholics for life, managing urge and maintaining abstinence.

**Primary cause of death:** cardiovascular disease. Other: poor food absorption, cancer, cirrhosis, pancreatitis, gastritis, nervous system damage, depression, dementia, seizures, DTs.

**Therapy:** detoxification + thiamine (B1) support, followed by peer-group therapy. Long-term abuse induces neurochemical brain changes reinforcing dependence.

## Cannabis Research Findings

**2004 Berkeley (92 alcoholic patients):**

- 45 patients (49%): "very effective" for reducing alcohol consumption

- 38 patients (41%): "effective"

- 9 patients (10%): able to give up alcohol altogether

**CB1 receptor studies (2005, 2008):** Blocking CB1 receptors effectively reduces alcohol preference and intake. Medications targeting CB1 may be beneficial for alcoholism treatment.

**2005 Bethesda (rats):** CBD protects nerve cells from alcohol-induced toxicity in dose-dependent manner (binge drinking model).

## Pharmacological Mechanisms

The endocannabinoid system and alcohol reward pathways are deeply interconnected, sharing neural circuitry in the mesolimbic dopamine system.

- **CB1-alcohol reward interaction:** Alcohol activates CB1 receptors indirectly by increasing endocannabinoid release in the nucleus accumbens and ventral tegmental area -- the brain's primary reward circuit. This CB1-mediated dopamine release is a key component of alcohol's reinforcing effects. Modulating this pathway with cannabinoids can alter alcohol craving and consumption.

- **CB1 antagonism reduces alcohol intake:** Multiple animal studies (2005, 2008) demonstrated that blocking CB1 receptors with antagonists like Rimonabant reduces alcohol preference, self-administration, and relapse behavior. This confirms that CB1 receptor signaling is necessary for alcohol's rewarding effects.

- **CBD's neuroprotective role:** Chronic alcohol use causes neuronal damage through [[blesching-cannabis-safety-profile-adverse-effects-and-overdose-risk]], and withdrawal danger of alcohol.
