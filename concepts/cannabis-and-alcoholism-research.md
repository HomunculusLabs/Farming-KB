# Cannabis and Alcoholism Research

**Source:** [[uwe-blesching]], The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] (2017)

## Condition Overview

Alcohol abuse: neurological and mental illness causing domestic violence, child/spousal abuse, homicide, suicide, accidents, fatal overdose, poverty. No antidote for acute alcohol overdose exists. Orthodox medicine: no cure; patients remain alcoholics for life, managing urge and maintaining abstinence.

**Primary cause of death:** cardiovascular disease. Other: poor food absorption, cancer, cirrhosis, pancreatitis, gastritis, nervous system damage, depression, dementia, seizures, DTs.

**Therapy:** detoxification + thiamine (B1) support, followed by peer-group therapy. Long-term abuse induces neurochemical brain changes reinforcing dependence.

## Cannabis Research Findings

**2004 Berkeley (92 alcoholic patients):**

- 45 patients (49%): "very effective" for reducing alcohol consumption

- 38 patients (41%): "effective"

- 9 patients (10%): able to give up alcohol altogether

**CB1 receptor studies (2005, 2008):** Blocking CB1 receptors effectively reduces alcohol preference and intake. Medications targeting CB1 may be beneficial for alcoholism treatment.

**2005 Bethesda (rats):** CBD protects nerve cells from alcohol-induced toxicity in dose-dependent manner (binge drinking model).

## Pharmacological Mechanisms

The endocannabinoid system and alcohol reward pathways are deeply interconnected, sharing neural circuitry in the mesolimbic dopamine system.

- **CB1-alcohol reward interaction:** Alcohol activates CB1 receptors indirectly by increasing endocannabinoid release in the nucleus accumbens and ventral tegmental area -- the brain's primary reward circuit. This CB1-mediated dopamine release is a key component of alcohol's reinforcing effects. Modulating this pathway with cannabinoids can alter alcohol craving and consumption.

- **CB1 antagonism reduces alcohol intake:** Multiple animal studies (2005, 2008) demonstrated that blocking CB1 receptors with antagonists like Rimonabant reduces alcohol preference, self-administration, and relapse behavior. This confirms that CB1 receptor signaling is necessary for alcohol's rewarding effects.

- **CBD's neuroprotective role:** Chronic alcohol use causes neuronal damage through [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]], excitotoxicity, and neuroinflammation. CBD protects neurons through antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-apoptotic mechanisms that are independent of CB1/CB2 receptors, making it valuable for treating alcohol-related brain damage.

- **Substitution hypothesis:** Cannabis may serve as a safer substitute for alcohol, providing some of the relaxation and euphoria that alcohol-dependent individuals seek, without the severe organ toxicity, [[blesching-cannabis-safety-profile-adverse-effects-and-overdose-risk]], and withdrawal danger of alcohol.

- **Anxiety and stress circuit modulation:** Alcohol withdrawal and craving are driven by dysregulated [[fungal-stress-responses]] in the amygdala and hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis. CBD modulates these stress circuits through 5-HT1A receptor activation, potentially reducing the anxiety that drives relapse.

- **GABAergic modulation:** Both alcohol and cannabinoids influence GABA, the brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter. Cannabis may help normalize GABAergic dysfunction that develops during chronic alcohol use and persists during withdrawal.

## Clinical Evidence

The clinical evidence for cannabis in alcoholism is modest but encouraging, with a CHI value of 9.

- **Patient-reported effectiveness (2004, Berkeley):** In the largest clinical survey, 90% of 92 alcoholic patients reported cannabis as effective or very effective in reducing alcohol consumption, and 10% reported being able to quit alcohol entirely using cannabis. While this is a self-reported case study rather than a controlled trial, the consistency of positive responses is notable.

- **CB1 antagonism reduces alcohol seeking (2005, 2008):** Animal studies from Brookhaven National Laboratory and NYS Psychiatric Institute demonstrated that CB1 receptor blockade reduces alcohol preference, voluntary intake, and relapse-like behavior. These findings provide mechanistic validation for the CB1-alcohol reward pathway.

- **Neuroprotection from alcohol toxicity (2005, Bethesda):** CBD's dose-dependent protection of neurons from alcohol-induced damage addresses a critical need -- chronic alcoholism causes progressive brain damage that is only partially reversible with abstinence alone. CBD may accelerate or enhance brain recovery during sobriety.

- **Emerging substitution evidence:** Population-level studies suggest that in jurisdictions where cannabis is legally available, alcohol consumption decreases, supporting the substitution hypothesis.

## Dosing Considerations

- **For alcohol substitution:** THC 5-10 mg as needed when alcohol cravings arise may provide sufficient reward pathway modulation to reduce the urge to drink. Start with low doses to assess individual response.

- **For neuroprotection during recovery:** CBD 25-50 mg twice daily provides neuroprotective and anti-anxiety benefits without psychoactive effects, supporting brain recovery during alcohol abstinence.

- **For acute withdrawal support:** CBD 50-100 mg per day, divided into multiple doses, may help manage anxiety, insomnia, and craving during the acute withdrawal period (first 1-2 weeks).
