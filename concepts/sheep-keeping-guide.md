Sheep are manageable livestock for small-scale farms, providing meat, wool, and [[fukuoka-natural-predator-protection]] and stress management.

## How Sheep Fit into a Permaculture System

- **Orchard foraging**: Can be allowed into the orchard after trees are at least 7 years old, though they must be monitored for tree damage
- **Tree forage**: Trees provide food, shelter, soil benefits, [[comparison-silvopasture-systems-vs-pasture-management]]**: Sheep graze efficiently when properly rotated
- **Wool production**: Annual shearing provides fiber for textiles or sale
- **Meat**: Lambs provide high-quality meat

## Feeding

### Pasture

- **[[coleman-spring-transition]]**: Gradually introduce sheep to pasture each spring so they can adjust
- **Winter foraging**: Harvested corn fields and roots left in the garden supplement winter diet
- **Winter hay**: Each sheep eats 75 pounds (34 kg) of grain and 10 bales of hay over the winter

### Grain Supplementation

- **Late pregnancy**: 0.5 to 1 pound (0.22 to 0.5 kg) of grain per day per ewe
- **After lambing — one lamb**: 1 pound (0.5 kg) of grain per day
- **After lambing — two lambs**: 1.5 to 2 pounds (0.7 to 0.9 kg) of grain per day
- **Tapering**: Gradually reduce grain over the next 2 months
- **Ram during breeding**: 1 pound of grain per day
- **Tree forage offset**: These grain requirements can be reduced when tree forage is available

### Water and Minerals

- **Water**: 1.5 gallons (6 liters) of fresh, clean water per sheep per day
- **Salt**: Provide a salt lick, sheltered from weather

## Housing

- **Minimal housing needed**: A three-sided shelter in the pasture is sufficient
- **Barn space**: Additional space in the barn for lambing or extreme weather
- **Bedding**: Clean, dry bedding essential

## Predation

Sheep are extremely vulnerable to predators:

- **Stray dogs**: Can chase a sheep to death without biting
- **Larger predators**: Coyotes, wolves, and other large animals will kill sheep
- **Guard dog solution**: The most effective prevention — a Great Pyrenees was bred specifically to live in the field and attack sheep predators
- **Herding dog**: A border collie can herd sheep, providing management assistance
- **Fencing**: Good fencing is essential but not always sufficient against determined predators

## Stress Management

Sheep are very vulnerable to stress, more so than most livestock:

- **Moving/transport**: Can cause sheep to stop eating or even have a heart attack
- **Feed changes**: Must be gradual to avoid stress
- **Loud storms**: Can trigger severe [[fungal-stress-responses]]
- **Approach**: Handle quietly and gently; minimize sudden movements and loud noises

## Breeding

- **Breeding season**: Breed in October for spring lambs (5-month gestation)
- **Preparation**: If sheep have woolly faces, clip wool away from the eyes; tag or clip wool away from the vagina
- **Health checks**: Every handling is a good time to check feet
- **Ram options**: Buy a yearling ram each year (eat him afterwards), or keep a ram full time
- **Important**: Never use rams from the same family for breeding
- **Lambing**: Very similar to goat kidding — orphan lambs can drink goat milk as a replacement

## Health Considerations

- **Hoof care**: Regular hoof trimming (check at every handling)
- **Shearing**: Annual shearing for wool breeds
- **Vaccinations**: Follow local veterinary recommendations
- **Parasites**: Pasture rotation is the primary defense against internal parasites
- **Regular observation**: Daily flock checks catch problems early
