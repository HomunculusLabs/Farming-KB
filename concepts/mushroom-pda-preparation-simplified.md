Source: FAO RAP Publication 2001/12, "A Training Manual on Mushroom Cultivation for People with Disabilities" by Johanne Hanko.

A simplified [[mushroom-agar-media-recipes]] preparation method suitable for small-scale production, demonstrated by disabled trainees in Thailand. This procedure is used for [[comparison-tissue-culture-vs-wild-isolation]] maintenance and [[mushroom-spawn-making-detailed]].

## Ingredients

- Potatoes (peeled and sliced) - 200g
- Dextrose (sugar) - 20g
- Agar-agar (gelatin) - 20g
- Water - 1 liter

## Equipment

- Clean bottles or test tubes
- Cotton (gauze) for plugs
- Rubber bands
- Knife and cutting board
- Measuring tools
- Stove or heat source
- Autoclave or pressure cooker

## Step-by-Step Procedure

### 1. Prepare the potato extract
Peel and wash 200g of potatoes. Slice thinly and boil in 1 liter of water for approximately 30 minutes until potatoes are soft.

### 2. Strain the extract
Filter the potato extract through a clean cloth. Discard the potato solids. The resulting liquid is the potato extract.

### 3. Add nutrients
Add 20g of dextrose (or regular sugar) and 20g of agar-agar to the potato extract while still warm. Stir until completely dissolved.

### 4. Adjust volume
Add clean water to bring the total volume back to 1 liter if any was lost during boiling.

### 5. Fill bottles
Pour the medium into clean bottles or test tubes. Fill to approximately one-third of the bottle volume.

### 6. Plug bottles
Insert cotton plugs into the neck of each bottle. Secure with rubber bands. The cotton plug allows gas exchange while filtering contaminants.

### 7. Sterilize
Place bottles in autoclave at 121C for 20-30 minutes to ensure complete sterilization. If using a pressure cooker, maintain pressure for at least 30 minutes.

### 8. Cool
Let cool down to approximately 37C (warm to touch but not hot).

### 9. Slant bottles
Place bottles in a slanted position to increase the surface area of the medium. The PDA should come close to the neck but must not touch the cotton plug. This creates a larger surface for mycelium growth.

### 10. Transfer to clean shelf
After the PDA medium has solidified in the slanted position, transfer all bottles to a clean shelf in the clean room.

### 11. Check for contamination
After 2-3 days, check for contamination (dark spots or lines on the medium). Contaminated bottles must be separated and cleaned or discarded.

## Contamination Indicators

Healthy PDA plates should appear clear to slightly cloudy amber with no discoloration, bubbles, or foreign growth. Common contaminants include bacterial colonies (appearing as smooth, shiny, often pigmented spots spreading in streaks), mold (fuzzy growth in colors other than white — green, black, pink, or yellow), and yeast (small, round, raised colonies often appearing within 24-48 hours). If contamination appears on more than 10% of plates, review sterilization time and aseptic technique.

## Variations

Lower-cost alternatives to standard PDA include using sweeter potatoes (higher natural sugar content reduces need for added dextrose), or substituting dextrose with malt extract or honey (use 10g instead of 10g dextrose). Potato carrot agar (PCA) uses a 1:1 mix of potato and carrot extract, providing additional vitamins beneficial for [[mycelium-as-network-organism]] growth. For [[lichens]] and slow-growing fungi, a weaker medium (half-strength PDA) often produces better isolation results.

## Notes for Small-Scale Producers

- Not all trainees need to master PDA preparation. Most will buy inoculated bags from nearby farms or the training center
- Only trainees especially interested in spawn production need hands-on PDA practice
- The training center can supply inoculated bags, generating income for the center
- Cleanliness is the single most important factor - work near flame, wash hands and tools with alcohol

## Storage

Mature mycelium on PDA can be kept in a cool place or in the refrigerator (vegetable section) for extended storage. Check periodically for contamination.
