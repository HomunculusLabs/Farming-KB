# PF TEK Dual Chambered Terrarium: Spray Shield Humidity Physics and Molecular Water Dynamics

## Overview

The dual chambered terrarium is the [[fruiting-environment-parameters]] at the heart of the PF TEK [[boil-a-bag-mushroom-cultivation-method]]. Its defining feature is a slanted acrylic spray shield that partitions the interior into a primary spraying chamber and a secondary chamber where fungal cakes are housed. This design solves the fundamental challenge of providing near-100% humidity without directly wetting the delicate fungal mycelium with water droplets.

## The Physical Problem

### Water Droplets vs. Molecular Humidity

Mushroom cultivation requires extremely high ambient humidity for successful fruiting. However, the fungal organism faces a critical physical constraint: its [[gadd-mycelial-network-dynamics]] consists of extremely fine hyphal threads that operate at a microscopic scale. A water droplet from a spray bottle represents what PF Tek author Robert McPherson describes as "giant ponds of water in relation to the fine [[fungal-mycelial-networks-nutrient-translocation]] of the fungal threads." When such a droplet lands directly on mycelium or developing primordia:

1. **Drowning**: The water droplet physically engulfs and suffocates the microscopic mycelial structures
2. **Contamination**: Standing water creates an ideal breeding ground for bacterial and mold contamination
3. **Inhibition**: Growth is halted or severely impaired in the affected area
4. **Osmotic disruption**: Direct contact with large volumes of pure water can create osmotic imbalances

The challenge, therefore, is to convert large water droplets into fine molecular humidity — water evaporated into individual H₂O molecules suspended in air — that can penetrate the mycelial structures without overwhelming them.

## Dual Chamber Design Specifications

### The Aquarium

The base of the terrarium is a standard **10-gallon aquarium** with:

- **Outside dimensions**: 24" × 14½" (approximately 61 cm × 37 cm)
- **Inside dimensions**: 21¼" × 12" (approximately 54 cm × 30.5 cm)
- **Material**: Clear glass, providing visibility for monitoring growth
- **Critical requirement**: Must be sealable with no air leaks for humidity retention

### The Airtight Lid

The lid is constructed from a wooden frame holding clear polyethylene plastic film taut:

- Frame made from flat, unwrapped ¼" thick board or four wood strips connected by screws
- The inner rectangular cutout of the frame must be **larger** than the top of the aquarium
- Polyethylene film is tacked to the underside or upper side of the frame
- The frame hangs by the plastic film, which creates a seal against the aquarium top
- A simpler alternative is saran wrap or similar flexible clear plastic
- The key requirement is an airtight seal to prevent humidity escape

### The Spray Shield (Chamber Partition)

The spray shield is the central innovation of the dual chamber design:

- **Material**: ⅛" thick clear acrylic (Plexiglas) window insulation
- **Dimensions**: Approximately 15" × 18" (cut to fit the specific aquarium with a tight fit)
- **Orientation**: Slanted/angled to serve as both a spray deflector and a drip shield
- **Position**: Sits inside the aquarium, partitioning it into two chambers

The slant of the shield serves a dual purpose: it deflects spray into finer particles AND catches condensation that forms on its surface, directing dripping water away from the fungal cakes below.

## Physics of the Spray Shield Mechanism

### Step 1: Primary Chamber Spraying

The spray bottle is directed downward through the gap between the lid and the aquarium top, aiming at the center of the spray shield in the **primary chamber** (the space between the lid and the shield). The spray bottle delivers water as relatively coarse droplets — the "initial spray" described as unsuitable for direct contact with fungus.

### Step 2: Impact Atomization

When the coarse spray strikes the acrylic spray shield, a physical process of impact atomization occurs:

- **Kinetic energy transfer**: The water droplets transfer kinetic energy to the shield surface
- **Droplet fragmentation**: Upon impact, each droplet breaks apart into multiple smaller droplets
- **Secondary aerosolization**: The fragmented droplets are small enough to be carried by air currents
- **Fine mist generation**: The result is a mist composed of much smaller water particles than the original spray

This process is analogous to how a waterfall creates mist at its base — the impact of large water masses against a surface converts bulk water into fine aerosolized particles.

### Step 3: Secondary Chamber Distribution

The fine mist generated by shield impact flows around the **sides** of the spray shield into the secondary chamber — the space below the shield where the fungal cakes reside. Key aspects of this flow:

- **Gravity**: Some mist particles settle downward through natural gravitational settling
- **Air currents**: Opening and closing the lid creates air movement that distributes mist
- **Diffusion**: Fine water particles diffuse through the air space from high-concentration areas near the shield to lower-concentration areas near the cakes
- **Exclusion of large droplets**: The shield physically blocks any remaining large droplets from reaching the secondary chamber

### Step 4: Molecular Humidity Saturation

Within the sealed secondary chamber, the fine mist continues to evaporate until the air approaches **100% relative humidity** — the state where "there is the maximum number of water molecules floating amongst the air atoms." At this saturation point:

- Water molecules are individually dispersed in the gas phase
- The partial pressure of water vapor equals the saturation vapor pressure at the [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]]
- Any additional water introduced will condense rather than remain as vapor
- The fungal mycelium is "bathed in a shroud of floating water molecules"

### Step 5: Condensation and Drip Shielding

Over time, the humidity within the sealed terrarium condenses on cooler surfaces:

- **Condensation surfaces**: The glass walls, the lid, and the spray shield itself accumulate condensed water
- **Drip direction**: The slanted spray shield directs condensed water to drip down its angled surface to the sides and edges of the terrarium, away from the cakes below
- **Beneficial condensation**: PF Tek explicitly states "the more condensation the better" because it indicates high humidity and the drip shield protects the cakes
- **Water accumulation**: Condensed water collects in the bottom of the terrarium and must be periodically siphoned out to prevent bacterial buildup

## The Spraying Protocol

### Procedure

The PF TEK specifies a precise spraying procedure:

1. **Pre-conditioning**: Before placing cakes, spray all interior surfaces including the shield and lid
2. **Cake placement**: Insert fungal cakes and position the spray shield and lid
3. **Spraying**: Lift the lid slightly, insert the spray nozzle, and spray downward at the shield for approximately 5 seconds
4. **Sealing**: Immediately withdraw the nozzle and lower the lid to trap the swirling mist
5. **Frequency**: Spray at least twice daily; compensate for missed daytime sprays with multiple evening sprays
6. **Visual indicator**: All interior surfaces should appear foggy or dripping with water

### Ventilation

Each spraying session should be accompanied by ventilation:

1. Remove the lid
2. Hold the spray shield vertically
3. Fan the chamber with a piece of cardboard
4. This exchanges CO₂-rich air with fresh air containing normal CO₂ levels
5. Spray as described above after ventilation

### Maintenance

- **Water removal**: Siphon accumulated water from the terrarium bottom using a rubber bulb battery filler or enema bottle to prevent bacterial contamination
- **Lighting**: Expose to indirect sunlight or a low-wattage fluorescent plant light for phototropic upward growth
- **Temperature**: Maintain room temperature; avoid direct heating which creates a drying influence

## The Humidity Cycle

The dual chambered terrarium creates a cyclical humidity pattern:

1. **Spraying event**: Rapid introduction of water as coarse droplets into the primary chamber
2. **Atomization**: Droplets are broken down by shield impact into fine mist
3. **Distribution**: Mist flows into the secondary chamber and evaporates to molecular humidity
4. **Saturation**: Air reaches near-100% relative humidity; fungal cakes absorb moisture from the humid air
5. **Condensation**: Gradual condensation on surfaces as temperature micro-fluctuations occur
6. **Drip collection**: Slanted shield directs condensed water safely away from cakes
7. **Gradual decline**: Humidity slowly decreases as air exchanges with the environment during lid openings
8. **Re-spraying**: Cycle repeats with next spraying event

This cycle can maintain adequate humidity for **24 hours or longer** from a single thorough spraying, though twice-daily spraying is recommended for optimal results.

## Temperature and Humidity Interaction

The PF TEK explicitly warns against heating the terrarium:

- Direct heating works against humidification by increasing the air's capacity to hold water vapor (raising the dew point), which accelerates evaporation from surfaces
- At 60°F (approximately 15.5°C), the fungi grow well and humidity is more easily maintained
- At higher temperatures, more frequent spraying is needed to compensate for increased evaporation rates
- Heating cables, heat pads, and blankets are specifically discouraged
- Grow lights should be kept at a safe distance to avoid radiant heating
