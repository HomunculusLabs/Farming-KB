## Mycorrhizal Phosphatase Enzyme Activity and Phosphorus Acquisition

Mycorrhizal fungi play a central role in phosphorus acquisition for host plants,
particularly in soils where phosphorus is bound in organic or complex inorganic
forms. The production of phosphatase enzymes by mycorrhizal hyphae enables
direct cycling of phosphorus from otherwise unavailable sources, a process that
is especially important in forests and heathlands where decomposition rates are
low and organic matter accumulates.

The ability of ectomycorrhizal fungi to access phosphorus from complex organic
forms such as inositol hexaphosphate has been demonstrated repeatedly (Dighton,
1983; Mousain and Salsac, 1986; Antibus et al., 1992, 1997). Regulation of
phosphatase expression by external concentrations of orthophosphate has been
shown by MacFall et al. (1991), indicating a negative feedback mechanism.

Antibus et al. (1992) showed that in some ectomycorrhizal fungi, phosphorus
uptake was greater from organic supplies than from inorganic supplies due to
the action of acid phosphatase and phytase enzymes. Species such as Entoloma
sericeum showed notably high uptake of 32P from organic sources compared to
inorganic sources.

produce phosphatase and other enzymes (Dighton, 1983, 1991; Lapeyrie et al.,
