## Sterilized Casing Soil Recipe: Peat, Vermiculite, and Limestone

## Overview

[[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]]. The
classic recipe using peat, vermiculite, and limestone (marl) has been used
by cultivators for decades and remains one of the most reliable formulations
for Psilocybe and Panaeolus species on grass seed substrates.

## The 10:5:2 Recipe

The recommended formulation uses three ingredients in specific proportions:

- **10 parts peat**: The primary casing material. Peat moss provides water
  retention, a stable structure for [[abort-dead-pinhead-cleanup-casing-surface-hygiene]].

- **2 parts limestone (marl)**: Crushed limestone or marl raises the pH
  of the acidic peat to a range favorable for mushroom formation
  (approximately pH 7.0–7.5). Most cultivated mushroom species fruit best
  near neutral pH, while the peat alone would be too acidic at pH 3.5–4.5.

## Ingredient Selection

### Peat Moss

Canadian sphagnum peat moss is the standard choice. It should be finely
textured — coarse or fibrous peat does not level as smoothly on the casing
surface. The moisture content varies between brands and batches, which
directly affects how much water must be added during preparation. Some
peats arrive nearly dry and absorb large volumes of water; others are
pre-moistened to varying degrees.

### Vermiculite

Horticultural-grade vermiculite in fine to medium grade is appropriate.
Fine vermiculite blends more uniformly with the peat, while coarse
vermiculite provides better aeration. A mix of grades can be used. The
vermiculite should be clean and free of dust.

### Limestone (Marl)

Marl is a natural calcium carbonate mudstone that has been used
traditionally in European [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] an
  uncontested environment.
