# EMCDDA Hallucinogenic Mushroom School Surveys and Adolescent Use

## Overview

School-based surveys provide crucial data on [[health-risks-and-harm-reduction-for-hallucinogenic-mushroom-use]] among
adolescents across Europe. The ESPAD (European School Survey Project on
Alcohol and Other Drugs) 2003 survey covered 22 EU Member States, Norway,
and four EU candidate countries (Croatia, Bulgaria, Turkey, and Romania),
offering the most comprehensive picture of adolescent hallucinogenic mushroom
use available. Historically, drug surveys collected data on LSD or general
hallucinogen categories rather than mushroom-specific data, creating a
paucity of historical trend information.

## ESPAD 2003 Survey Results

The ESPAD 2003 school survey collected data on prevalence of hallucinogenic
mushroom use among students aged 15 to 16 years old across 27 countries.

### Lifetime Prevalence Range

- Lifetime use of [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]] among 15 to 16 year olds ranged
  from 0% to 8% across surveyed countries.
- The Czech Republic, Netherlands, France, and Belgium had the highest
  prevalence estimates.
- Three out of 27 countries reported zero lifetime prevalence: Cyprus,
  Finland, and Romania.

### Comparison with Ecstasy

A notable finding was that lifetime use [[health-risks-of-hallucinogenic-mushrooms]] was equal
to or higher than lifetime use of ecstasy in nine of the surveyed countries.
This challenged the assumption that ecstasy was the dominant hallucinogenic
drug among European adolescents.

### Gender Differences

Following common patterns for illegal drug use, male students generally had
higher prevalence rates than female students for hallucinogenic mushroom
use (ESPAD, Hibell et al, 2003).

## Substance Use Correlations

Analysis of data from 11 EU Member States (Finland, Germany, Greece, Hungary,
Italy, Lithuania, Malta, Slovakia, Slovenia, Sweden, United Kingdom) together
with Norway and Croatia demonstrated a clear pattern:

- School students aged 15 to 16 were much more likely to have used
  [[emcdda-eu-legislative-timeline-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-2001-2006]] if they had also used LSD or other hallucinogens,
  ecstasy, amphetamines, or cocaine.
- The association with cannabis or legal substances (alcohol, cigarettes)
  was much weaker.
- This suggests that hallucinogenic mushroom use among adolescents clusters
  with use of other "recreational" or "party" drugs rather than with
  gateway substance use patterns.

The average lifetime prevalence of hallucinogenic mushroom use by user group
(from 13 countries) showed that among LSD users, approximately 40 to 50% had
also used [[emcdda-herbal-highs-branding-smartshop-marketing-hallucinogenic-mushrooms]], while among cannabis-only users, rates
were substantially lower.

## Perceived Availability

The ESPAD 2003 survey also collected data on perceived availability of
hallucinogenic mushrooms among 15 to 16 year old students:

- Rates of students perceiving mushrooms as "very" or "fairly easy" to
  obtain ranged from 4% to 28% across countries.
- Under 10% of students in Cyprus, Finland, Greece, Hungary, Latvia,
  Lithuania, Romania, and Turkey reported easy access.
- Over 20% of students in the Czech Republic, Ireland, Italy, Poland, and
  the United Kingdom reported easy access.
