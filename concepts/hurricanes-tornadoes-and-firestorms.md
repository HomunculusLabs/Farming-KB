### Wind Speeds and Rotation

- Wind speeds around the vortex typically range from **128–192 km/h**
- Vortices revolve **anti-clockwise in the northern hemisphere** and **clockwise in
  the southern hemisphere**, driven by the Coriolis effect

### Tidal and Wave Effects

Hurricanes generate a **tidal bulge of up to 2.7 metres above sea level** beneath the
storm centre. The combined effects of fluctuating barometric pressures, the tidal
bulge, wave pile-up against the coast, and powerful wave backwash make hurricanes
devastating to coastal areas.

- **Worst-case scenario**: destructive wave attacks reach **6–9 metres above normal
  high-tide levels**, capable of sweeping entirely over low-lying settlements
- **Wave frequency** shifts from approximately **8 waves per minute** in calm
  conditions to **5 waves per minute** during a storm — waves become wider-spaced,
  faster, and significantly more destructive

### Warning Signs

Storm waves arrive **before** the cyclonic depression itself reaches the coast. Animals
detect this early warning through changes in atmospheric pressure and wave behaviour,
often fleeing or seeking shelter well before humans perceive the threat. Modern
satellite tracking technology provides **days of advance warning**, allowing communities
to prepare and evacuate.

### Design Strategies for Hurricane Safety

- **Building regulations** should be followed and, where possible, exceeded to ensure
  structural resilience
- **Refuge trenches and caves** in well-drained hillside sites offer critical shelter
  during the worst of the storm
- Settlements should avoid low-lying coastal zones; elevated, well-drained hillside
  sites provide the greatest natural protection

## Firestorms

### Formation and Behaviour

Intense wildfires can generate their own weather systems, creating **vortices similar
to tornadoes**. Mass ignition across a large area feeds a **powerful updraught** that
draws in surrounding cold, dry air at high speed. This rushing air further intensifies
the fire, creating a self-sustaining feedback loop that can overwhelm all
conventional firefighting efforts.

### Key Characteristics

- **Burning particles are carried aloft** on towering "smoke nimbus" clouds, which
  can deposit embers far ahead of the main fire front
- **House sections and large debris pinwheel across the sky** as the vortex lifts
  and spins heavy materials
- **Secondary firestorms** can ignite ahead of the main front as airborne embers
  land on dry fuel, effectively leapfrogging the fire forward and creating new

- **Observe warning signs** and integrate early-warning systems into community
  planning
- **Site settlements on elevated, well-drained ground** away from flood-prone coastal
  zones and dense fuel loads
- **Design refuge structures** that can withstand extreme winds, flying debris, and
  flooding

  features to reduce exposure
- **Plan evacuation routes and community shelters** as part of broader [[cool-climate-house-and-settlement-design]]
- **Maintain fire-resistant zones** around structures in [[firestorm]]-susceptible areas

communities in the face of nature's most powerful expressions.
