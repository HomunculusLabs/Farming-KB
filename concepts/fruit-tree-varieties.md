# Fruit Tree Varieties, Rootstocks, and Chill Hours

Detailed variety recommendations for zone 7-8 homestead orchards, plus rootstock selection and chill hour guidance. See [[orchard-management]] for site selection, planting, pruning, pest management, and seasonal care.

## Fruit Tree Selection

### Zone 7-8 Recommended Varieties

#### Apples (700-1000 chill hours for zone 7; 500-800 for zone 8)
| Variety | Disease Resistance | Pollination | Use | Chill Hours |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Liberty | Excellent (scab, cedar rust, fire blight) | Needs pollinizer | Fresh/cider | 800 |
| Enterprise | Very good | Needs pollinizer | Fresh/storage | 700 |
| Freedom | Very good | Needs pollinizer | Fresh/cider | 800 |
| GoldRush | Excellent scab resistance | Needs pollinizer | Fresh/storage | 700-800 |
| Arkansas Black | Good | Needs pollinizer | Storage/cider | 600-700 |
| Williams Pride | Good | Needs pollinizer | Fresh (early) | 600 |
| Pixie Crunch | Good | Needs pollinizer | Fresh | 600-700 |

**Note**: Most apples need cross-pollination from different variety blooming at same time. Plant at least 2-3 varieties. Triploids (Mutsu, Jonagold) need two other pollinizers.

#### Pears (600-900 chill hours)
| Variety | Disease Resistance | Pollination | Use | Chill Hours |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Harrow Sweet | Good fire blight resistance | Needs pollinizer | Fresh/canning | 800 |
| Moonglow | Good fire blight resistance | Partially self-fertile | Fresh | 600-700 |
| Magness | Excellent fire blight resistance | Sterile — needs pollinizer | Fresh | 600 |
| Potomac | Very good fire blight resistance | Needs pollinizer | Fresh | 700-800 |
| Kieffer | Very good | Self-fertile | Canning/preserves | 350-500 |
| Seckel | Fair | Needs pollinizer | Fresh (small) | 600-700 |

**Note**: Pears bloom early — frost risk. Asian pears (Hosui, Shinko) also viable in zone 7-8.

#### Peaches (500-900 chill hours)
| Variety | Disease Resistance | Pollination | Use | Chill Hours |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Reliance | Good cold hardiness | Self-fertile | Fresh/canning | 800-1000 |
| Contender | Good bacterial spot resistance | Self-fertile | Fresh | 900-1000 |
| Redhaven | Moderate | Self-fertile | Fresh/canning | 800-900 |
| Elberta | Moderate | Self-fertile | Canning | 800-900 |
| FloridaPrince | Good | Self-fertile | Fresh | 350-400 (zone 8b) |

**Note**: All peaches are self-fertile. Shorter-lived than apples/pears (12-15 years typical). Plant 2-3 varieties to extend harvest.

#### Plums (500-900 chill hours)
| Variety | Disease Resistance | Pollination | Use | Chill Hours |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Methley | Good | Self-fertile | Fresh/preserves | 500-600 |
| Ozark Premier | Good | Self-fertile | Fresh | 700-800 |
| Santa Rosa | Moderate | Self-fertile | Fresh/canning | 500-600 |
| Stanley | Good | Self-fertile | Drying/canning | 800-900 |

**Note**: Japanese plums mostly self-fertile. European plums often self-fertile but set better with cross-pollination.

#### Cherries (600-1000 chill hours)
| Variety | Disease Resistance | Pollination | Use | Chill Hours |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Stella | Good | Self-fertile | Fresh | 600-700 |
| Lapins | Good | Self-fertile | Fresh | 500-600 |
| North Star | Good (pie cherry) | Self-fertile | Pie/preserves | 1000 |
| Montmorency | Good (pie cherry) | Self-fertile | Pie/preserves | 1000+ |

**Note**: Sweet cherries crack in rain near harvest — consider pie cherries for reliability. Birds are major pest — plan netting.

#### Figs (100-300 chill hours)
| Variety | Cold Hardiness | Use | Notes |
|---------|---------------|------|-------|
| Chicago Hardy | Zone 5 roots/Zone 7 top | Fresh/drying | Dies back, regrows |
| Brown Turkey | Zone 7 | Fresh | Most reliable |
| Celeste | Zone 7 | Fresh | Small, sweet |
| LSU Purple | Zone 7-8 | Fresh | Disease resistant |

**Note**: All common figs are self-fertile (parthenocarpic). In zones 7-8, figs may die back in severe winters — mulch heavily.

### Pollination Summary
- **Self-fertile**: Peaches, most figs, most Japanese plums, pie cherries
- **Needs pollinizer**: Most apples, most pears, sweet cherries, some plums
- **Sterile/triploid**: Some apples (Mutsu, Jonagold) — cannot pollinate others
- **Timing matters**: Bloom periods must overlap (early, mid, late season)
- **Pollinators**: Plant understory flowers for bees. Mason bees are excellent orchard pollinators

## Rootstocks

Rootstocks determine tree size, precocity, disease resistance, soil adaptation, cold hardiness, and longevity.

### Key Rootstocks
| Rootstock | Size | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|
| M9 (apple) | Dwarf (8-10') | Precocious, needs staking, fire blight susceptible |
| Bud 9 (apple) | Dwarf (8-10') | More cold hardy than M9, better fire blight resistance |
| M7 (apple) | Semi-dwarf (12-15') | Well-anchored, good general-purpose |
| MM106 (apple) | Semi-dwarf (12-15') | Good anchorage, susceptible to crown rot in wet soils |
| MM111 (apple) | Semi-standard (15-18') | Good drought tolerance |
| Antonovka (apple) | Standard (20-25'+) | Very cold hardy, disease resistant |
| OHxF 333 (pear) | Semi-dwarf (12-15') | Good fire blight resistance |
| Lovell (peach) | ~75% standard | Deep-rooted, good drought tolerance |
| Myrobalan (plum) | Vigorous | Tolerant of wet soils |
| Gisela 5 (cherry) | Semi-dwarf | Precocious, may require more management |

### Choosing Rootstocks
| Situation | Recommended |
|-----------|-------------|
| Small space | Dwarf (M9, Bud 9) |
| Moderate space | Semi-dwarf (M7, MM106) |
| Large space, longevity | Standard or MM111 |
| Poor/dry soil | MM111, Lovell |
| Wet soil | Myrobalan, MM111 |
| Fire blight prone | Bud 9, OHxF series |

### Grafting Basics
- **Whip and tongue**: Late winter, matched diameter, strongest join
- **Cleft graft**: For top-working larger trees
- **Chip bud**: Late summer, most versatile budding method
- Key: Cambium alignment, clean cuts, seal with parafilm/wax, label everything

## Chill Hours

### What They Are
Accumulated hours at 32-45°F during dormancy that trigger budbreak and normal flowering.

### Why They Matter
- **Insufficient chill**: Delayed/irregular budbreak, reduced fruit set, poor quality
- **Zone 7**: Typically 700-1200 chill hours
- **Zone 8**: Typically 400-900 chill hours
