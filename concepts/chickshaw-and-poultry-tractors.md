# Chickshaw and Poultry Tractors

A poultry tractor (or [[chicken-tractor-entity]]) is a mobile coop without a floor that is moved regularly to fresh pasture, allowing chickens to forage on grass, weeds, seeds, and insects while fertilizing the ground. A chickshaw is a specific type of poultry tractor designed as a lightweight, cart-style coop on wheels that can be easily towed or pushed by hand.

## Benefits of Mobile Poultry Housing

-  **Fresh forage daily** — Chickens access fresh grass, clover, insects, and seeds, reducing feed costs by 10-30%
-  **Fertilization** — Manure is distributed evenly across the pasture rather than concentrating in one area
-  **Pasture improvement** — Chickens scratch and disturb the soil surface, incorporating manure and reducing thatch. Followed by a rest period, the area recovers with improved fertility
-  **Pest control** — Chickens consume ticks, grasshoppers, beetle larvae, and other pests in the pasture
-  **Reduced disease** — Fresh ground reduces parasite load and disease transmission compared to fixed runs
-  **No litter management** — No deep bedding to muck out; manure goes directly onto pasture

## Chickshaw Design

The chickshaw concept (popularized by Harvey Ussery and Justin Rhodes) is a lightweight, wheeled coop designed for easy relocation.

### Core Design Elements

-  **Frame** — 2x4 or 2x3 lumber frame, kept as light as possible. Use cedar or treated lumber for ground-contact parts.
-  **Wheels** — Two bicycle wheels, wheelbarrow wheels, or pneumatic cart wheels on one end. A skid plate or smaller wheels on the other end for steering. Wheel diameter of 20-26 inches works well for rough terrain.
-  **Towing** — A rope, chain, or handle attaches to the front for pulling by hand, ATV, or garden tractor.
-  **Floor** — Open bottom (no floor). Chickens have direct contact with the ground. Optional wire mesh floor for [[fukuoka-natural-predator-protection]] in areas with digging predators.
-  **Roosting area** — Elevated roosts at the back of the coop. Provide 20-25 cm of roost space per bird.
-  **Nest boxes** — One nest box per 4-5 hens. Accessible from outside the coop for easy egg collection.
-  **Shelter** — Plywood or metal roofing on the top 2/3 of the coop. The front 1/3 can be open with hardware cloth for ventilation.
-  **Size** — A typical chickshaw houses 8-15 birds. Provide 0.1-0.15 sq m (1.5-2 sq ft) of indoor floor space per bird.

### Weight Considerations

The entire unit should be light enough for one person to move. Target total weight under 100 kg (220 lbs) empty. Strategies:

- Use 1x4 or 2x3 framing instead of 2x4 where possible
- Avoid plywood thicker than 1/2 inch for walls
- Use hardware cloth (1 inch mesh) instead of chicken wire for predator protection — chicken wire keeps chickens in but does not keep predators out
- Remove wheels for winter stationary use to reduce material cost

## Chicken Tractor Variations

### A-Frame Tractor

The classic design — a triangular A-frame structure that is lightweight and easy to build.

-  **Pros**: Simple construction, self-supporting structure, good ventilation, easy to move
-  **Cons**: Limited headroom for the farmer during cleaning/egg collection, exposed to wind, less weather protection
-  **Best for**: Summer use in mild climates, 4-8 birds

### Hoop House Tractor

A arched frame covered with wire mesh and a tarp or [[coleman-wiggle-wire-channel-greenhouse-plastic-attachment-system]] cover.

-  **Pros**: Good headroom, excellent light, can add cold-weather cover, spacious
-  **Cons**: Heavier than A-frame, more expensive materials, wind can catch the tarp
-  **Best for**: Extended-season use, larger flocks of 10-20 birds

### Skid Tractor

A coop on wooden or metal runners (skids) without wheels. Dragged to new positions.

-  **Pros**: Simplest design, no wheel maintenance, very stable, can be heavy-duty
-  **Cons**: Hard to move on rough or wet ground, can damage [[pasture-sod-seeding]] when dragged
-  **Best for**: Flat, smooth pasture; small flocks; winter stationary use

## Pasture Rotation

The key to success with poultry tractors is regular, systematic movement.

### Move Frequency

- **Daily moves** — Ideal. Maximizes forage access, prevents overgrazing, distributes manure evenly. Standard for chickshaws and lightweight tractors.
- **Every 2-3 days** — Acceptable for larger or heavier tractors that are harder to move. Increases risk of overgrazing and parasite exposure.
- **Weekly** — Minimum acceptable interval. Beyond this, pasture damage and parasite problems increase significantly.

### Pasture Recovery

- Rest each paddock for at least 30 days before chickens return
- 60-90 days of rest is ideal for full pasture recovery
- Rotate with other livestock (cattle, sheep) — chickens follow grazers and break up manure patties while consuming fly larvae
- A simple rotation through 30+ paddocks allows continuous daily moves with adequate rest

### Stocking Density

- 15-25 birds per 100 sq m of pasture (move daily)
- Adjust based on forage quality and season — more birds on lush spring pasture, fewer in summer drought
- Supplement with [[black-soldier-fly-larvae-as-feed]] and grain to maintain nutrition

## Predator Protection

Mobile coops are more vulnerable to predators than fixed coops with concrete floors.

-  **Hardware cloth** — Use 1/2 inch hardware cloth on all openings (not chicken wire). Bury it 15-30 cm into the ground or attach a skirt that extends outward to deter diggers.
-  **Nighttime security** — Close chickens into the sheltered area each evening. Raccoons, weasels, and foxes are the primary nocturnal threats.
-  **Electric fencing** — Portable electric net fencing around the tractor provides an additional layer of protection. A single strand at 15 cm and another at 45 cm deters most predators.
-  **Guard animals** — Livestock guardian dogs are effective but add complexity. Geese or guinea fowl can provide some alert capability.
-  **Rodent proofing** — Rats and mice are attracted to feed. Store feed in metal containers and clean up spilled feed daily.

## Seasonal Considerations

- **Summer** — Ensure adequate ventilation. Shade cloth over part of the wire area reduces heat stress. Move to areas with shade trees during midday.
- **Winter** — Add a tarp or plastic cover to block wind and retain heat. Reduce move frequency (stationary with deep bedding if ground is frozen). Supplement light to maintain [[mollison-designers-chicken-tractors-egg-production]] — 14-16 hours of light per day.
- **Wet seasons** — Move to higher ground or well-drained areas. Wet conditions increase parasite load and foot problems. Add a wooden floor panel if the ground is consistently muddy.

## Egg Production in Mobile Systems

Hens in well-managed mobile systems produce 200-280 eggs per year per bird, comparable to fixed-coop systems. [[factors-affecting-mycoremediation-efficiency]] production:

- Consistent access to feed and water
- Protection from weather extremes
- 14-16 hours of light per day (supplement with solar-powered lights in winter)
- Low stress (minimal predator pressure, adequate space)
- Age of the flock — peak production at 20-30 weeks, declining gradually after 72 weeks

For more intensive egg production, see [[quail-egg-production]] for alternative small-scale poultry options.
