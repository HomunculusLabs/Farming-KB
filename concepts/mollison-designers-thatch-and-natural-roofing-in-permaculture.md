## Thatch as a Multi-Use Resource

Thatch species serve multiple functions in permaculture systems beyond
roofing. Arid-area grasses cover a range of uses from human food to
poultry and waterfowl forage, thatch, and green forage or hay. Mollison
recommends a carefully selected sowing of the best grasses for
specific purposes including:

- Durable thatch and cane fences, mats, screens, or baskets
- Bundles of seed-head for poultry and to feed wild birds
- Green hay for domestic stock

Rushes used for thatch and matting provide yet another multi-use
resource. Typha (cattail) species provide browse or reed thatch for
crafts, where acid conditions prevail. Small waterholes benefit from
being thatched to reduce evaporation.

## Thatch in Beekeeping

In cool climates, thatch provides excellent insulation for bee
colonies over winter. Denmark bees are over-wintered in thatched
attics, where the natural insulation maintains colony temperature.
Sheds for bees are best thatched or insulated against cold. This
demonstrates the cross-functional value of thatch beyond human
shelter, supporting [[mollison-designers-pig-tractors-forage-systems]]
and pollinator health in permaculture design.
