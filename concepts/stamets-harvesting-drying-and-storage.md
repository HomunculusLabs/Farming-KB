## Harvesting

### Timing
The most important factor in harvesting mushrooms is timing:
- **Agaricus brunnescens**: pick before the veil breaks and stem elongates
- **Psilocybe cubensis**: pick when the veil begins to tear or soon thereafter
- Mushrooms left too long release massive spore loads that cover the casing layer and inhibit further pinhead development
- High spore loads also cause allergic reactions in workers

### Technique
1. Equipped with basket and short-bladed paring knife, grasp the base of the stem
2. With a twisting motion, pull the mushroom from the casing layer -- careful not to disturb neighboring pinheads
3. Trim the stem base, removing only flesh to which casing or substrate is attached
4. Mushrooms with thin stems: clean using a knife in a downward scraping motion
5. All trimmings placed in sealed plastic bag and removed from cropping area
6. Clumps or clusters: break apart and harvest individually when possible
7. Leave immature mushrooms attached to casing/substrate to insure continued growth

### Key Principles
- Damage to resting pinheads and disturbance of casing soil must be minimised
- Pinheads in close proximity to developing mushrooms enlarge directly after mature ones are picked
- Damaged pinheads are easily parasitised by fly larvae and contaminants
- The best pickers are meticulous, unhurried, and treat mushrooms with care
- "Border breaks" (mushrooms forming between casing and glass in jars) are due to humidity pockets and premature light stimulation

## Watering During Cropping

### Before Button Stage
- Other than light mistings, substantial waterings before button stage can damage pins
- Once mushrooms reach button size, begin building casing moisture back up to pre-pinning peak

### Building Moisture
- Series of daily, light to moderate waterings with fine misting nozzle
- Goal: reach casing moisture capacity just prior to main harvest
- High water pressure and close nozzle proximity should be avoided
- Water 2-4 times per day rather than once (allows slow absorption without surface damage)
- Keep casing surface open and porous throughout cropping cycle
- Most common cause of "panning" (closed, impermeable surface): putting on too much water at once

### Between Flushes
- After first flush harvested, keep casing moist with light mistings until next flush reaches button stage
- Then build moisture up again
- Later flushes have fewer mushrooms and require less water
- Never allow casing to dry out -- mushrooms pulled from dry casing carry large chunks, creating gaps and exposing substrate to contaminants
- If substrate exposed during picking, fill holes with moist casing
- To recondition dry casing, add moisture slowly over a few days

### Disease and Watering
- Bacterial Blotch results from mushroom caps remaining wet for extended periods
- Dry recently watered caps quickly by: lowering humidity, increasing air circulation, introducing more fresh air, or raising air temperature 1-2F
- Stop watering once mushroom cap reaches adolescence
- Do not water contaminated areas -- this spreads infection
- Strategy for serious outbreaks: lower humidity and run casing drier than normal
- Some growers use slightly chlorinated water (150 ppm)

## Environmental Factors During Cropping

### Temperature
- Air temperature becomes the controlling factor once generative growth is initiated
- Lower temperatures: postpone or lengthen harvesting, allow maximum quality control
- Higher temperatures: shorten cropping period, promote rapid intense flushes
- Dangers of high temperatures: heat buildup in substrate, CO2 generation, faster insect/contaminant growth
- Commercial growers lower air temperature 2F, 48 hours prior to peak of first and second flushes
- Further flushes run hotter to speed crop to completion

### Flushing Pattern
- Mushrooms grow in cycles called flushes or "breaks" at 7-10 day intervals
- Each successive flush bears fewer mushrooms
- Even pinning sets up uniform flushing pattern throughout cropping cycle
- Poor first flushes indicate faulty pinning procedures, lead to lower total yields
- Goal: maximise yields in early flushes (progressive contaminant buildup eventually halts growth)
- Keep harvest period short and concise; remove late-developing mushrooms on or after peak production day

### Air Movement
- Even flow across all levels of cropping surface
- Counteracts temperature stratification and dead air pockets
- During pin initiation: fresh air removes metabolic gases
- Agaricus brunnescens: 4-6 air changes per hour
- Agaricus bitorquis: needs only half the fresh air of A. brunnescens
- Psilocybe cubensis: 2-3 changes per hour sufficient
- High CO2: long stems, small underdeveloped caps (A. brunnescens, P. ostreatus)
- Too much fresh air preferable to insufficient, but disrupts environment and overworks heating/humidification systems

### Humidity
- Once primordia are set: lower to 85-92% and hold constant
- Excessive humidity without adequate air movement retards development
- Below 85%: excessive drying, small stunted mushrooms, cracked scaly caps
- Evaporation aids nutrient transport from substrate to growing mushrooms
- Balance between air circulation, fresh air, and humidification is key to the "art" of mushroom culture

## Preserving Mushrooms

### Drying (most widely used method)
- Most mushrooms are 90% water -- must be dried within a few hours or fly larvae and bacteria consume them
- Place in flow of warm, dry air
- Dried mushrooms: smaller, lighter, less fragrant than fresh
- Seal in airtight, moisture-proof plastic containers and refrigerate -- preserved for years
- Rehydrate in water before cooking -- regain much of original size and flavor
- Commercial food dehydrators: well suited (trays sometimes too close together, requiring slicing)
- Build custom dehydrator: should dry in 24-48 hours with warm air no hotter than 110F
- Open air drying at room temperature feasible using dehumidifiers with air circulation fans
- "Flash" drying at high temperatures should be avoided (loss of nutritive value and active compounds)

### Freezing
- Without prior drying: frozen mushrooms are soggy and unappealing when thawed
- Water crystallises, cells break under stress
- Mostly used in soups or stews

### Freeze Drying (ideal method)
- Preserves flavor, nutrition, form, and active compound content
- Flash freeze fresh mushrooms, then place on heated trays in cooled, high vacuum chamber
- Frozen water sublimes directly to vapor, pumped out
- Preserves much original cell structure -- mushrooms often life-like in appearance
- Commercial freeze dryers prohibitively expensive for home use
- Frost-free refrigerator provides almost as good preservation

### Canning
- Carefully clean, precook 3-4 minutes in boiling water
- Insert into glass jars with small amount of vinegar
- Sterilize in pressure cooker (30-40 minutes at 10 psi)
- Pickled canned mushrooms preferred by many epicureans

### General Preservation Guidelines
- Fresh mushrooms always taste better than preserved
- Select young mushrooms over old ones
- Label each container with: species, grower/identifier name, date, place of origin
- When eating wild mushrooms for first time: always leave one or two small specimens aside in case of illness
