# Square Foot Gardening Guide

Square foot gardening (SFG) is an intensive planting method developed by Mel Bartholomew that divides raised beds into 1x1 foot squares, each planted with a specific number of crops based on plant size. The system maximizes yield per square foot while reducing wasted space, seeds, water, and weeding.

## Core Principles

1. **Grid your bed** — permanent grid divides bed into 1-foot squares.
2. **One crop per square** — each square contains a single variety.
3. **Plant by spacing** — number of plants per square depends on mature size.
4. **Never walk on soil** — access from paths around the bed.
5. **Mel's Mix soil** — 1/3 compost, 1/3 peat moss/coir, 1/3 vermiculite.
6. **Replant immediately** — when a square is harvested, add compost and replant.

## Spacing by Plant Size

### Extra-Large Plants (1 per square foot)
- Broccoli
- Cabbage
- Cauliflower
- Eggplant
- Peppers (bell)
- Tomatoes (determinate, 1 per sq ft; indeterminate need more space — consider 2 sq ft)
- Kale
- Collards
- Summer squash (1 plant per 2 sq ft recommended)
- Zucchini (1 plant per 2 sq ft recommended)

### Large Plants (4 per square foot — 6 inch spacing)
- Lettuce (leaf)
- Swiss chard
- Leeks
- Parsley
- Cilantro
- Corn (4 per square, but plant in blocks of 4+ squares for pollination)

### Medium Plants (9 per square foot — 4 inch spacing)
- Beets
- Bush beans
- Spinach
- Turnips
- Garlic (9 per sq ft for small heads; 4 for large)
- Kohlrabi
- Radishes (winter)
- Onions (from sets)

### Small Plants (16 per square foot — 3 inch spacing)
- Carrots
- Radishes (spring)
- Green onions
- Parsnips
- Arugula

## Grid Construction

- Use vinyl window lath (1x2 inch strips), cedar strips, or twine/nails.
- Attach permanently to the bed frame or lay loosely on soil surface.
- 4x4 foot bed = 16 squares (most common SFG size).
- 4x8 foot bed = 32 squares.

## Vertical Growing

Tall and vining crops are grown vertically to save ground space:

### Crops for Vertical Growing
- Tomatoes (indeterminate — stake or string trellis)
- Cucumbers (climbing varieties — trellis or A-frame)
- Pole beans (6-8 per square on a teepee or trellis)
- Peas (8 per square on a trellis)
- Small melons (1 per square on a strong trellis with slings)
- Malabar spinach
- Vining nasturtium

### Trellis Placement
- Install trellises on the **north side** of the bed to avoid shading other plants.
- Use nylon netting, cattle panel, or string trellis attached to a frame.
- Cattle panel arches between beds create a tunnel for climbing crops and shade underneath.

## Succession Planting in Squares

The key to maximizing SFG productivity is replanting each square as soon as it's harvested.

### Example Succession Plan (Zone 5-6)

| Square | Spring (Mar-Apr) | Summer (May-Jul) | Fall (Aug-Oct) |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------|
| 1 | Peas | Bush beans | Spinach |
| 2 | Lettuce | Basil | Lettuce |
| 3 | Radishes | Carrots | Radishes |
| 4 | Spinach | Peppers | Garlic (overwinter) |
| 5 | Beets | Tomatoes | Beets |
| 6 | Scallions | Cucumbers | Kale |

### Succession Timing Rules
- Know your days to maturity (DTM) for each crop.
- Start next crop's seeds indoors or in a nursery flat while current crop finishes.
- Pull spent plants, add 1-2 inches of compost, replant immediately.
- Cool-season crops (spinach, lettuce, peas, radishes) can be double- or triple-cropped.
- Warm-season crops (tomatoes, peppers) occupy a square all summer — plant garlic in fall after cleanup.

## Seasonal Planning

### Spring Planting
- Cool-tolerant crops first: peas, lettuce, spinach, radishes, carrots, beets, onions.
- Protect with row cover or cold frame for early starts.

### Summer Planting
- Replace spring crops with warm-season crops: tomatoes, peppers, beans, cucumbers, squash, basil.
- Use shade cloth for heat-sensitive crops (lettuce, spinach) in midsummer.

### Fall Planting
- Plant cool-season crops again: kale, chard, broccoli, cabbage, radishes, carrots, beets.
- Use row cover to extend the season into November in most zones.

### Winter (Mild Climates)
- Garlic, onions, and cover crops can occupy beds through winter.
- Cold frames or hoop tunnels allow year-round growing in zones 6-8.

## Square Foot Gardening vs. Row Gardening

| Feature | Square Foot | Row Gardening |
|---------|------------|---------------|
| Space efficiency | Very high | Low |
| Soil needed | Little (Mel's mix) | Large volume |
| Weeding | Minimal | Significant |
| Yield per sq ft | High | Low to moderate |
| Crop rotation | Easy (swap squares) | More planning needed |
| Seed use | Precise, minimal | Often over-planted |
| Setup cost | Moderate (beds + mix) | Low |
| Best for | Small spaces, beginners | Large-scale, mechanized |

## Common Mistakes

- Overcrowding — more plants per square does NOT mean more yield.
