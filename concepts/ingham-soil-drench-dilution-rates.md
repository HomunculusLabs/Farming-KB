# Soil Drench and Dilution Rates for Compost Tea

Soil drenching with [[compost-tea-aact]] delivers beneficial
organisms directly into the root zone, where they improve soil
structure, retain nutrients, cycle nutrients into plant-available
forms, and compete with root pathogens. According to Elaine Ingham,
the decision between foliar and soil application depends on what
problems need solving -- fungal root diseases require soil
drenching, while leaf diseases require foliar coverage.

## Why Soil Drench Instead of Foliar

If you have been doing only foliar sprays and wonder why soil
structure is not improving, the answer is straightforward: foliar
applications primarily benefit leaf surfaces. Soil drenches are
needed to build soil structure, increase water-holding capacity,
and establish beneficial organisms in the root zone. Fall is the
best time for soil drenches, spring the next best time. Apply both
if soil structure is poor.

## Standard Application Rates

From Ingham's field guide recommendations:

- **Planting time**: 15-20 gallons of tea per acre as soil drench
- **Growing season**: 20 gallons per acre for soil drench
- **Foliar companion**: 5 gallons per acre on foliage per
  application (with 3 applications during season)
- **Fruit trees**: 20 gallons per acre soil drench for each 6 feet
  of canopy height
- **Rice systems**: soil drench in fall and spring, then foliar at
  first sign of disease
- **Vineyards**: 150 liters per hectare as soil drench

## Water as Carrier

Water should be regarded only as a carrier for the tea. You make
certain to put the required gallons of tea per acre, then add water
to reach the total volume needed for your sprayer to evenly cover
the area. For example, if your sprayer needs 35 gallons to evenly
cover one acre, and you want 20 gallons of tea per acre, then 20
gallons will be tea and 15 gallons will be water. Always
de-chlorinate the water you add to the spray tank.

## Dilution Considerations

Dilution affects how well organisms attach and survive:
- For soil drenches, dilution is less critical -- organisms hold
  up on organic matter in soil and begin retaining nutrients
- For foliar applications, check your dilution if you are not
  getting good "stick" on leaf surfaces
- Too concentrated tea can create oxygen demand problems in soil
- Buffer with salt solutions may be needed when mixing with certain
  amendments

## Nutrient Delivery Through Tea

Adding nutrients to the tea rather than applying them directly to
soil is significantly more efficient:
- Nutrients processed by organisms in tea are more plant-available
  and better retained in soil
- You need approximately 80% LESS mineral nutrient when added to
  compost or tea compared to direct soil application
- Typical organic N needs: 25-35 lbs per acre in gardens, 50-75
  lbs per acre in agricultural fields
- Compost tea with 0.1% each of fish hydrolysate, kelp, and protein
  source equates to approximately 1 lb of N per acre -- but with
  much higher efficiency than inorganic fertilizer

The reason most N application rates are so high conventionally is
because nutrients leach without microorganisms to hold them in soil.
Stop leaching losses and you only replace what you remove in
harvest.

## Timing for Soil Drenches

- **Wait until soil is warm enough** for organisms to grow before
  applying spring drenches
- **Fall drenches** are critical -- they establish organisms to
  decompose leaf litter and prevent pathogen overwintering
- **Spring drenches** should be applied two weeks before bud break
  (but wait until soil warms)
- **Planting time** drenches get beneficial biology established
  around new roots immediately

## When Soil Drench Alone Is Insufficient

A fungal soil drench works well for many situations, but when the
soil is severely compacted or lacks organic matter, tea alone may
not be enough. If you apply tea to straight sand with no organic
matter, organisms will not survive. They need food resources. In
severely degraded situations, compost application alongside tea
provides the necessary habitat and food base.

## Multiple Application Strategy

The first year of a compost tea program requires the most intensive
application schedule:
- First 4 months: every 7-10 days
- Following months: once per month
- Maintenance phase: three times per growing season
- If disturbance occurs (tillage, pesticide use, compaction):
  return to weekly applications

The organisms will continue as long as you have plants in the
system or add foods to feed them. But many human activities kill
them -- pesticide drift, compaction from traffic, tillage, bare
soil -- requiring re-inoculation.

## Soil Drench for Root Disease

Applying a fungal soil drench is particularly helpful for:
- Trees with fungal root diseases attacking the root zone
- Areas where root-feeding nematodes are present
- Compacted soils needing fungal hyphae to build structure
- Post-herbicide recovery where soil biology has been damaged
- Establishing mycorrhizal colonization around new plantings
