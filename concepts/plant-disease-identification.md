# Plant Disease Identification

Accurate disease identification is the critical first step in effective [[plant-disease-management-in-organic-gardens]]. Misidentification leads to incorrect treatment, wasted resources, and potentially worse outcomes. This guide provides a systematic approach to diagnosing plant diseases based on symptoms, affected plant parts, and environmental conditions.

## Systematic Diagnostic Approach

1. Identify the plant -- know what species and variety you are dealing with
2. Note which parts are affected (leaves, stems, roots, fruit, flowers)
3. Observe symptom patterns (uniform, random, spreading from one point)
4. Consider environmental conditions (temperature, moisture, recent weather)
5. Look for signs of the pathogen (visible fungal structures, bacterial ooze)
6. Compare against known diseases for that plant species
7. Confirm diagnosis before implementing treatment

## Symptoms by Plant Part

### Leaf Symptoms

| Symptom Pattern | Likely Cause | Key Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|---|
| White powdery coating (top/bottom) | [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]] | Dry conditions, starts lower leaves, rubs off |
| Yellow patches with gray fuzz underneath | Downy mildew | Angular lesions bounded by veins, cool wet weather |
| Brown concentric ring spots ("bullseye") | Early blight (Alternaria) | Starts on lower leaves, solanaceous crops |
| Yellowing between veins (interveinal) | [[cannabis-grow-nutrient-deficiency-problem-solving]] | Often iron or manganese, pH-related |
| Mosaic pattern (light/dark green) | Virus (TMV, CMV) | Irregular pattern, may include leaf curling |
| Small dark spots with yellow halos | Bacterial spot | Water-soaked edges, "shot-hole" when centers drop |
| Rust-colored pustules on undersides | Rust fungi | Orange-brown powder when touched |
| Dark streaking along veins | Bacterial infection | Spreads from margins, systemic movement |

### Stem Symptoms

| Symptom Pattern | Likely Cause | Key Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|---|
| Dark sunken cankers | Fungal canker or fire blight | Oozing, cracked bark, girdling |
| White fluffy growth at base | Southern blight (Sclerotium) | Sclerotia (hard spheres) present in soil |
| Wilting of one side | Vascular wilt (fungal or bacterial) | Cut stem shows brown discolored tissue |
| Swollen tumors at base | Crown gall | Rough, woody galls, very wide host range |
| Soft, mushy, foul-smelling | Bacterial soft rot | Watery, rotten tissue, strong odor |

### Root Symptoms

| Symptom Pattern | Likely Cause | Key Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|---|
| Dark brown/black rotted roots | Pythium, Phytophthora | Wet soil conditions, "root rot" |
| Knots/galls on roots | Root-knot nematode | Visible swellings, stunted top growth |
| Reddish root discoloration | Fusarium wilt | Vascular discoloration extends into stem |
| Sparse, stunted root system | Overwatering or nutrient deficiency | No pathogen signs, uniform pattern |

### Fruit Symptoms

| Symptom Pattern | Likely Cause | Key Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|---|
| Water-soaked lesions, rapid collapse | Late blight | Fast-spreading, gray fuzz on margins |
| Sunken dark spots with pink spore masses | Anthracnose | Concentric rings possible, warm wet weather |
| Black leathery spots on blossom end | Blossom-end rot | [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]], not infectious |
| Gray fuzzy mold on soft fruit | Botrytis gray mold | Starts on damaged or senescing tissue |
| Mosaic pattern or ring spots on fruit | Virus | Associated leaf symptoms present |

## Common Diseases by Crop

### Tomatoes
- Early blight -- brown "bullseye" spots, lower leaves first
- Late blight -- rapid water-soaked lesions, entire plant collapse
- Septoria leaf spot -- small circular spots with dark borders and tiny black fruiting bodies
- Fusarium wilt -- yellowing on one side, vascular browning
- TMV -- leaf mosaic and distortion, fruit mottling
- Blossom-end rot -- black leathery bottom (calcium deficiency, not disease)

### Squash and Cucurbits
- Powdery mildew -- white coating on leaves, common in late summer
- Downy mildew -- angular yellow patches, gray fuzz underneath
- Bacterial wilt -- sudden wilting of entire vine, sticky white ooze from cut stem
- Anthracnose -- circular sunken spots on fruit
- Cucumber mosaic virus -- mottling, shoestring leaves, stunting

### Apples and Pears
- Apple scab -- olive-green velvety spots on leaves and fruit
- Fire blight -- blackened blossoms, shepherd's crook branch tips
- Cedar apple rust -- orange gelatinous spots on leaves and fruit
- Powdery mildew -- gray-white coating on leaves and shoots
- Flyspeck and sooty blotch -- dark smudges on fruit surface

### Peppers
- Bacterial spot -- small water-soaked spots, defoliation
- PVY -- mosaic, leaf distortion, fruit necrosis
- Anthracnose -- fruit rot with concentric rings
- Phytophthora blight -- rapid wilting, dark stem lesions
- CMV -- severe mosaic, ring spots, stunting

## Fungal vs Bacterial vs Viral: Quick Comparison

| Feature | Fungal | Bacterial | Viral |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visible structures | Spores, mold, mycelium | Ooze, water-soaking | None visible |
| Spread mechanism | Wind, water, soil, tools | Water splash, tools, insects | Insects, tools, seed |
| Environmental trigger | Humidity, specific temps | Moisture on leaves | Any time (vector-dependent) |
| Cure available | Fungicides (some) | Copper, streptomycin (preventive) | None -- prevention only |
| Composting safe | Usually no for infected tissue | No -- destroy | No -- destroy |

## Diagnostic Tools

- Hand lens (10-20x) for examining spores and fungal structures
- Knife for cutting stems to check vascular tissue
- Plastic bag with moist paper towel for incubating samples
- Camera for documenting symptoms over time
- Plant disease diagnostic apps (limited accuracy, use as starting point)
- Local extension service for lab confirmation
