# Information Paradigm of Consciousness

The information paradigm of consciousness proposes that mind is not a separate substance from matter but is rather a particular form of information embodied within the neuronal substrate of the brain.

This framework, developed most thoroughly by Simon G. Powell in The Psilocybin Solution, draws upon the phenomenological evidence provided by [[entheogen-culture]] to construct a unified view of reality in which everything, whether atoms, molecules, organisms, or thoughts, can be described in purely informational terms.

## Foundations of the Theory

The information paradigm emerges from two key observations about the psychedelic experience.

First, chemically induced changes in the global patterns of neuronal firing are directly associated with changes in consciousness.

Since global states of neuronal firing are necessarily global states of information, consciousness can be understood as a form of information processing.

Second, the content of [[psilocybin-and-mystical-experience]], particularly the vivid visionary experiences induced by substances like [[psilocybin-compound-profile]], demonstrates that cerebral information has a spontaneous tendency to organize and integrate itself into coherent patterns without deliberate effort on the part of the conscious self.

These observations lead to the central proposition: consciousness is an informational pattern substantiated within the brain's electrochemical activity, and this information naturally tends toward self-organization and integration.

## Huxley's Doors of Perception

The informational view builds directly upon Aldous Huxley's theory of Mind at Large, articulated in The Doors of Perception (1954).

Huxley proposed that ordinary waking consciousness represents a filtered and restricted subset of a vastly greater field of awareness, which he called Mind at Large.

The function of the brain, in Huxley's view, is not to produce consciousness but to reduce it, acting as a filter or reducing valve that admits only that portion of Mind at Large that is practically useful for survival.

Psychedelics, in this framework, temporarily disable or bypass the brain's filtering mechanism, allowing a greater volume of information from Mind at Large to enter conscious awareness.

This is why the psychedelic experience is characterized by an overwhelming influx of perceptions, insights, and visionary content that exceeds the normal processing capacity of the ego.

## Potential and Active Information

Powell distinguishes between potential information and active information, a distinction crucial to understanding how the information paradigm operates.

Potential information exists in a latent state, not yet perceived or actualized.

An egg contains an enormous amount of potential information, the complete genetic blueprint for a chicken, yet this information remains dormant until the egg is incubated under appropriate conditions.

Similarly, fingerprints on a gun contain potential information about who handled the weapon, but this information remains inaccessible until perceived by a forensic investigator.

The pattern of ink on an exam paper contains potential information about a student's knowledge, but it remains latent until read and interpreted by an examiner.

Active information, by contrast, is information that is currently being processed, integrated, or communicated.

It is information in the act of becoming meaningful.

The psychedelic experience can be understood as the rapid conversion of enormous quantities of potential information into active information within the brain.

Memories, associations, archetypal images, and perceptual patterns that normally remain dormant in the unconscious are suddenly brought into active consciousness, creating the characteristic flood of the psychedelic state.

## Information Integration in Visionary Experience

One of the strongest pieces of evidence for the informational paradigm comes from the nature of visionary experiences themselves.

The visions encountered during [[psilocybin-and-mystical-experience]] are not random noise but are richly structured, meaningful, and often organized around powerful symbolic patterns.

This suggests that large quantities of neuronal information are being integrated and cohered into structured forms, much as large numbers of water molecules organize themselves into the specific crystalline patterns of snowflakes under appropriate conditions.

The fact that these visionary patterns often exhibit universal features, appearing independently in different cultures and historical periods, suggests that there are stable, organized ways in which information within the human brain naturally structures itself when released from the normal constraints of the ego.

## Implications for the Mind-Body Problem

The information paradigm offers a potential resolution to the ancient [[mind-body-problem-and-psychedelics]] by proposing that both mind and matter are fundamentally the same kind of substance: information.

In this view, the apparent gulf between the mental and the physical dissolves when both are understood as different expressions of the same underlying informational reality.

Physical matter is simply information organized according to the rules of physics and chemistry.

Consciousness is information organized according to the rules of neurobiology and psychology.

Both are real, both are informational, and both are aspects of the same fundamental reality process.

## Consciousness as Information Processing

The paradigm suggests that the word consciousness itself, derived from roots meaning knowing together, points toward its essential nature as the integration and co-processing of multiple streams of information.

The greater the field of information being processed or integrated, the more conscious we become.

This explains why the psychedelic experience, which dramatically increases the volume and scope of information flowing through consciousness, is consistently described as an expansion or heightening of awareness rather than a reduction.

It also explains why certain practices like meditation, which gradually reduce the filtering mechanisms of the ego, produce experiences that share significant features with the psychedelic state, including the sense of expanded awareness, unity, and access to information normally outside conscious reach.

## The Other as Informational Intelligence

A particularly provocative implication of the information paradigm concerns the nature of the Other encountered in psychedelic visionary states.

If consciousness is a form of information and this information naturally tends toward self-organization, then sufficiently large-scale information integration within the brain might generate an informational pattern so complex and coherent that it appears to possess intentionality, intelligence, and agency distinct from the ego.

This Other, which appears across cultures and throughout history in shamanic, mystical, and psychedelic contexts, can be understood not as a supernatural entity but as the natural product of extreme information integration within the neuronal substrate.

## Relationship to Modern Neuroscience

Contemporary neuroscience has begun to converge with aspects of the information paradigm through concepts like integrated information theory (IIT), proposed by Giulio Tononi, which measures consciousness in terms of the degree of information integration within a system.

The finding that psychedelics increase the complexity and entropy of brain activity, as demonstrated by modern [[psilocybin-neuroscience]] and brain imaging studies, supports the idea that expanded consciousness corresponds to expanded information processing and integration.

## Criticisms and Limitations

Critics of the information paradigm note that it risks being overly broad, potentially reducing all of reality to a single vague category.

The challenge of defining information itself in a non-circular way remains significant.

Some philosophers argue that conflating consciousness with information merely substitutes one mystery for another, replacing the hard problem of consciousness with the equally puzzling question of what information fundamentally is.

Despite these challenges, the information paradigm provides a uniquely useful framework for understanding the psychedelic experience and its implications for our understanding of consciousness and reality.
