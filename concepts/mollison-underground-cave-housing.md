## Courtyard Pit Design

In North Africa, large pits (20 x 20 m) are cut into the ground, and
caves built off them. These courtyard pits, with rooms off them into
soft rock, are cool and protected environments. Good gutters are
needed to lead water around the pits, and the walls can be trellised,
as can the top opening. Water cisterns can be cut into the floor of
the pit or into the upper surface. The courtyards are drained by a
cave exit to a cliff face.

## Mediterranean Facade Houses

In the Canary Islands, houses are seen where only the facade stands
out, the rest hidden underground. Even the facade is shaded by grape
trellis, as are many roof areas. In many Mediterranean countries,
trellised entries and bright facades satisfy aesthetic needs, and
walls are often of veined, colourful, or patterned stone.

## Hot and Cold Cave Storage

Caves can be built to be "hot" or "cold" for storage purposes:

### Hot Caves
- A bubble of hot still air gathers in the cave during the day
- The entry is overhanging to trap hot upslope air
- Cave walls are an effective heat store
- Suit dried fruits and mummify wood and paper
- May be unhealthy for living but excellent grain and livestock
  storages in winter
- Must be built upslope from the entry to hold the hot air

### Cold Caves
- Excellent root storage areas and general storage for books,
  films, machine parts, and foodstuffs
- Entry lies in a dip on a hillside, permitting cold night air to
  pool in the downsloping cave
- A sill to the entry is essential where rains can affect drainage

## Underground Toilet Systems

In Coober Pedy, toilets are a vertical drill-hole 20-30 m deep, sited
a few feet from the door. Low-flush toilet systems can feed into them,
and they are vented to air. Alternatively, they can be dry pits with
compost, down-draught, and solar chimneys. In either case they last
for years, but are best occasionally pumped out for establishment of
trees around the site.

## The Forestieri House

One of the more extraordinary underground homes was cut and planted at
Fresno, California, under a calcrete ceiling by a miner (M. Forestieri).
His underground house covered some 2.8 hectares and included bathrooms,
fishponds, and a large citrus garden, of which the only sign on the
surface was the tops of the citrus trees.

## Lighting and Aesthetics

For those with deep-seated ambitions, Fresnel lenses and surface
mirrors, with reflector mirrors and light guides, will illuminate
tunnels and rooms for kilometres deep. "Camera obscurae" or lenses
throw scenes of the outer world on walls, where the outside world can
be admired but the heat avoided.
