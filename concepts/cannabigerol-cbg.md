# Cannabigerol (CBG)

Cannabigerol (CBG) is a non-psychoactive cannabinoid found in the cannabis plant, often called the "mother cannabinoid" because it serves as the biochemical precursor from which all other cannabinoids are synthesized. In the living cannabis plant, CBG exists primarily as its acidic form, cannabigerolic acid (CBGA), which is enzymatically converted by specific synthase enzymes into THCA, CBDA, and CBCA as the plant matures. Because most of the CBGA is converted into other cannabinoids during the plant's growth cycle, CBG typically constitutes less than 1% of the total cannabinoid content in mature cannabis, making it one of the most valuable and difficult-to-produce cannabinoids in the industry.

## Biosynthesis and the "Mother Cannabinoid" Role

CBG occupies a unique position in cannabis biochemistry as the stem cell of the cannabinoid family. The biosynthetic pathway works as follows:

1. **Olivetolic acid** and **geranyl pyrophosphate** combine through the enzyme cannabigerolic acid synthase to form **CBGA** (cannabigerolic acid)
2. CBGA then serves as the substrate for three key enzymes:
   - **THCA synthase** converts CBGA → THCA (which decarboxylates to thc)
   - **CBDA synthase** converts CBGA → CBDA (which decarboxylates to [[cannabidiol-cbd]])
   - **CBCA synthase** converts CBGA → CBCA (which decarboxylates to CBC)
3. Any CBGA not converted by these enzymes remains as CBGA, which upon heating (decarboxylation) becomes CBG

This central position in the biosynthetic pathway is why CBG levels are highest in young cannabis plants (2-6 weeks into flowering) before the majority of CBGA has been converted. Modern cultivators have begun selectively breeding high-CBG varieties and harvesting earlier to maximize CBG yields, though this requires careful timing to balance CBG production against overall cannabinoid yield.

## Chemical Properties

CBG is a neutral, non-acidic phytocannabinoid with the molecular formula C₂₁H₃₂O₂. Key chemical characteristics include:

- **Molecular weight**: 316.49 g/mol
- **Boiling point**: Approximately 125°C (257°F), requiring relatively low temperatures for vaporization
- **Solubility**: Lipophilic, with poor water solubility — typical of cannabinoids
- **Stability**: Relatively stable when protected from light and heat, though gradual degradation can occur over extended storage
- **Non-psychoactive**: Does not bind significantly to CB1 receptors in the psychoactive brain regions, producing no intoxication or cognitive impairment

## Mechanism of Action

CBG interacts with multiple receptor systems in the body, giving it a broad [[cannabidiol-cbd-therapeutic-profile-blesching]]:

- **CB1 receptor**: Acts as a partial agonist with very low efficacy, meaning it binds but produces minimal activation — this is why CBG is non-intoxicating
- **CB2 receptor**: Moderate agonist activity, contributing to anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects throughout the [[endocannabinoid-system]]
- **5-HT1A [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]]**: Agonist activity contributing to anxiolytic and antidepressant effects
- **Alpha-2 adrenoceptor**: Agonist activity that may contribute to pain relief and vascular effects
- **TRPV1 (vanilloid receptor)**: Agonist activity that influences pain perception and thermoregulation
- **PPAR-gamma**: Activation of this nuclear receptor contributes to anti-inflammatory and metabolic effects
- **GABA reuptake inhibition**: CBG inhibits the reuptake of gamma-aminobutyric acid, the brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter, contributing to muscle relaxant and anti-anxiety effects — this mechanism is shared with pharmaceutical benzodiazepines and some anticonvulsants

## Therapeutic Applications

### Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)

CBG has demonstrated particularly promising effects in models of inflammatory bowel disease, including Crohn's disease [[cannabis-and-ulcerative-colitis]]:

- Reduces intestinal inflammation and damage in murine models of colitis
- Decreases nitric oxide production in colon tissue, reducing oxidative stress
- Inhibits [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]] (ROS) formation in intestinal epithelial cells
- Reduces the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, iNOS) in the gut
- Shows efficacy comparable to 5-aminosalicylic acid (mesalamine), a standard IBD treatment, in preclinical models

### Neuroprotection

CBG shows significant neuroprotective properties across multiple disease models:

- **Huntington's disease**: In a landmark 2015 study, CBG demonstrated neuroprotective effects in a mouse model of Huntington's disease, improving motor deficits and protecting striatal neurons from degeneration. This represents one of the few cannabinoid compounds shown to be active against this currently untreatable neurodegenerative condition.
- **Parkinson's disease**: Protects dopaminergic neurons from oxidative damage and may improve motor function
- **[[blesching-cannabis-multiple-sclerosis]]**: Reduces neuroinflammation and may protect myelin-producing oligodendrocytes
- **General neuroprotection**: Exhibits antioxidant properties and reduces excitotoxicity through multiple receptor pathways

### Antibacterial Activity

CBG has emerged as one of the most potent antibacterial cannabinoids, with activity against clinically significant pathogens:

- Potent activity against **methicillin-resistant [[staphylococcus-aureus]]** (MRSA), including drug-resistant strains that do not respond to conventional antibiotics
- Effective against gram-positive bacteria with minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) comparable to standard antibiotics
- Shows synergy with certain conventional antibiotics, potentially restoring efficacy against resistant strains
- Does not appear to readily induce bacterial resistance, making it a promising candidate for addressing the antibiotic resistance crisis

### Glaucoma

CBG has demonstrated intraocular pressure-lowering effects that may be therapeutically relevant for glaucoma treatment:

- Reduces intraocular pressure in animal models through a mechanism distinct from THC
- May improve aqueous humor outflow through CB1 receptor-mediated pathways
- Shows neuroprotective effects on retinal ganglion cells, which are progressively damaged in glaucoma
- Unlike THC, produces this benefit without psychoactive side effects

### Pain and Inflammation

CBG's multi-receptor activity makes it effective for various pain conditions:

- Reduces inflammatory pain through CB2 receptor activation
