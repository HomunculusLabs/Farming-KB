# Domestic Small Animals in Zone One

In Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison assigns
small, quiet domestic animals to Zone 1, the area immediately
surrounding the home. These animals fish, rabbits, pigeons,
guinea pigs, and the [[holzer-medicinal-and-culinary-herbs-compendium]] used in food preparation.
Rainwater catchment tanks are also placed here. Within 6 m (20
feet) or so of a home, householders can produce most of the
food necessary to existence.

## The Principle of Everything Gardens

Mollison's principle that "everything makes its own garden"
applies directly to small animals. Rabbits make burrows and
defecation mounds, scratch out roots, create short swards or
lawns, and also create conditions favourable for weeds such as
thistles. We can use the rabbit directly as food, to help in
fire control, to prepare soil for cardoons and globe artichokes,
and to shelter many native animal species in their abandoned
burrows. Rabbits maintain species-rich moorland swards suited to
many orchids and other small plants.

It is a matter of careful consideration as to where the rabbit,
and ourselves, belong in any system, and whether we should
control or manage their effects or tolerate them. When we
examine how plants and animals change ecosystems, we may find
many allies in our efforts to sustain ourselves and other
species.

## Rabbits in the System

Rabbits are classified as megafauna of the soil biota (20 mm
upwards). Their tunnels give shelter to possum, squirrels,
bandicoots, snakes, and frogs. In the broader landscape,
rabbits industriously garden thistles, creating species-rich
swards. Their abandoned warrens are occupied by many other
species, making them inadvertent ecosystem engineers.

Guard dogs can be deployed for deer, rabbits, and other
vertebrate pests. Rabbits maintain species-rich moorland swards
suited to many orchids and other small plants. Their droppings
enrich soil, and their grazing behaviour can be managed to
prepare ground for planting of perennial crops.

## Guinea Pigs for Tree Establishment

In the [[mollison-tropical-home-garden-design]] garden, the guinea pig aids small tree
establishment as they "chip" the base of young grasses,
effectively controlling grass competition around newly planted
seedlings without disturbing the soil or damaging the trees.
This makes them valuable allies in the early phases of system
establishment where grass suppression is critical.

## Pigeons for Protein

Pigeons are most easily protected from predators by elevation on
pole structures, or over shallow ponds. They provide a reliable
source of protein in the home garden with minimal space
requirements. In many traditional systems [[fungi-around-the-world-species-distribution]],
pigeon lofts are integrated into the house or placed on nearby
poles, providing eggs and squab with very little feed input
when the birds can forage freely.

## Poultry Integration

Chickens are the most common Zone 1 animal, with laying boxes
placed close to hand. The chicken laying flock requires 365
visits per year for egg collection alone, justifying their
placement in the most intensively managed zone. Chickens
provide [[mushroom-pests-insect-control-stamets]], manure for gardens, and egg production.
Their foraging behaviour helps [[query-how-to-control-pests-without-chemicals]] and cycle nutrients
from kitchen wastes back into the garden system.

## Techniques for Zone One Animals

Techniques in Zone One include complete mulching, intensive
pruning of trees, annuals with fast replacement of crop, full
land use, and nutrient recycling of household wastes. The
integration of small animals into this intensive zone means that
wastes from the house feed the animals, and animal manures are
recycled to the garden beds within a very short distance.

Small domestic and quiet animals are preferred in this zone
because they do not require large forage ranges, are relatively
easy to manage and protect, and their products (eggs, meat,
manure) can be harvested without significant time or energy
expenditure. The zoning principle is that the more visits needed,
the closer the objects need to be.
