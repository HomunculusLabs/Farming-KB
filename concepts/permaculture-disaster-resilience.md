The fundamental insight from permaculture is that the same design strategies that create sustainable, productive systems also create resilient ones.

Diversity of species, redundancy of functions, distributed resources, local self-reliance, and biological robustness all serve everyday productivity while simultaneously providing buffers against disruption.

## The Vulnerability of Modern Systems

In modern affluent societies, the flow of energy in useful forms has become so reliably available that energy capture and storage have ceased to be major concerns.

People depend on money to purchase needs, and purchasing power depends on credit and permanent employment. No food or fuel is stored in the house.

At the same time, economic rationalism has led to the decline of large-scale stores of food, fuels, materials, essential spare parts, and permanent employment in the interests of efficiency.

This pursuit of efficiency increases the likelihood of disruption and disaster. The vulnerability of modern systems to slight disturbance was highlighted by the Y2K concern, which revealed the fragility of just-in-time supply chains.

## Self-Reliance as the Foundation

david holmgren argues that building greater personal and local self-reliance is essential to avoid disasters from inevitable disruptions to energy and resource supply lines.

Self-reliance means rebuilding traditional forms of energy storage in food, fuel, and other resources that complement the landscape storages of energy.

At Holmgren's Melliodora property, the seedbox, cellar full of produce, and stack of firewood represent traditional storages of modest wealth which are enjoyed and celebrated.

A pantry full of preserves captures the essence of a bountiful season and provides insurance against a bad one. A large wood stack represents both natural abundance and honest work maturing through weather and sunshine.

These household energy storages are by nature diverse, small, dispersed, easily used, and not portable enough to attract thieves.

A nation full of such wealth is much more secure and stable than one dependent on the high-throughput, centralized systems of the supermarket and electricity grid.

## Design Principles for Resilience

Natural systems provide the model for resilient design. Bodies of warm-blooded animals conserve heat for essential organs in extreme cold, allowing limbs to die if necessary.

In the evolution of Gaia, critical planetary functions are performed by microbes capable of surviving the greatest catastrophes. All the complexity of advanced plants and animals can be trashed by ice ages or meteor impacts without core planetary functions being affected.

In social unrest and famine, most functions break down while the family and household persist as the fundamental units of society.

Experience in African famines suggests that even when family relationships break down, the last to fail is that between mother and child.

Under extreme stress, systems fall back to lower-order, small-scale, and older, more proven structures and processes.

Modern society has lost or actively dispensed with most of its informal fallback strategies and back-up protection of essential functions.

## Bushfire as a Case Study

Permaculture One included a section on design against bushfire as an integral part of all rural design in Australia, the worst bushfire region in the world.

This design-based approach recognized that self-reliance in the face of natural disasters is essential for a society based on declining energy.

The experience of fighting large bushfires in semi-rural areas in the 1980s and 1990s made fire services realize the limits of top-down management when populations have a culture of dependence.

Community Fireguard groups now facilitate local self-reliance and encourage prepared householders to stay at home during severe fires.

Professional fire-fighting resources cannot substitute for personal, household, and community self-reliance. In 1983, without self-reliant communities, top-down management came close to causing deaths through evacuation.

## Systems Under Stress

Very powerful systems can insulate against small disasters but this leads to lack of fitness for large disasters.

Eventually a disaster arrives which overwhelms top-down management systems, hastening catastrophe. In nature, small stresses keep the system fit and adapted.

Today, peasants in the world's poorest countries remain amazingly resilient in the face of natural disasters. Self-reliant response remains the predominant factor in restoration of normality.

In the 1989 San Francisco earthquake, community response in poor neighborhoods was more effective than in affluent suburbs.

The vulnerability of modern society is so great that a series of events could lead to global depression or collapse of supply systems.
