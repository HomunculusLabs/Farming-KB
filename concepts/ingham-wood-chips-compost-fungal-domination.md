# Wood Chips in Compost for Fungal Domination

## Summary

Wood chips and other woody materials are the primary tool for creating
fungal-dominated compost, which is essential for perennial crops,
trees, and disease suppression in the soil foodweb. Their high carbon-
to-nitrogen ratio, recalcitrant lignin content, and physical structure
create ideal habitat for saprophytic fungi. Ingham provides specific
guidance on chip size, composting methods, and how wood chip compost
differs fundamentally from bacterial-dominated thermal compost. (see [[fungal-decomposition-resource-quality-determinants]]).

Materials with wide C:N ratios selectively favor fungi over bacteria. (see [[ingham-carbon-nitrogen-ratios-compost-tea]]).

The physical structure of wood chips also matters. Their size and shape
create air pockets in the compost pile, maintaining aerobic conditions
that fungi require. Fungal hyphae grow across and between chip surfaces,
using the wood as both substrate and structural support for their
network. This habitat function is as important as the nutritional
function. (see [[fungal-hyphae]]
