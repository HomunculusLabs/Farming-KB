# Panaeolus and Panaeolina Neurotropic Species

The genera **Panaeolus** and **Panaeolina** (family Coprinaceae) together contain **17 neurotropic species**, making this group one of the more significant contributors to the world's psychoactive mycoflora. These are predominantly **coprophilous** (dung inhabiting) mushrooms, commonly found on herbivore dung in pastures and grasslands worldwide. The definitive taxonomic treatment is by Gerhardt (1996).

## Taxonomic Distinction

The separation of Panaeolus and Panaeolina has been debated:

- **Panaeolus**: Two spored basidia; includes the majority of neurotropic species
- **Panaeolina**: Four spored basidia; primarily P. foenisecii

Gerhardt (1996) provided a comprehensive taxonomic revision of both genera. Some authorities have merged Panaeolina into Panaeolus, but the Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz (1998) treatment maintains the two genus distinction.

## Neurotropic Species in Panaeolina

| Species | Distribution | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| **P. foenisecii** (the haymaker's mushroom) | Cosmopolitan; Europe, Americas, Africa, Asia, Oceania | The most widespread species in the group; common in lawns |

The psychoactivity of P. foenisecii has been debated. Robbers et al. (1969), Fiussello and Ceruti Scurti (1972), and Allen and Merlin (1992c) have studied its indole alkaloid content. It generally contains very low levels of psilocybin and psilocin compared to other neurotropic species, and its psychoactivity is considered questionable by some authors.

## Geographic Distribution by Continent

### Europe

Europe has the best documented diversity for this group, with the following species recorded:

**Widely distributed across Europe:**
- P. ater, P. fimicola, P. olivaceus, P. papilionaceus, P. retirugis, P. sphinctrinus, P. subbalteatus

**Country specific records:**
- **Germany**: P. ater, P. fimicola, P. papilionaceus, P. retirugis, P. sphinctrinus, P. subbalteatus
- **Great Britain**: P. ater, P. castaneifolius, P. fimicola, P. olivaceus, P. papilionaceus, P. retirugis, P. sphinctrinus, P. subbalteatus
- **Italy**: P. ater, P. fimicola, P. papilionaceus, P. retirugis, P. sphinctrinus, P. subbalteatus
- **France**: P. papilionaceus, P. subbalteatus
- **Netherlands**: P. papilionaceus, P. subbalteatus
- **Scandinavia (Denmark, Finland, Norway, Sweden)**: P. ater, P. fimicola, P. olivaceus, P. papilionaceus, P. sphinctrinus, P. subbalteatus
- **Iceland**: P. ater, P. fimicola, P. papilionaceus, P. sphinctrinus, P. subbalteatus
- **Canary Islands**: P. sphinctrinus

## Chemistry and Potency

The Panaeolus/Panaeolina group generally contains **lower concentrations** of psilocybin and psilocin compared to Psilocybe and Copelandia species. Key chemical findings:

- **P. subbalteatus**: Contains psilocybin, psilocin, and baeocystin; considered moderately potent
- **P. sphinctrinus**: Contains low levels of psilocybin and psilocin
- **P. fimicola**: Contains trace amounts of indole alkaloids
- **P. foenisecii**: Questionable psychoactivity; contains very low levels of active compounds
- **P. africanus**: Contains psilocybin and related compounds

## Ecological Role

All Panaeolus and Panaeolina species are coprophilous, playing an important ecological role in decomposing herbivore dung. They are among the first fungal colonizers of fresh dung and contribute to nutrient cycling in grassland ecosystems. Their dung preference means they are commonly found in:

- Pastures and meadows
- Horse paddocks
- Cow fields
- Occasionally on enriched soil
