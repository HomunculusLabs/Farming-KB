## Soil [[holmgren-soil-humus-carbon-storage-rebuilding|Carbon Stores]] Under Climate Change

Roots and their mycorrhizal fungal associates represent a critically important but frequently overlooked component of terrestrial carbon storage. As atmospheric CO₂ concentrations and temperatures continue to rise, understanding how below-ground carbon dynamics respond is essential for predicting ecosystem-level feedbacks to climate change.

## The Importance of Below-Ground Carbon

### Carbon Allocation in Forest Ecosystems

Below-ground biomass carbon accounts for a substantial proportion of total tree carbon:

- **Pinus menziesii** ([[douglas-fir]]): 16% of total biomass carbon below ground
- **[[pinus-elliottii]]** (slash pine): 64% of total biomass carbon below ground

Vogt (1991) compared biomass allocation across 24 forest examples, finding that in only 9 cases were both above- and below-ground standing stock carbon quantified, representing just 4 tree species. This highlights the significant gap in knowledge about below-ground carbon pools.

### Soil Respiration

Root activity drives the majority of soil respiration in forested ecosystems:

- Nearly **two-thirds** of soil respiration in a temperate mixed hardwood forest comes from root activity
- Root litter decomposition contributes **70–80%** of total soil respiration across a range of forested ecosystems
- Root respiration varies from approximately **35%** in tulip poplar (*Liriodendron tulipifera*) to **62%** in slash pine (*Pinus elliottii*)

### Root Turnover and Nutrient Cycling

In heathland ecosystems, root turnover dominates nutrient cycling:
- *Molinia* root turnover contributes 67% of total litter production, 87% of litter nitrogen loss, and 84% of total litter phosphorus loss (Aerts et al., 1992)
- *Calluna* and *Deschampsia* in the same ecosystem contributed 2–3× lower values

## Temperature Effects on Decomposition

### Predicted Carbon Losses from Warming

Bonan and Van Cleve (1992) modeled the consequences of a 5°C temperature rise on boreal forest decomposition:

| Forest Type | Soil Temp Increase (degree days) | Soil Respiration Loss (g C m⁻²) | Net Carbon Flux (g C m⁻²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper birch | 500 | 439 | +23 (net gain) |
| White spruce | 300 | Higher | −129 (net loss) |
| Black spruce | 300 | 675 | +46 (net gain) |

White spruce was the only ecosystem where decomposition losses exceeded carbon gains from increased photosynthesis. This demonstrates that warming effects on soil carbon are highly ecosystem-dependent.

## Mycorrhizal Bridges and Inter-Plant Carbon Transfer

Finlay and Read (1986a) demonstrated significant carbon transfer between interconnected plants via mycorrhizal bridges:

- Transfer is enhanced between **source plants** (optimizing photosynthesis) and **sink plants** (growing in shade)
- Simard et al. (1997a,b) confirmed this transfer occurs both within and between tree species in the field
- Finlay and Read (1986b,c) also showed potential interplant nutrient transfer through the same mycorrhizal networks

This challenges the traditional concept of each plant and species acting as a competitive individual, suggesting instead that root-mycorrhizal networks function as a resource-sharing system for entire plant communities, conserving both carbon and nutrients within forest systems.

## Ectomycorrhizal Carbon Storage Potential

### Long-Lived Mycelium as a Carbon Sink

Treseder and Allen (2000) argued that ectomycorrhizal mycelium may be particularly important for long-term carbon storage because:

- Long-lived ectomycorrhizal mycelium is relatively resistant to microbial attack
- It can be incorporated into the "slow pool" of soil carbon
- Fruit body production of mycorrhizal [[biodiversity-of-fungi-biomass-carbon-soil-structure|fungi]] constitutes a major part (~15%) of forest floor biomass

### Fine Root Carbon Residence Times

Fine roots in forest trees of the eastern US have:
- Mean age of 3–18 years (living roots)
- Dead root age of 10–18 years
- Carbon fixed 3–8 years earlier in organic soil horizons, 11–18 years earlier in mineral soil horizons (Gaudinski et al., 2001)

This positions root biomass and necromass carbon as a **midterm** carbon store in soils.

### Glomalin — A Major Soil Carbon Pool

[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] produce the glycoprotein **glomalin** (Wright and Upadhyaya, 1996), which has extraordinary significance for soil carbon:

- Treseder and Allen (2000) calculate that glomalin could account for **30–60%** of the carbon in undisturbed soils
- Rillig et al. (2000) showed increased glomalin content in grassland soils under long-term elevated CO₂ exposure
- Glomalin contributes to soil aggregate stability, physically protecting associated carbon from rapid decomposition
- This carbon is somewhat protected in soil aggregates and represents a potential **long-term carbon sink**

### Hyphal Biomass Estimates

Arbuscular mycorrhizal hyphal biomass varies substantially across ecosystems:

| Ecosystem Type | Hyphal Length | Dry Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Poplar | 0.02 m g⁻¹ soil | — |
| Shrub steppe | 38 m ml⁻¹ soil | — |
| Prairie | 111 m ml⁻¹ soil | ~457 mg ml⁻¹ (~500 kg ha⁻¹) |

## Plantation Forestry — A Complicated Carbon Story

### The Ecuador Radiata Pine Case Study

Chapela et al. (2001) provided a cautionary counterexample to the assumption that plantation forestry automatically creates carbon sinks:

- In Ecuadorian radiata pine plantations (no site preparation disturbance), there was a **net carbon loss** from soil exceeding photosynthetic carbon gain
- Stable isotope studies showed **30% loss** of pre-existing paramo grassland soil carbon during the first 20 years of forest rotation
- A large proportion of this lost carbon was incorporated into just **three dominant ectomycorrhizal species**: *Suillus luteus*, *Thelephora terrestris*, and *Rhizopogon vulgaris*
- This dramatically reduced diversity (3 species vs. ~100 in the tree's natural range) was confirmed by both fruit body surveys and molecular analysis of root tips
- Prolific fruiting (1,200 kg dry weight ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹), especially of *Suillus luteus*, was a major contributor to respiratory carbon loss, as these fungi utilize organic forms of nutrient and non-host carbon

### Implications

The concept of plantation forestry as a reliable carbon sink must be viewed skeptically when:
- Exotic tree species are planted outside their native range
- Coevolved mycorrhizal associations are absent
- Reduced ectomycorrhizal diversity alters carbon flow patterns
- Site preparation causes massive soil disturbance and respiratory carbon loss

## Land-Use Change and Carbon Stores

Howard et al. (1995) documented land-use changes in England and Wales from 1984–1990, predicting a net loss of approximately 151,000 × 10³ t C to the atmosphere by 2044 using conservative Markov modeling. Key findings:

- Conversion from ley (temporary grassland) and upland grass to arable land were major carbon loss drivers
- Bog and upland heath losses contributed significantly
- The effect of increased CO₂ concentrations raising atmospheric and soil temperatures would likely increase soil respiration, creating a **positive feedback mechanism** exacerbating global warming

## Soil Aggregates and Carbon Protection

### Aggregate Formation as Carbon Sink

Jastrow (1996) demonstrated that [[soil-aggregate-formation]] is a critical pathway for carbon sequestration:

- In prairie soil restoration, the rate constant for aggregate formation was **35×** that for carbon accumulation in other soil components
- Time to reach 99% of equilibrium: 10.5 years for aggregates vs. 384 years for whole soil
- The C:N ratio of carbon in aggregates indicates very recent origin — derived from bacterial and fungal biomass rather than highly processed organic matter
- A significant fraction of aggregate-stabilizing material consists of **fungal cell wall residues** that bind microaggregates into macroaggregates

### Carbon Partitioning in Restored Prairies

| Carbon Fraction | Under Corn | 4-Season Restoration | 10-Season Restoration | Virgin Prairie |

| Mineral-associated macroaggregate C | 1,181 | 2,548 | 3,348 | 4,692 |
