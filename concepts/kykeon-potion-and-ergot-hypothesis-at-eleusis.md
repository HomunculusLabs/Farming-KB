# The Kykeon Potion and Ergot Hypothesis at Eleusis

The central ritual of the [[mckenna-food-of-the-gods-kykeon-and-eleusinian-mysteries]] involved the drinking of a potion called the kykeon, after which initiates experienced a transformative vision in the great telesterion (initiation hall). Wasson, Hofmann, and Ruck proposed that the kykeon contained an hallucinogenic extract of ergot ([[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]]) growing on barley -- Demeter's sacred grain.

## The Kykeon Formula

The formula for the kykeon is recorded in the [[demeter-and-persephone-the-homeric-hymn-and-eleusinian-foundation-myth]] to Demeter. In addition to barley, it contained water and a fragrant mint called blechon. The mint was initially considered as a possible psychoactive candidate, but evidence indicates it was unsuitable:

- It was neither sufficiently psychotropic to warrant secrecy
- It was openly despised as a sign of illicit union
- Demeter had changed a prostitute of Hades into mint in anger

Barley, not mint, is the revelation at Eleusis -- and it is to barley that the secret drug must be traced.

## The Preparation Ritual

The preparation of the kykeon was the central event of the Mysteries, performed with elaborate pageantry:

1. The hierophant (priest tracing descent to the first Mystery) removed the sclerotia of ergot from a free-standing room constructed inside the telesterion, over remains of the original Mycenaean temple
2. He intoned ancient chants in a falsetto voice -- his role was asexual, a male who had sacrificed his gender to the Great Goddess
3. Grain was conveyed in chalices to priestesses, who danced throughout the hall balancing vessels and lamps upon their heads
4. The grain was mixed with mint and water in urns
5. The sacred potion was ladled into special cups for the initiates to drink

After drinking, initiates chanted that they had consumed the potion and handled the secret objects carried in sealed baskets. Seated on tiers of steps lining the cavernous hall, in darkness, they waited.

## The Vision

From the potion, the initiates gradually entered ecstasy. The hallucinogen, under carefully controlled [[lsd-set-and-setting-framework]], produced extraordinary effects:

- Ventroloquistic effects (disturbing the inner ear)
- Music coming from hither and yon -- whispers, distant voices, depth of the earth
- Perfumes released in succession
- Music contrived for a crescendo of expectation
- Suddenly the inner chamber was flung open
- Spirits of light entered -- subdued, not blinding
- Among them: the spirit of Persephone with her newborn son, returned from Hades

The hierophant raised his voice in ancient measures: "The Terrible Queen has given birth to her son, the Terrible One." This was accompanied by the roar of a gong-like instrument from the bowels of the earth.

## The Ergot Hypothesis

### Why Barley Ergot

Barley was Demeter's sacred grain, grown in the Rarian plain adjacent to Eleusis (which may explain the site choice). Barley was thought particularly susceptible to ergot infection. The arguments for ergot as the kykeon's active ingredient:

- **Symbiotic symbolism**: Ergot and barley growing together represented the union of death and rebirth
- **Purple color**: Ergot's distinctive purple-brown matched Demeter's robe
- **Chemical plausibility**: Water-soluble hallucinogenic alkaloids (ergonovine, lysergic acid amide) could be extracted with ancient technology
- **Hofmann's self-experiment**: In 1976, Hofmann demonstrated that ergonovine (2mg) produces psychotropic, mood-changing, slightly hallucinogenic effects

### Separation of Soluble and Insoluble Alkaloids

The critical insight is that hallucinogenic ergot alkaloids (lysergic acid amide, lysergic acid hydroxyethylamide, ergonovine) are water-soluble, while the toxic ergotamine and ergotoxine group alkaloids are not. Ancient Greek herbalists, like those of pre-Conquest Mexico, could have discovered methods to separate the psychoactive from the poisonous constituents through simple water extraction.

### Supporting Evidence from Antiquity

- German folklore names for ergot included "drunken rye" (seigle ivre) and "mad grain" (Tollkorn), indicating folk knowledge of psychotropic effects
- French name "ivraie" for darnel indicated belief in psychotropic activity (attested from 1236 AD, likely much older)
- Claviceps paspali, growing on Paspalum distichum around the Mediterranean, contains only hallucinogenic alkaloids and could have been used directly
- Lolium temulentum (darnel/tares), a weed infesting grain crops, is notoriously infected by Claviceps purpurea

## Physical Symptoms

Ancient accounts describe physical symptoms consistent with ergot alkaloid intoxication:
- Fear and trembling in the limbs
- Vertigo
- Nausea
- Cold sweat
- Followed by vision in an aura of brilliant light

These are the symptomatic reactions not to drama or ceremony, but to a mystical vision induced by an hallucinogen.

## The Scandal of Profanation

In the fifth century BC, a scandal erupted when aristocratic Athenians were discovered celebrating the Mystery at home with groups of drunken guests at dinner parties. This confirms that the kykeon contained an identifiable psychoactive substance -- people were replicating the experience outside the sanctioned ritual context.
