# BOD

Beta-Methoxy-2C-D; 4-Methyl-2,5,beta-trimethoxyphenethylamine

## Overview

BOD is the founding member of the BOX series of phenethylamines -- compounds where a beta-oxygen atom (benzylic oxygen) has been added to the phenethylamine backbone of known psychedelic drugs. The addition mimics the natural enzymatic conversion of dopamine to norepinephrine in the brain. BOD is the beta-methoxy derivative of [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]]'s BOX variant would be "BOMB"), but this was dropped. Other BOX members include:

- BOB (beta-methoxy-2C-B): The most potent BOX member, but with concerning neurological overtones
- BOH (beta-hydroxy-2C-D): The unmethylated version
- BOM (beta-methoxy-2C-M)
- BOED (beta-ethoxy-2C-D): Three times less potent than BOD (70-75 mg). Anorexic and diuretic. No visual effects or insights, just a highly intoxicated state with ~10 hour duration.

### Biochemical Rationale

The beta-oxygen addition mimics the natural enzymatic conversion of dopamine to norepinephrine by beta-hydroxylase. The same chemistry converts tyramine to octopamine (discovered in the salivary glands of Octopus vulgaris). By blocking the oxygen with a methyl group, the compound becomes more lipophilic and better able to cross the blood-brain barrier, potentially increasing psychedelic activity.

### Chirality

The added beta-methoxy group creates a new asymmetric center, allowing separation into two optical isomers. Future amphetamine homologues would have two chiral centers, requiring synthesis of two racemic compounds and study of four isomers total.

## Synthesis Summary

The : The parent compound without beta-oxygen. 2C-D is milder, more ambiguous, and shorter-acting. BOD adds visual richness and conversational enhancement at the cost of body load and longer duration.
