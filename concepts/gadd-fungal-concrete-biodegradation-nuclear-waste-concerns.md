## Mechanisms of Fungal Deterioration

### Biomechanical Weathering

Fungi degrade mineral substrates through biomechanical and biochemical weathering acting in synergy. Two synergistic actions — biomechanical and biochemical — drive the degradation of mineral substrates.

Biomechanical weathering can be direct or indirect:

- **Direct biomechanical degradation** occurs through extensive penetration by fungal hyphae into decayed rocks and tunnelling into otherwise intact mineral matter, progressing along crystal planes, cleavage, cracks, and grain boundaries in calcitic and dolomitic rocks, sandstone, and concrete.
- **[[fungal-bioweathering-and-mineral-transformations|acidolysis]], complexolysis, and redoxolysis, all of which may be enhanced by metal accumulation in and/or around the fungal biomass. The primary fungal impact on biogeochemical cycling appears to result from acidolysis and complexolysis.

#### Acidolysis

Acidolysis (proton-promoted dissolution) occurs when fungi acidify their microenvironment through:

- Excretion of protons
- Excretion of organic acids
- Formation of carbonic acid from respiratory CO₂

Many fungi are capable of excreting large amounts of organic acids, with [[staycare-fungal-heterotrophic-leaching-biosorption]]" — the fungal-mediated release of metal cations from mineral matrices through acidification. The process of respiration-driven CO₂ dissolution in water to form carbonic acid represents an additional, often overlooked, pathway by which even non-acid-excreting fungi may contribute to mineral dissolution.

#### Complexolysis

Fungi excrete metal-complexing metabolites associated with complexolysis (ligand-promoted dissolution), including:

- Carboxylic acids (especially oxalic and citric acid, which perform aggressive attack on mineral surfaces)
- Amino acids
- Siderophores
