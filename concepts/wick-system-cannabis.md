# Wick System for Cannabis

## The Dutch Flood Method

Dutch growers have developed a highly productive passive system that works on wick principles. They line the floor of a room with heavy plastic or pond liner, fill three-gallon pots with an absorbent soilless mix that holds plenty of air, and flood the garden floor with two to three inches (6 to 9 cm) of nutrient solution. The soilless mix in the pots wicks up the nutrient solution, which is absorbed by plant roots over two to five days. No nutrient solution is drained off; it is all absorbed by the plants.

This method allows a grower to flood the garden on Monday and return on Friday to find plants strong and healthy, making it practical for growers with limited daily access. It demonstrates how effective passive capillary action can be when the right growing medium is selected.

## Suitable Plants

Wick systems are best suited for:
- Smaller plants and [[deep-water-culture]] or [[jadam-salt-accumulation-in-soil]].
- Replace wicks if they become clogged, degraded, or discolored.
- Adjust the number of wicks based on plant size and water consumption.
- Clean the reservoir between grows to prevent algae and bacterial growth.
- Check that the wick material has not become compacted or blocked.
