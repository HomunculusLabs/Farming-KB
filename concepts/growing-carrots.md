# Growing Carrots

The carrot exemplifies the survival strategy of all biennial root crops: sneak a deep taproot through the opposition; then begin drawing on moisture and nutrition located deeper than its neighbors can reach. A supply of raw materials assured, it fills a storage chamber with surplus food, rests over the winter, and in spring starts with a huge nutritional storehouse that it uses to rapidly overtop its neighbors and shade them out, then begins making seeds.

## The Three Requirements for Good Carrots

Slow-growing, struggling plants make woody, bitter carrots. Three things contribute to rapid growth:

1. **Fertility** — but not too much, or you'll end up with more top than root
2. **Loose soil** — especially a top foot (30 cm) that graciously allows the root to push down as fast as it wishes
3. **Steady moisture supply** — the root can't struggle against too much competition

## Soil Preparation

### Light Soils

Commercial carrot crops are grown on light, sandy soils because the edible part is not quite so sensitive. If you have light soil, just use decent fertility.

### Heavy Soils

If you have hard soil, you must do the necessary work to make it friable and loose enough for the roots to push through easily. Otherwise the soil pushes back, making carrots tougher and irregularly shaped.

To [[query-how-do-i-improve-heavy-clay-soil-in-my-garden]] soil for carrots:
- Dig in a large quantity of [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] — perhaps as much as a 2-inch-thick (5 cm) layer of compost or well-rotted manure
- This is one crop where using very airy compost is important
- If the layer of soil the carrots grow in is too fertile, the roots may fork — carrots discover most of their nutrients in the subsoil

## Sowing Technique

Getting even spacing is crucial for success with carrots.

### The Sand Mixing Method

1. Thoroughly blend a heaping 1/2 teaspoon (2.5 ml) of strong carrot seed into a quart (liter) of fine sand or compost
2. Uniformly sprinkle this mixture into the bottom of 25 feet (8 m) of furrow
3. This results in evenly spaced seedlings standing about 1/2 inch (1.25 cm) apart

### The Rule

> First, try not to sow too much seed.

Overcrowding is the primary cause of small, misshapen carrots. The sand mixing method prevents this.

## Thinning Strategy

Thinning is success with carrots. The process:

1. **Immediately after germination**: Do a preliminary thinning if seedlings came up thicker than 1/2 inch apart
2. **First month**: As they grow, thin the row progressively, steadily reducing competition
3. **When tops are 3-4 inches (7-10 cm) tall**: By this time, there should be no more losses due to diseases
4. **Final thinning**: Thin the row to the desired final spacing

### Final Spacing

How far apart depends on the size of your variety. Some are the size of your fingers; others may be as much as 3 inches across at the top at maturity.

**Key insight**: Providing the crop with a steady supply of moisture has more to do with thinning than with watering. Proper thinning eliminates [[root-competition-plant-spacing-solomon]] and allows each plant to access its own moisture zone.

## Moisture Management

- Carrots need consistent moisture — not floods, not droughts
- The steady moisture supply has more to do with proper thinning (eliminating competition) than with irrigation frequency
- Once thinned properly, the deep taproot will find its own moisture
- Mulching helps maintain consistent soil moisture

## Harvest and Storage

### When to Harvest

Most varieties of carrot will continue enlarging without becoming woody or tasteless if they have room to grow. Pulling every second carrot in the row helps the patch remain in better eating condition.

### Storage

- In mild climates, carrots may overwinter in the garden with crowns under a few inches of soil
- For longest storage: dig, remove tops, store in a barrel of moist sand in the root cellar
- Ideal storage temperature: 32-35°F (0-2°C)
- Carrots store well for 4-6 months under proper conditions

### Because This Crop Is So Well-Suited to Being a Reliable Staple

The combination of good storage, reliable yield, and nutritional value makes carrots one of the best [[grain-and-staple-crops]] for the home garden. They can be eaten raw, cooked, juiced, or fermented.

## Pests and Diseases

- Carrot rust fly is the main pest — rotate crops and avoid planting near where carrots grew the previous year
- Proper thinning reduces disease by improving [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]
- Healthy, rapidly growing plants are more resistant

## Saving Seed

Carrots are biennial and insect-pollinated (mainly by flies and small bees):
- Need isolation of at least 1/4 mile (400 m) from other carrot varieties or wild *[[daucus-carota]]* (Queen Anne's lace)
- Overwinter roots in the root cellar (like beets), replant in spring
- Each plant makes a large quantity of seed
- Include at least 20 plants in the gene pool to maintain vigor
- Seed is relatively short-lived compared to some crops — plan to refresh every 3-4 years
