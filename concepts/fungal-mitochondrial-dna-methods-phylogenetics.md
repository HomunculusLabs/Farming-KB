# Fungal Mitochondrial DNA — Methods and Phylogenetics

Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is an excellent system for studying [[fungal-speciation-evolution]]ary genetics, biodiversity, and taxonomy. Its small size, ease of purification, high copy number, and simple restriction patterns make it particularly valuable for phylogenetic analysis.

## Advantages of mtDNA for Fungal Studies

- **Small genome size:** 20-80 kb (vs. 15-820 million bp for nuclear DNA)
- **Lack of methylated bases:** Easier purification
- **High copy number:** Easier to obtain sufficient DNA
- **Simple restriction patterns:** Easier to analyze
- **Haploid nature:** No recombination; preserves allelic forms for phylogenetics
- **Maternal inheritance:** Tracks maternal lineages
- **Presence of RFLPs:** Useful genetic markers
- **Available gene sequences:** For PCR amplification

## Mitochondrial Gene Content

Despite variation in mtDNA size (10-fold range) and shape, similar functional gene sets are found in most fungal mitochondria:
- **Replication, transcription, translation genes**
- **rRNA genes:** Large (lrRNA) and small (srRNA) ribosomal RNA
- **tRNA genes:** 22-26 different tRNAs (sufficient to translate entire mitochondrial genetic code)
- **Protein-coding genes:** Cytochrome B, ATPases 6 and 9, COX I, COX II, NADH dehydrogenase subunits
- **Group I and II introns:** Common; Podospora anserina has 31 group I + 2 group II introns

## Mitochondrial Codon Usage

Fungal mitochondria deviate from the universal genetic code:

| Codon | Universal | Ascomycetes | Non-Ascomycetes |
|-------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| UGA | Stop | Trp | Absent |
| AUA | Ile | Met or Ile | Met |
| CUN | Leu | Thr or Leu | Leu |

Ascomycete mitochondria use expanded codon recognition where a single tRNA type recognizes all four codons in a family (CUN=Leu, GUN=Val, UCN=Ser, etc.). tRNA anticodon loop structure (reduced number of base pairs) enables this flexibility.

## Genome Mapping Methods

### Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism (RFLP)
- Clone mtDNA fragments; analyze with restriction enzymes
- RFLP patterns serve as taxonomic characters
- Correlated with pathogenicity or mating type in some species
- Within-species heterogeneity may or may not correlate with geography
- Limited resolution: all sites for one enzyme treated as single character

### DNA Sequencing
- Provides greater resolution than RFLPs
- Distinguishes conformational mutations from genome length changes
- High-resolution restriction mapping (HRRM): PCR amplify variable regions, sequence
- Allele-specific PCR for known mutations
- First complete mtDNA sequence: Podospora anserina (94,192 bp; Cummings et al. 1990)

### DNA Extraction from Fungi

**Standard method:**
1. Harvest cultures by centrifugation (3000 x g, 15 min)
2. Wash with distilled water
3. Freeze-dry or dry at 65C overnight
4. Weigh; extract DNA

**For polysaccharide-rich fungi (CTAB/SDS method):**
- Hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) + sodium dodecyl sulfate
- Removes polysaccharides that interfere with restriction enzymes and PCR

**Protoplast method:**
1. Harvest cultures, wash with dithiothreitol in Tris-HCl (pH 7.5)
2. Suspend in 0.6 M ammonium sulfate, 0.02 M potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0)
3. Add Trichoderma viride cellulase + Penicillium funiculosum cellulase
4. Optional: Aspergillus niger hemicellulase for improved protoplast formation
5. Incubate 37C, 2 hours
6. Add Proteinase K, incubate 55C overnight
7. Extract with phenol/chloroform; precipitate DNA

**For slow-growing basidiomycetes:**
- Airlift culture system decreases moribund mycelium and removes polysaccharides
- Yields 150-320 mg DNA per gram mycelium; 83-97% purity

## mtDNA Purification

From purified mitochondria (obtained from protoplasts by differential or gradient centrifugation):
1. Lysed protoplasts; remove debris at 1,500 x g
2. Pellet mitochondria at 15,000 x g, 20 minutes
3. Resuspend in 0.9 M sorbitol + Tris + EDTA
4. Centrifuge at 20,000 x g for purity
5. Add MgCl2 + pancreatic DNase (to degrade nuclear DNA); incubate on ice 2 hours
6. Inactivate DNase with SDS; extract DNA

## Phylogenetic Applications

mtDNA has been used to:
- Assign Beauveria species to mitotypes
- Test mitochondrial compatibility
- Determine natural population structures
- Study organelle transmission in asexual fungi
- Monitor cytoplasmic inheritance in interspecific crosses
- Compare gene order among fungi (highly variable between genera)
- Study intron evolution and endonuclease-mediated intron transposition

## Gene Order Variation
Relative positions and orientations of mitochondrial genes are highly variable among fungi. Comparisons of Neurospora, Aspergillus, Podospora, Beauveria, and yeast show dramatically different gene arrangements, reflecting ancient divergence.
