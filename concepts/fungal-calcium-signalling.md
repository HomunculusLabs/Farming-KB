# Fungal Calcium Signalling

Calcium (Ca2+) is a critical second messenger in filamentous fungi, regulating
numerous cellular processes including [[gadd-hyphal-tip-growth-and-branching-mechanisms]] growth and
branching, circadian clocks, and responses to environmental stimuli. The
fungal calcium signalling toolkit is substantially more complex than that of
budding yeast, reflecting the greater [[fungal-environmental-sensing-signal-transduction]] demands of
filamentous growth.

## The Calcium Signalling Toolkit

Calcium signalling in fungi operates through transient increases in cytosolic
free Ca2+ ([Ca2+]c), which are often localized in specific subcellular regions.
These increases provide intracellular signals that directly or indirectly
regulate numerous downstream proteins. Different combinations of Ca2+ channels,
pumps, transporters, and other Ca2+-signalling proteins produce [Ca2+]c signals
with different dynamic, spatial, and temporal characteristics.

In Neurospora crassa, the genome encodes:

- Three Ca2+ channel proteins
- Nine Ca2+-ATPases
- Eight Ca2+-exchangers

This 'tool kit' of Ca2+-signalling proteins is substantially greater than that
found in Saccharomyces cerevisiae, reflecting the more complex environmental
sensing requirements of filamentous fungi.

## Downstream Calcium-Regulated Proteins

Downstream proteins regulated by Ca2+ include:

- **Calcineurin**: a Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent serine/threonine protein
  phosphatase involved in stress responses, morphogenesis, and virulence.
- **Calmodulin**: a small Ca2+-binding protein that relays calcium signals
  to various target enzymes.
- **Numerous Ca2+/calmodulin-regulated proteins**: identified in the genome
  of N. crassa through bioinformatic analyses.

## Unique Features of Fungal Calcium Signalling

Fungal calcium signalling differs from animal and plant systems in several
important ways:

- Key proteins important in generating increased [Ca2+]c by release of Ca2+
  from internal stores in animals and plants are not present in fungi. This
  suggests that fungi rely primarily on external calcium sources and plasma
  membrane channels rather than internal stores.
- Neurospora possesses both Ca2+/H+ and Ca2+/Na+ antiporters, whereas plants
  only possess the former and animals only the latter. This dual antiporter
  system may provide fungi with greater flexibility in calcium homeostasis
  across diverse environmental conditions.
