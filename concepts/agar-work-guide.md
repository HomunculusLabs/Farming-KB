# Agar Work Guide

Agar is the foundation of advanced mushroom cultivation. Working with agar allows you to isolate clean genetics from spores, rescue contaminated cultures, store long-term, and propagate without losing vigor.

Related: [[mushroom-sterile-technique]], [[mushroom-spore-printing-and-storage]]

## Agar Media Recipes

### Malt Extract Agar (MEA) — Standard
- 20g malt extract powder
- 15-20g agar powder
- 1L water
- (Optional) 1g nutritional yeast for improved growth

### Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA) — Classic
- 300g sliced potatoes, boiled in 1L water for 30 min, strained
- 10g dextrose (or substitute with 20g malt extract)
- 15-20g agar per liter of potato water

### Dog Food Agar (DFA) — Nutrient-Rich
- 1 can plain dog food (no gravy, no artificial colors)
- Blend with 1L water, strain through cloth
- 20g agar per liter of extract
- Excellent for recovering weak or old spores

### Grain Water Agar — Zero-Waste
- Water from boiling/soaking grain spawn (1:2 grain:water ratio)
- 15-20g agar per liter of grain water
- Already contains nutrients leached from grain

### Light Malt Extract Agar — Clean Growth
- 10g light malt extract (reduced from standard 20g)
- 15-20g agar per liter
- Less nutrients = slower but cleaner growth, easier to spot contamination

## Pouring Plates

1. Mix ingredients in a flask or jar. Heat to dissolve agar completely.
2. Sterilize at 15 psi for 30-45 minutes in a pressure cooker.
3. Allow to cool to ~120F (warm to touch but not hot enough to cause heavy condensation).
4. Pour inside a still air box or in front of a laminar flow hood.
5. Fill plates 1/3 full (~20ml per 90mm plate).
6. Crack lids slightly during cooling to reduce condensation, then close.
7. Store inverted at room temperature. Use within 2-4 weeks.

## Inoculation Techniques

### Spore Syringe to Agar
1. Flame-sterilize the needle until red hot, cool on agar surface.
2. Place a single drop of spore solution at the center of the plate or streak across the surface.
3. Seal with parafilm and incubate.

### Agar Wedge Transfer
1. Flame-sterilize scalpel, cool on agar or in air.
2. Cut a small wedge (3-5mm) from the leading edge of clean mycelium growth.
3. Transfer wedge face-down to fresh plate.
4. Seal and incubate at species-appropriate temperature.

### Tissue Culture
1. Select a healthy fruiting body from inside (less contamination risk than exterior).
2. Tear the mushroom open with sterile hands — do not cut through the outer skin.
3. Flame scalpel, take a tiny piece of inner tissue.
4. Place on agar. Mycelium should grow from the tissue in 3-7 days.

## Incubation

- Most species: 75-80F (24-27C) in complete darkness
- Warm-loving species (cubensis): 80-85F
- Cool-loving species (azurescens): 65-72F
- Wrap plates in parafilm to prevent drying

## Sectoring and Isolation

When mycelium grows from spores, it produces sectors — wedge-shaped zones showing different genetics. To isolate a single strain:

1. Look for the fastest, most rhizomorphic (ropy, strand-like) growth sector
2. Transfer a small wedge from the leading edge of that sector to a fresh plate
3. Repeat 3-5 times until growth is uniform and sector-free
4. This isolated strain is a monoculture — genetically consistent

Rhizomorphic growth generally indicates stronger fruiting potential than cottony (tomentose) growth.

## Long-Term Storage

### Refrigerator Storage
- Seal plates with parafilm, store at 35-38F. Slows growth dramatically.
- Transfer to fresh plates every 6-12 months.

### Slants
- Fill test tubes 1/3 full with agar, sterilize, cool at an angle to create a slanted surface.
- Inoculate, seal with cap or parafilm.
- Store at room temp for 6-12 months, refrigerated for 1-2 years.

### Mineral Oil Overlay
- After mycelium colonizes a slant, pour sterile mineral oil over the surface.
- Oil excludes oxygen, putting mycelium in stasis.
- Storage: 5-20 years. Rehydrate on fresh agar to revive.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| No growth at all | Dead spores, agar too hot, incorrect temp | Fresh spores; cool agar to 120F before pouring |
| Slow growth | Low nutrition, cold temp, old genetics | Use MEA; check temp; fresh source material |
| Bacterial contamination (slime, sour smell) | Poor sterile technique, dirty tools | Improve technique; work in SAB or flow hood |
| Mold contamination (green, black, fuzzy) | Airborne spores landing on plate | Better filtration; slower movements; parafilm |
| Mycelium stops growing at edge | Depleted nutrition, contamination | Transfer to fresh plate |
| Excess condensation on plates | Poured too hot, rapid temp change | Cool agar longer; store plates warm-side-up initially |
