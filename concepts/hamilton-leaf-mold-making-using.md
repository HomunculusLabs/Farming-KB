# Leaf Mold — Making and Using It

Leaf mold is one of the finest soil conditioners available to the organic
gardener, yet it requires nothing more than patience and autumn leaves.
Unlike conventional [[query-can-fungi-break-down-plastic-pollution]] more
quickly. When the leaves have fully decomposed into a dark, crumbly
material with no recognisable leaf shapes remaining, the leaf mold is
ready.

It is important to note that leaves collected from busy roads or
pavements may be polluted with vehicle exhaust deposits. If there is
concern about contamination, stacking the leaves for at least a year
helps ensure that any chemical traces have leached out or broken down
to an acceptable level.

## Using Leaf Mold in the Garden

Leaf mold can be used in several ways throughout the garden. Its primary
value lies as a potting and seed-sowing compost, either on its own or
mixed with other ingredients such as loam and sand. The fine, crumbly
texture is ideal for germinating seeds and growing on young plants.

As a soil conditioner, leaf mold can be dug into beds to improve
structure. It is particularly beneficial for sandy soils, where it helps
retain moisture, and for clay soils, where it opens up the structure
and improves drainage. A layer of leaf mold worked into the top few
inches of soil will noticeably improve its workability and fertility
over time.

Leaf mold also serves as an excellent [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] without the relatively high nutrient load
of fully mature garden compost.
