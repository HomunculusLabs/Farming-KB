## Agaricus bitorquis (Rodman"s Agaricus) Cultivation

## Overview and Taxonomy

Agaricus bitorquis is a warm-weather species also known as Rodman's Agaricus.
The species epithet bitorquis means "having two rings," referring to the double
annulus that distinguishes this species from Agaricus campestris. Synonyms
include Agaricus rodmanii Peck and Agaricus edulis.

## Commercial Strains

The first commercial strain was Horst B30, developed by Gerda Fritsche at the
Dutch Mushroom Research Center. Second-generation strains Horst K26 and K32
fruit earlier, give higher yields, and have slightly longer stems.

## General Description

The cap is smooth, white, and thick fleshed, convex to broadly convex, becoming
plane with age. The cap margin is incurved at first but soon decurves. The gills
are pinkish at first, soon darkening to chocolate brown with spore maturity.
The stem is thick, relatively short, and adorned with a double membranous
annulus (the lower ring is often a thin annular zone). Spores are dark
chocolate brown in mass.

## Natural Habitat

Naturally found in lawns, gardens, roadside areas, pastures, in enriched
grounds, and on hard-packed soil. A temperate species widely distributed, A.
bitorquis fruits primarily in the spring and to a lesser degree in the fall.
