# Agaricus bitorquis (Rodman's Agaricus) Cultivation

## Overview and Taxonomy

Agaricus bitorquis is a warm-weather species also known as Rodman's Agaricus.
The species epithet bitorquis means "having two rings," referring to the double
annulus that distinguishes this species from Agaricus campestris. Synonyms
include Agaricus rodmanii Peck and Agaricus edulis.

## Commercial Strains

The first commercial strain was Horst B30, developed by Gerda Fritsche at the
Dutch Mushroom Research Center. Second-generation strains Horst K26 and K32
fruit earlier, give higher yields, and have slightly longer stems.

## General Description

The cap is smooth, white, and thick fleshed, convex to broadly convex, becoming
plane with age. The cap margin is incurved at first but soon decurves. The gills
are pinkish at first, soon darkening to chocolate brown with spore maturity.
The stem is thick, relatively short, and adorned with a double membranous
annulus (the lower ring is often a thin annular zone). Spores are dark
chocolate brown in mass.

## Natural Habitat

Naturally found in lawns, gardens, roadside areas, pastures, in enriched
grounds, and on hard-packed soil. A temperate species widely distributed, A.
bitorquis fruits primarily in the spring and to a lesser degree in the fall.

## Spawn Preparation

Rye grain buffered with calcium carbonate and/or calcium sulfate serves as the
standard spawn medium. See [[stamets-spawn-making-and-grain-culture]].

## Fruiting Substrate

Nitrogen-enriched wheat straw and/or horse manure-based compost balanced to
71 to 74% moisture content is the standard substrate. Compost preparation
follows the methods described in [[stamets-compost-preparation]].

## Pasteurization Method

Pasteurization is achieved through exposure to live steam for 2 hours at
140 degrees Fahrenheit throughout the substrate. Compost should be filled to a
depth of 6 to 12 inches. For more on pasteurization vs. sterilization, see
[[stamets-cultivator-pasteurization-vs-sterilization-methods]].

## Spawn Run Parameters

- Relative Humidity: 90 to 100%
- Substrate Temperature: 84 to 86 degrees Fahrenheit
- Thermal Death Limit: 93 degrees Fahrenheit over prolonged exposure
- Duration: 2 weeks (14 days)
- CO2: 5,000 to 10,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 0 per hour

## Casing Layer

After full colonization, cover with the standard casing layer to a depth of
1 to 2 inches. The casing should be balanced to a pH of 7.2 to 7.5. See
[[stamets-casing-layer-design]] for casing preparation details.

## Post-Casing and Pre-Pinning

- Relative Humidity: 90 to 100%
- Bed Temperature: 84 to 86 degrees Fahrenheit
- Case Run Duration: 10 to 12 days
- CO2: 5,000 to 10,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 0 per hour

## Primordia Formation

- Relative Humidity: 95 to 100%
- Bed Temperature: 77 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit
- Air Temperature: 75 to 77 degrees Fahrenheit
- Lighting: None required
- CO2: less than 2,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 2 to 4 per hour
- Watering: Regular misting (once to twice daily) stimulates primordia
- See [[stamets-pinning-initiation-stages-environmental-control]] for details

## Cropping Cycle

- Relative Humidity: 85 to 92%
- Air Temperature: 75 to 77 degrees Fahrenheit
- CO2: less than 3,000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 2 to 4 per hour
- Flushing Interval: Every 8 to 9 days
- Harvest Stage: Directly before the partial veil stretches

## Yield Potential

Average commercial yields are reported at 3 pounds per square foot over a 5
week cropping period. Maximum yields are 4 pounds per square foot. Moisture
content is 92% water with 8% dry matter. Nutritional content is similar to
Agaricus brunnescens.

## Advantages Over Agaricus brunnescens

The development of Agaricus bitorquis has given commercial growers greater
flexibility, especially those in warmer climates where elevated temperatures
have been a limiting factor. Key advantages include resistance to virus (a
devastating disease that attacks A. brunnescens) and tolerance of high CO2
levels. The mushroom is also coarser, firmer, more strongly flavored, and has
a longer shelf life than its close relative.

## Disadvantages

A disadvantage is the higher incidence of disease endemic to the temperature
range in which this species flourishes. Warm conditions favor many competitor
organisms and pathogens. See [[stamets-mushroom-disease-pathogens-contaminants]]
for disease management information.
## Warm-Weather Cultivation Strategy

Agaricus bitorquis represents an important option for cultivators in warm
climates who cannot maintain the lower temperatures required by A. brunnescens.
The higher temperature tolerance throughout all growth stages makes it feasible
to cultivate during seasons when other Agaricus species would fail. See
[[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] for environmental
control approaches suited to warm-weather growing.

## Genetic Characteristics

Basidia are tetrapolar (4-spored), forming haploid spores. The species is
heterothallic. Compatible monokaryon mating can result in fruiting strains.
Clamp connections are absent, typical for the genus Agaricus. See
[[stamets-mushroom-genetics-mating-systems-reproduction]].
