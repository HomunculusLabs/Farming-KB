# Seed Establishment and Plant Recruitment

The establishment of new plants from seed is one of the most critical
yet overlooked processes in rangeland management. Savory's observations
reveal that seed production is rarely the limiting factor -- the
conditions for germination and establishment are the weak link. This
understanding is essential to advancing plant succession and  community dynamics and reversing [[desertification]] causes.

## The Weak Link: Germination to Establishment

The weak link in plant recruitment lies between germination and
establishment. Because of exponential growth from a single established
individual, the number of seeds or their distribution matters little
compared to establishment conditions. A single established plant
produces millions of seeds over its lifetime, but millions of seeds
that fail to establish produce nothing. Reseeding programs that address
seed supply rather than establishment conditions typically fail.

## What Seeds Need to Establish

For successful seedling establishment, several conditions must coincide:

1. **Adequate moisture** from an effective water cycle.
2. **Appropriate temperature** buffered by surface conditions.
3. **Soil contact** -- seeds on hard-capped surfaces may germinate but
   seedlings cannot root effectively.
4. **Soil porosity** -- compacted or crusted surfaces prevent taproot
   penetration.
5. **Protection from desiccation** -- mulch and shade from existing
   plants give seedlings time to develop roots.
6. **Absence of severe competition** from established plants.

## Failure Under Rest

One of Savory's most striking observations is the near-complete absence
of seedlings on rested land in brittle environments, despite massive
seed production. At the Sevilleta Wildlife Refuge in New Mexico, years
of total rest produced grassland with wide bare spaces between dying
perennials. Despite abundant seed production, no seedlings established
on the bare, undisturbed surface. Research stations in Zimbabwe and
South Africa showed the same pattern: "the most shocking characteristic
was the almost complete absence of new seedlings despite massive seed
production."

## Soil Disturbance as a Prerequisite

In brittle environments, soil disturbance is typically required for
seed establishment. Evidence is clear and consistent:

- Across a fence from undisturbed, seedling-free ground at Sevilleta,
  where cattle had broken and firmed the surface, thousands of new
  perennial grass seedlings had sprouted.
- In northern Mexico, young winterthorn trees established where
  elephants had broken the hard soil cap.
- In New Mexico, trampling by cattle eliminated cracks in puffy soil
  and allowed perennial grass seedlings to establish between old
  clumps.

Disturbance forms include physical trampling by herbivores, weathering
that creates cracks, biological activity (earthworms, root channels),
and mechanical disturbance from machinery.

## Compaction vs. Puffiness

Two extremes are problematic for seed establishment:

### Overly Compacted Soil
Rain impact creates a hard cap. Seeds germinate but seedlings cannot
penetrate. Water cannot infiltrate. Characteristic of overgrazed areas
with bare ground.

### Overly Puffy Soil
Outside the tropics, prolonged rest allows freezing and thawing to
create soft, airy soil. Seeds germinate but only tap-rooted forbs
(weeds) establish, as the soil is too loose for fibrous-rooted grass
seedlings. Characteristic of rested areas with dead root systems.

The ideal is firm but not capped -- soil compacted enough for root
contact but penetrable by seedlings. This is created by moderate
animal impact and herd effect from properly planned grazing.

## Forbs vs. Grasses: Different Soil Preferences

Different surface conditions favor different plant types:

- **Puffy soil with dead root channels** favors tap-rooted forbs. The
  seedling pushes its taproot down channels left by dead grass roots.
- **Firm, broken soil** favors fibrous-rooted grasses. Seeds make
  good soil contact and the firm surface supports shallow-rooted
  seedlings.

This explains why rested brittle environments shift from grassland to
forb dominance: dying grass roots create ideal conditions for tap-
rooted species while preventing grass seedling establishment.

## The Role of Old Plant Material

Old material plays complex roles: scattered mulch conserves moisture
and moderates temperature; material lying uniformly in one direction
suppresses growth; excessive accumulation shades growing points and
kills grass plants; dead root systems create channels favoring tap-
rooted establishment.

## Animal Impact for Establishment

animal impact and herd effect is the most practical tool for
creating favorable conditions: high stock density for short periods
breaks soil capping without erosion; trampling incorporates litter;
dung adds nutrients; grazing removes old growth shading growing points.
