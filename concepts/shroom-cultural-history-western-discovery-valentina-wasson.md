## Robert Gordon Wasson (1898-1986)

Robert Gordon Wasson was born on 22 September 1898, the son of an Episcopalian minister. After serving in the First World War, he graduated from Harvard with a bachelor"s in literature and began his career as a journalist, eventually crossing into banking at J. P. ## The Quest for the Living Cult

What drove Wasson through ten gruelling expeditions to Mexico (1953-1970s) was the conviction that the ancient mushroom cult had "not died out entirely." Hidden in the remote Sierra Mazateca, it might have "clung on in some half-remembered form, of which the indigenous mushroom ceremonies were, in fact, the very last vestige."

## The Mexican Expeditions

### First Trip (1953)

Wasson received two letters in 1952 alerting him to the Mesoamerican "Mushroom Stones" and the fact that teonanacatl had been a mushroom. He wrote to Blas Pablo Reko, who forwarded his letter to Eunice Pike, a missionary in Huautla. She confirmed mushrooms were used in curing rituals. In late 1953, accompanied by his daughter Masha and guided by Robert Weitlaner, Wasson made his first trip to Mexico. They discovered their local guide, Aurelio Carreras, was himself a curandero, and witnessed their first velada.

### The Historic Session (1955)

On his third trip, with photographer Allan Richardson, Wasson returned to Huautla and met María Sabina. She agreed to let both men eat the sacred mushrooms — making them "the first Westerners ever intentionally to do so." Wasson's second ceremony, on 2 July 1955, produced his celebrated visionary account.

## The Scientific Team

Wasson enlisted two notable scientists:

- **Roger Heim** (1900-1979), Professor of Mycology at the Muséum National d'Histoire Naturelle in Paris, identified and described the mushrooms (primarily Psilocybe, Conocybe, and Stropharia species)
- **Albert Hofmann** (1906-), the Swiss chemist who discovered LSD, isolated the psychoactive alkaloids: in 1958, while working for Sandoz, he successfully synthesised **psilocybin and psilocin**, named after the mushrooms' Latin epithet
