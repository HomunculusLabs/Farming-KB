## Claviceps purpurea: Ergot Alkaloids and Phytochemistry

## The Fungus and Its Biology

*[[claviceps-purpurea]]* (Fr.) Tulasne is a parasitic fungus that infects
the inflorescence of rye (*[[secale-cereale]]*) and other grasses. While
Ergot infects a number of different grasses, it is best known as a
parasite on rye. The hard, brown or purplish black sclerotium
originating in the caryopsis of rye is exceedingly common in Europe.
When grain is infected by Ergot, long black growths appear on the
heads, called sclerotium. The specific name *purpurea* means "purple"
-- a color that in antiquity was linked with powers of the underworld.

The native nomenclature of *Claviceps purpurea* is remarkably complex.
Ergot, the French word for "spur" of a cock, is now generally
employed in numerous languages. There are two dozen other words for
the sclerotium in French; sixty-two vernacular names in German
(*Mutterkorn* being the most common); twenty-one in Dutch; fifteen
in the Scandinavian languages; fourteen in Italian; and seven in
English. This proliferation of vernacular terminology indicates the
great importance of the fungus in European countries.
