## Overview
Month-by-month garden tasks for temperate climates (USDA zones 5-7). Covers planting, harvesting, and maintenance to keep the garden productive year-round.
## January-February: Planning and Indoor Starts
### January
- Review seed catalogs and place orders; plan garden layout
- Test soil pH if not done in fall; inventory and clean tools
- Order bare-root fruit trees, berry bushes, and asparagus crowns
- Check stored crops (squash, root vegetables) for spoilage
- Start onion and leek seeds indoors (10-12 weeks before last frost)
- Build or repair structures, trellises, raised beds, cold frames
### February
- Start peppers and eggplant indoors (8-10 weeks before last frost)
- Start tomatoes indoors (6-8 weeks)
- Start broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts indoors (6-8 weeks); see [[brassica-growing-guide]]
- Start lettuce, kale, and cool-season greens indoors
- Prune dormant fruit trees and grapes; see [[apple-tree-care]] and [[grape-growing-guide]]
- Apply dormant oil spray and lime-sulfur to fruit trees
- Turn compost piles; spread compost on garden beds
## March: Early Spring Planting
### Indoor Seed Starting
- Start herbs indoors: basil, parsley, cilantro, oregano; see [[herb-growing-guide]]
- Start melons and squash indoors (4-6 weeks before last frost)
- Start cucumber seeds indoors for early transplanting
### Outdoor Planting
- Direct sow peas, spinach, radishes, and turnips as soon as soil is workable
- Plant bare-root asparagus, rhubarb, fruit trees, berry bushes, grape vines
- Transplant hardened-off broccoli, cabbage, and kale
- Plant seed potatoes (2-3 weeks before last frost)
- Direct sow lettuce and greens under row cover
- Set up trellises, stakes, cages, and irrigation systems
## April: Main Spring Planting
### Direct Sowing
- Direct sow carrots (soil 50F+); see [[carrot-growing-guide]]
- Direct sow beets, Swiss chard, radishes, bush beans (soil 60F+), corn (soil 55F+)
### Transplanting
- Transplant tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, cucumbers, summer squash after hardening off
- Transplant basil and tender herbs
- See [[cucumber-growing-guide]] for trellis setup
### Orchard and Maintenance
- Finish pruning stone fruits before bloom; see [[stone-fruit-tree-care]]
- Remove strawberry mulch as growth resumes; apply fungicide sprays
- Thin seedlings, begin weeding, apply mulch, install row covers on brassicas
- Set up slug traps
## May: Warm Season Begins
### Planting
- Direct sow pole beans with trellises; see [[bean-growing-guide]]
- Succession plant lettuce, beans, radishes; plant sweet potato slips (60F+ soil)
- Transplant melons and squash; plant heat-loving herbs
### Orchard
- Monitor for codling moth; apply petal fall sprays
- Thin apple and peach fruit to 6-8 inches apart; see [[apple-tree-care]]
- Install bird netting on strawberries
### Maintenance
- Begin consistent watering and fertilizing; side-dress heavy feeders
- Train vines onto trellises; scout for pests daily
- Begin harvesting asparagus (stop by mid-June)
## June: Peak Planting and First Harvests
### Planting and Harvesting
- Succession plant beans, cucumbers, summer squash, corn
- Sow fall brassicas for August-September transplant
- Harvest lettuce, spinach, radishes, peas, strawberries, early potatoes, garlic scapes
- Harvest garlic when lower leaves brown (late June)
### Maintenance and Orchard
- Prune tomato suckers; train cucumbers and beans onto trellises
- Mulch heavily; monitor for squash vine borer; apply kaolin clay for cucumber beetles
- Summer prune fruit trees; net blueberries and cherries; thin overloaded trees
- Monitor for brown rot on stone fruits
## July: Peak Summer
### Planting and Harvesting
- Succession plant beans, carrots, beets for fall; start fall brassica transplants indoors
- Sow cover crops in harvested areas; plant fall lettuce under shade cloth
- Peak harvest: tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, summer squash, beans, garlic, onions, blueberries
- Harvest herbs for drying; see [[herb-growing-guide]]
### Maintenance
- Water deeply and consistently; harvest frequently to maintain production
- Watch for squash vine borer and tomato blight; remove affected material
- Side-dress tomatoes and peppers; remove spent plants, replant with fall crops
## August: Transition to Fall
### Planting and Harvesting
- Transplant fall brassicas; direct sow carrots, beets, radishes, turnips, spinach
- Sow cover crops (crimson clover, winter rye, oats); plant garlic
- Harvest melons, peppers, plums, potatoes, herbs
### Maintenance
- Reduce water on potatoes; prepare cold frames for season extension
- Renovate strawberry beds; see [[strawberry-growing-guide]]
- Continue pest and disease monitoring; remove diseased material
## September: Fall Harvest and Planting
### Planting and Harvesting
- Direct sow spinach, lettuce, radishes, Asian greens for fall/winter
- Plant garlic, spring-flowering bulbs, perennial divisions; sow cover crops on all vacant beds
- Harvest winter squash (before frost), sweet potatoes, late apples, pears, fall brassicas, drying beans
### Maintenance
- Clean up spent annuals; apply fall fertilizer to perennials
- Mulch fall plantings heavily; install low tunnels or cold frames
- Take soil samples; cure winter squash at 75-85F for 10-14 days
## October: Final Harvest and Bed Prep
### Planting and Harvesting
- Plant garlic and shallots (last chance); spring bulbs; bare-root trees (zones 6-7)
- Direct sow spinach and mache for overwintering; sow cover crops on bare soil
- Harvest final tomatoes, Brussels sprouts (after frost), kale, parsnips, carrots, late apples
### Maintenance
- Complete garden cleanup; destroy diseased material
- Cut back perennial herbs; mulch all beds and fruit trees heavily (4-6 inches)
- Drain irrigation systems; clean and store tools; apply compost top-dress
- See [[carrot-growing-guide]] for winter carrot storage
## November-December: Rest and Plan
### November
- Harvest remaining root crops before hard freeze; store in cool, humid conditions
- Complete final cleanup; mulch perennial beds and fruit trees
- Review garden journal; plan crop rotation; order seed catalogs
### December
- Minimal outdoor activity; plan next year's garden
- Maintain indoor herb garden; check stored crops for spoilage
- Read, attend workshops, enjoy preserved harvest
