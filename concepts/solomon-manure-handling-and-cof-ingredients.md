## Ingredient Details and Sourcing

### Seed Meal (4 parts)

Seed meal is the primary nitrogen source in COF. It is the byproduct remaining after oil extraction from seeds. Common types include:

- **Cottonseed meal**: Widely available, moderate N (~6-7%), slightly acidic. Solomon's default recommendation.
- **Soybean meal**: Higher N (~7%), more neutral pH. Increasingly easy to find at feed stores.
- **Canola/rapeseed meal**: Good N (~6%), often the most economical option in some regions.
- **Sunflower seed meal**: Moderate N (~5%), worth using if locally available and competitively priced.

**Sourcing**: Farm and ranch supply stores (e.g., Feed stores, Tractor Supply, local co-ops) are the most reliable sources. Buy in 50 lb bags for best pricing. Seed meal is sold as animal feed, so it is generally available wherever livestock feed is sold. Avoid meal from seeds treated with pesticides or herbicides — organic feed-grade is preferred.

### Agricultural Lime (1 part)

Agricultural lime (calcium carbonate, CaCO₃) supplies calcium and raises soil pH. Solomon includes it to ensure adequate calcium availability and to buffer soil acidity that can result from ongoing fertilization and organic matter decomposition.

**Sourcing**: Garden centers, agricultural supply stores, and lime quarries. Available in finely ground (agricultural) or pelletized forms. Finely ground lime acts faster. A standard soil test will tell you whether your soil needs lime; if your pH is already above 6.5, substitute gypsum or reduce the lime portion.

### Gypsum — Calcium Sulfate (½ part, optional)

Gypsum supplies calcium and sulfur without affecting soil pH. It is useful in soils that are already at or above the target pH range, where adding more lime would be counterproductive.

**Sourcing**: Garden centers and agricultural suppliers. Often sold alongside lime. Available in powdered or granular forms.

### Colloidal Phosphate / Rock Phosphate (1 part)

Colloidal phosphate (also called soft rock phosphate) is a natural source of phosphorus and calcium. It releases phosphorus slowly over several years, making it ideal for building long-term soil reserves. Alternatively, bone meal can be substituted — it breaks down faster but is more expensive.

**Sourcing**: Colloidal phosphate can be found through organic fertilizer suppliers and some garden centers. Bone meal is universally available at any garden center. For large quantities, order from specialty suppliers like Seven Springs Farm, Fertrell, or similar organic input distributors.

### Kelp Meal (½ part)

Kelp meal provides potassium and a full spectrum of trace minerals including iron, manganese, zinc, copper, boron, and molybdenum. These micronutrients are often the limiting factor in [[cannabis-organic-fertilizers]] are convenient but expensive per unit of actual nutrient. A typical 5 lb box of branded organic vegetable fertilizer at a garden center may cost $15-25 and cover a small garden for one season. By contrast, buying ingredients in bulk and mixing COF yourself can reduce your fertilizer cost by 60-80%.

Beyond economics, mixing your own gives you **control over the formula**. You can adjust ratios based on your soil test results — increasing phosphorus if your soil is deficient, reducing lime if your pH is high, or adding extra kelp meal for trace [[jadam-seawater-mineral-supplementation]]. Commercial blends are one-size-fits-all; your COF can be tailored to your specific soil.
