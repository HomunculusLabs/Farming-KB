Updated: 2026-04-18

## pH Is a Symptom, Not a Cause

Ingham's core position: pH is a CONSEQUENCE of the biology in the soil, not the other way around.

- A good soil foodweb buffers pH, bringing it back into the right range rapidly
- In tea or in soil, the right ratio of fungi to bacteria maintains pH correct for the plant
- As long as there is good life, plants don't particularly care about pH (as long as pH is between 4.5 and 10)
- Calcium does strange things with clay at pH extremes (< 4.5 or > 10)

The analogy: "You can be sick and not have a temperature. But a temperature is a sure sign you are sick. pH is a symptom that everything is not right."

## Aerobic Organisms and pH

### Fungi
- Aerobic soil and compost fungi buffer their medium between pH 5.5 and 7.0
- They produce organic acids that maintain this range
- This is the range beneficial for perennial plant growth
- Low pH does NOT select for [[mollison-designers-tropical-pasture-grass-species-and-establishment]] in Australia, pH 8.5 might match the soil pH the plants are growing in

### Why pH Matters in Tea
If pH is WILDLY off, it will kill the organisms in the compost. The practical question: how much wiggle room?

Answer: If you have active bacteria and fungi in the tea, don't worry about pH. If you don't have the biology, then consider that your water isn't right for the organisms in your compost.

### Testing for pH Problems
1. Dechlorinate the water
2. Brew the tea
3. Take a sample — test for active bacteria and fungi
4. If biology is present, pH is fine
5. If biology is absent AND pH is way off, find another water source or buffer the water

## Adjusting Water pH

### Lowering pH (for alkaline water)
- 3 teaspoons of citric acid drops 100 gallons of water by approximately 1 pH unit
