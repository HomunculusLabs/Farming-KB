# New United Nations Concept

## Overview

The permaculture critique of the existing United Nations is that it is neither united nor represents nations. [[bill-mollison]] drew parallels with the oft-quoted "moral majority," which is also neither of those things. Many true nations --- such as the Iroquois confederation or any tribal alliance with a common ethic --- are not represented by such a body. Nations including the Basques, Tartars, Kurds, Palestinians, Hawaiians, Hopi, Tibetans, Pitjatjantjara, Misquito, Aranda, Basarwa, and Herrero remain unrepresented.

Moreover, most nations within the United Nations repress a majority of peoples on earth. In conversations with Thomas Banyaca, a Hopi messenger of his people, it became clear that a new concept of "nation" and a new representative body to speak for them is needed.

## Redefining Nation

The permaculture framework defines a nation as a people subscribing to a common ethic and aspiring to a similar culture. Such nations may not have a common land base or language, but they share a common ethic, minimally comprising:

1. **To care for the earth** --- to repair and conserve
2. **To seek peace** --- and to guard human rights everywhere
3. **To invest all capital, intelligence, goodwill, and labour** to these ends

## The Ethical Majority

At present, many thousands of organisations, affinities, tribes, bioregions, and spiritual and non-government organisations aspire to these beneficial ends. On every continent, a majority of people --- the "ethical majority" --- want peace, a clean and forested earth, cessation of torture, malnutrition, and oppression, and a right to work towards these ends.

It would take very little additional organisation for these groups to meet together, count their numbers, and recognise each other's rights. There are, for instance, far fewer paid-up or active members of political parties or oppressive societies than there are organic gardeners whose life works seek peace and plenty. As groups discuss and accept the minimal ethic, they can quickly proceed to recognise each other.

## Existing Initiatives

Such initiatives have in fact commenced, particularly among Amerindian groups subject to national (political) oppression in both North and South America. Throughout the world, groups are talking of issuing their own passports or adopting world citizen status, given a common aim. The first moves toward a new body of nations united in earth care may be the bioregional and tribal congresses that are occurring today.

## Decentralised Structure

Unlike the present United Nations, this proposed body does not need a world centre or paid administrators. Instead, groups can meet as affinity groups --- in alternative economic summits, [[bioregional-congresses-and-global-nation-permaculture]], tribal conferences, garden and farm design groups --- to deal with specific areas of interest and make these affinities global in scope.

### Advantages of Decentralisation

- **Avoiding centralised administrations** eliminates power blocs
- **Avoiding tax funding** eliminates inefficiency
- **Fees for a regional secretariat** arise from an annual fee forwarded by participant groups

### Building Global Networks

Once continental groups and some global groups have allied, these congresses can increasingly bring in less informed or more remote groups to share resources in a humane alliance. Global seed exchanges, technology groups, gardening forums, and regional groups already meet and are increasing in cooperation. A concept of a global nation is, in fact, very well developed in such groups, and the idea of war or oppression across race, language, or territory is anathema to those allied in good works.

The advantage of such alliances is that even isolated people can find global affinities --- this is not necessarily true of regional organisations alone.

## The Unrepresented Nations

The list of unrepresented peoples cited in the permaculture literature is extensive and illustrative of the gap between the current geopolitical order and true representation:

- **Tribal confederations**: Iroquois, Hopi
- **Ethnic nations without states**: Basques, Tartars, Kurds, Palestinians
- **Indigenous Pacific peoples**: Hawaiians, Pitjatjantjara
- **Central Asian peoples**: Tibetans
- **Americas indigenous**: Misquito, Aranda
- **African indigenous**: Basarwa, Herrero

Each of these represents a people with a common ethic, culture, and often a long history of sustainable land management --- precisely the qualities that the permaculture definition of "nation" values most highly.

## Relationship to Bioregional Organisation

The new United Nations concept complements bioregional organisation by providing the global layer of connection. While bioregional associations handle local governance, [[permaculture-resource-assessment-and-client-consultation]], [[village-financing-and-community-services]], the global congress of ethical nations provides the framework for international cooperation, cultural exchange, and mutual aid between bioregions and peoples who share common ethical commitments regardless of geography.

## Connection to the Seventh Generation Principle

The minimal ethic of caring for the earth directly embodies the seventh generation principle --- the Iroquois concept that decisions should be evaluated for their impact on descendants approximately 100 years in the future. This is not coincidental, as it was through dialogue with Hopi and other indigenous peoples that Mollison developed this framework for redefining what constitutes a true nation.
