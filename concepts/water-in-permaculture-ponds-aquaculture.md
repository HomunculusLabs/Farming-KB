## Water in Permaculture: Ponds and Aquaculture

Water is the primary determinant of what can be grown and how productive a permaculture system becomes. While only a tiny fraction of Earth's water moves through the active cycle, permaculture design focuses on maximizing surface storage, soil retention, and productive use of every available drop — particularly through integrated [[pond-and-aquaculture]] systems.

## Global Water Context

### Distribution
- 75% of fresh water is unavailable (ice caps, deep aquifers)
- Only about 1% of all water actively moves through the cycle
- World mean average rainfall is 33.8 inches per year
- Of atmospheric water, 77% falls on oceans, 23% on land
- Of the 23% falling on land, 16 parts transpire or evaporate, leaving only 7 parts as runoff

### The Forest Role
The land receives an additional seven parts of its water from horizontal advection — moisture carried by wind from oceans. Forests are the primary mechanism for intercepting and converting this advected moisture into usable precipitation and soil storage.

### Design Imperative
Most of the world needs more surface storage at greater elevations, not just in low valleys. Runoff must be reduced through forest establishment, swale construction (especially in urban areas), and soil treatment with tools like the Wallace plow.

## Water and Plant Establishment

Plants must be buffered against drought extremes, particularly during establishment. Every planting design must ensure the ability to water at least twice during summer — either from on-site storage or off-site sources. Seaweed and seaweed concentrates work as jel in soils, greatly assisting water storage and plant resistance to wilt in dry conditions.

## Pond Design Principles

### Structure
- **Floor**: Sloping or stepped, allowing complete drainage
- **Ideal**: Ability to drain into a second pond for a dryland cycle
- **Depth variety**: Creates multiple planting zones from shallow margins to deep water
- **Gleying**: A fermented organic layer seals pond bottoms; once established, it perpetuates itself in the soil

### pH Management
- Pond pH ranges from 6 to 8 — 100 to 200 times less acidic than most garden soils (pH 5–6.5)
- Lime the entire base when constructing a new pond
- Most pond organisms prefer lime; limed pond water is excellent for irrigating garden plants
- Monitor pH regularly, as it differs markedly from terrestrial cultivation

### The Dryland Cycle
After several years as a productive pond:
1. Drain the pond (ideally into a second pond, transferring critical species)
2. Grow heavy-feeding dryland crops in the first year
3. Taper off with modest crops in subsequent years
4. The mud surface fixes passing nutrients, particularly through diatoms and freshwater mussels
5. After 3–4 years of dryland cropping, re-flood — no further manuring is needed

### Nutrient Cycling
- **Diatoms** (invisible) and **freshwater mussels** are the primary nutrient fixers
- Mussels pump nitrogen and phosphorus into the mud floor, filtering about 200 gallons of water per day per mussel
- They are the superior phosphorus fixer of all pond organisms
- Mussels should be harvested only modestly (for chicken grit) to maintain their critical function

## Aquatic Plant Zones

### Marginal and Emergent Plants (0–3 feet depth)
- **Cattails**: Excellent production; control by cutting tops before seeding in small ponds; good for annual gardens in tiny ponds
- **Wild rice**: Requires planting seed in mud balls; essential duck food; 3-week dropping period for harvest; grows in still lagoons at 18 inches to 3 feet depth with slight water movement
- **Arrowheads and duck potatoes**: Important wildfowl food; at least 80 species with wide climate range
- **Reeds and rushes**: Heavy crops for paper, fodder, and mats; papyrus grows here
- **Mints**: Extremely productive but invasive — confine with dense bushes on either side; black peppermint can yield $70,000 from just 2–3 acres through [[comparison-steam-distillation-vs-co2-extraction-for-essential-oils]] to menthol
- **Bamboo**: Lives on well-drained soil above the water table but sends feeder roots down to water; never tolerates sodden conditions

### Floating and Submerged Plants (3–9 feet depth)
- **Water lilies**: Plant in manure bags inside old tires for easy harvesting and containment
- **[[chinese-water-chestnut-mollison]]**: Grows as a mud rush; harvested by wading slowly through stems
- **Indian water chestnut**: Floating with anchor stem; graceful harvest by women in bronze bowls in India
- **Lotus**: Some species have popcorn-like seeds that can be gathered and popped
- **Ferns and duckweed**: Continue productivity at depth; duckweed thrives on the surface

### Algae Management
- **Well-manured pond**: White painted disc disappears at about 2.5 feet depth in soft green water
- **Over-manured**: Disc blacks out at about 1 foot — reduce inputs
- **Under-manured**: Disc visible at 5 feet — add chicken manure
- Optimal algae bloom supports shrimp, prawn, yabby, and fish production

## Animal Integration

### Frogs
Essential for [[ultra-low-herbal-smoker-greenhouse-pest-control]] — they are effective slug eaters. Toads (*Bufo* spp.) control slugs and cutworms effectively as all are nocturnal; bullfrogs are less useful as they feed in daylight when pests are inactive.

### Ducks
- **Stocking rate**: Eight ducks per quarter acre
- **Benefits**: Additional egg crop, manure input, energy turnover in the pond
- **[[fukuoka-natural-predator-protection]]**: Fences into the water with top netting; islands are ideal; duck sheds placed behind fencing
- **Compatible plants**: Wild rice grows above duck height, providing food during its 3-week dropping period

### Crawfish and Crayfish
- **Preferred depth**: About 3 feet
- **Habitat**: Brush piles in water provide shelter
- **Restricted-burrowing species**: Essential to prevent tunneling through dam walls (yabbies can tunnel 25 feet)
- **Harvesting**: Beer cans tied to corks as habitats — draw 20 cans, remove 20 large crawfish, reset
- **Economic potential**: 30 quarter-acre ponds in marine culture can support a family with $20,000–$30,000 annual income (1981 dollars)

### Mussels and Shrimp
- Mussels don't harm plants and are superior nutrient cyclers
- Freshwater shrimp are prime algae harvesters, bridging the gap from diatoms to fish
- Neither mussels nor shrimp compete with fish

### Fish Species
- **Catfish**: Low on the trophic ladder, good eating, obviously suited to pond culture
- **Trout**: Extensive trout require shade and escape depth; intensive trout demand fine adjustments and are problematic
- **Polyculture approach**: Multiple small ponds at different pH levels feed into a main harvest pond through trickle systems with grilles that prevent fish movement between stages

## Pond as Multi-Function System

[[query-how-can-duckweed-be-used-in-a-permaculture-pond]] serves far more than aquaculture:
- **Barrier function**: Often saves a half-mile of fencing
- **Reflection function**: Light bouncing off water surface
- **Fire protection**: Water source for firefighting
- **Heat storage**: Thermal mass moderating nearby temperatures
- **Recreation**: Diving rock by deep areas for safe swimming
- **Water cleaning**: Efficiently collects essential nutrients in mud
- **Manure production**: Chinese consider canals and ponds primarily for field fertilization

### Sewage Integration
- Raw sewage should flow into a marsh first, not directly into a pond
- Marsh plants (mints, bog plants) have phenomenal sewage demand
- [[query-how-do-i-grow-figs-in-cold-climates]], a holding pond is needed because marsh plants go dormant in winter
- After marsh treatment, water contains only dissolved nutrients (mainly phosphates and nitrates) suitable for pond input
- Swiss research identifies rushes (*Juncus* spp.) as the most efficient water cleansers
- Case study: A town of 8,000 saved $30,000 annually in engineering costs and equivalent fuel costs through a permaculture sewage system using mint and bamboo as industrial crops

## Pond Construction Practicalities

- Concrete tanks from 5,000 to 25,000 gallons are available off-the-shelf in Australia
- A simple sheet metal mold can produce a 6-foot diameter, 2-foot deep production pond
- Even a 6-foot diameter pond is worth having as a production unit
- Ponds that are properly gleyed and maintained can persist for millennia
- Shallow ponds made from loose fill are the most prone to silting; gleyed ponds in stable soils endure
