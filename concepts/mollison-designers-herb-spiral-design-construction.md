# Herb Spiral Design and Construction

The herb spiral is one of the most iconic and widely recognized
elements of [[bill-mollison]]'s [[permaculture-principles]] of
relative location, each element performing multiple functions, and
designing from patterns to details.

## Design Concept

The herb spiral is a three-dimensional mound built in a rising spiral
from ground level to approximately 1-1.5 meters in height. The spiral
form compresses a linear herb garden (which might stretch 5-10 meters
in a row) into a circular footprint of roughly 1.5-2 meters diameter,
dramatically increasing the edge-to-area ratio and creating varied
conditions for different plants along its length.

The fundamental insight is that different herbs require different
growing conditions -- some prefer full sun and dry soil, others prefer
shade and moisture, and most fall somewhere between. A spiral naturally
creates this gradient: the top of the spiral is sunny, warm, and
well-drained; the bottom is cooler, shadier, and wetter. The north
side (in the southern hemisphere, south side) receives less sun than
the south face, adding a second gradient.

## Construction Materials

Mollison describes construction using locally available materials:

- **Stone walls:** The most durable and aesthetically pleasing option.
  Local stone (granite, limestone, basalt, sandstone) stacked dry
  without mortar. Stone provides thermal mass that moderates
  temperature fluctuations.

- **Brick:** Recycled bricks work well and provide a uniform building
  unit. Mortared or dry-laid depending on skill and permanence
  desired.

- **Concrete blocks:** Less attractive but functional and widely
  available. Can be faced with stone or plastered.

- **Wood and earth:** In areas where stone is unavailable, logs,
  bamboo, or earth bags can form the retaining structure. These
  degrade faster but are biodegradable and inexpensive.

The interior fill consists of a mix of topsoil, compost, sand, and
gravel. Mollison recommends placing gravel and rubble at the base for
drainage, with progressively finer material toward the surface. This
creates the moisture gradient essential to the spiral's function.

## Building the Spiral

Step-by-step construction:

1. **Mark the footprint:** Draw a circle 1.5-2m in diameter on the
   ground. Mark the center point where the spiral reaches its
   maximum height.

2. **Prepare the base:** Excavate 10-15cm of topsoil from the circle.
   Fill the base with gravel, rubble, or coarse organic matter for
   drainage. This layer connects to the broader [[backyard-water-harvesting]] systems, ensuring the moist base zone receives adequate
water even during dry periods.
