## PF TEK Fruiting Chamber

The Dual Chambered Terrarium is Psylocybe Fanaticus's original fruiting chamber design for the PF TEK. It uses a standard 10-gallon aquarium with a Plexiglas spray shield partition to create molecularized humidity -- fine mist that bathes the fungus without direct water contact. See [[pf-tek]] for the complete substrate and inoculation method.

## Why a Dual Chamber?

The mushroom gets water from two sources: the substrate it grows on, and the humid air surrounding it. The fungus needs to "bathe in a shroud of floating water molecules" at near-100% humidity. The dual chambered design achieves this through three principles:

1. **Never spray the fungus directly** -- water droplets are "giant ponds" relative to fine mycelial networks and can drown or contaminate growth
2. **Molecularized water** (evaporated humidity) is what the fungus actually absorbs
3. The spray shield breaks coarse spray into fine mist that flows around into the secondary chamber

## Construction

### Materials

- 10-gallon aquarium
- 1/8 inch thick clear acrylic (Plexiglas) window insulation, cut ~15 x 18 inches
- Strips of wood with connectors and screws (for terrarium lid frame)
- Clear plastic film and thumb tacks (lid covering)
- Small wall-type thermometer
- High-quality adjustable nozzle water spray bottle (critical piece of equipment)
- Rubber bulb battery filler or enema bottle (for siphoning pooled water)

### The Spray Shield

The spray shield is the key innovation. Cut from 1/8 inch clear acrylic to fit tightly inside the aquarium. It creates two chambers:

- **Primary chamber**: Receives the initial spray; spray hits the shield and breaks down into finer mist
- **Secondary chamber**: Where the fungus cakes sit; bathed in fine humidity safely away from water droplets

The shield is slanted so it also acts as a drip shield/roof -- condensation runs off rather than dripping directly onto the cakes. More condensation is better.

### Lid

Frame made from wood strips with plastic film stretched across and tacked down. The lid seals the terrarium to trap swirling mist inside after spraying.

## Spraying Procedure

1. **Before placing cakes**: Spray all inside surfaces of the terrarium, including spray shield and lid
2. **Insert fungus cakes** and put spray shield and lid in place
3. **To spray**: Slightly lift the lid, insert spray bottle nozzle between lid and aquarium top, spray vigorously downward into the middle of the shield for ~5 seconds
4. **Immediately withdraw** the sprayer and let the lid drop to seal the swirling mist inside
5. **Come back after a few minutes** and give another spraying if desired
6. **Third spray** if the terrarium will be left untended until evening

### Frequency

- Spray at least 2 times per day; the more the better
- Compensate for missed daytime sprays by spraying several times in the evening
- Make sure all inside surfaces are foggy or dripping with water -- this itself helps generate humidity
- Mushrooms will grow with only one good spraying a day, and even less

## Ventilation

Each time the terrarium is sprayed, ventilate the fungus:
1. Take off the lid
2. Hold the spray shield vertically
3. Fan the chamber with a piece of cardboard
4. Then spray as above

## Water Management

- **Siphon out pooled water** from the terrarium bottom (prevents bacteria buildup)
- Use a rubber bulb battery filler (auto parts store) or enema bottle
- Check for standing water regularly

## Temperature

- **Do NOT directly heat the terrarium** -- any direct heating works against humidification and adds drying influence
- No heating cables, heat pads, or blankets
- Don't shine light directly down into the terrarium
- Keep any plant grow light a safe distance away
- **Ideal range**: 65-85 degrees Fahrenheit
- Fungi grow well at 60 degrees F (slowly)
- A too-hot terrarium causes lots of spreading mycelium but no fruiting

## Lighting

- Expose to normal room light (indirect sunlight)
- A small low-wattage fluorescent plant light makes phototropic mushrooms grow upward
- Can be left on all the time if desired
- Mushrooms need light to know which direction to grow

## Perlite Humidification (Alternative/Supplement)

An anonymous internet technique that works well with the dual chambered terrarium:

- Create a "floor" of wetted perlite (bright white, from plant shops) at the bottom of the terrarium
- Perlite absorbs water through its porous structure and evaporates it gradually over weeks
- Cakes can sit directly on the perlite (wick-like action transfers moisture to cakes)
- Can replace or supplement spray humidification
- Works especially well for multi-flush fruiting

## Symptoms of Low Humidity

- **Fuzzy white mycelium on caps**: Crown-like white hair on mushroom caps -- not contamination, but indicates humidity could be higher
- **Deformed, convoluted, withering mushrooms and primordia**: Clear sign of insufficient humidity
- **Stalled growth**: Mushrooms stop developing
