# Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor)

## Overview

Turkey tail is one of the most common mushrooms on Earth, found on every continent except Antarctica. Named for its colorful concentric zones of brown, tan, white, blue, and orange that resemble a wild turkey's tail feathers. It is a bracket polypore that grows on dead hardwood logs and stumps worldwide.

Turkey tail is the most extensively researched medicinal mushroom for immune support and cancer adjunct therapy. Its polysaccharide derivatives PSK and PSP are approved pharmaceutical drugs in Japan and China respectively, prescribed alongside conventional cancer treatments.

## Key Compounds

- **PSK (Polysaccharide-K, krestin):** Protein-bound polysaccharide. Approved as a cancer adjuvant drug in Japan since 1977. Extracted via hot water and precipitation.
- **PSP (Polysaccharide-peptide):** Similar protein-bound polysaccharide. Approved as a drug in China since 1987.
- **Beta-glucans:** The primary immunomodulatory compounds, particularly 1,3-beta and 1,6-beta-glucans.
- **Triterpenes:** Present at lower concentrations than reishi or chaga.
- **[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]] and flavonoids:** Contribute antioxidant activity.

## PSK and PSP — Mechanisms

### PSK (Krestin)
- Molecular weight approximately 100 kDa.
- Contains a beta-glucan backbone with protein components.
- Mechanisms include:
  - Activation of dendritic cells and antigen-presenting cells.
  - Enhancement of T-cell proliferation and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte (CTL) activity.
  - Stimulation of natural killer (NK) cell function.
  - Induction of apoptosis in cancer cells via caspase activation.
  - Anti-angiogenic effects (inhibiting tumor blood vessel formation).
  - Reduction of chemotherapy side effects (maintains white blood cell counts, reduces nausea, improves appetite).

### PSP
- Similar to PSK but with a slightly different molecular structure.
- Stronger anti-inflammatory profile than PSK in some studies.
- Also enhances NK cell and macrophage activity.
- Protects against chemotherapy-induced immunosuppression.

## Cancer Adjunct Therapy

Turkey tail is the best-evidenced medicinal mushroom for cancer support:

### Clinical Evidence
- PSK has been studied in hundreds of clinical trials, primarily in Japan.
- Meta-analyses show improved survival rates in gastric, colorectal, breast, and lung cancers when PSK is added to conventional chemotherapy.
- 5-year survival improvement of 5-15% in various cancer types.
- PSP shows similar results in Chinese clinical trials.

### How It Is Used
- Always as an adjunct — never as a standalone cancer treatment.
- Prescribed alongside chemotherapy and/or radiation.
- Dosing in clinical settings: 3g/day PSK, typically starting before or during chemotherapy and continuing post-treatment.
- Improves quality of life during cancer treatment.

### Important Caveats
- Most clinical research uses isolated PSK or PSP, not whole mushroom extracts.
- Supplement quality varies enormously — few consumer products contain clinically relevant PSK/PSP levels.
- Consult oncologist before using turkey tail alongside cancer treatment.

## Immune Modulation

Turkey tail is a broad-spectrum immunomodulator:

1. **Innate immunity:** Activates macrophages, neutrophils, and NK cells. Increases phagocytic activity.
2. **Adaptive immunity:** Enhances T-cell and B-cell responses. Promotes antibody production.
3. **Gut immune health:** Acts as a prebiotic, supporting beneficial gut bacteria that produce short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs). SCFAs in turn support immune regulation.
4. **Anti-inflammatory:** Reduces excessive inflammatory cytokine production (IL-6, TNF-alpha) while maintaining pathogen-fighting capacity.
5. **Antiviral activity:** Inhibits viral replication of HPV, influenza, and herpes in laboratory studies. Used as an adjunct in HPV treatment protocols.

## Preparation Methods

### Hot Water Decoction
The traditional and most effective method for extracting polysaccharides:
1. Break dried turkey tail into small pieces.
2. Simmer in water for 60-120 minutes (long simmer needed due to tough chitin).
3. Strain and drink as tea. The tea is mild and slightly earthy.

### Dual Extraction Tincture
Captures both water-soluble polysaccharides and alcohol-soluble triterpenes:
1. Hot water extraction first (60-90 min).
2. Alcohol maceration of spent material (4-6 weeks in 40-50% ethanol).
3. Combine extracts.

### Capsules
Common supplement form. Look for products with verified beta-glucan content (preferably 30%+ beta-glucans) and third-party testing for PSK/PSP content.

## Dosing Guidelines

- **General immune support:** 1-3g dried mushroom powder or extract per day.
- **Cancer adjunct (under medical supervision):** 3-6g/day, divided into 2-3 doses.
- **Tincture:** 2-4ml dual extract, 2-3 times daily.
- **Tea:** 5-10g dried mushroom per liter of water, simmered 60-90 minutes.

Consistency is essential. Immune modulation occurs over weeks of daily use.

## Foraging and Identification

Turkey tail is one of the easiest mushrooms to identify, but has lookalikes:

### Key Features
- Thin, flexible brackets (2-8cm wide) with distinct concentric color zones.
- Pore surface (underside) is white to cream with tiny pores visible to the naked eye.
- Grows in overlapping clusters on dead hardwood logs and stumps.
- No stem — brackets attach directly to wood.
- Flexible and leathery when fresh; becomes tough when dry but can be rehydrated.

### Lookalikes
- **False turkey tail (Stereum ostrea):** Similar zonate cap but has a smooth, poreless underside (no pores visible). Generally considered non-toxic but not medicinal.
- **[[trametes-hirsuta]]:** Very similar but less colorful; also medicinal.

### Habitat
- Dead hardwood (oak, maple, beech, birch).
- Found year-round [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]].
- One of the most common wood-decay fungi worldwide.

## Cultivation

Turkey tail can be cultivated but is often wild-harvested due to abundance:
- **Substrate:** Hardwood sawdust, supplemented with bran.
- **Temperature:** 70-80°F (21-27°C) for colonization.
- **Fruiting:** Requires high humidity and fresh air exchange.
- **Note:** Cultivated turkey tail grown on grain may have lower active compound concentrations than wild-harvested from hardwood.

## Safety Considerations

- Extremely safe with long history of use as food and medicine.
- No significant toxicity in animal or human studies even at high doses.
- May enhance immune function — caution with autoimmune conditions (lupus, MS, [[blesching-cannabis-rheumatoid-arthritis]]).
- Possible interactions with immunosuppressant medications.
- Safe during most chemotherapy regimens but always consult oncologist.
