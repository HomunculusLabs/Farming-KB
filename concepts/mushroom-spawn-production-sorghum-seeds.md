# Mushroom Spawn Production on Sorghum Seeds

Multiplying [[mushroom-spawn]] on sorghum seeds is a critical step [[flammability-hazard-alcohol-lysol-flame-sterilization-mycology]] during inoculation
- **PDA bottles with mycelium** — the source of pure mycelium culture from tissue culture
- **Laminar flow cabinet** — provides a sterile workspace for inoculation
- **UV lamp** — used to sterilize the laminar flow cabinet interior before work

## Grain Requirements

Selecting high-quality grain is essential to minimize contamination and maximize mycelium growth. Sorghum seeds used for [[spawn-production]] must meet the following criteria:

- **Recently harvested** — fresh grains have better nutritional content and lower microbial load
- **Few broken kernels** — damaged grains release starches that promote bacterial contamination
- **Little contamination** — avoid grains with visible mold, discoloration, or spoilage
- **No fungi, no insects** — grain must be free of any fungal growth or insect damage
- **No more than 12% humidity** — excess moisture in raw grain can lead to anaerobic conditions and bacterial growth after sterilization

If sorghum is unavailable, millet and wheat are acceptable alternatives, though sorghum generally provides the best results due to its hard outer coat, which resists over-absorption of water during soaking.

## Preparation Process

The following 23-step process covers everything from grain preparation through inoculation:

### Phase 1: Grain Preparation and Sterilization (Steps 1–10)

1. **Soak sorghum overnight** — Use 2 liters of water per 1 kg of grain. This hydrates the seeds evenly and removes surface dust and debris.
2. **Wash and strain** — Rinse the soaked grain thoroughly and strain to remove all excess water.
3. **Steam the grain** — Steam for 30–45 minutes to soften the grains and cook approximately 25% of each kernel. Partial cooking makes nutrients accessible to the mycelium while preventing the grains from becoming mushy.
4. **Drain and cool** — Drain all water from the steamed grain, then spread the seeds out on a clean surface to cool and decrease surface moisture to the appropriate level.
5. **Fill bottles** — Fill approximately ¾ of each flask-type bottle with the prepared sorghum seeds. Leaving headspace allows room for shaking during incubation and prevents the grain from blocking the cotton plug.
6. **Prepare cotton plugs** — Carefully prepare the cotton (gauze) plugs, shaping them to fit snugly into the bottle mouths.
7. **Plug bottles** — Tightly plug the mouth of each bottle with cotton. Leave a small portion protruding outward to allow ventilation while filtering airborne contaminants.
8. **Transfer to sterilization chamber** — Move all plugged bottles into the autoclave or [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]].
10. **Cool bottles** — Allow bottles to cool after sterilization, then transfer them to a clean, cool place until inoculation.

### Phase 2: Inoculation (Steps 11–23)

11. **Clean and prepare bottles** — Wipe down the exterior of all sorghum bottles to remove any surface contamination before moving them to the inoculation area.
12. **Clean laminar flow cabinet** — Wipe all interior surfaces of the laminar flow cabinet with alcohol to create a sterile working environment.
13. **Transfer materials** — Bring the PDA culture bottles, sorghum seed bottles, paper squares, and rubber bands into the laminar flow cabinet. Turn on the UV lamp for 10–15 minutes to sterilize the chamber interior and all materials.
14. **Clean hands** — Thoroughly clean hands with alcohol before entering the sterile work area.
15. **Flame the needle** — Hold the inoculation needle or scalpel in the alcohol lamp flame until it glows red. This burns off any residual alcohol and sterilizes the metal completely.
16. **Cut PDA mycelium** — Using the sterilized needle or scalpel, cut a 5 mm × 5 mm square of PDA mycelium. Select only the white, healthy-growing mycelium — avoid any discolored or contaminated areas.
17. **Close PDA bottle** — Immediately close the PDA culture bottle near the flame to prevent airborne contamination from entering.
18. **Open sorghum bottle** — Flame around the mouth of the sorghum seed bottle to sterilize the rim, then open the bottle while keeping the mouth near the flame.
19. **Inoculate** — Insert the sterilized needle through the bottle mouth and place the mycelium piece into the middle of the sorghum seeds. Positioning it in the center promotes even radial colonization.
20. **Close bottle** — Close the sorghum seed bottle immediately after inoculation to minimize exposure to potential contaminants.
21. **Seal with paper** — Place a 7 cm × 7 cm paper square over the cotton plug and secure it with a rubber band. This protects the cotton from dust and moisture.
22. **Label** — Clearly label each bottle with: the date of inoculation, spawn number, reference to the parent PDA culture, and inoculation time.
23. **Check for infection** — After inoculation, inspect all bottles for signs of early contamination. Remove any contaminated bottles immediately. Approximately 3% loss due to contamination is normal and expected.

## Key Principles

Adherence to the following principles is essential throughout the inoculation process to maintain sterility and ensure successful spawn production:

- **Mouth of bottle always higher than the bottom** — Keep bottles tilted so the mouth is elevated. This prevents airborne particles from falling into the open bottle during inoculation.
- **Mouth stays near flame at all times** — The flame creates an upward convection current that acts as a sterile air barrier. Never remove the bottle mouth from the flame zone while it is open.
- **PDA mycelium must not touch anything before entering the sorghum bottle** — The mycelium piece should go directly from the PDA bottle to the sorghum bottle without contacting any surface, tool, or the bottle rim.
- **Never touch mouth of bottle with PDA piece** — The mycelium square must be placed inside the bottle without dragging it across the mouth or rim, which could transfer contaminants.

## Incubation

After inoculation, bottles are placed in a dark, clean incubation room or area with appropriate temperature for the mushroom species being cultivated.

- **Colonization time** — Full-grown sorghum grain mycelium typically takes 10–15 days depending on the species. Faster-growing species such as oyster mushrooms may colonize in as few as 10 days, while slower species may take up to 15 days.
- **Storage of mature spawn** — Once fully colonized, spawn should be stored in a cool place or in the vegetable compartment of a refrigerator if not used immediately. Properly stored spawn remains viable for several weeks.
- **Regular inspection** — Check bottles regularly during incubation for signs of contamination such as unusual colors (green, black, pink), off-odors, or stunted mycelium growth.

## Quality Control

Maintaining rigorous quality control throughout the spawn [[directed-inoculum-production-process-for-amf]] ensures high success rates:

- **Observe and record data** — Keep detailed notes on each batch, including grain source, sterilization times, inoculation dates, and any observations about growth rate or contamination.
- **Remove contaminated bottles** — Any bottle showing signs of contamination must be removed immediately from the incubation area to prevent the spread of spores to healthy cultures.
- **Clean contaminated bottles** — Clean contaminated bottles as normal glassware — wash thoroughly with detergent, rinse, and dry before reuse. Do not reuse bottles that contained severe contamination without thorough sterilization.
- **Expected contamination rate** — A 3% contamination loss is considered normal and acceptable. Rates significantly above this indicate problems with technique, sterilization, or grain quality that should be investigated and corrected.
