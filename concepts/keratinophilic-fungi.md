# Keratinophilic Fungi
Keratinophilic fungi are a specialized group of fungi capable of degrading
keratin, the structural protein found in hair, feathers, nails, horns, hooves,
and skin. They play important [[ecological-roles-fungi-ecosystems]] in [[soil-protozoa-nutrient-cycling-and-mineralization]] include
clinically significant species that cause dermatophytosis in humans and
animals.

## Keratin as a Fungal Substrate

Keratin is one of the most resistant structural proteins in nature,
characterized by:

- High content of disulfide bonds (cysteine cross-links) that confer
  mechanical strength
- Insolubility in water and most organic solvents
- Resistance to degradation by most proteolytic enzymes
- Abundance in terrestrial environments from vertebrate shedding and
  [[decomposition]]
Only a specialized subset of fungi possess the enzymatic machinery (keratinases
and sulfite-producing pathways) required to [[query-can-fungi-break-down-plastic-pollution]] keratin. These fungi
reduce disulfide bonds, enabling proteolytic degradation of the keratin
polypeptide chain.

## Taxonomic Composition

Keratinophilic fungi span several major fungal groups:

### Dermatophytes

The dermatophytes (anamorphic Ascomycetes) are the most clinically significant
keratinophilic fungi:

- **Trichophyton species**: cause infections of skin, hair, and nails
  (tinea, ringworm)
- **Microsporum species**: primarily infect hair and skin, especially in
  children
- **Epidermophyton floccosum**: infects skin and nails but not hair
- Geophilic (soil-associated), zoophilic (animal-associated), and
  anthropophilic (human-associated) ecological categories

### Non-Dermatophyte Keratinophilic Fungi

Many non-pathogenic keratinophilic fungi are common in soil:

- **Keratinomyces ajelloi**: a geophilic species frequently isolated from
  soil
- **Chrysosporium species**: widespread soil fungi with keratinolytic activity
- **Trichophyton terrestre**: a soil-dwelling species with low pathogenicity
- Species of Acremonium, Fusarium, and Scopulariopsis also demonstrate
  keratinolytic ability

## Ecological Distribution

Keratinophilic fungi are widely distributed in soils, particularly in habitats
rich in keratinous material:

- Agricultural soils (especially where livestock are present)
- Soils near poultry farms and animal enclosures
- Cave soils occupied by bats and other mammals
- Compost and manure-amended soils
- Natural areas with high vertebrate activity

The prevalence of keratinophilic fungi in soil is correlated with the presence
of keratinous substrata. Soils receiving regular inputs of animal hair,
feathers, or skin scales typically harbor larger populations and greater
species diversity of keratinophilic fungi.

## Isolation Methods

### Hair Baiting Technique

The standard method for isolating keratinophilic fungi from soil:

1. Small pieces of sterilized hair (human, horse, or other mammal) are
   placed on the surface of moistened soil in a Petri dish
2. Plates are incubated at 25-28 degrees C for 2-6 weeks
3. Hair is examined microscopically for fungal colonization (conidia,
   hyphae, or sporulating structures)
4. Colonized hair fragments are transferred to isolation media (Sabouraud
   dextrose agar with antibiotics)
