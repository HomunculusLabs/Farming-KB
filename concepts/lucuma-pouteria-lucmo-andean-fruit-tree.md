# Lucuma (*Pouteria obovata*, syn. *Pouteria lucmo*)

## Overview

Lucuma (pronounced "loo-koo-mah") is an attractive evergreen medium-sized tree (8–15 m) native to the subtropical Andes of [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]].

## Fruit

A single tree can produce up to 500 fruits per year. Individual fruits can weigh up to 1 kg. The fruit has smooth, bronze-colored skin and bright yellow to orange flesh. Although variable, the flavor can be remarkably like maple syrup. The fruit falls to the ground when ripe and is then stored in dry hay or sawdust until soft.

**Nutritional value**: Rich in provitamin A (carotene), vitamin B3, carbohydrate, calories, and iron.

**Processing**: The fruit is frequently blended with milk to make milkshakes. It can be dried and stored as a flour that keeps for years and is added to other flours for making desserts. This long shelf-life of the dried flour makes lucuma an important food security crop.

**Animal forage**: The fruit is valuable chicken forage, notably promoting eggs with bright orange yolks (likely due to the high carotene content).

## Other Uses

- **Timber**: Wood used in construction
- **Ornamental**: Attractive evergreen canopy

## Propagation

Lucuma can be grown from seed and cuttings, but grafting is reported as difficult. Seedling trees show genetic variability in fruit quality.

## Permaculture Applications

1. **Food security**: The storable dried flour provides year-round nutrition
2. **High yield**: Up to 500 fruits per tree per year
3. **Chicken integration**: Fruit promotes enhanced egg yolk coloration
4. **Evergreen canopy**: Year-round shade and [[amanita]]**: One of the most commercially important fruits in its native range, offering market potential
