## Community Banking Principles
Mollison's model for community banking draws on historical precedents including
the Wörgl experiment in Austria (1932-33), where a stamped currency stimulated
local trade and reduced unemployment during the Great Depression. Community
banks serve as exchange institutions that hold reserves, issue currency, and
facilitate local trade. Unlike conventional banks, community banks are owned
and governed by their members, with profits returned to the community rather
than extracted by external shareholders. They operate on the principle that
money should circulate within the local economy rather than being siphoned to
distant financial centres.
