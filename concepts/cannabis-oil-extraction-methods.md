## Cannabis Oil Extraction Methods

An overview of solvent-based [[cannabis-oil-extraction]] as documented in [[ben-lemon]]'s
*[[nicolas-flamel]]. The book
was one of the first widely distributed practical guides to chemistry-based cannabis
extraction, predating the modern commercial concentrate industry by decades.

## Historical Context

Cannabis Alchemy was written during a time of rapidly [[cannabis-concentrates-and-extracts]].

## Fundamental Extraction Principle

The core principle of solvent-based extraction is selective solubility. Cannabis
plant material consists of cellulose (insoluble structural fiber) and [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]]
containing THC, related cannabinoids, chlorophyll, waxes, and terpenes. When immersed
in an appropriate solvent and heated, the essential oils dissolve while the cellulose
matrix remains intact. After filtration, the solvent evaporates, leaving concentrated
cannabis oil. The choice of solvent determines which compounds are extracted and the
resulting oil's purity and potency.

## Preparation of Cannabis Material

### Marijuana Preparation

1. **Seed removal**: Seeds are removed by hand -- they contribute no desirable
   compounds and add unnecessary bulk.
2. **Drying**: Material is crumbled and dried in an oven at 250°F (121°C) on a
   cookie sheet for fifteen-minute intervals until parts crumble easily to powder
   between the palms.
3. **Pulverization**: Dried material is reduced to the finest possible powder using
   a heavy-duty blender at slower speed, agitated with a wooden stick when not
   running. Finer powder ruptures more cell walls, increasing extraction efficiency
   and reducing the volume of plant material to be processed.

### Hashish Preparation

Hashish is heated at low heat until it begins to smoke slightly, then crumbled by
hand or ground in a mortar and pestle. Pressed hashish may be shredded with a cheese
grater before blending. Powdered hashish exposed to air decreases in potency, so
grinding should be done immediately before extraction.

## The Refluxing Apparatus

The centerpiece of Lemon's method is a water-bath-based refluxing apparatus designed
for safety. It avoids direct heating of flammable solvents by using boiling water
as an indirect heat source, eliminating the primary ignition hazard.

The kitchen-scale apparatus consists of a **small stainless-steel pot** (holding the
powdered cannabis and solvent) inside a **larger stew pot** (the water bath), topped
with an **inverted ice-cooled lid**. **Polyethylene bags** and **inner-tube rubber
bands** provide seals. The entire assembly sits inside a **boiling-water tub**.

Solvent vapors rise, condense on the ice-cooled lid, and drip back onto the cannabis.
This continuous cycling -- refluxing -- ensures thorough extraction over 3--4 hours.
The water bath means the solvent never reaches flame or electric element temperature.

For large-scale operations, Lemon describes a two-drum apparatus (55-gallon oil drums)
with copper tube condensers, sparkless electric hotplates, and an immersion pump.

## Extraction Solvents: Comparative Analysis

### Methanol (Wood Alcohol)

- **Boiling point**: 64°C (147°F)
- **Toxicity**: High -- fumes cause body ache and potential permanent damage
- **Flammability**: Explosive
- **Availability**: Commonly available

Methanol is effective and was the most commonly used solvent. A notable advantage is
that it does not extract many water-soluble, non-psychoactive tars, yielding a
cleaner initial extract. Primary drawbacks are significant toxicity and explosive
flammability. Fumes must never be inhaled.

### Isopropyl Rubbing Alcohol (70%)

- **Boiling point**: ~82°C (varies with water content)
- **Toxicity**: Moderate -- less toxic than methanol
- **Flammability**: Less explosive
- **Availability**: Widely available at low cost

The most accessible option. The 30% water content extracts non-psychoactive
substances, approximately doubling oil yield while halving potency. Water also raises
evaporation temperature, making solvent removal slower.

### Ethanol (Grain Alcohol)

- **Boiling point**: 78.5°C (173°F)
- **Toxicity**: Low (food-safe when pure)
- **Flammability**: Flammable
- **Availability**: Difficult due to liquor taxation

The ideal solvent -- effective, food-safe, and producing clean results. However, pure
ethanol was difficult to obtain in 1979 due to taxation. Denatured ethanol contains
non-removable poisons and must never be used.

### Petroleum Ether

- **Boiling point**: 30--60°C (86--140°F)
- **Toxicity**: Moderate (hydrocarbon solvent)
- **Flammability**: Extremely explosive
- **Availability**: Available from chemical suppliers

The most selective solvent. Unlike alcohols, it preferentially dissolves psychoactive
cannabinoids and terpenes, producing oil approximately twice as potent by weight. Due
to extreme explosiveness, Lemon recommends using it primarily for re-extraction of
already-extracted oil rather than the initial crude extraction.

## The Basic Extraction Process

1. **Preparation**: Dry, deseed, and condition the cannabis material
2. **Pulverization**: Reduce to finest possible powder in a blender
3. **Refluxing**: Boil powdered material in solvent for 3--4 hours using the
   water-bath apparatus; condensed solvent continuously cycles back through the
   plant material
4. **Soxhleting**: Drain the oil-bearing solvent and wash spent cannabis repeatedly
   with clean solvent through a coffee-filter-lined colander until wash drops leave
   no colored residue when evaporated on glass
5. **Solvent removal**: Distill solvent off by replacing spent cannabis with a
   collection pan; condensed solvent is saved for reuse. For toxic solvents, water
   is added and evaporated at ~220°F to ensure complete removal
6. **Purification**: Crude oil undergoes further refinement through petroleum ether
   re-extraction, isomerization, or charcoal filtration

## Safety Considerations

- **Indirect heating**: Never apply flame or direct heat to flammable solvents; the
  water-bath design prevents solvent temperature from exceeding 100°C
- **Ventilation**: All extraction must be performed in well-ventilated areas; solvent
  fumes are both toxic and explosive
- **No open flames**: Eliminate all ignition sources from the extraction area
- **Solvent recovery**: The refluxing apparatus condenses and recovers solvent vapor
- **Residual solvent removal**: The water-addition evaporation step ensures complete
  removal of all solvent traces (all described solvents evaporate below water's
  boiling point)
