# Selective Agents for Fungal Isolation — Antibiotics and Fungicides

Selective isolation agents are chemicals added to culture media to suppress specific groups of microorganisms while allowing target fungi to grow. They are essential tools for isolating specific fungal groups from mixed natural samples such as soil, plant tissue, leaf litter, and water.

## Why Selective Media Are Needed

Natural samples contain complex mixtures of:
- **Fast-growing fungi** that overgrow slower species (Trichoderma, Mucorales, Rhizopus)
- **Bacteria** that outcompete fungi on rich media
- **Oomycetes** (Straminipiles) that grow rapidly on general media
- **Yeasts** that form spreading colonies obscuring filamentous fungi

Without selective agents, a few rapidly growing species dominate cultures and rare or slow-growing species are missed entirely.

## Antibacterial Agents

### Broad-Spectrum
| Agent | Target | Concentration | Notes |
|-------|--------|--------------|-------|
| Chloramphenicol | Bacteria | 50-200 mg/L | Autoclavable |
| Tetracycline | Bacteria | 25-100 mg/L | Heat labile |
| Ampicillin | Bacteria | 100-300 mg/L | Heat labile, pH sensitive |

### Gram-Specific
| Agent | Target | Concentration | Notes |
|-------|--------|--------------|-------|
| Penicillins | Gram-positive | 30-100 IU/mL | Heat labile, pH sensitive |
| Streptomycin | Gram-negative | 50-500 mg/L | Heat labile |
| Rifampicin | Bacteria | 5-25 mg/L | Photosensitive |
| Vancomycin | Bacteria | 50-200 mg/L | Heat labile |

## Antifungal Agents

### Broad-Spectrum Fungal Suppressors
| Agent | Target | Concentration | Notes |
|-------|--------|--------------|-------|
| Cycloheximide | Filamentous fungi | 100-200 mg/L | Autoclavable |
| Nystatin | Filamentous fungi | 2-10 mg/L | Photosensitive |
| Natamycin (pimaricin) | Filamentous fungi | 2-30 mg/L | Autoclavable, photosensitive |
| Amphotericin B | Filamentous fungi | 0.5-10 mg/L | Sterol synthesis inhibitor |

### Group-Specific Agents
| Agent | Target | Concentration | Notes |
|-------|--------|--------------|-------|
| Dichloran (Botran) | Mucorales, Penicillium | 2-100 mg/L | Less hazardous than PCNB |
| PCNB | Aspergillus, filamentous | 100 mg-1 g/L | Carcinogen |

| Rose bengal | Bacteria, filamentous | 50-500 mg/L | Photosensitive |

## Applications

### Soil Fungal Isolation
- Rose bengal + streptomycin: suppress bacteria, slow fast fungi
- Dichloran rose bengal chloramphenicol (DRBC): widely used soil medium
- PCNB + antibiotics: select for specific soil groups
- Antibiotics alone: when all fungi are targets, only bacteria must be suppressed

### Endophyte Isolation
- Weak media + cyclosporin A: slow-growing endophytes
- Chloramphenicol + antibiotics: suppress bacteria from plant tissue
- Multiple media combinations recommended for each host

### Oomycete Isolation
- Pimaricin + vancomycin + PCNB (PVP): standard oomycete selective medium
- Oxgall: suppresses bacteria and many true fungi
- Antibiotics: essential (oomycetes grow poorly when bacteria present)

### Yeast Isolation
- Antibiotics: suppress bacterial contaminants
- Acidified media (pH 3.5-4.5): suppress many filamentous fungi
- Dichloran: suppress molds

## Important Considerations

### Autoclavability
- **Autoclavable:** Chloramphenicol, cycloheximide, natamycin, oxgall
- **Heat labile (add after autoclaving, cooled to ~50C):** Penicillins, streptomycin, ampicillin, tetracycline, cyclosporin A, rifampicin

### Photosensitivity
- Store light-sensitive agents (rose bengal, rifampicin, natamycin, nystatin) in dark
- Wrap plates in foil if incubation exceeds a few days

### Combinations
- Multiple agents can be combined (e.g., DRBC = dichloran + rose bengal + chloramphenicol)
- Test for interactions between agents before use
- Some combinations may be too suppressive for target organisms

### Optimization
- No single medium/isolation protocol recovers all fungi from a sample
- Use multiple media with different selective agents for comprehensive surveys
- Always include nonselective control plates
- Experiment with concentrations for specific sample types
