# Mollison Free-Range Poultry Forage Design

Mollison's comprehensive design for free-range poultry systems integrated with permaculture, including forage species lists, yield regulation, and integration with other systems.

## System Components (broad scale, 2000 sq m+)

1. Hardy free-range system of Zone II plus normal orchard species and stored foods
2. **Strawyards** for seasonal forage, gleaning, and stored seed production
3. **Throwover pens** for tender greens and highly-selected browse (e.g., chard)
4. **Storage bins/sheds** for hard foods during spring "hungry gap"

## Establishment

- Control browsing herbivores, sow herbal leys
- Nitrogenous/leguminous plants as manure crop for slower-evolving trees, shrubs, herbs, roots
- Initial costs: fencing, mulching strawyards, buildings, water supply, plant/animal stock
- Ducks, hens, guinea-fowl stocked from beginning; geese after first year
- Fungi and insects soon complicate the system, providing unplanned resources

## Forage Species by Season

### Summer Seeds and Pods (Trees/Shrubs)
- Tree Lucerne (Chamaecytisus proliferus): early-mid summer seed drop, foliage edible
- Lespedeza, Atriplex (salt-bushes), Pea Trees (Caragana), Carob, Honey Locust
- Black Locust (seed; leaves may poison stock), Mesquite, Acacia species
- Albizia

### Autumn-Winter Nuts and Acorns
- Black Walnut: store 12 months
- Persian Walnut: store 12 months
- Chestnut: store 6 months (unless chilled/dried)
- Oaks: almost all acorns edible for poultry
- Beech: oil-rich nuts, irregular yield

### Late Summer-Mid Winter Berries and Fruits
- Lycium (matrimony vine), Coprosma, Mulberries, Elderberries
- Emu Berry, Palms, Karreboom (Rhus lancea)
- Crataegus (hawthorn), serviceberries

### Vines for Fences and Trellis
- Dolichos (annual and perennial beans), Passiflora (passionfruit)
- Kudzu Vine (Thunbergia), Chayote, Gabizura

### Roots
- Nut-grass (Eleocharis), sour-grass (Oxalis), Jerusalem Artichoke

### Herb Layer
- Clovers, medics, lucerne, chicory, asparagus, plantain, fennel
- Pokeweed, wood millet, lupins, partridge berry, checkerberry
- Wild rye, wild rice (for duck forage), Lotus spp.

### Broadcast Sowing in Strawyards
- Sunflower, millets, corn, buckwheat, wheat, rye, barley, oats, teff
- Chenopodium, chick pea, lentils, lab-lab, field peas
- Amaranthus grains (1000+ varieties), linseed

## Poultry Forage Days (P.F.D.)

Mollison proposes measuring plant value as "poultry forage days":
- Tree lucerne: ~2.5 kg seed = 30 P.F.D. for one hen
- Black walnut at maturity: keeps 8 hens all year = 2,920 P.F.D.
- Carob: 3-6 bean daily supplement for 3 goats = 1,000+ G.F.D.

## Yield Regulation

### Steady Forage Yield
- Hard seed (Acacia, Robinia): mainly early summer, some residue all year
- Berries: late summer-autumn, persist to late winter
- Large seeds (walnuts, acorns): autumn-spring
- Greens and annuals: spring-early summer

### Regular Egg Yield
- Dorkings for winter, heavy breeds for spring, Leghorns for summer
- Pigeon and quail yield most of the year if age classes mixed
- Wyandottes/silky bantams for brooding other species' eggs

## Integration with Other Systems

- Fire control by scratching, raking, grazing
- Glasshouse heating from body heat of birds
- Honey production from forage crop flowers
- Stored food for larger stock (goats)
- Home orchard production
- Seed production for store/sale
- Manurial waste for composting
- Pest control (grasshoppers, snails) as garden barrier
- Evolution to large-animal (cattle, deer) forage system

## Predator Control
- Tree-forage system allows escape into shrubberies/trees
- Hens as "baits" for ground predators; sheds fitted with ground-level live-traps for foxes
- Large pigeon flocks in secure raised lofts largely immune from predation

## Poultry as Weeders
- Oxalis: eagerly eaten by hens, pest to small-fruit growers
- Shepherds Purse: "stimulating effect on egg production"
- Cleavers: "love the seed and readily consume the whole plant" — iron and iodine content
- Plantain: absorbs chicken manure, produces green fodder; eagerly sought by ducks
- Poultry will kill out persistent selected herbs over time
