---# House Design for Climate Zones

[[bill-mollison]]'s Permaculture: A Designers' Manual (Chapter 14, The Built
Environment) provides detailed guidance on designing houses adapted to
specific climate zones. The core principle is that building form, materials,
orientation, and ventilation strategies must respond directly to local
climatic conditions rather than importing inappropriate designs.

## Tropical House Design (Wet and Dry Tropics)

In hot humid tropical climates, the primary design challenges are excessive
solar gain, high humidity, and the need for constant air movement. Mollison
emphasises the following design strategies:

- High-pitched roofs with large overhangs to shed heavy monsoon rain and
  provide deep shade to walls and windows
- Elevated floors on stilts or piers to promote under-floor airflow and
  avoid flooding, dampness, and ground-dwelling insects
- Open wall construction using louvres, screens, or adjustable shutters
  rather than glass, maximising cross-ventilation
- Roof vents or vented ridgelines to allow hot air to escape by stack
  effect, drawing cooler air through lower openings
- Lightweight materials (bamboo, thatch, timber) that do not store heat
  and cool rapidly at night
- Deep verandahs on all sides creating a transitional zone between
  interior and exterior living space

The tropical house is fundamentally open and permeable. Mollison notes that
European-style enclosed houses in the tropics become intolerably hot and
promote mould growth. The building envelope should be as open as privacy
and rain protection allow.
