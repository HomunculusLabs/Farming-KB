# Maitake Fruiting Body Success Rate and Environmental Management

### Humidity Thresholds

| Condition | Relative Humidity | Effect |
|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| Optimal fruiting | 85-95% | Normal development |
| Below 80% | Drought risk | Death of fruiting bodies |
| Near 100% | Excess moisture | Rot and bacterial growth |

Young fruiting bodies are especially vulnerable to drought stress.
Any period of low humidity can cause irreversible damage to
developing clusters.

## [[mushroom-primordia-formation-triggers]] success.

### Light [[fruiting-body-development]], with 2000-5000 ppm (0.2-0.5%)
  tolerated during stem development.
- **Chinese practice**: High O2 maintained with bag tops opened
  and ventilation 5-6 times per day.

### Effects of Oxygen Deficiency

- **Low O2 with high CO2**: Produces antler-form fruiting bodies
  instead of normal capped fruiting clusters.
- **Extreme O2 deficiency**: Completely arrests fruiting body
  development and leads to decay of the substrate block.

## Temperature and Bag Opening

The fruiting temperature range for maitake is notably narrow:
8-15 C (home), 10-15.6 C up to 18 C (North American),
16-18 C (Japanese, strict 2 C tolerance), and 15-20 C (Chinese).
Substrate temperature runs 2-3 C higher than ambient air due to
fungal biomass respiration. Temperature fluctuation beyond optimal
range arrests fruiting body differentiation.

For bag opening: transfer bags to the fruiting environment, allow
2-3 days for adjustment, then open tops or cut slits for oxygen.
Never remove the entire bag; retain it to preserve moisture.

## Increasing Yield

Fruiting efficiency is generally low (1.1-2.2 kg or 4.4 kg per
bag). Yield can be improved through larger substrate volumes
(15.4 kg vs 11 kg), soil casing, and second flush generation by
embedding spent substrate in sawdust covered with soil.
