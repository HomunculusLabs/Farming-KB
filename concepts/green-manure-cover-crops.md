## Green Manure Cover Crops

Green manure is a crop grown specifically to be dug back into the soil, adding [[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]].

## Types of Green Manure Crops

### Nitrogen-Fixing Crops

| Crop | Scientific Name | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------|
| Alfalfa (Lucerne) | Medicago sativa | Deep-rooted perennial. Sow spring or late summer, dig in fall or spring. 1/2 oz per sq yd. |
| Broad/Fava Bean | Vicia faba | Excellent all-round crop. Withstands winter. Sow fall or early summer. Beans can be eaten. |
| Red Clover | Trifolium pratense | Low-growing, extensive root system. Sow spring or late summer (not fall). 1 oz per sq yd. |
| Lupin | Lupinus angustifolius | Deep-rooting, adds nitrogen and phosphates. Sow spring. Space 4in in rows 12in apart. |
| Winter Tare | Vicia villosa | Grows in winter when land is vacant. Tall, extensive roots, fixes nitrogen. Sow late summer. |

### Non Nitrogen-Fixing Crops

| Crop | Scientific Name | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------|
| Buckwheat | Fagopyrum esculentum | Tall, extensive roots. Attracts hoverflies. Sow [[query-how-to-fix-nitrogen-toxicity-in-cannabis]]. |
| Phacelia | Phacelia tanacetifolia | One of the best despite not fixing nitrogen. Fast growing. Dig in after 8 weeks. |
| Mustard | Sinapsis alba | Quick, shallow-rooting. Good weed suppressor. Beware: member of cabbage family — may harbor clubroot. |
| Rye | Secale cereale | Extensive root system. Sow perennial variety late summer/fall. Non-legume. |
| Italian Ryegrass | Lolium multiflorum | Fast-growing, bulky. Use only annual "Westerwolds" strain. Dig in before seeding. |

## Sowing and Incorporating

### Sowing

The soil needs to be in good condition for the crop to succeed. Pre-sowing cultivation should be as thorough as for any other crop. Small seeds can be sown in rows 6in (15cm) apart or scattered by hand and raked in. Large seeds go in rows 12in (30cm) apart.

### Digging In

Do not let the crop become too woody before digging in, or the rotting process will take nitrogen from the soil. Low-growing crops can be cut down with a spade and left to wilt for a few days, then dug in. Taller plants can be worked into the surface with a rotary cultivator.

Key points:
- Cut plants at the base and leave to wilt before digging
- Do not bury material deeper than about 6in (15cm)
