# Polyculture Design Principles

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Polyculture is the practice of growing multiple plant species together in the same space and time, mimicking natural plant communities to increase yields, reduce pests, and build soil health. Polycultures are a fundamental tool in permaculture design.

## Definition and Rationale

Traditional societies have long used polycultures, but most employ plants unfamiliar to [[holmgren-ecological-succession-and-pulsing]]
- Provide a lengthy and varied harvest from minimal area
- Are more resistant to [[comparison-season-extension-techniques-vs-cold-frame-gardening]].

## Ianto Evans's Design Guidelines

1. **Seed several varieties of each species** to lengthen harvest season, provide information on best types, and more fully occupy ecological niches
2. **Don't sow seed too thickly** -- reduce to about 80% of suggested rate since you'll be eating nearly every plant
3. **Begin harvest early** -- harvest plants especially greens when they begin to crowd, not when mature
4. **Mix plant families, not just species** -- closely related plants compete for the same nutrients
5. **Include many fast-growing, shallow-rooted species** (radishes, mustard greens, fenugreek, buckwheat) to cover soil quickly and thwart weeds
6. **Overlap harvests** -- plant several varieties of each species with different ripening times; blend fast with slow growers
7. **Avoid root and light competition** -- sprawling plants like tomatoes and potatoes may shade out companions; preponderance of root crops competes for soil space
8. **Harvest whole plants** -- pull up entire plants to make room for other contenders; harvest from densest zones first
9. **Save a few plants for seed** -- leave healthiest individuals to go to seed on the north side of the bed
10. **Examine your polyculture every day** -- things happen fast; after about three weeks, polyculture reaches maximum density and needs daily harvest

## Designing Your Own Polycultures

### Key Questions
- What climate zone and season are you designing for?
- What is the mature size and shape of each variety?
- What are the root patterns (deep, shallow, spreading)?
- What nutrients does each species need vs. contribute?
- Which species attract [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]]?
- Which species deter pests?
- What is the harvest timing for each species?
