# Holmgren Tripartite Altruism

Updated: 2026-04-19

Tripartite altruism is Howard T. Odum's concept adopted by David Holmgren in *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* (2002) to explain how energy flows through ecosystems and societies, requiring feedback mechanisms of altruism at multiple levels for system health.

## Origin and Definition

The concept appears in Holmgren's discussion of [[holmgren-self-regulation-feedback-detailed]] and [[permaculture-principle-apply-self-regulation]]. In nature and in low-energy pre-industrial societies, tripartite altruism is set into hierarchical structures. Those at the start of the energy chain (producers) are numerous but have little individual power, while those at the end of the chain (consumers) are less numerous but more powerful.

The concept is illustrated by Odum's diagram of tripartite altruism for distribution of available energy, which shows how energy flowing through any system requires feedback loops at multiple levels to maintain system health and prevent destructive accumulation or depletion.

## The Three Levels

The tripartite structure operates through three domains of altruistic feedback:

1. **Contribution to the system** — producers at the base (peasants, workers, plants) generate the energy and resources that flow upward through the hierarchy

2. **Redistribution of surplus** — intermediate levels (trades, professions) process and redistribute energy, returning some benefit to lower levels

3. **Guidance and control for long-term system health** — elites at the top (spiritual/temporal leaders, royalty) have a traditional role of providing control and guidance for the long-term good of the whole system

Each level must contribute altruistically to the health of the whole system for it to function sustainably. Without this multi-level feedback, energy accumulates at certain points and the system becomes unstable.

## Application to Modern Society

Holmgren provides examples: a worker who devotes their best energy to productive work rather than selfish pursuit, an entrepreneur who invests in the future, and a citizen who pays taxes to ensure the wider society they depend on continues to function. All these reflect Odum's tripartite altruism.

The modern economy, despite its complexity, fails to provide critically important feedback signals to ensure appropriate behaviour. Key unanswered questions include: how much is enough to consume, how much work is enough, how should we pay, and what should we contribute to the greater good?

## Failure of Self-Regulation by Elites

A critical problem identified by Holmgren is that elites begin to believe the whole social ecosystem exists simply for their benefit. This corrupts the process of acknowledging higher power (god or nature) and fails to provide functional feedback control to keep the system healthy. This breakdown of self-regulation and tripartite altruism at the highest levels tends to cascade back through the social hierarchy until corruption at all levels leads to breakdown and reform.

This connects directly to [[holmgren-ethics-cultural-mechanisms]], where Holmgren argues that ethics function as cultural feedback mechanisms to maintain system health. When elites abandon their altruistic role, the ethical mechanism fails at the most critical level.

## Connection to Energy Hierarchy

The concept is inseparable from the [[holmgren-trophic-pyramid-energy-hierarchy]] model. The trophic pyramid in ecosystems and traditional societies shows each level founded and dependent on the level below. All levels are functional and complementary; none is more important than another, but individuals at lower levels have less influence and power than those at higher levels.

This hierarchical model of energy flows through systems is one of the most fundamental insights from systems ecology that permaculture draws upon. It explains why all levels of society are necessary and why each must contribute appropriately to the whole.

## Practical Application

Holmgren suggests one concrete application: allocating one-third of time to providing for material needs, one-third to self-development and reflection, and one-third to wider societal benefit. This reflects the tripartite structure in personal life design.

This practical allocation echoes the structure of [[holmgren-permaculture-ethics-foundations]]: Care for Earth (one-third), Care for Self (one-third), and Redistribute Surplus (one-third). The tripartite model thus connects the ethical framework to daily practice.

## Elite Isolation from Consequences

Holmgren notes that a high-energy society develops so many hierarchical levels that all are occupied by fallible humans who lack the wisdom for those levels. People at the highest levels function as self-reinforcing communities, stratospherically removed from the effects of their actions on lower levels. This isolation prevents the feedback essential for tripartite altruism to function.

Since the European Enlightenment, social democratic, Marxist, and other ideologies have sought to overcome this elite problem, but the historical record of achieving effective guidance and wisdom without corruption has not been great.

## Permaculture as Bottom-Up Feedback

Permaculture's approach of [[holmgren-top-down-thinking-bottom-up-action]] can be understood as an attempt to restore functional feedback at all levels of society. By starting from household and local community design, permaculture rebuilds the energy hierarchy from the base up, ensuring that producers are empowered and that feedback flows work effectively.

## Altruism and True Giving

In both the social and the natural domain, how to give in ways that show true altruism has always been problematic, but in the modern world it is an ethical question of great magnitude and complexity. Many social and environmental good works can be seen as atonement for collective sins rather than necessarily providing what people or nature really need. Sometimes giving is a disguised form of self-interest that creates dependency rather than building capacity.

## Relevance to Energy Descent

Within the holmgren tripartite altruism framework, restoring functional tripartite altruism becomes increasingly urgent. As energy descent reduces the surplus available for elite consumption, the feedback mechanisms that bind social hierarchies together will need to be rebuilt from the bottom up. Permaculture design at the household and community level provides practical templates for this rebuilding.

# Holmgren Energy Descent Mountain Peak

Updated: 2026-04-18

The energy descent concept is the central analytical framework of David Holmgren's *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* (2002). Holmgren describes it as "the most important element of my book" and uses it to frame all twelve permaculture design principles.

## The Mountain Peak Model

The energy descent model envisions human energy use (primarily fossil fuel) as following the shape of a mountain peak: rising steeply over the past two centuries of industrial growth, reaching a summit, and then declining. The descent side is not a mirror of the ascent — the social, ecological, and economic conditions will be fundamentally different from those of the growth phase.

## Conscious Design of the Descent Pathway

Holmgren's key contribution is the idea that the descent pathway can be consciously designed. He writes: "Permaculture uses these conditions to consciously design our energy descent pathway." This means using permaculture principles to anticipate and prepare for declining energy availability rather than being swept along by chaotic collapse.

## Historical Energy Transitions

Holmgren frames human history through energy transitions:

- **Hunter-gatherer societies** — natural environment provided all material needs; human effort mainly required for harvesting
- **Pre-industrial agricultural societies** — high population densities required large and continuous input of human labour
- **Industrial society** — depends on large and continuous inputs of fossil fuel energy

Permaculture-designed systems differ from all three by being information and design intensive rather than labour intensive or energy intensive.

## Two Scenarios

Holmgren contrasts two broad responses to energy descent:

1. **Techno-explosion** — belief that new energy sources and technologies will maintain growth
2. **Green technology stability** — faith that efficiency improvements and renewable energy can maintain current lifestyles

He argues both are unrealistic and that a genuine energy descent pathway must accept declining net energy and redesign systems accordingly.

## Speed of Descent

The rate of descent is critically important. A rapid, chaotic descent leads to collapse scenarios. A slower, managed descent allows for adaptive redesign of infrastructure, institutions, and culture. Permaculture design aims to facilitate the slowest practical descent by building resilient local systems.
