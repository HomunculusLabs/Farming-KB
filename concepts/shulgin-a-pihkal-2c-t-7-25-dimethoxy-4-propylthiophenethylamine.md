# 2C-T-7 (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Propylthiophenethylamine)

## Chemical Identity

2C-T-7 is compound #43 in PiHKAL, the common name for 2,5-dimethoxy-4-(n)-propylthiophenethylamine hydrochloride. It belongs to the 2C-T subfamily of phenethylamines, characterized by a sulfur-containing alkylthio group at the 4-position of the 2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine core. The propylthio chain gives 2C-T-7 its distinctive pharmacological profile among the thiophenethylamines.

### METHYL-2C-T-7

An attempt was made to create an N-methyl-N-isopropyl analogue by reductive alkylation of the N-methyl formamide with acetone and sodium cyanoborohydride. The METHYL-2C-T-7 product was obtained but only 65 mg of impure brown hydrochloride was produced, and the project was abandoned due to insufficient material.

## Safety Considerations

- The 8-15 hour duration demands a full day commitment and appropriate preparation
- Periodic nausea is a consistent feature at 20 mg doses
- Muscle tension (neck, shoulders, legs) is commonly reported and may be uncomfortable
- The emotional depth and processing capacity of 2C-T-7 can surface difficult psychological material — appropriate support should be available
- The steep dose range and long duration make accurate dosing critical
- The 2CT7-2ETO aftereffect of next-day headache suggests that even structural analogues may carry unexpected toxicity
- 2C-T-7 has been associated with fatalities in non-controlled settings, often involving combinations with other substances or unsafe environments
- As with all 2C-T compounds, the sulfur-containing group may introduce unique metabolic pathways and potential interactions not seen with simpler 2C analogues
