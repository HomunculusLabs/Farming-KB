# Beta-Carboline Chemistry

Beta-carbolines (9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indoles) are a family of tricyclic compounds formed by the condensation of tryptamine derivatives with aldehydes or ketones, producing a fused ring system incorporating the original indole plus a third nitrogen-containing ring. In [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]], three beta-carbolines are documented as individual entries: harmaline (#13), harmine (#14), and tetrahydroharmine. These compounds are central to [[ayahuasca-preparation-and-ceremony]] pharmacology and serve as the oldest known monoamine oxidase inhibitors used by humans.

## Chemical Structure

The beta-carboline skeleton arises from the Pictet-Spengler condensation of a tryptamine with an aldehyde or alpha-hydroxyaldehyde, forming a new carbon-carbon bond between the alpha-carbon of the tryptamine side chain and the carbonyl carbon of the aldehyde. This creates a tetrahydro-beta-carboline (when fully saturated at the new ring's C1 position) or a dihydro-beta-carboline (when unsaturated). Further oxidation or dehydrogenation yields the fully aromatic beta-carboline system.

Three categories are recognized by their degree of saturation:

- **Tetrahydro-beta-carbolines** (fully saturated at C1): harmaline, tetrahydroharmine, and their relatives
- **Dihydro-beta-carbolines** (partially unsaturated): intermediates in the formation of fully aromatic forms
- **Fully aromatic beta-carbolines**: harmine and related compounds

## Harmaline (TIHKAL #13)

Synthesis from 6-methoxytryptamine and glycolaldehyde: The aldehyde condenses with the primary amine of the tryptamine, forming 1-hydroxymethyl-7-methoxy-tetrahydro-beta-carboline, which is dehydrated with phosphoric acid to yield the final product.

**Dosage**: 150-300 mg oral
**Duration**: 5-8 hours

Effects include visual tracers, shimmer, and a general "clouding" of consciousness. Shulgin notes dietary concerns associated with MAO inhibition and provides detailed reports of harmaline combined with [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]]:

- 80 mg harmaline + 40 mg DMT: mild kaleidoscopic visuals without loss of identity or reality contact
- 100 mg harmaline + 120 mg DMT: more intense kaleidoscopic visuals, still without the total ego dissolution characteristic of high-dose smoked DMT

The harmaline-DMT combination data from TIHKAL constitutes some of the most detailed quantitative documentation of the ayahuasca experience available in the scientific literature.

## Harmine (TIHKAL #14)

Synthesis from harmaline hydrochloride via HNO3 oxidation: The tetrahydro compound is oxidized to the fully aromatic beta-carboline.

**Dosage**: Greater than 300 mg oral (essentially inactive as a psychedelic at any oral dose)
**Duration**: Variable

Shulgin's data on harmine reveals a surprisingly subtle pharmacological profile:

- 35-40 mg oral: restlessness and excitation without visual effects
- Greater than 40 mg: belligerence reported
- 140 mg oral: mild sedation only
- 150-200 mg IV: visual hallucinations in 5 of 11 research subjects

This entry contains Shulgin's pedagogical "tennis ball" isomer analogy, explaining how carbon chain branching (n-butyl, isobutyl, sec-butyl, tert-butyl) creates molecules with identical atomic composition but dramatically different three-dimensional shapes and correspondingly different pharmacological properties. This serves as a foundational illustration of 5-methoxy-compounds|structure-activity relationship principles.

## Tetrahydroharmine

The partially saturated form, present in Banisteriopsis caapi bark at concentrations often exceeding those of harmine and harmaline. While not given its own numbered entry in TIHKAL, tetrahydroharmine is noted as a weak serotonin reuptake inhibitor (rather than a potent MAOI like harmine and harmaline), contributing additional serotonergic modulation to the ayahuasca experience.

## Pharmacological Significance

The beta-carbolines in ayahuasca serve two critical functions:

1. **MAO inhibition**: Harmine and harmaline are reversible inhibitors of monoamine oxidase type A (MAO-A), preventing the oxidative deamination of DMT in the gut and liver, thereby enabling oral activity of a compound that would otherwise be completely inactive when swallowed
2. **Direct psychoactivity**: At sufficient doses (particularly IV), harmine produces visual hallucinations of its own, though harmaline's contribution is primarily a somatic-intoxicant quality (tracers, clouding)

## Natural Occurrence

Beta-carbolines are found in:

- Banisteriopsis caapi (ayahuasca vine): harmine, harmaline, tetrahydroharmine -- see [[harmala-alkaloids-and-ayahuasca-preparation]]
- Peganum harmala (Syrian rue): harmine and harmaline in concentrations up to 3-7% of seed weight
- Passiflora incarnata (passionflower): trace amounts of harmala alkaloids
- Human tissues: endogenous beta-carbolines (pinoline, harmane) are found in the pineal gland and brain
