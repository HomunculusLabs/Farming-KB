# Beta-Carboline Chemistry

Beta-carbolines (9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indoles) are a family of tricyclic compounds formed by the condensation of tryptamine derivatives with aldehydes or ketones, producing a fused ring system incorporating the original indole plus a third nitrogen-containing ring. In  pharmacology and serve as the oldest known monoamine oxidase inhibitors used by humans.

## Chemical Structure

The beta-carboline skeleton arises from the Pictet-Spengler condensation of a tryptamine with an aldehyde or alpha-hydroxyaldehyde, forming a new carbon-carbon bond between the alpha-carbon of the tryptamine side chain and the carbonyl carbon of the aldehyde. This creates a tetrahydro-beta-carboline (when fully saturated at the new ring's C1 position) or a dihydro-beta-carboline (when unsaturated). Further oxidation or dehydrogenation yields the fully aromatic beta-carboline system.

Three categories are recognized by their degree of saturation:

- **Tetrahydro-beta-carbolines** (fully saturated at C1): harmaline, tetrahydroharmine, and their relatives
- **Dihydro-beta-carbolines** (partially unsaturated): intermediates in the formation of fully aromatic forms
- **Fully aromatic beta-carbolines**: harmine and related compounds

## Harmaline (TIHKAL #13)
