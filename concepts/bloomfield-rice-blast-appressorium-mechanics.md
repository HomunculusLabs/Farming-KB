## Genome and Genetics

Magnaporthe has been studied intensively by geneticists for many years.
It possesses seven chromosomes containing about three times more DNA
than saccharomyces-cerevisiae and 80 times less than a human. The
relationship between the amount of DNA in each nucleus and the
complexity of an organism is quite messy; many plants and amphibians
have much more DNA than humans.

The reason so much effort is being expended on sequencing the rice
blast genome is that there is a high probability of finding distinctive
Magnaporthe genes whose products have not been found in any other
organisms. Because Magnaporthe has been engaged as a cereal predator
for millions of years, we can anticipate genes that orchestrate the
intimate relationship between the fungus and its hosts: genes that
control the timing of conidium germination and melanin synthesis,
genes turned on only when the fungus penetrates the epidermis, and
others expressed when it begins to feed on the cytoplasm.

## O-Ring Glue Secretion

Once the appressorium has stuck itself to the leaf by secreting an
"O-ring" of glue, it deposits a veneer of pigment molecules against

pressurization process, preventing the structure from being pushed away
