# Earth Constructs — Banks and Raised Structures

Wherever earth is dug, banks are raised. While modern practice concentrates heavily on holes (mining pits, foundations, dams) and far less on mounds, the spoil from excavation can become a valuable resource rather than a pollutant — provided topsoil is first removed and then returned to cover the spoil. Earth banks and raised structures serve multiple productive functions in [[dam-construction]] for refuge purposes

## Rail, Canal, and Road Grades

Common banks and cuttings for transportation infrastructure are well-known earthworks that, with permaculture-informed design, can integrate productive functions rather than serving single-purpose transportation goals.

## Earth Ramps and Stands

Earth ramps and stands serve multiple permanent functions:

- **Unloading trucks** from the side or back (heavy objects and wheeled vehicles)
- **Loading cattle** to trucks at various levels
- **Unloading hay or bales** into upper floors of barns for use at lower levels
- **Loading and unloading boats** into water

Once built, they are permanent in use, and sometimes a whole district can share one loading/unloading ramp. The **loading face must be stabilized** with stone, concrete, or beams.

## Earth Walls and "Ha-Ha" Fences

The **ha-ha** is a below-grade ditch acting as a fence — a deep pit (dry or wet) with one steep wall faced by stone. Applications include:

- **Classical vista gardens** where views are to remain uninterrupted
- **Zoos** for direct viewing of large and potentially dangerous animals
- **Village defense** against stock where resources don't allow wire fences but labour or a machine is available

The ha-ha can be scaled to size for the species excluded.

## Road Enhancement

Roads represent a major opportunity for integrated design:

- Roadsides can preserve **bunchgrasses, sagebrushes, rangeland and meadow plants**, and remnant forests
- **Pull-over areas** with vegetation maps, geological features, and archaeological or fossil remains
- **Heat pipes** under bitumen surfaces could heat towns through which roads pass
- **Road run-off harvesting** for local irrigation and wetlands

Such planning must precede actual construction. The same machines that build roads can also build wetlands, swales, small dams for wildfire control and wildlife, underpasses, and guide fences for migrating wildlife.
