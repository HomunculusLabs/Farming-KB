# Harvesting and Storage in The Mushroom Cultivator

Source: The Mushroom Cultivator (Stamets & Chilton, 1983), Chapter X
Updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

Harvesting technique and timing dramatically affect future flushes and total yield. The Mushroom Cultivator covers detailed harvesting methods, post-harvest handling, and multiple [[biodiversity-of-fungi-fungal-culture-preservation-techniques]] for home and commercial cultivators.

## Harvesting

### Timing

The most important factor in harvesting is timing:
- **[[agaricus-brunnescens]]**: Pick before the veil breaks and the stem elongates
- **[[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]**: Pick when the veil begins to tear or soon thereafter
- Massive [[mushroom-veil-function-wind-light-protection-prior-to-spore-release]] from mature mushrooms can cover the [[gourmet-medicinal-mushroom-casing-layer-techniques]] and inhibit further pinhead development
- High spore loads can also cause allergic reactions among workers

### Harvesting Technique

The way mushrooms are picked can dramatically affect future flushes. Damage to resting pinheads and disturbance of the casing soil must be minimized.

**Step-by-step procedure:**
1. Equipped with a basket and short-bladed paring knife, grasp the base of the stem
2. With a twisting motion, pull the mushroom from the casing layer -- careful not to disturb neighboring pinheads
3. Trim the stem base, removing only flesh to which casing or substrate is attached
4. Mushrooms with thin stems: clean using a knife in a downward scraping motion
5. All trimmings placed in sealed plastic bag and removed from cropping area

**Clump harvesting:**
- Mushrooms growing in clusters or clusters should be broken apart and harvested individually when possible
- Special care with clumps containing both mature and immature mushrooms
- Leave immature mushrooms attached to the casing layer to insure continued growth

### Between-Flush Management

- Flushes come in 7-10 day intervals, each successive flush bearing fewer mushrooms
- Keep harvest period short and concise per flush -- the sooner a flush is completely removed, the quicker the next appears
- Late developing mushrooms removed on or after peak production day
- Stunted undeveloped mushrooms cleared between breaks -- do not disturb the casing
- Small dead pinheads left in place cause little harm
- Never over-handle the casing in an attempt to clean -- this spreads disease spores

## Watering During Cropping

### Before Button Stage
- Other than light mistings, no substantial waterings before button stage (damages pins)
- Mushrooms on uncased substrates require 90-95% humidity and periodic misting

### After Button Stage
- Begin building [[mushroom-casing-moisture-management]] back up to pre-pinning peak
- Daily light to moderate waterings with fine misting nozzle
- Water 2-4 times per day rather than once -- prevents panning
- Goal: reach moisture capacity just prior to main harvest
- Avoid high water pressures and close nozzle proximity to casing

### Between Flushes
- After first flush: keep casing moist with light mistings until next flush reaches button stage
- Build moisture up again for each flush
- [[psilocin-emergence-in-later-flushes]] need less water (fewer mushrooms)
- Never allow casing to dry out -- dried casing causes mushrooms to carry large chunks when picked

### Disease and Watering
- [[growing-gourmet-bacterial-blotch-disease-management]] results from caps remaining wet for extended periods
- Agaricus growers dry recently watered caps quickly by lowering humidity
- Stop watering once mushroom cap reaches adolescence
- Do not water contaminated areas -- this spreads infection
- For serious outbreaks: lower relative humidity, run casing drier than normal
- Slightly chlorinated water (150 ppm) can be used

## Preserving Mushrooms

If not served within four days, mushrooms can be preserved by drying, freezing, or canning.

### Air Drying (Most Common for Home Cultivators)

- Most mushrooms are 90% water and must be dried within a few hours or fly larvae and bacteria will consume them
- Placed in a flow of warm, dry air, the water soon evaporates
- Dried mushrooms are smaller, lighter, and less fragrant than fresh ones
