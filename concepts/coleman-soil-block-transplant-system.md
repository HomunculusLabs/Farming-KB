# Soil Block Transplant System — Growing Seedlings Without Plastic Pots

## Overview

The soil block system grows seedlings in cubes of compressed potting mix rather than plastic pots or cell trays. Developed in Europe and refined by Eliot Coleman for American organic growers, soil blocks eliminate the need for plastic containers, reduce [[clay-seed-pellets-direct-seeding]]
- **4-inch blocks**: For large transplants requiring extended greenhouse time

The indentation (or "dimple") in the top of each block serves two purposes: it provides a recessed spot for sowing seeds, and it accepts smaller blocks for potting-on without disturbing roots.

## Advantages Over Plastic Pots

### Root Development

In plastic pots, seedling roots reach the container wall and begin circling—a condition called root-bound. Circling roots must be untangled or cut at transplanting, causing transplant shock. In soil blocks, roots reach the air at the block surface and are naturally "air-pruned." This stops root extension and stimulates lateral branching within the block, producing a dense, fibrous root system that establishes rapidly when planted out.

### Elimination of Plastic

Soil blocks eliminate the need for plastic pots, cell packs, and inserts. This reduces:

- Upfront costs for containers
- Storage space for stacks of pots between seasons
