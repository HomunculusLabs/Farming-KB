# Row Cover Fabric Management

Floating row covers are a critical component of Eliot Coleman's winter
harvest system. These spun-bonded polyester or polypropylene fabrics
provide a secondary layer of protection within high tunnels, adding
several degrees of [[permaculture-frost-protection-strategies]] while allowing light, water, and
air to pass through to the crop below.

## Types of Row Cover Fabric

Row covers are available in several weights, each offering different
levels of protection and light transmission. Coleman categorizes them
by weight in ounces per square yard:
- **Lightweight (0.5 oz/sq yd):** Provides 2 to 4 degrees of frost
  protection with approximately 85 to 90 percent light transmission.
  Used primarily for insect exclusion in summer and mild frost
  protection in fall and spring.
- **Medium weight (1.0 oz/sq yd):** Provides 4 to 6 degrees of
  protection with approximately 70 percent light transmission. This
  is Coleman's standard choice for winter production inside high
  tunnels.
- **Heavyweight (1.5 oz/sq yd):** Provides 6 to 8 degrees of
  protection with approximately 50 percent light transmission. Used
  for the most extreme cold events but removed promptly when
  temperatures moderate to prevent [[light-deprivation-cannabis]].

The medium-weight cover is the workhorse of the winter system. It
provides meaningful frost protection without significantly reducing
light, which is already scarce during winter months.

## Securing and Installation Methods

Proper installation of row covers is essential for effective
protection. Coleman uses several methods to secure covers:
- **Wire hoops:** 9-gauge galvanized wire bent into low hoops
  spaced every 4 to 5 feet along the bed. The row cover drapes
  over these hoops, creating a mini-tunnel that prevents the
  fabric from resting directly on the crop.
- **Sandbags and soil:** The edges of the row cover are buried
  in soil or weighted with sandbags to seal the perimeter and
  prevent wind from lifting the fabric.
- **Snap clamps:** On high tunnel structures, snap clamps or
  poly clips attach the row cover directly to the greenhouse
  bows, creating a double-layer system.

Wire hoops are the preferred method for individual bed protection
within a high tunnel. They keep the fabric elevated above the crop,
preventing abrasion damage and creating a small air space that
improves insulation value. The hoops also make it easier to lift
the cover for harvesting and maintenance.

## Light Transmission Considerations

Light is the most limiting factor in winter production. Every layer
of covering reduces light reaching the crop. Coleman's high tunnel
with double-inflated poly transmits approximately 60 to 70 percent
of ambient light. Adding a medium-weight row cover brings total
transmission down to approximately 40 to 50 percent.

This is sufficient for cold-hardy greens during winter because their
growth rate is already limited by low temperatures and short day
length. However, row covers should be removed during warm, sunny
periods whenever possible to maximize light exposure. Coleman
monitors forecasts and removes covers when nighttime temperatures
are above the frost threshold for the crop in question.

## Frost Protection Ratings

The temperature protection provided by row covers depends on the
weight of the fabric and the conditions under which it is deployed.
Key factors include:
- **Wind speed:** Calm conditions provide the best insulation.
  Windy conditions strip heat from the covered area rapidly.
- **Soil heat:** Well-prepared beds with high organic matter
  radiate more stored heat. See [[coleman-soil-preparation-for-winter]].
- **Humidity:** Moist air within the cover acts as a thermal buffer,
  but excessive condensation can promote disease.

In calm, still conditions, a medium-weight row cover over crops
inside a high tunnel can provide protection down to approximately 0
degrees Fahrenheit for the hardiest species. Combined protection
from both layers creates a microclimate that is significantly warmer
than ambient conditions.

## Pest Exclusion Benefits

Beyond frost protection, row covers serve as physical barriers to
insect pests. In early spring and fall, floating row covers exclude
flea beetles, aphids, cabbage worms, and other common pests from
brassica and leafy green crops. This eliminates the need for
insecticides in the winter production system.

For pest exclusion, the cover must be sealed completely at the edges
with no gaps. Even small openings allow pests to enter and colonize
the protected area. Coleman recommends burying edges in soil rather
than simply weighting them with sandbags for the most effective seal.

## When to Remove Row Covers

Row covers should be removed or lifted under several conditions:
- **Warm sunny days:** To prevent heat buildup and maximize light.
- **During harvest:** To allow full access to the crop.
- **For pollination:** When crops are flowering and require insect
  access.
- **When pest pressure is absent:** To reduce disease risk from
  trapped moisture.
- **Periodically for weed management:** To allow hoeing or flame
  weeding between rows.

Coleman trains farm workers to check row covers daily and adjust them
based on weather conditions. The ability to quickly remove and replace
covers is a key logistical consideration in the winter harvest system.

## Durability and Lifespan

Quality row covers can last 3 to 5 seasons with proper handling.
Coleman recommends folding and storing covers during the off-season,
out of direct sunlight, to extend their lifespan. Small tears should
be repaired with row cover tape or a simple whip stitch to prevent
them from spreading.

## Integration with High Tunnels

Within a high tunnel, row covers create a secondary protected zone.
This double-layer approach is central to Coleman's winter production
philosophy. See [[coleman-high-tunnel-cold-frame-design]] for the
primary structure and [[coleman-moveable-greenhouse-system]] for how
multiple layers of protection work together across different growing
locations.
