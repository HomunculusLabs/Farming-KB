# JADAM Water Softener (JWS)

Hard water containing calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), and iron (Fe) is the single biggest enemy of [[ultra-low-jadam-wetting-agent-jwa]]. JWS uses ion-exchange resin to strip these minerals from water, producing soft water that produces vigorous foam and clear solution when mixed with JWA. Without soft water, pesticide will clot, leave marks on produce, and have greatly reduced effectiveness.

## Why Soft Water Matters

- JWA is a soap (saponified oil). Soap reacts with Ca/Mg/Fe in hard water to form insoluble precipitates -- this is the "clotting" or murkiness you see
- Clotted pesticide leaves marks on fruits and leaves, causing concentration stress and reduced quality
- Soft water mixed with JWA stays completely clear and produces abundant foam -- this foam IS the pesticide's wetting power
- Wetting power determines whether pesticide coats the pest thoroughly; without it, pest control fails regardless of active ingredients
- Even synthetic chemical pesticides are affected by hard water (less foam = less wetting)

## How to Test for Soft Water

1. Take a sample of water in a clear container
2. Add a few drops of JWA
3. Shake vigorously
4. **Good (soft) water**: stays completely clear, produces lots of foam that persists
5. **Bad (hard) water**: turns murky/cloudy like milk, little or no foam
6. Gradations exist -- the clearer and foamier, the better

Use only water that passes this test for making JWA, boiling JHS, culturing JMS, and mixing all [[query-how-do-i-make-jadam-natural-pesticide-jnp]].

## JADAM Water Softener Specifications

- Dimensions: 150 x 1100 mm (6 x 43.3 in)
- Weight: 27 kg (60 lb)
- Ion-exchange resin: 17 L (4.5 gal)
- Capacity: 1 L resin softens approximately 1 T (264 gal) of water
- Total capacity per charge: ~15 T (4,000 gal) of soft water
- At 500 L per spray: enough for ~30 applications
- Operating cost: ~$5/month
- Resin lifespan: approximately 1 year

## Installation Steps

1. Fix the water softener upright on a stable stand
2. Connect filter to inlet (port 1). Connect 15 mm (0.6 in) hose to water input (port 2)
3. Clean filter (port 3) frequently with a toothbrush
4. Connect 15 mm hose to water output (port 5)
5. Discard the initial brownish water that comes out
6. Production rate: ~30 minutes for 500 L (132 gal) of soft water

## Salt Water Treatment (Resin Regeneration)

When the JWA test shows murkiness, it is time to regenerate the resin. You can perform salt water treatment approximately 15 times before resin replacement is needed.

**Recipe for salt water:**
- Salt: 2.5 kg (5.5 lb)
- Water: 7 L (1.85 gal)
- Dissolve completely and remove any impurities

**Procedure:**
1. Lower and open hose at port 5; completely drain the device
2. Raise the end of the hose higher than the top of the softener
3. Open port 4 and pour the salt water in
4. Let sit for over 1 hour
5. Lower the hose at port 5 and drain the machine
6. Run clean water through the softener for 10 minutes to wash out salt residue
7. Take 0.5 L of softened water and perform JWA test -- should be clear with lots of foam

## Resin Exchange

When regeneration no longer produces soft water:
1. Open port 5, remove filter, and run water through -- resins will flow out with water
2. Remove all old resin completely
3. Place filter back and seal port 5
4. Open port 4 and pour in 14 kg (31 lb) of new ion-exchange resin (do not fill completely)

## Alternatives to JWS

- **Rainwater**: Collect rainwater 30+ minutes after rain begins (discard first 30 min which contain pollutants). Rainwater is naturally soft. Excellent for all JADAM inputs.
- **Commercial wetting agents**: If you have hard water and cannot install JWS, you can use commercial synthetic wetting agents (check organic certification compatibility). However, JWS at $5/month is far cheaper than commercial wetting agents.

## Relationship to JNP Dosage

The softer the water, the less JWA you need. With very soft water, JWA 3L per 500L may be sufficient. With borderline water, you may need JWA 8-10L. Water quality directly affects the cost-per-spray of your pesticide program.
