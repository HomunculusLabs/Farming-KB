# Sector Analysis in Permaculture Design
 [[permaculture]] sectors are an analytical tool to help understand the flow of forces across a site, using a circular diagram centered on the home to map
energies like sun, wind, fire, and noise that shape the design. Sector analysis is one of the foundational [[permaculture-design]], providing a systematic framework for understanding
external energies that influence the design.

## Understanding Sectors

A sector analysis is presented as a circle over your site, with the circle
centered on your Zone 0, or the center of your lowest zone. Pie slices out
of that circle represent forces that flow across the site. For example, if

## Common Sectors to Analyze

### Fire Hazard

Fire hazard is worth noting on every design, identifying which direction a
wild or other fire would most likely come from. The fire sector combines
topography analysis with prevailing [[wind-patterns-and-beaufort-scale]] to determine the most
likely fire approach direction. Fire travels fastest uphill and with the
wind, so the designer must analyze both slope and wind direction together to
accurately map the fire sector.

### Wind Sectors

Summer wind sector typically captures the predominant wind direction during
summer, with a wedge about 80 degrees wide on the circle. Winter wind sector
captures the typically different predominant wind direction during winter
time, also about 80 degrees wide. Some simple climatological research should
reveal these patterns for any given location.

### Solar Sectors

Summer sun sector denotes the solar azimuth during the summer solstice, with
actual angles matching the real solar azimuth. Winter sun sector denotes the
solar azimuth during the winter solstice, also reflecting actual angles.
These solar sectors are essential for [[passive-solar-design]] decisions
including building orientation, window placement, and greenhouse siting.

### Noise and View Sectors

Noise sectors account for large noise generators near the site such as roads,
neighbors, or commercial areas. Create a noise sector for each source, with
angular width at your discretion. Undesirable views and desirable views
should be noted as sectors so the design can block or preserve sight lines
as appropriate.

## Drawing the Sector Diagram

While sector diagrams can sometimes be frustrating because information visually
overlaps, a recommended approach is to start with a large circle centered on
Zone 0. Then make a number of concentric rings equal to the total number of
sectors you plan on drawing. Starting from the mid point draw the angle
lines out to the appropriate concentric ring and label it.

It is typical to put the summer sun sector on the outermost ring, the winter
sun second, the prevailing winds third, and then the rest in whatever order
suits your fancy. You can either color in the outermost arc of each sector,
or color in the whole wedge. If you choose to color the whole wedge it is
still suggested to first color the arcs then go back to fill in the wedges to
avoid overlapping colors.

## Using Sectors in Design

Before beginning your sector analysis the designer should sit down and make a
list of factors to be analyzed. Once you have the sector analysis complete you
can look at it and let it tell you where various elements should go. Sectors
inform placement decisions to either block a force across your site, allow
that force access, or catch and harness that force.
