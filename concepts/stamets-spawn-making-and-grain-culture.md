Source: Paul Stamets, *The Mushroom Cultivator* (1983)

Grain spawn is the preferred medium for inoculating bulk substrates in most mushroom cultivation. Grain's high nutritional value, small particle size, and loose structure make it ideal for rapid mycelial colonisation.

## Grain Selection

Rye grain is preferred because:
- Does not clump together when cooked
- Individual kernels remain separate after sterilisation
- Excellent nutritional profile for mycelial growth

Other grains used: millet, wheat, sorghum, brown rice, and others. Each has specific characteristics.

## Grain Preparation

1. Measure grain and water in roughly equal volumes (by weight: 1 part grain to 1.5-2 parts water)
2. Boil or simmer grain until it has absorbed water and is soft but not burst
3. Drain excess water and allow grain to steam dry
4. Grain should be dry on the surface but moist within
5. Fill jars 2/3 to 3/4 full
6. Cover with lids fitted with filter patches or tyvek for gas exchange
7. Sterilise at 15 psi for 60-90 minutes

Moisture content is critical -- too wet and bacteria thrive, too dry and mycelium grows slowly. Properly prepared grain yields to a firm squeeze with a few drops between the fingers.

## Inoculation Methods

### Traditional Transfer
Using a flame-sterilised scalpel, transfer agar wedges to the grain jar surface. One petri dish typically inoculates 6-10 quart jars. After inoculation, shake jars to distribute mycelial fragments.

### Liquid Inoculation (Preferred for Scale)
A highly effective technique using suspension of fragmented mycelia in sterile water:
- One plate can inoculate up to 100 jars (10x traditional method)
- Method 1: Inject 30-50ml sterile water into a healthy culture, scrape surface, draw up fragments. As little as 5ml inoculates a quart jar.
- Method 2: Blender with autoclavable container. Cut agar culture into quadrants, blend at high speed for no longer than 5 seconds (longer fractures individual cells, making them inviable). Draw up 5-10ml per jar.
- 10:1 dilution further expands capacity: 50ml concentrate into four vessels of 450ml sterile water, then 10-15ml per grain jar.

Liquid inoculation eliminates repeated shaking but has a critical disadvantage: any contamination on the culture dish will be distributed throughout all inoculated jars. If a dish is contaminated, up to 100 jars could be lost.

## Alternative Spawn Media

### Sawdust/Bran Spawn
For wood-inhabiting species (Lentinus edodes, Flammulina velutipes):
- 4 parts hardwood sawdust
- 1 part rice or wheat bran
- Soak sawdust 24+ hours, drain, mix in bran
- Sterilise 60-90 minutes at 15 psi
- Japanese spawn makers bore a 1/2 inch hole for inoculation

### Perlite Spawn
- 120ml water, 40g perlite, 50g wheat bran, 6g gypsum, 1.5g calcium carbonate
- Screen perlite to remove fine powder
- Mix dry ingredients, add water, mix until thoroughly moistened
- Sterilise 1 hour at 15 psi

## Incubation

- Spawn jars fully colonise in 7 days to 2 weeks after inoculation
- Store on shelves in a semisterile room with controlled temperature
- Light and humidity not important (sealed jar retains moisture)
- Air circulation important only if jars overheat
- Best vegetative growth temperature is several degrees higher than fruiting temperature
- Temperature within a spawn jar is several degrees higher than surrounding air

## Contamination During Incubation

Common contaminants: Penicillium and Aspergillus (green molds). Daily inspection is essential. Not all grain discolorations are contamination -- mushroom mycelium exudes yellowish liquid metabolites (ethanol and acetone). Excessive "sweating" indicates:
1. Incubation at too high a temperature
2. Over-aging of cultures
3. Lack of gas exchange encouraging anaerobic contamination

Contaminated jars should be autoclaved 30 minutes at 15 psi and cleaned soon after. Several contaminants are pathogenic to humans.

## Exponential Expansion

Grain-to-grain transfer enables exponential expansion: 1 petri dish inoculates 10 jars (G-1), which inoculate 100 jars (G-2), which inoculate 1000 jars (G-3) -- provided the culture remains pure. This diagrammatic expansion is the foundation of commercial spawn production.
