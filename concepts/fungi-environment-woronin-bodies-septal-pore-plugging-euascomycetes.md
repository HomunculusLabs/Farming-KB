## Core Concept

[[woronin-bodies]] are dense, spherical organelles unique to filamentous
ascomycetes (euascomycetes) that function as emergency plugs for septal pores.
When a hypha is damaged, Woronin bodies rapidly migrate to the septum and
physically occlude the pore, preventing catastrophic loss of cytoplasm and
preventing the spread of harmful agents (toxins, viruses, organelle-free
cytoplasm) from the damaged compartment to the rest of the mycelium. They

### HEX-1 and Self-Assembly
The HEX-1 protein is the major structural component of the Woronin body
core. It forms hexagonal crystals through self-assembly, creating an
extremely dense, mechanically robust plug. Mutants lacking HEX-1 produce
peroxisomes but not functional Woronin bodies, and these mutants show
dramatically increased sensitivity to hyphal damage — confirming the
protective role of Woronin body plugging.

## Plugging Mechanics

### Response to Damage
When a hyphal compartment is wounded, the pressure differential between
the damaged compartment (which may be losing turgor) and adjacent healthy
compartments drives Woronin body movement toward the septal pore. The
plugging response occurs within seconds of damage, fast enough to prevent
significant cytoplasmic loss.

### Positioning and Anchoring
In undamaged hyphae, Woronin bodies are held in close proximity to septal
pores by anchoring structures. This pre-positioning is critical — it means
the organelle does not need to travel far to reach the pore, enabling the
rapid response time. Some species maintain multiple Woronin bodies per pore,
providing redundancy.

### Seal Permanence
Once plugged, the seal is effectively permanent for that particular pore.
The Woronin body does not detach after the damage is repaired. Instead,
the sealed septum remains, and the damaged compartment is sacrificed.
The colony compensates by growing new hyphae around the damaged area.

## Functional Significance

### Cytoplasmic Retention
The primary function is preventing loss of cytoplasm from healthy
compartments when a neighboring compartment is damaged. In the absence of
Woronin bodies, cytoplasmic streaming through septal pores can drain
multiple compartments of their contents.

### Compartmentalization of Damage
By sealing off damaged compartments, Woronin bodies contain cellular damage
to a single hyphal segment. This is analogous to fire doors in a building
— they sacrifice one compartment to protect the rest of the structure.
