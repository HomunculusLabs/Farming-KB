Cool-water and cold-water aquaculture species offer high-value protein

## Species Selection for Cool and Cold Water Systems

Mollison categorises aquaculture species by their temperature preferences and
uses this classification to match species to site conditions:

### Rainbow Trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss)
The primary cold-water species for [[permaculture-aquaculture-guide]] in temperate
regions. Rainbow trout are the most widely cultured cool-water fish globally
and offer several advantages for integrated systems:

- **[[aact-dissolved-oxygen-fine-bubble-aeration-ingham]] above 6 mg/L (higher than
  most warm-water species); pH 6.5-8.0; low turbidity; low ammonia and nitrite.

species occupies a distinct [[ecological-niche]], maximising total pond
productivity while minimising competition.

### Niche Partitioning in Polyculture
- **Surface feeders**: Tilapia (in warmer systems), silver carp (filter
  feeders on phytoplankton), and duckweed-grazing species.
- **Mid-water feeders**: Rainbow trout, perch, and silver perch.
- **Bottom feeders**: Common carp, catfish, and freshwater mussels.
- **Detritivores**: Snails, shrimp, and crayfish that process organic
  matter on the pond floor.

### Stocking Ratios for Cool-Water Polyculture
Mollison suggests the following approximate ratios for temperate pond
polyculture:
- 40-50% carp (bottom feeder and nutrient recycler)
- 30-40% silver carp or other filter feeder (phytoplankton control)
- 10-20% predatory species (trout, perch, or bass) for population control
  of forage fish and direct harvest
- Forage fish (minnows, shiners) stocked separately and allowed to breed

### Stream Diversion
Where springs are unavailable, stream water can be diverted through ponds.
Mollison stresses that diversion systems must:
- Maintain sufficient environmental flow downstream.
- Include screening to prevent escape of cultured fish.
- Provide bypass channels for flood events.
- Allow for temperature management through pond depth and shading.
