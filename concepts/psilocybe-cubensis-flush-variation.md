## [[mycelium]] may rest before producing another flush. Bigwood & Beug
studied up to six flushes from three independent minicultures of the **Amazon
strain** of *[[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]* (originally collected in Pucallpa, Peru).

The key finding was that **[[psilocybin]] and psilocin do not follow the same
pattern** across flushes. Their accumulation dynamics are independent, suggesting
different biosynthetic regulation or [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]] successive flushes:

### Flushes 1–2: Absent or Near-Zero

In the first flush, psilocin was either **undetectable** or present only in
trace amounts (0 to 0.5 mg/g dry weight). This was consistent across all three
minicultures studied. The second flush similarly showed very low psilocin
levels. This early absence suggests that psilocin accumulation requires either
a maturation of the mycelium's biosynthetic machinery or the depletion of
specific substrate nutrients that, when exhausted, shift metabolic pathways
toward psilocin production.

### Flushes 3–4: Peak Accumulation

Psilocin concentrations **rose sharply** beginning around the third flush and
reached their **maximum values by the fourth flush**. At peak, psilocin levels
of 1.8 to 2.9 mg/g dry weight were recorded. This represents a dramatic
increase from the near-zero values of the first flushes, with psilocin
increasing by a factor of 10× or more between flush 1 and flush 4.

### Flushes 5–6: Decline

After peaking at flush 4, psilocin levels began to **decline** in subsequent
flushes. By flushes 5 and 6, concentrations had dropped substantially from
their peak values, though they generally remained above the near-zero levels
seen in the earliest flushes. This decline likely reflects the progressive
depletion of substrate nutrients and the aging of the mycelial culture.

## Psilocybin: No Clear Trend

In contrast to psilocin, psilocybin showed **no consistent directional trend**
across flushes. Instead, psilocybin concentrations fluctuated considerably
between flushes and between minicultures:

- **Flush 1**: 5.1 to 8.3 mg/g dry weight
- **Flush 4**: 3.2 to 4.8 mg/g dry weight
- Overall variation: approximately a **4-fold difference** between the lowest
  and highest recorded values

The absence of a clear trend for psilocybin means that:

1. **Early flushes are not necessarily low in total potency**: While psilocin
   is absent, psilocybin levels in flush 1 can be as high as or higher than
   those in later flushes.
2. **Late flushes are not necessarily high in total potency**: Even though
   psilocin peaks at flush 4, the concurrent psilocybin levels may be lower
   than in earlier flushes.
3. **Total alkaloid content is unpredictable** from flush number alone,
   because the two compounds vary independently.

## Experimental Design

Bigwood & Beug studied three independent [[miniculture-rye-grain-cultivation-method-for-psilocybe-cubensis]] minicultures of the Amazon
strain, each producing up to six flushes over a cultivation period of
approximately 20 weeks. This design allowed assessment of both **within-strain
variability** (differences between minicultures) and **between-flush
variability** (differences across successive harvests from the same culture).

Mushrooms from each flush were **individually analyzed** by HPLC, and the
results were reported as mg of alkaloid per gram of dry mushroom tissue. The
use of multiple independent minicultures helped distinguish true biological
variation from culture-specific artifacts.
