# KNF Vegetative Growth Stage

## Overview

The vegetative growth stage is the period when plants develop their roots and shoots through time until they mature. This is the foundational growth phase that establishes the plant's structural framework and its capacity for later reproductive success.

During this stage, the plant's energy is directed primarily toward building biomass — leaves, stems, and root systems. A strong vegetative foundation directly correlates with the plant's ability to produce abundant flowers and fruit in subsequent stages.

Foliar spraying is the most common application method during vegetative growth. Plants absorb nutrients efficiently through their leaves, especially when applied in the early morning or late evening when stomata are open.

Typical dilution ratios for vegetative-stage inputs range from 1:500 to 1:1000, depending on the specific input and the plant's growth rate. Over-concentration can burn leaves or disrupt the plant's natural metabolism.

Soil drenches with IMO and LAB are also valuable during this stage, ensuring that the root zone microbiome is robust and active. A healthy soil biology is the foundation upon which all vegetative growth depends.

Application frequency varies but generally follows a pattern of weekly treatments during active growth periods. Observing the plant's response is more important than following a rigid calendar.

As the plant nears the transition to reproductive growth, nitrogen-rich inputs like FAA should be gradually reduced while calcium and phosphorus inputs are increased.
