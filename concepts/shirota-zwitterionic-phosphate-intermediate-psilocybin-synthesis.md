    19|# Zwitterionic Phosphate Intermediate in Psilocybin Synthesis
    20|
    21|Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) achieved the first practical large-scale
    22|synthesis of psilocybin from psilocin without chromatographic purification,
    23|through the discovery of a zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate intermediate.
    24|This intermediate enabled isolation by simple filtration, dramatically
    25|improving the scalability of psilocybin production for use as an analytical
    26|reference standard.
    27|
    28|## Regulatory Motivation
    29|
    30|Since June 6, 2002, fungi containing psilocybin and psilocin have been
    31|regulated by the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law in Japan.
    32|Morphological and microscopic identification of magic mushrooms is difficult
    33|without expert mycologists, so chromatographic methods including TLC, GC, and
    34|HPLC are standard for enforcement. These methods require pure reference
    35|compounds. Isolating psilocybin and psilocin from mushrooms at gram scale is
    36|impractical because psilocin decomposes readily and psilocybin is highly
    37|polar. Chemical synthesis was needed to produce reference standards.
    38|
    39|## The Phosphorylation Challenge
    40|
    41|Converting psilocin (the dephosphorylated, less polar active compound) to
    42|psilocybin (the 4-O-phosphate ester) proved technically demanding. Most
    43|phosphorylation methods failed to consume psilocin at all. Two approaches
    44|showed promise: the phosphoryl iodide method using tribenzyl phosphite,
    45|iodine, and DMAP, and the pyrophosphate method using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate
    46|and n-butyllithium. The pyrophosphate method was selected for its easy handling
    47|and reagent stability.
    48|
    49|## Discovery of the Zwitterionic Intermediate
    50|
    51|After the phosphorylation reaction, the expected O,O-dibenzyl phosphate
    52|derivative (compound 7) was produced, but workup revealed an unexpected
    53|transformation. During aqueous workup to remove excess reagents, hydrolytic
    54|cleavage of one O-benzyl group occurred, and the freed benzyl group migrated
    55|to the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethyl moiety. This intramolecular rearrangement
    56|produced a zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (compound 8) — a
    57|molecule carrying both a positive charge (quaternary ammonium) and a negative
    58|charge (phosphate).
    59|
    60|Nichols and Frescas had previously observed that hydrolytic cleavage of one
    61|O-benzyl group occurred rapidly and the resulting zwitterionic O-monobenzyl
    62|phosphate was obtained as a mixture. However, they had not characterized or
    63|exploited this intermediate for isolation purposes.
    64|
    65|## Structural Elucidation by 2D NMR
    66|
    67|The structure of compound 8 was fully determined using two-dimensional NMR
    68|techniques. Key spectral features included two sets of benzyl group signals in
    69|the proton NMR, with one set shifted to high field (delta H 4.56, 2H, s)
    70|compared to the other (delta H 4.98, 1H, s; 4.96, 1H, s). The methylene and
    71|N,N-dimethyl protons on the psilocin core were shifted to low field relative to
    72|psilocin itself. A phosphorus-31 NMR signal confirmed the phosphate moiety at
    73|delta P minus 5.45.
    74|
    75|HMBC (heteronuclear multiple bond correlation) experiments revealed that one
    76|benzyl group was directly linked at the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethyl group,
    77|forming a quaternary ammonium ion. NOESY (nuclear Overhauser effect
    78|spectroscopy) data supported these assignments by showing through-space
    79|correlations consistent with the zwitterionic structure. These data confirmed
    80|that compound 8 was a zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative formed by
    81|intramolecular migration of a benzyl group from oxygen to nitrogen.
    82|
    83|## Practical Significance of Zwitterion Formation
    84|
    85|The zwitterionic nature of compound 8 was the key to scalable synthesis.
    86|Unlike the neutral O,O-dibenzyl phosphate, the zwitterion was insoluble in
    87|dichloromethane. When the worked-up reaction mixture was suspended in
    88|dichloromethane overnight, compound 8 precipitated as a white powder that could
    89|be collected by simple filtration in over 85 percent yield. Excess dibenzyl
    90|phosphate remained dissolved and was removed by washing with dichloromethane.
    91|
    92|This eliminated the need for preparative HPLC or any chromatographic
    93|purification, which had been the major obstacle to producing psilocybin in
    94|gram-scale quantities for use as a reference standard.
    95|
    96|## Catalytic Hydrogenolysis to Psilocybin
    97|
    98|The final step involved catalytic hydrogenolysis of compound 8 using 10
    99|percent palladium on carbon under a hydrogen atmosphere in methanol. Both
   100|benzyl groups (the N-benzyl and the remaining O-benzyl) were cleaved
   101|simultaneously, yielding psilocybin as a white needle crystalline powder. The
   102|overall isolated yield of psilocybin from psilocin was greater than 72 percent,
   103|even at gram scale — a dramatic improvement over previously reported yields of
   104|20 percent and 47 percent. Water was added during the reaction due to product
   105|deposition, and the mixture was stirred for one additional hour under hydrogen.
   106|
   107|## The Complete Psilocin-to-Psilocybin Pathway
   108|
   109|The Shirota synthesis route for psilocybin proceeds through these stages:
   110|
   111|1. Phosphorylation of psilocin with tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-BuLi at
   112|   minus 78 to 0 degrees Celsius in THF
   113|2. Aqueous workup inducing zwitterion formation through benzyl migration
   114|3. Suspension in dichloromethane overnight for precipitation
   115|4. Filtration to isolate the zwitterionic intermediate (85.2 percent yield)
   116|5. Catalytic hydrogenolysis with Pd/C in methanol under H2 atmosphere
   117|6. Crystallization of psilocybin (87.5 percent yield from intermediate)
   118|
   119|## The Psilocin Synthesis Route
   120|
   121|The same paper presents the concise synthesis of psilocin from commercially
   122|available 4-hydroxyindole in three high-yielding steps:
   123|
   124|1. Acetylation of 4-hydroxyindole with acetic anhydride and pyridine in
   125|   dichloromethane at 0 degrees Celsius to room temperature, yielding 4-
   126|   acetylindole (compound 4) as ivory white crystals
   127|2. Oxalyl chloride treatment in diethyl ether at 0 degrees Celsius, then
   128|   addition of dimethylamine in THF with pyridine to form the glyoxalylamide
   129|   (compound 6) in 80 percent yield
   130|3. Lithium aluminum hydride reduction in THF under argon to psilocin in over
   131|   85 percent yield
   132|
   133|Each intermediate was crystalline and isolated by filtration without
   134|chromatography. The overall route from 4-hydroxyindole to psilocin was
   135|designed to avoid column chromatography at every stage.
   136|
