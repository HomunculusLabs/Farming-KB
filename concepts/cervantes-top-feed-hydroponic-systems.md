# Top-Feed Hydroponic Systems for Cannabis

Top-feed hydroponic systems are among the most productive,
controllable, and widely used [[cervantes-passive-wick-hydroponic-systems]] configurations for cannabis cultivation. Nutrient
solution is metered in specific doses and delivered via
spaghetti tubing or emitters placed at the base of individual
plants. Aerated nutrient solution flows into the
[[cervantes-growing-medium]] and is absorbed
by roots, with runoff directed back to the reservoir.

## How Top-Feed Systems Work

In a top-feed system, a submersible pump in the
[[cervantes-nutrient-solution-reservoir-management]] pushes aerated nutrient solution through a manifold
to individual delivery tubes. The solution flows into the
growing medium, is taken up by roots, and excess drains
through the container back to the reservoir. This cycle
repeats on a timer, typically several times per day. Common
growing mediums include [[cervantes-rockwool-growing-substrate]], gravel, [[cervantes-guerrilla-growing-tactics]],
and expanded clay pellets.

## Advantages

Top-feed systems offer numerous benefits for cannabis growers:

- Very productive with precise control over
  [[cervantes-nutrient-stock-solutions-concentrates]]
- Easy to maintain with straightforward mechanics
- Efficient [[cervantes-drain-to-waste-vs-recirculating]] of nutrient solution
- Versatile: works with individual containers or slabs
- Easy to isolate and replace sick plants
- Suitable for both small and large-scale operations
- Compatible with [[cervantes-case-study-energy-efficient-sea-of-green]]
  and [[comparison-main-lining-vs-screen-of-green]] methods

## Top-Feed Bucket Systems

Self-contained top-feed buckets are one of the most popular
configurations. Each bucket consists of a growing container
nested inside a reservoir containing a pump. Two main designs
exist:

- Net pot design: A net pot is suspended in the lid of a
  five-gallon (19 L) bucket/reservoir. Roots hang down
  into the nutrient solution, which is aerated by an air
  stone. A separate pump cycles irrigation to the container.
- Expanded clay design: A larger growing container filled
  with expanded clay pellets sits in the reservoir. A pump
  constantly cycles nutrient solution, aerating it and
  irrigating the plant. Roots grow down into the solution.
  Some systems include a one-inch (3 cm) pipe to draw air
  directly to the root zone.

Individual buckets make culling out and replacing sick plants
quick and easy. Self-contained systems are also perfect for
growing large [[cervantes-mother-plants]] because
containers can be moved anywhere easily.

## Multiple Bucket Systems

Multiple bucket top-feed systems connect several buckets to a
central reservoir. Key design elements include:

- Flexible drain hose attached near the bottom of each
  bucket connects to a drainage manifold
- Runoff nutrient solution shuttles back to the central
  reservoir for reuse
- Each reservoir below the growing container holds an inch
  or two of water
- Regular irrigation cycling prevents stagnation in the
  bottom of buckets
- Square containers make the most efficient use of space
- Plants are fed with irrigation tubing attached to a
  manifold running between rows

## Top-Feed Slab Systems

Top-feed slab systems are popular among indoor and
[[query-how-do-i-choose-between-indoor-outdoor-and-greenhouse-cannabis-growing]] growers. Rockwool
or coco slabs covered in plastic serve as growing containers.
Nutrient solution is delivered via spaghetti tubes from the
top of each slab, with emitters attached to control dosage.

Slabs can be arranged in individual trays or placed on
drainage tables. When using tables, ensure the surface is set
up on an incline so it drains evenly. Pockets of standing
water on the table contain less oxygen and promote root rot.
Individual rockwool blocks on top of slabs allow gardeners
to remove or change plants if necessary.

## Vertical Top-Feed Systems

Vertical gardens can increase overall yield more than tenfold
over a flat garden. Substrate bags, tubes, or slabs are
positioned vertically around an HID lamp. Short plants are
placed in the medium and fed individually with
[[cervantes-drip-irrigation-systems]]. Runoff
drains through the growing medium and back to the reservoir
for recirculation. This configuration maximizes light
utilization and floor space efficiency.

## Management Tips

Successful top-feed systems require attention to several
factors:

- Maintain proper [[cervantes-ph-management-testing]]
  (5.8-6.5 in hydroponics)
- Monitor [[cervantes-ec-tds-nutrient-solution-strength]]
  to avoid [[cervantes-nutrient-burn-over-fertilization]]
- Ensure drainage is rapid to prevent root zone saturation
- Screen drain openings in reservoirs to prevent roots
  from blocking them
- Use a pre-filter on pumps to prevent emitter clogging
