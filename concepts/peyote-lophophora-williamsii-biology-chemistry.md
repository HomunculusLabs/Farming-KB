# Peyote — *Lophophora williamsii* Biology, Chemistry, and Ethnobotany

## Overview

Peyote is a small, spineless, tufted, blue-green, button-like cactus known botanically as *[[lophophora-williamsii]]*. It is the most famous of the [[gottlieb-psychoactive-cacti-san-pedro-and-peruvian-torch]], growing wild from [[psychoactive-cacti-of-central-mexico]] to Northern Texas in the Chihuahuan Desert. Its known history dates back to pre-Columbian times, possibly as early as 300 BC. During the past two centuries, the religious use of peyote spread northward into the United States and Canada among many Plains Indian Tribes (Navajo, Comanche, Sioux, Kiowa), eventually replacing the hallucinogenic but dangerous red mescal bean (*[[sophora-secundiflora-entity]]*) as a ceremonial sacrament.

## Alkaloid Chemistry

### Primary Psychoactive Compound

- **Mescaline** (3,4,5-trimethoxy-β-phenethylamine) — the main psychoactive component.
- First isolated by [[arthur-heffter]] in 1896 — the first hallucinogenic compound isolated by man.
- Approximately 350 mg required for a psychotropic experience; definite effects from as little as 100 mg.
- Mescaline may comprise up to **6% of dried button weight**, but more commonly ~1%.
- An average dried button (~quarter diameter) weighs about 2 grams; 6–10 buttons typically needed.

### The Experience

The peyote experience is **quantitatively different from pure mescaline** — more physical, due to the synergistic effects of other alkaloids. The full experience lasts 6–12 hours.

**Timeline**:
- ~30 minutes: First effects — strange intoxication, shifting consciousness, minor perceptual changes, respiratory pressure, muscle tension, possible nausea.
- ~1 hour: Unpleasant sensations fade; altered consciousness deepens.
- Several hours: Deepening visual effects, enhanced colours, halos/auras, altered spatial perception, closed-eye patterns.
- Later: Thought becomes more ordered, contemplative.
- Aftermath: No comedown; pleasant relaxation.

## Mescaline Biosynthetic Pathway

Research by Rosenberg, McLaughlin, and Paul (University of Michigan, 1966) established:

1. **Tyrosine** → Tyramine + DOPA
2. Tyramine + DOPA → **Dopamine** (the most immediate and efficient mescaline precursor)
3. Dopamine → **Nor-mescaline**
4. Nor-mescaline → **Mescaline**

Tyramine and DOPA are also precursors, but dopamine is the most direct and efficient.

## Ethnobotanical Uses

### Huichol Indians
- Rub fresh peyote juices into wounds to prevent infection and promote healing (due to phenolic alkaloids with antibacterial activity).

### Tarahumara Indians
- Consume small amounts to combat hunger, thirst, and exhaustion during hunting.
- Known to run for days after deer with no food, water, or rest.

### Folkloric Medicine
- Arthritis, consumption (tuberculosis), influenza, intestinal disorders, diabetes, snake and scorpion bites, datura poisoning.

### Two Forms Recognized
- **Tzinouritehua-hikuri** ("peyote of the Gods"): Larger, more potent, more bitter.
- **Rhaitoumuanitari-hikuri** ("peyote of the goddesses"): Smaller, more palatable, milder.
- Difference may be due to age (alkaloids accumulate with age) or possibly different species.

## Species

### *Lophophora williamsii*
- The primary peyote species.
- Blue-green, spineless, button-like.

### *Lophophora diffusa*
- Discovered by H.H. Bravo in 1967 near Querétaro, south-central Mexico.
- Yellow-green, soft, ribless.
- Different alkaloid mixture with **far less mescaline** than *L. williamsii*.

## Related Psychoactive Cacti

### *Trichocereus pachanoi* (San Pedro)
- Native to the Andes of Peru and Ecuador.
- Large, multi-branched (10–15 feet in habitat).
- [[gottlieb-peyote-botany-and-mescaline-content]]: 0.3–1.2% (less than peyote, but larger size makes it more economical).
- Effects described as more pleasant, less overwhelming, more tranquil than peyote.
- Ritual use dates back to 1000 BC; still used by curanderos of northern Peru.
- Related mescaline-bearing species: *T. bridgesii*, *T. macrogonus*, *T. terscheckii*, *T. werdermannianus*.

### Other Central Mexican Peyote Substitutes

| Common Name | Species | Active Compounds | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peyotillo | *Pelecyphora aselliformis* | Traces of mescaline, various alkaloids | Alkaloids too minute for effect |
| Tsuwiri (False Peyote) | *Ariocarpus retusus* | Hordenine, N-methyltryamine | Insufficient for psychoactivity |
| Sunami | *Ariocarpus fissuratus* | Mostly hordenine | Used in folkloric medicine |
| Doñana | *Coryphantha macromeris* | Macromerine (~0.1%) | ~1/5 mescaline potency; extraction needed |
| Dolichothele | Various species | N-methylphenethylamine, synephrine, etc. | Used as sacrament by some tribes |
| Mulato | *Mammillaria micromeris* | Various alkaloids | Prolongs life, gives speed, clarifies vision (claimed) |

### Macromerine
- Found only in *Coryphantha* species.
- Reputed to possess approximately **1/5 the potency of mescaline**.
- Present at ~0.1% in *C. macromeris* (Doñana).
- Precursor: synephrine.
- Also found in: *C. pectinada*, *C. elephantideus*, *C. runyonii*, *C. cornifera* var. *echinus*.

## Harvesting and Potency

- **Best time**: After a long dry spell (alkaloids accumulate during drought).
- **Worst time**: During or after rain (alkaloid content may drop below 50%).
- **Soil indicator**: Nitrogen-rich soil correlates with higher alkaloid content.
- **Proper method**: Decapitate above ground level, leaving roots intact for regeneration.
- A button 3 inches in diameter may be **20+ years old**.
- Roots contain **no mescaline**.

## Cultivation

- **Soil**: 3 parts coarse sand, 1 part loam, 1 part leaf mold; bake at 400°F for 1 hour before use.
- **Growth cycle**: Grow mostly in spring/autumn, send down roots in summer, rest through winter.
- **Watering**: Thoroughly 1–2× per week; cut back to less than half in winter.
- **Best planting time**: Late spring.
- **Cuttings**: Can produce 4–6 inches of growth plus 1–2 branches in 6 months.
