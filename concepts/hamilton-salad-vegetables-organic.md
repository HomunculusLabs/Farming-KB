# Salad Vegetables Organic Growing

Growing your own salad vegetables is particularly beneficial because
their quality and flavor depends on freshness. They take up
comparatively little space and most can be grown all year-round if
some protection against frost is provided. Salad crops are fast-growing
and are not usually troubled by many pests and diseases. They all
require a moisture-retentive soil.

## Choosing Salad Vegetables

Salad vegetables deteriorate rapidly after harvesting. Leaf crops are
not nearly as crisp and fresh when bought from a shop compared to
those picked fresh from the garden. Sweet corn starts to turn its
sugar into starch the very instant it is picked. Growing your own
salads ensures peak quality and flavor that cannot be matched by
store-bought produce.

## Successional Sowing

Sow short rows of fast-maturing varieties at frequent intervals for
successional harvesting of peak-condition vegetables. Try to fit some
salads in between wider-spaced, long-term crops like Brussels sprouts
and cauliflower. This intercropping approach maximizes the productive
use of available space.

## Lettuce - *Lactuca sativa*

Lettuce is perhaps the most widely grown salad vegetable. There are
several distinct types, each with its own characteristics.

### Romaine Lettuce

A particularly crisp and refreshing salad vegetable, Romaine lettuce
has erect bright-green leaves with a prominent central vein. It is
well suited to organic cultivation and grows readily in most gardens.

### Red Leaf Lettuce

This distinctive form of lettuce has crinkle-edged leaves that are
tinged with red. Like the other lettuces, it is a suitable crop for
growing in the [[hamilton-greenhouse-management-organic]]
as well as outdoors.

### Butter Lettuce

Known for its soft, tender leaves and mild, sweet flavor, butter
lettuce is a favorite for salads. It requires consistent moisture to
prevent bolting.

### Cut-and-Come-Again Lettuce

This type allows repeated harvesting from the same plant. Instead of
cutting the whole head, individual leaves are picked as needed,
allowing the plant to continue producing. This extends the harvest
period considerably and is ideal for small gardens.

## Mustard and Cress - *Brassica hirta* and *Lepidium sativum*

Mustard and cress seedlings are quick and easy to grow, and can be
eaten fresh all year. The seeds can be germinated on moist kitchen
paper indoors, making them ideal for winter salads. They are ready to
harvest in just a few days from sowing.

## Watercress - *Nasturtium officinale*

Because it grows wild in fast-flowing streams, watercress needs shade,
a moisture-retentive soil, and plenty of water. It can be grown
successfully in the organic garden by creating a damp, shaded position
or in a container that can be kept consistently moist.

## Chicory - *Cichorium intybus*

The example shown as a "chicon" is dug up and blanched in deep, moist
peat. Both the forced chicons and the unforced green leaves are used
in winter salads. Chicory is a valuable crop for extending the salad
season through the colder months.

## Soil Preparation and Rotation

Most salad vegetables belong in Plot B of the [[hamilton-three-year-crop-rotation]], which receives single digging and application of blood, fish, and bone meal. They generally do well without the addition of extra fertilizer, though moisture retention is key. Adding [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] to the soil before sowing helps maintain the moisture levels that salad crops require.

## Growing Under Protection

Salad vegetables are particularly well suited to growing under
[[hamilton-cloches-and-frost-protection]] and in
[[hamilton-cold-frames-and-hardening-off]]. Lettuce,
early cabbage, and cauliflower can be started in seed trays and
planted out under cloches in early spring. In mid to late summer,
another sowing of early varieties can be made with cloches put on in
early fall to hasten maturity and extend the season.

## Greenhouse Cultivation

Many salad vegetables are suitable for greenhouse growing. Red leaf
lettuce and other varieties can be grown in the greenhouse borders or
in growing bags. This allows year-round production regardless of
outside conditions. Greenhouse salads benefit from regular watering
and feeding with liquid [[hamilton-organic-fertilizers-and-plant-nutrition]].

## Pest and Disease Management

Salad crops are relatively trouble-free in the organic garden. The
main threats are slugs and snails, which can be managed through
physical barriers, hand-picking, and encouraging natural predators.
Aphids occasionally affect lettuce but are generally less problematic
than on other crops. Good [[hamilton-pest-and-disease-control-organic]] and proper spacing for air circulation help prevent most issues.
