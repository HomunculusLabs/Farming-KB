# Beta-Caryophyllene as a Dietary Cannabinoid

(E)-beta-caryophyllene ((E)-BCP) is a naturally occurring compound found
in many common spices and cannabis that functions as a selective CB2
receptor agonist. Discovered as a dietary cannabinoid by an international
group of researchers from Switzerland, Germany, Italy, and the U.S.
(2008, 2012), it represents a food-based pathway to activating the
[[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]], which are predominantly found in the immune system and peripheral tissues. CB2 activation modulates immune responses, reduces inflammation, and influences pain signaling without affecting the central nervous system's psychoactive pathways.

Scientists suggest that activation of CB2 receptors via this dietary
plant-based cannabinoid might present a new and additional therapeutic
strategy in the treatment of a multitude of diseases associated with
inflammation and oxidative stress, both underlying factors in a host of
different pathologies.

## Therapeutic Properties

Research has demonstrated that (E)-beta-caryophyllene may:

- Protect against inflammation and inflammatory diseases
- Provide antimicrobial activity
- Offer pain-relieving properties
- Protect against cancer
- Reduce oxidative stress
- Support immune system regulation

These properties overlap significantly with the therapeutic benefits
attributed to cannabis-derived cannabinoids, but through a dietary
pathway that is fully legal and widely accessible.

## Dietary Sources

This food-based cannabinoid is fully accepted by the U.S. government
with the FDA's seal of approval. Key (E)-beta-caryophyllene-containing
organic spices are relatively easy to obtain.

### High (E)-BCP Content Sources

- **Black Ashanti Pepper (Piper guineense)**: (E)-BCP content of 58%
  in test samples
- **White Ashanti Pepper**: (E)-BCP content of 52% in test samples
- **Clove**: Among the richest common culinary sources
- **Cinnamon**: Readily available source with meaningful BCP content
- **Black Pepper**: Common household spice with notable BCP levels

### Moderate (E)-BCP Content Sources

- Basil
- Indian bay-leaf
- Alligator pepper
- Rosemary
- Caraway
- Mexican oregano
- Copal resin (used traditionally as incense)

## Cannabis Connection

Beta-caryophyllene is also found in cannabis, where it contributes to
the plant's [[cannabis-tinctures-and-edibles]] creates
a multi-source cannabinoid approach to healing.

The concept of dietary cannabinoids also supports the broader
understanding that the endocannabinoid system evolved to interact with
plant-derived compounds, and that humans have likely been modulating
this system through diet for thousands of years before cannabis was
identified as a specific medicine.
