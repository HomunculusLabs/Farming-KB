# Exotic Mushroom Cultivation on Sterilised Grass Seed

## Summary

This guide covers the complete cultivation cycle for several exotic mushroom species on sterilised grass seed substrate, from substrate preparation through fruiting or sclerotia harvest. Species addressed include **Psilocybe mexicana**, **Psilocybe tampanensis**, **Psilocybe cubensis**, and **Panaeolus subbalteatus**. The core technique uses rye grass seed (*Lolium perenne*) in jars sterilised in a pressure cooker, inoculated with spore or mycelium syringes or agar squares, then either cased for mushroom production or left in jars for sclerotia formation. Casing soil is a peat, vermiculite, and limestone mix also sterilised before use. Fruiting takes place in simple clear plastic bins with polyethylene covering and passive air exchange. Sclerotia of *P. tampanensis* and *P. mexicana* develop directly on colonised grain over several months without casing.

## Species Covered

| Species | Mushrooms | Sclerotia | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Psilocybe mexicana* | Yes | Yes | One big flush then a small second flush |
| *Psilocybe tampanensis* | Yes | Yes | Sclerotia enlarge up to 4 months |
| *Psilocybe cubensis* | Yes | No | Rye grain preferred over grass seed |
| *Panaeolus subbalteatus* | Yes | No | Multiple even flushes |

## Substrate Preparation

The primary substrate is grass seed. The casing soil recipe uses:

| Ingredient | Parts |
|---|---|
| Peat | 10 |
| Vermiculite | 5 |
| Limestone (Marl) | 2 |

Mix dry, then add water gradually until maximum retention without turning to mud. Pack into bags or jars and **sterilise 1 hour** in the pressure cooker. The source author recommends sterilisation despite some authors advising against it, reporting only bad experiences with untreated or pasteurised casing soil.

## Fruiting

### Fruiting Chamber

Use simple **clear plastic bins** covered with polyethylene sheeting, stackable for efficiency. Melt small holes in the sides for passive air exchange (cover with mesh to exclude flies). **Five trays per bin**; evaporation from casing surfaces maintains adequate humidity. No humidifiers or cool-mist devices are needed.

### Cold Shock

Place harder-to-fruit strains in the refrigerator for **one night** before fruiting. **Not necessary** for easy strains (e.g., Ecuador cubensis) and **no benefit** for *P. mexicana*, *P. tampanensis*, or *P. subbalteatus*.

### Misting and Watering

Mist casings **daily** and never let the surface dry out. Increase watering after picking a flush since maturing mushrooms pull significant moisture. Exact amounts develop with experience.

### Fruiting Timeline

| Stage | Duration |
|---|---|
| Pinhead appearance | 6 to 15 days after placing in bins |
| Mushroom maturation | 5 to 7 days after pins appear |
| Typical flush count | 3 flushes (up to 5 or 6 with proper watering) |

*P. mexicana* typically produces one big flush and a small second; other species produce more constant flushes. Keep casing surfaces clean by promptly removing **dead pinheads (aborts)** to prevent mould contamination.

## Sclerotia Cultivation

*P. tampanensis* and *P. mexicana* produce **sclerotia** directly on colonised grain **without casing**. Keep jars in a clean, preferably dark location. Sclerotia also form naturally in the casing layer of cased trays. Based on experience with *P. tampanensis*, sclerotia continue to enlarge until approximately **4 months after inoculation**.

| Parameter | Value |
