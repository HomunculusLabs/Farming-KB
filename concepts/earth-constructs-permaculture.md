# Earth Constructs and Raised Banks

Earth constructs are shaped landforms — banks, mounds, walls, ridges, ramps, and excavated features — that serve multiple functional purposes in [[permaculture-design]]. Rather than treating earthworks as a single-purpose civil engineering exercise, Mollison frames them as multifunctional elements that simultaneously address shelter, water control, wind management, fire protection, noise abatement, and productive growing space.

## Compacted Earth Structures

### Earth-Compacted Walls
Machinery can raise earth-compacted walls for houses and barns in a single morning, with floor and roof completed in two days. These structures are fireproof, silent, and highly energy-conserving — the thermal mass of earth walls buffers temperature swings, reducing heating and cooling demands. They represent a low-cost, durable building method suited to rural and homestead settings where earth materials are readily available.

### Sand Walls with Cement-Filled Bags
In arid or sandy environments, walls can be stabilized using bags filled with cement. This technique provides structural integrity where raw earth or sand alone would be unstable, creating solid barriers suitable for shelter, windbreaks, or containment.

## Earthworks for Water and Wetland Management

### Polders and Levees
Polders and levees function as reverse dams, reclaiming land from water by enclosing and draining it. They are critical for flood and tide control in low-lying and deltaic areas, allowing agriculture and settlement on otherwise inundated land.

### Flood Refuge Islands
In deltaic regions such as Bangladesh, earth mounds serve as vital flood refuge islands. Mollison specifically notes the need for wooded refuge mounds — elevated landforms planted with trees that provide both safety from floodwaters and resources (food, fuel, timber) during extended inundation events.

### Marsh Tracks and Plant Sites
Earthworks in marshy terrain include earthbanks, islands, and mounds that create solid ground for tracks, plantings, and access. Construction in soft ground requires specialized equipment:
- **Swamp tractors** with wide tyres and swivel buckets for earth moving
- **Wooden mat systems** laid temporarily to allow equipment crossing over soft, waterlogged surfaces

### Mounds in Ponds
Raising mounds within ponds allows isolation of rampant or invasive aquatic species, preventing them from overtaking the entire water body while still maintaining them in a productive system.

## Wind and Water Deflection Designs

### Ridge Wind Tunnels
The Afghan ridge wind tunnel is a traditional design that channels and accelerates wind along a constructed ridge for mechanical power generation (milling). This demonstrates how earth shaping can harvest wind energy without any moving parts in the landform itself.

### Windbreak Plant Sites on Sidecasts
Sidecasts (earth displaced during excavation) provide elevated, well-drained planting sites for windbreak species. Willows, poplars, and tamarisk are particularly suited to sidecast positions above waterlogged ground, where their roots can access moisture without suffering waterlogging stress.

## Fire Protection Structures

### Wildfire Radiation Refuges
Above-ground earth constructs can serve as radiation refuges during wildfires. Earth and stone absorb and deflect radiant heat, providing safe shelter for people, livestock, and equipment when fire passes through the landscape.

### Containments for Inflammable Fluids
Earth banks and excavated pits create secure containments for fuel and chemical storage, preventing spills from migrating and reducing fire hazard.

### Moated Islands for Fire-Prone Sites
In fire-vulnerable areas, moated islands — earth mounds surrounded by water-filled ditches — provide effective protection. The moat acts as both a physical firebreak and a water source for firefighting.

## Noise Control

### Traffic Noise Deflectors and Absorbers
Earth banks constructed alongside roads and railways serve as noise deflectors and absorbers. The mass of compacted earth dampens sound transmission, protecting nearby settlements, gardens, and wildlife areas from traffic noise pollution.

## Agricultural and Infrastructure Earthworks

### Earth Ramps and Loading Stands
Earth ramps and raised stands facilitate loading and unloading operations for trucks, cattle, hay, and boats. By shaping the land to match the working height of vehicles and animals, these constructs reduce the need for mechanical lifting equipment and improve operational efficiency.

### Rail, Canal, and Road Grades
Earthworks are fundamental to creating grades for transport infrastructure. Properly designed grades minimize erosion, manage water runoff, and reduce energy expenditure for vehicles traversing the landscape.

### "Ha Ha" Fences
The "ha ha" is a below-grade ditch with stone-facing on the inner wall. It creates a visual and physical barrier for livestock without interrupting the open view of the landscape — an elegant solution that predates modern fencing and remains relevant in permaculture for its aesthetic and functional qualities.

## Road Enhancement and Integration

Roads in permaculture design are not merely transport corridors but integrated productive elements:

- **Heat pump pipes under bitumen**: Roads absorb solar heat; buried pipes can harvest this thermal energy via heat pump systems, providing heating for nearby buildings.
- **Road run-off harvesting**: Graded roadsides channel rainfall into [[irrigation-systems]], wetlands, or storage ponds, turning a waste product into a productive resource.
- **Roadside vegetation preservation**: Roadsides are managed to preserve bunchgrasses, sagebrushes, and remnant forest patches, maintaining ecological corridors and reducing maintenance needs.

## Design Principles

Earth constructs in permaculture follow several key principles:

1. **Multifunctionality**: Every earthwork should serve at least three functions — e.g., a road bank that provides noise reduction, water harvesting, and windbreak planting.
2. **Minimal energy input**: Use gravity, water flow, and existing topography to minimize the energy required for construction and maintenance.
3. **Integration with living systems**: Earthworks are planted immediately after construction to stabilize soil, provide yield, and integrate the structure into the broader ecosystem.
4. **Scale-appropriate technology**: From hand-shaped garden mounds to machine-compacted walls, the method matches the scale and resources available.
5. **Succession planning**: Earth structures evolve — compacted walls may eventually be covered in vegetation, mounds may develop into complex micro-ecosystems, and roadsides may become habitat corridors.

## Species Notes

- **Willows (Salix spp.)**: Excellent for waterlogged sidecast sites; rapid growth, soil stabilization, and multiple yields.
- **Poplars (Populus spp.)**: Fast-growing windbreak species for elevated, well-drained positions.
- **Tamarisk (Tamarix spp.)**: Salt-tolerant, suited to sidecast sites above saline or waterlogged ground.
