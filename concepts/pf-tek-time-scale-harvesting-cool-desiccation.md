## Time Scale of Mushroom Development

### Spore Germination (Days 3-7)

After inoculation, visible white mycelial growth appears within 3-7 days assuming viable spores, correct moisture, proper incubation temperature (70-80°F / 21-27°C), and intact dry vermiculite barrier.

**Non-germination**: If no growth appears after 7-10 days, possible causes include dead spores or overheated/dried substrate. Dried substrate is evidenced by initial germination that subsequently halts — the fungus spreads but stops at a certain point.

### Colonization (Days 7-21)

Mycelium colonizes the substrate cake over 1-3 weeks total from inoculation:

- White mycelial threads spread through the [[brown-rice-flour-substrate]]/[[pf-tek-brown-rice-vermiculite-substrate]]
- Vermiculite provides air space, allowing threads to stretch across gaps (unlike grain substrates that form dense masses)
- Fastest colonization at 75-80°F (24-27°C)
- Jars stored in dark, draft-free location

PF emphasizes jars larger than 1/2 pint are unreliable — the 1/2 pint size provides optimal surface-to-volume ratio.

### Fruiting Signals and Birthing (Day 14-28)

After full colonization, mycelium shows fruiting signals: primordia (tiny pin structures), condensation on jar walls, aerial mycelium knots. The cake is then "birthed" — removed from the jar into the fruiting terrarium.

### Fruiting and Maturation (Days 1-14 after birthing)

- Primordia develop into pins within days
- Pins mature over 3-7 days
- Multiple flushes possible from each cake
- Immature specimens: light color, white stems, conical caps before veil breaks

**Overall timeline**: From inoculation to first harvest: approximately 3-5 weeks. Subsequent flushes at weekly intervals.

## Harvesting Technique

### Optimal Harvest Time

- **Just before or right after the veil breaks** (partial veil connecting cap to stem ruptures)
- Caps conical, not fully expanded
- Gills light-colored (sporulation not yet begun)
- Specimens very light-colored with white stems

### Why Harvest Early

Immature specimens are superior in:

1. **Quality**: Better texture and appearance
2. **Digestibility**: Less nausea
3. **Potency**: Higher alkaloid concentration per unit weight

As mushrooms mature and sporulate, metabolic resources shift from alkaloid to spore production. Mature mushrooms also have higher water content, diluting alkaloid concentration.

### Aborts

Aborts are mushrooms that begin forming but stop growing. PF identifies these as particularly potent and recommends harvesting alongside mature specimens. Aborts are identifiable by miniature size and darkened caps.

## Cool Desiccation (Drying) Method

### Critical Rule: Never Use Heat

> "Never dry them in an oven or use hot air dryers. The heat leaches the [[guzman-magic-mushrooms-chemical-constituents]] and reduces their quality."

Heat destroys [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]]. All drying must be at ambient or cool temperatures.

### Desiccant Drying (Recommended)

**Materials**: Drierite or Silica Gel desiccant, wire screen, airtight plastic container, plastic bag with tie-off.

**Procedure**:
1. Preheat desiccant per manufacturer instructions (store-bought desiccant is often damp)
2. Place 1-inch layer of desiccant on container bottom
3. Wire screen over desiccant, mushrooms on screen
4. Container in plastic bag, seal with wire tie
5. After 24 hours: initial shriveling visible
6. After 4-5 days: mushrooms "rock hard" dry
7. Verify: stem should snap cleanly when bent

### Cool Desiccation Enhancement

For best alkaloid preservation, place the desiccant box in the refrigerator. Drying at near-freezing temperatures minimizes thermal degradation.

### Pre-Drying Option

If room humidity is low, pre-dry on a wire screen in open air for a couple of days, then transfer to desiccant box for complete drying.
