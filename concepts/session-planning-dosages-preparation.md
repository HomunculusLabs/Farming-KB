# Session Planning, Dosages, Preparation, and the Guide Role

## Overview

The practical appendix to *The [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]* (Section III: "Some Technical Comments About [[bardo-instruction-texts-psychedelic-sessions]]") provides an operational framework for conducting psychedelic sessions. Drawing from Leary, Metzner, and Alpert's [[hofmann-leary-case-and-harvard-research]], it covers drug dosages, preparatory practices, environmental design, the guide role, and group composition — grounded in the Tibetan [[bardo-thodol]] model.

## Planning a Session

### Defining the Goal

The first question is always "what is the goal?" The manual adopts four classical Hindu goals:

1. **Power and insight** — increased personal power, intellectual understanding, sharpened insight into self and culture, accelerated learning, professional growth.
2. **Duty and help** — providing care, rehabilitation, and rebirth for fellow humans.
3. **Fun and aesthetics** — sensuous enjoyment, aesthetic pleasure, interpersonal closeness, pure experience.
4. **Transcendence and liberation** — liberation from ego and space-time limits; attainment of mystical union.

The manual aims primarily at the fourth goal, but emphasizes this does not preclude the others — illumination requires stepping beyond game problems of personality, role, and professional status.

### Extroverted vs. Introverted Experience

- **Extroverted**: self ecstatically fused with external objects (flowers, other people). Requires bringing objects/symbols — candles, pictures, incense, music.
- **Introverted**: self ecstatically fused with internal life processes (lights, energy-waves, bodily events). Requires elimination of all stimulation — no light, sound, smell, or movement.

Communication modes should be pre-arranged — silent signals, clasping hands, embracing — to avoid game-misinterpretations during heightened sensitivity. When multiple people share a session, they should agree collaboratively on goals; unexpected manipulations by one participant can trap others into paranoid [[third-bardo]] delusions.

## Drugs and Dosages

Two dosage figures per substance: first for inexperienced persons, second for experienced persons or group sessions.

| Substance | Inexperienced | Experienced |
|---|---|---|
| LSD-25 | 200–500 μg | 100–200 μg |
| Mescaline | 600–800 mg | 300–500 mg |
| Psilocybin | 40–60 mg | 20–30 mg |

### Onset and Duration

Taken orally on empty stomach: LSD and psilocybin onset ~20–30 min; mescaline ~1–2 hours. Duration: LSD and mescaline 8–10 hours; psilocybin 5–6 hours. DMT (50–60 mg IM) produces ~500 μg LSD-equivalent experience lasting only ~30 minutes.

### Adjunct Drugs

- **Pre-session calming**: 30–40 mg Librium ~1 hour before for anxious persons.
- **Euphoric induction**: Methedrine prior to session.
- **Staggered administration**: Initial 200 μg LSD, then 200 μg "booster" once familiar with effects.
- **Anti-nauseants**: Marezine, Bonamine, Dramamine, or Tigan (nausea is usually a mental symptom indicating fear, but can be physiological with morning-glory seeds and peyote).
- **Breaking repetitive loops**: 50 mg DMT or 25 mg Dexedrine/Methedrine (with subject's consent).
- **Emergency antidote**: Thorazine (100–200 mg IM) terminates effects — should not be used simply from fear; Third Bardo sections should be read first.

## Preparation: Set and Setting

### The Primacy of Set and Setting

Psychedelic chemicals have no specific expected reaction sequence. The reaction is chiefly a function of **[[set-and-setting]]**. The better the preparation, the more ecstatic and revelatory the session. In initial sessions with unprepared persons, setting — particularly the actions of others — is most important.

### Long-Range Set

Refers to personal history and enduring personality. More important are the reflex mechanisms for dealing with anxiety: defenses and protective maneuvers.

- **Facilitating**: flexibility, basic trust, religious faith, openness, courage, warmth, creativity.
- **Obstructing**: rigidity, desire to control, distrust, cynicism, narrowness, cowardice, coldness.
- **Most important**: insight — understanding one's own "recording machinery" and recognizing when not functioning as desired.

### Immediate Set

Expectations about the session itself that can shape or trap the experience:

- **Medical**: imposing a medical model, interpreting sensations as sickness/health, placing the guide in a doctor-role — especially problematic if the guide also does this.
- **Rebellion**: doing something "far out" or vaguely naughty — a naive set.
- **Intellectual**: dangerous for novices. "Turn your mind off" is the best advice; intellectual exercises reserved for the session's last stage.
- **Religious**: float with the stream; postpone theological interpretations until later.
- **Recreational/aesthetic**: natural and valid, but can become Third Bardo ego games preventing pure ego-loss.
- **Planned**: the manual prepares for a [[mystical-experience]] per the Tibetan model, while acknowledging [[the-bardo-thodol-as-living-guide]] is itself a human artifact.

### Practical Recommendations

- Set aside at least **three days**: day before, session day, follow-up day — guarantees reduced external pressure and sober commitment.
- Talk to experienced voyagers; observe a session; read accounts by Huxley, Watts, Wasson.
- **Meditation is probably the best preparation** — those who have spent time managing the mind and reaching higher concentration stages are the best candidates, recognizing ego-loss as an end eagerly awaited.

## The Setting

The setting must be removed from usual social/interpersonal games and free from distractions. Trust in surroundings and privacy are necessary. At least three days should be set aside for the experience to run its natural course with time for reflection. A too-hasty return to game-involvements blurs clarity and reduces learning potential.

Many report deeper experiences in the evening, but the person should choose by temperament. Natural settings (gardens, beaches, forests) have specific influences — comfort is essential. Music, lighting, food, and drink should be planned beforehand; most prefer simple foods later: bread, cheese, wine, fresh fruit. The senses are wide open — the taste of a fresh orange is unforgettable.

In group sessions, beds or mattresses should be provided. One arrangement places heads together in a **star pattern**. An extra room should be available for seclusion. All decisions should be made collaboratively.

## The Psychedelic Guide

### Fundamental Requirement

For initial sessions, the guide's attitude is critical. With the cognitive mind suspended, the subject is in heightened suggestibility — the guide can move consciousness with the slightest gesture.

The key: **the guide must turn off his own ego and social games** — muffling power needs and fears. To be relaxed, solid, accepting, secure. The Tao wisdom of **creative quietism** — sense all, do nothing except let the subject know your wise presence.

### The Ground Control Analogy

The guide is ground control in the airport tower — always there to receive messages, ready to help navigate. An operator who imposes his own personality upon the pilot is unheard of. Pilots have their own flight plan; ground control is there to serve. If the flier suspects selfish motives, the security bond crumbles.

### Experience and Fear

The guide should have **considerable experience** in psychedelic sessions and guiding others. To administer psychedelics without [[palmer-mushroom-cooking-and-personal-experience]] is unethical and dangerous. The greatest problem is **fear** — the authors conclude almost every negative LSD reaction was caused by guide fear augmenting the subject's transient fear. When the guide acts to protect himself, he communicates concern to the subject.

### Routine: One Guide "Up," One Sober

Maintaining alert quietism for hours is difficult for most Westerners. The routine: **one trained guide** takes a low dose with subjects (providing intimacy and cosmic companionship), while **one staff member** remains sober in "ground control" handling practical matters. Large doses are not recommended for guides during new-subject sessions — the subject's intense hallucinatory games can pull the guide out of the First Bardo void.

### The Guide as Liberator

The guide role is described as perhaps the most exciting in society — literally a liberator providing illumination and freeing people from lifelong internal bondage. The psychedelic liberation has built-in protection against professionalism: awe and gratitude, not pride, are the rewards.

## Group Composition

Trust in fellow voyagers is essential. With strangers, share as much time and space as possible prior to the session. Participants should set collaborative goals and explore mutual expectations and past experiences.

- Small groups preferable initially; groups exceeding six or seven are demonstrably less profound and generate more paranoid hallucinations.
- For five or six people, at least **two guides** should be present.
- If possible, one guide should be an **experienced woman** providing spiritual nurturing and comfort.
- Initial sessions for married couples should be **separate** so the marriage game doesn't dominate; with experience, it may be explored for increased intimacy or clearer communication.

## The Manual as Instrument

The General Conclusion frames the manual as a precision instrument for liberation — a catapult, a trough restoring continuity with positive karma, a bridle controlling a horse's course. The extreme suggestibility of consciousness in the ego-transcendent state ensures efficacy of hearing or reading the teachings, without ritual or complex meditation. The manual liberates "by being seen or heard."
