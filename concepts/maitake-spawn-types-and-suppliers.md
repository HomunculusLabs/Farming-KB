# Maitake Spawn Types and Supplier Directory

## Overview

Spawn is the [[vegetative-propagation-methods-mollison]] stage of mushrooms, consisting of
vigorous [[mycelial-growth-and-colonization]] on a chosen substrate material. This living
pure culture is used to inoculate new substrates under sterile
conditions. For [[grifola-frondosa]] (maitake) cultivation, spawn is
placed in specially designed polypropylene bags with microfilter
windows that allow gas exchange while maintaining sterility.

## Types of Spawn

Four main types of spawn are commonly used by mushroom growers
including those cultivating maitake:

### 1. Liquid Spawn

Liquid spawn can be labor-saving during inoculation through the use
of a specially designed inoculator. Two production methods exist:

- **Liquid-surface culture**: [[mycelium]] grows on the surface of a
  liquid nutrient medium.
- **Submerged fermentation**: Mycelium grows throughout the liquid
  medium. This method can be accomplished on an industrial scale
  (Fang et al., 1998), making it suitable for large-scale
  operations.

Liquid spawn offers rapid colonization and even distribution but
requires specialized equipment for production and inoculation.

### 2. Grain Spawn

A variety of grains can serve as the substrate base for grain spawn:

- Millet
- Rye
- Wheat
- Sorghum
- Milo

Grain spawn is widely used because grains provide excellent nutrition
for mycelial growth and the individual grains allow for easy
distribution throughout the substrate during inoculation. Millet-based
spawn has been specifically noted for enabling very rapid spawn runs
when used in sufficient quantity.

### 3. Sawdust Spawn

Supplemented sawdust-bran substrates are typically used for sawdust
spawn. This type closely matches the final substrate composition,
which can reduce adaptation time. Sawdust spawn is particularly
suitable for species like maitake that naturally grow on wood.

### 4. Wooden Stick (Skewer) Spawn

Wooden stick spawn uses small wooden dowels or skewers colonized
by mushroom mycelium. This type of spawn can be easy to manipulate
during inoculation (Oei, 1996). The wooden sticks are inserted into
the substrate, providing inoculation points that mimic natural
conditions where maitake colonizes wood.

## Obtaining Spawn vs. Ready-to-Fruit Blocks

Growers have two options for starting [[maitake-cultivation]]:

1. **Ready-to-fruit blocks**: Colonized synthetic logs already
   inoculated with the chosen species, obtainable from established
   mushroom growers. This option eliminates the need for sterile
   inoculation procedures.
2. **Spawn inoculation**: With training in aseptic techniques,
   growers can inoculate their own synthetic logs using purchased
   spawn. This requires more skill and equipment but offers greater
   control over substrate composition and strain selection.

## Spawn Rate Recommendations

For maitake cultivation, a spawn-to-substrate ratio of 1:10 or 1:20
is generally adopted. Increasing spawn quantity can dramatically
shorten the spawn run period. One demonstration showed that
increasing millet-based spawn shortened the colonization time to
approximately one week.
