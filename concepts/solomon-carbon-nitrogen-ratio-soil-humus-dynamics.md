## Carbon-Nitrogen Ratio and Soil Humus Dynamics

## Overview

In *Gardening When It Counts*, [[steve-solomon]] provides one of the clearest practical explanations of how the carbon-to-nitrogen (C/N) ratio governs [[cervantes-organic-amendments-chart]]—because the C/N ratio of what you add to soil determines whether you build or destroy soil humus, and whether you feed plants or feed soil microbes.

## The 12:1 Standard

### Soil Humus C/N

All healthy soil contains humus—the stable, dark, decomposed residue of [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]]. Regardless of climate, soil type, or location, **soil humus has a C/N ratio of approximately 12:1**. This is the stable equilibrium point that soil biology maintains. Every amendment added to soil will be decomposed until its C/N approaches this ratio, and the processes involved have major consequences for soil health.

### What C/N Means

The C/N ratio expresses the proportion of carbon (carbohydrate/energy) to nitrogen (protein/nutrient) in any organic material:
- **High C/N (>12:1)**: Carbon-rich, nitrogen-poor (e.g., sawdust, straw, dry leaves, paper)
- **Low C/N (<12:1)**: Nitrogen-rich, carbon-poor (e.g., fresh manure, [[cotton-seed-meal]], legume greens)
- **C/N = 12:1**: Balanced—this is soil humus itself

## What Happens When You Amend Soil

### Adding High C/N Materials (>12:1)

When organic matter with a C/N above 12:1 is mixed into soil, soil microbes must supplement the insufficient nitrogen by drawing it from the surrounding soil. The process:

1. Microbes use the abundant carbon as energy
2. They scavenge nitrogen from the soil solution to build their own protein
3. **Plant-available nitrogen is temporarily immobilized**—taken up by microbes and withheld from plants
4. As decomposition proceeds, the C/N of the amendment drops toward 12:1
5. When decomposition is complete, a net increase in soil humus results
6. Plant nutrients that were locked in the undecomposed material become available

**Net effect**: Soil humus increases. Soil tilth improves. Fertility eventually increases. But plants may suffer nitrogen deficiency during the decomposition period.

### Adding Low C/N Materials (<12:1)

When nitrogen-rich material (C/N below 12:1) enters the soil, the opposite occurs:

1. The surplus nitrogen exceeds what microbes need for their own growth
2. Bacteria convert excess nitrogen to ammonia gas
3. Ammonia is almost immediately converted by other soil bacteria to water-soluble **nitrates**—a first-class fertilizer
4. Plants grow rapidly in response to the nitrate surge
5. Elevated nitrate levels stimulate microbial population growth
6. Expanded microbial populations more aggressively attack existing soil humus
7. **Net result: soil humus decreases**

**Net effect**: Short-term fertility boost, but long-term soil degradation. This is exactly the same mechanism by which [[query-how-do-i-start-growing-hazelnut-bushes]], and when it is all over we have slightly reduced the soil's humus content, a less-healthy microbial population, and poorer tilth."

This means that excessive use of even organic nitrogen-rich fertilizers (like poultry manure or seed meals) can degrade soil structure over time—exactly the same problem caused by synthetic [[cannabis-chemical-fertilizers]], just slower.

## C/N Ratios of Common Materials

### Very High C/N (Slow Decomposition, Nitrogen Immoblization)

| Material | Approximate C/N |
|----------|----------------|
| Sawdust | 500:1 |
| Paper | 175:1 |
| Dry cornstalks | 100:1 |
| Straw (cereal) | ~80:1 |
| Tree bark | ~100:1 |
| Conifer needles | ~80:1 |

### Moderate C/N

| Material | Approximate C/N |
|----------|----------------|
| Dry tree leaves (deciduous) | ~60:1 |
| Poor grass hay | ~50:1 |
| Corrugated cardboard | ~50:1 |
| Summer grass (fresh) | ~25:1 |
| Legume hulls | ~30:1 |

### Low C/N (Rapid Decomposition, Nitrogen Release)

| Material | Approximate C/N |
|----------|----------------|
| Seaweed | ~19:1 |
| Fresh [[coleman-mobile-greenhouse-green-manure-soil-building]] | ~15:1 |
| [[knf-green-grass-liquid-fertilizer]] clippings | ~12:1 |
