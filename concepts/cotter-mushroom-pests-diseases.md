## Prevention: The First Line of Defense

Cleanliness is the most important factor in preventing pest and disease problems. These practices are part of a broader [[trichoderma-forest-green-mold-guide]] for a detailed guide.
- Appears as rapidly spreading green colonies on substrate and agar plates
- Thrives in warm, humid conditions with excess moisture
- Often co-occurs with fungus gnat infestations
- Can outcompete mushroom mycelium on supplemented substrates

**Management:**
- If detected, isolate the contaminated block or container immediately
- Remove and destroy contaminated material; do not compost in mushroom areas
- Reduce humidity slightly to slow Trichoderma growth
- Improve air circulation
- Check for and address insect vectors (especially fungus gnats)
- Do not open contaminated containers near the laminar flow hood

### Other Mold Contaminants

- **Aspergillus**: Green to black powdery colonies; produces airborne spores. Common on agar plates.
- **Penicillium**: Blue-green colonies; common contaminant on agar and grain.
- **Neurospora**: Pinkish-orange, fast-growing mold; especially problematic in tropical climates.
- **Mucor/Rhizopus**: Gray, fuzzy, fast-growing; thrives in wet conditions.

### Bacterial Contamination

- Appears as smooth, shiny, irregular colonies (clear, cloudy, yellowish, or rose-colored)
- Can slimy or wet spots on substrate
- Common on agar plates when tissue samples are too wet
- Slows or stops mycelial growth

**Management:**
- Add antibiotics to agar (penicillin G, ampicillin, streptomycin)
- Ensure tissue samples are relatively dry before cloning
- Surface-sterilize dirty specimens with 3% hydrogen peroxide
- Maintain proper sterile technique during all transfers

## Cultural Practices for Disease Prevention

### Harvest Timing
- Pick gilled mushrooms before the cap edge turns upward
- At this stage, the mushroom begins spore production, attracting pests
- Overmature mushrooms are more vulnerable to infestation and contamination
- Regular harvesting removes potential pest breeding sites

### Environmental Control
- Maintain appropriate temperature ranges for each species
- Avoid temperature fluctuations that stress the mycelium
- Proper humidity (85-95% for fruiting) without excess condensation
- Adequate fresh air exchange to prevent CO2 buildup and stagnant air
- Proper lighting to promote healthy fruiting

### Substrate Management
- Proper pasteurization or sterilization eliminates most competitors
- Do not over-supplement substrates (increases contamination risk)
- Maintain proper moisture levels; avoid waterlogged substrates
- Use fresh substrate materials; old or contaminated materials increase risk
- The two-dunk rule: maximum 2 batches per pasteurization barrel to avoid nutrient buildup

### Sanitation Schedule

**Daily:**
- Sweep all floors
- Remove spent or damaged mushrooms and substrate debris
- Check crops for early signs of pests or disease
- Monitor sticky traps

**Weekly:**
- Clean drains with bleach solution
- Wipe down walls, shelves, and surfaces
- Check and replace sticky traps
- Inspect air handling systems

**Monthly:**
- Deep clean the entire growing area
- Inspect and clean ventilation systems and filters
- Evaluate pest pressure trends and adjust management
- Review and restock pest management supplies

## Outdoor Pest Management

- Use poultry wire or hardware cloth to protect logs and beds from animals
- Chicken wire over beds prevents scratching by birds and rodents
- Keep grass and weeds trimmed around growing areas to reduce habitat for pests
- Use fresh logs (cut within 1 month) to give mushroom mycelium a head start over wild fungi
- [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] with pest-repelling plants around garden mushroom beds
- Beer traps for slugs and snails around outdoor fruiting areas
- Beneficial insects (ladybugs, predatory mites) for general pest management
