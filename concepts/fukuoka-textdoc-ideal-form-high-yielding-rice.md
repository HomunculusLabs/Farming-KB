## Eight Characteristics of Ideal Rice

Fukuoka identified eight major characteristics of rice plants with an
ideal form:

**1. Short-stalked dwarf rice** with short, wide, erect leaves. Stalk
height is just twenty-one inches with fifteen to twenty-two tillers per
plant.

**2. Extraordinary grain-to-straw ratio**. Unhulled grain weight is 150
to 167 percent of straw weight, versus less than 70 percent in ordinary
rice. The balance point on a fingertip is near the panicle neck.

**3. Elongated first internode** comprising more than fifty percent of
stalk length, with the panicle extending below the stalk base when bent.

**4. Long second leaf with decreasing length downward**. An important
characteristic is that the leaf blade on the second leaf down is longer
than that of any other leaf. Thereafter, the leaf blade becomes shorter
as one moves down the stalk.

**5. Progressively shorter leaf sheaths**. Total leaf length is longest
for the first and second leaves and decreases downward, unlike
non-high-yielding rice where lower leaves are longer.

**6. Minimal above-ground nodes**. Only four nodes grow, the fourth at
ground level or lower, versus five or six in normal rice. The top three
leaves alone yield more than 100 full grains per head.

**7. Excellent grain filling**. Weight per thousand grains is 23 grams
for small-grained and 24.5 to 25 grams for normal-grained rice.

**8. Density tolerance**. Even at a density of 500 stalks to the square
yard, hardy upright dwarf rice shows no decline in the number of grains
per head or percent of ripened grains.
