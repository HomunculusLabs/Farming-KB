## The Infection Process: Spore Attachment to Penetration

The life cycle begins when three-celled asexual spores (conidia), dispersed by
wind, dew, or rain splash, attach to the hydrophobic rice leaf surface (Ou, 1985;
Talbot, 1995). The conidial apex releases an adhesive that attaches the spore
tightly to the leaf surface (Hamer et al., 1988). Within an hour, a germ tube
15–30 μm in length develops. Within four hours the tube "hooks," changes direction,
swells, and flattens against the leaf surface (Bourett & Howard, 1990), marking the
beginning of differentiation into a specialized infection structure called the
appressorium (Gilbert et al., 1996; De Zwaan et al., 1999).

## Signal Transduction Pathways

Three well-characterized MAPK (Mitogen Activated Protein Kinase) pathways govern
infection-related development in [[fungal-hyphae-invasive-growth-mechanisms]], and mediates mass transfer of storage reserves to the
appressorium (Thines et al., 2000). Pmk1 mutants cannot form appressoria. Exogenous
cAMP fails to rescue this phenotype but increases germ-tube swelling, suggesting
Pmk1 may act downstream of cAMP signalling (Xu & Hamer, 1996).

**Osm1** (osmoregulation MAP kinase) controls arabitol accumulation, the main
compatible solute during hyperosmotic stress (Dixon et al., 1999). Δosm1 mutants
form multiple appressoria under chronic osmotic stress, suggesting Osm1 negatively
regulates appressorium development during severe osmotic stress.

Cross-talk between pathways has been demonstrated: Mps1 phosphorylation increases
in Δpmk1 mutant backgrounds, suggesting compensatory mechanisms, while Pmk1
phosphorylation is repressed during appressorium formation in wild-type strains
(Zhao et al., 2005).
