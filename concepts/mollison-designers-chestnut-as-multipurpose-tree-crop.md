# Chestnut as Multipurpose Tree Crop

The chestnut (Castanea spp., including C. sativa, C. dentata, and C.
pumila/chinquapin) is cited throughout the Permaculture [[permaculture-livestock-forage-systems]]. Mollison treats
chestnut as one of the most valuable temperate tree crops for permaculture
systems.

## Slope Stabilisation and Root Systems

Chestnut plays a central role in Mollison"s discussion of root systems and
their implications for designers. Unstable slopes are pegged with the
great root "tree nails" of chestnut and pine, oak and walnut. Even after a
hundred years, the steep slopes of Tasmania are only just starting to
collapse as the roots of cleared forests rot, and could still be saved by
pines, Acacias, or chestnuts.

The deep and extensive root system of chestnut trees makes them ideal for
anchoring slopes, preventing landslides, and stabilising earthworks. On
very steep slopes, upslope plantings of chestnut, Acacia, carob, or olive
cascade seed downslope over time, gradually reforesting eroded terrain.
