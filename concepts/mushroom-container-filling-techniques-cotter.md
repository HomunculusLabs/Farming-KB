### What Are Spy Bags

- A few clear bags or containers used alongside opaque ones in each
  batch
- Allow visual monitoring of colonization progress without opening
  containers
- Enable early detection of contamination as [[fungicide-treated-seed-contamination-risk-mushroom-substrate]]
- Clear bags let you see contamination developing from outside
- Early detection means early removal before spores spread
- Use 1–3 spy bags per batch depending on size

### Placement Strategy

- Distribute spy bags throughout the batch (top, middle, bottom of
  stacks)
- They represent the range of conditions in the [[cotter-growing-space-design-fruiting-rooms]]
- If a spy bag at the bottom shows contamination, check all lower
  containers
- If only top spy bags are contaminated, the issue may be airflow or
  temperature stratification

## Packing Density

### Why Dense Packing Matters

Containers must be packed densely to avoid air pockets:
- Air pockets create spaces where mushrooms may try to form
- Mushrooms forming inside substrate become trapped and cannot mature
- These "aborted mushrooms" rot inside the block
- Rotting mushrooms attract flies, harbor molds, create bacterial blooms
- Dense packing eliminates internal voids
