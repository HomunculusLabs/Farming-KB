## Glass Jars and Bottles

Wash carefully and store with lids for reuse:
- Store beads, buttons, bread tags, bottle caps, dried goods, seeds
- Use for canning and preserving (see [[faires-preserving-whole-foods]]
- Puncture to make lanterns
- Puncture to make garden waterers (slow drip irrigation)
- Use for string telephones (children's projects)

## Community-Scale Waste Management

The management of waste is a tremendous task even in a small community. Sorting all materials from multiple households in one place wastes enormous energy, so all waste material should be sorted at the source. Each family should:
- Be responsible for sorting their own waste (glass, paper, metal, plastic)
- Have their own composting bins for organic kitchen and yard waste
- The community should have shared recycling stations and composting facilities

## Container Gardening with Recycled Materials

Many waste items make excellent plant containers:
- Old tires stacked for growing potatoes and root crops
- Metal drums cut in half for raised beds
- Wooden boxes and crates lined with plastic
- Plastic tubs with drainage holes
- Broken buckets and troughs

## Papercrete

Waste paper can be mixed with Portland cement and water to create papercrete, a lightweight building material used for walls, blocks, and insulation. This turns paper waste into a construction resource.

## The Zero-Waste Goal

The ultimate target is zero waste leaving the property. Everything produced on site either composts, feeds animals, becomes building material, or is reused in another function. This aligns with Permaculture Principle #8: Produce no waste. When waste is designed out of the system, the homestead becomes truly self-reliant.
