### Comparison with Other Substances

The EMCDDA emphasized that overall prevalence of hallucinogenic mushroom use is considerably lower than cannabis across all EU countries. However, among school students aged 15-16, lifetime prevalence of mushroom use in some countries equals that of ecstasy, representing a significant finding given that mushroom use was historically considered far less common than synthetic drugs.

## School Survey Findings (Ages 15-16)

### ESPAD Prevalence Data

The ESPAD surveys provided the most detailed cross-national comparison of hallucinogenic mushroom use among European adolescents. Key findings:

- Mushroom use among school students is concentrated in countries with established retail markets
- Prevalence increased notably during the late 1990s and early 2000s, coinciding with the expansion of smartshop and internet sales
- Male students generally reported higher prevalence than female students
- Use was strongly associated with use of other illegal drugs, particularly cannabis and ecstasy

### Polydrug Use Pattern

A consistent finding across surveys was that hallucinogenic mushroom use rarely occurs in isolation:

- Users of hallucinogenic mushrooms were significantly more likely to have used cannabis, ecstasy, amphetamines, and cocaine
- Mushrooms were rarely the first illegal drug used; cannabis typically preceded mushroom use
- The association with other drug use was stronger for mushrooms than for many other substances, suggesting that mushroom users tend to be experienced drug users rather than experimenters

### Clubbing Survey Data

Drug surveys conducted in club settings showed that prevalence of illegal drug use is consistently higher than among the general or school populations. Hallucinogenic mushroom use was more common among young people who have used other illegal drugs than among those who have not, consistent with the polydrug use pattern observed in school surveys.
