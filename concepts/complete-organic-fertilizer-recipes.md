# Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF) Recipes

Steve Solomon's Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF) is a balanced organic fertilizer mix designed for vegetable gardens. The formula provides complete nutrition including trace minerals, unlike most chemical fertilizers which supply only NPK. Solomon developed this recipe specifically for [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]] and gardens that rely on minimal irrigation.

## Basic COF Formula (by volume, NOT weight)

Mix fairly uniformly in parts by volume using a scoop or bucket:

**Base (choose one):**
- 4 parts any kind of seedmeal (except coprameal), OR
- 3 parts any seedmeal + 1 part tankage ("blood-and-bone" or "meatmeal") for higher nitrogen (better for leafy crops in spring), OR
- 4 parts coprameal + 1 part tankage (to boost nitrogen content of the weaker coprameal)

**Blend with:**
- 1/2 part ordinary agricultural lime (finely ground)
- 1/4 part gypsum (if no gypsum, double the agricultural lime quantity)
- 1/2 part dolomite lime

**Plus (for best results):**
- 1 part of one phosphorus source: finely ground rock phosphate (hard or soft), bonemeal, or high-phosphate guano
- 1/2 part kelpmeal OR 1/2 part basalt dust

## Application Rates

- Apply 4 to 6 liters of COF per 4x25-foot raised bed, or per 50 feet of row 12-18 inches wide
- Apply at least once per year, best in spring
- Work into top few inches of soil with hoe or spade
- For unirrigated gardens, just spread on the soil surface
- For hills, mix an additional dose of COF into each hill

## Side-Dressing Medium and High-Demand Vegetables

After seedlings emerge or are transplanted, sprinkle small amounts around plants:

- Side-dress every 3-4 weeks as plants grow
- Place each dusting just beyond the leaf spread
- Medium-demand crops: beets at ~2-3 lbs per 100 sq ft per full season
- Stop side-dressing once the crop approaches maturity

## Crop-Specific Application Guidelines

Different vegetable families have different nutrient demands. Adjust COF application accordingly:

- **Heavy feeders** (tomatoes, corn, brassicas, squash): Side-dress every 3 weeks with additional COF. These crops benefit from the higher-nitrogen formula (3 parts seedmeal + 1 part tankage)
- **Medium feeders** (lettuce, carrots, onions, beets): Standard COF application at planting plus one or two side-dressings is usually sufficient
- **Light feeders** (peas, beans, radishes): Standard COF at planting only. Legumes fix their own nitrogen but benefit from the phosphorus and trace minerals in COF
- **Root crops** (carrots, parsnips, potatoes): Avoid excess nitrogen, which promotes foliage at the expense of root development. Use the basic seedmeal-only formula without added tankage

## Ingredient Notes

**Seedmeals** are byproducts of oil extraction from soybeans, cottonseed, canola, linseed, sunflower, or safflower. Most analyze around 6-4-2 NPK. Buy whichever has the highest protein percentage for the lowest cost. Store dry and protected from moisture in a tight-lid container.

**Tankage** (blood-and-bone) analyzes around 10-4-0. Slightly higher in nitrogen than seedmeals.

**Limes:** Three types serve different purposes. Agricultural lime is relatively pure calcium carbonate. Dolomitic lime contains both calcium and magnesium. Gypsum is calcium sulfate. Do NOT use quicklime, burnt lime, or hydrated lime. Buy in large sacks from agricultural suppliers.

**Kelpmeal** adds trace minerals, growth regulators, and natural hormones. Expensive but worth it -- a 55-pound sack costs less than equivalent hydroponic micronutrient supplements. Can substitute basalt dust if kelpmeal is too costly.

**Rock phosphate** provides slow-release phosphorus. Soft rock phosphate (colloidal) breaks down faster than hard rock phosphate but both are very slow-acting. Bonemeal is an alternative that decomposes more quickly but is more expensive per unit of phosphorus.

**Gypsum** (calcium sulfate) improves soil structure in clay soils and provides calcium without raising pH, unlike agricultural lime. It is particularly valuable in soils that are already alkaline but calcium-deficient.

## Alternative COF Formulations

Solomon provides variations for specific conditions:

- **Low-budget COF**: Omit kelpmeal and basalt dust entirely. The result lacks trace minerals but still provides balanced NPK and calcium/magnesium
- **High-phosphorus COF**: Double the phosphorus source for soils testing low in phosphorus, or for crops like carrots and root vegetables that benefit from extra phosphorus
- **Acidic soil COF**: Increase dolomite lime to 1 full part and reduce agricultural lime to 1/4 part. Always test soil pH before adjusting
- **Alkaline soil COF**: Eliminate dolomite lime entirely, use gypsum instead of agricultural lime, and double the gypsum portion to 1/2 part

## Making Liquid Feeds from COF

A liquid version of COF can be made for foliar feeding or quick supplementation:

- Place 2-3 handfuls of COF in a porous cloth bag
- Steep in 5 gallons of water for 24-48 hours
- Use the resulting liquid as a drench around plants or as a foliar spray
- This method provides faster nutrient availability than dry application, similar to [[cervantes-compost-tea-organic-brewing]]

## Why Not Chemical Fertilizer

All inexpensive chemical fertilizers (analyzing 10-10-10 or similar) are incomplete -- they supply only nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. Plants also need calcium, magnesium, sulfur, and many trace minerals. Plants grown with chemical-only fertilizers are more susceptible to insects and disease, and are less nutritious.

Organic materials release nutrients only as they decompose, at rates determined by soil temperature. The rate roughly doubles for each 10°F increase. Complete decomposition of COF takes about two months in warm soil. This slow, steady release means plants are fed continuously rather than receiving a sudden flush followed by starvation.

## Compatibility with Other Organic Methods

COF works well alongside other organic soil-building practices:

- Combine with [[composting-science-solomon]] applications for optimal soil health
- Use [[natural-farming-knf-overview]] preparations as supplements for specific growth stages
- [[master-cho-knf-ffj-fermented-fruit-juice-guide]] can supplement COF during fruiting stages for improved flavor
- [[knf-fish-amino-acid]] provides a quick nitrogen boost similar to COF side-dressing

## Cost Effectiveness

Solomon reports that a full year's supply of COF ingredients for a large garden (3 sacks seedmeal, 2 sacks meatmeal, 1 kelpmeal, guano, limes) costs under $300 and produces approximately $4,000 worth of vegetables at supermarket prices.

## Storage and Shelf Life

Store mixed COF in airtight containers in a cool, dry location. Seedmeals can go rancid if exposed to moisture. Properly stored, mixed COF remains effective for at least one year. Individual ingredients have longer shelf lives: limes and rock phosphate are essentially inert and last indefinitely; seedmeals should be used within 1-2 years of purchase.
