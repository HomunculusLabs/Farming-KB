Contemporary research into psychedelic therapy has moved beyond the question of
whether these substances produce therapeutic effects to investigating the specific
psychological, neurobiological, and pharmacological mechanisms through which
change occurs. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial for optimizing protocols,
predicting treatment response, and integrating psychedelics into mainstream
psychiatric practice.

## Psychological Mechanisms

The acute [[blesching-cannabis-emotional-processing-expansive-constricting-emotions]] allows patients to revisit traumatic memories with
reduced avoidance and increased emotional acceptance. Under psychedelics, amygdala
reactivity to negative stimuli is reduced while emotional awareness is heightened,

overwhelmed. This may parallel the mechanism of exposure-based therapies but with
a dramatically reduced fear response.

Increased psychological flexibility — the ability to adopt different perspectives
and adapt behavior patterns — is a core mechanism identified across multiple
therapeutic modalities. Psychedelics appear to produce a rapid and sometimes

## Neuroplasticity

Emerging evidence suggests that psychedelics promote structural and functional
neuroplasticity. Preclinical studies show that psilocybin and LSD increase
dendritic spine density and promote the expression of brain-derived neurotrophic
factor (BDNF) in cortical neurons. These neuroplastic effects may create an
"extended window of plasticity" lasting days to weeks after the acute experience,
during which new behavioral patterns and cognitive habits can be more easily
established.

This mechanism parallels the proposed action of ketamine in treating depression
and may explain why psychedelic therapy often requires only one or a few sessions
rather than the chronic dosing typical of conventional psychiatric medications.
The combination of DMN disruption (loosening rigid thought patterns) with
enhanced neuroplasticity (making new patterns easier to establish) represents a
potentially powerful one-two punch against entrenched mental health conditions.
