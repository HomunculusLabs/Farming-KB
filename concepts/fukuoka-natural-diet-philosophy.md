## Principles of Natural Diet

Fukuoka outlined several core principles:

1. **Food should be whole and unprocessed:** Eat foods as they are found in nature,
not processed, refined, or chemically preserved. Brown rice over white rice, whole
vegetables over extracted juices, unrefined salt over refined.

2. **Food should be local and seasonal:** Eat what grows naturally in your region
during its natural season. This aligns human consumption with natural cycles and
eliminates the need for preservation, transportation, and artificial growing conditions.

3. **One should eat foods that grow together naturally:** A diet composed of locally
adapted, mutually supporting crops is nutritionally superior to one assembled from
ingredients sourced globally.

4. **Food should be eaten with gratitude and awareness:** The act of eating should
be conscious and appreciative, recognizing the relationship between the eater, the
food, and the natural systems that produced it.

## Critique of Modern Nutrition Science

Fukuoka was deeply skeptical of nutritional science, which he saw as another manifestation
of the reductionist error. Isolating individual nutrients (vitamins, minerals, proteins)
and prescribing them misses the holistic quality of whole foods. The interaction
between nutrients within a whole food is more important than any single component.

He noted that nutritional science keeps changing its recommendations — fats are good,
then bad; carbohydrates are fine, then problematic — because it studies parts rather
than wholes. A natural diet based on local, whole, seasonal foods requires no nutritional
calculation because nature has already balanced the nutrients.

## The Body Knows

Fukuoka argued that the human body, when not confused by artificial flavors, processed
foods, and dietary fads, naturally craves what it needs. The modern eater, bombarded
by processed food designed to stimulate appetite rather than nourish, has lost this
natural discernment. Returning to whole, simple foods restores the body's innate
nutritional wisdom.
