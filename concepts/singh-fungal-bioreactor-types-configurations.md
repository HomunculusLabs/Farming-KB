# Fungal Bioreactor Types and Configurations

Fungal bioreactors (mycoreactors) are engineered systems designed to
harness the metabolic capabilities of fungi for pollutant removal and
wastewater treatment. These reactors operate under aerobic or anaerobic
conditions and can be configured as batch, semibatch, sequencing batch,
or continuous systems. The selection of reactor type depends on the nature
of the contaminant, fungal species employed, and desired treatment
efficiency.

## Classification of Mycoreactors

Mycoreactors fall into two principal categories based on growth
configuration:

1. **Biofilm or immobilized systems**: Fungi are attached to or entrapped
   within a solid support matrix, providing high cell density and easy
   biomass-liquid separation.

2. **Suspended growth systems**: Fungal mycelium grows freely in the
   liquid phase, requiring subsequent separation steps.

Within these categories, numerous specific configurations exist for
pollutant removal applications.

## Attached Growth Reactors

### Trickling Filters

Trickling filters are among the earliest biofilm reactor types applied in
wastewater treatment. Wastewater is distributed over a packing material
colonized by fungal biofilm, allowing contact between the liquid and the
attached biomass as it percolates downward. Phanerochaete chrysosporium
has been used in immobilized trickling filter configurations for color
removal from bleach plant effluents, achieving approximately 50%
decolorization in 3 to 6 hours.

### Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs)

RBCs consist of a series of rotating disks partially submerged in
wastewater. Fungal biofilm grows on the disk surfaces, and rotation
alternately exposes the biomass to the wastewater and the atmosphere,
providing both contact and aeration. RBCs have been applied for PAH
removal using immobilized Phanerochaete chrysosporium, achieving over
90% PAH removal after 60 hours of continuous operation. The RBC can be
operated for approximately 40 days without diminished effective removal
capacity.

### Upflow Fixed-Film Reactors

In upflow fixed-film reactors, wastewater flows upward through a packed
bed colonized by fungal biomass. This configuration ensures good contact
between the contaminant and the biofilm while minimizing clogging. The
upflow design helps maintain even distribution of the liquid phase across
the bed cross-section.

## Fluidized-Bed and Airlift Reactors

Fluidized-bed reactors suspend solid particles (or fungal pellets) in an
upward-flowing liquid stream. Three-phase fluidized and airlift suspension
reactors are used for both aerobic and anaerobic wastewater treatment and
for the cultivation of immobilized and pellet-forming microorganisms.
Airlift tower loop reactors provide internal circulation of liquid and gas
without mechanical agitation, reducing energy costs and shear damage to
fungal mycelium.

Pellet-forming fungi can be cultivated in aerated fluidized beds, bubble
columns, or suspended airlift loop reactors. However, pellets can grow
excessively at low energy dissipation rates. Above a critical pellet size,
depletion of oxygen leads to cell death, resulting in the formation of
hollow pellets with reduced metabolic activity.

## Stirred-Tank Reactors

Stirred-tank reactors (STRs) are the most common mycoreactors for
submerged fungal growth. They provide good mixing and oxygen transfer but
can cause shear damage to filamentous fungal mycelium. STRs are used in
batch, fed-batch, and continuous modes for the treatment of starch-
processing wastewater, palm oil mill effluent, and other industrial
wastewaters. Aspergillus niger pellets in stirred-tank reactors have
demonstrated 90% starch decomposition and 75% TOC removal.

## Membrane Bioreactors

Membrane bioreactors (MBRs) employ ultrafiltration or microfiltration
membranes for complete retention of fungal biomass. The MBR Bio-Filt,
based on an FM module for ultrafiltration and a modular bioreactor design,
has been developed for wastewater purification. MBRs offer the advantage
of complete biomass retention, allowing high cell densities and long
residence times, but membrane fouling by filamentous fungi remains a
challenge.

## Immobilization Methods

Several types of cell immobilization are employed in fungal bioreactors:

1. **Surface attachment**: Microorganisms attach to the surface of a
   carrier material such as Celite, ceramic honeycomb, or jute rope.
2. **Porous carrier colonization**: Organisms colonize the internal
   structure of porous support materials.
3. **Hydrogel entrapment**: Fungal cells are entrapped within polymer
   gels such as calcium alginate, polyurethane foam, or chitosan.
4. **Pellet and granule formation**: Microbial pellets or granulated
   sludges form naturally or are induced through process control.

Celite is commonly used as a carrier for fungi, but clogging occurs as a
result of excessive growth and fluffy biofilm formation. This can be
controlled by phosphate limitation, low spore concentration, dilute
medium, and low shear stress, which favor pellet formation.

## Process Monitoring and Control

Physical, chemical, and biological parameters affecting the bioprocess
are important in monitoring and improving bioreactor performance.
Mathematical process modeling involving various assumptions can predict
the efficiency of contaminant removal. The metabolic and genetic
diversity of fungi in bioreactors can be analyzed by PCR amplification
of DNA. Knowledge-based control systems have been applied in
biotechnological processes for bioprocess control of fed-batch fungal
cultivations.
