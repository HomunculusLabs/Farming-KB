## EMCDDA Smartshop Online Risk Information Quality Audit (2006)

## Overview

In January 2006, the EMCDDA conducted a systematic audit of risk information provided by 21 online shops selling hallucinogenic mushroom products. This snapshot revealed significant variation in the quality, completeness, and balance of information available to consumers. The audit found a consistent bias toward promoting positive effects while omitting or downplaying potential risks, creating an information asymmetry that could endanger consumers.

## Audit Methodology

The EMCDDA reviewed 21 online shops identified through systematic Google and AltaVista searches using four keyword combinations. The audit assessed each site for the presence or absence of specific categories of risk information, dosage guidance, and safe use practices.

## General Risk Warnings

### What Most Shops Provided

The majority of the 21 online shops warned consumers:
- Not to use hallucinogenic mushrooms when taking medication
- Not to combine mushrooms with alcohol or other drugs (especially stimulants)
- Not to use when pregnant

### What Fewer Shops Provided

Only approximately two-thirds (about 14 of 21 shops) recommended against use when the user suffers from depression or psychosis. This is a significant gap, as individuals with pre-existing mental health conditions are at elevated risk for adverse psychological reactions to psilocybin.

## Dosage Information

About half of the sites provided dosage information. However, the quality and specificity of dosage guidance varied considerably. Some sites gave approximate dose ranges while others provided only vague recommendations. The information typically did not account for:
- Variation in potency between species
- Individual sensitivity differences
- Body weight considerations
- The difference between fresh and dried mushroom dosing
- First-time user precautions

## Safe Use Practices

About half of the sites included information on safe use practices, such as:
- Consume on an empty stomach
- Drink liquids during the trip
- Consume in a quiet, safe environment

However, these recommendations were often presented briefly without detailed context about why they matter or what can go wrong when they are not followed.

## Smartshop Physical Label Example

The EMCDDA documented a specific example of a smartshop product label found on a package of fresh Psilocybe cubensis purchased in Amsterdam in February 2006. The label provided warnings in English, including:

- Persons under 18 should not use the product
- Do not use when pregnant
- Do not use when taking medication
- Do not use when suffering from mental illness
- Do not use when driving or operating machines
- Do not use in combination with alcohol

Notably, the label included the number of a UK Drug Helpline, suggesting that UK customers were being specifically targeted by Dutch retailers. This cross-border marketing pattern reflected the significant UK customer base (14% of visitors to one Dutch online shop).

## Physical Retail vs. Online Information Quality

The Dutch Risk Assessment Report (CAM, 2000) had previously found that the quality and amount of information provided by smartshop retailers varied considerably. Information leaflets typically provided no information about:
- Maximum shelf life of products
- The nature of possible side effects
- The proportion of active substances (psilocybin and psilocin)

The 2006 online audit confirmed that these information gaps persisted six years later and extended to the digital retail environment.

## Implications for Consumer Safety

The systematic bias toward positive information and the omission of risk information raises several concerns:
- Consumers cannot make informed decisions about whether to use mushrooms
- Individuals with mental health vulnerabilities may not be adequately warned
- The lack of dosage precision increases the risk of overwhelming experiences
- The absence of interaction warnings increases polydrug risk in club settings
- First-time users may have unrealistic expectations about the experience
