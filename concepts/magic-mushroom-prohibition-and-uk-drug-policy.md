The government's response was characterised by confusion. Customs and
Excise were apparently generating revenues in the region of one million
pounds from traders, while simultaneously the Home Office sought to
deter trade by warning sellers that they were breaking the law. The
government foolishly appeared to be demanding tax on a product it
considered illegal, the left hand unaware of quite what the right was
doing.

## The Gloucester Case and Legal Chaos

In 2004, a small number of shops were raided by police and charged
with illegally selling hallucinogenic fungi as a Class A drug. The
first case, concerning Dennis Mardle and Colin Evans who ran the
head-shop Collector's Choice in Gloucester, came to court in December
2004 and was promptly thrown out by the Crown Court. The judge,
Claire Miskin, found the law so ambiguous that it would be an abuse of
process for the two men to stand trial. In a carefully aimed rebuke,
she chided the government for expecting the judiciary to sort out a
legal mess of Parliament's making.
