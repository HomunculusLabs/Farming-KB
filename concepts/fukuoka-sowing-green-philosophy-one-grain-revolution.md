# Fukuoka's Green Philosophy and One-Grain Revolution

*Fukuoka's vision for transforming human civilization through [[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]] —
the green philosophy, the economic critique, and the spiritual revolution that
underlies his plan to revegetate the earth.*

## The Natural Culture Alternative

Fukuoka proposed "natural culture and community" as the alternative to modern
civilization. Natural culture is a way of life in which people enjoy the truth
and beauty of nature — climbing mountains, playing in meadows, bathing in warm
sunlight, breathing pure air, drinking crystalline water, and experiencing the
true joy of life. The society he described is one in which people create a free
and generous community.

He warned that once the primal source of nature is destroyed, it will no longer
restore itself. Many species become extinct each day, and the disappearance of
one bird or one plant is connected with the destruction of the harmony of all
living things. There is only one sustainable course: find the way back to true
nature and set to the task of revitalizing the earth. "Regreening the earth,
sowing seeds in the desert — that is the path society must follow."

## The Dragonfly Will Be the Messiah

Fukuoka looked forward to the day when there would be no need for sacred
scriptures or sutras. "The dragonfly will be the messiah." He saw that in the
present age of disintegration, when industry, government, and the military join
forces in the struggle for ultimate power, the people who most easily perceive
nature's sacredness are a few religious people, artists of great sensitivity,
and children. He critiqued religious imposters who hold both deities and people
captive to acquire believers, money, and power.

## The Money-Sucking Octopus Economy

Fukuoka developed a detailed critique of the modern economic system, which he
called the "money-sucking octopus economy." At the center are politicians and
the military-industrial-government complex. The octopus's eight legs are: (1)
transportation network maintenance, (2) transportation agency control, (3)
communications supervision, (4) economic information networks, (5) education and
administrative advising, (6) financial institution control, (7) information
control, and (8) citizens' personal computers and registration.

Everything is pulled to the center. Wealth accumulates centrally and is used for
establishing more centralized authority and strengthening armaments. This tragic
dance is performed on the backs of common people and farmers. Pride goes before
a fall, and in the end the octopus will either be hauled up by the master
fisherman or will eat its own legs.

## Critique of Agricultural Economics

Fukuoka observed that when it became more profitable to deal in diamonds than
grow pumpkins, everyone stopped growing pumpkins. He visited Vienna where fruit
was extremely expensive because Italian farmers were refusing to grow it. The
next day in Italy, he saw a bulldozer destroying beautiful peach trees because
the local agricultural cooperative ordered "production limits" — the same day
French farmers were overturning trucks of imported grapes at the border. The
middlemen who control information about production and cost always determine
prices to their own advantage.

## The Illusion of Material Value

Fukuoka argued that material things have no intrinsic value — value appears only
because people create conditions that make things seem necessary. Change the
conditions and the value is lost. Economies that aim at production and
consumption of unnecessary products are themselves meaningless. An Ethiopian
tribesman rejected Fukuoka's farming ideas: "Are you asking me to become a
farmer? To be attached to the soil and to accumulate things are the acts of a
degraded person." Fukuoka called this a perceptive criticism of modern society.

## Healing the Desert of the Human Heart

A recurring theme throughout the book is the connection between ecological and
spiritual restoration. "Gradually I came to realize that the process of saving
the desert of the human heart and revegetating the actual desert is actually the
same thing." At an Ethiopian refugee hospital, the caring presence of nurses
gave people the courage to live, while children planted vegetable seeds in the
gravel with Fukuoka, understanding that it would be wonderful if the area turned
green.
