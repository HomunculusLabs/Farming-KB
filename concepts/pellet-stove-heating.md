## Overview

Pellet stoves burn compressed wood pellets — small cylinders of dried, ground wood — to provide efficient, controllable home heating. They combine the convenience of [[holmgren-fossil-fuel-pulse-and-energy-descent-cycle]] heating with the renewable benefits of biomass fuel. For off-grid homesteads, pellet stoves offer high efficiency (75-90%), programmable thermostats, and the ability to produce fuel on-site from wood waste.

## Wood Pellets

### Composition and Standards

Quality wood pellets are made from compressed sawdust and wood shavings with no binders — the natural lignin in wood acts as the binding agent when heated under pressure in the pellet mill.

Premium pellet standards (PFI in the US, ENplus in Europe):
- Diameter: 6mm (1/4 inch) standard, 8mm available
- Length: 10-30mm
- Moisture content: below 8%
- Ash content: below 1% for premium, below 3% for standard
- Bulk density: 35-40 pounds per cubic foot
- Energy content: approximately 8,000 BTU per pound

### Pellet Production at Home

Small-scale pellet production is possible but requires significant equipment investment:

1. **Raw material preparation** — Dry sawdust or [[query-how-do-i-use-wood-chips-to-grow-mushrooms-in-my-garden]] below 10% moisture content. A hammer mill or chipper/shredder reduces material to uniform small particles.
2. **Conditioning** — Steam or water is added to bring moisture to exactly 12-15%. This activates the lignin for binding.
3. **Pelletizing** — A flat die or ring die pellet mill compresses the material through holes under high pressure and temperature (150-200F).
4. **Cooling** — Fresh pellets are hot and soft. A cooling tray or bin brings them to [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]], hardening them.
5. **Screening** — Remove fines (broken pellets and dust) for reuse.
6. **Storage** — Store in sealed bags or bulk bins in a dry location.

**Equipment cost:** Small pellet mills (200-400 lbs/hour) range from $2,000-$8,000. Hammer mills add $500-$2,000. For most homesteaders, buying pellets in bulk ($200-$300 per ton) is more economical than producing them unless free sawdust is abundant.

### Buying Pellets

- Buy in bulk (by the ton) during summer for best pricing
- Premium hardwood pellets produce the most heat and least ash
- Softwood pellets (pine) often burn hotter due to higher resin content
- Avoid pellets with visible bark content (higher ash)
- Store in a dry area — pellets absorb moisture and crumble
- A typical home uses 2-5 tons per heating season

## Pellet Stove Types

### Freestanding Pellet Stoves

Self-contained units placed in a room, similar to a woodstove. Features:

- Cast iron or steel construction
- Hopper capacity: 40-80 pounds (provides 12-24 hours of burn time)
- Heat output: 30,000-50,000 BTU/hr
- Heats 1,000-2,000 square feet depending on insulation and climate
- Requires a 3-4 inch exhaust vent (can go directly through a wall — no chimney needed)

### Pellet Inserts

Designed to fit into existing fireplaces:

- Utilizes the existing chimney with a stainless steel liner
- Similar heat output to freestanding stoves
- More aesthetically integrated with the room
- Slightly lower efficiency due to heat loss through the fireplace opening

### Pellet Furnaces/Boilers

Whole-house heating via forced air or hydronic systems:

- Large hopper capacity (up to 200 pounds or more with bulk hopper)
- Heat output: 50,000-100,000+ BTU/hr
- Can be ducted into existing central heating systems
- Higher cost but heats the entire home from one unit
- Some models can burn corn or other [[holmgren-biomass-fuels-and-energy-yield-ratios]] in addition to pellets

## Stove Components

### Hopper

The fuel storage bin on top or back of the stove. Capacity determines burn time between refills:

- Small (40 lbs): 12-18 hours
- Medium (60 lbs): 18-30 hours
- Large (80+ lbs): 24-48 hours
- Auger mechanism at the hopper bottom feeds pellets to the burn pot

### Auger Feed System

A motorized screw (auger) transfers pellets from the hopper to the burn pot. The feed rate is controlled by the thermostat and determines the heat output:

- Low feed rate: 1-2 lbs/hour (maintenance burn)
- Medium feed rate: 2-3 lbs/hour (moderate heating)
- High feed rate: 3-5 lbs/hour (maximum heating)

### Burn Pot

Where combustion occurs. Pellets are fed into the pot, ignited, and burn with forced combustion air. Design varies:

- Standard burn pot: Cast iron or steel, requires regular cleaning
- Pot with agitator: Mechanically stirs the ash to prevent clinker formation
- Bottom-feed design: Pellets enter from below, pushing ash upward and out

### Combustion Fan

Forces air into the burn pot for efficient combustion. Also creates negative pressure in the exhaust system, preventing smoke from entering the room. A second convection fan circulates [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] around the hot combustion chamber and out into the room.

### Control Board

Electronic controls manage:
- Feed rate (heat output)
- Combustion fan speed
- Room temperature thermostat (built-in or external)
- Ignition cycle (automatic electric ignition)
- Safety shutdown (high temperature, low draft, power failure)

## Efficiency and Performance

### Heat Output Comparison

| Fuel | BTU per unit | Efficiency | Cost per million BTU (approx.) |
|------|-------------|------------|-------------------------------|
| Wood pellets | 8,000/lb | 75-90% | $15-25 |
| Cord wood | 6,000-8,000/lb | 55-70% | $10-20 |
| Heating oil | 138,000/gal | 80-90% | $20-35 |
| Natural gas | 100,000/therm | 85-98% | $12-25 |
| Propane | 91,500/gal | 85-95% | $25-40 |

### Advantages Over Woodstoves

- Higher and more consistent efficiency, programmable thermostat control
- No firewood splitting, stacking, or seasoning
- Cleaner burning (particulate emissions 50-90% lower), no creosote buildup
- Automatic ignition and shutdown

### Disadvantages

- Requires electricity (100-400 watts), pellet fuel costs more than free firewood
- Mechanical components can fail (auger jams, fan motors)
- Regular maintenance required (daily cleaning, annual service)

## Off-Grid Power Considerations

Since pellet stoves require electricity, off-grid installations need a 12V battery with inverter (runs 8-12 hours during outages) and ideally a 200W solar panel. Always have a backup heating plan (woodstove, propane heater). Power consumption: 100-400 watts while running; startup draws 300-500 watts for 5-10 minutes.
