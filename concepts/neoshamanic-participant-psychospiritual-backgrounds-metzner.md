# Neoshamanic Circle Participant Psychospiritual Backgrounds

## Overview

Ralph Metzner provided a detailed description of the typical
participant profile in [[metzner-neoshamanic-medicine-circles-structure-and-practice]] circle rituals, based on
personal observation of over 100 such rituals in Europe and North
America involving several hundred participants. His account reveals a
population with extensive prior experience in multiple psychospiritual
practices, representing a sophisticated convergence of indigenous and
Western approaches to consciousness exploration.

## Metzner's Research Context

Metzner's observations came from over a dozen years of participation
in hybrid therapeutic-shamanic circle rituals. These circles blend
psychotherapeutic and traditional shamanic approaches, using plant
entheogens including [[grass-seed-cultivation-of-psilocybe-mushrooms]], ayahuasca, San Pedro cactus,
and iboga. His interest focused on "the nature of the psychospiritual
transformation undergone by participants in such circle rituals."

## Prior Practice Profiles

Metzner reported that most participants were experienced in one or
more psychospiritual practices. The specific practices he identified
include:

- **Shamanic drum journeying**, entering non-ordinary states through rhythmic drumming, widely practiced in shamanistic societies of the Northern Hemisphere

- **Buddhist vipassana meditation**, insight meditation emphasizing direct observation of sensory experience and mental phenomena

- **Tantra yoga**, practices integrating physical, energetic, and spiritual dimensions

- **[[metzner-holotropic-breathwork-and-grof-perinatal-matrices]]**, Stanislav Grof's breathing method for accessing non-ordinary states, derived from observations of breathing patterns in LSD psychotherapy

- **Various forms of psychotherapy**, including body-oriented therapy deriving from Wilhelm Reich

This multi-modal background indicates that neoshamanic circle
participants are not typically novices. They bring sophisticated
understanding from multiple traditions to their work with entheogenic
plant medicines.

## Integration of Multiple Practice Traditions

Metzner observed that "the insights and learnings from these practices
are woven by the participants into their work with the entheogenic
medicines." This represents genuine integration of diverse approaches.
Participants draw on meditation training for focused attention during
ceremonies, body-oriented therapy experience for somatic awareness,
and shamanic journeying experience for navigating visionary states.

Participants confirm that "the entheogenic plant medicines, when
combined with meditative or therapeutic insight processes, function to
amplify awareness and sensitize perception, particularly amplifying
somatic, emotional and instinctual awareness."

## Intention and Preparation Practices

Experienced entheogenic explorers understand the importance of set and
"devote considerable attention to clarifying their intentions with
respect to [[metzner-shamanic-healing-and-divination-paradigm]]." They also understand setting
importance and "devote considerable care to arranging a peaceful place
and time, filled with natural beauty and free from outside
distractions."

Preparation typically includes sharing intentions among participants,
meditation, solo time in nature, or expressive arts such as drawing,
painting, or journal work. This preparation reflects participants'
sophistication in optimizing entheogenic experiences.

## Post-Ceremony Integration

After the circle ritual, typically the following morning, an
integration practice involves participants sharing lessons learned to
be applied in their lives. This phase mirrors practices in both
therapeutic and contemplative traditions, reflecting understanding
that the ceremonial experience begins a longer process of growth.

## Worldview Expansion

Metzner observed that the majority of Westerners developing ongoing
entheogenic practice "seem to have expanded their belief systems
beyond the boundaries of the conventional materialistic paradigm."
While accepting Western psychological insights from Freud, Jung, and
Reich, they accept "the reality of nonmaterial spirit beings and to
recognize that we live in multiple worlds of consciousness."

This worldview expansion suggests that sustained entheogenic practice
within neoshamanic circles produces fundamental shifts in metaphysical
assumptions beyond specific therapeutic outcomes.

## The Council Practice

A talking staff or singing staff is often used. With this practice,
originating among [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] Indians and now called "council,"
only the person holding the circulating staff sings or speaks. There
is no discussion or interpretation as in group psychotherapy. This
reflects indigenous influence and supports authentic sharing without
analysis.

## Significance

Metzner's participant profile is valuable for understanding the
sociological dimensions of modern entheogenic practice. The circles
represent a self-selected population of experienced consciousness
explorers. Their sophisticated backgrounds and integrated approaches
distinguish them from both recreational users and [[hofmann-lsd-clinical-research-early-studies]]
subjects.
