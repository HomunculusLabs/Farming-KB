# Cannabis and Cancer Pain Research

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Cancer pain, particularly pain due to advanced cancer that no longer responds adequately to opioid medications, is a critical area where cannabis has shown significant therapeutic potential. [[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]])
- **CB2 receptors:** Involved in inflammation modulation; may address inflammatory components of cancer pain
- **Endogenous opioid system interaction:** Cannabinoids may enhance the body's natural opioid-mediated pain relief
- **Anti-inflammatory action:** Both THC and CBD have anti-inflammatory properties
- **NMDA receptor interaction:** May reduce opioid tolerance and hyperalgesia

## Beyond Pain: Additional Cancer Benefits

While the primary research cited here addresses pain, other areas of cannabis and cancer research include:
- Anti-nausea and anti-emetic effects (well-established for chemotherapy-induced nausea)
- Appetite stimulation (important for cancer-related cachexia)
- Sleep improvement (often disrupted by cancer pain and anxiety)
- Anti-tumor properties (pre-clinical research suggests cannabinoids may have direct anti-cancer effects, though this is not yet clinically proven)
- Anxiety and depression relief (psychological support for cancer patients)
