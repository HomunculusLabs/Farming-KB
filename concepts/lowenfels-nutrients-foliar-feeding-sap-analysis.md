### Immobile Nutrients (Poor Candidates for Foliar Feeding)

Calcium, boron, sulfur, iron, manganese, and copper are usually immobile once inside plants.
Deficiencies of these immobile elements appear first in the youngest parts of the plant, the
terminal buds and roots. Foliar feeding is of limited value for these nutrients because even if
they enter the leaf, they cannot be transported to where they are needed in the growing tips.

## When Foliar Feeding Is Appropriate

Foliar spraying is fine for a temporary quick fix for deficiencies of mobile nutrients like iron
and zinc. The macronutrients can sometimes enter the leaves this way too, but gardeners cannot
supply plants with enough of these by foliar spraying to keep the plants thriving. Foliar feeding
is best used as a supplement to soil-based nutrition, not a replacement.

Liquid fertilizers such as fish emulsions and kelp extracts are sometimes applied as foliar
sprays. These work faster than most dry fertilizers, provided there is a healthy soil food web.
However, liquids are normally more effective when applied to roots where they can be taken up in
the quantities plants actually need.
