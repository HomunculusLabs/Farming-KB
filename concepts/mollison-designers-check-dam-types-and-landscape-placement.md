# Check Dam Types and Landscape Placement

Check dams are barrier structures built across watercourses
that are not primarily intended to create water storages,
but to retard flow, spread water, capture silt, and
stabilise eroding channels. They are essential components of

### Concrete Flood Check Dams
Concrete structures that allow normal flow to pass while
retarding floods and preventing rapid flood discharge.
These are permanent, high-strength structures suitable for
critical locations where failure would be catastrophic. They
are placed at intervals along watercourses to create a
stepped retardation of flood peaks.

### Silt Check Dams
Earth or concrete walls, or gabions, that hold silt fields,
spread water, and reduce silt load in streams. For farm
construction, walls 0.5-2 m (2-6.5 feet) high are usual.
The purpose is not to store free surface water, but to
create a flat area where silt loads can usefully deposit.

### Gabion Check Dams
In drylands, permeable barriers of rock-filled mesh
baskets (gabions) will create silt fields and
water-spreading across eroding valleys. These are
particularly effective because their permeability allows
water to pass through while trapping sediment. They resist
floods well and can be built incrementally as silt
accumulates.

Eroding gullies are best stabilised using strong wire
baskets to contain stones or shingle. These resist floods
well and can be built by hand labour without heavy
equipment.

### Ram Pump and Water Wheel Check Dams
These create only 1-3 m of head but enable lift pumps or
diversion to canals. They represent the integration of
water retardation with [[fukuoka-three-dimensional-solar-energy-capture-rice-paddy]], embodying the
permaculture principle of multiple functions from single
elements.

## Landscape Placement Principles

The landscape can be viewed (as though sliced into layers
through contours) as a set of catchment, storage, usage,
and revitalisation zones. Check dams are placed at the
boundaries and within these zones to manage water movement:

- In catchment zones: diversion check dams redirect water
  to storages
- In transition zones: silt check dams capture sediment
  before it reaches lower slopes
