# Acidobacteria iii1-15 Decline in Cannabis Endorhiza

## Overview

The dramatic decline of Acidobacteria (order iii1-15) in the cannabis
endorhiza is one of the most statistically robust findings from Winston
et al. (2014) and provides compelling empirical support for the two-tier
selection model of plant-microbiome assembly. As [[plant-signaling-pathways]].

A central prediction is a **dramatic reduction in Acidobacteria within
the endosphere**, driven by their oligotrophic physiology being
incompatible with carbon-rich rhizosphere and endorhiza environments.

## Statistical Evidence

### Most Significant OTU: Acidobacteria iii1-15

The single most statistically significant OTU differentiating the three
root compartments was an Acidobacteria member of order iii1-15, showing
a steep decline from bulk soil to endorhiza with a Bonferroni-corrected
ANOVA p-value of **1.12 × 10⁻⁷**.

### Community-Level Shifts

The study identified 51 differentiating OTUs across compartments. The
overall pattern confirmed two-tier model predictions: a **decrease in
abundance of Acidobacteria and an increase of Proteobacteria and
Actinobacteria relative to the rhizosphere and bulk soil**. Of the 51
differentiating OTUs, **17 increased** in the endorhiza — predominantly
Proteobacteria, including several Rhizobiales associated with nitrogen
fixation and intimate plant-microbe symbioses.

### Pearson Correlation of Mean OTU Abundance

| Compartment Pair | Pearson ρ | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk soil ↔ Rhizosphere | **0.92** | Strong similarity; first-tier filtering is conservative |
| Rhizosphere ↔ Endorhiza | **0.63** | Moderate divergence; second-tier selection is disruptive |
| Bulk soil ↔ Endorhiza | **0.42** | Weak similarity; cumulative filtering produces distinct communities |

The sharpest correlation drop occurs between rhizosphere and endorhiza,
consistent with host genotype-dependent selection being the more
disruptive filter of the two tiers.

## Ternary Plot Visualization (Figure 4)

The ternary plot from Winston et al. (2014, Figure 4) spatially
represents community shifts across compartments:

- **Acidobacteria** cluster heavily toward the bulk soil vertex,
  declining through the rhizosphere and reaching minimal
  representation at the endorhiza vertex.
- **Proteobacteria** display the opposite trajectory — minimal in bulk
  soil, strongly enriched at the endorhiza vertex.
- **Actinobacteria** occupy an intermediate position, increasing from
  bulk soil to endorhiza but less dramatically than Proteobacteria.

This opposing trajectory is a hallmark signature of the two-tier model.

## Oligotrophic Physiology Explains the Decline

Acidobacteria are oligotrophic organisms adapted to low-nutrient,
low-carbon environments. Key traits include slow growth rates, high-
affinity transport systems for scarce nutrients, genomic streamlining,
and preference for complex recalcitrant organic matter over labile
exudates.

These traits confer competitiveness in bulk soil where carbon is
heterogeneous and nutrients scarce. However, they are mismatched to the
rhizosphere and endorhiza, where root exudates create concentrated
zones of labile sugars, amino acids, and organic acids. Copiotrophic
Proteobacteria — with rapid growth and ability to exploit simple carbon
sources — outcompete Acidobacteria in these carbon-rich niches.
